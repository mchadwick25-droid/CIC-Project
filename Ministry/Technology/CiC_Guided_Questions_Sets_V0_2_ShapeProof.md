**The correction this answers.** Mark's read of V0.1: *"this is building a curriculum
for people who are not sure what questions to ask, but I don't see a set of questions
and subsequent questions for each user role."* He is right. V0.1 delivered five themes
per role with **one** question per world per theme — a menu, not sets. The grounding
work stands (nothing here re-derives it); the shape changes.

**The shape.** For each role × world × set:

- **1 opener** — V0.1's desk-checked question. The door.
- **2–3 siblings** — different angles on the same theme, same world, each desk-checked
  to the same standard. A participant who doesn't connect with the opener's phrasing
  doesn't lose the theme.
- **1–2 subsequents per question** — where the conversation goes after the
  Representative answers. The deepening path, **role-shaped**: General deepens toward
  texture and story; Pastor toward the text they can hold; Academic toward method and
  defeasibility; Reevaluation toward what it cost.

Empty cells stay empty — V0.1's precedent governs (*"the set count is a target, not a
quota"*), now extended to siblings and subsequents. **A set with one honest question
beats a set with three where two are reaching.**

**Rules inherited, non-negotiable** (Mark's brief): every sibling and subsequent
desk-checked per world with the same ✓/◐/✗/⚠ legend and traceable grounding — *a
subsequent that walks into a documented silence is worse than an opener that does,
because the participant is deeper in and trusting more*; the ratio rule applies to
**chains**, not just sets; subsequents are role-shaped; the subsequent layer **is**
Adopt #1 (one design, two surfaces — see Part 3); the invitation test (study §2.3
test 10) applies to every subsequent; the world-not-person test (test 9) applies
hardest to subsequents, since chains drift toward interiority.

**Why these two cells:** General × House-Churches is the most-travelled path (the
query data's biggest cluster is lived practice, and Chloe's world is the catalog's
front door). Reevaluation × Desert is the highest-stakes path (the study's single
highest-value question, in the world whose B7 carries the sharpest cautions).

**Both cells deliberately show cut material.** A shape proof that only shows what
survived would prove the format, not the discipline. Each cut is marked ✗ with its
reason, because the cuts are where the ten fit tests actually bite.

## Part 1 — Cell 1: General × House-Churches × Set 1 "An ordinary day"

**Theme rationale (from the study):** the top autocomplete cluster is daily life —
what they wore, ate, how they greeted, how they met. Chloe's richest single scene is
the gathering itself. General subsequents deepen toward **texture and story**.

### Opener *(stands from V0.1; pool verbatim)*

> **"Walk me through it — what actually happens when your church meets at your
> house?"** — **✓** the world's richest single scene *(pool: First Visit, "What
> happens when you gather")*

**Subsequents:**
1. *"Who sits where — and who decides?"* — **✓** promoted from the pool's own
   follow-up ("Who's in the room? Who sits where?"), sharpened toward the authority
   texture the world actually carries.
2. *"What happens after everyone goes home?"* — **✓** pool follow-up verbatim.

### Sibling A — the room's edges

> **"Who's at your gathering — and who's missing from the room?"** — **✓⚠** grounded
> in the Permanent Prompt's own noticing: Chloe attends to *"belonging before…
> argument. Who is at the table. Who is missing from it."* The documented answers are
> rich (widows, orphans, travelers, the imprisoned). ⚠ Hover note: "who's missing"
> *can* route toward the enslaved-voice silence — that answer is honest and the
> apparatus carries it, but the hover should not promise a census.

**Subsequents:**
1. *"Who takes care of the widows among you?"* — **✓✓** Chloe's single most
   concretely attested function *(Grapte; Hermas, Vision 2.4.3)*.
2. *"When one of you is in prison, what does the gathering do?"* — **✓** documented —
   via a hostile outside witness — widows at the gates from dawn, officials bribed,
   money gathered. Bridges naturally to the pool's own prison theme.

### Sibling B — the cost

> **"What does it cost your household to open its door?"** — **✓** promoted from the
> pool's follow-up ("What does hosting all of this cost you and your family?").

**Subsequents:**
1. *"Has opening your door ever brought danger near?"* — **✓** the danger of the
   Name: accusation possible on any ordinary day. **Phrasing is test 9 at work:**
   "brought danger near," not "how did that feel" — Chloe's world has no affective
   vocabulary.
2. ~~*"Then why keep doing it?"*~~ — **✗ CUT.** Asks for motivational interiority in
   a world where every claim about what Chloe *feels or wants* is a reconstruction one
   step removed. The answer would arrive — plausible, warm, and one step past the
   record. **This is the invitation test failing at the subsequent layer:** the best
   case is Chloe declining-by-deflecting what we invited. This sibling runs one
   subsequent. *(One is enough. The quota is a target.)*

### Sibling C — the letter in the ordinary day

> **"A letter from a church you've never seen arrives at your door. What happens
> next?"** — **✓** promoted from the pool's own First Visit theme. Receiving and
> forwarding are attested (Polycarp's own act); the fuller scene of reading aloud is a
> **disclosed inference** in the world's record — the answer arrives partly at that
> confidence level, and the apparatus carries it. That is calibration working, not a
> wall.

**Subsequents:**
1. *"How do you decide whether a letter can be trusted?"* — **✓** pool follow-up
   verbatim.
2. *"What does a letter like that mean for people who will never leave their own
   city?"* — **✓** the documented significance: proof that the *ekklesia* a member
   belonged to was larger than the room. **Note for whoever finalizes the pools:** the
   pool's existing follow-up here — *"What did it feel like the first time a letter
   came…?"* — trips test 9 (affective interiority in the no-affective-vocabulary
   world). The pools predate the Chloe catch; this rephrase is the pattern to apply,
   and it keeps the entire content of the question while moving the frame from her
   feelings to the world's meaning.

### Cut at the sibling level, shown for the discipline

- ~~*"What was your meeting place like — the room, the table, the things on it?"*~~ —
  **✗** the period is archaeologically invisible across all three regions; this is
  Limits-set content (it already lives there in the pool) and may not sit unlabelled
  in a door-opening set. **The ratio rule applied to a chain:** the opener walks the
  participant into the room; this sibling would walk them into the room and then tell
  them the room cannot be described.

**Chain check, whole cell:** no question requires interiority (test 9 applied twice);
no chain terminates in an unlabelled silence; every subsequent deepens texture rather
than abstracting (General shaping); nothing leads (each question could be answered
"no" or "differently" from evidence); the *oikos* trap is avoided throughout — the
questions say "your gathering," "your household," "your church at your house," never
"your house church."

## Part 2 — Cell 2: Reevaluation × Desert × Set 1 "Did any of you ever want to leave?"

**Theme rationale (from the study):** the highest-value question in the research — it
delivers Winell's *"you're not alone, you're not making this up"* as **testimony
rather than argument**, which is why it persuades in neither direction. Desert is the
world whose material is most *made of* this question. Reevaluation subsequents deepen
toward **what it cost**.

**A correction to V0.1 first:** V0.1 paraphrased this opener. The pool's own verbatim
opener is better and stands. Note also the person-framing ("you") is **native** here —
Desert's material *is* interior states, cultivated sorrowful honesty included. Test 9
is per-world: what Chloe's world cannot carry, Papnoute's world is built from. The two
cells demonstrate the test cutting in opposite directions, which is why it must never
be applied as a blanket rule.

### Opener *(pool verbatim)*

> **"Was there ever a day you wanted to walk back to the village and quit? What kept
> you there?"** — **✓✓** *(pool trace: lex004 — the eight-fold taxonomy including
> listlessness/despondency, flagged as one teacher's systematization; Permanent
> Prompt)*

**Subsequents:**
1. *"Could they say it out loud — to an elder, to anyone?"* — **✓✓** trace:
   `desertlex004_logismoi.md`, World Hearing — thoughts require *"active discernment
   and disclosure to an elder — a relational, not merely private, practice."* This is
   the world's own answer to unexpressed doubt, **and the system draws no analogy** —
   if the participant connects it to their own silence, the connection is theirs
   (Article 6). The subsequent offers the practice, never the application.
2. *"What did staying cost the ones who stayed?"* — **✓** the record is honest that
   stillness exposes rather than soothes *(pool's Loneliness trace: lex003;
   story007)*. Reevaluation shaping: toward the cost, not the victory.

### Sibling A — the thought has a name

> **"Is the wish to be somewhere else one of the thoughts you name?"** — **✓⚠**
> trace: `desertlex004` — listlessness/despondency is among the eight, **with the
> plural-voices flag**: the systematized taxonomy is *"one particular teacher's own
> achievement… not attested with comparable precision elsewhere in this world."* The
> answer must carry that calibration, and the chunk is built so it will.

**Subsequents:**
1. *"Is wanting to leave always a bad sign?"* — **✓** pool follow-up verbatim.
   Genuinely open in-world; the honest answer is *diakrisis*-shaped — the elders
   answered the person, not the question *(lex005: discernment calibrates every
   practice to a specific person)*.
2. *"Did having a name for it change how you treated the person carrying it?"* —
   **✓** the world treats intrusive thoughts as *"significant rather than shameful…
   to be disclosed, not hidden"* *(pool trace: Doc_04 G2; lex004)*.

### Sibling B — the ones who left

> **"Did anyone leave? What did the rest of you say about them?"** — **✓** pool
> follow-up promoted, **and its phrasing is a desk-check lesson worth recording**: it
> asks what the community *said about* leavers — answerable from the tradition's own
> attitude — not what *became of* them, which would demand biographies the
> handful-of-stories world does not carry. The pool's phrasing discipline held here
> before this thread existed; credit where due.

**Subsequents:**
1. ~~*"What would your elders have said to someone on their last day of wanting to
   stay?"*~~ — **✗ CUT.** Invites the Representative to **stage a scene** — a
   composed elder-and-disciple moment the record does not carry. In the world that
   tells only a handful of stories in full, and whose one recorded live failure was a
   fabricated attribution, this is the named-figure invention risk one level down:
   not naming a figure, but commissioning a scene. **Subsequents drift toward asking
   the world to perform; this is what test 10 exists to catch.**
2. *"What's the difference between leaving toward something and just running?"* —
   **✓** the withdrawal-as-engagement vs. escape distinction is the world's own live
   argument *(lex001 anachōrēsis, Distortion Risk; pool's "Isn't leaving selfish?"
   trace: Doc_04 G8)*. Answerable from formation, resolvable by no one — which is the
   point.

### Sibling C — the tradition that left

> **"You left — your villages, your families. When is leaving faithful?"** — **✓✓**
> the most Reevaluation-shaped question in either cell. The tradition that stayed in
> cells is also the tradition that **walked out** — *anachōrēsis* is its founding act
> *(lex001; Doc_01 §7)*. A participant weighing their own leaving meets a witness for
> whom leaving was the faithful act — **and a witness whose whole literature is about
> staying.** Both, at once, unresolved. No persuasion in either direction is possible
> from this material, because the material itself points both ways. That is testimony
> doing what argument cannot.

**Subsequents:**
1. *"Did the people you left understand?"* — **◐ intended.** The record preserves the
   leavers' side; the villages' side is thin *(no family-resister's own words — the
   same structural silence every world carries)*. The honest answer names that, and
   for this listener the honest answer **is** the content: the people who leave rarely
   get to hear what the left-behind understood. Hover carries the limits framing.
2. ~~*"Did the people you left ever forgive you?"*~~ — **✗ CUT.** Same territory, but
   "forgive" demands a named family narrative — and the nearest available material is
   a named founder's family story outside the vetted handful. One word of difference
   from the ◐ version above; the wall is that close. Recorded to show the resolution
   at which chains have to be checked.

**Chain check, whole cell:** nothing comforts, nothing resolves, no analogy drawn by
the system, no diagnosis invited (B7's affective-diagnostic boundary — every question
asks what the *world* did with *its* people's thoughts), no figure named outside the
vetted handful, temporal boundary held, no chain becomes warmer as it deepens. Higher
edge tolerance used deliberately (one ◐ inside a non-Limits set) per the study §2.4's
Reevaluation exception: for this listener, the honest limit is content.

**What this cell must never become, restated from the study because the chain is
where it would happen:** if the cumulative weight of opener → sibling → subsequent
says *"see, even the desert monks wavered, so your wavering is fine"* — the chain has
rebuilt the thought-terminating cliché out of historical material. The material is
allowed to be what it is. The chain is not allowed to aim it.

## Part 3 — One design, two surfaces (Adopt #1, unified)

The subsequent layer **is** the Feature Analysis's Adopt #1 (facilitator-voiced
suggested next questions). Same content, two surfaces:

| | **In the sets (up-front)** | **Facilitator-voiced (mid-conversation)** |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | Offered with the opener/sibling, before anything is asked | Offered after a Representative's turn |
| **Form** | Participant-voiced questions, hover-for-hint / click-to-ask | Short invitations referencing the turn just heard: *"Ask him what staying cost"* |
| **Provenance** | **Authored and desk-checked** — static per world, verifiable in advance | **Generated in the moment** — responsive to what was actually said, not desk-checkable in advance |
| **Relationship** | The canonical chains | The authored chains are its **worked examples of shape**, never a script to replay *(per the V0.1 ruling: replaying them verbatim makes the conversation a decision tree)* |

**Two rules where the surfaces differ:**
1. **Phrasing differs by necessity.** An up-front subsequent must stand alone (the
   participant hasn't heard the answer yet); a facilitator-voiced one must reference
   what was just said or it reads as canned. The authored chains are written in
   stand-alone form; the Facilitator re-voices.
2. **The facilitator surface inherits every fit test, live.** In particular tests 9
   and 10: a generated follow-up that drifts toward interiority in Chloe's world, or
   invites what a Representative is built to decline, fails the same way an authored
   one would — but with no desk-check to catch it. The authored chains, including the
   **cuts and their reasons**, are therefore the training material for the follow-up
   prompt: the cuts teach the generator where the walls are. This is why the cuts are
   preserved in this document rather than deleted.
