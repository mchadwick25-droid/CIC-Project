## 1. Mission — read this first, it governs everything below

Church in Conversation exists to help people experience Jesus in new ways through engagement with the living traditions and practices of His Church throughout history and today. Everything below serves that mission through one instrument: a genuinely engaging, natural, and rigorous conversation between a modern participant and Representatives who voice historical Christian worlds — never an invented individual, always the world's own collective "we."

## 2. What this redesign is for

This is not a data-modeling exercise for its own sake. The test of every decision below is: does this make the conversation more engaging, more natural, and more faithful, for a real participant, at the table, right now. A data structure that's elegant but doesn't serve a better conversation is wrong. A shortcut that serves a better conversation but breaks rigor is also wrong. Holding both at once is the actual design problem — conversation dynamics are the ground this whole redesign stands on, not an afterthought once the data model is settled.

**Research doc 10 is the direct evidence base for that claim** — read it, not just this paragraph's summary of it. Realness measurably collapses over a long conversation through three distinct modes (declining initiative, agreement-drift, verbosity/style drift), and the deepest persona failure found across the research is that a voice *states* its character but fails to *enact* it — especially by refusing to sustain disagreement. For CiC that's not a separate naturalness problem: a Representative that stops pushing back theologically stops feeling real and stops being faithful to its own world at the same moment. Any conversation-dynamics design in this brief should be checked against doc 10's findings directly.

A third thing grounds this equally, not as a secondary constraint: **real API/runtime cost has to come down, not just stay acceptable.** This isn't in tension with the two goals above — in at least one place the research found, it's the same fix. Retrieval volume has never once been tuned or measured, and a share of what gets injected into every turn is exactly the scholarly-register material already shown to be leaking into voice as an unwanted "documentation" tone. Organizing that correctly is very plausibly a quality win and a cost win at the same time, not a tradeoff between them. Treat every proposal in this brief through that same lens: where quality and cost pull in the same direction, take it; where they'd genuinely conflict, rigor governs, but don't assume a conflict exists before checking.

**Two caveats on how to do the cost work — get these wrong and the cost comparison will be wrong by roughly 2x.** First, **never cost this system on raw token counts.** Cache-read tokens are billed at a small fraction of the raw rate. Research doc 01 records a real prior decision in this project approved at "~1.4x once the cache-read portion is weighted at its true rate, not the raw 3x token-count multiple" — the raw multiple would have rejected a change that was actually affordable. Second, the direction can genuinely reverse: a *larger but stable* cached prefix can cost less per turn than a smaller prefix that varies every turn, because a stable prefix stays cached. Research doc 10 records Character.AI reporting ~95% cache-hit rates on exactly this principle — holding the truncation point fixed across several turns rather than re-truncating every turn. A proposal that shrinks per-turn context but destabilizes the cached prefix can *raise* real cost while looking cheaper on a token count. **One more caching fact doc 10 flags as worth watching, not urgent:** Anthropic's cache TTL is 5 minutes by default — a participant who pauses mid-session longer than that, which this product's own contemplative pacing invites, loses the cache advantage and becomes a real, currently-unmodeled cost variable. **The real, current baseline — directionally solid, not a precise figure yet.** Real per-conversation cost runs roughly $0.06–0.08/exchange — not the earlier $0.42/question figure, which turned out to be a mis-attribution (it covered five separate sessions, not one). Real measured spend is running close to $2/hour against an original $1/hour funding assumption — that gap is the concrete thing this redesign's cost goal needs to close. **One honest caveat on both numbers:** they were measured on the streaming endpoint before a logging bug was found and fixed there — cache-read stats were invisible on exactly that code path, the one every real user turn takes, so a cache-aware re-measurement hasn't been done yet. Treat these as an order-of-magnitude floor, not a precise baseline to design a cost comparison against to the dollar. The fuller context is in `Ministry/Funding/CiC_Go_Live_Cost_Model_V0_1.md` ($0.20–0.35/typical conversation, $0.30–0.75/Living-Table round) — same caveat applies.

**One constitutional note on how far the cost goal reaches.** Constitution Article 5 states rigor is a constitutional requirement that no resource constraint overrides. The cost goal above is real and grounds this redesign, but it does not outrank rigor — where they genuinely conflict, Article 5 decides it, and the brief's "grounds this equally" framing should be read within that bound, not against it.

**This brief covers the build process, representative-construction methodology, facilitator governance, and table/multi-world dynamics together, as one system — deliberately, not because it's convenient.** The research found the real failures live at the seams between these, not inside any one of them: a lens synthesis stripped from a world's own build on Article 3 grounds because it was "heavily Representative-oriented" — Step-10 representative-construction work landing in a Step-4 document, a **governance-mandated firewall between build stages**, not an editorial scope call; real relational insight that reaches Doc_04 and never reaches Doc_08, the Permanent Prompt, or the Facilitator's own table-selection logic. Redesigning the data layer without redesigning how representative construction, facilitator governance, and the table consume it would risk producing the exact same disconnection this research diagnosed, one level up — including reproducing the same firewall problem, since the boundary that caused the original deletion doesn't go away just because the schema changes. The design should be unified even if the rollout is phased — see §10.

**Five architecture facts, checked directly against the running code, that would otherwise invalidate a design built on assumptions:**
- **The LangGraph graph is vestigial.** Real orchestration for an ongoing conversation is hand-wired in `main.py`; `builder.py`'s documented flow doesn't run it. Don't design against `builder.py` as if it were the real flow.
- **Two incompatible multi-world turn engines already coexist** — the streaming endpoint's `select_next_speaker` loop and the plain endpoint's fixed-order `multi_representative_engages`. **This is more severe than "two engines of differing sophistication":** the single-vs-all routing decision (the "prefer single when in doubt" conservatism this brief already leans on) exists only on the non-streaming path. The live streaming endpoint has no such decision at all — a two-turn floor keeps a second world structurally compelled to speak even when a participant addresses one world by name. Any facilitator-governance redesign has to either unify these or explicitly account for both, and the streaming path is the one that actually needs the fix.
- **The participant-role/lane register system is dead code**, gated on a field nothing ever sets. A three-level-access design proposed on top of it needs to know the layer it's extending is already inert.
- **Only one drift signal survives per turn by design** — the code itself names the risk, that a real fabrication can lose its slot to a milder stylistic complaint.
- **`GET /api/session/{id}/audit` already exists** and covers part of the repository/reference job (§6) — don't design it from scratch as if nothing does this today.

## 4. What NOT to rebuild — this is a redesign, not a restart

None of the items below are mechanisms frozen in place. Fable should feel free to redesign any of them, including moving where in the process they're handled entirely, if a better mechanism serves the same end. What's actually fixed is the **purpose and the outcome** underneath each one — the current mechanism is just today's best attempt at it, not a boundary. Several of these may genuinely be better managed elsewhere in the pipeline than where they live today; that's an improvement, not a violation of scope.

**Purpose (non-negotiable) → current mechanism (open to redesign):**

- **A Representative always voices the world's own collective life, never an invented individual** — independently validated by outside industry practice, not just an internal preference (see research doc 09). Current mechanism: pronoun discipline written into the prompt, checked by adversarial testing.
- **No claim may be spoken without being traceable to an actual source, with honest confidence attached** — this is the outcome, not the specific registry format. Current mechanism: Doc_02's Source Registry checkpoint. This whole redesign is partly about generalizing this pattern; the pattern itself, or something that achieves the same guarantee better, should carry forward.
- **A participant in real crisis, or drifting toward an unhealthy dependency on a Representative, is met deliberately — never left to whatever a Representative happens to improvise.** Current mechanism: a classify-then-route safety pipeline, live-tested to 19/20 clean. The track record is what's worth protecting; the specific pipeline shape isn't sacred if something else holds the same guarantee.
- **Full-model cost is never spent on a decision a cheaper, reliable check could make.** Current mechanism: a hard split between the generation model and a cheaper model for every classifier. Directly relevant to the cost requirement above — and not the only place cost discipline already exists, just the most consistently applied one: prompt caching on the static prompt and adjudication prefix, retrieval-filter batching, session/message/table-size caps framed explicitly as spend control, and real per-call usage instrumentation are all already in place too. Extend the principle across all of them, don't lose any.
- **A real fabrication must never be missed, and a real conviction must never be second-guessed into false hesitation** — two different mistakes, two different acceptable error directions. Current mechanism: two adjudicators, deliberately biased in opposite directions — FABRICATION fails toward *finding* when genuinely uncertain, OVER_SETTLING fails toward *clearing*, deliberately the opposite way. Both already anticipate real, published findings rather than being house jargon. The over-settling check has an exact published name — **certainty distortion**, violating a property the field calls **epistemic faithfulness** — from a 2026 finding that models inflate expressed certainty 1.5–2x more often than they deflate it. That inflation direction is why the check exists at all; its own deliberate bias toward *clearing* defends the same property one layer down, since a false OVER_SETTLED verdict manufactures the opposite failure — a genuine conviction talked into false hesitation. Doc 13's own formulation: CiC is defending both directions, inside this one mechanism. The fabrication adjudicator's opposite bias is a separate axis, not part of the same pair, and it should split its single FABRICATED verdict into **intrinsic** (contradicts the world's own record) versus **extrinsic** (unsupported by what was retrieved, not necessarily false) — the field's own basic hallucination taxonomy, and a distinction the adjudicator's own prompt already draws in prose without carrying into its output.
- **A modern participant unfamiliar with period vocabulary should never be lost by it.** Current mechanism: a confirmed-gloss whitelist — expanded and bug-fixed the same night this research was compiled (a case-sensitivity detection fix, then a bulk-confirm pass across all six worlds' glossaries), not the mechanism that broke that night. What actually broke, same night, was a different mechanism entirely: the sensed-closing-sequence's four Facilitator prompts, imported but never defined, so every naturally winding-down conversation threw a mid-stream error instead of closing gracefully (fixed same night). Keep this purpose's goal; the confirmed-gloss mechanism is still fair game to redesign if something serves it better — on its own merits, not because it was broken.

## 5. The diagnosis — four converging findings, found independently

Three research passes plus live bug-hunting on the night this was compiled arrived at the same underlying cause from four different directions (full evidence in research docs 04, 05, 06, 07, 08):

**a. Build process.** Quality does not improve with build order on the two hardest quantitative proxies — the last world built (Imperial-Juridical) has the worst one-round-clear rate of any world (0 of 10) and the highest round count (40% of documents needing 3+ rounds). That's real, but not the whole picture: fabrication frequency drops somewhat later in the build order — Syriac and Desert (the two earliest-built worlds) ran four-plus fabricated citations each, versus zero in Alexandria and one in Imperial-Juridical — but the type never disappears; it recurred in the very last world built, six weeks and roughly twenty Change Orders after the project's anti-fabrication apparatus (Constitution Articles 26/28, the Source Registry mechanism) was substantially mature. And part of Imperial-Juridical's round count has a specific, named cause rather than being a general regression: its one genuinely new process component (a Step-0 gate) needed 3 rounds on its own, and its own review record attributes that to the gate's template being new and under-specified, not to builder inexperience. The failures cluster in document types that require a prose narrative to stay in sync with a separate structured artifact by hand — a lexicon narrative and its companion chunk files, a forces document and its companion index. Every failure in these types takes the same shape: the fix landed where a reviewer looked, not in the other places the same fact also lives. **A further, differently-shaped defect, not previously named here:** the document that's supposed to carry a world's own relational knowledge to the Facilitator — the World Facilitation Brief — is fully review-cleared for only 2 of 6 worlds and doesn't exist at all for the last world built. Research doc 17 attributes this to a different cause than the prose/structured-artifact desync above: a hand-authored artifact positioned at the end of a 17-step build sequence is exactly the kind of thing that gets dropped under end-of-process attrition, not a synchronization failure — worth designing the build sequence itself to protect against, not just catching it at a verification gate after the fact. **One more data point directly relevant to Pass 1 deliverable 3 (the verification gate):** a written-only standing rule against self-certified dismissal of a blocking finding was adopted at Syriac (World #7) specifically to stop this pattern — before Desert (#3) and Hieronymian (#9) were even built — and the identical pattern recurred in both of them anyway. It then recurred three more times in Imperial-Juridical, the very last world built, at the same density as the failures that prompted the rule in the first place. A written rule alone has a demonstrated failure rate in this project — whatever gate Pass 1 designs needs to hold up against that track record, not just exist as an instruction.

**b. Representative construction.** Voice failures are overwhelmingly organization failures, not evidence failures. A story was told with the right content and the wrong occasion, in the same transcript that got it right elsewhere. A fully-built, indexed martyrdom account never reached a Representative because the prompt's own grief list pointed at six different, unnarratable names instead. The material existed. It wasn't reachable at the moment of speaking.

**c. Runtime retrieval.** Generation output length has been tuned with real rigor — measured, revised, re-measured. Retrieval input has never been tuned once since the original scaffold. Concretely: three of six worlds have every lexicon chunk marked Tier 1, so retrieval conditions are never evaluated at all; some conditions are a literal em-dash; the one field built for safe, lightweight surfacing (Quick Meaning) is silently dropped by the parser while heavy scholarly-register content is injected raw into every turn — a live, per-turn source of exactly the "documentation voice" register leaks found across every world's own testing. This is also a real cost lever sitting unexamined: Alexandria alone can push roughly 4,300 words of retrieved context into a single turn against a ~900-word output cap — tokens being paid for that are actively working against voice quality, not for it. **The mechanism underneath this is worse than "never tuned" — it's structurally blind.** The embedding model that decides what's even a retrieval candidate has a 256-word-piece limit, and `Retrieve-When`/`Do-Not-Retrieve-When` — the one field an author actually writes to control retrieval — is never embedded at all, only read afterward by a separate relevance filter. For a 1,767-word Alexandria chunk, that means roughly the first 150-170 words are all the similarity search ever sees (research doc 15 rates the exact cutoff Medium confidence — 256 word-pieces isn't 256 words, and the ratio shifts for Greek and Syriac script); the rest, including the author's own trigger conditions, might as well not exist for the purpose of finding it. **One completeness gap the Quick Meaning fix above depends on closing first:** Desert currently has zero of nine terms with a Quick Meaning field populated at all — not dropped by the parser, never written in the first place, so there's nothing yet for the parser to drop.

**d. Cross-cutting.** Real, good relational insight already exists and never reaches the Representative. Doc_04's own vocabulary for how forces reinforce, compete with, or reshape *each other* ("reshaping") self-organized independently across four of six worlds before any template asked for it — the template itself, written after those four worlds existed, calls it "the one structural element all four prior worlds converged on independently"; Desert uses none of these terms at all — and it still reaches a Representative-side document in only 2 of 6 worlds, zero Permanent Prompts. **One thing to carry into the schema honestly, not smooth over:** what this redesign actually wants — a force reshaped by *the world's own response to it* — is a relationship the current vocabulary can't express at all. Every found "reshaping" instance is force-to-force or gravity-to-gravity; the one case that comes closest had to be smuggled into the schema as a force-to-force connection, with the entry itself conceding it couldn't honestly be held any other way. A real academic model exists for exactly this relationship and names it precisely: Margaret Archer's **morphogenetic cycle** (structural/cultural conditioning → social interaction → structural/cultural elaboration **or stasis** — the cycle doesn't always move forward) is built specifically to trace how agents reshape the very structures that conditioned them. **Confidence on this one is Medium-High, not verified against the source text directly** (research doc 13: "I did not read Realist Social Theory") — cite the cycle itself, not a claim about how faithfully it's being applied, and don't take this as adopting critical realism as CiC's philosophy of history, only the one sequence. Add a fourth documentation layer per force — what changed in the world's own conditions as a result of living through it, including the real possibility that nothing changed — modeled on that sequence, not invented from scratch. The lexicon's "Ecological Function" section genuinely reveals culture, not just definition, in 26 of 38 surveyed chunks (68%) — but that average hides a real, mechanically-explained 3x spread: Desert's entries are verb-shaped ("sustains," "governs"), which forces a direction, and hit 9/9 genuine; Hieronymian-Ascetic-Literary's are noun-shaped ("anchors G3"), which can label importance without ever saying what leans on what, and hit 3/9. That's a concrete, actionable schema constraint the averaged number drops — and either way, the section is read by nothing downstream. Compare "Related-Terms," the same class of relational information, which was structured and indexed, and became the backbone of a real analytical finding. The difference was never quality. It was whether anything downstream could query it.

## 6. The eight jobs every piece of information must serve

Organize the schema around these explicitly. A field may serve more than one job at once — that's fine. The failure mode is a job with *no* field responsible for it, left to memory, the way "which sources a Representative may draw from" was an unwritten convention until a Representative reached for the wrong world's source and broke it.

- **Rigor/grounding** — is this claim true, how sure are we, per the confidence vocabulary.
- **Repository/reference** — browsable and queryable on its own, for a human to look something up without a conversation happening.
- **Representative voice-construction** — what a Representative may actually draw from, and how, when speaking. Distinct from rigor: a source can be well-attested and still be the wrong one for this Representative to reach for.
- **Runtime retrieval** — findable at the right moment in a live conversation. A timing job, not a truth job.
- **Cross-reference/consistency** — how this relates to other terms, other worlds, other gravities, so a fix in one place doesn't leave three related places stale.
- **Participant-facing translation** — making the same underlying content legible to someone with no background, without touching the underlying claim.
- **Anachronism boundary** — whether this belongs to this world's own era at all.
- **Contestation/pressure-holding** — what this world actually contests, resists, or refuses to concede when pushed, distinct from what it merely states about itself. §2's own headline finding (research doc 10) is that a voice stating its character while failing to *enact* it — especially by folding under disagreement — is a realness failure and a faithfulness failure at the same moment. No other job above owns this. Leaving it out would mean this schema reproduces, in its own design, the exact failure mode this section already names: "a job with no field responsible for it."

## 7. Adopt, don't reinvent — what already exists in the wild, and one warning that cuts against this brief's own proposal

Full evidence and verdicts in research doc 09, which renders every verdict as one of three values — TRANSFERS CLEANLY, NEEDS ADAPTATION, DOESN'T APPLY — not a simple adopt/reject. The most load-bearing items from the first two columns are summarized below — not the complete table, read doc 09 directly for that. The third column (DOESN'T APPLY) isn't summarized below at all, but is real and worth knowing before designing: per-individual affect states (emotion sprites, avatar/voice rendering), key-value demographic personas, and Character-LLM-style "experience reconstruction" (training on a specific historical figure's profile) all carry DOESN'T APPLY — CiC's collective-voice commitment rules out the individual-figure premise these techniques are built on. Read doc 09 directly for the full table.

**Read this one first.** It's the single most important verdict in doc 09, and it cuts directly against §5c/§5d's proposal to make more lexicon and relational material reachable at speech time: with original, verbatim personas, models "unwittingly repeat profile information either verbatim or with significant word overlap" (PersonaChat, ACL 2018). **A world's own distinctive vocabulary in the retrieval/persona slot is the highest-parroting-risk configuration there is** — TRANSFERS CLEANLY, and is a warning, not an endorsement. Whatever this redesign does to make more material reachable, it needs an explicit guard against parroting instead of voice, not just more reachable material.

**What transfers cleanly — mechanics, not content:**
- **Keyword/embedding-triggered, budgeted, priority-ranked knowledge entries.** Every AI persona-building tool that solves "give a voice the right fact at the right moment" converges on the same pattern, and every implementation calls it *World* Info, not persona info. This directly targets the retrieval-specificity gap found in §5c.
- **Explicit permanence/eviction ranking on every field.** The most reused persona-building spec in the industry splits fields into "always present" versus "prune first under token pressure," and production systems tag every assembled prompt piece with an explicit priority for exactly this. CiC already has a version of this in the data, not just as an industry pattern to adopt — the `Quick Meaning` field (§5c) is exactly this signal, and the runtime silently drops it before the parser ever reaches it. That's a plumbing defect on top of a correct design, not a missing concept. Schema this explicitly as two separate per-field attributes, not one merged value: `eviction_priority` (what gets dropped first under token pressure) and `cache_stability` (whether the field can change turn-to-turn without breaking the cached prefix) — the two can pull opposite ways, since a high-value field that changes every turn is a caching liability even though it's an eviction-priority asset.
- **Situation-conditioned trait intensity, not a fixed character.** Model voice as named traits whose intensity varies by situation (the same tradition speaks differently about death than about food), scored against a fixed rubric.
- **The SPEAKING model** (Setting, Participants, Ends, Act sequence, Key, Instrumentalities, Norms, Genre) — the one framework surveyed whose theoretical foundation already assumes a *speech community*, not an individual speaker.

**What needs adaptation — every identity-describing field, without exception.** `description`, `personality`, `scenario`, self-description fields, and exemplar-character fields all carry NEEDS ADAPTATION in doc 09, not TRANSFERS CLEANLY. The *slot* transfers; the *conventional contents* — individual biography, a self a world doesn't have — are exactly what CiC's collective-voice commitment rules out. Redesign what goes in these slots; don't reuse their usual contents by default.

**Demonstration dialogue — the evidence is not unanimous; don't treat it as if it were.** What *is* unanimous: every framework that treats example dialogue as first-class fixes the trait/adjective rubric first and judges dialogue against it — the rubric is the deliverable, the examples are calibration against it. The count is genuinely contested: Character.AI gives description 1.5% of the budget it gives demonstration (500 vs. 32,000 characters, weighted toward more); "3-5 examples" is Anthropic's general few-shot-prompting guidance, not persona-specific; BlendedSkillTalk runs on two persona sentences; Microsoft demotes sample dialogue to an optional "scratch pad"; IBM's persona checklist omits it entirely. Treat "how many, how curated" as an open question the rubric answers, not a number to import.

**Negative constraints — real tension with CiC's own record, worth carrying into the design rather than resolving here.** No framework surveyed has a working "things this voice would never say" field that functions well as direct model input; the pattern that recurs is an author/reviewer-facing rubric (Microsoft's "5 TRAITS WE AVOID," Alexa's IS-NOT lists), not a prohibition fed to the model. But CiC's own retest record complicates a flat "rubric-only" conclusion: research doc 08 found Desert's *soft*, hedged anti-fabrication instruction ("say so plainly... rather than inventing") "was not absolute enough to survive direct narrative pressure" and failed on retest, while more categorical, absolute guards elsewhere in the system have held. A soft, rubric-style instruction is not automatically safer or more effective than a direct, categorical one for a fabrication-class guard specifically — the new design should account for this rather than adopt "rubric, not model input" as a universal rule.

## 8. The concrete objectives this redesign must hit

1. **Build process.** One dataset-first, source-discovery-collection-and-rating discipline for primary sources, generalizing Doc_02's Source Registry pattern to every document type that currently fans out (lexicon, forces, gravity discovery) — the highest standard for discovering, collecting, organizing, and rating what's primary to the world. Discovery specifically means a reproducible search, not just an accurate one — research doc 14 found this project reviews source accuracy heavily but has never once audited whether a second builder would find the same sources, and recommends a STARLITE-style search-strategy record over a full PRISMA protocol: reproducible, not comprehensive, since comprehensiveness isn't the actual goal here. **And a prospective discipline, not just a disciplined one** — every verification gate in this project's actual history was built reactively, only after a specific defect was caught (research doc 04); this redesign needs at least one standard that exists before a world is built, not one written in response to the world that just broke it. See §10 deliverable 10.
2. **Three-level participant access.** The live conversation itself; an on-request plain explanation at a genuine, checkable reading-level floor; the full scholarly apparatus underneath — all three drawn from one dataset, not three separately-maintained representations that can drift apart.
3. **Full scholarly coverage.** Lexicon, stories, quotes, general referencing — everything a genuine scholarly library of primary and secondary sources would make available — organized from the start to do double duty: a real, browsable repository, *and* the material a Representative can actually speak from naturally, at the table, with other Representatives, not just with the participant.
4. **Representative-construction methodology, redesigned alongside the data it draws from, not left as-is on top of a new schema.** How a Permanent Prompt actually gets assembled from the dataset — what's always-present versus retrieved on demand, how voice examples are selected and how many, how the lens/synthesis work (§2) actually reaches this step instead of being cut before it. Informed directly by §7's external patterns.
5. **Facilitator governance, redesigned as its own methodology — not just "whatever data the Representatives have, plus a translation layer."** This covers the Facilitator's actual jobs: bridging between a world and a modern participant, translating without flattening, holding safety and rigor, and — for a multi-world table — deciding who speaks next and why. Two live bugs found the same night this research was compiled are direct evidence this needs real design attention, not incremental patching: an anachronism-bridge intercept that silently ended a multi-world round after one speaker regardless of how many worlds were seated, and a term-matching gap that let a universal question misfire as a narrow modern doctrine. Table dynamics — turn selection, cross-world vocabulary distinctness, forces/gravities actually informing how worlds engage each other, not just what each says alone — are part of this, not a separate concern.
6. **Real API/runtime cost, lower than today's, not just "acceptable."** Every part of the design should be checked against this directly — what gets retrieved, how much, in what form, for every turn. Where a fix already found serves both quality and cost at once (§5c), take it as one decision, not two competing ones.

## 9. The shape of the output

Not prose as the source of truth. One structured record per addressable unit — a term, a story, a quote, a force, a source, a gravity — each carrying both its structured fields (confidence, tier, retrieval triggers, related-terms, which of the eight jobs it serves) *and* its own prose content as a field inside that same record, not a separately-maintained companion document. Deployment artifacts — Permanent Prompts, lexicon chunks, indexes — are generated views over this dataset, not independently hand-authored documents kept in sync by memory. Nothing is quality-reduced by this. The depth stays, and should grow, because it finally becomes reachable instead of stranded.

**Every term record needs to hold a historically-layered sense, not a flattened one — this is the one piece of this redesign not to compromise on.** A world's own vocabulary can share a word with classical usage before it and modern usage after it while meaning something genuinely different in each — the same problem the field of conceptual history (*Begriffsgeschichte*) exists to solve, and the same reason patristic Greek needed its own dedicated lexicon (Lampe's *A Patristic Greek Lexicon*) rather than assuming a general classical one already covered it — an illustrative analogy from general lexicographic practice, not itself a claim checked against any research doc here. Structure this explicitly, not as prose buried in World Meaning: `period_sense` (the term's meaning strictly within this world's own conceptual universe), `prior_sense` (its classical or pre-existing sense, where one exists, stated so the contrast is visible rather than assumed), `modern_sense` (current usage, so a participant's own use of the "same word" can be checked against it — formalizing what the anachronism bridge already does live, as structured data instead of a runtime-only judgment), and `conceptual_distance_note` (why the senses diverge despite sharing a word). That fix covers *when* a term meant something; it doesn't yet cover *how* it relates to the world's other terms at the same time. The lexicon's stranded "Ecological Function" prose and the untyped "Related-Terms" list are both reaching for the same thing a term's real academic field already has a name and a working instrument for: a **semantic domain** (Louw & Nida's domain-based lexicon organizes New Testament Greek vocabulary by a term's relations to its neighbors, not alphabetically — the principle to adopt, not a specific lexicon to import: CiC's six worlds mix Greek, Latin, and bilingual vocabulary unevenly, so no single published instrument covers all of them, which is why research doc 13 recommends the organizing principle itself, not this specific lexicon, as what transfers). Add `semantic_domain` and a typed `field_relations` list — direction required, using the language CiC's own best entries already reach for ("presupposed by," "the precondition for") — as the structured, synchronic complement to `period_sense`. And name the specific hazard the whole four-field block guards against: James Barr's paired terms **illegitimate totality transfer** (reading a term's full range of attested senses into one occurrence) and **root fallacy** (reading a word's etymology into its actual use), both aimed at exactly this genre of reference work — research doc 13 hasn't read Barr directly, only the terms and their standard target (Kittel's *TDNT*), so cite the terms and that target, not a specific claim about Barr's own argument. **This whole four-field block expands a world's own vocabulary surface at exactly the spot §7 names as highest-parroting-risk — connect the two rather than treating them separately: whatever guards §7 asks for need to cover these fields specifically, not just the retrieval slot in general.**

**One field name worth getting right across every record type, not just terms.** CiC already draws a sharp line between a world's own vocabulary and the Facilitator's comparative, translating vocabulary — under several different improvised names in different places (a Representative's "Total Embeddedness," a drift signal for slipping into scholarly framing, the standing rule that the world glossary belongs to the world and the modern glossary belongs to the Facilitator). That line has a real name: **emic** (the world's own categories) versus **etic** (the analyst's comparative ones). Add `register: emic / etic / emic-unavailable` wherever it's relevant, and let it double as the honest answer for cases where a world genuinely never had its own word for something, rather than letting a builder's stand-in word quietly become the world's own.

**One requirement for how these records get used in conversation, not just how they're structured.** Every record needs an implicit or derived grounding criterion — how sure the design needs to be that a Representative or participant actually understood a given piece before moving on, not just whether it was said. A term whose `conceptual_distance_note` shows a sharp then-vs-now gap is a high-criterion case in exactly this sense; the field already being added above is the input this needs, not a new one.

**One job from §6 has no field anywhere above, and it needs one.** Every other of the eight jobs picked up at least one new field in this section; contestation/pressure-holding (job 8) hasn't. Add a field — `contested_claims` or equivalent — recording what a world actually holds firm on when challenged versus what it concedes, keyed to the same distinctions §10 deliverable 9's held-position/concession-rate metric needs data for. Without it, job 8 is served only by a runtime mechanism and a metric, never by a record — the exact failure mode §6 itself warns against.

**Every sourced record — a source itself, but also a term, a story, or a quote that draws on one — should be organized around real academic categories, not improvised ones.** Research doc 12 surveyed how top research libraries, digital-humanities projects, and historical method actually organize source data, checked directly against CiC's own existing apparatus rather than a strawman. It found CiC already exceeds several real standards (per-claim source traceability stricter than TEI requires; an affirmative duty to name missing voices that the field's own dominant content standard, DACS, doesn't impose) alongside real, named gaps — highest-severity is no attribution-status field (genuine / dubia / spuria — the *Clavis Patrum Graecorum*'s per-author marking convention; doc 12 rates this Medium confidence, sourced to Brepols/Corpus Christianorum project pages rather than the printed Clavis itself, so verify the exact editorial language before citing it as settled), which CiC's own data already needed and handled correctly in prose, with no field to make the judgment queryable for the next builder. Build the schema from doc 12's field-level recommendations (attribution status, level of description, genre/form, a split confidence axis, and more) directly, rather than from a fresh guess at category names — and render citations to a real convention (Chicago/Turabian for historical sources: edition and translator named, secondhand citation disclosed as "quoted in" — doc 12 also rates this finding Medium confidence), not an ad hoc format. Two specific fields carry the highest cost if left out: `attribution_status` (genuine / dubia / spuria — independent of source type, the field CiC's own Syriac registry already needed and got right in prose, per above), and `discovery_channel` (field-bibliography, snowballed from another source, builder's own prior knowledge, and so on) — a companion research pass (doc 14) found source *discovery* is reviewed heavily for accuracy but never once audited for whether a search was reproducible, and discovery channel itself has never been recorded field-by-field, so no distribution can be measured yet. What can be said is doc 14's own characterization, not a measured concentration: the one real live-fabrication pattern found in this project's own record is exactly the kind doc 14 says the recall channel produces, when it appears at all. Split `Confidence` itself into three axes rather than one value — `citation_specificity`, `verification_state`, and `evidentiary_weight` — since today's single grade quietly conflates how precisely a source is pinned down with how thoroughly it was checked with how much weight it can actually carry for a given claim.

**Operational/variable parameters are not content records, and the same discipline applies to them separately.** Token budgets, turn caps, table-size ceilings, cost thresholds, and similar tunable values need one canonical, editable location — separate from the content dataset above — with every world document, governance document, and template pointing to it rather than restating the value inline. This isn't hypothetical: the table-size ceiling already exists as three different live numbers across current Facilitator Governance documents (§3) precisely because it was never centralized. One place to change a number, not several documents to hunt down and edit in sync. (Eviction priority and cache stability are a related but separate discipline — those are per-field schema attributes, not operational parameters; see §7.)

**One governance lesson from research doc 10, directly relevant to "generated views":** treat a Representative's voice as a versioned artifact, not something safely regenerated in place. A real prompt-guidance change was found to alter personality, not just formatting — and the industry lesson doc 10 cites (Replika, and independently a 2026 model-rollout backlash) is that users reject an "improved" personality even when it's objectively better, because their relationship was with the specific voice, not with "a better AI." Before a regenerated Permanent Prompt reaches a returning participant, run a continuity-regression check — same probes, old version vs. new, diff the actual voice — not just a quality check against the new dataset in isolation.

**The fields above, consolidated — this is the minimum, not the full schema §10 deliverable 1 still owes:**

| Field | Job(s) it serves (§6) | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| `period_sense` / `prior_sense` / `modern_sense` | Rigor, Anachronism boundary, Participant-facing translation | A term's meaning inside this world, before it, and now — kept distinct, not flattened |
| `conceptual_distance_note` | Anachronism boundary, Participant-facing translation | Why the senses diverge despite sharing a word |
| `semantic_domain` / `field_relations` | Cross-reference/consistency | A term's place among its neighbors, not alphabetical order |
| `register` (emic / etic / emic-unavailable) | Representative voice-construction, Anachronism boundary | Whether a word is the world's own or the Facilitator's comparative vocabulary |
| `contested_claims` | Contestation/pressure-holding | What a world holds firm on under challenge versus what it concedes |
| `attribution_status` (genuine / dubia / spuria) | Rigor/grounding, Repository/reference | Authorship reliability, independent of source type |
| `discovery_channel` | Rigor/grounding, Cross-reference/consistency | How a source was found — field-bibliography, snowballed, recall — so a search is reproducible |
| `citation_specificity` / `verification_state` / `evidentiary_weight` | Rigor/grounding | The three things today's single `Confidence` grade currently conflates |
| `eviction_priority` / `cache_stability` (§7) | Runtime retrieval | What drops first under token pressure, and whether a field can safely vary turn-to-turn |

## 10. Two passes — this brief is Pass 1 only

**§10 is the actual output contract.** §6, §7, §8, and §9 inform it; this section defines what "done" means. Neither pass asks Fable to write final schema files, rewrite the actual templates, migrate real world data, or produce ready-to-ship artifacts. Pass 1 thinks through the whole system's complexity at once — something no single build-process pass, representative-construction pass, or facilitator-governance pass has ever done together. Pass 2, later, turns the result into something buildable.

**What Pass 1's output should actually be:** a design document, written for Mark and for the later Pass 2 thread to work from — not code, not schema files, not patches to existing documents. Prose plus concrete field-level specification tables where a schema is being defined. Long enough to hold the interdependencies honestly; no target length beyond that. Where a claim from this brief or the research is load-bearing for a design decision, verify it against the primary source and say what you checked.

### Pass 1 (this thread) — the design

Needs to hold the full complexity and interdependency of the system, not treat any piece in isolation:

1. A concrete schema/field specification per record type, explicitly mapped to the eight jobs in §6 — including a search-strategy record for source discovery (§8 objective 1's reproducible-search requirement): what was searched, where, and when, not just what was found and how well it checked out. §9's `discovery_channel` field records the outcome of a search; this deliverable is the record of the search itself.
2. **A design for three-level participant access and a browsable repository** (§8 objectives 2 and 3), drawn from the one dataset in #1: the live conversation itself, an on-request plain-language explanation at the Article 30 reading-level floor, and the full scholarly apparatus underneath, plus a queryable/browsable view of the same records for a human looking something up with no conversation happening. All three levels and the repository view are generated from the same records, not separately-maintained representations that can drift apart. **This deliverable must specify per-level content, not just that three levels exist.** Level 1 (conversation) draws on `period_sense` only, never `modern_sense` or raw citation apparatus. Level 2 (on-request plain explanation) states `period_sense` plainly, plus a plain-language confidence statement and the `conceptual_distance_note` wherever a then-vs-now gap exists — the exact place a participant needs to be told a word meant something different then. Level 3 (full scholarly apparatus) is the complete record — attribution status, edition and translation, evidentiary weight, **Author Gravity** (already at real academic standard per doc 12 — an existing CiC strength doc 12 uses as its own baseline, not something doc 12 is recommending be added), plus everything else doc 12's field-level schema actually recommends — presented as a real scaffold (doc 12 names the Library of Congress's Observe→Reflect→Question model as a tested shape for this, sitting above Level 3 with the full registry row reachable beneath it, not as Level 3's own content), not a raw field dump.
3. A build-process methodology — template plus verification gate — for each document type currently prone to fan-out drift (§5a). **Two ready-made answers already exist in this project — build from them rather than inventing a gate from nothing:** the Permanent Prompt Template's Final Assembly Instruction (a numeric Flesch-Kincaid check plus a separate register-fidelity check) and Construction Framework V7.4's Validation Protocol Rigor (two independent trials, held-out probes, blind grading). Neither is universal today; both are real, working answers to exactly this deliverable's own ask. **One more gate this deliverable needs, and it isn't a reordering of anything — checked against four fields (living-history first-person interpretation, worldbuilding/game design, multi-agent AI orchestration, Stanislavski-tradition actor training), nobody recommends reordering world→representative→table; the methodology leans toward building context before persona or is silent about it (research doc 17) — though doc 17 itself flags that this isn't unanimous even within worldbuilding/game design, where a real minority view argues for building world and character together, not world-then-character.** What's actually missing is a per-world Table Readiness Round before a world freezes: seat the newly-built Representative with one other, already-live Representative on a question where the two worlds genuinely diverge, and check specifically for cross-world vocabulary borrowing, anachronistic reach under multi-voice pressure, and whether the world holds its own position rather than converging. This isn't hypothetical — the world-build Construction Framework (V7.4) never mentions "table" once across its full text, and Facilitator Governance (V3.6) never mentions "forces" or "gravity" once either; only the Representative Construction Framework (V3.2) bridges the two at all, and only in five mentions of "table" across the whole document. And a real governance requirement (anchoring a cross-reference to another world's own name) was never checked as a per-world gate: multi-world testing happened repeatedly across the build — three-Representative live testing, a multi-world acute-distress test, the four-voice roundtable — but never once inside a world's own build loop, so this specific requirement's own violation slipped through all five earlier worlds and was only caught, then fixed once manually at Mark's direct request, after the sixth and last world's build. These governing documents currently share almost no vocabulary with each other; this gate is one low-cost way to start forcing them to. **One more real gap doc 17 names directly:** today's per-world gate loop is strictly forward-only, even though the methodology's own governing document says elsewhere that the workflow isn't strictly linear — the Table Readiness Round needs an explicit path back to an earlier step when it finds a real problem, not just a pass/fail at the end.
4. A representative-construction methodology — how a Permanent Prompt is actually assembled from the new dataset, replacing today's largely-hand-authored process.
5. A facilitator-governance design — safety/translation/rigor-bridging logic and table/multi-world turn-selection dynamics, built to actually read the new dataset's relational and cross-reference fields rather than working around them. **Three specific, measured requirements, not just general direction:** first, turn selection needs a direct-address rule — checking whether the previous turn named or clearly designated a specific world before running a broader selection judgment — a controlled measurement in a comparable multi-agent dialogue system (research doc 16) found this rule outperforms both of that system's own baseline turn-selection approaches at p<0.001; CiC's streaming selector implements neither baseline cleanly today, but shares their failure shape closely enough that the same fix applies. Second, the design needs at least one mechanism that confirms understanding, not only mechanisms that detect trouble — doc 16 ranks this its single highest-value structural fix, and a separate controlled study it cites (Roque & Traum's Degrees of Grounding) found it improves response appropriateness at p<0.01/p<0.05 — every drift/monitoring signal in the current system is negative evidence, and grounding-in-conversation theory's founding result is that negative evidence alone isn't enough to trust that two parties actually understood each other. Third, account explicitly for a real vulnerability in the model this system runs: doc 16 reports it reverses roughly a quarter of previously-correct answers when a participant simply pushes back ("are you sure?") — **doc 16 rates this specific figure Medium confidence, drawn from a fetch-summary rather than a direct read, so verify the number before citing it as settled; the underlying vulnerability is worth designing against regardless.** A Representative needs to be able to tell a grounded pushback from an ungrounded one and respond differently, using the same source-checking machinery the fabrication guard already has, not resist correction uniformly.
6. An explicit accounting of what from §4 is being preserved as-is, versus where a listed purpose is now being served by a different, better mechanism — and why.
7. A real, honest cost comparison against today's system — where the redesign is expected to cost less, and by roughly how much, not just an assertion that it will.
8. **A pressure test against real transcripts, not a hypothetical.** No saved transcript of the "what is faith" case exists anywhere in the repo — research doc 08 only analyzes the bug at the code level. Real transcripts do exist, but **be honest about their actual shape rather than assuming a large pool of real multi-world conversations to pick from — there isn't one:**
   - `CiC_Live_Safety_Testing_Script_2026-07-21.docx` (repo root) — 20 turns, 7 batteries, all 5 worlds at the time, both endpoints, including one real three-world table. This is the source of the "19/20 clean" finding cited in §4.
   - `World-Builds/Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation.md` — 22 parts, but only **PART II ("Four-Voice Roundtable")** is an actual multi-world conversation (four voices, 4 rounds); the rest are single-world adversarial-probe and rule-validation records, not roundtable transcripts. **Only on branch `CiC-Fable-Experiment`, not on `main`.** Read it via `git show CiC-Fable-Experiment:World-Builds/Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation.md`.
   - `cic-poc/backend/transcripts/` — 24 short real runtime sessions, useful for verifying retrieval/citation behavior at real conversational scale, not as extended conversations to test against: 23 of the 24 are single-world, and the one multi-world file is 2 worlds, 1 user turn, 7 messages total. Currently untracked in git — read directly from the working tree.
   - The per-world Phase 5 transcript files: real for four of six worlds, but not uniform. Desert-Monasticism has no Phase 5 file — its live-test transcript is `LiveTest_Transcripts_2026-07-11.md` instead. Imperial-Juridical-Christianity's "Phase 5" file is a *Boundary Testing Record*, not a conversation transcript. Check what actually exists per world before citing one.

   **The honest count: exactly two real multi-world conversation records exist in the repo** — the safety script's three-world table, and Cross-World Roundtable PART II. Run the design against both of those directly, plus at least one single-world transcript for contrast, and show explicitly where the new design would have produced a better result than today's system actually did, turn by turn, not in the abstract. Where a scenario resembling "what is faith" is useful, treat it as a specification of a known bug pattern (a bare universal question meeting a seated table), not a transcript that exists — and note that a good multi-world test question is one where the seated worlds genuinely diverge; "what is faith" may not be that question, and a stronger one from the two real records above may serve the test better.
9. **A measurement plan — the standing scorecard this redesign will actually be judged against, not just a design that sounds right.** Borrow real, external frameworks rather than inventing metrics from scratch, across six categories: **design** quality is a validation question ("are we building the right thing") answered by every §8 objective having a corresponding deliverable here, checked directly — the same completeness check an earlier adversarial review of this brief used to catch two missing deliverables before this version. **Build** quality is a verification question ("are we building it right") answered by extending the six-world defect-rate tracking already in §5a — one-round-clear rate, self-certification recurrence — to the new process, plus a real coverage number for source discovery (relative recall against a seeded gold-standard set, per research doc 14) that no world currently has at all. **Organization** quality is field-completion rate per required schema field, per world — the same measurement that already caught real variance from 7% to 100% across two fields, for three of the six worlds so far (research docs 01, 03), generalized to the whole schema rather than a one-off audit. **Retrieval** quality is Pass@k, recall@k, and MRR — research doc 15's proposed ID-based evaluation harness, chosen specifically to avoid a judge model and its own API cost, but not free: it needs a hand-authored golden set of retrieval cases first, roughly a day of code plus a day per world of case-writing. **Output** quality is groundedness — whether a spoken claim is actually attributable to what was retrieved, which is the field's own published name (AIS, Rashkin et al. 2023 — see research doc 13) for exactly what the fabrication adjudicator already checks — plus calibration accuracy under pressure: held-position rate when a challenged claim is actually supported by the record, and concession rate when it isn't, tracked as two separate numbers, not one. **Cost** is the sixth category, not folded into the other five — track real per-turn and per-conversation spend against the §2 baseline and the §10 deliverable 7 cost comparison, using the same real-rate, not-raw-token-count discipline §2 sets out. Specify all six as the baseline measurement Mark's build should run before claiming the redesign improved anything — Pass 1 is a design document, not code, so running it belongs to implementation, not this pass.
10. **A world-build completion standard — what "done" actually means, stated before a world is built, not discovered afterward by audit.** Every completion-variance finding elsewhere in this brief was found by someone auditing after the fact, never caught by a standing definition of done: Quick Meaning populated for zero of nine Desert terms, Author-Gravity compliance ranging from 7% to 100% across worlds, the World Facilitation Brief fully review-cleared for only 2 of 6 worlds, "table" appearing zero times across the world-build Construction Framework's full text. This is the gap research doc 04 names directly: every verification gate in this project's history was built reactively, in response to a specific defect already found, never prospectively. One document, checked at world-freeze, should state: which record types and fields (§9's schema, generalized per deliverable 1 above) are required versus optional and at what tier; which build-process gates (deliverable 3's verification gate, the Table Readiness Round) must show a real pass, not a self-report; and which measurement-plan numbers (deliverable 9) a world needs to clear before it's called finished — name *which* metrics gate a freeze now, but leave the actual threshold values for later, once real baselines exist; setting a number today would be exactly the fabricated-baseline risk deliverable 9 already guards against. This isn't a separate thing to design from scratch — it's the single reference every other deliverable in this section already assumes exists, stated once and explicitly, instead of implied differently by each one on its own.
