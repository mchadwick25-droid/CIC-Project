**What this document is.** The Step 0 Methodology's procedure begins with a "broad
survey… of historically and academically documented Christian movements within the
phase's stated scope, using standard historical/religious-studies terminology." This
atlas runs that broad-survey layer across *all* of Christian history in advance —
era by era, 70 CE to the present — so that (a) the Orientation Map has honest,
complete content from the entry screen onward, and (b) when the project opens a new
release phase, its Step 0 starts from a maintained candidate pool instead of a blank
page.

**What this document is not.**
- **It is not a Step 0 run.** Step 0 is a phase-level process run once per release
  phase, with citation-grounded Section A screening and Section B scoring. Only Era I
  below has been through that process (the Phase One Step 0 Conclusion, whose results
  are reproduced as ground truth). Every other era's entries are **pre-survey
  candidates carrying signals, not dispositions.**
- **It renders no floor verdicts of its own.** Where an entry carries a floor note,
  it uses the Methodology's own vocabulary — "plain-reading" observations (A1),
  continuity questions (A2), interpretive-fidelity questions (A3), hand-selected
  adjacent candidacy (A4), person-defined-movement questions (Criterion 2) — as
  *questions the era's Step 0 will have to answer*, except where the Methodology's
  own text or the Phase One Conclusion has already answered them.
- **It is not bound by the post-build documents.** Its governing instruments are the
  pre-build ones: Constitution Article 4 (Movement Scope), the Step 0 Methodology
  V1.0 (Sections A and B), and the Phase One Step 0 Conclusion. Deployment and
  runtime documents govern worlds after they exist; this atlas is about the landscape
  before any world exists.

**How each entry reads.**
- *Sourcing signal* — a preliminary read (rich / moderate / thin / uneven /
  hostile-source) on whether the movement's own inner voice survives: not histories
  *about* it, but its prayers, letters, testimonies, records, and daily texture — the
  material a Source Ecology (Doc_02) needs. A signal, not a B1 score.
- *Ecology signal* — whether what survives could plausibly support a lived world
  across the eight lenses, or mostly one lens (usually doctrine). A signal, not a B2
  score.
- *Floor note* — per the discipline above. Entries with no stated note present no
  plain-reading question.
- *Relations* — lineage and reaction lines the map will draw, confidence-marked at
  map-build time.
- Entries marked **⚑ living tradition** touch communities alive today; any build
  would hold the Vision's living-traditions distinction (historical window ≠
  present-day self-understanding).

**Era boundaries and titles are CONFIRMED (Mark, 2026-07-16) as the map's ten
pickable eras, numbered plainly 1–10 (Amendment E):** 1 The Early Church Era
(70–312) · 2 The Imperial Church Era (312–451) · 3 The Age of Monks and Empires
(451–622) · 4 The Early Medieval Era (622–1054) · 5 The High Medieval Era
(1054–1300) · 6 The Late Medieval Era (1300–1517) · 7 The Reformation Era
(1517–1650) · 8 The Enlightenment & Awakening Era (1650–1815) · 9 The Missionary
Era (1815–1906) · 10 The Global Church Era (1906–present). Phase One's window
(70–451, spanning map eras 1–2) is fixed by its own Step 0 Conclusion. **Atlas IDs
(I.1, II.5, …) are stable record keys — the Roman numeral is this document's survey
part, not the map era number** (survey Part I spans map eras 1–2; Parts II–IX map
to eras 3–10). Amendment notes written before this renumbering may reference the
earlier Ia/Ib/II–IX scheme; they describe decisions as made at the time. One standing caveat survives confirmation: map eras organize *reading*;
the window of any future release phase remains that phase's own Step 0 decision and
need not coincide with a map era. Era *titles* are user-facing: plain names a
participant will recognize (The Reformation Era, The Enlightenment & Awakening
Era), with the dates kept visible alongside. A companion
spreadsheet, `CiC_World_Atlas_Census_V0_13.xlsx`, carries this entire survey as a
filterable census (Eras, World Census, Status Summary sheets), including every world
the Phase One Step 0 identified but did not build, with its recorded reason. The apostolic age (c. 30–70)
appears on the map as context only: it is Scripture's own ground, and the project
deliberately begins where the apostles' generation ends.

## Map Eras 1 & 2 (Atlas Part I) — The Early Church Era (70–312) and The Imperial Church Era (312–451) — together, Phase One, **Step 0 complete**

The only eras with real dispositions. Everything here reproduces the Phase One Step 0
Conclusion (merged FINAL v2); nothing is re-decided.

### Deferred by Step 0, not dropped
- **Armenia and Aksum/Ethiopia** — real conversion-and-royal-establishment
  narratives; evidence supports periods and transitions more confidently than richly
  separable internal sub-traditions before 451. → Era II candidates.
- **Persian Church of the East** — clearest institutional self-identity matures after
  this window. → Era II candidate.
- **Antiochene exegetical Christianity (Chrysostom-centered)** — strongest candidate
  raised and tested; held out pending a validated primary-gravity contrast against
  World #8 that doesn't rely on geography or language.
- **Cyrilline/miaphysite Egyptian tradition** — self-definition as a distinct
  communion crystallizes after Chalcedon; "the natural first candidate for whatever
  phase picks up where this one's 451 ceiling ends." → Era II, first in line.
- Also on the record as live, strong, deferred: **Jerusalem liturgical-pilgrimage
  Christianity** (Egeria, Cyril of Jerusalem; strong material-archaeological record;
  open question is boundedness of community, not evidence); **Ambrosian Milan**
  (folded into #6, thin alone); **Tertullian's own voice** (a dropped voice, not a
  dropped world, since the #8/#9 split); **Priscillianist asceticism** (unresolved
  Section A questions); **Pelagianism** (clears the floor; ecology too thin to build —
  the record's own proof that doctrinal eligibility and construction viability are
  separate tests).

### Excluded — Criterion 1 (the doctrinal floor), a design effect, not an evidentiary judgment
- **Marcion / Marcionism** — two deities, non-material Christ; the Constitution's own
  paradigm exclusion case.
- **Valentinian and other Gnostic Christianities** — apparent-body Christology,
  pleroma cosmology; appear inside the worlds that defined themselves against them
  (A5), per the record.
- **Manichaeism** — co-original dualism; most scholarship treats it as a distinct
  universal religion.
- **Homoian ("Arian") Christianity** — for stretches of the 4th century, *the
  imperial church itself*, and for centuries after, the faith of the Gothic and
  Vandal peoples through Ulfila. Exclusion is a floor effect, not unimportance; the
  **open structural question** on the record is whether it enters through A4's
  bounded hand-selected mechanism rather than by moving the floor. The map shows it
  honestly either way.
- **Ebionite/Nazoraean current** — *contested-evidentiary, not a settled doctrinal
  exclusion*: the hostile heresiological record can neither confirm nor clear the
  floor. The map's copy carries that distinction.

### Excluded — Criterion 2 (person-defined movement; theology not the problem)
- **Montanism** — Nicene-compatible confession; excluded because its authority claim
  rests on three named prophets. Live question on the record: whether the Phrygian
  epigraphic record documents a wider communal ecology that should make this test
  yield.
- **Novatianism** — Novatian's *De Trinitate* is broadly orthodox; excluded as
  one man's rigorist stance and rival consecration. Redundancy risk vs. Donatism
  noted if ever revisited.

## Era 3 (Atlas Part II) — The Age of Monks and Empires (451–622) — *the parting of the communions*

*Step 0 status: not run. Everything below is pre-survey signal, not disposition.*
The era in which the church's great communions take their permanent shapes:
Chalcedonian, miaphysite, and East Syriac Christianity become distinct, self-aware
families, while the West enters the post-Roman kingdoms and monasticism becomes the
era's formative engine everywhere.

**II.1 · Cyrilline / Miaphysite Egyptian Christianity** (c. 451–642 · Egypt) ⚑
The communion that refused Chalcedon and built a parallel patriarchate — Timothy
Aelurus, Severus of Antioch's exile years in Egypt, a church increasingly worshiping
in Coptic and defining itself by costly fidelity. Named by the Phase One record as
the natural first candidate of the next phase.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (Severus's huge corpus survives in Syriac translation —
a transmission-history flag; Coptic hagiography; papyri give real ordinary-life
texture). *Ecology:* plausible across most lenses. *Relations:* continues Era I
Alexandria + Desert; contested sibling of II.2 and II.5.

**II.2 · Syriac Orthodox (West Syriac) Christianity** (c. 451–7th c. · Syria,
Mesopotamia) ⚑
The miaphysite church of Syria — Jacob Baradaeus's clandestine ordinations under
persecution, John of Ephesus's *Lives of the Eastern Saints*, a whole communion built
while officially illegal.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich, much of it inside-voice (John of Ephesus is
extraordinary ordinary-texture material). *Floor note:* miaphysite Christology is a
rejection of Chalcedon's *formula*, not of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan content
Article 4 states — the floor is the Creed, not Chalcedon; no plain-reading question,
but the era's Step 0 should state this explicitly since participants will ask.
*Relations:* heir of Era I World #7's Roman-side (Ephrem's Edessa); sibling-rival of
II.5.

**II.3 · The Church of the East under Persia** (c. 424–651 · Persian Mesopotamia) ⚑
Deferred from Phase One, now in its own window: the school of Nisibis (Narsai,
statutes surviving!), synods building an independent hierarchy, Abraham of Kashkar's
monastic reform — a self-consciously non-Roman Christianity organizing under a
Zoroastrian empire.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich and unusually institutional-plus-formational (school
statutes are formation documents almost nowhere else has). *Floor note:* the "Nestorian"
label is largely a heresiological artifact; its own confession (e.g., the synod of
486) affirms the creedal content — an A2/A1 continuity check the era's Step 0 runs,
not assumes, with Theodore of Mopsuestia's condemnation-and-veneration split already
flagged in the Phase One record as a named comparandum. *Relations:* direct heir of
Era I World #7 (Nisibis side).

**II.4 · Armenian Christianity after Avarayr** (c. 451–7th c. · Armenia) ⚑
Deferred from Phase One: the church of a people who fought a doctrinal-cultural war
of survival (Avarayr, 451, canonized in Yeghishe's history), translated everything
(the Mashtots alphabet century), and rejected Chalcedon at Dvin.
*Sourcing:* moderate (rich historiography and liturgy; thinner on ordinary-member
interior texture). *Ecology:* the martyr-national fusion is a genuinely distinct
formation logic. *Relations:* formed via Cappadocia (Basil's circle ordained its
early catholicoi) and Syriac Edessa — both lines documented.

**II.5 · Chalcedonian Byzantine Monasticism — the Judean Desert & Gaza**
(c. 450–620 · Palestine) ⚑
Sabas and the Judean lavras (Cyril of Scythopolis's lives are dated, named,
localized — near-unique documentary hagiography), and the Gaza school of direction:
Barsanuphius and John's ~850 surviving letters of spiritual counsel to ordinary
people, Dorotheos's discourses.
*Sourcing:* **rich — among the best inside-voice corpora of any era** (the Gaza
letters are real Q&A with laypeople, monks, bishops). *Ecology:* strong across
lenses. *Relations:* direct heir of Era I World #3; feeds John Climacus (Sinai) and
the whole later Orthodox tradition.

**II.6 · Early Benedictine / Italian Monastic Christianity** (c. 500–604 · Italy) ⚑
Benedict's Rule (with the Master), Cassiodorus's Vivarium, and Gregory the Great —
monk-pope narrating Benedict to the world — in a peninsula shattered by the Gothic
wars.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (the Rule is a formation document par excellence;
Gregory's 850+ letters give lived administration; Dialogues carry Tier-2/3 material
needing care). *Relations:* consciously receives the desert via Cassian (Era I
bridge); parent of the whole Latin monastic line (Eras III–IV).

**II.7 · Merovingian Gallic Christianity** (c. 480–620 · Gaul) 
Caesarius of Arles preaching to half-pagan congregations (his sermons survive in
bulk, aimed at ordinary people), Gregory of Tours's world of shrines and healing,
Radegund's double monastery and Venantius Fortunatus's circle.
*Sourcing:* rich and unusually *ordinary* — this era's best window on lay,
semi-Christianized rural religion. *Ecology:* strong Human/Community lenses; the
"muddy, saint-and-relic parish world" is a formation ecology no current world
covers. *Relations:* receives Martin of Tours's ascetic line; parent of Carolingian
Christianity.

**II.8 · Insular / Irish Monastic Christianity** (c. 450–664 · Ireland, Iona,
Northumbria) ⚑
Patrick's own two writings (a genuine first-person voice at the era's start),
Columba and Iona, Columbanus's rules and letters, the penitentials — private,
repeatable penance as a formation technology that reshaped the whole Western church.
*Sourcing:* moderate (real inside voices at the poles; thin in the middle decades;
later hagiography needs tier discipline). *Ecology:* distinctive — peregrinatio,
soul-friendship (anamchara), monastic federations instead of territorial dioceses.
*Relations:* contested scholarly line from Gallic/Eastern asceticism; parent of
Anglo-Saxon mission Christianity (III.4).

**II.9 · Aksumite Christianity** (c. 330–7th c. · Ethiopia/Eritrea) ⚑
Deferred from Phase One: the Nine Saints, the Garima Gospels (radiocarbon-dated
astonishingly early), Ge'ez translation, royal-church fusion at Aksum.
*Sourcing:* thin-to-moderate for interior texture in this window (inscriptions,
manuscripts, archaeology; richer narrative sources are much later) — the Phase One
record's caution stands. *Relations:* child of Alexandria (Frumentius consecrated by
Athanasius — documented); parent of Era IV–V Ethiopian flowering, where sourcing
becomes rich.

**II.10 · Georgian Christianity** (c. 330–7th c. · Caucasus) ⚑
Nino's evangelization traditions, the Jerusalem-linked liturgy (the Georgian
lectionary preserves Jerusalem's own early worship), early martyr literature
(Shushanik, 5th c. — the oldest surviving Georgian text is a martyrdom).
*Sourcing:* thin-to-moderate in this window; liturgical evidence unusually strong.
*Relations:* Jerusalem and Armenian lines; splits from Armenian communion at 607
(Chalcedonian side).

**II.11 · Nubian Christianity** (c. 543–7th c. onward · Nile Sudan)
The Makurian and Nobadian kingdoms' conversion (rival Chalcedonian and miaphysite
missions — Justinian and Theodora's competing embassies), cathedral culture at
Faras.
*Sourcing:* thin for inside voice in this window (substantially archaeological —
wall paintings, graffiti, later documentary finds like the Qasr Ibrim texts). An
honest "the assessment would weigh archaeology-heavy evidence against our
inside-voice requirement" case. *Relations:* child of Egypt (both communions).

**Era II adjacent register (floor-question entries, shown honestly on the map):**
- **Gothic and Vandal Homoian Christianity** (to 589 Toledo / 534 Africa) ⚑extinct —
  the Era I open structural question continues here at full strength: an entire
  post-Roman kingdom church (Ulfila's Bible, the Vandal persecution of Nicenes as
  documented by Victor of Vita) excluded by the floor as it stands; the A4 question
  belongs to whichever Step 0 first touches this era.
- **Aphthartodocetist and Tritheist currents** — intra-miaphysite controversies;
  appear inside II.1/II.2's own reconstruction (A5 pattern), not as candidate worlds.

## Era 4 (Atlas Part III) — The Early Medieval Era (622–1054) — *under new empires, from Ireland to China*

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
Christianity becomes a minority faith across its old heartlands under Islam, converts
new northern peoples, fights an internal war over images, and sends its longest
missions east. Sourcing is thinner and more institutional in much of this era — a
pattern the map should say aloud rather than hide.

**III.1 · The Church of the East on the Silk Road** (c. 635–1000+ · Persia, Central
Asia, Tang China) ⚑
Timothy I debating in the caliph's court and ordaining metropolitans for the Turks;
the Xi'an stele (781) and the Dunhuang/Turfan Christian texts; monastic Christianity
in Sogdian, Uyghur, and Chinese.
*Sourcing:* uneven — spectacular fragments (a real inside-voice miracle at the edges)
over long silent stretches; a Source Ecology would have to be honest about how much
is reconstruction between fragments. *Ecology:* mission-frontier formation, unique.
*Relations:* continuation of II.3; the map's longest single lineage line (Edessa →
Xi'an).

**III.2 · Melkite Arabic Christianity** (c. 700–1000 · Palestine, Syria) ⚑
The first church to think in Arabic: John of Damascus at Mar Saba (the last Father of
the Greek East writing inside the caliphate), Theodore Abu Qurrah's Arabic apologetics,
the Palestinian monasteries translating their faith into the new empire's tongue.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (substantial corpora; strong monastic anchor).
*Ecology:* dhimmi-existence formation — faith under negotiated subordination — is a
distinct and deeply relevant formation logic. *Relations:* heir of II.5; sibling of
III.3.

**III.3 · Coptic Christianity under early Islam** (c. 642–1000 · Egypt) ⚑
The *History of the Patriarchs*, waves of martyrdom and mass conversion pressure,
monasticism holding the communion's spine (Wadi Natrun), the slow shift from Coptic
toward Arabic.
*Sourcing:* moderate (institutional chronicle strong; ordinary interior texture
thinner in this window than before or after). *Relations:* continuation of II.1.

**III.4 · Anglo-Saxon Christianity** (c. 597–800 · England) 
Bede's world: Canterbury and Iona lines converging (Whitby, 664), Cuthbert,
Lindisfarne, Hilda's double monastery, Caedmon's vernacular hymn — then Boniface
exporting it all to the continent.
*Sourcing:* rich for the era (Bede is exceptional; letters of Boniface's circle
include women's correspondence — Leoba, Eadburh). *Ecology:* strong; conversion-age
formation with both Roman and Irish DNA visibly contending. *Relations:* child of
II.6 + II.8; parent of III.5.

**III.5 · Carolingian Reform Christianity** (c. 750–900 · Francia) 
Alcuin, Benedict of Aniane's monastic standardization, the *correctio* — an empire
trying to make a whole society's worship, texts, and learning uniform and right.
*Sourcing:* rich institutionally; ordinary voice mostly inferred (capitularies tell
us what people were told, less what they felt). *Ecology:* top-down formation-by-
reform; distinct, though a Step 0 would test whether it's a lived world or a policy
world. *Relations:* child of III.4 + II.7; parent of IV-era Cluny.

**III.6 · Iconophile Byzantine Monasticism** (c. 726–843 · Constantinople, Bithynia) ⚑
The Studite world: Theodore the Studite's letters and catecheses, monastic resistance
under two iconoclasms, John of Damascus's image theology written from safety in the
caliphate, the empresses Irene and Theodora, the "Triumph of Orthodoxy" (843).
*Sourcing:* rich (Theodore's corpus is huge and pastoral; hagiography dense —
tier discipline needed since it's victors' literature; iconoclast side survives only
in hostile quotation — a named Author-Gravity/transmission problem). *Relations:*
heir of II.5/6-era Greek monasticism; parent of IV.6 and the Athonite line.

**III.7 · Cyrillo-Methodian Slavic Christianity** (c. 863–950 · Moravia, Bulgaria,
Ohrid) ⚑
Scripture and liturgy deliberately re-clothed in a new language (Glagolitic/Cyrillic),
the disciples' exile to Bulgaria, Clement of Ohrid's mass formation of a Slavic
clergy.
*Sourcing:* moderate (vitas, translations themselves as evidence, Bulgarian court
literature). *Ecology:* translation-as-formation — the era's clearest expression of
a conviction the project already cares about. *Relations:* child of Byzantine
Christianity; parent of III.8.

**III.8 · Kievan Rus' Christianity** (c. 988–1054+ · Kyiv, Novgorod) ⚑
Vladimir's conversion, Boris and Gleb's non-resistant deaths creating a native
sanctity type ("passion-bearers"), the Kyiv Caves community (its *Paterik* full of
named monks' struggles), Hilarion's *Sermon on Law and Grace*.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich just past this era's edge (the Paterik and chronicle
are 11th–13th c. compilations — tier care). *Relations:* child of III.7 and
Byzantium; parent of the whole Russian line (V.8, VII.8, VIII.11).

**III.9 · Mozarabic Christianity** (c. 711–1000 · al-Andalus) ⚑extinct-rite-alive
Christian life under Córdoba: Eulogius and the voluntary martyrs' crisis (850s) —
a community arguing with itself about whether provocation of martyrdom is
faithfulness — and the old Hispanic liturgy preserved under Islam.
*Sourcing:* moderate but vivid at the crisis (Eulogius and Alvarus are real inside
voices in a live communal argument). *Ecology:* minority-negotiation formation with
an internal tension the sources themselves carry — attractive for honest complexity.
*Relations:* heir of Visigothic (ex-Homoian, post-589) Iberia.

**III.10 · Cluniac Reform Monasticism** (909–1054+ · Burgundy → Europe) 
Cluny's liturgical maximalism, freedom from lay lords, the federated empire of
prayer, care for the dead (All Souls) reshaping lay piety.
*Sourcing:* rich institutionally (customaries, charters in quantity, abbots' vitae);
interior ordinary voice moderate. *Relations:* child of III.5's Benedict-of-Aniane
line; the establishment Era IV's new orders react against.

**III.11 · St. Thomas Christians of India** (attested by this era · Malabar Coast) ⚑
A real, ancient communion (copper-plate grants, East Syriac hierarchy, travelers'
notices) whose own internal documentation before the 16th century is genuinely thin —
liturgy, oral tradition, and epigraphy more than letters and lives.
*Sourcing:* **thin for inside voice in every pre-1500 window** — an honest flagship
example of "real and important, and the assessment would have to face how little of
its own early voice survives." *Relations:* daughter/partner of the Church of the
East (III.1).

**III.14 · Scandinavian Conversion Christianity** (c. 826–1104 · Denmark, Norway,
Sweden, Iceland) ⚑
Ansgar's missions, royal conversions, rune-stone Christianity — and Iceland's
conversion by communal decision at the Althing (1000), one of history's only
documented votes to become Christian. Nidaros grows toward its archbishopric.
*Sourcing:* moderate — rune stones and Adam of Bremen contemporaneously; the vivid
saga accounts are 13th-century retrospectives (tier discipline required).
*Relations:* child of Anglo-Saxon (III.4) and Hamburg-Bremen missions; parent of the
medieval Nordic church and, far downstream, VIII-era Nordic revivals. *(Added in
Amendment B, external review.)*

**Era III adjacent register:**
- **Paulicians** (Armenia/Byzantium, 7th–9th c.) — known almost wholly through
  hostile heresiology attributing dualism and adoptionism; a **contested-evidentiary**
  case in the Ebionite pattern (the record cannot confirm or clear the floor), plus a
  named victim of Byzantine forced deportations — the map should present the
  uncertainty itself honestly.
- **Bogomils** (Bulgaria, 10th c. onward) — dualist cosmology per both hostile and
  (later) internal-adjacent sources; plain-reading Criterion 1 divergence on "maker
  of all things," in the Manichaean pattern; formal disposition to the era's Step 0.
- **Iconoclasm** — not a candidate world but the inside of III.6's own conflict
  (A5 pattern); named because a participant will ask where the other side went, and
  the honest answer is "its own voice survives mostly in its enemies' quotations."

## Era 5 (Atlas Part IV) — The High Medieval Era (1054–1300) — *reform, renewal, and the new orders*

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
The Latin West's great creative explosion — new orders, new lay movements, women's
religious life at unprecedented documented scale — while Byzantium consolidates and
the East endures. Sourcing turns abundant; the harder question becomes uniqueness
between candidates.

**IV.1 · Cistercian Monasticism** (1098–1300 · Burgundy → Europe) ⚑
The reform that re-founded Benedict on simplicity and affect: Bernard of Clairvaux's
sermons on the Song of Songs, Aelred of Rievaulx's *Spiritual Friendship*, lay
brothers (conversi) bringing peasants inside monastic formation, exempla collections
full of ordinary monks.
*Sourcing:* **rich** — formation documents, letters, chapter statutes, and an
affective interior literature that is exactly inside-voice. *Relations:* reform child
of III.10; parent-influence on IV.4's spirituality and V-era mysticism.

**IV.2 · Carthusian Life** (1084–1300+ · Chartreuse → Europe)
The solitary-communal hybrid that "never reformed because never deformed": Guigo I's
*Customs* and *Meditations*, Guigo II's *Ladder of Monks* (lectio divina's classic
formulation).
*Sourcing:* moderate (strong formation texts; deliberately few personalities — which
is itself the formation logic). *Ecology:* narrow but deep; a Step 0 would test
whether the silence that defines it leaves enough breadth. *Relations:* desert
revival inside the Latin West — a conscious Era I World #3 echo.

**IV.3 · Canons Regular / the Victorine School** (c. 1100–1250 · Paris and beyond)
Priests living the common life; Hugh and Richard of St. Victor making the soul's
ascent teachable; the bridge between cloister and the new schools.
*Sourcing:* rich textually; community texture moderate. *Uniqueness question* vs.
IV.1 and the universities — a real B3 test. *Relations:* parent-influence on scholastic
and mystical lines both.

**IV.4 · The Franciscan Movement** (1209–1300 · Italy → everywhere) ⚑
Francis's own writings (unusually, the founder's authentic voice survives), Clare's
letters and rule (the first monastic rule written by a woman), the early companions'
memories, the movement's own agonized argument about poverty after his death.
*Sourcing:* **rich, with a famous Author-Gravity/tier problem** (layered and partisan
hagiography — exactly what the tier system exists for). *Ecology:* strong across all
lenses, including ordinary-lay reach through the Third Order. *Relations:* parent of
V-era Observants and Spirituals; sibling-rival of IV.5.

**IV.5 · The Dominican Order** (1216–1300 · Toulouse, Paris, Bologna →) ⚑
Preaching as the organizing gravity: study built into the rule from day one, the
first constitutional government in religious life, Dominic remembered through the
canonization inquest's sworn testimonies.
*Sourcing:* rich (constitutions, inquest records, early lives, Humbert of Romans on
formation). *Relations:* sibling-rival of IV.4; parent of women's Dominican mysticism
(V.2's German convents).

**IV.6 · Athonite and Comnenian Byzantine Monasticism** (c. 963–1300 · Athos,
Constantinople) ⚑
The Holy Mountain's federation of monasteries; Symeon the New Theologian's insistence
on *experienced* grace (just before this era opens, formative throughout it); typika
(founders' charters) as formation documents.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (typika, Symeon's corpus, saints' lives). *Relations:*
heir of III.6; parent of V.4 hesychasm.

**IV.7 · The Beguines** (c. 1190–1310 · Low Countries, Rhineland) 
Lay women living devout common life without vows or male governance — Europe's first
mass women's religious movement outside cloister: Hadewijch's visions and letters,
Mechthild of Magdeburg's *Flowing Light*, Marguerite Porete's book and her burning
(1310).
*Sourcing:* **rich in first-person women's voice — the rarest commodity in the whole
atlas**; institutional records (beguinages) survive too. *Ecology:* strong; formation
outside every standard structure. *Relations:* pastoral care links to IV.5/IV.1;
suppression pressure from the same institutions — an honest tension the sources
carry. Flagship candidate for the project's marginalized-voices duty (Article 20).

**IV.8 · The Waldensians** (c. 1173–onward · Lyon, Alpine valleys) ⚑
Lay preaching and vernacular Scripture, condemned not for doctrine but for
unauthorized preaching; centuries of clandestine survival; adherence to the
Reformation in 1532.
*Sourcing:* **hostile-source problem at the start** (inquisition registers — rich
detail, enemy pen) with genuine internal literature later (the *barbes'* books);
a named methodological challenge the map should state plainly. *Floor note:* no
plain-reading doctrinal question (the record's own point: discipline, not doctrine).
*Relations:* long line to VI.10/Reformed; the map's clearest "dissent that survived"
thread.

**IV.9 · Zagwe and Early Solomonic Ethiopian Christianity** (c. 1137–1300 ·
Ethiopian highlands) ⚑
Lalibela's rock churches as a "new Jerusalem," the deep Ge'ez liturgical world, the
eve of the great monastic flowering.
*Sourcing:* moderate here, **rich in Era V** (this is the honest hinge — a Step 0
might set the window later to catch the hagiographic and royal-chronicle wealth).
*Relations:* continuation of II.9; formal dependence on Coptic patriarchate (III.3).

**IV.10 · Maronite Christianity** (consolidating by this era · Mount Lebanon) ⚑
The mountain communion around the monastery of Maron's heirs, entering union with
Rome in the Crusader period (1182 tradition).
*Sourcing:* thin-to-moderate for early inside voice (much early history is later
reconstruction under polemical pressure — monothelete-question historiography is
itself contested). An honest contested-historiography case. *Relations:* West Syriac
liturgical family; unique Rome connection.

**IV.11 · Military-Religious Orders** (1120–1291 · Latin East, Iberia)
Templars and Hospitallers: monastic vows fused with arms — Bernard's *In Praise of
the New Knighthood*, rules, and the Hospital's serving-the-sick-poor ethos.
*Sourcing:* rich institutionally; interior voice thin. *Ecology question:* whether
this is a formation world or an institution — and the obvious hard question (holy
violence) would need the project's honest-tension discipline at full strength. A
Step 0 would face both squarely.

**IV.15 · The Georgian Golden Age** (c. 1000–1250 · Georgia) ⚑
David the Builder's and Tamar's Christian kingdom: the Gelati academy ("a second
Jerusalem... a new Athens"), Athonite ties through Iviron, the Davit Gareja desert
monasteries, hymnography and chronicle in a language the church had made literary.
*Sourcing:* moderate (chronicles, typika, hymn collections, hagiography).
*Relations:* continuation of II.10 — Georgia had vanished from the map after 622;
this restores the lane. Partner-world of Byzantine monasticism (IV.6). *(Amendment B.)*

**IV.16 · Serbian and Bulgarian Medieval Monasticism** (c. 1170–1396 · Balkans) ⚑
Sava of Serbia and Hilandar (1198), the Nemanjić royal-monastic fusion (kings ending
as monks, žitija — royal saints' lives — as a native genre), Studenica's typikon; and
Bulgaria's Tarnovo school under Evtimiy carrying hesychast literary reform, with Rila
as its heart.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (typika, žitija, chrysobulls). *Relations:* children of
Byzantium/Athos (IV.6); carriers of hesychasm (V.4) into the Slavic lands; parents of
Ottoman-era survival (VI.17). Fixes the Slavic lane's Kyiv→Moscow jump. *(Amendment B.)*

**Era IV adjacent register:**
- **Cathars / Albigensians** (Languedoc, 12th–13th c.) — dualist cosmology and
  docetic Christology per both inquisition records and the movement's own surviving
  ritual; the era's clearest Criterion 1 analogue to Manichaeism. The map presents
  the exclusion as doctrinal-floor design, *and* names honestly that this community
  was also the target of crusade and inquisition — exclusion from the conversation
  set is not endorsement of what was done to them.
- **Joachimite current** (from Joachim of Fiore, d. 1202) — a speculative-prophetic
  reading program more than a community; where it became communal (Franciscan
  Spirituals), it appears inside V-era Franciscan material (A5 pattern). Criterion 2
  question if ever proposed alone.
- **"Free Spirit" heresy** — substantially a heresiological construct (a scholarly
  consensus since Lerner); **contested-evidentiary**: the map's honest copy is that
  the "movement" may never have existed as its enemies described it.

## Era 6 (Atlas Part V) — The Late Medieval Era (1300–1517) — *crisis and devotion on the eve of reform*

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
Plague, schism, and institutional crisis — met by the most interior, vernacular, and
lay-accessible devotional literature Christianity had yet produced.

**V.1 · The English Mystics and Devout** (c. 1330–1450 · England)
Richard Rolle, Walter Hilton, the *Cloud of Unknowing*, Julian of Norwich's *Showings*
(the first English book we know to be authored by a woman), and Margery Kempe's
*Book* — an ordinary laywoman's spiritual autobiography, wailing and all.
*Sourcing:* **rich, first-person, vernacular, including two women's voices** —
Margery is possibly the single best "ordinary believer inside voice" document in the
atlas. *Ecology:* anchoritic + lay devout networks; strong. *Relations:* Carthusian
transmission (IV.2 copied and preserved these texts) — documented.

**V.2 · Rhineland Mysticism and the Friends of God** (c. 1300–1400 · Rhineland,
Switzerland)
Eckhart's preaching to Dominican nuns, Tauler, Suso's autobiographical *Life*, the
lay-clerical Friends of God networks, the *Theologia Deutsch*.
*Sourcing:* rich (sermons *as delivered to communities*, convent sister-books —
collective women's memory literature). *Floor note:* Eckhart's posthumous
condemnation is an intra-Catholic contest over language, not a creedal-floor
question; carry the tension, don't resolve it (A3 vocabulary applies only if a
community, not a preacher, is the candidate). *Relations:* Dominican line (IV.5);
feeds V.3 and Luther (VI.1 published the *Theologia Deutsch*).

**V.3 · Devotio Moderna / Brethren of the Common Life** (c. 1380–1500 · Low
Countries)
Geert Groote's lay communities without vows, the *rapiaria* (personal devotional
scrapbooks — formation technology!), schools that taught half of northern Europe,
Thomas à Kempis's *Imitation of Christ*.
*Sourcing:* **rich** (customaries, lives of the brothers and sisters, the era's
best-selling book). *Ecology:* strong; deliberately ordinary, urban, semi-lay.
*Relations:* parent-influence on both VI.1 (Luther schooled in its orbit) and VI.11
(Jesuit schooling debts) — a rare both-directions ancestor.

**V.4 · Hesychasm and the Palamite Synthesis** (c. 1300–1400 · Athos,
Thessalonica) ⚑
The Jesus Prayer tradition defended as real participation in God's energies —
Gregory Palamas, the councils of the 1340s–50s, Nicholas Cabasilas's lay-accessible
*Life in Christ*.
*Sourcing:* rich theologically; moderate on ordinary practice (though Cabasilas is
precisely a lay-formation voice). *Relations:* heir of IV.6/III.6 line; parent of
VII.8 and VIII.11 (Philokalia line).

**V.5 · Lollardy** (c. 1380–1520 · England)
Wycliffite vernacular Scripture and itinerant preaching driven underground:
trial-record depositions preserving ordinary artisans' and women's actual recorded
beliefs.
*Sourcing:* hostile-source problem (like IV.8) plus real internal texts (sermon
cycles, the Wycliffite Bible). *Floor note:* no plain-reading creedal question
(eucharistic and ecclesiological dissent, not trinitarian); Criterion 2 question
(Wycliffe-defined?) probably fails to bite — the movement demonstrably outlived and
outgrew him — but the era's Step 0 should run it. *Relations:* contested influence
line to VI-era English reform.

**V.6 · The Hussite and Bohemian Brethren Movement** (1415–1517 · Bohemia) ⚑
From Hus's death at Constance through Utraquist Prague and Tábor to the Unitas
Fratrum (1457) — a functioning non-Roman national church a century before Luther,
ending this era as a quiet brotherhood with its own bishops, catechisms, and
hymnals.
*Sourcing:* rich (confessions, hymnody, discipline records, correspondence with the
reformers at the era's edge). *Relations:* child of V.5's influence + native reform;
parent of VII.4 (Moravians are its literal renewal); the map's best "reform before
the Reformation" line.

**V.7 · The Ethiopian Monastic Flowering** (c. 1270–1500 · Ethiopian highlands) ⚑
Ewostatewos and Täklä Haymanot's movements, the Ge'ez hagiographic golden age, Zar'a
Ya'qob's reforming (and coercive) royal theology, the Ethiopian Sabbath controversy
settled at Däbrä Metmaq — an African Christian civilization at full documented
strength.
*Sourcing:* **rich** (royal chronicles, gadlat/saints' lives in quantity, homiliaries;
Author-Gravity care for royal-sponsored texts). *Relations:* continuation of IV.9;
the atlas's strongest non-European medieval candidate.

**V.8 · Muscovite Monastic Christianity** (c. 1340–1517 · Russian north) ⚑
Sergius of Radonezh and the forest monasteries colonizing the north; the
possessors/non-possessors argument (Joseph of Volokolamsk vs. Nil Sorsky — a real,
sourced, unresolved tension about property and holiness); Andrei Rublev's Trinity
as theology in paint.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (lives, skete rules, polemics; ordinary-peasant voice
thin). *Relations:* child of III.8 + V.4 (hesychast import); parent of VII.8.

**V.9 · Observant Reform and Popular Preaching** (c. 1400–1500 · Italy, Spain)
The Franciscan/Dominican Observance; Bernardino of Siena's recorded vernacular
sermon-cycles (taken down as spoken — near-transcripts of mass lay formation);
Savonarola's Florence at the era's end.
*Sourcing:* rich (sermon reportationes are extraordinary ordinary-facing evidence).
*Uniqueness question* vs. IV.4/IV.5 (is Observance a world or a phase of the
mendicant worlds?) — a genuine merge/split test in the Phase One Conclusion's own
style. Savonarola alone would be a Criterion 2 exclusion; Observance as a movement
is broader.

**V.10 · Late-Medieval Lay Parish Piety** (c. 1400–1517 · England as documented
case)
The world Duffy reconstructed: guilds, primers, processions, chantries, the whole
"traditional religion" of ordinary parishioners on the eve of reform — championed
here as a *candidate world of the ordinary laity as such*, the constituency every
other world reaches only at its edges.
*Sourcing:* rich but oblique (churchwardens' accounts, wills, primers — practice
more than interior voice; reconstruction leans inferential for feeling). *Ecology:*
this IS the Human/Community lens made central. A Step 0 would test whether "the
parish" coheres as one world (the Phase One record's boundedness question about
Jerusalem pilgrimage applies here too).

**Era V adjacent register:**
- **Taborite radicalism** (1420s Bohemia) — apocalyptic-communal extreme of V.6;
  mostly appears inside V.6's own story (A5) rather than alone.
- **Fraticelli** — Spiritual-Franciscan rigorists declared heretical over poverty
  doctrine, not the creed; honest copy: an obedience-and-property rupture, presented
  inside the Franciscan world's own tensions.
- **Adamites, Luciferians, and similar inquisition-record "sects"** — largely
  heresiological artifacts (Free-Spirit pattern); contested-evidentiary, named to
  model honesty about sources rather than to be built.

## Era 7 (Atlas Part VI) — The Reformation Era (1517–1650)

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
Every candidate here is documentation-rich; the Step 0 problems become uniqueness,
window-setting, and living-tradition care (nearly everything here has direct
present-day descendants). The era also opens the project's hardest future question —
how many of these can be built without the map reading as a Reformation catalog.

**VI.1 · Lutheran Wittenberg and Its Congregations** (1517–1580 · Saxony →) ⚑
Luther's own overwhelming corpus (plus Katharina von Bora's household, the *Table
Talk*), Melanchthon's schooling, the catechisms as mass formation instruments,
visitation records showing what villagers actually knew and did (often
disillusioningly — which is honest gold), hymnody as the laity's theology.
*Sourcing:* **rich at every level including ordinary** (visitation protocols!).
*Relations:* child of V.2/V.3 influences; defining rival of VI.11 and sibling-rival
of VI.2/VI.4.

**VI.2 · The Reformed Cities — Zurich and Geneva** (1519–1600 · Swiss lands →) ⚑
Zwingli and Bullinger's Zurich; Calvin's Geneva with its *consistory registers* —
week-by-week records of ordinary people's marriages, quarrels, beliefs, and
discipline, among the richest ordinary-life archives in Christian history; the
refugee-city dynamic (Knox: "the most perfect school of Christ").
*Sourcing:* **rich, with the consistory registers as flagship ordinary-voice
evidence.** *Relations:* parent of VI.8, VI.9, VI.10, VII.6-line; rival-sibling of
VI.1 (Marburg) and VI.3.

**VI.3 · The Anabaptist Movements** (1525–1600 · Swiss lands, Moravia,
Netherlands) ⚑
Swiss Brethren (Schleitheim, 1527), Hutterite community of goods (their own
*Chronicle*), Menno Simons's Dutch communities; the *Martyrs Mirror* and the court
testimonies of weavers and housewives defending believers' baptism to their judges.
*Sourcing:* **rich in exactly the inside-ordinary-voice dimension** (martyr
testimonies, hymns from prison, congregational orders). *Floor note:* mainstream
Anabaptism is creedally orthodox (the record's exclusions like Servetus are
different people entirely); anti-trinitarian currents that emerged nearby are in the
adjacent register, not here. *Relations:* radical child of VI.2's milieu;
persecuted by VI.1/VI.2/VI.11 alike — a tension the sources carry loudly; parent of
the whole free-church line.

**VI.4 · The English Reformation and Elizabethan Settlement** (1530s–1600 ·
England) ⚑
Cranmer's Book of Common Prayer forming piety through repeated words; the Marian
martyrs and exiles (Foxe's documentation project); the settlement's via-media
argument with itself (Hooker).
*Sourcing:* rich (liturgy, martyrology, visitation, wills showing belief-change
across decades). *Relations:* child of VI.1/VI.2 influences on native soil; parent
of VI.8/VII-era Anglican and dissenting lines both.

**VI.5 · The Scottish Reformation and Kirk** (1560–1650 · Scotland) ⚑
Knox to the Covenanters: kirk-session discipline records (even denser than Geneva's),
metrical psalms as the people's voice, the National Covenant (1638) as communal
self-binding.
*Sourcing:* **rich; session records are a world-class ordinary archive.**
*Uniqueness question* vs. VI.2 (same theology, distinct national-covenantal
formation logic — a genuine B3 test). *Relations:* child of VI.2; parent of
VII.6-adjacent Covenanter piety and Presbyterian worlds abroad.

**VI.6 · The Huguenots** (1550s–1629/1685 · France) ⚑
A Reformed church under the cross: synodical organization built while illegal,
psalm-singing as identity under persecution, the St. Bartholomew trauma, Duplessis-
Mornay's academies — then the long attrition to the Revocation.
*Sourcing:* rich (synod acts, memoirs, martyrologies, refugee narratives).
*Relations:* child of VI.2; parent of the refuge diaspora (VII-era) — the map's
clearest persecution-scatter line after Era I.

**VI.7 · Recusant English Catholicism** (1559–1650 · England) ⚑
The Mass in hiding: priest-holes, Campion and the mission priests, Margaret
Clitherow pressed to death, women running the clandestine household church.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (mission letters, trial records, devotional
manuscripts; women unusually visible because households were the church).
*Ecology:* minority-fidelity formation inside a hostile establishment — the Era I
House-Churches' own dynamic, sixteen centuries on (the map should draw that echo).
*Relations:* child of VI.11's mission arm and medieval English piety (V.10).

**VI.8 · Puritanism** (c. 1560–1660 · England, New England) ⚑
The "godly": conversion-narrative culture, spiritual diaries (Wallington's notebooks,
thousands of pages of an artisan's soul), household worship, the New England
congregational experiment requiring narrated grace for membership.
*Sourcing:* **rich in first-person interior voice at ordinary social levels — diaries
and conversion narratives are this world's own native genre.** *Relations:* child of
VI.2/VI.4; parent of VII.5, VII.6, and the evangelical line.

**VI.9 · The Dutch Further Reformation & Refuge Culture** (c. 1570–1650 ·
Netherlands)
The *Nadere Reformatie*'s precision piety, Dort's international synod, Amsterdam as
Europe's refuge (where VI.3's Mennonites, VI.8's separatists, and Sephardim shared
streets).
*Sourcing:* rich. *Uniqueness question* vs. VI.2/VI.8 — possibly a supporting
context rather than its own world; a Step 0 call.

**VI.10 · The Waldensian-Reformed Union** (1532–1655+ · Alpine valleys) ⚑
IV.8's line adhering to Geneva at Chanforan, keeping its valleys through the
massacres (1655, Milton's sonnet) — medieval dissent grafted into Reformed
Protestantism without losing its own memory.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich. *Relations:* the atlas's single longest continuous
dissent lineage (IV.8 → present).

**VI.11 · The Society of Jesus** (1540–1650 · Rome → the world) ⚑
Ignatius's *Spiritual Exercises* (a formation technology as explicit as any the
project will ever assess), the *Constitutions*, thousands of surviving letters, the
colleges, and the mission worlds it seeded (VI.13).
*Sourcing:* **rich to a fault** (the archive is oceanic; Author Gravity of the
founder-corpus needs the standard discipline). *Relations:* spearhead of VI.12;
rival of VI.1/VI.2 worlds; parent of VI.13's missions.

**VI.12 · Tridentine Parish Renewal — Borromean Milan** (1560–1610 · Lombardy →
model for the Catholic world) ⚑
Carlo Borromeo's Milan as the laboratory of reformed Catholic parish life: seminaries,
Sunday schools (Schools of Christian Doctrine — mass lay catechesis), visitation
records again showing the gap between prescription and practice.
*Sourcing:* rich institutionally + visitation ordinary-texture. *Uniqueness:* the
Catholic mirror of VI.1's parish question; a Step 0 would test it against VI.11
(institutional overlap) — the Phase One merge/split tests apply directly.

**VI.13 · The Mission-Born Christianities** (1549–1650 · Japan, China, Kongo,
Spanish America) ⚑
Not one world but a family the era's Step 0 would have to split honestly:
**Kirishitan Japan** (1549–1640s: a mass church, then apocalyptic persecution,
apostasy-tests, martyrdom, and two centuries of *Kakure* hiddenness — inside sources
exist: confraternity books, martyr acts, the *Kakure* oral texts); **Kongo
Christianity** (from 1491: Afonso I's own letters survive — an African king arguing
theology with Lisbon — through the Antonian movement's crisis, VII-era); **early
Chinese Christianity** (Ricci to the rites controversy; literati-convert writings
survive); **New Spain/Andes** (mass baptism, syncretism-negotiation, native-language
catechetical literature, Guadalupe).
*Sourcing:* uneven by region — genuinely moderate-to-rich in each named case, with
heavy missionary-pen Author Gravity, and real native inside voices in Japan and
Kongo especially. *Floor:* no plain-reading question (these are Catholic
Christianities); the deep question is voice — whose world is it, the mission's or
the converts'? That is a Source Asymmetry question the project's own Article 20
discipline is built for. *Relations:* children of VI.11/VI.12; parents of Era IX
global Christianities.

**VI.17 · Orthodoxy under the Ottomans — Parish, Millet, and the Neomartyrs**
(1453–1821 · Anatolia, Balkans, Greek islands) ⚑
The Rum millet: the patriarch as ethnarch, parish and monastery holding a people's
identity for four centuries without a Christian state; the neomartyrs — including
the documented pastoral practice of elders preparing would-be martyrs — and the
rhythms of a church that learned to endure as its whole vocation.
*Sourcing:* moderate — neomartyrologies (Nicodemus compiled them), parish and
monastic registers, travelers' accounts; ordinary interior voice thinner
mid-period, richer at both edges. *Relations:* continuation of the Byzantine
worlds after 1453; VIII.12's Kollyvades are its own late reform edge. Fixes the
Orthodox lane's three-century hole between Palamas (V.4) and Paisius (VII.8).
*(Amendment B.)*

**VI.18 · St. Thomas Christians under Portuguese Pressure — Diamper and the Coonan
Cross** (1599–1665 · Malabar Coast) ⚑
The Synod of Diamper (1599) forcibly Latinizing an ancient East Syriac church —
burning its books, documented by the burners — and the Coonan Cross Oath (1653): a
crowd swearing at a leaning cross never to submit to Jesuit rule, splitting the
community into the lines that become today's Syro-Malabar and Malankara churches.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich *for this window* (synod acts, missionary records with
hostile-pen gravity, the community's own tradition) — the window where III.11's
thin-early-sources problem resolves. *Relations:* continuation of III.11; ancestor
of seven-plus living Kerala churches; VIII.27's Mar Thoma reform descends from the
oath's line. *(Amendment B.)*

**VI.19 · Philippine Catholicism** (1565–present · Philippines) ⚑
Mass conversion under Spain becoming, over centuries, a genuinely Filipino
Christianity: the *pasyon* chanted in homes through Holy Week (vernacular
inside-voice by definition), Marian and Santo Niño devotion, cofradías — today
Asia's largest Christian nation, whose 1986 revolution knelt before tanks with
rosaries.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (missionary-pen gravity early; pasyon texts,
confraternity records, and devotional practice richly documented). *Relations:*
child of the VI.13 family, given its own row rather than a footnote — the census
previously carried the Philippines only via an adjacent-register entry, an
inversion this corrects. *(Amendment B.)*

**VI.20 · The Union of Brest and Ruthenian Greek Catholic Christianity**
(1596–1700s · Ruthenia — today's Ukraine and Belarus) ⚑
An Orthodox metropolia entering communion with Rome while keeping the Byzantine
rite — a communion born inside an argument (brotherhoods resisting, allegiances
contested, polemic as the native genre), holding East-and-West identity tension as
its daily formation.
*Sourcing:* moderate (synodal acts, brotherhood records, polemical literature).
*Relations:* sibling-rival of VI.21 — the same argument's other side; parent of
IX.21's underground church. Restores a whole absent communion family (Eastern
Catholics, 18+ million today). *(Amendment B.)*

**VI.21 · The Kyiv-Mohyla Orthodox Revival** (1615–1700 · Kyiv) ⚑
Brotherhood schools, Peter Mohyla's academy and confession — Orthodoxy answering
Reformation and Counter-Reformation pressure with its own confessional formation,
the Kyiv Caves heritage renewed as a teaching church.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (academy records, catechisms, brotherhood statutes).
*Relations:* the response to VI.20's union; grandparent of later Russian theological
schooling. *(Amendment B.)*

**Era VI adjacent register:**
- **Anti-trinitarian Reformation currents — Servetus; the Polish Brethren
  (Socinians); Transylvanian Unitarianism** ⚑ — plain-reading Criterion 1
  divergence (explicit rejection of the Creed's trinitarian content by their own
  confessions — the Racovian Catechism is candid); historically significant,
  intellectually serious, honestly mapped as outside the floor by their own words,
  with formal disposition to the era's Step 0.
- **Schwenckfelders** ⚑ — Christ's "celestial flesh" and suspension of sacraments; a
  genuine A4 test case (one named divergence, arguably not touching the
  never-waivable full-divinity commitment — precisely what the hand-selected path
  exists to weigh).
- **Family of Love / Spiritualists (Franck, Niclaes)** — interiorized-symbolic
  readings of creedal content; the era's A3 (interpretive-fidelity) test cases, plus
  Criterion 2 questions.

## Era 8 (Atlas Part VII) — The Enlightenment & Awakening Era (1650–1815)

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
Established churches settle; renewal goes small-group, experiential, and
transatlantic. First-person spiritual narrative becomes a mass genre — this era's
sourcing is disproportionately *interior*.

**VII.1 · The Quakers** (1650s–1800 · England, Atlantic world) ⚑
The Friends: Fox's *Journal*, Margaret Fell's Swarthmoor network (women organizing
from the start), sufferings meticulously recorded, silence-and-Light worship, early
antislavery discernment (Woolman's *Journal* — a moral-formation document of the
first rank).
*Sourcing:* **rich — journals are the movement's own native genre; meeting records
dense.** *Floor note:* a genuine, carefully-stated A1/A3 case, era-specific: early
Friends' confessions (Fox, Barclay's *Apology*) affirm Christ's divinity and the
core creedal content in plain terms while rejecting creedal *subscription* itself;
later Quakerism diversifies across the floor (evangelical vs. non-realist liberal
strands — the Methodology's own A3 distinction). Assessment would be
window-specific and confession-specific, exactly as A3 requires — the 17th-century
window and a 20th-century unprogrammed meeting are different candidates.
*Relations:* radical child of VI.8's ferment; parent-influence on abolition
(VIII-era).

**VII.2 · German Pietism — Spener, Francke, Halle** (1675–1750 · Germany) ⚑
*Pia Desideria*'s small-group program (collegia pietatis), Halle's orphanage-
university-mission complex, conversion narrative (*Busskampf*) culture, the first
Protestant missions (Ziegenbalg to Tranquebar).
*Sourcing:* rich (correspondence networks, narratives, institutional records).
*Relations:* child of VI.1 + VI.9 influence; parent of VII.4, VII.5-influence, and
via Tranquebar the Protestant mission line.

**VII.3 · Radical Pietism and the Brethren** (1690s–1800 · Germany → Pennsylvania) ⚑
Schwarzenau Brethren (Dunkers), Ephrata's celibate community, the emigration worlds
of Pennsylvania where Europe's persecuted small churches all landed and had to live
together — itself a formation experiment.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich. *Uniqueness question* vs. VII.2/VI.3 (a real
B3 test — believer-baptist Pietism sits between them). ⚑ living Brethren
descendants.

**VII.4 · The Moravian Church at Herrnhut** (1722–1800 · Saxony → global) ⚑
V.6's Unitas Fratrum literally renewed on Zinzendorf's estate: the 1727 communion
experience, choir-system formation by age and state of life, the lot, the
*Losungen* (daily watchwords, published continuously since 1731), Lebensläufe
(memoirs read at funerals — every member an author), missions to enslaved and
Arctic peoples decades before anyone else.
*Sourcing:* **rich to an almost unique degree — the archive is a formation-world
builder's dream (congregational diaries kept daily).** *Floor note:* none for the
creed; Zinzendorf's "blood and wounds" excesses of the Sifting Time are an internal
tension the sources themselves name and correct — carry it. *Relations:* renewal of
V.6; decisive influence on VII.5 (Aldersgate) and on VIII-era missions.

**VII.5 · The Methodist Revival** (1738–1800 · Britain → America) ⚑
Wesley's journals and the connexion: class meetings (weekly mutual examination —
formation technology at mass scale), field preaching, the hymn corpus as sung
theology (Charles's 6,000+), lay preachers including women, Primitive-vs-establishment
tension built in from the start.
*Sourcing:* **rich at every level** (journals, class records, thousands of member
narratives). *Relations:* child of VI.8-piety + VII.4 influence inside VI.4's
establishment; parent of VIII.2, VIII.4, and (via both) IX.1.

**VII.6 · The Evangelical Awakening in New England** (1734–1760s · New England) ⚑
Edwards's Northampton: the *Faithful Narrative*'s case histories (Abigail
Hutchinson, four-year-old Phebe Bartlet — ordinary people as the actual subject),
*Religious Affections* as discernment methodology, Stockbridge mission years,
Brainerd's diary.
*Sourcing:* rich (revival narratives were the era's own documentation project).
*Uniqueness question* vs. VII.5/VI.8 (transatlantic one-world-or-three test).
*Relations:* child of VI.8; parent of VIII.2's revival culture and the mission
movement (Brainerd's diary formed generations).

**VII.7 · The Old Believers** (1666–1800+ · Russian north, Siberia) ⚑
The raskol: Avvakum's autobiography (a masterpiece of first-person suffering
literature, written in his own earthy Russian), communities choosing exile,
self-rule, and sometimes fire over a changed sign of the cross; priestly and
priestless branches improvising church life without bishops.
*Sourcing:* **rich in inside voice** (Avvakum, polemics, community rules; later
merchant-community records). *Floor note:* no creedal question whatsoever (the
dispute is ritual fidelity) — a perfect honest example that the floor is the Creed,
not liturgical correctness. *Relations:* child of V.8's line; standing tension-mirror
to VIII.11's establishment renewal.

**VII.8 · The Hesychast Revival — Paisius and the Philokalia** (1746–1815 ·
Athos, Moldavia, Russia) ⚑
Paisius Velichkovsky's translation communities (Neamt), the *Philokalia* (1782,
Nicodemus & Macarius on Athos; Slavonic *Dobrotolubiye* 1793), starets-guided
formation spreading through Moldavian and Russian monasteries — the direct source
of VIII.11.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (lives, letters, the anthology-making itself as
formation evidence). *Relations:* revival of V.4 line; parent of VIII.11 and, via
*The Way of a Pilgrim*, of modern Orthodox lay spirituality.

**VII.9 · Port-Royal Jansenism** (1640–1710 · France) 
Augustinian rigor as a lived community: the Arnauld family, the solitaires' schools,
Pascal's *Provinciales* and *Pensées* from its orbit, the nuns who "were pure as
angels and proud as devils" refusing signature under duress, razed in 1710.
*Sourcing:* rich (memoirs, letters, polemics — a self-documenting community).
*Floor note:* none creedally (an intra-Catholic grace controversy; papal
condemnation ≠ floor failure — same distinction as V.2). *Relations:* Augustine's
line (Era I World #8's own long shadow) inside VI.12's church.

**VII.10 · The Welsh Revival Tradition** (1735–1815 · Wales) ⚑
Howell Harris and Daniel Rowland's seiadau (experience meetings), Griffith Jones's
circulating schools making a peasantry literate for Scripture's sake, William
Williams's hymns, Ann Griffiths — a farm woman whose memorized hymns survive as some
of the era's most startling theology.
*Sourcing:* rich for its size, in Welsh (a translation-layer flag), with ordinary
and women's voices unusually prominent. *Uniqueness question* vs. VII.5 (calvinistic
Methodism as its own world — likely yes: distinct language-world, distinct
formation genre). *Relations:* sibling of VII.5; parent of IX.2.

**VII.11 · The Antonian Movement in Kongo** (1704–1706 · Kongo) ⚑context
Kimpa Vita (Dona Beatriz)'s prophetic movement to restore the Kongo kingdom and
church — Christianity already two centuries African (VI.13) producing its own
prophetess, crushed at the stake.
*Sourcing:* moderate but real (missionary eyewitness Bernardo da Gallo etc.; hostile
pen — Author Gravity discipline). *Floor note:* Criterion 2 question (a movement
defined by one prophetess's revelation) *and* syncretism questions — but its deepest
value on the map may be inside VI.13-Kongo's own story (A5 pattern) as the tension
its parent world produced. An honest test of how the map handles African-initiated
prophecy long before Era IX makes it a global pattern.

**VII.16 · The Baptists** (1609–1800 · England, then America) ⚑
General Baptists (1609, Amsterdam — Helwys's plea to the king for liberty of
conscience, an early religious-freedom classic he died in prison for) and Particular
Baptists (1630s–40s); believer's baptism recovered within English dissent; Bunyan
writing *Grace Abounding* and *Pilgrim's Progress* from jail; church covenants and
minute-books documenting congregational self-governance at ordinary-member level;
the London Confessions; the family that would become Protestantism's largest.
*Sourcing:* **rich** — church minute books, covenants, Bunyan's spiritual
autobiography, association records. *Floor note:* none. *Relations:* child of
Puritan-Separatist ferment (VI.8), with Anabaptist-resembling convictions arrived at
largely independently (the connection is a contested scholarly line — carried as
such); parent of Carey's mission world (VIII.7) and of the Black Baptist churches
inside VIII.1. **The external review's most conspicuous omission, corrected.**
*(Amendment B.)*

**VII.17 · Colonial Latin American Devotional Catholicism** (c. 1600–1810 · New
Spain, Peru) ⚑
The criollo and mestizo devotional world after the mission moment: Guadalupe's cult
growing at once native and universal, cofradías keeping their own books, santos and
processions, Rose of Lima and Martín de Porres venerated by their neighbors in
their own lifetimes, the convent world that produced Sor Juana.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (cofradía books, canonization processes, convent
writings — women's among them). *Relations:* continuation of VI.13's American
branch; the piety IX.8's base communities later re-read from below. Fills the
250-year lane hole the external review found. *(Amendment B.)*

**Era VII adjacent register:**
- **Shakers** (Ann Lee, 1774–) — Christ's second appearing identified with the
  founder; plain-reading Criterion 1 + Criterion 2 divergences by their own
  confession; historically luminous community records — honest copy would name both
  the exclusion grounds and the extraordinary archive.
- **Swedenborgian New Church** — new-revelation authority and non-trinitarian
  theology by its own account; Criterion 1 + 2.
- **Quietist controversy (Molinos, Guyon)** — a spirituality contest inside VI.12's
  church, not a community; appears inside VII.9-adjacent Catholic material (A5).
- **Camisard prophetism** — persecution-born ecstatic prophecy inside VI.6's
  aftermath; likely A5 material within a Huguenot world.

## Era 9 (Atlas Part VIII) — The Missionary Era (1815–1906) — *revival, missions, and a globalizing faith*

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
Christianity globalizes under empire's shadow and against it; the era's honest
theme is entanglement — mission and colonialism, revival and respectability,
liberation and paternalism — exactly the tensions-held-as-tensions discipline the
project exists for.

**VIII.1 · The Black Church in America** (c. 1770s–1906 · United States) ⚑
From the "invisible institution" under slavery (spirituals, hush harbors, the ring
shout) through Richard Allen's AME (1816), Black Baptist associations, emancipation-
era institution-building, and the exodus-era church as the center of a people's
whole public life.
*Sourcing:* **rich and inside-voice**: slave narratives (with named editorial-frame
Author Gravity), spirituals as communal theology, conversion accounts (*God Struck
Me Dead*), autobiographies (Allen, Jarena Lee — a woman preacher's own account),
denominational records. Article 20's affirmative duty applies at full strength: the
record was made under conditions designed to silence it.
*Relations:* child of VII.5/VII.6 revivalism transformed in the praise house; parent
of IX.1 (Azusa's leadership and worship DNA) and the civil-rights church.

**VIII.2 · The Second Great Awakening & Camp-Meeting World** (1795–1840 ·
American frontier) ⚑
Cane Ridge, circuit riders (Cartwright's autobiography), the mourner's bench, new
measures (Finney), a whole society's conversion culture — and its schisms.
*Sourcing:* rich. *Uniqueness question* vs. VII.5/VIII.4 (revivalism as world vs.
era-atmosphere — a hard, honest B2/B3 call). *Relations:* child of VII.5/VII.6;
parent of VIII.3, VIII.4.

**VIII.3 · The Stone-Campbell Restoration Movement** (1801–1906 · American
frontier) ⚑
"No creed but Christ": Cane Ridge's Stone + the Campbells' *Declaration and
Address*, weekly communion, believer's baptism, a unity movement that (with grim
irony it documents itself) divided.
*Sourcing:* rich (journals like the *Millennial Harbinger*, congregational records,
debate transcripts — debating was the native genre). *Floor note:* an A3-shaped
check the era's Step 0 runs: the movement rejects creedal *subscription* while its
mainstream confessed the creedal *content* (Stone's own trinitarian reservations are
a named internal strand — Article 21 strand-level assessment applies, not
strand-by-strand exclusion). *Relations:* child of VIII.2 + Presbyterian line.

**VIII.4 · The Holiness Movement** (1835–1906 · N. America, Britain) ⚑
Phoebe Palmer's Tuesday Meetings (a laywoman's parlor as a movement's engine),
entire-sanctification testimony culture, camp meetings (Vineland 1867), the
come-outer churches (Free Methodists, Nazarenes, Salvation Army kin), women
preaching defended in print (Palmer's *Promise of the Father*).
*Sourcing:* **rich, testimony-native, women-prominent.** *Relations:* child of
VII.5's perfectionist strand; **the immediate parent of IX.1** — Azusa is
unintelligible without it.

**VIII.5 · The Oxford Movement and Anglo-Catholic Revival** (1833–1906 ·
England) ⚑
Tracts, Newman's departure, Pusey's persistence — then the second generation's
ritualist slum priests (St. Alban's Holborn, Dolling) and the revival of Anglican
religious orders for women (the first since the Reformation — sisterhoods nursing
cholera wards).
*Sourcing:* rich (tracts, memoirs, parish records, sisterhood archives).
*Relations:* child of VI.4's high-church strand; rival-sibling of evangelical
Anglicanism (VIII.7's home base).

**VIII.6 · The Salvation Army** (1865–1906 · East London → world) ⚑
The Booths' corps: uniformed holiness militancy among the urban poor, women
officers from the start ("Hallelujah lasses"), *In Darkest England*, brass bands as
liturgy, statistics kept on every soul and soup bowl.
*Sourcing:* **rich and ordinary-facing by design.** *Floor note:* non-observance of
sacraments is practice, not creed — an honest note, not a floor question.
*Relations:* child of VIII.4 + VII.5; the era's clearest embodied-mercy formation
world.

**VIII.7 · The Protestant Missionary Movement's Native Churches** (1793–1906 ·
India, West Africa, Pacific, China) ⚑
The era's real story is not the societies but the churches they seeded — and the
map should name them as such: **Serampore and Indian Christianity** (Carey's trio
plus Krishna Pal's generation of converts); **Sierra Leone and the Niger Mission**
(recaptive Christianity — freed-slave villages becoming a missionary-sending church;
Samuel Ajayi Crowther, kidnapped child to first African Anglican bishop, his own
journals surviving; the CMS's late-century racial betrayal of him — a tension the
sources carry and the map must not soften); **the Pacific islands' mass movements**
(Tonga, Fiji — native teachers did most of the evangelizing); **China coast
Christianity and the CIM** (Hudson Taylor's identification principle; Pastor Xi
Shengmo's opium-refuge churches — a Chinese-led holiness world).
*Sourcing:* rich but missionary-pen-weighted (Author Gravity + Article 20 at full
strength; native inside voices exist — Crowther, Xi, Pacific teachers' narratives —
and must anchor any build). *Relations:* children of VII.2/VII.4/VII.5/VI.13 lines;
parents of Era IX's global south worlds.

**VIII.8 · The Haugean Awakening** (1796–1850 · Norway) ⚑
Hans Nielsen Hauge — a farmer's son jailed for lay preaching under the Conventicle
Act — and the lay-led prayer-house culture (bedehus) that formed Norwegian ordinary
piety and, via emigration, the American Lutheran low-church strand.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (Hauge's own thirty-three books and letters; friends'
societies' records). *Criterion 2 check:* movement demonstrably outlived and
outgrew the founder into a broad lay culture — the test as the Phase One record
frames it would likely not bite, but the era's Step 0 runs it. *Relations:* child of
VII.2's line inside VI.1-heritage establishment.

**VIII.9 · The Réveil and Inner-Mission Awakening** (1815–1880 · Switzerland,
France, Netherlands, Germany) 
Geneva's revival (Malan, Monod), Dutch Réveil circles (Da Costa, Groen), Wichern's
Rauhes Haus and the Inner Mission (deaconess motherhouses — Fliedner's Kaiserswerth,
where Nightingale trained).
*Sourcing:* rich institutional + personal. *Uniqueness question:* one world or
national siblings? A Step 0 split test. *Relations:* child of VII.2/VII.4 lines;
the deaconess line is a genuine women's-formation world inside it.

**VIII.10 · The Restorationist-Apocalyptic Ferment → Adventism** (1831–1906 ·
N. America) ⚑
Miller's movement and the Great Disappointment (1844) — ordinary people's own
accounts of the morning after — and Seventh-day Adventism's consolidation (sabbath,
sanctuary, health reform, Ellen White).
*Sourcing:* rich (the movement documented itself compulsively). *Floor note:* a
two-part honest note the era's Step 0 must run: early Adventist leadership included
explicit anti-trinitarians (a plain A1 problem *for that window*), while the
denomination's own later confession (28 Fundamentals) is trinitarian — the
window-specificity rule (which candidate, which years) decides everything here.
*Criterion 2 check* re: Ellen White's prophetic authority is the second live
question. Both are questions, not verdicts. *Relations:* child of VIII.2's ferment.

**VIII.11 · The Optina Elders and Russian Ascetic Renewal** (1821–1906 ·
Russia) ⚑
The starchestvo at Optina Pustyn (Leonid, Macarius, Ambrose — thousands of
surviving letters of direction to laypeople), *The Way of a Pilgrim*, Seraphim of
Sarov's conversation with Motovilov, Theophan the Recluse's correspondence-ministry,
John of Kronstadt's urban eucharistic revival and diary.
*Sourcing:* **rich and profoundly inside-voice (spiritual direction by mail is
ordinary-life evidence by nature).** *Relations:* child of VII.8; the world Dostoevsky
put in a novel; parent of IX-era émigré Orthodoxy (IX.9).

**VIII.12 · The Kollyvades and Greek Renewal** (1750s–1860s · Athos, Greek
islands) ⚑
Nicodemus the Hagiorite and the frequent-communion controversy, the *Philokalia*'s
Greek side, new martyrs under Ottoman rule (with the spiritual preparation of
would-be martyrs as documented pastoral practice), Kosmas Aitolos's village
missions.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich. *Uniqueness/window question* vs. VII.8 (same renewal,
two empires — merge or split is a real test). *Relations:* sibling of VII.8; parent
of modern Greek monastic revival.

**VIII.13 · Coptic Revival under Cyril IV** (1854–1906 · Egypt) ⚑
The "Father of Reform": schools (including the first Coptic girls' schools),
printing press, clergy education — a millennium-old dhimmi communion renewing
itself into modernity.
*Sourcing:* moderate (institutional and biographical; a build might wait for the
richer IX.10 window — an honest window-setting call). *Relations:* continuation of
III.3; parent of IX.10.

**VIII.21 · Korean Catholic Origins** (1784–1886 · Korea) ⚑
A church self-founded by lay Confucian scholars who studied Jesuit books brought
from Beijing, baptized one another, and organized church life for years before any
priest arrived — then held it through waves of persecution (1801, 1839, 1846, 1866;
some ten thousand martyrs, Andrew Kim Taegon among them). A world-history singular
of lay initiative.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (martyrs' letters and trial records; the pre-missionary
church documented by the missionaries who finally reached it). *Relations:* child of
VI.13-China's literati Christianity at one remove; precursor-sibling of IX.2's
Korean Protestant story. *(Amendment B.)*

**VIII.22 · Laestadianism and the Nordic Revivals** (1844–1900s · Lapland, Finland,
Norway, Sweden) ⚑
Laestadius's revival among the Sámi and settlers — preaching that broke the liquor
trade's grip, the movement's practice of spoken absolution ("believe your sins
forgiven"), reading-house culture across the north — alongside the wider Nordic
inner-mission currents beyond Hauge's earlier awakening.
*Sourcing:* moderate (sermons, movement periodicals, later oral histories; the Sámi
dimension adds real marginalized-voice value). *Relations:* child of Pietism's long
line (VII.2) in the Lutheran north; living Laestadian communities today.
*(Amendment B.)*

**VIII.23 · Plymouth Brethren and Dispensational Formation** (1827–1900s · Dublin,
England, then global) ⚑
Darby's circles: the weekly Breaking of Bread without clergy, prophecy conferences,
and the dispensational reading system that — through Scofield's 1909 Bible —
reshaped how millions read Scripture; Müller's Bristol orphanages as the Open
Brethren's practical-faith signature.
*Sourcing:* rich (movement periodicals, Darby's vast corpus, assembly records,
Müller's narratives). *Floor note:* none; the person-defined check must run and
would likely clear — the movement demonstrably outgrew Darby into Open and
Exclusive streams. *Relations:* child of evangelical dissent; strong influence on
the Bible-institute world (VIII.24). *(Amendment B.)*

**VIII.24 · The Sunday School and Bible-Institute Movement** (1780–1900s · Britain,
N. America)
Raikes's Gloucester schools (1780) growing into a mass lay-teaching culture — by
mid-century a majority of English children attended — the American Sunday School
Union's frontier libraries, then Moody's institute (1886) and the network that
followed: lay formation technologies at industrial scale.
*Sourcing:* rich institutionally, with teachers' and students' memoirs. *Ecology
question:* movement-as-world versus formation-technology-inside-worlds — a genuine
Step 0 test, like VIII.2's. *Relations:* child of the revival lines; parent of
20th-century evangelical infrastructure. *(Amendment B.)*

**VIII.25 · The Student Volunteer Movement and Edinburgh 1910** (1886–1920s ·
N. America, Britain, global)
Mount Hermon 1886, the watchword ("the evangelization of the world in this
generation"), morning-watch piety, thousands of student volunteers sailing — and
Edinburgh 1910 as the movement's summit and the ecumenical movement's seed (one of
Noll's turning points the census previously lacked).
*Sourcing:* rich (SVM records, volunteers' diaries and letters, conference
reports). *Relations:* child of Methodist-evangelical lines and VIII.7's mission
world; parent of both IX-era ecumenism and evangelical missions. *(Amendment B.)*

**VIII.26 · Indonesian Christianity** (1861–present · Sumatra, Timor, the
archipelago) ⚑
Nommensen among the Batak and the HKBP — one of the largest Lutheran bodies on
earth, self-governing early — older Ambonese and Minahasa Reformed roots, and the
1965–71 Timor revival: a mass movement amid national trauma. One of the largest
Christian communities in the Muslim-majority world, previously absent from the
census entirely.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (mission records with gravity; the Batak church's own
records; revival testimonies). *Relations:* child of VIII.7's family; sibling of
IX.11's growth. *(Amendment B.)*

**VIII.27 · Northeast India Revivals and the Mar Thoma Reform** (1836–present ·
Kerala; Khasi, Mizo, and Naga hills) ⚑
Abraham Malpan's reform within the Malankara church becoming the Mar Thoma Syrian
Church — an ancient-rite church with evangelical reform, honestly extending the St.
Thomas lane — and the Welsh-mission hill fields swept by indigenous revivals (Khasi
1906, Mizo waves 1913–30s) that made these among the most Christian regions on
earth.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (church records, revival accounts, hymnody in local
languages). *Relations:* continuation of the III.11 → VI.18 lane; kin of IX.2's
revival family. *(Amendment B.)*

**Era VIII adjacent register:**
- **Latter-day Saints (LDS)** ⚑ and **Jehovah's Witnesses (Bible Students)** ⚑ —
  the Methodology's own A1 worked examples: confessions differing from Article 4's
  content on plain terms (stated there as factual confessional description, not
  value judgment). The map carries exactly that framing, plus Criterion 2 notes
  (founding-revelation authority structures).
- **Christian Science** ⚑ — non-realist reinterpretation of creedal content by its
  own texts (A3's clearest 19th-century case) + Criterion 2.
- **Christadelphians** ⚑ — explicit non-trinitarianism by confession; A1 plain
  reading.
- **Catholic Apostolic Church (Irvingites)** — trinitarian and creedally
  conventional (no A1 question) but restored-apostles authority structure raises
  the Criterion 2 question in near-pure form; historically fascinating, honestly
  noted as a test the era's Step 0 would run.
- **Oneida community** — perfectionism plus complex marriage under one founder's
  total authority; Criterion 2 and A3 both.
- **Spiritualism / New Thought** — outside the atlas's scope (not confessionally
  Christian movements in Article 4's sense); named only because participants will
  ask.

## Era 10 (Atlas Part IX) — The Global Church Era (1906–present)

*Step 0 status: not run. Pre-survey signals only.*
Christianity's center of gravity moves south and east; the Spirit-movement family
becomes the largest new Christian reality since the Reformation; and nearly every
candidate is a **living tradition at maximum sensitivity** — every build here would
lean hardest on the Vision's living-traditions distinction, and several involve
communities still under active persecution, where the project's honesty duties
include safety.

**IX.1 · Azusa Street and Early Pentecostalism** (1906–1920s · Los Angeles →
global) ⚑
Seymour's mission: interracial worship under Jim Crow ("the color line was washed
away in the blood" — Bartleman), *The Apostolic Faith* paper, tongues as missionary
sign, the movement's own rapid arguments and splits (finished work, Oneness, racial
re-segregation) all documented in its own press.
*Sourcing:* **rich and self-documenting.** *Relations:* child of VIII.4 + VIII.1
(Seymour's worship DNA); parent of most of this era's family; the Oneness split
belongs to the adjacent register.

**IX.2 · The Welsh Revival and Its Kin** (1904–1910 · Wales, Korea, India) ⚑
Evan Roberts's Wales (newspapers covered meetings verbatim), the Khasi Hills and
Mukti (Pandita Ramabai's praying women — an Indian-led revival at a widows' home),
Pyongyang 1907 (public confession culture founding Korean Protestant identity —
dawn prayer, Bible study by the thousand).
*Sourcing:* rich (revival journalism, mission records, Korean church's own
histories). *Relations:* child of VIII-era revival lines; siblings of IX.1 without
descending from it — the map should show revival as polygenetic, which is itself
honest history.

**IX.3 · The East African Revival (Balokole)** (1930s–1970s · Rwanda, Uganda,
Kenya, Tanzania) ⚑
"Walking in the light": public confession and restitution culture, hymn *Tukutendereza
Yesu*, African-led within (and often against) mission Anglicanism, its members'
costly stands in Mau-Mau Kenya and Amin's Uganda, martyrs including Janani Luwum.
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich (testimony culture generated records; oral-history
projects; some material still gathering — a real "assessment would weigh recency"
case). *Relations:* child of VIII.7's Anglican line, transformed African-inward;
parent of East African evangelical identity.

**IX.4 · African-Initiated Churches** (1900s–present · Nigeria, Ghana, Congo,
South Africa) ⚑
A family the era's Step 0 would split: **Harrist churches** (William Wadé Harris's
1913–15 walk — the most successful single evangelist in African history, and the
churches his converts built waiting for missionaries who came late); **Aladura**
(praying churches — Christ Apostolic, Cherubim & Seraphim, Celestial Church);
**Zionist/Apostolic southern Africa** (the ZCC and thousands more); **Kimbanguism**
(Simon Kimbangu's 1921 ministry, his 30-year imprisonment, the church's growth
under colonial ban).
*Sourcing:* moderate-to-rich and improving (their own archives, oral histories,
liturgies; colonial-record Author Gravity severe). *Floor notes, honestly per
branch:* Harrist/Aladura mainstreams are creedally conventional; Kimbanguist
official theology has carried claims about the Kimbangu family that raise a real,

## Cross-era observations for the map build

1. **The longest lanes are the map's spine.** Egypt (Era I #2/#3 → II.1 → III.3 →
   VIII.13 → IX.10) and the Syriac East (Era I #7 → II.3 → III.1 → III.11 → living
   descendants) each run the whole scroll — the map should render these continuities
   proudly; they are the visual proof of Conviction 1's "vast testimony."
2. **Persecution-formation recurs in every era** (Era I worlds; II.2; III.9; VI.3,
   VI.6, VI.7; VII.7; VIII.1; IX.5, IX.12) — a hover-discoverable *theme thread*
   across eras may be worth designing (a map affordance, logged as an open design
   question, not decided).
3. **Women's-voice-rich candidates cluster identifiably** (IV.7, V.1, VII.4's
   memoir-culture, VII.10, VIII.4, IX.2's Mukti, IX.9's Mother Maria) — directly
   serving Article 20; Step 0s should weigh this as the real Voice Breadth asset the
   Phase One record already prized in World #9.
4. **The ordinary-believer inside voice is rarest before 1200 and after 1900** —
   early because it didn't survive, recently because it's unpublished or unsafe.
   The two honest sentences differ; the map's copy should never blur them.
5. **Era VI–IX candidates are nearly all living traditions** — the Vision's
   living-traditions care, and Article 29's confirmation gate, scale up sharply as
   the map moves right. The map's own copy carries the distinction at hover level
   from Era VI onward.
6. **Roughly 135 within-floor candidates and ~26 adjacent-register entries** are
   named above across nine eras (post-Amendment B). At the Phase One record's own
   selection rate (nine worlds from one era's larger pool), the full map plausibly
   matures toward the 50–100-world catalog the front-end thread's Ultimate Vision
   entry already anticipated — the numbers independently agree, which is worth
   noticing.
7. **Two scope rules, stated once (from the external review's Tier C):** the map
   charts *formation communities, not schools of thought* — scholasticism,
   "evangelicalism," and every -ism live inside the community worlds that carried
   them, or as bounded windows, never as unbounded wholes; and *events and
   councils* (the Crusades, the French Revolution, Vatican II as an event) are
   context-strip or force material except where a lived community world expresses
   them (IV.11, IX.23).
8. **Bible translation is a cross-era theme thread worth surfacing** (borrowed
   gratefully from the Rose-chart tradition): Ulfila → Jerome → Mashtots → Cyril &
   Methodius → Wycliffe → Luther → Carey → the vernacular explosion of Eras
   VIII–IX. The census already contains it implicitly; the map could make it
   hover-discoverable alongside the persecution-formation thread (observation 2) —
   a design affordance, logged as an open question, not decided.

## The Wet Ink Horizon — how the atlas holds still-forming currents

*(Added 2026-07-16, Amendment G — Mark's insight, stated in his own terms: current
movements "are still forming and tend to overlay other more established
movements"; building them rigorously is far future, but the atlas must capture
them creatively now.)*

The last half-century sits inside the **historiographical horizon**: close enough
that nothing has settled — no closed windows, no scholarly consensus, outcomes
unknown. Entries there differ *in kind* from historical worlds, in three ways the
atlas now marks explicitly:

1. **They are overlays, not territories.** A person may worship in a megachurch,
   learn Scripture from the narrative-kingdom current, and be deconstructing —
   *simultaneously*. Bands-in-lanes misrepresents that structurally, so six Era-10
   entries carry the **Overlay Current** form (≈): Lausanne-era evangelicalism,
   the Emerging Church, Dispensational Prophecy Culture, the Megachurch/Seeker
   movement, Deconstruction communities, and the Narrative–Kingdom Renewal. On the
   map they render as **wet ink** — translucent, soft-edged, unfinished at the
   right — where historical worlds are engraved. The chart's engraving runs out at
   its right edge on purpose; a participant should *see* that the present is
   unfixed.
2. **Their source problem is inverted.** Historical worlds: scarce sources,
   settled interpretation — the discipline is *recovery*. Currents: oceanic
   sources (every sermon streamed, every argument archived, until the platform
   dies), zero interpretive settling, living people at every point — the
   disciplines are *selection, preservation, and consent*. A Source Ecology
   built for scarcity does not transfer unmodified.
3. **Membership is non-exclusive and self-described.** Historical worlds had
   boundaries their own sources attest; a current's "membership" is often a
   reading practice, a podcast feed, a posture. Any future build must capture
   interplay as a first-class feature, not noise.

**Future-methodology flag (a handoff, not this thread's work):** when the project
someday builds inside the horizon, the Construction Framework will need a named
instrument — a *Current Ecology* variant of Doc_02 — handling magnitude
(selection from abundance), platform-fragility preservation, living-person
consent and safety, overlay membership, and the bounded-window rule as a
requirement rather than a caution. That instrument belongs to the methodology
thread when the time comes; the atlas's job, done here, is to hold the currents
honestly until then.

## What must happen before any of this becomes a build list
