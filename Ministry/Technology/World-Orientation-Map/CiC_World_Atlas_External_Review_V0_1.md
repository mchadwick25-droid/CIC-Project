# The World Atlas Against the Field — External Review V0.1

**Status:** Review for Mark. Compares the Pre-Step-0 Survey / Census V0.2 (147
entries, nine eras) against the standard scholarly periodizations, the major
taxonomies of world Christianity, and the popular timeline/chart products. Answers
two questions: **how do we fit**, and **what is missing.** Nothing here changes the
census yet — every proposed addition waits on Mark's yes.

**What was checked against:** the Cambridge History of Christianity's nine-volume
periodization; Mark Noll's *Turning Points* (14 decisive moments); Justo González's
*Story of Christianity*; Kenneth Scott Latourette's "Great Century" framing; Diarmaid
MacCulloch's *Christianity: The First Three Thousand Years*; the World Christian
Encyclopedia's traditions taxonomy; and the visual products a participant is most
likely to have seen — Rose Publishing's church-history timelines, the
UsefulCharts/ARDA-style denominational family trees, and the Adams/Priestley
wall-chart tradition already cited in the map spec. (Sources linked at the end.)

---

## Part 1 — How our periodization fits

### 1.1 Against the Cambridge History of Christianity (the field's reference periodization)

| Our era | Our boundary | Cambridge's boundary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. The Early Church Era | 70–451 | Vol 1: Origins→Constantine; Vol 2: Constantine→c.600 | **Deliberate divergence.** Cambridge breaks at Constantine (312/325); our Step 0 kept 70–451 as one phase and put the Constantinian rupture *inside* it as a selected world (Imperial & Juridical, #6). That is a defensible, even superior, choice for a formation-world map: the rupture becomes something you can talk to, not a wall between chapters. Our 451 ceiling is Chalcedon — Cambridge has no break there, but Noll does (below). |
| II. Age of Monks and Empires | 451–622 | Vol 2 ends c.600 | **Near-match** (622 vs c.600). Ours uses the rise of Islam — arguably the cleaner participant-facing hinge; Cambridge's c.600 is a round number around Gregory the Great. |
| III. Early Medieval Era | 622–1054 | Vol 3: c.600–c.1100 | **Near-match** (1054 vs c.1100). Ours uses the Schism — the single date participants already know; Cambridge's c.1100 is the Gregorian-reform round number. |
| IV–V. High / Late Medieval | 1054–1300–1517 | Vol 4: c.1100–c.1500, one volume | **We split what Cambridge lumps.** Justified for our purposes: the 1300–1517 devotional world (mystics, Devotio Moderna, lay parish piety) is a genuinely different formation landscape from the 1100s' new orders, and it is where our women's-voice and ordinary-voice candidates cluster. |
| VI. Reformation Era | 1517–1650 | Vol 6: Reform and Expansion 1500–1660 | **Match** within a decade at both ends. |
| VII. Enlightenment & Awakening | 1650–1815 | Vol 7: Enlightenment, Reawakening and Revolution 1660–1815 | **Match** — end date exact, start within a decade; even the double-barreled title logic matches. |
| VIII. Missionary Era | 1815–1906 | Vol 8: World Christianities c.1815–c.1914 | **Start date exact.** This is also Latourette's "Great Century" (1815–1914) — our era title says what his famous label says. |
| IX. Global Church Era | 1906–present | Vol 9: World Christianities c.1914–c.2000 | **Deliberate divergence** (1906 vs 1914). Cambridge breaks at a world war; we break at Azusa Street — a church-internal hinge rather than a geopolitical one. For a map of *formation worlds*, ours is the more honest marker: the thing that actually reshaped world Christianity's next century began in 1906, not 1914. Worth stating on the map. |

**Verdict:** our nine eras sit inside the field's consensus envelope everywhere, with
three knowing divergences (no Constantine break; Azusa over WWI; splitting the late
Middle Ages) — each explainable in one sentence, and each tied to the project's own
formation-world logic rather than taste. This is a strong position: recognizable to
anyone who knows the standard shelf, and self-consistent where it differs.

### 1.2 Against Noll's *Turning Points* (the most-taught "hinges" list)

Noll's fourteen: Jerusalem's fall (70) · Nicaea (325) · Chalcedon (451) · Benedict's
Rule (530) · Charlemagne's coronation (800) · the Great Schism (1054) · the Diet of
Worms (1521) · the English Act of Supremacy (1534) · the founding of the Jesuits
(1540) · the conversion of the Wesleys (1738) · the French Revolution (1789) ·
Edinburgh Missionary Conference (1910) · Vatican II & Lausanne (1962–74).

- **Our start date is his first turning point** (70 CE), and **our Era I/II boundary
  is his third** (Chalcedon). Nicaea, Benedict, Charlemagne, 1054, Worms, the Act of
  Supremacy, the Jesuits, and the Wesleys all appear either as our era boundaries or
  as census worlds (I.5–I.6 carry Nicaea's world; II.6 Benedict; III.5 the
  Carolingian world; VI.1/VI.4/VI.11; VII.5).
- **Three of his fourteen have no home in our census: Edinburgh 1910, Vatican II,
  and Lausanne 1974.** All three are *conference/council-shaped* rather than
  community-shaped, which is why our formation-world lens missed them — but each
  sits atop a real formation world we also don't carry (the Student Volunteer
  Movement's missionary formation culture; the post-conciliar Catholic parish; the
  global-evangelical network). Logged as gaps below (B-tier).
- The **French Revolution** appears in our atlas only as a context-strip event, which
  is right — it is a force on worlds, not a world.

### 1.3 Against González and MacCulloch

González's two-volume narrative (early church → imperial church → medieval →
Reformation → the colonial expansions → the "Great Century" → global present) is the
seminary-standard reading Mark's likely reviewers know; our eras map onto his parts
without friction, and our Era VI.13 (mission-born Christianities) reflects his
signature insistence — as a Latin American historian — that the colonial church is
part of the main story, not an appendix. MacCulloch's structural bet is the one that
matters most for us: he tells the whole history as **three braided strands (Latin
West, Greek East, and the non-Chalcedonian/Syriac East)** rather than one stream
with tributaries — which is, structurally, our lane system. The atlas's lanes have
the field's most ambitious single-volume history on their side.

## Part 2 — How our coverage fits the taxonomies

The World Christian Encyclopedia tradition (Barrett; now Johnson/Zurlo) classifies
world Christianity into major blocs: **Catholic, Orthodox (Eastern + Oriental +
Church of the East), Protestant, Anglican, Independent** — with older editions
carrying a separate **"marginal Christians"** category for groups whose confessions
diverge from trinitarian Christianity (LDS, JW, Christian Science, etc.).

| Bloc | Our coverage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Catholic | 20+ entries across Eras IV–IX (orders, Tridentine parish, missions, renewal) | Strong |
| Eastern Orthodox | 12+ entries (Byzantine lanes, Slavic, hesychast, Optina, émigré) | Strong, with one real hole (Ottoman-era parish world — below) |
| Oriental Orthodox | Coptic ×4 eras, Ethiopian ×3, Armenian ×2, Syriac Orthodox, Nubia | Strong to 1500; **thin after** (below) |
| Church of the East | 4 entries (I.7 → II.3 → III.1 → III.11) | Strong to ~1300; **lane goes silent after** (below) |
| Anglican | VI.4, VIII.5, plus Balokole inside the communion | Adequate |
| Protestant | ~30 entries | Strong — **except the Baptists (below), a genuinely embarrassing miss** |
| Independent | AICs, Chinese house churches, Pentecostal families | Strong — and the WCE's decision to make "Independent" its own bloc independently corroborates our giving these first-class rows rather than footnoting them under Protestantism |
| "Marginal" (their term) | Our adjacent register (17 floor-question entries with named grounds) | **Structurally corroborated** — the field's own reference work also found it needed a distinct category here; ours differs by carrying *reasons* (C1/C2/A3, per movement) instead of a bare label, which is more honest than the convention |

The six-tradition academic model (Church of the East, Oriental Orthodoxy, Eastern
Orthodoxy, Catholicism, Protestantism, Restorationism) is also fully represented —
Restorationism via VIII.3/VIII.10.

## Part 3 — How we fit against the visual products a participant has actually seen

- **Rose Publishing timelines / Christian History Institute charts** (the church-
  bookstore standard): 300+ *people and events*, icon-coded (councils, Bible
  translation, persecution), Jesus-to-present. Figure- and event-centric, with a
  well-documented Western-Protestant weighting. *Our differences:* we map
  communities rather than celebrities; our context strip carries their event layer;
  our Figure lifespan lines carry their people layer — but subordinated to worlds.
  One genuinely good idea of theirs we lack: **Bible translation as a marked
  cross-era thread** (Ulfila → Jerome → Cyril & Methodius → Wycliffe → Luther →
  Carey…). It is already latent in our census; surfacing it as a hover-discoverable
  theme thread would cost little (design note below).
- **Denominational family trees (UsefulCharts poster, ARDA's interactive trees):**
  institutional descent diagrams — splits at 451, 1054, 1517 with hundreds of
  denominational branches. *Our difference is the deepest one:* a family tree
  answers "who split from whom"; our map answers "what formed people, where, and
  what of it can you actually meet." A tree must include every branch; we select for
  buildable formation ecologies and say honestly why the rest aren't here. We should
  not try to compete on denominational completeness — but the trees set participant
  expectations (someone WILL look for "Baptist" and "Presbyterian" as words), which
  strengthens the case for the Tier-A Baptist addition and for lane/search copy
  using denominational names participants know.
- **Adams/Priestley/Histomap wall charts:** already the spec's own cited ancestors;
  nothing new to import except the reminder that Adams-style charts give *era-dense
  regions more room* — which our piecewise time scale already does.
- **What nothing on the market has** (our distinctives, confirmed rather than
  dented by this review): build-status honesty as a visual layer; confidence-marked
  relationship edges; sourcing signals ("whose own voice survives") per entry; an
  adjacent register with *stated grounds* instead of silence or a slur; and the
  worlds-not-denominations frame itself.

## Part 4 — What is missing (the gap list)

### Tier A — real gaps; recommend adding as census rows in V0.3 (~10 rows)

1. **The Baptists** (General 1609 / Particular 1630s; Bunyan; the associations;
   eventually the largest Protestant family on earth) — *Era VII.* The census's
   single most conspicuous omission: every comparison product carries them, and a
   participant who knows one Protestant word knows this one. Sourcing signal would
   be rich (church books, Bunyan's own *Grace Abounding*, association records).
2. **Ottoman-era Orthodox parish life and the neomartyrs** (1453–1800s; the millet
   world; Kosmas Aitolos already appears but only inside VIII.12's edge) — *Era
   VI–VII.* Our Orthodox lane currently jumps from Palamas (V.4) to Paisius (VII.8):
   three centuries of the largest Orthodox lived experience — faith under the
   sultan — are missing.
3. **Korean Catholic origins** (1784–1886; a church famously self-founded by lay
   scholars before any missionary arrived; ~10,000 martyrs) — *Era VIII.* A
   world-history singular that our Korea coverage (Protestant Pyongyang 1907)
   entirely misses.
4. **St. Thomas Christians under Portuguese pressure — Diamper (1599) and the
   Coonan Cross Oath (1653)** — *Era VI.* Our India lane ends at III.11; its most
   dramatic, best-documented chapter (an ancient church resisting forced
   Latinization, swearing an oath at a leaning cross) is missing — and it fixes
   III.11's "thin early sources" problem by moving to a window where the sources
   are rich.
5. **The Syriac lane's modern chapter — Sayfo (1915) and the Assyrian/Chaldean
   diaspora** — *Era IX.* The map's "longest lane" claim (Edessa → Xi'an) currently
   goes silent after ~1300, which for living Syriac communities reads as exactly
   the erasure the map promises not to commit. Genocide-era and diaspora sources
   are real (survivor accounts, diaspora church archives).
6. **Modern Ethiopian & Eritrean Christianity** (Täwahedo under Haile Selassie and
   the Derg's persecution; the P'ent'ay evangelical movement) — *Era IX.* Same
   lane-goes-silent problem: our strongest non-European medieval candidate (V.7)
   currently has no living continuation on the map.
7. **Philippine Catholicism** (1565–present; Asia's largest Christian nation; folk-
   Catholic formation, Marian piety, the pasyon) — *Eras VI and IX.* Currently the
   Philippines appears only via Iglesia ni Cristo in the adjacent register — an
   inversion a Filipino participant would rightly find strange.
8. **Eastern Catholic (Greek Catholic/Uniate) worlds** (Union of Brest 1596; the
   Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church — including its 1946–1989 underground existence
   as the world's largest banned church) — *Eras VI and IX.* A whole communion
   family (18+ million today) with zero census presence.
9. **The Russian Orthodox new martyrs and catacomb church** (1917–1991) — *Era IX.*
   IX.12 currently carries Baptist and Romanian suffering but not the largest
   persecution of Christians in the century — of the establishment church itself.
10. **Colonial Latin American devotional Catholicism** (17th–18th c.; Guadalupan
    piety, cofradías, the criollo devotional world) — *Era VII.* Currently a
    250-year hole between the VI.13 mission moment and IX.8's base communities —
    González's own corrective, applied to our own map.

### Tier B — worth rows, second pass (~12 rows; add selectively)

- **Scandinavian conversion Christianity** (Era III) and **Laestadianism / Nordic
  revivals beyond Hauge** (Era VIII).
- **Medieval Georgian golden age** (Era III–IV) — Georgia vanishes after II.10.
- **Serbian and Bulgarian medieval monasticism** (Hilandar, Tarnovo school; Era
  IV–V) — the Slavic Orthodox lane currently jumps Kyiv → Moscow.
- **Kyiv-Mohyla Orthodox confessional revival** (Era VI–VII) — Orthodoxy's own
  Reformation-era answer, missing.
- **Plymouth Brethren and the dispensational formation world** (Era VIII) —
  disproportionate global influence on how millions read the Bible.
- **The Sunday School movement and Bible-institute evangelicalism** (Era VIII) —
  mass lay formation technologies, exactly this project's subject.
- **Student Volunteer Movement / Edinburgh 1910 missionary formation culture**
  (Era VIII–IX) — Noll's turning point; watchword-era piety is a real world.
- **The post-Vatican-II parish world** (Era IX) — Noll's turning point; our IX.7
  carries the movements but not the reshaped ordinary parish.
- **Lausanne-era global evangelicalism** (Era IX) — Noll's turning point; arguably
  too diffuse (the IX.11 window-setting rule applies), but name it.
- **Indonesian Christianity** (Batak HKBP; the 1965–71 Timor revival; Era
  VIII–IX) — one of the largest Christian bodies in the Muslim-majority world,
  absent.
- **Northeast India revivals (Mizoram/Nagaland) and the Mar Thoma reform** (Era
  VIII–IX) — the latter also extends the St. Thomas lane honestly.
- **African diaspora / reverse-mission churches in Europe** (Era IX) — the newest
  chapter of the map's own migration logic.

### Tier C — scope notes, not rows (name the rule once)

- **Scholasticism and the universities** (Aquinas, Paris): an intellectual movement
  the mendicant worlds partially carry. Deliberate rule worth stating: *the map
  charts formation communities, not schools of thought* — else it owes rows to
  nominalism, neo-Thomism, and every -ism since.
- **The Crusades** as such: a force and context-strip event; the military orders
  (IV.11) are its community expression.
- **The ecumenical movement / WCC**: institutional history; appears as context.
- **"Evangelicalism" and "Fundamentalism" as unbounded wholes**: same rule as
  IX.11 — only bounded windows are honest.
- **Bible translation as a theme thread** (from Part 3): a cross-era
  hover-discoverable thread, not new rows — joins the persecution-formation thread
  already noted in the atlas's cross-era observations.

### Census arithmetic if Mark approves

Tier A ≈ +10 rows, Tier B ≈ +12, plus 2–3 register entries (e.g., Ratana and
Pacific adjustment movements as floor-question cases) → census grows from 147 to
~170, and — more importantly — four "lane goes silent" problems (Syriac, Ethiopian,
St. Thomas, Ottoman Orthodox) get fixed, which protects the map's central claim
that the long lanes run the whole scroll.

## Part 5 — The one-paragraph answer

**How do we fit:** squarely inside the field's consensus skeleton — our era
boundaries track the Cambridge History within a decade almost everywhere, our start
date and Era I/II hinge are two of Noll's canonical turning points, our lanes are
MacCulloch's braided-strands architecture, and our adjacent register independently
reproduces (with better manners and stated reasons) a category the World Christian
Encyclopedia also found necessary. Where we diverge — no Constantine break, Azusa
over 1914, splitting the late Middle Ages — each divergence is deliberate,
one-sentence-explainable, and serves the formation-world lens. **What's missing:**
one embarrassing family (the Baptists), three centuries of Ottoman-era Orthodoxy,
four lanes that go silent centuries before their living communities do (Syriac,
Ethiopian, St. Thomas India, and colonial-era Latin America), several
world-history singulars (Korean Catholic origins, the Coonan Cross, the Ukrainian
underground church, the Russian new martyrs, Philippine Catholicism), and the three
Noll turning points that are conference-shaped rather than community-shaped. All
addressable in ~25 rows without changing the atlas's structure, statuses, or
discipline.
