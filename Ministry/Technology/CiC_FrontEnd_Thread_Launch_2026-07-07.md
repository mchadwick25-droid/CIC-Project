**The confidence and transparency apparatus, precisely — these are two different things, easy to conflate:**
- **Three-Level Transparency** (Constitution Article 30) is about *depth of access*: Level 1 is the surface encounter itself; Level 2 is reachable on request — accessible explanations, sourcing; Level 3 is the full scholarly derivation record. Levels 2 and 3 are always reachable from Level 1, by constitutional invariant — never gated by participant type or deployment state.
- **Transparency Mode** (Mode One / Mode Two, from the Table Design Document and Pre-Encounter Experience Design) is about *default visibility during the conversation itself* — a participant's own choice, changeable at any time. Mode One (Simple Conversation): the apparatus governs from underneath, nothing surfaced unless asked. Mode Two (Transparent Sourcing): terms highlighted, sources accessible, confidence calibration visible by default. Both modes are equally rigorous — the difference is what the participant sees while it's happening, not how carefully it was built.
- **The five-level confidence vocabulary** (Constitution Article 17) is the evidential scale every claim in the system is calibrated against, strongest to weakest: Documented, Widely Accepted, Dominant Modern Reconstruction, Contested, Inferential/Thin. This is what Mode Two makes visible when a participant wants to see it.

A spec document that flattens these three into one "transparency" idea will be wrong. Keep them distinct.
