# The Church in Conversation — Guided Questions Curriculum V1.0

**Status:** For Mark, 2026-07-16. The deliverable. Design and content only.

**What this is.** A participant who doesn't know what to ask clicks a tab and gets a
choice of five sets. Each set walks them through a conversation. Four roles, five sets
each, four to five questions per set — **100 questions, one document, reviewable in a
sitting.**

**The questions are role-generic.** They work in any world, because the Representatives
handle the per-world variation. That is not a compromise — it is what the live
calibration showed. A generic question that meets a thin record produces an honest,
calibrated answer: Chloe volunteered *"We keep no record of who has been held this way,
or how often"*; Papnoute answered *"absence does not answer."* **A question landing in a
world's silence is the project's thesis, not its failure mode.**

**Each set is a walk, not a menu.** The questions are in a deliberate order, each a
natural next step from the last. **The participant can jump anywhere** — the order is
the default walk, not a track.

**The one hard rule: question 1 of every set is world-framed, never
participant-framed.** *"Did anyone out there struggle with thoughts they couldn't turn
off?"* — never *"I have thoughts I can't turn off."* This is a safety rule with a
mechanism behind it: a starter is **turn zero**, and the relational-safety classifier's
gentlest category has no prior transcript to anchor to, so first-person present-tense
distress phrasing reads as a real disclosure and triages the participant for accepting
our own invitation. Tested live; it happened. Later questions in a walk have transcript
behind them and can sit closer to the participant.

**Grounding:** the role needs, the anti-pattern catalog, and the fit tests are in
`CiC_Guided_Questions_Design_Study_V0_1.md`. Every question below passes them. Where a
question needed a judgment call, there is a note.

**Data contract:** `CiC_Guided_Questions_Curriculum_V1_0.json`, authored alongside this
document, not transcribed from it afterward.

## General — the curious visitor

*What the research found they actually ask: lived practice, not doctrine. Clothing,
food, greetings, marriage, fasting. And every top query under "why were early
christians…" is about persecution or accusation — they want to know how these people
looked from the outside.*

### 1. An ordinary day
*What a week actually looked like — the room, the food, the work, the people.*

1. Walk me through an ordinary day among your people — from waking to sleeping.
2. What did you eat, and who ate with you?
3. Would I have picked you out in a crowd, or did you look like everyone else?
4. What did people do for work — and did belonging to you change it?
5. What happened in a household once the day was over?

### 2. The table you gathered at
*The meal at the centre of the gathering — who came, who led, what it meant.*

1. When your people gathered, what actually happened in the room?
2. Tell me about the meal you shared. Why did it matter so much?
3. Who led it — and how was that decided?
4. Was it done the same way everywhere, or differently in different places?
5. Was there anyone you wouldn't share it with?

### 3. How you looked from outside
*What your neighbours thought you were — the rumours, the accusations, the danger.*

1. What did your neighbours think you were?
2. What were you accused of?
3. Was it dangerous to be what you were? What could actually happen, and who decided?
4. How did an ordinary person go about a week with that hanging over them?
5. Did it ever come near anyone you knew?

### 4. Becoming one of you
*How someone joined — what they were taught, what changed, what it cost.*

1. How did someone become one of you?
2. What were they taught first — and why that first?
3. How long did it take, and who decided they were ready?
4. What changed for a person afterwards?
5. What did it cost them — with their family, their work, their old life?

### 5. The argument you lived inside
*The question your people never settled, and how they stayed one people anyway.*

1. What did your people disagree about among themselves?
2. Did it ever come close to breaking you apart?
3. How did you stay one people with those who did it differently?
4. Did you expect it to be settled one day? Did you want it settled?
5. What would you say to someone who told you that you should all just agree?

> **Note on set 5.** Offered to the general visitor deliberately, not held back as
> advanced material. Governance §9 says hard questions are met at the level of the
> participant's need, not withheld — and this is the set that most decisively
> distinguishes a reconstructed world from a chatbot. It is also the direct answer to
> the sharpest external critique of AI historical figures: *"There are histories. There
> is no one narrative."*

## Pastor / Teacher

*What the evidence found: 87% of pastors use AI for research and raw material, only 24%
for authorship, and 71% describe themselves as cautious. Every documented objection to
canned material is an objection to a finished product replacing their own wrestling.
**So: hand over the text, the voice, and the question. Never the lesson.** No question
below names an application.*

### 1. Who led, and how that was decided
*Leadership as your people actually worked it out — offices, disputes, and what was never settled.*

1. Who led among your people — and how was that decided?
2. What did you call your leaders, and what did those words actually mean?
3. What happened when a leader failed, or was removed wrongly?
4. Did other communities do it differently? How did you regard them?
5. Which of your own writings would you put in my hands on this?

### 2. Forming someone new
*How a person was taught, tested, and brought in — the practice behind the teaching.*

1. How did your people teach someone who was new?
2. What did you teach them first — and why that first?
3. Who did the teaching? Was it an office, or whoever could?
4. How did you know when someone was ready?
5. Is there a text you taught from that I could sit with myself?

### 3. Failure, and what came after
*What your people did when someone fell — mercy, discipline, and the arguments about both.*

1. What happened among your people when someone failed badly?
2. Was there a way back? Who decided?
3. Did your people agree about that, or argue about it?
4. What did you do with someone who failed the same way again?
5. What did you say to the ones who were afraid they'd used up their chance?

### 4. When you disagreed
*Conflict as your people actually handled it — the mechanics, not the ideal.*

1. What did your communities disagree about?
2. How did a disagreement actually get worked out — who spoke, who decided?
3. Did one community ever correct another? What happened?
4. What made an argument legitimate, and what made it a rupture?
5. Did any of it end badly?

### 5. The texts you'd put in my hands
*The writings your people actually used, and what they were for.*

1. What did your people read when you gathered?
2. Where did those writings come from, and how did you get them?
3. How did you decide whether something could be trusted?
4. Which one would you hand to someone who teaches others?
5. What's in your writings that people misread?

## Academic / Scholar

*Built at the intersection the research found: Paul's Socratic taxonomy and the
historiographic probe register are structurally the same instrument. The register marker
is that **a probing question names its own defeasibility condition** — hence 1.5 and 5.5.*

### 1. How do you know that?
*The evidential standing of what you're being told, volunteered rather than extracted.*

1. How do your people know what you've told me — what stands behind it?
2. How much of what you know comes down through a single voice?
3. Where does documentation end and inference begin for you?
4. What in your own record is disputed — its dating, its authorship, whether it's genuine?
5. What would have to be true for you to be wrong about it — and would we be able to tell?

### 2. Whose voice is this?
*Who wrote your record, who didn't, and what that does to what survives.*

1. Who among your people could write?
2. Whose voices are missing from your record entirely?
3. What do you know about those people, and how do you know it?
4. Does anything survive written by them, or only written about them?
5. What would you not claim to know about them?

### 3. What you argued about
*The disagreements inside your own community — the evidence that this is a world, not a position.*

1. What did your people genuinely disagree about among themselves?
2. Was there a settled answer by the end of your time?
3. Who held each side, and what did each argue from?
4. Did either side treat the other as outside the faith?
5. Is there an argument your record preserves only one side of?

### 4. Where your record thins
*The shape of the silences, and what kind of silence each one is.*

1. Where is your record thinnest?
2. Is that because nothing was written, or because nothing survived?
3. Is there something I've been asking about that your people wouldn't have recognised as a question at all?
4. What do people assume about your world that your record can't actually support?
5. What has been dug up from your world, and what hasn't?

> **Note on 4.2 and 4.3.** These are the same distinction the study named as an
> anachronism trap we could commit inside the very set built to be honest about silence:
> *we have no evidence of it* and *the category did not exist for them* are different
> answers, and conflating them implies a world should have had our category and merely
> failed to record it. 4.2 asks for the first; **4.3 asks for the second directly**, and
> is the one question in this document that tests our own framing rather than the
> world's.

### 5. The contested ground
*Where your world's own material is disputed, and how you hold that.*

1. What in your record do people who study you fight about?
2. Is there something attributed to your people that they didn't actually say?
3. What in your tradition's memory of itself might be later polish?
4. Where does your world's account of itself flatter it?
5. What's the strongest case against how you've just described yourselves?

## Reevaluation

*The deepest treatment, per the launch. The mechanism, from the research: **a
pre-offered hard question costs the participant no standing to ask.** Where spiritual
and epistemic credibility are fused, the act of asking lowers your standing to ask — so
the house asking first is the whole point.*

*The governing constraint: **testimony, not argument.** A Representative who genuinely
wanted to leave and says so delivers "you're not alone, you're not making this up"
without persuading in either direction. And the failure mode to avoid: if the cumulative
weight of a set says "see, we were unsettled too, so it's fine," we have rebuilt the
thought-terminating cliché out of historical material. **The world is allowed to be what
it was. The walk is not allowed to aim it.***

### 1. Did any of you ever want to leave?
*Whether anyone in your world wanted out — asked of them, answered as testimony.*

1. Did any of you ever want to leave?
2. What was that like from the inside, for the ones who felt it?
3. Could they say it out loud? To whom?
4. Did anyone actually go? What did the rest of you say about them?
5. What kept the ones who stayed — honestly?

### 2. When it went wrong among you
*Your world's own failures, named by it rather than about it.*

1. What went wrong among your people?
2. Did anyone get hurt by the way you did things?
3. Was there anything done in your name that you'd disown?
4. Did your people ever admit it — at the time, later, or never?
5. What's in your record that you're not proud of?

### 3. Doubt, and whether there was room for it
*What your people did with someone who wasn't sure.*

1. Was there room among you for someone who doubted?
2. What did your people do with a person who wasn't sure?
3. Did anyone among you carry a doubt for years?
4. Was doubt treated as a failure, or as part of it?
5. Would there have been a place at your table for someone with more questions than answers?

### 4. What you never settled
*The questions your world left open, held as they held them.*

1. What did your people never settle?
2. How did you live with it unsettled?
3. Did that trouble anyone, or was it just how things were?
4. How do you give your life to something that hasn't made up its mind about its own biggest questions?
5. Did you expect it to be settled? Did you want it to be?

### 5. Say the hardest thing first
*The most honest thing your world can say about itself, offered first rather than last.*

1. What's the hardest true thing about your people?
2. What would you rather I didn't know?
3. What did belonging actually cost among you?
4. Who paid for your way of life besides you?
5. If someone left your community for good, what would you have wanted them to know?

> **Note on 5.5.** Phrased about *someone*, not *you*, on purpose. The participant-framed
> version — "if I walked away, what would you want to have told me?" — invites the
> Representative to make a case, which is a persuasion arc wearing an honesty costume.
> World-framed, it stays testimony.
>
> **Note on 2.5.** In the Syriac world this routes to the anti-Jewish polemic — that
> world's genuinely hardest true thing about itself, and the material its own brief calls
> the most sensitive in the record, recommended for external scholarly review before wide
> deployment. **That is a per-world answer, not a per-world question**, and it belongs in
> the coverage card as a routing caution rather than as a hole in this curriculum. Mark's
> ruling stands; the place it is enforced changed.

## What every question here has been checked against

The ten fit tests from the study, plus the two the live calibration added. On generic
questions this is one check per question rather than four.

| Test | Applied here as |
|---|---|
| **Category (Skinner)** | No question imports a category a world may not have had. This is why there is no "what was your theology of X" (coherence), no "were you the beginning of Y" (prolepsis), and no "how did you do church" (parochialism — the trap our own shorthand walks into). |
| **Non-leading (FRE 611c)** | Every question can be answered "no," "differently," or "we never did that" from evidence. We are structurally on direct examination — a cooperative witness adopts a suggestion, so presupposing questions are the contamination risk. |
| **Lived practice** | Every question opens onto practice, formation, worship, community texture, or experience — the registers a *community* voice is built to carry. None demands modern-controversy adjudication, single-figure interiority, or post-dated knowledge. |
| **Generativity** | Every question raises further questions and requires justification rather than terminating in a retrievable fact. |
| **Register** | Every question is answerable in a Representative's own measure — a householder's short sentences, an elder's grave watchfulness. |
| **Invariance** | Nothing is offered to one role and withheld from another. All twenty sets stay reachable to every role behind "show me everything." **Role changes what's offered first, never what's reachable.** |
| **World-not-person (hard, Q1 of every set)** | Question 1 is world-framed in all twenty sets. Verified below. |
| **Invitation (guard-check)** | Reframed after calibration: a graceful redirect is a good participant experience, not a defect. Cut only where no guard covers the failure. |
| **Disclosure** | No question is a first-person present-tense disclosure of distress. |
| **Named story** | No question invites an attested named narrative *without its name* — the shape that reads as fabricated. |

**Q1 world-framing check, all twenty sets:** every one opens with *"your people," "your
neighbours," "did any of you," "what did your communities,"* or *"how did someone."*
**None opens with "I."**

## What this document does not establish

1. **No question here has faced a live model.** The four cells that were tested came from
   the superseded per-world sets; these are their generic descendants. **The calibration
   established that the *Representatives* handle generic questions and thin records
   honestly** — it did not test these hundred strings.
2. **Per Wei et al., we are the least reliable judges of what reads as inviting.** The
   confidence here is about evidence and safety, not appeal.
3. **Per Schuman & Presser, this set will miss what participants would ask on their
   own — and no amount of pre-testing fixes that.** The pilot should log what testers
   type into the box and whether they clicked a tab at all. **That harvest, not this
   document, is the evidence for V1.1.**
