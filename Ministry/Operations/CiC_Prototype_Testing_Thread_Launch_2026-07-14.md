## Where the actual system stands right now (2026-07-14 readiness audit, carried over so this thread doesn't have to rediscover it)

A same-day readiness assessment on the running prototype (`cic-poc`) found the following, directly relevant to pilot design:

- **No in-app onboarding exists at all.** A tester lands straight on a world-picker grid with zero explanation of what a "world"/"representative" is, what the lexicon highlighting or citation markers mean, or the historical-reconstruction-vs-living-tradition distinction. This thread should design what that onboarding content actually says — the system thread will build it, but the words and structure are this thread's to write, since they're closer to the Vision/Constitution language than to engineering.
- **Sessions are not persistent** — a backend restart or a tester's browser refresh loses their conversation with no recovery. This affects pilot *process* design (e.g., should testers be told to keep a tab open and not refresh; should each testing session be a single sitting rather than something a tester dips in and out of over days) even though the underlying fix is the system thread's job.
- **The Three-Level Transparency apparatus (lexicon highlighting, inline citation markers for stories/sources) is functionally working** as of tonight, including a real cross-world collision bug that was found and fixed — worth knowing this is a genuine, working part of the experience to ask testers about specifically, not a half-built feature to route around.
- **Four worlds are live**: Chloe (The House-Churches), Mar Yausep (Syriac Christianity), Papnoute (Desert Fathers and Mothers), Albina (The Bethlehem Circle) — single-representative "Deep Interview" style conversation and multi-world (2-3) "Compare Worlds" tables are both functional, though the two-mode entry-point framing itself (per the 2026-07-14 front-end decision log entries) is a naming/UX idea not yet built as a distinct choice — testers today see a "Single Representative" / "Multiple Representatives" toggle, not the richer framing Mark described.

## What to produce

Work through these with Mark - some need his direct input (recruitment, count, timeline), some this thread can draft and bring back to him for reaction, per this project's standing practice of showing full drafts rather than summaries:

1. **Pilot process and timeline.** How the 5-10 testers are actually recruited and invited, what a testing "session" looks like (a single guided sitting? open access over a set window - a week, two weeks?), and what happens at the end (a debrief call? a written reflection? both?).

2. **Tester count and composition.** Confirm or refine the 5-10 number, and surface whether intentional diversity across the four existing informal roles (regular visitor, pastor/teacher, academic-adjacent, deconstructing/reconstructing) is worth recruiting for even before the role-based UI feature exists - a pilot group that's accidentally homogeneous will miss failure modes a diverse one would catch.

3. **Onboarding content.** Draft the actual words a first-time tester needs before or as they start: what this project is, what a "world" and "representative" mean, how to read the lexicon highlights and citation markers, and the honest disclaimer about historical reconstruction versus a living tradition's own self-understanding (this is a real Constitution-level distinction, Article 6 and elsewhere - don't soften it into generic AI-app boilerplate). This becomes a concrete build requirement for the system thread once drafted.

4. **Recording and feedback capture - resolve the open privacy question first.** Before designing how tester conversations or reactions get recorded, get Mark's explicit decision on the still-open data/privacy logging question named above. Once that's resolved, design the actual mechanism: are full conversation transcripts logged automatically (with what consent/disclosure to the tester), and separately, how does structured feedback get captured (a short survey after each session? a live debrief? both?).

5. **The question sets** - Mark's own framing: two or three **preselected/structured threads** (guided topics a tester is invited to explore with a Representative or a table) plus two or three **open-ended** prompts (no guidance, see what a tester does unprompted). Draft actual candidate questions for both categories, grounded in what the four worlds actually are (not generic "what do you think of this app" filler) - e.g. a structured thread might deliberately route a tester into a multi-world comparison to test the "Compare Worlds" experience, or into a single deep encounter to test the "Deep Interview" depth; an open-ended prompt tests what happens with zero guidance at all. Cross-reference `anthropic-skills:cic-validation-suite` if that skill is available in this thread's environment - it defines the project's own internal probe-battery categories for validating a Representative before launch, and tester-facing questions should complement those categories (testing real human impressions the internal probes can't check) rather than duplicating them wholesale.
