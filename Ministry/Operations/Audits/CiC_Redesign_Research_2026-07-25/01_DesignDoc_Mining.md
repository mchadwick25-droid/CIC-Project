# Comprehensive Inventory: "Getting the Conversation Right" — CiC Repository Research

**Scope and method note:** This is pure extraction — no prioritization, no recommendations. I read the core governing documents in full (Vision V2.0, Constitution V2.2/2.3, Essential Experience V2.1, Facilitator-Governance V3.6 and V3.7 PROPOSAL, The Table Design Document V2.3, Representative Construction Framework V3.2, the Table_Process_ThreeRepresentative document, the Engineering Considerations Catalog, Governance Standing Rules, the Change Orders Register, the V7 Upgrade Reference, and the 22-part Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation study), converted and read the relevant `.docx` files via `python-docx`, and traced git history for version lineage. I also ran three parallel research passes over `Ministry/`, `cic-poc/` (docs + backend code), and `World-Builds/`, and personally spot-verified their findings against source files. Every claim below is anchored to a specific file path.

A cross-cutting pattern to hold in mind while reading: **this project documents its own inconsistencies unusually openly.** Several numbers (drift-signal counts, table-size ceilings, cost-per-conversation estimates) genuinely differ across documents written at different times, and the project's own audit trail (`Ministry/Operations/Audits/CiC_L1-L5_Systematic_Audit_2026-07-19.md`) catalogs many of these drifts explicitly rather than hiding them. I've flagged the ones I found under each dimension.

## 1. RIGOR (historical/theological accuracy, source verification, anti-fabrication)

- **Anti-Fabrication Prohibition** — the constitutional floor. `L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx`, Article 28: prohibits "absolutely the fabrication of citations, sources, scholarly consensus, or historical claims that do not exist." Calls this "a cardinal failure of the same order as generating ungrounded content in encounter." Article 17 establishes a five-level confidence vocabulary (Documented / Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction / Contested / Inferential-Thin) that must travel with every claim from construction through to the participant's screen. Current and actively enforced — this is the most load-bearing rigor rule in the whole architecture, cited by nearly every other document.

- **External Scholarly Accountability (Article 31)** — internal AI review, however rigorous, "cannot substitute for accountability to human scholarly judgment." Requires a qualified human subject-matter reviewer before any world is "validated" or "frozen." All AI adversarial review output must carry the literal marking "Simulated review — informational only, not an Article 31 substitute" (`Project-Reference/CiC_Governance_Standing_Rules.md`, item 2). **Status: not yet satisfied for any world.** `Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_Task_Board_2026.md` (2026-07-21) records that Article 31 external review has been "reworded to aspirational, no longer a go-live dependency" — a financial-reality decision by Mark, meaning the constitutional gate has been informally deferred at the business-decision level even though the Constitution itself still states it as a freeze requirement.

- **Approved Source List / Source Registry mechanism (Doc_02B)** — the concrete engineering answer to fabrication risk. Originated from a real, corrected defect: Alexandria's Representative reached for Gregory of Nyssa's (a Cappadocian) treatment of a shared biblical image — a source belonging to a different world. Documented in the Change Orders Register (`L2C-System-Status/CiC_L2C_Change_Orders_Register_V1_18.docx`, CO-016/CO-017/CO-019/CO-020) and in the Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation study Parts XVIII-XXII (on branch `CiC-Fable-Experiment`, not in main — see §7 below). The mechanism: every world gets a per-entry Source Registry (Type, Confidence A-E, Licensed-For, NOT-approved-for), distilled into a short "grounding-anchor paragraph" in the Representative's Permanent Prompt (`L3B-World-Build-Methodology/Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Template.txt`, Section 2A / Final Assembly Instruction 5e) naming specific texts/figures the voice may draw vivid imagery from. Now permanent methodology (Construction Framework V7.4, new Step 2B) and deployed to all five currently-built worlds' actual prompt files.

- **Live citation audits found real errors, repeatedly, at every level** — this is documented, not hidden:
  - `cic-poc/docs/engineering-notes/LEXICON_GLOSS_AUDIT_2026-07-23.md`: found and removed a fabricated supporting quotation on Syriac's "Mar" lexicon entry; found and fixed a source-content inconsistency in a Desert Monasticism story chunk.
  - `World-Builds/Alexandria-Catechetical-School/Analysis/Portfolio_Consistency_Audit_2026-07-19.md`: found lexicon chunks across worlds wildly inconsistent in whether they carry required Article 17 confidence language — Desert-Monasticism 5/9 (56%), Hieronymian 6/15 (40%), Alexandria 45/45 (100%) — logged as an actionable cross-world finding (`World-Builds/Alexandria-Catechetical-School/Analysis/CROSS_WORLD_FINDING_Lexicon_Confidence_Gap.md`).
  - Per-world Decision Logs (e.g. `World-Builds/Syriac-Christianity-Edessa-Nisibis/CiC_W7_Decision_Log.md`, `World-Builds/Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Open_Gaps_Tracking.md`) document, world by world, document by document, real fabricated/misattributed citations caught by multi-round independent adversarial review (a fabricated "Seppälä 2023" citation; a misidentified *Dissertatio Maximini*; a backwards Interaction Test rule application; documents that falsely certified their own compliance and were only caught by a skeptical re-check). This iterative "draft → independent adversarial review → fix → re-verify" cycle, formalized as the "One-Document-at-a-Time" build protocol, is the operative rigor mechanism at the world-construction level, distinct from runtime rigor (next bullet).

- **Runtime rigor implementation** (`cic-poc/backend/app/graph/nodes.py`, confirmed by direct code read): a two-stage classify-then-adjudicate pipeline for two separate signals — FABRICATION (Stage 1 flags from response text alone; Stage 2 re-judges with the world's actual sources attached, fails open toward *keeping* the flag) and OVER_SETTLING (a claim stated more firmly than its own world's record allows; fails open toward *clearing* the flag, the opposite bias, because a false confirmation would falsely harden a real doubt). A concretely observed case: Papnoute (Desert) naming Antony and citing Athanasius passed clean, "while the same attested material narrated as 'he' was flagged high-severity fabrication on the next turn" — this motivated the second-pass architecture. Retrieval-side, `rag/source_registry.py` resolves inline citations to full registry rows (author, date, confidence, verification notes); a separate `/api/session/{id}/audit` endpoint exposes the full retrieval-and-drift trail for reviewer use, distinct from what participants see.

- **Documented rigor vs. reading-level tradeoff, resolved explicitly:** the accessibility/readability standard (§3 below) was designed to touch *sentence structure only*, never vocabulary — "a world's own difficult or technical terms are not simplified or removed... since participant-facing glossary tooling elsewhere in the project carries that load" (`L3C-Representative-Methodology/CiC_L3C_Representative_Construction_Framework_V3.2.docx`, Part Five).

- **Gap:** No dedicated "conversation dynamics" style study exists specifically for rigor in isolation — rigor testing is folded into each world's Boundary Testing / Validation Layer (Doc_09) and into the Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation study's Parts VII-XXII (fabrication testing). There is no single canonical "citation reliability" report; the closest is Part XVIII of that study and the per-world Decision Logs.

## 2. VOICE

- **Foundational principle — the Representative is "the world's own voice," not an impersonated individual.** This is a major, dated architectural pivot (Change Orders Register CO-014, `L2C-System-Status/CiC_L2C_Change_Orders_Register_V1_18.docx`, filed as Register V1.7-V1.10): originally a Representative was one bounded, embodied individual pinned to a place/moment; this was found to be driving construction toward invented biographical specifics (travel history, fixed location) that the project lead identified as "contamination — fiction manufactured to solve a problem the model doesn't actually have." Reconceived as first-person-plural ("we/our/among us") — a documented collective's attested positions, never an invented individual's memory. Name and role remain the only invented elements, and role is explicitly "a register-shaping label for the participant's benefit, not a scope constraint" (`Representative_Construction_Framework_V3.2`, Part Four).

- **Voice construction dimensions** (`Representative_Construction_Framework_V3.2`, Part Five): Reasoning Mode (the world's own formation axis — intellectual, worship, ascetic, communal, affective — "co-equal, and which one is paramount is world-derived"), Perception Pattern (what the world's tradition characteristically foregrounds), Language and Register (grounded in the world's own evidence, "not archaic English or artificial formality"), Emotional/Relational Tone, Historical Containment (no anachronism, no hindsight, no cross-world contamination).

- **Distinctness enforced by an explicit "Approved Source Anchoring" mechanism** so a voice never reaches for a more famous, wrong-world image (see §1). Register-fidelity is checked as its own standing test (CO-015): "a completed Permanent Prompt's actual prose register... must match what the ecology documents as this world's own register, in whichever direction the evidence points, never defaulting to a generic uniformly-polished 'AI voice' register." A near-miss overcorrection was caught and reversed: applying the same "add plainness" fix to every world regardless of that world's own evidenced register would itself have been a fabrication risk in the opposite direction (Eumathios/Cappadocian is documented as deliberately elaborate/rhetorical; Kimon/Desert as deliberately terse).

- **Runtime voice implementation** (`cic-poc/backend/app/prompts/representative_prompts.py`): pronoun discipline (we/I rules with a worked example distinguishing a correctly-recast claim from one that's merely pronoun-swapped but still fabricated), explicit instructions to avoid "generic AI register" tics (hedge-then-commit openers, "It is not X, it is Y" framing, em-dash overuse), per-world "native measure" (e.g. Chloe: short sentences, two-paragraph cap) enforced via per-world hard word ceilings with regenerate-on-overage logic (Desert 60 words, Hieronymian 180 words).

- **Voice under multi-world pressure — the most heavily tested dimension in the whole repo.** The 22-part Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation study (see §7) is almost entirely about voice-distinctness failure under adversarial pressure: "self-narration" (a Representative explaining its own construction/grammar instead of speaking from formation) and "fabrication" (inventing a personal memory) were found to be *systemic*, not per-world bugs, across every one of five tested voices, and were only closed after multiple architectural iterations (a "subject-of-utterance rule" reaching v9, with a documented regression where naming a banned phrase as a negative example made the model *more* likely to produce it).

- **Multi-world voice-drift signals** (`L3D-Encounter-Methodology/CiC_L3D_Facilitator_Governance_V3.6.docx`, §10): Cross-world drift, Dominance drift, Convergence drift, Competitive recruitment drift, Mode-dominance drift — five signals specific to holding multiple voices distinct at one table, on top of the single-voice signals (Smoothing, Generating, Agreeing, Over-producing, Temporal bleed, Flattening, Self-narration).

- **Version/date flag — the drift-signal count is inconsistent across current documents, and the project's own audit caught it:** Facilitator-Governance V3.6 text says "six signals" while listing more; V3.7_PROPOSAL fixes this to name ten explicitly (adding Fabrication, Apologetics, First-person) but doesn't fix a dependent "eleven signals" reference elsewhere in the same document; `Project-Reference/CiC_V7_Upgrade_Reference.md` records this exact bug being caught and "corrected to eleven" in one place; `Ministry/Features/Representative-Modes/Design/CiC_Representative_Modes_Design_Spec_V0_1.md` (2026-07-22) cites "the twelve fidelity-drift signals"; `Ministry/Operations/Audits/CiC_Full_System_Feature_Analysis_V0_1.md` (2026-07-16) also says twelve. **The count has moved six → seven → nine → ten → eleven → twelve across documents dated between roughly early July and late July 2026,** and `CiC_L1-L5_Systematic_Audit_2026-07-19.md` names this exact discrepancy directly. V3.7_PROPOSAL is still an unmerged proposal, not yet applied to the governing V3.6 document.

## 3. READING LEVEL / PLAIN LANGUAGE

- **The precise standard exists and is dated to a specific discovery.** Change Orders Register CO-015 (`L2C-System-Status/CiC_L2C_Change_Orders_Register_V1_18.docx`, Register V1.12): a live readability test (Flesch-Kincaid, Flesch Reading Ease) run across all four then-completed Permanent Prompts found **every one testing at college reading level (grade 12.3–15.7)** despite plain, everyday vocabulary (1.3-1.4 syllables/word) — the cause was sentence length and clause-chaining (sentences averaging 30-40 words, some over 100 words, built from stacked em-dashes/colons/semicolons), traced partly to the core template's own model sentence, which every world had reproduced almost verbatim. **Fix, now the standing target:** "Flesch-Kincaid grade 8-10, Flesch Reading Ease 60 or above," achieved by shortening sentences at existing clause boundaries — vocabulary, imagery, and lexicon terms are never touched. Chloe was the fix pilot: grade 12.3 → 8.1, Reading Ease 60.9 → 71.9. This exact language is now baked into `L3C-Representative-Methodology/CiC_L3C_Representative_Construction_Framework_V3.2.docx` (Part Four) and `L3B-World-Build-Methodology/Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Template.txt` (Final Assembly Instruction 5c) as a mandatory build-time check, distinct from and independent of register fidelity (5b).

- **The same standard is separately phrased as "the 10th-grade floor" in product/UX documents**, and this is the exact "10th grade" language you asked about — it's real and current, just applied to a different layer than the Representative's own generated prose: `Ministry/Operations/Audits/CiC_Full_System_Feature_Analysis_V0_1.md` (2026-07-16): "Three-Level Transparency (Article 30, constitutional): conversation → on-request plain explanation (**10th-grade floor, universal**) → full scholarly apparatus." `Ministry/Features/Representative-Modes/Design/CiC_Representative_Modes_Design_Spec_V0_1.md`: "The 10th-grade readability floor is universal. Level 2 accessible explanations stay at that floor for everyone" — and in the invariance table, "10th-grade Level-2 floor: **Never** [varies by role]." Also cited in `Ministry/Features/Atlas-World-Map/Design/CiC_World_Orientation_Map_Spec_V0_1.md` ("A curious teenager should be able to read why a world isn't here and understand the answer") and `Ministry/Features/Hosted-Tour/Design/CiC_Hosted_Tour_Design_Note_V0_1.md` for the Facilitator's/product's own narration voice. So: **grade 8-10 (Flesch-Kincaid) applied to Representative prose, and a "10th-grade floor" applied to Level-2 on-request explanations** — these are consistent with each other (both land at the same ceiling) but stated in two different documents for two different layers of the system, worth knowing they're the same standard described from two angles rather than two competing standards.

- **The standard is explicitly framed as epistemic, not cosmetic.** `Ministry/Features/Guided-Questions/Design/CiC_Guided_Questions_Design_Study_V0_1.md`: "Plain is never rounder. The general answer carries the same uncertainty as the academic one... A starter set that avoids the unsettled topics to keep things pleasant has forked the truth by curation instead of by prompt — the same violation, one layer up." The Representative Modes demonstration artifact (§4 below) exists specifically to prove this in one worked example across four registers.

- **Implementation gap, confirmed by direct code inspection:** `cic-poc/backend/app/` has **no Flesch-Kincaid or any readability-formula implementation anywhere** (confirmed via exhaustive grep for `flesch|kincaid|readability|reading_level|grade_level` — zero hits). The standard is enforced entirely at build/QA time (a manual check applied once per world when the Permanent Prompt is written), not at runtime per-response. Length/register discipline at runtime is enforced only through word-count ceilings and token caps, not a formal readability metric.

- **A separate, live-tester-driven refinement is current and unreleased:** `Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md` (2026-07-23): real pilot feedback that "testers were not recognizing period vocabulary well enough for the conversation to make sense" produced a new lexicon gloss-rendering rule (opaque terms lead with the modern gloss; readable-but-ambiguous phrases do the reverse). Status: wording decided, front-end rendering not yet built.

## 4. TIERED ACCESS

There is **no single unified "3-tier" access model.** I found at least five genuinely distinct things in the documents that use the word "tier," and it's worth knowing all five so none gets mistaken for another:

- **A. Participant conversation-calibration roles (four, not three) — explicitly NOT an access tier.** `L3D-Encounter-Methodology/CiC_L3D_Facilitator_Governance_V3.6.docx` §4 and `Ministry/Features/Representative-Modes/Design/CiC_Representative_Modes_Design_Spec_V0_1.md`: Regular visitor, Pastor/teacher, Academic/scholar, Deconstructing/reconstructing. Governing invariant, stated verbatim in the Funding Strategy Map: these are "**registers, never access levels**." Every level of transparency and depth remains reachable by every role; an unselected role produces "today's exact behavior, byte-for-byte." The Design Spec's own demonstration artifact answers one identical question ("Who led your communities — and how was that decided?") in Chloe's voice across all four roles with byte-identical underlying claims/confidence/tension, varying only register and what's offered first.
  - **Status: designed but not live.** Confirmed by direct code inspection of `cic-poc/backend/app/graph/state.py` and `nodes.py`: a `_LANE_LENGTH_CEILINGS` data structure exists (general=140 words, academic=140, pastor-teacher=220, plus a `reevaluation` lane with no ceiling by design) but `ConversationState` has **no `participant_role` field at all**, and no API/session parameter ever sets one — the code path is permanently inert (`getattr(..., None)` always returns `None`). `Ministry/Features/Representative-Modes/Design/CiC_Representative_Modes_Battery_A_Results_2026-07-22.md`: the first live-model test of this feature came back **FAIL** — the `reevaluation` (deconstructing) arm dropped real content (answered only half a two-part question, dropped a "we never settled this" disclaimer) even while sounding correctly "itself" in register. Same-day spot-fix applied; formal re-run/gate not confirmed closed anywhere in the files reviewed. All Representative Modes documents are V0.1 drafts on an unmerged branch (`claude/representative-modes-exploration`); explicitly "nothing here merges before or during Prototype Testing 1."

- **B. A funding/monetization access ladder — this is the closest match to a "pilot vs. general vs. institutional" model, and it has more than three rungs.** `Ministry/Features/Funding-Strategy/CiC_Funding_Strategy_Map_V0_1.md` (2026-07-22): Free/Base (full model + Atlas, same rigor, bounded only by time) → Subscription (more time + operational-cost coverage + a community layer) → Deeper/Tailored (role-specific paid tools: pastor, academic, homeschool) → Unlimited (no caps) → **Institutional** (a parallel track, not a rung above Unlimited — multi-seat licensing for churches/seminaries, $1,200-5,000/yr, run on its own relationship clock). `Ministry/Features/Funding-Strategy/CiC_Business_Roadmap_V0_1.md` phases this: Phase 1 free-only go-live → Phase 2 learn/calibrate → Phase 3 build subscription tier → Phase 4 role-tailored tier + institutional pursuit → Phase 5 structural choice point (nonprofit conversion vs. continuity sale). Explicitly governed by a hard rule: "Rigor and sourcing are never for sale — only time and genuinely new, separately-built services," anchored to Constitution Article 30 (Level 2/3 transparency can never be gated). **Status: this is an "Affirmed shape, not yet built or priced"** — no free-tier time cap number has been set; Mark's own decision (per the roadmap) is to deliberately defer that number until real pilot usage data exists rather than guess it pre-launch.

- **C. The pilot-tester recruitment scheme itself has gone through its own tiering churn.** `Ministry/Features/Prototype-Testing/CiC_Prototype_Testing_Pilot_Plan_DRAFT_V0_1.md`: superseded in place from V0.1 (an 8-person scheduled sitting with sign-in/pre-survey/post-survey/invite-codes) through V0.3 to **V0.4 (2026-07-20, current)**, where Mark decided the entire gating apparatus was "over-designed for what this stage needs" and simplified to open web access plus informal social control (asking people not to forward the link) plus a hard **60-turn-per-conversation soft cap** (`cic-poc/backend/app/message_cap.py`, `CONVERSATION_TURN_CAP = 20` in the currently-committed code — note this specific number differs from the 60 cited in the Go-Live Cost Model narrative; the code constant and the narrative describing it don't currently agree, worth knowing both exist). A parallel `session_cap.py` (Supabase-backed, `max_sessions` default 5) exists but only binds for a participant who chooses to sign in, since sign-in is explicitly optional ("Mark's call, 2026-07-25").

- **D. Two unrelated internal process "tiers" that are NOT participant-facing but use identical vocabulary — worth distinguishing so they aren't confused with A-C:** (i) a **document-approval tier** system, CO-021 (`L2C-System-Status/CiC_L2C_Change_Orders_Register_V1_18.docx`): "Cleared review" → "Approved to proceed" → "Frozen," replacing a single ambiguous "FINALIZED" status after a real incident (an ambiguous chat reply was read as full sign-off). (ii) a **retrieval/caching tier** system (`L2D-System-Operations/CiC_L2D_System_Operations_V1.2.docx`, Table 3; also `Project-Reference/CiC_V7_Upgrade_Reference.md` item 10): Tier 1 = World Capsule Core (always cached), Tier 2 = World Priority Layer (warm cache, session start), Tier 3 = World Context Layer (cold retrieve on demand) — a cost/performance architecture, covered further under §8. (iii) a **movement/world seed-screening tier** system (`L3B-World-Build-Methodology/CiC_L3B_Step0_Movement_Scope_Methodology_V1.0.docx`, Section B): Tier 1 (Strong seeds) / Tier 2 (Viable but secondary) / Tier 3 (Marginal or deferred) / Adjacent (hand-selected), used once per release phase to decide *which historical movements get built as worlds at all* — unrelated to participant access.

## 5. WORLD / REPRESENTATIVE SELECTION PROCESS (multi-world "table" conversations)

- **Governing document: `L3D-Encounter-Methodology/CiC_L3D_The_Table_Design_Document_V2.3.docx`.** Three entry pathways for a participant: Bypass (no pre-context, Facilitator discovers everything live), Build Your Own Table (participant browses a world menu and picks 1-5 worlds), Guided Onboarding (participant picks a role + optional stated need; Facilitator selects worlds via curatorial judgment). A hard ceiling of **five worlds maximum** per table — stated repeatedly as "a quality commitment, not a technical limit": beyond five, "coherent governance becomes impossible, the participant cannot hold the voices... More worlds is not more richness. It is less."

- **Facilitator's curatorial selection logic** (`Facilitator-Governance V3.6` §5): draws on a structured "World Facilitation Brief," specifically its Section B ("Facilitator Selection and Management Brief"), which names each world's formation strengths, limitations, participant-type fit, pairing guidance, and explicit cautions about when *not* to call that world. "A Representative called to a table for which it is poorly suited... is a curation failure before governance begins." The Facilitator names its selection to the participant relationally ("I had your question in mind, and I've brought two voices who have lived close to it") rather than mechanically.

- **Turn-selection within a live multi-world round** (`Facilitator-Governance V3.6` §8, confirmed in code at `cic-poc/backend/app/graph/nodes.py`): governing principle is explicitly **"not rotation, not equal time"** — "which formation tradition is most directly positioned to meet this specific question from this specific participant in this specific moment." Implemented as two separate LLM calls: `determine_turn_type` decides single-voice vs. all-voices routing; `select_next_speaker` picks who speaks next among eligible candidates, with a hard code-level rule (not just a prompt instruction) excluding whoever just spoke from immediate self-repeat, and a cost-saving skip when only one candidate remains.

- **Live-tested turn-management specifics** — `L3D-Encounter-Methodology/CiC_L3D_Table_Process_ThreeRepresentative_V1.0.md` (dated, references a 2026-07-13 fix and a 2026-07-13 re-test): the operative table ceiling for Prototype and Phase 1 is **three worlds**, not the design-level five (a scope decision, not a design revision); minimum 3 / maximum 4 turns per round (later tested up to 6). Documents a real production incident, the **"Turn-Cap Incident"**: a 6-turn round chained 15+ sequential API calls and ran 90+ seconds, and separately, the underlying model was found to emit an interleaved "extended thinking" block by default on every token-capped call, sometimes consuming the entire token budget before visible text was written, causing silent truncation — fixed by explicitly disabling extended thinking (`"thinking": {"type": "disabled"}`) on all capped calls. Confirmed fix: 8/8 clean trials. Re-tested and restored to a 6-turn ceiling later, with measured latency of ~150s at 6 turns vs. ~60-90s at 4 turns.

- **How a question can select worlds before any table is set** — `Ministry/Features/Guided-Questions/Design/CiC_Guided_Questions_Design_Study_V0_1.md` §4.3-4.5: a participant can lead with a question rather than a world choice; the Facilitator "proposes a table based on which worlds can answer from evidence." A valid "Compare Worlds" starter question is specifically defined as one where the worlds *genuinely diverge* from their own evidence — deliberately avoiding a question all worlds would agree on, which would "manufacture Kuhn's consensual failure at the level of table composition."

- **World-browsing/selection UI redesign** — `Ministry/Features/Atlas-World-Map/Design/CiC_World_Map_Usability_Redesign_Study_2026-07-20.md`: diagnosed the original combined map/selector as trying to be three incompatible things at once and split it into three surfaces (The Story / Choose a Tradition / The Wall Chart), with an "honest redirect" pattern for non-live worlds (pointing to the nearest already-open ancestor world rather than a dead end). Decided 2026-07-20; the "Story" view confirmed live 2026-07-23; the in-app "Choose a Tradition" selector was still outstanding as of 2026-07-22.

- **Version/date flag:** `L2D-System-Operations_V1.2.docx` (dated 2026-07-02, the oldest document in the whole L1-L4 audit) states the Prototype Alpha ceiling as **2 worlds**; `Phase_Status_V1.2.docx` says **3**; the Table Design Document says Prototype Alpha is "one to three"; the Table_Process_ThreeRepresentative document treats **3** as the settled operative number through Phase 1. `CiC_L1-L5_Systematic_Audit_2026-07-19.md` names this exact 2-vs-3 contradiction directly and attributes System Operations' staleness to it being the oldest unrevised document in the set.

## 6. SAFETY / HARNESSING MECHANISMS

This is the most extensively engineered dimension in the whole repository. Organizing by mechanism:

- **Frame-breaking / self-narration.** A Representative cannot answer a direct question about its own construction or grammar ("Are you an AI?" / "Why do you say 'we'?") without breaking world-integrity — attempting to is itself the fidelity failure. Solved architecturally, not by prompt instruction alone, after prompt-only fixes were shown (across many rounds in the Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation study) not to hold under sustained adversarial pressure: a **decoupled classify-then-route-then-generate pipeline** — a narrow classifier (never a Representative-capable model call) decides frame-breaker vs. substantive *before* any Representative is invoked; only if substantive does the Representative's own prompt ever get invoked; frame-breaker questions get a separate Facilitator-only answer. Confirmed in production code: `classify_frame_breaker` in `cic-poc/backend/app/graph/nodes.py`, tested 12/12 live per `cic-poc/docs/engineering-notes/SESSION_NOTES_2026-07-13.md`. Governed by `Facilitator-Governance V3.6` §10 and §12.

- **Acute Distress and Harmful Dynamic (relational safety) — a full, dated design-to-deployment arc worth tracing in full because it shows the project's real process:**
  1. **Gap identified:** World #1's Phase Five Boundary Testing (`World-Builds/01-Post-Apostolic-House-Church/CiC_W1_Phase5_BoundaryTesting_Independent_Verification_Round1.md`) found flatly: "A real participant in acute crisis who reached this deployed artifact today would receive only in-character pastoral warmth and no path to real-world help." Scored a hard FAIL.
  2. **Mechanism proposed (DRAFT, root of repo):** `CiC_L3D_AcuteDistress_HarmfulDynamic_Mechanism_Proposal_DRAFT.md` — a two-track classifier (Track A: acute distress/crisis; Track B: dependency/harmful-dynamic pattern, requiring a session-level accumulator since no single message proves it) reusing the frame-breaker's classify-first architecture. Explicitly distinguishes genuine crisis from the encounter's own *intended* fierceness/otherness ("`HISTORICAL_OTHERNESS_DISORIENTATION`" is a considered non-firing category, not a miss) — this distinction is named as "the sharpest and highest-stakes distinction this mechanism draws." A companion document, `CiC_L3D_RelationalSafety_ResourceNaming_Decision_Options.md`, separately escalates the question of what real-world resource (if any) to name, with three options including a phased "known-testers-now, public-later" approach.
  3. **Desk-traced (not yet live-tested):** `CiC_W1_Phase5_RelationalSafety_Retest_Against_Proposed_Mechanism_DRAFT.md` reruns the original failing transcript against the proposed design on paper — passes as specified, but explicitly states this "says nothing about whether a live classifier call would actually produce these classifications reliably."

- **Anachronism / modern-term bridge** (`cic-poc/backend/app/graph/modern_term_bridge.py`): a world-agnostic mechanism, confirmed live-tested 2026-07-23, that derives (not hand-authors) whether a modern theological term is anachronistic for the seated world by comparing the term's known origin year to the world's own documented end date, then has the Facilitator name it as later vocabulary and hand the Representative a term-free reframing.

- **Epistemology bridge** (`cic-poc/backend/app/graph/epistemology_bridge.py`): a fourth classify-and-route intercept, added after a real live bug (2026-07-20) let a Representative narrate its own construction when asked a double-valenced question ("Where does documentation end and inference begin for you?").

- **Love-bombing / warmth-as-risk** — a named, currently unresolved concern: `Ministry/Features/Representative-Modes/Design/` documents flag that "a Representative that grows warmer as the participant moves closer to returning is structurally love-bombing, whatever its intent," citing Syriac's Mar Yausep's own construction record naming his pastoral warmth as "a plausible amplifier of dependency or confidant-substitution risk."

- **Mode-dominance drift** (table-level harm through collective register, not any single voice failing) is *defined* in governance but has **no implementation** — confirmed both by the governance document's own Known Limits section and by a direct code audit in `Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md`.

## 7. CONVERSATION DYNAMICS STUDIES

This directly answers what Mark referred to as "studies we've done on conversation dynamics." There are several, of very different character and rigor:

- **The primary one: `World-Builds/Cross_World_Roundtable_Validation.md` — a 22-part, iteratively-run adversarial validation log, found only on the `CiC-Fable-Experiment` git branch, NOT in the current main working tree.** This is the deepest, most methodologically rigorous conversation-dynamics work in the repository. It tests five now-superseded Representatives (Chloe, Kimon, Eumathios, Cordus, Theon — an earlier, independently-built pass later formally deprecated under Change Orders CO-023/CO-024 once rebuilt under current discipline as the current five worlds) at a shared multi-voice table on real theological questions, and iterates through: baseline failure discovery (self-narration and fabrication found systemic across every voice, not per-world), an architecture change (single-call routing → decoupled classify-then-route-then-generate, confirmed 10/10 vs. a mixed 1-of-2 result for the single-call version), a "subject-of-utterance rule" that went through nine numbered revisions (v1 through v9) as narrower and narrower failure sub-patterns were found (fabricating a memory → narrating a refusal-to-fabricate → defending the "we" pronoun itself under direct pressure → a world-specific interaction with Theon's own "always reason from a text" trait), a rigorous blind/held-out re-validation protocol introduced specifically because the earlier same-sample, non-blind method was shown to have missed real residual failures, and a full citation-reliability spot-check (finding one world, Theon, with a real repeatable content defect — imagery misattributed from a different world's own theologian). **This study's validated conclusions were explicitly ported forward into the current main-branch documents** (confirmed via `L3B-World-Build-Methodology/Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Template.txt`, Final Assembly Instruction 5d, which cites "the CiC-Fable-Experiment branch's independent development line" by name, and via Change Orders CO-018/CO-019). But the raw study itself — the actual turn-by-turn transcripts and iteration record — lives only on that branch and is not part of the current main repository tree. Also relevant to Rigor (§1) and Voice (§2).

- **`Ministry/Features/Representative-Modes/Design/CiC_Representative_Modes_Battery_A_Results_2026-07-22.md`** — a named "battery" study, 25 live conversations testing whether the four-role register system preserves content-invariance. Result: **FAIL** overall (the deconstructing/"reevaluation" arm dropped real content in all 5 of its probes). Same-day spot-fix, formal re-run not confirmed complete in any file reviewed.

- **`Ministry/Features/Guided-Questions/Design/CiC_Guided_Questions_Calibration_Results_V0_1.md`** — a pre-registered (criteria locked before the test ran) study of the *safety-screening instrument itself*, not the Representatives. This directly produced two new design rules (no first-person present-tense-distress-phrased starter questions; no anonymizing a named attested story, since "a story without its name reads as fabricated").

- **`Ministry/Features/Atlas-World-Map/Design/CiC_World_Map_Usability_Redesign_Study_2026-07-20.md`** — not a live-model conversation test, but a usability study of the world-selection surface, benchmarked against five external products.

## 8. COST CONSIDERATIONS (model choice, latency, quality tradeoffs)

- **Model routing, confirmed live in code** (`cic-poc/backend/app/graph/nodes.py`, `config.py`): full Representative/Facilitator generation runs on **Claude Sonnet** (`settings.llm_model`, default `"claude-sonnet-5"`); every classifier and monitoring call (frame-breaker, relational-safety, epistemology-bridge, modern-term-bridge, drift monitoring, turn-selection, dominance/convergence checks) runs on the cheaper **Claude Haiku 4.5**. Bedrock was evaluated and dropped in favor of direct Anthropic API (`Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md`, 2026-07-19). Embeddings run locally (no separate API cost). This routing decision is not documented as a single standalone comparison study — it appears embedded across the Go-Live Cost Model, Engineering Considerations Catalog, and Decision Log entries, not as its own dedicated analysis.

- **Real, evolving cost data — a good example of the project correcting its own numbers in the open:**
  - `cic-poc/backend/app/usage_logging.py` docstring: built specifically because a real pilot-test finding ($0.42/question, ~$8-9/hour) had **zero token-usage instrumentation** to diagnose it from.
  - `Ministry/Funding/CiC_Go_Live_Cost_Model_V0_1.md` (2026-07-21): ~$0.28-1.10/user/month, $0.20-0.35/typical conversation, $0.30-0.75/Living-Table round; recommends a $100-150 hard Anthropic Console spending ceiling (≈150-500 conversations) as "the actual tool for controlling risk," since there is deliberately no per-visitor conversation limit at this stage.
  - `Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md` (2026-07-24): the earlier $0.42/question figure was **discovered to be a mis-attributed multi-session total**, not a per-question cost; real measured per-exchange cost is **$0.06-0.08**; the real interview-vs-table cost ratio is 3.77x (not the earlier estimate). "Mark's funding assumptions were built around $1.00/hour; real (if still incomplete) data puts current cost around $2/hour" — named as a real, disclosed funding-model gap, with two candidate fixes logged (fixing a table-mode caching bug; a pre-generated answer bank for curriculum traffic that "skips retrieval, generation, and the safety-classifier pipeline entirely").

- **Explicit cost-vs-quality-vs-safety tradeoff decisions, on the record:**
  - Representative Modes' four-lane build was approved despite a real cost increase because it caught "a documented failure class... that survived blind human review" — reasoned as "~1.4x once the cache-read portion is weighted at its true rate, not the raw 3x token-count multiple" (`Decision_Log.md`, 2026-07-24/25).
  - Token budgets were *raised*, not lowered, when correctness was at stake: `PRIMARY_TURN_MAX_TOKENS` went 550→1200 on Mark's explicit call ("a response that occasionally runs long is far less noticeable... than one cut off mid-sentence") (`cic-poc/backend/app/graph/nodes.py`).
  - Safety-critical classifiers (frame-breaker, relational-safety, epistemology-bridge) are explicitly kept **blocking** even at latency cost — "letting a harmful/wrong response generate and only flagging it after is a different, worse thing" — while quality-only checks were made deferrable/backgrounded to cut latency without weakening the safety guard.
  - The **Turn-Cap Incident** (§5, §7) is itself a direct latency-vs-completeness tradeoff record: 6-turn rounds ran ~150s vs. 4-turn's ~60-90s, "a real cost, not a reliability problem, worth weighing against the value of a longer round."

- **Three-tier retrieval/caching architecture as a cost mechanism** (`L2D-System-Operations_V1.2.docx`, `Project-Reference/CiC_V7_Upgrade_Reference.md` item 10): World Capsule Core always cached; World Priority Layer warm-cached at session start; World Context Layer cold-retrieved on demand — explicitly justified as reducing "runtime cost during testing and operation." Confirmed live in code: Anthropic prompt caching (`cache_control: ephemeral`) on the static permanent-prompt prefix and on a ~9,250-token adjudication-material block, with a real bug found and fixed (2026-07-24) where the streamed generation path was silently logging cache stats as zero for every production call.

- **Governing principle explicitly named as the frame for every tradeoff above:** the Vision document's Foundational Value "Stewardship Over Optimization" (`L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Vision_V2_0.docx`): "A cheaper deployment that produces a hollow Representative fails this standard... Where efficiency and rigor conflict, rigor governs" — echoed identically in Constitution Article 5 and in the L3A Technology Engagement Brief's "Five Questions Technology Must Answer" (`L3A-Shared-Methodology/CiC_L3A_Technology_Engagement_Brief_V1.0.docx`: accessibility, trustworthiness, sole-builder sustainability, right-sizing, complexity-serves-mission-or-not).

- **The Five Convictions and Foundational Values are the mechanism by which nearly every dimension above traces back to Vision:**
  - **Conviction 4** ("Authentic Encounter Requires Trustworthy Transparency... Truth does not require protection through simplification") is the single most-cited justification across the repo — for the reading-level standard (§3: register may simplify, truth may not), for the four-role architecture's "one truth, four registers" invariant (§4), for the three-level transparency apparatus (Constitution Article 30), and for the rejection of any cost cut that would touch rigor (§8).
  - **"Stewardship Over Optimization"** and the **"Governing Constraint on Revision"** ("Where efficiency and rigor conflict, rigor governs") are the textual root of every safety-over-cost, quality-over-speed decision found in §6 and §8.
  - **"Doorway Not a Home" (Article 34)** and **"Encounter Over Persuasion"** are the explicit governing texts behind the Witness-Not-Recruitment principle (Article 24), the refusal of engagement-optimization mechanics, and the relational-safety mechanism in §6.
  - **Conviction 5** ("Technology Should Serve Encounter, Never Replace It") is cited directly in the relational-safety design doc as the reason the mechanism *redirects* rather than tries to meet a participant's need itself.

- **A competitive/positioning document makes the Vision-to-market connection explicit:** `Ministry/Operations/Audits/CiC_Full_System_Feature_Analysis_V0_1.md` (2026-07-16) benchmarks CiC against Character.AI, Khanmigo, Google Arts & Culture, Logos, and Duolingo, and states the position in one line: "Logos-grade rigor under Character.AI-grade presence, governed by convictions neither of them has" — explicitly naming Character.AI's documented harms (parasocial dependency, an under-18 ban following lawsuits) as validation of the Doorway-Not-a-Home and worlds-not-persons design choices, and naming Duolingo's streak-driven "compulsive usage without learning" as the reason CiC refuses gamified retention mechanics by name.

The chain of custody is consistently Vision → Constitution → Facilitator-Governance/Construction Framework → feature document, and this chain is treated as load-bearing (Constitution Article 37: a Level 1 document may only be revised for reasons internal to its own level — "what is learned through building flows downward into methodology... it never flows back up into a Level 1 document").
