# CiC System Redesign Research — Index

Compiled 2026-07-25, the night before the Pass 1 Fable design brief was drafted (`Ministry/Technology/CiC_System_Redesign_Fable_Brief_2026-07-25.md` — see that document for the actual ask). These are the full, unabridged research passes that informed the brief. The brief itself only summarizes and cites this material — read these directly for the full evidence, quotes, and file citations behind every claim in it.

**Order matters less than coverage — read what's relevant to the section of the brief you're working on.**

| 12 | `12_Academic_Source_Organization_Standards.md` | Dispatched 2026-07-26, at Mark's direction, to answer directly: how do top universities and academic institutions organize source data, and how does CiC's own Source Registry compare? Verdict-per-finding against CiC's real, populated apparatus (not a strawman): 12 things CiC already does at real academic standard or better (per-claim traceability, graded confidence, an append-only record, an affirmative duty to name missing voices exceeding the field's own dominant content standard), and 15 real gaps ranked by severity — highest is no attribution/authenticity field (genuine vs. dubious vs. pseudonymous authorship), which CiC's own Syriac registry already needed and handled correctly in prose with no field to make the judgment queryable. Ends with a concrete, field-level schema addition (Tier 1/2/3) and three non-schema actions, including a named, low-cost path to real outside academic peer review (*Reviews in Digital Humanities*, which accepts projects mid-development). **Partially applied:** its highest-severity finding and the then-vs-now semantic discipline it's adjacent to are now in brief §9 (`period_sense`/`prior_sense`/`modern_sense`/`conceptual_distance_note`, quote as first-class type). The full field-level schema, the lexicon-template standardization, and the three-level-content spec are still open, now informed by docs 13-16 below as well. |
| 13 | `13_Terminology_Alignment_Audit.md` | A systematic audit of CiC's home-grown vocabulary against real academic and technical fields, dispatched to check whether "we intuitively tried to build something that we can standardize to industry terminology." Reads every real CiC mechanism first (the live adjudicator prompts, Doc_04/07/08 templates), then verdicts each: **certainty distortion / epistemic faithfulness** (a 2026 AI paper) is the exact published name for "Over-Settling," and CiC's opposite-fail-open asymmetry anticipates the paper's own measured 1.5-2x inflation bias; **emic/etic** names a distinction CiC makes in five places under five different improvised names; **FABRICATED** should split into intrinsic/extrinsic per the field's own hallucination taxonomy; "lens" and next-speaker selection already align with the field; **gravity + force as a pair map onto nothing published** and are reported honestly as CiC's own contribution, though Ortner's "key symbols" and Archer's morphogenetic cycle are named precedents for pieces of each. Closes with a table locating every recommendation against the specific brief section or code file it touches. |
| 14 | `14_Source_Identification_Discovery_Methodology.md` | Upstream of doc 12: not how a source is described once found, but how it's found at all — would a second builder discover the same set? Reads all of Doc_02's real governing documents and three real built worlds' source registries first. Finding: source discovery is *not* untuned like retrieval volume — it's one of the most heavily reviewed things in the project (nine documented rounds on one world's Doc_02 alone) — but every finding in that review record is about traceability or accuracy, never about a source that was never found. Real gaps: no search-strategy field (STARLITE/PRISMA-S), no stopping criterion for Step 2 specifically (though the project wrote one, well, for a different step, and executed it once in an archived build), and a named, free, patristics-specific bibliography (BIBP) never once consulted. Closes with three new registry fields and a field-bibliography sweep as the cheapest real fix found in this whole research arc. |
| 15 | `15_Retrieval_Architecture_Best_Practices.md` | CiC's actual retrieval code measured against current RAG practice — and it found a real, previously-undiscovered bug: the embedding model's 256-word-piece limit means roughly 85% of an Alexandria lexicon chunk is invisible to similarity search, and `Retrieve-When`/`Do-Not-Retrieve-When` — the single most retrieval-relevant field an author writes — is never embedded at all, only read later by the LLM filter. This is the mechanical cause of doc 08's "pure vector similarity" finding, and it's worse than that finding stated. Also: Anthropic's own contextual-retrieval guidance says five of six worlds are small enough not to need retrieval at all, and CiC's own discarded `Quick Meaning` field would make all of a world's terms reachable at a tenth of current cost; GraphRAG's traversal is worth adopting, its expensive extraction stage is not, since CiC's hand-authored Related-Terms graph is already better than what GraphRAG would infer. Closes with a zero-API-cost retrieval evaluation harness (ID-based RAGAS variants, computable from data CiC already logs) as the single highest-value, lowest-cost fix in the document. |
| 16 | `16_MultiParty_Dialogue_Architecture.md` | The Facilitator's turn-selection and multi-world table mechanics against conversational grounding theory, conversation analysis, and multi-party dialogue-systems research. Reads the real turn-selection code first and finds the single-vs-all routing decision the brief already flagged as a two-engine problem is worse than described: it doesn't exist on the live streaming path at all, so a directly-addressed single-world question is still structurally compelled to pull in a second voice. Also found: every CiC drift signal is negative evidence only, which the founding grounding-theory paper explicitly names as insufficient by itself; a second, undocumented drift-signal bottleneck worse than the one the brief already names; and Facilitator turns are entirely excluded from what Representatives can read, which silently breaks the modern-term bridge's own repair mechanism. Closes with a prioritized fix list led by a repair-initiation classifier addressing a measured, model-specific vulnerability (Claude reverses ~25% of correct answers under third-position pushback) that is live today, not hypothetical. |
| 17 | `17_Build_Process_Sequencing_Methodology.md` | Dispatched to answer directly: is CiC building world → representative → facilitator/table in the right order? **The premise turned out to be wrong in a way that matters.** CiC did not build the Facilitator/Table layer last — it built the governance specification first, before multiple worlds existed, then never made it a per-world gate. Verified against four fields (living-history first-person interpretation, worldbuilding/game design, multi-agent AI orchestration, Stanislavski-tradition actor training): nobody recommends reordering world→representative→table, so don't. The real defect, measured directly from the governing documents: the Construction Framework says "table" zero times; Facilitator Governance says "forces" or "gravity" zero times; and a governance requirement (anchor to another world's name) reached zero of five deployed Permanent Prompts until the sixth world's multi-voice test finally caught it. Four documented defects only a shared table ever surfaced, none catchable by any solo-world probe. Closes with one recommended gate (a per-world "Table Readiness Round," not a reorder) plus five smaller fixes. |
| 18 | `18_Opus_Adversarial_Review_Round3_of_Brief.md` | A third adversarial review of the Fable brief, dispatched 2026-07-26 to check everything folded in since the second round (docs 12-17, the §9 field additions, the measurement-plan deliverable) — none of it had been checked by anyone. Found five send-blocking defects, the most severe an inverted causal claim in §4 (the certainty-distortion clause names the wrong direction of a live safety bias) that could lead Fable to flip it. Also flags a misattributed statistic and an unsourced "industry-standard" framework in the new measurement-plan deliverable, plus 14 P1 and 12 P2 findings. See `Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_Adversarial_Review_Standard_Practice.md` for the review method this and the two prior rounds all followed. |
