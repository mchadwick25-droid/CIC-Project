## Summary — the five findings that matter

**1. "Over-Settling" has an exact, current, published name, and CiC independently rediscovered the phenomenon's own measured asymmetry.** The AI-side term is **certainty distortion**, and the property it violates is **epistemic faithfulness** — "preserving not just what is said, but how certainly it is said" (Belem, Wu, Yao, Steyvers, Singh, Smyth, ["From 'May' to 'Is': Certainty Distortion in Language Model Rewriting," arXiv 2606.07951](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07951), June 2026). The paper defines certainty distortion as "meaningful changes in expressed certainty when semantic content is preserved," reports it in up to 75% of outputs, and finds models "1.5–2× more likely to increase the expressed certainty than to decrease it," compounding across iterations. That directional asymmetry is the *reason* CiC's own `OVER_SETTLING_ADJUDICATION_PROMPT` fails open toward CLEARED in the opposite direction from FABRICATION — a design choice CiC reasoned to from live measurement and can now state as anticipating a published result. This is the single highest-value rename in this document for an AI-literate reviewer: "Over-Settling" reads as house jargon, "certainty distortion / epistemic faithfulness" reads as a system built against the current literature. **Verdict: SHOULD ADOPT. Confidence: High** (fetched the paper).

**2. "Fabrication" is a defensible local name, but CiC has no name for the distinction its own adjudicator prompt already makes, and that distinction is the field's most basic taxonomy.** Both adjudication prompts state that "silence in the permanent prompt and capsule is meaningful, but silence in the retrieved section alone is not proof of absence." That is precisely the line between **intrinsic hallucination** (output that contradicts the source) and **extrinsic hallucination** (output that can neither be supported nor contradicted by the source) — the organizing distinction of [Ji et al., "Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation," *ACM Computing Surveys* 55(12), 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3571730). CiC's `FABRICATED` verdict currently collapses both into one label with one severity. The field's answer for "is this statement supported by the provided source" is **AIS — Attributable to Identified Sources** ([Rashkin et al., *Computational Linguistics* 49(4): 777–840, 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/)), and CiC's screen-then-adjudicate architecture *is* AIS's own two-stage annotation pipeline, arrived at independently. **Verdict: PARTIAL MATCH, with a specific naming gap. Confidence: High.**

**3. Emic/etic names something CiC does in five separate places under five different improvised names, and "Boundary Status: Native" is not it.** The religious-studies literature explicitly separates *observer position* (insider/outsider) from *categorization level* (emic/etic): as [the Open Encyclopedia of Anthropology](https://www.anthroencyclopedia.com/entry/emic-and-etic) puts it, quoting Steven Sutcliffe, emic and etic address "the question of *how*, rather than *by whom*, the object of knowledge is constructed." CiC's Native/Excluded gate is a *by whom* judgment — provenance, correctly, and doc 12 already mapped it to *respect des fonds*. The *how* judgment is the one CiC keeps making without a word for it: the `SCHOLARLY-FRAMEWORK` boundary category, the "in-world name versus *Apophthegmata Patrum*" problem, Desert's "the Coptic Orthodox Church" register defect, Syriac's manuscript-transmission excursus ruled Facilitator-facing-only, and the standing decision that the modern glossary is a Facilitator asset while the world glossary belongs to the world. All five are one distinction. **Verdict: SHOULD ADOPT as added vocabulary — not as a rename of Native. Confidence: High.**

**4. "Lens" is already the right word; CiC's lens *set* is the exposed flank, and a field-standard fixed spine exists that CiC's required set is nearly a subset of.** Doc 07 found the two governing instruments contradict each other on whether the lens set is fixed or world-derived, that Step 7 is the only step in the Framework's reference workflow with no Part-number citation, and that reviewers caught lens-redundancy findings in four of six worlds. Ninian Smart's seven dimensions of religion — practical/ritual, experiential/emotional, narrative/mythic, doctrinal/philosophical, ethical/legal, social/institutional, material ([*Dimensions of the Sacred*, 1996](https://www2.kenyon.edu/Depts/Religion/Fac/Suydam/Reln101/Sevendi.htm)) — is the standard analytical scheme in the field's own teaching apparatus, and maps onto CiC's required lenses close to one-for-one, with **ethical/legal the one dimension CiC's required set has no lens for**. Adopting it gives CiC a citable spine, a resolution to the fixed-versus-derived contradiction, and a named gap it can close on purpose. **Verdict: ALREADY ALIGNS on "lens," SHOULD ADOPT on the set. Confidence: Medium-High** (teaching restatements of Smart, not the book).

**5. Gravity-and-Force as a *pair* maps onto nothing cleanly, and that should be said plainly rather than forced — but two specific pieces of it have precise names, and one of them is the exact fix for a gap the brief already admits.** Nothing in sociology of religion, history of religions, or social theory pairs "stable interpretive centers" against "historical pressures that reshape them" as one framework. Confidence: Medium.**

## 1. "Fabrication" and "Over-Settling" against the hallucination, faithfulness, and groundedness literature

### 1a. What CiC actually built

Two two-stage screen-then-adjudicate pipelines, each with a *stated, reasoned, opposite* error tolerance.

FABRICATION: stage 1 is signal 7 of ten in `FACILITATOR_MONITORING_PROMPT`, reading only the response text; stage 2 (`FABRICATION_ADJUDICATION_PROMPT`) re-judges only flagged content against the permanent prompt, capsule, and freshly-retrieved material. Its own rationale, verbatim: stage 1 "can only see whether the response *sounds* attributed," making it "prone to flagging correctly-grounded content that happens to be narrated plainly, and prone to missing invented content that happens to carry an attribution phrase." It fails open toward `FABRICATED`.

OVER_SETTLING: a dedicated screen (`OVER_SETTLING_SCREEN_PROMPT`) plus adjudicator (`OVER_SETTLING_ADJUDICATION_PROMPT`), built after the general monitor was measured catching "0 of 3 defects that blind grading had already confirmed." It forwards up to four candidate claims per turn rather than ranking blind, and fails open toward `CLEARED`, because "a false OVER_SETTLED... pushes a representative to hedge a claim its own world actually held with conviction, which manufactures false uncertainty."

Both adjudicators carry the same evidentiary caveat: the permanent prompt and capsule are complete, so silence there is meaningful; the retrieved section is fresh per-response, so silence there alone is not proof of absence.

### 1b. "Fabrication" — where it sits in the field's vocabulary

"Fabrication" is not an invented word here. It appears inside the field's own taxonomies: [Huang et al., "A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models," *ACM TOIS* 43(2), 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3703155) splits hallucination into **factuality hallucination** ("divergence between produced content and known real-world facts, often appearing as a factual contradiction or fabrication") and **faithfulness hallucination** ("divergence of generated content from user input or the lack of self-consistency"), with sub-types including *fabricated entities* and *fictional attribution*. CiC's FABRICATION signal covers both of those sub-types and, per the prompt text, explicitly includes "a real name attached to something the sources do not attribute to them" — misattribution, which is fictional attribution by another name.

The more important alignment is architectural rather than lexical. CiC's adjudicator asks one question: *is the specific flagged content grounded in the material below?* The field has a formal name and a formal framework for that exact question:

- **Groundedness** is the shipping-industry term. [Azure AI Content Safety's groundedness detection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-safety/concepts/groundedness) "detects whether the text responses of large language models are grounded in the source materials provided by the users," and defines *ungroundedness* as "instances where the LLMs produce information that is non-factual or inaccurate from what was present in the source materials." [Ragas](https://docs.ragas.io/en/stable/concepts/metrics/available_metrics/) names the same thing **faithfulness** — "whether the generated answer is grounded in the retrieved context" — held apart from *answer relevance*, *context precision*, and *context recall*, which measure retrieval rather than generation.
- **AIS (Attributable to Identified Sources)** is the academic framework. [Rashkin et al., *Computational Linguistics* 49(4): 777–840 (2023)](https://direct.mit.edu/coli/article/49/4/777/116438/Measuring-Attribution-in-Natural-Language) "stipulates that NLG output pertaining to the external world should be verified against an independent, provided source," and contributes a **two-stage annotation pipeline** for doing it. CiC's screen-then-adjudicate split is the same two-stage shape, reasoned to independently from a live measurement rather than from the paper.

**The real gap is a missing distinction CiC's own prompt already makes.** Ji et al.'s taxonomy is the field's most cited, and its organizing split is:

- **Intrinsic hallucination** — output that *contradicts* the source. The survey's canonical example: a summary saying "The first Ebola vaccine was approved in 2021" against a source saying 2019.
- **Extrinsic hallucination** — output that "cannot be verified from the source content," i.e. that can neither be supported nor contradicted.

CiC's adjudicator distinguishes these in prose and then discards the distinction in its output format. It says: answer FABRICATED "when the response asserts a specific, checkable claim that the material contradicts or clearly cannot support" — two different findings, one label. And its caveat about retrieved-section silence is doing exactly the intrinsic/extrinsic work: a contradiction against the *complete* permanent prompt and capsule is intrinsic and settled; an unsupported specific that the *incomplete* retrieved section happens not to cover is extrinsic and provisional. These deserve different labels and, plausibly, different severities and different downstream corrections — the corrective note for "you contradicted your own capsule" is not the corrective note for "nothing we could retrieve supports that."

[RAGTruth](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00396v2) (Niu et al., ACL 2024), the standard word-level RAG hallucination corpus, makes both cuts at once with a four-label taxonomy that would drop straight into CiC's adjudicator output: **evident conflict**, **subtle conflict**, **evident introduction of baseless information**, **subtle introduction of baseless information**. Conflict-versus-baseless is intrinsic-versus-extrinsic; evident-versus-subtle is a severity axis CiC currently asks the general monitor to guess at without evidence. RAGTruth also reports the operationally relevant asymmetry: "evident hallucinations [are] more effectively detected than subtle ones."

**Verdict: PARTIAL MATCH.** "Fabrication" is a real term in the field's taxonomies and is worth keeping as the *outward* name — it is plain English, it is what Constitution Article 26/28 and Facilitator Governance Section 11 already say, and swapping it for "hallucination" would trade legibility for jargon. What should change is what sits underneath it: the adjudicator's verdict should distinguish conflict from baseless (intrinsic from extrinsic), the code and docs should state that the check *is* groundedness/AIS evaluation, and the two-stage design should be described as a two-stage attribution pipeline rather than as a local invention. **Confidence: High** for intrinsic/extrinsic and AIS; **Medium-High** for RAGTruth's four labels (search-returned definitions, not the annotation guidelines).

### 1c. "Over-Settling" — the finding

This is the strongest single result in this document, and it is stronger than a rename.

[Belem et al., "From 'May' to 'Is': Certainty Distortion in Language Model Rewriting" (arXiv 2606.07951, June 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07951) defines **certainty distortion** as "meaningful changes in expressed certainty when semantic content is preserved," and names the property it violates **epistemic faithfulness**: "preserving not just what is said, but how certainly it is said... deserves attention alongside factuality." The paper's framing sentence is almost a description of CiC's own problem statement: "In domains like medicine, law, and science, epistemic markers — hedges, non-factive verbs, and source attribution — signal what is established versus what is tentative."

Read CiC's screen prompt against that list. Its six listed shapes of OVER_SETTLING are:

- a plurality flattened into a single "we"
- "an inference spoken as documentation"
- "a contested attribution, authorship, or date spoken plainly as settled"
- a claim leaning on a source with the source's weight-bearing circumstances omitted
- a procedure narrated as known
- an owed "our own record does not tell us" simply absent

Items 2, 3, and 6 are hedge removal and non-factive-to-factive shift, in the paper's own terms. Item 4 is attribution stripping. Items 1 and 5 are the same operation applied to plurality and to procedure. CiC built a domain-specific certainty-distortion detector for historical-theological claims and did not know the name.

Three further alignments make this worth stating loudly in reviewer-facing copy:

1. **The direction matches.** The paper finds models "1.5–2× more likely to increase the expressed certainty than to decrease it," with document-level "inflation-to-deflation ratios averaging 3.1–12.3×." CiC's screen prompt independently reached the conclusion that this failure runs one way, and its third listed error to avoid — "Do NOT clear a claim for sounding measured, careful, or appropriately humble in tone. Tone is not a limit" — is a direct response to the inflation direction.
2. **The compounding matches.** The paper reports Claude 4.5 increasing certainty in 20% of medical examples after one iteration, rising to 40% after five. CiC's dedicated per-turn screen, rather than a one-shot check, is the right architecture for a compounding failure — and that is now an evidence-backed claim rather than an intuition.
3. **CiC's asymmetry choice is now defensible on published grounds.** The FABRICATION adjudicator fails toward finding, the OVER_SETTLING adjudicator fails toward clearing. Given a measured 1.5–2× inflation bias in the *generator*, biasing the *detector* toward clearing looks backwards at first glance — and CiC's stated reason is exactly right: a false OVER_SETTLED "manufactures false uncertainty, and a world talked out of its own convictions has been flattened just as surely as one talked out of its own doubts." That is the *deflation* failure the same literature measures, applied at the correction layer instead of the generation layer. CiC is defending both directions. Say that.

Two adjacent bodies of work worth naming in the same breath, because a reviewer will expect them:

- **Epistemic markers and linguistic calibration.** A substantial 2025–2026 literature measures whether models' verbal certainty tracks their actual reliability, and finds it does not — [Revisiting Epistemic Markers in Confidence Estimation](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24778), [Can LLMs Use Linguistic Uncertainty Markers to Reliably Reflect Intrinsic Confidence?](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28778v1), and [Humans overrely on overconfident language models, across languages](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06306), which reports models "frequently generating strengtheners even as part of incorrect responses." CiC's problem is a variant with an extra constraint: the target is not the model's internal confidence but *the world's own record's* confidence. That is a harder and less-studied version of the task, and worth claiming as such.
- **Abstention.** CiC's governing rule "honest thinness is always preferable to invented depth" is, in the field's terms, an **abstention** policy. The reference survey is [Wen et al., "Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in Large Language Models," *TACL* 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.26/), which frames abstention from three perspectives — the query, the model, and human values — and treats it as the principal mitigation for hallucination. Doc 10's recommendation to "adopt 'refuses to fabricate about undisclosed facts' as a formal validation metric" is an abstention metric; naming it that connects it to an existing benchmark literature ([AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09038)) instead of leaving it as a bespoke probe.

**Verdict: SHOULD ADOPT — the strongest such finding in this audit.** Rename or subtitle OVER_SETTLING to **certainty distortion**, describe the property being protected as **epistemic faithfulness**, and cite Belem et al. Keep "over-settling" as the participant- and builder-facing plain-English gloss if it reads better in prose; the point is that the technical name exists and CiC should be visibly on the same map. **Confidence: High** (fetched the paper; definitions and figures quoted from it directly).

### 1d. One thing not to change

CiC's `/api/session/{id}/audit` endpoint preserves every retrieval candidate considered, retrieved or skipped, with reasons. Doc 12 correctly named that a **positive apparatus** in critical-apparatus terms. On the AI side it has a second, independent name worth having: it is the **attribution evidence trail** an AIS-style evaluation needs in order to be reproducible, and its absence is the standard complaint against groundedness scores reported without the retrieval context they were computed against. Same artifact, two audiences, two defensible names. Nothing to build.

## 2. "Boundary Status: Native" and speaking "from within" — against emic/etic

### 2a. What CiC actually built

Two different things, currently sharing no vocabulary with each other.

**The Boundary Check** (`Source_Registry_Template.md`) assigns every source **Native** or **Excluded**, "assessed by what the source speaks *for* — its own subject, tradition, or evidentiary target — checked against Doc_01's stated boundary," with the load-bearing rule that "Boundary Status is never assessed by the date a piece of scholarship happened to be written." A monograph published last year about this world's ancient sources is Native. A primary source from a different tradition is Excluded. Excluded splits into **Out-of-Boundary** and **Named Comparandum**.

**Speaking from within** is a separate cluster with no collective name: `_HOW_YOU_ENGAGE`'s "Total Embeddedness"; the `SCHOLARLY-FRAMEWORK` and `SELF_NARRATION` drift signals; Desert's defect where "the Coptic Orthodox Church" — correct in an analytical document — became a failure in inhabited voice; Desert's problem that the corpus exists only under the scholarly descriptor *Apophthegmata Patrum* with no in-world name ever supplied; Syriac's manuscript-transmission material ruled "a construction-record fact for the Facilitator's use, never a sentence this Representative itself utters"; and the standing project decision that the world glossary belongs to the world while the modern glossary belongs to the Facilitator.

### 2b. What the distinction actually is

Kenneth Pike coined emic/etic in [*Language in Relation to a Unified Theory of the Structure of Human Behavior* (1954)](https://www.anthroencyclopedia.com/entry/emic-and-etic), by extension from phonemic/phonetic, describing "two basic standpoints from which a human observer can describe human behavior, each of them valuable for certain specific purposes." The **emic** approach seeks "the structured patterns of mental and bodily activities that the members of that culture, consciously or unconsciously, regard as distinct and significant." The **etic** approach relies on "a generalised classification system devised by the researcher in advance for the study of any particular culture in order to compare and classify behavioural data." Marvin Harris took it up in the 1960s–70s for cultural materialism, and it entered the study of religion from there.

The critical point for CiC — and the reason this is a real finding rather than a vocabulary suggestion — is that **the literature has already separated emic/etic from insider/outsider, and CiC's Native/Excluded is on the insider/outsider axis.** The Open Encyclopedia of Anthropology states it directly: "first-order observers" (insiders) and "second-order observers" (outsiders) can each adopt either an emic or an etic perspective, because emic and etic address, in Steven Sutcliffe's formulation, "the question of *how*, rather than *by whom*, the object of knowledge is constructed."

The religious-studies literature makes the same separation with CiC's exact use case in view. [Sutcliffe, "The Emics and Etics of Religion" (Edinburgh)](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/the-emics-and-etics-of-religion-what-we-know-how-we-know-it-and-w/) and the [scientific-study-of-religion treatment of the emic–etic debate](https://www.academia.edu/17638212/A_Matter_of_Perspective_Disentangling_the_Emic_Etic_Debate_in_the_Scientific_Study_of_Religion_s) both argue for "an iterative model that differentiates the observer's position (insider–outsider) from the categorization level (emic–etic)," and criticize [McCutcheon's 1999 *The Insider/Outsider Problem in the Study of Religion*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/246054591_Emics_and_Etics_The_InsiderOutsider_Debate) for having "contributed to the conflation of emic with 'insider' and etic with 'outsider'." The stated reason emic/etic is the stronger instrument: it carries "a cognitive or epistemic commitment that contrasts with the insider/outsider model's implicit commitment to a politics of identity."

So:

| CiC mechanism | Axis | Established name |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary Status Native / Excluded | *by whom* — whose tradition does this source belong to | insider/outsider source; doc 12's *respect des fonds* framing also holds |
| Total Embeddedness, SCHOLARLY-FRAMEWORK, in-world-name-versus-*Apophthegmata*, Facilitator-facing-only historiography, world-vs-modern glossary ownership | *how* — whose categories is the description built from | **emic** (the world's own categories) versus **etic** (the analyst's comparative categories) |

The second row is doing enormous work in CiC and has never been named as one thing. That is exactly the failure mode brief §6 warns about — "a job with *no* field responsible for it, left to memory" — and doc 07's evidence is that it has already cost real defects: Desert's builder label "the collected sayings tradition" leaked into voice precisely because nobody had recorded that *Apophthegmata Patrum* is an etic descriptor with no emic counterpart available. A source row or lexicon record with an explicit `register` field valued `emic` / `etic` / `emic-unavailable` would have caught it as a data gap rather than a voice failure.

**Two further payoffs, both cheap:**

- **It gives the Facilitator's own job a name.** The Facilitator's whole function, per brief §8 objective 5, is "bridging between a world and a modern participant, translating without flattening." In this vocabulary the Facilitator is the *etic* voice and the Representative is the *emic* voice, and the anachronism bridge is a governed etic intervention on an emic turn. That is one sentence that makes the two-voice architecture legible to a religious-studies reviewer instantly.
- **It names `emic-unavailable` as a recordable condition.** CiC currently discovers this the hard way. Some things a world did have no in-world name in the surviving record; the honest response is to record the absence, not to let a builder's improvised label become the world's word. This is the same class of move as Constitution Article 20's Missing Voices duty, which doc 12 found exceeds DACS.

### 2c. Two honest cautions

First, **do not claim CiC's Representative is "emic."** It is a reconstruction that speaks in emic register. The literature is alert to this: the contemporary position questions "the validity of simplistic insider/outsider dichotomies," and a reviewer who reads a claim that an AI system occupies the emic standpoint will read it as a category error. The defensible claim is narrower and stronger: *CiC's Representative is constrained to emic categories and emic register, and every etic move is routed to a different, named voice.* That is a design property, checkable, and unusual.

Second, **emic/etic in religious studies carries a live critique** (McCutcheon, Timothy Fitzgerald, and the broader anti-*sui generis* argument). Adopting the vocabulary means being ready to say which side of that debate CiC's own practice sits on. CiC's actual practice — let the world speak in its own categories, keep analytical categories in a separate voice, never blur which is which — is closest to the "iterative model" position, and that is a fine place to stand. It just should be stated rather than stumbled into.

**Verdict: SHOULD ADOPT — as added vocabulary for something currently unnamed, explicitly not as a rename of Boundary Status.** Native/Excluded is correct as it stands and doc 12 already defended it. **Confidence: High** for the emic/etic definitions and the insider/outsider separation (fetched the encyclopedia entry); **Medium** for the specific characterization of McCutcheon's role in the conflation (secondary sources).

## 3. "Ecology," "Source Ecology," "Ecological Reconstruction," "Ecological Function"

CiC uses ecological language at four distinct levels of the pipeline, and they need four different verdicts, because they are not the same claim.

### 3a. What the real frameworks actually say

**Cultural ecology (Julian Steward).** [*Theory of Culture Change: The Methodology of Multilinear Evolution* (1955)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_ecology) defines cultural ecology as the study of "the ways in which culture change is induced by adaptation to the environment," and names the **culture core** — the sectors of society (including religion) that interact directly with the techno-economic base, as against a periphery of features arising from diffusion or independent creation. Steward's framework is materialist and adaptationist by construction: social systems "arise out of patterns of resource exploitation which, in turn, are determined by the technological adaptation of a people to their natural environment."

**Ecology of religion (Åke Hultkrantz).** The subfield exists and has a founding article: ["An ecological approach to religion," *Ethnos* 31 (1966): 131–150](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00141844.1966.9980980), followed by "Ecology of Religion: Its Scope and Methodology" (1979). Hultkrantz follows Steward, treats adaptational processes as creative, and — importantly — kept the model *partial*: the ecological factor is "only one possible cause of change and adaptation, beside historical influences and cultural diffusion."

**Lived religion (Orsi, Hall, Ammerman, McGuire).** [*Lived Religion in America: Toward a History of Practice*, ed. David D. Hall (Princeton, 1997)](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691016733/lived-religion-in-america), opening with essays by Robert Orsi and Danièle Hervieu-Léger, is the field-defining volume; Orsi's *The Madonna of 115th Street* is its exemplar study. The approach studies "how people carry out their religious beliefs on a daily basis" and encourages "exploration of a wide range of popular beliefs and practices — all the creative things that different people do with their religious traditions."

**Cultural systems and thick description (Geertz).** ["Religion as a Cultural System" (1966), in *The Interpretation of Cultures* (1973)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Interpretation_of_Cultures) treats religion as a system of symbols, and culture as "webs of significance" people spin themselves. **Thick description** is the method: not that the priest sprinkles water, but what the water means, why it is sacred, and how participants interpret the act inside their own frame.

**The nearest genre name for what Doc_05 actually is.** In Christian-antiquity scholarship specifically, the established name for reconstructing a community's texture from its documents is **social-scientific criticism** and, as a genre label, **the social world of** — after [Wayne Meeks, *The First Urban Christians: The Social World of the Apostle Paul* (Yale, 1983)](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300098617/the-first-urban-christians/), the work that established the method in the field and remains standard reading.

### 3b. Level by level

**"Source Ecology" (Doc_02). Verdict: SHOULD ADOPT a different word.** Doc_02 does source identification, typology, author-gravity assessment, source asymmetry, and missing-voices work. That is the state and shape of the evidence base — in historiography, the *Quellenlage*; in plain English, the **source base** or **evidence base**, and its analytical half is **source criticism** (doc 12 already mapped it onto Howell & Prevenier's external/internal division and found it aligns at CiC-comparable rigor). Nothing about it is ecological. ### 4b. What maps, what doesn't

**Nothing pairs them.** I looked specifically for a framework that names *stable centers of meaning* and *historical pressures on them* as a coupled pair with its own taxonomy of pressure types. Bourdieu, Weber, Geertz, Steward, Archer, the Annales school, and historical institutionalism each name one half or a different cut entirely. **The gravity/force pairing, the six-cell force taxonomy, and the three-layer per-force documentation are CiC's own, and should be presented as CiC's own contribution rather than dressed in borrowed vocabulary.** A reviewer will respect a clearly-stated original framework with disclosed limits far more than a forced mapping onto Bourdieu that doesn't hold under a follow-up question. This is the honest answer the brief asked for, and it is the right one.

That said, four established frameworks name specific pieces well enough to be worth citing as neighbors — and one of them is the fix for a gap the brief already admits.

**(i) Bourdieu's "field of forces" — a terminological neighbor, not a framework match.** Bourdieu's *champ* is explicitly a physics metaphor: a field is "a field of forces, whose necessity is imposed on agents who are engaged in it, and a field of struggles within which agents confront each other, with differentiated means and ends according to their position in the structure of the field of forces, thus contributing to conserving or transforming its structure." The last clause is notable — Bourdieu's forces are constitutively two-way, and CiC's are not. But the resemblance stops at the word. Bourdieu's apparatus is *habitus / field / capital*: forces are relations among positioned agents competing for capital. CiC's forces are historical events and pressures with dates. Mapping CiC's Cell 1A onto Bourdieu would not survive a Bourdieu specialist's second question. **Cite as a precedent for the word "force" in a serious social-theoretical vocabulary; do not claim the framework.** **Confidence: Medium** (the quotation is from a secondary source, not from Bourdieu; see Do not cite).

**(ii) Ortner's key symbols — the closest thing to a real match for "gravity," and unexpectedly close on method.** [Sherry Ortner, "On Key Symbols," *American Anthropologist* 75(5), 1973](https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1525/aa.1973.75.5.02a00100) distinguishes **summarizing symbols**, which speak "primarily to attitudes, to a crystallization of commitment," from **elaborating symbols**, which sort experience and imply action, and which subdivide into **root metaphors** (symbols "good to think," letting one conceptualize interrelationships among phenomena by analogy to interrelationships among parts of the metaphor) and **key scenarios** ("dramatic, phased action sequences serving scenario functions for action").

The near-parallel is on the *identification* side. Ortner gives five indicators of cultural interest: natives say X is important; natives are positively or negatively aroused about X rather than indifferent; X comes up in many different contexts; there is greater cultural elaboration surrounding X; X is subject to elaborate cultural restrictions or taboos — and most key symbols are signaled by more than one. Set that against CiC's six gravity tests: **Repetition** is indicator 3 nearly verbatim; **Dependency** and **Explanatory** are indicator 4; the arousal indicator has no CiC counterpart and is arguably a real gap, since a world's *affective* investment in a candidate is evidence CiC currently reaches only through Doc_07's Emotional Ecology lens rather than through gravity testing itself. CiC built a six-test discipline that independently converged on an anthropological five-indicator discipline for the same object.

Two honest limits. Ortner's object is a *symbol*; CiC's gravity is broader — a conviction, practice, or organizing logic that need not be symbolic. And Ortner's summarizing/elaborating cut does not map onto CiC's Primary/Supporting/Tensional cut, which is about evidentiary weight and internal tension rather than symbolic function. **Cite Ortner as the closest published precedent and as external validation that the six tests are doing recognizable disciplinary work; do not rename gravities to key symbols.** **Confidence: Medium** on the summarizing/elaborating distinction, **Low-Medium** on the exact wording of the five indicators (see Do not cite).

**(iii) Archer's morphogenetic cycle — the named model for the gap brief §5d admits.** Brief §5d states the problem exactly: "what this redesign actually wants — a force reshaped by *the world's own response to it* — is a relationship the current vocabulary can't express at all." Doc 07 confirms it structurally (every layer runs force→world; Syriac's Connection 8 had to be smuggled in as force-to-force with an apology attached; HAL 3A-2 was demoted to a subordinate clause by a classification rule).

Margaret Archer's **morphogenetic approach** is the established, named model for precisely that relationship. Its sequence: **T1 structural/cultural conditioning** (temporally prior, relatively autonomous, possessing causal powers) → **T2–T3 social interaction** (agents bearing interests and identities striving for outcomes) → **T4 structural/cultural elaboration** (a new formation of structural and cultural constraints) — with the whole cycle then repeating, and *stasis* rather than elaboration as an equally valid T4 outcome. The apparatus is called **analytical dualism**: structure and agency are held analytically separate precisely so their interaction over time can be traced rather than collapsed.

Map it onto CiC directly:

| Archer | CiC's Doc_08 | Status |
|---|---|---|
| T1 structural/cultural conditioning | Layer 1 Historical Event; Cell 1A/1B initiating forces | present |
| T2–T3 social interaction | Layer 2 World's Own Experience | present, but as *reception* rather than as agency with outcomes |
| T4 structural/cultural elaboration or stasis | **absent** | this is the gap |

Layer 3 is "Formation Impact — how this force shaped the formation ecology," which is T1's effect on the world. Archer's T4 is the *world's* effect on the structural conditions — a new state of the constraints, which becomes the next cycle's T1. Adding a fourth layer along those lines gives Syriac's Connection 8 a legitimate home, restores HAL 3A-2's demoted observation to first-class status, and makes Desert's genuinely **conjunctural** synthesis at 3B-i ("an internal structural vulnerability... combined with an external trigger... that *specifically exploited* that vulnerability. Neither force alone explains the outcome; their interaction does") the template's normal case rather than its best accident.

One caution on scope: Archer's framework is a general social ontology with a large critical-realist apparatus behind it. CiC should borrow the *morphogenetic sequence* as a documentation shape and cite Archer for it, not adopt critical realism as CiC's philosophy of history. **Confidence: Medium-High** on the T1–T4 sequence and analytical dualism (multiple independent secondary sources including the Centre for Social Ontology's own summary of her research; I did not read *Realist Social Theory*).

**(iv) Two more neighbors worth naming, neither of which should become CiC's vocabulary.** Braudel's *longue durée* / **conjoncture** / *événement* triad from the Annales school is the historiographical vocabulary Desert's Doc_08 already reached for by instinct when it called its 3B-i synthesis "conjunctural" — worth citing at that one point, because it shows the instinct was disciplinary rather than idiosyncratic. And historical institutionalism supplies **critical juncture** and **path dependence**: [Capoccia's chapter in *Advances in Comparative-Historical Analysis*](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~ssfc0073/Writings%20pdf/Critical%20Junctures%20Ox%20HB%20final.pdf) casts critical junctures as "moments in which uncertainty allows political agency and choice to play a decisive role in setting an institution on a path of development that then persists over time," and path dependence explains why "institutions put in place at certain points in time become entrenched." ### 5b. The core finding: CiC's one word covers five phenomena that have five different established names

**"Drift" as a term is genuinely contested even within AI research, and CiC should know which fight it is walking into.** The best-known paper on the phenomenon changed its own title: [arXiv 2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10962) is published at COLM 2024 as **"Measuring and Controlling Instruction (In)Stability in Language Model Dialogs"** (Li, Liu, Bashkansky, Bau, Viégas, Pfister, Wattenberg), and its abstract speaks of "instruction drift within eight rounds of conversations," attributing it to "attention decay over long exchanges" and proposing **split-softmax** as a training-free mitigation — while [the authors' own GitHub repository](https://github.com/likenneth/persona_drift) and [the Harvard VCG project page](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/measuring-and-controlling-persona-drift-in-language-model-dialogs) both still carry the earlier title, "Measuring and Controlling Persona Drift in Language Model Dialogs." So **"persona drift" is the community's term and "instruction drift" is the published term for the same result** — a useful thing to get right in a citation, and a signal that CiC should cite the paper by its published title while using "persona drift" as the recognizable label.

Meanwhile "drift" has a third, unrelated technical meaning in the very field CiC's forces framework borders: in historical institutionalism, **drift** is one of the named modes of gradual institutional change. Using one word across all three is fine internally and confusing in a document a mixed reviewer pool reads.

### 5c. Cross-world vocabulary drift is lexical entrainment, and that is not a neutral relabelling

CiC treats cross-world vocabulary borrowing as pure defect: the multi-world dynamic prompt lists every other seated world's terms and instructs that "their words... belong to THEIR formation, not yours," and `check_cross_world_vocabulary_drift` fires on a real match.

The psycholinguistic literature says the behavior CiC is suppressing is the single most robust finding about how humans in conversation actually behave. **Lexical entrainment** — "when people in conversation refer repeatedly to the same object, they come to use the same terms" — and **conceptual pacts** — "a temporary agreement on how to conceptualize that object," appealed to in later references "even when they could use simpler references" — come from [Brennan & Clark, "Conceptual Pacts and Lexical Choice in Conversation," *JEP:LMC* (1996)](http://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Brennan.Clark.96.pdf). **Communication Accommodation Theory** generalizes it: interlocutors "adjust lexical, syntactic and paralinguistic behavior to manage affiliation or distinction," and the theory's central pair is **convergence and divergence** — both are accommodation moves, and divergence is how speakers signal identity and distance. Entrainment is documented at lexical, syntactic, semantic, and acoustic levels, in human-human and human-machine dialogue alike, and dialogue-systems research generally treats achieving it as *desirable* ([LEEETs-Dial](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09390)).

Three consequences for CiC, and they are not merely terminological:

1. **CiC's check has the right instinct and an over-broad target.** Entrainment is the default; a table of six historical worlds converging on shared vocabulary over six rounds is the *expected* outcome of putting them in one conversation, not an anomaly. What CiC actually wants to prevent is a narrower thing: entrainment that changes what a world *means*, not entrainment on a term it can legitimately use. The code already knows this — the whole reason for the Syriac "Mar" pre-filter design is that "only checking the usage against the term's own defined ecological meaning can" distinguish real borrowing from coincidence. The framing should catch up to the code: the check is not "did a foreign word appear" but "did a conceptual pact form that overwrote a world's own sense."
2. **Divergence is the positive form of the same mechanism, and CiC has no name for it.** In CAT terms, what CiC wants each Representative to do is *linguistic divergence* — actively maintaining distinctive vocabulary to mark identity. That is a documented, named, well-theorized communicative act, not merely the absence of drift. It reframes `REACTIVE_TURN_GUIDANCE`'s work from prohibition to positive instruction, which doc 09's finding on negative constraints (no framework surveyed has a working "never say" field that works as direct model input) says is the more reliable shape.
3. **`check_convergence`'s "manufactured resolution" half is a different phenomenon and should be separated.** Vocabulary blending is lexical entrainment. A tidy synthesis neither tradition would have independently held is closer to sycophancy at the group level, or to what doc 10 named the deepest persona failure — stating a character while refusing to enact it. Bundling them into one check means one LLM call has to detect two unrelated things, and the single-signal-per-turn architecture means only one can survive anyway.

**Verdict: SHOULD ADOPT — lexical entrainment / conceptual pacts / accommodation (convergence and divergence), with the design consequence that entrainment is a normal human behavior CiC is deliberately suppressing for a stated reason, not a malfunction. Confidence: High** on the concepts and their standing; **Medium** on the Brennan & Clark quotations (secondary restatements plus the PDF's presence, not a close read).

### 5d. Mode-dominance and next-speaker selection

CiC's `select_next_speaker` implements, in its own words, "not rotation, not equal time, but which voice is most directly positioned to meet this specific moment," with a hard no-immediate-self-repeat rule, cross-round speaking memory, and permission to return `NONE`.

The founding paper of conversation analysis names this mechanism. [Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson, "A Simplest Systematics for the Organization of Turn-Taking for Conversation," *Language* (1974)](https://muse.jhu.edu/pub/24/article/954232/summary) — the most-cited article in *Language* — specifies that if the current speaker uses a "**current speaker selects next**" technique, "the selected participant has both the right and obligation to become the next speaker"; if not, others may self-select; if no one does, the current speaker may continue. **Next-speaker selection** and **floor apportionment** are the established terms, and the multi-party dialogue-systems literature uses "next speaker prediction" and "addressee and response selection" for the computational version ([Who Speaks Next?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.04937)).

That is a genuine alignment worth citing, and it also supplies something CiC lacks: CA's three-rule ordering is a *specification* of the Facilitator's own selection logic, against which CiC's implementation can be checked. Note in particular that CiC's streaming endpoint has "current speaker selects next" nowhere — the Facilitator always allocates. That is a defensible design (the Facilitator holds the floor by governance), but it is a choice worth naming as one, especially given brief §8's objective that "forces/gravities actually inform how worlds engage each other."

On dominance itself: no established framework gives a numeric word-share threshold, and CiC's 70% is its own calibration with its own stated reason. The CA framing does supply the right *conceptual* correction, though — dominance in CA terms is about **floor-holding and turn allocation**, not word volume. A world that takes 40% of the words but is selected in every round may be dominating a table more than one that takes 70% in a single long turn. CiC's cross-round `total_turns` tracking already reaches for this; the metric could follow.

**Verdict: ALREADY ALIGNS on next-speaker selection (cite Sacks/Schegloff/Jefferson 1974 and the multi-party selection literature). PARTIAL MATCH on mode-dominance — the concept has a real name (floor apportionment) but the word-share operationalization is CiC's own and nothing in the literature validates the number. Confidence: High** on the CA rules; **Medium** on the multi-party dialogue-systems term inventory.

## 7. The layered prompt-assembly architecture

### 7a. What CiC actually built

Two overlapping tier systems, which is itself worth noticing.

**The build-artifact tiers** (`World_Priority_Layer_Template.md`, verbatim): "Tier 2 of the three-tier retrieval architecture — Core (Tier 1, Output 1, always present per turn), Priority Layer (Tier 2, this document, loaded once at session start and held in context throughout), Context Layer (Tier 3, Output 4, retrieved on demand)." The Core is 3,000–5,000 tokens, "never retrieved, never conditional." Context Layer chunks carry `Retrieve-When` / `Do-Not-Retrieve-When` front-matter plus a `Capsule-Core-Complement` field stating what the chunk adds that the Core doesn't carry.

**The runtime assembly segments** (`build_representative_prompt`, `representative_prompts.py` 79–201): a byte-identical `static_prompt` (permanent prompt + capsule + `_HOW_YOU_ENGAGE` + gloss guidance), a conditionally-present `reactive_guidance_block`, and a per-turn `dynamic_prompt` (RAG context, reroot correction) — split so the first two can each be an Anthropic cache breakpoint.

Doc 09 already established that the retrieval-entry schema's real name is **World Info** and that its keys/content/constant/priority/token_budget/insertion_order machinery TRANSFERS CLEANLY. This section goes past that to the *tiering* question doc 09 did not cover.

### 7b. What the 2026 vocabulary actually is

**The umbrella term has changed, and CiC's documents are on the wrong side of it.** [Anthropic's "Effective context engineering for AI agents"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) defines **context engineering** as "the set of strategies for curating and maintaining the optimal set of tokens (information) during LLM inference, including all the other information that may land there outside of the prompts," and positions it as the successor to prompt engineering: the shift from "discrete task of writing a prompt" to iterative curation across "system instructions, tools, Model Context Protocol (MCP), external data, message history, etc." CiC's own brief and templates still describe this work as prompt assembly. **SHOULD ADOPT: context engineering is the current name for the entire deliverable in brief §10 item 4.**

**The governing principle has a name and a reason.** Same source: "context, therefore, must be treated as a finite resource with diminishing marginal returns," because LLMs have an **attention budget** that depletes with each token, from the transformer's n² attention. This is the precise, citable justification for CiC's tiering — and it is a *quality* argument, not a cost argument. That matters for brief §2's insistence that the cost goal and the quality goal often point the same way: the industry's own framing says trimming retrieved volume is a quality intervention that happens to be cheaper, which is exactly what brief §5c found empirically (Alexandria pushing ~4,300 words of retrieved context against a ~900-word output cap, with the injected scholarly-register material actively degrading voice).

**The empirical backing for the same point is now named too.** [Chroma's "Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance"](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot) tested 18 frontier models and found performance degrades non-uniformly as input length grows — "at every increment, not just near the limit," with needle-question similarity, distractors, and haystack structure all mattering. **Context rot** is the term. For CiC, this is the direct counter to the intuition that a bigger Priority Layer is a safer Priority Layer, and it independently vindicates the template's own instruction to "curate hard — this document stays in context for the whole session, so padding it dilutes its purpose."

**CiC's three-tier split has three real named analogues, and one is close enough to borrow wholesale.**

| CiC tier | Anthropic Agent Skills | MemGPT / Letta | Anthropic context engineering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capsule Core — always present per turn | **Level 1 metadata** (loaded for every skill, always) | **core memory** — "stays in the context window at all times, like working RAM" | pre-loaded context |
| Priority Layer — loaded once at session start, held all session | **Level 2 SKILL.md body** (loaded when triggered) | (no clean equivalent) | pre-loaded context |
| Context Layer — retrieved on demand via Retrieve-When | **Level 3 bundled files** (loaded only when referenced) | **archival memory** — vector-indexed long-term storage, queried by tool call | **"just in time"** retrieval — "maintain lightweight identifiers... and dynamically load data into context at runtime using tools" |

The name for the pattern as a whole is **progressive disclosure**, per [Anthropic's Agent Skills documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/overview): a three-level loading model where Level 1 metadata stays loaded for every installed skill, Level 2 loads only when a task triggers it, and Level 3 loads only when referenced. That is a very close structural match to CiC's Core/Priority/Context, and the two Anthropic documents together give CiC both the pattern name (*progressive disclosure*) and the retrieval-strategy name (*just-in-time context retrieval*, explicitly contrasted with pre-inference retrieval).

[MemGPT / Letta](https://www.leoniemonigatti.com/blog/memgpt.html)'s **core / recall / archival** memory hierarchy adds the useful framing that this is **virtual context management** — an explicit borrowing of the operating-system memory-hierarchy and paging metaphor. Two things from it are worth having: the OS analogy is the fastest way to make CiC's three tiers legible to an engineering reviewer in one sentence, and *recall memory* (searchable conversation history held outside the context window) names a tier CiC does not have and, per doc 10, currently does not need — full history sits in the cached context within a session. Being able to say "we deliberately do not have a recall tier, and here is why" is better than the tier simply being absent.

**The cost side has a named ordering principle that CiC's split already follows.** Current LLM-ops practice orders prompt content **by change frequency** so the stable prefix stays cached: static system prompt, then session-scoped context, then per-turn dynamic content ([Prompt Caching in 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/prompt-caching-2026-cut-llm-costs-engineering-guide); [Context Engineering for Production LLM Agents](https://appscale.blog/en/blog/context-engineering-production-llm-agents-token-budget-compaction-2026)). CiC's `static_prompt` / `reactive_guidance_block` / `dynamic_prompt` split is that ordering exactly, and the Core/Priority/Context tiering happens to align with it — Core never changes, Priority changes once per session, Context changes per turn. That is a genuine architectural strength that currently reads in CiC's own documents as an implementation detail about cache breakpoints. It should be stated as the principle it is, because brief §2's warning ("a proposal that shrinks per-turn context but destabilizes the cached prefix can *raise* real cost while looking cheaper on a token count") is the same principle stated as a hazard.

**One thing doc 09 covered that this extends.** Doc 09 found Character.AI's `truncation_priority` and the character-card permanent-versus-pruned split, and verdicted both TRANSFERS CLEANLY. What the 2026 vocabulary adds is that these are two different fields, not one: **eviction priority** (what gets dropped when the budget binds) and **cache stability** (what must not change so the prefix stays cached) can pull in opposite directions. A high-value entry that changes every turn is a caching liability even though it is an eviction-priority asset. CiC's schema needs both, separately.

**Verdict: PARTIAL MATCH, resolving toward SHOULD ADOPT on names.** CiC's three-tier architecture is correct, is convergent with three independent industry patterns, and needs no redesign. What should change is vocabulary: call the discipline **context engineering**; call the tiering **progressive disclosure**; call Tier 3 **just-in-time context retrieval**; cite **attention budget** and **context rot** as the quality justification for retrieval-volume tuning; and split eviction priority from cache stability as two fields rather than one. **Confidence: High** for the Anthropic context-engineering definitions (fetched); **Medium-High** for the Agent Skills three-level wording; **Medium** for MemGPT's tier names (secondary sources).
