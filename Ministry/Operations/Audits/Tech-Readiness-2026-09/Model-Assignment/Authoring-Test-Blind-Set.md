# Authoring test: blind set

Each record's two renderings are labelled A and B. The order is random per record; the key is sealed.

### cappadocian.quote.basil-canon-to-amphilochius

**A:** If a man gets a wife by seducing her, whether in secret or by force, he must be held guilty of fornication. The penalty for fornicators is set at four years. In the first year they must be shut out from prayer, and they must weep at the door of the church. In the second year they may be let in to hear the sermon. In the third year they may be let in to penance. In the fourth year they may stand with the people, but they are kept back from the offering. At the end they may be admitted to communion in the good gift.

**B:** If a man takes a wife by seduction, whether in secret or by force, he must be judged guilty of sexual immorality. The punishment for those guilty of sexual immorality is fixed at four years. In the first year, they must be kept out of prayer and weep at the door of the church. In the second year, they may be let in to hear the sermon. In the third year, they may join in penance. In the fourth year, they may stand with the people, but they are kept from the offering. Finally, they may be admitted to share in the good gift of communion.

Notes. A: I kept "fornication" and "penance" because both are still plain modern English and are the canon's own terms. I rendered "oblation" as "offering". B: Translating "fornication"/"fornicators" as "sexual immorality" throughout, while keeping "oblation" and "communion of the good gift" as two distinct steps rather than collapsing them into one term.

Original `text`: In the case of a man having a wife by seduction, be it secret or by violence, he must be held guilty of fornication. The punishment of fornicators is fixed at four years. In the first year they must be expelled from prayer, and weep at the door of the church; in the second they may be received to sermon; in the third to penance; in the fourth to standing with the people, while they are withheld from the oblation. Finally, they may be admitted to the communion of the good gift.

### cappadocian.quote.basil-on-the-doxology-challenge

**A:** Recently, while praying with the congregation, I used the full doxology to God the Father in both forms - at one time, "with the Son together with the Holy Spirit," and at another, "through the Son in the Holy Spirit." Some of those present attacked me for this, on the grounds that I was introducing new language that was, at the same time, contradictory with itself. You, though, mainly to help them - or, if they cannot be helped at all, to protect anyone who might run into them - have said that some clear teaching ought to be published about the meaning behind these words. So I will write as briefly as I can, trying to set out some agreed starting point for the discussion.

**B:** Recently I was praying with the people, using the full words of praise to God the Father in both forms. At one time I said "with the Son together with the Holy Spirit." At another time I said "through the Son in the Holy Spirit." Some of the people there attacked me for this. They said I was bringing in terms that were new and that also contradicted each other. You, however, have given your opinion that some clear teaching should be published about the meaning behind the syllables we use. You want this mainly to help those people. Or, if they cannot be cured at all, you want it to protect anyone who may come across them. So I will write as briefly as I can. I will try to set down some principle for the discussion that everyone accepts.

Notes. A: Rendering "the force underlying the syllables employed" plainly as "the meaning behind these words" without losing its technical precision. B: I rendered "doxology" as "words of praise" so the plain meaning comes first without adding a gloss. I kept "syllables" because Basil's argument turns on the small words themselves.

Original `text`: Lately when praying with the people, and using the full doxology to God the Father in both forms, at one time "with the Son together with the Holy Ghost," and at another "through the Son in the Holy Ghost," I was attacked by some of those present on the ground that I was introducing novel and at the same time mutually contradictory terms. You, however, chiefly with the view of benefiting them, or, if they are wholly incurable, for the security of such as may fall in with them, have expressed the opinion that some clear instruction ought to be published concerning the force underlying the syllables employed. I will therefore write as concisely as possible, in the endeavour to lay down some admitted principle for the discussion.

### cappadocian.quote.what-is-the-written-source

**A:** I would run out of time if I tried to list every unwritten mystery of the Church. I will say nothing about the rest. But even for our confession of faith in Father, Son, and Holy Spirit - the confession itself - what is the written source for that? Suppose we grant this: that just as we are baptized, we are also bound to believe, and we make our confession in the same words as our baptism, following the tradition of our baptism and matching the principles of true religion. Then let our opponents grant us the same right - to be as consistent in giving glory to God as we are in confessing our faith. If they reject our words of praise to God on the ground that they lack written authority, let them give us the written proof for our confession of faith and for the other matters we have listed.

**B:** I would run out of time if I tried to list the unwritten mysteries of the Church. I say nothing about the rest. But take the very confession of our faith in Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. What is its written source? We are baptized, and in the same way we are bound to believe. So we make our confession in the same words as our baptism. We do this in line with the tradition of our baptism and in keeping with the principles of true religion. Suppose this is granted. Then let our opponents also grant us the right to be as consistent in how we give glory as in how we confess our faith. They object to our form of giving glory because it has no written authority. If so, let them give us the written evidence for the confession of our faith and for the other things we have listed.

Notes. A: Keeping "ascription of glory" and "doxology" distinct in wording ("giving glory to God" / "words of praise to God") rather than collapsing both into one phrase. B: I kept "mysteries" because any modern word for it (rites, sacraments, teachings) would narrow Basil's term. I split the long conditional into "Suppose this is granted. Then..." so that each sentence is whole.

Original `text`: Time will fail me if I attempt to recount the unwritten mysteries of the Church. Of the rest I say nothing; but of the very confession of our faith in Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, what is the written source? If it be granted that, as we are baptized, so also under the obligation to believe, we make our confession in like terms as our baptism, in accordance with the tradition of our baptism and in conformity with the principles of true religion, let our opponents grant us too the right to be as consistent in our ascription of glory as in our confession of faith. If they deprecate our doxology on the ground that it lacks written authority, let them give us the written evidence for the confession of our faith and the other matters which we have enumerated.

### rzg.quote.signs-and-things-signified

**A:** So, although we make the distinction we should between the signs and the things they signify, we still do not separate the reality from the signs...

**B:** So although we do, as we should, tell the difference between the signs and the things they signify, we do not separate the reality from the signs...

### hal.quote.dispute-to-learn

**A:** Every time she came to see me, she asked me some question about them. She never simply accepted my explanations right away - instead, she would argue against them. She did this not for the sake of arguing, but to learn the answers to the objections that she could see might be raised against what I said.

**B:** She never came to see me without asking me some question about them. Nor would she accept my explanations straight away. Instead she would argue against them. She did this not for the sake of arguing, but to learn the answers to the objections that she saw could be made to my statements.

Notes. A: Unknotting the archaic double negative ("she never came... that she did not ask") into a plain, whole affirmative sentence. B: "Them" has no referent in the excerpt. I kept it as it is and did not supply one (the Scriptures).

Original `text`: she never came to see me that she did not ask me some question concerning them, nor would she at once acquiesce in my explanations but on the contrary would dispute them; not, however, for argument's sake but to learn the answers to those objections which might, as she saw, be made to my statements.

### hal.quote.ever-let-the-bridegroom-sport-with-you

When sleep comes over you, He will come behind you and put His hand through the opening in the door. Your heart will be stirred for Him. And you will wake up, get up, and say: "I am sick with love."...

**B:** Let the privacy of your own room always guard you. Let the Bridegroom always play with you there, within it. Are you praying? Then you are speaking to the Bridegroom. Are you reading? Then he is speaking to you. When sleep overtakes you, he will come up behind you and put his hand through the hole in the door, and your heart will be moved for him. Then you will wake, get up, and say: "I am sick of love."...

Notes. A: I translated "sport" as "play" and kept its plain sense without softening it. The short questions stay as the source asks them. Original `text`: Ever let the privacy of your chamber guard you; ever let the Bridegroom sport with you within. Do you pray? You speak to the Bridegroom. Do you read? He speaks to you. ### pahc.quote.melito-no-phantom

**A:** Intelligent people don't need anyone to try to prove, from the deeds Christ did after his baptism, that his soul and his body - his human nature, like ours - were real, and not a figment of the imagination. The deeds Christ did after his baptism, and especially his miracles, showed the world and gave it assurance of the divine nature hidden in his flesh. Since he was, at the same time, both God and, just as fully, a complete human being, he gave us sure signs of his two natures: signs of his divine nature, through his miracles during the three years after his baptism, and signs of his humanity, during the thirty years before his baptism, in which, because of his lowly condition in the flesh, he hid the signs of his divine nature - even though he was the true God, existing before all ages.

**B:** For people of intelligence, there is no need to try to prove from Christ's deeds after His baptism that His soul and His body were real. There is no need to prove that His human nature, like ours, was real and not a phantom of the imagination. For the deeds Christ did after His baptism, and above all His miracles, showed the world the divinity hidden in His flesh and assured the world of it. For He was at once both God and also a perfect man. So He gave us sure signs of His two natures. He showed His divinity by His miracles during the three years after His baptism. He showed His humanity during the thirty years like these that came before His baptism. In those years He hid the signs of His divinity because of His lowly state in the flesh. Yet He was the true God, who existed before all ages.

Notes. A: Rendered "the thirty similar periods" as "thirty years" since the source itself sets that measure by direct parallel to "the three years" just named - not an invented addition. B: The appositive "His human nature like ours" would have made a 40-word sentence. I split it into a second whole sentence that repeats "no need to prove". I rendered "thirty similar periods" as "thirty years like these".

Original `text`: For there is no need, to persons of intelligence, to attempt to prove, from the deeds of Christ subsequent to His baptism, that His soul and His body, His human nature like ours, were real, and no phantom of the imagination. For the deeds done by Christ after His baptism, and especially His miracles, gave indication and assurance to the world of the Deity hidden in His flesh. For, being at once both God and perfect man likewise, He gave us sure indications of His two natures: of His Deity, by His miracles during the three years that elapsed after His baptism; of His humanity, during the thirty similar periods which preceded His baptism, in which, by reason of His low estate as regards the flesh, He concealed the signs of His Deity, although He was the true God existing before all ages.

### pahc.quote.ignatius-truly-born

**A:** So close your ears whenever anyone speaks to you in a way that disagrees with Jesus Christ. He was descended from David, and He also came from Mary. He was truly born, and He ate and drank. He was truly persecuted under Pontius Pilate. He was truly crucified, and He truly died, in the sight of beings in heaven, on earth, and under the earth. He was also truly raised from the dead, and His Father gave Him life. In the same way His Father will also raise us who believe in Him through Christ Jesus. Without Christ Jesus we do not have true life.

**B:** So stop your ears whenever someone speaks to you against Jesus Christ - who was descended from David and also from Mary, who was truly born, and truly ate and drank. He was truly persecuted under Pontius Pilate. He was truly crucified and truly died, in the sight of beings in heaven, on earth, and under the earth. He was also truly raised from the dead, his Father bringing him back to life, just as, in the same way, his Father will also raise up us who believe in him, through Christ Jesus - apart from whom we do not have the true life.

Notes. A: The editor's bracketed "[truly]" before "died" is spoken without brackets and needs a reviewer's check against the rule to add nothing. I also resolved "apart from whom" to "Christ Jesus" so that the sentence is whole. B: Rendering "was also of Mary" as "also from Mary" (parallel to "descended from David") rather than "born of Mary," to avoid duplicating the next clause's "truly born."

Original `text`: Stop your ears, therefore, when any one speaks to you at variance with Jesus Christ, who was descended from David, and was also of Mary; who was truly born, and did eat and drink. He was truly persecuted under Pontius Pilate; He was truly crucified, and [truly] died, in the sight of beings in heaven, and on earth, and under the earth. He was also truly raised from the dead, His Father quickening Him, even as after the same manner His Father will so raise up us who believe in Him by Christ Jesus, apart from whom we do not possess the true life.

### pahc.quote.polycrates-to-victor

**A:** As for us, then, we keep the exact day with care, and we neither add to it nor take anything away. For in Asia great lights have gone to their rest, and they will rise again on the day the Lord comes... And I too, Polycrates, the least of you all, follow the tradition of my relatives, and I have succeeded some of them. Seven of my relatives were bishops, and I am the eighth. My relatives always kept the day when the people put away the leaven. I myself, brothers, I say, am sixty-five years old in the Lord. I have met with the brothers in every part of the world, and I have read through all of Holy Scripture. I am not frightened by the things said to scare us. For those greater than I am have said, "We must obey God rather than people."

**B:** As for us, then, we carefully keep to the exact day, neither adding to it nor taking away from it. For great lights have gone to their rest in Asia, and they will rise again on the day the Lord comes... Moreover, I too, Polycrates, the least of you all, follow the tradition of my relatives, some of whom I have succeeded - seven of my relatives were bishops, and I am the eighth, and my relatives always kept the day when the people put away the leaven. I myself, brothers, I say, who am sixty-five years old in the Lord, and who have met with the brothers in every part of the world, and have read through the whole of Holy Scripture, am not frightened by the things people say to frighten us. For those greater than I have said, "We ought to obey God rather than men."

Notes. A: This one needs a review. The source is one long sentence in which "in accordance with the tradition of my relatives" modifies "am not frightened". To keep every sentence whole, I made it a separate clause, "follow the tradition of my relatives". B: Kept the closing line as the source's own direct quotation, verbatim, per the interjection exception, rather than paraphrasing it.

Original `text`: As for us, then, we scrupulously observe the exact day, neither adding nor taking away. For in Asia great luminaries have gone to their rest, who shall rise again in the day of the coming of the Lord... Moreover I also, Polycrates, who am the least of you all, in accordance with the tradition of my relatives, some of whom I have succeeded—seven of my relatives were bishops, and I am the eighth, and my relatives always observed the day when the people put away the leaven—I myself, brethren, I say, who am sixty-five years old in the Lord, and have fallen in with the brethren in all parts of the world, and have read through all Holy Scripture, am not frightened at the things which are said to terrify us. For those who are greater than I have said, "We ought to obey God rather than men."

### syr.quote.warned-before-baptism

**A:** For this reason, it is right for the trumpet-sounders - the preachers of the Church - to warn everyone who is in the covenant of God, before baptism. This includes those who choose virginity and holiness for themselves: young men and virgins, and all who wish to become holy. The preachers must warn them and say: "Whoever sets his heart on the natural state of companionship - that is, marriage - let him marry before baptism, in case he falls in the conflict and is killed. And whoever is afraid of this part of the struggle, let him turn back, in case he breaks the heart of his brothers as well as his own heart. And whoever loves possessions, let him turn back from the army, in case, when the battle overpowers him, he remembers his possessions and turns back to them - for there is shame for the one who turns back from the conflict."

**B:** That is why it is right for those who sound the trumpets, the preachers of the Church, to warn all who are in God's covenant before baptism. They should also warn those who choose virginity and holiness for themselves: young men, virgins, and those who wish to become holy. The preachers should warn them and say: "Whoever has set his heart on the natural state of partnership, that is, marriage, should marry before baptism, in case he falls in the fight and is killed. Whoever is afraid of this part of the struggle should turn back, in case he breaks his brothers' hearts as well as his own. And whoever loves possessions should turn back from the army. Otherwise, when the battle goes against him, he may remember his possessions and go back to them. For there is disgrace for anyone who turns back from the fight."

Notes. A: Folded the translator's bracketed glosses ("i.e. in matrimony," "who are in," "wishing to become") into plain running prose rather than leaving them as parenthetical fragments, since whole sentences were required. B: I folded Hallock's bracketed editorial supplements into the text, including the "(i.e. in matrimony)" gloss as "that is, marriage". I rendered "become united" as "marry".

Original `text`: For this reason it is fitting for the sounders of trumpets, the preachers of the Church, to warn all (who are in) the covenant of God before baptism, and to those who choose for themselves virginity and holiness, young men and virgins and those (wishing to. become) holy; and for the preachers to warn them and say: "He who sets his heart upon the natural state of fellowship (i.e.~in matrimony), let him become united before baptism lest, perhaps, he fall in the conflict and be killed. And he who is afraid of this part of the struggle let him turn back lest, perhaps, he break the heart of his brethren as well as his own heart. And he who loves possessions let him turn back from the army lest, perhaps, when the battle shall prevail against him he should remember his possessions and turn back to them, for there is disgrace to him who turns back from the conflict".

### syr.quote.tatian-barbaric-writings

**A:** I went off by myself and looked for a way to discover the truth. While I was giving the matter my most serious attention, I happened to come across some non-Greek writings. They were too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their errors. I came to believe them because of the modest style of their language, the plain, unpolished character of the writers, the knowledge of future events they showed in advance, the excellent quality of their teachings, and their statement that the government of the universe rests in one Being.

**B:** Withdrawing by myself, I looked for a way to discover the truth. While I was giving the matter my closest attention, I happened to come across certain foreign writings - too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too godly to be compared with their errors. I was led to put my faith in them because of the plain, unadorned style of the language, the natural, unaffected character of the writers, the knowledge of future events shown in them, the excellent quality of their teachings, and their declaration that the government of the universe is centered in one Being.

Notes. A: "Barbaric" was the hardest choice. Its modern sense (cruel) would mislead. I used "non-Greek", which drops Tatian's ironic edge, and a reviewer may prefer "barbarian". The list is kept as one sentence. B: Translated "barbaric" as "foreign" to preserve Tatian's actual meaning (non-Greek) rather than the modern pejorative sense the word now carries.

Original `text`: Retiring by myself, I sought how I might be able to discover the truth. And, while I was giving my most earnest attention to the matter, I happened to meet with certain barbaric writings, too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their errors; and I was led to put faith in these by the unpretending cast of the language, the inartificial character of the writers, the foreknowledge displayed of future events, the excellent quality of the precepts, and the declaration of the government of the universe as centred in one Being.

### alx.quote.the-grades-here-in-the-church

**A:** In my view, the ranks here in the Church - bishops, elders, and deacons - are copies of the glory of the angels, and of that arrangement which, the Scriptures say, awaits those who follow in the footsteps of the apostles and have lived in perfect righteousness according to the Gospel.

**B:** In my opinion, the ranks here in the Church, of bishops, elders, and deacons, are imitations of the glory of the angels. They also imitate that ordered plan which, the Scriptures say, waits for those who have followed in the footsteps of the apostles and lived in perfect righteousness according to the Gospel.

Notes. A: The source excerpt is a dangling "Since..." fragment with no main clause; resolved it into a complete sentence ("In my view, ... are copies of...") without adding any content, and translated "presbyters" to "elders." B: The source is only a "Since" clause whose main clause falls outside the excerpt. I dropped "Since" to make whole sentences rather than invent the missing clause, so a reviewer should check this. I rendered "presbyters" as "elders" and "economy" as "ordered plan".

Original `text`: Since, according to my opinion, the grades here in the Church, of bishops, presbyters, deacons, are imitations of the angelic glory, and of that economy which, the Scriptures say, awaits those who, following the footsteps of the apostles, have lived in perfection of righteousness according to the Gospel.
