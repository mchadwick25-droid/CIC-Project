## The two-layer scope, both real, both his

**Layer 1 — the Kit's own rules.** Not just "does the site follow the Kit" but "is the Kit
itself right." Concretely, this means opening for real reconsideration things the site-audit
work has so far only *applied*: the retired-words list (honest/honestly, authentic, bare
"voice," "real" as an evidence-adjective), the protected-lines list (what's actually earned
that status vs. what just hasn't been challenged yet — Mark already retired one supposedly-
protected line this month, *"It will never try to convert you,"* ruling on 2026-07-20 that
even a true denial "undermines trust" by answering a suspicion nobody voiced — that is exactly
the kind of rule-level challenge this thread should be open to making again, not a one-time
exception), the "no new X-not-Y contrast framing" rule's edges (is it truly absolute, or are
there constructions it wrongly catches), and the two still-RECOMMENDED visual directions.

**Layer 2 — the site's actual structure and messaging**, using the 2026-07-20 Structure Plan
as the working draft, not a blank page: work it section by section, the way every other piece
of copy in this project has been built — present a page, propose the beat it should carry,
react to what Mark says, move to the next. Reconcile against what's already live (item 3
above) rather than re-proposing it.

## How Mark needs this paced — this governs every message in this thread, not a preference to weigh against the rest

**His own words:** *"i get overwelmed with desisions in context of a large about of data or
pages of documentation, I want the process of redesign to go one issue at a time, with
prompted questions and small edits at a time... i want to see the decisions one at a time."*
He said this applies to all interaction, not just this thread — so it also overrides this
document's own instinct to front-load context. Concretely:

- **One issue at a time.** Never open with (or hand him mid-thread) a bulk audit, a multi-page
  table, or a menu of several decisions at once — even the Structure Plan's own audit table
  is exactly the shape to avoid re-presenting wholesale. Take one page, or one Kit rule,
  work it to a decision, then move to the next. If a document has ten flagged lines, that's
  ten small turns, not one long one.
- **Every phrasing suggestion is three options, not one draft.** Whenever you propose a
  tagline, a sentence, a rule, a name for something — give your actual recommendation, two
  genuinely different alternatives (not trivial rewordings of the same idea), and then hand
  it to him to edit. Don't present a single finished line for accept/reject.
- **Push back is welcome, and so is iterating on his edit.** If his change reopens a problem
  the original draft solved, say so and propose the fix inline — this is a real back-and-forth
  per line, not a rubber stamp in either direction.
- **Small edits at a time.** Resist the urge to fix everything you can see wrong with a page
  in one pass. Fix what's being worked right now; note anything else you notice for its own
  later turn rather than folding it in.
