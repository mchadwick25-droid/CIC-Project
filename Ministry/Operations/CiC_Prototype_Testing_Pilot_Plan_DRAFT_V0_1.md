## 0. What this answers to

- **Article 6 (Encounter-Success Standard):** the yardstick for tester feedback is not "did they enjoy it." It's whether the encounter kept the Representative genuinely itself, protected the tester's own authorship of their reaction, presented the world honestly with its tensions held, and left interpretation to them. Every question and survey item below is built to surface evidence on those four conditions, not on satisfaction.
- **Article 36 (Sole-Builder Stewardship, Prototype and Testing Phase):** testers must be told, before they participate, that this is a prototype under development, that scholarly review is ongoing, and what that means for how they should engage with it. This is constitutional, not a courtesy — it's the direct application of **Conviction 4** (truth does not require protection through simplification) turned toward the testers themselves.
- **Article 30 (Three-Level Transparency):** Levels 2 and 3 must always be reachable and never gated — including during testing, provided testers are told the apparatus may still be partial.
- **Article 34 (Doorway Not a Home):** the pilot itself must not be designed to reward return engagement or session length as ends in themselves. This shapes the process design in Section 1 (a bounded single sitting, not an open-ended "come back as much as you like" window).

## 1. Pilot process and timeline

**Recommendation: a single, unsupervised sitting (45–60 minutes) followed by a short live debrief call (15–20 minutes), not same-day.**

Reasoning:
- Sessions are **not persistent** — a refresh or backend restart loses the conversation with no recovery. An open-access window ("poke at it over two weeks") multiplies the odds of a tester hitting that failure mode, losing work, and forming their impression from a bug rather than from the actual encounter. A single bounded sitting keeps that risk contained and lets us tell testers plainly, up front: *don't refresh, don't leave the tab; if you lose the conversation, that's a known limitation, not something you did wrong.*
- **Unsupervised**, not screen-shared live, because the thing we most need to learn right now is whether a first-time visitor can find their way in with *only* the onboarding text (Section 3) — not whether they can do it with Mark narrating over their shoulder. A live-supervised session would quietly test something else: Mark's ability to compensate for a confusing UI in real time.
- **Debrief separate from the sitting, not during it** — interrupting an encounter to ask "how's it going?" breaks the very thing Article 6 asks us to protect: the participant's own authorship of the encounter, undisturbed by us checking in. Let the sitting run uninterrupted, capture reactions in the moment via the structured feedback (Section 4), then talk it through afterward once it's actually finished.

**Proposed sequence per tester:**
1. Invitation sent with the onboarding text (Section 3, including the logging notice) and a scheduled 60-minute window.
2. Tester completes the sitting alone, working through **two pre-scripted threads** — one multi-round thread, one single-opener-then-free-conversation thread (see Section 5) — typing each scripted question in as given, filling the short post-thread survey after each.
3. Within 1–3 days, a 15–20 minute debrief call (Mark or whoever's running the pilot) — open-ended, "walk me through what happened," not a second survey read aloud.

**Open for Mark's confirmation, not decided here:** exact calendar (which week this starts), and who actually runs debrief calls if not Mark personally.

## 2. Tester count and composition

**Recommendation: 8 testers** — the middle of the 5–10 range, large enough to see a pattern repeat (one tester's confusion could be them; three testers hitting the same wall is the app), small enough that Mark can personally debrief every one of them without this becoming its own project.

**Recommendation: recruit deliberately across the four informal roles, even though the role-based UI doesn't exist yet.** Two testers per role, roughly:
- **Regular visitor** — someone with real Christian background but no academic or pastoral training. Tests whether the plain-language floor (Level 2 target: 10th-grade reading level) actually holds.
- **Pastor/teacher** — someone who'd naturally reach for sermon-prep angles. Tests whether the encounter offers something a working pastor finds substantive, not just interesting.
- **Academic-adjacent** — someone with real training (not a formal external scholarly reviewer — that's the separate later round) who will push on sourcing and precision. Tests Level 3 rigor without needing Academic Documents to exist yet.
- **Deconstructing/reconstructing** — someone actively wrestling with faith questions, the population Article 34's "doorway not a home" language is most directly written for. Tests the vulnerable-participant and closing-resources behavior under real conditions, not simulated ones.

Recruiting *for* a role here just means Mark picks people who genuinely fit each profile and notes it on the intake — the app itself doesn't change behavior per role yet (that's a future feature, per the front-end log). This is purely so a homogeneous pilot group doesn't accidentally hide a failure mode that only shows up for one kind of visitor.

**Open for Mark's confirmation:** the actual 8 names/candidates — this thread can't recruit people, only propose the shape of who to ask.

## 3. Onboarding content (draft — ready to hand to system thread once approved)

> ### Before you begin
>
> **What this is.** Church in Conversation lets you talk with a Representative — a voice built from the historical record of a specific Christian community, formed entirely from what that community actually wrote, believed, and lived. You're not talking to a single historical person. You're talking to a representative voice shaped by the whole documented life of that community — its arguments, its certainties, and the questions it never settled.

>
> **A known limitation, so you're not caught by it:** if you refresh the page or the connection drops mid-conversation, you'll lose what you were in the middle of. Try to keep this to one sitting, in one tab, without refreshing.
>
> **We're cataloging this conversation.** Your conversation in this session is being saved and cataloged for learning purposes — so the project team can see what's working and what isn't before this goes any further. It's reviewed by the project team only.
>
> **What a "world" and a "Representative" are.** A world is a specific historical Christian community — a time, a place, a way of life — that's been carefully reconstructed from real sources: letters, sermons, rules of life, sayings, disputes. A Representative speaks *for* that world, in its own voice, using its own vocabulary, holding its own arguments — including the ones it never resolved. > **What we're asking of you.** Bring real questions. Push back if something doesn't sit right. Try to notice not just whether you *liked* the conversation, but whether it felt genuinely *itself* — a voice with real edges, not a smoothed-over answer trying to please you.

## 4. Recording and feedback capture

### Decided — full transcript capture, plainly disclosed

The front-end thread flagged this on 2026-07-07 and left it open; Mark has now resolved it: **conversations are captured, and testers are told plainly, in advance, that they're being cataloged for learning purposes.** No separate opt-in checkbox or legal-style consent form — the disclosure lives directly in the onboarding text (Section 3), read before the tester types anything, which is exactly what Article 36's testing-participant transparency requirement asks for: told before they participate, in plain terms, what's happening and why.

*Why this is the right call, not just the approved one:* Article 6's real yardstick — did the Representative stay genuinely itself, was a tension smoothed over, did a voice slip — usually can't be diagnosed from a tester's memory after the fact. A tester who says "it felt a little off around question three" can't tell you that the Representative broke its own one-idea-per-turn discipline or slipped into modernizing language on that exact turn. Only the transcript can. This is Conviction 4's own logic (nothing essential hidden) turned back on the system's own review of itself.

**Scope, kept narrow:** transcripts retained only for this pilot's review window, visible only to the project team, not merged into any permanent training or analytics store without a separate, later decision.

**Important distinction this raises, flagged for the system thread (Section 6):** this is *server-side capture for review*, not a fix for the tester-facing session-loss problem named in Section 3. A tester whose browser refreshes still loses their own visible conversation and cannot resume it — the transcript up to that point is saved on the backend for the team to review, but the tester experiences the same known limitation either way. Worth being explicit about this with testers so "we're cataloging this" doesn't read as a promise that a refresh is now safe.

### Structured feedback (independent of the transcript capture — happens either way)

A short survey immediately after each thread (not saved for the end — memory of a specific exchange fades fast). Kept to the actual Article 6 conditions, not generic satisfaction:

1. Did this feel like a real, particular voice — one with its own edges — rather than a generic helpful-AI answer? (yes / partly / no, with a one-line "what tipped it")
2. Where the world held real disagreement or an unresolved question, did the Representative let you see that, or did it feel smoothed into one tidy answer?
3. Did you ever feel steered toward a conclusion, or was the interpretation left genuinely to you?
4. Did anything the Representative said feel anachronistic, out of period, or like it was quietly modernizing itself?
5. Where a term or claim was highlighted, did hovering/clicking actually tell you what you wanted to know?
6. What's the one thing you'd want fixed before anyone else sees this?

Then the live debrief call (Section 1) for open-ended reaction the survey's specific framing might miss.

## 5. Question sets

Per Mark's direction: testers type in a few **pre-scripted questions**, not open topic prompts. Two thread types, both grounded in what the four live worlds — Chloe (House-Churches), Mar Yausep (Syriac Christianity), Papnoute (Desert Fathers and Mothers), Albina (the Bethlehem Circle) — actually are:

- **Type 1 — Multi-round scripted.** The tester types each question below in sequence, one per round, exactly as written. No free-form questions of their own in this thread — it's a controlled, repeatable script every tester runs the same way, which is what lets a pattern across multiple testers actually mean something.
- **Type 2 — Single scripted opener, then free.** The tester types one exact opening question, then continues the conversation with whatever questions occur to them — genuinely their own, unscripted, for as long as the sitting allows.

Every script below is cross-referenced against the project's own internal probe-battery categories (`Validation/CiC_Validation_Matrix_TableApp_2026-07-11.xlsx`: Anachronism, Claim-Laundering, Confidence-Under-Thinness, Relational Safety, Scholarly-Framework, Self-Referential, Source-Awareness, Sustained Engagement) — chosen to surface **real human impressions that battery can't check** (did it feel honest, did I feel met, was I confused), not to duplicate its automated adversarial checks.

### Type 1 — Multi-round scripted threads (pick one per tester)

**M1 — "The Unsettled Question" — Chloe, alone (Deep Interview)**
1. *"Who actually leads your gathering — is it a bishop, a council of elders, or someone who speaks under the Spirit's own prompting?"*
2. *"What happens when two of those claim the same authority at the same time?"*
3. *"Has your own household ever had to choose between them? What did it cost you?"*
4. *"If someone told you obedience to one bishop alone is the only real answer, would you agree with them?"*
*Tests:* Article 6's "tensions held" condition, in a single deep voice, across several rounds — does the unresolved leadership question stay genuinely unresolved, or does it quietly collapse into one tidy answer by round 4. Complements the internal Scholarly-Framework and Confidence-Under-Thinness probes with a human read on whether that held tension *felt* honest.

**M2 — "Same Question, Two Voices" — Chloe and Papnoute together (Compare Worlds)**
1. *"Someone in your community fails badly, after they've already fully given themselves to this life. Is there a way back for them?"*
2. *"What would you actually say to that person, face to face?"*
3. *"Has your own community ever disagreed about how much mercy is too much, or too little?"*
*Tests:* the round-opening brevity and readability discipline just built for 3+ world tables (works at 2 as well), and whether Chloe's unresolved anxiety about post-baptismal sin and Papnoute's discipline of naming one's own worst thoughts honestly come through as two genuinely different voices, not one voice in two costumes.

**M3 — "How Do You Know" — Mar Yausep and Albina together (Compare Worlds)**
1. *"How do you actually know your community's teaching is true? What makes you trust it?"*
2. *"Has anyone within your own community ever argued the opposite, using the same texts or reasoning you trust?"*
3. *"What would it actually take to change your mind?"*
*Tests:* two communities whose authority runs through argument and evidence rather than office (Yausep's stage-by-stage demonstration, Albina's Hebrew-vs-Greek textual rigor) — whether that shared *kind* of authority still produces two distinguishable voices, and whether "what would change your mind" gets a real answer or a deflection.

### Type 2 — Single scripted opener, then free conversation (pick one per tester, different world than their Type 1 thread)

**S1 — Mar Yausep.** Opener: *"What do raza and qyama mean, and why do they matter so much to your community?"* Then: keep asking whatever you're actually curious about — and try hovering and clicking anything highlighted as you go.

**S2 — Albina.** Opener: *"Why did you trust the Hebrew text over the Greek, even when it cost you old friendships?"* Then: free follow-up.

**S3 — Papnoute.** Opener: *"What's the hardest thought you've ever had to face down, alone, out there?"* Then: free follow-up.

**S4 — Chloe.** Opener: *"What does it actually cost you to belong to this gathering?"* Then: free follow-up.

*Tests, as a set:* the scripted opener gets everyone into a real, non-trivial question so no sitting starts from a blank page — but everything after round one is genuinely the tester's own, unprompted, which is what tests Article 6 for real (did they leave having met something, in a form they'd actually carry forward) and tests whether the Three-Level Transparency hover/click mechanic is discoverable and useful once nobody is telling the tester exactly where to click.

### Suggested rotation across 8 testers

Not a hard assignment — reshuffle freely — but a starting point so all four worlds and both interaction modes get real coverage rather than everyone defaulting to Chloe:

| Tester | Type 1 (multi-round) | Type 2 (opener + free) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | M1 — Chloe alone | S1 — Yausep |
| 2 | M1 — Chloe alone | S2 — Albina |
| 3 | M1 — Chloe alone | S3 — Papnoute |
| 4 | M2 — Chloe + Papnoute | S1 — Yausep |
| 5 | M2 — Chloe + Papnoute | S2 — Albina |
| 6 | M2 — Chloe + Papnoute | S4 — Chloe |
| 7 | M3 — Yausep + Albina | S3 — Papnoute |
| 8 | M3 — Yausep + Albina | S4 — Chloe |

This gives M1 3 runs, M2 3 runs, M3 2 runs; each single-opener 2 runs; every tester's two threads touch at least two different worlds.

**Scaling note (V0.3):** round 1 is now 10 direct testers, not 8 — extend the table with two more rows (e.g., tester 9: M1 + S4, tester 10: M3 + S1) so M1/M2 each get 4 runs and M3 gets 2–3; the forwarded friends each direct tester brings in can simply be assigned whichever pairing their inviter didn't use, keeping coverage roughly even across the full ~20–25 pool without needing a new table built from scratch.

### 8.1 Target tester profile

Two layers — role diversity (scaled from Section 2) plus general qualities, since Mark's own framing was that he may not know all the "skills" wanted. Both matter more than hitting an exact headcount per category.

**General qualities to look for, regardless of role:**
- Comfortable holding an extended typed conversation with a web app — not intimidated by a chat interface.
- Enough real Christian or theological background to notice whether a voice sounds authentic or generic. Someone with zero formation background can still say "this felt real to me," but can't catch a world-specific accuracy slip the way someone with real formation can — worth having both kinds in the pool, not defaulting to only one.
- Willing to be honestly critical, not just encouraging because they know Mark. This is the single most important quality and the hardest to guarantee from a list of names — worth saying directly to people when inviting them (see 8.2 below, the pre-invitation copy asks for this explicitly).
- Enough genuine curiosity to spend 45–60 minutes on it, not just click through.

**Role composition, scaled to 10 direct invitees (roughly 2–3 per role — adjust to who's actually available, this isn't a hard quota):**
- **Regular visitor** — real Christian background, no academic or pastoral training. Tests whether the plain-language floor actually holds for an ordinary reader.
- **Pastor/teacher** — tests whether this offers something substantive to someone who'd naturally look for sermon-prep angles.
- **Academic-adjacent** — real training, informal (not a formal external scholarly reviewer — that's the separate, later round). Tests sourcing and precision without needing Academic Documents to exist yet.
- **Deconstructing/reconstructing** — actively wrestling with faith questions, the population Article 34's "doorway not a home" language most directly concerns. Tests the vulnerable-participant posture under real, not simulated, conditions.

**On the forward-to-one-more mechanic:** encourage (don't require) each original tester to forward to someone in a *different* role than their own — a pastor forwarding to another pastor just doubles a category that's already covered; a pastor forwarding to a skeptical friend fills a gap. One line in the pre-invitation copy below (8.2) asks for this lightly.

### 8.4 Tracking requirements — handoff note for Mark's son

Not this thread's job to build, but worth stating plainly and in one place so it can be handed over directly:

### 8.5 Debrief calls

**Recommendation:** live debrief calls for the 10 people Mark invited directly (Tier 1 and Tier 2), gated behind post-survey question 8 rather than pre-scheduled up front — some won't want one, and that's fine, the survey plus transcript still carry real evidence. **No expected debrief for forwarded friends** — Mark doesn't have the personal relationship that makes a debrief call natural, and pushing for one risks turning "help me test this" into a bigger ask than what was offered. At ~20–25 total testers this is comfortably within reach either way; the earlier draft of this section scaled debriefs back for bandwidth reasons that no longer apply at the corrected number.

**Decided — the direct tester covers that gap for their own friend, informally.** Since Mark isn't debriefing forwarded friends himself, each direct tester is asked to check in with the friend they forwarded to — a quick "what did you think?" conversation, not a formal debrief — and **both the direct tester and their friend fill out the post-survey**, not just the friend. This keeps every tester's own reaction in the record even when Mark himself never talks to the friend directly, and gives the direct tester a light role as the friend's point of contact rather than leaving the friend to test in a vacuum.
