## Part One — Research Brief

### Who is being pictured

Fidelis is not one documented historical bishop. Per `don_Representative_Identity_Options.md` (decided by
Mark, 2026-09-07, refined 2026-09-08), he is a constructed composite voicing the whole Donatist episcopate
across the world's full 311/312–439 CE span as "we" — the same category as Theon, Marius, Yausep, and
Papnoute, not a documented individual like the real bishops named in this world's own Source Registry
(Petilian, Donatus, Parmenian, Primian, etc., all of whom are explicitly excluded from naming or grounding
the Representative — `don_Representative_Identity_Options.md` §0). Appearance is therefore silent and
emblematic in the world's own record, exactly as for the other four composite Representatives, and must be
grounded the same disciplined way: (1) this world's own documents first, (2) time-bound and geography-bound
external historical sources second, (3) only then, defensible diversity the sources themselves actually
support — never a starting default (the standing three-step order, Decision Log 2026-07-24, "The
appearance-research process formalized").

### Step 1 — What Donatism's own record establishes

- **Geography and social center of gravity.** Doc_01 §2 names Carthage as the seat of both rival
  bishoprics, but names **Numidia** as "the region of Donatism's greatest institutional strength —
  numerically dominant there, and the origin region of several of its most prominent figures." The World
  Profile (§4C) sharpens this: "for substantial periods and regions, [Donatism] was not a minority sect but
  the numerically dominant church — concentrated heavily in Numidia's own countryside, against a more urban
  rival strength centered on Carthage and Proconsular Africa." Doc_07 §5 names the same fact as one of this
  world's own distinguishing features against its neighbors in the project's portfolio.
- **Social register.** The same sources are explicit that this is not an elite-minority world: Doc_01 §2
  states participation "spans from bishops and clergy of the parallel hierarchy down to a genuinely broad
  lay base," and Doc_02 §5 notes the access problem here is source-mediation, not scale — "ordinary
  believer" experience is the bulk of the movement, not a separate, thin population. A bishop drawn from,
  and serving, this world is therefore better grounded toward Numidia's rural, agrarian register than toward
  Carthage's Romanized urban elite — the same distinction the build-cycle prompt itself flags against
  Marius's precedent (below).
- **No physical description of any kind.** No figure file (`records/don/figure/*.md`), no gravity file, no
  story chunk, and no Representative-construction phase document (Phase One through Phase Seven,
  `World-Builds/Donatism/Representative/`) describes hair, skin, build, or dress for any Donatist bishop,
  named or composite. This is consistent with the whole portfolio's finding for every composite
  Representative to date.

### Step 2 — Time-bound, geography-bound external sources

**Region and complexion.** Doc_02 §5 itself already names the relevant external-scholarship question, and
already flags exactly where the contest in that scholarship sits — which matters for using it correctly
here. Frend (`don.source`, Registry row 23, already vendored and cited in this world's own build) reads
Numidia's rural strength through "a native-social-protest thesis tied to a Punic/Libyan-speaking rural
substrate resisting a Latinized, Romanized urban Church." Shaw (row 24) and Brown (row 26) are named as "the
standard correctives to that reading, treating the ethnic-substrate frame as considerably overstated
against the actual textual evidence." **The correction that scholarship applies is narrow and does not
reach the use made here:** what Shaw and Brown correct is Frend's *causal* claim — that this Punic/Libyan
substrate explains Circumcellion character and violence. Doc_02 §5 states this precisely: the contest is
over "the native-social-protest / Punic-substrate *reading* of Donatism's rural strength and the
Circumcellions... against the standard correctives to it" — a dispute about interpretation of motive and
character, not about the underlying demographic fact of a Punic- and Libyan-speaking (i.e., indigenous
North African / Berber-descended) rural population coexisting under a Latinized administrative and
liturgical surface in Numidia specifically. That bare demographic fact is not itself part of the contested
claim, and this document does not use it for the contested causal purpose (explaining Circumcellion
conduct) — only for the narrower, uncontested purpose of grounding a rural-Numidian bishop's own regional
population base. On this basis: **Fidelis is grounded as of indigenous North African (Berber/Numidian)
descent, from the rural Numidian population Doc_01/Doc_07 identify as this world's own heartland** — a
warm olive-to-brown Mediterranean-North-African complexion, distinct from a generically "Roman" pale
reading and distinct from the more thoroughly Romanized, cosmopolitan urban-elite look that would be
appropriate for a Carthage-centered figure (which Fidelis, representing the whole communion but grounded
toward its own numerically dominant heartland, is not).

**Hair.** The same Roman-imperial portraiture convention already independently verified for Theon (150–400
CE) and Marius (312–451 CE) — real period portrait sculpture across the whole Roman world shows no defined
side part at any point in this era; that specific styling is a 19th–20th-century Western barbering
convention, not attested in antiquity — applies without modification to Fidelis's own span (311–439 CE),
which sits inside the same broad window already researched twice for this portfolio. Fidelis's own
best-attested textual center of gravity (the Macarian repression, 347–348; the Maximianist affair, 393–398;
the 411 Conference) sits at the later end of that cycle, matching Marius's own already-confirmed convention:
short, close-cropped hair, no part.

**Beard.** Genuinely mixed evidence, disclosed honestly rather than smoothed into false certainty (the same
discipline Papnoute's entry modeled for its own ethnicity gap). Civic Roman fashion in the Latin West
trended toward clean-shaven after Constantine (the same fact Yausep's entry used, by contrast, to ground his
own full beard as distinctively Parthian-sphere rather than Roman). Set against that: a beard carried a
specific, independent authority-coding across the whole late-antique Mediterranean, East and West alike, as
the mark of a philosopher, teacher, or holy man — a coding that begins appearing in the earliest Christian
episcopal and apostolic imagery of exactly this broad period, and that a rural Numidian bishop, formed
outside Carthage's more thoroughly Romanized civic fashion, is at least as likely to carry as to depart
from. Call made, flagged as INFERENCE rather than settled fact: a modest, close-cropped beard — signaling
the provincial, non-metropolitan register this world's own record grounds him in, rather than Carthage's
closer adherence to high Roman civic fashion.

**Dress.** Marius's own already-confirmed research (2026-07-24, "later still") found no distinct clerical
costume existed anywhere in the Latin or Greek church across this whole portfolio's 150–451 CE range —
clergy wore ordinary dress of their own social class, and deliberately shabby dress was itself criticized as
a vanity (Jerome, Ep. 52, c. 394, to a deacon). That finding transfers here at the level of "no distinct
clerical garment," but the *class register* does not transfer unmodified: Marius's own base layer sat under
an already-dyed oxblood outer robe appropriate to his three urban imperial sees (Rome, Milan,
Constantinople). Fidelis's own world explicitly and repeatedly grounds him toward Numidia's rural, agrarian,
non-elite constituency rather than Carthage's urban elite (Doc_01 §2; Doc_07 §4C; World Profile §4C) — the
same standing caution Chloe's own build named directly ("dress/status details from one world's own theme...
must not default onto another world's figure"): here the correction runs the other way from Chloe's own
case, since a rural-register world should not be dressed as though it were an urban-elite one. Grounded
accordingly: a plain, undyed or simply earth-toned (dun, oatmeal, unbleached wool) tunic, unadorned, with a
modest dark, unadorned wool mantle or pallium as the outer garment marking him as a man of some communal
standing without ornament — no gold, no elaborate weave, no jewelry (the locked style rule's own standing
prohibition, doubly appropriate here given the plain register this world's own record actually supports).

### Step 3 — Defensible diversity, not a default

No further diversity move is invented beyond what Steps 1–2 already ground: a rural-Numidian, indigenous
North African (Berber-descended) bishop is itself the sources' own supported reading, not a generic
Mediterranean default and not an unsupported departure from it. Nothing here leans into diversity for its
own sake — it follows the same regionally-grounded discipline the Marius entry modeled (where the
population evidence pointed toward an Anatolian/Aegean-Greek reading, that reading was adopted; here, where
it points toward indigenous North African Numidia, that reading is adopted instead).

## Part Two — The Held Object: Comparative Reasoning (re-derived 2026-09-10)

Per the isolated-portrait two-state rule, the object is held at the chest, fully visible in both hands —
not resting on a table, not a hinted-at edge detail (the Yausep framing lesson). **Hard constraint governing
every candidate below:** no book, codex, scroll, tablet, or any bound/rolled/flat written-text object of any
kind — this rules out the prior candidate (b) outright, and rules out anything that would collide with
Theon (an opened scroll) or Albina (a wax tablet + stylus) by the same logic that disqualifies the prior
pick against Yausep.

This search re-derives candidates from Donatism's own full build record — not only G2 and G3, the two
gravities the prior pass drew on, but every confirmed gravity (G1, G4, G5), both Tensional gravities (T1,
T2), and Doc_02's own material-culture record — checking each for a genuinely documented physical object,
not a metaphor, and checking every candidate that survives that bar against composite-safety (does it belong
to the whole communion, or fabricate a figure's own particular story the way the Papnoute water-jar
correction already ruled out once) and against silhouette-collision with all six existing objects (Chloe's
cup, Theon's opened scroll, Yausep's closed codex, Marius's scroll-case, Papnoute's palm-frond rope-work,
Albina's tablet-and-stylus).

### Candidate (a): A plain water vessel or ewer, for rebaptism (G2) — REJECTED, re-confirmed

**The case for it.** G2 (Rebaptism as Boundary-Marking Practice) is a confirmed Primary gravity and, per
Doc_04 §3.2, "the most concretely enacted, individually experienced marker of belonging this world's own
record documents" — arguably this world's single most historically definitive, literally-enacted practice,
administered by bishops themselves.

**Why it is rejected, now doubly confirmed.** The world's own record never describes a specific physical
implement for this rite at all. Every reference across Doc_01, Doc_05, Doc_07, the World Profile, the
Permanent Prompt, and every gravity/story record checked for this brief speaks only of "the water," "the
font," "the washing" — the *act*, never an object. This is not incidental: baptism and rebaptism in
4th–5th-century Roman North Africa were administered at a fixed baptismal font (a sunken or raised stone
basin, architecture, not a portable vessel) or by immersion, not by a bishop pouring from a hand-held ewer.
This re-derivation checked the point directly against the Representative's own validation record:
`don.demo.baptism-threshold` states explicitly that "this world's own vendored corpus, checked directly this
session, gives the washing's theological meaning, not its procedural mechanics, and reaching past that into
another world's own procedural imagery would itself be the Approved-Source-Anchoring failure Voice
Construction §6 warns against" — no immersion, pouring, or implement detail is anywhere supplied for this
world specifically. Inventing a "water ewer" would not be reaching for a documented but underused detail
(the Papnoute water-jar problem, corrected because it tied to one named monk); it would be inventing a
physical prop this world's own record never supplies at all, purely because the rite it gestures at is well
documented. It would also read as generic Christian baptismal iconography (a pitcher of water) rather than
anything specifically evidenced for this world, and — separately — a water vessel of any kind risks reading
as a drinking-vessel cousin of Chloe's cup at icon scale, exactly the silhouette collision this search is
meant to avoid.

**A specific, targeted check this re-derivation adds: ceremonial hand-washing (*lavabo*) before the rite,**
tied to G1's own "clean hand" image rather than to G2's washing itself. Checked directly against this
world's own build record — `World-Builds/Donatism/` in full and every file under `records/don/` — for
`lavabo`, hand-washing, a basin, or an ewer used by the minister rather than the one being baptized: **no
match anywhere.** This world's own record does not attest a ceremonial hand-washing practice at all, for
Donatism specifically. Per this task's own governing instruction, that absence is reported plainly rather
than imported from generic late-antique Christian practice recalled from outside knowledge. **Rejected for
lack of any grounding in this world's own record**, not merely set aside as a weaker option.

### Candidate (b), SUPERSEDED: A codex containing the martyrs' Passio (G3) — REJECTED, collides with Yausep

The prior pass's pick. Genuinely well-grounded on its own terms — G3 (Martyr-Cult Identity) is this world's
least Author-Gravity-encumbered Primary gravity, resting on three separate Donatist-voiced texts plus
independent epigraphy — but a codex is categorically barred by this reconsideration's own hard constraint,
regardless of how the prior draft differentiated it from Yausep's (open vs. closed). Silhouette recognition
at icon/table-scene scale does not survive that distinction; two of seven Representatives reading as "a
bishop holding a book" defeats the object system's own purpose. **Superseded, not merely revised.**

### Candidate (c): Martyr-cult commemoration, non-text physical elements (G3) — checked directly, none found

Doc_02 §4's own commemoration record (the *Passio Donati* sermon, delivered "*in solemni et anniversaria
commemoratione*," at the martyrs' own grave, annually) was re-read start to finish for anything physical
attached to the commemoration *beyond the reading itself* — lamps, incense, grave-goods, or anything carried
in procession. **None is documented.** The sermon describes the persecution narrative and the annual
reading; `don.story.passio-marculi` and `don.story.macrobius-letter-isaac-maximianus` both describe burial
("took them and buried them with the same rites and the same joy," "the brethren could find him and take
him for burial") but neither names an object placed at the grave or carried to it. `don.ambient.doubled-towns`
names "two processions" as part of the bare physical/social shape of a town carrying two rival congregations
side by side — but that record is explicit that it states only "the visible, physical/social shape," with
`formation_claim_barred: true`, and names no object carried in either procession. Doc_05's own Ecological
Reconstruction repeats the same finding: the community "remembers at the martyr's grave, on the day
appointed" — the reading, not a further physical practice. **This world's own record documents the reading
and the burial; it does not document a further physical commemorative object, and none is invented here.**

Two further items surfaced by this same close re-read of the martyr-cult material, each considered directly
and rejected on its own terms rather than passed over:

- **Marculus's vision of a cup, a crown, and a palm** (`don.story.passio-marculi`), shown to him before his
  death at the cliff of Novapetra. This is formally cited as one of only three manifestations of the
  confirmed G3 gravity (`don.gravity.martyr-cult-identity`), which gives it real standing to consider — but
  the story record's own confidence rating marks the visionary detail specifically as **Inferential/Thin**
  ("the specific visionary and miraculous detail — the cup/crown/palm vision, the unbroken fall, the guiding
  light — [is] Inferential/Thin," `don.story.passio-marculi`'s own divergence note), a Tier 3 hagiographic
  convention, not a documented communal practice. It is also tied to one named martyr's own vision, not
  independently attested for the movement broadly — the exact pattern this project's own Papnoute correction
  already ruled out ("reach for what many, not one, are independently attested doing"). And the cup element
  alone would collide with Chloe's held object regardless of any of the above. Rejected on three independent
  grounds, any one of which would be sufficient alone.
- **A martyr's relic, per the founding-dispute account of Lucilla "kissing the bone of a martyr she kept with
  her"** (`don.story.lucilla-consecration-dispute`, Optatus I.16–20, Confidence A, Documented). This is the
  single most concretely-documented physical object anywhere in this world's vendored corpus — a real
  temptation to reach for, precisely because so little else in the record is physical at all. It is rejected
  regardless: the practice is attested for exactly one named figure, and that figure is Lucilla, a wealthy
  Carthaginian **laywoman**, not a bishop and not the Donatist episcopate — the object belongs to her own
  story, not to Fidelis's office. Worse, the specific incident is a **rebuke**: Caecilian (the archdeacon who
  becomes the rival Caecilianist bishop, not a Donatist figure) corrects her for venerating a martyr "not yet
  even... formally acknowledged" — a disputed, hostile-framed episode about premature veneration, not an
  attested, settled Donatist practice this world's record shows any bishop, or the communion broadly,
  carrying forward. This is precisely the pattern the Papnoute correction already closed off: a vivid,
  well-sourced object tied to one person's own particular story, reached for because it is the only
  physical thing available, not because it is documented as this figure's or this office's own practice.

### Candidate (d): Episcopal insignia tied to ordination or office (G4/G5) — checked directly, not attested; independently anachronism-risked

Checked specifically for any object functioning as an ordination or office marker for a Donatist bishop —
a pallium, a distinctive garment clasp, a staff, or any comparable insignia — across `don.gravity.parallel-institutional-hierarchy`,
`don.gravity.refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy`, Doc_02's own institutional-record section, and the *Gesta
Collationis Carthaginiensis* material (the fullest surviving record of Donatist bishops acting in their own
office, the 411 Conference). **None is attested.** The record documents the *fact* of ordination and
consecration (rival consecrations, the Bagai and Cebarsussi sentences, bishops "recogn[izing]" the
Conference's acts) but never a physical marker of the office itself. Checked independently, per this task's
own instruction not to assume: a bishop's staff or crosier as an episcopal symbol is not attested for this
period and region at all — the crosier's standard use as an episcopal-office marker is a materially later
development (the pastoral staff becomes a standard part of Western episcopal insignia well after this
world's own 311–439 span, closer to the early medieval period), and the pallium in this era is a marker tied
to specific Roman/metropolitan usage, not attested for the rival North African hierarchies this world's own
record actually describes. Proposing either here would be exactly the imported-anachronism risk this task
asked to be checked directly rather than assumed — checked, and rejected on that basis, not merely on the
absence of a Donatist-specific source.

### Candidate (e): The two Tensional gravities (T1, T2) — checked directly, no physical-object content

T1 (Principled Refusal vs. Pragmatic Recourse to Imperial Power) and T2 (Purity-Rigor vs. Institutional
Reception, the Maximianist precedent) were both re-read in full. Both are contests of **acts** — three named
turns to imperial machinery (a petition, forwarded through a governor's *relatio*; a further petition; an
invocation of existing legislation) for T1, and a conciliar reception decision (Bagai, 394) for T2 — not
material practices. Neither gravity's own record names, or implies, any physical object. No candidate is
drawn from either.

### Candidate (f): Doc_02's own material-culture record, re-checked for a portable form

Doc_02 §5 (Material Culture and Daily Life Sources) was re-read for anything renderable as a portable held
object without the fixed-architecture problem that already ruled out the *Deo laudes* stone in the prior
pass. It does not survive re-examination:

- **The *Deo laudes* acclamation** (four catalogued CIL VIII inscriptions, the strongest, least-mediated
  material evidence in this world's record) remains fixed architecture — pillars, an architrave — never a
  portable object in antiquity, and, separately, an inscribed stone is itself a flat written-text object,
  barred by this reconsideration's own hard constraint on exactly the same terms as a book or tablet. Same
  objection as the prior pass, now doubly grounded.
- **Basilica archaeology across Numidia**, named in the same section, is architecture by definition and
  additionally sits at Confidence C, "not yet independently verified against a specific site report" (Doc_02
  §5, §9 item 2) — too evidentially thin to support a specific, vivid held-object claim even before the
  fixed-architecture objection is reached.
- **Imperial legislation** (*Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.52 and the surrounding sequence) is textual and
  document-based throughout, barred by the same hard constraint as the codex.

No candidate survives this re-check; the same objection that closed off the *Deo laudes* stone the first
time closes off every other item in this section too.

### Disposition

**No second, well-grounded, non-text, composite-safe, silhouette-distinct physical object exists in this
world's own record.** This is stated plainly rather than smoothed over: every gravity this world's own
Doc_04 confirms (G1 through G5), both Tensional gravities, and the full material-culture record in Doc_02
have now been checked directly for a genuine, documented physical object — not a metaphor, not an inference
stacked past what a single disputed incident actually supports — and none clears the bar this project's own
discipline already set with the Papnoute correction and the original rebaptism-ewer rejection: real,
specifically-this-world's, not one person's particular story, not generic import from outside knowledge.

Rather than force a weak pick — the same discipline that already ruled out an unattested ewer for the
single most concretely-enacted practice in this entire world's record — this document recommends against
inventing a prop this world's own record does not supply. **The recommended resolution instead makes this
world's own strongest-attested image visible directly, without an external object: Fidelis's own two hands,
open and raised together at the chest, bare and unadorned, in the gesture of administering the rite.** This
is not a substitute prop reached for because nothing else was available — it is this world's own single most
repeated, most directly Donatist-voiced image, and it is stronger-attested than any of the six rejected
candidates above. Petilian of Constantina's own quoted words — "what we look to is the conscience of the
giver, to cleanse that of the recipient" (Registry row 12, Documented, cross-voice attested per Doc_04 §5,
not merely Augustine's characterization) — are this world's fullest surviving Donatist voice naming exactly
this image. Fidelis's own deployed Permanent Prompt (`don_Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Fidelis.txt`,
lines 29 and 41) carries the same image as his own defining vocabulary: "the traditor's tainted hand," and
"the clean hand against the tainted one" — independently confirmed as this voice's own characteristic
imagery in `don.craft.fidelis-voice` ("reaches for the clean hand against the tainted one... Voice
Construction §3, §6"). No other image in this world's entire gravity set is this directly, repeatedly, and
non-hostile-mediated attested as Fidelis's own.

Silhouette-distinctness is real, not incidental: bare, open hands at the chest — no cup, no scroll, no case,
no rope, no tablet — is a genuinely distinct seventh silhouette, arguably the most immediately legible of
all seven precisely because it holds nothing.

**This is flagged, explicitly and not quietly, as a departure from the governing style lock's own "a
historically-grounded held object" line (2026-07-24) and from all six existing Representatives' own
object-holding convention** — a real design pivot, not a default. Every comparable pivot in this project's
own history (the isolated-vs-table two-state rule, the flat-icon-to-portraiture direction change, the
Yausep dress override) has been the project lead's own direct call, not a call this build thread made for
itself. This document's own status line already states research and prompt-drafting only, no image
generated — consistent with that standing discipline, this recommendation is offered for Mark's own review
and decision before any image of Fidelis is generated, not treated here as already settled.

## Part Three — The Gemini Generation Prompt

A fine-art oil portrait, painterly and richly textured, of a North African bishop of Roman late antiquity, painted in the same style as a Rembrandt-period Old Master character study: visible canvas weave and grain throughout, fine cracquelure across the paint surface, warm layered brushwork rather than any photographic or digital smoothness. He is a man in late middle age, of indigenous North African, Numidian descent — a warm olive-to-brown Mediterranean-North-African complexion, dark eyes, short and close-cropped dark hair with a touch of grey at the temples and no side part of any kind, and a modest, close-cropped dark beard, also touched with grey. His expression is warm and open, as though caught mid-conversation with someone he trusts, gently engaged rather than stern, solemn, or mid-speech with an open mouth. His head, shoulders, and body face directly forward, squared straight on to the viewer at eye level — not turned three-quarters or angled to either side — and he looks directly out of the canvas at the viewer, making direct eye contact, so the whole composition sits symmetrically on a single central point of perspective. He wears plain, unbleached and undyed wool garments in a muted oatmeal or dun tone — a simple long tunic beneath a modest dark, unadorned wool mantle draped over one shoulder — with no gold thread, no embroidery, no elaborate weave, and no jewelry of any kind: no rings, no pectoral cross, no ornamented clasp. He holds no object. Instead, his own two bare hands are brought together at his chest, fully visible, gently cupped toward one another so the palms and fingers curve inward and slightly downward, forming a soft hollow between them as though cradling and about to pour water in the middle of administering a rite — a warm, inward gesture of giving and receiving, not raised flat toward the viewer and not a gesture of halting or refusal; nothing held, nothing worn on either hand. The background is a flat, plain, softly warm neutral tone with no scenery, no architecture, and no other figures, in keeping with an isolated character-study portrait. Compose the image as a wide horizontal landscape-oriented bust-up portrait, framed from roughly waist height up and centered directly on the figure, so both hands are clearly and fully visible at the chest rather than only hinted at along the frame's edge — do not crop tightly to the face or shoulders alone.
