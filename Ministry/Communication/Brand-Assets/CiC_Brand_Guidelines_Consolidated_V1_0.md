# Church in Conversation — Brand Guidelines
**Consolidated V1.0 · 2026-07-23**

One-place reference combining the current Writer's Quick Reference (voice/messaging,
updated 2026-07-22) and the Logo Usage Sheet (visual identity, approved 2026-07-18).
Built for handoff/upload use — team onboarding, Canva Brand Kit setup, etc. Source
documents are linked at the bottom; treat those as authoritative if anything here
ever lags behind them.

---

## The name

**Church in Conversation** — no "The." Masterbrand on everything public.

- Internal short form: **CiC**
- Experiential short form: **"the Table"**
- Era series titles sit beneath the masterbrand, never alone — e.g. *Conversations
  with the Early Church*.

## Say it in one breath

*Church in Conversation rebuilds documented Christian movements from their own
words — and you don't just read about them: you join them at a table where they
talk with you, and with each other — openly.*

## Three words

**Transparent · Inviting · Experiential**

## The voice — five pairs (every sentence survives all five)

- **Witness but not recruiting** — state the mission plainly; never push.
- **Rigorous but not distracting** — scholarship underneath, one click away.
- **Accessible but not shallow** — everyday English; full depth reachable.
- **Technology but not seen** — experience first, governance second, mechanism third.
- **Relational but not human** — real conversation; never pretends to be a person.

**Sound like:** N.T. Wright · Tim Mackie · Justo González — a scholar-host at a
table. Warmth through shared discovery, not comfort. BibleProject balance;
10th-grade reading floor.

## The four messages, in order

1. **The whole Church, in the open** — many movements, one table, real disagreement.
2. **It shows its work** — sources and confidence visible; "where the historical
   record is thin, we let the silence stand."
3. **Witness, never recruitment** — representing each movement in full, testimony
   beautiful and hard alike; what it means for you is yours to own and share.
4. **The build is the product** — months of review-gated scholarship; free to any
   seeker; "help us build the next world."

## Use verbatim — never paraphrase

- "A doorway to sit with the Christian movements of history"
- "We measure whether each Christian movement is represented in an engaging and
  accurate way. What it means for you is yours to own and share."
- "We are committed to representing each Christian movement in full — its
  testimony, beautiful and hard alike."
- "Come and join us at the Table"
- "Twenty centuries of the Church. One table. A chair pulled out for you."
- "Where the historical record is thin, we let the silence stand."

## Never

"Finding the right answers" (or any promised answers) · promised transformation
(testify with real quotes only, never promise) · urgency or scarcity · "the only /
the first" · hype and exclamation marks · engagement numbers as success · the
founder in the spotlight · a single tradition as the face of the project ·
Christianese or project jargon · "users" · "honest/honestly" as the catch-all word
(say *open*, *transparent*, or the concrete thing) · "authentic/authentically" ·
denying a suspicion nobody raised — say what it is, once, and stop · "talking" as
the primary word for the interaction (say *conversation*) · stacked "not X, it's Y"
contrast as a sentence shape, even when true — it reads as generated, not crafted.

## Always

Raise the AI trust question yourself, first — then answer it (locked to sources,
shows confidence, tested, reviewed — and what's unfinished, said plainly). Show the
work before the caveat. Real dates, no deadlines. Participation verbs: join, ask,
meet, explore. Numbers: check with Mark before print. Say "conversation," not
"talking." A clarifying contrast naming what kind of thing this is stays fine — the
rule targets *denial*, not *category*.

## Who we're writing to

Publicly: anyone curious how differently Christians have known Jesus — the
curious, the searching, the rebuilding. Silently prioritized: the person whose
faith is unraveling, and the person who's never seen past one doorway. **The
seeker's voice always outranks the donor's.**

## Visual identity — the mark

The C-as-table: a single ring stroke, opening facing right, with one madder dot
outside the mouth, at the threshold. Ink on parchment. Never any third element.

**The public sentence** (said once beside the mark at first contact, never
elaborated in donor material): *"The mark is a table; the opening is the way in —
and it never closes."*

### Colors

- **Ink** `#2A2521` — primary line/text color.
- **Madder** `#A13E2B` — the threshold dot only. Never brightened, never
  duplicated, never recolored.
- **Dark register** `#EDE5D6` / `#CB6E52` — for dark-ground contexts.

### Geometry

ViewBox 100 · ring center (50,50) r 31 · opening ±50° · dot r 6.5 at (88.5, 50),
always outside the ring, never inside in any state.

### Clearspace & minimum sizes

- Clearspace: the ring's stroke width (11/100 of the mark) on all sides — nothing
  enters it, including the wordmark outside lockup spacing.
- Minimums: mark alone 16px (geometric variant below 48px); modulated master never
  below 48px; full lockup minimum 120px wide.

### Wordmark & lockup

**Church *in* Conversation**, set in **Alegreya** — roman, italic "in," no "The."
Mark at cap-height ×1.6; clearspace between mark and wordmark = one dot diameter.
No body/UI typeface is locked in yet — don't declare one as final in any brand kit
tool until that's decided.

### The never-list (the mark)

Never a cross, dove, flame, or speech bubble; nothing tech-flavored · the dot never
inside the ring, never duplicated, never another color · the opening never
narrowed, closed, or pointed anywhere but right · madder never brightened · never
on photography without a parchment or ink plate · never spun, bounced, pulsed at
alert speed, or moved to attract a click.

### Companion illustration

The table-and-chair glyph (`CiC_Mark_TableChair_*.svg`) is **illustration, not
identity** — empty states, tour art, print corners. Never presented as the logo,
never in the favicon slot, never adjacent to the mark in the same composition (two
table-metaphors side by side read as two brands).
