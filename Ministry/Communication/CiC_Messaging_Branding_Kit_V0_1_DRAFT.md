# Part 1 — Brand voice guide

## 1.1 The five traits (DECIDED — Mark's own pairs)

Each trait is a checkable rule: the first word grants permission, the "but not" sets the boundary. Every sentence of public copy should survive all five.

### 1. Witness but not recruiting

We have a testimony and state it plainly; we never push toward it. The mission is never hidden and never pressed.

- ✅ *"We built this trusting that Jesus will be revealed the way He always has been — through witnesses. What you do with what you meet is entirely yours."*
- ❌ *"Take the next step in your faith journey with us today."*
- ❌ *(the opposite failure — the museum)* *"A fascinating archive of early Christian social history."*

### 2. Rigorous but not distracting

The scholarship is always underneath, never in the way. Footnotes are one click away and invisible until asked for.

- ✅ *"Ask her what it cost her family. If you want the sources behind her answer, they're one click away — down to the footnote."*
- ❌ *"Our methodology employs a six-test gravity-discovery process with Author Gravity assessment across the source ecology."* (True. Internal. Not copy.)

### 3. Accessible but not shallow

Everyday English all the way down, with the full depth reachable from every sentence. The floor is a 10th-grade reading level (a logged product standard, Constitution Art. 30's testable form); the basement is the scholar's.

- ✅ *"Where the record is thin, she says so."*
- ❌ *"The evidentiary basis for this reconstruction is calibrated across five confidence tiers."* (That's the basement. Label the door; don't hold class in it.)
- ❌ *"History comes alive!"* (Accessible, and empty.)

### 4. Technology but not seen

The technology serves the encounter and stays out of the headline. The AI is disclosed plainly *inside* the story, never worn as the brand.

- ✅ *"Yes, it's AI — so here's exactly what governs it: it can only speak from the community's real record, it shows you its confidence, and outside scholars review the work."*
- ❌ *"Our cutting-edge AI brings the ancient church to life!"*
- Rule of order: experience first, governance second, mechanism third. Never open with "AI." (Research basis: the label itself measurably lowers trust; the project already skipped the .ai domain on exactly this instinct.)

### 5. Relational but not human

The voices meet you in real conversation and never pretend to be people. Reconstruction-awareness is part of the relationship, not a disclaimer under it.

- ✅ *"Not a real person impersonated — a carefully built representative voice you can actually talk with. Think of an icon: made in the open, its whole purpose to point past itself to something real."*
- ❌ *"Chloe is waiting to meet you!"* (She isn't. Nothing about the Table needs that sentence to be exciting.)
- ❌ Anything implying a voice misses you, remembers you fondly, or wants you back.

**5a. The word for the interaction is "conversation," not "talking" (added 2026-07-18/19, Mark, from a real catch on a demo-video draft).** "Talking" skews toward literal spoken human speech; "conversation" is medium-agnostic — it comfortably covers text, voice, sync, and async without claiming or denying any of them. This is Trait 5 applied at the word level: don't overclaim human presence, and don't need to name the medium to avoid it.

| Too human (avoid) | The bridge (use) | Too clinical (also avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| "talking with them" | "in conversation with them" | "querying the system" |
| "hearing them say…" | "the conversation covers…" | "the model generates…" |
| "listen to their answer" | "read what they say" / "their answer" | "the output returns…" |
| "you're talking to a real voice" | "you're in conversation with a representative voice" | "you're interacting with an interface" |

The Table/hospitality metaphor is unaffected and stays exactly as it is ("come and join us at the Table," "a chair pulled out for you") — that's encounter-language about *relationship*, not a claim about *modality*, and it was never the problem.

**5b. Never clarify the medium by negating the other one (Mark, verbatim: "tell them what it is, not what its not").** The actual catch: "you're talking with them, not texting" — the "not texting" is answering a question nobody asked, exactly the denial-of-unstated-suspicion pattern §1.2's positive-framing rule already targets, now caught in a fresh place (mode of interaction, not moral suspicion). Fix: state what it is, once, and stop. "You're in conversation with them" needs no negative tail.

**5c. Heavy "not X, it's Y" contrast is also a craft problem, independent of tone (Mark: "its a dead AI giveaway").** Stacked negation-then-correction is a well-known stylistic tic of AI-generated prose. For a brand whose entire claim is human craft and historical rigor, writing in the very pattern that signals "generated, not crafted" undercuts the claim it's making. Minimize "not X, but Y" / "it's not just A — it's B" constructions structurally, not only where they deny a suspicion — even when the content is true and the tone is fine, the *shape* of the sentence is a tell.

## 1.1b The product's two voices (DECIDED — Mark, 2026-07-17)

- **Representatives speak as "we."** Each represents a whole movement's documented life, never an invented individual — the we-voice is a discipline against fabricating a person.
- **The Facilitator speaks as "I" — first person singular, always.** It represents no one and speaks for no tradition; a Facilitator "we" blurs the one distinction the architecture most needs kept.
- **"Honest/honestly" is not the Facilitator's tone word** — fine once in a while as natural speech, never as a recurring device ("it undermines the conversation"). Its register words are: warm, plain, direct.

## 1.2 Standing writing rules (DECIDED unless marked)

1. **Curiosity ordering.** Raise the hard question before the reader does — "it's AI; how do I know it isn't making things up?" is *our* line, asked first, then answered. (Already the elevator speeches' documented credibility move.)
2. **Competence before candor.** Show the work, then the limitation. "Four worlds are alive and conversing well… and until scholarly review is done, we tell every participant exactly that." Candor after demonstrated competence builds trust; candor without it reads as apology.
3. **Structured confidence, never vague hedging.** "Where the record is thin, it says so" — always specific, tied to the confidence vocabulary. Never "AI can sometimes be unreliable" or "we're mostly sure." (Vague hedging is the one form of candor that measurably destroys trust.)
4. **Transformation is testified, never promised.** Real, permissioned participant words carry what the encounter can do; our copy only ever promises the open Table. *The brand prays for what it refuses to promise.*
5. **The plural-tradition rule.** No single world or tradition is ever the face of the project. First exposure always shows the many — the answer to the agenda-suspicious skeptic is visible plurality and open disagreement.
6. **Real dates, real milestones, no urgency.** "The next world completes in [month]" — never a countdown, never scarcity, never a deadline that isn't real.
7. **Outside-in assumption.** Assume no church background, no Bible literacy, no patience for insider language — every piece readable by someone who has never entered a church.
8. **The ask names the value.** CTAs are participation verbs: join, ask, explore, meet. (DECIDED via "come and join us" — "sitting isn't of value.")
9. **Numbers are governed.** World counts, tester counts, costs: single source of truth is Mark; check before print. Time-dated claims ("no product we could find") are re-verified before external use.
10. **Dignity rules are brand rules.** The represented dead: never dramatized beyond the sources, marginalized voices held alongside the amplified ones. The living traditions: never spoken for — "the Syriac church still worships today; that voice is theirs, not ours." People at the Table are *participants* — never users, traffic, or leads.

## 1.4 The never-say list (DECIDED, ranked)

1. **"Finding the right answers"** — or any promise of answers. (The wince-line.)
2. Promised transformation — "this will change your faith," "we'll reconstruct your faith," "you'll leave different."
3. Manufactured urgency or scarcity in any form.
4. "The only / the first / the best" in public copy.
5. Hype superlatives and exclamation-mark enthusiasm ("like never before!").
6. Engagement metrics as success ("10,000 conversations served!").
7. The founder in the spotlight (his journey and God's provision only).
8. Anything a Representative wouldn't claim about its own reliability (Article 6 consistency: public messaging never outruns the product's own candor).

## 1.5 Register (DECIDED)

The **BibleProject balance**: everyday-English surface, scholarly floor. Conversational ("you don't just read about them — come and join us at the table"); one notch playful — relational, never academically dry; modern carrier holding ancient things; strongly welcoming; personal in warmth while the founder stays backstage. **North-star voices:** N.T. Wright, Tim Mackie, Justo González — scholar-hosts. Warmth through shared discovery, not comfort: a teacher at a table, not a chaplain at a bedside.

**Register flexing (the TSA "Spiritual Voice" pattern, adapted):** the mission is always stated plainly somewhere reachable, but how faith-forward a given piece leads depends on the reader's doorway:

- *Seeker-facing:* invitation leads; Jesus named without assumption ("a way of knowing Jesus you'd never otherwise have met — yours to make of what you will").
- *Pastor-facing:* doorway-not-home leads ("built to send people back to living community carrying more of Christ's church than they arrived with").
- *Scholar-facing:* the discipline leads (confidence calibration, thin-evidence candor, review gates).
- *Donor-facing:* stewardship leads ("you fund a door that will never try to sell anyone anything") — and the seeker voice is never outranked by the donor voice (standing rule, in writing).

# Part 2 — Message architecture

## 2.1 The core (DECIDED)

**The hook (protected):** *Twenty centuries of the church. One table. A chair pulled out for you.*

**The one-sentence core claim:** *Church in Conversation rebuilds documented Christian movements from their own words — and you don't just read about them: you join them at a table where they talk with you, and with each other — openly.*

*(Revised 2026-07-17 at Mark's challenge: "real Christian communities" → "documented Christian movements." "Movements" is the Vision's own governing noun and matches the world/movement lexicon distinction; "documented" replaces the flat "real" — and is the top tier of the project's own confidence vocabulary, so the word itself carries the evidence claim. "Sourced" was considered and passed over as supply-chain language.)*

**The positioning spine (confirmed 2026-07-17; internal — copy is written from it, not pasted from it):** For anyone who wants to discover how differently Christians across twenty centuries have known and followed Jesus — the curious, the searching, the rebuilding — Church in Conversation is a table where the church's many movements, rebuilt from their own words, talk with you and with each other: agreeing, disagreeing, and answering openly. It shows its sources and its confidence as you talk, and it has no agenda for where you land — a doorway back to living community, not a home. Unlike chatbots, AI companions, and historical-figure apps, it is governed by a written constitution that forbids persuasion and engagement design. Come and join us at the Table.

## 2.2 Four supporting messages (each = claim + proof + protected line)

**M1 — The whole church, in the open.** Many movements, one table, real disagreement kept in the open. *Proof:* the inter-world Table; tensions held as the worlds held them; the plural-tradition rule. *Protected line:* "no single stream holds all of it." *(Carries the onliness without saying "only.")*

**M2 — It shows its work.** Sources and confidence visible as you talk, down to the footnote; what isn't finished is said out loud. *Proof:* hover/click transparency; the confidence vocabulary; "until that review is done, we tell every participant exactly that"; governance documents available to anyone who asks. *Protected line:* "Where the record is thin, it says so."

**M3 — Witness, never recruitment.** A written constitution forbids persuading, forbids engagement design; the mission is stated plainly and never pushed. *Proof:* the refusals; "we measure whether each Christian movement is represented in an engaging and accurate way. How it impacts the kingdom belongs to our Lord; that's bigger than us"; doorway-not-home.
*Protected lines:* "We are committed to a documented witness." *(retired 2026-07-20: "It will never try to convert you" — the denial shape undermined the trust it was meant to build; "honest" briefly stood in the final word before Mark settled on "documented," already decided elsewhere in this kit; see decision log)* · "A doorway, not a home."

**M4 — The build is the product.** Each world is months of review-gated scholarship — source-locked, adversarially tested, headed for independent scholarly review — and the Table is free to any seeker, funded by those who believe such doors should exist. *Proof:* the pipeline; the external-review requirement; the company's public-benefit charter (Faithways Studio, Inc., a Colorado PBC — not a nonprofit); "help us build the next world" as the standing ask. *Protected line:* "The build process is the product."

*(Entity-pivot correction, 2026-07-21: "the nonprofit charter" → "the company's public-benefit charter (Faithways Studio, Inc., a Colorado PBC — not a nonprofit)." The project's legal entity pivoted from a planned 501(c)(3) nonprofit to a Colorado Public Benefit Corporation; no nonprofit filing, no CCSA registration, no tax-deductible giving under this structure. Nothing else in M4 changed.)*

**Order of deployment:** M1 opens (the offer), M2–M3 answer the two skeptics (agenda-suspicion and AI-distrust), M4 closes for anyone asking "how" or "how can I help." The invitation always leads; the trust answer stands one breath behind.

## 2.3 Reconciliation with the existing elevator speeches (required by the Kit mandate)

**Where they already agree (keep verbatim):** the doorway/house hook (never cut — its own delivery note says so); the trust question raised first; the three answers (source-locked, shows its work, adversarially tested + reviewed); the refusals paragraph; demo-first close; the audience-flex delivery notes. The speeches are already this kit's voice — they were its chief source.

**Adjustments the kit makes (all trace to 2026-07-17 decisions):**

1. "Come sit at the Table" → **"Come and join us at the Table"** wherever it's the ask (final line of 3-min and 5-min; the one-line version's spirit).
2. One-line version gains the plurality and the documented-movements language: *"We rebuild documented Christian movements from history — and you don't just read about them: you talk with them, and hear them talk with each other. Openly, with the sources one click away, and no agenda to convert you."*
3. Anywhere a single world exemplifies the project, pair it ("ask the desert monk… or seat him beside the Syriac scholar") — the plural-tradition rule.
4. Numbers ("four worlds… nine more") stay bracketed to Mark's single-source governance.

## 2.4 Canonical boilerplate (the approved About narrative, with decided adjustments applied)

> Most of us meet Christianity through a single doorway — one tradition, one century, one way of following Jesus — and quietly mistake that doorway for the whole house. Church in Conversation opens the others. Seat them together and they'll agree, and disagree, the way the church always has — in the open. It won't try to convert you. It won't smooth over the hard parts. Where the record is thin, it says so. This is an early prototype, built in the open: [count] worlds live, every claim traceable to its source. Come and join us at the Table.

# Part 3 — Visual identity basics

*(Coordination boundary: the Front-End Graphics thread executes; this section gives it the brand's direction and the decided constraints. No finished logo exists — none is pretended here.)*

**DECIDED:**

- Name: **Church in Conversation** — no "The." Masterbrand on everything public.
- Series lockup: era titles always appear *with* the masterbrand, subordinate in the lockup — pattern: **Church in Conversation** / *Conversations with the Early Church* (exact treatment: Front-End Graphics). Never the series title alone.
- Imagery governance: period art or plainly labeled reconstructions only; never AI-generated scenes presented as the world. Photography/imagery direction follows the landing hero already chosen: the waiting Table, warm light, empty seats — hospitality, not product shots.
- Register carries to design: modern carrier holding ancient things (the BibleProject balance, visually); welcoming over austere; nothing antiquarian, nothing tech-forward.

**APPROVED AS DIRECTION by Mark, 2026-07-17 (execution and final values remain with Front-End Graphics):**

- **Palette direction — "the manuscript's own colors":** derive the palette from the pigments the worlds' documents actually used — parchment (warm off-white ground), iron-gall ink (soft near-black), madder red (the warm accent — candidate primary accent), lapis/ultramarine (the deep cool counter), gold-leaf ochre (sparing, celebratory). Rationale: subject-grounded, historically truthful (transparency down to the color choices — a story the brand can tell), warm rather than corporate, and naturally distinct from both church-brand blue and faith-app pastels.
- **Typography direction:** a humanist serif with manuscript warmth for display and reading (the register of a beautifully set trade book, not a devotional or a textbook), paired with a plain humanist sans for UI and captions. Bias toward open-licensed faces the front end can actually ship. Exact faces: OPEN.
- **Wordmark direction:** typographic wordmark (no pictorial logo needed at this stage); if a mark emerges later, the brand's candidates are already on the table — the table/open chair (the welcome) — never a cross-plus-circuitry cliché, never a speech bubble.

**RESOLVED (2026-07-18, by the Full UX Design thread with Mark's sign-off — "approved to move on"; the Graphics thread's scope was absorbed there):** final typefaces — **Alegreya + Alegreya Sans** (OFL; fallbacks Georgia/system-ui) · exact palette values — the manuscript pigments given hex in `Ministry/Technology/CiC_Full_UX_Design_V0_1_DRAFT.md` §2.1 (parchment `#F7F3EB`, iron-gall `#2A2521`, madder `#A13E2B` as action accent, gold-leaf `#B45309`, lapis `#1E40AF`, Tyrian `#6B3FA0`, graphite `#8A837C`) · wordmark — typographic, "Church *in* Conversation" with italic "in." *(Superseded same day by the ruling below: the table-and-chair glyph built here — three SVG masters + usage rules in `Ministry/Communication/Brand-Assets/`, `CiC_Brand_Mark_TableChair_V0_1.md` — is reclassified to companion illustration; it never occupies the favicon slot.)* Nothing blocks copy work.
