# CiC Branding & Messaging Analysis — V0.1 DRAFT

**Date:** 2026-07-17 · **Thread:** Branding & Messaging Analysis · **Status:** DRAFT —
Phase 1 deliverable (analysis and outline of suggestions, not a finished kit) for
Mark's review. Feeds the Messaging & Branding Kit thread, which builds the actual
artifacts.

**What this is:** A researched analysis of what makes branding and messaging genuinely
effective for a donor-funded, honesty-governed formation project — grounded in real
outward research (nonprofit brand scholarship, naming/brand-architecture practice,
trust-messaging evidence), not house intuition — plus the two-name analysis and a
recommended structure for the fuller portfolio.

**What it builds on rather than redoing:** the Marketplace Learning thread's landscape
scan and `CiC_Positioning_Brief_DRAFT_V0_1.md` (category exits, four verified claims,
overclaim register, proof points — all deferred to, none re-derived here); the Vision
V1.1 (Encounter Over Persuasion, Stewardship Over Optimization); Constitution
Article 6 (public messaging may claim nothing a Representative wouldn't claim about
itself); and the existing comms corpus (elevator speeches, landing page, Letter to
Friends, FAQ, Telling the Story plan).

**Discipline for this document:** every technique evaluated below is checked against
the Convictions. Where effective secular practice conflicts with

the Conviction wins. The honest headline of the research, though, is that the two
conflict far less than feared: the strongest available evidence repeatedly lands on
the side the Convictions already chose.

## 1. What makes branding and messaging genuinely effective — the researched analysis

Sources for this section come from three parallel research passes (nonprofit brand
scholarship and case studies; naming and brand architecture; trust-messaging and
ministry-communications ethics), run 2026-07-17. Findings below are tagged where
evidence strength matters; single-study findings are named as such. Full citations
are preserved in the research appendices logged with this thread.

### 1.1 In this sector, brand is trust infrastructure, not a fundraising lever

The foundational study of nonprofit branding (the Harvard Hauser Center / Rockefeller
study published as "The Role of Brand in the Nonprofit Sector," SSIR 2012, and the
follow-on Brand IDEA framework) reframes what a brand is *for* in mission-driven
organizations: its primary job is internal cohesion (everyone inside can say what the
mission is), external trust across many stakeholder types, and the organizational
capacity that follows from trust — not revenue. Resources follow trust, not the other
way around.

The same study documents that nonprofit leaders' instinctive suspicion of "branding"
(fear of mission distortion, of vanity, of marketing overriding participatory values)
is the correct design input, not an obstacle: the fix is to encode those anxieties
into the brand strategy itself.

**What this means for CiC:** the project's charter — the refusals, the visible
confidence, the review gates — is not something the brand has to work *around*. It is
the brand's primary content. The closest case: YouVersion's public identity is built
on its stated refusals ("we would make billions if we monetized; here's why we never
will" — no ads, no data sale, donor-funded, free at 1B+ installs). The constraint is
the message. CiC's constitution gives it more refusals, more precisely stated, than
any product found in the Marketplace scan; branding work is largely the craft of
making those refusals visible, quotable, and believable to someone who will never
read a governance document.

### 1.2 The trust economics this project walks into

- **The trust gap is the sector's defining condition.** Give.org's Donor Trust Report
  (annual, ~2,100+ U.S. adults) finds ~68% of donors say trust is essential before
  giving, while only ~18% report high trust in charities. Donors' top stated
  accountability priorities include **appeal accuracy** — honest messaging is a donor
  priority in the data, not just an ethical stance.
- **Religiously affiliated donors are the most trust-ready segment** (28% high trust
  vs. ~10% among the unaffiliated, Give.org 2024 — single survey wave). That is CiC's
  natural base — and it is borrowed capital, easily destroyed by one overclaim.
- **Transparency measurably pays.** Nonprofits voluntarily disclosing beyond legal
  requirements (GuideStar Seal) averaged 53% more contributions the following year in
  a 6,300-organization study (Harris & Neely 2018 — single large peer-reviewed study,
  direction corroborated by donor-survey data).
- **The overhead ratio is a repudiated metric.** GuideStar, Charity Navigator, and BBB
  jointly disavowed overhead-percentage marketing in 2013. Efficiency-ratio framing
  (including the celebrated but contested charity: water "100% model") is a trap for
  an honesty-governed project: what transfers cleanly is the **evidence loop** — show
  what the money built (charity: water publishes GPS coordinates and photos of every
  well; CiC's analogue is proof-of-method: sources, confidence records, build
  documents, all public) — not the percentage claim.

### 1.3 Honesty is not just permitted to be effective — the evidence says it is the effective strategy, with craft conditions

This is the section that matters most, because it converts the Convictions from
constraints into method:

- **Two-sided messaging (stating real limitations) reliably increases source
  credibility** — a 217-effect-size meta-analysis (Eisend 2006). The credibility gain
  is strongest when disclosure is *voluntary* — offered before anyone forces it.
- **Candor has boundary conditions.** The "blemishing effect" (Ein-Gar, Tormala &
  Shiv 2012) shows a small negative disclosed *after* positive information
  strengthens overall evaluation; the pratfall effect shows admitting flaws helps
  only when competence is already demonstrated. **The craft rule: show the work
  first, then the limitation.** ("Four worlds are alive and conversing well… and
  until scholarly review is done, we tell every participant exactly that." The
  elevator speeches already sequence it correctly.)
- **Structured uncertainty preserves trust; vague hedging destroys it.** The
  strongest single finding for CiC: a five-survey PNAS study (van der Bles et al.
  2020, n=5,780) found communicating uncertainty as ranges/confidence levels causes
  little to no loss of trust in the source — while vague verbal hedging ("there is
  some uncertainty") is what damages credibility. CiC's fixed confidence vocabulary
  (Documented → Widely Accepted → … → Inferential/Thin) is precisely the trust-safe
  form. This is empirical validation, from outside, of Conviction 4's bet — and a
  warning to never let public copy soften the calibrated vocabulary into mush.
- **Published failure builds trust when paired with responsiveness.** charity: water's
  live-streamed failed well outperformed its success broadcasts; Engineers Without
  Borders' annual public Failure Reports became a sector differentiator because
  almost nobody dares copy them. The caution (from charity: water's critics):
  *performed* transparency without responsiveness to hard questions gets called out.
  CiC's "built in the open" status section and change-log discipline are the right
  species of this; the standing obligation is answering the hard follow-up, not just
  publishing the cheerful confession.

### 1.4 Differentiation lives in voice and refusals, not in goodness claims

- Peer-reviewed donor research (Sargeant, Ford & Hudson 2008) found "benevolence"
  differentiates no charity from any other — every charity reads as benevolent. What
  differentiates and correlates with giving: **emotional engagement, a distinctive
  voice, service, tradition.** CiC's distinctive voice already exists in the corpus
  ("a doorway, not a home"; "we measure whether the encounter was honest and free —
  never whether you changed") — the research says that unusual, quotable stance *is*
  the differentiation, and generic warmth is not.
- **Identity-fit is a principal documented driver of giving** (Aaker & Akutsu 2009;
  2025 NVSQ meta-analysis). People give to express who they already are. The donor
  message that fits both the evidence and the charter: speak to the person who
  already wishes seekers could meet church history honestly — donor as **steward of a
  commons**, never donor-as-hero (the StoryBrand hero-framing draws substantive
  ethical critique and misfits a project where the participant's encounter, not the
  donor's ego, is the point).
- **One repeatable sentence outperforms campaigns.** BibleProject's "the Bible as a
  unified story that leads to Jesus" did more work than any marketing push. CiC needs
  its equivalent locked (the corpus has strong candidates — see §3).

### 1.5 The durable-case pattern vs. the blowup pattern

Cross-reading the case studies, the pattern is stark:

| Durable (BibleProject, YouVersion, Wikipedia's "facts banner") | Blowups (Wounded Warrior, Kony 2012, Olive Cooke scandal, Wikimedia urgency banners) |
|---|---|
| Free, genuinely excellent artifact shared peer-to-peer | Paid acquisition / viral spike imported an audience the identity couldn't hold |
| Ask tied to the next concrete output ("we'll make another video if you help") | Fundraising growth treated as mission success (metric substitution) |
| Plain factual asks — Wikipedia's plain "facts banner" beat the emotional appeal by up to 300% | Manufactured urgency, later audited and repudiated by the org's own community (45–3 editor vote) |
| Small committed minority funds a vast free majority (YouVersion: ~40K donors, 1B installs) | Industrialized ask-frequency triggered national scandal and UK regulation |
| Refusals stated publicly and kept | Efficiency claims inflated, then exposed (~$100M donation collapse) |

Two additional pieces close the loop:

- **FrameWorks Institute's randomized framing experiments** — the most rigorous
  evidence base in cause communication — find crisis/urgency framing breeds fatalism
  and *depresses* engagement, while problem-plus-credible-path framing sustains it.
  The no-urgency rule is not a handicap; it is what the controlled experiments
  recommend.
- **BibleProject's flywheel maps exactly onto CiC's shape:** a world-by-world
  construction pipeline is naturally funded by "help us build the next world" — the
  ask always tied to the next concrete artifact, never to organizational need. This
  is the single most directly transferable funding-message mechanic found, and it is
  already consistent with the funding workstream's growth plan.

### 1.6 Messaging AI to wary audiences — the specific craft

- **"AI" as a headline noun measurably lowers trust** (Gursoy et al. 2024: products
  labeled "AI-powered" drew significantly lower intent than identical "high tech"
  framing, worst in high-risk categories — and faith is a high-perceived-risk
  category). Lead with what the experience is and how it is governed; disclose the
  mechanism honestly *inside* the story rather than as the banner. The project's own
  instinct already matches: the nonprofit thread deliberately skipped the .ai domain
  because "the packet leads with ministry and encounter, with the AI explained
  honestly inside."
- **The Christian audience baseline is dual:** Barna/Gloo find pastors far more
  cautious than laypeople (71% vs. 36%), and 72% of practicing Christians fear AI
  replacing pastors and spiritual leaders. The message that answers this is not
  capability but **boundary**: what this will never do (replace church, hear
  confession, claim authority, try to convert). CiC's doorway-not-home language is
  the pre-built answer; it should lead with pastors.
- **Constraint-forward framing is the proven pattern for sacred material.**
  Dimensions in Testimony leads with "never animated, edited, or manipulated" —
  messaging its constraints as its ethics. Father Justin is the negative case: the
  failure was persona overclaim (an "AI priest" claiming an office), and the fix was
  re-labeling to "lay apologist" within ~48 hours. CiC's witness-not-clergy,
  composite-not-impersonation architecture is the structurally safe position — the
  messaging job is naming it before the skeptic asks.
- **Show oversight architecture, don't just promise it.** Khanmigo converted worried
  parents and teachers with visible chat history and a published responsible-AI
  framework. CiC's analogues — visible sources, visible confidence, published
  constitution and build documents "available to anyone who asks" — are the same
  move, already in the landing page draft.
- **Open from the skeptic's position.** The demo-first ask ladder ("come sit at the
  Table, then tell me what you found") is independently supported by the market
  record (critics of religious AI are disproportionately non-users). Raising the
  trust question first — as the elevator speeches and landing page already do — is
  the documented credibility move.

### 1.7 The accreditation floor becomes positioning

ECFA Standard 7.1 (all representations "current, complete, and accurate"; no
communication that "would tend to create a false impression"), AFP's Code (only
documentable claims), and BBB Standard 15 (accurate "expressed, or implied") make
no-overclaim the *baseline* for the donor-funded ministry space CiC will inhabit.
None of them forbids story or emotion; all three forbid manufactured false
impressions. Two implications:

1. CiC's charter exceeds the sector's own truthfulness floor — "the standards our
   accreditors require are our starting point, and our constitution goes further" is
   honest, verifiable positioning (a natural extension of the Marketplace brief's
   "compliance by conviction" claim about AI regulation).
2. The ethics-of-evangelism scholarship (Thiessen's criteria: dignity, freedom to
   reject, undistorted presentation; the witness/proselytism distinction) gives CiC
   *theological* vocabulary for its refusals — the charter forbids what that
   literature already classifies as illegitimate influence, while permitting honest
   witness. This roots Encounter-Over-Persuasion in a respected tradition rather
   than leaving it to sound like secular squeamishness. The attractional-church
   critique (including Willow Creek's own "Reveal" self-study) is the in-house
   confirmation that engagement-optimization fails at formation — the same
   conclusion the charter reaches from conviction.

### 1.8 The refusal register — what common practice CiC must not import, and what to do instead

Consolidated from all three research passes. These are the places where effective-
looking practice conflicts with the Convictions; in each, the Conviction wins — and
in most, the evidence turns out to agree with the Conviction anyway.

| Common practice | Why refused | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Manufactured urgency / deadlines / scarcity | Charter; also empirically backfires (FrameWorks fatalism data; Wikimedia's 45–3 editor repudiation) | Real milestones, real dates: "the next world completes in [month]; here's what remains unfinished today" |
| StoryBrand stakes-agitation and villain-manufacture | Pressure mechanics; formulaic sameness | Keep the clarity spine (one-liner, guide posture, one clear next step); the pivot is an invitation, not a threat |
| Donor-as-hero flattery | Savior-complex critique; misfits a formation project | Donor as steward/patron: "you fund a door that will never try to sell anyone anything" |
| Efficiency-ratio marketing, "100% model" framing | Repudiated by the sector's own watchdogs; contested even for charity: water | Evidence loop: show what the money built (worlds, review, access) |
| Engagement metrics as success story | Charter refuses to collect them; WWP shows metric substitution ends in collapse | Article 6's conditions as the reported measure; formation stories as the currency |
| Viral-spike ambitions | Kony 2012: viral audiences the identity can't retain are a liability | Slow peer sharing through churches, seminaries, classrooms — programs, not funnels |
| "AI-powered" as headline | Measurably lowers trust; draws the category baggage the positioning brief exits | Experience-and-governance first; honest AI disclosure inside the narrative |
| Vague reassurance hedging ("mostly reliable") | The one form of candor that measurably damages trust (PNAS) | The fixed confidence vocabulary, surfaced at the point of claim |
| Neuroscience-flavored persuasion claims (Golden Circle etc.) | Empirically unsupported; overclaim about one's own messaging is still overclaim | State purpose plainly; let the published governance carry conviction |
| Pressure-frequency donor comms | Olive Cooke aftermath; regulatory and trust blowback | Low-frequency, high-substance honest updates ("including what isn't finished yet" — already the landing page's promise) |

One genuine tension to hold honestly rather than resolve on paper: **identity-cueing**
("you are the kind of person who…") is among the best-documented drivers of giving,
and at full strength it is a manipulation technique. The charter-compatible form is
*description, not pressure*: name plainly who the project serves and what a supporter
makes possible, and let self-recognition do the rest. Where a future copy draft
crosses from describing an identity to engineering one, Stewardship Over Optimization
decides against it.

## 2. The two-name analysis

*Clearly labeled, as required: this section frames the decision and recommends. The
decision is Mark's.*

### 2.1 The factual record first

The launch prompt framed this as "both names currently in use." The repo's record is
more lopsided than that framing suggests:

- **"Church in Conversation"** is the reserved Colorado entity name (Statement of
  Reservation filed, per the nonprofit formation log), the registered domain
  (churchinconversation.org / .com, purchased by Mark), the name on every governance
  document, and appears ~376 times across ~89 files.
- **"Conversations with the Early Church"** appears in exactly one substantive place:
  the front-end decision log (2026-07-07), where it was decided as *"Phase 1's real
  name… the named title of the Phase 1 Implementation stage"* in the Vision and
  Phased Plan document.
- A third variant exists and is unresolved: **"The Church in Conversation"** (with
  "The") was applied across the front-end workstream's three documents on
  2026-07-07, deliberately *not* cascaded to the L1 governance documents, with an
  explicit note that it needs Mark's own confirmation.

So the honest starting point: the project's own record already treats these as
**organization name + phase/product title**, not as two candidates for the same
slot. What has not happened is a deliberate, logged *branding* decision confirming
that architecture — the Phase-1-title decision was made inside the front-end
workstream, in passing, without the naming-architecture analysis in front of it.

### 2.2 What the research says about the two framings

**Framing A — masterbrand + era-bound series title** ("Church in Conversation" as the
organization/platform; "Conversations with the Early Church" as the named title of
the current 70–430 CE offering):

- This is a standard, well-documented position on the brand-architecture spectrum
  (Aaker & Joachimsthaler's Brand Relationship Spectrum), and the dominant
  convention in CiC's closest analog spaces: museums title exhibitions with
  narrowly era-bound names under the institution's umbrella ("Apollo's Muse: The
  Moon in the Age of Photography"); The Great Courses carries hundreds of
  era-bound course titles; BibleProject's Classroom titles are book-bound
  ("Ezekiel," "Adam to Noah") with zero era-neutrality — safe because the umbrella
  carries all the identity.
- Practitioner consensus for small, resource-constrained, trust-dependent
  organizations points hard at a single masterbrand: every additional coequal brand
  multiplies marketing cost and splits reputation accrual, and the empirical work
  (Rao 2004; Srinivasan et al. 2015) favors branded-house/sub-brand structures.
- Trademark logic agrees: "Church in Conversation" is suggestive-leaning (more
  protectable); "Conversations with the Early Church" is descriptive (weak alone —
  which doesn't matter one level down, because the umbrella holds the legal and
  reputational equity).

**Framing B — genuine either/or** (pick one name for everything):

- If the pick were "Conversations with the Early Church," the organization would
  adopt an era-bound name **while Reformation-era worlds are already the named
  Phase 2 plan** (Mark's own answer, logged in the Marketplace decision log
  2026-07-16). That is structurally the scope-bound-name trap the case record
  documents: Campus Crusade's "Campus," Gospel for Asia's "Asia," Dunkin's
  "Donuts" — names whose built-in limitation later forced a rename. The ministry
  flagship case, Campus Crusade → Cru (2011), took a two-year board process, 1,600
  candidate names, and still lost donors and weathered public controversy.
  Nonprofit rebrand pricing runs roughly $25–35K for naming alone, $50–100K and
  9–18 months for full execution — plus the donor-goodwill cost money doesn't
  measure. Choosing the era-unbounded name now is the cheap path relative to
  renaming after donor equity accumulates.
- If the pick were "Church in Conversation" alone (no product-line title), the
  project loses a genuinely useful asset: the era-bound title is *honest and
  immediately comprehensible* for what exists today — a four-world early-church
  offering — and (per the Wharton "reflect what you already are" principle) it is
  accurate now in a way it could never safely be at the organization level.

**The drift risk the two-name structure must manage** (this is the finding that was
not obvious before the research): RBC Ministries let its product name, *Our Daily
Bread*, quietly accumulate all the public equity until the organization had to
rename itself after its product (2015). If "Conversations with the Early Church"
becomes the public-facing workhorse while "Church in Conversation" stays backstage
on governance documents, gravitational pull runs exactly that way. The two-name
structure is safe only if the masterbrand is deliberately kept on everything public.

### 2.3 Recommendation (for Mark's decision, not made here)

**Adopt Framing A explicitly:**

1. **"Church in Conversation" is the organization, platform, and public masterbrand** —
   the name on the entity, the domain, the site header, every public artifact. (This
   confirms, as a deliberate branding decision, what the legal and file record
   already de facto is.)
2. **"Conversations with the Early Church" is the named title of the current
   era-bound offering** — the Phase 1 product line / series covering the 70–430 CE
   worlds — always visually and verbally tethered to the masterbrand ("Church in
   Conversation presents: Conversations with the Early Church," or equivalent
   treatment the Kit thread designs). When Reformation-era worlds arrive, they get a
   sibling series title under the same pattern; the naming system extends instead of
   breaking.
3. **Guard against the Our-Daily-Bread drift deliberately:** the masterbrand appears
   on everything public; the series title never appears without it; the Kit thread's
   name-treatment rules encode this.

**Three sub-questions that need Mark's explicit call alongside the main one:**

- **(a) The "The" question.** "The Church in Conversation" vs. "Church in
  Conversation" has been open since 2026-07-07 and now touches the reserved entity
  name and the domain. One form should be canonical everywhere; this analysis leans
  to **"Church in Conversation" without "The"** (matches the reserved entity name,
  the domain, and all L1 governance documents — the lowest-cost consistent state),
  but it is a taste-level call that is genuinely his.
- **(b) Is the series title public-facing or internal?** The current corpus
  (landing page, FAQ, letter, elevator speeches) uses "Church in Conversation"
  exclusively and never needs the series title. A legitimate minimal option is to
  keep "Conversations with the Early Church" as the *phase/catalog* label (visible
  in-product where worlds are browsed, on curricula, in the world map) rather than
  in headline marketing. The Kit thread should decide placement once Mark confirms
  the architecture.
- **(c) The short-form question.** Both names fail everyday say-ability heuristics
  (7–8 syllables each; naming research converges on names getting shortened past ~3
  syllables regardless of intent). Rather than letting abbreviations emerge
  unmanaged, engineer them: "CiC" is already the universal internal handle, and
  **"the Table"** is already emerging organically across the corpus as the
  experiential handle ("come sit at the Table," "a chair pulled out for you") — the
  Cru precedent (an organic internal nickname eventually ratified as the name) says
  take the organic short form seriously as a deliberate brand asset, without
  renaming anything.

### 2.4 What committing to the recommendation would and would not foreclose

It would *not* foreclose: a future era-bound series brand for each phase; the
"Table" experiential language growing into the primary consumer-facing handle; or
even an eventual considered rename (nothing forecloses that — it just gets more
expensive every year donor equity accumulates). It *would* foreclose: marketing the
current offering under "Conversations with the Early Church" as a standalone
identity with its own separate equity — which the research says a project this size
should not fund anyway.

## 3. Recommended portfolio structure — the outline of suggestions

*Structure only, per the Phase 1 mandate. The Messaging & Branding Kit thread fills
this in; sections it already owns are marked. Order is deliberate: each section
depends only on what precedes it.*

**0. Naming architecture decision record** *(blocks everything; needs Mark)* —
the Framing A confirmation (or alternative), the "The" resolution, series-title
placement, sanctioned short forms. One page. Until this exists, the Kit thread
should not finalize a wordmark.

**1. Foundations inheritance** — one page stating what the portfolio answers to and
where it lives: Vision V1.1 (the Convictions as brand content), Constitution
Article 6 (no public claim a Representative wouldn't make about itself), the
Positioning Brief's category exits / four verified claims / overclaim register /
proof points, and this analysis. Purpose: every future writer sees the governing
chain in one place instead of rediscovering it.

**2. Brand voice guide** *(Kit thread deliverable)* — 3–5 checkable traits grounded
in the existing corpus, which this analysis suggests should include at minimum:
*competence-before-candor sequencing* (§1.3), *structured-confidence language, never
vague hedging* (§1.3), *refusals stated in first person as identity, not fine print*
(§1.1, §1.4), *invitation at every pivot where marketing convention expects
pressure* (§1.8). Plus the "we would never say" list — the refusal register in §1.8
and the Positioning Brief's overclaim register are its raw material.

**3. Message architecture** *(Kit thread deliverable)* — the one repeatable sentence
(§1.4; strong existing candidates: the elevator one-liner, "twenty centuries — one
table — a chair pulled out for you," the doorway/house hook); 3–4 supporting
messages (this analysis nominates: the refusals; the build-process-is-the-product;
doorway-not-home; free-to-any-seeker); audience flexes for donor / pastor / scholar
/ seeker / skeptic (the elevator speeches' delivery notes already sketch these);
and the boundary answers pastors need first (§1.6).

**4. Story & evidence assets** — the founder story (already strong in the 5-minute
speech), per-world stories, the P1 tester-quote protocol (already specified in the
Telling the Story plan — never invent, wait for the real one), third-party proof
points (Positioning Brief §4), and a *build-in-the-open cadence*: the monthly
honest-status update, including what isn't finished, as a standing brand asset
(§1.3's failure-publishing evidence; the landing page already promises this).

**5. Visual identity basics** *(Kit thread deliverable; coordinate with Front-End
Graphics thread)* — wordmark treatment (blocked on section 0), palette, typography.
This analysis adds one research-grounded constraint: the name treatment must make
the masterbrand/series-title relationship visible (§2.3), and imagery must follow
the already-logged governance rule (period art or labeled reconstructions, never
AI-generated scenes presented as the world).

**6. Application guides** — how the kit applies to each existing artifact: the
refresh list for landing page / FAQ / Letter / elevator speeches once the kit
exists (the Kit thread's gap list), donor-communication cadence rules
(low-frequency, high-substance — §1.8), and the ask-ladder timing already governed
by the Telling the Story plan (no public give button before CCSA registration).

**7. Brand governance** — who approves public claims (single source of truth: Mark,
already the rule for world counts); maintenance of the overclaim register;
re-verification discipline for time-dated claims ("no product we could find" must
be re-checked before print use — Marketplace log's standing item); the
ECFA-alignment checkpoint (§1.7) as a pre-publication test; and the rule that
every future imported technique gets checked against the Convictions the way §1.8
did.

   rather than re-deriving it.

   the naming-architecture confirmation, the "The" question, series-title
   placement, and short-form sanctioning are Mark's calls (§2.3).

   item remains open; §2's trademark observations are strategy-level, not legal
   clearance. A real knockout search belongs with the attorney work already
   planned.
6. **Did not design UI screens or app graphics** — Front-End Graphics thread.
7. **Did not test any message empirically.** Wikimedia's lesson (§1.5) is that
   disciplined message testing is compatible with honesty; a light, honest testing
   practice (e.g., which of two true sentences lands better) is a Phase 2+
   possibility once public traffic exists — flagged, not built.

   analysis feeds it (esp. §§1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.8).
9. **Research caveats carried forward:** single-source findings are tagged in §1;
   the three research appendices (full citations) are preserved in this thread's
   record; time-sensitive claims should be re-verified before any print use, same
   discipline as the Marketplace scan.
