# CiC Branding Portfolio Benchmark — V0.1

**Date:** 2026-07-17 · **Thread:** Branding & Messaging Analysis · **Status:** DRAFT for
Mark's review. Companion to `CiC_Branding_Messaging_Analysis_V0_1_DRAFT.md` (whose §3
outline this benchmarks) — amends that outline where the benchmark found gaps.

**What this is:** a structural comparison of the recommended CiC portfolio against the
actual published brand portfolios of the top mission-driven organizations — not for
similarity of content, but for **consistency of anatomy**: what components, pieces, and
principles do the best branding strategies in this space all have, and does the CiC
plan have all the organs?

**How it was built:** a research pass (2026-07-17) extracted the real tables of
contents and governing rules from eleven primary brand documents: charity: water's
Brand Book, UNICEF's Brand Toolkit, Habitat for Humanity's 2025 global Brand User
Guide, WWF's Brand Book + brand hub, **Alpha's 2014 Brand Guidelines and December 2025
Messaging Document**, Cru's post-rename brand system and Voice & Tone guide,
Life.Church's Brand Guidelines, The Salvation Army's Brand Strategy + 2019 Standards,
Compassion International's brand handbook + message platform, and Mailchimp's Content
Style Guide (the canonical voice-and-tone reference). Source URLs are preserved in the
thread's research record. Audubon and YouVersion's brand sites exist but could only be
read secondhand; He Gets Us publishes no brand book — all three are excluded from the
anatomy claims.

---

## 1. The headline: Mark's instinct matches the strongest pattern in the set

Every top portfolio examined is organized around **one non-negotiable center of
gravity, stated before any visual rule appears** — and in the best kits it is framed
as a *test*, not a slogan:

| Organization | Their center | How it's framed |
|---|---|---|
| charity: water | Hope instead of guilt, plus proof | "Reinvent charity… genuine transparency… a brand built around hope instead of guilt" |
| Habitat for Humanity | "Together, we build ___ / ___ starts with a home" | Explicitly "a lens through which to assess stories and content ideas," **not** "a set tagline to simply repeat" |
| Alpha | The conversation itself — no-pressure invitation | "Our brand feels like being in a great Alpha small group"; the logo is a question mark |
| The Salvation Army | "Doing the Most Good" | Brand vision *and* "signature promise" |
| Compassion | Reputation as stewardship before God | Built on Proverbs 22:1: "a good name is more desirable than great riches" |
| WWF | The network's DNA | "Our brand is about so much more than our panda logo. It's our DNA" |

Look closer and the strongest centers on that table are **two-poled: an offer plus a
guard**. charity: water offers hope and guards it with proof. Habitat offers "together,
we build" and guards it with printed research. Alpha offers the conversation ("Join
the Conversation" is literally its brand page's title) and guards it with no-pressure
("no pressure, no follow up, no charge"). An offer without a guard is hype; a guard
without an offer is a compliance document.

**CiC's center has the same two poles, and Mark has named both** (2026-07-17):

- **The offer: invitation, conversation.** The open chair at the Table; twenty
  centuries of the church you can actually talk with. This pole was hiding in plain
  sight — it is the project's own name ("Church in *Conversation*"), the hero copy
  ("a chair pulled out for you"), and the standing CTA ("come sit at the Table").
- **The guard: trust, safety, witness-not-recruitment.** The refusals, the visible
  confidence, the doorway-not-home charter — what makes the invitation safe to
  accept.

And CiC starts one step ahead of everyone on the table: its guard already *is* a
test, formally. Constitution Article 6 defines four observable conditions every
encounter is evaluated against (Representative genuinely itself; participant's
authorship protected; world presented honestly with tensions held; interpretation
left to the participant). Habitat had to invent a "lens"; CiC's lens is
constitutional. The portfolio's job is to make every public sentence answerable to
both poles — leading with the invitation, ready with the trust answer — which is
exactly how the existing corpus already sequences its best pages (hero invitation
first, "how is this honest?" one anchor-link below).

## 3. The Alpha comparison — how "witness not recruitment" gets structurally encoded

Mark named the two poles of the center: invitation and conversation as the offer;
trust, safety, and witness-not-recruitment as the guard. The benchmark's sharpest
finding is that the one major ministry brand
built on the same posture — Alpha, an *evangelistic* organization whose entire brand
is no-pressure invitation — does not leave that posture to communicator judgment. It
encodes it in seven structural mechanisms. CiC already possesses raw material for
every one:

| # | Alpha's mechanism | CiC's equivalent (existing material → what the kit should do with it) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brand = replica of the room.** "Our brand feels like being in a great Alpha small group" — every choice testable against the room | CiC's room already exists: **the Table.** Governing test for all public material: *does this sound like something said at an honest Table?* The corpus's warmest language ("a chair pulled out for you") already passes it |
| 2 | **The logo carries the promise.** Alpha's mark is a question mark — inquiry, not answer | The front-end's chosen hero is the waiting Table with empty seats — hospitality, not product. The wordmark direction (Kit + Graphics threads) should carry the same weight: invitation encoded in the mark itself |
| 3 | **Negative space in the voice definition.** "We are not preachy… we don't spin or sugar-coat" — pressure ruled out at the voice level so no campaign can reintroduce it | The refusal register (analysis §1.8) + the Positioning Brief's overclaim register → the kit's "we would never say" list, written as *voice rules*, not policy footnotes |
| 4 | **Audience-voice hierarchy.** Written rule: the donor voice never louder than the guest voice; "giving comes after" | **Adopt verbatim in spirit: the seeker-facing voice outranks the donor-facing voice, in writing.** CiC's free-to-any-seeker charter and the Telling-the-Story ask-ladder already imply it; one sentence in the message architecture makes it enforceable |
| 5 | **Curiosity ordering.** "Lead with curiosity rather than conclusions" — questions before claims, as a writing instruction | CiC's version is already practiced: raise the trust question first ("it's AI — how do I know it isn't making things up?" is the credibility move per the elevator delivery notes). Make it a written rule |
| 6 | **The no-pressure triad as canonical, protected copy.** "No pressure, no follow up, no charge" is a guarded verbal asset used everywhere | CiC owns equivalents that should get **protected verbal asset** status: "a doorway, not a home" · "we measure whether the encounter was honest and free — never whether you changed" · "come sit at the Table" · the doorway/house hook. Protected = used verbatim, never paraphrased into mush, listed in the kit |
| 7 | **Distributed trust with licensed guardrails.** "One Alpha, Many Contexts"; every user a "brand guardian"; a Brand License Agreement — consistency framed as protecting the *guest's* experience | Future-stage for CiC, but this is what §7's partnership placeholder becomes when churches and seminaries run CiC programs: guardianship framed as protecting the participant's encounter, not the institution |

The cross-ministry echo confirms the pattern: Cru encodes the posture as bounded
pairs ("**Missional but not pushy**"); TSA as register-flexing permission ("we
needn't always overtly address spirituality"); Habitat as the open door ("all faiths
and no faith"). The structural common denominator, in the researcher's words: *these
brands write rules that constrain their own evangelistic impulse in guest-facing
contexts, and they place those constraints in governed brand documents — voice
definitions, channel hierarchies, and license agreements — rather than leaving them
to communicator judgment.* That is precisely what CiC's Constitution already does at
the product layer; the kit's job is doing it at the communications layer.

## 4. The principles the top strategies share — and CiC's position against each

1. **One organizing idea everything answers to, stated as a test.** CiC: Article 6 —
   already a four-condition test. *Ahead of benchmark.*
2. **Identity and narrative before hex codes** — the strong kits spend their first
   third on who-we-are. CiC outline ordering already matches. *Consistent.*
3. **Verbal assets engineered as rigorously as visual ones.** charity: water has more
   pages of language rules than logo rules; Alpha ships a standalone Messaging
   Document; Mailchimp's canonical guide is entirely verbal. *Directive for the Kit
   thread: weight the kit toward the verbal system; the wordmark is not the hard
   part.*
4. **Voice defined by bounded contrasts, not adjectives.** The recurring device is
   "X but not Y" (Cru's eight pairs; Alpha's we-are/we-are-not tables). *Adopt as the
   voice guide's format.* Candidate CiC pairs, to react to, not to keep: *Honest but
   not hedging · Warm but not recruiting · Confident but never past the evidence ·
   Reverent but not precious · Open about AI but never led by it.*
5. **Do/don't concreteness with sample copy and sample failures** — the best kits
   pair every rule with a passing and failing example. *Adopt throughout.*
6. **Audience-flexed, never contradictory** — one voice, many tones. ✔ planned.
7. **Consistency via empowerment plus governance, not policing** — templates and
   free assets on one side; contractual guardrails on the other. *Right-sized for
   CiC now (Mark is the guardian); becomes the partnership model later.*
8. **Claims governed by evidence** — Habitat prints its research; cw routes numbers
   through its Programs Team and forbids guaranteeing outcomes ("use can, not
   will"). CiC's confidence vocabulary, freeze gates, and re-verification discipline
   are *ahead of benchmark* — the kit just needs to say so out loud.
9. **Dignity rules are brand rules.** CiC's version — the represented dead and the
   living traditions — is arguably the project's most distinctive contribution to
   this genre. *Formalize it.*
10. **Two-document architecture, versioned and dated.** ✔ already CiC practice.

## 5. Consistency verdict and the amended outline

**Verdict:** the CiC portfolio outline is structurally consistent with the consensus
anatomy of the space's best brand systems, and *ahead* of benchmark in three places —
the organizing-idea-as-test (Article 6), evidence-governed claims (confidence
vocabulary + registers), and naming architecture (§0, which most published kits
lack). The gaps are concentrated at the **word and asset level**, all cheap to add,
and every one of them *reinforces* the trust/safety/witness center rather than
competing with it:

**Amendments to analysis §3 (all become Kit-thread inputs):**

1. **§2 Voice guide gains:** the bounded-pairs format (principle 4); the word-level
   lexicon (use/never-use table); the Christianese-and-project-jargon ban with the
   outside-in assumption rule; the 10th-grade accessible-register floor (already
   decided, needs writing in); the curiosity-ordering rule (trust question first).
2. **§3 Message architecture gains:** the **audience-voice hierarchy** — the
   seeker-facing voice outranks the donor-facing voice, in writing (Alpha's rule,
   CiC's charter); the register-flex section with paired examples (TSA pattern).
3. **New §3.5 (or within §4): Protected verbal assets register** — the phrases used
   verbatim and never paraphrased: "a doorway, not a home"; "honest and free — never
   whether you changed"; "come sit at the Table"; the doorway/house hook. (These are
   to CiC what "no pressure, no follow up, no charge" is to Alpha.)
4. **§4 Story & evidence gains:** the dignity rules for the represented dead and
   living traditions, stated as brand rules.
5. **§7 Governance gains:** a partnership/co-branding placeholder (rules to be
   written before the first external use of the name — seminary, church pilot, or
   press); the brand-guardian framing for whenever anyone beyond Mark writes public
   copy.

**What was checked and deliberately not imported:** brand-health metrics dashboards
(absent from actual published practice; Article 6's conditions are CiC's principled
version), template/tooling libraries (premature), full channel guides (no channels
yet), and scripture-as-structural-device (a register question that belongs to Mark,
not to a benchmark).
