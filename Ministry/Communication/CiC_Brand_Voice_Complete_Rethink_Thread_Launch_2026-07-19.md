## The actual critique, in Mark's own words and terms — work from this, don't soften it

**1. Too negative — the contrast habit, no exceptions carved out.**
Stop constructing sentences as "not X" or "X, not Y." This includes phrases a prior pass
explicitly protected as an exception — Mark is naming this directly: *"even a door not a home
should be on the table, why not just a door."* Don't treat "a doorway, not a home" as untouchable.
Ask what it's actually trying to say, and say that thing plainly instead of by contrast.

**2. Too abstract — sayings that don't mean anything.**
Find every phrase that sounds resonant but, if you ask "what does this actually claim,
specifically?", has no real answer. Replace it with something concrete enough to be wrong -
a claim so specific it could fail to be true, because that's the only kind of claim that's
actually saying something.

**3. No filler, no lazy word choice.**
Every word earns its place. If a sentence works with a word removed, remove it. If a more exact
word exists, use it. This is a line-by-line pass, not a vibe check.

**4. Structure: what comes second should be the experience and the hope, not the governance.**
Mark's direct example: *"the second paragraph isnt about the governed by, it should be about
the experience and hope."* Wherever the copy's structure currently reaches for
policy/governance/charter language early, move it later and let the actual experience - what
happens, what it's like, what it opens up - come first.

**5. Don't assert virtues. Demonstrate them.**
*"We don't need to say things we are trustworthy or we are committed to... that should be seen
by how we do it."* Cut every sentence whose content is a claim about the project's own character
(trustworthy, committed, rigorous, honest) unless it's immediately followed by the specific,
checkable thing that makes it true. If the specific thing is enough on its own, the adjective is
redundant - cut the adjective, keep the specific thing.

**6. Don't sell inspiration. Show the thing.**
*"I want to focus on the why, what and how. not the inspiration, the product will inspire if
they see what it is, we dont have to say its inspiring."* Organize content around three real
questions - why this exists, what it actually is, how it actually works - not around a fourth
mode of "and isn't this moving." If the why/what/how are told well, inspiration is a side effect,
not a section.
