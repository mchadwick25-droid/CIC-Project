# D1 Direction 01 — Editorial / Long-Form Storytelling

**Sandbox artifact, 2026-09-02. Not a deliverable. One of several independent D1 directions; written without sight of the others.**

Files: `homepage.html` (the issue: front matter, opening essay, contents, seven chapter openings, four essays, the door, colophon) and `representative-chloe.html` (the key inner journey: one Representative's full introduction as a feature essay, with the in-app lexicon/citation grammar reused). Both open directly from disk. Portraits are referenced relatively from `cic-website/assets/portraits/`; everything else is inline. Copy marked `draft` is mine and unreviewed; the brand's protected lines appear verbatim and unmarked; the seven traditions, Representatives, dates, regions, and colors are hand-copied from `world-census.json` as of today (seven live, Cappadocian included).

## The philosophy

The site is a book, and the visitor is a reader. Specifically: it is a literary quarterly whose first issue is *The First Centuries* — front matter, a contents page, two Parts, seven chapters, a few essays, a colophon. Each Christian tradition is a chapter, and each chapter is opened by its Representative in their own voice, the way a good anthology lets each contributor speak before the editor does. The prose does the persuading. There is no hero-and-button stack, no feature grid, no card carousel, no tiles. There is a running head, a reading column, plates, marginal notes, and one door.

Why this and not a nice "editorial feel" on a marketing template: the product's own constitution already describes the Table as "a beautifully set trade book on parchment," and the transcript as "a printed dialogue that breathes." A visitor who reads the site as a book and then sits at the Table arrives already inside the register. The site stops being a brochure *about* the experience and becomes the first pages *of* it. That is the whole bet, and it is a real bet — it costs things, named below.

Three consequences I have pushed hard rather than softened:

1. **Narrative sequence over navigation.** The seven Representatives are met in chronological order, inside two Parts with their own part-title pages, not selected from a grid. You meet Chloe first because 70 CE comes first. Every chapter opening is a spread: the plate on one side, the text on the other, alternating recto/verso down the page.
2. **The apparatus is visible, in the margin.** "It shows its work" is the brand's second message, so the site shows its work the way a scholarly edition does: ✲ markers in the text, sidenotes in the outer margin at ≥1100px (inline below that), the same ✲ the app uses for citations. On the Chloe page, lexicon terms carry the app's exact Level-2/Level-3 grammar — dotted Tyrian underline on first occurrence, hover/focus for the short card, click for the full entry in a side panel (bottom sheet on phone), never a modal.
3. **One door, always in the same place.** "Come and join us at the Table" lives in the running head on every page, in madder, and appears once more as the closing line of the issue and of each chapter. Each chapter also carries a quiet "begin a conversation with X / bring X to the Table" line. That is the entire CTA inventory.

## How it serves the four goals

**Accessibility.** Long-form reading is the whole design, so the reading conditions are the design system: 20px Alegreya at 1.55 line-height on a 38rem measure (≈62–66 characters), old-style figures, true paragraph indents, no text under 13.6px carrying meaning. Because everything is prose, body text is held to AAA (7:1), not just AA — iron-gall on parchment is 13.7:1. A page with no competing surfaces is also a page a screen-reader user can move through by headings alone: masthead → contents (a real `<nav>` of in-page links) → Part → Chapter → essay, and every chapter is an `<article>` with its own `aria-labelledby`. Sidenotes are `<aside>`s reached from their marker and readable in flow. Reduced motion removes every transition; with no JavaScript, nothing is hidden.

**Clear storytelling / communication.** This is the goal the direction is built for. The opening essay carries the argument in five paragraphs and raises the AI question itself, first, in the third — before any Representative speaks. Each tradition then gets what the brand asks for and a tile cannot give: a dateline, its approved one-sentence description, and a first paragraph in its own voice that shows what "let the world speak" sounds like — Chloe keeping the bishop-or-council question open, Marius refusing to say which see wins. The Chloe page shows the payoff at full length: a "we" voice that keeps disagreement visible, a section titled *Where we are quiet* that treats the tradition's absent stories as part of the story, and "Where the historical record is thin, we let the silence stand" used as an epigraph rather than a slogan.

**Easy access to features.** Argued, not assumed: the contents page lists every chapter, every essay, the Atlas, and the door in reading order, one screen below the masthead, so a returning visitor is one click from anything. The running head keeps the door at top right on every page, at every scroll position, without asking for attention. Each chapter has its own direct handoff into the app with the tradition pre-seated. What this direction refuses is *multiplying* access points; it argues that one door, always in the same place, is easier to find than four doors in four styles.

**Professional, cutting-edge design that draws people into the story.** The claim is that the most distinctive thing this site can look like, against every other faith-tech and nonprofit site, is a well-set book. Where "cutting-edge" is usually motion and gradient, here it is typographic craft: a genuine masthead, part-title pages, decorative roman numerals set in the rule color behind each chapter, plates with tradition-colored bookmark ribbons, dotted-leader contents, hanging punctuation, small caps from the same superfamily. The slow reveal is the only motion, and it is the page turning, not the page performing.

## What it sacrifices, honestly

- **Speed to the first click.** The door is in the running head from the first pixel, but the page's *rhythm* asks a visitor to read. Someone who wants to try a conversation in ten seconds gets less encouragement here than any other plausible direction gives them. The pilot-recruitment goal (four perspectives, "keep to about five conversations") is carried in one quiet paragraph under the closing line, not surfaced early.
- **Scanability and mobile skimming.** Seven full chapter openings is a long homepage. On a phone, the spreads stack and the page is genuinely long; the contents page is the mitigation, not a cure. A grid says "seven" at a glance; this says it over several screens.
- **The Atlas becomes a fold-out, not a spine.** It is an essay with a link. A visitor who would be caught by the map first is served worse here.
- **Content volume as an ongoing cost.** Every Representative now needs a real, reviewed feature essay — seven now, nine soon, and the direction is only as good as those essays. The current draft-and-approve discipline already rewrites support copy several times a week; this asks it to sign off on long prose in a Representative's voice for every tradition. That is real editorial labor forever, not a one-time build.
- **The cost/support slot is deliberately underweighted.** It is a colophon paragraph. The inheritance note's seam F (six rewrites in a week) is the reason: it is a single content slot with two links, and nothing else in the layout depends on its wording.
- **Portrait resolution.** The locked portraits are 511–748px square; at plate size on a 2× display they will read soft. Either higher-resolution masters or an accepted painterly softness — a production decision, not a design one.

## Typography, color, motion — inside the locked system

**Type.** Alegreya for every reading and display size; Alegreya Sans only for apparatus (running head links, captions, sidenotes, the draft tags). Display scale is pushed: masthead wordmark to 5.4rem, the hook and chapter titles to ~3rem at weight 400 (a book title, not a bold headline), the door line to 3.6rem. Drop caps open essays. Small-caps labels (Part, Chapter, kickers, the contents) use **Alegreya SC** — the same OFL superfamily, its small-caps cut. *Proposed for Mark:* the brand names Alegreya + Alegreya Sans and says no body/UI face is locked; Alegreya SC is an addition within the family, not a new face, but it is not on the list and should be ruled on rather than assumed.

**Color.** Palette untouched. Parchment ground, vellum for the running head, panel, and footer, iron-gall for all prose. Madder does what the constitution says it does — links, the door, focus rings. Gold-leaf marks the Representative's voice (the small-caps "Chloe · Household Leader" label and the rule beside her paragraph), exactly as the app labels a Representative turn. Tyrian marks the apparatus — ✲ markers, lexicon terms, the Level-3 panel. Graphite appears only as a hairline (the provenance rule), never as text, because it fails 4.5:1 on parchment (3.38:1). Per-tradition census colors appear as the plate ribbon (non-text) and the chapter kicker only — every one of the seven passes AA on parchment (4.54–8.40:1), the weakest being Mar Yausep's #b45309 at 4.54.

**Motion.** Two things move. Blocks reveal once as they enter the viewport (1.1 s, 14px rise, never re-hidden, off under reduced motion and absent without JS). The "Arriving" mark plays the brand's own sequence once, in the colophon, beside the one public sentence — the mark's first-contact rule observed once per page, and never on a photograph. Nothing pulses, nothing follows the cursor, nothing moves to attract a click. The table-and-chair glyph is not used anywhere, so the adjacency guard is never tested.

## Accessibility floor

**WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere, with AAA (7:1) contrast for all running prose.** The higher bar on prose is not caution; it follows from the philosophy. A direction whose entire persuasion is long text has no excuse for text that is merely legal. Contrast, measured for this file's actual tokens on parchment `#F7F3EB`: iron-gall 13.70 (prose), ink-faded 5.39 (used only for large italic standfirsts, captions ≥13.6px, and apparatus — AA), madder 5.86 (links), tyrian 6.67, gold-leaf 4.54 (small-caps labels ≥14.4px only). Dark register, on `#17130F`: `#F1E9DD` 15.35, `#B8AEA1` 8.45, `#E08C74` 7.19, `#E0A458` 8.47, `#C9A6E8` 8.89 — all above 7:1. Beyond contrast: 2.2's focus-appearance (2px madder outline, 3px offset, never obscured by the sticky head because the head is 56px and anchors scroll past it), target size (every inline control ≥24×24 through padding and the 31px line box), dragging not required anywhere, skip link, landmarks, a heading outline that reads as the book's contents, reduced-motion parity, and a print stylesheet — a quarterly should print.

## Proposed stretches, for Mark to rule on

1. **The site has one door, not the app's three.** The constitution's S0 threshold has three co-equal doors; this direction hands off to the app's Table field (`?mode=table`) through a single sentence and lets the three doors live in-app. If the three-door threshold is meant to be *visible from the marketing site*, this direction needs a different closing section.
2. **Chapter handoffs carry both links** — "begin a conversation" (`mode=interview`) and "bring to the Table" (`mode=table`) — inheriting seam G from the inheritance note unchanged. If the old gate still stands, the second link is cut everywhere with no layout consequence.
3. **Alegreya SC**, as above.
4. **Lexicon Level-3 on the marketing site.** Reusing the app's grammar for essay lexicon terms means maintaining a web-facing copy of lexicon entries; the mockup's entries are drafts. Either the site reads the same records the app reads, or the essays drop Level-3 and keep only the hover card.
5. **The Atlas palette debt** is inherited, and paid at build: the map should read as this book's fold-out, on the era-ground palette the constitution already assigns it.

Nothing here contradicts the constitution's app rules; the site does not reproduce the Table, the Living Table scene, modes, or guided onboarding, and no copy promises them.
