Method

The full statement, on the About page →

How a tradition is built before anyone speaks for it

Each Christian tradition is built by going through a disciplined ten-step process. We gather and weigh its own letters, sermons, records, and validated sources. We research its own vocabulary, and the internal and external forces that shaped its beliefs and convictions. Every claim is checked against those sources directly and labeled for how well it's attested. Only then, last, is a representative voice created out of that finished, self-contained record.

0

Step 0 — Movement-Scope Eligibility and Seed Identification

The gate before the build. Is this a Christian movement within the project's own stated sense of one, and where does it begin? The answer, and the reasons, go on record either way — a tradition set aside is named and explained, never silently dropped.draft

  1. 1

    World Identification, Boundaries, and Orientation

    Where and when the tradition lived, where its edges are, and a first sight of the forces that shaped it.

  2. 2

    Source Ecology and Source Registry

    Every source it left, weighed for reliability and for the pull of its own author.

  3. 3

    Lexicon Candidate List

    The words the tradition used for itself, gathered before any of them is defined.

  4. 4

    Gravity Discovery

    What organized this tradition. Each candidate is tested six ways and classed Primary, Supporting, or Tensional — or declined, on record.

  5. 5

    Ecological Reconstruction

    The tradition's daily life reassembled from what the sources allow — and the silence kept where they don't.

  6. 6

    Full Lexicon Development

    Each term defined from the sources and labeled with its confidence level.

  7. 7

    Integrated Ecology Analysis

    The tradition checked against itself, for coherence and for contradiction.

  8. 8

    Forces Document

    The initiating, ongoing, and ending forces — from inside and out — that moved it.

  9. 9

    Story Inventory, World Profile, and Validation

    Its stories, tiered by evidence; its profile; its declared limits; the tests it must pass.

  10. 10

    Representative Emergence

    Only now, a voice — built from the frozen record and nothing else. It speaks only from the tradition's own letters, sermons, and primary sources.

Confidence

The five labels are the Constitution's own (Article 17), verbatim, strongest to weakest. The glosses are draft.

Purple is reserved, across the site and inside every conversation, for this apparatus alone.

Every claim carries a label for how well the record supports it

You will meet these five words inside every conversation, on every term in a tradition's vocabulary, and on every story a Representative tells. They are never adjusted for who is asking.draft

  1. Documented Stated directly in the tradition's own surviving sources.
  2. Widely Accepted Not stated outright, but the settled reading across the field.
  3. Dominant Modern Reconstruction The leading scholarly account — a reconstruction, and named as one.
  4. Contested The record supports more than one reading. The disagreement is carried, not resolved.
  5. Inferential / Thin Drawn from little evidence, and said only with that thinness disclosed.

StrongestWeakest

Where the historical record is thin, we let the silence stand.

One of the project's six standing lines. It is a rule of construction, not a slogan: a Representative is built to say "I don't know that" where its tradition left no trace.draft

In a conversation, a term from the tradition's own vocabulary — ekklesia, say — is underlined the first time it appears. A claim that rests on a source carries a small mark at the end of the turn.✲

Hover for the short entry; click for the full one, with sources and confidence — down to the footnote. On a phone: tap, then "Full entry". This is the same grammar the conversation itself uses; the site does not invent a second one.

Disclosure

Raised here by us, before anyone has to ask. The work is shown before the caveat; the caveat is still shown.

This is an AI system. Here is what holds it to the record, and what is still unfinished.

What holds it

  • Locked to sources. A Representative is built from a completed, frozen record and speaks only from the tradition's own letters, sermons, and primary sources.
  • Shows its confidence. The five-level scale above is carried into every conversation and never adjusted for the person asking.
  • Tested before admission. Each tradition passes a sealed probe battery before it is seated; the most recent passed 28 of 28.
  • Watched while it speaks. Every conversation is watched in real time for a softened truth, an invented detail, or knowledge the voice shouldn't have — and corrected before it reaches you.
  • Never a person. A Representative is a voice of its tradition — real conversation, never a pretense of being human, and it will never try to convert you.

Bullets are draft, assembled from the About page and the census record.draft

What is unfinished, said plainly

  • External academic review has not begun. An advisory board to facilitate independent review of each tradition is being formed; it needs funding and volunteers.
  • Representative Modes have not shipped. Every conversation runs on the plain, single-mode Representative today.
  • Later centuries are planned, not built. What is live is the first chapter — the Church's own first four hundred years. Each later era is its own careful, review-gated build, not a shortcut on top of the first.
  • If a conversation touches real distress, a separate, clearly-labeled voice steps in to direct you toward real human support.

We are committed to representing each Christian movement in full — its testimony, beautiful and hard alike.

5

Review

Where external review stands, and what a reviewer receives.

Independent review is the standard. It has not yet begun.

We measure whether each Christian movement is represented in an engaging and accurate way. What it means for you is yours to own and share.

A reviewer's brief exists for the first tradition built — a ten-minute orientation distilled from the record itself, with the full apparatus behind it: world identification, source ecology, lexicon, gravity discovery, ecological reconstruction, forces analysis, story inventory with tier justifications, and validation testing. It ends with the four questions we would most want a scholar of the period to press on. If that is your field, we would welcome your eye: info@churchinconversation.com.draft

Other ways to help that cost nothing: introduce us to a seminary, professor, or scholar who might serve as an external reviewer; or simply ask us questions — the sharpest ones make the work better.
