# Atlas Reimagined — Divergent-Phase Capture (Session 1)

**Status: RAW MATERIAL. Nothing decided, nothing ranked, nothing authorized to
build.** Captured at Mark's request, mid-session, to pause and hold what's
accumulated before continuing. This document organizes by theme, not
chronology — the conversation looped and reconnected ideas across the
session; this groups them so nothing gets lost.

**Frame:** Sam Kaner's diamond (divergent → groan zone → convergent). Mark's
explicit framing: this session is the **divergent zone** — quantity and
openness over judgment, nothing narrowed prematurely. Companion document:
`CiC_Atlas_Reimagined_Research_and_Ideas_V0_1_DRAFT.md` (an earlier,
separately-authored research pass — this document is Mark's own live
brainstorm, captured faithfully, not filtered through that portfolio).

**Standing constraint, restated because it governs everything below:** this
is a sandbox exploration. The live Atlas (`cic-website/atlas-v3.html`,
`data/world-census.json`) is untouched and keeps serving participants until a
replacement exists **and** is judged clearly, significantly better — not
merely different.

## 1. The heart of it, in Mark's own words

*"the atlas/map is a visual way to see Jesus at work, in different ways,
across history and today — and inside that single seeing is access to a mass
of information, organized in more than one way at once."* Not one taxonomy
that wins; many lenses over the same underlying "Jesus at work," coexisting
rather than competing.

## 2. Where "Age of Empires" actually pointed, once unpacked

- The real draw is **tech-tree logic** — seeing how one thing depends on and
  leads to another — not progression/unlocking, which Mark explicitly
  downgraded.
- Art/texture identity (each era having its own visual feel) and scale made
  to feel approachable rather than overwhelming are both real, secondary
  pulls.
- Mark is explicit he is not a gamer and is not wedded to games as the source
  of answers — a personal touchpoint from years ago, offered as a seed, with
  an open invitation to look at better/different models. This is what led to
  the non-game systems research in §7.

## 3. The felt qualities wanted

- **Energy, the "wow" of discovery, a treasure hunt of cool things** —
  finding what you didn't know was there.
- **Serious, deep engagement** with what different movements across 2000
  years and today actually believed and practiced — "what did they think
  about baptism, Jesus, etc." Acknowledged this partly exists already inside
  a conversation, but wanted **other ways to capture it**, not gated behind
  starting a session.
- **Hardships, overdone strengths, and where the church did damage** —
  explicitly *not* wanting these dramatized or spotlighted. Mark's own words:
  *"humble transparency that isn't front and center, but honest
  accessibility for those who are searching for truth, not hype."* This
  later turned out to rhyme with how he wants tension/interaction between
  traditions shown too (§8) — a real, recurring aesthetic instinct, not a
  one-off.

## 4. Organizing lenses named (ways to view the same underlying world data)

Geography, tradition family, theological distinctives, and practices
(communion, baptism, prayer, worship, teaching, hermeneutics, theology,
doctrine, Bible use). Noted in-session: geography and tradition family
already exist as real filters on the live map; theological distinctives and
practices don't yet exist as their own organized facet, though the sourced
content those facets would draw on already exists in the world-build records.

## 5. Entry points and "plug and play" modules

- **The world is the primary door.** The Atlas is *one* way to approach that
  door, not the only one.
- Mark's own framing: *"the same world dataset, pulled in different ways...
  the atlas/map is one way to engage and then we have other ways."* Noted
  in-session that this is close to how the project already thinks under the
  hood (the census file's own metadata once called itself the single source
  of truth for three separate Atlas surfaces, before they were consolidated
  into one page — a real precedent, not a verdict on the new idea).
- **Other ways in, brainstormed:**
  - Direct conversation access (already exists — "obviously").
  - **Sermon-prep access** — a pastor pulling cross-world insight on a
    passage or topic as raw material for their own preparation. Open,
    unresolved heart question: is this still fundamentally an *encounter*
    that overflows into a sermon, or a more purely utilitarian research tool
    where the pastor isn't necessarily the one being formed? Not answered
    this session.
  - **A curriculum for deeper engagement** on one world or era — bigger in
    scale than the app's existing single-sitting "walks"; more like a
    multi-session course. Shape not yet defined.
  - Once at a world: **open an interview, take a tour, research a topic,
    compare practices with other worlds** — a menu of things to do there,
    "brainstorms not set directions" in Mark's words.

## 6. Scope boundary set mid-session (not a raw idea — an actual decision)

*"this thread is about the visual and engagement improvement of the
atlas/map... building on what was built."* Confirmed explicitly: sermon
prep, a curriculum program, direct interview/Table access, and the
separately-already-scoped living-movements-today program
(`CiC_FrontEnd_Decision_Log.md`, 2026-08-14 — its own program, sequenced
after this) are **context for the heart of it, not this thread's build
target.** In scope: the map's own visuals and engagement.

## 7. The core diagnosis (Mark's own words)

*"the current system accommodates the access and tries to lay out a visual
representation that is good in concept but still too hard to use in engaging
and clear way."* Broken down further:

- **Density** and **unclear interaction** both confirmed as real, but —
- **The real one is scope:** *"you can't see the whole picture and dive in,
  its like a spreadsheet that doesn't fit on the page, but to get it on the
  page its too small. we need another way that makes the scale come to
  life, pictures, headlines, scaling etc."* This is the classic
  overview-vs-detail tension named in information design — the whole and the
  readable can't occupy the same pixels at once, at the current visual
  approach's scale.

## 8. The emerging core visual concept — a living landscape

- **Axes: time and swim lanes** (tradition family) — confirmed as the right
  underlying structure, not something to replace. This already matches the
  live census's own data model (`lane`, `laneOrder` fields exist today) — the
  ask is a new visual execution of an already-correct structure, not a new
  structure.
- **Realized as rivers/streams flowing through time.** Noted in-session: the
  current build's own internal design name for this exact area is "Ongoing
  Streams" — a flow metaphor already present in naming, never visually
  built out. Mapped cleanly onto existing, sourced data with nothing new to
  research:
  - A tributary joining → a `formed`/`transmitted to` edge.
  - The same river continuing under a new name → `continuesAs`.
  - A river drying up → a movement's own end date.
  - Each tradition family → its own river system (already color-coded by
    lane today).
- **Interaction and tension between traditions** (the `in tension with` /
  `argued against` edges) — confirmed this is the one piece that doesn't
  fall out of pure hydrology for free. Resolved, lightly, in Mark's own
  words: *"maybe a small symbol or line that represents interaction, it can
  maybe lightly field tension, but interaction would be key."* A quiet mark
  where two streams cross; tension is a modifier on that same mark, not a
  separate dramatic visual language. Named explicitly as the same instinct
  as §3's "humble, not front-and-center" — a real, recurring aesthetic
  principle now showing up twice.
- **The mark can carry scale** — visual weight tied to an honest, sourced
  property (how many connections, how well-attested), not an invented
  importance judgment. Connects to two things surfaced in research: the
  project's own prior rejection of Histomap's width-as-importance encoding
  (1931, "an unearned historical claim"), and Kumu's centrality-based sizing
  (a computed graph fact, not an opinion).
- **The mark should obey the existing hover=short/click=full grammar**
  already locked everywhere else in this product — confirmed as fitting
  without needing a new interaction verb.
- **Still open, not yet resolved this session:**
  - What a "headline" would actually say for a world or era (a punchier,
    more human framing than the formal name — never answered concretely).
  - What "pictures" means in practice (real period art/artifacts, per the
    standing brand rule against AI-generated scene art — vs. something more
    illustrative/stylized — never answered concretely).
  - What the terrain *around* the rivers looks like, and what a "world"
    looks like sitting on this landscape, and what zooming in actually does
    to the view.
  - Whether "scaling" (Mark's word) means zoom-depth, visual weight, or both
    — flagged, not resolved.

## 9. Research surfaced this session (for reference — nothing chosen)

**Non-game, non-atlas systems that organize hundreds+ of connected things:**

- **Kumu** — systems/relationship mapping; filters to "N degrees of
  separation" from a selected node; sizes/colors by computed centrality
  (an honest property, not an opinion).
- **Obsidian's graph view vs. Roam's** — same math (force-directed graph of
  notes), but Obsidian reportedly stays legible at hundreds/thousands of
  nodes while Roam becomes "a wall of undifferentiated grey confusion" at
  the same scale — evidence that hierarchy/differentiation at zoom, not raw
  node count, is what separates a workable large-network view from an
  overwhelming one.
- **Every Noise at Once** (700+ music genres) — placed on a real two-axis
  plot (position means something), common genres rendered bigger, obscure
  ones smaller, colors bleeding between clusters. Famous for being
  addictively browsable at exactly this density-plus-hierarchy combination.
- **Genealogy fan charts** — concentric generation-rings, legible for
  several generations at a glance; for truly huge trees, a different
  geometry entirely (a matrix "quilt") rather than forcing one shape to
  scale infinitely. Principle: different geometry at different scale, even
  where the exact shape (a fan) doesn't transfer to a non-tree dataset.

**Tools evaluated for possible investment (map-focused):**

- **Kumu** — see above; free tier exists, paid tiers for larger projects.
- **Flourish** (BBC/newsroom pedigree, now Canva-owned) — no-code, has a
  built-in network-graph chart type (images on nodes, radial layouts,
  filters, popups), responsive embed. Cheap enough to test directly against
  the real census data before committing to anything custom-built.
- **Rive** — production-grade interactive vector animation, state-machine
  driven (changes happen on real interaction, not ambient looping — fits the
  stillness posture better than most animation tools), tiny file size,
  already shipping in real apps (Duolingo, Google).
- **Spline** — browser-based real-time 3D, $9–20/month, strong for "make
  something feel expensive" on the web. Named tension, unresolved: a real
  style departure from the current flat, manuscript/parchment register.

**Tour-specific tooling (explicitly parked — Phase 2, see §10):**

- **Marzipano / Pannellum** — free, open-source 360° panorama room-tour
  viewers (the museum-virtual-tour category); hotspots for info; Pannellum
  is 21KB, zero dependencies, pure client-side. Fits "sit in a reconstructed
  room, look around, no live actors, no video" exactly.
- **Ren'Py** — mature, free, open-source visual-novel engine (8,000+
  published works); still backgrounds + text/audio dialogue + sound, no
  video, no actors; exports to web. Closer to "witness the service unfold
  beat by beat" than "look around a room" — not mutually exclusive with the
  panorama approach.
- **Working hypothesis, not decided:** no single tool obviously serves both
  the map's density problem and the tour's immersive-scene problem. Possibly
  not one tool, but one consistent commissioned art style/pipeline feeding
  two different lightweight viewers.
