Church in Conversation · Atlas reimagined

Rivers of the Church

A creative storyboard for what the Atlas could look and feel like — ten scenes, one for each of the census's ten eras, walking the whole sheet in order.

Nothing here is decided, built, or authorized to build. Every named world, era, date, edge, and confidence grade is taken directly from cic-website/data/world-census.json (292 movements, 10 eras, 69 sourced edges). Every piece of period art or architecture described is a real, citable object or place, framed the way a museum wall label would frame it. Where this draft takes a creative swing on an open question, it carries a small Proposed mark — so it can be weighed, not mistaken for something already settled.

The founding principle — Mark's own words: “we are building for all the worlds, build and not, just the built worlds are marked so they can dive deeper… the base program is about the entire ecology, not the build worlds.” This is not a map of six built worlds with placeholders around them — it is a map of the real, whole census as it stands today, in which "built" is one honest attribute a movement can carry, never the organizing principle of what the map shows. As more worlds are built, they get marked and deepened in place; the map's own scope doesn't grow to catch up — it was already whole.
Revision 2 — restructured at Mark's explicit request: “we need 10 eras covered, it should be 10 scenes 1 for each era.” The first draft's seven scenes jumped zoom levels and left six eras uncovered. This version walks all ten, in order, one scene each — see the Document log at the bottom for exactly what changed.

The constraints this draft works inside, stated once so the scenes don't have to keep explaining them:

  • The locked manuscript palette — parchment, iron-gall, madder (muted, never brightened toward alert-red), gold-leaf, lapis, Tyrian, graphite.
  • The anti-ghost principle — "these must feel like real people at a table, not spirits or ghosts… no lighting or glow."
  • Hover = short, click = full, and no new interaction verb.
  • No motion on the board at all. The only motion in the whole experience is the participant's own panning and zooming.

The board, stated once

Time flows down. The sheet reads like a page: 70 CE at the top edge, the present at the bottom. Ten era bands cross the sheet horizontally, each on its own approved ground from the census, the three tones alternating in a quiet cadence — warm aged parchment, pale vellum-green, cool pale blue-grey — down all ten bands to the Global Church Era at the bottom, back on warm. Proposed lanes are placed with laneOrder ascending right to left, so the Syriac East & Asia river rises at the sheet's eastern edge and the Protestant and Global Revival lanes open toward the western margin — a loose compass feeling, not a geographic claim.

Each tradition family is a river. The census's own lane/laneOrder fields are the riverbeds; nothing structural is invented. A tributary joining the main channel is a real formed/transmitted to edge. The same river continuing under a new name is a real continuesAs chain. A river ending is a movement's own recorded end date. The one thing a river never does is move. No shimmer, no animated current, no drifting texture. Ever.

Honesty is rendered, not asserted. Built & Live worlds sit on the landscape at full pigment. Everything else is drawn in graphite underdrawing. Confidence keeps its existing honest line-language everywhere a course is drawn: Documented = solid, Widely Accepted = dashed, Contested = dotted — the map never inks a stream it can't defend.

How the scenes walk — the structure, fixed at Mark's direction

Ten scenes, Scene I through Scene X: one for each of the census's ten eras, in order, top of the sheet to bottom. Each scene is that era's own — its real title, dates, tag, ground color, and the real movements and edges the census actually files in it. One honest fact governs how the ten differ: as of V0.20, all six Built & Live worlds sit in Eras I and II; Eras III through X hold none yet. So the first two scenes arrive at inhabited, full-pigment worlds, and the remaining eight are drawn entirely in the graphite-underdrawing register — real names, real dates, real sourced marks, ruled but not yet inked. That is not eight lesser scenes. It is the survey rendered honestly.

What makes still art alive — the creative swing Proposed

  1. Evidence of use. Things shown mid-use, just set down — bread broken, not whole; a letter unfolded, creases showing. No figures needed — the warmth of people is in the room they just stepped out of.
  2. Light with a time of day. Not glow — weather. Lamplight low on plaster in Era I; white desert noon in Egypt; grey first light for vigils. Anti-ghost stays absolute: light lands on objects and architecture, never as an aura on a person.
  3. The mark of the hand in the rendering itself. Visible ink texture, pigment pooling, the graphite underdrawing showing at a river's bank. A made object, not a rendered surface.

Scene I · Era I · 70–312 CE

The Early Church Era

"The apostles' children, under an empire that could turn on them." The participant arrives, and descends to the spring.

What the participant just did

Arriving at the world Proposed

One deliberate pinch further, on a Built & Live world only, and the world opens the way the old mappae mundi drew cities: a small architectural vignette becomes, at deepest zoom, the whole frame — the world's own terrain, organized by the two Primary gravities its Gravity Discovery (Doc_04) actually classified:

  • The table (Liturgical Practice, Primary). An ordinary dining room with the fourth wall open. Bread broken, the shared cup (Chloe's locked emblem, at its native size), a lamp, benches pushed back. Someone was just here.
  • The letter-roads (Translocal Correspondence Network, Primary). Roads lettered Antioch, Smyrna, Corinth, Rome; by the door, letters, unfolded, creases showing. On hover: Polycarp bundling Ignatius's letters for Philippi is "a specific, directly attested transmission event, named by the person who performed it, in his own surviving letter" (Doc_09, Tier 1).
  • The smaller features, honestly scaled: a workshop back-room fitted with benches; a rented upper room above a bathhouse — Justin's school; and, small and unspotlit, a street door, solid, closed, a lamp behind its crack. One click away, undflinching: Pliny's interrogation, and the story inventory's plain statement that this world's single most granular detail of worship "was extracted from two enslaved women, called ministrae, under torture." Present, honestly findable, never a hook.

The art, and where it's drawn from

Baptistery wall paintings, Dura-Europos house-church, Syria, c. 233–256 — an ordinary home converted for worship, the earliest identified Christian church building, preserved because a Roman defensive rampart buried it; the paintings, probably the oldest surviving Christian paintings anywhere, are on display at the Yale University Art Gallery.

The band's texture takes the Dura paintings' own qualities at whisper strength behind the rivers, full legibility inside the opened world. One word policed hard: any human form inside the sourced art stays the solid painted figure it is, never re-rendered translucent. Fidelity to the artifact and anti-ghost turn out to be the same rule.

Alive, without moving

All three principles converge at the spring. Lamplight as real weather — this world met at night and before dawn. No person depicted, and the whole frame warm with people — Mark's own heart for "alive": "not experiencing old dead worlds, but getting a glimpse of life, theology and practices lived."

On screen — the world at the riverbank

The Monastery of Saint Anthony, Eastern Desert, Egypt — a Coptic Orthodox monastery grown up around Antony's own cave, still inhabited and open to visitors.

The hinted presence — anti-ghost, kept

No monk is depicted. A woven rope basket, mid-weave, set down. A water jug in the shade. People are everywhere in this picture; none of them are shown, and none of them are ghosts.

The era's other two live worlds, real and connected

Where the Greek East and Latin West rivers run closest, Imperial and Juridical Christianity is filed not in a lane but on the bridge between two ("Greek East / Latin West"), drawn as an engineered channel — straight banks, cut stone. Its site-mark: the Archbasilica of St John Lateran, Rome — founded under Constantine in the 320s and still the cathedral church of the bishop of Rome. On the Latin West's eastern reach, the Bethlehem Circle (c. 382–420), its site-mark the Church of the Nativity and the Pilgrimage Route, Bethlehem — whose underground caves include the one long shown as Jerome's study. Two real edges tie the Desert onward, both Documented, both solid: "Jerome and Paula toured the Egyptian ascetic settlements" (transmitted to), and, flowing out of the band entirely, "Basil visited Egypt; the whole Greek monastic tradition claims the desert as its root" (formed, downstream). One tributary in; all later monasticism out.

A small mark, seen here first Proposed

Between the Cappadocian course and the Bethlehem Circle, a tiny iron-gall footbridge — whole, solid, Documented — for the edge whose note is simply: "Jerome heard Gregory of Nazianzus teach in Constantinople and calls him his teacher." The interaction mark's first appearance, and it appears the way most of the sheet's bridges are: whole. The full grammar is specified in Scene V.

Alive, without moving

Evidence of use at full strength; and this vignette lives at white noon, the one scene lit mercilessly from above, because that is the desert's true weather and it makes the cave's shade legible as mercy. Stillness here is not a compromise — it is the content.

The featured course — the desert goes west

The mark, specified Proposed

The interaction mark, everywhere on the sheet, is the footbridge first seen whole in Scene II. Its modifiers are honest and quiet:

  • Contemporary contact: the bridge whole.
  • Tension: the same bridge drawn open at the center.
  • Argument: the open bridge with a small book-glyph at one end — nearly every argument in this census is a book.
  • Confidence: the linework goes solid/dashed/dotted as everywhere else.
  • Weight: grows only by an honest, computed property — how many sourced edges meet at that crossing — never an editorial judgment.

No rapids. No storm. No red. The mark between Rome and Constantinople — the largest estrangement on the sheet — is a broken footbridge a few pixels wide, drawn solid because the record is Documented.

Alive, without moving
