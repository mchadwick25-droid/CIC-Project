# Tour Shape Proof — "Experience a Worship Service" (PAHC / Chloe)
## A worked example, not a finished artifact

**Status:** DRAFT shape-proof, written to test the 2026-08-03 redesign decisions
against real content before the redesign thread builds a formal Tour Manifest.
**Not reviewed, not sourced-verified line by line, not ready to ship.** Any exact
quotation below is a best-effort placeholder demonstrating *where* and *how* a
real quotation belongs — the actual wording must be checked against a real
critical edition/translation during construction, the same discipline every
other quoted primary text in this project goes through. Nothing here has been
independently reviewed. This exists to test the shape (does the design actually
work when you try to write it?), the same way Guided Questions proved its shape
with two worked cells before filling the rest.

**Anchor:** `pahcstory006`, Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 65–67, c. 155 CE, Rome.
Tier 1, Class A — the strongest single source in the whole portfolio.

**Entry point:** the Atlas, on The House-Churches' movement card — two options,
side by side: *"Talk with Chloe"* and *"Experience a Worship Service."*

---

## Beat 0 — The Facilitator's threshold (welcome to the experience)

*Spoken once. Warm, brief, orienting. Never repeated once the scene begins.*

> Before you go in — a word about what this is.
>
> What you're about to walk through is built from one real account: a Roman
> writer named Justin, describing his own community's Sunday gathering to the
> emperor, around the year 155. His words are the spine of everything that
> follows. Where he's silent, we say so plainly and keep going, rather than
> filling the silence with a guess dressed up as a fact.
>
> A few things worth knowing before you start: no image survives of any room
> like this one from Rome in this period — none exists to show you, so we
> won't invent one. The exact words of the prayers were never written down.
> And of the ordinary people who might have filled a room like this, almost
> no one is named anywhere in the record.
>
> You can stop, ask a question, or step out at any point — nothing here
> requires you to stay to the end. When you're ready, go in.

## Beat 1 — Chloe's threshold (welcome into the scene, as an observer)

*Chloe, in her own bound voice — content, not narration. She's genuinely
here, doing what a host does. **Correction, per Mark: the participant
experiences this as an observer, not as a member of the gathering** — Chloe
welcomes them to stand and watch, not to take part in what follows. This
matters beyond pacing: simulating a participant personally receiving a
sacrament isn't something this project should stage. Watching a real
community share something honestly is a different, better posture.*

*This is the room-description beat, and per Mark's follow-up, where the
exemplar image belongs — the moment the participant is first looking at the
physical space, before anyone else has arrived to fill it. Per the
2026-08-03 visual-policy decision: a real photograph of the Pompeii/
Herculaneum excavations, never a generated or commissioned reconstruction,
with a small museum-label caption — not a banner, not spoken by Chloe or the
Facilitator, just a quiet, always-discoverable tag on the image itself.*

> Come in — stand there, by the wall, where you can see the whole room.
>
> You don't need to have been here before, and you don't need to do
> anything but watch. We keep the room the way it's always kept on the
> first day: swept, the lamps filled. In a little while it'll be full.

## Beat 2 — The gathering assembles

*Facilitator narration, brief, sensory. Honest-silence beat folded in, not
apologized for — just stated and moved past.*

> They come from across the city and in from the countryside around it —
> Justin's own words for who filled a room like this. A greeting at the
> door, someone finding a place to stand, a child quieted. Ordinary
> arrival, in an ordinary room.
>
> Almost none of them have a name that survives anywhere. Of everyone who
> might have stood in a room like this, in this whole world's entire record,
> two names come down to us: Tavia, and the wife of Epitropus. A name and a
> greeting. Nothing more.

---

## Beat 3 — The reading

*The primary-source voice (distinct treatment from both the Facilitator and
Chloe) — "the memoirs of the apostles, or the writings of the prophets, as
long as time permits." Justin doesn't name the specific text read that day.
This is where the fill-density/exemplar rule does its work — filled with a
real letter, honestly marked as an example, not a claim about this occasion.*

> **[FACILITATOR, briefly, before the reading begins]**
> Justin doesn't tell us which text was read that day — only that it was
> read "as long as time permits." What follows is a real letter these
> communities are known to have read aloud in gatherings like this one — not
> a record of what was read at this specific meeting, but a genuine example
> of the kind of thing that would have been.

> **[QUOTED TEXT — candidate: Ignatius, Letter to the Romans, c. 107–117 CE
> — verify exact wording and translation before use]**
> *"I am writing to all the churches, and I give injunctions to all, that I
> am dying willingly for God's sake, if you do not hinder it. I beg of you
> not to be unseasonably kind to me... Suffer me to be food for the wild
> beasts, through whom I can attain to God. I am God's wheat, and I am
> ground by the teeth of wild beasts, that I may be found pure bread of
> Christ."*

*(Construction note: this is the strongest, most famous candidate line — but
the redesign thread should weigh whether a martyrdom letter fits a Sunday
reading beat, or whether 1 Clement's calmer, more communal register — a
Roman church writing to another church about order and unity — is the better
fit for "what Rome itself might have read aloud." Named as an open call, not
decided here.)*

---

## Beat 4 — The exhortation and the common prayers

*Honest-silence beats, stated plainly, not dwelt on.*

> The one presiding speaks — urging the room, in Justin's words, to imitate
> the good things they've just heard. What he actually said that day isn't
> recorded anywhere. We don't know.
>
> Then everyone stands, together, for prayer — for themselves, Justin says,
> and for the whole world besides. The exact words of that prayer were
> never written down either. What we have is its shape: standing, together,
> praying past their own room to everyone alive.

---

## Beat 5 — The bread and the cup

*This is the beat Mark asked to be experienced, not summarized. Slow, full
sensory pacing, real time rather than compressed. Facilitator narration
throughout, since this is the room's own attested action, not a claim
requiring the exemplar framing — it's what Justin says plainly happened.
**Corrected, per Mark: watched, not received.** The participant is standing
where Chloe placed them at the threshold — present in the room, seeing
everything, receiving nothing themselves. The intimacy comes from watching a
real community do this for each other, not from simulating personal
reception of a sacrament.*

*Opens with a brief Facilitator aside, per Mark's addition — the diversity-
first rule made explicit and spoken, not just noted in a construction
comment. This is where a participant would naturally wonder "wasn't there a
meal?" — so the honest answer belongs exactly here, not buried at the
threshold where it would be forgotten by now.*

> **[FACILITATOR]**
> A word before this part. Practices like this varied across this
> movement. Earlier, in Corinth, Paul's own letters describe a fuller meal
> held alongside this same rite — one that caused real trouble, when some
> ate before others could even arrive. Here, in Rome, as Justin describes
> it, the meal has become something more compact: bread, and a cup, and
> nothing more. We're staying with what he tells us.

> Bread is brought forward. Wine, mixed with water, in a cup.
>
> The one presiding takes them and gives thanks — at some length, Justin
> says, "according to his ability" — for everything they've been given.
> Nobody wrote down the words. What's certain is the shape of it: real
> thanks, spoken aloud, over real bread and a real cup, by someone who
> could speak longer or shorter depending on what the day called for.
>
> When he finishes, the whole room answers together: *Amen.* One word,
> every voice at once — the only line of that whole prayer that Justin
> actually records.
>
> Then you watch it move. The deacons carry it person to person — bread
> pressed into one hand after another, the cup held while each person
> drinks, passed on down the row. An old man near the door. Someone who
> looks like they only just arrived in the city. A child, given a small
> piece by the person holding her. Justin is specific about one more thing:
> what's left over doesn't stay in the room. Once everyone here has been
> given a share, the deacons take a portion out afterward, to the people
> who couldn't come — the sick, the imprisoned, whoever was kept away.
> What you're watching doesn't end at this door. It goes out looking for
> the people who were missing from it.

---

## Beat 6 — The collection

*Facilitator narration, concrete and specific — Justin's own account again,
attested detail, no filling needed.*

> Before they leave, whoever is willing and able gives — however much they
> choose, whenever they choose, nothing required of anyone. It's laid with
> the one presiding, and it goes out from there: to orphans, widows, the
> sick, people in prison, strangers passing through with nowhere to stay.
> Justin's own phrase for who it reaches: "all who are in need."

---

## Beat 7 — Return

*Facilitator, closing the frame it opened at Beat 0. The boundary door.*

> That's what one Roman writer tells us happened, on the first day of the
> week, in a room very much like this one.
>
> If you have a question this didn't answer, I can tell you what we do and
> don't know beyond what you've just seen. Or, if you'd like to keep going
> — Chloe is here, and you're welcome to sit with her yourself.

## Open construction questions this draft surfaces (for the redesign thread, not answered here)

1. **The letter candidate** — Ignatius's Romans 4 is vivid and famous but is a
   martyrdom letter; 1 Clement may better fit "an ordinary Sunday reading in
   Rome." Needs a real editorial call, not a default to the more dramatic
   option.
2. **Exact wording of every quoted passage** — Justin's own lines above are
   paraphrased close to the standard translations from memory, not copied from
   a verified edition. Every quotation in this draft needs checking against a
   real, citable translation before anything is treated as final text.
3. **Pacing/length** — this draft is written at roughly the density Mark asked
   for; whether it's too long, too short, or right needs an actual read-through
   at the pace a participant would experience it, not just a read on the page.
4. **The image itself** — placement (Beat 1, the room-description beat) and
   caption text are now drafted. What's still open: which specific Pompeii or
   Herculaneum site/photograph to use, and verifying its rights/licensing —
   a real production step for the construction thread, not decided here.

## Settled by this draft, worth carrying forward as a named rule

**The participant is an observer throughout, never a participant in what's
depicted — this is now a hard design rule, not a stylistic choice for this
one tour.** The first draft of Beats 1 and 5 wrote the participant as an
active member of the gathering (welcomed as a guest arriving to share the
meal, bread "pressed into your hand"). Corrected per Mark: they stand where
Chloe places them and watch. This isn't only a pacing/immersion call — it
draws a real boundary this project should keep regardless of any single
tour's content: simulating a participant personally receiving a sacrament
through an AI-mediated experience isn't something to stage, however well
sourced the surrounding scene is. Watching a real community do something for
each other is a different, better, honest posture, and it should be checked
for in every future tour's beats, not just this one.
