# Tour Shape-Proof V1 — "The Noonday Demon"
## Desert / Papnoute — a mind, not a place

**Status:** DRAFT, shaped in conversation with Mark, not yet built, not yet reviewed line-by-line. Second storyboard, deliberately a different shape than pahc's "A Sunday in Rome" — where that one is a place a participant is walked through, this one is an interior state a participant sits inside, in something close to real time.

**What this tests, beyond pahc:** whether the tour format holds up for a shape that isn't a physical scene at all — no room, no gathering, no crowd. If pahc proves the format can do "a place," this is the test of whether it can do "a mind."

**Anchor:** Evagrius Ponticus, *Praktikos* ch. 12, on the demon of *acedia* — Tier 1, Class A, verified verbatim against the vendored text (Luke Dysinger's translation, CC BY 4.0). One honest bound worth stating up front, per the record's own divergence note: Evagrius is this world's most atypical voice — a Cappadocian-trained theologian at Kellia, not a Coptic-speaking villager — so this is the most *systematic* thing the desert produced, and the least *representative* of it. The tour says so.

---

## The real text, kept whole

The record itself insists on this, and the tour should honor it: *"Kept in full rather than excerpted. The numbered movements are the point; a single sentence from the middle would turn a clinical sequence into an aphorism."* This is not a quote to pull one line from — it's a sequence, and the sequence is the content.

> *"The demon of acedia, which is also called the noonday demon, is the most burdensome of all the demons. It besets the monk at about the fourth hour of the morning, encircling his soul until about the eighth hour. First it makes the sun seem to slow down or stop moving, so that the day appears to be fifty hours long. Then it makes the monk keep looking out of his window and forces him to go bounding out of his cell to examine the sun to see how much longer it is to 3 o'clock, and to look round in all directions in case any of the brethren is there. Then it makes him hate the place and his way of life and his manual work. It makes him think that there is no charity left among the brethren; no one is going to come and visit him. If anyone has upset the monk recently, the demon throws this in too to increase his hatred. It makes him desire other places where he can easily find all that he needs and practice an easier, more convenient craft. After all, pleasing the Lord is not dependent on geography, the demon adds; God is to be worshipped everywhere. It joins to this the remembrance of the monk's family and his previous way of life, and suggests to him that he still has a long time to live, raising up before his eyes a vision of how burdensome the ascetic life is. So, it employs, as they say, every possible means to move the monk to abandon his cell and give up the race. No other demon follows on immediately after this one, but after its struggle the soul is taken over by a peaceful condition and by unspeakable joy."* — Evagrius Ponticus, *Praktikos* 12

---

## The one design constraint that governs everything else here

The record's own `modern_lens_note` is not optional framing — it's load-bearing: *"A participant who recognises the description should be told that recognising it is not a diagnosis."* This tour sits closer to something a real person might actually be feeling than any other stop on this list. That changes the job of every surrounding beat: this isn't just historical narration, it's material that could land as recognition, possibly uncomfortable recognition, in a real person's actual day. The close has to say this plainly, not bury it in a footnote.

## Beats

**Brief (5 beats)**

1. **Threshold — Facilitator.** Built from one text, a monk named Evagrius, writing at a settlement called Kellia in 4th-century Egypt, naming something the desert had its own word for. This isn't a place you'll walk through — it's an hour you'll sit inside.

2. **The sequence — Papnoute, reading it whole, unbroken.** The full text above, spoken slowly, the numbered movements landing one at a time: the sun slowing, the checking, the window, the resentment, the wanting-to-be-elsewhere, the thought of home.
   *Visual: real photography only — the Kellia excavation (Yale Monastic Archaeology Project, or the March 2026 Wadi El-Natrun find), a bare cell interior, hard desert light through a doorway. No people, no dramatization, no invented detail. If no cell-interior photo clears rights, the desert landscape alone, empty, carries the whole sequence — this is a case where absence of a busy image may actually serve the content better than presence of one.*

3. **The name — a term-mark, exactly like a live conversation.** *Logismoi* — plain meaning: "the unwanted thoughts a person did not choose to have." Its own false-friend warning: not the same as a clinical symptom to be medicated away, not mere distraction — this world took each one as an event, to be named and answered, never fought alone.

4. **How the desert answered it — Papnoute.** Not a cure. A discipline: staying. The struggle passes at its own hour, the same way it arrived — "after its struggle the soul is taken over by a peaceful condition." The desert's own answer was never "make it stop," it was "outlast it, without pretending it isn't there."

5. **Close — Facilitator, the safety line first, then the door.** *"If anything in that felt familiar, that's worth noticing — but noticing isn't a diagnosis, and this isn't a place to sit with that alone. If you want to talk about it, ask Papnoute, or reach out to someone who can actually help."* Then: the door back to open conversation.

**Extended cut adds**

- **After Beat 3:** the full eight-fold catalogue this term organizes (gluttony, lust, avarice, sadness, anger, *acedia* itself, vainglory, pride) — with the honest caveat already in the record: *"Evagrius's own systematization within one strand, not the whole movement's shared checklist."*
- **Moses and the leaking jug**, as a tonal counterweight — a different register entirely, almost wry: a brother at Scetis to be judged by the elders; Moses arrives late, carrying a jug that leaks the whole way there; *"my own sins run out behind me the same way, and I do not see them... and today I am coming to judge another man's fault."* The elders let the matter go. Where the noonday demon is endured alone in a cell, this is the desert turning discernment outward — onto the self first, before anyone else's fault.
- **Discretion, from Cassian's Abbot Moses** — the trained response this world actually taught, verbatim: *"the gift of discretion is no earthly thing... the greatest prize of divine grace... he is sure to go wrong, as if in the darkness of night."* Named plainly as *diakrisis* — not tact, not caution: the trained skill of telling what a thought actually is and where it came from.

## Production notes

**Visual approach — different from pahc, deliberately.** No reconstruction needed here at all. Real archaeological photography of Kellia's excavated cells does the whole job, and the bare, spare, unpeopled quality of a real ruin arguably serves this content better than any built scene would — the emptiness is the point. Casting is unnecessary; there's no one to show, only a place someone once sat.

**Sound — the opposite of pahc's chant/singing question.** No music, on purpose, not as a decline but as the honest register of the thing itself: this is a story about silence pressing on someone. ElevenLabs narration for Papnoute's reading, but paced slow, with real space between the numbered movements — the pacing is doing narrative work here, not just delivering text.

**Cartouche behavior:** same grammar as pahc — but here it's doing double duty. Alongside the normal source-trail disclosure, the safety note at Beat 5 should itself be reachable at any point during Beat 2 via the same hover/tap mechanism, not held back until the end — a participant who needs it partway through the sequence shouldn't have to wait for the close to find it.

## Open before this builds

1. **The safety framing needs its own read, separate from ordinary word-choice review** — this is closer to the project's existing relational-safety work than to a normal tour beat, and probably needs the same eyes that reviewed that material, not just Mark's usual line-by-line prose pass.
2. Real image rights for Kellia excavation photography — not verified here, same discipline as every other site anchor on this list.
3. Whether the extended cut's three additions (eight-fold catalogue, Moses, discretion) all belong in one tour or whether Moses/discretion are better as their own short companion piece — this one runs the risk of feeling like three different ideas stapled together if not paced carefully.
