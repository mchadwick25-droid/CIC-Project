# Library Access Gate — D3 freeze check (independent)

**Sandbox artifact, 2026-09-15. Phase: the gate on D3, before Mark's freeze.**
This is the same step Website V2's own D3 passed through (root `CLAUDE.md`,
"How we work"): the converged design, checked independently against the
charter, the constitution and the struggle record, reported back as a named,
itemized verdict. **It is not a ruling.** Only Mark's own word freezes D3.
Nothing in `Sandbox/D3-Converged-Design.md` was edited; every problem found
below is named here for Mark's fix pass, not fixed in place.

D3 was drafted by Fable. This check was run by a different agent against the
real checkout, re-measuring every load-bearing number rather than reading the
document's own arithmetic. Section 4 reports what was re-run and what came
back different. Where this check reproduces D3's figure exactly, it says so —
that is evidence, not politeness.

## 1. Fidelity to the nine rulings (Decision-Log entries 3–13)

### Q1 — scope: structure yes, judgment no (entry 3)

**Held.** §3 is the whole answer: seven named dependency items (CM-1…CM-7),
each split into "structure needed" and "judgment involved (corpus-map's)",
and the design builds exactly one of them — CM-6, the `fixture: true`
tolerance Q8 put in scope — plus the readers. No bucket row is assigned, no
`voice_of` value is chosen, no pair is ruled. The strongest evidence that the
split is real is §3's closing move: the worklist for CM-2/CM-3/CM-5 is
*regenerated by every `report` run* rather than written into D3, so this
workstream never becomes the place a classification decision is recorded.

Two notes, neither a drift:

- CM-1's proposed `row_id` shape is marked `[extrapolation]` and its
  "judgment involved" column says "none — mechanical." That is correct, and
  it is the one CM item this workstream could arguably build itself. D3
  leaves it with corpus-map anyway, which is the conservative reading of
  entry 3.
- §6.1 has this workstream *writing* `cic/corpus-map/fixture-synthetic.yaml`
  and a `PAIRS.yaml` skeleton. That is inside Q8's grant (entry 10), not a
  breach of Q1 — but see Required fix **RF-9**, because the mechanics of
  putting a new file into `cic/corpus-map/` are under-specified.

### Q2 — voicing: documented tradition-*pair* mutual awareness (entry 4)

**Implemented in the right shape, and widened in one respect Mark should be
asked about.**

The shape is right, and it matters that it is: entry 4 is the ruling Mark
reached *beyond* D1's three drafted options, and the easiest way for a
convergent document to drift would have been to quietly re-land on the role
tag. D3 does not. §1.4's `voicing-pair` resolves the *pair*
`(world census_id, row.voice_of)` against `PAIRS.yaml` and requires
`relation: mutual-awareness`; §2/I3 states plainly that this "is exactly the
mapping all five D1 directions got wrong"; §1.2's row D refuses the runtime
role cut on the explicit ground that "under Q2 the verdict is a pair
judgment, which is not a per-turn decision." Entry 4's structural
consequence — that the relationship is a property of a tradition *pair* and
lives in corpus-map — is carried through as CM-3, with entry 4's own two
worked examples (Donatists ↔ Optatus' Catholics = mutual-awareness;
Alexandria ↔ Eusebius' tradition = one-way) transcribed into the dependency
spec unchanged.

**The widening.** Entry 4 says each side's Representative "may voice **that
documented exchange**." D3's rule grants voicing to *everything* on any row
whose `voice_of` is a mutual-awareness partner — the whole of Optatus, not
only the parts where the argument is on the record. D3 half-notices this in
§8 item 2 (Cyprian, via the `antecedent` case) but presents it there as a
grain problem about role, not as what it also is: the pair rule, once true
for a pair, is true for every word of every work that pair's partner wrote.
That may well be the only implementable reading — a per-passage
"is this sentence part of the documented exchange" test is a judgment no
mechanism can make — but it is a real distance from the words of entry 4 and
Mark should confirm it rather than inherit it. Named as **RF-15**.

### Q3 — enforcement: staged rollout by waiver (entry 5)

**Implemented, and materially larger in scope than the ruling described.**

The mechanism is right and it is the best-argued part of D3. §4.1 builds
directly on D2 §1.3(e) — the finding that nothing blocks anything today —
and §4.2's four hygiene rules (unlisted → red; count exact in *both*
directions; deadline is a date not a comment; grandfathering closed) are a
faithful reading of `CLAUDE.md`'s "an unlisted defect is new drift and fails
the run; a stale waiver for something already fixed also fails — remove it."
The closure rule is the mechanical expression of entry 5's "new worlds must
pass it clean before admission going forward," and D3 states it that way.

Two things Mark ruled on a smaller object than the one he now gets:

- Entry 5 grandfathers "existing worlds with findings today (`don`'s 52,
  `syr`'s 1)" — 53 findings. D3's day-one waiver list is roughly **1,200**
  (§4.3 plus this check's corrections in §4 below). D3 is honest about the
  size in §8 item 3 and does not hide it. But entry 5 was a ruling about 53
  findings, and a freeze on D3 is a ruling about 1,200. That is worth
  re-stating at freeze rather than treating as already decided.
- The un-waivable half of the closure rule has a consequence D3 never
  states: see **RF-7**. It is the single most consequential unstated
  implication in the document.

### Q4 — unvendored sources: etic-only (entry 6)

**Implemented, with one tightening beyond the ruling.**

`emic-vendored-only` (§1.4) is exactly entry 6: an emic record may not cite a
source record whose material the library does not hold. §5's rule block is
clean, and §4.3's commentary is the right kind of honesty — "it is not a
design choice — it is Q4 applied."

The tightening: §5 adds `kind: unvendored ⇒ register: etic` on the *source
record itself*. Entry 6 governs which *citing* record may cite unvendored
material; it does not say the source record carrying the citation must be
etic. D3 notes the cost is tiny (2 records fleet-wide, verified below) and it
is a defensible reading, but it is an addition. Named as **RF-16** — a
one-line confirmation, not a redesign.

The real cost of Q4-as-implemented is bigger than §4.3 says, because the
measurement omits the `kind: absence` half of the check's own definition.
See **RF-2**.

### Q5 — absence records: dedicated kind + build-time-only read (entry 7)

## 3. Internal consistency — where the borrowed pieces do not meet

D3 claims in §1.2 to close the two seams D2 §4 named. It closes the A+B
precedence seam for *source records vs. quotes* convincingly. Three seams
remain, one of them structural.

**(a) `locus-within-work` reopens the A+B seam D3 says it closed.**
§1.2 states the design's own rule: "There is no pointer gate on quotes to
disagree with the byte gate… quotes are checked by bytes." Q7-B ran it that
way deliberately, "ignoring what the quote's own `sources[].source_id`
claims." But §1.4's `locus-within-work` reads: "the passage unit a
`verbatim-in-shelf` match landed in has a `locus` inside **the resolved
row's** `locus_ids`." There is no resolved row. The byte match returns a
*file and a passage unit*; the row is a property of the *source record's
pointer*, which this check path deliberately does not consult — and on a
shared compendium a single file carries many rows with different
`locus_ids`. Increment 8 cannot be built as specified without either
re-admitting the pointer (which re-opens the precedence question D3 says it
closed) or re-defining the check as "landed in *some* row of this file on
this shelf," which is a much weaker claim than I2. **RF-5.**

**(b) The day-one waiver table measures three different populations under
one waiver key.** §1.4 says `shelf-row` fires on "every `kind: vendored`
source record" — ~283 on day one, since no record carries `shelf_row` yet.
§8 item 3 repeats "~283 shelf-row." §4.3's table row labelled
`m9:shelf-row` sums to 116 and is described as "off-shelf at file grain."
Re-measured (§4 below), those are three genuinely different numbers over
three genuinely different populations: 283 vendored source records, 23 of
them off-shelf, and 116 *emic citations* to off-shelf files. Under §4.2's own
"count is exact → fewer is stale, more is new drift, both red" rule, a
waiver written from §4.3's table is red on its first run. **RF-1.**

**(c) Increment 7 depends on CM-1, which §7 says nothing waits on.**
§7 closes with "increment 8 is the only one that cannot ship until CM-4
exists." Increment 7 writes `shelf_row` "where the row exists (copied, never
guessed)" — and `shelf_row` is defined in §5 as the bucket row's own
`row_id`, which is CM-1. Until CM-1 lands, there is no `row_id` in any real
bucket to copy, and the no-guessing rule forbids inventing one. Increment 7's
`shelf_row` half is blocked on CM-1 exactly as increment 8 is blocked on
CM-4; only its `kind` half (mechanical from `edition`) can proceed. **RF-6.**

**(d) `voicing-pair` has no defined behaviour when there is no row at all.**
§1.4 enumerates the finding texts: "Missing `voice_of`, missing pair,
`one-way`, `none`, or `needs-ruling`." A source record that is
`kind: unvendored`, or vendored-but-off-shelf, resolves to no row, so none of
the five applies. Those citations would either fall silently through the
check (fail-open, which spec principle 12 forbids) or double-report against
`emic-vendored-only` / `shelf-row`. Stated, this is one sentence; unstated,
it is the difference between the check being sound and being half-specified.
**RF-14.**

**(e) Two `ACCEPTED_OPEN` registries, one discipline.** §4.2 says the new
waiver list "follows `engine/m1/cross_world.py`'s convention exactly." It
does not, in two ways verified below (key gains a `<layer>:` prefix; value
becomes a `Waiver` object where the existing one is `dict[str, str]`). That
is a fine design decision and a wrong sentence. The larger issue is that
`CLAUDE.md`'s gap-tracking rule speaks of *the* `ACCEPTED_OPEN` waiver list,
and after increment 4 the repo has two, in two modules, with two key formats
and no stated relationship. A defect waived in one is unlisted in the other.
**RF-10.**

**(f) §4.4's stated reason for leaving M3 alone is wrong** (the conclusion is
right). See §4 below. **RF-11.**

## 6. Verdict

### Ready to freeze with 16 required fixes and 4 rulings.

D3 is a genuinely converged design, not a menu with a preference. It
implements all nine of Mark's rulings in the shape he ruled them — including
Q2, the one that was hardest to implement and easiest to quietly revert to
the role tag, and which it does not revert. It builds on every one of D2's
corrections rather than around them, and it kills a component (the ratio
instrument) on the strength of a measurement rather than defending it. Its
§8 is the work of a document trying to be caught rather than to look
finished. Every load-bearing code claim this check re-verified came back
true, and the four largest day-one counts reproduce to the record.

What it is not yet is arithmetically safe to enforce. Three of the five
waiver classes in §4.3 are mis-scoped in ways that would turn CI red on the
first real run under §4.2's own rules — and because those rules cannot
distinguish "the grain changed" from "new drift," a wrong ceiling is not a
cosmetic problem. That is the substance of the required-fix list; most of the
rest is one-line.

**The fixes, each specifically.**

1. **RF-1 — Re-key §4.3's `m9:shelf-row` row.** Its 116 is the count of emic
   citations to off-shelf files; the `shelf-row` check emits ~283 findings on
   day one (§1.4, §8). Give the 116 its own row under whatever key actually
   reports it, or drop it to prose, and put 283 (per world) under
   `m9:shelf-row`.
2. **RF-2 — Correct `emic-vendored-only` to 166.** The 152 omits the
   `kind: absence` half of the check's own definition. `gallic`'s cell is 24,
   not 10. Name the 12 `gallic` emic records (list in §4.3 above) as a
   consequence of Q4+Q5 for the `gallic` thread.
3. **RF-3 — Stop calling `voicing-pair`'s 296 a ceiling.** It is a file-grain
   floor for a row-grain check; 111 further emic citations sit on files with
   both `tradition` and non-tradition rows. State the range (296–407) and
   state that increment 4 seeds `count` from the first row-grain run, not
   from §4.3.
4. **RF-4 — Two numeric corrections.** `gallic`→npnf203 is **56** citations
   across 55 records, not 52 (§3/CM-5, §8 item 3). The non-`source`
   `sources[]` population is **374**, or 373 if `search_record` targets are

    exactly" (the existing one is `dict[str, str]` keyed `<check>/<world>`),

16. **RF-16 — Flag the Q4 tightening.** `kind: unvendored ⇒ register: etic`
    on the *source record itself* goes beyond entry 6, which governs the
    citing record. Cheap (2 records) and defensible, but it is an addition
    and should be confirmed, not inherited.

**The four rulings Mark must make before increment 4 can land** (two are
D3's own; two are added here):

- **R-1 (D3 §8 item 1).** Does Q2 applied literally — `alx` unable to voice
  Dionysius via Eusebius, 37 emic citations — stand? D3's position is that

**What this check is not saying.** It is not saying the design is wrong. The
mechanism — computed shelf, bytes for quotes, a copied row key for sources, a
pair lookup for voicing, waivers with exact counts and dates, and a runtime
nobody touched — is coherent, buildable, and the right size. Sixteen fixes on
a document this long is a low rate, and thirteen of them are a paragraph
each. The three that are not (RF-3, RF-5, RF-7) are the ones worth Mark's
attention before he says the word.
