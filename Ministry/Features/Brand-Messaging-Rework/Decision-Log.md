## 2026-07-22 (same day, continued) — Theological deep-dive: a real principle worked out for "telling the story of what God is doing," now a standing Kit rule

**Started from Tier 1 item 1** (the elevator speech's "We measure qualitatively to
tell the story of what God is doing"). Rather than pick a rewording, Mark asked to
work the actual theology first: "there are passages in scripture that affirm counting
and telling the story and others that condemn counting. what are the principles we
pull from that."

**The texts weighed, in the order they came up:**
- **Condemned:** David's census (2 Samuel 24) — read as numbering Israel's strength to
  secure confidence in the count itself rather than in God, the same logic behind God
  shrinking Gideon's army in Judges 7 ("lest Israel boast... my own hand has saved
  me"). Matthew 6:1-4 — giving corrupted specifically by being performed for a human
  audience's credit, not by being visible as such. 1 Corinthians 3:6-7 — "I planted,
  Apollos watered, but God gave the growth... neither he who plants nor he who waters
  is anything" — Paul refuses personal credit, not the fact of growth being named.
- **Commanded:** Acts 2 — "about three thousand souls," "the Lord added to their
  number day by day" — a real count, stated plainly, credited to God, arising out of a
  description of ordinary faithful life (teaching, fellowship, prayer), not chased as
  a headline. The stewardship parables (the Talents) — real accounting is good, but
  it's the master's reckoning of the servant's faithfulness, not the servant's
  self-congratulation.
- **Added by Mark, and load-bearing:** Acts 14:27, 15:4, 15:12, 21:19-20 — Paul and
  Barnabas repeatedly "declared all that God had done" to the gathered church and the
  Jerusalem council, including "signs and wonders" offered as real testimony that
  informed the council's actual decision — and the result named each time is "they
  glorified God," not admiration of Paul. **John 4** (the Samaritan woman: "Come, see
  a man who told me all that I ever did") and **Mark 5:19** (the healed man,
  commissioned by Jesus: "tell them how much the Lord has done for you") — personal
  testimony carried outward by the person themselves as invitation, careful to point
  past itself to the hearer's own encounter (John 4:42: "we no longer believe just
  because of what you said").

**First-pass principle (confirmed by Mark as capturing it correctly):** the dividing
line was never counting or telling as such — it's whether credit, security, or glory
relocates from God to the human actor or institution, and for what audience. Reporting
faithfully to those responsible for discerning and stewarding the work together is the
Jerusalem-council pattern; claiming the same material as proof of institutional
effectiveness aimed at strangers is the David's-census pattern.

**Real correction from Mark, sharpening it further:** an early framing of this implied
CiC should never benefit from a told story — Mark named that as overreach. Paul's own
report to Jerusalem *practically benefited* his Gentile mission's legitimacy within the
wider church; benefit was never the corrupting thing. **The actual test is narrower and
cleaner: ask real permission, tell freely, and never let the credit move off God and
onto CiC.** Audience and benefit both turned out to be red herrings; attribution is the
whole test.

**FINAL — new standing Kit rule (Part 1.2, rule 11):** *"Report freely, credit
rightly. Real stories of what God is doing through the work — asked for with real
permission, never harvested by default — aren't something to hide or ration, and reach
isn't the test: a story can genuinely benefit CiC without that being a corruption. What
must never move is the attribution. However far a story travels, it has to keep
pointing at what God is doing, never at what CiC built, achieved, or accomplished. Ask
permission, tell freely, credit God — never ourselves."* Applied to the Kit document.

**Also resolved along the way:** the measurement line's "yours to own and share"
(finalized earlier today) needs no change — it already hands full ownership to the
participant, which already covers the John 4/Mark 5 invitational use case without
adding anything.

**Not yet applied:** the actual elevator-speech line that started this (5-min
version's qualitative/quantitative paragraph) — options being drafted next, per Mark's
own earlier instruction to avoid absolute "always/never" language and "say it
straight."

**Next action:** pick the actual replacement wording for the elevator speech line,
then return to the rest of the punch list (Tiers 1-4).

**FINAL, applied to the elevator speech (5-min):** *"Ask, and we'll gladly tell you
what God is doing here. What we track is whatever helps us steward it faithfully."*
Tier 1 item 1 is closed. The paragraph's trailing sentence ("There are numbers we
