## The immediate constraint

You said it plainly: you can't keep funding this personally if it grows. That's not a someday problem — build-phase costs (deep research, multi-round review across nine worlds) are the current driver, and that's happening now, not at some future launch. The question that can't wait for a full nonprofit setup: **what's the bridge?** Options worth putting in front of you directly:
- Informal support (friends/family/early believers in the vision) with no formal entity yet.
- A simple donation page under your own name/project, pre-entity.
- **Fiscal sponsorship** — operating under an existing 501(c)(3)'s umbrella so donors get a tax deduction now, without you forming and filing your own nonprofit first. Common for early-stage ministry projects; trades some autonomy for speed.

## Legal structure — the real decision, not urgent this week but shapes everything downstream

- **501(c)(3) nonprofit** — donor tax-deductibility, grant eligibility, but real setup cost/time (formation, board, bylaws, ongoing compliance) and loses some unilateral control (board governance).
- **For-profit (LLC/corp)** — faster to stand up, keeps you in full control, but no donor tax-deductibility and grants aimed at nonprofits/ministries are closed to you; revenue has to come from subscriptions/licensing instead of gifts.
- **Hybrid** — a nonprofit for the mission/formation-world content plus a for-profit arm for any commercial deployment (e.g., licensing to institutions) — more complex, sometimes worth it, sometimes not.
- **Fiscal sponsorship now, spin out later** — the bridge option above, deferring the full decision without stalling funding.

None of these needs deciding today. What's worth deciding soon is which of these you want researched in depth first, since the answer changes what "communication/branding" and "funding" work even mean.

## Cost model — needed before any funding ask is concrete

"AI time" isn't one number. At minimum this should separate:
- **Build-phase cost** — deep research and multi-round review across however many worlds are in active construction at once (this is the cost happening right now, parallel builds included).
- **Future end-user cost** — per-conversation cost once real participants exist, which scales with usage in a way build-phase cost doesn't.

A real budget model split this way is what turns "I can't keep funding this myself" into something a donor, grant committee, or board can actually respond to.

## Funding sources, mapped to phase (building on the existing funding doc's phase sketch)

- **Individual donors / early believers in the vision** — realistic now, pre-entity or via fiscal sponsorship.
- **Church/denominational partnerships** — realistic once there's something demonstrable (Alpha/Beta results).
- **Grants** (religious education foundations, tech-for-good/AI-for-good programs) — realistic once there's a legal entity (or fiscal sponsor) and a track record.
- **Earned revenue** (subscription, institutional licensing to churches/seminaries) — realistic at Phase 1 launch and beyond, per the existing funding doc's own framing.

Which of these you want to pursue first is a real open question, not something to default on.

## Branding / communications — what's still missing underneath the existing Vision/FAQ/Letter

- A public-facing identity (name treatment, site, visual identity) distinct from the internal governance voice of the Vision document.
- A **case for support** — a donor/partner-facing narrative, related to but not identical to the internal Vision doc, built for people encountering the project for the first time.
- A **trust/legitimacy narrative** specifically addressing the real reputational risk here: AI generating the voice of historical Christian figures and traditions is going to draw skepticism or pushback from some audiences before they've seen it work. The existing Five Convictions (especially Trustworthy Transparency and Encounter Over Persuasion) are the actual answer to that skepticism — the communications work is making that answer visible and credible to someone who hasn't read the governance documents.
