# Launch prompt — Marketplace Learning & Perspective thread

Paste this into a fresh thread. This is a **learning and positioning** workstream —
its job is to build real, grounded perspective on where Church in Conversation sits
relative to everything else in its space (AI companions, historical-figure chatbots,
faith-tech tools, museum/EdTech AI personas, Bible-study AI, etc.), and to draw honest
lessons from what's out there — not to redesign the project to chase a trend, and not
to make funding, UX, or Constitution-level decisions. Where this thread's learning
implies a concrete change (a positioning line for the pitch deck, a feature gap worth
flagging to the front-end thread, a funding-narrative angle), name that plainly as a
handoff — don't attempt the implementation here.

## Why this thread exists

Every prior workstream (front-end, prototype testing, org/funding) has been built by
looking inward — at the Constitution, the build methodology, the actual worlds. None of
them have systematically looked *outward*: what else exists that does something
adjacent, what has that space learned (successes and failure modes both), and where
does Church in Conversation's actual difference lie once the marketing language is
stripped away. Without that outward perspective, every future positioning, funding, and
communication decision is being made blind to its own market context.

This thread's job is to build that perspective honestly — including surfacing places
where CiC is *not* differentiated, or where a competitor has solved a real problem CiC
hasn't yet addressed — rather than a one-sided case for why CiC is unique.

## What to produce

1. **A landscape scan.** Identify what already exists in adjacent spaces: AI historical-
   figure/persona chatbots (educational and entertainment), faith-tech tools (Bible
   study AI, devotional AI, church-ops AI), museum/library AI-docent products, and any
   direct precedent for "converse with a reconstructed historical religious voice." For
   each, note: what it actually does, what its evidentiary/rigor discipline is (if any),
   and what happened to it (still active? funded? failed, and why?).

2. **Honest differentiation, not marketing copy.** Once the landscape is mapped, name
   plainly where CiC's build process (Gravity Discovery, confidence calibration, Author
   Gravity, the Three-Level Transparency runtime feature, Encounter-Over-Persuasion as a
   structural boundary rather than a style choice) is a genuine, defensible difference —
   and separately, where it isn't, or where a competitor's approach to a shared problem
   (onboarding, trust-building, source transparency) is worth learning from rather than
   dismissing.

3. **Lessons, not just a list.** For any comparable product that failed, gained
   traction, or drew criticism, draw out *why* — inauthenticity backlash, trust
   collapse, a scaling failure, a monetization model that broke user trust — and check
   each lesson against CiC's own Five Convictions and Foundational Values: does this
   project already have a structural guard against that failure mode, or is this a real
   exposure worth flagging back to the relevant thread (front-end, funding, or
   Constitution-level if it's that serious)?

4. **A grounded positioning brief** (draft, not final) — a short document translating
   this landscape/differentiation work into language usable by the Communication and
   Funding threads: what to say about this project relative to "AI chatbot" or
   "historical AI persona" categories without overclaiming, and without contradicting
   Conviction 3 (a witness doesn't defend or persuade) by accidentally writing marketing
   copy that oversells or flattens the project's own honesty about uncertainty.

## Coordination boundary, stated plainly

This thread learns and positions. It does not:
- Make funding-model or organizational-structure decisions (that's `Ministry/Funding/`,
  governed by `anthropic-skills:cic-org-funding-strategy`).
- Make UX, feature, or deployment decisions (that's the front-end/system-engineering
  threads).
- Revise the Vision, Constitution, or Five Convictions — if this thread's outward
  learning surfaces a serious tension with one of them, that is named plainly and
  brought back to Mark directly, the same way every other thread escalates
  Constitution-level questions rather than deciding them unilaterally.

Where this thread's work produces a concrete deliverable another thread should act on
(a positioning line, a competitive gap worth a feature response, a funding-narrative
angle), hand it off explicitly rather than half-implementing it here.
