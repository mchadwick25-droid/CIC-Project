## 1. The category problem, stated plainly

When a stranger hears about CiC, they will file it under one of four existing
categories, each carrying baggage CiC did not earn:

| They'll think… | That category now means… | The one-sentence exit |
|---|---|---|
| "An AI chatbot" | Confident fabrication; a bot built to always have an answer | "It's governed to prefer honest uncertainty over confident invention — it tells you plainly when the record is thin." *(existing packet language — keep)* |
| "An AI companion" | Lawsuits, teen harm, FTC inquiry, paywalled affection | "It's a doorway, not a home — its charter forbids designing for attachment, and it sends you back to living community." |
| "A historical-figure app" | Five-minute personas; sanitized Nazis; a whitewashed Anne Frank | "It doesn't impersonate famous individuals. It rebuilds a whole community from its own documents — and the build takes months of review-gated scholarship, not a prompt." |
| "A Bible/faith app" | Devotional content, subscriptions, growth marketing | "It's free to any seeker by charter, it will never try to convert you, and it's built like a scholarly edition, not a devotional feed." |

The lesson from the scan: **the difference is invisible at category level and only
appears when the process and the refusals are named.** Every positioning artifact
should do both in its first breath: what it is (reconstructed communities you can talk
with) and what it refuses (persuasion, engagement design, hidden uncertainty).

## 2. The four claims that survived verification

Usable as stated; the parenthetical is the evidence behind each.

1. **"No product we could find anywhere reconstructs a historical Christian
   *community* — rather than a famous individual — as a voice you can talk with."**
   (July 2026 scan across AI-persona, faith-tech, museum, and edtech spaces. Nearest
   analogues: Magisterium's Saint Chat = named saints + doctrine; Colonial
   Williamsburg = the analog version of our method; one vendor pilot of a period
   soldier voice.)

2. **"No product we could find shows you, mid-conversation, how confident the
   history behind each claim actually is."** (Citations exist — Magisterium AI does
   them well. Calibrated, participant-visible uncertainty does not exist anywhere we
   looked. The field's substitutes are deflection and topic-refusal, which is exactly
   how a classroom bot ended up whitewashing Anne Frank's death.)

3. **"The safeguards regulators began forcing on AI-companion apps in 2025–26 were in
   our charter before the laws existed — and ours go further."** (CA SB 243, NY's
   companion law, and EU AI Act Art. 50 now mandate AI-disclosure and crisis
   protocols; CiC's Constitution additionally forbids persuasion and
   engagement-maximization outright — which no law requires and no competitor was
   found to do. Compliance by conviction, not by statute.)

4. **"The build process is the product."** (Each world passes a review-gated
   pipeline — source ecology, source-reliability assessment, gravity discovery,
   confidence calibration, story-tier rules that forbid invention, adversarial
   testing — before anyone meets its Representative, and no world is called validated
   until an independent scholar in that specific field has reviewed it. The market's
   alternative is a persona generated from a prompt in minutes.)

## 3. What NOT to claim (overclaim register)

- **Not "the only" / "the first."** Say "no product we could find" — accurate,
  humble, and it invites the listener to check. Uniqueness claims also expire without
  notice: the composite-period-persona idea already exists in pilot form elsewhere.
- **Not "scholarly validated" — yet.** External review (Article 31) is a lead, not a
  completed fact. The elevator speeches' existing line — "until that review is done,
  we tell every participant exactly that" — is the right pattern; honesty about what
  isn't finished is part of the product. Positioning must never outrun this.
- **Not "it can't hallucinate."** Say what's true: it is locked to its world's
  sources, it lowers its confidence out loud, it is adversarially tested — and the
  institution stands behind every sentence it says (that's why the gates exist).
- **Not anti-competitor copy.** Naming failures (Anne Frank bot, Father Justin) as
  *lessons the field learned* is honest and useful; ridiculing named products is
  recruiting-by-contrast and beneath the project's own standard.
- **Not "better engagement."** CiC refuses engagement metrics on principle. Never
  borrow the market's success vocabulary (DAU, retention, session length) even
  favorably; the measure is Article 6's conditions, and the story language for that
  already exists ("we measure whether each Christian movement is represented in an
  engaging and accurate way. How it impacts the kingdom belongs to our Lord;
  that's bigger than us"). *[Quote updated 2026-07-18 to the current protected
  measurement line — see the approved Messaging & Branding Kit V0.2 and its
  decision log; the earlier "never its impact" and "honest and free" wordings are
  both retired.]*

## 4. Third-party proof points now available (from the scan)

For the Communication thread to deploy as evidence that this category of experience
is real and that CiC's disciplines matter:

- **Deus in Machina (Lucerne, 2024):** ~1,000 people talked with a source-bounded AI
  Jesus in a chapel; per the chapel's own theologian, over two-thirds reported a
  "spiritual experience." Also: its harshest critics had never used it — supporting
  the demo-first ask ("come sit at the Table, then tell me what you found").
- **The failure record as a foil:** the Anne Frank bot that wouldn't name who was
  responsible for her death; the Van Gogh bot retuned to deflect the suicide
  question; the Historical Figures app's remorseful Hitler. Each is a documented case
  of what happens *without* confidence calibration and tension-holding — the exact
  disciplines CiC treats as constitutional.
- **Father Justin (2024):** demoted from "AI priest" to lay theologian within ~48
  hours — evidence that role-boundary humility (witness, not clergy; doorway, not
  authority) is not caution for its own sake but the difference between trust and
  backlash.
- **YouVersion (500M+ installs, donor-funded, free):** existence proof for the
  funding thread that free-to-everyone, donor-sustained faith-tech works at the
  largest scale in the sector.

## 5. Draft positioning paragraph (to react to, not to keep)

For the funder/partner register — deliberately compatible with the existing "About"
narrative draft in the Telling the Story plan, adding the market context that
document doesn't carry:

> There are AI apps that let you text a famous saint, and AI companions built to keep
> you talking. Church in Conversation is neither. It rebuilds entire historical
> Christian communities — a first-century house church, the Syriac schools of the
> East — from their own surviving documents, through a months-long, review-gated
> scholarly process, and lets you sit down and talk with a voice from inside that
> world. It shows you its evidence and its confidence as you talk — no product we
> could find anywhere does that. Its written constitution forbids the two things the
> AI industry is being forced by regulators to curb: it will never optimize for your
> attention, and it will never try to convert you. It is a doorway, not a home — free
> to any seeker, funded by those who believe such doors should exist.
