# Colorado Public Benefit Corporation — Verified Legal & Tax Research

**V0.1 · 2026-07-19 · Companion research for `CiC_PBC_Structure_Analysis_V0_1.md`**

Legal/tax research, not legal or tax advice. A Colorado business attorney and CPA should confirm
anything load-bearing before acting on it. Every claim below is sourced; conflicts across sources
are flagged rather than silently resolved.

## 1. The statute, its history, and how "light" or "heavy" Colorado's version is

Colorado's public benefit corporation law lives inside the **Colorado Business Corporation Act**,
Title 7 of the Colorado Revised Statutes, **Article 101, Part 5 (§§ 7-101-501 through
7-101-509)** — not a separate freestanding statute book. ([Colorado Revised Statutes Title 7,
Article 101](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-7/corporations-continued/colorado-business-corporations/article-101/part-5/);
[C.R.S. § 7-101-503, FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-7-corporations-and-associations/co-rev-st-sect-7-101-503/))

Originated as the "Public Benefit Corporation Act of Colorado," enacted 2013 (session-law
Chapter 230), **effective April 1, 2014.** ([2013 session law, Ch. 230](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/2013a_sl_230.pdf);
[Colorado Public Benefit Corporations: Q&A, Fairfield and Woods](https://www.fwlaw.com/insights/colorado-public-benefit-corporations-qa))
One source cites the originating bill as HB 13-1138 — **unconfirmed against a primary
legislative-history source**, treat as probable but unverified.
([Law Office of E.C. Lewis](https://www.eclewis.com/colorado-benefit-corporations/))

Amended twice since:
- **HB17-1200** (effective Aug. 9, 2017): made "PBC" naming optional, clarified annual-report
  mechanics, extended eligibility to certain cooperatives.
  ([HB17-1200](http://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb17-1200))
- **SB22-045** (effective Aug. 10, 2022): lowered the shareholder-vote threshold to convert
  into/out of PBC status from two-thirds to a simple majority, eliminated dissenters'/appraisal
  rights on that conversion, clarified stock ownership alone isn't a director conflict of
  interest, tightened the enforcement-action standard. ([SB22-045](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb22-045))

**Colorado is a light-touch, Delaware-style jurisdiction.** No mandatory benefit director/officer,
no third-party audit or certification requirement — the company self-selects its own benchmark.
([Fairfield and Woods Q&A](https://www.fwlaw.com/insights/colorado-public-benefit-corporations-qa);
[C.R.S. § 7-101-506](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_7-101-506))

## 2. Formation requirements

**Naming:** including "PBC," "Pub. Ben. Corp.," "P.B.C.," or "public benefit corporation" in the
name is **optional** since the 2017 amendment. If omitted, the statute requires notice of PBC
status to anyone receiving issued stock (with an exception for federally registered securities).
([C.R.S. § 7-101-503](https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-7-corporations-and-associations/co-rev-st-sect-7-101-503/))

**Articles content:** must (a) state the entity is a public benefit corporation, (b) identify one
or more specific public benefits within the business-purpose statement.

**Custom purpose, broad statutory frame:** "public benefit" is defined expansively as "one or more
positive effects or reduction of negative effects on one or more categories of persons, entities,
communities, or interests other than shareholders … including effects of an artistic, charitable,
cultural, economic, educational, environmental, literary, medical, **religious**, scientific, or
technological nature." **"Religious" is explicitly enumerated** — a faith-mission purpose
statement fits squarely, not as a stretch. ([C.R.S. § 7-101-503](https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-7-corporations-and-associations/co-rev-st-sect-7-101-503/);
[Colorado SOS PBC FAQ](https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/business/FAQs/pbc.html))

Filed via the standard "Articles of Incorporation for a Profit Corporation" form with a PBC
supplement; **$50 filing fee.** ([Colorado SOS fee schedule](https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/info_center/fees/business.html);
[Sample PBC Articles form](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/business/sampleForms/ARTINC_PCPBC.pdf))

Converting an existing corporation into/out of PBC status now needs only a **majority
shareholder vote** (post-2022); dissenting shareholders have no appraisal rights on that specific
conversion. ([SB22-045](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb22-045))

## 3. Governance and accountability

**No mandated benefit director/officer.** The balancing duty sits on the **entire board**
(§7-101-506(1)): "The board of directors shall manage or direct the business and affairs of a
public benefit corporation in a manner that balances the pecuniary interests of the shareholders,
the best interests of those materially affected by the corporation's conduct, and the specific
public benefit identified in its articles of incorporation." Directors are protected if a decision
is "informed and disinterested and not such that no person of ordinary, sound judgment would
approve"; mere stock ownership isn't a conflict of interest (SB22-045).
([C.R.S. § 7-101-506](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_7-101-506))

**Annual benefit report** (§7-101-507): narrative of how the company promoted its stated public
benefit, obstacles encountered, rationale for its chosen third-party standard, plus an assessment
against that standard. **Third-party standard is self-selected** — only constraint is the company
didn't help create it and it defines its own criteria/weightings. **No audit or certification
required.** Goes to every shareholder and the company's public website (financial/proprietary
info may be redacted); a free copy on request if no website.
([C.R.S. § 7-101-507](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_7-101-507);
[Colorado SOS PBC FAQ](https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/business/FAQs/pbc.html))

> **Flagged conflict:** LegalClarity.org states the report is required "biennially" — this
> contradicts the statutory text itself, the Colorado SOS FAQ, the Fairfield and Woods Q&A, and
> the HB17-1200 summary, all of which say **annual**. Trust "annual."
> ([LegalClarity — flagged](https://legalclarity.org/establishing-and-managing-public-benefit-corporations-in-colorado/)
> vs. [C.R.S. § 7-101-507](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_7-101-507))

**Benefit enforcement proceeding — narrow standing.** §7-101-508: no enforcement action may be
brought unless the parties bringing it own, individually or collectively, **at least 2% of
outstanding shares** (or, for exchange-listed companies, the lesser of 2% or $2M market value).
Standing is limited to qualifying shareholders — **not** directors generally, employees,
customers, the public, or beneficiaries of the stated public benefit. **A single 100%-owner,
sole-director founder has, as a structural matter, no external party who can bring this kind of
action.** Accountability here is reputational/self-imposed via the public annual report, not a
real litigation threat, unless outside shareholders cross the 2% line.
([C.R.S. § 7-101-508](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_7-101-508);
[Fairfield and Woods Q&A](https://www.fwlaw.com/insights/colorado-public-benefit-corporations-qa))

## 4. Tax treatment

**Not tax-exempt** — a PBC is taxed exactly as any C-corporation, federal and state, unless it
elects otherwise. ([JWRH Law](https://www.jwrhlaw.com/blog/public-benefit-corporations))

**S-corp election:** available in principle (≤100 shareholders, individuals/certain trusts, U.S.
persons, one stock class) — a federal eligibility question the PBC statute doesn't restrict.
Colorado automatically recognizes a federal S-election; no separate state election; S-corps aren't
subject to Colorado corporate income tax (pass-through). Realistically available to a solo founder
with no outside institutional investors; disappears once a VC fund or non-individual investor
becomes a shareholder. ([Colorado Dept. of Revenue Corporate Income Tax Guide](https://tax.colorado.gov/corporate-income-tax-guide);
[S-Corp Election Colorado guide](https://electscorp.net/s-corp-election-colorado))

**No Colorado benefit-LLC** — that statutory form exists in only five states (Delaware, Maryland,
Oregon, Pennsylvania, Utah). A Colorado LLC can only *functionally* approximate it via a custom
operating agreement, or by foreign-qualifying an out-of-state benefit LLC.
([Pote Law Firm](https://www.potelawfirm.com/blog/2019/public-benefit-llcs/))

**Rough all-in corporate tax burden:** Colorado's flat corporate rate is currently **4.40%** (a
possible mid-year drop to 4.25% exists if state revenue triggers are met — confirm current rate
at filing time). Federal flat rate: **21%** (unchanged for 2026). Simple sum: **25.4%**; more
precise (state tax deductible against federal): **≈24.5%**. Both are back-of-envelope; actual
liability depends on apportionment, credits, and whether S-corp pass-through avoids the
entity-level layer entirely. ([Tax Foundation — Colorado](https://taxfoundation.org/location/colorado/);
[Corporate tax in the United States, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax_in_the_United_States))

## 5. PBC (legal status) vs. B Corp (private certification)

**Public Benefit Corporation** is a legal entity status created by state statute. **"B Corp" /
Certified B Corporation** is a private certification from B Lab (a nonprofit), available to *any*
legal structure — not itself a legal status. Certification requires passing the B Impact
Assessment with a minimum verified score of 80/200 across five areas (Governance, Workers,
Community, Environment, Customers), recertified roughly every two years.
([B Lab US & Canada](https://usca.bcorporation.net/benefit-corporation-vs-b-corp/);
[Hutchison Law](https://www.hutchlaw.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-a-public-benefit-corporation-and-a-b-corp-certification))

A Colorado PBC can also pursue B Corp certification — commonly paired (Patagonia, Allbirds, New
Belgium, Kickstarter, Hallow are all both). Roughly **$500/year for companies under $2M in
revenue**, plus assessment overhead, in exchange for partner discounts and market credibility that
mostly compounds at scale. At a very small stage, the legal PBC form alone likely delivers most of
the mission-lock value wanted, with certification as a later add-on.
([U.S. News](https://money.usnews.com/investing/articles/public-benefit-corporation-vs-b-corp-whats-the-difference);
[Hutchison Law](https://www.hutchlaw.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-a-public-benefit-corporation-and-a-b-corp-certification))

## 6. Real-world precedent

**Colorado-specific:** New Belgium Brewing (Fort Collins) converted to PBC status in **August
2018**, having waited for the statute's rough edges (per the 2017/2022 amendments) to be worked
out. ([Ashvegas](http://ashvegas.com/new-belgium-to-create-public-benefit-corporation-under-new-colorado-law);
[B The Change](https://bthechange.com/new-belgium-brewing-makes-legal-commitment-to-create-positive-impact-fff6cd29f6c5))
GoLite is cited by one source as a Colorado PBC example — **unverified, and likely inaccurate**
(GoLite wound down around 2014). No verified small Colorado faith- or education-adjacent PBC was
found in general search — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

**Faith-adjacent, directly on point:** **Hallow** explicitly considered and rejected 501(c)(3)
status (fundraising drag, limited pool, couldn't compete on compensation), then rejected a plain
C-corp (feared profit pressure eroding the mission), landing on PBC status "through prayer and
discussions with both start-up and spiritual advisors" — enabling VC fundraising and equity
compensation while making the faith mission a **legal**, not just cultural, obligation. Since
raised a $50M Series C and became the first religious app to reach Apple's top-10 chart —
demonstrating the structure doesn't cap growth. ([Hallow — Why We Made Hallow a Public-Benefit
Corp](https://hallow.com/blog/why-we-made-hallow-a-public-benefit-corp/);
[Homebrew.co](https://homebrew.co/blog/2023/05/18/hallow-raises-usd50-million-series-c-after-becoming-first-religious-app-to-reach-apple-top-10))

**Kickstarter** (converted to Delaware PBC, 2015): charter states specific, checkable commitments
(5% of after-tax profit to arts/education/inequality causes; refusing to lobby against its own
values even where profitable not to). More than 100 shareholders had to affirmatively vote away
their legal right to compel profit-maximization. **The transferable lesson: the charter's real
value is as a self-binding commitment device constraining future leadership** (successors, a
future acquirer) — not primarily external enforcement today, since a small/closely-held company
has little real enforcement exposure regardless (see Part 3 above).
([Kickstarter Magazine](https://medium.com/kickstarter/kickstarter-is-a-pbc-heres-what-that-means-and-why-it-matters-d90b2389ea6c);
[Kickstarter Charter](https://www.kickstarter.com/charter))

**Patagonia** (California's first benefit corporation, 2012) shows the structure's long-run
flexibility — Chouinard used it as connective tissue toward his 2022 transfer of ownership into a
purpose trust so the company could never be sold or taken public against its mission. PBC status
is a *floor*, not a ceiling; it doesn't itself replicate what Chouinard eventually did, but it
doesn't prevent later, more radical mission-protection either.
([KPCC](https://www.scpr.org/blogs/environment/2012/01/06/4190/patagonia-californias-first-benefit-corporation/);
[Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/09/15/patagonia-founder-yvon-chouinard-what-will-happen-to-his-company-now/))

**Allbirds** elected Delaware PBC status in **February 2016** — while still a small startup fresh
off a Kickstarter-funded prototype, well before any real scale, and years before its 2021 IPO
under the same structure. **Scale is not a precondition** — several of these companies chose PBC
status specifically while small, as a founding-stage decision.
([Allbirds S-1, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653909/000162828021017824/allbirdss-1.htm);
[The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/as-it-files-to-go-public-what-does-allbirds-status-as-a-public-benefit-corporation-really-mean/))
