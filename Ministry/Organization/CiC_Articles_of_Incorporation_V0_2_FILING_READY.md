## Article I — Name

The name of the corporation is **Church in Conversation**.

## Article II — Duration and Type

The corporation is a nonprofit corporation organized under the Colorado Revised
Nonprofit Corporation Act, C.R.S. § 7-121-101 *et seq.*, with perpetual duration. The
corporation shall have no members; governance is vested in the Board of Directors.

## Article III — Purpose

The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable, religious, and educational
purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or the
corresponding section of any future federal tax code. Its specific purpose is to help
people experience Jesus in new ways through engagement with the living traditions and
practices of His Church throughout history and today — including, without limitation:
reconstructing historical Christian communities from their primary sources with
scholarly rigor and published methodology; making those reconstructions available to the
public through honest, transparent, conversational encounter; supporting religious and
historical education in churches, schools, seminaries, and homes; and pursuing related
research, publication, and educational activity.

## Article IV — Foundational Commitments (Mission Lock)

The following commitments are constitutive of the corporation's charitable purpose, and
every exercise of corporate power is subject to them.

1. **Access.** The corporation's core encounter offering shall always be available free
   of charge to individual seekers. Fees, licenses, and tiers may fund that access; they
   shall never replace it.
2. **Witness, not recruitment.** The corporation's programs shall not be designed to
   persuade participants toward any tradition, position, or outcome. The corporation
   shall not measure, target, or report religious conversion as an outcome of its work.
3. **Transparency never gated.** The evidentiary transparency of the corporation's
   reconstructions (sources, confidence, limitations) shall be available to every
   participant at every access tier, without exception.
4. **No engagement optimization.** No program, product, or system of the corporation
   shall be designed or configured to maximize participant engagement duration, return
   frequency, or emotional dependency as an objective.
5. **Independent accountability.** The corporation's historical reconstructions shall be
   subject to independent external scholarly review, honestly disclosed, and never
   represented as validated on internal review alone.
6. **Money buys access, never voice.** No gift, grant, or sponsorship shall determine,
   alter, or soften the content or testimony of the corporation's reconstructions. The
   Board shall maintain a gift-acceptance policy implementing this commitment.

**Amendment protection.** This Article IV and Article VIII may be amended only by the
affirmative vote of at least four-fifths (4/5) of all directors then in office, including
a majority of the independent directors.

## Article V — Limitations (IRS-Required Language)

No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be
distributable to, its directors, officers, or other private persons, except that the
corporation is authorized to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to
make payments and distributions in furtherance of its purposes. No substantial part of
the corporation's activities shall consist of carrying on propaganda or otherwise
attempting to influence legislation, and the corporation shall not participate or
intervene in any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for
public office. Notwithstanding any other provision of these Articles, the corporation
shall not carry on any activities not permitted (a) by a corporation exempt from federal
income tax under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or (b) by a corporation
to which contributions are deductible under Section 170(c)(2) of the Internal Revenue
Code.

## Article VI — Directors

The number, terms, election, and qualifications of directors shall be as set forth in the
bylaws, provided that the Board shall consist of no fewer than five (5) directors, a
majority of whom shall at all times be independent — neither related by family (within
the meaning applied by the Internal Revenue Service for Form 990 reporting) to, nor in a
compensated or business relationship with, the founder or any officer. The initial Board
of Directors shall be seated by the incorporator's organizational action following
incorporation.

Upon dissolution of the corporation, after payment of its liabilities, all remaining
assets shall be distributed to one or more organizations exempt under Section 501(c)(3)
of the Internal Revenue Code whose purposes are consistent with Article III and, to the
extent practicable, with the Foundational Commitments of Article IV — or to the federal,
state, or local government for a public purpose. No assets shall be distributed to any
director, officer, or private person.
