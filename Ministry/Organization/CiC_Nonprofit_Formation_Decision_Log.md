## 2026-07-15 — Formation workstream launched; roadmap and 1023-EZ-vs-1023 eligibility read produced

**Inherited (from the funding log, 2026-07-15, "STRUCTURE DECIDED"):** CiC will be an earned-revenue nonprofit. Facts verified against current published sources 2026-07-15 (Colorado SOS fee schedule, IRS 1023/1023-EZ pages and instructions, Colorado Charitable Solicitations Act); each figure labeled. **This is exploration with real stakes, not legal advice — the items that need the attorney consult already budgeted in the packet ($500–1,500) are flagged explicitly in the roadmap and below.**

**The eligibility read (the recommendation with the most riding on it):**

- Form 1023-EZ ($275, weeks) requires *attesting* that projected annual gross receipts will not exceed $50,000 in **each** of the next three years. Gross receipts means everything — gifts, grants, and earned revenue combined.
- The project's own planning documents fail that test: the Ministry Proposal Packet's midpoint planning scenario is **$55.1K in Phase 1 Year 1** (growing to ~$105K by Year 3), and its Track A midpoint funding target is $55K. Only the minimum scenario ($18.1K → $28K) stays under the line. A single Praxis grant ($50K unrestricted) or any Lilly-scale money crosses it instantly.
- **Recommendation: file the full Form 1023 ($600, ~3–6 months).** Three reasons, one of them the heart-level one: (1) *Integrity* — CiC cannot circulate a funding packet whose planning figure is $55K while attesting to the IRS that it projects under $50K. Trustworthy Transparency applies to government forms, not just conversations at the Table. This is the project's own conviction applied to its own paperwork. (2) *Credibility* — the full 1023's narrative and budget become a reusable credibility artifact for funders and DAF sponsors; the Proposal Packet has already written most of it. (3) *Timeline absorbs it* — filed January 2027, determination lands ~April–August 2027, inside the window everything else needs anyway.

**Timeline recommendation (refines "by Q1 2027," does not change it):** incorporate **December 2026 – mid-January 2027**, not late Q1. Reason: filing the 1023 in January gives the determination letter its 3–6 months to arrive around the Praxis application window (opens April 2027) and before serious Ring 2 fundraising. Retroactivity makes early incorporation cheap: exemption filed within 27 months of formation is retroactive to the incorporation date, so nothing is lost and several months are gained. Costs of incorporating early are trivial ($25/yr periodic report; 990-N e-postcard while receipts are small).

**Interim mechanics position (carried from funding log, now sequenced):** the church-designated fund is the giving channel from now until the *determination letter arrives* — not merely until incorporation. Gifts to the corporation post-incorporation but pre-determination become retroactively deductible only if approval comes; the church fund carries zero donor risk, so it stays primary until the letter is in hand. At determination: direct receipting begins, the church grants over any remaining designated balance (wind-down flagged for the attorney consult), NCF/DAF gifts become possible, and the nonprofit cloud-credit programs (AWS/Google/Microsoft) open.

**Formation budget (verified figures):** CO Articles of Incorporation $50 (online only) · EIN $0 · CO charitable solicitation registration $10 initial + annual renewal (required before soliciting in CO; register at incorporation regardless of the <$25K exemption since Year 1 targets exceed it) · CO periodic report $25/yr · Form 1023 $600 · attorney consult $500–1,500 (already budgeted in the packet) · D&O insurance ~$600–1,200/yr (quote when board is seated) · registered agent $0 if Mark serves (requires a physical Colorado street address) or ~$100–150/yr for a service. **Bare filings ≈ $660–685; full first-year formation cost including attorney and D&O ≈ $1,800–3,400.**

**Flagged for the budgeted attorney consult (not DIY items):**

Articles/bylaws lock language — putting access-for-all and never-gated transparency into the *articles of incorporation* (hardest to amend), with supermajority amendment protection on the mission provisions; honest note recorded: no governing document is un-amendable — the durable lock is articles language + supermajority + board composition together.
2. IP assignment — the constitution, worlds, code, and methodology currently belong to Mark personally; transferring them to the nonprofit (and how the $750 personal spend is characterized — gift vs. reimbursable) is real legal work.
3. Founder-compensation procedure — the board-approved-visible-line rule, drafted to fit the IRC 4958 rebuttable-presumption safe harbor (disinterested directors, comparability data, contemporaneous documentation).
4. Church-designated-fund wind-down terms — the church must hold genuine discretion (variance power) while the fund operates; the transfer at determination should be clean.
