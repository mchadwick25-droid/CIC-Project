## Part 0 — The one decision to make before you file: now vs. ~December

The formation thread's considered recommendation was **reserve the name now, incorporate
~December 2026.** You've said you'd rather file now. Here's the honest read so you choose
with eyes open — this is a real fork, not a formality.

**The case for filing now (it's stronger than it first looks):**
- You're raising warm-network gifts **right now.** Once you're incorporated, every gift given
  *after* the incorporation date becomes **retroactively tax-deductible** the moment your
  501(c)(3) is approved (the "27-month rule"). Incorporating now stretches that retroactive
  umbrella back over the money you're raising this fall.
- The entity existing lets you open a bank account, hold the name for real (no
  reservation-renewal dance), and start moving IP/domain into the nonprofit.
- Your own standing **speed directive** (2026-07-16) and the fact that **Prototype 1 is
  launching now** dissolve the original "don't rush formation before P1" caution.
- Early-incorporation cost is trivial — your formation log already priced it: **~$25/yr
  periodic report + a 990-N e-postcard** while receipts are low.

**The case for waiting** was mostly about not incorporating before Prototype 1 and before
board cultivation. But Colorado does **not** require you to name directors in the Articles,
so you can incorporate as sole incorporator now and seat the 5-person board afterward — the
board isn't a blocker. That argument has largely expired.

**Recommendation: filing now is a sound choice — with one gate.** The only place where
"file now, fix later" is genuinely expensive is **Article IV (the mission-lock supermajority
amendment protection)**. Its whole purpose is to be *hard to amend* — so if it's drafted in a
way that isn't enforceable under Colorado law, you can't cleanly fix it later without
defeating the point. That provision is exactly what the budgeted **$500 attorney review**
(already in the packet) is for. So "file now" really means: **quick attorney pass on the
Articles → file within a week or two.** Everything else in this package you can prepare today.

**→ Your call. Everything below is ready regardless of which date you pick.**

## Part 2 — Pre-filing checklist (do these first, ~30 minutes total)

**☑ 1. Name — already reserved. No action needed; skip the availability check.**
Mark reserved **"Church in Conversation"** with the CO SOS (~early July 2026). A Colorado name
reservation holds the name for **120 days**, so it's valid into roughly **late October / early
November 2026** — ample runway to incorporate now.
- **There is no separate "convert the reservation" step.** You simply file the Articles
  (Part 3) and type the exact reserved name into the name field; because it's reserved *to
  you*, it clears the availability check and you proceed straight into the Articles.
- **Two things to make it clean:** (a) file the Articles while **signed in under the same CO
  SOS account / reserving party** you used to reserve the name, so the system recognizes it as
  yours rather than blocking it as taken; (b) keep your **reservation confirmation number** on
  hand in case the form asks for it.
- If incorporation slipped past the 120-day mark you'd re-file the reservation ($25) — not a
  concern at this timing.
- *(Trademark note, unchanged and non-blocking: state name-availability ≠ trademark clearance;
  a real clearance is an attorney item you can handle later.)*

**☐ 2. Decide your registered agent.** The registered agent receives legal/official mail and
must have a **physical Colorado street address** (no PO boxes) available during business hours.
- **Option A — you serve (free):** your CO street address goes on the public record.
- **Option B — a registered-agent service (~$100–150/yr):** keeps your home address private,
  never misses service, forwards mail. Common for solo founders who work from home.
- *Recommendation:* if privacy of your home address matters to you at all, Option B is worth
  the ~$120/yr. If not, Option A is free and fine.

**☐ 3. Have a credit/debit card ready** — the $50 fee is paid online at submission.

**☐ 4. Prepare the "Additional Provisions" PDF** (Part 4 below) — this is the step DIY filers
most often miss. Save Part 4 as a PDF before you start; you'll upload it during filing.

**☐ 5. (Recommended) Attorney pass on the Articles** — per Part 0, especially Article IV.

Colorado filing is **online only** (no paper accepted). Start here:
**File a form → Business → Form a new Business/Nonprofit:** <https://www.coloradosos.gov/biz/FileDoc.do>
Choose **"Articles of Incorporation for a Nonprofit Corporation."**

| Form field | What to enter |
|------------|---------------|
| **Entity name** | `Church in Conversation` — your **reserved** name; enter it exactly, signed in under the reserving account (see Part 2, item 1) |
| **Principal office — street address** | *[your CiC street address]* |
| **Principal office — mailing address** | Same as street, unless different |
| **Registered agent — name** | *You (Mark Chadwick)* **or** *[the service's name]* |
| **Registered agent — street address (CO)** | *[CO street address]* — physical, no PO box |
| **Registered agent — mailing address** | Same, or the service's |
| **Registered agent consent** | The form affirms the agent consents by this filing — confirm the box |
| **Individual causing the document to be delivered (incorporator)** | `Chadwick, Mark` + *[your mailing address]* |
| **Additional individuals?** | **No** (you're the sole incorporator) |
| **Additional provisions / additional information included?** | **YES** — then upload the Part 4 PDF |

Then: review → enter your name as the filer to sign → pay **$50** → submit. **Approval and the
stamped Articles are immediate** — you can download the filed document and your record shows
the entity as active the same minute.

> **Why the Part 4 attachment matters:** Colorado's default online Articles are bare-bones —
> they collect name, address, and agent, but they do **not** include the IRS-required
> 501(c)(3) language (purpose limitation, no-private-inurement, dissolution clause). Without
> that language, the IRS will reject your Form 1023 later. Marking **"additional provisions:
> YES"** and uploading Part 4 is how that required language gets into your Articles now, so the
> federal step goes smoothly later.

**ADDITIONAL PROVISIONS TO THE ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION OF CHURCH IN CONVERSATION**

**Purpose.** The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable, religious, and
educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or
the corresponding section of any future federal tax code. Its specific purpose is to help
people experience Jesus in new ways through engagement with the living traditions and
practices of His Church throughout history and today — including, without limitation:
reconstructing historical Christian communities from their primary sources with scholarly
rigor and published methodology; making those reconstructions available to the public through
honest, transparent, conversational encounter; supporting religious and historical education
in churches, schools, seminaries, and homes; and pursuing related research, publication, and
educational activity.

**Foundational Commitments (Mission Lock).** The following commitments are constitutive of the
corporation's charitable purpose, and every exercise of corporate power is subject to them.
- *Access.* The corporation's core encounter offering shall always be available free of charge
  to individual seekers. Fees, licenses, and tiers may fund that access; they shall never
  replace it.
- *Witness, not recruitment.* The corporation's programs shall not be designed to persuade
  participants toward any tradition, position, or outcome. The corporation shall not measure,
  target, or report religious conversion as an outcome of its work.
- *Transparency never gated.* The evidentiary transparency of the corporation's reconstructions
  (sources, confidence, limitations) shall be available to every participant at every access
  tier, without exception.
- *No engagement optimization.* No program, product, or system of the corporation shall be
  designed or configured to maximize participant engagement duration, return frequency, or
  emotional dependency as an objective.
- *Independent accountability.* The corporation's historical reconstructions shall be subject
  to independent external scholarly review, honestly disclosed, and never represented as
  validated on internal review alone.
- *Money buys access, never voice.* No gift, grant, or sponsorship shall determine, alter, or
  soften the content or testimony of the corporation's reconstructions. The Board shall
  maintain a gift-acceptance policy implementing this commitment.
- *Amendment protection.* This Foundational Commitments section and the Dissolution provision
  below may be amended only by the affirmative vote of at least four-fifths (4/5) of all
  directors then in office, including a majority of the independent directors.

**Limitations (IRS-required).** No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to
the benefit of, or be distributable to, its directors, officers, or other private persons,
except that the corporation is authorized to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered
and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of its purposes. No substantial part of
the corporation's activities shall consist of carrying on propaganda or otherwise attempting
to influence legislation, and the corporation shall not participate or intervene in any
political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office.
Notwithstanding any other provision of these Articles, the corporation shall not carry on any
activities not permitted (a) by a corporation exempt from federal income tax under Section
501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or (b) by a corporation to which contributions are
deductible under Section 170(c)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code.

**Directors.** The corporation shall have no members; governance is vested in the Board of
Directors. The Board shall consist of no fewer than five (5) directors, a majority of whom
shall at all times be independent — neither related by family (within the meaning applied by
the Internal Revenue Service for Form 990 reporting) to, nor in a compensated or business
relationship with, the founder or any officer. The number, terms, election, and qualifications
of directors shall otherwise be as set forth in the bylaws.

**Dissolution.** Upon dissolution of the corporation, after payment of its liabilities, all
remaining assets shall be distributed to one or more organizations exempt under Section
501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code whose purposes are consistent with the Purpose and, to
the extent practicable, with the Foundational Commitments of these Articles — or to the
federal, state, or local government for a public purpose. No assets shall be distributed to
any director, officer, or private person.

## Part 5 — Immediately after filing (same day / same week)

**☐ 1. Download and save the stamped Articles** — the filing confirmation and the file-stamped
PDF. This is proof of existence you'll need for the bank, the EIN, and the 1023.

**☐ 2. Get an EIN** — free, online, ~10 minutes, issued instantly:
<https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online>
Choose "View Additional Types… → Church-controlled org / Other Nonprofit." You need the EIN
before opening a bank account. *(Not legal/tax advice — but this step is routine.)*

**☐ 3. Calendar the annual Periodic Report** — Colorado emails a reminder; it's ~$25/yr, filed
online in the same system. Missing it eventually leads to administrative dissolution, so put a
recurring reminder on the calendar now.

**☐ 4. Colorado Charitable Solicitations (CCSA) registration** — required *before* soliciting
publicly in Colorado (~$10 initial). Your Year-1 targets exceed the <$25K exemption, so plan to
register — but this pairs with the *public* fundraising flip (~November per the Acceleration
Plan), not necessarily filing day. Don't let it block incorporation.

**☐ 5. Hand off to the formation thread** — bylaws adoption, first board meeting/organizational
resolutions, the 5-seat board, and the Form 1023 all live there. Incorporation is the trigger
that starts those.

## Part 6 — Honest flags (what a lawyer's 30 minutes is actually for)

1. **Article IV mission-lock enforceability (the important one).** The 4/5-supermajority-
   including-a-majority-of-independents amendment lock is unusual and is the provision you most
   want correct *on the first filing* (its value is being hard to change). Confirm the mechanism
   is enforceable under the Colorado Revised Nonprofit Corporation Act before you file.
2. **Registered-agent address privacy** — decided in Part 2; just be aware whatever you use is
   public.
3. **Name/trademark** — state name-availability ≠ trademark clearance. Fine to file the state
   entity on availability alone; the trademark question can follow.
4. **These are drafts, not legal advice.** This package and the underlying Articles were
   prepared by the project, not an attorney. The ~$500 consult already in your budget is the
   right and sufficient safeguard.
