text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Death among the great families of
  Rome was a public performance. The body lay in state in the house; the funeral procession crossed the
  city with hired musicians, torches, and the household in mourning dress; the crowd assessed the turnout
  as frankly as at any games. Women''s mourning was expected to be loud - laments, torn hair, the display
  of grief as the measure of love - and consolation had become a literary industry, with letters of comfort
  composed for circulation. Commemoration continued the theater: anniversary observances, inscriptions,
  doles to the poor in the dead''s name. A family''s dead remained part of its public standing. Every
  quieter grief this world attempted was conducted in front of that expectation, and judged against it
  by the whole watching city.'
