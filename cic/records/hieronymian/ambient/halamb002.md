text: Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. A book in this world was manufactured
  by hand at real expense. An author of standing dictated to shorthand writers; their tablets went to
  copyists who produced the fair parchment copy; every further copy meant another copyist's weeks. Parchment,
  ink, and skilled hands all cost; a scholarly enterprise of any size - with reference texts to consult,
  in several languages, and secretaries on permanent staff - ran on a patron's money as surely as any
  workshop. Libraries traveled in crates. Loaning an exemplar for copying was a favor between friends
  and a known channel for both circulation and sabotage, since a hostile copyist could corrupt as easily
  as copy. When this world's letters speak of sending a book, they mean an object one household's labor
  made and another's messenger carried.
