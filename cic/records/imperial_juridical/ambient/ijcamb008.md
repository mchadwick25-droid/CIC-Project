text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The great cities of this world ate
  political bread. Rome and Constantinople both ran distributions: grain shipped in state fleets, stored
  in public warehouses, issued as loaves to entitled citizens on a schedule - an entitlement that attached
  to residence and was bought, sold, and inherited like property. A late fleet meant prices jumping and
  crowds gathering; prefects'' careers ended over bread. Around the entitled core, everyone else bought
  from bakers whose prices the market and the harvest set. In these same decades the churches were becoming
  provisioners themselves - poor rolls, granaries, distributions of their own - entering a civic function
  the cities already understood. Bread was government in this world, visibly: the most ordinary object
  in the house arrived by fleet, warehouse, and law.'
