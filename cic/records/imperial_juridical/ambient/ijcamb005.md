text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. When the emperor summoned a council,
  the roads filled with bishops. The imperial post - relays of horses and lodgings normally reserved for
  state business - was opened to them; old men who had never left their provinces crossed the empire at
  government expense, each with secretaries, deacons, and baggage. A great council assembled hundreds
  of bishops in one provincial city for months: lodgings found, markets strained, stenographers recording
  sessions, couriers running drafts to the court. Locals watched the church arrive as a traffic phenomenon.
  The bishops themselves learned the empire bodily - its distances, its inns, its harbor delays - and
  a see''s real weight could be read in how its man traveled and who met him at each stage. Doctrine moved
  at the speed of the imperial roads.'
