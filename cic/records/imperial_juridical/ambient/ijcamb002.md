text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Money carried the new order into every
  hand - slowly, then boldly. Under Constantine the sign the emperor had adopted appeared on the coinage
  only here and there, small marks on a few issues; within a generation it grew bold - wreathed, filling
  whole reverses, set on the helmet - and the coins went where coins go: wages, taxes, market stalls,
  dice games, the poor-box. A money-changer sorted it by weight without a thought. Older coins with older
  gods stayed in circulation beside it for decades, so a single purse could hold Jupiter, the deified
  emperors, and the chi-rho together - which was, in miniature, the empire''s own condition. Coinage was
  the one image everyone touched. What the state stamped on it was the quietest, most constant announcement
  of what the state had become.'
