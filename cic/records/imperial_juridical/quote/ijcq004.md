Added 2026-08-15 at Mark's explicit request ("deepen imperial_juridical, add a quote from Chalcedon"),
using the newly-vendored NPNF2-14 (Seven Ecumenical Councils). This is imperial_juridical's first Leo
quote (ijcfig007), despite Leo already owning a full story (ijcstory006, "The Tome That Would Not
Bend") - that gap is closed here. Wording transcribed directly from
cic/texts/npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml, div2 id="xi.vii" ("The Tome of St. Leo"), paragraph
id="xi.vii-p7" - the Tome's own Christological core, matching ijcfig007's bridge_line almost exactly
("His letter on Christ's two natures was read at Chalcedon").

Deliberately quotes the Tome's TEXT rather than the bishops' famous acclamation on hearing it read
("Peter has spoken thus through Leo," div2 xi.viii-p4, confirmed present in the same Acts, immediately
following this passage) - that acclamation is collective, spoken by "the most reverend bishops" as a
body, and this world's figure registry (ijcfig001-009) has no composite/collective figure it could be
correctly attributed to. Attributing it to Leo himself would misattribute another party's words to him,
the same category of error the Ambrose quote (ijcq002) caught by a different route. Creating a new
composite figure was judged out of scope for "add a quote" and left for a future decision.

Locus and provenance were chosen deliberately: this is the Tome's text specifically AS IT SITS IN THE
ACTS OF CHALCEDON (div1 id="xi", confirmed by walking element ancestry before use), immediately
preceding the bishops' acclamation in the same Acts - not a citation to a general Leo letters
collection. TRANSLATOR verified at the passage itself, not assumed from the volume's general editor
(Percival): the text carries its own inline attribution, "The translation here given is that of Rev.
Wm. Bright, D.D. ... London, 1886" - a different translator than Percival's own editorial voice, the
same per-passage check that caught srcIJC42's and srcIJC45's translator splits. srcIJC46 (new row) is
scoped tightly to this passage; srcIJC12 (the world's existing general Tome-to-Flavian primary-source
row) is left untouched.
