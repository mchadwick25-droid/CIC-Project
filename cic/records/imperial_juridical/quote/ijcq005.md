Added 2026-08-15 at Mark's explicit request ("add a quote from Letter CVI"), following the same-day
locus search that identified this letter (div3 id="ii.iv.ci" in
cic/texts/npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml) as the strongest of Leo's three-letter dispatch of 22 May
452 rejecting Canon 28. This is imperial_juridical's second Leo quote (after ijcq004, the Tome's
Christological core) - a different register entirely: not Christology but Leo's own constitutional
argument for why a council vote cannot alter Nicaea's fixed order. Directly continues ijcstory006 ("The
Tome That Would Not Bend"), whose own text narrates this exact refusal ("rank secured by nearness to a
throne is not the same kind of claim as rank secured by an apostle's own grave, and a council cannot
make the two the same claim by voting") without previously having a verbatim key_line to ground it.

Wording transcribed directly (tail-aware ElementTree walker, paragraph id="ii.iv.ci-p15") from the
letter's own core argument: the Nicene canons bind "till the end of the world," and anything a later
council does against them is void from the outset - not merely disputed, but "ipso facto null and
void." Selected over the letter's more personally-addressed lines ("I grieve, beloved, that you have
fallen into this too...") because this sentence stands on its own without needing Anatolius's specific
conduct as context, and states Leo's general principle rather than a personal rebuke - the load-bearing
claim ijcstory006 and ijcfig007's own attribution_note both turn on.
