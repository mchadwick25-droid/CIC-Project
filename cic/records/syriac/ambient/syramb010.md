text: >-
  Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. No one in these towns lived only among
  their own. The old gods kept their temples and their feast days: Nebo and Bel had their worship in
  Edessa, the planets their devotees, and the fish pools below the citadel their reverence. The Jewish
  communities of Mesopotamia were old, large, and settled - synagogue, school, and trade standing in
  the same streets, speaking a sister Aramaic, reading many of the same Scriptures. And the followers
  of other Christian teachers - of Marcion, of Bardaisan, later of Mani - met in their own gatherings,
  sometimes larger than any other in a given town. A market day mixed all of them. Neighbors of
  different allegiances bought from the same stalls, drew the same water, buried their dead in the
  same hills, and knew one another's festivals by the noise and the smoke. Whatever any community
  taught inside its own walls, the street itself belonged to everyone and to no one.
