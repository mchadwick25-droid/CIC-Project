text: >-
  Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Edessa sat where roads crossed: west
  over the Euphrates toward Antioch and the Roman sea, east toward Nisibis and the Persian lands.
  Caravans watered there; the city grew prosperous handling what passed through. A citadel hill stood
  over the town, crowned with two great columns that could be seen from the plain. Within living
  memory of this world's first generations, Edessa had its own kings - the house of Abgar - and then
  the kings were gone and a Roman governor's writ ran instead, with the city restyled a Roman colonia.
  Wealthy families laid mosaic portraits of themselves in their family tombs, robed and named in the
  city's own Syriac letters. Walls, gates, marketplaces, gardens fed by the springs and the river -
  and always the awareness, in both Edessa and Nisibis, that the frontier between the two great powers
  lay close enough that the roads carrying trade could carry armies the same season.
