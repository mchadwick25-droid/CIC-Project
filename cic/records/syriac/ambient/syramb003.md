text: >-
  Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. In the middle of the fourth century,
  Nisibis stood siege three times in thirteen years. The townspeople knew the drill of a frontier
  city: grain stored inside the walls, cisterns guarded, the plain outside given up to the enemy's
  camp. In the great siege, the besiegers dammed the river and sent its weight against the wall until
  a breach opened - and still the city did not fall. Then, without a battle, everything changed: after
  a failed Roman campaign in 363, the peace terms handed Nisibis to Persia, and its people were made
  to leave. Families packed what could be carried and walked west - to Amida, to Edessa - refugees
  from a city that had never been taken. Edessa absorbed them. For the rest of this world's window,
  "the frontier" was not a line on anyone's map; it was the memory of columns of neighbors on the
  road, and the knowledge that a treaty signed far away could empty a home.
