text: >-
  Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The towns of this world ate from the
  land around them: rain-fed grain from the plain, bread as the meal's center, gardens and orchards
  watered by spring and river, oil and wine coming in along the roads. That dependence had an edge.
  When the winter rains failed, the countryside emptied into the cities, and the cities discovered how
  thin the margin was. In one remembered famine at Edessa, late in this world's window, those with
  grain held it back while its price climbed, and the poor died in the open - in the streets and under
  the public porticoes where they sheltered. Relief, when it came, was organized the way everything
  civic was organized: beds set up, distributions made, the dead buried at common expense. A good year
  erased none of the memory. Everyone in these towns, whatever their allegiance, knew that bread was
  the daily question and that a failed sky could turn a city inside out in a season.
