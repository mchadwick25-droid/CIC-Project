text: Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The food of the desert was bread -
  flat dried loaves, baked hard so they would keep for months, softened in water to be eaten. With it,
  salt; water from the well or carried in; sometimes lentils or a little oil; green things rarely; wine
  known but watched. Loaves came from the valley - bought at market, earned by handwork, or baked in batches
  at the bigger settlements - and a man stored his supply in the cell and counted it. Two loaves a day
  was ordinary; the old and the sick were fed more and told to eat. When a guest came, the pot went on
  and the rule of the day bent to the meal, because a traveler had crossed desert to arrive. Hunger was
  never mysterious here. Everyone knew exactly what bread cost and how far a sack of it had walked.
