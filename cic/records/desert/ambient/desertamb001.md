text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. A cell was a built thing: mud brick,
  usually two or three small rooms off a little walled courtyard, a niche for prayer, a mat, a lamp, a
  water jar, sometimes a well shared between neighbors. The excavated settlements show them scattered
  like a thin village - near enough to a neighbor to walk to, far enough not to hear him. A man built
  his own or took over one left empty, patched the roof after the rare rains, kept a small store of bread
  and salt, and worked at his handcraft by the door''s light. Nothing about the building was strange to
  an Egyptian villager; it was a farm laborer''s hut set down where no farm was. The strangeness was only
  in where it stood and how long a man stayed in it.'
