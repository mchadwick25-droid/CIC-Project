text: Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The desert worked for its bread. Palm
  fronds came up from the valley in bundles; a man soaked them, split them, and plaited rope and baskets
  through the day's hours, and the finished goods went back down to the villages to be sold or bartered
  for loaves. Some went out at harvest and hired themselves to the fields like any laborer, and were paid
  like any laborer. Prices were known and discussed; a basket was worth so much, a day's reaping so much,
  and cheating a monk at market was as possible as cheating anyone. The coin or grain that came back bought
  bread, covered a guest, or went to the poor of the villages. The desert had no separate economy - it
  hung from the valley's one, by a rope of its own plaiting.
