  were a rumor. The street knew that certain people met early, would not join the temples'' festivals,
  and called each other brother and sister; from there gossip did its work - talk of secret suppers, of
  bodies and blood, of a founder executed in a province, of stubbornness that insulted the gods and might
  draw their anger onto the whole street. Roman writers passed the movement in a line or two: a mischievous
  superstition, a nuisance over someone called Chrestus, people convicted of hating humankind. None of
  it was investigation; all of it was reputation. Living in these cities meant living inside that talk
  - buying from, working beside, and renting from neighbors who had heard things about you, and mostly
