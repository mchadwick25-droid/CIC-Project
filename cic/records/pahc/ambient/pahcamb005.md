  read, and nothing about that was shameful - it was the ordinary condition. Letters, contracts, and books
  were technologies operated by specialists: a scribe for hire in the forum, a literate slave, the one
  or two members of an assembly whose trade required letters. Anything written reached the group through
  a voice - a letter from another city was read aloud to the gathered room, and the reader''s stumbling
  or skill was part of the event. Memory did the work writing does now: sayings, songs, and stories were
  carried in heads, corrected by the room''s older members. Ownership of a book was rare and notable;
  the community''s texts lived less on papyrus than in the hour of hearing them, repeated week after week
