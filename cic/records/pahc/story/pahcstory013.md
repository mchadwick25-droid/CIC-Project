text: 'This is how it would have been for a member of this community imprisoned under threat of execution
  - though no one narrates this member by name. One outside observer left us a picture of it, almost despite
  himself. He was hostile and mocking. He was writing to mock a man he considered a fraud - a man who
  had once been part of this community. Even so, his account shows us what happened around such an imprisonment.
  Widows and orphans waited at the prison gates from early morning. Officials were bribed so visitors
  could get inside. People traveled from the cities of Asia, sent by their own communities, to comfort
  the one held. Sacred books were read aloud to him inside the prison. And gifts of money were brought
  - "no small sums," in the outsider''s own resentful account.


  A separate, later witness defended this world''s own communities to a hostile Roman audience. He describes
  the standing practice behind such moments. A voluntary monthly gift was given, but only by those willing
  and able. It was "not spent on feasts and drinking-parties," he says, but on burying the poor, supporting
  orphans and the elderly, and caring for those "in the mines, or exiled to islands, or in prison" for
  their standing in the community.


  Together, these two outside accounts - one mocking, one defending - describe the same pattern from opposite
  intentions: a community that organized real support, in goods and in person, around a member facing
  exactly this kind of threat.'
