text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Egypt wrote on papyrus, and the dry
  ground kept what it wrote. From the towns up the river survive the ordinary papers of people of this
  world: private letters commending a traveler, greetings between kin, accounts, receipts, lists. Christians
  wrote as their neighbors wrote - the same formulas of greeting, the same abbreviations a scribe''s hand
  fell into - and their letters sit in the finds beside tax rolls and marriage contracts. Their books
  took a form the trade found unfashionable: the codex, leaves bound like a notebook, where the booksellers''
  classics kept to the roll. A reader held scripture the way a tradesman held his accounts. Most people
  met any written word through a voice reading aloud; a letter from a distant brother was a small public
  event, read to the gathered house.'
