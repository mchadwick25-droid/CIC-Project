text: 'Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. All Egypt lived by the river''s year.
  The Nile rose in summer, drowned the fields it fed, and left them black and ready; the height of the
  flood was measured anxiously, because a few cubits short meant hunger the following year and a few too
  many meant ruin of another kind. Alexandria felt the river at one remove - the city ate from the valley''s
  harvest and taxed it, and the grain fleets it sent abroad were the empire''s bread. When the flood failed,
  or war interrupted the valley, the greatest city of the East learned dearth like any village: prices
  climbing, the poor pressed first, grain hoarded and guarded. Everyone in this world, however learned,
