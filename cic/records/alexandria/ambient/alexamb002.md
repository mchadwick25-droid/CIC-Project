text: Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Plague came to Alexandria more than
  once, and one visitation in the middle of the third century was remembered in writing by people who
  lived through it. There was no house without its dead, the account says. Mourning filled the city; the
  healthy fled the sick; bodies went unburied in the streets. Famine and civil strife ran alongside the
  disease, and the city's population shrank so sharply that men wrote of counting fewer citizens from
  childhood to old age than had once been counted in a single generation's rolls. Sickness on that scale
  was not an interruption of the city's life. For those years it was the city's life, the shared weather
  of every quarter and every allegiance.
