term: Soul / Psyche
aliases:
- psyche
- the human soul
- the inner person

quick_meaning: >-
  Not a ghost living inside a body. The whole person, made for God. We bear
  his image. We are alive through the body, turned toward God by our nature,
  and being restored toward the likeness of the one whose image we bear.
world_meaning: 'There is a misunderstanding to clear away before the soul can be understood. It is easy
  to picture the soul as a kind of inner resident — a spirit lodged in flesh, waiting to be freed from
  its material prison and returned to where it truly belongs. That is not what *psyche* means for us.
  And it is not merely wrong; it is precisely the account of the human person we consistently and openly
  refuse.


  For us the soul is the whole person, understood as an image-bearer turned toward God. The word *psyche*
  first names the life-principle — what makes a body alive rather than merely material. The soul animates
  the body, and expresses itself through it. It is not caged in the body; it lives and acts through the
  body. When we pray, the soul prays — through lips and breath and posture and attention. When we fast,
  the soul fasts — through the body''s restraint and the retraining of desire that restraint works. When
  we love, the soul loves — through every material and relational act love asks for. The body is not the
  soul''s cell. It is the soul''s instrument and, in its own way, its partner in formation.


  This is why our practices are bodily. Fasting, vigil, kneeling, the Eucharist, baptism — these are not
  concessions to people who need physical props for spiritual thoughts. They are formation practices because
  the soul is formed through the body, not in spite of it. The transformation of the soul toward God is
  the transformation of the whole person — desire, perception, action, relationship — and not of inner
  conviction alone.


  The soul has depth. In much of our anthropology the person is spoken of as body, soul (*psyche*), and
  spirit or intellect (*nous*). The soul as *psyche* is the animating middle — what joins the body''s
  material life to the nous''s capacity for divine perception. The nous is not a second thing standing
  beside the soul; it is the soul''s own highest capacity, its depth rather than a separate resident.
  When we speak of the soul being formed and of the nous being illumined, we are speaking of one person
  at different depths of a single reality. Formation reaches us at every level: the body''s practices
  shape the soul''s desires, the soul''s practices shape the nous''s capacity, and the nous''s formed
  perception turns the soul''s whole orientation. And the soul is what all of this is for — what receives
  catechesis, what is illumined, what gnosis transforms, what wisdom shapes, what participates in the
  divine life, and what theosis names in its final restoration.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  The soul is the whole person as oriented toward God — neither ghost-in-a-machine nor poetic fiction.
  Both modern readings miss the orientation that defines it. The soul is not the inner part of a person;
  it is the person as constituted by the image of God, capable of formation toward God, the subject of
  the divine–human relationship formation serves — the living human being seen from the angle of their
  deepest making.'

    Note: the broadly Alexandrian account — the soul as the whole person oriented toward God, the subject
    of formation, embodied rather than imprisoned — is Widely Accepted across Clement, Origen, and Athanasius
    (with differences of emphasis). The tripartite body–soul–nous structure is Dominant Modern Reconstruction
    (different figures deploy it differently). Origen''s distinctive extension — the soul''s pre-existence,
    its fall from an original contemplative *nous*-life into embodied psychic existence, and the eventual
    restoration of all souls — is his own systematization, later condemned alongside the *apokatastasis*
    propositions; it is Dominant Modern Reconstruction for ecology-wide claims, carries condemnation risk,
    and should not be presented as universally Alexandrian. The contested continuity of that stratum with
    the condemned propositions is carried by the Fall/Descent, Logikos, Nous, and Apokatastasis entries,
    not here.'
