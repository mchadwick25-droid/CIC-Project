term: Likeness of God
aliases:

quick_meaning: 'What the image of God becomes through formation. Not a second gift, but the image progressively restored
  and fulfilled. You see it in the quality of a person''s perception, their love, and their life, as
  formation shapes them toward the character of the God whose image they bear.'
world_meaning: 'There are two words in the Genesis text, and they are not synonyms. "Let us make humankind
  in our image (*eikon*), according to our likeness (*homoiosis*)." The image is given. The likeness is
  the image''s goal. The image is what every one of us is simply by being human — the indelible mark that
  makes us capable of God. The likeness is what we are being formed toward — the slow conforming of the
  whole person to the character and life of God that a lifetime of formation works.


  This distinction carries weight. Confuse image and likeness and you collapse the given into the goal
  (as if formation manufactured our capacity for God rather than restoring it) or the goal into the given
  (as if the likeness were already ours and needed only to be noticed). Held apart, the two open the whole
  shape of our life: the image is already here, damaged but present, which is why formation is possible;
  the likeness is where the image is going, which is why formation has a direction.


  The likeness is not a quality the soul holds statically. It shows in the *mode* of a person''s life
  — in how they perceive, love, speak, suffer, serve. When we picture the one in whom the likeness has
  grown far, we picture someone who knows at depth and loves at depth, whose knowing and loving are one
  thing, whose clarity of sight and generosity of life are recognizably the character of the God they
  are being formed toward. This is not moral performance. It is what genuine formation makes of a whole
  person. And the stages name how it grows: catechesis opens the door; illumination deepens perception,
  and deeper perception of divine reality is a deepening of the image of the Logos, which is what the
  likeness is; gnosis increases real contact with God, and to be in contact with God is to become more
  like him; wisdom is the likeness once gnosis has had time to permeate a life; participation is the likeness
  enacted, the soul genuinely sharing the divine life and so growing like the life it shares; and theosis
  is the likeness completed, the horizon where image and likeness finally coincide.


  So the likeness is not a goal standing apart from the practices. It is what they produce, stage by stage,
  what each deepens, what the whole sequence builds toward. And its possibility is grounded in the Incarnation:
  the Logos who is himself the Image of the Father entered our nature, so that our nature is now, from
  within, being restored toward the image and drawn toward the likeness by the very one whose likeness
  it was always meant to bear — genuinely like God, in the only way a creature can be: expressing, in
  creaturely form, the character, love, wisdom, and life of the one whose image we carry.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  The moral qualities the modern hearing notices — the formed person loves more freely, sees more clearly,
  serves more generously — are real, and we do not deny them. But they are the fruit of an ontological
  change, not the change itself. The likeness is not imitation laid over a self that stays the same; it
  is what the self looks like once genuine formation has altered what the self *is*. Two people may perform
  the same generous act — one who has decided to, and one whose desire has been genuinely reordered over
  years of formation. The modern hearing sees the acts rightly; it mislocates them as the goal rather
  than the fruit.'
