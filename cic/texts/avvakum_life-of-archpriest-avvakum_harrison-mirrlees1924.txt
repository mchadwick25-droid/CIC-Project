By adopting the Christian Faith from the 
Greeks Russia became a Province of By- 
zantine Christendom, and such she remained 
till the Reform—or rather Revolution—of 
Peter the Great. But Russia was not 
connected with the centres of Byzantine 
civilization in the same way as the countries 
of Western Europe were with those of Latin 
culture. 

In the Eastern Church the Writ and the 
Liturgies were translated into the vernacular 
of every newly converted nation, and this 


custom dispensed all these nations from the ° 


necessity of knowing Greek. Old Slavonic, 
and not Greek, was the language of Civiliza- 
tion and Religion in all the Slavonic countries. 
Greek remained a sealed book to the old 
Russians, unnecessary for the ordinary needs 
of the Church and consequently unstudied. 
The fifteenth century is an important 
turning - point in the history of Eastern 


Christendom. Constantinople was taken by |
