OUR  FORTIETH  ANNUAL  STATEMENT 


The  close  of  our  fiscal  year  ending  Sept.  15th, 
1916  find  Old  Pacific  Garden  Mission  with  the  bless- 
ing of  God  still  upon  it.  Indeed  we  look  forth  into 
the  new  year  with  new  hopes  and  with  plans  for  a 
greater  work  than  we  have  ever  known  for  the 
bringing  of  the  gospel  to  lost  men  and  women  in 
Down  Town  Chicago. 


Our  dear  brother  Harry  Monroe  has  gone  home 
to  a rich  reward,  for  his  thirty-five  years  spent  in  a 
noble  effort  to  help  men  and  women  into  the  expe- 
rience which  he  had  of  a loving  and  keeping  Saviour. 


3 


Mrs.  Clarke  still  lingers  with  us,  frail  in  body, 
very  near  the  crossing  of  the  river,  but  full  of 
interest  in  everything  which  concerns  Pacific  Gar- 
den Mission  and  the  welfare  of  God's  Kingdom 
everywhere. 


4 


Pursuant  to  a very  urgent  request  of  the  Board 
of  Trustees,  Mi*.  Melvin  E.  Trotter  takes  up  his 
duties  on  Nov.  1st  as  General  Superintendent  of  a 
larger  Pacific  Garden  Mission  work  in  Chicago, 
assisted  by  Mr.  Horace  W.  Ingersoll.  Mr.  Ingersoll 
will  give  much  time  and  attention  to  following  up 
and  helping  those  who  are  sincere  in  their  desire  to 
begin  a new  life. 

Miss  Dorothy  Russell  as  an  assistant  in  the 
office  and  with  her  splendid  service  at  the  piano  is 
proving  a very  valuable  helper. 


5 


Mi*.  James  Calverly,  with  his  pungent  exposition 
of  the  word  in  the  Sunday  morning  convert’s  meet- 
ings, brings  a means  of  strength  and  encouragement 
to  many  who  are  making  a start  in  the  Christian 
life  and  who  meet  many  obstacles  and  temptations. 

It  is  with  gratitude  and  sincere  appreciation 
that  we  remember  the  unselfish  and  untiring  efforts 
of  the  many  helpers  in  the  mission  work  during  the 
past  year.  Also  the  gifts  of  money  and  materials 
which  have  been  so  generously  made  by  those  who 
have  God’s  work  at  heart.  Without  this  conse- 
crated giving  of  time  and  substance,  very  little 
could  have  been  done. 

Plans  for  the  enlarged  work  embrace  noon 
meetings,  meetings  for  young  women  employed 
down  town,  a yearly  bible  conference,  a thorough 
follow-up  system  for  the  needy,  meetings  in 
churches  about  the  city  to  bring  the  mission  work 
before  many  who  would  like  to  have  a part  in  it, 
etc. 

With  grateful  hearts  we  are  reminded  that 
Pacific  Garden  Mission  has  never  closed  for  a single 
night  in  the  past  forty  years,  and  every  night  the  old 
message  has  rung  out  with  no  uncertain  sound, 
“Jesus  Saves.”  Men  and  women  have  believed  the 
message  and  have  gone  out  all  over  this  poor  sin- 
cursed  world  to  make  good. 

SARAH  D.  CLARKE, 
MELVIN  E.  TROTTER
