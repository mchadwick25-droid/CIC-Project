Title: Lettere del glorioso arcivescovo di Milano S. Carlo Borromeo, cardinale di S. Prassede, per la prima volta date in luce
Creator: Carlo Borromeo (1538-1584): letters written by his secretary, annotated and signed by Borromeo, to Cesare Speciano, his agent at Rome, 1579-1581, as the printer's Avviso states; the editor is not named (the Avviso and preface are signed by nobody, the Aggiunta's by "Il Libraio")
Publisher: Pietro Bassaglia, Venice
Publication Date: 1762
Rights: Public Domain (published 1762; public domain by date)
Language: ita
Source: Internet Archive item bub_gb_KCok-f08s_AC, file bub_gb_KCok-f08s_AC_djvu.txt, fetched directly by the library/source-research thread (Era VII session), 2026-10-16; host rights statement: licenseurl http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ (Public Domain Mark), no possible-copyright-status field, access-restricted-item not set; contributor National Central Library of Florence (Google Books scan). A second scan of the same edition is on archive.org as bub_gb_dMFftlJBUvsC; a 1762 French translation printed with the Italian text following it is on archive.org as 10569301bsb and 10402261bsb (Bayerische Staatsbibliothek copies).
Provenance note: Header prepended; the OCR text follows unchanged (long s count 0, so no long-s normalisation; nothing retyped, nothing dropped, nothing corrected). Printed at Venice in 1762, so public domain by date.
Content note: Borromeo's own letters in Italian, copied (the Avviso says) from the originals in the Ambrosiana archive, with Borromeo's autograph additions marked (a) in the margin. The Avviso states that some letters were left out. The collection was published in 1762, in the years of the campaign against the Society of Jesus, and was selected for what it shows of Borromeo's disputes with the Milan Jesuits over the seminary and other matters; it is a purposeful selection, not his whole correspondence. Letters are headed by place and date ("Di Milano alli ... MDLXXVIIII" and so on). OCR sampled at 10/30/50/70/90 % and found legible, with the long s read as f in places; the last pages are the printer's catalogue of other books, not Borromeo's text.
