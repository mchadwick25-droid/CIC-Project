rE  stand  on  the  threshold  of  another  year.  September  15, 
1908,  marks  the  most  eventful  in  the  history  of  this  work.  For 
over  11,000  consecutive  nights,  the  meetings  have  been  attended 
with  unabated  interest,  where  for  31  years  the  Mission  has  stood 
as  a Light-house  in  the  darkest  part  of  our  great  city. 

It  would  be  impossible  to  convey  the  thrilling  testimonies 
that  fall  from  human  lips  nightly,  when  touched  by  the  mighty 
power  of  God.  As  the  witnesses  go  forth  to  magnify  His  name, 
they  are  reproduced  in  the  lives  of  others,  influences  reaching  to  the  uttermost  parts 
of  the  earth.  England,  Ireland,  Scotland  and  the  Isles  of  the  Sea,  from  near  and  far 
the  good  news  reaches  us  that  the  results  of  this  work  has  set  in  motion  influences  that 
will  never  cease. 


Practical  results  are  happy  homes,  husbands  and  fathers  restored  to  their  families, 
wives  and  children  made  happy,  multitude  rescued,  and  the  world  made  better. 

The  magnitude  of  this  work  will  never  be  known  until  Eternitie’s  records  are  dis- 
closed. Little  did  we  think  31  years  ago  that  so  humble  a beginning  would  be 
attended  with  such  remarkable  results,  and  today  be  recognized  as  foremost  among 
Rescue  Missions. 

While  scepticism,  infidelity,  higher  criticism  and  all  other  “Isms”  have  been  agita- 
ting the  minds  of  the  people,  the  Great  Jehovah  has  been  performing  miracles  that 
bring  to  naught  all  theories  and  speculations  conceived  by  finite  minds. 

The  Old  Book  is  still  demonstrating  it  has  not  lost  its  power,  and  no  weapon  formed 
against  it  will  prosper.

A Great  Need 


important  auxiliary  has  been  added  to  the  Mission  during  the  past  year — 
‘A  Home  for  Homeless  Girls.” 

The  great  need  of  a Christian  home  where  girls  homeless,  friendless  and 
dmost  hopeless,  needing  a warning  voice  and  helping  hand  to  protect  them 
from  the  many  allurements  of  a great  city,  and  when  almost  driven  to  desperation,  with 
a heart  yearning  for  some  kind  word  and  human  sympathy,  to  be  betrayed  by  some 
designing  monster  that  has  robbed  her  of  her  all — {Gods  noblest  gift)  when  an  open  door, 
a kind  avo  d and  loving  heart  might  have  shielded  her  from  utter  disgrace. 

It  is  a lamentable  fact— of  the  present  day  to  see  how  many  of  our  beautiful 
innocent  girls  are  being  victimized  by  some  heartless  villian  who  holds  up  his  head  high 
in  society,  -while  his  victim  is  crushed  to  the  earth. 

While  calling  in  a large  apartment  wholly  given  up  to  “roomers”  as  usual  talking 
and  praying  with  them,  one  lonely  girl  testified  in  a Mission  not  long  after  “that  it  was 
not  so  much  what  the  lady  said,  but  when  she  went  away  she  put  her  arms  around  me

"Do  you  know  the  world  is  dying  for  a little  bit  of  love?”

Another  Important  Department  Considered 


MONG  the  many  converts  are  found  men  of  unusual  ability  and  intelli- 
gence and  it  has  become  a great  and  serious  problem,  how  to  utilize  their 
gifts  and  find  opportunities  for  their  developement. 

To  this  end  it  is  thought  another  Mission  could  be  successfully  and  profit- 
ably maintained,  to  extend  the  usefulness  of  this  rapidly  growing  work. 


The  business  men’s  quartette  continue  to  render  most  efficient  service  by  their 
inspiring  songs,  both  at  the  Mission  and  on  the  Gospel  Wagon. 

These  gentlemen  sing  the  Gospel  in  the  good  old  fashion  way,  with  the  spirit  and 
understanding  also,  and  multitudes  of  the  hopeless  and  discouraged  have  been  awakened 
to  the  fact  that  life  is  worth  living  and  through  the  sweet  ministry  of  song  have  been 
won  to  a better  life. 

They  have  become  an  important  factor  of  the  Mission;  may  they  be  long  spared  to 
sing  His  praises.

How  we  reach  the  Masses. 


Fishing  for  Men 


UKE  14-21.  We  have  a Divine  comimssion  for  Gospel  Wagon  work.  “Go 
out  quickly  into  the  streets  and  lanes  of  the  city  and  bring  in  hither  the 
poor  and  the  mained  and  the  halt  and  the  blind”  was  the  command  of  the 
Saviour.  In  this  modern  day  there  is  no  medium  quite  so  attractive  and 
profitable  in  reaching  the  masses  as  the  Gospel  Wagon. 

All  kinds  and  conditions  of  men  and  women  are  reached  and  it  is  not  an  uncommon 
sight  to  see  two  or  three  hundred  lost  and  hopeless  ones  with  uplifted  faces  taking  in 
every  word  of  cheer  and  hope  that  the  Gospel  of  Christ  affords,  and  the  thrilling  testi- 
monies and  pathetic  songs  rendered  by  the  Business  Men’s  Quartette  whose  singing  has 
been  so  signally  blessed.  An  impassioned  appeal  is  made  for  the  people  to  repent  and 
give  themselves  to  Christ. 

What  a sight  follows — can  you 'imagine  in  one  of  Chicago’s  busy  and  wickedest 
thoroughfares,  25  or  30  men  raising  their  hands  for  prayer  and  then  in  the  old  time  way 
15  or  20  of  these  bow  their  knees  on  the  sidewalk,  while  a company  of  workers  are 
distributed  among  them  and  they  are  pointed  to  the  Lamb  of  God  “That  taketh  away 
the  sin  of  the  world.” 

Each  seeker  is  given  a copy  of  the  New  Testament,  many  find  .there  way  to  the 
Mission  and  often  bear  testimony  that  as  the  result  of  the  Gospel  Wagon  meetings  they 
were  rescued  from  sinful  lives.  It  pays — its  practical — it  gets  results.

He  Has  Fought  a Good  Fight 


22  years  ago,  while  suffering  from  the  effects  of  a. 
whirlwind  life,  a strange  vision  came  to  me  of  the  awful 
consequences  of  sin,  I saw  myself  a lost  man  and  became 
alarmed,  was  warned  by  this  admonition  to  go  to  Pacific 
Garden  Mission  where  I had  often  been  entreated  to  give 
my  heart  to  God — discouraged,  dishardened  and  friend- 
less I there  found  the  friend  of  friends,  who  healed  my 
broken  heart  and  set  the  captive  free.  22  years  have 
passed  away,  and  as  I recall  His  loving  kindness  to  even 
me,  my  glad  heart  says  in  the  language  of  the  Psalmist: 
“Bless  the  Lord  to  my  Soul  and  forget  not  all  His 
benefits.” 

Mr.  Geo.  E.  Wilson

A Brand  Plucked  from  the  Burning 


It  is  my  purpose  to  write  briefly  what  the  Pacific 
Garden  Mission  has  done  for  me.  My  young  manhood 
was  spent  under  the  most  pleasant  environments — An 
indulgent  father  and  mother  and  every  advantage,  that 
a young  man  might  desire  to  make  life  a success — A 
prosperous  business,  happy  domestic  relations  and  in  fact 
prospect  for  a bright  future — But  alas,  the  dark  hour 
came  and  all  I held  near  and  dear  was  sacrificed,  that  the 
awful  appetite  for  drink,  gambling  and  kindred  associa- 
tions might  be  gratified. 

I became  a helpless  hopeless,  booze  fighter,  from  the 
gilded  saloon  to  the  lowest  brothel,  from  the  Pullman 
coach  to  the  cattle  car,  from  a successful  salesman  to  a 
hobo,  utterly  hopeless  I had  made  up  my  mind  to  end 
my  miserable  existance. 

It  was  at  this  crisis  the  spirit  of  God  directed  my 
foot  steps  to  the  dear  old  Mission,  I was  awakened  to  my 
awful  condition  and  seemed  to  hear  the  sweet  voice  of 
Jesus  say,  “Come  unto  me  and  I will  give  you  rest,”  and 
when  I heard  the  redeemed  men  testify,  that  God  had 
saved  them  and  broken  chains  similar  to  mine,  then  dear 
old  Col.  Clarke  came  and  pleaded  with  me  to  give  my 
heart  to  the  Saviour  and  that  dark  night  a poor  helpless 
drunkard,  I bowed  at  Jesus's  feet  and  raised  the  cry  that 
never  fails  to  bring  relief,  “God  have  mercy  on  me  a 
Sinner”  and  praise  the  Lord  the  captive  was^set  free. 
Time  has  rolled  by,  there -has  been  many  stof%*  a.nd  tes- 
tings, but  I can  say  today  that  Jesus  sweetly  sltves-  and 
I am  kept  by  His  mighty  power.

You  to  a Modern'  Miracle 


When  our  country  heeded  volunteers  in  the  dark  days 
of  ’61,  when  our  honored  President  issued  his  famous 
call  for  volunteers  to  defend  our  country’s  flag,  the  sub- 
ject of  this  sketch  shouldered  his  musket  and  said  to  his 
country,  “Here  am  I,”  send  me,,  and  for  three  years  in 
the  battlefield  of  the  south,  proved  himself  a brave  soldier, 
but  alas,  the  demon  of  whiskey  had  bound  him  with 
chains  that  no  human  power  could  sever,  a broken  hearted 
wife — a ruined  home. 

Once  in  prospered  business,  all  sacrificed  on  the  alter 
of  Bacus,  but  thank  God  one  day  while  a helpless  wander 
in  the  street  I happened  to  meet  my  dear  wife  and  she 
took  pity  on  me  and  pleaded  with  me  in  my  rags,  to 
go  to  Pacific  Garden  Mission  where  she  believed  the  Lord 
would  help  me.  I-  shall  never  forget  that  eventful  night 
when  I bowed  under  the  old  clock  and  asked  the  Lord  to 
have  mercy  upon  me  and  forgive  me,  afid  there  the 
Lord  heard  my  cry  and  broke  the  awful  chains  of  sin. 

Now  a happiy  wife,  a pleasant  home  and  association 
with  one  of  the  city  churches.  The  preacher  occupies  the 
pulpit  but  I have  the  privilege  of  ringing  the  church  bell 
which  speaks  to  the  whole  community. 

Ring  the  bells  of  Heaven,  there  is  joy  to-day, 

For  a soul  returneth  from  the  world, 

See  the  Father  meets  him  out  upon  the  way.

A Marvel  of  Grace. 


Mr.  S.  M.  Connor 


No  unworthiness  of  mine  can  be  traced  to  early  influ- 
ences for  I was  reared  by  faithful  Christian  parents;  my 
early  manhood  was  actively  identified  with  church,  Sunday 
school  and  temperance  work — though  knowing  nothing 
of  my  need  of  a Savior. 

Later  I came  to  Chicago  where  I secured  and  held 
for  eight  years  a responsible  position  with  a large  corpor- 
tion.  Having  no  anchor  I drifted  into  association  with 
drinking  men.  From  this  point  the  descent  was  sure  and 
rapid,  and  finally  I found  myself  without  a position,  with- 
out money,  without  friends,  and  without  hope.  In  this 
deplorable  condition  I began  to  think,  I knew  I alone 
was  responsible,  and  I knew  also  that  God  alone  could 
help  me.  With  this  conviction  in  my  soul  I wandered 
about  the  city,  the  clouds  hanging  lower  and  ever  lower 
over  me,  till  one  night  the  Spirit  of  God  led  me  into  the 
Pacific  Garden  Mission  which  has  been  to  thousands  be- 
fore me  the  very  gate  of  heaven  to  burdened  souls.  There 
I gave  my  heart  and  surrendered  my  will  and  my  life  to 
Jesus.  He  pardoned  my  sins,  gave  me  a clean  heart,  made 
me  a new  creature,  and  has  daily  led  me  step  by  step.  I 
have  had  trials  and  temptations,  but  through  His  help  and 
power  I have  triumphed  over  them.  My  testimony  is: — 
The  power  of  the  blood  of  Jesus  Christ  not  only  can  save 
but  can  keep  safely  all  who  put  their  trust  in  Him.  I 
praise  and  thank  Him  for  salvation  from  sin  and  shame 
and  for  making  a new  man  of  me.  And  to  all  who  have 
met  like  defeat  without  Him  I wish  to  say  that  with  Him 
there  is  no  defeat,  but  a victorious  life.  What  He  has 
done  for  me  He  can  do  for  you.

Endowment  Effort 


For  some  months  our  friends  have  been  organizing  an  effort  to  raise  an  endowment 
fund  of  One  hundred  thousand  dollars;  the  interest  only  of  this  fund  to  be  paid  to  this 
Mission  and  its  successors  for  all  time. 

It  has  been  arranged  that  The  Northern  Trust  Company  of  Chicago  shall  be  the 
Trustee  of  this  fund. 

Subscriptions  are  being  taken  on  conditi  on  that  none  are  binding  unless  the  full 
amount  is  subscribed  by  January  1st,  1910. 

We  hope  no  annual  givers  will  discontinue  their  gifts  on  this  account,  as  we  trust 
the  work  can  be  enlarged  and  other  similar  Missions  established. 

We  do  not  propose  to  elaborate  these  premises,  as  that  might  deter  many 
unfortunate  ones  from  entering.  We  want  to  continue  the  Pacific  Garden  Mission  and 
similar  missions  just  as  this  work  has  been  conducted  for  the  past  31  years. 

It  should  also  be  remembered  that  the  results  of  this  effort  may  not  be  known 
until  the  Fall  of  1909.
