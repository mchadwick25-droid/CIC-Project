THE AND THE SERBIAN CHURCH 
By THe Rev. W. Kk. LOWTHER CLARKE 


‘HE Society for Pzomoting Christian Knowledge has been 

interested in Eastern Church affairs ever since its beginning, but 
until the great war broke out had taken no peor! cognizance of 
“Serbia. 

In September, 1917, it was tepresented to the Society that the 

Serbian soldiers were in a state of spiritual destitution, so far as 
‘literature went, and that the Serbian” authorities would greatly 
appreciate the kindness if something could be provided by S.P.C.K. in 
the way of a prayer book, just as the Bible Society provided Bibles. 
The cases were not quite parallel: the Bible Society provided what 
was common to both English and Serbs; the S.P.C.K. was being asked 
to produce a book of Eastern Church devotions, such as would inevit- 
ably be contrary to Anglican formularies in some respects. However, 
the Committee consented to help, and Father Nicolai Velimirovic¢ 
_ compiled a book of prayers in Serbian, which was probably an advance 
on anything produced before in Serbia. Several beautiful pictures of 
churches and ikons were included, and to many a Serbian exile thc 
book must have seemed like a breath of air from home. -The Serbian 
Relief Fund assisted in the distribution of the books, which went to the 
‘Western Front, Archangel, Bizerta, Corfu, and Salona: 10,000 copies 

in.all were disposed of... 

At the end of 1918 the way was open for a return of the exiles On - 
arriving in Serbia the ecclesiastical authorities found that the invaders 
had destroyed mogt of the Church-service books. A further appeal for 
help was made to S.P.C.K. The Society consulted its president, the 
Archbishop.of Canterbury, atid, helped by his advice, drew up the 
following scheme for meeting the urgent need: 

1. A Reprint of the Book of Prayers.—A further 10,000 were printed 
and sent to Serbia through the Serbian Relief Fund. Unfortunately, 
they were shipped to Fiume, where for a long while they remained. 

2. A Serbian Edition of Philaret’s (Russtan) Orthodox. Catechism.— 

The translation was made by Father Yanitch, with the assistance of 
Mr. Georgevitch. Two thousand copies were. printed and sent to 
Serbia, but they were stopped, like the foregoing, at Fiume.
