INTRODUCTION. 


That the community of Shakers has excited so little attention from “the world,” forming as it 
has for three quarters of a century, an unprecedented movement in society, is due to the very 
many exciting themes of the age. Itis true much has been said and written of them; yet in com- 
parison with their peculiar origin, their wonderful claims, and their position in the world, the inter- 
est excited has been inconsiderable. And yet, we have reason to believe there are many who 
would appreciate reliable information on the subject. That one who has been a resident in. a so- 
ciety of Shakers over forty-six years, and was a member of one of its two most sacred offices more 
than twenty-seven years of that time, must possess some of the essentials to a truthful delinea- 
ion of its history and present position, will be granted, if, in addition to this experience, the ab- 
sence of any influence from the Society may be depended upon. ‘This is my position—yet with the 
sane confidence in the Founders of the Community, and the same good will to its present members, 
as when I was owned by them as a *‘sister in the Gospel.” How this can be will be shown in 
due time. ° : 

The writing of these sketches is only initiatory, and is for the purpose of giving the Covenant 
to ithe world in accordance with the wishes of many friends, and as it appears without offence to 
the “community.” It is not, however, my first effort in this direction. I have commenced a se- 
rial entitled ‘‘Reminiscences of Life among the Shakers; Historical and Biographical Sketclies, 
Shaker Poems, ete,” published in Tur Mirtronairs, by Lorenzo D. Grosvenor; dwelling upon 
those subjects which I deem best calculated to give as clear a view of what Shakerism is, as my 
limits will permit. 

With respect to any incident related of Mother Ann Lee which may seem incredible, I can only 
say I have stated nothing that was not regarded by those who compiled a narrative of the facts, soon 
after her decease—a part only of which has been published—as fully substantiated by witnesses 
living at the time; these witnesses having received their information from Ann Lee herself, and 
those who came with her from England. Much that has never been written but by myself, I learned 
of those who were eye and ear witnesses, with whom an acquaintance of many years served only 
to deepen my confidence in their truthfulness. 

That whatever has been done in this, or any cause, deemed miraculous, is in harmony with the 
imnutable laws of God, is doubtless true; but until we can trace those laws in the formation of the 
least organic germ out, of invisible elements, we are scarcely competent to decide in what way they 
shall manifest themselves with respect to a life as far transcending the merely organic, as that is 
superior to the physical law. 

The fact, were it duly considered, of a community existing for nearly cighty years with the 
Shaker characteristics of se/f-denial and consecration, without the seclusion imposed by the Rom- 
ish Church in securing these results, is suggestive of a power, which, if it exists in the masses of 
humanity is not usually put to the test. The temporal thrift of the Shaker societies has ‘been the 


1 oy 


‘‘star of hope” to all experimenters in community life, and the possibility of celibacy with perfect 
chastity, has a voice, if listened to, from which much may be learned. 

It is true the Societies are now mostly on thedecline. This was predicted by Father Joseph 
Meachem, the principal founder of ‘‘church relation,”’ in defense of which the Covenant in its pres- 
ent form was instituted; yet I have no reason to suppose he regarded his arduous labors as a fail. 
ure in any sense of the word. How the ‘‘Dispensation,” now admitted by its leading members to 
be in its ‘‘decadence,” is to be a power for good in succeeding ages must be left to the Great De- 
signer. : | 

Truth is a gem worth searching for. The inspiration by which it is elucidated must ever be free 
to take to itself such means, and assume such forms as best serve the present needs of humanity. 
It would be the ardent desire of every one, (even with selfishness the prevailing sentiment,) to 
have the best means of doing good prevail at whatever cost or sacrifice, could each fully realize 
how inimately blended are the interests and happiness of all created intelligences. 

The Covenant must speak for itself. With all the power it gives it would be harmless in the 
hands of such persons as were the Founders of the Institution. But who shall vouch for ‘their 
successors forever?’’ Has the decree of the Almighty gone forth that he will so rule the hearts and 
enlighten the understandings of all who may be appointed to the leadership in Shakerdom, that no 
injustice or oppression shall ensue? And would such decree be in harmony with the volition with 
which he has endowed mankind? I leave these questions for the consideration of an enlighted 


public.

PREAMBLE. 


We, the Brethren and Sisters of the United Society of Believers (called Shakers), residing in the 
county of Worcester, and State of Massachusetts, being connected together as a religious and so- 
cial community, distinguished by the name and title of the Church of the United Society, in the 
town of Harvard, which for many years has been established and in successful operation, under 
the charge of the Ministry and Eldership thereof, and, feeling the importance not only of renew- 
ing and confirming our spiritual covenant with God and each other, but also of renewing and im- 
proving our social compact, and amending the written form thereof, do make, ordain, and de- 
clare the following articles of agreement as a summary of the principles, rules, and regulations 
established in the Church of the said United Society, which are to be kept and maintained by us, 
both in our collective and individual capacities, as a covenant or constitution, which shall stand as 
a lawful testimony of our religious and social compact, before all men, and in all cases of ques- 
tion and law relating to the possession and improvement of our united and consecrated interest, 
property, and estate: 


ARTICLE I. 
Of the Gospel Ministry. 
Section 1. Their origin, call and institution. 


We solemnly declare to each other and to all whom it may concern, that we have received, 
and do hereby acknowledge as the foundation of our faith, order and government, the testimony or 
Gospel of Christ, in his first and second appearing, and we do hereby solemnly agree to support 
- and inaintain the true primitive faith and Christian principles, the morals, rules and manners per- 
taining to the said Gospel, as ministered by the founders of this Society, and kept and conveyed 

through a regular order of ministration, down to the present day. 

And, although, as a religious society, we are variously associated, with respect to the local 
situations: of our respective communities, yet we are known and distinguished as a peculiar people, 
and consider and acknowledge ourselves as members of one general.community, possessing one 
faith, and subject to one united, parental and ministerial administration, which has been regularly 
supported from the first foundation pillars of the Institution, and which continues to operate for 
the support, protection and strength of every part of said community. 


Srecrion 2.‘ Their order and office. 


We further acknowledge and declare that for the purpose of promoting and maintaining union, or- 
der and harmony throughout the various branches of this community, the primary administration of 
parental authority has been settled in the first established ministry at New Lebanon, there to rest 
and remain as the center of union to all who are in Gospel relation and communion with the So- 
ciety. ‘The established order of this ministry includes four persons; two of each sex. 


Section 3. Perpetuity of their office and how supplied. 


We further acknowledge and declare that the said primary administration, of parental authority 
has been and is perpetuated as follows: Namely, that the first in that office and calling possess

prescribe ordlirecs any rezulation or appointment which they may judge most proper and necessary

Church, subsequent to their decease. But in case no such regulation or appointment be so pre-

This is agreeable to the example recorded in the Scriptures, and continued by the founders of —

maintained in the Community from the beginning. 


SECTION 4. Of the ministerial office in the several societies or communities. 

We further acknow ledge and declare, covenant and agree that the ministerial office and author- 
ity In any Society or Community of our faith, which has emanated, or which may emanate, in a 
reoular line of order from the center of union aforesaid, is; and shall be acknowledged, owned and 
respected, as the spiritual and primary authority of such Society or Community, in all matters per- 
taining to the ministerial office. And in case of the decease or removal of any individual of said 
ministry, in any such Society, his or her lot and place shall be filled by agreement of the sur- 
viving ministers in council with the Elders and others, as the nature of the case may require, to- 
gether with the knowledge and approbation of the primary ministerial authority at New Lebanon | 
aforesaid, to which they are responsible. 

SECTION 5. Powers and duties of the ministry. 

The ministry being: appointed and established as aforesaid are vested with the primary author- 
ity of the Chureh and its various branches. | 

Hence, it becomes their special duty to guide and superintend the spiritual concerns of the So- 
ciety as a body of people under their care and government, and in connection with the Elders in 
their respective familics and departments, who shall act in union with them, to give and establish 
such orders, rules and regulations as may be found necessary for the government and protection of 
the Church and Society within the limits of their jurisdiction, and also to counsel, advise and . 
judge in all matters of importance, whether spiritual or temporal. The said ministry are also in- 
vested with authority in connection with the Elders as aforesaid, to nominate and appoint to 
office ministers, elders, deacons, and trustees, and to -assign offices of care and trust to such: 
brethren and sisters as they, the said ministers and Elders, shall judge to be best qualified for the 
several offices to which they may be appointed ; and we do hereby covenant ‘and agree that such 
nominations and appointments being made and officially communicated to those concerned, and re- 
ceiving the general approbation of the Church, or of the families concerned, shall thenceforth be. 
confirmed and supported, until altered or revoked by the authority aforesaid.

ARTICLE Ii. 
| Institution of the Church. 
Section 1. The object and design of Church relation. 


We further acknowledge and declare that the great object, purpose and design of our uniting 
ourselves together as a church or body of people, in social and religious compact, is faithfully and 


5) 


honestly to occupy, improve and diffuse the various gifts and talents, both of a spiritual and tem- 
poral nature, with which Divine wisdom has blest us for the service of God, fer the honor of the 
Gospel, and for the mutual protection, support, comfort and happiness of each other as brethren 
and sisters in the Gospel, and for such other pious and charitable purposes as the Gospel may re- 
quire. 

Section 2. Who are not admissible into Church relation. 

As the unity, stability and purity of the Church essentially depend on the character and quali- 
fications of its members, and as it is a matter of importance that it should not be encumbered with 
persons who are under any involvement or incapacity, natural or moral: Therefore, no member 
of any company or association in business or civil concern, no co-partner in trade, no person under 
any legal embarrassment or obligations of service, no minor, no slave or bond servant, no insane 
person, no profane person, nor any person who lives in the wilful violation of the known and ac- . 
knowledged principles of moral conduct, shall be deemed qualified for admission into the covenant 
relation and communion of the Church. 


Section 3. Preparation for admission into Church relation. 


In order that believers may be prepared for entering into the sacred privilege of Church rela- 
tion, it is of primary im >< rtance that sufficient opportunity and privilege should be afforded under 
the. ministry of the Gospel, for them to acquire suitable instruction in the genuine principles of 
righteousness, honesty, justice and true holiness, and also that they should prove their faith and 
Christian morality by their practical obedience to the precepts of the Gospel according to 
their instructions. It is also indispensably necessary for them to rece've the one uniting spirit 
of Christ, and to become so far of one heart and one mind, that they are willing to sacrifice all 
other relations for this sacred one. Another essential step, is to settle all just and equitable claims 
of creditors and filial heirs, so that whatever property they may possess, shall be justly their own. 
When this is done, and they feel themselves sufficiently prepared to make a deliberate and final 
choice, to devote themselves, with all they possess, wholly to the service of God, without reserve, 
and it shall be deemed proper by the leading authority of the Church, after examination and due 
consideration, to allow them to associate together in the capacity of a Church, or a branch thereof 
in Gospel order, they may then consecrate themselves and all they possess to tie service of 
God forever, and confirm the same by signing and sealing a written covenant predicatsd upon the 
‘principles herein contained, and fulfiling, on their part, all its obligations. 


Section 4. Admission of new members. 

As the door must be kept open for the admission of new members into the Chureh, when duly 
prepared, it is agreed that each and every person who shall at any time after the date and execu- 
tion of the Church Covenant, in any branch of the Community, be admitted into the Church as a 
member thereof, shall previously have a fair opportunity to obtain a full, clear and explicit under- 
standing of the object and design of the Church Covenant, and of the obligations it enjoins upon 
the members. For this purpose, he or she shall, in the presence cf two of the deacons or actirg 
trustees of the Church, read, or hear the same distinetly read, so as to be able freely to acknowl- 
edge his or her full approbation and acceptance thereof, in all its parts. Then he, she or they (as 
the case may be), shall be at liberty to sign the same; and having signed and sealed it, and heing 
subject to all the obligations required of the original signers, shall thenceforth be entitled to all 
the benefits aud privileges thereunto appertaining; and the signature or signatures thus added 
shall be certified by the said deacons or trustees, together with the date thereof. 


Section 5. Concerning youth and children. 


© Youth and children, being minors, cannot be received as members of the Church possessing a 
consecrated interest in a united capacity, yet it is agreed that they may be received under the im- 
mediate care and government of the Church, at the desire or consent-of such person or persons as 
have a lawful right to or control of such minors, together with their own desire or consent. But 
no minor under the care of the Church can be employed therein for wages of any kind.

ARTICLE IIT. 
Of the Trusteeship. 
Secrion 1. Appointment, qualifications and powers of Trustees. 
It has been found necessary for the establishment of order in the Society in its various branches, 
that superintending Deacons and Deaconesses should be appointed and authorized to act as Trus- 
* tees or agents of the temporalities of the Church. They must be recommended by honesty and 
integrity, their fidelity in trust and their capacity for the transaction of business; of these qualifi- 
cations the Ministry and Elders must be the judges. The ofticial Trustees of the Chureh are 
generally known among us by the title of Office Deacons, of which there must be two or more; 
and being appointed by the authority aforesaid, they are invested with power to take the genera! 
charge and oversight of all the property, estate and interest, dedicated, devoted, consecrated and 
given up for the benefit, of the Church ; to hold in trust the fee of all the lands belonging to the 
Church ; also all gifts, grants and donations which have been, or may hereatter be dedicated, de- 
voted, consecrated and given up as aforesaid ; and the said property, estate, interest, gifts, grants 
and donations shall constitute the united and consecrated interest of the Church, and shall be held 
in trust by the said Deacons, as acting Trustees, in their official capacity, and by their suecessors 
in said office and trust forever. 


SgotTion 2. Duties of the Trustees. 


It is and shall be the duty of the said Deacons or acting Trustees to improve. use and appro- 
priate the said united interest for the benefit of the church in all its departments; and for such 
other religious and charitable purposes as the Gospel may require; and also to make all just and 
equitable defense in law for the protection and security of the consecrated and united interest, 
rights and privileges of the Church and Society, jointly and severally as an associated community, 
as far as circumstances and the nature of the case may require; Provided, nevertheless, that all the 
transactions of the said Deacons or acting Trustees, in the use, management, protection, defense 
and disposal of the aforesaid interest shall be for the benefit and privilege and in behalf of the 
Church or Society as aforesaid; and not for any private interest, object or purpose whatever. 


Srotion 3. Trustees to give information and be responsible to the Ministry and Elders. 


It shall also be the duty of the Trustees to give information to the Ministry and Elders of the 
chureh of the general state of the temporal concerns of the church and society committed to their 
charge ; and also to report to the said authority all losses sustained in the united interest thereof, 
which shall come under their cognizance. And no disposal of any real estate of the church, nor ~ 
any important contract shall be considered valid without the previous approbation of the authority _ 
aforesaid, to whom the said Deacons and Trustees are and shall, at all times, be held responsible 
in all their transactions.

ARTICLE IV. 
Of the EHidership. 

Section 1. Choice and appointment of Elders. 

The united interests and objects of believers established in Gospel order, require that Elders 
should be chosen and appointed for the spiritual protection of families, whose business it is to take 
the lead in their several departments in the care and government of the concerns of the Church 
and of the different families established in and pertaining to the Society. Their number and order 
should coirespond with that of the Ministry. They are required to be persons of good under- 
standing, of approved faithfulness and integrity, and gifted in spiritual administration. They 
must be selected and appointed by the Ministry, who are to judge of their qualifications. 

Section 2. Duties of the Elders. ; | 

As faithful watchmen upon the walls of Zion, it becomes the duty of the Elders to watch over 
their respective families ; to instruct the members in their respective duties ; to counsel, encourage, 
admonish, exhort and reprove as occasion may require; to lead the worship; to be examples to 
the members, of obedience to the principles and orders of the Gospel, and to see that the orders, 
rules and regulations pertaining to their respective families or departments are properly kept. 

ARTICLE V. 
Of Family Deacons and Deaconesses. 

Secrion 1. Their qualifications and appointments. 

The office of family Deacons and Deaconesses has long been established in the Chureh, and is 
essentially necessary for the care, management and direction of the domestic concerns in each 
family, order or branch of the Church. They are required to be persons of correct and well- 


erounded faith in the established principles of the Gospel, faithful in duty, closely united to their 


Elders, and of sufficient capacity in business. Of the qualifications the Ministry and Elders by 
whom they are chosen and appointed: must be the judges. Their number in each family is gener- 
ally two of each sex, but may be more or less, according to the size of the family and the extent 
of their various duties. 

Section 2. Their duties and obligations. 

The Deacons and Deaconesses of families are intrusted with the care and over-sight of the

CHAPTER I. 


KHeperience of Mother Ann Lee in England— Voyage to America. 


ANN Lex, the founder of the sect called Shakers, known among her fellowers by the affectionate and rev- 
erential title of Mother, did not originate the term Shakers. She united with a sect,—descendants from 
the French Prophets,—who were called by that name on account of the peculiarity of their religious wor- 
Ship, when in the 23rd year of her age. By dint of a most extraordinary experience of about nine years 
she became its acknowledged leader A. D. 1770, not as successor to those who had been the leaders of the 
Sect, but by their conviction, announced to the society, that their light was eclipsed by hers. 

A comprchensive history of her life would form a volume of no small magnitude and interest. (The best 
ever given to the world is in the Millenial Church, from which I shall quote occasionally.) 

‘‘She was born in Manchester, England, Feb. 29th, 1836.” Her parents were pious and respectable, but 
too poor to.give their eight children the first rudiments of literary education. 

Her visions in childhood; the burden which ever rested upon her spirit for the salvation of Soule! her 
early marriage and subsequent struggles for emancipation—not from its duties, but from its wieelditnaes: 
the alternations of heavenly joy with an intensity of suffering in which the scenes of Gethsemane were re- 
peated, even to that degree of anguish which caused the copious perspiration induced, to be suffused with 
blood; the cruel persecution, of which an imprisonment of fourteen days without food, save what milk and 
wine could be conveyed through the key-hole of the prison door by inserting a pipe-stem and pouring the 
liquid into its bow] once in twenty-four hours, and this not until after the frst two days, was a specimen; the 
assertion of learned men before whom she was arraigned that she spoke in seventy two different languages 
(although of herself she could not read her native tongue) are ail parts of her history as I learned it from 
those who were acquainted with her. Concerning the last incident I quote from ‘‘America the Land of 
Emanuel,” by Lorenzo D. Grosvenor. ‘‘Herenemies disingenuously asserted that in the unknown tongue 
she uttered blasphemy against God. An appeal was made to the learned. As she sat before the judges 
one of them said to her, ‘Woman, why don’t thou speak?’ ‘Why do not those trees wave in the breeze?’ 
said Mother. ‘Because,’ said one of the bishops, ‘there is no breeze.’ ‘So then,’ said she, ‘you must 

wait till the power of God moves me to speak.’ 

The council reported that she spoke in seventy two different languages or dialects, and that her inspira- 
tions contained deep and interesting mysteries of things pertaining to the kingdom of God. They recom- 
mended that no further proceedings be had against her. 

I give this as it was told to me by Elder Elijah Woorster who had the statements from the first founders ; 
aman who himself possessed the gift of tongues and of healing. This experience of Mother Ann was, of 
course, in England where a council of learned men could be easily convened.” 

In the year 1774 Mother Ann received a call to leave her native country, and repair to America—not to 
escape persecution, this had ceased for two years previous—but to raise the standard of gospel light and 
purity, as God had commanded in this land of liberty. What would worldly wisdom have predicted con- 
cerning this enterprise; when the right of woman to speak or teach was not admitted—and no woman had 
ever crossed the oceanas a missionary—her doctrine requiring of her converts ‘‘the full and free confes- 
sion of every known sin,” and the ‘‘righting of every wrong” so far as was practicable; the renunciation of 
all worldly honor and pleasure, living a life of celibacy; or as she would say, ‘‘following Christ in the re- 
generation;” ‘“‘putting their hands to work and giving their hearts to God?” Not the result which has fol- 
lowed surely. But to resume our narrative—

CHAPTER II. 


Residence in the Wilderness—‘‘ Opening of the Testimony to the World’—Decease of Mother Ann—Admninistra-

New York city, the place of landing, became the abode of Mother Ann for about a year and a half, when 
she and her companions became united after a temporary separation for want of a home, in the wilderness 
of Niskeunia (now Watervliet), where a lease of land had been secured for that purpose. 

Here they made a clearing, and erected buildings; and here they remained full three years and a half, un- 
known to all, except their near neighbors, who had no more idea of their misson than had the natives of the . 
forest. Indeed, it was one of these who first discovered ought peculiar about them, save the meekness of their — 
deportment, and the transparent honesty of their dealings. One ‘poor Indian” saw a “bright light’ around 
Mother, and prophesied in true native simplicity, that the ‘Great Spirit had sent her to do much good.” 
And this was all the encouragement they received from mortals during this time, in which they toiled, and 
wept, and prayed, till all hearts failed but hers whose sufferings exceeded all the rest, but ‘whose mind was 
staid on Him” whose promise cannot fail. 

In the spring of 1780, they were providentially discovered, and the news of themyspread throughout the 
adjacent country; there being a band of waiting revivalists who gladly received their testimony. There 
Juture formed a perfect contrast with the past, as from that time they had scarcely leisure to eat or sleep on 
account of the multitude who sought them, from various motives. Persecution raged with violence, 
equalled. in this country ouly by the persecution of the Quakers. I do not except them as suffering more 
than did Mother Ann, although her life was spared. They visited different localitics in the State of New 
York, also in Massachusetts, Connecticut, and Rhode Island. Wherever they were, their resideuce was 


called **The Church,” and was a place of gathering for all the surrounding country. 


The ‘Square House,” in the town of Harvard, was their home longer than any place after their mission 
commenced. As Harvard was my home for over thirty years, [ icarned mach from those who were with 
them there, of the events connected with their residence, most of which [ mast omit, as my present put 
pose is to give only an outline of the history which I hope to give hereafter, Not only were meetings held 
almost constantly by day, and sometimes occupying mich of the night, but hundreds were fed at the table 
of those who had cotsecrated their all to the service of God.

Soon after their return to Niskeunia, Father William Lee, whose thrilling experience of toil and suffering 
was second only to that of Mother Ann, departed this life July 21st, 1784, aged 44 years. Mother Ann 
survived him only until the 8th of Sept. following. Then ‘‘the world” predicted that the ‘‘delusion would 
soon come to an end,” but God is able to work by means of his own choosing. 

Elder James Whittaker had been named by Mother Ann as her successor while they were yet in England. 
The spiritually minded devotion of his youthful season, his experience in this country previous to the 
opening of their testimony to the world, of poverty, toil and waiting, and subsequently in preaching and la- 
boring with the people for which he had received imprisonment, stripes and bruises; in connection with 
rare natural endowments, eminently qualified him for this position. So angelic his devotion, so affectionate 
his ministrations, and so pleasing his manner, that the cross he held forth seemed adorned with the 
flowers of Paradise. He was born Feb. 28, 1751, and finished his work on earth July 20, 1787 leaving the 
little flock in deep tribulation. 

The ‘*Three Witnesses” who came from England had all prophecied of a ‘‘gathering of the Church into 
order,” by their disciples in America; but Father James had not appointed a successor. Joseph Meachem, 
the leader of the-revivalists, had been named by Mother Ann as being instrumental in this great work; but the 
prophecy concerning him was merged in others of like import concerning several of the leading converts; as 
the idea had not then occurred to these disciples, that societies would be formed in other localities as branches 
of the Mother Church, each requiring its Father and Mother. 

As no preponderating excellence appeared where so much native talent and intellectual power were the 
endowment of each, combined apparently with equal religious zeal and spiritual attainment, it seemed im- 
possible for aught save Divine Wisdom to decide the election, and for this they devoutly sought. No con- 
fusion or dissension ensued. No eye but that which was single to the glory of God might look in upon 
that praying band assembled as were the apostles at the day of Pentecost to learn whom God had chosen to 
lead His people to.the promised inheritance in which ail should share alike the blessings of a bounteous 
Providence. And lo! the heavenly influence descended without measure. So great was the power mani- 
fested, so eloquent its utterances, so encouraging its promises, that a beholder might well suy as each de- 
clared the message given, ‘‘Surely the Lord hath chosen this,’’ yet no such evidance of power was vouch- 
safed to the burdened spirit of the chosen one as meekly he listened to the divinely inspired eloquenee of his 
fellow laborers. When all the candidates had ceased—still waiting for a decisive manifestation, the voice ofa 
youth, Job Bishup, was heard, calm and decided, declaring with power which left not the shadow of a doubt 
on the mind of any one present, that the silent listener,Joseph Meachem, was the anointed of God,to lead 
his people. No dissent was ever heard—it was his calling to ‘‘gather the Church into order.” 

In this he was assisted by Mother Lucy Wright, concerning whom we can do no justice in this brief 
sketch. That Father Joseph felt the importance and necessity of having woman share in the government, 
showed the lessons of wisdom he had learned. ‘These parents of the Church had each a helper, thus com- 
pleting the Order of Ministry, two of each sex. The Covenant in its present form is due to a later admin- 
istration. |
