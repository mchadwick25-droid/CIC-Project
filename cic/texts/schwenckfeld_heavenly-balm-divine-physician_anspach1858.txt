NOTE BY THE TRANSLATOR 


The works of Caspar Schwenckfeld were written during the 
sixteenth century, none of which have hitherto appeared in 
an English dress. He was a devout Christian, and differing in 
some unessential points from the two great branches of the 
Church of the Reformation, he, in connection with others, 
founded a religious society. About the time of the American 
Revolution a colony of those who had adopted his peculiar 
views immigrated to this country, and settled in 
Montgomery and Bucks counties, Pennsylvania. They 
organized a few congregations, one in Bucks county, and 
one near Chestnut Hill, Philadelphia county. As their ministry 
was exclusively German, and their descendants grew up 
without learning the language, their congregations gradually 
declined until they ceased to have regular preaching in the 
lower congregation. As ten years of my early ministry were 
spent in the community where these families resided, I bear 
a cheerful testimony to the Christian character of these dear 
friends. Mr. Heydrick and others are among the most 
respectable and influential citizens in all that beautiful region 
of Whitemarsh. A number of their children and children’s 
children became members of the Lutheran Church. Others of 
their descendants united with other denominations. 

The fathers and mothers in their little communion 
were diligent readers of the Bible, and also highly valued the 
practical works of the founder of their society. With a view 
to benefit their children and others, and also with a noble 
desire to leave to their offspring and the world a monument 
of their faith, they engaged my services to translate The 
Divine Physician and the Heavenly Balm, and also The Three 
Kinds of Life, written by Caspar Schwenckfeld. They could 
not leave a more excellent and endearing testimonial of 
their love for Christ and precious souls. 


As the German language has greatly changed in its 
construction, &c., I found it necessary in some instances to 
give it a free translation. I did, however, rigidly adhere to 
the text and faithfully rendered his ideas into English, even 
at the risk sometimes of making long sentences, though I 
trust sufficiently clear to the reader. 

I take pleasure in commending the work to a 
Christian public. The author wrote with Christian simplicity 
and great earnestness of spirit, exhibiting as truly as forcibly 
the wretched state of man by nature; and having laid open 
the deep depravity of the heart and exhibited the wounds of 
the bleeding soul, he leads it to Christ, the heavenly 
Physician. 

In his treatise on the Three Kinds of Life, he discusses 
very fully the rewards of good works; and if the reader may 
not coincide with the author in every particular, he will, 
notwithstanding, rise from its perusal with gratitude to God 
for the instruction which he has received while the motives 
of his actions were searched as by fire, by the sainted 
author, “who being dead yet speaketh.” 

F.R.A. 


PREFACE 


The Heavenly Balm, brought under review in these pages, is 
exceedingly precious. It is well described in the Holy 
Scriptures, and is essential to the salvation of sinners. It is 
especially pleasant to poor sinners who are anxious to be 
delivered from their sins; it is profitable and comforting to 
penitents, though comparatively few persons are concerned 
about it. To those, however, who appreciate the remedy 
which God has provided for the relief of human misery, it 
manifests how a merciful Father deals with His chosen 
whom He receives into His favor. He translates them from 
the kingdom of Satan into the kingdom of Heaven, 
regenerates them, and thus heals the soul and restores it, 
through Jesus Christ, His Son and our Lord. 

There are four distinct points which I propose to treat 
here, so that what follows may be more easily understood. 
In the first place—the spiritually sick are all those who are in 
a state of nature; all, without a single exception, who have 
sprung from the flesh and blood of Adam and Eve. 

In the second place—the sickness is nothing else than 
the depraved, sinful nature, which leads to everlasting 
death, and under this depravity all men are included, that 
God might have mercy upon all. Rom. 11:32. 

In the third place—Jesus Christ, with His grace, is the 
only physician of the afflicted soul, and not only for one, but 
for the entire race who became disobedient and depraved in 
Adam. God, the merciful Father of all, appointed Jesus as 
physician and Saviour of the perishing, after He had 
invested Him with all power and given Him a name above 
every other name, so that all who believe on Him should not 
perish, but that they might be saved and made happy. 

In the fourth place—the medicine with which this 
physician heals our spiritual maladies and makes whole all 
wounded hearts, is pre-eminently the word of God, together 


5 


with the blood of Jesus Christ, which He poured out for the 
forgiveness of sin; that is, for the healing of our sickness 
and our everlasting well-being. All the prescriptions of this 
Divine Balm which are to secure the restoration of man or 
the salvation of sinners, must embrace the death, the 
martyrdom and blood-shedding of Christ. 

The Holy Spirit is the one who prepares and applies 
the balm of life to the afflicted souls of men. 

This world is a hospital, filled with afflicted, dying 
souls. We are all by nature sick and lying helpless before 
God, until awakened by the Son of God, and brought from 
the pestilential air of sin into a holy atmosphere, and 
separated from those who are still diseased. Should anyone 
regard the hospital as more appropriately symbolized by the 
Church, I will not object; for it is indeed true that the great 
Physician is ever active in His church in restoring the 
afflicted to health, as the good Samaritan carried the 
wounded man to an inn and had him cared for; provided 
that the spiritually sick whom Christ conducts into His fold 
do not continue to bear upon them the tokens of disease, 
but grow better from day to day. 

What, then, is the healthfulness of the soul? It is 
nothing less than righteousness, holiness, and salvation. The 
person made whole is the new inward man; that is, created 
after God, in holiness, righteousness and truth, whom Paul 
admonishes us to put on, that we may be renewed in the 
spirit of our minds. Eph. 4:23. 

The way of approach to this physician is the prayer of 
faith. Without faith it is impossible to please God; for he that 
cometh to God must believe that He is, and that He is a 
rewarder of all them that diligently seek Him. Heb. 11. 

In like manner as every physician aims to produce 
certain results by the mode of his practice, thus also does 
our heavenly Physician secure to those who submit to His 
treatment, regeneration, the forgiveness of sin, their 


6 


reception of the Holy Ghost, justification and sanctification; 
and in this state of holiness we are admonished to advance. 
And as those who have relieved men from bodily disease 
prescribe certain regulations of diet and exercise for the 
preservation of health, so also has our Physician, Jesus 
Christ, given rules in his Gospel and through His apostles, 
whereby our spiritual health is preserved, viz: that we 
should no longer sin, but lead a godly life and keep His 
commandments. We behold therefore in these particulars 
the entire course of treatment observed by Jesus Christ with 
His afflicted ones, whom He heals, besides other matters 
which will hereafter be more extensively treated in several 
chapters. May the Almighty and merciful God grant to us 
poor afflicted sinners grace, that we may faithfully rely on 
the Physician, Jesus, and make us partakers of His healing 
power unto everlasting life. Amen. 


CHAPTER 1 
Jesus Christ, the Divine Physician, and miserable, sinful 
Man, the Patient, the Wounded, or Sick. 


When our beloved Lord and Master, Jesus Christ, called 
Matthew, the publican, to become His disciple, that he might 
be an apostle and evangelist, the latter made a feast in 
honor to his Lord, and many publicans and sinners sat at 
table with Him and His disciples. But when the scribes and 
Pharisees saw this, they murmured and said to his disciples, 
Why does your Master eat with publicans and sinners? When 
Jesus heard this, he said unto them: The whole need not the 
physician, but they that are sick; but go learn what this 
meaneth, I will have mercy and not sacrifice; for I am not 
come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance. 
Matthew 9:13, Luke 5:31,32. 

Among other profitable things which our Lord Jesus 
presents and teaches in this portion of the Gospel, he first 
pictures the depravity, the inherited disease and misery of 
human nature, in connection with the grace of Almighty 
God, and His own exalted office as the anointed of God, 
which He performs in the restoration of man to the Divine 
favor. As man through the disobedience and fall of Adam 
became thoroughly depraved, diseased and frail, and now, 
as a poor sinner, stands in need of mercy, grace and help: 
thus, on the other hand, Jesus is the true Saviour, the only 
one who can heal the soul—the Physician, the Helper and 
the Redeemer of man. 

Our heavenly Father, after investing his Son with all 
power, and giving him a name above every other name, 
offered him to all men as their physician and Saviour, and 
clothed Him with the office of healing the spiritually 
diseased: i.e. He is given to save us from our sins; therefore 
shall the sin-sick seek no other physician, but hasten to 
Jesus and confide only in His help and counsel. For such 


8 


power and like office and remedies are nowhere else found, 
neither can they appertain to anyone but Him; and he who 
presumes to cure souls by human power and wisdom, will 
only destroy them and conduct them to ruin. 

Above all things should we diligently, by the exercise 
of Christian faith, study, ponder, understand, and impress 
upon our minds what we are by nature, what advantage 
Christ the Son of God is to us, and why He became 
incarnate, and what the efficacy and strength of His grace 
accomplish in us; how and by what means this Divine 
Physician of our souls is to meet the wants and remove the 
woes of our being. This He images in this parable and by 
many other beautiful representations in the Gospel; so that 
a sorrowful sinner, who ponders these things and believes 
the word of God, finds his heart overflowing with joy, for the 
word of the Lord is true, and what He has spoken, He will 
surely perform. Ps. 33:4. 

Therefore I have, through the grace of God, to the 
praise of Jesus our Lord, and for the comfort of all afflicted 
and contrite consciences something to write concerning this 
physician and his heavenly balm, (as also concerning the 
sickness) that I may soothe the distressed. Let us however 
also consider and be admonished in regard to original sin 
and its fatal tendencies, which we have inherited from 
Adam, that we may more fully understand our state by 
nature, to the end that we may more earnestly seek the 
help and balm of this great Physician, and that, in the lively 
exercise of faith, we may obediently submit to his healing 
power. 


CHAPTER 2 

Concerning the Visitation of the Afflicted, and how Christ, 
through his manifold miracles and benevolence, manifested 
himself as the Everlasting Physician of Souls. 


In the same manner as a faithful physician of the body takes 
in charge the sick and wounded; comforts and prescribes for 
them, using his best efforts to help them; eats and drinks 
with them; is considerate in respect to everything that can 
promote their recovery, or refresh and alleviate their minds, 
and patiently endures the unpleasant air of the sick-room, 
so that he may be of service to them—thus also has Jesus, 
our faithful soul-physician, subjected himself to the 
humiliations incident to our humanity, that He might deliver 
us from our woes. He compassionated the misery of 
depraved man, and was not ashamed of calling the humble 
His brethren. Even now He is present with every weeping 
penitent, neither does His love shrink from communion with 
the fallen, for whose sake He laid aside His glory and honor 
in heaven and descended to the dust of the earth; yea, He 
humbled himself to the death of the cross that He might 
heal mankind. 

The fact that we are permitted to enjoy, without 
interruption, his offices and his spiritual balm, should greatly 
contribute to the comfort and assurance of the timid 
conscience. Our gracious Lord Jesus manifested in the 
commencement of His miracles, wrought for the benefit of 
man, the purposes of his mission. He healed the sick, gave 
sight to the blind, hearing to the deaf, made the lame walk, 
cleansed the impure, set free those possessed of devils, and 
restored the dead to life, that by these outward benefits to 
man, He might symbolize those spiritual deliverances and 
mercies which the soul obtains through Him. He healed all 
manner of disorders among the people that He might 
thereby show that, after the accomplishment of His 


10 


sufferings and His ascension to heaven, where He opened 
the treasury of grace, He would, through the operations of 
the Holy Spirit, do for sin-sick souls what He had done for 
the bodies of men. 

For in truth men are not otherwise constituted in the 
sight of God, in that which pertains to their spiritual nature, 
than those afflicted with divers disorders, such as lunatics 
and those possessed of evil spirits, whom Jesus healed 
according to the flesh. By nature, man is dead in sin, is 
possessed of the evil spirit, is blind, is deaf to the voice of 
God, dumb and offensive in the sight of the Lord; that is, he 
is morally diseased, miserable, and full of sin. These inward 
woes, connected with the depravity of the carnal man, were 
fully exposed by the Redeemer, and while healing the bodies 
of men, He assured them that He was also prepared to cure 
the spiritual maladies of the soul. 

The Eternal Word, Jesus Christ, therefore assumed 
our nature and died, that He might awaken the soul from its 
death in sin, and deliver it out of that eternal condemnation 
into which it was plunged by the fall of Adam. It was the 
gracious purpose of our Lord to deliver man from the 
dominion of evil. He desired to open the eyes of his mind 
and the ears of his understanding, and enrich him with a 
perfect righteousness, with Divine wisdom and truth, that 
his conscience might rejoice in the assurance that Jesus had 
made him efficient to every good work to the praise of God. 

What other meaning can we attach to the parable in 
Luke, concerning him who fell among thieves and 
murderers? Poor, afflicted man should console himself with 
the fact that that which the unfortunate man experienced in 
bodily comfort from the good Samaritan, the wounded soul 
receives like care and greater mercy from Jesus, for it is the 
privilege of all to enjoy the healing virtues of that balm 
which our heavenly Physician has brought to earth. 


11 


That the Redeemer performs these offices and is 
ready to bestow these blessings, is evident from that 
exhibition of love and compassion manifested in that 
amazing condescension which led Him into fellowship with 
afflicted and wretched sinners. He associated with the poor, 
the despised and ignorant; He taught them, walked with 
them, and did eat and drink with publicans and sinners, as 
the Gospel bears witness. Thus had the Son of God to risk 
the good opinion of men, and as a faithful physician He had 
to endure the reproach and all that was disagreeable in the 
fallen, so that He was held and adjudged, in the estimation 
of the Pharisees, as the companion of these men, and was 
regarded also as a sinner, a Samaritan, and as one allied 
with the devil. John 8:48. O, the amazing greatness of the 
love wherewith Christ Jesus loved us! Would that we might 
cherish a corresponding love in return, and that we might 
have hearts forever overflowing with gratitude to Him and 
our heavenly Father for such transcendent goodness.

CHAPTER 3 
Concerning the Office of this Physician and the Sick whom 
he is to Heal, and how they must Realize that they are Sick. 


The Gospel narrative informs us that, after the scribes and 
Pharisees, the high priest and rulers of the people, saw that 
the Lord did not shun such despised persons as publicans 
and sinners, but received invitations to their social 
entertainments, and made Himself acquainted with them, 
they murmured and, being displeased, spoke disparagingly 
of the Lord: “Behold a man, gluttonous and a wine-bibber, 
the friend of publicans and sinners.” Matt 11:19. As if they 
would say—were He such a holy man as He is esteemed by 
some, and a true prophet; yea, were He the Messiah, as He 
pretends to be, He would doubtless conduct Himself with 
more dignity; He would not associate so much with 
disreputable people. 

The Lord answered them with great prudence and 
propriety: “A physician is not for the whole and the strong, 
for they do not require his services, but for the weak and 
sick.” By which He meant to show that He was a physician 
who came expressly for the sake of the afflicted, and that 
His mission was confined only to such as were needy and 
lowly. Further, He declared what kind of physician He was, 
viz: for the soul, the heavenly physician of all mankind, 
wherefore He says—“I came not to call the righteous, but 
sinners, to repentance.” 

After He had given the Pharisees to understand 
plainly that the objections which they raised against Him, by 
which they meant to disparage His character, namely, His 
fellowship with sinners, were rather a commendation, since 
the object of His labors was to lead them to repentance. 
This was a work of grace. Let those sinners, then, who 
receive this Physician with a view to obtain spiritual health, 
and who earnestly desire to become acceptable to God 


13 


through Him, understand that it becomes them henceforth 
to love righteousness, to lay aside all evil, and to 
perseveringly pursue a chaste and godly life. Who, then, are 
the spiritually sick, according to the teachings of the Gospel? 
Assuredly those who have felt their condition, and who, in 
obedience to the call of Christ, have come out from the 
world, and having been healed by his grace, now compose 
the congregation of Jesus, for whose sake he came from 
heaven into our world. For this reason did the Son of God 
become flesh, that He might call sinful men to repentance, 
regenerate them, sanctify them, and make their hearts His 
abode. The Word became clothed with our disordered 
humanity, that He might restore it to a healthful and holy 
condition—that the soul, the heart and the conscience, 
naturally impure, might be cleansed from the pollutions of 
sin. “And the redeemed shall come to Zion, and unto them 
that turn from transgression in Jacob, saith the Lord. As for 
me, this is my covenant with them, saith the Lord. My spirit 
that is upon thee, and my words that I have put into thy 
mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the 
mouth of thy seed.” 

The Lord Jesus moreover unfolded to the arrogant 
Pharisees the state of their own hearts, of which they were 
profoundly ignorant, in that He says, I am come not on 
account of the righteous, that is, for the sake of those who 
are lifted up with a sense of their personal holiness, and 
comparing others with their standard of moral excellence, 
condemn them, of which we have a forcible illustration in 
Luke 18:11-14, in the Pharisee and the publican. For the 
benefit of such I did not come (says our Lord;) for they have 
no need of the balm of life, since, trusting in their personal 
goodness, are not conscious that they need my help. Why? 
Because they are their own physicians, believe they know 
other healing sources, and notwithstanding they discover 
some tokens of disease about them, they do not see 


14 


themselves as God sees them, poor, miserable and sick, but 
such only Jesus came to heal and to save. The humble who 
are painfully conscious of their spiritual malady, who 
understand the plague of their hearts, who acknowledge 
their wretched depravity, who lament and mourn over their 
sins; and who also heartily desire to obtain health from God 
—i.e., grace and piety; these are the persons whom I came 
to redeem. 

Such are, with Zaccheus, truly Abraham's children, to 
whom is promised the kingdom of heaven. To such our Lord 
declares shall be imparted help, consolation and life. As the 
holy Jews (as they esteemed themselves, but who were 
really full of evil) murmured when He entered into the house 
of Zaccheus, who was regarded as a sinner, to enjoy his 
hospitality, He rebuked them for their want of charity when 
He said: “The son of man is come to seek and to save that 
which was lost.” Which means, that Jesus Christ, the 
heavenly physician, came to visit and to heal the manifold 
infirmities of poor sinners, who before God are sick, corrupt 
and estranged from the life of God, and dead under the 
dominion of evil. 

In like manner do we read in the Gospel according to 
St. John, where the miracle of restoring sight to one born 
blind is recorded: “I came to judge the world, so that those 
that are blind may be made to see, and those who see may 
become blind.” Those who do not see, are those truly 
humble souls who realize their sinfulness, from which the 
blindness of the heart proceeds, and fly in the exercise of 
faith to Christ, the restorer of the blind, the enlightener of 
all souls who have recourse to Him for help. But those who 
are the seeing, of whom the Lord here speaks, are the proud 
Pharisees, who vainly supposed that they saw and 
understood all things, but who were totally ignorant of their 
evil and malicious hearts; therefore must their condition 
remain as it is, and their sins rest upon their own heads. 


15 


Jesus desired to convey the same idea in that Gospel 
record where he says: “The whole need not the physician, 
but the sick, and I came to call sinners and not the 
righteous to repentance.” As if he had said: There is come 
from heaven a physician to this world with extraordinary 
powers, in order that through Him the sick may be made 
whole and the whole may become sick. That the poor in God 
might be rich, and the rich might become poor; that the 
sinner might be justified and the righteous might be 
condemned, that is, lost forever. This is seldom understood, 
for sinful man finds it too high to comprehend it, but he who 
repents and is converted and receives from God the spirit of 
humility and love, feels the plague of his heart and receives 
Christ with joy as the physician of his soul. 


16 


CHAPTER 4 

Concerning the Faithfulness, Love and Mercy of the Divine 
Physician, as illustrated in the Faith, Consolation and Hope 
which he awakens in Afflicted Souls. 


And now I will proclaim and praise the faithfulness and love 
of our most gracious Physician, Jesus Christ, and according 
to my humble ability write further concerning Him, if but one 
among the many thousands of spiritually sick will but lay his 
heart at the feet of Jesus, commit himself entirely to His 
healing power, and seek in Him his true health, I shall be 
amply rewarded. Rather than see our afflicted and wounded 
humanity sink into perdition and die the second death, our 
magnanimous Physician suffered himself to be wounded and 
smitten for man, became afflicted with our disease, so that 
from the crown of His head to the sole of His feet there were 
wounds and bruises; yea, not only was He greatly afflicted, 
but He even died for us. His death was both ignominious 
and painful, viz: the death of the cross, that wretched man 
might be healed through His pains and blood. Thus is the 
sinner made whole by the affliction, and is made alive by 
the death of his Physician. Through His shame, man is 
raised to honor, glory and immortality. 

“In this was manifested the love of God toward us, 
because that God sent his only begotten Son into the world, 
that we might live through him. And we have seen and do 
testify that the Father sent the Son to be the Saviour of the 
world.” 1 John 4:9,14. “My little children, these things write 
I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an 
advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous. And he 
is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but 
also for the sins of the whole world.” All of which is made 
known to afflicted mankind, and is designed for the 
consolation of all sinners, through the faithfulness and 
compassion of Jesus Christ, our heavenly Physician. 


17 


Saint Paul says to his beloved Ephesians—“Wherefore 
I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus; that he 
would grant you according to the riches of his glory, to be 
strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man. That 
Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith, that being rooted 
and grounded in love, you may be able to comprehend with 
all saints, what is the length and the breadth, and the height 
and the depth: And to know the love of Christ which passeth 
knowledge.” He would indicate that Christ loved us so 
intensely, and still loves us so ardently, that no man without 
special grace and extraordinary revelations from God can 
understand or portray that love. 

The Lord Jesus himself speaks of this amazing 
condescension when he discoursed with His disciples and 
said: “As my Father has loved me, even so have I loved 
you. No man has greater love than this, that he lay down his 
life for his friends.” And this Christ did, as He elsewhere 
declares: “The Son of man came not to be ministered unto, 
but to minister and to give his life a ransom for many.” 
Matt. 20:28. And again: “I am the good shepherd; a good 
shepherd lays down his life for his sheep.” John 10:11. And 
in the same chapter, v. 15: “I lay down my life for the 
sheep.” 

Now as Jesus is the only true shepherd who 
surrendered His life for the sheep, with whom they find 
green pastures and enjoy full happiness; thus also is He the 
merciful high priest and the bishop of our souls, who himself 
was tempted in all points as we are, yet without sin, so that 
He might sympathize with our weakness and succor those 
who are tempted. In like manner is He the only faithful 
physician, who gives and distributes His body and blood, 
yea, all that He has, for the restoration of many afflicted and 
poor sinners. That through Him lost souls may be delivered 
from sin and its evils, and through Jesus obtain salvation 
and peace, and be crowned with everlasting glory. 


18 


CHAPTER 5 

The heavenly Physician was himself afflicted, smitten and 
crucified, that miserable Man might be made alive through 
Him and have his beauty and health renewed. 


Call him what you will, there is no physician on earth who 
would subject himself to such sacrifices as the Redeemer 
endured for man. None other feels such an interest in his 
patients as to take upon himself their sufferings and 
languishings if he could, much less would consent to die for 
them. But Jesus, our precious, holy physician, very 
cheerfully endured the sufferings that were due to the 
guilty, and after having borne in His own person the griefs 
and sorrows of sinners, bestows upon penitent transgressors 
everlasting health. Finally, this Divine Physician took upon 
himself the shame of our entire afflicted race; as it is written 
where, through the spirit of prophecy, He recounts His 
sorrows: “Reproach has broken my heart and I am full of 
heaviness; and I looked for some to take pity, but there was 
none; and for comforters, but I found none. They gave me 
also gall for my meat, and in my thirst they gave me 
vinegar to drink.” Ps. 69:20,21. 

Concerning all these amazing transactions did Isaiah 
prophecy, long anterior to their occurrence, when casting his 
prophetic eye upon the suffering Messiah he exclaims: 
“Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows; 
yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God and 
afflicted.” That is, so blinded and perverted were men, that 
they did not apprehend the meaning and true significance of 
those agonies and reproaches endured by the Saviour. As 
they were ignorant of the great love of this physician, they 
never supposed that He designed to help them, but 
regarded Him as one who had himself become obnoxious to 
the law of God; in like manner as those still consider his 


19 


death, who are not moved with gratitude to God for a 
crucified Redeemer. 

“But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was 
bruised for our iniquities.” That is, on account of our misery 
did He assume these infirmities and griefs; for our sins did 
He appear upon the cross as one accursed, as a sinner and 
malefactor. Not that He had sinned, for in Him was no guile, 
but He there suffered in the room of sinners. “The 
chastisement of our peace was upon him, and with his 
stripes we are healed.” Isa. 53:5. Yea, that we might have 
everlasting peace in our conscience, in our souls and hearts, 
and that through His wounds we might be made whole. For 
those wounds are fountains of spiritual health, they send 
streams of healing over our afflicted and weary world. “All 
we like sheep had gone astray;” each one in his own way, 
(the way of perdition,) “but the Lord laid upon him the 
iniquity of us all.” O this is a consoling truth to the penitent 
sinner, also to the dying who devoutly ponder it, and to all 
who through faith and the Holy Spirit have rightly learned 
these precious truths and treasured them up in their hearts. 
Furthermore does the prophet continue: “Yet it pleased the 
Lord to bruise him; he hath put him to grief, when thou 
shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, 
he shall prolong his days,” (that is, He shall after His 
suffering rise again from the dead and through divine power 
bring forth humanity new and immortal,) “and the pleasure 
of the Lord shall prosper in his hands.” That is, the heavenly 
balm which issues from the Saviour’s wounds and the 
healing work which it shall accomplish upon poor sinners, 
will be carried forward by the hand of the Lord. Thus shall 
the sick be made whole, the sinner become righteous; 
because His soul labored on the cross for them in the 
martyrdom which He endured. And now that the balm of 
healing has been provided in the shedding of His blood, He 


20 


will behold his pleasure in the vivifying and restoring effects 
of the only medicine that cures the sin-sick soul. 

“He shall see the travail of his soul and be satisfied; 
by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many, 
for he shall bear their iniquities. Therefore will I divide him a 
portion with the great, and he shall divide the spoil with the 
strong; because he hath poured out his soul unto death.” 
Through the work of His mediation the Father has exalted 
him Lord over all, so that He reigns in the hearts of those 
afflicted sinners whom he heals, enriches with grace and 
sanctifies, while He exercises dominion over the sick and the 
whole, the dead and the living. 

Behold, then, thou inquirer after the Beautiful and the 
Good, this is our God, our Lord and Master, Jesus Christ, 
through whose help and knowledge alone we can become 
just and holy, and inherit everlasting life! O how precious 
and invaluable is this true knowledge of this heavenly 
physician, Jesus Christ! It is the only science (the science of 
salvation) which men may study through all their life 
without exhausting its treasures. The only one, by which 
they become learned, are made rich in experience, and 
perfect, since His knowledge, as He himself declares, brings 
with it everlasting life and makes us partakers of the divine 
nature. 

It should surprise no one, that unworthy as I am, that 
from my own experience and in the presence of all true 
believers, I bear testimony of His goodness, and to the 
extent of my ability strive to acquaint everyone with His love 
and beneficence. For I would proclaim it to all mankind, that 
I know Him to be the truly begotten Son of God, co-equal 
with the Father and the Holy Ghost. He has (to His praise be 
it spoken) manifested most tenderly His offices and skill in 
the application of this spiritual balm to my diseased soul; 
and continues to do so in the daily ministrations of the 
healing influences of His grace to my body and soul. When I 


21 


fall He raises me up; in seasons of trial He supports me; 
when in danger He delivers; when I err He guides me, when 
I go astray He brings me back; when I am smitten He pours 
oil into my wounds and heals me; and if I have need of 
anything, He provides for me. O, He is the faithful 
everlasting Father, as the prophet Isaiah calls Him, in 
chapter 9:6. What a debt of gratitude do I owe Him for His 
kindness in showing to me, His unworthy child, the true road 
(which He is himself) to the attainment of a good 
conscience, and to everlasting life. Truly He has delivered 
my soul from death, my feet from falling, that I might walk 
before God in the light of life.— Ps. 56:13. 

What wilt thou say, O my soul, in the presence of 
such transcendent love? “It is a faithful saying, and one 
worthy of all acceptation, that Jesus Christ came into the 
world to save sinners, of whom I am chief.” Aye, for this 
reason He receives me in mercy, though so unworthy that I 
might be under the greater obligation to adore His love—to 
admire His grace and truth—to show forth His divine glory, 
that others, now in a lost and ruined state, might be induced 
to seek Him as their only Saviour. Wherefore should we not 
then praise this man, Jesus of Nazareth, and receive Him, in 
all His offices, as our Lord, and strive to magnify Him in the 
estimation of others? To the God-Man we can never ascribe 
too much honor. I could wish, therefore, with all my heart, 
that God the Father would grant to all the afflicted and 
sorrowful, a saving knowledge of His Son Jesus. For 
whoever knows this physician, and apprehends him by the 
eye of faith as the source of help, is already relieved, and all 
His wounds will be healed by the Lord of glory. He has peace 
and joy; for this physician can, by His almighty power, so 
thoroughly heal the deepest wounds that sin has inflicted, 
that they are altogether obliterated in the sight of God, and 
there will remain nothing upon the renovated soul that 
would make it an object of displeasure to the Most High. The 


22 


evil conscience is displaced by a conscience at peace with 
Heaven; and in this state of grace there is a consciousness 
of the indwelling righteousness and love of God. Yea, 
through the efficacy of His power, “He renews man’s youth 
like the eagle’s, and crowns him with grace and glory.” Ps. 
103:4,5. So pre-eminent is the skill of this physician, that 
none of those afflicted souls who commit themselves to His 
care, and abide under His direction to the end, shall ever 
perish or taste the second death. 


23 


CHAPTER 6 
The Gracious Invitation of the Holy Ghost, to the Afflicted, 
to come to Jesus Christ, the heavenly Physician. 


With a view to appreciate more fully and to admire more 
joyfully the love of God, as manifested in the gift of His Son, 
let us carefully ponder what Paul writes in Rom. 5:6, &c.: 
“For when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ 
died for the ungodly. For scarcely for a righteous man will 
one die; yet peradventure for a good man one would even 
dare to die. But God commendeth his love towards us, in 
that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. Much 
more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be 
saved from wrath through him. For if when we were 
enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, 
much more being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life, 
and not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord 
Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the 
atonement.” 

This is a very precious portion of scripture, full of 
spiritual wealth, containing the invitation of the Holy Spirit 
to afflicted souls, and in which the entire office of our Divine 
Physician is embraced, admired and unfolded. This part of 
the Holy Word is very appropriate to dying believers, and 
should by all means be read and expounded to those on a 
dying bed, that their affections may be directed to Christ, 
since there is nothing which displays the immeasurable love 
and mercy of God towards us poor sinners so fully as the 
bitter sufferings and death of our Lord Jesus. What can be 
better fitted to strengthen our faith and to pacify our 
consciences, than the remembrance that the beloved Son of 
God offered himself up as a sacrifice for the ungodly, for His 
and His Father’s enemies? That He is our Redeemer and has 
paid the price of our ransom? Where can you find a 
physician who would without compensation administer a 


24 


remedy to his mortal enemy who might again attempt his 
life? Who would take upon himself a grievous illness, to 
relieve a sworn foe from it? We will not even mention such a 
thing as to die for another. Where did it ever happen in this 
world, that anyone died for an unrighteous person? Perhaps 
some good person might die for one whom he loves. Many 
also die for the public good, and a few, as history informs 
us, have laid down their life for the cause of truth. 

But Jesus Christ, our gracious and holy Lord, died for 
the ungodly, forasmuch as He died while we were yet 
sinners, without strength and ungodly, yea, while we were 
His enemies. How much more will He now, (after the 
accomplishment of His cruel death and having poured out 
His blood for us,) being risen, and living, and reigning as our 
Mediatorial King, vouchsafe to us the blessings of His 
salvation? For if through the death of Christ we were 
reconciled to God, how much more beneficial to us is His 
Divine Life in heaven, where the God-Man, in and with God, 
triumphs and rules? He is our advocate with the Father, and 
not only shields us from the wrath of God and heals our 
spiritual maladies, but He bestows on us the Holy Ghost, 
gives the spirit of adoption and love, in which we rejoice in 
God, as heirs of the kingdom of heaven and of all heavenly 
riches, and receive them as our own. The sainted Paul, in 
the scripture already cited, proclaims for the everlasting 
consolation of the wretched children of earth, the riches of 
Divine grace in Jesus Christ. Why should we not avail 
ourselves of all the good, the healing power and comfort 
which this gracious physician offers so freely to all? Why 
should we not desire to have this restorer of the soul, and 
greatly delight in His presence? Why not love him and follow 
his advice? And who would not, if he has only learned to 
know this Saviour, unite with the pious David in the 
beautiful psalm and sing: “O, give thanks unto the Lord, for 
he is good, and his mercy endureth for ever. Let the 


25 


redeemed of the Lord say so, whom he hath redeemed from 
the hand of the enemy. And gathered them out of the lands, 
from the east and from the west, from the north and from 
the south. They wandered in the wilderness in a solitary 
way, they found no city to dwell in. Hungry and thirsty, their 
soul fainted in them. Then they cried unto the Lord in their 
trouble and he delivered them out of their distresses. And 
he led them forth by the right way, that they might go to a 
city of habitation. O, that men would praise the Lord for his 
goodness and for his wonderful works to the children of 
men!” Ps. 107. Come, Lord Jesus, come and relieve our 
sickness; take away our sins and imperfections, make us 
whole and strengthen us, that we may serve thee to the 
praise of thy glory! 


26 


CHAPTER 7 
The Skill of our heavenly Physician, and his own Invitation 
to the Afflicted to come unto him. 


To the eternal praise of Jesus, I will yet relate a brief 
account from Isaiah, which contains those testimonials of 
skill which He, like all other unknown physicians, publishes 
to the world. “The spirit of the Lord God is upon me: 
because the Lord God is upon me: because the Lord hath 
anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; He hath 
sent me to bind up the broken-hearted—to proclaim liberty 
to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that 
are bound; to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord, and 
the day of vengeance of our God—to comfort all that mourn; 
to appoint unto them that mourn in Zion—to give unto them 
beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning—the garment 
of praise for the spirit of heaviness, that they might be 
called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that 
he might be glorified.”—Isa. 61:1-3. 

Behold, then, here you have the consoling credentials 
of our spiritual physician, setting forth His ability and the 
efficacy of His healing balm, on the testimony of the 
prophet, who, through the Holy Spirit, predicted, long before 
our Saviour’s incarnation, the character he would exhibit 
and the offices he would fill. When the Redeemer made His 
appearance in the synagogue at Nazareth, he opened the 
Scriptures and read the above quotation from Isaiah, and 
having closed the book, said—“To-day is this scripture 
fulfilled before your eyes, and they all bore witness, and 
were astonished at the gracious words that proceeded out of 
his mouth.” 

In like manner does our Lord, in other parts of the 
Gospel, graciously invite everyone to become a partaker of 
His grace—“Come unto me, all ye that labor and are heavy 
laden, and I will give you rest.” To me, saith the Lord, come 


27 


only to me to whom the Father has given all power, and 
appointed me the divine physician, the deliverer and Saviour 
of mankind. To him shall all afflicted, sorrowful hearts come, 
notwithstanding their spiritual maladies—let them cheerfully 
and freely come. I will give you rest from your sins, rest 
from the heavy and intolerable yoke and burden of an 
accusing conscience, and will heal your soul. 

This matchless physician, Jesus Christ, as He more 
fully represents His faithfulness and the singular efficacy of 
His balm, does not appear clothed with that pomp and 
worldly honor that sometimes invest other physicians, but 
comes before men without ostentation and clothed with 
humility as He further speaks of His office. “You will no 
doubt address to me the proverb, Physician, heal thyself.” 
As if He would say, I do not seek my own honor and profit, 
or well-being, with my medicine, as others who practice the 
healing art, who often seek not the interest of the sick, but 
who desire to enrich themselves and their friends by their 
art. I seek to benefit the sick to the praise of God my 
Father, who sent me upon this mission, that I might give 
everything, even myself, to their advantage, that 
deliverance might be brought to them, as it is foretold by 
the prophet. For I will restore health unto thee, and I will 
heal thee of thy wounds, saith the Lord, because they called 
thee an outcast, saying: “This is Zion, whom no man 
seeketh after.” Jer. 30:17. That is the flock of Christ, which 
is still in the eyes of the world unprofitable, and its members 
are esteemed as outcasts and self-righteous fanatics. To 
such an office of the spiritual healing art was the God-Man, 
Jesus of Nazareth, set apart and anointed by the Father with 
the fulness of the Holy Ghost. He was instinct with the oil of 
joy, and filled with heavenly balm, so that He shed around 
His path a delicious fragrance, and that He might always, 
while the day of grace endures, proclaim joyful news, heal 
the sick, enlighten the ignorant, comfort the distressed, and 


28 


strengthen and refresh the weary pilgrims of earth. But who 
will participate in these blessings? Surely those only who 
realize that they are poor, afflicted, blind, but who, while 
they lament their wretchedness, exercise faith in the 
Redeemer. Take heed, therefore, beloved reader, that you 
may properly understand your sickness, and daily renew 
your resolution to seek health for your soul in the wounds of 
Jesus, prayerfully consider also his divine calling; for all 
these things, as already stated, redound to the glory of God. 
For the more earnestly we seek him, and the more 
confidently we commit our souls to his care, so much the 
more do we honor His unspeakable love which He 
manifested towards the children of men. 

Concerning all this does the prophet Isaiah truly 
predict when he exclaims: “Ah, sinful nation, a people laden 
with iniquity, a seed of evildoers, children that are 
corruptors! they have forsaken the Lord; they have 
provoked the Holy One of Israel unto anger; they are gone 
away backward, &c. The whole head is sick and the heart is 
faint. From the sole of the foot, even unto the head, there is 
no soundness in it, but wounds and bruises and putrefying 
sores; they have not been closed, neither bound up, neither 
mollified with ointment.” Isa. 1:4,6. And Jeremiah, chap. 
15:18 says: “Why is my pain perpetual, and my wound 
incurable, which refuseth to be healed.” “Heal me, O Lord, 
and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved.” Jer. 
17:14. Behold how the Spirit of God, through the prophets, 
portrays the depravity of man and his spiritual sickness 
under the imagery of the ancient economy, and presents the 
Jewish people as examples to us of those whose afflictions 
are confessed in these prophecies. Mark also how he speaks 
of sin as a fatal disease, and represents it by wounds which 
no one can heal but God. The Holy Spirit further delineates 
the unspeakable evil effects produced by sin, that feeling 
the wretchedness it occasions and the anguish it kindles, 


29 


they might, under a consciousness of their sad state look 
forward with more earnest yearnings to the promised 
physician as the restorer of Israel. 

That sin is the most intolerable plague that can befall 
man; yea, so agonizing and distressing to the soul, that 
even the worst of bodily diseases known on earth afford but 
an imperfect emblem of its malignity and virulence—those 
will most readily concede who have been properly convicted, 
and felt it as the gnawings of the undying worm in their 
conscience. O Lord, my God, if there were no advice, no 
hope, and no comfort held out to the awakened sinner in the 
Gospel, what would be the result? Did not the gracious 
Physician come to the sinner’s rescue, his nature could not 
long endure the fear and sorrow, but would sink under the 
dread burden. 

The individual who has such a knowledge of his 
depravity, is prepared to pour out the experience of his 
heart in the seven penitential and other Psalms, and cry 
with David, “Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy 
great compassion, for mine iniquities have gone over my 
head, they are a burden too heavy to be borne. I am sore, 
broken and bruised. I cry and have no rest in my heart, my 
tears have been my meat and drink. My heart within me 
faileth. Fearfulness and trembling have come upon me, and 
horror has seized my soul. O God, help me, for thy name’s 
sake, for the terrors of death encompass me and the pains 
of hell gat hold of me.” 

From this it may be inferred what a sore and 
intolerable evil sin must be; for he who can thus speak of 
his spiritual sickness without hypocrisy, as David certainly 
did, must assuredly first realize fear, anguish, and the need 
of help. He must have been sorrowful, weak and sick 
personally, and to such an extent that he could pray for the 
necessities of all the pious, and lament before God the evil 
that attaches to our humanity. When this is felt, then is it 


30 


the proper time to call on the physician, and speedily go to 
Him and commit the healing of the soul to Him. Then may 
the afflicted turn to the true Physician with a lively faith and, 
awaiting His help, say: “O Lord, heal me, for I am sick. O 
God, speak peace to my soul. Say, I am thy salvation (Lord 
Jesus) while there is no soundness in me.” 

Behold, the Lord presents still more gracious 
promises, through his prophets! “Let all those that seek thee 
rejoice and be glad in thee: let such as love thy salvation 
say continually, The Lord be magnified.” Ps. 40:16. Such 
promises are a sure testimony and a solid ground of hope, 
that all who seek our Lord Jesus with an earnest heart and 
in faith, will speedily be delivered by him from the terrible 
evil of sin and be made joyful and whole. 

The matter resolves itself, therefore, into this, as 
intimated: let those who truly realize their sinful condition, 
and who feel the plague of their own hearts, earnestly long 
after the physician, Jesus Christ; for if they do feel their 
misery they will yield up everything in order to obtain help. 
For such are the tortures of an accusing conscience, that 
they are fitly represented by the gnawings of the undying 
worm, and then in this soul agony there is a pressure on 
every side, and the whole world is but a prison of the 
immortal spirit, and the awful burden of guilt is such that if 
not removed by Jesus, it will sink the soul into the depths of 
despair, where it will experience the pangs of the second 
death. O it is amid the dark horrors of that hour of 
indescribable misery when the agitated spirit rushes into the 
arms of Jesus, that His worth is appreciated and the 
vastness of His benevolence is felt. 

It would be truly wonderful if he could be joyful who 
has a realizing sense of his spiritual sickness, and properly 
considers the fatal effects in which it must issue, if not 
cured; strange indeed if he, destitute of Jesus Christ and 
without his balm, could be otherwise than miserable in the 


31 


extreme. We have numerous examples in Holy Scripture of 
the wretchedness which an awakened conscience produces. 
One, and a noble example, exclaims: “I have eaten ashes 
like bread, and mingled my drink with tears.” Another says: 
“Whether I eat or drink, wake or sleep, or whatever I do, 
there is a voice ringing in my ears, arise ye dead and come 
to judgment.” This dreadful depravity and this lost condition 
of the unregenerate soul, is discovered by some only in the 
hour of death, when they have a foretaste of the first 
judgment of God; and if the physician, Jesus Christ, does 
not come to their rescue they are lost forever, for without 
Christ and His balm none can inherit everlasting life. 
Therefore I said, that such a spiritually sick and 
anguished person, without Christ, will find this world too 
narrow for him, since he feels the painfulness of those 
wounds which sin has made, and cannot obtain rest, 
because the terrors of perdition have laid hold upon him; so 
that he may very truly exclaim with the affrighted David: 
“Oh that I had wings like a dove! for then would I fly away 
and be at rest. Lo, then would I wander far off, and remain 
in the wilderness.” Ps. 55:6,7. And again he says: “Save 
me, O God, for the waters are come in unto my soul. I sink 
in deep mire, where there is no standing; I am come into 
deep waters, where the floods overflow me.” Ps. 69:1,2. 


32 


CHAPTER 8 

Forasmuch as the sickness of the soul is a weighty calamity, 
so much the more should we rejoice in the gracious and 
consoling healing power of our Lord Jesus Christ. 


That the sickness of the soul is such an alarming and great 
evil that no one can advise or afford relief but the physician, 
Jesus Christ, God has clearly revealed through the prophet. 
“For thus saith the Lord: Thy bruise is incurable, and thy 
wound is grievous. There is none to plead thy cause, that 
thou mayest be bound up: thou hast no healing medicines. 
For I will restore health unto thee, and I will heal thee of thy 
wounds.” Jer. 30:12,13,17. And in Psalm 147:3, “He healeth 
the broken in heart and bindeth up their wounds.” 

In a case of bodily disease, where the malady is an 
obstinate and dangerous one, praise will redound to the skill 
of the physician, in proportion to the difficulties which He 
overcomes in the restoration of health. We should above all 
things consider the inexpressible virulence of our spiritual 
sickness; forasmuch as the soul is far nobler than the body, 
thus also, without doubt, is the malady with which it is 
afflicted greater, more dangerous and more intolerable than 
all the plagues which may visit the body. As it is manifest, 
moreover, that persons who are suffering under bodily 
indisposition, or as they conceive are in the agonies of 
death, send a great distance and without reference to 
expense for those who are celebrated physicians, and as 
these afflicted ones rejoice when those skilled in the healing 
art arrive to prescribe for them, are comforted by their 
presence, and even derive hope and improvement from the 
words and the looks of the physician: thus also can believers 
learn and appreciate the immense value, the divine power 
and loveliness of the soul’s Physician from this image of the 
feelings and experience of those who are languishing under 
bodily pains. As the sickness of the soul is severer, more 


33 


fatal and intolerable than that of the body, so much the 
more should those who are conscious of this rejoice that 
they know this eminent physician, Jesus Christ, that he is 
accessible to them; for they are already assured that He 
stands at their door and knocks, and only waits the pleasure 
of the sin-sick soul to invite Him, that He may help and heal. 
Rev. 3:20. 

O how touching is the attitude of our Lord Jesus 
towards us! Behold, daily is He stretching out His hand to 
deliver us from all manner of sickness and danger—yea, 
from all evil, as He is personified by Solomon under 
Wisdom, Prov. 1:24,25: “I have called, and ye refused; I 
have stretched out my hand and no man regarded. But ye 
have set at naught all my counsel, and would none of my 
reproof.” We are, however, lacking most of all in this, that 
as poor patients and foolish persons we do not properly 
realize the great evil, and the mortal sickness of our souls— 
nay, are sometimes unwilling to learn our true condition, or 
we would cheerfully and without intermission inquire for 
Jesus Christ and strive to obtain a knowledge of His healing 
balm. We would answer His call, and gladly hear of Him; 
yea, we would rejoice in His presence, and seek our highest 
consolation in His word and find His smiles the joy and 
sunlight of our souls. 


34 


CHAPTER 9 
The nature of Spiritual Sickness, and whence its origin. 


What is this sickness of the soul? It is nothing else, as 
already partially stated, but sin with its power, with its fear 
and its sting; this weighty and intolerable evil, and the 
depravity of our minds, as it is portrayed in one of the 
penitential Psalms of David: “O Lord, be merciful unto me, 
and heal my soul, for I have sinned against thee.” And 
again: “There is no soundness in my flesh because of thine 
anger; neither is there any rest in my bones, because of my 
sins.” “My wounds stink and are corrupt, because of my 
foolishness.” Here we see that the sickness and the wounds 
of the soul from which the prophet desired and prayed to be 
delivered was nothing else but sin, together with its 
energies and all the evil which it inflicts upon man. It 
includes also that unrest which always accompanies it in the 
soul, the undying worm, the trembling and fear of hell and 
the second death, and the wrath and trouble from an 
unreconciled God, with which the conscience of the 
righteous soul is tormented. It is the torment and pain, yea, 
the everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord 
and glory of his power. 2 Thes. 1:9. 

For sin divorces the soul forever from that God who is 
the highest and the most unspeakably glorious good. “Your 
iniquities,” saith the Holy Ghost, in Isa. 59:2, “separate 
between you and your God, and your sins have hid His face, 
that your prayers are not heard.” Such as the dead and 
already offensive Lazarus whom the Lord called from the 
sleep of death would appear in the eyes of an effeminate 
earthly king, so does much more the corrupt sinner appear 
in the sight of the pure God and Jesus, the king of heaven. 
Therefore it must needs be that sin—that is, this soul- 
sickness—where it is not removed, will separate from God 
and rob the individual of the divine glory. Sin also makes 


35 


angels sorrowful, injures our neighbor, and divides the 
communion of saints. Sin, where it triumphs, grieves the 
Holy Ghost, dispels the energies of the inner man, and 
conducts into gross darkness, yea, into the terrors of the 
second death. 

How could we conceive of a more dreadful sickness, 
or plague, or misfortune, than that which may not only be 
attended with fatal effects to the diseased, but breathes a 
blighting and destructive influence upon those who associate 
with him? And as no one can long endure the odor that 
arises from a decomposing corpse, so neither can angels or 
the children of the Most High (much less God himself) 
endure the offensiveness and fellowship of gross impenitent 
sinners, unless indeed they are led astray and poisoned by 
the wicked. This disease of the soul, like a cancer, eats its 
way, and as it consumes the inner moral strength, it 
destroys and triumphs over the outer man. 1 Cor. 5:11. Ina 
word, soul-sickness is the most injurious and distressing 
evil. It is the undying worm in the sinful heart of man, which 
in several portions of the Psalms, and also in other parts of 
Holy Scripture, is variously designated, but has always 
associated with it an image of the greatest distress and 
pain. It is called the bands of death, the pains of hell, 
thunder and lightning, fire and brimstone. For as an object 
struck by the thunderbolt is set on fire by the lightning, so 
also is the conscience of the ungodly sinner smitten by the 
thunders of divine wrath through the convictions of the 
Spirit and kindled with the fires of remorse that burn and 
agitate the sinner, so that no one can extinguish it but the 
God-Man, the heavenly physician, Jesus Christ. 

O God, help us, that we may all be delivered in this 
world from this dreadful illness, and escape the awful 
vengeance of fire, by being made whole and saved. It 
should, however, be carefully remarked that this original 
sickness is internal and affects the heart, the soul and the 


36 


conscience; that the injury is not merely in the flesh, but 
extends to the whole depraved nature; that it works from 
within and diffuses its poison through the whole being, just 
as a cancer enters the blood and is carried through the 
whole body,—so that the effects of this disease are seen in 
the words and works of man; yea, even in matters which he 
may deem blameless. As, therefore, the seat of this malady 
is within, it becomes necessary to copy the example of those 
eminent in the healing art, and attack the foundations of the 
disease, pull it up by its roots, or it will remain unsubdued 
and break out afresh; and the oftener it is allowed to do 
this, the worse it will become. 

Our physician, Jesus Christ, fully acquainted with the 
nature of the plague of our hearts, begins to restore health 
and the fountain of life, that his spiritual health may unfold 
itself in a life of penitence and love and holiness. Our 
gracious deliverer opens the heart through the quickening 
influences of His Spirit and purifies it through the faith which 
He inspires. “Out of the (natural) heart proceed evil 
thoughts, adultery, fornication, uncleanliness, murder, theft, 
avarice, false witness, lust, pride and folly.” The renewed 
heart is filled with opposite affections, and being thus 
occupied there is no place for that which is unholy. 

I felt anxious to present these views, for the reason 
that there are teachers and preachers who have no just 
conceptions of this spiritual sickness, neither do they 
understand its nature, nor recognize the seat of the disease; 
but, Supposing it to reside only in the flesh and in the 
outward actions of men, they, therefore, strive to remove 
the malady by outward correctives, or a mere reformation of 
manners. As they do not seek the root of the disease in the 
soul and conscience, they can form but a low estimate of the 
efficacy of the Redeemer’s spiritual balm, neither do they 
present its true value, and therefore all their efforts to 
benefit the afflicted are worthless, nay, positively injurious 


37 


to the souls who submit to their guidance. Such do not 
resort to the heavenly physician, Jesus Christ, nor to the 
treasury of his grace, but, as we have already said, 
undervalue the help of Christ, and would themselves be 
esteemed as physicians of the soul. As such they may give 
good consolation, but in the end it becomes manifest to the 
sin-sick soul that it is lost, and laments that it rejected the 
only true physician, Jesus Christ. These men should be 
reproved for their presumption, while a Christian people 
should be warned not to accord to another the honor that is 
due from all men to the only true physician, Jesus Christ. 
But upon this point (God willing) we will hereafter speak. 

Do you then ask, whence comes this sickness? I 
answer: The sickness or depravity of the soul proceeds 
originally from the disobedience and the transgression of 
God’s commandments. It is an inherited disease, it is the 
evil sap or life as it flows from our progenitors in Eden to us. 
When Adam rebelled against his Lord and Maker, he 
sundered that bond of union which made him immortal and 
holy, and he was smitten with death and became corrupt, 
and all who are born into the world are partakers of the 
same evil nature. Thus was this fatal malady introduced into 
the world by one man, and then passed upon all and is 
rooted in their being, which is abundantly proved by the 
penalty, which is death, (the wages of sin,) that is now 
visited upon all mankind. Such is the testimony of the Holy 
Ghost, as given by Paul. Rom. 5:12; 1 Cor. 15:21. But fallen 
man is frequently not satisfied with this inherited sickness, 
but by numerous transgressions increases his misery, and 
through a gross and disorderly life augments his spiritual 
maladies, so that his restoration and salvation are rendered 
almost hopeless.

CHAPTER 10 

Concerning those who have partially realized their Spiritual 
Sickness, but who do not properly know, nor desire to know 
Jesus and his Balm. 


At a former period there were some who, having realized 
their spiritual sickness, that is, a consciousness of sin and 
the gnawing worm in their conscience, fled to a monastery, 
yea from one cloister to another, with the hope of obtaining 
deliverance for their souls. These took upon themselves 
stringent observances, submitted to painful exercises, and 
expected by good works, and various ceremonies and 
penances, to secure spiritual health without the aid of Jesus 
Christ. 

They determined to subdue their sins by 
mortifications of the flesh, and trusted for deliverance from 
the pains of death and hell to fasting and prayer. But can 
the health of the soul be found in these outward 
observances? Do these enshrine the treasures of peace, joy 
and salvation? Alas, no! These are divine gifts, and can only 
be correctly sought by faith in Jesus Christ, and in no other 
object can they be found or hoped for. 

Concerning such afflicted souls does God speak under 
the image of the children of Israel, in the prophecy of 
Hosea: “Ephraim is oppressed (that is, in his conscience) 
and broken in judgment, because he willingly walked after 
the commandment. Therefore will I be unto Ephraim as a 
moth, and unto the house of Judah as rottenness. When 
Ephraim saw his sickness, and Judah saw his wound, then 
went Ephraim to the Assyrian, and sent to King Jareb; yet 
could he not heal you, nor cure you of your wound.” Hos. 
5:11-13. 

Those who elsewhere than with Jesus, the Divine 
Physician, seek for some balm to heal their wounded spirits, 
or by some antidote of their own devising, such as their own 


39 


imperfect righteousness, by which they try to make 
themselves whole, are like those who resort to pictures of 
saints, pious pilgrimages, or to soothsayers and astrologers 
for the purpose of obtaining help in bodily affliction. They 
may imagine that they derive benefit from such means and 
console themselves with this idea, although the disease is 
unbroken, which will become manifest in the day of 
adversity and especially in death, when they perish in 
unbelief and sink appalled with fear into hell. Such persons 
are like Ahab, who sent messengers to Baal, while he was 
sick, to ascertain whether he should recover from his illness; 
but whom Elijah sent back to the king, saying: “Thou shalt 
not arise from the bed on which thou liest, but shalt surely 
die.” 2 Kings 1:4. 

The penitence and resolution of such persons are not 
based upon the proper foundation, for although they may 
acknowledge their sickness and desire to be freed from it, 
yet they would be relieved from the evil of sin upon their 
own merits. Their sorrow and repentance do not spring from 
the love of God. It is not the dying of the old man and the 
quickening of the new creature; neither is this change after 
the only legitimate rule of the physician, Jesus Christ, in the 
freeness of his Spirit, but it originates rather in self-love, 
and arises only from the fear of the pains of perdition, which 
they have merited by their sins, and there is therefore no 
recognition of the only physician, helper and Saviour, Jesus 
Christ. 

It is therefore evident that no repentance or 
reformation is genuine if it be not in accordance with the 
requisitions of the Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ; for that 
which is not in conformity with the direction of this Divine 
physician, cannot minister to the restoration of the soul’s 
health. Through the delusions of Satan, some may imagine 
themselves in a healthful condition and despise the warning 
of God. “Because ye have said—we have made a covenant 


40 


with death, and with hell are we at agreement; when the 
overflowing scourge shall pass through, it shall not come 
unto us; for we have made lies our refuge and under 
falsehood have we hid ourselves.” Isa. 28:15. But while they 
provided themselves with a false refuge and a deceitful 
protection, they shall not be able to stand in the evil day. 
For the enemy can apparently heal a spiritual malady, that 
is, induce a renunciation of some gross vices, but while 
destroying one evil, he may pierce the heart with many 
other fatal darts. 

The heart may be lulled into a false security while it is 
filled with the love of honor, the pride of life, hatred, malice, 
bitterness, discontent, carnal security, unbelief, spiritual 
pride, and glory in an ostentatious holiness, and relying on 
its own merits, although it is under the power of these and 
other reigning sins. The most alarming spiritual state is that 
where a soul is afflicted with every plague, and yet esteems 
itself in a hopeful condition. Satan, our most artful enemy, 
may, when he sees a soul congratulating itself upon its 
supposed spiritual excellencies, leave it undisturbed for a 
season; but as the end approaches and the realities of the 
judgment of God are pressing upon the mind, then will 
conscience awake from its slumbers and inflict the pangs of 
the gnawing worm. Alas! then, when there is no more help, 
will it be seen that out of Christ, the great physician, there is 
no health nor safety for the soul. 

There are other persons who deceive themselves by 
not understanding the nature of spiritual sickness. Such 
perhaps for the want of instruction remain ignorant of their 
spiritual disease, so that at first they cannot regard 
themselves as sinners, as they recognize sin only in gross 
outward transgressions and not in evil desires and 
intentions. Hence they suppose that as long as their desires 
do not break out in gross sins, they have no occasion to 
sorrow over them nor repent. Such persons do not honor 


41 


their Lord Jesus, forasmuch as they slight Him as their High- 
priest and heavenly physician. Although they may frequently 
speak of Him, confess Him and call upon his name; yet all 
this takes place without implying that He should heal their 
soul-sickness, or that he should forgive their sins; but only 
to manifest that they have through him and his 
intercessions, a preference, before all others to the full 
fruitions of heaven. It is not, therefore, with an humble faith 
that they worship him, but by their self-complacency they 
minister to the gratification of the flesh, and by manifold 
works they presume to purchase the favor of God and 
eternal life. 

The reason why individuals of this description have 
such unworthy views of God, and know as little about their 
spiritual maladies as children, is to be sought in their own 
erroneous plans, and that order of life which they have 
established. Inasmuch as in the beginning of their 
reformation they do not rightly apprehend the nature of 
their moral disease, and therefore, do not apply to Jesus 
Christ as the only physician of the soul—neither seek with 
earnest hearts that balm of healing power which he 
bestows. But how false and unjust such a view is before 
God, and into what a dangerous situation such are placed, is 
sufficiently evident from the preceding remarks. I brought 
these points forward at this place, as an illustration, that we 
might first of all have a correct view of our sickness, and 
behold it in all its properties and trace it into all its hiding 
places in the chambers of the heart. That is, that we might 
know sin in all its internal and external power and 
operations. “For we are all unclean, and our righteousness is 
as filthy rags. We all do fade as the leaf, and are driven 
away by our iniquities.” Isa. 64:6. 

Again, it is not enough simply to understand properly 
the nature of sin and the character of our spiritual sickness; 
but we must also have a knowledge of the physician, and 


42 


seek him in faith and out of love to him. Then only does the 
consolation of the Holy Ghost properly arise in the heart 
through which we become conscious of the joyful health of 
the soul, as in the case of David. What does he say to such? 
“Let all that put their trust in thee rejoice; let them ever 
shout for joy, because thou defendest them; let them, also, 
that love thy name be joyful in thee.” Ps. 5:11. 

At this period it also happens that there are persons 
who understand their afflicted state, who do not attempt like 
those mentioned to heal their spiritual maladies by the balm 
of their own works; but as they only have a knowledge of 
the letter and not of the spirit of God’s word, they strive to 
heal by writings or signs, or some other outward methods. 
These build their faith and the hope of their salvation alone 
upon the letter of the promise, or a few passages of 
Scripture without the spirit, and consequently their life is 
without improvement. Yea, these hear, but do not the will of 
God, and thus build their house upon the sand, Matt. 7:27, 
and do not come to the rock Christ Jesus. They, therefore, 
come short of life, for in him are all the promises fulfilled, 
and he is the only dispenser of all heavenly gifts, yea, He 
alone giveth salvation, grace and everlasting well-being, to 
whom also the Holy Scriptures with all outward churchly 
solemnities point as they all speak gloriously of him and his 
office. 

To him, namely, to the heavenly physician, Jesus 
Christ, must all come in the obedience of faith, renouncing 
every creature, and rising above all human laws, traditions, 
signs, or works, that the heart may rest only upon the 
merits of atoning blood, that its affections may be fixed in 
heaven. Otherwise no one can receive salvation, be made 
whole and saved. “For he shall deliver the needy when he 
crieth, the poor also, and him that hath no helper. He shall 
spare the poor and needy, and shall save the souls of the 
needy.” Ps. 72:12,13.

CHAPTER 11 
The condition of unregenerate worldly men in its relation to 
this spiritual sickness and the balm of life. 


What can we say concerning those careless worldly-minded 
men, who are destitute of the fear of God, who neither 
regard their own sickness, nor esteem the physician or the 
balm mercifully provided for our afflicted race? Their heart is 
possessed with desires for earthly honor, riches, pride and 
fleshly lusts; in these they have their heaven, and neither 
ask for any other inheritance, nor seek earnestly after God. 
Of these David speaks when he says: “The fool hath said in 
his heart, there is no God. They are corrupt, they have done 
abominable works, there is none that doeth good.” 
Notwithstanding their conscience and soul are penetrated by 
the most virulent poison and are utterly depraved, and are 
an offence and stink before God; they are not conscious of 
it, undervalue everything spiritual, are full of levity and think 
themselves whole, and that they are to abide in their 
present place forever. O, how cheerfully would they 
relinquish God and heaven, if they could but possess the 
world and abide in it in peace forever! all of which manifests 
itself in their anxious looks in a dying hour. But it will not fall 
out as ye wish, ye men of the world, for God is Lord not only 
of heaven, but also of the earth. “Behold, thou hast made 
my days as a hand-breadth, and mine age is as nothing 
before thee; verily every man at his best state is altogether 
vanity—Selah.” Ps. 39:5. 

Such worldly-minded persons, who never earnestly 
seek after God, may be properly likened in their projects to 
a maniac who is very ill, but whose reason is dethroned and 
who shouts, sings, and laughs without intermission, just as 
if he were perfectly well and at liberty, and therefore is 
unwilling to receive advice from a physician, neither will he 
take any medicine, but tears open his wounds. We observe 


44 


a similar behaviour on the part of those ungodly persons 
who have cast off the fear of God. They are fettered and 
bound in sin and are full of evil, and if anyone out of love to 
their souls speaks to them about Christ, their spiritual 
sickness and the kingdom of God, they ridicule and mock, 
for they would rather perish than be made whole, aye, they 
might well say: “I will not accept this doctrine (that is, the 
Gospel of Jesus,) I do not wish to hear it, for I am only 
disturbed by it. I must take care of my children and see to 
my household, and therefore, have no leisure to attend to 
my soul, I will not do as God has commanded.” 

Behold, is not this an infallible sign that the soul is 
smitten with the fatal disease? Is it not palpable proof that 
the disease has made fearful ravages in their spiritual 
being? A man in affluent circumstances and in the 
enjoyment of full health, may select such articles of food, 
from an abundant market, as his taste may relish and as his 
experience may teach him is fitted to promote his comfort 
and strength. And thus also may those whose spiritual 
health has been restored, partake from a great variety of 
instruction such truths and promises, that are best fitted to 
impart moral strength; but this should be done with an 
enlightened judgment and in the fear of God, as St. Paul 
admonishes Christians, when he says: “Quench not the 
Spirit; despise not prophesying; prove all things, (that is, 
taste all things,) hold fast that which is good.” In like 
manner does our Saviour direct: “He that hath ears, let him 
hear.” “See what ye hear.” And again he saith to his 
disciples: “Are ye also without understanding?” Matt. 15:16. 

The individuals to whom we have referred, who shrink 
from the inquiry, and who shun the true knowledge of Jesus 
Christ and the healing doctrine of his Gospel, as though 
these would lead them into error and disturb their peace: 
these persons have never yet learned to know themselves 
and do not feel their wretchedness. They have never desired 


45 


to know the way of righteousness and the word of life; nor 
have they sought or considered the well-being of their souls. 
For that individual before whom is set a strange diet, which 
is known to be nutritive, if he turns from it untasted, 
because his stomach nauseates it, is certainly not in good 
health. The analogy holds good in spiritual things; for there 
are many in our day, who if anyone offers to their 
consideration the nature of this Divine balm, that is, speaks 
to them of repentance, and of the saving power of his grace 
and kingdom, they not only hear it with reluctance but 
reject it, and haughtily set aside whatever is disagreeable to 
the old Adam, whom they cherish. 

Should you administer to such faithful warnings of 
coming retribution, as the penalty of sin, and of God’s 
judgment, then they smart under the truth and oppose 
themselves to the doctrine, just as a child that unwillingly 
receives a disagreeable potion. Their carnal nature would 
prefer something milder and more pleasant and agreeable, 
for it desires no change, although at the close of an unholy 
life they would rise with all their pollution into the beautiful 
abode of the holy. O that their conscience might be 
awakened through God’s word, to the importance of their 
salvation! O, that they might consider the instability of 
earthly possessions and the end of all things! that they 
might pray with Moses: “O Lord, teach us the measure of 
our days, that we may apply our hearts to wisdom.” Ps. 
90:12. Then would they seek their spiritual health and 
appreciate their everlasting salvation!

CHAPTER 12 

The World cannot endure a Manifestation, on the part of the 
Physician, of its Deadly Malady, much less will it be healed 
by Him. 


It is to be lamented that the world will not endure a 
knowledge of its condition, nor is it willing to accept of 
deliverance from its evils. The children of this world do not 
wish to hear anything of the great evil which has befallen 
them, and which is described by the prophet Isaiah, as 
under the direction of God he pictures the affliction of all 
mankind by the miseries of the Jewish people: “Now go, 
write it before them in a table, and note it in a book, that it 
may be for the time to come, for ever and for ever. This is a 
rebellious people, lying children, children that will not hear 
the law of God. Which say to seers, see not; and to the 
prophets, prophesy not unto us right things; speak unto us 
smooth things, prophesy deceits. Get thee out of the way, 
turn aside out of the path, cause the Holy One of Israel to 
cease from before us.” Isa. 30:8-11. 

As if they had said: It is not our pleasure that you 
should preach so much of the want of spiritual peace in us, 
and the presence of this plague and sickness in our souls; 
permit this Divine physician to remain in heaven, and let 
him dispense His bitter medicines elsewhere; the crucified 
Redeemer is not for us, and his bitter wine or drink is too 
strong for us. After this manner does the world surely still 
speak in its heart; it will not permit the physician Jesus to 
enter into its internal consciousness, that He might reveal its 
hidden corruption. This would alarm the worldling, but he 
does not wish to behold his disease in its fearful reality and 
therefore avoids the presence of the physician, and rejects 
the advice which He so freely proffers, and all simply 
because he has no desire for salvation. 


47 


It is moreover true that those languishing under 
bodily disease do not relish medicine, but rather dislike that 
which is prescribed. But when the danger is imminent, and 
his life is in peril, the fear of death will cause him to 
overcome his dislike for medicine; yea, some will suffer their 
limb to be amputated, that the whole body may not perish. 
Thus it is necessary that one should submit to the treatment 
of the physician if he would recover his health; for a skillful 
man will not administer more than the nature of the disease 
demands or the patient can bear. We should observe the 
same conditions with reference to the spiritual physician and 
His medicine. As the greatest possible evil to body and soul, 
namely, the pains of the second death, will assuredly befall 
those who are not delivered from their sins, no remedy that 
will secure everlasting health should be esteemed too 
unpalatable. The afflicted soul should moreover cheerfully 
submit itself to the physician, for He will not impose any 
requirements which His grace and assistance will not enable 
him to endure. “But God is faithful, who will not suffer you 
to be tempted above that you are able; but will with the 
temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able 
to bear it.” 1 Cor. 10:13. 

In regard to this does our Saviour himself give 
direction for our spiritual health, when He says in the 
Gospel: “Woe unto the world, because of offences! 
Wherefore, if thy hand or thy foot offend thee, cut them off 
and cast them from thee: it is better for thee to enter life 
halt or maimed, rather than having two hands or two feet, 
to be cast into everlasting fire.” Matt. 18:8. But the world is 
so perverted, that men will not only not acknowledge any 
offence, or sickness, or sin, but they also ridicule and reject 
the healthful teaching concerning the Saviour’s Balm, and 
will not allow anyone to help them. 

In reference to this does Paul prophesy, as he writes 
to Timothy: “For the time will come when they will not 


48 


endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they 
heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears. And they 
shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be 
turned unto fables.” 2 Tim. 4:3,4. This applies with sufficient 
clearness to all those who, having heard about their spiritual 
sickness, are unwilling to turn to the Divine Physician, that 
they might through Him be thoroughly healed. On the other 
hand, there are not wanting quacks who set up for 
physicians, who promise abundant health, but who never 
properly understand nor lay hold of the disease, and who 
never bring health; but who, through their spurious 
nostrums, make the sick worse and induce death; thus 
filling the church-yard, the grave, and (unless God in mercy 
interposes) hell with their patients. 

This state of things seems to be acceptable to the 
world, as the Holy One of Israel, that is, Jesus, says in 
Isaiah of the ungodly of all times and places: “Wherefore, 
thus saith the Holy One of Israel, because ye despise this 
word, and trust in oppression and perverseness, and stay 
thereon; therefore this iniquity shall be to you as a breach 
ready to fall, swelling out in a high wall, whose breaking 
cometh suddenly, at an instant. And he shall break it as a 
potter’s vessel that is broken in pieces; he shall not spare; 
so that there shall not be found in the bursting of it a sherd 
to take fire from the hearth, or to take water withal out of 
the pit.” Isa. 30:12-14. And farther, this is done by unskillful 
physicians—“who call good evil, and evil good; that put 
darkness for light and light for darkness.” Isa. 5:20. And 
this is unquestionably also what Paul meant, when he writes 
of the punishment of the impenitent and morally diseased: 
“For when they shall say, peace and safety, then suddenly 
destruction cometh upon them, as travail upon a woman 
with child, and they shall not escape.” 1 Thes. 5:3. This 
anguish and overthrow of the wicked, is graphically 
portrayed in the destruction of Jerusalem, and those terrific 


49 


disasters that burst upon that city are symbolic of those 
dark calamities that shall overwhelm and consume an 
impenitent world in that day when Jesus shall judge the 
world. 

It was, therefore, not without cause that Jesus wept 
over Jerusalem, and in her name over the entire world, as it 
slumbers in its security and will not become sensible of its 
sins and plagues that threaten its ruin. How touchingly does 
our Lord deplore that insensibility that made them unwilling 
to receive him as their only Saviour! “O, that thou hadst 
known, ever thou at least in this thy day, the things which 
belong to thy peace, but now they are hid from thine eyes.” 
The approaching evil that hangs over our guilty world, as 
the impending ruin of the Jews, will yet bathe the world in 
the fires of God’s wrath, as the prelude to the second death, 
into which the incorrigible sinners will be cast with the devil 
and his angels. O, lay it to heart, ye children of men, how 
precious the privilege to serve the Lord and to praise his 
name! 

Thus also does the Spirit of God conclude through the 
prophet in this consolatory strain: “Thus saith the Lord God, 
the Holy One of Israel, in returning and rest shall ye be 
saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your 
strength.” When ye were quiet, help came—that is, when ye 
did not cry for help to others, but quietly yielded your souls 
into the hands of this gracious physician, ye were healed. O, 
what noble and consoling promises are these to us poor 
sinners! There is nothing required of the miserable and 
afflicted to be made whole but that they should become 
conscious of their sickness and desire to be healed, then 
coming to Jesus in humble faith and quietly resting on his 
promises he will find deliverance and salvation. 

Hear again what the Spirit says through the apostle: 
“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and 
forbearance and long-suffering; not knowing that the 


50 


goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?” “And 
therefore will the Lord wait that he may be gracious unto 
thee, and therefore will he be exalted that he may have 
mercy on thee, for the Lord is a God of judgment; blessed 
are all they that wait for him. He will be very gracious unto 
thee at the cry of thy voice; when he shall hear it, he will 
answer thee. And though the Lord give you the bread of 
adversity and the water of affliction, yet shall not thy 
teachers be removed into a corner any more, but thine eyes 
shall see thy teachers: and thine ears shall hear a word 
behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye 
turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left.” Isa. 
30:18-21. 

These quotations from the evangelical prophet, as 
Isaiah may be truly called, I have presented for our comfort 
and instruction. They exhibit in a very striking manner our 
inherited misery on the one hand, and the love and mercy of 
our heavenly Father on the other. That all this applies to our 
Lord Jesus and his heavenly balm, is manifest from the 
subsequent part of this chapter, in which Isaiah speaks of 
the worth, the manifestations of this physician, and 
describes his government and kingdom and his office. 
“Moreover the light of the moon shall be as the light of the 
sun; and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, as the light 
of seven days.” That is, there shall be great joy, a light clear 
and brilliant shall arise in the hearts of believers at that time 
when the Lord shall bind the breach of his people, and heal 
the stroke of their wound. That is the fullness of time when 
Christ was made flesh, suffered, died and rose from the 
dead and ascended up on high, leading captivity captive; 
that He might be exalted as a prince and Saviour, and 
become the physician whose healing power should restore 
the health of all nations. This will be the earth’s jubilee, 
when peace and joy shall illumine the world. 


51 


This is already partially fulfilled in the appearance of 
Jesus, in the revelations of His Gospel and the setting up of 
His kingdom in our world; and is fulfilled in all those who in 
faith embrace Jesus as their Saviour. His kingdom is now 
advancing with power, and its glory gleams through the 
earth, and, as He goes forth on His mission of healing joy 
follows His footsteps, and this joy will enlarge as his work 
extends, until the day of judgment, when our afflicted 
humanity shall be recovered, healed and redeemed, shall 
rejoice with everlasting joy in the completeness of its 
redemption through the Messiah. The Lord commandeth his 
blessing, even life for evermore.

CHAPTER 13 

The manner in which we may form a correct knowledge of 
this Soul-Sickness, and the means by which it may be 
healed. 


The first and indispensable step to be taken by all persons, 
without distinction, who aspire to an heirship in the kingdom 
of God and to salvation through Jesus Christ, is to 
understand the nature of their spiritual sickness. A 
knowledge of the dreadful nature of this disease can be 
most effectually acquired by meditating on the awful 
sufferings of Christ. He stood in the place of the sinner, and 
all that He endured was necessary to heal the moral 
afflictions of man. Surely it could have been no ordinary evil 
that preyed upon the human soul when the infinite Jehovah 
gave up His only begotten Son as a sacrifice, so that the 
blood of the meek and innocent Saviour might become a 
balm to heal the afflicted. It was on account of our sins, that 
is, for our soul-sickness, and on account of the depravity of 
all men, that Jesus endured death, yea, even the death of 
the cross: “Surely he has borne our griefs and carried our 
sorrows; yet we did esteem him smitten of God and 
afflicted.” And the Son himself says: “I restored that which I 
had not taken away.” Was the most beloved and holy child 
smitten and not spared, what then shall become of 
unregenerated and ungodly men? If the righteous are 
scarcely saved, where shall the ungodly and sinner appear? 
Wherefore if an individual finds himself insensible and 
cold, and cannot realize the greatness of his guilt nor see 
the extent of his misery, though he has narrowly surveyed 
his heart, his life, and his actions; let him earnestly 
contemplate a suffering Redeemer, and let him put the 
inquiry to his conscience, why did the exalted Son of God 
endure the bitter and ignominious death of the cross? And a 
voice from the Holy Word proclaims emphatically that our 


53 


sins and iniquities caused Him all his anguish. My sins, 
through which I was a child of wrath, occasioned His 
martyrdom and death, as He declared to the women that 
lamented His fate as He was going to Calvary: “Daughters of 
Jerusalem, weep not for me; but weep for yourselves and 
your children. For if they do this in the green tree, what 
shall become of the dry?” As if he would say, if I, a 
flourishing and fruit-bearing tree by the water brooks, (Ps. 
1:3,) endure this suffering you should learn the dreadful end 
that awaits the dry and corrupt trees; namely, all 
incorrigible and impenitent sinners. 

An individual should first consider, in connection with 
the sufferings of Christ, that all who do not become 
partakers of the merits of Christ by repentance towards God 
and faith in Jesus, are exposed to the wrath of God which 
the Redeemer endured. For he who has not yet attained to a 
proper self-knowledge, and the design of Christ’s death, can 
derive no advantage from His atonement. But when he feels 
his depravity, and realizes his lost condition, and heartily 
repents of his sins, and comes in humble faith to the 
compassionate physician of souls; then will he experience 
the unspeakable love and mercy of God in Christ Jesus, and 
rejoice in his recovered spiritual health. This is very 
touchingly and beautifully presented by the prophet when he 
says: “Thou hast made me to serve with thy sins; thou hast 
wearied me with thine iniquities.” Then he says: “I, (that is, 
Jesus,) even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions 
for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.” Isa. 
43:24,25. O, this is a precious assurance from the gracious 
promises of God, to us poor afflicted souls. 

Although the just and eternal God might have left us 
in our fatal sickness to be finally condemned and lost, and 
His throne would have remained spotless; yet in the 
exercise of His great mercy, He had compassion on His 
fallen creatures, and out of love to us did not withhold His 


54 


Son, but gave Him even unto death, that He might restore 
to us the Divine favor. This love of God and of Christ, the 
sorrowful penitent must learn to embrace by faith. And as 
soon as he has become fully acquainted with his spiritual 
malady, he must hasten to the physician, and as he learned 
to feel his sins by meditating on the sufferings of Christ, so 
must he now lay all those sins again on Christ, as the Lamb 
of God who taketh away the sins of the world. He must also 
strengthen his conscience by exercising faith in Jesus, and 
apply His blood as a balm to the wounds of his soul, and his 
salvation is secured. 


55 


CHAPTER 14 
The Benefits and Fruits of the Suffering, Death and 
Resurrection of the Physician Jesus Christ. 


Jesus Christ did not only die for our sins, but he was also 
raised for our justification, that we might be made righteous 
through Him and become heirs of everlasting life. In His 
suffering, He took upon himself our sins and affliction, by 
His death He atoned for our sins, delivered us from guilt; 
but in His resurrection (by which he became our 
righteousness) He shows us how we, by faith in His life, may 
secure health, life and salvation. As in our risen and glorified 
Redeemer there is no token of sickness or wounds, thus can 
He also make us, in a twinkling of an eye, in God’s sight 
altogether whole; provided we accept Him as our Lord and 
physician and are engrafted in Him by faith. The afflicted 
soul must exercise itself in meditations on the death of 
Christ, and also consider His resurrection as a pledge of our 
righteousness through Him. We will thus behold more and 
more of the displays of divine love, and be established 
through the word of God, while through the teaching of the 
Holy Spirit we grow in grace and in the knowledge of Jesus. 
It was customary in the primitive church in the time 
of the fathers, to conduct pupils, or beginners in the 
Christian faith, directly to the cross, that they might there 
behold an image of their spiritual sickness, as it was 
reflected in the sufferings of Christ. It was intended that 
these beginners in the faith, should behold in the sufferings 
of the cross of the Master, an illustration of their sickness 
and health, while His life and spirit should become the 
model of their life of cross-bearing through this world into 
eternal life. By the sign of the cross they were at all times 
admonished to endure patiently the trials of earth. The 
sufferings of the Redeemer were the daily study of primitive 
Christians, for those sufferings are a great mystery, under 


56 


which there lies shrouded a great deal of instruction. And 
surely if the amazing scenes of crucifixion do not alarm the 
conscience, and awaken not the heart to holy purposes and 
a new life, then neither will Moses or the law awaken him to 
a sense of his condition. 

The Almighty Father knows full well how obdurate the 
heart becomes when He withdraws from it; yea, even under 
the most favorable circumstances, the heart is cold and 
perverted, so that it would not turn to God its maker, much 
less would it perceive and appreciate the design of Christ’s 
suffering. To overcome this natural stupidity of the human 
heart, a merciful Father sends misfortunes and sufferings, 
that these may bring him to reflection. For the same 
benevolent reason does God permit disappointments, 
sickness and crosses of various kinds to befall man, that he 
may through these trials be brought to a knowledge of his 
spiritual afflictions and turn to Jesus, the divine physician, 
and secure the salvation of his soul. That all these trials are 
sent in love, is evident from the fact that they are designed 
to secure his reconciliation with heaven, and if this is 
accomplished at the expense of all earthly good, it is still a 
great mercy. “For what shall it profit a man, if he should 
gain the whole world and lose his soul? Or what shall he 
give in exchange for this soul?” Finally, those who are not 
drawn to the Lord by the blessings He bestows, are 
sometimes chastened until they grow weary of earthly 
things and seek those which are at God’s right hand. 

In relation to this, we have a forcible example in 
Manasseh, king of Israel; who, while flourishing in might 
and power, and crowned with honor, would not think of God 
nor study his own heart; but distinguished himself above the 
mass of the wicked by his gross idolatries, his cruelties and 
crimes. But when God permitted him to fall into the hands of 
his enemies, and he was carried captive to Babylon, in his 
deep wretchedness, misery and fear, he confessed what his 


57 


prosperity, pride and licentiousness once caused him 
haughtily to reject. He acknowledged his sins, and lamented 
when he saw the disorders of his soul, he humbled himself 
before God and prayed for mercy, as we see in his prayer as 
recorded. And so God heard him, and brought him again to 
his kingdom at Jerusalem. Then, for the first time, did he 
understand that the Lord is God. 2 Chron. 33:13. As it is 
written often concerning Israel: “When he slew them, then 
they sought him, and they returned and inquired early after 
God. And they remembered that God was their Rock, and 
the high God their Redeemer.” Ps. 78:34, 35. And what is 
the parable of the prodigal son? Amid the comforts of home, 
in his father’s house, he had no inclination to consider his 
spiritual sickness, he would not lay it to heart; but when he 
came to want, when hunger pressed and he realized his 
wretchedness, he smote upon his breast and said: “O, 
Father, I have sinned before heaven and in thy sight, and 
am no more worthy to be called thy son,” &c. Behold, God 
must also chasten us that He may prevent eternal ruin. It is 
written: “Before I was afflicted I went astray; but now have 
I kept thy word. It is good for me that I have been afflicted, 
that I might learn thy statutes.” Such was the language of 
David, and the experience of all saints is like his. 


58 


CHAPTER 15 

The sum of Salvation and the Spiritual Health of the Soul 
consists in a two-fold knowledge, viz: Of Christ the 
Physician, and Ourselves. 


Let us now direct our meditations to the afflictions of the 
soul, and how we may seek with comfort our salvation: for 
in these two particulars there is comprehended the entire 
foundation of salvation,—first, that the sinner becomes 
thoroughly conscious of his sickness, that is, sin, and feels 
that he is a depraved and fallen being; and then to 
understand what he may and should become through Jesus 
as his spiritual physician. One of these without the other is 
worthless in God’s sight; just as the fear of God without love 
to God, and, like faith without works, profiteth nothing, as 
we have already stated, and will hereafter more fully 
discuss. David illustrates the same truth when he says 
—“They that sow in tears (that is to nail the old and sinful 
man to the cross with Christ,) shall reap with joy,” (a new 
and sound man.) “And those that go forth bearing precious 
seed, shall doubtless return with joy, bearing their sheaves.” 
That is, those who water the seed, which is God’s word sown 
in their hearts, with tears of penitence shall come again to 
Christ rejoicing in the harvest of blessing which the renewed 
soul obtains. 

He who has arrived at a knowledge of his internal 
disorder, and with penitential sorrow seeks deliverance and 
repents, is already partially illumined by the dawnings of 
divine grace. He has the beginning of the new birth in the 
longings of his heart after spiritual health: for by nature he 
could not have such a desire, for such inclinations are only 
produced by the Holy Spirit—so that his redemption draweth 
nigh, since those yearnings which grace has kindled are the 
harbingers of that work which God is about to perform on 
his soul. It is, therefore, absolutely necessary that an 


59 


individual advance in self-knowledge as he makes progress 
in the knowledge of Christ: for unless he sees the extent of 
his disorder and his utter helplessness, he will not earnestly 
apply to the Saviour for help; and, as already mentioned, it 
is not enough that you know your affliction of soul, and that 
you bewail it with tears, but you must form a proper 
estimate of Christ as a physician, and learn to know the 
grace and mercy of God in Christ Jesus, that you may 
render suitable offerings of gratitude and praise for the 
healing of your soul, when, as it is termed, you receive, 
Luke 1:77, “the knowledge of salvation through the 
remission of sin.” For the knowledge of this salvation, John 
the Baptist was sent to prepare the way, and it is 
immediately after explained by Zacharias, as consisting in 
the forgiveness of sin, through the tender mercy of our God, 
whereby the Day-spring from on high hath visited us. 

This experience is also described in the Holy 
Scriptures as the knowledge of God; and where such a 
knowledge does not exist it cannot be well with the soul. 
The vanity of such persons without the knowledge of God, is 
portrayed in the Wisdom of Sol. 13:10-19. The Lord saith in 
Hosea 6:6—“I have pleasure in love and not in sacrifice; and 
in the knowledge of God and not in burnt offering.” Those 
who willfully remain ignorant concerning these things, let 
them remain ignorant. As Paul says—God will not know or 
acknowledge such in the day of judgment. And again, 1 Cor. 
14:20—“Beloved brethren, be not children in knowledge, but 
children as to that which is evil; but in knowledge be ye 
perfect.” Concerning the Pharisees, Jesus says they are blind 
leaders of the blind, while they never understood properly 
either themselves, the Redeemer, nor God, his Father in 
heaven.

CHAPTER 16 
The Lord Jesus heals all Sicknesses, only one excepted. 


At this stage of our meditations it is perhaps fitting to dwell 
somewhat on the different aspects of this sickness, or the 
different kinds of sin, but the various symptoms of the 
disease, or the different developments of sin, are best 
known to the Lord Jesus, who is fully acquainted with all the 
evils that rage with destructive power in human nature. But 
this I know, blessed be God, that no sin or transgression or 
injury of the soul is beyond His cure, with the exception of 
the sin against the Holy Ghost, and which consists in 
blasphemy and the rejection of the Lord Jesus. Those who 
do not receive him as the only begotten Son of God, and 
believe not that He in and through the Holy Ghost forgives 
sin, that He is the true Messiah, Redeemer and Saviour, are 
guilty of this sin. If anyone has once been enlightened and 
believed in Him as the restorer of man, but afterwards 
denies Him and repudiates the Gospel—ascribes the works 
of Christ through the Holy Spirit to Satan, and calls the Holy 
Ghost a devil, as the Scribes and Pharisees, through hatred 
and a wicked mind did when they ascribed His miracles to 
the agency of Beelzebub; such an one has voluntarily placed 
himself in a condition in which there is no mercy for his soul 
in this world nor in that which is to come. 

We have therefore presented to our view the amiable 
and glorified Physician of sin-sick souls endowed with 
healing abilities that enable Him to cure all the spiritual 
maladies that are found in our disordered nature. He has 
made satisfaction to the law, and has become a propitiation 
no only for original, but also for actual sin; yea, He has 
atoned not only for ours, but for the sins of the whole world. 
It should be further observed that Jesus does not, like other 
physicians, only heal some diseases, or offer remedies that 
death will defy in the end, but His healing powers invariably 


61 


secure the health of those upon whom they are exerted, and 
are never overcome by death, but secure to the patients 
everlasting life. Aye, so effectual is His grace, that all the 
wounds, bruises and putrefying sores once on the soul are 
removed, not leaving even a scar or wrinkle, but clothing 
the spirit in immortal youth and beauty, that it may become 
an heir of God and a companion of angels. It is a source of 
joy that the reigning King of glory still exercises His office 
and the skill of His heavenly kingdom under the economy of 
grace, in applying His healing balm to those that are sick. 
His remedies are offered without money and without price, 
and the only conditions that He makes are proposed by the 
prophet Isa. 55:1,2, and by the psalmist in Ps. 95:6,7. And 
no poor afflicted sinner may excuse himself as though it 
were the Physician’s fault if he is not made whole. 

In our day we are so highly favored, that much more 
abundant provision is made for the spiritually sick, than 
were known to those who lived centuries ago. The Lord 
Jesus also graciously manifests himself, and shows to a lost 
world that with Him alone can be found spiritual health. 
Therefore should no afflicted one delay, but today, while it is 
the accepted time and day of salvation, should he hasten, 
yea, run to Jesus, and seek him while He may be found. It is 
Jesus, the physician of the soul, whom we must seek in love 
and in the exercise of faith; and we should never resort 
elsewhere for remedies to heal the disorders of the heart. 
Not as the Jews whom the Lord rebukes in Hos. 5:13: 
“When Ephraim saw his sickness and Judah saw his wound, 
then went Ephraim to the Assyrian, and sent to King Jareb; 
yet could he not heal you, nor cure you of your wound.” “I 
will go and return to my place till they acknowledge their 
offence and seek my face; in their affliction they will seek 
me early.” In connection with this should we also with 
Jeremiah pray: “Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; 
save me, and I shall be saved, for thou art my praise.” 


62 


It was stated in a former part of this treatise, how He 
has sent out His credentials, and has posted and circulated 
His letters in which help is promised to everybody. The 
asylum has been opened; its gates are wide open to receive 
all the sick. In it are stored all manner of remedies, for His 
balm heals all kinds of sicknesses to which poor souls are 
subject. Only come, ye wretched, perishing ones, and 
receive Him in faith, and suffer Him to apply His healing 
power to your souls. O, turn to Him, afflicted souls, trust His 
balm for your salvation. He stands waiting to be gracious. 
He is accessible at all times, by day and by night, and His 
medicine is never diminished; it is sufficient to heal the 
whole world, and all who use it as He directs, will find it 
efficacious in the restoration of health and in securing for all 
everlasting life. 


63 


CHAPTER 17 
The Three Sick Persons 


One of three methods is usually adopted by persons who are 
sick. Some, aS soon as they become conscious of their 
sickness, apply to the best physician and place themselves 
under his treatment. Others postpone their application, and 
wait to see whether the disease will not pass off without a 
resort to medicine, until they are laid upon a bed of 
affliction, when they call in the physician, give a description 
of their disease and ask for advice and help. The last class is 
made up of those who, although dangerously sick and 
confined to their couch, do not send for the physician; and if 
he would even come or be brought into the chamber of the 
sick by some sympathizing friend, the afflicted would reject 
his proffered services. And although the man of skill may 
see the danger and warn him of the consequences, yet will 
he persist in his rejection of the means that would save his 
life, and perish because of his obstinacy. 

It happens very similarly with the spiritually sick. 
There are those who, as soon as they discern their spiritual 
sickness, seek after the Lord Jesus, the Physician of souls; 
or if they have been surprised by a temptation, they hasten 
to the feet of mercy, and there, denouncing their 
wickedness and confessing their faults, beg for the 
forgiveness of their sins. They would not allow sin to tarry 
all night in their hearts. They do not suffer their spiritual 
sickness to gain the ascendancy; they judge themselves, so 
that they escape the judgment of God; that is, they 
acknowledge their transgressions early and with deep regret 
before the Restorer of their souls, and therefore, like David, 
they find that there is mercy with God, that he may be 
feared. “I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity 
have I not hid. I said I will confess my transgressions unto 
the Lord, and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. For this 


64 


shall every one that is godly pray unto thee in a time when 
thou mayest be found: surely, in the floods of great waters 
they shall not come nigh unto thee.” Ps. 32:5,6. Such praise 
the Lord continually for His goodness and His precious balm, 
because He forgiveth all their sins and healeth all their 
diseases. Ps. 103:3. 

There are others who, while they are pleased to hear 
of the infinite skill and boundless compassion of the great 
Physician, Jesus, neither are offended when their spiritual 
disease is unveiled, yet do they delay their coming to the 
true source of help; they intend to repent and enter upon a 
holy life, but they have not yet made a beginning. They 
procrastinate, supposing there is time enough to attend to 
these things. But the Lord delighteth not in the death of 
sinners, and therefore permits great calamities to befall 
them, and visits them with such afflictions as are fitted to 
bring them to a decision upon the subject of their salvation. 
Like the prodigal and King Manasseh whom we have 
mentioned, they are driven by the scourge of God’s 
providence to Him to whom they would not be drawn by 
cords of love. Then they cry out: “O Lord, rebuke me not in 
thine anger, neither chasten me in thy hot displeasure. Have 
mercy upon me, O Lord, for I am weak: O Lord, heal me, for 
my bones are vexed. My soul is also sore vexed, but thou, O 
Lord, how long! Return, O Lord, deliver my soul: O save me 
for thy mercies’ sake.” 

Those procrastinators, who defer their repentance and 
their return to the Lord, are warned of the evil 
consequences that may flow from such a course: “Be ye not 
as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding; 
whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they 
come near unto thee.” Ps. 32:9. In regard to those who are 
in this frame of mind the Saviour speaks in the parable of 
the great supper, when He says: “Compel them to come in, 
that my house may be full.” 


65 


The third class of whom mention has already been 
made, are fatally sick, full of sin and iniquity, but who 
neither fear the judgment of God, nor desire a knowledge of 
Christ, and will hear nothing about the disorders of their 
soul. These are the ungodly children of this world, who will 
not trouble themselves about the salvation of their souls. 
Every one of them does according to the imagination of his 
own wicked heart. Notwithstanding the kind physician, our 
Lord Jesus, offers himself to them, and permits the science 
of salvation and his healing art to be proclaimed in the 
Gospel, they reject it all, and mock and blaspheme, yea, 
even persecute the Lord and those who are his. They remain 
firm in their evil ways and will not allow themselves to be 
conducted to that which is good. Of such it is written: “The 
transgression of the wicked saith within my heart that there 
is no fear of God before his eyes.” Ps. 36:1. And while they 
despise the wholesome balm of life so kindly proffered and 
embrace lying vanities rather than the truth, they will retain 
their sickness and die the second death, and thus sink into 
perdition, where they must endure the pains of damnation 
before the presence of the Lord; as it is written, Isaiah 
66:24: “Their worm dieth not and their fire is not quenched, 
and they are a horror to all flesh.” Therefore should we 
earnestly, in the day of grace, seek after the Lord Jesus, the 
great physician, and suffer ourselves to be healed, that we 
may be delivered from all spiritual afflictions. 

Here it is also well to consider the relations of some 
afflicted ones to the physician, or to what degree those have 
advanced towards health, who are not entirely restored. 
Although not entirely healed, they have discovered the 
plague of their hearts, and are seeking Jesus, waiting for 
Him, and learning to know him through His goodness, and 
patiently wait in love and hope for pardon and salvation. The 
desires of such are breathed in the following earnest 
aspirations of the psalmist: “I wait for the Lord, my soul 


66 


doth wait, and in His word do I hope. My soul doth wait for 
the Lord, more than they that watch for the morning; I say, 
more than they that watch for the morning.” Ps. 130:5,6. O, 
then “seek the Lord while He may be found, call upon Him 
while He is near. Let the wicked forsake his way, and the 
unrighteous man his thoughts, and let him return to the 
Lord, and he will have mercy upon him and to our God, for 
he will abundantly pardon.” Isa. 55:6,7. 

Those who abide under the care of the Physician will 
finally become altogether whole and rejoice in the hope of 
glory. “They are as trees planted by the water brooks, 
whose leaf shall not wither and whose fruit shall not fail. 
And whatsoever they do shall prosper.” Ps. 1:3. But there 
are thoughtless souls who depart from the treatment under 
which they have derived benefit—they disregard the rules of 
health which Jesus has prescribed; and their last state is 
worse than their first, unless they return to the Lord. Truly 
has Peter discerned their wretchedness when he writes 
—“For it had been better for them not to have known the 
way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to 
turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them. But 
it has happened to them according to the true proverb. The 
dog is turned unto his own vomit again; and the sow that 
was washed, to her wallowing in the mire.” 2 Peter 2:21,22. 

The last class have no realizing sense of their need of 
the Divine Physician; they know not that they are sick; they 
are self-righteous and without sin, or they will help 
themselves, or seek relief in human devices, such as good 
works and the observance of ceremonies. Such were the 
Scribes and Pharisees, who would not come to the only true 
Physician Jesus, and behaved as though they did not require 
the interposition of His healing power. Therefore did our 
Lord utter the reproof in the Gospel—“The whole need not 
the Physician, but they that are sick. I came not to call the 
righteous, but sinners to repentance.”

CHAPTER 18 
Of the Diseases in general which Christ, the great Physician, 
heals. 


I will now speak of the ordinary evils of this spiritual 
sickness. Some teachers comprehend all the evils of this 
moral disease under a threefold form. First, the wounds 
which sin has inflicted upon the internal man, which are 
seen in a perverted will and depraved faculties, from which 
spring evil desires and thoughts. The second class of evils 
are manifest in the works into which the imaginations of the 
heart are embodied, and are the results of man’s 
acquiescence in the clamors of his corruptions for 
indulgence. The third is the evil customs or habits which are 
established and by which the soul becomes immersed in sin, 
so that there is scarcely a ray of hope for his recovery. The 
pious Augustine regarded this state represented or imaged 
in those three dead persons whom Jesus awakened out of 
the sleep of death, viz: the little daughter, the youth, and 
Lazarus. In the first instance, he supposed that the little 
daughter of the ruler of the Synagogue (Matt. 9:25) imaged 
the healing of the inward sickness or the removal of sin from 
the heart. The raising of the youth, who was already carried 
to the gate of the city, (Luke 7:12), was designed to 
represent the restoration to spiritual life from the outward 
disorders of this sickness, which are manifest in sinful 
works. And finally that Lazarus, who was already buried, but 
whom Christ called back to life, was intended as emblematic 
of the soul which has grown old in sinful habits, and become 
dead in trespasses and sin, but is awakened to spiritual life 
by the Lord of glory. 

From all those evils which sin has inflicted upon the 
soul, will Jesus Christ deliver those who earnestly seek His 
help. To this end did the Father ordain Him the Physician of 
souls, that he might save not only from the sinful conditions 


68 


indicated above, but from all the miseries and guilt under 
which mankind are suffering. While we, however, obtain 
help and are restored to the divine favor, we are not at once 
perfectly healed, for so aggravated are the wounds which 
sin has made, that we have continually need of the 
application of the heavenly balm. And in view of the 
imperfections which cling to the Christian, we have reason 
to pray throughout our life-time: “Forgive us our debts, as 
we forgive our debtors.” It is therefore manifest that while 
life endures we shall have our conflicts with sin, and hence 
we must always cherish our love for the Divine Physician, 
“who healeth all our diseases.” 

Christ is a physician of infinite skill, and imparts to His 
balm a specific power for every spiritual malady, and thus 
delivers not merely from original sin, but cleanses from all 
actual sins which those who believe in Him have ever 
committed. The followers of Jesus are not perfect; they have 
sins of infirmity, sins committed ignorantly, or when 
surprised by a powerful temptation; but “the blood of Jesus 
Christ his Son cleanses from all sin.” 

The life of God in the soul is not of rapid growth, it is 
a gradual work, and even after conversion, which is the birth 
of that higher life, comes the work of sanctification which is 
progressive, for it is written: “They go from strength to 
strength, until every one appears perfected in Zion before 
God.” This gradual improvement in moral excellence enables 
us to see the extent of this deadly malady, and the 
difficulties that attend its entire cure, and we are therefore 
so much the more led to admire the faithfulness and love of 
the Healer and Saviour of our souls. 

The experience of those who are whole, enables them 
to exercise patience with those who are still sick, for having 
once all the aversions to holiness that sinners feel, they 
know how to bear with them. He who has obtained spiritual 
health through the mercy of God, contemplates Christ as the 


69 


one altogether lovely, and not only submits himself to the 
healing power of the physician, but carefully observes all the 
rules prescribed to promote the well-being of the soul. He 
dies unto the world, crucifies his passions and resists sin; 
but he delights in the spiritual meat and drink provided for 
him, and the more he feeds upon the hidden manna and 
drinks from the fountain of life, the stronger and healthier 
he becomes. With Paul he can exclaim: “As for me to live is 
Christ, but to die is gain.” With this realization of union with 
Christ, he shall, on the morning of the resurrection, rise 
perfected and complete in the Redeemer. 

We should also remember that to those who are in 
Christ Jesus, that is, those who have passed under his 
tuition and who trust to His righteousness, God does not 
impute any sin. For the sake of Him who bore our iniquities, 
the Father will remember our sins no more forever, He will 
blot out our transgressions and cast our sins into the depths 
of the sea. That such is the gracious condition of those who 
have recognized in Jesus God’s only Son, and received Him 
as the Divine Physician, who walk not in the flesh, but in the 
spirit, is evident from numerous declarations in the Holy 
Word: “For I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember 
their sin no more.” This is God’s covenant which He makes 
with his people, it was established through the death of 
Jesus and was sealed by His blood. For Christ through His 
offering takes at once the conscience, wounded and 
enfeebled by sin, before the Father, cleansed by His 
precious blood, and gives to the penitent believer a peaceful 
conscience, and fills him with peace and the joy of salvation. 
O Lord! increase our faith, that we may experience in our 
hearts that rich unspeakable blessing, that we may serve 
thee in the beauty of holiness with body and soul to all 
eternity. Amen.

CHAPTER 19 
The Heavenly Balm, with which the Physician Jesus heals, 
and by which the sick are made whole and are saved. 


As we have now heard the testimony of the Holy Scriptures 
concerning Him who is the Divine Physician, and also 
respecting those whom He heals and the diseases of the 
soul, it is proper now to say something in regard to the 
nature of that balm which heals the afflicted. For just as it is 
with the Physician, the sick and the diseased, so must there 
also be an appropriateness in the medicine designed to 
restore spiritual health. As the Physician is divine, thus also 
the healing which He produces, through the office of the 
Holy Spirit, is after a spiritual manner, and has reference to 
the corrupt spirit of man, and includes the restoration of the 
soul. It is, therefore, highly important that the antidote 
should be of such a character as powerfully to affect the sin- 
sick soul, that it may mollify and heal the wounded heart 
and take away the disease, while it begets a new life and 
substitutes everlasting health for disease and death. This 
powerful balm is nothing less than the living Word of God— 
the Word that was made flesh, through which all things were 
created, are preserved and governed, and through which, 
after Adam’s fall, we were restored, born again, and had our 
sins forgiven. As David says—“He sent his word and healed 
them, and delivered them from their destructions.” Ps. 
107:20. It is, therefore, clearly established that the eternal 
Word, Jesus Christ, as well as His flesh and blood in the 
Word, is the true balm with which God helps and heals the 
bodies and souls of men. This fact was also demonstrated by 
Jesus in the days of His flesh, as He, through the word, 
awakened the dead, cast out devils, forgave sins, and 
healed all manner of diseases. “For as the rain cometh 
down, and the snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, 
but watereth the earth and maketh it bring forth and bud, 


71 


that it may give seed to the sower and bread to the eater, 
so shall my word be that goeth out of my mouth: it shall not 
return unto me void: but it shall accomplish that which I 
please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it.” 
Isa. 55:10,11. 

That which the snow and the rain accomplish for the 
parched earth, the word of God does for our race. As the 
earth is watered and nourished by the moisture descending 
from heaven, and is thus quickened that it produces fruits 
and is clothed with golden harvests, so does the word of 
God distil its quickening and refreshing influences on the 
parched fields of humanity, until there arise many new-born 
souls clothed in the beauty of holiness and laden with the 
precious fruits of righteousness. Therefore should the 
afflicted children of men pray earnestly and believingly that 
God’s word may exert its quickening and renewing power 
upon their souls, that they may be made whole and be 
delivered from death. Then shall our hearts be purified and 
become temples of the Holy Ghost. As our Lord said unto 
His disciples—“Ye are pure through the words that I spake 
unto you.” 

We will here present the history of the brazen serpent 
in the wilderness, as an emblem of the antidote which we 
have under discussion, and also with a view to obtain 
clearer conceptions of the nature of spiritual sickness. “And 
the people spake against God and against Moses, Wherefore 
have ye brought us up out of Egypt, to die in the 
wilderness? for there is no bread, neither is there any water: 
and our soul loatheth this light bread. And the Lord sent 
fiery serpents among the people; and they bit the people; 
and much people of Israel died. Therefore the people came 
to Moses and said, We have sinned, for we have spoken 
against the Lord and against thee; pray unto the Lord, that 
he take away the serpents from us. And Moses prayed for 
the people. And the Lord said unto Moses, Make thee a 


72 


brazen serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to 
pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it 
shall live. And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it 
upon a pole; and it came to pass that if a serpent had bitten 
any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.” 

Under this figure we behold the poisonous bite of that 
old serpent, the devil, through whom our nature was ruined, 
and which first inflicted its terrible fangs upon the souls of 
our first parents in Paradise. Satan, through the 
instrumentality of the serpent, made them disobedient, 
caused them to despise eternal life, and made them heirs of 
death. Sin, the poison first infused by the serpent, entered 
into the blood, and is transmitted from parent to child, so 
that all by nature have the seed of death in them, and can 
only be healed and delivered from its terrible effects by the 
Redeemer. 

God designed to picture to Israel through the bite of 
the fiery serpent their innate poison and their fatal spiritual 
disease, that they might have a knowledge of the evils that 
are cherished in the human heart. At the same time did the 
Lord teach them by the lifting up of the brazen serpent, that 
the eternal Word, that should become manifest in the flesh, 
would thus be transfixed upon the cross and there endure 
the torture of the old serpent. But though the serpent 
wounded (as predicted) the seed of the woman, He bruised 
the head of the serpent. In virtue of the Redeemer’s 
suffering, He was glorified and exalted to be a Prince and a 
Saviour, and has now become the only saving cure for the 
deadly diseases of sin. Blessed be God that all who now 
realize and see their spiritual sickness, as the Israelites felt 
the wounds of the serpents, and confess as they did, (we 
have sinned,) and embrace the word of God with a living 
faith, looking upon an uplifted Christ and accept Him as their 
Helper and Saviour, shall obtain life. He has left us the most 
gracious assurances that all who yield obedience to His 


73 


commandment shall have those wounds healed that were 
inflicted by the serpent—that is, shall experience the 
forgiveness of sin and obtain health and salvation. 

That we are to put this interpretation upon this 
Scripture narrative, is further evident from the book styled 
the Wisdom of Solomon. There it is also recorded that those 
who turned themselves to this sign were healed. Not, 
indeed, through that symbol, but through Him whom it was 
intended to represent as the propitiation for sin. “They are 
not healed either by herb or salve, but thy word, O Lord, is 
that which healed them.” Wisdom 16:7-12. 

Thus does the Scripture teach us that God and his 
word are our Physician and balm. From this it 
incontrovertibly follows that Christ is God, as His word is 
undoubtedly the healing power, since it corrects all the 
moral evils to which men are liable. It is therefore called the 
word of salvation. This corresponds with what has 
heretofore been observed concerning this heavenly 
medicine. “He sent his word and healed them and delivered 
them from their destructions.”

CHAPTER 20 

How the Word is Jesus Christ himself, and the manner in 
which his flesh and blood in the Word exert a healing power 
upon the soul and restore the afflicted. 


That antidote which is offered freely by the Saviour to all 
who desire the restoration of their spiritual health, is at the 
same time pleasant as well as healing in its properties. It 
soothes the anguished spirit and refreshes the weary and 
wounded soul. It is, as heretofore mentioned, in its origin 
the eternal Word of God, in which we obtain the forgiveness 
of sin, health for the soul, and everlasting life. In the 
fullness of time that Word was made flesh; and this is, in a 
word, nothing else but Jesus Christ, the Son of the 
everlasting Father, through whom the Father originally 
made man, and after his transgression he was smitten by 
sin with a deadly malady, redeemed His fallen creatures 
through the Son. It is Jesus who forgives sin and saves the 
offender from a second death, and makes him whole and 
happy. “O that men would praise the Lord for his goodness 
and his wonderful works to the children of men.” 

When the Redeemer was lifted upon the cross He 
fulfilled the design of the figure of the brazen serpent, for as 
the crucified Saviour he holds the same relation to us that 
the serpent did to the Israelites, viz: God’s appointed means 
to restore the wounded. This explanation of that symbol is 
given by Jesus in His discourse as recorded in John 3:14,15. 
“As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must 
the Son of man be lifted up: that whosoever believeth on 
him might not perish, but have everlasting life.” From this 
we may easily comprehend the meaning of the words which 
the Lord addressed to the Israelites. “He that is bitten and 
looks upon the serpent shall live.” Almost all things in the 
old dispensation pointed to Jesus Christ, and were prophetic 
of the help and the blessings that would attend the mission 


75 


of Jesus into our world. The eternal Word having become 
flesh was crucified, and afterwards exalted to a throne at 
God’s right hand, that He might be sought through faith as 
the effectual means of restoring the sinner to the divine 
favor and healing the soul of all its maladies. He is our 
health and our righteousness, and while He rules and reigns 
on high He works through the Holy Spirit, and heals through 
our spiritual knowledge of Him, which we obtain through the 
Spirit, and by the word of life delivers from death [and] hell. 
He takes away our sin and unrighteousness and gives us in 
return His grace and righteousness, and through His victory 
everlasting life. Isaiah prophecied concerning this when he 
exclaimed: “He shall swallow up death in victory, and the 
Lord God will wipe away tears from all faces: and the rebuke 
of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for 
the Lord hath spoken it.” And in Hosea Jesus speaks through 
His servant—“I will ransom them from the power of the 
grave: I will redeem them from death. O death, I will be thy 
plague: O grave, I will be thy destruction.” “Death is 
swallowed up in victory.” “O death, where is thy sting? O 
grave, where is thy victory?” 1 Cor. 15:55. 

All this has been accomplished through the death and 
suffering of Christ, and in the outpouring of His blood He 
had united His divine nature with flesh; and hence that 
spiritual balm which is for the healing of the soul, derives its 
efficacy from the death and cruel sufferings and from the 
shedding of the blood of God’s beloved Son. It is of no avail 
that persons seek other means, for whatever antidotes they 
may devise to correct the disorders of the heart, they can 
never restore the health or purity of the soul. The merits of 
the Redeemer’s death constitute the only plea of the sinner 
before God, and His blood the only hope of the perishing. 
Jesus Christ, as true God and man, and one with the Father, 
is the Physician, and Himself became the medicine whereby 
we may have our afflicted souls healed. Though exalted on 


76 


His mediatorial throne, He is accessible by prayer: and faith 
may reach her unseen hand into the heavens, the 
storehouse of mercy, and bring such supplies of grace as we 
daily need to promote our spiritual comfort. Only let our 
prayer be importunate: that we may be sprinkled with His 
blood, that our wounds may be mollified with His grace, and 
we Shall be thrilled with the rapturous pulsations of a new 
and higher life. Christ, I would once more speak it to His 
praise, is an all-sufficient Physician and is Himself the Balm 
of life: as He is the High Priest and the offering, the one who 
makes righteous and is the righteousness, the Master of 
food and the food itself, the Tree of life and the fruit of it. 
Yea, all the good effects His balm produces are also in itself 
—for it is itself health, righteousness, power, peace, joy and 
everlasting salvation. 

Jesus has not only procured this costly and precious 
medicine from God for us, but offers it to us Himself and 
dispenses it liberally through the Holy Ghost, together with 
all that is necessarily connected with it out of the heavenly 
treasury. It is through Christ and for His sake that we obtain 
counsel and help to everlasting life. He has prescribed the 
mode of treatment, and permits it to be preached and 
proclaimed in the Gospel amid the music of the sanctuary, 
while He sets before us the examples of those whom He has 
relieved, that He may incline many sin-sick souls to come 
unto Him for help. Here in the Gospel may the sick learn 
how to approach the Physician and how they may obtain His 
service. Yea, He comes before them and shows them the 
tokens of their sickness, and offers His help while they may 
yet be saved. He even enters the homes of the afflicted, and 
so manifests His ruling power in all their affairs, that they 
may become conscious of HiS presence and implore His 
mercy, and thus be made heirs of the kingdom of grace. It 
was necessary to urge this again, though repetition is not so 
pleasant, because there are always those who would 


77 


obscure the way of life and who do not conduct their 
neighbor directly to Christ, but through by-paths, through 
human devices and ceremonies through which the soul only 
becomes worse. Blessed be God, the poor sinner may come 
to Jesus, the Physician of afflicted souls, without the 
intervention of man, and may tell Him his wants, and draw 
from the exhaustless fountain the waters of life freely. As it 
is written—“From his fullness have we all received, and 
grace for grace.” John 1:16. 

It is not, therefore, with the spiritual Physician, 
Christ, and His balm, as it is with other bodily physicians, 
who strive to restore their patients by external applications 
or with medicinal plants, and give their skill for pecuniary 
considerations. Jesus is very different, having no selfish 
motives: He acts in the presence of Almighty God, and 
ministers as our faithful High Priest and Mediator without 
intermission even to-day, that He may heal our diseases— 
that we may realize our misery and appreciate His healing 
provision. He is more faithful in doing good to the most 
needy than all others; there is indeed no comparison 
between the skill, the assiduity and gentleness of this Divine 
Physician, and those of our fellow men who are devoted to 
the healing art. His goodness and power, as well as the 
freeness of His offices, transcend our conceptions. He invites 
all—is ready to bless all. “Whosoever is athirst, let him 
come, and whosoever will, let him partake of the waters of 
life freely.” Rev. 22:17. For He does not heal after the 
manner of men, but through His word, His spirit and His life, 
He renews the youth of the soul, restores harmony in the 
heart, and reconciles us to heaven. And having removed the 
disorders of our spiritual nature, He makes us heirs of 
everlasting life. 

It is to be observed here also that Jesus heals by the 
exercise of His own power, as it is declared in the Gospel 
—“And all the people desired to touch him; for a virtue 


78 


proceeded from him and healed them.” Even to this day 
there emanates such a power from Him that He daily heals 
the spiritual infirmities of those who come in contact with 
Him. He removes the sins of all those who touch Him or fix 
their hopes upon Him by faith. Though faith should be 
feeble, only let it be sincere and lively, and if it can impart 
ever so small a blessing from Christ, it will soon strengthen, 
until the healthful influences of grace will form the soul 
anew and clothe it in the beauty of holiness. Such are the 
blessings promised by the prophet. “He giveth power to the 
faint, and to them that have no might he increaseth 
strength. They that wait upon the Lord shall renew their 
strength: they shall mount up with wings as eagles: they 
shall run and not be weary, they shall walk and not faint.” 
Isa. 40:29,31. 

It is through the precious flesh and blood of our Lord 
Jesus that we are healed, for out of these there springs a 
fountain of health, whose streams impart strength, 
righteousness and joy. For God has made the flesh and 
blood of Christ in His word, the balm, that is to heal our 
humanity by connecting it with the Divine nature, and thus 
renew man by its living power. He heals with the wine and 
oil of joy of the Spirit and the truth: with these He bathes 
and dries our wounds, and delivers the conscience from the 
bondage of spiritual death, and comforts and cheers our 
fearful hearts. In a word, as Christ carries in the power of 
His word everything, so also does it contain all manner of 
balsam for the injuries of the soul. He conveys through His 
word everything that is serviceable to our souls, yea, 
imparts through it all that is needful and profitable to our 
restoration and the healing of our diseased nature. 

In the word is contained that pure water, the water of 
life, with which He cleanses the sores of those afflicted by 
sin—sprinkles and purifies them. In the word He carries the 
salt that is necessary to the offering up of the old man, that 


79 


it may consume the foul flesh and cleanse the wounds. In 
that same word are found the oil of joy and that heavenly 
salve, which are necessary to the healing of a wounded 
spirit. In that word there is also that consuming fire, which 
is intended to burn all the dross of sin and kindle in the 
heart the love of God. Thus does Jesus Christ carry in His 
word food and drink, viz: His body and blood, with which He 
may regale the heart of the afflicted and quicken the poor 
fainting soul, while with this same provision He nourishes 
those who have been made whole, and causes them to 
advance in the new life and flourish as trees of 
righteousness. 

Our heavenly Physician alone has spiritual treasure; 
He is the sole possessor of that efficacious power which is 
required in the healing of souls. It is by the word of His 
power (Heb. 1:3) that He raises the dead sinner to 
everlasting life, and preserves him pure and holy forever. 
And whatever of weakness and imperfection may remain in 
the flesh of the Christian will be destroyed by death, and he 
shall come forth in the resurrection with a body fashioned 
like unto the glorious body of Jesus, without spot or 
blemish, and arrayed in the beautiful robes of a sinless 
immortality. If anyone desires to examine this subject more 
fully and learn the difference between the afflicted and the 
whole, the sinner and the saint, the old and the new man, 
and contrast the wounds and deformities of the one with the 
grace and glory of the other, let him diligently read the 
graphic portrait drawn by Ezekiel in the sixteenth chapter. 
For the Spirit of God presents the two characters in striking 
contrast in that chapter under the image of Jerusalem. As all 
those spiritual blessings necessary to the restoration of the 
soul, to its health, joy and comfort, are only to be found in 
our Lord Jesus, we dare not seek them elsewhere or in any 
other object. We must repair to the well of salvation and 
with a cheerful faith draw from it the waters of life. Christ is 


80 


that fountain—come unto Him, ye thirsty, fainting souls, and 
live! 

That the efficacy of the Saviour’s balm is not entirely 
dependent upon His Divine nature, but consists rather in the 
union of the Divine and human, is evident from the fact that 
He performed His miracles not only by the utterance of a 
word, but also by the use of the members of His body. He 
took the ruler’s daughter by the hand, as He uttered the 
command for her to arise. He anointed with His fingers the 
eyes of the blind man, while he looked up to heaven and 
said, Be ye opened. This is designed to show that both His 
Divine and human nature are active in the healing of souls. 
For as hearing, sight and life attended His touch, so now is 
that union of the two natures in Christ, the secret of that 
renewing and saving power which issues from the God-man 
upon a perishing world, and through which our afflicted 
humanity will be quickened with a new life. The Redeemer 
could unquestionably have wrought those splendid miracles 
by speaking a word, as He did with the centurion’s child; but 
His whole life, and all the particulars of His actions, were 
freighted with instruction. In the exertion of His two-fold 
nature when He wrought miracles, He illustrated the 
mystery of His incarnation, and indicated the prospective 
dignity and glory to which humanity should be exalted in 
heaven through the Divine nature with which He formed an 
indissoluble connection. He thus became emphatically the 
balm, to heal and to restore to its original, sinless glory, the 
soul that was created in the image of God. 

Finally, it may be expedient to remark, lest anyone 
should fall into error, that the Divine remedy for the evils of 
sin, though presented under a variety of names, when its 
exhaustless resources and its Divine efficacy are spoken of, 
(as already indicated,) yet is there, after all, a oneness in 
that remedy. Jesus Christ, with His Spirit and grace, 
constitute the means of deliverance from all the evils of sin; 


81 


these contain the power to cleanse from the pollutions of all 
iniquity. Precious Physician of souls! Glorious deliverer of 
sinners, exert thy healing and saving power, that our weary, 
wounded and afflicted race may experience the power of thy 
grace and be saved from death and hell!

CHAPTER 21 

Why Christ is called the Heavenly Physician—What we are to 
hold and believe concerning Him—What constitutes a 
profession of true Christian Faith. 


Jesus Christ is called the heavenly Physician, in the first 
place, because, his name is Jesus, which means, Saviour, 
Redeemer, Deliverer, as it is written in the Gospel: “Thou 
shalt call His name Jesus, for he shall save His people from 
their sins.” That is, deliver them from soul-sickness. In like 
manner does Peter speak to the Jews on the day of 
Pentecost: “The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom 
ye slew and hanged on a tree. Him hath God exalted with 
his right hand, to be a Prince and Saviour, for to give 
repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sin.” Acts 5:30,31. 
Now mark what is meant by a Saviour, when and how he 
became Saviour; and you will readily understand the office 
of our heavenly Physician with the afflicted; even as from 
repentance and forgiveness of sin, we may understand the 
sickness and the balm for its healing. 

But our Physician is not only called Jesus, but also 
Christ, which means the anointed one, on account of the 
glory of His humanity by the Divine anointing. He has, as 
God’s anointed, become ours in God according to the flesh, 
as Peter remarks: “Therefore let the house of Israel know 
assuredly that God hath made that same Jesus whom ye 
have crucified, both Lord and Christ.” Acts 2:36. As if he had 
said, this man whom ye esteemed so lightly and crucified, 
Him hath God glorified and made Him equal in substance, in 
power, in honor and glory with Himself. He baptized Him 
externally and internally with the Divine balm and balsam; 
yea, with the oil of joy of the Holy Ghost He filled Him and 
penetrated His whole being with the anointing, so that the 
head is not only consecrated by it, but it flows from His head 
down upon His garments, that is, upon all His members, and 


83 


from Him it issues in streams for the consecration of 
Christian, believing hearts. And now Jesus Christ, anointed 
by the Holy Ghost, is the salve, the good savor, the pleasant 
balm Himself, so that all other persons may not only be 
healed of many deadly maladies, but that they may also be 
anointed, sanctified and beautified, renewed and purified by 
Him, and thus become a sweet savor unto God, be made 
partakers of the Divine nature, and of the riches of the 
kingdom of heaven. Thus is Jesus Christ made unto us of 
God, wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption. 
1 Cor. 1:30. Yea, truly He is become our healing balm, the 
oil of joy and our anointing, a poured out oil, is the sweet 
and lovely name of Jesus. Through the word should the 
name, the being, the power, and the ability of Him be 
understood, and we should not strive after the outward word 
or name simply, but in faith draw near to Him who is 
represented by these names and symbols. 

The apostle Paul was chosen and commissioned as 
the servant of Jesus the Divine Physician, that he might 
make known to the whole world the Divine power, honor 
and glory of Jesus Christ, as these were associated with His 
human nature, so that he became the healing balm for an 
afflicted race. This is intimated by our Lord himself in His 
address to Ananias: “Go thy way for he is a chosen vessel 
unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, 
and the children of Israel.” Acts 9:15. For there is salvation 
in none other, neither is there any other name given among 
men through which we can be healed or saved. His name, 
says the Bride of this heavenly Physician, is like incense 
poured out, therefore, do the young maidens, that is, 
Christian souls, love Him. The anointing of Christ through 
the Holy Ghost has made His name fragrant to believing 
souls, for that name is associated with all their spiritual 
enjoyments, with all those manifestations that fill them with 
a holy rapture. The afflicted, on account of the Physician and 


84 


heavenly balm, are called Christians, as soon as He imparts 
spiritual health and becomes their portion through the Holy 
Spirit. The sweet fragrance of His grace exceeds the

clothes smell of costly myrrh, aloes and cassia.” Ps. 45:8. 
There issues from Him into all fainting and believing souls a 
sweet savor, that is, His word and grace. The word of 
salvation and life, the Gospel of peace, which is pleasant to 
all afflicted Christians, and brings, yea, more and more 
enduring health to the soul. 

This then is the true Christian faith, viz: to believe on 
and to confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, the Son of God, 
through which faith alone we obtain life, that is, health and 
salvation in His name. For it was ordained from all eternity, 
that this Jesus should be conceived of the Holy Ghost, born

all men should be crowned of God in virtue of His sufferings

of God, and have given to Him a name that is above every 
other name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should

suffering, that the Father should glorify Him in His humanity

Now this was for the consolation of all mankind richly 
fulfilled in the humanity of Christ, by the resurrection of the 
man Christ Jesus, His ascension to heaven, and His being 
seated at the right hand of the Majesty on High. He is thus

equal with God the Father, in power, might and honor, and 
is heir of all things in heaven and on earth, which He did not

always co-equal with the Father, and was like Him in His 
existence and eternity. According to His human nature did 
all these things happen, in which He was exalted to the right 
hand of God as Lord and Christ, and is made the head of His 
church, our physician and our balm of life. 

Therefore, I observed that this heavenly physician is 
not only called Jesus, but in consequence also through the 
anointing of the oil of the Holy Ghost is become the Christ, 
that we may not separate the eternity of the two natures in

as a Divine unity and being, and with all angels honor and 
worship Him. This is the more important, that we may 
obtain the balm, health, and other heavenly gifts not as 
from a half or divided, but from a whole Christ, true God

the heavenly balm, but the God-head and humanity in their 
completeness; on account of which and through which God 
only can be gracious and forgive us our sins, and enrich us 
with His spirit and the riches and glory of His kingdom. O, 
what a treasure have we in Jesus! Behold how His 
glorification exalts our race! Adorable Lamb of God, take 
away our sins and fill the world with Thy life and Thy glory!

CHAPTER 22 
The Splendor and Glory of the Attributes and Being of our 
Heavenly Physician, Jesus Christ. 


All the Scriptures, but especially the Psalms, testify of the 
sufferings and death and afterwards of the exaltation, the 
kingdom and glory of our heavenly Physician, Jesus Christ. 
In the 45" Psalm David speaks of Him in exalted terms as 
the King of glory, yea, as God clothed with Divine lustre and 
splendor. Among other attributes he enumerates the 
following—“Thou lovest righteousness, and hatest iniquity; 
therefore God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of 
gladness above thy fellows.” Behold, the prophet calls Him 
God, and shows in the spirit of prophecy how God the Father 
has anointed Him without measure with the oil of joy—that 
is, has invested Him with the fullness of the Godhead. Such 
is the anointing and the oil, on account of which He is called 
Christ, or the Anointed of God. 

As oil is possessed of three useful properties,—it gives 
light, it nourishes and heals,—so also does the anointed 
King, Jesus Christ, enlighten, heal and nourish. He is the sun 
and light of the world: without His light all mankind would 
walk in darkness. In the prophecy of Malachi it is written 
—“But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of 
righteousness arise with healing in his wings.” Mal. 4:2. The 
light of this Sun brings with it the dawn of that rapturous 
day of glory that shall endure forever, for He is the Sun of 
eternal day, and it shall never more set. He is the bread 
upon which the children of God are nourished, without which 
aliment all hungry souls would famish. This is beautifully 
presented in his discourse as recorded in the sixth chapter 
of John. Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness and 
are dead. I am that bread of life whereof if a man eat he 
shall never die. Except ye eat my flesh and drink my blood 
ye shall die. It is equally true that He only can heal our 


87 


spiritual diseases, and without His balm our soul-sickness 
would be unto everlasting death. 

I have thus endeavored to show the exaltation, the 
power, might and glory of Jesus Christ and His holy 
humanity, so that afflicted sinners might form suitable 
conceptions of their Physician and Saviour, and properly 
esteem and honor His antidotes for the evils and corruptions 
of our nature; that is, His holy flesh and blood, through 
which we are healed and nourished with immortal life. Those 
who properly consider the heavenly and Divine nature of 
Jesus, as it is unfolded in His humanity, and spiritually 
discern His flesh and blood, will find Him to be the meat and 
the drink of their souls, and therefore they reap the blessing 
promised to those who hunger and thirst after 
righteousness. Those who do not discern the Lord’s body nor 
accept His doctrine of life and salvation in its primal 
simplicity, will find no relief for their sickness anywhere else. 
And while they rob Him of His honor as the truly begotten 
Son of God by denying His divinity and mission, they 
become their own destroyers; forasmuch as they do not 
recognize in His flesh and blood a healing balm for wounded 
souls, and do not regard Him as the bread of life, and are 
therefore never made partakers of the benefits of His 
suffering and death. Suffer me here to utter a word of 
warning to all those who expect to become Christians in any 
other way than by repentance towards God and faith in 
Jesus, or those who would ascend to heaven and inherit 
everlasting life upon the merits of anyone save the merits of 
Jesus. For He has truly said—“I am the way, the truth and 
the life, no man cometh unto the Father but by me.”

CHAPTER 23 
The Physician, Jesus Christ, is yet to-day true Man, but in 
the Being of God and in Divine Glory. 


Although the flesh and blood of Christ, as also the Word 
(that is, the Divine nature,) constitute the spiritual medicine 
of poor afflicted souls, we should not understand it in such a 
sense, as though His flesh and blood (that is, His humanity,) 
did not exist even now. Notwithstanding the fact that Jesus 
Christ, after His humanity, has acquired Divine might and 
has inherited and received the heavenly mystery in the 
Divine unity, He is still a true man; yea, the man that was 
born of the Virgin Mary, and whose body was nailed upon 
the cross, but who is risen from the dead and has been 
made perfect. According to the testimony of God’s word, 
Heb. 2:7,9; 5:5,7, He is declared the new heavenly Man, 
declared and made equal with God, and in whom all former 
and perishable weaknesses of our nature, through His 
resurrection, ascension to heaven and enthronement at 
God’s right hand, were transformed into Divine power. “For 
though he was crucified through weakness, yet he liveth by 
the power of God.” 2 Cor. 13:4. From this may be clearly 
deduced the two-fold state of Christ, viz: the weak and the 
strong, the human and the Divine. The first ended with the 
man Christ; the second went gloriously forward, as He liveth 
in the power of God. Yea, the power of God is now His entire 
life, since Jesus Christ, according to His humanity, has all 
the ability or power which resided in Him as the eternal 
Word, when yet unclothed with humanity He dwelt with the 
Father. And now, as the only begotten Son, God and Man, 
He is enthroned with the Father and crowned with the 
highest Divine honor, as He is also invested with all the 
perfections of the Godhead. 

When, therefore, the apostle speaks of the new being 
and life as manifested in the man Christ Jesus, we must not 


89 


suppose that He laid aside flesh and blood, but retained 
these in a perfect and glorified form, in the same manner as 
believers are made partakers of the Divine nature through 
Him hereafter, and yet do not lay aside their personal 
identity. Our heavenly Physician, Jesus Christ, lives 
henceforth not in the earthly or temporal life, but His 
existence goes forward in God—in the power of God, as Paul 
declares. Rom. 6:9,10; 2 Cor. 13:4. Everything that pertains 
to Him has become new, eternal and spiritual, otherwise He 
could not have become the food of our souls. Neither could 
He in any other form be our spiritual Balm and Life. In a 
word, in His entire being He lives the life that from 
everlasting pertained to God, and which the Word (that is, 
the son of God) had with the Father before the world was. If 
it were not so then would Christ be divided—that is, His 
humanity would exist in a distinct and separate form from 
His divinity; He would have a two-fold life, and spirit and 
being, which every believer will perceive would be wholly 
inconsistent. 

Christ Jesus, I would once more affirm upon the 
testimony of the Scriptures, has two natures—He is God and 
man, indivisible; but these two natures exist in a Divine, 
eternal life and nature. 

This human life and being, now after His glorification, 
ascension to heaven, yea, His exaltation above all the 
heavens, should be contemplated not in the light and after 
the manner of reason only, which, though a noble faculty, is 
not in itself sufficient for a proper apprehension of Christ, 
but it should be considered as the Divine existence or being 
with and in God, which can only be understood by faith, 
without which it is impossible to know Christ aright. The gift 
of the Holy Ghost, the righteousness of God and the health 
of the soul, and finally eternal life and salvation, can never 
be realized by us unless it commences in a correct spiritual 
apprehension of Christ, and is then carried forward even to 


90 


the throne at God’s right hand, and connected with the 
humanity of Christ, who is the living Head and source of life. 
We are, therefore, not to regard our Divine Physician, Jesus 
Christ, together with the balm (His flesh and blood,) now, 
after the completion of the work of redemption, no more 
after their human aspect and form, but view them in a 
spiritual light and according to the existence in which He 
now reigns. This was foreshadowed in His own words when 
He said—“And I, when I shall be lifted up, will draw all men 
unto me.” John 12:32. For it is because of His being first 
lifted upon the cross and afterwards exalted to glory that 
the Holy Spirit is poured out upon all flesh, through whom 
also all those that believe in Christ shall be raised into the 
Divine, heavenly existence, and live for evermore. 

According to the nature and state indicated must the 
Lord Jesus be known through faith, that we may know that 
the fullness of the Godhead dwelleth in Him and that God 
may be seen in the face of Jesus Christ, in whom He shines 
and in whom also are found all the treasures of heaven. The 
Father has also ordained that Divine grace and the riches of 
the kingdom of heaven should come through none other 
than this Man, who is the Mediator between God and man. 
As it is stated by Paul in 1 Tim. 2:5,6—“There is one God, 
and one Mediator between God and man, namely, the man 
Christ Jesus, who gave himself a ransom for all, to be 
testified in due time.” He is our Saviour, our Physician, our 
balm, our help, our all.

CHAPTER 24 

How it was in regard to the Balm and the Physician of the 
Soul in the Old Testament, and before the Incarnation of 
Christ. 


Since I have remarked that not only the Word of God (or the 
Divine nature,) but also the humanity of Christ—that is, 
Christ as entire God, with His sufferings, death and the 
shedding of blood—is the Physician and balm of our souls, 
the question might be propounded by some one, how was it 
concerning these things before Christ became man, or 
before the Word was made flesh? In reply to this we answer, 
that in relation to the means of salvation which were then 
prospective to man, they were present with God. The order 
of salvation was the same before the incarnation that it was 
afterwards; the same prior to the giving of the law that it 
was under the law. As those who lived under the patriarchal 
and Mosaic dispensations through faith beheld the promises 
afar off and believed to the saving of their souls, so do we 
also appropriate through faith the merits of the ascended 
and glorified Redeemer, for He is the Saviour of all the 
chosen, as well the Lord and King of all. 

According to the testimony of the Scriptures, none 
can be made whole, that is, righteous, before God without 
Jesus; but as under the Old Testament dispensation there 
were many pious and righteous persons, yea, patriarchs and 
prophets, it must follow as a necessary consequence that 
Jesus Christ healed and saved them, forasmuch as they also 
were at one time sinners. And although at that period the 
Word was not yet made flesh—although Christ had not then 
suffered nor poured out His blood in death—yet all this was 
immediately after the fall of Adam and afterwards to 
Abraham given in a promise. It was announced, for the 
consolation of the first and all subsequent offenders, that 
the eternal Word should assume our nature and suffer for 


92 


our guilt: that He should bring back the wretched and lost, 
and heal the afflictions and the wounds of our fallen race. 
Those who then embraced with a living faith the 
promises of God in Christ, concerning His sufferings and His 
saving power, received Jesus as their Physician and also His 
balm, upon the evidence of their faith, just as truly as those 
receive Him who now truly believe on His name. They 
received not only the word of God as a solace to their souls, 
but were also made partakers of the benefits of the 
sufferings and blood of Christ in the word of promise which 
they believed. That word was spirit and life to the healing 
and salvation of their souls, notwithstanding the word was 
not yet made flesh. Faith, which makes that which is future 
present, according to the eternal word of truth, brought the 
blessing home to the hearts of true believers, so that they 
enjoyed salvation as fully as though all things concerning 
the work of redemption had already transpired. In the 
merciful dispensation of God, the saints of old were saved 
just as we are, by faith in the blood of the Lamb of God that 
taketh away the sins of the world. As they stood around the 
altar stained with the blood of the victim offered, they sang 
of Him who should come, and with the eye of faith, beheld 
the great sacrifice for sin that should be offered on Calvary. 
We will endeavor to establish this by a number of 
passages from the Scriptures. First of all, let us hear what 
Paul says: “Jesus Christ the same, yesterday, to-day, and 
for ever. Be not carried about with divers and strange 
doctrines, for it is a good thing that the heart be established 
with grace, not with meats which have not profited them 
that have been occupied therein.” Heb. 13:8,9. Here the 
apostle clearly affirms that Christ is the same and only 
Saviour under the old testament as well as under the new. 
Those who lived before the law was given, like those under 
the law, and under the Gospel, could only hope for salvation 
through Christ. Peter declares in Acts 15:11, the ground of 


93 


our hope. “But we believe that through the grace of the Lord 
Jesus Christ, we shall be saved even as they.” It follows, 
therefore, that Christ with all His mercies, the shedding of 
his blood, His cross and suffering, was as much their 
Saviour as ours, and was their physician and balm, before 
His birth as man. 

Again have we the same assurance from Jesus Christ, 
in His address to the Jews. “Your father Abraham desired to 
see my day, and he saw it and was glad.” This day of the 
Lord, means His advent in the flesh, together with His 
crucifixion and glorification, all of which Abraham beheld 
with the eye of faith in the offering up of his son Isaac, as if 
His death and the benefits resulting from it were already 
accomplished, and thus was it also with all other true 
believers. Abraham believed God, when He had assured him 
that in his seed all the nations of the earth should be 
blessed. And his faith was accounted to him for 
righteousness, and he was delivered from his spiritual 
sickness and made whole. 

In the book of Revelation, we also find an important 
and clear proof of the correctness of the statement already 
made. “And the Lamb which was slain from the foundation 
of the world.” Certainly not actually slain, but in the merciful 
design of God, for God has ordained that Jesus should suffer 
for our souls, that His blood might wash away our sins, and 
that He might be the healing balm of our souls; this was as 
real to their faith as though it had already been fulfilled. 
Therefore, John does not say that the Lamb should be slain, 
but that it was slain from the foundation of the world; that 
is, that all the elect of God from the beginning, were healed 
and saved through the efficacy of atoning blood. 

Hence, although the man Christ Jesus was not yet 
manifested at that period, neither was His blood shed for the 
remission of sin, still did the faith of all true believers bring 
the promised Saviour to the relief of their spiritual wants. 


94 


The afflicted and dying sinner was immediately after the fall, 
again invited to return, and through grace restored and 
accepted. The Physician was promised and, notwithstanding 
a long time elapsed before He would become man and shed 
His blood for the forgiveness of sin, yet was it absolutely 
certain to believers as they had implicit confidence in the 
word of God. To make this still clearer, I remark that 
although Jesus Christ according to His divine nature was 
from the beginning the Physician and Saviour of all 
believers, thus also after His incarnation was He ordained of 
the Father to fulfil these offices, and was at the same time 
set apart as the King and Head of the Church. Eph. 1:18-22. 
After this manner He has been the everlasting righteousness 
and wisdom of God, and yet, also after the completion of the 
work of redemption became our wisdom and righteousness. 
1 Cor. 1:30. Concerning this determination and will of God 
in regard to the incarnation, suffering and death, 
resurrection and glory of Christ, as well as our salvation 
through Him; all this have the prophets predicted through 
the help of the Holy Ghost. 1 Peter 1:10-12. And the 
beloved holy fathers of the Old Testament never doubted 
that it would transpire, and were, therefore, saved through 
Christ. Their faith clung to the promise of the Messiah, and 
they were conscious of those realizations of blessedness 
which now flow from the various offices of Christ, so that in 
reality He was the spiritual meat and drink, just as truly as 
He is to believers of the present day. True faith will readily 
comprehend to the saving of the soul that Jesus Christ is the 
same, yesterday, to-day and forever. 

Christian faith embraces the heavenly Physician, 
Jesus and His balm, not only after His Divine nature, but 
also in reference to His human nature, even before he was 
born. The believer grasps in Christ, not only the Divine, but 
the human, aye, the word, that was to be made flesh, and 
now has become flesh. For that which God had at that time 


95 


promised concerning Christ as yet future, that was already 
present to their faith as though it had already occurred. If 
we earnestly pray to the Holy Spirit for the true faith, and 
that He would teach us to know Jesus spiritually, that we 
may not confine Him to place, limit, or office, but receive 
Him in His eternal being, we shall then conceive of Him as 
He is with God. Whatever Christ has personally 
accomplished for our redemption and salvation, this was 
also done in the spirit that Jesus Christ the King of grace 
might, through the Holy Ghost, even to this day, dispense 
the blessings of salvation to them that believe.

CHAPTER 25 
A two-fold aspect of the Balm of Christ, with some notice of 
its power and efficacy, as displayed in afflicted man. 


At this stage of our discussion it is proper to observe that 
this heavenly medicine which we are considering has a two- 
fold particular power and efficacy, (notwithstanding it 
remains one Divine substance,) just as the physician 
operates in a two-fold manner. The one mode or power is 
painful, with which the wounds of the old nature are 
disturbed, the disease of sin attacked, the moral sores are 
cleansed, and the suppurated flesh is destroyed and healed. 
The other effect is pleasant, for it brings with it 
consolation and health and the soothing oil of joy, which 
always has a healing influence, strengthens the spirit, gives 
life to the soul, and while it removes the troubles of the 
heart, it transforms the sorrows of the mind into spiritual 
joy. These two-fold powers does our Physician, Jesus Christ, 
exercise, which He himself unfolds in the parable of the 
good Samaritan, who took oil and wine and poured into the 
wounds of the unfortunate stranger. These two modes of 
administering the balm, or its efficacy after this manner, 
disturbs the kingdom of sin, while it preserves him who is 
whole in his state and being; and should there be any fresh 
symptoms of the disease, he will at once come to the 
Physician, until the flesh with its lusts is totally subdued. 
The first power of this balm is the word of the cross, 
and it is intended for the old Adam, whose sickness it must 
subdue and destroy by mortifying the evil desires and 
overthrowing his false refuge out of Christ. This is the 
strong, sour myrrh wine, the bitter syrup of the cross and 
sufferings of Jesus Christ the Son of God, which the poor 
afflicted soul must drink when it seeks the Physician and 
desires to be healed. It is through the power of this word 
that he must seek the destruction of sin and the old nature. 


97 


And though painful, let him abide under the cross of Christ, 
and so meditate upon the sufferings of God’s Son that he 
may realize that he is chargeable with the death of the 
Redeemer—that his sins have inflicted upon the innocent 
Lamb of God pain and death—and he will crucify his sins and 
die unto the world. Let us remember, also, that all this must 
be experienced before we can be made whole or have 
everlasting life, as Paul writes in 2 Cor. 4:10,11—“Always 
bearing about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus, that 
the life also of Jesus might be made manifest in our body. 
For we which live are always delivered unto death for Jesus’ 
sake, that the life also of Jesus might be made manifest in 
our mortal flesh.” 

Concerning the power of this balm in the word of the 
cross, we may again quote the words of the apostle as 
recorded in Heb. 4:12—“For the word of God is quick, and 
powerful, and sharper than a two-edged sword, piercing 
even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the 
joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and 
intents of the heart.” As it not unfrequently happens in a 
protracted case of sickness that the first doses of a powerful 
medicine only seem to increase the sickness, so that the 
individual feels as though he would die; thus also does our 
heavenly Physician administer such pungent remedies that 
the poison—that is, sin—that lies diffused through the entire 
man, is driven to the heart and conscience, and manifests 
itself to the mind in all its horrid evils. But after He has 
produced in the sinner this sense of wretchedness, He 
imparts restoratives—that is, the grace of salvation and the 
spirit of faith—with which this tender Physician purifies the 
afflicted heart and gives health to the soul. 

As it would be unbecoming in one who labors under 
bodily disease to oppose the efforts to relieve him, so also 
would it be highly improper for a sin-sick soul either to 
resist, to doubt, or be alarmed, though he suffer anguish, 


98 


for the beginning of his restoration must be painful. The 
Spiritually sick should submit themselves in all patience to 
such treatment as their Divine Physician may prescribe, and 
though He should carry them through a long and painful 
experience, they may rest assured that He will subject them 
to no unnecessary suffering, nor will He permit their pain to 
be more than the removal of their malady will require. 

The blessings connected with the soul’s recovery from 
this moral sickness are unspeakably great and inexpressibly 
glorious, and in view therefore of that cloudless felicity 
which believers shall inherit, they should be willing to 
undergo any temporal calamity. “Our light afflictions are not 
worthy to be compared to the glory which shall be revealed 
in us.” Why should man, for the sake of a few perishable 
pleasures, rob himself of such stupendous glory? Why 
should he not, like the Master, drink the bitter cup which the 
Father gave Him, and afterwards entered into glory? O! why 
not drink the bitter cup, and then follow his Lord through 
tribulations and sorrows into everlasting life! 

The other power which we mentioned is called the 
word of life, the joyful, pleasant oil of joy, the Gospel of 
grace, which to the new man is spirit and life. It is attended 
with forgiveness of sin, produces peace, joy and consolation 
in the believing heart. In a word, it is in both aspects 
nothing else, (that is, the provision for sin-afflicted souls, ) 
than Jesus Christ rich in grace, crucified for our sakes, and 
raised again for our justification, and who is now seated at 
the right hand of the Majesty on high, ready to help those 
that believe, and ever dispensing His mercies among the 
needy. This all-sufficient Saviour, now reigning amid the 
glories of the New Jerusalem, is now transforming into His 
image the souls that fly to Him, and will, as He has 
promised, make them heirs of the imperishable glories of 
the kingdom of heaven. 


99 


In regard to this two-fold power of this heavenly balm 
the Almighty speaks to His people under the ancient 
economy—“See now that I, even I am he, and there is no 
God with me; I kill, and I make alive; I wound, and I heal; 
neither is there any that can deliver out of my hand.” Deut. 
32:39. Although this is uttered and is to be understood with 
reference to the punishments and judgments which God 
would send upon the Jews, yet may it be applied here in 
view of what is written in the 36™ verse—“For the Lord shall 
judge his people, and he will have compassion on his 
servants.” “The Lord killeth and maketh alive; he bringeth 
down to the grave and bringeth up.” 1 Samuel 2:6. He kills 
and wounds through the word of the cross such as rebel 
against His spirit. And as a surgeon who, with a sharp 
instrument, rips open a felon or boil, or removes a corrupt 
member of the body, would not on that account be regarded 
as unkind and unfeeling, though he occasions great pain to 
the sufferer—he does it all for the benefit of the afflicted; so 
neither should we deem God unmerciful when he afflicts, 
since it is for our profit. A desperate disease demands sharp 
remedies. Our Lord Jesus, whose voice so often fell gently 
as dew upon weary and wounded hearts as He uttered 
words of comfort, spake to persecuting Saul in thunder 
tones, so that he was prostrated to the earth as by a clap of 
thunder, and he lay trembling and afraid, until the Lord 
raised him up and comforted him with a word of hope. Acts 
9:3-6. He humbled the proud and exalted the humble. From 
these and other examples we can discover the two-fold 
power of the word. 

The first effect is to kill, the second is to make alive. 
The first reveals the disease, the other gives health. The 
first is bitter and alarms, the other is sweet and pleasant. 
The one agitates the heart and conscience, causing the 
sinner fear, pain and sorrow, that the afflicted soul scarcely 
knows what has happened or where it will end; the other 


100 


brings the blessed sunlight into the heart, dispels the clouds, 
the vapor and the previous tempest, so that the soul, like 
the earth that was rocked by the thunder and drenched by 
the rains, looks up through its tears with smiles of gratitude 
to the God of mercies. The first effect is that of the refiner, 
who places the silver in its impure state into his crucible and 
subjects it to a severe heat, and stirs it until the dross is 
removed from the pure metal, when the heat and the 
crucible are taken away. This much of the two-fold effects of 
this spiritual balm may suffice for the present. 

But we would not circumscribe our heavenly 
Physician, Jesus Christ, to these specified modes in His 
administering of the healing balm. I will not consider the 
ground of the coming grace of God, nor the incipient stage 
of repentance and the new birth, which belong to the first 
principles, as affirmed in Heb. 5-6; but the general mode in 
which the Divine Physician brings about the conversion of 
souls is thus presented in the Gospel. The Physician offers to 
the afflicted soul His help and cure, in the grace of God, by 
which He inspires faith, awakens from the death of sin and 
encourages the soul to seek Him, to love Him, and to 
understand and acknowledge his spiritual sickness. The 
Spirit of the Lord calls to the afflicted but slumbering sinner 
—“Awake thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and 
Christ shall give thee light.” That is, you will see your 
spiritual sickness and learn to know Him as your Physician, 
and your soul will be restored to health and receive the 
salvation of God. The Lord Jesus grant us this, that we may 
seek through earnest prayer, each one for himself, the 
power and efficacy of this heavenly balm, that appreciating 
it as God’s precious gift, we may be fitted to ascend into the 
presence of the Holy One, where there is fullness of joy, and 
obtain a place at His right hand, where there are pleasures 
for evermore. Amen.

CHAPTER 26 
Concerning the Test of the Soul’s Physician and of the 
Heavenly Balm, the Word of God in Christ. 


With a view to offer an example which may illustrate the 
condition of the afflicted as well as the character of the 
Physician and His balm, let us contemplate the miracle 
performed on one stricken with the palsy, whom the Lord 
healed not only internally of soul-sickness, but also restored 
his body to health by His almighty word, so that through 
those withered limbs the flood of life again coursed warmly. 
This is a true test of His divine mission, which He exhibited 
on earth before He permitted Himself to be addressed as the 
Physician of souls. “And behold, they brought to him a man 
sick of the palsy, lying on a bed; and Jesus, seeing their 
faith, said unto the sick of the palsy, Son, be of good cheer; 
thy sins be forgiven thee.” Matt. 9:2. 

In this instance we behold in the sick a two-fold 
disease, bodily and spiritual, in like manner does the 
Physician impart comfort and medicine in the word of the 
Lord. For after He had said to the sick of the palsy, “Son be 
of good cheer, thy sins be forgiven,” from this it can be 
readily perceived that the first effects of the word, viz: 
alarm, fear, sorrow and penitence, had instantly been 
experienced by the afflicted, consequent upon which the 
Lord comforts, and the other effect of His balm which 
proceeded from the word of life, and which was an oil of 
gladness to the wounded spirit. There is of course nothing in 
the pains of the body that compares with that anguish of 
spirit which is produced by sin through an awakened and an 
accusing conscience. No other pains so reduce the body and 
prostrate the spirit, as the terrors inspired by a lively 
apprehension of God’s wrath. Jesus, only as our soul’s 
Physician, can relieve from those dreadful apprehensions, 


102 


and without Him, there is no other creature under heaven 
that can deliver or save. 

We may also here readily comprehend the true skill, 
the art and mode of healing the soul, namely, that the 
heavenly Physician begins the work first of all internally, 
that is, by awakening the heart, and after stirring its 
profound depths, He corrects its evils and implants holy 
affections before He commences to change the outward life. 
He implants such a Divine energy within the soul, that it not 
only subdues the evil desires and sanctifies the thoughts, 
but from this holy centre there issues an influence that flows 
out into the daily life of the individual. The origin, or source 
of wickedness, as seen in the life of the depraved, is in the 
heart, as the Physician Himself has said: “Out of the heart 
proceed evil thoughts, murder, adultery, fornication, theft, 
false witness, blasphemy.” These are the things which 
pollute the man, and make him unclean and offensive in 
God’s sight. Matt. 15:19,20. 

This internal godly work manifests itself in this, that 
God the Father in the beginning thereof, directs the afflicted 
to His Son Jesus Christ, whom He sent into the world and 
ordained Him to be our Physician. John 6:44-65. He who 
hears this of the Father and learns, will come to Christ and 
allow himself to be helped, so that through the 
instrumentality of His balm the sinner is made whole. Thus, 
do I say, does the Lord commence His work of healing the 
soul: He stirs and awakens the heart of the sick, yea, often 
puts the whole internal man in commotion by His grace, and 
thus works from within, not from without, when He desires 
to restore health to the entire man. As it is written—“He saw 
their faith,”—that is, the faith of the sick man and of those 
who carried him—which confidence God the Father without 
doubt bestowed, and thereby drew them to His Son, the 
Physician Jesus, so that they sought Him, became 


103 


acquainted with Him, and by trusting in His power were 
made whole. 

The evil spirit, the adversary of the soul, begins the 
work of ruin in the heart of man by exciting his sinful 
desires, perverts the will, subverts all purposes of good, 
takes away the love, the faith and the fear and obedience of 
God; and thus, after expelling everything good from the 
heart, and putting in its place the love of sin and its kindred 
evils, he proceeds in corrupting man, until like a worm 
commences the work of devastation in the seed of an apple, 
and from that extends his ravages until the whole is 
consumed, so does Satan carry forward his work, until he 
has utterly destroyed his victim. Behold! thus also must the 
heavenly Physician, Jesus Christ, in healing the soul, 
commence with the operations of His grace in the inner 
man. He must cleanse the heart, seize the evil spirit and his 
crew and cast them out, destroy their weapons, remove 
unbelief, and make all the members of the body servants in 
the cause of God and righteousness. These things did our 
Lord desire to teach us in the history of him who was sick of 
the palsy. 

In order to present in a more forcible manner the 
divine power of His balm—that is, the irresistible energy of 
His healing word in its action upon the body and to manifest 
Himself as the Physician of the soul—He performed these 
external cures, which are much more easily accomplished 
than the healing of the soul, forasmuch as the one only 
extends to the perishable, but the other lays hold of 
eternity. Therefore it is further affirmed in the Gospel—“And 
behold certain of the Scribes said within themselves, This 
man blasphemeth. And Jesus knowing their thoughts, said, 
Wherefore think ye evil in your hearts? For whether is 
easier, to say, Thy sins be forgiven thee: or to say, Arise 
and walk? But that ye may know that the Son of man hath 
power on earth to forgive sins, (then saith he to the sick of 


104 


the palsy,) Arise, take up thy bed and walk, and go to thine 
own house. And he arose and departed to his own house. 
But when the multitude saw it, they marveled and glorified 
God, which had given such power to man.” 

Here we have again a beautiful portraiture of our 
heavenly Physician, Jesus Christ, and His work and office are 
suitably set forth. How He, through the energy of His 
almighty word, had both healed the inner and the outer man 
of this victim of the palsy. This clearly establishes the fact 
that He did not come into the world chiefly to heal the bodily 
wounds or afflictions of men, and that His blood was poured 
out not solely to heal a transient injury of the flesh, but 
more especially that the object of His mission and death had 
reference to the healing of that spiritual malady which perils 
the everlasting happiness of the soul. As He has said—“I 
came to call sinners to repentance;” but of this the 
Pharisees and Scribes did not know anything correctly, and 
although they most of all had need of this soul Physician, 
very few of them were healed, for they despised the 
Physician and rejected the counsel of God, and would on no 
account hear of His medicine, neither would they examine it 
or receive it. 

The individual who yet to-day hears how Christ raised 
up the sick of the palsy, strengthened and made him whole, 
and takes knowledge through this that he is likewise 
afflicted with a similar disease, yea, that in truth his soul is 
smitten with the palsy, that his entire spiritual nature is 
paralyzed, so that of himself he is incapable of doing that 
which is good. If he then comes under a realizing sense of 
his sad state to the Physician Jesus, lays before Him his 
wounds and sickness, and consoles himself with the grace, 
the help and mercy of the Redeemer, and receives the balm 
of the living word; he will hear the precious words of the 
Lord resounding through his heart. Believe, son! Thy sins be 
forgiven thee. If he embraces the promise by faith, he shall 


105 


yet to-day be renewed and be joyful in his spirit. He will 
have inward consolation and receive strength and power, 
that he may joyfully and uprightly advance on the way to 
the kingdom of God, and finally obtain everlasting salvation. 
“For ever, O Lord, is thy word established in the heavens, 
and thy faithfulness endureth from age to age.” Ps. 119. 

Concerning this we have an interesting illustration in 
the centurion and ruler of Capernaum. For although he was 
a heathen, he recognized through the grace of God the 
Physician and the balm, as he found it to the comfort of all, 
and with a strong faith acknowledged the healing power of 
Christ, and praised and glorified His office as he said to 
Jesus—“O Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest come 
under my roof, but speak only a word and my servant shall 
be healed.” The word of Christ was the healing to which the 
ruler appealed and which he desired. And what did the Lord 
answer? “He was astonished at his faith and said, Go, 
according to your faith so be it; and his servant was made 
whole in that very hour.” Did this happen to the centurion, a 
man of the world, to whom, however, the Lord bore this 
testimony—that He had not found such great faith in Israel? 
As this ruler was the recipient of such a blessing, let no one 
doubt the willingness and ability of Christ to heal, for His 
compassion still yearns over sinners: neither let anyone 
faint while he hopes for His help. No afflicted soul will be 
neglected or left to perish, if he applies to this skillful 
Physician. Let him but acknowledge and feel his sickness, 
and let him come with an earnest heart and seek Christ, ask 
help of Him, and receive in love and in faith the word of life. 
However protracted or sudden and alarming the sickness 
may be, if the afflicted only perseveres in seeking help and 
comfort from the Physician Jesus, and trusts in Him, he shall 
assuredly find deliverance from all evil. 

Hence, in the use of the Holy Sacrament, if we would 
derive comfort and obtain a healing blessing, it is absolutely 


106 


necessary that the Christian believer beforehand confesses 
and says—“O Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest 
come under my roof, but only speak a word, and my soul 
shall be healed.” From this it is proper to infer that 
Christians or true believers seek their consolation, help, yea, 
the healing balm of the soul, with God in Christ, and have at 
all times through faith obtained these blessings directly from 
Christ. Without faith in the Lamb of God, slain as our 
passover, the use of the sacrament will profit nothing, nay, 
will increase our condemnation. Dear, afflicted soul, look up 
to Jesus, and feed on Him by faith daily, as the living manna 
that even now falls on every household that is sanctified by 
the Holy Ghost. “Blessed are they that hunger and thirst 
after righteousness, for they shall be filled.”

CHAPTER 27 

In what way an Individual may recognize the operation of 
this Balm, and know that the Physician has taken him under 
His treatment. 


After we have considered the test of this heavenly Physician 
and His proficiency, we should not forget to examine 
ourselves with reference to the balm and its operation. We 
should carefully observe whether the Physician has received 
us, whether we are under His treatment, or whether He has 
forsaken us and we have rejected the remedy, or if this 
balm is working in us and what are its effects—that is, how 
much we have been benefited by it. 

This will a person experience by the diminution of 
error in the heart, and its increase in the truth as it is in 
Christ, growing in faith and exhibiting a new life. If the soul 
is under the influence of Christ, old things pass away—the 
carnal mind, a life of sin, and the lusts of the flesh are all 
destroyed. When the afflicted soul has been convinced of sin 
by the Holy Ghost, and weeps and mourns over its sad 
condition, but begins to look to heaven to obtain help 
through Jesus, and is alarmed on account of its sinful life, it 
may safely conclude that the remedy for evil is doing its 
work effectually upon him. 

Another token of the effectual working of the Divine 
balm is when the individual hates his sickness—that is, his 
sinful life—for the Physician hates the disease, and therefore 
applies His skill to the subduing of it. When, therefore, the 
afflicted begins to hate himself in his unsound state, and 
begins to deny himself and take up his cross, and follows 
the Physician, desiring the means of restoration and the 
health which the Lord can only give, then he agrees with the 
Physician, cheerfully does what is required of him, and the 
more thoroughly he is healed the more he loves the 
commandments of Christ—“yea, these are more precious 


108 


than gold: yea, than much fine gold.” These are 
unmistakable signs that the Physician has received the 
person under His care, and that His remedy begins to work 
happily; if he is not at once healed, let him still wait, and 
pray, and hope, and ask the prayers of others in his behalf. 
Although the Physician, Jesus Christ, knows the 
origin, the cause and the root of all the evils of body and 
soul, and is abundantly able to heal them, yet after we have 
become acquainted with the gracious hand of the Physician 
and His heavenly balm, yet is it meet that, as he would with 
a physician of the body, he should commune with Him 
concerning his spiritual sickness, and make the evils of his 
heart manifest to the Divine helper. Not trying to conceal, 
but faithfully own his disorders, and confess his loathsome 
malady, and call upon the Physician to restore his health 
and take away all his infirmities. As he begins to find out the 
value of the balm of life, let him take cheerfully all that is 
proffered by the Holy Spirit, and whenever any of the 
symptoms of the former disease appear, he must again 
resort through prayer and faith to the indulgent Physician 
who is ever ready to apply the healing balm. We should 
acknowledge our sickness—that is, confess our sins to Jesus 
—not because he has not from the first known all that is in 
us, and is cognizant of all our human ills and infirmities, but 
that He may see that we discover and lament the evils of 
our state. He knows all; is fully conversant with all that is 
within man, (John 2:25;) but this humiliation and confession 
are designed to remind us of our sickness, that we may 
more and more confess with sorrow and distress our 
unhappy and guilty state. We humble ourselves and lament 
our depravity, because we are blameworthy and have 
willingly sinned; but when we have seen the extent of our 
wretchedness and the depth of our misery occasioned by 
sin, we will rejoice the more when we experience the power 
of salvation, and with renewed spiritual health we look up 


109 


joyfully to heaven, praising the Lamb that was slain and that 
has taken away our sins. 

Another token of returning health to the soul is when 
we are thankful to the Physician, when we praise His balm 
and proclaim to others his love and faithfulness, that they 
may also repair to Him and abide in Him, and look to Him 
for every blessing. This He also requires of us, not merely 
for the benefit which thereby accrues to others, but also on 
account of the good which such labors of love secure for us, 
concerning which there is much to be found in the Psalms. 
To praise the skill of this matchless Physician and to keep 
constantly in view the healing balm, is profitable, as it daily 
leads our meditation to those themes, and keeps up an 
association of our thoughts with those objects that fill the 
soul with glory. 

Thus the afflicted soul, that has learned of Jesus and 
has been received into the Divine favor, knows full well from 
experience the efficacy of the heavenly balm and the 
goodness and mercy of the divine Physician. He has 
therefore come out of the dark and pestilential air of sin and 
walks in the light of God’s favor, and now that he has 
become a new creature he has power through grace to 
partake of all those means provided in God’s word for the 
strengthening of his soul, and may bring from the treasury 
which contains the balm of life such supplies as his 
infirmities may require, thus keeping under any symptoms 
that may appear of the disease which the great Physician 
has subdued. In seasons of doubt and temptation he repairs 
to the promises, knows from experience the particular 
promise or truth which will meet his case. Yea, the Bible is 
to him a very pleasant garden, which he enters to gather 
such fruits as his spiritual appetite craves, such flowers 
whose fragrance may refresh his spirit, and such herbs as 
are for the healing of the soul. O how delightful does he find 
the manifold provisions of grace! An heir of God and joint 


110 


heir with Christ, he rejoices amid the glories and the 
raptures of the kingdom of God, and sings to the praise of 
the balm and Physician through whom his soul is healed 
from all evil.

CHAPTER 28 
Concerning the Service and the Servants of the Heavenly 
Physician, Jesus Christ. 


Our Lord Jesus, like other valued and affluent physicians, 
has His appointed service and servants. These are prophets, 
apostles, evangelists, pastors and teachers, whose office it 
is to proclaim and publish Him as much as possible through 
the whole world as the Healer and Saviour of all men. They 
must praise His faithfulness, His love and mercy, which He 
exhibits towards the children of men and acquaint fully all 
afflicted ones—that is, all poor sinners—with His kingdom, 
grace, balm and benevolence, that they serve Him in the 
healing art as well as in the knowledge of this spiritual 
sickness, and direct all the afflicted to Him, and meekly 
invite and guide all the perishing to the Saviour of the world. 

Their office, moreover, demands that they carefully 
preserve in their hearts the balm of life, which Christ himself 
gives and distributes, namely, the word of salvation, the 
word of health and everlasting life. They must also faithfully 
follow their Master and Physician, that their example may be 
a testimony to others of the efficacy and power of the balm 
which He administers for the healing of the soul. That they 
must not undertake anything without His will is evident from 
their relation to Him, and in their ministrations to sinners 
they must look to Him alone, and in all circumstances which 
may seem adverse they must fly to His bosom. As He is 
their Lord and Master, they must honor Him with a cheerful 
obedience; neither may they adulterate the medicine, nor 
sell old specifics for some new nostrum. These are the 
offices of a servant of this Physician: therefore, above all 
things, must he be truthful, firm and upright. He must 
moreover, be well informed in regard to the Physician and 
the balm, and must perfectly understand the virtue of 
medicinal plants, and know how and when to apply the 


112 


water and the oil, that all may minister to the comfort and 
health of the patients who are brought under his treatment. 

Such a servant was the apostle Paul, (as well as other 
prophets and apostles and evangelists,) namely, a highly 
esteemed and unchangeable servant of our Lord Jesus. A 
servant of the New Testament, and a zealous and true 
steward of the mysteries of God; as he very beautifully 
writes to all the afflicted, as he speaks of himself and 
Timothy—“We preach not ourselves, but Christ Jesus the 
Lord, and ourselves your servants for Jesus’ sake.” That is, 
we are not the physicians or Saviours, but only servants of 
the only Healer and Saviour, who serve to promote your 
salvation by carrying about and making known the balm of 
life. “For God, who commanded the light to shine out of 
darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the 
knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 
Cor. 4:5,6. 

Here we have the Lord Jesus likened unto the Father 
from whom He received His office and full power of healing, 
through whom He was also sealed. Him should the apostle 
proclaim, and with word and a holy life he should bear 
testimony of Him. To all poor sinners should he convey a 
joyful message concerning Him: he was to remain faithful in 
this holy service while he made Him known in all the world. 
He did not dare to do anything in this service, neither speak 
nor purpose, unless the Physician Jesus wrought it in him, 
as he himself declares. Rom. 15:18. 


113 


CHAPTER 29 

It is not in all respects the same with the Servants of this 
Heavenly Physician, and the Servants of those who heal the 
body. 


Here we are to remember that the servants of Jesus Christ 
are not in every particular, as those who serve a bodily 
physician, who assist in the preparation of the medicine, 
carry it to the sick and sometimes administer it in his 
absence. But the servants of this Spiritual Physician, do not 
attempt to work upon the balm of life, neither to assume the 
place of the Physician in administering it to the sick, but 
they only point to the Physician, describe the medicine, and 
in this way serve Him. 

And notwithstanding they carry, so to speak, the 
vessel that contains the balm and salve that are good for 
the wounded soul, and make known by their holy life the 
excellency and power of the means provided by Jesus for 
the restoration of man to the Divine favor; yet does Christ 
the Physician never take from their vessel the healing balm 
for others. Nor could they, if they would, by their own power 
or skill restore the health of a single soul. The Holy Spirit is 
associated with Christ in His work of healing, and like the 
apothecary who compounds medicines according to the 
prescriptions sent by the physician, thus also is it the office 
of the Spirit to take of the things of Christ and show them to 
the sinner. For while the servants of this physician have the 
oil and the balm which he provides for their own use, they 
have not the power to heal others, but are merely the 
instruments through whom a knowledge of Christ and the 
Holy Spirit is conveyed to the miserable and wretched 
children of sin. The healing part properly belongs to these 
divine persons, and therefore no faithful servant of the Lord 
ever asks sinners to come to him, or to receive from his 
hands the grace of healing, but always magnifies the office 


114 


of Christ and guides the stricken and afflicted to the feet of 
Him who has said: “Come unto me, and I will give you rest.” 

Again, Christ first healed His servant Paul, (and also 
others,) and preserved in him this heavenly balm, that he 
might continually bear witness of its power, and serve the 
Physician with a joyful and free spirit. Paul, the servant of 
Jesus Christ, had indeed in his own heart the oil and balm, 
which is the Holy Ghost, to his everlasting well-being, but 
then he served his Master acceptably; yet never did he 
represent himself as the physician of souls; neither would he 
attempt to cure by his own power. He was too faithful, and 
had too high an appreciation of the character of Christ, to 
usurp His office, or to presume upon the exercise of 
prerogatives which pertained to God alone. Therefore, 
although he was made whole by the skill of this Physician, 
and had found the balm efficacious in the healing of his own 
spirit, yet he never invites souls to come to him for life, 
neither did he ever assert that he and those in his calling 
could heal souls. They pointed no one to themselves, nor to 
their service as meritorious, nor to anything outward 
through which sinners might attain salvation and health of 
soul. But they directed all to Him who had healed their 
spiritual maladies; namely, to Christ, who had helped them; 
for they knew full well that there was but one ordained of 
God who could restore health and beauty to the vitiated 
soul. But they found pleasure in the service of Jesus, and 
were never happier than when they could incline others to 
come with humble hearts and penitent minds to this great 
Restorer of our race, for they knew too well that unless 
those poor sinners would betake themselves to Jesus, they 
must perish forever. 

In regard to this service and the office of a servant, 
he (Paul) wrote to the Corinthians, as he could not endure 
that anything should be taken from the honor, the praise 
and the glory of the Physician, Jesus, and given to the 


115 


servants of the Lord. For this cause he clearly distinguishes 
between the work of the Lord and that of the servant, as he 
says: “For while one saith, I am of Paul, and another, I am 
of Apollos; are ye not carnal? Who, then, is Paul? And who is 
Apollos, but ministers by whom ye believed, as the Lord 
gave to every one.” 1 Cor. 3:4,5. That is, they are not lords, 
nor the bestowers of a living faith, or the health of the soul, 
but servants through whose labor ye were brought to the 
Physician, Jesus, and made whole. 

We are servants, says Paul, further, in v. 6 and 7, and 
have that which the Lord hath given us. “I have planted, 
Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. So then neither 
is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but 
God that giveth the increase.” But what is this increase? 
What but the health of the soul which succeeds the 
forgiveness of sin and the reception of the Holy Ghost. It is 
the living growth of the new man in the grace of God, which 
vitality is given and wrought not by the servant, but by the 
Lord. It is the work of the divine Physician, Jesus, through 
the Holy Spirit, and Paul was outwardly serving the cause by 
preaching and by directing the sinner to the King of grace. 
He was always proclaiming the skill and power of Christ, and 
among poor, afflicted sinners he seemed as though he knew 
nothing else than to speak of his Lord and Master; namely, 
that He, through His cross and suffering, became our 
physician in His blood, as he had before said. “For I 
determined not to know any thing among you save Jesus 
Christ and him crucified.” In whom we have a knowledge of 
our spiritual sickness, and in whom we also find the balm of 
life and eternal salvation.

CHAPTER 30 
The relation which this Balm sustains to the Health of the 
Christian Church and her Members. 


A distinguished physician in a large city has often many 
afflicted and men with various diseases under his care and 
treatment; these he proposes to cure by drawing from his 
medicinal treasury such antidotes as may be subservient to 
this end. Although these sick persons are scattered here and 
there, he attends upon them as frequently as he can, and 
when he cannot see them all he sends those in his service to 
minister to the wants of his patients. Very similar is it with 
Christ, the divine Physician. His afflicted ones are scattered 
all through the world, among all people, in all climes; but 
before Him they all lie as in one city, and He visits each one 
with His grace, and permits His servants to direct all to the 
same office, where they are exhorted to remain steadfast 
under His treatment. Although they are separated in person 
and are far from each other, and even do not know each 
other—but they all know the Physician and His balm, 
through which they obtain relief and through which they are 
by the same Spirit restored to the same spiritual health in 
one body or congregation, are baptized under the Head of 
the church, Jesus Christ, and drink in the same spirit. 1 Cor. 
12:13. From this people the forementioned sick are 
gathered by the Physician, Jesus Christ, into one fold, who 
shall with Him inherit and possess the kingdom of heaven. 
For Jesus was to die for this people, and not for them alone, 
but that He might bring together the scattered children of 
God. John 11:52. 

This is now the holy Christian church, the 
congregation of the people of God, the bride and the body of 
Christ. This is the company of all the chosen of God, (as 
Augustine describes the church,) who are scattered all over 
the world, but through the oneness of the Spirit and faith 


117 


through Christ, are gathered into one congregation or 
church, are healed and saved, and who alone are properly 
known to Christ, their Head, Lord and King, as they are 
governed by Him, and grow and are nourished and 
sustained by His holy influence, which descends upon them 
as the dew upon the grass. 

We do not speak here of the Christian church and her 
members, as she shall appear according to His purpose all 
gathered in eternal life, when her members will have no 
need of any more of the balm of life, for then all remaining 
infirmities are taken away, and she is pure and perfect and 
possessed of everduring health, for then all tears shall be 
wiped away from all eyes and God shall be all in all. That is 
called the church triumphant, of which we do not now 
speak, but of the congregation of believers, as it is yet here 
upon earth, and how she is born in Christ through His spirit, 
and which as in Him the Head she is united, and by the 
service of apostles and servants of Christ she is gathered 
and built up, whose members the heavenly Physician has 
healed, and to which He still adds of such as shall be saved. 

The holy Christian church, in which we believe, is 
scattered through the whole world, and is only known to 
Christ, into which are gathered the children of God to be 
nourished and healed, yea, they are already healed before 
God in the new birth of the Spirit through faith. But they are 
in reality partially sick on account of the flesh and blood 
which still clings to them, in which are yet found the 
remains of sin; on this account they will require the help of 
the Physician while they live. They are whole according to 
the new man born of God, sick in virtue of their old nature, 
which however diminishes day by day, and will be renewed 
that their spiritual health may improve. 

Briefly, if we consider them as they appear in God’s 
favor, we shall discover the members of the body of Christ— 
that is, the congregation of saints or the church—without 


118 


spot or wrinkle. Eph. 5:27. This character they acquire 
through the Divine skill and knowledge of the Physician and 
their faith in Him, and they are consequently not regarded 
by Almighty God any more according to the old man of sin— 
that is, that which is not entirely healed of their infirmities— 
but He looks upon them as the purchase of the Saviour’s 
blood and as new creatures. Their sins are forgiven for 
Christ’s sake, and they have received the Holy Spirit, who is 
a perfect balm for the soul, and whatever of evil and 
weakness there may remain in their natures, will not for the 
sake of Christ and the Spirit be laid to their charge. Hence 
they no longer walk as formerly in the blindness of the flesh, 
but in the spirit and in light, and through the Spirit they 
mortify the deeds of the body. They grow in grace, and 
under the influence of the heavenly Physician advance in 
purity, until even their flesh shall rise glorified and holy from 
the tomb on the morning of the resurrection of the just. To 
the dawn of that glorious day faith now looks with joyful 
hope, for out of the ashes of this poor shattered mortality 
there shall arise a body like unto Christ’s, clothed with the 
youth and beauty of a fadeless immortality. 

Concerning such a church and the spiritual health of 
her members, as she already appears before God, Paul 
speaks to the Ephesians when he says—“Christ is the head 
of the church, and the Saviour of her body;” yea, her Helper 
and Physician, as he afterwards affirms. That He has loved 
the church, and out of great love gave Himself even unto 
death for her, that He might redeem her, so that He might 
sanctify her through His blood and cleanse her through the 
washing of regeneration by the word of life, that He might 
present the church to Himself,—a glorious church, without 
spot and without blemish, or wrinkle or any such thing,—but 
that she should be holy. 

There are some teachers who do not distinguish 
between the new and the old man, and who do not 


119 


understand the Christian church; who suppose that this 
passage points to the future church triumphant, all 
perfected, although the text unmistakably points to the 
present militant church. He speaks of her as she is here 
begotten of the flesh and members of Christ’s body: as 
through the service of apostles, evangelists, and such 
servants as He enumerates in the fourth chapter is built up, 
sanctified and blameless: such were the Ephesians and all 
true believers. 

Paul says Jesus Christ, our Saviour, has healed the 
church and her members, has washed, purified and 
sanctified them, namely, cleansed them from their 
impurities and spiritual sickness. Wherefore? Aye, therefore, 
that they might walk to the praise of His grace and glory, 
holy and blameless. That they should henceforth no more be 
the servants of sin unto death, but of righteousness and life. 
In his Epistle to the Romans he speaks yet more fully on the 
importance of the church’s purity, and says nothing unclean 
shall enter into the city of God, the New Jerusalem—that is, 
the kingdom of heaven. In the Song of Solomon it is written 
—“Thou art very fair, my love, and there is no spot on thee; 
thy sweet odors are more precious than frankincense.” 

From this it may be easily inferred what the Christian 
church is, and the aspect in which we are to view her 
members in relation to the balm of life and spiritual health, 
in how far they have become whole, and to what extent 
they are yet sick. They are yet living in a world where they 
have to do with flesh and blood. But we may also see that 
those err who suppose that Paul, when he speaks of the 
purified and sanctified church, points to the future in yonder 
world—to the holy in heaven who compose the general 
assembly of the just. But such teachers look only upon the 
outward, and regard the simple mass of people, who go to 
hear a discourse or partake of the sacrament, but who do 
not look upon their spiritual state as it is in God’s sight, nor 


120 


yet regard Christ as the head of the church. Neither have 
they discerned the Lord’s body: how it is joined to the head, 
and how the members of His body partake of His life. But 
the Holy Scriptures do not speak otherwise of the church 
than that which we have already advanced. 1 Cor. 12; 2 
Cor. 11; Col. 2; Eph. 4:5. 

In regard to the parables in the Gospel of the tares, 
the rejected fishes, the foolish virgins, and the unproductive 
branches, &c., with which some attempt to support their 
theory, these can all be interpreted consistently with the 
plan of salvation without adopting the idea that the 
sacraments are sources of grace. What profit is baptism and 
the Lord’s Supper if the heart is not changed? There is no 
power in the ordinances of religion to change the soul 
against the consent of the individual. The foolish virgins, the 
tares, &c., are intended to represent such as imitate the 
example of true believers: they assume their form of 
conversation and outwardly bend to the usages of the 
church, and thus deceive even the ministers of religion, until 
there is a manifestation of their true character and they 
stand exposed as hypocrites. 

The parables to which we have referred should not be 
understood as designed to represent open and gross 
sinners, but those hypocritical professors who mingle with 
God’s people, who never regarded themselves as spiritually 
sick, but are puffed up on account of their works and 
ceremonies. They in their pride esteem themselves whole, 
although they are the most afflicted in God’s sight, and in 
consequence of their arrogance and censorious spirit, are 
the most miserable of all the sinners that are healed by 
Jesus Christ. They reject the relief offered by the Saviour, 
and by their walk and conduct deprive themselves of the 
healing power of the Physician, because they do not esteem 
Him nor use His balm, but desire to help themselves; they 


121 


may not consider themselves as sinners, but as righteous 
persons. 

The Christian church is holy, and as the noble bride of 
Christ, altogether pure and glorious in the sight of God. 
Although she was sick and unholy, the Son of God, Jesus 
Christ, offered Himself up for her, and through His own 
blood He healed and purified and sanctified her. The 
members of the church, in consequence of the flesh and 
indwelling sin, are still somewhat sick and imperfect; as 
James says, “In many things we all offend,” and therefore 
have they daily need of the Physician and the Pardoner of 
sin, until they are made perfect. Yet before God, according 
to his foreknowledge, they are already through His grace 
whole, for they are cleansed and sanctified with the blood of 
Christ through His life-giving word, and with which Christ, 
the High Priest, still sprinkles them to salvation. Yea, they 
are according to His word entirely pure, although they must 
humbly walk while in the flesh and with all God’s saints pray 
—“Forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who 
trespass against us.” They know, with Paul, that in their 
flesh there dwelleth no good thing, and that the flesh lusteth 
against the Spirit of God. They also exclaim with John—“If 
we say that we have no sin (that is, we are not sick) we 
deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us; but if we 
confess our sins, he is faithful to forgive our sins and to 
cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” That is, He helps us 
and delivers us from all the remaining evils of our spiritual 
sickness. 1 John 1:8,9. Although all Christians have sins, 
they should not therefore commit sin, for he who has not 
been healed by Christ the Physician, neither does the work 
of one who has been restored to spiritual health, can in no 
way be regarded as a member of the Christian church. 

From the company of members which compose the 
Christian church and whom the Lord Jesus has helped, He 
chooses special servants, who are to make everyone 


122 


acquainted with the Physician of the soul, and through their 
instruction and life also bring many to Him. For they know 
that all men by nature are sick, depraved and imperfect: 
that they are full of sin and have need of this Physician. The 
servants of the Lord comfort men by the promise of health 
to all who come in faith to this Physician. The servants do 
not claim power to give spiritual health, neither do they 
pretend to heal the wounds of the soul, but joyfully point all 
to the Lord Jesus Christ as the all-sufficient Saviour. Neither 
do they conceal from the despisers of Christ the injury and 
damnation that must result from such a course. But they are 
also forbearing, friendly and patient towards the afflicted. 
“For we (says Paul) preach not ourselves, but Jesus Christ 
the Lord, and we your servants for Jesus’ sake.” 

The foregoing is said of the service of Christ and to 
the praise and honor of our heavenly Lord, whose servant 
Paul was, and who would not have the glory of healing souls 
ascribed to himself nor to anyone, but to the Lord Jesus. He 
only served, and the Master healed; he admonished the sick 
to repair to the Physician and seek His help. St. Paul speaks 
under the tuition of the Holy Ghost, 2 Cor. 4, concerning this 
balm of life and of the grace of the Gospel, which he, by 
order of this divine Physician, carried about. “We have this 
treasure,” namely, the balm of Christ, “with which we and 
all wounded consciences are healed; and by which all feeble 
hearts are comforted, and all the afflicted in body and soul 
healed to everlasting life. This precious treasure,” says he, 
“we have in earthen vessels, that the power might be of God 
and not of us.” Here then we perceive clearly that the balm 
does not belong to the servant Paul, neither does it proceed 
from his ability. Therefore does the power of healing—that 
is, righteousness and salvation—come not from the servant 
into the heart and soul of the spiritually sick, that no one 
might glory in the flesh; but it proceeds directly from God in 
Christ, who is the Head of the church, the fountain of health, 


123 


of salvation and life, so that the glory of this power 
redounds again to the praise of God forever. 

That all this is to be understood of the heavenly balm 
in its influence upon the sick, is evident from the language 
of Paul, 2 Cor. 2:14-17. “Now thanks be unto God, which 
always causes us to triumph in Christ, and maketh manifest 
the savour of his knowledge by us in every place. For we are 
unto God a sweet savour of Christ, in them that are saved, 
and in them that perish. To the one we are the savour of 
death unto death; and to the other the savour of life unto 
life...For we are not as many who corrupt the word of God, 
but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God, speak 
we in Christ.” 

In a word, Christ heals and treats through the Spirit, 
who is indeed much more present to us through grace than 
the servants of Christ could be, as it is also written in the 
Psalms—“The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him, 
that call upon him in truth.” So that the true knowledge of 
God and Christ is through the Spirit, whom Paul serves by 
laboring with the outward man, to bring him under the 
influence of the Gospel; but the Spirit applies the balm and 
imparts courage, peace and life. 

We have endeavored to make this point clear,—so 
transparent that there may be no room for 
misapprehension,—that while the chosen servant of God has 
received a special anointing, divine strength, and various 
gifts from Christ, that these might minister to his own health 
and to that of others, and that while his influence should be 
good and his virtues breathe a delicious fragrance around 
the path of life; yet must not the afflicted seek in the 
minister or his service that healing balm which restores the 
soul. The sin-sick soul must seek help from the Lord Jesus; 
the servant must point the inquirer to the right hand of God, 
where He sits who dispenses the blessings of life and 
salvation. The servant cannot pour this blessing into the 


124 


hearts of men; but the Holy Spirit quickens and makes alive. 
Where the Spirit is not present, there is assuredly nothing 
properly done towards the restoration of the soul—there is 
no improvement—everything is dead and without benefit; 
the servant may do how and as he will. 

The Lord Jesus will Himself apply the salve to the 
wounds of the soul and heal her maladies; this should be 
shown for the comfort of the sick, for no one can enter into 
the depths of hearts, nor to the bottom of the soul, nor into 
the secrets of the conscience, but God in Christ through His 
word of life. Therefore does Paul say in the passage already 
cited, that the power and the ability are not with the servant 
but in God. That the sweet savour of the balm with which 
we are healed is Christ’s and not his own, as he also 
elsewhere expresses himself. “But by the grace of God I am 
what I am; and his grace, which was bestowed upon me, 
was not in vain; but I labored more abundantly than they 
all: yet not I, but the grace of God which was in me.” 1 Cor. 
15:10. 

Therefore should we not direct afflicted souls to 
outward recipes, to human ceremonies, or church 
regulations, with a view finally to obtain spiritual health, but 
away from and up through all to the fountain of life—to 
Christ, the spring of all our spiritual joys and hopes. 
Directing the sin-sick to the Physician Jesus, to His blood 
and Spirit, and to His saving word of grace, they will not be 
deceived nor disappointed. None should regard the outward 
forms of religion as the heavenly balm, but simply to Jesus 
Christ, his own office in the life-giving power of health, 
should be ascribed all glory, as in Psalm 115:1. “Not unto 
us, O Lord, not unto us, but to thy name give glory.”

CHAPTER 31 

How the Heavenly Balm is represented in the Holy 
Scriptures, and how its manifold properties are set forth 
under various names. 


In regard to this Heavenly Balm, and the appointed servants 
who follow the Lord Jesus, and in their ministrations to the 
church of God dispense these blessings, the apostle speaks 
of it in another place as the work of salvation. The reason 
for this name is, that the word heals the wounded 
conscience. In Acts 13:26, he thus preaches to the Jews: 
“Men and brethren, children of the stock of Abraham, and 
whosoever among you feareth God, to you is this word of 
salvation sent.” Here we are to observe for whom this Balm 
is sent, namely, in behalf of the children of Abraham who 
fear God, whether Jews or gentiles. There are such as are 
ordained to eternal life, and belong to the kingdom of God. 
They have, in an evangelical sense, felt the evil of sin, and 
experienced in their souls the poison of the old serpent, and 
having seen and lamented their spiritual disorder, they 
yearned and sighed for the Physician and His balm. They are 
the true-hearted and meek, who cherish a godly fear and 
have abiding sorrow for their sins. “The fear of the Lord is a 
fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death.” Prov. 
14:27. Again, those who fear the Lord will prepare their 
hearts for the word of salvation, which is able to save their 
souls. “Be it known to you (says Paul) therefore, men and 
brethren, that through this man is preached unto you the 
forgiveness of sins. And by him all that believe are justified 
from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the 
law of Moses.” 

We have then briefly comprehended in the sermon of 
Paul the substance of this Divine means, though in various 
words, yet meaning the same thing—that is, the heavenly 
Physician and the balm of life, which is the word of God. The 


126 


apostle also mentions the sickness of the soul, which is sin, 
or rebellion against God and other iniquities. Afterwards he 
speaks of spiritual health, which is the forgiveness of sin, life 
and righteousness, which are all beautifully presented in the 
labors of Paul, and also how these are related to Jesus and 
obtained by faith in His atoning merits. He has clearly 
proved that neither Moses nor the law (much less other 
doctrines of men) are able to deliver from sin—that is, heal 
the soul of its manifold infirmities—but that Jesus Christ is 
the only Physician, and that His word, which is spirit and life 
and the power of God, is the only balm of life by which the 
wounds which sin has inflicted can be cured, and the soul 
healed and made a joyful heir of the kingdom of God. 
Therefore I affirm that it is not an unmeaning phrase 
which Paul uses when he calls it the word of salvation, since 
it heals the wounded conscience, while it makes the person 
pious and righteous. For the same reason, when he 
addresses Titus and Timothy, he calls this spiritual balm the 
wholesome doctrine and the healing word of our Lord Jesus 
Christ, and that through this ointment and the blood of 
Christ the broken heart is bound up and the afflicted soul is 
made whole. Again, he calls this balm the word of 
consolation. For what can be more soothing to the afflicted 
than to have his pains diminished, yea, that through the 
Holy Spirit he obtains as a gift everlasting health? In 2 Cor. 
5:18,19, he calls this divine medicine the word of 
reconciliation, because it reconciles alienated and 
disobedient man to God, and brings him into fellowship with 
his Lord. “And all things are of God, who has reconciled us 
to himself by Jesus Christ, and has given to us the ministry 
of reconciliation. To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling 
the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto 
them, and has committed unto us the word of 
reconciliation.” He has sealed us to this ministry—He has 
given unto us the Holy Spirit and qualified us to proclaim 


127 


publicly to you this balm of life, in which capacity we serve 
the Physician. 

“Now then we are ambassadors (says Paul) for Christ, 
as though God did beseech you by us, be ye reconciled to 
God.” As if he had said, Jesus Christ does no longer speak or 
act for Himself outwardly, but willing that you should 
become acquainted with the balm of life and know of the 
healing art, He has chosen us, that we might serve you in 
external things for Him to your salvation. Suffer then the 
Lord Jesus to heal you, that ye may be made whole. And 
hereupon follows a beautiful conclusion as he says—“For he 
(that is, God the Father) hath made him (that is, His 
beloved Son, who had no personal disobedience or sin,) to 
be sin for us, who knew no sin: that we might be made the 
righteousness of God through him.” He who prayerfully 
meditates on this passage will ere long learn to know better 
the Physician, whom to know is life everlasting. 

In Rom. 1:16, he calls this balm the Gospel of Christ. 
“It is the power of God to salvation to every one that 
believeth.” Then again he calls it the word of faith, because 
it is alone through faith that it can be received into the soul, 
and through which the heart is sanctified. Through the 
power of the word the soul is transformed, man is made a 
new creature, and his thoughts and affections are lifted from 
the perishable objects of life to that crown of glory that is 
offered to all who endure faithful unto the end. “Seeing that 
ye have purified your souls in obeying the truth through the 
Spirit unto unfeigned love of the brethren, see that ye love 
one another with a pure heart fervently. Being born again, 
not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible by the word of 
God, which liveth and abideth for ever.” 1 Pet. 1:22,23. 

Behold how Peter here designates the word of God as 
a living seed, that falls into the believing heart, through 
which we are born again and brought from death to life. For 
it is true, as we have already remarked, that whatever this 


128 


balm of life operates in the believer, is only an effect of its 
own nature. For it is spiritual health—it is the power, 
strength, peace, life and everlasting salvation. All these, 
together with inestimable heavenly treasure, does this balm 
secure to the believing soul. It makes sound heavenly- 
minded men, who do not think nor live according to flesh 
and blood, but they are minded according to God and walk 
in the Spirit. This is, however, not attained by any creature 
power, but is given by Christ to them that love and obey His 
Gospel. 

There are other names by which this balm of life is 
known in the Holy Scriptures. It is called bread, the true 
bread of the children of God, that drives away the hunger of 
the soul, and strengthens the weakness of the heart. From 
this bread the Canaanitish woman begged to obtain but a 
single crumb, for her and her daughter’s health. It was 
concerning this that David in the spirit of prophecy said 
—“And bread that strengtheneth the heart of man.” “This is 
the bread that cometh down from heaven, whereof if a man 
eat he shall never die.” 

Again, this heavenly balm is called water. The water 
of grace and life, which Christ gives and through which the 
afflicted, panting soul is quickened, refreshed and made 
whole, and concerning which the Saviour said to the 
Samaritan woman—“Whosoever shall drink the water that I 
will give him, shall thirst never more; but the water that I 
give, shall be in him a well springing up into everlasting 
life.” John 4:14. To this water of life does the prophet Isaiah 
invite, when he cries—“Ho! every one that thirsteth; come 
ye to the water of life; and he that hath no money come ye, 
buy wine and milk, without money and without price.” Isa. 
55:1. Thus also does the Spirit cry to a perishing world in 
Rev. 22:17—“Whosoever is athirst, let him come; and 
whosoever will, let him partake of the waters of life freely.” 
“And wherever this stream shall flow it will give life.” Ez. 


129 


47:8-12. That is, this water is the vivifying and fructifying 
power of the heart, and shall clothe our world with the 
beauty of salvation. 

Another emblem of this heavenly balm is fire. That 
divine fire which consumes the internal sickness—that is, sin 
and the evil desires of the flesh—and purifies the heart, 
concerning which Christ says—“I am come to kindle a fire on 
earth, and I wish it to burn already.” And in Mal. 3:2,3—“For 
he is like a refiner’s fire and like fuller’s soap. And he shall 
sit as a refiner and purifier of silver, and he shall purify the 
sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver, that they 
may offer unto the Lord an offering of righteousness.” 

This heavenly balm is finally spoken of as the tree of 
life. The fruit of this tree shall be for the nourishment of 
pious souls, and its leaves shall be for the healing of 
nations. “To him that overcometh, I will give to eat of the 
tree of life that is in the midst of the Paradise of God.” But 
who could enumerate the manifold names under which this 
heavenly balm, this unspeakable treasure is represented in 
God’s word? And all this through the love and grace of God. 
In it are concealed eternal glory, unfading riches, an 
imperishable kingdom: and this all is offered to poor, hell- 
deserving sinners, and freely bestowed on believing souls 
through the atoning merits of Jesus Christ. How sad the 
condition of those who have no desire to be relieved! Even 
God mourns over them in that pathetic language—“Is there 
no balm in Gilead, is there no physician there? Why then is 
the health of the daughter of my people not recovered?”

CHAPTER 32 

The Necessity and Value of the Holy Scriptures to a correct 
apprehension of the Divine Physician and the nature of this 
Heavenly Balm. 


We have now presented the heavenly balm under a variety 
of names, such as the eternal word of life, as well as the 
human nature of Christ in the word of the Lord Jesus, and 
also the Holy Ghost and the grace of God, and many other 
terms under which this remedy for afflicted souls is imaged. 
And all was then summed up in this: that Jesus Christ, 
together with His riches, is the Saviour, the Physician and 
the Balm of our souls. Now the question arises, whether the 
Holy Scriptures may not also be regarded as a medicine for 
the soul? Upon this point we desire to speak wisely and 
cautiously. The Scriptures, as contained in the Old and New 
Testament, were pronounced by the Spirit of God; they were 
written by the direction of God, who moved holy men to 
teach, to reprove, to instruct and to enlighten mankind, and 
were recorded for the benefit of all Christian believers, that 
they might be profitable to their knowledge of Christ and to 
their faith in the Saviour of the world. We should therefore 
gladly hear the word of God and study it diligently, that we 
may thereby be exercised in the grace of God. The Holy 
Scriptures are worthy of our esteem, and we should regard 
them as a precious treasure, and love them above all other 
writings, for however excellent the latter may be they are 
not divine. But although the Scriptures are more precious 
than gold, they cannot subserve as a physician to the soul, 
for the word of itself cannot heal or save anyone. This is 
manifest from the scribes in our Saviour’s day, and is also 
evident in those who at this time handle and teach this 
word, but are themselves strangers to God. The letter 
killeth, the Spirit maketh alive. Although the Lord Jesus acts 
through many instrumentalities, yet the office of spiritual 


131 


Physician belongs solely to Him, and He alone can exercise it 
through the Spirit in the regeneration and sanctification of 
souls. The Father invested the Son with power and sealed 
Him to this work of salvation. Our Lord teaches this fact 
distinctly as He speaks to the Jews—“Search the Scriptures, 
for in them ye think ye have eternal life, and they are they 
which testify of me.” “But ye will not come unto me that ye 
might have life.” 

Here then we see clearly what the office of the Holy 
Scriptures is, and at the same time we discover the office 
which peculiarly belongs to Jesus Christ, as our divine 
Physician, and He alone has eternal life. As the author of 
life, He alone is able to bestow life—that is, spiritual health— 
upon those who truly believe. Therefore do the Scriptures 
show us who this Physician of souls is and what the balm of 
life is, while they also speak of the sick and the disease from 
which they are suffering. The Bible testifies abundantly 
concerning the promised Messiah, and also with respect to 
the balm of life by which He was designed to work His 
wondrous cures. But the word of God is not in itself the balm 
of life for perishing souls; not more so than the minister of 
Christ, neither should it be so regarded; but the Spirit gives 
efficacy to the truth. 

This fact is beautifully illustrated in the example of the 
prophet and his servant as recorded in 2 Kings 4:31. The 
servant Gehazi in vain attempted to bring back to life the 
child of the Shunammite woman, but the prophet Elisha 
must himself come down and with his own breath infuse life 
into the child. Christ, our Elisha, must Himself come down 
from heaven, that, as a divine Physician, He might heal and 
make alive poor sinners. Neither servant nor staff, neither 
prophet nor the word, neither Moses nor the law could 
restore the spiritually dead to life. 

Thus it is even now: He who is the life of the world 
must animate the dead spirit of sinners with His life-giving 


132 


breath, for only through Him can we be made whole, pure 
and holy, and become partakers of everlasting life. The 
Shunammite woman was not satisfied with the servant or 
the staff of the prophet, but Elisha must come himself; thus 
also must we seek Jesus in true faith, and not trust in 
anything but Him. And this can only take place when we lift 
our hearts above all other objects, and look beyond the 
mountains that intervene to Christ and fix our affections 
upon Him who sitteth at the right hand of God. We must, as 
much as possible, lift our afflicted mind through grace to 
God, and as we are taught by Paul, “present our bodies as a 
living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is our 
reasonable service.” 

This is, moreover, abundantly presented in the Psalms 
for our comfort—how the Holy Spirit, through the prophet, 
promises health and deliverance. “Unto thee lift I up mine 
eyes, O thou that dwellest in the heavens.” Ps. 123:1. And 
again, Ps. 121:1,2—“I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, 
from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the 
Lord, who made heaven and earth.” Paul also directs us to 
the same source when he speaks of the incipient operations 
of this heavenly balm. “Let us draw near with a true heart, 
in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from 
an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.” 
Heb. 10:22. “Let us hold fast the profession of our faith 
without wavering: for he is faithful that promised. Let us 
consider one another, to provoke unto love and good works. 
Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together.” In 
regard to love and good works we understand especially 
that the individual abides with the Physician, and that as his 
salvation has already commenced, he is anxious to have it 
perfected. Blessed are those whose strength is in Christ, and 
who follow Him with all the heart. Ps. 84:5.

CHAPTER 33 

Wherefore God has ordained the services of the Sanctuary; 
and how it is to be marked that the Servants of this Divine 
Physician and Shepherd are themselves sometimes called 
Spiritual Physicians and Shepherds. 


We have shown from the Holy Scriptures that Jesus Christ, 
the heavenly Physician, does Himself execute His divine 
office in ministering to the afflicted, and that He personally 
confirms and establishes His mission. That from Himself, 
through the issues of His power and Spirit, there proceeds 
the balm which blesses and imparts health. Yet 
notwithstanding these facts, He would not have undervalued 
the service to which His chosen and sent servants are called, 
who understand this balm of life and this oil of joy, and who, 
knowing Christ and His medicine for the afflicted, are able to 
serve Him by directing the poor and perishing to the source 
of life. Forasmuch as man is not flesh and blood simply, but 
these combined with soul and spirit, thus constituting a two- 
fold nature; thus also has the Lord been willing to deal with 
him after a two-fold manner, and enable him to obtain a 
knowledge of the internal and external natures of His 
kingdom. The internal character of man learns through 
Christ and His spirit, but the external through His chosen 
servants, the Scriptures, visible sacraments, and creatures 
of His power. “The spirit of truth will testify of me, and ye 
also shall bear witness.” John 15:26,27. 

Thus has a merciful God, through His internal healing 
power in Christ, and out of love and goodness, also ordained 
an external service by chosen servants, teachers and 
ministers, in whom He placed a great treasure for the 
outward man; so that the whole being, not only within, but 
also without, might be healed. Hence he receives instruction 
in regard to the Physician and the balm, and obtains a 
knowledge of his sickness by being reminded of his 


134 


depravity; thus awakened to a sense of his condition, he is 
conducted to Jesus, and his soul and body are sanctified and 
sealed to the day of redemption. Our gracious and merciful 
God and Father in Jesus Christ has placed over against the 
internal work of the Spirit these visible solemnities of 
religion, that the ministrations of the word and the 
sacraments in His church might redound to the praise and 
glory of Him who has ordained our Christian worship. 
Notwithstanding these means of grace, we are not to 
presume that the Master, the Physician Christ, is limited or 
confined to service or servants, that He is circumscribed in 
His gracious operations to this or that place; but God 
operates in Christ according to His freedom, without law, or 
ordinance or rule, other than His own pleasure. The Saviour 
should, therefore, be precious to us at all times and in all 
places, for the spirit worketh where he will, and may make 
to the soul a palace out of a prison. Yet such a delightful 
service and office is that to which the servant is called, that 
the faithful minister, who out of gratitude to His Lord, 
conveys the balm of life and the oil of joy, through which he 
himself was made whole to the afflicted whom he serves, 
and for the sake of his Lord he holds these remedies for 
spiritual sickness in high estimation, concerning which the 
Spirit admonishes through Paul. “Let the elders that rule 
well, be counted worthy of double honor, especially they 
who labor in the word and doctrine.” 1 Tim. 5:17. The word 
of God is the source of the balm for the soul, and the 
wholesome doctrine of the Holy Ghost delivers from eternal 
death, and makes us spiritually whole. For it is written, 
“Honor the Physician with marked distinction, that you may 
have Him in the time of need.” Although this might be 
regarded as spoken pre-eminently of the Lord Jesus, yet 
may it also with propriety be applied to those subordinate 
Physicians and watchers over souls, who according to the 
command of their Lord, minister to afflicted men, and 


135 


faithfully administer the balm of His word. But these do not 
point to themselves, but direct the afflicted to the true 
Physician of whom they testify. 

But the Lord Jesus out of pure love and grace often 
ascribes to His servants, that which He does through them, 
and also throws the shield of His personality about them and 
identifies Himself with them so closely that He regards the 
rejection of them as though the indignity were offered to the 
Master: “He that believeth in me, shall do the works that I 
do, and greater works, because I go to the Father.” John 
14:12. “He that heareth you, heareth me.” It happens, 
therefore, that the servants of the chief Physician, Christ, 
are also called physicians, just as they under the great 
Shepherd of the sheep are called shepherds of souls. For 
whoever is sent forth by Christ, is also sealed by the Holy 
Spirit to the office of shepherd, or carer of souls. The Lord 
and master of these servants further shows His interest in 
them, in that He works in them and through them with 
Divine power, strengthens them and helps, that the balm 
which they administer to the sick may be blest and become 
effectual to his health and salvation. 

Thus did our Lord at first send out His apostles 
clothed with power to wake the dead, to cast out devils, to 
cleanse the lepers, and to heal all manner of sickness. These 
are, therefore, called (1 Cor. 3:9) co-workers with God. Yea, 
He Himself, as frequently said, takes up His abode in them, 
and accomplishes all this in their service. As he has been 
exalted to be a Prince and a Saviour, He is the great 
dispenser of spiritual gifts; yet in view of His exaltation He 
suffered the believer to look upon those who minister to him 
as his physicians. The intelligent and pious minister 
understands full well that whatever good he effects, is to be 
ascribed to the power, and grace, and spirit of the great 
Restorer of souls, and not to Himself. 1 Cor. 12:11. He 
knows that God works everything in believing souls, 


136 


therefore, He directs all things to this end, that Christ, with 
God the Father and the Holy Spirit, may receive all the 
honor and the glory. 

Thus did Paul when the Iconians were about to offer 
sacrifice to him and Barnabas, when he healed one who was 
a cripple from his mother’s womb. “Sirs, why do ye these 
things? We also are men as ye are.” Acts 14:15. Peter, 
when he saw the Jewish multitude wondering and amazed 
when, in the name of Jesus, he had healed the lame man, 
also ascribed the glory to the Lord. “Ye men of Israel, why 
marvel ye at this? Or why look ye so earnestly on us, as 
though by our own power or holiness, we had made this 
man to walk? Faith in Jesus Christ hath given him this 
soundness in presence of you all.” Acts 3:12,16. 

While we are here speaking of the subordinate 
servants of the great Physician Jesus, we must also observe 
that as intelligent and skillful physicians of the body are 
governed in their prescriptions by the age, the nature, or 
idiosyncrasies of the patient, and also have respect to the 
character of the disease, so that they may administer that 
which is profitable to the afflicted; the same rules are to be 
observed in spiritual things. If a physician has a student of 
the healing art under his care, and sends him to visit 
persons under his treatment, the subordinate is always 
subject to the directions of his superior. The same regulation 
should be observed by those who are in the service of Jesus 
Christ. He has set apart His word, His death, His flesh and 
His blood for all men, for their spiritual healing, and now 
those who minister in these holy things are directed to 
impart to all such a measure of this heavenly balm as the 
capacities and wants of different men may require. Some 
must be nourished upon the milk of the word, others may be 
able to partake of strong meat. “The peace of God (says 
Sirach) is over all the earth.” Therefore must the servants or 
subordinate physicians exercise great diligence, first in 


137 


obtaining a thorough knowledge of this heavenly balm, and 
learn to discriminate properly, that they may give to each 
one in due season his portion. They must also strictly 
adhere to the commands of Christ, and present Him in all 
His offices to the afflicted, hold Him constantly in the highest 
estimation, await help from Him, that their labors for the 
sick that are entrusted to their care may not be neglected or 
grow worse and perish. 

And since this internal sickness of the soul is much 
more serious, weighty and dangerous than all other 
afflictions of the body, we should so much the more 
earnestly implore Almighty God, and the chief Physician 
Jesus, to send us good and faithful spiritual physicians. We 
should pray for ministers and laborers, as He Himself has 
taught us, that we may have provided against our 
weakness, our ignorance and forgetfulness, in their faithful 
warnings and admonitions, so shall we be encouraged and 
built up in our faith, and be able to bring others to the 
fountain of health. 

We are also exhorted to prove by their works those 
who represent themselves as servants of the Physician, and 
examine the spirits whether they be of God. That we may 
know in truth those who come to us, and what the nature of 
their ministrations is, and the kind of balm they prescribe. 
We should be convinced that the servant points to Christ, 
and glorifies and praises the Master of life; yea, and 
ascertain whether he truly knows the Lord Jesus, is 
thoroughly acquainted with the Word which is balm for the 
soul, and carries it in his heart, which may be known by the 
fruits of his life and the influence of his example, whether he 
goes after the sin-sick into the highways among the hedges, 
and leads them directly to Christ. All these things must we 
diligently consider, that we may not receive a false prophet 
instead of a true one, an unfaithful for a faithful one, one 
who leads rather the flock away from, than to the Lord and 


138 


Master of His balm. But the afflicted soul should itself learn 
to discriminate between the servant and the Lord, and 
understand the character of the medicine which it uses, 
whether the balm works within him, promoting his health 
and comfort as it certainly should. In a word he should 
observe whether this balm of life awakens joy and brings 
consolation, and whether his afflicted spirit is restored to 
health and salvation. 

All these things should in truth be patiently 
considered in Christ, by all those who console themselves 
with the hope of salvation and yearn for everlasting life. It 
should also be the constant desire of such to increase in 
wisdom, in grace, and in the knowledge of Jesus. The 
ancient proverb is yet to-day significant: “Beware of the 
unskillful physician,” which is to be pondered amid the 
distresses of the soul and the conscience much more 
earnestly than in seasons of bodily distress. But who are the 
unskillful and inexperienced physicians? There are both 
bodily and spiritual physicians who are so inexperienced, 
that they have no skill either in determining the nature of 
the disease which they are to subdue, neither understand 
the constitution of the body or the soul. Of such should we 
beware, who rely upon one thing, or one recipe for all 
diseases, who make little inquiry into the nature of the 
sickness and manifest no zeal with the afflicted soul, yea, 
who are themselves sick and cannot help themselves. There 
are also compounders who adulterate the medicine, who 
otherwise obtain their remedies from the right source; or 
they are not able so to administer the balm of life, as to 
quicken, strengthen, and console the poor afflicted soul in 
God’s grace. These cannot establish their skill either from 
experience, neither have they derived it from the Holy 
Scriptures. Beware then, those of you who would be 
established and confirmed in the faith and in the hope of 


139 


glory, of those who themselves are ignorant of Christ, and 
who know not that they have passed from death to life. 

The Lord, our heavenly Physician Jesus Christ, heal 
our hearts of all sickness. Deliver us from all evil desires and 
propensities, and fill our souls with holy affections. O, my 
God, do thou grant that the Lord Jesus, may be our 
salvation, and remain our consolation and balm in the Holy 
Spirit, so that we may be made Spiritually whole, be purified 
and qualified for His name’s sake, to suffer, and make us 
steadfast in professing the truth, that the Divine healing 
may be perfected in Him and God the Father, that we may 
be fitted to dwell forever in the kingdom of heaven. Amen. 
CASPAR SCHWENCKFELD.
