Committee to judge how far a church inthe City meets the needs of a 


population scattered over all parts of London. So far the attendance « 


has been very satisfactory. The needs of the congregation of St. Mary- 


le-Bow. have been met by the reopening, on Sunday mornings, of

Regard must. be directed especially to the period before the division |

This means a very strong insistence on the principle of authority. 
Kiréeff lays down four points: (1) There exists in religion such a thing

Universal Church. (3) That Church exists in perpetuity. (4) Its 
decisions are irreversible. 

But this authority of the Councils of the Church does not at all

irreversible. What Easterns maintain is that acceptance by the Church 
is indispensable to give validity to conciliar decrees.? For the hierarchy 
is not the Church. The consent of the laity is necessary to raise a 
Council to the rank of universal authority. © -— 
Hence, in the Orthodox conception, the ancient doctrine may become 
clearly defined, but no new doctrine can be added to the contents of 
the Faith. Only in this restricted sense can the principle of develop-

A FEW WORDS ABOUT THE CHURCH 
OF RUMANIA 
By MADAME ROMALO 


HE Orthodox Church of Rumania was at the height of her 

culture and-influence in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. 
In spite of Turkish suzerainty, which, though extorting ample revenues 
from the tributary country, kept disdainfully aloof from mixing in 
religious questions, and did not interfere with the free practice of the 
Christian religion, the epoch was rich in larned and saintly prelates, 
to whose writings the Orthodox Church in the East owes much of her 
culture, and who helped to seal immutably the doctrines of the 
Orthodox faith, strengthening it against the often renewed attempts 
of proselytizing from the Roman Catholics and Protestants. 

Pious princes and nobles at that time were building monasteries 
and churches that even to-day remain the most beautiful architectural 
treasures of the country. Rich gifts of estates acéompanied these 
establishments, enabling the Church to found monastery schools which 
for centuries were the only ones in the country. Unfortunately, many 
of the donors were animated by the most enthusiastic devotion for the 
~holy places, fountain- heads of their religion: such as the Monastery of 


_., the Holy Mountain Athos, the Churches of Jerusalem and Constanti- 


nople; and they transferred the land and the revenues-of their religious 
endowments to these sanctuaries abroad. 


This alienating of Rumanian lands and possessions to the benefit of 


. doreign institutions may have hastened the confiscation of Church 


property. It. spreadslowly with the is of the philosophic and

at the beginning of the nineteenth century. The Government preferred 
to use the income of the ecclesiastical estates for the needs of the 
country itself instead of allowing it to be taken to Greece and Con- 
-stantinople, where at that time, between 1815 and 1821, it was spent 
for Greek patriotic propaganda work, which sometimes was directed 
against Rumanian interests. In 1860, the last Prince of Rumanian 
nationality, Cuza-Voda, abruptly secularized the remaining ecclesias-

henceforth put under Government control and became a department 
of the State under the Ministry of Education. Lay schools were being

Government as a necessary concession to the superstitions of the

conscious of the high calling entrusted to them, and though the 
Ministry provided them with two seminaries, and later with two 
Faculties of Theology, the professors in the institutions being mostly 
secular and openly indifferent to the Christian Faith, the latter state of 
the priests became worse than the first. 

From being a Government department the Church soon became a 


political instrument, and the election of the higher clergy was decided — 


by party interests. The most deserving were not elected to the 
episcopal Sees, but those most protected by the party in power. 
Holiness and wisdom were at a discount, and flattery and intrigue won 
the day as never before. The lower ranks of the clergy, miserably 
underpaid and overworked, lost all faith in justice and right, and tried 


to scramble for a better living by every possible means, so as to be able 


to bring up their children more or less decently. 

The course at the ecclesiastical seminaries is divided into eight 
classes; having passed through these, the students are ordained and 
become village priests without further instruction, whereas the town 


priests are obliged to pass through the Universities,.after which some 


of the most able among them are sent to the Theological Faculties of 
Moscow, Czernovitz, or Athens, where they become wise in doctrine 
and theology, but remain barren in spirit. The few who went to 
Liepzig, though returning ‘with their minds still better furnished with 
exegesis and Critical studies, priding themselves on their modern 
- standpoint, certainly did not contribute to the sia revival of the 
Church.

Thé Church could have benefited’ more than it did by the reign of

Christians and spiritually minded, they were’ debarred through their 
alien religion from taking more than an official interest in the Orthodox 
Church. The King and Queen showed every respect for the religious 
institutions, following, as sovereigns, the customs of the national 
Church, but they tactfully refrained from countenancing any missionary 
work, however helpful it might have been. They knew that the raw 
susceptibilities of a clergy, which through the centuries had to repulse 
many proselytizing attempts, would immediately take alarm and 


stubbornly refuse even the most useful and helpful suggestions. This .

mainly centred in the worship, Dogmatic discussion has-not the same interest for him. 


The main duty in church. life is faithfulness to the tradition; and the typical

Indeed, on the contrary, he is constitutionally suspicious of himself in all matters 
of religious judgment. In the sphere of worship, equalty, he feels the subjective to

as large, are symbolical, and have their dramatic value at least in that way.

involves a crossing, and a bow at least, if not a prostration. ... These external 


actions do not spoil the prayer, nor must they take the place of “it. But they
