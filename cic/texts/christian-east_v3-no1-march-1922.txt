Most REVEREND AND HOLy SynNop, 

We, the undersigned, the Delegates of the “ Great Church,” and 
the other members of the Patriarchal Mission, having fulfilled, so far 
as circumstances permitted, the duty which you laid upon us, thus 
present briefly our report of the transactions. si 

As we consider, most reverend and holy Synod, the time of our stay 
in London, and as we recollect the lavish exhibitions of honour and 
sympathy bestowed upon the deputation of the GEcumenical Chair, the 
great thing we see before our eyes is the kindly countenance of the 
Most Eminent Archbishop of the Church of England, Randall Davidson. 
The Archbishop, who holds a distinguished position, not only in the 
Church, but in the State, treated us with the greatest honour, and even 
affection, from the first moment of our arrival till the last, showing 
thus his great reverence and sincere respect for the Apostolic Chair of 
Constantinople, and the historic glory which surrounds it. | 

Among these things of which we have spoken were the flattering 
meeting at the London railway station ; the honourable reception at the 
Archiepiscopal Palace, where His Reverence the Bishop of Demotica 
spoke some fitting words; an official dinner; an honourable reception 
in full meeting of the Synod of Bishops; and the presentation to the 
King and Queen at a reception at Buckingham Palace which was held 
at the time. | 

Meanwhile the gentle and friendly Archbishop and his no less 
gentle wife used every opportunity to show honour and sy mpathy. to 
the Patriarchal deputation. ec

Cecil; Sir S. Hoare, M.P.; Mr. Athelstan Riley, who loves our country 
and our Church; the University authorities of London; the heads of 
the libraries and museums; the ecclesiastical authorities and teachers 
of the different seminaries-and colleges at Winchester and Oxford

EASTERN ORTHODOXY AND ANGLICANISM 
By Proressor NICHOLAS GLOUBOKOVSKY, D.D. 


With reference to the book entitled “ The Relations of the Anglican Churches with the 
Eastern-Orthodox, especially in regard lo Anglican Orders,” by the Rev. J. A. Douglas, 
B.D., Vicar of St. Luke, Camberwell, London. 1921. 


NYONE who, like myself, is in the unhappy position of a Russian 
refugee of the Intelligentsia class, and has lost everything at home 
together with his dear Motherland, and has not even the right of free 
existence abroad owing to various severe restrictions and humiliating 
suspicions, cannot but find the greatest consolation in any uplifting 
calls which raise us above the maelstrom of the present-day devilry, 


and lead towards the general union of mankind in the salvation | 


of truth. 

Just such feelings have been rekindled? in us by the perusal of the 
attractive book by the Rev. J. A. Douglas, for it is distinguished by a 
highly religious purity of aim and a self-denying spirit of sincerity. 
One would like to say a few words, but during the last four years of 
Bolshevist barbarity, which has been openly permitted by “civilized,” 
“Christian,” and “humane” Europe, to tyrannize over and destroy 
unhappy Russia, we have suffered so much physically and morally that 


we have lost the habit of intellectual work, and often cannot hold a _ 


pen in our hand. In our exile abroad we have not the least facilities 
for even the mechanical work of writing. For this reason we hope 
that our readers will pardon the fragmentariness and the rather poor 
arrangement of the first comments which occur to us under the 
impression produced upon our mind by the fine work of the Rev. 
J. A. Douglas. | 

First of all, we value his burning zeal for that mutual Christian 
union which is not only our constant ideal aim, but also a duty of 
primary and practical importance, since panchristian solidarity is now 


becoming a matter of vital necessity. It is impossible, and even sinful, — 


for anyone not to see that the Socialist tyranny of Satanic atheism is 
challenging the very existence of Christianity, and is making organized 
attempts to destroy it altogether. The awful spectacle of Communist 
Russia, where Socialist ruffians are sacrilegiously defiling and trampling 


upon everything appertaining to Christianity, is too inexpressibly con- ” 
_vincing for us to have any doubts as to the devilish power and influence 


' We say “rekindled,” because we experienced these comforting feelings by 
being in direct communication with his Grace the Archbishop of Upsala, Lars Olaf 
Jonathan Soderblom, who is himself aflame with and inspires others to mutual 
Christian unity by his apostolic energy on its behalf.

OR the second year in succession an official service, under the joint auspices of 

the Government of Palestine and the Anglican Bishop in Jerusalem, was held 

in St. George’s Cathedral on December g, 1921, to commemorate the day of the 
deliverance of Jerusalem by the British Army. Among those officially attending 
the service were H.E. the High Commissioner and staff together with Lady Samuel, 
Mr. Ronald Storrs, Governor of Jerusalem, the heads of Government Departments 
and other senior officials and their ladies, the Mayor of Jerusalem, Ragheb Bey 
Nashashibi, C.B.E., the Consular body, Major-General Wardrop and his staff 
together with the officers of the Jerusalem Brigade, the heads of several communi- 
ties, including the Moslem notables and Grand Rabbi Jacob Meir, who is the head 
of the Sephardi Jews in Palestine, and His Excellency General Gabre Selassie, head 
of the special Abyssinian Mission then visiting Palestine. In the sanctuary, in the 
six stalls of the Episcopal Canons, were placed the heads of the other Churches 
(other than the Roman Catholic representatives, who did not attend)—namely, His 
Beatitude the Orthodox Patriarch Damianos, His Beatitude the newly-elected 
Armenian Patriarch Turian, the Venerable Jacobite Bishop of Jerusalem, and the 
Coptic and Abyssinian Superiors. 

The service followed the lines of the previous year, parts of it being peudered 4 in 
Arabic, Hebrew, and Greek. Four lessons were read—namely, by the Governor of 
Jerusalem in English, by*the Orthodox Patriarch in Greek, by the Rev. H. Danby in 
Hebrew, and by General Wardrop in English. The collects were read in English, 
Arabic, and Hebrew. The Bishop in Jerusalem and the East, Dr. MacInnes, preaching 
in English and Arabic, made an eloquent appeal to both Zionist and Arabic extremists 
to abate unjust pretensions, and by mutual concessions to enable peace to prevail i in 
Palestine. The very impressive service ended with “God Save the King’’ and 
Kipling’ s “ Recessional.” Bishop Bury, then ona visit to Palestine, also took part 
in the service, both he and Bishop MacInnes being vested in cope and mitre. 

On New Year’s Day Bishop MacInnes installed the Rev. H. Danby to a resi- 
dentiary and the Rev. Ibrahim. Baz to an honorary canonry of St. George's 
Cathedral. Canon Danby 1s well known as a distinguished Orientalist ; while 
Canon Baz, the first Palestinian to receive a canonry of St. George's, has been for 
over fifty years in holy orders of the Church of England, and is the incumbent — 
of St. Paul’s Church, the church of the Arabic-speaking members of the Church of | 
England in Jerusalem. Canon Baz, who remained at his post during the war,

MEMORANDUM ON SALE OF ORTHODOX

(Cished in the Pre misunderstanding, partly due to erroneous statements pub- 
lished in the Press, seems to exist as to the financial affairs of the Orthodox 
| Patriarchate of Jerusalem, and as to the efforts of the Financial Commission 
which is now endeavouring to repair its shattered finances and to restore it to a 
state of solvency. It may be recollected that a Commission of Inquiry, consisting 
of Sir Anton Bertram, Chief Justice of Ceylon, and Mr. H. C. Luke, Assistant 
Governor of ‘Jerusalem, was appointed by the High Commissioner for Palestine in 
January, 1921, to report upon the internal dissensions then existing within the 
Patriarchate, and to-make recommendations for the repayment of its debts, then 
amounting to some £600,000.’ It may also be recollected that the Patriarchate was, 
and is, preserved from bankruptcy only by the existence of a special Government 
moratorium. The Commissioners, whose Report has now been published by the 
Oxford University Press, recommend the appointment of a Commission of Financial 
Control, composed entirely of Christians. The duties of this Financial Commission

RELATIONS WITH MAR IGNATIOS 


By J. A. DOUGLAS 
» (Continued) 


N our December issue, as a preface to discussing Mar Ignatios’ 

enquiry as to the willingness of the Anglican Churches to give 
sacramental and other ministrations to his people in emergency, I 
analyzed, as I understand them, the chief causes of the separation of 
the ‘‘ Monophysite’’ Communion from the Eastern Orthodox. 

I also pointed out that the last two Lambeth Conferences had 
requested the Archbishop of Canterbury, in conjunction with thé other 
Anglican Primates, to enter into such an arrangement as that to which 
Mar Ignatios’ enquiry points, provided—(r) that he is satisfied that the 
Christology of these Churches 1s free from heresy, and (2) that, inasmuch 
as the Coptic and Syrian Jacobite Churches claim to represent the 
pre-Chalcedonian Patriarchs of Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem, 
the Eastern Orthodox would not hold such arrangement to be 
sustaining schism. In conclusion of this article, I propose to examine 
those two conditions. 

1. Is the Christology of the Jacobites heretical?—To answer this 
question in a final negative would require a more complete investiga- 
tion than is at present in my power. Unlike the Copts and Armenians, 
the Jacobites to-day are an extremely depressed Church, the members of 
which, numbering somewhere from gne to two hundred thousand, are

either to stark Eutychianism or stark Nestorianism. There were 
different local schools at Alexandria, Antioch, and so forth, in the 
Byzantinized upper world which from time immemorial had expressed 
their Christologies in forms that approximated’ to an over emphasis of 
the Godhead that in effect denied the Manhood. These formed material 
for little schisms, but only for theologians’ schisms. The whole body 
of the Church was intensely conscious of its Founder’s Personality, 
and had doubt neither of the reality of both His Godhead and His 
Manhood, nor of their “ hypostatic’’ union in one Christ. That the 
mass of Christians could have been brought knowingly to adopt a 
doctrine clearly and obviously inconsistent with that consciousness 
was impossible, and the avowed Monophysite heresiarchs of the day, 
for the most part, wrapped up their speculations in equivocations. To 
stampede the Egyptian and Syrian natives into schism a catch-cry was 
necessary. The phrase ‘‘in two natures’ served as such, and Chalcedon 
was rejected, not because it laid down the Catholic doctrine, but because 
it was challenged as contradicting it. .

Council was that the rejection of the phrase could only mean. the 
denial of the perfect Manhood. Thus the appellation ‘‘ Monophysite ” 
became a label of all who rejected Chalcedon. In the succeeding
