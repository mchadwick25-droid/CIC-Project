## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Alexandria kept its dead at its edges: necropolis districts east and west of the walls, old and vast, cut with tombs in every fashion the city's mixed peoples had brought - Greek chamber tombs, Egyptian embalmers' workshops still practicing an ancient trade, humble pit graves for the poor. Funerals moved through the streets with mourners and, among the old families, hired lament. Egyptian habit kept the dead close and tended: visits, offerings of food, the grave as a place a family returned to. Christians buried rather than burned, gathered their dead together where they could, and in time built chapels among the graves. The customs of grief - procession, lament, the meal at the tomb - were the city's shared inheritance before any community's teaching touched them.
