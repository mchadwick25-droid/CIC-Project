## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The baths were part of an Alexandrian day - men's hours and women's hours, the water warmed, attendants for hire, the baths a place to be seen and to talk business as much as to wash. The prosperous dressed to be read: purple-dyed cloth, gold set with stones, hair built and dyed, cosmetics laid on with practiced skill, rings carrying seals for signing. Mirrors, hairpins, perfume flasks - the furniture of a household's morning. A teacher of this world could catalogue all of it from daily sight. Poorer households knew the same culture from below: plain wool, a single garment mended, the public fountain instead of the bath's warm rooms on most days. The body was public in this city, and everyone dressed for the streets they had to walk.

## Period Note

Second-to-third-century texture from the same conduct manual: the body culture it addresses. Its counsel is Native-Constitutive.
