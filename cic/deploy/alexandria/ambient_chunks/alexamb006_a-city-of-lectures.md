## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Alexandria sold learning the way other cities sold grain. The Museum's fame was old; lecture halls, hired teachers, and private tutors filled the city, and young men traveled by sea to study there as a matter of course. A teacher's reputation was a public asset, argued over in the streets like a charioteer's; students attached themselves to a master, followed him for years, and carried his name afterward like a family name. Grammar, rhetoric, philosophy, medicine, mathematics - each had its teachers and its fee. The habit of the lecture, the habit of the question pressed in public, the habit of following an argument for the pleasure of it: these were civic customs, the air of the place, before they belonged to anyone's school in particular.
