## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Egypt wrote on papyrus, and the dry ground kept what it wrote. From the towns up the river survive the ordinary papers of people of this world: private letters commending a traveler, greetings between kin, accounts, receipts, lists. Christians wrote as their neighbors wrote - the same formulas of greeting, the same abbreviations a scribe's hand fell into - and their letters sit in the finds beside tax rolls and marriage contracts. Their books took a form the trade found unfashionable: the codex, leaves bound like a notebook, where the booksellers' classics kept to the roll. A reader held scripture the way a tradesman held his accounts. Most people met any written word through a voice reading aloud; a letter from a distant brother was a small public event, read to the gathered house.

## Period Note

Holds across the window; the papyri are documentary finds from Egypt's towns. What Christians WROTE as teaching is Native-Constitutive; this record carries writing as ordinary furniture.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
