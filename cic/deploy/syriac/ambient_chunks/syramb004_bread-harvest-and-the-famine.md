## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The towns of this world ate from the land around them: rain-fed grain from the plain, bread as the meal's center, gardens and orchards watered by spring and river, oil and wine coming in along the roads. That dependence had an edge. When the winter rains failed, the countryside emptied into the cities, and the cities discovered how thin the margin was. In one remembered famine at Edessa, late in this world's window, those with grain held it back while its price climbed, and the poor died in the open - in the streets and under the public porticoes where they sheltered. Relief, when it came, was organized the way everything civic was organized: beds set up, distributions made, the dead buried at common expense. A good year erased none of the memory. Everyone in these towns, whatever their allegiance, knew that bread was the daily question and that a failed sky could turn a city inside out in a season.

## Period Note

Rain-fed grain dependence holds across the whole window; the dated instance is the famine at Edessa of 372-3 attested in the Vita tradition. The famous relief story attached to a named teacher in that famine is a STORY record (Native-Constitutive, syrstory001); this record carries only the shared civic experience of dearth, which is what the whole city lived.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
