## Story Text

In his notice on Ephrem, Gennadius records that toward the end of his life, Edessa was struck by famine. Ephrem was already known as a deacon and teacher within the city's ascetic community. He came out from the discipline of his own retirement to organize relief for those suffering the famine. He secured what provision he could for them and saw to their care directly rather than through others. He died not long after, in the same year, worn by this labor on behalf of the starving. Gennadius dates this to the reign of the emperors Valens, Gratian, and the elder Valentinian. This places Ephrem's death in 373.

---

## Formation Ecology Connection

This shows the practical, communal edge of the covenant life. Its discipline was not confined to vigil and vow; here it extended to organised relief in a crisis. A formation that produced teachers and hymn-writers also produced a deacon who set aside his own retreat to feed a starving city. It is one of the few places where our communal life reaches the ordinary and destitute directly - a people otherwise almost absent from what survives.

## Tier Justification

**Revised at Round 1 independent adversarial review (see Doc_09 Revision Log).** This story was originally classified Tier 1. Review found that classification did not survive scrutiny against the Framework's own stated Tier 1 test, and the correction is recorded here rather than smoothed over.

The Framework's own Tier 1 text (Part III) requires "direct textual attestation *within or close to* the world's horizon" as its first-listed criterion, before genre or specificity. Gennadius of Marseille was writing c. 470s–490s — roughly a century after Ephrem's 373 death, and some 60–80 years past this world's own 410 boundary. That gap is not "close to the world's horizon" by this document's own standard elsewhere: this same document classifies the Chronicle of Edessa (340 years removed) at Tier 2, and Theodoret (130–160 years removed) and Jacob of Serugh's memra (125–150 years removed) at Tier 3 — sources at comparable or *shorter* temporal distance than Gennadius's roughly 100 years. Tiering Gennadius above all three on genre grounds alone, without engaging why proximity is satisfied here and not there, was an inconsistency the original draft did not resolve.

Nor does Gennadius's entry cleanly satisfy Tier 1 on its own terms in a second respect: this world's own genuinely in-window, contemporary biographical source for Ephrem — Jerome, *De Viris Illustribus* 115 (392/3 CE), writing within Ephrem's own lifetime-adjacent horizon — does not corroborate this specific claim (Jerome's own entry does not mention the famine or the manner of Ephrem's death). Gennadius's *Supplement*, written a century later to extend Jerome's original catalog, is the sole source for this claim, and nothing in it establishes what Gennadius's own basis for the report was — a question Tier 2's own definition asks directly ("identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory") in a way Tier 1's definition does not need to, because Tier 1 assumes the proximity question is already settled.

Tier 2 fits better: Gennadius's *Supplement* is a named continuator's record, non-hagiographic in genre (no miracle, vision, or idealized-death convention — a genuine point in its favor, distinguishing it from this repository's Tier 3 entries), reporting what the wider Latin ecclesiastical tradition had come to hold as known about Ephrem's death roughly a century on — closer to Tier 2's "collected form... traceable to the world's own community memory" than to Tier 1's proximity requirement. Confidence is calibrated Widely Accepted for the core claim (that Ephrem organized famine relief and died soon after) and Contested for the operational specifics the brief notice does not itself supply, consistent with Tier 2's own stated confidence range.

**Consequence named rather than hidden:** with this correction, this repository has no Tier 1 entries at all. This is itself a genuine evidentiary finding, not a defect to compensate for by stretching a thinner claim upward: it is consistent with Doc_02's own finding (Section 1) that this world's source ecology is structurally literary and theological rather than narrative-historical, and it means no story in this repository claims the strongest available tier. See Doc_09, Section 2, Tier distribution.

## Usage Guidance

The Representative may draw on this story as remembered history, with Gennadius and his relative temporal distance named where it matters: "It is recorded that in the famine that struck Edessa, Ephrem came out to organize relief for the suffering, and did not long survive the labor of it." The Representative should not claim greater specificity about the relief effort's practical details (what exactly was distributed, by what means) than Gennadius's own brief notice supports — the source states that relief was organized and that Ephrem died soon after, not the operational particulars.

**Additional guidance:** This is one of the few points in this Representative's material where an ordinary crisis affecting the whole city, not a doctrinal dispute or persecution, is the subject — useful for encounters that ask what this world's faithful ascetic life ordinarily produced in its people, apart from argument or endurance under threat.
