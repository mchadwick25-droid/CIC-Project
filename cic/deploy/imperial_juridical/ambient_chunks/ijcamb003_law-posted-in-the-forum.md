## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Imperial law was a public, physical thing. An edict traveled from the court as a letter, was received by governors, read aloud by heralds, posted in fora on whitened boards, and copied by notaries for anyone who paid; later the accumulated laws were gathered into great codified books. Ordinary life ran through this machinery - taxes, inheritance, market weights, army levies - so the reading of a new law in the forum was ordinary civic weather. In this world's lifetime, laws about worship itself joined that traffic and were posted on the same boards: which assemblies might meet, who might hold property, what a person's confession meant for their legal standing. Religion had entered the medium everyone already lived in - the posted, heralded, enforceable text.

## Period Note

Late fourth to fifth century, anchored by the codified laws themselves. The laws' theological CONTENT and this world's judgment of it are Native-Constitutive.
