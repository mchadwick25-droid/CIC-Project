## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The empire's cities kept their Jewish communities through this whole world's window - old congregations with synagogues, courts of their own for internal matters, patriarchs whose dignity imperial law recognized. The law books show coexistence as the daily default and show it under pressure at once: statutes protecting synagogues from seizure and worship from disruption stand beside new prohibitions - on holding certain offices, on certain marriages, on building new synagogues - accumulating decade by decade. Street reality moved with and sometimes ahead of the statutes: markets and neighborhoods shared, festivals mutually audible, and violence flaring in some cities with the law arriving afterward to rule on what the mob had done. A person of this world lived among Jewish neighbors whose standing was real, legally armored, and visibly narrowing.

## Period Note

Late fourth to fifth century, anchored by the legal texts: coexistence as legal and street fact. This world's own polemic and theology about Judaism are Native-Constitutive and are NOT carried here.
