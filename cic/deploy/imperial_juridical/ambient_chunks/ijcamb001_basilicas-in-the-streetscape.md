## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. In this world's cities the church became architecture on an imperial scale. Basilicas rose with public money: halls longer than any temple, marble columns reused from older monuments, gold on the ceilings, forecourts and fountains, whole processional routes organized around them. Rome's great shrine church went up over an old roadside cemetery on the Vatican hill; Milan held several such halls at once, close enough that a person could walk between them in an afternoon. These were the biggest interior spaces most people would ever stand in, and they belonged to the city's daily geography - landmarks for directions, shelter from weather, sites of crowds, employment for builders, mosaicists, and doorkeepers. A child of this world grew up assuming that the church, like the law, came in monumental stone.
