## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The working day owned everyone. Shops opened early; labor was hired by the day at first light; clients waited at patrons' doors in the morning hours; the afternoon ran on until baths and supper. A group of working people who wanted an hour together had to steal it from the edges - and an outside official, reporting what arrested members of such a group told him, recorded exactly that: they met on a fixed day before daylight, went off to the day's business, and came together again later to share ordinary food. Nothing in the schedule would have puzzled any tradesman. Dawn was when free time existed. The empire's cities granted no weekend; a community's calendar had to fit inside a working city's, in the dark hours the workday did not want.

## Period Note

Early second century, anchored by an outside official's own report: the meeting hour and the working day around it.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
