## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Death in these cities was frequent, public, and expensive to dignify. Funerals moved through the streets; tombs lined the roads outside every gate, from family monuments to the trench graves of the poor; ordinary tradespeople joined burial clubs that collected dues and guaranteed a member a decent funeral and a niche. There were no Christian cemeteries in this world's window - that came later - so the community's dead lay in the common landscape: a family tomb if the family had one, a club's columbarium, a plain grave. What a poor assembly could do, it did the way any burial club did - dues, hands, and attendance - and being carried to the grave by people who knew your name was, in these cities, a real inheritance to leave.

## Period Note

Holds across the window: urban death and burial as shared custom. Christian teaching about the dead is Native-Constitutive; the walked-back catacomb datings keep this record honest about what did NOT yet exist.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
