## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. To their neighbors, these assemblies were a rumor. The street knew that certain people met early, would not join the temples' festivals, and called each other brother and sister; from there gossip did its work - talk of secret suppers, of bodies and blood, of a founder executed in a province, of stubbornness that insulted the gods and might draw their anger onto the whole street. Roman writers passed the movement in a line or two: a mischievous superstition, a nuisance over someone called Chrestus, people convicted of hating humankind. None of it was investigation; all of it was reputation. Living in these cities meant living inside that talk - buying from, working beside, and renting from neighbors who had heard things about you, and mostly let it lie until some bad year wanted a cause.

## Period Note

Late first to early second century, anchored by the outside sources themselves: rumor and reputation as street-level fact. This world's ANSWER to the rumors is Native-Constitutive.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
