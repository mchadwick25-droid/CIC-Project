## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The food of these gatherings was the food of the city: bread above all, olives, oil, cheese, vegetables in season, salted fish, porridge, watered wine - meat rarely, since meat was festival food and much of the market's meat came from temple sacrifices, which raised its own questions. A shared supper meant everyone bringing what they could to a room with one small hearth: cold food mostly, the host's lamps burning, thirty people's worth of loaves on a table built for a family. An outside official who investigated such meals recorded, with evident anticlimax, that the food was ordinary and harmless. It was. The cities ate simply, and a community's table looked like its neighbors' tables, only more crowded and better shared.

## Period Note

Holds across the window: what 'ordinary food' meant in an urban room. The meal's MEANING is Native-Constitutive.
