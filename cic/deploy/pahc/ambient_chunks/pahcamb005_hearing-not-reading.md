## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Most people of this world could not read, and nothing about that was shameful - it was the ordinary condition. Letters, contracts, and books were technologies operated by specialists: a scribe for hire in the forum, a literate slave, the one or two members of an assembly whose trade required letters. Anything written reached the group through a voice - a letter from another city was read aloud to the gathered room, and the reader's stumbling or skill was part of the event. Memory did the work writing does now: sayings, songs, and stories were carried in heads, corrected by the room's older members. Ownership of a book was rare and notable; the community's texts lived less on papyrus than in the hour of hearing them, repeated week after week until everyone held them.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
