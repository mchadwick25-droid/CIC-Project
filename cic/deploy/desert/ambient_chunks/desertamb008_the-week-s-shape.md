## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The desert kept a week. Five days a man was at his cell - work, prayer, bread at the ninth hour, no one's voice but his own and a neighbor's through the wall of silence. On the weekend the settlement walked to its church: the gathering, the common meal, the exchange of finished handwork for the next week's palm and loaves, news traded, disputes aired, the sick reported. Then the file of men walked back out to their doors carrying bread and rope-fiber, and the silence closed again for five days. Villages within reach kept the same feast days everyone in Egypt kept, and the settlements marked the great ones. Time out here was not shapeless; it was a wheel of seven days, and every man knew which day it was by what his hands were doing.

## Period Note

Holds across the window: the weekly schedule as institutional fact. What the gathering MEANT - its worship and teaching - is Native-Constitutive.
