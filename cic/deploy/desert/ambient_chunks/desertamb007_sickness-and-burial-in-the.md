## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Sickness in the desert was nursed by neighbors. A man too ill to work was fed from other men's stores; the settlements kept their old alive for years past their strength, carrying water and bread to cells whose owners could no longer walk. Fevers came up from the valley; eyes failed in the glare and the smoke; the ordinary diseases of the country did not respect the boundary of the sand. When a man died, his neighbors washed and wrapped the body, said the psalms, and buried him in the sand or the rock near his cell, often before the day was out, as the climate demanded. Graves were plain and mostly unmarked. The practical shape of dying here was a village's shape, transplanted - neighbors' hands, quick burial, and the survivors dividing what little the cell held.

## Period Note

Holds across the window: illness, age, and burial as the life's practical facts. The teaching about death is Native-Constitutive.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
