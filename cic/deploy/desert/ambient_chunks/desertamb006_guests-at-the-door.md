## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The desert had visitors the way a shrine has pilgrims. Parties came up from the cities and even from across the sea - the curious, the devout, the desperate - and the settlements received them: water for feet, the pot set on, a mat for the night, an escort to the next settlement. Whole books were written by such travelers, counting the days and naming their hosts. Local traffic was thicker still: villagers bringing sick relatives, buyers for baskets, young men testing the life, families visiting a son. A famous elder's door could be as busy as a magistrate's. However fiercely a man guarded his solitude, the knock at the door was part of the desert's week, and everyone had a store of bread against it.

## Period Note

Holds across the window: the visiting traffic the literature itself is made from. What hospitality MEANT is Native-Constitutive.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
