## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The desert of the monks began where the black land stopped: a line a person could stand on, one foot in the sown, one in the sand. The valley was never far - a walk of hours, not weeks, for most settlements - and its life ran alongside: villages, canals, harvest and tax seasons, boats working the river. Travelers came out from the valley to visit the settlements, hiring guides, carrying water, sometimes losing the track; accounts of such journeys count the days between settlements and name the dangers - thirst, robbers, wrong turnings. Monks went the other way for markets and festivals and were known figures in the villages. The two worlds faced each other across that line every day, each entirely visible to the other.
