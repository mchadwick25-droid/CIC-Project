## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Rome's banquets were engineering: multiple courses, birds and fish brought at expense, wines vintaged and discussed, the meal built to display the house. Against that table this world set another: bread, garden vegetables, oil, watered wine or none, fish sometimes, meat rarely or never; fasts that skipped meals or days on a fixed calendar, eased for the sick, the old, and the traveling. In Bethlehem the community's table was institutional - regular, plain, provisioned like any establishment feeding many mouths daily, from gardens, markets, and purchased stores. Both tables were real and everyone in this world had eaten at both; the letters can describe a fashionable dinner course by course precisely because their writers had reclined through many before renouncing them.

## Period Note

Late fourth century: the banquet table and the ascetic table as the letters describe both. The MEANING of fasting is Native-Constitutive.
