## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Aristocratic Rome ran on a social calendar. Mornings began with the salutation - dependents and friends received in rank order; days were paid out in visits, litters crossing the city between houses; dinners were instruments of alliance, with guest lists read as declarations. The churches had joined the round: fashionable congregations, clergy who dined well and knew whose atrium to appear in, funerals and feast days as society events drawing the whole connected world. A great lady's day was public business - her dress, her escort, which widows and virgins gathered in her hall, which teacher read with them. This world's own letters describe that round in merciless detail, precisely because everyone addressed lived inside it and every choice about it was legible to the whole city.
