## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Bethlehem in this world's years was a small hill town with one enormous fact: the basilica over the cave, and the pilgrim traffic it drew. The town lived on that traffic - lodgings, guides, sellers of provisions, the road up from the coast delivering parties in season. New establishments rose beside it built with Roman money: houses for vowed men and for vowed women, and a hostel on the road so that travelers, as the founders put it, should not lack the welcome their Lord's own family once lacked there. Around it lay ordinary Judaea: villages, olive terraces, shepherds' hills, Jerusalem an easy ride away with its own far larger pilgrim machinery. Latin, Greek, Syriac, and Aramaic could all be heard in one market. It was a working town that world-famous neighbors had moved into.
