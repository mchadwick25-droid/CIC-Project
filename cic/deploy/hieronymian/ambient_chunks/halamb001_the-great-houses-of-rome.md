## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. The households this world moved through were among the richest in the empire: town houses on Rome's hills with reception halls, libraries, and staffs of dozens; estates in several provinces feeding rents to the city; marriages arranged as alliances between fortunes. A widow of such a house controlled real power - property, patronage, dependents - and her remarriage or refusal to remarry was family politics of the first order. Daily life ran on ceremony: the morning greeting of dependents, the litter through the streets, dinners where placement announced rank. Children were raised by nurses and tutors; the silk, the jewels, and the inherited plate were not decoration but the family's standing made visible. Every renunciation this world became famous for was measured against exactly this scale of what was being renounced.
