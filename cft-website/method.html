How the tree is made

Checked by AI, not yet by scholars

Every claim and every link on the tree has been checked by AI models against the sources, working to our written method. None of it has yet been reviewed by an independent scholar.

When that review happens, the release will say which parts were reviewed and by whom. Academic review is the project's biggest need. The data and citation page says how to help.

How a link is made

A link starts in a movement's own text. Each movement's page tells its story and names the movements it touched.

Every relationship that text names becomes a link. If the sources cannot support it, the text drops it or says plainly that it is uncertain.

An AI model drafts each link. It gives the link a kind, a grade, a short note and its sources.

Most sources are old texts kept as files in the project's library. A quoted source names its file and the exact lines.

A script then checks every quotation, word for word, against that file. A quotation that does not match fails, and so does its link.

Next, a second AI model reviews the work on its own. It is given the data and the sources, not the first model's reasoning. It checks each kind, grade, note and quotation.

The review runs both ways. Every relationship in the text must have its link, and every link must match the text.

Work that fails goes back for changes, for at most three rounds of review. Anything still open after the third round goes to the project lead to decide.

Seven kinds of link

Each link reads as a sentence: one movement, the kind of link, then the other movement. There are seven kinds.

Gave rise to
The second movement grew out of the first. Its people, communities or institutions came from the first one.
Continued as
The same community, carried on into a later movement on the tree. This is often the same church in a later age, or under a new name.
Influenced
Ideas, texts or practices passed from the first movement to the second. This can happen with or without contact between them.
Argued against
The first movement wrote or acted against the second, by name. The arrow points away from the critic. When both sides wrote against each other, the tree draws two links.
Broke with
A break that both sides recorded. It runs both ways, so it has no arrow.
In contact with
A recorded meeting, letter or shared event. It claims no influence. Living at the same time is not enough to count as contact.
Member of
A church that joined a council or fellowship of churches, on that body's own record. The arrow points from the member to the council. Membership claims no descent and no influence.

Five grades of confidence

Each link has a grade. The grade says how sure the record is of the link itself, not of the events its note mentions. A council that names a group can document an argument, for example. It does not, by itself, show that one movement grew out of another.

Documented
A primary source, written at the time or by the people involved, shows the link itself. Its words are quoted and checked against the file. If that text is not in the library, the link cannot be Documented.
Widely Accepted
A primary source shows the facts underneath the link, and modern scholars broadly agree on what they mean. The link names at least one of those modern works.
Dominant Modern Reconstruction
The leading account modern scholars have pieced together from the evidence. The link names the works it comes from.
Contested
Scholars disagree. The link gives sources for each side, and its note says what the disagreement is.
Inferential-Thin
The evidence is thin, and the note says so. The link names what little it rests on.

Reading confidence at a glance

On the map, a line's color shows the kind of link, and its pattern shows the grade.

Solid line
Documented.
Long dashes
Widely Accepted, or Dominant Modern Reconstruction. These two grades share the same long dashes, so the name on the card tells them apart.
Short dashes
Contested.
Dots
Inferential-Thin.
Green
Gave rise to, or continued as.
Amber
Influenced.
Red
Argued against, or broke with.
Gray
In contact with.

Point at a line, or tap it, to read the link with its grade spelled out.

A dotted gold thread joins a movement to the one it continues as in another river. Point at it, too, to see that link's grade.

Every movement's card on the map, and every movement's page, lists its links. Each link there shows its grade by name.

Major links

The map opens with no lines. Under Connections, the Major links switch draws only the major ones.

Three kinds of link are always major: gave rise to, continued as and broke with. They trace lineage and recorded breaks.

Any other link is major only when its own sources show an act of authority. That can be a council, canon, synod or law that condemned or shut out a group or its teaching. It can also be any authority, church or state, removing a bishop or other church head from office.

It can also be a split into a separate church, or a rule or settlement that an authority changed. Such a link carries one sentence on that lasting result, drawn from its sources.

A teaching or book that passed from one side to the other does not, by itself, make a link major.

Minor links are not hidden for good. Every link stays on its movement's card and page. Pick a movement on the map to see all of its links.

What the tree does not claim

A link claims only what its kind says. "In contact with" claims no influence. "Influenced" claims no shared community.

Two movements alive at the same time are not linked for that reason alone.

A river's width shows how many recorded movements in that family were active at the time. It does not measure membership or size.

Each movement's page also tells its story. Some sentences there give well-known background facts that are not yet tied to a source in the library. The links carry the quoted evidence.

No grade on the tree has yet been confirmed by an independent scholar.

Where the history ends

The tree's history runs to 2010. Movements since then will be added as they become history.

Nothing after 2010 is mapped yet.

What the living mark means

A living mark means one thing: the tradition is alive today, in 2026. It is the only statement the tree makes about the present. Everything else on an entry is history.

The mark belongs to the tradition, not to the entry's dates. An entry that ends in 1300 still carries it when the church, religious order or community it describes survives today. Its later history belongs to a later entry. Where the tree has no later entry yet, the gap is named, and the dates are never stretched to cover it.

An episode counts. A revival, a reform or a single period in a church's history is marked living when the wider tradition it belongs to is still alive.

Surviving ideas are not enough. A school, a party or a sect whose own community ended is not marked living, even when its writings are still widely read. A modern group that adopts an ancient name is not counted as its heir unless a record shows an unbroken community.

Decline is not an ending. A tradition is marked ended only when the record shows a clear end: it dissolved, it merged into another body, or its last member died.

Each mark rests on dated evidence, beginning with the body's own recent words: a dated statement, report or rule. A mark is "documented" when an independent source confirms it and we have checked that source. It is "reported" when it rests on the body's own word, or on an outside source we have not yet checked. Where we hold no dated source yet, the record says so, and the missing source is listed as wanted.

We describe what a body's dated documents say, never what its members believe. A living church can change its teaching, so each mark is a dated reading of the record, not a permanent verdict.

Sometimes two churches claim the same ancestors, or the record breaks off for centuries. Then the claim of descent gets its own confidence grade, on the same five levels the tree uses for links.

What is still to come

Links are built one era at a time. Each era ships as its own numbered release of the data.

The tenth era, The Global Church Era (1906-c. 2010), is not yet linked, because its traditions are still being built. Its movements stay on the map, but the full set of their links is not yet drawn.

Today the tree holds 736 links among 396 movements.
