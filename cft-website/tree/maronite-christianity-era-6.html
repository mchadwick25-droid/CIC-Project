Maronite Christianity — the Union Formalized

Mount Lebanon and Cyprus, 1300 to 1517 — a mountain church that turned a long-claimed friendship with Rome into a formal, permanent union.

About This Movement

Audio Brief · Narrated Edition 1:22

Read along

The Maronites were a Syriac-speaking church of the Lebanese mountains. Their patriarch was based in the Qadisha valley, and they had communities on Cyprus as well. The fourteenth century opened badly. Around 1305 Mamluk campaigns against the Kesrwan drove Christians from their homes. For most of this period the church survived largely by being hard to reach. Its monasteries and rock-cut hermit cells were tucked into a gorge that armies found costly to enter. Its tie to Rome, claimed since the crusades, was confirmed twice in this era. On 7 August 1445 a bull of Pope Eugenius IV received the Maronites of Cyprus into union with the Roman church. It was issued at the Council of Florence, which had by then moved to Rome. In 1516 envoys of the Maronite patriarch pledged his obedience at the Fifth Lateran Council. Unlike almost every other Eastern church, the Maronites hold that they were never out of communion with Rome at all. The Roman documents of this era tell it another way. The bull of 1445 says the Maronites of Cyprus had held that Christ had only one will.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

The Maronites were a Syriac-speaking church of the Lebanese mountains, with a patriarch in the Qadisha valley. They survived Mamluk campaigns against the Kesrwan around 1305, and the rest of this era, largely by being hard to reach. Their monasteries and rock-cut hermit cells were tucked into places that armies found too costly to enter. Their long-claimed friendship with Rome was made formal in this era. In 1445 a papal bull received the Maronites of Cyprus into union with the Roman church. It was issued at the Council of Florence, which had by then moved to Rome. In 1516 the patriarch's envoys pledged his obedience at the Fifth Lateran Council. Unlike almost every other Eastern church, the Maronites hold that they were never out of communion with Rome at all.

Legacy

It is a living church. The Maronite Church is still in full communion with Rome. It is the largest Christian body in Lebanon, and it has members all over the world. Its patriarch still carries the title of Antioch. The Syriac liturgy of these centuries is still in use, in a later, fuller form. The Qadisha valley stayed the patriarch's refuge for centuries afterward, and its monasteries and hermitages are still there.

Experience It Today

  • Ouadi Qadisha (the Holy Valley) and the Forest of the Cedars of God, Lebanon — the gorge that sheltered the Maronite patriarchate and its monasteries, a UNESCO World Heritage Site
