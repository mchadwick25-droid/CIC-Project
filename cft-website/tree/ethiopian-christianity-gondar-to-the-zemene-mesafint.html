Ethiopian Christianity — Gondar to the Zemene Mesafint

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

By 1650 the Jesuits were gone. Hiob Ludolf, writing in 1682, says that Fasilides drove them out of all his lands, together with their patriarch. Ludolf adds that Europeans knew little more of the emperor after that, because he would have no more dealings with them.

The head of the church was the abun, and he came from Egypt. James Bruce, who was in Ethiopia in the early 1770s, calls him a Coptic monk raised in the desert of St Macarius. Bruce says people looked on the abun as the patriarch of their church, and that they knew little of the Coptic patriarch of Alexandria. He adds that the abun seldom understood the language of the country.

The monks held great weight. Bruce says the Itchege, the chief of the monks of Debra Libanos, mattered more than the abun in troubled times. He names the monks of Gojam, who followed St Eustathius, as the low church of Ethiopia, and says they leaned toward turmoil in matters of religion. Bruce was an outside witness. He wrote decades after some of the events he tells.

Councils of clergy met when a new king took the throne or a new abun came from Egypt. Bruce tells of one in the reign of David. The head of the monks of Debra Libanos brought a complaint against three Catholic priests and their Ethiopian interpreter, also named Abba Gregorius. The council asked whether they accepted the Council of Chalcedon and the authority of Pope Leo. They said that they did, and the crowd cried out, "Stone them." The missionaries were dragged to a place behind the church of Abbo and stoned to death.

The Catholic Encyclopedia of 1907 also tells of three missionaries stoned in a public square at Gondar. It dates this to 1752. No other source read here gives that date.

The article also says that in 1846 Rome made Abyssinia a vicariate. Rome gave it to the Lazarists. It says one of them, Justin de Jacobis, stirred the jealousy of the clergy. These accounts are Catholic. They speak for the missionaries.

The Church Missionary Society had long turned its eyes toward Ethiopia. It sent Samuel Gobat and Christian Kugler into Egypt in 1826 to seek a way in. On 26 February 1830 Gobat left Adigrate, in Tigray, and set out for Gondar.

Gobat's journal also shows how the church settled a quarrel in Tigray. A letter of Kugler's tells that Girgis and the chief priest of Tigray, Guebra Mariam, came from Cairo. They brought the decision of the Coptic patriarch on the priests' quarrels. One priest, Guebra Messih, held that Christ had three births. On 28 September the dispute was settled in the presence of the Tigray ruler Sebagadis.

The next entry says that for a century before 1855 the emperors were only figureheads, while regional lords ruled. No source read for this entry tells of those years.
