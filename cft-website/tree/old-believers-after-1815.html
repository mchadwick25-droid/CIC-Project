The Old Believers After 1815

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

This entry carries the Old Believers on from 1815. By then the movement was split in two. The priestly branch took priests from the national church. The priestless branch had no priests at all, or priests of its own making.

Nicholas I pressed them hard. In 1827 a governor threatened to close the monastery of Nizhni-Voskresenski. The monks must join the state church, on terms Paul I had offered. That year the tsar also cut off the runaway priests who served the Rogozhskoe cemetery in Moscow and other places. The Popovtsy had long relied on those priests for the sacraments.

In 1832 the older laws that eased their lot were repealed. After that the Popovtsy could keep only priests who had joined them before 1826.

In 1854 the metropolitan Philaret made one chapel at the Rogozhskoe cemetery a church of the state church. In 1856 the old rites came back, with priests of Austrian ordination. The Popovtsy church was shut. Conybeare says its altars stayed sealed until 3 May 1883.

The priestly Old Believers wanted a bishop of their own. In 1844 the emperor Ferdinand let them bring one into his lands. He would sit at Belaya Krinitsa. Their agents searched the East. They found Ambrose, a Greek who had been metropolitan of Sarajevo. The Turks had expelled him from his see. He reached Belaya Krinitsa, in Austrian Bukovina, on 12 October 1846.

Ivanovski, a writer for the Holy Synod, says a monk who was there called the rite uncanonical. Conybeare passes this on with care. The Old Believers took Ambrose as their bishop all the same.

The new hierarchy grew fast. In 1849 a new bishop, Cyril, made Sophronius bishop of Simbirsk. Sophronius led the Russian Popovtsy. Within twelve years the church had ten sees across Russia. In 1861 it broke free of Belaya Krinitsa. From 1863 the state began to tolerate its priests.

Orthodox writers said the new bishops were not true bishops. So the priestly Old Believers asked the Patriarch of Constantinople to accept them, in 1875, 1892 and 1896. A commission there ruled for the bishop of Sarajevo in 1899. Conybeare calls this a quiet yes.

The priestless branch split over marriage. Conybeare says one group allowed it and the other did not, and each counted millions of souls.

On 17 April 1905 an imperial decree set the Old Ritualists apart from other dissenters. It let them form a church, with the rights of the Lutherans and Catholics. Their clergy no longer had to serve in the army.

On 25 August 1905 a synod of the priestly branch met. It ordered that the day of the decree be kept as a holy day for ever.

  • The Old Believers Documented Major — In 1814 an English account says the Old Believers were divided into priestly and priestless groups. The priestly group took priests from the national church. By 1827 Nicholas I cut off the runaway priests the Popovtsy relied on. In 1846 the same priestly Old Believers found a bishop at Belaya Krinitsa.

Opposed By

  • The Russian Church — the Synodal Century Documented — Notes to an English history of the Russian Church, printed in 1842, call the Old Believers 'the Schism'. They count 19,833 Russian dissenters who joined the state church in 1839. Conybeare says Nicholas I tried to force the Old Believers into the state church from 1827. He says Russian Orthodox writers kept denying that the Belaya Krinitsa orders were valid.
