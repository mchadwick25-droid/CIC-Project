The Missionary Era · 1844-1906 · East Africa, Buganda

East Africa · Buganda, today in Uganda

East African Christianity & the Uganda Martyrs

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Christianity came back to East Africa along the coast first — Johann Ludwig Krapf landed at Mombasa in 1844 and began the language and mapping work later missions relied on — and then inland to the kingdom of Buganda, where Anglican missionaries arrived in 1877 and French Catholic White Fathers in 1879. Their converts were largely young men of the royal court, and when the kabaka Mwanga II turned on them between 1885 and 1887 he killed both kinds together: Anglican and Catholic pages executed side by side, the largest group burned alive at Namugongo on 3 June 1886 under the leadership of Charles Lwanga, who is said to have arranged his own pyre. Persecution did what it often does. The church grew, and Baganda converts carried it outward as evangelists into the surrounding kingdoms faster than any mission society could have, so that by the agreement of 1900 that settled Buganda's relations with Britain, it was a Christian kingdom.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • The Victorian Church of England Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — In 1877 the Church Missionary Society, an Anglican body from England, sent men to the kingdom of Buganda. Their first converts, mostly young men at the king's court, became the first Anglican church there. Modern histories tell this, Adrian Hastings's among them.
  • The Catholic Church of the Long Nineteenth Century Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — In 1879 the White Fathers, a French Catholic mission, reached the kingdom of Buganda. Their converts formed the Catholic church there, and some died with Anglican pages in the killings of 1885 to 1887. Modern histories tell this, Adrian Hastings's among them.

Gave Rise To

  • The East African Revival (Balokole) Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — The revival broke out on a large scale at Gahini in Rwanda at the end of 1933. Gahini's mission hospital was an outpost of the Anglican church of Uganda. The historian Kevin Ward finds the revival's roots in that church, and most of all in Buganda.

Continued As

  • The Catholic Church in Africa in the Twentieth Century Widely Accepted Major — In 1964 Pope Paul VI greeted the bishops, priests and faithful of Uganda in Rome, after he made the Uganda Martyrs saints. In 1969 he came to Namugongo to bless the altar of a shrine raised in their memory.
