Japanese Christianity after 1906

Japan's Christians after 1906, and a Protestant union formed under wartime pressure that in 1967 confessed its part in the war.

About This Movement

Audio Brief · Narrated Edition 1:11

Read along

In wartime, the Kyodan later wrote, the government demanded that religious bodies unite and cooperate with the war effort. Most Japanese Protestants had long wanted a single evangelical church. Their leaders used the demand to enter a church union, and the Kyodan was formed. It marked its twenty-fifth anniversary in October 1966. On 26 October 1954 its Eighth General Assembly enacted a Confession of Faith. It called the Bible "the unerring standard of faith and life", and it confessed the Apostles' Creed. On Easter Sunday, 26 March 1967, its moderator, Suzuki Masahisa, issued a confession on the church's wartime responsibility. The church, it said, "should not have aligned itself with that war effort". It asked forgiveness from the peoples of Asia and from their churches. Beside the Kyodan grew Japan's own Christian movements, which Mark Mullins studied in Christianity Made in Japan (1998).

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

In wartime the Japanese government demanded that religious bodies unite and support the war. Japan's Protestant leaders used the demand to form one church, the Kyodan. In 1967 the Kyodan confessed that it had approved and supported that war.

Legacy

In 1967 the Kyodan feared that Japan was "once again headed in a dangerous direction". It prayed that it would not repeat its errors.
