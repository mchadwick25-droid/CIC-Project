The Hutterite Brethren

Moravia, Hungary and Transylvania, 1528 to 1785 — Anabaptists who laid their goods on one cloak. They held all things in common, and their chronicle tells what it cost them.

About This Tradition

Audio Brief 1:42

Read along

In 1528 Jakob Widman led his followers out of Nikolsburg in Moravia. They were some two hundred people, not counting the children. They had parted from the Anabaptists there who kept the sword. In a deserted village on the road, their leaders spread a cloak on the ground. Everyone laid down their goods on it, freely and unforced. They pointed to the prophets and the apostles.

Jakob Hutter led the community for more than two years. In 1535 he was betrayed and seized at Klausen, and taken to King Ferdinand's government at Innsbruck. He was tortured and then burned alive early in 1536. The Brethren took his name, and their chronicle says they were not ashamed of it.

The communities spread across Moravia. By 1622, the Brethren said, they had lived there more than eighty years and served many lords well. That October the emperor Ferdinand drove them out of all Moravia. They left twenty-four households behind, with their grain, wine and herds. They went into Hungary with almost nothing, as winter set in.

In Hungary and Transylvania they lived on for some decades more, growing fewer. From 1757 the government broke up the last communities, and most members became Catholics. A few families would not give up their fathers' faith. They moved to Wischenka in Russia, and in 1784 others fled to join them.

  • Peter Riedemann — a minister of the community, jailed at Nuremberg from 1533, author of its account of its faith, the Rechenschaft

Connections

In Its Own Record

The Hutterites grew out of the Anabaptist church at Nikolsburg in 1528. Habsburg rulers hunted them. They burned Jakob Hutter in 1536. In 1622 they drove the Brethren out of Moravia. From 1757 the government in Hungary broke up the last of their homes. A few families kept the faith and moved on to Russia.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

In 1528 a band of Anabaptists left Nikolsburg in Moravia. On the road their leaders spread a cloak on the ground. Everyone laid down their goods on it, freely and unforced. From that act came a church that held all its goods in common. It took its name from Jakob Hutter, burned in 1536. Its own chronicle tells the story from the inside.

Legacy

The Brethren never let their chronicle go. Beck found their families in Russia, near the Sea of Azov, by 1852. In 1874 the Brethren moved from Russia to America and took the book with them. In 1923 the Hutterian Brethren in Canada published it, as a house book for every member.

How Sure the Record Is

Rich in inside voice. The Brethren's own chronicle runs to 1665. It is printed in Wolkan's edition of 1923. Beck's edition of 1883 adds their later records and the state's orders against them, up to 1785. Both are in German, and no public-domain English version exists.
