The Global Church Era · 1960-1994 · South Africa

The Church Struggle against Apartheid

A movement across churches

South Africa, 1960 to 1994 — churches in the struggle against apartheid, from the Cottesloe Consultation to the Kairos Document and the Belhar Confession.

About This Tradition

Audio Brief 0:59

Read along

This story opens with the Cottesloe Consultation, which met in Johannesburg from 7 to 14 December 1960. In September 1985 a group who called themselves the Kairos Theologians issued the Kairos Document. It was made public on 25 September. Its full title called it a theological comment on the political crisis in South Africa. The number of people who signed it differs from one edition to another. In October 1982 a synod of the Dutch Reformed Mission Church, meeting at Belhar, accepted a draft confession. The church adopted it as a confession in 1986. Its first article confesses the triune God, who through his Word and Spirit gathers, protects and cares for his church. The movement ended with apartheid in 1994.

Voices in the Record

  • The Kairos Theologians — who issued the Kairos Document, made public on 25 September 1985
  • The Dutch Reformed Mission Church — whose synod accepted the Belhar Confession in draft in 1982 and adopted it in 1986. It opens: « Ons glo in die drie-enige God, Vader, Seun en Heilige Gees » ('We believe in the triune God, Father, Son and Holy Spirit', a close English rendering of the Afrikaans)
  • The South African Council of Churches and the Institute for Contextual Theology — two of the bodies that carried the struggle

Connections

In Its Own Record

It stands beside the Confessing Church in Germany as a comparison, not a line of descent. Its carriers included the South African Council of Churches, the Institute for Contextual Theology and the Dutch Reformed Mission Church.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

In South Africa the churches' struggle against apartheid has a name of its own. John de Gruchy's standard history of 1979 calls it the church struggle. Its dated texts include the Kairos Document of 1985 and the Belhar Confession of 1986.

Legacy

John de Gruchy's history came out again in 2004, in a 25th-anniversary edition written with Steve de Gruchy. Its title gives the movement its name.
