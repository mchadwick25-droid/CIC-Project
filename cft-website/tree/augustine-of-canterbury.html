Augustine of Canterbury

A person. The tradition that tells their story: The Roman Church in the Age of Gregory the Great.

Known for
Monk of Gregory the Great's monastery sent by Gregory to the English, who baptized King Ethelbert's people of Kent and became the first archbishop of Canterbury.
Also called
Augustinus
Dates
Sent by Gregory in 596 and landed in Britain in 597, by Bede's own years. His epitaph gives his death on 26 May, with no year; Bede tells it after the ordinations he dates to 604
Place
Rome; Kent, with his see at Canterbury
Role
Monk and provost; missionary; first archbishop of Canterbury

About This Person

King Ethelbert of Kent would not meet the strangers indoors. Bede of Jarrow says he feared they might work magic on him, so he sat in the open air on Thanet. Augustine of Canterbury and his companions came singing a litany. They bore a silver cross for a banner and a picture of Christ painted on a board. The king called their words fair but new, and would not leave the faith his people had long kept. He let them stay and preach.

Augustine had been provost of Pope Gregory the Great's own monastery, Gregory's letters say. Bede says Gregory sent him to the English in 596, and the mission landed the next year. On the way, Bede tells, the monks lost heart at the thought of a fierce people whose language they did not know. They sent Augustine back to ask to be excused. Gregory sent him on with a letter: "When Augustine, your chief, returns, whom we also constitute your abbat, humbly obey him in all things."

At Canterbury the king believed and was baptized. Bede says Augustine then went to Arles to be made archbishop of the English. In Bede's telling, Aetherius of Arles ordained him; the editor Nicola Turchi names Vergilius. Gregory wrote to Eulogius of Alexandria that more than ten thousand English were reported baptized one Christmas.

Augustine sent questions to Rome, and Bede copied them with Gregory's answers. One asks: "Whereas the faith is one and the same, why are there different customs in different churches?" Gregory told him to choose what was good from every church, "for things are not to be loved for the sake of places, but places for the sake of good things." Ludwig Hartmann, editing Gregory's letters, says these answers have often been doubted, and judges them genuine.

Gregory also put all the bishops of Britain in his care. Bede tells of two meetings with British bishops, the first at Augustine's Oak. Before the second, a hermit told them to follow him if he rose to greet them. Augustine stayed seated, and they refused him. If they would not preach to the English, Augustine is said to have warned, they would suffer death at English hands. Bede, no friend of the Britons, calls them "that perfidious nation".

Augustine died on the twenty-sixth of May. His epitaph gives the day but not the year, and calls him the first archbishop of Canterbury.

Key Works

  • Questions to Gregory the Great, with Gregory's answers: nine questions on a bishop's life and income, the differing customs of the churches, theft from the church, marriage, ordaining bishops, the bishops of Gaul and Britain, and purity at baptism and communion; kept as Bede copied them, and in the Latin of Gregory's Register

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Bede says King Ethelbert believed and was baptized, and gave the monks a home at Canterbury; Gregory wrote that over ten thousand English were reported baptized one Christmas.
  • Gregory sent him the pallium and a plan for twelve bishops, and put all the bishops of Britain in his care. In 604, Bede says, he ordained Mellitus and Justus as bishops.

How they're remembered

  • His epitaph, which Bede copies, calls him the first archbishop of Canterbury, sent by Gregory and supported with miracles, who brought King Ethelbert and his nation from idols to the faith of Christ.
  • Bede calls him the beloved of God, Father Augustine. Gregory's letter to him on his miracles tells him to rejoice with fear.

What Is Uncertain

  • Bede, the main witness, wrote his History long after the mission and is hostile to the Britons, whom he calls "that perfidious nation". He tells the second meeting with the British bishops, after the first at Augustine's Oak, with the hedge "is said" on Augustine's warning, and presents the later slaughter of the Bangor monks as its fulfillment.
  • Who ordained him at Arles. Bede names Aetherius, archbishop of that city; L. Cecil Jane's note to Bede says Aetherius was really archbishop of Lyons. Nicola Turchi's note to Gregory's letters names Vergilius of Arles. Gregory says only that he was made bishop by the bishops of the Germanies. Gregory's letter to Eulogius has him made bishop before he was brought to the English; Bede has him go to Arles after the king's baptism.
  • The questions and answers. Ludwig Hartmann edited this part of Gregory's Register in the Monumenta Germaniae Historica after Paul Ewald's death. He says they have often been doubted, and judges them genuine. He reports Theodor Mommsen's view that only extracts survive. James Barmby, translating the Register, holds Bede's copy the most authentic. Boniface reported that no copy could be found at Rome. Hartmann dates his letter to 735 in his preface and to before 741 in his note.
  • The year of his death. Bede tells it after the ordinations he dates to 604 and gives no year of his own; the epitaph gives 26 May, in King Ethelbert's reign. Ludwig Hartmann has Laurentius succeed him in 606.
  • The date of Ethelbert's baptism. Bede gives none. Turchi dates it to Christmas 597, from Gregory's letter to Eulogius, which reports the ten thousand baptized at Christmas without naming the king.
  • His monastery. Gregory calls him the former provost of his monastery and does not name it. L. Cecil Jane's note to Bede names the monastery of Saint Anthony at Rome and calls him a Benedictine; no letter of Gregory used here says either.
