The Reformation Era · 1517-1650 · Rome, Latin Christendom

Rome, today in Italy · Latin Christendom

The Tridentine Church

About This Movement

The Catholic Church's answer to the Reformation took decades to assemble and then held for four centuries. The Council of Trent met in three widely separated periods between 1545 and 1563, restating Catholic teaching on Scripture and tradition, justification and the sacraments, and — as important in practice — ordering bishops to reside in their dioceses and to found seminaries. What followed was wholesale standardization: a Roman Catechism, a revised Missal and Breviary issued for the whole Latin church, and in 1622 a Roman office for the missions, Propaganda Fide. The same period produced the Roman Inquisition and the Index of Forbidden Books, and a wave of new active orders — Capuchins, Ursulines, Oratorians — doing work the older monastic rules had never been designed for.

In Its Own Record

Continues

  • The Latin Papal Church (Avignon to Fifth Lateran) Documented Major — In 1546 the Council of Trent enforced a penalty on unapproved religious books 'imposed in a canon of the last Council of Lateran'. In 1551 it renewed and enlarged a law that Pope Clement V had issued at the Council of Vienne. The same church carried its own laws on into the age of the Reformation.

Continued As

  • The Catholic Church of the Ancien Régime Documented Major — In 1699 the bishops of the Paris province met to accept a ruling by Pope Innocent XII. Their archbishop said the church of France had received 'the faith of the Council of Trent' without exception. Trent's rules for church order, he added, were not yet received everywhere there.

Influenced By

  • Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity Widely Accepted — In 1546 the Council of Trent ruled that the old Latin Vulgate was authentic. It was to be the text for public reading and preaching. Its English translator of 1848 says the council chose the Vulgate as Jerome's version. Augustine had said Jerome's work was a translation from the Hebrew.
  • The Society of Jesus Widely Accepted Major — Pope Paul III approved the Society of Jesus by bull in 1540. In 1546 Ignatius sent instructions to his men at the Council of Trent, where Laínez and Salmerón served as the pope's theologians. Laínez argued there against a two-part view of justification, and the council's decree ruled it out.Lasting result: In 1540 Pope Paul III gave the Society of Jesus his approval in a bull. He used his power as pope to confirm it.
  • Tridentine Parish Renewal - Borromean Milan Widely Accepted — Francisco Peña wrote in 1610, from the hearings held to make Borromeo a saint. He says bishops in many lands followed his example and his councils' decrees. Many came from beyond the Alps to learn from him. His biographer says he hoped other bishops would copy his seminaries.

Influenced

  • Tridentine Parish Renewal - Borromean Milan Documented Major — Charles Borromeo wrote that he called Milan's first provincial council because the Council of Trent had ordered it. The council met in 1565. Its decree on seminaries quotes Trent's order. Then it taxes church incomes across the province to pay for one.Lasting result: Milan's first provincial council did what Trent told it to do. It taxed church incomes in the province to found and pay for a seminary.
  • The Carmelite Reform and the Spanish Mystics Documented — Teresa of Ávila wrote that before each new house she first sought the local bishop's consent, 'according to the decree of the council'. She also tells how Trent's decree on hermits made the hermit Mariano want to appeal to Rome. After she showed him her rule, he joined her reform.
  • The Post-Vatican-II Parish Tradition Inferential-Thin — In 1570 Pope Pius V issued the Roman Missal, reformed under a decree of the Council of Trent. It stayed the standard Mass book until the Second Vatican Council's liturgy decree of 1963 ordered the rite revised. This rests on the 1570 bull alone, so the tie is thin.
  • The Ruthenian Uniate Church — Zamość to the Partitions Documented — The Ruthenian synod at Zamość in 1720 wrote that it followed in the footsteps of the fathers of the Council of Trent. Those present professed that they received the Council of Trent. The synod barred parish priests from keeping feasts outside its list, and that list included Corpus Christi, a feast of the Latin church.

Argued Against

  • The Humiliati Documented Major — In 1571 Pope Pius V shut down the order of Humiliati brothers by bull. The bull names Cardinal Charles Borromeo as the order's protector, sent to reform it. The bull did not cover the women's houses.Lasting result: Pope Pius V put an end to the order of Humiliati brothers. He also did away with its general, its heads in each province and all its statutes.
  • Lutheran Wittenberg & Its Congregations Documented Major — In June 1520 a papal bull, Exsurge Domine, condemned forty-one of Martin Luther's claims as errors. Later the Council of Trent ruled on how sinners are made right with God. Its English translator says one key chapter aims mainly at Luther's errors.Lasting result: The bull also condemned Luther's books and ordered them burned. If he and his followers did not take back their views within sixty days, they would be punished.
  • The Anabaptist Movements Documented Major — In March 1547 the Council of Trent condemned anyone who said that those baptized as babies must be baptized again as adults. The canon does not name the Anabaptists. A Mennonite martyr book quotes a writer who says they were hunted 'first especially in popedom.' It says about six hundred died at Ensisheim alone.Lasting result: In March 1547 the Council of Trent condemned all who taught that a child baptized at birth must be baptized again.
  • Orthodoxy under the Ottomans - Parish, Millet & Neomartyrs Documented — A papal brief for the envoy Canachio Rossi set out the case against Patriarch Cyril Lucaris. It said he denied prayer to the saints, honor to images and relics, Christ's real presence in the Eucharist, and free will. Thomas Smith printed it in 1707. He named Greeks raised on Rome's money at the Greek College among Lucaris's worst enemies.
  • St. Thomas Christians under Portuguese Pressure - Diamper & the Coonan Cross Documented Major — In 1599 the Archbishop of Goa, Aleixo de Menezes, held a synod at Diamper and bound it to the Council of Trent. Its decrees called the community's Syriac books the work of Nestorian heretics. They forbade anyone to read them and ordered them destroyed.Lasting result: The synod said many Syriac books came from Nestorian heretics. It forbade all Christians to read them and ordered them destroyed.

Opposed By

  • Lutheran Wittenberg & Its Congregations Documented — In 1537 Luther drew up the Smalcald Articles for the council that Pope Paul III had called. They set out how far his side could yield to 'the Papists'. In them Luther called the pope 'the very Antichrist', who had set himself above Christ.
  • The Scottish Reformation & Kirk Documented Major — In August 1560 the Scottish Parliament ruled that the Pope should have no power in the realm from then on. Anyone who sought a title or right from him could be banished and lose any office.Lasting result: The Act of 1560 ended the Pope's power in Scotland by law, on pain of banishment.

Broke With

  • The English Reformation & Elizabethan Settlement Documented Major — Henry VIII's orders of 1536 told the clergy to keep the laws that ended the Pope's 'usurped power' in England. Under Mary I, in 1555, London parishes prayed for the Pope again. In 1562 John Jewel wrote that the English had left Rome and gave thanks for it. In 1570 Pope Pius V cut Elizabeth off from the church.
  • The Scandinavian Reformation Kingdoms Widely Accepted Major — In Sweden the quarrel grew when Rome would not confirm bishops the cathedral chapters chose, the historian Anjou writes. Denmark broke harder. In 1536 its bishops were jailed, and its church law of 1537 charged the 'papist bishops' with tyranny.

In Contact With

  • The Church of the East — the Schism of 1552 Documented Major — Andreas Masius wrote that Sulaqa's supporters sent him to Rome with letters. A Vatican scholar's list of 1725 says he made a profession of faith there in February 1553. Pope Julius III then proclaimed him patriarch of the Chaldeans.Lasting result: Pope Julius III made Sulaqa patriarch in 1553 while his rival held the old see, so the church had two rival patriarchs.
  • The Union of Brest & Ruthenian Greek Catholic Christianity Documented Major — In December 1595 Pope Clement VIII met two Ruthenian bishops, Hypatius and Cyril, in Rome. His bull Magnus Dominus then took their metropolitan, bishops, clergy and people into the Roman church. It let them keep their own rites, as long as these did not clash with Catholic teaching.Lasting result: The pope's bull took the Ruthenian bishops, clergy and people into the Roman church and let them keep their own rites.
