Thomas of Marga

A person. The tradition that tells their story: The Church of the East on the Silk Road.

Also called Mar Thomas (Bishop of Marga)

Monk of Beth 'Abhe and author of the Book of Governors, the histories of the holy men and monks of his monastery in the Church of the East. He was secretary to the catholicos Abraham, then bishop of Marga and metropolitan of Beth Garmai.

About This Person

In the Arab year 217, a young man came to the monastery of Beth 'Abhe. He began asking the old monks about the holy men who had lived there. He tells it himself. By reason of the fervor that burned in him, he writes, he made inquiries about each of them from the old men he found there. Of one holy man he says he "did not know that I should become the historian" of his deeds, and so had not asked more.

He was Thomas of Marga, a monk of the Church of the East. E. A. Wallis Budge, who translated his Book of Governors in 1893, reckons his arrival as 832. In 837, Budge says, he was secretary to the catholicos Abraham, the head of his church. Thomas recalls copying letters "before the Patriarchal throne" in the days of his youth. Budge says Abraham made him bishop of Marga, and that he later became metropolitan of Beth Garmai.

A monk named 'Abhd-Isho' had urged him for many years to write. Thomas says he would not, for two reasons. Writing histories is severe labor, and it keeps a man from his daily work. In the end he wrote the histories of the holy men and monks of Beth 'Abhe. He names his sources. Among them are an old deacon of Shalmath, the letters of the patriarch Timothy I, and Abraham himself, who "related to me fully" one story. His stories, he writes, are "not vain imaginations of my own".

Budge's title dates the book to 840. Jérôme Labourt, the French historian, says he wrote between 832 and 850.

Mari ibn Sulayman, a later historian of that church, tells of Thomas in his life of the patriarch Theodosius. The caliph al-Mutawakkil held the patriarch in prison for three years and six months. All that time, Mari says, the patriarch's brother Thomas, metropolitan of Beth Garmai, stood by him and cared for him.

Thomas pictured his work as a harvest. He would go into the gardens of their deeds as into a field, he wrote, and gather "the choice and well-filled ears of corn" into one sheaf.

Key Works

  • The Book of Governors (Historia Monastica): six books of histories of the holy men and monks of Beth 'Abhe and of the monks who went out from it as bishops, written at the request of the monk 'Abhd-Isho'. Held in E. A. Wallis Budge's English of 1893, volume 2. The Syriac volume is not among the sources used here
  • Metrical homily on Maran-'ammeh: a homily in verse on Maran-'ammeh, metropolitan of Adiabene, printed in the Book of Governors after Book III. In Budge's English

Legacy and Today

What traces to them

  • Alphonse Mingana cites him for how Timothy I chose and ordained bishops and sent them to all the countries of the East.
  • Thomas records that he learned of David, metropolitan of Beth Sinaye, from Timothy's letters. Budge glosses Beth Sinaye as China.
  • Jérôme Labourt uses his silence about Saint Eugene, the monk Awgin, to doubt that tradition. W. A. Wigram notes the same silence, but holds that Awgin's existence need not be questioned for it.

How they're remembered

  • The copyist's closing note to the Book of Governors calls him "the divinely pious man and spiritual philosopher".
  • Joseph Assemani says the history adds much to a general knowledge of the monks and also of the clergy of his church.
  • Mari ibn Sulayman says that while the caliph al-Mutawakkil held the patriarch Theodosius in prison, his brother Thomas, metropolitan of Beth Garmai, stood by him and showed him every kindness. Assemani tells the same from the chronicler Amr ibn Matta.

Open Questions

  • When he wrote. Budge's title gives 840. Jérôme Labourt says between 832 and 850, and Joseph Assemani says he flourished at the start of the ninth century.
  • His age on arrival. Budge's English has "a young man", and Assemani's Latin of the same line has a boy.
  • The year 832. Budge thinks some words in that line are corrupt and should probably be omitted. He reads the Arab year 217 as 832.
  • Who made him metropolitan. Budge says Abraham made him bishop of Marga, and that he afterwards became metropolitan of Beth Garmai. Assemani thinks his brother Theodosius seems to have raised him.
  • Mari's line names the patriarch only as the patriarch. It falls in his life of Theodosius, and Budge and Assemani both name Theodosius as Thomas's brother.
  • Budge's introduction, in his first volume, is not among the sources used here. His note on Thomas's life is one footnote in the second volume.
