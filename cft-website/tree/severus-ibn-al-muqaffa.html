Severus ibn al-Muqaffa

About This Person

A dog walked past while Severus, bishop of al-Ushmunain in Upper Egypt, stood before the chief judge. Was the dog a Christian or a Muslim, someone asked. Ask the dog, Severus said, and it will answer for itself. Today is a fast day, he went on. Christians eat no meat, and in the evening they drink a little wine. Muslims do not fast today, and drink no wine. So set meat and wine before the dog. If it eats the meat, it is a Muslim. If it leaves the meat and drinks the wine, it is a Christian.

Eusèbe Renaudot, a Catholic scholar who wrote a Latin history of the Coptic patriarchs in 1713, tells this story. The bishop was Severus ibn al-Muqaffa, and Renaudot says he was famous for his mastery of Arabic. Under the caliph al-Mu'izz, a Jew named Moses, a friend of the caliph's adviser, asked to debate the patriarch Abraham the Syrian. The patriarch chose Severus, who said such a debate was beneath the caliph's dignity, and it went no further. Little else of his life is recorded.

His name is joined to the History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria, and a preface in that History speaks in his voice. Severus says he asked Christian brothers to help him translate the histories written in Coptic and Greek into Arabic. Most people in Egypt, he writes, "are ignorant of the Coptic and the Greek."

B. Evetts, the History's translator, says no one can know how far Severus's work of translation reaches. From the seventh century, Evetts says, its lives were written by men of their own time, such as John the deacon. Renaudot names later writers who carried it on. He also doubts that Severus knew any Greek. Evetts himself changed his date for Severus. He placed him in the ninth century in 1895, and near the end of the tenth in 1904.

The preface under his name ends with a prayer: "In thy mercy be not angry with me for that which I have written, but pardon and forgive the faults therein caused by my negligence."

Key Works

  • History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria, first part: the lives of the patriarchs from Saint Mark, gathered from Coptic and Greek documents and put into Arabic with helpers, his preface says. How far his part reaches is disputed
  • Refutation of Eutychius: against Eutychius, also called Said ibn Batriq, a Melkite patriarch of Alexandria and historian. B. Evetts and Eusèbe Renaudot name it. The text is not held
  • History of the first four councils: a short history of the councils, named in Renaudot's list and in Joseph Assemani's catalog. The text is not held

Legacy and Today

What traces to them

  • Mauhub, son of Mansur, a deacon of Alexandria, gathered more lives in 1088. He set those Severus had collected first, Renaudot says.
  • His tract on Easter and the Eucharist was valued so much that it is in most manuscripts on preparing for communion, Renaudot says.
  • Joseph Assemani's catalog of Vatican manuscripts lists his history of the first four councils and a tract against the Armenians, in Arabic.

How they're remembered

  • The Ethiopic Synaxarium, a book of saints' days, names him beside the patriarch Abraham at the debate before the caliph. In E. A. Wallis Budge's English, they win it.
  • Renaudot says Eastern writers praise him and list his works, but few recall his work on the History.
  • The History's life of the patriarch Philotheus, as Renaudot gives it, praises his learning, shown in many books.
  • Renaudot says Severus was a close friend of Wadih, son of Raja, a Muslim of Misr who became a Christian and wrote books in Arabic. Wadih wrote down a miracle story Severus told him.

Open Questions

  • Little of his life is recorded. No held source gives his birth, his consecration as bishop or his death.
  • How much of the History is his. A preface in his voice says he had Coptic and Greek histories put into Arabic. B. Evetts says no one can know how far that work reaches.
  • Evetts says the lives from the seventh century were written by men of their own time, such as John the deacon and the archdeacon George.
  • Renaudot names later writers who carried the History on, among them Michael, bishop of Tanis, and Mauhub, son of Mansur. He doubts that Severus knew Greek, against the preface's own claim.
  • Renaudot cites the History under Severus's name, as in his life of Simon I. In his life of Philotheus he keeps Severus apart from the History's author.
  • His century. Evetts said the ninth in 1895 and near the end of the tenth in 1904. Renaudot places him under al-Mu'izz and says he lived past a hundred.
  • The debate before al-Mu'izz. Renaudot says it went no further. The Ethiopic Synaxarium says they argued with the man and beat him.
  • The History's life of the patriarch Mark, son of Zaraa, as Renaudot gives it, says Severus wrote the life of the patriarch Abraham.
  • The story of the dog, and the debate, come through Renaudot, who tells them as others told them.
