Documented Stories

Seven People in a Kyiv Flat

Sunday Adelaja arrived in Belarus from Nigeria in 1986 on a Soviet scholarship to study journalism in Minsk, one of thousands of African students brought to the USSR in its last years. He was converted through a small Pentecostal fellowship there, stayed after the Union dissolved, and in 1993 moved to Kyiv, where he began a Bible study in a flat with seven people. In 1994 it became a church, the Word of Faith, later renamed the Embassy of the Blessed Kingdom of God for All Nations. What set it apart from other African-founded congregations in Europe was who came: not migrants, but Ukrainians and Russians, many of them people the new economy had discarded, addicts and the homeless among them, and within a decade it claimed some twenty thousand members in Kyiv and daughter churches across the former Soviet Union, with a Black African pastor preaching in Russian to a white Slavic congregation. It ran rehabilitation programs and soup kitchens on a scale the city noticed. In the Orange Revolution of November and December 2004 Adelaja's people were visibly on Independence Square, and Adelaja himself addressed the crowds; the church's presence in the protests made it, for a season, part of the national story. It was the largest church in Europe founded by an African, and it was the clearest single case of what the phrase reverse mission was coined to describe.

The Regulator and the Birthday

Kingsway International Christian Centre was founded in London in 1992 by Matthew Ashimolowo, a Nigerian-born pastor, and within a decade it filled a converted warehouse in Hackney with congregations of several thousand each Sunday, most of them of West African and Caribbean background, and was widely reported as the largest single congregation in Britain. It was registered as a charity, which in England places a body under the Charity Commission. In 2002 the Commission opened a statutory inquiry into KICC's finances. Its report, published in 2005, found that the trustees had failed in their duties: that the charity had paid some £120,000 towards the founder's birthday celebrations, that he had received unauthorized payments and benefits, including a car, and that the governance arrangements had allowed the founder effectively to direct a body whose trustees were supposed to be independent of him. The Commission removed the existing trustees, appointed new ones, and required repayment of the unauthorized benefits, which was made. It did not find that funds had been stolen, and it did not remove Ashimolowo from his ministry, which was not within its powers; the new trustees retained him as senior pastor, and the church continued to grow. The report is a public document, and the church's own account of the episode, which stresses the regulator's confirmation that no criminal conduct occurred, is also published.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

Within a single lifetime the mission map turned over: by 2010 churches that European missionaries had once planted in Africa were planting congregations back among Europeans. The Redeemed Christian Church of God, founded in Nigeria in 1952, opened Jesus House in London in 1994, the same year Sunday Adelaja founded the Embassy of the Blessed Kingdom of God for All Nations in Kyiv. Aladura and Kimbanguist congregations had already been meeting in European cities since the 1960s, the earliest layer of what came to be called reverse mission.

Legacy

In 2010 this was a current in progress rather than a finished chapter, and it was not one confession. The churches involved came from several different African traditions and answered to their own bodies. Claims that one or another was the largest congregation in Britain or in Europe circulated widely and rested mostly on the churches' own counts. What is clear is that by 2010 the direction of Christian mission had reversed within living memory. By then European Christianity's growing edge was often African. It is also the map's newest instance of its oldest pattern: the faith traveling with people who have been scattered.
