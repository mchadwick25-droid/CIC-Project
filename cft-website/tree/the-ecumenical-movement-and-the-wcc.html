The Ecumenical Movement & the World Council of Churches

About This Movement

The World Missionary Conference at Edinburgh in 1910 left three currents running, and over the next decades they came together. The International Missionary Council was founded in October 1921. Life and Work, which dealt with the churches' common action in society, took shape in 1925. Faith and Order, which dealt with the teachings that divided them, followed in 1927. The last two merged into the World Council of Churches. It was formed at Amsterdam on 22 August 1948 with 147 member churches, and the missionary council joined it at New Delhi in 1961. It was a council of churches, not a church. It had no power over its members and no creed of its own, and each member kept its own. Orthodox churches were members. The Roman Catholic Church was not. In 1928 Pope Pius XI ruled that Catholics could not take part. After the Second Vatican Council, Rome worked with the council in other ways. In the Middle East, the Middle East Council of Churches held its first assembly at Nicosia from 28 to 30 May 1974.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • The Student Volunteer Movement & Edinburgh 1910 Widely Accepted Major — In 1910 the World Missionary Conference met at Edinburgh. It moved one of those there, Charles Brent, to call a world meeting on the beliefs that split the churches. The World Council of Churches names Edinburgh as one of its roots.

Influenced By

  • The Greek Church and the Ecumenical Patriarchate in the Twentieth Century Widely Accepted — In January 1920 the Ecumenical Patriarchate sent a letter to all the churches. It said that their differences in belief need not keep them apart. The World Council of Churches names this letter as one of its roots.

Opposed By

  • The Catholic Church — Pius X to the Eve of the Council Widely Accepted Major — In 1928 Pope Pius XI wrote against the movement to unite the churches. He ruled that Rome would not join its meetings and that Catholics could not support it. A modern study says the letter seemed to settle whether Catholics could take part.Lasting result: In 1928 Pope Pius XI ruled that Rome would not take part in the movement's meetings and that Catholics could not lawfully support it.

Members

  • Disciples of Christ & the Independent Christian Churches Widely Accepted — The Disciples of Christ in the United States joined the World Council of Churches when it was formed in 1948. The council's own list of member churches gives that date.
  • The Greek Church and the Ecumenical Patriarchate in the Twentieth Century Widely Accepted — The Ecumenical Patriarchate and the Church of Greece both joined the World Council of Churches in 1948. The council's own list of member churches gives that year for each.
  • African-Initiated Churches (Harrist; Aladura; Zionist/ZCC; Kimbanguist) Widely Accepted — In August 1969 the World Council of Churches met at Canterbury. It voted to take in the Kimbanguist church as a member. The church's head, Joseph Diangienda, thanked the council.
