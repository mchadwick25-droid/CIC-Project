Charismatic Renewal & the Jesus Movement

About This Movement

On 3 April 1960 Dennis Bennett told his congregation in Van Nuys, California, that he had spoken in tongues. The announcement is conventionally taken as the start of the charismatic movement inside the historic Protestant churches, and it went where the earlier Pentecostal revival had not: into mainline Protestant parishes, whose members mostly stayed where they were. Bennett published his own account, Nine O'Clock in the Morning, in 1970. Alongside it and overlapping it came the Jesus movement, roughly 1967 to 1972, which reached young people outside the churches altogether and brought a great many of them in — Calvary Chapel, begun in 1965, became its best-known institutional home, and the music that came out of it changed what congregational worship sounded like. The Vineyard association formed in 1982, at the edge of this window, as the wave handed on to what came next.

In Its Own Record

Gave Rise To

  • The Third Wave & the Vineyard Widely Accepted Major — In 1977 John Wimber planted a Calvary Chapel church at Yorba Linda. In 1982 it left Calvary Chapel, because some of its leaders did not share the church's stress on the Holy Spirit. It joined the Vineyard churches, which Wimber came to lead.

Influenced By

  • Azusa Street & Early Pentecostalism Widely Accepted — A Presbyterian report of 1969 said Pentecostal faith was in its second time of fast growth. Now it was growing in the old churches too, as the charismatic renewal.

Influenced

  • The Mainline Century — Liberal Protestantism & Neo-Orthodoxy Widely Accepted — In 1969 a Presbyterian report found a small but growing charismatic movement in America's main churches. In 1970 it told pastors and church boards how to live with it.
