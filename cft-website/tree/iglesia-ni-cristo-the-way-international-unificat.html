N. America, today in United States and Mexico · Korea, today in South Korea and beyond

The Way International; Unification Church; La Luz del Mundo

About This Movement

Three separately founded bodies share this entry, but not a confession. Each is described from its own published texts. The Way International began in 1942 as Victor Paul Wierwille's Vesper Chimes radio ministry. It took its later name in 1955. His Power for Abundant Living appeared in 1971, and Jesus Christ Is Not God in 1975, a title that states its own position. Sun Myung Moon founded the Unification Church in Seoul on 1 May 1954. It was later known as the Family Federation for World Peace and Unification. Moon's Divine Principle appeared in Korean in 1966. Eusebio Joaquín González founded La Luz del Mundo in Guadalajara on 6 April 1926. From 1964 a second apostle led it, in succession from him.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • Oneness Pentecostalism Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — Scholars trace La Luz del Mundo to Oneness Pentecostal believers in northern Mexico. Its founder, Eusebio Joaquín González, met them through his wife. He was baptized in late 1925. The church does not call itself Pentecostal.

Sources

  • The Way International: Wierwille, Jesus Christ Is Not God (1975); Power for Abundant Living (1971); current statements-of-belief (primary) — The founder's own book title states the movement's core doctrinal position directly.
  • Unification Church: Divine Principle (Wolli Kangnon 1966; ET 1973); Moon's published speeches corpus (primary) — The movement's own scripture-plus-interpretation, at the center of its theology.
  • La Luz del Mundo: the church's own Apostleship confession (founding 1926; succession through Samuel and Naason Joaquin in its own publications); the 2022 California guilty plea and sentencing of Naason Joaquin Garcia (primary) — The criminal court record is a legal proceeding, not the church's own confessional material - a different kind of evidence, read on its own terms.
  • Eileen Barker, The Making of a Moonie (Blackwell, 1984); the NRM-sociology literature (secondary) — Academic scholarship on The Way and La Luz del Mundo is notably thin relative to how large these movements are.
