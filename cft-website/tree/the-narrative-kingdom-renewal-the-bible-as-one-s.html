The Global Church Era · c. 1990-c. 2010 · N. America, UK, then global (digital)

The Narrative-Kingdom Renewal (the Bible as one story fulfilled in Jesus)

North America and the UK, c. 1990 to 2010 — a reading of the Bible as one story fulfilled in Jesus, carried from Newbigin through Willard to Wright's academic and popular books.

About This Tradition

Audio Brief 0:59

Read along

A way of reading the Bible as a single story, running from creation to Jesus and outward from him, spread across evangelical and mainline churches from about 1990 without ever becoming an organization. Lesslie Newbigin's The Gospel in a Pluralist Society (1989) set the terms by asking what the church is for in a culture that has heard the story and moved on. Dallas Willard's The Divine Conspiracy (1998) put the kingdom back at the center of discipleship. N. T. Wright's academic volumes — The New Testament and the People of God (1992), Jesus and the Victory of God (1996) — and his popular ones, Simply Christian (2006) and Surprised by Hope (2008), carried it to a general readership.

Documented Stories

Home by Bus, Then Winson Green

Lesslie Newbigin had gone to India as a missionary in 1936, had been one of the first bishops of the united Church of South India in 1947, and had served as bishop in Madras. In 1974, at sixty-five, he and his wife Helen left India for England by bus, a journey of two months by local transport through Pakistan, Afghanistan, Iran and Turkey, staying with churches along the way. He taught mission at Selly Oak in Birmingham for five years. Then, at seventy, having retired again, he learned that the United Reformed Church intended to close its small congregation in Winson Green, an inner-city district beside the prison and the gasworks, where almost none of the neighbors were English or Christian. He asked to be its minister. He served it from 1980 to 1988, visiting door to door in a district where he was often the only white face, and learning what he had known in theory: that England was now as much a mission field as Tamil Nadu, and less aware of it. Out of Winson Green came the books, The Other Side of 1984 (1983), Foolishness to the Greeks (1986) and The Gospel in a Pluralist Society (1989), which argued that the church in the West had accepted a private place in a public culture and must learn to tell the whole story again. He kept visiting the prison until he could no longer see to walk there.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

From about 1990 a reading of the Bible as one continuous story running from creation to Jesus spread through evangelical and mainline churches without ever forming an organization, moving from Lesslie Newbigin's and Dallas Willard's books to N. T. Wright's academic and popular writing. It stood as a direct alternative to reading the Bible as a chart of the future.

Legacy

In 2010 it was a teaching current, not a body with members. It lived in reading plans, classes and church courses, and this map takes that at face value. It gave a generation a way to read the Bible as a story rather than a scheme. That set it apart from the dispensational reading culture shown elsewhere on this map. No standard study of it had appeared by 2010, which is itself worth saying. So no study yet shows how far its writers set out to answer that culture.
