The Early Church Era · c. 240-312 · Neocaesarea in Pontus, Olympus in Lycia

Neocaesarea · Olympus, today in Turkey

The Anatolian Church in the Third Century

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Gregory of Neocaesarea went south to study law and came back a bishop. Instead of law, he had fallen in with Origen at Caesarea and stayed five years. A farewell address bears his name. It is one of the warmest pictures of a teacher that anyone wrote in antiquity, though some scholars doubt that he wrote it. What he did in Pontus after that is mostly legend. One story says he found seventeen Christians in the city and left seventeen pagans. But that story comes from a century after his time. One document survives, though, that is not legend at all. When Gothic raiders came through in the 250s, Gregory wrote a canonical letter, a letter of church rules. It worked out what to do about Christians who had looted alongside the raiders, or informed, or kept what the raiders left. It shows a bishop dealing with a wrecked province, case by case. A generation later and further south, Methodius was bishop of Olympus in Lycia. He wrote the Banquet of the Ten Virgins, a Christian rewriting of Plato's Symposium. In it, ten women take turns praising chastity. He also became one of the first substantial critics of Origen, whose most devoted pupil had been Gregory. Methodius died in the persecution around 311.

In Its Own Record

Influenced By

  • Alexandrian Catechetical / Christian-Platonist Tradition Documented — Eusebius says the brothers Gregory and Athenodorus studied under Origen for five years. Both then became bishops in Pontus. Gregory's farewell speech to Origen is traditionally his, though some scholars dispute its authorship.

Influenced

  • Cappadocian Christianity Documented — Writing to the Neocaesareans around 375, Basil says his grandmother Macrina, who came from their city, taught him the words of Gregory the Wonderworker. He offers this as proof that his faith is sound. In On the Holy Spirit he defends a form of doxology as one Gregory handed down.
  • The Byzantine Imperial Church (Heraclian & Iconoclast Eras) Documented Major — In 692 the Council in Trullo at Constantinople confirmed a list of older church canons as binding. The list includes the canons of Gregory the Wonderworker, bishop of Neocaesarea.Lasting result: At Constantinople, the Council in Trullo set its seal on the canons of Gregory the Wonderworker. It put them with those of the great councils.
  • Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity Documented — In his catalog of Christian writers, Jerome lists the works of Methodius of Olympus, among them the Banquet of the ten virgins. He names the book, but whether he drew on it in his own writing is not shown.

Argued Against

  • Alexandrian Catechetical / Christian-Platonist Tradition Documented — Methodius, bishop of Olympus in Lycia, wrote On the Resurrection against Origen and On Free Will against him too. Jerome lists both works. Photius summarizes the first as arguing that the body is not the soul's prison.

In Contact With

  • The Church of Antioch in the Third Century Documented Major — Eusebius says bishops from many provinces gathered at Antioch to deal with Paul of Samosata. Among the most eminent he names Gregory and his brother Athenodorus, pastors of the churches in Pontus.Lasting result: Gregory and Athenodorus took part in the meetings at Antioch on Paul of Samosata. These came to an end when a last synod cut him off from communion.
