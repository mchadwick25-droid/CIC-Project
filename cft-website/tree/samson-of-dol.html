Samson of Dol

A person. The tradition that tells their story: British and Welsh Christianity.

Known for
A British monk and bishop, trained at Eltut's school, who crossed by way of Cornwall to Brittany and founded the monastery of Dol, known from a Life of uncertain date and a signature at a council of Paris.
Also called
Saint Samson, Samson, bishop of Dol
Dates
Signed a council at Paris that Friedrich Maassen dates between 556 and 573. Thomas Taylor reckons from the Life that he was made bishop probably in 521, and born about 486 if he was then thirty-five; both years are Taylor's inference
Place
Of Demetia (Dyfed) in Wales, by the Life; his father of Dyfed and his mother of Gwent, by Taylor's summary; Eltut's school at Llantwit; Ireland; Cornwall; Dol in Brittany, where he died
Role
Monk, abbot and bishop; founder of the monastery of Dol

About This Person

In Cornwall, in a district called Tricurius, Samson of Dol heard people worshipping an idol with a play. On a hilltop stood their image. He went to them with two of his monks and told them to leave it. The writer of his Life adds that he has stood on that hill himself. With his own hand, he says, he traced the cross Samson had carved with an iron tool on a standing stone.

Samson was a British monk and bishop who founded the monastery of Dol in Brittany. His Life says he was taken as a boy to the school of Eltut, the most learned of the Britons. He became an abbot, sailed to Ireland, and was made a bishop. Then he crossed to Brittany and founded Dol and many other monasteries. He died and was buried at Dol.

The Life is a book of miracles, with a dove at his ordination and serpents driven away. Its writer gives no name. He says he wrote for Bishop Tigernomalus, from what an old man, a cousin of Samson, told him, and from accounts the deacon Henoc brought.

When he wrote is disputed. Thomas Taylor, who put the Life into English, accepts 610 to 615. He follows Abbé Duine, who found nothing borrowed from writings later than the 500s. Taylor reports that the scholar Fawtier called the work spurious and rejected what its writer says of its authorship and origin. Duine and Professor Loth answered him.

A second witness stands beside the Life. Friedrich Maassen dates a council at Paris between 556 and 573. One of its bishops signed, "Samson subscripsi et consensi in nomine Christi": I, Samson, have signed and agreed, in the name of Christ. It names no see, and Maassen's note gives Dol with a question mark.

Taylor says later Lives of saints tried to outdo its miracles. The Life, he writes, "actually furnished the mould into which many of them were cast."

Legacy

What traces to them

  • The Life says he founded Dol and many other monasteries throughout almost the whole province, and that he went to King Hiltbert, whom Taylor names as Childebert, to free a captive named Judual.
  • Taylor reports pilgrimages from Britain and Brittany to his tomb at Dol, a church and parish bearing his name near Fowey, and a church at Southill dedicated to him.

How they're remembered

  • Taylor: later Lives of saints tried to rival this one's miracles, and it "actually furnished the mould into which many of them were cast".
  • A later Life of Illtud, printed by W. J. Rees, counts him among Illtud's pupils with Paulinus, Gildas and Dewi.

What Is Uncertain

  • Nearly all that is known of him comes from one Life, a book of miracles whose writer gives no name. The writer says he drew on an old man of Samson's family and on accounts the deacon Henoc brought from overseas.
  • When the Life was written. Taylor accepts 610 to 615, following Abbé Duine, who found in it nothing borrowed from writings later than the end of the 500s. Taylor reports that Fawtier called the work spurious and did not accept its writer's statements on its authorship and origin; Duine and Professor Loth answered him. Hugh Williams, editing Gildas in 1899, puts it no earlier than the seventh century and no later than 824.
  • The council signature is a second witness to the man, not to the Life's details. It reads only "Samson", with no see; Maassen's note gives Dol with a question mark, and his index lists him as bishop of Dol.
  • His birth and consecration years are Taylor's reckoning from the Life's notes on the day of his consecration, and the Life gives no year.
