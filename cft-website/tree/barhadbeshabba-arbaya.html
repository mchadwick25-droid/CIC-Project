Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia of Halwan

A person. The tradition that tells their story: The Church of the East under Persia.

Known for
Bishop of Halwan in the Church of the East who wrote the Cause of the Foundation of the Schools. Addai Scher calls it the first history of the school of Nisibis. It names the school's masters from Narsai of Nisibis to the author's own master, Henana of Adiabene.
Also called
Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia, bishop of Halwan, Barhadbšabba 'Arbaya, Bar Hadbesabba, Hadbsabba 'Arbaya, Bar Hadhbésabba urbis Holwan, Barhadbesciaba Arabs
Dates
No source used here gives the year of his birth or death. Addai Scher dates the Cause after Ishoyahb of Arzun became head of the church, which Scher puts in 581, and before 604. R. H. Connolly puts it a little after 585, and a note by the editors of the Patrologia Orientalis about 590. A Bar Hadbeshabba, bishop of Halwan, signs the acts of the synod of Gregory of Prat, which J.-B. Chabot dates to April 605. A chronicle Ignazio Guidi edited names a Bar Hadbeshabba of Halwan in the vacancy after Gregory's death. François Nau and Scher give that vacancy as 609 to 628.
Place
Beth 'Arbaye by origin, by his second name, Addai Scher says; Joseph Assemani puts it near Nisibis. The school of Nisibis, in the Persian empire. Halwan, spelled Holwan in the synod's acts, as bishop
Role
Student of the school of Nisibis; bishop of Halwan; writer of the Cause of the Foundation of the Schools

About This Person

The bishops of the Church of the East met at the Persian king's court to choose a new head. J.-B. Chabot, who edited their acts, dates the meeting to April 605. The acts say King Khosrau the Second had the distant bishops brought on his own beasts. They chose Gregory of Prat. The twenty-seventh to sign is Bar Hadbeshabba, bishop of Halwan.

Addai Scher and François Nau take this bishop to be Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia, who wrote the Cause of the Foundation of the Schools. The title Scher prints calls him bishop of Halwan. Scher says his second name marks him as a man of Beth 'Arbaye, and that he studied at Nisibis under Henana of Adiabene.

The Cause tells of the schools through which God taught angels and people, from Adam to the schools of Edessa and Nisibis.

The Cause speaks of Henana as a living master. It praises his humility and prays that God will lengthen his days. Henana's own church, Scher says, always charged him with leaving Theodore of Mopsuestia's reading of Scripture. Scher thinks the author probably hid the truth to praise him.

The Chronicle of Seert tells of a walk-out from the school of Nisibis. Some three hundred students left when the head of the church took Henana's side. It names among them a Hadbeshabba 'Arbaya, later made metropolitan of Halwan. Scher takes him for the Cause's author.

Part of a history of the holy Fathers persecuted for the truth survives from the same school. Nau gives it to Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia, a priest of the school of Nisibis, and dates it to the end of the 500s. He thinks it not impossible that this writer is the bishop of Halwan.

Of the school that Narsai of Nisibis founded, the Cause says, in R. H. Connolly's English, "Edessa was darkened, but Nisibis shone forth; and the dominions of the Romans were filled with error, but those of Persia with the knowledge of true religion".

Key Works

  • Cause of the Foundation of the Schools: the schools through which God taught angels and people, from Adam to the apostles and the schools of Alexandria and Antioch. It ends with the schools of Edessa and Nisibis, and the masters of Nisibis down to Henana of Adiabene. A note by the editors of the Patrologia Orientalis reads it as an opening address for the school session

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Addai Scher calls the Cause's account of the schools of Edessa and Nisibis very precious. He calls it the first work to trace the history of the school of Nisibis. Since it was written at the end of the sixth century, he trusts it over later accounts on two points. These are Narsai's forty-five years at Nisibis, and Elisha, Narsai's successor, never being a bishop.
  • R. H. Connolly calls the part of it that Alphonsus Mingana first printed of first-rate importance for the history of the school of Nisibis. He reckons Narsai's death from its forty-five years.

How they're remembered

  • An anonymous chronicle that Ignazio Guidi edited tells of the years after Gregory of Prat died. In those years, it says, Bar Hadbeshabba of Halwan won fame for writing books.
  • J. Labourt calls Barhadbeshabba of Holwan a distinguished writer.
  • Abdisho of Nisibis lists the books of a Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia. Among them are the Treasures in three volumes, controversies against all sects and a church history. He also lists a work on the followers of Diodore and a commentary on Mark and the Psalms. Addai Scher gives the list to the Cause's author, and François Nau to the History's writer.

What Is Uncertain

  • His own life is thin in the sources. It rests on the title and words of the Cause and a signature in the synod of 605. It also rests on a line in the Chronicle of Seert and one in the chronicle Guidi edited. Addai Scher joins all of these to one man. François Nau joins the signature and Guidi's chronicle to the Cause's author.
  • The signer. The acts of 605 list a Bar Hadbeshabba, bishop of Holwan, without the epithet 'Arbaia. Scher and Nau take him to be the Cause's author, whose title calls him bishop of Halwan.
  • Bishop or metropolitan. The synod's acts and the Cause's title call him bishop. The Chronicle of Seert says a Hadbeshabba 'Arbaya was made metropolitan of Houlwan; Scher reads the name as Barhadbeshabba.
  • The History and the Cause. The History's title calls its writer a priest and head of the overseers of the school of Nisibis. François Nau takes him to be the Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia whose books Abdisho of Nisibis lists. He thinks it not impossible that this man is also the bishop of Halwan. For that, Nau says, it is enough to suppose he wrote the History young and the Cause in old age. Scher treats them as one, giving the Cause's author Abdisho's list of books, and notes that the list does not name the Cause.
  • When the Cause was written. Scher dates it between Ishoyahb of Arzun's rise and 604, from the events it tells and the walk-out. R. H. Connolly puts it a little after 585, the year he gives for Henana's condemnation. The editors of the Patrologia Orientalis say about 590.
  • His part with Henana. The Cause praises Henana as a living master and calls the troubles against him the devil's work. Scher thinks the author probably hid the truth to praise his master, since Henana's church always charged him with leaving Theodore of Mopsuestia. The Chronicle of Seert puts a Hadbeshabba 'Arbaya among the students who left the school in protest. Scher asks why the pupil would leave the master he praised, and guesses he followed the stronger party. He also suggests the Cause was written to bring peace back to the school.
  • The Cause's worth. Scher reports that Alphonsus Mingana took it for part of Barhadbeshabba's history, and that J.-B. Chabot judged it a poorly joined set of older accounts. Scher holds it a separate work to be trusted fully. He thinks the passages that troubled Chabot, found in Mingana's manuscript, were added by a copyist.
  • The line in Guidi's chronicle. In Guidi's Latin, Bar Hadbeshabba of Holwan won fame for writing books, and others of the time stood out for their way of life. Nau's French gives him both.
  • Namesakes. The Chronicle of Seert and Mari ibn Sulayman name a Barhadbeshabba, monk of Mount Sa'ran. They say Sabrisho of Lashom, head of the church, wanted him as his successor. J. Labourt thinks the bishop of Holwan is perhaps that man. Joseph Assemani thinks Abdisho's Barhadbeshabba a different man from the one who lived in the time of Jacob, founder of the monastery of Beth Abe. Others are the deacon Barhadbeshabba of Arbela, Barhadbeshabba of Qardu, and the archimandrite Bar Hadbeshabba to whom Ishoyahb III wrote.
