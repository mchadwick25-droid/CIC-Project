The Church of the East — Timur's Catastrophe and the Northern Remnant

From Mesopotamia to the Kurdish mountains, 1317 to 1517. The most widely spread church of the Middle Ages shrinks to a remnant on the upper Tigris and in the highland valleys.

About This Movement

Audio Brief · Narrated Edition 1:18

Read along

For centuries the Church of the East had spread wider than any other church on earth. Its provinces ran from Baghdad across Central Asia to India and China. Within a lifetime most of it was gone. The patriarch Yahballaha III, a monk born far to the east among the Öngüd, died in 1317. By then the Mongol rulers of Persia had turned to Islam, and Christians in Mesopotamia were a persecuted minority. Then Timur's campaigns, from the 1380s to 1405, destroyed the lowland dioceses and much of the population that had kept them alive. What survived did so by geography, in the Hakkari mountains and around Lake Van and Lake Urmia. The patriarchs of the late fifteenth century lived on the upper Tigris, at Gazarta and Mosul. By the sixteenth century the office passed within one family, from uncle to nephew. The one province Timur never reached was the community on the Malabar coast of India. It went on receiving bishops from its patriarch, who sent them out in 1490 and again in 1503.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

For centuries the Church of the East had spread wider than any other church on earth. It reached from Baghdad to Central Asia, India and China. Then Timur's campaigns, from the 1380s to 1405, destroyed its lowland dioceses and much of the population that kept them alive. What survived did so by geography, in the Hakkari mountains and around Lake Van and Lake Urmia. By the sixteenth century the patriarch's office passed within one family. The one province Timur never reached was the community on India's Malabar coast. It kept receiving bishops from its patriarch. He sent them from the upper Tigris in 1490 and again in 1503.

Legacy

The remnant endured. In 1552 its patriarchate, held by one family, split in two. Part of the church entered communion with Rome. The two lines run to the present as the Assyrian Church of the East and the Chaldean Catholic Church. Their Syriac liturgy and their old books live on. The people who keep them were scattered far and wide by the persecutions of the twentieth century. And the Indian province that outlasted the catastrophe became the St Thomas Christians of Kerala. Their descendants number several million.
