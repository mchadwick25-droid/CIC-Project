Gregory of Tours

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Merovingian Gallic Christianity.

Known for
Bishop of Tours under the Frankish kings and writer of the History of the Franks, the Miracles of Martin of Tours and the Lives of the Fathers, who stood against King Chilperic on the Trinity and cleared himself by oath when charged with slandering Queen Fredegund.
Also called
Georgius Florentius, Gregorius Turonensis
Dates
Born in 538 or 539 and died on 17 November 594, in W. Arndt's reckoning; Bruno Krusch, in the same edition, puts his death on 17 November 593. Bishop from 573, which W. Arndt reckons from Gregory's own date, the twelfth year of King Sigibert. Gregory also calls it the 172nd year after Martin's passing, a count Arndt says does not agree. No ancient source used here gives the year of his birth or death
Place
Clermont in Auvergne, where Ernest Brehaut says he was born and grew up; Tours, as its bishop
Role
Bishop of Tours; historian and writer of saints' miracles and lives

About This Person

Gregory of Tours lay ill with fever and sores, unable to eat or drink. He thought only of his burial, he writes. He set out for the tomb of Martin of Tours. In a forest the fever came back, and his friends begged him to go home. He refused. If he died on the way, he said, they must "carry my dead body there and bury it", in Ernest Brehaut's English. After a third night's vigil at Martin's church, he slept and woke with all weakness and pain gone.

Gregory was born at Clermont, Brehaut says. In 573, by the reckoning of his editor W. Arndt, he became bishop of Tours, in Frankish Gaul. There he wrote the History of the Franks, often the sole authority, Brehaut says, for the events it tells.

Gregory's own History tells of his clash with King Chilperic, who held Tours. Brehaut says the two were as a rule unfriendly. The king ordered that the Trinity be called only God, with no distinct Persons. "This is my will, that such be thy faith," he told Gregory, in O. M. Dalton's English. Gregory answered that the Father is different from the Son, and the Son from the Holy Ghost. Whoever followed the king, he said, "will never be a wise man, but a fool." The king gnashed his teeth, but held his peace.

In 580, by Arndt's date, Leudast, once count of Tours, went to the king. He said Gregory claimed Queen Fredegund lived in adultery with Bertram, bishop of Bordeaux. Before a council of bishops, Gregory denied it. He said Mass at three altars and cleared himself by oath. The account is the accused man's own.

Arndt reckons he died on 17 November 594. Bruno Krusch, in the same volume, argues for 593.

Closing his History, Gregory charges later bishops of Tours to leave his books "complete and intact in your time just as I myself have left them."

Key Works

  • History of the Franks (Historia Francorum), in ten books: the Franks and the church of Gaul from the Creation to 591, in Brehaut's account, opening with Gregory's own creed and closing with his charge that the books be kept whole
  • Miracles of Martin of Tours (De virtutibus sancti Martini), in four books: the cures at Martin's tomb, his own among them; Bruno Krusch thinks Gregory died before finishing the fourth book
  • Lives of the Fathers (Vita Patrum): Lives of saints of Gaul, among them his uncle Gallus of Clermont and his mother's uncle Nicetius of Lyons

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Ernest Brehaut says his History is often the sole authority for the events it tells, "the perishing of the Roman Empire and the beginning of a great modern state".
  • Paul the Deacon, writing the History of the Langobards, sends readers to "the books of the reverend man Gregory, bishop of Tours", in W. D. Foulke's English.
  • He set his own creed at the head of the History, "that whoso reads may doubt not that I hold the catholic faith", in O. M. Dalton's English.

How they're remembered

  • Venantius Fortunatus, an Italian poet at Poitiers and his close friend, wrote verses for his arrival at Tours, saying King Sigibert and Queen Brunhild favored him. W. Arndt regrets that Fortunatus, who outlived him, left no Life of him.
  • The calendars of Tours keep his feast on 17 November, as Arndt quotes them from Ruinart's collection.

What Is Uncertain

  • The trial. His own History is the account of the accused party, and Leudast's charge and the trial are known only from Gregory. The council met at Brinnacum in the Latin, which is Berny in O. M. Dalton's English and Braine in Ernest Brehaut's. Venantius Fortunatus wrote a poem to King Chilperic for it; Dalton notes that the poem praises Queen Fredegund and says nothing of the affair. Brehaut warns that readers "must allow largely for error and prejudice in his statements of fact".
  • His years. No ancient source used here gives the year of his birth or death. W. Arndt reckons his birth in 538 or 539, from his mother's healing thirty-four years after it, and his death on 17 November 594. Bruno Krusch, in the same volume, puts the death on 17 November 593, from Gregory's twenty-first year as bishop and the unfinished fourth book of the Miracles of Martin. Brehaut gives 538 to 594, and a note in W. D. Foulke's translation of Paul the Deacon gives about 538 and 594 or 595. The Tours calendars give the day only.
  • His consecration day. Krusch puts it on 20 August 573, Arndt on 23 August and Dalton on 24 August. Arndt thinks it likely that King Sigibert placed him at Tours, rather than the citizens choosing him.
  • His great-grandfather. Gregory's Latin calls Gregory, bishop of Langres, "proavum nostrum", and Arndt and Krusch make that Gregory his mother Armentaria's grandfather. Brehaut says great-grandfather; Dalton translates "our ancestor" and calls him great-great-grandfather in a note.
  • The year of his healing at Martin's tomb. His Latin gives the 163rd year after Martin's passing, which Arndt reckons as 563; Brehaut's English has the hundred and sixtieth. Arndt says Gregory was already a deacon then.
  • A journey to Rome. A Life of Gregory, which Arndt dates to the tenth century, says he went to Rome before his death; Arndt rejects it. No copy of that Life is quoted here.
  • His name. Arndt says it is doubtful whether he took the name Gregory only when ordained; Brehaut says he dropped the name Georgius Florentinus to honor his great-grandfather.
