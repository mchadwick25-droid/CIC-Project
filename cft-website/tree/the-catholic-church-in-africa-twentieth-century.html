The Catholic Church in Africa in the Twentieth Century

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

On 25 May 1939 Joseph Kiwanuka was named vicar apostolic of Masaka, in Uganda. Pius XII consecrated him in Saint Peter's on 29 October that year. In 1956 a group of priests published Des prêtres noirs s'interrogent ('Black priests ask themselves questions'). Its writers included A. Abblé, A. Kagame, V. Mulago and M. Hebga. On 31 July 1969 Paul VI closed a symposium of Africa's bishops at Kampala. In his homily he told them they could, and must, have an African Christianity. In Zaire a trial text of the Mass came into use in 1974. Rome approved the Roman Missal for the Dioceses of Zaire on 30 April 1988, and Cardinal Joseph-Albert Malula was its chief promoter. In Katanga, in the Belgian Congo, the Franciscan Placide Tempels began the Jamaa movement. Its name is Swahili for 'family', and Johannes Fabian studied it in 1971 as a charismatic movement. In Rwanda the churches lived through the genocide of 1994. Timothy Longman argued that the churches had aligned with the state, and that church leaders backed the genocide to protect their own power. His thesis is contested. A special assembly of the Synod of Bishops for Africa met in Rome from 10 April to 8 May 1994. John Paul II signed its closing exhortation, Ecclesia in Africa, at Yaoundé on 14 September 1995.

  • The Catholic Church of the Long Nineteenth Century Inferential-Thin Major — The Catholic Church in Africa of the twentieth century grew from missions like Lavigerie's White Fathers. He reached Algiers as archbishop on 16 May 1867. In 1888 he said his missioners were settled in the Sahara and in Central Africa. The evidence read is thin: one editor's book of 1889, and no text that follows the line into the later church.

Continues

  • East African Christianity & the Uganda Martyrs Widely Accepted Major — In 1964 Pope Paul VI greeted the bishops, priests and faithful of Uganda in Rome, after he made the Uganda Martyrs saints. In 1969 he came to Namugongo to bless the altar of a shrine raised in their memory.
