Julian, missionary to the Nobadae

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Nubian Christianity.

Known for
Old presbyter among the clergy of Theodosius of Alexandria who, with the empress Theodora's backing, brought the Nobadae beyond the Thebais to baptism and to the side that refused the Council of Chalcedon.
Also called
Julianus
Dates
Sixth century, under Justinian and Theodora. John of Ephesus gives no year. He says Julian stayed two years among the Nobadae and died a very short time before Theodosius of Alexandria
Place
Constantinople; the land of the Nobadae, beyond the Thebais
Role
Presbyter and missionary

About This Person

The empress Theodora wrote to the duke of the Thebais, John of Ephesus says. She and her husband Justinian had each planned an embassy to the Nobadae, a people beyond the Thebais, at the edge of Egypt. She was sending a blessed man named Julian, and her envoy had to arrive first. If the duke let the emperor's envoy through before hers, she warned, "your life shall answer for it; for I will immediately send and take off your head." So the duke held back the emperor's envoy, saying he still needed pack animals and men who knew the deserts.

Julian was a presbyter and an old man, one of the clergy of Theodosius of Alexandria. John says Julian longed to make Christians of this people and took the plan to Theodora. Justinian disliked it, John writes, because Julian opposed the Council of Chalcedon. So the emperor told bishops of his own side in the Thebais to go and teach them.

All that is known of Julian comes from John. John's English translator, Payne Smith, notes that John belonged to the party that refused Chalcedon, and John tells the story from Julian's side. The later chronicle of Michael the Syrian shortens John's account.

The king of the Nobadae received Julian with joy. He and his nobles gave up the gods of their fathers. Julian then told them of Chalcedon, and of Theodosius, who had lost his throne rather than accept the council. When the emperor's envoy came, they took the gifts and refused the faith. They would follow "the example of pope Theodosius," they answered.

Julian stayed two years. He used to say that "from nine o'clock until four in the afternoon" he sheltered in caves full of water, wearing only a linen cloth like the local people. Even so he taught, and baptized the king, his nobles and many people. He left them in the care of Theodore, an old bishop from the Thebais, whom John later calls bishop of Philae.

Julian returned safely to Constantinople, where Theodora received him with honor. He died a short time before Theodosius. John gives no year for any of it.

Afterwards a priest named Longinus was made bishop for the Nobadae, as Theodora had ordered. John calls Julian "their teacher."

Legacy

What traces to them

  • John of Ephesus says Julian left the Nobadae in the care of Theodore, an old bishop from the Thebais, whom he later calls bishop of Philae.
  • After Julian's death, John says, the priest Longinus was made bishop for the Nobadae, as the empress Theodora had ordered.

How they're remembered

  • John of Ephesus calls him "the blessed Julian their teacher".
  • Michael the Syrian's twelfth-century chronicle retells the mission in short form; its French translator, J.-B. Chabot, notes that the account is shortened from John of Ephesus.

What Is Uncertain

  • Every fact rests on one witness, John of Ephesus, who tells the story from Julian's side. John's English translator, Payne Smith, notes that John belonged to the party that refused the Council of Chalcedon. Michael the Syrian's account is shortened from John's, and Bar Hebraeus's, from the thirteenth century, is later still; neither is a second witness.
  • John gives no year for the mission, the two years among the Nobadae, or Julian's death.
  • The race between the empress's envoy and the emperor's is John's story, told as an episode of the empress's craft. Michael the Syrian and Bar Hebraeus retell it after him. No held source tells it from the emperor's side.
  • John does not say where Julian died. Michael the Syrian says he died in the imperial city after his return.
  • Julian's words about the caves come as John reports them: "he used to say".
