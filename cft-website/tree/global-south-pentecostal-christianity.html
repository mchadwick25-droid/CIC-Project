The Global Church Era · 1910-c. 2010 · Brazil, Nigeria, Ghana, Korea

Global South Pentecostal Christianity

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

By the second half of the twentieth century the weight of Pentecostal faith had moved south. Most of the churches that carried it were founded in the Global South by people from there. The Assembleias de Deus in Brazil date from 1911. The Redeemed Christian Church of God was founded in Nigeria in 1952. Yoido Full Gospel Church in Seoul dates from 1958, and the Igreja Universal in Brazil from 1977. They are not one tradition. Only a close look, at one body in one country in one generation, shows anything real. In South India a Pentecostal movement grew through the twentieth century. Michael Bergunder wrote its history in 2008. The family's sharpest inside argument was over prosperity teaching. It is on the public record in the sermons that teach it. It is also in critiques by the Lausanne movement and by African evangelical writers who rejected it.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • Azusa Street & Early Pentecostalism Contested Major — Scholars disagree on how far Pentecostal churches in the Global South go back to Azusa Street. In 1906 its paper said six missionaries had left for Africa. But Allan Anderson finds churches in Lagos that stressed gifts of the Spirit from 1918, led by Africans.
  • African-Initiated Churches (Harrist; Aladura; Zionist/ZCC; Kimbanguist) Widely Accepted Major — Josiah Akindayomi joined the Cherubim and Seraphim, one of Nigeria's prayer churches, in 1931. So says the church he went on to found. He left in 1952 and began the Redeemed Christian Church of God. Allan Anderson puts a second Nigerian church halfway between the prayer churches and the Pentecostals.

Gave Rise To

  • African Diaspora & Reverse-Mission Churches in Europe Widely Accepted Major — The Redeemed Christian Church of God was founded in Lagos in 1952. Its own history says the church has spread to England, Germany and France. Its parishes there are among the African-led churches planted in Europe.

Opposed By

  • Lausanne-Era Global Evangelicalism Inferential-Thin — In 2008 and 2009 Lausanne theologians met in Ghana. They called the prosperity gospel a false gospel, found in Pentecostal and other churches. But they said they spoke to the teaching, not to any one church. In 2010 the Cape Town Commitment rejected its excesses.
