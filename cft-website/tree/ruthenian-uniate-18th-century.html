Ruthenia, today in Ukraine and Belarus · Austrian Galicia

The Ruthenian Uniate Church — Zamość to the Partitions

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

A century after the Union of Brest, the Greek Catholic church of the Ruthenian lands — today's Ukraine and Belarus — was at its numerical height, holding the loyalty of most of the peasantry of the eastern Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. The Synod of Zamość in 1720 consolidated it and also latinized it, importing Roman devotional practices into a Byzantine rite and settling its identity as something distinctly its own rather than a temporary arrangement. Then the political ground vanished: the partitions of Poland between 1772 and 1795 handed most of the church's people to Catherine II's Russia, which regarded the union as an amputation to be reversed, and campaigns of forced reversion from 1794 stripped it of the great majority of its parishes within two years. What survived did so in Austrian Galicia, where the Habsburgs raised the Greek Catholic clergy's status and paid for their education. And where the church became, over the following century, the carrier of Ukrainian national identity.

In Its Own Record

Continues

  • The Union of Brest & Ruthenian Greek Catholic Christianity Widely Accepted Major — In 1728 the Latin bishop of Lutsk and Brest wrote to Leo Kiszka, 'Metropolitan of Kyiv and all Rus'. He promised to keep a rule of Pope Urban VIII that kept Ruthenians from moving to the Latin rite. The historian Harasiewicz lists Kiszka among the heads of this church in communion with Rome since the union of 1596.

Continued As

  • The Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church — Polotsk to Galicia Documented Major — In 1807 Pope Pius VII freed the Ruthenian sees of Lviv, Chełm and Przemyśl, until then under the metropolitan of Kyiv. He made the bishop of Lviv also bishop of the ancient see of Halych, with Chełm and Przemyśl under him. A Lviv canon's history of 1862 follows this same hierarchy under Austrian rule from 1808.

Influenced By

  • The Tridentine Church Documented — The Ruthenian synod at Zamość in 1720 wrote that it followed in the footsteps of the fathers of the Council of Trent. Those present professed that they received the Council of Trent. The synod barred parish priests from keeping feasts outside its list, and that list included Corpus Christi, a feast of the Latin church.

Opposed By

  • The Russian Church — Nikon to the Holy Synod Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — In 1794 Victor Sadkowski, the Orthodox archbishop of Minsk, sent out a letter. It urged the Uniates to come back to the Orthodox church. Priests he sent took over parish churches where even a few people agreed, with Russian officials and soldiers at their side. Two Greek Catholic historians tell this from the Uniate side, in 1862 and 1880.Lasting result: In 1795 Catherine II ruled that all those whose families had joined the union after 1595 must go back to the Russian church.
