Transylvania, today in Romania · Blaj

The Romanian Greek Catholic Church, 1698 to 1815

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

On 7 October 1698 Romanian clergy at Alba Iulia joined the Roman Catholic Church. The act is dated in Latin. It names the bishop, the archpriests and the priests of the Wallachian churches as its makers. Wallachian was the Latin word for Romanian.

The act asks for the same privileges that Roman Church priests have, under the emperor's decree. The Jesuit house history says an imperial letter of 1692 had given united priests of the Greek rite the rights of the Latin clergy. Nilles prints it.

The Jesuits of Alba Iulia opened the talks. The synod of 1701 says so. Its Latin says that the talks of union began through the work and skill of the Jesuit fathers living at Alba Iulia (opera ac solertia Patrum S. J., Albae-Juliae degentium). Nilles was himself a Jesuit. He adds that the house history shows the Jesuit fathers almost alone tending the Lord's vineyard at Alba Iulia in those years.

The state kept a hand on the church. In 1728 Charles VI ordered the united Romanian clergy to meet in an electoral synod and give him three candidates for the bishop's seat. The synod met at Alba Iulia on 4 June 1728. The Catholic bishop of Transylvania was there as the king's commissioner. So was the rector of the Jesuit college at Cluj, as the emperor's order required.

The bishop's seat moved to Blaj. Blaj was then only a Calvinist and Hungarian village. A contract with the court chamber, dated 31 August 1736, gave the estate of Blaj to the bishopric. The bishop took it in May 1737, in Bunea's dating, and moved there at once.

Orthodox Romanians pushed back. In 1744 a Serbian monk, Visarion, crossed southern Transylvania and stirred the people against the union. He was arrested on the way into Sibiu. He was questioned on 27 April 1744. Empress Maria Theresa wrote in 1746 of that impostor, the false monk (acel pseudo-monach impostor), as Bunea quotes her. Bunea adds that the movement grew far beyond one man. The people were also angry at how the bishop's requests had been met.

Bishop Klein pressed the court for his people's rights. Asked to calm the movement, he answered: „Cum vreţi să liniştesc spiritele: cu mijlóce blânde ori cu mijlóce violente?“ In a plain rendering: how do you want me to calm the people, with mild means or with force? If with mild means, he said, the rights asked for on the basis of the diplomas must be promised to us and truly given. In 1747 his supporters wrote from Daia that, if he were forced out, the Romanian people would abandon the union. Bunea says Klein himself urged that letter.

On 7 May 1751 Klein gave up his see. He did so by a formal act placed in the pope's hands.
