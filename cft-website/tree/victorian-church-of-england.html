The Victorian Church of England

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

The Church of England entered the nineteenth century as a comfortable establishment and left it a contested one. Three parties argued for its soul. Evangelicals who had inherited the awakening's fervor and its campaigns against slavery and squalor, high churchmen who rediscovered the church's catholic inheritance, and broad churchmen who wanted room for the new biblical criticism and the new geology. The quarrels went to law: the Gorham case of 1850 over what baptism does, the storm over the essayists of 1860 who applied critical methods to Scripture, ritual prosecutions that put clergymen in prison over vestments and candles. Underneath the controversies the church rebuilt itself, in thousands of new buildings and slum parishes at home and in a mission network abroad that by 1867 had produced enough overseas bishops that a first Lambeth Conference had to be called to hold them together.

In Its Own Record

Gave Rise To

  • The Oxford Movement & Anglo-Catholic Revival Widely Accepted Major — The Oxford Movement began in 1833 among Church of England clergy who taught at Oxford. A Scottish book list of 1846 calls them "the New Anglican School, commonly called Puseyites". Their critics in the church included the Dean of Bristol.
  • East African Christianity & the Uganda Martyrs Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — In 1877 the Church Missionary Society, an Anglican body from England, sent men to the kingdom of Buganda. Their first converts, mostly young men at the king's court, became the first Anglican church there. Modern histories tell this, Adrian Hastings's among them.

Continues

  • The Restoration and Georgian Church of England Documented Major — The 1662 Prayer Book has a sermon or a homily follow the Creed at communion. In 1859 an Oxford editor said this rule stood 'nearly the same as we have it at the present day.' The same book and church carried on into the 1800s.

In Contact With

  • The Church of the East & the Chaldean Consolidation Documented — In 1892 two members of the 'Archbishop of Canterbury's Assyrian Mission' described their years in Kurdistan and northern Persia. They wrote that the Archbishop sent the mission at the people's repeated request. Its aim was to help restore their old church, not to draw them away from it.
  • The Old Catholic Churches — Utrecht to the Union Documented — Döllinger called a meeting at Bonn in 1874 to work for church unity. English members came, among them Bishop Harold Browne of Winchester and Dean Howson of Chester. When they met again in 1875, Howson spoke for some evangelical Anglicans on one point about the Lord's Supper.
