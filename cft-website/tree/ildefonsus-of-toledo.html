The Early Medieval Era · bishop c. 659-669 · Agali, Toledo

Ildefonsus of Toledo

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Nicene Visigothic Iberia.

Known for
Monk and abbot of Agali, then bishop of Toledo in Visigothic Spain. He wrote On the Virginity of Holy Mary and carried on the list of famous church writers begun by Jerome.
Also called
Hildefonsus
Dates
Made bishop in the ninth year of King Reccesvinth and bishop for nine years and about two months, Julian of Toledo says. He died on the tenth day before the Kalends of February, which is 23 January, after the eighteenth year of the king. Nicolás Antonio, in his Bibliotheca Hispana vetus, reckons these as 659 and 669. An abbot Ildefonsus signed the eighth and ninth councils of Toledo. The acts date them to the Spanish eras 691 and 693, that is 653 and 655
Place
The monastery of Agali, which Nicolás Antonio and a thirteenth-century Life call the monastery of Saints Cosmas and Damian, near Toledo. Toledo, where he was bishop and was buried in the church of Saint Leocadia
Role
Abbot; bishop of Toledo

About This Person

The boy Ildefonsus had run away to the monastery of Agali to become a monk. His father came after him in a fury. Only a single wall stood between them. The father pushed on into the inner rooms and passed the place where the boy was hiding. Then he attacked the monastery, sword in hand, found nothing, and went home to mourn a lost son.

Ildefonsus became bishop of Toledo, in Visigothic Spain, in the seventh century. The story comes from Julian of Toledo, who lived in his time and later became bishop of the city. Julian's account is a short tribute to him. Julian says the boy came back to Agali once his father had given up, and lived there as a monk for many years. He built a convent for women and endowed it from his own wealth. Then he became abbot of Agali. An abbot Ildefonsus signed the acts of two councils of Toledo in the 650s.

The king's force brought him back to Toledo, Julian says, and he became bishop after Eugenius the second. He was made bishop in the ninth year of King Reccesvinth and held the office for nine years and about two months. He died on 23 January and was buried in the church of Saint Leocadia, at the feet of the bishop before him.

Julian lists many books. Among them is On the Virginity of Holy Mary, written, Julian says, against three unbelievers. Ildefonsus also carried on the list of famous church writers that Jerome began and Isidore of Seville continued. He wrote it, he says, so that silence would not hide the memory of the great men of his church.

A Life written later by Cixila, a bishop of Toledo under Muslim rule, adds a wonder. Leocadia, the virgin saint of the city, rose from her tomb before the king and the people. She is said to have thanked God that her Lady lived through the life of Ildefonsus.

Key Works

  • On the Virginity of Holy Mary: written, Julian of Toledo says, against three unbelievers. Nicolás Antonio names them as Jovinian, Helvidius and a Jew
  • On Famous Men: a list of church writers that carries on the lists of Jerome, Gennadius and Isidore of Seville. He wrote it, he says, so that silence would not hide the memory of his see's great men
  • On the Knowledge of Baptism and On the Progress through the Spiritual Desert: two books Julian names in his list, with the Prosopopoeia of his own weakness, letters, Masses, hymns and sermons

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Julian of Toledo, a later bishop of the city, added a chapter on him to Ildefonsus's own On Famous Men.
  • Cixila, a later bishop of Toledo under Muslim rule, wrote a Life of him. He says Ildefonsus composed Masses, among them one to be sung in Mary's praise on her feast, which he counts as the seventh.

How they're remembered

  • Cixila, a bishop of Toledo under Muslim rule, wrote a later Life. In it Saint Leocadia rises from her tomb before King Reccesvinth. She is said to have thanked God that her Lady lived through the life of Ildefonsus.
  • Cixila says the king offered his own knife to cut the saint's veil. Ildefonsus cut it, and kept the knife with the relics in silver cases.
  • Antonio says his memory is kept in the Roman Martyrology.

What Is Uncertain

  • His life rests on Julian of Toledo's short tribute to him. Cixila's Life, written later under Muslim rule, adds wonders that Julian does not tell.
  • His birth and family. Report has it, Nicolás Antonio says, that he was born at Toledo in 607, to noble parents named Stephen and Lucia. Lucia is said to have been the sister of Eugenius, the bishop before him.
  • His death day. Julian gives the tenth day before the Kalends of February, 23 January, as does the Mozarabic calendar in the Missale mixtum. Nicolás Antonio quotes Julian's day, yet gives 25 January, the day he says the Roman Martyrology keeps his memory.
  • Whether he is the abbot Ildefonsus who signed the councils of 653 and 655. The acts give only the name and the title abbot. Nicolás Antonio takes the signatures as his, and puts the ninth council about 657.
  • Which of his books survive. Julian lists more than the volume prints. Nicolás Antonio counts several as lost, and a note to his notice counts one as lost or doubtful.
