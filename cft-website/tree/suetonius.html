About This Person

In his life of the emperor Claudius, the Roman writer Suetonius gives one short line about trouble at Rome. In John Rolfe's translation it reads, "Since the Jews constantly made disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus," Claudius "expelled them from Rome." Suetonius says nothing more about who Chrestus was.

Gaius Suetonius Tranquillus wrote the Lives of the Caesars, from Julius Caesar to Domitian. Rolfe notes that he tells us little about himself. His father was a Roman knight who fought for Otho at Betriacum, the battle that gave the empire to Vitellius. The letters of his friend Pliny the Younger show him practicing law for a time. Later he served the emperor Hadrian as his secretary, until Hadrian dismissed him, about the year 121.

His second notice comes in the life of Nero. It sits in a list of public measures, between a limit on spending and a ban on the revels of the charioteers. There he writes that Nero "inflicted punishments on the Christians, a sort of people who held a new and impious superstition." That is the translation of Alexander Thomson, revised by T. Forester. Suetonius blames Nero for the great fire elsewhere in the same life, but he does not link the Christians to it.

Rolfe's note on the line calls Chrestus "Another form of Christus." He thinks Suetonius probably got the date wrong, though he allows that a Jew of that name may be meant. The notes in Thomson and Forester's edition also read it as Christ. They say Suetonius moved Christ from Jerusalem to Rome, and into the reign of Claudius. Their translation says "one Chrestus." Rolfe notes that the Latin has no word for "one" here, and takes that as a sign that Christ is meant.

Rolfe says that Tertullian of Carthage drew on his work about the public shows. Paulinus, secretary to Ambrose of Milan, followed Suetonius's way of writing a life.

Legacy

How they're remembered

  • John Rolfe, his translator, says Tertullian of Carthage drew on Suetonius's work about the public shows. Rolfe adds that the influence of Suetonius is seen in the form of the Life of Ambrose by Paulinus, Ambrose's secretary.

What Is Uncertain

  • Rolfe and the notes to Thomson and Forester's edition both read "Chrestus" as Christ. Rolfe calls it another form of Christus and thinks Suetonius probably got the date wrong, though a Jew of that name may be meant. The Thomson and Forester notes say Suetonius moved Christ to Rome and into the reign of Claudius.
  • Thomson and Forester translate "one Chrestus." Rolfe notes that the Latin has no word for "one" here, and takes that as a sign that Christ is meant.
  • The Thomson and Forester notes cite Luke, in Acts 18:2, for the expulsion of the Jews by Claudius. They add that Dio Cassius says he only forbade their meetings.
  • Rolfe reports two dates for his birth: 77, from Mommsen, and 69, from Macé, which Rolfe thinks somewhat more probable. The Thomson and Forester preface puts it late in the reign of Vespasian.
  • Suetonius blames Nero for the great fire, but his notice of the Christians does not mention it.
