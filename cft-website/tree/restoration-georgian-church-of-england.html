The Enlightenment & Awakening Era · 1660-1815 · England, Wales, Ireland; the colonies

England · Wales, today in United Kingdom and beyond

The Restoration and Georgian Church of England

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

When the monarchy came back in 1660 so did the bishops and the Prayer Book, and the Act of Uniformity of 1662 required every minister to accept both. About two thousand refused and were ejected from their livings on a single day, creating English Dissent as a permanent fact. The church that remained was the established church of the long eighteenth century, and it has had a poor press — pluralism, absentee clergy, comfortable sermons. But it was also the church of the Prayer Book as ordinary people actually knew it, of parish registers and charity schools, and of the first Anglican missionary societies, the SPCK in 1698 and the SPG in 1701. It had its own principled casualties. The nonjurors, several hundred clergy including the Archbishop of Canterbury, who would not swear allegiance to William and Mary in 1689 while James II lived, and walked out rather than break an oath. And it was the church John and Charles Wesley were born in, ordained in, and never meant to leave.

In Its Own Record

Gave Rise To

  • The Methodist Revival Documented Major — Methodism began as societies of people inside the Church of England, gathered by John Wesley. In 1766 the preachers' conference said, 'we are not Dissenters from the Church now'. In 1785 Wesley recalled how these 'little companies' had spread through the three kingdoms.
  • Early American Unitarians & Universalists Documented Major — King's Chapel in Boston was a Church of England congregation. In 1785, 'torn from that King and Church', it printed its own prayer book. It took most of the text from the Book of Common Prayer. But it left out the Gloria Patri, the old praise of Father, Son and Spirit, and it prayed to God the Father alone, through Christ.
  • English Unitarianism Documented Major — Theophilus Lindsey was a priest of the Church of England at Catterick. Priestley says he grew uneasy and thought of leaving. In 1774 Lindsey opened a chapel in London. He called it a separate church, apart from the national church.

Continues

  • The English Reformation & Elizabethan Settlement Documented Major — The Act of Uniformity of 1662 begins by recalling the Prayer Book that Parliament made the law in Elizabeth's first year. It put that order back in place after what it called 'the late unhappy Troubles.'

Continued As

  • The Victorian Church of England Documented Major — The 1662 Prayer Book has a sermon or a homily follow the Creed at communion. In 1859 an Oxford editor said this rule stood 'nearly the same as we have it at the present day.' The same book and church carried on into the 1800s.

Influenced

  • The Oxford Movement & Anglo-Catholic Revival Dominant Modern Reconstruction — The Oxford Movement drew on an older high-church tradition in the Church of England. The historian Peter Nockles shows that the Oxford men both carried that tradition on and broke with parts of it. This rests on his modern study.

Argued Against

  • The Baptists Documented Major — John Bunyan of Bedford was charged before the justices. They said he stayed away from the parish church and held unlawful meetings. An Act of Parliament in 1664 banned any religious meeting of more than four people outside Church of England ways. A Baptist historian wrote in 1909 that for three years the General Baptists could hold no open assembly.Lasting result: A law of 1664 banned any religious meeting of more than four people that did not follow the ways of the Church of England.

Broke With

  • English Old Dissent — Presbyterians and Congregationalists Documented Major — The Act of Uniformity of 1662 set a deadline: St Bartholomew's Day. A minister who had not accepted the Prayer Book and a bishop's ordination by then would lose his post. Richard Baxter wrote that when the day came, about 1,800 or 2,000 ministers were cast out.
  • The Methodist Revival Widely Accepted Major — In 1785 John Wesley wrote that he had varied from the Church of England's discipline, though not from its doctrines, and only 'by constraint'. He had preached in the fields because the churches were shut to him. If anyone called this separating from the Church, he wrote, 'he may'. In 1795 the Methodist Conference let a chapel hold the Lord's Supper if its trustees, its leaders and the Conference agreed. It was a long step toward a separate church.
  • The Welsh Revival Tradition Documented Major — Many Welsh Calvinistic Methodists did not want their children baptized in the Church of England. So in 1811 the movement ordained its own preachers to give the sacraments. It ordained eight in North Wales in June and thirteen in the South in August.
