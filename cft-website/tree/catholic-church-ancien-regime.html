The Catholic Church of the Ancien Régime

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

This was the Catholic Church at the height of its establishment and on the edge of its worst crisis: enormously wealthy, woven into the monarchies of Europe, and increasingly unable to control them. Rome's condemnation of Jansenism in the bull Unigenitus (1713) turned into a fifty-year political crisis in France, with parlements and bishops fighting over it. Then the Catholic monarchies turned on the church's most effective order, expelling the Jesuits from Portugal, France, Spain and Naples and forcing the pope to suppress the Society altogether in 1773. Then came the Revolution: church property nationalized, clergy required to swear to a Civil Constitution that split them into jurors and refractories, thousands killed or driven abroad, and Pope Pius VI carried off to die a French prisoner in 1799. His successor signed a concordat with Napoleon in 1801, was imprisoned by him in turn, outlived him, and restored the Jesuits in 1814.

Rome ran its missions through Propaganda, the congregation for spreading the faith. Its collected decrees show three acts in these years.

In 1656 the Holy Office ruled on a set of questions. Missionaries in China had sent them to Propaganda in 1645. Chinese Christians could keep certain rites, it said. The cult, it said, seemed merely civil and political.

In 1659 Propaganda sent orders to its vicars apostolic. They were not to urge any people to change its rites, customs and ways. What is more absurd, it asked, than to carry France, Spain or Italy into China (in a plain English rendering)?

In 1726 Propaganda ruled that native priests must swear an oath too. This covered China, Tonkin and Cochinchina. The oath was the one set by the constitution Ex illa die.

In Its Own Record

Continues

  • The Tridentine Church Documented Major — In 1699 the bishops of the Paris province met to accept a ruling by Pope Innocent XII. Their archbishop said the church of France had received 'the faith of the Council of Trent' without exception. Trent's rules for church order, he added, were not yet received everywhere there.

Continued As

  • The Catholic Church of the Long Nineteenth Century Documented Major — Pope Pius VII made a concordat with France in 1801 that let the Catholic religion be practiced freely there. In 1817 the same pope signed a concordat with Bavaria. So one pope led the same church in both years.

Influenced By

  • The Latin Papal Church (Avignon to Fifth Lateran) Documented — The French clergy appealed to the Council of Constance in their Articles, which Rome turned down in 1682. They held that its decrees on the power of general councils, made in its fourth and fifth sessions, still stand firm. A papal decree condemned the Articles in 1690.

Argued Against

  • Port-Royal Jansenism Documented Major — In May 1653 Rome condemned five propositions said to come from Jansen's book Augustinus. In 1664 it made a form to be sworn, rejecting them in the sense Jansen meant. Later a papal bull was obtained to close the convent of Port-Royal itself.Lasting result: In 1653 Rome condemned five points from Jansen's book, and in 1664 it made people swear to a form that rejected them.
  • Quietist Controversy (Molinos, Guyon) Documented Major — In 1687 Rome condemned errors drawn from the writings of Miguel de Molinos. In France, Bossuet tried in person to show Fénelon the errors in Guyon's books. In 1699 Rome condemned twenty-three propositions from Fénelon's Maxims of the Saints, and Fénelon condemned them himself in a pastoral letter.Lasting result: In 1687 Rome condemned errors drawn from Molinos, and in 1699 it condemned 23 claims from a book by Fénelon.
  • Armenian Apostolic Church — Etchmiadzin and Constantinople Documented — Jesuit missionaries wrote that the Armenians' chief error was to confess only one nature in Christ. They called it the root of their schism. Missionaries from Rome, led by Father Clement Galano, worked in the sultan's capital to win Armenians to their side.
  • The Society of Jesus, 1650 to 1814 Documented Major — In 1773 Pope Clement XIV closed the Society of Jesus by his own power as pope. His brief says the kings of France, Spain, Portugal and Sicily had already driven its members out. In 1814 Pius VII restored the Society to all states.Lasting result: In 1773 Clement XIV closed the Society after four kings had expelled it. Pius VII restored it in 1814.

Opposed By

  • Armenian Apostolic Church — Etchmiadzin and Constantinople Documented — Before he became head of the church, Alexander of Julfa wrote a book against the pope's power and the Council of Chalcedon. Once elected, he sent the pope a respectful letter in the nation's name. Yet a Jesuit report of the time says he waged a secret war on the Catholics. Later Simeon of Erevan, who held the same seat, wrote a book that poked fun at what Rome taught.

Broke With

  • The Church of Utrecht Documented Major — In 1703 Rome told the Dutch church's vicars-general to stop all their spiritual work, or be cut off. A history printed with Utrecht's synod of 1763 dates the split from these acts. In 1725 Barchman Wuytiers was made archbishop. A papal letter then declared him and his clergy cut off from the church.

In Contact With

  • Armenian Christianity — the Mekhitarists and the Catholic Patriarchate Documented Major — In 1742 Pope Benedict XIV wrote to Abbot Mekhitar in Venice to thank him for a printed Armenian Bible. In the same year, says the Armenian church leader Malachia Ormanian, the pope gave the Armenians in union with Rome a church of their own. Its patriarch was placed under Rome.Lasting result: In 1742 Pope Benedict XIV gave the Armenians in union with Rome a church of their own, with a patriarch placed under Rome.
  • The Melkite Greek Catholic Split Documented Major — In 1729 Rome's mission council ruled that nothing stood in the way of Cyril's consecration as Greek patriarch of Antioch. It set a condition: he must swear to change none of the Greek rites without Rome's consent. In 1730 the pope's envoy, the Capuchin Dorotheus, confirmed him.Lasting result: In 1729 Rome said Cyril could be patriarch if he swore to change no Greek rites without its leave. Its envoy confirmed him in 1730.
  • The St Thomas Christians — Two Obediences Documented — The community's own history says the pope sent a Carmelite bishop. He was later called the Bishop of Verapoly. From then on, it says, the pope sent bishops there regularly.
  • The Church of the East — Three Patriarchates Widely Accepted Major — In 1681 Pope Innocent XI made Joseph the first Catholic patriarch of the Chaldeans at Diyarbakır. In 1782 Pope Pius VI let Hormizd act as patriarch of the plain line, though not use the patriarchal seal. Badger and Labourt tell it from opposite sides.Lasting result: In 1681 Pope Innocent XI named Joseph patriarch of the Chaldeans at Diyarbakır. That made a third patriarchal line beside the two of the Church of the East.
  • Chinese Catholics Under Edict and Treaty Documented Major — In 1656 the Holy Office said Chinese Christians could keep certain rites. In 1726 Propaganda ruled on the native priests of China, Tonkin and Cochinchina. Rome ruled on the church of China in both years.Lasting result: In 1726 Rome ruled that Chinese priests must swear an oath. So must those of Tonkin and Cochinchina. The constitution Ex illa die set the oath.
