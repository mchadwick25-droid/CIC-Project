Jonas of Bobbio

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Insular / Irish Monastic Christianity.

Also called Ionas

Monk of Bobbio who wrote the Life of Columbanus, the Irish founder of Luxeuil and Bobbio. Later, as an abbot, he wrote the Life of John of Réomé.

About This Person

For three years, Jonas of Bobbio wrote, he was carried along the shores of the Ocean. He sailed the river Scheldt, and the slow marsh of the Elnone often soaked his feet. He had gone to help Bishop Amand, who was curbing the old errors of the people there with the blade of the Gospel.

Jonas says so in the preface to his Life of Columbanus, the Irish monk who founded Luxeuil in Gaul and Bobbio in Italy. Jonas was a monk of Bobbio in the seventh century. His translator, Dana Munro, says he entered it three years after Columbanus died. Some three years before the preface, Jonas had promised at Abbot Bertulf's command to write the saint's deeds. Many who had seen them were still alive, Jonas says, and he learned more from Attala and Eustasius, the abbots who followed Columbanus.

It is a saint's Life, and its miracles are many, as Munro notes. Bruno Krusch, an editor of the Frankish saints' Lives, dates it to about 640. Jonas often names the man who told him a story. "Theudegisil himself told us of this", he writes of one wonder, in Munro's English.

In the third year of King Clothar, which Krusch reckons as 659, Jonas was an abbot himself. On a journey ordered by the king and his mother, Queen Balthild, he rested a few days at the monastery of Réomé. Its monks begged him to write down what the disciples of their founder, John, and those after them had passed on, and he did so. Krusch says two forgers of the Carolingian age later reworked the book, and one claimed to have lived before Jonas.

Krusch believes Jonas also wrote the older Life of Vedast, bishop of Arras, since its style and one of its wonders match his.

If readers found him praising someone still living, Jonas wrote, they should not call him a flatterer. He meant only to commend to memory what deserved it.

Timeline

Dates
Entered Bobbio three years after Columbanus's death, Dana Munro says. Wrote the Life of Columbanus about 640, by Bruno Krusch's date, before 642. An abbot in the third year of King Clothar, which Krusch reckons as 659. No held source gives his birth year or his death
Place
Born at Susa in Italy, at the foot of the Alps, say the Acta Sanctorum editors G. Henschenius and D. Papebrochius. A monk of Bobbio; with Bishop Amand on the Scheldt and the marsh of the Elnone; at Réomé, on his way to Chalon
Role
Monk and abbot; hagiographer

Key Works

  • Life of Columbanus: written at Abbot Bertulf's command, from the stories of the saint's companions and of the abbots Attala and Eustasius. Its preface is addressed to the abbots Waldebert and Bobolenus
  • Life of John of Réomé: written at the request of the monks of Réomé, during a journey ordered by King Clothar and Queen Balthild. Bruno Krusch says two forgers of the Carolingian age reworked it
  • Life of Eustasius: the Life of Columbanus's pupil and abbot of Luxeuil. Giovanni Domenico Mansi quotes its account of the council of Mâcon

Legacy and Today

What traces to them

  • Bruno Krusch says the later Lives of John of Réomé were built on Jonas's book. One forger claimed to have lived before Jonas. Another claimed he wrote from men who had often seen John.
  • Krusch finds a prologue like Jonas's, and a phrase Jonas often used, in a Life of Bibianus of Saintes. He places its language in the Carolingian age.

How they're remembered

  • Dana Munro, his translator, says almost all accounts of the Irish missionaries are lost, so this Life of Columbanus is of great value.
  • Munro holds that the Life reflects the companions' feelings faithfully and often keeps the saint's own words. He adds that, as in such Lives, its miracles are many.
  • Laurentius Surius, in a heading Vincentius Barralis prints, calls Jonas an abbot and a contemporary of Columbanus.

Open Questions

  • Whether he wrote the older Life of Vedast, bishop of Arras. Krusch believes so, from its style and a wonder told in nearly the same words. He says Louis Duchesne agreed, and later G. Kurth. The Life itself names no author.
  • His own life is known mostly from his prefaces and the opening of his Life of John. Dana Munro, Bruno Krusch and the Acta Sanctorum editors add a little.
  • His origin. The Acta Sanctorum editors say he was Italian, not Irish, and born at Susa. They rest this on their Life of Attala, which is not held.
  • Whether he was abbot of Bobbio. The Acta Sanctorum editors list him after Bobolenus among its abbots, with a perhaps. The Réomé opening calls him abbot but names no house.
  • Munro's translation leaves out the preface. Its Latin is held only in a hard-to-read copy of Vincentius Barralis's book of 1613.
