Kerala, today in India and beyond

The Malankara Churches — Orthodox & Jacobite

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

In 1912 Patriarch Abdul Masih II consecrated a Catholicos of the East for Malankara, and the ancient St Thomas community in Kerala divided into two: an autocephalous party, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, and a patriarchal party, the Jacobite Syrian Christian Church, which held to the Syriac Orthodox patriarchate of Antioch. A constitution followed in 1934. A Supreme Court ruling in 1958 brought a reunion that held for a generation; in 1975 the patriarch deposed the Catholicos and the split reopened. A further Supreme Court ruling came in 1995. This is a century of ecclesiology argued in civil courts — over parishes, properties and endowments as much as over jurisdiction — and both parties wrote the record, since both were litigants throughout.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • St. Thomas Christians under Portuguese Pressure - Diamper & the Coonan Cross Widely Accepted Major — In 1869 Edavalikel Philipos, a priest at Kottayam, wrote that those who left Rome under a bishop from Antioch were called the New Party. The Malankara Orthodox Church traces its own start to that break with Rome.

Broke With

  • Northeast India Revivals & the Mar Thoma Reform Widely Accepted Major — In 1847 the Patriarch of Antioch repeated his predecessor's curse on Mathew Mar Athanasius, the reforming bishop in Kerala. He ordered that 'none shall pray with him'. A rival bishop had already come from Antioch to check the reform. Then a royal court verdict in 1889 left the two sides as separate churches.

In Contact With

  • The Sayfo & the Syriac Diaspora Widely Accepted Major — The Malankara Orthodox Church's own history says that in 1912 Patriarch Abdul Messiah II made Baselios Paulose I its head. The church then split, as one side backed the new head and the other the patriarch. In 1975 a later patriarch excommunicated the head of the church, and it split again.Lasting result: In 1975 the patriarch of Antioch cut the head of the Malankara church off from communion, and the church split in two again.
