The High Medieval Era · 1099-1291 · Jerusalem, Antioch, Acre, Cyprus

Jerusalem · Antioch, today in Turkey and beyond

The Latin Church of Outremer

About This Movement

When the First Crusade took Jerusalem in 1099, the conquerors did not simply add a diocese. They built a whole Latin church on top of an older Christian landscape. They set up a Latin patriarch in Jerusalem, and then another at Antioch. There the Greek patriarch soon left for Constantinople. For nearly two centuries this transplanted church ran parishes and cathedrals. It also served a huge flow of pilgrims to the holy places. The canons of the Holy Sepulchre stood at its center, with a liturgy shaped by processions to the sites themselves. Most of the people it ruled were not Latin. They were Greek, Syriac, Armenian and Muslim. Its dealings with them ran from workable peace to plain dispossession. When Jerusalem was lost in 1187 the patriarchate moved to Acre. When Acre fell in 1291, the church of Outremer ended with it.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • The Latin Papal Church (Gregorian Reform to Innocent III) Widely Accepted Major — At Clermont in 1095 Pope Urban II decreed that the journey to free the church of Jerusalem would count in place of all penance. After the crusade, Latin clergy filled the new sees in the East. The first Latin patriarch of Antioch had come as chaplain to the bishop of Le Puy. The see of Jerusalem went to the archbishop of Pisa.

Influenced

  • The Carmelites Documented Major — The Carmelites' rule opens as a letter from 'Albert, by the grace of God patriarch of Jerusalem'. He wrote to Brocard and the other hermits who lived 'by the well of Elias on Mount Carmel'. Albert was the Latin patriarch of the crusader kingdom. The editor of Teresa's Foundations says he was killed in 1214.Lasting result: The hermits on Mount Carmel got their rule from Albert, the patriarch of Jerusalem. It was written as a letter to them.

Argued Against

  • The Byzantine Church after the Schism Documented — Michael the Syrian, a Syriac patriarch who lived through these events, tells how the prince of Antioch brought back a Greek patriarch, Athanasius, from Constantinople. The Latin patriarch, Aimery, left the city and cut its people off from the church. After an earthquake in 1170, Aimery told them to throw the Greek patriarch out 'with contempt' as an intruder.

In Contact With

  • Armenian Christianity in Cilicia Documented — Kirakos of Gandzak wrote a history of Armenia in the 1200s. He tells how Catholicos Gregory III went on pilgrimage. At Antioch the people led him to the throne of Saint Peter. In Jerusalem, Kirakos says, the Franks and their patriarch made their bond with the Armenians stronger than ever.
  • Maronite Christianity Dominant Modern Reconstruction — William of Tyre was archbishop of Tyre in the crusader kingdom. He wrote that the Maronites joined the Latin church around 1182. This rests on modern historians of the Maronites, not on his own text.
