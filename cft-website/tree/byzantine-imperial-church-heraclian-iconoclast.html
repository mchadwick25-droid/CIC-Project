The Early Medieval Era · 622-843 · Constantinople, Anatolia, the Greek East

Constantinople · Anatolia, today in Turkey and beyond

The Byzantine Imperial Church (Heraclian & Iconoclast Eras)

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

This is the imperial church of the empire's worst century and a half. Heraclius recovered the True Cross from Persia in 630 and within a decade had lost Syria, Palestine and Egypt to the Arab armies. His government's attempt to hold the remaining eastern Christians together with a compromise formula — that Christ had two natures but only one will — was pushed as state policy and resisted by a monk, Maximus, who was tried, had his tongue and right hand cut off, and died in exile. Twenty years later a council at Constantinople condemned the formula and vindicated him. Then came iconoclasm: emperors from Leo III onward removed and destroyed religious images, a council of 338 bishops endorsed the policy in 754, the Council of Nicaea reversed it in 787, a second imperial iconoclasm followed, and only in 843 was the matter closed for good. Through all of it the same institution — palace, patriarchate, councils — kept running, which is why this era looks so different depending on whether you stand with the court or with the monasteries.

In Its Own Record

Continues

  • The Byzantine Imperial Church (Justinianic) Documented Major — The patriarchate of Constantinople ran on through the reign of Heraclius. Sergius was its patriarch when Heraclius was crowned in 610. The council of 681 names Sergius, and after him Pyrrhus, Paul and Peter, 'who died bishops of this God-preserved city'.

Continued As

  • The Byzantine Imperial Church (Macedonian Era) Documented Major — The see of Constantinople ran on through 843 with no break. A later Byzantine list of its patriarchs names John, an iconoclast whom the empress Theodora threw out. Next it names Methodius, a monk. Photius, patriarch from 858, wrote that at the council of 787 the iconoclasts were defeated.

Influenced By

  • The Anatolian Church in the Third Century Documented — In 692 the Council in Trullo met at Constantinople. It set its seal on a list of older church canons. The list takes in the canons of Gregory the Wonderworker, bishop of Neocaesarea.

Argued Against

  • Iconophile Byzantine Monasticism Documented Major — In 754 a council called by the emperor Constantine V banned holy images. At its close the bishops cursed John of Damascus as a teacher of impiety, calling him by his family name, Mansur. Later historians write that the emperor then pressed the monks to obey. Many fled, they say, and some were killed.Lasting result: In 754 a council against icons said John of Damascus was deposed and cursed. It said the same of George and of Germanus of Constantinople.
  • Paulicians Dominant Modern Reconstruction — Franjo Rački, a Croatian historian writing in 1869, tells of a persecution of the Paulicians under the emperor Michael I, from 811 to 813. He says it failed to root them out. This rests on his history alone.
  • Melkite Arabic Christianity Documented — In 754 a council of bishops at Constantinople backed the emperor's ban on icons and cursed John of Damascus. It used his family name, Mansur. Its closing cries damn him as a man 'who has an evil name and Saracen opinions'.

Opposed By

  • Latin African Christianity under Byzantium Documented — In 646 the bishops of Numidia, Byzacena and Mauretania wrote to Pope Theodore. They wrote against a 'novelty' that was spreading from Constantinople. Pope Martin told the Roman synod of 649 that they had also turned to the emperor. They made their case to him against its defender, Paul, the patriarch of that city.
  • Iconophile Byzantine Monasticism Widely Accepted Major — John of Damascus answered those who "find fault with us" for honoring images, and the iconoclast council of 754 cursed his teaching. Monks also sat at the council of 787 that reversed it. Harnack wrote that the resistance came from the ranks of the monks.Lasting result: At the Second Council of Nicaea, bishops who had been against images read out confessions that venerated them. The synod then took them back.
