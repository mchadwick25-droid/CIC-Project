The Late Medieval Era · c. 1300-1400 · Rhineland, Switzerland

Rhineland, today in Germany · Switzerland

Rhineland Mysticism & the Friends of God

About This Movement

Along the Rhine, a generation of Dominican preachers began to preach in German. Meister Eckhart, Johannes Tauler and Henry Suso spoke to convents of nuns and to devout lay people. To do it, they had to invent words for the inner life that German did not yet have. Eckhart pushed furthest. He spoke of the ground of the soul, and of a birth of God within it. His words got him put on trial for heresy. He died before the case closed. In 1329 a papal bull condemned twenty-eight articles drawn from his work, and that is how his memory reached everyone who came after. Around Strasbourg and Basel, a mixed circle of clergy and lay people called themselves Gottesfreunde, the Friends of God. They carried the same piety outside the cloister. Out of that circle came the Theologia Deutsch, a book whose author is unknown. Much of what survives does so because women kept it. Nuns took sermons down as they were preached. Convents wrote sister-books about the lives of their own communities. And Elsbeth Stagel worked with Suso on the book of his own life.

In Its Own Record

Grew Out Of

  • The Dominican Order Documented Major — In 1326 Meister Eckhart answered his accusers as Brother Eckhart of the Order of Preachers. Henry Suso's Life names his spiritual daughter, Elsbeth Stagel, as a Dominican nun at Töss. She drew his teaching out of him and wrote it down.

Influenced

  • Lutheran Wittenberg & Its Congregations Documented — Martin Luther printed the Theologia Deutsch at Wittenberg, part of it in 1516 and a fuller text in 1518. In his preface he wrote that, next to the Bible and Augustine, no book had taught him more of God, Christ and man. In 1521 he cited Tauler on purgatory.
  • Devotio Moderna / Brethren of the Common Life Documented — Johannes Busch kept the chronicle of the Windesheim canons. He tells how their prior, at the council of Constance, gladly visited the cell of the friar who wrote the Clock of Eternal Wisdom. The chronicle's 1886 editor names that friar as Henry Suso. A 1902 editor of Thomas a Kempis lists Suso among the writers Thomas took sayings from.

Opposed By

  • The Latin Papal Church (Avignon to Fifth Lateran) Documented Major — In 1326 Meister Eckhart answered charges of heresy at Cologne. On 27 March 1329 Pope John XXII condemned twenty-eight of his articles in a bull, In agro dominico. Seventeen were called heresy. By then Eckhart was dead, and he had taken back any errors put before the pope.Lasting result: In 1329 Pope John XXII condemned twenty-eight articles from Eckhart's teaching, and any of his books that held them.

Sources

  • Meister Eckhart, The Essential Sermons, Commentaries, Treatises and Defense (trans. Colledge & McGinn, Classics of Western Spirituality, 1981) (primary) — Latin and German works, with his trial Defense. The papal bull In agro dominico (1329) condemned 28 of his propositions, taken out of context, after his death - an institutional condemnation that has shaped how his legacy has been read ever since.
  • Johannes Tauler, Sermons (trans. Shrady, CWS, 1985) and Henry Suso, The Exemplar (trans. Tobin, CWS, 1989) (primary) — Tauler preaching to Dominican nuns; Suso's Life of the Servant, co-produced with Elsbeth Stagel - a man's interior life reaching us through a woman's editorial hand. These sermons survive as listeners' transcripts and convent copies, not the preacher's own manuscript.
  • The Dominican convent Sister-Books (Schwesternbuecher: Toess, Unterlinden, Engelthal, St Katharinental and kin); study-with-translations: Gertrude Jaron Lewis, By Women, for Women, about Women (PIMS, 1996) (primary) — Nuns writing their own communities' graces, austerities, and deaths - collective women's memory in its own genre. English translations exist only piecemeal.
  • Theologia Deutsch (trans. Bengt Hoffman, The Theologia Germanica of Martin Luther, CWS, 1980) (primary) — The anonymous Frankfurt priest's treatise from the Friends of God milieu. The standard English translation is made from Luther's own 1518 edition, so the Luther connection is built into how the text reaches English readers at all. Rulman Merswin's Friends-of-God literature stands behind it.
  • Bernard McGinn, The Harvest of Mysticism in Medieval Germany (Presence of God vol. 4, 2005) (secondary) — Current standard on exactly this ecosystem, Eckhart through the Sister-Books.
  • Cambridge History of Christianity vol. 4, ch. 19 'Mysticism and transcendence' (Hollywood) (reference)
