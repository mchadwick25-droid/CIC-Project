Hilary of Arles

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Gallic Monastic-Ascetic Christianity.

Known for
A monk of Lérins who followed Honoratus as bishop of Arles, wrote the sermon on Honoratus's life, and clashed with Leo of Rome over the bishops of Gaul.

About This Person

Celidonius, a bishop removed from office in Gaul, went to Rome and said he had been condemned unjustly. Hilary, bishop of Arles, heard of it and set out after him on foot. He hurried into Rome without horse or pack animal, says the Life of Hilary. He told Leo, bishop of Rome, that he had come to pay his respects, not to argue a case.

The Life takes Hilary's side, and it survives here only as a passage quoted in a collection of council records. The editors of that collection give it to Honoratus of Marseilles, or to whoever else wrote it.

Leo tells it the other way. Writing to the bishops of the province of Vienne, he says Celidonius proved he had been wrongly deposed. Hilary, with no good answer, poured out words "such as no layman could make and no priest listen to." He rushed through the provinces like a courier, Leo says, with soldiers behind him. Leo took from him his power over the province of Vienne. Leo was a party to the quarrel, and Charles Lett Feltoe, who translated his letters, calls it his "not very creditable encounter with Hilary."

Hilary preached a sermon on Honoratus of Arles, who had led him to the monks' island of Lérins. In it he says Honoratus came back to their homeland for his sake, and that he fought against him. Hilary later followed Honoratus as bishop of Arles. Honoratus, he says, sought in him a vein of faith, as of his own blood, and his editors take them for kinsmen.

As bishop, Gennadius of Marseilles says, he chose poverty and farmed beyond his strength. He was of noble birth and had been brought up very differently. By August 449 Leo was writing of "Hilary of holy memory."

In the sermon Hilary gave thanks for his own turning. Good Jesus, he prayed, you broke my chains, moved by the prayers of your servant Honoratus.

Key Works

  • Sermon on the Life of Saint Honoratus: his sermon on the life of Honoratus of Arles, founder of the community on Lérins; held in Latin

Legacy

What traces to them

  • His sermon is the source the Library holds for the life of Honoratus of Arles.
  • Gennadius of Marseilles calls the sermon a work "of so great practical value to many".

How they're remembered

  • Constantius of Lyon, in his Life of Germanus of Auxerre, calls him fiery in faith and a torrent of heavenly speech.
  • Leo of Rome, after his death: "Hilary of holy memory".
  • Charles Lett Feltoe calls Leo's quarrel with him Leo's "not very creditable encounter with Hilary".

What Is Uncertain

  • The quarrel with Leo of Rome is told by two partisan sides. The Life of Hilary takes his side and survives here only as a passage quoted in Mansi's collection of council records, whose editors leave its author open: Honoratus of Marseilles, or another. Leo was a party to the dispute.
  • Whether Celidonius was rightly deposed is disputed. The Life says the charge was confirmed by witnesses. Leo says Celidonius proved he had been wrongly deposed. Feltoe thinks Celidonius was probably bishop of Vienne or of Besançon.
  • That he and Honoratus were kinsmen is his editors' reading of his own words, that Honoratus sought in him a vein of faith, as of his own blood.
  • His birth is disputed among editors: Richardson gives about 401, while Jackson's death at forty-one in 449 implies a later year. Gennadius says he died under the emperors Valentinian and Marcian.
  • The years of the quarrel, about 444 or 445, are editors' dates.
