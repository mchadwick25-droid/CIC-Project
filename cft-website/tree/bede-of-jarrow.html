The Early Medieval Era · c. 673-735 · Jarrow, England

Jarrow · England, today in United Kingdom

Bede

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Anglo-Saxon Christianity.

Known for
Monk and priest of Wearmouth and Jarrow who wrote the Ecclesiastical History of the English nation.
Role
Monk, priest and historian

About This Person

Bede was born about 673 on land that belonged to the monastery at Wearmouth and Jarrow. At seven he was given to Abbot Benedict to be educated, and later to Ceolfrid. He says he spent the rest of his life in that monastery.

He became a deacon in his nineteenth year and a priest in his thirtieth. He wrote that he always took delight in learning, teaching and writing. His History was addressed to King Ceolwulf, who had asked to read it. He finished it about 731.

He died on Ascension Day in 735, aged sixty-two. His pupil Cuthbert described the scene at his deathbed. Bede had just finished translating the Gospel of John.

Key Works

  • Ecclesiastical History of the English Nation: five books on the English church, sent to King Ceolwulph
  • Life of Cuthbert: the monk and bishop, in verse and then in prose
  • On the Times: his book on reckoning time, where, he says, he proved Aidan's way of keeping Easter wrong

Legacy

What traces to them

  • About the year 800, Alcuin, in the books against Felix of Urgel that he sent to Charlemagne, cites blessed Bede the priest on the Acts of the Apostles and on the Song of Songs.
  • Much later an English Lollard tract still leans on Bede: the signs of miracles, it says with him and with Gregory the Great, were needed only at the church's beginning, as we water plants until they have taken root and then stop.

How they're remembered

  • Rome honors him. Its Breviary keeps Bede the Venerable on the twenty-seventh of May as Confessor and Doctor of the Church, and an English Roman Missal of 1843 calls on God, who enlightened his church by the learning of blessed Bede.

What Is Uncertain

  • Bede means to be fair but does not hide his side. He praises Aidan's peace, humility and care for the poor while rejecting his Easter, and even defends him: Aidan kept it on the Lord's day, for faith in the resurrection, not on the fourteenth moon like the Jews, as some falsely imagine. Of the Britons he writes that most of them, through innate hatred, oppose the English and the appointed Easter.
  • Bede's own account of his life stops in his fifty-ninth year, while he was still at work. The story of his last hours was written by his pupil Cuthbert.
