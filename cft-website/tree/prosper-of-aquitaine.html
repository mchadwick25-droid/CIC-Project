About This Person

Near the end of Augustine of Hippo's life, a man in Gaul named Prosper wrote to him in Latin. They had never met, he said, but had traded letters through a deacon, Leontius. Many servants of Christ in Marseilles, he wrote, thought Augustine's teaching on how God calls those he has chosen went against the Fathers.

Gennadius, a priest of Marseilles, calls Prosper a man of Aquitaine, "scholastic in style and vigorous in statement." No ancient source used here gives his city, birth or death, and later writers disagree on whether he was a layman. Edgar Gibson, who translated John Cassian, says he was. Theodor Mommsen, who edited his chronicle, thinks he was a cleric, since Prosper calls the deacon Leontius his brother.

Gibson dates the letter to 429. Prosper then wrote a long poem, On the Ungrateful, against those he saw as enemies of grace. After Augustine died, Gibson says, Prosper and a layman named Hilary appealed to Pope Celestine. This Hilary had also written to Augustine. Gibson keeps him apart from Hilary of Arles. Mommsen takes the letter's writer for Hilary of Arles, writing before he became bishop. They won a letter from the pope to the bishops of Gaul.

Prosper also wrote against Cassian, whom Gibson calls the leading priest of Marseilles. Gennadius thought an unsigned book against Cassian was Prosper's. Gennadius, who sided with Cassian, says Prosper "brands as injurious" works that the church finds helpful. Gibson calls the book powerful, "although not scrupulously fair."

He also kept a chronicle. One entry, which Mommsen's edition sets in 429, has Pope Celestine send Germanus, bishop of Auxerre, to win the Britons back from the Pelagians. Gennadius adds that Leo of Rome's letters against the monk Eutyches were thought to have been dictated by Prosper.

In the chronicle Prosper wrote of Augustine's death. Augustine died during the Vandal siege, he says, still answering Julian of Eclanum, and still defending the grace of Christ.

Key Works

  • Letter to Augustine: reports that many servants of Christ at Marseilles thought Augustine's teaching on God's calling of the chosen went against the Fathers; held in Latin among Augustine's letters
  • Chronicle (Epitoma Chronicon): a chronicle from the creation to his own day; its entries give the mission of Germanus of Auxerre to Britain, Palladius sent to the Irish, and the death of Augustine of Hippo
  • Book against Cassian: an unsigned book against works of John Cassian that Gennadius of Marseilles thought was Prosper's; not held

Legacy

What traces to them

  • His chronicle is the source the Library holds for Pope Celestine sending Germanus, bishop of Auxerre, to Britain, and for Palladius sent as first bishop to the Irish who believed in Christ.
  • Gennadius of Marseilles reports that letters of Leo of Rome against Eutyches were thought to have been dictated by him.

How they're remembered

  • Gennadius of Marseilles: "a man scholastic in style and vigorous in statement".
  • Photius of Constantinople, in his Library, calls him "truly a man of God".
  • Edgar Gibson, translator of John Cassian, on his book against Cassian: "a treatise of considerable power and force, although not scrupulously fair".

What Is Uncertain

  • Whether he was a layman is disputed. Edgar Gibson calls him "also a layman", and Garnier, editor of Marius Mercator, counts him among laymen. Theodor Mommsen thinks him a cleric when he wrote to Augustine, since he calls the deacon Leontius his brother. Mommsen also rejects the view that he was a bishop.
  • That the unsigned book against Cassian is Prosper's is the judgment of Gennadius of Marseilles, who sided with Cassian. The book is not held.
  • Gennadius says letters of Leo against Eutyches "are also thought" to have been dictated by him; some manuscripts read "said", as Richardson's note gives it. Mommsen says medieval writers made him a notary of the Roman church, and that Gennadius's account does not show he even left Gaul.
  • Gibson dates the letter to Augustine to 429 because it names Hilary as newly bishop of Arles. The appeal to Pope Celestine with a layman named Hilary is Gibson's account. Mommsen takes the Hilary who wrote with him for Hilary of Arles before he became bishop; Gibson and the Maurist note in Augustine's letters keep them apart.
  • No ancient source used here gives his birth, death or city. Gennadius gives his region, Aquitaine. The years 403 to 463 are Richardson's note to Gennadius and a note in J. H. Freese's translation of Photius; Mommsen says only that he lived at least to 455.
  • The chronicle's years are Mommsen's: Prosper dates by consuls.
