Liberius of Rome

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Imperial and Juridical Christianity.

Known for
Bishop of Rome who refused the emperor Constantius's demand to condemn Athanasius of Alexandria, was exiled to Beroea, and, by some accounts, gave way there.
Dates
Followed Julius as bishop of Rome, by Socrates of Constantinople and Theodoret of Cyrrhus. Died on 24 September, in a year named by its consuls in the account of Liberius and Felix; Chester Hartranft gives 366. The year 352 for his election is an editors' date, from the Liberian Catalogue as Louise Ropes Loomis reports it
Place
Rome; Milan, by Blomfield Jackson's note; Beroea in Thrace

About This Person

The emperor Constantius wanted Liberius, bishop of Rome, to condemn Athanasius of Alexandria. A record of their talk, which Theodoret of Cyrrhus copied into his Church History, gives his words. Asked why he stood alone, he said, "My standing alone does not make the truth a whit the weaker." Given three days to change his mind, he said, "Neither three days nor three months can change my sentiments. Send me wherever you please." He was banished to Beroea in Thrace. When the emperor sent him gold for the journey, he sent it back. The emperor, he said, needed it "to pay his troops."

Ammianus Marcellinus, a historian outside the church, tells the same refusal. He says Liberius was taken from Rome by night, for fear of the people, "among whom he was exceedingly popular." Theodoret says a deacon, Felix, was put in his place.

What happened in exile is disputed. Athanasius, his ally, says that after two years Liberius "gave way, and from fear of threatened death subscribed," and he blames those who forced him. Jerome says Liberius, worn out by exile, signed heresy and entered Rome as if he had won. Sozomen, a church historian of the next century, says he signed a document drawn up by Eastern bishops at Sirmium, with his own statement added. Theodoret tells of no lapse at all. Letters in which Liberius says he gave up Athanasius survive in Latin in a dossier gathered by Hilary of Poitiers. Feder, who edited the dossier, marks those letters as doubtful.

Theodoret says the noblewomen of Rome begged Constantius to bring their bishop back. The emperor proposed that Liberius and Felix share the church. The crowd cried, "One God, one Christ, one bishop." Liberius returned, and Felix withdrew.

Ambrose of Milan passed on the words Liberius spoke at Christmas in Saint Peter's, when Ambrose's sister made her profession as a virgin. "You," he said, "my daughter, have desired a good espousal."

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Theodoret of Cyrrhus copies into his Church History a record of his talk with Constantius, which pious men of the time kept to stir others to the same zeal.
  • Ambrose of Milan passes on, from his sister, the sermon he gave when she made her profession as a virgin.

How they're remembered

  • Theodoret of Cyrrhus: "This victorious champion of the truth".
  • Ammianus Marcellinus: the people of Rome, "among whom he was exceedingly popular".
  • Athanasius of Alexandria: he signed "from fear of threatened death", which shows "the hatred of Liberius against the heresy".

At Major Councils

  • Councils of Ariminum and Seleucia, 359: Not present or represented, Hefele says

What Is Uncertain

  • What happened in exile is disputed. Athanasius, his ally, says he signed after two years from fear of death, and blames those who forced him. Jerome says that, worn out by exile, he signed heresy and entered Rome as if he had won, and in his Lives of Illustrious Men blames Fortunatianus, bishop of Aquileia. Sozomen says he signed a document drawn up by Eastern bishops at Sirmium, with his own statement added. Theodoret tells no lapse.
  • The letters in which he says he did not defend Athanasius survive in Latin in the dossier of Hilary of Poitiers, who adds his own angry notes to them. Feder, who edited the dossier, marks those letters as doubtful.
  • Who put the deacon Felix in his place is told several ways. Socrates says the party of Ursacius; Jerome says Acacius of Caesarea; the Book of the Popes, which its translator Loomis calls a medley of fact and legend, says Liberius himself.
  • Why he was recalled is told several ways: a riot, by Socrates; a Western embassy, by Sozomen; the noblewomen of Rome, by Theodoret.
  • Theodoret copies the talk with Constantius from a record kept by others. The place, Milan, is Blomfield Jackson's note.
  • His years as bishop, 352 to 366, are editors' dates.
