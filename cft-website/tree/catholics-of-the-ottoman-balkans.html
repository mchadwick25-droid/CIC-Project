The Enlightenment & Awakening Era · 1650-1815 · Bosnia, Slavonia

Catholics of the Ottoman Balkans: the Bosnian Franciscans

About This Movement

The Catholics of Bosnia and the lands north of it were served by the friars of the Bosnian province. A calendar of their documents, in Latin, was printed at Zagreb in 1892. It is the main source for these years.

On 1 January 1689 the fathers of the Bosnian province met at Požega, in Slavonia. The calendar says they spoke in their own name and the people's. They declared that the bishops of Zagreb had never held jurisdiction in the Slavonian parts. The clergy and the Catholic people between the Sava and the Drava said that no bishop but the bishop of Bosnia had built up and ruled the church of Požega, for at least 170 years.

The Crown took a hand. In 1690, from Vienna, King Leopold ordered that the bishop of Bosnia be kept and defended in his rights in the district of Požega. This was to last until a judge had settled the questions.

In 1746 Franjo Ivanović wrote from Vienna to the guardians of Slavonia. He was provincial of Bosnia Argentina. That means Silver Bosnia (a plain rendering). He asked them to gather testimonies from nobles and faithful. The province would use them at the court of Vienna. They were to defend its rights in the Slavonian parishes.

These are records of fights about parishes. They show the friars speaking for their people. They do not show how the people themselves saw the friars.

People

The calendar prints the 1689 declaration in Latin, in the name of the clergy and people. It also prints the opening lines of Ivanović's 1746 letter in Croatian, but the scan is too damaged to quote. No single friar's own words are quoted.
