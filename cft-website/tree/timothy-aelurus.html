Timothy Aelurus

A person. The tradition that tells their story: The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt.

Known for
Bishop of Alexandria chosen by the people and monks against Proterius, the bishop Chalcedon had installed; he rejected the council and Leo of Rome's letter, was exiled for many years, and returned under the emperor Basiliscus.
Also called
Timothy the Great, Timotheus Aelurus, Timothy the Weasel, Timothy the Cat
Dates
Chosen after the emperor Marcian's death, which Charles Lett Feltoe dates to 457; deposed and replaced by 460, by Feltoe's account; recalled in the eighteenth year of his exile by Evagrius of Epiphania, after eighteen years by Zachariah of Mitylene, and died soon after the emperor Zeno's return. B. Evetts's heading in the History of the Patriarchs gives his see as 458 to 480; that History gives him twenty-two years as bishop. No source used here gives his birth
Place
Alexandria; Gangra; Cherson
Role
Bishop of Alexandria

About This Person

When news of the emperor Marcian's death reached Alexandria, the people and the monks seized a priest named Timothy. They brought him to the great church called Caesarian to make him their bishop. The canons wanted three bishops for that, and only two were there. So they found Peter the Iberian in the city and carried him on their shoulders, not letting him touch the ground.

This is how the Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene tells it, from the side that rejected Chalcedon. It says Timothy, a man of ascetic life, had been brought from the wilderness by force to Cyril of Alexandria, who made him a priest. He was "of the same faith as Dioscorus," the bishop of Alexandria whom Chalcedon had deposed. He was so thin from self-denial that the party of Proterius, Chalcedon's bishop in the city, called him "the Weasel." Evagrius of Epiphania calls him Aelurus, which E. Walford's English gives as "the Cat."

Then Proterius was killed. A petition to the emperor Leo from the Egyptian bishops and the clergy of Alexandria, which Evagrius quotes, blames Timothy. Evagrius, from that report, says some Alexandrians killed Proterius in the baptistery "at the instigation of Timotheus." Zachariah says a Roman soldier stabbed him.

Zachariah gives a petition Timothy sent to the emperor. In it he says, "I do not agree with the transactions of Chalcedon, because I find in them divisions and cleavage of the dispensation."

Zachariah says the clergy of Proterius's party dragged Timothy from the font where he had fled. He was sent to Gangra and then to Cherson. In exile, it says, he wrote against the followers of Eutyches as well as the Nestorians. A long work of his against Chalcedon and the letter of Leo of Rome comes down to us in Armenian.

The emperor Basiliscus called him back. Evagrius says it was in the eighteenth year of his exile, and Zachariah after eighteen years. The History of the Patriarchs of the Coptic Church gives seven years. Zachariah says he brought home the bones of Dioscorus in a silver coffin. When the emperor Zeno meant to expel him, Evagrius says, he was told Timothy was already old and near death. He died soon after.

He had told the emperor that the faith of Nicaea needed nothing more. In his words, "neither does the definition of the faith which the fathers proclaimed require addition."

Key Works

  • Petition to the emperor Leo: his refusal of Chalcedon and of Leo of Rome's letter, given in English in the chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene
  • Letters from exile against the followers of Eutyches: letters to Alexandria, Palestine and Constantinople, given in the same chronicle
  • Refutation of the teaching fixed at Chalcedon: a long work against the council and Leo's letter, surviving in Armenian, in the edition of Karapet Ter-Mekerttschian and Erwand Ter-Minassiantz

Legacy

What traces to them

  • The chronicle known as that of Zachariah names him second, after Dioscorus of Alexandria, among those who wrote censures of the points in Leo of Rome's letter that he asked the emperor to correct.
  • Zachariah says Peter, whom Evagrius calls Peter Mongus, was consecrated by the bishops of the country and succeeded him at Alexandria.
  • Zachariah says he brought the bones of Dioscorus back to Alexandria in a silver coffin.

How they're remembered

  • The chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene calls him "Timothy the Great" and "the friend of the poor".
  • The petition of the Egyptian bishops and clergy of Alexandria to the emperor Leo, quoted by Evagrius of Epiphania: "Of all these transactions Timotheus was the guilty cause, and the skilful builder of the scheme of mischief".

What Is Uncertain

  • His story comes mostly through the chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene, from the side that rejected Chalcedon, and through Evagrius of Epiphania, who quotes the petition of his opponents.
  • Who killed Proterius is told two ways. Evagrius, following the petition of the Egyptian bishops and clergy of Alexandria, says Alexandrians did it at Timothy's instigation. Zachariah says a Roman soldier stabbed him, and Evagrius reports that the Life of Peter the Iberian also says a soldier.
  • The length of his exile is told two ways. Evagrius says he was recalled in its eighteenth year, and Zachariah after eighteen years; the History of the Patriarchs of the Coptic Church says he was banished to Gangra with his brother Anatolius for seven years.
  • Zachariah's translators give his nickname as "the Weasel"; E. Walford's English of Evagrius and Feltoe give "the Cat". Their note gives the Greek word behind it.
  • No ancient source used here gives the years of his see or of his death; the years on this card are Feltoe's and Evetts's.
  • His long work against Chalcedon survives in Armenian, and it is not quoted here.
