The Age of Monks and Empires · c. 480-543 · Subiaco, Monte Cassino

Benedict of Nursia

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Early Benedictine / Italian Monasticism.

Known for
Founder of the monasteries of Subiaco and Monte Cassino, and author of the Rule for monks that the Benedictines take their name from, known through Gregory the Great's Dialogues.
Also called
Benedictus, Bennet
Dates
Born, by Edmund Gardner, about 480, and by Francis Gasquet in 480; died, by the tradition of his order, in 543, as both give it. Gregory the Great gives no years. Gardner dates the founding of Monte Cassino to 528 or 529
Place
Nursia; Rome; Subiaco; Monte Cassino
Role
Monk and abbot; author of the Rule

About This Person

In a cave at Subiaco, about forty miles from Rome, a young man lived for three years known only to one monk. Gregory the Great tells how the monk, Romanus, let bread down to him from the rock above on a long rope. A little bell was tied to it, so he would know when it came. He was Benedict of Nursia, who would found Monte Cassino and write a rule for monks.

Nearly all we know of him comes from the second book of Gregory's Dialogues. It is a book of miracles, written to honor a saint. Edmund Gardner, its English editor, dates it to 593. Gregory says he could not learn all that Benedict did, and that his stories came from four of Benedict's disciples, whom he names.

Gregory says Benedict was born in the province of Nursia, to an honorable family, and sent to Rome to study. Seeing many students fall into loose living, he left his books and his father's house. Gardner gives his birth as about 480, and the editor Francis Gasquet as 480.

Later the monks of a nearby monastery asked him to be their abbot. When he held them to a strict life, they poisoned his wine, and the glass broke as he blessed it. He told them, "Go your ways, and seek ye out some other father suitable to your own conditions." Back in the wilderness, he built twelve monasteries, with twelve monks in each.

At Cassino, country people still worshipped Apollo. Benedict broke the idol and built chapels to Saint Martin and Saint John the Baptist. Gardner dates the founding to 528 or 529.

Gregory adds that Benedict "wrote a rule for his monks, both excellent for discretion and also eloquent for the style." The Rule survives, and begins, "Listen, my son, and turn the ear of thine heart to the precepts of thy Master." It promises "a school for the service of God, in which we hope nothing harsh nor burdensome will be ordained." It sets hours for manual work and holy reading, and asks that guests be received as Christ. At its end it calls itself "this little Rule written for beginners".

Gregory says Benedict foretold his death and died standing in the chapel, held up by his disciples, praying. Gardner and Gasquet give the traditional date, 543. Of the Rule, Gregory wrote that "the holy man could not otherwise teach, than himself lived."

Key Works

  • The Rule of Saint Benedict: a prologue and seventy-three chapters on the life of a monastery: obedience, the hours of prayer, manual work and holy reading, the abbot, and the reception of guests as Christ; at its end it calls itself a little rule for beginners

Legacy

What traces to them

  • His Rule, which Gregory the Great calls "excellent for discretion and also eloquent for the style", survives in Latin, and Francis Gasquet and Oswald Hunter Blair translate it into English.
  • Gregory says he built twelve monasteries near Subiaco, with twelve monks in each, before he founded Monte Cassino.

How they're remembered

  • Gregory the Great gave the whole second book of his Dialogues to his miracles and wrote that "the holy man could not otherwise teach, than himself lived."
  • Gregory names the four disciples he had his stories from: Constantinus, the next abbot after him; Valentinianus, long in charge of the Lateran monastery; Simplicius, the third head of his order; and Honoratus, abbot of the house where Benedict first began.

What Is Uncertain

  • Nearly all that is known of his life comes from one witness, Gregory the Great's Dialogues, a book of miracles written to honor a saint. Gregory says he could not learn all that Benedict did and had his stories from four disciples. At least one story he had at second hand: Honoratus heard it from other monks.
  • Gregory gives no years. The birth about 480 and the death in 543 are Edmund Gardner's and Francis Gasquet's; both call 543 the tradition. Gardner says the date he left Rome is disputed and puts it a few years before 500.
  • Gasquet thinks Benedict probably conceived the Rule at Subiaco and finished it at Monte Cassino. Gregory says only that he wrote it.
