New Calvinism (Young, Restless, Reformed)

North America and then the digital world, c. 1998 to 2010 — the 2000s conservative Reformed resurgence named in Christianity Today in 2006, built on Acts 29, The Gospel Coalition and Together for the Gospel.

About This Movement

Audio Brief · Narrated Edition 1:13

Read along

In September 2006 Christianity Today ran a piece by Collin Hansen called 'Young, Restless, Reformed', and it gave a name to something already under way: a wave of younger American evangelicals adopting Reformed theology, organized through networks rather than through denominations. Its most-cited earlier text was John Piper's Desiring God (1986). Its church-planting turn came earlier than its name — Mars Hill in Seattle in 1996, and the Acts 29 network founded in 1998 by David Nicholas with Mark Driscoll — and its institutional wave came after: The Gospel Coalition, initiated by D. A. Carson and Tim Keller in 2004, first meeting in 2005, launched with its Confessional Statement in 2007; Together for the Gospel, whose first conference was in 2006. It ran on conferences, seminaries and publishing rather than on denominations, and it crossed a great many of them. Hansen's book followed in 2008.

Documented Stories

The Sunday After

Tim Keller had planted Redeemer Presbyterian Church in Manhattan in 1989, against the advice of people who told him that young professionals in New York would not come to a church with a Westminster confession; by 2001 some three thousand did each Sunday, meeting in rented auditoriums. On Sunday 16 September 2001, five days after the attacks, the congregation's numbers roughly doubled, with people standing along the walls of the Hunter College auditorium and a second overflow site. Keller preached on John 11, the raising of Lazarus, and on the two words 'Jesus wept'; his sermon, later published as 'Truth, Tears, Anger and Grace', said that Jesus did not explain Lazarus's death to Mary but wept with her, that anger at death was right because death was an enemy, and that grace was the only thing that would keep grief from turning to hate. The church spent the following months in the work of a parish in a wounded city, and much of the crowd stayed. In 2005 Keller and D. A. Carson gathered some forty pastors at Trinity Evangelical Divinity School to consider what a coalition around the gospel might be; in May 2007 The Gospel Coalition launched publicly with a Confessional Statement and a Theological Vision for Ministry that Keller had largely drafted, the New Calvinism's nearest thing to a common confession.

Connections

In Its Own Record

This is the conservative Reformed resurgence of the 2000s. It was built around John Piper and around networks for planting churches and publishing books. Among them were Acts 29, founded in 1998, The Gospel Coalition (2005 to 2007) and Together for the Gospel (from 2006). It grew out of the wider evangelical world. One of its early leaders, Mark Driscoll, was still counted among the emerging churches in 2006. In the same years he criticized their theology in print.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

Collin Hansen named the current 'Young, Restless, Reformed' in a 2006 Christianity Today article, but its roots ran through John Piper's Desiring God (1986) and church-planting networks like Acts 29, founded in 1998, and institutions like The Gospel Coalition, launched with its Confessional Statement in 2007.

Legacy

In 2010 it was a living current, and a young one: its main institutions were only a few years old.

How Sure the Record Is

Rich and well-documented: Collin Hansen's naming article (Christianity Today, September 2006) and book (2008); the networks' own founding records (The Gospel Coalition from 2005, Together for the Gospel from 2006, Acts 29 from 1998); John Piper's Desiring God (1986); conference and publishing archives and Mars Hill's own record from 1996.

  • Collin Hansen, 'Young, Restless, Reformed' (Christianity Today, September 2006) and Young, Restless, Reformed: A Journalist's Journey with the New Calvinists (Crossway, 2008) (primary) — Participant journalism — the author later took office in one of the movement's own institutions, a real connection named here rather than hidden.
  • The Gospel Coalition foundation documents: the Confessional Statement and Theological Vision of Ministry (initiated 2004–05 by Carson and Keller; launched, with the confession adopted, in 2007) (primary) — The movement's dated own-voice statement; the commonly cited 'founded 2005' resolves as initiated 2004, first meeting 2005, launch 2007.
  • John Piper, Desiring God: Meditations of a Christian Hedonist (1986); Together for the Gospel conference records (from 2006, ended 2022); Desiring God and 9Marks media (primary) — The wider own-voice corpus; editions and the T4G dates from standard reference.
  • The Acts 29 and Mars Hill record: Acts 29 founded 1998 (Nicholas with Driscoll; incorporated 2000); Mars Hill Seattle 1996; Driscoll's 2012 handoff of the Acts 29 presidency to Chandler; Acts 29's published removal of Driscoll and Mars Hill (August 2014); the dissolution announcement of 31 October 2014 (primary) — Dated institutional record about living people — founding dates, organizational actions and published statements only.
  • Brad Vermurlen, Reformed Resurgence: The New Calvinist Movement and the Battle over American Evangelicalism (Oxford, 2020) (secondary) — The current's one dedicated scholarly monograph found to date.
