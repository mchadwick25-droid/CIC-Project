The Global Church Era · 1916-c. 2010 · Panama, Guatemala, Colombia, Bolivia, Peru, Mexico

Protestantism in Latin America

About This Movement

The Panama congress met from 10 to 20 February 1916. A committee formed in 1913 organized it. It drew 304 delegates and visitors from 21 nations. Few of them were Latin Americans. Its reports came out in three volumes in 1917. In November 1969 the first Latin American Congress on Evangelization, called CLADE, met in Bogotá. In 1970 the Latin American Theological Fraternity met for the first time, at Cochabamba in Bolivia. Churches met at Huampaní in Peru in 1977 and at Oaxtepec in Mexico in 1978. They meant to form a council of their own. That council, CLAI, was set up at Huampaní in 1982. One source gives 1983. A more conservative body, CONELA, formed as its alternative. Virginia Garrard-Burnett found that Protestants in Guatemala won few converts until the 1960s. Then military power and violence spread. They weakened people's old ties. Indigenous evangelicals in Ecuador organized themselves too. Julián Guamán is an Indigenous author from Ecuador. He dates the start of that organizing to Chimborazo in 1966. The national council of these evangelicals, FEINE, was founded in 1980. Guamán counts more than 1,500 local congregations among them. Susana Andrade studied why Indigenous people in Chimborazo became Protestants. Her book of 2004 sets out to show how Pentecostalism there took on a Quichua form. It took back themes from Indigenous religion. The themes include prayer, the interpretation of dreams and baptism in lagoons.

In Its Own Record

Influenced By

  • The Student Volunteer Movement & Edinburgh 1910 Widely Accepted — Edinburgh 1910 left Latin America off its agenda. During the meeting, missionaries from the region met over lunch. They agreed that Latin America should one day have a meeting of its own, like Edinburgh. The report of the 1916 Panama congress tells this as its own start.

Influenced

  • Lausanne-Era Global Evangelicalism Widely Accepted — In July 1974 René Padilla spoke at the Lausanne congress. He said the gospel bears on the whole of life. In its covenant the congress then repented of setting evangelism and social concern against each other. Historians trace that turn in part to Latin America.
