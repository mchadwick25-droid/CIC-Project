The Early Church Era · c. 290-312 · Olympus in Lycia

Olympus, today in Turkey

Methodius of Olympus

A person. The tradition that tells their story: The Anatolian Church in the Third Century.

Known for
Lycian bishop and martyr who argued against Origen that the same body rises, and who praised the life of chastity.
Role
Bishop, martyr and writer

About This Person

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

The historian Socrates tells an odd story about Methodius, a bishop in Lycia. Methodius attacked Origen in many places, Socrates says, and then, as if taking it all back, praised him in a dialogue. Socrates defended Origen and called his critics worthless, so he is not a neutral witness.

Little else is certain. Jerome says he was bishop of Olympus and later of Tyre, and that he died a martyr at Chalcis at the end of the recent persecution. That was around the year 312. Others put it under Decius and Valerian, but that cannot be right, since Methodius wrote against Origen long after Origen died.

Epiphanius of Cyprus called him a learned man and a valiant defender of the truth, and Jerome praised his style. Theodoret kept a passage from his sermon on martyrs.

Jerome lists his book On the Resurrection as against Origen. Origen had taught that the soul was chained in a body after its fall. Methodius answered, Photius reports, that the body is no chain, and that a person is soul and body joined. He pictures sin as a wild fig tree grown into a temple's stones. Death tears the tree out, so that the temple can stand again. The flesh is then raised, he writes, "with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed."

Key Works

  • The Banquet of the Ten Virgins: A dialogue in which ten women praise chastity in a garden.
  • On the Resurrection: Argues against Origen that the body is no fetter and rises again.
  • On Free Will: A treatise Jerome lists as his, also against Origen.

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Epiphanius of Cyprus made great use of him in his own refutations of Origen.
  • Most of what he wrote has been lost. His books against Porphyry of Tyre survive only in slight fragments, and his commentaries are lost. The longer pieces of On the Resurrection come to us through Epiphanius and Photius.
  • Theodoret copied a passage from his sermon on the martyrs into a set of testimonies from earlier bishops. In it Methodius of Olympus says martyrdom is so precious that Christ himself honored it.
  • The complete Banquet was first printed at Rome in 1656, from a manuscript in the Vatican.

How they're remembered

  • Remembered chiefly as Origen's opponent. The church historian Socrates of Constantinople names him first among the four who reviled Origen.
  • Honored as a martyr. Jerome says he was crowned with martyrdom at Chalcis, and Theodoret calls him the holy Methodius, bishop and martyr.

What Is Uncertain

  • Jerome knew two dates for his death. The later one, around 312, fits his writing against Origen after Origen's death, and the earlier one, under Decius, does not. Some place Chalcis in Syria rather than Greece, because Syria lay nearer his diocese. Epiphanius also calls him Eubulius, which may only be the pen name he used in his Banquet.
  • Only Socrates of Constantinople tells of his change of heart, and Socrates admired Origen. He names the dialogue in which Methodius of Olympus praised him: Xenon.
