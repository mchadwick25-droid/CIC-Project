Zachariah Rhetor

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Syriac Orthodox (West Syriac) Christianity.

Known for
Lawyer from the port of Gaza who wrote a Life of his fellow student Severus of Antioch and a Greek church history that a later Syriac chronicle used for four of its books.
Also called
Zachariah the Rhetorician, Zachariah Scholasticus, Zacharie le Scholastique, Zachariah of Mitylene
Dates
Studied at Alexandria and Berytus in the emperor Zeno's reign and wrote his church history, which ends in 491, before his Life of Severus, by E. W. Brooks's account. Brooks takes him for the Zachariah of Mitylene at the synod of 536. No source used here gives his birth. By Brooks's identification he died or lost his see between 536 and 553
Place
The port of Gaza; Alexandria and Berytus as a student; Constantinople as an advocate; by Brooks's identification, Mitylene as bishop
Role
Historian and biographer; advocate, and by Brooks's identification bishop of Mitylene

About This Person

Zachariah Rhetor came to Berytus, now Beirut, to study civil law, and he expected trouble. In his Life of Severus of Antioch he says new students were teased by the others, and he feared Severus most of all. On his first day in the law school of Leontius, he found Severus among the students. Severus greeted him kindly.

Zachariah came from the port of Gaza, as he says in that Life. He had studied beside Severus at Alexandria, hearing the same teachers. E. W. Brooks introduced the English translation of the chronicle that bears Zachariah's name. From the Life, he places their studies in the emperor Zeno's reign and says Zachariah later worked as an advocate in Constantinople.

He wrote the Life to defend his friend, who was by then bishop of Antioch. It opens with a friend who comes to him from the bookstalls of the Royal Portico in Constantinople, upset by a pamphlet that mocks Severus. Brooks says enemies charged that Severus had followed pagan rites in his youth. Brooks finds Zachariah's answer "a very lame one."

Zachariah also wrote a church history in Greek for Eupraxius, who served at the imperial palace, ending in 491 by Brooks's reckoning. Its preface attacks the Council of Chalcedon. A later Syriac compiler used the history in a longer chronicle. Brooks says the compiler followed Zachariah in books three to six only, and that later writers wrongly gave him the whole work. Land, its editor of 1870, gives him book seven as well.

Brooks also takes him for the Zachariah Scholasticus who, by the heading of a Greek dialogue, later became bishop of Mitylene. A bishop of that name was at a synod in 536, Brooks says. Brooks suggests, with a "perhaps," that he had come over to Chalcedon's side when he became bishop.

In that preface Zachariah says the council "tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ, woven from the top throughout."

Key Works

  • Life of Severus: the student years of Severus of Antioch at Alexandria and Berytus, written to answer a pamphlet against him; written in Greek and kept in Syriac
  • Ecclesiastical History: a church history in Greek, written for Eupraxius of the imperial palace and ending in 491, by Brooks's account; books three to six of the Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene are drawn from it

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Books three to six of the Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene follow his history, Brooks says, and Evagrius of Epiphania cites a Zachariah the Rhetor whose citations agree closely with that text.
  • His Life of Severus is the main source for Severus's early life, as Brooks's introduction to Severus's letters says.

How they're remembered

  • Brooks says later writers wrongly gave the name of Zachariah to the compiler of the whole chronicle.
  • Brooks judges the Life's answer to the charge that Severus followed pagan rites "a very lame one".

What Is Uncertain

  • That the author of the Life and the history became bishop of Mitylene is Brooks's identification. It rests on the Greek heading of a dialogue by Zachariah Scholasticus, later bishop of Mitylene, whose preface says he studied at Alexandria, and on a citation by Dionysius Bar Salibi of "Zachariah the Rhetor and bishop of Melitene", which Brooks takes for a slip for Mitylene.
  • Brooks places a Zachariah of Mitylene at the synod of 536, and notes that his name is not among the signatures to its decree; he may possibly have been a nominee of Anthimus, Brooks says.
  • Brooks suggests, with a "perhaps", that he accepted Chalcedon when he became bishop. No held line of Zachariah's own says so.
  • How much of the chronicle is his: Brooks says the compiler followed him in books three to six only; Land, its editor of 1870, gives him book seven as well, as Brooks reports.
  • Zachariah says in the Life of Severus that he also wrote a Life of Peter the Iberian. Brooks says the Life of Peter that survives is not his. A Syriac manuscript ascribes to him a Life of Isaiah the monk.
