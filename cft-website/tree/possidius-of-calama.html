Possidius of Calama

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity.

Known for
Bishop of Calama and the friend of Augustine of Hippo for almost forty years, who was with him through the Vandal siege of Hippo and wrote his Life, with a list of his works.
Also called
Possidius, Posidius
Dates
Bishop of Calama by 411, when the Carthage conference lists him, and probably by 408, by Augustine's letters; from 397, by Herbert Weiskotten's reckoning; at Augustine's deathbed in 430; driven from his see in 437, if he is the Posidius of Prosper of Aquitaine's Chronicle
Place
Hippo; Calama, about forty miles from Hippo
Role
Bishop of Calama and biographer

About This Person

The Vandals had shut Hippo in by land and sea. Possidius of Calama and other bishops had taken refuge there with Augustine of Hippo. One day at table, Augustine told them what he asked of God. He asked God to free the city, or to make his servants brave to bear his will. Or else, he said, "that He may take me from this world unto Himself." In the third month of the siege, Augustine fell ill with fever.

Possidius tells this in his Life of Augustine, in Herbert Weiskotten's 1919 translation. He had lived beside Augustine, he says, "for almost forty years." He wrote to build up the church, and set down "the things that I have seen in him and heard from him."

In 408 pagans at Calama stoned the church three times, then tried to burn it, Augustine writes. Its bishop forced himself into a crevice, "in which he lay folded double," while they hunted him. Augustine does not name him there. A later letter to the same man, Nectarius of Calama, points to Possidius. He told Nectarius that Possidius loved the town's people in a way more likely to do them good than his own. To another bishop he wrote that in Possidius "you will find one who is very much the same as myself."

At the Carthage conference of 411, Possidius of Calama was one of seven bishops named to speak for the Catholic side.

Possidius was with Augustine when he died in 430, "while we stood by and watched and prayed." He wrote the Life and added a list of Augustine's books, so readers could seek copies in the library at Hippo. Prosper of Aquitaine names a Posidius among the bishops whom the Vandal king Geiseric drove from their cities in 437. Weiskotten takes him to be the same man.

Possidius closed by asking readers to pray that he might "continue to emulate and imitate him in this world."

Key Works

  • Life of Augustine (Sancti Augustini Vita): Augustine's life from his conversion to his death in the siege of Hippo, written by a friend from what he saw and heard
  • The Indiculus: his list of Augustine's books, sermons and letters, added at the end of the Life so readers could find copies; not held in the Library

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Herbert Weiskotten names his Life, with Augustine's Confessions, as one of the two main sources for the life of Augustine.
  • He wrote that readers could seek copies of Augustine's works "from the library of the church of Hippo."

How they're remembered

  • Augustine wrote that in Possidius "you will find one who is very much the same as myself," a man nourished by his labours "with the Lord's bread."
  • To Nectarius of Calama, Augustine said Possidius loved the town's people "in a way more likely to promote their welfare than you do yourself."
  • Weiskotten says that, apart from his ties with Augustine, Possidius is practically unknown.

At Major Councils

  • Conference of Carthage, 411: One of the seven Catholic speakers; told it in his Life of Augustine

What Is Uncertain

  • His Life is a friend's memorial, written, he says, to build up the church, and it records what he saw in Augustine and heard from him.
  • Augustine's letter on the riot at Calama in 408 speaks of "the bishop" without a name. The bishop is taken to be Possidius from Augustine's next letter to Nectarius of Calama, from the list of the 411 conference, which names "Possidius Calamensis", and from a note in J. G. Cunningham's English translation of the letters.
  • The Life tells of a bishop of Calama, trained in Augustine's monastery, who was ambushed and wounded by Donatists. Possidius does not name him. A note to Letter 88 in Cunningham's translation names Possidius as the bishop that Crispinus, the Donatist bishop of Calama, was charged with trying to kill.
  • Prosper of Aquitaine names a Posidius among the bishops the Vandal king Geiseric drove out in 437, but gives no see. Weiskotten takes him for Possidius of Calama.
  • Weiskotten puts his birth between 360 and 370 and his consecration in 397, after Bishop Megalius of Calama. These are his estimates, and no early witness used here gives either date.
  • Of his last years after 437 Weiskotten writes, "He may have gone to Italy, but there is no evidence to that effect."
