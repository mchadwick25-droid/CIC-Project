The Global Church Era · 1906-c. 2010 · China

Chinese Catholicism — the First Chinese Bishops to the Divided Church

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

In 1926 Pope Pius XI consecrated Chinese bishops in Saint Peter's Basilica. His successor Pius XII later recalled them, in Pius XI's words, as "the flowers and . . . latest buds of the Chinese episcopate". On 18 February 1946 Thomas Tian Gengxin became the first Chinese cardinal. On 11 April 1946 Pius XII set up a full hierarchy in China, of 137 dioceses and prefectures in 20 regions. Chinese clergy led 28 of them. From 17 July to 2 August 1957 the authorities held a national assembly of Catholic representatives in Beijing. It approved the statutes of the Chinese Catholic Patriotic Association. In 1958 Pius XII wrote that missionaries had been driven out, and that bishops, priests and laypeople had been imprisoned. He ruled that bishops elected and consecrated against Rome's orders had no power to teach or to govern. In May 1980 a national assembly under state control set up a bishops' conference. Rome judged such bodies incompatible with Catholic teaching.
