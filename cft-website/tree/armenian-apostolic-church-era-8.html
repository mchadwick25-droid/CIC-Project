Armenian Apostolic Church — Etchmiadzin and Constantinople

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Two seats led the Armenian Apostolic Church in these years. The catholicos lived at Etchmiadzin, in lands ruled from Persia. The Persian court had to approve his election. A patriarch led the Armenians of Constantinople, and that city became the center of the nation. Catholic missionaries worked hard to win Armenians to Rome. One of their party once seized the patriarch's seat, but the people drove him out. At the same time a rival bishop had himself proclaimed supreme patriarch of the sultan's lands, against Etchmiadzin. In 1664 the catholicos Hacob IV went to Constantinople to restore order. He died there on a second journey in 1680. The quarrel grew bitter. The patriarch's side had the Ottoman government act against Armenians who had become Catholics. The Catholic side, for its part, used the French ambassadors against the patriarch. In 1703 the patriarch Avedik was put in prison when the French ambassador stepped in. Later he was carried off to France, where he died. Catholic writers record that in 1707 the patriarch John handed the priest Gomidas to the Turks, accusing him of following the Franks. "I am a Christian and a follower of St. Gregory the Illuminator, Armenian by birth," Gomidas told them. He was beheaded. In 1742 the pope approved a separate Armenian Catholic patriarchate. Even so, a Catholic Armenian wrote in 1783 that his people still had to obey the patriarch. They still went to his churches to baptize, marry and bury. In 1722 Afghan armies reached Isfahan, and the Armenians of New Julfa had to ransom their town more than once. In 1736 the catholicos Abraham of Crete went to Nadir's camp on the Mughan plain, where an assembly asked Nadir to rule Persia. Abraham was one of three men chosen to speak for the Armenians, and he came home with seven written orders he had asked for. From 1763 to 1780 the catholicos Simeon of Erevan founded a college, brought in printing and built a paper mill. He also opened the church's first ties with the Russian empire. As Persian rule grew harsher, Armenians began to look to the czar. In 1810 a new catholicos, Ephraim, came to Etchmiadzin under a guard of Russian soldiers.

He still led the church in 1817, when he backed a Russian society's plan to print the Armenian Bible. In Constantinople, talks with the Catholic party begun in 1810 started again in 1816.

Argued Against

  • Armenian Christianity — the Mekhitarists and the Catholic Patriarchate Documented Major — The patriarch's side in the capital moved against Armenians who had joined Rome. Malachia Ormanian, a later patriarch, writes that it got the Ottoman government to act against them. Catholic writers say the patriarch John gave the priest Gomidas up to the Turks. In 1783 a Catholic Armenian wrote that by the sultan's law his people had to obey the patriarch. The same law let him punish them hard if they did not.Lasting result: By the sultan's law, Armenians who joined Rome still had to obey the patriarch, and he could punish them hard if they did not.
  • The Catholic Church of the Ancien Régime Documented — Before he became head of the church, Alexander of Julfa wrote a book against the pope's power and the Council of Chalcedon. Once elected, he sent the pope a respectful letter in the nation's name. Yet a Jesuit report of the time says he waged a secret war on the Catholics. Later Simeon of Erevan, who held the same seat, wrote a book that poked fun at what Rome taught.

Opposed By

  • Armenian Christianity — the Mekhitarists and the Catholic Patriarchate Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — Malachia Ormanian, a later patriarch, writes that the Catholics, in turn, used the French envoys to coerce the patriarch. Catholic writers tell what happened to Avedik, who held that seat. He had fined a man on the French envoy's staff for no good reason. The envoy complained, and so did thirty leading Armenians. Then the vizier had Avedik beaten, stripped of his robes and sent off to an island.Lasting result: The French envoy and thirty leading Armenians complained of the patriarch, Avedik. So the vizier had him beaten, stripped of his robes and sent off to an island.
  • The Catholic Church of the Ancien Régime Documented — Jesuit missionaries wrote that the Armenians' chief error was to confess only one nature in Christ. They called it the root of their schism. Missionaries from Rome, led by Father Clement Galano, worked in the sultan's capital to win Armenians to their side.

Broke With

  • Armenian Christianity — the Mekhitarists and the Catholic Patriarchate Widely Accepted Major — Two Armenian bishops, one of them Abraham Ardzivian, went over to Rome. Malachia Ormanian, a later Armenian patriarch, calls this a defection. In 1742 the pope approved a new Catholic see with Abraham at its head. A Catholic history says the other bishops of Sis met and said his election did not count. They chose a rival. As late as 1783, a Catholic Armenian wrote that his people still could not have churches of their own in Ottoman lands.
