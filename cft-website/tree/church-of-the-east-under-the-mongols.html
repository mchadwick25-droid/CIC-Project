The High Medieval Era · c. 1220-1317 · Central Asia, the Ilkhanate, China

The Church of the East under the Mongols

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

The Church of the East had spread far into Central Asia centuries earlier, and when the Mongols swept west a good many of the peoples they absorbed — the Kerait and the Öngüd among them — were already Christian in this tradition, as were several of the great khans' wives and mothers. For a few decades the church that Latin Europe called Nestorian had extraordinary access: tolerated and sometimes favored at the Mongol court, and in 1281 a monk who had traveled the whole width of Asia from China was elected its catholicos as Yahballaha III. His teacher, Rabban Bar Sauma, had set out with him on pilgrimage toward Jerusalem and ended up instead as the Ilkhan's ambassador to the West, reaching Rome and the courts of France and England and writing the journey down — one of the very few medieval descriptions of Europe by a traveler from Asia. Yahballaha died in 1317, and the favor did not survive him.

In Its Own Record

Continues

  • The Church of the East under the Abbasids Documented Major — The same office carried on as the Mongols came west. Bar Hebraeus lived through these years. He records that the catholicos Sabrisho Bar Masihi held it for thirty-one years and died in 1256. Makkikha of Nisibis took his place.

Continued As

  • The Church of the East — Timur's Catastrophe and the Northern Remnant Documented Major — A history of the church's heads, written in the fourteenth century, says Yahballaha III died in November 1317. It adds that his seat then stood empty for three months and eight days. The scholar Assemani, writing in 1725, says Timothy II, bishop of Mosul and Arbela, was chosen in February 1318.

In Contact With

  • The Latin Papal Church (Gregorian Reform to Innocent III) Documented — Rabban Sawma, a monk of the Church of the East, reached Rome as envoy of the Mongol king Arghun and of his own catholicos. He told the cardinals he carried letters 'concerning the matter of Jerusalem'. He then gave communion to the king of England, probably in Gascony. Back in Rome, he received it from the new pope, Nicholas IV.
  • St Thomas Christians, 1300-1517 Inferential-Thin — In 1301 a scribe in Malabar finished a book of Paul's letters for the church. His closing note names the patriarch Yahballaha, 'the Turk', as head of the Church of the East, and Jacob as metropolitan of India. It shows the Indian church honoring its patriarch. But no letter or meeting from these years survives, so the tie rests on this note alone.
