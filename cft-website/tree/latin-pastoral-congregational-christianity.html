The Early Church Era · c. 240s-430 · Carthage, Hippo Regius

Carthage, today in Tunisia · Hippo Regius, today in Algeria

Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity

About This Movement

This is the congregational life of Latin-speaking North Africa. It is the week in, week out work of a bishop with a flock, and the hard disputes that pressed on that work. Cyprian became bishop of Carthage around 248. When the emperor Decius ordered everyone to sacrifice, he went into hiding. He came back to the hardest pastoral problem of the age. Thousands of his own people had complied, and now they wanted back in.

He organized relief through the plague that still bears his name. He told his congregation to nurse pagan neighbors as well as their own. He refused communion to the envoys of Novatian, a rival bishop at Rome. He argued with Stephen of Rome over baptism given outside the church. He was beheaded outside Carthage in 258.

A century and a half later, Augustine was bishop of Hippo Regius from 395 until his death in 430. He preached to the same congregation several times a week for thirty-five years, and stenographers took much of it down. Hundreds of those sermons survive. They argue, joke, scold and console actual people whose lives he knew.

His pastoral work ran through the great disputes of his day. A rival Donatist church stood in the same towns, and he argued with it for decades. He answered the Manichaeans, whom he had followed for nine years in his youth. In his last years he wrote against Pelagius and his followers on grace and human nature.

Between the two bishops lay the ordinary round. Catechumens were taught, and new believers were baptized at Easter. Penitents did public penance. The church cared for widows and the poor. And the bishop sat as judge in his own people's disputes.

In Its Own Record

Influenced By

  • Tertullian Documented — Jerome says Cyprian read Tertullian every day and asked for him as 'the master'. Jerome had this from an old man who had been Cyprian's secretary. Augustine later said that many of Tertullian's works were still read, and that they were very well written.
  • Ambrosian Milan (standalone) Documented — Augustine says in his Confessions that he was led to Ambrose at Milan so that Ambrose might lead him to God. Before his baptism in 387 he wrote to Ambrose about his past errors and asked what he should read.
  • The Gallic Nicene Episcopate Documented — Augustine quotes Hilary in his own book On the Trinity. He gives Hilary's short formula for the three persons and names Hilary's book as its source. He calls Hilary a man of real authority on Scripture and the faith.
  • Donatism Documented — Tyconius was a Donatist writer of about 380. In On Christian Doctrine, Augustine calls him a Donatist and sets out the seven rules of his Book of Rules. He says they give real help in opening the hidden sense of Scripture.

Influenced

  • The Reformed Cities - Zurich & Geneva Documented — In his Institutes, Calvin calls on Augustine as his guide on grace and on God's choice of the saved. He says he could fill a whole book with Augustine's words alone and need no others.
  • Donatism Documented — Cyprian of Carthage was a leading voice of the church in Africa in the 250s. Augustine tells the Donatists that they keep quoting Cyprian's letters, his opinion and his council. He asks why they use Cyprian's authority to defend their split.
  • Latin African Christianity under Byzantium Documented — Around 550 Facundus of Hermiane wrote in defense of the Three Chapters. In it he cited 'blessed Cyprian, bishop of Carthage and martyr'. He called Augustine the 'most wise and most famous teacher of the churches of Christ'. Procopius says the people of Carthage in 533 revered Cyprian above all other holy men.
  • Port-Royal Jansenism Documented — Port-Royal took its view of grace from Augustine of Hippo. Rome condemned five propositions in 1653. They were said to come from Cornelius Jansen's book, which he named Augustinus. In 1656 Pascal mocked the idea that Augustine's grace became false the moment Antoine Arnauld defended it.

Argued Against

  • Pelagianism Documented — Augustine, bishop of Hippo, wrote against Pelagius by name. In On the Merits and Forgiveness of Sins, he answers points he says Pelagius made against original sin. In his Retractations he says he replied to a book by Pelagius. That book stood up for human nature against the grace of God.
  • Donatism Documented — Augustine, bishop of Hippo, wrote a long book to the Donatists. He wrote it after their bishops and his met at Carthage in 411. He lists it in his Retractations. Earlier, Bishop Optatus of Milevis had written against the Donatist bishop Parmenian.
  • Manichaeism Documented — Augustine, bishop of Hippo, spent nine years among the Manichaeans in his youth, as his Confessions record. Around 400 he wrote a long reply to Faustus, a Manichaean teacher he had once met.
  • Novatianism Documented — Envoys came to Cyprian of Carthage with word that Novatian had been made bishop. Cyprian would not take them into communion. He later wrote that Novatian 'is not in the Church'. Augustine asked whether repentance had been fully taught 'before the Novatians opposed'.
  • Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity Documented — Augustine urged Jerome to translate from the Greek Septuagint, not from the Hebrew. He feared the Latin and Greek churches would end up reading different Bibles. Even after Jerome died, he still held that no translator should be put above the Seventy.
  • Gallic Monastic-Ascetic Christianity Documented — Prosper said the men who raised doubts lived at Marseilles. Augustine answered them in On the Predestination of the Saints. He wrote it in 428 or 429 to Prosper and Hilary, for the brothers they were worried about. Prosper adds that Augustine's earlier book On Rebuke and Grace had met like doubts in Africa.
  • The Luciferians Documented — Augustine warns his readers not to listen to the Luciferians. They do not baptize again, he says, and in that they do right. But they cut themselves off from the unity of the church, and would rather be called Luciferians than Catholics.
  • The Roman Church in the Third Century Documented — In 255 Cyprian and a council of African bishops wrote to Stephen of Rome. Those coming over from heretics, they said, must be baptized. Stephen replied that nothing new should be done, only hands laid on in penance. Cyprian sent his reply to Pompey and called it Stephen's error.

Opposed By

  • Donatism Widely Accepted — Optatus quotes Parmenian, who led the Donatists at Carthage as their bishop. He said that 'one who was stained' could not wash away sins in baptism. Optatus says the Donatists baptized the faithful a second time. They used this rule on the clergy of the Catholic side.
  • Gallic Monastic-Ascetic Christianity Documented — Around 428 Prosper of Aquitaine wrote to Augustine from Gaul. He reported that many servants of Christ at Marseilles thought Augustine's writing on election ran against the Fathers. They thought it ran against the mind of the church as well.
  • Pelagianism Documented — Julian of Eclanum wrote letters that named Augustine and called the Catholic party Manichaeans. Augustine says the Pope sent them on to him, and he answered in four books. He also says Pelagius at Rome could not bear his prayer, 'Give what Thou commandest.'

Broke With

  • Donatism Documented Major — Optatus of Milevis was a Catholic bishop. He says the split began when a rival party made Majorinus bishop at Carthage. Yet Caecilian was bishop there by then. Optatus also copies a plea that the Donatist bishops sent to Constantine. In it they say that disputes had come up between them and the rest of the bishops of Africa.

In Contact With

  • Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity Documented — Jerome and Augustine wrote to each other across the sea for years. Augustine told Jerome of a bishop whose congregation rose up over the new wording of a line in Jonah. Jerome later wrote that he was resolved to love and respect Augustine and to defend his opinions as his own.
