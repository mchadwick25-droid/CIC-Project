The Russian Orthodox Church — Council, Patriarchate and Reunion

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

A Local Council of the Russian church sat in 1917 and 1918. On 4 November 1917 it restored the patriarchate and ruled that a Patriarch would head the church's government. The church then lived under the Soviet regime. Dimitry Pospielovsky's history follows it from 1917 to 1982. Mikhail Shkarovsky and Tatiana Chumachenko have studied how it dealt with the state under Stalin and Khrushchev. In August 2000 the Bishops' Council adopted the Basis of the Social Concept of the Russian Orthodox Church. Its first section sets out the theology behind the church's social teaching. On 17 May 2007, in Moscow, Patriarch Alexy II and Metropolitan Laurus signed the Act of Canonical Communion. It restored communion between the church in Russia and the Russian Church Abroad. Orthodox Christians also lived in Central Asia. Sébastien Peyrouse (2004) found Orthodox, Catholic, Armenian and Protestant minorities there. Most belonged to European minorities that came with Russian colonization in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

In Its Own Record

Gave Rise To

  • American Orthodoxy — Autocephaly & the Convert Wave Widely Accepted Major — By the early 1900s most Orthodox parishes in North America formed one diocese under the Russian church. That is how the church in America tells its own past. In 1970 the patriarch of Moscow and his synod granted it full self-rule.
  • Emigre Orthodoxy & the Paris School Widely Accepted Major — In 1921 Patriarch Tikhon put Archbishop Euloge in charge of the Russian parishes in western Europe. Russians who fled the revolution of 1917 set up parishes in France. Euloge founded the Saint Sergius Institute in Paris to train their priests.

Continues

  • The Russian Church — the Synodal Century Widely Accepted Major — On 4 November 1917 a council of the Russian Orthodox Church ruled that the patriarchate was restored. A patriarch would again lead the church. In December the council ruled that he would chair its Holy Synod.

Argued Against

  • The Ukrainian Greek Catholic Underground Dominant Modern Reconstruction — In March 1946 a synod at Lviv, held under Soviet rule, folded the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church into the Russian Orthodox Church. Bohdan Bociurkiw's study of the church and the Soviet state tells how it was done.

Broke With

  • The Russian New Martyrs & the Catacomb Church Widely Accepted Major — In 1927 Metropolitan Sergius said the church would be loyal to the Soviet state. Many priests and lay people then broke with him. In 1929 Sergius called them a new split. In 1930 Metropolitan Joseph wrote of the movement against Sergius while held by the secret police.

In Contact With

  • The Georgian Orthodox Church Restored Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — In 1943 the Russian and Georgian churches restored communion with each other, a modern study says. It ties the step to Stalin's change of church policy that year.Lasting result: In 1943 the churches of Russia and Georgia were back in full communion with each other.
