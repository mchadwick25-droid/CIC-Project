The High Medieval Era · c. 1206-1300 · Mount Carmel, then Europe

Mount Carmel, today in Israel and beyond

The Carmelites

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Some time between 1206 and 1214 a group of Latin hermits living in a wadi on Mount Carmel above the bay from crusader Acre asked Albert, the patriarch of Jerusalem — who kept his seat at Acre — for a written way of life. What he gave them was short and severe: separate cells, silence, work, daily Mass, and constant meditation on the law of the Lord. As the crusader states contracted the hermits moved west, into England, France and Italy, where a hermit's rule made little sense in a town — so the life was adapted into a mendicant order alongside the Franciscans and Dominicans, and Innocent IV approved the revised Rule in 1247. The order kept the mountain in its name and in its self-understanding, and traced its origins, in its own later retellings, all the way back to the prophet Elijah.

In Its Own Record

Influenced By

  • The Latin Papal Church (Gregorian Reform to Innocent III) Documented Major — Pope Innocent IV wrote to 'the prior and brethren, hermits of blessed Mary of Mount Carmel'. At their request two delegates had explained, corrected and softened their rule, and he confirmed the result. His letter is dated at Lyons in the fifth year of his reign, which was 1247.Lasting result: In 1247 the Carmelites asked Pope Innocent IV to confirm their rule in a form that had been corrected and softened. He did so.
  • The Dominican Order Inferential-Thin — In 1247 the pope sent two men to revise the Carmelite rule. They were Hugh, a cardinal, and William, bishop of Tortosa. The editor of Teresa's Foundations says both were Dominicans. Their letter calls the Carmelites friars, while the pope's own letter still calls them hermits. Neither letter says that the Dominicans shaped the order. So this tie is thin.
  • The Latin Church of Outremer Documented Major — The Carmelites' rule opens as a letter from 'Albert, by the grace of God patriarch of Jerusalem'. He wrote to Brocard and the other hermits who lived 'by the well of Elias on Mount Carmel'. Albert was the Latin patriarch of the crusader kingdom. The editor of Teresa's Foundations says he was killed in 1214.Lasting result: Albert, patriarch of Jerusalem, gave the Carmelite friars their rule. Later the friars asked the pope to explain, correct and soften it.

Influenced

  • The Carmelite Reform and the Spanish Mystics Documented — Teresa of Ávila wrote that her reformed nuns kept 'the rule of our Lady of Carmel, not the rule of the Mitigation'. She meant the rule as it was revised under Pope Innocent IV. Her editor dates his approval to 1247, though she gives 1248.
