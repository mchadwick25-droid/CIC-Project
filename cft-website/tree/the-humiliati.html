The High Medieval Era · c. 1170-1300 · Lombardy

Lombardy, today in Italy

The Humiliati

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

In twelfth-century Lombardy, groups of lay wool-workers and weavers began to live a simple, demanding life of penance. They wore plain dress and worked with their hands. They refused the oaths and the violence that feudal society expected. They called themselves the Humiliati, 'the humbled ones.' In 1184 Pope Lucius III condemned the Waldensians for preaching without leave. Later writers say he condemned the Humiliati with them. If so, their story then took the opposite turn. The Waldensians stayed apart for good, but Pope Innocent III took the Humiliati back around 1200. He made them three recognized orders. Two were religious orders. The third was for married lay people who stayed in ordinary working life under a shared rule. For about two centuries they were a real presence in the towns of northern Italy. Their rule and community records still survive. In 1571 a papal bull shut down the order of Humiliati brothers.

People

No one is shown. The Humiliati were groups of lay people, organized by trade and town, and no founder or leader is named on record.
