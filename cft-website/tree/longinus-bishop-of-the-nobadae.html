Longinus, bishop of the Nobadae

About This Person

For three years Longinus was watched in Constantinople. At last, John of Ephesus writes, "he disguised himself, and put a wig on his head,—for he was very bald ;—and taking with him two servants, he fled."

Longinus was a bishop of the party that refused the council of Chalcedon. He was sent to the Nobadae, on the eastern borders of the Thebais beyond Egypt, whom the presbyter Julian had first taught. He was consecrated as soon as the patriarch Theodosius of Alexandria died. John says the empress Theodora had ordered it; his chapter heading says Theodosius did. Almost all of this comes from John, of his own party.

Among the Nobadae, Longinus built a church, ordained clergy and taught them the order of worship. Their envoy came to the emperor's court. There, John says, "in the presence of myself", he praised Longinus: "Though we were Christians in name, yet we did not really know what Christianity was until Longinus came to us."

After about five years he was called north, to help make a patriarch for his party at Alexandria. Back among the Nobadae, he went on to the Alodaei, a powerful people many score leagues beyond them. The Alexandrians sent word that he was a heretic and deposed. "We will receive him who baptized the Nobadæ," the Alodaei answered, "and by him will we be baptized."

John gives part of a letter Longinus wrote from that land. On the road, Longinus says, they all fell ill, and "we lost no less than seventeen camels." Then the king "came out in person to meet us, and received us with great joy." He baptized the king and his nobles. "For I desire neither silver, nor gold, nor dresses, as God is my witness," he writes.

John dates these things to a year R. Payne Smith, his translator, reckons as 580. No source tells of Longinus after that, or of his death.

Key Works

Legacy

What traces to them

  • Among the Nobadae he built a church, ordained clergy and taught the order of worship, by John of Ephesus's account.
  • He baptized the king of the Alodaei and his nobles. The Alodaei king's letter to the king of the Nobadae calls him "my father, the holy father Longinus".

How they're remembered

  • The Nobadae envoy at Constantinople: "Though we were Christians in name, yet we did not really know what Christianity was until Longinus came to us."
  • Michael the Syrian, a twelfth-century patriarch of John's church, retells his mission from John.

What Is Uncertain

  • Almost all that is known of him comes from John of Ephesus, who wrote from Longinus's own party and calls the Alexandrian clergy who opposed him envious. Michael the Syrian abridges John.
  • Who ordered his appointment. John's narrative says the empress Theodora had ordered it, and that it was carried out at the death of Theodosius. John's own chapter heading, and Michael, say Theodosius ordered it.
  • How long he first stayed in Nubia. John says about five years in one place and six in another.
  • His earlier life. Payne Smith says he was a priest who served in the aged Theodosius's place; that line is Payne Smith's own bridge, with no source in the surviving text.
  • The sources give nothing of his origin, his age or his death.
