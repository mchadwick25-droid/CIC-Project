The High Medieval Era · 1054-1300 · Constantinople; Nicaea (1204-1261)

Constantinople · Nicaea, today in Turkey

The Byzantine Church after the Schism

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

In July 1054 a papal legate laid a bull of excommunication on the altar of Hagia Sophia during the liturgy and walked out. The patriarch, Michael Cerularius, condemned him in return. It was not yet a final break. But the split between the Greek and Latin churches did not heal, and in 1204 it turned to disaster. A crusading army sacked Constantinople and set up a Latin patriarch. The church of the empire was driven out to Nicaea for nearly sixty years. The Byzantines won back their city in 1261. In 1274 Emperor Michael VIII needed help from the West against his enemies, so he accepted union with Rome at the Second Council of Lyon. His own clergy and monks refused it, and the union fell apart.

In Its Own Record

Continues

  • The Byzantine Imperial Church (Macedonian Era) Documented Major — Michael Cerularius was patriarch of Constantinople when the pope's envoys came in 1054. He kept the post until 1058. A later Byzantine list of the city's patriarchs names them in order. It runs from Photius in the ninth century to the patriarchs who served under the emperor Manuel in the twelfth.

Continued As

  • The Byzantine Church, Palaiologan Era Documented Major — The church of Constantinople kept its records and its rulings across the year 1300. In 1324 its synod settled a dispute by a decision made long before at Nymphaion, under Patriarch Arsenios. The patriarchs' register also copies a ruling of his from 1256, found in the church's old books.

Influenced

  • Kievan Rus' Christianity, 1054-1240 Dominant Modern Reconstruction — The church of Rus' was a province of the church of Constantinople. Mouravieff, a Russian church historian writing in 1838, says its chief bishops were named at Constantinople and answered to the patriarch there. One of them, Matthew, was sent from the city just before the crusaders took it.

Argued Against

  • Balkan Bogomilism, 1054-1300 Dominant Modern Reconstruction — Around 1100 the emperor Alexios I and the patriarch Nicholas put the Bogomil teacher Basil on trial. Basil would not give up his faith, so the emperor had him burned in the Hippodrome. Alexios also told the monk Euthymios Zigabenos to write a book against the Bogomils. The emperor's daughter Anna Komnene tells the story. Here it comes as Franjo Rački told it in 1869.

Opposed By

  • The Latin Papal Church (Gregorian Reform to Innocent III) Documented — In July 1054 Cardinal Humbert and two other envoys of the pope cast the patriarch of Constantinople, Michael Cerularius, out of the church. Their written sentence calls him a patriarch in name only. It says the seeds of heresy are sown among his followers every day. Crusaders took Constantinople in 1204. Pope Innocent III then treated its church as his own. In 1205 he wrote that it had lately come back into unity with Rome.
  • The Latin Church of Outremer Documented — Michael the Syrian, a Syriac patriarch who lived through these events, tells how the prince of Antioch brought back a Greek patriarch, Athanasius, from Constantinople. The Latin patriarch, Aimery, left the city and cut its people off from the church. After an earthquake in 1170, Aimery told them to throw the Greek patriarch out 'with contempt' as an intruder.

Broke With

  • The Latin Papal Church (Gregorian Reform to Innocent III) Documented Major — Cardinal Humbert's own account says the papal legates laid their excommunication on the main altar of Hagia Sophia on 16 July 1054. He adds that Patriarch Michael then tried to bring them before a council there. Only the Latin side's record of this break is quoted here.

In Contact With

  • Armenian Christianity in Cilicia Documented — Vartan, an Armenian historian of the 1200s, says Emperor Manuel asked the catholicos Gregory and his brother Nerses to set out their faith. They wrote it down and sought union with the Greeks, but the work was never finished. Michael the Syrian quotes a letter from Nerses. It lists what the Greeks asked of him, among them belief in two natures in Christ and honor for the fourth council.
  • The Latin Papal Church (Gregorian Reform to Innocent III) Widely Accepted — At the Second Council of Lyon in 1274, the Greek side made a profession of faith. In it they accepted the full primacy of the Roman church. Its 1911 editor says Pope Clement IV first put the text to Emperor Michael VIII in 1267. Michael then offered it to Pope Gregory X at Lyon. The profession does not show how the Greek church received the union.
