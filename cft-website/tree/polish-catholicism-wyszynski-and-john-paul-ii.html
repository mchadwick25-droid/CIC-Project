The Global Church Era · 1918-c. 2010 · Poland

Polish Catholicism — Wyszyński and John Paul II

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Cardinal Stefan Wyszyński wrote the Jasna Góra Vows of the Polish Nation while interned at Komańcza. He finished them on 16 May 1956. On 26 August 1956 Bishop Michał Klepacz read them at Jasna Góra. On 18 November 1965, in Rome, the Polish bishops sent a message to their German brother bishops. Archbishop Bolesław Kominek drafted it, and 34 bishops signed it, Wyszyński and Karol Wojtyła among them. On 2 June 1979 Wojtyła, now Pope John Paul II, preached in Victory Square in Warsaw. He prayed for the Spirit to come down and renew the face of the earth, and of this land. A lay renewal grew inside the church too. The Light-Life movement began with a first oasis retreat in 1954, led by Father Franciszek Blachnicki. From 1969 it was called the Movement of the Living Church, and in 1976 it took the name Light-Life. In neighboring Lithuania, Father Sigitas Tamkevičius produced the Chronicle of the Catholic Church in Lithuania at Simnas. Its first issue is dated 19 March 1972, and its 81 issues ran to 19 March 1989.
