The Late Medieval Era · c. 1230s-1463 · Bosnia

The Bosnian Church (Krstjani)

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Between Catholic Hungary and Orthodox Serbia, medieval Bosnia had a church of its own. Its members called themselves krstjani, Christians. It used a Slavonic liturgy and had its own leaders. At the head was a djed, a 'grandfather', with gosts and starci under him. Its communities, called hiže, or houses, were scattered through the countryside. Rome condemned it as heretical again and again, and from time to time sent friars against it. Bosnia's own rulers protected it and made use of it. What its members actually believed is still in real dispute. Since the 1970s most historians have rejected the long-held view that they were Bogomil dualists. The one major document written from inside is the will of Gost Radin, made in 1466. It reads like ordinary Christianity, with a cross drawn down its margin. Bosnia fell to the Ottomans in 1463, and the church did not outlast the kingdom.

In Its Own Record

Opposed By

  • The Latin Papal Church (Avignon to Fifth Lateran) Widely Accepted — About 1370 Pope Urban V wrote that many heretics lived in Bosnia. He told the archbishops of Split and Dubrovnik to cut off from the church anyone who sheltered them or traded with them. In 1451 Pope Nicholas V called the Bosnian lord Stephen a 'faithless Patarene'. An 1877 history says Roman writers always gave that name to the dissenters of Bosnia.
