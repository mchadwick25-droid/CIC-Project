Alexandria, today in Egypt

Simon I of Alexandria

A person. The tradition that tells their story: Coptic Christianity under Early Islam.

Known for
Coptic patriarch of Alexandria, a Syrian chosen over his own teacher. The History tells how he survived poison, and would not ordain a bishop for India without the governor's leave.
Also called
Simon the Syrian, Simeon
Dates
B. Evetts heads his life 689 to 701, and René Basset's French of the Arabic Synaxarium brackets 689 to 702. The History says he died on the 24th of the Coptic month Abib, in the year 416 of Diocletian, after nine and a half years. Eusèbe Renaudot gives seven years and six months, and reads the year as 415
Place
Born in the East, the History says. Brought up at the monastery of Az-Zajaj, and buried there. The History says the body of Saint Severus lies in a shrine there. The Ethiopic Synaxarium, in E. A. Wallis Budge's English, names him Severus of Antioch. Both synaxaria place the monastery west of Alexandria
Role
Patriarch of Alexandria, the forty-second in the History's count

About This Person

John, head of the monastery of Az-Zajaj, was handsome, and the Amir Abd al-Aziz agreed to make him patriarch. Then one bishop stood up, says the History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria. "This man shall not be our patriarch," he said. "Simon is worthy of this degree."

Simon I of Alexandria was a Syrian, from the East. The Amir asked why they could not choose a man of their own country. "The matter belongs to God, and in the second place to thee," the bishops replied. The Amir had him ordained.

The History is a later Arabic history of the Coptic church. B. Evetts, who translated it, dates Simon's patriarchate 689 to 701. The History says Simon's parents had given him to the church as a boy, like Samuel, at John's monastery. John the deacon, who wrote the History's life of Michael I, says Simon's archdeacon George wrote the history of these years.

Some of Simon's own priests poisoned his cup, but it did him no harm. Then they gave him poisoned figs while he fasted, and he lay forty days in pain. When the Amir ordered the poisoners burned, Simon fell on his face. He could not stay patriarch if they died, he said, and the priests were set free. The Ethiopic Synaxarium, a book of saints' days, says instead that they were sent into exile.

A priest came from India asking for a bishop. Simon would not ordain one without the Amir's command. The Gaianites, a rival sect, ordained a man anyway. The caliph Abd al-Malik had the hands and feet of the new bishop and his two priests cut off. He wrote to the Amir that the patriarch had sent news of Egypt to India. The Amir questioned Simon at night, and Simon said: "If a voice came from heaven, bidding me deviate from the truth, I would say no otherwise." The Amir gave him three days. At sunset on the second day, Simon's spiritual son found the Indian priest. The next morning the priest told the Amir all that had happened, and the Amir saw that Simon had told the truth.

Before he died, he went to see the monks of Wadi Habib. "I shall not see them again after this time in the body," he said.

Legacy

What traces to them

  • He wrote a synodical letter to Julian, patriarch of Antioch, on the unity of their two sees. Julian answered and sent back rich gifts, the History says.
  • He ordained Zacharias bishop of Sakha and Ptolemy bishop of Upper Manuf, among many others, the History says.
  • John the deacon, who wrote the History's life of Michael I, says Simon's archdeacon George wrote its account from Cyril of Alexandria to Alexander II.

How they're remembered

  • The Arabic Synaxarium, the Coptic church's book of saints' days, keeps him on the 24th of Abib in René Basset's French. Like the History, it has the priests freed and the magicians burned.
  • The Ethiopic Synaxarium, in E. A. Wallis Budge's English, keeps him on the 24th of Hamle. It has him sick for four days, and the priests sent into exile.
  • Eusèbe Renaudot, a Catholic scholar writing in Latin in 1713, says the Copts keep his memory on the 24th of Abib.
  • A book on the churches of Egypt, under the name of Abu Salih, calls him Simon the Syrian. It dates a monastery at Hulwan to his time and Isaac's.

What Is Uncertain

  • His life rests on the History of the Patriarchs, a later Arabic history of the Coptic church. Its life of Simon names no writer.
  • Who wrote these years. John the deacon says George's history ran from Cyril to the days of Alexander II, down to the time of the caliph Sulaiman. Renaudot gives George the same end point. He says many wrote the lives from Cyril to Simon, George above all.
  • The poisoning. The History says he lay forty days and recovered. Budge's Ethiopic says four days. Renaudot reports the historian Elmacinus, who says a fourth dose killed him.
  • The poisoners. The History and Basset's synaxarium say the priests were freed at his plea. Budge's Ethiopic and Renaudot say they were exiled.
  • The cups. B. Evetts's English of the History has his cup poisoned twice before the figs. Renaudot's reading of the History, Basset's synaxarium and Budge's Ethiopic have three times.
  • His years. The History gives nine and a half, Renaudot seven years and six months.
  • The order of patriarchs. Michael the Syrian, a twelfth-century patriarch of the Syriac Orthodox church, puts Simon before Isaac. The History puts him after.
