The Global Church Era · 1979-c. 2010 · United States

The Christian Right

A movement across churches

In 1979 five ministers founded the Moral Majority, a political organization that drew support from several faiths.

About This Movement

Jerry Falwell, Tim LaHaye, Charles Stanley, D. James Kennedy and Greg Dixon founded the Moral Majority on 6 June 1979. It named four principles of its own: it was pro-life, pro-family, pro-morals and pro-American. From its base in Washington, it worked in politics for those aims. In August 1980 Falwell called it entirely political, not a religious group. Its backers, he said, were not all evangelicals. That month Ronald Reagan spoke in Dallas, at a briefing run by another group, the Religious Roundtable. He told the crowd that he endorsed them and what they were doing. The Moral Majority closed in 1989. That same year Pat Robertson founded the Christian Coalition, with Ralph Reed as its executive director. Reed went on to found the Faith and Freedom Coalition in 2009.

In Its Own Record

The entry on Fundamentalism traces this movement back to it. Its founders were ministers. Its groups drew support from several churches and faiths, and none became a church. They were the Moral Majority (1979–1989), the Christian Coalition (from 1989) and the Faith and Freedom Coalition (from 2009).

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

In June 1979 Jerry Falwell and four other ministers founded the Moral Majority. In August 1980 Ronald Reagan, then running for president, spoke at a briefing in Dallas. Another group, the Religious Roundtable, ran it. He said the gathering was nonpartisan, so it could not endorse him. Then he added that he endorsed them and what they were doing. The Moral Majority closed in 1989, and Pat Robertson founded the Christian Coalition that year. In 2009 Ralph Reed, the Christian Coalition's first executive director, founded the Faith and Freedom Coalition.

Legacy

Its groups changed over thirty years, but the movement carried on. In 2010 the historian Daniel K. Williams told its story in God's Own Party. The record shows its political work well. It shows much less of what the movement meant for its members' faith.

Sources

Doctrinal Floor Note

In August 1980 Jerry Falwell said the Moral Majority was not a religious group. It was entirely political, he said. He counted pastors, Catholic priests, Jewish rabbis and Mormon elders among its members. On his account, it had no confession of its own to test. No statement of faith has been found for it or the later groups. The churches its members came from are not tested through it. This reading is not settled until the later groups' own statements are checked.

How Sure the Record Is

Moderate to rich. The Moral Majority's papers are kept at Liberty University. In a 1980 interview Jerry Falwell gave his own account of it. Three studies trace its history: William Martin (1996), Clyde Wilcox (1996, revised to 2010) and Daniel K. Williams (2010).
