Kievan Rus' Christianity, 1054-1240

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

In the two centuries after Yaroslav the Wise died in 1054, the Christianity that had arrived in Rus' only two generations earlier put down roots of its own. Its center was the Caves Monastery at Kyiv, begun by a hermit named Anthony in a hillside cave above the Dnieper and turned into a community by Theodosius, its third abbot, who gave it a Byzantine rule and a distinctive emphasis on humility, manual work and care for the poor. Out of that monastery came bishops, chronicles, and a book of stories about its own monks. The church was formally a metropolitanate of Constantinople, usually headed by a Greek, but native metropolitans appeared and a native literature and a native calendar of saints grew up around the princely dynasty. It ended abruptly: the Mongols sacked Kyiv in 1240.

In Its Own Record

Gave Rise To

  • Muscovite Monastic Christianity Documented Major — A ruling of the patriarch's council in Constantinople, from the 1300s, says Kyiv had long held the cathedral of the chief bishop of Rus'. When Kyiv fell into ruin, the bishops moved their seat to Vladimir. Herberstein, an envoy to Moscow in the 1500s, wrote that the seat had since moved on to Moscow.

Continues

  • Kievan Rus' Christianity Inferential-Thin Major — Before he became head of the church in Kyiv in 1051, the priest Ilarion dug a cave above the Dnieper. Mouravieff, a Russian church historian writing in 1838, calls that cave the seed of the Caves Monastery and of every religious house in Russia. Herberstein retold the Rus' annals in 1549. He says Russia has kept the faith of Christ since Vladimir's baptism. The claim of one unbroken church rests on these two later accounts.

Influenced By

  • Iconophile Byzantine Monasticism Dominant Modern Reconstruction — Mouravieff's history of the Russian church draws on the chronicler Nestor. It says Theodosius of the Kyiv Caves wrote out the rule of the Stoudios house for his monks. A monk from Constantinople had brought it. Other Rus' houses then took it as their model.
  • Athonite & Comnenian Byzantine Monasticism Inferential-Thin — A. N. Mouravieff's history of the Russian church, published in 1838, says Anthony took his vows on Mount Athos. He then came home and began the Caves Monastery at Kyiv. The same history says the house's rule came from the Stoudios monastery in Constantinople. It names no earlier witness, so the tie to Athos rests on this one account.

In Contact With

  • The Byzantine Church after the Schism Inferential-Thin Major — The church of Rus' was a province of the church of Constantinople. Mouravieff, a Russian church historian writing in 1838, says its chief bishops were named at Constantinople and answered to the patriarch there. One of them, Matthew, was sent from the city just before the crusaders took it. This rests on Mouravieff's one account.Lasting result: In 1124 the patriarch John made Nicetas the chief bishop of Rus'.
