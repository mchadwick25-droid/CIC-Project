The Late Medieval Era · c. 1300-1400 · Athos, Thessalonica

Athos · Thessalonica, today in Greece

Hesychasm & the Palamite Synthesis

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

On Mount Athos monks had long practiced hesychia, or stillness. They sat alone, held the breath steady, and repeated the Jesus Prayer until it went on by itself. Some of them said they saw an uncreated light. The Calabrian scholar Barlaam mocked the practice. Gregory Palamas, an Athonite monk who later became archbishop of Thessalonica, answered him in the Triads. God cannot be known in his essence, he argued, and yet he is truly given in his energies. So what the praying monk meets is God himself, not some created stand-in. The argument ran for two decades. Councils at Constantinople in 1341, 1347 and 1351 settled it in Palamas's favor, and the hesychast view became the standard of Orthodox theology. Beside it stands a very different voice. Nicholas Cabasilas, a layman of Thessalonica, wrote The Life in Christ. He showed that the same life in God came through the sacraments to people who would never enter a monastery.

In Its Own Record

Influenced By

  • Athonite & Comnenian Byzantine Monasticism Documented — Kallistos Telikoudes, a teacher of hesychast prayer, told monks to read chapters on prayer. He named 'especially' those of the New Theologian. That is Symeon, an abbot in Constantinople around the year 1000. The hesychasts' own case also went out from Athos, as the Tome of the Holy Mountain.

Influenced

  • The Byzantine Church, Palaiologan Era Documented Major — A synod at Constantinople ruled on the quarrel over the monks' prayer. Its tome found the monks above Barlaam's charges against them. It warned that Barlaam would be cut off from the church unless he repented.Lasting result: A synod at Constantinople ruled for the hesychast monks. It said Barlaam would be cut off from the church unless he repented.
  • The Hesychast Revival - Paisius & the Philokalia Documented — The Philokalia is a book of the old prayer teachers. It was first printed at Venice, and it holds the work of Gregory Palamas. Paisius Velichkovsky also put a piece by Palamas on prayer into Slavonic.
  • Muscovite Monastic Christianity Documented — Nil Sorsky's rule for his hermitage quotes Gregory of Sinai on the Jesus Prayer. Nil also says he saw hermits living in twos and threes with his own eyes, on Mount Athos and near Constantinople.
  • The Optina Elders & Russian Ascetic Renewal Documented — The monks of Optina printed two books of the old prayer teachers, in 1847 and 1849. One holds a preface to the book of Gregory of Sinai. The other holds Gregory Palamas on prayer, in the Slavonic of Paisius Velichkovsky.

Sources

  • Gregory Palamas, The Triads (ed. Meyendorff, trans. Gendle, Classics of Western Spirituality, 1983) (primary) — In Defense of the Holy Hesychasts (1338-41) - the synthesis's cornerstone in the movement's leading voice.
  • The synodal tomoi of 1341, 1347, 1351 (and 1368), in Norman Russell, Gregory Palamas: The Hesychast Controversy and the Debate with Islam (Translated Texts for Byzantinists, Liverpool, 2020) (primary) — The conciliar documents that made hesychasm the Orthodox norm, most translated into English for the first time here. These are the winning side's records - the anti-Palamite side (Barlaam, Akindynos, Gregoras) is largely untranslated and reaches us mainly through its condemnations.
  • The hesychast method texts: Nikephoros the Monk On Watchfulness, ps.-Symeon's method, Gregory of Sinai - as carried in the Philokalia (vol. 4, trans. Palmer, Sherrard & Ware) (primary) — The Philokalia is a 1782 compilation - a later, retrospective anthology of these 14th-century texts, not a contemporary one.
  • Nicholas Cabasilas, The Life in Christ (trans. deCatanzaro, SVS Press, 1974) (primary) — Sacramental life for people outside the monastery, by a lay theologian of hesychast Thessalonica. His Commentary on the Divine Liturgy is a companion work.
  • John Meyendorff, A Study of Gregory Palamas (1959; Eng. 1964), with Norman Russell, Gregory Palamas and the Making of Palamism in the Modern Age (OUP, 2019) (secondary) — Classic frame plus current corrective on how 'Palamism' was retrospectively systematized.
  • Cambridge History of Christianity vol. 5, ch. 4 'The rise of hesychasm' (Krausmuller) (reference) — This era is the chapter's home ground - a direct fit.
