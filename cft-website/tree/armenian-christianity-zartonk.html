Armenian Christianity — the Zartonk Century

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Zartonk — awakening — is what Armenians call their nineteenth century. A revival of language, schools, printing and civic life carried largely inside the church, because under Ottoman rule the church was the nation's legal body. Its central document is the Armenian National Constitution of 1863, which seated lay delegates in a national assembly and limited what the Patriarch of Constantinople could do alone, an unusual experiment in giving a laity formal power over its own hierarchy. The century also divided the community: converts of American missionaries were expelled from the Apostolic Church and organized as the Armenian Evangelical Church in 1846, recognized by the Ottoman state as a separate Protestant community in 1850, while an Armenian Catholic patriarchate continued alongside. And it ended in blood — the Hamidian massacres of 1894-96 killed tens of thousands and emptied whole districts.

Russia took Erivan and Etchmiadzin in 1828. Armenian volunteers helped.

In 1836 a Russian rule, the Polozhenie, tied the catholicos's rule to the state. Ormanian says it let the state meddle.

It also left the last word on bishops to the tsar. The catholicos named two men, and the tsar chose.

A Catholic work says a papal bull of 1867 caused a schism in Armenia. The bull was called Reversurus.

  • The Armenian Church after the Genocide (Medz Yeghern) Widely Accepted Major — Malachia Ormanian, once patriarch of Constantinople, wrote a book on his church that came out in English in 1912. He showed it still run by a national assembly, and put its people at about four million. Historians follow the same church through the genocide of 1915.
