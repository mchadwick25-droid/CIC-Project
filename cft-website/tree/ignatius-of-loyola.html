Ignatius of Loyola

A person. The tradition that tells their story: The Society of Jesus.

About This Person

Ignatius of Loyola was born in 1491 at the castle of Loyola, and grew up a page and then a soldier. At the siege of Pamplona a cannon ball broke one of his legs. In his slow recovery he read the lives of Christ and the saints, because the house had no other books.

He set out as a pilgrim. At Manresa he lived on the alms he begged each day. He reached Jerusalem, but he could not stay there. So he went back to school, to be better fitted to help souls.

At Paris he took his master's degree in March 1534. Francis Xavier and Peter Faber were his fellow students there. He and his companions meant to spend their lives in Jerusalem. If they could not, they would offer themselves to the pope.

No ship could take them, so they went to Rome. In 1540 Pope Paul III approved their Society. From Rome Ignatius sent out letters and instructions, among them orders for his men at the Council of Trent in 1546. He died on 31 July 1556.
