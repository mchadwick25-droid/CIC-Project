The Catholic Church of the Long Nineteenth Century

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Between the fall of Napoleon and the eve of the First World War, the Catholic Church lost a state and gained a papacy. Gregory XVI and then Pius IX met the liberal century head-on: Pius defined the Immaculate Conception in 1854, cataloged the errors of the age in the Syllabus of 1864, and in July 1870 saw the First Vatican Council define papal infallibility. Weeks before Italian troops entered Rome and ended the Papal States. Stripped of territory, the papacy grew in reach instead: bishops and ordinary Catholics looked to Rome as never before, new congregations of sisters and brothers staffed schools and hospitals across the world, and mass devotion gathered around Marian apparitions like Lourdes in 1858. Under Leo XIII the church that had spent decades condemning the modern world began to negotiate with it, most consequentially in Rerum Novarum (1891), which put the church on the side of a worker's right to a living wage and to organize.

Rome still ran the missions through Propaganda. In 1845 it wrote to a vicar apostolic. It called it a grave abuse, not to be borne, to baptize again all whom catechists had baptized (a plain rendering).

Mission work grew far beyond Europe. Charles Lavigerie reached Algiers as archbishop on 16 May 1867. In London on 31 July 1888 he spoke of his missioners, the White Fathers. They were settled in the Sahara and on the high lands of Central Africa.

Daniele Comboni wrote a plan for Africa. The press of Propaganda in Rome printed it in 1867. He asked whether Africa could be won by means of Africa (a plain rendering).

In 1893 Launay printed a story told by a seminary director in Paris. Some priests, he said, had taken his society for the Lazarists, the Jesuits or the Holy Spirit Society. His society, he said, was quite distinct from the others.

His society was the Foreign Missions of Paris. Launay says it was born in 1658. It grew in the persecutions of Tonkin, Cochinchina and China.

In 1814 Pius VII had restored the Jesuits to all states.

The Catholic Church in Africa in the twentieth century has its own entry. It grew from missions like these, though the sources here stop at the White Fathers of 1888.

In Its Own Record

Gave Rise To

  • East African Christianity & the Uganda Martyrs Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — In 1879 the White Fathers, a French Catholic mission, reached the kingdom of Buganda. Their converts formed the Catholic church there, and some died with Anglican pages in the killings of 1885 to 1887. Modern histories tell this, Adrian Hastings's among them.
  • The Catholic Church in Africa in the Twentieth Century Inferential-Thin Major — The Catholic Church in Africa of the twentieth century grew from missions like Lavigerie's White Fathers. He reached Algiers as archbishop on 16 May 1867. In 1888 he said his missioners were settled in the Sahara and in Central Africa. The evidence read is thin: one editor's book of 1889, and no text that follows the line into the later church.

Continues

  • The Catholic Church of the Ancien Régime Documented Major — Pope Pius VII made a concordat with France in 1801 that let the Catholic religion be practiced freely there. In 1817 the same pope signed a concordat with Bavaria. So one pope led the same church in both years.

Continued As

  • The Catholic Church — Pius X to the Eve of the Council Documented Major — In February 1906 Pope Pius X wrote to the bishops, clergy and people of France about the new law that cut church from state. He called Leo XIII his illustrious predecessor and pointed to his teaching on church and state. The same Roman see and church carried on into the new century.

Influenced

  • Nineteenth-Century Latin American Catholicism Widely Accepted Major — In 1862 Pius IX and Ecuador signed a pact in Rome that kept the state's faith Catholic. It tied every school to church teaching and let bishops alone pick the books for religion. In 1895 Mexico's image of Guadalupe was to be crowned in the name of Pope Leo XIII, by his order. Historians call this pull toward Rome 'Romanization'.Lasting result: In 1862 Pope Pius IX and Ecuador agreed that every school would follow Catholic teaching. Only bishops would choose the books used to teach religion.

Opposed By

  • The Stone-Campbell Restoration Movement Dominant Modern Reconstruction — In 1837 Alexander Campbell held a public debate with a Catholic bishop, John Purcell. It went on for days, and the movement printed the whole of it. This rests on one modern work, a reference book on the movement.

Broke With

  • The Old Catholic Churches — Utrecht to the Union Documented Major — In August 1870 a declaration signed at Nuremberg refused to accept the new Vatican decrees. In 1889 five Old Catholic bishops met at Utrecht and rejected them in writing. In 1893 Pope Leo XIII cast out Gerard Gul, the new archbishop of Utrecht, and said all who backed him were in schism.
  • The Haitian Church — Rupture to Concordat Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — Historians describe a long break between Haiti and Rome after independence in 1804. Rome would not recognize the new state, and the government named its own clergy. A concordat signed on 28 March 1860 ended the break and set up a regular hierarchy.

In Contact With

  • Japanese Christianity — the Hidden Christians’ Emergence & the Meiji Church Dominant Modern Reconstruction — On 17 March 1865 villagers from Urakami came into the new French church at Oura in Nagasaki. They told its priest, Bernard Petitjean, that they shared his faith. Stephen Turnbull's study of Japan's hidden Christians tells the story.
  • The Church of the East & the Chaldean Consolidation Documented Major — George Percy Badger, a clergyman who lived at Mosul, wrote in 1852 that the Pope recognized Mutran Hanna as head of all the Chaldeans. Rome let him act as patriarch only if he made none of his relatives bishops, and later sent him the pallium. Badger judged that this ended the old rule of passing the office down in one family.Lasting result: Rome recognized Mutran Hanna as head of the Chaldeans on one condition: he must make none of his kin bishops. That ended passing the office down in one family.
  • The Melkite Greek Catholic Church after 1815 Documented — In 1831 Rome told its envoy, Aloisio Gandolfi, to call all the Melkite bishops to a synod. They met that year at Zuq Mikhail, on Mount Lebanon. In 1903 Rome confirmed the patriarch elected in 1902.
