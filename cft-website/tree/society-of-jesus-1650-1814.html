The Society of Jesus, 1650 to 1814

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

After 1650 the Society of Jesus carried on the order told in the earlier entry on the Society. Two acts of Rome frame these years. Clement XIV closed the Society by his brief of 21 July 1773. Pius VII restored it by his bull of 7 August 1814.

In Paraguay the Jesuits ran towns for the Guaraní people. Such towns were called reductions. In 1743 a book about them was printed at Venice. Its author, Muratori, was the Duke of Modena's librarian.

The name Reduction, he says, meant a people gathered and brought to live as a society and a commonwealth (a plain rendering). His title calls the work Happy Christianity.

In 1717, he says, there were more than thirty reductions. They held 121,178 souls. Jesuit fathers had baptized them all. They were the only missionaries. He also says that raiders from the Portuguese colony of São Paulo had once destroyed fourteen Christian reductions in the Guaraní country.

In 1741 Pope Benedict XIV wrote to the bishops of Brazil, Paraguay and the Río de la Plata. He condemned those who forced Indians into slavery or sold them, even baptized Indians. The bull does not name the Society.

Then the Catholic kings turned on the order. Clement XIV's brief says the kings of France, Spain, Portugal and Sicily felt forced to drive its members from their lands. Among its charges are quarrels over missions in Europe, Africa and America. It also names "certain idolatrous ceremonies adopted in certain places". The brief does not name China. The pope then suppressed the company. The entry on the Catholic Church of the old regime tells that act from Rome's side. In 1703 a Jesuit, François Noël, wrote from Rome, where he had been sent over the honors paid in China to Confucius and to the dead. The entry on the Catholic Church in China tells the Rites dispute and the court mission at Peking.

In Russia some of its former members carried on. Pius VII's bull says that Francis Kareu and other secular priests had lived many years in the empire. They had been members of the Company of Jesus. They asked leave to unite in a body. Emperor Paul I had recommended them in a dispatch of 11 August 1800. By a brief of 7 March 1801 the pope let them form a congregation of the Company in Russia, and he named Kareu its general. In 1804 he gave the same leave to the Two Sicilies. On 7 August 1814 he extended it to all states.

The Jesuits also worked in Transylvania. A synod text of 1701, printed by Nilles in 1886, says the talks of union with Rome began by the work and skill of the Jesuit fathers at Alba Iulia (in a plain English rendering). The entry on the Romanian Greek Catholic Church tells that union, which was signed in 1698.

This entry ends with the restoration. The Society's nineteenth-century story is not yet told in an entry of its own.

Clement XIV's brief of 1773 speaks of the same company under Innocent XI, Innocent XIII and Benedict XIV. The Society restored in 1814 is the same order.

In Contact With

  • The Romanian Greek Catholic Church, 1698 to 1815 Inferential-Thin — A synod text of 1701 says the talks of union with Rome began by the work and skill of the Jesuit fathers at Alba Iulia (in a plain English rendering). An act of union of the Romanian clergy is dated Alba Iulia, 7 October 1698. This rests on one Latin edition of 1885, and its scan is badly damaged.
  • Chinese Catholics Under Edict and Treaty Widely Accepted — In 1703 the Jesuit François Noël wrote to his General on the China missions. He had come to Rome over the honors paid in China to Confucius and to the dead. His order then had three churches at Peking. A Catholic study of 1908 says the Jesuits stood in high favor at the court, as astronomers and interpreters.
