The Enlightenment & Awakening Era · 1650-1815 · Tonkin, Cochinchina

Vietnamese Catholicism — Vicars Apostolic and Catechists

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Jesuits sent under the Portuguese crown began the Catholic mission in Tonkin in the 1620s. Among them was the French Jesuit Alexandre de Rhodes. The mission ran under the Padroado, the Portuguese king's patronage over the church in Asia. Some estimates put Tonkin's Christians at 80,000 in 1639 and 350,000 in 1663. The figures vary a great deal. In 1651 a catechism by Rhodes was printed at Rome, in Latin and Vietnamese, at the press of Propaganda, the Roman office for the missions.

In 1658 Propaganda proposed Pallu and Lambert de la Motte as vicars apostolic for China and its neighbors. In August the pope named them bishops. Dutton dates their choice as vicars to 1660. Pallu took Tonkin and Laos, and Lambert took Cochinchina. A vicar apostolic answered to the pope, not to the Portuguese crown. A new Paris mission society, the MEP, was set up to send them priests. The first vicars wrote a rule book for missionaries, the Monita. It said that native priests should be chosen from the catechists.

Lambert de la Motte reached Tonkin in 1670. He ordained seven priests. He gave lower orders to 48 Vietnamese converts. A month later he held the first synod in Vietnam. That year he also founded the Lovers of the Cross, a community of women. In 1673 Pope Clement X accepted the synod's decisions. In 1675 Rome granted indulgences to their groups in Tonkin and Cochinchina.

In 1678 Tonkin was split into an eastern and a western vicariate. The Paris mission kept the west. The east went to the Dominicans after 1693. The Jesuits' old flock, the Padroado Christians, kept their loyalty to Portugal. When the Jesuit order was dissolved in 1773, they refused to obey the vicars. They were called schismatics and kept from the sacraments. In 1796 the priest Philiphê Bỉnh sailed to Lisbon to ask the Portuguese ruler for a bishop for them.

Sources

  • Adrien Launay, Histoire générale de la Société des Missions-Étrangères, vol. 1 (Paris: Téqui, 1894) (secondary) — A history of the Paris mission by one of its own priests. It gives the naming of the vicars in 1658 and the rules the first vicars wrote.
  • Adrien Launay, Mgr Retord et le Tonkin catholique, 1831-1858 (Paris, 1893) (secondary) — A history by a priest of the Paris mission. It names Rhodes as Tonkin's first great converter and dates the Lovers of the Cross to 1670.
  • George E. Dutton, A Vietnamese Moses: Philiphê Bỉnh and the Geographies of Early Modern Catholicism (University of California Press, 2017) (secondary) — Open access. It reads Bỉnh's own Vietnamese writings and tells the quarrel between the Jesuits of the Portuguese mission and the vicars.
  • Collectanea S. Congregationis de Propaganda Fide, vol. 1 (Rome, 1907), the entry of 25 August 1675 (primary) — Propaganda's grant of indulgences to the confraternities of the Lovers of the Cross in Tonkin and Cochinchina.
  • Alexandre de Rhodes, Cathechismus pro iis, qui volunt suscipere Baptismum in octo dies divisus (Rome: Propaganda Fide, 1651) (primary) — Its title page and license were read. Its teaching was not.
