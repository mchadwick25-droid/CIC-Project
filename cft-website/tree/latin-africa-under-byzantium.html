The Age of Monks and Empires · 533-c. 698 · Carthage and Byzantine North Africa

Carthage, today in Tunisia · Byzantine North Africa

Latin African Christianity under Byzantium

Byzantine North Africa, 533 to c. 698 CE — the church Justinian's armies restored, which promptly told him he was wrong, and went on arguing until the conquest ended the argument.

About This Movement

Audio Brief · Narrated Edition 1:22

Read along

Belisarius destroyed the Vandal kingdom in 533. The Nicene church of Roman Africa came out of nearly a hundred years of Vandal rule and waves of persecution. It got back its buildings and its bishops' sees. Within twenty years it was among the most stubborn foes Justinian faced anywhere in his own empire. When he condemned the Three Chapters to appease the Miaphysites, the bishops of Africa said no. Facundus of Hermiane wrote twelve books against his decree. Reparatus of Carthage lost his see and was sent into exile. The chronicler Victor of Tunnuna spent years in prison and exile for the same stand. Africa stayed a place where theology got argued at full volume. Maximus the Confessor lived here for some years, and in 645 he debated the ousted patriarch Pyrrhus in Africa. In 646 the councils of Africa threw out the empire's next bid for a compromise on the wills of Christ. Then Arab armies reached Carthage in the 690s. The old African church, which had given the Latin church Tertullian, Cyprian and Augustine, was gone within a few generations.

Documented Stories

Victor of Tunnuna Records His Own Imprisonment

Victor, bishop of Tunnuna in the province of Byzacena, wrote a chronicle continuing Prosper’s, one entry a year, in the flat annalistic Latin of the genre. Under the year 551 he records that the emperor Justinian summoned the African bishops to Constantinople over the Three Chapters — the writings of Theodore, Theodoret and Ibas that the emperor wanted condemned to please the Miaphysites and the Africans refused to condemn because Chalcedon had not. Reparatus, archbishop of Carthage and primate of Africa, went and would not sign; Victor records his deposition on a trumped-up charge of treason, his exile to Euchaita in Pontus, and the installation in his place of his own deacon Primosus, ‘who had promised to consent’. Under 555: ‘Victor, bishop of the church of Tunnuna, and Theodore of Cebarsussa, are sent into exile to the Balearic islands for defending the Three Chapters.’ Under 564: ‘Victor, Theodore, and other African bishops are summoned to Constantinople, and having disputed with the emperor and the patriarch in the palace, are confined in various monasteries of the city.’ Under 565 he notes the death of Justinian and, under 566, of the archbishop Reparatus in his exile, ‘worthy of memory’. He is one of only two African bishops of the period whose own words survive on the affair, and his method — putting his imprisonment in the same tone as a consul’s election — is itself the record of a church that regarded resisting the emperor on doctrine as ordinary duty.

In Its Own Record

The Nicene church of Roman Africa, back on its feet after nearly a hundred years of Vandal rule and waves of persecution. Its bishops still quoted Cyprian and Augustine. Both were great teachers of the old church of Africa, which Tertullian also came from. It was one of the main voices that stood against Justinian when he condemned the Three Chapters.

  • argued against The Byzantine Imperial Church (Justinianic) Documented Major — Facundus of Hermiane wrote twelve books in defense of the Three Chapters. He addressed them to Justinian himself. Victor of Tunnuna says that in 550 the bishops of Africa wrote to the emperor in their defense. He adds that Reparatus of Carthage was deposed and sent into exile because he would not condemn them.Lasting result: Reparatus of Carthage would not condemn the Three Chapters. For this he lost his office and was exiled, and his own envoy was made bishop in his place.
  • argued against The Byzantine Imperial Church (Heraclian & Iconoclast Eras) Documented — In 646 the bishops of Numidia, Byzacena and Mauretania wrote to Pope Theodore. They made their case to him against its defender, Paul, the patriarch of that city.

Legacy and Today

Why It Is on the Tree

When Belisarius destroyed the Vandal kingdom in 533, the Nicene church of Roman Africa got back its buildings. It had lived through nearly a hundred years of Vandal rule. Within twenty years it was saying no to Justinian's own compromise on the faith, over the Three Chapters. Bishops like Reparatus of Carthage lost their sees and were sent into exile for that stand. Arab armies reached Carthage in the 690s, and within a few generations the church of Tertullian, Cyprian and Augustine came to an end.

Legacy

This church left no successor. Latin Christianity in North Africa dwindled after the conquest and had all but disappeared by the later Middle Ages. It is the only major region of the ancient Christian world to vanish so completely. But it handed on its refusal. Its bishops wrote to the emperor to defend three sets of writings that they believed the council of Chalcedon had cleared. They would not condemn them. In 646 its councils wrote to Rome against the teaching of a single will in Christ. Their letters were read out at the Roman synod of 649, and the council of 680-681 later condemned that teaching. Its earlier centuries had already given the Latin church Tertullian, Cyprian and Augustine. What this last century added was proof that a church in the provinces could tell an empire no. The ruins of Carthage and of dozens of African basilicas remain.

Experience It Today

  • The archaeological site of Carthage, Tunisia — a UNESCO World Heritage site whose remains include the basilicas and cisterns of the late antique Christian city
