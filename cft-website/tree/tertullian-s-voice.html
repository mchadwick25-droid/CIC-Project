The Early Church Era · c. 197-220 · Carthage

Carthage, today in Tunisia

Tertullian's Voice

A person, told within The Latin Apologists.

About This Person

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Tertullian was the first major Christian author to write in Latin rather than Greek, which meant that a great deal of what he wanted to say had no words yet — so he made them. Trinitas, persona, substantia, sacramentum: the technical vocabulary Western Christians still use came substantially off his pen, and his formula of one substance in three persons became the standard Latin way of stating the doctrine of God. He wrote like the advocate he was trained to be: the Apology dismantling Roman slanders against Christians, Against Praxeas arguing that Father and Son are genuinely distinct, and On the Prescription of Heretics contending that heretics have no standing even to cite Scripture, since the Scriptures belong to the churches that received them. In his later years he grew steadily more rigorist — against remarriage, against flight in persecution, against what he saw as a church going soft. And aligned himself with the New Prophecy out of Phrygia, which is one reason the Latin church quoted him endlessly and never made him a saint.

In Its Own Record

Influenced By

  • Montanism (the New Prophecy) Documented — Tertullian of Carthage took up the New Prophecy's claims. He quotes a saying of the Paraclete given 'by the mouth of the prophetess Prisca'. In Against Praxeas he writes that his side withdrew from the 'carnally-minded' over the Paraclete.

Influenced

  • The Latin Apologists Documented — Lactantius wrote the Divine Institutes. In it he says Tertullian had already argued the same cause in full. He did so in his book called the Apology.
  • Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity Documented — Jerome says Cyprian read Tertullian every day and asked for him as 'the master'. Jerome had this from an old man who had been Cyprian's secretary. Augustine later said that many of Tertullian's works were still read, and that they were very well written.
  • Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity Documented — Jerome found Tertullian 'packed with meaning' but rough in style. He wrote that Origen should sometimes be read for his learning, 'just as we read Tertullian', taking the good and leaving the bad.

Argued Against

  • Modalist Monarchianism Documented — In Against Praxeas, Tertullian names Praxeas. He charges that Praxeas 'crucified the Father'. He also answers those who think that if the Trinity has an order, its unity is split.
  • Marcion / Marcionism Documented — Tertullian wrote five books Against Marcion. In them he attacks Marcion by name, charging that 'the heretic of Pontus introduces two Gods'.
  • Valentinian & other Gnostic Christianities Documented — Tertullian wrote a book Against the Valentinians. He traces the school back to its founder. He says Valentinus had hoped to become a bishop. When a confessor was chosen instead, Valentinus broke with the church.
