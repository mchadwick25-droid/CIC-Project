Armenian Christianity — the Mekhitarists and the Catholic Patriarchate

About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Mekhitar of Sebaste founded a religious congregation in 1701 with a purpose as much scholarly as devotional. To gather, edit and print the Armenian inheritance for a people scattered across empires. Driven out of Ottoman territory, he and his monks were given the small island of San Lazzaro in the Venetian lagoon in 1717, and turned it into one of the great centers of Armenian learning — a press, a library of thousands of manuscripts, and the dictionaries and translations that effectively created modern Armenian lexicography. Meanwhile, inside the Ottoman capital, the growing number of Armenians in communion with Rome created a problem the millet system had no room for. In 1742 Benedict XIV confirmed Abraham Petros I as Catholic patriarch of Cilicia, with a seat established at Bzommar in Mount Lebanon in 1750. The Armenian Apostolic Church remained overwhelmingly the larger body, but from that point there were two Armenian churches, and the argument between them was fought out in the politics of Constantinople.

In Its Own Record

Influenced

  • Armenian Christianity — the Zartonk Century Widely Accepted — In 1837 a French scholar wrote that Mekhitar's monks took the books they printed at San Lazzaro to much of Asia. Later the Armenian church leader Malachia Ormanian praised all they had done for the Armenian language and its books.

Argued Against

  • Armenian Apostolic Church — Etchmiadzin and Constantinople Dominant Modern Reconstruction Major — Malachia Ormanian, a later patriarch, writes that the Catholics, in turn, used the French envoys to coerce the patriarch. Catholic writers tell what happened to Avedik, who held that seat. He had fined a man on the French envoy's staff for no good reason. The envoy complained, and so did thirty leading Armenians. Then the vizier had Avedik beaten, stripped of his robes and sent off to an island.Lasting result: The French envoy and thirty leading Armenians complained of the patriarch, Avedik. So the vizier had him beaten, stripped of his robes and sent off to an island.

Opposed By

  • Armenian Apostolic Church — Etchmiadzin and Constantinople Documented Major — The patriarch's side in the capital moved against Armenians who had joined Rome. Malachia Ormanian, a later patriarch, writes that it got the Ottoman government to act against them. Catholic writers say the patriarch John gave the priest Gomidas up to the Turks. In 1783 a Catholic Armenian wrote that by the sultan's law his people had to obey the patriarch. The same law let him punish them hard if they did not.Lasting result: By the sultan's law, Armenians who joined Rome still had to obey the patriarch, and he could punish them hard if they did not.

Broke With

  • Armenian Apostolic Church — Etchmiadzin and Constantinople Widely Accepted Major — Two Armenian bishops, one of them Abraham Ardzivian, went over to Rome. Malachia Ormanian, a later Armenian patriarch, calls this a defection. In 1742 the pope approved a new Catholic see with Abraham at its head. A Catholic history says the other bishops of Sis met and said his election did not count. They chose a rival. As late as 1783, a Catholic Armenian wrote that his people still could not have churches of their own in Ottoman lands.

In Contact With

  • The Catholic Church of the Ancien Régime Documented Major — In 1742 Pope Benedict XIV wrote to Abbot Mekhitar in Venice to thank him for a printed Armenian Bible. In the same year, says the Armenian church leader Malachia Ormanian, the pope gave the Armenians in union with Rome a church of their own. Its patriarch was placed under Rome.Lasting result: In 1742 Pope Benedict XIV gave the Armenians in union with Rome a church of their own, with a patriarch placed under Rome.
