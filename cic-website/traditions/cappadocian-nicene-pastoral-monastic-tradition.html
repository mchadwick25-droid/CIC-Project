The Imperial Church Era · c. 325–394 CE · Cappadocia / Anatolia

The Cappadocian Churches

Cappadocian Pastoral-Monastic and Trinitarian Tradition

Open for conversation — you can sit down with this tradition now.

The Nicene-confessing churches of Cappadocia and Pontus, from about 325 to 394 CE, were carried by one family and one friendship: Basil of Caesarea, his sister Macrina, his brother Gregory of Nyssa, and Basil's friend Gregory of Nazianzus. A radical ascetic ferment had already dissolved households and turned slaves against masters before Basil built something else instead: brotherhoods bound by rule, obedience, and an open guest-door. Julian's brief pagan revival cost them a martyr at Caesarea in 362; the Homoian church that followed under Valens drove Gregory of Nyssa into exile, before this Nicene minority inherited the empire's favor in 381.

Chilo is a representative voice for this tradition, not a person who lived — built from its own letters and records, and honest about where they run out.

Who is speaking

A representative voice, speaking for a whole people

Chilo is a voice, not a person who lived — this tradition's own surviving voices (Basil of Caesarea, Macrina, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa, Emmelia, Eupsychius of Caesarea, Eustathius of Sebaste) given one voice, speaking the way a people speaks of itself: we, our, among us.

If a conversation touches real distress, a separate, clearly-labeled voice steps in to direct you toward real human support.

Where we are quiet

Whose voice you are really hearing

Almost everything you have from us was written down by one big family and one small circle of friends who went to school together. No woman among us left one line in her own hand. The woman we call our own greatest teacher reaches you only through her brother's pen, in a book he wrote after she died, giving her his own words to speak. We are not saying sorry for this. We are telling you plainly, because a record that hides its own gaps is not one you should trust about anything else it tells you either.
