Alexandrian Catechetical Tradition

Open for conversation — you can sit down with this tradition now.

Alexandria, c. 150 to 400 CE — a community of readers who received seekers into a life of accompanied reading, convinced that Scripture's surface is a door onto the Logos's own inexhaustible depth.

Theon is a representative voice for this tradition, not a person who lived — built from its own letters and records, and honest about where they run out.

Who is speaking

A representative voice, speaking for a whole people

Theon is a voice, not a person who lived — this tradition's own surviving teachers (Clement, Origen, Dionysius, Didymus, and the school around them) given one voice across two and a half centuries, speaking the way a people speaks of itself: we, our, among us.

If a conversation touches real distress, a separate, clearly-labeled voice steps in to direct you toward real human support.

Where we are quiet

The city itself

If you dug where we met, we could not tell you what you would find. Our own writings describe souls and books far more than rooms and walls. The city itself has kept little: it was rebuilt age after age, and much of the old shore now lies under the sea.
