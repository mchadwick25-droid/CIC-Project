What's Next

Where we're headed

Representative Modes

Choose how the conversation meets you

A pastor preparing a sermon and a student working through doubt need different pacing and depth from the same conversation — one default mode can't serve both well. We designed and built four modes for this: General, Pastor/Teacher, Academic, and Reevaluation, for someone actively re-examining their faith. The Representative wouldn't know anything different or say anything different in substance — it would adjust pacing, which examples it reaches for first, and how deep it goes into contested debate.

Right now, every conversation runs on the plain, single-mode Representative — Representative Modes, including General, haven't shipped yet. We designed and built all four once already, and testing found real, specific problems worth fixing before any of them meet a participant. That build was against an earlier version of this system, ahead of this year's full rebuild of the underlying engine, so before Representative Modes ships it needs to be re-integrated and re-validated against what's actually live today, not just picked back up where it left off. If you're a pastor, teacher, or academic using the pilot as yourself, real feedback from you is exactly what tells us it's worth prioritizing that work — the feedback form is how that reaches us.

More Traditions

Two new traditions are in development

  • Latin Pastoral-Congregational ChristianityCarthage & Hippo Regius — Cyprian, and Augustine's own congregation
  • DonatismA rival North African tradition shaped by martyr-memory

Each fills in a major, distinct strand of the ancient church not yet represented — ordinary settled parish life, and a persecuted rival tradition. Each gets the same full build as the seven traditions already live: its own representative voice, sourced vocabulary, and story material, built from its own letters, sermons, and sources.

Reformation Era

Seven more traditions, spanning the Reformation

A second wave of traditions is planned to follow the ancient set, spanning the Reformation — one of the most consequential periods of upheaval and renewal in the Church's history. The count is set; which specific traditions isn't chosen yet. That selection happens once the ancient-world set is complete.

Academic Review

Forming an advisory board for independent academic review

We're putting together an advisory board to facilitate independent academic review of each tradition and Church Family Tree itself. This is a top priority, and it needs real support to happen — both funding and volunteers. If you can help with either, we'd like to hear from you: info@churchinconversation.com.

User Access

Open to more people, including in more languages

Accessibility is one of our values. The real challenge: every conversation costs something to run, and that cost currently limits how many people can use the basic platform and Church Family Tree. We're building a reserve toward that — the Accessibility Fund — so the basic experience can open up to more people, including translation into other languages. See Get Involved if you'd like to help it grow.
