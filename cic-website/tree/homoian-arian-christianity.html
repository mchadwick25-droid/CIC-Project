About This Movement

For long stretches of the fourth century, the church the emperors backed was not the one that eventually won. The Homoians — from the Greek homoios, "like" — taught that the Son is like the Father according to the Scriptures, and refused to go further and call him of one being with the Father, on the grounds that Scripture never used the word and no council should force it on anyone; they did not call themselves Arians, and that label came from opponents who wanted them tied to a man most of them had never followed. Backed by the emperors Constantius II and later Valens, and adopted at the councils of 359 and 360, this was the imperial church's official position long enough for Jerome to write that the world groaned to find itself Arian. Its most lasting work was done outside the empire: Ulfila, consecrated bishop around 341, spent his life among the Goths, made an alphabet for their language, and translated the Bible into it. Through that mission the Goths, Vandals, Burgundians, and Lombards became Christians in this form, and Homoian kingdoms ruled Italy, Spain, and North Africa for generations after the empire itself had turned Nicene — the Visigoths of Spain going over to the Nicene creed at Toledo in 589, with the Lombards of Italy the last to follow, two generations later.

In Its Own Record

Formed From

  • The Gallic Nicene Episcopate — De Trinitate is the major Latin answer to the Arian case, and De Synodis a primary account of the Homoian conciliar creeds.
  • Latin Christianity in the Danubian Provinces — The same provinces, fifty years on: Valens of Mursa and Ursacius of Singidunum made Illyricum the base of the Homoian episcopate.
  • The Church of Antioch in the Third Century — Arius and his early backers were Lucian's pupils and used the name; the Arian party's leadership came out of this school.

Formed or Influenced

  • Anomoean (Eunomian) Christianity — The two wings split at Constantinople in 360, when the Homoian creed was imposed and Aetius exiled. The two are often confused with each other, but they are historically distinct movements.
