About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Dominic de Guzmán was a Spanish cathedral canon who, travelling through Languedoc, concluded that the church was losing the argument with dissenters because its own preachers arrived on horseback with baggage while their opponents walked and fasted. His answer was an order built backwards from a purpose: friars trained in theology, living poor and mobile, whose stated reason for existing was preaching. Approved in 1216, the Order of Preachers wrote itself a constitution of unusual sophistication — elected superiors, representative chapters, and a clause stating that the rule did not bind under sin, so that it could never get in the way of study — and planted itself immediately in the new universities at Paris and Bologna. Its convents of women, in the German lands especially, became one of the very few settings in medieval Europe where women wrote theology and were read.
