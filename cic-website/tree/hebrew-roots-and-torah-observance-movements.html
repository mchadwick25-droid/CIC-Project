About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Since the 1990s a largely gentile current of Christians has taken up Torah observance — keeping the Sabbath and the biblical feasts — in congregations, Torah study groups and, above all, online. It is not Messianic Judaism, which is a movement of Jewish believers, and the two worlds' own publications draw that line themselves. Its best-documented institution is First Fruits of Zion, founded in 1992, whose own teaching changed in public: in 2009 Boaz Michael and D. Thomas Lancaster set out in Messiah Journal a shift away from 'One Law' toward what they called Divine Invitation, and the One-Law side answered in print — the clearest dated record of the argument running inside this current. Digital ministries followed, 119 Ministries among them from around 2010, though founding dates for many of them simply cannot be established. There is an older root as well: the Sacred Name movement of the 1930s, out of Adventist and Church of God (Seventh Day) circles, whose periodical The Faith dates from 1937.

Sources

  • First Fruits of Zion's 2009 shift from 'One Law' to 'Divine Invitation': Boaz Michael and D. Thomas Lancaster in Messiah Journal (2009), with the One-Law side's published rebuttals (primary) — This is the movement's one well-documented institutional position change, dated from both sides of the debate.
  • First Fruits of Zion's founding (1992) and its published teaching corpus (primary) — The founding year is confirmed. This is the current's most clearly organized body.
  • The digital-first teaching layer: 119 Ministries and comparable ministries, and the congregational-network layer around them (primary) — Founding years for these ministries aren't given here, since they can't be confirmed and haven't been guessed. This layer is platform-fragile, largely undated, with no central confession.
  • The older Sacred Name layer: the 1930s movement out of Adventist and Church of God (Seventh Day) circles, and its periodical The Faith (from 1937) (primary) — The current's deeper root; details from standard reference. Bodies in this layer are assessed individually, on their own dated statements.
  • The thin secondary shelf, named honestly: religious journalism on the Hebrew Roots current, and countercult and apologetics literature (positioned, and flagged as such) (secondary) — No dedicated scholarly monograph treats this current; the Messianic Judaism scholarship covers the adjacent movement, not this one.
