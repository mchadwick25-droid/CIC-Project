About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

The Council of Trent decided what Catholic reform ought to mean; Milan under Archbishop Charles Borromeo showed what it looked like in a parish. From 1565 Borromeo set about redesigning a whole archdiocese around the question of what a parish priest was actually for — founding seminaries to train them, holding provincial councils and diocesan synods, walking his own visitations through remote alpine valleys, and issuing minutely detailed instructions on everything from the fabric of a church building to the enclosed confessional box, which his diocese did much to standardise. He also mobilised the laity, through Schools of Christian Doctrine in which ordinary men and women taught catechism to children and through confraternities that organised charity. When plague struck Milan in 1576 he stayed in the city, and his reputation as a pastor was made.

Sources

  • Acta Ecclesiae Mediolanensis (1582) - Borromeo's provincial councils, diocesan synods, and instructions (incl. Instructiones fabricae, 1577) (primary) — The model diocese's own legislation, later adopted across the Catholic world. Available only in Latin and Italian.
  • The Milan visitation records - Borromeo's and successors' parish visitations across the archdiocese (primary) — A Catholic counterpart to the Lutheran Saxon visitation records, showing ordinary parish life. Largely archival material in Italian.
  • Lay formation records: Schools of Christian Doctrine rules and catechist rosters (laymen and laywomen teaching), confraternity statutes and registers (primary) — Shows the renewal as ordinary people's weekly practice.
  • Giussano's Life of St Charles Borromeo (1610; Eng. trans. 1884) and the canonization-process testimony (1604-10) (primary) — Written for a hagiographic purpose; the canonization testimony is structured, guided evidence rather than a free-standing account.
  • Wietse de Boer, The Conquest of the Soul (Brill, 2001), with Headley & Tomaro (eds.), San Carlo Borromeo (1988) (secondary) — The current standard work on how Borromeo's discipline was actually lived.
  • Cambridge History of Christianity vol. 6, ch. 9 'Redefining Catholicism: Trent and beyond' (Bireley), with ch. 12 (Ditchfield) (reference)
