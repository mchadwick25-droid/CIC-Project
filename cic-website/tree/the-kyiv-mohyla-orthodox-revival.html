About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Orthodox Kyiv answered the Union of Brest not with a lament but with a school. A lay brotherhood founded one in 1615, and Petro Mohyla, metropolitan from 1633, turned it into a college on the Jesuit model — Latin, rhetoric, philosophy, theology. On the reasoning that a church cannot defend itself against arguments it cannot read. He also gave the Kyivan church its own instruments. An Orthodox Confession of faith, approved in revised form by a council at Iaşi in 1642, a short catechism, and a great service book, the Trebnyk of 1646, which regularised how priests were actually to celebrate the sacraments. It is the clearest case anywhere of a tradition taking up its rivals' tools in order to remain itself.

In Its Own Record

Sources

  • Mohyla's confessional corpus: the Orthodox Confession (Kyiv synod 1640; Syrigos's revision approved Iasi 1642; Eng. trans. from Overbeck 1898 onward) and the Short Catechism (1645) (primary) — The printed Confession is Syrigos's Greek revision, not exactly the original Kyiv text; the 1645 Short Catechism stays closer to Mohyla's own hand. Together they show Orthodoxy answering Reformation and Tridentine pressure with its own confessional formation.
  • The Trebnyk of 1646 (Mohyla's Euchologion) with its pastoral rubrics (primary) — The revival's most parish-facing text — formation carried out through worship itself.
  • Academy and brotherhood records: the Kyiv Epiphany brotherhood statutes and school records, the Mohyla Collegium's curriculum, disputation, and panegyric materials (primary) — Latin-style schooling put in service of Orthodoxy — the underlying identity tension is visible in the syllabus itself.
  • The apologetic-patristic layer: Kopystens'kyj's Palinodija (1620s) and the Kyiv Caves Lavra press's patristic and hagiographic printing program (primary) — The revival's scholarly self-defense and publishing arm.
  • Ihor Sevcenko, 'The Many Worlds of Peter Mohyla' (HUS, 1984), with current Kyivan-academy scholarship (Sharipova/Charipova) (secondary) — A classic orientation essay, plus current archive-based studies.
  • Cambridge History of Christianity vol. 6, ch. 18 'Latin Christianity and eastern Orthodoxy' (Dmitriev) (reference) — No dedicated chapter on this Ruthenian revival was found in volume 5.
