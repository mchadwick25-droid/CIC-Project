About This Movement

Synthesized voice — not a recording.

Christianity reached the Nubian kingdoms south of Egypt through competing missions in the 540s — a Miaphysite priest named Julian sent with the empress Theodora's backing, and a Chalcedonian effort sent with Justinian's. And the story of that race survives only because a Syrian bishop, John of Ephesus, wrote it down from the winning side. What Nubia itself left is not narrative but walls. The cathedral at Faras, built a few generations later,, buried in sand and rescued before the Aswan dam flooded the region, gave up a list of its bishops and more than a hundred painted figures: Nubian bishops, kings and queens, saints, some with dark faces and some pale, in a style nobody had seen before. When Arab armies pushed south after taking Egypt, Nubian archers held them at Dongola, and in 652 the two sides signed the Baqt. A treaty of exchange rather than of conquest, which left Christian Nubia standing on its own terms.

Sources

  • John of Ephesus, Ecclesiastical History, Part III, bk 4 (trans. R. Payne Smith, 1860; online tertullian.org) (primary) — The near-contemporary narrative of Nobadia's conversion (Julian 543, Longinus 569-80s), told from the Miaphysite mission's side — an outside (Syrian) voice, not an inside one. Compare John of Biclarum for the rival Chalcedonian claim on Makuria.
  • Faras cathedral excavations and murals (K. Michalowski, Faras publications, 1960s-70s) (primary) — The material inside-voice: bishops' portraits, painted liturgy, bishop lists from the rescued cathedral; publications scattered (Polish/French/German series; murals split Warsaw/Khartoum museums).
  • Qasr Ibrim excavation texts (Egypt Exploration Society reports and text editions) (primary) — Documentary and liturgical finds from the fortress-cathedral site; mostly later than this era but anchoring the tradition's texture; specialist publications only.
  • Derek A. Welsby, The Medieval Kingdoms of Nubia (2002) (secondary) — The standard accessible synthesis of Christian Nobadia/Makuria/Alwa from conversion onward - verified (British Museum Press).
  • Cambridge History of Christianity, vol. 2, ch. 4, 'Early Asian and East African Christianities' (David Bundy) (reference) — Reference chapter whose scope includes Nubia; verified in vol. 2 TOC. No dedicated Cambridge chapter for Christian Nubia in this era exists.
