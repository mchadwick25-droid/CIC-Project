Who's at the table

Who would you like to have a conversation with?

Christian traditions from the Church's first four centuries, each with one voice that speaks for it from its own letters and records. Ask any of them. You can bring the same question to more than one.

  1. ChloeHousehold Leader

    The House-Churches

    70–200 CE · Antioch, Asia Minor, Rome

    The house-churches of Antioch, Asia Minor, and Rome, 70 to 200 CE — the scattered gatherings that held together after the apostles were gone, connected by letters, formed around the table, still discerning who should lead and what the body's suffering truly means.

    Her record → — The House-Churches

  2. TheonCatechetical Teacher

    Alexandrian Christianity

    c. 150–400 CE · Alexandria, Egypt

    Alexandria, c. 150 to 400 CE — a community of readers who received seekers into a life of accompanied reading, convinced that Scripture's surface is a door onto the Logos's own inexhaustible depth.

    His record → — Alexandrian Christianity

  3. Mar YausepTeacher of the Covenant Order

    Syriac Christianity

    200–410 CE · Edessa & Nisibis

    Edessa and Nisibis, 200 to 410 CE — in what is now southeastern Turkey — a community shaped by persecution under Persian rule, holding together through covenant vows and typological reading of Scripture.

    His record → — Syriac Christianity

  1. MariusDeacon of the Letters

    Church and Empire

    c. 312-451 · Rome, Constantinople, Milan

    Rome, Constantinople, and Milan, c. 312 to 451 CE — the church's first century beside a throne rather than beneath a sword, working out what the emperor's favor bought and what it could still take away.

    His record → — Church and Empire

  2. PapnouteAbba (Elder)

    Desert Fathers and Mothers

    c. 320–430 CE · Nile Valley & Desert, Egypt

    The Nile Valley and the desert of Egypt, c. 320 to 430 CE — communities who left settled village life to wage a lifelong combat against the thoughts that trouble a person from within.

    His record → — Desert Fathers and Mothers

  3. ChiloElder of the Brotherhoods

    The Cappadocian Churches

    c. 325-394 CE · Cappadocia / Anatolia

    The Nicene-confessing churches of Cappadocia and Pontus, c. 325 to 394 CE — the households and brotherhoods around Basil of Caesarea, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa, and Macrina, who held, defended, and inherited the imperial establishment of the Nicene confession while feeding the hungry through famine and burying their own martyr.

    His record → — The Cappadocian Churches

  4. AlbinaWidow of the Household

    The Bethlehem Circle

    c. 382–420 CE · Rome & Bethlehem

    Rome and Bethlehem, c. 382 to 420 CE — a circle of scholars and ascetics who gave up wealth and rank to test Scripture's Latin translation against the Hebrew it was first given in.

    Her record → — The Bethlehem Circle

Start with your question

Choose who to ask →

Whether you come curious, with a sermon to write, or with a question about faith you've carried for years — there is a chair.

Write it in your own words. It stays in this browser tab and is sent to no one until you press Send in the room — for now, you will type it again there.

You will be in conversation with a representative voice, not a person who lived — built from one tradition's own letters and records, and honest about where they run out.

Or begin with one of these — questions people bring

  • I grew up being told doubt was sin. Was there room among your people for doubt?
  • Did any of you ever want to leave?
  • I pray and nothing happens. Did your people know that silence?
  • The people who taught me the faith turned out to be hypocrites. Did that happen among you?
  • Why does God allow suffering like this? Where was he when it happened to your people — and to mine?
  • I want to believe in Jesus, but I can't. What would you say to me?

These are from the project's own set of questions, the same set every voice is prepared to meet. Any of them, or your own, is asked in the room.
