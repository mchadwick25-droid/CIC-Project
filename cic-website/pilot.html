Be one of the first to sit at the Table

Before Church in Conversation opens more broadly, a small group of early testers is helping us see it clearly — what works, what doesn't, and where it still falls short. We're looking for four kinds of readers, and we'd like at least one of each.

Come and join us at the Table

What this actually is

This is an early build, not a finished product. Scholarly review of the historical reconstructions is still ongoing — it hasn't yet been checked by outside historians and theologians the way it eventually will be. You'd be testing something real and cared-for, but still a serious first draft. If something feels off, thin, or wrong, that's exactly the kind of thing we need to hear.

The commitment is small: one sitting (45–60 minutes) at your own pace, followed by a short 15–20 minute conversation afterward about what you noticed. That's it.

The four seats we're looking to fill

Which one sounds like you?

Seat 1

Regular visitor

Real Christian background, no academic or pastoral training. Tests whether this is genuinely readable and clear for someone without specialized vocabulary.

Seat 2

Pastor or teacher

Someone who naturally reaches for sermon-prep or teaching angles. Tests whether the encounter offers something substantive to someone who already teaches others.

Seat 3

Academic or scholar

Real training in history, theology, or a related field. Tests whether the sourcing and precision hold up to someone who knows how to press on a claim.

Seat 4

Reevaluation

Someone actively wrestling with faith questions — reconsidering, questioning, or reconstructing what they believe. This is exactly who "a doorway to sit with the Christian movements of history" is written for.
