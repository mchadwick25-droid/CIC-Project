License

What you may do with our work

For most people

You never need to ask

Reading, studying and sharing a page with a friend are free. So is using our work in a Bible study, a class, a sermon or your own family history. There is no form and no permission to get.

This page is for people who want to do more: reuse our data, publish it, or build something with it.

Who we are

One policy for all our work

Faithways Studio, Inc. is a public benefit corporation in Colorado. Its charter commits it to a public purpose. It makes Church in Conversation and the Church Family Tree, and keeps the library of old texts behind them.

This policy covers our data, our writing and our recordings. It does not cover the talks people have in the app. Our privacy page says how we handle those.

What is covered

Four kinds of material

Our data. This is the census of movements, with their dates, regions, lineage links, confidence grades, sources and IDs. It is free for everyone to reuse for non-commercial purposes, with credit. Churches, ministries and scholars can do more.

Our writing and recordings. This means movement pages, descriptions, link notes, narration and our method notes. The terms are the same as for our data.

Quotations from old texts. These are the words of writers like Athanasius, Optatus or Evagrius, quoted as sources. Each one keeps the license of the edition it comes from. Almost all are in the public domain.

Our names and marks. These are not licensed. You may use them only to credit us truthfully.

Free for everyone

Non-commercial use, with credit

Our data and our writing are published under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International license (CC BY-NC 4.0). In plain words, anyone may copy, share, translate, adapt and build on them, for any purpose that is not commercial.

You must credit us, link to the license, and say if you changed anything. Credit like this is enough: “From the Church Family Tree by Faithways Studio, Inc., release 1 (November 6, 2026), CC BY-NC 4.0.”

Ministry and scholarship

The Ministry and Scholarship Permission

Ministry and open scholarship often need more than “non-commercial.” A church sells a study book to cover printing. A seminary charges course fees. An open research project wants to include our links in a dataset anyone can use. This permission allows these, free of charge.

Who can ask for it:

  • churches, parishes, congregations and denominations
  • ministries, mission agencies and religious orders
  • nonprofits and charities, in any country
  • schools, colleges, seminaries and libraries
  • public benefit corporations and similar mission-locked companies
  • researchers and open scholarly projects, such as research databases, argument maps and reference works that publish their own data openly
  • individuals doing ministry, teaching or writing on their own, even when their work earns a little, such as a self-published study guide or a paid course

What it adds. In ministry, you may use our data and writing in work you sell or charge for, as long as the work serves your mission. In open scholarship, you may include our data in an open project under that project’s own license. Every claim you take from us must keep its confidence grade and its sources.

What it asks of you:

  1. Credit us, and name the release you used.
  2. Keep the confidence grade with every claim you take from us. Don’t present a contested claim as settled.
  3. Mark your changes, and don’t attribute them to us.
  4. Don’t imply that we endorse your work. Say “uses data from,” never “by” or “approved by.”
  5. Keep the release’s review status with the data.

If you break these, the permission ends for that work. The free terms above still apply.

How to get it. Fill in our short request form. Tell us who you are and what you plan to make. We approve requests unless there is a clear reason not to, and we reply by email. The form also asks whether you’d like to help review our data. That part is optional, and it doesn’t affect your request.

Companies

Commercial use by agreement

Companies that don’t qualify for the permission above may still use our data and writing commercially. This needs a separate written agreement, usually for a fee. Write to info@churchinconversation.com. The fee helps pay for the work and keeps it free for everyone else.

Our names

Names and marks are not licensed

“Church in Conversation,” “Church Family Tree,” “Faithways Studio” and our logos belong to Faithways Studio. You may use our names to credit us truthfully.

You may not use them to name your own product. You may not use them to suggest we made or approved your work. And no page, app or voice may claim to speak for us, or for a tradition on our behalf.

Not covered

Our software and methods stay ours

This policy covers only what our websites publish. Our software, our build methods, our prompts and our internal records are not licensed, and all rights are reserved.

The same goes for how our library is organized: which works belong to which tradition, and in what role. A list of sources shown on a page is covered as published work. The old texts themselves stay in the public domain.

Quotations

Old texts keep their own license

Most of the old texts we quote are in the public domain, so anyone may use them. A few come from new digital copies that have a license of their own. Then that license applies. Each quote in our data names the license of the copy it comes from.

Training AI

Not for training AI models

We don’t permit the use of our websites, data or writing to train AI models, unless we have agreed to it in writing. We reserve this right as far as the law allows. Our websites ask known AI training crawlers to stay out.

An assistant that reads a page to answer a person’s question is not training. Neither is a search engine.

How it was checked

Checked by AI, not yet by scholars

Each release of our data says how it was checked. Today, every quotation is checked word for word against its source file. Every claim has been reviewed by AI models working to our written method. None of it has yet been reviewed by an independent scholar.

When that review happens, the release will say which parts were reviewed and by whom.

Help us review it. Are you a scholar of church history, theology or the sources we use? If you’d like to help review our data, write to info@churchinconversation.com.

Versions

Numbered releases and permanent IDs

Our data comes in numbered, dated releases. Movement and link IDs never change. When we correct something, the new release says so, and older releases can still be cited. Please name the release you used.
