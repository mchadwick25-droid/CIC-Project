Privacy

What we do with a conversation

What's stored

Your conversation is saved

This is a pilot. This page says what we store, how long we keep it, and how to ask us to delete it.

When you talk with a Representative or at a Table, we save the conversation. That includes your messages and every reply. We use it to check accuracy, catch problems, and see how real conversations go.

We do not ask for your name or email. We do not collect them unless you send them to us yourself, for example by writing to us.

Each conversation gets a session code. We store only a scrambled version of that code (a hash), not the code itself. The code lets you come back to your conversation later.

Your messages are stored in the same database as that scrambled code and your visitor ID (see below). A conversation can be linked to both.

We also keep a usage log. It records the cost and size of each model call, the time, and which tradition was used. It does not hold the text of your messages.

Contributions

Giving goes through Stripe

If you give to the project, you pay on Stripe's own pages. Stripe holds your name, email and payment details. Our conversation system never receives them, and nothing links a contribution to a conversation.

The visitor cookie

One cookie, to keep the free allowance fair

When you start a conversation, our server may set a cookie named cic_visitor. It holds a random ID and a signature that proves we issued it. It holds no name, email, or device details.

The cookie does one job. It lets us count unique visitors.

The cookie does two jobs. It keeps track of your free allowance, described in the next section. It also lets us count unique visitors.

The cookie is only sent to this site. It does not follow you to other sites, and we do not use it for advertising. You do not need an account.

It lasts up to 400 days. You can delete it in your browser settings at any time. If you do, you get a new ID.

Get more conversations

The free allowance and the pilot pack

You can buy more tokens. You pay on Stripe's own page. Stripe asks for your name, email and card, and we never see your card details.

We keep only the payment's ID and its amount, with the code it made, and never your name or email. The amount and ID are deleted after 90 days. The ID also stays with the code's record until that record is deleted. Stripe's own privacy policy covers what Stripe keeps.

Your free allowance is kept for each visitor. We store a scrambled value made from your visitor ID, the day you first used it, and how much you have used. If your browser has no visitor cookie, we use your network address instead, scrambled the same way. We delete this record 30 days after that first day.

To keep the free allowance fair, we also count messages that got a safety check and no reply from a Representative. We keep one count for your visitor ID and one for your network address, each as a scrambled value and a number. We delete each count 30 days after the first message it counted.

If you join the pilot, you get a code that holds your free pack of tokens. The code itself stays in your browser. We store only a scrambled version of it (a hash). With it we keep how many tokens it holds and has used, the day it was made, and the week it was last used.

We do not store the code with your visitor ID or your conversations. We do not record which conversation used it. We keep the record while the code still has tokens. Once they are used up, we delete it 30 days after the end of the week it was last used.

So that each connection gets one pack, we keep your network address only in scrambled form. With it we keep how many packs it has had and the day of the first. We delete it 90 days after that day.

When you join, you choose which description fits you best. We keep only a count of each answer. Your answer is not linked to you or to your code.

A backup can keep any of these records for up to 14 more days. Someone who held our server's secret key, and knew your visitor ID or network address, could match it to these records. If you delete your cookie, we can no longer match you to your free allowance.

Accounts

An account is optional

You do not need an account to use this site. An account keeps your conversations and your tokens in one place, and lets you open them on any device.

Accounts are for people 18 or over. We ask you to confirm it with one click. We do not ask for a date of birth, and we do not keep one. A way in for younger people, with a parent or teacher, is not open yet.

Accounts

What an account holds

We keep no name. You type your email address to sign in. We keep it encrypted, and we use it only to send you sign-in codes and notices about your account and your purchases. We never use it for marketing.

We also keep a scrambled copy of your address, made with a secret key. It lets us find your account when you sign in. It cannot be turned back into your address.

Your account holds the day you made it, the day your free tokens refill, the week you last used it, and that you confirmed you are 18 or over. It holds the ways you sign in, and a list of the conversations you saved. It also holds a scrambled link to each code you add, so your tokens follow you to any device.

Stripe knows who paid. What you say in a conversation is never stored with your payment. Without an account, it is never stored with your code either. With an account, your account joins your tokens and your conversations. That is how they follow you from one device to another.

Nothing about you is carried from one conversation to the next.

Accounts

Signing in

You sign in with a code we email you. It works once, for ten minutes. There is no password.

You can also sign in with Google or Apple, if we offer it. They learn that you use this service. We keep a scrambled copy of the identifier they give us, and nothing else from them.

Accounts

A way back

After your first saved conversation, we offer you a recovery code. We keep only a scrambled copy of it, so we cannot show it again.

If you use it to move to a new email address, we wait a week and we tell your old address first. That gives you time to stop it.

If you lose both your email and your recovery code, we cannot get your account back.

Accounts

Download or delete

In your account you can download your data, delete one conversation, or delete your whole account. Downloading your data and deleting your account each ask for a fresh code first, so no one else can do it from your screen.

The download gives you the facts we hold about your account. Your saved conversations are not in it yet.

When you delete your account, it is closed the same day, and you are signed out. You have 90 days to re-open it by signing in. Until then we keep everything in it. After 90 days we erase it for good.

Erasing removes every saved conversation and its words. It also removes the review copy of them. Your email address goes too, both the encrypted copy and the scrambled one. So does the link between your account and your codes.

Some things stay for a while. Stripe keeps its own record of any payment. The token system keeps each code's row and its payment reference, with no name. Our email provider and our host may keep short records that a message was sent or a page was fetched. While your account waits to be erased, our backup storage holds a scrambled mark of its random ID and its last day. That way a restored backup cannot re-open it. Once it is erased, a scrambled mark of its random ID stays for 16 days. That way a restored backup cannot bring it back. Backups drop deleted data within 14 days. Backups of account files are encrypted before they leave our server.

If you do not use your account for two years, we close it the same way. You have 90 days to re-open it. After that we delete it, with everything saved in it.

If someone gives us an email address, we can check whether it has an account. We can do that only with that address.

Retention

Kept for 90 days

We delete a conversation after 90 days. Copies we make to check quality are deleted on the same schedule.

If a conversation sits untouched for 7 days, we mark it as idle. That is a label only. You can still pick the conversation back up with your code.

Every day we back up our databases to private object storage. The backups include saved conversations. We keep the last 14 days of backups, and older ones are deleted automatically. So a deleted conversation can stay in a backup for up to 14 more days.

Quality checks

Kept apart and scrubbed

To improve the conversations, we keep a separate copy of questions and replies. We remove names, email addresses, phone numbers and web addresses from it. It is kept apart from your conversation. We delete it after 90 days.

The scrubbing does not catch everything. A place, a job or a story about yourself may stay in the copy.

When we erase your account, we delete the review copy of your saved conversations too.

Who reads it

Reviewed by our team, for quality

Our project team reviews conversations to check that each reply is accurate and true to its sources. Conversations never ask for your name or email, so we don't know who you are unless you tell us.

We do not sell your conversations, and we never quote from them.

Feedback

What we keep when you tell us something

Under each reply in a conversation, you can mark that it helped. We then store which conversation and which reply it was, and the sources behind it.

You can also mark that a reply was not clear or not accurate. We then store which conversation and which sentence it was, the sources behind that sentence, the reason you chose, and any line you added.

In neither record do we store the words of the reply or of your conversation.

When you use “Tell us what you think,” we store the page you were on, what you wrote, your answer about quoting, and your email if you gave one.

We keep each item until a person has checked it, and for 90 days after that. We delete your email when we reply, or when we close the item if no reply is owed.

We quote a comment only if you said we may, and only in the way you chose. A mark on a reply is never quoted.

At the bottom of a conversation, a Table and each Church Family Tree page, you can press a like or send a short report about accuracy or craft. A like stores the page it was pressed on and the time. A report stores the page, the kind of problem (accuracy or craft), your note, the time and, only if you give it, your email. Neither stores the words of any conversation or sets a cookie. Your browser remembers locally that you pressed a like, so you do not press it twice. We keep a report until a person has checked it, and for 90 days after that. We delete your email when we reply, or when we close the report.

Services

Who helps us run it

Replies are written by Anthropic's Claude, running on Amazon's servers. Amazon keeps your messages out of AI training.

Render hosts our server. Cloudflare hosts this website and our backups.

Amazon's email service sends our sign-in emails. Google or Apple see your sign-in only if you choose them.

We count visits to this website to see which pages are read. This count uses no cookies and does not identify you.

Deletion

Delete it sooner, any time

To delete a conversation before the 90 days are up, use "Delete this conversation" in the conversation. You can also email info@churchinconversation.com with your session code, or tell us roughly when you talked and which tradition you spoke with. We'll confirm when it's done.
