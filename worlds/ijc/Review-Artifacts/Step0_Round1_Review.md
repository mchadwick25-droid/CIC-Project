## Findings

### 1. [HIGH] The A1/A2 resolution rested on an invented rationale for A2, not A2's actual stated one

The draft's original argument for why A1, not A2, was the applicable test relied on a gloss ("A2 exists for a movement whose own confessional identity was substantially fixed before, and independent of, Nicaea") not actually present in the Methodology text. The Methodology's own Procedure section states "Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)" — meaning A1 is default and A2 is conditionally triggered, not automatically triggered by a pre-325 start date — and this line was not cited or used. A more textually faithful resolution: both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of the same movement (312–325 under A2; 325–451 under A1), and both clear.

**Status: FIXED.** §2 rebuilt on the actual Procedure text; adopts "both apply, to different phases" rather than a forced either/or.

### 2. [HIGH] Section 5's Process Finding 1 reported a "gap" the Methodology's own Procedure section may already close

The original Finding 1 claimed Section A gives no instruction for telling apart a true pre-Nicene confession case from a start-date-only overlap case. The Procedure's "as applicable" language is precisely that instruction, uncited in the original finding.

**Status: FIXED.** §5 Finding 1 narrowed to what's genuinely still unstated (how "applicable" is determined for a straddling case in the first place), with the drafting error named plainly rather than silently corrected.

### 3. [HIGH] Section 6's disposition claim didn't survive contact with the document's own Sections 1 and 5

(a) §1's original naming-note language ("naming/title decisions are a standing escalation category under cic-build-cycle regardless of source") invoked the Representative-identity escalation category for what is actually a world-name wording discrepancy — a different thing. (b) §6's "reported, not enacted" claim for §5's findings was false for Finding 1 specifically, whose resolution was used with stated confidence to decide this world's own Section A outcome — arguably enactment, not mere reporting, when that resolution turned on an invented rationale.

**Status: FIXED.** §1 corrected to distinguish the world's descriptive name from the Representative-identity category. §6 rewritten: since the A1/A2 resolution is now a direct application of existing Procedure text (per fix #1) rather than a novel methodology interpretation, the enactment concern no longer applies on the same terms.

### 5. [LOW] Minor historical overstatement: Damasus's pontificate framed as operating "against Arian/Homoian imperial pressure"

Damasus's own see (Rome, 366–384) was largely secure Nicene territory under Valentinian I and Gratian; the sustained Homoian imperial pressure of the period fell principally on the East under Valens, not on Rome. Ambrose, not Damasus, is the figure with a direct standoff against a Homoian imperial court (Justina/Valentinian II, Milan, 386).

**Status: FIXED.** §2 (A1) reworded to place the Homoian-imperial-court confrontation accurately with Ambrose, and to describe Damasus's own role more precisely (confirmation/assertion of primacy claims from secure territory, not resistance to an Arian court at Rome itself).
