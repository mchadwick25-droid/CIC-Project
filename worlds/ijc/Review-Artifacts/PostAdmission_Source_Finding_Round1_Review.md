**H2. §2.3's load-bearing "narrative, not content" distinction is contradicted by the vendored text the document says it read.** §2.3 rests the whole not-structural verdict on: *"Philostorgius supplies **narrative**, not Homoian **theological content**. The vendored file speaks directly to *what was being enforced*, twice, in the exact chapters the document cites and in creedal terms:
- Book IV.10 (line 226): the synod of Rimini "entirely rejected the use of the term 'substance,' but declaring the Son to be like to the Father according to the Scriptures, it confirmed that belief with the signatures of the bishops present."
- Book IV.12 (line 230): "Now, in the letter were contained the following words, ' That the Son is like to the Father according to the Scriptures.'"

That is the Homoian formula reproduced verbatim, with its enforcement mechanism narrated, by a non-Nicene writer — precisely the axis on which the divergence is drawn. The document's own §2.2(c) claims to have reached its conclusion by "reading the actual vendored file rather than reasoning from the label"; it read the file for *hostility* and not for *content*, then asserted the absence of content as its decisive ground. **H3. §2.3's dismissal of the other five candidates is asserted, not tested, and is wrong for at least two of them.** The document disposes of Candidates 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 in one sentence: *"Philostorgius touches none of their evidentiary bases."* No test is run. Against Doc_04's own text:

The Anomoean/Homoian split of 359–360 was a schism inside a coalition, and Philostorgius narrates it from inside that coalition. The document's characterisation — "a text written by a member of the party the Homoians themselves deposed and coerced," "misrepresenting both sides at once" — flattens that. **The §2.2 verdict survives untouched on legs (a) and (b) alone**, which are sound; but (c) is labelled "Most decisive" and is in fact the one leg that does not hold at the strength claimed.
