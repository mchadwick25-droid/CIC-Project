## 3. CT (Contested-Tradition) tagging — assigned, not defaulted

Per Constitution Article 26 §B, the [CT] tag applies to terms/claims subject to live scholarly contest within the relevant subfield. Assigned here, with each Contest Type specified per the Chunk Template's own requirement that this section never be left as template filler:

- ***primatus* / *sedes apostolica*** — [CT], **Relationship to present-day traditions**: the historical content of Rome's own 4th/5th-century primacy claim and its relationship to the present-day papacy's own claimed authority is a live point of contest between traditions that regard this world's own record as the origin of a divinely-instituted office and traditions that regard it as a historically contingent institutional development — this document does not resolve that contest and this chunk carries the tag so a participant reaching Level 3 sees it named as live, not settled.
- ***homoousios*** — [CT], **Meaning**: Eusebius's own hedged subscription (Doc_02 §2) is itself evidence of genuine, contemporaneous contest over what the term committed a subscriber to, not merely later disagreement about a settled 325 meaning.
- ***haeresis*** — [CT], **Historical scope**: whether "heresy" functioned as a settled juridical category from Nicaea onward or only became so gradually, alongside and partly through the Theodosian Code's own legal innovations, is itself contested in the modern scholarship this document draws on (Hanson, Doc_02 §3).
- ***presbeia*** — [CT], **Meaning**: the "New Rome" reasoning behind Canon 3 and Canon 28 is itself contested — was it a description of an already-accepted fact about Constantinople's status, or a novel juridical claim dressed as description? Leo's own rejection (Doc_01 §4) is direct evidence the claim was contested even at the time, not only by later historians. (*Nea Rhōmē*, the linked entry naming the underlying geographic-political fact this reasoning rests on, cross-references this tag rather than carrying its own separate Contest Type section, since the contest itself is over how that fact is used, not over the fact's own existence.)

No other candidate term carries a [CT] tag — *homoios*, *communio*, the Ambrosian formula, *concilium*, *Tomus*, *basilica*, and *martyrium* are all live, real terms in this world's own record, but this document does not find evidence of the specific kind of ongoing scholarly contest Article 26 §B requires for the tag, as opposed to the general historical uncertainty every term in this world carries to some degree.

## 4. Distortion Risk pattern, named once here rather than twelve times

Every chunk file states its own Modern Hearing/World Hearing pair per the Template, but one pattern recurs across nearly all of them, worth naming at the document level: modern readers bring a post-Reformation, post-Enlightenment assumption that religious and political authority are separable categories, and hear this world's own juridical-religious vocabulary (*primatus*, *communio*, *haeresis*) as either "really about religion, expressed in political language" or "really about politics, dressed in religious language." This world's own record does not support either reduction — the vocabulary is native to a formation logic (Doc_05 §4, §9) in which the two are not yet separable categories at all. Each chunk's own Distortion Risk section states this pattern in terms specific to that term, not as a repeated boilerplate line.
