## HIGH

### H-1 · `ijc.figure.ambrose` — the anti-conflation note is itself a conflation error

The note asserts three distinct Auxentii; on the dominant scholarly identification (Meslin 1967; Gryson; Hanson 1988; McLynn 1994; D.H. Williams 1995), the 386 opponent and the Ulfila-letter author are **the same man** — Mercurinus Auxentius of Durostorum, who took the name of Ambrose's Homoian predecessor on arriving at Milan. Ambrose himself supplies the identification in *Sermo contra Auxentium* 22 (vendored, npnf210:42417ff): "he changed his name so as to call himself Auxentius... He is called by one name in the parts of Scythia [Durostorum], he is called by another here."

**Why HIGH:** runs against the Homoian-recentering obligation rather than for it — if correct, the losing side's confrontation at Milan has a named voice whose own theological writing survives, materially changing the Absent Stories item (2) and `ijc.contested.homoian-content`.

**Fix requires:** rewrite the note to two Auxentii (not three), with *Sermo c. Aux.* 22 as warrant, at real (dominant-not-unanimous) confidence; propagate to `ijc.source.auxentius-letter-ulfila`, `ijc.contested.homoian-content`, `ijc.core.imperial-juridical` absent-stories item (2), `ijc.story.vigil-in-basilica`.

### H-2 · Callinicum (388) survives only as a euphemism; "synagogue"/"Jewish" appear nowhere substantive

`ijc.gravity.episcopal-independence` lists Callinicum as "the emperor pressed to withdraw a command, on sacramental leverage" — omitting that the command was to rebuild a synagogue Christians had burned, and the bishop's leverage shielded arsonists from restitution, per Ambrose's own Epp. 40-41 (vendored, npnf210:1462ff, 43050ff). This falsifies `ijc.limit.f6-e-earlier-windows`'s claim to have named "what was done by us."

**Fix requires:** a story or at minimum an honest gravity-manifestation rewrite naming the actual content; add to f6-e-earlier-windows's hard-evidence inventory.

### H-3 · `ijc.dw.f4-e-ancient-custom` — one city's practice widened to "the whole Western church" at maximum confidence, plus an unattested "antiphonal"

Augustine (verified, npnf101:13770ff) says the custom "is imitated by many, yea, by almost all" — not that it began there for the whole West. Hilary's Latin hymnody predates 386 by two decades (vendored npnf209, this build's own registered source). "Antiphonal" appears in neither vendored witness.

**Fix requires:** rescope to Augustine's own hedge; strike or footnote "antiphonal"; note Hilary as counter-datum.

### H-4 · The Chalcedon interval: a self-declared correction that lands wrong, three different ways

`ijc.story.tome-that-would-not-bend` claims to correct the legacy build to "days later"; `ijc.force.authority-contest` says "the same week"; `ijc.quote.peter-has-spoken` says "within days." Actual: Session II (Tome received) 10 Oct 451; Canon 28 passed 31 Oct 451 — **three weeks**, checkable in the vendored Percival volume itself (npnf214:20517, 20915).

**Fix requires:** the actual dates, identically stated, in all three records.

### H-5 · `ijc.gravity.primacy-claiming` — inverted: "Julius asserting Rome's authority to review Alexandria's judgment" — Alexandria issued no judgment; an Eastern council deposed Athanasius OF Alexandria and Julius reviewed THAT council's judgment

Contradicted by the gravity's own `manifestations` list and by `ijc.story.letter-that-outranked-a-council`, both of which get it right.

**Fix requires:** "Julius asserting Rome's standing to review an Eastern council's deposition of the bishop of Alexandria (341)."
