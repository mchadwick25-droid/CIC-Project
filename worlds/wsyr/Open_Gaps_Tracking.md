# Open Gaps — World (proposed code `wsyr`): Syriac Orthodox (West Syriac) Christianity

this world (Steps 0-2: Step 0 Movement-Scope Confirmation, Doc_01 World
Identification, Doc_02 Source Ecology), commissioned by the project lead
the same day ("world batch c"). Every gap below either surfaced during this
session's own work or was inherited unresolved from a pre-existing project
record and is carried forward here per this project's own append-only,
never-renumbered discipline (CLAUDE.md, "Track gaps and exceptions

renumbered when a later revision within this same session corrected an

4. **The Zachariah Rhetor compilation's own book-by-book own-voice /
   opponent-voice tagging.** OPEN, corrected in part, Revision 2. Revision
   1 characterized the compilation's core historical books (III-VI) as
   written by "a Chalcedonian author," Zacharias Scholasticus, reporting
   only "sympathetically" on this world's own figures — and rated them
   closer to opponent-voice than own-voice. An independent adversarial
   review found this backwards: the vendored 1899 edition's own
   introduction (lines 255-370) shows Zacharias wrote his own History
   (450-491) between 491 and 518 as a companion of Severus who also wrote
   a Life of Severus defending him, and states any conformity to
   Chalcedonian office, if it happened at all, came only later ("perhaps")
   when he was made bishop of Mitylene — i.e., Books III-VI are
   anti-Chalcedonian, Severus-sympathetic material, not opponent testimony.
   This has been corrected in the corpus-map staging file, Doc_01, and
   Doc_02. What remains genuinely open: the surrounding books (I-II,
   VII-XII), assembled by an anonymous continuator c. 569, have not been
   individually dated, characterized, or assessed for own-voice/
   opponent-voice status at all.

5. **Strand-singular finding — withdrawn and reopened, Revision 2, source
   attribution corrected Revision 4.** Revision 1 found this world
   strand-singular using a sequential-phases argument borrowed from `ijc`'s
   own reasoning. An independent adversarial review found this misapplied
   `ijc`'s own test (sequential phases are an Article 15 world-merge
   finding, not an Article 21 strand test) and missed real internal
   divergence in the vendored Ecclesiastical History: a Trinitarian
   controversy within the anti-Chalcedonian camp itself (Book I — John
   Ascunages, John Philoponus) and a later Paulite/Jacobite schism within
   Jacob Baradaeus's own hierarchy (Book IV). A second review pass found
   the controversy's own doctrinal background (the Ascunages creed, the
   Condobaudite name, the reported four-day public disputation) is the
   1860 translator's own editorial excursus (citing the 13th-century
   chronicler Bar-Hebraeus by name) and should not be cited as John of
   Ephesus's own words — but a third review pass found the prior fix had
   overcorrected into the opposite error (denying John reports on the
   controversy at all), when in fact John's own narrative, resuming at
   Conon's arrest, covers the controversy at real length in his own voice
   (his own refusal of the Tritheites' bribes, a synod debate, and his own
   naming of John Philoponus). Both corrections now stand together,
   precisely split by locus (Doc_02 §2). Doc_01 §6 withdraws the
   strand-singular disposition and leaves the question open for Doc_02's
   fuller treatment: whether either divergence is an Article-21 strand, a
   boundary case (the `syr`/Bardaisan model), or an internal controversy
   the movement's own mainstream resolved. Not decided by this session.

6. **Severus of Antioch's own doctrinal corpus — narrower gap than first
   stated, Revision 2.** Revision 1 called Severus's vendored letters
   "administrative correspondence" and named his own doctrinal
   argument "the single most consequential acquisition gap" this session
   leaves open. An independent adversarial review found the vendored
   Select Letters already carry real doctrinal argument: Letter I.1 (Book
   VI, Part I, lines 687-731) calls Chalcedon's doctrine and Leo's Tome
   "the life blood of the abomination of Nestorius" and explicitly rejects
   treating Chalcedon as merely a rejection of Nestorius and Eutyches. The
   gap is real but narrower: his separate treatises and homilies
   specifically (his Cathedral Homilies, trans. Maurice Brière, Patrologia
   Orientalis, various volumes) are not yet vendored — see the Source
   Readiness Dossier §3 for what is and is not yet confirmed against a
   real host. **What Mark would need to fetch by hand, if pursued:**
   confirm the exact archive.org (or equivalent) identifier and volume
   range for Brière's translation (Severus's Homilies span many PO volumes
   published across several decades, so PD-by-date status needs checking
   per volume, not assumed uniform).
