## 0. Purpose and Scope of This Document

This document performs Step 1 (World Identification, Boundaries,
Orientation) for the movement Step 0 confirmed. It sets the world's
temporal, geographic, and cultural scope; determines whether it is
strand-singular or strand-plural (Article 21); does preliminary forces
identification; and states this world's relationship to the one built world
already adjacent to it in this project's own lineage, `syr` (Syriac
Christianity, Edessa/Nisibis, c. 200-410). It draws on the census's own
substantial pre-existing research package for this movement
(`cic-website/data/world-census.json`) and on primary sources vendored and
directly spot-verified this session (see §2 note and Doc_02 for the full
account) — not on secondary summary alone.

## 1. Distinct World Criteria

**What recurring formation ecology exists?** A church that formed its own
identity, structure, and self-understanding almost entirely around
sustaining ordained ministry and communal cohesion **without state
recognition, and for long stretches under active state suppression** —
first as one contested party among several after 451, then, from Justinian's
reign (527-565) onward, as a communion Roman law did not recognize as
Christian at all. Authority was carried less by settled territorial sees
(Severus, this world's own foremost theologian and its last legitimately
appointed Chalcedon-era patriarch of Antioch, spent his last two decades in
exile, 518-538, never returning to his own see) than by mobile, personally
risked ordination: Jacob Baradaeus's decades of disguised travel consecrating
bishops and priests for a hierarchy with no legal right to exist. Ghassanid
Arab royal patronage (Al-Harith ibn Jabalah) and covert court protection
(the empress Theodora) supplied the political cover that sustained
ministry an emperor's own laws forbade.

**What recurring gravities exist?** (Provisional — full classification is
Doc_04's job, not this document's, but the candidates visible even at this
orientation stage are:) a Christological formula defended at real personal
cost as the movement's own doctrinal center of gravity (Severus's own
writing and exile); itinerant, personally-risked ordination as the
institution-sustaining mechanism, replacing settled territorial episcopal
succession as the movement's practical mode of continuity; and dependence on
external, non-Roman political patronage (Ghassanid Arab kingship) for the
protection ordinary imperial law would not supply — a structural feature
`syr` and `ijc`, this project's other two built worlds nearest this one in
time, do not share in anything like the same degree.

**What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** Most sharply from
`ijc` (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, 312-451): `ijc`'s whole
formation logic is church office becoming a form of *state-aligned* power
(the Constantinian alliance, papal jurisdictional consolidation); this
world's formation logic is a church sustaining itself in the *forced
absence* of state alignment, for at least one long stretch (roughly
535-565, and again intermittently after) in the *active denial* of legal
existence altogether. From `syr`: different problem entirely — `syr` is a
frontier ecology finding its own linguistic-literary voice against local
rivals (Marcion, Bardaisan, Mani) before any institutional crisis; this
world is a formation defined by institutional crisis itself, precipitated
not locally but by an ecumenical council's own ruling. See §8 for the full
continuity-and-distinction argument, including the precise way this world
does and does not continue from `syr`.

## 2. Temporal Scope

**Beginning point: 451 CE (Council of Chalcedon).** Unlike `syr`, whose
beginning point required real argument against a legendary alternative (the
Abgar/Addai foundation-story), this world's own opening is unusually clean:
its identity is constituted by a documented, dateable act of ecclesiastical
rupture, not a gradual formation process retrojected onto an earlier
legendary point. Severus of Antioch was not born until c. 459 and Jacob
Baradaeus's own consecration is decades later still (c. 542) — this world's
own opening date precedes its own named voices' own careers, which is
expected and correct: 451 marks the rupture the movement organizes itself
around, not the date any of its own figures were first active. Philoxenus of
Mabbug (c. 440-523) is the one partial exception — born before 451, but his
whole public career (bishop of Mabbug from 485, exiled 519) falls entirely
inside this world's own window; treated as a boundary-adjacent figure whose
birth date does not affect this world's own opening, the same way `syr`
treated Bardaisan's earlier activity as boundary-adjacent without moving its
own start date. — **Confidence: Documented** for 451 as the Chalcedon date
and for the named figures' own dates.

**Ending point: 636 CE (Battle of Yarmouk).** The census's own working
end point is adopted, and independently supported by this document's own
reasoning, not merely inherited: Yarmouk (636) is the battle at which
Byzantine military power collapsed in Syria before the Rashidun conquest,
ending the Roman imperial state's own capacity to enforce anti-miaphysite
law in this world's own territory at all. This is a genuine
institutional-structural change by the same test `syr`'s own Doc_01 applies
(a synod or comparable act that changes the church's actual legal/structural
position, not merely a biographical marker) — though here the change is
external and political rather than an internal ecclesiastical act: the
persecuting authority itself loses the power to persecute. The census's own
`continuesAs` field points to a successor world, `syriac-orthodox-under-islam`,
not yet built — this document's own working position is that 636 is the
right boundary for the same reason 410 was right for `syr`: it is where this
world's own defining condition (illegality under a Christian empire that
could and did enforce against it) structurally ends, not merely where a
convenient round-number stopping point falls. — **Confidence: Widely
Accepted** for Yarmouk's own date and military-political significance;
**Dominant Modern Reconstruction** that it is the correct periodization
boundary for *this specific world's own formation logic* specifically
(a defensible, argued choice, not a claim of scholarly unanimity that 636
is the only reasonable cutoff — a case could also be made for extending
to the Third Council of Constantinople 680/681 or even later administrative
settlement under early Islamic rule; this document does not adopt those
alternatives, but names them rather than treating 636 as uncontested).

**Internal developments vs. transition, within 451-636.** Two candidate
internal hinges, neither treated as a world-ending boundary:

- **518** — Justin I's accession and Severus's deposition/exile from
  Antioch. A sharp change in this world's own fortunes (from a functioning,
  if contested, patriarchate under Anastasius I's tacit toleration to open
  persecution), but not a change in the movement's own formation logic —
  Severus continues as the movement's foremost voice for two more decades,
  writing from exile. Treated as internal, the same way `syr` treated the
  363 Nisibis cession as internal because the formation logic persisted
  across it.
- **542** — Jacob Baradaeus's consecration. The point at which the
  movement's own institution-sustaining mechanism shifts decisively from
  settled patriarchal office (already effectively ended by 518) to
  itinerant clandestine ordination. This is arguably the single most
  consequential internal hinge in this world's own window — worth Doc_04's
  close attention as a candidate gravity-defining moment — but it
  intensifies and formalizes a condition (illegality, dependence on
  irregular ordination) already underway since 518/527, rather than
  introducing a wholly new one. Treated as internal.

**Flag for Doc_02:** exact chronology of Ghassanid patronage (Al-Harith
ibn Jabalah's own reign dates and the precise circumstances of his 542
appeal to Theodora) and Theodora's own death date (548, per the vendored
Lives, ch. 47/49 material — see Doc_02) both warrant closer primary-source
confirmation before being treated as fixed anchor points for Doc_04 gravity
work.

## 3. Geographic Scope

**Core geography:** Syria and Mesopotamia under Roman/Byzantine
administration — Antioch (this world's own contested patriarchal see),
Edessa (where Jacob Baradaeus was consecrated bishop), Amida (John of
Ephesus's home city), and the desert-frontier territory under Ghassanid
Arab phylarchal authority, itself formally a Roman client structure but
functionally the one political space this world's own hierarchy could
depend on.

**Political status.** Unlike `syr`, whose geographic scope genuinely spans
two empires (Roman Osroene and Sasanian Persia) with materially different
political conditions on each side, this world's own core geography sits
entirely inside the Eastern Roman/Byzantine Empire throughout its own
window — the defining political fact here is not a frontier between two
states but this world's own relationship to the *single* state whose
territory it occupies: sometimes tolerated, sometimes actively outlawed,
never simply outside Roman authority the way, say, Aphrahat's Persian
context was genuinely outside it. The Ghassanids complicate this only
partially: they were Rome's own federate allies (a *foederati* client
relationship, not an independent state), so Ghassanid patronage is better
characterized as this world's own hierarchy finding shelter within a
semi-autonomous corner of Roman client structure, not outside the empire
altogether. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the Ghassanids' own
federate status; **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for this document's own
characterization of what that meant functionally for this world's own
hierarchy (a considered synthesis, not a claim every source states this
exact way).

**A structural asymmetry with `syr`, worth naming directly.** `syr`'s own
geographic-scope section (Doc_01 §3) had to argue against a Step 0
characterization ("outside the Roman imperial frame") that turned out not to
fit Edessa well. This world presents the opposite risk: it would be easy to
default to "a persecuted church, therefore outside imperial structures," and
that would be equally imprecise. This world's own hierarchy operated
*within* Roman provincial geography throughout — Severus's own see was a
major Roman provincial capital; John of Ephesus operated *as an imperial
agent* in Justinian's own service even while his own communion was outlawed
(see §7, and the census's own second documented story, verified in Doc_02).
The correct characterization is not "outside the empire" but **a
communion inside the empire's own territory, alternately tolerated and
outlawed by the same state, whose survival depended on exploiting the
empire's own internal inconsistencies (a sympathetic empress, a federate
buffer zone) rather than on existing beyond its reach.**

## 4. Cultural Scope

**Language.** Syriac is this world's own primary literary and liturgical
language, continuous with `syr`'s own linguistic tradition (both are
Christian Aramaic dialects descending from the same Edessene literary
Syriac) — but this world's own leading voice, Severus of Antioch, wrote
primarily in **Greek**; his surviving corpus is known today mainly through
early Syriac translation (the vendored Select Letters, Book VI, is itself
Severus's Greek letters in Athanasius of Nisibis's Syriac translation, per
that file's own title page). This is a real complication for a "Syriac
Christianity" world-family label, worth stating plainly rather than
smoothing over: this world's own foremost theologian is transmitted to us
almost entirely in translation from a language (Greek) that was not, in his
own case, a barrier to full participation in the tradition his community
built. By contrast, John of Ephesus (Syriac-writing throughout) and
Philoxenus of Mabbug (Syriac-writing, and himself one of the tradition's
influential Syriac Bible revisers, per the wider scholarship — not yet
independently verified against a vendored source this session) represent
the world's own vernacular-Syriac literary register directly. — **Confidence:
Widely Accepted** for Severus's own Greek authorship and its Syriac
transmission; **flagged for Doc_02/Doc_03** to track own-language
composition versus translation-transmission per figure, rather than
treating "Syriac" as a uniform linguistic fact about every voice in this
world.

**Liturgical inheritance.** The corpus-map's existing (pre-this-session)
entry for the Divine Liturgy of St James (`role: tradition, confidence:
provisional`) names it "the ancestral anaphora of the West Syriac (Syriac
Orthodox) church" — real, but the vendored text (in `anf07`) is a Greek
liturgical tradition (the Jerusalem/Antiochene rite) whose received form
postdates its own apostolic attribution and predates this world's own
opening by composition (`COVERAGE` in `engine/m1/cross_world.py` lists
`anf07`'s own span as 90-380 CE). It documents ancestry and continuity, not
this world's own lived liturgical practice within 451-636 specifically —
carried forward as an open characterization question for Doc_02, not
resolved here.

**Confessional self-understanding.** Per Step 0 §1, this world's own
confession affirms the Nicene-Constantinopolitan floor directly; its own
distinctive vocabulary is Christological (the miaphysite formula), not
Trinitarian. "Monophysite" is avoided as this document's own working label
per the same reasoning; the tradition's own attested self-designation(s)
remain a Doc_02 task (see Step 0 §1).

## 5. Boundary-Adjacent Figures and the Source-Fidelity Discipline They Require

Three vendored sources this session carry real complication for this
world's own boundaries, named here rather than left implicit for Doc_02 to
discover cold:

- **Philoxenus of Mabbug** (c. 440-523): born before 451, his episcopate
  and exile fall entirely inside this world's window (§2). His *Discourses*
  (vendored, Budge 1894) are ascetic-spiritual homilies, not Christological
  polemic — a source record citing him for this world's own doctrinal
  center of gravity should look to his separate doctrinal corpus (not
  vendored this session), not assume the Discourses carry it.
- **The Chronicle of Joshua the Stylite** (Wright 1882, newly vendored this
  session): describes 494/5-506, inside this world's own window and core
  territory (Edessa), but its own chronicler's theological allegiance is
  not established either way — unlike the Chronicle of Edessa (explicitly
  Chalcedonian per its own introduction), this chronicle reads as civically
  focused rather than confessionally argued on either side of 451. Its
  corpus-map role (`context` vs. `tradition`) is left an open question
  (see corpus-map staging note), not resolved by this document.
- **The Syriac Chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene**
  (Hamilton & Brooks 1899, newly vendored): a composite compilation whose
  core historical books (III-VI) derive from a Chalcedonian author
  (Zacharias Scholasticus) sympathetically reporting on this world's own
  early figures (including Severus, his own former fellow-student), later
  assembled and continued by an anonymous miaphysite-sympathetic
  continuator c. 569. Own-voice/opponent-voice tagging needs to happen
  book-by-book, not as a single whole-work judgment — flagged for Doc_02,
  not resolved here.

These are not flaws in this world's own sourcing; they are the ordinary
condition of working with compiled, translated, and composite late-antique
material, and naming them precisely here is what keeps Doc_02's own Source
Registry honest rather than papering over genuine attribution complexity.

## 6. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

**Question:** does the evidence support distinct internal strands within
this world, or does it present as strand-singular?

**Candidate strand split considered:** a "patriarchal/theological strand"
(Severus, writing and organizing from exile, doctrinally focused) versus an
"itinerant/institutional strand" (Jacob Baradaeus, consecrating clergy
across the whole region, practically focused on sheer institutional
survival).

**Preliminary finding: strand-singular**, though this document holds the
finding more provisionally than `syr`'s own Round 3 disposition, since this
is a first pass, not a reviewed one. The two candidate strands are
temporally sequential phases of one continuous crisis response, not
co-existing alternative formations: Severus (patriarch 512-518, exile
518-538) establishes the movement's own doctrinal self-definition against
Chalcedon; Jacob Baradaeus (active from c. 542, a full four years after
Severus's own death) inherits an already-doctrinally-settled movement and
solves its resulting institutional-survival problem — no ordained
hierarchy left to sustain it. This reads as one movement moving through two
successive necessities (doctrinal self-definition, then institutional
survival), the same kind of sequential-not-parallel relationship that led
this project's own Phase One conclusion to merge Constantine's alliance,
Ambrose's confrontation, and Damasus-through-Leo's consolidation into one
world (`ijc`) rather than three. John of Ephesus, active across both
periods (writing under Severus's own doctrinal shadow, personally
consecrated and commissioned within Jacob's own generation's institutional
structure), is a real bridge figure whose own career spans both phases
without needing to be assigned to either strand exclusively.

**What would overturn this finding:** direct evidence that Jacob
Baradaeus's own itinerant-ordination program represented a genuinely
different authority theory or ecclesiology from Severus's own — rather than
a practical adaptation forced by circumstance onto an already-shared
theological identity. This is a real, testable question Doc_02's fuller
source work (especially a closer reading of Jacob's own portrait in the
Lives, and of whatever Severus himself wrote about the proper theory of
ordination before his own death) should test directly, not assume answered
by this preliminary reading. — **Confidence: Dominant Modern
Reconstruction**, explicitly held more loosely than `syr`'s own
triple-reviewed strand finding, pending Doc_02's fuller treatment.

## 7. Preliminary Forces Identification

**What generated this world?** A single ecumenical council's own
Christological ruling (Chalcedon, 451), rejected by a substantial party
within the Eastern Roman Empire's own Greek- and Syriac-speaking
territories (not only Syria — Egypt's own rejection is the larger, and
earlier-institutionalized, parallel case, deferred in this project's own
Phase One conclusion) on the grounds that the council's own "two natures"
formula inadequately protected the unity of Christ's person as Cyril of
Alexandria's own prior, broadly accepted language had stated it.

**What larger world was it embedded in?** The Eastern Roman Empire's own
fraught internal religious politics across four reigns of alternating
policy: Anastasius I's tacit miaphysite sympathy, Justin I's restoration of
Chalcedonian orthodoxy and Severus's exile, Justinian's own contradictory
policy (his wife Theodora's active protection of miaphysite leaders
alongside his own legal persecution of the same movement), and Justin II's
imprisonment of John of Ephesus. Also embedded in the broader
sixth-century contest for the Arabian desert frontier's own loyalty between
Rome's Ghassanid federates and Persia's rival Lakhmid federates — a
political-military rivalry this world's own religious history rides on
directly (Ghassanid patronage of a Roman-illegal church as one dimension of
that wider federate competition).

**What was it responding to?** Directly and explicitly, the Council of
Chalcedon's own Christological formula and the imperial state's own
enforcement of it. Also, on the evidence of John of Ephesus's own double
career (§3, §5), the empire's continuing missionary ambition toward
still-pagan populations within its own borders (Asia Minor) — a force this
world's own most important individual historian was himself, at the very
same time, an instrument of.

**What was it refusing?** The Chalcedonian settlement's own claim that "two
natures" was theologically necessary language, not merely one permissible
formula among others; and, at least for the period 527-565 and
intermittently after, Roman civil law's own claim to determine who counted
as a legitimate Christian minister at all.

This preliminary forces work frames Doc_02's scope directly: sources should
be sought that illuminate the doctrinal argument against Chalcedon in
Severus's own words (not only reported by opponents), the lived experience
of clandestine ordination and its risks, and the political mechanics of
Ghassanid and imperial-court patronage as sustaining forces. — **Confidence:
Widely Accepted** for the documented political/reign chronology; **Dominant
Modern Reconstruction** for this document's own synthesis of what the world
was structurally "refusing."

## 8. World Continuity & Distinction

**What does this world inherit from `syr`, precisely — and what it does
not.** This is the single most important boundary question this document
answers, and it requires precision rather than a single clean label.

`syr`'s own Doc_01 §8 names its own successor explicitly: "the
institutionally organized Church of the East" opening onto `syr`'s own 410
end point — and the census's own `continuesAs` field for
`syriac-edessa-nisibis` confirms this points to `persian-church-of-the-east-early`,
the Persian/East Syriac line, **not to this world.** This world does not
sit downstream of `syr`'s own declared 410 continuation in a simple
baton-pass sense.

What this world *does* inherit from `syr` is narrower and more specific:
Ephrem's own Edessene, Roman-side literary-theological legacy — the census's
own `relationsSummary` field states this directly ("Heir of the Syriac
tradition's Roman side (Ephrem's Edessa)"). `syr`'s own world spans *both*
the Roman (Edessa, Ephrem) and Persian (Adiabene, Aphrahat) sides of the
Mesopotamian frontier under one strand-singular formation (`syr` Doc_01 §6);
this world descends from the Roman half of that inheritance specifically,
while `syr`'s own declared successor descends from developments on the
Persian/Church-of-the-East side. **The two are sibling branches off `syr`'s
own bifurcation at Chalcedon (451) — the same council that constitutes this
world's own opening (§2) is also, from `syr`'s own vantage, the point past
which the Roman-side Edessene inheritance and the Persian-side inheritance
diverge into two separate successor formations, one of which (this one) sits
inside the Roman Chalcedon controversy and one of which (the Church of the
East) sits substantially outside it, in Persian territory under a different
state's own religious politics entirely.** This document treats "syr's own
West Syriac successor" language, if used loosely elsewhere in this project,
as needing exactly this correction: this world is not "next" after `syr` in
a single chronological line; it is one of two things `syr`'s own legacy
becomes, running in parallel to, not in succession from, `syr`'s own
explicitly declared 410 continuation.

Concretely, this means: figures and institutions this world shares with
`syr` are shared by common Edessene-Roman ancestry, not by direct
institutional succession. Jacob Baradaeus's own consecration as bishop **of
Edessa** specifically (§ Doc_02 will detail) is the clearest thread — the
same city `syr`'s own Doc_01 treats as its core Roman-side territory,
still functioning as an episcopal see, now inside this world's own,
different, ecclesial structure entirely.

**Where does apparent continuity (shared city, shared language family) mask
genuine ecological change?** Edessa itself — the same city, the same
Syriac-adjacent linguistic culture — but a completely different formation
logic operating in it: `syr`'s own Edessa is a frontier city finding its
theological voice against local rivals under no acute institutional threat;
this world's Edessa is a contested episcopal see inside an empire-wide
Christological schism, its own bishop consecrated in secret by a fugitive.

**What does this world transmit forward?** The census's own `continuesAs`
field names `syriac-orthodox-under-islam` — not yet built, and correctly
outside this document's own scope to develop, beyond naming that this
world's own 636 end point (§2) is precisely chosen to open onto it (the
transition from Byzantine-enforced illegality to a different political
regime's own, differently structured, relationship to this community).

## 9. Summary of Working Decisions (for reviewer convenience)

1. Beginning point: **451 CE** (Council of Chalcedon) — an unusually clean
   boundary, constituted by a documented rupture rather than a legendary
   retrojection.
2. End point: **636 CE** (Battle of Yarmouk) — the point at which the
   persecuting state's own enforcement capacity ends, by the same
   institutional-structural-change test `syr` applies; named as a defensible,
   argued choice, not an uncontested one (680/681 and other later dates are
   real alternatives, not adopted).
3. Geographic scope: Roman/Byzantine Syria and Mesopotamia throughout —
   unlike `syr`, this world does not span two states; its defining political
   fact is variable toleration/persecution *within* one empire's own
   territory, not a frontier between two.
4. Cultural scope: Syriac-continuous with `syr`, but with a real
   complication — this world's own foremost theologian (Severus) wrote
   primarily in Greek, known to us mainly via Syriac translation; "Syriac"
   should not be treated as a uniform linguistic fact about every voice in
   this world without checking figure by figure.
5. Strand Determination: **strand-singular (preliminary)** — Severus's own
   doctrinal-definition phase and Jacob Baradaeus's own institutional-survival
   phase read as sequential necessities within one movement, not parallel
   distinct strands; held more provisionally than `syr`'s own triply-reviewed
   finding, pending Doc_02's fuller test.
6. World Continuity: this world is **one of two sibling branches** `syr`'s
   own Chalcedon-era bifurcation produces — the Roman-side/Ephrem's-Edessa
   inheritance — running in parallel to, not in succession from, `syr`'s own
   explicitly declared 410 continuation into the Persian/Church-of-the-East
   line. Not a simple "next world after syr."
7. Boundary-adjacent figures requiring careful handling in Doc_02:
   Philoxenus of Mabbug (born before 451, career entirely inside window);
   the chronicler of Joshua the Stylite (uncertain confessional allegiance);
   the composite Zachariah Rhetor compilation (mixed Chalcedonian-authored
   and miaphysite-continuator material requiring book-by-book tagging).
