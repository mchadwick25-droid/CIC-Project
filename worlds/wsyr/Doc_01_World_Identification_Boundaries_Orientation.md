## 0. Purpose and Scope of This Document

This document performs Step 1 (World Identification, Boundaries,
Orientation) for the movement Step 0 confirmed. It sets the world's
temporal, geographic, and cultural scope; determines whether it is
strand-singular or strand-plural (Article 21); does preliminary forces
identification; and states this world's relationship to the one built world
already adjacent to it in this project's own lineage, `syr` (Syriac
Christianity, Edessa/Nisibis, c. 200-410).

## 1. Distinct World Criteria

**What recurring formation ecology exists?** A church that formed its own
identity and, for long stretches, its own institutional survival mechanism
around defending a specific Christological formula against Chalcedon
(451), while also, on a separate internal front, contesting a rival
Trinitarian reading some of its own members pressed too far (§6) — under
variable and often actively hostile imperial policy. Severus of Antioch,
patriarch 512-518, is this world's own foremost theologian; his own
deposition in 518 did not end a legitimate miaphysite patriarchal
succession at Antioch — Sergius of Tella (consecrated some years after
Severus's 538 death; the vendored Lives states only "some years after,"
and this document does not adopt an exact year not independently
confirmed), Paul (the "Black," consecrated c. 564), and later Peter of
Callinicum and Athanasius
Gamolo (who held the see into the 630s) continued it, though as an
unrecognized, parallel hierarchy rather than the empire's own installed
patriarch. What Severus's exile did end was legal possession of Antioch's
own physical see and imperial recognition — the movement's own defining
condition throughout most of this world's window is running a real
ecclesiastical structure the state did not recognize, not having no
structure at all. Jacob Baradaeus's decades of disguised travel (from
c. 542) consecrating bishops and priests extended this same
unrecognized-but-functioning structure across Syria and Mesopotamia far
beyond what a single contested patriarchal throne could reach. Ghassanid
Arab royal patronage (Al-Harith ibn Jabalah) and covert court protection
(the empress Theodora) supplied political cover an emperor's own laws
otherwise forbade.

**What recurring gravities exist?** (Provisional — full classification is
Doc_04's job, but the candidates visible even at this orientation stage
are:) a Christological formula ("one united nature," against Chalcedon's
"two natures") defended at real personal cost, and defended on more than
one internal front (§6); an unrecognized-but-functioning parallel
hierarchy as the institution-sustaining mechanism; and dependence on
external, non-Roman-law political patronage (Ghassanid Arab kingship, a
sympathetic empress acting against her own husband's law) for protection
ordinary imperial law would not supply.

**What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** Most sharply from
`ijc` (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, 312-451): `ijc`'s whole
formation logic is church office becoming a form of state-aligned power;
this world's formation logic is a church sustaining a real institutional
structure without, and at times explicitly against, state recognition. From
`syr`: different problem entirely — `syr` is a frontier ecology finding its
own linguistic-literary voice against local rivals (Marcion, Bardaisan,
Mani) before any institutional crisis; this world is a formation defined by
institutional crisis precipitated by an ecumenical council's own ruling,
and — as this document's own review found (§6) — by real disagreement
*within* the anti-Chalcedonian camp itself about how far the Chalcedon
critique should be pressed. See §8 for the full continuity-and-distinction
argument with `syr`.

## 2. Temporal Scope

**Beginning point: 451 CE (Council of Chalcedon).** This world's own
opening is unusually clean: its identity is constituted by a documented,
dateable act of ecclesiastical rupture, not a gradual formation process
retrojected onto an earlier legendary point. Severus of Antioch was not
born until c. 459 and Jacob Baradaeus's own consecration is decades later
still (c. 542) — this world's own opening date precedes its own named
voices' own careers, which is expected and correct: 451 marks the rupture
the movement organizes itself around. Philoxenus of Mabbug (c. 440-523) is
the one partial exception — born before 451, but his whole public career
(bishop of Mabbug from 485, exiled 519) falls entirely inside this world's
own window; treated as a boundary-adjacent figure whose birth date does not
move this world's own start date, the same way `syr` treated Bardaisan's
earlier activity as boundary-adjacent. — **Confidence: Documented** for 451
and for the named figures' own dates.

**Ending point: 636 CE (Battle of Yarmouk) — held, but with a real
complication named rather than smoothed over.** The census's own working
end point is adopted: Yarmouk is the battle at which Byzantine military
power collapsed in Syria before the Rashidun conquest, ending the Roman
imperial state's own capacity to enforce anti-miaphysite law in this
world's own core territory. This document's own review pass found a real
complication this boundary needs to disclose rather than ignore: **Roman
control of Syria and Mesopotamia was already interrupted once before
Yarmouk** — the Sasanian Persian conquest and occupation of Syria,
Palestine, and Egypt from c. 610 to 628 (Khosrow II's war against
Heraclius), during which Sasanian policy was, if anything, more favorable
to the miaphysite party than Roman policy had usually been. This does not
change 636 as the right boundary for *this world's specific formation
logic* (its own defining condition — a communion inside Roman territory
whose legal status the Roman state itself controlled — resumes under
Heraclius's own restored rule after 628, and 636 is where it ends for
good), but it means the document's own geographic-scope claim (§3) cannot
say this world's territory was under continuous Roman/Byzantine control
throughout 451-636. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for Yarmouk's own
date and military significance and for the 610-628 Persian occupation;
**Dominant Modern Reconstruction** that 636, not 628 or a later date, is
the correct periodization boundary for this world's own formation logic
specifically — a defensible, argued choice, not a claim of scholarly
unanimity.

**Internal developments vs. transition, within 451-636.** Four candidate
internal hinges, none treated as a world-ending boundary:

- **518** — Justin I's accession and Severus's deposition/exile from
  Antioch. A sharp change in fortunes, not a change in formation logic —
  Severus continues as the movement's foremost voice for two more decades,
  writing from exile, and a parallel miaphysite patriarchal succession
  continues at Antioch afterward (§1). Treated as internal.
- **542 onward** — Jacob Baradaeus's consecration and a Trinitarian
  (not Christological) controversy within the movement itself, associated
  with John Ascunages and later defended philosophically by John
  Philoponus, active mid-to-late 6th century (see §6 for the correct
  characterization — this is a dispute over how many "Godheads" the
  Trinity contains, applying a count-by-number-of-persons logic these
  figures' own opponents rejected, not a rival reading of the movement's
  own "one nature" Christological formula, which the Tritheist faction's
  own founder, Ascunages, explicitly affirmed in the same breath). Treated
  as internal — a real internal factional development Doc_04 should
  examine closely, not smoothed into a single undifferentiated "the
  movement."
- **586-616** — a schism between the Antiochene and Alexandrian
  miaphysite hierarchies, and (through the 620s-630s) Emperor Heraclius's
  own attempted doctrinal reunion efforts (eventually producing the
  monoenergist/monothelete formula) directed in part at this world's own
  leadership. Named here as a real gap in this document's own account
  that a closer Doc_02/Doc_04 pass should develop — not yet researched in
  any depth by this session.
- **610-628** — the Persian occupation (above). A real interruption to
  Roman authority in this world's own territory, but not, on the evidence
  reviewed so far, a change in this world's own internal formation logic —
  treated as internal, flagged for Doc_04/Doc_08 to examine further rather
  than assumed settled by this one-pass characterization.

**Flag for Doc_02:** exact chronology of the Trinitarian/Tritheist
controversy's own timeline relative to Jacob Baradaeus's consecration, of
the 586-616 Antioch-Alexandria schism, and of the Ghassanid patronage
relationship, all warrant closer primary-source work before Doc_04 gravity
work treats them as fixed anchor points.

## 3. Geographic Scope

**Core geography:** Syria and Mesopotamia — Antioch (this world's own
contested patriarchal see, held by a parallel unrecognized succession after
518), Edessa (where Jacob Baradaeus was consecrated bishop, in absentia,
while himself resident in Constantinople — he did not travel to Edessa to
be consecrated there; the see was his assigned charge, not his location of
ordination), Amida (John of Ephesus's home city), Tagrit and the Persian-
Mesopotamian frontier (where the movement also organized a hierarchy under
Sasanian rule — see below), and the desert-frontier territory under
Ghassanid Arab phylarchal authority.

**Political status — corrected from Revision 1.** This world's own core
territory is **not** under continuous Roman/Byzantine control throughout
451-636: Sasanian Persia held Syria, Palestine, and Egypt c. 610-628 (§2).
Nor is this world's own hierarchy confined to Roman territory even in
ordinary times — the movement organized a real ecclesiastical structure on
the Persian side of the frontier as well: Ahudemmeh, consecrated by Jacob
Baradaeus c. 559 as "Metropolitan of the East" for the miaphysite
communities in Sasanian Mesopotamia, based at Tagrit; this Persian-side
structure grew into the Maphrianate of Tagrit, formally established 629.
This document's own Revision 1 overstated the case when it said this
world's "core geography sits entirely inside the Eastern Roman/Byzantine
Empire throughout its own window" and "does not span two states" — both
claims are corrected here. The more accurate characterization: this
world's own core territory and hierarchy are centered in Roman Syria and
Mesopotamia, but the movement itself, like `syr` before it, did not stay
confined to one empire's own territory, and Roman control of even its own
core territory was not continuous across the whole 451-636 window.

**Ghassanid patronage, restated.** The Ghassanids were Rome's own federate
allies (a *foederati* client relationship), so their patronage is a
Roman-client-structure story, not an outside-the-empire one — this part of
Revision 1's characterization is not overturned.

**Corrected relationship to `ijc`, `syr`, and the empire.** Revision 1's
attempt to draw a clean contrast ("this world operated within Roman
provincial geography throughout... `syr`'s own scope genuinely spans two
empires... this world's own core geography sits entirely inside" one) does
not survive review. The more precise statement: this world resembles `syr`
more than Revision 1 allowed — both are formations whose own hierarchy and
membership extend across a Roman/Persian political boundary, though for
different periods and for different reasons (`syr`'s own frontier
geography is permanent across its whole window; this world's is a
combination of a standing Persian-side missionary structure and a
temporary 610-628 Persian occupation of the Roman-side territory itself).

## 4. Cultural Scope

**Language.** Syriac is this world's own primary literary and liturgical
language, continuous with `syr`'s own linguistic tradition — but this
world's own leading voice, Severus of Antioch, wrote primarily in
**Greek**; his surviving corpus is known today mainly through early
Syriac translation (the vendored Select Letters, Book VI, is Severus's
Greek letters in Athanasius of Nisibis's Syriac translation). John of
Ephesus and Philoxenus of Mabbug represent the world's own vernacular-
Syriac literary register directly. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for
Severus's own Greek authorship and its Syriac transmission.

**Confessional self-understanding — corrected from Revision 1.** Revision
1 stated this tradition's own self-designation was "not located in any
vendored source" and rated the question Inferential-Thin. This document's
own review found this wrong: the vendored sources use it repeatedly, in
John of Ephesus's own narrative voice, not an opponent's characterization.
"The party of the believers" (e.g. Lives, line 8166 and elsewhere) and
"the orthodox party"/"the orthodox" (e.g. Ecclesiastical History, Part III,
lines 709, 2807, 2906, 2966, and elsewhere) are both directly attested,
first-person self-designations this movement used for itself, in this
world's own window. "Jacobite," the outside label later attached to the
movement after Jacob Baradaeus, is a separate question this document does
not overreach on: the vendored ch. 49 account of Jacob's own nickname
(Doc_02 §2) is about a garment, not a claim about what the movement called
itself, and later Syriac Orthodox usage of "Jacobite" as a self-designation
is a real, later development this session did not check — this document
does not claim the label was never used by the tradition itself at any
point, only that "the orthodox"/"the party of the believers" are this
world's own attested self-designation within 451-636. — **Confidence:
Documented** that "the orthodox"/"the party of the believers" are directly
attested self-designations in this world's own primary sources.

## 5. Philoxenus of Mabbug — corrected characterization

Revision 1 characterized the vendored Philoxenus volume (Budge 1894, Vol.
II) as containing only ascetic-spiritual Discourses, "not Christological
polemic," and stated his doctrinal corpus was "not vendored." This
document's own review found this wrong: **the same vendored volume's own
table of contents** (not a separate, unvendored work) lists, alongside the
thirteen ascetic Discourses, a substantial body of his own directly
Christological writing — "The Creed of Philoxenus," "A Confession of
Faith," "Against Those Who Divide Our Lord," "Against Those Who Maintain
Two Natures," "Against Every Nestorian," "Against Nestorius," and "On the
Heresies of Mani, Nestorius, and Others." Philoxenus (c. 440-523, bishop of
Mabbug 485-519) is therefore directly available in this world's own
vendored corpus as a second doctrinal voice alongside Severus, not only as
an ascetic-homiletic one — a correction with real consequences for Doc_02's
own thin-evidence map (this world's doctrinal center of gravity is better
sourced than Revision 1 represented) and for any future Doc_04 gravity
work. — **Confidence: Documented** that these titles are present in the
vendored file's own contents listing; their content has not yet been
individually read and quote-verified this session — a task for Doc_02's
own closer pass, not claimed as done here.

## 6. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21) — substantially
revised

**Revision 1's error, named directly.** Revision 1 found this world
strand-singular using a "sequential phases" argument borrowed from `ijc`'s
own reasoning for merging three components into one world. This document's
own review found that test misapplied: `ijc`'s own Doc_01 states plainly
that sequential-phase reasoning is a *world-merge* finding under Article
15, not a Strand Determination finding under Article 21. Article 21 asks
whether *simultaneous* internal divergence in formation emphasis,
authority structure, or ecological orientation exists — a different
question from whether two phases of one movement's history happened in
temporal sequence.

**Correcting to the right test, this document finds real evidence of
simultaneous internal divergence that Revision 1 missed entirely** — both
directly attested in the vendored Ecclesiastical History, Part III:

- **The Tritheist/Condobaudite controversy** (Book I; see §2 above and
  Doc_02 §2 for the source-attribution correction below). A faction within
  the anti-Chalcedonian movement itself, led first by John Ascunages and
  then defended philosophically by John Philoponus, agreed with the
  movement's own mainstream that Christ himself is "one nature" — Ascunages's
  own creed affirms this directly — but applied a parallel logic to the
  Trinity, counting "natures and substances and Godheads according to the
  number of the persons," a move the movement's own mainstream rejected as
  tending toward three Gods. This is a genuine, simultaneous, named
  internal division — a Trinitarian dispute arising inside the
  anti-Chalcedonian camp, not a rival reading of the Christological formula
  that defines this world's own dispute with Chalcedon — exactly what
  Revision 1's "doctrinally settled movement" framing concealed. **Source
  note:** the controversy itself is real and documented, but this build's
  own knowledge of it comes from the 1860 translator's own editorial
  excursus (explicitly citing the 13th-century chronicler Bar-Hebraeus),
  not from John of Ephesus's own contemporary narrative — an earlier
  revision of this document wrongly presented the reported four-day public
  disputation as something John himself witnessed and reported; John's own
  narrative in this stretch begins only with Conon's arrest, after the
  controversy's own background has already been laid out in the
  translator's voice. See Doc_02 §2 for the exact locus and the
  correction.
- **The Paulite schism** (Book IV). A later split within the movement's
  own hierarchy — the census's own pre-drafted story already flags that
  Jacob Baradaeus's own bishops "fell into quarrels" in his last years,
  and this session's review found the vendored Book IV develops this at
  length, including Jacob's own death (578) occurring on a journey to
  Egypt undertaken specifically to help settle it.

**Revised finding: this document no longer asserts strand-singular with
the confidence Revision 1 claimed.** Two real, simultaneous internal
divergences are attested (a Trinitarian dispute within the
anti-Chalcedonian camp; a later hierarchy-level schism). Whether either
rises to an Article-21 "distinct strand" (a difference in formation
emphasis, authority structure, or ecological orientation) or is better
classified as an internal controversy/schism the movement's own mainstream
resolved (the Tritheist dispute is reported, per Bar-Hebraeus via the
translator's excursus, to have ended in the Tritheite party's own defeat;
the Paulite schism's own long-term resolution is not yet established from
the sources checked) is **left open for Doc_02's fuller treatment**, not
decided by this document. This document withdraws Revision 1's specific
strand-singular disposition and states plainly that Doc_01 alone cannot
responsibly close this question on the evidence reviewed so far.

**What Doc_02 needs to establish:** whether the Tritheist and Paulite
divisions are best modeled as (a) Article-21 strands within this world, (b)
boundary cases analogous to how `syr` treated Bardaisan (real contemporary
rivals the world's own sources argue against, but not this world's own
internal formation), or (c) internal controversies belonging to this
world's own single-strand history the way a later Christian world's own
internal heresy disputes usually do. This document names the question
precisely rather than forcing a premature answer.

## 7. Preliminary Forces Identification

**What generated this world?** A single ecumenical council's own
Christological ruling (Chalcedon, 451), rejected by a substantial party
within the Eastern Roman Empire's own Greek- and Syriac-speaking
territories (Egypt's own rejection, deferred in this project's own Phase
One conclusion, is the larger and earlier-institutionalized parallel case)
on the grounds that the council's own "two natures" formula inadequately
protected the unity of Christ's person as Cyril of Alexandria's own prior
language had stated it.

**What larger world was it embedded in?** The Eastern Roman Empire's own
fraught internal religious politics across several reigns of alternating
policy (Anastasius I's tacit sympathy, Justin I's restored Chalcedonian
orthodoxy and Severus's exile, Justinian's own contradictory policy,
Justin II's imprisonment of John of Ephesus); the broader contest for the
Arabian desert frontier between Rome's Ghassanid federates and Persia's
Lakhmid federates; and, from 610, direct Sasanian Persian conquest and
governance of this world's own core territory (§2, §3).

**What was it responding to?** Directly, the Council of Chalcedon's own
Christological formula and the imperial state's own enforcement of it. On
a second, internal front (§6), a rival reading of its own "one nature"
formula that its own mainstream leadership judged to collapse into
tritheism.

**What was it refusing?** The Chalcedonian settlement's own claim that "two
natures" was theologically necessary language; Roman civil law's own claim
to determine who counted as a legitimate Christian minister; and, on its
own internal front, a reading of Cyril's "one nature" language its own
leaders judged a real theological error, not merely an emphasis they
happened not to share.

## 8. World Continuity & Distinction — corrected from Revision 1

**Revision 1's error, named directly.** Revision 1 claimed `syr` "bifurcates
at Chalcedon (451)" into an East Syriac line and a West Syriac line, with
Ephrem's own legacy assigned specifically to the Roman/West side. This
document's own review found this is not what `syr`'s own Doc_01 §8 says,
and is not historically accurate. `syr` Doc_01 §8 never mentions Chalcedon
at all; it names "the institutionally organized Church of the East"
(constituted at the 410 Synod of Seleucia-Ctesiphon) as `syr`'s own
successor, and explicitly assigns **Ephrem's own hymnic corpus and
exegetical method** to that East Syriac successor line — not to a
Roman-side line distinct from it. The historical East/West Syriac
separation was not a single Chalcedon-triggered event either: it unfolded
institutionally at 410/424 (Persian church independence) and
theologically/administratively across 431-489 (Beth Lapat 484, Seleucia
486, the School of Edessa's own 489 closure and relocation to Nisibis) —
decades before and after 451, not because of it. Edessa itself, this
world's own core city, fed personnel into the *East* Syriac line as well
as this one (Ibas of Edessa and the "School of the Persians" there both
belong to that story).

**Corrected statement.** This world and the Church of the East are both
real heirs of `syr`'s own Edessene-Syriac tradition — the census's own
`relationsSummary` field for `syr` itself states this plainly: `syr` is
"Parent of the Church of the East and the Syriac Orthodox Church." Both
successor traditions claim Ephrem; this document does not adopt Revision
1's claim that Ephrem's own legacy belongs distinctly to this world's own
side of a Chalcedon-era split. This world is a sibling descendant of
`syr`, alongside the Church of the East, by a process that took decades
and multiple causes (institutional independence in Persia, then a separate
and later Christological rupture at Chalcedon affecting Roman-territory
Christians specifically) — not a single clean bifurcation at one council.

**What does this world inherit from `syr`, precisely?** The Syriac
language and literary tradition broadly, and — on equal footing with the
Church of the East, not exclusively — Ephrem's own legacy. What is more
distinctly this world's own is geographic and institutional: continued
presence in Edessa specifically (Jacob Baradaeus's own assigned see, §3)
and in the wider Roman-administered parts of `syr`'s own former territory,
now organized under a completely different ecclesial structure (an
unrecognized parallel hierarchy, not `syr`'s own pre-institutional
covenant-order pattern).

**What does this world transmit forward?** The census's own `continuesAs`
field names `syriac-orthodox-under-islam`, not yet built. This world's own
636 end point (§2) is chosen to open onto it.
