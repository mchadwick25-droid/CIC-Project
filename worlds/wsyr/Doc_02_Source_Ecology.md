## 1. Source Ecology Overview

This world's evidentiary base has an unusual shape: one insider historian
(John of Ephesus) supplies genuinely rich, personally-witnessed narrative
material across two separate works, now confirmed (this revision) to
include real doctrinal/controversy content (the Tritheist and Paulite
disputes, Book I and Book IV of the Ecclesiastical History) that Revision 1
missed entirely by mischaracterizing the file's own contents (§2). Two
theologians (Severus of Antioch and Philoxenus of Mabbug) supply real
doctrinal argument in their own hand (via translation) — this revision
corrects Revision 1's claim that this world's doctrinal center of gravity
was thinly sourced; it is not. The movement's single most consequential
institutional actor, Jacob Baradaeus, left no writing independently
confirmed as vendorable this session, though a real, unconfirmed lead
exists (§11).

## 3. Author Gravity Assessment (Constitution Article 16; preliminary — full classification is Doc_04's own job)

**Severus of Antioch.** Representativeness: high for elite theological
argument and patriarchal administration. Authenticity: high. Boundary
status: native, the movement's own foremost self-articulated voice.
**Corrected this revision:** the vendored Select Letters are not merely
administrative — Letter I.1 alone carries substantive doctrinal argument
against Chalcedon and Eutyches (§2). His separate treatises and homilies
remain unvendored, a narrower gap than Revision 1 stated.

**John of Ephesus.** Representativeness: unusually high, now confirmed
(this revision) to extend to internal controversy material (the Tritheist
dispute, the Paulite schism) as well as persecution narrative and personal
portraiture — a materially richer source than Revision 1 recognized.
Authenticity: high. Boundary status: native, with the same double-position
tension (persecuted bishop / imperial missionary agent) Revision 1 already
named.

**Jacob Baradaeus.** No writing independently confirmed as vendorable this
session — corrected from Revision 1's stronger claim that no writing of
his survives at all. A real, unconfirmed lead exists (letters via Chabot's
CSCO *Documenta*, and a traditionally-ascribed anaphora) — see §11.
Representativeness/Authenticity questions otherwise still attach to John
of Ephesus's own reliability as his principal witness. Institutional
position: the central figure of this world's own itinerant-ordination
period — a Doc_09/figure-record subject.

**Philoxenus of Mabbug.** **Corrected this revision.** The vendored volume
itself carries a Creed, a Confession of Faith, and direct anti-Nestorian/
anti-"two natures" polemic, alongside the thirteen ascetic Discourses —
Philoxenus is a second directly-sourced doctrinal voice for this world,
not only an ascetic-homiletic one as Revision 1 stated. Authenticity:
high. Boundary status: boundary-adjacent by birth date, native by career.

**Zacharias Scholasticus (as the historical author behind
Ecclesiastical-History Books III-VI of the Zachariah Rhetor compilation).**
**Corrected this revision.** Not a Chalcedonian outside witness as
Revision 1 stated — a companion of Severus who wrote in his defense,
before any possible later conformity to Chalcedonian office. His own
material (Books III-VI) is own-voice, sympathetic testimony, not
opponent-voice.

## 7. Source Asymmetry and Missing Voices

**Corrected this revision.** Revision 1 stated women appear in this
world's own sources "only as reported patrons." This is not accurate: the
*Lives of the Eastern Saints* gives whole chapters to women ascetics in
their own right — this session's review identified chapters on two named
sisters (ch. 12), a virgin named Susan (ch. 27), the anchorite Mary
(ch. 28), and the patrician Caesaria (ch. 54) — not yet individually
locus-verified by this drafting session (a task for whoever next works
this file closely). What Revision 1 got right, and what still stands: no
source vendored this session directly attests a woman's own *first-person
words* — the chapters above are John of Ephesus's own third-person
narration about these women, the same register as his portraits of male
ascetics, not a first-person voice. The corrected, narrower claim: women
are well-represented as *subjects* of this world's own narrative
tradition, not merely as patrons, but are not directly attested as
first-person *speakers* in anything vendored this session.
