## 0. World Brief Recap (from Step 0 Conclusion, binding scope)

- **Name:** Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity
- **Date range:** c. 380s–420 CE
- **Region:** Bethlehem, with sustained ties to Rome
- **Core description, per Step 0:** Jerome's monastic community and his Vulgate translation work, *plus* the aristocratic Roman women's ascetic-patronage network — Paula, Eustochium, Marcella, Fabiola — with the binding instruction that "the case should be built around that fuller ecology rather than around Jerome as a single scholarly figure." The women's network is named a deliberate Voice Breadth asset in Step 0's own comparative language.

This document treats that instruction as a hard scoping constraint, not an aspiration — but it also names, rather than papers over, a genuine limit on how far that constraint can be met: **every substantive piece of evidence for the women's own agency, without exception, survives only in Jerome's own hand** (his letters, his obituaries for Paula, Marcella, and Fabiola, his dedications). No text independently composed by Paula, Eustochium, or Marcella survives (see §9, Open Issue #3, formalized at Doc_02). This document's claim is therefore narrower and more honest than "the women are recovered as independent voices": it is that **the women's pole is a real, load-bearing, evidentially-attested ecological center that this world's own sources will not let collapse into mere supporting color for Jerome** — Marcella's independent household exegetical authority (§3.3), Paula's role as the material and institutional founder of Bethlehem (not merely its financier), and Fabiola's autonomous hospital-founding are all attested as things the women *did*, not only things Jerome *said about* them — even though the record of what they did reaches us exclusively through his framing. Sections 3, 4, and 8 are written to hold both halves of this together: the women's ecological centrality is real and argued from evidence, and the mediation of that evidence through a single male author is a standing interpretive constraint carried forward explicitly into Doc_02's Author Gravity Assessment, not resolved here.

## 1. Temporal Scope

### 1.1 Beginning point

**Recommended beginning point: c. 382 CE**, when Jerome arrives in Rome as secretary and scriptural adviser to Pope Damasus I, and converges with Marcella's already-established Aventine ascetic household.

Marcella's own dating requires two separate claims, not one compound label: her **personal commitment to widowed ascetic life**, following her refusal of a second marriage to a man of consular rank (*vir consularis* — Jerome's Ep. 127 names him Cerealis but does not call him "consul-elect," a status the source does not support) and her exposure to desert monasticism through the visiting exiled Bishop Athanasius (and later Peter of Alexandria), dates to the 340s and rests on Jerome's own obituary letter for her (Ep. 127) — Widely Accepted as an account of Marcella's own turn to asceticism, though it is Jerome's retrospective framing decades later. Separately, and this is the claim actually relevant to this world's formation ecology: Jerome's own letter states explicitly that it was only "many years after" that Marcella's example was followed first by Sophronia and "then by others" — meaning her household's transition from one ascetic widow to a **functioning multi-woman formation site** is not independently dated to the 340s at all. The household's operation as a formation community is best placed in the decades immediately preceding Jerome's 382 arrival (consistent with a 360s–370s range), at **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** confidence — no primary source gives a precise year for this transition, and this document declines to manufacture false precision by importing an unsourced "forty years before Jerome" figure that circulates in some popular (non-critical-scholarly) accounts.

This beginning point is a deliberate choice against two alternatives, both rejected:
- **Beginning at 385** (Jerome's departure from Rome, the Bethlehem foundation) would treat the Roman phase as mere prologue rather than constitutive ecology — precisely the Jerome-centered collapse the Step 0 brief warns against. Marcella's Aventine circle has its own pre-Jerome formation history that this world's ecology inherits and depends on.
- **Beginning earlier, at Marcella's own conversion to ascetic life (c. 340s)**, would extend the world beyond what the confirmed ecology (Jerome + the four named women in relation) can support with evidence, and would blur the boundary with a distinct, not-yet-built "Roman aristocratic asceticism before Jerome" formation world. Marcella's pre-382 personal conversion and household are treated in this world as **inherited background condition**, not as part of the bounded ecology itself (see §1.3, §7).

**382 CE** is therefore the point at which the two evidentiary streams this world reconstructs — Jerome's own corpus, and the Roman women's circle — first attest to direct, sustained mutual engagement. Confidence: Widely Accepted for Jerome's 382 arrival in Rome; Widely Accepted for the pre-existing Marcella circle's existence in some form; the specific claim that 382 is where they *converge* is a construction-boundary judgment, not itself a historical fact requiring independent sourcing.

### 1.2 Ending point

Both candidate termini for this world's close are attached to dates that are themselves genuinely unsettled, and this document tests both explicitly rather than defaulting to the male figure's death without argument.

**Candidate A — Eustochium's death**, as the last *resident* leader of the world's own women's-monastic institution at Bethlehem. Her death date is not a clean two-way contest: sources give **418, 419, or 420** depending on the reference consulted (Catholic Encyclopedia and the received tradition favor "28 September, 419 or 420"; Columbia's Epistolae project gives "418–419?"; some derivations from Kelly's biography give 420). Confidence: **Contested**, across a three-year window, not a settled single date or clean binary.

**Candidate B — Jerome's death**, traditionally dated 30 September 420. This date is likewise not independently documented at Documented-level confidence: the year 420 is Widely Accepted, but the specific day (30 September) descends from the liturgical feast tradition and from Prosper of Aquitaine's continuation-chronicle entry, not from a contemporary eyewitness record within Jerome's own circle. Specialist literature (e.g., debate over Jerome's age at death between Kelly and Rebenich) shows the year itself is not wholly free of scholarly friction. Confidence: Widely Accepted (year); Inferential/Thin (exact day — traditionally fixed by liturgical feast and Prosper's chronicle, not independently documented).

**Recommendation: retain 420 (nominally, Jerome's death) as the working terminus, but explicitly on institutional-continuity grounds rather than biographical ones, and explicitly acknowledging the tension named above.** The reasoning: Eustochium's death, whichever year it actually falls in (418–420), removes the last attested resident leadership of the Bethlehem women's community *before or in the same narrow window as* Jerome's own death — the two events cluster within roughly one to two years of each other across the range the sources actually support. Given that clustering, and given that Jerome's own death date is itself only traditionally precise, this document does not treat 420 as privileging Jerome's biography over Eustochium's — it treats the world's close as the point at which **both** attested threads of resident leadership (Jerome's monastery, Eustochium's monastery) have ended, within a dating window too narrow and too uncertain on both sides to assign a clean priority to either figure's date over the other's. Doc_02 and Doc_05 must carry this dating uncertainty forward explicitly rather than resolve it by picking a single year for either death.

This is a **generational**, not merely biographical, boundary: the world's own attested formation ecology — the specific constellation of Jerome's scholarly-ascetic project and the Roman women's patronage network, in live relationship with one another — ends because every voice that constituted that relationship (Marcella, 410; Paula, 404; Eustochium, 418–420; Jerome, 420) is gone within a few years of one another. What Bethlehem becomes afterward is a distinct question this world does not claim to answer (see §7, World Continuity & Distinction).

### 1.3 What remains internal vs. what indicates transition

**Internal to this world** (developments within the 382–420 span that do not break the ecology, but are part of its own instability and development per Constitution Article 15):
- The 385 rupture with Rome and relocation to Bethlehem — a geographic and institutional shift, but not a change in the ecology's core authority mode (charismatic-scholarly + voluntary aristocratic patronage) or its core relational structure (Jerome and the women's circle in sustained, mutually constitutive relationship).
- The Origenist controversy (c. 393–403) and the rupture with Rufinus — a major internal fracture, but one that reshapes rather than replaces this world's formation logic.
- Paula's death (404) and Eustochium's assumption of leadership — a succession internal to the world's own women's-monastic structure, not a boundary event.
- The 416 Pelagian attack on the Bethlehem monastery — a violent external force (Forces Framework territory, see §6) that presses on the ecology without ending it.

**Indicating transition (boundary-adjacent, flagged for Doc_02/Doc_04 attention):**
- Marcella's death in 410, during the Gothic sack of Rome — this removes the Rome-based pole of the women's network roughly eight to ten years before the world's own close, meaning the final decade of this world is Bethlehem-concentrated in a way the earlier decades were not. This asymmetry must be visible in Doc_05's proportionality assessment, not silently smoothed over.
- Fabiola's death (399/400, dating contested) after only a single, curtailed visit to Bethlehem (395) — her formation-ecology role is real but time-limited and Rome-based; she is a genuine node in the network's patronage and hospital-founding dimension, not a Bethlehem-resident figure. This is addressed directly in §3.3 and Doc_02, not smoothed into equivalence with Paula's or Eustochium's Bethlehem residency.
- Eustochium's own death (418–420, Contested — see §1.2) sits close enough to Jerome's that the two cannot be treated as cleanly sequential; Doc_02 should not assert a fixed order or interval between them beyond "within a few years of one another."

## 2. Geographic Scope

**Primary sites:** Bethlehem (the double monastery — men's community under Jerome, women's community under Paula then Eustochium; the pilgrim hospice funded by Paula; the site's proximity to the Grotto of the Nativity, which the community's self-understanding treats as formatively significant) and Rome (the Aventine Hill circle around Marcella; the aristocratic Christian households of Paula, Eustochium pre-385, and Fabiola).

**Secondary/transit sites, evidentially attested but not sites of sustained community life:** Antioch (where Jerome was ordained presbyter, c. 378–379, and where his desert sojourn near Chalcis occurred before this world's temporal boundary); Cyprus (Paula and Eustochium's 385 stop, visiting Bishop Epiphanius of Salamis); Alexandria and Nitria in Egypt (385–386 tour, including the meeting with Didymus the Blind); Jerusalem (repeatedly visited from Bethlehem, and the site of Jerome's rupture with Bishop John of Jerusalem during the Origenist controversy).

This world's geography is therefore **bipolar, not merely provincial**: it does not reduce to "Bethlehem" alone, precisely because the Roman aristocratic network is not simply the ecology's point of origin but a continuously active pole throughout the period (via correspondence, continued patronage, dedicatees for Jerome's Bethlehem-produced works, and — through Marcella specifically — an independent center of scriptural authority operating in Rome without Jerome physically present after 385). This bipolarity is a structural finding, not a narrative convenience: the world's own evidence (Jerome's continuing letters *to* Rome, Ep. 127's account of Marcella fielding scriptural disputes in Rome after 385) requires it.

## 3. Cultural Scope

### 3.1 Linguistic

Latin is the operative language of this world's own literary output and of its aristocratic Roman participants' native culture. Greek remains a scholarly-access language (Jerome's engagement with Origen, Didymus, and the wider Greek patristic and Alexandrian exegetical tradition). Hebrew occupies a distinctive and contested position: Jerome's claim to substantial Hebrew fluency, pursued through named Jewish teachers, is central to his own self-presentation and to the *Hebraica veritas* principle governing the Vulgate project — but Megan Hale Williams's analysis (*The Monk and the Book*, 2006) treats Jerome's account of his own Hebrew formation as a retrospective literary construction inconsistent with earlier, more modest claims. This tension is carried into Doc_02 at Contested confidence, not resolved here.

### 3.2 Social and institutional

This is an **elite, literate, urban-and-monastic** world by its own evidentiary character: its participants are drawn from the Roman senatorial aristocracy (Paula's family claimed descent from the Scipios and the Gracchi; Marcella's household was Aventine-patrician; Fabiola came from the gens Fabia) and from a scholarly clerical class (Jerome, presbyter, trained in grammar and classical literature under the grammarian Aelius Donatus, whom Jerome names his "praeceptor" — his rhetorical training is attributed elsewhere in the standard biographical accounts, not to Donatus specifically). This social location is not incidental color — it is constitutive of the ecology's authority mode (see §3.3, §8) and must be disclosed as a sourcing-skew concern in Doc_02 per the project-wide disclosure obligation.

### 3.3 Institutional/authority character

Jerome held no episcopal office and no territorial jurisdiction; he was ordained presbyter at Antioch (c. 378–379) on terms that preserved his ascetic-scholarly independence, and his authority over the course of this world's span rested on **scholarly reputation and reception** (as translator, exegete, and correspondent) and on **voluntary aristocratic patronage relationships**, chiefly with Paula, who financed his travel, his Hebrew study, and the entire Bethlehem foundation. This is a documented and load-bearing structural fact (Rebenich, Cain), not incidental biography — it is the basis for the authority-mode differentiation argued in §8.1.

Independently, and this is a finding the reconstruction must not subordinate to Jerome's own framing: Marcella's household in Rome functioned, per Jerome's own obituary letter for her (Ep. 127), as a **standing point of scriptural-exegetical reference for Roman clergy** after Jerome's 385 departure — clergy, "sometimes priests," reportedly brought disputed scriptural questions to her for resolution. This is the strongest attested instance of independent (non-Jerome-mediated-in-the-moment) female intellectual authority in this world's evidence base, even though the only surviving account of it is Jerome's own retrospective, polemically-inflected obituary (Andrew Cain's analysis treats Ep. 127 as partly a vehicle for validating Jerome's own exegetical method against Origenist critics). Doc_02's Author Gravity Assessment carries this tension in full; it is named here because it is directly relevant to whether this world is strand-singular (§4).

## 4. Strand Determination

**Finding: strand-singular, with a documented internal bipolarity that does not rise to the level of distinct strands.**

The Framework requires this determination be a finding, not a presupposed schema (Constitution Article 21). Round 1 review identified that the strongest steelman for strand plurality had not actually been engaged in the initial draft: not merely a Rome-vs-Bethlehem *geographic* candidate, but a **Jerome-vs-women *authority-mode*** candidate — since §3.3 itself argues Marcella's household exegetical authority is independent and "a finding the reconstruction must not subordinate" to Jerome's framing. That candidate is engaged directly here, and the original Reason #1 (which asserted "no distinct authority structure" in a way that flatly contradicted §3.3) is withdrawn and replaced with an argument that actually confronts the tension rather than talking past it.

**The authority-mode candidate for strand plurality, steelmanned:** Jerome's authority (scholarly reputation, translation and commentary production, correspondence-based spiritual direction, financially dependent on patrons) and Marcella's authority (a settled aristocratic Roman matron whose own household became a standing point of reference for visiting clergy's scriptural disputes, exercised in person, in Rome, without financial dependency on anyone) are not the same texture of authority. One is patronage-dependent and mobile; the other is patronage-independent (Marcella's own attested wealth and settled Aventine household mean she does not depend on anyone else's patronage to sustain her position, unlike Jerome) and stationary. Marcella's own financial role beyond this is not securely attested: Jerome's named financial patron for his own travel, Hebrew study, and the Bethlehem foundation is Paula, not Marcella. Doc_02 must establish what, if anything, can be said about Marcella's own financial role before any claim along these lines is introduced. A schema built on "authority mode" as its organizing axis could plausibly split here.

**Why this world is nonetheless found strand-singular, not strand-plural, on this axis:** The distinction is real but does not rise to strand status because it is a difference of **degree and position within one shared authority logic**, not a difference of **kind**. Both Jerome's and Marcella's authority rest on the same underlying currency — personal scriptural-scholarly credibility recognized voluntarily by peers and social equals, outside any office or territorial jurisdiction — rather than on two structurally different bases (e.g., one charismatic-personal and one institutional-episcopal, which *would* constitute a difference of kind and would be the relevant test against World #8, see §8.1). Marcella's authority is better described as **the same charismatic-scholarly-recognition logic operating from a position of independent wealth and settled social standing**, rather than a rival authority structure. The evidence does not show Marcella's household and Jerome's Bethlehem monastery operating under two different, competing accounts of what makes someone authoritative to consult on scripture — both communities recognize the same thing (demonstrated scriptural learning, personally verified through direct engagement) as authorizing. This is a finding this document holds provisionally: if Doc_02's fuller Author Gravity Assessment or Doc_04's gravity testing surfaces stronger evidence that Marcella's authority operated on a genuinely distinct basis, this strand-singular finding must be reopened, not defended past the evidence.

Two further reasons support strand-singular status, both explicitly secondary to the authority-mode analysis above and not load-bearing on their own:

1. **No distinct formation practice.** The ascetic disciplines attested for the Roman women (fasting, wealth renunciation, virginity/widowed continence, scriptural study) are the same disciplines constitutive of the Bethlehem double monastery, not a variant practice. Paula and Eustochium physically carry the "Roman" formation pattern to Bethlehem and it becomes indistinguishable there from the "Bethlehem" pattern.
2. **Continuous, not parallel, relationship.** The two poles are not two separate communities that happen to share a formation world (which would support strand status); they are one continuously interacting network — the same individuals (Paula, Eustochium) move from one pole to the other, and Jerome's Bethlehem literary output is continuously dedicated to and shaped by requests from the Rome-connected women throughout the period.

The Rome/Bethlehem bipolarity and the Jerome/Marcella authority-texture distinction are therefore both recorded as **structures requiring proportionality and Author Gravity attention** (per §2, §3.3, and Doc_05's proportionality assessment), not as a strand division. **This finding governs all subsequent strand attribution: this world is treated as strand-singular throughout Docs 02–10, subject to reopening if Doc_02 or Doc_04 surfaces stronger evidence of a genuine difference in kind.**

Cross-strand gravity testing (Step 4) is therefore not applicable in its strand-comparative form; however, Doc_04 must still test whether candidate gravities hold across *both poles* of the bipolar geography (Rome and Bethlehem) as a substitute discipline, since a gravity visible only at one pole would raise the same concern (an artificially unified ecology) that cross-strand testing exists to catch.

## 5. Distinct World Criteria

### 5.1 Recurring formation ecology

This world's recurring formation ecology is organized around **textual asceticism**: the fusion of ascetic renunciation (fasting, celibacy/continence, wealth divestment, manual and scriptorium labor) with scholarly-literary production (biblical translation, commentary, epistolary spiritual direction, hagiographic literature) as a single, mutually reinforcing formation practice — not as two separate activities occurring in the same community. A participant in this world is formed as much by the discipline of Hebrew study, textual correction, and sustained correspondence as by fasting and prayer; the ecology does not treat scholarship as a distraction from asceticism or asceticism as a precondition merely tolerated around scholarship. This fusion, evidenced across both Jerome's own practice and the women's engagement (Paula and Eustochium's attested education and scriptural engagement; Marcella's exegetical role; Fabiola's use of literacy and wealth in hospital-founding), is what makes this a **recurring, not incidental,** ecology.

### 5.2 Recurring gravities (preliminary — full testing at Doc_04)

Candidates to be formally tested at Step 4, named here only as orientation: (a) *Hebraica veritas* / textual authority as a formation practice; (b) voluntary ascetic self-impoverishment as aristocratic Christian formation; (c) patronage as the operative authority structure; (d) letter-writing as the primary medium of spiritual formation and community-maintenance across distance. These are not final classifications — they are what Doc_02's source ecology work will be scoped to test for.

### 5.3 Recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority

- **Worship:** monastic horarium at Bethlehem — the general fact of a structured community life is Widely Accepted (Ep. 108), but specific schedule details are Dominant Modern Reconstruction (inferred by analogy to other monastic rules, not directly attested — flagged for Doc_02); proximity-to-Nativity-site devotional practice; no attested distinctively different Roman-household worship pattern from the Bethlehem pattern once the community relocates.
- **Formation:** apprenticeship in languages (Latin rhetoric, Greek, contested-level Hebrew) as spiritual formation; hagiographic literature (Jerome's *Vita Pauli*, *Vita Malchi*, *Vita Hilarionis*) as formation-modeling genre.
- **Interpretation:** *Hebraica veritas* as an interpretive commitment with real controversy attached (the Augustine correspondence, the Oea "ivy/gourd" incident) — this world takes a side in a live scriptural-authority debate of its period, which is itself formative of its identity.
- **Belonging:** voluntary aristocratic patronage network sustained by correspondence across distance, not territorial parish membership.
- **Authority:** charismatic-scholarly reputation (Jerome) and independently-attested exegetical authority within a patronage household (Marcella), not episcopal office — see §3.3, §8.1.

### 5.4 What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds

## 6. Preliminary Forces Identification

*(Forces Framework Step 1 — orientation only; full compilation at Doc_08. This section governs how Doc_02's source ecology work is scoped: which sources are sought, what silences are expected to be meaningful.)*

**What generated this world?** The convergence of two pre-existing conditions: (1) a late-fourth-century Roman aristocratic Christian ascetic movement among senatorial women, already established under figures like Marcella before Jerome's arrival, seeking scholarly-spiritual direction beyond what the existing Roman clergy could offer; and (2) Jerome's own arrival in Rome as a trained classical rhetorician and biblical scholar with papal backing (Damasus), possessing exactly the philological credentials this circle sought, but lacking any independent institutional base of his own.

**What larger world was it embedded in?** Late fourth-century Roman aristocratic Christianity in the period of Christianity's rapid elite social ascent following Theodosian establishment, still contending with a resilient pagan senatorial culture (the circle around Symmachus, the Altar of Victory controversy) and with an emerging, still-forming distinction between "worldly" and ascetic Christian aristocratic identity. It was also embedded in the wider fourth-century Egyptian and Palestinian monastic movement — specifically the **Nitrian** ascetic communities. Ep. 108 §14 narrates, as a specific encounter, that Bishop Isidore came out to meet Paula at Nitria "accompanied by countless multitudes of monks" (unnamed). In the same passage Jerome also invokes "the Macarii, Arsenius, Serapion, or other pillars of Christ" — but as a rhetorical flourish ("need I speak of...") immediately following the Isidore encounter, not as a second, literal claim that Paula personally met each named figure. This distinction matters and is independently checkable: **Arsenius the Great did not withdraw to the Egyptian desert until c. 394**, roughly eight years after Paula's 385–386 visit, making a literal encounter chronologically impossible. This is corrected from a Round 2 draft, which read the rhetorical listing as a literal reception roster — an error caught only by independently pulling Ep. 108's actual text a second time and checking the Arsenius chronology against it, illustrating why a stated verification claim (like this document's own) must never be trusted without a further independent check. The safest claim this document can make: Ep. 108 attests a specific encounter with Isidore and an unnamed monastic multitude at Nitria; it also invokes several renowned desert-father names in a conventional rhetorical register that should not be read as a literal itemization of individuals met. Alexandria and a meeting with Didymus the Blind are attested by other Jerome sources, not by Ep. 108 itself, and are carried at correspondingly lower confidence pending Doc_02's independent source check. Separately: Ep. 108 records Paula's own desire to press on to the more remote monastic communities of the Egyptian desert further south, but she turned back toward the Holy Land before reaching them; there is no attested visit to Pachomian communities of the Thebaid on this journey. Jerome's later, separate familiarity with Pachomian material (via his own translation of the Pachomian Rule, c. 404) is a different and later evidentiary thread. Bethlehem's own monastic organization borrows organizational and devotional patterns from this Nitrian/Egyptian ascetic movement while producing a distinctly Latin-literary variant.

**What was it responding to?** A perceived deficiency in Latin biblical scholarship relative to Greek and Hebrew textual traditions (motivating the Vulgate project); a perceived laxity or worldliness among some Roman Christian aristocratic women not yet embracing ascetic renunciation (motivating the rhetorically intense exhortations to virginity and renunciation in letters like Ep. 22); and, for the women themselves, a search for scriptural authority and spiritual direction not readily available through ordinary episcopal/parish structures given their social position and educational level.

**What was it refusing?** Continued participation in conventional senatorial marriage-and-inheritance strategies (Paula's family's resistance to her renunciation — attested, but only through Jerome's own account; see the naming of this as a structural silence below); the pagan aristocratic culture of the Roman Senate; and, following the 384–385 crisis — Blaesilla's death (384) from ascetic excess, the loss of Pope Damasus's protection (his death, December 384), and resulting clerical hostility — continued residence within a Roman clerical establishment that had turned hostile to Jerome specifically, leading to his August 385 departure.

This preliminary identification flags for Doc_02: seek sources on the pagan aristocratic backlash (the Symmachus circle, general accounts of anti-ascetic sentiment in Rome in the 380s) as context for the forces pressing on this world from outside; seek sources on Egyptian/Palestinian monasticism as the institutional template partially adopted and partially adapted; and treat the near-total loss of the women's own institutional and family-side perspective (their relatives' resistance, e.g., Toxotius's grief at Paula's departure, is **attested only through Jerome's own account, not independently documented**) as a **structural silence requiring explicit naming**, not a gap to quietly fill.

## 7. World Continuity & Distinction

**Inheritance from prior worlds:** This world inherits from the preceding *Desert Monasticism* formation world (World #3) both a template of ascetic renunciation and a genre — Jerome's own hagiographic *Vitae* are explicitly modeled on and in conversation with Egyptian desert monastic literature, and the 385–386 journey included direct encounters with Nitrian and Alexandrian ascetic communities. It inherits from the broader Roman aristocratic Christian world (not itself one of the nine confirmed formation worlds, but part of this world's own background) the social structures of patronage, senatorial wealth, and household religious practice that the ascetic movement redirects rather than invents.

**What it transmits to subsequent worlds:** Jerome's translation work becomes, over subsequent centuries, an increasingly authoritative Latin biblical text for Western Christianity — though it coexisted with, and only gradually and unevenly displaced, the older Vetus Latina translations over a period of centuries, not immediately or uniformly. Its eventual standing as "the Vulgate" in the later, settled sense is a transmission whose scale and timeline vastly exceed this world's own bounded timeframe and belongs to later worlds' own reconstruction, not to this one; this document does not assert that Jerome's own version achieved standard status within the 382–420 span itself. The pattern of aristocratic women's ascetic patronage as a legitimate and influential form of female Christian authority also continues into later Western monastic and patronage history, though tracing that transmission belongs to whatever later Latin-tradition formation world eventually reconstructs it.

**Shared material functioning differently within this ecology:** Scripture itself is shared with every other Christian formation world in this project, but functions here distinctively as **contested translated text** — this world's own defining controversy (Hebrew versus Septuagint textual authority) has no equivalent centrality in, for instance, Desert Monasticism's or Post-Apostolic House-Church's formation ecology, where the text's authority was less actively contested at the level of translation philology.

**Where continuity could mask genuine ecological change:** The shared vocabulary of "asceticism," "monastery," and "virginity" across this world and Desert Monasticism risks masking a real difference in formation logic — Desert Monasticism's formation center of gravity is oral, communal, and largely non-literary (sayings, direct teacher-disciple encounter); this world's is textual and literary even in its ascetic dimension. Doc_03's lexicon work must not import Desert Monasticism's vocabulary uncritically.

## 8. World Differentiation (Step 0-mandated section)

### 8.1 Differentiation from World #8 (Latin Pastoral-Congregational, Cyprian/Augustine — not yet built)

*(This world's own evidence, not World #8's content, grounds this argument — World #8 has not been built and its documents have not been read.)*

The authority-mode contrast is grounded entirely in what this world's own evidence establishes about Jerome's structural position, not in any claim about World #8's actual content (which has not been built or read).

What this world's own evidence actually establishes, on its own terms: Jerome held no episcopal office, no territorial jurisdiction, and no diocesan administrative or juridical role of any kind (§3.3) throughout the 382–420 span. He was ordained presbyter at Antioch on terms explicitly preserving his ascetic-scholarly independence, never sought or held higher clerical office, and his authority was continuously renegotiated through personal reputation and voluntary patronage rather than guaranteed by any office — demonstrably precarious, since the 384–385 crisis (Blaesilla's death, the loss of Pope Damasus's protection, resulting clerical hostility) drove him from Rome within months with no institutional recourse. This world's authority mode is therefore **charismatic-scholarly and voluntary-aristocratic-patronage-based, structurally independent of any episcopal or territorial office** — a description this document can defend entirely from its own evidence base. A "Latin Pastoral-Congregational" world organized around named figures who *did* hold territorial episcopal office (a claim this document does not itself verify, since it has not read World #8's build) would, by definition of holding such office, differ from this world on that specific axis — but this document limits itself to establishing what is true of *this* world, and leaves the comparison's other half to World #8's own, independently-sourced construction.

### 8.2 Differentiation from a hypothetical Jerusalem pilgrimage/catechetical world (not yet built)

This world's own evidence is **elite, textual, and scholarly** in character (§3.2, §5.1): its formation practice centers on literacy, philological training, biblical translation, and sustained correspondence among a socially narrow, aristocratic, highly literate population. This world's own sources attest **no catechumenate**, no structured pre-baptismal instruction for a broad lay population, and no formation medium accessible to anyone without substantial literacy and leisure — its participant base, on its own evidence, is a small number of named aristocratic individuals and their household dependents, not a mass lay population. A world organized around Jerusalem pilgrimage and catechesis would, by the nature of what pilgrimage and catechesis for ordinary pilgrims and catechumens actually are, necessarily involve a broader and more socially mixed formation population than this world's own evidence shows for itself — but this document limits its claim to what is true of *this* world's own participant base, without characterizing what such a world would "presumably" contain.
