# Guided Starters — Desert Monasticism (DRAFT V0.1)

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Mark's review. Not deployed.
**Purpose:** Entry points for participants who don't know what to ask. Questions open doors; they never walk the participant through them (Encounter Over Persuasion applies to curricula).
**Grounding:** Every topic below traces to this world's approved build documents. Nothing is invented. Compact traces appear in brackets after each topic's "why" line (Doc numbers = CiC_W3 build documents; "G#" = gravity numbers per Doc_04 §6; "lex" = Lexicon-Chunks; "story" = Story-Chunks / Doc_09a).

## First Visit (start here)

**Why leave at all?** — Withdrawal is this world's defining act, so almost every other door opens off this one. [Doc_04 G1; lex001 anachōrēsis; Doc_01 §7]
*Opening question:* "What made you leave your village? What were you looking for out here?"
Follow-ups: "Was there one moment when you knew, or did it happen slowly?" · "What did leaving actually cost you?" · "Did anyone try to talk you out of it?"

**An ordinary day** — The concrete daily rhythm (prayer, rope, cell, silence) is where this world's ideals become visible as a life someone actually lived. [Doc_09a Story 4.1; story008; Doc_04 G4]
*Opening question:* "Walk me through a normal day for you — from waking up to going to sleep."
Follow-ups: "What part of the day is hardest?" · "How do you keep track of time out there?" · "What happens on the days when nothing feels like it's working?"

**The work of your hands** — Weaving rope and baskets is this world's most materially documented practice, and it understands the work itself as prayer's companion, not a chore beside it. [Doc_04 G4; lex007 cheirōnaxia]
*Opening question:* "Why do you spend so much time weaving rope and baskets? Isn't that a distraction from the spiritual stuff?"
Follow-ups: "What do you do with what you make?" · "What is your mind doing while your hands work?" · "What happens to someone here who has nothing to do?"

**The thoughts** — Spiritual combat with intrusive thoughts (*logismoi*) is the interior side of withdrawal; it's the struggle this world talks about most. [Doc_04 G2; lex004 logismoi]
*Opening question:* "You talk about fighting your thoughts. What does that actually mean?"
Follow-ups: "What kinds of thoughts do you mean — can you give me examples?" · "How do you tell a dangerous thought from an ordinary one?" · "Does the fighting ever stop?"

**Elders and disciples** — Teaching here passes through a person, not a book: a tested word from someone whose life has proven it. [Doc_04 G3; lex006 gerōn/abba/amma; lex008 apophthegma]
*Opening question:* "How does someone learn this life? Is there training, or a teacher?"
Follow-ups: "What makes someone an abba or an amma — who decides?" · "What's it like to tell an elder your worst thoughts?" · "What if the elder is wrong?"

**Alone or together?** — This world holds three genuinely different patterns — full solitude, a rule-governed common household, and cells gathered loosely around shared worship — and never chose one over the others. [Doc_01 §6; Doc_04 G6, G10; lex009 koinōnia]
*Opening question:* "Do you all live alone out there, or together? How does it actually work?"
Follow-ups: "How would someone know which kind of life was right for them?" · "Do the solitary ones and the community ones respect each other?" · "Did anyone move between the two ways?"

## Going Deeper

**Discernment (*diakrisis*)** — This world's own master skill: judging rightly between thoughts, practices, and counsel — and the check on ascetic excess. [Doc_04 G5; lex005 diakrisis]
*Opening question:* "How do you know how much is enough — fasting, solitude, all of it? Who decides?"
Follow-ups: "Has an elder ever told someone to do *less*?" · "Can a person trust their own judgment about themselves?" · "How do you learn discernment — can it be taught?"

**Stillness (*hēsychia*)** — The stillness sought here is not relaxation; the world describes it as a discipline that exposes what's inside rather than soothing it. [lex003 hēsychia]
*Opening question:* "What is this stillness you're after? Is it like peace of mind?"
Follow-ups: "What does the silence do to a person over the years?" · "Is it easier to find stillness alone or with others?" · "Have you reached it?"

**Renunciation (*apotagē*)** — Giving everything up here is an entry act that never finishes — re-enacted daily, not a one-time dramatic gesture. [lex002 apotagē; Doc_09a Story 1.1]
*Opening question:* "When you gave everything away, was that the end of it — or does the giving-up keep happening?"
Follow-ups: "What was hardest to let go of?" · "Do people ever regret it and go back?" · "What do you still own, if anything?"

**Scripture heard as personal address** — This world reads scripture as a word spoken directly to the hearer, to be obeyed, not analyzed — Antony's whole life turned on one verse heard that way. [Doc_04 G7; story001; Doc_09a Story 1.1]
*Opening question:* "How do you read the Bible out here? What does it mean to you?"
Follow-ups: "Tell me about Antony hearing that verse in church — what happened?" · "What if two people hear the same words differently?" · "Do you ever study it just to understand it, or is it always about doing?"

**The weekly gathering** — For the semi-solitary ascetics, the Saturday-to-Sunday *synaxis* — vigil, worship, one shared meal — is the only regular break in a week of solitude. [Doc_01 §6 Strand C; Doc_09a Story 4.1]
*Opening question:* "You're alone most of the week — what happens when you all come together?"
Follow-ups: "What is it like walking back to the cell afterward?" · "Why gather at all, if solitude is the point?" · "What do you eat, and who provides it?"

**The stories you carry** — This world teaches through a small number of kept stories — Moses and the leaking jug, Arsenius told to flee and be still, Amma Sarah answering the elders — and it will say honestly when a story is all it has. [Doc_09a §2; story004–006]
*Opening question:* "Tell me a story from among your people — one that gets told again and again."
Follow-ups: "Why do you think that one survived?" · "Who was Amma Sarah? What do you remember of her?" · "Are there people whose names you know but whose stories you've lost?"

**A word tested by a life, or a written rule?** — This world never settled whether true authority lives in a person or an office — the tension between the elder's word and Pachomius's Rule ran its whole span. [Doc_04 G10; Doc_01 §4; lex009]
*Opening question:* "Some of you follow an elder, some follow a written rule. Which is the real authority?"
Follow-ups: "What does the rule give that an elder can't?" · "What does an elder give that a rule can't?" · "Did the two sides argue about it?"

**How separate were you, really?** — The record itself shows ongoing trade and ties with nearby villages alongside the language of total withdrawal — a tension this world's own documents preserve rather than hide. [Doc_04 G8 (Tensional); Doc_01 §3]
*Opening question:* "You sell your baskets to the villages — so did you ever really leave the world behind?"
Follow-ups: "Who do you meet when the goods change hands?" · "Does the money change anything?" · "Is there a difference between leaving the world and never seeing it?"

## For the Wrestling (hard questions welcome here)

**Thoughts that won't stop** — Unwanted, intrusive thoughts are this world's primary battlefield; it treats them as significant rather than shameful, and as something to be disclosed, not hidden. [Doc_04 G2; lex004]
*Opening question:* "Did anyone out there struggle with thoughts they couldn't turn off?"
Follow-ups: "What did they actually do when one kept coming back?" · "Did the strongest among you still have them?" · "Was having the thought treated as a fault, or just as the work?"

**Wanting to give up** — The tradition names the wish to be somewhere else — restlessness, despondency, the noon-day weariness — among the thoughts it watched most carefully. [lex004 (the eight-fold taxonomy incl. listlessness/despondency, flagged as one teacher's systematization); Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "Was there ever a day you wanted to walk back to the village and quit? What kept you there?"
Follow-ups: "What does that kind of weariness feel like from the inside?" · "Is wanting to leave always a bad sign?" · "Did anyone leave? What did the rest of you say about them?"

**Failing badly** — This world's most carried story — Moses walking to a judgment with a leaking jug — is about a community deciding what to do with someone's failure. [story004; Doc_09a §2.1]
*Opening question:* "What happens when one of you fails — really fails? Is there a way back?"
Follow-ups: "Tell me about Abba Moses and the jug." · "How do you keep from judging each other out there?" · "What about someone who fails the same way over and over?"

**Loneliness** — Solitude is chosen here, but the record is honest that stillness exposes rather than soothes — worth asking what the aloneness actually did to people. [lex003; Doc_09a Story 4.1; story007]
*Opening question:* "Doesn't the loneliness ever get to you? Years alone in a cell — how does that not break a person?"
Follow-ups: "What's the difference between solitude and isolation, if there is one?" · "Were there people the solitude harmed?" · "Is it lonelier out there, or was it lonelier back in the village?"

**Isn't leaving selfish?** — A challenge the participant may fairly press: the world claims withdrawal is engagement, not escape — the Representative can be asked to answer for that. [lex001 Distortion Risk; Doc_04 G8; Doc_01 §7]
*Opening question:* "Honestly — people back in the villages needed help, and you walked away. Isn't that selfish?"
Follow-ups: "What good did your leaving do for anyone but you?" · "You give alms from your labor — is that enough of an answer?" · "If everyone did what you did, what would happen to the world?"

**Couldn't you have followed God at home?** — This world arose asking what total devotion looks like once martyrdom was no longer the available path — but a participant can press whether ordinary life was really insufficient. [Doc_01 §7; Doc_04 §4]
*Opening question:* "Why couldn't you just live a faithful life in your village — family, work, church? Why wasn't that enough?"
Follow-ups: "Are you saying ordinary Christians are doing it wrong?" · "What was it about the end of the persecutions that changed things?" · "Would you tell everyone to do what you did?"

## Questions This World Answers Honestly With Its Limits

These are good questions to ask precisely because this world will not pretend to more than its record holds. Its own build documents disclose where the evidence runs thin, and the Representative is built to say so plainly.

**"What was this life like for the women among you?"** — Named ammas (Sarah, Syncletica, Theodora) are genuinely attested, and their preserved sayings are real — but they are a handful of fragments beside the male-authored bulk of the record. The Representative will share what it carries and name how little that is, rather than inventing a fuller picture. [Doc_02 §1.6 via Doc_09b §2; Doc_09a §5; lex006 plural-voices flag]

**"What did your worship actually sound like — the words, the prayers?"** — This world's record preserves worship's shape and rhythm (the psalms, the weekly gathering) but not its word-for-word content. The honest answer is the shape and the hour, not the script. [Doc_09b §2; Permanent Prompt]

**"What did the ordinary monks — the ones nobody wrote about — actually feel?"** — Most participants in this world were Coptic-speaking villagers who left no writings; nearly everything known comes through Greek-literate outsiders and later compilers describing them. What the famous sources say about ordinary interior experience is projection as much as report, and this world says so. [Doc_01 §4; Doc_09b §2, §4]

**"Are the famous stories true, or were they polished up?"** — The sources here are openly hagiographic. Athanasius's *Life of Antony* is the tradition's own shaped portrait, not neutral biography — even Antony's famed lack of education is genuinely contested by scholars. The Representative distinguishes what the tradition tells from what is documented, and marks the difference out loud. [Doc_01 §10; Doc_09a §§1.1, 3.1; Doc_09b §4]

**"Who else was out there in the desert with you?"** — Rival ascetic communities (the Melitians) shared the same desert in the same years, and this world's own sources are nearly silent about them — a silence the build reads as itself telling. The Representative knows they were there and will say honestly that it cannot speak from inside their life. [Doc_01 §8.2; Doc_09a §5; Doc_09b §2]
