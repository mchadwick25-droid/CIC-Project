**The standing-rule application problem this round was chartered to close is closed at the level it was failing.** Every standing rule stated by every source record is now satisfied by every term record whose claims that rule governs, at the registration level and, with the exceptions in B2/B3, at the claim level. ## B. Findings

**B1 - SUBSTANTIAL by class, one-clause by size. `apotage.md:40` (evidential) says "this build."**

> "For the Pachomian rule-form, this specific condition rests on modern scholarship on the Latin Rule tradition - itself reaching **this build** two stages removed from the Coptic, through a Greek intermediary before Jerome's Latin..."

`git show c73e0ca8` confirms the phrase was introduced by this commit, replacing "itself three removes from the Coptic". It is the **sole instance across all 199 compiled-facing field instances**: a regex over the whole `this X` family returns 40 hits, and every other one is `this world`, `the tradition`, or `the movement` - the settled in-world convention Rounds 2, 6 and 7 each cleared explicitly. `this build` is not that convention. The build is the construction apparatus - Doc_01, Doc_02, this record set - not the world a hearer is being formed by, and it is the exact register Round 1 excised as "this corpus," Round 2 as "in this lexicon," Round 2 again as "this corpus can quote directly," and Round 3 named as jargon family #1 ("corpus/build self-reference"). A hearer reading this sense is told that a fourth-century renunciation practice reaches *a software build* at two removes.

The companion `locus` on the same record also gained "reaching this build only through consult-only modern scholarship." That one I do **not** flag: `locus` fields are not compiled, and "consult-only" already lives in five other loci (antirrhesis, apatheia, theoria, nepsis, logismoi) as this lexicon's settled apparatus convention. The sense field is the defect; the locus is fine as written.

Fix: delete two words. The sentence reads correctly as "...itself two stages removed from the Coptic, through a Greek intermediary before Jerome's Latin."
