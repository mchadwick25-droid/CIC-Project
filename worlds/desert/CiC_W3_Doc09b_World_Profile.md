## 1. Ecology Summary

Desert Monasticism (c. 320s–c. 430, Egypt) is a formation world organized around withdrawal (*anachōrēsis*) as its defining act — not preparation for formation but formation itself, reinvented as martyrdom's structural successor once systematic persecution ended (Doc_08, Force 1A-i). Six Primary gravities organize its ecology broadly and cross-strand: withdrawal, spiritual combat against intrusive thoughts, elder-mediated authority, manual labor as concurrent ascetic discipline, discernment (*diakrisis*) as this world's own master virtue and ecological hub (Doc_05 §11), and practical, non-systematized scriptural engagement. Two Supporting gravities — Pachomius's cenobitic *koinōnia* and Evagrius's systematized interior psychology — organize significant but strand-bound portions of the ecology (Strand B and Strand C's own intellectual elaboration, respectively) without achieving cross-strand persistence. Two Tensional gravities — ongoing economic/social embeddedness in surrounding village life, and the never-resolved tension between person-based and office-based authority — function as persistent counter-forces that prevent this world from being reducible to its own primary self-understanding (Doc_04 §6).

This world is not strand-singular (Doc_01 §6; Doc_04 §7): three organizational patterns — Strand A (anchoritic, Antony's own model), Strand B (cenobitic, Pachomius's *koinōnia*), and Strand C (semi-anchoritic, the Nitria/Kellia/Scetis settlements) — coexist across the same c. 320s–c. 430 span, genuinely distinct in formation practice and authority structure, yet unified by the six cross-strand Primary gravities. Its formation logic collapses categories a modern reader might expect to remain separate: feeling and diagnosis, scripture and practice, theology and lived experience are, in this world's own idiom, largely the same activity viewed from different angles (Doc_07 §11) — with authority structure standing as the one place this integration does not hold, and the clearest candidate for where this world's own eventual transformation was rooted (Doc_08 §2).

## 2. Characteristic Incompleteness

This world's evidence base is characteristically incomplete along three axes, each honestly carried forward rather than resolved:

**Voice asymmetry.** The overwhelming majority of this world's participants left no surviving text in their own composition; what survives passes through Greek-literate outside observers (Athanasius, Palladius, the *Historia Monachorum* author) or through later, anonymous compilers (the *Apophthegmata*'s editors), predominantly describing a Coptic-speaking, often non-literate population (Doc_02 §6). Named women's voices survive only as thin, if genuine, fragments (Doc_02 §1.6; Doc_09a, Absent Stories).

**Boundary thinness.** This world's own literary self-presentation is comparatively silent about the Melitian schism's parallel ascetic communities occupying the same geographic and social space (Doc_01 §11 item 1; Doc_08, Force 2A-ii) — a silence this document reads as potentially informative (Athanasius's own role as the schism's leading ecclesiastical adversary) rather than neutral, but does not resolve.

**Liturgical and typological thinness.** This world's own liturgical content, as opposed to structure and rhythm, is Inferential/Thin throughout (Doc_02 §9), and this document's evidence base has not independently verified whether commonly assumed biblical typology (wilderness/exile) is textually present in this world's own primary sources versus a plausible but unconfirmed scholarly assumption (Doc_05 §12 item 1).

## 3. World Differentiation

**vs. World #9 (Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity):** confirmed distinct on social structure (communal/non-elite, labor-sustained vs. elite patronage-network), transmission medium (oral sayings vs. text-centered scholarly production), and language (predominantly Coptic vs. Latin) — "ascetic" is a surface similarity between the two worlds that does not extend to formation logic (Doc_01 §8.1).

**vs. World #2 (Alexandrian Catechetical/Christian-Platonist Tradition):** distinct interpretive mode — practical, personally-addressed scriptural engagement (this world) vs. systematic allegorical exegesis (World #2) — with the one point of genuine terminological proximity (deification-adjacent vocabulary) deliberately routed through this world's own *apatheia*/*theōria* register rather than through World #2's *theosis* vocabulary, per explicit cross-world differentiation reasoning (Doc_03 §2; Doc_06, entries 2.2–2.3).

**vs. Worlds #4 and #6 (Donatism; Imperial and Juridical Christianity):** this world's boundary-drawing energy runs predominantly inward (against the *logismoi*) rather than outward (against rival ecclesial claimants or imperial power), a genuine structural contrast this document flags as its own claim pending cross-world confirmation, not an established parallel (Doc_07 §4, §12 item 5).

## 4. Confidence Overview

Applying the Framework's five-level vocabulary across this world's own construction record: **Documented/Widely Accepted** confidence anchors the core chronology (Antony's and Pachomius's approximate careers, the Nitria/Kellia/Scetis foundings, the 399–400 Origenist controversy's basic sequence), the six Primary gravities' cross-strand attestation, and the material/papyrological evidence (Kellia, the Nepheros archive's existence if not its full representativeness). **Contested** confidence governs several load-bearing but genuinely disputed points, carried at full strength throughout this build rather than resolved: the *Vita Antonii*'s incident-level reliability and the Rubenson/Gould dispute over Antony's own philosophical literacy (Doc_01 §10; Doc_06 §2.2's [CT] tag); Theophilus's motives for his 399-to-400 reversal (Doc_08, Force 3A-i); the Pachomian *Lives*' recension priority (Doc_09a, Story 1.3). **Inferential/Thin** confidence governs this world's specific liturgical content, the wilderness/exile typology question, and any claim about ordinary, non-literate participants' own interior experience as distinct from what literate observers projected onto them (Doc_02 §6).
