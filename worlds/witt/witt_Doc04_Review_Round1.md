## S5 — The Law-and-Gospel fold is argued against G1 and G2 only, while the document's own evidence shows G4 and G5 depending on it

§2.2 calls this "the strongest fold in this list," and its reasoning is confined to two candidates:

The document's own Evidence sections contradict the last clause twice.

**G5.** The Evidence for the terrified-and-comforted conscience is, at its centre, the Law/Gospel sequence itself — verified verbatim at v2 11786–11807: the commands "teach a man to know himself, that through them he may recognize his inability to do good and may despair of his powers," and then "the promises of God … stand ready." **G4.** The Evidence for the household catechism turns on LC 2967–2975, verified verbatim: "the Creed is a doctrine quite different from the Ten Commandments; for the latter teaches indeed what we ought to do, but the former tells what God does for us." That is the Law/Gospel distinction supplying the catechism's *structure* — the order of its parts. G4's Explanatory test says the candidate "explains the entire 1529 output"; the shape of that output is Law-then-Gospel.

Under the Framework's Dependency Test ("Do other dimensions depend upon it?"), a candidate on which four dimensions demonstrably depend is not disposed of by an argument addressed to two. The fold may still be right — Law and Gospel may be a rule that operates through G1 and G2 rather than a force in its own right — but it is not right *for the reason given*, and the document's own evidence is the counter-case.

**Required:** re-argue the fold against G4 and G5 as well as G1 and G2, or advance Law and Gospel as a fourteenth candidate and test it. If the fold stands, G4's and G5's Dependency entries should name it, so the fold is visible where the dependency actually sits rather than only at §2.2.

## S6 — "Primary (prescriptive side only)" is not one of the Framework's classifications, and it is carried in the Template's required index table

G4's classification line reads: "**Classification: PRIMARY — on the prescriptive evidence alone, with the divergence stated, not resolved.**" §6.1 — the table the Template exists to guarantee — records it in the Classification column as "**Primary (prescriptive side only)**." §6.2 repeats it.

**First, what is not wrong here, stated plainly, because it is the document's central judgment and it deserves a direct answer rather than a hedge.** The Cross-Check is *not* violated. Framework para 309 bars Primary status for a candidate that "rests primarily on Inferential/Thin evidence." G4's governing evidence — the 1520 *Kurze Form*, both 1529 catechisms, the hymnal prefaces, AC and Apology XXIV — is Documented, verified verbatim at every locus I opened (LC 43–47, 100–106, 241–243, 333–336; SC 45, 557–558, 611; v2 13182–13186; Ap 6889–6901; AC 790–792). The divergence is flagged, not resolved; §6.3 records it as the build's highest-stakes cross-check; the reception side is named as outside the classification's scope; the counter-reading is stated against the document's own interest; and the two acquisitions that would move it are named. That is the discipline the Cross-Check exists to produce, and the document produces it.

**What is wrong is the label, and the label has a real consequence.** The Framework gives three classifications (paras 304–306) and the Template's index table gives four values (Primary / Supporting / Tensional / did-not-reach-gravity-status). "Primary (prescriptive side only)" is a fourth thing: a Primary of a candidate narrower than the one generated at §2.1. §2.1 generates G4 as "**The household catechism** — the three parts every Christian must know, taught by the father, examined weekly, sung and prayed morning and evening" — a description of a practice in households. The classification is then awarded to something else: "the household catechism as this world's prescribed formation mechanism." The scope narrowing happens *at classification*, not at generation, and the index table — whose entire purpose, per the Template, is that "a coach or downstream builder can go to confirm, without reading the full document, that every candidate actually received every required check" — hands that reader a value it cannot interpret without reading the full document.

This also leaves the Formation Test doing odd work. The test asks "Does it shape participants?" G4's answer is scored a pass on a prescription; §7's Index shows `P (program) / I/T (achieved)` and §6.1 calls it "Passes five." The same evidence read from the other side — the founder's own testimony that the program was taught and not held — is G13, and is Tensional, "barred from use as evidence of lay state." The document is not inconsistent in substance (it says both of these things deliberately, and G4–G13 is one of its two named structural tensions), but a reader of §6.1 alone sees the same evidentiary situation resolved two different ways with no way to see why.

**Required — one of two, the document's choice, both acceptable:**
- **(a)** Restate the candidate at §2.1 as what is actually classified — "the household catechism as this world's prescribed formation mechanism" — so that a plain **Primary** is awarded to a properly scoped candidate, with the reception gap carried in the Cross-Check column where §6.1 already puts it; or
- **(b)** Classify **Supporting**, on the reading that the Formation Test cannot be passed by a prescription, and carry the prescriptive strength in the Cross-Check column instead.

Either way the Classification column in §6.1 must carry one of the Template's four values with no parenthetical redefining the candidate. ("Primary (provisional)" at G5 is a different case and is fine — Framework para 313 licenses "classify provisionally" by name.)
