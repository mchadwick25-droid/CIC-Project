## S1 — §3.1 mis-cites "in and under" to the Small Catechism; the phrase is the Large Catechism's, and this falsifies §13's verification result

§3.1, "What the sequence is," line 95:

> the examined are admitted to the sign (the Supper **"in and under," SC 496–500**)

**SC 496–500 reads** (`luther_small-catechism_smith1994.txt`):

The phrase "in and under" does not occur at SC 496–500, or anywhere in the Small Catechism file — a whole-file search returns nothing. It occurs at **LC 4075–4076**:

> It is the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, **in and under the bread and wine** which we Christians are commanded by the Word of Christ to eat and to drink.

**Doc_04 §3 G10 has this exactly right** and is the document Doc_07 is drawing on: *"'It is the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, in and under the bread and wine…' (LC 4075–4077); SC 496–500 'the true body and blood… under bread and wine' (per Doc_03 5.3)."* Doc_04 keeps the two loci and the two wordings apart; Doc_07 collapses them and attaches the LC's wording to the SC's locus.

This is not cosmetic for three reasons. (i) "In and under" is G10's headline phrase — the one word-level formulation on which Doc_01 §8.1 rests this world's boundary against the Reformed cities, and the one Doc_03 5.3 carries its whole [AS] tag on. (ii) §13's verification result states "**170 of 172 matched; the two residuals are the two OCR-exempt Cole quotations**." That claim is false: this is a third residual, and it is a real defect, not a checker limitation. (iii) It is the precise failure mode §13's own closing sentence names — *"a substring match against ±1 line confirms a quotation appears near its citation, not that it is* the *quotation the citation names"* — arriving in the document that names it.

**Fix:** either cite LC 4075–4076 for "in and under," or quote SC 496–500's own "under bread and wine." Then correct §13's count.

## S5 — §3.2's headline novelty claim rests on a pairing that runs against one of its two quotations' own context, and sits uncited against a reviewed Doc_05 finding

Both quotations verify verbatim. The use does not.

**LC 4326–4345 in context**, read this pass:

> But if you say: How if I feel that I am not prepared? Answer: That is also my scruple… Because nature and reason see this, they refuse to approach and tarry until they are prepared so long that one week trails another… But if you are to regard how good and pure you are, and labor to have no compunctions, you must never approach.

**Fix:** drop the LC 4337–4338 pairing from the novelty claim (it can stay in the body as what it is — the answer to the over-scrupulous); rest the claim on the coldness cluster alone; and cite Doc_05 §8's "the goal is that the compunction stop" directly, stating how the two sit together.
