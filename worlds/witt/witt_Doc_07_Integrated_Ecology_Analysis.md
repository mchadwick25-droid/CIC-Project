## 3. Smart's seven dimensions, asked in order

### 3.1 Ritual / practical

**What this lens reveals for this world.** Doc_05 §2.2 laid the practices out in three columns — prescribed, claimed, attested — and §3 answered the Framework's seven worship questions. What the ritual lens shows when those are held together with Doc_06's sacramental cluster is *the order in which the rites are meant to be met*, and that this order is the same in the house and the parish: every rite is arranged so that a person arrives at a promise.

**The rites, in the world's own sequence.** The day is the house's: the sign of the cross, the creed and the Lord's Prayer at rising, "with joy go about your work and perhaps sing a song inspired by the Ten Commandments or your own thoughts" (SC 557–558); folded hands and the psalm at table (SC 583–587); the evening's mirror of the morning and "go to sleep immediately with joy" (SC 575); the three parts "when they arise in the morning when they sit down to their meals, and when they retire at night; and until they repeat them, they should be given neither food nor drink" (LC 331–334). The week is the father's examination "at least once a week" (LC 241–243) and, as the movement claimed it, the Mass "every Lord's Day and on the other festivals" (Ap 8492–8493) with German hymns "interspersed here and there" (AC 789–791). The gate is the examination: "none are admitted except they be first examined" (AC 797), examined — per the Apology — on the three parts (Ap 6889–6892). The altar is a testament received, "not with any work or strength or merit, but with faith alone" (v2 7341–7343). Confession is kept, uncompelled, and multiplied: "we must have many absolutions, so that we may strengthen our timid consciences" (v2 15694–15695). The year is un-shaped by principle: meat "every day, whether it be Sunday or Friday, Lent or Advent" (v2 15963–15969, per Doc_03 8.6), the Supper "bound to no place or appointed time" (LC 4287–4288), holy-days observed "without sin" but not as necessary (AC 391–398). Burial is treated at §5.1.

**What the sequence is.** Doc_05 §10B named the chain G2 → G4 → G3 → G5 as the ecology's strongest relationship. The ritual lens shows that chain as the *shape of the rites themselves*: the Word is made sayable (the three parts), the sayable is examined (the father's week; the pastor's gate), the examined are admitted to the sign (the Supper "in and under the bread and wine," LC 4075–4076; the Small Catechism has it as "under bread and wine," SC 496–500 — corrected Revision 1, Round 1 finding S1, which caught this document attaching the Large Catechism's wording to the Small Catechism's locus), and the sign lands in the conscience ("I cannot doubt I have a gracious God," v2 15724). No rite in this world stands outside that sequence; the ones that did — private masses, the Easter compulsion, the seven canonical hours — are exactly the ones refused (v2 14850–14853; v2 15468–15470; LC 71–72).

**What the lens reveals about the kept rites.** Doc_06 §3(b) named the "abolished" assumption as a distortion pattern: a reader assumes this world abolished confession, the Mass, fasting, images, Latin, the year. The ritual lens states the positive rule behind the pattern: this world re-founded nearly every inherited rite by changing what the rite *does* rather than whether it is done — the Mass "retained among us, and celebrated with the highest reverence" (AC 787–788) but as a promise received, not a sacrifice offered (Ap 8551–8553); confession kept but made, in Doc_06 1.2's own analytic phrase, "an occasion of God's promise" rather than a hunt for sins (SC 464–466's own words: "he should not look for other sins or invent them, because that would turn confession into torture" — attributed Revision 1, Round 1 finding C1, which caught the phrase printed as though it were the Small Catechism's own); fasting kept as "excellent disciplines for the body" that do not make one worthy (SC 530–532); Latin kept "on account of those who are learning" (Ap 8512–8513); the old melodies kept and the words changed — "The main point is the correcting of the words, not of the music" (Hy 1051–1052). The single ritual innovation the library shows the movement adding, rather than re-founding, is the German hymn inside the Mass, "which have been added to teach the people" (AC 790–791).

**Gravities most visible.** G3 (the promise-and-sign logic every rite lands in), G4 (the household's day and week), G8 (the "must"/"free" line at the table, Doc_05 §1.2), G11 (the vernacular hymn as the one addition), G13 (the examination gate and the food withheld as the program's answer to negligence).

**What ritual/practical reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §3 found that worship "shapes the day and the week by prescription and un-shapes the year by principle"; §10B found the chain. This lens adds the relationship between them: *the un-shaping of the year and the prescribing of the day are one rule*, the "must"/"free" line applied to time — the day's three hours are a "must" because they carry the three parts, the year's fasts and feasts are "free" because they carry no promise — and the criterion of which rites survive is whether the rite delivers a promise to a conscience. Weight: heavy on the prescriptive side; Inferential/Thin as any household's or parish's practice (Doc_04 §6.3's G4 divergence, carried).

### 3.2 Experiential / emotional

**What this lens reveals for this world.** Doc_05 §8 reconstructed hopes, fears, weighting and patterns; Doc_06 gave the affective Tier 1 vocabulary its depth (2.8 conscience and 4.7 the devil at Tier 1, with 9.1 comfort, 9.3 assurance and 9.2 temptation as Tier 2 satellites — corrected Revision 1, Round 1 finding S2, which caught 4.7 misstated as Tier 2 against both Doc_06's own header and §1's own eighteen-entry list). Held together, they show that this world's emotional ecology is not a climate but a *court*: the affective register has the same structure as the doctrinal one, and the world engineered a specific emotion — terror — on purpose, in order that another could land.

**Dominant register.** Certainty after terror. The confession locates its whole teaching there: "This whole doctrine is to be referred to that conflict of the terrified conscience, neither can it be understood apart from that conflict" (AC 552–553); the catechism's goal is "That I should be certain that such prayers are acceptable" (SC 351–352); the sermons' summit is "I cannot doubt I have a gracious God" (v2 15724). Its daily form is joy commanded twice (SC 557, 575); its sung form is "Cheerful and merry must we be in heart and mind, when we would sing" (Hy 1116–1117, Bacon's English); its last form is death as "a deep, sound, sweet sleep" (Hy 957–962, Bacon's English).

**Emotionally dangerous states.** Three, each named in the world's own words. Despair — the inheritance's product: "many fell into despair" (AC 991–992, per Doc_03 9.3), and the devil's aim: "to despair, denial of God, blasphemy" (LC 3654). Uncertainty as a habit — praying "conditionaliter, conditionally, and therefore uncertainly" (TT 3063–3064, Bell's English, Contested as verbatim), the "old way under the Pope, in which a person tortured himself" (LC 4327–4328). And coldness — the failure the founder and his wife both name: "we are very cold and careless in praying" (TT 3147–3148, Bell's English, Contested as verbatim), "the shameful vice and secret infection of security and satiety" (LC 86–87), those who "become quite brutish" (LC 4247–4248). The list has an order that is itself the finding: the world's first two dangers are too much fear; its third is too little.

**The arc.** Entry is the terrified conscience: the commands "teach a man to know himself… and may despair of his powers," then "the second part of the Scriptures stands ready — the promises of God" (v2 11786–11807, per Doc_04 §3 G5). The middle is the daily exercise of that sequence — the three parts recited, the weekly examination, "many absolutions," the walk to the Sacrament "not upon any worthiness, but upon Thy Word" (LC 4361–4362) — under a life-course of temptation the catechism states as ordinary: "the young suffer especially from the flesh, afterwards, they that attain to middle life and old age, from the world, but others who are occupied with spiritual matters, that is, strong Christians, from the devil" (LC 3677–3680). Maturity is not the end of trial but patience inside it — "my whole life is nothing but patience" (TT 3014–3015, Bell's English) — and the capacity to stand alone before death on a text: "every one must stand on his own feet and be prepared to give battle to the devil. You must rest upon a strong and clear text of Scripture if you would stand the test" (v2 14998–15000).

**Gravities most visible.** G5 (the site hub, Doc_05 §10B), G1 (referred to G5 by the confession itself), G12 (the embattled frame that keeps the Sacrament a food "so as not to fall back in such a battle," LC 4152–4154), G13 (coldness as the named failure), G8 (the deathbed of the weak as the reason liberty is restrained, v2 14798–14801).

**What experiential/emotional reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §8 found the plot "terror, then comfort" and named coldness the failure — "**compunction is not weighted as progress; the goal is that the compunction stop**," with 'labor to have no compunctions, you must never approach' (LC 4337–4338) named there as the error the catechism warns *against* in the over-scrupulous. This lens narrows its own claim to what the coldness evidence alone supports, rather than pairing LC 4337–4338 against its own context (corrected Revision 1, Round 1 finding S5, which caught the original pairing reversed against Doc_05 §8's already-reviewed reading of the same locus): *this world fears the absence of fear more than fear itself.* The Law has an office — "The Law always accuses and terrifies consciences" (Ap 822–823) — and the program is built to deliver that terror on schedule, daily, so that the promise has somewhere to land; the catechism's warning against the "security and satiety" of those who "throw the book into a corner" (LC 86–89), against the "shameful vice…of security" (LC 86–87), and against a coldness that goes "so far that they become quite brutish" (LC 4247–4248) is the evidence this claim actually rests on — a warning against too little fear, not too much, and consistent with, not contrary to, Doc_05 §8's finding about the over-scrupulous at the opposite end of the same scale. The mature practitioner is therefore not a calm person but one whose terror is *answered* — and the emotional texture the Representative will carry is not serenity but the daily alternation, with anger reserved to the magistrate (TT 2428, Bell's English) and, in the founder's own sermons, to the founder (v2 15325–15326; Doc_05 §8's "tonal signature by register").

### 3.3 Narrative / mythic

**What this lens reveals for this world.** Asked of the evidence, this dimension yields thinly — and the thinness is a finding about the world's own mode of transmission, not a coverage failure. Doc_05 §6A stated it: "Storytelling — thin, and the catechism has almost none"; Doc_02 §10's tier table holds eleven candidate stories and no fifth tier; the founder's *Vita* within the window is absent (R93). One caveat belongs at this finding's own point rather than only at §9/§12: the one martyr narrative this library carries (below) is itself a hymn, and the thirty-six hymn texts beyond the four openings read anywhere in this build (Hy 1258 ff.) were not read this pass (OG-10 item 5) — the world's own most narrative-capable register is the least-read one, so this finding should be held as a finding from what was read, not a claim that the register was exhausted (added Revision 1, Round 1 finding C7). What the lens adds is *why*, from the world's own logic.

**What narrative the world does carry.** A restoration plot rather than a founding one: the Gospel "which by God's grace hath again risen" (Hy 912), "the blessed light of Christian truth, suppressed for a time by the pope and his followers, has again risen" (v3 10653–10654, per Doc_03 3.2), the three parts "which have remained in Christendom from of old" (LC 251–253). A cosmic plot supplied by G12: "Fast bound in Satan's chains I lay" (Hy 1276, Bacon's English); "there are two kingdoms in the world mutually militating against each other" (Co 14747–14750, OCR-degraded in the file — "miUtatingagainst" — recovered reading per Doc_04 §3 G12 and Doc_03 7.1); the devil who "sees the true light rising, and to keep it from shining into his eyes he would make a flank attack" (v2 14752–14756). One martyr narrative — the Brussels ballad, whose climax is not the burning but the estate: "True priests of God's own making" (Hy 1789–1800, Bacon's English). The founder's own retellings — Augsburg, Worms — in triple-filtered Table Talk (Doc_02 §10, Tier 2), and his 1545 self-portrait, "Such a Saul was I" (v1 365, Contested as self-characterization). And the Creed's "history," which faith must believe "not merely the history, but also the effect of the history" (AC 568–573).

**Why the yield is thin, in the world's terms.** Three of the world's own commitments work against story as a formation vehicle. First, transformation is located in *hearing a promise*, not in imitating a life — "faith cometh by hearing" (Ap 1045); the exempla the catechism uses are Scripture's, and its one contemporary scene is a negligent pastor (LC 58). Second, the founder refuses to be a subject: "I would gladly have seen all my books forgotten and destroyed" (v1 260–261); "I did nothing; the Word did it all" (v2 14931) — the Article 26 trap Doc_04 §2.2 named, which is also a narrative refusal. Third, the one story the world did tell about its own people ends by dissolving the person into the doctrine: the martyrs matter as "true priests," which is G7's claim, not a hagiographic model of a formed life. The apocalyptic reading that would give this world a larger myth (Oberman, R82) is Dominant Modern Reconstruction at most and unread (Doc_04 §3 G12); the texts' own frame is the devil "like one who well knows and feels that his time is short" (v1 407–408), quoted as the world's own sentence and not as a thesis about it (Doc_05 §7.3).

**Gravities most visible.** G12 (the only supplier of plot), G2 (restoration as the Word's return), G7 (the ballad's climax), G13 (the one contemporary scene is a complaint).

**What narrative/mythic reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §6A found the thinness and §6B found memory to be "an institution of restoration." This lens states what neither says: *the absence of formation stories is itself an expression of the formation logic* — a world that holds "the Word must do this thing, and not we poor sinners" (v2 14917–14918) has no use for the exemplary life as a vehicle, and the Representative will therefore be a figure with doctrine, practice, song and complaint in abundance and almost no tale to tell of anyone but the two Brussels youths. Weight: light; the yield is the result.

### 3.4 Doctrinal / philosophical

**What this lens reveals for this world.** The template's Philosophical Ecology asks how a world structured reality. Doc_05 §2.4 gave the world's seven pieces of its own formation logic and §9A its thirteen theological patterns; Doc_06 gave the conceptual Tier 1 terms their depth. Held together, they show a single ontological commitment under all of them, which this document names as the world's own and which no prior document names as one thing: *what is most real is a word spoken from outside that does what it says.*

**Fundamental ontological assumption.** God speaks first, and the speaking is effective. "Not that man begin lay the first stone, but that God alone, without any entreaty or desire of man, must first come and give him a promise" (v1 11044–11047). The Word "is not like some other silly prattle… but… the power of God" (LC 143–145). It begets — the Church is "the mother that begets and bears every Christian through the Word of God" (LC 2800–2802); it makes sacraments — "when the Word is joined to the element or natural substance, it becomes a Sacrament" (LC 3861–3864); it absolves — "A person receives absolution or forgiveness from the confessor, as if from God Himself, without doubting it" (SC 414–417); it reforms — "I did nothing; the Word did it all" (v2 14931); it fells the devil — "One little word can fell him" (Hy 3695, Bacon's English). The Spirit is given "through the Word and Sacraments, as through instruments," against any who think the Spirit comes "without the external Word" (AC 246–255). Everything Doc_06 §3(c) lists as "given and bound" — faith, conscience, liberty, calling, the Word — follows from this one assumption: each is received, not produced, because the effective agent is the speech, not the hearer.

**The human person.** A conscience under two kinds of speech. "All the Scriptures of God are divided into two parts — commands and promises" (v2 11782–11783); the commands "show us what we ought to do, but do not give us the power to do it" (v2 11786–11788); the promises "give what the commands of God ask" (v2 11817–11818). The person is "born with sin," "without the fear of God, without trust in God, and with concupiscence" (AC 192–207, per Doc_03 2.10); the will is bound — Erasmus alone "saw, what was the [grand] hinge, upon which the [whole] turned," free will being, in Doc_04 §3 G1's own gloss on the same quotation, "the ground of merit" (Co 15139–15142, OCR-degraded in the file; the bracketed words are Doc_02 §1.1's recovered reading per [R34], quoted with Doc_04's gloss restored Revision 1, Round 1 finding C2, which caught the doctrinal claim left uncited when the gloss was dropped); and the conscience is "not a guide but a court" (Doc_06 2.8) — terrified by the Law, comforted by the promise, burdened wrongly by "human traditions instituted to propitiate God, to merit grace, and to make satisfaction for sins" (AC 400–402; the founder's own name for them is "doctrines of men," Doc_03 3.4). To have a god at all is to trust: "the confidence and faith of the heart alone make both God and an idol" (LC 411–412, per Doc_06 §10 item 1). The person's fundamental problem is not ignorance but uncertainty; its fundamental possibility is a verdict from outside.

**Temporal assumptions.** Restoration, not novelty (§3.3); a daily rather than a yearly time — baptism means the old Adam "should be drowned by daily sorrow and repentance" and "a new person daily come forth" (SC 398–400), and "we shall daily obtain… nothing but the forgiveness of sin" (LC 2887–2889, per Doc_03); the liturgical year made free (§3.1); the deathbed as the last exam (v2 14641–14644); and an eschatology the library holds only thinly — the 1545 preface's friar who "shrank from the Last Day with great anxiety and fear and terror" (v1 373–375) is remembered, not prescribed, and the devil's short time (v1 407–408) is a simile. That thinness is a finding of the same kind as §3.3's: the world's time is the day, the week, and the hour of death, not the age.

**How the philosophical ecology grounds the emotional.** A court can be made sure only by a verdict from outside it. That is why §3.2's terror is engineered and its comfort is external — "put yourself into this YOU, that He may not speak to you in vain" (LC 4376–4377, capitals Bente/Dau's) — and why the world's diagnosis of its inheritance is exactly that it prayed "at a venture" (LC 3083): a promise not spoken is a conscience not sure. The affective plot and the speech-act ontology are one structure seen from two sides.

**Gravities most visible.** G2 (the agent), G1 (the content), G3 (the promise joined to a sign), G5 (the site), the Law/Gospel fold (the grammar, Doc_04 §2.2), G10 (the plain sense taken "clinging simply to His word," v2 7185–7188).

**What doctrinal/philosophical reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §10B found the Word to be the *agent* hub and the conscience the *site* hub. This lens states the ontology that makes those two hubs necessary rather than merely observed: if the effective agent is external speech and the person is a court, then the ecology must have exactly one agent and exactly one site, and every practice must be a way of carrying the one to the other. It also explains a feature no prior document explains — why the ecology's sharpest tension is G2–G8 ("right according to the Scriptures… but what becomes of order?", v2 14761–14762): the same ontology that makes the Word the only agent forbids anyone else from acting as one — "We have the *jus verbi*, but not the *executio*" (v2 14864–14866) — so restraint is not a counter-principle to the Word but the Word's own consequence for its preachers.

### 3.5 Ethical / legal

**What this lens reveals for this world.** M4 added this lens because it was "the one Smart dimension with no CiC lens," and Doc_05 has no section for it: its material is scattered across §2.3 (communal expectations), §4.3 (the criterion of legitimacy), §5 (ministry as vocation), §7.1 (the "must"/"free" boundary) and §1.4 (the economic datum). Gathered, the material shows something the scattering hid: this world has a *legal* theory precisely at the point where it refuses law, and its ethics is a doctrine of what may not be commanded.

**The ethical content.** Good works are redefined by their site: "when they work at their trade, walk, stand, eat, drink, sleep" (v1 6863–6865), "no good works except those which God has commanded" (v1 6817–6818, per Doc_03 2.3); every estate is holy (SC 611–612); the neighbour is the measure — "There must also be love, and through love we must do unto one another as God has done unto us through faith. For without love faith is nothing" (v2 14672–14674); "In all these matters love is the captain" (v2 15142–15143). The economy is confessed at the household scale — "charged him too much, cheated him and sold him badly made goods" (SC 458–459) — and named at the market's: "bad coin… daily oppression and raising of prices in common trade, bargaining and labor on the part of those who wantonly oppress the poor and deprive them of their daily bread" (LC 3535–3541), answered not by a statute but by exclusion from the household's prayer (Doc_05 §1.4). Forgiveness is reciprocal and signed: "if you forgive, you have this consolation and assurance, that you are forgiven in heaven" (LC 3597–3599). Failure has the founder's own measure: "no one as yet has given his goods to the poor, no one has yet been burned" (v2 15586–15587) — a complaint, not a fact about the town (Discipline 6).

**The legal content.** Two governments, both God's: the Church's power "without human force, simply by the Word" (AC 1339–1340), the sword's by "bodily punishments in order to preserve civil justice and peace" (AC 1306–1309, per Doc_03 7.1); obedience "save only when commanded to sin; for then they ought to obey God rather than men" (AC 426–428); the ban "before the whole assembly" by Matthew 18 (v2 15646) — prescribed and, by the founder's admission, absent (v2 15629–15631). The household is a legal order in its own right: "among us there must necessarily be such inequality and ordered difference, and therefore God commands it to be observed, that you obey me as your father, and that I have the supremacy" (LC 1072–1075). And the world's shortest rule of law is a rule against it: "the less law, the more justice; the fewer commandments, the more good works" (v1 10917–10918).

**The jurisprudence of the free.** The world's own test for a wicked law is stated in both voices: traditions become "opposed to the Gospel" when "instituted to propitiate God, to merit grace, and to make satisfaction for sins" (AC 400–404, per Doc_03 3.4); "it is wicked to make a necessity and a commandment of that which is free" (v2 15979–15980); "consciences are not to be burdened" (AC 396–398). The categories are "must" and "free" (v2 14790–14796), and the rule for the free is the weak: "the use of liberty is to be so controlled that the inexperienced may not be offended" (Ap 6978–6979). This is a jurisprudence — it has categories, a test, a rule of application, and a named court (the conscience) — and its object is to *limit what may be commanded*, not to command.

**The exception the program keeps.** Doc_04 §5's G4–G8 cell recorded it: the catechism is "the 'must' taught as such," and "food withheld 'until they repeat them' (LC 333–334) is compulsion where G8 forbids it." Doc_05 §2.3 recorded the 1529 turn: "no one should by any means be coerced or compelled, lest we institute a new murdering of souls," and yet those who withdraw from the Supper "are not to be considered Christians" (LC 4250–4254); "If you wish such liberty, you may just as well have the liberty to be no Christian" (LC 4295–4296). The ethical/legal lens names the shape: the world commands exactly one thing — that the promise be *heard* (the three parts said, the examination passed, the table attended) — and forbids the commanding of everything else; the one compulsion it keeps is the one that carries the Word to the ears.

**Gravities most visible.** G8 (the categories), G6 (the two governments), G7 (works redefined by estate), G5 (the court), G4 (the one "must"), G2 (the power that acts "simply by the Word").

**What ethical/legal reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §4.3 gave the criterion of legitimacy and §10B named the "must"/"free" tension. This lens shows the two as one system and states its rule: *in this world a command is legitimate only if it delivers a promise, and illegitimate if it binds a conscience apart from one* — which is why the same texts can withhold a child's supper and forbid the pope's Easter law without contradiction. It also identifies where that rule was tested hardest and cannot be seen: the peasants (1525) and the settlement (1555) are the two moments at which the sword's side of the doctrine was actually exercised, and both are outside the library (Doc_04 §10 item 4). Weight: moderate–heavy; the world's own theory is full, its application at the parish absent.

### 3.6 Social / institutional

**What this lens reveals for this world.** Doc_05 §4 built the Organizational lens around G6, G7 and G2's *jus verbi*/*executio*, and §4.4 found authority "felt as *being called and being answerable*, not as rank." The social/institutional lens, asked with Doc_05 §1.1's participants and §2.1's relationships in view, shows the structure those findings imply: authority in this world runs downward into the house and upward to the prince by the same warrant, and has no independent clerical middle.

**Primary sources of authority.** The Word, through a call. "Through baptism all of us are consecrated to the priesthood" (v2 2176–2177) makes the father a priest in his house (SC 45; LC 4497–4499) and the temporal rulers "priests and bishops" (v2 2211–2214); "there is among them no difference at all but that of office" (v2 2163–2164). The public office is fenced from below — "no one must put himself forward… without our consent and election" (v2 2216–2219, per Doc_04 §5); "I was regularly called by the Council to preach in this place" (v2 14778–14779); "unless he be regularly called" (AC 383–384) — and the office is not the man: "when deposed, he is a peasant or a townsman like the rest" (v2 2223–2226, per Doc_03 6.2). The Fathers are witnesses, not judges (AC 534–537; v1 292–294); the bishops obeyed "But when they teach or ordain anything against the Gospel, then the congregations have a commandment of God prohibiting obedience" (AC 1342–1344); the founder refuses his own person as an authority (v1 260–261; v2 14931) and claims only a call and a text (Doc_05 §4.2).

**Transmission of authority.** Not succession but call and examination: the father examines the household (LC 241–243), the pastor examines the communicant (AC 797), the visitor — by reference only [R51] — examines the parish, and the prince inspects the church he protects (Doc_04 §3 G6). "We train the people who are to come after us and succeed us in our office and work, in order that they also may bring up their children" (LC 4493–4495): the ecology's own account of succession is the household, not the chapter.

**Sites of contestation, held as the world held them.** G2–G8 (Doc_04 §5): the Word licenses, love restrains. The 1522 congregation acting on its own leaders' program before the founder's return — addressed as one body that "have gone too fast" (v2 14727–14728), not as a second pattern (Doc_04 §4; Doc_05 §10B). The two voices' naming — "office/estate" against "calling" (G7), "Antichrist" against "the Church of Rome" (Doc_03 6.7 [PV]) — "a plurality inside one program that a Representative must carry" (Doc_05 §10B). And the founder's own authority, exercised as personal only in the threat to leave (v2 15400–15403) and framed even then as what the Word requires.

**Authority and formation.** The household father is "the ecology's first officer" (Doc_05 §4.3) because his whole work is to make each person able to stand alone on a text — "every one must know for himself the chief things in Christianity, and be armed therewith" (v2 14645–14646). The pastor's formation of others is prescribed to the hour and his own formation is nowhere in the library (Doc_02 §9; Doc_05 §10A). Rank is real and confessed from — "Consider here your place in life according to the Ten Commandments. Are you a father? A mother?… A servant?" (SC 428–430) — and no one may compel faith: "I can drive no man to heaven with a club" (v2 14974).

**Gravities most visible.** G7 (the warrant), G6 (the frame), G2 (the criterion), G8 (the pace), G4 (the house as the first office), G13 (the negligence the structure exists to answer).

**What social/institutional reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §10A found the pastor "the office that ran the parishes" and "under" relative to probable ecology because it "has no voice of its own in the library." This lens states a structural reason that is the world's own, not the library's: the pastor is the one office in this ecology that is *answerable in both directions and authoritative in neither on its own* — fenced from below by the call ("regularly called"), rebuked from above by the founder ("swineherds and dog-tenders," LC 68), inspected by the prince's visitor (by reference), starved by nobles who "let the parishes decay" (LC 95–96), and owed honor by hearers (SC 619). The baptismal warrant that makes every father and prince a priest leaves the pastor with an office and no estate — which is Supporting-shaped behaviour for G7 exactly as Doc_04 §3 classified it, and is why the parish is the ecology's largest silence in the world's own structure and not only in the library's.

### 3.7 Material

**What this lens reveals for this world.** M4 promoted this lens to required. Doc_02 §6 opens "None is vendored," and the Framework's own rule applies: "The ecology existed in material conditions whether or not those conditions are recoverable." Asked of what the library holds *by report*, the lens yields a moderate result, and one thing the prior documents record separately but do not connect: the material vehicle of this ecology is the printed vernacular object in a stratified house — and the world itself named that object as a danger to its own formation.

**The material formation practices the world reports.** The body's day: the sign of the cross, kneeling, folded hands, the table, the bed (SC 543–607); "flesh and blood remain flesh and blood" (LC 1726–1729); the sick neighbour helped (SC 93); epidemic only as a stock item in the fourth petition's list of calamities (LC 3507–3509; Doc_02 §6). The house: stratified and served, "his domestics, ma-servants and maid-servants" (LC 335–336, typo as found), with a Home Chart of estates from bishops to "Servants, Maids, Hired Hands and Workers" (SC 609–668) — Documented as presupposition, Inferential/Thin as to how widely such households existed outside towns (Doc_02 §6). The objects: the printed quarto and the hymn-sheet (Doc_02 §6's instruction to Doc_05, discharged at §1.4); the catechism as the thing one can "read through at one time, and then immediately know it, throw the book into a corner" (LC 87–89); the hymnal "with name attached" (Hy 1084) and "not our book published at Wittenberg" if amended (Hy 1093–1094); the charity-scholars singing "from door to door" for their bread (Hy 775–776, Walter per [R45], late, three hands); the loaf the world would stamp on the prince's coat-of-arms "instead of a lion" (LC 3480–3484); the churchyard renamed "Cemeteries," "resting and sleeping places" (Hy 980–981); the coin (LC 3535–3541). The inherited objects refused in their bodily forms: the altar-linen no woman may wash (v2 15300–15302), the cowl that "will not kill him" (v2 15026), the saints as functional protectors — Lawrence against fire, Sebastian against pestilence (LC 451–455, per Doc_02 §6) — replaced by the Word as "the true holy water and holy sign from which he flees" (LC 137–138). Print as the one richly documented material register: twelve known editions of one treatise, printers named (v1 12304–12309 [R85], editorial); the woodcut polemic glimpsed only in Cole's 1823 advertisement (Doc_02 §6).

**How material culture connected to the gravities and formation logic.** The body is where G4's program runs (the three hours, the table, the food withheld) and where G8's line is felt (meat on Friday; the deathbed, v2 14798–14801); the printed object is G11's medium and G2's carrier; the house is G9's site and G7's first office. The tune — the one material item the movement itself said mattered most, "The notes and melodies are of great price; it were pity to let them perish" (Hy 1017–1018) — is the item this library lost at its last transmission step, "a plain-text conversion" (Doc_05 §7.3's table).

**Gravities most visible.** G4, G11, G9, G8, G12 (the saints' protections replaced), G13 (the print market corrupting the hymns).

**What material reveals that Doc_05 alone did not.** Doc_05 §1.4 found that print "enters the house as the book itself, and the world already feared what the book would do," and §2.5(d) found the hymnal as "the medium turned against the community's own song." This lens connects those to the world's ontology (§3.4): a world whose effective agent is the *spoken* Word — "faith cometh by hearing" (Ap 1045); "I can get no farther than to men's ears" (v2 14859–14860) — must regard its own printed object with suspicion, because a book can be owned without being heard. The founder's wish that his books "will yet be forgotten in the dust" (v1 299–300), the nobles' "we have everything in books, and every one can easily learn it by himself" (LC 94–95), and the hymns "more perverted the more they are printed" (Hy 1068–1069) are one material anxiety, and it is the ecology's own: the medium that carried the household catechism is, in the world's consciousness, the medium most likely to let the ears go cold. Weight: thin by evidence, moderate by report; the yield is stated as such.

## 4. CiC's two additions

### 4.1 Boundary Structures

**What this lens reveals for this world.** Doc_05 §7 listed ten boundaries the world drew (§7.1), how the edge was felt (§7.2), and the three movements with Transmission Specificity (§7.3). This lens does not re-present those (Gallic S6 is the error class); it asks what they have in common when the forces from Doc_01 §7, Doc_02 §13 and Doc_04's notations are laid over them, and finds one rule and one asymmetry. The rule: *this world's boundary is drawn at the conscience, not at the territory.* The asymmetry: the boundaries history drew hardest are the ones this library cannot show being felt.

**Membership logic.** Inside is defined by hearing and coming. "He who does not know this could not be numbered with the Christians nor be admitted to any Sacrament" (LC 233–234); "none are admitted except they be first examined" (AC 797); "We Christians have no other outward sign by which we may be distinguished from others than this sacrament and baptism" (v2 15446–15447); those who withdraw "so long a time are not to be considered Christians" (LC 4252–4254), though "no one should by any means be coerced or compelled" (LC 4250–4251). The body is "the congregation of saints, in which the Gospel is rightly taught and the Sacraments are rightly administered" (AC 277–279), known by its marks and not by its hierarchy (Doc_06 5.8); its inner edge counts "Muscovites, Russians, Greeks, Bohemians" inside Christendom though outside Rome's confirmation (v1 12786–12796, per Doc_03 6.6). The weak are inside by love before they are inside by knowledge: "brothers and sisters on the other side who belong to us, and must still be won" (v2 14728–14729). ### 4.2 Formation Logic — the synthesis lens, applied last

**What this lens reveals for this world.** The organizing principle that unifies §3.1–§3.7 and §4.1 can be named in the world's own two sentences: "I can get no farther than to men's ears; their hearts I cannot reach" (v2 14859–14860), and "That I should be certain that such prayers are acceptable" (SC 351–352). *This world forms a person by carrying God's promise to the ears — word for word, in the person's own tongue, daily, examined and absolved — until the terrified conscience is made sure by it; the teacher can reach only the ears, the Word must do the rest, and the pace is set by the weakest hearer.* Everything the seven dimensions showed is a way of moving the promise from the ears to the conscience, or a name for what happens when it does not arrive.

## 8. Integrative Observation
