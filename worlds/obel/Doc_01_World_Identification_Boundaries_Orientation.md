## 1. Distinct World Criteria

What makes this a distinct formation world, not merely "17th-century
Russian Orthodoxy in general":

- **A single, datable rupture with a named cause.** In the 1650s, Patriarch
  Nikon corrected the Russian liturgical books and certain ritual
  practices against contemporary Greek Orthodox usage. The sign of the
  cross made with three fingers instead of two is **[Documented]**,
  verified directly against this world's own vendored source (§9 below,
  the p. 120 dialogue). The spelling of the name of Jesus (Isus → Iisus),
  the direction of processions around a church, and the number of
  prosphora used in the liturgy are **[Widely Accepted]** as part of the
  standard historical account of Nikon's corrections — this document has
  not yet independently verified these three specific details against a
  vendored primary source, and states them at that lower confidence
  rather than borrow the two-fingers detail's own stronger verification
  for the whole list. A substantial part of the Russian church refused
  these changes as innovations rather than corrections. The Moscow
  council of 1666-1667 anathematized those who refused — **[Documented]**
  on the census's own record and the Decision-Log's own Era 8 gate entry
  (§2.1 below). This is a single, nameable event with a named cause
  (Nikon's reforms) and a named consequence (anathema), not a diffuse
  regional or generational drift.
- **A recurring, sharply evidenced self-understanding: fidelity to
  inherited ritual practice as the whole of the case.** The movement's own
  central voice states this directly and repeatedly. Archpriest Avvakum,
  in his own dialogue with the Eastern patriarchs recorded in his own
  autobiography, argues the dispute entirely on the ground of continuity
  with "the tradition of our holy fathers" and inherited practice, naming,
  in the file's own exact spelling, "Meletina of Antioch, Theodoret, the
  Heart Bishop of Cyrene, Peter of Damascus and Maxim the Greek" as
  authorities for the two-fingered sign of the cross
  (`avvakum_life-of-archpriest-avvakum_harrison-mirrlees1924.txt`,
  p. 120). **Two names in this list are given here exactly as the
  vendored file spells them, not silently normalized to the more familiar
  historical form, per this project's own quote-fidelity discipline:**
  "Meletina" is almost certainly this translation's own rendering of
  Meletius (of Antioch, a genuine 4th-century patristic authority whom an
  Old Believer writer citing two-fingered practice could plausibly
  invoke) — but this document does not silently correct it to "Meletius,"
  since doing so risks exactly the misattribution error this project's
  own governing rules warn against; the possibility that "Meletina" names
  a different, less familiar figure is not ruled out. "The Heart Bishop of
  Cyrene" is likewise left as printed — evidently a corruption of "the
  Bishop of Cyrus" (Theodoret of Cyrus, already named as "Theodoret"
  earlier in the same list, so if this phrase names the same man twice
  that is itself worth flagging, not assumed) — rather than resolved by
  assumption. Both flagged for Doc_02/Doc_03's own further checking, not
  resolved here. **[Documented]**, verified directly against the vendored
  file, as to the fact that this list of names appears and is invoked for
  this purpose; **[Inferential-Thin]** as to the identity of "Meletina"
  and "the Heart Bishop of Cyrene" specifically.
- **A recurring pattern of authority, suffering, and testimony.** Exile,
  underground imprisonment, and execution for refusing the corrected rite
  recur across the movement's own record — Avvakum's own decade in
  Siberian exile and his final fourteen years imprisoned in an earthen pit
  at Pustozersk before his burning in 1682; the eight-year siege and fall
  of the Solovetsky Monastery (1668-1676); the mass self-immolations of
  the 1670s-1680s. These are not isolated incidents but a recurring
  pattern the movement's own literature (a martyrology genre, by its own
  later practitioners' own naming — Semyon Denisov's histories of the
  Solovki fathers and sufferers) treats as constitutive of what the
  movement understands itself to be.
- **What distinguishes this world from its nearest neighbor.** From the
  official Synodal Russian church (Atlas entry unbuilt as of this
  writing; the Era 8 gate's own Decision-Log entry, 2026-08-03, records
  that "the Synodal Russian church" was drafted at the same gate pass with
  "Old Believers re-scoped as dissent" — i.e., this world was deliberately
  kept as its own separate Atlas entry, VII.7, rather than folded into the
  Synodal church's own entry), this world is the ritual position the 1666
  council's own anathema was directed against, not the anathematizing
  body. Doc_01 §9 below states plainly why this is a ritual and not a
  doctrinal distinction.

## 2. Temporal Scope

### 2.1 Beginning point: 1666, the Moscow council's anathema

The census's own `start` field gives 1666. Nikon's own liturgical
corrections began circulating from the mid-1650s (the printed service
books of 1653-1656), and resistance to them predates the council — but
1666-1667 is the point at which refusal to accept the corrections became
a formally anathematized position rather than one live option among
others inside the church, which is the boundary this world's own identity
turns on (§1 above: a single, named rupture). **[Documented]**, on the
census's own record; this document's own vendored source does not carry
an independent, dated account of the council itself (Avvakum's
autobiography narrates his own life and exile, not the council's own
proceedings as an institutional event) — a genuine limit of this world's
current library, named here and carried to Doc_02 and
`Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` rather than papered over with a confidence tag
the source does not support.

### 2.2 Ending point: 1815 — the Era 8 portfolio boundary, not an argued
movement-specific closing point

**This is stated plainly rather than left to imply a false historical
precision.** Era 8 is fleet-scoped as 1650-1815, per a forward-reference
note at the close of the immediately preceding Era 7 Frozen entry
(`Ministry/Features/Atlas-World-Map/Decision-Log.md`, 2026-08-02):
"Next: A1.E8 (1650–1815) with its banked forward flags... Old Believers
at era 8..." — this world is named directly, confirming it was already
in view when this boundary was set (the source's own en-dash preserved
exactly as printed in this quotation; this document's own surrounding
prose uses a plain hyphen for readability). The Era 8 gate's own Frozen
entry (2026-08-03, "ERA 8 FROZEN by Mark") separately records that this
gate pass "replaced" a "round-1800 artifact cluster" across several Era 8
entries "with honest 1815 caps" — i.e., 1815 is the fleet's own
portfolio-level closing year for the whole era, applied across multiple
movements at the same gate pass, not a date independently argued from
this movement's own history. This document does not re-litigate that
portfolio-level decision (outside this thread's authority; see the
escalation categories in `CLAUDE.md` and the build-cycle skill) but
states its actual basis rather than imply a stronger claim than the
record supports.

Within that boundary, the nearest genuinely Old-Believer-relevant event is
**Edinoverie's establishment in 1800** — the first formal reconciliation
mechanism between the Synodal church and old-rite worship, permitting
communities to keep the pre-Nikonian rite under Synodal episcopal
oversight. The census's own `statusDescription` already names this as
giving the mechanical 1815 cap "a stated reason" where a "previously
near-principled accident" existed before. This document treats Edinoverie
(1800) as the real, substantive event nearest the window's close, and
1815 itself as the administrative boundary — a distinction Doc_02 and
later documents should preserve rather than collapse.

**Explicitly out of scope, as a later development:** the priestly Old
Believers' restoration of their own episcopate, the Belokrinitsa
hierarchy, in 1846 — thirty-one years after this window's own close. Named
here so a later document does not need to rediscover that it falls
outside scope.

### 2.3 What remains internal vs. what indicates transition

**Internal to this world, across the whole 1666-1815 span:** the refusal
of the corrected rite as the movement's own constitutive act; martyrdom
and suffering under state persecution as a recurring, expected pattern
rather than an isolated episode; and the movement's own literature of
self-witness (autobiography, petition, martyrology) as the primary way it
records itself, from Avvakum's own Life (c. 1673) through Denisov's
histories of Solovki and the Vyg community (1720s-1730s) and the Pomorian
Answers (1723).

**Indicates transition, within the window:**

- **1668-1676 — the Solovetsky Monastery siege and fall.** The monastery's
  own fifth petition (1667) is, per the census's own sourcing note, "the
  fullest early statement of the Old Believer case," and its fall by
  betrayal in January 1676, with the tsar's own death a week later, was
  read by the movement itself as judgment. This document has not yet read
  the petition or a siege account directly (neither is vendored; see
  Doc_02 §3, acquisition leads) and states this at the confidence its
  current library supports: **[Widely Accepted]**, on the strength of
  multiple independent secondary accounts already on record in the census
  (Crummey; Michels), not yet independently verified against a primary
  text this pass.
- **1670s-1680s — the mass self-immolations, and the movement's own
  internal disagreement about them.** This is not a single settled
  practice but a contested one within the movement's own record. Avvakum's
  own Pustozersk epistles are cited (census; not yet independently
  verified against a vendored file this pass) as approving the practice;
  Evfrosin's *Otrazitel'noe pisanie o novoizobretennom puti
  samoubiistvennykh smertei* (1691) is a contemporary Old Believer tract
  arguing against it. **This document treats self-immolation as a real,
  disputed practice within the movement's own record, not a univocal
  position the whole movement held** — the single most important framing
  point for Doc_09's later story work, named here so it is not lost.
  Death-toll figures (e.g. the ~2,700 figure at Paleostrovsky Monastery in
  1687) are **[Contested]**: the census's own sourcing note states state
  reports, Old Believer martyrologies, and hostile accounts "agree on the
  fact and disagree on the numbers," and this document has not yet
  independently verified any specific figure against a primary source.
- **1723 — the Pomorian Answers.** The Vyg community's formal, written
  self-defense (bespopovtsy) marks the point at which the priestless
  position moves from practical improvisation to an articulated,
  systematic theological argument. Not yet vendored (Doc_02 §3).
- **1800 — Edinoverie**, already discussed at §2.2, is also a genuine
  internal transition: the point at which formal reconciliation with the
  Synodal church, on terms preserving the old rite, becomes possible for
  the first time within this window — a real softening of the boundary
  this world's own identity otherwise turns on (§1).

## 3. Geographic Scope

**Core:** Central and northern Muscovite Russia at the movement's own
founding — Moscow (the 1666-1667 council; Avvakum's own early ministry
and disputations), and the Russian north — the White Sea coast and the
Solovetsky Monastery, the Vyg community on Lake Onega's own watershed. The
census's own `region` field states "Russian north, Siberia." **[Documented]**
on the census's own record.

**Siberia**, specifically the Dauria region beyond Lake Baikal and the
Tobolsk-Yeniseysk corridor, enters this world's own geography directly
through Avvakum's own decade of exile there (1653-1663) under voevoda
Afanasy Pashkov, narrated at length in his own autobiography — this is not
a secondary or inferred detail but the setting of a large fraction of the
Life's own narrative. **[Documented]**, verified directly against the
vendored file (the exile narrative runs continuously across a large
portion of the autobiography's own middle section, including the
Markovna passage at p. 80, discussed at §9 below).

**Expansion within window:** the movement's own later dispersal into
Siberia (as ongoing refuge, not only Avvakum's own imposed exile), the
Baltic communities (Vetka, later dispersed), and — per the census's own
`legacy` field, describing the movement's continuing life well past this
window's own close — eventual communities as far as Alaska, Oregon, and
South America. This document treats the far dispersal (post-1815) as
outside this world's own window (§2.2) and the Siberian/northern core as
the geography this world's own construction should center on.

## 4. Cultural Scope

**Language:** Russian and Church Slavonic — the liturgical and literary
register of the movement's own self-record. Avvakum's autobiography is
written, by his own translators' account, in "the plain, angry, comic
Russian of speech rather than of books" (census `documentedStories`
entry), a genuinely distinctive register within this world's own
literature: a deliberately plain, spoken idiom set directly against the
literary Church Slavonic of official church writing. Avvakum's own
opening declaration states this directly, in the original-language text
(`avvakum_zhitie-protopopa-avvakuma-orv_wikisource-transcription-nd.txt`,
opening lines), quoted verbatim rather than translated (no vendored
English translation of this specific passage exists — see the
discrepancy noted immediately below):

> "не позазрите просторечию нашему, понеже люблю свой русской природной
> язык, виршами философскими не обык речи красить, понеже не словес
> красных бог слушает, но дел наших хощет."

In substance — this document's own working gloss, not a vendored
translation and not itself to be quoted as one — Avvakum asks the reader
not to scorn his plain speech, since he loves his own native Russian
tongue and is not in the habit of decorating his speech with
philosophical verses (*вирши философские*, a borrowed literary-verse
form), because God does not listen to fine words but wants deeds. This is
a genuine, quotable statement of the movement's own self-conscious
literary register, in the movement's own original language. **A material
finding for Doc_02, not yet resolved: the vendored
1924 English translation's own opening does not carry this same
declaration in the same place** — its own narrative begins instead at
"Avvakum, archpriest, was bidden by the monk Epiphanius... to write down
my life" (p. 32), which is a different passage than the original's own
opening rhetorical declaration. This document does not know, on the
evidence in hand, whether the English translators abridged, relocated, or
handled this passage differently — a genuine open question for Doc_02,
not resolved by assumption.

**Material and visual culture**, per the census's own `legacy` field: old
icon-painting styles, znamenny chant, and manuscript-copying practices
that the official church had modernized away survived specifically in Old
Believer communities and were later recovered from them by collectors and
scholars. This document has no vendored source for this claim beyond the
census's own assertion and marks it **[Widely Accepted]**, not yet
independently verified.

## 5. World Separation Criteria

This world is separated from the Synodal Russian church (its immediate
institutional origin and ongoing adversary) by the single ritual rupture
named at §1. It is separated from other Orthodox formation worlds in this
project's own fleet (none yet built in the Greek East / Orthodoxy lane as
of this writing, per `cic/corpus-map/WORKS.yaml`'s own scan — see Doc_02
§2) by language, geography, and the specific 1666-1667 rupture, which has
no equivalent event outside Russian Orthodoxy. No cross-world overlap or
boundary tension was found this pass (Doc_02 §9 confirms this
independently).

## 6. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

**Not decided at this stage — carried forward as an explicit open
question, per Step 0 §4.** The movement divided, within this window,
between those who retained priests ordained before 1666 or received from
elsewhere (popovtsy) and those who concluded, over time, that a valid
priesthood could no longer be had at all (bezpopovtsy) — a real,
consequential internal division, though one that (per §9 below) does not
touch the Creed itself. The Vyg community and the Pomorian Answers (1723)
are a bezpopovtsy production; the Belokrinitsa hierarchy (1846, out of
scope, §2.2) is the eventual popovtsy resolution.

This document does not force a strand determination now, for two
reasons. First, Article-21 strand analysis (per the PAHC/IJC precedent
V1.8 §2 names) is substantial work properly done once the library
supports it — this world's current vendored library is Avvakum's own
voice only, and Avvakum himself died in 1682, decades before the
popovtsy/bezpopovtsy division hardened into two distinct, self-conscious
positions. Reasoning about the strand question from Avvakum's own text
alone risks reading a later division backward into a source that predates
it. Second, the Representative-identity decision (M1, after Doc_09) is
the point at which this question's practical weight actually bears — and
deciding a strand determination now, before that library is built out,
risks constraining that later, more consequential decision on a thinner
evidentiary base than it deserves.

**Carried to `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (entry 1)** as a live boundary
question for the world-build thread (Step 3 onward) to resolve once
Doc_03-Doc_09's own research has filled in the priestless/priestly
division's own evidentiary base.

## 9. The floor: ritual schism, not doctrinal heresy — stated plainly, per
Step 0 §2 and the task's own instruction

**No question of doctrine arises here.** Old Believers held the same
Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed as the church they left. The dispute, on
the movement's own terms, is about ritual practice and the authority to
alter it — not about the content of belief. This is independently
verified against the vendored primary source, not merely repeated from
the census. Avvakum's own dialogue with the Eastern patriarchs at the
Chudov Monastery, quoted verbatim (`avvakum_life-of-archpriest-avvakum_
harrison-mirrlees1924.txt`, p. 120):

> "Why," said they, "art thou stubborn? The folk of Palestine, Serbia,
> Albania, the Wallachians, they of Rome and Poland, all these do cross
> themselves with three fingers, only thou standest out in thine
> obstinacy and dost cross thyself with two fingers; it is not seemly."

And Avvakum's own reply, also quoted verbatim from the same page:

> "By the gift of God among us there is autocracy; till the time of
> Nikon, the apostate, in our Russia under our pious princes and tsars
> the orthodox faith was pure and undefiled, and in the Church was no
> sedition. Nikon, the wolf, together with the devil, ordained that men
> should cross themselves with three fingers, but our first shepherds
> made the sign of the cross and blessed men as of old with two fingers,
> according to the tradition of our holy fathers..."

Neither side's own words, in this exchange, raise a question about the
content of the Creed. The whole argument, on Avvakum's own terms, is
about which side has kept the inherited practice and who had the
authority to change it. This document states this plainly, as instructed,
rather than let the movement's own fierce, uncompromising language about
Nikon ("the wolf," "the apostate," "together with the devil") be
misread as evidence of a doctrinal dispute — it is the language of a
ritual betrayal argued in the highest possible register, not a
theological heresy charge.

**A further, separate honesty point, carried to Doc_02 rather than
resolved here:** the priestless (bezpopovtsy) branch's own later
reasoning — that a valid priesthood could no longer be had at all, because
the visible church itself had fallen to Antichrist — edges toward
ecclesiological territory that is doctrine-adjacent, even though it does
not touch the Creed's own content. §6 above already declines to resolve
the strand question at this stage for this same reason; this document
flags the tension explicitly rather than let the floor claim (properly
made about the 1666-1667 rupture and Avvakum's own generation) be read as
a claim about every later development inside this movement's whole
window.
