## 1. Distinct World Criteria

What makes this a distinct formation world, not merely "17th-century
Russian Orthodoxy in general":

- **A single, datable rupture with a named cause.** In the 1650s, Patriarch
  Nikon corrected the Russian liturgical books and certain ritual
  practices against contemporary Greek Orthodox usage. The sign of the
  cross made with three fingers instead of two is **[Documented]**,
  verified directly against this world's own vendored source (§9 below,
  the p. 120–121 dialogue). The spelling of the name of Jesus (Isus →
  Iisus), the direction of processions around a church, and the number of
  prosphora used in the liturgy are **[Widely Accepted]** as part of the
  standard historical account of Nikon's corrections — not yet
  independently verified against a vendored primary source, stated at
  that lower confidence rather than borrowed from the two-fingers
  detail's own stronger verification. A substantial part of the Russian
  church refused these changes as innovations rather than corrections.
  The Moscow council of 1666-1667 anathematized those who refused —
  **[Widely Accepted]**, on the census's own record and the Decision-Log's
  own Era 8 gate entry (§2.1 below; **corrected, Round 2 review Finding
  23** — a project-internal census record is not itself a primary source,
  so this is tagged at the tier that reflects what was actually
  consulted, not [Documented]). This is a single, nameable event with a
  named cause (Nikon's reforms) and a named consequence (anathema), not a
  diffuse regional or generational drift.
- **A recurring, sharply evidenced self-understanding: fidelity to
  inherited ritual practice as a central part of the case — though, per
  §9 below, not the whole of it.** The movement's own central voice
  argues this directly and repeatedly. Archpriest Avvakum, in his own
  dialogue with the Eastern patriarchs recorded in his own autobiography,
  invokes "the tradition of our holy fathers" and names four authorities
  for the two-fingered sign of the cross. **Corrected, Round 2 (review
  Findings 4, 5, 6) — the earlier draft misquoted this passage and then
  misread its own correctly-quoted Russian witness.** Quoted exactly as
  each vendored file prints it:

  - English (p. 121): "Meletina of Antioch, Theodoret, the Heart Bishop of
    Cyrene, Peter of Damascus and Maxim the Greek."
  - Russian (same locus): "Мелетия антиохийскаго и Феодора Блаженнаго,
    епископа киринейскаго, Петра Дамаскина и Максима Грека" — "Meletios
    of Antioch and Theodore the Blessed, bishop of Cyrene, Peter of
    Damascus and Maxim the Greek."

  Read against each other, these two witnesses name the same **four**
  authorities, not five, and the earlier draft's own reading was wrong on
  every point it asserted: **"Cyrene" is the faithful reading, not a
  corruption** — Russian *киринейскаго* plainly means "of Cyrene," so the
  translators rendered it correctly. **The actual OCR corruption is
  "Heart,"** almost certainly a misread of "Blest"/"Blessed" (Russian
  *Блаженнаго*), which this document did not notice on its first pass.
  **The Russian witness names "Феодора" (Theodore), not "Феодорит"
  (Theodoret)**, as the one figure carrying both the epithet and the see
  of Cyrene; the English "Theodoret, the Heart Bishop of Cyrene" collapses
  to that same single person. **There is no earlier, separate "Theodoret"
  in this list for the phrase to duplicate** — the earlier draft's claim
  that the passage "has already named [Theodoret] separately" is simply
  false; there is one Theodore/Theodoret in the whole list, not two.
  Whether the English translators' "Theodoret" is their own rendering
  choice for "Феодора," a transliteration slip, or reflects a different
  manuscript reading is not settled here; the identity of the "Феодорит"/
  "Феодор" Old Believers cited for the two-fingered sign (the
  pseudepigraphic *Slovo Feodoritovo*) is itself a live question in the
  secondary literature, which this document names as a `contested_claim`
  candidate for Doc_02/Doc_03 rather than resolves. **[Documented]**, that
  this list appears and is invoked for this purpose, verified directly
  against both vendored files; **[Contested]**, as to the identity behind
  "Theodoret"/"Theodore," not [Inferential-Thin] as an earlier draft had
  it — the uncertainty here is a real, named scholarly dispute, not
  merely thin evidence.
- **A recurring pattern of authority, suffering, and testimony.** Exile,
  underground imprisonment, and execution for refusing the corrected rite
  recur across the movement's own record — Avvakum's own decade in
  Siberian exile (1653-1664, per the census's own account of his return
  to Moscow) and his final fourteen years imprisoned in an earthen pit at
  Pustozersk before his burning in 1682; the eight-year siege and fall of
  the Solovetsky Monastery (1668-1676); the mass self-immolations of the
  1670s-1680s. These are not isolated incidents but a recurring pattern
  the movement's own literature (a martyrology genre, by its own later
  practitioners' own naming — Semyon Denisov's own histories of the
  Solovki fathers and sufferers, and separately the Vyg community's
  *Pomorskie otvety* of 1723, whose primary authorship is Andrei Denisov

  implicitly agreeing with a misattribution to Semyon Denisov elsewhere in
  this world's own package; see Doc_02 §2 and the Registry) treats as
  constitutive of what the movement understands itself to be.
- **What distinguishes this world from its nearest neighbor.** From the
  official Synodal Russian church (Atlas entry unbuilt as of this
  writing), this world is the ritual position the 1666 council's own
  anathema was directed against, not the anathematizing body.
  **Corrected, Round 2 (review Finding 18):** an earlier draft read the
  Decision-Log's 2026-08-03 Era 8 gate entry — which lists among that
  gate's "12 drafts (VII.18–VII.29)" an entry for "the Synodal Russian
  church (Old Believers re-scoped as dissent)" — as evidence that VII.7
  itself was drafted at that pass. VII.7 is outside that numbered range.
  What the Decision-Log actually shows is that VII.7 (this world)
  **pre-existed** that pass as a banked entry: the 2026-08-02 Era 7 Frozen
  entry records "NOT written: VI.24→VII.4, VI.23→VII.7 (era 8's)," and
  the same entry's own forward-reference note names "Old Believers at era

## 2. Temporal Scope

### 2.1 Beginning point: 1666, the Moscow council's anathema

The census's own `start` field gives 1666. Nikon's own liturgical
corrections began circulating from the mid-1650s (the printed service
books of 1653-1656), and resistance to them predates the council — but
1666-1667 is the point at which refusal to accept the corrections became
a formally anathematized position rather than one live option among
others inside the church, which is the boundary this world's own identity
turns on (§1 above: a single, named rupture). **[Widely Accepted]**, on
the census's own record (corrected from [Documented], Finding 23 — see
§1); this document's own vendored source does not carry an independent,
dated account of the council itself (Avvakum's autobiography narrates his
own life and exile, not the council's own proceedings as an institutional
event) — a genuine limit of this world's current library, named here and
carried to Doc_02 and `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md`.

### 2.2 Ending point: 1815 — the Era 8 portfolio boundary, not an argued
movement-specific closing point

**This is stated plainly rather than left to imply a false historical
precision.** Era 8 is fleet-scoped as 1650-1815, per a forward-reference
note at the close of the immediately preceding Era 7 Frozen entry
(`Ministry/Features/Atlas-World-Map/Decision-Log.md`, 2026-08-02):
"Next: A1.E8 (1650–1815) with its banked forward flags... Old Believers
at era 8..." — this world is named directly, confirming it was already
in view when this boundary was set (the source's own en-dash preserved
exactly as printed in this quotation; this document's own surrounding
prose uses a plain hyphen for readability). The Era 8 gate's own Frozen
entry (2026-08-03, "ERA 8 FROZEN by Mark") separately records that this
gate pass "replaced" a "round-1800 artifact cluster" across several Era 8
entries "with honest 1815 caps" — i.e., 1815 is the fleet's own
portfolio-level closing year for the whole era, applied across multiple
movements at the same gate pass, not a date independently argued from
this movement's own history. Correcting the census's own boundary
reasoning, if it ever needs correcting, is a portfolio-level matter
outside this thread's authority; this document states its actual basis
rather than a stronger claim than the record supports.

Within that boundary, the nearest genuinely Old-Believer-relevant event is
**Edinoverie's establishment in 1800** — the first formal reconciliation
mechanism between the Synodal church and old-rite worship, permitting
communities to keep the pre-Nikonian rite under Synodal episcopal
oversight. The census's own `statusDescription` names this as giving the
mechanical 1815 cap a substantive nearby anchor. This document treats
Edinoverie (1800) as the real, substantive event nearest the window's
close, and 1815 itself as the administrative boundary — a distinction
Doc_02 and later documents should preserve rather than collapse.

**Explicitly out of scope, as a later development:** the priestly Old
Believers' restoration of their own episcopate, the Belokrinitsa
hierarchy, in 1846 — thirty-one years after this window's own close.

### 2.3 What remains internal vs. what indicates transition

**Internal to this world, across the whole 1666-1815 span:** the refusal
of the corrected rite as the movement's own constitutive act; martyrdom
and suffering under state persecution as a recurring, expected pattern;
and the movement's own literature of self-witness (autobiography,
petition, martyrology) as the primary way it records itself, from
Avvakum's own Life (c. 1673) through Denisov's histories of Solovki and
the Vyg community's Pomorian Answers (1723).

**Indicates transition, within the window:**

- **1668-1676 — the Solovetsky Monastery siege and fall.** The monastery's
  own fifth petition (1667) is, per the census's own sourcing note, "the
  fullest early statement of the Old Believer case," and its fall by
  betrayal in January 1676, with the tsar's own death a week later, was
  read by the movement itself as judgment. Neither the petition nor a
  siege account is vendored (Doc_02 §3, acquisition leads): **[Widely
  Accepted]**, on the strength of secondary accounts named in the census
  (Crummey; Michels — **corrected, Round 2 review Finding 23**: neither
  work was independently re-checked this session, so the actual basis is
  two titles named in the census, not independently corroborated
  scholarship; the tier itself still holds as the right one for
  well-attested history of this kind).
- **1670s-1680s — the mass self-immolations, and the movement's own
  internal disagreement about them.** Not a single settled practice but a
  contested one within the movement's own record. Avvakum's own
  Pustozersk epistles are cited (census; not yet independently verified
  against a vendored file) as approving the practice; Evfrosin's
  *Otrazitel'noe pisanie o novoizobretennom puti samoubiistvennykh
  smertei* (1691) is a contemporary Old Believer tract arguing against
  it. Self-immolation is treated here as a real, disputed practice within
  the movement's own record, not a univocal position the whole movement
  held — the single most important framing point for Doc_09's later
  story work. Death-toll figures (e.g. the ~2,700 figure at Paleostrovsky
  Monastery in 1687) are **[Contested]**: state reports, Old Believer
  martyrologies, and hostile accounts "agree on the fact and disagree on
  the numbers," per the census's own sourcing note.
- **1723 — the Pomorian Answers.** The Vyg community's formal, written
  self-defense (bespopovtsy), primarily authored by Andrei Denisov
  (**corrected, Finding 11**; see §1), marks the point at which the
  priestless position moves from practical improvisation to an
  articulated, systematic theological argument. Not yet vendored (Doc_02
  §3).
- **1800 — Edinoverie**, already discussed at §2.2, is also a genuine
  internal transition: the point at which formal reconciliation with the
  Synodal church, on terms preserving the old rite, becomes possible for
  the first time within this window.

## 3. Geographic Scope

**Core:** Central and northern Muscovite Russia at the movement's own
founding — Moscow (the 1666-1667 council; Avvakum's own early ministry
and disputations), and the Russian north — the White Sea coast and the
Solovetsky Monastery, the Vyg community on Lake Onega's own watershed. The
census's own `region` field states "Russian north, Siberia." **[Widely
Accepted]** on the census's own record (corrected from [Documented],
Finding 23).

**Siberia**, specifically the Dauria region beyond Lake Baikal and the
Tobolsk-Yeniseysk corridor, enters this world's own geography directly
through Avvakum's own exile there (1653-1664, per the census's own
account of his return to Moscow — **corrected, cosmetic Finding 8**, a
prior draft gave 1653-1663), narrated at length in his own autobiography
— this is not a secondary or inferred detail but the setting of a large
fraction of the Life's own narrative. **[Documented]**, verified directly
against the vendored file (the exile narrative runs continuously across a
large portion of the autobiography's own middle section, including the
Markovna passage at p. 80, discussed at §9 below).

**Expansion within window:** the movement's own later dispersal into
Siberia (as ongoing refuge, not only Avvakum's own imposed exile), the
Baltic communities (Vetka, later dispersed), and — per the census's own
`legacy` field, describing the movement's continuing life well past this
window's own close — eventual communities as far as Alaska, Oregon, and
South America. The far dispersal (post-1815) is outside this world's own
window (§2.2); the Siberian/northern core is the geography this world's
own construction should center on.

## 4. Cultural Scope

**Language:** Russian and Church Slavonic — the liturgical and literary

**Corrected in full, Round 2 (review Findings 3, 14, 28) — this section
was substantially wrong on its first draft and is re-grounded here on

Three separate errors, now fixed together because they compound:

1. **Misattribution (Finding 28).** The phrase "the plain, angry, comic
   Russian of speech rather than of books" was attributed to "Avvakum's
   own translators' account." It is not the translators' prose — it is
   `cic-website/data/world-census.json`'s own `documentedStories[0].text`
   field, this project's own editorial characterization, and it does not
   occur anywhere in the vendored 1924 edition, preface included. It is
   cited here only as the census's own claim, at the confidence that
   supports: **[Widely Accepted]**.
2. **A false "different passage" premise (Finding 14).** An earlier draft
   quoted Avvakum's Russian-language opening declaration on plain speech
   and "philosophical verses," and stated that the English translation's
   opening — "Avvakum, archpriest, was bidden by the monk Epiphanius…"
   (p. 33; corrected from p. 32, Finding 7) — was "a different passage,"
   leaving open whether the English translators had "abridged, relocated,
   or handled this passage differently." **The evidence in hand settles
   this, and it does not need to be left open.** Both openings are
   positionally aligned: both begin with the Epiphanius attribution, both
   close that same paragraph with "Amen," and both are immediately
   followed by an identical next paragraph (the Holy Trinity invocation).
   The English opening **is** the same passage as the Russian's — with
   Avvakum's plain-speech apologia and his Pauline citation dropped out of
   the middle of it, at a specific, identifiable point. A search of the
   whole English file for any equivalent of that apologia returns nothing
   (it is not relocated elsewhere in the translation either). **The
   honest, available finding is an edition finding, not an open
   question:** the 1924 Harrison & Mirrlees translation omits Avvakum's
   own plain-speech apologia from an otherwise positionally aligned
   opening. **[Widely Accepted]**, on this document's own direct
   comparison of both vendored files.
3. **Evidentiary misuse (Findings 2, 3).** An earlier draft used the
   Russian-only passage as free-standing evidence for a substantive claim
   ("a genuine, quotable statement of the movement's own self-conscious
   literary register"), licensed by a citation to a ruling that does not
   exist (see this document's own front matter, above). Under INTAKE.md's
   actual, real rule
   (2026-09-02), an original-language file is a second witness only,
   never itself citable as evidence — usable to cross-check an English
   rendering, not to carry a claim alone. Because Finding 14 shows there
   is, in fact, an English rendering to cross-check against (the omitted
   apologia is the "same passage," not a different one, even though the
   specific sentence about plain speech is only present in Russian), the
   correct, rule-conforming statement is the edition finding in point 2
   above — an observation about what the English translation omits,
   corroborated by the Russian witness — not a free-standing claim
   sourced to the Russian text alone. **This document withdraws the
   earlier claim rather than reword around the rule**, per the Round 1
   review's own explicit instruction.

The Russian text itself, quoted for the edition-comparison finding above
(exact, verified programmatically against the vendored file):

> "не позазрите просторечию нашему, понеже люблю свой русской природной
> язык, виршами философскими не обык речи красить, понеже не словес
> красных бог слушает, но дел наших хощет."

In substance — this document's own working gloss, offered only to support
the edition-comparison finding above, not as a free-standing translation
or quotable rendering: Avvakum asks the reader not to scorn his plain
speech, since he loves his own native Russian tongue and is not in the
habit of decorating his speech with philosophical verses (*вирши
философские*), because God does not listen to fine words but wants
deeds. **A further, disclosed hazard for any future quotation of this
file: the vendored Russian text carries roughly ninety bracketed
modern-Russian editorial glosses interpolated directly into the running
sentences** (e.g. `[правое]`, `[потому что]`, `[так]`) — a modernized
reading edition's own apparatus, not Avvakum's words, and any future
quotation must strip them; see Doc_02 §7 for the full disclosure

**Material and visual culture**, per the census's own `legacy` field: old

## 6. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

**Not decided at this stage — carried forward as an explicit open
question, per Step 0 §4.** The movement divided, within this window,
between those who retained priests ordained before 1666 or received from
elsewhere (popovtsy) and those who concluded, over time, that a valid
priesthood could no longer be had at all (bezpopovtsy) — a real,
consequential internal division, though one that (per §9 below) does not
touch the Creed's own content. The Vyg community and the Pomorian Answers
(1723) are a bezpopovtsy production; the Belokrinitsa hierarchy (1846,
out of scope, §2.2) is the eventual popovtsy resolution.

This document does not force a strand determination now, for two
reasons. First, full Article-21 strand analysis (the shape the PAHC/IJC
precedent worlds used) is substantial work properly done once the
library supports it — this world's current vendored library is Avvakum's
own voice only, and Avvakum himself died in 1682, decades before the
popovtsy/bezpopovtsy division hardened into two distinct, self-conscious
positions. Reasoning about the strand question from Avvakum's own text
alone risks reading a later division backward into a source that
predates it. Second, the Representative-identity decision (the M1
checkpoint, after Doc_09 in this project's own document sequence) is the
point at which this question's practical weight actually bears — and
deciding a strand determination now, before that library is built out,
risks constraining that later, more consequential decision on a thinner
evidentiary base than it deserves.

**Disclosed, Round 2 (review Finding 20):** an earlier draft grounded
this reasoning in "V1.8 §2," a specification not present in this
checkout (`reference/method/CiC_Record_Native_World_Build_Process_V1.8.md`
does not exist here; only V1.5/1.6 does). The document sequence and the
Doc_09-then-M1 ordering stated above are independently confirmed against
`reference/method/CiC_Record_Native_World_Build_Process_V1.5.md`, which
**is** in this checkout and states the same sequence and stop. The
substantive reasoning above does not depend on V1.8's own text and is
re-grounded on V1.5 here rather than on an unreadable citation. Whether a
newer process document should govern this build at all is a
governance/methodology question for Mark, not decided by this document —
see the front-matter note above and `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` entry 7.

**Carried to `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (entry 1)** as a live boundary

**What the vendored source actually shows, read in full rather than from

At p. 34, arguing the wording of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed's

This is not an argument about ritual gesture or calendar practice. It is

**What is actually true, and what this document now says instead of the
