## Quick Meaning

Scripture alone, not church tradition, papal decree, or council ruling, is the final authority for both doctrine and civic order — the shared conviction underlying Zurich's own public disputations and Calvin's own systematic theology alike, even though each city enacted it differently.

---

## World Meaning

Whatever Scripture will not yield, the church has no standing to require; whatever it does yield, no pope, council, or inherited custom may set aside. This is not one doctrine among others in this world's own teaching — it is the method by which every other doctrine here is tested and defended. Our own founder at Zurich confessed it plainly at the very outset of his own public case: everything he preached rested on Scripture, and he offered to be corrected, but only from that same Scripture, if he had misunderstood it.

The two cities enacted this one conviction through two different instruments, and the difference is itself worth naming rather than smoothing over. At Zurich, it took the shape of public disputation — argument tested openly, before the city's own council, until one side could no longer answer from the text. At Geneva, it took the shape of sustained, systematic instruction — doctrine built book upon book, catechism taught to the whole population, until the shape of what Scripture actually teaches stood built up rather than merely defended in a single public contest. Neither city borrowed the other's own method. Both arrived, independently, at the same governing principle, which is why the same conviction, differently enacted, is what this document names as this world's own single most defining shared ground.

---

## Ecological Function

This conviction is the method that derives and defends both of this world's other central doctrines: the sovereignty of God in predestination, argued from Scripture in both Zwingli's and Calvin's own hands, and the rejection of any corporeal presence in the Supper, argued against a literal reading of "this is my body." It also licenses the specific civic form each city's own reform took — Disputation at Zurich, Catechism and consistorial oversight at Geneva — as two enactments of one conviction rather than two unrelated practices.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A slogan about individual Bible-reading, implying each believer simply decides doctrine for themselves from personal study, without an authoritative church structure at all.

**World Hearing:**
Not private interpretation but a public, testable standard: a claim only counts as scripturally grounded if it can be defended, publicly and specifically, against the actual text — at Zurich, literally in open disputation before the city; at Geneva, through sustained catechetical instruction answerable to a whole trained pastoral body. The individual conscience is bound by Scripture, but the *testing* of what Scripture teaches was never a private act in either city.
