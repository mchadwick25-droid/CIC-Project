## Verdict: **Substantial revision required**

The document's methodology is sound and unusually well-grounded: the M4/L4-template disclosure is accurate and independently verified against the live Framework text and against Donatism's own Doc_07 (the precedent ruling it invokes is real, not fabricated); all nine required lenses are genuinely applied, not stubbed; the forces section is a real synthesis of already-established Doc_01/Doc_04/Doc_05 material rather than a fresh forces claim; none of this world's three previously-corrected defects (the "beatifies" quote, the Second Helvetic line-684 citation for the *first* quotation, the Institutes IV.3.8 locus) has regressed; and the world's own hard-won disciplines (the two distinct asymmetries kept separate, G4's strand-scope qualification, G1's corrected Cell 1A/2B placement, the CT tag left unadjudicated) are all carried forward correctly. This is real, checked good news, and most of the document does not need to be touched.

But the review found one **High**-severity defect that is exactly the failure mode this world's build has now been caught on repeatedly (Doc_04, Doc_05, Doc_06 all had real citation/quotation defects at review): a direct quotation from the Second Helvetic Confession that both inserts a word not present in the source and cites the wrong line range for it. Given this project's own "every quote must be re-verified verbatim" standard and this world's specific, three-times-demonstrated pattern, a fabricated word inside a quoted primary-source sentence is not a cosmetic nitpick regardless of how small the fix is. Two Medium findings (a misattributed cross-reference and an undisclosed inferential claim) repeat smaller instances of this same general discipline gap. All three are narrow, bounded, and fixable without touching the document's actual analytical content.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — HIGH — The Second Helvetic Confession "mirror" quotation inserts a word not in the source and cites the wrong line range

**Checked:** §2B (Experiential/Emotional), which quotes: *"Let... [our] Christ be the mirror in which we behold our predestination"* (Bullinger, 1566, lines 683–684, "onr" corrected to "our," an OCR artifact, disclosed rather than silently cleaned). The construction note at the end of §2B repeats the same citation ("lines 667, 683–684"). This is exactly the passage the review brief flagged for special scrutiny, and it is one of this world's own three specifically-flagged prior defects (the same quotation, previously mis-pinpointed at line 677, corrected to 684 at Doc_04 Round 1 review).

**Found — two independent defects, verified directly against `cic/texts/schaff_second-helvetic-confession-heidelberg-catechism_1919.txt`:**

1. **A word is inserted into the quotation that does not exist in the source, at the wrong place.** The vendored text (confirmed with `awk 'NR==684'`) reads: `Let, therefore, Christ be the mirror in which we behold onr predes-` (line 684), continuing `tination.` (line 685). There is no "our"/"onr" anywhere near the word "Christ" — the only OCR-garbled word in this sentence is "onr," which appears immediately before "predestination." Doc_07's rendering, `"Let... [our] Christ be the mirror in which we behold our predestination"`, places a bracketed "[our]" directly before "Christ" — a word that is not in the source at that position, in either its correct or its OCR-garbled form. The result is a quotation that appears to attest "our Christ," a phrase the Second Helvetic Confession does not contain here, while the actual, correctly-placed OCR correction ("our predestination") is also present later in the same sentence — producing a doubled "our" where the source has exactly one, correctly located after "behold," not after "Let."
2. **The line citation is wrong even for the sentence's own correct portion.** The quoted material spans lines 684–685 (line 684 ends mid-word at "predes-"; line 685 completes it: "tination."). Doc_07 cites "lines 683–684." Line 683 is blank in the source (confirmed by direct line check); the actual continuation line, 685, is omitted from the citation entirely.

**Why it matters:** this is precisely the category CLAUDE.md's own Source Fidelity section names as this project's single most serious source-fidelity risk ("misattributed and mis-transcribed quotes have been a real, recurring defect here") and the specific pattern this world's build has now been caught on at Doc_04 (a substituted word, "beatifies"→"[justification]"), Doc_05 (a regressed forces-cell citation), and Doc_06 (a false companion-artifact claim and a drifted index). A reader relying on this document's own "independently re-verified this pass" assertion would come away believing the Second Helvetic Confession contains the phrase "our Christ" at this point, which it does not.

**Fix required:** correct the quotation to either (a) render it accurately as `"Let, therefore, Christ be the mirror in which we behold onr [our] predestination"` (bracket placed correctly, immediately before "predestination," matching where the actual OCR artifact sits) or (b) keep the current ellipsis-shortened form but move the bracketed correction to its correct location: `"Let... Christ be the mirror in which we behold [our] predestination."` Either way, remove the fabricated "[our] Christ" and correct the citation to **lines 684–685** (not 683–684), in both the main text of §2B and its own construction note.

### Finding 2 — MEDIUM — A quoted phrase is attributed to two documents when only one of them actually contains it

**Checked:** §5, "Third" cross-lens finding: *"Doc_01 §1 and Doc_05 §6.3 already name the republican city-council structure... as 'the single sharpest structural contrast with the sibling Lutheran Wittenberg world'"* — a direct quotation attributed jointly to both citations.

**Found:** the exact phrase appears verbatim only in Doc_05 §6.3 (*"This world's reform proceeded through republican city-councils, not princely territorial churches — the single sharpest structural contrast with the sibling Lutheran Wittenberg world (Doc_01 §1)"*). Doc_01 §1's own Distinctive Contribution paragraph discusses the same underlying comparison (Wittenberg's princely territorial church versus this world's republican city-councils and consistorial courts) but never uses this phrase, or any comparably worded superlative, anywhere in the document (confirmed by direct search — no occurrence of "sharpest" in Doc_01 §1; Doc_01's own three uses of "sharpest" are about Servetus, Dort's controversy, and the Anabaptist refusal, none of them this comparison). Doc_05 §6.3 itself cites Doc_01 §1 only as the source of the underlying *content*, not as a second location containing this exact wording — Doc_07's own citation collapses that distinction and presents the quoted phrase as though both documents state it.

**Why it matters:** this is a smaller instance of the same misattributed-cross-reference pattern this world's build has now been caught on repeatedly (Doc_03 Round 1: a non-existent Doc_01 §5 quotation and a non-existent Doc_02 §8 finding; Doc_06 Round 1: two inapposite Donatism precedent citations). It does not change the underlying finding — the civic-political contrast with Wittenberg is real and well-supported by Doc_01 §1's own content — but the quotation marks assert a specific verbatim match that does not hold for one of the two named sources.

**Fix required:** cite the quoted phrase to Doc_05 §6.3 alone, and cite Doc_01 §1 separately (without quotation marks) as the source of the underlying comparative content the phrase characterizes — e.g., "Doc_05 §6.3 (drawing on the comparison Doc_01 §1 itself already makes) names... as 'the single sharpest structural contrast...'"

### Finding 3 — MEDIUM — Beza's motive for publishing the *Tabula praedestinationis* is stated as though documented, but no cited source supports the claimed motive

**Checked:** §3, "Memory Structures": *"Beza's own Tabula praedestinationis (1555) performs the same memory-fixing function for G1's own systematized form, published nine years before Calvin's death specifically so the doctrine would not depend on Calvin's own continued presence to be taught correctly (Doc_01 §7)."*

**Found:** Doc_01 §7 (independently checked) states only the bare facts — the *Tabula*'s 1555 publication date, that this is nine years before Calvin's 1564 death, and its role as "a real theological throughline toward the controversy the Remonstrance of 1610 answers." Doc_01 §7 makes no claim anywhere about *why* Beza published it, and no other upstream document (Doc_02, Doc_04, Doc_05) makes such a claim either. The specific motive Doc_07 states — that the timing was deliberate, so the doctrine's correct transmission would not depend on Calvin's continued presence — is this document's own inference, drawn by analogy with Doc_05 §6.4's own, differently-sourced claim about the Second Helvetic Confession ("a founding generation's oral and disputational method needed a fixed text to outlive it"). That analogy may well be reasonable, but Doc_07's own construction note for this section states only that "the Second Helvetic Confession and Beza's *Tabula* dating are Documented (Doc_01 §7; Doc_05 §6.4)" — vouching for the *dates*, not for the motive claim riding alongside them. This is the one place in the Additional Lenses section where the document does not apply the discipline it explicitly applies elsewhere (e.g., §2B's construction note: "The mirror image's own rhetorical function as evidence of intended emotional register is this document's own reading, not an inherited finding").

**Why it matters:** presenting an inferred historical motive in the same sentence and confidence register as an independently documented date risks a reader treating the motive as equally well-grounded, when it is not attested anywhere in this world's build.

**Fix required:** either cite an actual source for Beza's stated motive, or — more straightforwardly — flag the motive claim explicitly as this document's own inference by analogy, the same way §2B's construction note flags a comparable interpretive move (e.g., "this document's own inferred parallel to the Second Helvetic Confession's stated purpose, not an independently attested motive").

### Finding 5 — LOW / COSMETIC — The Consensus Tigurinus quotation silently drops its own leading word, an inherited (not new) instance of this world's own repeated silent-elision pattern

**Checked:** the Consensus Tigurinus 9th Head of Agreement quotation, used identically in §2A and its construction note: *"though we distinguish, as we ought, between the signs and the things signified, yet we do not disjoin the reality from the signs"* (lines 768–770).

**Found:** the vendored source (confirmed directly) reads *"Wherefore, though we distinguish, as we ought, between the signs and the things signified, yet we do not disjoin the reality from the signs..."* (line 768 begins "Wherefore, though we distinguish"). The quotation as used drops the leading word "Wherefore," with no ellipsis marking the elision. This is the identical defect category Doc_06 Round 1 Finding 8a and Doc_06 Round 2 Finding 6 both caught elsewhere in this world's build (a leading word silently dropped from a direct quotation). However, this exact wording is not new to Doc_07 — it is carried forward unchanged from Doc_04 §3.2 and Doc_05 §3, where it was previously reviewed and never flagged. This is not a new defect Doc_07 introduces, and per `cic-build-cycle` this document does not reopen already-approved Doc_04/Doc_05 content on its own authority.

**Fix required:** none required of Doc_07 itself, since it inherits rather than introduces this wording. Flagged here only because the review brief specifically asked this line range be checked, and because the pattern is worth naming to a coach thread as a small, purely technical correction that could be swept across Doc_04/Doc_05/Doc_07 together (adding "Wherefore, " or an opening ellipsis) without touching any substantive claim.
