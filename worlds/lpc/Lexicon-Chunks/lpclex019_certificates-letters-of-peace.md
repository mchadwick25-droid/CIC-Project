## Quick Meaning

For us, a certificate is the written request a martyr or confessor sends to the bishop asking that a named lapsed person be received back to communion — the actual instrument by which the confessors' claim to grant peace was made, and the thing Cyprian spends his crisis correspondence trying to regulate rather than abolish.

---

## World Meaning

The claim is not made in the abstract. It is made on paper, and the paper arrives.

A confessor who stood before the magistrate writes to the bishop and asks that this man, or this woman, be received. Cyprian does not say such letters should not exist — he says they must be written a particular way. *Designate by name in the certificate those whom you yourselves see, whom you have known, whose penitence you see to be very near to full satisfaction, and so direct to us letters in conformity with faith and discipline.* Name the person. Know them. Judge their penitence yourself before you ask.

What actually arrives is often not that. *Certificates are so given to some as that it is said, "Let such a one be received to communion along with his friends"* — a name and then an open door behind it, so that whoever can claim to be a friend, a neighbour, a freedman or a servant of the man named walks through on his paper. And the scale is his own word for it: *thousands of certificates were daily given, contrary to the law of the Gospel* — not a few hard cases but a parallel readmission system running faster than the bishop's own.

So the argument is not whether the confessors' suffering counts. It is whether a certificate is a judgment about a particular person, made by someone who knows them — or a token that can be spent. Cyprian's answer is that it must be the first and has become the second, and that a *vague and blind petition* will in the end *heap reproach upon us*. The confessors' answer is that they paid for the standing to write these letters, and they did.

---

## Ecological Function

This is the **mechanism** behind the confessor-authority tension, and without it that tension is a claim with no instrument. A participant who understands this term understands how the dispute actually operated day to day: the lapsed are the subject, reconciliation is the road, the confessor is the claimant, and the certificate is the thing that moves between them. It is also why penitential discipline is argued for so insistently rather than simply announced — the bishop is not legislating into a vacuum but competing with a working system that already has thousands of documents in circulation.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant who has just heard about the *libelli* will assume this is the same document, or a version of it — a certificate to do with the persecution. They will also tend to hear "letter of peace" as a devotional or ceremonial gesture.

**World Hearing:**
The opposite document, travelling the opposite way. The sacrifice-certificate proves you complied with the empire and made you one of the lapsed; this certificate asks the church to take you back. One is issued by the state's process, the other by a Christian who survived that process. And it is administratively real — issued in thousands, argued over by name, and regulated in writing.

## Key Sources

Cyprian's own crisis correspondence, Registry row 1 (Confidence A), where the whole argument is conducted: the regulating instruction (*"designate by name in the certificate…"*), the complaint about open-ended grants (*"Let such a one be received to communion along with his friends"*, with its named loophole of *"neighbours and connections, and freedmen and servants"*), the scale (*"thousands of certificates were daily given, contrary to the law of the Gospel, I wrote letters in which I recalled by my advice, as much as possible, the martyrs and confessors to the Lord's commands"* — Epistle XIV), and the martyrs' side stated in Cyprian's own words (*"the peace which the martyrs have desired, by their letters to us, to be granted to them"*). All quoted here are re-verified at source in `cic/texts/anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml`, scoped to Cyprian's own `div1` span.

**Attestation, stated at the two figures it actually has.** *"Certificate"* and *"certificates"* occur **42 times within the `div1` titled *Cyprian***, of which **4 sit inside `<note>` elements — the 19th-century editorial apparatus — leaving 38 in Cyprian's own text.** The rule, so it can be re-run: case-insensitive `certificates?`, scoped to that `div1` rather than the whole bound volume, which also carries Hippolytus; note spans marked *before* tags are stripped, since stripping first makes the editorial matter indistinguishable from the text. **38 is the figure that attests the term; 42 is the figure a naive sweep returns**, and the gap between them is this corpus's standing hazard.

 The first version of this entry reported a flat **42** and quoted, as re-verified Cyprian, the sentence *"thousands of certificates were given, against the Gospel law."* **That wording is the editorial endnote's, not Cyprian's** — his own text reads *"were **daily** given, **contrary to the law of the Gospel**."* Both variants sit within a few hundred words of each other and the drafting pass took the wrong one. **This entry was added to close a discovery gap and promptly committed the defect this deliverable keeps a register for**, which is the strongest argument available that the register must be built by sweeping quotations against the marked-up source rather than by recall.

**Author Gravity note.** The regulating voice is Cyprian's throughout, as with every entry in this cluster. **The confessors' own certificates do not survive**; what survives is Cyprian quoting, paraphrasing and objecting to them. Their side of the argument reaches this record through the man arguing against it — though Epistles XX–XXI (row 1) do preserve two confessors writing in their own first-person voices about a specific reconciliation, which is as close as this world's record comes.
