## Assessment of the Round 5 structural simplification

Round 5's fix pass made a deliberate structural change alongside its nine substantive fixes: four self-referential passages were shortened and pointed at a single source rather than restating facts in several places. This section assesses that change on its own terms, since it is the first time this build has tried to fix the *shape* of the problem rather than the instances.

**What was shortened, and how each fared:**

| Passage | Change | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Doc_02 §9 item 6 (recall-test / PRESS summary) | Numbers deleted; points at the Registry's saturation statement as *"the single authoritative count"* | **Worked.** The duplication defect that recurred at Rounds 3, 4 and 5 is gone. No CF V7.4 requirement is lost — see below. |
| Doc_02 §10 completeness sentence | Same | **Worked**, same reasoning. |
| Doc_02 §10 "two reviews disagreeing" paragraph | Inline re-narration deleted; points at `lpc_Decision_Log.md` | **Worked in form; the target degraded.** See below. |
| Registry affirmative-licensing paragraph (line 140) | Per-row enumeration replaced by one general statement | **Failed.** M5 — the general statement contradicts the section's own exemption rule and the flag lists mislabel seven rows. |
| Both Status lines | Round-by-round narration replaced by two summary clauses | **Failed.** M1 and L1 — both new clauses are false. |

**Does pointing at another document satisfy what CF V7.4 requires?** This is the question the simplification turns on, and the answer differs by target.

CF V7.4's Part VII Step 2 entry, extracted whole this session, places the saturation obligation on the Registry, not on Doc_02: *"**Close the Registry with a saturation statement** naming the last unproductive searches — which queries, against which instruments, returned nothing new. A Registry without a saturation statement is not complete."* And it places the recall-test/PRESS recording obligation on the review artifact, not on Doc_02: *"The answer is **recorded in the review artifact**, and every named work is dispositioned — rowed, or excluded with a reason."* **Nothing in CF V7.4 requires Doc_02 itself to carry the count.** Doc_02 §9 item 6 pointing at the Registry's saturation statement is therefore sound: it points at a sibling document in the same disposition-eligible set, at the exact artifact the Framework names as the bearer of that content, and the Registry's statement does carry it — five rounds, five instrument sets, 0/10 · 3/10 · 0/10 · 0/10 · 0/10, with the reading of the 3/10 stated and the unrun field-bibliography sweep disclosed rather than absorbed. It also still satisfies the sentence's harder half: the Registry names its instruments per round and states plainly that it is *"**not** closed with a full Construction Framework V7.4 saturation statement."* The only cost is that the single authoritative statement is itself partly stale in its framing (L3).

The §10 disagreement paragraph is the more interesting case, because CO-022's *Revision decision* rule says only *"log that disagreement explicitly rather than quietly siding with whichever review happened most recently"* — it does not say where. `lpc_Decision_Log.md` **is** the log, and pointing at it is closer to the rule's intent than re-narrating instances inside the document under review. I would keep this change. But the simplification concentrated the record in one file without hardening it, and that file is now the least reliable artifact in the set (M3, M4, L11) — including two irreconcilable definitions of the same event written in the same commit. **The risk was moved, not removed; and it was moved into the one document nobody had been reviewing as closely as the three under review.**

**The general conclusion I reach.** The simplification is directionally right and should not be reversed, but it was applied with the wrong distinction. What generated defects across five rounds was not length as such — it was **maintained enumerations**: lists of row numbers, counts of edits, ranges, and running totals, each of which must be updated whenever anything is added beside it. Round 5 shortened four passages; of those, the two that **deleted** an enumeration (§9 item 6, §10) are clean, and the two that **compressed** an enumeration into a summary claim (the status lines, the affirmative-licensing paragraph) are the source of three of this round's nine MEDIUM findings. A shorter claim about many rows is not safer than a longer claim about many rows; it is the same claim with fewer places to check it.

The evidence for that reading is that **five of this round's nine MEDIUM findings are un-extended enumerations** — M5 (flag lists), M6 (§9 item 3's row range), M7 (Manifest §3's list and range), M9 (the network-limitation identifier list), and M2 (a running edit count) — and every one is the same defect the enumeration it belongs to has already had at least once before. The remedy that would actually converge is not shorter prose. It is (a) deleting the enumerations that another document already holds, as §9 item 6 did, and (b) for the ones that must stay, replacing "rows X–Y" and "N of them" with a stated rule plus a pointer to the table, so that adding a row cannot falsify a sentence somewhere else. M5's remedy, M6's and M7's are the same remedy.

**One thing the compression deleted that is worth naming, though it breaks no requirement.** The affirmative-licensing paragraph replaced at line 140 was the only place in the Registry that quoted CF V7.4's own Licensed-For rule — *"the specific gravity, force, lexicon term, or Representative trait it justifies"* — verified present at `ce8246f2` and absent at `8360e103`. CF V7.4 requires the Registry to *name* what each Native source is licensed for; it does not require the rule to be quoted, so nothing mandatory is lost. But the paragraph that was deleted was the one holding the definition, and the confusion that replaced it (M5) is precisely a confusion about what "licensed" means across seven rows. When a compression removes a governing-text anchor, the generalisation that replaces it has nothing to be checked against.

One further observation, offered as evidence rather than a finding. The Round 5 commit message states *"**Sixteen** new Registry rows (71-87)."* Rows 71–87 are seventeen. The Registry's own saturation statement gets it right (7 + 3 + 7 = 17). The wrong figure did not reach a live document this time — it stopped at the commit message — which is the first round in which an unre-derived count has been caught by the build's own arithmetic rather than by the next review. That is a real, if small, improvement, and it argues the same way: the count was right where it was **derived** from the dispositions and wrong where it was **restated**.
