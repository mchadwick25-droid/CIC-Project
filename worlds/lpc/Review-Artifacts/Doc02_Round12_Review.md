**Why this is wrong, checked against the same document's own numbers.** Doc_01's own boundary — stated identically throughout Doc_02, including three words later in this very sentence ("this world's own 246–430 congregational life") — is **c. 246–430 CE**. Monica's death, the specific date this sentence itself cites, is **387 CE**: 141 years *after* the boundary's own start and 43 years *before* its own end. It does not predate 246; it sits squarely inside the stated window. Doc_02's own §8 Confidence Map, in the same document, independently confirms this: it lists "Augustine's conversion 386 and baptism 387" under "Documented / Widely Accepted" as part of "the core dated sequence already established in Doc_01" (line 109) — i.e., this document elsewhere treats 386–387 as squarely inside this world's own documented timeline, not before its boundary. The parenthetical's own logic conflates two different reference points: "before Augustine's own presbyterate" (391) is true of 387, but being before Augustine's presbyterate is not the same claim as predating the *world's* boundary (246), and the sentence needs the second claim, not the first, to reach its own stated conclusion.

That this is a real error, not a defensible loose usage, is confirmed by Doc_02's own established, consistent usage of the identical phrase elsewhere in the same document — both other uses are correct on their own terms: §1 states that *The Passion of the Scillitan Martyrs* (180 CE, Registry row 28) "predates this world's own c. 246 beginning by 66 years" (true: 180 < 246), and §6's own immediately preceding sentence states that "the *Passion of Perpetua* itself... predates this world's own c. 246 start" (true: the *Passion of Perpetua* is dated 203 CE, 43 years before 246). The Monica sentence is the only one of the document's three uses of "predates this world's own boundary/beginning/start" where the date named does not, in fact, predate it — and it sits four sentences after the second of the two correct uses, inside the same paragraph, making the same kind of claim on the same kind of evidence.

> "Gillian Clark, *Monica: An Ordinary Saint*, Women in Antiquity (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2015) | S | C | **Excluded** | **Out-of-Boundary** | N/A — excluded | **Builder's call, per Round 7's own disposition options: excluded rather than rowed.** Monica's own formation and most of her life lie outside this world's own Carthage/Hippo congregational-life focus, and Doc_02 §6 already excludes her presence from the Article 20 discharge on exactly this ground; **excluded here for consistency with that already-stated position** rather than rowed and left in tension with it"
