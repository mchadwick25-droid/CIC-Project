# Possidius, *Vita Augustini* — complete read, all thirty-one chapters

**Read by:** the `lpc` build thread, on the project lead's instruction of 2026-09-16.
**Source:** `cic/texts/possidius_vita-augustini_weiskotten1919.txt` — Weiskotten 1919, Registry **row 192**, Native, vendored.
**Scope:** Preface and chapters I–XXXI, ~17,000 words of English. Supersedes and extends `Possidius_XIX-XXVII_Read_2026-09-15.md`, which covered nine chapters; that artifact's findings are unaffected and are not restated here.

**Method.** The file is bilingual with facing Latin, and hyphenates across line breaks; every search was run against a de-hyphenated, whitespace-collapsed copy, or it returns false negatives. **Weiskotten's own forty-page Introduction, his apparatus criticus and his endnotes were excluded from the read span** and are not Possidius's voice: the body runs from Possidius's Preface to the `NOTES` heading. This matters — Weiskotten's Introduction states that a council *"decided to appeal to the Emperor for protection,"* which read as the world's own voice would produce a false finding about this world's relation to state power. It is the editorial-apparatus trap this build has hit before.

**OCR note.** The scan is imperfect. Quotations are given as the source prints them; where a reading is obviously corrupt the correction is marked. `clamored most elageriy` (ch. VIII) is the scan's rendering of *eagerly*, confirmed against the facing Latin.

## 1. The finding that changes an existing record

**Congregational demand overriding a reluctant candidate occurs at BOTH of Augustine's offices, not only at his presbyterate.**

- **Chapter IV, the presbyterate.** Valerius *"addressed the people of God and exhorted them to provide and ordain a presbyter for the city."* The Catholics *"laid hands on him — for he was standing there among the people secure and unaware of what was about to happen… and they demanded it with great zeal and clamor, while he wept freely."*
- **Chapter VIII, the episcopate.** Valerius made his desire known to the bishops present, to all the clergy of Hippo, *"and to all the people."* *"But while all who heard rejoiced and clamored most [eagerly] that this should be done and accomplished, the presbyter refused to accept the episcopate contrary to the custom of the Church, since his bishop was still living. However, when they had convinced him… under compulsion and constraint he yielded and accepted the ordination to the higher office."* The Latin is `universae plebi… ingenti desiderio clamantibus… recusabat… compulsus atque coactus succubuit`.

**What this refutes.** `Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §2 finds that the pattern *"recurs at the episcopate for Cyprian and at the presbyterate for Augustine, **not at the same office for both**."* Chapter VIII is the same pattern at the same office — announcement to the whole people, popular clamour, the candidate's refusal, compulsion, yielding.

**Where the refuted claim has propagated:** Doc_01 §2 (origin); Doc_03 (two sites, and its *suffrage* caution that the pattern is *"attested through different figures, different offices, and different words, not one term"*); Doc_05 §4.1; Doc_07 §2F; `lpc_World_Profile.md` (three sites). **Six documents, all at *Approved to proceed*.**

**This strengthens the world rather than weakening it.** Congregational consent moves from a loose family resemblance across two phases to one recurring pattern at the same office in both — a cross-phase feature of how authority was constituted here, bearing directly on G1 and on Doc_01 §5's own Ecological-orientation test.

**Not resolved here.** Doc_01 is self-disposed *Approved to proceed*, and the sentence sits inside its strand-singular test — the determination Article 21 says *"once made, governs all subsequent strand attribution."* CO-022 category 4 ("a finding that cuts against an earlier decision") applies. Escalated.

## 2. Findings the build does not currently hold

1. **The fine is gold, corroborated from a second Native source (ch. XII).** Crispinus, Donatist bishop of Calama, *"was admonished that he was liable to the fine of gold fixed by the civil laws against heretics."* This independently corroborates `Source_Registry.md` row 44's identification of *CTh* XVI.5.21 (ten pounds of **gold**, heretical clergy generally) rather than XVI.5.52 (the Donatist schedule assessed in **silver**), which that row reached from Letter 185 §25 alone.
2. **The monastery is a clergy nursery, and congregational demand drives that too (ch. XI).** *"The Church, for the sake of its peace and unity, first began to demand with great eagerness bishops and clergy from the monastery… and later obtained them. And there were **about ten men**, holy and venerable, continent and learned, whom the most blessed Augustine furnished in answer to our request to various churches, some of them quite prominent."* A formation mechanism — monastic formation feeding the episcopate — that no current document carries.
3. **The monastic rule, in Possidius's terms (ch. V).** *"No one should possess anything of his own, but… all things should be held in common and be distributed to each one as he had need."*
4. **Congregational demand also compels disputation (ch. VI).** The Christians of Hippo, *"whether citizens or strangers, Catholics and even Donatists, came to the presbyter Augustine and demanded that he should meet this presbyter of the Manichaeans."* A third instance of the congregation acting as a body with real leverage — over a bishop's agenda, not only over ordination.
5. **A named layman's account of his own formation, relayed (ch. XV).** Firmus, a merchant, converted by a sermon; *"he confessed that he had followed the sect of the Manichaeans… he told us."* Not a first-person written voice, so it does not overturn the lay-voice limit — but it is a layman's own account of what formed him, recorded, and the Profile's Honest Limits should be tested against it.
6. **Women's monasteries with women overseers (ch. XXXI).** Augustine *"left to the Church a fully sufficient body of clergy and **monasteries of men and women with their continent overseers**."* Bears on the *woman's own narrating voice* domain.
7. **An Arian controversy the build does not register (ch. XVII).** Pascentius, an Arian Count, debated publicly at Carthage; also the Arian bishop Maximinus. The build's controversy set is Manichaean, Donatist, Pelagian.
8. **Augustine died intestate by choice (ch. XXXI).** *"He made no will, because as a poor man of God he had nothing from which to make it,"* and he *"repeatedly ordered that the library of the church and all the books should be carefully preserved for future generations."*
9. **Tier 3 markers are present in the Augustine phase (ch. XXIX).** Demons departing at his prayer; a sick man healed after a vision instructed him to seek Augustine's hand. Relevant to Doc_09's tiering, which currently carries one Tier 3 story and locates hagiographic convention in the Cyprian phase.

## 3. Claims tested against the full text and upheld

- **"Confessor" occurs zero times** in Possidius's own text. G8's Cyprian-phase bound is **stronger** than the build had established, not weaker.
- **"Punic" occurs zero times.** The rural and Punic/Berber limit holds.
- **No woman speaks in her own voice anywhere in Possidius's text.** The Manichaean Elect women of ch. XVI are examined and their disclosures entered in ecclesiastical records — mediated and hostile, not a narrating voice. Monnica material sits in Weiskotten's Introduction, not in Possidius.
- **No order of service and no liturgical treatise.** The rite is visible only through the disputes about it.
- **No founding narrative or origin myth.** Possidius narrates a life, not a founding.
- **Nothing dates from inside the 133-year gap.** Possidius writes after 430.
