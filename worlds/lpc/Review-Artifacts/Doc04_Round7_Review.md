### H8 — Supporting's stated basis does not hold: the third clause is a concession attached to a requirement, it does not reach Formation, and the definition's gating clause is denied by §3's own Cross-Check eight lines earlier

**Sites:** Doc_04 line 99 (§3 Classification); line 96 (§3 Cross-Check); line 164 (§4); `lpc_Decision_Log.md` third entry, *"What Supporting now rests on, stated exactly."*

**The definition, extracted character-for-character from `word/document.xml` paragraph 305:**

> *"Supporting Gravities organize significant portions of the ecology but function within the context established by primary gravities. They pass multiple gravity tests but may not demonstrate the same breadth of dependency or persistence."*

**What the document builds on it:**

> *"The Framework's Supporting definition has three clauses, and the third is the one that carries this candidate… **That third clause is decisive here, because it expressly contemplates a gravity whose dependency and persistence are weaker than a Primary's** — which is this candidate's profile, Persistence included."*

**Four problems, in ascending order of weight.**

**(a) The segmentation is chosen, and the choice does the work.** The definition is two sentences. Splitting them into three "clauses" is grammatically available, but it detaches *"they pass multiple gravity tests but may not demonstrate the same breadth of dependency or persistence"* from the sentence it belongs to and promotes it to a free-standing qualifying criterion. It is not one. Its own first half is a **requirement** (*they pass multiple gravity tests*); its second half is a **concession** subordinate to that requirement. A concession cannot qualify a candidate; it can only excuse a candidate that has already qualified.

**(b) The concession is relative to Primary's breadth, not to a failed test.** *"May not demonstrate the same breadth of… persistence"* contemplates a gravity whose persistence is narrower than a Primary's. Doc_04's Persistence bullet does not say Candidate 5's persistence is narrow. It says *"**Does not pass** at the world level."* Not-passing is not lesser-breadth, and the clause does not reach it.

**(c) The clause says nothing about Formation.** It names dependency and persistence only. Formation *"does not clearly pass"* on this document's own direct finding, and nothing in the Supporting definition excuses that. The Classification paragraph handles Formation by excluding Primary and then never returns to it.

**(d) Decisively: the definition's gating first clause is expressly denied by this document, eight lines earlier, in the same subsection.** Line 96:

> *"…their **organizing breadth** is not supported at the same level, this candidate's own six-test profile above being **narrow at best**. The classification is set by that profile and is **not** upgraded to match the vividness of the two quoted formulas."*

And §7 Open Item 1 carries that denial forward to Doc_05 and Doc_08 as a live divergence. *"Organize significant portions of the ecology"* is the clause the whole definition gates on; line 99 quotes it and never argues it; line 96 and Open Item 1 say it is not supported; and line 164 asserts it as met. The document simultaneously certifies and denies the one requirement Supporting actually imposes.

**There is also a comparative test the document had available and did not run.** Its own other two Supporting candidates: Candidate 4 — *"Passes Repetition, Formation, and Persistence cleanly; passes Dependency, Explanatory, and Interaction only narrowly"*; Candidate 7 — *"Repetition/Formation/Explanatory pass within Augustine's phase; Persistence fails at world level; narrow pass on Dependency and Interaction."* Candidate 5 is the only Supporting in this document that passes **neither** Formation nor Persistence. It is strictly weaker than both benchmarks it sits beside in §4, and §4's own table shows it.

**And the matrix profile does not help.** Three reinforcing, four none, zero competing. The three reinforcing relations are with Candidates 1, 3 and 6 — all Primary — and one of them is glossed *"Reinforcing, weakly."* That profile fits the definition's **second** clause (*functions within the context established by primary gravities*) exactly, and it is the one clause the document argues (*"which this candidate plainly does"*). But functioning within a Primary's context is what a Supporting gravity does **in addition to** organizing significant portions of the ecology, not instead of it. Four "no demonstrated relationship" cells out of seven is weak evidence of organizing significant portions of anything.

**What this review does and does not say.** Candidate 5's classification is the project lead's ruling and is not this review's to revisit. The finding is narrower and is about the document: **the profile-fit limb of the stated basis does not hold, and with it gone, Supporting rests on the ruling alone.** The Status line already half-says that — *"rests on the candidate's profile fit… **and on the ruling alone**"* — a sentence that names two grounds and then calls one of them alone (L4). The honest version is the shorter one.

**Fix:** either (i) state that Supporting rests on the ruling, record the profile as the ruling's subject rather than its ground, and carry the divergence between the profile and the definition's first clause to Open Item 1 where the Cross-Check already sends it; or (ii) argue the first clause — say what significant portion of this ecology Candidate 5 organizes — and reconcile that argument with line 96 in the same breath. What cannot stand is quoting three clauses, arguing two, and letting a concession carry the label.

## Has the failure mode migrated again, and into what form?

The remedy history, as the build records it: re-derive quotations at source (R2) → read the word-level diff (R3) → open every named destination (R4) → derive the destination set by search, not copy (R5) → widen the terms and run explicit contradiction assertions (R6) → **state the domain the check runs over, open what it excludes, and check propositions rather than tokens (R7, this pass)**.

**The R7 remedy worked where it was pointed, and it was pointed at half the problem.** §5 is propositionally consistent for the first time in four rounds (CLEAN 7). The two governing records agree (CLEAN 8). The §8 omission Round 6 predicted was closed. What it did not reach is everything the pass was not the author of: §4's table, the Dependency bullet, §5's late summary, Open Item 7, §8's inherited entries, the Decision Log's earlier entries.

**Two migrations, both visible in this pass.**

**1. From the domain of a check to the direction of a check.** Every remedy so far, including this one, asks *is the new thing right?* A withdrawal inverts the question and the instrument did not invert with it. A proposition-level check over a fully stated domain still cannot see a **restoration**, because a restored sentence is not new, produces an insertion hunk that looks like a revert rather than a claim, and carries the authority of having once been reviewed. H6 is that failure exactly: a sentence approved at Round 4 and reinstated at Round 7, falsified in between by the very finding the same pass chose to keep. **The unasked question is: *does what I restored agree with what I kept?*** Restoration is an insertion wearing the clothes of a deletion.

**2. From completeness to truth — and this is the one that will recur.** Rounds 2–7 have all asked *is the check complete?* — over quotations, diffs, destinations, lists, terms, domains. None asks *is the proposition being propagated true?* Consistency is defined relative to the proposition; a consistency check is structurally incapable of testing it. A document can be internally consistent and uniformly wrong, and this document is one edit away from demonstrating it.

**Predicted next form, concretely.** The next fix pass will do exactly what this review asks. It will take the proposition *"Supporting rests on the third clause of the CF V7.4 definition plus the ruling"*, enumerate every site bearing on it — §3, §4's cell, §5's line 188, §7 Open Items 2, 6 and 7, §8, both Decision Log entries — propagate it to all of them, and run a propositional check over a stated full domain that opens what it excludes. **Every finding in this review's HIGH section will close.
