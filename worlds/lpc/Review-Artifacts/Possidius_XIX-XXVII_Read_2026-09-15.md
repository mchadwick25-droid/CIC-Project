## What is actually in it

**XIX — hearing cases.** Opens by quoting 1 Corinthians 6 at length. Augustine *"heard their cases carefully and dutifully"* for *"Christians or… men of any sect"*, keeping in mind one who *"preferred to hear cases between strangers rather than between friends; for of the strangers he could gain the one as a friend… whereas he would lose the one of his friends against whom judgment was passed."* He *"asked no other reward"* than obedience owed to God and man, acting as one whom the Lord made *"a watchman unto the house of Israel."* And the work was a burden: it *"took him away from better things"* and **he regarded it as *"a kind of conscription."***

**XX — intercession.** He generally refused letters of intercession to civil authorities, on a rule that *"the authority which is petitioned afterward becomes oppressive."* When he judged intercession necessary he did it so that he *"seemed admirable"* rather than annoying. The reply of **Macedonius, Vicar of Africa**, is quoted in full: *"You do not insist, like most men in your position, on extorting all that the suppliant asks."*

**XXI — councils.** He attended *"seeking in them not his own but the things of Jesus Christ,"* for two purposes: that the faith remain inviolate, and that clergy *"justly or unjustly excommunicated might be either absolved or rejected."* On ordination: **the agreement of the majority of Christians and the custom of the Church should be followed.**

**XXII — food and clothing.** *"Neither too fine nor yet too mean"*; he *"held a middle course."* Herbs and lentils, with meat for guests or weaker brethren, and always wine. **Silver spoons, earthen, wooden or marble vessels** — *"not from the compulsion of necessity, but from the choice of his own will."* His table carried a verse inscription against slandering the absent, and when **fellow-bishops** ignored it he *"so sternly rebuked them"* that he threatened to leave the meal — *"Both I and the others who were at the table experienced this."*

**XXIII — church revenues.** He spent on the poor from the same funds he and his household lived on. When jealousy arose among the clergy about the possessions, he offered publicly **to give them back** and live on offerings instead — *"But this the laity were never willing to undertake."*

**XXIV — household affairs.** Property was entrusted to *"the more capable clergy."* **He never held the key nor wore his ring**; overseers recorded receipts and expenditure and *"at the end of the year the accounts were read to him."* He never bought property, accepted what was freely given or bequeathed, but **refused legacies that should in justice have gone to the deceased's family.** The Hippo benefactor episode is told at length: a gift offered, then reclaimed by the donor's son; Augustine returned the tablets, **spurned the hundred gold pieces**, and wrote reproving him.

**XXV — household discipline.** No one was to swear an oath, *"not even at the table"*; an offender **lost one drink**, the cups being *"fixed beforehand."* Matthew 5 and 18 governed reconciliation and rebuke, up to *"seventy times seven."*

**XXVI — the companionship of women.** **No woman lived in his house — not his widowed sister, not his nieces**, though councils permitted the exception. His stated reason is about the servants and visitors such a household would draw, and the *"stumbling-block… in the way of the weak."* Women who came to see him *"never came in to him without some of the clergy as witnesses."*

**XXVII — the needy and sick.** He visited *"only the widows and orphans in their afflictions"*, but when the sick asked him to come and lay hands on them **he went without delay.** Women's monasteries he visited *"only in extreme emergencies."*

**Also in the span:** three rules he took from **Ambrose** — never seek a wife for another, never urge anyone to military service, never accept a feast invitation in his own community — each with its reason; Ambrose's deathbed reply, *"I have not so lived that I should be ashamed to live among you: but neither do I fear to die, for we have a Lord who is good"*; and a fellow-bishop's *"If I were never to die it would be well; but if I am ever to die, why not now?"*

## The find that bears on this world's central question

**Possidius, closing the Augustine phase, quotes Cyprian directly** — *"the holy martyr Cyprian… in his letter which he wrote on Mortality."* Verified at both ends:

- **ANF, Treatise VII** (*On the Mortality*), resolved by the XML's `div3 title=` rather than by position: *"a youth, venerable in honour and majesty, lofty in stature and shining in aspect… You fear to suffer, you do not wish to depart; what shall I do to you?"*
- **Weiskotten's rendering of Possidius's quotation:** *"a youth, venerable in glory and majesty, tall of stature and with radiant countenance… 'You fear to suffer, you do not wish to die; what shall I do with you?'"*

Same passage, two translators, both in this world's own vendored corpus.

**This is a second, independent in-world link across the 133-year gap.** Doc_01 argued this world's coherence from Augustine arguing *against* Cyprian's rebaptism ruling while refusing to let the disagreement touch communion. Here is Augustine's own biographer, at the close of the world's span, reaching for Cyprian to make a point about a bishop's death — not polemically, but as a shared possession. **It bears on the open Article 3 question and is offered to the project lead as evidence, not as a ruling.**
