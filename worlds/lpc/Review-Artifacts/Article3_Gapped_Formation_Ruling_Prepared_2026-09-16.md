# Article 3 and gapped formation-types — prepared for the project lead's ruling

**Prepared by:** the `lpc` build thread, on the project lead's instruction of 2026-09-16.
**Status: NOT A RULING.** Article 3 is constitutional and the question is portfolio-level: it governs every world that spans a temporal gap, not `lpc` alone. CO-022 reserves both categories to the project lead. **Section 5 below is a draft ruling text for adoption, redline or rejection — it is not in force and this document does not put it in force.**

---

## 1. The question, as the portfolio logged it

`CiC_Step0_Conclusion_FINAL_v2.docx`, under *"Constitutional ambiguity flagged, not resolved"*, quoted via `lpc_Gapped_Formation_Precedent.md` §1:

> Article 3 (Formation-World Principle) requires "sufficient historical coherence" but **does not define whether a formation-type may recur across a temporal gap with a rival claimant contesting the interval** (Cyprian/Augustine, with Donatism in between), **or whether it requires continuous, unbroken community existence.** Checked directly against the Constitution's actual text and found genuinely undefined.

`lpc` is the first world to stand inside this gap — **not a defect `lpc` introduced**, a pre-existing gap in the Constitution that `lpc`'s build was the first to actually test.

## 2. What Article 3 says, read at source

From `L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx`:

> A formation world within this architecture is: **a historically situated communal interpretive ecology through which Christians perceived** worship, belonging, holiness, suffering, continuity, reconciliation, memory, and faithfulness **together**.

> Formation worlds should possess sufficient: **historical coherence / ecological distinctiveness / evidential support** — to justify treatment as distinct worlds of participation.

> **Boundary governance belongs constitutionally. Boundary evaluation belongs methodologically.**

**That last line is the most important sentence for this ruling, and the build has not used it.** Article 3 itself divides the labour: the Constitution governs *that* worlds have boundaries and what they must possess; **evaluating any particular boundary is assigned to methodology.** Whether a specific gapped candidate holds together is therefore an evaluation question, which Article 3 has already delegated.

**Article 3 requires no amendment to answer this.** What is missing is not constitutional text but a **named methodological test**, which Article 3 expects the build documents to supply.

Note also what Article 3 does *not* say: it nowhere requires continuous or unbroken community existence. It requires *sufficient historical coherence*. The second limb of the logged question — *"or whether it requires continuous, unbroken community existence"* — has no textual basis to rest on. Article 15 is the nearest related text and runs the other way, recording development and instability as normal rather than disqualifying.

## 3. The test already exists in practice; it has never been stated as a rule

`lpc` built one and used it, and `lpc_Gapped_Formation_Precedent.md` §3 generalises it:

> A gapped world's coherence argument should rest on candidates **independently well-attested on *both* sides of the gap**. **If the coherence argument only works by treating the gap as bridged, that is the signal the world may not be one formation-type.**

Operationally, from Doc_04: **phase testing substitutes for cross-strand testing.** Every candidate gravity is tested independently in each phase, and anything visible in only one phase is flagged phase-bound and **excluded from the coherence argument.** `lpc` excludes G2 and G8 on exactly this ground.

**The test is falsifiable and it has teeth**: it can fail a world, and it removes evidence from the argument rather than adding it.

## 4. How `lpc` scores, on evidence that has strengthened twice since the question was framed

| Evidence | Status |
|---|---|
| Doc_01 §5's strand-singular finding | Rests on formation emphasis and ecological orientation **demonstrated independently in both phases** — two self-standing demonstrations, not one thread stretched across |
| Gap-crossing mechanisms | **Two, both textual and both verified at both ends** — Augustine engaging Cyprian's conciliar acts, and Possidius quoting Cyprian's *De Mortalitate* (`Possidius_Full_Read_2026-09-16.md`) |
| Congregational consent | **New, 2026-09-16:** attested at **the same office in both phases** — Cyprian's *"your suffrage and God's judgment"*, and Possidius ch. VIII at Augustine's episcopate. A recurring **practice**, independently attested on each side, not a text read later |
| Phase-bound candidates | G2 and G8 **excluded** from the coherence argument, as the test requires |

**`lpc` passes its own test, and passes it without needing the crossings.** The crossings are corroboration; the coherence argument stands on the two independent demonstrations. That is precisely the shape the precedent says should be required.

## 5. Draft ruling text — for the project lead's adoption, redline or rejection

> **Article 3 and gapped formation-types — ruling, [date].**
>
> Article 3 does not require continuous or unbroken community existence, and nothing in the Constitution imposes that requirement. Article 3 requires **sufficient historical coherence, ecological distinctiveness and evidential support**, and it assigns boundary **evaluation** to methodology while retaining boundary **governance** constitutionally.
>
> **A formation-type may therefore recur across a temporal gap, including where a rival claimant contests the interval, provided its coherence is established by the following test**, which is hereby adopted as the methodological standard Article 3 delegates:
>
> 1. **Both-sides independence.** The coherence argument must rest on candidates **independently well-attested on each side of the gap**. Each side must stand on its own evidence.
> 2. **Phase testing substitutes for cross-strand testing.** Every candidate is tested independently in each phase. Anything attested in only one phase is **flagged phase-bound and excluded** from the coherence argument.
> 3. **The bridging disqualifier.** If the coherence argument works **only** by treating the gap as bridged, the world is **not** one formation-type. Demonstrated crossings may corroborate coherence; they may not constitute it.
> 4. **The interval is disclosed, not filled.** A rival claimant occupying the interval is named, and its material is not drawn on to supply the gapped world's own content.
>
> **Applied to `lpc`:** the test is met. Doc_01 §5's strand-singular finding rests on two independent demonstrations; G2 and G8 are excluded as phase-bound; the two demonstrated crossings and the same-office congregational pattern corroborate without carrying the argument. **`lpc` is one formation world.**
>
> This ruling is portfolio-level and binds every gapped candidate. It amends no Constitution article; it states the methodological standard Article 3 already delegates, and belongs in the build methodology rather than in the Constitution.

## 6. The alternatives, so the recommendation is a choice and not the only option

- **Rule the other way — require continuous existence.** Would split `lpc` into two worlds. **No textual basis in Article 3**, and it would discard two independent demonstrations of coherence to satisfy a requirement the Constitution does not state.
- **Amend Article 3 instead of ruling.** Available, but heavier than needed and arguably wrong: Article 3 already delegates evaluation to methodology, so an amendment would take back a delegation the Article deliberately makes.
- **Defer.** Leaves every future gapped candidate blocked at Step 0, and leaves `lpc` describing itself as one world on a basis nobody has ratified.

**Recommendation: adopt §5, or redline it.** It is the lightest instrument that closes the question, it uses Article 3's own delegation rather than working around it, and its test can fail a world.
