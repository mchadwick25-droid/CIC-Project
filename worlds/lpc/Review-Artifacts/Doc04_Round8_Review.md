## HIGH

### H1 — The gating-clause denial conflates evidential support for organizing breadth with organizing breadth itself, and reaches it through a spliced quotation of the document's own Cross-Check

**Sites:** line 99 (§3 Classification); propagated to line 3 (Status), line 164 (§4 classification cell), line 210 (Open Item 2). **Source:** line 96 (§3 Cross-Check); CF V7.4 Part III, Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check.

**What line 96 actually says, read whole:**

> *"Divergence flagged rather than resolved, per CF V7.4's own Cross-Check rule…: the formulas are Documented; **their organizing breadth is not supported at the same level, this candidate's own six-test profile above being narrow at best.**"*

**What line 99 quotes it as saying:**

> *"§3's Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check above records that this candidate's organizing breadth **'is not supported at the same level… narrow at best,'** which is the opposite of organizing significant portions of the ecology."*

**(a) The ellipsis moves a predicate.** The seven words the ellipsis removes are *"this candidate's own six-test profile above being."* In line 96, `narrow at best` is predicated of **the six-test profile**, in an absolute clause supplying the *reason*. In line 99's quotation it is predicated of **organizing breadth**. The document misquotes its own sentence, by ellipsis, in the direction that makes its conclusion follow — the same defect class as Round 1's M8 spliced coercive-capacity quotation and Round 2's M3, fixed twice in this document already.

**(b) Even unspliced, the premise is about confidence, not about breadth.** *"Not supported at the same level"* means: the evidence for organizing breadth does not reach the level the formulas' existence reaches, which line 96 names as **Documented**. That is a claim about evidential support. It leaves Widely Accepted, Dominant Modern Reconstruction and Inferential all available. *"The evidence for X is weaker than the evidence for Y"* is not *"not-X."* Line 99 treats it as *"not-X"* — *"which is the opposite of organizing significant portions of the ecology"* — and that inference is invalid on its face.

**(c) The Framework says in terms that these are two different properties, in a sentence no pass in this build has ever quoted.** Immediately after the Cross-Check rule, CF V7.4 Part III reads:

> *"This cross-check exists because **gravity strength and evidential confidence are distinct properties that can diverge.** A world's reconstruction is not trustworthy if its most prominent organizing forces are also its most evidentially fragile ones without that tension being visible."*

The whole point of the Cross-Check is that a gravity can organize strongly and be evidentially thin. Deriving *"does not organize significant portions"* from *"organizing breadth is not evidentially supported at Documented level"* is exactly the collapse the instrument exists to prevent. **The pass conflated the two properties the Cross-Check was written to keep apart, and then used the result to deny the Supporting gate.**

**(d) The rule is also being run backwards from its written scope.** CF V7.4's operative sentence is: *"A candidate that passes the gravity tests impressively but rests primarily on Inferential/Thin evidence should be classified Supporting or Tensional regardless of how strongly it appears to organize the ecology, with the discrepancy … noted explicitly rather than resolved by upgrading the classification."* That is a **bar on upgrading** a strong-tests/thin-evidence candidate. Candidate 5 is the converse case — Documented evidence, narrow tests — which the rule does not address at all, and about which it certainly does not authorise a *downgrade* or a denial. Doc_04's line 96 paraphrase (*"where attestation and organizing strength diverge"*) silently generalises the rule to both directions; line 99 then leans the generalisation as if it were the text.

**(e) The conflation is visible inside a single table row.** §4 line 164's Cross-Check cell: *"organizing breadth **not comparably supported**, flagged not upgraded"* — evidential. §4 line 164's classification cell, two cells to the right: *"Supporting's gating clause, 'organize significant portions of the ecology,' **is not met** on this document's own Cross-Check finding"* — substantive denial. Same row, same source, two incompatible readings.

**Why this is the round's central finding.** The pass's entire defence is that it verified its proposition against the governing sentence before propagating it. It did — against the **Framework's** sentence. It then built the proposition's decisive ground on a **misquotation of its own document's** sentence and propagated that too. Verifying the external governing text does not verify the internal premise, and nothing the pass ran looked at line 96 at all.

**Fix:** state the divergence as line 96 and Open Item 1 state it — *"the two formulas' existence is Documented; the evidence for their organizing breadth does not reach that level"* — and stop deriving a denial of the gating clause from it. If the gating clause is to be denied, deny it on the substance (what portion of this ecology does this candidate organize?), not on a confidence finding, and argue it against Candidates 4 and 7 as well (M1).

## Testing the pass's negative-claim defence

The defence, from the commit message and the Decision Log's fourth entry:

> *"Round 7's closing prediction was that the next pass would select a proposition that fits, propagate it consistently, pass its own check, and leave the document uniformly wrong… **A negative claim is the one shape that prediction does not reach: it concedes rather than asserts, and it cannot be made uniformly wrong by being propagated.**"*

It is a good argument and it does not hold. Four reasons, in ascending order of weight.

**1. A negative claim is a claim, and it is false when it is too weak.** *"E does not establish P"* is truth-apt; it is false exactly when E establishes P. Propagating a false negative produces a document that is uniformly wrong in the understating direction — which is a real failure mode for a construction document, because Doc_05, Doc_07 and Doc_08 inherit the understatement and build on it. Open Item 2 now instructs three downstream documents to *"not treat its organizing breadth as established."* If the profile does support organizing breadth at a lower confidence level — which is what §3's Cross-Check actually says — that instruction is wrong, and it is wrong in a way no consistency check reaches. **The defence's premise is simply that error only comes in the over-claiming direction. Seven rounds of over-claiming is precisely what would install that assumption, and it is false.**

**2. The pass did not propagate a bare negative.** It propagated a negative headline with a positive ground welded to it: *"Supporting's gating clause is denied by §3's own Cross-Check finding"* (line 3), *"which is the opposite of organizing significant portions of the ecology"* (line 99), *"is not met on this document's own Cross-Check finding"* (line 164), *"do not treat its organizing breadth as established"* (line 210). Each of those is an assertion about what the Framework requires and what the document's own Cross-Check found. They are exactly the shape Round 7 predicted — *a proposition selected because it fits, propagated to every site, checked for consistency, returned clean* — and they are false (H1). **The negative half is what makes them read as concessions; the positive half is what does the work.** The defence describes the headline and not the payload.

**3. The concession is *concessive about the wrong thing*, which is the specific way a negative can be too weak here.** The document concedes that *its own testing* establishes nothing. That is a strong claim about the testing, dressed as modesty about the classification. It would be true if the six-test profile were indeterminate between all available labels. It is not: the profile rules out Primary decisively, rules out Tensional on this document's own Round 2 reasoning (nothing depends on it, it moves under no named external force, all three demonstrated relations are reinforcing), and leaves Supporting and the non-Framework fourth label. That is a profile that **narrows** the classification substantially. Saying it establishes nothing understates it, and understating it is what lets §4's cell record a classification with reasoning that negates it (H9).

**4. Round 7's prediction is satisfied, not evaded — one notch inward.** Round 7 predicted the pass would verify against the governing text and still be wrong, *"because line 96 will have been made consistent too."* What happened is subtler and worse: line 96 was **not** made consistent (H7 — four sites still state it evidentially, four now state it as a denial), and the pass's *agreement* check passed anyway, because it compared each site to the proposition rather than to line 96. The pass verified the **external** governing sentence, correctly and in full, and then misread the **internal** premise it built on — and every check it ran was scoped to the proposition, so none of them looked at line 96 at all.

**Verdict on the defence: it does not hold.** The one true thing in it is that a negative claim is harder to falsify by inspection than a positive one — which is not a reason to prefer it, it is a reason to check it harder. Every check the pass ran tested for the concession's **presence**. None tested its **correctness**, and the commission's suspicion is confirmed: the over-correction into understatement was invisible to all twenty of them.
