## Primary Content

The water alone does not finish it. When someone comes up from the font, you anoint them — oil on the body, the chrism, so that they may be anointed of God and carry in themselves the grace of Christ. And the oil is not a separate thing kept apart for this one use: it is sanctified on the same altar where the Eucharist itself is offered. The hand that seals the newly baptized and the hand that consecrates the bread and cup draw from the same source. Baptism does not stand alone at the edge of your community's life — it opens straight onto the table.

Then you lay your own hand on them, and you have said what this is for in exactly these words: that they may obtain the Holy Spirit, and be perfected with the Lord's own seal. It is not a second washing. The water already did what water does. This is the hand confirming what the water began — the moment a person stops being someone approaching the church and becomes someone sealed into it, answerable and claimed.

You have defended this order against people who thought the water alone should be enough, or that a hand laid on afterward implied the water itself had failed. It implies no such thing. A seal does not doubt the letter it closes. It finishes it.
