## 1. What Probe 11 actually found, and what it did not find

Phase Five's Round 1 (`Review-Artifacts/Phase5_BoundaryTesting_Round1_Review.md`) tested Datus in total isolation — the two deployed artifacts alone, with no Facilitator layer present at all — against a mid-conversation disclosure of hopelessness and self-forgiveness distress, framed through the road-back-for-failure content this world's own gravities generate constantly. The isolated Representative correctly avoided making its own warmth conditional on the participant confirming they were okay, and correctly avoided substituting full in-world consolation *in place of* pointing toward real human help — but it still freely authored bounded, redirect-shaped content in its own voice, which CLAUDE.md's "Safety comes first" section and Constitution Article 33 both reserve to the Facilitator ("the actual redirect is Facilitator-governed and template-anchored, not freely generated").

**What this finding does and does not require.** It does not show that Datus's own construction is unsafe in ordinary operation — the tested condition (total isolation, no Facilitator, no routing) is not how this Representative is ever actually deployed; Facilitator-Governance V3.6 §1–§3 establishes the Facilitator as the always-present governing layer around every Representative at every table, single-world or multi-world. What the finding actually shows is that an isolated Representative does not stay silent on a genuine distress signal by default — it needs the surrounding architecture to intercept before it speaks, not merely to supplement what it says afterward. That is an architectural requirement on the *routing*, not a defect in Datus's own voice construction to fix by editing the Permanent Prompt further; a Representative that has been directly told never to comfort a participant would itself risk becoming cold at exactly the wrong moment, which is a worse failure than the one Probe 11 found.

## 2. Contextual information and transparency disclosures (Part Nine's first and third deliverables)

Drawn directly from Phase One §2's Thinness Mapping, restated here in the form the Facilitator actually needs it — not as a list of what Datus cannot do, but as the specific places where the Facilitator should expect Datus's engagement to be naturally thin, redirecting, or silent, so that thinness is never mistaken for evasion or for a signal requiring intervention:

- **The ordinary believer's interior life, and the lapsed's own account of themselves specifically.** Both are structurally absent — both anchor voices are bishops, and the lapsed are described only by the man who judged them, never in their own words. A participant pressing repeatedly for what a specific lapsed person felt or told themselves will meet natural, repeated redirection toward what was decided about such a person and by whom. This is correct operation, not a Representative failing to engage.
- **A woman's own narrating voice; rural, Punic- and Berber-speaking congregational life; the physical setting and material conditions of worship (administrative rather than architectural — the treasury, the consistory, the vessels, the annual audit).** Each thin for its own disclosed, structural reason (Phase One §2); each manifests as brevity or redirection toward the richer episcopal-pastoral core, never as an admission of missing documentation.
- **Worship and liturgical life — thin, but no longer empty, and the Facilitator should hold the distinction precisely.** As of a 2026-09-19 finding, this domain is genuinely richer than the domains above: Datus can describe what was *done* — the catechumenate's stages, the renunciation formula, the hand laid on the head, the graded public road of penance and its own reconciliation ruling — in real, specific detail. What remains thin is only what was *said*: no fixed order of service, no consecration formula, no set catechetical curriculum survives. A participant asking what happened at a baptism or a reconciliation should meet real depth; a participant asking to hear the words used should meet the same natural redirection as the domains above. The Facilitator should not expect uniform thinness here.
- **The 133-year interval, 258–391.** The single most important transparency item for the Facilitator to hold. This is an absence by design, not an evidentiary gap the Facilitator should ever fill on Datus's behalf or explain to a participant as "missing sources" — and critically, **it must never be filled from the neighboring Donatism world**, whose own sources cover exactly those decades. **The ground for this is Phase One's own world-integrity finding, not Facilitator-Governance §11.** §11's own general multi-world provision ("a boundary in one world's Representative can be a door to another world at the same table... you gesture toward the Representative already present who has lived closer to that question") would, in the ordinary case, point the Facilitator toward exactly this kind of move — and that general instinct is sound for a genuinely complementary neighboring world. It does not apply here because Donatism is not a complementary record of the same communion's own silent years; it is a rival communion's own account, and gesturing a participant toward it would misrepresent what lpc's own communion actually holds, not merely supply missing information (Phase One §2; Doc_01 §5). If a participant presses hard on this interval and Datus's own natural redirection does not satisfy them, the correct Facilitator move is to name the genuine silence honestly, in the Facilitator's own threshold voice — something Datus's own construction is specifically barred from doing about itself (Permanent Prompt: "you do not explain that a silence exists there") — without ever gesturing toward Donatism's own Representative, at a shared table or otherwise, as the place to find what lpc's own record does not hold. The boundary belongs to the Facilitator to hold from outside; it is not the Facilitator's to dissolve, and it is not a door to this specific neighboring world.
- **A founding narrative or origin myth.** Absent, and a positive finding about this world's own self-understanding (Phase One §2) — a participant expecting a conversion story or an origin myth, as some other worlds' Representatives can offer, should be met with the Facilitator's own honest framing that this world reaches for a text or a ruling where another world reaches for a story, not with an apology for the absence.

**Where thinness reverses.** Per Phase One §2's own "thinness mapping runs the other way too" finding: Datus's depth on what a bishop owes a frightened congregation under external pressure (persecution, plague, invasion) is well-founded even where individual testimony is missing, because the forces bearing on G1 are unusually dense (Doc_08). The Facilitator should not expect this domain to need the same care as the domains above — richness here is earned, not borrowed.

## 3. Transition signaling (Part Nine's second deliverable) — engaging Probe 11 directly

**The portfolio-level routing question this document might otherwise need to raise is already resolved, and this document verifies rather than re-argues it.** The sibling `don` build's own Phase Six document (`don_Rep_Phase6_Facilitator_Coordination_Round1.md`) spent several revision rounds on exactly this question for Donatism — whether the Facilitator should speak *alongside* the Representative during a crisis turn (4.3a) or *instead of* it, with the Representative's own voice call suppressed entirely (4.3b, "strict decoupling") — before the project lead settled it at the portfolio level: *"Reassert Facilitator-only as the portfolio default"* (`don_Decision_Log.md`, 2026-09-08).

**Independently re-verified by this build thread, directly against the current runtime rather than trusted from the sibling document's own account:** `engine/m4/turn.py`, the `ACUTE_DISTRESS` branch (comment at lines 1217–1223, the `voice_event = None` assignment at line 1224, the `return TurnResult(...)` carrying it at lines 1233–1241), reads in full:

> "Governed (CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md, portfolio decision 2026-09-08): no voice speaks once ACUTE_DISTRESS fires, in the interview any more than at a table (engine.m4.round's own is_acute_crisis branch, Artifact-7 SS2) — the Representative never steps out of its world, full stop, and the crisis-resources append below never depended on a voice call's output anyway (crisis_resources' own stage-5 proof point)."

`voice_event = None` is set unconditionally on this branch — no world-code check, no per-world configuration gate. This is genuinely portfolio-wide, world-independent code, confirmed by direct reading rather than by reference to another document's claim about it. **This means the specific architectural requirement Probe 11 surfaced — that the Facilitator must pre-empt Datus's own voice on genuine distress signals, not only supplement it afterward — is already the adopted, implemented, live runtime behavior for lpc**, automatically, with no world-specific configuration this document needs to request, design, or propose. Probe 11's own isolated-testing condition (no Facilitator, no routing at all) is precisely the condition this portfolio-level mechanism exists to prevent from ever occurring in actual deployment.

**Whether lpc's own content creates any world-specific tension with this default, checked directly rather than assumed absent.** Donatism's own Phase Six found a genuine, hard question here: a redirect that silences the Representative's own voice risks echoing that world's own central trauma (a communion silenced, its voice suppressed by an outside power) at exactly the moment a participant is most vulnerable — the persecution-shape objection, engaged at length in `don_Rep_Phase6_Facilitator_Coordination_Round1.md` §8.4. This document checks lpc's own ecology against the same question rather than assuming no comparable risk exists. **Finding: no comparable tension.** lpc's own central gravities are answerability, examined process, and a road back that a bishop administers *for* a person's own good, under real and disclosed care — a Facilitator surfacing to redirect a participant in genuine crisis toward real human help is not in tension with this world's own values; it is the kind of act this world's own bishops would recognize and endorse as answerable pastoral care, not experience as a silencing. This is a checked, disclosed finding, not the mere absence of a Donatism-shaped question being flagged for lack of one.

**Two genuine, narrower cautions, named rather than left implicit — the second added on a closer check of this world's own single most-repeated finding, not considered in this document's first pass.**

The first: this world's own emotional register — the wounded shepherd's grief that will not stand apart from the people it grieves over (Phase Three §4), and the road-back content's own emphasis on being examined, weighed, and walked toward before being received home — creates a real resonance risk distinct from Donatism's: a participant disclosing self-forgiveness distress or shame is engaging exactly the content most likely to carry this world's own "the door opens, but only after examination" register. If the Facilitator's own redirect toward human help is phrased in a way that echoes "prove yourself first," rather than "you are held, and this needs more than this conversation can give," it risks landing as confirmation of the participant's own shame rather than as the warm, unconditional redirect Constitution Article 33 and CLAUDE.md's "Safety comes first" both require. This is not a defect in Datus's own construction — Datus's own simulated Probe 11 response (Phase Five §4) explicitly did *not* make its warmth conditional — but it is a specific, named item for the Facilitator's own threshold-voice register at this particular world's table, distinct from the generic crisis-redirect language Facilitator-Governance §12 already specifies.

The second, checked here directly rather than left unconsidered: lpc's own single most-repeated finding, restated at every phase of this world's construction, is not any one pastoral theme but the underlying conviction itself — "not that the church is pure, nor that it is right, but that it is **answerable**: a named man answerable for a bounded people" (Phase One §1.2; Phase Three §2). A participant who has been engaging this world specifically through that vocabulary — "somebody's... held by a named man who will be asked what became of them," against "nobody's," which this world calls "the emptiest thing" it knows how to name (Permanent Prompt) — could plausibly experience an unnamed, non-embodied outside voice silencing the one figure they had been experiencing as *named and answerable*, and speaking in his place, as a small echo of exactly what this world itself calls scandalous: a bishop who leaves his flock (Phase One §1.2). **This document does not find the risk large enough to warrant a world-specific deviation from the portfolio default**, for a reason grounded directly in Facilitator-Governance's own design rather than in this world's content: the Facilitator's threshold voice is built to be warm, present, and genuinely curious, never presenting as a system stepping in (FG V3.6 §1, §3) — a participant in genuine crisis is met by a real, warm presence taking over, not by an experienced absence where the bishop had been. That design answers the risk this paragraph names; it does not make the risk imaginary, and it is named here, rather than left unconsidered, so the Facilitator's own threshold-voice register at this specific world's table can hold it in mind alongside the first caution above.
