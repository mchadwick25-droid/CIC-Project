## 1. Candidate identification

From the census entry (`world-census.json`, II.18, quoted verbatim, re-verified directly against the live file this revision):

> **Name:** "The Roman Church in the Age of Gregory the Great"
> **Dates:** "c. 560-604" (`start: 560, end: 604`)
> **Region:** "Rome, its Sicilian and Italian estates, and the mission field beyond"
> **Sourcing:** "Very good. Gregory's Book of Pastoral Rule (about seventy thousand words) and the Register of his letters (a hundred and sixty thousand) both survive — the Register especially valuable because it is a working archive rather than a literary work: grain shipments, episcopal discipline, the ransom of captives, instructions to a mission, and the daily correspondence of a man administering a city the empire had largely stopped administering."
> **Why:** "Gregory the Great became bishop of Rome in 590, during a plague, in a city whose Roman government had already collapsed under the Gothic wars and the Lombard invasions. He ran the papal estates in Sicily as the city's food supply, negotiated directly with Lombard dukes when the imperial governor at Ravenna would not, and in 596 sent his own monks to Kent to begin a mission whose questions and his answers still survive in his letters."
> **Relations summary:** "Overlaps early-benedictine-italian-monasticism, whose window closes at Gregory's death, in person rather than in kind: he was a monk and wrote the only life of Benedict there is, but the Pastoral Rule is an episcopal manual and the Register is a government's paperwork. Its letters reach anglo-saxon-christianity, merovingian-gallic-christianity and byzantine-imperial-church-justinianic as evidence about them."

The candidate centers on one man's fourteen-year pontificate (590–604), though the census's own window (560–604) reaches back roughly thirty years earlier, to Gregory's own birth (c. 540) and his career as a Roman civic official and monk before his election — the formation that produced the pope, not merely the papacy itself. That formation includes a real Constantinopolitan phase (c. 579–c. 586), when Gregory served as papal representative (*apocrisiarius*) at the imperial court — verified directly against Gregory's own words in the Moralia's dedicatory epistle to Leander: *"When I knew you long since at Constantinople, my most blessed brother, at the time that I was kept there..."* This phase is carried forward as binding on Doc_01's own boundary work (§4.7 below), not developed further here.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing, quoted directly from the Methodology: *"This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."*

### A1 — The floor

The Methodology states the ordinary case resolves most candidates "without needing any further test" where "a candidate whose plain confession simply denies one or more of Article 4's commitments" fails the floor. Gregory I is a Chalcedonian bishop of Rome, whose window (from 560) opens 109 years after Chalcedon (451) and 235 years after Nicaea (325), whose own theological writing (the Moralia in Job, above all) is squarely within the Latin Nicene-Chalcedonian mainstream — his Christology, Trinitarian doctrine, and account of Christ's passion, resurrection, and return are not in question anywhere in the surviving corpus, and no serious scholarship treats Gregory as doctrinally marginal on these points. **A1 clears directly, with no complication.**

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements

Not applicable. The window (560–604) is more than two centuries after Nicaea; there is no pre-325 sliver to test, unlike World #6's 312 starting point. A2's continuity test simply does not arise here.

### A3 — Contemporary movements and interpretive fidelity

Not applicable. This is a historical movement of the late sixth and early seventh centuries, not a present-day community reinterpreting Nicene vocabulary non-literally.

### A4 — Hand-selected adjacent inclusion

Not invoked and not needed — A1 clears this candidate directly on its own confessional terms.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

Quoted directly: *"A movement's historical rivals... are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under this section... they appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates."*

This world's own window puts Gregory in direct, documented contention with several rivals and outsiders, none of which are separately screened here — each is carried forward as Doc_02's own subject matter, not resolved in this section:

- **The Lombards**, an Arian (Homoian) or, in some warbands, pagan people whose invasion and occupation of much of Italy from 568 onward is the single most consequential fact of Gregory's own world — his letters negotiate truces, ransom captives, and manage a frontier with an Arian power on Rome's own doorstep, in a way distinct from World #6's own Homoian-establishment disclosure (there, an internal imperial-church rival within Nicene territory two centuries earlier; here, an external Arian occupying power).
- **The Istrian schism (the "Three Chapters" controversy)** — a group of North Italian bishops (centered on Aquileia and Grado) who refused to accept the Second Council of Constantinople's (553) condemnation of the Three Chapters, remaining out of communion with Rome through much of Gregory's own pontificate. An internal Chalcedonian dispute over reception of a later council, not a doctrinal-floor question — Gregory's own engagement with it is real and belongs in Doc_02.
- **Surviving North African Donatism** — already confirmed as a live cross-link at the library stage (`donatism.yaml`'s own `context` row on Gregory's *Epistolae Selectae*, per the Source Readiness Dossier §2) — Gregory's own correspondence describes and combats a still-lingering Donatist remnant some 150 years after Augustine, not a fresh Donatist eligibility question.
- **The Constantinopolitan patriarchate**, specifically Gregory's own sustained protest against Patriarch John IV "the Faster's" use of the title "Ecumenical Patriarch" — an internal Chalcedonian jurisdictional dispute between two sees in full communion, not a floor question.

None of these requires a separate Step 0 pass; A5 routes all four into this world's own Doc_02 as named opponents and rivals, exactly as it routed Homoian Christianity into World #6's.

Strand Determination (Article 21) is explicitly Step 1's own work, not Step 0's — flagged forward to Doc_01, not resolved here, though §6 below names the reason this candidate's strand question is likely to resolve differently than World #6's own three-strand finding.

### Section A conclusion

**The candidate clears Section A.** A1 applies directly and is satisfied without complication; A2 and A3 do not apply; A4 is not needed; A5 correctly routes this world's real historical rivals and outsiders (the Lombards, the Istrian schismatics, surviving Donatism, the Constantinopolitan jurisdictional dispute) into Doc_02 rather than treating any of them as a separate eligibility question.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Test, quoted directly: *"does enough independent, checkable evidence survive to support a defensible reconstruction at Documented or Widely Accepted confidence for its core claims, with the asymmetry named rather than hidden?"*

Strong pass, confirmed directly against the library package rather than assumed from the census note. Four works are vendored in Gregory's own hand and confirmed present in `cic/texts/` (§7 below, and in full in `Source_Registry.md`) — a substantial but not exhaustive share of his own surviving corpus (see the further surviving-but-unvendored works named below):

- The *Register of the Epistles* (Books I–XIV, selections; `npnf212`, `npnf213`) — a working administrative archive of roughly 160,000 words: grain shipments, episcopal discipline, the ransom of captives, negotiations with the Lombards and with Constantinople.
- *The Book of Pastoral Rule* (`npnf212`) — roughly 70,000 words, the single most influential handbook on episcopal office the Latin Middle Ages inherited (Carolingian legislation required bishops to know it; Alfred the Great translated it into Old English three centuries later — noted here as reception history, not asserted as this world's own evidence, and not independently re-verified against a primary source this session).
- The *Moralia in Job* (`bliss1844`, `bliss1845`, `bliss1847`, `bliss1850`) — Gregory's own massive allegorical commentary on Job, by far his largest single work (roughly 1.02 million words by direct count, some six times the Register's own ~160,000 — corrected this revision from an earlier, inexact "four times"), begun orally, before his pontificate, while he served as papal representative (*apocrisiarius*) at Constantinople, and completed and dedicated to Leander of Seville only later, well into his pontificate (corrected this revision from an earlier claim that the dedication predates the Register's own Leander correspondence — see Doc_02 §1 for the full correction) — directly confirmed by Gregory's own words in its dedicatory epistle: *"When I knew you long since at Constantinople, my most blessed brother, at the time that I was kept there..."*
- *The Dialogues* (`gregory-great_dialogues_gardner1911`) — four books of hagiographical and theological dialogue, Book II of which is the only surviving *Life of St. Benedict of Nursia*.

**Further works of Gregory's own survive but are not currently vendored in this corpus**, named here so B1's own evidentiary picture is not overstated: the forty *Homilies on the Gospels* (preached to the Roman populace — the closest surviving Gregorian material to a genuinely lay-facing register, directly relevant to B2's own concern below); the twenty-two *Homilies on Ezekiel*; and an exposition on the Song of Songs. None of these is Native to this world's currently vendored holdings; each is carried forward as a binding Doc_02 acquisition lead, not treated as available.

Together the four vendored works run to well over a million words (the Register and Pastoral Rule alone are the census's own cited quarter-million; the four Moralia volumes alone add roughly another million on top of that, by direct word count), essentially all of it Gregory's own voice — a scale and directness of evidence available to very few candidates anywhere in this project's portfolio. The asymmetry this strength carries is named here, not deferred: this is one man's own writing and self-presentation almost in its entirety; corroborating, independent testimony about Gregory from outside his own hand is comparatively thin within this world's own window (his earliest connected biography, by a monk of Whitby, dates from roughly a century after his death and falls outside 560–604 and is not currently vendored; Paul the Deacon's later *Historia Langobardorum*, also outside the window, likewise is not vendored; Gregory of Tours's own *History of the Franks*, Book X, is a genuinely independent, within-window narrative source — written c. 591–594, describing the 590 plague and election from an eyewitness deacon's own report — and is also not currently vendored). This is carried forward as a binding Doc_02 disclosure and acquisition-lead list, not resolved here.

**Passes B1 comfortably**, with the source-asymmetry (one man's own testimony, thin external corroboration inside the window) named as a binding disclosure obligation for Doc_02.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

Test, quoted directly: *"can this candidate plausibly support most or all eight lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only (most commonly theological/doctrinal argument alone)?"*

This is a real question raised by the Source Readiness Dossier's own framing (§0 above, correcting this document's earlier misattribution of it to the live census) rather than by the census text itself, and it deserves a direct answer rather than a restatement of the worry. The concern — whether an entry can properly be built around a single person at all — is a real question about ecology, not merely about authorship, and it is answered here on the evidence rather than assumed either way.

The honest finding is that this candidate's own surviving voice is unusually broad *in kind*, even though it is narrow *in author*. Gregory's four vendored works are not four versions of the same genre: the *Register* is administrative and pastoral-practical (estate management, discipline, diplomacy); the *Pastoral Rule* is a manual of clerical formation and spiritual direction; the *Moralia* is scriptural-allegorical and ascetical-contemplative, written by a man who had been a monk and who structured his own household at the Lateran along quasi-monastic lines even as pope; the *Dialogues* are hagiographical and popular-devotional, aimed at edifying belief in miracle and the unseen world among Gregory's own Italian contemporaries. Read together, rather than as one man's opinion repeated four times, they give this world real, direct material across Organizational/Authority Structures (the *Register*'s own governance), Formation Ecology and Meaning Transmission (the *Pastoral Rule*'s explicit subject), Worship Integration and ascetical/contemplative practice (the *Moralia*'s governing mode, and Gregory's own liturgical reputation — the Gregorian chant tradition later attributed to him, noted here as reception legend requiring its own confidence tagging at Doc_02, not asserted as documented fact), and Power/Influence & Historical Dynamics (the Lombard crisis, running through the *Register*). This is a genuinely wider ecological base than a single genre would supply, even though it converges on a single author — the honest limitation, carried forward rather than minimized, is that it converges on a single, elite, male, monastic-then-episcopal vantage throughout, with no comparably-attested ordinary Roman layperson's own perspective on any of it (see B1's disclosure above, and Doc_02 §6).

**Passes B2**, with the single-person concern answered directly (breadth of genre and function within one man's corpus, not breadth of authorship, is what carries this world's own ecology) and the resulting source-skew logged as binding on Doc_02, on the same B2 discipline World #6's own Step 0 already used.

### B3 — Uniqueness (differentiation)

Test, quoted directly: judged relative to what is already selected or built for the phase, not in the abstract.

- **Versus World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, c. 312–451):** the closest thematic neighbor in the built portfolio — both worlds are substantially about the Roman episcopate's own claims to authority. The distinction is not the roughly 110-year gap between them alone but a genuine change in what "Roman primacy" actually means on the ground: World #6's Damasus-through-Leo strand consolidates primacy claims from a position of active imperial partnership, inside a still-functioning, still-Nicene-contested empire. Gregory's own position is a real change in degree, corrected here from this draft's original overstatement — not imperial *absence* (Gregory's own election required imperial confirmation; he spent roughly six years, within this world's own window, as papal representative at Maurice's own court in Constantinople; and the "Universal Bishop" dispute this world's own Doc_02 draws on directly is Gregory petitioning that same emperor to act against a rival see's claim, itself a juridical contest, not evidence of imperial irrelevance). The real, defensible contrast is narrower and better grounded: Byzantine *military and administrative* authority in Italy specifically is functionally incapable of defending or provisioning Rome (mediated through an underresourced exarch at Ravenna, while the old Western imperial structure itself is gone entirely — Romulus Augustulus's deposition, 476, lies well inside the gap between these two worlds), even as Gregory continues to operate as a loyal, engaged partner of the same imperial church structure World #6 reconstructs the consolidation of. Gregory's own primacy-adjacent authority is exercised substantially because no one else is left to feed Rome, ransom its captives, or negotiate with the Lombards at its gates, *alongside*, not instead of, continued juridical partnership with Constantinople — administrative and pastoral necessity layered onto, not replacing, World #6's own juridical-partnership logic. This is named here as this candidate's own primary-gravity distinction from World #6, carried forward as binding on Doc_01 (§6 below), stated in this corrected, narrower form.
- **Versus `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism` (not yet built):** the census's own `relationsSummary` for II.18 (quoted in full, §1 above) already states the overlap is "in person rather than in kind" — Gregory was a monk and wrote the only surviving *Life of St. Benedict* (Dialogues Book II), but the *Pastoral Rule* and *Register* are episcopal and governmental, not monastic, material. Confirmed directly against the Source Readiness Dossier (§5): this boundary is real but requires active vigilance once the Dialogues are drawn on, since Book II specifically is monastic-biographical material sitting inside an otherwise non-monastic corpus, and the Register itself is separately and already carried as `context` on `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism.yaml` ("monastic discipline in Italy and Sicily," per that world's own corpus-map row). Carried forward as binding on Doc_01 and Doc_02, not resolved here.
- **Versus `byzantine-imperial-church-justinianic`, `anglo-saxon-christianity`, `merovingian-gallic-christianity` (built or on record):** the census's own `relationsSummary` (§1 above) confirms Gregory's letters "reach" each of these "as evidence about them" — already correctly carried as `context`-only links (Source Readiness Dossier §1), not this world's own claim on their material. No overlap risk. (Surviving North African Donatism is a related but distinct case, evidenced by different material — this document's own §2, A5, above — and is not part of this specific `relationsSummary`-confirmed list.)

**Passes B3.** The one boundary requiring active vigilance during construction (early-benedictine-italian-monasticism, via Dialogues Book II specifically) is named here so Doc_01/Doc_02 treat it as a live question, not an assumed-obvious one — the same discipline World #6's own Step 0 used for its World #5 adjacency.

### B4 — User needs (audience fit)

Honest assessment, not maximized: this world's strongest, least arguable fit is with *pastors and teachers* — the *Pastoral Rule* is historically the single most influential handbook on episcopal office the Latin Middle Ages inherited (§3, B1 above: required reading under Carolingian episcopal legislation; translated into Old English by Alfred the Great). Whether it remains in active use in seminary formation specifically today is not independently verified this session and is not asserted here; this document rests the B4 case on its documented historical influence, not on an unverified claim about its current pedagogical status. It offers real material to *historically interested learners* (the birth of the medieval papacy, the first monk to become pope, the Gregorian mission to Kent) and to *thoughtful believers* drawn to the *Moralia*'s own ascetical and contemplative register. Its fit with *curious seekers* and *deconstructing Christians* is honestly weaker and should not be overstated, matching the same caution World #6's own Step 0 applied to itself: this is an administrative and monastic-formation register, not a devotional-existential one in the way Desert Monasticism is. **Passes B4, honestly scoped rather than claimed universally.**

### B5 — Scale (tiebreak only)

Rome remains a name of the first importance, but by Gregory's own day the city itself is materially diminished — repeated Lombard sieges, a population a fraction of its imperial-era size, aqueducts and grain supply under real strain, all directly attested in the *Register* itself. This is not a scale boost in the ordinary sense; if anything, the candidate's distinctiveness (§B3 above) depends partly on this diminishment. Logged as a modest, non-decisive B5 note per the Methodology's own instruction that Scale is "a tiebreak note only," not weighed further.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Against the tiering rubric: this candidate clears Section A directly (§2); clears Sourcing comfortably, with a named source-asymmetry (B1); clears Ecology, with the single-person concern answered directly rather than assumed away (B2); offers genuine differentiation from its nearest thematic neighbor (World #6) on a substantive, narrowly-stated ground, with one adjacency (early-benedictine-italian-monasticism) flagged for active vigilance (B3); and serves multiple named audiences honestly (B4). **The candidate's own ecology supports a Tier 1 — Strong seed reading of Section B's five criteria.** Per §0 above, this document treats that reading as a recommendation grounded entirely in this candidate's own evidence — not a self-executing portfolio-level Tier assignment — because Section B's own text describes tiering as part of a phase-level process this document does not itself run; see §6 below.
