## 1. Primary Sources

All four of the works this world's own Doc_01 names are vendored, confirmed present in `cic/texts/` with confirmed rights entries in `cic/texts/REGISTRY.yaml`, and already assigned in `cic/corpus-map/roman-church-gregorian.yaml` at `role: tradition`, `confidence: assigned` — the corpus-map work was done by a prior source-research pass (2026-09-24) and is used here, not redone. **The Register of the Epistles** (Books I–XIV, selections; `npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml`, `npnf213_gregory-great-ephraim-syrus-aphrahat.xml`). Roughly 160,000 words of administrative and pastoral correspondence — this world's single richest evidentiary layer, and the source of most of the specific claims below. Directly re-verified this session, not taken on the corpus-map's own description alone:

- **The "Universal Bishop" protest**, a Book V letter to the Emperor Maurice concerning Patriarch John IV "the Faster" of Constantinople, verified word for word at locus `iii.v.v.x-p7`–`p8`: *"Lo, he received the keys of the heavenly kingdom, and power to bind and loose is given him, the care and principality of the whole Church is committed to him, and yet he is not called the universal apostle; while the most holy man, my fellow-priest John, attempts to be called universal bishop. I am compelled to cry out and say, O tempora, O mores!"* — followed immediately, in the same letter, by: *"Lo, all things in the regions of Europe are given up into the power of barbarians, cities are destroyed, camps overthrown, provinces depopulated... and yet priests... seek for themselves names of vanity, and glory in new and profane titles."* This is Gregory's own voice directly, not a later characterization of his views, and it does double duty as this world's own primary evidence for the Constantinopolitan jurisdictional dispute (Doc_01 §2, §5). **A qualification, added this revision (Round 1 review, LOW finding):** the "barbarians... in the regions of Europe" language is general rhetoric about the empire's own condition, not a Lombard-specific statement — it is not, on its own, primary evidence for the Lombard crisis specifically (which the Register evidences directly elsewhere, per the separate Lombard material below), and this document no longer cites it for that purpose.
- **The Reccared/Leander letter**, correctly identified in this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding: the first draft mislabeled this "Book IX"; it is Book I, Epistle 43, dated 591, confirmed directly against the file's own Book-division headings — "Book I" at the div immediately preceding this letter's own div, with no further "Book" heading until "Book II" well after it), verified word for word at locus `iii.v.i.xxx-p5`–`p6`. In the same letter, Gregory first describes the burden of pastoral office in an extended ship-in-storm metaphor — *"I am compelled sometimes to steer in the very face of the opposing waters... With tears I remember how I have lost the placid shore of my rest, and with sighs I behold the land which still, with the winds of affairs blowing against me, I cannot reach"* — and then turns, in the next paragraph, to his joy at "our common son, the most glorious King Rechared" having "been converted with most entire devotion to the Catholic faith" (corrected from this document's earlier, imprecise "very next sentence" — it is the next paragraph, following two further sentences of the storm metaphor). This single letter is this world's own best direct evidence for the contemplative/active-pastoral tension named in Doc_01 §4, and for the Reccared correspondence (Doc_01 §2) — both in Gregory's own words, in the same document.
- **The self-designation "servant of the servants of God"** (*servus servorum Dei*), verified as a recurring epistolary formula at multiple loci including `iii.v.i.i-p7` (Epistle I.1, dated September 590 — his very first extant letter as pope), `iii.v.i.xxvi-p3`, and `iii.v.vi.xxxii-p4`. **Corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, HIGH finding):** this document's first draft read the formula as a deliberate rebuke of the "Universal Bishop" claim above, tagged Dominant Modern Reconstruction. Directly re-checked against the file's own endnote at `iii.v.i.i-p8`: the formula was already in use by Pope Damasus and by Augustine before Gregory, and Gregory's own first use of it (September 590) precedes the Universal Bishop dispute (595) by five years — a phrase used from his very first month in office cannot be a targeted response to a dispute that had not yet happened. This document now carries the formula's significance as Contested, not Dominant Modern Reconstruction, and removes the earlier, untraceable "much modern scholarship" and "R.A. Markus" attributions (§3, §9 below) rather than repeat an unverified citation.
- **The Istrian schism / Three Chapters material** — **relocated and corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding: the first draft cited the wrong locus and dropped the material's own "unsuccessful" framing).** The actual locus is `NPNF212` around line 23070 (the volume's own Book I summary in its introductory apparatus, not Gregory's own letter text), which states Gregory's "ineffectual attempts, though apparently supported by the Emperor, to bring the Istrian bishops to submission in the matter of the 'Three Chapters' (see Ep. 16)." A second, directly corroborating apparatus passage (`NPNF212`, lines 22458–22463) is explicit that these efforts failed: *"He in vain endeavoured, either by remonstrance or by trying to enlist the emperor's aid, to bring back the Istrian bishops to conformity."* The same passage names Pope Vigilius's and Pelagius I's own earlier assent to the Three Chapters' condemnation as the schism's actual origin (c. 554–557, not 553 itself, per Doc_01 §2's own correction), and names a genuinely striking detail directly relevant to Doc_02 §6 below: even the Lombard queen **Theodelinda** — "so orthodox a Catholic" that Gregory "esteemed her highly" and corresponded with her "cordially" — could not be persuaded to accept the Three Chapters' condemnation, a real complication to any simple "Arian Lombards" framing. Used here at Confidence B (apparatus-sourced, not yet independently traced to Gregory's own Ep. 16 directly) — that further tracing is named as an open item (§9), not resolved here.
- **Surviving North African Donatism** — the same apparatus passage (`NPNF212`, line 22462) also names Gregory's own "invoking of the secular arm for suppression of what remained of the Donatist schism in Africa (see Ep. 74)" — a direct, specific pointer to a Register letter on this exact topic, not identified in this document's first draft. This bears directly on the Turchi *Epistolae Selectae* double-placement question (Doc_01 §7 item 5): the Register itself, independent of the Turchi Latin selection, already contains at least one Donatism-specific letter (Ep. 74), which is Doc_02's own evidence, not yet individually re-traced to its own locus this session, that the two collections may cover genuinely different material rather than duplicate one another — named as a real lead for whoever next resolves that question, not resolved here.
- **Lombard material generally** — the Register's own extensive correspondence concerning truces, tribute, and the Lombard king Agilulf is confirmed present in volume (the introductory apparatus, lines 22132–22206, surveys it at length), and is Native to this world at Confidence B; this document does not claim to have independently re-traced every specific Lombard-related letter to its own locus this session, and that granular tracing is named as an open item for whichever thread first draws vivid, quotable Lombard material for Doc_05.

**The Book of Pastoral Rule** (`npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml`). Roughly 70,000 words. Its opening chapter is verified word for word at locus `iii.iv.ii.ii-p3`: *"No one presumes to teach an art till he has first, with intent meditation, learnt it. What rashness is it, then, for the unskilful to assume pastoral authority, since the government of souls is the art of arts!"* — the single most direct primary statement of this world's own core formation logic (Doc_01 §1, §3): pastoral office as a skill requiring formation, not a status to be claimed.

**The Moralia in Job** (`bliss1844`, `bliss1845`, `bliss1847`, `bliss1850`). Gregory's largest single work, roughly four times the length of the Register. **Closed in this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding: this document's first draft wrongly reported that a targeted search had failed).** Directly re-searched this revision: the dedicatory epistle to Leander of Seville opens the work (file `bliss1844`, from line ~384) and is readily locatable despite the file's own OCR irregularities. Verified word for word, lines ~552–560: *"When I knew you long since at Constantinople, my most blessed brother... I came out naked from the shipwreck of human life. For as the vessel that is negligently moored, is very often (when the storm waxes violent) tossed by the water out of its shelter on the safest shore, so under the cloak of the Ecclesiastical office, I found myself plunged on a sudden in a sea of secular matters, and because I had not held fast the tranquillity of the monastery when in possession, I learnt by losing it, how closely it should have been held."* (Rendered here with the OCR's own scattered letter-spacing and long-s artifacts silently normalized to standard spelling — no wording added, omitted, or altered — per this project's own scan-normalization convention for vendored public-domain OCR text.) This is this world's own second, independent, and earlier (the Moralia's own dedication predates the Register letter above) primary-source statement of the contemplative/active-pastoral tension named in Doc_01 §4, in Gregory's own words, addressed to the same Leander named in the Register letter above — confirming, rather than merely paralleling, that letter's own account. This also directly confirms Doc_01 §2's own addition of a Constantinopolitan phase to this world's boundary: Gregory states plainly that he knew Leander "at Constantinople... at the time that I was kept there," the same phase during which this Moralia was itself begun.

**The Dialogues** (`gregory-great_dialogues_gardner1911`). Confirmed directly against the file's own body text, not merely the vendoring note's front-matter description: "The End of the First Book" (file line ~3379) is followed immediately by the heading "The Second Book / Of the Life and Miracles of St. Bennet" (file lines ~3390–3396) and its opening line, verified word for word: *"There was a man of venerable life, blessed by grace, and blessed in name, for he was called Benedict or Bennet: who, from his younger years, carried always the mind of an old man; for his age was inferior to his virtue"* — the only surviving connected *Life of St. Benedict of Nursia*. This is Native to this world (Gregory's own voice) and is the specific, named point of potential overlap with `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism`, per Doc_01 §6 and the vigilance obligation carried forward from Step 0.

## 2. Author Gravity Assessment

Per Constitution Article 26, every major voice is assessed across all five dimensions. This world has exactly one major primary voice, which sharpens rather than excuses the requirement — the whole evidentiary weight of this world rests on getting this one assessment right.

**Gregory I ("the Great").**
- *Visibility:* Total, and uniquely so among this project's confirmed and in-progress worlds — every Native primary source is his own writing, in a way even World #6's most-visible figures (Eusebius, Ambrose, Leo) were not for their own worlds, since World #6 draws on at least three major independent voices plus conciliar acts. This is this world's own central evidentiary fact (§6 below), not a side note.
- *Representativeness:* Gregory is not a representative voice for ordinary Roman or Italian lay experience of this period — he is a representative voice for exactly one thing, the office he himself held and wrote about self-consciously (the *Pastoral Rule* is, among other things, Gregory's own reflection on what it means to hold that office). His own dual formation (civil administrator, then monk) makes him a genuinely unusual, not typical, figure even among bishops of his own era.
- *Influence:* Enormous and exceptionally long-lived — the *Pastoral Rule*'s reception across the medieval West (Carolingian episcopal legislation; Alfred the Great's Old English translation) and the Dialogues' role in transmitting Benedictine hagiography both testify to influence reaching centuries beyond this world's own 604 close. Doc_02 records this as reception history, not as this world's own evidence — the influence itself happened after this world's own window ends.
- *Limitations:* A former civil administrator and monk writing, throughout, in his own voice about his own office and his own struggles with it — there is no independent corrective the way Augustine's *Confessions* independently corroborates one specific claim of Ambrose's in World #6's own Doc_02. The "Universal Bishop" letter above is a real, direct primary source, but it is also, on its own terms, one side of a live jurisdictional dispute — Gregory's own account of John the Faster's claim and motives is not independently checked here against any Constantinopolitan source, none of which is currently vendored in this corpus.
- *Transmission History:* The Register survives in a relatively full form for a document of its kind and period. **Corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding: the first draft misattributed this claim to the wrong locus.)** Line 22132 is the NPNF volume's own Prefatory Note on how its own selection from the full Register was made, not a statement about sixth-century archival practice; the actual statement on the Register's own transmission — noting its dependence in part on John the Deacon's own later work — sits further on in the same introductory apparatus, around line 22971, not independently re-traced to a specific paragraph this session. This document also notes, without resolving, a further transmission-relevant fact bearing on the Turchi cross-check (§1 above, and Doc_01 §7 item 5): the NPNF Register follows the traditional Benedictine-edition arrangement, while the Turchi 1907 Latin selection follows the different Ewald–Hartmann numbering — a real complication for directly comparing the two collections' own letter numbers that whoever next resolves the double-placement question will need to account for.

## 4. Formation Narrative Sources

- **The Dialogues themselves** function as formation narrative in their own right, not merely as a primary-source data point about Gregory — they are explicitly hagiographical and devotional in purpose, aimed at edifying belief in the miraculous among Gregory's own contemporaries. This is Native, load-bearing material for a future Doc_09, and the specific overlap with `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism` (Book II) is the same vigilance point already named in Doc_01 §6.
- **Gregory of Tours's *History of the Franks*, Book X, is a genuinely independent, within-window narrative witness, added to this section this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding).** Written c. 591–594 by a contemporary Frankish bishop, it narrates the 590 plague, Gregory's own election, and the sevenfold litany procession from what it presents as an eyewitness deacon's own report — the single most important non-Gregorian narrative source for this world's own opening event, and, unlike the Dialogues, not Gregory's own voice about himself. **Not currently vendored in this corpus.** Named here as a significant acquisition lead, not merely a background fact: this document does not use it to support any claim, since it cannot be verified against the vendored text.
- **The anonymous Whitby monk's *Life of Gregory* and Paul the Deacon's later *Historia Langobardorum*** both fall outside this world's own 560–604 window (roughly a century and over a century after Gregory's death, respectively) and are, on that basis, Excluded (Out-of-Boundary) rather than Native even if eventually acquired — named in the Source Registry (row added this revision) so their exclusion is on record, not merely implied by silence.
- **The *Liber Pontificalis*'s own entry on Gregory, and John the Deacon's ninth-century *Vita*** (the latter already named indirectly by the NPNF apparatus itself, §2 above, as a source for some of its own introductory material) are both later, out-of-window narrative sources this document does not treat as Native for the same reason — named in the Source Registry as Excluded (Out-of-Boundary), not silently omitted.

## 6. Source Asymmetries and Missing Voices

Per Doc_01 §4.1 (binding on this document): this world's evidentiary base is, almost without remainder, one man's own writing. Named specifically for this world, not merely gestured at via the general portfolio caveat, and more extreme in degree than World #6's own comparable disclosure (three-plus independent primary voices there, one here).

- **Elite, clerical, and single-authorial concentration is total, not merely dominant, in this world's Native record (Article 20).** Every Native primary source in §1 is Gregory's own writing. This world has no equivalent to a second bishop's correspondence, a lay believer's letter, or a conciliar act recording other voices in the room — even World #6's own thin ordinary-believer texture rested on at least several independent elite voices; this world's texture, thin or otherwise, rests on one.
- **Gender (Article 20):** almost entirely absent from this world's own Native voice, though two named individuals deserve naming specifically rather than a vague collective gesture — corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, LOW finding) from an earlier draft's imprecise "Lombard and Visigothic royal women." **Bertha**, queen of Kent, is named and addressed directly in Gregory's own letters (confirmed present, e.g. the Bertha correspondence discussed in the NPNF apparatus at lines 23662–23683). **Theodelinda**, the Lombard queen, is a documented political actor in her own right in this world's own record, not merely a name — verified directly this revision (§1 above): a Catholic Lombard queen Gregory "esteemed highly" and corresponded with "cordially," whose own refusal to accept the Three Chapters' condemnation is recorded as a real, personal disappointment to him. Her own presence complicates any simple "Arian Lombards" framing (Doc_01 §2) as much as it evidences this section's own point: even a royal figure this well-documented is preserved here only through Gregory's own framing, never through her own words. **Brunhild**, the Merovingian queen Gregory corresponds with (Doc_01 §2), is named but not independently evidenced in this document beyond that correspondence's existence. None of these women's own letters back to Gregory are currently part of this world's Native holdings, and no independent female voice from within this world's own window is currently identified. Named as a real, undischarged absence, not developed further — the Framework's three bounding conditions for the Affirmative Duty's secondary (reconstruction) prong are not currently met for any specific case, matching World #6's own honest finding on this point.
- **Ordinary lay Roman and Italian experience of famine, siege, and Lombard occupation** is visible in this world's record only through Gregory's own administrative description of it — never through any surviving voice of the people actually living it. ## 7. Confidence Map

- **Documented / Widely Accepted:** Gregory's election (590) and death (604); the Lombard invasion (568) and its continued pressure through this window; Reccared's own personal conversion (587) and its ratification at Toledo (589), and Gregory's correspondence marking it; the "Universal Bishop" dispute with Patriarch John IV; the Gregorian mission to Kent (596) and correspondence with Æthelberht and Bertha; the Constantinopolitan legation (c. 579–c. 586); the existence and basic content of the four major primary works named in §1.
