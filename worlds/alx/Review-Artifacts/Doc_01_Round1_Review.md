## FINDINGS

### 1. [SUBSTANTIAL] — Section 1.2 (and Section 11, item 1): the "completely false" quotation is misattributed to van den Hoek; it is R. van den Broek's.

**Location:** Section 1.2, second sentence of the block quote; repeated in Section 11 summary item 1 ("van den Hoek 1997").

**What the document says:** *'Annewies van den Hoek argues that "the whole idea of a Christian school with a succession of teachers handing down a fixed tradition of learning to their pupil successors is completely false, at least until the second decade of the third century."'*

**What is wrong:** The quoted sentence is not van den Hoek's own formulation. It is a direct quotation from **Roelof van den Broek**, "The Christian 'School' of Alexandria in the Second and Third Centuries," in J. W. Drijvers & A. A. MacDonald (eds.), *Centres of Learning: Learning and Location in Pre-Modern Europe and the Near East* (Leiden: Brill, 1995), pp. 39–47. Van den Hoek's 1997 HTR article quotes van den Broek; the document has collapsed the quotation into van den Hoek's own voice and cited only her article. Attributing one scholar's quoted words to the citing author is precisely the kind of citation misrepresentation Article 28 prohibits, and the benchmark Syriac Doc_01 is scrupulous about this class of error (cf. its Round 3 correction of the qyama citation's author).

**Evidence (web verification):**
- Van den Hoek's article is VERIFIED to exist: "The 'Catechetical' School of Early Christian Alexandria and Its Philonic Heritage," *Harvard Theological Review* 90.1 (1997): 59–87. (Cambridge Core: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/catechetical-school-of-early-christian-alexandria-and-its-philonic-heritage/F5D56A086813C4D58D4AAE8041292B2D)
- The quoted sentence is van den Broek's. A secondary treatment (Oliver, "The Catechetical School in Alexandria," *Verbum et Ecclesia* 36.1, 2015) attributes the wording to "Broek," and van den Broek's own 1995 essay is confirmed to argue there was "no school, in the sense of a Christian academy, with a regular teaching programme," with teachers as charismatic laymen rather than incorporated chair-holders. (https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2074-77052015000100019 ; van den Broek essay: https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789004247154/B9789004247154-s005.xml)

**Recommended fix:** Attribute the quotation to van den Broek (1995), e.g., *"as R. van den Broek puts it — quoted approvingly by van den Hoek — 'the whole idea of a Christian school … is completely false, at least until the second decade of the third century' (van den Broek, 'The Christian "School" of Alexandria,' in* Centres of Learning*, 1995, 39–47; cited in van den Hoek 1997)."* Update Section 11 item 1 accordingly. This does not weaken the point — it strengthens it (two named scholars, not one) — but the attribution must be correct.

### 2. [SUBSTANTIAL] — Section 1.2 (and Sections 11, 12): Scholten's thesis is misrepresented; he argues the school *was* a genuine institution, the opposite of the position he is enlisted to support.

**Location:** Section 1.2, sentence beginning "Clemens Scholten likewise argues…"; propagated to Section 11 item 1 and Section 12 (Doc_02 handoff).

**What the document says:** *"Clemens Scholten likewise argues that 'catechetical school' is a misnomer and that the Eusebian succession-list is a later reconstruction rather than a contemporary institutional fact."* The word "likewise" places Scholten alongside van den Hoek/van den Broek as a co-witness against the school's existence as a formal institution.

**What is wrong:** Only the first half is right. Scholten does argue that the label "catechetical school" (*Katechetenschule*) is a misnomer — but his point is that the institution was **not** a facility for baptismal/catechetical preparation; it was instead, in his own words, *"die theologische Hochschule der dortigen Kirche"* (the theological college of that church), with an advanced curriculum. In other words, Scholten **affirms** that there was a genuine institutional theological school; he reclassifies its function rather than denying its institutional existence. He therefore sits substantially on the *opposite* side of the institution question from van den Hoek and van den Broek. Enlisting him with "likewise" as support for "the didaskaleion was not a formal institution / the succession-list is merely a later reconstruction" inverts his actual position and is a misrepresentation under Article 28. It also over-loads the scope argument: Section 1.2 presents two scholarly pillars against the narrow reading, but one of them (Scholten) actually cuts the other way on the very question at issue.

**Evidence (web verification):** Scholten's article is VERIFIED to exist — "Die alexandrinische Katechetenschule," *Jahrbuch für Antike und Christentum* 38 (1995): 16–37. Its thesis, as reported in the secondary literature, is that the term "catechetical school" should not be used because it misleads, and that the institution functioned as the *theologische Hochschule der dortigen Kirche* (advanced theological school of the church), not a baptismal-catechesis school — i.e., an affirmation of an institutional school, not a denial. (Oliver 2015, https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2074-77052015000100019 ; BibSonomy record https://www.bibsonomy.org/bibtex/32db9ed5cda1ee349e36d423506b6d12 ; publisher listing https://www.antike-und-christentum.de/jbac/jbacBaende/content/799)

**Why this is substantial and not cosmetic:** It changes a sourcing conclusion and materially affects the scope argument. Note, importantly, that correcting it does **not** collapse the Section 1.2 "Contested" confidence rating — if anything it *supports* "Contested," because a genuine scholarly disagreement (van den Hoek/van den Broek: no early formal institution; Scholten + the traditional picture: a real institutional school) is exactly what "Contested" should denote. The fix is to represent the disagreement accurately, not to drop the rating.

**Recommended fix:** Rewrite the Scholten sentence to represent his actual position, e.g.: *"Clemens Scholten, by contrast, retains an institutional reading but reframes it: 'catechetical school' is a misnomer because the institution was not baptismal-catechesis but the church's advanced theological school (*theologische Hochschule*) — a position that itself illustrates that the institution's nature, not merely its existence, is unsettled."* Remove "likewise." Adjust Section 11 item 1 so Scholten is not cited as jointly supporting the anti-institution reading, and adjust the Section 12 Doc_02 handoff (which currently says only "confirm the Scholten (1995) citation") to also flag that Scholten's thesis must be represented on the correct side.

### 4. [COSMETIC] — Section 4 ("Institutional/social scope"): the "Era of the Martyrs" epoch is Diocletian's accession (284 CE), not the 303–313 persecution.

**Location:** Section 4, martyr-tradition sentence: "the Great Persecution under Diocletian, 303–313, fell heavily on Egypt and anchors Coptic identity and the Coptic calendar's 'Era of the Martyrs.'"

**What is wrong:** Nothing is factually mis-dated *as stated*, but the juxtaposition risks implying the Era of the Martyrs (Anno Martyrum) is reckoned from the 303–313 persecution. The Coptic calendar's epoch is conventionally **284 CE** (Diocletian's accession year), even though the era commemorates the martyrs of the later Diocletianic persecution. A reader could conflate the two.

**Recommended fix:** Add a half-clause of precision, e.g., "…the Coptic calendar's 'Era of the Martyrs' (conventionally reckoned from Diocletian's accession, 284 CE)." Minor; wording only.

### 5. [COSMETIC] — Section 3.3: Rubenson edition/subtitle precision.

**Location:** Section 3.3, Rubenson citation: *"Rubenson,* The Letters of St. Antony: Origenist Theology, Monastic Tradition and the Making of a Saint*, 1990/1995."*

**What is wrong:** The subtitle given is the **1990** Lund University Press edition's. The **1995** reissue (Fortress Press, Studies in Antiquity and Christianity) was retitled *The Letters of St. Antony: Monasticism and the Making of a Saint*. Pairing "1990/1995" with the 1990 subtitle is slightly imprecise for the later edition. The thesis itself is VERIFIED and accurately represented: Rubenson argues from the *Letters* that Antony was literate and philosophically (Origenist) formed, against the illiterate-holy-man portrait in Athanasius's *Life* — and the document correctly notes this is contested (e.g., Graham Gould).

**Evidence:** 1990 Lund edition (Church History review, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/church-history/article/abs/letters-of-st-antony-origenist-theology-monastic-tradition-and-the-making-of-a-saint-...); 1995 Fortress reissue with the altered subtitle (https://www.amazon.com/Letters-St-Antony-Monasticism-Christianity/dp/0800629108).

**Recommended fix:** Either cite the 1990 edition alone with its subtitle, or note the 1995 reissue carries the subtitle "Monasticism and the Making of a Saint." Optional; does not affect the argument.
