## Substantial findings

### F1 — [SUBSTANTIAL] §5 justifies "no standalone Material-Culture lens" by pointing to a lens the document does not contain, and to an element no lens treats
**Location:** §5 Proportionality of Lens Selection — "its material elements (Eucharist, baptism, the fast, the festal calendar) are ecologically *inside* the Worship, Formation-Logic, and Memory lenses rather than a separate axis."
**What's wrong:** Doc_07 contains no Worship lens. Its full lens inventory is 1A Affective, 1B Intellectual, 1C Authority, 1D Boundary, 1E Formation-Logic, 2A Memory, 2B Interpretive, 2C Reasoning-Patterns, and §3 Forces. "Worship" is a leftover reference to Doc_05's ecology structure (Doc_05 §3 Worship Ecology), not a lens in this document. Separately, of the four material elements named, only the Eucharist and the Paschal fast are actually housed in a lens (1E; also 1A), and the festal calendar in 2A (Festal-Letter/Paschal cycle). **Baptism is treated in no lens at all.** The Step-7 requirement here is that the choice *not* to run a standalone material-culture lens be argued as a proportionality finding rather than a silent omission (per the framing the document itself claims). The argument is made, but its evidentiary backing is partly fictional: it points to a container that isn't here and asserts coverage of an element that isn't covered.
**Evidence:** Full-document lens inventory (§1–§3); no "Worship" heading exists; "baptism" appears only in this §5 sentence, in no lens body.
**Fix:** Either (a) rewrite the sentence to name the lenses that actually carry these elements — Eucharist/fast in 1E Formation-Logic and 1A Affective; festal calendar in 2A Memory — and drop or relocate "baptism" (or add a one-line treatment of it where it belongs), or (b) if a Worship strand is genuinely intended to be one of the required lenses, add it. As written the proportionality justification is internally inconsistent.

### F2 — [SUBSTANTIAL] §4C's "seven (not six)" correction double-counts the Speculative–Doctrinal tension, so the corrected count is itself a miscount
**Location:** §4C Tensions the Reconstruction Must Preserve — "Seven tensions … (not six — correcting the source's miscount)," items **#2 "The Origen inheritance"** and **#5 "The speculative/doctrinal boundary."**
**What's wrong:** The document's own §1D says the internal Speculative-vs-Doctrinal boundary's "primary site [is] the Origen inheritance," and Doc_04 §3.6 defines Tensional gravity **T3** as "Speculative-Theological-Freedom vs Doctrinal-Boundary-Maintenance," with "the world's relationship to Origen … a world-level feature" of that same tension. So #2 (Origen inheritance) and #5 (speculative/doctrinal boundary) are one tension (T3) described at two grains — the general boundary and its paradigmatic instance. Counting both as distinct preserved tensions is what produces "seven"; the genuinely distinct set is **six** (T1 Teacher–Bishop, T2 Learning–Community/stratum non-integration, T3 Speculative–Doctrinal incl. the Origen inheritance, T4 Martyrdom–Contemplative, plus incompleteness-holding and transmission-fragility as world-level features). The draft foregrounds "seven not six" as a correction of the source, so re-introducing a double-count in that exact place is a self-inflicted consistency error — and it is precisely the count the review brief asks be verified. (Secondary note, same location: #6 "incompleteness of formation" is a posture and #7 "transmission fragility" is a vulnerability — neither is a two-pole tension in the Doc_04 sense; §4C's stated scope ["features … that must not be smoothed away"] permits them, but their inclusion under the word "tensions" further loosens the count.)
**Evidence:** §1D ("its primary site being the Origen inheritance"); Doc_04 §3.6 (T3 definition, Origen relationship as world-level feature of T3); §4C items #2 and #5.
**Fix:** Either merge #2 into #5 and carry the count as six (naming the Origen inheritance as T3's paradigmatic site, matching §1D and Doc_04 §3.6), or, if the paradigmatic instance is deliberately held as a separately-preserved tension, argue explicitly why an instance of T3 is a distinct preserved tension from T3 — and reconcile that with §1D. Do not assert "seven not six" as a bare correction while the seventh is a re-description of another item.

## Cosmetic findings

### F3 — [COSMETIC] §0.1 uses Documented-level language ("securely attested") for the early/mid horizon where Doc_04 carried the gravity cores at Widely Accepted
**Location:** §0.1 — "early/mid horizon securely attested through Clement and Origen."
**What's wrong:** "Securely attested" is the Article 17 permitted language for **Documented**. Doc_04 carries the Primary-gravity cores at **Widely Accepted**, not Documented. ### F4 — [COSMETIC] §1A carries the affective register at "Widely Accepted"
**Location:** §1A construction note — "the affective register is **Widely Accepted for the school tradition**."
**What's wrong:** Emotional/affective reconstruction is inherently more inferential than structural claims; "Widely Accepted" is a strong calibration for a felt register even when scoped to the school tradition. Defensible as "tone attested directly in" the corpora, but the calibration should distinguish directly-readable tone from reconstructed interior affect.
**Fix:** Either qualify as "the *tone* is Widely Accepted; the affective *reconstruction* is DMR," or carry the register at DMR.

### F5 — [COSMETIC] §1E header labels Formation-Logic "the deployment-critical lens," which sits against §0.5's "issues no deployment instruction"
**Location:** §1E header — "*(the deployment-critical lens: how formation actually works)*."

### F6 — [COSMETIC] §4B says "four visible effects" but the supporting parenthetical enumerates five
**Location:** §4B The Two Hubs — "producing **four visible effects at once** (interactions 3 and 4 above, plus the emotional and boundary inflections of 1A/1D)."

### F7 — [COSMETIC] §1A attributes the full late-horizon span "c. 296–400" to Athanasius alone
**Location:** §1A — "Late / post-Nicene (Athanasius, c. 296–400)."
**What's wrong:** Doc_04 §0 attributes the c. 296–400 late phase to **Athanasius and Didymus**; Athanasius died 373, so the span to 400 requires Didymus. §1B does cite Didymus, so this is a local dropout, not a scheme change.

### F8 — [COSMETIC] §1B carries an uncited cross-world superlative
**Location:** §1B — "the **most philosophically engaged early Christian formation world** of its first four centuries."
**What's wrong:** This is a comparative claim *across worlds*; the construction note tags the surrounding content "Widely Accepted for the school tradition," which scopes the internal character, not the cross-world superlative. Conventional in scholarship, but presented without confidence framing on the comparative claim itself.
**Fix:** Soften to a scoped claim ("among the most philosophically engaged…") or attach a confidence note to the comparative assertion.

### F9 — [COSMETIC] §4A.3 calls Teacher–Bishop and Learning–Community "the same structural reality … two angles," which sits awkwardly with §4C listing them as two distinct preserved tensions
**Location:** §4A.3 vs §4C #1 and #4.
