## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

John Chrysostom's own doctrinal writing is squarely, uncontroversially Nicene-Chalcedonian-orthodox on every point the surviving corpus speaks to — his Trinitarian and Christological positions were never themselves the subject of conciliar condemnation, in his own lifetime or after. **Corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding):** this document's first draft claimed the window "closes before the Nestorian controversy," which is not accurate — the controversy itself erupts in 428–429 (Nestorius's own preaching against the title *Theotokos*, soon after his 428 elevation to Constantinople) and runs through Cyril's own Twelve Anathemas (November 430), both inside this world's own 350–430 window; only the Council of Ephesus's own formal condemnation of Nestorius (431) falls one year past the window's own close. This does not change A1's own clearance for Chrysostom himself, who died in 407, well before any of this — **A1 clears directly** for Chrysostom and for the window's own Native corpus generally — but this document no longer claims the window was drawn to avoid the controversy; it simply ends, per the census's own stated dates, one year before Ephesus's own verdict.

**A boundary caution, named rather than smoothed over:** Theodoret of Cyrrhus, already carried on this world's corpus-map at `role: tradition`, sits at this world's own outer edge in a doctrinally live way, not merely a chronological one — he died c. 457 and wrote his *Ecclesiastical History* c. 449, after the Nestorian controversy. **Corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, HIGH finding):** this document's first draft claimed none of Theodoret's own carried material "is itself condemned content." This is false and independently re-verified against the vendored text: the corpus-map carries *The Anathemas of Cyril with the Counter-statements of Theodoret*, and the Second Council of Constantinople (553) — vendored, `npnf214` — explicitly anathematizes, by name, "the impious writings of Theodoret, directed against the true faith and against the first holy Synod of Ephesus and against St. Cyril and his XII. Anathemas" (canon XIII, `npnf214` line 23945, re-transcribed exactly this round after Round 2 review found the first citation had spliced in wording — "and his Twelve Chapters" — from a separate excursus passage nearby, line 23097, rather than quoting canon XIII itself verbatim). Theodoret was himself personally rehabilitated at Chalcedon (451, outside this window); the Counter-statements specifically, carried on this world's own corpus-map, were condemned by name a century later. This document also corrects the confidence claimed for Theodoret's own material (Round 1 review, M5): it is not uniformly `provisional` — the corpus-map carries his *Eranistes* and *Letters* at `confidence: assigned`, and only the *Ecclesiastical History* and the (now-confirmed condemned) Counter-statements at `provisional`. None of this resolves Theodoret's own fit question, which remains a live boundary question flagged forward to Doc_01/Doc_02, not decided here, per the dossier's own open item — but it must be flagged with the condemnation disclosed, not denied.

This document now states only what is independently, generally documented and confirmed directly against this corpus: the homilies exist, date to approximately September 386 per the `npnf109` apparatus (`~lines 22425–22490`, which quotes one sentence of one homily in translation), and are addressed, on their traditional title, to the relationship between the Antiochene church and the city's own Jewish community. These homilies are a documented, historically significant, and troubling part of Chrysostom's own corpus and later reception — including a documented history of being invoked in later antisemitic rhetoric, a reception history this world's own build must not repeat by handling the material uncritically, in either direction. **A further correction, this revision:** the first draft also claimed the corpus-map's own Ignatius-related note already used "Judaizing" in this technical sense — independently re-checked against the corpus-map file directly, no such note exists; the claim is removed, not repeated. **Confirmed directly against this corpus: the primary text of these homilies is not currently vendored anywhere in `cic/texts/`** — only the editorial apparatus above is present; no NPNF volume in this vendored set includes them (the 19th-century NPNF Chrysostom selection is documented to have omitted this material). This is named as a binding disclosure and acquisition obligation for Doc_02 (§4 below), not resolved here — this world's build must not proceed to characterize this material's specific content, tone, target, or theological argument beyond what is stated above until the primary text itself is vendored and can be read and quoted directly, per this project's own quote-verification discipline.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Exceptionally strong, confirmed directly against the library package rather than assumed from the census note. `cic/corpus-map/antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml` currently holds 47 work-rows (corrected this round from "46" — Ammianus was added after the original count), roughly 3.34 million words on disk across the six vendored NPNF volumes (`npnf109`–`npnf114`) — one of the largest single-figure holdings in this project's entire corpus, confirmed directly by the Source Readiness Dossier's own `wc -w` count (§1). Roughly 34 of those rows are Chrysostom's own voice at `role: tradition`, overwhelmingly `confidence: assigned` — continuous homiletic commentary on Matthew (the largest single series, roughly 466,000 words), John, Acts, Romans, the Corinthian and Pauline epistles, and Hebrews, together with the *Statues* homilies, the *De Sacerdotio*, and his exile correspondence.

The dossier's own §1 confirms this vendored set represents roughly 60–70% of his translatable corpus — missing the 67 Homilies on Genesis, the Psalms and Isaiah commentaries, and scattered freestanding moral homilies untranslated in NPNF, in addition to the *Adversus Judaeos* material named in §2 (A5) above. **Passes B1 comfortably**, with both the scale of what survives and the specific, named gaps in what's vendored carried forward as binding Doc_02 disclosures.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

The Step 0 Conclusion's own prior finding — "rich ordinary-lay ethical-formation material" — is, if anything, understated relative to what the vendored corpus actually shows. Chrysostom's homilies are not abstract doctrinal argument; they are sustained, practical moral exhortation addressed to an ordinary urban congregation's actual daily life — wealth and its use, family relationships, entertainment (the hippodrome, the theatre), civic behavior, almsgiving — genre after genre of it, across essentially the entire assigned corpus. This gives real, direct material to Formation Ecology and Meaning Transmission in a way this project's other elite-authored worlds (World #6, and `roman-church-gregorian`, both administrative/institutional in register) do not match. Worship Integration has real material (Chrysostom's own reputation and liturgical influence, and the *Statues* homilies' own occasion — a series of twenty-one sermons preached to a terrified congregation after the 387 riot, per the corpus-map's own note (corrected this revision from an unsupported "delivered daily through Lent" claim), a direct record of preaching as a communal, worship-embedded event). Organizational/Authority material is present but not this world's own center of gravity (contrast World #6): the *De Sacerdotio* treats priestly office theologically and pastorally, not primarily jurisdictionally, and the exile correspondence (to Innocent of Rome, to Olympias) evidences church-state conflict without this world's own build centering on it the way World #6's does. **Passes B2 comfortably**, arguably the strongest ecological fit of any candidate this document is aware of in the current portfolio for ordinary-believer formation material specifically.
