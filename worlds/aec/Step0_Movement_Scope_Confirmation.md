## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

John Chrysostom's own doctrinal writing is squarely, uncontroversially Nicene-Chalcedonian-orthodox on every point the surviving corpus speaks to — his Trinitarian and Christological positions were never themselves the subject of conciliar condemnation, in his own lifetime or after. This world's window (350–430) closes before the Nestorian controversy (which erupts after 428, when Nestorius — trained in the same Antiochene exegetical tradition, though not himself part of this world's own Native source base) is condemned at Ephesus (431), one year past this window's own close. **A1 clears directly** for the window and the figure this world actually centers on.

**A boundary caution, named rather than smoothed over:** Theodoret of Cyrrhus, already carried on this world's corpus-map at `role: tradition`, `confidence: provisional` (per the existing `cic/corpus-map/antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml`), sits at this world's own outer edge in a doctrinally live way, not merely a chronological one — he died c. 457 and wrote his *Ecclesiastical History* c. 449, after the Nestorian controversy the window's own 430 close is drawn to fall just short of. Theodoret was himself personally rehabilitated at Chalcedon (451, outside this window), but some of his specific anti-Cyrilline polemical writings were condemned over a century later, at the Second Council of Constantinople (553, also outside this window). This does not affect A1's own clearance (Theodoret was never doctrinally excluded in his own person on Chalcedonian terms, and the material carried here — his *Ecclesiastical History*, *Eranistes*, and letters — is not itself condemned content), but it is a live fit question this document does not resolve, flagged forward to Doc_01/Doc_02 rather than decided here, consistent with the dossier's own open item on this point.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

Quoted directly from the Methodology: *"A movement's historical rivals... are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under this section... they appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates."*

Chrysostom's own surviving corpus names several rivals, each routed here rather than separately screened:

- **The Anomoeans (Eunomians).** Confirmed directly against the corpus-map's own existing entry: Chrysostom's *Homily on the Paralytic* is there because it "proves the equality of Father and Son against the Anomoeans," and the corpus-map's own note states plainly that "Anomoean-Eunomian Christianity is now on the census for exactly the party" this material opposes — a separate, already-identified census candidate, not re-screened here. Per A5, this world's own material about the Anomoeans stays here, as Chrysostom's own opponents in his own reconstruction; the Anomoeans' own eligibility, if ever pursued, is that separate census entry's own question.
- **Marcionites and Manichaeans.** Chrysostom's own *Homily Against Marcionists and Manichaeans* (already assigned, corpus-map row) is, per that row's own note, "material about the two doctrinal-floor movements it argues against" — both fail Article 4's floor outright on their own confessions (Marcion's rejection of the Hebrew scriptures and denial of Christ's real humanity; Manichaeism's dualist cosmology), so neither is a live A1 question of its own; they appear here only as this world's own named opponents.
- **Jewish communities of Antioch, and "Judaizing" Christians.** This is not a rival Christian movement and is not routed by A5 at all — it is a real, serious, and separate disclosure question this document names here rather than deferring silently, because of its own ethical weight, not because A5 assigns it here mechanically. Chrysostom delivered a set of eight homilies at Antioch (386–387), commonly known by their traditional Latin title *Adversus Judaeos* ("Against the Jews"), harshly polemical against Christians who continued to observe Jewish festivals and practices alongside, or in place of, specifically Christian ones ("Judaizing" in the technical sense the corpus-map's own existing Ignatius-related note already uses correctly). These homilies are a documented, historically significant, and troubling part of Chrysostom's own corpus and later reception — including a documented history of being invoked in later antisemitic rhetoric, a reception history this world's own build must not repeat by handling the material uncritically. **Confirmed directly against this corpus: the primary text of these eight homilies is not currently vendored anywhere in `cic/texts/`** — only editorial apparatus discussing their existence and approximate date (`npnf109`, lines 1764 and 22425–22490) is present; no NPNF volume in this vendored set includes them (the 19th-century NPNF Chrysostom selection is documented to have omitted this material). This is named as a binding disclosure and acquisition obligation for Doc_02 (§4 below), not resolved here — this world's build must not proceed to characterize this material's specific content, tone, or theological argument beyond what is independently, generally documented (its existence, approximate date, and general subject, all confirmed above) until the primary text itself is vendored and can be read and quoted directly, per this project's own quote-verification discipline.

Strand Determination (Article 21) is Step 1's own work, flagged forward to Doc_01, not resolved here.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Exceptionally strong, confirmed directly against the library package rather than assumed from the census note. `cic/corpus-map/antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml` currently holds 46 work-rows, roughly 3.34 million words on disk across the six vendored NPNF volumes (`npnf109`–`npnf114`) — one of the largest single-figure holdings in this project's entire corpus, confirmed directly by the Source Readiness Dossier's own `wc -w` count (§1). Roughly 34 of those rows are Chrysostom's own voice at `role: tradition`, overwhelmingly `confidence: assigned` — continuous homiletic commentary on Matthew (the largest single series, roughly 466,000 words), John, Acts, Romans, the Corinthian and Pauline epistles, and Hebrews, together with the *Statues* homilies, the *De Sacerdotio*, and his exile correspondence.

Named honestly rather than left implicit: this is not the whole of Chrysostom's authentic output. The dossier's own §1 confirms this vendored set represents roughly 60–70% of his translatable corpus — missing the 67 Homilies on Genesis, the Psalms and Isaiah commentaries, and scattered freestanding moral homilies untranslated in NPNF, in addition to the *Adversus Judaeos* material named in §2 (A5) above. **Passes B1 comfortably**, with both the scale of what survives and the specific, named gaps in what's vendored carried forward as binding Doc_02 disclosures.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

The Step 0 Conclusion's own prior finding — "rich ordinary-lay ethical-formation material" — is, if anything, understated relative to what the vendored corpus actually shows. Chrysostom's homilies are not abstract doctrinal argument; they are sustained, practical moral exhortation addressed to an ordinary urban congregation's actual daily life — wealth and its use, family relationships, entertainment (the hippodrome, the theatre), civic behavior, almsgiving — genre after genre of it, across essentially the entire assigned corpus. This gives real, direct material to Formation Ecology and Meaning Transmission in a way this project's other elite-authored worlds (World #6, and `roman-church-gregorian`, both administrative/institutional in register) do not match. Worship Integration has real material (Chrysostom's own reputation and liturgical influence, and the *Statues* homilies' own occasion — sermons delivered daily through Lent 387 to a terrified congregation, a direct record of preaching as a communal, worship-embedded event). Organizational/Authority material is present but not this world's own center of gravity (contrast World #6): the *De Sacerdotio* treats priestly office theologically and pastorally, not primarily jurisdictionally, and the exile correspondence (to Innocent of Rome, to Olympias) evidences church-state conflict without this world's own build centering on it the way World #6's does. **Passes B2 comfortably**, arguably the strongest ecological fit of any candidate this document is aware of in the current portfolio for ordinary-believer formation material specifically.

### B3 — Uniqueness (differentiation) — the condition this document must actually resolve

This is where the Step 0 Conclusion's own deferral bites, and this section does the actual work the prior cycle left undone.

**The required primary-gravity contrast against World #8, stated on its own terms, not on geography or language.** World #8's own defining gravity, quoted directly in §0 above, is institutional-pastoral: authority exercised "through office, sacrament, and territorial care of an entire flock" — Cyprian navigating the practical crisis of the lapsed and the unity of the episcopate under persecution; Augustine's ordinary sacramental administration, catechesis, and doctrinal controversy (anti-Manichaean, anti-Donatist, anti-Pelagian) for his own congregation at Hippo. The office itself — what a bishop institutionally is, and does, for a flock under his territorial care — is World #8's own organizing subject.

This world's own gravity, evidenced directly by the shape of its own surviving corpus (§B1 above, and confirmed directly against the corpus-map's own rows), is a different thing: **continuous, verse-by-verse scriptural exposition, read in its plain historical and grammatical sense, as the primary mechanism of ethical formation** — not administration of the office, but the specific interpretive method by which ordinary believers are formed. This is the scholarly distinction the Step 0 Conclusion's own text already gestures at ("a historical-literal exegetical mode distinct from Alexandria's allegorical method") but did not yet test against the actually relevant comparandum, World #8, rather than Alexandria. The contrast holds independent of geography or language: even set beside a Latin-speaking, North African congregation under an equally learned, equally pastorally serious bishop, Chrysostom's own homiletic corpus is organized, almost without exception, around expounding a scriptural text verse by verse and drawing an ethical conclusion from its literal sense — a compositional shape essentially absent from Cyprian's own occasional, crisis-driven treatises (*On the Lapsed*, *On the Unity of the Church*) and present in Augustine only as one genre among several dominated, in his own most characteristic and influential work, by doctrinal-controversial argument (anti-Manichaean, anti-Donatist, anti-Pelagian) and introspective theological narrative (the *Confessions*) rather than sustained, serial scriptural exposition as the primary vehicle. This distinction between Antiochene historical-grammatical exegesis and other patristic interpretive modes is well-established in modern scholarship (Frances Young, *Biblical Exegesis and the Formation of Christian Culture*, Cambridge University Press, 1997, Confidence B, not independently re-read this session — cited for this specific, well-known point of scholarly consensus) and is not this document's own novel claim; what this document adds is testing it specifically against World #8's own stated gravity, which the prior cycle named as the missing step.

**This is a genuine construction finding, offered here for the first time against the actual comparandum, not decided at any earlier level** — flagged as new work this Step 0 pass performs, per §0's own framing, not merely restated from the deferred entry.

- **Versus World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, c. 312–451, built):** no meaningful overlap — World #6's gravity is juridical-institutional primacy-consolidation; this world's is exegetical-formational. Real, minor cross-link content exists (Chrysostom's own exile correspondence with Innocent of Rome, already correctly carried as a second `tradition` id on this world's own corpus-map rows per the "second id" pattern already documented there) without creating a boundary risk.
- **Versus `roman-church-gregorian` (this same handoff, not yet admitted):** no overlap — different centuries, regions, and languages, and, more substantively, different gravities (administrative-pastoral necessity there; exegetical-formational method here).
- **Versus the Antiochene school's other pillars (Diodore of Tarsus, Theodore of Mopsuestia):** genuinely adjacent but not competing, since neither is currently includable — the Source Readiness Dossier (§4) confirms no public-domain English translation path exists for either figure's own most relevant work. This world is honestly "Chrysostom-centered," not a full "Antiochene school" reconstruction, a scoping limitation named here and carried forward as binding on Doc_01, not smoothed over by the world's own name.

**Passes B3, on the specific ground the Step 0 Conclusion required.**

A modest, non-decisive B5 factor.
