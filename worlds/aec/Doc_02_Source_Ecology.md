## 1. Primary Sources

This world's own corpus-map bucket, `cic/corpus-map/antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml`, holds 47 work-rows assembled by a prior source-research pass, with real, individually-documented verification work behind most of them (e.g. the Ammianus row's own directly-quoted, locus-cited confirmation). This document uses that work rather than re-deriving it, and adds its own fresh verification specifically where Doc_02 draws a claim vivid or specific enough to require it. Full row-by-row detail (individual loci, notes, provenance) lives in the corpus-map file itself and in `Source_Registry.md`; this section narrates the ecology, not a duplicate listing.

**Chrysostom's own continuous exegetical-homiletic corpus** — the overwhelming bulk of this world's Native holdings, spanning `npnf109` through `npnf114`: complete or substantially complete homily series on Matthew (the largest single series, ~466,000 words), John, Acts, Romans, First and Second Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, First and Second Thessalonians, First and Second Timothy, Titus, Philemon, and Hebrews — seventeen series rows on the corpus-map (§8 below). Two representative loci are independently verified this session: the *Homily to Those Who Had Not Attended the Assembly* (on Romans 12:20, `npnf109`, locus `xiii.ii-p31`), a genuine, verified example of Chrysostom's own close-reading method (working through "by so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head" as an apparent difficulty, then applying it to conduct) — though a standalone `npnf109` sermon, not part of the seventeen serial commentary rows (`npnf110`–`114`) the primary-gravity argument actually rests on — and, directly against the Matthew series, Homily II on Matthew (`npnf110`, locus `iii.v-p2`–`p3`), which opens by quoting its own verse directly — *"Matt. I. 1... 'The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham'"* — and proceeds from it, confirming the same verse-anchored compositional method at the level of an actual serial commentary.

**The Homilies on the Statues** (`npnf109`, div1 19, twenty-one homilies). This world's own most direct record of preaching meeting acute civic crisis. Not independently re-verified at a specific locus this session beyond the corpus-map's own existing confirmation; named here as Native and load-bearing regardless (Confidence B, per the Registry).

**The two Homilies on Eutropius** (`npnf109`, div1 15). Directly re-verified this session, word for word, at locus `xv.iii-p5`: *"'Vanity of vanities, all is vanity'—it is always seasonable to utter this but more especially at the present time. Where are now the brilliant surroundings of thy consulship? where are the gleaming torches?... They were all mere visions of the night, and dreams which have vanished with the dawn of day."* Preached over the fallen minister Eutropius, who had taken refuge at the altar — this is simultaneously this world's own best directly-verified evidence of Chrysostom's homiletic method applied to a real, named, socially maximal case, and material this world already correctly shares with `imperial-juridical-christianity` (church asylum against imperial power), per the corpus-map's own existing "second id" note.

**The *De Sacerdotio* (Treatise Concerning the Christian Priesthood)** (`npnf109`, div1 4, six books). This world's own direct, theological treatment of pastoral office — distinguished in Doc_01 §3 from World #6's own institutional-jurisdictional register: this text treats priesthood pastorally and spiritually, not primarily as a jurisdictional claim.

**The exile correspondence** (Letters to Olympias; Letters to Innocent of Rome; the Innocent replies) — already correctly carried, per the corpus-map's own "second id" pattern, as material shared with `imperial-juridical-christianity`, evidencing the deposition-and-exile conflict with the imperial court without this world's own build centering on that conflict institutionally.

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus's four works** (`npnf203`) — carried on the existing corpus-map at a mix of `assigned` (*Eranistes*, *Letters*) and `provisional` (*Ecclesiastical History*, Counter-statements) confidence, per Doc_01 §4's own per-work boundary finding, **by subject matter, not by a work-level date:** the *Ecclesiastical History* (323–428) is genuinely Native to this world's own 350–430 window, read as later-historian testimony alongside Socrates and Sozomen, not as a second instance of Chrysostom's own core exegetical-homiletic voice; the *Eranistes* (c. 447) and the bulk of the 181 Letters (the corpus-map's own note: pastoral administration, the Eutychian controversy, appeals to Leo) address subject matter outside this world's own window and are not treated here as Native; the Counter-statements to Cyril's Anathemas (430–431), together with the Letters' own run of Nestorian-controversy correspondence (430–431, e.g. John of Antioch's letter to Nestorius and Theodoret's own letters from Ephesus, independently verified present in the vendored file), sit at the window's own edge by composition date though their subject falls inside it, and the Counter-statements specifically are, per Step 0 §2 (A1), condemned material at the Second Council of Constantinople (553) — a live edge case, not a settled inclusion. This document does not use any Theodoret material to support a specific claim; the corpus-map's own existing confidence tags for the *Eranistes* and Letters specifically should be revisited against this finding by whoever next touches this world's own corpus-map assignments, named here as an open item (§9) rather than silently corrected in a file this document does not itself edit.

**Context/hostile-witness material**, already correctly held as `context` rather than `tradition` on the existing corpus-map: Socrates Scholasticus and Sozomen's ecclesiastical histories (Chrysostom's episcopate and fall, `npnf202`); Cassian's *De Incarnatione* (an opponent's testimony to Antiochene Christology, `npnf211`); Cyril of Alexandria's anti-Nestorian letters and the Ephesus (431) and Second Constantinople (553) conciliar acts (`npnf214`); Ammianus Marcellinus's eyewitness account of Julian at Antioch (`ammianus-marcellinus_roman-history_yonge1862`, added 2026-09-25, already directly quote-verified by the prior research pass). None of these requires fresh verification by this document — they are context, not Native voice, and their existing verification is sound.

**The *Apostolic Constitutions*** (`anf07`) — carried at `confidence: provisional`, same city and generation as Chrysostom but a different genre (church-order/liturgical compilation); this document does not elevate it beyond the corpus-map's own already-careful provisional status.

## 2. Author Gravity Assessment

**John Chrysostom.**
- *Visibility:* Extremely high, and across two distinct settings (Antiochene presbyter; Constantinopolitan bishop and exile) — a genuine advantage over a single-office-holder world like `roman-church-gregorian`, since the two settings let this world's own account of his consistency (Doc_01 §4) be checked against two different sets of circumstances, not asserted from one alone.
- *Representativeness:* Chrysostom is not a representative voice for an ordinary Antiochene layperson's own experience — he is the preacher addressing them, not one of them. His own family background is verified directly against the NPNF introduction itself (`npnf109`, locus `iii.ii-p9`): *"His father, Secundus, was a distinguished military officer (magister militum) in the imperial army of Syria, and died..."* His own social position — a senior military officer's son, classically educated under Libanius — is itself elite, even though his subject matter is unusually attentive to ordinary and poor believers' lives by this project's own comparative standard (Doc_01 §3).
- *Influence:* Enormous, among the largest of any figure in this project's portfolio — his homiletic corpus becomes a model for later Christian preaching across multiple traditions, well beyond this world's own window.
- *Limitations:* A preacher's own account of his listeners, their failings, and his own conflicts (with Eudoxia, with the Antiochene or Constantinopolitan authorities) is one side of each dispute — no independent corroborating voice for the Eudoxia conflict specifically is currently part of this world's Native holdings (Socrates and Sozomen, the nearest available check, are `context`, not independent primary testimony from the other side). His own celebrated rhetorical skill is itself a limitation worth naming plainly: a gifted preacher's own framing of a conflict is not thereby a neutral account of it.
- *Transmission History:* Unusually complete for a figure of this period (Step 0 §3, B1) — the scale itself (roughly 3.3 million words on disk) is itself a transmission-history fact worth naming, likely reflecting both his own enormous contemporary reputation and centuries of sustained later interest in preserving him, a continuity with this project's other worlds' recurring finding that institutional prestige and later reputation shape what survives (Doc_01 §5, Cell 2B).

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus.** Given the per-work boundary finding now established (Doc_01 §4, §1 above) — most of his own carried material either out-of-window by date or a live edge case — a full five-dimension assessment is deferred rather than performed prematurely for material that is, on the current finding, largely not this world's own to begin with. If the *Ecclesiastical History* specifically is confirmed Native at a future step, its own Author Gravity Assessment (as a later-historian narrative source, alongside Socrates and Sozomen) is owed at that point. Flagged as an open item (§9 below) rather than skipped silently.

## 3. Secondary Scholarship Assessment

- **Frances Young, *Biblical Exegesis and the Formation of Christian Culture* (Cambridge University Press, 1997).** Confidence C, flagged for priority second-opinion review: an independent-review caution, unverified but credible, that Young's actual thesis argues *against* a simple literal-versus-allegorical, "historical-grammatical" characterization of Antiochene exegesis, recasting the Antiochene/Alexandrian difference in more technical terms (rhetorical versus philosophical reading; on some accounts, "ikonic" versus "symbolic" mimesis), and that the label itself is contested in the wider scholarship (O'Keefe and others). This document does not resolve which characterization is correct — the book was never independently re-read this session — and does not cite Young as settled support for a claim it may substantially complicate. **The underlying claim (Doc_01 §1, Step 0 §3 B3) rests on the ancient description of Diodore of Tarsus's own method, not on this secondary source alone:** Socrates' own *Ecclesiastical History* (`npnf202`, `ii.ix.iv-p2`) describes Diodore of Tarsus's method, not Chrysostom's own — Doc_01 §2 itself states Chrysostom's own link to Diodore is Widely Accepted but not independently verified against a primary text naming him by name. Socrates' own actual line, describing Diodore directly, reads: "he limited his attention to the literal sense of scripture, avoiding that which was mystical" (`ii.ix.iv-p2`) — distinct from the specific "literal and historical meaning... in preference to the allegorical and mystical interpretations... of Origen and the Alexandrians" wording, which is the NPNF volume's own 19th-century endnote, not Socrates' own ancient words. Licensed for: naming that a scholarly conversation about how to characterize Antiochene exegesis exists — not for the specific literal-vs-allegorical dichotomy as settled fact.
- **J.N.D. Kelly, *Golden Mouth: The Story of John Chrysostom* (Cornell University Press, 1995).** Confidence B — a standard modern biography, not independently re-read this session. Licensed for: general biographical framing (formation under Libanius, the arc from Antioch to Constantinople to exile) — not for any single specific vivid claim on its own.

## 4. Formation Narrative Sources

- **The Statues homilies themselves** (§1 above) function as formation narrative in their own right, not merely as primary-source data about Chrysostom — a real-time record of a community's own fear and the preaching addressed to it, directly relevant to a future Doc_09.
- **Palladius's *Dialogue Concerning the Life of Chrysostom*** — a contemporary, sympathetic eyewitness account of Chrysostom's fall and exile, the specific kind of formation-narrative material this world's own holdings currently lack. **Not vendored.** Confirmed as a verified, not-yet-downloaded acquisition lead already logged in `worlds/_cross-world/download-queue-seed.yaml` (verified 2026-09-13; re-confirmed present in that file, status `not-yet-downloaded`, this session). Named here as an open acquisition, not treated as available.
- **The *Adversus Judaeos* homilies.** Per Step 0 §2 (A5) and §4.2, and Doc_01 §7 item 1: **not vendored anywhere in this corpus.** This document does not characterize their content, tone, target, or argument beyond what is independently, generally documented and confirmed directly against this corpus's own apparatus (`npnf109`, `xix.ii-p12`, which dates "a certain discourse" to September 386 and itself discusses a Paschal-table dating difficulty) and Socrates' own separate mention of an earlier work by this title (Step 0 §2, A5). **No public-domain translation search has been attempted this session.** The standard modern scholarly translation (Paul W. Harkins, *Discourses Against Judaizing Christians*, Fathers of the Church series, Catholic University of America Press, 1979) is well within copyright and is not a candidate. This is this world's single most consequential source gap, both because the census's own `documentedStories` field for this world names this material as one of only two documented stories, and because of the material's own serious ethical weight — it cannot be responsibly treated, in either direction, without the primary text in hand.

## 6. Source Asymmetries and Missing Voices

Per Step 0 §4 (binding on this document):

- **Elite, clerical, male authorship dominates this world's own Native voice (Article 20)**, though less totally than `roman-church-gregorian`'s single-author concentration — Chrysostom's own corpus is vast but still one man's. Theodoret does not add even a provisional second voice to this world's own Native corpus specifically — his *Ecclesiastical History* is Native but context-like (a later-historian witness, not this world's own exegetical-homiletic mode), the *Eranistes* and the bulk of the Letters are out-of-boundary by subject, and the Counter-statements (with the Letters' own Nestorian-controversy correspondence) are a live, condemned-content edge case, not a settled second voice (§1, §2 above; Doc_01 §4). Unlike World #6 or `roman-church-gregorian`, this world's own *subject matter* — ordinary believers' wealth, conduct, and family life — is unusually attentive to non-elite experience even though its *authorship* is not; this distinction, between whose life is described and whose voice describes it, is named here explicitly rather than left to blur together.
- **Gender (Article 20):** Olympias, the deaconess to whom much of Chrysostom's exile correspondence is addressed, is a documented, named figure of real substance in this world's own record — but her own letters back to him do not survive at all, and her own voice, on her own terms, is not independently attested anywhere. **Olympias is corroborated by a second author**, Palladius, in his own *Lausiac History* (`palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt`, Chapter LVI, "Olympias," verified directly against the vendored text), which describes her as "that most venerable and devoted lady," names her descent (daughter of Seleucus the ex-count, granddaughter of Ablavius the ex-prefect), and records that she "dispersed all her goods and gave to the poor," "instructed many women, addressed priests reverently, and honoured bishops." **Palladius is not independent of Chrysostom's own circle:** the same vendored text states directly that Palladius himself was "embroiled in the disturbance connected with the blessed John" and traveled "to Rome because of the blessed bishop John" — he is himself a partisan of Chrysostom, in the same controversy, not an outside or neutral witness. What this corroborates is that Olympias is a real, substantial figure attested by more than one author from within Chrysostom's own circle — not independent attestation from outside it. It is still not her own voice, on her own terms, either way: it is Palladius's own praise of her, in the same register as Chrysostom's, not a surviving word from Olympias herself. The absence named above stands. A separate chapter of the same work (Chapter XLI, "Holy Women," fifteen chapters before the Olympias chapter) separately names, in a single clause among a list of women Palladius says he knew, "the deaconess Sabaniana, aunt of John the bishop of Constantinople" — a distinct Antiochene deaconess, independently attested, whose own further connection to this world (if any beyond that one clause) is not established by this document and is named as a possible future research lead, not developed further here. Named as a real, undischarged absence per the Affirmative Duty's primary duty; the Framework's three bounding conditions for the secondary (reconstruction) prong are not currently met for any specific case.
- **Antioch's Jewish community (Article 23, not Article 20 — the same routing this project's other worlds use for an anathematized or opposed party rather than an internal marginalized voice).** This world's record of Antiochene Jewish life and practice survives, so far as this world's own currently-vendored holdings show, only through the *lens* of Chrysostom's own hostility to Judaizing practice — and even that lens is not currently readable directly, since the *Adversus Judaeos* text itself is unvendored (§4 above). This is a doubled absence, worth naming as such rather than folded into a generic disclosure: not only is the Jewish community's own voice absent, but so, currently, is even the hostile Christian voice that would at least evidence their presence and the anxiety they provoked. Both are binding gaps for future construction work, not resolved here.
- **The Marcionites/Manichaeans (Article 23)** — this world's record of both survives only through Chrysostom's own polemical framing (Step 0 §2, A5); no independent source for either is currently part of this world's holdings.
- **The Anomoeans/Eunomians (Article 23).** Confirmed directly against `cic/texts/`: Eunomius's own *First Apology* (`eunomius_first-apology_whiston1711.txt`) and Philostorgius's *Ecclesiastical History* (`philostorgius_ecclesiastical-history_walford1855.txt`, an Anomoean-sympathetic account transmitted via Photius's epitome) are both vendored in this corpus, though neither is currently assigned to this world's own corpus-map bucket. Named here as own-voice acquisition/assignment candidates for the Anomoeans' own side specifically, per this project's own `CORPUS-USE.md` tier method — not resolved or assigned by this document.

**Affirmative Duty (Article 20), applied:** the primary duty is discharged throughout this section. The secondary duty (bounded reconstruction) is not exercised — no case currently meets the Framework's three bounding conditions. Re-check at Doc_05/Doc_07.

## 7. Confidence Map

- **Documented / Widely Accepted:** Chrysostom's own dates and career arc (ordination 386, Constantinopolitan episcopate 397–404, exile 404–407, death 407); the Statues crisis (387) and its resolution; the Eutropius affair; the existence and basic content of the major primary works named in §1.
- **Dominant Modern Reconstruction:** the reading of Chrysostom's own Constantinopolitan conflicts as continuous with, rather than a departure from, his Antiochene homiletic mode (Doc_01 §4). The Antiochene historical-grammatical / Alexandrian allegorical distinction is Contested pending the second-opinion review flagged in §3 above — the ancient description of Diodore of Tarsus's own method (Socrates VI.3, `ii.ix.iv-p2`, Doc_01 §2 — a description of Diodore, not a Chrysostom self-description) remains Documented, but the modern scholarly characterization built on top of it does not.
- **Contested:** the edge-of-window fit specifically of Theodoret's Counter-statements and the Letters' own Nestorian-controversy correspondence (Doc_01 §4 — the *Ecclesiastical History*'s own Native status, and the *Eranistes*'/bulk-Letters' own exclusion, are resolved findings, not contested); the precise degree to which any single Statues or Eutropius homily's surviving text reflects verbatim delivery versus later editorial polish — not independently investigated this session, named as an open question rather than assumed either way.
- **Inferential/Thin:** any claim about an ordinary Antiochene layperson's own experience beyond what Chrysostom's own preaching describes (§6); any claim about Olympias's own perspective beyond what Chrysostom's letters to her state; any claim whatsoever about the content of the *Adversus Judaeos* homilies (§4, §6) — this document makes none, and any future document that does must ground it in the vendored primary text, not general reputation.
