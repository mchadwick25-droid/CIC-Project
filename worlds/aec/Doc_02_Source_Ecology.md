Directly confirmed by this document, this session, at one representative locus rather than assumed from the corpus-map's own description alone: the *Homily to Those Who Had Not Attended the Assembly* (on Romans 12:20), verified at locus `xiii.ii-p31` in `npnf109`, shows Chrysostom's own characteristic method exactly as Doc_01 §1 describes it — close attention to a specific verse's own wording ("by so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head"), worked through as an apparent difficulty and resolved, then applied directly to the hearer's own conduct toward an enemy. This is the compositional shape underwriting Step 0 §3 (B3)'s entire primary-gravity argument, spot-checked directly rather than only asserted from the corpus-map's row count.

**The Homilies on the Statues** (`npnf109`, div1 19, twenty-one homilies). This world's own most direct record of preaching meeting acute civic crisis. Not independently re-verified at a specific locus this session beyond the corpus-map's own existing confirmation; named here as Native and load-bearing regardless (Confidence B, per the Registry).

**The two Homilies on Eutropius** (`npnf109`, div1 15). Directly re-verified this session, word for word, at locus `xv.iii-p5`: *"'Vanity of vanities, all is vanity'—it is always seasonable to utter this but more especially at the present time. ## 2. Author Gravity Assessment

**John Chrysostom.**
- *Visibility:* Extremely high, and across two distinct settings (Antiochene presbyter; Constantinopolitan bishop and exile) — a genuine advantage over a single-office-holder world like `roman-church-gregorian`, since the two settings let this world's own account of his consistency (Doc_01 §4) be checked against two different sets of circumstances, not asserted from one alone.
- *Representativeness:* Chrysostom is not a representative voice for an ordinary Antiochene layperson's own experience — he is the preacher addressing them, not one of them. His own social position (a decurion-class family, classically educated under Libanius) is itself elite, even though his subject matter is unusually attentive to ordinary and poor believers' lives by this project's own comparative standard (Doc_01 §3).
- *Influence:* Enormous, among the largest of any figure in this project's portfolio — his homiletic corpus becomes a model for later Christian preaching across multiple traditions, well beyond this world's own window.
- *Limitations:* A preacher's own account of his listeners, their failings, and his own conflicts (with Eudoxia, with the Antiochene or Constantinopolitan authorities) is one side of each dispute — no independent corroborating voice for the Eudoxia conflict specifically is currently part of this world's Native holdings (Socrates and Sozomen, the nearest available check, are `context`, not independent primary testimony from the other side). His own celebrated rhetorical skill is itself a limitation worth naming plainly: a gifted preacher's own framing of a conflict is not thereby a neutral account of it.
- *Transmission History:* Unusually complete for a figure of this period (Step 0 §3, B1) — the scale itself (roughly 3.3 million words on disk) is itself a transmission-history fact worth naming, likely reflecting both his own enormous contemporary reputation and centuries of sustained later interest in preserving him, a continuity with this project's other worlds' recurring finding that institutional prestige and later reputation shape what survives (Doc_01 §5, Cell 2B).

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus.** Given his own provisional, unresolved fit (Doc_01 §4), a full five-dimension assessment is deferred rather than performed prematurely here — assigning binding Author Gravity findings to a voice whose own strand/inclusion status is still open would risk exactly the kind of unforced commitment Doc_01 §4 explicitly declines to make. Flagged as an open item (§9 below) rather than skipped silently.

## 6. Source Asymmetries and Missing Voices

Per Step 0 §4 (binding on this document):

- **Elite, clerical, male authorship dominates this world's own Native voice (Article 20)**, though less totally than `roman-church-gregorian`'s single-author concentration — Chrysostom's own corpus is vast but still one man's; Theodoret adds a second voice only provisionally (§1, §2 above). Unlike World #6 or `roman-church-gregorian`, this world's own *subject matter* — ordinary believers' wealth, conduct, and family life — is unusually attentive to non-elite experience even though its *authorship* is not; this distinction, between whose life is described and whose voice describes it, is named here explicitly rather than left to blur together.
- **Gender (Article 20):** Olympias, the deaconess to whom much of Chrysostom's exile correspondence is addressed, is a documented, named figure of real substance in this world's own record — but her own letters back to him are not currently part of this world's Native holdings (only Chrysostom's side of the correspondence is vendored), and her own voice, on her own terms, is not independently attested here. Named as a real, undischarged absence per the Affirmative Duty's primary duty; the Framework's three bounding conditions for the secondary (reconstruction) prong are not currently met for any specific case.
- **Antioch's Jewish community (Article 23, not Article 20 — the same routing this project's other worlds use for an anathematized or opposed party rather than an internal marginalized voice).** This world's record of Antiochene Jewish life and practice survives, so far as this world's own currently-vendored holdings show, only through the *lens* of Chrysostom's own hostility to Judaizing practice — and even that lens is not currently readable directly, since the *Adversus Judaeos* text itself is unvendored (§4 above). This is a doubled absence, worth naming as such rather than folded into a generic disclosure: not only is the Jewish community's own voice absent, but so, currently, is even the hostile Christian voice that would at least evidence their presence and the anxiety they provoked. Both are binding gaps for future construction work, not resolved here.
- **The Anomoeans/Eunomians and the Marcionites/Manichaeans (Article 23)** — this world's record of each survives only through Chrysostom's own polemical framing (Step 0 §2, A5); no independent source for any of the three is currently part of this world's holdings.
