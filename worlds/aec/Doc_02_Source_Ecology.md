## 1. Primary Sources

This world's own corpus-map bucket, `cic/corpus-map/antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml`, holds 47 work-rows assembled by a prior source-research pass (corrected this revision from "46" — Ammianus was added after the original count), with real, individually-documented verification work behind most of them (e.g. the Ammianus row's own directly-quoted, locus-cited confirmation). This document uses that work rather than re-deriving it, and adds its own fresh verification specifically where Doc_02 draws a claim vivid or specific enough to require it. Full row-by-row detail (individual loci, notes, provenance) lives in the corpus-map file itself and in `Source_Registry.md`; this section narrates the ecology, not a duplicate listing.

**Chrysostom's own continuous exegetical-homiletic corpus** — the overwhelming bulk of this world's Native holdings, spanning `npnf109` through `npnf114`: complete or substantially complete homily series on Matthew (the largest single series, ~466,000 words), John, Acts, Romans, First and Second Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, First and Second Thessalonians, First and Second Timothy, Titus, Philemon, and Hebrews — seventeen series rows on the corpus-map, not twenty (corrected this revision, §8 below). **A representativeness caution, added this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding):** this document's first draft verified only the *Homily to Those Who Had Not Attended the Assembly* (on Romans 12:20, `npnf109`, locus `xiii.ii-p31`) as its own spot-check for this claim — but that homily is a standalone `npnf109` sermon, not part of any of the seventeen serial commentary rows (`npnf110`–`114`) the primary-gravity argument actually rests on. It remains a genuine, verified example of Chrysostom's own close-reading method (working through "by so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head" as an apparent difficulty, then applying it to conduct), but this document does not claim it stands in for the serial commentaries specifically. **A second spot-check, directly against the Matthew series, closes that gap:** Homily II on Matthew (`npnf110`, locus `iii.v-p2`–`p3`), verified word for word, opens by quoting its own verse directly — *"Matt. I. 1... 'The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham'"* — and proceeds from it, confirming the same verse-anchored compositional method at the level of an actual serial commentary, not only the standalone homily.

**The Homilies on the Statues** (`npnf109`, div1 19, twenty-one homilies). This world's own most direct record of preaching meeting acute civic crisis. Not independently re-verified at a specific locus this session beyond the corpus-map's own existing confirmation; named here as Native and load-bearing regardless (Confidence B, per the Registry).

**The two Homilies on Eutropius** (`npnf109`, div1 15). Directly re-verified this session, word for word, at locus `xv.iii-p5`: *"'Vanity of vanities, all is vanity'—it is always seasonable to utter this but more especially at the present time. Where are now the brilliant surroundings of thy consulship? where are the gleaming torches?... They were all mere visions of the night, and dreams which have vanished with the dawn of day."* Preached over the fallen minister Eutropius, who had taken refuge at the altar — this is simultaneously this world's own best directly-verified evidence of Chrysostom's homiletic method applied to a real, named, socially maximal case, and material this world already correctly shares with `imperial-juridical-christianity` (church asylum against imperial power), per the corpus-map's own existing "second id" note.

**The *De Sacerdotio* (Treatise Concerning the Christian Priesthood)** (`npnf109`, div1 4, six books). This world's own direct, theological treatment of pastoral office — distinguished in Doc_01 §3 from World #6's own institutional-jurisdictional register: this text treats priesthood pastorally and spiritually, not primarily as a jurisdictional claim.

**The exile correspondence** (Letters to Olympias; Letters to Innocent of Rome; the Innocent replies) — already correctly carried, per the corpus-map's own "second id" pattern, as material shared with `imperial-juridical-christianity`, evidencing the deposition-and-exile conflict with the imperial court without this world's own build centering on that conflict institutionally.

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus's four works** (`npnf203`) — carried on the existing corpus-map at a mix of `assigned` (*Eranistes*, *Letters*) and `provisional` (*Ecclesiastical History*, Counter-statements) confidence — corrected this revision, per Doc_01 §4's own new per-work boundary finding, from an earlier draft's blanket "uniformly provisional" description. That finding, applied here rather than merely cited: the *Ecclesiastical History* (323–428) is genuinely Native to this world's own 350–430 window, read as later-historian testimony alongside Socrates and Sozomen, not as a second instance of Chrysostom's own core exegetical-homiletic voice; the *Eranistes* (c. 447) and the 181 Letters (largely 440s material) fall outside this world's own window by date and are not treated here as Native; the Counter-statements to Cyril's Anathemas (430–431) sit at the window's own edge and are, per Step 0 §2 (A1), condemned material at the Second Council of Constantinople (553) — a live edge case, not a settled inclusion. This document does not use any Theodoret material to support a specific claim; the corpus-map's own existing confidence tags for the *Eranistes* and Letters specifically should be revisited against this finding by whoever next touches this world's own corpus-map assignments, named here as an open item (§9) rather than silently corrected in a file this document does not itself edit.

**Context/hostile-witness material**, already correctly held as `context` rather than `tradition` on the existing corpus-map: Socrates Scholasticus and Sozomen's ecclesiastical histories (Chrysostom's episcopate and fall, `npnf202`); Cassian's *De Incarnatione* (an opponent's testimony to Antiochene Christology, `npnf211`); Cyril of Alexandria's anti-Nestorian letters and the Ephesus (431) and Second Constantinople (553) conciliar acts (`npnf214`); Ammianus Marcellinus's eyewitness account of Julian at Antioch (`ammianus-marcellinus_roman-history_yonge1862`, added 2026-09-25, already directly quote-verified by the prior research pass). None of these requires fresh verification by this document — they are context, not Native voice, and their existing verification is sound.

**The *Apostolic Constitutions*** (`anf07`) — carried at `confidence: provisional`, same city and generation as Chrysostom but a different genre (church-order/liturgical compilation); this document does not elevate it beyond the corpus-map's own already-careful provisional status.

## 2. Author Gravity Assessment

**John Chrysostom.**
- *Visibility:* Extremely high, and across two distinct settings (Antiochene presbyter; Constantinopolitan bishop and exile) — a genuine advantage over a single-office-holder world like `roman-church-gregorian`, since the two settings let this world's own account of his consistency (Doc_01 §4) be checked against two different sets of circumstances, not asserted from one alone.
- *Representativeness:* Chrysostom is not a representative voice for an ordinary Antiochene layperson's own experience — he is the preacher addressing them, not one of them. **Corrected in this revision (Round 1 review, HIGH finding):** this document's first draft described his family as "decurion-class," a detail this document did not verify and that is not, in fact, what the vendored source says. Verified directly against the NPNF introduction itself (`npnf109`, locus `iii.ii-p9`): *"His father, Secundus, was a distinguished military officer (magister militum) in the imperial army."* His own social position — a senior military officer's son, classically educated under Libanius — is itself elite, even though his subject matter is unusually attentive to ordinary and poor believers' lives by this project's own comparative standard (Doc_01 §3).
- *Influence:* Enormous, among the largest of any figure in this project's portfolio — his homiletic corpus becomes a model for later Christian preaching across multiple traditions, well beyond this world's own window.
- *Limitations:* A preacher's own account of his listeners, their failings, and his own conflicts (with Eudoxia, with the Antiochene or Constantinopolitan authorities) is one side of each dispute — no independent corroborating voice for the Eudoxia conflict specifically is currently part of this world's Native holdings (Socrates and Sozomen, the nearest available check, are `context`, not independent primary testimony from the other side). His own celebrated rhetorical skill is itself a limitation worth naming plainly: a gifted preacher's own framing of a conflict is not thereby a neutral account of it.
- *Transmission History:* Unusually complete for a figure of this period (Step 0 §3, B1) — the scale itself (roughly 3.3 million words on disk) is itself a transmission-history fact worth naming, likely reflecting both his own enormous contemporary reputation and centuries of sustained later interest in preserving him, a continuity with this project's other worlds' recurring finding that institutional prestige and later reputation shape what survives (Doc_01 §5, Cell 2B).

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus.** Given the per-work boundary finding now established (Doc_01 §4, §1 above) — most of his own carried material either out-of-window by date or a live edge case — a full five-dimension assessment is deferred rather than performed prematurely for material that is, on the current finding, largely not this world's own to begin with. If the *Ecclesiastical History* specifically is confirmed Native at a future step, its own Author Gravity Assessment (as a later-historian narrative source, alongside Socrates and Sozomen) is owed at that point. Flagged as an open item (§9 below) rather than skipped silently.

## 3. Secondary Scholarship Assessment

- **Frances Young, *Biblical Exegesis and the Formation of Christian Culture* (Cambridge University Press, 1997).** **Confidence downgraded to C this revision, and flagged for priority second-opinion review (Round 1 review, HIGH finding).** This document's first draft cited Young as settled, Dominant-Modern-Reconstruction support for a simple literal-versus-allegorical, "historical-grammatical" characterization of Antiochene exegesis — without having independently re-read the book. Independent review flagged, as its own unverified but credible caution, that Young's actual thesis is understood to argue *against* exactly that simple dichotomy, recasting the Antiochene/Alexandrian difference in more technical terms (rhetorical versus philosophical reading; on some accounts, "ikonic" versus "symbolic" mimesis), and that the label itself is contested in the wider scholarship (O'Keefe and others). This document does not resolve which characterization is correct — it was never independently re-read this session, by either draft — but it no longer cites Young as settled support for a claim the book may substantially complicate. The underlying claim (Doc_01 §1, Step 0 §3 B3) now rests primarily on the ancient self-description directly verified this revision (Socrates VI.3, Doc_01 §2) and on the corpus's own compositional shape (§1 above), not on this secondary source alone. Licensed for: naming that a scholarly conversation about how to characterize Antiochene exegesis exists — not for the specific literal-vs-allegorical dichotomy as settled fact.
- **J.N.D. Kelly, *Golden Mouth: The Story of John Chrysostom* (Cornell University Press, 1995).** Confidence B — a standard modern biography, not independently re-read this session. Licensed for: general biographical framing (formation under Libanius, the arc from Antioch to Constantinople to exile) — not for any single specific vivid claim on its own.

## 6. Source Asymmetries and Missing Voices

Per Step 0 §4 (binding on this document):

- **Elite, clerical, male authorship dominates this world's own Native voice (Article 20)**, though less totally than `roman-church-gregorian`'s single-author concentration — Chrysostom's own corpus is vast but still one man's; Theodoret adds a second voice only provisionally (§1, §2 above). Unlike World #6 or `roman-church-gregorian`, this world's own *subject matter* — ordinary believers' wealth, conduct, and family life — is unusually attentive to non-elite experience even though its *authorship* is not; this distinction, between whose life is described and whose voice describes it, is named here explicitly rather than left to blur together.
- **Gender (Article 20):** Olympias, the deaconess to whom much of Chrysostom's exile correspondence is addressed, is a documented, named figure of real substance in this world's own record — but her own letters back to him do not survive at all (corrected this revision, LOW finding, from an imprecise "not currently part of this world's Native holdings," which implied a vendoring gap rather than a real, total loss), and her own voice, on her own terms, is not independently attested anywhere. Named as a real, undischarged absence per the Affirmative Duty's primary duty; the Framework's three bounding conditions for the secondary (reconstruction) prong are not currently met for any specific case.
- **Antioch's Jewish community (Article 23, not Article 20 — the same routing this project's other worlds use for an anathematized or opposed party rather than an internal marginalized voice).** This world's record of Antiochene Jewish life and practice survives, so far as this world's own currently-vendored holdings show, only through the *lens* of Chrysostom's own hostility to Judaizing practice — and even that lens is not currently readable directly, since the *Adversus Judaeos* text itself is unvendored (§4 above). This is a doubled absence, worth naming as such rather than folded into a generic disclosure: not only is the Jewish community's own voice absent, but so, currently, is even the hostile Christian voice that would at least evidence their presence and the anxiety they provoked. Both are binding gaps for future construction work, not resolved here.
- **The Marcionites/Manichaeans (Article 23)** — this world's record of both survives only through Chrysostom's own polemical framing (Step 0 §2, A5); no independent source for either is currently part of this world's holdings.
- **The Anomoeans/Eunomians (Article 23) — corrected this revision (Round 1 review, MEDIUM finding).** This document's first draft claimed this world's record of the Anomoeans "survives only through Chrysostom's own framing." Confirmed directly against `cic/texts/`: Eunomius's own *First Apology* (`eunomius_first-apology_whiston1711.txt`) and Philostorgius's *Ecclesiastical History* (`philostorgius_ecclesiastical-history_walford1855.txt`, an Anomoean-sympathetic account transmitted via Photius's epitome) are both vendored in this corpus, though neither is currently assigned to this world's own corpus-map bucket. Named here as own-voice acquisition/assignment candidates for the Anomoeans' own side specifically, per this project's own `CORPUS-USE.md` tier method, rather than repeating the false "only through Chrysostom's framing" claim — not resolved or assigned by this document.

**Affirmative Duty (Article 20), applied:** the primary duty is discharged throughout this section. The secondary duty (bounded reconstruction) is not exercised — no case currently meets the Framework's three bounding conditions. Re-check at Doc_05/Doc_07.
