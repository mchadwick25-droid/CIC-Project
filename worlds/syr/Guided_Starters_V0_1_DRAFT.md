# Guided Starters — Syriac Christianity (Edessa/Nisibis) (DRAFT V0.1)

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Mark's review. Not deployed.
**World:** Syriac Christianity (Edessa/Nisibis), c. 200–410 CE — Edessa and Nisibis, upper Mesopotamia, on the contested Roman-Persian frontier. Representative: Mar Yausep, a teacher within the covenant order (qyama), anchored specifically in Aphrahat's Persian-side life and teaching (Roman/Edessa-side material carried by Ephrem — the fuller *raza* register, sung hymnody, the argument with Bardaisan/Marcion/Mani — is held only "by report, across the frontier," not as this Representative's own lived experience), community "we"-voice.
**Purpose:** Entry points for participants who don't know what to ask. Questions open doors; they never walk the participant through them (Encounter Over Persuasion applies to curricula).
**Grounding:** Every topic below traces to this world's approved build documents (Doc_01 World Identification, Doc_02 Source Ecology, Doc_04 Confirmed Gravities, Doc_06 Full Lexicon + Lexicon-Chunks, Doc_07 Integrated Ecology Analysis, Doc_08 Forces Document, Doc_09 Story Inventory + Absent Stories) and the Mar Yausep artifacts (World Capsule Core, Permanent Prompt, Representative Construction Notes §6, and Syriac Phase Six Facilitator Coordination §B7, which supplies this Representative's own Ephrem/Aphrahat anchor calibration). Nothing is invented.

**Read this first — the caveat that governs the whole list.** Per `CiC_World_Coverage_Cards_V0_1.md`, starter questions are **largely role-generic**: the Representative handles per-world variation, and the live calibration proved a generic question meeting a thin record produces an honest calibrated answer rather than a failure. The premise that starters must be authored per world was cancelled. So the openers below are **illustrative routing seeds, not per-world authored gates** — they show the *kinds* of doors this world opens especially well and where its honest silences lie, to help a participant who doesn't yet know what to ask. A participant is never confined to them, and Mar Yausep — who speaks, as this world's own voice does, in the first-person plural of a whole gathered people rather than as one witness recalling his own life — will meet a question this list never anticipated on the same terms.

**A second thing worth knowing before the table is called:** this particular Representative is Persian-anchored — Aphrahat's own life and teaching, known directly, not Ephrem's (Phase Six, §B7, calls this "the single most consequential fact about this Representative for calibrating a participant's expectations correctly"). The topics below are chosen and worded accordingly: the vow, the demonstration, the twenty-year vacancy, the named martyrs, and the plainer register of *raza* are this world's own center of gravity for Mar Yausep specifically. Where Ephrem's richer, more famous material belongs (sung hymnody, the fuller *raza* hermeneutic, the direct argument against Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani), it is marked below as something Mar Yausep knows only "the way one hears of a quarrel in a distant town" — real, but not his own to have carried.

## First Visit (start here)

**The vow that doesn't end** — This world's whole shape turns on one lifelong promise: the qyama, entered deliberately and kept in the middle of ordinary town life among one's own kin, not in flight from them — this world's own Primary, world-spanning gravity. [Doc_04 C2; syrlex002 qyama; Capsule "The World You Inhabit"]
*Opening question:* "You've taken some kind of vow — the qyama? What does that actually mean, day to day?"
Follow-ups: "Is it like becoming a monk?" · "What happens on the day the feeling that made you take it has faded?" · "Do you ever regret it?"

**Becoming the Single One (Iḥidaya)** — A fully formed person here is named, in this world's own vocabulary, Iḥidaya — undivided in allegiance, sharing a root with the very word for Christ as Only-Begotten — attested directly in Aphrahat's own Demonstrations. [syrlex007 ihidaya; Doc_04 C2; Doc_08 Force 1B-2]
*Opening question:* "You called someone 'Iḥidaya' — the Single One. What is that, exactly?"
Follow-ups: "Is that a title you're given, or something you become?" · "How does that connect to what you believe about Christ?" · "Have you become that yet, or are you still on the way?"

**Reading by raza** — At a plainer, less elaborated register than the fuller telling given across the frontier, this world reads Scripture and creation as woven through with real connections a rightly-formed eye can see — not signs invented by a clever reader. [Doc_04 C1 (Author Gravity note: Aphrahat's own usage is real but lighter than Ephrem's); syrlex001 raza-shrara; Capsule "What Organizes Everything"]
*Opening question:* "How did you read the old Scriptures? Was it just history to you, or something more?"
Follow-ups: "Can you give me an example of a story that reaches forward like that?" · "Is that something you were taught to see, or does it come naturally?" · "What happens when you read a passage and don't see anything?"

**One Gospel, not four** — Throughout this world, "the Gospel" did not mean four books held in tension; it meant a single, continuous harmonized narrative, quoted directly by this world's own Persian-side teacher as well as its Roman-side one. [Doc_04 C5; syrlex006 ewangeliyon-da-mhallete; Doc_01 §4]
*Opening question:* "You keep talking about 'the Gospel' as if it's one story. Didn't Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John each write their own?"
Follow-ups: "So what happened to the four separate accounts?" · "Did you ever wonder what got left out when they were woven together?" · "Do you know the story any other way?"

**A demonstration, built letter by letter** — Teaching here moved as a taḥwîṯâ — a sustained, reasoned case, several of them scaffolded on the twenty-two-letter Syriac alphabet, so the argument could be held whole in memory rather than announced all at once. [syrlex003 tahwyata; Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "What is a 'demonstration'? Is that a sermon, a letter, something else?"
Follow-ups: "Why build an argument around the alphabet?" · "How long would one of these take to give?" · "Who were they actually written for?"

**A killing king, and a promise you couldn't verify** — This world's Persian-side life was formed inside the reach of a king whose anger took the lives of those who led it — not a distant fact, but the water this community always lived in. [Doc_04 C6; Doc_08 Force 2A-1; Doc_02 §11]
*Opening question:* "Was it actually dangerous to be a Christian where you lived — I mean, dangerous enough to die for it?"
Follow-ups: "What would get someone arrested?" · "Did you ever think about renouncing your faith to stay safe?" · "How did you keep going, knowing what had already happened to others?"

## Going Deeper

**Three authorities that never resolved** — This world held at least three real, non-collapsing sources of standing — ordained office, the vow, and a teacher's own received word — and never found the place where they became one settled thing, Aphrahat's own place among them included. [Doc_04 C4; Doc_07 §2C; Doc_01 §6]
*Opening question:* "Who was really in charge among you — the bishop, the one who kept the vow, or the teacher whose word people actually followed?"
Follow-ups: "Did that ever cause real confusion?" · "Was your own standing ever settled, or did that stay an open question too?" · "Wouldn't it have been easier with one clear answer?"

**The twenty-year empty seat** — After a bishop was martyred, no successor dared take a seat that meant near-certain execution — a primatial see stood vacant for two decades, a wound persecution itself tore open rather than a gap this community chose. [Doc_08 Force 2A-1 → Force 2B-1, Connection 3; Doc_02 §11; Capsule "What This World Holds Without Resolution"]
*Opening question:* "You said a bishop's seat stood empty for twenty years. How did the church even keep functioning without anyone in charge?"
Follow-ups: "Why didn't anyone step up sooner?" · "What held the community together in the meantime?" · "Did anyone ever suggest the office itself wasn't necessary?"

**Living between two crowns** — This world's teaching returned more than once to the anxiety of standing between two empires at war, addressed directly in a demonstration devoted to the subject. [Doc_04 C6; Doc_02 §2 (Demonstration 5, "On Wars"); Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "Your people lived caught between two empires at war with each other. Whose side were you actually on?"
Follow-ups: "Did the Persian king trust you, given you shared a faith with Rome?" · "What did you actually teach about it, when people asked?" · "Was there ever a right answer, or just a dangerous one either way?"

**Simeon our bishop, and Gushtazad who returned** — This world's fullest attested account of faithful endurance under the gravest pressure: a bishop who would not collect the king's tax or bow to the sun, and a royal official who had once denied the faith and returned to it before Simeon's own eyes, dying first. [Doc_09 syrstory005; Doc_04 C6]
*Opening question:* "Tell me about Simeon — the bishop who was executed. What actually happened?"
Follow-ups: "Who was Gushtazad, and why does his story matter alongside Simeon's?" · "Did Simeon ever waver, even once?" · "How is this remembered among you now?"

**The bishops we lost by name** — This world holds its dead by name rather than as a category of suffering — Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles of Susa, Acepsimas, Mareas, Bicor — a specific, communal memory-practice this world's own record shows functioning differently than the more archival memory kept on the Roman side. [Doc_07 §2F; Doc_02 §11; Capsule "What It Feels Like Here"]
*Opening question:* "You listed several names just now — bishops who died. Why does it matter to you to name them individually?"
Follow-ups: "Do you know anything about each of them beyond their names?" · "Is naming them a kind of grief, or something else?" · "Does it get easier to say the names, over time?"

**The tangled name** — An old colophon calls this world's Persian-side teacher "Mar Jacob, the Persian sage" — a title the record entangles with a different, better-known bishop of Nisibis; Mar Yausep's own name was chosen precisely so a participant need not untangle that confusion to receive the teaching itself. [World Profile §9, "Figures of Contested Standing"; Doc_02 §2; Representative Construction Notes §6; Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "I've read that some old manuscripts call your teacher — or you — 'Mar Jacob.' Is that the same name as you?"
Follow-ups: "Why would two teachers end up sharing a name in the record?" · "Does it matter, in the end, which name is on the teaching?" · "How would you even know which sayings are truly his and which belong to someone else?"

## For the Wrestling (hard questions welcome here)

**Isn't "we never settled it" just an admission you don't know?** — This world's own authority structure failed its own Formation test when honestly assessed — the record shows this plurality persisted, but not how it was actually lived day to day. A participant can fairly press whether that's honesty or evasion. [Doc_04 C4, Formation test result recorded as a fail rather than smoothed; Doc_07 §2C]
*Opening question:* "If you never resolved who's actually in charge, how do I know your teaching is reliable at all?"
Follow-ups: "Doesn't that just mean anyone could claim authority and you'd have no way to check them?" · "Did that plurality ever get exploited by someone who shouldn't have been trusted?" · "Would you actually want it settled, if you could settle it?"

**Your own story says your city could never fall — but it did** — This world's founding legend has Christ himself bless Edessa so "no enemy shall ever rule over it." Yet this world's own record also shows Nisibis ceded to Persia and Edessa's own church swept away in a flood. [Doc_09 syrstory004; Doc_09 syrstory002; Doc_01 §2-3]
*Opening question:* "Your own story says Christ promised your city could never be conquered. But Nisibis was handed to Persia, and I hear even your church was destroyed once. Doesn't your own history contradict the promise?"
Follow-ups: "How do you hold onto a promise your own record shows being broken?" · "Did anyone in your own community ever ask this same question?" · "Is the promise about something other than the city walls, then?"

**Gushtazad denied the faith once — why is he honored the same as Simeon?** — This world's fullest martyrdom account holds a man who recanted once alongside a bishop who never wavered, without ranking one above the other. A participant can fairly press whether that's mercy or a cheapening of Simeon's own constancy. [Doc_09 syrstory005]
*Opening question:* "Gushtazad denied his faith once before coming back to it. Simeon never wavered at all. Why do you honor them the same way — doesn't that make Simeon's own steadfastness worth less?"
Follow-ups: "Would Gushtazad have been honored the same way if he'd died still denying it?" · "Is there a difference between someone who never fell and someone who fell and got back up?" · "Does that make it easier or harder to forgive yourself, if you were the one who fell?"

**Doesn't honoring endurance just teach people to accept a killing king?** — This world's whole formation on the Persian side is organized around bearing what a hostile state does rather than resisting it. A participant can fairly press whether that's faithfulness or a way of making people easier to rule. [Doc_04 C6; Doc_08 Force 2A-1; Capsule "What This World Is Responding To"]
*Opening question:* "Isn't teaching people to endure persecution just a way of making them accept injustice instead of fighting it?"
Follow-ups: "Did anyone among you ever fight back instead of enduring?" · "Who actually benefits when a persecuted people is taught to endure quietly?" · "What would you say to someone who thinks resistance was the more faithful choice?"

**Doesn't harmonizing four Gospels into one lose what each writer was actually saying?** — This world received the Gospel as a single woven narrative rather than four separate witnesses. A participant can fairly ask what got smoothed over to make that possible. [Doc_04 C5; syrlex006 ewangeliyon-da-mhallete]
*Opening question:* "If you wove four Gospels into one story, doesn't that mean you lost whatever each writer was actually trying to say on his own?"
Follow-ups: "Did you know the four were ever separate, or was this always just 'the Gospel' to you?" · "What happens when two of the original accounts actually disagree with each other?" · "Would you want to hear them apart, now, knowing what you know?"

## Questions This World Answers Honestly With Its Limits

*These are good questions to ask precisely because this world will not pretend to more than its record holds. Its own build documents (Doc_09 §3 Absent Stories; Doc_02 §7 Source Asymmetry and Missing Voices) name where the record runs thin, and Mar Yausep is built to say so plainly and turn back to what he can open well.*

**"What was Aphrahat's own life like — where he grew up, how he became a teacher?"** — No separate biographical or hagiographic tradition exists for Aphrahat at all, unlike Ephrem's rich legendary corpus. A vivid anecdotal tradition does circulate under the name "Aphrahat," but this project's research traces it to a different, later Persian-born hermit near Antioch who died decades after the Demonstrations' own author had finished writing — and this repository does not draw on that conflated material. This world's Aphrahat is known only through his own discursive prose and thin, contradictory external notices. [Doc_09 §3]

**"Tell me the stories of the other bishops you named — Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles, Acepsimas."** — Beyond Simeon bar Sabbae, this world's own construction did not verify individual narrative content with the same confidence, and one scholarly source explicitly flags the Acts of Miles as containing "fable and fantasy" requiring particular caution. Mareas and Bicor are attested only as names in the martyrological list, with no individual Acts located at all. Mar Yausep will hold their names without inventing scenes for them. [Doc_09 §3, citing Vööbus, *History of Asceticism in the Syrian Orient*]

**"What did the daughters of the covenant say, in their own words, about the life they lived?"** — No composition by a bnat qyama woman survives from this world's own window. What exists about them is a teacher's own construction of their communal role, later remembered by a further teacher a century on. Mar Yausep will name this silence rather than speak over it. [Doc_09 §3; Doc_02 §7]

**"What did your worship actually sound like — the prayers, the readings, the words used?"** — This world's Persian-side text "offers almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail" — a real thinness on the very side of the frontier this Representative is anchored to, standing alongside a comparatively richer worship account kept on the Roman side. Expect the bare shape of the practice, not a reconstructed script. [Doc_02 §3]

**"Tell me about Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani — you argued against them, didn't you?"** — This argument belongs to Ephrem's own corpus and Roman/Edessa-anchor context specifically; no comparable engagement is demonstrated in Aphrahat's own Demonstrations at all. For this Persian-anchored Representative, these names are known only "the way one hears of a quarrel in a distant town" — real, but not his own to have argued down. *(Note for the Facilitator: if a participant expects an informed refutation of these three by name, that expectation belongs to Ephrem's side of this world, not this Representative's own anchor — see Phase Six §B7, "the single most consequential fact... for calibrating a participant's expectations correctly.")* [Doc_04 C3, Author Gravity risk flagged High and Ephrem-specific; Doc_01 §5, §7; Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"]

**"Is this still your tradition today? Could I go find a church that lives this out now?"** — Yes: this world's later descendants are named directly in the construction record as today's Church of the East, Syriac Orthodox Church, and Chaldean Catholic Church. But Mar Yausep's own temporal horizon ends at the 410 Synod that closes this world's own window — before the fifth-century councils that shaped how those traditions distinguish themselves from one another today — and he genuinely does not know, and will not speak for, what came after. [World Profile §9; Representative Construction Notes §6; Permanent Prompt]
