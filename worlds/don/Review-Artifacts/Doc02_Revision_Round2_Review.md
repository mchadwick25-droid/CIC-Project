# MEDIUM SEVERITY

## M1. §9 item 12 — Augustine's *Homilia XI in Ioannem* is not "not yet located in a vendored NPNF volume"; it is already vendored, and it is the exact passage Mabillon cites

**Location:** §9, item 12, final clause.

**Claim made:** "Mabillon's own cross-references on the Leontius/Ursatius persecution (Optatus III.4–5, both vendored... and Augustine, *Hom.* XI *in Ioannem*, **not yet located in a vendored NPNF volume**) have not been independently checked against the vendored Optatus text, though they now could be."

**What I found on checking:** `cic/texts/npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml` is Augustine's *Homilies on the Gospel of John* (the *Tractates in Ioannem*) — already present in the shared `cic/texts/` corpus, just not referenced anywhere in this world's own corpus map, Registry, or Doc_02 (confirmed by grep: `npnf107` appears in none of the three). Tractate XI is present at that file's own div `id="iii.xii"`, `shorttitle="Tractate XI"`, and it contains, verbatim, the exact passage Mabillon's apparatus cites:

> "See, Marculus was hurled headlong from a rock; see, **Donatus of Bagaia was thrown into a well**. When have the Roman authorities decreed such punishments as casting men down rocks? But what do those of our party reply?... Our men declare that those men cast themselves down headlong."

This is precisely the "Donatus... in puteum missus" content Mabillon cites (Migne PL8 lines 192–196, already vendored and already quoted elsewhere in this same file's Variorum Notae) to argue that the sermon's "Donatus" cannot be Donatus of Bagai, since the sermon's own martyrs died in the basilica rather than a well.

**Why it matters:** This is a specific, checkable factual claim about the current state of the vendored corpus, made in the section of the document whose entire job is to tell a future builder what remains open and why. The claim is wrong, and wrong in a way a single grep resolves: the source is not merely "vendored somewhere and unlinked" in the abstract — it is the movement's own most relevant homiletic cross-reference, sitting unused in a shared library this exact document already draws other Augustine material from (`npnf104`, `npnf101`). A future pass reading §9 item 12 at face value would conclude this check requires a fresh acquisition; it does not. This should be corrected to say the Augustine cross-reference is now locatable (`npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml`, Tractate XI) and, like the Optatus cross-reference, could be checked this pass rather than a future one — and the corpus map maintainers should be told `npnf107` is relevant to `donatism` and currently absent from `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml`, since that is a census-level gap this document's own methodology exists to surface (see §9 items 7–9's own precedent for routing this kind of finding).

**Note on severity:** this does not corrupt anything currently asserted in Doc_02's body — the document nowhere claims Augustine's homily is unavailable in general, only that it hasn't been located *in a vendored NPNF volume*, which is the specific claim that's false. I rate it Medium rather than High because its blast radius is confined to one open item, not a body claim the Confidence Map or Formation Narrative evaluation depends on.

# LOW SEVERITY

**L1. §1's Tyconius "genre" paragraph still mischaracterizes a chapter argument-summary line as "distinct sections" — a Round 1 finding (L9) that was not reached this pass.** Current text: "his own chapter outline names 'Tyconius's condemnation by a council' and 'his final years' as distinct sections." Checked directly against Monceaux (`monceaux_histoire-litteraire-afrique-chretienne-tome5_1920.txt`, lines 8416–8421): these are two clauses inside one continuous argument-summary line at the head of chapter section I ("Démêlés avec Parmenianus et les chefs de l'Église schismatique. — Rupture avec les Donatistes. — Condamnation de Tyconius par un concile. — Ses dernières années. — Chronologie de ses œuvres."), not separate chapter divisions. The substantive point the sentence makes (Monceaux's treatment of this episode is fuller than this document's own) is correct and unaffected; only "distinct sections" is wrong.

**L2. §4's Proximity clause still translates "sermonem habitum" as a text "written," not "delivered."** Current text: "on Mabillon's alone, a text written while 'the sect of the Donatists still flourished in Africa.'" Checked against Migne PL8 line 223: "patetque, sermonem habitum eo tempore quo Donatistarum secta vigebat in Africa" — "it is clear the sermon was *delivered* at the time the Donatists' sect was flourishing." This is the same small point Round 1's M5 flagged in passing ("Also minor: 'sermonem habitum' is 'the sermon was delivered,' not 'writing'") and it was not picked up in this revision. Trivial on its own, but the document is a genre-conventions section explicitly distinguishing a homiletic performance from a written composition, which is exactly the distinction this mistranslation blurs.

**L3. §4 and §8 present Mabillon's dating as a flat "*circa* 340" without his own hedge.** Migne PL8 lines 183–185 read "quæ hic referuntur contigisse circa annum [3]40, **aut paulo post**" — "or a little after." Doc_02's §4 ("Mabillon dates the persecution described to *circa* 340") and §8's Confidence Map ("Mabillon dates the same events more precisely to... Mabillon dates the persecution described to *circa* 340") both drop "aut paulo post." This doesn't affect the Contested framing or the Monceaux comparison, which are both otherwise accurate, but it slightly overstates the precision of the authority being contested against.

**L4. §7's "Doc_01's own corrected reasoning (Doc_01 §2)" is ambiguous phrasing that a reader could misread as review-process narration.** Checked against the standing clean-documents rule (`don_Decision_Log.md`: "no inline revision annotations, no 'corrected in vN' markers, no review-round commentary woven into the analysis") and against Doc_01 §2 itself, which contains no narrative of having been corrected — it simply distinguishes the 439 construction-window boundary from the movement's actual historical persistence. I read "corrected reasoning" here as almost certainly meaning "the more precise reasoning, as against a naïve reading that 439 ends the whole two-party contest" rather than a reference to a prior draft being fixed — Doc_01 was never found wrong on this point by any prior review that I can find. It is very likely not a violation, but the word "corrected" is exactly the kind of word the clean-documents rule exists to keep out of a document's own voice, and it costs nothing to reword as "Doc_01's own precise reasoning" or similar.

**L5 (very minor, not requiring action).** §4's rendering of Monceaux's alternative title hypothesis — "possibly the preacher's own name attached to that bishop's death" — compresses Monceaux's actual two-part proposal (lines 3255–3258: either "Donatus" is the name of the bishop of Advocata who died, which Monceaux adopts as his working title, *or* "Donatus" is the preacher's own name, requiring the words reordered as *Sermo Donati de Passione episcopi Advocatensis*) into a single clause that a reader unfamiliar with Monceaux could read either way. The substance is not wrong, just tightly compressed; I would not require a fix, only flag it as a place a future editor tightening this passage should look at Monceaux's own words again.
