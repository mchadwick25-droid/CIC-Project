## 1. Section 2A / Approved Source Anchoring — quotation and citation-locus check

Six named anchors appear in artifact 1's Section 2A paragraph ("When a specific image, story, or teacher's word would make an answer vivid..."). Each was traced to its actual cited locus, independent of Phase Three's own (already twice-reviewed) characterization, per this project's documented recurring failure mode of citation-locus errors.

- **Petilian** — "What Petilian argued: that what is sought is the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient." Traced to Doc_07 §2D: *"what we look for is the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient,"* attributed there to Petilian's own quoted words, sourced to Source Registry row 12 (Confidence C, "not independently checkable outside Augustine's own quotation" — confirmed verbatim against the Registry itself). - **Deo laudes / Bagai** — "The acclamation Deo laudes, cut into stone at Bagai, where the rival says Deo gratias instead." Traced to Doc_02 §5: the primary catalogued witness, CIL VIII 17732, is confirmed there as "found on two pillars near Bagai itself," with the editors' own note distinguishing *Deo laudes* (Donatist) from *Deo gratias* (Catholic). Naming Bagai specifically for the strongest of the four catalogued inscriptions is accurate, not an overclaim that all four are from Bagai. **Clean.**
- **The anonymous sermon** — "The sermon read every year at the martyrs' own grave." Traced to Doc_02 §4 (Mabillon's admonitio, *"in solemni et anniversaria commemoratione"*). No name is invented for an anonymous text; correct handling. **Clean.**
- **Macrobius** — "Macrobius's own letter, written in a burning eagerness rather than grief, carrying word of two of our own dead." Traced to Doc_02 §4: Macrobius's own letter to the Carthage congregation, opening *"Opportunus me, fratres, et laetus scribendi ad vos... ardor accendit,"* concerning Isaac and Maximianus (the Macarian-repression martyrs, correctly the pair meant — not to be confused with the Doc_02-flagged homonym, Maximian the deposed deacon of the later Maximianist schism, whom this sentence does not touch). **Clean.**
- **Emeritus of Caesarea** — "Emeritus of Caesarea's own plea before the tribunal at Carthage, that the truth was hidden by a great device." Traced to Doc_02 §1: at act 50 of the *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis* (the 411 Conference, held at Carthage), Emeritus presses procedural objection, closing *"magno argumento veritas occultatur"* — "the truth is concealed by a great [rhetorical] device." Accurate paraphrase, correct locus, correct occasion. **Clean.**
- **The retort attributed to "our own founder"** — see Finding 1 below. This is the one anchor in the paragraph that does not check out cleanly.

The fallback instruction ("we do not reach past it toward a more vivid one from the hand that argued against us, or from some other room not yet ours to carry") correctly tracks Phase Three §6's own fallback instruction, which names Augustine's *Psalmus contra Partem Donati* and Optatus's Lucilla narrative as the specific material this voice must not reach for. No borrowed imagery from those excluded sources appears anywhere in either artifact.

## Finding 1 (Medium-High) — "our own founder" is not supported by this build's own record

**Location:** `don_Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Fidelis.txt`, Section 2A: *"The retort our own founder is remembered to have given the emperor's own claim on the church."*

**What's wrong:** This refers to Donatus's reported retort, *"Quid est imperatori cum ecclesia?"* — but calls him "our own founder" rather than naming him. Two problems, not one:

1. **Not the established language.** Every governing document in this build that uses the word "founder(s)" for this world uses it in the plural, generic form — "this world's own founders" — never as a singular title attached to Donatus specifically. Confirmed in Doc_01 §5 ("which this world's own founders sharpened into a claim..."), Doc_08 (three separate occurrences, all plural/generic: "this world's own founders read as continuous with," "the conviction this world's own founders held to be simply true," "this world's own founders could hold their position as fidelity rather than novelty"), Doc_09 donstory007, and `donstory008_bagai-reconciliation.md`. No governing build document (Doc_01–Doc_09, the World Profile, the Phase documents) ever calls Donatus "the founder" singular. The one place that phrase appears at all is `Review-Artifacts/Doc01_Round1_Review.md` — a reviewer's own informal aside ("the communion whose founder said...— see M4"), not authoritative build content and not itself independently verified.

2. **Likely inaccurate on the build's own terms, not just unestablished.** Doc_01 §1 states the founding rupture directly: "the disputed election and consecration of Caecilian as bishop of Carthage... which produced the rival consecration of **Majorinus** by the rigorist party (succeeded, from c. 313, by **Donatus**, from whom the movement's name derives)." Doc_04 §3.6 independently confirms the same sequencing: "Cell 1B (the rival consecration of **Majorinus**, succeeded by **Donatus**, as the institutional expression of G1's founding logic)." On this build's own record, Majorinus — not Donatus — is the bishop actually consecrated at the founding rupture; Donatus succeeded him roughly a year or two later and lent the movement its name and its primacy (313–347 per Doc_02 §4's Monceaux material). Calling Donatus "our own founder" therefore states something this world's own construction record does not support, and arguably contradicts what it does establish.

There is also an internal-consistency problem worth naming: every other anchor in this same Section 2A paragraph is named directly — Petilian, Macrobius, Emeritus of Caesarea. Donatus alone is referred to by an implicit, unestablished title instead of by name, for no evident reason (nothing about "we"-voice discipline requires this — naming a teacher's own words is exactly what the backstop paragraphs' operational test asks for: "a story our own record kept and can be pointed to").

**Correct source text:** Doc_01 §1 ("...succeeded, from c. 313, by Donatus, from whom the movement's name derives"); Doc_04 §3.6, §3.7 (quote verified against `optatus_against-the-donatists.txt`, Book III); Phase Three §6, entry 2 (which correctly names "Donatus's own reported retort").

**Recommendation (not a directive — review only):** name him directly, consistent with every other anchor in the same sentence and with Phase Three's own entry 2 — e.g., "The retort Donatus himself is remembered to have given the emperor's own claim on the church" — or otherwise avoid the unsupported "founder" claim. This is a wording-level fix, but the underlying issue (an unestablished, likely inaccurate characterization of who founded the schism) touches a claim's substance, not merely its phrasing.

## 3. Living Traditions Distinction (artifact 1, final paragraph)

**Location:** *"Your world does not give rise directly to a tradition with present-day institutional adherents who would claim it as their own. You speak from your formation without this distinction being necessary. What you say is not confused with any living community's present-day identity. **Finding: none.** This section does what World Profile §9 asked for — carries Limb 2's open sensitivity into the deployed artifact as disclosed, not resolved.

## 5. Internal consistency between artifact 1 and artifact 2

Checked the specific claims named in the review brief, plus others discovered along the way, against each other and against source.

- **Temporal horizon (311/312–439).** Consistent. Artifact 1: "Your temporal horizon runs from 311 and 312 to 439... It closes when an invading army took Carthage." Artifact 2: "from the year a bishop was accused of surrendering the scriptures under persecution, and a rival was consecrated because of it, to the year an invading army took Carthage from the empire that had so often ruled against you." Both match Doc_01 §1's *c.* 311/312–439 CE scope. No contradiction.
- **The three imperial-appeal moments (T1).** Consistent in substance. Artifact 1 states only the count ("gone to that same emperor three times, when it served the case") without naming the three; artifact 2 names all three explicitly (founding petition, Julian petition for buildings, the turn to law against the Maximianists) in two places (Sections "What This World Is Responding To" and "What This World Holds Without Resolution"), matching World Profile T1 and Doc_04 §3.6 exactly. Vaguer in one document than the other, but not inconsistent — no contradiction to disclose.
- **Maximianist reception-without-rebaptism (T2).** Facts consistent in both (a returning company received into office without repeating rebaptism/reordination; the harsh Bagai decree language — "shipwrecked... dashed by the waves of truth upon the sharp rocks... fail to find so much as burial," verified verbatim against `donstory008_bagai-reconciliation.md` — sits alongside the reception, and neither is softened to excuse the other). **One structural note, not a factual contradiction:** artifact 1's Section 2 (Formation and World) presents T1 and T2 together under a single frame — "You live with one real tension" — folding both the imperial-recourse pattern and the rebaptism-exception episode into one stated tension, whereas artifact 2 and the World Profile treat T1 and T2 as two analytically distinct Tensional Gravities, and artifact 2's own Section 7 ("What This World Holds Without Resolution") correctly holds them as two separate, named tensions. This is defensible — World Profile §10 (Doc_07 §6's Integrative Observation, carried verbatim) itself synthesizes T1 and T2 together as illustrating one general formation capacity — and the Permanent Prompt template's own Section 2 asks for a single "characteristic tension," not an inventory. But it is worth naming: a participant who encountered only the Permanent Prompt could come away thinking this world holds one unresolved tension rather than two, where the Capsule Core (correctly) shows two. **Low severity, informational — not a contradiction, a difference in how many tensions are surfaced.**
- **The Bagai Deo laudes / Deo gratias contrast.** Same underlying fact stated with different specificity, undisclosed. Artifact 1 names both terms and the place: "The acclamation Deo laudes, cut into stone at Bagai, where the rival says Deo gratias instead." Artifact 2 states the same fact but withholds both proper nouns: "Deo laudes, praise to God, spoken where your rival says a different word for the same gratitude... are cut into stone in more than one place." Not a contradiction — both are accurate to Doc_02 §5 — but per this project's own cross-document consistency rule ("carry the exact wording across, or state plainly why the wording differs"), no reason is given anywhere for why one document names Bagai and Deo gratias explicitly and the other declines to. **Low severity.**
- **Circumcellion/*agonistici* characterization.** Appears only in artifact 2 (Section "What Organizes Everything" and "What This World Is Responding To" — the empire's fines, the rival's hostile characterization, the movement's own "leaders of the saints" counter-claim); does not appear anywhere in artifact 1. Not a contradiction, since there is nothing in artifact 1 to conflict with it — and its absence from the compact Permanent Prompt is consistent with Phase Four's own instruction that this topic should be "briefer and redirecting" in engagement, and with Doc_04 §3.5's classification of D-A as Supporting and regionally-scoped (Numidia only), not one of the four Primary gravities the Permanent Prompt concentrates on. **No finding.**

No World #7-style direct contradiction (one document asserting a fact the other denies) and no World #1-style "kept" vs. "survives" mismatched restatement of an identical claim were found. The two Low findings above are gaps in disclosed-difference discipline, not factual conflicts.

## Overall Verdict

**Needs revision — narrow, one substantive finding.**

Everything checked against the seven specific rigor items in the review brief passes cleanly except Finding 1 (the "our own founder" naming of Donatus in artifact 1's Section 2A), plus two Low-severity, disclosed-difference gaps between the two artifacts (the Bagai/*Deo gratias* naming asymmetry, and T1/T2 presented as one tension in artifact 1 versus two in artifact 2 — both defensible on their own terms but neither explicitly disclosed as intentional).

Per this project's own revision-decision test: Finding 1 is **substantial**, not cosmetic. Although the mechanical fix is a short wording change, what it fixes is a claim's substance and sourcing — an unestablished (and, on this build's own record, likely inaccurate) characterization of who founded this world's schism, stated as though it were settled, in the one place in the document (Section 2A) whose entire governing purpose is to hold the line against exactly this kind of unanchored claim. The two Low findings in Section 5 are cosmetic/disclosure-level and would not on their own require a revision round.

Recommend: fix Finding 1 (name Donatus directly, or otherwise remove the unsupported "founder" characterization), optionally note the two Low findings for the builder's own judgment, then re-review per `cic-build-cycle`'s substantial-revision path.
