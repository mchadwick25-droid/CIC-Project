## Findings — Medium Severity

### M1. Companion-index drift on the "Catholic" / *Catholicus* entry (Section 7 check — companion index fidelity)

**Claim checked:** The `.md` roster (Cluster 2) reads: *"Both hierarchies read 'catholic' (universal) as their own rightful title; Mabillon's own annotation on the Passio Donati sermon describes its Donatist author twisting the word into a sarcastic pun — 'catholic' not as universal but as the place where wrongdoing is committed with impunity."*

**What I checked it against:** `cic/texts/monumenta-vetera-donatistarum_migne-pl8.txt` directly. The sermon's own running text (l. 644–650) contains the wordplay itself: *"qualis esse possit apostasia, si digne talis appellatur catholica... ut eam vulgo dici fecisset catholicam, ubi impune committitur quicquid publico jure damnatur"* — the sermon's own author arguing the rival church could rightly be called "catholica" because it is where wrongdoing goes unpunished. A "VARIORUM NOTAE" editorial annotation later in the same file (l. 750–757) glosses this exact passage: *"Sarcasmus est et lusus in voce Catholici... at catholicae Ecclesiae illudens iste scriptor, alio vocem istam torquet, ut dicatur catholica, quia impune illic committitur, quod jure... damnatur"* — confirming, in the editorial apparatus this file attributes throughout to Mabillon's edition, that this is deliberate sarcasm by "this writer" (the sermon's own author) mocking the Catholic Church's name. **The `.md`'s claim is accurate**, and correctly locates the pun in the sermon's own text with the annotation describing it.

**What I found:** The companion `Lexicon_Candidate_Index.xlsx` "Candidate Roster" sheet gives a *different* one-line meaning for the same term: *"Both hierarchies read 'catholic' (universal) as their own rightful title; the Donatist sermon's own admonitio preserves a Donatist author mocking the Caecilianist use of the word as a hollow appropriation."* This is not a paraphrase of the `.md` text — it is a substantively different and less accurate claim: (a) it attributes an "admonitio" to "the Donatist sermon" itself, when the admonitio is Mabillon's own editorial device (a different, distinct annotation from the one actually describing this wordplay, which the file labels "VARIORUM NOTAE," not "admonitio"); and (b) it replaces the specific, textually-verified content of the pun (catholica = the place where wrongdoing is committed with impunity) with a vaguer, unverified paraphrase ("hollow appropriation") that loses the actual mechanism of the joke. A builder or downstream reviewer consulting only the index — which is exactly the index's stated purpose — would come away with a materially different and less precise understanding of this entry than the `.md` supports. This is a direct hit on the Section 7 check this review was instructed to run: the index does not, in fact, match the `.md` on this row.

**Disposition:** Correct the xlsx cell to match the `.md`'s verified wording (or a faithful compression of it), and re-check the rest of the "One-Line World-Meaning" column for silent divergence of this kind — this review checked all 21 rows and found this to be the only one-line-meaning content mismatch, but the mechanism that produced it (the index apparently drafted somewhat independently rather than mechanically derived from the `.md`) is worth a full re-derivation pass rather than a single-cell patch.

### M2. Self-designation/existence conflation on the sole [CT]-tagged term (Agonistici)

**Claim checked:** Doc_03 §0 states: *"Independent, non-hostile-mediated attestation exists for only a narrow set of terms: the Deo laudes acclamation... and the agonistici self-designation and CTh 16.5.52 (imperial legislation, independent of Optatus/Augustine's own framing, though the law's own text remains unvendored...)."* The roster's own AG-risk cell for Agonistici reads: *"Augustine/Optatus (HIGH — character and scale only; existence and self-designation are independently attested)."* §2 then instructs Doc_04 to *"treat the Deo laudes and agonistici-self-designation terms as this world's rare, valuable non-Augustine-mediated anchors."*

**What I checked it against:** `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6, the section both of these claims are sourced to. Doc_02 §6 states plainly: *"the group's existence is independently attested outside hostile polemic — imperial legislation names them directly (Codex Theodosianus 16.5.52...) — and Augustine himself reports their own self-designation, agonistici ('contestants'), a term of the group's own rather than merely an outsiders' label."* Doc_02 itself does bundle "existence and self-designation" together as one evidentiary bucket contrasted with "character" (a judgment call about source reliability for factual reportage versus interpretive framing, which is defensible on its own terms) — so this framing is not new to Doc_03; it is carried forward from an already-approved Doc_02.

**What I found:** The specific *word* "agonistici" reaches this build's evidentiary record only through Augustine's report of it — there is no vendored or even unvendored-but-cited independent source that contains the term itself outside Augustine's mouth (CTh 16.5.52 remains completely unvendored after five acquisition attempts per Doc_02 §1/Registry row 16, and even Doc_02 itself never claims the term appears in the law). Calling the self-designation "independent of Optatus/Augustine's own framing" and a "non-Augustine-mediated anchor" overstates the case: it is Augustine-*mediated* in the literal sense of transmission (we have the word because Augustine wrote it down), even if Doc_02's own judgment is that Augustine is a reliable *reporter* of this specific fact despite being a hostile *interpreter* of the group's character. Doc_03 propagates this bundling into its own AG-risk apparatus and into direct instructions to Doc_04, without adding the clarifying distinction — on exactly the one term in the whole document carrying the [CT] tag, where precision about what is and is not independently attested matters most. A Doc_04 gravity-tester taking §2's instruction at face value could treat "agonistici" as evidentially on par with the epigraphically-attested *Deo laudes* (stone, no hostile mediation at any point), when it is not: one is a physical inscription; the other is a word we only have because the group's opponent chose to record it.

**Disposition:** Not a fabrication and not solely Doc_03's error (the root framing is Doc_02's), but Doc_03 is the document actually instructing Doc_04's use of this distinction, so it should be the one to sharpen it: split "existence" (independently attested, imperial legislation, though unvendored) from "self-designation" (Augustine-reported, treated as reliable reportage rather than hostile framing, but not independent of him) rather than continuing to present both under one "independently attested" / "non-Augustine-mediated anchor" label.

### M3. AG-risk gap on the flagship Tier-1 term (Traditor/Traditio)

**Claim checked:** The Cluster 1 roster entry for "Traditor / Traditio" (Tier 1, tags AS DR TC RT) carries no AG-risk flag (the column reads "—"). The cluster heading cites "Doc_02 §1, §6; Registry rows 3, 4, 6, 17, 18, 31, 39, 47."

**What I checked it against:** `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §1, on Optatus's Appendix of Documents (which includes the *Acta Purgationis Felicis*, 314 — the actual trial record of Felix of Aptungi, the consecrator whose alleged *traditio* is the term's founding accusation): *"This appendix's contents are court acts and imperial correspondence; its selection, assembly, and transmission are still Optatus's own act, so it sits inside this world's Author Gravity concentration rather than outside it, even though it is a comparatively less rhetorically shaped layer than Optatus's own argumentative prose."* This is Registry row 2, which governs exactly this material.

**What I found:** Registry row 2 is the primary vendored source for the *traditio* founding-narrative content this term names, and Doc_02 explicitly and specifically warns that this material — despite reading as comparatively neutral court documentation — is still inside the world's Author Gravity concentration because Optatus chose what to include and how to frame it. Cluster 1's own heading list of Registry rows omits row 2 entirely (it cites rows tied mainly to the Maximianist affair and Augustine's letters, not the founding *traditio* material), and the term's own AG-risk cell is blank. This is inconsistent with Doc_02's own explicit instruction to carry this specific caution forward, and it is a more consequential gap than a purely cosmetic one because Traditor/Traditio is one of the six terms §5 flags for full Tier-1 treatment at Doc_06 — exactly where an AG-risk omission would propagate furthest.

**Disposition:** Add an AG-risk note for Optatus (citing Registry row 2) to the Traditor/Traditio entry, and add row 2 to the Cluster 1 heading's Registry-row list.

## Findings — Low Severity

### L1. Author-Gravity apparatus stretched to cover modern editorial mediation

The "Anniversaria Commemoratio" entry's AG-risk cell reads "Mabillon/Monceaux (mediated)." The Framework's Author Gravity apparatus (Construction Framework V7.4 Part II) is defined for primary ancient voices whose selection and framing shape the evidentiary record (the worked examples throughout Doc_01/Doc_02 are all Optatus, Augustine, Petilian, Tyconius). Naming two *modern* editors under the same column header is a defensible extension in spirit (their editorial mediation genuinely shapes what reaches this document), but it is a different kind of risk than the column's other entries name, and the "(mediated)" qualifier is doing double duty for two different things (17th-century editorial framing and 20th-century secondary-scholarship interpretation). Not misleading as written, but worth a clearer label at Doc_06 (e.g. a distinct "Editorial/Transmission mediation" note rather than folding it into "AG-risk").

### L2. Loose framing in the "Donatist" / *Pars Donati* one-liner

The phrase "the record is genuinely divided on its origin" (Cluster 2) is imprecise. What the verified text actually shows is a contrast between the party's formal legal self-identification in a petition to Constantine ("of the party of Donatus") and Optatus's rhetorical use of that same signature against them — not two competing accounts of where the name itself originated. The underlying claim is accurate (verified against `optatus_against-the-donatists.txt`, Book III); only this one framing phrase overstates what "divided... on its origin" would normally imply.

### L3. [DR] tag applied to 16 of 21 candidates (76%) with limited per-term differentiation

High Distortion Risk is tagged on 16 of 21 terms. This is not a Framework violation — full Distortion Risk articulation (the paired Modern Hearing / World Hearing lists) is a Tier 1/2 *entry* requirement at Doc_06, not a Step 3 candidate-list requirement, and the Lexicon Development Framework's Tiered Depth Principle governs entry depth, not tag frequency directly. But applied this broadly with one-line meanings that mostly don't gesture at what a modern reader would specifically get wrong, the tag risks doing less discriminating work than intended by Doc_06. Worth a genuine per-term distortion-risk pass at Doc_06 rather than treating the candidate-stage tag as already-justified.
