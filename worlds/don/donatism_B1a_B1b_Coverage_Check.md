## Step 1 — independent ten-item recall list

Ten sources a specialist bibliography on Donatism (the North African rigorist
schism, c. 311/312–439; Donatus, Caecilian, the Circumcellions, the 411
Conference of Carthage) would be expected to contain, named from general
knowledge of late-antique North African Christianity and patristics alone.
Seven primary, three modern scholarly.

1. **Optatus of Milevis, *Against the Donatists* (c. 364–367), with its
   documentary appendix.** The earliest sustained Catholic account of the
   schism's origins and the principal narrative source for 311–320. Its
   appended dossier (*Gesta apud Zenophilum*, the Cirta acta, Constantine's
   letters, the Caecilian hearings) preserves documents surviving nowhere
   else — the evidentiary spine of the field, and a generation earlier than
   Augustine.
2. **Augustine, *De baptismo contra Donatistas* (c. 400/401).** Where the
   validity-vs-efficacy distinction is worked out — and worked out by arguing
   *against* Cyprian's rebaptism authority while conceding his holiness, which
   is the exact hinge on which the Donatist claim to African tradition turns.
3. **Augustine, *Epistula* 185 (*De correctione Donatistarum*, 417), with the
   anti-Donatist letters generally (43, 44, 76, 87, 88, 93, 105, 108, 139).**
   Where the *compelle intrare* justification for imperial coercion is
   constructed — the most consequential single position to come out of this
   schism, and the running record of Augustine's own shift from persuasion to
   endorsing state pressure.
4. **The *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis* (June 411).** The stenographic
   record of the state-convened debate that ended the schism's legal standing;
   the only document in which both sides argue at length, the basis for all
   demographic work on the schism's geography, and the proximate cause of the
   412 legislation.
5. **Tyconius, *Liber Regularum* (c. 380s), and his *Commentary on the
   Apocalypse* (reconstructed).** The one substantial surviving body of
   theology by a Donatist — structurally indispensable, since Donatism is
   otherwise known almost entirely through its enemies.
6. **Donatist martyr literature: the *Acta/Passio* of the Abitinian Martyrs,
   the *Passio Donati*, the *Passio Marculi*, the *Passio Maximiani et
   Isaac*.** The corpus in which the schism said, in its own voice, that it was
   the church of the martyrs against the church of the *traditores*; the
   primary evidence for Donatist responses to the Macarian repression of 347.
7. **Cyprian of Carthage, *De unitate*, the *Letters* (esp. 69–75), and the
   *Sententiae episcoporum* of Carthage 256.** The decisive African antecedent
   both sides fought over. The schism is largely a fight over who inherits
   Cyprian.
8. **W.H.C. Frend, *The Donatist Church* (Oxford, 1952).** Field-defining for
   half a century; the source of the social/nativist reading all later work
   either builds on or argues against.
9. **Brent D. Shaw, *Sacred Violence* (Cambridge, 2011).** The major
   contemporary revisionist synthesis and the standard current treatment of the
   Circumcellions and of religious violence in the schism.
10. **Maureen A. Tilley, *The Bible in Christian North Africa: The Donatist
    World* (Fortress, 1997), with her *Donatist Martyr Stories* (TTH, 1996).**
    The standard modern reading of Donatism from the inside on its own
    hermeneutical terms rather than through Augustine's frame.

*(Reasoning for each pick, written before any Donatism file was opened, is
preserved verbatim at the scratch file and condensed above.)*

## Step 3 — the PRESS question, asked verbatim

*"Name up to three sources you would expect a bibliography of this world to
contain that this registry does not hold. If you can name none, say so
explicitly."*

**Answered — three named.** None is a re-list of a Step-2 miss, because there
were none. All three came from the discovery work below and from live web
search, which was available and used this session.

**(1) The Vienna corpus of anonymous Donatist sermons (Österreichische
Nationalbibliothek, Ms. Lat. 4147; also circulated as the *Sermones Escorial*;
dissident origin established by François Leroy, 1994/1999).** Roughly sixty
sermons, identified since the mid-1990s as Donatist rather than Catholic —
by a wide margin the largest surviving body of Donatist-authored preaching in
existence. This matters to *this* world more than to any other, because the
Registry's own Doc_02 §6 (Source Asymmetries and Missing Voices) names
Donatist-authored primary voice as this world's scarcest category, and row 50's
own Licensed-For field says so in as many words: the *Passio Donati* is "a
genuine surviving Donatist-authored primary voice — the scarcest category this
Registry's own Discovery methodology and Doc_02 §6 name for this world." The
Registry holds exactly one Donatist sermon and does not know that sixty more
have been identified. Everything else it has in the movement's own voice is
mediated through its enemies (Petilian inside Augustine, row 12, at Confidence
C and explicitly "not independently checkable outside Augustine's own
quotation"; Gaudentius inside Augustine, row 56). This corpus is the single
largest available correction to the Author Gravity concentration the Registry
names as its central structural problem — larger than Tyconius, which Doc_02 §1
currently calls "the single largest available correction." Acquisition status
is not trivial (the modern editions are in copyright and partly scattered
across *PLS* 4 and journal publications), which is exactly why it belongs on an
acquisition manifest rather than in a hopeful note.

**(2) Jesse A. Hoover, *The Donatist Church in an Apocalyptic Age*, Oxford
Early Christian Studies (Oxford University Press, 2018).** The Registry's
newest secondary work is Shaw 2011; its scholarship layer otherwise runs Frend
(1952), Tengström (1964), Brown (1967/1972), Mandouze (1982), Duval (1982),
Maier (1987), Tilley (1996/1997), Shaw (2011). Hoover is the major monograph of
the following decade, and it is not merely "more recent" — it is the study that
reads exactly the two sources this Registry has vendored but cannot yet
interpret. Row 21, the *Liber genealogus*, is vendored, confirmed, dated, and
carries the explicit note "Not yet licensed for any specific claim —
chronological/genealogical content only; use to be determined at Doc_09."
Hoover's book is built substantially on the *Liber genealogus*, the Donatist
Dossier (the Registry's own row 36, Maier, unacquired), and the Vienna Homilies
at (1) above. Rows 15/41 (Tyconius) sit in the same condition: vendored,
identity confirmed, and the Author Gravity assessment Doc_02 §1 calls the
largest available correction explicitly "not itself performed this session."
Hoover is the current scholarship on precisely that reading. A world whose two
most promising unexploited primary sources are the two this monograph is about
should have the monograph on its list.

**(3) Eusebius of Caesarea, *Historia Ecclesiastica* X.5–7 (with the *Life of
Constantine*).** Surfaced by the B-1a sweep below rather than by the blind
Step-1 list, and named here because it answers a problem the Registry states
against itself. Row 2 rows Optatus's Appendix — Constantine's letters, the
Anulinus *relatio*, the Arles letter, the Cirta acta — and its own Verification
Note concedes: "contents are court acts and correspondence, but
selection/transmission is Optatus's own act — sits inside Author Gravity
concentration." Eusebius HE X.5–7 preserves several of the same founding
imperial documents in an entirely independent Greek transmission: Constantine
to Anulinus (twice), and Constantine to Miltiades convening the Rome hearing of
313. That is the one available non-Optatan, non-Augustinian route to the
schism's founding paperwork, and it is the direct answer to row 2's own stated
worry. It is not an acquisition problem: it is already vendored as
`cic/texts/npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml`, and the
word "Eusebius" does not appear anywhere in the 56-row Registry. See the sweep
for the verified detail.

**Disposition (all three):** (1) and (2) route to the pre-freeze re-sweep as
acquisition targets, named with enough detail (author, title, series, publisher,

### Finding 1 — six further Augustine anti-Donatist works sitting unrowed inside an already-vendored file, and a Registry claim that is wrong

`cic/texts/augustini_scripta-contra-donatistas-pars-i-iii_petschenig1908-1910.txt`
is the Petschenig CSEL edition rowed at 39 and relied on by rows 17, 18, 37, and
56. Row 39 concluded, from its single surviving title page plus an errata
section, that the file bundles **Pars I (CSEL 51) and Pars III (CSEL 53)**, and
stated as a directly-checked finding: *"Pars II (CSEL 52, Contra litteras
Petiliani) is confirmed absent, checked directly — found only as a
cross-reference abbreviation, never as body text."*

**That is wrong, and the file's own running headers show it.** The file carries
a continuous twelve-work numbered sequence in its page headers:

| # | Work | Registry status |
|---|---|---|
| I | Psalmus contra partem Donati | row 37 |
| II | Contra epistulam Parmeniani | row 18 |
| III | De baptismo | row 3 (via NPNF English); Latin unrowed |
| IIII | **Contra litteras Petiliani** | **none — declared absent by row 39** |
| V | **Epistula ad catholicos de secta Donatistarum** | **none** |
| VI | Contra Cresconium | row 17 |
| VII | **De unico baptismo contra Petilianum** | **none** |
| VIII | **Breviculus collationis cum Donatistis** | **none** |
| VIIII | **Contra partem Donati post gesta** | **none** |
| X | **Sermo ad Caesariensis ecclesiae plebem** | **none** |
| XI | **Gesta cum Emerito** | **none** |
| XII | Contra Gaudentium | row 56 |

Each of the six bolded works was opened at its first running header and read
into its body text; all six carry genuine Latin text with full critical
apparatus, not cross-references. *Contra litteras Petiliani* alone carries 59+
running-header occurrences and reads, at the first of them, as continuous
argument on rebaptism with apparatus citing *Cresc.* II 25, 80 — unmistakably
body text.

**Why row 39 got it wrong is checkable and worth recording.** Row 39's
reasoning rested on the errata section at the file's end "explicitly
correct[ing] both 'VOL. LI' and 'VOL. LIII' by name." The errata in fact carries
**three** volume headings, not two — at line 101362 it reads `VOL. LIL`, which
is OCR damage for **VOL. LII**, sitting exactly where it should between VOL. LI
and VOL. LIII, with its own three corrections beneath it. A single mis-OCR'd
final `I` is what produced the wrong conclusion. The filename's own
`pars-i-iii` means Pars I *through* III, not Pars I *and* III.

**Why this matters, not just that it is wrong.** Row 12 (Petilian's letters)
sits at Confidence C, licensed as "this world's own fullest surviving primary
voice **in the vendored corpus**," with the note "Not independently checkable
outside Augustine's own quotation." The critical Latin edition of the very work
that preserves that voice has been on disk since 2026-09-01, declared absent.
And *Gesta cum Emerito* (XI) and *Breviculus collationis* (VIII) both bear
directly on the 411 Conference material rows 14/55 are still working through:
Doc_02 §3 already notes, from Monceaux Tome VI, that the *Gesta cum Emerito* is
"a different work from the 411 *Collatio*, **not itself vendored or checked this
session**." It is vendored. It has been all along.

This world's own corpus map already half-knows this: `donatism.yaml` carries
full entries for *De unico baptismo contra Petilianum* (`role: tradition`,
`confidence: assigned`) and *Contra (Adversus) Fulgentium Donatistam*
(`role: tradition`, `confidence: provisional`) against this same file — neither
of which has a Registry row or a source record. The Registry and the corpus map
have drifted apart on the contents of a file they both cite.

### Finding 2 — the *agonistici* open item is closed by material already on disk

Doc_02 §9 item 1a(v) carries this forward as an open item, and rows 16 and 51
both state it: *"the* agonistici *self-designation term's own specific source
passage in Augustine's corpus remains unidentified."* Rows 16/51 confirmed the
term absent from the Theodosian Code; Doc_02 records a further check against the
*Contra Cresconium*/*Parmeniani* volume that found only "a generic
athletic-metaphor usage."

**The term is in four vendored files, three of which this Registry already
rows.** Searched with a `(?<!nt)` guard, because a plain search for "agonistic"
matches "antagonistic" and buries the real hits — which is very likely why
earlier checks missed it:

- **`optatus_against-the-donatists.txt` — the Registry's own row 1 file.**
  Vassall-Phillips' note 115, on Optatus III.4: *"circumcelliones agonisticos."*
  The term is Optatus's own Latin, sitting in the file row 1 already cites, with
  the translator's note cross-referencing Augustine's *Contra Cresconium* for the
  etymology.
- **`optatus_libri-vii-critical_ziwsa1893.txt` — row 38.** Ziwsa's own index
  records `agonisticos circumcelliones 81, 19`, confirming it in the critical
  Latin text at a specific page and line.
- **`monceaux_histoire-litteraire-afrique-chretienne-tome4_1912.txt` — row 52,
  vendored 2026-09-07 but read only to title page and chapter headings.**
  Monceaux gives the missing Augustine locus verbatim in his own footnote:
  *"Augustin, Enarr. in Psalm. 132, 6: 'Milites Christi Agonistici appellantur.
  Utinam ergo milites Christi essent, et non milites Diaboli, a quibus plus
  timetur* Deo laudes *quam fremitus leonis.'"* — which names *Deo laudes*, row
  27's own subject, in the same sentence. Monceaux's body text at that point
  also states the self-designation directly: *"Ils s'appelaient eux-mêmes les
  « Agonistiques » (Agonistici), ou les « soldats du Christ » (milites
  Christi)."*
- **`monumenta-vetera-donatistarum_migne-pl8.txt` — rows 19/20/50.**
  *"hic Agonisticos (quos vocabat) seu Circumcelliones… armavit."*

Two unrowed files corroborate independently:
`npnf106`'s own editorial note ("By the Donatists called Agonistici (St.
Augustin, *In Ps.* 133. 6)") and Weiskotten's note in `possidius_vita-augustini`
("They called themselves *Milites Christi Agonistici*, see Optatus, *De
Schismate Donatistarum*, PL ii, 1007").

**The honest limit on this finding:** the vendored NPNF English of the
*Enarrationes* (`npnf108`) does *not* carry the term — searched directly, zero
occurrences of "Agonistici," "Milites Christi," or "soldiers of Christ." It
carries the *substance* of the surrounding passages (the Circumcellion clubs
called "Israels"; "Circumcelliones armed everywhere remain not quiet… for the
blood of innocent men they thirst"), but the self-designation itself reaches
this corpus only through the Latin and through Monceaux. So the locus is now
identified and citable at Confidence A from a vendored text; a verbatim English
rendering of it is not yet on disk. That distinction is the finding, and it is
not softened here.

**A live downstream consequence, observed while gate-checking and reported
because it was found.** The concurrent pass writing into this worktree has
already built `records/don/term/don.term.agonistici.md`, and — necessarily,
given the open item — it attributes the self-designation to
`don.source.augustine-contra-gaudentium` at the locus *"Augustine's report of
the group's self-designation and conduct."* That is an unlocated attribution
standing in for the passage nobody could find, and *Contra Gaudentium* is very
likely not where the term is: the Registry's own check of that same Petschenig
volume found only "a generic athletic-metaphor usage unrelated to the
Circumcellion group name." Finding 2 supplies what that record actually needs —
Optatus III.4 (row 1's own file, and Ziwsa's index at row 38) for the Latin
term itself, and Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 132.6 for the self-designation, via
Monceaux Tome IV's verbatim quotation at row 52. Not edited here — that record
belongs to another pass, in flight — but routed to it by name, since it is the
one place in the build where this open item is currently doing load-bearing
work.

### Finding 3 — the ancient prosopography of Donatism's own authors, vendored and unrowed

`cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` — rowed by the
Cappadocian world, never by this one; the Registry contains no reference to
Jerome or Gennadius at all. Read directly, it carries four notices squarely
inside this world's boundary and squarely on its open questions:

- **Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 93, "Donatus the heresiarch."** Contemporary
  notice on Donatus the Great, stating that *"many of his works, which relate to
  his heresy, are extant, including* On the Holy Spirit, *a work which is Arian
  in doctrine."* Ancient testimony that Donatus himself was an author — bearing
  directly on Doc_02 §6's scarcest-category problem.
- **Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 110, on Optatus of Milevis.** Optatus "wrote
  in behalf of the Catholic party **six books** against the calumny of the
  Donatian party." Row 1's Verification Note leaves the Books I–VII /
  second-edition question explicitly open and names Ziwsa's apparatus (row 38,
  unread for that purpose) and Labrousse (row 44, unacquired) as the only two
  instruments that would settle it. Here is a third, ancient, already-vendored
  witness that gives a six-book count — precisely the kind of evidence the
  two-edition hypothesis rests on.
- **Gennadius, *De viris illustribus* 4, "Vitellius the African."** A named
  Donatist author, "defending the Donatist schism," with three works titled:
  *Why the servants of God are hated by the world*; *Against the nations and
  against us as traditors of the Holy Scriptures in times of persecution*; and
  *On ecclesiastical procedure*. The Registry does not mention Vitellius
  anywhere. This is a Donatist author the build does not know exists.
- **Gennadius, *De viris illustribus* 5, "Macrobius."** "As I learned from the
  writings of Optatus, afterwards secretly bishop of the Donatians in Rome" —
  the ancient source for the very identification row 20 currently takes at
  second hand from Migne's own editorial note. Macrobius is the author of row
  20's *Passio Isaac et Maximiani*. Gennadius also credits him with a further
  work, *To confessors and virgins*.
- **Gennadius, *De viris illustribus* 18, "Tichonius the African."** The
  principal ancient biographical notice on Tyconius — naming *On internal war*
  and *Expositions of various causes*, from which "he is recognized to have been
  a Donatist"; recording that "he composed also **eight** Rules… compressing
  them into one volume" (against the seven in Burkitt's vendored edition, row
  41 — a textual datum worth having); and confirming "he also expounded the
  Apocalypse of John entire, regarding nothing in it in a carnal sense, but all
  in a spiritual sense." That last is the ancient attestation of the very work
  Step 2 marks as this check's one partial: Tyconius' Apocalypse commentary.

### Homonym traps — real ones, found by reading rather than assuming

The Registry already flags two internally and deserves credit for both: the two
Optatuses (Optatus of Milevis vs. Optatus Gildonianus of Thamugadi, row 4) and
the two Maximians (the Maximianist-schism deacon vs. the martyr of row 20, rows
17/18/20). Three further traps surfaced in this sweep, each the exact shape the
Cappadocian precedent warned about — a volume belonging to another world's own
domain, reachable by keyword:

- **"Honoratus."** Row 50's *Passio Donati* turns on Honoratus, bishop of
  Sicilibba. A keyword sweep for that name across `cic/texts/` returns 30 files,
  the densest being `hilary-arles_sermo-de-vita-sancti-honorati_migne-pl50.txt`
  — Honoratus of Arles, the Gallic Monastic world's own founder-subject, with no
  connection whatever to North Africa. Precisely the Cappadocian
  "volume-shared-with-another-world's-domain" trap, with a different name doing
  the tripping.
- **"Gaudentius."** Gaudentius of Thamugadi (row 56) against Gaudentius of
  Brescia, who appears across `npnf203`, `npnf206`, `npnf204` and `npnf214` in
  Rufinus/Jerome contexts. Any keyword check for row 56's subject will pull
  those in.
- **"Fulgentius."** Fulgentius the Donatist (the *Contra Fulgentium* in the
  Petschenig file, and Monceaux Tome VI ch. VI) against Fulgentius of Ruspe, the
  sixth-century anti-Arian African — a *same-province, same-church, different-
  century, different-party* homonym, which is harder to catch than a
  cross-regional one because every contextual signal except the date agrees.

And one trap this world is structurally exposed to that has *not* yet caused a
problem, worth naming pre-emptively: **"Donatus."** Donatus the Great, Donatus
of Casae Nigrae, Donatus of Bagai, the Donatus of row 50's manuscript title
(which Monceaux argues is a corruption naming the bishop of Advocata), and
Aelius Donatus the grammarian — Jerome's own teacher, and therefore live in
`npnf206`, the Hieronymian world's own volume. Row 50 already handles the
third-to-last of these carefully; the last is not currently a live risk only
because nothing has run a keyword sweep on the bare name yet.

### Saturation statement

**What this sweep can claim.** It is complete over the question it actually
asked: every one of the 98 entries under `cic/texts/` was triaged, every
candidate that survived triage was opened and read into its own body text or
structural apparatus, and no candidate was accepted or dismissed on a filename
or a bare keyword count. Where a finding contradicts an existing Registry
statement (Findings 1 and 2), the contradiction was verified twice against
primary file content — running headers plus body text plus errata for Finding 1;
four independent files with a regex guard against the `antagonistic` false-match
for Finding 2 — and the *mechanism* of the original error is named, not just the
error.

**What it explicitly could not check.** (a) **Depth inside vendored files.**
Finding 1 establishes that six further works are present and are real body text;
it does not establish what they say. Nobody has read the *Breviculus*, the
*Gesta cum Emerito*, or *Contra litteras Petiliani*'s Latin in this build, and
this pass did not either — it read enough of each to prove presence and identity
and stopped there, per the Registry's own Confidence A calibration rule. The
same limit applies to the `npnf203` notices: they were read in full as notices,
but Gennadius' and Jerome's own reliability on any of them is not assessed here.
(b) **The PL 11 volume's unreconciled contents.** Row 55 names three
still-unread items in `pl11-zeno-optatus-collatio-carthaginiensis_migne.txt` — a
*Historia Donatistarum* (col. 771), a second Optatus edition (col. 883), and a
*Monumenta vetera* section (col. 1170) with a title identical to the already-
vendored PL 8 file. That reconciliation is a substantial reading task, still
open, and this sweep did not attempt it. (c) **The wider literature.** The sweep
asks what is on disk; it cannot ask what the field contains. Step 3 answers that
question separately, and only three items deep. Live web search was available
and was used to ground Step 3's items, but no systematic field-bibliography
sweep against standard reference instruments was run — that remains open, as the
Registry's own saturation statement already says.

**Confidence, stated plainly.** High for "nothing further is sitting unrowed in
`cic/texts/`" — this is a bounded, exhaustively enumerable question and it was
exhaustively enumerated. Moderate for "the Registry's existing rows accurately
describe what is in the files they cite" — two of them demonstrably did not, and
both errors were of the same kind (a conclusion drawn from partial reading of an
OCR-damaged apparatus, stated in the Registry as directly checked). That second
figure is the one worth carrying forward: it says the failure mode for this
world is not missing sources but over-confident description of the sources it
has.

## Verdict

**On coverage as B-1b tests it, this is the strongest result the fleet has
produced: 10/10, with all six declared near-misses also present, and no primary
or secondary expectation missing.** The Registry's field coverage is genuinely
excellent, and the Step-1 instrument found nothing to fault in it.

**On coverage as B-1a tests it, this is the weakest result the fleet has
produced.** Cappadocian's sweep found zero vendored-but-unrowed files.
Donatism's found six Augustine anti-Donatist works inside a file the Registry
already cites — one of which the Registry explicitly declares absent — plus
Possidius, plus the ancient prosopographical notices in `npnf203`, plus
Augustine's entire pastoral anti-Donatist register, plus the critical editions
of Letter 185 and of Cyprian. It also closed, from four already-vendored files,
an open item the build has been carrying since 2026-09-07.

These two results are not in tension; they are the same finding stated twice.
This world knows what exists in its field and has been diligent about naming it.
What it has been less reliable about is knowing what is inside the files it has
already got — and the two Registry statements this sweep contradicts were both
produced the same way: a conclusion drawn from a partial reading of damaged OCR,
recorded in the Registry as a direct check. That is a correctable habit, and
naming it is more useful than the individual corrections.

Adequate to proceed. Every finding above is specific enough — file, line,
running header, row number, verbatim string — for the pre-freeze re-sweep or the
next Doc_02 revision pass to act on directly without re-deriving any of it.
