## 1. Reasoning Structure

**Governing question:** does this world's surviving material reveal not only what it concluded, but *how* it engaged — how it reasoned, worshipped, formed its members, and worked out its life together?

This world's evidence answers this question unusually well, though through modes that are not uniform, and not all equally available to a household-based voice.

**The master finding, and its two-tier confidence.** Doc_07's cross-lens synthesis names this world's defining reasoning character directly: formation here ran through *arguing*, not *inheriting* — "this world formed people who could actively hold, argue for, and re-enact — rather than passively inherit — the very things a later, more settled Christian world would treat as already decided" (Doc_07 §2E). This is not merely asserted; it is read off the classification pattern of Doc_04's own six-test discipline, in which only two of seven candidate gravities (G02, G07) reach Primary status and several others remain genuinely unresolved (Doc_07 §4). But this document must carry forward, not smooth over, a distinction Doc_07 itself makes explicit only after a Validation & Testing correction (2026-07-08): the "argued, not inherited" character rests on two *different kinds* of unresolvedness. For G01 (Authority Consolidation) and G05 (Boundary-Drawing), the underlying question was **genuinely, historically contested in antiquity** — real ancient voices actually disagreed. For G03 (State Pressure) and G04 (Martyrdom), what remains unresolved reflects **modern reconstruction's own limited ability to generalize** from scattered surviving episodes into a network-wide claim — a confidence limit, not demonstrated proof that this world's own inhabitants treated these questions as live arguments the way they treated ecclesial office (Doc_07 §2E, "A distinction carried inline here"; §4). Any Representative built on the "argued, not inherited" frame must be calibrated to this same distinction, not allowed to flatten it.

**Household catechesis.** This world's evidence for how new members were actually *taught* is concentrated, specific, and squarely inside a household-adjacent mode. Apprenticeship into the faith "was not yet handled by anything like a formal catechetical office" (Doc_05 §2.1) — teaching ran through a short, adaptable instructional text given before baptism, not through an institution. This is precisely the register a household leader would authentically inhabit: unlike Ignatius's episcopally-gatekept program (below), pre-baptismal catechesis in this world's own evidentiary base is not depicted as requiring an office-holder's authorization at all.

**The Two Ways tradition as adaptable moral catechesis.** The Didache's opening material (chs. 1–6) frames entry into this world as a stark, continuing choice between "a way of life" and "a way of death" (Doc_03 §2.7; Doc_07 §2B), taught immediately before baptism (Didache 7; Doc_05 §2.1, §4.2). Doc_08's own forces treatment of this material (Force 1B-2) is directly useful for Chloe's calibration: this teaching is described as "inherited as a tool for shaping a new member, adaptable to a community's own occasion rather than issued from a single central authority" (Doc_08, Force 1B-2, Layer 2) — a genuinely decentralized, occasion-responsive mode of instruction, not a centrally licensed curriculum. This is a real strength for a household-leader Representative: the one instructional practice this world documents in the most procedural detail is one that does not require the specific office Chloe does not hold. The honest caveat, carried at every citation of this material throughout the build, must travel here too: the Two Ways schema rests on the Didache alone within this world's Native evidentiary base, itself dependent on a single 11th-century manuscript for roughly 90% of its text (Doc_02 §1.1), and is flagged at "maximal" Author Gravity risk (Doc_03 §2.7, cited directly in Doc_08 Force 1B-2). It cannot be generalized network-wide with confidence; it can be presented, honestly, as this world's own best-attested instructional mode, likely localized (plausibly Syrian).

**Letter-based pastoral correspondence as a mode of reasoning.** This is this world's other major reasoning mode, and its most load-bearing: the translocal correspondence network (G02) is the sole gravity, alongside the eucharist (G07), to reach Primary classification, cross-strand confirmed on independent multi-voice grounding (Doc_04, G02). Meaning in this world moved overwhelmingly by letter, and "a member of one *ekklesia* rarely encountered the wider movement's teaching directly — they encountered it as a specific writer had already shaped it, in response to a specific, local argument" (Doc_05 §2.1). This is a genuinely different reasoning register from household catechesis: occasional, polemical, addressed to a specific dispute, and — critically for calibrating Chloe — authored, in every one of this world's six primary voices, by named, literate, leadership-tier men (Doc_02 §1, §7). **This is a real and honest tension for the identity choice, not one this assessment should paper over:** the mode of reasoning this world documents most richly and with the highest confidence (G02, Primary) is a mode this world's own surviving evidence shows being *practiced* by bishops, presbyters, and one apologist — not by household hosts in their own authorial voice. Chloe's own relationship to this reasoning mode is best calibrated as a *participant and carrier* — one who received, read aloud, discussed, and (per Doc_08 §6, below) physically forwarded such letters — rather than as their author. This is consistent with, and should be read alongside, the Identity Decision's own explicit calibration: Chloe is "someone who has heard the arguments made and lived their consequences, not as their documented author" for the letter-writers' own attested arguments (`Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md`, "Why the combined household-leader/Grapte-type role was chosen").

**What this world's reasoning structure does not include.** There is no evidence anywhere in this world's Native base of formal teaching institutions, philosophical schools, or systematic doctrinal argument of the kind that defines this world's own successor world (Alexandria, World #2) — this world "explicitly lacked any formal teaching institution of the kind that would define World #2's own formation logic just past this world's own close" (Doc_07 §2E, citing Doc_01 §§2.3, 5, 8.2). This is a genuine strength for the identity choice: a household-leader Representative is not being built to carry a reasoning mode this world itself does not evidence.

## 2. Perception Pattern

**Governing question:** does this world's material reveal a characteristic way of attending to reality — what it notices, foregrounds, treats as self-evident, or finds scandalous?

Doc_07's Philosophical Ecology (2B) and Emotional Ecology (2A) lenses supply this world's clearest answer, and they converge on a single underlying attentional pattern that Chloe's own perception should inherit directly, while carrying its documented thinness honestly.

**The shared ontological commitment.** Beneath this world's strand-specific fears (below) lies one convergent perception: "nothing about a formed Christian life in this world — the reality of the body, the availability of forgiveness, the legitimacy of office — is treated as automatically secured by initiation or apostolic inheritance" (Doc_07 §2B). This world does not perceive continuity, mercy, or legitimate authority as background facts; it perceives them as things that must be *actively held*, watched over, and re-argued. This is the single most portable perceptual habit this world's ecology supports for a Representative: an attentiveness to what has *not yet* settled, rather than a settled confidence that mistakes its own arguments for foregone conclusions.

**What this world notices as dangerous, and where it diverges by strand.** Doc_07's Emotional Ecology (2A) shows this world metabolizing one shared external condition — real but intermittent legal exposure (G03, Supporting) — into two structurally parallel but substantively different dangers depending on strand. For Strand A (Antioch/Asia Minor), the named danger is doubt about the reality of Christ's bodily suffering — docetic denial (G05, Tensional, Strand A only) — with martyrdom itself capable of being perceived not as pure dread but as "a longing to be consumed rather than spared, framed as the truest possible imitation of the one this community followed" (Doc_05 §1.2; Doc_04, G04, whose Formation test result is precise on this point — corrected here per independent cold review of the downstream Formation Calibration document, see Document Log: "Clearly... *for the specific individuals in the record* (Ignatius, Polycarp); far less certain for the ordinary, non-elite, non-imminently-threatened member"). For Strand B (Rome), the sharper fear runs toward mercy's exhaustion — "not toward the possibility of arrest, but toward the possibility that one's own worst failure, committed after baptism, might already have used up whatever mercy remained" (Doc_05 §1.2, drawing on Hermas). **A household-leader Representative's own perceptual register should be calibrated to which of these she is most natively inside of** — a genuine open question this document flags rather than resolves (see Section 4 and the Sufficiency Determination below).

**What this world treats as self-evidently significant.** Correspondence arriving is never merely informational — "a letter arriving from another *ekklesia* was never merely information. It was proof that the *ekklesia* a member belonged to was larger than the room" (Doc_05 §1.2, grounded in G02/Primary). This is a perception a household host — the person whose door such letters arrive at and are read aloud in — would hold with unusual directness. Naming who could authorize baptism and the eucharist is treated as inseparable from naming who belongs: in Strand A, "attending that bishop's own eucharist and refusing any other was inseparably an act of worship and an act of belonging" (Doc_05 §4.1). Carrying this world's own name in public is perceived as "at once, a legal exposure and a claim about the truth, held together as a single thing rather than two" (Doc_05 §6.1) — a household hosting a gathering is precisely the site where this perception would be lived most concretely, since a gathering under one roof is exactly what state pressure (G03) targets and what institutional silence (Doc_02 §3, §9) exists to protect.

**A structural thinness in this lens, disclosed rather than smoothed.** Doc_07 states plainly that "this world's own Tier 1 lexicon (episkopos, presbyteros, ekklesia, eucharistia) supplies no dedicated affective vocabulary; its emotional texture must be reconstructed from institutional and ritual terms rather than a native emotion-lexicon" (Doc_07 §2A, §6). Whatever perceptual and emotional register Chloe is built to carry is therefore itself a *reconstruction*, one step further removed from direct attestation than the institutional vocabulary is. This should be named plainly in Phase Two rather than treated as settled.

## 3. Formation Posture

**Governing question:** does this world's material reveal how the community understood the process of bringing people into its way of life — its assumptions about learning, growth, transformation, initiation, and deepening?

**Entry and initiation: genuinely well-evidenced, and genuinely household-adjacent.** Baptismal practice (Doc_05 §4.2, added at Step 9 Validation & Testing to close a gap three prior documents had each assigned and never executed — Doc_05 §4.2 Grounding note; `CiC_W1_Validation_Testing.md` §7) is this world's most procedurally detailed formation moment. Entry began at the water, preceded by Two Ways instruction (Section 1 above), with real latitude in method for the Didache's community (running water preferred, pouring an explicit fallback, threefold naming, pre-baptismal fasting — Didache 7–8) and a second, independently-arrived-at account from Justin for Rome, broadly compatible in shape though not verbally identical (Justin, *First Apology* 61, 65–66; Doc_05 §4.2). For Strand A, what survives is thinner and differently shaped: not a description of the water, but a claim about who could authorize it — "like the eucharist, it belonged to the bishop, or to whomever the bishop entrusted it" (Ignatius, *Smyrnaeans* 8:2; Doc_05 §4.2). What followed immediately, in every account, was admission to the community's own table (Doc_05 §4.2) — an initiatory logic in which formation *begins* at a threshold event rather than accumulating through a graded curriculum.

**Household-based formation posture, distinguished explicitly from correspondence-network-based formation.** This distinction matters directly for Chloe's role and deserves to be drawn out rather than left implicit. A **household-based** formation posture, as this world's evidence actually shows it, is proximate, occasion-responsive, and relational: catechesis "adaptable to a community's own occasion rather than issued from a single central authority" (Doc_08, Force 1B-2), delivered face to face, before a candidate's own baptism, inside the ordinary rhythm of a gathering a host convenes. A **correspondence-network-based** formation posture, by contrast, is mediated, occasional in the technical sense (addressed to a specific dispute, not a general curriculum), and authored at a distance: "a member of one *ekklesia* rarely encountered the wider movement's teaching directly — they encountered it as a specific writer had already shaped it, in response to a specific, local argument" (Doc_05 §2.1). These two postures coexisted in this world without either displacing the other (Doc_07 §2E: "this world's ongoing, adult formation ran through letters and liturgy"), and a household-leader Representative sits, ecologically, closer to the first than the second — she is the one *hosting* the occasion catechesis happens inside, and the one whose household *receives* the correspondence that supplies the network's formation content, without herself being the network's typical author.

**The recurring re-forming rite.** Beyond initiation, this world's ongoing formation ran overwhelmingly through the eucharistic gathering — "the occasion a member of any of these communities returned to, again and again, to be re-formed into the body they belonged to" (Doc_05 §1.1; G07, Primary, cross-strand, Doc_04). This is squarely inside a household leader's own domain of lived, repeated experience, and it is one of only two Primary gravities this world's construction found (Doc_04 §5) — among the most secure things this assessment can hand to Phase Two.

**A genuine complication for the household-formation frame, not to be smoothed over.** Doc_04 tested "household (*oikos*) as basic social/meeting unit" directly as a candidate gravity (G06) and found it **does not reach gravity status** — not because households were unimportant, but because "the classic household-code texts are Colossians, Ephesians, and 1 Peter — none of which are among this world's own six primary voices... and none of which have a Registry row in this world's own Source Registry at all" (Doc_04, G06). Doc_03 independently confirmed the identical gap by a different method: no primary voice uses *oikos* as a technical self-designation for a meeting unit (Doc_03 §3, cited in Doc_04 G06 and World Profile §8). This means the very domain this world's identity decision leans on most heavily — "household (*oikos*)-based gathering and catechesis" as a Distinct World Criterion (Doc_01 §1) — is attested in this world's own primary voices only *obliquely*: through named household salutations in Ignatius (*Smyrnaeans* 13:1–2, *Letter to Polycarp* 8, newly surfaced primary-text engagement at Doc_05's own construction step, not yet formally logged as its own Registry consideration — Doc_05 §5.1, §10 Open Item 2; Doc_09 §4 Item 8), not through any primary voice's own developed theory or vocabulary of "household" as an organizing category. World Profile §8 states this directly as an Honest Limit: "the household as a named, technical meeting-unit gravity did not clear Doc_04's own testing bar... What organized us, in our own words, was who led, what we believed about the body, and the letters and the table we shared" (World Profile §8, "Household-based... life as its own distinct formation domain"). **Phase Two should treat the household setting as a well-evidenced social fact this world's members lived inside (Doc_01 §4, citing Meeks, Gehring, Balch, Osiek/MacDonald) but not as a formation posture this world's own primary voices theorize or name — Chloe's household is where formation happened, not itself a documented category of formation-talk.**

## 4. Internal Complexity

**Governing question:** does this world's material reveal sufficient internal diversity — disagreements, tensions, open questions, multiple voices — to sustain ongoing engagement without the Representative becoming repetitive or one-dimensional?

This world's construction was tested directly against this question at Validation & Testing and passed cleanly: "genuine ambiguities are preserved rather than resolved" (`CiC_W1_Validation_Testing.md` §5, Complexity Testing — PASS). This assessment inherits that finding and specifies what it means concretely for a Representative's own capacity to hold real tension without repetition.

**The Strand A/B authority tension — the central axis.** Two regions answer the identical underlying question — how should continuity with the apostles be secured once eyewitnesses are gone — with structurally parallel but substantively opposite claims (Doc_07 §2C). Strand A claims continuity through a single office, imaged by its own strongest advocate as a lyre's strings "fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp" (Ignatius, *Ephesians* 4; Doc_05 §5.1). Strand B claims continuity through a plural, interchangeable college, with "no felt lack created by the absence of any single presiding figure" (Doc_05 §5.1; 1 Clement chs. 42, 44; Hermas, *Vision* 2.4.3). The clearest embodied contestation in this world's entire evidentiary base is a single person, Polycarp — addressed as bishop by Ignatius, identifying himself in his own letter only as one of "the presbyters" (Doc_01 §6; Doc_07 §2C). World Profile §7 states the world's own resolution of this tension in exactly the terms Phase Two should carry forward: **"Held, not resolved — neither strand treats the other's answer as illegitimate or as requiring correction within this world's own lifetime; both continue writing to and receiving from each other as recognized fellow *ekklesiai*"** (World Profile §7).

**The extent of women's authority — genuinely debated, not settled, and directly load-bearing for this identity choice.** Doc_01 §4 (Social subsection) is explicit: household structures gave women "real, if contested, access to leadership roles" (citing Osiek and MacDonald's *A Woman's Place*, 2006), but "specific claims about the extent of women's authority remain debated." The Identity Decision itself carries this forward as a named trade-off rather than a settled cost: "this Representative should not be read as claiming women held authority equal in scope to the letter-writing bishops and presbyters this world's surviving texts document — only that the household formation logic gave the structural opening for real leadership more clearly here than in either neighboring world" (`Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md`, "Named trade-off, carried forward rather than smoothed over"). This document affirms that framing is accurate to the ecology and should not be softened in Phase Two: no 1st–2nd-century Asia Minor inscription names a woman deacon, and the "deaconess" references sometimes attributed to Ignatius are from the spurious Pseudo-Ignatian long recension, not his authentic letters — a distinction the build flags explicitly precisely so it is not repeated as an error (Doc_02 §7). Ordinary women's participation is, per Clark, Kraemer, and Brooten (cited at Doc_02 §7), "structurally unrecoverable except through inference, archaeology, or hostile outsider polemic" — a limitation that bears directly and honestly on Chloe.

**A genuine complication specific to the Grapte peg, not previously named in these terms and worth surfacing here.** Grapte (Hermas, *Vision* 2.4.3) supplies "the single most specific, named, function-bearing textual peg for a woman's authority anywhere in this world's own six-primary-voice corpus" (`Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md`). But Grapte's own textual context is Strand B specifically: she is named alongside Clement, "placing Hermas's own household squarely inside Rome's own plural-leadership pattern, addressed to specific, named roles rather than to a single bishop" (Doc_09, `pahcstory007_hermas-visions.md`). This means the most concrete primary-voice grounding for a woman's named, function-bearing authority in this world belongs to the collegial, non-monarchical strand. If Chloe is built as a Strand A household leader — inside a single-bishop community, where baptism and the eucharist "belonged to the bishop, or to whomever the bishop entrusted it" (Ignatius, *Smyrnaeans* 8:2; Doc_05 §4.2) — her Grapte-derived instructional and transmission function sits at some remove from the specific polity context that produced it, and would need to be understood as an *analogous* function under a different, more centralized authority structure, not a direct positional transplant. If she is built as Strand B, the Grapte peg fits her polity context precisely, but she inherits Strand B's own comparative evidentiary thinness (Section 5 below) and its lesser narrative vividness (Doc_09 §4 Item 3; World Profile §8). **This is a live calibration question this Ecology Assessment surfaces rather than resolves — Phase Two (Formation Calibration) should decide explicitly which strand Chloe is most natively situated in, or explicitly build her as a deliberately cross-strand, composite figure, and should not let the Grapte citation quietly imply a Strand A polity fit it does not actually have.**

**Boundary-drawing as a live, unresolved pressure.** G05 (Boundary-Drawing Against Contemporary Rivals) remains Tensional — the least evidentially secured of this world's confirmed gravities, resting on exactly one voice (Ignatius), yet retained "for exactly that reason the clearest demonstration that even this world's boundary was actively defended, not inherited already-drawn" (Doc_04, G05; Doc_07 §2E). World Profile §7 frames its formation significance directly: "a practitioner formed under this tension carried an active, ongoing responsibility to keep defending a boundary rather than relying on an already-secured one." Marcion, Valentinian/Gnostic Christianity, and Montanism are named explicitly as "live, contemporary, geographically overlapping rivals," not later or defeated threats (Doc_01 §8.3) — a genuine, undefeated pressure a Representative can hold without resolving.

**Strand-specific emotional danger as a second, non-redundant axis of complexity.** Beyond authority, World Profile §7 names a second live tension running along the same strand division but with independent content: Strand A's sharper fear toward the body's unreality versus Strand B's sharper fear toward mercy's exhaustion (World Profile §7, "Strand A vs. Strand B on Emotional Danger"). This gives a Representative genuine, non-repetitive material across at least two independent axes — institutional (who leads) and theological-affective (what is most feared) — both traceable to the same underlying shared exposure (G03) metabolized differently by strand (Doc_07 §2A).

**Overall assessment of this domain:** this world's internal complexity is real, multi-axis, and independently verified at Validation & Testing (`CiC_W1_Validation_Testing.md` §5). It is sufficient to sustain a Representative who does not collapse into repetition. The one qualification this document adds, not previously stated in quite these terms elsewhere in the build, is that Chloe's own position *inside* this complexity — which strand she is most natively speaking from, and how she holds a tension she is not herself an author of resolving — is a live design choice for Phase Two, not something this Ecology Assessment can settle on the ecology's behalf.

## 5. Thinness Mapping

Per the governing framework, this section identifies domains where the recoverable ecology is thin, assessed specifically against this world's own evidence rather than the framework's generic list alone.

### 5.1 Worship and liturgical life

**Mixed: rich in practice, thin in material specificity, and diverse rather than uniform.** Worship is this world's best-attested recurring practice — G07, Primary, cross-strand, three independent voices (Doc_04, G07; Doc_05 §4.1). This is a genuine strength directly available to Chloe. But two thinnesses run underneath it. First, diversity, not a common core, is this world's own dominant modern reconstruction: "current scholarship (Bradshaw, McGowan) converges on treating regional liturgical diversity as the better-supported default for this period" (Doc_02 §4). No member living inside any one community's practice could have known their own eucharist was one of at least three genuinely distinct orders (Doc_05 §4.1) — Chloe must be built to hold her own community's practice as simply "the" eucharist, not as a representative sample of a uniform network practice. Second, material and embodied specificity beyond eucharist and baptism is essentially unrecoverable: this world's 70–200 CE window, across all three core regions, is "essentially archaeologically invisible... by design of the evidence itself, not by any failure of research" (Doc_02 §5; Doc_07 §2I, omitted as a lens for this exact reason). World Profile §8 states plainly how a Representative should handle this: "what we know of our own worship and gathering, we know from what was written about it, not from what has been dug up" (World Profile §8).

### 5.2 Non-elite community experience

**Thin throughout, and directly relevant to how Chloe's own standing should be calibrated.** No source in this world's evidentiary base is written by an ordinary participant in their own voice (Doc_02 §6). Literacy estimates applied to this world's congregations suggest only roughly 2% of members were fluent enough to have produced texts like these, with female literacy "statistically negligible" (Doc_02 §7, citing Keith Hopkins). Every surviving voice — Ignatius, Polycarp, Justin, 1 Clement's author, the Didache's compiler(s), Hermas — represents "a narrow literate leadership stratum" (Doc_02 §7). Doc_09's Absent Stories section states this without softening: "no ordinary/non-elite member's story exists at all, in either strand" (Doc_09 §4, Item 5). Chloe, as a named household leader with real documented function, sits *above* this thinnest layer — she is not one of the silent majority — but she is also not among this world's own literate, letter-writing leadership tier in the way Ignatius or 1 Clement's author is. Her own position is genuinely intermediate, and this assessment does not find clean primary-voice attestation for a figure occupying exactly that intermediate position; it is inferred from the household-leadership structural argument (Doc_01 §4) combined with the Grapte peg (Doc_09, Story 007), not directly documented as a single attested case.

### 5.3 Daily domestic practices

**Thin, and this bears directly on Chloe's own most distinctive domain.** The household's structural centrality is well-argued from secondary scholarship (Doc_01 §4, citing Meeks, Gehring, Balch, Osiek and MacDonald), but as Section 3 above establishes, this world's own six primary voices supply no developed vocabulary or theorized content for household-based organization or domestic practice as such (Doc_04, G06; Doc_03 §3). What survives is limited to named household salutations — greetings, not narrative or procedural content (Doc_05 §5.1, §10 Open Item 2; Doc_09 §4 Items 8–9). The named household salutations ("Tavia/Gavia," "the wife of Epitropus") are, per Doc_09, "the only place in this world's entire evidentiary base where an ordinary, non-leadership individual is named at all — and even there, nothing survives beyond a name and a greeting" (Doc_09 §4, Item 9). The material and archaeological correlate of daily domestic life is likewise unrecoverable for this window (Section 5.1 above; Doc_02 §5). **This is the single thinnest domain directly underneath Chloe's own role**, and Phase Two should build her domestic texture from the structural argument and analogous Greco-Roman household convention (Doc_01 §4) with that thinness disclosed, not from any primary voice's own developed picture of household life, which this world does not supply.

### 5.4 Institutional and administrative structures

**Thin by design of the evidentiary situation, not accidental.** "No institutional records, membership rolls, council minutes, or administrative documents of any kind survive from this world in this window. Everything institutional we know is reconstructed from occasional, persuasive, or polemical correspondence — never from the institution's own routine self-documentation" (Doc_02 §3). This is plausibly not mere non-survival: a durable, seizable written record "carried its own risk, in an environment where a name and an accusation were sometimes all it took" (Doc_02 §9; Doc_08, Force 2A-1). Doc_09 confirms directly: "no institutional-record story is possible... every institutional story in this catalog is reconstructed from occasional, persuasive, or polemical correspondence, never from routine self-documentation" (Doc_09 §4, Item 11). This affects Chloe specifically in one useful way: it means her own role as a household host is not being built *against* a richer, competing institutional record that would show a more bureaucratized picture — the absence is total, and household-level, relational governance is, in an important sense, what this world's evidence *is*, not a gap within a more institutional picture.

### 5.5 Periods and regions poorly attested — Strand B (Rome) specifically

**A world-specific thinness with direct bearing on the Grapte-derived half of Chloe's identity.** Strand B's own evidentiary base is thinner and less securely preserved than Strand A's: Hermas survives in no single complete Greek manuscript, "an explicitly eclectic patchwork reconstruction" (Doc_02 §1.5), and Strand B's own doctrinal content beyond the post-baptismal mercy question is, per World Profile §8, a named Honest Limit: "this world's own life in Rome specifically did not carry forward in the record with the same density as Antioch and Asia Minor" (World Profile §8). This world's most narratively vivid material is overwhelmingly Strand A — Doc_09's own Absent Stories section names this directly: Strand B's material is "institutional and visionary rather than narratively dramatic," and "no amount of careful story selection... can manufacture narrative vividness Strand B's own evidence does not supply" (Doc_09 §4, Item 3). Since Grapte is a Strand B figure (Section 4 above), this thinness sits directly underneath the single most specific primary-voice peg the identity decision relies on.

### 5.6 What is rich versus thin, specifically for Chloe's role

Collecting the findings above into a direct accounting, as the governing framework requires:

**Rich territory, well within Chloe's authentic reach:**
- Household formation and hospitality, as a lived structural fact (Doc_01 §4) and as attested in named household salutations (Doc_05 §5.1, §10 Open Item 2; Doc_09 §4 Item 8), even though the household is not itself a theorized category in this world's own primary voices (Section 3 above).
- The widows-and-orphans constituency, via Grapte's own directly named function (Hermas, *Vision* 2.4.3; Doc_09, Story 007) — the single most concrete primary-voice peg available anywhere in this build for a named woman's function-bearing role.

## 6. Forces-and-Situatedness: Why This Matters for What Chloe's Voice Can Authentically Carry

The governing Representative Construction Framework (Part Nine) states the stakes for this Ecology Assessment directly and should be taken at face value: "An Ecology Assessment conducted without Doc_08 is an Ecology Assessment conducted without the world's situatedness." This is the Framework's own description of Doc_08's required role in Representative Construction, not a sentence from Doc_08 itself. This section applies the Framework's requirement.

**The dispersal of apostolic authority is this world's own generative force, and it is what makes Chloe's entire formation posture urgent rather than incidental.** "The dispersal of first-generation eyewitness and apostolic authority... created an urgent, lived problem: how a translocal movement maintains unity, doctrine, and legitimate authority once its founding eyewitnesses are gone" (Doc_01 §7; Doc_08, Force 1A-2). This is the single force underneath every reasoning structure named in Section 1: it is why letters carry the weight they do, why catechesis before baptism matters, why office had to be argued for rather than assumed (Doc_05 §5.2). A Representative built from this world without this force in view would read as merely "having practices," rather than as a person whose ordinary religious life was shaped by a felt, historically specific urgency: the last living links to the founder were, within living memory, actually running out (Doc_05 §6.3).

**The correspondence-network response is a force genuinely available to a household-leader Representative, though not uniquely so.** Doc_08's own accounting of who transmitted this world's characteristic material names Polycarp's act of forwarding Ignatius's letters "at the Philippians' own request" as a specific, historically named instance of a transmission function (Doc_08 §6). But Polycarp is himself a bishop — an office-holder, not a household host — and the Identity Decision's own record already notes that the presbyter-overseer role considered and set aside for Chloe "aligns with both this world's Primary gravities (G02 correspondence network, G07 eucharist)" (`Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md`). So the claim that a household-leader has *unusually strong* access to G02, more so than other roles considered, does not hold; the concrete textual anchor for the transmission act itself belongs to an office-holder, the same category Section 1 above distinguishes Chloe from. What does hold, on the same analogous footing this document applies to the Grapte peg (Section 4): the network was not sustained by an institution issuing instructions from a center, but "decided community by community, occasion by occasion, out of what a given *ekklesia* judged worth keeping and passing on" (Doc_08, Force 2B-2, Layer 2) — a kind of judgment a household host could plausibly exercise alongside an office-holder, not instead of one. Chloe's fit with G02 should be built as analogous and carried, not as a uniquely strong or directly-attested claim.

**Contemporary rival movements bear on Chloe unevenly, and this document should say so rather than assume even coverage.** Marcion, Valentinian/Gnostic Christianity, and Montanism were "live, undefeated, contemporary neighbors" (Doc_01 §8.3; Doc_08, Force 2A-2), and the anxiety they generated (G05) is Strand-A-specific and single-voice attested (Section 4 above). If Chloe is calibrated toward Strand A, this force bears on her directly — a household hosting a gathering is exactly the site where "learning where the edges of a safe public identity lay" (Doc_05 §3.1) and where a rival teacher's own competing hospitality (and competing eucharist) would have to be assessed and refused. If she is calibrated toward Strand B, this specific force has no developed textual bearing on her at all within this world's own evidentiary base (Doc_04, G05, Strand-bound), though the general condition of legal precarity (G03, cross-strand) still applies.

**State legal precarity is a force that lands, with unusual directness, on exactly the household setting Chloe's role is built around.** A gathering in a home is the physical site this world's own outside evidence describes state pressure actually reaching: Pliny's letter describes information "extracted under torture from two enslaved women, called *ministrae*" — women serving inside a Christian household context, the closest analogue this world's evidence offers anywhere to a female functionary's position under direct legal threat (Doc_02 §6, §9; Doc_08, Force 2A-1). This is a grim rather than a flattering parallel, and this document names it as such rather than smoothing it into a heroic precedent: it is evidence of exposure and structural vulnerability, not of authority. It nonetheless confirms, from an outside and hostile source, that household-level gatherings were the actual locus this world's external pressure bore down on — directly relevant to how Chloe's own risk-calculus and hospitality-management should be calibrated in Phase Two.

**The unevenness of these forces is itself a condition of possibility for the identity this build has chosen.** Doc_07's own forces synthesis makes a point directly useful here: this world's external pressures "never became severe or centralized enough to force one strand's answer to displace the other's before this world's own close" (Doc_07 §3; Doc_08 §5, "What Forces Analysis Adds"). A more severe, more centralized persecution, arriving earlier, could plausibly have collapsed this world's genuine plurality — including its household-based, non-monarchical formation logic — well before c. 200. The mildness and unevenness of external pressure is not incidental to Chloe's own viability as a Representative; it is a structural precondition of the very plurality that makes a household-leader voice (rather than only a bishop's or an apologist's) a defensible choice for this world at all.

## 7. Sufficiency Determination

**Does this world's ecology support constructing a living Representative at all?** Yes, and this finding rests on firm ground independently verified at Validation & Testing, not on this document's own assertion. This world clears Complexity Testing (`CiC_W1_Validation_Testing.md` §5), Emergence Testing (§6), Worship Integration Testing (§7), Differentiation Testing (§8), Author Dominance Testing (§9), and Anachronism Testing (§10) — all PASS, with every finding traceable to a specific, cited document and section, and every gap the build itself found already disclosed rather than concealed. The reasoning structure (Section 1), perception pattern (Section 2), and internal complexity (Section 4) this world supports are genuine, sourced, and — per the Complexity Testing pass specifically — sufficient to sustain ongoing engagement without collapsing into repetition. This is not a marginal or borderline world for Representative construction. Its own construction record shows extensive, real review activity across every prior document (Doc_01 through Doc_09, the World Profile, and Validation & Testing each carry independently verified review rounds — see each document's own Document Log), though this assessment does not have comparative data on other world-builds and does not claim this is uniquely or unusually thorough relative to them — corrected here per independent cold review (see Document Log).

**Does it specifically support the household-leader/Grapte-type identity already chosen?** Yes, with two real, named qualifications that Phase Two must address explicitly rather than paper over.

**First: the household is a well-argued structural fact, but not a theorized category in this world's own primary voices.** Section 3 and Section 5.3 above establish this directly: Doc_04's own testing of household-as-basic-unit (G06) found it does not reach gravity status, because the classic household-code texts are not among this world's six primary voices and no primary voice uses *oikos* as a technical self-designation (Doc_04, G06; Doc_03 §3). This does not mean households were unimportant to how these communities actually lived and met — the structural argument for that (Doc_01 §4, drawing on Meeks, Gehring, Balch, Osiek and MacDonald) is well-evidenced and this document does not dispute it. It means Phase Two should build Chloe's household setting and domestic texture explicitly from that structural, secondary-scholarship-grounded argument, disclosed as such, rather than implying this world's own letter-writers left a developed picture of household religious life they did not leave.

**Second: the Grapte peg is real and specific, but strand-located, and Chloe's own strand-position is not yet settled by this ecology or by the Identity Decision itself.** Section 4 above surfaces this as a genuine complication, not previously stated in these terms elsewhere in the build: Grapte's own textual context (Hermas, *Vision* 2.4.3) places her inside Strand B's plural-presbyter polity, while the household-host role more generally, and its clearest textual anchor (Ignatius's own household salutations), sits inside material generated by a Strand A, single-bishop-oriented voice. These are not incompatible — a household leader could plausibly exist in either strand's communities, and household hospitality itself is not shown to be strand-specific — but the *specific instructional and cross-community-transmission function* borrowed from Grapte belongs, in this world's own evidence, to the non-monarchical strand. Building Chloe without deciding this explicitly risks a Representative whose authority-structure surroundings are quietly ambiguous in a way this world's own evidence is not: this world holds its authority tension openly and by name (World Profile §7, "Held, not resolved"), and Chloe's own position relative to that tension should be held with the same explicitness, not left as an unexamined default.

**What this document recommends Phase Two (Formation Calibration) address explicitly, rather than inherit by default:**

1. **Decide, and disclose, Chloe's own strand position** — Strand A, Strand B, or a deliberately-named composite/cross-strand figure — since this determines which authority structure she lives inside, which emotional-danger register (docetic doubt versus mercy-exhaustion, World Profile §7) is her own, and how directly the Grapte peg fits her polity context (Section 4 above).
2. **Calibrate her relationship to the letter-writers' own arguments (anti-docetic polemic, succession theology) as a lived, carried, non-authored inheritance** — consistent with, and no more confident than, the Identity Decision's own stated calibration (Section 1, Section 5.6 above) — rather than allowing the vividness of Ignatian material (repeatedly flagged throughout this build as this world's own most quotable and most concentrated-risk content — Doc_02 §1.8; World Profile §8) to migrate into Chloe's own voice as though she had authored it.
3. **Build her domestic and hospitality texture explicitly from the structural, secondary-scholarship argument (Doc_01 §4), disclosed as such**, given the genuine thinness of primary-voice household detail established in Section 5.3.
4. **Carry the two-tier confidence distinction in the "argued, not inherited" master frame** (Section 1 above; Doc_07 §2E) into Chloe's own formation logic, so that her manner of holding open questions about office and embodiment (genuinely, historically contested) is not flattened into the same register as her manner of holding open questions about the scope of state pressure or the representativeness of martyrdom (limits of modern reconstruction, not ancient debate).

None of these four items represents a gap this world's ecology cannot support — each is a real, present, well-evidenced feature of this world's own recoverable life. What they share is that this Ecology Assessment found them requiring an explicit design decision rather than a default inheritance, and naming them here is this document's own discharge of that responsibility, consistent with the discipline this entire build has followed since Doc_01: disclose the dependency, rather than let a downstream document discover it by accident.
