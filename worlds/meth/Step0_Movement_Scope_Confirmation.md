## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas VII.5, "The Methodist Revival," 1738–1815 (census `dateRationale`, below), Britain then America. John Wesley (1703–1791) as the movement's central organizing figure; his brother Charles Wesley (1707–1788) as its principal hymn-writer; George Whitefield (1714–1770) as its most powerful open-air preacher and Wesley's own Calvinist counterpart; Francis Asbury (1745–1816) as the figure who carried the connexion across the American frontier and shaped it into an independent church there.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, per this project's own standing convention for every unbuilt candidate: this document's own Section A "clears" are a considered signal for this build thread's own use in proceeding, not a claim of independent verification beyond what is stated below.

### A1 — The floor: clears cleanly

This window sits 1,357 years after the Council of Constantinople (381) and 1,287 years after Chalcedon (451), so A1 is the direct, uncomplicated test. **It clears without qualification.** John Wesley was an ordained priest of the Church of England from 1728 to his death, and never formally left it; the Methodist connexion he built operated for its entire 1738–1791 core span as a movement of societies within the Established Church, governed doctrinally by the Thirty-Nine Articles (Wesley's own 1784 abridgment for American use, the Twenty-Four Articles, retains the Trinitarian and Christological articles unaltered — Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence; this pass did not independently verify the Twenty-Four Articles' own text against a vendored primary source, since it is not among the sources vendored this pass, see §4 and Doc_02). No floor complication of any kind is known for Wesley, Charles Wesley, Whitefield, or Asbury: all four are securely, uncontroversially Trinitarian and Chalcedonian in the tradition's own broad Anglican/Protestant confessional inheritance. This is Widely Accepted historical fact, not a contested reading.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity

Not applicable — this window opens nearly a millennium and a half after Nicaea.

### A3 — Contemporary movements

Real and disclosed, several genuine boundary questions rather than one:

1. **The Calvinistic Methodist / Whitefieldian wing.** George Whitefield preached the same "new birth" doctrine as Wesley and is named in this world's own census entry as "Wesley's Calvinist counterpart and friendly rival." The two men's doctrine of grace diverged sharply on predestination and the possibility of falling from grace: Wesley preached free grace and full sanctification (Christian Perfection); Whitefield held to Calvinist election. The breach became public in 1739–41 (Wesley's sermon "Free Grace," 1739, and the pamphlet exchange that followed) and was never fully healed, though the two men remained personally cordial and Wesley preached at Whitefield's funeral (1770, per Widely Accepted historical record; not independently checked against a primary vendored source this pass). The Welsh Methodist revival (Howell Harris, Daniel Rowland, from the 1730s) is a related, largely Calvinistic, and organizationally separate movement from the same broad "Evangelical Revival." **This is a real Article 21 strand-or-separate-movement question, not resolved here** — carried forward as a binding item for Doc_01 (§4 below).
2. **The Moravian relationship — the single most consequential contemporary-movement question this document surfaces, per this build's own launch instruction.** Wesley's own Aldersgate experience (24 May 1738 — this world's own doorway event, verified directly against the vendored Curnock Journal, see Doc_02) took place at a religious society meeting where, per Wesley's own account, "one was reading Luther's preface to the Epistle to the Romans." That society sat within London's Moravian-influenced Fetter Lane circle; three months later, in August–September 1738, Wesley traveled to Herrnhut itself to meet Count Zinzendorf and the Moravian community there — Widely Accepted historical fact, not independently checked against a primary vendored source this pass. The relationship soured within two years: Wesley broke from the Fetter Lane Society in 1740 over the Moravians' own "stillness" doctrine (that a soul lacking full assurance should cease outward religious observance until grace came), a real, documented doctrinal rupture, not a personality dispute alone. **The census records `the-moravian-church-at-herrnhut` as Atlas VII.4 — a separate, "Pre-Survey Candidate" entry with no corpus-map bucket or vendored texts of its own, confirmed 2026-09-25.** This document does not decide, and this build thread is not authorized to decide, how (or whether) that world's eventual own build should characterize its relationship to this one — it is named here as a documented cross-world relationship and carried to `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (item 2) as a genuine open question for a future Moravian build thread or the project lead, exactly as this build's own instructions require.
3. **The broader Evangelical Revival within the Church of England** (non-Methodist figures such as the Countess of Huntingdon's Connexion, itself Calvinistic) sits adjacent to, and partly overlapping with, this candidate — not resolved here, flagged for Doc_01.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing: strong, with real and specific disclosed gaps

This pass independently host-verified and vendored five works, seven files, direct into `cic/texts/` (full detail and rights basis in Doc_02 and `cic/texts/REGISTRY.yaml`):

- **John Wesley's Journal**, the standard scholarly Curnock edition (8 vols., 1909–1916) — **Volume I only** (birth through the Aldersgate entry, 24 May 1738). Volumes II–VIII, covering essentially the entire working span of the revival (1738–1790), are **not vendored** — the single largest disclosed acquisition gap this pass leaves open.
- **Wesley's Sermons on Several Occasions, Vol. I** (Bristol, 1771) — twelve of the Forty-Four Standard Sermons, including "Salvation by Faith" and "The Almost Christian." The remaining ~32 sermons (in the collection's later volumes) are not vendored this pass.
- **The Large Minutes** (Minutes of Several Conversations..., an 1850 reprint of an 1797 London printing) — the connexion's own cumulative catechism-form discipline document. This is **not** the annual Conference Minutes proper (published yearly from 1744) nor the final 1789 edition issued in Wesley's own lifetime; both remain unvendored, disclosed gaps.
- **Charles Wesley's hymns**: *A Collection of Hymns, for the Use of the People Called Methodists* (1780) — the movement's theology in sung form, 525 hymns, verified genuinely present in this specific edition rather than assumed from later, better-known Methodist hymnals.
- **Francis Asbury's Journal**, the complete original 1821 first American edition, all three volumes (1771–1815) — genuinely strong, complete coverage of the American connexion's own founding figure across this world's entire American window, explicitly **not** the still-copyrighted 1958 critical edition.

**Not acquired this pass, and named plainly rather than silently dropped (full detail in Doc_02 and `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md`):** any primary text by George Whitefield (journals or sermons); the annual Conference Minutes proper; class/band records, class papers, society and circuit records (the census's own description of this world's "lived organizational core" — genuinely unvendored); Mary Bosanquet Fletcher's 1771 letter; Hester Ann Rogers's *Account*; the *Arminian Magazine* (1778–) lay narratives; Richard Allen's conversion narrative (not published until 1833, an era-edge case the census itself flags); the Wesley–Crosby correspondence (1761).

**A genuine host-verification correction, disclosed rather than silently repeated:** an initial web search summary misidentified one Internet Archive identifier (`journalofrevfran00asbu`) as Asbury's Journal Volume I; direct inspection of the vendored file's own title page showed it to in fact be Volume II. Corrected on direct inspection before vendoring, per this project's own verify-against-the-host discipline (full detail in `cic/texts/REGISTRY.yaml`).

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six-plus already-built and in-build worlds:** no era overlap with any patristic-era world (all a millennium or more earlier); no figure overlap with the Reformed Cities (Zurich/Geneva, VI.2, 1519–1650) or Lutheran Wittenberg, though a real doctrinal echo exists — Whitefield's own Calvinism (§2 A3.1) restates, within this movement's own contemporary-movements boundary, the identical predestination question the Reformed tradition argues on its own terms a full two centuries earlier; Doc_02 should note the echo without claiming any institutional link. **Versus VII.4 (the Moravian Church at Herrnhut):** real, substantial, and disclosed overlap at the level of formative influence and a shared founding-adjacent event (§2 A3.2) — not movement identity; the two remain, on all evidence surveyed, genuinely separate ecclesial bodies from 1740 onward.

### B4 — Audiences

A genuinely distinct pull versus every other world this portfolio has built or is building: field-preaching to the unchurched urban and industrial poor (Kingswood's colliers, Moorfields, Kennington Common) who were, by the census's own account, "shut out of ordinary church pulpits" — an audience-and-method combination (mass open-air preaching paired with small-group weekly accountability) with no real precedent among this project's already-built worlds.

### B5 — Scale

Britain, Ireland, and America within this world's own 1738–1815 window; by the census's own `legacy` field, the tradition "became one of the largest church families in the world" through its own later descendants (Holiness, Pentecostalism, the AME/AME Zion churches) — named here for orientation only, since descendant-tradition scale is not this world's own window-scale, a distinction this document keeps rather than blurs.

**Tier 1 — Strong seed, with two binding conditions carried forward, not smoothed over.** Sourcing is strong overall but carries a specific, serious volume-level gap (seven of eight Journal volumes; the great majority of the Sermons; the annual Conference Minutes proper; every ordinary-member voice source the census itself names) that Doc_02 must state as a disclosed acquisition limit, not imply as closed.
