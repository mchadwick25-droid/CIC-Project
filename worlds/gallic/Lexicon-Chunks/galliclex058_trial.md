## Quick Meaning

For this world a trial is God's proving of love — "the Lord your God trieth you, to know whether you love Him" — applied by Vincent to the whole Church when "certain excellent persons, and of position in the Church, are often permitted by God to preach novel doctrines," by Cassian to the individual will God tests as He tested Job, and by Sulpitius to Martin, who "was indeed tried by that danger" of the fire "but passed through it with true acceptance."

---

## World Meaning

Vincent asks the hard question about Origen and Tertullian. "How is it then, that certain excellent persons, and of position in the Church, are often permitted by God to preach novel doctrines to Catholics?" The answer is Moses's: "one holding office as a Doctor in the Church, who is believed by his disciples or auditors to teach by revelation" may arise with signs and wonders, and yet — "For the Lord, your God, trieth you, to know whether you love Him with all your heart and with all your soul." The more learned the erring teacher, the greater the trial: Origen, "by far the first place among the Greeks," and Tertullian among the Latins, are the examples "in order that all true Catholics may understand that it behoves them with the Church to receive Teachers, not with Teachers to desert the faith of the Church." A great teacher's fall is not a scandal to be explained away but a test set for the people.

Cassian's Chaeremon reads the same verse of the single soul. God "makes trial of the strength of man's will" — as in Job, "His well tried athlete" — and Deuteronomy's "for the Lord your God surely trieth thee, whether thou lovest Him with all thine heart" is the reason God does not always remove temptation but lets the will fight, helped. And at Tours the word is spoken of a night in a burning sacristy: "let every one who reads this letter understand that Martin was indeed tried by that danger, but passed through it with true acceptance," having sought "assistance from the cross" only when his own strength failed at the door. One verse; three rooms; in each, a love proved by what is permitted against it.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A trial as an ordeal or misfortune only; "temptation" as enticement to sin; a court trial; or the erring teacher as a problem for the Church's credibility.

**World Hearing:**
God's proving of love — of a people through a learned teacher's novelty, of a will through a permitted assault, of a saint through fire — in which the greater the teacher or the danger, the greater the proof required, and in which the right response is to hold to the Church, fight with help, or return to the cross.
