## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, mortification is the state of being "dead to this world and its deeds and desires," figured as crucifixion — "as then one who is crucified no longer has the power of moving or turning his limbs in any direction as he pleases, so we also ought to affix our wishes and desires" — and read into the monk's very clothes, the girdle of dead skin that "signifies that he bears about the mortification of those members in which are contained the seeds of lust."

---

## World Meaning

Abbot Pinufius gives it to the new monk as the meaning of what has just happened to him: "our renunciation is nothing but mortification and the image of the Crucified. Renunciation is nothing but the evidence of the cross and of mortification. And so you must know that to-day you are dead to this world and its deeds and desires, and that, as the Apostle says, you are crucified to this world and this world to you." Then the figure is worked out: "The fear of the Lord is our cross. As then one who is crucified no longer has the power of moving or turning his limbs in any direction as he pleases, so we also ought to affix our wishes and desires—not in accordance with what is pleasant and delightful to us now, but in accordance with the law of the Lord." The dead man's freedom is the point: no lust, no property, no will of his own to turn on the nails. The monk's dress says the same. The girdle of dead skin "signifies that he bears about the mortification of those members in which are contained the seeds of lust and lasciviousness"; the linen garment and sleeveless tunic "teach that they are dead to all earthly conversation." And humility's first sign is "if a man has all his desires mortified." Mortification is not a practice among others but the condition every practice presupposes: a man already dead cannot be tempted by what the living want.

At Tours the body is mortified without the word — sackcloth, ashes, "hunger, in watchings, in nakedness, in fastings" — as the weapons of a soldier and the martyrdom of a man denied the sword. The south names the state; the north displays the practice.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Self-harm, self-hatred, or flagellation; "mortification" as embarrassment; asceticism as contempt for the body.

**World Hearing:**
A death already accomplished — "crucified to this world" — that frees the monk from the pull of what the living desire, worn in a girdle and a tunic, presupposed by every practice, and at Tours shown rather than named in sackcloth and fasting.
