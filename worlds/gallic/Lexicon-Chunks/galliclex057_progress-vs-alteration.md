## Quick Meaning

At Lérins the answer to "Shall there, then, be no progress in Christ's Church?" is "Certainly; all possible progress" — "yet on condition that it be real progress, not alteration of the faith. For progress requires that the subject be enlarged in itself, alteration, that it be transformed into something else": the grown man has "the same number of joints" he had as a child.

---

## World Meaning

Vincent asks the objection himself and answers it without flinching. "But some one will say, perhaps, Shall there, then, be no progress in Christ's Church? Certainly; all possible progress. For what being is there, so envious of men, so full of hatred to God, who would seek to forbid it? Yet on condition that it be real progress, not alteration of the faith. For progress requires that the subject be enlarged in itself, alteration, that it be transformed into something else. The intelligence, then, the knowledge, the wisdom, as well of individuals as of all, as well of one man as of the whole Church, ought, in the course of ages and centuries, to increase and make much and vigorous progress; but yet only in its own kind; that is to say, in the same doctrine, in the same sense, and in the same meaning." Then the pictures: the body, which grows from infancy to age while remaining one body — "men when full grown have the same number of joints that they had when children"; the seed, from which the harvest springs true to kind. And the practice: councils, pressed by heresy, have done nothing more than commit to writing what they received from the ancients, "and often, for the better understanding, designating an old article of the faith by the characteristic of a new name" — the deity of the Lord by the word *Homousios*. Growth is enlargement; a new word may serve an old truth; a new truth is alteration, and alteration is the heretic's work.

The thought is Vincent's own and belongs to Lérins. Cassian's one addition to the office — the new morning service, argued for as the elders' decision "after long discussion" — is progress in this sense without the word; Martin's refusal of an unattested cult is the refusal of alteration. But the distinction, and the body's joints, are the *Commonitory*'s.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Development of doctrine" as Newman's theory, or its opposite — Vincent as a charter for change or as a proof-text against all change; "progress" as improvement or novelty.

**World Hearing:**
Enlargement without transformation — the same body grown, the same seed harvested, the same faith under a clarifying new name — sanctioned "in the same doctrine, in the same sense, and in the same meaning," and refused the moment it becomes "something else."
