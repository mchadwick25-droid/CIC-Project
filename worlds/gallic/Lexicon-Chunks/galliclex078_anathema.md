## Quick Meaning

At Lérins anathema is Paul's word in Galatians as Vincent reads it — "let him be accursed," "i.e., separated, segregated, excluded, lest the dire contagion of a single sheep contaminate the guiltless flock of Christ" — and a standing duty: "to anathematize those who preach anything other than what has once been received, always was a duty, always is a duty, always will be a duty."

---

## World Meaning

Vincent lingers on the apostle's "though we, or an angel from heaven": "Tremendous severity! He spares neither himself nor his fellow apostles." The word's force is exclusion — "not 'let him be honoured, let him be praised, welcomed,'—no; but 'let him be accursed,' [anathema] i.e., separated, segregated, excluded, lest the dire contagion of a single sheep contaminate the guiltless flock of Christ by his poisonous intermixture with them." And its permanence is the rule's: "to preach any doctrine ... other than that which has been received never was lawful, never is lawful, never will be lawful: and to anathematize those who preach anything other than what has once been received, always was a duty, always is a duty, always will be a duty." The list follows — "Accursed then be Photinus," and the rest. At Tours the word appears once, in a bad priest's mouth: shut out by a virgin who received no man, he "would have resolved that she should be laid under an anathema" — the sanction of exclusion turned, wrongly, on the one who kept herself apart.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:** "anathema" heard as a loose, general-purpose synonym for strong disapproval or condemnation.

**World Hearing:** it is a specific, permanent formula of exclusion, bound to Galatians 1:8's own severity and applied even against an apostle or an angel who preaches otherwise — and, at Tours, its one appearance shows the sanction turned wrongly, in a bad priest's mouth, against a virgin who had done nothing but keep herself apart.
