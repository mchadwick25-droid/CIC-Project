## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, discretion is the virtue by which every other is kept from destroying itself — "the mother of all virtues, as well as their guardian and regulator," the "eye and light of the body," the "royal road" between excess and slackness — gained "only ... by true humility," that is, by testing every thought, like a good money-changer testing coin, against the judgment of the elders.

---

## World Meaning

The teaching comes as a verdict. Abbot Moses reports a night's council in the desert at which the old men debated which virtue leads most surely to God, and "by the judgment of the blessed Antony as well as of all others it has been laid down that it is discretion which leads a fearless monk by fixed stages to God, and preserves the virtues mentioned above continually intact." Every other virtue fails without it: the men who fasted, watched, and gave alms most fiercely are the ones who fell, because "they who toil most willingly cannot reach the heights of perfection. For discretion is the mother of all virtues, as well as their guardian and regulator." It "is no earthly thing and no slight matter, but the greatest prize of divine grace." It is "termed in the gospel the 'eye,' 'and light of the body'"; it "teaches a monk always to walk along the royal road," neither "puffed up on the right hand of virtue" nor slack "with the opposite spirit of luke-warmness"; and its rule is that "extremes meet. For excess of fasting and gluttony come to the same thing." The proof is old Heron, "a man whom we remember to have lived for fifty years in this desert" practising "incessant abstinence and fasting with such severity," who, "having too little of the virtue of discretion ... preferred to be guided by his own judgment rather than to obey the counsels and conference of the brethren and the regulations of the elders," and so "received with the utmost reverence an angel of Satan as an angel of light and with blind slavishness obeyed his commands and cast himself down a well."

Where it comes from is the heart of the teaching. "True discretion, said he, is only secured by true humility. And of this humility the first proof is given by reserving everything (not only what you do but also what you think), for the scrutiny of the elders" — "the power of discretion which comes from the words and warnings of the fathers." The monk is to be a "good money-changer": to test whether a thought is real gold, whether it bears the right stamp — "stamped by those who have the right to coin, i.e., the approved Catholic fathers" — whether it is light with "some conceit of novelty." Discretion is not a private faculty but the elders' judgment made one's own. At Tours the thing is done without the name: Martin refuses Anatolius's robe and the purple-robed "Christ" — "I will not believe that Christ has come, unless he appears with that appearance and form in which he suffered" — but no one there calls it discretion or forms disciples by it.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Tact, prudence, or confidentiality; "discernment" as a private spiritual intuition or a decision-making technique; moderation as common sense.

**World Hearing:**
The regulator of all the virtues, awarded that title by a council of Egyptian fathers, whose whole substance is the elders' judgment received through humility and disclosure — a money-changer's test of every thought against "the approved Catholic fathers," on a royal road where "extremes meet"; a gift of grace, not a temperament; and at Tours the thing itself in Martin's refusals, without the word.
