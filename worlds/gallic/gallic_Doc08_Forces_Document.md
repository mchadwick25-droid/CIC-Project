### CELL 1A — INITIATING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external conditions made this particular form of Christianity possible, necessary, or urgent at its origin?

#### Force 1A-1: The Egyptian standard arriving in Gaul — as a rule to receive (south) and a measure to match (north)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
Egyptian and Palestinian monastic custom reached Gaul in two ways that the two nodes' texts record separately. In the south it arrived as a written program at a bishop's request: Cassian's *Institutes* Preface (`iv.ii`) addresses Castor, "anxious that the institutions of the East and especially of Egypt should be established in your province, which is at present without monasteries," and undertakes to set out "the customs of the monasteries which we have seen observed throughout Egypt and Palestine, as they were there delivered to us by the Fathers," adapting only where the rule "either because of the severity of the climate, or owing to some difficulty or diversity of habits, is impossible in these countries," supplementing "by the customs of the monasteries which are found throughout Pontus and Mesopotamia," and hoping the work will serve "a new establishment in the West, in the parts of Gaul." The program arrived as a standing correction of what Gaul already did: "we cannot recollect any one who joined our monasteries keeping it up unbroken even for a year" (*Inst.* IV.2, `iv.iii.iv.ii`); those "in this country" who "make haste to prostrate themselves for prayer, in their hurry to finish the service" (II.7); the Gallic Gloria "we have never heard anywhere throughout the East" (II.8); those who "go back again to bed after their Mattin prayers are finished" (III.5). Gallic monks were to receive "into their cells the authors of the Conferences" so as not to be left to find a way "difficult and almost unknown in this country" (*Conf.* Pref. III, `iv.vi.i`). At Lérins, Eucherius names John and Macarius as the pattern of a life "made in heaven" while in the deserts (*De Laude Eremi* §27, row 26, file line 445, Doc_04 G2's rendering, wording Inferential/Thin), and Gennadius (ch. LXII, ancient text) confirms that Cassian "compiled Conferences with the Egyptian fathers." In the north the standard arrived a generation earlier and without Cassian, as a rival to be matched: Gennadius describes the *Dialogues*' subject as "the manner of life of the oriental monks and of St. Martin" (ch. XIX); Postumianus — the traveller just returned from Egypt — concedes to Gallus, "you have conquered, O Gaul ... you have conquered all the eremites and anchorites" (*Dial.* II.5, `ii.iv.ii.v`; corrected, Round 1 review finding 5 — the prior draft reversed speaker and addressee; the Egyptian visitor's own concession is the stronger fact for this force), and "Europe will not yield to it, or to all Asia, in having only Martin" (*Dial.* III.17, `ii.iv.iii.xvii`). *Dial.* I (row 4) is Excluded and not used. **Confidence:** Documented for the southern receptive mode (Cassian's own preface; Eucherius; Gennadius); Widely Accepted for the northern comparative mode (one voice, structurally confirmed by Gennadius) — exactly Doc_04 G2's split rating.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
In the south, the customs of the monasteries came as the fathers' own, handed down, and the house received them as a debtor receives what it cannot repay. The brethren knew themselves to be the colder, weaker, later people: a province "frozen as it were with the cold of Gaul" (*Conf.* Pref. II), where one of the holy brothers wished to go to Egypt to see the fathers in the flesh; where no one had kept the fathers' perseverance a whole year; where the Gloria was sung loud by all, a thing the East never heard. The book was the father in the cell: one talked with the elders of Egypt "by daily questions and answers" because the living way was almost unknown here. To be formed was to be corrected by a custom older and better than one's own, and to hold that custom as "no appointment of man's invention" but the fathers' tradition. In the north the fathers of Egypt were a glory the brethren had heard of, and Martin was Gaul's answer to it: let Egypt, "justly proud of the numbers and virtues of its own saints," hear that Europe would not yield in having only Martin. The Tours brethren did not receive Egypt's customs; they matched Egypt's saints. [Reported-Experience Status applies to the northern sentence — one hagiographer — and to the Lérins sentence — rough OCR.]

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**
This is the initiating force of the whole southern ecology and the measuring-pole of the northern one — G2 (Primary, split-mode) in Doc_04's own terms. In the south it supplied the *content* of formation — G7's interior road, the office (Doc_05 §3), the entrance sequence (Doc_05 §2.1), dress and the fault-list — all of it received (Doc_04 §7; Doc_05 §7.2), and it supplied the *manner* of formation: a program read in the teacher's absence, which is where G5 begins its shift from heard to read a generation before Vincent (Doc_05 §2.1). It pressed the community toward the vocabulary of reception ("the customs of the monasteries," chunk 005; "the fathers," 004) and produced the affect Doc_05 §1.4 named: a mild shame at being the frozen province, and the standing fear of cooling (Cell 2B-5). It also planted the doctrinal seed: the received program presupposes that effort matters, which is the internal logic Cell 2B-1 documents and the external pressure of Cell 2A-1 acts on. In the north it produced G6's comparative frame — Martin's *virtutes* told so as to outdo Egypt's — and gave the north no program, which is why Tours documents "a place and a person" and the south "a program" (Doc_05 §2.2). The two modes are not one impact: Egypt let in at Marseilles and matched from Tours are the "permeable inward"/"comparative outward" boundary Doc_05 §7.2 drew.

#### Force 1A-2: The late-Roman army and civil rank as the "before" of every founding biography

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
Each founding narrative at each house begins with a departure from Roman service or rank. Martin, a soldier, "regarded not so much as being a soldier as a monk" (*Vita* ch. II, `ii.ii.iii`), is discharged before Caesar at the moment the barbarians "were rushing within the two divisions of Gaul": "Hitherto I have served you as a soldier: allow me now to become a soldier to God ... I am the soldier of Christ: it is not lawful for me to fight" (*Vita* ch. IV, `ii.ii.v`), and goes out "protected by the sign of the cross, and not by shield or helmet." Vincent comes from "the manifold and deplorable tempests of secular warfare" to "cast anchor in the harbour of religion" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2], `iii.ii`). Honoratus and his brother, in Hilary of Arles's Latin, could not reach obscurity because their nobility would not permit it (row 27, Doc_05 §1.1's rendering, wording Inferential/Thin). Cassian's junior at Marseilles is a man whose worldly clothes the steward keeps "until by different sorts of temptations and trials they can recognize the excellence of his progress" (*Inst.* IV.6, `iv.iii.iv.vi`). Gennadius's text corroborates the north's founding shape: "a Life of the holy Martin, monk and bishop" (ch. XIX). **Confidence:** Documented (the texts' own founding scenes, two voices independently; Hilary's Latin at Inferential/Thin for wording).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
The renunciant did not stop being a soldier; he changed service. He had taken "the oath of allegiance to Christ" (*Ep.* III) as he had once taken Caesar's; his dress was armour and his day a campaign; a fault was a combat and cooling was desertion — "a runaway from His service, and a deserter" (*Inst.* X.3). The world he left was kept in a cupboard against the day he might want it back. The man who came to Lérins had come out of the world's warfare into a harbour; the man who came to Marmoutier had told Caesar to his face that it was not lawful for him to fight. Rank, too, had to be forced down: men "such as are deemed of noble rank ... had forced themselves down to this degree of humility" (*Vita* ch. X). [Reported-Experience Status for the Tours sentences, one hagiographer.]

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**
This force gave both nodes their idiom (G8, Supporting, cross-node — literal at Tours, dress-mysticism at Marseilles, per Doc_04) and gave G1 its first half, the entry act of renunciation (Doc_04 §2.2 folded renunciation into G1). It made the formed person's self-understanding a change of allegiance rather than a change of nature, which is why the north's biography hinges on a discharge and the south's manual opens "A monk, then, as a soldier of Christ" (*Inst.* I.1). It is also the reason the world's withdrawal is modest and its exit an office: a man who changes service does not leave the service; he can be posted. The connection to Cell 1B-1 is the closest in the matrix (Section 4, Connection 4).

#### Force 1A-3: The unconverted Gallic countryside (Tours)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
The northern node was founded facing a rural population Sulpitius describes as unconverted: "before the times of Martin, very few, nay, almost none, in those regions had received the name of Christ," and "wherever he destroyed heathen temples, there he used immediately to build either churches or monasteries" (*Vita* ch. XIII, `ii.ii.xiv`); a whole heathen crowd is made catechumens "by placing his hand upon the whole of them" (*Dial.* II.4, `ii.iv.ii.iv`). No corresponding mission-field appears in Cassian, Vincent, Hilary, or Eucherius as read; the south's outward work is teaching by writing and hospitality (Doc_05 §5). **Confidence:** Widely Accepted as to the mission pattern (one voice, Sulpitius; not corroborated by Gennadius beyond Martin's fame "for signs and wonders and virtues," ch. XIX). The historicity of particular episodes is not what this force asserts and is not rated (Stancliffe, row 18, unread).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
The countryside was heathen and the saint's power was for it. Where a temple came down a church or a monastery went up; the possessed were brought to him; a crowd on a plain, seeing a dead boy restored, "began to rush in crowds to the knees of the blessed man, sincerely imploring that he would make them Christians" (*Dial.* II.4). The brethren at Marmoutier were formed by watching this done before their eyes, and the churches that later took them wanted priests from among those who had watched. [Reported-Experience Status — one hagiographer.]

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**
Node-bound northern. This force is a principal reason the north's literature is made of *virtutes* (G6, Tensional, node-bound northern): power was needed outward, for the rustics, and the disciple was formed by seeing it used (G5 in its seen medium, Doc_05 §2.1; Doc_07 §5's seen/read axis). It has no southern counterpart, and this is one of the places where the two nodes' literatures are made of what the other lacks (Section 4, Connection 8).

#### Force 1B-1: Renunciation that does not leave the Church — the founding conviction

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
The community at each house brought with it, from its first attested moment, the conviction that withdrawal from the world and continued life within the Church's ordinary reach were compatible — the conviction Doc_01 §1 makes this world's distinct-world criterion and Doc_04 G1 finds Documented cross-node. At Tours the founder is "monk and bishop" (Gennadius ch. XIX) and "kept up the position of a bishop properly, yet in such a way as not to lay aside the objects and virtues of a monk" (*Vita* ch. X, `ii.ii.xi`); Marmoutier sits across the river from the bishop's own city. In the south, Cassian's whole output is addressed to bishops or to men who became bishops (*Inst.* Pref. to Castor; *Conf.* Prefs. I–III; Doc_04 G1), and the Egyptian counter-case he chooses to carry west is a bishop — Archebius, "carried off from the assembly of anchorites and given as Bishop to the town of Panephysis," who "complained that he had been expelled from the monastic system as unworthy of it" (*Conf.* XI.2, `iv.v.ii.ii`). At Lérins the founding sermon says of Honoratus, in Hilary's Latin, that "he who had refused to go to the dignity — the dignity came to him" (*ad ipsum dignitas venit*, row 27, file line 716, Doc_04 G1's rendering, Inferential/Thin for wording; presence checkable by grep). Vincent writes from "the seclusion of a Monastery" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]) and appeals to "the twofold authority of the Apostolic See" (ch. 32 [84], `iii.xxxiii`). **Confidence:** Documented (multiple independent ancient witnesses: Sulpitius, Cassian's own dedications, Hilary of Arles, Gennadius).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
To renounce the world was not to leave the Church. The monk went out of the city, across a river or onto an island, into a desert near the city, and the Church did not let go of him: the churches wanted their priests from among Martin's brethren, and the fillet fastened on the fugitive. A bishop could keep "the objects and virtues of a monk"; a monk who was made bishop had not left the service but been posted. The brethren did not experience this as a contradiction but as the shape of the life — fled, and overtaken. What the world could not conceive was the monk who was his own master: the Sarabaite, who makes "renunciation only as a public profession" and remains "their own masters" (*Conf.* XVIII.7), is the world's name for renunciation that leaves everything, including the fathers.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**
This conviction grounds G1 (Primary) directly, gives G9 (Tensional) its field — one cannot flee bishops and be made one without a conviction that both are possible — and explains, with 1A-2, why this world's withdrawal is "geographically modest" (Doc_01 §7; Doc_04 G1's Explanatory test). It made the community inclined toward the office as destiny and resistant to the desire for it (Cell 2B-3); it made every founding story a capture (Doc_07 §2C); and it made the world's ending a change of register rather than a collapse (Cell 3A-1, Cell 3B-1). It also grounds the world's one Native temporal horizon in the founding voices — Vincent's "awful expectation of the approach of the divine judgment" that "demands increased earnestness in religion" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]) is the motive of a man who has come into the harbour and means to stay, and Cassian's "fear of hell," one of Chaeremon's three restraints alongside hope of reward and love of virtue (*Conf.* XI.6, `iv.v.ii.vi`, corrected, Round 1 review finding 8 — the prior draft cited the same chapter against the wrong div, `iv.iv.v.ii`, actually *Conf.* IV.2), is the beginner's restraint inside the house — the thin, cross-node motif of G10 that exists independently of the barbarian force (Cell 2A-5).

#### Force 1B-2: Legitimacy by reception — antiquity, the Fathers, and consent against novelty

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
All three founding voices state, in their own words and from the outset, that what is legitimate is what was received. Cassian's *Institutes* Preface: the customs "as they were there delivered to us by the Fathers"; *Inst.* I.2 (`iv.iii.i.ii`), on sackcloth, that allegiance is owed "not to those customs and rules which the will of a few have introduced, but to those which a long standing antiquity and numbers of the holy fathers have passed on by an unanimous decision to those that come after"; *Inst.* II.3–4: monasteries stand "through a succession of fathers and their traditions," the office is "no appointment of man's invention," and the fault is to "appoint ourselves Abbots before we have, as we ought, professed ourselves disciples," being "readier to require the observance of our own inventions than to preserve the well-tried teaching of our predecessors"; *Conf.* Pref. I: "the Institutes which are not mine but the fathers'." Vincent: "universality, antiquity, consent" (*Comm.* ch. 2 [6], `iii.iii`); "prefer the decrees, if such there be, of an ancient General Council to the rashness and ignorance of a few" (ch. 3 [8]); Stephen's "Let there be no innovation" (ch. 6 [16], `iii.vii`); "not an author but a keeper, not a teacher but a disciple, not a leader but a follower" (ch. 22 [53], `iii.xxiii`); "be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a martyr" (ch. 28 [72], `iii.xxix`); and the same criterion applied to a council, Ephesus, whose bishops "innovated nothing, presumed nothing" (ch. 31 [82], `iii.xxxii`). Sulpitius: Martin refusing a cult because "no steady tradition respecting them had come down from antiquity," "lest a mere superstition should obtain a firmer footing" (*Vita* ch. XI, `ii.ii.xii`). **Confidence:** Documented — three independent voices state the criterion; Vincent's formulation is Documented to 434 by internal dating (Doc_01 §2.3). The Tours attestation is one episode against the south's systematic statement — an asymmetry of weight, not presence (Doc_04 G4).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
Nothing a man made up was binding. The number of psalms bound because an angel had sung it to the fathers; the customs bound because the fathers had handed them on with one voice; the faith bound because it had been held everywhere, always, by all. A teacher who held anything alone, "be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a martyr," held a private fancy. The brother was a keeper, not an author; he had received gold and must give gold; a *Commonitory* was written "with the fidelity of a narrator rather than the presumption of an author" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]). Even the saint, asked to honour a tomb, asked his elders whether any tradition had come down, and would not lend his authority to the crowd. #### Force 2A-1: The pressure from Africa and Rome on the grace teaching (Lérins–Marseilles)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
Augustine's later teaching, reported from Gaul, and the Roman see's response constitute the one sustained external doctrinal pressure this world's texts register — and they register it almost entirely through outside report and editorial apparatus, not in the world's own name. What the world held is Cassian's: "the grace of God always co-operates with our will for its advantage" (*Conf.* XIII.13, `iv.v.iv.xiii`); the double error of those who assert either side "more widely than is right" (XIII.11); "laid down by all the Catholic fathers who have taught perfection of heart not by empty disputes of words, but in deed and act" that "the choice of free will is open to either side" (XIII.18); the refusal, as "grievous blasphemy," of the view that "He does not generally will all men, but only some instead of all to be saved" (XIII.7, `iv.v.iv.vii`); and, on the other side, "the profane notion of some who attribute everything to free will" (XIII.16, `iv.v.iv.xvi`). What the outside reported is Augustine's: the brethren "have attained to the confession that men's wills are anticipated by God's grace" but "as yet they are in darkness on the question concerning the predestination of the saints" (*Praed.* ch. 2, `npnf105` `xxi.ii.ii`, row 15, context only; the chapter heading "To What Extent the Massilians Withdraw from the Pelagians" and Warfield's note that Prosper "called them *reliquiæ Pelagianorum*, i.e., 'the remnants of the Pelagians,'" are editorial). Gennadius (ch. And Heurtley's Appendix III (`iii.xxxvii`, editorial), read for the first time in this build, gives Celestine's own charge as Vincent quotes it at *Comm.* ch. 32 [85]: "Rebuke these people. **Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**

#### Force 2A-3: The imperial court sitting in an ecclesiastical cause — Trier (Tours)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
The Priscillianist affair brought Martin before the usurper Maximus at Trier, where "the priestly dignity had, with degenerate submissiveness, taken a second place to the royal retinue," while "in Martin alone, apostolic authority continued to assert itself" (*Vita* ch. #### Force 2A-4: The cold of Gaul (Lérins–Marseilles)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**

#### Force 2B-1: The received program's own logic — effort required and not sufficient (Lérins–Marseilles)

The brother fasted, watched, laboured, kept the cell, and laid every thought bare to the senior — and then, at the moment he felt the fast had done something, he was to say "Not I, but the grace of God with me." This was not a doctrine argued but a humility taught, and the fathers' own: God's mercy is given to the man who labours, and the labouring itself is not his. **Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**

#### Force 2B-3: The office as temptation and destiny inside the house

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
The community wrote the churches' drain (Cell 2A-2) into its own formation program as a fault and into its own stories as a fate. Vainglory "creates a wish to take holy orders, and a desire for the priesthood or diaconate" (*Inst.* XI.14, `iv.iii.xi.xiv`); the devil "incites a man to desire the holy office of the clergy under the pretext of edifying many people ... to draw us away from the humility and strictness of our life" (*Conf.* I.20, `iv.iv.ii.xx`); the proud monk "has grown cold through pride" and wants to rule (*Inst.* XII.30, title, `iv.iii.xii.xxx`). XXVII, `ii.ii.xxviii`). **Confidence:** Documented (both voices' own statements).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
To want the office was to have gone cold; to be given it was to be posted. The brethren were taught to suspect in themselves any wish to be set over others, and taught by their stories that the churches would come for them anyway. The bishop who was a monk did not use his office against the clerics who wronged him, and knew he had had more power as a monk than he had now. The house held both at once and did not feel it as contradiction: the thing one must not desire was the thing one would be seized for.

#### Force 2B-4: Power displayed against power disowned — *virtus* and humility, a tension the world did not experience as one

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**

#### Force 2B-5: Cooling — the fear of fervour lost (Lérins–Marseilles)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
