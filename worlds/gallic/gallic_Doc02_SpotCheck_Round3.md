## NEW ISSUES INTRODUCED BY THIS FIX ROUND

The pattern was worth testing for. It recurred, though at much lower severity than in Rounds 1 and 2.

1. **Rows 37/38's fix landed one column off** (see N12 above). This is the round's one genuinely new defect of consequence: an edit was made, it went into the wrong fields, and the log records the intended result rather than the actual one.

2. **§10(b) miscounts the Prefatory Note's category V.** Doc_02 says Letters 225/226 fall under the same policy "that excludes **six** other named letters (94, 109, 121, 160, 168, 230)." The Note's category V reads: "94, 109, 121, 160, 168, 225, 226, 230, **270**." Excluding 225/226 leaves **seven**, and 270 is dropped from the list. Cosmetic, but it is a count error of exactly the N13 shape, in the paragraph written to fix a counting-and-attribution failure.

3. **Registry row 31's letter counts conflict with the source quoted in the same cell.** The row states "168 of 269 Benedictine-edition letters are printed, 28 separate gaps." The Prefatory Note — which the same cell says was "read in full and quoted verbatim this revision" — says the Benedictine edition contains **272** and that **160** are translated. The 168/269 figures are the Round 2 reviewer's own count of the vendored file's printed sequence, adopted here and relabelled as Benedictine-edition figures. Minor, but it is a reviewer-sourced number presented as a source-verified one.

4. **An overgeneralisation about Gennadius's chapter endings.** The preamble (§ opening) says Gennadius "ends every chapter with a regnal formula and never a year-number," and §1.2 repeats "as all his chapters do"; Registry row 30 says "every chapter closes with a bare regnal formula." Chs. LXII, LXV and LXX do; **ch. XIX does not** — it ends on the Pelagian sentence with no regnal formula, as §1.1 of the same document correctly states four lines earlier. The load-bearing point (no year-numbers in the ancient text) is sound; the universal quantifier is not.

5. **A builder-supplied date inside a report of the prolegomena.** §1.2 describes "a **431** letter obtained by Prosper and Hilary from Pope Celestine." `npnf211`'s prolegomena, the cited source for that whole passage, gives no year for the letter — only that it followed Augustine's death (Aug 430) and that Celestine died April 432. 431 is the conventional date and is not wrong, but it is supplied from outside the source inside a sentence that reads as a report of it — the precise register-mixing the revision's own opening rule forbids. One clause of attribution fixes it.

6. **The declared "third editorial layer" has no Registry row.** The disposition asked that Richardson's `npnf203` apparatus be folded into row 17's Author Gravity or given its own. Doc_02 §2 gives it its own prose assessment, but the Registry does neither: row 17's Source field still names only Series II vol. 11 and Series I vol. 5, and row 30 remains a single Type P / Confidence A row for Gennadius. The apparatus is now used and assessed in Doc_02 without a row, which is the checkpoint rule's own failure mode.

7. *Nit:* §1.1 renders Roberts's dates as "about 363…about 420"; the title page reads "born in Aquitania about a.d. 363, and died, as is generally supposed, in a.d. 420."
