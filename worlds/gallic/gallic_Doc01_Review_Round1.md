## VERDICT: SUBSTANTIAL REVISION REQUIRED

**18 substantial findings, 12 cosmetic.**

The document is well-organised, unusually candid about its own limits, and does not commit the failure this project has seen before of omitting World Separation Criteria outright. But it fails on four fronts that go to substance:

1. It **quietly settles the one-world-or-two-worlds question** it says three separate times it is not settling — its temporal floor (§2.1) and its entry criterion (§5) both presuppose the answer.
2. Its **two central connective claims about the world's internal coherence are contradicted by this world's own vendored primary text**, which the document had in hand and did not check: the Institutes' dedication (§2.3, §3) and Sulpicius's Dialogues as cross-node evidence (§6).
3. It **carries a factual identification, "Hilary of Arles," that its own vendored source contradicts**, unlabelled, through five sections.
4. It **argues a c. 450 closing boundary without the single figure who bears most on it** — Faustus of Riez — and then calls the resulting 450–480 interval "undocumented."

Three of the findings below (S3, S4, S12) are arithmetic or textual errors that would have been caught by opening a file the document already cites.

## SUBSTANTIAL FINDINGS

### S1. §2.1 and §5 resolve the one-world-or-two-worlds question the document says it is reserving for Doc_04

**What's wrong.** §2.3, §6, §11 item 1 and the document's §12 escalation check all state that whether Martin's Tours and the Lérins–Marseilles circle are one world or a lineage of two is reserved for Doc_04. But §2.1 proposes "**c. 360 (Ligugé), not 372 (Marmoutier), as the working floor**" *for this world*, and §5's first question — "Has a new gravity emerged marking entry into this world?" — is answered "Yes … Martin's founding of Ligugé under Hilary's patronage (c. 360)." Both statements are only coherent if Martin's Tours is inside the same world as Lérins and Marseilles. If Doc_04 finds a lineage of two, this world's floor is c. 400–415 (Lérins/Marseilles), and §5's entry gravity is a different gravity entirely at a different date.

**Why it matters.** This is precisely the overstep the case document warns against: "Whether that is one formation world or a lineage of two is a Step 0 question this document cannot settle" (case §5). Doc_01 does not argue the point — it assumes it, in the two places where the assumption is load-bearing, while asserting elsewhere that it has not.

**Fix.** Either (a) state the floor conditionally — "c. 360 if this is one world; c. 410 if Doc_04 finds a lineage of two" — and answer §5's entry question twice, or (b) drop the pretence of non-resolution and argue for one world openly. The current arrangement claims (b)'s benefit while paying (a)'s costs.

**Classification: substantial** (scope boundary).

### S3. §6's "one world, two strands" argument rests on a word-count figure that is arithmetically wrong for the use made of it

**What's wrong.** §6, arguing *for* "one world, two strands": "per the case document's own count — both are counted inside the same 366,371-word figure that first surfaced this world as a gap in the census."

From the case document's own table (§3):

| author | words |
|---|---:|
| Cassian | 364,648 |
| Hilary of Poitiers | 228,578 |
| Sulpitius Severus | 109,539 |
| Vincent of Lérins | 28,254 |
| **total** | **731,019** |

Case §4 says removing Hilary "drops the figure to **366,371** words." It does not. 731,019 − 228,578 = **502,441**.

**366,371 = 731,019 − 364,648** — that is the total with **Cassian** removed and **Hilary still in**. Case §5 confirms this reading in its own words: "the Gallic entry's *distinctive* weight is the 366k **without him** [Cassian]." The case document is internally inconsistent between its §4 and its §5, and §4 is the erroneous one.

**Why this specifically breaks §6.** The figure Doc_01 invokes to show that *both nodes* are "counted inside the same figure" is the one figure that **excludes Cassian** — the Marseilles node's entire voice — and **includes Hilary of Poitiers**, whom this world deliberately excludes. It is the worst possible number for the argument it is asked to carry, and it was cited without being checked.

**Propagation.** G1's "Total citable material: ≈461,000 words" is 366,371 + 95,295 (Augustine) = 461,666. The same error is already downstream. The census entry's own `why` field also says "roughly 366,000 words."

**Fix.** Correct the arithmetic; recompute the with-Hilary-removed figure as 502,441; and find a different argument for §6's first bullet, because a word-count aggregate is in any case not evidence that two nodes belong to one formation world.

**Classification: substantial** (a sourcing conclusion, and the evidence for a reserved scope question).

### S4. §6's one testable cross-node candidate is not testable across both nodes — the Dialogues do not attest Lérins or Marseilles

**What's wrong.** §6, third bullet: a shared value on eyewitness testimony as the guarantee of a text's authority, "**testable across both nodes because both are attested in Sulpitius's own corpus (Dialogues, carrying both the Martin material and Postumianus's Egyptian travel account)**."

Postumianus's travel account is of **Egypt**, not of Lérins or Marseilles. I read the passage directly in this world's own vendored file. Postumianus sails *from* Narbonne to Africa and on to Egypt (Dial. I.3: "after setting sail from Narbonne, on the fifth day we entered a port of Africa"), and on the return leg "landing on the thirtieth day at Marseilles, I came on from that and arrived here." Marseilles appears once, as a port of disembarkation, in a work written c. 404–406 — roughly a decade before Cassian arrived there and founded anything. Lérins does not appear at all.

So Sulpicius's corpus attests **Martin** and **Egypt**. It does not attest the Lérins–Marseilles node in any form. The one candidate gravity §6 offers as actually testable across both nodes cannot be tested across both nodes from the evidence named.

**Fix.** Delete the parenthetical justification or replace it. If the eyewitness-authority candidate is to survive, it needs Cassian's own prefaces (where he grounds the Conferences in what he personally heard in Egypt) as the Marseilles-side evidence — which is available, and better.

**Classification: substantial** (a claim's substance and a sourcing conclusion).

### S6. "Prosper and Hilary of Arles" is a contested identification, asserted flatly, and contradicted by this world's own vendored source

**What's wrong.** The document names Augustine's Gallic correspondents as "Prosper and Hilary of Arles" in §2.3, §2.5, §4, §7 and §8, with no confidence label anywhere, and treats their 431 journey to Rome as established (§4).

The Hilary of Augustine's *Epistle* 226 has **three competing identifications** in the literature: the bishop of Arles; a Gallic lay monk; and the Hilary who wrote to Augustine from Syracuse in 414. The reference literature's standard summary is that the lay-monk reading "appears tolerably clear" — i.e. the bishop-of-Arles identification is the *least* favoured of the three.

**Decisively, this world's own vendored file contradicts the document.** The editorial introduction to the *Commonitory* in `npnf211` places Hilary of Arles on the **opposite** side of the quarrel: "Among its more prominent members, contemporary with Vincentius, were Honoratus and Hilary, afterwards successively bishops of Arles, and Faustus, afterwards bishop of Riez, **all of them in sympathy with the neighbouring clergy of Marseilles, opposed to St. Augustine's later teaching**, and holding what was afterwards called Semipelagian doctrine." Modern reference accounts likewise list Hilary of Arles among the Massilian party. Having him write *to* Augustine *against* the Massilians is close to a straight inversion.

**Why it matters beyond the name.** §7 and §8.2 build this world's distinctiveness on "a live, two-way written theological argument with the wider Latin church's most authoritative living voice" — with Prosper and "Hilary of Arles" as this world's own side of the correspondence. If, as is more likely, both correspondents were *pro-Augustinian* Gallic figures reporting on the Massilians (Prosper certainly was), then the letters are not this world speaking; they are opponents inside Gaul reporting this world to Augustine. That materially changes the shape of the exchange the document builds §7 and §8.2 on.

**Note on inheritance.** The error originates in the case document ("written to Prosper and Hilary of Arles — Gallic monks at Marseilles and Lérins") and G1 repeats it. Doc_01's §12 claims a "targeted live research pass … on Prosper/Hilary of Arles," so it cannot be excused as inherited.

**Fix.** Restate as "Prosper of Aquitaine and a Hilary usually identified as a Gallic lay monk (the identification with Hilary of Arles is contested and is against the weight of opinion)" and label it **Contested** per Article 17. Re-examine §7's and §8.2's "two-way argument" framing on that basis.

**Classification: substantial** (a claim's substance, a confidence rating, and a downstream argument).

### S7. The Cassian/Augustine relative chronology is treated as settled in both directions; it is live scholarly dispute

**What's wrong.** §10 states as bare fact, with no confidence label: "Cassian's Conference XIII, **which precedes rather than answers** Augustine's 428/429 replies." §7 casts what this world refused as "the specific implication its own monks read out of *On Rebuke and Grace*," with Conference XIII as "this world's own primary text on the question."

Both statements cannot comfortably be true, and neither is settled:

- On the dating internal to **this world's own vendored edition**, Conferences XI–XVII "must have been published not later than" 426, because Honoratus is addressed as *frater* rather than *Episcopus* and became bishop of Arles in 426. That puts Conference XIII at or before *De correptione et gratia* (426/427) — so it cannot be a reply to *that* either, not merely to the 428/429 treatises.
- Owen Chadwick redated Honoratus's episcopate specifically to create room for Conference XIII to be a response to Augustine. **Augustine Casiday** (*Tradition and Theology in St John Cassian*, OUP 2007) rejects Chadwick's redating and argues that Conference 13 contains little evidence of anti-Augustinianism at all.
- This project's own desert source record dates the Conferences as a whole "c. 426–429," which does not resolve the question either.

This is textbook Article 17 **Contested** material: "Live scholarly disagreement; no dominant position. Named scholarly tensions are carried at full strength and never quietly resolved. … Carry the tension; do not resolve it." Doc_01 resolves it silently, and inconsistently between §7 and §10.

**Why it matters.** The prompt's own history flags an inverted chronology shipping in a past first draft. This is the same shape of error: a relative-chronology claim stated as fact where the underlying question is exactly what specialists disagree about, and where the world's entire "position held under pressure" framing depends on the answer.

**Fix.** Label the Conference XIII/Augustine relation **Contested**, name Chadwick and Casiday as the two positions, and rewrite §7's "what it was refusing" so it does not depend on Conference XIII being a response to a text it may predate.

**Classification: substantial** (confidence rating and a claim's substance).

### S8. The c. 450 close is argued without Faustus of Riez, who is the strongest evidence against it

**What's wrong.** §2.4, §2.5 and §5 close the world at c. 450 on two grounds: the controversy "settling into an inherited, no-longer-live theological position," and Lérins consolidating into Mathisen's ecclesiastical-political "power elite."

**Faustus of Riez is never mentioned.** He was a monk of Lérins, its **third abbot from c. 433**, later bishop of Riez, and he wrote *De gratia* **c. 475** at the express request of the synods of **Arles and Lyons (475)**, against the predestinarian presbyter Lucidus. He is the direct institutional and doctrinal continuation of this world's own defining argument, inside this world's own institutional network, a quarter-century past the proposed close. The controversy was not settled into an inherited position until the **Second Council of Orange (529)**.

Faustus is not obscure to this build: he is named in this world's **own census entry** ("Honoratus and Hilary to Arles, Lupus to Troyes, **Faustus to Riez**") and in the **introduction to its own vendored NPNF volume** (as one of the Lérins figures "opposed to St. Augustine's later teaching").

**Second problem in the same argument.** The Mathisen ground is used backwards. §2.4 says c. 450 "sits **before** the mid-to-late 5th-century consolidation … whose influence peaked closer to 450 and after." A boundary cannot sit "before" a process that peaked at that boundary. Mathisen's Lérins faction rises **following Hilary of Arles's tenure** (bishop 430–449) — i.e. the consolidation the document uses as an exit marker begins roughly twenty years *inside* the proposed window.

**Fix.** §2.4 and §5 must engage Faustus explicitly. He may still be excludable (a plausible reading: the argument by 475 is being conducted by bishops through synods rather than by a formation community, which is itself the change in kind the document wants) — but that argument has to be made, not skipped. As written, the c. 450 boundary is under-argued against the best available counter-evidence.

**Classification: substantial** (scope boundary).

### S9. The "undocumented thirty-year gap" is not undocumented, and the document overstates its own ignorance

**What's wrong.** §2.4: "`merovingian-gallic-christianity` … opens c. 480 — a full **thirty years after** this world's proposed c. 450 close. **This document does not know, and does not attempt to invent, what fills that gap**." §5 calls it "the single largest open item this document carries"; §11 item 2 calls it "the largest single open boundary question."

The 450–480 interval in southern Gaul is one of the **better-attested** stretches of the period: Faustus of Riez and *De gratia* (c. 475); the synods of Arles and Lyons (475); Sidonius Apollinaris's letter corpus, which is among the fullest surviving windows onto Gallic episcopal and aristocratic life in exactly these decades. The gap is between two **census entries' date ranges**, not in the historical record. Calling it "undocumented" and declaring that the document "does not know what fills it" is a category error, and it inflates a housekeeping question into this document's headline open item.

**Compounding.** §12 says `world-census.json` was consulted for "this world's own entry and five neighboring entries." This world's own `relationsSummary` there states continuity in the other direction — it calls this world "the seedbed of the Merovingian Gallic church that follows it" — and the Merovingian entry's own summary says it "Receives Martin of Tours's line." Neither is engaged.

**Fix.** Restate as an Atlas date-coverage question rather than an evidentiary void; name what is actually in the interval; and engage the census's own continuity claim, which either supports or complicates §8.5.

**Classification: substantial** (scope boundary and a stated open item's premise).

### S11. Two quotations of the Institutes are not quotations

**What's wrong.** §1: Cassian's "own stated purpose in the *Institutes* is to carry Egyptian practice to Gallic houses **'so far as the nature of the climate would allow'**." §2.5 repeats it as **"so far as the climate allows."** Both are inside quotation marks.

Neither string occurs in this world's vendored NPNF text. I searched every occurrence of "climate" in the file. The Preface reads:

### S13. Five confidence labels are not supported by what independent verification shows

Article 17's vocabulary is fixed and the Framework requires it be used "consistently," not loosely.

**(a) §2.2, [Documented].** "Martin's ordination as exorcist under Hilary and his founding of Ligugé under Hilary's protection are reported consistently across the secondary literature this document consulted." *Documented* requires "**multiple independent sources** with no serious scholarly dispute." The sole ancient source for both facts is **Sulpicius Severus, *Vita Martini* chs. 5–7** — one interested hagiographer writing to build a cult, whose reliability this same document's §10 flags via Stancliffe. Secondary literature that all descends from Sulpicius is not independent attestation; it is one source counted many times. → **Widely Accepted** at most.

**(b) §2.1, [Widely Accepted].** Justified by "Ligugé Abbey's own historical summary and multiple encyclopedia accounts." *Widely Accepted* means "dominant in scholarship." An abbey's own website and encyclopedias are neither scholarship nor evidence about scholarship. The date itself (361) holds up well against independent checking and against this world's own vendored NPNF introduction ("Ligugé was founded shortly after 360"); it is the **basis stated for the rating** that is wrong.

**(c) §2.3, [Widely Accepted].** "Vincent joined the community at Lérins **c. 425**." Independent checking finds no scholarly date for Vincent's entry — standard reference accounts say only that he entered Lérins after secular/military pursuits, and Gennadius gives no date. "c. 425" appears to be a floating convention, not a dominant scholarly position. → **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** or **Inferential/Thin**.

**(d) §1, [Documented — case document §4].** Covered at S2: a project decision record cannot ground a Documented rating.

**(e) §10, "Contested" for the Vincent–Lupus of Troyes brother claim.** *Contested* means "live scholarly disagreement; no dominant position." What the evidence actually shows is a traditional claim that reference works carry with hedges ("is believed to be the brother of Lupus of Troyes") and which Gennadius does not corroborate — no live disagreement, just thin support and general caution. → **Inferential/Thin**. The document's *caution* here is right and should be preserved; the label drawn from the fixed vocabulary is the wrong one.

**Classification: substantial** (confidence ratings).

### S18. "Massilian" is described as this world's own self-designation; it is the opponents' label

**What's wrong.** §7: "**This world's own name for itself, where the sources supply one, is 'Massilian'** (from Massilia, Marseilles)."

*Massilienses* is **Prosper's** designation of the group, in his letter to Augustine reporting them — the same letter in which he calls them "the remnants of the Pelagians." It is an outsider's geographical label applied by a hostile reporter. It is genuinely period-contemporary rather than a retrojection, and preferring it over "semi-Pelagian" is the right call; but describing it as a self-designation is wrong, and it weakens the document's own reasoning, which rests on the distinction between what a group called itself and what was imposed on it.

**Second problem.** "Massilian" is a Marseilles term. It does not naturally cover Vincent or the Lérins figures — which matters for §6, since adopting it as this build's standing vocabulary quietly privileges the Marseilles node.

**Fix.** "The period-contemporary term, used by Prosper of the group rather than by the group of itself, is 'Massilian'" — and note its Marseilles-specific reach.

**Classification: substantial** (a claim's substance; sets standing vocabulary for Doc_03/Doc_06).

## COSMETIC FINDINGS

**C1.** §7: "under an strong doctrine of unconditional predestination" — typo; "a strong."

**C2.** §7 compresses two distinct sixteenth-century moves. Beza's c. 1556 coinage is **verified** (Backus & Goudriaan, *JEH* 65 [2014], 25–46), but their finding is that Beza coined it for *contemporary Roman Catholic* teaching, and the Formula of Concord (1577) applied it to Lutheran synergists; the **retrospective** application to fifth-century Gaul is usually credited to the Roman Catholic controversialist **Nicholas Sanders (1571)**. So the coinage was Protestant and the backward application was not. "Applied backward to this world's own participants" by Protestant controversialists conflates the two.

**C3.** §7's quoted "inappropriate, ambiguous and unjust" is credited to "at least one specialist assessment." Independent checking finds the phrase circulating in general reference material as an unattributed characterisation, not as a locatable quotation from a named specialist. Either name the specialist or drop the quotation marks.

**C4.** §2.3: "the twin houses of St. Victor (one for men, one for women)." The men's house is **Saint-Victor**; the women's house is **Saint-Sauveur**. §4's "St. Victor's women's community" and §11 item 3's "no named individual has yet surfaced" should at minimum record that the house itself has a name.

**C5.** §3: "Tours sits roughly 700 km from Marseilles **by the routes available in this period**." Straight-line is ~600 km; any real route is materially longer (modern road ~800+ km, Roman itineraries longer again). A by-the-roads figure should exceed the straight line, not fall between it and nothing.

**C6.** §10 dates Prosper's *Contra Collatorem* "c. 432." The usual dating is 432–433, most often **c. 433** — Prosper wrote it after returning from Rome, and its own "twenty years and more" since the start of the Pelagian heresy (413 in his *Chronicle*) points to ~433. Not wrong, but the early edge stated as if settled.

**C7.** §10 credits Leyser with treating Cassian's "close association with the Lérins circle, including **his standing with Pope Celestine I**." Cassian's documented Roman connection runs through **Leo**, then archdeacon under Celestine, who commissioned *De Incarnatione* (429/430). Restate as Leo, or verify the Celestine framing before attributing it to Leyser. (Leyser's bibliographic details and the "language of moral authority" characterisation both check out.)

**C8.** §8.3 quotes the census `why` field as ending "…takes durable institutional form here, in a Rule." The actual field continues "…in a Rule **that later Western monasticism kept returning to**." Truncation inside quotation marks at a point that reads as a sentence end.

**C9.** §10 lists Cassian's works as "*Institutes*, *Conferences* **I–III**, *De Incarnatione*" — inherited from the case document's table, where "I–III" means the three published **parts**. G1 renders the same corpus as "Conferences I–XXIV." A reader of Doc_01 alone would conclude Conference XIII sits outside this world's corpus, contradicting §7 and §10's reliance on it.

**C10.** §8.3 calls Benedict's commendation "a documented line of transmission, not an inference." RB 73 commends "the Conferences of the Fathers, their Institutes and their Lives" — the identification with Cassian is very well supported but is an identification, and RB 73 commends the **Conferences** at least as much as the Institutes (RB 42 prescribes them as daily reading). Singling out the Institutes obscures the stronger half of the link.

**C11.** §5's contrast — "ascetic withdrawal organized as a replicable community, rather than **a bishop's individual ascetic household**" — leaves the referent undefined. There is no evidence Hilary of Poitiers kept such a household, and the case document uses that phrase of **Martin's own Tours**, not of Hilary's Poitiers. As written the contrast reads as against Hilary and is unsupported.

**C12.** §12: "Governed by: Formation World Construction Framework V7.4." V7.4's own status block reads "DRAFT — pending Opus deep review and project-lead review; not yet ratified," and its own Record Integrity addendum flags six worlds having frozen against it as "the practical situation the Record Integrity Principle exists to prevent." The comparison world was built on V7.3. Worth a one-line disclosure that this build is the seventh world freezing against an unratified document.

## Reviewer's note on the shape of the failures

Eleven of the eighteen substantial findings share one cause: **the document reasoned about sources it had rather than reading them.** The Institutes' dedication (S12), the two non-quotations (S11), Postumianus's itinerary (S4), the Conferences XI–XVII dedication (S5), the Hilary of Arles inversion (S6), the Conference XIII dating window (S7), Faustus of Riez (S8), and the Conference XII/XXII edition gap (S17) are **all** resolvable — most of them decisively — from `cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml`, a file this world has vendored, corpus-mapped, and cites by name in its own header. The word-count error (S3) is resolvable from the case document's own table with four additions.

The live web research pass the document performed was real and mostly good — the terminology work in particular is genuinely well done. But it was performed *instead of*, not *alongside*, reading the world's own library. That is the mechanism worth naming for the revision, and for the next world's Doc_01.
