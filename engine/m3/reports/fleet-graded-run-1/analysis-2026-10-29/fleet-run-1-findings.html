Bottom line

  • The automatic checks cannot be a gate. They passed every answer in every world, while reviewers found 33 wrong ones and 97 more that need repair.
  • Most damage comes from a few shared causes, not from the records. The engine's cut step removes true sentences and leaves the next one pointing at nothing. The evidence block tells the voice that anything not listed is “our honest limit”. Build words reach the reader.
  • The test did not run the live answer path. It skipped the live directive, the gate and reader, the recitation retry and the length limit. Some of what we measured is the test, not the product.
  • The same false-silence error appears in almost every world and our own review could not see it, because it checks answers against our own records. Outside scholars did.
  • lpc is not ready. Both methods agree on its main errors. The gap is specific and mostly small.

One summary per feedback aspect

1 · Automatic checks

Passed everything, which tells us nothing cannot gate

  • All 28 answers passed in every world except cappadocian, whose report was never written (a retried call broke the harness count).
  • The one real check, that citations trace to a source, passes almost by construction. The engine has already dropped every unverified tag before the check runs.
  • No readability gate runs on either path. A per-turn readability check exists in the code but nothing in the answer path calls it.
2 · Rigor, checked against our records (12 Opus reviewers, 336 answers)

Mostly acceptable: 90% are sound or acceptable 33 blocked

  • Standard used: would a competent scholar accept it? Not perfection. 155 sound, 148 acceptable, 33 not acceptable.
  • Cause of the 33: the voice saying more or other than the records (22), engine cuts (5), the right record not offered (3), harness (2), no record (1).
  • Weakest: lpc (6 blocks), then gallic, ijc, rzg and pahc (4 each). don has none, witt one.
  • Blind spot: this method checks against our records. When a record is thin, the reviewer cannot tell the voice is wrong about the period.

5 · Cost

Cheap per answer. The savings are in length within cap

  • $16.55 of $17.00 spent. The cap was raised from $12 to $16 and then $17, each time logged as the project lead's ruling.
  • The 12 admission runs cost about $8.60 (from the run ledgers). The rest, about $7.95 by subtraction, went on the 30 lpc conversations, the six-question replay, and reruns.
  • $0.018 to $0.040 per answer, driven mostly by prompt size, which output length does not change.
  • The part we can change is output. Reviewers estimate 1,400 to 3,000 unneeded words per world. Tightening answers would cut output tokens by a quarter to a half.
  • The reviews, the code map and the replay did not touch the $17 cap. They used this session's own tokens.
6 · Information flow (read from the code, not yet run)

Where a question becomes an answer, and where it goes wrong

build   records ──► prompt   cautions, horizon, demos become citable "ground"      [distorted]
turn    question ──► gate + reader ──► directive (speaker names, no quote, length)  [skipped in test]
        ──► evidence: match words ──► up to 2 cells ──► records (cap 12)           [loses right record]
        ──► block header: "anything beyond these is our honest limit"              [false silence]
        ──► VOICE drafts   (adds outside knowledge, block labels, raw tags)
        ──► guards, retry, self-revision                                           [skipped in test]
        ──► CUT STEP removes sentences it cannot place; repairs nothing            [orphans, wrong attribution]
        ──► answer ships; no readability gate on either path
  • False silence comes from retrieval matching words only and ignoring the question's own category, from an empty evidence block dropping the thin-topic notes, and from the header turning a wrong match into “honest limit”.
  • After-window knowledge slips through because the check knows only dated events, and citing the world's core record clears it.
  • Cautions compile as citable text written as instructions to the builder, so the voice reads them aloud or misreads them as denials.
  • Correction after the replay: the test did show the voice each quote's speaker name. What only the live directive adds is “use the name exactly as listed” and “do not paraphrase the quote first”.

Are we going in circles?

This is what the data shows, and where it stops. We do not have the history of each earlier engine change in this run's data, so I cannot show which change moved which number.

  • Same errors across runs. In lpc, cautions 13, 14 and 15 have now failed four runs in a row. Compared with run 3 on 37 shared rows, 7 were fixed, 5 still fail and 3 newly fail. The new failures came from the new Letter 228 record.
  • Fixes that move the damage. Removing a sentence fixes one defect and creates another (an orphan or a wrong attribution). Under the old rule any one flaw failed the row, so fixes that traded one flaw for another showed no net gain.
  • Testing the wrong path. A change judged on the admission harness cannot show whether it helps live, so a fix can look wrong or right for the wrong reason.
  • Patching wording per world cannot reach causes that are shared by all 12.
Proposed stop rule: every engine change gets a line in a ledger that names the failure it targets and the exact saved answers it should change. Each change is tested first by replaying the same 308 saved drafts, which costs nothing. Anything that does not move its named failure is reverted, not patched on top.

What this does not tell us

  • The outside “scholars” are AI models acting as specialists. Most used their own knowledge with no web search. Their corrections need a human specialist before we change records on their word.
  • The code map was read from the code, not run, and the 12 worlds ran at 11 different commits. The live-path effect of the missing directive is not measured. That needs paid calls.
  • Cappadocian has no automatic result, no per-answer cost and no replay.
  • The orphan counts are a heuristic (about 60% precise on a 30-case sample).

Plan, in order

  • 1. Free: use the 308-draft replay as the test bench. Open a ledger of every engine change with the failure it targets.
  • 2. Engine, general: repair or drop sentences that depended on a cut; stop cutting grounded text; rename the evidence-block labels and strip any [[tag]]; stop the header turning a wrong match into “honest limit”; widen the after-window check.
  • 3. Voice rule: “our record does not hold this”, never “no such source exists”.
  • 4. Records, per world: about 50 fixes, each with the source the outside referee named. For lpc: regeneration in baptism, the Fussala letter, Perpetua and archaeology, two quote not_for lines.
  • 5. Gate before go-live: a live-path run, an outside referee pass, and one or two human specialists on the answers the AI referees rejected.

Decisions for the project lead: lpc go-live (recommend not yet), whether to line up human specialists, and a re-test budget when the free steps are done.
