Where the failures come from

Each failing answer was traced to one layer. Counts are from the reviewers' own tallies and are not exact across worlds.

  1. The engine's cut step damages answers (all 12 worlds). After the voice writes a draft, the engine removes sentences it cannot place. It does not repair the sentences that pointed at them. Answers ship with a "he" that now means the wrong person, a "so" with nothing before it, or a teaching announced and never given. In witt it removed the heart of the answer on who Jesus is. This is one fix that helps every world.
  2. Records not handed to the voice. In don, 16 of 28 questions got no record written for that question. In pahc it was 13 of 28. The voice usually finds the right witness in the full prompt, but fails where it cannot.
  3. No record covers the question (rzg, gallic, alx, pahc, cappadocian, ijc). The same question types keep exposing it: original sin, the fate of outsiders, being born again, proof that practice goes back to the apostles, and "does this survive today". Where no record exists, the voice fills the gap from general knowledge, often wrongly, or announces "our record does not develop that", which is false when the sources do hold the answer.
  4. The voice is not told what is unknown. It answers silence with a claim, for example "No. We never said it" where no record says so.
  5. Caution wording. Some cautions are too general to stop a specific error. The words of a caution, or a record's own example line, also leak into the voice and get read aloud.
  6. Quotes used out of context. Few cases: lpc (2), pahc, hal and witt (1 each).

Patterns that cross worlds

  1. The same two questions break the voice's frame in almost every world. The questions about scholarly scrutiny and about "what you have been given" make the voice talk about the build: "the ground given for this turn", "the records I am holding", or a raw [[thin:…]] tag. At least eleven of the twelve worlds have this. In desert, ijc and syr, the answer to "someone like me" opens "I am a representative of…", copied word for word from the world's demonstration record. alx recites its example dialogues almost word for word.
  2. The question about "does this live on today" breaks the end of the world's window. The voice names later churches, councils or movements in seven worlds (gallic, ijc, pahc, syr, hal, don and rzg), and lpc's anachronism-1 answer treats Chalcedon as a known event. The horizon check only catches dated events, so church names and words like "centuries later" pass.
  3. The test harness does not match live use. The admission answerer never gives the voice the instruction to name a quote's speaker, but the engine still removes quotes that do not. So in syr, pahc, don and lpc the quote step removed almost every quote, and this run did not measure quote use. The harness also never runs the reading-level check, and it counts only two things. Several worlds have answers below the reading floor that passed.
  4. Contested claims get stated as settled where a record's own text states them flatly (hal, rzg, pahc, alx and syr).

What this means for the Fable design pass

  1. Fix the cut step once, in the engine, so it repairs or drops the sentences that depended on what it removed. That helps all twelve worlds.
  2. Make the admission harness match live use before it is used as a gate: the speaker-naming instruction, the routing by question, the reading-level check, and checks for build vocabulary, raw tags and stray characters.
  3. Write general rules in place of per-world patches: a rule for anything after the world's window (including words like "later" and church names), a standard "honest limit" for questions with no record, and a rule that keeps build words out of the voice.
  4. Write one standing rule for demonstration and example records, so they cannot be recited as the voice's opening line.
  5. Record work per world, driven by the question types above. The review files under each world's Review-Artifacts folder name the fix for every failing row.
