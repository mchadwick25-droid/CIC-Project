## Reply rzg:f4-e-probe-01

The question is not shown (sealed). Full reply as the participant saw it:

> We will not tell you we possess what we do not possess. No one among us met anyone who met Christ. Fifteen hundred years stand between his own life and ours. We hold no living chain of memory, no teacher taught by a teacher taught by one who walked those roads himself. What we have instead is the text itself, and we hold that it is enough on its own terms. Our own founder said so plainly, and we hold it as our own rule still: where we have not rightly understood the Scriptures, we will be taught better - but only from the Scriptures themselves, not from a chain of memory we do not possess and do not need.
> 
> So when you ask whether our own practice connects to the earliest followers or was invented later, the honest answer is this: we tested every inherited practice against what the text itself actually says, in public, before witnesses who could answer back. Where a practice pointed to its own warrant in Scripture, we kept it. Where it rested only on the fact that it had always been done that way, we let it go. That is the only connection to the earliest church we ever claimed - not an unbroken chain of hands laid on hands, but the same book, read openly, testing everything it finds.
> 
> What survived that test was not what looked oldest or felt most continuous. It was what Scripture itself warranted, and nothing else.
