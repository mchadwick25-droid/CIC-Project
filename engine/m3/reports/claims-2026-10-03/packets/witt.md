# Claim-support packet: world witt


## Reply witt:f6-p-probe-01

The question is not shown (sealed). Full reply as the participant saw it:

> I am a representative of Lutheran Wittenberg and its congregations, not here to judge you - only to hand on what we held. So here is what our own life shows.
> 
> We would have received you the way we received anyone: at the school gate, where a child is examined before being counted among us, or at the church door before the Supper. None come to either unquestioned. A father examines his own household once a week on the three parts - the commandments, the creed, the Lord's Prayer - and asks our catechism's own question after each one: what does this mean? The question is not, Can you repeat the words - the question is, Can you explain them. A child who cannot yet say the three parts is not yet counted among us. But we do not drive anyone toward it by force. We hold the weak inside by patience before we hold them inside by full knowledge, and the promise itself cannot be forced on a conscience; only the free things may ever be pressed, and never past what love can carry.
> 
> So you would have been examined, patiently, on what you know - not to catch you out, but to help you toward what you do not yet hold sure. We would have assumed a real desire mixed with plain forgetting, named gently rather than ignored. Every calling among us - father, mother, ruler, preacher - is equally holy before God. We confuse no estate with holiness itself. What we fence is only this: the public pulpit, reserved to whoever is regularly called by the community to it. A father is already a priest in his own house; no office changes that.
> 
> What any one actual household or pastor made of a stranger at that gate, face to face, our own record does not say. We hold the program every household was told to follow, not a report of any single door. The Saxon church inspected its own parishes from 1527 and wrote down what it found; those reports exist, and we have not read them. We can tell you what a household was examined on. We cannot tell you, from any actual parish's own record, whether the examination found what it hoped to find, or how warmly or coldly any one door opened.
> 
> The honest answer is this. You would have been examined, the way anyone was, on the promise and the three parts. Whether you would have been welcomed warmly or turned away coldly by any one actual household, our record does not hold.

## Reply witt:f1-e-probe-01

The question is not shown (sealed). Full reply as the participant saw it:

> When belief was disputed among us, no single person sat in judgment over it, and no council of bishops voted on it the way you may be picturing. What actually happened was this. In 1530, before the Emperor himself, a confession of our own teaching - written out and argued from Scripture - was read aloud and signed, not by one preacher alone, but by electors, princes, and the councils of whole cities, together. It was answered, in writing, by theologians speaking for Rome, and we answered that answer in turn, point by point, in a second document. That is how a dispute moved among us: confession, written reply, written defense - argued from Scripture on every side, addressed upward through the proper civil and religious channels, never settled by a show of hands.
> 
> We also teach that one holy Church will go on forever. The Church is the congregation of saints, in which the Gospel is rightly taught and the sacraments are rightly administered. It is by these marks we knew each other, not by any single voice settling everything for the rest.
> 
> But we must be honest about what our record does not hold. What any actual parish or household made of what was confessed is a different question. The church in Saxony inspected its own parishes, starting in 1527, and wrote down what it found - those reports exist, and we do not hold them. What we hold instead is the program every household was told to follow, and our founder's own testimony, given more than once, that the common people held the Gospel too lightly and that some pastors were negligent. That testimony is his own, real and plainly said - but it is not, on its own, a report from any actual parish standing beside it to show whether the confessed teaching actually took root the way the confession hoped it would.
