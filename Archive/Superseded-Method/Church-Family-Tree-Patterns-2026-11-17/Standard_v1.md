# The one-page standard

A story of about two minutes that lets anyone, anywhere, meet one person from the church's history. It is heard once, often by someone listening in a second language.

## Craft

- Open on something that draws a listener in, from the outline's openings. Within the first few sentences, give the century or year, the place and the tradition, in the outline's spoken words.
- Tell it as a story by someone who knows the record well, not a report about the record. Each sentence leads to the next.
- Vary the sentences. Join facts that belong together; keep a short sentence for the moment that needs it. No sentence past about 30 words. No run of sentences that all begin "He did" or "He said".
- Tell it in your own words. Use only the outline's quotes, and only where a voice does what your words cannot.
- About a third of the story is what came of them. End there, as an event or in the record's own words: who carried their witness on, or how far it reached. No moral, no summing up.

## Rigor

- Tell only what the outline holds. Add nothing for effect: no feelings, weather or thoughts the record does not give.
- Name a writer or witness only when the listener needs to know who is speaking: praise or blame, a wonder, a source written much later, or a link on the tree. Say it once, plainly, in the story's own voice.
- A wonder stays in its witnesses' voice, with no sneer and no endorsement.
- Where the outline marks something uncertain, say so plainly. Do not present it as settled, and do not bury it in hedging.

## Accessibility

- Plain modern words. Simplify the language, never the thought.
- Give the reason for any act, or for any word or custom that would puzzle a newcomer, in the same sentence. Take it from the outline.
- One thought per sentence, with each subject close to its verb.
- Aim for reading grade 8 to 9. Grade 10 and reading ease 60 are the limits. A lower grade is judged by ear: is it clear, or has it gone flat?
- Scholars, editions and debates go on the card, not in the story.

## Before it is done

- Heard once, could someone anywhere follow it?
- Do they come away knowing why this person mattered to the church?
- Can every sentence be traced to the outline?
