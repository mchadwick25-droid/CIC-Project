## Question 1 — What a good business plan looks like for a venture of this exact shape

### 1a. The genre problem: most "business plan" advice is written for the wrong company

The dominant online guidance splits documents into two genres that both assume a growth-and-exit company: the **pitch deck** (10–20 slides, built to make a VC quickly want a meeting) and the **full business plan** (10–100 pages, often built to satisfy a *bank/lender* or later-stage investor doing diligence). Standard sources frame the plan's job as securing loans or guiding a capital raise toward scale. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([privateequitylist](https://blog.privateequitylist.com/pitch-deck-vs-business-plan-key-differences/), [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/pitch-deck-vs-business-plan/), [OGSCapital](https://ogscapital.com/article/pitch-deck-vs-business-plan/))

Both defaults mis-fit CiC on three axes at once: the plan's implied *purpose* (raise dilutive equity or debt), its implied *reader* (a return-maximizing investor or a lender underwriting repayment), and its implied *ending* (a large outcome). CiC's reader is a contributor/mission-aligned supporter, its financing must avoid both debt and control transfer, and its "ending" is a modest ~$2M continuity sale. So the useful move is not "deck vs. plan" but **choosing the mission-driven / social-enterprise plan genre and grafting on the credibility apparatus of a real business plan.**

### 1b. What mission-driven / social-enterprise plans actually contain, and in what order

The social-enterprise plan differs from a for-profit plan mainly by carrying a **double bottom line** — mission outcomes tracked in parallel with financials — and by adding two sections a VC plan lacks: a **Theory of Change** and a **Funding & Sustainability** section that explicitly explains income diversification rather than a single raise. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([Propel Nonprofits summary](https://propelnonprofits.org/resources/social-enterprise-business-plan/), [Root Cause "Business Planning for Enduring Social Impact"](https://rootcause.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Business_Planning_for_Enduring_Social_Impact.pdf), [Businessplansuite social-enterprise guide](https://businessplansuite.com/blogs/blog/writing-business-plan-social-enterprise))

The recurring section set across these guides:
1. **Executive summary** — the single most-read section; must earn the rest.
2. **Mission + Theory of Change** — the causal chain from activity → intended change. Social-enterprise guidance treats this as co-equal with the business model, not a preamble. ([CoBS Insights](https://cobsinsights.org/2023/04/19/social-impact-monitoring-and-measuring-it-using-theory-of-change/), [Rank & File](https://rankandfilemag.com/issue-10/theory-of-change-guide-for-social-impact-modeling/))
3. **The offering / model** (your worlds + Representatives + access ladder).
4. **Market / who it serves.**
5. **Impact measurement plan** — frameworks named in the literature: Theory of Change, IRIS+, the Five Dimensions of Impact. Impact is measured across three streams: financial sustainability (unit economics, churn), stakeholder voice (qualitative feedback), and outcome evidence. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**]
6. **Financial plan** — projections ≈3 years with a **narrative of key assumptions** (the assumptions narrative matters more than the numbers this early).
7. **Funding & sustainability strategy** — how income is *diversified* and how social return grows.

**What a generic VC plan gets wrong for this founder** [Confidence: **Inferential-Thin → Widely Accepted** synthesis]:
- It leads with a "$X market → we capture Y% → hockey-stick" logic. For a modest-exit, values-constrained venture that framing is not just unnecessary, it actively *undermines trust* with the donor/sponsor audience you actually need.
- It treats mission as a "why now / founder passion" slide, then subordinates it to returns. CiC's mission is the load-bearing wall and a legal obligation, not decoration.
- It assumes the plan's job is to *sell control for capital.* Yours must do the opposite: demonstrate that capital can enter without buying influence (B3).

### 1c. The PBC structure is itself a credibility asset — and it has one real limit worth stating plainly

A Public Benefit Corporation legally binds the mission ("mission lock"), which multiple sources frame as a credibility and stability advantage: the mission is a governing-document obligation, not a marketing line, and is meant to survive market pressure and leadership change. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([Upmetrics](https://upmetrics.co/business-terms/public-benefit-corporation), [UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com/public-benefit-corporation), [Terms.law](https://www.terms.law/2023/11/18/benefit-corporations-comprehensive-guide-for-business-attorneys/))

**The limit to disclose honestly in the plan:** the public-benefit purpose in the charter can typically be amended or removed by a supermajority vote (commonly two-thirds of outstanding stock). [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com/public-benefit-corporation)) So the PBC form protects the mission *only as long as you control that stock.* If future equity is sold widely enough that outsiders reach a two-thirds bloc, the lock can be undone — which is precisely why the mission-protection structures in Section 2g (golden share / trust) exist as a belt-and-suspenders layer. This directly implicates B3.

### 1d. The solo-founder / steady-state framing is a strength to state openly, not hide

Solo, bootstrapped, no-VC founding is now mainstream, not marginal: the share of new US startups launched by solo founders climbed from 22% (2015) to 38% (2024); roughly half of solopreneurs launched on under $5,000; a large majority report first-year profitability. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**, single-survey figures — treat as directional] ([solofounders.com](https://solofounders.com/blog/solo-founders-in-2025-why-one-third-of-all-startups-are-flying-solo), [Bizee](https://bizee.com/articles/strategies/build-solo-business)) A **lifestyle/steady-state business** is explicitly defined as one run to sustain a purpose or way of life rather than to maximize growth for shareholders. [Confidence: **Documented**] ([Wikipedia: Lifestyle business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifestyle_business)) The consistent bootstrapping advice — default to revenue over funding; the first goal is a paying customer, not a term sheet — maps cleanly onto CiC's free→paid ladder. ([Bizee](https://bizee.com/articles/strategies/build-solo-business), [Entrepreneur Loop](https://entrepreneurloop.substack.com/p/building-a-startup-alone-solo-founder-playbook))

## Question 2 — Case studies (deep), and financing mechanisms compatible with the three boundaries

### 2a. BibleProject — the closest values-match, and a model that does NOT map 1:1 onto a PBC

- **Structure:** a 501(c)(3) nonprofit (status granted July 2014), based in Portland, OR. [Confidence: **Documented**] ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/bibleproject-experiences-rapid-growth-going-into-seventh-year/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BibleProject))
- **Funding model:** describes itself as **"100% crowdfunded"** and says it does *no traditional proactive fundraising* — instead a simple invitation to those who value the content to contribute. [Confidence: **Documented**] ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/bibleproject-experiences-rapid-growth-going-into-seventh-year/), [BibleProject Give page](https://bibleproject.com/give/)) It relies on many small recurring gifts (reported as tens of thousands of patrons averaging ~$20/month). [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/the-bible-project-videos-reaching-millions-with-help-of-youtube-and-crowdfunding.html))
- **Scale/trajectory:** <$900K (2015) → $3.5M (2017) → $7.1M (2018) → >$9M (2019); ~41,000 patrons and 24M audience in 2020; later reporting shows ~$14.5M (2022) → ~$26.7M (2023) and ~$30.6M total revenue against a ~$26.2M budget for the 2024 filing year. [Confidence: **Documented** for 2015–2020 via MinistryWatch; **Widely Accepted** for 2022–2024 via aggregators] ([MinistryWatch spotlight](https://ministrywatch.com/ministry-spotlight-the-bible-project/), [Impala profile](https://impala.digital/public/profiles/46-4277592/overview))
- **A discipline worth copying:** they hold a reserves policy of ~6 months operating expenses and ran surpluses ($3.4M in 2018, $1.4M in 2019). [Confidence: **Documented**] ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/bibleproject-experiences-rapid-growth-going-into-seventh-year/))
- **Friction/limitation:** no documented criticism of the crowdfunding model's sustainability itself — existing critiques are theological, not financial. [Confidence: **Documented (absence)**]

**The load-bearing caveat for CiC:** BibleProject's engine runs on **tax-deductible** gifts *because it is a 501(c)(3)*. CiC is a for-profit PBC — see 2h — so it can copy the *culture* (simple invitation, small recurring patrons, reserves discipline, no aggressive fundraising) but **cannot** offer donors the same deduction without a structural workaround. This is the single most important asymmetry between your inspiration and your entity.

### 2b. Khan Academy — the model most structurally analogous to CiC's cost curve

- **Structure/funding:** a nonprofit backed by major philanthropy (Gates Foundation, Google.org, Bank of America, and others subsidizing Title I access); core content is free forever. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([AI for Cause](https://aiforcause.org/stories/khanmigo-ai-tutor))
- **The AI-cost asymmetry is real and confirmed.** Sal Khan has said the organization *would have gone bankrupt* had it given the Khanmigo AI tutor away free from the start — models are expensive to run. [Confidence: **Documented**] ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)) Corroborating cautionary data point: **Quizlet shut down its "Q-Chat" AI tutor in 2025 because per-user inference costs ate the margins.** [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] ([AI for Cause](https://aiforcause.org/stories/khanmigo-ai-tutor))
- **Pricing:** Khanmigo individual/family ~$4/month (~$44/yr); district license per-student (reported reductions $60 → $35 → $15/student/yr), 250-license minimum. [Confidence: **Documented/Widely Accepted**]
- **A new lever worth naming:** the paid AI tier's cost problem was later *partly solved by an in-kind infrastructure partnership* — Microsoft donated Azure/OpenAI capacity in 2024, letting Khan make the teacher tool free. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] **Implication for CiC:** donated/discounted compute is a legitimate, boundary-safe way to lower the AI-cost floor — not debt, no personal outlay, no editorial control conferred.

**Takeaway:** Khan Academy validates the exact ladder architecture already designed here — free core, paid where marginal AI cost bites, per-seat institutional licensing.

### 2c. Hallow — the closest *structural* comparable (faith app, for-profit, PBC)

- **Structure:** organized as a public benefit corporation. [Confidence: **Documented**]
- **Capitalization:** heavily **VC-backed** — Series A ~$12M (2021), Series B ~$40–50M (2021), Series C **$50M in May 2023** led by Goodwater Capital, total ~$105M (some trackers ~$157M). [Confidence: **Documented** for Series C; **Widely Accepted** for the rest]
- **Monetization:** freemium, ~$9.99/mo premium, ~$69.99/yr, "give a subscription" pay-it-forward mechanic.
- **Valuation/cap table:** undisclosed, cannot be verified.
- **Investor influence over content:** **not publicly verifiable either way.** Real mission/content tension is documented (growing pressure to take stances as it scales, the Liam Neeson partnership controversy) but sourced to market pressure, not an investor mandate. [Confidence: **Documented (the tension); Inferential-Thin (any investor mechanism)**]

**Takeaway:** Hallow proves a faith-app PBC *can* scale — but only by taking the exact kind of large VC capital your boundaries rule out. Best evidence the structure works, and the clearest illustration of the path not being taken here.

### 2d. Calm & Headspace — the cautionary structure (what NOT to emulate)

VC-scale capital reshaped both: Headspace raised ~$351M, Calm ~$191M; Calm hit ~$2B valuation (Dec 2020), Headspace ~$3B after merging with Ginger (2021). [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] Both went through repeated layoffs since. An employee review of the post-merger shift: **"from being member-obsessed to profit-obsessed."** [Confidence: **Documented (as characterization)**] The adjacent hard-failure case is **Cerebral** — scaled to ~$4.8B on growth-at-all-costs, then collapsed under a DOJ investigation.

**Takeaway:** VC capital didn't kill these companies, but visibly pulled them toward profit-maximization and away from their founding ethos — the precise mission-drift-under-investor-pressure that boundary 3 exists to prevent.

### 2e. Cautionary tales, actively sought — the three failure modes the boundaries target

**(i) Mission compromised by investor/shareholder pressure — Etsy.** Certified B Corp; after its 2015 IPO and a stock slump, activist investors pushed for conventional growth; in 2017 the board fired the CEO, disbanded the values/impact team, and let its B Corp certification lapse. Observers describe subsequent drift toward ads, fees, and mass-produced goods undercutting the artisans it was built for. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] **The single best-documented case that public shareholders can override a mission a company genuinely held — validates boundary 3 directly.**

**(ii) Founders pushed out by investor-controlled boards.** Real and recurring across tech history (Jobs/Apple, Kalanick/Uber, Eberhard/Tesla, Conrad/Zenefits, Mason/Groupon, Yang/Yahoo), mechanism is board composition and investor voting/protective rights, not day-to-day performance. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**]

**(iii) Debt that couldn't be serviced — faith-adjacent examples.** **Family Christian Stores** (largest US Christian retailer) filed Chapter 11 in Feb 2015 with $120M+ debt; suppliers *forgave ~$127M* to keep it alive; it still liquidated all 240 stores in Feb 2017, 3,000+ jobs lost — even massive debt relief couldn't save it. **Crystal Cathedral** filed Chapter 11 in 2010 over vendor debts. Broader data: 497 Chapter 11 filings by 454 religious institutions (2006–2011), 90%+ Christian, heavy debt named as a recurring aggravator. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] **Validates boundary 1: debt is what turns a downturn into a death spiral for mission organizations.**

### 2g. Mission-protection structures (relevant to B3 — in tension with the exit goal)

If outside equity is ever taken, purpose-built tools exist to keep control: a **golden share** (independent-party veto over sale/mission change, separated from economic participation), a **perpetual purpose trust** (a trust holds ≥51% in perpetuity — used by Patagonia, Organically Grown Company, Ecosia's Purpose Foundation golden share), and broader steward-ownership. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**]

**Two honest tensions, not resolved here:**
1. A **perpetual purpose trust conflicts directly with the stated ~5-year sale** — PPTs are built to make a company *unsellable* in the ordinary sense. A lighter **golden share / charter-provision** approach likely fits better: protects editorial integrity through a modest sale to a successor without freezing the company forever. [Confidence: **Inferential-Thin** — verify with counsel.]
2. The **Academic Advisory Circle is, in governance terms, a soft version of these protections** — makes capture visible and reputationally costly without giving anyone equity or a board veto. Worth naming explicitly as a mission guard in any future plan.

### 2h. The tax-status fact that reshapes the contributor-facing framing

**Gifts to a for-profit PBC are generally not tax-deductible for the giver** — deductibility requires 501(c)(3) status, which the PBC doesn't have. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] The recognized workaround is **fiscal sponsorship** — partnering with an existing 501(c)(3) that receives tax-deductible gifts earmarked for the charitable-purpose parts of the work (commonly "Model A" comprehensive or "Model C" pre-approved grant). [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**]

**Why this matters:** several revenue streams already named in this thread — the keep-it-open gesture, the Letter to Friends ask, the founding-cohort ask, directed sponsorship — read like donations but, given the entity, are legally gifts/revenue to a business with **no donor deduction** unless routed through fiscal sponsorship or a companion 501(c)(3) for the most clearly-charitable pieces (academic-review funding is the strongest candidate). Not fatal — plenty of people support businesses they believe in — but every supporter ask should **frame itself honestly as "support," not imply a tax benefit**, and fiscal sponsorship is worth a real look specifically for the academic-review/world-building costs. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted** on the tax mechanics; the fiscal-sponsorship fit for CiC specifically is **Inferential-Thin** — check with a CPA/attorney before building a program around it.]

## Question 3 — How to assemble the document itself (for contributors, not VCs)

### 3a. Use the "case for support" genre as the spine

The nonprofit **case for support** genre fits a contributor audience — short (2–7pp core), ordered: case summary → the model/how change happens → history/what's proven → vision → call to action, tiered. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**]

### 3b. Framing rules that separate this genre from a VC deck

- **Balance story and data deliberately** — donors split into "moved by story" and "moved by numbers"; serve both.
- **Strength/opportunity frame, not deficit/savior frame** — "help keep rigorous, honest formation open to everyone," not "we'll fail without you."
- **Adopt BibleProject's tone** — a simple invitation to those who already value the work, explicitly not aggressive fundraising; the posture itself is part of the trust.

### 3c. Founder-story vs. traction vs. financials — weighting for this stage

- **Founder story:** prominent but purposeful — establishes credibility and values (the deliberate PBC choice, refusing to sell the truth, multi-tradition commitment), not a personality pitch.
- **Traction:** lead with it. The strongest asset against a "shoestring about to disappear" perception is that the product is already built and tested — five worlds live, the Atlas live, real converged unit economics (~$0.20–0.75/conversation), a bounded ~$110–160 launch cost and ~$357–407/mo run cost. Concrete, cheap, real, disciplined.
- **Financials:** right-sized, not elaborate — ~3 years, anchored by a clear narrative of assumptions, not false precision. Lead with the believable, small path to break-even the real unit economics allow, paired with a BibleProject-style reserves discipline.

### 3d. Patterns worth borrowing from comparable orgs

- **BibleProject:** a public "Give" page framed as partnership/invitation, transparent about being crowdfunded, radiating stewardship (reserves, surpluses). Borrow the transparency-as-trust posture.
- **Khan Academy:** free-forever core as the moral center; paid/licensed tiers framed as *what keeps the free tier open*, not as the point — the honest, non-tip framing already wanted for the keep-it-open gesture.
- **Hallow:** "give a subscription to someone in need" — a clean model for the keep-it-open gesture that reframes it as a gift to another participant, never a tip to the house.

### 3e. On the anti-tipping instinct

Well-supported: ~9 in 10 Americans say tipping has gotten out of control, ~65% report tipping fatigue. [Confidence: **Widely Accepted**] The research-supported alternative framings are pay-it-forward ("fund a seat for another participant," Hallow's model) and membership/patronage (fund the mission, not the transaction) — both redirect the gesture from rewarding a transaction to extending access to someone else, exactly the intent already established.

## Synthesis — a recommendation to react to, not a decision

1. **Write it as a social-enterprise "case for support," not a VC plan or a lender's business plan** — case-summary → model/theory-of-change → what's already proven → vision → tiered ask, with a right-sized, assumption-driven financial section leading with the real unit economics and small break-even path.

2. **Fund primarily from boundary-safe, non-dilutive, non-debt earned + gifted revenue** — the tiered ladder + institutional licensing (Khan-validated), patronage/membership (Patreon-validated), directed sponsorship, founding-cohort and Letter to Friends asks, pay-it-forward access gifts (Hallow-validated). This stack honors all three boundaries cleanly.

3. **Treat outside equity as optional, and only in boundary-safe form** — a capped, non-voting SAFE / Reg CF community round (no board seats, refuse control side-letters) or an equity-form mission-related investment are the only compatible options, and even these add owners eventually wanting a return at a modest exit. **Explicitly rule out** bank debt, lines of credit, vendor financing, and revenue-based financing — the last one *reads* boundary-safe in its marketing but creates a real repayment obligation.

4. **Solve the tax-status mismatch before building a donor program** — frame every supporter ask honestly as "support," not tax-advantaged giving, and/or stand up fiscal sponsorship specifically for the most defensibly-charitable pieces (academic review), where the Academic Advisory Circle gives the charitable narrative real teeth. Verify with a CPA/attorney before scaling it.

5. **Protect the mission with light-touch structures that don't freeze the exit** — the PBC charter + Academic Advisory Circle + a possible golden-share/charter veto on editorial integrity are the right guardrails. Avoid a full perpetual purpose trust — built to make a company unsellable, which fights the ~5-year continuity-sale goal directly. The cautionary trio (Etsy losing its mission to shareholders, founders ousted via board control, Family Christian/Crystal Cathedral dying under debt) is well-founded precedent, not paranoia.

6. **The sharpest credibility lever already exists:** a built, tested product with genuinely cheap, converged unit economics and a tiny cost-to-launch. In a genre where most early plans sell a dream, this can sell a working, disciplined thing that just needs to be kept open — exactly the "stable, not a shoestring" story the modest-exit thesis depends on.

**Confidence on the synthesis overall: Inferential-Thin-to-Widely-Accepted** — each building block is sourced above; the combination is the agent's reasoned recommendation. The two items most worth independent professional confirmation before building on them: the fiscal-sponsorship tax mechanics, and the exact mission-protection instrument (golden share vs. charter provisions vs. trust) if outside capital is ever taken.
