## Why Under a PBC Umbrella

*Confirmed through several rounds of live editing with Mark, 2026-07-22 — reads as approved in
its current form, not locked as final until the full document comes together.*

We considered the traditional nonprofit path first. But between us, Susan and I have spent a
lifetime in Christian ministry. We've served on boards and mission teams, raised support
ourselves, worked alongside large sponsorship and development organizations, and been part of two
different mission startups. That experience taught us something we've carried into this work:
build something real first, and let it earn trust, before asking anyone to support it.

So we built Church in Conversation first, and tested it with real people in real conversations,
before we ever asked anyone to support it. Faithways Studio, Inc., the Public Benefit Corporation
that houses this work, let us do that without waiting years on an uncertain nonprofit
determination. It also legally binds us, under Colorado law, to the mission we're actually
building toward.

Accountability matters to us as much as flexibility does. Colorado law requires Faithways Studio
to file a public annual benefit report showing how we're actually living out our stated mission,
not just claiming it. The mission itself is written into our founding documents in a way that
can't be quietly changed. And we built in flexibility on purpose. Faithways Studio can host other
platforms over time, or Church in Conversation specifically could become a nonprofit later if
that turns out to serve the mission better. Right now, this structure is what let us start
building instead of waiting years for an uncertain approval. That's why it's the right choice for
where we are today, not necessarily the only shape this will ever take.

Plainly, so it's never a surprise later: gifts to Faithways Studio are not tax-deductible under
this structure. We chose to start building rather than wait. That is the honest cost of that
choice.
