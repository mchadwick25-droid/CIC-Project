## 1. Cross-tradition comparative brief (parallel presentation, not synthesis)

**Origin:** Mark, mid-walk, 2026-07-22, explicitly framed as "not for implementation now."

**The idea:** a paid, pastor/teacher-facing feature that queries across the full world
database (100+ worlds, eventually) for a given theme — e.g., "how did different traditions
understand faith?" — and returns a brief built from **multiple traditions' own full,
independently-sourced answers laid out side by side**, not merged into one.

**The critical distinction, stated precisely by Mark and worth preserving exactly:** this is
**not** synthesis and **not** conclusion-drawing. Each tradition's entry — quotes, stories,
lexicon terms, sourcing — stands entirely on its own, generated with the same discipline and
transparency as any single-world conversation already uses. The system's job is retrieval and
honest curation (which traditions actually engaged this theme with real depth) and parallel
presentation, never editorial verdict about what the comparison means. The reader — a pastor
building a sermon series, a teacher preparing a unit — does the comparing themselves, informed
by primary-source-grade material for each tradition at once, at a depth that would otherwise
take a scholar years of cross-referencing to assemble by hand.

**Why it's architecturally different from every other idea in this thread:** every other
funding idea sits on top of the existing one-world, one-conversation product. This is the
first idea that only exists *because* the dataset is large — it doesn't work with 5 worlds,
it works because there might eventually be 100+. It also doesn't require inventing new
interpretive capability: it's the same per-world honest-answer generation the app already
does, run once per relevant tradition, laid out together. No new authority is being created —
which is also why it stays clean against this project's own anti-fabrication and
world-speaks-for-itself commitments (see `[[world-speaks-for-itself]]` in project memory):
nothing is ever said *about* the comparison, only *within* each tradition's own answer.

**Two ways a pastor could get to the brief, both floated 2026-07-22, neither decided:**
1. **Manual selection from the Atlas** — the pastor picks which traditions/worlds to include
   themselves, browsing the existing Atlas structure.
2. **Comparative search by strength of presence** — a query surfaces which worlds engaged a
   given word or theme most frequently or most substantively in their own source material,
   and suggests traditions on that basis rather than requiring the pastor to already know
   which ones are relevant.

**Real design considerations already surfaced, for whenever this gets built (not now):**
- Even *which* traditions get selected/surfaced for a given brief is itself a small editorial
  choice (relevance-ranked, not exhaustive) — worth being as transparent about that selection
  logic as everything else in this project already is about sourcing.
- Each entry keeps its own confidence labeling (Documented / Widely Accepted / Contested /
  Inferential-Thin) exactly as a single-world answer would — nothing about presenting several
  side by side should make any one of them read as more settled than it is.
- Open question, not answered: whether each brief gets generated fresh per query, or the
  project eventually builds toward a standing, theme-tagged reference index that gets richer
  every time someone asks. A later architecture decision, not a now one.
- This is a natural product to sell into the "tailored services for academics/pastors"
  segment already named in this thread's own decision log, and it's also a plausible answer
  to "what else can the 100+-world database do beyond the conversation engine" — it sits at
  the intersection of two of the five funding streams, not just one.

**Status:** parked. No further development in this thread unless Mark reopens it.

## 2. Broader-access, healthier-organization, and growth ideas (batch)

**Origin:** Mark, 2026-07-22, asking directly "what else should we brainstorm" across four
named goals — broader access, healthier organization, financial stability, and growth — using
the core system already built. Pure divergent-mode output, none evaluated or chosen.

**Broader access:**
- Sliding-scale/regional pricing on any future paid tier, so a fixed price doesn't quietly
  become a wealthy-country-only feature.
- A low-bandwidth / text-first mode for people on limited connections or older devices.
- Public/community access points (libraries, community centers) for people without a
  reliable personal device or connection.

**Healthier organization — the one goal nothing else in this thread had touched yet:**
- An informal advisory circle (pastors/scholars/friends) for real counsel and accountability,
  lighter than a formal board — reduces single-founder risk without adding governance weight
  a solo-stage PBC doesn't need yet.
- Documenting the world-building *method* itself, not just the worlds it produces — reduces
  bus-factor risk, and is also plausibly what makes the project actually sellable in 5 years
  (a buyer needs a repeatable system, not just Mark's personal knack).
- Colorado PBCs already owe an annual benefit report by law — that filing could double as a
  real public "state of the Table" letter, turning a compliance requirement into the same
  transparency habit already promised to sponsors, just extended to everyone once a year.
- A volunteer/contributor layer, not just a consumer one — e.g. theology students helping with
  source research under supervision, a structured reader/feedback program — real capacity
  without needing cash, a natural extension of the community layer already inside the tier
  model (see Decision-Log's tier-structure entry).

**Financial stability and growth:**
- Impact investing / mission-related investment as its own distinct capital category —
  different from both the nonprofit grants already set aside (mostly 501(c)(3)-gated) and a
  friends-and-family angel round. Not yet explored at all in this thread.
- Borrowing reach instead of buying it — guest appearances, syndicated content, cross-
  promotion with aligned podcasts/publishers/homeschool curriculum makers who already have an
  audience CiC doesn't have yet. The actual answer to "does it open doors," raised earlier
  against Patreon specifically.
- A founding-cohort ask, distinct from both the after-experience gesture and any subscription
  — a one-time, non-recurring "help bring this into the world" identity. Early backers tend to
  become word-of-mouth engines on their own, feeding growth as much as the treasury.

**Status:** parked. No further development in this thread unless Mark reopens one of these.
