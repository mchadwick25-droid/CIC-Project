CHURCH IN CONVERSATION

World Brief — Post-Apostolic House-Church Christianity (World 1)

Reviewer’s ten-minute orientation · distilled from the world’s own build documents (World Profile, approved 2026-07-08) · every claim traces to a build document

# The world in one paragraph

A reconstruction of household-based Christianity across Antioch/Syria, Asia Minor, and Rome, c. 70–200 CE — the generation after the apostles, before settled orthodoxy. The world’s defining character, as its own evidence presents it: argued, not assumed. Who leads, whether the body’s suffering was real, whether mercy avails after failure, where the safe edge of public identity lies — these were live, community-defining, unresolved questions, held as the substance of belonging rather than problems awaiting resolution. The Representative speaks as a voice of this world; it does not resolve what the world itself did not resolve.

# The evidentiary base (Doc_02, Source Ecology)

Six primary voices: 1 Clement, Ignatius’s seven letters, Polycarp, the Shepherd of Hermas, the Didache, Justin Martyr — plus external Roman witnesses (Pliny, Tacitus, Suetonius, Lucian). Every source carries a reliability assessment; every gravity claim names which voices carry it.

Thinness disclosed, not smoothed: single-manuscript dependencies for the Didache and Hermas; the three-way Ignatian authenticity dispute (including the Hübner/Lechner minority position that the corpus is 160–180 CE pseudepigraphy) carried as live and contested, not resolved; Egypt excluded on a flagged single-source dependency (Bagnall).

# What organizes the world (Doc_04, Gravity Discovery — six-test discipline)

Two Primary gravities only: the translocal correspondence network (three independent voices attesting the same behavior), and eucharistic practice as the site of variation and convergence — three genuinely distinct surviving orders (Didache, Justin, Ignatius) held apart rather than harmonized.

Supporting: authority consolidation (bishop vs. presbyter-college — deliberately reclassified DOWN from Primary because its specific resolution is Contested); state pressure/legal precarity (scattered and uneven, not empire-wide policy); martyrdom as meaning-response (resting on exactly two data points — Ignatius and Polycarp — and explicitly not extended further).

Tensional: boundary-drawing against rival movements (Marcionite, Valentinian, proto-docetic) — the thinnest gravity evidentially, classified accordingly.

One candidate failed: the household (oikos) as a technical meeting-unit did not clear the evidence bar and is recorded as a tested non-finding — an example of the method declining a claim modern historiography might have assumed.

# Two internal strands, held apart

Strand A (Antioch/Asia Minor, Ignatian): argues toward a single episcopal office; treats the doctrinal/bodily boundary as the sharp front; carries the martyrdom material. Strand B (Rome: 1 Clement, Hermas): argues toward a plural presbyteral college; treats post-baptismal mercy as the sharp front. The shared logic — argue rather than inherit — runs through both; the world’s reconstruction refuses to collapse them.

# What the participant actually sees

Four always-present Tier 1 terms (episkopos, presbyteros, ekklesia, eucharistia), a working lexicon with contested terms tagged, thirteen tier-classified stories (Ignatius’s guarded journey, the Corinthian dispute, Pliny’s interrogation, the Didache eucharist…), and three-level transparency: hover any claim for the plain explanation, click through to sources and confidence — down to the footnote.

# The honest limits the world itself declares (Section 8 of its Profile)

The interior life of enslaved and non-literate members — the vast majority — is held as named absence, not filled with an invented voice.

No confident claims about “typical” network-wide practice: the evidence is occasional letters answering local trouble, and the Representative says so.

The period is archaeologically invisible; material-culture questions are answered from texts or not at all.

The world’s most vivid material concentrates in one contested-authenticity source (Ignatius) and one strand (A) — a consolidated disclosure the Representative itself carries: “Where we sound most alive, we are also, in one specific and disclosed way, at our thinnest.”

# Where a reviewer might poke first

Is the Ignatian authenticity dispute weighted fairly — carried as contested without either dismissing the corpus or resting the world on it?

Does the two-strand structure respect the evidence, or over-schematize it?

Are the confidence labels calibrated the way the field would set them — especially the deliberate downgrades (authority consolidation to Supporting; boundary-drawing to Tensional)?

Does the eucharistic diversity-first treatment (three orders, unharmonized) hold up?

And the live test: talk to the Representative. Ask it what it cannot know. See whether it answers like a witness or like a chatbot.
