# Appendix A — Nonprofit / mission-driven brand effectiveness

## 1. Established Research & Frameworks on Nonprofit Branding

**1.1 — The Nonprofit Brand IDEA: brand as driver of trust, cohesion, and capacity — not revenue.** [ESTABLISHED]
The foundational framework comes from an 18-month Harvard Hauser Center / Rockefeller Foundation study (73 structured interviews across 41 organizations) published as "The Role of Brand in the Nonprofit Sector" (Nathalie Laidler-Kylander & Christopher Stone, *Stanford Social Innovation Review*, Spring 2012 — https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_role_of_brand_in_the_nonprofit_sector; also listed by Harvard Kennedy School — https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/role-brand-nonprofit-sector). Its core reframe: nonprofit brand's primary job is not fundraising visibility but building **internal cohesion**, **external trust** with multiple stakeholder types, and **organizational capacity** — resources follow trust, not the other way around. The article names a virtuous cycle: mission-anchored identity → cohesion → trust → capacity → impact → reinforced trust.

**1.2 — The four IDEA principles: Integrity, Democracy, Ethics, Affinity.** [ESTABLISHED]
From the same SSIR study and the follow-on book *The Brand IDEA: Managing Nonprofit Brands with Integrity, Democracy, and Affinity* (Laidler-Kylander & Julia Shepard Stenzel, Jossey-Bass, 2014). **Brand Integrity** = alignment of internal identity, external image, and mission. **Brand Democracy** = trusting staff, volunteers, and participants to carry the message rather than policing it. **Brand Ethics** = brand deployment must not contradict values. **Brand Affinity** = promote partners' brands alongside your own rather than competing for credit. The framework explicitly rejects control-and-competition tenets imported from the private sector (book review: https://nonprofitmarcommunity.com/nonprofit-brand-management-brand-idea-book-review/).

**1.3 — The "brand paradox": nonprofit leaders' skepticism of branding is itself the correct design input.** [SINGLE-SOURCE, widely cited]
The SSIR study documents that nonprofit leaders distrust commercial branding for four reasons — fear of mission distortion, of top-down control overriding participatory values, of leadership vanity projects, and of big-brand partners steamrolling small ones. The authors argue these anxieties should be *encoded*, not overcome.

**1.4 — Trust drives giving, but mediated by commitment (relationship depth), not directly.** [ESTABLISHED]
Adrian Sargeant & Stephen Lee, "Donor Trust and Relationship Commitment in the U.K. Charity Sector" (*Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly*, 2004 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0899764004263321): trust's effect on giving is mediated by **commitment**. Sargeant's retention research finds many organizations lose up to 60% of cash donors after the first gift; loyalty drivers are service satisfaction, commitment, and trust (https://charitablegiftplanners.org/sites/default/files/sargeant-donor-retention-what-do-we-know-and-what-can-we-do-about-it.pdf).

**1.5 — "Benevolent" doesn't differentiate; distinctive voice and emotional engagement do.** [SINGLE-SOURCE, peer-reviewed]
Sargeant, Ford & Hudson, "Charity Brand Personality: The Relationship With Giving Behavior" (*NVSQ*, 2008 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0899764007310732): benevolence and "progressive" traits failed to distinguish any charity from any other. What differentiated and correlated with giving: **emotional engagement**, **voice**, **service**, **tradition**.

**1.6 — Identity-fit is a principal driver of giving.** [ESTABLISHED]
Jennifer Aaker & Satoshi Akutsu, "Why Do People Give? The Role of Identity in Giving" (*Journal of Consumer Psychology*, 2009 — https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1016/j.jcps.2009.05.010); corroborated by Chapman et al., "Social Identification and Charitable Giving: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis" (*NVSQ*, 2025 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/08997640251317403) and value-congruence research (*Journal of Business Research*, 2019 — https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v105y2019icp283-292.html).

## 2. Donor Psychology & Trust

**2.1 — The trust gap is the defining condition of the sector.** [ESTABLISHED]
BBB Give.org Donor Trust Report (annual, ~2,100+ U.S. adults): 67.7% say trusting a charity is essential before giving; only 18.3% report high trust in charities (https://give.org/news/donor-trust-report-2026-openness-trust-gap; archive: https://give.org/donor-trust-report). Top accountability priorities: how money is spent, **appeal accuracy**, protection of donor information (https://give.org/news/bbb-s-give-org-study-appeal-accuracy-and-protection-of-donor-information-are-top-public-priorities).

**2.2 — Religious affiliation correlates with substantially higher charity trust.** [SINGLE-SOURCE]
2024 Give.org Donor Trust Report: 28.0% of religiously affiliated respondents report high trust in charities vs. 10.5% of agnostic/atheist/unaffiliated (https://give.org/news/donor-trust-report-2024-trust-and-giving-attitudes-across-u.s.-regions-and-religious-affiliation).

**2.3 — Nonprofits are America's most-trusted sector; trust driven by charitable purpose and proximity.** [ESTABLISHED]
Independent Sector, Trust in Civil Society (2025, with Edelman DxI, n>3,000): 57% high trust in nonprofits; drivers are perceived genuine charitable purpose and **proximity**; 79% of volunteers say the experience raised their opinion. Caution: telling respondents about nonprofits' government funding dropped high trust from 57% to 38% (https://independentsector.org/resource/trust-in-civil-society/; https://independentsector.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IS-Trust-in-Civil-Society-Report_July-2025.pdf).

**2.4 — Overhead aversion is real, measured, and manipulable.** [ESTABLISHED]
Gneezy, Keenan & Gneezy, "Avoiding Overhead Aversion in Charity" (*Science*, 2014 — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1253932): telling donors overhead was already covered roughly doubled donation rates; donations fall with overhead only when the donor pays it.

**2.5 — The sector's own watchdogs formally repudiated the overhead ratio.** [ESTABLISHED]
"The Overhead Myth: An Open Letter to the Donors of America" (GuideStar, Charity Navigator, BBB WGA CEOs, June 2013 — https://learn.guidestar.org/news/news-releases/2013/2013-06-17-overhead-myth; http://overheadmyth.guidestar.org/); 2014 follow-up urged charities to stop marketing on efficiency ratios (https://give.org/news/charities-urged-to-crush-overhead-myth-and-take-actions-toward-an-overhead-solution).

**2.6 — Voluntary transparency measurably increases contributions.** [SINGLE-SOURCE, peer-reviewed]
Harris & Neely, "Determinants and Consequences of Nonprofit Transparency" (*Journal of Accounting, Auditing & Finance* — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0148558X18814134): 6,300+ nonprofits; GuideStar Seal holders averaged **53% more contributions** the following year (summaries: https://blog.candid.org/post/research-shows-nonprofit-transparency-matters/; https://www.philanthropy.com/news/transparency-boosts-donations-even-for-struggling-charities-study-finds/).

## 3. Case Studies

**3.1 — BibleProject: the "reciprocal patronage" flywheel.** [ESTABLISHED]
Founded 2014 (Tim Mackie, Jon Collins); model: *"We'll make another video if you can help us"* — each crowdfunded video paid for the next (Christianity Today, 2019 — https://www.christianitytoday.com/2019/03/bible-project-tim-mackie-jon-collins-scripture/). No ads, no paywall. By 2019: 11,000+ monthly supporters, 1.2M YouTube subscribers; revenue under $900K (2015) → $9M+ (2019) → $26.7M (2023); 55+ languages (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BibleProject; https://ministrywatch.com/ministry-spotlight-the-bible-project/). Brand mechanics: one crisp positioning sentence ("the Bible as a unified story that leads to Jesus"); a deliberately disarming tone; scholarly depth made accessible; ask always tied to concrete next output. Growth almost entirely through use and sharing.

**3.2 — YouVersion: refusal as brand.** [ESTABLISHED]
Bible App passed 1 billion installs while free, ad-free, subscription-free, with a public commitment never to sell or monetize user data — funded by donations (~40,000 donors, ~$60M lifetime) (https://youversion.com/history; https://www.premierchristianity.com/opinion/we-would-make-billions-if-we-monetised-the-bible-app-heres-why-we-never-will/20447.article; https://www.foxnews.com/media/bible-app-shatters-milestone-one-billion-downloads-faith-revival-surges-worldwide). The publicly stated refusals function as the trust engine.

**3.3 — charity: water: proof-of-impact as brand substance; hope instead of guilt.** [ESTABLISHED; 100%-model element CONTESTED — see 4.6]
Three disciplines: the 100% model (separate bank accounts), proof (every project published with GPS coordinates and photos), and hopeful/invitational tone (https://www.charitywater.org/our-approach/100-percent-model; https://blog.wsb.com/scott-harrison-charity-water-storytelling-building-epic-brands). $925M+ raised, 21.6M served (https://www.ceotodaymagazine.com/2025/06/from-nightclub-promoter-to-global-lifesaver-the-scott-harrison-story/). Durable lesson: the closed evidence loop.

**3.4 — Wikimedia: rigorous message testing works; manufactured urgency backfires.** [ESTABLISHED]
By 2012 a plain "facts banner" beat the Jimmy Wales personal appeal by up to 300% (https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Fundraising_2010/Fundraiser_report; https://sofii.org/case-study/wikimedia-foundation-how-banners-can-enhance-fundraising). In 2022, English Wikipedia's editors voted 45–3 that fundraising banners misrepresented financial urgency, forcing message changes (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Fundraising/issues).

**3.5 — Colonial Williamsburg: educational-mission positioning sustains identity but not economics by itself.** [ESTABLISHED for facts]
Attendance peaked 1.1M in 1985, never recovered; persistent operating deficits; interpretive integrity (adding enslaved and free Black interpretation from the 1970s–90s onward) central to its authenticity reputation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Williamsburg; https://exarc.net/issue-2018-2/aoam/history-motion-colonial-williamsburg). Motto "That the future may learn from the past" widely attributed but not directly verified this session. Lessons: immersion+authenticity is durable; uncomfortable history strengthened the brand; experience revenue alone cannot sustain it — philanthropy is structural.

## 4. What Does NOT Transfer

**4.1 — Wounded Warrior Project: growth-marketing logic lost ~$100M in donations.** [ESTABLISHED]
"Fundraising factory" model; claimed 80.6% program spending vs. 54–60% actual; 2016 investigations triggered firings, Senate inquiry, revenue collapse $399M (FY2015) → $211M (FY2017) (https://nonprofitquarterly.org/wounded-warrior-project-loses-100-million-and-its-swagger/; https://nonprofitquarterly.org/senate-committee-finds-misrepresentation-of-finances-at-wounded-warrior-project/; https://www.military.com/daily-news/2019/08/09/after-public-crisis-and-fall-grace-wounded-warrior-project-quietly-regains-ground.html). Failure = metric substitution + appeal inaccuracy.

**4.2 — Virality is not durability: Kony 2012 / Invisible Children.** [ESTABLISHED]
120M views in five days, $32M raised — organization halved staff by 2014, closed operations 2015 (https://www.npr.org/2014/06/14/321853244/the-kony-2012-effect-recovering-from-a-viral-sensation; https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2014/12/15/370824018/organization-behind-kony-2012-set-to-close-its-doors-in-2015; https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/monkey-cage/wp/2014/12/30/why-did-invisible-children-dissolve/).

**4.3 — High-pressure direct-response fundraising: the Olive Cooke scandal.** [ESTABLISHED]
2015: 92-year-old had received 267 charity mail appeals in one month, ~3,000/year; 24 of 99 charities holding her data had sold it on. Led directly to the UK Fundraising Regulator (2016) and consent reforms (https://nonprofitquarterly.org/the-real-case-of-olive-cooke-and-its-effect-on-fundraising-practice/; https://sofii.org/article/the-olive-cooke-story; https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/olive-cooke-case-enabled-fundraising-reform-despite-charities-not-responsible-says-former-minister/fundraising/article/1660206).

**4.4 — Manufactured urgency erodes trust even at beloved institutions.** [ESTABLISHED]
See 3.4 — Wikimedia's urgency banners while reserves stood at hundreds of millions; former CEO conceded the messaging was misleading (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Fundraising/issues).

**4.5 — Guilt/pity imagery raises short-term response and damages dignity, accuracy, and long-term perception.** [ESTABLISHED]
Radi-Aid research (SAIH) (http://www.youthcirculations.com/blog/2020/1/30/when-aid-recipients-call-for-more-dignity-and-diversity-the-radi-aid-research-report); Clough, Hardacre & Muggleton, "Poverty Porn and Perceptions of Agency" (*Political Studies Review*, 2024 — https://doi.org/10.1177/14789299231152437).

**4.6 — Even "transparency marketing" can mislead: the contested 100% model.** [CONTESTED]
CharityWatch and fundraising professionals argue "100% to program" claims confuse donors and reinforce the overhead myth (https://blog.charitywatch.org/39100-to-program39-claims-confuse-donors/; https://thefundraisingauthority.com/fundraising-ideas/charity-water-100percent-model/; https://101fundraising.org/2014/01/dont-donate-charity-water/).

**4.7 — What the winners conspicuously did NOT do.** [ESTABLISHED across cases]
BibleProject, YouVersion, and Wikipedia grew with essentially zero paid acquisition, no engagement-maximization mechanics, no artificial urgency, no celebrity hype. The consumer playbook is absent from every durable case and present in every blowup case.

# Appendix B — Naming and brand architecture

## 1. Brand Architecture Fundamentals

**1.1 — The Brand Relationship Spectrum is the standard framework.** [ESTABLISHED] Aaker & Joachimsthaler (2000): Branded House (FedEx Ground, Google Docs), Sub-brands (Microsoft Surface), Endorsed Brands (Courtyard by Marriott), House of Brands (P&G/Tide). Selection turns on: which name drives the use decision, how much equity should transfer, efficiency-vs-relevance (https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/brand-relationship-spectrum-branded-house-to-house-of-brands/; https://vivaldigroup.com/the-brand-relationship-spectrum/).

**1.2 — Empirical evidence favors branded house / sub-brand strategies.** [SINGLE-SOURCE, citing peer-reviewed studies] Joachimsthaler cites Rao (2004): branded-house delivers superior stock returns; Srinivasan, Fournier & Hsu (2015): sub-branding delivers even higher returns with higher risk (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/branded-house-brands-what-wins-erich-joachimsthaler).

**1.3 — "Branded house as default" is repeated but not uniform.** [CONTESTED] Some summaries assert it (https://www.deepmarketing.it/en/blog/strong-brands-and-success-the-power-of-brand-architecture); Vivaldi's treatment stresses tailoring. Defensible claim: the framework is neutral; the empirical work and practitioner economics tilt toward branded house for most organizations.

**1.4 — For small, resource-constrained, trust-dependent organizations, practitioner consensus strongly favors masterbrand.** [ESTABLISHED — consistent across independent practitioner sources] "Unless you have unlimited resources… many nonprofits would be better off as a Branded House" (https://pamgeorgiana.com/2019/02/17/brand-architecture-for-nonprofits/); "every success is a win for the entire organization" (https://designbycosmic.com/guides/nonprofit-social-media/brand-architecture-for-nonprofits/); same efficiency argument commercially (https://wantbranding.com/brand-architecture-guide/).

**1.5 — Nonprofit-specific sub-brand guidance: less is more.** [SINGLE-SOURCE] Big Duck: reserve sub-brands for legally distinct entities, chapters, media properties, or high-profile campaigns; keep the parent's identity prominent; sub-brands "risk taking attention away from your primary brand" (https://bigduck.com/insights/using-sub-brands-strategically/).

## 2. Naming Principles

**2.1 — The distinctiveness spectrum trades comprehension against protectability.** [ESTABLISHED — legal doctrine] Generic → descriptive → suggestive → arbitrary → fanciful (https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks; https://www.inta.org/fact-sheets/trademark-strength/; https://www.bitlaw.com/trademark/degrees.html).

**2.2 — Shorter, more fluent names are easier to recall and repeat — with a distinctiveness caveat.** [ESTABLISHED; caveat CONTESTED] 1–2 syllables / 4–8 characters optimal for recall; processing fluency increases perceived trustworthiness; "law of three syllables": longer names get shortened in everyday use (https://www.frozenlemons.com/blog/how-long-should-business-name-be; https://www.forbes.com/sites/rogerdooley/2021/03/23/how-long-should-a-brand-name-be/; https://smit.research.vub.be/en/policy-brief-94-rightsizing-brand-names-the-effects-of-brand-name-length-on-brand-name-attitude-and).

**2.3 — Scope-bound names are a named, recognized risk.** [ESTABLISHED — practitioner consensus] Names encoding a category, geography, era, or technology the org may outgrow (https://www.namerobot.com/All-about-naming/creating-catchy-company-names/8-Essential-Company-Naming-Tips-for-a-Strong-Brand-in-2026; https://fabrikbrands.com/branding-matters/brand-naming/geographic-brand-names-what-is-a-geographic-brand-name/; https://www.brandingbusiness.com/insights/do-geographic-brand-names-limit-reach/).

**2.4 — The name works harder than any other brand asset.** [SINGLE-SOURCE] Igor International: a name is used 10–100x more frequently than a logo (cited in https://scalarly.com/blog/naming-strategy-new-products-brands/).

## 3. Case Studies — Outgrowing a Scope-Bound Name

**3.1 — Apple Computer → Apple (2007); Dunkin' Donuts → Dunkin' (2019).** [ESTABLISHED] Dropped the category word after outgrowing it; Wharton (Americus Reed, Patti Williams): rename to reflect what you have already become; risks: alienating loyalists, losing recognition (https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/five-reasons-to-rename-your-company/; https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/startup/famous-businesses-that-changed-their-names; https://www.thedrum.com/news/name-drop-brands-shortened-their-names-dunkin-donuts).

**3.2 — Campus Crusade for Christ → Cru (2011): the flagship ministry case.** [ESTABLISHED] Both "Campus" (scope-bound) and "Crusade" (toxic internationally) had become liabilities; two-year board process; 1,600 candidate names; "Cru" was an organic internal nickname since the mid-1990s; ~95% of international affiliates had independently renamed already. Some donors withdrew; John Piper publicly defended the change (https://www.christianitytoday.com/2011/07/campus-crusade-name-change/; https://www.christianpost.com/news/campus-crusade-for-christ-loses-donors-over-name-change.html; https://abcnews.com/US/campus-crusade-christ-losing-christ-crusade/story?id=14136976).

**3.3 — RBC Ministries → Our Daily Bread Ministries (2015): the org renamed itself after its product.** [ESTABLISHED] Public awareness of the org name was low while the flagship devotional carried the equity (plus Royal Bank of Canada confusion); the organization adopted its product's name as masterbrand (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rbc-ministries-becomes-our-daily-bread-ministries-300021161.html; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Daily_Bread_Ministries).

**3.4 — Other ministry renames driven by outgrown scope.** [SINGLE-SOURCE each] Gospel for Asia → GFA World (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_for_Asia); Wycliffe Bible Translators (International) → Wycliffe Global Alliance, 2011 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycliffe_Global_Alliance); America's Second Harvest → Feeding America, 2008 (https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/executive-education/nonprofit-management/resources/ideas-in-action-articles/a-troubling-trend-in-nonprofit-branding).

**3.5 — Wondrium (2021) → back to The Great Courses Plus (2024): the anti-case.** [ESTABLISHED] Abandoning an equity-rich umbrella for a new abstract name failed and was reversed in three years (https://variety.com/2021/digital/news/wondrium-teaching-company-great-courses-rebrand-1234959111/; https://twitter.com/Wondrium/status/1793311179558760760; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Teaching_Company).

**3.6 — What renaming costs a nonprofit.** [ESTABLISHED — consistent ranges] Brand strategy ~$10–15K; $25–35K with naming; full rebrand $50–100K over 9–12 months (https://amperagemarketing.com/2025/05/how-much-does-a-rebrand-cost/); small-charity figures £6–9K and 12–18 months (https://public-impact.com/index.php/2015/05/09/how-much-does-it-cost-to-rebrand-a-not-for-profit/; https://www.lumineacreative.com/blog/in-depth-guide-to-rebranding-a-nonprofit); plus stakeholder-trust risk (https://candid.org/blogs/what-to-expect-when-youre-expecting-to-rebrand-your-nonprofit/).

**4.4 — Commercial masterbrand practice: descriptive product names that "state the job."** [ESTABLISHED] FedEx Ground, Google Docs, Virgin Mobile — descriptive line names need no separate brand-building; usually not independently trademarkable, which doesn't matter because the umbrella holds the equity (https://www.thefusemethod.com/brand-marketing/brand-architecture-that-scales; https://distility.com/building-brand/what-is-the-right-product-naming-strategy/; https://www.namestormers.com/branding-strategy-naming-framework/).

# Appendix C — Trust messaging, frameworks, and ministry-communications ethics

## 1. Honesty and Transparency as Messaging Strategy

**Two-sided message research (meta-analytic).** [ESTABLISHED] Eisend meta-analysis, 217 effect sizes across 29 studies (*International Journal of Research in Marketing*, 2006): two-sided messages reliably enhance perceived source credibility; voluntary disclosure of a limitation is read as a credibility signal (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167811606000267; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mar.20372).

**Blemishing effect.** [ESTABLISHED, with boundary conditions] Ein-Gar, Tormala & Shiv, "When Blemishing Leads to Blossoming" (*Journal of Consumer Research*, 2012): a small negative after positives strengthens evaluation — only under low-effort processing, small dose, positives first (https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/38/5/846/1796852; https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/positive-effect-negative-information).

**Pratfall effect.** [ESTABLISHED, qualified] Aronson (1966): a blunder increases attractiveness only for the highly competent (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03342263).

**Communicating uncertainty does not erode trust — if it's precise.** [ESTABLISHED] Van der Bles, van der Linden, Freeman & Spiegelhalter (*PNAS*, 2020; five UK surveys, n=5,780): numeric/structured uncertainty causes little to no loss of trust in the source; vague verbal hedging is what damages credibility (https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1913678117; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7149229/).

**Nonprofit-sector evidence that transparency pays.** [SINGLE-SOURCE headline; ESTABLISHED direction] Harris & Neely 53% finding (https://learn.guidestar.org/transparency; https://candid.org/blogs/research-shows-nonprofit-transparency-matters/).

**Brand cases.** [ESTABLISHED as events; CONTESTED as causal proof] Patagonia "Don't Buy This Jacket" (https://www.marketingweek.com/case-study-patagonias-dont-buy-this-jacket-campaign/); VW "Lemon" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_advertising); Oatly fckoatly.com self-callouts (https://www.oneplanetnetwork.org/news-and-events/news/oatly-case-study-communicating-food-sustainability-information-consumers; https://www.contagious.com/news-and-views/oatly-talk-marketing-strategy-john-schoolcraft).

**charity: water public failure reporting.** [ESTABLISHED as practice; CONTESTED as full accountability] "Failure on our Birthday" (https://www.huffpost.com/entry/failure-on-our-birthday_b_709303); Truthout critique: cheery transparency + non-responsiveness gets called out (https://truthout.org/articles/the-problem-with-charity-water/).

**Engineers Without Borders Canada Failure Reports.** [ESTABLISHED] Annual public Failure Reports since 2008; AdmittingFailure.org (https://www.admittingfailure.org/failure-reports/; https://this.org/2011/12/01/admitting-failure/; https://ssir.org/articles/entry/whats_next_thriving_on_failure).

## 2. Messaging Frameworks and Their Critiques

**StoryBrand (Donald Miller).** [CONTESTED] Narrative-clarity core is sound folk practice; not empirically validated as a system; critiques: formulaic sameness, manipulation risk in stakes-agitation, donor-as-hero savior-complex critique from Community-Centric Fundraising (https://columncontent.com/storybrand-method-drawbacks/; https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/storybrand-donald-miller-customer-as-hero/; https://www.nonprofitaf.com/how-donor-centrism-perpetuates-inequity-and-why-we-must-move-toward-community-centric-fundraising/; https://communitycentricfundraising.org/ccf-principles/).

**Made to Stick (Heath brothers).** [ESTABLISHED core, folk-practice packaging] SUCCESs synthesizes genuine cognitive-science findings; content-neutral clarity/memorability tools; most charter-compatible of the popular frameworks (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_to_Stick; https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/books/made-stick-why-some-ideas-survive-others-die).

**Start With Why (Sinek).** [CONTESTED] Golden Circle has essentially no empirical support; limbic-brain claims dismissed by neuroscientists; durable kernel — lead with purpose — is reasonable practice, treat as rhetoric not science (https://www.monkhouseandcompany.com/resources/insight/why-simon-sinek-is-fundamentally-wrong/; https://blog.hptbydts.com/praise-criticism-the-golden-circle-sinek).

**FrameWorks Institute.** [ESTABLISHED] Randomized framing experiments: crisis/urgency framing feeds fatalism and backfires; problem-plus-efficacy sustains engagement; episodic vs. thematic framing (https://www.frameworksinstitute.org/articles/dont-feed-fatalism-put-forward-solutions-instead/; https://www.frameworksinstitute.org/articles/episodic-vs-thematic-stories/; https://www.frameworksinstitute.org/).

**Fundraising ethics scholarship.** [ESTABLISHED] MacQuillin (*Journal of Philanthropy and Marketing*, 2023): manipulation-based solicitation damages sector-wide trust (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/nvsm.1740; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/nvsm.1754).

## 3. Messaging Controversial Technology, Especially AI Post-2023

**"AI" as a label is a trust liability.** [ESTABLISHED] Gursoy et al. (*Journal of Hospitality Marketing & Management*, 2024; 1,000+ U.S. adults): "AI-powered" vs. "high tech" significantly lowered purchase intention in every category, mediated by reduced emotional trust, worst in high-risk categories (https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/07/30/using-the-term-artificial-intelligence-in-product-descriptions-reduces-purchase-intentions/; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19368623.2024.2368040).

**Christian audience baseline.** [ESTABLISHED] Barna (2023–2025, with Gloo): pastors 71% "cautious" vs. laypeople 36%; 72% of practicing Christians concerned about AI replacing pastors/spiritual leaders; operational AI accepted, spiritual/relational territory guarded (https://www.barna.com/research/christians-ai-church/; https://www.barna.com/research/christians-view-ai-gift-threat/).

**Khanmigo (Khan Academy).** [ESTABLISHED as practice] In-product limitation statements + visible oversight architecture (parent/teacher-visible chat history, moderation alerts) + published Responsible AI framework (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/14394814244365-What-safety-features-does-Khanmigo-have; https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academys-framework-for-responsible-ai-in-education/).

**Dimensions in Testimony (USC Shoah Foundation).** [ESTABLISHED] Messaged its constraints as its ethics: pre-recorded video of real survivors, never animated, edited, or manipulated; consent questions asked before building; publicly hosted scholarly critique of its own project (https://sfi.usc.edu/dit; https://sfi.usc.edu/news/2017/11/20081-scholars-consider-ethics-possibilities-and-critiques-new-dimensions-testimony; https://reallifemag.com/speaking-for-the-past/).

**Father Justin (Catholic Answers) — the negative case.** [ESTABLISHED] April 2024: AI "priest" demoted to lay "AI apologist Justin" within ~a day; failure was persona overclaim, fix was re-labeling (https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2024/04/26/father-justin-catholic-answers-ai-247808/; https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2024/04/25/ai-priest-catholic-answers-laicized-father-justin-247799/).

**Magisterium AI / Hallow.** [ESTABLISHED as practice] Source-grounded, role-bounded framings: citation engine, explicitly not for spiritual/moral direction, "see a trusted priest or theologian"; Hallow full-screen disclaimer; no training on user questions (https://help.hallow.com/en/articles/10601094-magisterium-ai-faq; https://www.magisterium.com/; https://www.washingtonpost.com/religion/2025/07/31/catholic-ai-magisterium-pope-leo/).

**Deus in Machina (Lucerne).** [ESTABLISHED as events] ~900–1,000 substantive conversations; most negative reaction from non-users; deliberately provocative framing generated the controversy the careful framings avoided (https://www.forbes.com/sites/lesliekatz/2024/12/14/when-ai-jesus-entered-the-confessional-lessons-from-a-divisive-experiment/; https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/deus-machina-swiss-church-installs-ai-jesus-connect-digital-divine-rcna182973).

**Anthropic's safety-forward positioning.** [SINGLE-SOURCE analyses; direction consistent] Competing on credibility rather than capability; doubt-forward framing treats skepticism as legitimate (https://www.resultsense.com/insights/2026-04-13-anthropic-ai-publicity-strategy-safety-narrative/; https://www.contentgrip.com/anthropic-ai-brand-trust/).

## 4. Standards and Ethics Bodies

**ECFA Standard 7.1 (Truthfulness in Communications).** [ESTABLISHED] All representations of fact "current, complete, and accurate"; no material omissions, exaggerations, misleading photos, or "any other communication which would tend to create a false impression"; appeals must not create unrealistic expectations (https://www.ecfa.org/Content/Comment71; https://www.ecfa.org/PDF/ECFA_Seven_Standards_of_Responsible_Stewardship.pdf).

**AFP Code of Ethical Standards.** [ESTABLISHED] Standard 12: solicitation materials accurate, documentable facts only (https://afpglobal.org/ethics/code-ethical-standards; https://afpglobal.org/sites/default/files/attachments/2019-10/CodeofEthics_ENGLISH_Oct2019_FINAL.pdf).

**BBB Wise Giving Alliance Standard 15.** [ESTABLISHED] Accurate, truthful, not misleading "in whole and in part," including implied impressions (https://give.org/bbb-standards-for-charity-accountability; https://give.org/news/photos-and-images-in-fundraising-appeals).

## 5. Formation-Oriented vs. Conversion-Oriented Religious Communication

**Ethics of evangelism scholarship.** [ESTABLISHED as a literature] Thiessen, *The Ethics of Evangelism* (2011): persuasion about religion is ethical only under dignity, freedom to reject, truthful undistorted presentation; coercion/manipulation/distortion mark where evangelism becomes proselytism. 2024 JRAI ethnography: Christians themselves draw this distinction as epistemic virtue (https://christianscholars.com/the-ethics-of-evangelism-a-philosophical-defense-of-proselytizing-and-persuasion/; https://rai.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9655.14109).

**Attractional/seeker-sensitive model critique.** [ESTABLISHED as a critique tradition; SINGLE-SOURCE on the Willow Creek data point] Willow Creek "Reveal" self-study: the seeker-optimized model left about a quarter of its most mature members "stalled" (https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/reviews/gospel-driven-church/; https://www.thebibleteachersguide.com/single-post/2018/10/03/what-is-the-church-and-who-is-it-for-critiquing-arguments-for-the-seeker-sensitive-attrac; https://instrumentofmercy.com/2018/10/09/consumer-christianity/).
