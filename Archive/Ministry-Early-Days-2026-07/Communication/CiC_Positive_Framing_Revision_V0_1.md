# The Positive-Framing Revision — V0.1

**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Mark's direction (verbatim):** "the negatives around never trying to convert you etc. are not coming across correctly and yes that is in our convictions Witness not recruitment, but the negative is not working... lets stay focused on what we will do and say things positively."

**What this is NOT:** a change to the conviction. Witness-not-recruitment stays absolute, unchanged, non-negotiable — it governs the *product* (Constitution Art. 24, the refusal architecture) whether or not any sentence ever names it. **What this IS:** a change to how the brand *talks about* that conviction — leading with the affirmative identity instead of a list of refusals.

## Why the negative framing was failing (the diagnosis, confirmed by research already on file)

A brand that keeps saying "we won't convert you" does three things it doesn't intend:

1. **It plants the suspicion it's trying to dispel.** Naming a bad behavior to deny it keeps the behavior in the reader's head — the "don't think of an elephant" effect. Said once, briefly, a denial can build trust (the two-sided-messaging research already in the Kit). Said as the load-bearing *identity claim*, repeated across headline, bullets, and FAQ, it reads as defensive — like a business insisting "we are not a scam."
2. **It answers a question the reader hadn't asked yet.** A hero line that opens with what the thing refuses to do assumes the reader already suspects it. Most readers haven't gotten there — they're still asking "what *is* this?"
3. **It's the wrong half of the finding already in the Kit.** FrameWorks Institute's research (cited in the original analysis) found crisis/negative framing breeds fatalism while a stated positive path sustains engagement. CiC's own copy had been doing the framing-research equivalent of leading with the crisis.

**The fix is not removing the boundary — it's stating it from the inside.** "It will never try to convert you" and "every conclusion stays yours" are the same fact. One is a refusal about the brand; the other is a promise about the reader. The promise is what belongs in the lead.

## The rule (NEW — for the Kit's writing rules, Part 1.2; CORRECTED per Mark's read)

**Mark's correction, which sharpens the rule considerably:** contrast is not the target. *"A doorway, not a home"* is not negative framing — it's **clarifying**: naming what kind of thing this is, the way a job title clarifies a role. Nobody suspects CiC of secretly trying to be a home; the line isn't denying an accusation, it's placing the thing in a category. That survives untouched, everywhere it appears.

**What actually needs fixing is narrower and sharper: denying a suspicion nobody stated.** "It will never try to convert you" answers an implicit fear ("is this going to pressure me?") that the reader may not have consciously formed yet — and naming it to deny it plants it. That's the specific pattern:

> **Lead with what it does. Reserve denial for a suspicion the copy itself would otherwise raise.** A role-clarifying contrast ("a doorway, not a home," "a witness, not a lawyer") is not negative framing and needs no fix — keep those. A denial of an unstated accusation ("won't convert you," "no agenda," "never hides") is what gets reframed into its positive twin — the thing the denial was protecting, stated as a fact about the reader instead of a refusal about the brand.

**The FAQ exception stands as before:** a direct question gets a direct answer, including "no" or "neither" — that's honesty answering honesty, not brand-defensiveness. The rule targets *unprompted* denial, not an honest answer to a question actually asked.

### Tier 2 — the measurement line: four register options (his call)

**"A doorway, not a home" is confirmed KEPT, unchanged, everywhere** — it's a clarifying contrast, not a denial, per the corrected rule above. Retracted from the flagged list.

**The measurement line is the live question.** Mark likes the direction — "and leave the impact to God" — but flagged a real risk: does the *verb* ("leave it to God") read as stock evangelical rhetoric to exactly the audiences the mission most wants to reach — the seeker with no church background, and the reevaluation persona who left because that rhetoric stopped sounding true? The diagnosis: it isn't the word "God" that's the problem (he already confirmed God-language is fine, ruling out "the Holy Spirit" earlier today for a different reason — insider vocabulary). It's the specific *template* — "leave/give/trust X to God" is the idiom people reach for reflexively to end a conversation ("just leave it to God," "let go and let God"). Four ways to keep the same meaning without that template:

| # | Candidate | Register | Best for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | "…What it becomes belongs to God, not us." | Declarative — a fact stated, not an act of releasing | Keeps God explicit and plain; "belongs to" is ownership-language, not devotional-idiom language | Still names God directly — fine if that's the intent |
| B | **"…The harvest was never ours to produce, so it's not ours to count."** | Scriptural echo (sower and growth, 1 Cor. 3:6-7), no idiom, no direct "God" | Already tested and living in the Proposal Packet's own measurement paragraph today — proven register, not a new risk | Requires the reader to feel the echo even without knowing the verse; works because "harvest/produce/count" is vivid on its own |
| C | "…What it becomes in you is yours — not ours to claim." | Fully secular-safe; ties directly to "every conclusion stays yours" from Tier 1, unifying two protected lines around one idea (agency) | Safest for the reevaluation persona specifically — the core wound there is often *lost autonomy*; this hands it back explicitly | Drops explicit God-reference — may feel like it's dodging the very thing Mark wants stated (kingdom-centered, not academic) |
| D | "…What it becomes is not ours to measure. That's bigger than us." | Minimal change — keeps "that's bigger than us" doing all the transcendence work it already does, adds nothing new | Lowest-risk, most conservative option | Weakest theologically — doesn't add the "trust/faith" note his Session 1 answer wanted |

**A consultant's lean, held loosely:** B. It's already written, in your own project, tested in a real donor document today without objection — it says the same thing "leave it to God" says, but through image instead of idiom, which is exactly the kind of language that survives both a seminary reader and someone who's never opened a Bible. But this is the most load-bearing line in the kit — your call, not mine.

## DECIDED (2026-07-18) — Mark's own line, arrived at through live iteration

Mark tried three versions in sequence — "…belongs to Him, not us" → "…belongs to our Lord, not us" → **"How it impacts the kingdom belongs to our Lord, not us"** — landing on the third. **Final, applied everywhere:**

> **"We measure whether each Christian movement is represented in an engaging and accurate way. How it impacts the kingdom belongs to our Lord. That's bigger than us."**
>
> *(Trimmed once more, same day: Mark cut "not us" — the last small negation in an otherwise positive line. "Belongs to our Lord" already says it; "that's bigger than us" carries the rest.)*

**What his own iteration found, better than any option on the table above:** naming *the kingdom* as what's impacted — not just "impact" in the abstract — ties the line directly back to his own Session 1 mission language ("trusting Jesus will be revealed… in the grand narrative of the kingdom of God," "we want it to be about the kingdom, not an academic experience"). This isn't generic God-language; it's *his* specific theological register, arrived at independently twice in one day. "Our Lord" also reads as warmer and more personal than generic "God" while staying legible to a reader with no church background — broadly understood without requiring trinitarian literacy the way "the Holy Spirit" did (which he'd already ruled out earlier for exactly that reason).

**Applied to all nine live locations** where the line appeared: the Kit (message architecture + the canonical Protected Verbal Assets registry, with the full revision trail preserved in the rationale), the QuickRef, the Brand Brief, the FAQ, the Elevator Speeches (live copy + delivery notes), the funding-thread briefing, the donor support page, and both Marketplace documents that quote the line. Historical change-log entries and the Kit's gap-list item 10 were left untouched as dated records, with a pointer added so nobody executes stale instructions.

**Resolved:** the repetition flagged above is gone — Mark cut "not us" himself, which was exactly the redundant clause. "That's bigger than us" now carries all the weight it always did, cleanly.

### Tier 3 — context-dependent; kept, with the rule stated

- **FAQ direct answers** ("Neither." "This is a doorway and a teaching companion, not a congregation." "Are you putting words in historical figures' mouths?" → direct no) — **kept as the exception**: these are plain answers to posed skeptical questions, which is what an honest FAQ does. Tightened only where a positive lead is available without dodging the question (e.g., "This is a doorway and a teaching companion" can stand alone — the next sentences already clarify without needing "not a congregation" appended).
- **Onboarding/consent copy** ("You're not talking to a single historical person." "A Representative will not modernize itself, defend itself like a lawyer, or tell you what to think.") — **kept, different register**: this is informed-consent language read once before a participant begins, closer to a nutrition label than a pitch. Precision matters more than positive framing here. Not touched by this revision.
- **Mark's own bio answer** ("I am not a credentialed historian… and I have never pretended to be") — **kept untouched**: personal humility in his own voice, not brand-defensiveness. Rewriting it into positive marketing-speak would flatten something true and disarming into something managed.

## What this changes in the Kit itself (Part 1.2, new rule; Part 4 protected lines)

- New writing rule added (above) as **rule 11**.
- Protected-lines list updated: "Every conclusion stays yours" replaces "It will never try to convert you" as the primary protected line (pending your word on Tier 2's two flagged lines).
- The five voice pairs are **unaffected** — they're the internal check, not output copy; "witness but not recruiting" still tells a writer what to test a sentence against. What changes is that a sentence passing the test now gets written in its positive form, not its refusal form.

## What's NOT touched (the conviction, unchanged)

Constitution Article 24 (forbids persuasion), the refusal architecture in the product itself, the Facilitator's actual behavior, the validation battery's adversarial testing for recruitment-drift — **none of this changes.** The product still structurally cannot pressure anyone toward a conclusion. This revision only changes how the brand *talks about* that fact.
