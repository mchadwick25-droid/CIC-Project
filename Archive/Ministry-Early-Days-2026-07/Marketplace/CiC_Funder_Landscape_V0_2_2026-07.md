## Part 2 — What this table changes about the funding strategy

1. **No open, general-nonprofit-eligible Christian-funder call exists for CiC to apply
   to today.** Every clean-eligibility door (Exploring Christian Practices, JTF's OFI,
   Wabash's 2026 cycle) has already closed for this round. This is a timing fact, not
   a fit problem — the real work now is **tracking reopenings** (Exploring Christian
   Practices' next cycle, JTF's next OFI window, Wabash's next RFP) rather than
   writing proposals against closed doors.
2. **The seminary-partnership sequence already in the growth plan is now doubly
   justified.** It isn't only the Article 31 review-and-credibility play — it is the
   literal eligibility gate for the largest pools of money in this space (Lilly's
   FORWARD-style digital sub-grants, Wabash, arguably Luce). Landing one anchor
   seminary doesn't just validate the classroom pilot; it opens funding doors CiC
   cannot open alone.
3. **Future of Life Institute is the one genuine surprise and should move up the
   priority list ahead of Lilly.** CiC doesn't need to reframe anything to fit its
   ask — the Constitution's anti-persuasion, anti-engagement-optimization design is
   already the exact artifact the RFP wants funded. Watch for its next cycle
   actively; this is the best single-sentence grant pitch CiC has anywhere in this
   research: *"we already built the theological red line you're funding people to
   propose."*
4. **NEH Digital Humanities Advancement Grants is open now, with a real near-term
   deadline (Sept 16, 2026), and requires no religious-funder framing at all** — it
   funds the construction methodology on its own secular, federal terms. Given the
   Acceleration Plan's own reviewer-recruitment and validation timeline, this is
   worth a serious look as a parallel, faster-moving track to the Christian-funder
   list above.

## Part 3 — Updated positioning statement (upgraded program, brand-compliant)

For funder/partner use — every phrase checked against the approved Messaging &
Branding Kit (2026-07-17); the protected verbatim measurement line is used exactly
as finalized, not paraphrased:

> Church in Conversation rebuilds documented Christian movements from their own
> words, through a review-gated scholarly construction pipeline, and lets people sit
> down and talk with them — several at once, seated together at one table, agreeing
> and disagreeing openly, exactly as history holds them. Five movements are live
> today — a first-century house-church network, the Egyptian desert, the Alexandria
> catechetical school, Syriac Christianity of Edessa-Nisibis, and a Bethlehem
> ascetic-literary circle — each with its own built visual identity, each speaking
> through a voice locked to primary sources, showing its own confidence as it talks:
> every claim carries how well it's attested, down to the footnote, before anyone
> asks. It is governed by a written constitution that forbids persuasion and
> engagement-maximization by design — the opposite of an AI companion or a
> historical-figure chatbot. No product we could find anywhere else does this:
> reconstructs a Christian movement rather than a named individual, seats several
> such voices together in visible disagreement, or shows calibrated uncertainty
> inside the conversation itself. We measure whether each Christian movement is
> represented in an engaging and accurate way. How it impacts the kingdom belongs
> to our Lord. That's bigger than us.

## Part 4 — Competitive/differentiation refresh (2026-07-18)

Both of the V0.1 scan's core claims **hold, re-verified today**, including
specifically against the Living Table's multi-voice pattern — the closest thing to a
competitor search found was generic multi-LLM debate tools (MultipleChat.ai, Forge)
and Magisterium's Saint Chat "debate mode," both individual-named-figure vs.
individual-named-figure, never community-vs-community, and neither calibrated.

**Two prior claims should be dropped, not restated** — the research could not
re-verify them and treats their earlier inclusion as stale: the "Private Forsythe"
period-soldier pilot (no trace found in any 2026 source) and the live/dead status of
Ask Dalí or the Met's "Chat with Natalie" (no news either way — genuinely unknown,
not confirmed-shut-down).

**One sharpened point for the "AI companion" category-exit:** Character.AI's
regulatory situation has escalated materially since the V0.1 scan — a Pennsylvania
AG suit (May 2026) over chatbots impersonating licensed medical professionals, a
COPPA overhaul (Jan 2026) requiring separate parental consent for AI training data,
and the Google settlement (Jan 2026) — all *after* V0.1 was written. The "opposite of
an AI companion" positioning line has gotten stronger, not staler, with time.
