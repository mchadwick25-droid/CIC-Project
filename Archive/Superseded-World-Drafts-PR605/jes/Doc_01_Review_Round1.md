## Findings by severity

### Major (substantial — each changes a claim's substance or a sourcing conclusion)

**M1. The Monte Cassino footnote is misread. The claim built on it is wrong in §1, §3 and §6.**
The draft says Ignatius "gave each of the early companions gathered at Monte Cassino a copy of *De Contemptu Mundi*" (§1). §6 repeats this as "the Imitation of Christ given to each of them at Monte Cassino", and §3 refers to it again. The vendored footnote (`francis-xavier_life-and-letters-v1_coleridge1872.txt`, lines 4531–4536) reads: "he gave each **monk** at Monte Cassino a copy of the book de Contemptu Mundi, i.e. the Imitation of Christ." The recipients were the Benedictine monks of the abbey, not the companions. The narrative around the footnote (lines 4494–4504) has Ignatius at Monte Cassino with Dr. Ortiz, giving him the Exercises. Step 0 §3 B2 had this right ("each monk"). The draft brought the error in while claiming it had been "directly re-verified against the vendored file". §8 repeats that verification claim for "every quoted or closely paraphrased claim", so the self-certification is also inaccurate.
Related points:
- The draft cites "pp. 4531–36, 3506–09". These are line numbers in the vendored file, not page numbers. The printed pages are pp. 29 and 49. "Autobiography … ff. 361–400" has the same problem.
- The draft missed a much stronger anchor that is primary and in Ignatius's own voice. The Exercises themselves, Second Week, Second Note (`ignatius-loyola_spiritual-exercises_mullan1914.txt`, lines 2216–2219), read: "it is very helpful to read at intervals in the books of the Imitation of Christ, or of the Gospels, and of lives of Saints."
- The Coleridge material is an 1872 editor relaying a later chronicler (Alcazar). The Devotio Moderna Step 0 already notes it is "not in Xavier's own words."
Fix: correct the recipients. Rest the inheritance claim on the Exercises' own Second Note, with the Paris reading-practice passage (Coleridge p. 29) as corroboration. Keep the Alcazar footnote only as what it is: evidence of Ignatius giving the Imitation away. Replace line numbers with page references, or label them clearly as line numbers. Remove the blanket "every claim verified" wording.

**M2. The status of Nadal's *Scholia* and Nadal's *Adnotationes* is swapped in §3 and §5. This understates the primary institutional evidence available.**
§3 says "the Constitutions and Nadal's *Scholia* explaining them — both vendored, both held at second-witness status". §5 says the Constitutions "and Nadal's *Scholia* … sits at second-witness status … (both are vendored but flagged as garbled scans; Step 0 §3 B1, §4 item 1)".
- Step 0 §3 B1 and §4 item 1 name the **1606 Constitutions and the 1595 *Adnotationes*** as the garbled, second-witness texts.
- The corpus-map entry for *Scholia in Constitutiones* (1883) reads "the scan is clean, not flagged under Mark's OCR ruling". The *Scholia* are quotable primary text.
- §7 item 1 gets this right, so the draft contradicts itself.
This matters beyond wording. §5 concludes that the order's refusals can only be characterized from the founder letters "rather than on the order's own fullest institutional self-statement". That is a sourcing conclusion built on the error. Nadal's *Scholia*, a clean, primary, first-generation commentary on the Constitutions, is available.
Fix: correct §3 and §5, and revise §5's evidentiary-limit paragraph to match. Only the Constitutions text and the *Adnotationes* are second-witness.

**M3. The Trent distinctiveness argument leaves out the Jesuits' own part in Trent. That is the strongest overlap, and it is attested in the vendored corpus.**
The argument is honestly two-sided in structure (see "Confirmed accurate"). But it describes Trent as "a program the Society did not itself convene or author" and sets up the relationship as "implementing and executing, not originating" (§6). It never mentions that Lainez and Salmeron went to Trent as papal theologians. This world's own vendored Latin volumes record it:
- Salmeron, *Epistolae* Tomus Primus (`salmeron_epistolae-v2-lat_1906.txt`): "ab Ignatio Romam accersitur, ut ad concilium tridentinum ex summi pontificis voluntate se conferat" (c. line 509); "inter theologos pontificios in concilio sententiam suam dicere" (c. line 701).
- Lainez, *Epistolae et Acta* I (`lainez_epistolae-et-acta-v1-lat_1912.txt`), c. lines 313, 432, 889.
This does not collapse the distinction. The Society did not convene Trent, and theological input is not authorship of conciliar decrees. But leaving it out makes the "separate" reading look cleaner than the evidence allows. Two smaller overstatements sit in the same passage:
- (a) "Trent's own conciliar output … legislates sacramental discipline and doctrinal boundary rather than personal spiritual formation" is broadly fair. But Trent did legislate clerical formation: the seminary decree (Session XXIII, ch. XVIII; Waterworth c. lines 19938ff). The claim should be narrowed to *this kind* of individually undergone discernment, as the draft half-does.
- (b) "Trent's own missionary instrument, the later Propaganda Fide (1622)" is inaccurate. Propaganda Fide was a papal congregation erected by Gregory XV, not an organ of the Council. Step 0 §3 B4 correctly attributes it to "the Tridentine Church's own census entry", not to Trent.
Fix: add the Jesuit-theologians-at-Trent overlap, with its vendored citation, to the "executing instrument" reading. Soften "did not … author" accordingly. Qualify the formation claim against the seminary decree. Correct the Propaganda Fide attribution.

**M4. Forces Cell 2B treats a vendoring gap as a historical transmission force.**
The Transmission dimension says the missionary/global voice "is preserved disproportionately in the founding generation only (to Xavier's 1552 death) — a real survivorship asymmetry … not a neutral gap". That describes this project's acquisition state, not historical survivorship. Historically, Jesuit missionary writing after 1552 survives in great volume:
- the Jesuit Relations (1632–73)
- Ricci's journals
- the printed *Cartas* from Japan
- the annual letters
Step 0 itself calls this gap a sourcing condition to be closed by acquisition. Under the Forces Framework, Transmission Specificity (Principle 5) asks who transmitted the world's material and with what selection effects. The one real historical transmission force here is barely named. The order centralized its correspondence in the Roman archive, and later edited and published it itself (the MHSI series, from 1894, is the source of most of the vendored Latin volumes).
Fix: take the vendoring-coverage statement out of Cell 2B. It belongs in §2/§7 as a sourcing disclosure, where it already appears. Replace it with a Layer-1 transmission observation that stands up historically: the order's own Roman archival centralization and self-editing, and what that selection favours. Mark it Widely Accepted and leave Doc_08 to develop it.

### Moderate

**S5. A stale dossier date has come back in Cell 3A: "1704/1742/1774".** No rites condemnation took place in 1774. This is the dossier §6 error that Step 0 Revision 3 explicitly corrected (Benedict XIV's Malabar condemnation was 1744; 1773 is the suppression). §2 of the draft itself gives 1744 correctly, so the two sections now disagree. Fix: 1704/1742/1744.

**S6. Two phrases are put in quotation marks without any vendored source.**
- "regarding the missions" (the fourth vow, §1). This phrase is not in the vendored English Ignatian texts. The Formula of the Institute is not vendored. "circa missiones" returns 0 hits in the vendored Constitutions or *Scholia*.
- "finding God in all things" (§1). This is presented as the Exercises' own orientation, but the phrase does not occur in the vendored Mullan Exercises. It is a later Ignatian formula associated with Nadal and the Constitutions tradition.
Under the source-fidelity rule, quotation marks are a claim of verbatim sourcing. Fix: either source each phrase to a vendored locus, or drop the quotation marks and state it as paraphrase at the right confidence level. For the second phrase, the Exercises' own Contemplation to Attain Love is the vendored anchor to use.

**S7. "Conversion (1521, at Pamplona …)" (§1) and "1521 conversion at Pamplona and Manresa" (Cell 1B) put the conversion in the wrong place.** The draft's own cited passage (Autobiography, lines 361–400) has Ignatius wounded at Pamplona and then "borne on a litter to Loyola". The conversion took place during his convalescence at Loyola. §5 describes this correctly, so the sections disagree. Fix: "wounded at Pamplona (1521); conversion during convalescence at Loyola; Manresa (1522–23)."

**S8. The Geographic Centers section gives "Japan and China (Xavier's final years to his 1552 death)" at Documented confidence.** Xavier never entered mainland China. He died on Shangchuan Island waiting for passage. Listing China as a documented founding-generation mission center overstates the vendored record. Fix: "Japan (1549–51), and Xavier's attempt to enter China, ending with his death off the coast in 1552."

**S9. Strand Determination: the reasoning mode is valid, but two supports are weak.**
- The draft asserts that Xavier's vendored letters show adaptation "continuous with, not yet clearly divergent from" the order's shared formation logic. It cites no passage. That is an unexamined claim in the opposite direction, of the same kind the section itself forbids. Either cite representative Xavier passages, or say the check has not yet been run on Coleridge I–II and pass it to Doc_04.
- "Dominant Modern Reconstruction" is the confidence vocabulary for historical claims that carry scholarly consensus. Here it is applied to this build's own working construction finding, which rests on absent evidence. The tag implies a scholarly consensus that is neither cited nor likely to exist. Label it as a construction working disposition with its evidentiary basis stated, or at most Inferential-Thin.
This changes a confidence rating, so it counts as substantial under the stated definition, though it is small in scope.

### Minor

**m10. The time-horizon endpoint argument is honest in structure but padded.**
- "Widely Accepted for 'founding century' as a periodization" overclaims for 1650. The order's own centenary retrospective, *Imago primi saeculi*, is dated 1640, and 1650 is the census window's convention.
- "1650 sits after the order's own first four Superior Generals" does not argue anything: by 1650 there had been eight.
State plainly that 1650 is the census/Step 0 window, defended as a century-plus span that closes before the eighteenth-century condemnations. The honest core is already there.

**m11. §1 says the Society is "one of the largest" orders; §6 says "the largest religious order of men".** The two sections disagree, and §6 tags an unsourced present-day claim as Documented. Use the §1 wording.

**m12. §7 item 7 (the dossier staleness disclosure) is correctly its own Open Item but lists only two superseded entries.** The dossier is also stale on:
- §1: the Constitutions and *Adnotationes* are called "PRIMARY", which the scan ruling supersedes.
- §1: Trent's confidence is given as "provisional"; the corpus-map now has "assigned".
- §6: it says Trent's canons "explicitly reaffirm" Nicaea and Chalcedon, which Step 0 A1 contradicts.
- §6: it treats the Rites as "correctly out-of-window" and gives "1774", both corrected by Step 0.
List these too, so the refresh pass has the full set.

**m13. The draft attributes to Step 0 §1 the claim that Ignatius "directed the order's rapid expansion by letter".** Step 0 §1 does not say this. The claim is Widely Accepted in its own right; cite it as that, not to Step 0.

**m14. The preliminary six-cell table carries no confidence levels.** The Forces Framework (Layer 1) says "every force documented at Layer 1 carries its confidence level explicitly." The prose in §5 does carry them; the table should too, or point to the prose.

**m15. The Viola letter is dated in the edition itself as "Rome (August, 1542?)".** The question mark is the editor's. The draft gives "1542" three times as settled. Use "1542 (date uncertain in the edition)" once.
