## Architecture note (read this before the rest)

The LangGraph `StateGraph` in `graph/builder.py` is **only invoked once per session**, in `POST /api/session/start` (`main.py` ~line 270, `graph.invoke(initial_state)`), to run `facilitator_receives -> facilitator_handoff -> END`. Neither message endpoint (`POST /api/session/{id}/message` or `.../message/stream`) ever calls `graph.invoke()` again — both import `representative_engages`, `facilitator_monitors`, `facilitator_reroots`, `facilitator_closes`, `select_next_speaker`, and all five classify-then-route intercepts as plain Python functions and hand-wire the control flow directly in `main.py`. The graph's own docstring diagram (`builder.py` lines 22-60, a wait-for-input loop with `route_after_monitoring`'s reroot/wait/close conditional edges) describes a flow that is never actually exercised at runtime for an ongoing conversation — the real orchestration (frame-breaker, relational-safety, epistemology-bridge, modern-term-bridge, closing-sequence, multi-world turn selection, per-round drift checks) exists entirely outside the graph, in `main.py`'s two endpoint bodies. `graph/agents/facilitator.py` and `graph/agents/representative.py` are dead placeholder modules (18 and 26 lines) that say outright "logic is implemented in graph/nodes.py" and are never imported anywhere else; `representative.py`'s own descriptive constant is hardcoded to Mar Yausep/Syriac only, stale against the 6-world manifest.

## 1. Rigor (accuracy, sourcing, anti-fabrication)

**Two-stage FABRICATION detection.** Stage 1 is one of ten signals in the general drift monitor (`FACILITATOR_MONITORING_PROMPT`, `facilitator_prompts.py` lines 132-173; signal 7 defined at 150), reading only the response text with no access to sources. Stage 2, `_adjudicate_fabrication` (`nodes.py` 1655-1696) and `FABRICATION_ADJUDICATION_PROMPT` (`facilitator_prompts.py` 176-215), re-judges only stage-1-flagged content against the representative's actual permanent prompt, world capsule, and freshly-retrieved material (`_gather_world_evidence`, `nodes.py` 1855-1904). Stated rationale, quoted directly: stage 1 "can only see whether the response *sounds* attributed," which is "prone to flagging correctly-grounded content that happens to be narrated plainly, and prone to missing invented content that happens to carry an attribution phrase" — confirmed live: "Papnoute naming Antony and citing Athanasius passed clean, while the same attested material narrated as 'he' was flagged high-severity fabrication on the next turn." The adjudicator fails open **toward FABRICATED** on genuine uncertainty (docstring: "the two errors are not symmetrical... a missed fabrication is the cardinal sin of this system, Facilitator Governance Section 11").

**Two-stage OVER_SETTLING detection** (a claim spoken more firmly than the world's own record holds it) is structurally identical but was built as its own dedicated screen-then-adjudicate pair (`_screen_over_settling`/`_over_settling_signal`/`_adjudicate_over_settling`, `nodes.py` 1728-1852; prompts at `facilitator_prompts.py` 218-293) after the general 10-signal monitor was measured to catch "0 of 3 defects that blind grading had already confirmed" for this specific failure mode — "a turn that reads well overall reads as clean." Deliberately **opposite** asymmetry from fabrication: the adjudicator fails open **toward CLEARED** on uncertainty, because "a false OVER_SETTLED... pushes a representative to hedge a claim its own world actually held with conviction, which manufactures false uncertainty" — an explicit, reasoned choice to run the two adjudicators' error-tolerance in opposite directions.

**Retrieval as a rigor gate, not just context.** `LexiconRetriever`/`StoryRetriever` (`rag/retriever.py`, `rag/story_retriever.py`) implement "Retrieve-When / Do-Not-Retrieve-When" conditions authored per lexicon/story entry. Tier-1 ("this world's own declared center of gravity") candidates ranking near the top of semantic search short-circuit straight to retrieval without an LLM vote (`batch_evaluate.partition_tier1_short_circuit`, `rag/batch_evaluate.py` 45-95) — added because asking the LLM to judge Tier-1 relevance "generously" was tried three ways and "still reverted to literal keyword-matching... once real conversation context was present." Every candidate the retriever considered — retrieved or skipped, and why — is preserved as a `RetrievalEvaluation` and exposed via `GET /api/session/{id}/audit` (`main.py` 1353-1413), a reviewer-facing rigor audit trail distinct from the participant-facing citations.

**Source Registry resolution** (`rag/source_registry.py`): inline citations like "(Source Registry #26, cross-checked)" are resolved to full registry rows (author, date, confidence, boundary status) via `resolve_references`, attached to both lexicon and story citations.

**Self-disclosed evidentiary confidence per world**, in `world_manifest.py`'s `facilitator_cautions` field: e.g. Albina/`hieronymian-ascetic-literary` is flagged "the newest and least live-tested of the four worlds... any multi-world pairing involving Albina should be treated as unevidenced" (lines 262-264); Marius/`imperial-juridical-christianity` similarly "the newest... any multi-world pairing should be treated as thin evidence until proven otherwise" (378-381). This is Facilitator-only, never voiced — an internal honesty mechanism about the system's own confidence level, not a participant-facing disclosure.

**Anti-fabrication pronoun/scene discipline** lives in `representative_prompts.py`'s `_HOW_YOU_ENGAGE` (lines 28-30): the "we"-not-"I" rule is explicitly tied to fabrication, not just style — "a memory that never happened is no more true attached to a whole people than to one person in it" — with worked failure/success examples for a representative reaching for an invented concrete scene even in the collective voice.

**Incompleteness/inconsistency noted in the code itself:** `_adjudicate_over_settling` costs a full ~9,250-token adjudication call (mitigated by `_cached_adjudication_message`, see Cost section); the retrieved-material silence caveat is explicit both adjudication prompts state that "silence in the retrieved section alone is not proof of absence" (only permanent-prompt/capsule silence counts as meaningful) — a real evidentiary limit on how confidently either adjudicator can clear or confirm a finding.

## 2. Voice (per-world construction, distinctiveness)

**Three-segment prompt assembly**, `build_representative_prompt` (`prompts/representative_prompts.py` 79-201): a byte-identical `static_prompt` (permanent prompt + world capsule + `_HOW_YOU_ENGAGE` + confirmed-gloss guidance), a conditionally-present `reactive_guidance_block`, and a per-turn `dynamic_prompt` (RAG context, reroot correction) — split specifically so the first two can each be an Anthropic prompt-cache breakpoint (see Cost section).

**`_HOW_YOU_ENGAGE`** (same file, lines 5-76) is the universal voice-discipline block every Representative shares: "Total Embeddedness" (never aware of being an AI/reconstruction — the single hardest-to-hold rule, with an explicit worked list of "tells that this has gone wrong"), the "I" vs "we" collective-voice rule, "Avoid the Generic Register" (names the default modern-AI hedge-then-balance shape as itself "a voice, a modern one"), "Avoid Mechanical AI Prose Habits" (specifically bans the em-dash-as-connector tic and the "it is not X, it is Y" reflexive opener as habits "none of these worlds' own actual voices... would produce"), and "Let the Question Set the Shape, Not a Habit" (flags a voice that produces "the same length and the same internal shape... turn after turn" as itself a failure signal).

**Per-world differentiation mechanisms**, all keyed off `world_manifest.py`'s single-source-of-truth `WORLD_MANIFEST` (399 lines, 6 worlds): `permanent_prompt`/`world_capsule` files loaded per world (`config.WorldConfig`), `facilitator_cautions` (never voiced, Facilitator-only, e.g. Marius "holds all three strands... as one unresolved 'we'... should not be pressed into declaring one side correct"), and world-specific hard length ceilings for two worlds whose own Permanent Prompt states a numeric measure (`_WORLD_LENGTH_CEILINGS`, `nodes.py` 2471-2484: desert-monasticism=60 words, hieronymian-ascetic-literary=180 words) with a documented, tuned regenerate-once mechanism (`stream_representative_turn`, `nodes.py` ~1294-1364) after "soft guidance had already been tested against twice... and still missed badly."

**Cross-world vocabulary segregation** at generation time: the multi-world dynamic prompt (`nodes.py` 1033-1054) explicitly lists each other seated world's own terms ("their words... belong to THEIR formation, not yours") and instructs translation into the speaker's own vocabulary — enforced after the fact by `check_cross_world_vocabulary_drift` (see Safety section).

**`REACTIVE_TURN_GUIDANCE`/`OPENING_TURN_LARGE_TABLE_GUIDANCE`** (`prompts/table_discourse.py`, 96 lines) govern voice specifically *in relation to other voices at the table*: naming what another rep actually said, disagreement-shape diversity ("do not default to a single... template regardless of which world you are"), and an extensive "Do Not Manufacture Resolution" section with a worked, multi-paragraph account of convergence "arriving gradually... a borrowed image, with no shared vocabulary at all."

**Incompleteness noted:** the lane-based register system (`general`/`academic`/`pastor-teacher`, `_LANE_LENGTH_CEILINGS`, `nodes.py` 2513-2517) is fully authored with a measured rationale ("lanes told to hold 90-140 words came in at 169 and 197") but is **structurally unreachable** — see Selection/Safety incompleteness note below; `state.participant_role` does not exist as a field, so this voice-register mechanism never actually fires in practice today.

## 3. Reading level / plain language

This is the **thinnest** of the seven dimensions relative to how developed the others are. What exists:

**Confirmed-gloss system** (`prompts/confirmed_glosses.py`, 215 lines) — the most concrete plain-language mechanism in the codebase. A per-world, project-owner-reviewed list of exact bracket-gloss renderings (`CONFIRMED_GLOSSES` dict, 56-156), two patterns: Category A ("a sign tied to a hidden truth (raza)") and Category B ("the day named for the sun (Sunday)"). `get_gloss_guidance` (159-188) instructs the Representative to use the *exact* wording verbatim — "the only place in your own speech where an exact form is asked of you." `find_glosses_used` (191-215) is a real post-hoc detection pass (case-insensitive substring match) that surfaces which confirmed glosses actually appeared, attached to the message and streamed to the frontend (`speaker_end` event's `glosses_used` field, `main.py` ~1165). Explicitly scoped: "Medium-confidence and flagged... items are reviewed and added one at a time, per the project owner's own request" — not a fully-automated glossing system, a curated whitelist.

**Length/brevity constraints double as readability constraints**: `_HOW_YOU_ENGAGE`'s "A Turn Has a Measure" (most turns one to two short paragraphs), `REACTIVE_TURN_MAX_TOKENS`/`PRIMARY_TURN_MAX_TOKENS`/`OPENING_TURN_MAX_TOKENS` (`nodes.py` 73-140), and the `_WORLD_LENGTH_CEILINGS`/`OVER_PRODUCING` monitor signal (`facilitator_prompts.py` 144) all constrain *length*, which is adjacent to but not the same as reading-level/complexity.

**The anachronism bridge** (`modern_term_bridge.py`) is dimension-crossing: its whole purpose is giving a modern reader a neutral plain-language gloss of a period-anachronistic term ("Give its modern sense, neutrally," `FACILITATOR_MODERN_TERM_BRIDGE_PROMPT`) — the task groups it under Safety/harnessing and I report it fully there, but note it here as the other real plain-language mechanism in the system.

**What is absent:** no quantitative readability metric (grade level, sentence-length distribution, vocabulary-frequency check) exists anywhere in the pipeline. No monitoring signal flags "used unglossed period jargon a modern reader wouldn't know" outside the pre-curated confirmed-gloss list — an obscure term not on that list gets no plain-language treatment at all beyond whatever the Representative's own prompt happens to do. Sentence-complexity is never measured or constrained directly; only paragraph/word counts are.

## 4. Tiered access

**Session-count cap**, `session_cap.py` (62 lines) — the one genuinely identity-based mechanism. `check_and_reserve_session_slot` (32-61) counts existing rows in the Supabase `sessions` table per `user.user_id` against `profiles.max_sessions` (default 5). Off entirely if Supabase isn't configured, **and** off for any anonymous request even when Supabase *is* configured, per `auth.py`'s documented 2026-07-25 product decision: "sign-in is an ask for this pilot, not a requirement... a request with no signed-in identity has nothing to count sessions against and is let through." Net effect stated plainly in the module docstring: "This cap only actually bites for a participant who chose to sign in."

**`AuthedUser`** (`auth.py` 36-39, `get_current_user` 53-97): loads `user_id`, `pilot_cohort`, `max_sessions` from Supabase Auth + `profiles` table, verified via `Authorization: Bearer` header. Returns an unlimited dev placeholder (`max_sessions=999`) both when Supabase is unconfigured and when a request carries no token — only a *present-but-invalid* token is rejected (401).

**Flat, identity-free conversation-length cap**, `message_cap.py` (46 lines): `CONVERSATION_TURN_CAP = 20`, checked against `state.turn_count` before every Representative turn (`check_message_cap`), on both endpoints (`main.py` 500-512, 676-693). This is explicitly the *replacement* for `session_cap.py`'s protection under the pilot's actual current design (no sign-in, no invite codes): "session_cap.py's own... check is a permanent no-op" without Supabase, so this "stays on unconditionally" as "the actual safety net for exactly the no-Supabase case."

**Referral/invite mechanism** (`main.py` 337-455): `generate_referral`/`redeem_referral` endpoints set `max_sessions: 5` on a newly-invited profile and enforce one-hop-only referral (refuses if `profiles.referred_by_invite_id` is already set) — a tier-shaping mechanism for growth, not conversation quality per se, but it is the only place `max_sessions` is actually assigned in code (besides the schema default).

**World-count cap** (`main.py` ~226): `request.world_ids[:3]` — a **flat, non-tiered** product-scope limit ("Cap at 3 worlds max (Prototype/Phase 1 scope — cost and complexity)"), applied identically to every user regardless of identity.

**Incompleteness noted:** `pilot_cohort` (`auth.py` 38, populated from `profiles.pilot_cohort`) is loaded on every authenticated request but **never read anywhere else in the codebase** to gate a feature, world, or limit — confirmed by repo-wide search; it exists purely as manual-review metadata for Mark, not a functioning tier. As currently designed, the *only* limit that reliably binds on real (mostly anonymous, mostly unsigned-in) pilot traffic today is the flat 20-turn `message_cap`, not the identity-tiered `session_cap` — the tiered mechanism is fully built and coherent but is, by explicit product decision, mostly inactive in the pilot's actual current operating mode.

## 5. Selection process (next-speaker / turn-taking)

**`select_next_speaker`** (`nodes.py` 2187-2322) is the core mechanism for the streaming endpoint's multi-world rounds. Governing principle, quoted: "not rotation, not equal time, but which voice is most directly positioned to meet this specific moment" (Facilitator Governance V3.6 Section 8). It tracks both per-round (`already_spoken`) and **cross-round, whole-conversation** speaking history (`total_turns` computed from the full message list, 2261-2264) specifically because "a world silent for several consecutive rounds is invisible to a per-round count." Hard rule: no immediate self-repeat. A cheap short-circuit skips the LLM call entirely when `must_continue=True` and only one candidate exists (2228-2236) — "the LLM call would only ever confirm the sole candidate the fallback logic would pick anyway." When `must_continue=False`, the selector may also return `NONE` ("a round does not have to keep going until someone forces a point"), and an unparseable/invalid LLM response under `must_continue=True` falls back to the first eligible candidate rather than silently ending the round (2315-2321).

**`determine_turn_type`** (`nodes.py` 2065-2161) decides single-vs-all before speaker selection even begins, with an explicit conservative default: "When genuinely in doubt between single and all, prefer single — a table where every question fans out to every world flattens into a panel" (line 2126).

**Turn-count floor/ceiling**, `main.py` 833-866: `MIN_MULTI_WORLD_TURNS = 2` (forces at least one real exchange, tuned down from 3 per "the historical forms this table hosts include the one-sentence desert word — the floor must be low enough for that form to exist"), `MAX_MULTI_WORLD_TURNS = 6` (pure cost/latency backstop, re-tested against a since-fixed truncation bug and restored from a temporary 4 back to 6, with measured "150s end to end... a real cost worth knowing, not a reliability problem"). `must_continue = turns_completed < MIN_MULTI_WORLD_TURNS` gates whether `select_next_speaker` is even allowed to return `None`.

**`pending_guidance`** (`state.py` 90-95): per-world-keyed correction queue populated by dominance/convergence/length/question-stacking/drift checks and consumed exactly once by the next turn that world takes (`nodes.py` 966, 1120) — ensures correction "reaches only the representative who actually drifted, not whoever happens to speak next."

**Inconsistency found:** the **non-streaming** `/api/session/{id}/message` endpoint uses a materially different, simpler multi-world mechanism — `multi_representative_engages` (`nodes.py` 1424-1489) marches through `determine_turn_type`'s chosen `responding_worlds` list once, in fixed order, with no `select_next_speaker` dynamic selection, no `MIN`/`MAX` turn logic, no cross-round-memory weighting, and no `must_continue` concept. The streaming endpoint's much more developed selection logic (cross-round memory, floor/ceiling, NONE-ending, pending-guidance consumption) simply does not exist on the plain endpoint's code path. Two coexisting multi-world turn-taking implementations, of visibly different sophistication, live side by side in the same codebase.

## 6. Safety / harnessing (classify-then-route intercept chain)

All five intercepts below share one architectural pattern, stated explicitly in each classifier's own docstring: a narrow Haiku classifier call, no Representative-generation role, seeing only the raw message (plus session state for relational-safety), failing open to the safe/non-intercepting default on any parse error or ambiguity, with a stated reason per case for *which* direction "safe" points.

**1. Frame-breaker** — `classify_frame_breaker` (`nodes.py` 380-421), prompt at `facilitator_prompts.py` 321-336. Intercepts direct/adversarial questions about the system's own construction ("Are you an AI?"). Fails open to `False` (treat as substantive) because "a false positive would incorrectly deny the participant a real answer." Response: `stream_frame_breaker_response` (424-469), Facilitator-only, "surface, answer, recede." Notable self-correction documented in the docstring: a claimed backstop (SELF_NARRATION monitoring) "was fiction until 2026-07-16... a Representative volunteering self-narration unprompted went unwatched" — since fixed (`FACILITATOR_MONITORING_PROMPT` signal 10, `facilitator_prompts.py` 156).

**2. Relational safety** — Track A (Acute Distress) / Track B (Harmful Dynamic), `classify_relational_safety` (486-566), state machine in `update_relational_safety_state` (569-646) and `relational_safety_should_fire` (649-669), full session-level fields in `state.py` 97-145. Five categories including a deliberately-named non-firing case, `HISTORICAL_OTHERNESS_DISORIENTATION` ("the encounter working as intended, not a crisis signal... exists so that non-firing is a considered decision, not an accidental miss"). Track B accumulates tags (`_HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_POOLED_THRESHOLD = 2`, `nodes.py` 476) with an explicit self-disclosure that this is "starting values per the design doc's own calibration disclosure — not a validated threshold." "Sustained attention" withholds the Representative for every subsequent turn until two consecutive `NO_SIGNAL` turns clear it (`_DEESCALATION_TURNS_REQUIRED = 2`, also self-disclosed as unvalidated). Response templates are severity/escalation-aware (A1 vs A2 vs continuation) and explicitly forbid naming any resource/hotline or requesting a safety promise (`FACILITATOR_ACUTE_DISTRESS_A1/A2_PROMPT`).

**3. Modern-term ("anachronism") bridge** — `modern_term_bridge.py`, fully world-agnostic by design: whether a term bridges is *derived* from comparing `origin_year` in a shared `definitions.json` against the seated world's parsed end-year (`_is_anachronistic`/`_world_end_year`, 78-102), "so 'the Trinity' bridges for a pre-Nicaea house-church but not for a post-381 world - the dates do the work, not a hand-authored disposition." Three-beat structure: Facilitator names it as later, gives the modern sense neutrally ("Do NOT say whether it is right... several of these terms are themselves contested"), then hands the Representative a **term-free** reframed question that is never persisted to the transcript. `main.py` 786-1041 documents a real, fixed production bug here: this classifier is deliberately run *sequentially*, not concurrently with the other three, because firing it changes what the Representative is even asked — running it concurrently "would mean starting [generation] against the WRONG input on every turn."

**4. Epistemology bridge** — `epistemology_bridge.py`, a narrower fourth intercept for genuinely double-valenced questions ("Where does documentation end and inference begin for you?") that the frame-breaker classifier is *deliberately* conservative about catching. Confirmed live 2026-07-20 against a specific prior incident (referenced by filename in the module docstring) as something "the frame-breaker classifier alone... is not enough on its own for this specific ambiguity by design." Unlike the other four classifiers, this one's fail-open direction is reversed in its own stated cost-benefit: "unlike the frame-breaker classifier's bias toward SUBSTANTIVE..., the cost of firing here is small... just after a brief, honest word from the Facilitator first" — so it prefers firing when unsure.

**5. Sensed closing sequence** — `closing_sequence.py`, a four-stage state machine (`state.closing_stage`: none -> anything_else_asked -> resources_offered -> resources_shown -> closed) with its own wind-down classifier (`classify_wind_down`) and reply interpreters, gated to run only after the four intercepts above and explicitly never triggered by "turn count, session length, or depth" alone.
**This mechanism was found broken and fixed during tonight's own session** — `stream_closing_turn` (`closing_sequence.py` 203-250) imported `FACILITATOR_ANYTHING_ELSE_PROMPT`, `FACILITATOR_RESOURCES_OFFER_PROMPT`, `FACILITATOR_RESOURCES_SHOW_PROMPT`, and `FACILITATOR_SENSED_CLOSE_PROMPT` from `app.prompts.facilitator_prompts` — **none of these four names were defined anywhere in the repository at the time this report was written.** The classification/routing logic around it was fully wired and coherent — `main.py` 769-985's `closing_turns` branch, `route_closing_stage`, the `state.closing_stage` machine — but the instant any of the four turn kinds was actually reached, the import failed and the request raised an exception, caught only by the branch's own generic `except Exception as exc: yield sse({"type": "error", ...})`. Fixed the same night: three prompts were authored fresh and the fourth (`sensed_close`) was aliased to the already-live `FACILITATOR_CLOSING_PROMPT` rather than duplicated, verified against the mock LLM before shipping.

**Also under this dimension** (heuristic, non-LLM, all in `nodes.py`, all explicitly framed as backstops for prompt-only rules that measurably didn't hold on their own):
- **Dominance** — `check_dominance` (2325-2384): cumulative word-share >=70% across the *whole conversation* (not one turn) triggers correction; threshold set at 70 rather than lower because "legitimate cross-world length asymmetry is by design... dominance here means crowding out, not merely out-speaking a form that is short on purpose."
- **Convergence** — `check_convergence` (2387-2468), one LLM call, catches both vocabulary-blending and "manufactured resolution" (a tidy synthesis neither tradition would have independently held) — attributed to *every* speaker in the round, "a relationship between voices, not one representative's individual fault."
- **Length ceiling** — `check_length_ceiling`/`_effective_length_ceiling` (2520-2603): world ceilings only ever tighten, never loosen, a lane ceiling (`min()` logic) — see the lane-system incompleteness noted under Voice/Selection.
- **Question stacking** — `check_question_stacking` (2606-2644): flags a round ending on >2 open questions across speakers.
- **Cross-world vocabulary drift** — `check_cross_world_vocabulary_drift` (2695-2796): cheap deterministic word-boundary pre-filter against every other seated world's own lexicon terms, escalating to a targeted LLM call *only* on an actual string match, specifically because "Syriac's own lexicon term 'Mar' is also the ordinary English verb 'to mar'... only checking the usage against the term's own defined ecological meaning can" distinguish real borrowing from coincidence.
- **Signal-selection tie-break** — `_select_monitor_finding`/`_MONITOR_SIGNAL_PRIORITY` (1492-1530): when the general monitor names several drift signals in one turn, only one `DriftSignal` is returned per turn by design, so severity-then-priority ordering decides which survives — explicitly built after a measured failure where a real, cheap-to-produce signal (OVER_PRODUCING) never once won the slot across 45 live turns because "ten signals were competing for it," and the code names the danger case directly: "a fabrication losing that slot to a stylistic complaint."

**Cross-cutting incompleteness:** the length-ceiling retry logic has a self-disclosed "dead zone" — a turn over its hard ceiling but under the retry-trigger multiple (1.5x/1.2x) is deliberately left uncorrected, "not corrected here (that would defeat the point of bounding the trigger), but logged so this zone's actual frequency is visible" (`nodes.py` 1353-1363). The lane-ceiling half of `check_length_ceiling` (the `general`/`academic`/`pastor-teacher` system) reads `getattr(state, "participant_role", None)` (2565) but `participant_role` is not a field anywhere on `ConversationState` and is never set by any endpoint — confirmed by a repo-wide search turning up exactly one hit (that `getattr` call). This entire lane/role safety-and-voice mechanism is fully authored, extensively rationalized in comments, and permanently dormant.

## 7. Cost/latency tradeoffs

**Two-model split, the central cost lever.** `get_llm` (`nodes.py` 168-206) uses `settings.llm_model` (default `claude-sonnet-5`) for actual Representative/Facilitator generation. `get_monitoring_llm` (249-291) hardcodes `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` for *every* classifier/router/filter: frame-breaker, relational-safety, modern-term, epistemology-bridge, wind-down/anything-else/offer-reply classifiers, `determine_turn_type`, `select_next_speaker`, `check_convergence`, `check_cross_world_vocabulary_drift`'s targeted call, and both retrievers' relevance-filter LLM (`rag/retriever.py` 84-87, `rag/story_retriever.py` 56-59, `rag/batch_evaluate.py`'s `_FILTER_MODEL`). This is the single largest, most consistently-applied cost/quality tradeoff in the codebase — every routing/monitoring decision is deliberately cheaper and (implicitly) less capable than the generation it gates.

**Token caps as a quality-preserving (not purely cost-cutting) mechanism**, notably running in the *opposite* direction from a naive cost-minimization read: `PRIMARY_TURN_MAX_TOKENS` was raised from 550 to 1200 (`nodes.py` 96-140) specifically because "a response running long is far less noticeable to a participant than one cut off mid-sentence" — Mark's own call to protect against truncation rather than to bound spend. `REACTIVE_TURN_MAX_TOKENS` was raised twice (500->700->900) for the same reason. `CLASSIFIER_MAX_TOKENS = 60` is the one cap actually sized tightly for cost, "generously above" the longest real classifier output shape. All capped calls explicitly disable extended thinking (`"thinking": {"type": "disabled"}`) because a live-tested bug showed an interleaved thinking block "can consume most of a tight max_tokens budget before any visible text is written."

**Prompt caching** — two dedicated Anthropic `cache_control` breakpoint schemes: `_cached_system_message` (`nodes.py` 1123-1160) for the static representative prompt + reactive-guidance block (byte-identical per representative/per-turn-type), and `_cached_adjudication_message` (1701-1725) for the ~9,250-token fabrication/over-settling adjudication prefix — both pure cost mechanisms with zero effect on output quality.

**Retrieval-filter batching** (`rag/batch_evaluate.py`): collapses what used to be "4-6 filter calls just for the lexicon, plus more for stories" into one batched call per retriever per turn, with Tier-1 candidates split into their own smaller batch to avoid "diluting the generous-weighting instruction" — a case where a cost optimization (batching) was tuned specifically because it initially *hurt* quality (uniform literal keyword-matching) until Tier-1/Tier-2-3 were separated.

**Concurrency for latency, not cost**: `ThreadPoolExecutor` runs lexicon+story retrieval concurrently (`nodes.py` 940-953); `asyncio.gather` runs frame-breaker + relational-safety (+ epistemology-bridge, streaming only) concurrently in both endpoints, explicitly trading "one possibly-wasted API call" (relational-safety always runs even when its result won't be used) for lower time-to-first-token. The modern-term-bridge classifier is the one deliberate **exception** — kept sequential despite the pattern, because firing it changes the Representative's input and "starting the Representative's generation concurrently... would mean starting it against the WRONG input on every turn... not safely buildable and API-verified in the time available."

**Session/message caps as spend control**: `message_cap.py`'s flat 20-turn ceiling is framed explicitly as "a direct proxy for API spend on this session"; `session_cap.py`'s per-user cap exists because "a single shared budget lets whoever dives in first burn the whole pilot's spend before anyone else gets a turn." The 3-world-per-table cap (`main.py` ~226) is commented directly as "cost and complexity."

**Instrumentation**: `usage_logging.log_llm_usage` (89 lines) was added in direct response to "a real pilot-test cost finding ($0.42/question, ~$8-9/hour) with zero token-usage instrumentation anywhere in the app" — purely observational, logs input/output/cache tokens per call site by label, and documents a real bug it found and fixed (streamed calls silently logging cache stats as 0 under the wrong metadata key).

**`mock_llm.py`** (68 lines): a zero-network, zero-cost stand-in gated by `settings.mock_llm`, explicitly for exercising "session flow, SSE token streaming, turn selection/routing... not for validating conversation quality" (config.py 56-62) — a dev-cost-avoidance mode, not a quality shortcut used in any real deployment path.

## Summary of the most load-bearing findings

- **The lane/role-based voice-and-length register system is dead code** — fully authored (`_LANE_LENGTH_CEILINGS` and its rationale), gated on a `state.participant_role` field that does not exist and is never set anywhere.
- **Two incompatible multi-world turn-selection engines coexist** — the streaming endpoint's dynamic, cross-round-aware `select_next_speaker` loop vs. the plain endpoint's fixed-order `multi_representative_engages`.
- **`pilot_cohort` is loaded but inert** — the only tiering lever that actually does anything today is `max_sessions`, and it is off for the majority of real (unsigned-in) traffic by explicit product decision — meaning the binding limit in practice is the flat, identity-free `message_cap`, not the built tiered mechanism.
- **The LangGraph graph is largely vestigial** relative to how a live conversation is actually driven — worth knowing before treating `builder.py`'s diagram as documentation of current behavior.

No dimension in the task's list came back empty — Reading level/plain language is the thinnest (one curated gloss whitelist plus qualitative brevity prompting, no quantitative readability check anywhere), and Tiered access is real but currently mostly dormant by design rather than by gap.
