## Quick Meaning

The Likeness of God is what the Image of God becomes through formation — not a separate gift but the image progressively restored and fulfilled, visible in the quality of a person's perception, love, and life as formation has shaped them toward the character of the God in whose image they were made.

---

## World Meaning

There are two words in the Genesis text, and they are not synonyms.

"Let us make humankind in our image (eikon), according to our likeness (homoiosis)." The image is given. The likeness is the image's goal. The image is what every human being is by virtue of being human — the indelible ontological mark that constitutes the soul's capacity for God (alexlex009). The likeness is what every human being is being formed toward — the progressive conformity of the person to the character and life of God that formation produces over a lifetime.

This distinction is load-bearing. Confuse the image with the likeness and you collapse either the given into the goal (as if formation creates the soul's God-capacity rather than restoring and fulfilling it) or the goal into the given (as if the likeness is already present and needs only acknowledgment). The distinction holds open the entire formation ecology: the image is already there, damaged but present, which is why formation is possible; the likeness is what the image is moving toward, which is why formation has a direction.

What the likeness looks like when formation has been doing its work:

The likeness is not a quality the soul possesses statically. It is visible in the mode of a person's life — in how they perceive, love, speak, suffer, serve, and inhabit the world. Clement's portrait of the *gnostikos* is a portrait of someone in whom the likeness has grown considerably: a person who knows at depth and loves at depth, whose knowledge is inseparable from their love, whose love is expressed in a clarity of perception and a generosity of life that is recognizable as the character of the God they are being formed toward. This is not moral performance. It is the visible expression of what genuine formation produces in the whole person.

The formation sequence names the stages through which the likeness grows:
- Catechesis (alexlex003) initiates the person's entry into the formation ecology in which the likeness can begin to grow.
- Illumination (alexlex004) deepens the person's perception — and deeper perception of divine reality is a deepening expression of the image of the Logos, which is what the likeness is.
- Knowledge/Gnosis (alexlex005) increases the soul's genuine contact with God — and genuine contact with God is the person becoming more like the one they are in contact with.
- Wisdom (alexlex006) is what the likeness looks like when gnosis has had time and formation to permeate the whole life — the wise person shows the likeness visibly, in the quality of their character and perception.
- Participation (alexlex007) is the likeness enacted — the soul genuinely sharing in the divine life and thereby becoming more like the one whose life it shares.
- Theosis (alexlex008) is the likeness completed — the eschatological horizon at which the image and the likeness coincide, when the soul is fully what God created it to become.

The likeness is therefore not a goal that stands apart from the formation practices and stages. It is what those practices produce progressively, what each stage deepens, what the whole sequence is building toward. The formation ecology exists because the likeness is real and because the person is genuinely becoming it, stage by stage, through the divine pedagogy (alexlex002) that works through every dimension of the ecology.

The Incarnation grounds the likeness's possibility. The Logos (alexlex001) who is himself the Image of the Father entered human nature — which means human nature is now, from within, being restored to the image and drawn toward the likeness by the one whose likeness it was always meant to bear. Athanasius sees this clearly: God became human so that the human, restored through genuine contact with the one who made them, might become what God intended — genuinely like God, in the only sense that a creature can be like the Creator: genuinely expressing, in creaturely form, the character, love, wisdom, and life of the one whose image they bear.

## Ecological Function

The Likeness of God is the formation goal of the entire anthropological stack. It is what Image-bearers are moving toward through the formation sequence, and it names what that movement arrives at.

**What the Likeness of God anchors:**

- **The formation sequence's direction**: Every stage — catechesis, illumination, gnosis, wisdom, participation — is a stage of growing likeness. The sequence has a direction because the likeness provides it: toward the character, perception, love, and life of the God whose image the soul bears.

- **Wisdom's visible form**: Wisdom (alexlex006) is the likeness made visible in the quality of a person's life. The wise person is not merely knowledgeable; they are, in some recognizable way, like the God they know. Their perception, love, and life express the character of the one they have been formed toward.

- **The Image/Likeness distinction**: The Image of God is what every human being is by constitution; the Likeness is what the image-bearer is becoming through formation. This distinction keeps the formation ecology from being either presumptuous (the likeness is already complete) or despairing (the image is too damaged to recover). The image is the ground; the likeness is the growth from that ground.

- **The connection to Theosis**: Theosis (alexlex008) is the likeness's eschatological completion. What the formation ecology grows here and now — partial, real, genuine — is the same likeness that theosis names as completed. The difference is not in kind but in fullness. Formation grows the likeness progressively; theosis is the likeness fully expressed in the final condition the soul is created for.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing**: "Becoming like God" in modern hearing typically means moral imitation — trying to behave as God would behave, following God's ethical example, modeling one's conduct on the divine character. On this reading, the likeness is a moral achievement: the person who acts generously, justly, and compassionately has become like God in the morally relevant sense. The likeness is imitated from outside, as one imitates a model, and the goal is behavioral conformity.

**World Hearing**: The moral qualities that the modern hearing correctly notices — the formed person loves more freely, perceives more clearly, serves more generously — are real, and the Alexandrian account does not deny them. But they are downstream of the ontological change rather than the change itself. The likeness is not moral imitation applied to a self that remains unchanged; it is what the self looks like when genuine formation has altered what the self is. The difference is between a person who has decided to behave generously and a person whose desire has been genuinely reordered toward generosity through years of formation — both may perform the same acts, but in the latter the acts are expressions of what the person has become rather than performances of what the person has chosen to do. The moral-imitation hearing sees the acts correctly; it mislocates them as the goal rather than the fruit. The world hearing sees the same acts and asks what produced them — and the answer is the genuine growth of the likeness through the formation that progressively conforms the image-bearer to the character of the one whose image they bear.

## Key Sources

- **Genesis 1:26** (scriptural) — "In our image, according to our likeness" — the foundational text; the two words that this entry holds distinct.
- **Clement of Alexandria**, *Stromata*, Book II, chs. 19, 22; Book VII, chs. 1–3 — The likeness as what formation produces in the *gnostikos*: visible conformity to God's character through wisdom and love.
- **Origen**, *On First Principles*, Book III, ch. 6 — The image given in creation, the likeness as the goal of the soul's journey; the gradual restoration of the image toward the likeness through formation. [Author Gravity note: Origen's account of the likeness connects to his speculative account of the soul's final restoration, which is adjacent to the Apokatastasis propositions. The broadly shared account of likeness as the formation goal is separately attested in Clement.]
- **Athanasius**, *On the Incarnation*, chs. 3–8 — The Logos enters human nature to restore the image and draw it toward the likeness; the incarnation as the ground of the likeness's possibility.
- **2 Corinthians 3:18** (scriptural) — "We are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another" — the scriptural expression of the progressive growth of the likeness.
