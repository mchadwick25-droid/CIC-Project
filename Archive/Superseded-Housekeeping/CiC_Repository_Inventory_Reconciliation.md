## What this means for the cleaning queue

The naming/version mismatches are themselves exactly the kind of finding this whole cleaning process exists to catch — they are not a reason to distrust the documents' content, only their filing. But it means a "Critic review for contamination" pass on, say, the Facilitator-Governance document should not proceed under the assumption that the file is "V3.3, needs 11 drift signals" — it needs to start from what v3.2 actually says.

Recommended order of operations going forward:
1. Resolve the filing-only items first (rename/version-correct the 9 misnamed documents, archive the 6 stale duplicates) — this is a naming pass, not a contamination review, and can likely be batched safely since it's mechanical.
2. Decide what to do about the 6 missing documents — for each, determine whether content exists somewhere unmigrated (e.g., check the original OneDrive source noted in `CiC_File_Inventory.txt`) or whether it needs to be rebuilt from scratch.
3. Resume the contamination-cleaning pipeline (Steps 1–6) document by document or in small batches, now grounded in accurate filenames and versions.
