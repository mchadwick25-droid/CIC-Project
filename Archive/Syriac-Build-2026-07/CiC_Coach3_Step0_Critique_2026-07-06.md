# Coach 3 — Original Critique of the Step 0 Conclusion

**Status:** Filed as-received, verbatim. This is Coach 3's first-pass review of the Step 0 Conclusion (Phase One World Selection), independent of any later reconciliation or correction pass. Filed alongside the Step 0 Conclusion itself so the reasoning trail travels with the document rather than living only in review correspondence.

---

Reviewing this against what I already know (my own Step 0 draft, its Opus review, and the Step 2 comparative pass), plus checking the specific claims.

Methodology and most selections hold up. The sourcing-plus-distinctiveness priority over geographic completeness is coherent and consistently applied to the deferrals (Armenia, Aksum, Persian Church of the East). Splitting "Syriac Christianity (Edessa/Nisibis)" from "the Persian Church of the East" is a real, defensible refinement — Ephrem/Aphrahat/the Diatessaron is an earlier, literary-hymnic formation logic distinct from the 410-Synod institutional church I worked up in my own Step 2 pass. Good catch also on flagging #7's own floor-proximity risk: early Edessene Christianity sits close to Bardaisan of Edessa's circle, whose astrological/quasi-docetic material is genuinely contested territory, not a clean Nicene-trajectory case the way Ephrem's own corpus is. That needs real Section A scrutiny in #7's own Step 1/2 work, not an assumed pass.

One direct validation of my own error, worth naming plainly: the correction to de-anchor Susan Wessel from world #5 matches a mistake in my own Step 2 draft — I listed her under Cappadocian secondary scholarship when her actual anchor (Cyril of Alexandria, Leo the Great) belongs with world #6/Alexandria instead. The other source corrections (Martens, Rubenson, Burton-Christie, Rousseau, Van Dam, Holman, McLynn, Barnes, Murray, Griffith, the Ebbeler/Schor de-anchoring) all check out against what I know of these scholars' actual specializations. This is real quality control, not cosmetic.

Two structural concerns, applying the document's own stated test back to itself:

World #8 (Cyprian + Augustine + Jerome) is the sharpest one. The doc shows its work for why #8 is distinct from #6 ("non-overlapping authority structure... temporal overlap rather than sequence"), but shows no equivalent reasoning for why Cyprian's crisis-era Carthage episcopate, Augustine's mass urban preaching at Hippo, and Jerome's ascetic-literary patronage circle in Bethlehem — different centuries, different regions, arguably different formation logics — count as one coherent world rather than a "several related but distinct currents" case, the same question my own 4.7 had to sit with honestly. Jerome in particular reads as a stretch: Bethlehem is closer in formation-logic to the Jerusalem/Palestinian monastic-literary world than to North African pastoral-congregational life.

World #6 (Eusebius/Constantine + Ambrose + Damasus-through-Leo) gets the same question less sharply but for the same reason: three genuinely different postures toward imperial power — alliance, confrontation, independent jurisdictional consolidation — merged without the explicit sequential-vs-independent test being shown the way it was for #8. It's plausibly defensible as one throughline (the church's relationship to state power evolving in stages), but that's an inference on my part, not something the document itself argues.

One silent omission: the Cyrilline/miaphysite tradition (Tier 2 in my own earlier list) isn't in the 8, isn't in "deliberately deferred," and isn't in "excluded by floor" — it just isn't mentioned. That's a natural consequence of moving the ceiling to 451 rather than 461 (its self-definition as a distinct communion crystallizes in the years just after Chalcedon), but the document should say that explicitly rather than let the omission look unaccounted for.

On the open structural question — genuine input, not a decision: the case for keeping the floor is that it's a Level 1 constitutional commitment (Article 4), not a Step 0-level choice, and it lets the project make one coherent, defensible claim rather than an unbounded one — and it doesn't erase Homoian Christianity, since #5 and #6 are already required to recenter it as the actual imperial establishment they contended with. The case against is that a floor excluding a once-dominant, centuries-persisting tradition by design creates exactly the kind of representational asymmetry Article 20 already polices on other axes (elite/literate/male/urban), just on a doctrinal axis instead — and the project's own A4 hand-selected exception mechanism reads like it exists precisely for a case this significant, more than for Marcion or the Ebionites. If this is going to be reconsidered at all, A4 inclusion of Homoian Christianity as an explicit, disclosed exception seems like the natural mechanism to test it against, rather than moving the floor itself. But this is a project-identity decision, not mine to make.

The nine-world boundary/contrast review doesn't exist yet as its own document — the distinctiveness reasoning so far has only covered the pairs that came up as live problems (the #8/#9 split, #6's internal merge, #8 vs #6, Antiochene vs #6/#8/#2). Here's a proper pass covering the pairs that were never explicitly tested, using the same primary-gravity-first method:

**#4 Donatism vs. #8 Latin Pastoral-Congregational (Cyprian+Augustine).** Real risk, since Donatists claimed to be Cyprian's authentic heirs, and Cyprian now sits inside #8. Holds anyway, on the same logic already worked out for the Cyprian/Donatism question: #8's Cyprian is defined by crisis pastoral management within one unified communion under external persecution; #4 is defined by permanent schism into two rival hierarchies over a different, later crisis (the Diocletian-era traditor controversy), with enduring martyr-cult identity as the organizing content. External-management-within-unity versus internal-schism-into-division — different gravity, not just different century.

**#3 Desert Monasticism vs. #9 Hieronymian.** Both are "ascetic," which is the only reason to check this. Distinct on nearly every real axis: #3 is communal, non-elite, oral, Coptic/Greek, defined by withdrawal from society; #9 is individual-patronage-based, elite, textual, Latin, defined by scholarly production sustained by wealth, not by withdrawal. Low risk, but worth stating so "ascetic" doesn't do false work as a similarity.

**#2 Alexandria vs. #9 Hieronymian.** Both produce serious biblical scholarship, which is the surface similarity. Distinct in exegetical mode (Alexandria's Platonist-allegorical synthesis vs. Jerome's philological, Hebrew-based translation work — Jerome is actually closer to Antiochene literalism here than to Alexandria's own method) and in social structure (a teaching relationship vs. a patronage/spiritual-direction relationship). Low risk.

**#1 Post-Apostolic House-Church vs. #7 Syriac Christianity.** The strongest distinctiveness pairing in the whole set — different language (Greek vs. Aramaic), different political frame (inside the Roman Mediterranean vs. formation outside it entirely), different expressive genre (community letters and emerging office vs. Ephrem's hymnic-symbolic poetry), different authority structure (plural presbyters vs. the bnay/bnat qyama covenant order). Nearly opposite on every axis except rough contemporaneity.

**#5 Cappadocians vs. #6 Imperial/Juridical.** The only overlap point is Gregory of Nazianzus's Constantinople tenure — but that episode is a brief, failed retreat in his own career, not a power-consolidation narrative the way it is for #6's figures. #5's gravity is theological-ascetic; #6's is institutional-power. Distinct.

**#4 Donatism vs. #6 Imperial/Juridical** — worth naming as a strength, not a risk. These are two sides of the same imperial-religious question: #6 is the church that aligns with and juridically consolidates around state power; #4 is the church that permanently splits from the mainstream specifically in refusal of that same power. That's a deliberate gain in representational range, not a coincidence.

Overall verdict: the nine-world set's pairwise distinctiveness holds up. Every previously-tested close call (Cyprian/Augustine's internal cohesion, the #8/#9 split, #6's internal merge, #8 vs #6) survives, and every newly-checked pair here clears on a real difference in formation-logic — not merely geography or language, which the project's own criteria already deprioritize as a distinguishing factor.
