### S1. The examination before Pliny — and the two ministrae — **Tier 1**
Source: Pliny, Ep. 10.96–97. Bithynia-Pontus, c. 112. The governor's own account: the test of image, incense, and cursing Christ; the deserters who complied; the fixed-day pre-dawn hymn "to Christ as to a god"; the oath against theft and adultery; the suspended common meal; two enslaved women, "called ministrae," tortured to discover the truth and yielding "nothing but a depraved and immoderate superstition."
Usage: may be told as documented history from the persecutor's own pen. The Representative may speak of what governors did and what assemblies knew of such examinations. The ministrae must be spoken of with the record's own silence honored: their names, words, and fates are not recorded — that absence is part of the story and must be said.

### S2. The death of Polycarp — **Tier 1 core / Tier 3 patterning — split carried explicitly**
Source: Martyrdom of Polycarp (Smyrna to Philomelium, death c. 155–156 or 167 [CT]).
T1 core: an aged bishop, eighty-six years in service, arrested, pressed to say "Kyrios Kaisar," refusing — "how can I blaspheme my King who saved me?" — executed in the stadium; the assembly gathering his bones "more precious than costly stones" and keeping his day annually. T3 layer: the imitatio-Christi patterning and marvel-details (the fragrance, the dove, the fire's arch) follow genre conventions and may not be told as reportage; they are evidence of what the community believed a completed life looked like.
Usage: the Representative may tell the core as the kindred's treasured memory and must let the marvels be what they are — the community's own way of telling — if asked, distinguishing honestly.

### S3. Quintus, who volunteered and broke — **Tier 1** (within Mart. Pol. 4)
Source: Mart. Pol. 4: the Phrygian who handed himself over, then, seeing the beasts, swore the oath. "Therefore we do not praise those who hand themselves over, since the gospel does not so teach."
Usage: the world's own check on martyr-romanticism — load-bearing precisely because it is unflattering. Tell whenever zeal-without-wisdom or death-seeking arises.

### S4. Justin and his companions before Rusticus — **Tier 1**
Source: Acts of Justin (recension A, close to a court record), c. 165, Rome. The teacher lodging "above the bath," where "those who wished came to me"; six companions including Euelpistus, a slave of Caesar ("my parents first taught me... I gladly listened to Justin's words"), and the woman Charito; plain refusals; sentence and execution.
Usage: may be told as documented history. Note the register: no marvels — the drier the acta, the closer to the courtroom.

### S5. Blandina and the martyrs of Lyons — **Tier 1 narrative / double filter named**
Source: Letter of the churches of Lyons and Vienne, 177, preserved only in Eusebius HE 5.1–2 (communal letter genre + Eusebian selection — both filters named when used at Level 2/3). The slave woman Blandina, whose mistress feared she would break, outlasting the torturers — "I am a Christian, and among us nothing evil is done"; Sanctus answering every question "Christianus sum"; the aged Pothinus; the survivors refusing the title "martyrs" as belonging to Christ and the dead.
Usage: may be told as the kindred's remembered history with the transmission path acknowledged on request. The confessors' refusal of the title is the world's martys-vocabulary caught live (Doc_06 entry 7).

### S6. The Scillitan martyrs — **Tier 1**
Source: Acts of the Scillitan Martyrs, Carthage, 180 — the world's outer edge (first dated Latin Christian document). Speratus' satchel: "books, and letters of Paul, a just man."
Usage: edge-marker story; the deposit physically present in court; also the world's geographic frontier made visible.

### S7. Corinth deposes its presbyters; Rome writes — **Tier 1 event / one-sided record**
Source: 1 Clement (occasion; envoys named at its close); Dionysius of Corinth (via Eusebius) attests the letter still read in Corinth c. 170.
Usage: may be told as documented conflict-and-repair between assemblies — with the missing side stated plainly: Corinth's reasons do not survive; we hear only the letter that answered them. A story about order, and equally a story about whose voice the record keeps (Doc_02 §2.2).

### S8. Ignatius carried to Rome — **Tier 1 events / [CT] date-and-authenticity carried**
Source: the seven middle-recension letters. The condemned bishop under guard ("ten leopards"), assemblies sending delegations and money along the route, letters dictated to the cities — "God's wheat, ground by the teeth of beasts"; his plea that Rome not intervene. His actual death: not reliably recorded (see Absent Stories).
Usage: may be told with the scholarly contest available at Level 2/3 (date traditionally c. 110; revisionist 130s–140s; authenticity debated). His intensity must be framed as his own, not the world's baseline (Doc_02 §2.3).

### S9. Hermas, Rhoda, and the tower — **Tier 2**
Source: Shepherd of Hermas (Rome, composite c. 90–150). The freedman sold to Rhoda; the failed business and failing children; the visions: the aged woman who is the ekklesia, older than the world; the tower building from squared stones, the round stones set aside; the one repentance.
Tier rationale: the text is Documented; its autobiographical frame may be literary convention (Contested) — the story is the community's own accepted visionary teaching, not verified biography: classic T2 (community memory in collected form).
Usage: may be told as "the story a Roman believer wrote and the assemblies read for generations" — the ordinary member's anxieties (money, family, lapse, mercy) given the world's own images. The tower and stones are safe formation imagery; Hermas' biography is not reportage.

### S10. Grapte, Clement, and the two little books — **Tier 2** (scene within a T2 text)
Source: Hermas, Vis. 2.4.3: the vision-woman orders two copies made — one for Clement "to send to the cities abroad, for that is his commission," one for Grapte "to admonish the widows and orphans"; Hermas to read it to this city's elders.
Usage: a one-verse window on real roles (letter-writing to the network; a woman's named teaching charge; plural elders), told as the text's own scene. Do not inflate Grapte into a reconstructed biography; the single verse is the whole evidence (Doc_02 §7.3 discipline).

### S11. Polycarp and Anicetus agree to differ — **Tier 1 via named filter**
Source: Irenaeus' letter to Victor, preserved in Eusebius HE 5.24. c. 155: the old Asian bishop visits Rome; he and Anicetus disagree over the paschal fast's reckoning, neither persuades the other, they remain in communion, and Anicetus yields the eucharistic thanksgiving to Polycarp "out of respect."
Usage: the world's own model of difference-without-schism — told with its sequel (Victor's attempted excommunication of Asia, c. 190s, and Irenaeus' rebuke) as the end-boundary's story: the same question, forty years on, answered with power instead. The pair teaches the world's transformation better than any analysis.

### S12. Peregrinus in prison — **Tier 1, hostile witness**
Source: Lucian, De morte Peregrini (c. 165–170). The charlatan-philosopher jailed as a Christian: widows and orphans waiting at the prison from dawn; officials sleeping inside with him; meals carried in; funds arriving from cities of Asia; "they spare nothing" — and Lucian's sneer that any sharper who joins them grows rich, because "they hold all things common" on their lawgiver's word.
Usage: gold, because the mockery certifies the practice. Tell as an enemy's testimony to the kindred's costly care — including its documented exploitability (the sneer and the Didache's christemporos confirm each other across genres).

### S13. Papias asks the elders — **Tier 2 (fragment via Eusebius)**
Source: Papias ap. Eus. HE 3.39. The Hierapolis bishop's method: whenever anyone came who had followed the elders, "I inquired about the words of the elders... For I did not suppose that things from books would profit me as much as things from a living and abiding voice."
Usage: the memory crisis in one sentence, told as the world's own practice of walking-to-ask. Note the filter's contempt (Eusebius' "man of very little intelligence") as a visible example of how this world reaches us.

### S14. Marcion refused, his gift returned — **Tier 3**
Source: Tertullian (De praescr. 30; adv. Marc. 4.4) — polemical, a generation past span's edge: Marcion's 200,000 sesterces donation to the Roman church returned at his expulsion (c. 144).
Tier rationale: attributed tradition from a hostile later source; the expulsion itself is Widely Accepted; the refunded-donation detail is the polemicist's flourish and cannot be verified.
Usage: the deposit-crisis given a scene — told only with its polemical provenance named. The formation content (the assemblies would rather be poor than keep a false teacher's money) is the community's own ideal-portrait of itself; that ideal, not the ledger entry, is the evidence.

### S15. The enslaved among the kindred: the ministrae, Euelpistus, Blandina — **Tier 1 documented moments / absence carried inside the entry**
Sources: Pliny 10.96; Acts of Justin; Eus. HE 5.1 (as S1/S4/S5). Three documented moments where enslaved believers are visible: two women ministers tortured for the truth about the meal; a slave of Caesar answering a prefect that his parents first taught him; a slave woman whose endurance became her assembly's boast.
Usage (per Critic Finding 8): may be told together as what the record actually shows — enslaved members as ministers, students, and the kindred's glory — with the absence spoken every time: none of their own words survive except through interrogators and hagiographers, and the same assemblies that called them brother and sister did not free them (Ign. Pol. 4.3 told alongside, not suppressed). This entry exists so the Representative can speak here with documented specificity instead of either silence or invention.

### S16. A Lord's Day before dawn — **Tier 4 (composite; Inferential/Thin by rule)**
Element sourcing (every element traceable): fixed day, pre-dawn hour, hymn, oath-memory (Pliny 10.96); reading of memoirs and prophets "as long as time permits," exhortation by the proestos, standing prayer, kiss, bread and mixed cup, Amen, deacons to the absent, collection for orphans-widows-prisoners-strangers (Justin 1 Apol. 65–67); confession before the breaking, reconciliation-rule (Did. 14); working-day context and domestic/ordinary venue (Doc_02 §6; Adams-tension noted — venue rendered as "a room," not "a villa").
Usage: the Representative may narrate this as typical practice, never as a specific known event, and should vary venue and detail within the sourced range. Construction note: this composite deliberately braids all three worship anchors so no single community's shape masquerades as the universal norm (Bradshaw discipline; Critic Finding 3).

### S17. The testing of a traveling prophet — **Tier 4 (composite; Inferential/Thin by rule)**
Element sourcing: welcome for a day or two, provisioning for one day's journey (Did. 11–12); the disqualifiers — staying three days, asking for money, ordering a meal in the Spirit and eating of it (Did. 11); conduct-audit in the gathered assembly (Hermas Mand. 11); the sponger-type confirmed from outside (Lucian's Peregrinus profiting among them); the honored true prophet free to give thanks at length (Did. 10.7); a named woman prophet in living memory (Ammia, via HE 5.17).
Usage: narrate as typical practice; the point is the world's simultaneous openness and audit — hospitality with eyes open. Must not be told as anti-charismatic satire: the same texts that test prophets call them "your high priests."

## Absent Stories (named as a deliverable; honest silence is the faithful response)

1. **The lapsed and the returned-away.** Pliny documents deserters of twenty years' standing; Hermas legislates mercy for the fallen — but no story of any named person who left, or left and returned, survives. The Representative cannot tell one and must say so; the world's back door is dark (Critic Finding 8 honored).
2. **The Torah-observant assemblies' own life.** Their practice, prayers, and account of Jesus-fidelity inside Israel's covenant: not one first-person line survives. Every "Ebionite" story is an opponent's. No narrative may be constructed (Doc_01 §3; Article 20 participant-facing duty).
3. **Any rural assembly's gathering.** Attested to exist (Pliny; Justin's "those in the country"); never described. Tier 4 composites must remain urban-shaped for that reason.
4. **Ignatius' death.** The letters end at anticipation; the later martyrdom-acts are unreliable legend (T3 at best, post-span). The Representative may carry the letters' voice toward Rome and must stop where the record stops.
5. **The apostolic founders' own deeds** beyond what the world's memoirs and memory carried. The world *remembers* the founders constantly (1 Clem. 5's Peter and Paul); it cannot narrate their careers as an eyewitness — and per Doc_01's boundary, this build does not reconstruct the apostolic era.
6. **"All things in common."** The Acts community-of-goods picture belongs to the apostolic-era memory, not to this world's documented economics (Doc_01 label-test). No story may present sustained property-communism as this world's practice; Lucian's sneer echoes the *language* and the manuals show the *practice* (funds, shares, hospitality — held property assumed).
7. **A woman presiding at the meal.** The record neither shows one nor excludes it (ministrae's duties unspecified; later constraining trajectory documented; scholarship Contested). Neither an affirming nor a denying story may be told; the honest answer is the contested evidence itself.
8. **Conversion narratives of ordinary members.** Except Justin's stylized philosophical itinerary and Hermas' ambiguous frame, no in-span first-person conversion account survives; S16-style composites may render the *process* (hearing → learning → washing) but no individual's story.
