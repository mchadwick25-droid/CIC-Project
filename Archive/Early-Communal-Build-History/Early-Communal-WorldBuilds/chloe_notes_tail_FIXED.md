## Section 9 — Readability Pass (Sentence-Length Fix, v2.2, project-lead directed)

Accessibility against modern academic-tool comparisons is a deliberate project differentiator, and this build was tested against it directly this session: a Flesch-Kincaid / Flesch Reading Ease pass on the Permanent Prompt as originally written scored grade 12.3 (college level), Reading Ease 60.9, with a longest single sentence of 102 words. Average syllables-per-word (1.36) showed the vocabulary itself was plain — Chloe's native lexicon was never the problem. The cause was sentence structure: long chains of clauses joined by em-dashes, colons, and semicolons, several running past three or four clauses in a single sentence.

**Fix applied:** every sentence over roughly 25-30 words was broken into two or more shorter sentences at its existing clause boundaries (the em-dashes, colons, and semicolons that were already there). No word was changed, no image or lexicon term was removed, no idea was cut or simplified — word count is effectively unchanged (1,945 to 1,941). Result: grade 8.1, Reading Ease 71.9, longest sentence 54 words. This is a pure restructuring, not a simplification; it should not be read as evidence against Section 2's plain/oral register finding or Section 8's unevenness addition — both stand as written, now carried in shorter sentences.

**Confidence:** the readability scores are Documented (direct computation, not inference). The judgment that this restructuring preserves voice and content is Widely Accepted pending a fresh read-aloud test (Section 6's testing plan already calls for one; not yet re-run against this revision).

**Template-level note:** this same problem was traced partly to the core Representative Permanent Prompt Template's own Section 1 model sentence, which every built Representative reproduced in a near-identical 100+-word form. The template has been corrected (v2.2, Final Assembly Instruction check 5c) so future builds do not inherit this defect. Kimon (Desert) and Eumathios (Cappadocian) were tested at the same time and found to have the same structural problem (grades 15.7 and 13.8 respectively) — flagged for the same fix, not yet applied, per the project lead's review-phase sequencing.
