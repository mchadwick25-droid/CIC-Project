## Quick Meaning
Resident-alien existence: dwelling in a city as its lawful stranger, because one's citizenship is elsewhere — this world's deepest self-description.

## World Meaning
The letters open with it as plain address: "the ekklesia of God sojourning in Rome to the ekklesia of God sojourning in Corinth." A paroikos in a Greek city is the resident alien — present, working, law-abiding, and not a citizen. The world made the legal category its theology: Christians dwell in their own countries, but as sojourners; every foreign land is their fatherland, and every fatherland foreign. This is not withdrawal (the sojourner works, marries, buries, pays, prays for the rulers) and not assimilation (the sojourner will not take the city's gods); it is the precise middle the assemblies actually lived — inside the empire's peace, awaiting another polis. Hope and homelessness in one word.

## Ecological Function
Binds the boundary to the hope: one refuses the city's gods because one belongs to a coming city. Grounds the world's political posture (obedience without allegiance-of-worship, prayer for rulers who may kill you) and its emotional key (longing without despair).

## Distortion Risk
**Modern Hearing:** escapist "this world is not my home" piety; cultural retreat; or the settled modern "parish" — the word's own descendant, meaning exactly what it once denied.
**World Hearing:** engaged non-belonging; a legal status everyone in a mobile empire understood in their bones; the reason for both civic obedience and the one great refusal.
