## Quick Meaning
The gathering of the brothers and sisters itself — never a building, not yet an institution; God's called-together people, existing wherever they assemble.

## World Meaning
In this world ekklesia is an event with a people attached: the word Greek cities used for their citizen assembly, claimed by communities whose citizenship is elsewhere. One is not "at church"; one is, with the others, the assembly. It gathers in whatever ordinary rooms are available — houses, upper floors, workrooms — before dawn if the working day demands. It is at once utterly local (faces in one room, whose conduct all know) and cosmically one (letters address "the ekklesia of God sojourning in" each city; the meal-prayer asks God to gather it "from the ends of the earth into the kingdom"). Hermas is shown the ekklesia as an aged woman created before all things, for whose sake the world was framed — the rented-room gathering understood, from inside, as older than the mountains.

## Ecological Function
Load-bearing for every gravity of this world: site of the meal, home of the kinship, the incriminating act under the Name, the reading-room of the deposit. The world's live tensions are all fights over the gathering: who speaks in it, whose book it keeps, who may return to it.

## Distortion Risk
**Modern Hearing:** a building; a Sunday institution; a denomination; something attended or left.
**World Hearing:** the gathering itself; a kinship entered at cost; the one place a whole life converges; something that can be scattered but not unjoined without unjoining life.
