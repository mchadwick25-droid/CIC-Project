# Needs-ruling — what the corpus assignment would not decide on its own

Generated by `gen_needs_ruling.py` from `cic/corpus-map/`. **84 works, 101 assignments.**

Every one of these was placed with a best guess and flagged, never forced and never dropped. The brief's instruction was that a flagged uncertainty is worth more than a confident wrong assignment, and ten workers took it seriously.

**Read the group headings, not the list.** These are not 87 decisions. Ten workers who could not see each other kept hitting the same small set of walls, and the dominant one is not doubt about texts at all — it is *this work is real, and the census has no entry for its region and period.* One answer clears five or seven works at a time.

Nothing here blocks anything. The map is valid and usable as it stands; a `needs-ruling` work is assigned, just not on the run's own authority.

## `post-apostolic-house-church` — 22 work(s)

**`pahc` is the corpus's overflow shelf, and that is the finding.** Its window is 70-200 CE, and it collected 22 needs-ruling works because it is the only early entry there is. Three distinct questions sit inside this one pile: (a) *the Greek apologists* have no entry of their own - Athenagoras, Mathetes, the dubious Justiniana; (b) *early third-century Rome* has no entry, so Hippolytus (c. 170-236) and Caius fall off the end of pahc's window; (c) *later pseudepigrapha* impersonating first-century figures - the Spurious Epistles of Ignatius are 4th-century forgeries sitting in a 70-200 entry. Splitting even one of these off pahc would clear most of the pile.

## `syriac-edessa-nisibis` — 4 work(s)

**The Clementine literature, and what `syr` is for.** Recognitions and Homilies sit between `ebionite-nazoraean-current` (the Jewish-Christian source theory) and this entry (Syrian provenance) - one ruling covers the whole Pseudo-Clementine corpus, the Two Epistles on Virginity with it. The deeper question underneath all four works here is whether `syr` means *Edessene* or means *transmitted in Syriac*: the Ambrose hypomnemata is Greek apologetic that survives only in Syriac, which is a fact about transmission, not about the tradition.

It is Christian apologetic, not Edessene as such; syr marks only where the Syriac transmission preserved it. Needs a ruling — arguably belongs with the Greek apologists, who sit in other volumes.

**The Clementine Homilies** · `pseudo-clement` · `anf08` · also flagged against `ebionite-nazoraean-current`
> Same ruling and same doubt as the Recognitions: pseudonymous Clement romance with Jewish-Christian theology (the True Prophet, anti-Pauline edge), Syrian provenance c. 300-320.

**The Recognitions of Clement** · `pseudo-clement` · `anf08` · also flagged against `ebionite-nazoraean-current`

## How to answer one

A ruling is a sentence, not a document. Either name the entry the work should go to, or say the census needs a new entry and roughly what it covers. The staging file for that source volume gets edited and `python cic/engine/corpus_map_merge.py` re-run; the work's `confidence` moves to `assigned` and it drops off this list.

Where the answer is a **new census entry**, that is a change to `cic-website/data/world-census.json` and belongs to whoever owns the Atlas, not to a corpus thread. This report is the evidence that the entry is wanted: it names the material already in hand that would fill it.
