decision · **Status:** ACTED ON — Mark ruled option 2, 2026-08-26

> **Outcome.** `gallic-monastic-ascetic-christianity` (I.27, era 2, c. 360–450)
> was added to the census as **Possible Future World (on record)**, with four
> edges connecting it. Seven works were re-pointed to it from the Merovingian
> entry they antedate; six moved to `assigned`, and the *Doubtful Letters* stay
> `needs-ruling` because the editors' own attribution doubt is untouched by the
> new entry. Fleet-wide `needs-ruling` fell from 87 works to 84.
>
> The entry's `why` field records that this was **not a Step 0 verdict** — it
> says so in as many words, so nobody later mistakes it for one. The open
> question §4 raises, whether Martin's Tours and Cassian's Marseilles are one
> world or a lineage of two, is named there for a survey to settle.

Mark's question, on reading the needs-ruling list: *"did we miss some potential
worlds when we created the atlas?"*

Mostly no. Of the census gaps the run surfaced, most are in eras whose survey
has not run, and most of the rest are heresy shelves rather than worlds. **One
survives both tests**, and this document states it so it can be argued with.

---

## 1. Why this question is answerable at all

Eras 1 and 2 are **exhaustively assessed**: 11 and 15 entries respectively,
**zero** marked `Pre-Survey Candidate`. Phase One deliberately spanned them and
both are frozen. Era 3 onward is the opposite — era 3 carries 1 assessed entry
against 16 pre-survey, whose status says in as many words: *"The survey for this
era has not yet run. No verdict exists — and none is implied."*

The vendored corpus is overwhelmingly eras 1–2 material. So it presses on the
Atlas exactly where the Atlas claims to be complete, which is what makes a
mismatch evidence rather than noise. A gap in era 5 means nothing yet. A gap in
era 2 means a completed survey either considered the thing and passed, or did
not consider it.

**No Gaul.** The only Gallic entry anywhere before era 4 is

Two workers who could not see each other's work hit the hole from opposite
ends: one from Hilary of Poitiers in `npnf209`, one from Martin, Sulpitius,
Vincent and Cassian in `npnf211`. Neither was looking for it. Between them they
filed **eight `needs-ruling` works** whose entire doubt is *this is real and
there is nowhere to put it.*

Rebuke and Grace*, *Predestination of the Saints* and *Gift of Perseverance*

Marseilles and Lérins. The controversy is documented from both sides.

## 4. Refining the claim — it is not "Gaul"

The honest version of this case is narrower than the geography suggests, and
the narrowing makes it stronger.

**Hilary of Poitiers should probably not be in it.** He is a Nicene
controversialist whose Gallic location is close to incidental to what he wrote;
*De Trinitate* is about the Arian crisis, not about a Gallic church. He is
better served by whatever answers the Anomoean/Homoian shelf question. Removing
him drops the figure to **366,371 words**, still 2.0% of the corpus.

**What remains is a single coherent thing:** the monastic-ascetic Gaul of
Martin's Tours, the island community at Lérins, and Cassian's houses at
Marseilles, c. 360–450. It has the marks the project looks for in a world:

- **A lived ecology, not a reading list.** An island monastery, a bishop's
  ascetic household at Tours, two houses at Marseilles — with people moving
  between them. Sulpitius knew Martin. Vincent and Cassian were both of the
  Lérins–Marseilles circle.
- **Its own voice, not only outsiders'.** Cassian's *Conferences* are that
  school's own formation literature; Vincent's *Commonitory* is a Lérins
  insider document. Sulpitius on Martin is hagiography and would carry the
  usual tier caution, but the entry does not rest on it.
- **A distinctive theological position it held against pressure** — the Gallic
  resistance to Augustinian predestination, which is precisely why Augustine
  wrote those three treatises to Prosper and Hilary of Arles.
- **A distinctive social function.** Lérins formed bishops. It is not a
  withdrawal world; it is a seminary that looked like a monastery.

## 5. Arguments against, taken seriously

**"Cassian is already assigned to `desert-monasticism`."** He is, as
`tradition`, and that was the answer to audit finding F-24 — his *De
Incarnatione* is the desert tradition's own author writing Christology.
Assignment is not exclusive, so a Gallic entry takes nothing away. But it does
mean the Gallic entry's *distinctive* weight is the 366k without him, and that
Cassian would sit in both, which is exactly the Athanasius/*Vita Antonii*
shape.

**"This is desert monasticism in Latin."** Partly true and the most serious
objection. Cassian's whole project was transmitting Egyptian teaching westward.
The reply is that Lérins was not Scetis: island cenobitism, episcopal
formation, and a doctrine of grace that Egypt never argued about. If the entry
were built, its relationship to `desert-monasticism` would need stating on the
tile — but "descended from" is not "identical to", and the Atlas already holds
entries in that relation.

**"It is three things, not one."** Real, and the reason §4 cuts Hilary. Even
after the cut, Martin (d. 397) and Vincent (d. c. 445) are two generations
apart. Whether that is one formation world or a lineage of two is a Step 0
question this document cannot settle.

**"Just widen `merovingian-gallic-christianity` back to c. 360."** Cheapest fix
and probably wrong. The Merovingian church is post-Roman, Frankish, and
royal-episcopal; Lérins is late-Roman provincial monasticism. Backdating one
entry to cover both would blur the transition that makes each interesting.

**"Step 0 considered and passed on it."** Possible, and this document cannot
see the Step 0 record. If that is what happened, the answer is not a new entry
but a `Possible Future World (on record)` entry — the status that exists
precisely to say *considered, set aside, reasoning on file*. Which would still
be a change, because right now the material has nowhere to point.

---

## 6. What this document does NOT claim

**It does not claim the Atlas should be reshaped to fit the corpus.** The
control is decisive: `aksumite-christianity-conversion-era` received **zero**
works from the assignment run and `armenian-christianity-conversion-era`
received **one**. The Atlas holds entries this corpus cannot feed, which proves
it was built from historical survey rather than from what happens to be on the
shelf. That is correct and should stay true.

This corpus is Schaff's ANF/NPNF — a nineteenth-century Anglo-American
Protestant selection. Its shape is evidence about **what exists in English
translation**, not about what history had. Any gap that shows up only because
CCEL printed a lot of something should be treated with suspicion.

Gallic monasticism survives that test because the argument in §4 is about
lived ecology — institutions, people moving between them, a position held under
pressure — and not about word count. The word count is only what makes it
*buildable*.

---

## 7. What a ruling looks like

Three options, in ascending cost:

1. **Nothing.** The eight works stay `needs-ruling` and point at
   `merovingian-gallic-christianity` as a best guess. The map remains valid;
   the material remains findable; the flag remains honest.
2. **A `Possible Future World (on record)` entry**, era 2, Gaul, c. 360–450 —
   with whatever Step 0 reasoning applies. Cheap, and it gives 366k–731k words
   of vendored text a home they can be assigned to rather than parked against.
3. **`Selected - Not Yet Built`**, if Step 0 would now choose it. That is a
   survey decision, not a corpus decision, and not this document's to make.

Whichever way it goes, the corpus map does not need to change first — a
`needs-ruling` work **is** assigned, just not on the run's own authority. This
is a decision about the Atlas, and it belongs to whoever owns the Atlas.
