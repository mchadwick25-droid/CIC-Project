The substantive part matters more than the count. This letter **proves that there were not only Catholic but Meletian monks**, and these not hermits but in societies… (The existence of Meletian monks is attested independently of this letter…)"

I read *Apol. Ar.* §67 directly and the letter is there, in Athanasius's own defence document, with the NPNF editor's own note: "This intercepted letter demonstrates the existence of Meletian monks, of which there is other evidence also."

So: (a) the numeric claim is false as written, twice; (b) the record's Layer 2 rests on an authorial silence that the corpus's own vendored material partially contradicts, with **named** Melitian monastics in an Athanasian text; (c) the record's own `description` survives this — "essentially silent about Melitian ascetics as a **live, named rival presence**" is hedged, and the *Apologia* names them as accomplices in the Arsenius affair rather than as rival ascetic co-participants — but the unhedged manifestation the fix pass wrote does not. Ar.` 67.

This is the **third consecutive round** in which a fix pass has written a false claim about registered or vendored material into the sentence certifying another fix (Round 2's S1 and S2; Step3c Round 3's S1 and S2 before them). What is different, and worth recording, is that it is now **one** such claim rather than two, that it is a scope error rather than a copied wrong number, and that the correct scope was stated correctly in the review the fix was working from.

*Available correction, for whoever fixes this:* scope both sentences to the *Vita* (Round 2's own wording), and add — rather than suppress — the *Apol.
