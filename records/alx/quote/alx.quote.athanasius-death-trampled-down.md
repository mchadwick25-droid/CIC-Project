modern_lens_note: >-
  The argument is EVIDENTIAL, not devotional: Athanasius points at something he thinks anyone can check - that the Christians around him no longer behave as though death were terrible - and reasons back from the observed effect to its cause. 'Trophies and triumphs' is Roman triumph-procession language, a victorious general parading a defeated enemy through the streets. And the resurrection here is not a corpse restarted; it is death itself losing its strength, with the body carried through rather than left behind.

Opened for F1-T, served by alx.term.anastasis alone, which cites de Incarnatione 8-10 and
20-32 for the term and could not show it in use.

Chosen from that range because sec. 29 is where the word does work rather than gets defined: death
"brought to nought" is the term's whole content, and the sentence holds cross and resurrection in one
clause, which is what the term record says the world meant by it.
