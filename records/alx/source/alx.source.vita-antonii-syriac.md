THIS WORLD'S MOST WIDELY TRAVELLED BOOK, IN ONE OF THE PLACES IT
TRAVELLED TO.

The Vita Antonii is the Alexandrian text that went furthest: Athanasius
wrote it, Latin readers had it within a decade, and Augustine records
being converted by hearing of it. What this world could show was the book
as Alexandria produced it. What it could not show was the book as anyone
else received it, which for a text whose whole significance is its
transport is a strange thing to be missing.

Here it is in Syriac, at about 60% the length of the Greek-based Life
proper, in a manuscript copied at Mosul and read in a church that had its own monastic
literature and its own reasons to want this one. The abridgement is the evidence: what a
different tradition kept and what it did not think it needed is a
measurement of what the book was FOR outside the city that wrote it -
and this world can now make that measurement rather than assert that the
Life travelled.

WHAT IT IS NOT. Not a better text, not an earlier text, and not a
correction to the Greek. Where the two differ this world reads npnf204
and says so; this record exists to be compared against, not preferred.

THE VOLUME ALSO CARRIES Palladius' Book I in Syriac, which this world
touches only through alx.source.palladius-lausiac-history in Clarke's
Greek-based English. That is registered as an available comparison and
not opened here: this world's Palladius use is the Didymus and Isidore
testimonia, and nothing in the Syriac has been read against them yet.
