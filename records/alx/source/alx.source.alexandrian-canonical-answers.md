WHAT IT ANSWERS IN THIS WORLD'S OWN THINNESS STATEMENT. This world's
registry entry says it is "richest in teaching, argument, and the
theology of formation; thinner on women's own words, ordinary
believers, and rural Coptic Egypt." Its twenty sources are Clement,
Origen, Athanasius, and the historians who wrote about them - a corpus
of teaching and controversy. These canons are the only documents in it
where the Alexandrian church says what an ordinary person should
actually do on an ordinary difficulty.

Timothy's Answers are put as questions and answered in a line each: a
boy of seven who ate the offering by accident (baptize him, he is called
by God); a catechumen who is possessed (baptize at the hour of death,
not otherwise); a sick man and the Paschal fast (he need not keep it); a
woman in childbed and the same fast (she need not); a clergyman asked to
bless a marriage he has heard is incestuous (he must not be partaker of
other men's sins). This is a bishop's desk, not a school.

WHAT IT IS NOT, AND THIS MATTERS. It does NOT fill
alx.limit.f5-women-own-words, and must never be used as though it did.
Several of Timothy's answers concern women - whether a woman may be
baptized when her period has come, whether a menstruating woman may
communicate, whether a woman in childbed keeps the fast - and every one
of them is a man ruling on a woman's body, in answer to a question put
by another man. That is the opposite of women's own words. It is
evidence about the conditions of ordinary believers' lives, which this
world says it lacks; the honest limit about women's voices stands
exactly where it stood.

WINDOW. Dionysius (d. 264), Timothy (d. 385) and Theophilus (bp. from
385) all fall inside this world's c. 150-400 window. The volume also
prints the Canonical Epistle of Cyril of Alexandria, which does NOT -
Cyril is bishop from 412 - and it is deliberately not drawn on here.

NOT A GAP AFTER ALL, checked before adding: the volume's extract from
Athanasius' XXXIX Festal Epistle is an epitome whose own headnote says
the full translation is in NPNF2 vol. 4, which this world already holds
as alx.source.athanasius-festal-letters. Nothing is taken from it here.
