claim: 'Origen himself held, as settled doctrine, the positions later condemned under his name: the pre-existence
  of souls, apokatastasis (universal restoration), grades of rational natures, subordinationist Christology.'
held_against:
- A large part of Origen survives only through Rufinus (who softened suspect passages in translation)
  and Jerome (who, after his turn, quoted adversarially) - fine-grained position-attributions pass through
  interested hands both ways
- Origen's own prefatory discipline frames much of De Principiis as inquiry into what the Rule of Faith
  leaves open, not as settled teaching
- Scholarship divides on whether the condemned propositions are Origen's or later systematizations of
  him (the prior build carried this named live debate unresolved)
concedes: Origen speculated boldly in these directions - the texts show that much even through the filters.
  What cannot be securely fixed is the precise dogmatic weight he gave each position.

The Philocalia relation matters operationally: where a passage exists
in both transmissions, the Greek wins. HORIZON DISCIPLINE: within this
world's window Origen's standing is inheritance-and-unease, not
condemnation (first controversy c. 399-400 at the edge; 553 far
beyond); the trap-map in the manifest governs what later material may
never be cited.
