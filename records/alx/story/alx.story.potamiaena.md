  alongside her. The vendored source is explicit that Potamiaena herself was not of Origen''s own circle
  of disciples - that place belongs to Basilides, the soldier who led her to execution and was himself
  converted by her courage. Under the persecution she was threatened with what ancient cruelty reserved for women,
  and stood fast. The judge sentenced her to death by boiling pitch, poured slowly. The soldier Basilides
  led her out - and, the tradition says, held back the crowd''s abuse with such gentleness that she thanked
  him and promised to ask her Lord for him when she was gone. She died with her mother. Not long after,
  Basilides, asked by his fellow soldiers to swear an oath, refused: he was a Christian, he said. His
  comrades thought he joked; he did not. He said Potamiaena had come to him in the night and set a crown
  on his head. He was beheaded. The church baptized him in prison before the end.'

One of the very few named women in the world's record - remembered, but
not self-attested, which alx.limit.f5-women-own-words states as the
structural fact. Serves F3-I (the danger was real) and F6-E (what
martyrdom meant from inside: witness that converted the executioner,
not death-seeking). Tier 3 discipline: the hagiographic elements are
flagged in the tier justification and inside the telling itself.

corrected 2026-09-08, records/alx audit: the opening sentence previously
placed Potamiaena "among the students of Origen's circle," which the
vendored source contradicts. The NPNF201 editorial note at the head of
HE VI.5 (line ~32928) states: "Potamiæna... is made by Rufinus a disciple
of Origen, but Eusebius does not say that she was, and indeed, in making
Basilides the seventh of Origen's disciples to suffer, he evidently
excludes Potamiæna from the number." The chapter text itself (lines
32949-32954, corrected 2026-09-08, records/alx audit, round 3, from an
earlier "32953-32954" that started after "Basilides" itself, which sits
at 32949) confirms it is Basilides, not Potamiaena, who is counted among
Origen's disciples: "Basilides may be counted the seventh of these. He
led to martyrdom the celebrated Potamiæna." The opening sentence is
corrected to place only Basilides in Origen's circle, and to keep
Potamiaena's own role (the martyrdom, her mother, the women's-stories
purpose this record serves) intact.
