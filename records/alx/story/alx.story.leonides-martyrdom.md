  of the sixteen-year-old Origen, was arrested and held for beheading. The boy burned to go and die beside
  his father - to volunteer. His mother begged him to spare her; he would not listen. So she hid all his
  clothes, and shame kept him home. He did the one thing left: he wrote to his father in prison - ''Take
  heed not to change your mind on our account.'' Leonides did not change his mind. He was beheaded, the
  family''s property was seized, and Origen, at seventeen, was left the eldest of seven children of a
  confessor''s widow.'

modern_contrast: >
  A modern reader may hear the teenage Origen's desire to die alongside his
  father as an alarming death-wish, closer to what a modern reader would flag
  as a crisis. This world's own record presents it as PREVENTED, not admired:
  his mother's intervention is the point, and the tradition elsewhere
  explicitly censures rushing on death as a failure of true martyrdom, not its
  highest form (see alx.quote.clement-rash-martyrdom, alx.term.martys) - the
  story shows both the wound of persecution and the community's own check
