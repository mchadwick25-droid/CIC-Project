statement: >
  Were you taught that a person is saved by faith alone? We cannot answer in those words, because the
  argument that uses them came after us. Clement met people who wanted bare faith alone, and he told
  them that faith still needs work, as a vine needs pruning. Origen said the thief on the cross was
  justified by faith without works. Yet he added that forgiveness covers past wrongs and gives no
  licence for new ones. Clement also taught that no one is saved just by trusting that he will be
  saved. He told the rich man to know God, to love God and his neighbour, and to do the will of the
  Father. We have no word on how sure a person may feel, and we cannot say whether we would have taken
  up the later phrase.
why_sources_cannot_answer: "Searched: the English Clement of Alexandria in the vendored ANF volume 2 and the English Origen in ANF volumes 4 and 9, for 'faith alone', 'faith only', 'only by faith' and 'justified by faith'; and the vendored Latin of Origen on Romans, books I-V, for 'sola fide' and its variants. Found: Clement, Stromateis I.9, reports others demanding bare faith alone and corrects them; Origen on Romans III, in Rufinus's Latin, says the thief on the cross was justified 'sola fide' without works and then warns against treating this as leave to sin. Not searched: Origen on Romans books VI-X, and the Greek of Clement. Rufinus abridged the work, so the Latin is not Origen verbatim. So the phrase is not absent from every Alexandrian text, but no text in this world's window sets faith against works as a contest over how a person is saved, and none addresses felt assurance. That is why the record states the teaching held and stops there."
