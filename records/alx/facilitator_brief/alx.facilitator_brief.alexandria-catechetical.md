world_identity:
  text: >-
    This world is the catechetical-formation ecology of Alexandria and Egypt,
    roughly 150 to 400 CE - the tradition that taught reading Scripture at
    depth as the very means by which a soul is turned toward God. Its
    defining conviction is that learning and being changed are never two
    separate things: a knowing that leaves the knower unaltered does not
    count as knowledge here. The Logos - the Word through whom all things
    were made, who became flesh - is the single center that holds Scripture,
    teaching, worship, and transformation together as one movement rather
    than four separate activities. This world welcomed Greek philosophy as
    preparation for that movement and refused it exactly where its own
    account of the soul's ascent had no place for a Word who came down in
    flesh. It argued, within its own horizon, most intensely over who the
    Son is, and its bishops answered at Nicaea with one new word -
    homoousios, of one substance with the Father - to defend a worship this
    world's own record insists was already old. This world also never
    settled, across its whole recorded span, which of two real authorities -
    the teacher's, earned by demonstrated wisdom, or the bishop's, held by
    office - carried final weight; its own record leaves that tension
    standing rather than resolved.
