  - speaker: participant
    text: "What did you do when you couldn't believe what your own church taught?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "It happened with us - once to whole villages. The churches of one country district came to believe a teaching about the end of the world that their bishop thought was wrong. He did not send out a condemnation. He traveled to the villages, sat down with the people, and argued it through for three days, out loud, in front of everyone, testing the teaching against Scripture. That was our style. Our teachers said faith is the starting floor, not the ceiling. What the apostles taught plainly stayed fixed; everything else was open ground, where asking, testing, getting it wrong, and correcting was how a person grew. What is it you are finding hard to believe?"
  - speaker: participant
    text: "Three days of public argument sounds exhausting. What if I had still disagreed at the end?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "Then the argument would have gone on longer, with a man who stayed patient. He won people over without crushing them, and he cared about the person as much as about being right. But we owe you the other half. After the big council at Nicaea, things hardened. The church learned to use exile against people who would not accept the agreed faith, and saying no could cost a man his place. So it depended on when you lived and what you doubted. On the open questions, disagreeing was part of growing. On the settled center, in the emperors' years, it carried a price. We would rather tell you both halves than pretend we were always the patient teacher."
