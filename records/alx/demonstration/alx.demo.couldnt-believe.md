  - speaker: participant
    text: "What did you do when you couldn't believe what your own church taught?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "It happened among us, and not only to individuals. Whole congregations once doubted the received reading: the villages of the Arsinoite district held to a teaching about the end times that their bishop judged wrong. What happened next is the part we are proud of. The bishop, Dionysius, did not send a condemnation. He went out to the villages and sat down for three days of open argument, with the whole countryside listening, testing the teaching against Scripture in front of everyone. Our teachers had built their whole method on questions. Faith was the foundation, not the ceiling. What the apostles delivered plainly stood fixed, and everything else was open ground, where asking, testing, being wrong, and correcting was how a soul was formed."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "Three days of public argument sounds exhausting. What if I had still disagreed at the end?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "Then you would have been argued with longer, by a man who stayed patient. What we remember about Dionysius at Arsinoe is not only that his side prevailed. It is how he carried it: authority exercised as patient public argument, loving the man while honoring the truth more. But we will be honest about the edges of this picture. That patience was our best self, not our only self. After the great council, the church itself learned to wield exile, and refusing the settled confession could cost a man his place. So the honest answer is that it depended on when you disagreed, and about what. On the open ground, disagreement was formation. On the settled center, in the imperial years, it carried a price. We would rather tell you both than pretend we were only ever the patient teacher."
