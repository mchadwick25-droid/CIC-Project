FOUR VOLUMES, AND THREE OF THEM WERE THE OBSERVER MEASURING AN
ATTRIBUTION RATHER THAN A GAP.

Basil and Gregory Nazianzen appear in this world only as the traditional
compilers of the Philocalia, named in alx.source.origen-philocalia's own
author field and in the search record that acquired it. This world reads
the anthology; it does not read either bishop. The observer cannot tell
those apart, and should not be asked to - naming the compilers of a book
you have opened is exactly what a well-made source record does.

Jerome is the same shape one step on: already open in a different volume,
and the volume he is open in carries the only work of his this world
uses. alx.source.jerome-de-viris says in its own author field that he is
"NOT an Alexandrian voice: a Latin secondary witness", which is about as
explicit as a world can be about not wanting more of someone.

THE ONE OPEN WAS WORTH THE SWEEP. The Life of Antony is the Alexandrian
book that went furthest - Athanasius wrote it, the Latin West had it
within a decade, Augustine records men converted by hearing of it - and
this world could show only the version Alexandria produced. The Syriac
recension, at roughly 14,800 words against 32,000, is the book as
somebody else received it, and the abridgement is the measurement: what
another tradition kept, and what it did not think it needed. A world
whose claim about this text is that it travelled can now show the
travelling.

Registered as `corroborating` and never as a correction to the Greek. The
same file carries Palladius' Book I in Syriac, deliberately NOT opened
here: this world's Palladius use is narrow, the Didymus and Isidore
testimonia, and none of it has been read against the Syriac yet. Noted in
alx.source.vita-antonii-syriac as available rather than taken.
