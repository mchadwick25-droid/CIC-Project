text: 'Could a woman carry real authority among us, and what did it cost her? Yes, twice, on the record''s
  own terms - and both times the cost was steep. Justina, regent for her young son, commanded the machinery
  of the state directly: when Ambrose would not surrender a basilica, the heaviest sentences were decreed
  first upon the whole body of merchants - two hundred pounds'' weight of gold required within three days,
  chains placed on the necks of innocent persons during Lent, the prisons full of trades-people, palace
  officials commanded to stand clear. That was real power, exercised in her own name. Its cost: total
  public failure - the basilica was never surrendered, and every word of the record that carries her
  command reaches us through the account of the bishop who defeated her. Pulcheria carried authority long
  enough to help convene the council that closed this world''s window - Leo himself writes to her directly
  as the power that could make a new synod happen, not as a courtesy. Her cost was different: real
  standing bought her a place in the correspondence of powerful men, and nothing beyond it in her own
  words. Both women held command. Neither holds her own voice.'
positions:
- women did exercise directly coercive state authority in this world's own record - Justina's regency
  commanded fines, imprisonment, and the machinery of the palace, not merely influence behind a throne
- that authority's exercise and its failure are both attested plainly - the record does not soften either
- 'the cost common to both documented cases is the same: authority attested, voice not - neither
  woman''s own words on holding or losing power survive anywhere in this world''s base'
tensions:
- both women reach us exclusively through hostile or interested male authors (Ambrose against Justina;
  Leo's correspondence, not Pulcheria's own letters, for Pulcheria) - the confidence in what they did is
  higher than the confidence in why
- this is two data points, not a pattern the record lets us generalize with - what these two women's
  experience does and does not say about women's authority more broadly is exactly what the sources
  cannot tell us
