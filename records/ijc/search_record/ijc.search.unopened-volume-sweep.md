TWELVE OF THIRTEEN WERE ANSWERED WITHOUT OPENING A FILE, by tracing each
match to the record and field that produced it - the rule desert's sweep
proposed, applied here for the first time to a world whose list is
dominated by multi-volume sets.

That domination is the finding. Ten of the thirteen are further volumes
of two authors this world already reads: Augustine in five, Chrysostom in
five. The observer cannot tell "has never read this author" from "reads
this author, in a different volume of the same series", and those are
very different states. This world was in the second one nine times out of
ten, and the useful question is therefore not whether the author is
opened but whether THIS volume answers something the world asks and
cannot answer.

ONE DID, AND IT WAS THE ONE THAT MATTERED MOST. Every claim this world
makes about the alliance between church and empire stands on Romans 13,
and this world held no patristic reading of Romans 13 at all - only the
events. Chrysostom's Homily XXIII supplies the distinction the whole
period turns on: rule as such is God's ordering of the world, and no
particular ruler is therefore chosen by God. "Is every ruler then elected
by God? This I do not say." That is what lets a bishop obey an emperor
and refuse him in the same year, and this world can now show the argument
rather than infer it from behaviour. ijc.quote.no-power-but-of-god
carries it, declared against BOTH church-state-alliance and
episcopal-independence because it is the alliance's best argument and the
independence's escape clause in one paragraph.

A NOTE ON THE VENDORED APPARATUS, worth carrying beyond this world. The
NPNF editor prints a bracketed objection immediately after Chrysostom's
distinction, saying it "belongs rather to a philosophical treatment of
the subject than to an exposition of the apostle's language" - a
nineteenth-century editor telling his readers a fourth-century bishop has
read something into Paul. It is not evidence about the fourth century and
it is not neutral. The vendored volumes have opinions, and a world
opening one for the first time should know where they are before quoting
around them.
