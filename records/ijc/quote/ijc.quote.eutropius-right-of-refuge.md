thence a warning voice to all." Nothing is elided mid-sentence and no
punctuation is altered.

This is the sharpest single line in the corpus on where the alliance
stops. Chrysostom is not arguing with the emperor here; he is arguing
with his own congregation, who want the man handed over, and the reason
he gives them is not law but the crucifixion. The rest of the sentence
is the harder half and is why this is not a piece of piety: he grants
that Eutropius destroyed the very protection now sheltering him, and
calls his ruin a public lesson. Mercy and satisfaction in the same
breath, from the same pulpit.
