Opened 2026-08-27, and it CORRECTS the record it is attached to rather than only supplementing
it. ijc.limit.reading-alone claimed that "our writers read scripture as men arguing for the
church's faith, not as men telling us what the reading cost them" - and cited exactly one source,
ijc.source.augustine-confessions, which is the source that falsifies the second half of that
sentence.

The limit's remaining claim is narrower and survives: an ORDINARY reader's struggles are still
absent. Augustine is a rhetorician turned bishop and the most literary man of his age; his
account of being repelled by scripture's plainness is a highly wrought retrospective, not a
window onto how a farmer in Hippo read. That distinction is now in the limit's own statement
rather than being papered over by a claim that nobody wrote about it at all.
