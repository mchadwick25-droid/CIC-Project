text: For neither do we say that certain Christian emperors were therefore happy because they ruled a
  long time, or, dying a peaceful death, left their sons to succeed them in the empire, or subdued the
  enemies of the republic, or were able both to guard against and to suppress the attempt of hostile
  citizens rising against them. These and other gifts or comforts of this sorrowful life even certain
  worshippers of demons have merited to receive, who do not belong to the kingdom of God to which these
  belong; and this is to be traced to the mercy of God, who would not have those who believe in Him
  desire such things as the highest good. But we say that they are happy if they rule justly; if they
  are not lifted up amid the praises of those who pay them sublime honors, and the obsequiousness of
  those who salute them with an excessive humility, but remember that they are men; if they make their
  power the handmaid of His majesty by using it for the greatest possible extension of His worship; if
  they fear, love, worship God; if more than their own they love that kingdom in which they are not
  afraid to have partners; if they are slow to punish, ready to pardon; if they apply that punishment
  as necessary to government and defence of the republic, and not in order to gratify their own enmity;
  if they grant pardon, not that iniquity may go unpunished, but with the hope that the transgressor may
  amend his ways; if they compensate with the lenity of mercy and the liberality of benevolence for
  whatever severity they may be compelled to decree; if their luxury is as much restrained as it might
  have been unrestrained; if they prefer to govern depraved desires rather than any nation whatever; and
  if they do all these things, not through ardent desire of empty glory, but through love of eternal
  felicity, not neglecting to offer to the true God, who is their God, for their sins, the sacrifices of
  humility, contrition, and prayer.
modern_rendering: >-
  For we do not say that certain Christian emperors were happy because they ruled a long time. Nor do
  we say so because they died a peaceful death and left their sons to succeed them in the empire. Nor
  is it because they defeated the enemies of the republic. Nor is it because they could both guard
  against and crush the attempts of hostile citizens who rose against them. Even certain worshippers
  of demons have deserved to receive these and other gifts or comforts of this sorrowful life. They do
  not belong to the kingdom of God, to which these emperors belong. This comes from the mercy of God.
  He does not want those who believe in him to desire such things as the highest good. But we say that
  they are happy if they rule justly. They are happy if they do not grow proud amid praise and lofty
  honors, or amid the fawning of those who greet them too humbly. They are happy if, instead, they
  remember that they are men. They are happy if they make their power the servant of God's majesty,
  using it to spread his worship as far as possible. They are happy if they fear, love and worship
  God. They are happy if they love, more than their own kingdom, that kingdom in which they are not
  afraid to have partners. They are happy if they are slow to punish and ready to pardon. They are
  happy if they use punishment as needed to govern and defend the republic, and not to satisfy their
  own hatred. They are happy if they grant pardon, not so that wrongdoing goes unpunished, but in the
  hope that the wrongdoer will mend his ways. They are happy if they make up for whatever severity
  they are forced to decree, with gentle mercy and generous kindness. They are happy if their luxury
  is held back as much as it might have run free. They are happy if they would rather govern wicked
  desires than any nation at all. And they are happy if they do all these things, not from a burning
  desire for empty glory, but from love of eternal happiness. They are happy if they do not fail to
  offer sacrifices for their sins to the true God, who is their God. These are the sacrifices of
  humility, contrition and prayer.
