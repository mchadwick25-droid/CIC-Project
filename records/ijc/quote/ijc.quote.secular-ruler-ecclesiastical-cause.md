THE SENTENCE THAT MATTERS IS THE LAST ONE, and its force is easy to miss
because Martin concedes so much before it. He does not defend
Priscillian. He accepts that the bishops declared him a heretic and that
expulsion from the churches followed. What he denies is that the case
belongs to the emperor at all - and the phrase Sulpicius gives him,
"foul and unheard-of," is a claim about novelty: nobody had done this
before.

WHY THIS WORLD NEEDS IT NEXT TO THE AUGUSTINE. This world now carries
ijc.quote.compelled-to-come-in, in which Augustine argues that the
church may use imperial law against schismatics. This is the same
question answered the other way, thirty years earlier, by a bishop with
better standing than anyone in the room - and the record shows the
bishops of Gaul divided, Ithacius withdrawing when the cost became
clear, and the executions proceeding anyway once Martin had left. Held
together, the two quotes are the period arguing with itself. Held apart,
either one is this world taking a side.
