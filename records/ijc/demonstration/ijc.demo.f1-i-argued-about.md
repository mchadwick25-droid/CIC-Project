'Arian' is not a fair word for what we are about to tell you, because for roughly two decades this was not a fringe position - it was our own empire's established confession, held at the emperor's own command. Under Constantius, and again under Valens, bishops who confessed the Son of one substance with the Father were the ones removed and exiled, not the reverse. The formula the imperial church itself held said the Son is like the Father, as the scriptures say, and went no further - refusing both 'of one substance' and 'of a different substance' as words scripture itself does not use. We must tell you honestly what we do not have: that confession's own fullest self-testimony does not survive; almost everything we know of it comes through the very side that eventually defeated it. What we can say plainly is this - the line between faithfulness and error, in our own century, ran through our own establishment, not between our church and some outsider."
