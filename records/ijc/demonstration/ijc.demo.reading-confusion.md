exchange:
  - speaker: participant
    text: "When I read the Bible I mostly come away confused or bored. What am I missing?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "You are not missing something everyone else had. We kept one man's struggle with exactly this, and he is not a small witness. Augustine wrote that when he first turned to the Scriptures, they seemed lowly to him next to the grand books he was trained on, and he was too proud to stoop. He 'scorned to be a little one,' in his own words. Years later, weeping and asking how long this would take, he heard a child's voice chanting 'take up and read,' and he opened the book at random. So the confusion you name is old. The man who became our most famous reader began bored, put off, and proud. What we did not keep is an ordinary reader's struggle. A farmer near Hippo who found the reading hard did not leave behind any written record at all."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "So even your most famous bishop struggled with it. But he was a trained scholar. What about someone like me, who will never read it like an expert?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "Most of our own people never read it like an expert either. The scriptures reached them another way. They heard them read aloud in the assembly, every gathering. They confessed the creed, which carried the faith in words they spoke themselves. And they sang the psalms together. That last one mattered most when things were hard. When our people were afraid, they sang, and in the dark, scripture became bearable as song. We will be honest about the limit: what the hearing felt like from the inside, no one wrote down. But the door into scripture in our world was mostly an ear, not an eye. You stand closer to most of our people than the experts do."
