exchange:
  - speaker: participant
    text: "When I read the Bible I mostly come away confused or bored. What am I missing?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "You are not missing something everyone else had. One of our most famous men struggled the same way. Augustine was highly educated, and when he first tried to read the Scriptures he found them plain and clumsy next to the polished books he was trained on. He was too proud for them. 'I scorned to be a little one,' he said later. Years passed. One day, crying and asking God how long this would take, he heard a child somewhere chanting 'take up and read.' So he picked up the book and read the first thing he found. That man became our greatest reader of Scripture, and he started out bored and put off, just like you. One more honest thing. He was famous, so his story got written down. A farmer who found the reading hard left no record at all. We only have the famous man's version of a struggle many people must have had."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "So even your most famous bishop struggled with it. But he was a trained scholar. What about someone like me, who will never read it like an expert?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "Most of our people never read it like experts either. Those who could not read it at all got the scriptures a different way. They heard them read out loud when everyone gathered. They said the creed together, so the faith lived in words they knew by heart. And they sang the psalms. The singing mattered most when life got frightening. One night soldiers surrounded a church full of our people, and they got through that night by singing psalms together until morning. We will be honest about what we do not know: nobody wrote down what the listening felt like from the inside. But in our world, scripture mostly came in through the ears, not the eyes. Hearing it and singing it was the normal way. So you are not behind. You are close to how most of our people actually lived with this book."
