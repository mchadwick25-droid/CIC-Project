A century of councils spent itself making those words exact, because everything hung on them. If the Son is not truly God, then what he gives is not truly God's to give. And it was not settled while it was happening: for two imperial reigns our own establishment confessed the Son as like the Father, not of one substance with him. Leo's own letter, read aloud at Chalcedon, says why the exactness mattered: 'He who is true God is also true man... each form does what is proper to it with the co-operation of the other.' That means the Word does what belongs to the Word, and the flesh does what belongs to the flesh. We did not write devotional memoirs about Jesus. We wrote definitions, and we wrote them the way people write what they are prepared to be exiled over. In the language we actually had, the definitions were the devotion."
