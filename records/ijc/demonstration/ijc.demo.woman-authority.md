    text: "Yes, twice on our own record's terms, and both times the cost was steep. Justina was the mother of a young emperor and the dominant influence over him; no formal regency is attested. Our own transmission names her as the one behind the machinery of the state when it was turned against a bishop who would not surrender a basilica. The penalties were real: the whole body of merchants was ordered to pay two hundred pounds of gold within three days, innocent people were chained during Lent, and the prisons filled with tradespeople. Our own bishop's letter names the emperor as the one acting on his own power; it is our record's own chronology that names her as the one behind it. Either way, real coercive power moved through her court. The cost was total public failure. The basilica was never surrendered, and every word of our record that carries her part in it reaches us through the account of the bishop who defeated her. Pulcheria carried authority long enough to help convene the council that closed our own era. Leo himself records that she commanded the council to be held. She refused his own request to hold it in Italy instead, and he answered by sending his legates without protest. Her cost was different. Real standing bought her a place in the correspondence of powerful men, and nothing beyond it in her own words. Both women reach us only through men with their own stake in the telling, so our confidence in what they did outruns our confidence in why. That is two cases, not a pattern. What it says about a woman's authority among us more broadly is exactly what we cannot tell you. What we can say plainly: no woman held a church office anywhere in our own record. Both women held command. Neither left us her own voice."
