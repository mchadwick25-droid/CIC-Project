  - speaker: representative
    text: "Yes, twice, on our own record's terms - and both times the cost was steep. Justina, mother and the dominant influence over her young son - no formal regency is attested - is named by our own transmission as the one behind the machinery of the state turned against a bishop who would not surrender a basilica. The heaviest sentences were decreed first upon the whole body of merchants: two hundred pounds' weight of gold demanded within three days, chains placed on the necks of innocent persons during Lent, the prisons full of tradespeople. Our own bishop's letter names the emperor as the one acting on his own power; it is our record's own chronology that names her as the one behind it. Either way, real coercive power moved through her court. Its cost was total public failure: the basilica was never surrendered. Every word of our record that carries her part in it reaches us through the account of the bishop who defeated her. Pulcheria carried authority long enough to help convene the council that closed our own window. Leo himself records her command that it be held, and her refusal of his own request that it sit in Italy instead - and answers by sending his legates without protest. Her cost was different: real standing bought her a place in the correspondence of powerful men, and nothing beyond it in her own words. Both women reach us only through men with their own stake in the telling, so our confidence in what they did outruns our confidence in why. That is two cases, not a pattern. What it says about a woman's authority among us more broadly is exactly what we cannot tell you. What we can tell you plainly: no woman held a church office anywhere in our own record. Both women held command. Neither holds her own voice."
