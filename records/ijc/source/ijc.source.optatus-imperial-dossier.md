WHAT IT IS. This world is called juridical, and until now it held almost
no juridical documents - it read the laws through Sozomen and the
councils through their canons. This is the paperwork itself: an emperor
convening a council by letter, a proconsul reporting to his emperor
about a church dispute, an emperor telling bishops what to do about
buildings. The Donatist controversy is the first case in which the newly
Christian state had to work out what it was for, and the dossier is that
working-out in the participants' own administrative prose.

THE DOCUMENT MOST WORTH KNOWING ABOUT IS APPENDIX X. In 330 the
Donatists refused to hand back basilicas they held, one of which
Constantine had built for Catholic use. He did not send troops. The
rescript leaves them "in undisturbed possession of their ill-gotten
goods" - the editor's phrase, not the emperor's - and provides for a new
church to be built at imperial expense instead. An emperor declining to
enforce, and paying twice rather than escalate, is a limit on the
alliance recorded from the state's side, which nothing else in this
world's registry supplies.
