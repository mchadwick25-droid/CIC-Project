I RANKED THIS VOLUME AS SMALL AND WAS WRONG, which is worth recording
because the error was methodological. The assignment pile was sorted by
word count and npnf211 came out near the bottom, so it was set aside as
low-yield. Word count does not measure whether a document is on a
world's subject. This one is: it is the first time in history that a
Christian state executed Christians for heresy, and it is reported by a
contemporary.

WHAT HAPPENED, AND WHY IT BELONGS HERE. In 385 Priscillian, bishop of
Avila, and several companions were tried before the prefect Evodius at
Trier under the emperor Maximus, and beheaded. Martin of Tours was in
the city and argued against it - not that Priscillian was innocent, but
that expulsion from the churches by episcopal sentence was punishment
enough, and that for a secular ruler to judge an ecclesiastical cause
was, in Sulpicius' phrase, a foul and unheard-of indignity. He extracted
a promise from the emperor, and the executions followed once he had
gone. Ithacius, the bishop who had brought the accusation, withdrew from
the capital trial when he saw what it would cost him among his
colleagues, and a treasury advocate was appointed in his place.

THIS IS THE COUNTER-CASE TO THE CORRECTION OF THE DONATISTS, and this
world now holds both. Augustine argues that the church may use the
state's coercive power against schismatics; Martin argues thirty years
earlier that it may not use it at all in a matter of doctrine, and the
bishops split over it. A world about church and empire that carried only
one of those arguments would be making a case rather than showing a
period.

WHAT IS NOT DRAWN FROM THIS FILE HERE. Sulpicius' Life of Martin and the
Dialogues, in the same volume, carry their own Priscillian material
(the Trier bishops at line 5078) and belong to the Gallic entries rather
than to this world; the corpus map assigns them elsewhere.
