WHY THIS WORLD DID NOT HAVE IT. The corpus assignment run put five
npnf109 works on this world and none of them was ever opened here.
That is not a gap in the volume's relevance - it is the plainest
church-and-empire document in the vendored corpus, and this world had
been built almost entirely from the West and from council records.
Chrysostom appears in no record of this world under any spelling.

WHAT IT IS. Eutropius was a eunuch, chamberlain to the emperor
Arcadius, and in 399 the first eunuch ever to hold the consulship. He
had procured laws cutting off the church's right of asylum. When he
fell, he ran to the altar of the Great Church - the sanctuary he had
himself stripped of protection - and Chrysostom preached over him while
he clung to it, in front of a congregation that wanted him handed over.
Chrysostom's answer to his own people is at line 17739: "But, you say,
he cut off the right of refuge here by his ordinances and divers kinds
of laws. Yes! yet now he has learned by experience what it was he did."
At line 17704 he defends himself for "having admitted him to the
sanctuary" against the charge that "he who continually made war upon
the Church has taken refuge within it." At line 17808 the emperor is
recorded restraining an army that "demanded him to be given up for
execution."

WHAT IT IS NOT. It is not disinterested. Chrysostom is preaching, in
public, in a live political crisis in which he is himself a party, and
he says so. The second homily is preached after the sanctuary failed:
Eutropius left the church, was taken, and was exiled to Cyprus and put
to death. Both the intercession and its failure are in the record, and
this world should carry both. Chrysostom's own deposition by the
imperial court five years later - the material in the same volume's
Correspondence with the Bishop of Rome, div1 at line 21717 - is not
drawn on by this record and remains unopened here.
