ROMANS 13 IS THE TEXT THIS WORLD IS ABOUT, and until now it had no
patristic reading of it.

Every claim this world makes about the alliance between church and
empire - ijc.gravity.church-state-alliance, ijc.gravity.episcopal-
independence and the tension between them - runs through a passage of
Paul that says the powers that be are ordained of God. What the world
held was the events: Constantine, the councils, the laws, Ambrose at
Milan, Augustine on compulsion. What it did not hold was a bishop
working the verse itself in front of a congregation.

Chrysostom's move is the one that matters, and he makes it against an
obvious objection he raises himself. Asked whether every ruler is
therefore elected by God, he answers: "This I do not say." The subjection
is owed to the INSTITUTION and not to the person holding it - "he does
not say, 'for there is no ruler but of God;' but it is the thing he
speaks of." What is divinely willed is that there be rule at all, rather
than everything carried on in one confusion.

That distinction is the whole hinge of this world. It is what lets a
bishop obey an emperor and excommunicate him in the same year without
contradiction, and it is what an emperor's apologist could not use.
ijc.quote.no-power-but-of-god carries it.

He also says, at the outset, that this is binding on everyone "even for
priests, and monks, and not for men of secular occupations only". A
world about clerical independence should notice that the classic proof
text for obedience was preached as reaching the clergy first.

THE VOLUME'S OWN EDITOR OBJECTS, in a bracketed note printed straight
after the distinction: that separating authority in the abstract from
authority in the concrete "belongs rather to a philosophical treatment of
the subject than to an exposition of the apostle's language". That is a
nineteenth-century Protestant editor telling his readers that Chrysostom
has read something into Paul. It is not evidence about the fourth
century, and it is a useful reminder that the vendored apparatus has
opinions.
