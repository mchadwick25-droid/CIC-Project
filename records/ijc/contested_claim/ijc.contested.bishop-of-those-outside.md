claim: When Constantine told the bishops "you are bishops whose jurisdiction is within the Church; I
  also am a bishop, ordained by God to overlook whatever is external to the church," he was stating a
  considered theory of imperial religious authority - the emperor as a kind of bishop for the world.
held_against:
- The saying survives only in Eusebius, reported from table-talk, inside a posthumous panegyric by the
  emperor's own admirer - single-source, and the source is the world's most interested witness
- What the remark meant - a theory of office, a genial dinner-table analogy, or Eusebius's own court
  theology put into his patron's mouth - is not recoverable from one reported sentence
- The Greek phrase's own ambiguity (bishop "of those outside" - outside what, overseeing whom?) is argued
  over in the scholarship to this day
concedes: 'The saying''s textual existence in the Vita is Documented, and its afterlife is real: whatever
  Constantine meant, the idea of imperially-adjacent religious authority it gestures at is the seed the
  Constantinopolitan strand''s later conciliar claims make institutionally explicit. The record supports
  the seed''s existence; it does not settle the sower''s intent.'
