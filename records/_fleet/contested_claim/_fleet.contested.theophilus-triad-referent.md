claim: "Theophilus of Antioch's triad of \"God, and His Word, and His wisdom\" (c. 169-188 CE, the earliest surviving use of the Greek word Τριάδος/\"Trinity\") is the same referent as the developed Trinitarian doctrine later formalized at Nicaea (325) and after - three co-equal, co-eternal persons in one being."
held_against:
- "The edition's own footnote on Theophilus II.15 reads his usage as unremarkable to him (\"illustrating an accepted word, not introducing a new one\"), not as a first statement of a developed doctrine; a second footnote on the same passage notes the referents assigned to the third member vary even within Theophilus's own corpus (Wisdom here; the Son elsewhere), which is not how the co-equal, co-eternal persons of the later formula are distinguished."
- "Tertullian's own Against Praxeas (from 208 CE on) is closer in time to Theophilus than to Nicaea, and is itself still arguing the question - one substance, three Persons, against a rival Monarchian reading - rather than reporting a settled formula; a doctrine still being argued in 208-213 is evidence that Theophilus's triad, roughly three decades to a century earlier, cannot simply be read as the same settled referent Nicaea later formalizes."
- "Reading the word's early appearances as continuous with the later doctrine risks projecting the fourth-century formula backward onto a second-century author who never states co-equality or co-eternity as such."
concedes: "The word's continuity is not in dispute: Theophilus, Tertullian, and Nicaea are all naming a threefold subject - Father/God, Son/Word, Spirit/Wisdom - and each later use draws on the same word this earliest one coined. What is contested is only whether the referent named stayed constant across that span, or whether the word survived while the doctrine it names developed substantially underneath it. This record does not resolve that question; _fleet.modern.trinity's own distinguishing_claim states both the word's early date and the doctrine's later formation without asserting either reading of the referent question."
