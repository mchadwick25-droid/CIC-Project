Rights verified from the file's own DC.Rights header (Public Domain).

Dating: the edition's own introductory notice states Theophilus "succeeded
to the bishopric of Antioch in the eighth year of the reign of Marcus
Aurelius, that is, in a.d. 168," and "is related to have died either in
a.d. 181, or in a.d. 188" - two competing traditions, both stated; the
same notice's own opening bracketed heading ("[a.d. 115-168-181.]") uses
181 as its single headline figure. Book III.28's editorial footnote fixes
the author's own chronological reckoning to the death of Verus, a.d. 169.
Together these support c. 169-181 as the tighter, edition-preferred
window, with a.d. 188 an explicit alternative the same notice also states
- neither is invented, and the treatise's own composition date is not
independently pinned beyond this. Book II.15's own footnote on Τριάδος:
"The earliest use of this word 'Trinity.' ... He does not lug it in as
something novel: 'types of the Trinity,' he says, illustrating an
accepted word, not introducing a new one." The main text itself, with
the footnote's own content set aside, reads "are types of the Trinity,
... of God, and His Word, and His wisdom" - Τριάδος is the footnote's own
Greek gloss on "Trinity," not a bracketed word inside that sentence. A
separate footnote on the same passage glosses the referent as God, His
Word (assigned elsewhere by Theophilus to the Son), and His Wisdom (here
assigned to the Holy Spirit) - the same footnote also quotes an eminent
authority (Kaye) stating "it is certain... God, His Word, and His wisdom
constitute a Trinity; and it should seem a Trinity of persons" - see
_fleet.contested.theophilus-triad-referent for both sides of whether this
triad is the same referent as the doctrine later formalized at Nicaea.
