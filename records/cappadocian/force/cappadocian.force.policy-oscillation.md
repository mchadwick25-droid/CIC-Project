description: |-
  Imperial policy on religion swung back and forth. Constantius backed Homoian councils and formulas. Julian restored the pagan cults and issued his school edict (361-363); he had spent his boyhood in Cappadocia, at Macellum. Valens pressed the Nicene clergy with depositions and exiles, among them Gregory of Nyssa (about 376-378). He also divided the province of Cappadocia (372). Then Theodosius reversed course (380-381). The events are documented. Where an encomium is the source, the detail of a scene is contested.

  In the world's own experience, the palace's faith changed with the reigns like weather off the mountains. One king signed the fathers' faith. Another pressed a different formula. An apostate loved the old gods and hated our schools. A soldier-king's officials tested the bishop at the church door. Then, at the end, came a Spaniard under whom the long-forbidden word became law. The world learned to hold its confession the way its farmers held seed through winter. They held it not because the season was kind, but because it would turn.

  For formation, this force kept the gravity of the contested church always active, as endurance, not settlement. It forced the Triune confession to be portable, held in formula, doxology and psalm, things no exile could take. It made exile the shape of being a confessor in this era. Through the school edict it armed the crisis of paideia converted. And it taught the difference between an age's weather and its direction.

- Theodosius' reversal (380-381)
