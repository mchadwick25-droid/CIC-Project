  In the case of trigamy and polygamy they laid down the same rule, in
  proportion, as in the case of digamy; namely one year for digamy (some
  authorities say two years); for trigamy men are separated for three
  and often for four years; but this is no longer described as marriage
  at all, but as polygamy; nay rather as limited fornication.

modern_lens_note: >-
  A modern reader may hear "one year for digamy" as an arbitrary
  penalty, or hear "limited fornication" (his word for a third
  marriage) as simple condemnation. Basil is doing something more
  precise: setting a graded scale, keyed to how far a practice departs
  from a first marriage kept whole - a second marriage gets the
  shortest, defined term of penance and is still called marriage; a
  third gets three or four years and is reclassified as something the
  Canon no longer dignifies with that name at all. The harshness rises
  with the departure from the norm, but even the harshest case in this
  passage still names a set term, not permanent exclusion.
