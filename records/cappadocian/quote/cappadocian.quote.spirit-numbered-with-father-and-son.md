modern_lens_note: >-
  A modern reader might hear "ranked with the Father" as a matter of
  protocol - precedence, or seating order - and miss that Basil is
  making a claim about being, not etiquette. His opponents' proposal
  was to "relegate" the Spirit "to the nature of a ministering
  spirit," the biblical language Hebrews uses for angels: exalted,
  but still creaturely, still less than God. Basil's point is that
  ranking the Spirit with the Father is not a courtesy extended to a
  lesser being but a confession of undivided, shared Godhead - which
  is exactly why he calls denying it a "necessary and saving
  doctrine" to get right, not a matter of theological etiquette.
