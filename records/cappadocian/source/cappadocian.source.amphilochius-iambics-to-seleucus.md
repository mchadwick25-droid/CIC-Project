The one piece of Amphilochius of Iconium's own argument this build actually holds
in an open English rendering, against Doc_02 §1.6's and Source Registry row 65's
prior statement that none had been located.

WHAT IT IS, MEASURED RATHER THAN ESTIMATED. About 90 rendered words of
Amphilochius inside a 129-word body. He warns that not every book called
Scripture is a safe guide -- "for some are tolerably sound and others are more
than doubtful" -- says he will therefore enumerate the books God's inspiration
has given, notes after the Old Testament list that "to these some add Esther,"
turns to the New Testament, notes that "some add to these the Revelation of
John, but by far the majority say that it is spurious," and closes: "This is the
most true canon of the divinely given Scriptures."

WHAT IS MISSING FROM IT, AND WHY THAT IS THE INTERESTING PART. The two lists
themselves are not here. Where they stood the editor put bracketed summaries of
his own -- that the Old Testament proto-canonical books follow with Esther alone
omitted and all deutero-canonical books omitted, and that all the New Testament
books follow except the Revelation. So the one surviving sample of this world's
junior figure in his own voice is a canon-list poem with the canon list taken
out. It is not verse in this rendering either; the editor's epitome is prose.

WHY ROW 65 IS CORRECTED RATHER THAN DELETED. Row 65's underlying judgment -- that
this world knows Amphilochius overwhelmingly as addressee and first receiver
rather than through his own extended voice -- is correct and survives this
finding intact, and 90 words with the substance excised does not begin to
disturb it. What row 65 got wrong was the flat factual claim that no open
English edition of any of his own work had been located, which was not true of
the shared library at the time it was written; the corrected row states the
narrower truth instead.
