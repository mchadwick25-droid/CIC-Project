text: >-
  Belonging cost real relationships here, not only in theory. One of our
  closest friendships, formed at school and remembered for years as one
  soul in two bodies, was wounded when one friend appointed the other to a
  small, ill-favored post for jurisdictional reasons of his own - and the
  friendship, though it never simply ended, carried that wound into
  everything that came after. One of our own left a promising career, and
  his household, for the wilderness, and died young there; his mother's
  grief, our record says, was severe. What held us together across the
  real distances of a mountain country with hard winters was not a single
  center. It was letters. Our own bishops and teachers wrote constantly -
  to each other, to their own scattered communities, to allies and to
  former friends turned adversaries. That correspondence carried authority
  the way a formula, a custom, or a psalm carried it elsewhere in our
  life: portable, personal, able to cross a closed mountain pass when a
  council or a bishop's own physical presence could not.
