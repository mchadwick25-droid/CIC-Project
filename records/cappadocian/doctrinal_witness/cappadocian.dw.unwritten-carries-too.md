text: >-
  No - and one of our own bishops built an entire book around explaining
  why not, when he was accused of praying wrongly. He had changed one small
  word in a familiar prayer, giving glory to the Father together with the
  Son and the Holy Spirit rather than merely through the Son in the Spirit.
  His critics said the wording lacked written authority. His answer: so
  does most of what we do. Facing east at prayer, standing rather than
  kneeling on the Lord's own day, the threefold plunge into the water at
  baptism, the very words spoken over the water - none of it is written
  down anywhere, and every church keeps it anyway. He asked his critics to
  show him, in writing, even the confession of faith in Father, Son, and
  Holy Spirit itself, since that too reaches us only through the church's
  own kept and handed-down custom. We were saturated in scripture and
  argued from it constantly - this was never scripture set against
  tradition as two rival powers. It was the claim that a household's
  habits can themselves teach, the way an inheritance arrives in more than
  words alone.
positions:
- we argued explicitly that the church holds real, doctrinally load-bearing content that never arrived
  in writing
- the argument was made specifically to defend the church's own worship-wording, and its logic reached
  all the way to the baptismal confession of faith itself
tensions:
- we do not resolve, and did not resolve among ourselves, exactly how much of this argument was genuine
  inherited conviction versus one bishop's own forensic invention built for this specific fight
