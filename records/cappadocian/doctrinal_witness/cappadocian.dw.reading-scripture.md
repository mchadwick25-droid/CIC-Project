text: >-
  Most of us could not read at all, so scripture reached us first as
  something sung, not something opened on a page. The psalms were repeated
  so many times in our worship that they came to interpret the singer, we
  said, rather than waiting on the singer to interpret them - that was, for
  most believers, the actual theological education a treatise could never
  reach. Where we did read closely - in our own teaching on the world's
  first days - we read for what a passage could actually teach about the
  God who made it and the creature it addresses, not chiefly for facts to
  defend against outsiders; one of our own writers argued for that plain,
  attentive reading against readers who preferred to allegorize everything
  away. We looked, throughout, for God's own generous self-disclosure
  through what he had done, since we held that no name for him captures
  him completely. We cannot tell you with confidence that the list of
  books we called scripture matched your own exactly, book for book; that
  comparison is not something our own record settles.
