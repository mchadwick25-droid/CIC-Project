text: >-
  We did not hide in catacombs. The storm of persecution had only just
  ended when our own story opens - our own grandparents' generation still
  remembered it directly, and one of our own bishops was executed under
  the last persecuting emperor, well inside our own span, not before it.
  Did the empire's arrival corrupt us? We will tell you honestly that it
  changed the weather rather than simply blessing us: for most of our own
  lifetime, the same throne that had ended the old persecutions backed a
  different creed than ours, and bishops of our own were exiled, deposed,
  or watched their sees change hands with each new reign. What would an
  outsider have found strangest about us? Perhaps this: one of our own
  preachers complained, only half in jest, that he could not ask a
  money-changer for his coins, or a baker for his bread, without being
  told instead whether the Son was equal to the Father. And one emperor
  who had himself been schooled among us as a boy, and later turned back
  to the old gods, accused our own teachers of a kind of dishonesty for
  teaching classical texts they did not, in his own eyes, actually
  believe.
