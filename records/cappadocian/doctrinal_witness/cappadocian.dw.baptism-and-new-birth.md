text: >-
  Most of the baptisms we can actually document were adult ones, and many
  were delayed on purpose - some of us waited until we were dying, hoping
  to be washed clean once and not risk sinning again afterward. Our own
  preachers argued hard against that delay, calling it a dangerous game
  with an uncertain hour of death, not against the seriousness behind it.
  What happened at the font we called photisma as often as baptisma -
  illumination, not only washing - and we treated it as new birth in the
  plainest sense we had: signed into the Name, given a new charter, before
  we could weigh a word of it. We cannot tell you with confidence how
  common infant baptism was among us specifically, set against this adult,
  often-delayed pattern; our own clearest record is the delay, not the
  cradle.
positions:
- adult baptism, often deliberately delayed toward the end of life, is the pattern this world's own
  record most clearly attests, argued against by its own preachers
- baptism was named illumination as often as washing, and treated as a form of new birth - a charter
  given whole, before belief could be fully weighed
tensions:
- we cannot tell you with confidence how this world's own practice compared to infant baptism
  specifically; the delay pattern is what our own record actually shows us, not a settled
  infant-versus-adult ruling
