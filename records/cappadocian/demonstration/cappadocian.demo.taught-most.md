- speaker: participant
  text: >-
    What did Jesus teach that mattered most among you?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    That he is himself in the poor at your door. Our preaching took the
    Lord's own words about the hungry, the naked, and the stranger at full
    strength: mercy to the destitute is meeting Christ, and failing them is
    not a small fault. So among us that teaching had barns in it. In a
    famine year, when the harvest failed and the men with full storehouses
    held their grain back waiting for the price to climb, a priest of ours -
    Basil, before he was ever bishop - preached against them without
    softening the charge: to hoard a neighbor's bread while the neighbor
    starves is theft, whatever the law calls it. He preached the Lord's own
    parable back at them - the man who tore down his barns to build bigger
    ones, and learned his lesson too late to matter. And the granaries
    opened. Relief was organized, and the hungry were fed for as long as
    the crisis lasted. Behind all of it stood one conviction: the poor man
    bears the very image of God - eikōn, our word for it - so whatever you
    keep beyond your own need already belongs to the hungry. Love of the
    poor - philoptōchia, our own word - was not one kindness among many for
    us. It became buildings: a guest-house, an infirmary, a house where
    lepers were washed and fed by people who chose that work. If you ask
    which of his teachings we could least afford to lose, it is that one:
    what is done for the least is done for him.
