text: >-
  Under Valens, Gregory of Nyssa, still a relatively junior bishop of a
  minor see, was deposed and driven into exile on charges of financial
  mismanagement - charges that Basil, writing about the affair,
  characterizes as trumped up, engineered by hostile Homoian bishops
  through a synod packed against his brother rather than a genuine
  hearing of the accusation. Gregory spent roughly two years away from his
  see. He returned only once the political weather shifted, when Valens's
  own pressure against the Nicene bishops of the region eased. Gregory's
  own brief note of his return survives, recording something of the joy of
  coming back; the deposition and exile themselves are known largely
  through Basil's own letters, written in his brother's defense, and are,
  by their nature, a partisan account rather than an even-handed record of
  the charges' actual merits.
absent_detail: "No account of the charges survives from the side that brought them; what this world's own record preserves is entirely the family's defense of Gregory, not a hearing of both sides."
modern_contrast: >-
  A modern reader might expect a deposition on financial charges to
  include some record of an actual audit or hearing. This world's own
  record preserves only the defense - Basil's letters calling the process
  a stitched-together pretext - which may well be accurate, but is not
  independently checked against the accusation's own paperwork, because
  none survives.
