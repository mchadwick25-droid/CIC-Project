text: >-
  Gregory of Nyssa, preaching in Constantinople during the pneumatological
  controversy's most intense years, complained that the argument had
  spread everywhere in the capital, down to people with no theological
  training at all. Ask a money-changer for your change, he says, and the
  man tells you instead how the Son differs from the Father. Ask a baker
  the price of bread, and you get an answer about the Father being greater
  and the Son subordinate to him. Ask whether your bath is ready, and the
  attendant tells you the Son was made out of nothing. This is Gregory's
  own complaint, aimed at showing how far a technical theological dispute -
  specifically the argument that the Father alone is truly Unbegotten, and
  that this fact by itself settles the Son's lesser status - had leaked
  into completely ordinary transactions, exasperating a preacher who
  wanted the argument handled with more care than a street exchange
  allows. What is Documented is that Gregory made a complaint of this
  substance, as part of his own preaching against that argument; the
  precise wording popularly attributed to it is not independently checked
  against a secured translation, so none of it is quoted here as verbatim.
absent_detail: "No independent confirmation exists that ordinary tradespeople in Constantinople actually discussed this doctrine as described; the complaint survives only as the preacher's own rhetorical report of his own frustration."
modern_contrast: >-
  A modern reader might take this as literal social-historical evidence
  that ancient laypeople were unusually theologically engaged. This
  world's own record frames it as a preacher's exasperated exaggeration,
  aimed at making a rhetorical point about a real, live dispute - vivid
  testimony to how seriously the argument was taken by someone, not a
  survey of the whole city.
