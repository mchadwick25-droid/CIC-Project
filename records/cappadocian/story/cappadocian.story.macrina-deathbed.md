text: >-
  In 379, Gregory of Nyssa traveled to see his sister Macrina - the eldest
  of his siblings, whom we called simply the Teacher, hē
  didaskalos - and found her already dying. She lay not on a couch but on a
  plank set on the ground, wrapped in a rough garment, with no possessions
  around her. As she was dying, brother and sister talked - about the soul,
  about the resurrection, about whether death should be feared - and
  Gregory later shaped that last conversation into a full philosophical
  dialogue, On the Soul and Resurrection, staged at her deathbed in the
  deliberate manner of Plato's own account of Socrates' last hours. She
  died that evening. Gregory records his own grief plainly: he says he
  wept, and struggled through the funeral rites that followed. A crowd
  gathered for her burial at the family's estate chapel at Annisa, where
  earlier generations of the family already lay, together, in Gregory's own
  account, with a company of virgins she had gathered and led as the
  community's own mother-figure. Everything in this scene comes through
  Gregory's own literary hand - the Life of Macrina, composed shortly
  after, and the dialogue shaped from their last conversation. He was
  present and grieving. What we hear is a bereaved, literarily accomplished
  brother's account of his sister's death, in words he gives her, so far as
  any of them can be called hers rather than his.
absent_detail: "Macrina's own words survive nowhere except inside her brother's literary composition; no independent account of her deathbed or funeral exists from any other witness."
modern_contrast: >-
  A modern reader might treat "eyewitness account" as settling the
  question of whose words these are. This world's own record does not let
  the two questions collapse into one: that Gregory was present and
  grieving is Documented; that the philosophical argument he puts in his
  dying sister's mouth is entirely hers is not - and this world tells the
  story with both of those things held together, told as the brother told
  it, rather than one substituted for the other.
