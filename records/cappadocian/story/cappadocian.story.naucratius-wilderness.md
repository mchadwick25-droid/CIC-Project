text: >-
  Among Macrina's brothers was Naucratius, gifted, and headed, it seemed,
  for a rhetorician's career. At twenty-two he turned instead to solitude,
  taking to the wilderness with a single household servant, Chrysapius, who
  chose to share the life with him rather than remain merely his servant.
  Naucratius lived there as a solitary, not within any organized community
  - no rule or brotherhood yet existed for anyone to join. He supported himself and, with what he caught,
  fed elderly and poor people nearby by hunting and fishing, while still
  caring for his mother from a distance. He died suddenly in an accident
  while still young; his body was recovered together with Chrysapius, who
  had died attempting to save or recover him. The family's grief is
  recorded as severe, his mother's especially, and Macrina is remembered as
  the one who held the household together and kept her mother from despair
  in the aftermath.
absent_detail: "Nothing survives of Naucratius's own voice or of Chrysapius's; both are known only through the family's grief as Gregory records it."

  A modern reader might read solitary renunciation and later organized

  communal - a reminder that the ordered brotherhoods this world became
  known for were not yet available to him.
