text: >-
  This is how it would have been, at a martyr's feast kept at a shrine
  somewhere in the countryside - reconstructed from what preachers who
  stood at such shrines themselves describe, offered as reconstruction,
  not as one dated occasion. On the vigil night before the feast, the
  faithful gathered at the shrine itself, kept awake through the dark
  hours by prayer and by the martyr's own passion retold - the homilies
  preached at these gatherings, on the Forty, on Gordius, on Theodore the
  soldier-martyr, are themselves the record of what such a night held,
  each preacher recounting the saint's own suffering to the crowd gathered
  to honor it. The feast drew more than worshippers. Basil's Longer Rule XL
  says that buying and selling at martyrs' tombs is not fitting for
  Christians. That points to trade near the shrines. Sermons may also
  have complained of noise and distraction, but this record has not
  checked that. The market-and-crowd pattern is attested only generally,
  not by any one dedicated account of a single festival, and no more
  precision than that is claimed here. The calendar itself - a fixed feast
  day, kept year after year - is documented from festal texts naming
  specific dates. Gregory's account of the Mamas festival is named but
  not checked here.
