plain_meaning: 'God''s own being is beyond every mind''s reach. We held that together with a second
  truth: God is really known through what he does, not through knowing his hidden essence. Neither claim
  canceled the other.'
world_word: akatalēpsia
false_friend:

senses:
  informational: 'Forged in the fight against Eunomius, who claimed the single word ''unbegotten'' delivers
    God''s own essence -- that God could be known exactly as God knows himself. This world''s answer built
    a whole way of speaking about God: we know God from his workings, not his hidden being; every word
    reaches truly and grasps nothing complete.'
  evidential: Documented as the era's argued position across Basil's and both Gregories' anti-Eunomian
    writings; the deeper mystical elaboration -- knowing God as an endless entry into darkness -- is chiefly
    Gregory of Nyssa's own development (his Life of Moses), not the whole circle's common property. Life
    of Moses itself is a genuinely unfilled gap in this build's acquired texts -- named, not smoothed
    past.
  personal: This was not caution dressed up as piety. The same authors who confessed God beyond every
    word fought to the edge of exile over a single preposition -- proof that 'we cannot fully know God'
    and 'this word matters enormously' were, for them, one and the same conviction.
  translational: '''Doesn''t this just mean nobody can really know anything about God?'' -- no: this world
    confessed exactly what was given and refused exactly what was not, with the mind fully used and finally
    surpassed. It was safety for worship, not fog for the intellect.'
quick_meaning: The truth that no mind can fully grasp God, though God is truly known in what he has shown.
