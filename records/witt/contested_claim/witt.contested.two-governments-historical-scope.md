claim: >-
  "The two kingdoms doctrine" names one coherent, later-systematized teaching that spans the founder's
  whole career and both of the pairings this library's texts call by similar English words: the spiritual
  and secular governments of Secular Authority and the Eight Wittenberg Sermons, and the Christ-against-Satan
  "two kingdoms" of Bondage of the Will - one doctrine, applied broadly, under one name.
held_against:
- >-
  The founder's own two pairings are not one term in his own usage. "Two classes," "two governments," and
  "the kingdom of God... the kingdom of the world" name the church/civil pairing in Secular Authority (v3
  12139-12141, 12318-12323); a differently built pairing, Christ against Satan, names cosmic allegiance in
  Bondage of the Will - "there are two kingdoms in the world mutually militating against each other... Satan
  reigns in the one... in the other kingdom Christ reigns" (Co 14747-14759). Nothing in this library's
  vendored text calls both pairings by one name or states that they are the same doctrine viewed twice.
- >-
  The label that does treat them as one is not this world's own word for either. Even the running marginal
  heading printed over the Secular Authority passage, "The Two Kingdoms," is the 1930 editor's addition (v3
  12123-12127), not the founder's; the confessional voice states the church/civil distinction under its own
  different name, "the power of the Church and the power of the sword" (AC 1281-1285), and elsewhere names a
  related but separately worded standing topic, "the kingdom of Christ, or the spiritual kingdom and
  political affairs" (Ap 6931-6932) - a third phrasing, not a citation of either of the founder's own two.
- >-
  The scholarly debate this contest actually rests on is unrowed in the Source Registry. Doc_06 names two
  leads for a Registry row - Cargill Thompson's 1969 article on "problems of Luther's Zwei-Reiche-Lehre"
  (a title that is, in Doc_06's own words, "the contest itself") and Wright's 2010 study - but cites neither
  as a source (Doc_06 SS2.2). The label's own further, and arguably sharper, complication - its contested
  twentieth-century Zweireichelehre appropriation - is named by Doc_06 as genuinely live in the field but is
  not demonstrated from anything this library holds. The historical-scope contest is real; this library's
  own basis for it is the in-text difference between two pairings, not a read secondary argument about
  either pairing's later career.
- >-
  The doctrine's most consequential tests lie entirely outside the library. The gravity this claim concerns
  is classified Supporting, not Primary, and the reason stated for that classification is a coverage gap,
  not doubt about the doctrine's content: "1525 and 1555 lie outside every vendored text" (Doc_04 SS6.3),
  the two moments - popular insurrection and the doctrine's eventual legal settlement - at which a single,
  stable "two kingdoms doctrine" would have been tested hardest against events. Whether the founder held the
  church/civil and Christ/Satan pairings apart consistently across a whole career, or whether a later system
  reads a settled architecture into usage this library shows only piecemeal and only through 1531, is a
  question this library cannot adjudicate either way; it can show only that the founder's own vendored words
  are not, on their face, one doctrine under one name.
concedes: >-
  Not contested: that the founder taught a real, load-bearing distinction between the spiritual rule of the
  Word and the secular rule of the sword, stated repeatedly across both voices (Secular Authority, the Eight
  Wittenberg Sermons, the Augsburg Confession, the Apology); that a second, differently built "two kingdoms"
  pairing of Christ against Satan also occurs, in Bondage of the Will; and that both pairings use similar
  English vocabulary ("kingdom(s)," "govern-") in this library's translations. Documented, all of it. What is
  not settled from this library: whether "the two kingdoms doctrine" names one coherent, later-systematized
  teaching that legitimately spans both pairings and the founder's whole career, or whether that is a modern
  retrospective architecture placed over usage this library shows only in pieces, undocumented at its two
  most consequential historical tests. Contested, and left unresolved here on purpose.
