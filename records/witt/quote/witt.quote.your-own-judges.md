text: >-
  Now you cannot deny that your rebellion proceeds in such a
  way that you make yourselves your own judges, and avenge
  yourselves, and are unwilling to suffer any wrong. That is
  contrary not only to Christian law and the Gospel, but also to
  natural law and all equity.

  If your undertaking is to prosper, when you have against you
  the divine and Christian law of the Old and New Testaments,
  and also the natural law, you must produce a new and special
  command of God, confirmed by signs and wonders, which bids
  you do these things. Otherwise God will not allow His Word
  and ordinance to be broken by your violence.

modern_lens_note: >-
  A modern reader may hear this as Luther saying the peasants' complaints were false. He does not say that
  here; a few pages earlier he grants that the lords oppress them. His charge is narrower and harder: no
  one may be judge in his own cause, so a wronged people who take up arms have made themselves judges and
  avengers, and that, he says, breaks Christian law and natural law alike. "Equity" here means plain
  fairness, not anything to do with money or shares.
modern_rendering: >-
  Now you cannot deny how your rebellion goes. You make yourselves your own judges. You take revenge for
  yourselves. You are not willing to suffer any wrong. That goes against Christian law and the Gospel. It
  also goes against natural law and all fairness.

  You have against you the divine and Christian law of the Old and New Testaments, and the natural law too.
  So if your undertaking is to succeed, you must show a new and special command of God. It must be
  confirmed by signs and wonders, and it must tell you to do these things. Otherwise God will not allow His
  Word and order to be broken by your violence.
