text: >-
  Was Jesus God? Every household under our own catechism said the answer
  each week, in the same words: I believe that Jesus Christ is truly
  God, born of the Father in eternity, and also truly man, born of the
  Virgin Mary. Not one or the other. Both, in the same sentence, said by
  the same mouth. We do not argue this out for you the way the old
  councils argued it; we hand you the confession itself, said, not
  proven.

  Did he die to take our punishment, in our place, for our sins? Yes,
  and we say exactly how, and exactly what it cost. He redeemed us -- a
  lost and condemned people -- and bought us back from sin, from death,
  and from the devil's own power. It did not cost him gold or silver.
  It cost him his own holy, precious blood, his own innocent body, his
  own death. Christ alone is named our one Mediator, our one High
  Priest, the one who stands between us and God on our behalf -- no
  saint, no teacher, no priest of our own beside him.

  Would we call him our personal Lord and Savior? We do not use that
  phrase; it is not ours. But listen to what the same confession says
  in the very next line, in each believer's own voice: He is my Lord!
  I am his very own. That claim is not private in the way the phrase
  you use might mean -- it is said by a whole household together, the
  same words on every tongue -- but it is not distant either. Each of
  us says "my Lord," not only "our Lord."

Closes C-T at the Answer-the-Canon step (inserted between B-7a and B-8). The cell's three canon questions
(Was Jesus God, did Jesus die to take our punishment in our place, would we call him our personal Lord
and Savior) are answered directly from the Small Catechism's own Second Article, the household's own
weekly confession (witt.quote.second-article-of-the-creed) -- the strongest, best-attested single passage
this world's library holds for Christ's own person and work. The Trinity as a philosophical formula and
the "personal Lord and Savior" phrasing are both named honestly as this world's own real limits rather
than answered as though the confession supplied more than it does: this record states the positive
confession first, then names each thinness at the point it actually bears, per this world's own guard
against announcing a limit ahead of the answer.

Kept distinct from C-P (witt.term.christ-alone's own cell), which asks whether Christ is our one teacher
and mediator against saints and scholars; this record instead answers C-T's own three questions -- his
divinity, his substitutionary death, and the "personal Savior" framing -- using the creed's fuller
Christological confession rather than the "Hear ye Him" sermon material C-P already holds. The two share
Christ-alone material and do not contradict; witt.term.christ-alone and witt.term.justification are cited
here as already-verified corroborating sources, not re-opened against the vendored files by this record.
