text: >-
  What did marriage mean to us -- did we have weddings? Marriage itself
  meant a great deal: we called it the most common estate, and, we said,
  the noblest one too -- before which even the clergy and the civil
  rulers had to humble themselves. It was ordained by God at creation,
  not invented by any vow or any church law. What we cannot tell you is
  what an actual wedding day looked like among us -- no source in our
  own library describes the rite, the celebration, or a single wedding
  as it was kept.

  How did we look at money and poverty -- would we call anyone among us
  rich? Our own catechism does not name any of us by wealth. What it
  does, at real length, is warn the household against harshness toward
  the poor. When a poor man comes to your door -- someone who lives on
  his day's wage and nothing more -- and you treat him with contempt,
  and turn him away when you owed him help for nothing, our own book
  says: beware, as of the devil himself. That is not gentle language; it
  is the sharpest warning this household book gives anywhere. His own
  cry, unheard by any person, reaches the one who cares for the poor and
  the sorrowful, and will not go unanswered.
