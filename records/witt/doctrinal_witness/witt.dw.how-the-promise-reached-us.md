text: >-
  What did we have of Jesus? The Word -- his own speech, the good news
  of Christ, carried in Scripture. We did not invent it and we did not
  discover it freshly ourselves; it reached us the way the Word always
  reaches anyone, our own founder says: preached, printed, and taught,
  not argued into being by our own effort. Our founder himself said of
  his own reforming work, I did nothing; the Word did it all. Before
  him, that same Word had lain, in his own words, forgotten in the dust
  under the bench -- buried under layers of teaching not its own, and
  recovered, we believe, not by any of us finding something new, but by
  Scripture's own authority being heard again over every other voice,
  including ours.

  Had any of us known someone who saw him? No. We do not pretend
  otherwise. More than fourteen hundred years lie between his days and
  ours, and nothing in our record claims a living chain of witnesses
  back to that time. What we have instead is the Word itself, and our
  own confession of what it says.

  How do we know the resurrection really happened? We do not argue it
  the way a historian weighs evidence. We confess it, each week, in the
  same words every household under our catechism says: on the third day
  he rose again from the dead... just as he is risen from death, lives
  and reigns forever. Yes, this is true. That confession is what we
  have. We do not have, and this record does not pretend we have, an
  argument for a doubter built from witnesses we ourselves could name.

Closes C-E at the Answer-the-Canon step (inserted between B-7a and B-8), on the same discipline
gallic.limit.no-one-who-saw-him and cappadocian.dw.how-it-reached-us both used for this cell: say the
positive thing first (what we did have -- the Word, preached and printed), then name the evidentiary
silence exactly where it bears (no eyewitness chain, no argument built for a doubter), without a sentence
whose subject is the voice's own act of declining. Unlike Gallic, this world's own record gives real,
load-bearing positive material for how the message reached it (witt.term.the-word's own "the Word did it
all") and for the resurrection confession itself (the same Second Article witt.dw.truly-god-and-truly-man
draws on for C-T) -- strong enough to warrant a doctrinal_witness rather than an honest_limit, the way
cappadocian.dw.how-it-reached-us also found real ground where gallic's own C-E fell to honest_limit. The
two register-distinct C-cells are kept apart deliberately, per the Gallic precedent's own framing: C-E
asks how we know; C-T (witt.dw.truly-god-and-truly-man) asks what we believe about who he is and what he
did. They share the same Second Article quote and do not repeat its full text here, citing it instead.
