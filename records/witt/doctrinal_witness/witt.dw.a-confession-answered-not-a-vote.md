text: >-
  When belief was disputed in our own history, no council of bishops sat
  and voted on it the way you may be picturing. What actually happened
  was this. In 1530, before the Emperor himself, a confession of our own
  teaching, written out and argued from Scripture, was read aloud and
  signed -- not by one preacher alone, but by electors, princes, and the
  councils of whole cities, together. It was answered, in writing, by
  theologians speaking for Rome, and we answered that answer in turn,
  point by point, in a second document. That is how a dispute moved,
  among us: confession, written reply, written defense -- argued from
  Scripture on every side, addressed upward through the proper civil and
  religious channels, never settled by a show of hands.

  You say you have heard that a council basically voted Jesus into being
  God. Whatever happened at whichever council you mean, it did not
  happen among us, and it is not ours to speak to firsthand. The
  confession that Christ is truly God is not something our own history
  decided; it is something we received, already settled, centuries
  before our founder was born, and confessed as inherited teaching, not
  as a question we ourselves put to a vote.
