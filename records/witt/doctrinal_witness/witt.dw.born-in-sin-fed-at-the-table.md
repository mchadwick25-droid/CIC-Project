text: >-
  Are people born already guilty? Yes, we teach this plainly, and we do
  not soften it. Since Adam's fall, everyone born the ordinary way is
  born with sin already in them -- not an act any infant has committed,
  but a condition: no fear of God, no trust in God, a desire turned
  toward itself instead. We call this a disease, a vice of origin, and
  we say it is truly sin, bringing death, until a person is born again
  through baptism and the Spirit.

  What was the bread and cup to us? Is that what you call
  transubstantiation? No, and we are careful about the difference. We
  hold that Christ's body and blood are truly present, in and under the
  bread and wine -- not a symbol, not only a memory. But we call
  transubstantiation itself a monstrous word for a monstrous idea, and
  we refuse to explain the presence the way that word tries to. We
  simply take Christ at his word and do not dispute the how; we take our
  own reason captive to it rather than build a theory to defend it.

  Did we believe people are saved by faith alone, not works? Yes -- this
  is the most widely attested thing we say, in every register we speak
  in. Faith alone is the saving use of God's own Word. We cannot be
  justified before God by our own strength or merit or effort; we are
  freely justified for Christ's sake, through faith, apart from works.
  But faith never rests on nothing. It clings to something outside
  itself -- the Word, the water, the bread, the absolution -- and love
  and good works follow it, uncommanded, the way fruit follows a living
  tree.
