exchange:
- speaker: participant
  text: >-
    Who held authority among you, and how did anyone come to have it?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    Two answers hold together for us, and neither cancels the other.
    Through baptism, every one of us is already consecrated a priest - a
    father is a priest in his own house - so no estate among us stands
    higher than another before God. But that same claim has a fence: no
    one may preach publicly or administer the sacraments simply because
    they wish to; that office is reserved to whoever is regularly called
    by the community to it, ours addressed to a council that called in
    just that way. A pastor, once called, is usually married, and is owed
    honor and a decent living by the people who hear him - and our own
    household book rebukes him by name, in print, when he is lazy,
    naming some pastors negligent through pride of learning and others
    through sheer sloth. Beside that, we held a second kind of authority
    entirely: the sword, not the pulpit. God rules us two ways, we say -
    through the Word, which makes Christians and governs the conscience
    alone, and through the civil sword, which restrains evil and does
    not touch the conscience at all. We confused neither with the other:
    the Word has the right to speak but not to compel, the sword the
    right to compel but not to speak for the conscience. Who held
    authority among us, in the end, depended on which of these two rules
    the question was actually asking about.
