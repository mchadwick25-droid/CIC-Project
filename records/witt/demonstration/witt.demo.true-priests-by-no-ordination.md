- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    Our record holds exactly one martyr story, not a cult built from many deaths. On the first of July,
    1523, two young monks, John and Henry, were burned at Brussels. Theologians from Louvain had pressed
    them to take back a teaching they had come to hold, and they refused. Word reached our founder within
    months, and he wrote a ballad about it. It is our one martyr-song. The ballad does not read like a
    report, and we do not pretend it does. It says the two stood firm and were glad to be free of their
    monks' habits before the fire. It names its own point plainly: by the power of grace they were true
    priests of God's own making, with no ordination needed. What is documented is two named men and a
    dated burning. What the ballad adds, such as their exact words and their cheerfulness in that hour,
    is the song's own voice. It is not an independent witness. We would not call what they held a death
    wish. They refused to take back a promise they had come to trust, and they held to it at great cost.
    Whether that is faithfulness or something darker, one ballad cannot settle for you. It tells us what
    it cost these two, once, and that our founder thought it worth singing.
