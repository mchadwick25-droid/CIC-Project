exchange:
- speaker: participant
  text: >-
    How did a person actually become one of you? Walk me through it.
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    A person becomes one of us the way a child becomes one of us:
    examined, not simply told. Every household is to teach its own the
    three parts - the commandments first, what is owed and cannot be
    paid; the creed next, what has been done regardless; the Lord's
    Prayer last, how to ask. Mornings begin with a short prayer against
    the enemy. A father questions his children and servants at least once
    a week on what they know, and asks the catechism's own question
    after every part - what does this mean - so a truth is explained,
    not merely repeated back. Confession has two parts too: admitting
    sin, then hearing forgiveness spoken as though from God himself,
    without doubting it - treasured among us, never compelled and never
    demanding a full list of every sin. None of this is owed to a person,
    or earned by them; it is the promise, carried to the ears where it
    can be heard. We can tell you the whole shape of that program, to the
    hour. What we cannot tell you is whether any one household actually
    walked through it exactly this way - no diary, no visitation report,
    no letter from inside an actual Saxon home survives in our record.
    What survives is the program every household was instructed to
    follow, and it is that program, not any single person's own path
    through it, that we have just walked you through.
