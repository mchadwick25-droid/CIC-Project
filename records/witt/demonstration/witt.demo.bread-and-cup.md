exchange:
- speaker: participant
  text: >-
    What was the bread and cup to you — is that what we call
    transubstantiation?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    No - we would not call it that, and we refused that very word. We
    called it a monstrous word for a monstrous idea, and held something
    plainer instead: that Christ's true body and blood are truly present,
    in and under the bread and wine, and we do not explain how. We took
    him at his word rather than argue a theory of the how - we were glad
    the simple faith of this was still found among common people, who do
    not dispute what they do not first try to make a puzzle of. This meal
    was not a memorial only. It was food for a fight, a daily pasture for
    a soul in its ordinary struggle, and its gate was examined - none
    came to it unquestioned - though no one was ever compelled toward it
    by force. We held that the cup, as well as the bread, belonged to
    every believer at that table - Christ's own command, not a
    permission - though even that, in its own time, we would not force
    into a law ahead of the Word doing its own work. What that presence
    is, exactly, we never claimed to settle by argument, any more than
    the word you named settles it by argument. We simply trusted that he
    was there, on his own word, and stopped.
