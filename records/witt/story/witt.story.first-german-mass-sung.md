text: >-
  This is what Johann Walter, Luther's own collaborator in music, remembered many years later, writing
  in his old age, of the work behind the first German Mass at Wittenberg.

  Luther had written to the Elector of Saxony and to Duke Johannsen, Walter recalled, asking them to send
  him and the old musician Conrad Rupff to Wittenberg. They were to help decide how the church's tones
  should be set. Luther chose the Eighth Tone for the Epistle and the Sixth for the Gospel. "Our Lord
  Christ is a good Friend," he said, "and his words are full of love; so we will take the Sixth Tone for
  the Gospel." He set the notes himself over the Epistles, the Gospels and the Words of Institution, and
  sang them over for Walter's judgment. He "kept me three weeks long at Wittenberg," Walter wrote, to
  write out the notes over some of the Gospels and Epistles. "I must needs stay to hear it," Walter wrote,
  "and take with me a copy of the Mass to Torgau and present it to His Grace the Elector from Doctor Luther."
  That was the day "the first German Mass was sung in the parish church."

  Walter remembered more besides the Mass itself. Luther gave orders to restore Vespers where it had fallen
  out of use, with short choral hymns for the students. The charity-scholars, who collected bread from
  door to door, were to sing Latin hymns, anthems and responses suited to the season. Luther was not content
  that the scholars sing only German songs in the streets. Walter's letter goes on: "The most profitable
  songs for the common multitude are the plain psalms and hymns ...," while the Latin songs "are useful
  for the learned and for students."

  Walter's letter was written, by his own account, in his old age, about events some forty years past.
  It reaches us only because a later editor, Bacon, quoted it from an earlier printing by Rambach, which
  had taken it from Praetorius. It is the one account of Wittenberg's worship we have from someone other
  than Luther himself.

  - filtered through three later hands before it reached this library. It is genuine testimony, but it
  is memory, not minutes.
