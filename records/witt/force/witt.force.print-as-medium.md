description: 'The history of print here is documented almost entirely in the editors'' notes, not in the primary texts. The
  Theses appeared in three editions within two months, at Wittenberg, Nurnberg, and as far away as Basel. The
  Papacy at Rome appeared in twelve known quarto editions, with the printers named. Karsthans ran to ten editions.
  The Grunenberg press reached Duke George within days. Print was considered as a gravity in its own right, and
  set aside. It carried a specific risk: it might reflect the builders'' own view of what mattered more than the
  world''s. It is carried here as a force instead. It is an initiating force in the strict sense. No text in this
  library reaches a reader except through print. And the world''s first act was a printed disputation.

  The world does not name the press. It names what the press does, and it fears it. The medium is the way "the
  divine truth" reaches "us simple laymen." That is the fictional peasant''s demand for German (v3 10556-10558).
  It is also the way hymns bring "the blessed Gospel" "into full play" (Hy 909-912). But already in 1539 the
  founder wishes his own printed books "forgotten and destroyed." He wishes it so that "the Bible would have kept
  its place in the pulpit" (v1 260-261, 329-330). He has his own image of what books do to the Book: "the Bible
  has come to lie forgotten in the dust under the bench" (v1 268-269).

  Together with the laity''s own need for assurance, print gave rise to the gravity of German for the people: "the
  medium and the audience." Print is "the thing printed" for the authority of the Word. It is "the printed
  household object" for the household catechism. It made the printed quarto and the hymn-sheet the physical base
  of this world''s body of sources. Its later turn against the world is a separate, internal force, print turned
  inward. That is because by 1529-43 the pressure comes from the world''s own output.'
manifestations:
- the Theses in three editions within two months -- Wittenberg, Nürnberg, and Basel (v1 488-491)
- The Papacy at Rome's "twelve known editions are all quartos," printers named (v1 12304-12309)
- '"Dear Luther, write the divine truth in our language, in German, that we simple laymen also may read
  it" (Karsthans, v3 10556-10558)'
- '"I would gladly have seen all my books forgotten and destroyed" (v1 260-261); "the Bible has come to
  lie forgotten in the dust under the bench" (v1 268-269)'
