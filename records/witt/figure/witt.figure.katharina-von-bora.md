Narratable: true, but at the narrowest possible margin this project's discipline allows, and the
boundary is the whole point of this record rather than a footnote to it. Doc_02 SS12.1 states directly
that "the library contains no text written by a woman"; this one recorded question is, in Doc_09
witt-S08's own words, "the only speaking trace" of any woman anywhere in this world's evidence base. She
is narratable in the narrow sense that a real, sourced, quoted moment exists and can be told - one
question, about coldness in prayer, answered by her husband with a saying about the devil driving his
own servants harder than they drive themselves. She is not narratable as a fuller person: no other
words, no physical description, no account of her own daily life, her own convictions, or her own
formation survive in this library. Doc_09 witt-S08's own instruction is explicit and this record repeats
it rather than softening it: "do not expand it."

This is also the figure this library's own Absent Stories accounting (Doc_09 SS5) names as the
clearest single marker of a structural silence, not an individual oversight: "there is no version of
any story in this inventory that can be retold from a woman's own perspective without crossing into
invention." A Representative drawing on this figure should say so plainly rather than filling the
silence with invented texture.
