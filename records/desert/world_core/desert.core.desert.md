horizon: "Egypt (the Nile valley and the desert's edge), c. 320s-c. 430 CE: the desert monastic formation ecology anchored on withdrawal as the whole work of formation, not merely its precondition. Three coexisting patterns - solitary anchorites following Antony's own model, a cenobitic federation under Pachomius's written rule in the Thebaid, and semi-anchoritic settlements at Nitria, Kellia, and Scetis - genuinely distinct in daily practice and in how authority is held, yet unified by a shared struggle against tempting thoughts, manual labor as concurrent discipline, elder-mediated correction, and discernment as the skill that calibrates how much of any of it a given person can bear."
formation_logic: "Formation as martyrdom's invented successor: with the persecutor gone, the demons of the interior life became the total confrontation formation now organizes around. Withdrawal from ordinary village life is a lifelong deepening, not a single decisive act. Once withdrawn, a person is formed through sustained vigilance against tempting thoughts, manual labor undertaken both to live and as discipline against idleness in its own right, and discernment - the skill that decides how much fasting, how much solitude, how much combat a particular person can safely carry, since there is no fixed syllabus here and what one elder prescribes for one disciple can be actively wrong for another. Meaning travels chiefly through the terse saying, given by an elder in answer to one disciple's one situation, and through apprenticeship - shared labor, physical proximity, observed example - rather than through formal curriculum. Authority is earned through recognized discernment and personal relationship in the solitary and semi-solitary patterns; in the cenobitic pattern a written rule and appointed offices govern the same ground, built using the personal model's own legitimating language even as it also formalizes what that model leaves informal - the one tension this ecology never resolves within its own span."
thinness: "Richest in the daily discipline of withdrawal, interior struggle, and discernment - this is where the surviving sayings and the founding narrative converge, and it shows in how specifically this material can speak about it. Genuinely strong, independently, on the material and economic side: excavated settlement remains and ordinary business letters corroborate a working, trade-connected community life running alongside the withdrawal rhetoric, not merely asserting it. Thin to silent: specific liturgical content beyond the Psalter and the Lord's Prayer; a woman's own extended, first-person account on the scale of the founding narratives (named women's sayings survive; a comparable narrative about a woman does not); a documentary Melitian community's own voice, as opposed to evidence of its ordinary business; whether the biblical wilderness-and-exile pattern was actually read into this life by its own participants, as against being a plausible later assumption; and any single preserved scene showing a person navigating personal-versus-institutional authority directly, where the tension is otherwise visible only structurally, never dramatized."
cautions: "1) SINGLE-VOICE CONCENTRATION is the central limit on two of this material's richest threads: the founding portrait rests overwhelmingly on one author's construction of one man, and the most systematic interior psychology here rests on one unusually educated participant's own writing rather than the wider movement's own vocabulary - state this plainly, never let either stand in for the whole. 2) COMPILER MEDIATION: the terse sayings carrying most of this world's own teaching survive because later editors, working after this ecology had substantially closed, chose to collect and arrange them by elder name - not because participants themselves were building an archive. Any claim about how a saying was actually delivered, to whom, or how often a pattern recurred is filtered through that later arrangement, not a transcript of it. 3) THE FOUNDING FIGURE'S OWN LEARNING remains a live, unresolved question - whether he was as unschooled as the account closest to him portrays, or more philosophically formed than that portrait allows, is not settled either way, and nothing here depends on settling it. 4) A RIVAL, ECCLESIASTICALLY DISTINCT ASCETIC MOVEMENT existed in the same region and period, close enough in organization that its own documentary evidence has been used as material evidence for parts of this ecology's economic life - whether an ordinary participant would have experienced crossing between the two as a real boundary or a minor administrative fact is not established. 5) THE THREE PATTERNS' OWN POROUSNESS is likewise open: whether participants experienced them as overlapping and porous, or whether that porousness is mostly a later editorial artifact of how the sayings were gathered into one shared corpus, is not resolved. 6) OUT-OF-HORIZON TRAP: a controversy that swept up the systematizing teacher's own reputation, and a later formal condemnation of his teaching, both fall at or past this ecology's own closing edge and decide nothing about his standing within this material's own span. 7) A NEIGHBORING FORMATION WORLD'S RELATIONSHIP to this one is held open rather than assumed either way - nothing here rests on this ecology being an extension of that world's own tradition, nor on the two being unrelated."

ABSENT STORIES (carried forward from the prior build's cleared Doc_09a
SS5, restated here in this record's own words rather than quoted, since
this build's own Doc_09 equivalent has not yet been built): the stories
this material conspicuously does not have, and why. (1) No named
woman's own extended first-person narrative, comparable to the founding
account centered on the movement's paradigmatic solitary figure - named
women's sayings survive; a comparable narrative does not, not because
women's ascetic practice was absent (the cenobitic federation included
women's houses from an early stage) but because the surviving literary
record was overwhelmingly produced by and about men. (2) No Melitian
ascetic's own first-person account - only documentary evidence of that
community's ordinary business survives, and the silence is itself
informative about whose literary output this world's own dominant,
Nicene-communion-adjacent sources chose to preserve. (3) No single
preserved narrative scene showing a participant directly navigating the
tension between personal and institutional authority - the tension is
well-evidenced structurally (see desert.gravity.authority-tension) but
not narratively dramatized in any source identified so far. These
absences are structural (who could write, and what got kept, not what
happened), are data for the thinness and cautions above, and are never
to be filled by invention.
