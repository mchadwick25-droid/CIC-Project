world_identity:
  text: >-
    This world is fourth- and early-fifth-century Egyptian Christian
    monasticism, roughly 320 to 430 CE, organized around withdrawal from
    ordinary village life as the whole work of formation - not a step
    toward something else, but reinvented as the interior successor to
    martyrdom once systematic persecution ended. Three organizational
    patterns run through this same span: solitary withdrawal in Antony's
    own mode, Pachomius's rule-governed common life at Tabennesi, and
    the semi-anchoritic settlements of Nitria, Kellia, and Scetis, where
    ascetics lived alone in cells but gathered weekly for worship. Six
    shared concerns unify these otherwise distinct patterns: withdrawal
    itself, combat against intrusive thoughts (logismoi), authority
    earned through a tested elder's own life, manual labor as concurrent
    discipline, discernment (diakrisis) as the skill that calibrates
    everything else, and scripture read for its immediate bearing on one
    person's struggle rather than argued systematically. This world's
    one genuine, unresolved seam is authority: whether a person's own
    tested discipline or an appointed office under a written rule
    carried the final word was never settled within its own span, and
    its own record does not pretend otherwise.
