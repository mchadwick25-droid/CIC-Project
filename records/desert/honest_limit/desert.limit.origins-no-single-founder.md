statement: >
  You ask who started us. No single man did, and the tellings we have do not agree. Antony is the one
  we remember best, and many call him the first. Jerome wrote about this from the Syrian desert. He
  says some reach back too far, to Elijah and John. He calls the view for Antony partly true. Antony
  did not so much come first, he says, as give the others their stimulus. Two of Antony's disciples,
  Amathas and Macarius, named Paul of Thebes as the leader. Jerome says that opinion has his approval.
  An elder named Piamun, in Cassian's book, says the shared life began with the believers at
  Jerusalem, who held all things in common. He says it ran on unbroken to the time of Paul and Antony,
  and that the solitaries came out of it. For life together under a written rule, we tell of Pachomius
  and the angel with the tablet. We cannot say which telling is right. None was written by someone
  who was there at the start.
why_sources_cannot_answer: "Searched: every desert record for 'Paul of Thebes', 'Paulus', 'first hermit' and 'founder'; the vendored Jerome, Life of Paulus, prologue, and Cassian, Conference XVIII, chapters V and VI, read in the file. Found: three accounts that differ. Jerome reports the dispute over who was first, calls the Antony view partly true (Antony gave the others 'the necessary stimulus'), says the Elijah-and-John view goes back too far, and sides with Amathas and Macarius for Paul ('this opinion has my approval also'); Cassian, through Piamun, puts the shared life in the apostles' day, says it continued unbroken up to Paul and Antony (Conference XVIII.5, npnf211 lines 42536-42539), and traces the anchorites to those two. Pachomius's founding of the Tabennesi houses is told in the Lausiac History and the Pachomian Lives, as desert.story.pachomius-founding carries it. Not searched: the Historia Monachorum and Rufinus's translation of it, which may carry other notices. None of the tellings is by someone present at the beginning: the Vita of Antony is by an outside bishop, Jerome wrote from Syria, Cassian wrote in Gaul decades later, and Palladius was a bishop. The record states the disagreement and names no founder."
