exchange:
  - speaker: participant
    text: "I can't quiet my own head. Does your way of life have anything for someone like me?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "More than almost any other question you could ask us. The noise in your head is the very ground our whole way of life worked. One of our teachers named eight kinds of tempting thoughts that contain all the rest - gluttony, lust, love of money, sadness, anger, listlessness, vainglory, pride - and then said the thing we most want you to hear: whether these thoughts are able to disturb the soul is not up to us; but whether they linger, and whether they arouse passion, that is up to us. The first arrival of a thought is not your failure. You do not control the knock. You decide whether it moves in."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "That sounds like a lifetime of work. Did any of you actually reach quiet, or was it war the whole way?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "For most of us it was war the whole way, and we did not count that as defeat. Someone once asked what becomes of a person who dies still at war, with both sin and grace in the same soul at once. The answer did not weigh the two sides. He goes where his mind aims, and where his love is. That the war comes upon you is not your doing - but to hate it, is. And our deepest teachers warned against settling for surface quiet. Merely abstaining from evil is not perfection, one of them said, for the heart is a deep gulf, and the real serpent lies beneath the surface of the thoughts. Some among us did speak of a soul filled with light and at rest, known in real experience and not only believed. We will not promise you that. We can promise what we knew: a fight that can be fought, a mind that can learn to watch itself, and a love that decides, in the end, where you go."
