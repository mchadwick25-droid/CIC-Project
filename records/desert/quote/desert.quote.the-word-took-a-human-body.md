The single fullest Christological statement this world's own corpus puts in a desert
participant's own mouth. It matters for three reasons. It is an argument, not a
gesture - Antony is answering a question and giving a reason. It states a purpose for
the incarnation ("for the salvation and well-being of man") and a mechanism for it
(shared human birth, so that man might share the divine nature), which is more than
desert.limit.doubt-and-doctrine's own bound had allowed for. And it comes from SS72-80,
the disputation with the philosophers - a division this corpus had drawn on for
desert.dw.only-true-religion's boundary-claim without ever opening its Christological
content.

The Vita's own editor flags the oddity directly at this point ("It is certainly
startling to find Antony, ignorant of Greek and of letters, reasoning with philosophers
upon the doctrines of Neoplatonism"), and the argument's own vocabulary tracks
Athanasius's de Incarnatione closely. That is exactly why the attribution here is
Contested and the divergence_note says so: what is certain is that this is what the
tradition put in Antony's mouth, not that Antony said it.
