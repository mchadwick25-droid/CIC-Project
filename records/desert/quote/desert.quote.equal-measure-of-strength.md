modern_lens_note: >-
  "Strength of will" is not a claim about equality in any modern sense; the sentence's own logic is
  that women have no EXCUSE - the gift removes an alibi rather than conferring a standing. And
  "necessary for us to remember also" concedes, in the same breath, that they were about to be
  forgotten: the women arrive at chapter thirty-five, after thirty-four chapters of men, in a
  section that has to argue for its own existence. Both halves of that are the evidence. A modern
  reader who takes only the generous half, or only the grudging half, has taken half a sentence.
