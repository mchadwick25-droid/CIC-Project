claim: "The three organizational patterns of desert monastic life (solitary/anchoritic; cenobitic under a written rule; semi-anchoritic settlements gathering weekly) mark real, lived organizational and authority differences that participants themselves experienced as boundaries - not a later editorial artifact of how the sayings tradition's compilers arranged inherited material into one corpus, and not a complete typology: the Nepheros archive's own documentary community fits cleanly into none of the three."
held_against:
- "Amoun founded Kellia (the semi-anchoritic pattern) on Antony's own advice - a direct personal link between the two patterns' most prominent figures, suggesting a participant could move between what this claim calls distinct patterns without necessarily experiencing that move as a boundary crossing"
- "The sayings tradition's own compiled structure draws material from elders associated with all three patterns into a single collected tradition, arranged by elder rather than by pattern - later compilers, at least, treated the material as belonging to one shared teaching corpus regardless of which pattern an elder belonged to"
- "The Nepheros archive's own editors describe its Melitian community as organizationally 'intermediary' - between the cenobitic pattern's full common life and the solitary and semi-solitary patterns' looser gathering - a genuinely different organizational pattern the three-pattern typology does not clearly place, distinct from the separate question of whether a Melitian community's practice differed from the Nicene-communion patterns otherwise centered here"
concedes: "Independent testing of how widely each named organizing practice actually held found most of them genuinely present across all three patterns, confirmed through evidence of independent origin rather than assumed - a result consistent with real porousness between the patterns, but it does not by itself resolve whether that porousness reflects participants' own lived experience or a later retrospective arrangement. The three-pattern finding itself is held as settled working structure and is not reopened here. What remains genuinely open is narrower: how sharply participants themselves would have felt the patterns' edges day to day, and whether the Nepheros community represents a genuine fourth organizational pattern, a Melitian variant of an existing one, or is simply unclassifiable on the evidence currently available. Whether the Melitian archive tells us anything about Nicene-communion practice at all is a related but separate open question, not resolved here either."
