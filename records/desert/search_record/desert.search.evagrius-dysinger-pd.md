NOT A CLEARED ACQUISITION, and a weaker lead than Schodde. Recorded so
the absolute claim in the earlier record ('no public-domain English
translation of Evagrius's works was located, and this search knows of
none') does not stand unqualified, and so a human can judge the
dedication's terms. Fourteen records in this world rest on Evagrius and
none of them may quote him on the strength of this.

RE-EXAMINED 2026-08-27, on a file Mark supplied, and the answer is still
no. The file is a capture of one page of the Guide to Evagrius Ponticus
(ed. Joel Kalvesmaki, evagriusponticus.net, 2026 edition) carrying Luke
Dysinger's English Praktikos in full: prologue, 100 chapters, epilogue,
with scriptural references - about 7,200 words, cleanly structured and
immediately usable. That is exactly why the rights question has to be
answered rather than waved through.

WHAT THE CAPTURE DOES NOT CARRY. Its two links that would settle this,
"Credits" and "about this transcription", travelled as bare navigation
labels with no target and no text. There is no licence statement, no
copyright line, no dedication, and no mention of Tugwell anywhere in the
7,200 words.

WHAT SEARCH REPORTED, AND WHY IT DOES NOT CLOSE THE QUESTION. Two
independent searches report the Guide as a whole under CC BY 4.0, and
both also report the standard carve-out: rights in non-public-domain
material rest with the credited author or the cited publication's
rightsholder, everything else with Kalvesmaki. One search summary added
that the Guide includes "only those versions without license
restrictions". Those two statements are compatible and, read together,
suggest Dysinger permitted his translation to appear THERE - which is not
the same as a licence to redistribute it inside another corpus. A search
engine's paraphrase of a licence is not a licence, and "freely available"
is routinely rendered as "public domain" by summarisers.

THE TUGWELL PROBLEM IS UNCHANGED AND IS THE REAL BLOCKER. This record
already noted that Dysinger's dedication reportedly acknowledges a debt to
Simon Tugwell's 1987 translation, which is in copyright. A translator
cannot dedicate to the public domain what is derivative of someone else's
protected work. Nothing found today touches that, because the dedication
itself has still never been read.

RULING, and it matches the one already made against the Ward
Apophthegmata: not vendored. A text circulating freely on an institutional
site is not thereby licensed, and this corpus's premise is wording a
reader can re-check against a stated right to hold it. The extracted text
was left in session-temporary storage and was NOT written to cic/texts/.

WHAT WOULD CLEAR IT - two pages, either of which a person with working
network access can read in a minute, and neither of which this build can
reach: (1) evagriusponticus.net/about.htm, the Credits page, for the
licence in its own words; (2) the front matter of Dysinger's own
translation, for the dedication's actual terms and the scope of the
Tugwell acknowledgement. If those together show a CC BY or public-domain
dedication covering the translation itself, this becomes a clean
acquisition and fourteen records in this world stop being unable to quote
the author they rest on.

CLEARED 2026-08-27, later the same day, and not by the route this record
was looking for. Mark supplied the Guide's Credits page verbatim. It reads:
"This work is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
International (CC BY 4.0) License... The Guide to Evagrius Ponticus
includes, under Writings, many versions of Evagrius's writings, and only
those versions without license restrictions."

So the answer was neither expiry nor a translator's public-domain
dedication. It was an open licence, deliberately granted, plus the
editor's own assertion that everything he hosts is unrestricted. This
record asked whether Evagrius was in the PUBLIC DOMAIN by any route, and
the honest answer to that question is still no for Dysinger's English - a
translation is a derivative work and he holds copyright in it. The
question that unblocked the corpus was a different one: is it LICENSED.
Twice now the search has failed by asking about the wrong category of
right, and that is the lesson worth carrying to the next blocked source.

cic/texts/evagrius_praktikos_dysinger.txt is vendored under CC BY 4.0, the
first file in this corpus that is not public domain, with the attribution

The Tugwell question is NOT resolved and is recorded as open on the source
record. It was never verified in the first place - it came from
search-result metadata, and the dedication has never been read, this
build's network access to evagriusponticus.net being blocked throughout.
It does not block the acquisition: the operative permission is the
editor's grant, and a derivative-rights question underneath that grant is
the Guide's to answer, not a downstream reader's. It stays on the record
so that a person who can open the page knows to look.
