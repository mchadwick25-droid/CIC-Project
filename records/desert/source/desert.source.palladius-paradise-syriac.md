WHAT THIS FILE GIVES THIS WORLD THAT IT DID NOT HAVE.

1. THE WOMEN'S CHAPTERS. From chapter xxxv, Palladius turns to women and
   announces that he is doing so: "it is necessary for us to remember
   also the chosen and mighty women unto whom God also gave an equal
   measure of strength of will as unto men". What follows is about
   fifteen named women with chapters of their own, several carrying
   direct reported speech - Taor on why she needs no sandals, Piamon's
   prayer on the roof, Melania the Great facing down a governor.

   THIS NARROWS THIS WORLD'S WOMEN'S GAP AND DOES NOT CLOSE IT, and the
   distinction has to be kept exactly. What is here is a man's
   third-person narration of women's lives, with their speech reported
   inside it. That is a different evidential object from an amma saying
   in the Apophthegmata, and a very different one from a woman's own
   first-person account, which this world still does not have from
   anyone. What it does correct is the shape of the gap: this world had
   been saying that named women's sayings survive but a comparable
   NARRATIVE does not. Comparable narratives do survive - the men in
   this same book are narrated in the third person too, by the same
   author, at the same length. What does not survive is a woman's own
   voice at length, in her own person, for herself.

2. THE ABRIDGED SYRIAC VITA ANTONII. About 14,800 words against roughly
   32,000 in the Greek at npnf204, which this world also holds. This
   world's single most load-bearing biography is demonstrably variable
   between transmission traditions, and now it can show that rather than
   assert it. Registered here rather than on
   desert.source.athanasius-vita-antonii so that the Greek record keeps
   its own integrity; both point at each other.

3. BUDGE'S PROVENANCE ACCOUNT. His Preface says where the manuscript
   was, who showed it to him, when it was copied and what else it
   contained. That is how this world can say where its own newly
   vendored text comes from instead of asserting it. It is 1907
   editorial apparatus and never quotable as Palladius.

WHAT IT DOES NOT CONTAIN, stated because the earlier fetch note got this
wrong twice: NOT the Historia Monachorum / Asketikon, and NOT the
Pachomian Rule. Budge's Introduction summarises Books I and II; only
Book I's text is printed, ending "Here endeth the first Book". Chapter
xx is Palladius' biography of Pachomius, not his legislation - for that
see desert.source.pachomian-corpus and the Ethiopic recension vendored
the same day.
