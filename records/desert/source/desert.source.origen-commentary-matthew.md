OPENED ON A REVERSAL, recorded because the reversal is the useful part.

The sweep's first reading of this volume was that its Origen is
Commentary on John and a partial Matthew, neither on the controversy
issues, and that anything this world wanted from Origen on scripture was
better served by desert.source.origen-philocalia. That was nearly right
and was wrong about one chapter.

Matthew 18:15 - "if thy brother sin against thee, go, shew him his fault
between thee and him alone" - is the New Testament's procedure for
correcting a brother, and Origen works it at length: whether it applies
to all sins or only small ones, whether a man who commits great sins is a
brother at all, and then the objection he raises against his own
conclusion, that treating a man as a Gentile and a publican after three
censures for reviling or over-drinking "would seem to be very harsh".

Set that beside desert.story.moses-leaking-jug, where the elders gather
at Scetis to judge a brother and Abba Moses arrives carrying a leaking
jug and will not do it. Origen builds the machinery of correction and
then flinches at what it costs; a century later, in the same tradition,
the desert's most repeated answer is to refuse the procedure outright.
That is not a contradiction to be resolved. It is two parts of one
tradition working the same problem from opposite ends, and this world can
now show both.

desert.quote.origen-on-the-sinning-brother carries the passage.
