TWO REASONS TO HOLD IT, and the second is the better one.

1. IT IS THE PART OF ORIGEN THAT WAS FOUND SAFE. Somebody in the middle
   of the fourth century went through Origen and chose twenty-seven
   chapters worth keeping - overwhelmingly on how to read scripture, and
   then on free will and providence. Forty years later a council in
   Alexandria condemned him. The anthology is therefore a dated
   measurement of what was and was not controversial about Origen BEFORE
   the controversy, made by people who admired him, and it can be set
   against desert.force.origenist-controversy directly. If the tradition
   ascribing it to Basil and Gregory is right, the men who curated Origen
   were the same generation as the men who condemned him.

2. IT CARRIES THE GREEK OF DE PRINCIPIIS WHERE RUFINUS IS ALL WE
   OTHERWISE HAVE. Chapter I says of itself that it is "From the work on
   'Principles'". desert.source.origen-de-principiis reads a partisan's
   Latin made during the controversy; this is the check on it, for the
   passages it happens to cover. That is a real, usable transmission
   fact rather than a general caution.

Its bearing on desert.gravity.scriptural-engagement is oblique and must
stay so. That gravity's own claim is that this world read scripture
practically and did NOT systematize. The Philocalia is the systematized
alternative, assembled in the same century, in Greek, by bishops. It is
useful as the contrast that makes the gravity's claim legible - never as
evidence about how desert ascetics themselves read.
