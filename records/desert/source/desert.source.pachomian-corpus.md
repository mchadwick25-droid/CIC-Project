Every Rule-content claim in this corpus must still name its actual
channel, but as of 2026-08-27 there are four channels, not two, and
the first of them is new:

  1. "as the Ethiopic Rule reads" - vendored, quotable, direct.
     Schodde 1885. Say the recension every time. It is not Jerome's
     Latin and its wording is not the Latin's wording.
  2. "as Palladius/Sozomen report the rule" - vendored, at one remove,
     a summary rather than the legislation.
  3. "as the Latin Rule transmits" - consult-only scholarship, three
     translation stages from the Coptic. Still unquotable.
  4. "as the Lives narrate" - consult-only, multiple recensions, used
     only where consistent across them, per the prior build's cleared
     practice.

THE ANGEL-TABLET FRAME WAS MISDESCRIBED IN THIS WORLD AND IS NOW
CORRECTED. Records here had treated the angel and the iron tablet as
the hagiographic packaging that Palladius and Sozomen wrapped around a
rule they knew from outside - "reports of the rule, not its text". The
Ethiopic text disproves that. Part I opens "In the name of the holy
Trinity. The ordinance which the angel of the Lord commanded to Abba
Pachomius", and the angel hands over the tablet inside the rule
document. The frame is in the received legislation itself. That is a
harder and more interesting fact about how this federation authorised
its own governance, and this world may now say it from the text.

THE THREE PARTS ARE NOT PEERS and no record may cite them as one
thing. Schodde: "The Ethiopic rules consist of three distinct parts,
which are undoubtedly different in origin and date, as they differ in
character." Part I he holds to be the same as the Greek form, reaching
"almost to the days of Pachomius himself, and if not verbatim from his
pen, are yet authentic enough for all practical purposes". Part II is
Pachomian-tradition legislation of uncertain date - the practical
horarium, and the women's house across the river. Part III is not
legislation at all but a late Ethiopic homily on ten symbolic
associations, five corrupt and five sound, surviving in no other
language; it must never be attributed to Pachomius, and its interest
is that it is a coenobitic federation describing its own decay.

The multi-stage translation chain remains formation-relevant data, and
the new file deepens rather than dissolves the point: Strand B's own
most internal texts reached this world's later readers through Coptic,
Greek, Latin, Ethiopic and German hands, and the only branch this
corpus can quote verbatim is the one that travelled furthest.
