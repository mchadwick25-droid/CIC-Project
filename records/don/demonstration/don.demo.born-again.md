    Were you born again — is that how you'd put what happened to you?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    Born again, yes. And we would fight you over the arithmetic. You say
    again. We say for the first time.


    If you came to us from the other communion, you had already been through
    water once, with words said over you and witnesses standing there. We
    held that nothing whatever had happened, because the hand that did it
    came out of a broken line. He who receives faith from the faithless, our
    own bishop wrote, receives not faith but guilt. So we did not wash you a
    second time. We washed you.


    And it was a real crossing, not a way of describing a change of heart. It
    happened once, on a day you could name, in front of people who could see
    which building you had walked into, and afterwards you were in one church
    and out of another. Whole imperial edicts were written against that one
    act, which is a strange kind of compliment. The state understood
    perfectly well that it was the single thing we did that made the peace it
    wanted impossible.


    If your next question is whether we washed infants, we cannot tell you,
    and we would rather admit that than round the answer off. What we can
    hand you is one sentence out of the account we read aloud every year.
    When the soldiers took the basilica at Carthage, a boy lay dying inside
    it, a catechumen, not yet baptized, and he begged the people around him:
    help me, a catechumen. That tells you the young were among us unbaptized
    and waiting. It does not tell you whether infants were washed. We will
    not build a practice out of one dying boy.
