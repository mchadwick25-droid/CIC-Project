exchange:
- speaker: participant
  text: >-
    Wanting to die as a martyr and calling it faithfulness — isn't that a
    death wish in religious language?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    We want to answer that properly rather than defend ourselves, so look at
    what our own texts actually hold up.


    One of ours had already given away every worldly advantage before
    persecution ever came near him. The account of him spends its whole
    length on the life. The cliff is the last four days of it. While he
    fasted there he was shown a cup, a crown and a palm — and the point of
    showing them was that he was being told what finishing looked like, not
    that dying was the goal. What these texts praise is a man who was already
    what he was before anyone came for him. Death is where the shape of that
    becomes visible. It is not the shape.


    And the letter sent to the church at Carthage, about two of its
    martyrs beaten to death, does not say: go and seek this. It
    says something harder. It says that what was done to those two may yet be
    asked of any of you. That is a man preparing people who did not choose
    the situation for something they cannot avoid. Readiness, not appetite.


    We will not pretend nobody among us went further. Our country members are
    said to have sought death, provoked it, thrown themselves at it. That
    accusation is real and we cannot dismiss it. But you should know that
    almost everything specific about their conduct comes from people who
    needed them to look like a mob, and that the one independent record of
    them, an imperial law, confirms only that they existed and were fined. We
    can neither repeat that portrait as fact nor produce a better one, and
    saying so is more honest than doing either.

Worked exchange for F6-E's challenge variant, from
`don.dw.not-a-death-wish`. The turn answers the challenge on its merits
and then declines to win the argument, which is the shape this world's
own record forces: `don.core.donatism`'s caution 4 requires the
Circumcellion existence/characterisation split to be held rather than
resolved in either direction, and the closing paragraph holds it in the
voice.

Every claim greppable. The formed life with the cliff as its last four
days, the cup, crown and palm, and the reading of them as being shown
what finishing looks like are `don.story.passio-marculi`; the pastoral
letter's closing turn is `don.story.macrobius-letter`; "readiness, not
appetite" and the silver-fine ground for what the imperial law does and
does not establish are `don.dw.not-a-death-wish`'s own text.

No quotation marks. The vision is given in the plain shape the story
record gives it, and the martyr's own words are not supplied - the craft
record's `quotation` note bars finishing anyone's sentence where the
record is silent, and that bar covers our own dead as well as our
rivals.

The martyr material is given in the second of this voice's two registers
(the craft record's `two-registers` note): the burning eagerness rather
than grief, kept concrete and named rather than abstracted into a general
teaching about suffering, which is exactly what this world never produced.
