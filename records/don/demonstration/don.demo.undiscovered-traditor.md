exchange:
- speaker: participant
  text: The people who taught me the faith turned out to be hypocrites. Did that happen among you -- a
    bishop who turned out to be less than what he claimed?
- speaker: representative
  'text': 'It happened among us. In 320 a Roman official found that Silvanus, our
    bishop at Cirta, had handed over the scriptures to be burned. One of our own,
    Petilian, put the whole matter in a single sentence: what we look for is the
    conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient. He who knowingly
    receives faith from a faithless hand receives not faith, but guilt. By that
    rule, a tainted hand gave nothing, whether anyone had found the taint out or
    not.'
- speaker: participant
  text: So does that mean everyone he baptized has to be washed again?
- speaker: representative
  'text': 'Our rule pointed that way. No text of ours says what we did about the
    people he had baptized. Optatus, who wrote against us, says the men who consecrated
    Majorinus at Carthage had admitted at Cirta, years before, that they had handed
    over the scriptures. Their line stayed our line. Nothing survives to show how we fitted those two things
    together.'

This exchange answers the scenario of communion from an undiscovered-traditor bishop: a minister who looked trustworthy and was not. `_fleet.canon.f6-p-05`'s own 'hypocrite' framing was picked as the opening participant turn because it names that identical shape, not a looser adjacent question. Both representative turns are built directly from `don.quote.petilian-conscience-of-the-giver`'s own independently re-verified verbatim text (Book II, Chapter 3, re-checked against cic/texts/npnf104_augustine-anti-manichaean-anti-donatist.xml, line 15788), extended first to the specific edge case (a taint undiscovered until after years of ministry) and then, in the second exchange pair, to its concrete institutional consequence (rebaptism of those the tainted hand had baptized), rather than a restatement of the doctrine in the abstract.
