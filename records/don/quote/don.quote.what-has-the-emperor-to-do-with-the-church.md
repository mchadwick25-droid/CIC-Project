modern_lens_note: >-
  Two things a modern reader will hear wrongly. First, this is not a
  principle of church-state separation in the constitutional sense.
  Nothing here says religion and government should occupy separate
  spheres. The claim is narrower and sharper: an emperor has no standing
  to decide WHICH church is the true one, because that is the church's
  own question and he has already taken a side in it. The same communion
  petitioned imperial power three times when it suited them - for judges
  in 313, for their confiscated basilicas under a later emperor, and for
  the anti-heretical law to be used against their own breakaway party -
  and did not treat those as breaking the rule.
  Second, and more important: the only person who wrote this sentence
  down was an enemy. Optatus reports it inside a chapter titled "The
  pride of Donatus," presents it as a man falling into a rage at
  commissioners who had come bearing alms for the poor, and follows it
  within a page by likening Donatus to the prince of Tyre who said "I am
  God." Every element of that setting was chosen to make the speaker look
  arrogant. The sentence may well be Donatus's own; the scene it sits in
  is Optatus's case against him.

Verified verbatim against the vendored `optatus_against-the-donatists.txt`
(Edwards' English translation, Registry row 2 lineage), Book III.3, where
the printed line reads exactly: "'What has the Emperor to do with the
Church?'" No wording added, dropped, or reordered. The surrounding
narrative - Constans sending Paul and Macarius, Donatus's reported fury,
the refusal of the alms, and the comparison to the prince of Tyre - was
read in full before this record was written, which is why the
`modern_lens_note` can state the framing specifically rather than
gesturing at hostility in general.

Register `emic`: Donatus is this communion's own primate, and the words
are attributed to him. That is a different situation from
`cappadocian.quote.julian-galilaeans`, where an outside adversary speaks
in his own voice and the record is `etic`. The hostile mediation here is
carried in `divergence_note` and `modern_lens_note` rather than by
changing the register, because the SPEAKER is ours even though the pen is
not.

`don.dw.the-emperor-and-the-church` names the hostile framing in the
voice as well, so a participant who hears only the witness is not left
with a cleaner provenance than the sentence actually has. Reciprocal
relation declared there.
