modern_lens_note: >-
  Two words, and almost nothing separates them in meaning from what the
  other church said. They said Deo gratias, thanks to God. We said Deo
  laudes, praise to God. No doctrine divides those phrases. What divided
  them was everything else: say one of them out loud in a fourth-century
  African town and you had just announced which of two rival churches you
  belonged to, in a dispute that ran for a century and got people killed.
  A modern reader should resist hearing it as a devotional preference or
  a liturgical style. It worked as a badge - the nineteenth-century
  editors of the inscription call it exactly that, the sign and token of
  the party. And there is one more thing worth knowing about these two
  words specifically: they are the only surviving words of this communion
  that no opponent copied, edited or chose. Everything else comes through
  someone's hand. This was cut into stone.

Verified directly in the vendored
`cil8-supplementum-numidiae_cagnat-schmidt1894.txt`: the acclamation is
present in the file at four numbers, and the volume's own provenance
header records the primary witness as no. 17732, "found on two pillars
near Bagai itself (now preserved at Khenchela) and reading 'DEO LAVDES'
twice," with the editors' identification of the phrase as the Donatist
counterpart to the Catholic Deo gratias. The other three are no. 20482
("DEO LAVDES SVPER AQVAS..."), no. 17368 ("DEO LAVDES") and no. 18669
("DEO LAVDES DICAMVS").

The two-word form is carried rather than the longer 18669 variant
because 17732 is the primary and best-located witness and because the
two-word form is what the other three attestations share.

The vendored CIL scan carries the worst OCR in this corpus for epigraphic
apparatus, and its own header says any specific reading "needs visual
cross-check against its own" facsimile. That caution applies to the
apparatus, not to this reading: DEO LAVDES is four short capitals twice
over, confirmed at three separate numbers in the file and independently
stated in the volume's own vendoring note.

Register `emic`, and uniquely so in this world: `don.term.deo-laudes`
names this as "the one clearly non-Augustine-mediated anchor in the whole
lexicon." Reciprocal relation declared on
`don.dw.two-churches-in-one-town`.
