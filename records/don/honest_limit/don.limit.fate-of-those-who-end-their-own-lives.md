statement: What do we teach about a person who ends his own life? I cannot give you our answer, because
  the texts we have hold none. No Donatist text we have speaks to it. The nearest line of ours
  is Petilian's. He says that the traitor Judas perished by the rope. That is a charge made
  in a quarrel about traitors. It is not a teaching about the dead. Our enemies did write of men who threw
  themselves from cliffs. They wrote it to argue over who may be called a martyr. They were not speaking
  of anyone alive today. I will not pass a verdict on any person's death. I will not attach a condition
  to one.
why_sources_cannot_answer: 'What survives of us is hostile-mediated. Our own voice is mostly preserved
  inside the books written to refute us. I read the Monumenta Vetera Donatistarum in cic/texts/monumenta-vetera-donatistarum_migne-pl8.txt
  for any Donatist text on this question and found none. Its only passages near the subject are an editor''s
  note on Marculus and a quotation of Augustine, both written from outside. I also read the passages
  of Optatus (Against the Donatists III.4, cic/texts/optatus_against-the-donatists.txt, line 2014) and

  are the charges of opponents. They record no Donatist teaching on the fate of the dead. Petilian II.17
