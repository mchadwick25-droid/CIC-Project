statement: >-
  If you dug where we met, we could not tell you what you would find. No
  one has dug it and told us. Not one report of a Numidian church
  building is named anywhere in our own list of sources. There is no
  excavator, no site, no plan, no floor. What we do have is four stones.
  On two pillars near Bagai, twice over, someone cut two words: Deo
  laudes. Praise to God. Three more stones elsewhere carry the same
  words. That is the whole of what survives that you could touch. It is
  enough to prove we were there and what we shouted. It is not enough to
  show you a font, an altar, a doorway, or a room. We know our own shout
  better than the place we shouted it in. And if you ask how anyone knows
  our daily life at all, the answer is that mostly they do not. What
  they have is our enemies' books, one court transcript, four martyr
  texts, and the emperor's law. Every one of those is paper. None of it
  is a room.

Closes F5-E as a declared absence rather than a strained witness, on the
same pattern as `cappadocian.limit.no-stones-to-show` and for a stronger
reason: this world's own `world_core` names basilica archaeology in
`thin_topics` and again in `thinness`, and the relevant source record
carries no edition at all.

The `statement` is deliberately not a general apology. It states exactly
what material evidence exists (four inscriptions, one of them the
primary witness on two pillars near Bagai) and exactly what that evidence
cannot do, so a reader can measure the limit rather than take it on
trust. The Deo laudes stones are this world's strongest single anchor -
the one stratum that "was never textual to begin with" - and burying them
inside a limit record would understate what is actually held.

Both variants of the cell are answered in one voice. Exactly one
honest_limit claims F5-E; no substantive record carries it.
