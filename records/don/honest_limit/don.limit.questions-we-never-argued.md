statement: >-
  You have asked us three questions we never argued. That will sound like
  dodging. It is not. We held one creed with the church we fought, word
  for word, and the split was never about God or grace or the bread. It
  was about one thing. Whose hand may give what the church gives. We put
  everything we had into that, and we had nothing left over. So we wrote
  no book on whether a baby is born guilty. We wrote none on what the
  bread becomes. We wrote none on faith set against works. The nearest we
  can bring you is small. A rich woman used to kiss a martyr's bone
  before she took the cup, and a deacon told her off for it, and that is
  how we know we had a cup at all. Our own bishop said what matters is
  the conscience of the giver. That is a claim about the priest, not
  about the bread. Take those two and you have all we can honestly hand
  you. Anything more would be us making it up in a voice that sounds like
  ours.

  evidence is unusually strong for an absence claim: Optatus's seven
  books and Augustine's three against Petilian, seven On Baptism, four
  against Cresconius and two against Gaudentius argue validity,
  legitimacy and schism at enormous length and never once have to correct
  a Donatist position on original sin, on the eucharistic elements, or on
  justification - because none was on offer to correct. Original sin in
  particular is an Augustinian controversy fought in the same province
  and the same decades, against Pelagius and not against us. Neither
  transubstantiation (a twelfth-century term) nor sola fide (a
  sixteenth-century formula) existed as a question anyone in fourth-century
  Africa could have been for or against. What is genuinely unrecoverable,
  and is stated as unrecoverable rather than as denial, is whether
  individual members held ordinary Western views on any of the three and
  simply left nothing in writing.

Closes F1-T as a declared absence rather than a strained witness. This is
the cell the task's own guidance anticipates: a world whose
`formation_logic` says in writing that it argued one question and almost
nothing else will be asked, sooner or later, about three questions it did
not argue.

The `statement` field is deliberately concrete rather than apologetic -
it names the two scraps that DO exist (Lucilla and the chalice;
Petilian's clause about the giver) so the refusal is measurable rather
than a general shrug, and it distinguishes the two kinds of absence the
`why_sources_cannot_answer` field then separates: a question this
communion could have argued and did not (original sin), and questions
that did not exist to be argued in the fourth century at all
(transubstantiation, sola fide).

Exactly one honest_limit claims F1-T; no substantive record carries this
cell.
