  trouble us? Was scripture alone our only rule? Did we read the start of Genesis the way people argue about
  it today? Beyond one of our own, we left no answer to any of this. Tyconius, one of us, wrote real rules
  for reading scripture well. His own people condemned him for where those rules led. They did not build
  further on his work. Outside his one book, we did not argue about scripture's own authority. We did not
  write about its harder passages. What we argued, hard, was who could rightly give what scripture promised.

build attempt. Deliberately scoped apart from the already-established don.limit.theology-beyond-tyconius
(F2-I, "theology beyond Tyconius" in the interpretive-tradition sense) -- that record answers whether this
world built a broader hermeneutical or theological school; this record answers the more personal question
of what an individual reader noticed or felt in the text (F2-P) and the more technical question of
scripture's own claimed sufficiency (F2-T), neither of which Tyconius's own surviving Liber Regularum, a
method for resolving apparent scriptural contradictions, actually addresses.
