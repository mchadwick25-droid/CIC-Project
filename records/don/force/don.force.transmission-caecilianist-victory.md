description: >-
  In the end, the Caecilianist party won the contest for the institution. That victory decided
  what got copied.

  Donatist writing survives almost entirely as quotation. Optatus and Augustine wrote works to
  refute it. It is embedded inside those works. A small remainder survives on its own. It is
  Tyconius's Liber Regularum. It is the Passio Marculi. It is the Passio Isaac et Maximiani. It is
  the commemorative sermon.

  There is also a documented record carved in stone. It is the Deo laudes acclamation. It survived
  largely because it was never a text to begin with.

  How this felt from inside cannot be recovered from the sources that survive. This world's own
  people left no account of what they believed they were preserving as their institutional
  position weakened. Nor did they say what they expected would, or would not, survive them.

  More than any other single force, this one shapes what this account can and cannot know about
  this world. The surviving texts of this world's martyr cult are, in large part, this world's own
  surviving voice. They express its identity as the church of the martyrs, as modern scholars call
  it.

  That is precisely because the martyr texts and the inscriptions sit outside the manuscript
  channel. This force otherwise controls that channel, partly or wholly.

  The pattern began earlier, while the movement was still active. Its words survived through the
  hostile party's own quotes and refutations. Once the Caecilianist victory was complete, that
  pattern became final. It could not be reversed. The same process ran through the whole period.
  At the end, it fixed exactly which small part of this world's own voice survives apart from it.
manifestations:
- Tyconius's Liber Regularum, surviving specifically because of its own influence on Augustine's De doctrina
  christiana III (a transmission mechanism Doc_02 SS2 explicitly does not resolve, and does not need to
  resolve)
- the Deo laudes acclamation, transmitted by stone itself, requiring no manuscript custodian at all
