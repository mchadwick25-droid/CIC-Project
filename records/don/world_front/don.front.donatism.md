      Between 311 and 439, in Roman North Africa, two churches stood in
      nearly every town, each with its own bishop, altar, and unbroken
      line of ordination. The rift began with an accusation: that the
      man who consecrated the new bishop of Carthage had once handed
      the scriptures over to be burned. If that hand was tainted, this
      world held, everything it touched afterward was empty too - so a
      whole rival church grew up around one demand: wash again, by a
      hand that has never broken. This communion called itself the
      Church of the Martyrs. When persecution came a second time, at a
      rival Christian party's own urging, it kept the names of its dead
      and read their story aloud every year at the grave. It refused
      any emperor's standing to decide who the true church was - though
      three times, when it served the case, it turned to that same
      emperor's courts anyway, and said so plainly in the same breath
      as the refusal. Nearly everything known of this world comes from
      the two bishops who wrote against it. Four short texts, and two
      words cut into stone, are what survive in its own voice.

      This world's own record runs from 311 or 312 to 439, centered on
      Carthage and the Roman provinces of North Africa - Africa
      Proconsularis, Numidia, Byzacena, and Mauretania. It is not a
      doctrinal heresy. Both this communion and the rival it split from
      confessed the same Nicene faith, word for word. What was
      contested was narrower and sharper: whether a bishop's own
      conduct under persecution could void what his hand later gave.
      One conviction runs through everything this world did - purity,
      rite, memory, and refusal are four faces of a single claim: this
      is the pure, persecuted, true church, proved by what it will not
      concede and by what it has suffered for refusing to concede it.
    grounded_in: [don.core.donatism]
  - text: >-
      The conviction was not new in 311. Half a century earlier,
      eighty-seven African bishops meeting at Carthage under Cyprian
      had already ruled that baptism given outside the true church
      washes nothing. This world's founders read themselves as
      continuing that African ruling, not inventing one - a claim
      their own opponents had to answer at length, arguing that
      Cyprian, however holy, was not a canonical authority. Then came
      the Diocletianic persecution of 303 to 305, and a demand no
      earlier dispute had tested: that clergy surrender the scriptures
      themselves, on pain of death. Some did. Some refused and suffered
      for it. What a minister did in that one moment, this world held,
      was not incidental to whether his hand could be trusted
      afterward.

      rebuked her for it in public, and she did not forget it. When

      Aptungi, was accused of having surrendered the scriptures

      voided the consecration and everything flowing from it. A rival
      party would not accept it: they consecrated Majorinus, from
      Lucilla's own household, beside Caecilian rather than in his
      place. Majorinus's successor Donatus gave the movement the name
      history remembers it by, though nothing suggests his own people
      chose that name for themselves. Lucilla is the one woman this
      world's own record names - and she reaches us only inside the
      hostile narrative that blamed her for the whole split.

describing the same underlying content under two different ids - one
member of each pair `register: emic` with `canon_cells: []`, the other
`register: etic` with `canon_cells` populated, differing mainly in
prose polish and in which downstream records their own `relations[]`
point at. Confirmed pairs: `don.gravity.rebaptism-boundary` /
`don.gravity.rebaptism-boundary-marking` (G2); `don.gravity.church-of-
the-martyrs` / `don.gravity.martyr-cult-identity` (G3);

hierarchy` (G4); `don.gravity.principled-refusal-vs-pragmatic-recourse`
/ `don.gravity.refusal-against-recourse` (T1); `don.gravity.purity-
rigor-vs-institutional-reception` / `don.gravity.rigor-against-
reception` (T2); at least nine matching pairs across `don.force.*`
(e.g. `don.force.diocletianic-persecution-traditio-demand` /
`don.force.diocletianic-traditio-demand`, `don.force.conference-of-

FINDING 2 - A NARROWER, DIFFERENT-SHAPED DEFECT INSIDE THAT SAME
PATTERN: `don.figure.optatus` and `don.figure.optatus-of-milevis` are
not merely two records about the same man (Optatus of Milevis, the

world's own register discipline backwards) and is flagged here rather
than silently routed around by omission alone.
