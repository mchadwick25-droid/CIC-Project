claim: >-
  Baptism given outside the true church is no baptism at all. A person who comes over to us is not
  re-baptised but baptised, first and truly, by a hand of unbroken standing - and this is the threshold
  that makes membership a thing crossed once and bodily rather than a thing asserted. It is not an
  innovation: it is what the African bishops assembled under Cyprian at Carthage in 256 already held.
held_against:
- Augustine answered the practice with seven books, and answered it from inside this practice's own claimed
  inheritance rather than from outside it - the whole force of On Baptism, Against the Donatists is that
  Cyprian, the African authority this rite invokes, did not break communion over the question, so the
  authority claimed here does not license what is done with it (don.source.augustine-on-baptism-against-the-donatists;
  don.source.cyprian-epistles)
- The practice's own boundary was not held at its own boundary case. The Maximianist clergy condemned at
  Bagai in 394 were received back into office without repeated baptism - the exception that presupposes
  the norm, and the exception Augustine pressed hardest (don.story.bagai-reconciliation)
- Successive imperial edicts named rebaptism of Catholics specifically as a targeted legal offence, so
  the rival communion's reading of the rite became the law that this communion then lived under. The practice's
  own most direct attestation across the window is therefore, repeatedly, legislation against it
  (don.source.codex-theodosianus-book-16)
- The corroboration this rite has from a Donatist voice comes from within the hostile text's own quotation
  of that voice - Petilian argued FOR the practice in words Augustine preserved in order to answer them
  - not from any source outside the Optatus/Augustine corpus, unlike the martyr-cult material, which is
  attested in Donatist-authored texts and in stone (don.gravity.rebaptism-boundary's own divergence_note;
  Doc_04 SS5)
- The one attestation of the practice surviving past this world's own window is likewise an opponent's -
  Gregory the Great's letters of 592 and 594, urging inquiry and suppression. Nothing about the rite's
  scale, doctrine or self-understanding at that date can be extended beyond what those letters themselves
  say
concedes: >-
  The bare practice is not in dispute and this record does not pretend it is contested ground: it is
  attested directly and repeatedly, argued for in a Donatist voice, legislated against by name, and still
  live in Numidia two centuries after this world's window closes. What this communion's own record concedes
  is narrower and harder. The rite's own councils suspended it for the Maximianist clergy without saying
  why, and nothing of that reasoning survives. And the claim that Cyprian's Carthage of 256 is simply this
  rite's own earlier self is a claim about continuity that the rival communion contested from inside the
  same texts - the African bishops of 256 held the position without making it a ground of separation, which
  is precisely the step taken here and not there.

THE CLAIM IS DELIBERATELY NOT "THE PRACTICE HAPPENED." That is Documented and contesting it would be
a false contest. What a Table Readiness Round needs from this gravity is the claim the practice
carries - that baptism outside this communion is nothing, so the rite is a first baptism and not a
repetition - together with what a Cyprianic-inheritance claim can and cannot bear. The concedes field
above states the second half of that at the strength this world's own construction record states it,
and no further.

THE CYPRIAN CONTINUITY POINT, STATED CAREFULLY. This record does not adjudicate whether Cyprian's own
position licenses this practice; it records that both communions argued the question from the same
African texts, that this communion read 256 as its own earlier self, and that Augustine's seven books
answer from inside that same inheritance rather than dismissing it. don.source.cyprian-epistles and
don.source.seventh-council-of-carthage-256-anf05 are cited above for the African material both sides
used, not as evidence that either reading of it is correct. Registry row 29's own standing exclusion
is respected here and named for the same reason it exists: Novatianist rebaptism at Rome c. 251 is a
genuine surface similarity to this rite and is NOT continuous with it, and nothing in this record
treats it as a comparandum.
