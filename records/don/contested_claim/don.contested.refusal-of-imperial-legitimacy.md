claim: >-
  Quid est imperatori cum ecclesia? The state has no standing to adjudicate who the true church is, and
  a verdict reached through a state-aligned process is not thereby a church verdict. That is why Rome in
  313 and Arles in 314 were rejected rather than accepted, why our bishops refused the chairs at Carthage
  in 411 and stood for three days, and why no imperial favour shown to our rival has ever amounted to an
  argument.
held_against:
- The interpretive frame is the most heavily synthesised thing in this world's gravity discovery. That
  the recurring episodes together constitute a principled refusal of the state's authority, rather than
  an unconnected series of grievances, is partly Doc_04's own reading of Doc_01 SS5, corroborated by exactly
  one directly-quoted Donatist utterance - Donatus's retort - which reaches this record inside Optatus's
  own hostile narrative frame, not through any Donatist-authored text
  (don.gravity.refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy's own divergence_note)
- This communion turned to the same imperial machinery three times - the 313 petition to Constantine forwarded
  through Anulinus's relatio, the 361 petition to Julian for restoration of confiscated basilicas, and
  the 390s invocation of existing imperial and proconsular anti-heretical legislation against its own Maximianist
  dissidents. The earliest of the three is not a late drift but the founding moment itself
  (don.gravity.refusal-against-recourse)
- Augustine answered the refusal in his own first-person voice and won the argument institutionally. In
  Letter 185, written to the imperial tribune Boniface around 417, he defends compelling schismatics into
  communion by law, grounds it on Luke 14:23, and records that he had himself once held the opposite view
  - a change of mind about the use of force, written by the man who changed it
  (don.source.augustine-correction-of-the-donatists)
- The 411 Conference shows the refusal being held and being overridden in the same room. Marcellinus conceded
  the principle - that after the contest the name Catholic belongs to those with whom the truth is found
  - and then said he was nevertheless bound to follow the imperial rescript, which had already been pleased
  to call the other side by it. Our own bishops both denied the court's standing and argued inside it for
  three days (don.story.conference-of-carthage-411)
- No Donatist-authored account of any of this survives - no chronicle of Arles, no administrative record
  of the Macarian repression, no reception of the 411 verdict in this communion's own words. The verdict
  itself is not in the numbered acts read for this world's record, and Doc_08 declines to let the hostile
  record's account of it stand in for the missing one (don.core.donatism thin_topics;
  don.gravity.refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy)
concedes: >-
  The refusal is dominant, not absolute, and this communion's own record says so in the same texts that
  state it at its most absolute. Three documented turns to imperial or proconsular machinery stand on the
  record - 313, 361, and the 390s against this communion's own dissidents - and each falls at a moment
  the external power briefly offered something to gain by engaging it. They are not embarrassments quietly
  managed. What cannot be settled from this record is whether this communion itself experienced them as
  exceptions to a principle or simply did not think of them as bearing on the principle at all, because
  no account of the tension from this side survives; the name given to the space between the two poles
  is the build's, not this world's own self-description.
