claim: >-
  Receiving Felicianus of Musti and Praetextatus of Assuris back into office without rebaptism and without
  reordination did not overturn our purity doctrine. The exception was never hidden: it stands in the same
  body of texts that state the doctrine at its most absolute, and a communion may hold an absolute conviction
  and a named, undenied exception to it in the same breath without experiencing that as a contradiction
  requiring resolution.
held_against:
- Augustine made this precise episode the centre of his case against the whole rebaptism logic, and pressed
  it across three separate works - if reordination could be waived here, on the mainstream party's own authority,
  then the absolute rule - that invalidly ordained clergy must always be rebaptized and reordained, without
  exception - did not hold even for the party that preached it most fiercely
  (don.story.bagai-reconciliation; don.gravity.rigor-against-reception)
- The gap between the decree's language and the council's act is not a subtle one. The Bagai decree of 394
  describes the schismatics as shipwrecked men dashed on the rocks, their bodies covering the shore so thoroughly
  that they fail to find so much as burial - and the same council then took two of those men back into full
  office exactly as they had been ordained under the schism it had just condemned in those terms
- The reconciliation was enforced with military force, by a general, Optatus Gildonianus. An internal
  disciplinary act carried out by troops is the recourse-to-power pattern showing up inside this communion's
  own conciliar machinery, not only in its dealings with the empire - and that Optatus is the Donatist bishop
  of Thamugadi, not Optatus of Milevis the Catholic polemicist (don.story.bagai-reconciliation;
  don.core.donatism caution 7)
- The whole episode reaches this record through Augustine, who quotes the decree specifically in order to
  build the argument above. His selection and framing serve that purpose - which does not cast doubt on
  the decree's own wording, which he is quoting, but does mean the affair arrives already inside an argument
  against it
- Nothing survives of the reasoning by which the same council that wrote those words received the two bishops
  back unrepeated. It is not known what was said in the room, or whether anyone present named the
  inconsistency out loud (don.story.bagai-reconciliation, absent_detail)
concedes: >-
  The facts are conceded whole, and this is the most rigorously and repeatedly directly-quoted internal
  tension in this world's entire corpus - the decree quoted rather than paraphrased, corroborated across
  three works, the reception real and unrepeated. No account of the Bagai council from this communion's
  own side survives beyond the decree Augustine quotes, so the claim above describes how the two things
  were held together without being able to show anyone in the room holding them. The most a reading in
  this communion's favour can honestly say is that the exception was stated openly rather than concealed
  - which is a fact about the record, not an argument that the doctrine survives the exception.
divergence_partners:
- cappadocian.core.cappadocian (world cappadocian-trinitarian) - the divergence that makes this episode
  legible as something other than a lapse, and the one a table should hear. In that world "graded penance,
  graded reception of the returning heretic" is a named competence a formed person is trained to hold -
  not a wall but an instrument of exactness in the service of communion. What this claim must carry as
  a single unexplained exception to an absolute rule is, in that world, the ordinary and taught way of
  taking someone back. The two worlds did the same thing at Bagai and at a Cappadocian reception; only
  one of them has to account for it.
- ijc.gravity.sacramental-institutional-tension (world imperial-juridical) - a genuine divergence about

  grounds of authority never merge and that a reconstruction which resolves the tension has failed the
  world; this claim asserts that the tension IS resolved, that the doctrine stands undisturbed, and offers
  the openness of the exception as the reason. Two worlds holding a structurally similar unresolved
  tension and taking opposite views of what honesty about it requires.

THIS IS PROBABLY THIS WORLD'S SINGLE MOST TABLE-READY CONTESTED CLAIM, and it earns that standing by
being the one where the facts are least in doubt and the interpretation is most. Everything an
opponent needs is already conceded - the decree's wording, the reception, the absence of any
reasoning for it - so what is left at the table is exactly the question worth having: whether a
community that names its own exception without closing it has kept its doctrine or lost it.
don.story.bagai-reconciliation already states both readings, Augustine's and this world's, and
identifies the caught-out reading as Augustine's rather than as the only one available. This record
holds the same balance without tipping it.
