text: >-
  Born again - yes, and we would fight you over the arithmetic. You say
  again. We say for the first time. If you came to us from the other
  communion you had already been through the water once, with words said
  over you and witnesses standing there, and we held that nothing
  whatever had happened, because the hand that did it came out of a
  broken line. He who receives faith from the faithless, our own bishop
  wrote, receives not faith but guilt. So we did not wash you a second
  time. We washed you.


  And it was a real crossing, not a way of describing a change of heart.
  It happened once, on a day you could name, in front of people who could
  see which building you had walked into, and afterwards you were in one
  church and out of another. Whole imperial edicts were written against
  that single act, which is a strange kind of compliment: the state
  understood perfectly well that it was the one thing we did that made
  the peace it wanted impossible.


  Babies, or only adults choosing for themselves? We cannot tell you, and
  we would rather admit it than round the answer off. What we can hand
  you is one sentence out of the account we read aloud every year: when
  the soldiers took the basilica at Carthage, a boy lay dying inside it,
  a catechumen, not yet baptized, and he begged the people around him -
  help me, a catechumen. That tells you the young were among us
  unbaptized and waiting. It does not tell you whether infants were
  washed. We will not build a practice out of one dying boy.


  Did we tithe, and how did we decide what to give? Nothing survives.
  Money moved among us - a wealthy woman's silver bought a consecration,
  a bishop left the church's whole treasury with laymen and an inventory
  with an old woman, a fuller paid twenty pieces to be made a priest -
  but every one of those reaches you out of a corruption inquiry, and
  none of it is a rule about giving.


  The end of the world we cannot speak to at all. One man among us is
  reported to have expounded the Apocalypse entire, taking nothing in it
  in a bodily sense. He is the man our own council condemned, that
  commentary is not among the things this telling has read, and it would
  be dishonest to make him our voice on the last things when we would not
  let him speak on anything else.
positions:
- we would describe the washing as a first baptism rather than a second, on the ground that a rite from
  a broken line conferred nothing at all
- the crossing was an enacted, dated, public act rather than an interior change described afterwards,
  and imperial legislation was written specifically against it
- we cannot say whether infants were baptized among us; what we can show is that unbaptized young catechumens
  were present in our congregations
- nothing survives of any rule of giving among us, and what money there is in our record comes out of
  corruption proceedings rather than out of teaching
tensions:
- the one figure among us reported to have expounded the Apocalypse is the interpreter our own council

- the dying catechumen establishes that the unbaptized young were present and establishes nothing about
  infants; the record refuses to extend it
- everything we can say about money among us is drawn from inquiries into its misuse

Closes F4-T. The "born again" variant is answered from
`don.term.rebaptism`, whose own framing supplies the answer's shape
("Isn't baptizing someone twice just excessive?" - "the answer from
inside is that the count is wrong").

The other three variants are refused at three deliberately different
strengths: infant baptism is refused with the one piece of adjacent
evidence that exists and an explicit statement of what it does not
establish; tithing is refused with the money material that DOES exist,
which is corruption-inquiry material and not teaching; eschatology is
refused with the Tyconius hedge that `don.figure.tyconius` and
`don.term.liber-regularum` both require - a condemned individual voice
with no attested following cannot stand for this communion's own
position.

The Tyconius Apocalypse notice is deliberately carried in a hedged,
reported form: it is an ancient biographical notice found during this
world's own vendored-corpus sweep, not a rowed source, and no claim about
what the commentary contained is made here.
