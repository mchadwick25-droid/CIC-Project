text: >-
  No, and the evidence for that is what our opponents had to say to beat
  us. Our case ran on two legs. One was scripture. The other was
  Carthage: eighty-seven African bishops, sitting under Cyprian half a
  century before there was any quarrel between us, who had already ruled
  that a sacrament from outside the true church confers nothing. When we
  argued for washing again the people who came to us from the other
  communion, we were not producing a novelty from a proof-text. We were
  standing on an African council and on the bishop who presided at it,
  and we thought that made us the older party, not the newer one. You can
  see how much that argument weighed by what it forced out of the other
  side: they had to answer, in writing, that Cyprian is not a canonical
  authority and the Scriptures alone are. Nobody has to say that unless
  the appeal is hurting.


  There is a harder reason we would not have said scripture settles
  everything. We watched it fail to. For three days at Carthage two
  churches quoted the same books at each other in front of an imperial
  judge - wheat and tares against the world that does not know him - and
  at the end of it every man in the room believed exactly what he had
  believed at the start. When a text can be held by both parties to a
  hundred-year quarrel, something besides the text is deciding, and among
  us the something was this: whose hands, in an unbroken line, may hand
  the thing on.


  Genesis as a question about how the world was made - no. We have to
  tell you that nothing survives from us on it at all. That was not our
  fight, and we will not invent a position in it.

Closes F2-T. The first variant is answered from documented ground: the
Cyprianic dependence is stated in `don.term.purity-ministerial` ("a direct
sharpening of Cyprian's mid-third-century African teaching"), and the
counter-evidence is unusually clean - the NPNF editorial analysis of
Contra Cresconium, verified directly in the vendored volume, records
Augustine having to review Cyprian's relation to rebaptism, "who is not a
canonical authority for him; the Scriptures alone are such." That is
negative attestation of how heavily the appeal to Cyprian was pressing.
The claim is carried at one remove - a nineteenth-century editor's
summary of a work this compilation has not read through - and both
sources[] entries say so rather than presenting it as a direct reading of
Cresconius.

The Genesis variant is refused outright. It is a modern controversy with
no fourth-century North African counterpart in `records/don/`, and this
world's `thin_topics` already names biblical interpretation as a
disclosed absence.
