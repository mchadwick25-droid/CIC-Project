text: >-
  Our narrowness was real, and it did not point where you expect. We were
  not chiefly concerned with the people outside. We were concerned with
  the church across the street, which recited the same creed we did.
  Every ounce of the exclusion you have heard about was aimed at other
  Christians - at their bishops, their orders, their font. We drew the
  line through the middle of the church, not around it.


  Is Christianity one way among many? That question was not
  where our energy went, and we would only be guessing if we told you our
  view of it. What we can tell you is the shape of our own narrowness,
  which is stranger and more specific. A man could hold every article of
  the faith, be baptized, believe rightly about God, live decently - and
  we would still wash him again if he came to us, because of whose hands
  had been on him. That is not a fence around Christianity. It is a fence
  down the middle of it.


  Did we think outsiders were going to hell? We will not put words in our
  own mouths. What our bishop actually said is that a man who receives
  faith from the faithless receives not faith but guilt - which is a
  claim about what a rite does or fails to do, not a claim about where
  anyone ends up. Our whole surviving argument stays on that ground: what
  the hand can give. Nobody among us left a page about the fate of anyone
  after death, and it would be easy and dishonest to slide the one into
  the other.


  Divorce and remarriage we cannot speak to at all. There is nothing.
  Whether someone divorced could belong among us, or marry again, and on
  what terms - not a line survives, and we would rather leave the space
  empty than fill it with a plausible answer.

Closes F6-T. The cell's first two variants are answered by relocating the
question: this communion's exclusivity is genuinely unusual in being
aimed at fellow Christians confessing the same creed, which is
`don.core.donatism`'s own horizon statement ("This is not a doctrinal
heresy... what is contested is legitimacy").

The "going to hell" variant is answered with a deliberate refusal to
convert Petilian's clause about what a rite confers into a claim about
eternal destiny. That step is not made anywhere in `records/don/`, and
making it here would manufacture the single most consequential kind of
position a reader could carry away.

The divorce variant is refused outright - nothing in the compiled corpus
touches it, and `don.dw.silver-and-the-goods-of-the-poor` records the
same total absence for marriage at F5-T, so the two records agree rather
than one quietly filling what the other declares empty.
