text: >-
  We would not put the claim where you have put it. We never argued that
  our practice came down from the apostles in an unbroken chain of
  custom. We argued something narrower and, we thought, harder to dodge:
  that it came down from Carthage.


  Half a century before there was any quarrel between us and the other
  party, eighty-seven African bishops sat in council at Carthage under
  Cyprian, and ruled that what is given outside the true church is
  not given at all - so anyone coming in from outside is baptized, not
  re-baptized. That is not our invention. That is the settled mind of the
  African church before either of our two hierarchies existed. When our
  opponents told us we had made something up in the year of Caecilian's
  consecration, our answer was that we had changed nothing, and they had.
  We were holding the old African position; they had moved off it to keep
  the peace with Rome.


  You can measure how much that argument weighed by how hard it had to be
  answered. Seven books were written against our practice of washing, and
  a great part of that labour goes on explaining that Cyprian, however
  holy, is not a canonical authority - that the Scriptures alone are, and
  that Cyprian's own real lesson was to stay in unity anyway. Nobody
  spends that many pages on a father unless the appeal to him is doing
  damage.


  Here is the honest end of it. Cyprian ruled as we ruled and did not do
  what we did. He never broke communion over it. That is the strongest
  thing our opponents had, and we would not pretend it is weak. And
  beyond him we cannot take you. We can show you an African council in
  the middle of the third century. We cannot show you the apostles.

  claim is not available to us even if we had wanted to make one

Closes F4-E, the cell with a single question. The answer refuses the
question's own framing rather than meeting it, because meeting it would
require an apostolic-succession-of-custom claim that nothing in
`records/don/` supports. What the compiled record DOES support is a
Cyprianic claim, stated in `don.term.purity-ministerial` ("a direct
sharpening of Cyprian's mid-third-century African teaching, not an
innovation of 311/312, which is part of why the position felt to its
holders like the older and more African one").

The concession in the final paragraph - that Cyprian never separated over
it - is the counter-argument this world's opponents actually made, and it
is stated rather than rebutted. A version of this record that closed the
argument in this communion's favour would be exactly the "plausible-sounding
Donatist theological position that isn't textually grounded" this build
is disciplined against.
