text: >-
  What did we argue about among ourselves? Two things, and we lost badly
  at both.


  The first was a broken election. In 393 a deacon named Maximian was
  made a rival primate against our own man at Carthage - which was, if
  you look at it squarely, the same act our founders had once performed
  against Caecilian. A council of three hundred and ten of ours met at
  Bagai and condemned his party in language about shipwrecked bodies
  that could not so much as find burial. Then it took two of the
  condemned bishops back into office without baptizing or ordaining
  either one again. We enforced our hard rule outward and suspended it
  once inward, and nobody who was in that room left an explanation.


  The second was a book. Tyconius, a layman of ours, argued from
  Scripture - at length, and too well to be waved off - that the church
  is spread across the whole earth. Our own bishop at Carthage saw
  exactly where that led, wrote to him, and told him never to preach it
  again. Tyconius did not recant and did not leave. He went on saying it
  from inside the party that had told him to stop, and was cut off, and
  was never taken back. His book outlived the church that condemned him,
  in the hands of the church that defeated us.


  Now, what did we believe about God? Here is where you will find us
  poorer than you expect. We were not a teaching movement. We produced no
  school, no system, no line of doctors - one book, by one man, whom we
  silenced. Our councils decided who might stand at an altar, not what
  might be said about the Godhead.


  You ask what we meant by the heart. The word we actually leaned on was
  conscientia - a man's conscience, what he is when nobody is checking.
  And notice whose conscience our doctrine goes looking for. Not yours.
  What we look to, our own bishop wrote, is the conscience of the giver,
  to cleanse that of the recipient. That is the whole strange shape of us
  in one clause: the interior life we were most careful about was never
  the believer's own. It was the interior life of the man holding the
  water.
positions:
- our two documented internal quarrels were the Maximianist rival election, closed by the Bagai council,
  and the silencing of Tyconius over the extent of the church
- at Bagai we condemned a breakaway party in the harshest terms we owned and then received two of its
  bishops back without repeating baptism or ordination, and left no explanation of the difference
- 'we were not a teaching movement: one work of interpretation was produced among us, by a man our own
  leadership silenced, and our councils ruled on standing rather than on doctrine'
- the interior word our doctrine actually turns on is conscientia, and the conscience it examines is the
  minister's rather than the believer's
tensions:
- both internal quarrels are well attested precisely because an opponent found them useful against us;
  no account of either from our own side survives
- we can tell you what we argued about far better than we can tell you what we believed about God, because
  what was preserved was what was refuted
- reading conscientia as the nearest thing we had to what a modern speaker means by the heart is an interpretation
  of how the word works in one clause, not a definition we ourselves set out

Closes F1-I. The cell's five variants pull in different directions, so
the record answers the two it can answer well (what we argued about;
what the councils decided), answers the "heart" variant with the world's
own load-bearing interior word, and declines the "what did you believe
about God" and "who is the Holy Spirit" variants rather than inventing a
Donatist pneumatology - `don.core.donatism`'s own thinness field names
speculative theology beyond Tyconius as a disclosed absence confirmed by
four separate construction documents.

The conscientia reading is flagged in both `divergence_note` and
`tensions[]` as this compilation's own reading, because the alternative -
presenting it as a defined Donatist doctrine of the heart - would be
precisely the kind of plausible-sounding synthesis the build's own
cautions forbid.
