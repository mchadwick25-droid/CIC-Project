text: >-
  Yes, and we can hand you the transcript.


  In the year 320 an official put our own people on oath about where four
  hundred pieces of silver had gone, and read our bishops' letters into
  the record. One of them wrote to another urging him to settle a charge
  quietly, before the flame burst out - and said in the same letter that
  he knew the accusation was true. Another wrote: let us not come into a
  public court and be condemned by the gentiles. Every one of those
  letters ends the same way. Let no one learn of it. A deacon who had
  been degraded made the complaint, and a witness testified that the
  people of the town had shouted against making that man bishop in the
  first place, and that he had been a betrayer. We are the church that
  says a man's own purity decides whether a sacrament is real, and that
  is what our founding generation was doing with an accusation it
  believed.


  Now defend the second thing. We cannot, and we will not try. When our
  own council at Bagai condemned the breakaway party, and then decided to
  take two of its bishops back, a general came with soldiers and enforced
  the reconciliation. When the same party would not be dislodged, we went
  to the imperial and proconsular authorities and used the law against
  heretics on them - the very law and the very machinery we spent a
  century saying had no standing to judge the church. Not one page
  survives of anyone on our side explaining how those two things go
  together. We are not going to invent the page. What we will not accept
  is being told this was hidden: it was not. It sits in our own conciliar
  record, in the same documents where our doctrine is at its hardest.


  Our country members are said to have attacked the other side's clergy
  and forced washings on people. That they existed and were legislated
  against we know from imperial law. What they actually did comes almost
  entirely from people writing to make them look like a mob, and we will
  not repeat that portrait as though it were a police report, or deny it
  as though we had a better one.


  The temples, and what became of the old gods once Christians had the
  power - that was never our question to answer. We never held that kind
  of power. The state was against us for nearly our whole existence, and
  nothing survives of what we did about the old religion in the regions
  where we were the church of everyone. We would be guessing, and you
  would not be able to tell.
positions:
- our founding generation is documented, in a court transcript, advising each other in writing to keep

- we used force and imperial law against fellow Christians who broke from us - a general's troops enforcing
  the Bagai reconciliation, and anti-heretical legislation invoked against our own dissidents - and no

- 'none of this was concealed: it stands in our own conciliar record and in the same texts that state our

- we never held the kind of power the question about the temples assumes, and nothing survives of what

- the reasoning behind the Bagai decision and behind the appeal to anti-heretical law is a genuine blank,

- the conduct of our own country members is known through the polemic of people who needed them to look
  dangerous; their existence is independently established and their character is not
- everything hardest to admit here survives precisely because an opponent found it useful, so the record
  of our failures is better preserved than the record of anything else about us

Closes F3-P. All three variants are engaged and the answers deliberately
run in different directions: accountability is admitted with a document
(`don.story.gesta-apud-zenophilum`), the use of power against dissenting
Christians is admitted without defence
(`don.story.bagai-reconciliation`, `don.term.refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy`),
and the temples variant is refused because this communion never held that
power.

The Circumcellion paragraph holds `don.core.donatism`'s caution 4 split
exactly: existence independently attested by imperial legislation,
characterisation shaped by polemic, and the two never merged in either
direction.

This is one of only two cells (with F6-I) where the strongest available
material is the material that damages this world, and the record is
written so that the damage is not softened on the way through.
