THE FULLEST SURVIVING VOICE OF THIS COMMUNION, AND THE MOST MEDIATED.
Augustine quotes him clause by clause across three books in order to
answer each clause, which preserves more of Petilian's actual sentences
than of any other Donatist author - and preserves them selected for
answerability. The selection effect is not a caveat attached to this
source; it is what this source is.

WHICH IS WHY THE 411 TRANSCRIPT CHANGES HIS EVIDENTIARY POSITION. At the
Conference the same man speaks on the record, at length, in exchanges no
opponent curated: every case begins with the persons; before the case is
decided the name they claim is empty, and whoever is found to be a
Christian will hold it at the end; you have chosen to stand in the place
of an accuser. And the refusal of the offered seats, which is his - the
whole speech thanking Marcellinus for the courtesy and declining it,
because Christ deigned to stand before the governor. Two channels, two
distortions, one man - see don.story.conference-of-carthage-411.

UNREAD MATERIAL, NAMED AS A GAP (don.core.donatism cautions item 10).
Monceaux's Tome VI opens with a dedicated chapter on Petilian that has
not been read into this build and could corroborate or complicate
everything above.
