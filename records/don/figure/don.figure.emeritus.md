THE STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT VOICE IN THIS WHOLE RECORD. Nearly everything
else here passed through an opponent's hands before reaching us, and the
specific mechanism was selection for the purpose of refutation. Emeritus
did not. His words were taken down by imperial notaries as he spoke
them, in exchange with named bishops on both sides and with the presiding
tribune, and he then signed the notary's account of them. That does not
make the record unmediated - a notary chose what to set down, the venue
and the rules were the state's, and the proceeding went against us - but
it removes the one distortion this world's evidentiary problem names
everywhere else.

WHAT HE ACTUALLY ARGUES, read directly rather than paraphrased, is
procedure before substance and God above the court: first the day, the
mandate, the persons, the cause, and only then the merits; on you,
truest of judges, nothing else falls but to hold to the truth; judge, in
a Christian matter, because it is God who judges this case; the truth is
being hidden by a great contrivance, when the other side will not name
its own representatives; no one can be called a respondent unless
someone first challenged him. And when Augustine argued the wheat and
the tares, Emeritus answered him verse for verse out of the same books
on what Scripture means by the world - closing, when the answer ran
long, with the observation that a man who runs on that long drowns
himself out.

THE SIGNATURE IS THE CHARACTER. He most often signs the bare formula - I,
Emeritus the bishop, have reviewed this - but repeatedly signs instead
in the qualified form, with his appeal reserved. He put his name to the
record of his own words while refusing, in the same stroke, to concede
the proceeding they were being recorded for. That variation is real and
must not be flattened into one uniform phrase; this world's own Doc_02
review round caught exactly that flattening once already.

NOT YET DONE. His later dialogue with Augustine at Caesarea in 418, the
Gesta cum Emerito, is a different work from the 411 Collatio and has
been neither vendored nor read.
