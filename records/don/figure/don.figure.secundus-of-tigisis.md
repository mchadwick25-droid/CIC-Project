THE MAN WHO PUT THE QUESTION AND THEN COULD NOT FINISH IT. Secundus
matters not as an administrator but because the scene at Cirta is the
one place in the founding record where the traditor question is asked of
our own leaders, out loud, in a room, and is left unresolved by mutual
consent. Whether that was evasion or an honest recognition that nothing
could actually be verified is exactly what the record does not settle -
see don.story.council-of-cirta, which keeps the question open rather
than resolving it toward the comfortable side.

SECUNDUS THE LESS, his brother's son, is named in the record as the man
whose advice was to remit the whole affair to God. He gets no figure
record: he appears once, in one sentence, and everything known about him
is inside that story already.
