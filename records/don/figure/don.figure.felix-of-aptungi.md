THE HINGE OF THE ENTIRE SCHISM IS THIS MAN'S HANDS. If Felix handed over
the scriptures, then on our own doctrine his consecration of Caecilian
gave nothing, Caecilian was never validly bishop of Carthage, and every
ordination downstream of him is empty. That is why the rebaptism
practice, the parallel hierarchy and the whole hundred-and-twenty-year
contest follow from one accusation about one bishop in one small
Proconsular town.

THE INQUIRY WENT AGAINST US AND THAT MUST NOT BE SOFTENED. See
don.story.acta-purgationis-felicis, which states the finding plainly and
carries the torture caveat in the same breath.

THREE DIFFERENT FELIXES APPEAR ACROSS THIS INVENTORY'S FOUNDING-ERA
STORIES, and conflating any two of them inverts the argument:
  - Felix of Aptungi, this record - Caecilian's consecrator, the accused.
  - The deacon Felix who hid in Mensurius's house at Carthage during the
    persecution (don.story.lucilla-affair) - a different man, no
    connection to the consecration.
  - Felix of Rotarium, one of the three unaccused bishops asked for
    judgement at the Council of Cirta (don.story.council-of-cirta).
None of the three has been shown to be either of the others, and this
record asserts no identification among them.
