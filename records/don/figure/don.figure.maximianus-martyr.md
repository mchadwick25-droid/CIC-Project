HOMONYM, AND THE ONE THIS WORLD'S OWN CAUTIONS NAME FIRST. THIS
Maximianus is a martyr of the Macarian repression, killed about 347 or
348 and commemorated as one of our own dead. He is NOT
don.figure.maximian-of-cebarsussi, the deacon deposed in 393 and made a
rival primate against Primian, whose schism don.story.bagai-reconciliation
concerns. The two records are deliberately kept separate, and each
states the distinction on its own face, precisely so that a retrieval
landing on either cannot silently collapse them. The scholarly name form
above carries the disambiguation inside the name itself, for the same
reason.

WHAT THE LETTER CLAIMS AND WHAT IT DOES NOT. That he was beaten, that he
was thrown into the sea weighted with jars of sand so the body could not
be venerated, and that the congregation recovered and buried him, are
the narrative core. The omen in the wine and the dream of fighting the
emperor are Macrobius's own account of what those events meant - offered
to a congregation being told, in the letter's closing turn, that the
same may be asked of any of them.
