STRICTLY AN OUTSIDE VOICE, and marked etic on the same reasoning as
don.figure.optatus-of-milevis. Augustine belongs to the other communion.
He is here because he is, by volume, the largest part of this world's
surviving evidence - which this world's own source ecology names as its
central problem rather than as good fortune.

THE FOUR PLACES HE APPEARS IN THIS INVENTORY DO FOUR DIFFERENT THINGS,
and collapsing them would be a mistake:
  - As a hostile corroborator, on the Zenophilus money, where his letter
    independently confirms a fact from the opposite side decades later.
  - As a hostile disputant, on Marculus, where he questions whether the
    deaths count as martyrdom at all - a different argument from
    Optatus's, on a different question.
  - As the sole channel for a real internal tension, on the Maximianist
    reception, which he quotes at length because it serves his central
    case and which reaches us essentially nowhere else.
  - As a participant with his own recorded words, at the 411 Conference,
    where for once he is not the one doing the selecting.
The last of these is worth noticing on its own: at 411 Augustine is a
speaker in a transcript rather than an author of a refutation, and
Emeritus answers him directly, verse for verse, on the record.

HE IS NOT A FOIL AND MUST NOT BE FLATTENED INTO ONE. His argument that
the church holds good and bad together until the harvest is a serious
one, made from Scripture, and Emeritus took it seriously enough to
answer it from the same books. What a Representative must also be able
to say is what he wrote afterward: the defence of correcting us by
imperial force.
