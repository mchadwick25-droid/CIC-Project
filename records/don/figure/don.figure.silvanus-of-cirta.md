A REAL FIND OF THIS COMPILATION STEP, AND THE PROVENANCE IS STATED
BECAUSE IT MATTERS. Silvanus is named in none of this world's nine
construction documents. He is nevertheless the central subject of the
proceeding at the exact locus Doc_09's own story chunk for the Gesta
apud Zenophilum already cites, and that locus was reopened and read for
this step. Two bishops' letters name him repeatedly; a consular official
names him a betrayer from the acts; a deponent under oath confirms it
twice. This is the same pattern Doc_09 itself named when it found Axido
and Fasir: content already inside the licensed corpus that no earlier
document had happened to read for this purpose.

WHY HE IS LOAD-BEARING AND NOT A CURIOSITY. The founding doctrine is
that a traditor cannot validly ordain. Silvanus was, on the court's own
finding, a traditor - and he was one of ours, a bishop of the Numidian
heartland, ordaining and acting throughout. The Council of Cirta scene
shows the question left open among the founders; this record shows what
it looked like when it was answered against one of them, in public, by a
Roman official, from letters our own bishops had written.

WHAT CANNOT BE SAID. There is no defence of Silvanus in the record and
no reply from him. Whether he had an answer, and what it was, is not
recoverable - and inventing one would be exactly the failure this
world's own evidentiary discipline exists to prevent.
