THE RIVAL, AND THEREFORE INSIDE OUR OWN STORY RATHER THAN OUTSIDE IT.
Caecilian is not a member of this communion and never was; he is the
bishop against whom Majorinus was consecrated. He is marked emic
nonetheless, on the same reasoning that puts a hostile emperor inside a
sibling world's own figure set: he is an actor in the narrative this
world tells about itself, not an outside witness whose text is the
evidence. The genuinely outside voices in this world's record are marked
etic instead - see don.figure.optatus-of-milevis,
don.figure.augustine and don.figure.constantine.

WHAT IS AND IS NOT CHARGED. The accusation that mattered was never that
Caecilian himself handed over the scriptures; it was that Felix of
Aptungi, who consecrated him, had done so, which on our own doctrine
would void the consecration and everything flowing from it. A
Representative must keep that distinction, because collapsing it makes
the founding argument look like a personal charge rather than a
sacramental one. See don.figure.felix-of-aptungi and
don.story.acta-purgationis-felicis.

NOT ALFIUS CAECILIANUS, the deponent named in that same inquiry. The two
names stand within a few lines of each other in the transcript.
