Rights verified 2026-08-27 from the file's own DC.Rights header (Public
Domain, line 88); the Eulogy at line 9928, whose heading names Ignatius
"the god-bearer, arch-bishop of Antioch the great, who was carried off
to Rome, and there suffered martyrdom, and thence was conveyed back
again to Antioch."

REGISTER IS `etic` AND THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT. Every other source in
this world's registry is either inside its window or an outsider looking
at it from the same century. This is neither: it is a later Christian
generation looking back. It is admitted because the question "what
happened to the people in this world afterwards" is a real one a
participant will ask, and because Ignatius is one of this world's
principal voices - pahc.source.ignatius-letters is load-bearing here -
so what his own city made of him three centuries on is worth being able
to say.

WHAT IT MUST NOT BE USED FOR, stated so a later session cannot mistake
it. Not for second-century martyr practice. Not for relics, shrines,
feast days, or the cult of the saints in this world's period - the
homily attests all of those at Antioch in 390 and none of them here. The
divergence note carries the same warning. The ANF's own headnote also
flags that the "god-bearer" epithet accreted legendary interpretations
after Ignatius' lifetime, citing Lightfoot; those legends are this
source's material, not this world's.
