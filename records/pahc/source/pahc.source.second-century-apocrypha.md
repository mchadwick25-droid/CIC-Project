Rights are verified from the file's own DC.Rights header (Public
Domain). The section's own Introductory Notice, by M. B. Riddle, is at
line 35806 and states the translation is Walker's from Tischendorf, that
none of these writings ever obtained general recognition, and that only
"a few of the Apocryphal Gospels are of comparatively early origin."

WHAT THIS IS FOR. Not for what happened - none of it is history - but
for what this world's people were writing and reading. A world whose
literate remains are letters, church order and apology gives a
participant the impression that the second century read only sober
things. It did not. It also produced Mary's childhood in the Temple, a
boy Jesus making sparrows out of clay, and a woman who baptised herself
and preached.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT IMPORTED, AND WHY. The corpus map assigns ten
works from this volume's Apocrypha section to this world, and only three
are taken. The Acts of Philip, of Peter and Paul, of Andrew and
Matthias, of Peter and Andrew, and of John the Theologian are left
where they belong, in
`apocryphal-and-pseudepigraphal-literature`. THAT ENTRY WAS MINTED
PRECISELY TO STOP THIS: its own record says thirteen works "had been
filed by the century they DEPICT rather than the century they were
written in," and its window was widened to c. 900 to hold them honestly.
Importing them into a world that ends at 200 because their characters
are apostles would re-commit the error the entry exists to correct.
The three kept here are the ones with a broad second-century case,
and the divergence note above says how weak even that is.

THE TESTAMENTS OF THE TWELVE PATRIARCHS AND THE REVELATION OF MOSES,
also in this volume and also assigned to this world, are not here
either: the map already carries them as `transmission` - Jewish works
that survive because Christian scribes went on copying them - which is
the correct relation and a different one from this record's.
