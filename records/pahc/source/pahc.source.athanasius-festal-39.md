IT CORRECTS THIS WORLD ON ITS OWN CLAIM, which is the reason it was worth
opening rather than declining on the date boundary.

pahc.force.selective-canonization says Hermas was "cited as scripture by
three major later fathers, then EXCLUDED by Athanasius". Read the letter.
Athanasius does not exclude the Shepherd. He puts it, with the Teaching
of the Apostles - the Didache, this world's other lost text - into a named
middle class: books "not indeed included in the Canon, but appointed by
the Fathers to be read by those who newly join us, and who wish for
instruction in the word of godliness", listed beside the Wisdom of
Solomon, Sirach, Esther, Judith and Tobit.

That is a sharper and more interesting fact than exclusion. The two texts
this world most depends on and most nearly lost were not thrown out; they
were reassigned - from scripture to catechesis, from what the church
reads to what beginners are given. A book you hand to newcomers is a book
whose survival now depends on whether anyone keeps copying it for them.
The force record's own account of how this world's material became thin
is strengthened by the correction, not weakened, and the wording is fixed
rather than left standing.

A SECOND USE, for pahc.source.second-century-apocrypha. The same letter
carries the ancient statement of the problem that source record has to
navigate: apocryphal writings are "an invention of heretics, who write
them when they choose, bestowing upon them their approbation, and
assigning to them a date, that so, using them as ancient writings, they
may find occasion to lead astray the simple." That is a fourth-century
bishop describing pseudepigraphy as a deliberate technique, including the
back-dating. Whether he is right about any particular text is a separate
question this record does not touch.

WHAT THIS RECORD DOES NOT LICENSE: the rest of the volume. Athanasius'
Orations against the Arians, the De Incarnatione and the Life of Antony
are all here and none of them is this world's business.
