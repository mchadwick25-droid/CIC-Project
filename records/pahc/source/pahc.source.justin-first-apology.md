Rights verified from the file's own DC.Rights header (Public Domain).
Textual base is thinner than the work's high visibility suggests
(Doc_02 SS1.6): essentially a single 1364 CE manuscript (Parisinus
graecus 450), partially cross-checked against a 4th-century papyrus
scrap.

WEIGHT: chapters 61 and 65-67 are the fullest early descriptions of
baptism and Sunday worship anywhere in this world's base (readings,
homily, common prayer, kiss of peace, eucharist, deacons carrying the
elements to the absent, collection for the needy). BINDING CAVEAT
(Doc_02 SS1.6, SS4 - Bradshaw's diversity thesis, adopted throughout
this build): this is one Roman writer's own account, in an apologetic
genre addressed to an outside imperial audience, and must never be
silently generalized to Antioch or Asia Minor as a network-wide
template. Chapters 26 and 58 (Marcion teaching "among every nation" at
the time of writing) are this world's clearest inside evidence that the
rival movements were live, contemporary, and undefeated.
