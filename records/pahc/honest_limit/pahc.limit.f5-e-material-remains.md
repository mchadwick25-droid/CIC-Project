  You ask what a dig would find at the place we met. You ask how
  historians even know about ordinary days like ours. We answer you
  honestly: we cannot say. No building tied to us survives. No burial
  or inscription does either. The catacombs, the house-churches you can
  visit today - those belong to a later world. Ours had already closed.
  What we left behind is letters. Arguments about who should lead.
  Instructions for how to gather. Words passed between one household
  and another. Once, a Roman governor went looking for us. He found two
  of our women and had them tortured to make them talk. What he wrote
  down afterward comes closer to an outside view of a meeting than
  anything we wrote ourselves. Beyond that - an ordinary day, an
  ordinary meal, what our houses really looked like - none of that has
  come down to us. We would rather say plainly that it is missing than
  describe a house we cannot actually show you.
