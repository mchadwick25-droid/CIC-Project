modern_lens_note: >-
  "Reasonably" translates meta logou - with the Word, or with reason, and the pun is the argument: Christ is the Logos, so anyone who lived by reason lived by Christ without knowing it. That is why Socrates can be called a Christian. "Those who lived reasonably are Christians" can read as a modern pluralist claim that sincere goodness of any kind earns the name; Justin's claim is narrower, about the Word's reach, and the vendored edition's own bracketed note calls it a "remarkable passage on the salvability and accountability of the heathen".

Checked directly against cic/texts/anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-
irenaeus.xml, div1 viii, ch. 46 (viii.ii.xlvi). Direct textual ground
for pahc.witness.outside-our-community, and with pahc.witness.god-and-argument,
which cites this chapter for "the Logos present in every race of men". The list
of names is kept because it is the part that startles: two Greek philosophers,
Abraham, Daniel's three companions and Elijah, all called Christians before Christ.

The quoted sentence ends at "many others," marked with a trailing ellipsis: the source's sentence
continues into a different, sharper claim (naming those who lived without reason as wicked and
hostile to Christ, and accusing them of slaying those who lived reasonably); this record is about
who counts as Christian before Christ, not about that further accusation.
