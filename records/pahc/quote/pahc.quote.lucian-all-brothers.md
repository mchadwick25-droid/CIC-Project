text: >-
  You see, these misguided creatures start with the general conviction that they are immortal for

  brothers, from the moment that they are converted, and deny the gods of Greece, and worship the
  crucified sage, and live after his laws. All this they take quite on trust, with the result that
  they despise all worldly goods alike, regarding them merely as common property.

modern_lens_note: >-
  "Their original lawgiver" and "the crucified sage" are Lucian's way of naming Jesus without
  using the name - the framing of a satirist who files this movement under the philosophical
  schools he mocks generally. "Take quite on trust" is his contempt for faith, and a modern reader
  should hear it as such rather than as a neutral description of how belief worked. What survives
  his contempt is a list: immortality, contempt of death, universal brotherhood on conversion,
  refusal of the gods of Greece, and property held loosely. An outsider with no stake got that far
  by about 165.
