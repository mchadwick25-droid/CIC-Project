description: "HISTORICAL EVENT: Ignatius's letters (Romans 4, 'food for wild beasts') and the Martyrdom of Polycarp (chapter 18) are, within this world's own base, exactly two data points. Both come from Syria and Asia Minor. WHAT THE TWO VOICES SAY: Ignatius writes already under guard, bound to a band of soldiers on the road to Rome. He asks the Roman church not to hinder his death. He wants to be ground like wheat by the beasts, not spared. He calls this imitating the passion of Christ (Romans 4 and 6). At Smyrna, the Martyrdom of Polycarp says the church gathered his bones, which it held 'more precious than the most exquisite jewels.' It laid them in a fitting place. It hoped, 'as opportunity is allowed us,' to keep the anniversary of his martyrdom there. That is a stated intention, not a record of yearly gatherings. The church calls the anniversary a memorial for those who died and a preparation for those who will follow in their steps. FORMATION IMPACT: this force grows with the legal danger of the time. It is the meaning that Ignatius and the Smyrna church made in answer to that danger. It begins to organize time around a martyr's death, though this world was only starting to hand that practice on."
