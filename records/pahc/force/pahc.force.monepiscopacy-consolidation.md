description: "HISTORICAL EVENT: rule by a single bishop appears earliest in Antioch and western Asia Minor, through Ignatius. It appears latest in Rome. Peter Lampe reads the Roman evidence as fractionated. On his account Rome may have had no single bishop before Victor I (189-199 CE). By c. 200, rule by one bishop is the common pattern even in Rome. That is a real shift away from this world's shared authority in formation. WHAT THE TEXTS SHOW: no source in this world's registry records the moment Rome settled on one bishop. This record does not guess how a member felt about it. The nearest witness in the period is Irenaeus. He writes to Victor of Rome, in Eusebius (Church History 5.24.14). He calls earlier Roman leaders presbyters who 'presided over the church' before Soter. He names them one at a time, from Xystus to Anicetus. In Against Heresies 3.3.3 he lists the same men in the line of the episcopate. So his wording does not settle whether Rome had one leader or a council. A slow, uneven fade of the plural-leader model is a modern reconstruction by Lampe and others. These texts do not record it. FORMATION IMPACT: this is the inner end of this world's typical form. A settled single-bishop answer ends the tension that authority consolidation kept open. This world could not outlast the settling of its own authority question."
