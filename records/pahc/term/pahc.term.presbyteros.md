plain_meaning: 'The elders who share the governing of a community. In some churches they are a
  council holding the whole trust themselves; in others they gather around one overseer.'
world_word: presbyteros

senses:
  informational: 'In Rome, when the church there wrote to Corinth over a quarrel, it spoke of
    presbyters removed from office - not of a bishop deposed - and nothing in the letter suggests a
    single presiding figure was missing. In Antioch and Asia Minor the presbyters gather around a
    bishop, supporting his oversight rather than holding it in common. The same word carries both
    arrangements.'
  evidential: 'The one man who carries both descriptions at once is Polycarp: addressed by Ignatius
    as Smyrna''s bishop, he names himself in his own letter only with ''the presbyters.'' Whether
    that is humility inside a secured office or evidence the single-bishop pattern had not yet been
    adopted locally - both readings stay live; the record does not force one.'
  personal: 'For a member, the elders were the faces of continuity - the ones who had served
    blamelessly for years, whose removal at Corinth struck another church as serious enough to
    write a long letter about.'
  translational: 'Modern ''presbyter'' or ''elder'' arrives carrying a denomination''s settled
    polity. Here the word''s meaning is still being shaped: council-rule in one region,
    bishop-support in another, and no umpire between them.'
quick_meaning: The elders who govern a community - as a council on their own in some churches, around a single overseer in others.
