claim: "Ignatius of Antioch's seven letters, in their middle (shorter) recension, are authentic - written by a bishop of Antioch under armed guard en route to execution in Rome, during the reign of Trajan, conventionally dated c. 107-117 CE."
held_against:
- "Barnes and Foster argue for a redating to the 130s-140s CE, on the grounds that the traditional Trajanic date rests on weak external synchronisms rather than secure internal evidence within the letters themselves."
- "Huebner and Lechner (the 'Munich school') argue the corpus is pseudepigraphic altogether - composed c. 160-180 CE, with Roman rather than Antiochene provenance, and so not a genuine early-2nd-century witness from Antioch/Asia Minor at all."
- "The middle recension's own authenticity was itself a two-century critical dispute before any of this (Ussher 1644/Vossius 1646/Pearson 1672 arguing for authenticity against Daille 1666's rejection; Cureton's 1843-45 case for a still-shorter Syriac recension against Zahn 1873/Lightfoot 1885-89), meaning even the traditional position is a defended scholarly consensus, not an unbroken inheritance."
concedes: "If either the redating or the pseudepigraphic position is correct, Strand A's evidentiary base - the monarchical-bishop program, the martyrdom-longing self-presentation, and the anti-docetic boundary-drawing that together supply roughly half this world's most vivid quotable material - is not a contemporaneous snapshot of Antioch/Asia Minor practice within this world's own 70-200 CE window. Three of this world's gravities (authority-consolidation's Strand A content, martyrdom-meaning, boundary-drawing) and every force record built from the same corpus would need reconsidering, not merely re-labeling with a later date. This build proceeds on the traditional dating as the working default, per the approved Doc_01/Doc_02 scoping, while carrying this three-way split forward at full strength in every downstream record that cites this source."
