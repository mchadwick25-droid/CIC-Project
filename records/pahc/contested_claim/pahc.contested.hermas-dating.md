claim: "The Shepherd of Hermas was composed at Rome across roughly 90-150 CE, in multiple compositional stages, by a figure named Hermas who identifies himself as both narrator and recipient of the work's own visions."
held_against:
- "Brox, Leutzsch, and Osiek's composite reading holds that no single date can be assigned to the whole work - different sections (the Visions, the Mandates, the Similitudes) plausibly reflect different compositional moments across a six-decade span."
- "The traditional mid-2nd-century date rests partly on the Muratorian Fragment's own claim that Hermas wrote 'very recently, in our times,' while his brother Pius occupied the episcopal chair - but Sundberg and Hahneman argue the Muratorian Fragment itself is a 4th-century Eastern document, not a late-2nd-century Roman one, which would undercut this synchronism as evidence for Hermas's own date entirely."
concedes: "If Hermas is genuinely composite across nine decades, no single moment of 'the world Hermas describes' exists - different visions may reflect different states of the Roman community's own presbyteral college and penitential practice at different times, and this record does not flatten that into one snapshot. Downstream records (authority-consolidation's Strand B content, translocal-network) carry the c. 90-150 range as a working default while disclosing that the range itself, and even the method for establishing it, are both actively contested."
