claim: "This world's own communities are best understood as organized into two coherent regional 'strands' - Strand A (Antioch/Asia Minor, moving toward a single bishop) and Strand B (Rome, holding a plural presbyteral college) - each internally consistent and mutually opposed on the authority question."
held_against:
- "The underlying primary-voice evidence supports only two DATA POINTS for authority structure (Ignatius's corpus for one pattern; 1 Clement and Hermas for the other), not a demonstrated network-wide regional consensus on either side - Antioch/Asia Minor's own 'strand' rests overwhelmingly on Ignatius alone, and no third independent Asia Minor voice confirms his program is representative even of his own claimed region."
- "The Didache's own transitional instruction to 'appoint bishops and deacons for yourselves' (15:1) does not cleanly sort into either strand - it is a third, Syrian, single-community data point that this build's own two-strand frame does not have a clean place for."
- "Packaging the evidence as two OPPOSED strands risks implying a settledness and internal coherence within each strand that the evidence itself does not establish - Polycarp himself is addressed as bishop by Ignatius while identifying himself, in his own letter, only alongside 'the presbyters,' suggesting even Strand A's own internal picture may not have been as settled as the two-strand frame can make it look."
concedes: "The two-strand frame is this build's own working interpretive packaging of real, Documented underlying evidence - useful for holding two demonstrably different, geographically-differentiated answers to the same question in view at once, but a provisional analytical tool, not a found fact this world's own voices themselves articulate. No primary voice in this world's evidentiary base uses 'Strand A'/'Strand B' language or otherwise names two opposed camps; the language belongs to this build, not to the sources."
