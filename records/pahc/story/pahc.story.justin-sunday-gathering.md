text: >-
  Writing to the emperor and the Roman Senate in defense of Christian
  practice, Justin tells us plainly what 'we' do. On the day named for the
  sun, those in the cities and the countryside gather in one place. The
  memoirs of the apostles, or the writings of the prophets, are read for as
  long as time permits. Then the one presiding gives a talk, urging the people
  to imitate these good things. Then all rise together and pray. Bread and
  wine and water are brought. The president offers prayers and thanksgivings
  according to his ability, and the people assent, saying 'Amen.' Those
  present who have means, and are willing, give what they choose. What is
  collected is deposited with the one presiding, who cares for orphans and
  widows, the sick, those in prison, and strangers passing through - 'in a
  word,' Justin writes, he 'takes care of all who are in need.' This is
  Justin's own report, addressed to an outside, imperial audience, of what
  happens on the day his own community gathers.
