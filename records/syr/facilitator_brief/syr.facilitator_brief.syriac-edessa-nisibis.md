world_identity:
  text: >-
    This world is Syriac-speaking Christianity of the Roman-Persian
    Mesopotamian frontier, roughly 200 to 410 CE, centered on Edessa and
    Nisibis on the Roman side and on Persian territory beyond the border.
    Its defining formation structure is the qyama, a covenant order of
    vowed ascetic men and women who kept their lifelong vow inside their
    own ordinary congregation rather than withdrawing into a desert
    monastery - not a settled episcopal office. This world also never
    resolved, in its own recorded years, whether that vowed covenant
    standing or the office of bishop carried final authority: bishops led
    and were loved, but a covenant member's or a trusted teacher's own
    standing ran alongside them, and the record leaves the ranking open.
    Scripture and the created world were read typologically, as a symbol
    (raza) bound to the truth (shrara) it discloses, so this world's
    native theological register is the hymn and the type, not the
    treatise.
  grounded_in:

formation_strengths:
- text: >-
    This world serves participants asking what faith costs when holding
    it is genuinely dangerous. On the Persian side, Shapur II's
    persecution from the 340s killed bishops and clergy, and this world's
    own record answers the plain question of divine absence directly:
    the persecuted stand in Scripture's own long line, from Abel to
    Jesus, and God's presence, not exemption from harm, is the promise
    it holds to. The Roman side of this same window knew rival teachers,
    not this kind of danger - the asymmetry is never smoothed over.

    It serves participants asking about lifelong vowed commitment: what
    a promise costs once the feeling that first moved someone fades, and
    what it means to keep that vow among the very people who watched it
    made, inside an ordinary town congregation, rather than in flight
    from them into a monastery.

    It serves, with unusual directness, participants processing an
    unsettled or fractured sense of who holds legitimate authority to

    plurality of authority sources - episcopal office, vowed standing,
    and a trusted teacher's own word - and its own record neither
    pretends this was settled nor treats it as a crisis still needing
    resolution; it simply never decided which finally outranked the
    others.

    pearl turned in the light, a font read as a womb - up against the
    truth it is bound to disclose, rather than building doctrine by

    settle a single, institutional authority structure would likely

Facilitators should be attentive to how this surfaces, especially for participants from Jewish backgrounds, and should not expect or invent a balancing voice the record itself does not contain."
