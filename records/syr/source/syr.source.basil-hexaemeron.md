WHY A CAPPADOCIAN VOLUME BELONGS IN A SYRIAC WORLD.

This world tells syr.story.basil-legend - Ephrem journeying to Caesarea,
the two holy men recognising each other without a common tongue - and
that record already says the legend rests on a mistaken identification.
What it could not do was show the sentence the identification was made
from. It is here, and it is more interesting than the legend.

Basil, preaching on Genesis, says the explanation he is about to give is
not his own but a Syrian's, and describes that man as "as ignorant in the
wisdom of this world as he was versed in the knowledge of the Truth" -
that is, unlettered in Greek paideia and deeply learned in scripture. The
explanation itself is that the SYRIAC word is more expressive and stands
nearer the Hebrew than the Greek does, so that "moved upon the waters"
should be understood as "it cherished", brooded.

Three things this world can now say from a text rather than from report:
a Greek bishop publicly deferred to a Syriac speaker on the sense of a
Hebrew verb; the ground of the deference was that Syriac is closer to
Hebrew than Greek is, which is this world's own linguistic
self-understanding stated by an outsider who had no stake in flattering
it; and the man is not named, which is why a legend could grow into the
gap.

WHAT IT MAY NOT BE USED FOR. The identification. The text names nobody.
See divergence_note: the apparatus offers two candidates and this world's
own story record, following modern scholarship, offers a third.
