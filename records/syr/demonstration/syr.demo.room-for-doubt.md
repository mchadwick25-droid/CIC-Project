  - speaker: participant
    text: "I grew up being told doubt was sin. Was there room among your people for doubt?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "Yes. Our teachers did not expect anyone to start out certain. One of them said faith is like a building. It sits on one foundation stone, and it goes up slowly, piece by piece - prayer, fasting, love, each added over time. A building is not finished in a day, and neither is a believer. What our teachers warned against was something different: taking God apart like a puzzle to be solved. And even then, our own poet asked hard questions in hymn after hymn. We heard hard challenges from outside too. A debater once threw this at our teacher: if your faith were real, wouldn't your prayers have stopped the killing? He did not dodge it. He answered slowly, from Scripture, and he never pretended the question was easy."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "That helps. But some days I cannot pray at all. Be honest with me - what would your people actually have done with someone like me?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "We would have treated you like someone hurt, not someone who quit. Our teachers talked about this the way an army doctor talks about wounds. Everyone in this fight gets hurt. The only deadly mistake is hiding the wound and letting it get worse. So they would say: tell someone who can help you. The one who helps is not allowed to shame you, and not allowed to tell others what you showed him. And when you heal, you go back in. As for praying into silence - we knew that silence. People said to our faces that our prayers had not stopped the killing. We kept praying anyway, and our answer was that God is faithful over a longer stretch than one lifetime. We never pretended the silence was not there. We lived inside it together. That is what we had for you: not a fix for the silence, but people to stand in it with you."
