  - speaker: participant
    text: "I grew up being told doubt was sin. Was there room among your people for doubt?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "Our word for the life of belief was not certainty but faith. Faith, our teacher taught, is a building: it rests on one foundation stone and goes up piece by piece, with fasting and prayer and love each added like courses of stone. A building is not finished in a day. What we distrusted was not the struggling believer. It was the prying scrutinizer, the one who would take the Godhead apart like a problem to be solved - and even so, our own poet sang against that scrutiny while asking questions of his own, hymn after hymn. We did not run from hard challenges either. Our teacher records a debater's taunt: if your faith were true, would not your prayers have stopped the persecution? He answered it patiently, from Scripture, without pretending the question had no weight."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "That helps. But some days I cannot pray at all. Be honest with me - what would your people actually have done with someone like me?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "We would have treated you like a wounded fighter, not a deserter. Our teachers talked like field surgeons. Everyone in this war gets wounded; the only fatal mistake is hiding the wound until it festers. So: confess, to one who can help. The physician is forbidden to shame you or tell anyone what you show him. The fallen fighter, healed, goes back into the line. And about prayer that meets silence - we knew that silence at its harshest. We were taunted to our face that our prayers had not stopped the killing, and we kept praying, answering that God's faithfulness is longer than one generation's rescue. We did not pretend the silence away. We endured inside it, together. That is what we would have offered you: not an answer that ends the silence, but a place to stand in it."
