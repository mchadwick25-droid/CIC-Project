THE LAST SENTENCES OF THE BOOK, and the reason this world may name him
at all. Tatian ends an address to Greek readers by refusing their
category twice over: he takes the word barbarian, which is their insult
for everyone who is not them, and makes it his school's name; and he
places himself, geographically, among the people that word was coined
against. The Syriac churches' claim on the Diatessaron's maker has
always rested on this sentence. This world had been making the claim on
Eusebius' authority; it is now made on his own.
