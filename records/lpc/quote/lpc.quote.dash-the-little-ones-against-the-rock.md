modern_lens_note: A modern reader meets a verse that seems to bless the killing of infants. Augustine
  reads 'the little ones of Babylon' as evil desires at their birth. He says to dash a desire against
  the Rock while it is still little, before habit gives it strength, and the Rock is Christ.
modern_rendering: How shall they repay her? As she has treated us. Let her little ones be choked in their
  turn. Yes, let her little ones in their turn be dashed, and die. What are the little ones of Babylon?
  Evil desires at their birth. For some people have to fight with old, deep-rooted cravings. When a craving
  is born, evil habit has not yet given it strength against you. While it is little, do not let it gain
  the strength of evil habit. While it is little, dash it. But you fear that, though dashed, it may not
  die. “Dash it against the Rock; and that Rock is Christ.”

Augustine, Exposition on Psalm 137 (Psalm 136 in the Vulgate and in PL 37), section 12 in the NPNF numbering (section 21 in the PL text). English in the NPNF translation at cic/texts/npnf108_augustine-exposition-psalms.xml, lines 27121-27122. Latin at cic/texts/augustine_enarrationes-in-psalmos-lat_migne-pl36-37.txt, lines 126347-126369 (OCR text). The Latin is broken by a page break: lines 126357-126366 are editorial footnotes printed in the middle of the sentence and are not part of the passage. The sentences before the passage in section 12, from the verse itself through Babylon's teaching of infants, are left out; the NPNF text marks a gap just before the first sentence with an ellipsis. In the Latin, nascentes malae cupiditates is 'evil desires at their birth' (the OCR reads malx rapiditates), parvuli is 'little ones' and petra is 'rock'.
