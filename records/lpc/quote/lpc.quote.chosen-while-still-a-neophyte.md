text: For the proof of his good works I think that this one thing is enough, that by the judgment of God
  and the favour of the people, he was chosen to the office of the priesthood and the degree of the episcopate
  while still a neophyte, and, as it was considered, a novice. Although still in the early days of his
  faith, and in the untaught season of his spiritual life, a generous disposition so shone forth in him,
  that although not yet resplendent with the glitter of office, but only of hope, he gave promise of entire
  trustworthiness for the priesthood that was coming upon him.

modern_lens_note: 'A modern listener may hear ''priesthood'' as the office of a parish priest. Pontius''s
  word, sacerdotium, here names the bishop''s own office, which is why the rendering says ''the sacred
  office''. A neophyte is someone newly baptized. Pontius presses the point: Cyprian was chosen before
  he had any training, on the strength of what people could already see in him.'
modern_rendering: I think this one fact is enough to prove his good works. By the judgment of God and
  the favour of the people, he was chosen for the sacred office and the rank of bishop. He was still newly
  baptized and, as people thought, a beginner. He was still in the first days of his faith, in the untaught
  season of his spiritual life. Yet a noble nature shone out in him. He did not yet shine with the glow
  of office, only with the glow of hope. Even so, he gave every promise that he could be trusted with
  the sacred office coming to him.
