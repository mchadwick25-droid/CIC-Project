Augustine, Sermon 6 in the NPNF numbering (56 in the Maurist numbering), section 7. English in the NPNF translation at cic/texts/npnf106_augustine-sermon-mount-harmony-gospels-homilies.xml, lines 10700-10701. Latin at cic/texts/augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38.txt, lines 26351-26356 (OCR text; the sermon starts at line 26159). The lines before the passage (NPNF lines 10693-10700) set out that God's will is done in the saved and in the lost; the passage opens in the middle of that argument ('Why do I say then'). In the Latin, a te and in te are exact and the whole argument rests on that pair; Augustine's form has no sicut ('as') in 'in coelo et in terra'. The NPNF English follows the Latin closely throughout, with nothing added or narrowed. The longest sentence is the opening question, made of two quoted petitions, and cannot be split without losing the contrast. The sermon carries the gravity lpc.gravity.grace-and-human-incapacity as catechesis on prayer.
