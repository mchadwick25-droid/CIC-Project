claim_guards:
- The passage names Judas as an example; it does not say that Judas baptized anyone, only that if he did,
  it would still be Christ who baptizes.
- It does not name the Donatists or any dispute, and the word 'valid' does not appear; the passage rests
  on John 1:33 and the dove.
- It speaks only of whose baptism it is; it does not say the minister's own sin is of no account to the
  minister. Section 15, outside it, says baptism held apart from the Church's unity harms the one who
  receives it; that is about the recipient, not the minister, and must not be cited for the minister.

Augustine, Tractate 6 on the Gospel of John (NPNF Tractate VI), section 7. English in the NPNF translation at cic/texts/npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml, lines 1420-1421. Latin at cic/texts/augustine_in-ioannis-evangelium-tractatus-lat_migne-garnier1902-t3-pt2.txt, lines 7937-7945 (OCR text). The passage is the end of section 7. The sentences before it, on what John learned from the dove and on how he already knew the Lord, are left out. In the Latin the closing sentence reads Petrus baptizet, hic est qui baptizat; Paulus baptizet, hic est qui baptizat; Judas baptizet, hic est qui baptizat.
