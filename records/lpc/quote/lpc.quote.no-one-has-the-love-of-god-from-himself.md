text: Finally, when he had said, “The love of God hath been shed abroad in our hearts;” that no man might
  think that he hath this love of God of himself, he added immediately, “By the Holy Spirit, who hath
  been given to us.” Therefore, that thou mayest love God, let God dwell in thee, and love Himself in
  thee, that is, to His love let Him move thee, enkindle, enlighten, arouse thee.

modern_lens_note: A modern listener may hear 'Finally' as the last point in a list and 'shed abroad' as
  spilled. The Latin, Denique, is 'indeed', and diffusa est is 'poured out'. Augustine reads Paul's words
  so that no one thinks his love for God comes from himself, and asks that God live in the believer, love
  Himself through him, and move him to love.
modern_rendering: Indeed, he had said, “The love of God has been poured out in our hearts.” Then he added

  his love for God came from himself. So if you are to love God, let God live in you and love Himself
  through you. That is, let Him move you to His love. Let Him kindle you, give you light and stir you
  up.

English in the NPNF translation at cic/texts/npnf106_augustine-sermon-mount-harmony-gospels-homilies.xml, line 19486. Latin at cic/texts/augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38.txt, lines 50082-50088 (OCR text; the sermon starts at line 49959). The line begins right after a short scripture-reference note (2 Cor. iv. 7, line 19485) that belongs to the earlier sentence, not this one. The Latin starts mid-line at PL 38 line 50082 after (II Cor. iv, 7), and carries the reference (Rom. v, 5) that the English line omits. In the Latin, Denique is 'indeed, in short', a se sibi esse quod diligit Deum is that his loving God comes from himself, habitet is 'live', and accendat, illuminet and excitet are 'set alight, give light, rouse'.
