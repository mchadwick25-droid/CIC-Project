  text: What a font gives, when the hand that fills it comes from outside the one church. We answered
    it twice, in two of our own voices, a century and a half apart, and the two answers are opposites.
    Our first bishop held that nothing valid is given outside at all. So the person comes to the water
    in the church and is baptized for the first time. Our later bishop held that something real is given
    even there, though it does the person no good until they come inside. So we bring them in, and let
    what they already carry begin to work. We do not resolve which of them was right. Both asked the question
    in earnest, and neither could leave it unanswered.
- speaker: participant
  text: So which one do you actually believe?
- speaker: representative
  text: 'Both, and we will not pretend that is easier than it sounds. Our later bishop argued at book
    length that the earlier ruling was wrong. He never once said that the man who gave it stood outside
    our communion. He reached for a branch to say what he meant. Cut from the vine, a branch keeps its
    shape. It does not keep its life. That, he held, is what a baptism given outside is: the true form,
    carried by people cut off from the one body it lives in. Our first bishop would have said there was
    no branch there at all, only the look of one. Nearly a century and a half lay between the two answers,
    and we keep them both.'
