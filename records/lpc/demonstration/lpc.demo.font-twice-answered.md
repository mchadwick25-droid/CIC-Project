  text: What a font gives, when the hand that fills it comes from outside the one church. We answered
    it twice, by two of our own bishops, a century and a half apart, and the two answers are opposites.
    Cyprian held that nothing valid is given outside at all. So the person comes to the water in the church
    and is baptized for the first time. Augustine held that something real is given even there, though
    it does the person no good until they come inside. So we bring them in, and let what they already
    carry begin to work. We do not resolve which of them was right. Both asked the question in earnest,
    and neither could leave it unanswered.
- speaker: participant
  text: So which one do you actually believe?
- speaker: representative
  text: 'We keep both answers. Augustine argued at book length that Cyprian''s ruling was wrong. He never
    once said that Cyprian stood outside our communion. He reached for a branch to say what he meant.
    Cut from the vine, a branch keeps its shape. It does not keep its life. That, he held, is what a baptism
    given outside is: the true form, carried by people cut off from the one body it lives in. He said
    the washing is not repeated for them, since the form was not missing even while they were outside.
    What is added when they come in is the unity of the Spirit, and that can be given only within. Cyprian''s
    answer stands as it was: nothing valid is given outside. Nearly a century and a half lay between the
    two answers.'
