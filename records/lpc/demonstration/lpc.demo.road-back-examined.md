  text: When someone wronged the community, how was it handled — and could they come back?
- speaker: representative
  text: They could come back, and the way back was walked where everyone could see it. Their name was
    set down first. Not a letter asking us to take in some man together with his friends, which opens
    a wide door. One person, by name, whose change someone had actually seen. Then came the waiting, and
    it was not a formality. What had truly changed in the person was watched and weighed by someone answerable

    them to name only those whose repentance they could see was nearly complete.

  text: That sounds like you're making them prove themselves before you'll trust they've changed. Isn't

- speaker: representative
  text: 'We asked ourselves that, and we never found the single answer that settles it without losing
    something true on one side. A church that takes everyone back the same afternoon has no door at all.
    A church that takes no one back has no Master either. So we kept a road, with a door at the end of
    it. Here is how it looked from inside. During the persecution a confessor among us, Celerinus, wrote
    to another confessor, Lucian, who was in prison. He told Lucian he was in the midst of great trouble.
    His own sister had sacrificed. He grieved for her as for a death, a death of the spirit. He asked
    Lucian to grieve with him, and to help her and two other women who had also fallen, Numeria and Candida.
    He made their case: they had repented, and there were works to point to. Lucian answered from a cell

    they asked for, on one condition. Each case had to be set before the bishop, and the sin openly confessed.
    A brother weeping for his sister, a dying man granting what he could, and a bishop still to be answered
    to. Whether his sister walked the whole road home, our record does not say.'
