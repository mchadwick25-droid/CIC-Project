narrative_tier_justification: 'Documented. Tier 1 (Doc_09 SS3), and the tier is assigned to this story
  rather than to its source. Pontius is an eyewitness with an identifiable social location -- our own
  bishop''s own deacon -- writing within this world''s horizon, which satisfies Tier 1''s own author test.
  But Pontius is also writing hagiography, and Doc_02 SS4 flags that genre risk by name. What settles

  and thirty-six co-signatories argue that a bishop should be chosen ''in the presence of the people,
  who have most fully known the life of each one,'' pointing to a case they assume their readers already

  celebrates it. Carried rather than resolved: the specific claim that Cyprian was a neophyte rests on
  Pontius alone.'
tellable_as: How we came to treat a recent convert's own election, over his reluctance, as God's own judgment
  made visible
text: 'Our own deacon Pontius, writing after his bishop had been executed, passed over most of what he
  could have said about Cyprian''s early years and settled on one fact as enough. That one fact, he wrote,
  was proof enough of his good works: by God''s judgment and the people''s favour, he was chosen bishop
  while still a neophyte, and, as people thought, a novice.


  A neophyte. Newly baptised. Pontius did not soften this -- he pressed it, noting that Cyprian was still
  in the first days of his faith, untaught as yet in the life of the spirit. What we saw in him was not
  training. It was something we thought we could already see.


  Two things are claimed at once in that sentence, and for Pontius they are not in tension: the judgment
  of God, and the favour of the people. The second is how the first became visible to us -- and years
  afterward, writing to defend his own contested episcopate against a faction that had never accepted
  it, Cyprian himself would call that same acclamation by its proper name: our own suffrage, joined to
  God''s own judgment.'
telling: "Our deacon Pontius wrote the life of Cyprian after his bishop had been put to death. He said it would take too long to tell all that Cyprian did, and that one fact was proof enough of his good works.\n\n\"By the judgment of God and the favour of the people, he was chosen for the sacred office and the rank of bishop. He was still newly baptized and, as people thought, a beginner.\" Pontius did not soften this. He pressed it, describing Cyprian as still in the untaught season of his spiritual life.\n\nCyprian did not reach for the office. When the whole people leapt forward in love for him, Pontius says, he humbly stepped back. He gave way to older men and thought himself unworthy. A crowd of the brothers besieged the doors of his house.\n\nFor Pontius, God's judgment and the people's favour were not two claims pulling against each other. The people's favour was how God's judgment became visible to us. Pontius does not say how the people made their favour known.\n\nYears later, rivals who had never accepted him renewed their attack. Cyprian answered them in a letter to us. They still carried, he wrote, \"that old venom against my office as bishop, which means against your suffrage and against God's judgment.\"\n\nOnly Pontius says that Cyprian was newly baptized when he was chosen, and no other witness of ours confirms or corrects it. Pontius was also writing in praise of his own bishop."
absent_detail: The specific claim that Cyprian was a neophyte rests on Pontius alone; no other witness
  of ours confirms or corrects it, and we do not claim more certainty about it than that one source supports.
  Pontius is writing in praise, and he says so himself.
modern_contrast: A modern listener may hear 'the people chose him' as describing a vote, with a candidacy
  and a count. We had none of that. What we had was a crowd whose favour we treated as evidence of God's
  own judgment, and a man who did not want the office in the first place.
