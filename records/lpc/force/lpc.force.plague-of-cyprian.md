name: Epidemic Disease -- the Plague of c. 249-262

description: >-
  A severe, well-attested pandemic ran through Carthage while Cyprian was bishop. He addressed it
  directly in his treatise De Mortalitate. The plague is documented.


  It was a pressure no discipline could sort. Persecution at least asked a
  question a person could answer rightly or wrongly, but the plague asked nothing -- it took the
  faithful and the lapsed alike.


  This is the clearest case in this world of a pressure met with teaching more than with structure. It
  left no lasting concern the world organizes around. Its best-attested response was a treatise. Pontius
  also records relief given out by rank, and the banishment that followed (Life 9 to 11). We leave open
  whether that ties the plague to the organized church and to the Valerian persecution. The treatise
  makes it our sharpest example of how we met crisis: by turning it into teaching, through preaching
  and catechesis.


  No connection to any other force is registered for this force. That is not a weakness in the entry. It
  is the clearest case of a pressure whose best-attested response shaped what was taught rather than how
  the church was built. Recording the open question honestly is better than inventing a link.
