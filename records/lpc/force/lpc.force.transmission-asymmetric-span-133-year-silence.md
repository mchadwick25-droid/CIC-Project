description: >-
  This world's closing decades are much better attested than its opening ones. Between its two phases
  lies a silence of roughly 133 years, from Cyprian's martyrdom to Augustine's ordination. No surviving
  voice continuous with Cyprian's ordinary pastoral mode, from within this world's boundary, that this
  build has assessed dates from within that gap. Nine tractatus in Morin 1917, one titled in a manuscript
  as a sermon of Optatus of Milevis, are not yet assessed. Some texts in our own source catalogue do date from it or record its events, such as
  Optatus's polemic, the council canons and imperial law, and none of them is that voice. The silence claim is contested
  for that reason. The uneven attestation is a documented fact about the record itself, and anyone can
  check it against the catalogue.


  Augustine does not receive Cyprian through a chain of teachers who knew him. He receives a set of
  council acts and letters, reads them, and argues with them. He treats a predecessor's ruling as a
  document to weigh, not a custom to continue. The inheritance was therefore received as text rather
  than carried as living memory. (That sentence reports how the world understood itself. It is not a
  judgment of historical accuracy.) Cyprian was a predecessor met on a page, weighed, answered, and never
  able to answer back. And the gap was not felt as a gap. Someone formed in either phase did not know
  their world had a documentary silence in it.


  This has three effects. First, continuity across the gap runs through texts, not through a line of
  successors. Augustine's engagement with Cyprian's council acts is the only force in this world that
  carries it across. Nothing else found so far does. The crossing is attested twice. The second case is
  Possidius quoting Cyprian's De Mortalitate, and it is not itself counted as a force.


  Second, the gap is richly attested, but by sources that belong to Donatism's territory, not this
  world's. So the silence calls for discipline at the boundary; it is not simply missing evidence. The
  temptation is to fill it from that other world, and we do not let ourselves do that.


  Third, concerns that belong to one phase only reflect the record as well as the world. Penitential
  discipline and the confessor-versus-bishop tension are attested only in the first phase. Grace and
  human incapacity appears only in the second. Each of these limits was confirmed by a positive check,
  not inferred from silence.


  This is the same pattern that let both men's writings survive on the dominant side. Here it shapes the
  span of the record rather than its content.
manifestations:
