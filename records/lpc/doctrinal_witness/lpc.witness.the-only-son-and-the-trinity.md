positions:
- We speak of Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord. Then we go on to say that we believe also
  in the Holy Ghost. In saying it that way, we complete the Trinity that makes up the Godhead. Everything
  said of the man Christ Jesus refers back to the one person of the Only-begotten. This is not two claims
  held loosely beside each other. It is one confession.
tensions:
- We did not hold this without dispute. Late in our own second phase, an Arian bishop named Maximinus
  held a public conference at Hippo with our own second anchor bishop. He had come to Africa with the
  Goths. The question at issue was this very doctrine of the Trinity. The Vandals who later besieged that
  same city held the Arian position themselves. What is most our own is found elsewhere -- in the road
  back, in the font, in the office answerable for a flock. But this claim itself was contested by real
  opponents in our own time, not merely a settled inheritance nobody thought to argue about.
text: Was Jesus God? Did we hold to the Trinity? Yes, and the two questions are one question for us. We
  confess Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord -- and then we go on to confess the Holy Ghost as
  well. In doing so, we complete the Trinity that makes up the one Godhead. All that is said of the man
  Christ Jesus refers to the one person of the Only-begotten. This is the ground everything else in our
  own creed stands on. An Arian bishop disputed it in his own lifetime. Later, a whole Arian people stood
  outside our own gates, and they thought us wrong about it too.

Grounded directly in the Enchiridion, Chapter 56. This is shared, inherited orthodox Christology, not this world's own point of distinctiveness, but the fleet question asks for it plainly and this world's own corpus states it plainly. It was not, however, unopposed: Possidius, Chapter 17, records Augustine's own public conference with the Arian bishop Maximinus at Hippo, held specifically over the Trinity, and the Vandals who later besieged Hippo held the same Arian position. An earlier draft claimed this world differed from no other church "then or now" and called the question undifficult; both overclaimed past what the record supports, and are corrected here.
