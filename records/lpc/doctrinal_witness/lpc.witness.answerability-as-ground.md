positions:
- 'Ask what holds us together, and our answer is not that we are pure. Nor is it that we are right about
  every hard question we argue. Our answer is that we are answerable. A named man is given a people, and
  he will be asked, one day, what became of them. We see everything else growing out of that one fact:
  the road back for someone who has failed, the font that either gives or withholds until a person comes
  home, the disagreement between colleagues that we carry without breaking over it. Take the answerability
  away, and to us each of these becomes a separate rule we argue about. Leave it in place, and we see
  one conviction from several sides.'
- As we see it, that is why one office teaches the newcomer, washes him, corrects him when he fails, and
  takes him home again. Take answerability away, and to us the road back becomes an empty form and the
  font a private exchange. In our eyes, a council would become an argument no one has to answer for. Held
  together, they are what we take ourselves to be.
tensions:
- We do not claim this conviction settles every hard case it touches. The very font we take this answerability
  to serve, we have answered twice, in opposite ways, a century and a half apart. Our later bishop judged
  the earlier answer wrong, yet he never put the man who gave it outside our communion. Being answerable
  for someone does not, on its own, tell you what is owed to them in every case.
text: 'As we understand ourselves, we are held together, first, by one plain fact: a particular people
  has been placed in a particular man''s care, and he will be asked what became of them. That is not a
  title he carries apart from the people it names. To us, it is the whole shape of what a bishop is. We
  see everything else we hold growing out of that one root. The road back for someone who has failed is
  not a formality. To us, it is what answerability looks like in practice. The font is never a small question,
  however plainly someone asks about it. And when our own bishops disagree, sharply, even across a whole
  century, they do not put each other outside the table over it. As we see it, the one bond they both
  answer to holds the disagreement inside it. We do not pretend this makes every hard question easy. We
  have answered the font question twice, in opposite ways. Our later bishop judged the earlier answer
  wrong. But he never put the man who gave it outside our communion. Being answerable for someone does
  not settle what is owed to them in every case. We take it as the ground everything else stands on, not
  a formula that resolves it.'
