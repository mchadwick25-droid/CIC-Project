positions:
- Augustine taught that a person is judged on the life he lived here. He wrote, "it is in this life that
  all the merit or demerit is acquired." No one, he said, need hope to "obtain merit with God" after death
  which he "neglected to secure here."
- Almsgiving does not change this. It must be used, he wrote, "to propitiate God for past sins, not to
  purchase impunity for the commission of such sins in the future." The life, he said, "must be changed
  for the better."
tensions:
- He left one question open. Some believers might pass through "a kind of purgatorial fire" after death.
  He said this "may be inquired into, and either ascertained or left doubtful." He added that it could
  not help those who "shall not inherit the kingdom of God" unless "after suitable repentance their sins
  be forgiven them." We do not claim a settled doctrine of purgatory for Augustine. His words here do
  not say what the fire is or how long it lasts.
claim_guards:
- The record does not teach purgatory as settled; Augustine's words are "may be inquired into, and either
  ascertained or left doubtful", and the speaker must not say he taught it as a doctrine.
- The record does not say what the fire is, how long it lasts or who passes through it beyond "some believers";
  the speaker must not supply it.
- The speaker must not say Augustine thought almsgiving counts for nothing; he says God "may blot out
  sins that are already committed, if we do not neglect to make proper satisfaction."

Grounded directly in Augustine, Enchiridion, chapters 69, 70 and 110 (chapters LXIX, LXX and CX in the Latin) -- verified against the vendored files at the lines above. The quoted phrases are the NPNF English word for word. "Something of the same kind" at the start of chapter 69 looks back to chapter 68, where a believer who loses what he loved is saved "so as by fire." The Latin behind "left doubtful" is latere, to lie hidden. The Latin is OCR text with misreads. No year for the Enchiridion is stated here, and none is stated by the vendored sources for this world. Cyprian's own teaching on almsgiving is at lpc.witness.almsgiving-quenches-sin.
