positions:
- Cyprian wrote to Donatus of his own baptism. Before it, he said, he thought it hard that a man should be
  "capable of being born again," and "quickened to a new life in the laver of saving water." After it, he
  wrote, "by the help of the water of new birth, the stain of former years had been washed away." Then "a
  second birth had restored me to a new man."
- Writing to Pompey against Stephen's letter, Cyprian called baptism the second birth. "The second birth
  is spiritual," he wrote, "by which we are born in Christ through the laver of regeneration." Water
  alone, he held, cannot cleanse away sins "unless he have also the Holy Spirit."
- Augustine used the same word of the dying. If ministers are absent at the hour of danger, he wrote to
  Honoratus, people leave this life "either not regenerated or not loosed from their sins."
tensions:
- Cyprian told his new birth as his own story, after baptism. Neither bishop tells us that every convert
  felt such a change, or that a felt change was what made the birth.
- Cyprian held that baptism given by heretics was no second birth. Augustine argued against him on that
  ruling. The record does not settle that dispute.
claim_guards:
- The record gives the words both bishops used for the new birth in baptism; the speaker must not present
  it as a modern born-again experience, a decision, or a moment a person had to feel.
- Augustine's word regenerated is quoted from one letter, about the dying; the speaker must not build from
  it a full account of his teaching on baptism.
- The record does not say how Cyprian's and Augustine's views of baptism outside the church compare in
  this matter; the speaker must not settle it.

Each quoted clause is the ANF or NPNF English word for word. The ANF editor's bracketed note "Another Nicodemus" after "born again" in section 3 is not Cyprian's and is left out. The Latin of Ad Donatum has renasci denuo in section 3 and natiuitas secunda in section 4; the Latin of Epistle LXXIV (Hartel) has natiuitas secunda spiritalis.
