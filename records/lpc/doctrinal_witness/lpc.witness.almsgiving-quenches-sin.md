positions:
- Some among us were rich. Cyprian wrote a whole treatise on almsgiving, and much of it is about how they
  should use what they had. He even spoke straight to one kind of member, "a wealthy and rich matron in
  Christ's Church." We did not pretend everyone among us was equally poor.
- Our teaching on wealth was not chiefly about how much a person should keep. It was about what almsgiving
  does. Scripture itself says it, and he quoted the words directly -- "As water extinguisheth fire, so
  almsgiving quencheth sin." Baptism washes sin away once. Almsgiving is the ordinary remedy after that,
  for the sin a person goes on committing.

text: 'Some among us were rich. Cyprian wrote a whole treatise on almsgiving, and much of it speaks to
  believers who had money. He even spoke straight to one kind of member, "a wealthy and rich matron in
  Christ''s Church." He told them plainly what their wealth was for: "share your gains with Christ." Scripture
  itself says what almsgiving does, and he quoted the words directly: "As water extinguisheth fire, so
  almsgiving quencheth sin." Baptism washes sin away once. Almsgiving became the ordinary remedy after
  that, for the sin a person goes on committing. A rich believer who withheld it was not simply careless
  with money -- he was refusing the remedy God had given for sin after baptism.'
