positions:
- We offered "the sacrifice of the Mediator" for our dead, and we gave alms in the church on their behalf.
  Augustine taught that these gifts help only those who earned that help in life. Offerings made "on behalf
  of all the baptized dead," he wrote, are "thank-offerings for the very good." For the not very bad they
  are "propitiatory offerings."
- Augustine's mother, Monica, made one request as she lay ill. "Lay this body anywhere," she told her
  sons. "This only I ask, that you will remember me at the Lord's altar, wherever you be."
tensions:
- For the very bad, Augustine wrote, the offerings do not help the dead. They are "a species of consolation

Grounded directly in Augustine, Enchiridion, chapter 110 (chapter CX in the Latin) and Confessions IX.xi.27 -- verified against the vendored files at the lines above. The quoted phrases are the NPNF English word for word. Monica's words are hers as Augustine reports them; he says she spoke "in such words as she could." The Latin is OCR text with misreads. Monica's death is dated 387 by the chronological table in the vendored NPNF volume (cic/texts/npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml, lines 3258-3260); Confessions IX.xi.28 (line 14225) says she died in "the fifty-sixth year of her age, and the thirty-third of mine."
