positions:
- 'Our first anchor bishop set a hard test. Writing against a rival bishop''s own claimed tradition, he
  asked: where does that tradition come from? Does it descend from the Lord''s own authority and the Gospel,
  or from the commands and letters of the apostles? If it is not written there, he would not keep it,
  however old the custom. He said it plainly: custom without truth is only the old age of error.'
tensions:
- Our second anchor bishop argued a related dispute a century and a half later. He used a different test.
  He held that a custom kept everywhere in the Church may fairly count as apostolic, even when no apostle's
  own writing says so. Many things, he said, rightly come from the apostles, even though never written
  down. We do not resolve which test is truer. We hold both, from two bishops on opposite sides of two
  different disputes about what we received from the apostles.
text: 'How do we test whether a practice of ours truly goes back to the apostles? We do not agree among
  ourselves on one single test. Our first anchor bishop held that nothing counts as apostolic unless it
  is written in the Gospel or in the apostles'' own letters. Custom alone, however old, is not enough.
  Custom without truth, he said, is only the old age of error. Our second anchor bishop argued a related
  question a century and a half later. He held the opposite: a custom kept everywhere in the Church may
  fairly be presumed apostolic, even where no apostle''s own writing says so. We give you both tests,
  because both are ours. Neither one, on its own, traces a specific practice of ours -- the road back,
  the teaching before the water -- to a named apostolic origin.'

Grounded directly in Cyprian, Epistle 73, and Augustine, On Baptism V.23. Answers F4-E's own question about how a practice's apostolic origin would be tested, using material this world's own corpus actually contains; does not claim either bishop traces catechesis, preaching, or reconciliation specifically to the apostles by name, which remains a genuine absence, now narrowed and restated in lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended.
