plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, thoughts are the raw material of the interior struggle. "It is impossible
  for the mind not to be approached by thoughts, but it is in the power of every earnest man either to
  admit them or to reject them." They come "from God, from the devil, and from ourselves." The mind is
  a mill-wheel that must grind something; the miller decides "whether he will have wheat or barley or
  darnel ground by it."

senses:
  informational: >-
    Germanus asks the question a Gallic reader would ask - what can the mind do about its own
    thoughts? - and Abbot Moses answers on both sides: "As then their rising up does not entirely
    depend on ourselves, so the rejection or admission of them lies in our own power." The mind is
    like a mill turned by a stream: it cannot stop turning, "but it is in the power of the man who
    directs it, to decide whether he will have wheat or barley or darnel ground by it." So the monk
    feeds it - the psalms by heart, meditation on Scripture, labour. Then the three origins: "from God,
    from the devil, and from ourselves" - from God "when He vouchsafes to visit us with the
    illumination of the Holy Ghost" or chastens us "with most salutary compunction"; from the devil,
    "deceitfully showing us evil as good, and transforming himself into an angel of light"; and a
    third range from ourselves. "We ought then carefully to notice this threefold order, and with a
    wise discretion to analyse the thoughts which arise in our hearts," like a good money-changer
    testing coin.
  evidential: >-
    Cassian only (Conferences I.17-20; Institutes II.14, IV.9), Moses's teaching received from Egypt.
    The Latin lemma is not attested.
  personal: >-
    Because thoughts are admitted or rejected, we are answerable for them; because their origin is
    threefold and hard to tell, we show them to the senior as soon as they arise; because the wheel
    never stops, the cell and the office keep it fed. Among us the first fault is not a deed but a
    thought entertained - and the discipline begins before any act.
  translational: >-
    'Are intrusive thoughts your fault - or is the mind a private place where nothing counts?' - for us
    a wheel that cannot stop and must be fed, whose grain the monk chooses: responsible admission or
    rejection, not responsibility for their arising; thoughts of three origins to be tested and shown to
    an elder - Egypt's teaching, received as the first lesson of the southern life.
quick_meaning: >-
  The mind is a mill that cannot stop turning; the monk chooses the grain. Thoughts come from God,
  the devil, or ourselves, and we answer for which we admit.
