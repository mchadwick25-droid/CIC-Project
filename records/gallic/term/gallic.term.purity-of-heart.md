plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, the immediate goal of the whole monastic life, as distinct from its end,
  the kingdom of heaven. It is the "definite mark" at which every fast, vigil, reading and renunciation
  is aimed, "which is charity." By it all of these are ranked as aids to perfection and never as
  perfection itself.
world_word: purity of heart
false_friend:
- sexual purity or moral spotlessness
- a pure heart as sincerity or good intentions
- perfection as flawlessness or perfectionism
senses:
  informational: >-
    The teaching arrives as Abbot Moses's, in an Egyptian cell, and Cassian sets it first among all
    the conferences. Every art "has some goal or mark; and end or aim of their own"; the monk's
    profession has both: "The end of our profession ... is the kingdom of God or the kingdom of heaven:
    but the immediate aim or goal, is purity of heart, without which no one can gain that end."
    Everything else is then ranked by it: fastings, vigils, meditation on the Scriptures, self-denial
    "are not perfection, but aids to perfection," all practised "with a view to our main object, i.e.,
    purity of heart, which is charity." Prayer is where the goal shows itself - "the aim of every monk
    and the perfection of his heart tends to continual and unbroken perseverance in prayer" - and the
    argument about grace is fought inside it: no bodily labour "can be sufficient for the acquisition
    of that true chastity of the inner man" without God's help. At Tours the words are not used;
    Martin has the thing itself, a heart that tests what it is offered, refusing a robe said to come
    from heaven and a purple-robed Christ.
  evidential: >-
    Directly attested in Cassian only (Conferences I.2-7, II.26, IX.2, XIII.18; Institutes IV.43,
    XII.11), framed by Cassian as received from Egypt; Gennadius corroborates the first Conference's
    title. The technical phrase puritas cordis is not attested in the vendored translation or the
    bounded Latin grep; the bare puritas occurs once in Hilary of Arles, non-technically. Sulpitius has
    the practice without the vocabulary. Conferences XII "On Chastity" is missing from the English text
    - one element of this term's own content is not readable in our own edition.
  personal: >-
    This is what the fathers handed down and Cassian brought to us, not what Gaul devised - and we say
    so. It is the standard by which we weigh every practice we are given: the office, the fast, the
    labour, each judged by whether it clears the heart, and none of them dropped for the sake of the
    very thing they serve. It is what we work toward and cannot make.
  translational: >-
    'Purity of heart - do you mean chastity, or just being sincere?' - neither: we mean the target
    itself, a state of undistracted charity and "tranquillity of mind" from which the kingdom is
    reached and by which every fast and vigil is measured and ranked; chastity is one element within
    it, and our own conference on chastity is missing from our English text.
quick_meaning: >-
  The monk's immediate goal, short of the kingdom itself: a heart cleared to charity. Every fast and
  vigil is ranked by it as an aid, and never as the point.
