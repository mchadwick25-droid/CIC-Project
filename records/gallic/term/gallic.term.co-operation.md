plain_meaning: >-
  Our own verb for how grace meets effort. God's grace works with our will - it goes before,
  helps, and crowns it. Yet it asks real effort of us, so as not to give gifts to one asleep.
world_word: co-operation

senses:
  informational: >-
    The verb is Paul's, and Chaeremon reads it as a monk reads a rule: "the grace of God always
    co-operates with our will for its advantage, and in all things assists, protects, and defends
    it, in such a way as sometimes even to require and look for some efforts of good will from it
    that it may not appear to confer its gifts on one who is asleep." The way it is felt from inside
    is a pair of names used in prayer: God is "Protector and Saviour" when He first calls us while
    we are still unwilling, and "Helper and Sponsor" when we are already striving and He brings
    help. Our formation manual gives the monk the sentence to say of every gain - "Not I, but the
    grace of God with me" - and insists that grace is bestowed "only upon those who labour and
    exert themselves."
  evidential: >-
    Directly attested in Cassian (Conf. XIII.13, XIII.17; III.12, III.22; Inst. XII.9, 14, 17) as
    Chaeremon's and Paphnutius's reported teaching. Single-voice: no other founding voice uses the
    verb in this sense, and the Lerins side (Faustus) is unread. No Augustine passage is needed
    here.
  personal: >-
    This is not a theory of shares - so much God's, so much mine. It is what a labouring monk finds:
    that his labour was helped before it began and will be crowned after it ends, and he names God
    twice for the two moments.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer may reach for "synergism" or "God helps those who help themselves." Neither is
    what we mean. Our word names an experience prayed before it was argued - grace that goes first,
    accompanies, requires effort, and crowns it - and we confess "Not I" of every virtue we gain.
quick_meaning: >-
  Our verb for grace and effort together. God's grace goes before our will, helps it, and crowns
  it - and still asks real work of us. We name God Protector and Helper for the two moments.
