senses:
  informational: >-
    Asked how faults are overcome, Chaeremon answers with an ascent: "three things which enable
    men to control their faults; viz., either the fear of hell or of laws even now imposed; or the
    hope and desire of the kingdom of heaven; or a liking for goodness itself and the love of
    virtue" - the slave's, the hireling's, the son's. "We must do our best to mount with perfect
    ardour of mind from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God." The formation manual ends
    the same way: humility leads "to love which knows no fear."
  evidential: >-

  personal: >-
    Fear is where the junior begins and where the son no longer lives. We are not ashamed to name
    it as the lowest rung.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer may take fear of hell as the whole of our piety, or as an embarrassment to
    explain away. For us it is deliberately the first and lowest rung of a ladder we are formed to
    climb past - a starting restraint, not an endpoint.
quick_meaning: >-

  hireling, son. We start at fear and are meant to climb past it.
