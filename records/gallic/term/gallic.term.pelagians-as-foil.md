plain_meaning: >-
  The named heresy we define ourselves against - those who gave so much to free will that they
  denied any need of God's grace.
world_word: the Pelagians ("frogs, fleas, and flies"; "the profane notion of some")
false_friend:
- that we were Pelagians, or "semi-Pelagians" as a settled verdict
- Pelagianism as a general term for "works" or optimism about human nature
senses:
  informational: >-
    Vincent uses them as the type of the novelty his rule exists to refuse - "an ephemeral, moribund
    set of frogs, fleas, and flies, such as the Pelagians," who say to Catholics, "Take our word,
    follow our lead, accept our exposition, condemn what you used to hold." His charge is exact: who
    before Pelagius "attributed so much antecedent strength to Free-will, as to deny the necessity of
    God's grace"? Chaeremon, at the very moment he defends the reality of the will, refuses the same
    thing - not "the profane notion of some who attribute everything to free will and lay down that
    the grace of God is dispensed in accordance with the desert of each man." For both, the
    Pelagian is the man who made grace unnecessary. We drew our boundary on that side by name; on
    the other side we drew it without naming anyone.
  evidential: >-
    Directly attested in Vincent (Comm. ch. 9, ch. 24) and Cassian (Conf. XIII.16). The men who
    reported us to Africa conceded the same: these brethren are "abundantly" distinguished "from
    the error of the Pelagians" (Praed. ch. 2, context only). Gennadius's charge that Sulpitius was
    led astray by the Pelagians in old age is his alone and is Contested. "Semi-Pelagian" appears in
    our vendored files only in the editors' apparatus.
  personal: >-
    We had a heretic we could name, and the name was not our own. Pelagius made grace unnecessary;
    we never did. That we were later called half of him is a word we did not hear and would not
    own.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer arrives with "semi-Pelagian" as a settled verdict on us. Look at where we drew
    the line: the Pelagians are the error on the far side from the one we were later accused of. We
    named them, refused them, and were conceded by Augustine's own reporters to be distinct from
    them.
quick_meaning: >-
  The heresy we named and refused. Vincent calls them frogs and flies; Cassian calls theirs "the
  profane notion." They made grace unnecessary. We never did.
