plain_meaning: >-
  The act that makes a monk: "nothing but the evidence of the cross and of mortification," by which,
  as Pinufius says, the monk is dead to the world "and its deeds and desires." In the south's received
  teaching it has three stages. Giving up one's goods, "of what is not our own," is only the first,
  and "is not enough of itself to confer perfection on the renunciant."
world_word: renunciation (renuntiatio)
false_friend:
- giving things up - poverty, celibacy, "detachment" - as the whole of the monastic act
- a heroic once-for-all rejection of society
- self-denial for its own sake
senses:
  informational: >-
    In the south the word is the title of a book, Cassian's fourth, and its charge is Pinufius's to the
    newly received: "Renunciation is nothing but the evidence of the cross and of mortification. And so
    you must know that to-day you are dead to this world and its deeds and desires." Paphnutius counts
    the stages against the Lord's word to Abraham, "Get thee out from thy country and from thy kinsfolk
    and from thy father's house": the first "as far as the body is concerned," making light of wealth;
    the second, rejecting "the fashions and vices and former affections of soul and flesh"; the third,
    detaching the soul "from all present and visible things." And he ranks them: the first "is of what
    is not our own, and therefore is not enough of itself." Salvian sees the rich who "make a show of
    renouncing their wealth," and also uses the word for what every Christian said at the font: "I
    renounce the devil, his pomps and spectacles and his works." At Tours the thing is counselled
    without the term - Martin pressed on Sulpitius "that the allurements of this world and secular
    burdens were to be abandoned" - and Martin's own renunciation is a discharge before Caesar.
  evidential: >-
    Cassian (Institutes IV.1, IV.4, IV.34, IV.36; Conferences III.6, III.10), Salvian (VI.6, III.3,
    within his licence) and Sulpitius (Life chs. IV, XXV). The threefold schema is Cassian's and
    received. The Latin renuntiantium is attested only in the editor's Prolegomena, giving the
    Institutes' title.
  personal: >-
    The monk's renunciation is the baptismal one carried further. Among us it can be lost by
    re-entanglement or faked "before the face of men," and the south asks of a poor monk whether he has
    renounced anything at all. And what our houses know above all is that a renunciant is not a man
    the Church has lost: our exemplars keep being fetched back for its sees.
  translational: >-
    'So renunciation means giving everything up - poverty, celibacy, leaving society for good?' - that
    is only the first and easiest of three stages for us, "of what is not our own"; the real
    renunciation is of what belongs to us, our affections and our will, a death "to this world" that
    extends what every baptized Christian renounced at the font - and after which the Church still
    comes to fetch the renunciant to be its bishop.
quick_meaning: >-
  The act that makes a monk - a death to the world of which giving up goods is only the first and
  easiest stage. And the Church still fetches renunciants for its sees.
