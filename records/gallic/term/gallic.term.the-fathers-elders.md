plain_meaning: >-
  The prior generations from whom everything legitimate among us is received and never invented. For
  Cassian, the Egyptian old men he sat with. For Vincent, the doctors whose collated writings form "a
  consentient council." For Martin, the presbyters who can say whether a tradition "had come down from
  antiquity."
world_word: the Fathers / the elders
false_friend:
- a settled patristic library - the "Church Fathers" as a canon of authors to be quoted
- elders as a modern church office or board
- abbot as the canonical head of a Benedictine house
senses:
  informational: >-
    The claim comes first, and it is a disclaimer: the Conferences are "the Institutes which are not
    mine but the fathers'," and the grace teaching is laid down "not giving my own opinion, but that of
    the elders." At Marseilles the fathers are men who were seen and heard - the junior lays every
    thought "bare to the senior," and the good coin of a thought is stamped by "the approved Catholic
    fathers"; even the Egyptian title Abbot is a father's name, not a rank, and grey hairs alone do not
    make one. At Lerins the fathers are read: Vincent collates "the opinions of the ancients" until they
    show what "all, or the more part" have held, "forming, as it were, a consentient council of
    doctors," against which no single teacher counts, "be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a
    martyr." At Tours the word is smaller: asked to venerate a tomb, Martin "often asked from those who
    were his elders, whether among the presbyters or clerics," and would not lend his authority without
    a tradition "from antiquity."
  evidential: >-
    Directly attested in Cassian (Institutes Preface, I.2, II.3, IV.9, XII.14, XII.19; Conferences
    Preface I, I.20, II.13, II.24, XIII.18), Vincent (Commonitory chs. 1, 3, 28) and Sulpitius (Life
    ch. XI). Hilary of Arles's own Latin uses patres / patrum; seniores and Cassian's own patres are
    not given against the vendored translation. The Tours attestation is one episode against the
    south's systematic statement - an asymmetry of weight, not of presence.
  personal: >-
    The word does the same work in three different rooms: it puts an authority behind the speaker that
    is older than the speaker, and it makes our task the keeping of something rather than the making of
    it. What a father is differs by house - a man in a cell, a book on a shelf, a presbyter who
    remembers - and within our own lifetime we moved from the first to the second.
  translational: >-
    'Do you mean the "Church Fathers" - a canon of great authors you quote?' - not quite: for Cassian
    the fathers were living men in Egyptian cells whose grey hairs still had to be tested; for Vincent a
    chorus of writings whose agreement, not any one author, was the authority; for Martin the local
    clergy who remembered what came down from antiquity - in every case, the reason a teaching is not
    one's own.
quick_meaning: >-
  Those we receive from and never invent beyond. Living old men for Cassian; collated doctors for
  Vincent; for Martin, the clergy who remember.
