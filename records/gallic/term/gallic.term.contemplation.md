plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, contemplation is "the chief good." The Lord makes it "consist in
  meditation; i.e., in divine contemplation" - Mary's part, set above Martha's "praiseworthy service."
  It is the second of two knowledges. The practical can be had without the theoretical, "but
  theoretical cannot possibly be gained without practical." First the faults are purged; then God is
  seen.
world_word: contemplation / practical vs. theoretical knowledge
false_friend:
- contemplation as reflection, mindfulness, or a mystical technique available to anyone
- practical vs. theoretical as doing vs. thinking
- meditation as a relaxation practice
- the fourfold sense as a medieval system
senses:
  informational: >-
    Abbot Moses reads the story of the two sisters: Martha summoned her sister "to no unworthy work,
    but to a praiseworthy service," and yet "the Lord makes the chief good consist in meditation; i.e.,
    in divine contemplation" - not in "practical work however praiseworthy and rich in fruits" but in
    the one thing. Every practice serves it: the anchorite's life is "grander than that of the
    Coenobium" because of "the contemplation of God"; the third renunciation detaches the soul to
    "contemplate only things to come"; accidie's victim forgets "the object of his profession, which is
    nothing but meditation and contemplation of that divine purity." Nesteros gives the order: knowledge
    is twofold - practical, "brought about by an improvement of morals and purification from faults,"
    and theoretical, "which consists in the contemplation of things Divine" - and "this practical
    knowledge can be acquired without theoretical, but theoretical cannot possibly be gained without
    practical." Spiritual knowledge has "three kinds, tropological, allegorical, anagogical," beyond the
    historical. At Tours the same two sisters teach hospitality: a queen serving Martin "both served
    like Martha and listened like Mary."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian only (Conferences Preface I, I.8, III.6, XIV.1-2, XIV.8; Institutes X.3), received from
    Moses and Nesteros; Sulpitius reuses the figure (Dialogues II.7) for a different point. Conf. IX-X
    read only in their openings; the editor's medieval mnemonic footnote at XIV.8 is editorial. The
    Greek is the translator's supply.
  personal: >-
    A monk who has not fought his faults cannot see; contemplation for us is not a technique but what a
    purged heart does. We will not let anyone contemplate who has not first fought, and "practical" is
    not opposed to "spiritual" - it is the road to it. The north keeps the two sisters for
    hospitality; the south keeps them for the one thing.
  translational: >-
    'Contemplation - mindfulness, a meditation technique anyone can learn?' - no: the chief good and the
    anchorite's pursuit, reached only after the practical purging of faults and never by technique;
    "meditation" meaning divine contemplation, and "theoretical" meaning the seeing of God - Egypt's
    teaching, received; and a different lesson from the same two sisters at Tours.
quick_meaning: >-
  The chief good - the seeing of God that only a heart purged of its faults can reach. Practical
  knowledge first, then the theoretical; Mary's part after Martha's.
