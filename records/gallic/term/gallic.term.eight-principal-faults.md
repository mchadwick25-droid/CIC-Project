plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, the fixed list of the monk's enemies. "First, Gluttony or the pleasures of
  the palate; secondly, Fornication; thirdly, Covetousness." Then anger, dejection, accidie, vainglory,
  pride - "of which they reckon eight." Each is a "spirit" fought in order, with "the remedies for
  them according to their traditions." Each has a book of its own.
world_word: the eight principal faults
false_friend:
- the seven deadly sins - a later, different, seven-item list treated as a moral catalogue
- vices as bad habits
- the faults as psychology rather than as spirits to be fought
senses:
  informational: >-
    Cassian promises Castor the whole system, "the origin and causes of the principal faults, of which
    they reckon eight, and the remedies for them according to their traditions." The fifth book opens
    the campaign and gives each fault its book and its number: "the first conflict we must enter upon
    is that against gluttony"; "our fourth conflict" anger; "our fifth combat ... dejection"; "our
    sixth combat" accidie; "our seventh combat" vainglory; and last, pride, whose remedy is the
    confession "Not I, but the grace of God with me." The order is the order of battle: a city "is laid
    waste by the giving up of one postern however small." The last two are the ones a good monk fears
    most - vainglory that "creates a wish to take holy orders, and a desire for the priesthood or
    diaconate," and pride, by which a man "grown cold ... wants to be put to rule other people."
    Gennadius remembers the eight books precisely, "a book on each sin."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian only (Institutes Preface, V-XII), Egyptian by his framing; Gennadius corroborates the
    eight books. Institutes VI is omitted from the edition; VII-IX read only for titles. Gibson's Greek
    at X.1 and XI.1 is supplied against the text; his "Accidie" and Chaucer footnote is editorial.
  personal: >-
    We receive the list as Egypt's - "they reckon eight" - and read it toward a purity of heart the
    faults obstruct. The faults are not a catalogue of sins but a sequence of spirits, each with its
    own remedy handed down, beginning with the belly and ending with the wish to rule - the very office
    our houses keep producing. Martin fights the devil, not eight spirits in order.
  translational: >-
    'You mean the seven deadly sins?' - a later, different list: ours are eight named spirits in a
    fixed order of battle, each with a book and a handed-down remedy, beginning with gluttony and
    ending with pride's wish to rule - Egypt's reckoning, received; and the second fault's own book is
    missing from our English text.
quick_meaning: >-
  Eight named spirits fought in a fixed order - gluttony first, pride last - each with a book and a
  handed-down remedy. Not the later "seven deadly sins."
