plain_meaning: >-
  For us a man denied a persecutor's age can still be a martyr. Martin "fully attained to the honor of
  martyrdom without shedding his blood," being "both by vow and virtues ... alike able and willing to
  be a martyr." Vincent's "Confessors" are bishops who suffered for the faith short of death. Cassian's
  strict coenobites, crucified daily to the world, are "made living martyrs."
world_word: bloodless martyrdom / confessor

senses:
  informational: >-
    Sulpitius answers a question his readers were asking - if Martin died in his bed, was he a martyr?
    "He will not remain destitute of the glory of a martyr, because both by vow and virtues he was
    alike able and willing to be a martyr"; in "the times of Nero and of Decius" he would have gone
    "to the rack of torture." Without them, "he fully attained to the honor of martyrdom without
    shedding his blood. For what agonies of human sufferings did he not endure ... in hunger, in
    watchings, in nakedness, in fastings," and "those daily struggles ... against the various conflicts
    with human and spiritual wickedness." Vincent uses the older Church word: those who resisted
    Arianism under Constantius were "accounted Confessors," "the blessed Confessor, Hilary" is cited
    as a witness - and yet no such glory licenses novelty: "be he a Confessor, be he a martyr, let that
    be regarded as a private fancy of his own." Cassian carries a third sense from Egypt: coenobites
    who never fulfil their own will are "crucified daily to this world and made living martyrs."
  evidential: >-
    Sulpitius (Letter II; Dialogues II.4), Vincent (Commonitory chs. 5, 18, 28), Cassian (Conferences
    XVIII.7). The three do not cite one another. The Latin lemma is not supplied against the text.
  personal: >-
    The ascetic life is the persecution God's providence supplies in a peaceful age; the sackcloth is
    the instrument; and the plain outside Tours "where the martyrs were wont to be consecrated" is
    where Martin made a heathen crowd catechumens. A Confessor's suffering gives him honour, not
    authority over antiquity. Martyrdom without blood is what a whole way of life is for.
  translational: >-
    'Wanting to be a martyr - isn't that a death wish?' - not as we meant it: Martin was called a
    martyr "by vow and virtues" precisely because he died in his bed, his persecution being hunger,
    watching and daily combat; a "Confessor" was one who suffered for the faith and lived; and the
    coenobite's daily crucifixion was to his own will, not to his life.
quick_meaning: >-
  A martyr without blood. Martin was one "by vow and virtues." A Confessor suffered for the faith and
  lived. A strict monk is "crucified daily" to his own will.
