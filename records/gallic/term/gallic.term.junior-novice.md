plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, the newly received monk as Egypt receives him. Ten days or more at the door
  "of set purpose repelled and scorned," searched for a hidden coin, stripped and reclothed "by the
  Abbot's hands," lodged a year under the guest-Elder, then handed to a dean over ten and taught "the
  alphabet ... in the direction of perfection." And Cassian's own admission: no one in our monasteries
  has kept it up "unbroken even for a year."
world_word: junior / novice
false_friend:
- a Benedictine or later canonical novitiate - a defined year, a novice-master, vows at its end
- novice as simply a beginner
- the contrary orders and the scorning at the door as hazing or abuse
senses:
  informational: >-
    The book for Castor's new house lays out the whole sequence as the Egyptians keep it: the candidate
    lies "outside the doors for ten days or even longer," prostrate before the brethren who pass and
    "of set purpose repelled and scorned by all of them"; is searched "whether he is contaminated by a
    single coin from his former possessions"; is "stripped of his own clothes, and clad by the Abbot's
    hands in the dress of the monastery," his old clothes kept by the steward against the day he grows
    cold; is given to the guest-Elder for a year and then to "another Elder, who is placed over ten of
    the juniors," whose first lesson is "to teach him first to conquer his own wishes" by orders
    "contrary to his liking," and whose second is disclosure of every thought - "the alphabet, as it
    were, and first syllables in the direction of perfection." Even a famous elder, Pinufius, "sought a
    distant monastery where he could be received as a novice." Then Cassian says what Gaul is: no one
    here has kept the Egyptians' perseverance a year - and he does not say what a Gallic house does
    instead.
  evidential: >-
    Cassian only (Institutes IV.2-10, IV.30), and Egyptian practice by his own framing; Sulpitius
    shows the absence of any such grade at Tours (Life ch. X). Nothing documents what a Marseilles or
    Lerins novice actually underwent. The Latin lemma is not supplied against the text.
  personal: >-
    A junior among us is told, in detail, what Egypt does to a novice, and told in the same book that
    no one here has borne it a year. What he actually undergoes is not written down. He is a man being
    unmade and remade by a fixed order - and the order is not his own house's, which is exactly its
    authority.
  translational: >-
    'Was the novitiate a year of training with vows at the end - and wasn't the scorning at the door
    abuse?' - what we have is Egypt's own probation, prescribed for a Gallic house that could not
    recall anyone keeping its perseverance a year: the door, the search, the stripping, the contrary
    command as the first lesson in conquering one's wishes, read by us as "the deed of Abraham" and
    not as cruelty - and no such grade at Tours at all.
quick_meaning: >-
  The newly received monk, as Egypt receives him - scorned at the door, searched, reclothed, taught to
  conquer his wishes. A standard our own houses admit they did not keep.
