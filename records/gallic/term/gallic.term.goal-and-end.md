plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, the pair with which every teaching begins. Every art "has some goal or
  mark; and end or aim of their own," and so does the monk's profession. Its end is "the kingdom of
  God or the kingdom of heaven"; its immediate goal is "purity of heart, without which no one can gain
  that end," to be aimed at "as if on a definite mark," like an archer's target.
world_word: goal and end (scopos / telos)
false_friend:
- goals as personal objectives or self-improvement targets
- end as death or the end of the world
- a philosopher's distinction rather than a monk's rule of thumb
senses:
  informational: >-
    Abbot Moses will not begin a conference without it. "All the arts and sciences, said he, have some
    goal or mark; and end or aim of their own" - the farmer bears heat and frost for a harvest, the
    merchant crosses seas for gain, the soldier serves for honour, "and our profession too has its own
    goal and end, for which we undergo all sorts of toils not merely without weariness but actually
    with delight." Then the two parts: "The end of our profession indeed ... is the kingdom of God or
    the kingdom of heaven: but the immediate aim or goal, is purity of heart, without which no one can
    gain that end: fixing our gaze then steadily on this goal, as if on a definite mark, let us direct
    our course as straight towards it as possible." The picture is the archer's - without a target no
    one can tell whether his aim has improved. Everything a monk does is then placed on this line:
    fasting, vigils, reading, solitude "are not perfection, but aids to perfection," to be dropped
    the moment they hinder the goal.
  evidential: >-
    Cassian only (Conferences I.2-7, II.26; Institutes V.15), framed as Moses's teaching received;
    Gennadius corroborates the first Conference's title. The Greek is the translator's supply. Tours
    does not speak of goal and end.
  personal: >-
    This pair is the shape of all the south's thinking. It tells us why the office exists (as a
    scaffold toward unceasing prayer), why the hardest faster may be the worst monk (he has mistaken an
    aid for the goal), and why the argument about grace matters (whether the goal can be reached by
    aiming at all). At Tours no one separates the kingdom from the purity that reaches it; Martin's
    whole hour is prayer.
  translational: >-
    'Goals - like personal targets, self-improvement?' - a received Egyptian teaching that every
    practice is measured by a nearer mark (purity of heart) on the way to a farther one (the kingdom),
    so that the mark, not the effort, judges the fast - an archer's discipline, not a philosophy, and
    absent as a vocabulary at Tours.
quick_meaning: >-
  The pair Cassian begins with. The end is the kingdom; the goal is purity of heart. Every fast and
  vigil is judged by the mark it is aimed at.
