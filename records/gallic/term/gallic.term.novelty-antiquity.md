plain_meaning: >-
  The axis on which all three of our founding voices judge what is legitimate. Whatever is new - a
  doctrine, a monastic custom, a fashion of dress, a cult, an opinion of one's own - is suspect for
  being new. Whatever "has been handed down" from antiquity is to be held fast. "If novelty is
  profane, antiquity is sacred."
world_word: novelty vs. antiquity
false_friend:
- novelty as harmless newness or as a virtue (innovation, creativity)
- conservative as a political posture
- tradition as sentiment or custom for its own sake
- progress as change
senses:
  informational: >-
    Vincent puts it in his title and turns the Apostle's charge to Timothy into a rule of contraries:
    "if novelty is to be shunned, antiquity is to be held fast; if novelty is profane, antiquity is
    sacred." Two popes are quoted for it - Stephen's "Let there be no innovation - nothing but what has
    been handed down," Celestine's "let novelty cease to assail antiquity." Yet Vincent asks, "Shall
    there, then, be no progress in Christ's Church?" and answers: all possible progress, "on condition
    that it be real progress, not alteration of the faith" - a child grows into a man with the same
    limbs. At Marseilles the axis runs through a monk's clothes and thoughts: dress must not be
    "remarkable for novelty of colour or fashion"; a thought is weighed for "some conceit of novelty";
    true discretion is "never to do anything novel." And the axis admits the new: a whole morning
    office was added "in our own day," by the elders, "after long discussion and anxious
    consideration." At Tours a bishop will not bless a tomb until his elders can say a tradition "had
    come down from antiquity."
  evidential: >-
    Attested in all three founding voices: Vincent (Commonitory title, chs. 1, 6, 21-23, 32), Cassian
    (Institutes I.2, I.10, II.3, III.4, XII.19; Conferences I.21, II.11, XVIII.7) and Sulpitius (Life
    ch. XI). Doc_03 found this the strongest lexical link between Vincent's doctrinal method, Cassian's
    monastic method and Martin, and Doc_04 confirmed the lexical finding is an organizing one. The
    Latin lemma is not given against the vendored text.
  personal: >-
    One instrument serves us three ways: it tests a doctrine, it tests a custom, and it tests a cult.
    What counts as old differs by house - Egypt's fathers, the consent of the ancients, the clergy's
    memory - but the direction of the judgment is the same everywhere, and it is what makes a thing
    not one's own. Our word for what goes wrong, in each house, is invention.
  translational: >-
    'Weren't you just conservatives, hostile to all change?' - novelty for us was a diagnostic of error
    and of pride, in a doctrine or a garment or a thought, and antiquity was what made a thing not
    one's own; but the old may grow, as a child grows with the same limbs, and the new could be made
    lawful by the consent of the old - which is how we admitted a whole new office in our own day.
quick_meaning: >-
  Our test of what is legitimate, in doctrine, custom and cult alike. The new is suspect for being
  new. The handed-down is held fast. The old may grow, but not become other.
