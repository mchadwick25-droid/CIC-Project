senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the garment is an arm. Refused an audience by Valentinian, Martin "had recourse to his
    well-known weapons - he clothes himself in sackcloth, scatters ashes upon his person, abstains
    from meat and drink." To exorcise, "clothed in sackcloth and sprinkled with ashes, he stretched
    himself on the ground in the midst of the church." Dying, asked to let straw be put under him:
    "It is not fitting that a Christian should die except among ashes; and I have sinned if I leave
    you a different example." Threads from that sackcloth "wrought frequent miracles upon those who
    were sick." At Marseilles the visibility is exactly the objection: the Egyptians "utterly
    disapproved of a robe of sackcloth as being visible to all and conspicuous," fit only to
    "minister to vanity and pride" and unsuitable for work. Cassian knows the north's kind of case
    and does not yield: "even if we hear of some respectable persons who have been dressed in this
    garb, a rule for the monasteries is not, therefore, to be passed by us."
  evidential: >-
    Both hearings are directly attested: Sulpitius (Vita XIV, XVIII; Letter III; Dial. II.5,
    III.6) and Cassian (Inst. I.2, the Egyptians' judgment received). Neither text has read the
    other. This is the ecology's second-best-documented cross-node divergence.
  personal: >-
    One garment; Tours's weapon and Marseilles's vanity. The northern body is displayed as a sign;
    the southern body is hidden as a discipline. We never reconciled them and we do not pretend to
    now.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer thinks of a medieval hair shirt, or of "sackcloth and ashes" as a phrase for
    regret, and expects one monastic view. We have two. At Tours the sackcloth is prayer, exorcism,
    and a good death made visible, and its threads cure; at Marseilles our fathers rejected it as
    conspicuous vanity, "even if we hear of some respectable persons" who wore it.
quick_meaning: >-

  healing the sick. At Marseilles, a conspicuous robe the fathers refused as vanity. Never
  reconciled.
