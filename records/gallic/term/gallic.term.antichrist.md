senses:
  informational: >-
    From the false Christs in Spain and the East, Martin infers "that the coming of Antichrist is
    at hand; for he is already practicing in these persons the mystery of iniquity." Asked about the
    end, "he said to us that Nero and Antichrist have first to come," and that Antichrist "was
    already born, and had, by this time, reached the years of boyhood. Now, this is the eighth year
    since we heard these words from his lips." Vincent begins his book because "a certain awful
    expectation of the approach of the divine judgment importunately demands increased earnestness
    in religion." Cassian names "the fear of hell" only as the first of three restraints.
  evidential: >-

    no Antichrist as read. In Salvian, in the same years, the judgment has begun.
  personal: >-
    Our religion, as Sulpitius put it, is "a weariness of the present world, a terror of judgment,
    a fear of punishment." We count the years since Martin's word.
  translational: >-
    Not a vague mood of the age. Martin gave a dated, near-term calculation - "this is the eighth
    year" - and Vincent names the same expectation as his book's own occasion; Salvian, by
    contrast, says the judgment is not awaited but under way.
quick_meaning: >-
