plain_meaning: >-
  The public discipline of our house. For a fault, a monk lies on the ground until the Abbot bids
  him rise, or is barred from prayer with the brethren. No priest is needed to restore him.

senses:
  informational: >-
    In Cassian's house every fault has its measure, and the first measure is the ground. The monk
    who breaks an earthenware jar, or does any small thing carelessly, "can only expiate his
    carelessness by public penance; and when all the brethren are assembled for service he must lie
    on the ground and ask for absolution until the service of the prayers is finished; and will
    obtain it when by the Abbot's command he is bidden to rise." The cook who let "three lentil
    beans" slip was "suspended from prayer," because everything in the house "is consecrated to the
    Lord"; and no one may pray with a suspended brother "before he performs his penance on the
    ground." The graver faults - open anger, a letter received without leave, "familiarity with
    women" - are "atoned for by expulsion" or "by stripes." Abbot Paphnutius, falsely accused of
    theft, "humbly asked for a plan of repentance." At Tours penance is what Martin's patience
    brings about: Brictio, raving, is "brought back to repentance" and throws himself at Martin's
    feet; soldiers plead that "they had been sufficiently punished by their conscience alone."
  evidential: >-
    Institutional at Marseilles (Inst. II.16, III.7, IV.16, IV.20; Conf. XVIII.15) - Egyptian
    coenobitic practice by Cassian's framing; personal at Tours (Dial. II.3, III.13, III.15). Conf.
    XX, the conference on true satisfaction, is unread; Gennadius attests only its title.
  personal: >-
    Three lentils cost a monk his place at prayer, and no priest is needed to give it back - the
    elder's word ends it. At Tours no grade of penance is written; the master's prayer and the
    sinner's own conscience do the work.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer thinks of private confession, a formula of absolution, and assigned penances.
    Ours is public, bodily, and ended by the elder's word: on the ground until the Abbot bids rise,
    excluded from the brethren's prayer, beaten or expelled for the worst - and, at Tours, a
    repentance brought about by the master's prayer.
quick_meaning: >-
  The house's public discipline. For a small fault, on the ground until the Abbot bids you rise;
  for more, barred from prayer with the brethren; for the worst, stripes or expulsion. At Tours,
  the master's prayer brings the sinner back.
