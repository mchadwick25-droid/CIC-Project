plain_meaning: >-
  For us an anchorite is the monk who, "first trained in the Coenobium," withdraws to the desert "to
  fight with the devils in open conflict." No Gallic voice claims the grade for himself. The boy Martin
  "desired to enter on the life of a hermit"; Marmoutier gives its bishop "the solitude of a hermit";
  Cassian hopes monks near our houses will "try to carry out the rule of anchorites."
world_word: anchorite / hermit (eremite)
false_friend:
- a recluse as the purest or most original form of monk, whom community life dilutes
- a medieval anchorhold walled into a church
senses:
  informational: >-
    The word comes into Gaul with its order fixed. Piamun: "the second [kind] is that of the
    anchorites, who were first trained in the Coenobium and then being made perfect in practical life
    chose the recesses of the desert" - "rightly termed anchorites; i.e., withdrawers," because, not
    satisfied with victory over the devil's hidden snares among men, "they were eager to fight with
    the devils in open conflict." Cassian sets his last Conferences under it, and names the Gallic
    case: books for "those who retire to no great distance from your monasteries, and try to carry out
    the rule of anchorites." At Tours the thing is older than the word's order: at twelve Martin
    "desired to enter on the life of a hermit"; as bishop he built Marmoutier where "he enjoyed in it
    the solitude of a hermit"; and Gallus tells Postumianus, back from the East, "you have conquered
    all the eremites and anchorites. For no one of them ... like Martin, ruled over deaths of all
    kinds."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian, Conferences XVIII.4-6 and Prefaces I and III (the order, framed as Egypt's); Sulpitius,
    Life chs. II, X and Dialogues II.5, II.11. The only lemma-witness toward anachoreta is the
    translator's "withdrawers." Dialogue I is Excluded and not used. No Gallic monk in what was read
    calls himself an anchorite.
  personal: >-
    For us the desert is a second step, not a first. A Gallic anchorite is one who goes a short way out
    from the house and tries. The unnamed fourth kind - the man who takes the name without the training
    - is the counterfeit the order exists to exclude; and Tours measures its bishop against the hermits
    rather than making him one.
  translational: >-
    'Isn't the lone hermit the truest, most original monk - the one institutions dilute?' - not in our
    order: the anchorite is a grade reached after the coenobium, whose whole point is open battle with
    the devil, hoped for in Egypt's order and only "tried" a short way from a Gallic house; at Tours it
    was a boy's wish, a bishop's retreat, and a standard the saint is said to have beaten.
quick_meaning: >-
  The monk who goes out to the desert after training in community, to fight the devils in the open.
  In Gaul, a grade tried a short way from the house, and claimed by no one.
