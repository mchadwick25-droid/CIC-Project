plain_meaning: >-
  Our formative bond is a named man and those formed by watching him. Martin's "eighty disciples, who
  were being disciplined after the example of the saintly master"; Clarus, "a disciple of Martin's";
  Sulpitius mourning "our master, Martin." The south keeps it as elder and junior. Vincent turns it on
  doctrine: the true Catholic is "not a teacher but a disciple, not a leader but a follower."

senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the whole house is a master and his disciples: eighty "disciplined after the example of
    the saintly master"; Clarus, seen in vision ascending "in the same way as I had seen his master";
    Sulpitius's second letter written because he had not described "the death of our master, Martin."
    Martin himself points his disciple to a further model, Paulinus, "the object of our imitation."
    In the south the bond is stated as a rule: examples "from a very few or indeed from one or two
    only"; the Lord does not show the way to anyone who despises "the teaching and training of the
    Elders"; and the master's side, from Chaeremon: "the authority of a teacher will never be strong
    unless he fixes it in the heart of his hearer by the actual performance of his duty." At Lerins
    Vincent takes the word out of the cell and applies it to the faith: whoever keeps the deposit is
    "not an author but a keeper, not a teacher but a disciple, not a leader but a follower."
  evidential: >-
    Sulpitius (Life chs. X, XXV; Letters II, III; Dialogues III.10), Cassian (Institutes IV.40;
    Conferences II.15, XI.4), Vincent (Commonitory ch. 22). Lerins's named succession of teachers
    reaches the build only editorially. The Latin lemmas are not attested in this sense.
  personal: >-
    A disciple among us is formed by what he sees his master do - his power at Tours, his rule at
    Marseilles - and becomes in turn what other men see. Even our most authoritative teacher calls
    himself a follower, and a master's word is worth only what his life has already done. That is why
    our literature is written by disciples about masters.
  translational: >-
    'A disciple - like a student in a class, learning doctrine from a teacher?' - not a curriculum: a
    man formed by living beside a named master and watching what he does, whose authority is only as
    strong as his own performance of it; and, for the faith itself, a keeper who refuses to be an
    author.
quick_meaning: >-
  A named master and the men formed by watching him. Even our great teacher calls himself a disciple,
  "not a leader but a follower."
