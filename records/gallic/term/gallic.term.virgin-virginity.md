senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the virgin is made by the bishop's hands and honoured by his refusal to be seen.
    Arborius "consecrated the girl to God, and devoted her to perpetual virginity," and would have
    no one but Martin place on her "the dress characteristic of virginity." Martin's parable of the
    meadow sets the order: the grazed part is marriage, the ruined part fornication, the flowered
    part virginity - "marriage belongs to those things which may be excused, while virginity points
    to glory, and fornication must incur punishment." A wife's "first excellence ... is, that she
    should not be seen." The virgin who "did not admit to her presence Martin himself" is praised
    above priests. At his funeral "there was the choir of virgins." In the south what our fathers
    taught at length about chastity was said in a conference our English text does not contain.
  evidential: >-
    Directly attested at Tours (Vita XIX; Letter III; Dial. II.2, II.8, II.10-12). At Marseilles
    only the edge survives in our English text (Inst. I.1, II.13, IV.16, X.2; Conf. XIV.7); the
    south's own conference On Chastity and two related books were removed by a nineteenth-century
    hand and are not supplied from elsewhere. Two women's houses stood; no woman in either speaks.
  personal: >-
    We had consecrated virgins and women's houses at Tours and at Marseilles. What we called their
    state was "glory" and "victory," in our own words. The sources let none of them be heard, and
    we will not put words in their mouths.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer may see mere abstinence, or repression, or an oppression we ought to have
    recognized - or may assume the south was silent on chastity. For us virginity was a dressed,
    episcopally-conferred state that "points to glory"; and the south's teaching on chastity
    existed, was written, and was cut from our English text by a later hand - a silence not ours.
quick_meaning: >-
  A consecrated state, given by the bishop's hands, with its own dress. Marriage is excused;
  virginity points to glory. The south's own teaching on chastity was cut from our English text.
