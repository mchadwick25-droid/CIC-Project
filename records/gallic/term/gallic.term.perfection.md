plain_meaning: >-
  For us perfection is the summit of a graded ascent, not a state of being without fault. It is the
  declared subject of the Institutes, "the attainment of the perfect life." It is reached "not ...
  simply by self-denial ... unless there is that charity," climbed "from this fear to hope, from hope
  to the love of God." And at the summit we confess it is a thing "we cannot even make the effort to
  obtain ... without the mercy and inspiration of God."
world_word: perfection / "the perfect life"
false_friend:
- perfectionism - flawlessness, sinlessness, an impossible standard producing anxiety
- the perfect as an elite class
- a Pelagian claim to be without sin
senses:
  informational: >-
    Cassian says in his first pages what his books are for: leaving aside the miracles, he will write
    "about the way to improve our character, and the attainment of the perfect life, in accordance
    with that which we received from our elders." Perfection is a road with stages. Moses ranks the
    practices beneath it - fasting, vigils, reading "are not perfection, but aids to perfection" - and
    names its substance: none avails "unless there is that charity." The manual's ladder ends "by
    purity of heart the perfection of apostolic love is acquired." Paphnutius says the first
    renunciation "is not enough of itself to confer perfection"; Chaeremon sets three restraints and
    only the last is perfection - "from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God"; Nesteros
    divides it: "this practical perfection depends on a double system; for its first method is to know
    the nature of all faults and the manner of their cure." And at the top the manual turns:
    "perfection cannot possibly be gained without these [efforts], but ... by these only without the
    grace of God nobody can ever attain it"; indeed "we cannot even make the effort to obtain
    perfection without the mercy and inspiration of God."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian (Institutes Preface, IV.39, IV.43, X.5, XII.14-16; Conferences Preface I, I.6-7, III.10,
    XI.7, XIV.3), received. Sulpitius has only the ordinary sense. Conf. XII is excised and Conf. XXIII
    unread; nothing from them is supplied. The Latin lemma is not attested.
  personal: >-
    The perfect monk among us is the one who knows he did not make himself. We can be told to aim at
    perfection and told in the same book that we cannot make the effort alone, and we do not find that
    a contradiction: it is a graded ascent with love at the top, measured by a goal and not by
    faultlessness, whose practices are aids and whose summit is a confession.
  translational: >-
    'Perfection - do you mean sinlessness, an impossible standard?' - no: a graded ascent with charity
    as its substance and love at its top, measured by a goal rather than by faultlessness, whose stages
    are fear, hope and love, and whose summit is the confession that neither the effort nor the
    understanding of the effort is one's own; at Tours, simply a word of praise.
quick_meaning: >-
  The summit of a graded ascent - fear, then hope, then love - with charity as its substance. Not
  flawlessness, and never reached without grace.
