senses:
  informational: >-
    Salvian, addressing Romans who go to the games, calls the people of the monasteries "the
    religious" and describes them from outside as men who chose their lot: "Religious men are
    lowly - they wish to be so; poor - they delight in poverty; without ambition - they spurn it;
    unesteemed - they flee from honors." When Carthage jeers at "servants of God," he writes of "the
    hatred of the Africans for the monks - that is, for the servants of God," glossing the monks'
    own word. The same class-noun names the lapsed: "the religious who have gained some reputation
    by a general repentance and now seek after new honors."
  evidential: >-
    Attested in Salvian alone (Gov. I.2, V.10, VIII.4, in Sanford's English), an outsider to the
    cell speaking of us to lapsed Romans.
  personal: >-
    What the cell calls a monk, the city calls a religious. It is how a neighbour named us, and it
    names the lapsed among us as readily as the faithful.
  translational: >-
    Not "religious" as a compliment about inner devotion, but a social class-noun for a whole
    category of people - lowly, poor, unesteemed by choice - the way a city presbyter named the
    monks.
quick_meaning: >-
  Salvian's word for us, from the city: a class of people who chose to be lowly, poor, and
  unesteemed. His gloss on "the monks."
