plain_meaning: >-
  For us humility is the root from which discretion and patience grow, and the only ground on which
  power may be held. Cassian gives it ten checkable signs. A count's son wins "the true nobility" by
  hawking baskets. At Tours it is "the same humility in his heart" that a bishop keeps from his cell,
  sitting on a "rude little stool, such as those in use by the lowest of servants."
world_word: humility (humilitas)
false_friend:
- modesty, self-deprecation, or low self-esteem
- a temperament
- a virtue opposed to confidence and leadership
senses:
  informational: >-
    Cassian makes it measurable: "Humility is attested by these signs: First of all if a man has all
    his desires mortified; secondly, if he conceals none of his actions or even of his thoughts from
    his senior," and so on through ten, ending in "love which knows no fear." It is what obedience
    buys - "that humility of Christ which is the true nobility" - and what a famous abbot goes looking
    for when honour has found him: Pinufius "sought a distant monastery where he could be received as a
    novice." It is the one source of discretion, and Nesteros sets it above every wonder: "Humility
    therefore is the mistress of all virtues, it is the surest foundation of the heavenly building."
    The last book of the formation manual teaches the monk to say of every virtue "Not I, but the
    grace of God with me" - "this then is that humility towards God." At Tours it is what does not
    change when a monk is made bishop: "the same humility in his heart, and the same homeliness in his
    garments"; no chair, but "a rude little stool, such as those in use by the lowest of servants,
    which we Gallic country-people call tripets"; and at eventide he himself washed his guest's feet.
    Vincent brings it to the harbour as "the sacrifice of Christian humility."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian (Institutes IV.29-30, IV.39, XII.19; Conferences II.10, XV.7, XVIII.13), Sulpitius (Life
    chs. IX, X; Dialogues II.1), Vincent (Commonitory ch. 1), Gennadius on Sulpitius himself. Hilary of
    Arles's Latin attests the lemma inflected.
  personal: >-
    Across every voice of ours it is the same thing: the one virtue that survives elevation. It is why
    the south distrusts miracle-fame, why an abbot becomes a novice again, and why a bishop's stool
    matters. And it is our answer, in the south, to the north's power - the "mistress of all virtues"
    set against the exorcist-saint.
  translational: >-
    'Humility - modesty, thinking little of yourself?' - for us a virtue with ten checkable signs, won
    by obedience, the sole source of discretion, and the "mistress" set above miracles; the thing a
    famous abbot fled his own house to recover, and at Tours the unchanged heart and garments of a
    bishop on a servant's stool.
quick_meaning: >-
  A virtue with ten checkable signs, won by obedience and set above miracles. The one virtue that
  survives being made a bishop.
