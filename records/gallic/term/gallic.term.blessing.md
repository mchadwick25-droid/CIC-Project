plain_meaning: >-
  At Tours, a power that passes from the saint into things - his touch, his word, the oil he
  blesses, threads from his robe, the straw he lay on - and heals.

senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the blessing is asked for on the knees and felt on the head - Sulpitius "embraced his
    sacred knees, and begged for his blessing according to custom," and felt Martin's hand on his
    head amid "the solemn words of benediction." It passes into things: oil sent to be blessed
    "such is the custom" multiplies under the blessing and overflows the jar; a blessed vessel
    knocked from a high window onto marble is found safe "as if it had fallen on the softest
    feathers"; threads from his sackcloth "wrought frequent miracles upon those who were sick"; a
    letter of Martin laid in a fevered girl's bosom drives the fever off; the virgins divide among
    themselves the very straw he lay on. Even his name carries it - a heathen merchant cries "Save
    us, O God of Martin," and the tempest is stilled. At Marseilles the word means only what it
    ordinarily means.
  evidential: >-
    Directly attested in Sulpitius alone (Vita XVIII-XIX; Letter II; Dial. II.8, II.12, III.3,
    III.14). Cassian's "blessing" is ordinary or liturgical. This is single-voice by node, and the
    historicity of the events falls under virtus / power.
  personal: >-
    At Tours we asked it on our knees and carried it home in a thread. The blessing of God bestowed
    on a vessel by Martin "could not possibly perish." This is how the saint's ministry reached where
    his body did not, and how we began to remember him in things while he still lived.
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer thinks of a blessing as a kind word or a prayer, and of relics as a medieval
    superstition. For us at Tours it was a transferable power, measured by what it did - and at
    Marseilles our teachers' warnings about wonders left no place for a saint's straw at all.
quick_meaning: >-
  At Tours, a power that passes from the saint into oil, thread, straw, a letter, even his name -
  asked on the knees and measured by what it heals. At Marseilles, only the ordinary word.
