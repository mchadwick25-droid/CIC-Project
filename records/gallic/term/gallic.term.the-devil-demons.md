senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the enemy has a face and a voice. On the road past Milan "the devil met him in the way,
    having assumed the form of a man," and Martin answered him with Scripture. Martin "held him so
    visible and ever under the power of his eyes" that he knew him "under whatever guise he
    appeared" - as Jupiter, Mercury, Minerva, even as a purple-robed Christ. Under exorcism the
    demons "revealed their names, too, of their own accord." Martin, resisting him firmly, even
    offered him the mercy of Christ if he would repent. At Marseilles the adversary is no less real
    and less often seen: "our cunning adversary" who cannot circumvent a monk once thoughts are
    disclosed; the one who came to Abbot John "in the shape of a filthy Ethiopian" and to Heron as
    "an angel of Satan as an angel of light." The rule of our whole southern discipline: "a thought
    is from the devil if we are ashamed to disclose it." At Lerins Vincent hears him quote
    Scripture, "the members of the Devil" speaking to "the members of Christ" in the Church's own
    words.
  evidential: >-
    Attested in all three founding voices: Sulpitius (Vita VI, XXI, XXII, XXIV; Letter III; Dial.
    III.6), Cassian (Inst. I.8, IV.9, IV.37; Conf. I.21, II.5, II.13, XVIII.15), Vincent (Comm.
    25-26). What is Sulpitius's alone is the emphasis on visibility; whether the appearances
    happened is a question this record does not settle.
  personal: >-
    Nowhere among us is he a figure of speech. He is what the soldier fights, what the cross
    repels, what our elders' discretion detects, and what our last words on a deathbed are spoken
    to - "Why do you stand here, thou bloody monster? Thou shalt find nothing in me."
  translational: >-
    A modern hearer wants to read our devil as psychology - inner conflict, a shadow, a primitive
    name for illness. That is the one reading we cannot allow. Ours is a named, embodied, sometimes
    visible adversary who takes the shapes of the old gods, who can be spoken to and even offered
    mercy, and who quotes Scripture in church.
quick_meaning: >-
  A real and present enemy, sometimes visible. Martin knew him in any shape and spoke to him. Our
  elders find him in the thought we are ashamed to tell. Never a figure of speech.
