plain_meaning: >-
  For us "the world" is what a monk has left - kin, property, honours, delights, "secular business."
  We name it from the far side of the renunciation, as a warfare one has been discharged from and a
  sea one has come out of. We never name it as a place from which God is absent.

senses:
  informational: >-
    Everyone speaks of the world from outside it. Vincent begins his book by saying why: "whereas I
    was at one time involved in the manifold and deplorable tempests of secular warfare, I have now at
    length, under Christ's auspices, cast anchor in the harbour of religion." Abbot Moses greets the
    two Gauls as men who "despised the love of kinsfolk, and scorned your fatherland, and the delights
    of this world." The new monk is stripped so that he may know "that he has laid aside all worldly
    pride," and his worldly clothes are kept by the steward in case he cools. The worst thing accidie
    does is send the soldier of Christ back: he "becomes a runaway from His service, and a deserter,
    and 'entangles himself in secular business.'" Martin presses on Sulpitius "that the allurements of
    this world and secular burdens were to be abandoned." But the world is not godless: Abbot John
    marvels at a layman "living in the world and bound by the ties of marriage" who casts out a demon
    by "the utterance of his name."
  evidential: >-
    Vincent (Commonitory ch. 1), Cassian (Conferences I.2, XIV.7; Institutes IV.5-6, X.3), Sulpitius
    (Life ch. XXV), Eucherius (rough OCR), and Salvian's view of the province under present judgment
    (carried at the government of God, not here). Eucherius's De Contemptu Mundi is unswept.
  personal: >-
    Our words for it are a soldier's and a sailor's: a set of burdens and allurements we have been
    discharged from and sailed out of, named always from the harbour. The line we crossed is one of
    profession, not of power - a married man in the world can still command a demon - and in one
    Marseilles voice, God's judgment is being carried out in that world now, in the ruin of the
    provinces, under the same God.
  translational: >-
    'By "secular" do you mean non-religious, and by "the world" society you despised?' - no: "secular"
    for us means "before I came in," what one has been discharged from, and "the world" is a place of
    burdens and allurements, not of God's absence, where a married layman may still cast out a demon.
quick_meaning: >-
  What a monk has left - kin, property, honours, business - named from the harbour as a warfare
  one was discharged from. Never a place where God is absent.
