plain_meaning: >-
  For us a conference is an elder's discourse drawn out by disciples' questions. It is the after-supper
  talk of an Egyptian cell that Cassian writes down so that our monks may receive the fathers "into
  their cells ... talking with them after a fashion by daily questions and answers." By Gennadius's
  word, it is also the form in which Sulpitius cast his account of Martin.
world_word: conference (collatio)

senses:
  informational: >-
    The thing exists in Egypt before it is a book. Cassian and Germanus come to Abbot Moses's cell,
    "and when after supper the spiritual conference had begun to be held, and the old man in answer to
    the questions which they had propounded was speaking about the sin of gluttony and the dominion of
    secret thoughts," a young brother pulls the stolen biscuit from his dress and confesses. Every
    teaching Cassian carries west has this shape: a question from the two Gauls, an old man's answer,
    "a sort of fire" that "supplies keener zest to our conference." Cassian says why the form matters -
    these things, "unless they are often discussed and well worn in frequent conferences with
    spiritual men, ... quickly fade away." For Gaul the conference becomes a way of having an elder
    one cannot see: the last Conferences are dedicated so that monks near Gallic houses, "receiving
    into their cells the authors of the Conferences together with the actual volumes ... may not be
    left to their own resources." And the north, without the word, keeps the form: Gennadius calls
    Sulpitius's Dialogues "a Conference between Postumianus and Gallus."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian (Institutes Preface; Conferences Prefaces I and III, I.23, II.11, XVIII.11), framed as
    Egypt's practice; Gennadius applies the word to both authors. Collatio in the ancient text is only
    Vincent's creed-etymology; the Cassianic sense of Collationes is editorial.
  personal: >-
    Our southern teaching is all reported speech - its authority is that someone heard it from a named
    father. And a Gallic monk's elder could be a book: the junior with the volume in his cell asks his
    questions of the page, and the page talks back.
  translational: >-
    'Are "the Conferences" Cassian's own systematic theology?' - no: they are an elder's answers to
    disciples' questions after supper, written down to be re-asked in a Gallic cell, teaching whose
    whole authority is that someone heard it from a named father.
quick_meaning: >-
  An elder's after-supper answers to his disciples' questions, written down. For a Gallic monk, an
  elder who is a book, and who talks back.
