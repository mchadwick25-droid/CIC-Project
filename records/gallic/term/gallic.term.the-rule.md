plain_meaning: >-
  Vincent's test at Lerins for telling "the truth of Catholic faith from the falsehood of heretical
  pravity." Hold "that faith which has been believed everywhere, always, by all." When a part, a
  novelty, or a few dissent, prefer the whole, antiquity, and consent. Consent means an ancient
  council, or the ancients collated.
world_word: the rule (of faith) - universality, antiquity, consent
false_friend:
- the Vincentian canon as a slogan in Anglican, Roman Catholic, or Orthodox polemic about which church is the true one
- a tidy three-part formula applied mechanically
- a monastic Rule
senses:
  informational: >-
    The rule exists because Scripture alone, though complete, is read in more than one way: "owing to
    the depth of Holy Scripture, all do not accept it in one and the same sense." So belief is fortified
    "first, by the authority of the Divine Law, and then, by the Tradition of the Catholic Church," and
    within the Church by holding "that faith which has been believed everywhere, always, by all ...
    This rule we shall observe if we follow universality, antiquity, consent." It comes with a
    procedure for each way it can fail: if a part breaks off, prefer the whole body; if a novelty
    threatens all, hold to antiquity; if within antiquity a few err, prefer "an ancient General
    Council"; if no council has spoken, "collate and consult and interrogate the opinions of the
    ancients" and hold what "all, or the more part" have held, "forming, as it were, a consentient
    council of doctors." Against that consent no single teacher stands, "be he a bishop, be he a
    Confessor, be he a martyr." Its cases are remembered: Stephen's "Let there be no innovation -
    nothing but what has been handed down"; Ephesus, which "innovated nothing." The same test governs
    what a monk wears (Cassian's antiquity, numbers, unanimity) and whether a bishop blesses a tomb.
  evidential: >-
    Single-voice as a formulated rule of faith: Vincent, Commonitory chs. 1-3, 6, 24, 28, 31, 32, the
    434 date documented by internal reference to Ephesus. Cassian's Institutes I.2 and II.4 state the
    same criterion for monastic custom without citing Vincent; Sulpitius's Life ch. XI shows the
    instinct at Tours. Gennadius corroborates Vincent's person and pseudonym. Vincent's own Latin word
    for "rule" is not given in the vendored translation, and the volume's two regula witnesses are the
    editor's. Heurtley's Appendix III and his Newman footnote are the in-file loci of the two contests
    and are editorial.
  personal: >-
    What the rule does to authority is the deepest thing about it. No office settles a question by
    itself - not a bishop's, not a confessor's, not a martyr's - and the fathers who settle it do so by
    agreeing, in writing, across times and places. A monk on an island, anxious about "new heretics"
    and his own failing memory, wrote it down so as not to forget it. It is not for every question:
    the pains are to be taken "where the Rule of Faith is concerned."
  translational: >-
    'Isn't this the "Vincentian canon" churches quote to prove they are the true one?' - that use is
    later and not ours: what Vincent wrote was a memory-aid with a stepwise procedure, whose hardest
    cases - great teachers erring - sit inside it, which also governs what a monk wears and how many
    psalms he sings, and which passes authority from a face to a consensus of texts; its later career
    in confessional argument must not enter our voice.
quick_meaning: >-
  Vincent's test for the faith: what has been believed everywhere, always, by all. Prefer the whole
  to the part, the old to the new, and the consent of the ancients to any one teacher.
