plain_meaning: >-
  For us the sign of the cross is a gesture with real force. It is the thing Martin will go into
  battle "protected by ... and not by shield or helmet." It halts a funeral crowd in its tracks and
  turns a falling pine. Cassian's monk, armed with his staff, uses it to "beat off" invisible beasts.
  An act of power against demons and elements, not a devotional habit.

senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the sign is a weapon and is called one. The discharged soldier offers to stand before the
    line "protected by the sign of the cross, and not by shield or helmet." Meeting rustics carrying
    what he takes for "the images of demons veiled with a white covering," he lifts "the sign of the
    cross opposite to them" and they stand rooted until he releases them. When the heathen fell a
    sacred pine to crush him, "simply holding up his hand against it, he put in its way the sign of
    salvation," and the tree spins away "after the manner of a spinning-top." Trapped by fire, he
    fails while he wrestles with the door and succeeds "as soon as he again sought assistance from the
    cross, and tried the weapons of prayer." At Marseilles the same act appears once, as the last item
    of the monk's kit: he carries a staff because he must never "walk unarmed among so many barking
    dogs of faults and invisible beasts of spiritual wickedness," and "ought to beat them off with the
    sign of the cross."
  evidential: >-
    Sulpitius (Life chs. IV, XII, XIII; Letters I, II), repeatedly; Cassian once in the dress-and-
    weapons idiom (Institutes I.8) and once of an abbot signing oil (V.30). The Latin lemma is not
    attested. The events' historicity is governed by virtus's Reported-Experience Status, not assessed
    here.
  personal: >-
    The cross is the soldier's weapon after the discharge - what our Life says replaces shield and
    helmet. What Tours does before a crowd, the south does in the cell against the unseen; but it is
    the same weapon, and it is the north's exorcism and mission in one motion.
  translational: >-
    'Crossing yourself - a pious habit, or a superstition?' - neither, for us: a weapon that replaces
    shield and helmet, with visible effect on crowds, trees, fire and demons, and in the south the last
    arm of the monk's kit against "invisible beasts."
quick_meaning: >-
  A gesture with real force - the soldier's weapon in place of shield and helmet, halting crowds,
  turning a falling tree, beating off demons.
