plain_meaning: >-
  The office our monks keep being taken into. A monk "carried off," "escorted under guard," "bound to
  the long-avoided office," who then keeps "the objects and virtues of a monk" in a see. And at the
  same time our own formation teaching names the desire for that office as vainglory's suggestion
  and the devil's pretext.
world_word: bishop / the monk-bishop
false_friend:
- a bishop as an administrator or prelate
- monk and bishop as opposed careers, so that a monk-bishop is either a promotion or a sell-out
- Pope as the bishop of Rome only
- episcopal election as a formal procedure
senses:
  informational: >-
    At Tours the bishop is made by a trick and against bishops: Martin is lured from his monastery by a
    citizen "pretending that his wife was ill," escorted "under a kind of guard," and wanted by the
    crowd, while "some of the bishops ... were impiously offering resistance, asserting forsooth that
    Martin's person was contemptible ... that his clothing was mean, and his hair disgusting." And then
    he does not change: he "kept up the position of a bishop properly, yet in such a way as not to lay
    aside the objects and virtues of a monk." The office costs him: less power as bishop; his
    calumniators "no others than bishops!"; after the Priscillianist executions he holds that "a
    secular ruler should be judge in an ecclesiastical cause" was "a foul and unheard-of indignity,"
    and "never again did he attend a synod." At Marseilles the office is the addressee and the danger
    at once: every book is written for a bishop or a man about to be one ("most blessed Pope Castor";
    Honoratus, a presiding brother in one preface and "a Bishop blessed in name as well as merits" in
    the next), while the Egyptian fathers say the devil "incites a man to desire the holy office of the
    clergy under the pretext of edifying many people," and the one Egyptian bishop Cassian describes,
    Archebius, "complained that he had been expelled from the monastic system as unworthy of it." At
    Lerins the founder is told as a capture: "the priestly fillet fastens on its fugitive."
  evidential: >-
    Three voices, both nodes: Sulpitius (Life chs. IX, X, XX, XXVI, XXVII; Dialogues II.4, III.13;
    Sacred History II.50), Cassian (Institutes Preface, II.3, V.1, XI.14, XII.30; Conferences I.20,
    Prefaces II and III, XI.2), Vincent (Commonitory chs. 28-29), plus Hilary of Arles's own Latin
    (rough OCR) and Gennadius ("monk and bishop"; Faustus "first abbot ... and then made bishop"). The
    Latin episcopus and sacerdotium are attested repeatedly in Hilary's Latin. Gibson's footnote on
    papa and Roberts's summus sacerdos footnote are editorial. Lerins's internal life is the ecology's
    least-documented institution.
  personal: >-
    The shape is the same in every house of ours: the office is fled and it overtakes; the man who
    wants it has gone cold; the man who has it keeps the monk's humility inside it, loses some of his
    power to it, and forms the men who will be seized after him. What city or church would not want its
    priests from Martin's monastery? To us that is not a contradiction. It is what a monk is for, and
    what a monk must never seek.
  translational: >-
    'Wasn't becoming a bishop either a promotion or a sell-out for a monk?' - neither, for us: the
    office was something one was seized into by a crowd, a trick, or a "fillet," it cost the saint his
    power, our own formation manual named wanting it as vainglory, and inside it a monk kept "the
    objects and virtues of a monk" - and a bishop's singular teaching still counted for nothing against
    the consent of the ancients.
quick_meaning: >-
  The office our monks are seized into and must never seek. Fled, then overtaken; kept with a monk's
  humility; costing the saint some of his power.
