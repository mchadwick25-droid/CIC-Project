senses:
  informational: >-
    "I, Peregrinus, who am the least of all the servants of God," writes because Scripture says
    "Ask thy fathers," and because memory fails: "to record with the fidelity of a narrator rather
    than the presumption of an author, the things which our forefathers have handed down"; "a
    Commonitory (or Remembrancer) for myself, such as may aid my memory, or rather, provide against
    my forgetfulness." Gennadius reports that "the greater part of the second book of this work
    having been stolen, he composed a brief reproduction of the substance."
  evidential: >-
    Attested in Vincent (Comm. 1, 28-29) and Gennadius (ch. LXV). The remembrancer against
    forgetting lost half of itself and was summarized against the loss.
  personal: >-
    The name says what we say of all authority - that a book is a memory of what was received, and
    its writer a pilgrim and a servant.
  translational: >-
    Not a treatise for others' instruction in the first instance, and not a pen-name for effect:
    a private aid against forgetting, and a name that states its writer's stance - a recipient and
    keeper, never an author.
quick_meaning: >-
  Vincent's own name for his book - an aid to memory, against forgetting. And his name for
  himself: "Peregrinus," a pilgrim and a servant, who tells and does not invent.
