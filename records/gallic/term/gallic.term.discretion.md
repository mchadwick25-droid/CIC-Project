plain_meaning: >-
  In Cassian's Marseilles, discretion is the virtue by which every other is kept from destroying
  itself. It is "the mother of all virtues, as well as their guardian and regulator," the "eye and
  light of the body," the "royal road" between excess and slackness. It is gained "only ... by true
  humility." That means testing every thought, like a good money-changer testing coin, against the
  judgment of the elders.
world_word: discretion (discretio)
false_friend:
- tact, prudence, or confidentiality
- discernment as a private spiritual intuition or a decision-making technique
- moderation as common sense
senses:
  informational: >-
    The teaching comes as a verdict. Abbot Moses reports a night's council in the desert at which "by
    the judgment of the blessed Antony as well as of all others it has been laid down that it is
    discretion which leads a fearless monk by fixed stages to God." Every other virtue fails without
    it: the men who fasted, watched and gave alms most fiercely are the ones who fell, for "discretion
    is the mother of all virtues, as well as their guardian and regulator." It "teaches a monk always
    to walk along the royal road," neither "puffed up on the right hand of virtue" nor slack with
    "luke-warmness"; "extremes meet. For excess of fasting and gluttony come to the same thing." The
    proof is old Heron, fifty years in the desert, who "preferred to be guided by his own judgment
    rather than to obey the counsels and conference of the brethren," received "an angel of Satan as an
    angel of light" and cast himself down a well. Where it comes from is the heart of the teaching:
    "True discretion ... is only secured by true humility," whose first proof is reserving every
    thought "for the scrutiny of the elders." The monk is a "good money-changer," testing whether a
    thought bears the stamp of "the approved Catholic fathers" or "some conceit of novelty."
  evidential: >-
    Cassian only (Conferences I.20, II.1-16), framed as received from Antony's council. Sulpitius shows
    the practice at Tours - Martin's refusal of Anatolius's robe and the purple-robed Christ - without
    the vocabulary. The Latin lemma is not attested in the vendored translation; Faustus's treatise
    carries it by grep only.
  personal: >-
    Discretion for us is not a private faculty but the elders' judgment made one's own - "the power of
    discretion which comes from the words and warnings of the fathers." It is "no earthly thing ...
    but the greatest prize of divine grace," and it is why the south ranks moderation above heroic
    fasting, and why a monk's judgment is never his own. We speak of it as received.
  translational: >-
    'Discretion - you mean tact, or knowing when to keep quiet?' - no: the regulator of all the
    virtues, awarded that title by a council of Egyptian fathers, whose whole substance is the elders'
    judgment received through humility and disclosure, a money-changer's test of every thought on a
    royal road where "extremes meet" - a gift of grace, not a temperament.
quick_meaning: >-
  The virtue that keeps every other virtue from ruining itself. The royal road between excess and
  slackness, gained only by showing one's thoughts to the elders.
