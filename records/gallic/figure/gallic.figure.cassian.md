The Marseilles node's founder and the transmitter of all six of its stories - and the protagonist, with
Germanus, of the two of them that are his own first-person encounters (Archebius; the scruple at
morning service), which is what makes narratable: true a real finding and not a default. The other
four he transmits: an origin-tradition he refers to "ecclesiastical history" for, an unplaced example,
an apophthegm, and an elder's telling of another man's youth - Tier 2 by Doc_09 §7(a)'s text-by-text
test (does the author attest his own presence at the event, or only at its telling?). He is related to
all six because in each the Story Text names him as the one who "handed on," "set down," or "records";
the reception discipline (Doc_04 §7; Doc_05 §7.2; Doc_06 §4(a)) runs through him - every southern story
is Egypt's own content, transmitted, not invented, and every story record says so in the narrative.

What is not tellable of him, declared: no story narrates an event at Marseilles itself, and none shows
a Gallic monk keeping or failing to keep the program (Doc_09 §8 item 4) - his own first-person witness
to Gaul is critique ("we cannot recollect any one who joined our monasteries keeping it up unbroken even
for a year," Inst. IV.2), not story; no Native voice narrates the controversy (Doc_09 §8 item 5) -
Prosper's report, Celestine's letter, Augustine's treatises arriving, his own Contra Collatorem
reception; and the three excised texts (Doc_09 §8 item 6) hold whatever exempla they held. Germanus,
his companion in three stories, is not given a figure record - Cassian's companion, never Germanus of
Auxerre (row 44, Excluded), and carried entirely by the stories. The Heron-and-the-well exemplum (Conf.
II.5) and the elder who laid his table six times (Inst. V.25) are Doc_09 §9 item 6's first candidates
for any future extension, not built.
