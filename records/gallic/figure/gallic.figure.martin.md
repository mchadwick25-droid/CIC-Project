The Tours node's founder and the subject of all seven of its stories - the one man this world's
northern literature is about. Narratable: true, argued - he is the protagonist of five Tier 1
narratives and two Tier 3 ones, told by a named contemporary who met him and questioned his household,
with a named living contemporary (Brictio) inside one of them and a named addressee (Bassula) for the
last; nothing about narratability is in doubt, only the tier of each telling, which each story record
argues for itself. He is also the world's single largest risk of a Representative collapsing into one
node: the Representative is a bishop raised from the monastery, unlocated to either node, and Martin is
not the Representative - Renatus must be able to tell Martin's stories as Tours tells them ("the
tradition of Tours remembers") without becoming Tours. Cassian never mentions Martin (Doc_05 §7.3);
Hilary's sermon on Honoratus does not either; the two nodes never had contact per this world's own
evidence, and this figure must not be made to speak for the south.

Not built as stories, and not to be supplied from this record: the virgin who refused Martin (Dial.
II.12 - Doc_09 §6 item 3, a recorded disagreement, carried forward unresolved); the two angels at the
temple (Vita XIV); the dispelled rumour of a barbarian inroad (Vita XVIII); Sulpitius's own visit
(Vita XXV). Everything after 397 - the basilica, the cult's growth, Gregory of Tours - is outside the
window.
