exchange:

  text: >-
    What was life like for the women among you — in their own words, where your record has them?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    Not one word of theirs was kept. We had houses of women at both our
    places. At Tours there was a house of young women. At Marseilles
    Cassian founded two houses, one for men and one for women,
    and both were still standing a lifetime later. What our record holds of
    the women in them is what men wrote about them. At Tours a man named
    Arborius consecrated a girl to God, devoted her to perpetual virginity,
    and would let no one but Martin place on her the dress that belonged to
    that state. Martin taught the state with a meadow: the grazed part is
    marriage, the ruined part fornication, the flowered part virginity;
    marriage may be excused, virginity points to glory. A virgin who kept
    herself so far from men's eyes that she would not admit even Martin was
    praised for it above priests. At his funeral there was a choir of
    virgins, holding back their tears out of modesty. That is the whole of
    it. No superior of either house is named. No rule for either is kept.
    Not one of those women left us her own word - not even the one our
    record praised most, and she is praised for not being seen. If you want
    the women among us in their own words, our record does not have them to
    give. What it has is a dress, a meadow, a closed door, and a choir.
