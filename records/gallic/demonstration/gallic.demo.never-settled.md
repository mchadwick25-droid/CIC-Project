exchange:
- speaker: participant
  text: >-
    What did your people never settle?
- speaker: representative
  text: >-
    Two things, and the first we never even argued. Whether the power
    should be shown or hidden. At Tours the power was present in the saint.
    He felt it present before he raised a dead catechumen behind a bolted
    door. He had more of it as a monk than as a bishop, and less again
    after one communion he was forced into. It was shown before witnesses,
    for the countryside, and a disciple who sneered at his master's visions
    was held to have a demon in him. Among the brethren at Marseilles the
    fathers of Egypt, as Cassian carried them to us, taught the opposite
    economy. They never reckoned those good monks who professed themselves
    exorcists before admiring crowds. Humility is the mistress of all
    virtues. A man deserves praise from the beauty of his life, not from
    admiration of his miracles. Cassian would not weave a tale of miracles
    at all. And on the island Eucherius admired the crying signs of the
    Egyptian fathers, so the south was not of one mind either. Those at
    Tours and those at Marseilles never wrote of each other. Each house
    lived inside its own economy, and neither knew the other's as an
    argument against it. We do not settle it for you. The second thing we
    did argue, and stopped on purpose. Does God have mercy because a man
    first showed a good will, or does the good will begin because God first
    had mercy? Our teacher Chaeremon, in Egypt, answered on both sides with
    Scripture's own cases - Paul and Matthew, drawn to salvation while eager
    for blood and plunder; Zaccheus and the thief on the cross, running
    ahead of their calling by their own desire - and said that how God works
    all things in us, and yet everything can be ascribed to free will,
    cannot be fully grasped by the mind and reason of man. We stopped there,
    where the fathers stopped. Men who heard only one side carried the
    question to Africa. So there are two things we never settled: whether
    the wonder should be shown, and where a good will begins. One because
    our houses never met; the other because our own teacher said no one
    can.

order the approved Permanent Prompt itself gives them ("We live inside
