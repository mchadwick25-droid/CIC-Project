claim: >-
  Conference XIII's teaching that "first beginnings of a good will" can "have arisen from our own
  efforts" is Cassian's own position, stated against Augustine: a considered reply to On Rebuke and
  Grace that keeps the beginning of faith on man's side and leaves its increase to God - the position
  Prosper's Contra Collatorem extracted from the Conference and condemned.
held_against:
- >-
  Cassian's own text says both, and says on purpose that both one-sided answers are errors. "The
  initiative not only of our actions but also of good thoughts comes from God, who inspires us with a
  good will to begin with" (XIII.3), the spark "He Himself has struck ... out of the hard flints of our
  hearts" (XIII.7), and Paphnutius's "the beginning of our good will is given to us by the inspiration
  of the Lord" (III.19) stand beside XIII.8's "arisen from our own efforts" and XIII.9's "first
  beginnings of a good will arise." The question is put in both directions at XIII.11 with cases on
  each side - Paul and Matthew drawn unwilling, Zaccheus and the thief coming first - and the answer is
  a declared limit: it "cannot be fully grasped by the mind and reason of man" (XIII.18). Which phrasing
  is "his position" is a question the text refuses to settle.
- >-
  The reading that fixed one phrasing as the position is the opponent's. Contra Collatorem, the work
  that extracted twelve propositions from this Conference and judged all but the first erroneous, is
  unvendored and known here through Gibson's prolegomena; its author never names Cassian. Augustine's
  own statement of what the brethren hold - "faith itself of ourselves, but ... its increase is of God"
  - is his characterization of a report by Prosper and Hilary whose own letters (Epp. 225-226) are
  absent from this library. The summary the claim rests on reaches this record at two removes and on
  one side.
- >-
  Whether the Conference answers Augustine at all is Contested. The vendored edition dates Conf.
  XI-XVII "not later than" 426, before even On Rebuke and Grace on that dating; Chadwick redated
  Honoratus's episcopate specifically to let XIII respond to Augustine, and Casiday rejects the
  redating and finds little direct anti-Augustinian polemic in the Conference (Doc_01 section 7; Doc_08
  section 7, named tension 1). If the Conference precedes the treatise, "stated against Augustine" is
  not a description of the text but of its later reception.
- >-
  The teaching exists as formation before it exists as polemic. Doc_05 found the same two-sided
  doctrine at Inst. XII.9-19, the formation manual's remedy for pride, with "Not I, but the grace of God
  with me" as the sentence a monk says of every virtue and XII.33 as the manual's own closing sentence
  - written for Castor's new monastery before any report went to Africa. Conf. XIII itself arises as a
  scruple at morning service (XIII.1). A doctrine that closes a formation manual is not obviously a
  position in a controversy.
- >-
  The Conference reaches modern readers through a text one intermediary cut to remove exactly this
  and through an editor who labels it before it is read: Dionysius Carthusianus's paraphrase "omits all
  that savours of Semi-Pelagianism; and from c. viii. onward there are large omissions and various
  suggestive alterations," and Gibson's own headnote to XIII is titled "On the Semi-Pelagianism of this
  Conference." The claim's tidy shape is partly the transmission's.
concedes: >-
  Not contested: Cassian's text is intact and vendored, both phrasings are genuinely his, a real
  controversy followed within participants' lifetimes (Prosper's appeal, Celestine's letter, Contra
  Collatorem, Gennadius's "at variance"), and the position genuinely differed from Augustine's on the
  further questions - Documented. What cannot be settled from this record is which of Cassian's two
  phrasings is his position, or whether asking is already to have left the world: its own teacher
  answered on both sides with Scripture's cases and declared the relation beyond reason's grasp, and
  the one work written to settle the question against him is in this library only as an opponent's
  summary of an opponent's work.
