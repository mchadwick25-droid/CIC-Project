claim: >-
  "Egypt as the measure" was one standard to which both houses answered: the north's saint
  "conquered all the eremites and anchorites," so that "Europe will not yield to it, or to all Asia,
  in having only Martin," and the south received the same Egypt as a rule "delivered to us by the
  Fathers" - one reference point, matched at Tours and received at Marseilles.
held_against:
- >-
  The Egyptian concession in the north is written by the Gaul. "You have conquered, O Gaul ... you have
  conquered all the eremites and anchorites" is spoken by Postumianus, the traveller just back from
  Egypt - but Postumianus is a character in Sulpitius's own Dialogue, and Egypt's judgment of Martin is
  not heard from any Egyptian. The north's fullest statement of the comparison (Dial. I.26) sits in an
  Excluded Registry row and is not used, so the comparative mode rests on one voice at Widely Accepted,
  confirmed only structurally by Gennadius's description of the work's subject.
- >-
  The south's Egypt refuses the north's measure. The Egyptian teaching Cassian carries west holds that
  "the excellence of gifts consists not in miracles but in humility" and that the fathers "never
  reckoned those as good monks ... who professed themselves exorcists among men" (Conf. XV.7), and his
  Preface declines to write "about God's miracles" at all. The standard by which Tours claims to have
  matched Egypt - virtutes, wonders outdoing the desert's - is one Cassian's Egypt would not recognize:
  a competition this build's own matrix records (G2 x G6, competing) and which neither node ever
  addressed, since the two literatures never cite each other.
- >-
  The south's "Egypt" is one author's composite. Cassian's own Preface describes a rule "seen ...
  throughout Egypt and Palestine," to be balanced "by the customs of the monasteries which are found
  throughout Pontus and Mesopotamia" wherever the Egyptian rule is "impossible in these countries"; the
  daily office of Book III is set out "according to the rule of the monasteries of Palestine and
  Mesopotamia"; and III.4's own chapter title admits that the morning office "was not appointed by an
  ancient tradition but was started in our own day," at Bethlehem, "after long discussion." A single
  transmitter's harmonized East, presented as antiquity received. Eucherius names John and Macarius
  independently, but at Inferential-Thin wording; and Doc_04 section 7 hands the received content
  itself to desert-monasticism as that world's own.
- >-
  The reception was partial by Cassian's own admission - no one in Gaul kept the Egyptian probation
  "even for a year" (Inst. IV.2), the dress was refitted for the cold (I.10), the Gloria was sung as the
  East never heard (II.8) - so what the south "received" was already Gaul's adaptation, which Doc_05
  section 7.2 identifies as the only part of Cassian that is this world's own.
- >-
  The split itself is a permanent qualifier, not a settled finding. Doc_04 classified G2 Primary "with
  the split-mode finding stated as a permanent qualifier," named the split-mode risk at generation and
  confirmed it under testing, and handed "one reference point, two modes" to the one-world-or-two
  question as input. Whether "Egypt as the measure" is one gravity in two modes or two lineages'
  separate inheritances is open.
concedes: >-
  Not contested: both nodes oriented themselves to Egypt as the standard of monastic authenticity -
  Documented in the south from Cassian's own Preface, independently in Eucherius and in Gennadius;
  Widely Accepted in the north from Sulpitius's text and Gennadius's description of the Dialogues.
  What cannot be settled from this record is whether the two Egypts are the same Egypt: the north's is
  a rival in wonders whose concession of defeat the Gaul scripts himself; the south's is a composite
  rule of Egypt, Palestine, Pontus, and Mesopotamia that refuses wonders as a measure; and no text in
  this world puts the two side by side.
