claim: >-
  The monks and clergy of Marseilles and Lerins were "the Massilians" - "the remnants of the
  Pelagians," the semi-Pelagian party - a school of one doctrine, opposed to predestination, that
  Cassian, Vincent, and Faustus alike held and that Rome condemned.
held_against:
- >-
  Neither name is this world's own. "Massilians" is a period-contemporary but hostile outsider's
  geographical label - Prosper's, on independent grounds (Doc_01 section 7) - and occurs in this
  build's vendored files only in Augustine's chapter heading and Warfield's note; "the remnants of the
  Pelagians" (reliquiae Pelagianorum) is Prosper's phrase as Warfield reports it. "Semi-Pelagian" is a
  sixteenth-century coinage (Beza, applied first to his own contemporaries; retrojected to fifth-century
  Gaul by Sanders) that appears in this world's files only where the nineteenth-century editors write it -
  Gibson's headnote, Heurtley's footnote, Warfield's Argument - never in any ancient text. No one at
  Marseilles or Lerins called himself either.
- >-
  The label's own inventors conceded the opposite of what it implies. Augustine grants that the brethren
  "have attained to the confession that men's wills are anticipated by God's grace" and "that no one can
  suffice to himself either for beginning or for completing any good work." "Predestination" is
  Augustine's word for what they are "in darkness" on, not a doctrine they wrote against: Cassian names
  the thing refused - that God "does not generally will all men, but only some instead of all to be
  saved," "grievous blasphemy" - without ever using the word, and names the Pelagian error on the other
  side in the same breath ("the profane notion of some who attribute everything to free will").
- >-
  The best later insider is grace-prevenient. Gennadius, an ancient witness from the same city, reports
  Faustus - abbot of Lerins, then bishop of Riez - as teaching that "the grace of God always invites,
  precedes and helps our will," and that what free will gains "is not its own desert, but the gift of
  grace" (ch. LXXXVI). That is not a Pelagian minimization of grace (Doc_02 section 1.4). Faustus's own
  De gratia is in this library but unread beyond a bounded grep, post-window as to his episcopate, and
  the one text that could show the Lerins side in its own words - a limit stated, not filled.
- >-
  Whether Vincent, or Lerins at all, held the position is Contested on his own editor's two sentences:
  Heurtley reads the Commonitory's silence on Augustine's name as evidence for a "Semipelagian
  leaning," against his own admission that "the express enunciation of it is nowhere to be found in the
  Commonitory." Vincent names only Pelagius. The label names one city, and by its own letters does not
  reach Lerins, let alone Tours, whose corpus closed before the question was posed - so "a school" is an
  outsider's grouping of three sites, one of which never spoke to the question.
- >-
  The party's shape reaches modern readers through hands with an interest in its name: a Conference
  Dionysius Carthusianus cut "to make Cassian orthodox"; an English edition whose headnotes pre-label
  the Conference; a Princeton Calvinist reviser's Argument "against the semi-Pelagians"; and a standard
  modern monograph that keeps "the Semi-Pelagian Controversy" in its title while disputing the content.
  What the "Massilians" called themselves was "the genuine faith of the ancient fathers."
concedes: >-
  Not contested: a real, multi-sided, institutionally consequential controversy existed within
  participants' lifetimes - Prosper's appeal, Celestine's letter naming Venerius of Marseilles first,
  Contra Collatorem, and Gennadius's own notice that Cassian and Prosper were "at variance" - and the
  position genuinely differed from Augustine's on the further questions of predestination and
  perseverance, which the brethren are reported to have resisted as undercutting exhortation. Documented.
  What cannot be settled from this record is whether any name that fits the group is anything but its
  opponents' - no participant's own account of the party as a party survives in this library, and the
  names it has for the argument are grace, free will, and the beginning of a good will.
