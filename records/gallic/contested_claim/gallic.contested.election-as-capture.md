claim: >-
  The monk-bishop came to office as a captive, never as a candidate. Martin was drawn from his
  monastery by a trick and made bishop of Tours by the one wish of all the people, over the impious
  resistance of a few bishops whose only objection was that his clothing was mean and his hair
  disgusting, and the objectors were confounded by a psalm; the same shape holds at every house -
  Honoratus overtaken by the dignity he fled, Archebius "carried off" - so that nobody in this
  world's own stories wanted the see.
held_against:
- >-
  One witness for Tours, not present, and no friend to the objectors: Sulpitius met Martin later and
  says he had his material "partly from himself ... and partly from those who had lived with him"
  (Vita ch. XXV), narrates the election at second hand, and elsewhere treats the episcopal college as a
  class of calumniators ("no others than bishops!", Vita ch. XXVII; Martin "never again did he attend
  a synod," Dial. III.13). The story record's own confidence note already carries "his hostility to the
  objecting bishops is his own perspective."
- >-
  The objecting bishops have no voice. Defensor is named and nothing of his case survives beyond what
  Sulpitius reduces it to - countenance, clothing, hair; whether the summoned bishops had a canonical,
  factional, or doctrinal objection to a monk in the see is unrecoverable. The psalm that "confounded"
  them is carried by Sulpitius himself only as what "was believed," and the pun that makes the psalm
  name Defensor is Roberts's editorial footnote, withdrawn from the story record as not Sulpitius's.
  Gennadius corroborates only the bare fact "monk and bishop."
- >-
  The south's two capture-stories are not Gallic eyewitness. Archebius is an Egyptian bishop's story,
  chosen by Cassian to carry west, and no witness to him exists outside Cassian's own writings (Gibson,
  editorial). Honoratus's "the dignity came to him" is a disciple's funeral eulogy known in this build
  only through its own rendering of rough OCR, wording Inferential-Thin; and the link that makes
  Cassian's monastery-presiding dedicatee Honoratus of Lerins is the editorial apparatus's, withdrawn
  as "doubly evidenced" at Doc_02 section 3 and never stated in Cassian's own words.
- >-
  The texts have a doctrinal reason to tell every elevation as capture. The received formation program
  names the wish for holy orders as vainglory's own suggestion (Inst. XI.14) and the devil's pretext
  (Conf. I.20); a house formed on that teaching could not narrate its founder's rise in any other shape
  without indicting him. The narrative form and the doctrine cannot be told apart, any more than, in
  the forces document's own words, "the drain and the gravity" can (Doc_08 Force 2A-2).
- >-
  The standard modern reconstruction reads the same events the other way. Mathisen's account of the
  Lerins circle as a factional "power elite" whose members moved between monastic, episcopal, and
  secular aristocratic status - Lerins-formed men filling the sees of southern Gaul, Honoratus and
  Hilary at Arles, Faustus at Riez - is a political reading whose contested premise, in this build's own
  forces document, is "the degree to which a factional-political reading fits texts that narrate
  elevation as capture" (Doc_08 section 7, Dominant Modern Reconstruction; named tension 7). Thesis-level,
  unread beyond that, and not resolved here.
concedes: >-
  Not contested: monks from these houses became bishops, in every founding voice, independently in
  Gennadius, and in Hilary of Arles's own Latin - the pattern is Documented and is this world's
  distinct-world criterion. Not contested either that the formation teaching genuinely made the wish for
  office a fault, so that a formed man had reason to flee it. What cannot be settled from this record
  is whether the reluctance was as total as every founding story says, or how the summoned bishops, the
  citizens who laid the ambush, and the aristocratic network the modern reconstruction describes saw
  the same elevations - because the only voices that narrate them are the houses' own, and the men who
  objected at the election left no account.

How the G1 lead was assessed. The election-at-Tours one-sidedness is real but, alone, thin - one
story whose record already discloses the caveat. It becomes a contested_claim when joined to what the
build itself already joined it to: the cross-house capture pattern (Doc_07 section 2C) and the one
named modern reading that reads those same texts against their own grain (Doc_08 section 7 item 7).
The claim is therefore stated at the pattern's level, with the Tours election as its founding
instance, and held for G1 rather than for G9 (authority-ambivalence, Tensional), because what is
contested is the shape of the monk-bishop's own founding narrative, not the world's stance toward
bishops.

Deliberately not used, with reasons. The Sulpitius-Pelagianism charge (Gennadius ch. XIX, Contested,
Doc_02 section 1.1) is excluded on Doc_06 section 3's own ruling for chunk 013 (monk-bishop): "a
biographical datum ... not a contest about this term," repeated in gallic.figure.sulpitius's and
gallic.term.monk-bishop's own notes; it would impugn the witness generally and bears on nothing in this
claim. Doc_06 section 3 declined a [CT] tag on 013 itself, and this record does not contradict that
finding: the lexicon found no live contest about what "monk-bishop" means; this record holds a contest
about how the founding stories of the monk-bishop represent the event, which Doc_08 section 7 does list
as a named tension. The G1 x G9 competition (Doc_04 section 4) is a build finding about two gravities and
is not this claim's content.
