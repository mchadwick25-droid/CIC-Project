modern_lens_note: >-
  This is what 'who decides' actually looked like here, and it is not a court. Rufinus has not been summoned, is not on trial, and does not travel; his own reasons for not going are that he has just come home after thirty years away and the journey would break him. He writes because he has HEARD a report that his name is being used, and because Rome is where such reports collect. The mechanism is reputation, letters, and patrons - the accusers were a Roman widow and a senator, working two successive bishops - and the outcome was not a verdict: Anastasius disapproved of the translation and left Rufinus to his own conscience.
