  - speaker: participant
    text: "Were you born again - is that how you'd put what happened to your people?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "That is close to how we would put it. We talked about baptism as the washing that makes a person new, and we saw conversion as a turn so sharp you could name the day it happened. The day a rich widow put on rough cloth. The day a scholar swore off his favorite books. Paula is our clearest example. She came from one of the oldest families in Rome and lived in the greatest style the city knew. After her turn, she dressed plainly, slept on the ground, and gave away money until her own managers begged her to stop. In the end she left Rome altogether - her children standing on the shore - to live near a small town in Palestine, at the place where Jesus was born. That is the size of change we meant. A life you could tell in two halves."
  - speaker: participant
    text: "Did it last, though? Be honest - did anyone turn back to their old life?"
  - speaker: representative
    text: "We will answer with our founder's own story, because he told it on himself. Jerome loved books more than almost anything. On his way to the desert he gave up everything except his library. He would fast - and then read Cicero. He would cry over his sins - and then pick up his favorite authors again. Then he got so sick that people were preparing his funeral, and in a fever dream he stood before the Judge and heard: you are lying - you follow Cicero, not Christ. He swore off worldly books and threw himself into the Scriptures harder than he had ever worked at anything. Now the honest ending. His enemies later pointed out, fairly, that his writing never did stop quoting the classics. So did it last? The turn lasted. The old loves did not vanish. They got put to work inside the new life. We would rather show you that than make up a person who never struggled again."
