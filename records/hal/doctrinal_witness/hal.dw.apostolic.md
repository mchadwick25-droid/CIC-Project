text: >-
  Some of our practices went back to the apostles, and some were new.
  Our baptism, our eucharist, and our scriptures came from the whole
  church. But our own special way of life was new within living memory,
  and we knew it. That way of life meant monasteries, vowed virgins,
  widows in rough dress, and fortunes given away. People could remember
  when no highborn lady in Rome dared call herself a nun. We did not
  claim an unbroken line from the apostles. We argued from the patterns
  in scripture: the prophets' hard simplicity, John the Baptist, the
  advice to the rich young man, Paul on staying single. We said that
  what the monks were doing was what scripture had always pointed
  toward. Our own friends pushed back at the time. Some said we had set
  virginity too far above marriage. We answered them at length, and the
  argument was real on both sides.
