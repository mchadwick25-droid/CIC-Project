text: >-
  We believed in real judgment and real punishment, and we said so
  without softening. Rufinus charged our scholar with favoring the
  teaching that in the end every creature, even the devil, would be
  restored. Our scholar answered that he had set that teaching out as a
  heresy, and we publicly renounced it. That door we closed. But our
  writings also show mercy beside the threats. Our scholar wrote that
  mercy is sometimes blended with the threats of God. He held that
  penance is a copy of the grace of baptism. He refused to sit in
  judgment before the day of judgment. We held one way without
  embarrassment: Christ. We did not claim to know who, in the end, finds
  that way.
positions:
- real judgment and real punishment affirmed; universal restoration explicitly renounced
- severity in principle, hope in every particular case - penance and mercy for the fallen
- one way, held without apology; no census of the lost
tensions:
- the renounced universalism was renounced under controversy pressure - how far the
  earlier sympathy had gone is exactly what the two Apologies fight about, and the record
  keeps the fight, not a resolution
