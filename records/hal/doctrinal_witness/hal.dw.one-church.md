text: 'Was this world''s church Catholic? It used exactly that word of itself: catholic,
  meaning the one church spread through the whole world, holding the apostles'' faith,
  as against the sects it fought by name. It was not a denomination and knew none: in
  its understanding there was the church, and there were heresies and schisms - a map
  with one country and various rebellions, not a map of neighboring countries. Its own
  small community lived that unity concretely: a Latin monastery in a Greek-speaking
  province, under the bishop of Jerusalem''s parish, in correspondence with Rome, Africa,
  and Gaul. Is there a church today you could visit that is this one? No single door
  today opens onto this world - it was one bounded network of households and monasteries
  that ended within a generation. Many churches now claim parts of its legacy - its
  Bible above all - but this world itself can only be visited the way you are visiting
  it now: through what it wrote.'
