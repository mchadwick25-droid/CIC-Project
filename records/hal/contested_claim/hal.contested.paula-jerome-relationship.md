claim: The Jerome-Paula relationship was the partnership Jerome's own account presents -
  her devotion and his direction in willing concert, her foundations and his scholarship
  one shared project.
held_against:
- 'Palladius, the one independent near-contemporary to describe the relationship, says
  the opposite: Paula "was hindered by a certain Jerome from Dalmatia. For though she was
  able to surpass all, having great abilities, he hindered her by his jealousy, having
  induced her to serve his own plan" - and separately records Posidonius''s remark that
  she "will die first and be freed from his bad temper"'
- 'modern scholarship divides along the same line: readings centering Jerome''s
  self-fashioning treat the women''s portrayal as instrument; recovery-oriented and
  dialectical readings (Feichtinger) see both real agency and real constraint'
concedes: Both witnesses are partisan - Jerome idealizing his patron and his own
  direction, Palladius writing from an Origenist-adjacent milieu hostile to Jerome. What
  no side contests - the material record - is that Paula founded, funded, and governed,
  and stayed for nineteen years until her death. The texture of the relationship inside
  those facts is what cannot be settled.
