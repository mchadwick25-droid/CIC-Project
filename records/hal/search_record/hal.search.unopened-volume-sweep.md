THE SHAPE THIS WORLD'S LIST TOOK, which is different from desert's and
will be the common one from here.

Nine of the twelve were volumes of an author this world ALREADY OPENS in
a different volume of the same set. Augustine at npnf101, Origen at
anf04, Palladius in Clarke. That is a much weaker signal than desert's,
where the world named Origen constantly and had opened no Origen at all,
and the right question is therefore not "has this world read the author"
but "does THIS volume answer something this world asks and cannot
answer". Three did. Six did not, and saying so is not a confession of a
gap.

WHAT THE THREE OPENS ADDED, since the test above only earns its keep if
the things that pass it are worth having.

Augustine's City of God gave this world the other side's case at full
strength and in public. In the letters he is persuading Jerome. In Book
XVIII he has stopped: he grants that Jerome is learned in all three
languages, grants that the Jews themselves call his work faithful,
grants that the Septuagint translators erred in many places - and rules
against him anyway, because the seventy were prophets and the churches
have received them. hal.quote.no-one-preferred-to-the-seventy carries it,
declared tension-with hebraica veritas rather than illustrating anything.

The Philocalia is the control on Rufinus. This world's rupture turns on
whether Rufinus softened De Principiis in translating it, and he admits
in his own prologue to correcting what he judged corrupted. The Greek is
lost - except where the anthology preserves it, and its first chapter
says of itself that it comes "From the work on 'Principles'". That turns
an accusation this world can only report into one it can partly test.

The Syriac Paradise did something the sweep did not expect. The Greek has
Posidonius saying Jerome "showed qualities of temper so disastrous that
they threw into the shade his splendid achievements", and PREDICTING that
Paula will die first and be freed from his bad temper. The Syriac says he
"possessed the vices of envy and evil-eyedness", and reports that she
departed "before her time in order that she might escape from his envy".
Bad temper has become the evil eye; a prophecy has become a fact. And the
same recension has no Paula in its women's chapters at all - the one
place her name occurs in the whole file is inside that report. The
tradition that hardened the charge is the tradition that dropped her from
the company of the holy women, and this world does not know why.
