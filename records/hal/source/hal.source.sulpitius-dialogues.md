Passages verified verbatim against the vendored text: ch. 8 'The presbyter
Jerome rules the church of this place; for it is a parish of the bishop who
has possession of Jerusalem' and 'learned not only in Latin and Greek, but
also Hebrew'; ch. 9 'He is always occupied in reading, always at his books
with his whole heart: he takes no rest day or night; he is perpetually
either reading or writing something' - and, in the same chapter, 'The
heretics hate him, because he never desists from attacking them; the
clerics hate him, because he assails their life and crimes.'

Evidentiary role: independent corroboration that (1) the Bethlehem
community existed and was internationally visible c. 400, (2) Jerome held
presbyteral, not episcopal, standing there, under Jerusalem's bishop, and
(3) his reputation for Hebrew learning was current among contemporaries.
On (3): an admirer's testimony to reputation, not evidence of measured
competence - it constrains but does not settle the Williams contest
(hal.contested.hebrew-fluency).
