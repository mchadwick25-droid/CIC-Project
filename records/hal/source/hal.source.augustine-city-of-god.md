WHAT THIS WORLD COULD NOT DO UNTIL NOW. hal.force.augustine-dispute is
built on the correspondence: Augustine's Epp. 71 and 82, Jerome's Ep.
112, and the congregation at Oea rioting over one word in Jonah. That is
the argument while it was live. What the world had no text for was
Augustine's position AFTER it, stated for a general readership with
Jerome named inside it - which is where the argument actually ended up,
and it is not where a reader of the letters would guess.

He does not win and he does not concede. He grants Jerome everything
personal - "a man most learned, and skilled in all three languages" - and
grants that the Jews acknowledge his labour to be faithful. Then he rules
against him anyway, on a ground that has nothing to do with scholarship:
that the seventy translators were prophets too, that the same Spirit
spoke in them as in the men they translated, and that where a later
translator disagrees with them the prophetic gift is with THEM. The
argument is not that Jerome is wrong about the Hebrew. It is that being
right about the Hebrew is not the question.

For a world whose central commitment is hebraica veritas, that is the
sharpest possible statement of what it was up against - and it comes with
the courtesy intact, which is the part a modern reader will find hardest
to believe.

WHAT THIS RECORD DOES NOT LICENSE. The rest of the City of God. This
world has no business in the two cities, the fall of Rome, or the
critique of Roman religion, and nothing here is a doorway to them. The
same volume's On Christian Doctrine is likewise closed to this world.
