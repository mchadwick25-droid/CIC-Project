WHY A WORLD ABOUT JEROME HOLDS ANOTHER MAN'S COMMENTARY. Because of what
Jerome did to it. Victorinus' voice belongs to
danubian-latin-christianity, where the corpus map assigns it; what
belongs here is the editing. Jerome reissued the commentary with its
millenarian ending rewritten, and the text every later reader has is his
version. The relation is custody, not authorship - the map marks it
`transmission` for exactly that reason.

IT SITS BESIDE THE RUFINUS PROLOGUE ON PURPOSE. This world's central
quarrel is about a translator's liberty to correct a text he believes
corrupted. Rufinus said he had done it to Origen and Jerome attacked him
for it. Jerome did it to Victorinus. Holding both documents lets the
question be put to this world in its own terms rather than as a modern
complaint: the practice was general, the standards were unsettled, and
the man who prosecuted it most fiercely also practised it.

CARRIED AS `corroborating` and with the divergence note above, because
nothing here separates the two hands line by line and this record does
not pretend to.
