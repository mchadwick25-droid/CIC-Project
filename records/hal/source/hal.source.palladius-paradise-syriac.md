TWO FINDINGS, and the second is the one this world should sit with.

FIRST: THE CHARGE CHANGES BETWEEN RECENSIONS. Clarke's Greek says Jerome
"showed qualities of temper so disastrous that they threw into the shade
his splendid achievements" - bad temper. Budge's Syriac says he
"possessed the vices of envy and evil-eyedness to such a degree that the
excellence of his very great skill and ability was entirely hidden" - the
evil eye. Those are not the same accusation. One is a difficult man; the
other is a man who wishes harm and whose wishing works.

And the Greek has Posidonius PREDICTING that Paula "will die first and be
freed from his bad temper, so I think", while the Syriac reports flatly
that she "departed from this world before her time in order that she
might escape from his envy". A guarded prophecy in one line of
transmission has become a completed fact in the other, with "before her
time" added. hal.quote.paula-escaped-his-envy carries it.

SECOND: PAULA IS NOT IN THE SYRIAC WOMEN'S CHAPTERS AT ALL. Clarke's
chapter 41, the holy women, opens with "the Roman lady Paula, mother of
Toxotius" and the sentence about Jerome hindering her that
hal.quote.hindered-by-jerome carries and this world's contested record is
built on. The Syriac recension's women's chapters - Piamon, Talida, Taor,
Melania the Great and the Younger, Olympias, Candida, Gelasia, Juliana,
Magna and the rest - do not include her. Searched directly: the only
occurrence of Paula's name in the whole file is inside the Possidonius
chapter.

That asymmetry is worth stating plainly rather than resolving. The
recension that hardened the charge against Jerome is the recension that
dropped Paula from the company of holy women. This world does not know
why, and nothing here settles whether that is loss, editorial choice, or
a different exemplar.

WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT CONTAIN: not the Historia Monachorum, not the
Sayings, not Book II. It ends "Here endeth the first Book".
