### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears for all four core figures, with four honest complications disclosed rather than smoothed over

**Correcting a heading undercount (Round 2 finding, same shape as I.35's N6):** the body below discloses four complications, not three — Minucius Felix (Christological absence), Lactantius (pneumatology and eschatology), Commodian (disputed date), and Arnobius (anthropology) — matching the Resolution sentence, the Section A conclusion, and §4 items 1, 2, 3, and 5, all of which already enumerate four.

**Minucius Felix clears, with a disclosure now stated precisely rather than softened (Round 1 finding B2).** The prior draft called the *Octavius* "genuinely thin" on distinctively Trinitarian or Christological content. Word-level inspection of the vendored text (`anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml`, div2 iv.iii, 23,819 words including editorial apparatus) finds it is not thin — it is, in Minucius' own prose, **absent**: zero occurrences of "Jesus," zero of "Son of God," zero of any incarnation language. **Correcting Revision 1's own re-count (Round 2 finding):** "Christ" occurs **twice**, not once — the ch. XXXVII *Argument* heading ("Confession of Christ's Name") and an editorial footnote citing Athenagoras — and "crucifi-" occurs twice more (the ch. IX *Argument* heading, "They Worship a Crucified Man," and an editorial gloss at ch. XXIX), neither previously mentioned. All four occurrences are ANF editorial apparatus — chapter headings and footnotes — not Minucius' own prose, so the substantive finding is unchanged and, if anything, more precisely confirmed: there is no positive Christological statement anywhere in the work itself. **Correcting a self-contradiction against the Section A conclusion below (Round 4 finding R25):** the one place the text meets the charge directly, ch. XXIX, reads "you wander far from the neighbourhood of the truth, in thinking either that a criminal deserved, or that an earthly being was able, to be believed God" and moves immediately to mocking Egyptian god-kings. Read directly, that sentence rejects the pagan's own characterization of Christ as "a criminal" rather than straightforwardly denying his divinity — it declines to deny Christ's divinity rather than denying it outright, and this document adopts that reading consistently rather than stating both readings in one sentence, as an earlier revision did. This is a live, long-running scholarly question, not a settled feature of persuasive strategy that this document is entitled to explain away, and it is named as such rather than resolved (see §4 item 3's correction below).

**Lactantius requires a real, disclosed complication, not an unqualified clearance (Round 1 finding B3).** The prior draft called him "the strongest possible A2 case in this candidate... clears without qualification." The biography supporting that is accurate — rhetor at Nicomedia, tutor to Constantine's son Crispus at Trier — but the theological claim is not. **Correcting a misquotation entered at Revision 2 and left uncaught through Round 3 (Round 4 finding R24, HIGH):** Jerome (*Ep.* 84.7, to Pammachius and Oceanus) does not say Lactantius denies the Spirit's substance "in his letters, and especially in those to Demetrianus." The vendored translation (`cic/texts/npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml`) reads: "Lactantius in his books and particularly in his letters to Demetrian altogether denies the subsistence of the Holy Spirit, and following the error of the Jews says that the passages in which he is spoken of refer to the Father or to the Son" — a charge against exactly Article 4's fifth commitment — **correcting a non-verbatim gloss against the header's own stated practice (Round 4 finding R26)**: "The Holy Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified together with the Father and the Son," quoted here in full rather than paraphrased — leveled at Lactantius' **books generally**, with the letters to Demetrianus named only as its sharpest instance, not its sole target. **Correcting the distinction §4 item 5 carries as a result (originally a Round 2 fix, now itself wrong):** the letters to Demetrianus are genuinely lost and not among the vendored texts in `cic/texts/`, and that is worth disclosing — but the charge does not attach to them *rather than* to the *Divine Institutes*; Jerome's own very next sentence asks who can forbid him to read the *Institutes* "simply because this opinion of his is to be abhorred," confirming the charge reaches Lactantius' surviving, vendored work. The *Divine Institutes* also carries a chiliastic reading of the end times (Book VII) and, read directly, a two-spirits cosmology at II.8–9 — God producing a second being "in whom the disposition of the divine origin did not remain," who "passed from good to evil" — precisely the binitarian-with-a-fallen-second-spirit structure the ancient charge concerns, not a structure outside its reach. Doc_02's disclosure must say plainly that no primary text of the charge's sharpest instance (the lost letters) can be shown, while the charge itself is leveled at the corpus this candidate does hold. None of this is a rival-current divergence — it is the same kind of disclosed, bounded idiosyncrasy this document already grants Minucius Felix and Arnobius, and withholding it from the candidate's own anchor figure, on the commitment where the ancient criticism is sharpest and best-attested, was an error of the prior draft's own internal consistency as much as of fact.

**Commodian and Arnobius each carry a real, disclosable complication, with Arnobius' now correctly attributed:**

- **Commodian's date is genuinely disputed in the scholarship** — proposals range across roughly three centuries, and some of that range would place him well after this candidate's own 320 close, outside Era 1 entirely. **This is a scope question, not a floor question, and this document does not conflate the two (Round 2 finding M3):** A2 tests continuity for a figure who belongs to this window; whether Commodian belongs to this window at all is prior to that test, not a complication within it. Not resolved here; Doc_01 needs an actual, cited dating judgment before Commodian can be treated as belonging to this candidate at all.
- **Arnobius' theology carries real, disclosed idiosyncrasy — correcting three attribution errors in the prior draft (Round 1 finding B6).** The prior draft wrote that Arnobius wrote *Against the Heathen* "by his own admission," "at his bishop's insistence," and that his freshly-converted, uncatechized status was established "on his own text's own evidence." All three misattribute to Arnobius what is Jerome's report alone. **Correcting an over-broad citation (Round 4 finding R27):** *De viris illustribus* 79 states only that Arnobius was "a most successful teacher of rhetoric at Sicca in Africa during the reign of Diocletian" who "wrote volumes Against the nations which may be found everywhere" — no dream, no bishop, no refusal, no pledge. The dream-and-bishop account is the *Chronicle*'s alone (ad ann. 327): Arnobius' **bishop refused to admit him**, doubting a conversion prompted by a dream, and Arnobius wrote the books as a pledge to obtain baptism — the reverse of "at his bishop's insistence." Nothing in the *Adversus Nationes* itself describes its author's own biography this way. The census's own entry carries the appropriate hedge, "if Jerome is to be believed," which this document adopts. The theological content itself is accurately described: Book II argues the soul is of a *media qualitas*, not naturally immortal, receiving immortality as a gift through Christ — a genuinely idiosyncratic position, disclosed here rather than laundered into settled orthodoxy.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Minucius Felix's Christological absence, Lactantius' pneumatological and eschatological complications, Commodian's dating, and Arnobius' anthropology (and its corrected provenance) all carried forward as binding, disclosed items in §4 — none smoothed over, and none, on the evidence available, disqualifying.
