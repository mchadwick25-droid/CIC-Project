## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas I.43, "The Latin Apologists," c. 200–320 CE. Rome and Ostia; Sicca in Numidia; Nicomedia and Trier. The census's own `voices` field names five figures, not four: Minucius Felix, Arnobius of Sicca, Commodian, Lactantius, and — explicitly named there as belonging to his own separate entry rather than to this one — **Tertullian**, "whose Apology of 197 begins the whole enterprise." Latin-language philosophical and polemical defenses of Christianity against Roman religion and the charge that Christians had brought the empire's troubles on itself, running from the early third century through the Great Persecution (Diocletian's edicts begin in 303; persecution continued in the East under Maximinus Daia until 313) and into the Constantinian settlement.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

Not the primary test: the candidate's window (c. 200–320) ends five years before Nicaea. **Correcting the prior draft's citation (Round 1 finding B8, carrying A9):** A2 governs directly not because of an IJC precedent — IJC's actual holding was that both A1 and A2 can apply to different phases of one straddling movement, which does not describe a window that never reaches 325 — but on LPC's own precedent, which runs A2 alone as the governing test for a wholly pre-Nicene phase.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears for all four core figures, with three honest complications disclosed rather than smoothed over

**Minucius Felix clears, with a disclosure now stated precisely rather than softened (Round 1 finding B2).** The prior draft called the *Octavius* "genuinely thin" on distinctively Trinitarian or Christological content. Word-level inspection of the vendored text (`anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml`, div2 iv.iii) finds it is not thin — it is **absent**: zero occurrences of "Jesus," zero of "Son of God," zero of any incarnation language; "Christ" occurs exactly once in the entire work, inside an ANF editor's chapter heading, not in Minucius' own prose. The one place the text meets the charge directly, ch. XXIX, denies that a crucified man could be God and moves immediately to mocking Egyptian god-kings — it does not affirm the divinity it declines to deny. This is a live, long-running scholarly question, not a settled feature of persuasive strategy that this document is entitled to explain away, and it is named as such rather than resolved (see §4 item 3's correction below).

**Lactantius requires a real, disclosed complication, not an unqualified clearance (Round 1 finding B3).** The prior draft called him "the strongest possible A2 case in this candidate... clears without qualification." The biography supporting that is accurate — rhetor at Nicomedia, tutor to Constantine's son Crispus at Trier — but the theological claim is not. Jerome (*Ep.* 84.7) states directly that Lactantius "denies the substance of the Holy Spirit, and by a Jewish error says that it is referred either to the Father or to the Son" — a charge against exactly Article 4's fifth commitment (the Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified with the Father and Son). The *Divine Institutes* also carries a chiliastic reading of the end times (Book VII) and a two-spirits cosmology (II.8–9) that later orthodoxy found awkward. None of this is a rival-current divergence — it is the same kind of disclosed, bounded idiosyncrasy this document already grants Minucius Felix and Arnobius, and withholding it from the candidate's own anchor figure, on the commitment where the ancient criticism is sharpest and best-attested, was an error of the prior draft's own internal consistency as much as of fact.

**Commodian and Arnobius each carry a real, disclosable complication, with Arnobius' now correctly attributed:**

- **Commodian's date is genuinely disputed in the scholarship** — proposals range across roughly three centuries, and some of that range would place him well after this candidate's own 320 close, outside Era 1 entirely. Not resolved here; Doc_01 needs an actual, cited dating judgment before Commodian can be treated as belonging to this candidate at all.
- **Arnobius' theology carries real, disclosed idiosyncrasy — correcting three attribution errors in the prior draft (Round 1 finding B6).** The prior draft wrote that Arnobius wrote *Against the Heathen* "by his own admission," "at his bishop's insistence," and that his freshly-converted, uncatechized status was established "on his own text's own evidence." All three misattribute to Arnobius what is Jerome's report alone (*De viris illustribus* 79; *Chronicle* ad ann. 327): Jerome's account is that Arnobius' **bishop refused to admit him**, doubting a conversion prompted by a dream, and that Arnobius wrote the books as a pledge to obtain baptism — the reverse of "at his bishop's insistence." Nothing in the *Adversus Nationes* itself describes its author's own biography this way. The census's own entry carries the appropriate hedge, "if Jerome is to be believed," which this document adopts. The theological content itself is accurately described: Book II argues the soul is of a *media qualitas*, not naturally immortal, receiving immortality as a gift through Christ — a genuinely idiosyncratic position, disclosed here rather than laundered into settled orthodoxy.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Minucius Felix's Christological absence, Lactantius' pneumatological and eschatological complications, Commodian's dating, and Arnobius' anthropology (and its corrected provenance) all carried forward as binding, disclosed items in §4 — none smoothed over, and none, on the evidence available, disqualifying.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This candidate's rivals are structurally different from I.35's: these authors argue primarily against Roman civic religion and mythology and against the political charge that Christian impiety caused Rome's troubles — not primarily against named Christian heresies. Roman religion appears correctly as this candidate's own opponent, inside its own reconstruction.

**Strand Determination (Article 21) — the question is real, and Tertullian's own absence from this candidate's roster is now the center of it, not a fact this document can leave uncited (Round 1 finding B1).** The prior draft proposed a developmental sequence — Minucius Felix's private dialogue, then Arnobius' polemical treatise, then Lactantius' systematic institutio, with Commodian's populist verse outside the sequence — without once naming Tertullian, whose own *Apology* (197) the candidate's own census entry calls the beginning "of the whole enterprise" this candidate is meant to represent, and who holds, on his own separate census entry (I.17, "Tertullian's Voice"), 33 works including *Ad Nationes*, *To Scapula*, *The Soul's Testimony*, and *An Answer to the Jews* — the entire Latin apologetic corpus proper, in one man, writing from the same city and roughly the same years as Minucius Felix. A "developing tradition" argument for this candidate has to answer, not avoid, the question of whether it can be told at all with its own opening act excluded. **A cleared, on-record precedent for exactly this exclusion already exists and should be the model, not reinvented:** LPC's own `Source_Registry.md` row 29 classifies Tertullian's corpus **Excluded / Named Comparandum**, with a stated reason — "he predates Cyprian's own episcopate by decades, wrote as a lay apologist rather than this world's own bishop-centered pastoral office" — that this candidate must engage rather than ignore, since "wrote as a lay apologist" is not a reason to exclude him from a *lay apologist's* own world; it is the reason LPC gives for why he is not *its* figure. Doc_01 must state affirmatively why Tertullian sits outside this candidate too, on this candidate's own terms, or else include him and revise the roster and the developmental-sequence argument accordingly.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on the same apologetic-argument register as I.35, in Latin rhetorical and legal idiom — Arnobius and Lactantius are both trained rhetoricians, sharpened by Lactantius writing during and immediately after the empire's most severe persecution effort. Genuinely thin, structurally so: none of these four texts primarily describes Christian worship practice — this candidate's own worship-practice lens is thin because the genre itself doesn't supply it, not because a sibling world claimed the material. Lay formation, women's voices, and enslaved persons' testimony are essentially absent, the same skew as I.35 and IJC.

**Passes B2**, with the thinness named as a property of the genre itself.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**A new bullet, entirely absent from the prior draft, must open this section (Round 1 finding B1).** **Versus Tertullian's Voice (I.17, Possible Future World, unbuilt):** the candidate's own census entry names Tertullian first among its `voices` and states plainly that he "has an entry of his own rather than a place in this one" — a separation this document adopts rather than merely notes, since `cic/corpus-map/tertullian-s-voice.yaml` holds 33 works and this candidate's own developmental-sequence argument in §2 A5 cannot be made honestly without confronting his absence directly. LPC's own precedent (Named Comparandum, "wrote as a lay apologist" as the *reason* he does not belong to a bishop-centered pastoral world) supplies a model but not an automatic answer here, since this candidate's own subject matter *is* lay apologetic — Doc_01 must make its own case, not borrow LPC's by analogy alone.

- **Versus I.35 (The Second-Century Greek Apologists, unbuilt candidate):** direct check performed, not assumed. No figure overlap — Minucius Felix, Commodian, Arnobius, and Lactantius appear nowhere in I.35's own roster. The relationship is sequential rather than competitive: a second, later, Latin-language act of the same basic project, resolving in the opposite direction from the Greek case (I.35's Justin dies not knowing whether the argument will be heeded; this candidate's Lactantius lives to tutor the emperor's own son).
- **Versus Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity (LPC):** verified directly, not merely re-asserted — `cic/corpus-map/latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity.yaml` contains no work by Arnobius, Lactantius, Minucius Felix, or Commodian (Arnobius having been moved out of it on the same 2026-08-27 ruling that brought him here). **Correcting the prior draft's characterization of LPC's own state (Round 1 finding C6):** LPC is not "mid-build" in the sense of having its own compiled `records/` yet — there is no `records/lpc/` — but it does carry a cleared, five-round-reviewed Step 0, a Doc_01, a Doc_02, and a full Source Registry, which is a real and checkable state, and the check above was run against that real state rather than deferred. - **Versus The Roman Church in the Third Century (I.33, Possible Future World, unbuilt) — new, per Round 1 finding B7.** Minucius Felix's own date is disputed across roughly a century, per the census's own sourcing note, and part of that range would place the *Octavius* — a dialogue set at Ostia, its speakers Roman — inside I.33's own window (c. 200–268, Rome). - **Versus Imperial-Juridical Christianity (IJC, Built & Live) — corrected on two points (Round 1 findings B4, B5).** *First*, the prior draft understated IJC's own claim: Lactantius' *De Mortibus Persecutorum* is not cited "once, for one story" — it is `evidentiary_weight: load-bearing` and cited by nine IJC records, including the Edict of Milan's own text (ch. 48), which IJC calls "the world's most direct witness to its own legal beginning." *Second, and more consequential*: the boundary this candidate needs is not open Doc_01 work, as the prior draft claimed — it is **already resolved**, by Mark, by name, in this candidate's own corpus map: "RULED BY MARK 2026-08-27: Lactantius lives in `latin-apologists`... THE RULING SPLITS THE AUTHOR, WHICH IS WHAT `per work` MEANS. Lactantius' apologetic corpus moves; *Of the Manner in Which the Persecutors Died* does NOT. That is the Athanasius case the schema names." What remains true and correctly claimed by the prior draft is the substance: `grep -rn "Divine Institutes" records/` returns zero hits across all eight built worlds, so the actual apologetic corpus this candidate's B1 case rests on is genuinely untouched by IJC. The relationship, respecting the already-ruled split, is a clean sequential bridge: this candidate is the intellectual case *for* the empire embracing Christianity; IJC is what happened once it did.
- **Versus the other six built worlds:** no figure or source overlap found in this pass beyond the IJC boundary above, which is resolved rather than open.
