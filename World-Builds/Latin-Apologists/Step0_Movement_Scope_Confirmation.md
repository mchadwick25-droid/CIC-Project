## 1. Candidate identification

> Atlas I.43, "The Latin Apologists," c. 200–320 CE. Rome and Ostia; Sicca in Numidia; Nicomedia and Trier. Minucius Felix, Commodian, Arnobius of Sicca, and Lactantius — Latin-language philosophical and polemical defenses of Christianity against Roman religion and the charge that Christians had brought the empire's troubles on itself, running from the early third century through the Great Persecution (303–311) and into the Constantinian settlement.

**Naming note:** the census's own registration text names this candidate's honest objection as *stronger* than I.35's identical genre-vs-community concern — Minucius Felix, Commodian, Arnobius, and Lactantius are not contemporaries, span more than a century, and the one asserted teacher-pupil link (Arnobius to Lactantius) rests on a single line in Jerome, not on independent corroboration. That objection is treated here as live and unresolved, exactly as for I.35.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

Not the primary test: the candidate's window (c. 200–320) ends five years before Nicaea, so, as with I.35 and per the same "as applicable" Procedure logic IJC's confirmation used for its own pre-325 sliver, A2 governs directly rather than A1.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears for the core figures; two honest complications disclosed rather than smoothed over

**Lactantius clears without qualification** — he is, if anything, the strongest possible A2 case in this candidate: a proto-Nicene establishment theologian, tutor to Constantine's own son at Trier, whose *Divine Institutes* argues the same substantive content the Creed would later formalize. **Minucius Felix clears, with a disclosure, not a caveat of divergence:** the *Octavius* is a defense of monotheism and Christian ethics against pagan slander conducted largely in the vocabulary of Ciceronian natural theology, and it is genuinely thin on distinctively Trinitarian or Christological content — this is a feature of the dialogue's own persuasive strategy (arguing from common philosophical ground to a pagan friend, not catechizing a believer) rather than evidence of divergence from proto-orthodoxy, but Doc_02 should name this thinness plainly rather than read later Christology into a text that does not supply it.

**Commodian and Arnobius each carry a real, disclosable complication:**

- **Commodian's date is genuinely disputed in the scholarship** — proposals range across roughly three centuries (as the census's own sourcing note already states), and some of that range would place him well after this candidate's own 320 close, outside Era 1 entirely. This is not resolved by this document; Doc_01 needs an actual, cited dating judgment before Commodian can be treated as belonging to this candidate at all, and if the later dating is judged more likely, he may need to be reassigned rather than retained.
- **Arnobius' theology carries real, disclosed idiosyncrasy, by his own admission.** Jerome reports Arnobius wrote *Against the Heathen* at his bishop's insistence, as proof of a conversion the church did not yet trust — he was, on his own text's own evidence, a rhetoric teacher freshly converted and not yet formed by sustained catechesis. Modern readers of the text have long noted views that sit awkwardly with later orthodoxy, most notably an argument that the soul is not naturally immortal but comes to immortality only through Christ (Book II) — a genuinely idiosyncratic position, not a rival-current divergence toward Gnosticism or Marcionism, but a real theological rough edge this candidate should name rather than launder into settled orthodoxy.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Commodian's dating and Arnobius' idiosyncratic anthropology carried forward as binding, disclosed items rather than resolved or hidden — the same discipline applied to Tatian in I.35's own confirmation.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This candidate's rivals are structurally different from I.35's: these four authors argue primarily against **Roman civic religion and mythology** (Arnobius' seven books are substantially a point-by-point demolition of the pagan pantheon) and against the specific political charge that Christian impiety caused Rome's military and economic troubles — not primarily against named Christian heresies. Per A5, Roman religion is not separately screened; it appears correctly as this candidate's own opponent, inside its own reconstruction.

**Strand Determination (Article 21) — flagged to Doc_01, not resolved here.** Unlike I.35's genre-mixing question (outward apology bundled with internal correspondence), this candidate's internal variation is one of literary register across a plausible developmental sequence: Minucius Felix's private literary dialogue (addressed to one pagan friend, gentle and Ciceronian) → Arnobius' aggressive polemical treatise (addressed to Roman religion broadly, written in provincial Numidia) → Lactantius' systematic institutio (addressed to the whole educated Roman world, the fullest and most comprehensive of the four) — with Commodian's populist verse sitting outside this sequence entirely, addressed to ordinary pagans and Jews in a plainer register, and uncertainly dated relative to the other three. Doc_01 should determine whether this is one coherent tradition developing in stages (favoring one Representative across the arc) or requires an explicit strand split — and should resolve Commodian's placement as part of the same work, not separately.

### Section A conclusion

**Clears Section A.** A2 is satisfied for all four figures, with Minucius Felix's Christological thinness, Commodian's disputed date, and Arnobius' idiosyncratic anthropology all named as disclosed, bounded items rather than smoothed over or treated as disqualifying. A5's rival-handling (Roman religion, not Christian heterodoxy) is straightforward. The Article 21 developmental-register question is real and carried to Doc_01.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing

Strong pass, independently verified against the library. Minucius Felix's *Octavius* (~24,000 words), Commodian's *Instructiones* (~15,000), and Arnobius' seven books *Against the Heathen* (~140,000) are vendored whole (`anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml`, `anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml`); Lactantius' *Divine Institutes*, *On the Anger of God*, *On the Workmanship of God*, and fragments are vendored whole (`anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml`). Just under half a million words in total — larger than most candidates in the census can call on, built or unbuilt.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on the same apologetic-argument register as I.35, translated into Latin rhetorical and legal idiom — Arnobius and Lactantius are both trained rhetoricians, and the genre's persecution-and-persuasion stakes run throughout, sharpened by the fact that Lactantius writes during and immediately after the empire's most severe persecution effort. Genuinely thin, and structurally so rather than by oversight: none of these four texts is primarily a description of Christian worship practice the way Justin's is for I.35 — this candidate's own worship-practice lens will be thin on its own terms, not because a sibling world already claimed the material, but because the genre itself (refuting pagan religion, not describing Christian liturgy) doesn't supply it. Ordinary lay formation, women's voices, and enslaved persons' own testimony are essentially absent, the same structural skew as I.35 and IJC before it.

**Passes B2**, with the worship-practice and lay-formation thinness named as a property of the genre itself, disclosed plainly rather than manufactured.

### B3 — Uniqueness

- **Versus I.35 (The Second-Century Greek Apologists, unbuilt candidate):** direct check performed, not assumed. No figure overlap — Minucius Felix, Commodian, Arnobius, and Lactantius appear nowhere in I.35's own roster (Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, Tatian, and the sub-apostolic fragments). The relationship is sequential rather than competitive: this candidate is explicitly a second, later, Latin-language act of the same basic project, a century on, and — as established in this same research conversation — the two periods resolve in opposite ways (I.35's Justin dies not knowing whether the argument will ever be heeded; this candidate's Lactantius lives to tutor the emperor's own son). That difference in lived outcome, not just language, is what keeps them from collapsing into one strand-plural world; see the parallel discussion already on record in I.35's own §1 naming note.
- **Versus Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity (LPC, Selected — Not Yet Built, dates c. 240s–430):** the more consequential adjacency, because the geography and language overlap closely — Arnobius writes in Sicca, Numidia, the same province as Carthage, and this candidate's tail (Arnobius, Lactantius) is contemporary with Cyprian's own successors and the opening of the Donatist dispute. Direct inspection shows the two projects are answering the *same crisis* (the Great Persecution and its aftermath) in opposite directions: LPC/Donatism's own tradition turns inward, fighting over who compromised under persecution and whose sacraments remain valid; this candidate turns outward, defending Christian loyalty and reasonableness to the empire that was doing the persecuting. No figure overlap was found between this candidate's four authors and LPC's own roster in this pass (LPC's own figure/source roster was not exhaustively re-checked here and should be at Doc_01, since LPC is itself mid-build).
- **Versus Imperial-Juridical Christianity (IJC, Built & Live):** real but narrow overlap, directly verified. Lactantius appears in IJC today exactly once, cited only for *De Mortibus Persecutorum* and only for one story — Constantine's pre-battle vision (`ijc.source.lactantius-de-mortibus.md`, `ijc.story.dream-before-battle.md`). His actual apologetic corpus, the *Divine Institutes* and its companion works — the bulk of what makes this candidate's B1 case strong — is completely untouched by IJC. This is a lighter version of I.35's Justin problem, closer to IJC's own "World #5 vigilance" pattern than to a hard boundary requiring a project-lead ruling before drafting: this candidate should draw on Lactantius' argumentative corpus and explicitly avoid re-deriving the De Mortibus vision narrative IJC already owns, and Doc_01 should log this as a live boundary rather than an assumed-obvious one. The relationship, once that boundary is respected, is a clean sequential bridge: this candidate covers the intellectual case *for* the empire embracing Christianity; IJC covers what happened once it did.
- **Versus the other six built worlds:** no figure or source overlap found in this pass; a full sweep should be completed at Doc_01.

**Passes B3 cleanly** — the Lactantius/IJC point requires the same kind of disclosed vigilance IJC itself already models for its own adjacencies, but does not rise to the binding, undecided ruling I.35's Justin question requires.

### B4 — Audiences

Same core fit as I.35 — *academically minded users*, *thoughtful believers* on faith-and-reason questions, and the underserved rational-apologetics audience — plus a distinct further pull for anyone already following the North African thread through LPC and Donatism, since this candidate's own tail sits in the same province and the same crisis, viewed from the opposite side.

### B5 — Scale

Stronger tiebreak than most candidates in the census, including I.35: Rome, Numidia, and the two imperial capitals of the persecution-and-settlement era, Nicomedia (Diocletian's east) and Trier (Constantine's west) — a genuine span across the empire's own administrative geography, not just several cities in one region.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A directly (§2), with two disclosed complications (Commodian's dating, Arnobius' anthropology) rather than any disqualifying divergence; clears Sourcing strongly, among the largest corpora in the census (B1); clears Ecology with a structurally-caused, honestly-named thinness (B2); clears Uniqueness cleanly, with one adjacency (Lactantius/IJC) flagged for ordinary vigilance rather than a binding pre-work ruling (B3); serves real, distinctive audiences (B4); strong geographic scale (B5). **Tier 1 — Strong seed.** Cleaner than I.35 on B3 specifically: no shared, fully-claimed figure comparable to Justin was found in any built or building world.
