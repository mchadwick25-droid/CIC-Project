## Quick Meaning

The church in this world is not an institution with membership — it is the formation community gathered around the Logos who gives himself to be shared, within which the soul's formation toward God occurs through Scripture, sacramental participation, community life, and the authority of teacher and bishop; it is both the school tradition's formation community and the whole community gathered in worship, holding both in the genuine tension the ecology names.

---

## World Meaning

Ekklesia means the called-out assembly — not a building, not an organization, not an institution with membership requirements. The assembly of those who have been called out from one life into another, gathered around the Logos who called them and who is the center around which they are gathered. Every time this community gathers — in the catechumenal instruction, in the Eucharistic assembly, in the bishop's governance of the whole community's formation life — it is the ekklesia: the community constituted by the Logos's address and the community's response to that address.

The church exists because formation cannot be private. The Soul/Psyche entry (alexlex010) established that the soul is not opposed to the body but expressed through it; and the soul's formation is not opposed to community but occurs within it. The catechumen is not formed privately and then joins the community; the catechumen is formed through belonging to the community that catechizes, worships, prays, gathers around the Eucharist, and embodies what formation toward God looks like in the communal life of actual people. The school tradition's students are not formed privately by their own study; they are formed through the teacher's accompaniment in the company of other students and of the community's formation inheritance. The community is not the place where the already-formed gather; it is the environment within which formation occurs.

The church holds in tension two genuinely different modes of being a formation community. The Learning-Community Tensional Gravity (Tensional Gravity 7) is operative in the church itself: the school tradition's formation community (relatively small, literate, organized around the teacher's interpretation of Scripture) and the whole community gathered in worship (including the non-literate majority, organized around the bishop's presidency at the Eucharist, reaching the whole baptized community through sacramental participation). Both are the ekklesia. Neither is the full ekklesia without the other. The school tradition without the gathered community is an intellectual enterprise disconnected from the community's full formation life. The gathered community without the school tradition lacks the interpretive depth that the school tradition provides. The ekklesia holds both in the genuine tension that is the ecology's live structural reality.

The Eucharist (alexlex026) is what gathers the ekklesia most fundamentally. The bishop who presides at the Eucharistic table is gathering the community around the Logos who gives himself to be shared — and in that gathering, the ekklesia is most fully what it is: the community of those who are participating together in the life of the one they are gathered around. The church is the Eucharistic community before it is anything else. Its institution, its structures, its governance, its catechetical work, its school tradition — all of these serve the community gathered around the participation the Eucharist enacts.

Belonging to the church is not holding membership in an organization. It is being incorporated into the formation community — through baptism, through the ongoing participation in the community's formation life, through the reception of the formation practices that the community provides, through the accountability to the Rule of Faith that the community carries. The baptized person who never participates in the community's formation life has a nominal belonging. The catechumen who has not yet been baptized but is genuinely participating in the community's formation — hearing Scripture, praying with the community, being formed by its catechetical work — is more fully in the church than the nominal member. Belonging is constituted by genuine participation in the formation community's life.

## Ecological Function

The church is the formation community within which the formation ecology operates — the environment within which the soul's formation toward God occurs and which the Primary Gravities organize.

**What the church anchors:**

- **Formation's communal necessity**: The formation ecology is not a set of practices that individuals engage in privately and then report on to the community. It is the community's shared life — what the community practices together, hears together, enacts together — through which the Primary Gravities reach individual souls. The church is the formation environment, not the reporting structure for individual formation.

- **The Eucharist's community-constituting function**: The Eucharist gathers the community around the Logos who gives himself to be shared. The church is constituted by that gathering — by the community's recurring enacted participation in the Logos's self-giving. Remove the Eucharist and the church is a voluntary association; restore the Eucharistic gathering and the church is the community constituted by its participation in the Logos's life.

- **The Teacher-Bishop tension's ecclesial form**: The Learning-Community Tensional Gravity is the ekklesia's internal live tension. Both the school tradition's formation community and the whole gathered community are the ekklesia; neither subsumes the other; the tension between these two modes of being the community is the church's live structural reality in this ecology.

- **The Rule of Faith's communal carrier**: The Rule of Faith (alexlex031) is what the church carries across generations — the received apostolic deposit that both teacher and bishop are accountable to and that neither may override. The church is the community that carries the Rule; the Rule is what identifies the continuity of the community across time.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing**: "Church" in modern English most commonly means either a building or an institution. The building hearing: the church is where Christians meet — a physical space with particular architectural features. The institutional hearing: the church is an organization with membership, governance, programs, and budget — a voluntary association of like-minded Christians who share beliefs and practices.

Both hearings miss what the ekklesia is in this world. The building hearing misses that the ekklesia is the assembly, not its location — the gathered community constituted by its gathering around the Logos, not the space in which it gathers. The institutional hearing misses that the ekklesia is a formation community rather than a voluntary association — constituted not by shared beliefs or mutual membership but by genuine participation in the Logos's life through the formation practices the community provides and enacts together.

A more theologically sophisticated modern hearing — the church as the body of Christ, the people of God — is closer to the world hearing but can still be held abstractly, as a theological concept applied to what is functionally an institution. The world hearing insists: the ekklesia is not a community that holds the correct theological concept of itself. The ekklesia is the community that is actually gathered around the Logos who gives himself to be shared, actually participating in the formation that the Logos's presence in Scripture and sacrament and community makes possible, actually being formed toward participation in divine life through the practices the community enacts.

**World Hearing**: The ekklesia is the formation community constituted by the Logos's address and the community's response to that address — gathered around the Eucharistic participation that is the community's recurring center, governed by the teacher's formation guidance and the bishop's formation governance, accountable to the Rule of Faith both carry, including the school tradition's literate formation community and the whole gathered community in the same genuinely tensional reality. Membership in the ekklesia is genuine participation in this community's formation life; belonging is constituted by participation, not by registration.

## Key Sources

- **Clement of Alexandria**, *Stromata*, Book VII, ch. 5; *Quis Dives Salvetur* — The community gathered around the Logos; the church as the formation community within which genuine gnosis and love develop.
- **Origen**, *Against Celsus*, Book III, chs. 29–30; *Commentary on Matthew* — The church as the community of genuine formation rather than merely nominal membership; the tension between the school tradition's community and the whole gathered community.
- **Athanasius**, *Festal Letters* — The bishop governing the whole Egyptian church's formation life through the annual calendar; the church as the whole community the bishop gathers.
- **1 Corinthians 12:12–27** (scriptural) — "For just as the body is one and has many members... so it is with Christ" — the body of Christ as the ekklesia; the community constituted by participation in the one body.
- **Matthew 16:18; 18:17** (scriptural) — "On this rock I will build my ekklesia" — the Logos constituting the ekklesia; the community as the Logos's own gathered formation community.
