## 1. Distinct World Criteria

What makes this a distinct formation world is a cluster of recurring, evidenced features, not merely "the Reformation in general":

- **Recurring formation ecology, in two registers the census itself keeps deliberately distinct.** One register is Luther's and Melanchthon's own written corpus — treatises, catechisms, confessional documents — produced from inside the Wittenberg theological faculty and addressed outward to specific named readers. The second register is the territorial churches' own lived, administered record: the Saxon visitation records of 1527–28 (Melanchthon's own *Instructions for the Visitors* governing them), church ordinances, and named lay voices publishing under their own names (Argula von Grumbach, Katharina Schütz Zell) — none of which are yet vendored into this project's library (see §10). The census's own source notes state the reason for holding these apart explicitly: **"Founder corpus kept distinct from the congregations' record so founder-gravity discipline can run"** — this is this world's own methodological self-awareness that a founder's stated program and a movement's actual lived reception are different evidentiary classes, not a claim that they are two strands (§6 below addresses why this is not a strand question).
- **Recurring candidate gravities** (preliminary only; Doc_04 is the formal test) — untagged for confidence here deliberately, since candidate-gravity status is itself provisional pending Doc_04's six-test assessment, not a claim this document is making at any confidence level yet: justification by faith apart from meritorious works, argued repeatedly and centrally rather than as one doctrine among many (Augsburg Confession Article IV: "men cannot be justified before God by their own strength, merits, or works, but are freely justified for Christ's sake, through faith"; the *simul justus et peccator* formula sometimes used to label this argument is this project's own corpus-map editorial note, not a phrase this document has found inside the six vendored files themselves — **corrected, Round 1 finding S4**, which located it nowhere in the library and traced it to Luther's Romans lectures and *Against Latomus* in the wider secondary literature, neither vendored here); Scripture's own authority set directly against an intervening institutional hierarchy (*To the Christian Nobility of the German Nation*; *The Papacy at Rome, an Answer to the Celebrated Romanist at Leipzig* — **title corrected to the file's own heading, Round 1 cosmetic finding C3**); the sacraments reduced from seven to two or three and re-argued from Scripture directly (*A Prelude on the Babylonian Captivity of the Church*, verified directly: "I must deny that there are seven sacraments, and hold for the present to but three — baptism, penance and the bread," later narrowed further to "but two sacraments in the Church of God — baptism and bread"); and — the congregational register's own candidate, distinct from the founder's — catechesis as the actual mechanism by which ordinary households, not only clergy, were expected to hold the new doctrine (the Small Catechism's own household framing, §4 below).
- **Recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority** (Construction Framework para 92 names all five; **this document answers formation, interpretation, and authority here and defers worship and belonging rather than silently skipping them, Round 1 cosmetic finding C12**): doctrine argued in writing, in both German and Latin (§4), addressed to specific named readers — Albrecht, Archbishop of Magdeburg and Mainz (1517); Pope Leo X; Herman Tulich, Luther's own friend and the dedicatee of the *Babylonian Captivity*; Nicholas von Amsdorf (*To the Christian Nobility*); and named territorial princes, Frederick and John of Saxony (*Fourteen of Consolation*, *A Treatise on Good Works*) — **corrected, Round 1 finding S14: no vendored work is addressed to "brother-monks," and that addressee is removed**; a movement that produced its own confessional self-definition twice within its own window, at increasing formality (Augsburg Confession, 1530; Book of Concord, 1580); and — per the census's own visitation-record leg — a self-conscious institutional practice of *checking* whether the doctrine had actually reached ordinary believers, with the checking itself part of the historical record rather than a modern historian's retrospective addition. **Worship** is addressed at §5 and §8; **belonging** — who counted as inside this world's own congregations, and on what terms — is not yet answerable from this library (visitation records and parish registers are unvendored, §10) and is named here as a real gap rather than answered thinly.
- **What distinguishes this world from its nearest neighbors** (argued fully under World Continuity & Distinction, §8): from the Reformed cities (VI.2, same batch), this world is Lutheran specifically on the point that actually broke fellowship between them — the Eucharist (§7, §8.1). From the Anabaptist movements (VI.3, same batch), this world is a territorial, state-recognized magisterial Reformation, not a movement whose adherents this world's own authority structures helped suppress (§8.2). From the Tridentine Church (VI.22, same batch), this world is the doctrinal position Trent's own canons were convened to answer, not the answering body (§8.3). From the Society of Jesus (VI.11, same batch), this world is one of the two movements the Jesuits' own Step 0 names as their direct rival and reason for existing, not a movement in mutual isolation from them (§8.4, corrected this revision — Round 1 finding S13).

## 2. Temporal Scope

### 2.1 Beginning point: 1517, on the movement's own founding document

**Documented, verified directly against this world's own vendored file, not inferred.** `luther_works-v1-selected_jacobs-spaeth1915.txt` carries, at its own head, a section title page (line 436: "THE DISPUTATION OF DOCTOR MARTIN LUTHER ON THE POWER AND EFFICACY OF INDULGENCES (THE NINETY-FIVE THESES), 1517, TOGETHER WITH THREE LETTERS EXPLANATORY OF THE THESES" — **corrected, Round 1 cosmetic finding C2**, which is the unit's own title page, not a differently-arranged front-matter heading as the first draft garbled it), followed by the editors' introduction, then Luther's own covering letter to Albrecht, Archbishop of Magdeburg and Mainz, editorially dated **"OCTOBER 31, 1517"** (line 992 — **corrected, Round 1 cosmetic finding C1**) and subscribed by Luther himself "From Wittenberg on the Vigil of All Saints, MDXVII," and then the Ninety-Five Theses themselves (beginning line 1138). The letter's own closing — "If it please the Most Reverend Father he may see these my Disputations, and learn how doubtful a thing is the opinion of indulgences" — confirms the Theses travel as the letter's own enclosure, in this edition and in the historical event both. This is the conventional founding date of the Lutheran Reformation and this document finds no reason in the vendored text to disturb it: **[Documented]**.

### 2.2 Ending point: 1580, matching the census and Step 0 both — no real tension between them

The census's own `dates` field and Step 0 §1 both give this world's window as **1517–1580**. Step 0's own sentence reads in full: *"the 95 Theses (1517) through the consolidation of the Lutheran territorial churches after the Peace of Augsburg (1555) and into the next generation"* [Step 0 §1] — **the first draft of this document truncated that sentence to cut "and into the next generation" and, on that truncation, manufactured a "real alternative" between 1555 and 1580 that Step 0 does not actually pose (corrected, Round 1 finding S8).** Step 0 passes through 1555 on its way to 1580; it does not offer 1555 as a competing closing candidate.

**What is genuinely true, stated without manufacturing a false tension:** 1555 (the Peace of Augsburg, imperial recognition of Lutheran princes' right to determine their territories' confession under *cuius regio, eius religio*) and 1580 (the Book of Concord, this world's own most complete confessional self-definition — the Augsburg Confession and its Apology, both vendored here; the Small and Large Catechisms, both vendored here; plus the Smalcald Articles and the Formula of Concord, not yet vendored) are two real, different-in-kind markers inside the same window — one political-legal, one doctrinal-confessional — and this document adopts 1580 as the working ceiling because it is what the census and Step 0 both already give, not because 1555 was ever a rejected alternative. Doc_02 and Doc_04 should treat 1555 as a genuine internal-transition marker within the window (§2.3), not a boundary question.

### 2.3 What remains internal vs. what indicates transition

**Internal to this world, across the whole 1517–1580 span:** the argument for justification by faith and Scripture's authority against the papacy, continuously restated rather than abandoned; catechesis as the practical mechanism for lay formation, present from the earliest treatises (*A Treatise on the Holy Sacrament of Baptism*, 1519) through the 1529 catechisms to the Book of Concord's own inclusion of both catechisms as confessional documents in 1580; the territorial-church structure (visitation, ordinances) that begins forming in the 1520s and is still the operative structure in 1580.

**Indicates transition, within the window, disclosed as real internal change rather than treated as a single flat continuity:**

- **1521–1522 — the Wartburg exile and the Wittenberg unrest.** `luther_works-v2-selected_jacobs-spaeth1916.txt` carries **The Eight Wittenberg Sermons**; this document's own reading of the file's editorial introduction independently corroborates the corpus-map's note — Wartburg from 4 May 1521; Luther's return to Wittenberg 6 March 1522; the sermons preached from Sunday 9 March "and the seven days following," to check unrest Melanchthon "could not hold back" led by Carlstadt and Zwilling; the file's own account ends with "Carlstadt was silenced, the city council made acknowledgment to Luther... and Wittenberg bowed to law and order" — direct evidence that Wittenberg's own reform was not monolithic even in its first years, and that Luther's own authority was exercised to check a more rapid or disorderly reform led by one of his own Wittenberg colleagues. This document does not treat this as a strand within this world (§6) — Karlstadt's own subsequent trajectory moves him outside Wittenberg's own institutional authority, which is precisely why this is a transition-indicating event rather than an internal-variance one. **[Widely Accepted]**, on the file's own direct testimony.
- **1524–1525 — the Peasants' War.** Named directly as a binding disclosure obligation by Step 0 (§4 item 2, binding on Doc_02): Luther's *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of Peasants* (1525) is not in this world's vendored library yet (§10). This document does not have the text in hand to characterize it beyond what Step 0 already names, and defers full disclosure to Doc_02 as Step 0 requires — but marks the event itself as a real internal transition point: after 1525, this world's own relationship to political authority (siding with territorial princes against a popular uprising with real economic and religious grievances) is settled in a way it was not before.
- **1529 — Marburg.** Addressed in full in §7 and §8.1 below, since Step 0 (§4 item 3) makes this binding specifically on Doc_01.
- **1530 and 1580 — the confessional documents themselves**, already treated in §2.2, are also internal developments in their own right: a movement that argued its case in occasional treatises in 1517–1520 had, by 1530, produced a single formal confession subscribed by named princes, and by 1580 a complete confessional corpus — a real change in institutional register, from protest literature to settled confession, that Doc_05 and Doc_07 should treat as a genuine ecological development rather than assume was already true from the start. **[Documented]** as to the documents' own dates; **[Inferential/Thin]** as to how sharply "register change" should be read as a substantive transition versus a continuous formalization — a Doc_05 question, not settled here.

## 3. Geographic Scope

**Core:** Wittenberg, in Electoral Saxony — the university town where Luther held his professorship and where the movement's own founding acts (the Theses, the early treatises, the catechisms) originated. **[Documented]**, but stated precisely about what actually verifies it, corrected from an overstatement Round 1 caught (finding S3): the two Holman *Works* volumes confirm this directly and repeatedly (e.g., "From Wittenberg on the Vigil of All Saints, MDXVII," §2.1) — but the four confessional/catechetical files do **not** independently confirm it. The historical claim itself is not in doubt; the verification basis is now stated as what actually supports it.

**Expansion within window:** the census's own `region` field states "Saxony, then N. Europe" and its `regions` field gives "North Europe." This document treats the *direction* of this spread — outward from Electoral Saxony into other German territorial states, and from there toward Scandinavia — as **Widely Accepted**, on uncontested general history this document has not independently re-verified against a dedicated secondary source this pass (a gap named for Doc_02, not concealed here): several German princes had adopted the Lutheran confession by the 1530s, and the Scandinavian kingdoms' own state churches moved toward Lutheran establishment within or just after this world's own window. **This document does not commit to specific Scandinavian conversion dates** — they are well short of a claim this document can support from the vendored library alone, and are logged as an open item for Doc_02 (§11) rather than stated here as fact.

**A named site this world's own vendored library speaks to directly but that is not itself a separate geographic node:** the Diet of Augsburg (1530), where the Augsburg Confession was submitted to Emperor Charles V, took place in Augsburg, not Wittenberg — a reminder that this world's own confessional acts happened at the level of the whole Empire's own political geography, not only inside Electoral Saxony.

## 5. World Separation Criteria

**Has a new gravity emerged marking entry into this world?** Yes **[Documented]**: Luther's 1517 protest against indulgence sales, escalating within three years (1520) into a full argument against the papacy's own sacramental and institutional authority, marks a clear entry point — a formation practice (doctrine argued from Scripture against an intervening hierarchy, addressed to specific named readers, §1) distinct from the late-medieval piety this world's own census `relationsSummary` names as its parent ("child of late-medieval piety and its protest").

**Has a major gravity disappeared?** Yes **[Documented]**: the medieval sacramental system's own seven-sacrament structure and the papacy's own claimed authority over doctrine are both explicitly and repeatedly refused within this world's own vendored texts (*A Prelude on the Babylonian Captivity of the Church*; *The Papacy at Rome*) — not merely absent, but argued against by name and by citation.

**Has formation changed substantially?** Yes **[Widely Accepted]**: from late-medieval sacramental and penitential practice to catechetical instruction grounded directly in Scripture and in a small set of confessional documents, with the household (not only the monastery or the confessional) as a formation site the movement explicitly targets (§4).

**Has worship changed substantially?** Yes, in argued doctrine, and — corrected this revision (Round 1 finding S10, which found the first draft's claim of absence flatly wrong) — **also directly evidenced in this world's own vendored library as lived practice, not only as doctrine.** The Babylonian Captivity and *A Treatise on the New Testament, That Is the Holy Mass* argue a changed theology of the Mass (testament/promise rather than sacrifice). **The Augsburg Confession Article XXIV, read directly, goes further and describes the changed practice itself, in the movement's own first-person voice, in 1530:** *"the Mass is retained among us, and celebrated with the highest reverence. Nearly all the usual ceremonies are also preserved, save that the parts sung in Latin are interspersed here and there with German hymns, which have been added to teach the people... The people are accustomed to partake of the Sacrament together, if any be fit for it... For none are admitted except they be first examined."* This is congregational German-language hymn-singing inside the Mass, communion practice, and pre-communion examination, all described by the movement about itself. **[Widely Accepted]**, with an honest limit stated rather than overclaimed: no actual service order or hymn text is vendored (§10), and Article XXIV is an apologetic self-description addressed to an emperor defending Lutheran practice against a charge of abolishing the Mass, not a neutral administrative record — so it is evidence of the movement's own claimed and defended practice, not independent confirmation of what every parish actually did. Doc_05 should treat worship as argued-for-in-doctrine **and** claimed-in-practice-by-the-movement-itself, with the gap being independent (non-self-reporting) confirmation, not lived practice as such.

**Has authority changed substantially?** Yes **[Documented]**: from papal and episcopal authority to a settlement in which territorial princes hold legal authority over their own churches' confession (§2.2, §3), advised by a university theological faculty rather than ruled by an independent church hierarchy.

**Has interpretation changed substantially?** Yes **[Documented]**: *sola scriptura* — Scripture's own authority set against councils, popes, and canon law where they conflict with it — is argued directly and repeatedly across the vendored treatises, a genuinely new interpretive rule for this world rather than an inherited one.

## 6. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

**Finding: strand-singular, per the Framework's own stated default — stated as this document's own considered finding, not as an unsettled question, even though the word "provisional" describes its own openness to Doc_04's testing rather than doubt in the reasoning itself (a distinction Round 1 correctly pressed, finding S6).**

Constitution Article 21 itself defines a strand as **"a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail"** — quoted here precisely, correcting the first draft's looser paraphrase in the Construction Framework's own plural wording, which dropped the clause doing the most work against treating ordinary variation as a strand (**corrected, Round 1 cosmetic finding C7**).

This document considered, and rejects on that precise definition, two candidates:

- **The founder-corpus/congregation-record asymmetry** named in §1 and §4 is not a strand in Article 21's sense. What the census's own source notes describe is an *evidentiary* asymmetry (one register documented by its own authors' treatises, the other documented by administrators observing lay reception) internal to a single, unified formation ecology — the kind of distinction Author Gravity Assessment (Constitution Article 26, Doc_02) exists to handle, not a finding that two coexisting communities practiced formation, worship, authority, or ecological orientation differently.
- **The 1522 Wittenberg unrest** (§2.3) shows real internal disagreement (Luther versus Karlstadt) but resolves within the window, under Luther's own continued authority, rather than persisting as two coexisting patterns — a transition event, not a strand. **Corrected, Round 1 finding S5: this document does not claim Gallic Monastic-Ascetic Christianity's own Doc_01 "rejected a comparable temptation" on this ground — it did not.** That document's own §6 record is provisionally strand-singular for a different, still-open reason (an unresolved one-world-or-two-worlds question it explicitly leaves for Doc_04, laying out evidence in both directions rather than rejecting either), and this document withdraws the invented precedent rather than repeat it.

**No positive evidence for genuinely distinct internal strands has surfaced in this document's own reading of the vendored library.** This world is recorded strand-singular, per the Framework's own default rule. **Methodological note, carried forward from the same correction the Gallic Doc_01 itself was forced to make on its own record (its own Round 2 finding N11), rather than reproducing only the hedge without the correction (Round 1 finding S6):** the Framework gives Step 4 *cross-strand gravity testing* across already-confirmed strands (Construction Framework para 122), not explicit authority to revise a Step 1 strand finding itself. This status is delivered as this document's own considered finding under the Framework's own default rule — not withheld as "not actually decided," and not something Doc_04 can simply overturn on its own say-so if it later suspects otherwise; a genuine reopening would need its own argued basis, stated as such.

## 7. Preliminary Forces Identification

**Quoted precisely** (Forces Framework V1.1 §4, Step 1 — **corrected, Round 1 finding S1, which printed the Construction Framework's four-separate-capitalized-question wording while claiming to quote the Forces Framework directly; the two documents say the same thing in different form, and this revision quotes the one it cites**): *"the builder conducts preliminary forces identification: what generated this world, what larger world was it embedded in, what was it responding to, what was it refusing?"*

**What generated this world [Documented]:** an Augustinian friar and university theology professor's own developing conviction — argued first over the specific, bounded question of indulgence sales, then rapidly generalized — that the practice of the late-medieval Western church had come to rest on an authority (the papacy's own claimed power to bind and loose, extended into the sale of indulgences) that Scripture itself does not support. The vendored 1517 letter to Albrecht of Mainz shows this beginning as a bounded, respectful protest to a specific archbishop about a specific abuse, not yet a movement — the escalation to a full institutional break happens across the following three years, evidenced directly in the 1520 treatises' own widening scope.

**What larger world it was embedded in [Documented]:** late-medieval Western European Christendom under a single, if already contested, papal authority, with the Holy Roman Empire as its immediate political frame — an empire of numerous semi-autonomous princes and cities whose own political fragmentation is part of why a territorial, prince-by-prince settlement (rather than a single empire-wide resolution) became this world's own eventual legal shape (§2.2, §3).

**What it was responding to [Widely Accepted]:** the practical and pastoral problem, argued directly in the vendored texts, of a laity taught to rely on indulgences, private confession to a priest, and the church's own sacramental system for assurance of salvation, rather than on direct trust in Scripture's own promise. *A Treatise on Christian Liberty* and the Babylonian Captivity both argue this is a pastoral failure with real consequences for ordinary believers' own spiritual confidence, not only an abstract doctrinal dispute.

**What it was refusing:** most directly and by name, the papacy's own claimed authority to define doctrine and administer the sacraments as a matter that binds the conscience apart from Scripture (*The Papacy at Rome*; the Babylonian Captivity's reduction of the sacraments) — refused in writing, addressed to named readers, rather than argued only within a closed circle of sympathizers **[Documented]**. **A second, later refusal, real and disclosed rather than smoothed into a single undifferentiated "Reformation":** the Eucharistic theology of the Reformed tradition, refused directly at the **Marburg Colloquy (1529)**. This world's own Step 0 (§2 A3) and the Reformed cities' own sibling Step 0 (§4 item 5 — **corrected, Round 1 cosmetic finding C5: the exact phrase "the Marburg break with Luther" is that document's §4 item 5 heading, not its §2 A3, which instead describes "the Marburg Colloquy (1529) Eucharistic break with Luther, from this movement's own side"**) both name this as a real, sharp, contemporary disagreement between two movements this project is building in the same batch. **This document does not have a vendored primary source describing Luther's own side of the Marburg exchange directly — verified this revision: "Marburg" and "Zwingli" both return zero hits across all six vendored files — and does not overstate what it can currently source.** What the library does support directly, without overreaching toward Marburg itself, is the Lutheran position on the substance of the disagreement: Augsburg Confession Article X states plainly, in the movement's own confessional voice, "the Body and Blood of Christ are truly present, and are distributed to those who eat the Supper of the Lord; and they reject those that teach otherwise" — the doctrinal content the Marburg break was fought over, independently confirmed in this world's own vendored text, even though the Colloquy itself is not.

## 8. World Continuity & Distinction

Construction Framework Part I asks five questions here — what this world inherits, what it transmits, what shared material functions differently across adjacent ecologies, where continuity masks change, and where discontinuity masks continuity — plus named comparisons against genuinely adjacent worlds. **The first draft answered only the comparisons and lost the five questions entirely (Round 1 finding S7); this revision answers both.**

### 8.0 The five questions, answered directly

**What does this world inherit from prior worlds?** Late-medieval Western piety and its own sacramental, penitential, and monastic culture — most concretely through the Augustinian Hermits, Luther's own order (§8.6) — and, more broadly, the entire inherited apparatus (canon law, the seven sacraments, the parish and diocesan structure) this world argues against rather than invents from nothing. A movement that refuses an inheritance is still shaped by having had to refuse it specifically, rather than something else.

**What does it transmit to subsequent worlds?** The census's own `legacy` field states this directly and this document adopts it rather than paraphrasing around it: *"an ongoing communion of tens of millions across Germany, Scandinavia, the Baltic and North America, and, through later missions, Africa and Asia... Its most durable everyday bequest is less doctrinal than liturgical and musical: congregational hymn-singing in the local language, the small catechism learned by heart at home, and a tradition of church music that runs from Luther's own tunes through to Bach."* **[Widely Accepted]** as a general claim about long-term legacy; this document has not independently verified the Bach lineage or the Africa/Asia mission claim against a dedicated source and does not overstate its own confidence in the specifics beyond what the census itself asserts.

**What shared material functions differently within this ecology than in adjacent ecologies?** Scripture's own authority against institutional hierarchy is the clearest case: this world shares that refusal with both the Reformed cities and the Anabaptist movements, but pairs it with territorial-magisterial authority (§3, §5) — princes and city councils administering the new confession — where the Anabaptist movements' own Step 0 (§2 A3) describes exactly this world and the Reformed cities as acting "from the establishment side" against them, and where the Reformed cities' own polity (per that world's own Step 0 B4, presbyterian/consistorial governance) organizes the same Scriptural authority through a different institutional form again. The same refusal of Rome; three different institutional answers to what replaces it.

**Where does continuity mask genuine ecological change?** The census's own "sibling-rival of the Reformed" language, and the shared vocabulary of justification-by-faith and Scripture's authority, can read as near-identity with the Reformed cities. Marburg (§7, §8.1) is the corrective: a real, sharp, fellowship-breaking theological difference sits under an apparent family resemblance, and this document names that directly rather than letting the surface similarity stand for the whole relationship.

**Where does apparent discontinuity mask deeper structural continuity?** This document states this cautiously, as a real question rather than a settled finding, since the evidence for it is not yet in hand: the public, sharp break with the papacy's own doctrinal authority is total and loudly declared in this world's own texts, but the territorial-church visitation system (§4) appears, on the census's own description, to administer and inspect *existing* Saxon parishes rather than to build new congregations from nothing — which would mean real administrative and parish-level continuity with late-medieval Saxon church life sitting underneath the doctrinal rupture. **[Inferential/Thin]**: this document has no vendored visitation-record text to confirm this and states it as a plausible hypothesis for Doc_02 to test, not a finding.
