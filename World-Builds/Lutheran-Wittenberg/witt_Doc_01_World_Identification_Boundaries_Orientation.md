## 1. Distinct World Criteria

What makes this a distinct formation world is a cluster of recurring, evidenced features, not merely "the Reformation in general":

- **Recurring formation ecology, in two registers the census itself keeps deliberately distinct.** One register is Luther's and Melanchthon's own written corpus — treatises, catechisms, confessional documents — produced from inside the Wittenberg theological faculty and addressed outward to specific named readers. The second register is the territorial churches' own lived, administered record: the Saxon visitation records of 1527–28 (Melanchthon's own *Instructions for the Visitors* governing them), church ordinances, and named lay voices publishing under their own names (Argula von Grumbach, Katharina Schütz Zell) — none of which are yet vendored into this project's library (see §10). The census's own source notes state the reason for holding these apart explicitly: **"Founder corpus kept distinct from the congregations' record so founder-gravity discipline can run"** — this is this world's own methodological self-awareness that a founder's stated program and a movement's actual lived reception are different evidentiary classes, not a claim that they are two strands (§6 below addresses why this is not a strand question).
- **Recurring candidate gravities** (preliminary only; Doc_04's own six-test assessment is what decides whether any of these actually functions as a gravity — **this document tags the evidence for each claim below at the confidence level the vendored text itself supports, correcting Round 2 finding N17, which caught the first revision conflating "Doc_04 hasn't confirmed gravity status yet" with "no confidence claim is being made" — the vocabulary rates the evidence, not the gravity determination**): justification by faith apart from meritorious works, argued repeatedly and centrally rather than as one doctrine among many **[Documented]** (Augsburg Confession Article IV: "men cannot be justified before God by their own strength, merits, or works, but are freely justified for Christ's sake, through faith"; the *simul justus et peccator* formula sometimes used to label this argument is this project's own corpus-map editorial note, not a phrase this document has found inside the six vendored files themselves — **corrected, Round 1 finding S4**, which located it nowhere in the library and traced it to Luther's Romans lectures and *Against Latomus* in the wider secondary literature, neither vendored here); Scripture's own authority set directly against an intervening institutional hierarchy **[Documented]** (*To the Christian Nobility of the German Nation*; *The Papacy at Rome, an Answer to the Celebrated Romanist at Leipzig* — the volume carries this title at two points, its own section title page (line 12211) and the translation's own heading, "To the Papacy at Rome" (line 12694); both are the file's own wording, and the corpus-map's citation was never actually wrong, correcting Round 1 cosmetic finding C3's own incomplete check, caught at Round 2 finding N6); the sacraments reduced from seven to two or three and re-argued from Scripture directly **[Documented]** (*A Prelude on the Babylonian Captivity of the Church*, verified directly: "I must deny that there are seven sacraments, and hold for the present to but three — baptism, penance and the bread," later narrowed further to "but two sacraments in the Church of God — baptism and bread"); and — the congregational register's own candidate, distinct from the founder's — catechesis as the actual mechanism by which ordinary households, not only clergy, were expected to hold the new doctrine **[Documented]** (the Small Catechism's own household framing; and, earlier still, *A Brief Explanation of the Ten Commandments, the Creed, and the Lord's Prayer* — the 1520 *Kurze Form*, `luther_works-v2-selected_jacobs-spaeth1916.txt` line ~13172 — which the corpus-map itself describes as "a direct precursor to the 1529 Small Catechism... the same three-part structure a decade earlier," and which this document had not previously named even though catechesis is both a named candidate gravity here and a named internal continuity at §2.3 — added this revision, Round 2 finding N13; §4 below).
- **Recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority** (Construction Framework para 92 names all five; **this document answers formation, interpretation, and authority here and defers worship and belonging rather than silently skipping them, Round 1 cosmetic finding C12**): doctrine argued in writing, in both German and Latin (§4), addressed to specific named readers — Albrecht, Archbishop of Magdeburg and Mainz (1517); Pope Leo X; Herman Tulich, Luther's own friend and the dedicatee of the *Babylonian Captivity*; Nicholas von Amsdorf (*To the Christian Nobility*); and named territorial princes, Frederick and John of Saxony (*Fourteen of Consolation*, *A Treatise on Good Works*) — **corrected, Round 1 finding S14: no vendored work is addressed to "brother-monks," and that addressee is removed**; a movement that produced its own confessional self-definition twice within its own window, at increasing formality (Augsburg Confession, 1530; Book of Concord, 1580); and — per the census's own visitation-record leg — a self-conscious institutional practice of *checking* whether the doctrine had actually reached ordinary believers, with the checking itself part of the historical record rather than a modern historian's retrospective addition. **Worship** is addressed at §5 (§8's own comparisons are doctrinal — what this world argued about worship — not practice, and the Round 1 fix that added §8 as a second pointer overstated its coverage, corrected here, Round 2 finding C12); **belonging** — who counted as inside this world's own congregations, and on what terms — is not yet answerable from this library (visitation records and parish registers are unvendored, §10) and is named here as a real gap rather than answered thinly.

## 2. Temporal Scope

### 2.1 Beginning point: 1517, on the movement's own founding document

**Documented, verified directly against this world's own vendored file, not inferred.** `luther_works-v1-selected_jacobs-spaeth1915.txt` carries, at its own head, a section title page spanning **lines 434–438** (line 436 itself is the single line "(THE NINETY-FIVE THESES)," part of the same title block: "THE DISPUTATION OF DOCTOR MARTIN LUTHER ON THE POWER AND EFFICACY OF INDULGENCES / (THE NINETY-FIVE THESES) / 1517 / TOGETHER WITH THREE LETTERS EXPLANATORY OF THE THESES" — **line range corrected, Round 2 finding N9, catching an error Round 1's own fix carried forward from finding C2**), followed by the editors' introduction, then Luther's own covering letter to Albrecht, Archbishop of Magdeburg and Mainz, editorially dated **"OCTOBER 31, 1517"** (line 992 — **corrected, Round 1 cosmetic finding C1**) and subscribed by Luther himself "From Wittenberg on the Vigil of All Saints, MDXVII," and then the Ninety-Five Theses themselves (section "II," heading at lines 1139–1140 — **corrected from "beginning line 1138," which is a blank line; Round 2 finding N9**). The letter's own closing — "If it please the Most Reverend Father he may see these my Disputations, and learn how doubtful a thing is the opinion of indulgences" — confirms the Theses travel as the letter's own enclosure, in this edition and in the historical event both. This is the conventional founding date of the Lutheran Reformation and this document finds no reason in the vendored text to disturb it: **[Documented]**.

### 2.2 Ending point: 1580, matching the census and Step 0 both — no real tension between them

The census's own `dates` field and Step 0 §1 both give this world's window as **1517–1580**. **Construction Framework para 85 states directly that "nothing in Step 0 performs Step 1's own boundary-determination work" — so Step 1 has to argue its own ending point rather than adopt one because Step 0 and the census already say so, the same standard §2.1 already meets for the beginning point. This document did not meet that standard on its first attempt here (Round 2 finding N4, which found the prior sentence's stated basis was "because it is what the census and Step 0 both already give") and argues the ending point directly now:** the Book of Concord (1580) is this world's own most complete confessional self-definition — the Augsburg Confession and its Apology, both vendored here; the Small and Large Catechisms, both vendored here; plus the Smalcald Articles and the Formula of Concord, not yet vendored — and 1580 is adopted as the working ceiling because it marks the point at which this world's own doctrinal self-definition reaches its final, most complete form within the window, the same kind of documents-in-hand argument §2.1 makes for 1517. **1555 (the Peace of Augsburg, imperial recognition of Lutheran princes' right to determine their territories' confession under *cuius regio, eius religio*) is a real marker of a different kind — political-legal rather than doctrinal-confessional — and is treated as a genuine internal-transition marker within the window (§2.3), not a competing boundary candidate and not the basis for the 1580 finding above.**

### 2.3 What remains internal vs. what indicates transition

**Internal to this world, across the whole 1517–1580 span:** the argument for justification by faith and Scripture's authority against the papacy, continuously restated rather than abandoned; catechesis as the practical mechanism for lay formation, present from the earliest treatises (*A Treatise on the Holy Sacrament of Baptism*, 1519) through the 1529 catechisms to the Book of Concord's own inclusion of both catechisms as confessional documents in 1580; the territorial-church structure (visitation, ordinances) that begins forming in the 1520s and is still the operative structure in 1580.

**Indicates transition, within the window, disclosed as real internal change rather than treated as a single flat continuity:**

- **1521–1522 — the Wartburg exile and the Wittenberg unrest.** `luther_works-v2-selected_jacobs-spaeth1916.txt` carries **The Eight Wittenberg Sermons**; this document's own reading of the file's editorial introduction independently corroborates the corpus-map's note — Wartburg from 4 May 1521; Luther's return to Wittenberg 6 March 1522; the sermons preached from Sunday 9 March "and the seven days following," to check unrest Melanchthon "could not hold back" led by Carlstadt (the file's own spelling, used here for consistency with it; the modern form "Karlstadt" appears elsewhere in this document's own prose, e.g. §1, §6, referring to the same person) and Zwilling; the file's own account ends with "Carlstadt was silenced, the city council made acknowledgment to Luther... and Wittenberg bowed to law and order" **[Widely Accepted]**, on the file's own direct testimony — direct evidence that Wittenberg's own reform was not monolithic even in its first years, and that Luther's own authority was exercised to check a more rapid or disorderly reform led by one of his own Wittenberg colleagues. This document does not treat this as a strand within this world (§6). **A further analytical claim, sourced separately rather than folded under the same tag (Round 2 finding N14, which caught the first revision covering it with a confidence tag the file itself does not support): that "Carlstadt's own subsequent trajectory moves him outside Wittenberg's own institutional authority" is general Reformation history this document has not independently verified this pass, not a claim the vendored file itself makes — the file states only that Carlstadt was silenced in 1522, not what became of him afterward. [Inferential/Thin, pending a Doc_02 source]** — the transition-versus-strand distinction (§6) rests on the documented 1522 silencing alone, which is sufficient for that purpose on its own.
- **1524–1525 — the Peasants' War.** Named directly as a binding disclosure obligation by Step 0 (§4 item 2, binding on Doc_02): Luther's *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of Peasants* (1525) is not in this world's vendored library yet (§10). This document does not have the text in hand to characterize it beyond what Step 0 already names, and defers full disclosure to Doc_02 as Step 0 requires — but marks the event itself as a real internal transition point: after 1525, this world's own relationship to political authority (siding with territorial princes against a popular uprising with real economic and religious grievances) is settled in a way it was not before.
- **1529 — Marburg.** Addressed in full in §7 and §8.1 below, since Step 0 (§4 item 3) makes this binding specifically on Doc_01.
- **1530 and 1580 — the confessional documents themselves**, already treated in §2.2, are also internal developments in their own right: a movement that argued its case in occasional treatises in 1517–1520 had, by 1530, produced a single formal confession subscribed by named princes, and by 1580 a complete confessional corpus — a real change in institutional register, from protest literature to settled confession, that Doc_05 and Doc_07 should treat as a genuine ecological development rather than assume was already true from the start. **[Documented]** as to the documents' own dates; **[Inferential/Thin]** as to how sharply "register change" should be read as a substantive transition versus a continuous formalization — a Doc_05 question, not settled here.

## 5. World Separation Criteria

**Has a new gravity emerged marking entry into this world?** Yes **[Documented]**: Luther's 1517 protest against indulgence sales, escalating within three years (1520) into a full argument against the papacy's own sacramental and institutional authority, marks a clear entry point — a formation practice (doctrine argued from Scripture against an intervening hierarchy, addressed to specific named readers, §1) distinct from the late-medieval piety this world's own census `relationsSummary` names as its parent ("child of late-medieval piety and its protest").

**Has a major gravity disappeared?** Yes **[Documented]**: the medieval sacramental system's own seven-sacrament structure and the papacy's own claimed authority over doctrine are both explicitly and repeatedly refused within this world's own vendored texts (*A Prelude on the Babylonian Captivity of the Church*; *The Papacy at Rome*) — not merely absent, but argued against by name and by citation.

**Has formation changed substantially?** Yes **[Widely Accepted]**: from late-medieval sacramental and penitential practice to catechetical instruction grounded directly in Scripture and in a small set of confessional documents, with the household (not only the monastery or the confessional) as a formation site the movement explicitly targets (§4).

**Has worship changed substantially?** Yes, in argued doctrine, and — corrected this revision (Round 1 finding S10, which found the first draft's claim of absence flatly wrong) — **also directly evidenced in this world's own vendored library as lived practice, not only as doctrine.** The Babylonian Captivity and *A Treatise on the New Testament, That Is the Holy Mass* argue a changed theology of the Mass (testament/promise rather than sacrifice). **The Augsburg Confession Article XXIV, read directly, goes further and describes the changed practice itself, in the movement's own first-person voice, in 1530:** *"the Mass is retained among us, and celebrated with the highest reverence. Nearly all the usual ceremonies are also preserved, save that the parts sung in Latin are interspersed here and there with German hymns, which have been added to teach the people... The people are accustomed to partake of the Sacrament together, if any be fit for it... For none are admitted except they be first examined."* This is congregational German-language hymn-singing inside the Mass, communion practice, and pre-communion examination, all described by the movement about itself. **[Widely Accepted]**, with an honest limit stated rather than overclaimed: no actual service order or hymn text is vendored (§10), and Article XXIV is an apologetic self-description addressed to an emperor defending Lutheran practice against a charge of abolishing the Mass, not a neutral administrative record — so it is evidence of the movement's own claimed and defended practice, not independent confirmation of what every parish actually did. Doc_05 should treat worship as argued-for-in-doctrine **and** claimed-in-practice-by-the-movement-itself, with the gap being independent (non-self-reporting) confirmation, not lived practice as such.

**Has authority changed substantially?** Yes **[Documented]**: from papal and episcopal authority to a settlement in which territorial princes hold legal authority over their own churches' confession (§2.2, §3), advised by a university theological faculty rather than ruled by an independent church hierarchy.

**Has interpretation changed substantially?** Yes **[Documented]**: *sola scriptura* — Scripture's own authority set against councils, popes, and canon law where they conflict with it — is argued directly and repeatedly across the vendored treatises, a genuinely new interpretive rule for this world rather than an inherited one.

## 6. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

**Finding: strand-singular, per the Framework's own stated default — stated as this document's own considered finding, not as an unsettled question, even though the word "provisional" describes its own openness to Doc_04's testing rather than doubt in the reasoning itself (a distinction Round 1 correctly pressed, finding S6).**

Constitution Article 21 itself defines a strand as **"a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail"** — quoted here precisely, correcting the first draft's looser paraphrase in the Construction Framework's own plural wording, which dropped the clause doing the most work against treating ordinary variation as a strand (**corrected, Round 1 cosmetic finding C7**).

This document considered, and rejects on that precise definition, two candidates:

- **The founder-corpus/congregation-record asymmetry** named in §1 and §4 is not a strand in Article 21's sense.
