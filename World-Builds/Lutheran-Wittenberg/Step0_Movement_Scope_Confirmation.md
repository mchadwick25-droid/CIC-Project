## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note.** This candidate has not been selected into a release phase; this document's own findings are a signal for Mark's use, not a self-administered disposition, the same standing this project's other unbuilt-candidate Step 0 documents carry.

### A1 — The floor: clears directly

Unlike a pre-Nicene candidate, where A2 (continuity toward a not-yet-formulated creed) governs, this candidate's entire window (1517–1580) sits more than a millennium after Chalcedon (451). A1 — the floor test itself — is the direct, primary test here: does the movement's own confession affirm the completed Nicene-Chalcedonian floor.

**It clears cleanly, on the movement's own confessional text, not by inference.** The Augsburg Confession (1530, Melanchthon's drafting, Luther's own doctrine) Article I explicitly invokes the Nicene decree by name: one divine essence, three persons, condemning the Arians, Manichaeans, and other ancient deniers. Article III affirms Christ's full deity and full humanity, virgin birth, true death and burial, descent, bodily resurrection, ascension, session at the Father's right hand, and return to judge — matching all five of Article 4's commitments above, point for point. Luther's own catechisms are doctrinally consistent throughout. **No floor complication exists here** — unlike most patristic-era candidates, this is not a live question for this movement.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity

Not applicable. This candidate's window opens over a thousand years after Nicaea; there is no continuity-toward-a-future-council question to test.

### A3 — Contemporary movements

Live and real, disclosed rather than smoothed over — this batch was chosen deliberately to include genuine contemporaries in real tension with one another, not isolated candidates. Three disclosed relationships: (1) **The Reformed cities (VI.2, same batch)** — the Eucharistic break at the Marburg Colloquy (1529) between Luther and Zwingli is a real, sharp, in-window doctrinal disagreement between two candidates in this same build run; neither document resolves it for the other, and Doc_01 for each should engage it directly. (2) **The Anabaptist movements (VI.3, same batch)** — Luther's own writings against the "radical" Reformation, and his support (however reluctant) for state suppression, is a real complication for this candidate's own self-presentation, not only the Anabaptists'. (3) **The Tridentine Church (VI.22, same batch)** — the direct doctrinal target of, and respondent to, this movement; Doc_02 for both worlds should expect to cite each other as the primary named rival.

### A4 — Hand-selected inclusion: invoked

Mark selected this candidate directly from the Era VII survey roster, 2026-09-15, on the named criteria stated in §0 above — not a Step 0 verdict reached independently by this document, but the actual mechanism that placed this candidate under consideration.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

Not applicable in the developmental-tradition sense Article 21 tests for pre-Nicene candidates (this movement doesn't argue toward a not-yet-settled orthodoxy) — its real Article-23 rival relationships are covered under A3 above, against genuinely contemporary movements rather than pre-screened ancient heresies.

### Section A conclusion

**Clears Section A directly**, on the movement's own confessional text (the Augsburg Confession), with three disclosed A3 contemporary-movement relationships (Reformed, Anabaptist, Tridentine, all within this same batch) named rather than resolved.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note carried from this project's other unbuilt-candidate Step 0 documents.** Section B is a phase-level process, not a per-world one; the Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: strong

Per the dossier (`.../lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations_Source_Readiness_Dossier.md`), directly host-verified, not title-matched: the Philadelphia Edition of Luther's Works (six PD volumes, early 20th c., Gutenberg and archive.org), the 1520 treatises (*To the Christian Nobility*, *On the Babylonian Captivity of the Church*), *The Bondage of the Will* (Cole 1823), both catechisms, *Table Talk*, Luther's hymns, and — for a genuine second voice — Melanchthon's Augsburg Confession and its Apology (Bente/Dau, Concordia Triglotta 1921). **A methodological caveat this document must carry honestly, unlike the patristic-era Step 0 precedent this project holds itself to:** no word count below is extracted from vendored XML, because nothing is vendored yet — every figure in this section is a rough read from confirmed PD editions' own page counts, not the div2-boundary text-extraction method the Latin Apologists' own Step 0 uses. On that basis: this is clearly one of the largest single-founder corpora this project could build, on the order of the merged Latin Apologists' 1.19M words once fully vendored, though that comparison is qualitative, not measured.

### B2 — Ecology: strong, with real disclosure obligations

Covers doctrine (Augsburg Confession, catechisms), pastoral formation (catechisms, sermons), polemic (the 1520 treatises, *Bondage of the Will*), and informal/personal voice (*Table Talk*, hymns) — a genuinely broad register. **Real, disclosable complications, named rather than smoothed over:** Luther's late-life antisemitic writings (*On the Jews and Their Lies*, 1543); *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of Peasants* (1525), written amid the Peasants' War; the Marburg break with the Reformed (§2 A3 above). None of these threaten the doctrinal-floor clearance; all are within-tradition disclosure obligations of the same kind the Latin Apologists' Step 0 already models for its own authors' complications.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds (all pre-451 CE):** no figure or source overlap possible — over a thousand years separate this candidate's window from every one of them. **Versus this same batch's other five candidates:** see §2 A3 for the Reformed, Anabaptist, and Tridentine relationships; no figure overlap with the Society of Jesus (VI.11, founded 1540, a full generation after Luther's own death in 1546, no direct contact) or Lollardy (V.5, England, 1380–1520, a different country and a movement that had already gone underground by the time Luther began).

### B4 — Audiences

The most broadly recognizable figure of the whole batch — strong pull for anyone approaching Reformation history from any direction, plus a distinct draw for participants specifically interested in the doctrine-of-grace argument this project's own thin Pelagianism candidate (I.19, Era 2) could never carry on its own.

### B5 — Scale

Saxony, then Northern Europe — the broadest immediate geographic reach of any candidate in this batch at the close of its own window (1580), reflecting Lutheranism's real spread across German and Scandinavian territories within a single generation.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A directly on its own confessional text; clears Sourcing strongly, on directly host-verified PD editions across every major genre (doctrine, catechesis, polemic, personal voice); clears Ecology with three real, disclosed complications (antisemitic writings, the Peasants' War tract, the Marburg break) rather than a laundered account; no built-world overlap; strong, recognizable audience pull; broadest immediate geographic scale in the batch.

**Tier 1 — Strong seed, no binding condition remaining beyond independent review itself.** Unlike the Latin Apologists' or Greek Apologists' own Step 0 documents, this candidate carries no open dating dispute or attribution question comparable to Minucius Felix's or Commodian's — Luther's and Melanchthon's own dates, authorship, and doctrine are not seriously contested history.
