## 2. Section 2 — Preliminary Forces Identification

*This section records Doc_01 §7 as it was made — the Step 1 orientation that governed Doc_02's scope — not a retrospective summary of what was later found. **What generated this world** (Doc_01 §7, **[Documented]**): "an Augustinian friar and university theology professor's own developing conviction — argued first over the specific, bounded question of indulgence sales, then rapidly generalized — that the practice of the late-medieval Western church had come to rest on an authority (the papacy's own claimed power to bind and loose, extended into the sale of indulgences) that Scripture itself does not support." Doc_01 read the 1517 letter to Albrecht of Mainz as showing "a bounded, respectful protest to a specific archbishop about a specific abuse, not yet a movement," with the escalation to institutional break "evidenced directly in the 1520 treatises' own widening scope." Coded at Doc_04 §1 as **[F-gen]**.

**What larger world it was embedded in** (Doc_01 §7, **[Documented]**): "late-medieval Western European Christendom under a single, if already contested, papal authority, with the Holy Roman Empire as its immediate political frame — an empire of numerous semi-autonomous princes and cities whose own political fragmentation is part of why a territorial, prince-by-prince settlement (rather than a single empire-wide resolution) became this world's own eventual legal shape." Coded **[F-emb]**.

**What it was responding to at its origin** (Doc_01 §7, **[Widely Accepted]**): "the practical and pastoral problem, argued directly in the vendored texts, of a laity taught to rely on indulgences, private confession to a priest, and the church's own sacramental system for assurance of salvation, rather than on direct trust in Scripture's own promise" — which *Christian Liberty* and the *Babylonian Captivity* "both argue… is a pastoral failure with real consequences for ordinary believers' own spiritual confidence, not only an abstract doctrinal dispute." Coded **[F-resp]**.

**What it was refusing** (Doc_01 §7): "most directly and by name, the papacy's own claimed authority to define doctrine and administer the sacraments as a matter that binds the conscience apart from Scripture (*The Papacy at Rome*; the Babylonian Captivity's reduction of the sacraments) — refused in writing, addressed to named readers" **[Documented]**; and "a second, later refusal, real and disclosed rather than smoothed into a single undifferentiated 'Reformation': the Eucharistic theology of the Reformed tradition, refused directly at the Marburg Colloquy (1529)" — for which Doc_01 found "Marburg" and "Zwingli" return zero hits across the six files then vendored, and rested the refusal's *content* on AC Article X alone. Coded **[F-pap]** and **[F-ref]**.
