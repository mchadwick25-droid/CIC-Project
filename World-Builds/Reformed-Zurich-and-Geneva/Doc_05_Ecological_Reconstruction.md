## 0. Method and the Governing Disciplines

**Purpose.** Step 4 asked what organizes this world; Step 5 asks what it was like to be formed inside it. Five disciplines govern every lens below.

1. **The gravity spine (from Doc_04).** Three Primary gravities organize this ecology: **G1 Sovereignty of God/Predestination and Election**, **G2 Spiritual Presence and the Rejection of Corporeal/Sacrificial Mediation**, and **G3 Scripture as Sole and Sufficient Authority, Enacted Through Disputation and Catechesis**. One Supporting gravity, **G4 Consistorial Church Discipline**, is confirmed only for the Geneva strand's own most distinctive institutional detail — its general doctrine is cross-strand attested, but its formation-defining specifics are not. Two Tensional gravities are held, not resolved: **T1 Council-Led Civic Authority (Zurich) vs. Consistorial Independence (Geneva)**, permanent within this world's own window per Doc_01 §4's explicit finding; and **T2 Zwingli's Own "Remembrance" Reading of the Supper vs. the Negotiated Spiritual-Presence Consensus**, resolved (in text, not necessarily in every believer's own experience) by 1549. Antistes and "Reformation" as a term were tested and did not advance (Doc_04 §2).

2. **This world is genuinely two-stranded, not strand-singular (Doc_01 §4/§5, confirmed by the project lead).** Unlike a strand-singular world, no lens below may write "this world's own formation" as though it were one undifferentiated population. Zurich (Zwingli, succeeded by Bullinger) and Geneva (Calvin, succeeded by Beza) are two real communities with two real formation logics, bridged doctrinally but not institutionally by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549). Every lens states, where it matters, which strand its material comes from, and does not extend a Geneva-specific finding to Zurich or vice versa without direct evidence (the same discipline Doc_04 §5's genuine Article 21 cross-strand testing already established).

3. **The Author-Gravity asymmetry (Doc_01 §8 item 1; Doc_02 §2/§6; Doc_04 §0) — active throughout, not a footnote.** Calvin's own vendored corpus is roughly four times Zwingli's by word count, with no vendored lay or institutional counterweight for either city. Doc_04 already found this asymmetry does not, on its own, make the confirmed Primary gravities Geneva-only artifacts — each has independent Zurich-strand grounding — but the *texture and detail* below is unavoidably richer for Geneva's Calvin than for Zurich's Zwingli/Bullinger, and every lens names this rather than papering over it with invented symmetry.

4. **The daily-life/institutional-practice absence (Doc_02 §5/§6, restated at Doc_04 §3.4/§7) — a second, distinct kind of thinness, named rather than filled.** The Ecclesiastical Ordinances, the Genevan Psalter, and Geneva's own consistory registers — the specific record the census selected this world for — remain unvendored. This is not a stratum split (there is no non-literate majority this corpus specifically excludes, unlike Donatism's rural Numidian believers) and not a hostile-transmission asymmetry (unlike Donatism's Optatus/Augustine mediation) — it is a **genre asymmetry**: this corpus holds confession, catechism, systematic theology, and contemporary disputation record in depth, and almost nothing of institutional daily practice. Every lens below marks plainly where it is reconstructing from doctrine about practice rather than from practice itself.

5. **Writing-From-Inside (Article 23), quarantined.** Inhabited passages render each strand's own logic in its own vocabulary — *Leutpriester* and *Antistes*, the Prophezei, the Consistory, *Deo laudes* has no equivalent here but "remembrance" and "sign and the thing signified" do — holding tensions as this world held them, no analytical-distance markers inside the inhabited layer. All confidence, Author-Gravity, and construction notes live in bracketed **[Construction note: …]** blocks outside the inhabited prose. Composite typical-practice narration is marked **[Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction]**; **no Tier 5 (free invention) appears anywhere in this document** (Article 19).

6. **Forces integrated into every lens (Forces Framework V1.1 Step 5).** The two independent 1519/1526–1536 origins, the Wars of Kappel, the Counter-Reformation's sustained pressure, and the Consensus Tigurinus's own bridging act are not background; they are constitutive of how this ecology felt from within. The Human, Community, and Boundary lenses in particular are written *through* the forces, per the Forces Framework's own naming of those three lenses specifically.

## 1. Human Ecology

**Who inhabited this world.** Two reforming pastors who never met: Ulrich Zwingli, *Leutpriester* at Zurich's Grossmünster from 1519, killed in battle at Kappel in 1531; and John Calvin, arriving at Geneva in 1536, exiled to Strasbourg 1538–1541, recalled to consolidate the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances. Each had a named successor who outlived him by decades and did the work of stabilizing what the founder began: Heinrich Bullinger, Zurich's *Antistes* for forty-four years (1531–1575); Theodore Beza, Calvin's own successor at Geneva (unvendored, Doc_02 §6, a genuine acquisition gap rather than a marginalized-voice case). Beneath them, at Geneva specifically, a body the vendored corpus can name in doctrine but not in daily operation: the Company of Pastors and a Consistory of pastors and lay elders — governors "selected from the people to unite with the bishops in pronouncing censures and exercising discipline," in Calvin's own words (Institutes IV.3.8). At Zurich, the corresponding body is the city council itself, acting on the outcome of public clerical disputation — no distinct lay-inclusive disciplinary court is attested there in this world's own vendored record (Doc_04 §3.4). And, at the edge of both cities, voices this corpus cannot recover in their own words: Marie Dentière, prosecuted through her own printer for her 1539 anonymous defense of women's right to speak on scripture; and the ordinary citizen of either city, whose own experience of a Sunday service or a consistory summons survives nowhere in what is vendored here.

*[Inhabited — Zurich, cross-strand grounded]* Scripture read continuously, book by book, not cut into a fixed year's own lectionary — that was the discipline Zwingli set for himself at the Grossmünster from his first January, and it is the discipline that made every later argument possible: whatever the text does not say, the church may not require.

*[Inhabited — Geneva]* A church is not rightly ordered by doctrine alone. Someone must watch how the doctrine is lived — not the magistrate, whose sword is for the body, but pastors and elders together, whose office is to unite in pronouncing censure where conscience and conduct diverge from what has been professed.

**Forces.** Two independent triggers, not one shared cause, split this human ecology at its very root: Zwingli's own Erasmian-humanist scholarship, catalyzed at Zurich by the 1522 Sausage Affair; and, years later and with no contact between the two, Geneva's own 1526 alliance with Bern and 1536 break from Savoy, which opened the civic space Calvin's arrival then filled. The two populations were never merged; they were bridged, in 1549, by a document neither founder lived to see negotiated in this form together (Zwingli was thirteen years dead; Calvin's own Geneva ministry had matured for a decade).

[Construction note: Zwingli's 1519 start, Calvin's 1536 arrival and 1541 recall, and Bullinger's 1531–1575 pastorate are **Documented** (Doc_01 §1, §2). The Consistory's general doctrine is **Documented**, Confidence A, directly in Calvin's own words (Institutes IV.3.8, independently re-verified at Doc_04 Round 1/2 review); Geneva's own specific institutional composition and daily operation remain Confidence E, unvendored (Registry row 14). Zurich's own comparable disciplinary mechanism, to the extent this corpus evidences one, runs through the council directly — no vendored text names a distinct Zurich consistorial body (Doc_04 §3.4/§5). Beza and Dentière are both named absences per Article 20 (Doc_02 §6): Beza a straightforward acquisition gap, Dentière a genuine marginalized-voice case — her own argument is not reconstructed here beyond the bare fact of her having written and been prosecuted for it, per Doc_02's own bounded-reconstruction discipline. **Ordinary citizens' own interior experience of either city's own religious life is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated here** — a genre-asymmetry absence (§0 item 4), not a stratum or transmission absence.]

## 2. Community Ecology

**Belonging as citizenship, not re-entry.** Unlike a world defined by a boundary-crossing rite of admission, this world's own communities were coextensive with the city itself — to be a citizen of Reformed Zurich or Reformed Geneva was, in the ordinary case, already to belong to its church. There was no rebaptism here, and pointedly so: this world's own confessional core rejects the specific Anabaptist claim that a scripturally consistent reform requires re-baptizing believing adults, even though that claim arose from within Zwingli's own circle. What made a citizen a formed member was continuous, repeated instruction — the Prophezei's public exposition at Zurich, the fixed catechism at Geneva — not a single threshold crossed once.

*[Inhabited — the Zurich/Anabaptist boundary, held from inside]* Some among us read the same Scripture and heard the same doctrine of a church gathered by profession, not born into by baptism as an infant — and where we stopped, they went further, and would have unmade the very unity of city and church that reform was built to strengthen, not divide. We do not deny they read Scripture; we deny they read it whole.

**Forces.** The 21 January 1525 first believer's baptisms, at Felix Manz's own house in Zurich — Conrad Grebel baptizing Georg Blaurock, who then baptized the others present, including Manz — split this world's own founding community from within, only two years after the First Zurich Disputation had settled its founding doctrine. Grebel and Manz were both formerly of Zwingli's own reform circle (Doc_01 §7, correcting the Source Readiness Dossier's and this world's own Step 0's shared "no figure overlap" error); this is an internal schism from this world's own Zurich strand, not an unrelated contemporary movement.

[Construction note: the Grebel/Blaurock/Manz sequence and its Zwingli-circle origin are **Documented** (Doc_01 §7, discharged at Doc_02 §2). The absence of a rebaptism practice in this world's own confessional identity is **Documented** by direct contrast with the confirmed presence of the doctrine in the adjacent, not-yet-built Anabaptist world (VI.3, per Doc_01 §7). **What continuous catechetical membership actually felt like to an ordinary citizen — as opposed to what it was doctrinally supposed to accomplish — is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated here** (§0 item 4).]

## 3. Worship Ecology

**A worship divided at its own outset, and the Supper doctrine both strands eventually agreed on.** Zurich's own worship, under Zwingli's direct instruction, suppressed instrumental music and congregational singing as without scriptural warrant — by most accounts the Grossmünster's own organ fell silent in the mid-1520s (sources vary on the exact year; not vendored; Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence, Doc_01 §5). Geneva's worship, by contrast, built congregational singing into the very center of its own devotional life through the metrical Genevan Psalter (Marot and Beza, complete 1562, not itself vendored — Registry row 15). This is T1's own sibling divergence in the Practice dimension (Doc_01 §5), though not itself classified Tensional at Doc_04 since Doc_01 §4 also finds later, partial convergence across the wider Swiss Reformed churches, unconfirmed against a primary source.

What both strands' own worship shared, and eventually stated jointly, is this world's own defining Eucharistic conviction. Zwingli's own founding statement already refused the Mass's sacrificial character: "the mass is not a sacrifice, but is a remembrance of the sacrifice and assurance of the salvation which Christ has given us" (Sixty-Seven Articles, Art. XVIII, 1523). Calvin's own Institutes devotes an extensive, repeated polemic to the same refusal. And in 1549, both strands' own pastors put their names to a single formula neither had used alone before: "though we distinguish, as we ought, between the signs and the things signified, yet we do not disjoin the reality from the signs" (Consensus Tigurinus, 9th Head of Agreement).

*[Inhabited — Zurich, pre-1549]* The bread is bread, and the body is in heaven; what happens at this table is remembrance and thanksgiving, not another sacrifice repeated, and any preacher who tells you Christ's own flesh is chewed by the mouth has not read what Scripture actually says of him.

*[Inhabited — the Consensus, post-1549, both strands' own agreed language]* We do not divide the sign from the thing it signifies, as though bread were merely bread and nothing more were given; but neither do we say the body itself descends into it. Christ's own promise, received in faith, is truly given here — not carnally, but by the Spirit's own power, to those who come believing.

**Forces.** Persecution does not touch this world's own worship the way it touches a martyr-cult world; the sharpest external force on worship here was doctrinal confrontation, not violence — the Marburg Colloquy (1–4 October 1529), where Zwingli and Oecolampadius stood against Luther and Melanchthon over exactly this question, with Calvin not yet a public figure in it at all (then about twenty, a law student; Geneva not yet reformed). The break at Marburg is what made the 1549 Consensus necessary twenty years later: two strands of the same wider Reformed movement needed their own jointly agreed answer to the same question Marburg had left unresolved between the Reformed and the Lutheran side.

[Construction note: Article XVIII is **Documented**, verbatim, independently re-verified against `zwingli_selected-works_jackson1901.txt` lines 4564–4568 (Doc_04 §3.2). The Consensus's own 9th Head is **Documented**, verbatim, independently re-verified against `calvin-zurich-pastors_consensus-tigurinus-mutual-consent-sacraments_beveridge1844.txt` (Doc_03 Round 2 review; Doc_04 §3.2). Marburg's own participants and Calvin's own absence are **Documented** (Doc_01 §6). The organ-silencing date is **Dominant Modern Reconstruction**, not vendor-verified (Doc_01 §5). The Consensus's own status as "genuine theological synthesis" versus "diplomatically ambiguous formula" is the live [CT] contest named at Doc_03 §4 and carried forward, tagged, not adjudicated here (Doc_04 §3.2's own Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check). **What either congregation actually heard or felt, singing or not singing, at an ordinary Sunday service, is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated beyond what the confessional texts themselves prescribe** (§0 item 4).]

## 4. Organizational (Authority) Ecology

**A council in one city, a Consistory in the other — and this world's own permanent, unconverged tension.** Zurich's reform was enacted and sustained, throughout the whole 1519–1650 window, by the city council acting on the outcome of public clerical disputation. Geneva's 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances created something structurally different: a Consistory Calvin fought to keep independent of civil-council control, a fight "substantially resolved in [his] favor" only by 1555 (Doc_01 §2, the Perrinist crisis). Doc_01 §4 states this plainly, and this document does not soften it: this divergence "did **not** converge within the window on Zurich's own side... a fact about this world's own descendants, not about Zurich and Geneva as they actually stood within this window." This is **T1**.

*[Inhabited — Geneva, the Perrinist crisis]* The magistrate governs bodies and property; he does not sit in judgment on whether a soul may come to the Lord's own table. When the council's own men tried to claim that authority for themselves, the Consistory did not yield it — not because the pastors sought power for its own sake, but because a discipline answerable to the city's own shifting politics is no discipline at all.

*[Inhabited — Zurich]* Here the council itself is Christ's own instrument for reform, tested and confirmed at open disputation before the whole city — there is no second court to appeal to, and none is wanted; the council that ratified the Sixty-Seven Articles is the same council that governs what follows from them.

**Forces.** Geneva's own recurring internal factional resistance — the Perrinist faction specifically — is this tension's own most concentrated ongoing-internal force: a genuine, named political party inside Geneva itself contesting the Consistory's own claimed independence, not an external threat. Zurich's own side of this tension faced no comparable internal contest in this world's own vendored record; its own authority structure simply persisted unchallenged from within.

[Construction note: Zurich's own council-led governance and Geneva's own 1541 Ordinances/1555 Perrinist resolution are **Documented** (Doc_01 §2, §4, §5). T1 is **Documented** per pole, and its own "permanent within-window non-convergence" characterization is Doc_01's own explicit finding, not this document's synthesis (Doc_04 §3.5). **The Consistory's own actual weekly case-by-case operation — what discipline looked like to the person summoned before it — is Confidence E, unvendored, and is not narrated here beyond Calvin's own general doctrinal statement of the office** (§0 item 4; Doc_04 §3.4).]

## 5. Ministry and Formation Ecology *(the central lens)*

**Formation here runs on three channels of one shared method, plus one strand-specific institutional elaboration.** G3's scriptural-sole-authority conviction is the shared method that generates both G1 (the sovereignty doctrine, derived from Scripture in both Zwingli's and Calvin's own hands) and G2 (the rejection of corporeal presence, argued on the same scriptural-literalist grounds against "carnal" misreadings). G4 is Geneva's own additional institutional elaboration of the same conviction — that scriptural discipline requires a standing body to enforce it directly — not a shared Zurich/Geneva channel. T1 and T2 are held here as the tensions this world actually lived, not resolved into one consistent position.

- **Scripture as the shared method.** *[Inhabited — Zurich]* Whatever the text will not yield, the church may not teach, and whatever it does yield, no council or pope may set aside — this is the whole of the founding case, argued in public, before the city, not asserted from a chair. *[Inhabited — Geneva]* The same conviction, worked out at length rather than argued at a single disputation: doctrine is built book by book, article by article, until the whole shape of what Scripture actually teaches about God, Christ, and the church stands built up rather than merely defended.
- **Sovereignty of God as formation ground.** *[Inhabited — cross-strand, with a named divergence in register]* Salvation rests on God's own free election, not on anything foreseen in the one elected — "God's election, predestination or marking out, calling, beatifies," as Zurich's own founder put it working through Paul's own Romans; and Geneva's own systematic elaboration carries the same conviction, at greater length and with sharper technical precision, into a full account of how election, calling, and glorification cohere. Zurich's own mature confession holds the same ground but turns it toward comfort rather than system: "Let... Christ be the mirror in which we behold our predestination" — look to Christ, not behind him, for assurance.
- **Rejection of corporeal mediation as boundary and comfort together.** Carried in §3 above: the conviction that shapes both what this world refuses (a repeated sacrifice, a localized body) and what it offers instead (a real, spiritual feeding, received by faith).
- **Consistorial discipline as Geneva's own further step — not a shared channel.** *[Inhabited — Geneva only]* Doctrine rightly taught and Scripture rightly preached still leave open whether a professed believer's own daily conduct answers to what has been professed; someone must ask that question directly, and answer for it before the whole body of pastors and elders together.

**Forces.** This formation ecology developed under two forces this world's own record names directly: the recurring civic-political pressure of the Swiss Confederacy's own cantonal structure, which made reform a city-by-city decision requiring the actual mechanism of public disputation (G3's own enactment) rather than a single princely settlement; and Geneva's own recurring internal disorder and refugee inflow, which is what the Consistory's own weekly discipline (G4) was built specifically to address (Doc_01 §6).

[Construction note: G1's core doctrine **Documented** cross-strand, systematized form Calvin/Beza-concentrated (Doc_04 §3.1); G2 **Documented** (§3.2); G3 **Documented**, the strongest cross-strand candidate in this document, no significant divergence (§3.3); G4 **Documented** on the general doctrine, Confidence E on Geneva's own specific operation, classified Supporting and strand-scope-qualified (§3.4). T1 and T2 are **Documented** per-pole and held as genuine Tensional gravities (§3.5). **The interior experience of holding these convictions together, for the ordinary Zurich or Geneva citizen whose own words do not survive, is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated** — the genre-asymmetry absence named throughout this document (§0 item 4).]

## 6. World-Specific Lenses

This world's own character calls for four further lenses beyond the standard five.

### 6.1 Strand Ecology *(this world's own most structurally central lens)*

Where a strand-singular world needs no such lens, this world's entire architecture rests on two confirmed strands bridged, not merged. Zurich: German-speaking, council-governed, formed by Zwingli's six combative reforming years (1519–1525) and stabilized by Bullinger's own forty-four-year pastorate; its own worship suppressed song, its own reform proceeded by public disputation. Geneva: French-speaking, substantially populated by religious refugees, formed by Calvin's own systematic theology and consolidated by a Consistory answerable to pastors and elders, not the council alone; its own worship built congregational singing into its own devotional center. The Consensus Tigurinus (1549) is this lens's own central artifact — the one document literally co-authored across the strand boundary, mediated by Bullinger's Zurich specifically, since Zwingli and Calvin themselves never met and Calvin's own Geneva ministry did not begin in earnest until five years after Zwingli's death.

*[Inhabited — the bridge itself, in the Consensus's own two signatory voices]* "The Ministers of the Church of Zurich" and "John Calvin, Minister of the Church of Geneva" put their names to one page together — not because their two cities had become one, but because each recognized in the other's own confession the same faith, differently arrived at.

**Forces.** The two strands' own independent origins — five years apart, with no contact between Zwingli's own Erasmian-humanist Zurich reform and Geneva's own political break from Savoy — is real separation evidence Doc_01 §4 names directly as the single strongest fact cutting against reading this as one undifferentiated ecology; the Consensus Tigurinus is the ongoing-internal force that answers it without erasing it.

[Construction note: the two independent origins and the Consensus's own bridging role are **Documented** (Doc_01 §4, §5). The CT title-page quotation is **Documented**, verbatim (Doc_02 §2). This document does not treat the Consensus as evidence the two cities' own authority structures converged — Doc_01 §4/§5 and this document's own §4 above hold that they explicitly did not, within the window.]

### 6.2 Boundary Ecology — A Triple Refusal

This world knew itself through refusal in three directions at once, a richer boundary structure than a world defined against a single rival. Against Rome: the Mass's own sacrificial character and image veneration, refused from Zurich's own founding statement onward (Art. XVIII). Against Wittenberg: once both strands' own positions had matured, any claim that Christ's body is locally or corporeally present in the bread and wine — the specific break named at Marburg (1529), a rupture within the wider Protestant movement itself, not against Rome. Against the Anabaptists: infant baptism's own legitimacy and the magistrate's own standing to enforce religious uniformity, refused by both Zwingli and Calvin against a movement that arose, at Zurich specifically, from within Zwingli's own circle — the sharpest of the three refusals, since it answers a challenge from inside rather than outside.

*[Inhabited]* Rome would have us trust a priest's own repeated offering; Wittenberg would have us trust that Christ's body travels with the bread itself; and those who were once beside us at Zurich would have us tear the church in two, gathering only the visibly worthy and unmaking the city's own single body. Against each, the same scriptural discipline answers differently, because each errs differently.

**Forces.** The Counter-Reformation is this world's own sustained external pressure across the whole window (Doc_01 §2), sharpest, though outside this document's own scope, at Servetus's 1553 execution (a separate registered case, VI.14) and, at the window's far end, in the Jesuit presence at Fribourg from 1580 directly facing Reformed Bern and Geneva (Doc_01 §7, not independently assessed this pass).

[Construction note: the Rome and Wittenberg refusals are **Documented** per §3 above. The Anabaptist refusal and its own Zwingli-circle origin are **Documented** (Doc_01 §7, Doc_02 §2). No comparably rich vendored polemic against the Anabaptists' own specific theological arguments has been independently assessed this pass beyond the general refusal already stated in Doc_01/Doc_02 — Zwingli's own 1527 *Refutation of the Tricks of the Baptists* (Registry row 7, newly surfaced at Doc_04) is the direct primary-source candidate for this, and a closer reading of it beyond the election material already drawn out at Doc_04 §3.1 remains a standing open item (Doc_04 §7).]

### 6.3 Civic-Political Ecology

This world's reform proceeded through republican city-councils, not princely territorial churches — the single sharpest structural contrast with the sibling Lutheran Wittenberg world (Doc_01 §1). The Swiss Confederacy's own decentralized cantonal structure made reform a city-by-city political decision, and meant Reformed and Catholic cantons went to war directly over it: the First War of Kappel (1529, resolved without battle) and the Second War of Kappel (1531), in which Zwingli himself was killed. Geneva's own civic situation ran differently: a 1526 alliance with Bern and Fribourg, a 1536 break from Savoy and the Prince-Bishop, and a recurring dependence on Bernese support that both stressed and internationalized the city — Geneva became a print and refugee center over this window, sheltering French Huguenots and, under Mary I, English Marian exiles.

*[Inhabited — Zurich, after Kappel]* Our own pastor was killed in that same fighting — reform here was never a private conviction sheltered from the world's own violence; it was a city's own public stand, paid for in the same coin as any other war the Confederacy has fought.

**Forces.** The Wars of Kappel are this lens's own defining external-ongoing force for Zurich; Geneva's own refugee inflow and Bernese dependence are its counterpart there — both are civic-political facts a purely doctrinal account of this world would miss entirely.

[Construction note: the Wars of Kappel and Zwingli's own death are **Documented** (Doc_01 §2). Geneva's own 1526/1536 political sequence and refugee status are **Documented** (Doc_01 §2). This lens draws on Doc_01's own material directly; no new vendored-text verification was performed for this section specifically.]

### 6.4 Interpretive and Confessional Ecology

Unlike a world where doctrine is comparatively thin, this world's own corpus is confessionally dense: the Sixty-Seven Articles (1523), the Second Helvetic Confession (1566), the Geneva Catechism, and the Heidelberg Catechism (1563, a third, non-Zurich/non-Geneva Reformed voice from the German wing, Doc_01 §2) together make confession-writing itself one of this world's own most distinctive formation products — a plurality of catechetical voices worth naming rather than treating as one uniform genre (Doc_03 Cluster 5). The Consensus Tigurinus belongs here too, as this world's own most direct evidence that confession-writing could bridge, not merely state, doctrine.

*[Inhabited]* A confession is not written to say something new; it is written so that what is already believed can be tested, taught, and handed to the next generation without drift — which is why Zurich's own mature statement and Geneva's own catechism, forty years and a language apart, can still be read as one faith stated twice.

**Forces.** No external force but the internal need for cross-generational transmission, once each city's own founding generation began to age — the Second Helvetic Confession (1566) is Bullinger's own mature statement, written decades after Zwingli's death, precisely because a founding generation's oral and disputational method needed a fixed text to outlive it.

[Construction note: all four confessional/catechetical texts named are **Documented**, vendored (Registry rows 4, 6, 9, 10). Whether the Heidelberg Catechism's own predestination treatment is as developed as Calvin's is also **Contested**, not independently checked against the vendored text this pass (Doc_02 §8).]

## 7. Temporal Configuration — Two Origins, One Bridge, No Internal Ending

Unlike a world organized around a single founding hinge, this ecology begins twice, independently, and closes not by fracture but by the census's own administrative convention.

1. **Initiating (1519–1536), two independent origins.** Zwingli's 1519 preaching start and the 1522 Sausage Affair generate Zurich's own reform; Geneva's 1526 alliance with Bern, 1536 break from Savoy, and Calvin's own arrival the same year generate Geneva's, five years after Zwingli's death and with no contact between the two triggers.
2. **Ongoing (1536–1618ish), the two strands' own bridged coexistence.** The Consensus Tigurinus (1549) formally bridges Zurich and Geneva without merging their own distinct authority structures (T1 remains permanently unconverged). The Bolsec controversy (1551) and Perrinist crisis (resolved 1555) test G1 and G4 respectively from within; the Second Helvetic Confession (1566) and Heidelberg Catechism (1563) later express the movement's shared confessional core in fixed, transmissible form.
3. **Ending/Transforming — none internal to this window is attested (Doc_01 §6, Cell 3A/3B).** The Synod of Dort (1618–19) sharpens this world's own predestination doctrine at the international level (Doc_01 §7's confirmed transmission scope) rather than closing anything; the window's own 1650 close is the census's administrative continues-cap for a still-living tradition, not a historical rupture. This world's own gravities continue, unbroken, into the four descendant traditions Doc_01 §7 names — the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritanism.

[Construction note: this three-phase structure is this document's own organizing device, built directly from Doc_01 §6's own six-cell sketch and Doc_04's own per-gravity Persistence tests, not an independently attested periodization in the vendored scholarship.]

## 8. Proportionality Assessment

Two distinct asymmetries run through this ecology, and they must be held separately rather than merged into one undifferentiated "thinness" finding. The first is the **Author-Gravity volume asymmetry** between the two strands: Calvin's own vendored corpus is roughly four times Zwingli's, with Bullinger's own single confession thinner still relative to his real historical centrality as the Zurich/Geneva bridge (Doc_02 §2). The second, entirely separate axis is the **genre asymmetry** between doctrinal/confessional content (richly vendored for both strands) and institutional daily-practice content (almost entirely absent for either): no Ecclesiastical Ordinances, no Genevan Psalter, no consistory registers, no material-culture source of any kind (Doc_02 §5). This second axis does not track the strand division at all — it is a gap in what survives about *practice*, for both cities alike, not a gap favoring one city's own doctrine over the other's. This world's probable ecology — real, differentiated congregational life in two cities, week by week, generation by generation — is not reachable from what this corpus currently holds beyond what the doctrinal and confessional texts themselves prescribe it should have looked like.

## 10. Inhabited Synthesis — What Formation Was Like in This World

*[Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction; composite typical-practice narration; no Tier 5. Written from inside each strand's own logic in turn (Article 23). Per-element traceability: "the mass is not a sacrifice, but is a remembrance" → Zwingli, Sixty-Seven Articles Art. No element is invented beyond these attested sources.]*

To be formed at Zurich was to sit under a pastor who read Scripture straight through, week by week, and to test every inherited practice against it in public, before the whole city, rather than take it on the church's own prior word. What that reading yielded was refused as much as affirmed: no repeated sacrifice at the altar, no image bowed to, and — once the question was pressed at Marburg — no claim that Christ's own body could be chewed by the mouth or located in the bread. And when some among that same circle read the same Scripture and concluded the church itself must be gathered anew, believer by professing believer, apart from the city's whole body, this world refused that too, and refused it from the inside, since the men who first baptized each other at Manz's house had themselves once stood with Zwingli.

To be formed at Geneva, a generation later, was to sit under a longer and more systematic instruction — doctrine built book upon book until the whole shape of it stood complete — and to have one's own daily conduct answerable, not only to that doctrine's own persuasion, but, in principle, to a body of pastors and elders charged with pronouncing censure directly. The same conviction that shaped Zurich's own refusals shaped Geneva's own doctrine of election: that salvation rests on God's free choice and nothing foreseen in the one chosen, held out not as a threat but, in the confession's own comforting cadence, as the ground of a settled life.

And when Zurich's own senior pastors and Geneva's own foremost theologian put their names to one page in 1549 — "the Ministers of the Church of Zurich" beside "John Calvin, Minister of the Church of Geneva" — they were not declaring two cities one. They were declaring that two reforms, begun independently and never in personal contact between their own founders, had arrived, each by its own path, at language neither could disown: a Supper in which the sign and the thing signified are not divided, though never simply the same — the reality truly given, and given only to those who receive it believing, not to the elements themselves.

*[Named absence, not a filled silence: what any of this actually felt like to the citizen of either city who was not a pastor, an elder, or a theologian — the ordinary weight of a consistory summons, the ordinary experience of a service sung or unsung, the daily texture the census itself selected this world to recover — the surviving evidence, confessionally rich and institutionally almost silent, does not let us say, and it is not narrated here. That the record this world's own tradition chose to preserve and transmit is doctrinally authoritative rather than institutionally documentary (Doc_02 §6) is itself part of the honest account of this world, per Article 20 and the genre-asymmetry constraint named at §0 item 4 above.]*
