## Section 2 — Tier Categories, Re-Confirmed Against the Current Governing Text

Re-read directly from the Construction Framework V7.4 text (Part V) for this document, rather than assumed from a remembered list.

- **Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative.** Direct textual attestation within or close to the world's horizon; named author with identifiable social location; datable with reasonable confidence; a historically credible narrative claim, with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective and genre.
- **Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material.** Transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory; individual attribution may be uncertain, but the tradition itself is treated as authentic.
- **Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition.** Attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition rather than direct documentation; hagiographic narrative is the specific type named within this tier.
- **Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** A composite reconstruction of typical practice, not about one named individual, every element traceable to attested evidence; reconstruction, not generation.
- **No Tier 5.** Generated or illustrative narrative — not traceable to this world's own evidence — is not permitted under any circumstance.

## Section 6 — Candidates Considered, Not Built This Pass

Recorded here so they are not lost, rather than silently dropped.

**Zwingli's death at Kappel (1531) is no longer listed here — corrected at Round 1 review.** An earlier draft of this document claimed no vendored source gives a narrative account of Zwingli's death, when in fact `zwingli_latin-works-correspondence-vol1_jackson1912.txt` (Source_Registry.md row 8, already Native and licensed) contains, embedded within it, Oswald Myconius's own "Original Life of Zwingli," whose Section XII narrates Zwingli's own death at the Second Battle of Kappel in first-person, near-eyewitness detail. This world's build has now been caught on this specific failure mode once before — Donatism's own Doc_09 §9 item 5 records a comparable false-absence claim for Tyconius's condemnation, corrected there by building a ninth story chunk — and is corrected here the same way: built as `Story-Chunks/rzgstory003_myconius-account-of-zwinglis-death.md` (Section 3 above), rather than left as an uncorrected absence.

- **The Second Zurich Disputation (October 1523) and the Anabaptist party's own emergence within it.** `zwingli_selected-works_jackson1901.txt`, lines 5047–5099, gives a real, specific narrative of this event — the debate's own subject (images and the sacraments), the Anabaptist party's own origin in a private inquiry meeting held in Zwingli's own presence, and Zwingli's own recorded distress at his followers' precipitance. **Not built as a story chunk this pass**, for a reason this world's own build has already named as a recurring risk (Doc_03 Round 1; Donatism's own Doc_09 §9 item 5): this passage is Jackson's own 1901 editorial introduction to Zwingli's *Refutation of the Tricks of the Baptists*, not a primary eyewitness account comparable to Hegenwald's — no named primary witness, in this world's own vendored corpus, narrates the Second Disputation the way Hegenwald narrates the First. Treating Jackson's own editorial narrative as though it carried Tier 1 primary-source weight would repeat the translator/editor-apparatus-mistaken-for-primary-content failure mode this project has caught before. This material remains available as background characterization (already partially reflected in Doc_05 §2's own account of the Anabaptist schism), but is not promoted to a story chunk without a primary eyewitness source to ground it.
- **Marburg Colloquy (1529).** `zwingli_latin-works-correspondence-vol1_jackson1912.txt` (Source_Registry.md row 8 — the same volume rzgstory003 draws from) mentions Zwingli's own journey to Marburg once, in passing, within Myconius's biography (line 1343: "He made also the long journey to Marburg, to the colloquy with Luther and Melanchthon upon the Lord's Supper. He feared nothing there, for the place was suitable"). This is real and Native, but too thin to support a story chunk on its own — it gives no narrative detail of the colloquy's own proceedings, only the bare fact of the journey and Myconius's own brief editorial comment on Zwingli's confidence. Named here as a disclosed, thin lead rather than a genuine absence, distinct from Bolsec and the Perrinist crisis below, for which no vendored mention at all — thin or otherwise — was found on this pass.
- **The Bolsec controversy (1551) and the Perrinist crisis (resolved 1555).** Both are Documented facts (Doc_01 §2; Doc_04 §3.1, §3.5) and both are treated as forces (Doc_08, Forces 2B-3, 2B-4), but neither rests on a vendored narrative-genre primary source in this world's own Registry — both are characterized from Doc_01's own general historical account, not from a text like Hegenwald's preface, Calvin's own prefatory letter, or Myconius's biography. A corpus-wide search for either episode across this world's own vendored files, run specifically for this document, found no mention. Building either as a story chunk would require acquiring the kind of primary narrative source (a contemporary account of the Bolsec trial; a narrative record of the Perrinist affair's own specific incidents) this world does not currently have vendored.

## Section 7 — Absent Stories

Answered with the specificity the governing skill requires, not left as a placeholder.

1. **No Tier 1, 2, or 3 story exists, or can exist, from the ordinary Zurich or Geneva citizen's own voice.** This is this world's own central, already-established absence (Doc_05 §0 item 4, restated throughout; Doc_08 Force 2B-6): the genre asymmetry between doctrinal/confessional content (richly vendored) and daily-practice/first-person content (almost entirely absent) means no surviving account exists of what an ordinary Sunday service, a consistory summons, or an ordinary citizen's own experience of either Reformation actually felt like. Section 3.1's own Tier 2 finding (zero stories) is a direct symptom of this same underlying cause, not a separate gap.
2. **No hagiographic (Tier 3) material exists, and none is built to fill the absence.** Unlike Donatism's own martyr-cult world, this world's own confessional core actively refuses the devotional genre hagiography belongs to — image veneration and cultic memory of a holy figure's life are refused from this world's own founding statement onward (Doc_05 §6.2; Doc_07 §2C, §2H). This absence is evidence the refusal worked, not evidence of a missing source.
3. **No narrative account meeting Tier 1's own bar survives of the Marburg Colloquy (1529, beyond the one thin, non-narrative mention named at Section 6), the Bolsec controversy (1551), or the Perrinist crisis (1555) — three of this world's own most consequential documented events.** All three are Documented as historical fact (Doc_01, Doc_04, Doc_08) and all three function as forces or gravity-tests throughout this build, but none currently has a vendored primary narrative source comparable to Hegenwald's preface, Calvin's own prefatory letter, or Myconius's own biography of Zwingli (the fourth event this category originally included, Zwingli's own death at Kappel, is no longer absent — see Section 6's own correction note and `rzgstory003`). This is a live acquisition question (Section 6 above), not a claim that no such source could ever exist — standard historiography attests participant correspondence and contemporary records for at least Marburg and the Perrinist crisis that this world's own Registry has not yet acquired.
4. **No Genevan consistory case narrative survives.** The census's own named archive for this world — Geneva's consistory registers — remains unvendored (Registry row 13, Confidence D, confirmed copyrighted in the one identified edition; Doc_02 §5/§9; Doc_08 Force 2B-6). This is this world's own single largest disclosed story-genre absence: the specific, individually-named disciplinary cases this archive would supply are exactly the kind of Tier 1 narrative material (a real person, a real hearing, a real outcome) this document cannot build without it.
