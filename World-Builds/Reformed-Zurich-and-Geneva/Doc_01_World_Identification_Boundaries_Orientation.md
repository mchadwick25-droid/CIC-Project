## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Reformed Cities — Zurich & Geneva.

**Short Description:** The Swiss branch of the magisterial Reformation, beginning with Ulrich Zwingli's reform preaching at Zurich's Grossmünster from January 1519, continuing through Heinrich Bullinger's four-decade Zurich pastorate (1531–1575) after Zwingli's death at the Second Battle of Kappel, and joined — not founded — by John Calvin's Geneva ministry from 1536, consolidated under the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances. The two cities' own traditions are formally bridged, not merged, by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), a negotiated doctrinal agreement between Bullinger's Zurich and Calvin's Geneva on the Lord's Supper. Window: 1519–1650, per the census's own already-decided continues-cap convention (Era 7 Freeze, Mark, 2026-08-02) — an administrative closing point for a still-living tradition, not a historical rupture (§2 below).

**Core Identity:** A world of civic, city-governed reform — church order remade by a city's own council and, at Geneva, by a Consistory of pastors and lay elders exercising disciplinary authority over the whole population's ordinary conduct — built on a theology in which predestination carries load-bearing doctrinal weight (Article 4 commitments (1)–(5), quoted in full in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` header, are affirmed throughout without qualification) and in which the Lord's Supper is read as spiritual rather than corporeal, the specific point of doctrine that separated this world from Wittenberg's own Lutheran Reformation at Marburg (1529, §6 below). Two real internal centers of gravity — Zurich's council-led, disputation-and-Prophezei-based reform and Geneva's Consistory-governed, catechism-and-Institutes-driven reform — are tested formally for strand status in §4 below, not assumed either as one voice or as two.

**Distinctive Contribution:** No other confirmed or in-build world in this project's portfolio reconstructs a Reformed presbyterian/consistorial church order in which lay elders, not only clergy, hold formal disciplinary authority over a whole city's ordinary conduct, week by week — the specific texture the census's own selection rationale for this world names directly (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`, this entry's `"why"` field: "one of the best ordinary-life archives in Christian history"). Set against this batch's own Lutheran Wittenberg (VI.1): both are magisterial Protestant reforms of a Latin-Christian civic and princely order, but Wittenberg's reform runs through a prince's territorial church and retains a corporeal real-presence Eucharistic theology, where this world's reform runs through republican city-councils and consistorial courts and holds a spiritual-presence or memorial Eucharistic theology — a genuinely different church-order and sacramental logic, not a second instance of the same one (Step0 §3 B4).

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed, and squarely triggered rather than merely possible. Article 29 attaches "where a formation world's reconstruction touches a tradition that continues into the present" — quoted here verbatim from `reference/L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx` (Article 29, whose internal text is V2.3 despite the filename), checked directly against the governing document rather than carried forward from a prior document's own paraphrase. This world's own case is stronger than Donatism's PENDING finding (no documented descendant) or comparable to Imperial-Juridical Christianity's own continuing-communion findings: the census's own `"legacy"` field names four traditions this map already carries as separate, not-yet-built entries — the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritanism — and Reformed and Presbyterian churches worldwide (the PC(USA), the Church of Scotland, the Christian Reformed Church, and others) trace direct, continuous institutional and confessional descent from Zurich and Geneva's own 16th-century church orders, not a diffuse influence relationship. Per Article 29's own terms, this world is not freeze-eligible until Living Tradition Status is confirmed — a project-lead act this document cannot perform on its own behalf. **Status: PENDING**, flagged here for the project lead's own weighing at freeze rather than resolved or assumed either way.

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** 1519–1650 CE, adopted directly from the census's own already-decided window (`"start": 1519, "end": 1650`, Era 7 Freeze, Mark, 2026-08-02) rather than re-derived — the same discipline Donatism's own Doc_01 applied to an already-settled portfolio boundary. This window differs in kind from Donatism's own 311/312–439: Donatism's ending point marked a genuine historical rupture (the Vandal capture of Carthage removing the Roman-imperial adjudicating power the whole world was defined against). This world's ending point marks no comparable rupture — the census's own `"dateRationale"` field states plainly that 1650 is a "continues-cap" convention applied because this is a `"living": true` tradition, replacing an earlier 1600 boundary that "matched nothing." Doc_02 and Doc_08 should treat 1650 as an administrative construction-window close, not a historical ending event, consistent with §5's Preliminary Forces sketch below, which records no Ending/Transforming force internal to the window itself.

- **Beginning point (1519):** Zwingli's appointment as *Leutpriester* (people's priest) at the Grossmünster, Zurich, and his decision — beginning 1 January 1519 — to preach in continuous exposition through the Gospel of Matthew rather than follow the fixed lectionary, the conventionally dated start of Zurich's own reform. This is a defensible beginning point under the Framework's own Temporal Scope test: before it, Zurich's church life ran on the standard late-medieval lectionary and clerical structure common to the whole Latin West; after it, a specific, documented program of scripture-driven civic reform was underway that had not existed the year before.
- **Ending point (1650):** adopted from the census as an administrative, not historical-rupture, closing point (above). No single event inside this world's own window marks its close the way Kappel (1531, for Zwingli's own life) or the Vandal capture of Carthage (439, for all of Donatism) does; the Reformed pattern this world reconstructs continues unbroken past 1650 into the four descendant traditions named in §6 below, a genuinely different shape of ending than several other confirmed worlds in this portfolio and one Doc_08's own Ending/Transforming cell must record honestly as such, per Article 22's own instruction that "where a world's formation continues unbroken into a living tradition... that finding is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur" (Constitution V2.3 Article 22, quoted verbatim).

**Geographic Centers:**

- **Zurich:** Zwingli's and then Bullinger's own see, German-speaking, an independent city-state within the Swiss Confederacy; the site of the Prophezei (from 1525, a daily communal public exposition of scripture in the original languages that also produced the Zurich Bible, completed 1531) and of the First and Second Zurich Disputations (January and October 1523).
- **Geneva:** Calvin's see from 1536 (with a 1538–1541 interruption, below), French-speaking, a city that had only just broken from the Prince-Bishop of Geneva and the Duchy of Savoy and allied with Bern (1536) when Calvin arrived — not yet under Swiss Confederacy membership itself in this period, but firmly within the Confederacy's own Reformed orbit and alliance network. The seat of the Consistory established under the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances and of the Genevan Academy Calvin founded in 1559.
- **Strasbourg:** not this world's own center, but the site of Calvin's 1538–1541 exile after his and Farel's expulsion from Geneva, under Martin Bucer's own pastoral influence — formative for the Consistory model and liturgy Calvin brought back to Geneva in 1541, named here for orientation, not as this world's own third center.
- **The Palatinate (Heidelberg):** the see of Ursinus and Olevianus, whose Heidelberg Catechism (1563) is directly vendored and corpus-mapped for this world (`cic/corpus-map/the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva.yaml`) as "a distinct pastoral/catechetical voice alongside Calvin's Geneva Catechism, from the Reformed tradition's German wing" — a real secondary center of Reformed confession-writing within this world's own window, geographically and politically distinct from both Zurich and Geneva.
- **Bern:** not a center of original reform but the Confederacy's decisive early ally — the 1528 Bern Disputation brought Switzerland's most powerful canton into the Reformed camp and directly enabled Geneva's own 1536 break from Savoy.

**Cultural Environment:** Two distinct vernaculars inside one Reformed movement — German-speaking Zurich (and Bern, and German Switzerland generally) against French-speaking Geneva, itself substantially populated by French religious refugees, of whom Calvin, a Frenchman by birth, was the most consequential. Both cities sit within the Swiss Confederacy's own decentralized cantonal political structure — sovereign city-councils rather than a single prince or territorial ruler — the same structural condition that let city-by-city reform proceed independently at Zurich and Geneva rather than through one territorial church, a real point of contrast with Wittenberg's own princely-patronage model (Step0 §2 A3; §6 below). Geneva in particular became an international print and refugee center over this window — French Huguenots and, under Mary I, English Marian exiles — extending this world's own cultural reach well past its two named cities by the later 16th century.

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Swiss Confederacy's cantonal structure itself, which made reform a city-by-city political decision rather than a single princely act, and which also meant reform provoked armed conflict between Reformed and Catholic *cantons* directly — the Kappel Wars.
- Catholic-canton military resistance: the First Kappel War (1529, resolved without battle) and the Second Kappel War (1531), in which Zwingli was killed on the battlefield at Kappel (11 October 1531) — a form of pressure this world's own founding figure did not survive, unlike Luther's own death of natural causes in 1546.
- Geneva's own political precarity before and during Calvin's ministry: the city's 1536 break from the Prince-Bishop and the Duchy of Savoy, its consequent dependence on alliance with Bern, and recurring internal factional resistance to consistorial discipline (the Bolsec controversy over predestination, 1551; the Perrinist faction's resistance to the Consistory's independent excommunication authority, resolved in Calvin's favor by 1555) — a genuine internal contest over how much authority the church, as distinct from the civil council, should hold, addressed further in §4 below.
- Refugee inflow to Geneva (French Huguenots fleeing persecution, English Protestants fleeing Mary I's restoration) that both stressed and internationalized the city's own institutions.
- The Counter-Reformation as an external pressure across the whole window, sharpest at its two named poles: Michael Servetus's 1553 execution for anti-Trinitarian teaching by Geneva's own council (**a separate, already-registered census entry, VI.14, "Floor Question (register)" — not re-litigated here; Calvin's own theology is not in question, his conduct toward a rival current is a different matter belonging to that document, per Step0 §2 A1**) and, at the window's far end, the Synod of Dort (1618–19), addressed as a scoping question in §6 below rather than treated as this world's own event.

**Historical Catalysts:** Zwingli's 1519 preaching start; the 1522 public breaking of the Lenten fast in Zurich (the "Affair of the Sausages") that opened public confrontation over ecclesiastical authority; the First Zurich Disputation (January 1523, presenting Zwingli's Sixty-Seven Articles, now directly vendored and corpus-mapped) and Second Zurich Disputation (October 1523); the 1525 first believer's baptism in Zurich by Conrad Grebel and Felix Manz, former members of Zwingli's own reform circle — an origin point for the Anabaptist movement discussed as a boundary correction in §6 below, not a footnote; the Bern Disputation (1528); the Marburg Colloquy (1529, §6 below); the Kappel Wars (1529, 1531) and Zwingli's death; Geneva's break from Savoy and alliance with Bern (1536) and Calvin's arrival the same year; Calvin and Farel's 1538 expulsion from Geneva and Calvin's Strasbourg exile (1538–1541); Calvin's 1541 recall and the Ecclesiastical Ordinances; the Consensus Tigurinus (1549); the Bolsec controversy (1551); Servetus's execution (1553); the Perrinist crisis's 1555 resolution; the Second Helvetic Confession (1566, Bullinger's own mature statement, directly vendored); the Heidelberg Catechism (1563); and, at the window's close, the Synod of Dort (1618–19, §6 below).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** A civic-reform ecology in which church order is remade through a city's own governing instruments — Zurich's council acting on the outcome of public disputations, Geneva's Consistory exercising direct weekly disciplinary oversight of ordinary conduct — joined to a theology in which the sovereignty of God in predestination and the rejection of any corporeal presence in the Supper are load-bearing, non-negotiable commitments distinguishing this world from its Lutheran counterpart.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally: the sovereignty of God, expressed as predestination as a systematized, load-bearing doctrine (most fully in Calvin, but present in seed form in Zwingli's own providential theology); a spiritual or memorial (never corporeal) reading of Christ's presence in the Supper, the direct point of the Marburg break; scripture as the sole and sufficient authority for reforming both doctrine and civic order, enacted through public disputation (Zurich) and catechesis (Geneva); and church discipline as a formal, institutionalized function of the visible church, not left to conscience or civil law alone (the Consistory). Named here as candidates for Step 4 testing, not as confirmed gravities.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Authority and belonging are the most developed in the vendored corpus so far: the Consistory's own disciplinary authority, and the confessional documents (Second Helvetic Confession, Heidelberg Catechism, Geneva Catechism) that define belonging by right doctrine held and professed. Worship shows a real, documented internal split rather than one pattern: Zurich under Zwingli removed organs and suppressed congregational singing as unscriptural, while Geneva under Calvin and then Beza built congregational worship around the metrical Genevan Psalter (complete 1562) — treated as primary evidence for the Strand Determination in §4 below, not merely a stylistic footnote. Interpretation and formation are strong in the Institutes and the catechisms but, as of this document, still asymmetric between the two cities in a way §7 below carries forward as binding on Doc_02.
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §6 below.

## 4. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21 (quoted verbatim above and in full at §5's own heading): strand is "a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail" — a finding accountable to evidence, "never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality." Step 0 named this question directly (§2 A2) but correctly left it to Step 1's own testing rather than deciding it in advance.

**Finding: two strands — the Zurich strand (Zwingli, succeeded by Bullinger) and the Geneva strand (Calvin, succeeded by Beza) — bridged doctrinally, not merged institutionally, by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549).**

The evidence clears Article 21's bar on more than one of its own named dimensions independently, not on a single contestable point:

1. **Practice.** Zurich's own worship, under Zwingli's direct instruction, removed organ music and suppressed congregational singing as without scriptural warrant — the Grossmünster's own organ was destroyed in 1524. Geneva's worship, by direct contrast, built congregational singing into the center of its own devotional life through the metrical Genevan Psalter (Marot and Beza, complete 1562), a document the census's own sources field names as "the movement's ordinary devotional voice." This is not a minor stylistic variation; it is two cities within the same reforming movement reaching opposite conclusions, on the same scriptural-warrant principle, about whether congregational song belongs in worship at all.
2. **Authority structure.** Zurich's reform was enacted through the city council acting on the outcome of public clerical disputation — the council, not a separate ecclesial body, held final civil and religious authority, with Zwingli and later Bullinger functioning as the council's own leading clergy rather than as heads of an independent ecclesiastical court. Geneva's 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances created a distinct institution — the Consistory, composed of pastors and lay elders — holding disciplinary authority (up to and including excommunication) that Calvin fought to keep independent of the civil council's own direct control, a fight substantially resolved only by 1555 (the Perrinist crisis, §2 above). These are two different authority structures, not one structure under two names.
3. **Formation emphasis.** Zurich's own formation instrument was the Prophezei, a public, communal, scholarly exposition of scripture in its original languages, oriented toward an educated clerical and civic audience and feeding directly into the Zurich Bible. Geneva's own formation instrument was the Genevan Catechism, a fixed, memorizable statement of doctrine for the whole population's own instruction, paired with the Consistory's own direct, individualized moral oversight. These are genuinely different formation logics — public scholarly exposition versus catechetical instruction backed by disciplinary enforcement — not the same practice differently named.

**What does not separate the strands, and must not be read as though it did:** both hold the same confessional core — predestination, scriptural sole authority, rejection of the Mass and of image veneration, and (after 1549) a formally agreed, if independently worded, spiritual reading of the Supper. The Consensus Tigurinus itself is the documented evidence that this is one movement with two strands, not two movements bridged after the fact: it is a negotiated agreement between two already-distinct parties on their most contested point of doctrine, not a founding merger creating a single new position. This world's own coherence rests on that 1549 bridge, exactly as Step0 §2 A2 already states, and on the shared confessional core the Second Helvetic Confession (1566, Bullinger's own mature statement) and the Heidelberg Catechism (1563, Palatinate) both later express for the movement as a whole.

**Cross-strand implication for Step 4 and Step 10:** per Article 21, "where strands exist, convergence across them is a test of a gravity's centrality" — the sovereignty-of-God/predestination gravity and the scripture-sole-authority gravity are visible in both the Zwingli/Bullinger and Calvin/Beza material and should be tested for convergence at Doc_04 on that basis. This finding also directly bears on the Representative-construction question the launch instructions for this build named as a real, live choice — a single Representative (most plausibly Calvin, whose own corpus vastly outweighs Zwingli's, per §7 below) versus a genuine multi-figure Representative reflecting the Zwingli/Bullinger/Calvin structure — by establishing, on the evidence, that the structure being weighed is a real two-strand finding and not merely a rhetorical framing of one figure's dominance. This document does not decide that question; per this build's own escalation categories, Representative identity and structure decisions are reserved for the project lead directly, at Step 10, packaged as named candidates with trade-offs.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation governing Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8. Constitution Article 22 (quoted in full): "Every formation world is situated against the forces that shaped it across both external and internal dimensions, and across the duration of its life... Where a world's formation continues unbroken into a living tradition, or where no internal fracture is attested by the evidence, that finding is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur."

**What generated this world:** two independent local triggers, not one shared cause — Zwingli's own Erasmian-humanist biblical scholarship, applied to Zurich's own civic reform context and catalyzed by the 1522 Sausage Affair; and, wholly separately, Geneva's 1536 political break from the Prince-Bishop and Savoy, which created the civic opening Calvin's own arrival and 1541 recall filled. The two triggers are connected only retrospectively, by the Consensus Tigurinus (§4 above) — this world's own coherence is a documented later bridge across two separate beginnings, not a single common origin.

**What larger world was it embedded in:** the same late-medieval Latin-Christian civic and clerical order Wittenberg's own Reformation contested, read through the Swiss Confederacy's own decentralized, republican city-state structure rather than a princely-territorial one; and, more distantly, the Augustinian tradition on predestination and grace — the census itself already records this transmission directly: "Calvin's Institutes draws explicitly and heavily on Augustine's anti-Pelagian writings... for total depravity, unconditional election, and irresistible grace — Calvin named Augustine his primary authority next to Scripture itself... A direct theological line across eleven centuries with no institutional continuity — influence, not identity" (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`, transmission edge, `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity` → `the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva`).

**What was it responding to:** the same late-medieval sacramental and clerical system Wittenberg responded to, but pressed further on two specific points Wittenberg did not press as far — the legitimacy of images and instrumental worship in a scripture-governed church, and (at Geneva specifically) genuine civic disorder and moral laxity the Consistory's own weekly discipline was built to address, per the census's own framing of the consistory registers as documenting "the ordinary conduct of ordinary people."

**What was it refusing:** image veneration and the Mass's own sacrificial character, refused alongside Wittenberg; and, distinctly from Wittenberg, any claim that Christ's body is locally or corporeally present in the bread and wine — Zwingli's own memorial reading and Calvin's own spiritual-presence position both refuse Luther's real-presence formula, the specific and irreducible content of the Marburg break (1529). This world's own Geneva strand also, over time, refused unchecked civil-magistrate control over church discipline specifically — the Consistory's own long fight for independent excommunication authority, resolved only by 1555.

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits from prior worlds:** the Augustinian tradition on predestination and grace (quoted transmission edge, §5 above) — not yet built as its own world (Augustine sits inside not-yet-built World #8, Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity, per Donatism's own Doc_01 §6 placement); and the broader Christian-humanist, Erasmian textual and philological method both Zwingli and Calvin were trained in, which shaped both cities' own scripture-first reforming method.

**What this world transmits to subsequent worlds/history:** genuinely the broadest of any world so far in this portfolio. The census's own `"legacy"` field names four descendant traditions this map already carries as separate, not-yet-built entries: the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritanism — named here for orientation only, not developed, since each is that future world's own reconstruction to build. One further, more specific transmission point requires this document's own decision, per Step0 §4 item 2 (binding):

**The Dort scoping decision.** The Synod of Dort (1618–19) sits chronologically inside this world's own 1519–1650 window, and Step0 flagged it as requiring an actual scope decision rather than an assumption either way. That decision is not, on inspection, this document's own to invent: the census's own already-adopted portfolio design gives Dort itself a named home. VI.26, "The Remonstrants and Dort" (Netherlands, 1610–1650), was added at the same Era 7 Freeze (Mark, 2026-08-02) that set this world's own 1650 boundary, and its own `"why"` field states plainly that it was "proposed by the completeness sweep: a real surviving communion born of a synod that had no named home anywhere in the census" — i.e., the portfolio itself already treats Dort, the Remonstrance, the Canons, and the exiled Remonstrant Brotherhood as VI.26's own story, not this world's. **Finding, applying that already-made portfolio decision rather than redeciding it: the Synod of Dort's own event — the Remonstrance, the Synod's proceedings and Canons, Arminius, Episcopius, Oldenbarnevelt, and the exiled Brotherhood — belongs to VI.26's own future construction, not this world's.** What this world does legitimately transmit toward that story, and what Doc_02 should carry forward rather than silently drop: Theodore Beza's own systematized double-predestination theology, developed at the Genevan Academy after Calvin's death and the direct target the Remonstrance itself was written against; and, per standard Reformation historiography — not independently verified against a primary vendored source this pass, and flagged here rather than asserted as settled — an international Reformed delegation attended the Synod of Dort including representatives from Geneva itself (commonly identified as Giovanni Diodati and Theodore Tronchin), which would make this world's own theological line a direct, attended party to Dort's own judgment rather than a merely retrospective influence. This is named as an open item for Doc_02 (§7 below), not resolved here.
