## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Reformed Cities — Zurich & Geneva.

**Short Description:** The Swiss branch of the magisterial Reformation, beginning with Ulrich (Huldrych) Zwingli's reform preaching at Zurich's Grossmünster from January 1519, continuing through Heinrich Bullinger's four-decade Zurich pastorate (1531–1575) after Zwingli's death at the Battle of Kappel (fought during the Second War of Kappel, 11 October 1531), and joined — not founded — by John Calvin's Geneva ministry from 1536, consolidated under the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances. The two cities' own traditions are formally bridged by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), a doctrinal agreement on the Lord's Supper mediated by Bullinger's own Zurich and Calvin's Geneva — not evidence of prior contact between Zwingli and Calvin, who never met and whose ministries did not overlap (Zwingli died in 1531; Calvin's Geneva ministry began in earnest in 1536), a distinction this document states plainly per Step0 §4 item 1's own binding instruction. Window: 1519–1650, per the census's own already-decided continues-cap convention (Era 7 Freeze, Mark, 2026-08-02) — an administrative closing point for a still-living tradition, not a historical rupture (§2 below).

**Core Identity:** A world of civic, city-governed reform — church order remade by a city's own council and, at Geneva, by a Consistory of pastors and lay elders exercising disciplinary authority over the whole population's ordinary conduct — built on a theology in which predestination carries load-bearing doctrinal weight (Article 4 commitments (1)–(3), quoted in full in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` header, are directly verified against this world's own confessional texts per Step0 §2 A1; commitments (4)–(5) are affirmed by the same confessional documents but were not individually checked against the text in that pass, a distinction this document carries forward rather than upgrades) and in which the Lord's Supper is read as spiritual rather than corporeal — the doctrine that, once developed on both sides, separated the mature Reformed tradition from Wittenberg's own Lutheran Reformation at Marburg (1529, §6 below). Two real internal centers of gravity — Zurich's council-led, disputation-and-Prophezei-based reform and Geneva's Consistory-governed, catechism-and-Institutes-driven reform — are tested formally for strand status in §5 below, only after §4 establishes that they belong to one world rather than two.

**Distinctive Contribution:** No other confirmed or in-build world in this project's portfolio reconstructs a Reformed presbyterian/consistorial church order in which lay elders, not only clergy, hold formal disciplinary authority over a whole city's ordinary conduct, week by week — the specific texture the census's own selection rationale for this world names directly (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`, this entry's `"why"` field: "one of the best ordinary-life archives in Christian history"). Set against this batch's own Lutheran Wittenberg (VI.1): both are magisterial Protestant reforms of a Latin-Christian civic and princely order, but Wittenberg's reform runs through a prince's territorial church and retains a corporeal real-presence Eucharistic theology, where this world's reform runs through republican city-councils and consistorial courts and holds a spiritual-presence or memorial Eucharistic theology — a genuinely different church-order and sacramental logic, not a second instance of the same one (Step0 §3 B4). This world's own church-government vocabulary is Reformed and non-episcopal throughout: no figure in this world holds a bishop's *see* — Zurich's Zwingli and Bullinger held the office of *Antistes*, the city's leading pastor answering to the council; Geneva was governed by the Company of Pastors and the lay-inclusive Consistory; the Palatinate church at Heidelberg was a Reformed territorial church under its Elector. Abolishing episcopal jurisdiction is itself part of what this world reconstructs, not incidental to it — the census's own `"legacy"` field names "church government by elected elders rather than by bishops" as part of what this world transmits.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed, and squarely triggered rather than merely possible. Article 29 attaches "where a formation world's reconstruction touches a tradition that continues into the present" — quoted here verbatim from `reference/L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx` (Article 29, whose internal text is V2.3 despite the filename), checked directly against the governing document. This world's own case is stronger than Donatism's PENDING finding (no documented descendant): the census's own `"legacy"` field names four traditions this map already carries as separate, not-yet-built entries — the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritanism — and Reformed and Presbyterian churches worldwide trace direct, continuous institutional and confessional descent from Zurich and Geneva's own 16th-century church orders. Per Article 29's own terms, this world is not freeze-eligible until Living Tradition Status is confirmed. The confirmation mechanism itself is not Article 29 alone, whose own closing sentence assigns only that "the method of confirmation is governed by the build documents" — the actual procedure is Formation World Blueprint V7.3 §17 ("Validation Documentation," subsection "Living Tradition Differentiation," verified directly against `reference/L3B-World-Build-Methodology/CiC_L3B_Formation_World_Blueprint_V7.3.docx`): three criteria (naming the present-day tradition(s); documenting material divergences in the Representative Construction Notes; project-lead confirmation with a date), the same citation pattern Donatism's and Imperial-Juridical Christianity's own Doc_01s use rather than resting the claim on Article 29 in isolation. **Status: PENDING**, flagged here for the project lead's own weighing at freeze rather than resolved or assumed either way.

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** 1519–1650 CE, adopted directly from the census's own already-decided window (`"start": 1519, "end": 1650`, Era 7 Freeze, Mark, 2026-08-02) rather than re-derived. This window differs in kind from Donatism's own 311/312–439: Donatism's ending point marked a genuine historical rupture. This world's ending point marks no comparable rupture — the census's own `"dateRationale"` field states plainly that 1650 is a "continues-cap" convention applied because this is a `"living": true` tradition, replacing an earlier 1600 boundary that "matched nothing." Doc_02 and Doc_08 should treat 1650 as an administrative construction-window close, not a historical ending event, consistent with §6's Preliminary Forces sketch below.

**What developments remain internal to this world's own window, and what indicate transition** (Part I's own Temporal Scope questions, both answered directly here rather than left implicit): internal — the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), the Bolsec and Perrinist controversies, the Second Helvetic Confession and Heidelberg Catechism (1563, 1566), all real developments within a continuous Reformed identity that do not indicate a transition out of this world. Transitional — the Synod of Dort (1618–19) is the one development inside this window whose own status as internal-to-this-world versus a hand-off to a neighboring, differently-scoped world is itself a live, disclosed, and (per §7 below) escalated question, precisely because Dort marks the point at which the Reformed movement's own doctrinal self-definition becomes an international and specifically Dutch-political affair rather than a Zurich/Geneva-centered one.

- **Beginning point (1519):** Zwingli's appointment as *Leutpriester* (people's priest) at the Grossmünster, Zurich, and his decision — beginning 1 January 1519 — to preach in continuous exposition through the Gospel of Matthew rather than follow the fixed lectionary, the conventionally dated start of Zurich's own reform.
- **Ending point (1650):** adopted from the census as an administrative, not historical-rupture, closing point (above). No single event inside this world's own window marks its close; the Reformed pattern this world reconstructs continues unbroken past 1650 into the four descendant traditions named in §7 below, a finding Doc_08's own Ending/Transforming cell must record honestly, per Article 22's own instruction that "where a world's formation continues unbroken into a living tradition... that finding is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur" (Constitution V2.3 Article 22, quoted verbatim).

**Geographic Centers:**

- **Zurich:** Zwingli's and then Bullinger's own city, German-speaking, an independent city-state within the Swiss Confederacy; the site of the Prophezei (from 1525, a public communal exposition of scripture in the original languages, meeting on most weekdays rather than daily, that also produced the Zurich Bible, completed 1531) and of the First and Second Zurich Disputations (January and October 1523).
- **Geneva:** Calvin's city from 1536 (with a 1538–1541 interruption, below), French-speaking. Geneva's own break from the Prince-Bishop of Geneva and the Duchy of Savoy, and its *combourgeoisie* alliance with Bern and Fribourg, dates from 1526 — a decade before Calvin's own arrival, not the same year (§7 below corrects this document's own earlier compression of the timeline); Bern's own 1536 military intervention and Vaud campaign and Geneva's 21 May 1536 reformation vote are the specific 1536 events, distinct from the 1526 alliance that made them possible. Geneva was not itself a Swiss Confederacy member in this period, but was firmly within the Confederacy's own Reformed orbit and alliance network. The seat of the Consistory established under the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances and of the Genevan Academy Calvin founded in 1559.
- **Strasbourg:** not this world's own center, but the site of Calvin's 1538–1541 exile after his and Farel's expulsion from Geneva, under Martin Bucer's own pastoral influence — formative for the Consistory model and liturgy Calvin brought back to Geneva in 1541, named here for orientation, not as this world's own third center.
- **The Palatinate (Heidelberg):** the see of no bishop but the territorial Reformed church associated with Zacharias Ursinus (traditionally, and per the corpus map's own attribution, joint author with Caspar Olevianus of the Heidelberg Catechism, 1563; current scholarship treats Olevianus's own share as disputed, with Ursinus generally read as principal author under Elector Frederick III's commission — carried here as the traditional attribution the corpus map itself uses, not independently adjudicated). The catechism is directly vendored and corpus-mapped for this world (`cic/corpus-map/the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva.yaml`) as "a distinct pastoral/catechetical voice alongside Calvin's Geneva Catechism, from the Reformed tradition's German wing" — a real secondary center of Reformed confession-writing within this world's own window.
- **Bern:** not a center of original reform but the Confederacy's decisive early ally — the 1528 Bern Disputation brought Switzerland's most powerful canton into the Reformed camp.

**Cultural Environment:** Two distinct vernaculars inside one Reformed movement — German-speaking Zurich (and Bern, and German Switzerland generally) against French-speaking Geneva, itself substantially populated by French religious refugees, of whom Calvin, a Frenchman by birth, was the most consequential. Both cities sit within the Swiss Confederacy's own decentralized cantonal political structure — sovereign city-councils rather than a single prince or territorial ruler — the condition that let city-by-city reform proceed independently at Zurich and Geneva rather than through one territorial church, a real point of contrast with Wittenberg's own princely-patronage model (this world's own §0/§3 B4 of `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md`). Geneva in particular became an international print and refugee center over this window — French Huguenots and, under Mary I, English Marian exiles.

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Swiss Confederacy's cantonal structure itself, which made reform a city-by-city political decision and also meant Reformed and Catholic *cantons* went to war directly over it — the Wars of Kappel.
- Catholic-canton military resistance: the First War of Kappel (1529, resolved without battle) and the Second War of Kappel (1531), in whose fighting Zwingli was killed (11 October 1531) — a form of pressure this world's own founding figure did not survive, unlike Luther's own death of natural causes in 1546.
- Geneva's own political situation before and during Calvin's ministry: the 1526 alliance with Bern and Fribourg, the 1536 break from Savoy and the Prince-Bishop, and recurring internal factional resistance to consistorial discipline (the Bolsec controversy over predestination, 1551; the Perrinist faction's resistance to the Consistory's independent excommunication authority, substantially resolved in Calvin's favor by 1555).
- Refugee inflow to Geneva (French Huguenots, English Protestants fleeing Mary I's restoration) that both stressed and internationalized the city's own institutions.
- The Counter-Reformation as an external pressure across the whole window, sharpest at Michael Servetus's 1553 execution for anti-Trinitarian teaching by Geneva's own council (**a separate, already-registered census entry, VI.14, "Floor Question (register)" — not re-litigated here; Calvin's own theology is not in question, his conduct toward a rival current is a different matter belonging to that document, per Step0 §2 A1**) and, at the window's far end, the Synod of Dort (1618–19), addressed as an escalated scoping question in §7 below rather than treated as this world's own event.

**Historical Catalysts:** Zwingli's 1519 preaching start; the 1522 public breaking of the Lenten fast in Zurich (the "Affair of the Sausages"); the First Zurich Disputation (January 1523, presenting Zwingli's Sixty-Seven Articles, now directly vendored and corpus-mapped) and Second Zurich Disputation (October 1523); the 21 January 1525 first believer's baptisms in Zurich, at Felix Manz's house — Conrad Grebel baptizing Georg Blaurock, who then baptized the others present, including Manz himself, all three formerly members of Zwingli's own reform circle (§7 below); the Bern Disputation (1528); the Marburg Colloquy (1529, §6, §7 below); the Wars of Kappel (1529, 1531) and Zwingli's death; Geneva's 1526 alliance with Bern and 1536 break from Savoy, and Calvin's arrival the same year; Calvin and Farel's 1538 expulsion from Geneva and Calvin's Strasbourg exile (1538–1541); Calvin's 1541 recall and the Ecclesiastical Ordinances; the Consensus Tigurinus (1549); Theodore Beza's *Tabula praedestinationis* (1555, §7 below); the Bolsec controversy (1551); Servetus's execution (1553); the Perrinist crisis's 1555 resolution; the Second Helvetic Confession (1566, Bullinger's own mature statement, directly vendored); the Heidelberg Catechism (1563); and, at the window's close, the Synod of Dort (1618–19, §7 below).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** A civic-reform ecology in which church order is remade through a city's own governing instruments — Zurich's council acting on the outcome of public disputations, Geneva's Consistory exercising direct weekly disciplinary oversight of ordinary conduct — joined to a theology in which the sovereignty of God in predestination and the rejection of any corporeal presence in the Supper are load-bearing, non-negotiable commitments distinguishing this world from its Lutheran counterpart.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally: the sovereignty of God, expressed as predestination as a systematized, load-bearing doctrine (most fully in Calvin and Beza, present in seed form in Zwingli's own providential theology); a spiritual or memorial reading of Christ's presence in the Supper; scripture as the sole and sufficient authority for reforming both doctrine and civic order, enacted through public disputation (Zurich) and catechesis (Geneva); and church discipline as a formal, institutionalized function of the visible church. Named here as candidates for Step 4 testing, not as confirmed gravities.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Authority and belonging are the most developed in the vendored corpus so far: the Consistory's own disciplinary authority (per the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances, not itself yet vendored — §8 below), and the confessional documents (Second Helvetic Confession, Heidelberg Catechism, Geneva Catechism) that define belonging by right doctrine held and professed. Worship shows a real, documented internal difference at this world's own outset rather than one uniform pattern from the start: Zurich under Zwingli suppressed instrumental music and congregational singing as unscriptural (§5 below), while Geneva under Calvin and then Beza built congregational worship around the metrical Genevan Psalter (complete 1562) — the Psalter, like the Ordinances, is not itself yet vendored (§8 below), so this characterization draws on the census's own descriptive note plus general knowledge, not a text-verified quotation. Interpretation and formation are strong in the Institutes and the catechisms but still asymmetric between the two cities (§8 below).
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §7 below.

## 4. World Separation Criteria

Part I of the Construction Framework requires this test — "When does one world become another?" — as its own named element, distinct from, and prior to, Strand Determination. §3 above answers Distinct World Criteria's own question about adjacent worlds; this section asks the different question of whether Zurich's and Geneva's own reforms are one world with internal variation or two worlds that happen to be treated together, and runs the Framework's own six named sub-questions directly rather than assuming the answer:

- **Has a new gravity emerged (Geneva versus Zurich)?** No support found for one. Calvin's and Beza's own systematized double predestination is a development in degree and rigor of a conviction already present in seed form in Zwingli's own providential theology — an intensification, not a doctrine absent from one city and newly present in the other.
- **Has a major gravity disappeared?** No. Bullinger's Second Helvetic Confession (1566) — Zurich's own mature confessional statement — affirms the same core commitments the Sixty-Seven Articles state in 1523, and Geneva-side documents do not repudiate anything Zurich held.
- **Has formation changed substantially?** Real variation in instrument — the Prophezei's public scholarly exposition against the Genevan Catechism's fixed instruction backed by Consistory discipline — but not in underlying logic: both are scripture-centered, communal, and civically embedded formation programs, differing in mechanism rather than in what formation is for.
- **Has worship changed substantially?** The sharpest documented difference, and real at this world's own outset — Zurich's suppression of congregational song against Geneva's Psalter-centered practice. This difference did not remain permanent on the Zurich side: by most accounts, congregational psalm-singing was adopted across the Swiss Reformed churches generally, including Zurich, within this world's own window — a convergence, not confirmed here against a vendored primary source, flagged at Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence rather than certainty.
- **Has authority changed substantially?** Real variation — council-led enactment at Zurich against the Consistory's own distinct disciplinary institution at Geneva — but the wider Reformed world's own later convergence toward a Geneva-influenced consistorial/presbyterian pattern (in the French, Scottish, and Dutch Reformed churches this world transmits to, §7 below) suggests Geneva's model became the general Reformed pattern rather than a permanently rival one.
- **Has interpretation changed substantially?** No evidence found of a divergent hermeneutic — both hold sola scriptura and the same humanist philological method both Zwingli and Calvin were trained in (§7 below).

**Finding: one world.** The two tests that most directly track a new confessional identity — gravity emergence and gravity loss — do not clear the bar at all; interpretation does not clear it either. Worship and authority show real, substantial variation, but the Framework's own six questions are named as diagnostic together, not as a simple majority vote, and the two that do clear are institutional-structural variations in service of a shared confessional core rather than evidence of a different core — a reading independently corroborated by the historical and theological convention of treating Zwinglian/Bullingerian Zurich and Calvinist/Bezan Geneva as one "Reformed" confessional family, distinct from Lutheranism, rather than as two separate traditions (Widely Accepted characterization in Reformation historiography, not independently verified against a primary source this pass). This finding is disclosed as a judgment call on real, weighed evidence, not a foregone conclusion — if a reviewer reads the worship/authority evidence as more decisive than this document does, that disagreement should be named rather than absorbed silently, consistent with `cic-build-cycle`'s own instruction to log rather than paper over review-to-review disagreement.

This finding is the necessary premise for §5's Strand Determination below, which Article 21 itself only applies "within a single world."

## 5. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21: strand is "a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail" — a finding accountable to evidence, "never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality." Step 0 named this question directly (§2 A2) but correctly left it to Step 1's own testing. §4 above establishes the single-world premise Article 21 requires; this section applies Article 21 to the same evidence, from the other side.

**Finding: two strands — the Zurich strand (Zwingli, succeeded by Bullinger) and the Geneva strand (Calvin, succeeded by Beza) — bridged doctrinally, not merged institutionally, by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), itself mediated by Bullinger's own Zurich rather than by any direct contact between Zwingli and Calvin, who never met.**

The same evidence §4 found insufficient to separate two *worlds* is sufficient, within one world, to separate two *strands*, on more than one of Article 21's own named dimensions:

1. **Practice.** Zurich's own worship, under Zwingli's direct instruction, suppressed instrumental music and congregational singing as without scriptural warrant — by most accounts the Grossmünster's own organ was silenced in the mid-1520s (sources vary on the exact year; this is not vendored and is flagged at Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence rather than asserted to a specific year). Geneva's worship, by direct contrast, built congregational singing into the center of its own devotional life through the metrical Genevan Psalter (Marot and Beza, complete 1562, not itself vendored — §8 below) — a document the census's own sources field names as "the movement's ordinary devotional voice."
2. **Authority structure.** Zurich's reform was enacted through the city council acting on the outcome of public clerical disputation. Geneva's 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances (also not vendored — §8 below) created a distinct institution, the Consistory, holding disciplinary authority Calvin fought to keep independent of civil-council control, substantially resolved only by 1555.
3. **Formation emphasis.** Zurich's own formation instrument was the Prophezei; Geneva's was the fixed Genevan Catechism paired with the Consistory's own direct, individualized moral oversight — genuinely different formation logics.

**What bridges, rather than separates, the strands:** the shared confessional core — predestination, scriptural sole authority, rejection of the Mass and of image veneration, and, after 1549, a formally agreed spiritual reading of the Supper. The Consensus Tigurinus is the documented evidence of that bridge, and its own mediating party is Bullinger's Zurich, not any relationship between Zwingli and Calvin themselves: Zwingli died in 1531, five years before Calvin's Geneva ministry began in earnest, and the two men never met. This world's own coherence rests on Bullinger's mediation specifically, per Step0 §4 item 1's own binding instruction, stated here without qualification.

**Cross-strand implication for Step 4 and Step 10:** per Article 21, "where strands exist, convergence across them is a test of a gravity's centrality" — the sovereignty-of-God/predestination gravity and the scripture-sole-authority gravity are visible in both strands and should be tested for convergence at Doc_04. This finding also directly bears on the Representative-construction question named as a live choice for this build — a single Representative versus a genuine multi-figure Representative reflecting the Zwingli/Bullinger/Calvin structure — by establishing, on properly-run evidence, that the structure being weighed is a real, tested two-strand finding rather than a rhetorical framing of one figure's dominance. This document does not decide that question; per this build's own escalation categories, Representative identity and structure decisions are reserved for the project lead directly, at Step 10, packaged as named candidates with trade-offs.

## 7. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits from prior worlds:** the Augustinian tradition on predestination and grace (quoted transmission edge, §6 above) — not yet built as its own world (Augustine sits inside not-yet-built World #8, Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity, per Donatism's own Doc_01 §6 placement); and the broader Christian-humanist, Erasmian textual and philological method both Zwingli and Calvin were trained in.

**What this world transmits to subsequent worlds/history:** genuinely the broadest of any world so far in this portfolio.
