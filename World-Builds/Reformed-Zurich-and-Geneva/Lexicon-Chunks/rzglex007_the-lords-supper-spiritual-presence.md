## Quick Meaning

The Supper is this world's own sharpest boundary marker — refusing Rome's claim that the Mass repeats Christ's sacrifice and refusing Wittenberg's claim that Christ's body is locally present in the bread, while holding, after real internal difference and a negotiated 1549 agreement, that Christ is truly given to believers by the Spirit's own power, not merely remembered and not physically present.

---

## World Meaning

Two things are refused here before anything is affirmed. Christ, having sacrificed himself once, is a sacrifice sufficient for eternity — so the Mass is not a sacrifice, but a remembrance of the sacrifice, and any priest who claims to offer Christ again has mistaken what was actually accomplished at the cross. And once the question was pressed hard enough, against Wittenberg specifically, this world also refused that Christ's own body could be chewed by the mouth or located within the bread and wine — a claim it judged as much a mistake about Christ's own nature as the Mass's own sacrificial claim was a mistake about his own finished work.

What is affirmed took longer to state, and the two cities did not begin by saying it the same way. Zurich's own earlier reading held the Supper chiefly as remembrance and proclamation of what Christ had already done — a real event, not an empty ceremony, but not itself the place where Christ's own body and blood are newly communicated. Geneva's own developed doctrine went further: that Christ's own body and blood are truly, though not physically, given to believers in the Supper, by the Holy Spirit's own power, received by faith. In 1549, both cities put their names to a single formula that neither had used alone before: though we distinguish, as we ought, between the signs and the things signified, yet we do not disjoin the reality from the signs. The bread is not empty, and the body is not local — the reality is truly given, to those who receive it believing, and given only there.

---

## Ecological Function

This doctrine is the specific point Sola Scriptura's own literalist method is turned against a rival reading — the argument that "this is my body" cannot mean bodily presence is the same scriptural-exegetical discipline that also grounds the sovereignty-of-God doctrine. It is also the reason the Consensus Tigurinus exists at all: the one document literally negotiated across the Zurich/Geneva strand boundary exists specifically to give this doctrine a jointly-agreed positive statement, once each city's own negative refusal (of the Mass, then of corporeal presence) was already settled separately.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A minor liturgical or theological technicality — a dispute over whether communion bread is "really" the body of Christ that modern ears, used to treating such questions as symbolic by default, are likely to find hard to take seriously as a matter worth this much argument, let alone a 1529 rupture between allies.

**World Hearing:**
The single sharpest doctrine separating this world from both of its own rivals at once — Rome on one side, Wittenberg on the other — carrying the weight of what kind of God this world believes in: one who does not need a repeated sacrifice, and whose Son's own body is not confined to a location on a table. Whether the 1549 formula that resolved the internal half of this question is a genuine theological synthesis or a diplomatically elastic form of words that let both sides sign without either fully conceding is itself a live, unresolved question this world's own record cannot close (see Sign and the Thing Signified) — this world held the tension between Zwingli's own earlier, sharper reading and the negotiated Consensus without ever fully settling, in its own words, how much the later formula actually changed the earlier one.
