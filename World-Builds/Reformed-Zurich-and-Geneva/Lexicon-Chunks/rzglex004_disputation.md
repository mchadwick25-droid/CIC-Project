## Quick Meaning

A Disputation is a public, formal debate held before Zurich's own city council, in which the reforming party defends its scriptural case against the old church's own defenders — the actual civic mechanism, not a private argument among clergy, by which Zurich's reform was decided.

---

## World Meaning

Reform here did not begin with a bishop's decree or a prince's command. It began with a public test: let the case be argued, before the city's own council and its own citizens, from Scripture alone, and let whichever side cannot answer from Scripture yield. This is what happened twice in 1523 — first in January, then again in October — and both times the council itself judged the outcome and acted on it. Our own founder confessed at the first of these that everything he had preached in the city rested on Scripture, and offered, in his own words, to be taught better himself, but only from that same Scripture, if he had misunderstood it anywhere.

This is not a formality staged to bless a decision already made elsewhere. The council's own judgment at these disputations is the actual mechanism by which images were removed, the Mass eventually abolished, and the reformed order established in law — not a separate civil ratification of a churchly decision, but the decision itself, made through the city's own governing instrument because that instrument, and no other, was this world's own way of testing a doctrine in public.

---

## Ecological Function

The Disputation is Sola Scriptura's own concrete enactment at Zurich — the specific civic form the shared scriptural-authority conviction took in this city, as catechesis and Consistory oversight took a different form at Geneva. It produced the Sixty-Seven Articles, the specific document defended at the first disputation, and it set the pattern (public, council-judged, scripture-argued) that this world's own later confrontations — with the Anabaptists in 1525, with the Catholic cantons through the 1520s — repeatedly returned to as the way a contested question gets settled here.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A formal academic debate, or a piece of political theater — a staged event whose outcome was likely settled beforehand, since councils rarely reverse the powerful.

**World Hearing:**
The actual, load-bearing mechanism of reform itself — not theater dressed around a foregone conclusion, but the specific civic-scriptural test this world trusted to settle a real question, because the council's own judgment, reached this way, is what this world took reform's own legitimacy to require.
