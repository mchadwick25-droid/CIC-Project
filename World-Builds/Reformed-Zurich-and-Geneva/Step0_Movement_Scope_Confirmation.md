## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every other unbuilt candidate in this project: this document's own Section A "clears" are a considered signal for Mark's use, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears cleanly

As for every other candidate in this batch, this window sits well after Chalcedon, so A1 is the direct test. **It clears without qualification, on the movement's own confessional documents, host-verified this pass:** the Second Helvetic Confession (Bullinger's own mature statement, 1566) chapter III affirms the Trinity and chapter XI affirms Christ's full deity and humanity in terms matching Article 4's commitments (1)–(3) directly; the Heidelberg Catechism (1563) is doctrinally consistent throughout. No floor complication found for Zwingli, Bullinger, or Calvin.

**One disclosure adjacent to, but distinct from, the floor test itself:** Calvin's own role in Michael Servetus's 1553 execution for anti-Trinitarian teaching belongs to a **separate, already-registered census entry** (the anti-Trinitarian current, VI.14, "Floor Question (register)" status) — not re-litigated here, but Doc_01 should cross-reference it directly rather than treat Calvin's own orthodoxy as somehow implicated by the case. Calvin's own theology is not in question; his conduct toward a rival current is a different matter, belonging to a different document.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity

Not applicable — this window opens over a millennium after Nicaea.

**A genuine internal continuity complication, disclosed here rather than smoothed over, on the same standard A2 would apply if it were the governing test:** Zwingli dies in 1531 (the Battle of Kappel), before Calvin's Geneva ministry begins in earnest in 1536. **There is no direct personal link between Zwingli and Calvin.** The actual documented bridge is Bullinger — Zwingli's real successor at Zurich — and the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), a formal doctrinal agreement between Bullinger's Zurich and Calvin's Geneva on the Lord's Supper. This candidate's coherence as one movement rests on that documented Bullinger-mediated link, not on any relationship between its two most famous names. Doc_01 must state this plainly rather than imply direct contact between Zwingli and Calvin that did not happen.

### A3 — Contemporary movements

Real and disclosed, shared with this batch's Lutheran Wittenberg sibling document: the Marburg Colloquy (1529) Eucharistic break with Luther, from this movement's own side; and — new here — the Synod of Dort (1618–19) sits inside this candidate's own stated window (1519–1650) but is **also a separate, already-registered census entry** (VI.26, "The Remonstrants and Dort"). Doc_01 needs an actual scoping decision — whether Dort's Calvinist-orthodoxy-versus-Arminian-Remonstrant controversy belongs inside this candidate's own story or is better left to stand as its own separate world if VI.26 is ever built — not resolved here, carried forward as a binding open item (§4).

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every other candidate: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: very strong, with a real internal asymmetry disclosed

Per the dossier, directly host-verified: Calvin's Institutes in **two** confirmed PD English translations (Allen 1813, Beveridge 1845 — the latter the standard cited edition); the ~22-volume Calvin Translation Society English commentary series (nearly the whole Bible); Calvin's letters (Bonnet, 4 vols., 1855–58); the Geneva Catechism (Waterman 1815); Zwingli's Latin Works and Correspondence (Jackson, 3 vols., 1912/1922/1929) and Selected Works (Jackson, 1901). **Same methodological caveat as every sibling document in this batch: no figure below is extracted from vendored XML, since nothing is vendored yet.** On a qualitative read, Calvin's own corpus alone is likely the single largest founder-corpus this project has ever considered — larger by a wide margin than Zwingli's, a real asymmetry Doc_02 will need to manage rather than average away.

**One genuinely open acquisition item, not closed:** the Consensus Tigurinus (1549) — the actual documented Zwingli/Bullinger-to-Calvin bridge named in §2 A2 above — has no confirmed PD English translation yet located. This is a binding open item, not a dead end (§4 below).

### B2 — Ecology

Strong across doctrine (Institutes, the confessions), pastoral practice (Geneva Catechism, the Ecclesiastical Ordinances — the latter not yet independently host-verified, per the dossier), and exegesis (the CTS commentary series, an unusually complete scriptural-commentary corpus for any candidate this project has considered). Thinner, and disclosed rather than assumed strong: Zwingli's own liturgical and ecclesiological writings beyond the Jackson volumes' confirmed contents (§4 below, table-of-contents verification still owed).

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no overlap possible, over a millennium's separation. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** the Marburg/Lutheran relationship (§2 A3); no figure overlap with the Jesuits, the Anabaptists, the Tridentine Church, or Lollardy — though the Tridentine Church's own decrees, once vendored, will almost certainly need to be read directly against this candidate's own confessional documents, the same "direct doctrinal rival" relationship Lutheran Wittenberg's own Step 0 names for its own relationship to Trent.

### B4 — Audiences

A genuinely distinct pull from Lutheran Wittenberg despite both being "magisterial Protestant" — Reformed church polity (presbyterian/consistorial governance), predestination as a load-bearing doctrine in a way Lutheranism treats differently, and Calvin's own systematic, commentary-driven exegetical method give this candidate its own real audience rather than duplicating its batch-mate's.

### B5 — Scale

Swiss lands, then all of Europe by 1650 — the broadest total geographic reach of any candidate in this batch by its window's close, reflecting the real spread of Reformed church polity across the continent within this period.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A directly on the movement's own confessional text, with the Zwingli-Calvin continuity question disclosed honestly rather than assumed and the Dort scoping question named as binding, not resolved; clears Sourcing very strongly, with one real internal asymmetry (Calvin vastly outweighs Zwingli) and one genuinely open acquisition item (the Consensus Tigurinus); clears Ecology broadly, with Zwingli's own liturgical/ecclesiological completeness not yet independently confirmed; no built-world overlap; genuinely distinct audience from its own batch-mate; broadest geographic scale in the batch.

**Tier 1 — Strong seed, two binding conditions remaining.** Unlike Lutheran Wittenberg's own sibling document, this candidate carries two real, disclosed open items rather than none: the Consensus Tigurinus acquisition (§3 B1) and the Dort scoping decision (§2 A3). Neither is disqualifying; both are genuine work for Doc_01/Doc_02, not smoothed-over gaps.
