## Quick Meaning
For this community, ousia and hypostasis are the paired words hammered out through the great quarrel to confess one God in three who truly are: one being, three who each truly subsist — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — without division of the one or blurring of the three.

## World Meaning
The two words began as near-neighbors, and the confusion was real and painful: those who said "three hypostaseis" were accused of preaching three gods; those who said "one hypostasis" were accused of collapsing the Three into masks of one. This world's teachers spent years of letters and treatises sorting the pair — not as philosophy for its own sake, but as fence-building around worship. The fence keeps two things safe at once: what God is (one, simple, beyond every mind's compass) and who God is (the Father, the Son, the Holy Spirit — each truly, none a creature, none a costume). Get the words wrong in one direction and you are praying to three gods; wrong in the other and the Son who saves you is a mode, not a person; wrong in a third way — the way the court's formulas drifted — and he is a creature, and no creature makes gods. The world paid for these distinctions in exiles and broken friendships, and it never mistook them for cleverness: the same teachers who built the fence insisted that what lives inside it exceeds every word ever said.

## Ecological Function
This pair anchors the world's central gravity: the Triune God confessed at the edge of the knowable. Without it, the doxology's defense, the baptismal formula's weight, and the whole quarrel with the court and the clever definer lose their grammar. It is also the world's signature habit made visible: distinctions drawn finely as acts of care — here, care for prayer itself.

## Distortion Risk
**Modern Hearing:** abstract metaphysics remote from real faith; "the Trinity" as a logic puzzle or a box to tick; the terms as Greek philosophy contaminating a simple gospel; or one's own tradition's later systematization read back as what these people meant.
**World Hearing:** the exact words that keep prayer true, placed with fear; the difference between worshipping God and worshipping a creature hanging on their correct use; hard-won, costly, and — the world would say first — insufficient: true, and not enough.

## Key Sources
Basil, Against Eunomius and the letters distinguishing the terms (the most famous such letter is transmitted under both Basil's and his brother's names — attribution contested, carried); Gregory of Nazianzus, the Theological Orations; Gregory of Nyssa, Against Eunomius and the short treatises on the distinction; the creed of 381.
