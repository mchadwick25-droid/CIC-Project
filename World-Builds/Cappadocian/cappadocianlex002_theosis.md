## Quick Meaning
For this community, theōsis is salvation's full size: becoming god by grace — the creature growing without end into the likeness of the God it can never comprehend and never stop approaching.

## World Meaning
The daring is deliberate, and this world knows it. The older teachers had said it before them — he became what we are, that we might become what he is — and this world pushed the saying into a noun and into the center of its greatest fight: if the Son deifies us, and the Spirit deifies us, then neither can be a creature, for no creature makes gods. Salvation itself was inside the quarrel over the words. But the boldness is fenced by the world's deepest conviction: the line between Creator and creature is never crossed from below. Becoming god by grace does not mean promotion out of creaturehood; it means participation — the image growing into the Likeness it was made from, kinship deepening without the gap ever closing. And because the God being approached is without limit, the approach has no finish line: perfection here is not arrival but direction, desire deepening rather than concluding. The world's grammar of salvation is therefore ambitious and humble in one breath: nothing less than likeness to God is on offer, and no one will ever have God measured.

## Ecological Function
Theōsis names the formation aim's doctrinal ground: it is why the whole way of life — the psalms, the hours, the open hand, the exact confession — points as high as it does, and why the smallest demotion of Son or Spirit was worth a generation's fight: empty the Name, and you empty this. It binds the world's ascent-language to its Trinity-language so tightly that neither can be told alone.

## Distortion Risk
**Modern Hearing:** self-divinization, spiritual inflation, "you can become a god" in the self-help or esoteric register; pantheism; or dismissal as Eastern exotica foreign to "real" Christianity.
**World Hearing:** the Gospel's full size, spoken with fear; grace's work from first to last; participation across an interval that never closes; the reason a preposition in the doxology was worth exile.

## Key Sources
Gregory of Nazianzus, orations and poems (the noun-form's coinage in this circle is widely accepted); Basil, On the Holy Spirit (the Spirit's deifying work as argument for his divinity); Gregory of Nyssa, the ascent-literature (with the endless-progress elaboration carried as his development, not the world's common property — flag per Doc_06). The inheritance from Alexandria's older formula is real and named; the deployment in the pneumatological fight is this world's own.
