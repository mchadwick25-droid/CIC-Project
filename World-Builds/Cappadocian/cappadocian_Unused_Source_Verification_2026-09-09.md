## 0. Why this document exists, and what it is not

An external, read-only discovery pass this week asked a question this world's own 2026-08-31 sweep (`records/cappadocian/search_record/cappadocian.search.unopened-volume-sweep.md`) did not ask. That sweep asked whether any *vendored file* lacked a **Source Registry row**, and correctly found none. The new pass asked a different question — whether vendored, assigned source material had never been drawn into the world's own **records**, and whether any of it was structural rather than supplemental. Different question, so no contradiction between the two; both can be right.

This document records what an independent check of the five candidates actually found, against the primary texts and the files themselves rather than against the discovery pass's description of them. **Three of its five characterizations turned out to need correction**, one of them materially. That is recorded here in full rather than smoothed over, because a verification pass that only confirms what it was handed has not verified anything.

## 1. Candidate 1 — CTh 16.1.3, the imperial communion law of 381

### 1.1 What was verified, and how

The Latin text was read directly in two independent public-domain witnesses: **Mommsen–Meyer 1905** (*Theodosiani libri XVI*, book 16, at the rubric `XVI, 1, 3 (381 Iul. 30)`, with its critical apparatus) and **the Latin Library's plain text** of the Code (at the label `CTh.16.1.3`). Both carry an identical clause and an identical bishop list. An independent English-language discussion, **W. K. Boyd, *The Ecclesiastical Edicts of the Theodosian Code* (1905)**, was also read at its own citation of this law (p. 46 n. 1).

The law, addressed *ad Auxonium proconsulem Asiae*, orders that all churches be handed to bishops who confess Father, Son and Holy Spirit of one majesty and power, of the same glory, one splendour — and it identifies those bishops **by naming whose communion they must be in**:

> …quos constabit communioni Nectari episcopi Constantinopolitanae ecclesiae nec non Timothei intra Aegyptum Alexandrinae urbis episcopi esse sociatos; quos etiam in Orientis partibus Pelagio episcopo Laodicensi et Diodoro episcopo Tarsensi: in Asia nec non proconsulari adque Asiana dioecesi **Amphilochio episcopo Iconiensi** et Optimo episcopo Antiocheno: in Pontica dioecesi **Helladio episc. Caesariensi** et **Otreio Meliteno** et **Gregorio episc. Nysseno**, Terennio episc. Scythiae, Marmario episc. Marcianopolitano communicare constiterit.

Those who dissent from the communion of the men "whom the special enumeration has expressed" are to be expelled from the churches as manifest heretics.

### 1.2 Three corrections to the finding as it was handed over

1. **The vendorings do not exist on the default branch.** The three files named — `codex-theodosianus_latinlibrary.txt`, `theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt`, and `boyd_ecclesiastical-edicts-theodosian-code_1905.txt` — are **not in `cic/texts/` on `main`**. They exist only on the unmerged branch `donatism-lpc-integration`, which is where this verification read them. Source Registry row 79's own statement ("the Mommsen–Meyer Latin text is public domain but not acquired into `cic/texts/` this session") is therefore still accurate as written. No builder working from the default branch alone can open this text today.
2. **The law names eleven bishops, not four.** Nectarius of Constantinople and Timothy of Alexandria head the list; Pelagius of Laodicea and Diodore of Tarsus stand for Oriens; Amphilochius of Iconium and Optimus for Asiana; Helladius of Caesarea, Otreius of Melitene, Gregory of Nyssa, Terennius of Scythia and Marmarius of Marcianopolis for Pontica. Four of the eleven belong to this world's own circle. Doc_02 §2's existing phrasing — "naming, **among** the East's touchstone bishops" — is the accurate one and needed no change; the discovery pass's framing ("names… *by name* as the legal standard for who counts as orthodox… in the empire") overstates it in two directions at once, since the instrument is also addressed to one proconsul for the eastern dioceses and names no western bishop at all.
3. **This is not unused material.** The law is already carried at Doc_01 §4a, Doc_02 §2/§7/§9, Source Registry rows 79 and 80, `records/cappadocian/source/cappadocian.source.imperial-communion-law-of-381.md`, and — the point that matters most for the structural question — **Doc_04's own forces notation for the situational gravity**, which already reads the Theodosian settlement as the force that ends this world by fulfilling its central fight (§3.3, first and eighth entries).

### 1.3 The Doc_04 judgment: supplemental, not structural

Doc_04 classifies **Gravity 3, "the contested church under the contested empire," as Primary with a situational annotation** (candidate 8 at §1), and carries at Open Item 1 the standing alternative that it should be **Supporting** instead. The question put to this pass was whether imperial legal recognition, by name, of these bishops should move that classification.

**It should not, and the reason is specific rather than general.** Doc_04's Primary-versus-Supporting question for this gravity turns on exactly one score: **the Formation test**, moderated to *moderate* at §3.1 on the reasoning that the ordinary Christian's becoming ran through household teaching, baptism, psalmody, festival, brotherhood and alms — instruments *conditioned* by the imperial contest but not *constituted* by it (§3.1's "decisive moderation" note). Read whole, CTh 16.1.3 is an instrument of **recognition and property**: it settles who may hold a church building and whose communion is the test of catholicity. It is not a formation instrument, it names no formation practice, and it therefore bears on none of the evidence the Formation moderation rests on. A law that changes who holds the basilica does not change whether the plateau farmer's becoming ran through the imperial contest.

Two further reasons the elevation argument fails on its own terms:

- **It argues for the annotation it would overturn.** Doc_04's situational annotation says this is a gravity of the world's *situation* — one with a complete force life-cycle inside the span, "created outright by 1A-1, exercised reign by reign under 2A-1, dissolved by 3A-1" (§3.3). A gravity whose culmination is an imperial chancery list of eleven names, issued to a proconsul, is a paradigm case of that reading, not a counter-example. The law strengthens the annotation rather than the upgrade.
- **The six tests were already scored at their ceiling on the dimensions the law touches.** Repetition, Dependency, Explanatory and Persistence are all already **strong** for candidate 8 (§3.1). Corroborating evidence cannot raise a score that is already at maximum, and the classification bottleneck is elsewhere.

**One genuinely new evidential contribution, recorded where it belongs.** Because this law is an imperial chancery document rather than a text this world's own circle wrote, it is **external, non-circle attestation** that two of this world's own primary voices (Gregory of Nyssa, Amphilochius) held recognized authority in their own lifetimes. That bears on **Doc_04 Open Item 2 — "the circle-versus-world exposure"**, which §9 calls "the deepest structural risk under this whole map" and says "cannot be retired from inside this build." It does not retire it either; the law attests the circle's *standing*, not the world's formation ecology beyond the circle. But it is a real, if small, external datum against a risk that has had almost none, and it is now recorded in the law's own source record rather than left in prose here.

**Disposition: supplemental. Doc_04 is not revised, and no gravity classification changes.** Under the `cic-build-cycle` discipline this is not a null result to be waved through: had the judgment gone the other way it would have hit escalation category 4 (a finding cutting against an earlier decision) and gone to the project lead rather than being actioned by a build thread.

### 1.4 What was changed for candidate 1

`records/cappadocian/source/cappadocian.source.imperial-communion-law-of-381.md`: `verification_state` `named-not-rechecked` → `verified-direct`; the eleven-name list and the operative mechanism recorded in a new `divergence_note`; `edition`, `rights_status` and `discovery_channel` rewritten to state precisely what was read, in which editions, and — held carefully apart from the verification claim, per this Registry's own rule that acquisition status is a note fact and not a confidence downgrade — that the files are **not** on the default branch.

## 2. Candidates 2 and 3 — Firmilian, and the Thaumaturgus Canonical Epistle

**Both verified present.** Firmilian's letter is in `cic/texts/anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml` under the heading *"Epistle LXXIV — Firmilian, Bishop of Cæsarea in Cappadocia, to Cyprian, Against the Letter of Stephen. A.D. 256."* Gregory Thaumaturgus' *Canonical Epistle* is in `cic/texts/anf06_…xml` under `div3` "Acknowledged Writings." Both have Source Registry rows (69, 66) and both have WRS source records. Neither has a row in `cic/corpus-map/cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition.yaml`. All of that is exactly as reported.

**But the corpus map is the wrong lever for the concern the finding raises, and this needs saying plainly.** `cic/corpus-map/README.md` states outright: *"this sits outside `records/` entirely… touched by nothing in the compile path. Changing anything here moves no package hash and no world's content."* A corpus-map row is a bibliographic assignment. It is not what the live Representative draws on — that is `records/cappadocian/` — and adding one would not make a single word available to the voice. The discovery pass used corpus-map rows as its diagnostic for "never drawn into the world's records," and for this repository those are two different things.

**Escalated rather than actioned, on two grounds:**

1. **Scope.** The `cic-build-cycle` skill scopes a build thread's write access to its own world's build folder, and routes cross-world decisions to the project lead (escalation category 2). `cic/corpus-map/_staging/` is a shared, per-source-volume file owned by the assignment thread, and both edits are cross-world: Firmilian's letter currently sits *inside* the row for "The Epistles of Cyprian," assigned to `latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity`, `novatianism` and `donatism`; the Canonical Epistle is assigned to `anatolian-church-third-century`.
2. **One of the two needs a ruling the other does not.** The Thaumaturgus fix is a one-line, non-exclusive `atlas_ids` addition, squarely within the file's own stated "non-exclusive by default" rule and consistent with the two sibling Thaumaturgus rows that already carry the Cappadocian id. The Firmilian fix is **not** a one-line addition — it requires splitting a work out of a letters-corpus row, against that file's own stated "letters-corpus granularity rule," which is exactly the kind of call the 2026-08-26 `npnf214` split took to Mark before making.

**The exact edits, specified so they are trivial to apply if approved:**

- `cic/corpus-map/_staging/anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-…yaml`, the `work: Canonical Epistle` entry: add `cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition` to its existing `atlas_ids`, keeping `anatolian-church-third-century`. Rationale for the world's own file: Doc_02 §1.6 and Doc_01 §4 both treat the Thaumaturgan penitential canons as this world's inherited pastoral-disciplinary practice, and this world already holds the two other Thaumaturgus works from the same volume.
- `cic/corpus-map/_staging/anf05_hippolytus-…yaml`: **needs a ruling first** — whether Firmilian's Epistle LXXIV/LXXV is split out of the Cyprian letters-corpus row as its own work (following the `npnf214` precedent) or left embedded with a note. If split, `role: tradition`, `confidence: assigned`, `atlas_ids: [cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition]`, `locus: div3 "Epistle LXXIV"`, with Doc_02 §1.6's own both-numberings instruction carried in the note.

Then re-run `python cic/engine/corpus_map.py` (or the merge script) to regenerate the bucket files, which are marked "never hand-edit."

## 3. Candidate 4 — the Amphilochius voice-fragment

**Verified, and this is the real defect of the five.** `cic/texts/npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml` carries, at `div2` 17.23 (element id `xvii.xxiii`), *"From the Iambics of St. Amphilochius the Bishop to Seleucus, on the Same Subject"* — roughly 240 words in Amphilochius' own voice on the canon of Scripture. `cic/corpus-map/cappadocian-…yaml` has carried a row for it since **2026-08-26**. Doc_02 §1.6 and Source Registry row 65 both asserted, on **2026-08-31**, that "no open English edition was located this session or previously."

That is a false claim about a source's availability, sitting in this world's own cleared documents, contradicted by a file in the shared library and by a corpus-map row that predates it by five days. It is the **sixth** instance of this exact defect class in this world's build — the Registry's own living-document log records five before it (the Eupsychius homily; rows 16 and 20; and others) — and every one has been caught the same way: by opening the file rather than trusting what a document already claimed about it.

**Two things worth stating about why three review rounds did not catch it.** They could not have. The contradicting evidence was in neither the Registry nor Doc_02; it was in a shared corpus-map file that neither document cross-checks, and that the 2026-08-31 sweep's own query (Registry rows against `texts_registry.py`) did not cover either. This is a process gap between two layers, not a research failure inside one. It is worth a standing check.

**What is *not* true:** the underlying judgment row 65 exists to carry — that this world knows Amphilochius overwhelmingly as addressee and first receiver rather than through his own extended voice — is correct and survives. One canon-list extract is not an edition, and it reaches us as the NPNF editor's own epitome (his translation of the Greek as printed in Beveridge's *Synodicon* II.179), with two stretches of the enumerated book-list replaced by his bracketed summary rather than translated line by line. So the correction is a narrowing, not a reversal, and `formation_confidence: Inferential-Thin` on the figure record is deliberately **left unchanged**.

**Changed:** Doc_02 §1.6 (corrected in place, with the prior claim named rather than quietly rewritten); Source Registry row 65 (corrected) and new row 117 (the extract, Confidence A, its epitome-not-edition limit in the Verification Note rather than taken out of the tier); `cappadocian.source.amphilochius-of-iconium-own-works` (corrected, now scoped to the corpus); new record `cappadocian.source.amphilochius-iambics-to-seleucus`; `cappadocian.figure.amphilochius` (divergence note corrected, new source wired in, body line added).

## 4. Candidate 5 — Macrina the Elder

**Verified, with one correction and one addition.**

Both letters Doc_02 §1.4 names are present in `cic/texts/npnf208_basil-letters-select-works.xml` and both carry the datum. **Epistle 204** (printed heading "Letter CCIV. Placed in 375. To the Neocæsareans."; element id `ix.ccv` — the printed-vs-`div` off-by-one this build has already recorded once, checked again here rather than assumed), section 6, is the substantive one: Basil names "the celebrated Macrina who taught me the words of the blessed Gregory," kept by her "as far as memory had preserved down to her day," who "fashioned and formed me, while yet a child, upon the doctrines of piety." **Epistle 223** (`ix.ccxxiv`, "Against Eustathius of Sebasteia") carries the shorter corroborating form.

**A translation fact a later editor needs, found only by reading the letter's own prefatory note:** this volume prints Letter CCIV as a braid of two English versions, and states that "the passages in brackets are Newman's version." The Macrina passage falls wholly inside one such bracketed stretch. So it is Basil's own text in **J. H. Newman's** rendering, not the NPNF translator's. A translation question, not an authenticity one — but not the sort of thing to discover after quoting it.

**A third passage neither Doc_02 nor the Registry names:** **Epistle 210** (`ix.ccxi`, "To the notables of Neocæsarea") also says "here I was brought up by my grandmother," and the volume's own prolegomena cites Epp. 204 and 210 together for Basil's upbringing under her. It is thinner — it establishes the upbringing but not the transmission of Thaumaturgus' teaching, which is the part that makes the datum load-bearing — so the source record's citation was extended to name it rather than rewritten around it.

**The finding's characterization needed correcting on one point.** It said the datum "lives *only* in Doc_02's own prose" and that "the live Representative currently cannot draw on it at all." Not so: it is already reasoned from in two compiled records, `cappadocian.gravity.household-lineage` and `cappadocian.force.gentry-household`. **What was true is narrower and still worth fixing:** no *quote* carried it, so the compiled voice could reason from the datum but never show it in Basil's own words — in a world whose own Doc_02 §1.4 calls this "this world's single most valuable non-male-mediated female datum."

**Changed:** new record `cappadocian.quote.macrina-the-elder-taught-me` (canon cell **C-E**, "how it reached us" — one of the cells the two earlier quote batches deliberately left unsupported on fit grounds, and which this passage answers directly: it names a real chain of memory, short, regional, ending at Gregory Thaumaturgus rather than an apostle, and running through a woman, which *specifies* that cell's claim rather than contradicting it); the reciprocal `associated-with` link added to `cappadocian.dw.how-it-reached-us`; and `cappadocian.source.basil-macrina-the-elder-letters` upgraded `verified-via-authority` → `verified-direct` with the full finding recorded.

**Deliberately not done:** no figure record for Macrina the Elder. `cappadocian.figure.macrina` already states on the record that she has none because no Doc_09 story entry narrates her. A quote is not a story, and manufacturing a story to justify a figure record would invert the order this build works in. The existing decision stands and is left visible.
