# World Introduction Brief — Cappadocian Christianity (Output 8)
## Facilitator opening-moment language, calibrated by participant type

*Read by the human Facilitator. Distinct from the Facilitation Brief (Output 6, comprehensive reference): this is only the opening-moment language for bringing Chilo to the table (renamed from Eumathios, 2026-09-01 - see CAPPADOCIAN_BUILD_LEDGER.md and cappadocian_Representative_Identity_Options.md's addendum; the opening-moment scripts below never spoke the persona's name in the first place, so nothing else here changes). **Regular visitor:**
"I'd like you to meet a voice from the mountain churches of fourth-century Anatolia — an elder whose work, his whole life, has been keeping the guest-door of a community: receiving strangers, feeding the poor, teaching anyone who asks. His people fought for a generation over the exact words of their worship, and he thinks precision and kindness are the same virtue. Ask him anything. The door is kept for guests."

**Pastor or teacher:**
"The voice I have in mind for you comes from the church that gave most of us our creed — but from inside the years when that creed was still being fought for, under emperors who confessed otherwise. He's an elder of the early ascetic communities: formation by psalms and hours, wealth preached about in famine, grief handled better than almost anywhere in church history, and hard-won wisdom about ambition and calling — his world's greatest leaders all fled ordination first. I suspect you'll recognize more than you expect."

**Academic or scholar:**
"You'll be speaking with a constructed voice from the Cappadocian-Pontic churches, c. 325 to c. 394 — positioned deliberately at the brotherhoods' guest-door rather than in the Basilian circle itself, to avoid ventriloquizing the three great authors. The reconstruction is source-grounded with calibrated confidence throughout, and I want to be straight about its state: no external scholarly review yet, citations unverified, and the 'Cappadocian settlement' historiography is carried as contested rather than resolved — probe wherever you like, and what's thin or disputed will be named as exactly that."

**Deconstructing or reconstructing Christian:**
"The voice here comes from the people who fought for the creed you may have grown up saying — but from before it won, when holding it meant watching bishops you loved sent into exile. He knows religious politics from inside: ambitious clergy, councils his own teachers came home sick of, a church that used power badly when it finally had some — and he doesn't pretend otherwise. He won't try to move you anywhere. He's a witness, not a recruiter — and if anything lands hard, we can pause whenever you want."

**Universal cautions for the opening moment:** never name Basil, the Gregories, or "the Cappadocian Fathers" in the introduction itself (let the world's own encounter introduce its figures); never call the world's creed "your creed" to the participant — let them discover the connection themselves; never promise "accuracy" without the prototype caveat where the setting calls for it (Article 36); the introduction must make a guest feel received at a door, not enrolled in a seminar.
