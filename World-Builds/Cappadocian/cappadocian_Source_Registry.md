See the living-document log below for the full, honestly-stated history of what each round found and fixed.

**The checkpoint this Registry exists to satisfy:** *"Doc_02 may not name a source in support of a specific claim unless that source has a corresponding Registry row."* Every source Doc_02 §§1–6 and Doc_01 name has a row below, including sources named only in passing (a scholar cited once in §1.1 rather than listed in §5; a specific homily or letter Doc_02 mentions without repeating in its own missing-voices register). Where a source is sought-but-unlocated (Amphilochius' own works; the Nicaea subscription lists; a copyrighted translation the manifest excludes), that row is included and marked accordingly — an unlocated or unvendored source is still worth recording as a named gap, per the template's own living-document discipline, and unvendored is never the same question as Excluded (see the Confidence-axis and Boundary-axis notes below).

**Confidence axis note, read together with Doc_01/Doc_02's own Article 17 vocabulary:** this Registry's A–E axis (Citation Reliability) is a different scale from Article 17's five-level Confidence vocabulary (Documented / Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction / Contested / Inferential-Thin) that Doc_01 §5 and Doc_02 §9 use. The two are not interchangeable and this document does not conflate them: Article 17 rates how confident the *world's own reconstruction* is; this Registry's A–E rates how independently *this specific citation* has been checked — nothing more. **This revision corrects a real drift the first draft made: several rows used D or E to encode attribution doubt or acquisition status rather than citation reliability as the template actually defines it.** The template's own table is followed literally below: a source with a specific work, letter number, or locus named is at least B, whether or not it has been acquired, translated into English, or settled in its authorship — acquisition status and attribution doubt belong in the Verification Note, not in a downgraded letter grade. E is reserved for sources with no traceable identity at all (a claim resting on nothing more specific than "the tradition holds"), not for sources this project simply hasn't gotten hold of yet.

**A–B distinction held throughout:** Citation Reliability A is reserved for sources this build session itself directly verified — the files Mark supplied 2026-08-30/31 (nine Registry rows' worth: rows 18, 21, 22, 33, 48, 57, 63, 71, 72; the exact underlying file count differs slightly depending on whether the superseded Macrina introduction-only file and the paired Nazianzen invective orations are each counted separately), checked for genuine identity, completeness, and provenance before vendoring (see `CAPPADOCIAN_BUILD_LEDGER.md` §9 for the individual verification record of each). Sources vendored in earlier sessions for other worlds (the NPNF/ANF volumes, present in `cic/texts/` since 2026-08-15) are real and usable but were not re-verified by this session — they are rated B, not A, on that basis alone, regardless of how solid their content actually is. This is a distinction about *when and by whom a check happened*, not a claim that the earlier-vendored volumes are less reliable. The independent review checked every Confidence-A row against this rule directly and found no violations — that discipline is unchanged in this revision.

**Boundary axis note:** per the template, Boundary Status is assessed by what a source speaks *for* — its own subject, tradition, or evidentiary target — never by whether it happens to be copyrighted, unvendored, or of contested authorship. Part F (secondary scholarship) is entirely Native for exactly this reason: every source there is in-copyright and none is vendored, but its *subject* is this world's own material. The review checked this discipline specifically and found it correctly applied; this revision does not change it, only extends it to sources missed the first time.
