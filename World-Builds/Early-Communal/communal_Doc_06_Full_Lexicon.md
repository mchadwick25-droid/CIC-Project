# TIER 1 — FULL ENTRIES

## 1. ekklesia — the assembly

**Quick Meaning:** The gathering of the brothers and sisters itself — never a building, not yet an institution; God's called-together people, existing wherever they assemble.

**World Meaning:** In this world ekklesia is an *event with a people attached*: the same word Greek cities used for the citizen assembly, claimed by communities of a Kyrios whose citizenship is elsewhere. One is not "at church" — one *is*, with the others, the assembly. It gathers in whatever room holds it, before dawn if the working day demands, and everything the world does to form a person happens inside it or points toward it (Doc_04 G1). It is simultaneously utterly local (the faces in one room, whose conduct everyone knows) and cosmically one (letters address "the ekklesia sojourning in" each city, one people scattered; the meal-prayer asks God to gather it "from the ends of the earth into your kingdom"). Hermas is shown the ekklesia as an old woman "created first of all things, and for her sake the world was framed" — the little rented-room gathering understood, from inside, as older than the mountains.

**Ecological Function:** Load-bearing for every gravity: site of the Table, home of the Kindred, the incriminating act under the Name, the reading-room of the Deposit. The Spirit/Order tension is a fight over who speaks *in it*; the Purity/Mercy tension over who may return *to it*.

**Distortion Risk [DR — highest in lexicon]:** *Modern Hearing:* a building; a Sunday institution; a denomination; something one attends or leaves. *World Hearing:* the gathering itself; a kinship one is grafted into at cost; the one place the whole of life converges; something that can be scattered but not unjoined without unjoining life.

**Key Texts:** Did. 9.4; 1 Clem. pref.; Ign. Smyrn. 8.2; Hermas Vis. 2.4.1; Justin 1 Apol. 67; Pliny Ep. 10.96.
**Key Sources:** the full Doc_02 core corpus; Lampe for the fractionated-Rome shape.
**Related Terms:** epi to auto; adelphoi; kyriake hemera; oikos; paroikia.

## 2. adelphoi — brothers and sisters

**Quick Meaning:** The world's ordinary word for its members: kin not by blood but by baptism, with kin-obligations meant literally.

**World Meaning:** The address is a social program. A slave, a freedwoman, a shipowner, and a magistrate's cook eat at one table and call each other by the kinship word — and the obligations follow the word: the fund, the visit, the burial, the ransom, the traveler's bed. Conversion dislocates (household gods refused sever households); the assembly re-houses. Outsiders noticed the word before the doctrine: "their first lawgiver persuaded them that they are all brothers of one another" (Lucian, mocking, accurate). The word's shadow-side is equally real in the record: kin-language over unabolished structures — masters with believing slaves told not to be haughty; slaves told not to demand freedom from the common fund (Ign. Pol. 4.3). The world holds the word and the structure together without resolving them; the entry holds what the world held.

**Ecological Function:** Names G2 and funds G5's economics; makes hospitality (philoxenia) kin-duty; generates both the world's beauty (aid) and its slanders (the incest libel answered by apologists — kinship language misheard from outside).

**Distortion Risk [DR]:** *Modern Hearing:* warm congregational rhetoric; "church family" as sentiment. *World Hearing:* a claim on purse, roof, and body; kinship that outranks — without abolishing — the household one was born into; dangerous language (it cost people their families and fed accusations).

**Key Texts:** passim in every in-world letter; Lucian, Peregr. 13; Justin 1 Apol. 65, 67; Ign. Pol. 4.3; Hermas Sim. 2.
**Key Sources:** Doc_02 core; Meeks; Osiek/Balch.
**Related Terms:** koinonia; philoxenia; hagioi; chera; ekklesia.

## 3. to onoma — the Name

**Quick Meaning:** "The Name" — of the Kyrios, borne by his people: what one is baptized into, gathers in, is accused for, and may die for.

**World Meaning:** The world's own compression of its entire legal-spiritual condition. Rome's procedure made the *nomen ipsum* — the name "Christian," without further crime — the chargeable thing (Pliny), and the world answered by making the Name its treasure: baptized into it, the assemblies "walk in the Name" (Hermas), suffer "for the Name" (1 Pet. 4:16's milieu), and answer tribunals with the one sentence that is both identity and verdict: *Christianus sum / Christiane eimi*. The Name is not a label but a bond of allegiance with a rival at large — every oath by the emperor's genius, every pinch of incense, every guild dinner under a god's patronage is a small tribunal asking the same question the great one asks. Most days the answer costs a little; some days everything.

**Ecological Function:** Names G3; organizes the boundary casuistry (eidolothyta, oaths, spectacles), the confession-vocabulary (homologia, martys), and baptism's meaning (into the Name). Explains why gathering itself has confession's weight.

**Distortion Risk [DR]:** *Modern Hearing:* "in Jesus' name" as prayer-formula; religious branding; persecution as constant state terror. *World Hearing:* a legal exposure worn as identity; allegiance-language with an imperial rival; ordinary life as continuous low-grade tribunal, with the lethal edge rare, local, and remembered.

**Key Texts:** Pliny Ep. 10.96–97; Mart. Pol. 8–12; Acts of Justin; Hermas Sim. 9.28; Did. 7 (baptism into the Name).
**Key Sources:** Pliny/Trajan as anchor; Moss-line vs. martyr-memory carried [CT: felt intensity of persecution].
**Related Terms:** martys; homologia; kyrios; Christianos; baptisma.

## 4. paroikia — sojourning

**Quick Meaning:** Resident-alien existence: dwelling in a city as its lawful stranger, because one's citizenship is elsewhere — the world's deepest self-description.

**World Meaning:** The letters open with it as plain address: "the ekklesia of God *sojourning in* Rome to the ekklesia of God *sojourning in* Corinth." A paroikos in the Greek city is the resident alien: present, contributing, law-abiding — and not a citizen. The world takes the legal category and makes it theology: Christians "dwell in their own countries, but as sojourners; every foreign land is their fatherland, and every fatherland foreign" (Diognetus). This is not withdrawal (the sojourner works, marries, buries, pays) and not assimilation (the sojourner will not take the city's gods); it is the precise middle the world actually lived — in the insula, at the trade, inside the empire's peace, awaiting another polis. Hope and homelessness in one word.

**Ecological Function:** Binds G3 (the boundary) to G6 (the expectation): one refuses the city's gods *because* one belongs to a coming city. Grounds the world's political posture (prayer for rulers who may kill you — 1 Clem. 60–61) and its emotional key (longing without despair).

**Distortion Risk [DR]:** *Modern Hearing:* "this world is not my home" as escapism or cultural retreat; or the modern "parish" (the word's own descendant, now meaning settled district — a perfect inversion). *World Hearing:* engaged non-belonging; a legal metaphor everyone in a mobile empire understood in their bones; the reason both for civic obedience and for the one great refusal.

**Key Texts:** 1 Clem. pref.; Mart. Pol. pref.; Diognetus 5–6; 1 Pet. 1:1, 2:11 milieu.
**Key Sources:** in-world addresses; Diognetus with its late-date caveat.
**Related Terms:** kosmos; ethnē; parousia; to onoma; ekklesia.

## 5. eucharistia — the thanksgiving

**Quick Meaning:** Thanksgiving: the prayer over the bread and cup, the meal-act it names, and the disposition beneath both — the assembly's central act.

**World Meaning:** First a verb-world (giving thanks), then the name of the act, then of the food itself — the drift is happening inside this span. At the Lord's-Day gathering the assembly's leader gives thanks over bread and mixed cup "to the best of his ability" and all seal it with Amen; portions go by deacons' hands to whoever could not come (Justin). In the Didache's meal the cup and broken bread carry prayers of the vine of David and the scattered loaf gathered — the church's own story eaten and drunk — with meal-fullness and Maranatha at the end. Ignatius, fighting men who denied the Kyrios' flesh, calls it "the medicine of immortality" and refuses any eucharist without the assembly's order — the highest register in the corpus, and *his*, not everyone's (author-gravity flag inside the entry per Doc_03). What is common under Bradshaw-discipline: thanksgiving at a table is the center; the shapes vary; the world never explains the act's mechanics because no one had yet asked the later questions.

**Ecological Function:** G5; the Kindred made visible and edible (one loaf, mixed statuses); the Expectation prayed (Maranatha at table); the boundary enforced at its rail (confession first, reconciliation first — Did. 14; the excluded are excluded from *this*).

**Distortion Risk [DR][TC][CT — historical scope: early diversity of shape and meaning]:** *Modern Hearing:* denominational eucharistic theology read backward (transubstantiation debates, memorialism debates); a ritual wafer detached from a meal; clergy-owned sacrament. *World Hearing:* a real meal becoming a rite (unevenly, by place and decade); thanksgiving as the fundamental posture; the one table that makes slaves and masters kin for an hour and incriminates everyone at it equally.

**Key Texts:** Did. 9–10, 14; Justin 1 Apol. 65–67; Ign. Eph. 20.2, Smyrn. 6–8; Pliny Ep. 10.96 (the suspended meal).
**Key Sources:** the three worship anchors (Doc_05 §3); Bradshaw governing.
**Related Terms:** agape; kyriake hemera; ekklesia; koinonia; Maranatha.

## 6. paradosis — the handing-over

**Quick Meaning:** The deposit: what was received from the founders and must be handed on intact — teaching, forms, and Way — against forgetting and counterfeit.

**World Meaning:** This world exists one generation after its eyewitnesses, and it knows it. Its whole literature is transmission-work: Gospels written "so that you may know the security of the things you were taught"; elders appointed with succession-reasoning exactly one generation deep (1 Clem. 42/44); prophets welcomed *and tested* because the deposit can be faked (Did. 11–13: the christemporos, the Christ-trafficker, has a name because he exists); Papias still walking to question those who had heard the elders, preferring "the living and abiding voice" to books while the books multiply. Paradosis is not nostalgia; it is the survival strategy of a memory under two attacks — time's erosion and the mid-span counterfeiters who arrived organized (Marcion with his severed Bible, the Valentinian teachers with their deeper "gnosis"). The world's answer is not yet creed or closed canon; it is *practiced pattern*: what is read, prayed, eaten, and done in the assemblies, guarded by conduct-tested people.

**Ecological Function:** G4; grounds office-legitimacy (Order's side of T7), the reading-ecology, the rule-of-faith embryo, and the world's transmission to every successor world. Its failure-mode is the boundary-anxiety that hardens into heresy-hunting at the doorway.

**Distortion Risk [DR][TC]:** *Modern Hearing:* "tradition" as ossified custom opposed to scripture; or Catholic/Protestant polemics about Tradition read backward. *World Hearing:* a relay in its second leg, run under threat; scripture and tradition not yet distinguishable as rivals (the graphai are Israel's; the memoirs are the paradosis in writing); fidelity as the supreme intellectual virtue.

**Key Texts:** 1 Clem. 42, 44; Did. 11–13; Papias ap. Eus. HE 3.39; Polycarp Phil. (the working library); Justin 1 Apol. 66–67.
**Key Sources:** Doc_02 core; the Ignatian-date and canon-pace contests carried [CT].
**Related Terms:** didache; graphai; prophetes; episkopos-presbyteroi-diakonoi; hai duo hodoi.

## 7. martys — witness

**Quick Meaning:** Witness — a word walking, within this very span, from "one who testifies" to "one whose death is the testimony."

**World Meaning:** The semantic drift is the world's history written in one word. Early in the span a martys is anyone who bears witness; by Smyrna (c. 155) the assembly writes up Polycarp's death as *martyrion* with the vocabulary crystallizing around blood; by Lyons (177) the drift is so far advanced that the confessors in prison *refuse* the title — it belongs to Christ and to those already dead; they are only *homologetai*, confessors (via Eusebius). The world polices the word because it polices the reality: voluntary death-seeking is refused ("we do not praise those who hand themselves over" — Mart. Pol. 4, told of Quintus who volunteered and broke), while faithful death is honored to the bone — literally: Polycarp's bones "more precious than costly stones," gathered, and his "birthday" kept yearly. The martyr-cult of the successor worlds is born here, small and local, at a grave outside Smyrna.

**Ecological Function:** G3's extreme edge (not a separate gravity — Doc_04's declined candidate, declination carried into the entry); feeds Memory (the annual commemoration is the calendar's first Christian addition beyond Pascha and the Lord's Day); calibrates the Name's cost truthfully — rare, local, defining.

**Distortion Risk [DR][TC][CT — pace and drivers of the semantic development]:** *Modern Hearing:* "martyr complex"; constant persecution; or the modern political martyr. *World Hearing:* a courtroom word sanctified; the extreme case of an allegiance everyone practices daily in small refusals; honored precisely because not sought.

**Key Texts:** Mart. Pol. 4, 8–18; Eus. HE 5.1–2 (Lyons); Acts of Justin; Rev. 2:13 milieu (Antipas ho martys).
**Key Sources:** martyr-acts with genre-discipline (Doc_09); Moss-line carried.
**Related Terms:** homologia; to onoma; anastasis; kyriake hemera (commemoration).

## 8. kyrios — Lord

**Quick Meaning:** "Lord" — the confession-word for Jesus, sung to him "as to a god," with a living rival claimant in every oath and image.

**World Meaning:** The world's creed fits in two words — *Kyrios Iesous* — and its trial transcript in three: asked to say *Kyrios Kaisar* and curse Christ, the old man answers, "Eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my King who saved me?" (Mart. Pol. 8–9). Kyrios is the Septuagint's rendering of the divine Name, the master-word of the household (slaves knew its weight before philosophers did), and the acclamation of the Caesar — and the world uses it of a crucified man in full knowledge of all three registers. The hymn "to Christ as to a god" that Pliny extracted from apostates is this word set to music before dawn. It is allegiance-language: not one god added to the city's shelf, but the shelf refused.

**Ecological Function:** Grounds G3 (the refusal has a positive core: another King), G5 (the Lord's table, the Lord's Day), and G6 (Maranatha — *our Lord, come*). The christological insistences of the span (truly flesh, truly raised — Ignatius) are defenses of this word's referent.

**Distortion Risk [DR]:** *Modern Hearing:* pious title emptied by familiarity; "Lord" as churchy honorific. *World Hearing:* the most political word available, spoken of the wrong person on purpose; the household word every slave understood; the Name's content.

**Key Texts:** Pliny Ep. 10.96 (carmen); Mart. Pol. 8–10; Did. 10.6; Ign. passim.
**Key Sources:** Hurtado on early devotion; the anchors of Doc_05 §3.
**Related Terms:** to onoma; Christianos; Maranatha; parousia; anastasis.

**9. epi to auto — "all together in one place."** QM: the world's quasi-technical phrase for the act of assembling. WM: three independent authors use it with liturgical flavor (1 Clem. 34.7; Ign. Eph. 13.1 — "gather more frequently epi to auto, for when you are together the powers of Satan are destroyed"; Justin 1 Apol. 67.3); gathering is itself spiritual warfare and the Name's boldest ordinary act. EF: G1's verb. DR: *Modern:* attendance. *World:* the act that constitutes the ekklesia and incriminates it. Key: as cited. Related: ekklesia; kyriake hemera. [AS][TC][RT]

**10. agape — love; the love-meal.** QM: love as concrete meal-practice; the shared supper of the kindred. WM: the double sense is native — the meal is called by love's name (Ign. Smyrn. 8.2; Jude 12's milieu); its relation to eucharistia varies by place and decade [CT]. Pliny's "ordinary and harmless food," suspended under the association ban, is probably this stratum. EF: G2 enacted; G5's fuller table. DR: *Modern:* abstract unconditional love; "agape vs. eros" seminar distinctions. *World:* a supper you could be arrested for; love with dishes to wash. Key: Ign. Smyrn. 8.2; Pliny 10.96; Did. 9–10 (meal-fullness). Related: eucharistia; koinonia; adelphoi. [AS][DR][RT][CT]

**11. baptisma — the washing.** QM: the once-for-all boundary-crossing bath into the Name. WM: preceded by instruction and communal fasting; running water preferred, pragmatism permitted (Did. 7); Justin names it photismos, illumination, and "the washing of repentance and knowledge of God"; Hermas calls the water the seal the stones rise through. It is a grave and a border: what one was ends; kin, table, and exposure begin. EF: gate of G1/G2/G3; T9's premise (hence the one-repentance crisis). DR: *Modern:* denominational baptism debates; a naming ceremony. *World:* defection from the gods executed in water; the unrepeatable crossing. Key: Did. 7; Justin 1 Apol. 61, 65; Hermas Mand. 4.3, Sim. 9.16. Related: sphragis; photismos; metanoia; to onoma. [SC][TC][DR][RT]

**12. hai duo hodoi — the Two Ways.** QM: the catechetical frame: a way of life and a way of death, taught before baptism. WM: conduct-catechesis (not doctrine-first): love of God and neighbor, the second-table commandments expanded into the textures of the day — infanticide and abortion named and refused, magic refused, grudging alms refused (Did. 1–6; Barn. 18–20). The convert learns the Way by walking-list before crossing the water. EF: G4's pedagogical arm; G3's daily content. DR: *Modern:* moralism vs. grace frames. *World:* the map of the crossing; ethics as the shape of allegiance. Key: Did. 1–6; Barn. 18–20. Related: baptisma; didache; metanoia. [AS][TC][RT]

**13. episkopos / presbyteroi / diakonoi — overseer, elders, servants.** QM: the assembly's offices, in mid-settlement, plural and locally varied. WM: appointed "worthy of the Lord" beside honored prophets (Did. 15); deposed and defended (1 Clem.); urged into the single-bishop pattern by Ignatius with proving fervor; women among the diakonoi on the dominant reading of Pliny's ministrae. Authority's grounds still plural: appointment, conduct, Spirit, seniority (Doc_05 §4). EF: Order's pole of T7; G4's institutional arm. DR [extreme]: *Modern:* later hierarchy (or modern anti-hierarchy) read backward; "bishop" with mitre. *World:* overseers of a room-sized flock, known by conduct, one office-pattern among several, becoming standard as the span closes. Key: Did. 15; 1 Clem. 42/44; Ign. passim; Pliny 10.96. Related: prophetes; paradosis; ekklesia. [SC][TC][DR][PV][CT]

**14. prophetes — the prophet.** QM: Spirit-speakers, resident and traveling, honored as "your high priests" and tested by conduct. WM: free to give thanks at the meal "as much as they wish" (Did. 10.7); tested — asking for money in the Spirit is the disqualifier (Did. 11); tested again in Hermas (Mand. 11: the true prophet is known by the assembly's prayer, the false by fees and corners); women among them remembered by name (Ammia of Philadelphia, via the anti-Montanist's grudging list). By span's end the New Prophecy makes the whole question explode. EF: Spirit's pole of T7. DR: *Modern:* prediction; charismatic-vs-cessationist debates. *World:* a living office with a fraud problem, honored and audited in the same chapter. Key: Did. 10–13; Hermas Mand. 11; Eus. HE 5.17. Related: charisma; paradosis; episkopos-entry. [SC][TC][PV][RT][CT]

**15. koinonia — sharing.** QM: fellowship as material participation: common fund, shared meal, shared risk. WM: "do not turn away the needy, but share all things with your brother, and do not say they are your own" (Did. 4.8 — the manual's own words; not sustained community of goods, Doc_01); Justin 67's collection-and-distribution is koinonia administered. EF: G2's economics. DR: *Modern:* fellowship-hall sociability. *World:* purse-opening kinship; participation that costs. Key: Did. 4.8; Justin 1 Apol. 67. Related: adelphoi; philoxenia; agape. [SC][DR][RT]

**16. philoxenia — hospitality.** QM: love of the stranger: the network's connective practice — bed, bread, letter, send-off. WM: with its audit (Did. 11–12; two days honored, the sponger named — christemporos); commended as the virtue of the great (1 Clem. 10–12 on Abraham, Lot, Rahab). EF: the network's circulatory system (Doc_05 §2). DR: *Modern:* entertaining friends. *World:* vetted, costly openness to unknown kin; the empire crossed on it. Key: Did. 11–12; 1 Clem. 10–12; 3 John milieu. Related: koinonia; adelphoi; paradosis (travelers carry it). [SC][RT]

**17. metanoia — repentance.** QM: the turning: entry's precondition and, once after baptism, the fallen member's one return. WM: Hermas' angel of repentance grants one post-baptismal metanoia — against rigorists (no return) and presumers (endless returns); the assembly confesses before the meal (Did. 4.14; 14.1). EF: T9's procedure. DR: *Modern:* feeling sorry; routine confession. *World:* a numbered mercy; the tower-stones' one re-cutting. Key: Hermas Mand. 4.3; Did. 14.1; 2 Clem. 8. Related: baptisma; dipsychia; ekklesia (discipline). [SC][DR][RT]

**18. dipsychia — double-mindedness.** QM: Hermas' name for the world's besetting disease: the divided heart hedging between two allegiances. WM: doubt in prayer, wealth-anxiety, boundary-hedging — the sojourner's characteristic temptation (author-gravity flag: Hermas-specific coinage; the *condition* is corpus-wide). EF: the emotional ecology's negative pole. DR: *Modern:* indecisiveness. *World:* spiritual bigamy; the incense-pinch of the heart. Key: Hermas Mand. 9; Vis. 3. Related: metanoia; pistis; kosmos. [AS][DR]

**19. eidololatria / eidolothyta — idolatry / idol-food.** QM: the gods' service, and meat from their altars: the boundary's daily casuistry. WM: "concerning food, bear what you can, but keep strictly from that offered to idols, for it is the worship of dead gods" (Did. 6.3) — the one non-negotiable inside a bearable-burden frame; the market, guild, and festival as daily tribunal. EF: G3's ordinary content. DR: *Modern:* "idols of the heart" only — metaphor without menu. *World:* an actual dinner invitation problem; the visible edge of allegiance. Key: Did. 6.3; Rev. 2 milieu; apologists passim. Related: to onoma; kosmos; hai duo hodoi. [SC][DR][RT]

**20. kyriake hemera — the Lord's Day.** QM: the first day, kept weekly for assembly and the meal — creation's day and resurrection's. WM: Did. 14.1 ("on the Lord's own day gather and break bread"); Ign. Magn. 9.1 (living kata kyriaken, "no longer sabbathizing" — the calendar as identity, with its Israel-question edge); Justin 67 (the day's double rationale). A working day: observance costs sleep, not leisure. EF: G1's clock; G5's schedule; T8 visible in the calendar. DR: *Modern:* weekend; "sabbath" conflation. *World:* the week's secret summit before work. Key: as cited; Rev. 1:10. Related: ekklesia; eucharistia; anastasis. [SC][DR][RT]

**21. graphai — the scriptures.** QM: Israel's scriptures in Greek — the world's Bible — with the apostles' memoirs newly read beside them. WM: read aloud "as long as time permits"; fought over (whose book? — Trypho's question and Marcion's knife); the two-Testament decision is this span's permanent bequest. EF: G4's textual body; T8's battlefield. DR: *Modern:* leather-bound complete Bible. *World:* scrolls and codices owned corporately, heard not read, claimed against two rivals. Key: Justin 1 Apol. 67; Dial. passim; 2 Clem. 2. Related: paradosis; hoi hypokritai (T8); didache. [SC][TC][DR][RT][CT — canon pace]

**22. parousia — the arrival.** QM: the Lord's awaited coming: the world's forward horizon. WM: prayed at table (Maranatha), rehearsed in the manual's last chapter (Did. 16: "be watchful... the Lord comes"), stretched by delay and renegotiated (Hermas' pause "for your sake"); developmental "cooling" narrative Contested — the synchronic evidence shows a hope woven into ordinary practice rather than a fever subsiding. EF: G6. DR: *Modern:* rapture-charts; or embarrassed dismissal. *World:* direction more than date; the sojourner's homeward lean. Key: Did. 10.6, 16; Hermas Vis. 3; Justin 1 Apol. 52. Related: Maranatha; paroikia; basileia; anastasis. [SC][DR][RT][CT]
