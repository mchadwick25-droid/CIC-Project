## 1. The Label Test: Does "Early Communal" Hold?

**Finding: a genuine, coherent formation world exists in the territory the label points toward, but the label itself both over-claims and under-specifies. Both failure modes are named below; a bounded self-description is recommended.**

### What territory the evidence actually supports

There is a recoverable formation ecology, distinct from every other registered world, in the period **between the passing of the founding eyewitness generation and the emergence of the visibly institutional, property-holding, school-and-basilica church of the third century**: Christians gathering in ordinary domestic and working spaces, in small assemblies knit into a trans-local network by letters and travelers, without purpose-built buildings, without a settled empire-wide clergy profile, under a legal regime in which the Name itself was punishable but enforcement was local, sporadic, and accusation-driven. Its documentary spine is the literature conventionally called the Apostolic Fathers (Didache, 1 Clement, the letters of Ignatius, Polycarp, the Shepherd of Hermas, 2 Clement, Barnabas, the Martyrdom of Polycarp), the earliest apologists (Justin above all), and the earliest outside witnesses (Pliny, Tacitus, Lucian, Celsus). That this cluster shares a recurring ecology — assembly, meal, fictive kinship, boundary-under-watch, tradition-anxiety — is developed and tested in Doc_02 and Doc_04; at this step it is asserted only as the candidate territory.

### Where "Early" over-claims

Read without qualification, "Early" claims the apostolic generation itself — the events of c. 30–70, the missions of Peter and Paul, the composition context of the earliest epistles. **This world does not claim that era.** Three reasons, in descending order of weight:

1. **Constitutional prudence (governing):** the apostolic era is the shared foundational memory of every subsequent Christian world and of every living tradition. A formation world that claimed to *be* the apostolic church would place the architecture in the position of adjudicating what every living communion holds as its own origin — the maximal Living Tradition hazard (Constitution Articles 28–29). The apostolic events belong to this world as **inherited memory carried into it** (an initiating internal force, §4 below), exactly as the Cyprianic inheritance was carried into Early Latin without being annexed by it.
2. **Evidential character:** reconstruction of the apostolic generation is the most contested terrain in the entire field; nearly every structural claim about it is Contested or worse. A world built there could not meet the confidence discipline.
3. **Ecological distinctness:** the generation that had living eyewitnesses among it, and the Temple still standing, was formed differently from the generation that had neither. The world begins precisely where that difference begins.

### Where "Early" under-specifies (the end boundary)

"Early" gives no ending. Without one, the label drifts toward everything pre-Nicene, which would swallow the third century — Cyprian's episcopal Africa, the formalized catechumenate, purpose-built and adapted church property (the Dura-Europos house-church renovation, c. 232–256, is the earliest surviving example), the Decian empire-wide enforcement of 250 that made lapse-and-restoration the church's central institutional crisis, and Alexandria's school under Origen. All of that is a recognizably different ecology: the church as a visible, propertied, mass institution negotiating with an empire that now saw it whole. It also overlaps territory already registered to Alexandria (150–400 CE, school-centered).

### Where "Communal" risks mis-claiming

Two distinct distortions, both flagged now because they will follow this world through every later document:

1. **The property-communism hearing.** A modern reader hears "communal" through Acts 2:44–45 ("all things in common"). Sustained community of goods is *not* documented as this world's normal economic form; what is documented is intense mutual aid — the common fund, hospitality, prison support, care of widows and orphans (Justin, 1 Apol. 67; Lucian, Peregrinus; Aristides) — inside ordinary household economics. The label must not smuggle in a romantic economic claim.
2. **The implied contrast.** "Communal" could imply that later worlds were not communal. They were. What the label rightly points at is narrower and defensible: **in this world the gathered community is itself the entire formation apparatus.** There is no school beside it, no monastery beyond it, no basilica or public liturgical order above it, no professional theological class within it (Justin's free-lance school in his Roman lodging is the embryo of an alternative, at the world's outer edge). Formation happens in the assembly or it does not happen. That is the sense in which this world, uniquely among the registered worlds, is *communal*: not more loving than its successors, but structurally coextensive with its gatherings.

### Boundary decision — beginning: c. 70

Taken as shorthand for the cumulative rupture of 64–70: the Neronian violence in Rome (Tacitus, Ann. 15.44 — the first state action that treats the Name as punishable), the deaths of the founding generation's central figures within living memory of that decade (Widely Accepted at the level of the generational fact; individual traditions vary in strength), and the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple in 70, which removed the geographic and cultic center of the movement's parent matrix and accelerated the long, uneven separation from the synagogue. After c. 70 the communities demonstrably reorganize around the problem this world never stops solving: **how to remain what the eyewitnesses founded, without the eyewitnesses** — the generation of written Gospels, of paradosis-language, of appointed and contested successions. Confidence: **Widely Accepted** as a genuine ecological break; the specific choice of c. 70 over c. 90 (death of the last eyewitnesses per tradition) is a construction judgment — c. 70 is preferred because the forces (Neronian precedent, Temple's fall, first succession arrangements) are datable events rather than traditional lifespans, but the first generation after 70 remains the world's thinnest-evidenced stretch and is treated as its penumbra, not its documented heartland. Flagged for external review.

### Boundary decision — ending: c. 200, soft edge

The world's distinct form dissolves gradually across roughly 180–230; c. 200 is the construction cut, chosen because the two decades on either side of it contain nearly every marker of transition:

- **Institutional:** the monarchical episcopate, still being *urged* by Ignatius (early-to-mid second century; date contested, see Doc_02) and demonstrably not yet the government of Rome's fractionated house churches through most of the second century (Lampe's reconstruction — Dominant Modern Reconstruction), is by the 190s ordinary enough that Victor of Rome can attempt to excommunicate the churches of Asia over the date of Pascha (Eusebius, HE 5.24) — a bishop acting trans-locally with assumed authority. The world's characteristic condition (order still contestable, offices still plural and local) is over.
- **Intellectual:** professional Christian literary culture arrives — Irenaeus (c. 180) writing systematic heresiology at book length, Clement teaching in Alexandria (c. 180–202), Tertullian in Carthage (fl. c. 197–), the catechetical school as an institution. Teaching detaches from the assembly and acquires its own institutions; that detachment is precisely the boundary with the Alexandria world (registered scope 150–400).
- **Material:** the world's defining material signature — total archaeological invisibility, worship in unmarked ordinary space — begins to fail: the earliest identifiably Christian art and burial (c. 200 onward), the Abercius inscription (c. 190s; Christian reading dominant though once contested), adapted church property in the early third century.
- **Boundary-legal:** the accusation-driven Trajanic regime ("they are not to be hunted") persists past 200, but the Severan period brings larger, more organized local persecutions, and Decius (250) changes the game's nature entirely. The legal edge is therefore the *softest* of the four markers and does not carry the cut by itself.

Confidence: **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for the coherence of a c. 200 transformation point — the individual markers are Documented; the judgment that they compose a world-boundary is a construction decision, and it is the build's softest edge. Irenaeus, Melito, Theophilus and the other figures of c. 170–200 stand *in the doorway*: their evidence is used for the world where it attests continuing communal ecology, and marked as transitional where it attests the successor world. Flagged for external review with specific attention requested to whether c. 190 (Victor/Polycrates) or c. 235 (pre-Decian) would be more defensible cuts.

### Geographic scope

The Greek-speaking urban network of the eastern Mediterranean and its western colonies: **Syria and Antioch** (Didache's milieu per the dominant reconstruction; Ignatius), **Asia Minor** (the densest single region: Pliny's Bithynia-Pontus, the seven assemblies of Revelation, Polycarp's Smyrna, Papias' Hierapolis, Melito's Sardis, the Phrygian countryside where the New Prophecy erupts at the world's edge), **Greece and the Aegean** (Corinth as addressee of 1 Clement and Dionysius' network of letters), **Rome** (1 Clement, Hermas, Justin, the immigrant Greek-speaking congregations — Rome's Christianity remains Greek-speaking through this entire world; Latin Christianity begins only at the world's outer edge, Scillitan Acts 180). **Participating but thin:** Egypt (documented Christian presence; almost no in-span ecology evidence outside later papyri — and what emerges c. 180+ belongs to Alexandria's registered world), Gaul (a single, blinding flash of evidence: the Lyons letter of 177 via Eusebius), North Africa (entering the record precisely as the world ends), Edessa/East Syria (traditions late and legendary — Inferential/Thin). **Palestine after 70:** the remnant Jerusalem community and the Jewish-Christian groups are part of this world's deepest internal tension but are evidenced almost wholly through later, hostile, or legendary sources (Inferential/Thin to Contested). Confidence: Documented for the named centers; the network-shape claim (that these assemblies understood themselves as one trans-local brotherhood — 1 Peter's "your brotherhood throughout the world," the letter-carrying habit) is Widely Accepted.

### Cultural scope

Greek-speaking, urban-centered, socially mixed. The dominant modern reconstruction (Meeks and successors) places the assemblies among artisans, traders, householders, freedpersons, and slaves, with occasional members of higher status — not a proletarian mass movement and not an elite one. Enslaved believers are documented inside the assemblies both as members and as officeholders (Pliny's two ancillae "called ministrae"); so are slaveholding believers (Ignatius to Polycarp 4.3) — an uncomfortable documented reality this world carries without resolution. Women are documented as hosts, patrons, prophets, deacons and (increasingly) enrolled widows, within a constraining trajectory that is itself Contested scholarship (see Doc_02 §6). The world's parent matrix is diaspora Judaism, and its long, uneven, regionally variable separation from the synagogue — with some communities remaining Torah-observant, some fiercely supersessionist, most somewhere unrecorded — is among its defining cultural facts; the older "parting of the ways" model versus the "ways that never parted" revision is carried as a named tension, not resolved. Rural participation is attested (Pliny: the contagion has spread "not only through the cities but also through the villages and farms"; Justin: "those who live in the country" gather with the city assembly) and almost entirely invisible in detail.

### Recommended naming

Retain the registry identifier **Early Communal** (stable architectural identifier, code `communal`), with the world's own working self-description: **"the assemblies of the brothers and sisters — the household churches of the Greek-speaking Mediterranean from the fall of the Temple to the generation of the schools (c. 70 – c. 200)."** The self-description deliberately uses the world's own vocabulary: *ekklesia* rendered as "assembly" (the word meant the gathering, not a building or an institution — the single most important translation decision this world requires); *adelphoi* as "brothers and sisters" (the world's ordinary term for its members). Plain statement for the record: "Early Communal" read without these boundaries would wrongly claim the apostolic generation on one side and the pre-Nicene institutional church on the other; both are adjacent worlds (the first unregistered and constitutionally hazardous to build; the second partially registered as Alexandria and otherwise unregistered).

## 2. Distinct World Criteria (summary against Construction Framework Part I)

- **Recurring formation ecology** (elements Documented; the pattern Widely Accepted): weekly assembly on the Lord's Day in domestic or ordinary rented space; the meal (eucharist and agape, their relation shifting across the span — Contested in detail); baptism as the boundary-crossing after a period of instruction in the Way; the Two Ways moral catechesis; fasting Wednesdays and Fridays; the common fund and its distribution to orphans, widows, prisoners, the sick, and traveling strangers; hospitality and letter-networks binding assemblies together; testing of traveling prophets and teachers; exclusion and restoration of the erring.
- **Recurring gravitational questions** (Documented as questions; their organization tested at Doc_04): how the assembly stays one and holy while meeting in scattered households (order, offices, schism — 1 Clement's whole occasion); how to keep the deposit now that the eyewitnesses are gone (paradosis, false-prophet anxiety, the writing and gathering of Gospels); how to live as confessors of one Kyrios inside a city full of other lords (idol-food, oaths, spectacles, the Name before magistrates); how long until the Lord comes, and what his delay means (Didache 16, Hermas' tower "still being built"); what Israel's scriptures and practices are to assemblies increasingly gentile (the world's deepest unresolved wound).
- **What distinguishes it from adjacent worlds:** see §1 and the continuity table below.
- **World continuity and distinction:** inherits from the apostolic generation its Kyrios-confession, its scriptures (Israel's, in Greek), its meal and baptism, its itinerant-network habit, and the memory of the founders; transmits to the third-century worlds a functioning trans-local episcopal order, a nearly-formed fourfold Gospel and Pauline collection, the martyr-cult in embryo (Polycarp's bones "more precious than gold"), the catechumenate in embryo, and the apologetic vocabulary. Continuity acknowledged; distinct ecological function demonstrated by the transformed function of shared material (e.g., in this world *martys* is still completing its journey from "witness" to "one whose death is the witness" — the semantic drift happens inside this span and is itself evidence; in successor worlds the martyr is an established cultic category).

**Differentiation against the four registered worlds** (developed fully at Doc_02/Doc_07; asserted here as boundary rationale):

| Adjacent world | Shared material | Decisive ecological difference |
|---|---|---|
| Alexandria (150–400) | Greek language; scripture; overlap 150–200 | Teacher-and-school as formation axis vs. assembly-as-formation-apparatus; Alexandria's world has an institution this world lacks by definition |
| Early Latin (c. 313–460) | Martyr memory; episcopal office; scripture | Opposite social position: established basilica church vs. legally exposed household assemblies; "the peace of the Church" vs. the accusation regime |
| Desert Christianity (4th–5th c.) | Renunciation impulse; Two Ways moral seriousness | Withdrawal *from* an established church vs. a world with nothing to withdraw from — the city assembly is the whole ecology |
| Nicene-Cappadocian (4th c.) | Kyrios-confession; scripture | Conciliar-imperial doctrinal definition vs. pre-creedal rule-of-faith fluidity; empire as patron vs. empire as hazard |

## 3. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

**Finding: strand-singular at the resolution the evidence permits — with the determination's honest weakness stated plainly and one declined candidate carried as the world's major internal plurality.**

Three candidate strand-divisions were tested:

1. **Torah-observant ("Jewish-Christian") assemblies vs. gentile-majority assemblies — DECLINED as a formal strand, carried as major internal plurality.** This is the strongest candidate and the closest call in this document. That communities of Jesus-confessors continuing Jewish practice existed through this span is Widely Accepted (Ignatius' polemic against sabbath-keeping and "Judaismos" in Asia Minor presupposes the live practice — against-the-grain evidence; the Didache's milieu stands visibly close to Jewish practice; Justin, Dial. 47, explicitly discusses Torah-observant believers and disagreements over their standing). But a formal strand requires *named evidence of a distinct formation pattern*, and nearly everything we possess about these communities is polemical, retrospective (Irenaeus' and later writers' "Ebionites"), or inferential. Their own voices do not survive. To erect a strand on hostile testimony would violate the evidence discipline; to deny the plurality would falsify the world. Disposition: strand status declined at **Contested/Inferential-Thin**; the plurality is carried at full strength through Doc_02 §6 (structurally suppressed voices), Doc_04 (the Israel tension as gravity), and the Facilitation Brief. **Flagged as the single determination most in need of external scholarly review.**
2. **Regional cultures (Syrian / Asian / Roman-Greek) — declined as strands, recorded as variation.** Real regional texture exists (Asia Minor's prophetic-apocalyptic intensity; Rome's fractionated plurality of immigrant congregations; Syria's itinerant-prophet economy), but the shared formation pattern across regions (assembly, meal, kinship, boundary, deposit) is demonstrably stronger than the variations, and the sources cross regions constantly (Ignatius of Antioch writing to Asia and Rome; 1 Clement from Rome to Corinth; Polycarp of Smyrna in Rome). Variation, not strand.
3. **"Trajectories" or school-communities (Johannine circle, Pauline network, etc.) — declined.** The older scholarly practice of reconstructing distinct "communities behind the texts" as sociologically separate churches is itself now heavily contested; building strands on it would embed a contested scholarly framework as world structure.

A world without established strands is treated as strand-singular throughout all subsequent steps. Cross-strand gravity testing at Doc_04 is therefore replaced by **cross-regional and cross-source-type testing** (does a candidate gravity hold in Syria, Asia, Greece, and Rome; in community manuals, letters, apocalypses, apologies, and outside testimony?) — a stricter substitute deliberately adopted because the strand-singular finding here rests on thinner ground than Early Latin's three-strand finding did.

## 4. Preliminary Forces Identification (Forces Framework Step 1 — orientation only)

**What generated this world:** the removal, within a single generation, of everything that had located the movement — the founding eyewitnesses (dead), the Temple (destroyed), the synagogue (increasingly closed to them), and legal innocuousness (gone since Nero made the Name actionable) — falling upon communities already formed with a Kyrios-confession, a meal, a baptism, and a network habit. The world is what those communities built to survive that fourfold removal.

**What larger world it was embedded in:** the high Roman Empire at its most stable — its roads, shipping lanes, and Greek koine making the network possible; its cities organized around cult (civic gods, trade-guild gods, household gods, the emperor's genius) making the Christians' refusal conspicuous at every meal, market, and festival; its associational culture (collegia, burial clubs, philosophical schools, synagogues) offering the templates neighbors used to parse what Christians were; its household structure providing the assembly's given social container and its deepest internal contradictions (slaves and masters at one table).

**What it was responding to:** the memory crisis (how does the Way outlive its eyewitnesses — written Gospels, guarded paradosis, appointed elders, tested prophets); the accusation regime (not empire-wide hunting but permanent local vulnerability — Pliny's procedure and Trajan's rescript define the world's legal weather); the rival-teacher flood from within (Didache's tests, 1 John's secessionists, and at mid-span the two great organized alternatives, Marcion and the Valentinian teachers, both operating *inside* the assemblies' own cities and vocabulary); the delay of the parousia; the unresolved question of Israel.

**What it was refusing:** the gods — civic, household, and imperial — and every practice that acknowledged them (idol-food, images, oaths by the emperor's genius, the spectacles); the synagogue's full Torah requirement for gentiles *and* Marcion's severed Bible (the world's two-front refusal that defines its scripture-posture: Israel's scriptures kept, Israel's practice not required); docetic denial that the Kyrios truly ate, suffered, and died (Ignatius' war); the voluntary pursuit of martyrdom (Mart. Pol. 4: "we do not praise those who hand themselves over"); and the surrounding culture's verdict that they were a superstitio — atheists, haters of humankind.

**How this orientation governs Doc_02 scope:** seek the outside witnesses (Pliny, Tacitus, Lucian, Celsus, Galen) as first-class evidence, since the world's own writers rarely describe what neighbors saw; treat the community manuals and letters (Didache, 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Hermas) as the ecological core rather than the later theological histories; expect and name the fourth-century Eusebian filter through which most narrative evidence arrives; expect meaningful silences from the Torah-observant, from the rural, from the enslaved except as objects, from women except at moments of crisis or as addressees, and from every group later orthodoxy did not preserve — and treat those silences as transmission effects to be named, not as absence of life; watch the material record's near-total emptiness as itself a primary datum about how this world inhabited space.

## 5. Confidence Summary for Doc_01

- World coherence as a distinct formation ecology: **Widely Accepted** (construction synthesis of Documented elements; the synthesis itself is this build's claim and is tested at Doc_04).
- Beginning boundary c. 70: **Widely Accepted** as an ecological break; the specific cut a construction judgment, flagged.
- Ending boundary c. 200: **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** — the build's softest edge; flagged for external review with alternatives named (c. 190; c. 235).
- Refusal of the apostolic era: constitutional and evidential judgment, stated openly; not itself a historical claim.
- Geographic scope: **Documented** for centers; network-unity claim **Widely Accepted**; Egypt/Palestine/Edessa thinness stated.
- Strand determination (strand-singular; Jewish-practice plurality carried, not stranded): **the build's most reviewable single finding** — the finding is a discipline-driven declination, not a confident historical claim; recorded at **Contested/Inferential-Thin** for the declined candidate.
- Preliminary forces: orientation only; no confidence claims beyond the Documented events named.

**Evidentiary character statement (carried through the whole build):** this world's source base is thinner, later-filtered, and more genre-difficult than that of any other registered world. Its two best descriptions of its own worship come from a hostile Roman governor and an apologist writing for outsiders. Its narrative history arrives almost entirely through a fourth-century editor. Its material record is, for most of the span, silence. The build therefore expects — and will report as a true finding, not a defect — a higher proportion of Contested and Inferential/Thin material than Early Latin carried. Where the honest answer is "we cannot recover this," that answer will be given.

**Open questions deliberately left open for later steps:** whether the assembly-as-formation-apparatus is the confirmed primary gravity or whether kinship or boundary-under-watch organizes more (Doc_04 only); whether the Didache's community should be treated as typical or as an outlier close to the Jewish-practice plurality (Doc_02); how much of Justin's worship description generalizes beyond Rome (Doc_02/Doc_05, Bradshaw-minimalism applied); whether the eschatological expectation "cooled" across the span or merely changed registers (Doc_04/Doc_05 — the cooling narrative is itself contested).
