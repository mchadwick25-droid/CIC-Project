# World Introduction Brief — Early Communal (communal)
## Participant-facing introduction, by participant type

**For the curious newcomer:** Before Christianity had church buildings, creeds, or legal standing, it had rooms, tables, and people. For about five generations — from just after the apostles died until around the year 200 — being a Christian meant belonging to a small assembly that met in someone's house before dawn: a meal called thanksgiving, a family made by baptism rather than blood, letters carried between cities, and a Name you could be brought before a governor for. Chloe kept one of those doors. Ask her anything about how it actually worked.

**For the participant from a church background:** Every Christian tradition looks back to this world — which is exactly why it surprises. You will find things missing that you may assume were always there (buildings, settled clergy, a finished New Testament, agreed answers) and things present that later centuries lost (prophets tested at the gathering, the meal as an actual meal, belonging that cost something on entry). Chloe will not tell you your church does it wrong or right; she will tell you how it was among her people, including what they argued about and never settled.

**For the skeptical or scholarly participant:** This world is reconstructed from a thin, difficult record — community manuals, occasional letters, one long visionary text, a hostile governor's interrogation report, a satirist, a philosopher's sneer — and the reconstruction says so. Chloe distinguishes what her people's memory holds firmly from what it holds thinly; press her on her edges and she will name them rather than improvise. The full scholarly apparatus behind her (sources, confidence levels, contested questions) is available at any depth you ask for.

**For the participant carrying a personal question:** This is a world that knew costly belonging, loss of family, fear, failure, and long waiting — and answered them with a table, a kinship, and a hope, not with formulas. Chloe will meet a serious question seriously. She will also tell you honestly that she is a voice from a far time: what she can offer is how her people lived such questions, and the encouragement to find living hands in your own day.
