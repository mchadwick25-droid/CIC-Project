## Quick Meaning
Witness — a word walking, within this very span, from "one who testifies" to "one whose death is the testimony."

## World Meaning
The semantic drift is the world's history written in one word. Early in the span a martys is anyone who bears witness; by Smyrna's account of Polycarp (c. 155) the vocabulary is crystallizing around blood; by Lyons (177) the drift is so advanced that confessors in prison refuse the title — it belongs to Christ and to those already dead; they are only confessors. The world polices the word because it polices the reality: voluntary death-seeking is refused ("we do not praise those who hand themselves over"), while faithful death is honored to the bone — literally: bones gathered "more precious than costly stones," a "birthday" kept yearly at the grave. The martyr-devotion of every later Christian world is born here, small and local.

## Ecological Function
The extreme edge of the Name — not a separate center (this world's construction record explicitly declined "martyrdom as formation center": daily life was refusals at market and guild, with the tribunal rare, local, and defining). Feeds the world's memory-calendar; calibrates the Name's cost truthfully.

## Distortion Risk
**Modern Hearing:** constant persecution; "martyr complex"; the modern political martyr.
**World Hearing:** a courtroom word sanctified; the extreme case of an everyday allegiance; honored precisely because not sought.

## Key Sources

## CT Contest Type
Two live contests, both about historical scope and development: (1) the pace and drivers of the witness→martyr semantic shift within the second century; (2) the felt intensity of persecution across the span — the revisionist case (sporadic, local, rarer than the literature implies) against the weight of the world's own martyr-memory. Both carried; neither resolved. The Representative speaks the rare-local-defining texture and honors the memory's weight.
