## Story Text
It is still dark when they come — by ones and twos, not together, through the courtyard of an ordinary house in an ordinary street. The room holds perhaps thirty: a household with its slaves, a shipwright, two widows the assembly keeps, a silversmith's freedwoman, children half-asleep. It is the first day of the week; work begins at sunrise, so they begin before it. One who can read stands and reads — from the prophets, and from the memoirs of the apostles — as long as time permits. The one who presides speaks on what was read, urging them to imitate it. They stand and pray together; they greet one another with the kiss. Bread is brought, and a cup of wine mixed with water; the presider gives thanks over them as he is able, and all answer Amen. The deacons hand the gifts around, and set aside portions to carry to those who could not come — the sick, the imprisoned. Those who are able and willing put something in freely; it stays with the presider, who is father to the orphans and widows from it. Before full light, they have gone out again by ones and twos, into the city, to work.

## Formation Ecology Connection
The whole formation apparatus in one hour: the reading world's teaching, the kinship enacted (kiss, gifts, portions carried), the thanksgiving at the center, the collection as ministry, the low profile as the boundary's shape, the working-day cost of the Lord's Day.

## Source Identification
- Fixed day, pre-dawn hour, arrival pattern implied by legal exposure: Pliny, Ep. 10.96.
- Reading of memoirs and prophets "as long as time permits"; presider's exhortation; standing prayer; bread and mixed cup; thanksgiving "as he is able"; Amen; deacons' distribution and portions to the absent; free-will collection kept by the presider for orphans, widows, sick, prisoners, strangers; first-day rationale: Justin, 1 Apol. 65–67.
- Kiss: Justin, 1 Apol. 65.
- Reconciliation/confession before the breaking (available variant detail): Didache 14.
- Mixed social composition incl. slaves, widows, freedpersons, children: Pliny 10.96 ("every age, every rank, both sexes"); Celsus ap. Origen 3.55; assembly-supported widows (Justin 67; Polycarp 4).
- Working-day context: first-day observance in a world with no Christian holiday (inference from calendar, flagged in construction record).
- Venue range: domestic-and-ordinary spaces (construction record's Adams-tension carried: "an ordinary house" is one attested option, not the only one).
