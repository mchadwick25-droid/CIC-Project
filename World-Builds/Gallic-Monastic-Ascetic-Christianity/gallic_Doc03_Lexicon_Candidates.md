## 0. What this document is, and three disciplines it applies

**What it is.** A working candidate list, organized by this world's own conceptual clusters, with preliminary source-grounded definitions, Origin and Risk/Function tags, provisional tier estimates, strand notes, and Author Gravity flags — so that Doc_04's gravity discovery can be conducted in this world's own words rather than in modern analytical vocabulary. **What it is not:** it is not Doc_06. No entry below carries a World Meaning, Ecological Function, or Distortion Risk pairing at Tier 1 depth; the Lexicon Framework (Part I) warns that "premature development at the candidate stage risks anchoring later full entries to an early, less-considered framing," and this document takes that warning literally.

**Discipline 1 — read, don't infer (Doc_01 §10, Doc_02 preamble).** Every term below was found in a passage read in the vendored file during this pass. The Document Log (§11) records exactly which sections were read in full and which were not. Where a term is prominent in a section *not* read (e.g., Conferences IV–VIII, XV–XVII, XIX–XXIV; *De Incarnatione*; the *Sacred History* apart from the Priscillianist chapters), that is stated as a coverage limit, not silently filled in.

**Discipline 2 — name the layer (Doc_02 preamble).** This world's vocabulary reaches this document through three nineteenth-century translators (Roberts, Heurtley, Gibson) and, for Gennadius, Richardson. Where a translator's footnote supplies the Latin lemma behind an English rendering (e.g., *virtus*, *gratia*, *cilicium*, *sacramentum*, *papa*, *cingulum*, *monasterium*, *acedia*), the lemma is recorded and attributed to the footnote, not to the ancient author. Where no footnote gives a lemma, this document's actual practice — stated here as it is, not as the first draft claimed it (Round 1 finding S5) — is to supply a conventional Latin lemma in the heading from general knowledge of monastic Latin **and to mark it "unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation."** The Round 1 reviewer's word-boundary search of the whole `npnf211` file found zero occurrences of, among others, *puritas*, *discretio*, *depositum*, *renuntiatio*, *compunctio*, *monachus*, *cella*, *magister*, *cogitationes*; an unverified lemma is a search aid for Doc_06, not an attestation, and carries no confidence of its own. Four lemmas the reviewer found corpus-supported only in the editorial layer (*fratres*, *professio*, *regula*, *perseverantia*) are cited to their exact editorial locus in their entries (1.6, 1.9, 3.6, 5.4) — the *regula* witness is a Rule-of-Benedict footnote and is flagged as Registry-row-36 territory. One lemma, *collatio* (3.5), is an active trap — it occurs in the corpus only in an unrelated sense — and its heading says so. **Doc_06 owes a Latin-lemma verification pass** against a Latin edition before any Tier 1 entry is written (§10, Open Item 1). Editorial glosses (Roberts's miracle-skepticism notes, Gibson's Benedictine cross-references, Warfield's "Massilians" note, Heurtley's appendices) are never treated as the world's own voice.

**Discipline 3 — three voices, not one.** Doc_02 §1 establishes that this world's Native primary corpus is dominated by three authors — Sulpitius Severus, John Cassian, Vincent of Lérins — plus context-only Augustine and corroborating Gennadius. CF V7.4 Step 3 requires flagging "terms that appear to carry unusual weight from specific voices." This document therefore records, for every term, **which of the three voices actually use it** as read, and flags as an Author Gravity risk any term attested in only one voice. This is the single most important classification in the document for Doc_04's purposes, and the one most likely to be wrong in detail — it is stated per term so that a reviewer can check it against the file rather than take it on trust.

**Strand attribution.** Doc_01 §6 records this world as **provisionally strand-singular** ("per the Framework's own stated default, pending Doc_04"), with the one-world-or-two-worlds question (Tours vs. Lérins–Marseilles) explicitly reserved for Doc_04 and, if it turns out to be a lineage of two, for a Step 0/Atlas-level act. This document therefore does **not** split any term by strand. It does, however, record for each term whether it is attested in the **northern node's voice (Sulpitius/Martin)**, the **southern node's voice (Cassian, Vincent)**, or both — because that node-attestation pattern is precisely the evidence Doc_04 will need if it tests the two-strand hypothesis, and because it costs nothing to record now. "Node" is used here as a descriptive label for where the attestation sits, not as a strand claim.

**Tag key (Lexicon Framework Part II).** Origin: **[AS]** Signature Vocabulary (distinctive to this world — the Framework's test is *comparative across worlds*: "unlikely to carry equivalent weight in neighboring worlds," and `desert-monasticism`, Doc_01 §8.2, is a built and live neighbour whose founding literature is Cassian's own text; so, after Round 1 (S1), vocabulary Cassian himself frames as received Egyptian teaching is tagged [SC] with a "received" note, and [AS] is kept only where the term is this world's own — Vincent's rule, deposit, progress-vs-alteration, Commonitory; Cassian's own formulation of the grace argument and of co-operation; Gaul); **[SC]** Shared Vocabulary (common Christian vocabulary carrying this world's own assumptions). Risk/Function: **[DR]** High Distortion Risk; **[TC]** Technical Concept; **[RT]** Likely Runtime Term; **[PV]** Plural Voices (important in some voices/periods but not universally representative — here applied where the three voices demonstrably differ); **[CT]** Contested Tradition (live scholarly contest — each [CT] states which of the four contest types applies). Tags are assigned from the term's demonstrated function in what was read, not for even coverage; several terms carry no Risk/Function tag at all.

**Tier estimate caveat (stated once, applying throughout).** The tier figure on every entry is a **provisional estimate only**. Full tier assignment is Doc_06's job, after Doc_04 has established which gravities are Primary and Supporting and Doc_05 has reconstructed the ecology — the Lexicon Framework's own tier discipline ties Tier 1 to "Primary or Supporting Historical Gravities," which do not yet exist. A term marked "Tier 1 (est.)" here is a flag that this drafting pass expects it to need full treatment, nothing more.

**Entry format.** Each term carries: *Preliminary definition* (source-grounded, one to three sentences) · *Evidence* (loci read, with quoted phrases) · *Voices* (which of the three primary voices attest it; node) · *Tags* · *Tier (est.)* · *AG* (Author Gravity flag: **none** / **single-voice** / **cross-voice tension**) · *Registry rows*.

## 1. The monk and the forms of the life

### 1.1 monk / solitary (Lat. *monachus*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation; "monks or solitaries")
- **Preliminary definition.** One who has cut himself off from marriage, kin, and "the life of this world" for a stricter life — but in this world explicitly *not* only the desert solitary: Martin is "regarded not so much as being a soldier as a monk" while still in the army, and as bishop keeps "the objects and virtues of a monk."
- **Evidence.** *Vita Martini* ch. 2 ("regarded not so much as being a soldier as a monk"); ch. 10 ("kept up the position of a bishop properly, yet in such a way as not to lay aside the objects and virtues of a monk"); Cassian, *Conf.* XVIII.5 (Piamun: those who "abstained from marriage and cut themselves off from intercourse with their kinsmen and the life of this world, were termed monks or solitaries from the strictness of their lonely and solitary life"); Gennadius ch. XIX calls Martin "monk and bishop."
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian; Gennadius corroborates. Vincent absent (no monastic use of "monk" in the *Commonitory* — Round 1 S2). Salvian (Registry row 43, a fourth Native voice outside Discipline 3's three, added in the Round 1 revision): "the monks — that is, ... the servants of God" (*Gov.* VIII.4), his one use of the word, glossed; his own class term is "the religious" (1.13). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: the modern hearing "cloistered, withdrawn" misses that this world's founding monk is a working bishop and its literature is addressed to bishops.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 10, 30, 43 — row 3 removed after Round 1 (S7/C3): nothing in the Evidence line comes from the *Dialogues*.

### 1.2 monastery (*monasterium*) / coenobium / coenobite
- **Preliminary definition.** *Monastery* is the dwelling-place of monks — in Sulpitius simply the place Martin founds wherever he settles (Milan, near Poitiers, two miles outside Tours), and also a women's house; Cassian's Piamun distinguishes it sharply from *coenobium*, which names "the character of the life and its system" of a united community under one elder, and notes that even Sarabaite groups are called "monasteries."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 6, 7, 10 ("established a monastery for himself about two miles outside the city"); *Dial.* II.11 (the wife placed "in the nunnery [fn: *monasterio*] of the young women" — Roberts's footnote supplies the lemma); *Conf.* XVIII.4 ("The first is that of the Cœnobites, who live together in a congregation and are governed by the direction of a single Elder"); XVIII.9–10 ("monastery is the title of the dwelling ... while Cœnobium describes the character of the life and its system"); *Inst.* Preface ("the brethren in your new monastery," Castor's province "at present without monasteries").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian; Gennadius ch. LXII ("founded two monasteries ... one for men and one for women"). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** The *coenobium/monastery* distinction is Cassian-only (single-voice); the word *monastery* itself is shared.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7, 10, 30.

### 1.3 anchorite / hermit / eremite ("withdrawers")
- **Preliminary definition.** The solitary who, having first been "trained in the Cœnobium," withdraws to the desert "to fight with the devils in open conflict." In Sulpitius, the hermit-life is what the boy Martin desired and what Marmoutier's site gives him ("the solitude of a hermit"); Postumianus concedes that Martin surpassed "all the eremites and anchorites."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* XVIII.4 ("The second is that of the anchorites, who were first trained in the Cœnobium and then being made perfect in practical life chose the recesses of the desert"); XVIII.6 ("rightly termed anchorites; i.e., withdrawers"); *Conf.* Pref. I ("the Anchorite's life is grander than that of the Cœnobium"); *Vita* ch. 2 ("desired to enter on the life of a hermit"), ch. 10 ("he enjoyed in it the solitude of a hermit"); *Dial.* II.5 ("you have conquered all the eremites and anchorites"); *Dial.* II.11 (the soldier-monk "as if with the purpose of leading the life of an eremite").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC]. Not [RT] as a self-description — no Gallic participant in what was read calls *himself* an anchorite; Cassian's Preface III hopes Gallic monks will "try to carry out the rule of anchorites."
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** none, though the coenobite→anchorite sequence as a *rule* is Cassian's.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 8, 10.

### 1.4 Sarabaite
- **Preliminary definition.** Cassian's (Piamun's) name for the "reprehensible" third kind of monk who makes "renunciation only as a public profession ... before the face of men," lives "two or three together," and is "their own masters," subject to no elder.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* XVIII.4, 7 (whole chapter; "rightly named in the Egyptian language Sarabaites"); XVIII.8 adds an unnamed fourth kind, the false anchorite.
- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] — received from the Egyptian/desert tradition per Cassian's own framing ("rightly named in the Egyptian language Sarabaites"), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). The category is Cassian's Egyptian taxonomy imported to Gaul and nothing in Sulpitius uses it — that is recorded as single-voice Author Gravity below, not as a PV tag, since an absence is not a demonstrated divergence between voices (Round 1 S9).
- **Tier (est.).** 3. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 10.

### 1.5 cell (Lat. *cella*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation)
- **Preliminary definition.** The monk's own dwelling and the place of prayer, sickness, vision, and temptation: Martin's "cell constructed of wood," the closed cell where he converses with saints, the cell where the devil appears as Christ; for Cassian the place one must not leave without leave, which accidie makes hateful.
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 7 (the catechumen's cell), ch. 10 ("a cell constructed of wood"), ch. 21, ch. 24 ("as he was praying in his cell"); Sulp. *Ep.* II ("sitting alone in my cell"); *Dial.* II.13 ("the door of his cell being closed"); *Inst.* IV.10 ("do not dare to leave their cell"); *Inst.* X.2 (accidie "produces dislike of the place, disgust with the cell"); *Conf.* XVIII.16 (patience must not rest "in the doors of our cell or the recesses of the desert").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 3. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 7, 10.

### 1.6 brethren (Lat. *fratres* — corpus-supported only in Gibson's prolegomena on the Conferences' dedicatees, "styled 'fratres'", editorial layer, Registry row 17; not at any ancient locus below) / brother
- **Preliminary definition.** The monks of a house or circle as a body; also the form of address between named correspondents (Cassian's "holy brothers Honoratus and Eucherius"; Sulpitius to Desiderius, "my like-minded brother").
- **Evidence.** *Vita* Preface, ch. 10 ("Many also of the brethren"), ch. 21 ("Martin assembled the brethren"); *Dial.* III.1 (the crowd of monks and clergy); *Conf.* Pref. II (addressed to "holy brothers Honoratus and Eucherius"); *Inst.* IV.5 ("the body of the brethren, with whom Christ was not ashamed to be numbered").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian. Vincent: **the same English word, a different referent** — "greatly beloved by the holy brethren" (*Comm.* ch. 11 [29]) is Nestorius's popularity among the general body of the faithful, not a monastic body; the Round 1 reviewer enumerated every "brethren"/"brother" in the *Commonitory* (Noah's brothers, two Pauline quotations, "the brother of Basil," and this) and found no monastic usage (S2). The first draft's "all three" is withdrawn; this is a two-voice term. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 3. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7, 9, 13, 17 (lemma witness only).

### 1.7 elder / senior / abbot / "the old man"
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian, the authority to whom a junior lays bare every thought and whose judgment replaces the junior's own; "Abbot" (*abbas*) is the title of every Egyptian teacher whose conference he reports. In Sulpitius, the equivalent figure is Martin himself, "the saintly master," and elders are consulted for tradition ("his elders, whether among the presbyters or clerics").
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.9 ("to lay them bare to the senior ... that is good or bad which is considered and pronounced so by the examination of the senior"); *Inst.* IV.7 (two Elders, the guest-master and the dean over ten juniors); *Conf.* II.10 ("reserving everything ... for the scrutiny of the elders"); *Conf.* II.13 (not every grey-haired man is an elder worth following); *Conf.* I.1 ("Abbot Moses"); *Vita* ch. 11 ("often asked from those who were his elders, whether among the presbyters or clerics").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius (as "elders/master"), Cassian (as "elders/seniors/Abbot"). The title "Abbot" for a Gallic superior does not appear in what was read; Cassian's Preface II calls Honoratus one "presiding ... over a large monastery," not "abbot." Both nodes, with a lexical gap on the title.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [RT]. [DR] on "abbot": the modern hearing (the canonical head of a Benedictine house) is a century downstream.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** cross-voice: the *function* is shared; the *title* "Abbot" is Cassian's Egyptian usage.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 9, 10.

### 1.8 junior / novice
- **Preliminary definition.** The newly-received monk, kept ten days at the door, stripped and reclothed by the Abbot, lodged a year in the guest-house under an Elder, and taught "the alphabet, as it were, and first syllables in the direction of perfection."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.3–9 (the whole entrance procedure; IV.9 "the alphabet ... and first syllables"); IV.30 (Pinufius "received as a novice").
- **Voices.** Cassian only (Sulpitius has "disciples," 3.1 below, not a probationary grade). Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC].
- **Tier (est.).** 3. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian)** — and specifically Egyptian practice that Cassian himself says Gallic houses do not keep ("we cannot recollect any one who joined our monasteries keeping it up unbroken even for a year," *Inst.* IV.2).
- **Registry.** 7.

### 1.9 profession (Lat. *professio* — corpus-supported only in Gibson's textual footnote at *Conf.* Pref. I, Petschenig's reading against Gazæus's, editorial layer, Registry row 17)
- **Preliminary definition.** The monastic undertaking as a public, binding commitment with its own "goal and end," whose breach makes a man "a deserter or lukewarm"; used by Cassian of the whole way of life and by Sulpitius of the individual ("having professed himself a monk," "under the profession of a monk").
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* I.2 ("our profession too has its own goal and end"); *Inst.* IV.33 ("the responsibility of this profession"); *Inst.* I.10 ("the humble character of our profession"); *Inst.* X.3 ("begins to forget the object of his profession"); *Dial.* II.11 ("having professed himself a monk"); *Vita* ch. 23 ("under the profession of a monk, falsely assumed every appearance of humility"); *Conf.* XVIII.7 (Sarabaites make "renunciation only as a public profession").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7, 8, 10.

### 1.10 conversion (Lat. *conversio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) — as entry into the monastic life
- **Preliminary definition.** Not a change of religion but the act of turning to the monastic life: Cassian speaks of "the early days of their conversion," of a murderer's "compulsory conversion," and of a monk "consecrated by a noble conversion at the outset."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* III.5 ("the early days of their conversion were so bright"; Abbot Moses of Calamus "made such use of his compulsory conversion that ... he turned it into a voluntary one"); *Conf.* III.7 ("in the fervour of the early days of my conversion"); *Inst.* IV.39 ("gain a start in conversion as well as purification from vices").
- **Voices.** Cassian only in this sense as read; Sulpitius uses "converts" of the robber and of pagans (*Vita* ch. 5, 13–15) in the religious-conversion sense — a real cross-voice difference in what the same English word denotes. Southern node for the monastic sense. Salvian (row 43, added in the Round 1 revision) attests the *monastic-entry* sense in a second southern voice: men who "repent of their conversion," "professing physical continence" yet running "riot in incontinence of spirit" — "this is not conversion to God but aversion from him" (*Gov.* V.10) — and the plain moral sense at III.11 ("the conversion of one man does not atone for the sins of the many").
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [TC]. Distortion: the modern hearing (evangelical conversion) is Sulpitius's sense, not Cassian's.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** cross-voice (different referents); the monastic sense is Cassian's and Salvian's.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 43.

### 1.11 renunciation (Lat. *renuntiatio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) / renunciant
- **Preliminary definition.** The act that makes a monk: "nothing but the evidence of the cross and of mortification," by which "you are dead to this world and its deeds and desires." Cassian's Paphnutius makes it threefold — of goods, of one's own vices and former affections, and of all visible things — and says the first alone "is not enough of itself to confer perfection on the renunciant."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV title ("Of the Institutes of the Renunciants"); IV.34 ("Renunciation is nothing but the evidence of the cross and of mortification"); IV.36 ("How our renunciation of the world is of no use if we are again entangled"); *Conf.* III.6 (the three renunciations, keyed to Gen. 12:1 "from thy country ... thy kinsfolk ... thy father's house"); III.10 ("The first renunciation ... is of what is not our own"); *Vita* ch. 25 (Martin's counsel to Sulpitius: "the allurements of this world and secular burdens were to be abandoned").
- **Voices.** Cassian (technical, threefold); Sulpitius (the thing, not the word — Martin's exhortation and Paulinus's example). Salvian (row 43, added in the Round 1 revision): the *baptismal* renunciation — "I renounce the devil, his pomps and spectacles and his works" (*Gov.* VI.6) — the same English word with a different, pre-monastic and universal referent; and the monastic sense once, of the half-hearted rich who "make a show of renouncing their wealth [and] do not appear to make their renunciation complete" (*Gov.* III.3). Both nodes, with the technical term southern.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** cross-voice: the *threefold* schema is Cassian's alone.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 43.

### 1.12 the world / secular / "in the world"
- **Preliminary definition.** What is renounced — kin, property, "the delights of this world," "secular business"; Vincent describes his own past as "secular warfare" before he "cast anchor in the harbour of religion."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 25 ("the allurements of this world and secular burdens"); *Inst.* IV.5 ("laid aside all worldly pride"); *Inst.* X.3 (accidie makes the soldier of Christ "entangle himself in secular business"); *Conf.* I.2 ("scorned your fatherland, and the delights of this world"); *Comm.* ch. 1 [2] ("the manifold and deplorable tempests of secular warfare ... cast anchor in the harbour of religion"); *Conf.* XIV.7 (a layman "living in the world and bound by the ties of marriage" who yet casts out a demon).
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 9, 13.

### 1.13 the religious / "servants of God" — Salvian's class term (Lat. *religiosi*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) *(added in the Round 1 revision, S6)*
- **Preliminary definition.** Salvian's ordinary name for those who have taken this world's way of life — not "monks," which he uses once and glosses, but "the religious": "Religious men are lowly — they wish to be so; poor — they delight in poverty; without ambition — they spurn it; unesteemed — they flee from honors; ... they are weak — nay, they rejoice in weakness," for whom "toil, fasting, poverty, humility and weakness are not burdensome"; and, of the Africans' hatred, "the monks — that is, ... the servants of God." The same word names the *lapsed* convert: "the religious who have gained some reputation by a general repentance and now seek after new honors."
- **Evidence.** *Gov.* I.2 (Sanford pp. 42–43: "those who live and act according to their own determination and vows. Religious men are lowly ..."; "the religious are happier than all others in this, that they have what they wish"); V.10 (pp. 153–154: "the religious who have gained some reputation by a general repentance ..."); VIII.4 (p. 229: "the hatred of the Africans for the monks — that is, for the servants of God"); III.3 (p. 83: those who "make a show of renouncing their wealth"). Sanford's footnotes at these loci (Seneca parallels; a Caesarius of Arles quotation at IV.7) are editorial and post-window and are not used.
- **Voices.** Salvian only — a fourth Native voice (Registry row 43), outside the three of §0 Discipline 3; recorded so Doc_04 can weigh it, not as a fourth node. Southern node (Marseilles).
- **Tags.** [SC] [RT] [DR]. Distortion: "religious" as an adjective of piety; here a *class noun* for the vowed, parallel to 1.1 monk.
- **Tier (est.).** 3 — a satellite of 1.1; Doc_06 should decide whether it is an alias of *monk* or its own entry. **AG.** **single-voice (Salvian).**
- **Registry.** 43.

## 2. Warfare: the soldier of Christ

### 2.1 soldier of Christ / to serve as a soldier to God (Lat. *militia* — in the vendored volume only as Vincent's *secularis militia*, the antonym, quoted in Heurtley's Introduction, editorial layer; *cingulum* per Roberts's fn. at *Dial.* II.11; the lemma for "soldier of Christ" itself is unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation; the military oath)
- **Preliminary definition.** The governing image of the monk's life in both nodes: Martin's discharge speech ("Hitherto I have served you as a soldier: allow me now to become a soldier to God ... I am the soldier of Christ"), Sulpitius's "young soldiers who had just taken the oath of allegiance to Christ," Cassian's opening sentence on the monk "as a soldier of Christ ever ready for battle," and accidie's monk who "becomes a runaway from His service, and a deserter."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 4; ch. 1 ("heavenly warfare"); Sulp. *Ep.* III ("young soldiers who had just taken the oath of allegiance to Christ"; Martin's own prayer "I will serve under thy standard"); *Dial.* II.11 (a soldier "had renounced the military [fn: *cingulum*] life ... adding that he was a soldier of Christ, and that she also had taken the oath of allegiance in the same service"; Martin: "Let not a woman enter the camp of men"); *Inst.* I.1 ("A monk, then, as a soldier of Christ ever ready for battle, ought always to walk with his loins girded"); *Inst.* I.11 ("the soldier of Christ"); *Inst.* X.3 ("the soldier of Christ becomes a runaway from His service, and a deserter"); *Comm.* ch. 1 [2] ("secular warfare," the contrast term).
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian; Vincent supplies the antonym. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: for Martin the metaphor is literal biography (a real discharge, a real oath) and for Cassian it is dress-mysticism (girdle, cords); the modern hearing of "spiritual warfare" flattens both.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none — the best-attested cross-node term found, attested in two of the three voices (Sulpitius, Cassian); Vincent supplies only the antonym, so it is not an "all three voices" term (Round 1 S2).

### 2.2 combat / struggle against the faults; "athlete"
- **Preliminary definition.** The monk's interior life described as a sequence of single combats — "our first struggle," "our sixth combat" — against named "spirits" (of gluttony, fornication, ... pride), and the monk as "the true Christian athlete."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* V.1 ("the struggle against the eight principal faults"), V.3 ("the first conflict we must enter upon is that against gluttony"); *Inst.* X.1 ("Our sixth combat is with what the Greeks call ἀκηδία"); X.5 ("the true Christian athlete who desires to strive lawfully in the lists of perfection"); *Conf.* XIII.14 (Job "His well tried athlete, when the devil had challenged him to single combat"); Sulp. *Ep.* II ("those daily struggles which he carried on against the various conflicts with human and spiritual wickedness").
- **Voices.** Cassian (systematically); Sulpitius (once, of Martin). Both nodes, weighted south.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 2, 7, 9.

### 2.3 sign of the cross (Lat. *signum crucis*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation; "sign of salvation"; "the weapons of prayer")
- **Preliminary definition.** A gesture with real power: Martin will go into battle "protected by the sign of the cross, and not by shield or helmet," halts a funeral crowd by "lifting up the sign of the cross," turns the falling pine with "the sign of salvation"; Cassian's staff-bearer must "beat them off with the sign of the cross."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 4, 12, 13; Sulp. *Ep.* I ("as soon as he again sought assistance from the cross, and tried the weapons of prayer"); *Ep.* II ("he repeated again and again the name of the cross so familiar to his lips"); *Inst.* I.8.
- **Voices.** Sulpitius (repeatedly), Cassian (once). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: not a devotional habit but an act of power against demons and elements.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Sulpitius.
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 7.

### 2.4 martyrdom "without shedding his blood" / confessor
- **Preliminary definition.** Sulpitius's claim that Martin, denied a persecutor's age, "fully attained to the honor of martyrdom without shedding his blood" through "hunger, watchings, nakedness, fastings, reproachings"; Vincent uses "Confessor" in the older sense of one who suffered for the faith short of death (Hilary, Athanasius, the Ariminum bishops), and Cassian calls strict coenobites "crucified daily to this world and made living martyrs."
- **Evidence.** Sulp. *Ep.* II (whole passage; "both by vow and virtues he was alike able and willing to be a martyr"); *Comm.* ch. 5 [12–14] ("not only to be accounted Confessors, but ... foremost among Confessors"), ch. 18 ("the blessed Confessor, Hilary"), ch. 28 [72]; *Conf.* XVIII.7 ("crucified daily to this world and made living martyrs"); *Dial.* II.4 ("that plain where the martyrs were wont to be consecrated").
- **Voices.** All three, differently. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [TC]. Distortion: "confessor" now means a priest hearing confession.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** cross-voice: Sulpitius's *bloodless martyrdom* is his own coinage for Martin; Vincent's *Confessor* is a church-historical category.
- **Registry.** 2, 3, 10, 13.

## 3. Formation: master, example, elders, discretion

### 3.1 disciple / master (Lat. *discipulus* / *magister*, both unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation)
- **Preliminary definition.** The formative relationship in the northern node: Martin's "eighty disciples, who were being disciplined after the example of the saintly master"; Clarus "a disciple of Martin's ... his master"; Sulpitius's "our master, Martin." Vincent inverts it for doctrine: the true Catholic is "not a teacher but a disciple, not a leader but a follower" of what was handed down.
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 10; Sulp. *Ep.* II; *Ep.* III ("the death of our master, Martin"); *Dial.* III.10 ("that disciple of Christ, imitating the miracles performed by the Saviour"); *Comm.* ch. 22 [53]; *Conf.* II.15 (Paul sent to Ananias — "the Lord does not Himself show the way of perfection to anyone who ... despises the teaching and training of the Elders").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius (person-to-person), Vincent (tradition), Cassian (elder-to-junior, 1.7). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 8, 13.

### 3.2 example (Lat. *exemplum*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) / imitation
- **Preliminary definition.** The mechanism by which formation is transmitted and the reason the literature exists: Sulpitius writes Martin's life "which shall serve in future as an example to others"; Martin presses Paulinus on Sulpitius as "the object of our imitation" and will not die on straw lest "I leave you a different example"; Cassian's novice is told to take his model "from one or two only and not from too many," and Cassian's Preface offers Castor's own life as "amply sufficient for an example."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* Preface/ch. 1, ch. 25; Sulp. *Ep.* III ("I have sinned if I leave you a different example"); *Dial.* II.7–8 ("those will be truly happy if they do not fall short of the excellence of the example in question"); *Dial.* II.12; *Inst.* IV.40 ("the examples of the perfect life ... should be sought from a very few or indeed from one or two only"); *Inst.* Preface; *Inst.* V.4 (Antony: gather each virtue from the one who "specially possesses it"); *Conf.* XI.4 (Chaeremon: "the authority of a teacher will never be strong unless he fixes it in the heart of his hearer by the actual performance of his duty"); *Conf.* XIV.7 (a good deed "wrongly taken by others as an example"); *Comm.* ch. 5 [15] ("Great then is the example of these same blessed men").
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: "example" as illustration vs. this world's *exemplum* as a formative instrument with its own dangers (Cassian's warning that some examples cannot be safely imitated).
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 7, 9, 13.

### 3.3 the Fathers / the elders / "our forefathers" / the ancients (as authority)
- **Preliminary definition.** The prior generation(s) whose teaching is received, not invented: Cassian will report only "the Institutes which are not mine but the fathers'," as "delivered to us by the Fathers"; Vincent's whole method is to "put down in writing the things which I have truthfully received from the holy Fathers" and to collate "the consentient opinions of the ancient masters"; Martin refuses a cult where "no steady tradition respecting them had come down from antiquity."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* Preface ("as they were there delivered to us by the Fathers"; "in accordance with that which we received from our elders"); *Inst.* I.2 ("a long standing antiquity and numbers of the holy fathers have passed on by an unanimous decision"); *Conf.* Pref. I ("Institutes which are not mine but the fathers'"); *Conf.* II.10, 13 ("the tradition of the Elders"); *Conf.* XIII.18 ("laid down by all the Catholic fathers who have taught perfection of heart not by empty disputes of words, but in deed and act"); *Comm.* ch. 1 [1, 3], ch. 3 [8] ("acknowledged and approved authorities"), ch. 28 [71–72] ("a consentient council of doctors"), ch. 29 [77] ("accepted as approved masters"); *Vita* ch. 11.
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: "Fathers" as a later patristic canon vs. living/recent elders one has personally heard (Cassian) or a collatable body of writings (Vincent).
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none as a term; **cross-voice tension in referent** (Cassian's fathers are Egyptian monks he met; Vincent's are bishops and doctors in writing; Martin's are local clerical elders).
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 9, 13.

### 3.4 tradition (Lat. *traditio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) / "handed down"
- **Preliminary definition.** What has been received from predecessors and must be passed on unchanged — Vincent's second criterion of truth after Scripture ("the Tradition of the Catholic Church"), Pope Stephen's rule "Let there be no innovation — nothing but what has been handed down"; in Cassian, "the traditions of the Elders" that govern a junior's judgment and the "traditions" by which the eight faults are remedied.
- **Evidence.** *Comm.* ch. 2 [4], ch. 6 [16], ch. 22 [53] ("not of private adoption, but of public tradition"), ch. 23 [59]; *Conf.* II.10 ("by their traditions"), II.24 ("relied on his own judgment rather than on the traditions of the Elders"); *Inst.* Preface ("the remedies for them according to their traditions"); *Conf.* XVIII.7 (Sarabaites are not "taught by their traditions"); *Vita* ch. 11.
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2 (may rise to 1 with 3.3 at Doc_06). **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 10, 13.

### 3.5 conference (Lat. *collatio* — **warning, Round 1 S5:** the word does occur in the vendored volume, three times, but all three are in Vincent's etymology of *Symbolum* at *Comm.* ch. 29–30, "what is called in Greek σύμβολος is termed in Latin *Collatio* ... a collection" — a creed-etymology with no relation to Cassian's genre; as the name of the Conference genre the lemma is **unverified in this corpus** and rests on the work's conventional Latin title only)
- **Preliminary definition.** The genre and the practice: an elder's discourse elicited by disciples' questions, "well worn in frequent conferences with spiritual men," which Cassian reproduces and Gallic monks are to receive "into their cells ... talking with them after a fashion by daily questions and answers"; also the customary after-supper talk in an Egyptian cell. Sulpitius's *Dialogues* are, per Gennadius, "a Conference between Postumianus and Gallus."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* Preface; *Conf.* I.1, I.23 ("a sort of fire supplies keener zest to our conference"); *Conf.* II.11 ("the spiritual conference had begun to be held"); *Conf.* XVIII.11 ("the customary Conference"); *Conf.* Pref. III; Gennadius ch. XIX ("a Conference between Postumianus and Gallus").
- **Voices.** Cassian; Gennadius applies the word to Sulpitius's *Dialogues*. Both nodes at the level of form (Sulpitius's Dialogues *are* a Gallic conference on Martin), southern at the level of the word.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] — received: the practice and the genre are the Egyptian fathers' own, reproduced ("Institutes which are not mine but the fathers'"), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1).
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 3, 7, 8, 10, 30.

### 3.6 Institutes / customs / rule (Lat. *instituta*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation; *regula* in the monastic sense — corpus-supported only in Gibson's footnote at *Inst.* I.10 quoting *Regula S. Bened.* c. lv, i.e. a Registry-row-36 Named-Comparandum cross-reference in the editorial layer, cited here as a lemma witness only and never as this world's own usage)
- **Preliminary definition.** The received "customs and rules of the monasteries" of Egypt and Palestine, which Cassian undertakes "faithfully to explain" and to adapt "in accordance with the rule which I have seen followed"; explicitly *not* a written Rule of his own devising — where Egyptian practice is "impossible in these countries" he will "balance it by the customs of the monasteries ... throughout Pontus and Mesopotamia." Sulpitius's Marmoutier has customs but no named rule.
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* Preface (whole; "the customs of the monasteries which we have seen observed throughout Egypt and Palestine"; the climate clause); *Inst.* I.1 ("the customs and rules of the monasteries"); I.2 ("the Catholic rule ... the general rule for all"); I.10 (climate modification); IV.1; *Conf.* Pref. III ("that holy and splendid monastic rule in the province of Gaul"); *Vita* ch. 10 (Marmoutier's practices: common property, no buying or selling, transcribing only, one meal, no wine, camel's hair).
- **Voices.** Cassian; Sulpitius describes without naming. Both nodes for the *thing*; the word is southern.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] — received: in Cassian's own words "the customs of the monasteries which we have seen observed throughout Egypt and Palestine," hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: hearing "Rule" as a codified document (Benedict) — Gibson's own footnotes repeatedly cross-reference RB, a Registry-row-36 comparandum this world's voice must not borrow.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 10, (36 as excluded comparandum).

### 3.7 discretion (Lat. *discretio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation)
- **Preliminary definition.** The "gift of discretion" that Antony's council judged the one virtue without which every other fails — "the mother of all virtues, as well as their guardian and regulator," the "eye and light of the body," the "royal road" between excess and slackness — gained only "by true humility," i.e., by submitting all one's thoughts to the elders' judgment; illustrated by the "good money-changer" who tests the coin of every thought.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* I.20–23 (money-changer; "the power of discretion which comes from the words and warnings of the fathers"); *Conf.* II.1–4 ("the greatest prize of divine grace"; "discretion is the mother of all virtues"); II.2 ("the royal road"); II.5–8 (Heron and other shipwrecks); II.10 ("True discretion ... is only secured by true humility"); II.16 ("extremes meet"); *Inst.* V.3 ("a perfect method of discrimination").
- **Voices.** Cassian only as a named virtue. (Sulpitius's Martin exercises the *thing* — testing Anatolius's robe, refusing the false Christ — without the word.) Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT] — received: the verdict of "the blessed Antony and the other fathers" in an Egyptian council (*Conf.* II.2–4), reported, hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: "discretion" as tact/prudence.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian)** — flagged for Doc_04 as a candidate gravity whose evidence is one author's.
- **Registry.** 7, 8.

### 3.8 disclosure / confession of thoughts to the elder
- **Preliminary definition.** The practice of laying every "itching thought" bare to the senior, on the rule that "a thought is from the devil if we are ashamed to disclose it," so that "a wrong thought is enfeebled at the moment that it is discovered"; Serapion's stolen biscuit and the burning lamp are its exemplum.
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.9; *Inst.* IV.37 ("watch his head, i.e. the first rise of thoughts, by bringing them at once to your superior"); *Conf.* II.10–11 ("your confession frees you from this slavery"); II.12–13 (the danger of a harsh elder).
- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] — received Egyptian coenobitic practice per Cassian's own framing (the *Institutes* as what "we have seen observed throughout Egypt"), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: hearing this as sacramental confession of sins; Cassian's practice is of *thoughts*, to an elder, before sin.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 7, 8.

### 3.9 obedience
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian the virtue the Egyptians "put not merely before manual labour and reading and silence and quietness in the cell, but even before all virtues," proved by absurd or impossible commands (the dry stick, the oil-cruse, the boy thrown into the river); the first thing a junior learns ("to conquer his own wishes"). Sulpitius shows it as Martin's own posture ("I felt so overcome by the authority he unconsciously exerted, that I deemed it unlawful to do anything but acquiesce").
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.8, 10, 12 ("the virtue of obedience, which they put ... even before all virtues"), IV.23–29 (John of Lycon, Patermucius, the count's son); IV.30 ("the virtue of obedience, which holds the first place among other good qualities"); *Vita* ch. 25.
- **Voices.** Cassian (as named first virtue); Sulpitius (the disposition). Both nodes, weighted south.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: the Patermucius story is exactly the kind of material a modern hearer will read as abuse; the world reads it as "the deed of Abraham" (*Inst.* IV.28).
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 1, 7.

### 3.10 humility
- **Preliminary definition.** For Cassian, the root from which discretion and true patience grow, "attested by these signs" (ten of them, *Inst.* IV.39), the "true nobility" a count's son wins by hawking baskets, and something a famous abbot flees his own house to find; for Sulpitius, Martin's "same humility in his heart" as bishop, washing his guest's feet, sitting on a "rude little stool" not a throne.
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.39 (ten signs), IV.29 ("that humility of Christ which is the true nobility"), IV.30–31 (Pinufius); *Conf.* II.10; *Conf.* XVIII.11–13 ("True patience and tranquillity is neither gained nor retained without profound humility of heart"; the mock-humble visitor); *Vita* ch. 10, ch. 25; *Dial.* II.1 (the three-legged stool "such as those in use by the lowest of servants"); Gennadius ch. XIX (Sulpitius "by his humility").
- **Voices.** All three (Vincent: "the sacrifice of Christian humility," *Comm.* ch. 1 [2]). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7, 8, 10, 13, 30.

### 3.11 lukewarmness / coldness / "grow cold"
- **Preliminary definition.** The characteristic failure-mode this world names for a monk: not apostasy but cooling — the novice who "once grow[s] cold in regard to the rule," the "deserter or lukewarm," the elders who "pass their old age in a lukewarmness which they contracted in youth," Sarabaites "cast down into hell by the lukewarmness of their purpose." (Cassian's "this province that is frozen as it were with the cold of Gaul," *Conf.* Pref. II, is in context a remark on Gaul's climate and a monk's wish to leave for Egypt — an authorial pun on the theme at most, **not an attestation of the term**; it does its honest work at 8.8. Round 1 C4.)
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.6, IV.33, IV.38 ("do not grow cold after the examples of the lukewarmness of many"); *Conf.* II.2 ("the opposite spirit of luke-warmness"), II.13; *Conf.* XVIII.5, 7, 8; *Conf.* XIII.11 ("if He finds that we are unwilling or have grown cold, He stirs our hearts"). *Conf.* Pref. II is no longer cited here as evidence (C4).
- **Voices.** Cassian, insistently. Sulpitius: the negative is present only obliquely (Brictio, *Dial.* III.15). Salvian (row 43, added in the Round 1 revision, not one of the three voices): "a lukewarm Christian and hateful to the Lord," "cast out because of lukewarmness," expounding Rev. 3:15–16 of ordinary Christians who "do not justify their profession of faith" (*Gov.* IV.19, Sanford pp. 130–131) — the word alive in Cassian's own city for the non-monastic Christian. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT] — the one retag Round 1 (S1) left to this revision's judgment; decided [SC] because the word is Scriptural common Christian vocabulary (Rev. 3:16) and because Salvian, a fourth Native voice in Cassian's own Marseilles (Registry row 43, *Gov.* IV.19), uses "lukewarm"/"lukewarmness" of ordinary Christians from that very text — so it is shared vocabulary carrying this world's own (monastic, damning) assumptions in Cassian, not signature vocabulary. Distortion: "lukewarm" as mild disapproval vs. this world's sense of a spiritual disease that damns.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 7, 8, 9, 10, 43 (Salvian attestation).

## 4. The interior road: purity of heart, thoughts, and the faults

### 4.1 purity of heart (Lat. *puritas cordis*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation)
- **Preliminary definition.** The monk's "immediate aim or goal" (Greek σκοπός, which Cassian says Paul "significantly uses"), as distinct from the "end" (τέλος), the kingdom of God; "which he not unfairly terms 'sanctification'"; identified with charity ("purity of heart, which is charity"); the standard by which fasting, vigils, reading and solitude are ranked as "aids to perfection" and not perfection itself.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* I.4–5 (the whole goal/end distinction; "fixing our gaze then steadily on this goal, as if on a definite mark"); I.6–7 ("fastings, vigils, meditation on the Scriptures, self-denial, and the abnegation of all possessions are not perfection, but aids to perfection"); *Inst.* IV.43 ("By purity of heart the perfection of apostolic love is acquired"); *Inst.* I.11; *Conf.* XIV.4; *Conf.* IX.2 ("a perpetual purity").
- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT] — received: Abbot Moses's teaching in an Egyptian cell, reported, hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1); whether it carries this world's *own* weight is exactly the Doc_04 question flagged below. Distortion: "purity" heard as sexual purity; Cassian's term is the *scopos* of the whole life.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian)** — the most consequential single-voice term in this list; Doc_04 should test whether it organizes anything in the northern node's evidence or is Cassian's alone.
- **Registry.** 7, 8, 9.

### 4.2 goal and end (σκοπός / τέλος; "mark")
- **Preliminary definition.** Cassian's structuring pair: every art has "some goal or mark; and end or aim of their own," and "our profession too"; the end is "the kingdom of God or the kingdom of heaven," the goal purity of heart (4.1); illustrated by the archer who cannot correct his aim without a visible target.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* I.2–5 (whole); *Conf.* II.26 ("the end and aim of the monastic life"); Gennadius ch. LXII lists the first Conference as "On the aim of a monk and his creed."
- **Voices.** Cassian only; Gennadius corroborates the title. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] — received (Abbot Moses, *Conf.* I), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1).
- **Tier (est.).** 2 (subordinate to 4.1). **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 8, 30.

### 4.3 perfection (Lat. *perfectio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) / "the perfect life"
- **Preliminary definition.** The summit toward which the whole "way of perfection" ascends, "not arrived at simply by self-denial ... unless there is that charity"; the *Institutes'* declared subject ("the attainment of the perfect life"); Cassian's Chaeremon makes its three restraints fear, hope, and love, of which only love is perfection. Sulpitius uses "perfectly" of Martin's practice rather than of a doctrine of perfection.
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* Preface ("instruction in the perfect life"; "the attainment of the perfect life"); *Conf.* I.6; *Conf.* III.10; *Conf.* XI title and ch. 6–8 ("from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God"); *Conf.* XIV.2–3 ("practical perfection depends on a double system"); *Conf.* Pref. I ("these lofty heights of perfection").
- **Voices.** Cassian (technical); Sulpitius (ordinary). Both nodes, technical sense southern.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR]. Distortion: "perfectionism"; Cassian's *perfectio* is a graded ascent with love at the top.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 7, 8, 9.

### 4.4 thoughts (Lat. *cogitationes*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) — their three origins; the mill-wheel
- **Preliminary definition.** The raw material of the interior struggle: "It is impossible for the mind not to be approached by thoughts, but it is in the power of every earnest man either to admit them or to reject them"; thoughts come "from God, from the devil, and from ourselves"; the mind is a mill-wheel that must grind *something*, and the miller chooses wheat or darnel.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* I.16–19; I.20 ("carefully to notice this threefold order"); *Inst.* IV.9 (thoughts to be disclosed).
- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] — received (Abbot Moses, *Conf.* I), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1).
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 7, 8.

### 4.5 the eight principal faults (gluttony, fornication, covetousness, anger, dejection, accidie, vainglory, pride)
- **Preliminary definition.** The fixed list "of which they reckon eight," each a "spirit" to be fought in order, with "the origin and causes ... and the remedies for them according to their traditions" — the subject of *Institutes* V–XII and, per Gennadius, of a Conference "On the nature of all sins."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* Preface; *Inst.* V.1 (the list, with Gibson's footnote on "Accidie"); V.2–3; *Inst.* X (accidie); Gennadius ch. LXII ("eight books On the origin, nature and remedies for the eight principal sins, a book on each sin").
- **Voices.** Cassian only; Gennadius corroborates. Southern node. Books VI–IX, XI–XII were not read this pass beyond their titles (§11).
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] — received: "of which they reckon eight ... according to their traditions" (*Inst.* Preface), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: the "seven deadly sins" — a later, different list; this world's has eight and orders them as a combat sequence.
- **Tier (est.).** 2 (the *list*); individual faults Tier 3 except accidie (4.6). **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 7, 30.

### 4.6 accidie (*acedia*; "weariness or distress of heart"; "the midday demon")
- **Preliminary definition.** The sixth fault, "especially trying to solitaries," which "produces dislike of the place, disgust with the cell, and disdain and contempt of the brethren," makes the monk gaze at the sun "as if it was too slow in setting," and dresses its escape as pious duty (visiting the sick, a religious woman in need) until "the soldier of Christ becomes a runaway ... and a deserter."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* X.1–5 (the whole description; X.1 "some of the elders ... declare that this is the 'midday demon' spoken of in the ninetieth Psalm"); *Inst.* V.1 (Gibson's footnote supplies the lemma and the Chaucer usage — editorial layer, not Cassian).
- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT] — received Egyptian teaching ("some of the elders ... declare"), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: "sloth"/"depression"; Cassian's accidie is a specifically monastic, cell-bound, hour-bound assault.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 7.

### 4.7 mortification / "the fear of the Lord is our cross"
- **Preliminary definition.** The state of being "dead to this world and its deeds and desires," figured as crucifixion: "as one who is crucified no longer has the power of moving or turning his limbs ... so we also ought to affix our wishes and desires"; the girdle of dead skin "signifies that he bears about the mortification of those members in which are contained the seeds of lust."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.34–35 (Pinufius's charge); *Inst.* I.11; *Inst.* I.4 (the sleeveless tunic teaches "that they are dead to all earthly conversation"); Gennadius ch. LXII lists a Conference "On mortification" (XXIV, not read).
- **Voices.** Cassian. Sulpitius has the practices (sackcloth, ashes, fasting) but not the theological term as read. Southern node for the term.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 7, 30.

### 4.8 compunction (Lat. *compunctio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation)
- **Preliminary definition.** The salutary pricking of heart from which renunciation springs: "From the fear of the Lord arises salutary compunction. From compunction of heart springs renunciation"; God "chastens us with most salutary compunction"; the angel tells Martin "Justly, O Martin, do you feel compunction."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.43; *Conf.* I.17, 19; *Conf.* II.11 ("my heart's compunction increased"); *Conf.* II.17 ("a healthy compunction"); *Dial.* III.13 (the angel to Martin after the Ithacian communion).

### 4.9 fear → hope → love (the three restraints; servant, hireling, son)
- **Preliminary definition.** Chaeremon's ladder: "three things which enable men to control their faults; viz., either the fear of hell ... or the hope and desire of the kingdom of heaven; or a liking for goodness itself and the love of virtue," mapped onto slave, hireling, and son (the prodigal), with the command to "mount ... from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* XI.6–8; *Inst.* IV.39 ("love which knows no fear"; "no longer now from regard of punishment ... but from love of goodness itself").

### 4.10 contemplation (Lat. *theoria*, unverified as a Latin lemma — zero occurrences in the vendored volume; "the one thing"; Martha and Mary) / practical vs. theoretical knowledge (πρακτική / θεωρητική)
- **Preliminary definition.** The "chief good," which "the Lord makes ... consist in meditation; i.e., in divine contemplation," Mary's "good part" against Martha's "praiseworthy service"; and Nesteros's twofold knowledge, practical ("improvement of morals and purification from faults") and theoretical ("the contemplation of things Divine"), of which "practical knowledge can be acquired without theoretical, but theoretical cannot possibly be gained without practical."

- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node. (Sulpitius's Martha/Mary is used of a queen's service, *Dial.* II.7 — the same figure, a different lesson.)
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] — received (Abbots Moses and Nesteros, reported), hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: "contemplation" as reflection; and "practical" as opposed to "theoretical" in the modern sense.

- **Registry.** 3, 8, 9.

### 4.11 unceasing prayer / "the canonical system" of psalms and prayers
- **Preliminary definition.** Two levels: the fixed "canonical system of Psalms and prayers which ought to be observed in the daily services throughout the monasteries" (*Institutes* II–III), and beyond it "continual and unbroken perseverance in prayer" as "the aim of every monk," the "crown of the building of all virtues." ### 4.12 the kingdom of God within / the kingdom of the devil

## 5. Grace, the will, and the controversy

*Note on licensing.* Registry rows 14–16 (Augustine) are Native, **context use only** — "the provoking texts for the Gallic monks' objections," not this world's own voice. The terms below are therefore defined from **Cassian's own text first** (*Conf.* III and XIII, Native rows 8–9) and from Augustine only for how the vocabulary was *contested* — what the Massilian brethren are reported to hold and to object to, in Augustine's report of Prosper's and Hilary's letters. Nothing in this section is Augustine's doctrine as such. Warfield's bracketed footnote on "Massilians" is a nineteenth-century editorial note and is treated as such.

### 5.1 grace (*gratia*) — of God, "the grace of Christ"
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian, the divine help without which "the exertions of the worker can do nothing," which "always co-operates with our will for its advantage," "not only ... inspires us with holy desires, but actually creates occasions," and which "ever stirs up the will of man, not so as to protect and defend it in all things in such a way as to cause it not to fight by its own efforts"; both prevenient ("My God will prevent me with His mercy") and, at other moments, waited for by a will that goes first. Vincent uses the word only incidentally. **Two other senses live alongside this one and must not be merged with it:** grace as *charism/power* (6.2 below) and grace as *favour/thanks* in ordinary usage.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* XIII.3 ("the initiative not only of our actions but also of good thoughts comes from God"), XIII.7–8 ("The Divine protection then is inseparably present with us ... His Providence not only accompanies it, but actually constantly precedes it"), XIII.9 (the catena of paired texts), XIII.11 ("does God have compassion upon us because we have shown the beginning of a good will, or does the beginning of a good will follow because God has had compassion upon us?"), XIII.12 (both prevent each other), XIII.13 ("the grace of God always co-operates with our will"), XIII.14 (nurse and infant), XIII.18 (three stages, in the text's own two phrasings — "the first stage in the Divine gift," then "the second stage in Divine grace"; the first draft's composite "stages in Divine grace" is corrected, Round 1 C1); *Conf.* III.10–22 ("the beginning of our salvation results from the call of the Lord ... the completion of perfection and purity is His gift in the same way"; "the assistance and grace of God are necessary to it every day and hour"); Aug. *Corrept.* ch. 2–3 (the "Catholic faith concerning law, grace, and free will" as Augustine states it: "to desire the help of grace is the beginning of grace"); Aug. *Praed.* ch. 2 (what the Massilian brethren "have attained": "that men's wills are anticipated by God's grace; and ... that no one can suffice to himself either for beginning or for completing any good work"). Gennadius ch. LXXXVI on Faustus: "the grace of God always invites, precedes and helps our will."
- **Voices.** Cassian (extensively); Vincent (incidentally); Augustine (context); Gennadius (Faustus). Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT] [CT]. **CT type: the term's meaning within its historical context** (whether Cassian's "co-operates" and "precedes" amount to what Augustine means by prevenient grace — Casiday vs. the older reading, Doc_01 §7) **and its application to this specific formation world** (whether the label "semi-Pelagian" — Heurtley's own footnote at *Comm.* ch. 24 [62] uses it — fairly describes the Massilian position; Doc_01 §7 rules the label anachronistic).
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** **cross-voice tension** — the one term where this world's own voice (Cassian), its provoking outsider (Augustine), and its later insider (Faustus, via Gennadius) all speak, and not in unison. Temporal note (Round 1 C6): Faustus is Native by Registry row 24, but his own episcopate runs into the 490s (Doc_01 §6), past this world's c. 450 window — the third leg of this classification sits outside the window and is carried as later insider testimony, not as in-window voice.
- **Registry.** 8, 9, 13, 14, 15, 16, 24 (via 30), 30.

### 5.2 free will / "the freedom of the will" / "the weakness of free will"
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian, a power "always ... free in man" that "can either neglect or delight in the grace of God," whose reality is proven by the disobedience of Israel and by David's thought to build the Temple ("good and from man"), yet whose "weakness" is such that "None of the righteous are sufficient of themselves to acquire righteousness"; "these two then; viz., the grace of God and free will seem opposed to each other, but really are in harmony."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* III.11–12 (Germanus: "Where then is there room for free will?"; Paphnutius: "the weakness of our free will is shown"), III.19 ("what we ought to ascribe to free will, and what to the design and daily assistance of the Lord"), III.21–22 ("we have no wish to do away with man's free will"); *Conf.* XIII.9–12 (XIII.10 "On the weakness of free will"; XIII.12 "the will always remains free in man"; "there are by nature some seeds of goodness in every soul implanted by the kindness of the Creator"); XIII.18 ("not to take away the freedom of the will which He Himself has once given"); Aug. *Praed.* ch. 2–6 (the Massilian position as reported: "that we have faith itself of ourselves, but that its increase is of God"); *Comm.* ch. 24 [62] (Pelagius "attributed so much antecedent strength to Free-will, as to deny the necessity of God's grace").
- **Voices.** Cassian; Vincent (against Pelagius); Augustine (context). Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT] [CT] — CT type, stated in full so the entry lifts individually at Doc_06 (Round 1 S8, minor): **the term's meaning within its historical context** (whether Cassian's "the will always remains free in man" and "seeds of goodness in every soul," *Conf.* XIII.12, amount to what Augustine grants or to what Prosper attacked — Casiday vs. the older reading, Doc_01 §7) and **its application to this specific formation world** (whether "semi-Pelagian" fairly describes the Massilian position; Doc_01 §7 rules the label anachronistic).
- **Tier (est.).** 1 (paired with 5.1). **AG.** weighted to Cassian; contested by Augustine.
- **Registry.** 8, 9, 13, 15.

### 5.3 the beginning of a good will / "the beginning of faith" (Lat. *initium fidei*, unverified — zero occurrences in the vendored `npnf211`; the English "beginning of faith" is Augustine's translators' phrase)
- **Preliminary definition.** The precise point of the controversy as this world's own text frames it: "does God have compassion upon us because we have shown the beginning of a good will, or does the beginning of a good will follow because God has had compassion upon us?" — a question Cassian answers with cases on both sides (Paul and Matthew vs. Zacchaeus and the thief) and declares beyond "the mind and reason of man." Augustine reports the Massilians as holding that faith's beginning is "of ourselves" while its increase is of God, and devotes the treatise to denying it.
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* XIII.11 (the question, verbatim), XIII.12 ("sometimes first beginnings of a good will arise, which however cannot attain to the complete performance of what is good unless it is guided by the Lord"), XIII.3 ("the initiative ... of good thoughts comes from God"), XIII.7 ("the very smallest spark of good will ... which He Himself has struck as it were out of the hard flints of our hearts"); *Conf.* III.19 ("the beginning of our good will is given to us by the inspiration of the Lord"); Aug. *Praed.* Argument and ch. 3 ("Even the Beginning of Faith is of God's Gift"), ch. 6 (the Massilian "compounds as it were with God to claim a portion of faith for himself"); *Persev.* Argument ("with the exception of the beginning of faith and the completion of perseverance").
- **Voices.** Cassian; Augustine (context). Southern node.
- **Tags.** [AS] [TC] [DR] [CT] — CT type: **the term's meaning within its historical context** (Cassian's own text says the beginning comes from God in some places and can arise "from our own efforts" in others, *Conf.* XIII.8; which is his position is the live question Prosper's *Contra Collatorem*, Registry row 32, was written to settle).
- **Tier (est.).** 1 — this is the world's own formulation of its defining argument.
- **AG.** **cross-voice tension** (Cassian vs. Augustine-reporting-Prosper/Hilary).
- **Registry.** 8, 9, 15, 16, 32 (via `npnf211` prolegomena — row 32's own text is unvendored and unread, known only through Gibson's description; Round 1 C5).

### 5.4 perseverance (Lat. *perseverantia* — corpus-supported only in Roberts's footnote on Halm's reading in the *Doubtful Letters*, editorial layer, Registry row 17; not at any Cassian or Augustine locus cited below)
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian's monastic usage, the endurance a novice proves at the door and must keep "even to the end" ("not he who begins these things, but he who endures in them to the end, shall be saved"), the third of Chaeremon's three stages of the Divine gift/grace at *Conf.* XIII.18 ("that it may be held by the persistence of the goodness already acquired"; the first draft's composite phrase "stages in Divine grace" is corrected, Round 1 C1). In Augustine's reply to Gaul, "the perseverance by which we persevere in Christ even to the end is the gift of God," and the Gallic brethren are reported as objecting that it must not be "so preached as that it cannot be obtained by prayer or lost by obstinacy."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.3 ("an evidence of his perseverance and desire"), IV.36; *Conf.* XIII.17 ("grants both the commencement of a good work and perseverance in it"), XIII.18 (the third stage); *Conf.* XIV.5 ("On perseverance in the line that has been chosen"); Aug. *Persev.* ch. 1, 10 (quoting Hilary's letter: "these brethren will not have this perseverance so preached..."); *Corrept.* Argument ("perseverance in good is truly a great gift of God"); *Vita* ch. 26 ("his perseverance and self-mastery in abstinence and fastings").
- **Voices.** Cassian; Augustine (context); Sulpitius (ordinary sense). Both nodes for the ordinary sense; the technical sense southern.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR]. Distortion: "perseverance of the saints" as a later Reformed doctrine — a modern-hearing gap, carried under the DR tag. The first draft's CT tag was removed after Round 1 (S8): it named a contest type but described only that modern-usage difference, and no live scholarly contest specific to this term was found to name; the Cassian/Augustine meaning-in-context contest belongs to 5.1 and 5.3 and is not re-asserted here as a separate contest.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** cross-voice.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 9, 14, 16.

### 5.5 predestination
- **Preliminary definition.** **Not this world's own word in anything read**: Cassian's Chaeremon argues that God "does not generally will all men, but only some instead of all to be saved" is "grievous blasphemy" and that "those ... who perish, perish against His will," without using the term; it is Augustine's word for what "those brethren" are "as yet in darkness on," and for what they object is "opposed to the usefulness of exhortation and rebuke."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* XIII.7 (the "all men to be saved" argument); Aug. *Praed.* Argument and ch. 2 ("as yet they are in darkness on the question concerning the predestination of the saints"); *Persev.* Argument ("that the definition of predestination is opposed to the usefulness of exhortation and rebuke"; "predestination should be preached in a harmonious manner").
- **Voices.** Augustine (context) only as a word; Cassian only as the thing denied. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [CT] — CT type: **application to this specific formation world** (it is the opponent's term, and Doc_02 §1.4 via Gennadius on Faustus shows the Massilian side's own positive doctrine was strongly grace-prevenient, not a simple denial).
- **Tier (est.).** 2 — as a contested *foreign* term the Representative must be able to handle at runtime, not as this world's own vocabulary.
- **AG.** **cross-voice tension.**
- **Registry.** 9, 15, 16.

### 5.6 co-operation / "the Lord's assistance" / "Helper," "Sponsor," "Protector"
- **Preliminary definition.** Cassian's characteristic verbs and titles for how grace relates to effort: "the co-operation of the Lord," grace that "co-operates with him when he was not idle or careless, but working and making an effort"; God as "Protector and Saviour" when He draws the unwilling and "Helper and Sponsor" when "we are already striving."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* III.12 ("by means of the co-operation of the Lord"), *Conf.* XIII.13 ("the grace of God always co-operates with our will"; "when he says: 'with me,' he affirms that it cooperates with him"), XIII.17 (the titles).
- **Voices.** Cassian only. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [AS] [TC] [DR]. Distortion: "synergism" as a later confessional label, which Doc_01 §7 already used ("synergy between divine grace and human will") — a modern analytic term this list flags rather than adopts; the world's own word is *co-operate*.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** **single-voice (Cassian).**
- **Registry.** 8, 9.

### 5.7 merit (Lat. *meritum*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) / "no antecedent merits"
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian, what human effort cannot claim over against grace ("from no antecedent merits of ours, but by the free grace of His pity He receives us"; "the main share in our salvation is to be ascribed not to the merit of our own works but to heavenly grace") — yet also a real category ("the merits of the saints," "merits ... of his own exertions" rejected). In Sulpitius, the ordinary Latin sense of a saint's *merita* by which a miracle is asked ("loose, by his pious merits, her tongue"; "the sacred merits of this man").
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* I.15; *Conf.* XIII.3, 12 ("we must take care not to refer all the merits of the saints to the Lord in such a way as to ascribe nothing but what is evil and perverse to human nature"), XIII.18; *Dial.* III.2, III.17; Aug. *Praed.* ch. 3 (Pelagius's condemned thesis "That the grace of God is given according to our merits").
- **Voices.** Cassian; Sulpitius (different sense); Augustine (context). Both nodes, different senses.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** cross-voice (different referents).
- **Registry.** 3, 8, 9, 15.

### 5.8 Massilians / "the Massilian clergy" — and "semi-Pelagian" (set aside)
- **Preliminary definition.** "Massilians" is a hostile outsider's geographical label for the Marseilles party, corroborated in Warfield's editorial footnote as Prosper's usage (with Prosper's own harsher phrase *reliquiae Pelagianorum*); it is **not** a self-designation in anything read, and by its etymology does not cover Lérins or Tours. "Semi-Pelagian" is a sixteenth-century coinage (Doc_01 §7) that appears in this world's vendored files only in the editors' apparatus (Gibson's headnote to *Conf.* XIII; Heurtley's footnote at *Comm.* ch. 24 [62]; Warfield's Argument to *Praed.*) — never in the ancient text.

They are now most commonly called 'Semi-Pelagians.'—W."); *Conf.* XIII headnote (Gibson: "On the Semi-Pelagianism of this Conference"); *Comm.* ch. 24 [62] fn. - **AG.** n/a (outsider vocabulary).
- **Registry.** 9, 13, 15, 17 (apparatus).

### 5.9 Pelagians as foil ("frogs, fleas, and flies"; "the profane notion of some")

## 6. Power, holiness, and the unseen

### 6.1 virtue / virtues (*virtus*, *virtutes*) = power, mighty work, miracle
- **Preliminary definition.** In Sulpitius, *virtus* is not primarily moral excellence but effective power — the "power" Martin perceives "present" before raising the dead, the "virtues [powers]" a raised man "experienced," the "abundance of power [*virtutum gratiam*]" Martin says he had more of as a monk than as a bishop, the "diminution of his power" after the Ithacian communion. Gennadius's own summary calls Martin "famous for signs and wonders and virtues," with Richardson glossing "Virtues or miracles." Cassian uses "virtue(s)" overwhelmingly in the moral sense and pointedly declines to narrate *virtutes*: "my purpose is to say a few words not about God's miracles, but about the way to improve our character."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 7 (Roberts's fn. "*adesse virtutem*"; "experienced the virtues [fn: Or 'powers'] of Martin"); ch. 13 ("through his virtues and example"); Sulp. *Ep.* I ("Was Martin really not possessed of power"); *Ep.* III fn. ("*potenti virtute verborum*"); *Dial.* II.4 (fn. "*eam virtutum gratiam*"); *Dial.* II.5 ("the works accomplished by his power"); *Dial.* III.6, 13–14; *Dial.* III (title "The Virtues of Martin Continued"); Gennadius ch. XIX (with Richardson's note); *Inst.* Preface ("Nor certainly shall I try to weave a tale of God's miracles and signs"); *Conf.* XVIII.1 ("not of the miracles of God, but of the institutes and pursuits of the saints"); *Inst.* IV.43 ("virtues shoot up and increase" — moral sense).
- **Voices.** Sulpitius (power sense, dominant); Cassian (moral sense; explicit refusal of the miracle-narrative); Gennadius corroborates the power sense for Martin. **Both nodes, in opposed senses.**
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [TC] [RT] [PV]. **PV: this is the sharpest cross-voice divergence found** — one voice's literature is built of *virtutes* the other declines on principle to record.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** **cross-voice tension** — flagged for Doc_04 as a candidate gravity that may hold only in the northern node.
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 7, 10, 30, 42 (Richardson's gloss).

### 6.2 grace as charism / "the gift of accomplishing cures" (*gratia* = χάρισμα)
- **Preliminary definition.** The second sense of *gratia* (distinguished from 5.1): a bestowed power — "the gift of accomplishing cures was so largely possessed by Martin," "the gifts of various graces," the "special grace of the Presbyter Isidore" to cast out demons, Abbot John's "grace of prophecy" won by obedience, "the gift of discretion."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 16 (Roberts's fn.: "The Latin word *gratia* here corresponds to the Greek χάρισμα"); *Dial.* III.10, III.13–14 ("repaired ... his grace, which had been diminished"); *Conf.* XVIII.15; *Inst.* IV.23 ("exalted even to the grace of prophecy"); *Conf.* II.1 ("the gift of discretion ... the greatest prize of divine grace"); *Conf.* XIV.7 ("his so evident grace"); *Conf.* XIV.5 ("according to the line which he has chosen as the grace which he has received").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [TC]. Distortion: collapsing this into 5.1 makes the grace controversy unreadable — Martin's "grace" is diminished by a bad communion; Cassian's controversial "grace" is the initiative of salvation.
- **Tier (est.).** 2 (must be developed alongside 5.1 at Doc_06). **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10.

### 6.3 blessing (Lat. *benedictio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) / blessed oil, threads, straw
- **Preliminary definition.** A transferable power residing in the saint's touch, word, and belongings: Martin blesses oil "such is the custom" and it multiplies; threads from his garment "wrought frequent miracles"; virgins hang his straw on a possessed person; Sulpitius "begged for his blessing according to custom"; a virgin's "blessing was by no means to be rejected by a priest."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 16, 18, 19 (the letter in the girl's bosom); Sulp. *Ep.* II; *Dial.* II.8, II.12, III.3 ("the blessing of God, bestowed on the vessel by Martin ... whose blessing could not possibly perish"); *Dial.* III.3 ("in the name of Martin, to be quiet"); *Dial.* III.14 (the Egyptian merchant: "Save us, O God of Martin"); *Conf.* XVIII.11 ("collect the prayer" — a different, liturgical sense, Gibson's fn.).
- **Voices.** Sulpitius. Northern node. (Cassian's *Inst.* IV.19 "the reward of this blessing" is the ordinary sense.)
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT].
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** **single-voice (Sulpitius).**
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 10.

### 6.4 the devil / demons — visible, named, conversed with
- **Preliminary definition.** In Sulpitius, an adversary Martin "held ... so visible and ever under the power of his eyes" that he perceives him "under whatever guise," who appears as a man on the road, as Jupiter, Mercury, Minerva, as a purple-robed Christ; demons "revealed their names" (Jupiter, Mercury) under exorcism, sit on a cow's back, behind a courtier's neck, on a rock calling Brictio. In Cassian, "our cunning adversary," "the subtle serpent ... watching our heel," who appears as "a filthy Ethiopian," as "an angel of light," and whose "illusions" the elders' discretion alone detects.
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 6, 21, 22 (Martin offers the devil mercy if he repents — Roberts's fn. records Hornius's outrage), 23, 24; *Dial.* II.9, II.13, III.6, III.8, III.15; *Inst.* IV.9, 37; *Conf.* I.21, II.5–8 (Heron; "an angel of Satan as an angel of light"), II.13 (the Ethiopian's fiery darts); *Conf.* XVIII.15 (the envious brother "possessed by a most fierce demon"); *Comm.* ch. 26 (the devil quotes Scripture; "the members of the Devil to the members of Christ").
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: the modern hearing (metaphor or psychology) vs. this world's literal, named, embodied adversary.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none — genuinely shared, though the *visibility* to Martin is Sulpitius's emphasis.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7, 8, 10, 13.

### 6.5 the possessed (energumens) / exorcism / exorcist

## 7. Rule, memory, and the Church's faith (Vincent's cluster, with cross-voice checks)

### 7.1 Catholic / "the Catholic faith" / "true and genuine Catholic"
- **Preliminary definition.** Vincent's central self-designation, defined etymologically: "that is truly and in the strictest sense 'Catholic,' which, as the name itself and the reason of the thing declare, comprehends all universally"; "he is the true and genuine Catholic who ... resolves that he will believe that, and that only, which he is sure the Catholic Church has held universally and from ancient time." Cassian uses it as the authority of "the approved Catholic fathers" who "have the right to coin," and of "the Catholic rule" for monastic dress; Sulpitius of the party opposed to Priscillian.
- **Evidence.** *Comm.* ch. 2 [4, 6], ch. 20 [48], ch. 27 [70], ch. 33 [86]; *Conf.* I.20 ("the approved Catholic fathers"); *Conf.* XIII.5 ("the value of the Catholic faith"), XIII.12, XIII.18 ("all the Catholic fathers"); *Inst.* I.2 ("the Catholic rule"); *Sacred History* II.51 ("revolted from the Catholics"); *Conf.* XVIII.7 ("the need of the Catholic faith compelled me to visit").
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [TC] [RT]. Distortion: "Catholic" heard as the Roman Catholic confession — a modern-hearing gap, carried under the DR tag. The first draft's CT tag was removed after Round 1 (S8): it named a contest type but described only that modern-usage difference, and no live scholarly contest specific to this term was found to name (the contests that do exist in this cluster are 7.2's, stated there).
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none as a word; Vincent's *definition* of it is single-voice.
- **Registry.** 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 13.

### 7.2 the rule (Lat. *regula*, unverified for Vincent — his own Latin for "rule" is not given in the vendored translation; the volume's only *regula* witness is the RB footnote noted at 3.6, which is monastic and editorial, not Vincent's) of faith — "universality, antiquity, consent"; "quod ubique, quod semper, quod ab omnibus"
- **Preliminary definition.** Vincent's "sure and so to speak universal rule" for distinguishing "the truth of Catholic faith from the falsehood of heretical pravity": "that we hold that faith which has been believed everywhere, always, by all. ... This rule we shall observe if we follow universality, antiquity, consent," with a procedure for each failure (a part against the whole; novelty against antiquity; a few against a General Council or the consentient Fathers).
- **Evidence.** *Comm.* ch. 2 [4–6], ch. 3 [7–8], ch. 27 [70], ch. 29 [76–77]; ch. 28 [71] ("where the Rule of Faith is concerned"). The Latin tag is Doc_01 §5's; Heurtley's translation gives "everywhere, always, by all."
- **Voices.** Vincent only as a formulated rule; but Cassian's *Inst.* I.2 independently states the same three-part test for monastic custom ("not to those customs and rules which the will of a few have introduced, but to those which a long standing antiquity and numbers of the holy fathers have passed on by an unanimous decision") — antiquity, numbers, unanimity. Southern node.
- **Tags.** [AS] [TC] [DR] [RT] [CT] — CT type: **historical scope** (whether the rule was ever applied as broadly as later use claims — Heurtley's own Appendix III on Vincent's handling of Celestine's letter is an in-file instance of that contest) and **relationship to present-day traditions** (the "Vincentian canon" in later Anglican/Catholic/Orthodox polemic).
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** **single-voice (Vincent)** as a doctrinal rule — but with the *Inst.* I.2 parallel as evidence that the same criterion governed monastic custom in Cassian; Doc_04 should test whether this is one gravity or two.
- **Registry.** 7, 13.

### 7.3 novelty (Lat. *novitas*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) vs. antiquity — "profane novelties"
- **Preliminary definition.** The axis of Vincent's whole treatise (its title: "Against the Profane Novelties of All Heresies"): "if novelty is to be shunned, antiquity is to be held fast; if novelty is profane, antiquity is sacred"; the papal sentence "let novelty cease to assail antiquity." **Independently attested in Cassian** as a monastic vice: thoughts must be weighed "whether it be light with human display or some conceit of novelty"; the disciple is "never to do anything novel nor to decide anything by or on our own responsibility"; dress must avoid "novelty of colour or fashion" and "the novelty of the garb." And in Sulpitius, Martin's refusal of a cult with "no steady tradition ... from antiquity."
- **Evidence.** *Comm.* title, ch. 4 [11], ch. 6 [16, 18], ch. 21 [51–52], ch. 24 [60–63], ch. 32 [84–85], ch. 33; *Conf.* I.21, II.11; *Inst.* I.2, I.10; *Vita* ch. 11; *Conf.* XVIII.7 ("the novel crime" of Ananias).
- **Voices.** All three. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] [RT]. Distortion: "novelty" as harmless newness.
- **Tier (est.).** 1. **AG.** none — a genuinely cross-voice value-word; this is the strongest lexical link found between Vincent's doctrinal method and Cassian's monastic method, and between both and Martin.
- **Registry.** 1, 7, 8, 10, 13.

### 7.5 progress (Lat. *profectus*) vs. alteration (Lat. *permutatio*) — both lemmas unverified, no lemma in the vendored translation — the growth of the body

### 7.6 trial (Lat. *tentatio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) — "the teacher's error is the people's trial"

Cassian's Chaeremon uses the same Deut. 13 text for God's testing of *individual* free will (Job, Abraham, the centurion); Sulpitius's Martin is "tried by that danger" of the fire and "tempted by the wiles of the devil."

### 7.7 Doctor / Master / "prophet" in the Church; Expositors (*Tractatores*)

### 7.8 council — "General," "Universal," "the holy council ... at Ephesus"

### 7.10 heretic / heresy / "heretical pravity"

### 7.11 communion (Lat. *communio*, unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation) — to be in / to withhold

## 8. Office, body, and place

### 8.1 bishop / "chief priest" (*summus sacerdos*) / priest / the episcopate — and the monk-bishop
- **Preliminary definition.** The office this world's ascetics keep entering: Martin "called upon to undertake the episcopate" by popular vote over bishops' objections to his "despicable" appearance; "we have seen numbers of these afterwards made bishops" from Marmoutier; Cassian's Archebius "carried off from the assembly of anchorites and given as Bishop," complaining "that he had been expelled from the monastic system as unworthy"; Cassian's own dedicatees are bishops; Vincent's Lérins produced bishops of Arles and Riez (Doc_01 §4, quoting Heurtley's introduction — editorial layer, not re-read this pass). ### 8.4 virgin / virginity / "the dress characteristic of virginity"; the "choir of virgins"
- **Preliminary definition.** A consecrated state with its own dress and its own "glory": Arborius's daughter "consecrated ... to God, and devoted ... to perpetual virginity" by Martin's placing the dress on her; "the choir of virgins" at his funeral; Martin's meadow-parable ("nothing is to be compared with virginity"; marriage "may be excused," virginity "points to glory"); the virgin who would not admit Martin himself, praised for it. In Cassian, "the flowers of a virgin life" of Elijah, and the layman who keeps his wife "as a virgin in the place of a sister." Registry row 5's pseudepigraphal letter to Claudia calls virgins "the brides of Christ" veiled "after the manner of brides."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 19; Sulp. *Ep.* III; *Dial.* II.10, II.12; *Dial.* II.2, II.8 (virgins as witnesses and relic-keepers); *Inst.* I.1; *Conf.* XIV.7; *Doubtful Letters* II.1–3 (Native, row 5, "pseudepigraphal literature"); *Comm.* ch. 24 [61] ("the virgins" among the faithful of all ages). **Conference XII "On Chastity" is absent from this edition** (Registry row 11).
- **Voices.** Sulpitius (dominant); Cassian; the Claudia letter. Both nodes, weighted north; the southern node's own chastity treatise is an evidential gap.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [RT]. No PV tag: the first draft's PV tag was removed after Round 1 (S9) — the gap here is an edition-level suppression (Conference XII absent), not a demonstrated divergence between voices, which is §0's stated criterion. Doc_01 §4's Missing Voices concern (women's presence) and the Conf. XII/XXII excision still meet the lexicon here; they are carried in the AG line and §10 item 4.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** weighted to Sulpitius **because the Cassian evidence is edition-suppressed**, not because Cassian lacked the vocabulary — a flag Doc_06 must carry.
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13.

### 8.5 sackcloth and ashes (*cilicium*)
- **Preliminary definition.** Martin's "well-known weapons" (sackcloth, ashes, fasting, continuous prayer) and his deathbed: "It is not fitting that a Christian should die except among ashes"; threads "plucked from the sackcloth which he wore" work cures. **Cassian's Egyptians hold the opposite:** "they utterly disapproved of a robe of sackcloth as being visible to all and conspicuous, and what from this very fact will not only confer no benefit on the soul but rather minister to vanity and pride" — while conceding "some respectable persons ... have been dressed in this garb."
- **Evidence.** *Vita* ch. 14 (Roberts's fn.: "*Cilicium* ... was worn by soldiers and others"), ch. 18; Sulp. *Ep.* I, *Ep.* III; *Dial.* II.5, III.6; *Inst.* I.2.
- **Voices.** Sulpitius (practice); Cassian (rejection). Both nodes, **directly opposed**.
- **Tags.** [SC] [DR] [PV] — the clearest documented disagreement in practice between the two nodes found in this pass.
- **Tier (est.).** 3 as a word; but the disagreement it carries is Doc_04 material.
- **AG.** **cross-voice tension.**
- **Registry.** 1, 2, 3, 7.

### 8.6 the monk's dress (girdle, hood, tunic/colobium, cords, mafors, sheepskin/melotes, staff, sandals) — and "the humble character of our profession"
- **Preliminary definition.** Cassian's opening subject: each Egyptian garment read as a "mystery [fn: *Sacramentum*]" of the interior life (girdle = mortified loins; sleeveless tunic = "dead to all earthly conversation"; hood = "the simplicity and innocence of little children"; staff = arms against "invisible beasts"), then explicitly modified for Gaul: "the severity of the winter does not allow us to be satisfied with slippers or tunics or a single frock; and the covering of tiny hoods or the wearing of a sheepskin would afford a subject for derision instead of edifying." Sulpitius's monks wear "garments of camels' hair" and Martin "his shaggy garment, with a long black cloak"; "any dress approaching to softness was there deemed criminal."
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* I.1–11 (whole; I.10 the climate clause, with Gibson's fn. that Gazæus omits it); *Vita* ch. 10; *Dial.* II.1 (Roberts's fns.: *amphibalum*, *bigerricam vestem*), II.3.
- **Voices.** Cassian (systematic); Sulpitius (descriptive). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR] — received Egyptian dress, explicitly adapted for Gaul, hence Origin SC, not AS (Round 1 S1). Distortion: "habit" as later uniform; Cassian's dress is a lexicon of meanings and an explicitly climate-adapted one.
- **Tier (est.).** 2 (as a cluster; individual garments Tier 3). **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 1, 3, 7.

### 8.7 penance / satisfaction / "suspended from prayer" / absolution
- **Preliminary definition.** In Cassian, the public discipline of the coenobium: to "expiate his carelessness by public penance," lying on the ground "and ask for absolution until the service of the prayers is finished"; "suspended from prayer" for three spilled lentils; graver faults "atoned for by expulsion" or stripes; Paphnutius, falsely accused, "humbly asked for a plan of repentance." In Sulpitius, Brictio "brought back to repentance," the soldiers "sufficiently punished by their conscience alone," Martin's own "compunction" after the Ithacian communion. Gennadius lists a Conference "On true satisfaction in repentance" (XX, not read).
- **Evidence.** *Inst.* IV.16, IV.20; *Conf.* XVIII.15; *Dial.* II.3, III.13, III.15; *Conf.* II.5 (Heron nearly denied "the memorial and oblation for those at rest"); Gennadius ch. LXII.
- **Voices.** Cassian (institutional), Sulpitius (personal). Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [SC] [TC] [DR]. Distortion: later sacramental penance.
- **Tier (est.).** 3. **AG.** weighted to Cassian.
- **Registry.** 3, 7, 8, 10, 30.

### 8.8 Gaul / "the cold of Gaul" / "We are Gauls"
- **Preliminary definition.** This world's own self-location, spoken in two registers: Cassian's "this province that is frozen as it were with the cold of Gaul," "a new establishment in the West, in the parts of Gaul," the climate that forbids Egyptian food and dress; and Sulpitius's self-mocking "as you are wont to say, when you are accused of being too fond of eating, 'We are Gauls,'" with "you men of learning, or those at least who are from Greece" set against "we Gallic country-people." Postumianus is charged to tell the East that "Christ has by no means forsaken Gaul, since he has granted it to possess such a man as Martin."
- **Evidence.** *Conf.* Pref. II; *Inst.* Preface; *Inst.* I.10, IV.10–11 ("in this country neither the climate nor the weakness of our constitution would permit"); *Conf.* Pref. III ("that way which is difficult and almost unknown in this country"); *Dial.* II.1, II.8, III.17–18; Gennadius ch. LXV ("Vincentius, the Gaul").
- **Voices.** Sulpitius, Cassian; Gennadius. Salvian (row 43, added in the Round 1 revision) supplies a third register: his self-location is "we Romans" against "the barbarians," and Gaul appears as the ruined province — "the country of the Belgae burst into flames, then the rich estates of the luxurious Aquitanians, and after these the whole body of the Gallic provinces" (*Gov.* VII.12); "we Gauls" is not his phrase in what was read. Both nodes.
- **Tags.** [AS] [DR] [RT] — kept [AS] after Round 1 (S1): self-location in Gaul is this world's own, not received. Distortion: modern "France"; and the assumption that a Gallic identity is proud rather than, as here, self-deprecating and derivative of Egypt.
- **Tier (est.).** 2. **AG.** none.
- **Registry.** 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 30, 43.
