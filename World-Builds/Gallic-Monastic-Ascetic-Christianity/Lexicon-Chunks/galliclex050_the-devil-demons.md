## Quick Meaning

For this world the devil is a real, present, and at times visible adversary: Martin "held him so visible and ever under the power of his eyes" that he knew him "under whatever guise he appeared" — a man on the road, Jupiter, Mercury, a purple-robed Christ — and demons "revealed their names"; Cassian's "cunning adversary" comes as "a filthy Ethiopian" or "an angel of light" and is detected by the elders' discretion; Vincent's devil quotes Scripture.

---

## World Meaning

At Tours the enemy has a face and a voice. On the road past Milan "the devil met him in the way, having assumed the form of a man," and said, "Wherever you go, or whatever you attempt, the devil will resist you"; Martin answered him "in the prophetical word." It is "well known" that Martin "held him so visible and ever under the power of his eyes, that whether he kept himself in his proper form, or changed himself into different shapes of spiritual wickedness, he was perceived by Martin, under whatever guise he appeared"; the devil "thrust himself upon him in a visible form, but in very various shapes. For sometimes he presented himself to his view changed into the person of Jupiter, often into that of Mercury and Minerva." Under exorcism the demons "revealed their names, too, of their own accord; one acknowledged that he was Jupiter, another Mercury." The devil accuses the brethren's past sins, and Martin, "resisting the devil firmly," offers even him the mercy he offers sinners: "If thou, thyself, wretched being, wouldst but desist from attacking mankind, and even, at this period, when the day of judgment is at hand, wouldst only repent of your deeds, I, with a true confidence in the Lord, would promise you the mercy of Christ." At the deathbed the last words to him are a soldier's: "Why do you stand here, thou bloody monster? Thou shalt find nothing in me, thou deadly one."

At Marseilles the adversary is no less real and less often seen. He is "our cunning adversary" who "cannot in any way circumvent a young and inexperienced monk" once thoughts are disclosed; "the subtle serpent" who is "ever 'watching our heel'"; the one who came to Abbot John "in the shape of a filthy Ethiopian" and to another "standing over against his cell and aiming fiery darts at him"; and, most dangerously, the one Heron "received with the utmost reverence" as "an angel of Satan as an angel of light." The rule of the whole southern discipline is that "a thought is from the devil if we are ashamed to disclose it." At Lérins Vincent reminds the reader that Satan "transforms himself into an angel of light" and quotes Scripture as he did to the Lord, so that "the members of the Devil" speak now "to the members of Christ" in the Church's own words. Nowhere in this world is he a figure of speech.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A metaphor for psychological conflict, "the shadow," or social evil; demons as a primitive explanation for illness; Martin's conversations as legend.

**World Hearing:**
A named, embodied, and sometimes visible adversary who takes the shapes of the old gods and of Christ himself, who is fought with the cross and disclosed by shame, who can be spoken to and even offered mercy, and who quotes Scripture in church — the object of the whole ecology's warfare, at both nodes.
