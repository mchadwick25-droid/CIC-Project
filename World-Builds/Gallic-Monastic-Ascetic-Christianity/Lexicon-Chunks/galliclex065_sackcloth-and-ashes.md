## Quick Meaning

For this world sackcloth and ashes are, at Tours, Martin's "well-known weapons" — worn before an emperor, worn to exorcise, worn on the deathbed ("It is not fitting that a Christian should die except among ashes"), and cut into threads that heal — while Cassian's Egyptians "utterly disapproved of a robe of sackcloth as being visible to all and conspicuous," fit only to "minister to vanity and pride."

## World Meaning

At Tours the garment is an arm. Refused an audience by Valentinian, Martin "had recourse to his well-known weapons—he clothes himself in sackcloth, scatters ashes upon his person, abstains from meat and drink." When a pagan crowd stops him from destroying a temple, he withdraws "and there for three days, clothed in sackcloth and ashes fasting and praying the whole time, he besought the Lord." To exorcise, "the doors being bolted, clothed in sackcloth and sprinkled with ashes, he stretched himself on the ground in the midst of the church, and turned to prayer." Dying, asked to let straw be put under him, he answers: "It is not fitting that a Christian should die except among ashes; and I have sinned if I leave you a different example." The people at his death exclaimed, "Who would ever believe that man to be clothed in sackcloth, or who would imagine that he was enveloped with ashes?" — so radiant was the body. And "threads from Martin's garment, or such as had been plucked from the sackcloth which he wore, wrought frequent miracles upon those who were sick." The sackcloth is the saint's power made visible and portable.

At Marseilles the visibility is exactly the objection. Setting out the Egyptians' dress, Cassian says "they utterly disapproved of a robe of sackcloth as being visible to all and conspicuous, and what from this very fact will not only confer no benefit on the soul but rather minister to vanity and pride, and as being inconvenient and unsuitable for the performance of necessary work." He knows the north's kind of case and does not yield to it: "even if we hear of some respectable persons who have been dressed in this garb, a rule for the monasteries is not, therefore, to be passed by us, nor should the ancient decrees of the holy fathers be interfered with" — the decision belongs to "those customs and rules ... which a long standing antiquity and numbers of the holy fathers have passed on by an unanimous decision." One garment; Tours's weapon and Marseilles's vanity; and neither text has read the other.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A hair shirt as medieval self-torment; "sackcloth and ashes" as a figure of speech for regret; or a single monastic view of penitential dress.

**World Hearing:**
At Tours, a weapon and a witness — the garment of prayer, exorcism, and death, whose threads cure; at Marseilles, a conspicuous robe the fathers rejected as vanity and as unfit for work, "even if we hear of some respectable persons" who wore it — two hearings the world never reconciled.
