## Quick Meaning

For this world a heretic is, in Vincent's Lérins, one who "dissever[s] himself from the consentient agreement of the universality and antiquity of the Catholic Church," arising always "under a definite name, at a definite place, at a definite time"; in Sulpitius's Tours, Priscillian's "Gnostics," condemned "by a sentence of the bishops" and then killed by an emperor — "a foul and unheard-of indignity, that a secular ruler should be judge in an ecclesiastical cause"; and in Cassian, the "errors of heretics" a monk is enticed into by "grace of style."

---

## World Meaning

Vincent defines the heretic by what he leaves. "What heresy ever burst forth save under a definite name, at a definite place, at a definite time? Who ever originated a heresy that did not first dissever himself from the consentient agreement of the universality and antiquity of the Catholic Church?" The book exists "to distinguish the truth of Catholic faith from the falsehood of heretical pravity," and its list runs from Pelagius to Nestorius, each a man who set his own opinion against all. Heresy is novelty with a birthday; the remedy is antiquity, and the sanction is Paul's — "let him be accursed," "separated, segregated, excluded, lest the dire contagion of a single sheep contaminate the guiltless flock." Cassian's elders know the heretic as a hazard of reading: monks "enticed by the grace of style, and certain doctrines of philosophers" are drawn "into the errors of heretics, and bombastic conceits" — coin not stamped by "the approved Catholic fathers."

At Tours the heretic is a man with a body. Priscillian's followers are "the Gnostics," and Sulpitius does not doubt they are heretics. But when the affair goes to Trier and the emperor's court, Martin's position is fixed: "he maintained that it was quite sufficient punishment that, having been declared heretics by a sentence of the bishops, they should have been expelled from the churches; and that it was, besides, a foul and unheard-of indignity, that a secular ruler should be judge in an ecclesiastical cause." He goes to Trier, as Gallus says, "to protect even heretics themselves," and the executions wait while he is there. The heretic is to be cast out of the Church; he is not to be killed; and the bishops who urged the sword are the ones Martin will not sit with again.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Heretic" as a slur for any dissenter, or as the Inquisition's category; heresy as intellectual nonconformity; Martin's defence of the Priscillianists as religious toleration.

**World Hearing:**
A man who has left the consent of the universal and ancient Church under his own name — to be excluded, anathematized, and kept from the monk's reading — but judged by bishops, not emperors, and expelled, not executed; a category in the south, a man in the north, and the state's sword "a foul and unheard-of indignity" in both.
