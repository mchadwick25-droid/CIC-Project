## Quick Meaning

For this world the end is near, not distant: false Christs in Spain and the East show "that the coming of Antichrist is at hand; for he is already practicing in these persons the mystery of iniquity"; Martin teaches that "Nero and Antichrist have first to come," that Antichrist "was already born, and had, by this time, reached the years of boyhood"; and Vincent writes under "a certain awful expectation of the approach of the divine judgment."

---

## World Meaning

Martin reads the times. From the young man in Spain who claimed to be Elias and then Christ, and another in the East, "we may infer ... that the coming of Antichrist is at hand; for he is already practicing in these persons the mystery of iniquity." Asked about the end of the world, "he said to us that Nero and Antichrist have first to come; that Nero will rule in the Western portion of the world," Antichrist in the East; and "that Antichrist, having been conceived by an evil spirit, was already born, and had, by this time, reached the years of boyhood, while he would assume power as soon as he reached the proper age. Now, this is the eighth year since we heard these words from his lips: you may conjecture, then, how nearly about to happen are those things which are feared in the future." Sulpitius's own religion is "a weariness of the present world, a terror of judgment, a fear of punishment"; even the devil is offered mercy "at this period, when the day of judgment is at hand." Vincent begins his book because "a certain awful expectation of the approach of the divine judgment importunately demands increased earnestness in religion, while the subtle craftiness of new heretics calls for no ordinary care." Cassian names "the fear of hell" only as the first of three restraints the monk climbs past. In Salvian, in the same years, the judgment has begun.
