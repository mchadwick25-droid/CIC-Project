## Quick Meaning

For this world tradition is what has been received from predecessors and must be passed on unchanged — Vincent's second criterion of truth after Scripture, "the Tradition of the Catholic Church"; Pope Stephen's rule, "Let there be no innovation—nothing but what has been handed down"; Cassian's "traditions of the Elders" by which thoughts are judged and faults remedied; and the "steady tradition ... from antiquity" without which Martin will not honour a tomb.

---

## World Meaning

Vincent puts the word second and makes it decisive. Catholic truth is guarded "in two ways; first, by the authority of the Divine Law, and then, by the Tradition of the Catholic Church" — the second needed because heretics quote Scripture too. The model is a Pope's letter to Africa: "Let there be no innovation—nothing but what has been handed down." What Timothy is to keep is "not of private adoption, but of public tradition; a matter brought to thee, not put forth by thee." Cassian's monks live by the same word in a smaller room. The faults are treated with "the remedies for them according to their traditions"; the junior learns "to acknowledge what ought to be considered good or bad by their traditions"; the monk who "relied on his own judgment rather than on the traditions of the Elders ... forsook the desert." Monasteries stand at all only "through a succession of fathers and their traditions," and the Sarabaites are those not "taught by their traditions." Tradition here is not a body of doctrine but the elders' handed-down way of judging a thought, a fast, a garment.

At Tours the word governs a cult. Shown a tomb the crowd revered, Martin "often asked from those who were his elders, whether among the presbyters or clerics, that the name of the martyr, or the time when he suffered, should be made known to him," because "no steady tradition respecting them had come down from antiquity" — and rather than lend his authority "lest a mere superstition should obtain a firmer footing," he goes and asks the dead man himself, who confesses he was a robber. The same test that Vincent applies to a doctrine and Cassian to a custom, Martin applies to a grave: nothing is honoured that was not handed down.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Tradition" as a rival authority to Scripture in a confessional debate; or as custom and folkways; or as conservatism.

**World Hearing:**
What was handed down, tested by whether it can be traced — a doctrine to the Fathers, a custom to the Elders, a cult to "antiquity" — and passed on unaltered by a keeper who is "not an author"; the ground on which both a Pope's letter and a monk's fast are judged, and on which a bishop refuses a tomb.
