## Quick Meaning

For this world the characteristic failure of a monk is not apostasy but cooling — the novice who "grow[s] cold in regard to the rule," the renunciant "turned out a deserter or lukewarm," the elders who "pass their old age in a lukewarmness which they contracted in youth," the Sarabaites "cast down into hell by the lukewarmness of their purpose" — a disease of temperature that damns.

---

## World Meaning

The word is on the steward's cupboard. The new monk's worldly clothes are kept, and the brethren "do not allow any one to be any longer dressed in" the monastery's dress "if they have seen him once grow cold in regard to the rule of their institution"; the renunciant who was "aglow with spiritual fervour" and then, "when he has cooled down," wants his money back is the case in point. Pinufius warns the newly received of the "punishment, if, when you had been too easily admitted by us without realizing the responsibility of this profession, you had afterwards turned out a deserter or lukewarm," and charges him: "Consider therefore that you belong to the few and elect; and do not grow cold after the examples of the lukewarmness of many." It is one of discretion's two ditches — "to grow slack with the opposite spirit of luke-warmness" — and it explains why a grey head is no elder: "there are some, and unhappily they form the majority, who pass their old age in a lukewarmness which they contracted in youth, and in sloth, and so obtain authority not from the ripeness of their character but simply from the number of their years." Even the Church's first days are remembered by it: the primitive community's common life held "when the fervent faith of the few had not yet grown lukewarm by being dispersed among the many." Of the false monks: "the others are cast down into hell by the lukewarmness of their purpose." And grace answers it — "if He finds that we are unwilling or have grown cold, He stirs our hearts with salutary exhortations."

In the same city Salvian reads the Apocalypse to ordinary Christians: "he who is betwixt and between is a lukewarm Christian and hateful to the Lord, who therefore says to him: 'Would that thou wert hot or cold'"; such men are "cast out because of lukewarmness." The monk's word is the Christian's word. At Tours no one says it; but Brictio, sneering at his master's "ridiculous fancies about visions," is what a cooled disciple looks like from outside.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Mild disapproval — "lukewarm" as half-hearted or unenthusiastic; burnout as an understandable phase; loss of zeal as maturity.

**World Hearing:**
A spiritual disease that damns — the deserter's condition, the false elder's old age, the Sarabaite's hell — measured against a fervour that even the apostolic Church lost by dispersion; the monk's word and, in Salvian, the ordinary Christian's, from the same verse of the Apocalypse.
