## Quick Meaning

For this world an illusion is the false appearance by which a monk is ruined — a robe said to come from heaven, a visible Christ in purple and a crown, an angel's promise at the well's edge, revelations that end in circumcision — the standing danger discretion exists to meet, told in the same shape at Tours and at Marseilles.

---

## World Meaning

At Tours the devil's deceptions are exposed by being brought before Martin. The youth Anatolius, "under the profession of a monk," claims angels speak with him and produces a robe "from heaven" — which vanishes when the brethren insist on taking him to Martin: "who can doubt that this, too, was an illustration of the power of Martin, so that the devil could no longer dissemble or conceal his own deception, when it was to be submitted to the eyes of Martin?" Then the devil himself comes "clothed in purple, and with a glittering crown upon his head," and says he is Christ; Martin, "dazzled by his first appearance," keeps "a long and deep silence" until the Spirit reveals it, and answers: "I will not believe that Christ has come, unless he appears with that appearance and form in which he suffered, and openly displaying the marks of his wounds upon the cross." The house has its own skeptic, too: Brictio, raging, calls his master "sunk into dotage by means of his baseless superstitions, and ridiculous fancies about visions" — the sneer from inside.

At Marseilles the same stories come from Egypt with the rule attached. "The illusion of Abbot John," who "had put off taking food during a fast of two days" and "on the third day," on his way to eat, was met by the devil "in the shape of a filthy Ethiopian" crying, "Pardon me because I appointed this labour for you"; Heron, who "was cast down by an illusion of the devil from the heights to the depths," having "received with the utmost reverence an angel of Satan as an angel of light" and thrown himself into a well on its promise; the man whose revelations ended in Judaism — each the fruit of trusting one's own judgment against the elders' discretion. The night has its illusions too: the monk must not sleep after the night office "lest our envious adversary ... should by some illusion in a dream pollute the purity which has been gained by the Psalms and prayers of the night." A whole conference, "On Nocturnal Illusions," was written on the subject; this world's English text says of it only, "This Conference is omitted."

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Hallucination or delusion as psychology; "discernment" as a private test; Martin's refusal of the purple Christ as skepticism about miracles in general.

**World Hearing:**
A real deception by a real deceiver, exposed by the saint's presence (Tours) or the elders' judgment (Marseilles), with the crucified form as the one test the devil cannot pass; a danger the world wrote a whole conference on, which this world's English text removed.
