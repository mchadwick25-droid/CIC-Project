## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, the practice of laying every "itching thought" bare to the senior as soon as it arises — "the alphabet, as it were, and first syllables in the direction of perfection" — on the elders' rule that "a thought is from the devil if we are ashamed to disclose it," so that "a wrong thought is enfeebled at the moment that it is discovered."

---

## World Meaning

It is the first thing the Egyptians teach and the last thing Cassian stops repeating. The juniors "are next taught not to conceal by a false shame any itching thoughts in their hearts, but, as soon as ever such arise, to lay them bare to the senior, and, in forming a judgment about them, not to trust anything to their own discretion, but to take it on trust that that is good or bad which is considered and pronounced so by the examination of the senior." The test is shame: "they lay it down as an universal and clear proof that a thought is from the devil if we are ashamed to disclose it to the senior." Thus "our cunning adversary cannot in any way circumvent a young and inexperienced monk." Abbot Pinufius makes it the monk's standing guard: "do you ever 'watch his head,' i.e. the first rise of thoughts, by bringing them at once to your superior." Abbot Moses names it the first proof of humility — "reserving everything (not only what you do but also what you think), for the scrutiny of the elders" — and gives its mechanism: "a wrong thought is enfeebled at the moment that it is discovered: and even before the sentence of discretion has been given," the serpent is dragged into the light.

The story that carries it is Serapion's. As a boy in the desert he stole a biscuit a day; hearing an old man speak after supper "about the sin of gluttony and the dominion of secret thoughts, and showing their nature and the awful power which they have so long as they are kept secret," he "produced from the folds of my dress which shared my theft and received it, the biscuit which I had carried off," and "lying on the ground an begging for forgiveness confessed." The old man's answer: "Without any words of mine, your confession frees you from this slavery. For you have today triumphed over your victorious adversary, by laying him low by your confession." The danger on the other side is a harsh elder who shames the one who discloses — Moses's next chapters — which is why not every grey head is fit to hear. What is disclosed is a thought, not a deed; it is told to an elder, not a priest; and it is told before it is acted on, so that it need never be.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Sacramental confession of sins to a priest, with absolution; or spiritual direction and "accountability" as optional counsel; or therapy.

**World Hearing:**
The disclosure of *thoughts* — before any deed — to an elder whose "examination" replaces one's own judgment, tested by shame and freeing "at the moment that it is discovered"; the first syllable of the monastic alphabet, and Egypt's practice, prescribed for a Gallic house.
