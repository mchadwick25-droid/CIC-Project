## Quick Meaning

At Lérins Vincent's book is, by its own name, a memory-aid — "a Commonitory (or Remembrancer) for myself, such as may aid my memory, or rather, provide against my forgetfulness" — written "with the fidelity of a narrator rather than the presumption of an author" by "Peregrinus," "the least of all the servants of God"; Gennadius knows it as "Peregrinus against heretics" and records that "the greater part of the second book of this work having been stolen, he composed a brief reproduction."

---

## World Meaning

"I, Peregrinus, who am the least of all the servants of God," writes because Scripture says "Ask thy fathers," and because memory fails. The plan is stated as a keeper's, not an author's: "to record with the fidelity of a narrator rather than the presumption of an author, the things which our forefathers have handed down to us"; "for me it will be enough to have provided a Commonitory (or Remembrancer) for myself, such as may aid my memory, or rather, provide against my forgetfulness." The name says what the whole ecology says about authority — that a book is a memory of what was received, and its writer a pilgrim and a servant. The memory-structure could not keep itself whole: Gennadius, who knows Vincent as "the Gaul, presbyter in the Monastery on the Island of Lerins," reports that "the greater part of the second book of this work having been stolen, he composed a brief reproduction of the substance" — the remembrancer against forgetting lost half of itself, and was summarized against the loss.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:** "Commonitory" heard as if it named a doctrinal treatise, or the pseudonym "Peregrinus" heard as a dramatic literary device for effect.

**World Hearing:** Vincent states plainly that the book is a private memory-aid against his own forgetting, not a treatise for others' instruction in the first instance; and the pen-name "Pilgrim" states his own stance toward the faith — a recipient and keeper of what was handed down, never its author.
