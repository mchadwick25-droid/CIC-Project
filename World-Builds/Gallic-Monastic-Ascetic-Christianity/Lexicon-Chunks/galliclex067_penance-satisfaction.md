## Quick Meaning

For this world penance is the public discipline of the coenobium — to "expiate his carelessness by public penance," lying on the ground "and ask for absolution until the service of the prayers is finished," "suspended from prayer" for three spilled lentils, graver faults "atoned for by expulsion" or stripes, a false accusation met by humbly asking "for a plan of repentance" — and, at Tours, a repentance of persons: Brictio "brought back to repentance," soldiers "sufficiently punished by their conscience alone."

---

## World Meaning

In Cassian's house every fault has its measure, and the first measure is the ground. The monk who "breaks an earthenware jar (which they call 'baucalis')," or who does any small thing carelessly, "can only expiate his carelessness by public penance; and when all the brethren are assembled for service he must lie on the ground and ask for absolution until the service of the prayers is finished; and will obtain it when by the Abbot's command he is bidden to rise from the ground." The latecomer stands outside the oratory "and while they are all coming out does penance lying on the ground, and obtains absolution for his carelessness and lateness." The cook who let "three lentil beans" slip from his hand was "suspended from prayer" by the steward's judgment, because "everything that they possess is consecrated to the Lord." And "if one of them has been suspended from prayer for some fault which he has committed, no one has any liberty of praying with him before he performs his penance on the ground" — for he is "delivered unto Satan." The graver faults — open anger, a letter received without the Abbot's leave, "familiarity with women" — are "atoned for by expulsion" or "by stripes." Abbot Paphnutius, falsely accused of theft, "gave himself up entirely to make amends and humbly asked for a plan of repentance," fearing that to deny it "he might further be branded as a liar." The penance is public, bodily, and ended by the elder's word.

At Tours penance is what Martin's patience brings about. Brictio, raving against his master, is "driven from his heart by the prayers of Martin, and he was now brought back to repentance. Speedily, then, he returns, and throws himself at the feet of Martin." Soldiers whose mules are stampeded by Martin's cloak plead that "they had been sufficiently punished by their conscience alone." And Martin himself, after the Ithacian communion, is told by an angel that his compunction is just. No grade of penance is written at Tours; the master's prayer and the sinner's conscience do the work.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Sacramental penance — private confession to a priest, absolution by formula, assigned penances; or "penance" as self-punishment; "satisfaction" as a scholastic category.

**World Hearing:**
A public, bodily discipline of the house — on the ground until the Abbot bids rise, excluded from the brethren's prayer, beaten or expelled for the worst — measured by the elder and restored by his word; and at Tours, a repentance brought about by the master's prayer and the sinner's own conscience.
