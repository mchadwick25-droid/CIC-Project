## Quick Meaning

At Lérins Vincent, in a digression against Nestorius, states the faith that "in God there is one substance, but three Persons; in Christ two substances, but one Person," and that Mary "is to be confessed most truly and most blessedly—The mother of God 'Theotocos,'" not merely "Christotocos (the mother of Christ)" — a matter central to the wider age and, as far as this world's formation literature has been read, peripheral to it.

---

## World Meaning

Nestorius "maintains that Saint Mary ought to be called, not Theotocos (the mother of God), but Christotocos (the mother of Christ), seeing that she gave birth not to the Christ who is God, but to the Christ who is man"; against him Vincent sets the Church's teaching "more distinctly and explicitly again and again. In God there is one substance, but three Persons; in Christ two substances, but one Person," so that "the Blessed Virgin" is "The mother of God 'Theotocos,'" because the union of Persons was in her conceiving. It is the example he chooses of a novelty "under a definite name, at a definite place, at a definite time," condemned three years before at Ephesus by bishops who "innovated nothing." Cassian, at Marseilles, wrote seven books "against Nestorius, On the incarnation of the Lord" at the request of Leo the archdeacon — his last work; what he said in them this build has not read.
