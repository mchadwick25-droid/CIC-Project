## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, contemplation is "the chief good," which "the Lord makes ... consist in meditation; i.e., in divine contemplation" — Mary's part against Martha's "praiseworthy service" — and the second of two knowledges: "practical knowledge can be acquired without theoretical, but theoretical cannot possibly be gained without practical," the purging of faults before the seeing of God.

---

## World Meaning

Abbot Moses reads the story of the two sisters. Martha summoned her sister "to no unworthy work, but to a praiseworthy service," and yet "you see then that the Lord makes the chief good consist in meditation; i.e., in divine contemplation" — "He makes the chief good consist not in practical work however praiseworthy and rich in fruits" but in the one thing. This is what every practice serves: the anchorite's life is "grander than that of the Cœnobium" because of "the contemplation of God, to which those inestimable men ever devoted themselves"; the third renunciation is "that by which we detach our soul from all present and visible things, and contemplate only things to come"; and accidie's victim "begins to forget the object of his profession, which is nothing but meditation and contemplation of that divine purity." Abbot Nesteros gives the order. Knowledge is twofold — "πρακτική, i.e., practical, which is brought about by an improvement of morals and purification from faults: secondly, θεωρητική, which consists in the contemplation of things Divine and the knowledge of most sacred thoughts" — and the order is fixed: "this practical knowledge can be acquired without theoretical, but theoretical cannot possibly be gained without practical." A monk who has not fought his faults cannot see; contemplation is not a technique but what a purged heart does. Of the seeing itself Nesteros says that spiritual knowledge has "three kinds, tropological, allegorical, anagogical," beyond the historical — the senses in which the Scripture the monk carries in his heart opens to him.

At Tours the same two sisters teach something else: a queen who serves Martin at table "fulfilled both parts: she both served like Martha and listened like Mary." The north keeps the figure for hospitality; the south keeps it for the one thing.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Contemplation" as reflection, mindfulness, or a mystical technique available to anyone; "practical vs. theoretical" as doing vs. thinking; "meditation" as a relaxation practice; the fourfold sense as a medieval system.

**World Hearing:**
The chief good and the anchorite's pursuit, reached only after the practical purging of faults and never by technique; "meditation" as divine contemplation, and "theoretical" as the seeing of God; senses of Scripture that open to a purified heart — Egypt's teaching, received; and a different lesson from the same two sisters at Tours.
