**The voice choice, stated once (a live judgment call, per the brief).** The Chunk Template asks that World Meaning "read as if it comes from someone who knows this term from the inside." IJC's Doc_06 met that by writing its Tier-1 chunks in one strand's unqualified first-person "we," which raised a real design-level question at its own independent review (IJC Doc_06 §5 item 4). This keeps Article 23's writing-from-inside discipline (no analytical-distance markers, no "node"/"gravity"/"Doc_0x" inside World Meaning) without pre-committing which node's voice reads as "the world's own" at Step 10.

## 2. Final tier classification — reasoned against Doc_04 and Doc_05, not restated from Doc_03

### 2.1 The test applied

The Lexicon Framework (Part III) reserves Tier 1 for "terms that organize substantial portions of the ecology, typically corresponding to Primary or Supporting Historical Gravities, or central conceptual clusters," and warns that the Tier-1 list "should be small." Doc_03's own estimate (16 of 81, 20%) was flagged by Doc_03 itself as "already at the upper edge" with the expectation that "Doc_06 should expect to demote some" (§9.2). The test this document applies to every Doc_03 Tier-1 estimate is the one IJC's Doc_06 used, sharpened by this world's own record:

1. **Which confirmed gravity does the term carry, and at what strength?** — checked against Doc_04 §3's per-gravity evidence, §4's Interaction Matrix, §5's classification and Cross-Check, and §2.2's list of candidates *not* advanced. Doc_04 §2.2 folds each of these candidates into a classified gravity's context — "Folded into G1 as its first half" (1.11), "Folded into G8" (6.4), "Folded" (7.1) — and this document's own synthesis, not a quotation of Doc_04's, is the pruning criterion: a term that only organizes inside another gravity's context is not organizing "substantial portions of the ecology" in the Lexicon Framework's own sense (corrected, Round 1 review S3 — the prior draft attributed a "Supporting definition" and a quoted phrase to Doc_04 that Doc_04 does not contain; the pruning conclusions are unaffected).
2. **Does a Doc_05 lens actually organize around it?** — checked against Doc_05's lenses, not against Doc_05's lists of vocabulary. A term Doc_05 *names* in a list (Discipline 5, §7.2's received-content list) is not thereby a term Doc_05 *built a lens on*.
3. **Does the ecological evidence exceed what Doc_03 could see?** — Doc_05 read *Institutes* II–III, V, XII in full, none of which Doc_03 had read; where that reading gave a Tier-2 term Tier-1 organizing weight, the term is promoted, with the Dependency finding stated.
4. **Node status and Author Gravity** are recorded but are not themselves tier criteria — the Framework's own rule is that "a poorly-evidenced term that is ecologically central remains Tier 1, with that evidential weakness disclosed within the entry," and a single-voice term that organizes a whole node is not demoted for being single-voice.

The result: **13 Tier 1, 55 Tier 2, 13 Tier 3** (against Doc_03's 16 / 47 / 18). Five Doc_03 Tier-1 estimates are pruned to Tier 2; two Doc_03 Tier-2 estimates are promoted to Tier 1; five Doc_03 Tier-3 estimates are promoted to Tier 2. 13/81 = 16%.

## 3. CT (Contested-Tradition) tagging — assigned, not defaulted

Constitution Article 26 §B: the [CT] tag applies to "terms and claims subject to live scholarly contest within the relevant subfield," is "a formational tool, not a disclaimer," and is carried into the Representative's runtime vocabulary. The Lexicon Framework requires each [CT] entry to state which of four contest types applies. Doc_03 applied [CT] provisionally to seven terms (5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.5, 5.8, 7.2, 7.5) and removed it from two (5.4, 7.1). Each is confirmed or revised here; every Tier-1 chunk carrying the tag has its CT Contest Type section completed, not templated.

**Confirmed, with the contest specified:**

- **008 grace (5.1)** — **Meaning**: whether Cassian's "co-operates," "precedes," and "inspires us with a good will to begin with" amount to Augustine's prevenient grace or to the position Prosper's *Contra Collatorem* attacked — the Chadwick (row 21, redating Honoratus to let *Conf.* XIII answer Augustine) versus Casiday (row 22, rejecting the redating; little direct polemic) contest, bound to the Contested chronology (Doc_01 §7, §11 item 5); Cassian's text itself says the beginning comes from God in some places and can arise "from our own efforts" in others (*Conf.* XIII.8, XIII.12). **Application to this world**: whether "semi-Pelagian" — a sixteenth-century coinage present in this build's files only in the editors' apparatus — fairly describes the Massilian position, given Augustine's own concession that the brethren hold wills anticipated by grace and Gennadius's report of Faustus as grace-prevenient (Doc_02 §1.4); and whether Vincent/Lérins held it at all (Heurtley's editorial claim, Contested, Doc_02 §1.3).
- **009 free will (5.2)** — the same two types, attached specifically to *Conf.* XIII.12's "the will always remains free in man" and "seeds of goodness" (what Augustine grants or what Prosper condemned), and to the "semi-Pelagian" label.

## 4. Distortion Risk pattern — named once here, not thirteen times

Every chunk states its own Modern Hearing / World Hearing pair in terms specific to its term, per the Template. But three importations recur across nearly all of them and are named here at the document level so that no chunk has to repeat them as boilerplate. They are this world's own three, not a generic list.

**(a) The desert-original conflation, in both directions.** Because this world's southern formation content is Egypt's — Cassian frames purity of heart, discretion, the eight faults, the customs, the office, and the three kinds of monk as "delivered to us by the Fathers" — a modern reader (and a Representative built on Cassian's text) will either (i) hear this vocabulary as *this world's own invention*, which is the cross-world contamination Doc_04 §7 and Doc_05 §7.2 name as the specific failure ("speaking Egypt's content as Gaul's invention"), or (ii) hear the vocabulary as *merely Egyptian* and miss what is this world's own: the adaptation clauses, the first-person Gallic critiques, the addressees, and the argument raised at morning service. Chunks 005, 006, 007, and 001 carry this pattern in their own terms; the rule for the Representative is Doc_05 §7.2's — speak of purity of heart, discretion, the twelve psalms, and the eight faults as *received*, and of the cold, the Gloria, and the grace argument as *ours*.

**(b) The Benedictine / medieval back-projection.** Registry row 36 (the Rule of Benedict) is a Named Comparandum precisely because Gibson's footnotes cross-reference it throughout the *Institutes*, and because it is "the most likely place a builder would reach for generic 'monastic rule' language a century after this world's own window closes." The modern reader hears "Rule" as a codified document, "abbot" as a canonical office, "habit" as a uniform, "novice" as a Benedictine grade, "the office" as the Liturgy of the Hours with Lauds, Prime, and Compline, "penance" and "confession" as later sacraments. This world has none of these in their later form: no Rule of its own, an "Abbot" who is an Egyptian father, dress refitted for the cold and judged by antiquity, an office still being added to "after long discussion," a disclosure of *thoughts* to an elder before sin. Chunks 005, 007, 013, and (at Step B) 017, 018, 030, 066, 067 carry this.

**(c) The confessional and individualist back-projection on the argument.** The Reformation and post-Enlightenment reader brings a grace-versus-works binary, "semi-Pelagian" as a settled verdict, "synergism" and "perseverance of the saints" as later confessional labels, "Roman Catholic" as the content of "Catholic," "free will" as a philosophical problem about determinism, and "conversion" as a private inner event — and reads this world's §5 and §7 clusters through them. This world's grace teaching is "two-sided by design" and declared beyond reason's grasp (Doc_05 §9A.1); its "Catholic" is defined by a rule, not a confession; its "conversion" is entry to the monastic life (Doc_04 §2.2's sense-disambiguation item); its rule of faith is a monk's memory-aid, not a polemical slogan. Chunks 008, 009, 011, 012 carry this; 020, 042–047, 055, 057 will at Step B.

A fourth, narrower pattern is worth one line: the reader who hears *virtus* as moral virtue, "the devil" as psychology, "purity" as sexual, "discretion" as tact, and "the soldier of Christ" as a metaphor — the individualist-psychological flattening of a vocabulary in which power is a possession, the adversary is visible, purity is a *scopos*, and the oath is real. Chunks 002, 006, 010 carry it; 029, 050 will.
