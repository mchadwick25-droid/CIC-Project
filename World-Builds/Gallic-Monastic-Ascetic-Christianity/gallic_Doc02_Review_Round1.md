### S1 — SUBSTANTIAL. The "Massilian is Prosper's own coinage" finding inverts its source, and the quotation's ellipsis removes the clause that contradicts it.

**Doc_02 §1.4 claims:**

> this world's own vendored Augustine volume's own editorial footnote, attached to Augustine's own Chapter 2 heading ... states without qualification: **"Prosper in his letter called them *reliquiæ Pelagianorum*... They are now most commonly called 'Semi-Pelagians.'"** This is independent, direct confirmation — from this world's own second vendored volume, not only from the first — that "Massilian" is Prosper's own coinage.

**What the footnote actually says**, in full (`npnf105_augustine-anti-pelagian-writings.xml`, line 20824):

> "[The party which Augustin is here opposing had its chief centre in Marseilles, and hence is called "Massilians." Prosper in his letter called them *reliquiæ Pelagianorum*, i.e., "the remnants of the Pelagians." They are now most commonly called "Semi-Pelagians."—W.]"

The footnote makes three claims and Doc_02 uses them to support a fourth that the footnote contradicts:

1. The party is called "Massilians" **because their chief centre was Marseilles** — an etymological explanation with *no author attributed to the coinage at all*.
2. **Prosper's own label, in his own letter, was *reliquiæ Pelagianorum*** — i.e. per this footnote, "Massilian" is precisely *not* the word Prosper used.
3. The modern label is "Semi-Pelagians."

So the footnote is, if anything, mild evidence **against** the proposition Doc_02 says it confirms. Doc_02's quotation begins at "Prosper in his letter called them..." — the ellipsis silently removes sentence 1, which is the one sentence in the footnote that speaks to where the word "Massilian" comes from, and which says it comes from the city, not from Prosper.

This is aggravated by what it does to the prior document. Doc_01 §7 carries a carefully-built hedge added at Round 2 finding N8: *"the file does not itself state whose word the term is, and this document no longer claims it does."* Doc_02 §1.4 announces that the file **does** state it, and says the finding "directly strengthens Doc_01 §7's terminology argument." It unwinds a correction that a prior review round specifically installed, on the basis of a misread.

Two further inaccuracies in the same sentence:
- The footnote is attached to the *chapter title*, and the chapter title ("To What Extent the Massilians Withdraw from the Pelagians") is the **NPNF editors' own** chapter heading, not "Augustine's own Chapter 2 heading."
- "states without qualification" is not true of a footnote whose first clause supplies a competing derivation.

**Required:** withdraw the finding. Doc_01 §7's independently-grounded position ("on independent historical grounds, Prosper of Aquitaine's own label") may stand on its own research, but Doc_02 must state plainly that this world's second vendored volume does **not** corroborate it and in fact offers a different derivation and a different Prosperian label. If anything is to be carried to Doc_03/Doc_06's lexicon work, it is that this library holds *three* competing labels for the same group — "Massilians" (geographical, unattributed), *reliquiæ Pelagianorum* (Prosper's, per the footnote), and "Semi-Pelagians" (retrospective) — which is a genuinely more interesting lexicon finding than the one claimed.

### S2 — SUBSTANTIAL. The "three-way Hilary tangle" is manufactured from two unrelated passages; the volume's own apparatus makes exactly one identification, and it is a rival to Doc_01's preferred reading rather than a corroboration of it.

**Doc_02 §1.4 claims** the "two laymen" finding is corroboration for Doc_01's lay-monk identification —

> not decisive on its own (a note elsewhere in the same volume calls the identification with "Hilary of Norbonne" only "probable," and separately flags a *third* candidate, "Hilary of Sicily," as "not certain... but seems probable" to be the same man) ...

— and carries forward "**three named candidates now on record** — a Gallic lay monk, 'Hilary of Norbonne,' and a Sicilian correspondent possibly conflated with him — genuinely more tangled than a two-option Contested," into §13 item 1 as a standing open item and into §15's escalation check.

**What the file actually contains.** There are exactly two Hilary-identification passages in `npnf105`, and they are about different men, different letters, and different decades:

- **Line 1258** (Warfield's Introduction, on the year **417**): *"At about this same time (417), the tireless bishop sent a short letter [n. 87: *Epistle* 178] to a Hilary, **who seems to be Hilary of Norbonne**, which is interesting from its undertaking to convey a characterization of Pelagianism to one who was as yet ignorant of it."* This concerns *Epistle 178*, eleven years before Prosper and Hilary write. The volume never connects this man to the 428 correspondence. The wording is "seems to be," not "probable."

- **Lines 1682–1684** (the same Introduction, on **428**): *"...by two laymen with whom he had already had correspondence,—Prosper and Hilary."* — with endnote 140 attached directly to that clause: *"Compare* Epistles *225, 1, and 156. It is, of course, not certain that this is the same Hilary that wrote to Augustin from Sicily, but it seems probable."*

So the volume's apparatus proposes **one** identification of Augustine's 428 correspondent: probably the Hilary who had written to Augustine from Sicily (the addressee of *Ep.* 157, c. 414). The volume's own **Index of Subjects** confirms this and closes the question of what the editor thought: it lists exactly three Hilarys — *"Hilary the commentator, 420"*; *"Hilary of Poitiers, 146"*; and *"Hilary of Sicily, 193, 206, 241, **497, 498**, 525"* (line 22933). Pages 497–498 are precisely *De praedestinatione* chs. 1–2, where "my dearest sons, Prosper and Hilary" appears; 525 is in *De dono perseverantiae*. There is **no index entry for "Hilary of Norbonne" and none for Hilary of Arles** anywhere near those pages.

Doc_02's representation is therefore wrong in three ways:

1. "Hilary of Norbonne" is not a candidate for the Prosper-correspondent at all. He is the volume's guess about the addressee of a different letter, in 417, on a different subject.
2. "Hilary of Sicily" is not a *third* candidate "possibly conflated with him." He is the volume's *own single positive proposal* for the 428 Hilary, and the index states it as settled editorial practice.
3. The finding does not cut the way Doc_02 says it does. A Sicilian correspondent is a **rival** to Doc_01 §10's "Gallic lay monk," not a corroboration of it. Doc_02 offers the passage as "a real, independently-arrived-at corroboration" of the identification Doc_01 favours, when the same page of the same volume proposes a candidate from a different province.

There is also a weaker, related overstatement worth naming while the subsection is open: "two laymen" is thinner support for excluding Hilary of Arles than Doc_02 implies. In 428 Hilary of Arles was still a monk (he succeeded Honoratus at Arles in 429/430 — `npnf211` line 15725), and an unordained monk is a layman. The phrase excludes a *bishop*; it does not exclude the man Doc_01 S6 was worried about, at the date in question.

**Required:** rewrite §1.4's second finding. The honest statement is: this world's second vendored volume identifies Augustine's 428 correspondent Hilary, in both a footnote and its own subject index, as **Hilary of Sicily** — a named third position that competes with both Doc_01's preferred Gallic lay monk and the Hilary of Arles reading Doc_01 S6 overturned. That genuinely does sharpen Doc_01's Contested rating and is worth carrying forward — but as a competing identification, not as corroboration, and without the fictitious Norbonne leg. §13 item 1 and §15's escalation note must be rewritten to match.

### S3 — SUBSTANTIAL. "Cassian's own dedication naming Honoratus as its abbot" of Lérins — the dedication says none of that. This is the load-bearing evidence for Doc_01's "documented southern pair."

**Doc_02 §3 claims:**

> **Lérins** — known through Vincent (as a resident) and, per Doc_01 §2.3's S5 finding, through **Cassian's own dedication naming Honoratus as its abbot.**

**What the dedication actually says** (`npnf211`, Preface to Conference XI, line 36721 ff.):

> "...yet still you, O holy brothers **Honoratus and Eucherius**, ... are so stirred by the great glory of those splendid men from whom we received the first principles of monasticism, that **one of you, presiding as he does over a large monastery of the brethren**, is hoping that his congregation ... may be instructed in the precepts of those fathers, while **the other** has been anxious to make his way to Egypt..."

Cassian's own text: names two men as "holy brothers"; says one of them presides over a large monastery; does not say **which**; does not use the word abbot; and **never mentions Lérins**.

Everything else comes from the nineteenth-century editorial Introduction — that Honoratus is the one presiding, that the monastery is Lérins, that Honoratus was raised to Arles in 426, and that Eucherius became bishop of Lyons in 434 (lines 15449, 15547–15553). Doc_01 §2.3 in fact quotes *the Introduction*, correctly attributed. Doc_02 restates the same evidence one level stronger, transferring the editor's identification into Cassian's own mouth, and the Registry licenses it in that stronger form.

This matters more than a citation slip, because this is the sole positive evidence for the finding Doc_01 §1 and §6 build on — "the southern pair (Lérins–Marseilles) is now documented as linked." The accurate version of that finding is: *Cassian, writing from Marseilles, addresses two named men as brothers, one of whom presides over a large monastic congregation; the identification of that congregation as Lérins and of Honoratus as its head rests on the volume's own editorial apparatus (Registry row 16) and on Gennadius, not on Cassian's own words.* That is still a real link. It is a link at Registry-row-16 confidence, not at primary-text confidence, and Doc_02 was the document charged with noticing the difference (Framework Step 2: "Conduct independent source verification for key sources and scholarly claims").

**Required:** correct §3 and Registry row 8's Licensed-For to state what Cassian's text says and to attribute the Lérins/abbot identification to the editorial apparatus. Note the downstream consequence for Doc_04 rather than burying it.

### S12 — SUBSTANTIAL. Registry row 3's internal boundary nuance violates the Template's one-row-one-status design, and buries an exclusion inside a Native row — the specific failure mode the Registry exists to prevent.

### S13 — SUBSTANTIAL. Rows 22–27 leave Confidence blank. Recording unvendored sources is right; blanking their Confidence letter is not, and it conflates acquisition status with citation reliability.

A note on the shape of these findings, offered as diagnosis rather than criticism. Doc_01's three rounds converged on a discipline — *read the file* — and Doc_02 genuinely applied it: the quotation accuracy in the "What Held Up" section is high, and several passages are quoted more carefully than Doc_01's first draft managed anywhere. What this round finds is the **next** failure mode along, and it is a different one: reading a passage correctly and then reasoning about it too quickly, in the direction the document already wanted to go. S1, S2 and S3 are all the same error — a source that *nearly* says the useful thing, quoted accurately and then characterized as saying it. The discipline that catches that is not "read the file" but "**state what the source says before stating what it supports**," and it is worth naming explicitly in the fix round, because a fourth Doc_02 finding built the same way would not be caught by re-checking quotations.

The second pattern is scope: this build treats "this world's library" as the two files G1 named, when the project holds one library and this world's material is scattered across at least four volumes of it (`npnf203`, `npnf101`, and whatever else a real sweep of `cic/texts/README.md` turns up). That is a one-hour fix with a large return, and it should happen before the Registry is called closed.
