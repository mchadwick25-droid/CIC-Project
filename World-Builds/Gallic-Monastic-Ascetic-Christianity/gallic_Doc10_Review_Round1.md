## HIGH

### H1 — Section 1's single-"we" decision, the build's own "most novel construction decision," is justified by a default and a quoted exception attributed to RCF V3.2 that do not exist in RCF V3.2

Construction Notes, Section 1, Strand attribution (line 25):

> "Renatus holds both documented nodes together as **one register-range**, per the RCF V3.2 Part Four default and the identity decision's binding instruction … **The RCF's stated exception to the single-voice default** — 'communions that refused each other's "we"' — was checked and found **not to apply** …"

and at line 11 ("per the RCF V3.2 Part Four default"), Judgment 1 (line 394, "Decided per the RCF default"), and line 418 ("would require reconsidering the multiple-named-voices construction (RCF V3.2; Construction Notes Template §1)").

Grep counts over the extracted RCF V3.2 text: `strand` = 0, `register-range` = 0, `multiple-named-voices` = 0, `refused each other` = 0, `communion` = 0, `single voice` = 0, `one voice` = 0, `Voice Grounding` = 0, `V7.2` = 0. RCF V3.2 Part Four (Identity Determination) contains no strand-attribution framework at all. Its only "register" language is a different concept — "What guards against genericism is no longer a single chosen biography but **unity of register**: the voice must speak, always and only, in this world's own distinctive manner."

Every element the Notes attribute to the RCF is in fact the L4 **Construction Notes Template** v2.2, Section 1, whose own text reads:

> "the default is that this Representative holds all documented strands together as one register-range … Anchoring this Representative to a single strand … should only be done where the world's internal complexity genuinely cannot be held within one register-range — e.g., **communions that refused each other's 'we.'** … note that this world may require the multiple-named-voices construction (**Construction Framework V7.2**, Representative Voice Grounding)"

So: the "default" is the template's, the quoted "exception" is the template's (quoted in the Notes inside quotation marks as the RCF's own words), and the multiple-named-voices allowance belongs to a third document (CF V7.2), not to RCF V3.2. This is the same defect Doc_09 Review Round 1 rated H1 in this build — a sibling document's language quoted as the governing framework's own — reappearing at the single decision Section 7 and Section 8 both name as this construction's most novel and most dependent on judgment. The *substance* of a single-voice default does survive in RCF Part Four ("the world's own voice … not one bounded person's biography"), so the decision is not thereby wrong; but the stated authority for it, and the stated test that was "checked and found not to apply," are both misattributed, and a reviewer sent to RCF Part Four to check them finds nothing there.

### H2 — Purity of heart is deployed at all three prompt loci with no Egyptian-reception marking, against the chunk's own voice note and Doc_06 §4(a); and Section 2 claims the prompt marks it, quoting a prompt phrase that does not

Doc_06 §4(a) names this world's first recurring distortion risk and states the rule verbatim: "speak of **purity of heart**, discretion, the twelve psalms, and the eight faults as *received*, and of the cold, the Gloria, and the grace argument as *ours*." Lexicon chunk 006's Voice note: "a southern (Marseilles) term, Cassian's only, and **framed by Cassian as Abbot Moses's teaching received from Egypt**," closing: "the Gallic monk who speaks of it speaks of 'what the fathers handed down and Cassian brought to us,' not of what Gaul devised."

The deployed prompt names purity of heart three times, and marks the *audience* or the *node* every time but never the *origin*:

- Line 27: "Among those who read Cassian, it is purity of heart, which is charity: the definite mark the archer fixes his gaze on …"
- Line 35: "What he aims at, in the south, is purity of heart."
- Line 47: "the aim the practice serves: purity of heart, the example that draws others, the faith kept whole."

"Among those who read Cassian" and "in the south" identify who holds the term, not whose it is — which is precisely the (i) half of Doc_06 §4(a)'s risk ("hear this vocabulary as *this world's own invention*"). The prompt applies the origin-marking discipline correctly and repeatedly elsewhere ("The customs of the monasteries are the fathers', not ours"; "The fathers of Egypt taught us that vainglory creates a wish for holy orders"; "What Cassian carried to us from the fathers of Egypt and the East … always as theirs"; "When we tell a story of the fathers of Egypt, we say whose story it is") — so this is the build's established third pattern, a discipline applied in most places and not propagated to the one term Doc_06 §4(a) lists first.

Compounding it, Construction Notes line 67 asserts the opposite and cites the prompt as its own proof:

> "**006 purity of heart** (single-voice, Cassian's, **framed as Abbot Moses's received teaching** — the prompt says 'Among those who read Cassian, it is purity of heart, which is charity')"

The quoted prompt phrase does not frame it as Abbot Moses's received teaching. The claim is falsified by the very text it quotes.
