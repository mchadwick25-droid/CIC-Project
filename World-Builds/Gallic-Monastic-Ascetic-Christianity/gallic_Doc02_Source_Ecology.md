## 1. Primary Voices

### 1.1 Sulpitius Severus

**Corrected, throughout (Round 2 findings N1, N3):** the prior revision described `npnf211`'s introduction and Gennadius's chapter as "two independent ancient authorities" disagreeing on Sulpitius's dates, and later described the Pelagianism-charge tension as "two ancient sources in actual disagreement." **Neither framing is accurate.** `npnf211`'s introduction is **Alexander Roberts's** own nineteenth-century composition (named on the volume's own title page: "Translated, with preface, and notes, by Rev. Alexander Roberts, D.D., Professor of Humanity, University of St. Andrews"), not an ancient source. And the specific dates the prior revision attributed to "Gennadius's own chapter" — "born after 353, died about 410" — are **Ernest Cushing Richardson's** own 1892 bracketed editorial endnote, not Gennadius's ancient text. Gennadius's own chapter (`npnf203`, ch. XIX, read directly again this revision) dates nothing; it closes with no year at all, only a narrative account of Sulpitius's life and works.

**Restated at the strength actually supported:** this world's library holds **one ancient biographical source** on Sulpitius (Gennadius, c. 495) and **two modern editorial treatments** of him (Roberts's `npnf211` introduction; Richardson's `npnf203` endnotes) — not two ancient authorities. The two modern editors' own dating endnotes disagree (`npnf211`'s "about 363...about 420" against Richardson's "after 353...about 410") — a real discrepancy, still worth disclosing, but a disagreement **between two nineteenth-century scholars**, not an ancient dispute. This document carries it as such.

**Independent ancient corroboration for the core biographical facts, correctly attributed:** Jerome (*Comm. Ezech.* 36); Augustine (*Ep.* 205); and Gennadius's own text, directly: "a man distinguished by his birth, by his excellent literary work, by his devotion to poverty and by his humility, beloved also of the sainted men Martin bishop of Tours and Paulinus Nolanus," and — a fact not previously in hand and genuinely Gennadius's own — "He wrote to his sister **many** Letters exhorting to love of God and contempt of the world." **[Widely Accepted, unchanged from the prior revision's correct downgrade from Documented, S5.]**

**The Pelagianism charge, re-argued at the correct evidentiary shape (Round 2 finding N3):** Gennadius's own text, read again directly, states the charge flatly and in his own voice, not as a report of rumor: *"In his old age, he was led astray by the Pelagians, and recognizing the guilt of much speaking, kept silent until his death, in order that by penitent silence he might atone for the sin which he had contracted by speaking."* This is one ancient author's own direct claim. The party who reports and rejects it in `npnf211` is **Roberts**, the Victorian translator, not a second ancient source — his own words are "it seems to us that there is no ground for any such conclusion," and his own phrase "some ancient writers" most plausibly refers to Gennadius himself, the very source Roberts is implicitly arguing against. **The correct shape of this tension is: one ancient source asserting the charge, and one modern translator denying it** — this still meets Article 17's *Contested* bar (a live disagreement about how to read the ancient record, not resolved), but the disagreement is not, as the prior revision stated, ancient-versus-ancient. Flagged for Doc_04 at this corrected strength.

**Author Gravity Assessment (five dimensions), corrected:**

- **Visibility, Representativeness, Influence, Limitations:** substantively unchanged from the prior revision; the Gennadius material corroborates rather than alters these findings.
- **Transmission History**, corrected (Round 2 finding N9, N10): the prior revision stated `npnf211`'s introduction was "anonymous as to its specific author." **It is not** — the volume's own title page names Roberts directly, with his institutional position (Professor of Humanity, University of St. Andrews), exactly the kind of detail this dimension exists to capture. ### 1.2 John Cassian

**Corrected (Round 2 finding N1):** the prior revision's claim that "Gennadius also gives a specific death date, 'died 450'" is Richardson's own bracketed endnote, not Gennadius's text — Gennadius's own chapter closes, as all his chapters do, with a bare regnal formula ("in the reign of Theodosius and Valentinianus"). **What Gennadius's own text does independently corroborate, read again directly:** the ordination as deacon "by bishop John the Great, at Constantinople," the presbyterate "at Marseilles," and — genuinely independent ancient confirmation — "founded two monasteries, that is to say one for men and one for women, **which are still standing**" (Gennadius wrote c. 495, within a lifetime of Cassian's own death, and confirms both houses' survival to his own day). Gennadius also independently confirms *De Incarnatione* was written "at the request of **Leo the archdeacon**, afterwards bishop of Rome" — corroborating Doc_01 §10's Leyser-sourced Leo detail from a source outside Leyser entirely — and lists all twenty-four Conferences by subject, in his own order (count verified: twenty-four, matching this world's own vendored structure).

**A substantial addition this revision, from a source this build had already read but not mined for this purpose (Round 2 finding N7, N10):** `npnf211`'s own Cassian prolegomena — read again directly — carries an extended, genuinely rich transmission-history discussion the prior two drafts did not use:

- **Prosper's own polemical response to Cassian is named, described, and located inside a source this world already has in hand.** The prolegomena state directly: *"the former of them [Prosper] finally determined to put down, if possible, the upholders of the objectionable tenets by a direct criticism of Cassian's Conferences. This was the origin of Prosper's work 'Contra Collatorem,' against the author of the Conferences... The treatise is given in Gazet's edition of Cassian."* The prolegomena further describe its actual content: an examination of Conference XIII specifically, extracting twelve propositions, of which Prosper judges the first orthodox and the rest erroneous — and note, tellingly, that "his antagonist never once names him directly, but merely speaks of him as a man of priestly rank." **This is not a Gennadius-sourced discovery, as the prior revision stated — it was available in this world's own already-read primary volume from the first drafting pass.** Registry row 32 (see companion file) is corrected accordingly.
- **A genuine doctrinally-motivated intermediary, correctly the textbook case Round 1's S9 asked for:** the same prolegomena describe **Dionysius Carthusianus** (a fifteenth-century Carthusian, "the doctor exstaticus"), who produced a free paraphrase of the *Institutes* and *Conferences* — and, specifically for Conference XIII, "in his endeavour to make Cassian orthodox, **omits all that savours of Semi-Pelagianism**; and from c. viii. onward there are large omissions and various suggestive alterations in the text." **This document states precisely what the source supports and no more:** Dionysius's paraphrase survives as a separate work, printed alongside the standard text in Gazet's 1616 edition (and thence Migne's); this document has not established, and does not claim, that Gibson's own NPNF translation follows Dionysius's redacted text rather than the standard one — the prolegomena discuss Dionysius as a documented historical instance of doctrinally-motivated editing of this exact Conference, not as the version this world's own English text is built from.
- **Also named, not previously in hand:** **Petschenig's** critical edition (the CSEL text) is cited by the prolegomena as a scholarly reference point, and a 431 letter obtained by Prosper and Hilary from Pope Celestine — addressed to the Gallican bishops including "**Venerius of Marseilles**" (Cassian's own bishop, named first in the salutation) — is described in detail, closing "with some words of eulogium on Augustine 'of holy memory'" despite the Massilians' continued resistance. This is a specific, textually-grounded instance of exactly the "forces" dynamic Doc_02 §11 discusses in general terms: papal authority invoked, named, and defied by name, inside a source this world already holds.

**Author Gravity Assessment, corrected:**

- **Transmission History**, corrected and substantially extended (Round 2 finding N10): translated by **Edgar C. S. Gibson** (title page: "Principal of the Theological College, Wells, Somerset"). This world's own vendored text is missing Conferences XII and XXII (Registry row 11, unchanged). The prolegomena's own transmission discussion (above) supplies the fuller picture Round 1's S9 asked for: Gazet's 1616 edition as the historical standard, Petschenig's later critical edition, and Dionysius Carthusianus's own doctrinally-motivated fifteenth-century paraphrase of Conference XIII specifically — three distinct editorial layers between Cassian's own hand and this world's own English text, now named rather than gestured at with a single footnote.

## 3. Institutional Evidence

**Corrected (Round 2 finding N4).** The prior revision's claim that the Lérins–Marseilles link is "now doubly evidenced" — by Gennadius's Faustus chapter "directly" and by the editorial apparatus — does not hold. Gennadius ch. LXXXVI concerns **Faustus**, whose own Lérins abbacy (433/4, per Richardson's endnote — a further instance of the same misattribution, now corrected: Gennadius's own text says only "first abbot of the monastery at Lerins, **and then made bishop** of Riez" — the "first... then" sequence, previously truncated before the second clause, restated here in full) postdates Cassian's own dedication by roughly a decade. **It says nothing about Honoratus, nothing about Cassian, and nothing about any Marseilles–Lérins relationship.** What it independently confirms is only that Lérins existed and had abbots — never in dispute — not the specific link the prior revision claimed it doubled.

**The link between Cassian's Marseilles and the institution at Lérins therefore still rests exactly where Round 1's S3 correction properly put it: on this world's own editorial apparatus (Roberts's and Gibson's identification of Cassian's unnamed dedicatee as Honoratus, abbot of Lérins), not on Cassian's own words, which name two "holy brothers" and one unnamed presiding monastery.**

**What does independently corroborate the Lérins node's own existence, correctly identified this revision (Round 2 finding N4):** Gennadius's own opening line on **Vincentius** — "presbyter in the Monastery on the Island of **Lerins**" — is genuine, near-contemporary, ancient testimony that Lérins existed and housed one of this world's own three primary voices. **Saint-Victor and Saint-Sauveur, Marseilles:** unchanged from the prior revision — Gennadius's own direct text ("two monasteries... which are still standing") remains genuine independent corroboration, correctly attributed.

**A further, small ancient transmission datum, previously read but not remarked (Round 2 finding N17):** Gennadius describes the *Dialogues* as "a Conference between Postumianus and Gallus... in two divisions" — against `npnf211`'s own three-Dialogue structure.
