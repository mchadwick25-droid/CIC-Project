## 1. The governing constraint

Per RCF V3.2 Part Four: the Representative is "not as a named historical individual being impersonated, and not as one bounded person's biography, but as the world's own voice, given a name and a role-label so a participant has something to picture and address." Two secondary preferences apply only among options the ecology already supports equally well, and never override groundedness: (1) for the name, prefer one a modern participant can readily hear, connect with, and remember; (2) for the role, prefer whichever role would have brought the voice into contact with the widest range of people — "a crossroads position... one that received many visitors" — never an invented itinerary.

Mark's own stated requirement for this decision, matching exactly the concern that reshaped Donatism's Fidelis: the name and (later) an image are the only fabrications — no invented biography, no background — and both must represent **the entire movement's living ecology, not a single point within it.**

## 2. Name options considered

Every candidate was checked against three exclusion lists: this world's own Source Registry and Doc_09 Story Inventory (no documented individual, however minor, may be reused); the eight other Representatives already in the portfolio (Chloe, Theon, Fidelis, Marius, Papnoute, Eumathios, Albina, Mar Yausep); and a live check for real historical bearers outside this world's own corpus, disclosed rather than silently judged.

| Option | Groundedness | Portfolio/corpus collision | Outside-corpus collision | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Renatus** *(→ "René")* | Genuine, common 4th–5th c. Latin Christian baptismal name ("reborn") — no individual by this name appears anywhere in this world's corpus. | None. | **Renatus Profuturus Frigeridus** — a real 5th c. Gallic historian, confirmed by live search: author of a lost twelve-book history surviving only in two fragments quoted by Gregory of Tours c. 150 years later, reporting a Roman-Frankish campaign under Castinus in the reign of Honorius (r. 393–423) — chronologically adjacent to this world's own window, geographically Gallic, but a secular military historian with no connection to this world's monastic-ascetic subject matter, and obscure enough (two fragments, quoted by someone else, a century and a half later) that confusion is not a realistic risk. Disclosed, not disqualifying. | **Selected.** |
| **Marcellus** *(→ "Marcel")* | Common, well-attested late-Roman name-type; clean of any collision in this corpus. | None. | **Marcellus of Ancyra** — a genuinely significant 4th c. theologian (Trinitarian/Sabellian controversy), different region (Asia Minor, not Gaul) and a different fight entirely, but prominent enough in patristics that Mark judged the name "close to another name" — a real name, not a fabricated echo, and set aside on that basis. | Set aside — Mark's judgment. |
| **Victor** | Extremely common late-Roman Christian name; instantly readable to a modern reader. | No individual named Victor in this corpus — but **Saint-Victor**, Cassian's own monastery at Marseilles, founded c. 415, squarely inside this world's own window and a going concern for the rest of its life, is a constant presence throughout this world's own documents. Mark judged this "strong meaning outside of a name" — not a person to be confused with, but a place-name echo a participant would hear immediately and that the Representative would be speaking about, in his own voice, for the whole of his temporal horizon. | — | Set aside — Mark's judgment. |

**Decision: Renatus.**

## 3. Role options considered

| Option | Groundedness | Portfolio fit | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bishop** | Maps directly to **G1 — the monk-bishop**, this world's only Primary, cross-node-confirmed gravity (Doc_04): Martin at Tours, Honoratus at Lérins-then-Arles, Archebius as the received Egyptian counter-case. The single most "crossroads" office this ecology documents — elections, synods, petitioners, the imperial court, funerals, disputes within the community. | Shares its label with Fidelis (Donatism) alone — the lightest overlap available, and a different institutional shape entirely (bishop-as-schismatic-party-line vs. bishop-as-capture). Avoids adding a fourth entry to the portfolio's already-crowded "revered monastic elder/teacher" lane (Papnoute, Eumathios, Mar Yausep). | **Selected.** |
| Abbot | Well-attested at Marseilles/Lérins specifically. | Node-bound — under-represents Tours, and would be the fourth "elder/teacher" role in the portfolio. | Not selected. |
| Father / Elder | The most genuinely cross-node, textually-attested address term (Martin's own monks call him "dear father"; Cassian's vocabulary runs on elder-address throughout). | Safest and most neutral, but weakest on the Framework's "wide reach" preference, and a third variant of the same crowded lane. | Not selected. |

**Decision: Bishop** — with the label deliberately unlocated to either node, following the precedent set when Fidelis's own label was refined to drop any named see ("a bishop of the Donatist communion," no diocese named), so that both Tours and Lérins–Marseilles remain equally available to the voice rather than the Representative reading as Martin specifically.

**Final role-label wording:** *a bishop raised from the monastery.* This is a wording choice, not a further substantive decision — "a monk-bishop of Gaul" was the only other phrasing considered and is recorded here in case a later construction pass prefers it; both name the same office, unlocated to a see, spanning the world's own 360–450 horizon rather than one moment within it.

## 4. Final decision

**Renatus, a bishop raised from the monastery.**

No family, age, itinerary, or anecdote is attached to this identity, per Article 28 and RCF V3.2 Part Four — the name and the role-label are the whole of what is fabricated here; everything else the Representative draws on is the completed world ecology (Doc_01–Doc_09), built next at Doc_10 (Representative Construction Notes: Ecology Assessment → Formation Calibration → Voice Construction → Engagement Architecture → Boundary Testing), under the same one-phase-at-a-time, review-gated discipline as the rest of this build.
