## 0. What this document is, and the disciplines it applies

**What it is.** The Framework's Step 4 output: a candidate list generated from traceable evidence, every candidate run through the six named tests, classified Primary / Supporting / Tensional against the Framework's own definitions, cross-checked against evidential confidence, tested across the Tours and Lérins–Marseilles nodes, and given a forces-connection notation. The Gravity Index (§8) and the Interaction Matrix (§4) are built as part of the discovery work, not appended to it.

**Discipline 1 — a gravity organizes; a topic recurs (CF Part III).** Candidates were generated only from elements recurring across *multiple* Doc_02 evidence streams (§1 below names the streams), or from Attention Presence/Absence patterns Doc_02 and Doc_03 documented. Doc_03's 81 lexicon entries were used as a map of where attention sits, not as a gravity list: a Tier-1 (est.) term is not a gravity until it passes the tests, and several did not (§2.2).

**Discipline 2 — read, don't infer (Doc_01 §10, Doc_02 preamble, Doc_03 §0).** Every quotation below was located and read in the vendored file during this pass, with the NPNF chapter and, where useful, the XML div id (e.g. `ii.ii.x` = *Vita* ch. IX). Where Doc_03's Evidence line and the file disagreed on wording or locus, the file wins and the correction is noted (§9). Two Doc_03-cited eyewitness passages turned out to sit in **Dialogue I** (Registry row 4, Excluded) — they are named in §2.3 and §9 and **not used as evidence**.

**Discipline 3 — name the layer (Doc_02 preamble).** Roberts's, Heurtley's, Gibson's, Warfield's, Richardson's, and Sanford's apparatus are named as editorial wherever they are drawn on, and never counted as a voice. Gennadius's own text (row 30) is the only ancient corroborating witness and is quoted only for what it textually says.

**Discipline 4 — Native rows only; Augustine as context; Salvian outside the grace question.** Rows 14–16 supply only what Augustine reports the Massilian brethren as holding; nothing in G3 is Augustine's doctrine as this world's own. Row 43 (Salvian) is used for forces and for candidates other than grace, per its own Licensed-For limit; nothing from Salvian appears in G3.

**Discipline 5 — flag Author Gravity at generation.** §2.1's table sets the flag when the candidate is proposed. Two candidates (G6, G7) carry a single-voice flag from the outset; one (G10) carries a "weight sits in a fourth, later-added voice" flag.

**Discipline 6 — provisionally strand-singular (Doc_01 §6).** No candidate is split by strand. Every candidate is tested across the **Tours node** (Sulpitius/Martin) and the **Lérins–Marseilles node** (Cassian, Vincent, Salvian, and — bounded Latin — Hilary of Arles and Eucherius), and the result is recorded as *cross-node confirmed*, *node-bound*, or *split finding*. "Node" is a descriptive label for where the attestation sits, not a strand claim.

**Discipline 7 — forces-connection is a separate, required field.** Per Forces Framework §4 Step 4, every classified gravity carries a notation of how it held, shifted, intensified, or fractured under the forces already identified at Doc_01 §7 and Doc_02 §11/§13A. Doc_08 does not yet exist and is not cited.

### G1 — The monk-bishop: renunciation that does not leave the Church

**Evidence.**
- *Tours.* *Vita* ch. II (`ii.ii.iii`): Martin in the army "regarded not so much as being a soldier as a monk." *Vita* ch. IX (`ii.ii.x`): at the election "some of the bishops, who had been summoned to appoint a chief priest, were impiously offering resistance, asserting forsooth that Martin's person was contemptible, that he was unworthy of the episcopate, that he was a man despicable in countenance, that his clothing was mean, and his hair disgusting." *Vita* ch. X (`ii.ii.xi`): as bishop he "kept up the position of a bishop properly, yet in such a way as not to lay aside the objects and virtues of a monk"; of Marmoutier's eighty disciples, "we have seen numbers of these afterwards made bishops. For what city or church would there be that would not desire to have its priests from among those in the monastery of Martin?" Gennadius ch. XIX (row 30, ancient text): "a Life of the holy Martin, monk and bishop."
- *Marseilles.* Cassian's whole literary output is addressed to bishops or to men who became bishops: *Inst.* Preface (`iv.ii`) to "most blessed Pope Castor," bishop of Apta Julia, for "your new monastery"; *Conf.* Pref. I (`iv.iv.i`) to "blessed Pope Leontius" and Helladius (Gibson's fn., editorial: "afterwards raised to the Episcopate"); *Conf.* Pref. III (`iv.vi.i`) names "Honoratus, a Bishop blessed in name as well as merits," the same man Pref. II (`iv.v.i`) had called "holy brother ... presiding as he does over a large monastery of the brethren" — the monk-to-bishop transition happening between two dedications, in Cassian's own text. And the Egyptian counter-case Cassian chooses to carry west: *Conf.* XI.2 (`iv.v.ii.ii`), Archebius, "carried off from the assembly of anchorites and given as Bishop to the town of Panephysis," who "complained that he had been expelled from the monastic system as unworthy of it."
- *Lérins.* Vincent, *Comm.* ch. 1 [2]: "avoiding the concourse and crowds of cities, I am dwelling in the seclusion of a Monastery." Heurtley's Introduction (`iii.i`, **editorial**): "Honoratus and Hilary, afterwards successively bishops of Arles, and Faustus, afterwards bishop of Riez." Gennadius ch. LXXXVI (ancient text): Faustus "first abbot of the monastery at Lerins, and then made bishop of Riez"; ch. LXX: Hilary of Arles "devoted to poverty," author of "his Life of Saint Honoratus, his predecessor." **Insider Latin, new this pass (row 27, my rendering of rough OCR — wording Inferential/Thin, presence checkable):** Hilary's sermon on Honoratus, at the passage beginning "Quid longius morer" (file lines c. 700–722), says of Honoratus at Lérins: *"Hic primum illigatur diu evitati clericatus officio; hic refugam suum sacerdotalis infula innectit; et qui ire ad dignitatem detrectaverat, ad ipsum dignitas venit"* — "Here he was first bound to the long-avoided office of the clergy; here the priestly fillet fastens on its fugitive; and he who had refused to go to the dignity — the dignity came to him"; and *"tam integram in sacerdotio monachi humilitatem conservabat, quam plene monachus sacerdotii merita possederat"* — "he kept a monk's humility as entire in the priesthood as, when a monk, he had fully possessed the merits of the priesthood." This is the southern node's own founding narrative stating the conjunction in its own words, independent of Cassian and of the editorial apparatus.

**Tests.** *Repetition* — passes: S, C, V, L, Gn, I, F, E. *Dependency* — passes: the addressee structure of the entire Cassianic corpus depends on it; Doc_01's temporal ceiling argument (§2.4, the register shift to synodal commission) depends on it; Doc_03's 8.1 and Doc_01 §1's distinct-world criteria depend on it. *Formation* — passes: Marmoutier forms men "after the example of the saintly master" and they become bishops; Cassian writes formation literature for a bishop's "new monastery"; Lérins is, in the case document's phrase Doc_01 §1 quotes, "a seminary that looked like a monastery." *Explanatory* — passes: it explains why this world's withdrawal is geographically modest (Doc_01 §7; Hilary's *"in eremum huic urbi propinquam"* — a desert near the city), why the grace argument reaches Rome, and why the world's ending is a change of institutional register rather than a collapse. *Persistence* — passes across both nodes, all voices, and every evidence stream. *Interaction* — passes: reinforcing with G2, G4, G5, G8; competing with G9 and G7; reshaping with G3 and G6 (§4).

**Cross-node status:** confirmed cross-node — the strongest cross-node convergence in this document, and the only one attested by an insider Latin source in the south independent of Cassian.

**Evidential confidence:** **Documented** — multiple independent ancient witnesses (Sulpitius; Cassian's own dedications; Hilary of Arles; Gennadius) converge; the editorial apparatus only corroborates.

**Classification: PRIMARY.** Passes all six with strong confidence; multiple dimensions depend on it; it shapes formation pervasively.

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** organizing strength and evidential confidence agree. No divergence.

**Forces-connection notation.** *Held and intensified.* Doc_01 §7's "what generated this world" — formation pursued "without severing a monk from institutional authority or even from eventual episcopal office" — is this gravity's origin. Under the ongoing force Doc_02 §11 documents (Prosper's appeal; Celestine's letter naming Cassian's own bishop Venerius first and warning that "priests ought not to teach so as to invade the episcopal prerogative," per Gibson's prolegomena `iv.i`, editorial), the gravity did not fracture: it intensified into the Lérins episcopal network Doc_01 §2.4 (Mathisen) describes, and by the 470s the argument itself is carried by a Lérins-formed bishop at synodal commission (Faustus). Under the barbarian pressure Salvian documents (Doc_02 §13A), the monk-bishop is the institution left standing — Gennadius's "two monasteries ... which are still standing" (ch. LXII) is written c. 495 from the same city. The forces did not shift this gravity; they made it the world's exit mechanism.

### G2 — Egypt as the measure

**Evidence.**
- *Marseilles (receptive mode).* *Inst.* Preface (`iv.ii`): Castor is "anxious that the institutions of the East and especially of Egypt should be established in your province, which is at present without monasteries"; Cassian will "declare ... the customs of the monasteries which we have seen observed throughout Egypt and Palestine, as they were there delivered to us by the Fathers"; he will correct anything founded "according to the fancy of any one who has founded a monastery" against "the rule which I have seen followed in the monasteries anciently founded throughout Egypt and Palestine, as I do not believe that a new establishment in the West, in the parts of Gaul could find anything more reasonable or more perfect"; and he will adapt "where I find anything in the rule of the Egyptians which, either because of the severity of the climate, or owing to some difficulty or diversity of habits, is impossible in these countries." *Inst.* I.10 (dress "would afford a subject for derision instead of edifying the spectators"); *Inst.* IV.2 (`iv.iii.iv.ii`): of the Egyptian probation, "we cannot recollect any one who joined our monasteries keeping it up unbroken even for a year"; *Inst.* IV.10–11 ("in this country neither the climate nor the weakness of our constitution would permit"). *Conf.* Pref. II (`iv.v.i`): Eucherius "anxious to make his way to Egypt to be edified by the sight of these in the flesh, that he might leave this province that is frozen as it were with the cold of Gaul." *Conf.* Pref. III (`iv.vi.i`): Gallic monks are to receive "into their cells the authors of the Conferences ... talking with them after a fashion by daily questions and answers," so as not to be "left to their own resources to find that way which is difficult and almost unknown in this country." Gennadius ch. LXII: "He also compiled Conferences with the Egyptian fathers."
- *Lérins (receptive mode, insider Latin, new this pass — row 26, my rendering, wording Inferential/Thin).* Eucherius, *De Laude Eremi* §27: *"Quid nunc ergo Joannem Macariumque commemorem, aliosque complures, quorum conversatio dum in desertis est, in coelis facta est?"* — "Why now should I recall John and Macarius, and many others, whose way of life, while it was in the deserts, was made in heaven?" — the Egyptian fathers named as the standard, in a letter addressed to Hilary of Lérins; and (§42 region) *"Lirinum meam ... quae procellosi naufragiis mundi effusos piissimis ulnis receptat venientes"* — Lérins receiving "those cast out by the shipwrecks of the stormy world," the same harbour image Vincent uses of himself (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]).
- *Marseilles (Salvian).* *Gov.* VIII.4 (Sanford p. 229): "any servant of God from the monasteries of Egypt or the sacred places of Jerusalem or the holy and venerable retreats of the desert" — Egypt as the byword for holiness, in a non-monastic voice.
- *Tours (comparative mode).* Gennadius ch. XIX on the *Dialogues*: "The subject matter was the manner of life of the oriental monks and of St. Martin" — an ancient witness that the comparison *is* the work's structure. *Dial.* II.5 (`ii.iv.ii.v`), Gallus to Postumianus: "you have conquered all the eremites and anchorites." *Dial.* III.17 (`ii.iv.iii.xvii`): "Christ has by no means forsaken Gaul, since he has granted it to possess such a man as Martin. But when you have come as far as Egypt, although it is justly proud of the numbers and virtues of its own saints, yet let it not disdain to hear how Europe will not yield to it, or to all Asia, in having only Martin." *Vita* ch. X: Marmoutier's monks "clothed in garments of camels' hair," the Egyptian dress Cassian says Gaul cannot wear. *Dial.* III.14: an Egyptian merchant "who was not yet a Christian" cries "Save us, O God of Martin." (Dialogue I.26, the fullest Tours-side statement, is Excluded and not used — §2.3.)

**Tests.** *Repetition* — passes: S, C, L, Sv, Gn, M. *Dependency* — passes: the *Institutes*' entire structure (dress, office, faults, "according to their traditions"), the *Conferences*' frame, the *Dialogues*' structure (Gennadius), and Doc_01 §8.2's world-continuity finding all depend on it. *Formation* — passes in the south (formation *is* the reception); in the north, Marmoutier's dress and common property are Egyptian in form, and the *Dialogues* form their readers by the comparison. *Explanatory* — passes: it explains the world's self-deprecating Gallic identity (Doc_03 8.8), its climate-adaptation vocabulary, Eucherius's wish to travel, and why Cassian's own text is simultaneously desert-monasticism's Latin transmission and this world's founding literature. *Persistence* — passes across both nodes and across S, C, L, Sv, Gn — **but in two different modes**: the south *receives and adapts* Egypt as authority; the north *measures Martin against* Egypt and finds Egypt matched or exceeded. *Interaction* — passes: reinforcing with G4, G5, G7, G8; reshaping with G3, G9; competing with G6 (§4).

**Cross-node status:** **split finding — one reference point, two modes.** Both nodes organize around Egypt as the standard of monastic authenticity (that is the gravity); they differ in whether the relationship is receptive (south) or comparative-competitive (north). This document records the split rather than calling it either convergence or divergence, and hands it to §6.

**Evidential confidence:** **Documented** for the southern receptive mode (Cassian's own preface; Eucherius independently; Gennadius); **Widely Accepted** for the northern comparative mode (Sulpitius's own text, structurally confirmed by Gennadius's description, but one voice and its ancient summarizer).

**Classification: PRIMARY**, with the split-mode finding stated as a permanent qualifier. It organizes broadly in both nodes; the mode difference is itself one of the more ecologically informative findings of this pass and is not smoothed.

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** no divergence — the gravity's strength is highest where its confidence is highest (the south). The northern mode's Widely Accepted rating does not bear on the Primary classification, which rests on both nodes together.

**Forces-connection notation.** *Held, then shifted in mode.* Doc_01 §7 names Egyptian teaching "carried west by Cassian" as what the world drew on, and the felt inadequacy of ordinary institutional life as what generated it. The initiating force acted differently at the two nodes: Tours (c. 360–397) had no Cassian and answered the Egyptian standard by *rivalry* (Martin surpasses); Marseilles–Lérins (c. 410–435) answered it by *reception with adaptation*. Under the ongoing force of the grace controversy (Doc_02 §11), the receptive mode hardened: Conf. XIII.18 grounds the Massilian position in "all the Catholic fathers who have taught perfection of heart ... in deed and act" — Egypt becomes the authority pleaded against Augustine and Rome. Under barbarian pressure (Doc_02 §13A), Salvian still measures holiness by "the monasteries of Egypt" (VIII.4). The gravity held; what shifted was what Egypt was needed *for* — a standard to match (north), then a rule to receive (south), then an authority to plead (south, under pressure).

### G3 — The grace-and-effort argument

**Evidence.** (Southern node only — that is the finding, not an omission.)
- *Cassian's own text.* *Conf.* III.19 (`iv.iv.iv.xix`): "the beginning of our good will is given to us by the inspiration of the Lord ... but that it is in our own power to follow up the encouragement." *Conf.* XIII.7 (`iv.v.iv.vii`): "how can we imagine without grievous blasphemy that He does not generally will all men, but only some instead of all to be saved? Those then who perish, perish against His will." XIII.11 (`iv.v.iv.xi`): "does God have compassion upon us because we have shown the beginning of a good will, or does the beginning of a good will follow because God has had compassion upon us? For many believing each of these and asserting them more widely than is right are entangled in all kinds of opposite errors." XIII.13 (`iv.v.iv.xiii`): "the grace of God always co-operates with our will for its advantage." XIII.18 (`iv.v.iv.xviii`): "laid down by all the Catholic fathers who have taught perfection of heart not by empty disputes of words, but in deed and act, that the first stage in the Divine gift is for each man to be inflamed with the desire of everything that is good, but in such a way that the choice of free will is open to either side ... not to take away the freedom of the will which He Himself has once given ... how God works all things in us and yet everything can be ascribed to free will, cannot be fully grasped by the mind and reason of man."

- *Ancient corroboration (row 30).* Gennadius ch. LXXXV: "an anonymous book against certain works of Cassianus, which the church of God finds salutary, but which he brands as injurious, and in fact, some of the opinions of Cassian and Prosper on the grace of God and on free will are at variance with one another." Ch. LXXXVI, on Faustus: "the grace of God always invites, precedes and helps our will, and whatever gain that freedom of will may attain for its pious effect, is not its own desert, but the gift of grace."
- *Lérins (Contested).* *Comm.* ch. 24 [62] names Pelagius as the one who "attributed so much antecedent strength to Free-will, as to deny the necessity of God's grace." That Vincent's "profane novelty" also targets Augustine is **Heurtley's claim** (`iii.i`, editorial: "not only does he omit the name of St. Augustine ... but he denounces his doctrine, though under a misrepresentation of it") — carried Contested per Doc_02 §1.3, not as Vincent's own attestation.

### G4 — Received, not invented: antiquity, the Fathers, and consent against novelty

**Evidence.**
- *Cassian.* *Inst.* Preface: "as they were there delivered to us by the Fathers"; "in accordance with that which we received from our elders"; the correction of anything founded "according to the fancy of any one who has founded a monastery" against "the example of the elders established by ancient custom." *Inst.* I.2 (`iv.iii.i.ii`), on sackcloth: "we ought to give unhesitating allegiance and unquestioning obedience, not to those customs and rules which the will of a few have introduced, but to those which a long standing antiquity and numbers of the holy fathers have passed on by an unanimous decision to those that come after" — antiquity, numbers, unanimity, applied to monastic custom. *Conf.* Pref. - *Vincent.* *Comm.* ch. 2 [6]: "that faith which has been believed everywhere, always, by all ... This rule we shall observe if we follow universality, antiquity, consent." Ch. 3 [8]: "prefer the decrees, if such there be, of an ancient General Council to the rashness and ignorance of a few ... collate and consult and interrogate the opinions of the ancients." Ch. 6 [16], Stephen's rule: "Let there be no innovation—nothing but what has been handed down." Ch. 22 [53]: "not of private adoption, but of public tradition; a matter brought to thee, not put forth by thee, wherein thou art bound to be not an author but a keeper, not a teacher but a disciple, not a leader but a follower." XI (`ii.ii.xii`): Martin, asked to venerate a supposed martyr's tomb, "often asked from those who were his elders, whether among the presbyters or clerics, that the name of the martyr, or the time when he suffered, should be made known to him ... inasmuch as no steady tradition respecting them had come down from antiquity," and would not lend "his authority to the opinion of the multitude, lest a mere superstition should obtain a firmer footing."

**Evidential confidence:** **Documented** — the texts themselves are the evidence, and three independent voices state the criterion in their own words; Vincent's formulation is Documented to 434 by internal dating (Doc_01 §2.3).

### G5 — Formation by named example and eyewitness authority

**Evidence.**
- *Tours.* *Vita* ch. I (`ii.ii.ii`): Sulpitius writes "the life of a most holy man, which shall serve in future as an example to others; by which, indeed, the readers shall be roused to the pursuit of true knowledge, and heavenly warfare, and divine virtue"; Martin "should not remain unknown who was a man worthy of imitation." *Vita* ch. X: "eighty disciples, who were being disciplined after the example of the saintly master." *Vita* ch. XXV (`ii.ii.xxvi`, "Intercourse of Sulpitius with Martin"): Martin "insisted strongly that that was the man [Paulinus] who should be made the object of our imitation, adding that the present age was fortunate in possessing such a model of faith and virtue." *Ep.* I (`ii.iii.i`): the work's authority defended against a skeptic. *Ep.* III (`ii.iii.iii`): "through his example so numerous plants had sprung up for the service of the Lord"; and Martin's own "I have sinned if I leave you a different example." *Dial.* II.4 (`ii.iv.ii.iv`), Gallus: "I myself am a witness to this latter occurrence."
- *Marseilles.* *Inst.* Preface: "these things cannot be taught save by one who has had experience of them"; Cassian will tell what his predecessors left untouched, "since they endeavoured to describe what they had heard rather than what they had experienced"; Castor's "life alone is amply sufficient for an example." *Conf.* Pref. I: God "has deemed us worthy both to see them and to learn from them and to dwell with them"; the men themselves "incorporated, as it were, in their own Institutes, and ... speaking in the Latin tongue." *Inst.* IV.40 (`iv.iii.iv.xl`): the novice takes his examples "from a very few or indeed from one or two only and not from too many." *Conf.* II.13: "examples of life and conversation" received from the Elders.
- *Lérins.* *Comm.* ch. 1 [1]: "the things which I have truthfully received from the holy Fathers" — but received as *writings* to be collated (ch. 3 [8]; ch. 28, "on collating the consentient opinions of the Ancient Masters"), not as men seen and heard. Gennadius ch. LXX (ancient text): Hilary of Arles's "Life of Saint Honoratus, his predecessor" — a disciple's *Life* of his own master, the Lérins counterpart of the *Vita Martini*.

**Tests.** *Repetition* — passes: S, C, V, L(by existence), F. *Dependency* — passes: the *form* of both nodes' literature depends on it — the *Dialogues* are structured as eyewitness testimony (Gallus, II.4), the *Conferences* as heard conferences ("Germanus and I asked"), and each corpus defends its authority on that ground (Ep. I; Inst. Preface). *Formation* — passes: imitation of a named exemplar is the stated mechanism of formation in both nodes. *Explanatory* — passes: it explains why Sulpitius writes a *Life* and Hilary a *Life*, why Cassian writes reported speech, and why this world's stories (Doc_09) will be almost entirely first-person-attested. *Persistence* — passes across nodes, with Vincent transforming the value from *heard* to *read*. *Interaction* — passes: reinforcing with G1, G2, G3, G6, G7, G8; reshaping with G4; competing (thin) with G9 (§4).

**Doc_01 §11 item 6, answered as far as the evidence allows.** *Is this one value operating across both nodes, or two instances of a common hagiographic convention?* Both, and the distinction matters for §6. It is *one value* in the sense the tests measure: in both nodes the literature's authority and the disciple's formation are made to depend on it, in the same structural way. But it is also a convention shared far beyond this world (the *Vita Antonii* and the Egyptian literature carry it — Registry row 34, Named Comparandum, not drawn on), and Doc_03 tags every term in this cluster [SC]. **Convergence on a shared convention is real convergence but weak evidence of one world** — two unconnected Latin monastic communities in 400 could each have inherited it separately. This document therefore counts G5 toward cross-node convergence for gravity purposes and discounts it as evidence in §6.

**Cross-node status:** confirmed cross-node.

**Evidential confidence:** **Documented** as to what the texts do; the *authors'* eyewitness claims are themselves the object of Stancliffe's critical study (row 18, not read), and their historicity is not what this gravity asserts.

**Classification: SUPPORTING.** It organizes the literature's form and the formation mechanism, but functions within the context G1 and G2 establish (the exemplar is a monk-bishop or an Egyptian father); and the Framework's Primary bar ("organize the ecology broadly") is not met by a convention the world shares with its neighbors.

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** no divergence.

**Forces-connection notation.** *Held; fractured at the edge by transmission.* Under the initiating force it is simply the world's inherited literary form. Under the grace controversy it becomes a weapon: Cassian's authority is that he *heard* Chaeremon (Conf. XIII is reported speech), and Prosper, per Gibson (editorial), "never once names him directly, but merely speaks of him as a man of priestly rank" — the eyewitness-teacher's standing survives even his opponent's attack. ### G6 — *Virtus*: the saint as bearer of power

**Evidence.**
- *Tours.* *Vita* ch. VII (`ii.ii.viii`): Martin, "perceiving by means of the Spirit of God that power was present" (Roberts's fn., editorial: "*adesse virtutem*"), raises the catechumen. *Dial.* II.4 (`ii.iv.ii.iv`): "such an abundance [Roberts's fn.: *eam virtutum gratiam*] of power was by no means granted him while he was a bishop, as he remembered to have possessed before he obtained that office." *Dial.* III.13 (`ii.iv.iii.xiii`): after communion with the Ithacians "he felt a diminution of his power on account of the evil of that communion." *Ep.* I (`ii.iii.i`): "Was Martin really not possessed of power, and not a partaker of holiness, because he became exposed to danger from fire?" — the whole letter defends the *virtus* against a skeptic. *Vita* ch. IV: "protected by the sign of the cross, and not by shield or helmet." Gennadius ch. XIX (ancient text): "a man famous for signs and wonders and virtues" (Richardson's fn. "Virtues or miracles" is editorial, row 42).
- *Marseilles — the refusal, and now the ranking.* *Inst.* Preface: "Nor certainly shall I try to weave a tale of God's miracles and signs, although we have not only heard of many such among our elders, and those past belief, but have also seen them fulfilled under our very eyes; yet, leaving out all these things which minister to the reader nothing but astonishment and no instruction in the perfect life ... my purpose is to say a few words not about God's miracles, but about the way to improve our character." *Conf.* XVIII.1 (`iv.vi.ii.i`): "not of the miracles of God, but of the institutes and pursuits of the saints." **New this pass — *Conf.* XV (Nesteros, "On Divine Gifts"), not read for Doc_03:** XV.1 (`iv.v.vi.i`) gives a threefold taxonomy of healing gifts "learnt from the tradition of the Elders," the third of which "is copied by the deceit and contrivance of devils"; XV.6: "Nor did they think that any one should be renowned for the gifts and marvels of God, but rather for the fruits of his own good deeds"; XV.7 (`iv.v.vi.vii`): "the working of miracles and signs is not always necessary, nor good for all, nor granted to all. Humility therefore is the mistress of all virtues"; and — the sentence that sets the two nodes' literatures directly against each other — "our predecessors never reckoned those as good monks or free from the fault of vainglory, who professed themselves exorcists among men, and proclaimed with boastful ostentation among admiring crowds the grace which they had either obtained or which they claimed ... he ought to meet with commendation from us not from admiration of his miracles, but from the beauty of his life, nor should we ask whether the devils are subject to him."
- *Lérins — a complication (row 26, Latin, my rendering).* Eucherius, *De Laude Eremi* §27, admires the Egyptian fathers who "brought forth the accompanying grace either in silent revelations or in crying signs" (*"comitantem gratiam aut revelationibus tacitis aut clamantibus signis protulerunt"*). A Lérins voice does not share Cassian's refusal. **The competition is therefore Cassian-versus-Sulpitius, not south-versus-north wholesale.**

**Tests.** *Repetition* — passes across streams (S, Gn, E, AA) but is **one voice's** organizing principle. *Dependency* — passes in the north: the *Vita*, *Letters*, and *Dialogues* are built of *virtutes*; Doc_09's story inventory will depend on it; the north's account of the episcopate (power diminished by office, Dial. II.4) depends on it. *Formation* — passes in the north (Ep. III: "through his example so numerous plants had sprung up"; the power is what draws disciples) and fails in the south by design (XV.7). *Explanatory* — passes: it explains the north's exorcism narratives, blessed objects, the Egyptian merchant, the skeptic of Ep. I, and Brictio's sneer at "ridiculous fancies about visions" (*Dial.* III.15, Doc_03 6.7). *Persistence* — **fails across nodes**: present in Tours, refused by Cassian, admired of the Egyptians by Eucherius. *Interaction* — passes: competing with G2 (south) and G7; reinforcing with G5, G8, G10 (thin); reshaping with G1, G4, G9 (§4).

**Cross-node status:** **node-bound (northern)**, with the internal southern disagreement (Cassian vs. Eucherius) recorded.

**Evidential confidence:** **Documented** that Sulpitius's corpus is organized this way and that Cassian refuses it (both are the texts' own statements); Widely Accepted that Martin's fame rested on it (Gennadius). The historicity of the miracles is not what this gravity asserts and is not rated.

It does not organize the whole world, but the world cannot be described without it.

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** no divergence for a Tensional classification. Author Gravity flag (single voice) stands and is the reason this cannot rise above Tensional even though it organizes the northern literature completely.

**Forces-connection notation.** *Held in the north; contested from within; shifted by office.* Under the initiating force it is the north's answer to the Egyptian standard (G2's comparative mode: Egypt "justly proud of the numbers and virtues of its own saints," *Dial.* III.17). ### G8 — The soldier of Christ

**Evidence.**

## 6. What the gravity evidence says to the one-world-or-two question (input only)

Doc_01 §6 reserves this question for a Step 0/Atlas-level act and records the world as provisionally strand-singular. This document does not resolve it. What the tests show:

1. **Three gravities are confirmed across both nodes on this world's own evidence, and two of them are Primary.** G1 (the monk-bishop) is attested in the north by Sulpitius and Gennadius and in the south by Cassian's own dedications, Hilary of Arles's own sermon, and Gennadius — the single strongest cross-node convergence in the build, and it is the feature Doc_01 §1 makes this world's distinct-world criterion. G4 (received, not invented) is stated in all three voices' own words. G8 and G9 also hold across both nodes; G9 holds *in opposed valences*, which is itself a shared structure.
2. **The convergences are of different evidential quality.** G1 and G4 converge on features that are *not* simply the common property of late-antique Christianity (a monastic culture that keeps producing bishops and distrusting them is a particular shape). G5 and G8 converge on shared conventions ([SC] throughout Doc_03) and are discounted as evidence of unity — two unconnected communities would converge on them anyway.
3. **Two gravities are node-bound, in opposite directions, and one is split.** G3 (grace) is southern, with the confound that the Tours corpus predates the question. G6 (*virtus*) is northern, and its counter-teaching in the south (Conf. XV.7) is Egyptian, not a response to Tours — the two literatures do not address each other. G2 is one reference point in two modes: Tours rivals Egypt; Lérins–Marseilles receives it.
4. **No gravity tested here requires a documented Tours–south link to hold, and none supplies one.** Doc_01 §2.3's finding stands: nothing read this pass connects the two nodes by person, letter, or institution. What this document adds is that the two nodes are organized by the *same* Primary gravities (G1, G4) while differing in *mode* on a third (G2) and diverging on their Tensional/Supporting layer (G6 north, G3/G7 south).

**Stated as input, not as a finding on the question:** the gravity evidence is consistent with *one world in two modes* (shared Primaries; mode-split on Egypt; complementary node-bound Supporting/Tensional gravities) and equally consistent with *two lineages that share a Latin-monastic inheritance* (the shared Primaries could be inherited separately; the node-bound gravities do not interact across nodes in any text). The decisive fact — that G1, this world's distinct-world criterion, holds in both nodes on independent insider evidence — is the strongest single argument the ecology offers for treating it as one world; the decisive absence — that G3 and G6, the two gravities that give each node its *particular* character, never meet in any text — is the strongest for two. Doc_01's provisionally-strand-singular status is **not contradicted** by anything here, and this document recommends it be kept operative for Doc_05–Doc_07, with the mode-split on G2 and the node-bound status of G3/G6/G7 recorded in every downstream document that touches them.
