### S1. The camel's-hair claim is contradicted by both texts it cites — the only outright factual error found

**Locus in Doc_04:** §3, G2 Evidence, *Tours (comparative mode)*, line 121:
> "*Vita* ch. X: Marmoutier's monks 'clothed in garments of camels' hair,' **the Egyptian dress Cassian says Gaul cannot wear**."

**Checked against:**
- `npnf211`, *Vita* ch. X (`ii.ii.xi`): "Most of them were clothed in garments of camels' hair." Roberts's adjacent footnote reads "**Cf. St. Matt. iii. 4**" — i.e. the translator himself reads it as a John-the-Baptist allusion, not an Egyptian-monastic marker.
- `npnf211`, *Inst.* I.10 (`iv.iii.i.x`), the climate-modification chapter Doc_04 cites two clauses earlier: the garments Cassian names as impossible in Gaul are "slippers or tunics or a single frock," "the covering of tiny hoods," and "the wearing of a sheepskin." Camel's hair is **not** among them.
- `npnf211`, *Inst.* I.1 (`iv.iii.i.i`): the **only** occurrence of "camel's hair" anywhere in Cassian is his *approving* citation of Matt. iii.4 — "the same John had his raiment of camel's hair and a girdle of skin about his loins" — as the scriptural type of the monastic girdle. (Grep across the whole 4MB file returns exactly two hits for camel/camel's hair: *Vita* X and *Inst.* I.1.)

So the one garment Sulpitius and Cassian actually share is used *positively* by both, and the claim inverts it. This is not a trivial slip: it is the sole [M] (material/daily-life) evidence for G2, and [M] is carried in G2's stream list at §2.1, in the §8 Gravity Index, and in G2's Repetition-test result ("passes: S, C, L, Sv, Gn, M").

**Remedy:** delete the clause "the Egyptian dress Cassian says Gaul cannot wear." Either drop [M] from G2's stream lists in §2.1 and §8, or re-ground it on *Inst.* I.10 and IV.10–11, both already quoted in the same Evidence block and both of which genuinely carry the climate-adaptation point.

### S6. G2's Salvian evidence does not attest the proposition G2 states

**Locus in Doc_04:** §3, G2 Evidence, line 120, and the forces notation at line 133.
> "*Gov.* VIII.4 (Sanford p. 229): 'any servant of God from the monasteries of Egypt or the sacred places of Jerusalem or the holy and venerable retreats of the desert' — Egypt as the byword for holiness, in a non-monastic voice."
