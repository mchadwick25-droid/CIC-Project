## 1. Candidate identification

> Atlas I.35, "The Second-Century Greek Apologists," c. 124–200 CE. Athens, Rome, Sardis, Antioch. Justin Martyr, Athenagoras, Theophilus of Antioch, Tatian, and the fragmentary sub-apostolic voices (Melito of Sardis, Quadratus, Aristides, Hegesippus, Dionysius of Corinth, and others). A body of individuals defending Christianity's reasonableness and legality to a skeptical or hostile Greco-Roman intellectual and political culture, in the vocabulary of Greek philosophy, roughly eighty years before the Nicene settlement.

**Naming note:** the census's own registration text calls the genre-vs-community objection "honest": *"apologetic is a genre rather than a community... this is a body of people doing one thing in one direction for eighty years, not a settlement with a bishop."* This document treats that objection as live and unresolved by Step 0 — Step 0 tests eligibility and differentiation, not whether "apologists" is the right frame for a Representative; that is Doc_01's own World Identification work, flagged forward in §4 below.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

Article 4's floor tests confession against the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed's content — full divinity and consubstantiality of Christ, true humanity, the Passion/resurrection/ascension/return, and the Spirit as Lord and giver of life. This candidate's entire window (124–200) predates the Creed itself by more than a century, so A1 is not the operative test in the primary sense (per the same "as applicable" Procedure text IJC's own confirmation relied on for its 312–325 sliver); A2 is the real gate here, since unlike IJC this candidate does not straddle 325 at all.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears, with one figure requiring honest disclosure rather than exclusion

A2 tests continuity with the strand that became Nicene orthodoxy, against divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar).

**Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, Aristides, and the fragmentary sub-apostolic voices clear cleanly and strongly.** Justin's Logos theology is not merely continuous with what became Nicene Trinitarian vocabulary — it is one of its direct historical sources; Athenagoras' *Plea* and *On the Resurrection* and Theophilus' *To Autolycus* are standard proto-orthodox texts with no divergence to test for.

**Tatian requires a disclosure, not an exclusion.** Tatian's *Address to the Greeks* is the pupil-of-Justin apologetic work vendored here — but Irenaeus reports directly (*Against Heresies* I.28) that after Justin's martyrdom Tatian went on to found or lead the Encratite current (rejecting marriage and wine on ascetic-dualist grounds), a genuine later divergence. The *Address* itself is generally read by the field as predating or independent of that later turn, and it is the *Address* alone under consideration here, not a later, more clearly divergent work — so A2 clears for the specific vendored text, but this must be named plainly in Doc_02 rather than left for a participant or reviewer to discover unflagged. This is the same discipline the project already applies to Bardaisan and Modalism elsewhere in the census (contested figures held as open questions rather than smoothed into either "settled orthodox" or "settled excluded").

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Tatian's specific and bounded caveat carried forward as a binding Doc_02 disclosure (§4).

### A3 — Contemporary movements

Not applicable. Second-century historical figures, not a contemporary confessional community.

### A4 — Hand-selected adjacent inclusion

Not invoked. A2 clears this candidate directly; no waiver of Article 4's content is needed.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This candidate's own defining activity is arguing directly against named rivals — Justin against Marcion by name (1 Apol. 26, 58) and at length against Judaism's own claim on the Scriptures (the whole of the *Dialogue with Trypho*). Per A5, Marcion is not separately screened here; he appears, correctly, as this candidate's own named opponent, the way Homoian Christianity appears inside IJC's own build rather than as an independent candidate. Trypho is a different case worth naming honestly: he represents an external interlocutor (rabbinic Judaism), not a Christian rival current, and Doc_02 will need its own care in how it frames a two-thousand-year-old Christian-Jewish polemical dialogue for a modern participant — flagged forward, not resolved here.

**Strand Determination (Article 21) — explicitly Step 1's work, flagged here rather than resolved:** the roughly dozen works assigned to this candidate are not uniform in genre. Justin, Athenagoras, Tatian, Theophilus, and Aristides are true outward apologetic — arguments addressed to emperors, magistrates, or an educated pagan public. Several of the fragmentary voices currently bundled with them in `pahc.source.second-third-century-remains.md` (Hegesippus, Dionysius of Corinth, Rhodon, Polycrates, Serapion, Apollonius) are, on inspection, internal church correspondence and early church-historical reporting — much closer in kind to PAHC's own genre than to an emperor-facing apology, even though the census currently files them under the same candidate. Quadratus and Melito sit in between (each did write an actual apology to an emperor, per Eusebius, though it is not yet confirmed here which specific fragments of Melito's are vendored under his name). **This is a real, unresolved boundary question for Doc_01, not a cosmetic one** — the candidate's own coherence as "the apologists" depends on drawing this line correctly rather than assuming the census's current bundling is already right.

### Section A conclusion

**Clears Section A.** A2 is satisfied directly for the core figures, with Tatian's later Encratite turn named as a bounded, disclosed caveat rather than smoothed over or treated as disqualifying. A5's rival-handling is straightforward for Marcion and requires care, not exclusion, for the Trypho material. The Article 21 strand/genre question inside the candidate's own roster is real and is carried forward to Doc_01 as binding work, not resolved here.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing

Strong pass, independently verified against the actual text library (not taken on the census's own word): Justin's two *Apologies* and the *Dialogue*, Athenagoras' *Plea* and *On the Resurrection*, Theophilus' *To Autolycus*, Tatian's *Address*, and the *Epistle to Diognetus* are all vendored whole (`anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml`, `anf02_hermas-tatian-athenagoras-theophilus-clement-alexandria.xml`). Aristides (recovered 1889) and roughly ten fragmentary sub-apostolic voices are also vendored. This is a genuinely rich, multi-author, whole-work-heavy corpus — more complete works in hand than several already-built worlds had at admission.

### B2 — Ecology

Real and uneven, in a specific and checkable way. The apologetic-argument register (engaging Greek philosophy, rebutting the atheism/cannibalism/sedition charges, arguing Christian ethics as superior) is exceptionally strong — this is what the genre *is*. Persecution and its stakes are load-bearing throughout (Justin's own martyrdom; the genre's whole occasion is legal jeopardy). **Worship-practice texture is thin here, and deliberately so, not by oversight:** the one substantial first-hand liturgical description in this material — Justin's account of baptism and Sunday worship, 1 Apol. 61 and 65–67 — is already fully claimed by PAHC's own figure record (`pahc.figure.justin.md`, `locus: whole work`), which built its own worship-practice ecology on exactly this passage. A build of this candidate should not re-derive that lens from the same material a sibling world already owns; its own worship-practice picture will correspondingly be thinner than a typical built world's, and Doc_05 must name that honestly rather than manufacture texture from a citation PAHC has already made its own. Ordinary lay formation, women's voices, and enslaved persons' own testimony are essentially absent — more so than in most built worlds, because the apologetic genre itself selects for classically-trained male rhetoricians writing to power, more narrowly than PAHC's already-noted "literate, male, leadership-tier" skew.

**Passes B2, with the worship-practice thinness named as a structural, disclosed limitation directly caused by the Justin/PAHC boundary — not treated as resolved by noting it here.**

### B3 — Uniqueness

- **Versus PAHC (Post-Apostolic House-Church Christianity, Built & Live):** this is the live, structurally significant adjacency, and it does not resolve to "distinct, no further action" the way IJC's Cappadocian check did. Justin Martyr is one of only eleven named figures in PAHC's own roster, with his own figure record, story, and quotes, and PAHC's own source metadata claims `locus: whole work` for both his *First Apology* and *Dialogue*. Direct inspection of what PAHC actually draws from him — the conversion narrative (Dialogue 2–8), the worship/baptism description (1 Apol. 61, 65–67), and one theological aside flagged in-record as "his own, not necessarily the whole world's" (1 Apol. 46) — shows PAHC using him narrowly, as a *community witness*, and never engaging his actual philosophical argument, his address to Roman power, or the Dialogue's extended debate with Trypho. That gives this candidate real, unclaimed territory in the same man's corpus — the precedent for this (a single figure serving two Representatives through two different projects) is Augustine, already split cleanly across HAL's Hebrew-vs-Septuagint correspondent and Donatism's anti-Donatist controversialist. **The difference from the Augustine precedent, and the reason this is flagged as binding rather than merely noted, is that no comparable project split has yet been ruled on for Justin specifically** — this document recommends Mark's own explicit ruling before Doc_01 begins, either (a) confirming Justin as a shared figure on the Augustine model, scoped explicitly to his unclaimed apologetic-argument and Trypho-debate material, or (b) excluding him from this build's own figure roster entirely and centering the Representative on Athenagoras, Theophilus, and Tatian instead. Both are live options; this document does not decide between them.
- **Versus Alexandrian Catechetical Christianity (Built & Live):** clean, sequential, and complementary rather than overlapping — this candidate is individual, pre-institutional apologists arguing ad hoc and often at personal risk; Alexandria is a settled catechetical school two generations later, using Greek learning inside an institution rather than defending Christianity's legality to one outside it. No figure overlap found. This candidate would function as the historical bridge between PAHC and Alexandria — the missing "how did the church learn to argue in Greek philosophy's own terms, before that engagement became a curriculum" chapter — a genuine, presently-uncovered piece of the map.
- **Versus the other six built worlds (Desert, PAHC as above, Hieronymian, Syriac, Imperial-Juridical, Cappadocian):** no figure or source overlap found in this pass. This check was run specifically against PAHC, the only built world sharing this candidate's own era and much of its source pool; a full cross-check against all eight built worlds' own figure/source rosters should be completed at Doc_01, not assumed complete from this document alone.

**B3 is real but not unconditional.** The Justin question is this candidate's single most consequential open item — larger than any adjacency flagged in IJC's own confirmation — and is carried forward as binding pre-Doc_01 work in §4, not treated as passed outright.

### B4 — Audiences

Strong, honestly-scoped fit with *academically minded users*, *thoughtful believers* working through faith-and-reason questions, and a real, currently underserved audience for this portfolio: people wanting a *rational, philosophically-argued* model of Christian defense rather than a devotional or formational one — including *deconstructing Christians* and skeptical seekers who want to see the case argued on philosophy's own terms rather than assumed. Weaker fit with *curious seekers* wanting lived spiritual formation; this is not a devotional world the way Desert Monasticism is.

### B5 — Scale

Athens, Rome, Antioch, and Sardis — major centers, though the candidate's own coherence as "one world" spanning four cities over eighty years is itself part of the genre-vs-community question flagged in A5/§4. Modest, non-decisive tiebreak factor only.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A directly (§2); clears Sourcing strongly (B1); clears Ecology with a specific, structurally-caused thinness named rather than hidden (B2); **does not cleanly clear B3** the way a Tier 1 candidate normally would, because the Justin/PAHC boundary is a real, unresolved, binding decision rather than a vigilance note; serves real, distinctive audiences honestly (B4). **Provisional Tier 1 — Strong seed, conditional on the Justin ruling in §4.** If Mark rules to exclude Justin from this build's own roster, this candidate's B1/B2 case weakens somewhat (Justin was part of what made the sourcing "unusually good") but very likely still clears Tier 1 on Athenagoras, Theophilus, Tatian (with his own caveat), and Aristides alone; if Mark rules to share Justin on the Augustine model, this stays a strong Tier 1 candidate outright.
