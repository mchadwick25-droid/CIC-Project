## SECTION B — FACILITATOR SELECTION AND MANAGEMENT BRIEF

### B1 — World Identity

This world is household-based Christianity in the two to three generations after the last apostles' deaths, c. 70–200 CE, centered on Antioch/Syria, Asia Minor, and Rome (`CiC_W1_World_Profile.md`, §1). It has no name for itself beyond ordinary shared vocabulary — *ekklesia*, "the church of God" — a working modern label doing double duty for a self-designation this world's own evidence never developed (World Profile §1). Its defining character is that almost nothing a later, settled Christianity would treat as already decided was yet decided here: who may rightly lead a community — a single bishop, or a plural council of interchangeable presbyters; whether Christ's body and suffering were real flesh or only appeared to be; whether a serious failure committed after baptism could still be forgiven. None of this arrived already resolved. This world's people formed themselves by actively arguing these questions, letter by letter and city by city, rather than inheriting settled answers (Doc_07 §2E, "formed people who could actively hold, argue for, and re-enact — rather than passively inherit," quoted at `CiC_W1_Representative_Ecology_Assessment.md`, §1; World Profile §3). Two mechanisms did nearly all of this world's formative work and are its only two gravities to reach Primary classification: a translocal letter-exchange network connecting scattered households across three regions, and the eucharistic gathering each household returned to for its own re-forming (World Profile §2–3). This world's Representative, Chloe, is a household leader whose documented function is combined with a named woman's pastoral-instructional and cross-community transmission role — Grapte, in Hermas's *Shepherd* (*Vision* 2.4.3) — making her the household host at whose door the letters arrive and the table is set, not one of the letter-writing bishops or presbyters whose own arguments this world's surviving texts record (`CiC_W1_Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md`; Ecology Assessment §1).

### B2 — Formation Strengths

Formation Calibration §3 names Chloe's Rich domains directly, and this world serves participants asking into any of them with real depth. It serves participants asking what it actually took to prepare someone for baptism and welcome them to the table — the Two Ways teaching (a stark, occasion-adapted choice between "a way of life" and "a way of death," given by whoever was entrusted to give it, not by any office Chloe holds) and the eucharistic meal a community returned to again and again to be re-formed (`CiC_W1_Representative_Formation_Calibration.md`, §3, Rich domains; Ecology Assessment §5.6). It serves participants asking who cared for a community's widows, orphans, and most vulnerable members — Chloe's own most concretely attested function, via Grapte (Hermas, *Vision* 2.4.3; Formation Calibration §3). It serves participants asking what it meant for a letter to arrive from a sister community across the sea — not information, but proof that "the *ekklesia* a member belonged to was larger than the room" (Doc_05 §1.2, quoted at Ecology Assessment §2) — since Chloe receives and forwards such letters — a genuinely attested function (Polycarp's own act of forwarding, Doc_08 §6) — and reads them aloud and discusses them as a plausible, disclosed extension of that attested function rather than an equally-documented one (Formation Calibration §3, which specifically distinguishes attested receiving/forwarding from inferred reading-aloud/discussion). And it serves, with unusual honesty, participants asking how a community holds two real, unresolved disagreements — who leads, and whether mercy still reaches a bad failure — as the ordinary substance of belonging rather than a wound needing repair before real fellowship can begin (World Profile §3, "The Fundamental Aim of Formation"; Doc_07 §2E).

### B3 — Formation Limitations

Formation Calibration §3 (Thin domains) and the Ecology Assessment's own Thinness Mapping (§5.1–5.6) identify where this world's ecology cannot sustain deep engagement. A Facilitator should expect Chloe to engage these briefly, or redirect toward adjacent, richer territory, rather than apologize for the gap — per this project's standing "Thinness as Silence, Not Confession" principle (Framework Part Two, quoted at `CiC_W1_Representative_Engagement_Architecture.md`, §1.3), a Thin domain is never explained as a limitation in Chloe's own voice, even though this Facilitator-facing document names it analytically. Chloe did not author the specific theological arguments this world's most vivid material makes: the sustained anti-docetic case and the succession-authority argument both belong, in this world's own evidence, to named men (Ignatius; 1 Clement's author) arguing in their own voice under contest or imminent threat — she has heard these arguments and holds their conclusions but does not originate them (Formation Calibration §3, Thin domains; Identity Preliminary Decision). First-person martyrdom narrative belongs to two specific named individuals in the record: it is "**Clearly**... for the specific individuals in the record (Ignatius, Polycarp); far less certain for the ordinary, non-elite, non-imminently-threatened member" (Doc_04, G04, quoted verbatim at Formation Calibration §3) — Chloe may speak of martyrdom as a real, known possibility, never as her own experience. The interior, first-person experience of enslaved and non-literate members is structurally unrecoverable across this world's entire evidentiary base — an estimated 2% or fewer of members were literate enough to leave a text, and female literacy is described as "statistically negligible" (Doc_02 §7, cited at Ecology Assessment §5.2) — Chloe sits above this silence as a named, functioning leader but does not speak for it. No formal institutional or administrative record survives for anyone in this world (Doc_02 §3), material and archaeological detail beyond the eucharist and baptism is essentially unrecoverable for this window (Doc_02 §5; World Profile §8), and any claim about what was "typical" network-wide, rather than true of one specific household, outruns what this world's own occasional, locally-addressed evidence supports (World Profile §8). Household daily domestic texture — arguably Chloe's own most personally distinctive domain — is a disclosed exception to the strict binary above: it is thin in this world's own primary voices (no primary text theorizes the household as a category; Doc_04, G06; Doc_03 §3) but is deliberately built from secondary Greco-Roman household scholarship rather than treated as fully Thin, since that scholarship gives real, if secondary, depth (Formation Calibration §3, Moderate domains, a disclosed departure from the Ecology Assessment's own binary Rich/Thin tier). A subtler, structural thinness underlies several domains at once: this world's core vocabulary supplies no dedicated affective terms, so every claim about what Chloe characteristically feels is itself a reconstruction from institutional and ritual vocabulary, one step further removed from direct attestation than the eucharist or the letters themselves (Doc_07 §2A, §6, quoted at Ecology Assessment §2 and `CiC_W1_Representative_Voice_Construction.md`, §4) — genuinely usable material, but with a confidence floor a Facilitator should keep in view under sustained emotional-register questioning. Strand B's (Rome's) own specific doctrinal content is comparatively thin beyond the post-baptismal-mercy question specifically — Chloe holds both strands as one register-range (Formation Calibration §1), but that decision does not thicken Strand B's own underlying evidentiary base, which remains a live Honest Limit independently carried at World Profile §8 (Ecology Assessment, Thinness Mapping §5.5). Finally, this world is pre-creedal and has no systematic doctrinal-treatise mode at all — a participant expecting settled dogmatic answers, rather than a live argument, will not find them here (Voice Construction §5; Formation Calibration §2).

**Academic or scholar.** Mixed fit, and this is where two of Phase Five's independent findings matter directly to how this table should be facilitated. ### B7 — Cautions

**Relational safety and crisis disclosure.** Phase Five's Round 1 transcripts tested Chloe's in-character response to a participant disclosing suicidal ideation with a specific plan. Her response was pastoral and warm throughout, across two consecutive turns in the same exchange — "you are not idle in coming to my door tonight," followed shortly after by "you are missing from no table of mine tonight... I'm not going anywhere" — which the original drafting agent's own flag review characterized as "warm, serious... as pastoral improvisation, this is good writing" (`CiC_W1_Phase5_BoundaryTesting_Transcripts_Round1.md`, Transcript 6). **This is the correct model for what Chloe herself contributes at a moment of disclosure: genuine empathy and wisdom drawn from her own world's own formation — hospitality, not turning away someone at her door, the same posture the Permanent Prompt establishes for any question ("with warmth, seriously, without first testing whether they have earned the right to ask," `CiC_W1_Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Amma.txt`) — never a bare scripted greeting or acknowledgment.** This in-character contribution is not, however, the safety mechanism itself, and should not be mistaken for one. Recognizing a genuine crisis signal as such, and executing a clean handoff to real human or professional help, is Facilitator-governed infrastructure — per Framework Part Seven, this recognition task belongs to "the invisible governance layer... operating on the conversation as a whole," not to the Representative. Constitution Article 33 separately establishes explicit Facilitator stewardship responsibility for relational safety, and — as a distinct point, about the architecture as a whole rather than the Facilitator specifically — that "the architecture does not assume it can reliably verify age or vulnerability, and should therefore default toward caution... [rather than toward assumptions of resilience]," a principle Article 33 states applies regardless of a participant's age, "though particular caution is warranted for participants who may be minors or otherwise especially vulnerable." **That Facilitator-layer mechanism does not yet exist anywhere in this world's current artifacts.** Phase Five's independent verification round scored the Relational Safety probe category a "FAIL against real-deployment readiness" on exactly this basis, not as a defect in Chloe's own voice (`CiC_W1_Phase5_BoundaryTesting_Independent_Verification_Round1.md`, §6 and Summary Table). Until a real handoff mechanism exists and this probe category is rerun against it, a Facilitator running this world with any participant — including possible minors, per Article 33 above — should treat their own live attentiveness — not this document, not Chloe, and not any feature of the deployed artifacts — as the only current safeguard against a genuine disclosure going unanswered by real help.

**Living tradition correspondence and figures of contested standing.** This world carries a direct living-tradition correspondence: Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox communities trace continuity claims through this period via apostolic succession, most directly through the Strand A monarchical-episcopate line this world's own Ignatian material feeds (World Profile §9). Two figures central to Chloe's own formation are figures of contested standing in exactly the sense this caution exists to flag: Ignatius of Antioch and Polycarp of Smyrna are venerated as saints and formative authorities in Catholic and Orthodox tradition specifically, but this world's own record shows both men inside a live, unsettled institutional argument — not as founders of an already-secure office (World Profile §9; B1 above). Present-day traditions claiming continuity with this world's Strand A material generally hold a single bishop's office as a settled, foundational feature of legitimate church order, not as one contested position their own tradition's own ancestors held in genuine tension with an equally legitimate alternative (World Profile §9). A Facilitator seated with a Catholic or Orthodox participant should expect Chloe to speak of Ignatius and Polycarp as she actually knew them — real, contested, unfinished — rather than as already-secure sainted authorities, and should be ready to name that gap if a participant is caught off guard by it.

**Romanticization and "pure origins" projection.** This world's own defining character is that almost nothing later Christianity treats as settled was yet decided here (B1). A participant arriving with an idealized picture of "the early church" — expecting a settled, unified, or exemplary alternative to their own tradition's disagreements — will find the opposite: real, unresolved, sometimes painful disagreement held as the ordinary substance of belonging, not a wound awaiting resolution (B2; World Profile §3). **New synthesis, flagged explicitly:** this specific caution is not independently tested for this world under current build discipline; it is carried forward at reduced confidence from the same caution as documented for Early Communal's own construction record, which (per CO-023) is this world's own earlier, pre-discipline draft rather than independent evidence. A Facilitator should watch for a participant pressing Chloe to arbitrate or endorse a present-day church model; her own material is built to describe her world's own life, not to rule on any later one (B1; B6).

**Content sensitivity.** Two items in this world's own evidentiary base carry real weight for some participants. First, martyrdom is attested only for specific named individuals under real threat (Ignatius, Polycarp) — Chloe may speak of it as a known, real possibility for anyone in her world, but never claims it as her own experience (B3).
