**(b) Minor wrong-section citations (content is real and traceable elsewhere in the evidentiary base, but the specific section cited does not contain it):**
- Section 2C: "1 Clement chs. 42, 44" is grounded in Doc_02 **§1.2**, not §1.5 as cited (the Hermas half of that same citation, §1.5, is correct).

The claim is fully supported by **Doc_05 §1.1** alone; §5.1 should be dropped from that citation.
- Section 2D: Doc_03 §§2.7/2.11's Author Gravity risk is quoted as "maximal" — Doc_03's actual wording is the adverbial "Yes, maximally." Trivial, but not a literal quote as presented.

**(c) One paraphrase presented with more apparent specificity than the source states verbatim** — Section 2C: "Rome's intervention at Corinth is itself an exercise and public demonstration of Rome's own perceived authority to correct another *ekklesia*" is not a quotation from Doc_02 §1.2 (which says only that Rome wrote "with real, exercised corrective authority to Corinth, the best-attested single act of translocal authority"). The underlying substance is supported — this is not a fabrication — but the specific phrasing is Doc_07's own gloss dressed close enough to the source's own register that a reader could mistake it for a paraphrase-of-record rather than an inference. Not a correction-required item, but worth tightening.

None of the above rises to "misattributes a source document" in the disqualifying sense the task asked about, and none reads as filled in from general period knowledge — every one of these is a citation-address problem (right claim, wrong door number), not a content problem, except (a), which is a genuine mismatch of topic.

## 4. The central analytical claim — classification pattern as evidence for "argued, not inherited"

This required the most judgment of any item in the review, and the finding here is genuinely mixed.

Doc_07's central move (2E, Section 4) is: Doc_04 classified only two Primary gravities (G02 Network, G07 Liturgy — both "delivery mechanisms") while G01 (Authority), G03 (State Pressure), G04 (Martyrdom), and G05 (Boundary-Drawing) — the "contents" being delivered — all landed Supporting or Tensional. Doc_07 reads this pattern as itself evidence that the world's formation logic is "argue, not inherit," rather than evidence of a thin or under-integrated construction.

**This is not simply an unfalsifiable post-hoc rationalization** — it is more specific than "few Primary gravities implies an argued world" (which would indeed be gameable against any pattern). It specifically ties Primary status to a *structural category* (mechanism vs. content), which is a testable, non-trivial distinction. And for at least two of the four downgraded gravities, the fit is genuinely good: **G01's** Cross-Check explicitly states the *coarse* claim (authority had to be actively organized) is Widely Accepted while only the *specific resolution* is Contested — i.e., Doc_04's own reasoning is that this was **historically, actually live and contested in antiquity**, not merely under-evidenced today. **G05** is similar in kind (Ignatius's own anti-docetic argument responding to live, contemporary rivals).

**The fit is weaker for G03 and G04**, and Doc_07 does not fully disclose this distinction. G03's downgrade (round 2 review) was driven by a *different* kind of problem: not that ancient people were still arguing about the reality or severity of state pressure, but that **modern reconstruction cannot responsibly generalize** from scattered episodes (Nero, Pliny, Ignatius's arrest) into one continuous condition — a confidence/evidentiary-generalization limit, not a claim that this world's own inhabitants left the question of state pressure "unsettled" in the same sense they left the question of who should lead unsettled. Similarly, G04's Supporting classification rests on a **representativeness** problem (formation-shaping for two named individuals, uncertain for the ordinary member) — again a question about the limits of what the surviving evidence can support, not evidence that the ancient community itself treated martyrdom's meaning as a live, argued question the way it treated ecclesial office.

Doc_07 folds G03 and G04 into the "argued, not inherited" reading somewhat more loosely than G01/G05 warrant — in 2E's "Formation logic and confirmed gravities" paragraph, G03 is (correctly, and more cautiously) treated as background condition rather than argued content, but Section 4's summary sentence ("the *contents* being delivered (office, boundary, martyrdom-meaning)... are, by this world's own defining character, exactly what remained genuinely unresolved") does lump martyrdom-meaning in with office and boundary as if all three were unresolved in the same historically-live sense, when Doc_04's own reasoning for G04 is substantially an artifact of surviving-evidence thinness (two data points, both Strand A, both Ignatius-adjacent) rather than demonstrated live ancient contestation. Doc_04 itself flags this ambiguity directly (Open Item 1: "current classification reflects an absence of evidence, not a confirmed absence of the phenomenon in Rome").

**Recommendation:** this is not a fatal flaw, but Section 4 and/or Section 6 should explicitly acknowledge that the "classification pattern as coherence-signature" argument rests on firmer ground for G01/G05 than for G03/G04, where evidentiary thinness (not demonstrated ancient contestation) is doing more of the work. As written, the claim reads slightly stronger than what Doc_04 actually licenses for two of its four supporting data points.
