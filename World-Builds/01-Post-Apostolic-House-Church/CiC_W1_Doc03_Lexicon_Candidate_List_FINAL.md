## 1. Method

Candidate terms were identified by re-reading Doc_02 section by section and asking, for each recurring word or phrase load-bearing enough to matter for Step 4's gravity discovery: (a) is this the world's own vocabulary — a term the primary voices themselves use or a term the outside/hostile witnesses used to categorize this world — or is it a modern scholarly label retrofitted onto the world after the fact; and (b) does it trace to a Native Source Registry row, not an Excluded one. Terms that failed test (a) are listed in Section 3 (terms considered and excluded) rather than silently dropped, since the Framework's stated purpose for this step is specifically to keep modern analytical vocabulary from substituting for the world's own language in Step 4.

Two source-asymmetry caveats carried forward from Doc_02 apply to every term below and are not repeated in each entry: first, every primary voice is a literate, male, leadership-tier author (Doc_02 Section 8), so every "preliminary definition" below is a leadership-tier definition, not necessarily how an ordinary, non-literate member would have used or understood the term; second, several terms rest on a single primary voice or a single community's usage (the Didache above all — Doc_02 Section 1.1 and Open Item 1 flag it as plausibly Syrian and single-community, not network-wide), and that limitation is carried into this document's Strand Attribution and Author Gravity Risk columns rather than smoothed over.

**Round-1 revision addition — Registry Priority-Review-Flag density.** The Source Registry's own Priority Review Queue flags 45 of its 70 rows for heightened scrutiny. Round-1 review correctly found that this document did not previously state, for any term, what fraction of its own cited rows fall inside that flagged set — information Doc_02 already generated but this document had not carried forward, even though it directly bears on how much weight Doc_04's Gravity Discovery should extend to a given term. Each term entry below now states a **Registry Flag Density** (flagged rows / total cited rows) so that carry-forward is explicit rather than requiring a reader to cross-check the Registry by hand.

## 2. Candidate Terms

### Tier 1 candidates (central to this world's own self-organization; will need full ecological treatment at Step 6)

**2.1 ἐπίσκοπος (episkopos) — "overseer / bishop"**
- **Preliminary definition:** A leadership office named across this world's institutional evidence, but whose actual content — a single, monarchical office versus one of several interchangeable terms for a plural governing college — is exactly this world's central, unresolved institutional question.
- **Strand attribution:** This term is the single clearest strand-differentiating word in the whole source set. Strand A (Antioch/Asia Minor, per Doc_01 Section 6): Ignatius uses *episkopos* for a single, monarchical office distinct from and superior to the *presbyteroi* (P03; Doc_02 Sections 1.3, 3, 4, 9). Strand B (Rome, per Doc_01 Section 6): 1 Clement uses *episkopos* and *presbyteros* interchangeably for a plural governing college (chs. 42, 44; P02; Doc_02 Section 3) — Hermas's plural "presbyters who preside over the Church" (*Vision* 2.4.3; P05) reinforces this reading for Rome specifically.
- **Primary source anchors:** P02 (1 Clement), P03 (Ignatius), P05 (Hermas), Didache 15:1 (P01, transitional instruction to appoint "bishops and deacons").
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes — the Strand A picture rests overwhelmingly on one voice (Ignatius) writing under armed guard, en route to execution, in a documented rhetorical genre (Doc_02 Section 1.3 flags this as the most contested representativeness question in the whole source set: does Ignatius describe an already-secured reality, or argue for one not yet won even among his own addressees?). This is not a settled definition; it is this world's own live institutional argument.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P02, P03, P05, S05, S06, S07, S08.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 4/7 cited rows flagged for priority review (P02, P03, P05, S06) — a majority, consistent with how genuinely unsettled this term's institutional content is.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** Yes. This term cannot be finalized before Doc_04's Gravity Discovery and Doc_05's Ecological Reconstruction address the Strand A/B tension directly.

**2.2 πρεσβύτερος (presbyteros) — "elder"**
- **Preliminary definition:** A leadership-college member; in Strand B usage, interchangeable with *episkopos*; in Strand A usage (per Ignatius), a distinct, subordinate office under a single *episkopos*.
- **Strand attribution:** Both strands use the word, but assign it different institutional weight — see 2.1 above. Notably, Polycarp — writing from within Strand A's own geography — identifies *himself* only as one of "the presbyters" in his own letter (P04; Doc_02 Section 1.4), even though Ignatius separately addresses him as Smyrna's bishop. Doc_01 already treats this as internal counter-evidence against reading Strand A's monarchical-episcopate program as fully secured.
- **Primary source anchors:** P02, P03, P04, P05.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes, but for a different reason than 2.1 — the term's Strand A meaning depends on reading Ignatius's usage as authoritative over Polycarp's own self-designation, which is itself a live interpretive choice, not a given.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P02, P03, P04, P05, S06, S07.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 5/6 cited rows flagged for priority review (P02, P03, P04, P05, S06).
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** Yes, paired with 2.1 — the two terms cannot be treated separately without reproducing the same Strand A/B tension twice.

**2.3 ἐκκλησία (ekklesia) — "assembly / church"**
- **Preliminary definition:** The community's own self-designation for itself as a gathered body, used by every primary voice in this source set to address or describe a given local community (1 Clement's own opening address, "the church of God which sojourns at Rome, to the church of God which sojourns at Corinth," is the clearest single instance).
- **Strand attribution:** Used across both strands without apparent variation in the word itself — the variation this world shows is in what office structure the word's own internal governance implies (see 2.1–2.2), not in the word *ekklesia* itself.
- **Primary source anchors:** P02 (opening address), P03, P04, Didache (P01).
- **Author Gravity risk:** No single-voice dependency — this is the most evenly-attested term in the candidate list.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P01, P02, P03, P04.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 4/4 cited rows flagged for priority review — notably, this term's even attestation across all four voices does not mean the underlying rows are individually uncontested; it means the term itself shows no strand-based variation, a distinct question from row-level confidence.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** Yes — as the base community-identity term, Step 6 should treat it fully even though (unlike 2.1–2.2) it is not itself a site of dispute, because the Framework requires load-bearing organizing vocabulary to receive full treatment regardless of whether it is contested.

### Tier 2 candidates (significant, but narrower in scope — single-strand, single-community, or single-topic)

**2.4 διάκονος (diakonos) — "deacon / servant"**
- **Preliminary definition:** A ministry office attested in both regions, but with different structural weight: a named, subordinate third tier of an explicit hierarchy in Strand A; a functioning but structurally under-described role in Strand B.
- **Strand attribution:** diakonos is cross-strand attested — the office itself is not strand-exclusive. Justin (P06), writing from Rome, is Strand B territory per Doc_01's own geography, and his deacons distributing the eucharist to the absent (First Apology 65) are real Strand B evidence for the office. What is strand-distinctive is not the existence of deacons but whether they appear as the explicit third tier of a named, three-part hierarchy (Strand A: Ignatius names bishop-presbyters-deacons together as a unit in every genuine letter but Romans) versus performing a charitable/liturgical function without any accompanying statement of a developed three-tier hierarchy (Strand B: Justin describes deacons acting, 1 Clement does not mention deacons distinctly from its bishop/presbyter discussion at all). Doc_05 should treat "diakonos as office" and "diakonos as named third tier of an explicit hierarchy" as two separable claims, not one.
- **Primary source anchors:** P03 (Ignatius, threefold hierarchy), P06 (Justin, distribution to the absent — Strand B; confirmed per round-2 review as the actual Registry row backing the Strand B claim, not an inheritance from Doc_01's general strand labeling alone — Doc_02 Section 4 explicitly attributes "distribution to the absent by deacons" to Justin's *First Apology* 65-67, which is P06's own licensed scope), Didache 15:1 (P01, transitional instruction naming both bishops and deacons together).
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes — Strand A's *hierarchy-tier* claim rests on Ignatius alone; the *bare-office* claim is better attested (Didache, Ignatius, Justin all independently).
- **Registry cross-reference:** P01, P03, P06.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 3/3 cited rows flagged for priority review.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** No — Tier 2 is adequate; this term's disputed content (the hierarchy-tier question) is derivative of the episkopos/presbyteros question rather than independently contested.

**2.5 πρεσβυτέριον (presbyterion) — "the presbytery / college of presbyters"**
- **Preliminary definition:** Ignatius's own term for the collective body of presbyters as an institution, used consistently alongside his single-*episkopos* language — i.e., his own texts assume both a single overseer and a plural presbyteral body simultaneously, which is itself worth preserving as a term rather than collapsing into 2.1–2.2.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand A only (Ignatius-specific coinage/usage within this source set).
- **Primary source anchors:** P03.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes, single-voice (Ignatius only) within this Native source set.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P03, S06, S09.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 2/3 cited rows flagged for priority review (P03, S06).
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** No.

**2.6 εὐχαριστία (eucharistia) — "thanksgiving / the eucharist"**
- **Preliminary definition:** The community's central shared meal-rite; attested with real variation in form and theological framing across the source set rather than one fixed practice.
- **Strand attribution:** Attested in both — Ignatius's "one eucharist" language (*Philadelphians* 4 — "Be careful, therefore, to use one eucharist... for there is one flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ, and one cup... and one altar, as there is one bishop"; *Smyrnaeans* 8 is a separately important passage — the earliest surviving use of "catholic church" and the source for eucharistic validity requiring the bishop's authorization — but does not itself contain the "one eucharist" phrase; cited here only for the bishop-presided/valid-eucharist point, not the "one eucharist" quotation, Strand A, P03), the Didache's cup-before-bread order with no institution narrative (P01, Doc_02 Section 4 — plausibly Syrian, single-community), and Justin's fuller Roman order (P06, bread/wine/water with thanksgiving, distribution to the absent).
- **Primary source anchors:** P01, P03, P06.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes — Doc_02 Section 4 already flags that Justin's description, despite being the fullest, should not be silently generalized into a network-wide template (Bradshaw's diversity thesis, adopted throughout Doc_02, versus Ferguson's common-core reading). Any Step 6 entry for this term must preserve that caution rather than default to Justin's account as if it were representative.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P01, P03, P06, S48, S49, S50.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 5/6 cited rows flagged for priority review (P01, P03, P06, S48, S49) — only S50 (Bradshaw) is unflagged, notable since Bradshaw's own diversity thesis is the interpretive stance this document adopts throughout.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** Yes — given how central and how genuinely contested (both in form and in representativeness) this term is, it should not be shortchanged as Tier 2, but this document flags it as a borderline Tier 1/2 case for Doc_04/Doc_05 to settle rather than deciding it here.

**2.7 The "Two Ways" (Didache 1–6)**
- **Preliminary definition:** A catechetical schema (a "way of life" versus a "way of death") structuring the Didache's opening instructional material, independently paralleled in the Latin *Doctrina Apostolorum* and Barnabas 18–20 (outside this world's own Native set, but relevant as evidence the schema circulated more widely than the Didache's own specific redaction of it).
- **Strand attribution:** Tied to the Didache specifically — per Doc_02 Section 1.1 and Open Item 1, this may reflect one (likely Syrian) community's own catechetical practice, not a network-wide instructional pattern. This document does not resolve that open item; it is carried forward, unresolved, into this term's own scope note.
- **Primary source anchors:** P01.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes, maximally — this entire term rests on the Didache alone within this world's Native set, and the Didache itself rests on a near-single-manuscript transmission chain (Doc_02 Section 1.1).
- **Registry cross-reference:** P01, S19, S20, S21; S41 cited separately as a **transmission-only, not content, reference** (see note below).
- **S41 citation corrected per Step 2 fix.** The first-finalized draft listed S41 (the Bryennios manuscript's 1873 discovery/1056 CE copying event) as an undifferentiated cross-reference alongside content-bearing rows (S19-S21), which mis-implies S41 supports a claim about the Two Ways schema's own content. S41 supports only the fact that this schema survives at all, via the same single-manuscript discovery chain documented in Doc_02 Section 1.1 -- it says nothing about the Two Ways material itself. Retained as a citation (the term's evidentiary base literally depends on that discovery event) but now explicitly separated from the content-bearing row set, mirroring the same boundary-citation discipline already applied to S16/S17 (2.9 above) and to Doc_02's own Eusebius citations.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 2/4 content-bearing rows flagged for priority review (P01 and S20 flagged; S19 and S21 not flagged). Direct check against the Registry confirms only two of the four content-bearing rows (P01, S20) actually carry the flag; S41 remains excluded from this count entirely as a transmission-only citation (see above), not because it would have been the missing third flag.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** No — Tier 2, specifically because its single-community grounding makes Tier 1 (network-wide organizing) treatment premature until Doc_05 addresses the Didache's representativeness question directly.

**2.8 προφήτης (prophetes) — "prophet" (itinerant)**
- **Preliminary definition:** A traveling, charismatic ministry figure the Didache addresses at length (chs. 11–13), including specific tests for distinguishing a genuine itinerant prophet from a false one — presented alongside apostles and teachers as an older ministry pattern the community is instructed to begin supplementing with locally-appointed bishops and deacons (Did. 15:1).
- **Strand attribution:** Didache-specific within this Native set; Doc_02 Section 3 reads Did. 15:1 as evidence of "an office structure still actively forming," with itinerant charismatic ministry not yet fully displaced by local office.
- **Primary source anchors:** P01.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes — same single-source dependency as 2.7.

**2.9 *ministrae* — Pliny's term for the two enslaved women he tortured for testimony (Latin, not this world's own self-designation)**
- **Preliminary definition:** An outside (Roman, non-Christian) administrative/legal term, not a term this world's own community used for itself — Pliny's own label for two enslaved women he identifies as holding some functional role in the community he is investigating, extracted from them under torture.
- **Strand attribution:** N/A — outside/etic term, not strand-specific; the two women themselves are unnamed and otherwise voiceless in the record (Doc_02 Section 7 names this directly as part of this world's structural source asymmetry).
- **Primary source anchors:** P07.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes, maximally — a single outside witness, and the underlying testimony was extracted under torture, a method ancient jurists themselves distrusted (Doc_02 Section 4). **Explicit distortion-risk flag carried forward for Step 6:** this term should never be silently translated as "deaconess" — Doc_02 Section 7 already flags that the "deaconess" title attributed to Ignatius's Antioch actually comes from the spurious Pseudo-Ignatian long recension, not his authentic letters, and no 1st–2nd century Asia Minor inscription names a woman deacon. Conflating *ministrae* with a later formal office would be exactly the kind of anachronistic misreading this candidate list exists to flag before Step 4 uses this vocabulary.
- **Boundary justification for S16/S17, added per round-1 review:** round-1 review correctly flagged that S16 and S17 are being used here to source a claim about the Pseudo-Ignatian long recension — a 4th-century text — and asked why this doesn't trigger the same boundary problem that got Henry Chadwick's 4th–6th century work Excluded from this Registry. The distinction, made explicit here rather than left implicit: Chadwick's Excluded material was being weighed as a **positive evidentiary source** for claims about this world's own period. S16/S17 are cited here only as a **negative, boundary-clarifying citation** — modern scholarship explaining why a later text's terminology must NOT be read backward into this world, not modern scholarship supplying content about this world. This is the same category of citation Doc_02 itself already used repeatedly (e.g., citing Eusebius, a 4th-century writer, solely to establish a transmission fact about Polycarp's authentic 2nd-century letter, not as evidence of 2nd-century practice in his own right — see Doc_02 Section 1.4's Transmission History discussion and Registry row P14). S16/S17 remain Native/licensed rows in their own right (Doc_02 Section 7, women/missing voices); this note exists so the distinction is auditable rather than assumed.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P07, S16, S17.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 3/3 cited rows flagged for priority review.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** No — Tier 2, but flagged as carrying an unusually high distortion risk for its tier, worth Step 6 attention out of proportion to its brief textual footprint.

**2.10 ἀγάπη (agape) as a proposed label for Pliny's "ordinary meal" — flagged as itself a contested attribution, not a settled term**
- **Preliminary definition:** Not Pliny's own word (he writes in Latin and does not use this Greek technical term at all) — "agape/love-feast" is one of three competing modern interpretive labels Doc_02 Section 4 considers for the second, ordinary meal Pliny describes the community reassembling for, alongside "this was the eucharist itself" and "this was a strategic split to evade Trajan's ban on political dining-clubs (*hetaeriae*)." Doc_02 explicitly leaves this unresolved.
- **Strand attribution:** N/A.
- **Primary source anchors:** P07 (for the underlying meal description Pliny reports) and P03 (**corrected this revision, process-integrity reopening** — Ignatius's *Smyrnaeans* 8 itself uses this exact term, ἀγάπην ποιεῖν, "to hold a love-feast," for a real communal practice requiring the bishop's own authorization; this is an inside, Native voice using the label directly, independent of and prior to any modern labeling exercise). The label therefore does have genuine primary-source anchoring somewhere in this world's own evidentiary base. What remains unresolved is not whether "agape" is an attested term at all — it now is settled that it is — but the narrower question of whether Ignatius's love-feast and Pliny's "ordinary meal" are the same practice, related practices in different communities, or unconnected. This entry exists to keep that narrower, still-live cross-identification question visible, not to flag the label itself as unanchored.
- **Author Gravity risk:** N/A in the usual sense — the risk here is a labeling/cross-identification risk, not a single-source dependency: whichever position a future document takes on whether Ignatius's love-feast and Pliny's meal are the same practice will shape how both are read, and none of the three options for Pliny's meal specifically is currently better than Contested.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P03, P07, S32, S33.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 2/4 cited rows flagged for priority review (P07 and P03; S32 and S33 unflagged).
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** No — but flagged as a genuinely live labeling/cross-identification dispute this document has not resolved and does not want a future step to resolve silently by default. (This entry does not name Step 6's own "CT/Contested Tradition" tag, since naming a later step's tagging apparatus inside a Step 3 document oversteps this step's own vocabulary. The underlying concern — don't let this label harden by default — stands on its own without borrowing Step 6's terms for it. The "agape" label is primary-source-anchored: Smyrnaeans 8 (the same passage discussing Ignatius's "one eucharist"/bishop-authorization clauses) also names the love-feast by this term — a citation Doc_02 Section 4 discloses directly (see Doc_02's Document Log, reopening entry). The companion Lexicon Candidate Index workbook's L10 row and Term-Registry Cross-Reference sheet reflect this — see Section 5 below.)

**2.11 βάπτισμα (baptisma) — "baptism" — added per round-1 review**
- **Preliminary definition:** The initiatory washing rite; the Didache's own instruction (ch. 7) specifies running ("living") water as preferred, with cold still water, then warm water, then triple pouring over the head as an explicit descending sequence of acceptable fallbacks, performed "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit," with fasting beforehand for both the baptizer, the baptized, and others able to do so.
- **Method note:** this document's own stated method — sweeping Doc_02 section by section for recurring, load-bearing terms — surfaces baptism. It is included here rather than folded silently into Section 3, because it traces to a Native Registry row with a specific, developed textual base.
- **Strand attribution:** Didache-specific within this document's actual developed evidentiary base (P01, ch. 7's specific fallback sequence). Justin's *First Apology* also describes a baptismal rite (ch. 61, illumination/regeneration language) and Ignatius's letters allude to baptism in passing, but **Doc_02's own text never developed either of those passages** (Doc_02 Section 4's Justin discussion is scoped specifically to chs. 65–67, the post-baptismal Sunday assembly, not ch. 61) — citing them here for baptism specifically would repeat exactly the S16/S17-style boundary problem round-1 review flagged elsewhere (see 2.9 above), citing a Registry row for a claim outside what Doc_02 actually developed from it. This document therefore anchors baptisma to the Didache alone and flags the Justin/Ignatius baptismal material as a genuine gap for Doc_05 to develop directly from the primary texts, rather than asserting network-wide baptismal practice on a citation base this document cannot actually support.
- **Primary source anchors:** P01 only, within this document's developed evidence.
- **Author Gravity risk:** Yes, maximally — same single-community dependency as 2.7 and 2.8, compounded by the fact that this document cannot yet say whether Rome or Antioch practiced anything similar.
- **Registry cross-reference:** P01.
- **Registry Flag Density:** 1/1 cited row flagged for priority review.
- **Requires full Tier 1 treatment at Step 6:** No — Tier 2, for the same single-community reason as 2.7/2.8, but flagged as needing a genuine Doc_05 primary-text lookup (Justin ch. 61, Ignatius's allusions) before Step 6, not just a citation of what Doc_02 already happened to develop.

## 3. Terms considered and explicitly excluded as modern analytical vocabulary

Per this step's own stated purpose — preventing modern analytical vocabulary from substituting for the world's own language before gravity discovery — the following candidates were considered, with the reasoning kept visible rather than silently dropped. Two dispositions are distinguished below, per round-1 review's correction: **confirmed exclusion** (a term is definitely a modern coinage/category with no native equivalent needed) versus **not yet confirmed** (this document could not establish native attestation one way or the other within its own evidentiary base, which is a different and more honest claim than exclusion).

- **"Monepiscopacy" / "monepiskopos."** This is a modern scholarly coinage describing the institutional pattern Ignatius promotes (a single bishop distinct from a plural presbyterate); Ignatius himself uses *episkopos* (singular office) and *presbyteros*/*presbyterion* (the plural body), but does not use a "mono-" prefixed term for his own program. Using "monepiscopacy" as if it were this world's own word would import exactly the kind of modern analytical shorthand this step exists to keep out of Step 4's vocabulary. The underlying institutional question is fully preserved above under 2.1–2.2 using the world's own two actual terms.
- **"Apostolic Fathers."** A modern (17th-century-onward) bibliographic category applied retroactively to a set of texts (including several of this world's own primary voices) that did not use this label for themselves or for each other; useful for modern scholarship's own organization (and used that way throughout Doc_02's Secondary Voices assessment) but not a candidate term for this world's own ecology.
- **"House church" — lookup performed, not merely deferred.** Doc_02's own text was searched directly (not from memory) for *oikos* or any household/domestic-meeting terminology attributed to one of the six primary voices themselves. The only occurrence of "oikos" anywhere in Doc_02 is in its Secondary Voices assessment, describing Roger Gehring as "a genuine oikos-structure specialist" (Doc_02 Section 2, S13) — that is a modern scholar's own specialization label, not a quotation of any primary voice using the term for itself. None of the six primary voices (Didache, 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Hermas, Justin) is quoted anywhere in Doc_02 using *oikos* or an equivalent as a technical self-designation for a meeting unit.
