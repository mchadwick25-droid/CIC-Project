## 1. Candidate Gravity Generation

Candidates were drawn only from elements recurring across multiple Doc_02 evidence streams (Primary Voices, Institutional Evidence, Liturgical Evidence, Ordinary Participant/Outside Witnesses, Secondary Scholarship) or from Doc_02's own Attention Presence/Absence patterns (Sections 3, 7, 8, 9), cross-checked against Doc_01's Preliminary Forces Identification (Section 7) and Doc_03's Tier 1/Author-Gravity-flagged terms. Two candidates considered before testing were **not advanced**, with reasons stated rather than silently dropped:

- **Source Asymmetry / elite-literate-voice monopoly** (Doc_02 Sections 7–8). This is a real, load-bearing fact about *what this document's evidence can support*, not a force the ancient ecology itself organized around. Treating an evidentiary limitation as if it were a historical gravity would misuse the concept the Framework defines — a gravity is something multiple dimensions of the ecology depended on and were shaped by, not a property of the modern record. This is carried forward as a standing constraint on every gravity tested below (see each candidate's Confidence note), not tested as its own candidate.
- **"Apostolic succession" as a distinct gravity from Authority Consolidation (G01 below).** Considered and folded into G01 rather than tested separately: no Native source in this world's Registry develops a doctrine of formal succession distinct from the live episkopos/presbyteros organizational question itself (formal succession theology is Irenaeus/Tertullian-era material, per Doc_01's own closing-boundary discussion — treating it as already present here would import a later category backward, exactly what Doc_03 Section 3 already declined to do for "monepiscopacy").

Seven candidates were generated and tested. One (G06) was tested and found not to reach gravity status; results are reported rather than omitted, per this step's own discipline against a clean-looking list that never shows its work.

## 2. Candidates and Six-Test Results

### G01 — Authority Consolidation (the episkopos/presbyteros/diakonos question)

**Generated from:** Primary Voices (1 Clement chs. 42/44; Ignatius's threefold program; Polycarp's self-designation as presbyter; Hermas's plural presiding presbyters; Justin's deacons); Institutional Evidence (Doc_02 Section 3, Didache 15:1's transition from itinerant ministry to local office); Doc_01's own Strand Determination, built directly on this axis; Doc_03's Tier 1 terms 2.1–2.2 (episkopos, presbyteros).

**Author Gravity risk (flagged at generation):** Yes. The Strand A (monarchical) resolution rests overwhelmingly on Ignatius alone, per Doc_02 Section 1.3's own representativeness caveat — carried forward here rather than re-litigated.

- **Repetition:** Passes strongly. Recurs across every primary voice in the Registry and across Doc_02's dedicated Institutional Evidence section.
- **Dependency:** Passes strongly. Liturgical presidency (G07), disciplinary authority (1 Clement's whole Corinthian intervention), and boundary-maintenance rhetoric (G05) all depend on how this question resolves.
- **Formation:** Passes. Directly shapes who is obeyed, who administers rites, how disputes are resolved.
- **Explanatory:** Passes strongly. Explains why the primary source corpus itself consists largely of letters about authority disputes, and connects directly to Doc_01 Section 7's "what it was responding to" (the felt need to formalize authority once eyewitness generation recedes).
- **Persistence:** Passes, with a caveat carried forward rather than smoothed: visible in both regions across the whole window, but its *specific shape* changes across that same span (1 Clement's plural-college picture is earlier than Ignatius's monarchical picture), which is itself part of what makes this a live, unresolved organizing question rather than a settled one.
- **Interaction:** See Section 4 (Interaction Matrix) — demonstrated relationships with every other classified candidate.

**Confidence supporting:** Split. That authority organization was an actively contested, live question — attested independently across multiple voices — is itself Widely Accepted. The *specific* Strand A claim (a secured monarchical office) is Contested, per Doc_01/02.

**Cross-strand status (Article 21):** The *force* (authority requires active organizing) is cross-strand confirmed. Its *resolution* is strand-bound: Strand A resolves toward a single office, Strand B toward a plural, interchangeable college. Both are recorded, neither is collapsed into the other.

**Forces test (Article 22):** Intensifies under state pressure — Ignatius explicitly deploys his own impending martyrdom as an argument *for* his authority program, and the loss-of-eyewitness-generation force named in Doc_01 Section 7 is essentially answered directly by this gravity.

**Classification: Supporting — reclassified per round-1 review (originally drafted Primary).**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, revised:** the first draft classified this Primary while flagging that Strand A's specific monarchical content rests only on Contested confidence — treating disclosure as a substitute for reclassification. Round-1 review correctly identified this as a rule violation: the Framework's own text is explicit that a candidate resting on sub-Documented/Widely-Accepted evidence "should be classified Supporting or Tensional regardless of how strongly it appears to organize the ecology... not resolved by upgrading" — and "not resolved by upgrading" cannot be read to also permit "not resolved by downgrading, just flagged instead." On correction, this document also could not identify a separable, non-trivial claim for G01 that survives once the Contested content is set aside: "some leadership existed" is nearly gravity-vacuous, while the specific content that actually does this gravity's interaction and dependency work (the Strand A/B differentiation itself, per Doc_03's own identification of episkopos/presbyteros as the central strand-differentiating vocabulary) is exactly the Contested part. Unlike G03 below, there is no independently-Documented base claim to fall back on here. **Reclassified Supporting**, meaning: it organizes a significant portion of the ecology (the institutional-office dimension) and functions within the context this document's Primary gravities (G02, G07) establish, but does not clear the confidence bar Primary requires at the level of content that actually matters.

### G02 — Translocal Correspondence Network

**Generated from:** 1 Clement's Rome-to-Corinth intervention; Ignatius's entire seven-letter corpus, written across a guarded journey; Polycarp's own letter (ch. 13) reporting he forwarded Ignatius's letters at the Philippians' request — three independent primary voices/events, not one.

**Author Gravity risk:** No significant single-voice dependency — this is the one candidate independently attested by voices from *both* strands behaving the same way (Rome via 1 Clement; Antioch/Asia Minor via Ignatius and Polycarp).

- **Repetition:** Passes strongly across both regions.
- **Dependency:** Passes strongly. G01's translocal authority claims are *exercised through* this network; doctrinal/memory consolidation depends on letters actually circulating; the shape of Doc_02's own primary-voice set is largely explainable by which texts entered and survived this network.
- **Formation:** Passes. Produces a felt sense of belonging to something larger than the local assembly.
- **Explanatory:** Passes strongly — arguably explains the shape of Doc_02 itself (Author Gravity Assessment Section 1's own survivorship discussion in Doc_02 Section 9).
- **Persistence:** Passes across all three named regions (Rome, Antioch, Smyrna).
- **Interaction:** See Section 4.

**Confidence supporting:** Widely Accepted / Documented that these letters exist and were exchanged this way — the strongest confidence footing of any candidate here.

**Cross-strand status:** Confirmed cross-strand — independently attested by both Strand A and Strand B texts.

**Forces test:** Intensifies under pressure rather than fracturing — Ignatius relies on the network precisely because he is under guard; martyr-narrative transmission (per Doc_01's martyr-cult disclosure) travels through this same channel.

**Classification:** **Primary.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Clean — no material divergence between organizing strength and evidential confidence. Both are high.

### G03 — State Pressure / Legal Precarity

**Generated from:** Pliny's Letters 10.96–97 (P07), Tacitus (P08), Suetonius (P09), Lucian's Peregrinus (P10, prisoner-visiting); Ignatius's own imprisonment framing throughout his corpus; Doc_02 Section 3's institutional-silence finding and Section 9's "silences reveal" argument.

**Author Gravity risk:** Moderate. Pliny is the clearest single direct description of state action, but Tacitus, Suetonius, and Ignatius's own internal evidence independently corroborate the broader pattern, reducing (not eliminating) single-source dependency.

- **Repetition:** Passes — four separate outside/hostile witnesses plus Ignatius's own corpus.
- **Dependency:** Passes strongly. G04 (martyrdom-meaning) is essentially the internal response to this force; G01's urgency partly depends on it; institutional silence (Doc_02 Section 3) is best explained by it.
- **Formation:** Passes — shapes risk calculus, willingness to gather, willingness to recant or not.
- **Explanatory:** Passes strongly.
- **Persistence:** Passes, **with an honest caveat this document does not smooth over**: the specific episodes evidenced (Nero/Rome c. 64 CE; Pliny/Bithynia-Pontus c. 111–113 CE; Ignatius's own arrest c. 107–130s depending on the dating camp) are geographically and temporally scattered, not one continuous, systematic policy. Doc_02 itself already characterizes this as "local, unpredictable legal exposure," not empire-wide system. This document treats the *underlying vulnerability* as persistent even though the *specific incidents* are intermittent — a distinction Doc_08's Forces Document should preserve rather than collapse into "constant persecution."
- **Interaction:** See Section 4.

**Confidence supporting:** Split. Documented that Pliny's letter is authentic and describes real legal action. Contested on further interpretive layers (whether a discrete, named persecution event occurred per the Shaw/Jones Tacitus debate; the Chrestus identification in Suetonius).

**Cross-strand status, corrected per round-3 review:** cross-strand confirmed via **Strand B** (Rome — Tacitus/Nero) and **Strand A** (Antioch/Asia Minor — Ignatius's own arrest), a genuine two-strand attestation on its own terms. Pliny's Bithynia-Pontus material is additional corroborating evidence that the underlying phenomenon was not confined to either strand's home territory, but per Article 21 it does not itself count toward the two-strand test — it is a third data point strengthening the general pattern, not a third strand.

**Forces test:** This candidate *is* one of the primary forces named in Doc_01 Section 7 and Doc_02 Section 9; its own "test" is better read as a question of character (systematic vs. intermittent) than of how it responds to some other force. Answer: intermittent, locally variable, not a straight-line escalation across the window.

**Classification: Supporting — reclassified per round-2 review (originally retained at Primary after round-1).**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, reclassified per round-2 review:** round-1 review retained G03 at Primary on the argument that its organizing work depends on a Documented base fact (Pliny's letter alone proves real legal jeopardy existed in one episode) rather than the Contested interpretive layer (named-event status, the Chrestus identification), and explicitly flagged this distinction for round-2 to test against G01's own reclassification. Round-2 review found the distinction does not survive scrutiny: the six-test results credited to G03 above — Dependency ("institutional silence, network reliance under guard"), Explanatory power, and the cross-strand Persistence claim across "both regions" — are not actually earned by the narrow Documented core (one governor, one province, one episode, c.111-113 CE) standing alone. They depend on the generalized, cross-episode reading that treats Nero (c.64 CE, Rome), Pliny (c.111-113 CE, Bithynia-Pontus), and Ignatius's own arrest (c.107-130s, Antioch/Asia Minor, itself of disputed date) as evidence of one standing, persistent condition of legal precarity — and that synthesis, not the Documented core alone, is exactly the Contested layer this document already discloses in its own Persistence-test caveat ("local, unpredictable legal exposure," not one continuous policy). Applying the identical test used for G01: set aside the Contested content (the cross-episode synthesis) and ask what survives. What's left — "a Roman governor in one province prosecuted some Christians around 111-113 CE" — does not by itself explain institutional silence in Rome, Antioch, or Smyrna; does not by itself drive G04 (built entirely from Ignatius and Polycarp, not Pliny); and does not by itself justify Persistence across "both regions" as claimed. Every piece of interaction, dependency, and explanatory work credited to G03 is done by the generalized reading — exactly the Contested layer, just as with G01. **Reclassified Supporting**, on the same footing as G01: it organizes a real portion of the ecology (the state-pressure dimension, and specifically the Documented Pliny episode itself) and functions within the context this document's remaining Primary gravities (G02, G07) establish, but the broader, persistent-organizing-force claim that would justify Primary does not clear the confidence bar once the Contested cross-episode synthesis is set aside.

### G04 — Martyrdom as Formation-Shaping Meaning-Response

**Generated from:** Doc_01 Section 9's expanded disclosure (Martyrdom of Polycarp ch. 18, "more precious than the finest jewels"/*dies natalis*; Ignatius, Romans 4, "food for wild beasts"); Ignatius's own sustained rhetorical self-presentation throughout his corpus.

**Author Gravity risk:** High, flagged explicitly at generation. Within this world's Native evidentiary base, this rests on exactly two data points — Ignatius's own voice and the Martyrdom of Polycarp — both Strand A.

- **Repetition:** Weak relative to G01–G03. Two data points, not the broad multi-stream recurrence the stronger candidates show.
- **Dependency:** Real but narrower than it first appears: Ignatius uses his own impending martyrdom to reinforce his authority claims (G01) and his anti-docetic argument (G05), so this gravity's dependency runs mostly *into* those two rather than the reverse. The liturgical commemoration this eventually produces (*dies natalis* as a feast day) is genuine but belongs more to what this world hands off to World #2/#7 than to something this world's own liturgical practice (G07) already organizes around — Doc_01's own closing-boundary logic applies here too.
- **Formation:** **Clearly** formation-shaping *for the specific individuals in the record* (Ignatius, Polycarp); far less certain for the ordinary, non-elite, non-imminently-threatened member, exactly the source-asymmetry limitation named in Section 1 above.
- **Explanatory:** Explains the rhetorical shape of Ignatius's own letters well; does not explain Rome/Strand B material at all — 1 Clement and Hermas show no comparable martyr-piety content in Doc_02's own treatment.
- **Persistence:** Fails to clear a broad bar — visible in two individuals' own experience, not "across regions, communities, and evidence streams."
- **Interaction:** See Section 4.

**Confidence supporting:** Contested/Inferential-Thin specifically on how *representative* this was of ordinary member experience — which is exactly Doc_01's own disclosure caveat, carried forward rather than softened here.

**Cross-strand status:** Strand-bound. Both data points are Strand A; no Strand B equivalent is developed anywhere in Doc_02.

**Forces test:** This gravity *is* the meaning-making response to G03 — it intensifies in direct proportion to state pressure and does not appear to operate independently of it in this world's own evidence.

**Classification:** **Supporting** (not Primary, despite the vividness of its textual evidence).

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** This is the clearest case in this document of the Cross-Check doing its job. G04's two data points are dramatic, quotable, and easy to over-read as organizing the whole world's piety — but Repetition and Persistence are both thin, representativeness confidence is Contested/Inferential-Thin, and the gravity is Strand-bound. Classifying this Primary on the strength of how vivid it reads would be exactly the failure mode the Cross-Check exists to prevent. It is real, it is Supporting, and its true scope is narrower than its rhetorical volume suggests.

**Why Supporting rather than Tensional — reusable criterion, tested against G01, G03, G04, and G05:** the criterion does not track *whether something remains unresolved* — every non-Primary gravity here has some unresolved dimension. It tracks **what level of the claim is unresolved, and whether that level's own six-test results are themselves confident or merely plausible.** For G01 and G03, the coarse-grained claim (authority had to be actively organized at all; real legal jeopardy existed and shaped behavior) is itself Widely Accepted or Documented; what remains unresolved is only the fine-grained resolution. For G04, the coarse-grained claim (martyrdom-meaning was broadly formation-shaping across this world's communities) is Contested/Inferential-Thin — but the narrower claim, that it shaped these two specific people's own self-presentation, is not merely asserted as a fallback: G04's own **Formation test result reads "Clearly formation-shaping for the specific individuals in the record"** — "Clearly," not "plausibly." That narrow claim clears Documented-level confidence on its own terms, which is what earns G04 its Supporting classification. **G05 does not have an analogous narrow claim that clears the same bar, and this is demonstrable from its own Formation-test result rather than merely asserted:** G05's Formation test reads **"Plausible for Ignatius's own direct addressees; unestablished more broadly"** — "Plausible," not "Clearly." Even at its narrowest possible scope (Ignatius's own direct addressees, the smallest population G04's narrow claim is tested against), G05's own six-test writeup hedges its Formation result at Contested/Dominant-Modern-Reconstruction confidence rather than Documented. There is no level of grain, coarse or fine, at which G05 clears the bar G04 clears at its narrow end. That is the reusable distinction, grounded in the two candidates' own stated test results rather than in a restated assertion: Supporting requires a genuine claim, at some level of grain, whose own six-test language reaches Documented/Widely Accepted confidence and does real organizing work at that level. Tensional means no level of the claim — checked against its own stated test results, not just against the coarse claim — clears that bar.

### G05 — Boundary-Drawing Against Contemporary Rival Movements (anti-docetic polemic)

**Generated from:** Ignatius's anti-docetic argument (present across several of his letters); Doc_01 Section 8.3's disclosure that Marcionite, Valentinian/Gnostic, and Montanist movements were live, contemporary, geographically overlapping rivals, not later or external threats.

**Author Gravity risk:** High, flagged at generation. Within Doc_02's own Native evidence streams, this candidate is substantively developed by exactly one voice (Ignatius). Doc_01 Section 8.3 is comparative/contextual analysis, not itself a Doc_02 evidence stream with independent primary-voice support — this document does not let Doc_01's synthesis substitute for the Repetition test's actual requirement (recurrence across Doc_02's own evidence streams).

- **Repetition:** Weak. Essentially one voice within the Native Registry.
- **Dependency:** Some — Ignatius ties his "obey the bishop" program directly to anti-docetic argument (G01 interaction), but this dependency runs mostly in one direction.
- **Formation:** Plausible for Ignatius's own direct addressees; unestablished more broadly.
- **Explanatory:** Explains Ignatius's own rhetoric; does not explain Rome/Strand B material, which shows no comparable content in Doc_02's treatment.
- **Persistence:** Fails broadly — not attested outside Ignatius within the Native set.
- **Interaction:** See Section 4.

**Confidence supporting:** Contested/Inferential-Thin for representativeness beyond Ignatius's own addressees.

**Cross-strand status:** Strand-bound (Strand A only).

**Forces test:** Plausibly connects to the same eyewitness-generation-loss force behind G01 (doctrinal drift anxiety once living apostolic testimony recedes) — but this specific causal link is this document's own inference, not something Doc_02 independently traces, and is flagged as such rather than presented as established.

**Classification:** **Tensional.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, strengthened per round-4 review:** the prior draft asserted G05 fails the Supporting/Tensional test "at every level" without pointing to textual grounding as specific as the test given to G04. Grounded now: G05's own **Formation test result reads "Plausible... unestablished,"** never rising to the "Clearly" language G04's Formation result uses even at its narrowest scope (Ignatius's own direct addressees — the same population). Thin, single-voice evidence prevents Primary or confident Supporting classification at any grain; the underlying phenomenon (a real, live, unsettled boundary against contemporary rivals) is exactly what a Tensional gravity is for — a persistent, unresolved pressure that keeps this world's ecology from reading as already-settled orthodoxy, without claiming the organizing weight Primary or Supporting would imply. See G04's entry for the full reusable Supporting/Tensional criterion this classification is tested against.

### G07 — Liturgical Practice (Eucharist) as Site of Variation and Convergence

**Generated from:** Didache 9–10 (P01), Ignatius *Smyrnaeans* 8 (P03), Justin *First Apology* 65–67 (P06) — three independent primary voices; Doc_02 Section 4 (a full dedicated Liturgical Evidence section); Doc_03's own borderline-Tier-1 eucharistia entry.

**Author Gravity risk:** Moderate. Three independent voices reduce single-source risk, though Justin's fuller description carries its own well-flagged over-generalization risk (Bradshaw vs. Ferguson).

- **Repetition:** Passes strongly — three voices, cross-regional (Syria-adjacent Didache, Antioch Ignatius, Rome Justin).
- **Dependency:** Passes. Communal boundary-marking depends on it (Ignatius's "one eucharist," per Doc_03's own corrected citation to Philadelphians 4, is simultaneously a G01 and G05 move); basic worship-gathering structure depends on it.
- **Formation:** Passes strongly — the central recurring communal ritual.
- **Explanatory:** Passes strongly — explains real variation in the evidence itself (cup-before-bread vs. institution-narrative forms) and explains why Doc_02 needed an explicit diversity-first calibration in the first place.
- **Persistence:** Passes across all three regions.
- **Interaction:** See Section 4 — **now thinner than earlier drafts stated, per round-4 review's full re-derivation; see that section for the honest accounting.**

**Confidence supporting:** Widely Accepted that these three practices/texts exist and differ from one another; Contested on whether any one is representative or network-wide (the same caution Doc_02/Doc_03 already apply throughout).

**Cross-strand status:** Confirmed cross-strand — independently attested in Strand A (Ignatius) and Strand B (Justin), plus the Didache's separate single-community witness.

**Forces test:** Connects to G01 — Ignatius's bishop-presided eucharist is explicitly an authority-consolidation move, not a purely devotional one. No demonstrated connection to G03 in this world's own developed evidence (see Section 4).

**Classification:** **Primary.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, strengthened per round-3 review, one soft spot acknowledged per round-4 review:** applying the set-aside-the-Contested-layer test directly: the Documented content (a shared eucharist ritual independently attested, in related but non-identical forms, by three geographically-separated voices across this world's whole window) is what earns Dependency/Explanatory/Formation — not the Contested "which form is representative" layer. Unlike G01/G03, the Contested content here is not what the interaction/dependency work depends on. **Acknowledged per round-4 review:** the Forces-test/Dependency link to G01 ("Ignatius's bishop-presided eucharist is explicitly an authority-consolidation move") itself imports one interpretive step — that Ignatius's own linkage of eucharist to episcopal authority is read as a general claim about the *practice*, not only about Ignatius's own rhetoric — which this document does not bracket with the same explicit care it applies elsewhere (e.g., G03's Persistence caveat). This is a minor, acknowledged softness, not a rule violation: the underlying textual fact (Ignatius does link the two in his own letters) is itself Documented, and the interpretive step is small and stated here rather than hidden. It does not disturb the Primary classification, which rests on the broader existence/variation claim, not this one dependency link.
