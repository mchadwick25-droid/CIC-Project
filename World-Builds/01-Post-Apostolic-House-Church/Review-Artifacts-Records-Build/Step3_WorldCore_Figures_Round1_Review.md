## Substantial findings

### Finding 1 — SUBSTANTIAL. `world_core.cautions` item 7 contains build-process language: "flagged as a load-bearing single-source dependency."

Cautions is one of the four compiled, spoken fields the no-build-attribution rule protects. Item 7 reads: "EGYPT EXCLUDED: Alexandria and Egypt are outside this world (a boundary resting substantially on one papyrological argument, **flagged as** a load-bearing single-source dependency)." "Flagged" has no in-world agent — the flagging is an act of this build (Doc_01 §3's round-1 review added the flag; Doc_02 §10 sharpened it). The mechanical gate passes (its patterns are ISO dates, "ruled by," and stale-status markers — confirmed by direct read of `gates.py` and by re-running the battery), but this is exactly the subtler process-language class the mechanical gate cannot see. Fix is one clause: state the epistemic fact without the process verb, e.g. "...outside this world — a boundary resting substantially on a single papyrological argument, a known single-source dependency." No other process language was found in the four fields on close reading (see Verified clean, item 2).

### Finding 2 — SUBSTANTIAL. `pahc.figure.ministrae`: an untraceable Latin quotation in the locus, and an in-world name tag that contradicts Doc_03's own classification of the term.

**(a) Quote discipline.** The sources locus reads `10.96 (the two ancillae 'quae ministrae dicebantur')`. That quoted Latin appears nowhere in Doc_02 (whose wording is "two enslaved women, called *ministrae*" — §4, §7), nowhere in Doc_03 §2.9, nowhere in the Pliny source record, and nowhere else in the project (grep of `World-Builds/` and `records/`). Pliny is unvendored, and `pahc.source.pliny-letters`'s own divergence_note — added at the step-2 review's Finding 5 fix — makes the row **paraphrase-only** until an edition is vendored. The Latin happens to be correct against the real text of Ep. 10.96, but nothing in this corpus can verify it, and quoting an unvendored source's wording on a `verified-via-authority` record whose named authority (Doc_02) does not contain that wording is the exact discipline the row exists to enforce. Fix: locus "10.96" plain, with the title carried in prose per Doc_02's wording.

**(b) Names tag vs. Doc_03.** The names[] entry "the two servants who were called ministrae" is tagged **in-world**. Doc_03 §2.9's approved heading is "*ministrae* — Pliny's term for the two enslaved women he tortured for testimony **(Latin, not this world's own self-designation)**," and its definition calls it "an outside (Roman, non-Christian) administrative/legal term, not a term this world's own community used for itself"; Doc_06 carries the same etic classification into the lexicon. The record body's "women serving under a recognized designation" leans the same overclaiming direction (Pliny's *dicebantur* does not establish whose designation it was — a point the body's own "(deacons? servers? the Latin gloss of a Greek title?)" hedge gets right two sentences later). Fix: retag the Pliny-derived name scholarly (or reword the in-world entry to something the community itself could own), and align "recognized designation" with Doc_03's "some functional role, per Pliny's label." The record's core discipline — `narratable: false`, existence-only anchor — is right and should not change.

### Finding 3 — SUBSTANTIAL. The world_core ABSENT STORIES section silently drops two of Doc_09 §4's twelve items that matter for the story set it claims to govern.

The body presents itself as "Doc_09 SS4's required question, carried here as **the standing answer for the new-regime story set**." Mapped item-by-item against Doc_09 §4's twelve: world_core items 1–9 trace cleanly to Doc_09 items 1, 4, 9(+4), 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12 (verified line-by-line; the Tavia/Gavia and wife-of-Epitropus names in item 4 were additionally verified present in the vendored anf01 at Smyrnaeans 13 and To Polycarp 8). Dropped without comment:

- **Doc_09 item 2** — G04 and G05 are Strand-A-only and severely thin *even within Strand A* (two data points; one voice), and no third story may be added to thicken them.
- **Doc_09 item 3** — this world's narratively vivid material is overwhelmingly Strand A, and the inventory must not smooth that imbalance (Doc_09: "it cannot manufacture narrative vividness Strand B's own evidence does not supply").

Both are load-bearing honesty constraints on exactly the story records this section says it stands answer for — a story-set builder working from this list alone would not know the imbalance disciplines exist. Doc_09 item 8 (the two unregistered-passage/P03 registry judgments) is also dropped; that one is process housekeeping and its omission is defensible, but the drop should be stated (one clause), not silent, since the body otherwise implies completeness. Fix: add the two imbalance items (they need no process language — both are statable as facts about the record) and a one-line note on what was deliberately not carried.

### Finding 4 — SUBSTANTIAL (narrow). `pahc.figure.papias` dates: "conventionally active c. 95-120" traces to nothing in this corpus and settles a genuinely disputed range.

Papias appears **nowhere in Doc_01–Doc_09** (grep of all prior-build finals) — this is the record set's one wholly new figure. Everything else in the record verifies cleanly against the vendored texts (see Verified clean, item 6): the five books, the title *Expositions of Oracles of the Lord*, Hierapolis, the Irenaeus/Eusebius transmission, and the living-voice quotation verbatim in npnf201 HE III.39. But "conventionally active c. 95-120" is verifiable in neither the vendored Eusebius (who gives no such range) nor any approved document, and it quietly leans one way in a live dispute: current scholarship splits between a c. 110 and a c. 130 dating for the *Expositions* (the older Eusebian-tradition placement vs. the later redating), which is precisely the kind of spread this record set's own dates fields elsewhere refuse to collapse (Ignatius's note even states the principle: "collapsing them would settle in a data field the dispute every telling must carry"). Fix: either carry the spread ("his writing is dated anywhere from c. 110 to c. 130; only fragments survive") or hedge to what the vendored carrier supports (a figure of the generation of Polycarp and Ignatius, per Eusebius's own placement) — and in either case the dates claim needs a traceable basis.

## Cosmetic findings

### Finding 5 — COSMETIC. Hermas: "sold to a woman named Rhoda" vs. the vendored edition's "Rhode."

The claim itself is verified: anf02 Vision 1 opens "The master who brought me up, sold me to one **Rhode** in Rome" (with the ANF's own footnote on variants — Vatican "Rada"; the name Rhode occurs in Acts 12:13). "Rhoda" is the standard scholarly spelling, but on a `verified-direct` row the body should either match the vendored spelling or note the convention, per the same reader-expectation rule the step-2 review applied to the "finest jewels" gloss (its Finding 3).

### Finding 6 — COSMETIC. Absent Stories item 5 overclaims production where Doc_09 claims survival.

World_core: "this world **produced no** sayings-collection genre." Doc_09 (§1 note and §4 item 6): "no such collected-narrative genre **survives** in this world's own Native evidentiary base." Survival-vs-production is a distinction this build enforces elsewhere (Doc_02 §9's survivorship-pattern discussion is explicitly Inferential-Thin on why things are absent). One verb fixes it.

### Finding 7 — COSMETIC. Present-tense references to record types that do not yet exist.

`records/pahc/` contains only source, search_record, figure, world_core. Yet: ministrae body — "the **term record carries** that contest"; pliny body — "the use discipline stated... on **pahc.limit/contested records** binds every narration"; ignatius body — "at the strength **the story records set**." All three should be future-tensed the way the world_core body already does it ("built at the contested-claims step"). Trailing-body text, so no gate or compile impact — but a reviewer or builder following these pointers today finds nothing.

### Finding 8 — COSMETIC. Etic figures' life dates are standard but corpus-untraceable.

Pliny "c. 61-c. 113" and Tacitus "c. 56-c. 120" appear in no approved document (the docs date only the letters/passage: 10.96–97 c. 111–113; Annals 15.44 written c. 116 — both correctly carried) and no vendored text. Both are uncontroversial reference-work facts — same class as the step-2 review's "publication years, noted without prejudice" — but unlike step 2, here they sit in a dates field rather than an edition field; a "per standard reference chronology" hedge would close it.

### Finding 9 — COSMETIC. Locus fields carrying commentary; one missing world_core source row.

World_core and network locus values editorialize beyond location ("whole letter (Strand B anchor)", "the letter as a network event", "all seven letters (Strand A anchor; three-way dating split applies)"). Harmless and even useful, but it stretches the locus's locate-function; the schema tolerates it (free string), so this is a convention note only. Related: the horizon narrates Smyrna's forwarding of Ignatius's letters "at another church's asking" — a Polycarp *Philippians* 13 fact — but `pahc.source.polycarp-philippians` is not among the world_core's sources[]; the five listed rows otherwise cover the horizon/formation_logic content.

### Finding 10 — COSMETIC. Horizon: "The 70 CE opening is a formal floor" is faintly process-flavored.

"Formal floor" describes the build's constitutional scoping decision (Doc_01 §2.1's "documented floor... inherited/structural") from the build's vantage. The sentence's substance — the transition firming up gradually across c. 64–100 rather than at any single date — is exactly Doc_01 §2.1's disposition and should stay; only the word "formal" imports the process register. Borderline; noted for the same close-reading pass Finding 1 requires anyway.
