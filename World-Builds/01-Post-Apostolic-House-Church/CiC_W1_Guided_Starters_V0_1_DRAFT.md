# Guided Starters — The House-Churches (Post-Apostolic/Sub-Apostolic World) (DRAFT V0.1)

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Mark's review. Not deployed.
**World:** Post-Apostolic/Sub-Apostolic House-Church Christianity ("the House-Churches"), c. 70–200 CE — Antioch/Syria, Asia Minor, Rome. Representative: Chloe, a household leader whose documented function is combined with a named woman's pastoral-instructional and cross-community letter-transmission role (Grapte, per Hermas *Vision* 2.4.3), community "we"-voice.
**Purpose:** Entry points for participants who don't know what to ask. Questions open doors; they never walk the participant through them (Encounter Over Persuasion applies to curricula).
**Grounding:** Every topic below traces to this world's approved build documents (Doc_01 World Identification, Doc_04 Gravity Discovery, Doc_06 Interpretive Lexicon, Doc_07 Integrated Ecology Analysis, Doc_08 Forces Document, Doc_09 Story Inventory + Absent Stories) and the Chloe artifacts (World Capsule Core, Permanent Prompt). Nothing is invented.

**Read this first — the caveat that governs the whole list.** Per `CiC_World_Coverage_Cards_V0_1.md`, starter questions are **largely role-generic**: the Representative handles per-world variation, and the live calibration proved a generic question meeting a thin record produces an honest calibrated answer rather than a failure. The premise that starters must be authored per world was cancelled. So the openers below are **illustrative routing seeds, not per-world authored gates** — they show the *kinds* of doors this world opens especially well and where its honest silences lie, to help a participant who doesn't yet know what to ask. A participant is never confined to them, and Chloe — who speaks, as this world's own voice does, in the first-person plural of a whole gathered people rather than as one witness recalling her own life — will meet a question this list never anticipated on the same terms.

## First Visit (start here)

**The letter at the door** — This world's unity ran on almost nothing but correspondence: a letter arriving from another *ekklesia* was never mere news, it was proof the community was larger than the room it gathered in — this is this world's single strongest, cross-strand-confirmed gravity. [Doc_04 G02 (Primary); Doc_01 §3; Capsule "What Organizes Everything"]
*Opening question:* "How did you even know what other churches, far away, were doing or believing, without any fast way to travel or communicate?"
Follow-ups: "Who actually carried the letters?" · "Did you trust a letter the moment it arrived, or did you have to check where it came from first?" · "What happened if two letters from two different churches disagreed?"

**The table** — Whatever else stayed unsettled, this world returned again and again to giving thanks over bread and cup, and holding to one's own table — not a rival's — was at once an act of worship and an act of belonging. [Doc_04 G07 (Primary); Doc_06 lex004 eucharistia; Capsule "What Organizes Everything"]
*Opening question:* "What actually happened when you all gathered to eat together? Walk me through it."
Follow-ups: "Who was allowed to preside — was that settled everywhere?" · "Was it the same meal every time, everywhere?" · "What if someone showed up who wasn't supposed to be there?"

**The path to the water** — Before baptism, a person was taught the Two Ways — a way of life and a way of death — then baptized, with running water preferred but real fallbacks allowed, after a short fast. [Doc_09 story009; Doc_06 lex007 Two Ways, lex011 baptisma]
*Opening question:* "What did you have to do, or learn, before you could be baptized?"
Follow-ups: "What if there was no river or clean running water nearby?" · "Was the teaching the same everywhere, or did it depend who was teaching you?" · "Did you fast before it? Why?"

**Who leads here?** — This world never settled whether one bishop or a council of presbyters carries a community's continuity — Antioch and Asia Minor pressed toward a single office, Rome held a plural, interchangeable college, and this world knew both from real contact. [Doc_04 G01 (Supporting); Doc_06 lex001 episkopos, lex002 presbyteros; Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "Who was actually in charge of your church — was there one leader, like a pastor, or something more like a board?"
Follow-ups: "Did every church do it the same way?" · "What happened when the two patterns came into contact with each other?" · "How did anyone become a leader in the first place?"

**Widows, orphans, and the stranger at the door** — Caring for those with no one else to provide for them — instructing widows, gathering what could be spared for orphans and prisoners — is this world's most concretely attested ongoing work, carried out by named, plural roles rather than a single office. [Doc_09 story007, Hermas *Vision* 2.4.3; Doc_04 G01; Doc_06 lex005 diakonos]
*Opening question:* "Who took care of the poor and vulnerable people in your community — the widows, the orphans, people with nowhere else to turn?"
Follow-ups: "Was that one person's job, or everyone's?" · "How did you know who actually needed help?" · "What did you do for someone in prison?"

**The danger of the Name** — A name and an accusation could be brought against any household on any ordinary day, without warning and without becoming a settled, systematic policy — a real, felt, unpredictable exposure rather than constant terror. [Doc_04 G03 (Supporting); Doc_09 story004, Pliny *Letters* 10.96–97; Capsule "What This World Is Responding To"]
*Opening question:* "Was it actually dangerous to be a Christian back then, day to day, or is that exaggerated?"
Follow-ups: "What would actually get someone in trouble?" · "Did you keep records, meeting rolls, anything like that?" · "Was every city like this, or just some?"

## Going Deeper

**Disagreement as belonging** — This world's whole formation logic was to hold real, community-defining, unresolved questions as the substance of belonging rather than a wound to be fixed — a mature person here could argue hard for one answer and still recognize a household holding a different one as truly "the church of God." [Doc_07 §2E, §4, §5; Capsule "How This World Forms People"]
*Opening question:* "How do you decide who's right when two churches you're in contact with genuinely disagree about something important?"
Follow-ups: "Doesn't that just mean nobody actually knows the truth?" · "Did disagreeing ever break fellowship between two churches?" · "Is there anything you would have broken communion over?"

**Two patterns, one people** — Antioch and Asia Minor pressed toward a single bishop, described by one of this world's own advocates as fitted together with his presbyters "as the strings are to the harp"; Rome's own texts show a plural council of presbyters, with no sense that anything was missing for want of one presiding figure. [Doc_01 §6; Doc_04 G01; Doc_07 §2C, quoting Ignatius, *Ephesians* 4; Doc_09 story012]
*Opening question:* "I've heard some early churches had bishops and some didn't. Which kind were you?"
Follow-ups: "Was the bishop's authority just obvious to everyone, or did people have to be persuaded of it?" · "Could the same person be described as a bishop by one letter-writer and just 'one of the presbyters' by himself?" · "Did the two patterns ever come into open conflict?"

**The body that really suffered** — For those shaped most directly by Ignatius's own example, whether Christ's body and suffering were real flesh — and not merely appearance — was not incidental doctrine; it was the ground everything else about faithfulness and dying well stood on. [Doc_04 G05 (Tensional); Doc_07 §2B, §2H]
*Opening question:* "Why did it matter so much to you whether Jesus's body was 'real' flesh and not just an appearance?"
Follow-ups: "What would it have meant if the doubters were right?" · "Was this argument settled by the time your world's story ends, or still live?" · "Did everyone in your network feel this as urgently as Ignatius did?"

**Mercy after the water runs out** — Rome's own visionary material wrestled at length with whether a real moral failure committed after baptism could still be forgiven, teaching one — and only one — further chance at repentance before that door closed. [Doc_09 story007, Hermas *Vision* 2.4.3; Doc_07 §2A, §2H]
*Opening question:* "What happened if someone sinned badly after they'd already been baptized? Was there a way back?"
Follow-ups: "Why only one more chance, and not endless mercy?" · "Who decided whether someone's repentance was genuine?" · "Did this fear sit as heavily on everyone as it seems to in the text?"

**The love-feast and the table: one meal or two?** — This world's own vocabulary includes the *agape*, love's own name given to an ordinary meal that fed the stranger alongside the household — and whether that meal was the eucharist itself or a separate table kept alongside it was never settled even inside this world's own practice. [Doc_06 lex010 "agape"; Doc_09 story004, Pliny's "ordinary meal"; Capsule "The Language You Think In"]
*Opening question:* "Was the meal where you fed hungry people the same as your communion meal, or two different things?"
Follow-ups: "Why would you even need two separate meals?" · "Did an outsider watching from the doorway know the difference?" · "Is that still an open question, or did later Christians settle it?"

**A rival's table, a rival's teacher** — Marcion, Valentinian/Gnostic teachers, and the early Montanist movement were live, undefeated, contemporary neighbors sharing this world's own cities in these same decades, not later heresies safely outside its frame — and refusing a rival's separate table was, for some, the same act as refusing his teaching. [Doc_01 §8.3; Doc_04 G05; Doc_09 story011]
*Opening question:* "Were there other Christian-sounding teachers or groups competing with you at the time — people who might have looked just as legitimate as you did?"
Follow-ups: "How could an ordinary person tell the difference?" · "Did you already have all your answers to them worked out, or were you still arguing it out yourselves?" · "Did any of your own people leave to join them?"

**What the letters actually carried** — A member of one *ekklesia* rarely met "the movement's" teaching directly; they met it already shaped by a specific writer answering a specific, local trouble — Rome correcting Corinth's deposed presbyters, Polycarp forwarding what Ignatius had written on his way to die. [Doc_07 §2E; Doc_08 Force 2B-2; Doc_09 story002, story003]
*Opening question:* "When a letter arrived from another church, what was actually in it — news, instructions, an argument, what?"
Follow-ups: "Could you tell if a letter was really from who it claimed to be?" · "Did you ever get a letter you disagreed with?" · "What made a church decide a particular letter was worth keeping and copying?"

**Testing the spirit: true prophets and false** — A figure who spoke under the Spirit's own prompting still moved among this world's households, fading rather than gone, welcomed at the table only once their claim had been tested against how they actually lived. [Doc_06 lex008 prophetes; Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "How did you know if someone claiming to speak for God was for real, or a fraud?"
Follow-ups: "What would disqualify someone?" · "Did that kind of figure become less common over time?" · "Who had the authority to say no to a claimed prophet?"

## For the Wrestling (hard questions welcome here)

**Isn't "we haven't settled it" just an excuse?** — This world's own build discloses that its unresolved questions are unresolved for two different reasons — some genuinely, historically contested in antiquity, some simply beyond what the surviving evidence can responsibly generalize — and a participant can fairly press whether that distinction is doing real work or just sounds better than admitting uncertainty. [Doc_04 G01, G05 Cross-Check; Doc_07 §4]
*Opening question:* "Isn't 'we're still arguing about it' just a nicer way of saying you don't actually know?"
Follow-ups: "Are there things you genuinely believe are simply true, or is everything up for argument?" · "What would it have taken for your world to actually settle one of these questions?" · "Doesn't refusing to settle anything make you unable to correct real error?"

**Two churches, two answers, same name — is that even one church?** — A household led by a single bishop and a household governed by a council of presbyters never found the place where their answers became one, and neither treated the other as wrong enough to break communion over — a participant can fairly ask whether that is unity or just two different religions sharing a label. [Doc_07 §4, §5; Capsule "What This World Holds Without Resolution"]
*Opening question:* "If one of your churches has a bishop and another doesn't even recognize that office, how can you honestly call yourselves the same church?"
Follow-ups: "What would it have taken for you to say the other side had actually left the faith?" · "Isn't 'we're still the same church' just what you tell yourselves so it doesn't feel like a split?" · "Did anyone ever try to force the question and make everyone choose one pattern?"

**You blamed two enslaved women's testimony on their torturer, and moved on** — This world's only outside description of its own worship survives because a Roman governor tortured two enslaved women, called *ministrae*, to get it — and this world's own account of what a gathering looked like rests, in part, on that. [Doc_09 story004; Doc_09 §4 Item 4; Doc_06 lex009 ministrae]
*Opening question:* "The clearest outside account of what your worship actually looked like came from torturing two enslaved women. Doesn't that taint everything we think we know from it?"
Follow-ups: "Did anyone in your own world ever say that was wrong?" · "Do you know anything about those two women themselves — their names, what happened to them after?" · "Why keep using that account at all, if it was produced that way?"

**A longing to be consumed — isn't that a death wish dressed up as faith?** — Ignatius wrote of wanting to be "food for wild beasts" as the truest imitation he could offer, and this world's own build restricts that longing's confidence to exactly two people in its evidentiary base, not a community-wide ideal. [Doc_04 G04 (Supporting, Strand A only); Doc_01 §9; Capsule "What It Feels Like Here"]
*Opening question:* "Wanting to be eaten alive by animals and calling it the truest way to follow Jesus — isn't that just a death wish wearing religious language?"
Follow-ups: "Did most people in your world feel that way, or was that unusual even then?" · "What would you say to someone who read that and felt frightened, not inspired?" · "Was there ever a voice inside your world that pushed back on it?"

**If mercy runs out once, what kind of God is that?** — Rome's own penitential teaching held that a real failure after baptism gets exactly one further chance, not an open door — a participant carrying real guilt, or watching someone they love fail more than once, can fairly press whether that is good news or a trap. [Doc_09 story007, Hermas *Vision* 2.4.3; Doc_07 §2A, §2H]
*Opening question:* "Only one chance at forgiveness after baptism? What happens to someone who fails a second time?"
Follow-ups: "Isn't that worse than having no promise of mercy at all?" · "Who is that rule actually protecting — the sinner, or the community?" · "Did this teaching comfort people, or mostly frighten them?"

## Questions This World Answers Honestly With Its Limits

*These are good questions to ask precisely because this world will not pretend to more than its record holds. Its own build documents (Doc_09 §4 Absent Stories; Doc_07 §6 Gaps and Limits) name where the evidence runs thin, and Chloe — speaking as this world's own gathered voice — is built to say so plainly and turn back to what she can open well.*

**"What did an enslaved member of your household actually think or feel?"** — No enslaved member's own voice survives anywhere in this world's evidentiary base. The closest approach is two unnamed *ministrae* Pliny tortured for information, known only through their torturer's report — not their own account. The household codes address enslaved members but are authored by free leaders instructing them toward obedience, never contesting an owner's control over their bodies. Chloe will name this absence rather than invent a voice for it. [Doc_09 §4 Item 4; Doc_09 story004; Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"]

**"What was it like for the ordinary people among you — the ones who never wrote anything down?"** — By the best modern estimate, only around 2% of this world's members were literate enough to have produced texts like the ones that survive. Every story in this world's own repository is authored by, or filtered through, a leadership-tier voice or a hostile outside observer. Chloe can tell you plainly what an ordinary member's life likely held — worship, hospitality, risk — but not what it felt like from the inside, in their own words, because no such words reached her. [Doc_09 §4 Item 5; Doc_07 §6; Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"]

**"Did Rome ever have its own martyrs, or its own arguments against false teachers, the way Antioch and Asia Minor did?"** — This world's build actively tested whether new evidence for Rome's own martyrdom-meaning or boundary-drawing existed and came up empty. Both remain attested only through Ignatius and the community around Polycarp — genuinely Strand A only. Whether that reflects a real difference in what Rome's own communities lived through, or simply an accident of which texts survived, is named as a live, unresolved question rather than settled either way. [Doc_09 §4 Items 1–3; Doc_08 §5 "Where Forces Analysis Surfaced Gaps," §9 Outstanding Items]

**"Was there a real institution called 'the house church,' with its own name and identity?"** — Tested directly and it does not hold: the classic household-code texts are not among this world's own primary voices, no primary voice uses *oikos* as a technical name for a meeting-place, and "household as basic unit" was tested as a candidate gravity and did not reach that status. Chloe will answer from what she actually knows — who leads, what she believes about the body, the letters and the table she shares — not from a category her own life never used for itself. [Doc_04 G06 (did not reach gravity status); Doc_06 §1 Open Item 3; Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"]

**"What did your meeting places actually look like — any buildings, objects, physical remains?"** — This world's own window, across all three of its regions, is essentially archaeologically invisible: no securely dated Christian architecture, funerary material, or unambiguous inscription survives from before this world's own closing years. This is a genuine feature of the evidence itself, not a gap in the research. Chloe can tell you what happened at the table; she cannot tell you what the room looked like. [Doc_07 §2I; Doc_09 §4 Item 10]

**"What did the women among you say, in their own words?"** — Two ordinary women are named anywhere in this world's entire evidentiary base at all — greeted in passing in a letter's closing lines — and even there, nothing survives beyond a name and a greeting. No narrative, no words of their own. Rome's own doctrinal material beyond the question of post-baptismal mercy is similarly thin. Chloe holds this silence honestly rather than filling it. [Doc_09 §4 Item 9; Doc_07 §6]
