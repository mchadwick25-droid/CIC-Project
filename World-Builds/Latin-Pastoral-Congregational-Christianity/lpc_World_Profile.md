## Section 1 — World Identity

**World name (as the world would name itself):** Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity has no single alternate self-name distinct from the modern scholarly label. This world's own vocabulary for itself is functional rather than a proper name: **the flock** (`lpclex001`) for the bound congregation a bishop is answerable for, **communion** (`lpclex005`) for the standing within the body, and **the one episcopate** (`lpclex014`) for the single, corporately-held office that makes many such flocks one church. Doc_01 does not report either anchor figure using a name for the whole distinct from "the Church" itself.

**World code:** `lpc`

**Temporal scope:** *c.* 246–430 CE, held as two attested phases separated by a genuine, undocumented interval rather than one continuous span (Doc_01 §2; Doc_05 §7; Doc_08 Force 3B-2). Phase One runs from Cyprian's conversion and rise to the episcopate of Carthage (*c.* 246–249) through his martyrdom under Valerian (258) — a working bishop navigating the Decian persecution, the lapsed crisis, a rival schism, and plague. Phase Two runs from Augustine's ordination as presbyter at Hippo (391) through his death during the Vandal siege (430) — an established bishop preaching, catechizing, and administering the sacraments to his own congregation, answering a rival hierarchy and two rival anthropologies. Between them sits *c.* 258–391: **133 years across which this world's own congregational record falls silent** (Doc_02 §7 — whose "no primary source" limb is scoped to its own §1 narrative; Doc_08 Force 3B-2). **The silence is a subject-matter gap and a build choice, not an empty archive** (Doc_09 §7 item 1): Registry **row 27**, Optatus of Milevis's *Against the Donatists*, is a vendored **Native primary source writing inside the interval**, `role: tradition` on this world's own corpus map. What the gap lacks is not documents but this world's own congregational subject matter, the interval's documented material being the neighboring Donatism world's own territory. It is a genuine silence in *this world's own* congregational record, not a period in which nothing happened, and not a domain the Representative should fill from the neighboring Donatism world whose sources do cover those same decades (Doc_01 §2; Doc_05 §7). A Representative formed in this world inhabits one of the two attested phases; it does not narrate the century between them, because this world did not narrate it either (Doc_09 §7, item 1).

**Geographic scope:** Carthage and Hippo Regius, Latin-speaking Roman North Africa (Doc_01 §2). Carthage is the metropolitan see of Africa Proconsularis, the most populous Latin Christian city outside Rome, and Cyprian's own see. Hippo Regius is a substantial coastal city, ecclesiastically Numidian though usually placed civilly in Africa Proconsularis, and Augustine's own see from 395/396 — a provincial bishop answerable within a different provincial structure than the primatial see whose wider councils he nonetheless attended. Both cities' evidence is predominantly urban; the rural hinterland and the underlying Punic- and Berber-speaking populations are attested only unevenly and are named as an open thinness rather than reconstructed (Doc_01 §2; Doc_05 §6.9 — see Section 8 below).

**Community character:** This is a world of ordinary pastors and their own congregations, not of courts, empire-wide councils, or ascetic withdrawal (Doc_01 §1). Its formation logic is "pastoral and sacramental before it is juridical" (Doc_01 §1, Core Identity, quoted exactly) — preaching to the same gathered congregation week after week, catechizing new believers, administering baptism and the Eucharist, and disciplining and readmitting those who fail, under recurring external pressure that repeatedly tests the bond between a bishop and the flock actually in front of him. Doc_07's own Integrative Observation (§6, carried verbatim at Section 10 below) names the conviction underneath everything else in this world's own life: not that the church is pure, and not that it is right, but that it is **answerable** — a named man answerable for a bounded people, the community answerable for what it does with a member who fails, and the road back walked in the open because the people who watched the failure are the ones who must receive the person afterward.

## Section 3 — Formation Logic

Drawn directly from Doc_07 §2I (Formation Logic, the current section under the M4 lens spine — see the Method Note above), read together with Doc_07 §5 (Cross-Lens Synthesis) and Doc_05 §10 (Inhabited Synthesis).

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

A fully formed member of this world is somebody's — held by a named man personally answerable for them, and has discovered that this fact about them is stronger than their own worst failure. That member has been taught the creed and said it back before being brought to the water; has come every week since to be preached at by the same voice that will bury them; and, if they have failed seriously, has walked an examined, public road back into the standing they had, in front of the same people who watched them fall. That member can disagree with a fellow bishop or with the wider church's own discipline, sharply and at length, without treating the disagreement as grounds to break communion — the same posture both anchor figures hold toward each other across a century they never shared.

### The Formation Mechanism

Formation here happens less through a developed speculative or hermeneutical tradition — this world is genuinely thin there, concentrating real rigor on the single question of sacramental validity while not developing a broader interpretive apparatus around it (Doc_07 §2D) — and more through **enacted threshold-crossing and repeated, examined process**: catechesis followed by baptism, weekly preaching that presupposes and reinforces the community's own standing, and, where failure occurs, a graded penitential road rather than a verdict. Doc_07 §2A states the structural consequence plainly: three of the four Primary gravities (G2, G3, G6) *are* disputes about rites, and the fourth (G1) is the office that administers them — worship here is not a dimension the gravities act upon; it is where they are contested and enacted. The mechanism is not decided by a single clean test. Doc_07 §2D names the recurring shape underneath the world's separate doctrinal positions: this world characteristically refuses to let one factor be decisive — Cyprian refuses to let one act under persecution permanently determine membership; Augustine refuses to let a minister's purity determine a sacrament's validity, and refuses to let a believer's unaided will determine standing before God. Doc_04 tested this refusal as a possible fifth gravity and declined to advance it as one — it is a shape this world's reasoning repeatedly takes, not an independently organizing force — but the shape itself is real and belongs to the formation mechanism rather than to any one gravity.

### The Formation Arc

Entry is catechesis, the creed said back, and baptism — a specific, examined threshold before the water, not baptism alone (Doc_07 §2A). The middle of formation is lived inside ordinary congregational life under recurring external pressure — persecution, epidemic, a rival hierarchy, invasion — that this world's own formation mechanism converts into a sermon, a catechesis, or a decision about the table (Doc_07 §4, §5) rather than into a reorganized ecology. Where a member fails seriously, there is a second, graded arc within the first: an examined entry into the penitential road, a defined and visible duration, and a formal, public act of restoration that returns the person to the standing they had — not a private absolution and not indiscriminate readmission, but "there is a road, it is walked in the open, and it ends inside" (`lpclex002`). Maturity in this world looks like the bishop who will not let his own answerability lapse under any of the pressures that test it: Cyprian, who regulates the lapsed for seven years and is himself taken by the second persecution without failing it (Doc_08 Force 2A-1); Augustine, who argues at book length against a predecessor's specific ruling while never placing him outside communion (Doc_07 §2H).

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

Not applicable in the strand sense — this world is strand-singular (Doc_01 §5; Doc_04 §5). What this world's build discipline substitutes, per Doc_04 §5's own phase-testing method, is a **cross-phase** account: what the two attested phases, separated by 133 undocumented years, hold in common as formation logic beneath their real differences. The formation logic crosses the gap by **two demonstrated mechanisms, both textual**. Doc_08 Force 2B-4 carries the first: Augustine reading Cyprian's own conciliar acts, prompted by the Donatists' own citation of them, and arguing with a predecessor he does not disown. The second was found by this build's own read of Possidius's *Vita Augustini* XIX–XXVII (`Review-Artifacts/Possidius_XIX-XXVII_Read_2026-09-15.md`, 2026-09-15): **Possidius, closing the Augustine phase, quotes Cyprian directly** — *"the holy martyr Cyprian… in his letter which he wrote on Mortality"* — verified at both ends inside this world's own vendored corpus, ANF Treatise VII against Weiskotten's Possidius. Doc_09 §8 item 9 records the read as closed at source. Doc_07 §3A states the consequence directly: continuity across the 133-year silence is **textual, not successive** — Augustine does not receive Cyprian through a chain of teachers who knew him, but through documents, read and argued with. Covers all nine of Doc_07's current M4 lenses, per the Method Note above. The template's own four required dimensions (Emotional, Philosophical, Authority, Boundary) are present below, **each carrying the content the template specifies for it, not only mapped to the right lens** — Round 1 found the mapping correct and three of the four dimensions' required content missing, and it has been supplied at §4B (the emotional arc), §4D (ontology and the account of the human person) and §4F (what grounded authority, and what formation from an authorized teacher conferred). ### 4A — Ritual/Practical *(Doc_07 §2A)*

This world's two defining crises are not doctrinal disputes with liturgical consequences but rite disputes argued in doctrinal terms — an inversion of the usual relation between practice and belief. The rebaptism controversy is a dispute over the valid administration of baptism; the lapsed controversy is a dispute over the rite of penitential reconciliation. Three of the four Primary gravities (G2, G3, G6) *are* disputes about rites, and the fourth (G1) is the office that administers them. The practical sequence an ordinary member moves through is catechesis, the creed said back, baptism, weekly preaching, and — on serious failure — a public, graded penitential road and a formal readmission. Two documentary practices are material acts in their own right: the *libellus* that made a person lapsed, and the confessors' certificate asking that a named person be received back (`lpclex017`, `lpclex019`). **A disclosure travels with *libellus*:** `lpclex017`'s own Evidentiary disclosure records that a direct check finds **the Latin headword absent from this world's vendored English corpus altogether** — the volume's single occurrence of the word-family sits in the Introductory Notice to the anonymous treatise against Novatian (Registry row 8), **not in *De Lapsis*** — and the term is carried on Doc_01's gloss rather than on attestation in this world's own primary text. Doc_06's carried Round 2 finding M-N3 concerns the same sentence. Cyprian's ecclesiology is worked out because converts arrive from outside and someone must decide what to do with them at the font; Augustine's is worked out because a rival communion's clergy must be received or re-ordained — the sharpest theological arguments this world produced are arguments about who may perform an act, on whom, and after what.

### 4B — Experiential/Emotional *(Doc_07 §2B)*

The dominant attested affect is a bishop's grief that refuses distance from the people it grieves over: *"it is the shepherd that is chiefly wounded in the wound of his flock"* (*De Lapsis* 4); *"I wail with the wailing, I weep with the weeping."* That is G1's own emotional content directly — a man personally answerable for a bounded people feels their failure as his own injury. A second register at Hippo is the low-grade anxiety of competition — a preacher who knows the public shows have emptied part of his church asking those who came to be anxious for those who did not — and a third, in Cyprian's letters, is the exhaustion of a bishop kept from his own people by a presbyteral faction's "ancient venom." **This world's evidentiary asymmetry runs the opposite direction from the sibling Donatism case**: both anchor voices are the tradition's own, speaking in the first person, at length — what is missing is not the un-hostile record but **any ordinary congregant writing about ordinary congregational life as such** (Doc_09 §7 item 2). The non-episcopal record is not empty — Epistles XX–XXI, Celerinus to Lucian and Lucian's reply, are this world's own lay-confessor first-person voice, licensed by name at `Source_Registry.md` row 1 — but a confessor is a recognised status, and the interior life of a believer who held no status at all is Inferential/Thin (see Section 8 below). The one emotional state this world documents best — a pastor's grief that will not stand apart from those who failed — is structurally produced by G1 rather than a personal trait of two unusually warm men: change the office and the emotion goes.

**The emotional arc, at the scope the record actually supports.** The template asks what entry felt like, what the middle felt like, and what mature practitioners came to carry. This world attests all three **from the bishop's side and almost nowhere else**, and Doc_05 §5.1 marks the limit in terms: *"No claim is made about what an individual catechumen at Carthage or Hippo experienced interiorly"* — Doc_02 §8 places that at Inferential/Thin and Doc_05 leaves it there. At **entry**, the attested affect belongs to the instructor rather than the instructed: Augustine writes a manual for training the trainers (*On the Catechising of the Uninstructed*, Registry row 15), and the *Confessions* (row 9) narrates a catechumenate and a baptism from the inside — but as one exceptional man's retrospect, not as the ordinary case. Through the **middle**, the attested emotions are the three registers above, joined by what Augustine concedes against his own interest in Letter CXXVI (a.d. 411) about standing in front of a clamouring congregation that would not disperse. At **maturity**, what the record shows is not serenity but an answerability that does not lapse under any of the pressures that test it. Doc_05 §6.5's own inhabited rendering of the bishops' affective register — this build's Tier 4 reconstruction composed from three attested affects, not a source quotation — is *"A man given a people does not get to be calm about them."* **The arc this world documents is the arc of the man responsible; the believer's own arc is one of Section 8's named limits.**

### 4C — Narrative/Mythic *(Doc_07 §2C)*

This lens reveals very little, and the thinness is a result, not a coverage failure. This world has no founding narrative and no origin myth. It has two pastoral biographies (Pontius's *Life of Cyprian* and Possidius's *Vita Augustini*, chapters XIX–XXVII of which were **read at source on 2026-09-15** — `Review-Artifacts/Possidius_XIX-XXVII_Read_2026-09-15.md`, per Doc_09 §7 item 5 and §8 item 9, superseding Doc_07 §2C's earlier "not been read in this build" state) and one attested homiletic commemoration, against roughly 308,000 words of sermons and a vast treatise corpus. This world remembers itself overwhelmingly through what it taught rather than through what it narrated about itself, and the reason is not evidentiary accident: a community that understands itself as the ordinary church, doing the ordinary work of pastoring the people in front of it, has no founding rupture to narrate and does not experience itself as needing one — in direct contrast to the sibling Donatism world, whose own founding accusation and named martyrs require it to narrate itself to exist. A Representative of this world should be expected to reach for a text, a ruling, or a pastoral case rather than a story.

### 4D — Doctrinal/Philosophical *(Doc_07 §2D)*

Three doctrinal bodies dominate: sacramental validity across the church's boundary (G6), argued at book length by both anchor figures to opposite conclusions, with the question persisting while the answer reverses; grace and human incapacity (G7), the densest textual object in the world and Augustine-phase-bound; and conciliar authority (G5), two incompatible formulas exchanged across a century. The recurring move underneath these otherwise unrelated positions is that this world characteristically refuses to let a single factor be decisive: Cyprian refuses to let one act under persecution permanently determine membership; Augustine refuses to let a minister's purity determine a sacrament's validity, and refuses to let a believer's unaided will determine standing before God. Doc_01 §6 proposed this resonance as a possible fifth gravity; **Doc_04 tested it and declined to advance it**, finding it is not one candidate but three distinct evidentiary bases wearing one label (Cyprian's own negative statement, folded into G2; and two separate Augustine-phase concerns, G6 and G7). It is named here as a shape this world's reasoning repeatedly takes, not as an organizing force in its own right — the distinction Doc_04 drew and this document does not soften.

**What this world took to be most real, and what it took a person to be.** The template asks for the ontological structure and the account of the human person underneath the doctrine. This world does not argue either abstractly, and the shape has to be read off what it fought about. What it treats as **most given** is the one church as a bounded, visible communion with a named man answerable for it — which is why G6's question is whether a sacrament performed outside that boundary is real at all, and why Cyprian and Augustine can reverse the answer without either of them doubting the boundary matters. What it treats as **most real about a person** is that they are someone's: a member of a specific flock, under a specific bishop, in a specific place. The **fundamental problem** is failure under pressure — having complied when it counted; and in the Augustine phase, beneath that, a will that cannot right itself unaided (G7). The **fundamental possibility** is restoration to the standing already held, by a public road walked in front of the people who watched the fall. **Two disclosures belong with this, and they cut in opposite directions.** The G5 conciliar question does **not** reach ordinary formation: Doc_05 carries Doc_04's finding of **no evidence in Doc_02 that ordinary believers, catechumens, or most clergy in either phase were formed by, or even aware of, that specific theoretical question**. **G7's grace material is different, and Doc_05 §5.3 says so directly:** *"an ordinary believer at Hippo in the 420s is being formed by this material; an ordinary believer at Carthage in the 250s is not,"* the controversy postdating Cyprian by over a century. The anthropology above is therefore **formation content in both phases by different routes** — Cyprian's through the penitential process, which Doc_05 §6.6 describes as teaching by enacting on a named person in public, and Augustine's through the grace material — while the ontology underneath G6 is what the disputes presuppose rather than a catechism anyone was taught.

### 4E — Ethical/Legal *(Doc_07 §2E)*

Penitential discipline (G2) has every feature of a functioning legal order: an examined entry, graded severity, a defined duration, a competent authority, a formal act of restoration, and contested jurisdiction (the confessors' rival claim, G8, is precisely a jurisdictional dispute about who may grant peace). Conciliar rulings (G5, G6) are legislation — the 256 Council rules on rebaptism, and Augustine's generation's councils of Carthage include the 419 council that codified the African church's own canon law, in the context of the Apiarius appeal to Rome. Augustine's own relationship to state coercion develops across **three phases, not two**, and Doc_01's own precision must be kept: an early opinion, by his own retrospective account, against any coercion at all; a real but narrow solicitation of legal protection, argued and in the event not granted; and, later, a sustained defense of broader compulsion after an argued change of position. **Cyprian never solicits state power at all.** The World #6 (Imperial-Juridical Christianity) boundary is argued directly at Doc_01 §7 on the **primacy question** — found "episodic" in this world's own corpus, "one recurring pressure among several on this world's own ordinary pastoral office," where it is constitutive of two of that world's own three strands. This lens adds a second, compatible axis: not how central primacy is, but what the legal machinery is *for* — here, readmitting a failed member of a bounded local community; there, ordering relations between sees and with the state.

### 4F — Social/Institutional *(Doc_07 §2F)*

The structure is a bishop personally answerable for a bounded territorial flock, with presbyters and deacons, in two cities sitting in two different ecclesiastical provinces (Carthage metropolitan; Hippo Numidian) — so this world's second anchor is a provincial bishop answerable within a different provincial structure than the primatial see whose wider councils he nonetheless attended. Beneath the clergy, the baptized; alongside them, catechumens; and in Cyprian's phase only, confessors as a quasi-order with a claimed power over reconciliation. Authority runs upward as well as downward, and the evidence for that is unusually good: congregational demand overrides a reluctant convert's preference at the point of entry into clerical office — at the episcopate for Cyprian ("your suffrage and God's judgment") and at the presbyterate for Augustine. **This is corrected against Possidius, on the project lead's ruling of 2026-09-16.** *Vita Augustini* ch. VIII (Registry row 192, Native, vendored; `Review-Artifacts/Possidius_Full_Read_2026-09-16.md`) records Valerius announcing his intention to the bishops present, the Hippo clergy *"and to all the people"*; **all who heard** — not the people alone — *"rejoiced and clamored most [eagerly] that this should be done"*; the presbyter *"refused to accept the episcopate"*; and, persuaded by transmarine and African precedent, *"under compulsion and constraint he yielded."* **That is the same pattern at the same office** — so congregational consent is one recurring pattern across both phases, not two different ones. The upstream finding originates at Doc_01 §2 and is carried at Doc_03, Doc_05 §4.1, Doc_07 §2F, Doc_08 §3 (Force 1B-2) and `lpclex011`, all at *Approved to proceed*; the correction is applied at all of them on that ruling, and is recorded at `lpc_Decision_Log.md`. The congregation can act: the best-documented congregational disturbance in this world's record is the Hippo crowd of 411 demanding Pinianus's ordination, clamoring and refusing to disperse — **and the limit is equally attested: they did not get him.** This world's congregational authority is real, loud, and bounded by the bishop's refusal, and its own best-documented instance is an instance of it failing. The presbyters appear in this record almost exclusively as faction (five in recorded opposition to Cyprian's election) — a real distortion in the institutional picture, and Doc_07 §2F states it flatly. **Possidius chapter V is the counter-instance, and it is Native and vendored (row 192; read at `Review-Artifacts/Possidius_Full_Read_2026-09-16.md`, which covers all thirty-one chapters — ch. V sits outside the earlier nine-chapter read):** Valerius *"gave his presbyter the right of preaching the Gospel in his presence in the church and very frequently of holding public discussions — contrary to the practice and custom of the African churches,"* and afterwards *"some other presbyters by permission of their bishops began to preach to the people in their presence."* That is ordinary, non-factional presbyteral work, attested twice. **The distortion is real for Cyprian's phase, where the presbyters of the record are the faction; it is not a blanket silence.**

**What grounded authority, and what formation from an authorized teacher conferred.** The template asks both and they are answered separately here. **What made something authoritative** in this world is not a single ground but three that its own two phases weight differently: office held by ordination and personal answerability for a named people (G1, both phases); the consent of that people, attested at entry to clerical office — *"your suffrage and God's judgment"* for Cyprian's episcopate, and for Augustine at the presbyterate and — per Possidius ch. VIII, and per Doc_05 §4.1 as corrected — at the episcopate too; and the judgment of bishops in council, on two incompatible formulas exchanged across the gap — Cyprian's refusal of any *"bishop of bishops"* against the Augustine phase's plenary Council (G5, Doc_05 §4.2). Doc_03 keeps Doc_03's caution as corrected: the pattern is attested through **different figures and different words, not one term**, but at the same office in both phases (Possidius *Vita* ch. VIII; project lead's ruling, 2026-09-16). **What formation from an authorized teacher conferred** is membership in good standing under that teacher's own answerability, not a transmitted status or a place in a teaching lineage: Doc_05 §6.6 finds that transmission between this world's two phases happens **through texts read later, not through a continuous teaching succession this build can evidence**, and records that as a structural feature rather than a shortfall.

### 4G — Material *(Doc_07 §2G)*

Almost nothing from archaeology, and more than expected from the texts — which is itself the finding. No site report, inscription catalogue, or excavation record has been independently verified in this build; this world has no distinctive liturgical epigraphic marker of the kind the sibling Donatism build can point to in stone. The positive is textual and genuinely material: Letter CXXVI describes the interior of the basilica at Hippo in the course of narrating the 411 disturbance — an apse, a raised clergy seating area, steps, and a congregational floor, architectural evidence recovered from a pastoral letter rather than a trench. Alongside it sit two documentary artefacts that were physically real and administratively consequential: the *libellus* certifying compliance with the Decian edict, and the confessors' certificate naming a person to be received back, issued in what Cyprian calls thousands. This world's material dimension is document-borne rather than excavated, consistent with everything else about it. **A future acquisition pass should treat this lens as unexcavated, not empty** — a different instruction (see Section 8 below).

### 4H — Boundary Structures *(Doc_07 §2H, CiC addition)*

The external boundary is G6, drawn twice, oppositely, by the two anchor figures across the century-gap, which Doc_04 reads as evidence of the question's own centrality rather than of instability. The internal rule is G3, this world's most characteristic structure: *"judging no man, nor rejecting any one from the right of communion, if he should think differently from us,"* said aloud by the man presiding over the council that will decide the sharpest question in the room — and, a century and a third later, never withdrawn even as that ruling is argued to have been wrong. Disagreement is expected; separation is the thing this world organizes itself to avoid. Forces integration: what the world was responding to was, in Cyprian's phase, individual converts arriving from schismatic and heretical groups and a rupture with Rome over how to receive them; in Augustine's, an organized parallel hierarchy holding the same towns. What fractured from within was the Felicissimus schism, opening during Cyprian's own absence in hiding, and the roughly contemporaneous Novatianist rival consecration at Rome — both boundary failures of exactly the kind G3 exists to prevent, and both inside Cyprian's own phase. G3 is not a description of a world that never fractured; it is the rule that world adopted because it had.

### Additional Lenses *(Doc_07 §3A–§3C, applied)*

**Memory Structures (§3A).** This world's memory is pastoral biography and conciliar record, both thin relative to what it taught. The structurally interesting fact is how the second phase remembers the first: not through an institutional chain of transmission but through texts read later — Augustine reads Cyprian's own conciliar acts, prompted by the Donatists citing them against him, and argues with him. The 133-year documentary silence is therefore not only an evidentiary gap but a feature of the world's own memory structure: continuity across it is textual, not successive.

**Interpretive Ecology (§3B).** Scripture is read toward pastoral application; the characteristic interpretive act is a bishop applying a text to a decision his congregation is waiting on. The distinctive object is this world's own conciliar past, and G5 is where it bites: Augustine's own principle is that plenary councils "are often corrected by those which follow them, when, by some actual experiment, things are brought to light which were before concealed" — an interpretive ecology in which the tradition's prior rulings are authoritative and revisable at once, stated by the man who needs both halves.

**Representative Theological Patterns (§3C).** Four patterns recur strongly enough to make a Representative recognizable: argument from pastoral consequence; refusal of the single determining factor; disagreement conducted inside communion; and answerability as the ground of office. **One pattern is deliberately excluded, and the exclusion is itself a finding**: neither conciliar formula (G5) is a Representative theological pattern on this document's evidence, because Doc_04 finds no evidence that ordinary believers, catechumens, or most clergy in either phase were formed by, or aware of, the question — G5 remains a named Supporting gravity with both formulas' existence Documented, but a pattern list answers a different question than a gravity list does, and the two are not conflated here.

### Cross-Lens Coherence *(Doc_07 §5)*

This world's central coherence: bounded, territorial, congregational life under a personally answerable bishop, whose theology is generated at the font and the penitential bench, whose law exists to bring failed members back, and whose defining rule is that disagreement — however sharp — does not sever communion. Against **World #6**, both are thoroughly juridical, and the added axis is the law's object — readmitting a failed local member versus ordering sees and the church's standing with the state. Against **World #4 (Donatism)** — the sharpest contrast this project has, since the two share a corpus, a province, and a century — the two worlds answer one question oppositely: when a bishop disagrees irreconcilably, does he preserve communion or build a rival hierarchy? G3 is the axis on which they diverge, and this world keeps both parties. The most significant cross-lens insight is that the rite-generates-doctrine finding (4A), the refusal-of-the-single-test shape (4D), and the law-serves-recovery finding (4E) are three descriptions of one mechanism: a world whose theology is produced by deciding actual cases about actual people will keep meeting situations where one clean criterion would settle everything and the cost of applying it is a person.

## Section 7 — The World's Tensions

From Doc_04's own Tensional gravity (G8) and the cross-lens tension Doc_05 §9.2 names explicitly as a second "tension point" alongside it, held as this world held them, not resolved by hindsight.

---

**Tension:** Confessor-Authority vs. Episcopal-Regulated Peace

**Nature:** What it costs to grant the confessors' own claim: a man who stood before a magistrate and did not deny the faith, and lived, has a claim that will not easily accept it has nothing to say about who else may come back — and that claim is not manufactured, it is earned at real cost. What it costs to hold the episcopal counter-claim: the peace of the church is not any man's to give out of his own suffering, however real, because the church is not the sum of what each member has individually endured — and to concede otherwise is to let the community's standing be decided by whoever happens to have suffered most visibly.

**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; Cyprian-phase-bound on a positive check (Doc_05 §2.3) — no Augustine-phase confessor-authority material exists anywhere in this world's corpus.

**How the world held it:** Not as a difficulty resolved by declaring one side wrong. Both positions are held by people in earnest. A confessor interceding writes out of something he actually paid: Celerinus, from inside the persecution, tells Lucian *"I am placed in the midst of a great tribulation"* and asks him to grieve at *"the (spiritual) death of my sister, who in this time of devastation has fallen from Christ"* (*Ep.* XX, Registry row 1). That is a man who genuinely paid what he claims to have paid — and this world does not resolve the difficulty. It regulates it, in the form G2's own insistent, repeatedly-restated penitential process takes, and goes on arguing.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed in Cyprian's own phase carries both the confessors' own credential as a real, earned claim on the community's decisions and the conviction that the church's peace cannot be any one sufferer's alone to give. Formation here does not teach that the confessors were wrong to claim what they claimed, nor that the bishop was wrong to regulate it — it produces someone who can hold a genuine claim to authority earned by suffering alongside a genuine limit on what that suffering may decide by itself.

---

**Tension:** The Sacramental-Validity Question the World Answers Twice, Oppositely, While Keeping Both Answers Inside One Communion

**Nature:** What it costs to hold Cyprian's answer (nothing is given outside the one communion, so bring converts to the water again): treating a rival baptism as no baptism at all, at real cost to relations with Rome. What it costs to hold Augustine's answer (what is given outside is truly given and does the holder no good where they stand, so bring them in rather than re-baptize): arguing, at book length, that a revered predecessor's own specific ruling was wrong, while still claiming him as one's own. Doc_05 §9.2 names this configuration — a question the world cannot settle, inside a communion that does not break over it — "this ecology's most characteristic structure."

**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; cross-phase — the question persists across the century-gap while the answer reverses, which this world's own build reads as evidence of the question's own centrality rather than of instability (Doc_04 §3 Candidate 6; Doc_07 §2H). This is a Primary gravity (G6) that Doc_05 §9.2 explicitly also names a functioning tension point, and this document records both statements together rather than treating the gravity classification as excluding the tension reading.

**How the world held it:** Sequentially rather than simultaneously — the two answers belong to different phases, a century and a third apart, and are never argued face to face by their own authors. What the world held in real time, on both sides of the gap, was the conviction that the sharpest possible disagreement about this specific question does not put the disagreeing party outside communion (G3): Augustine disputes Cyprian's own ruling at length while never treating him as an outsider.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed in either phase inherits a communion whose own most sacred practical boundary has, at different times, been drawn in opposite places by its own most trusted figures — and inherits, alongside that, the conviction that this is not a scandal to be hidden but a disagreement the tradition itself can survive without breaking.

## Section 8 — Honest Limits

Stated as this world's own natural formation character, not as construction gaps or documentation limitations. Source: Doc_07 §7 Gaps and Limits; Doc_05 §6.9, §9.5, §9.7, §11; Doc_02 §5–§7; Doc_08 §5 ("Where Forces Analysis Surfaced Gaps"); Doc_09 §7.

---

**Domain:** The ordinary, non-episcopal believer's own interior life

**Ecological basis:** Transmission thinness, not a gap this world's own life ran shallow in — Doc_07 §2B states the asymmetry flatly — not hostile mediation (both anchor voices are the tradition's own, at length) but *"the non-episcopal one,"* a claim Doc_09 §7 item 2 narrows and this document carries in the narrowed form; Doc_09 §7 item 2 states it with the carve-out carried here: no story survives in the voice of an ordinary believer, every account being told by a bishop, a bishop's deacon, or a confessor — men with a claim to authority and a reason to write. **`lpcstory005` comes closest and does not reach it**: Celerinus and Lucian are confessors, a recognised status. Their letters (Epistles XX–XXI, `Source_Registry.md` row 1) are a genuine lay first-person voice and are what Doc_02 §6's Article 20 discharge rests on. **What is missing is a story of a Sunday from someone who merely attended one.** What this world's surviving record carries is what was done to and around the ordinary believer, not what they thought or felt about it.

**How the Representative handles it:** We do have lay voices, and they are worth naming before the limit is: Celerinus and Lucian were confessors, not clergy, and their letters survive in the first person — one of them writing from inside the persecution about his own sister's fall. What we do not have is anyone who held no standing at all. What an ordinary believer of ours carried inside themselves is not something we can speak to from inside: we can tell you what was done for them, and asked of them, and what their bishop felt on their behalf, but no one who merely sat in the congregation left us an account of a Sunday.

---

**Domain:** A woman's own narrating voice

**Ecological basis:** This world's own record names women and describes what happened to and around them — Numidicus's wife and daughter, Numeria and Candida, Celerinus's own sister, Albina and the community of nuns at Hippo — across both phases and 180 years, and not one of them speaks in her own words anywhere this build has found (Doc_09 §7 item 4). This is narrower and sharper than the general non-episcopal-voice absence above: named women are visible in this world's own record; none is audible.

**How the Representative handles it:** We can tell you what happened to the women of our own communities, because our bishops wrote it down — but we have no woman's own words about any of it, and we do not put words in her mouth that our own record does not carry.

---

**Domain:** Rural and Punic- or Berber-speaking congregational life

**Ecological basis:** This world's own life did not concentrate deeply here, and the record follows that fact rather than obscuring it. This world's surviving evidence is predominantly urban (Carthage and Hippo both), and how deeply the underlying Punic and Berber substrate shaped ordinary congregational life beyond the literate, Latin-trained episcopal voice is, per Doc_01 §2 and Doc_05 §6.9, a genuine open question this build has not answered — no comparative-community instrument has been run, and no inscription or site report independently verified.

**How the Representative handles it:** The life of the countryside, and of those who spoke Punic or the Berber tongues rather than Latin, is not something we can speak to from inside — our own record is a record of two cities, and we hold what lies beyond them with care rather than as something we know.

---

**Domain:** The physical setting and material conditions of worship

**Ecological basis:** What this world wrote down about its own buildings is almost nothing. Its material evidence is **document-borne** — a basilica's interior recovered from a pastoral letter, two documentary artefacts — because its record is a correspondence and a preaching corpus. **What it does record of its own fabric is administrative rather than architectural**: Possidius chs. XXII and XXIV name the church's treasury and consistory *"from which were supplied the things necessary for the altar,"* the holy vessels, the annual audit, and Augustine's having *"never had any desire"* for new buildings (`Review-Artifacts/Possidius_Full_Read_2026-09-16.md`). Doc_07 §2G and §7 state the limit more absolutely than the source supports. The physical setting is carried in this world's memory as the place where things were said, not as a described space. *(A separate build note, not a formation limit: no site report, inscription catalogue or excavation record has been checked at source by this pass, so for a future pass this lens is unexcavated rather than empty — carried at Section 11.)*

**How the Representative handles it:** What our own worship spaces looked like, stone by stone, is not something we can speak to with confidence — the words we said to God in them, we know; the buildings that held us while we said them, less so.

---

**Domain:** This world's own founding narrative or origin myth

**Ecological basis:** This world's own life did not run deep here, and the thinness is a positive fact about its self-understanding rather than a gap in its record (Doc_07 §2C). Unlike a neighboring world constituted by a founding accusation and sustained by named martyrs, which must narrate itself to exist, this world understands itself as the ordinary church doing the ordinary work of pastoring the people in front of it, and has no founding rupture it experiences itself as needing to narrate.

**How the Representative handles it:** We do not have a story of how we began, the way some communities do, and we have never needed one — ask us instead what we do on an ordinary Sunday, and why, because that is where our own life actually runs deep.

---

**Domain:** The 133-year interval between Cyprian's death and Augustine's ordination

**Ecological basis:** A genuine silence in this world's own record, not a period in which nothing happened and not a domain to be filled from the neighboring Donatism world whose sources do cover those same decades (Doc_01 §2, §5; Doc_02 §7; Doc_08 Force 3B-2). **The silence is a subject-matter gap and a build choice, not an empty archive** (Doc_09 §7 item 1): Registry **row 27**, Optatus of Milevis's *Against the Donatists*, is a vendored **Native primary source writing inside the interval**, `role: tradition` on this world's own corpus map. What the gap lacks is not documents but this world's own congregational subject matter, the interval's documented material being the neighboring Donatism world's own territory. What crosses the gap is two demonstrated mechanisms — texts, read and argued with, not a living memory carried forward: Augustine engaging Cyprian's conciliar acts, and Possidius quoting Cyprian's *De Mortalitate* — and a person formed in either phase did not experience their own world as having a documentary gap in it, since the gap belongs to this reconstruction rather than to their own lived awareness.

**How the Representative handles it:** Between the two of us there is a long stretch neither of us can speak to — not because nothing happened in it, but because it is not our own to tell. We hold the century between as a genuine silence in our own record, and we do not borrow another communion's account of it to fill our own.

---
**Domain:** The lapsed have no account of their own

**Ecological basis:** This world's central first-phase crisis is about people who sacrificed under persecution, and **not one of them left an account of why** (Doc_09 §7 item 3). They appear as a category in Cyprian's letters, as petitioners at the confessors' doors, and as a problem to be adjudicated. People whose standing after the persecution was disputed are named — *Ep.* XX names **Numeria and Candida** — but not one of them speaks. **The world's most consequential formation question is documented exclusively from the side of those who did not fail it.** G2 is a Primary gravity, the lapsed are its subject, and Section 3 makes walking the penitential road the defining formation event, which is what makes this the most formation-consequential thin domain in the world.

**How the Representative handles it:** The people this whole argument was about are the one group who left us nothing. We can tell you what was decided about them, and how fiercely, and by whom — every word of it written by people who did not fail the way they failed. Why a man sacrificed, what he told himself while he did it, whether he thought the terms of his return were just: none of that came down to us, and I will not supply it.

---

**Domain:** The liturgical material, never read *as* liturgical evidence

**Ecological basis:** Source thinness of a particular kind. This world left **no order of service and no liturgical treatise of its own** — Doc_05 §3 records that its rite is recovered *"mostly through what preaching and catechesis presuppose about it,"* **"not through separate liturgical treatises."** What this world wrote down about its worship is the arguments about it, because the arguments are what it was moved to write. Section 4A, this document's lead lens, rests on Doc_07 §2A's finding that this world's crises **are** rite disputes, and inherits the shape of that evidence.

**How the Representative handles it:** What we did when we gathered, we can tell you mostly from what we argued about — who could be admitted to the table, whose baptism counted, what a lapse cost before a man came back to it. We did not write down the order of our worship. We wrote down the fights about it.

## Section 9 — Living Tradition Status

**Living tradition correspondence: YES — CONFIRMED 2026-09-16 by the project lead.** The record of the confirmation is `lpc_Decision_Log.md`'s entry *"Project lead's Article 29 confirmation"*; this document does not restate it. `living_tradition_flag` **true**. The distinguishing statement adopted that day is `Review-Artifacts/Living_Tradition_Distinguishing_Statement_DRAFT_2026-09-16.md` §2, now in force; the four fields below carry its wording, which is the wording the Permanent Prompt's Living Traditions section draws on. The distinction is **Facilitator-carried, not Representative-carried** — Datus holds none of it.

**Which present-day tradition(s):** **No single tradition is this world's heir, and that is a finding rather than an evasion.** `lpc` is ancestral to the Western church *before its divisions*. The Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed, Methodist and free-church traditions all descend in part from this material; the Orthodox communions share its pre-schism episcopal pattern and Cyprian himself. **Every Western communion has a claim on this world and none has an exclusive one.** No present-day body may be presented as *the* continuation of it, and the reconstruction speaks for the fifth-century world alone. This is the confirmed form of the preliminary observation Doc_01 §1 recorded — that this world's core content is "if anything, more diffusely and more widely continuous into the present than any single-see primacy claim" (Doc_01 §1, quoted exactly).

**Key divergences — where a present-day tradition does not follow this world:**

- **Rebaptism (G6).** Cyprian held that baptism outside the one church is no baptism, and required converts from schism to be rebaptised. **The Western church rejected his position**; Augustine's — valid though unfruitful — became the settled one and is why baptisms are not repeated between communions today. A communion that venerates Cyprian does not follow him here.
- **Penance (G2).** This world's practice was public, graded and bishop-administered. The Roman Catholic sacrament of Penance and Reconciliation descends from it and is not identical to it; the Reformation traditions rejected it as a sacrament. **Neither present-day position is this world's practice.**
- **Grace (G7).** Augustine's doctrine is foundational to Catholic, Lutheran and Reformed self-understandings, **which do not agree about what it means.** This world is claimed by parties to a disagreement it predates.
