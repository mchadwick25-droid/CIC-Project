## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity.

**Short Description:** The formation of ordinary Latin North African Christianity as territorial, congregational, pastoral life under episcopal office — Cyprian of Carthage navigating the Decian persecution, plague, and schism as a working bishop (248/249–258), and Augustine of Hippo preaching, catechizing, and administering the sacraments for his own congregation a century later (391–430) — bounded by two bishops' ordinary care of an entire local flock, not by a single continuous institutional narrative across the century between them.

**Core Identity:** This is a world of ordinary pastors and their own congregations, not of courts, councils convened to settle empire-wide jurisdiction, or ascetic withdrawal from congregational life. Its formation logic is pastoral and sacramental before it is juridical (contrast World #6) or philosophical-catechetical (contrast World #2) or ascetic-literary and patronage-based (contrast World #9): preaching to the same gathered congregation week after week, catechizing new believers, administering baptism and the Eucharist, and disciplining and readmitting those who fail — under recurring external pressure (persecution, epidemic, schism, invasion) that repeatedly tests the bond between a bishop and the flock actually in front of him.

**Distinctive Contribution:** No other confirmed world in this project's portfolio reconstructs ordinary congregational and lay formation — preaching, catechesis, sacramental life, and pastoral care of a bounded local community — as directly or as richly as this one (Step 0 §3 B2). Where World #6 asks how the church governed itself relative to the state, and World #9 asks how a network of ascetic scholars and their aristocratic patrons pursued textual and spiritual formation, this world asks the plainer question underneath both: what did it look like for an ordinary bishop to hold together, teach, and discipline the actual people in his own church, under real and recurring pressure to fail at it.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed. Per Constitution Article 29, Living Tradition Status confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform on its own behalf; carried to the freeze declaration as G4, per this build's own launch instructions. Noted for the record, not decided here: this world's core content — an ordinary bishop's territorial, sacramental, congregational care of a local flock — is, if anything, more diffusely and more widely continuous into the present than any single-see primacy claim: it is close to the default self-understanding of the parish or diocesan ministry of most historic Christian communions that have retained the office of bishop or pastor at all, not a claim particular to one see's own succession. **Status: PENDING.**

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** c. 246–430 CE.

- **Beginning point (c. 246):** Cyprian's own conversion, a trained rhetorician turning from a public career to Christian life, quickly ordained and elected bishop of Carthage by 248 or 249 (ancient sources do not agree to the year; both are defensible and the difference is not load-bearing here). This is a defensible beginning point under the Framework's Temporal Scope test for a reason distinct from mere biography: Cyprian's own formation pathway — an adult, rhetorically-trained convert rising rapidly to episcopal office — is not incidental color but the same pathway Augustine's own formation will independently recapitulate a century later (§4 below), making the conversion itself, not only the episcopate that follows it, part of this world's own recurring pattern.
- **Ending point (430):** Augustine's death at Hippo, 28 August 430, during the Vandal siege of the city. Defensible on two grounds: (1) it is Augustine's own life-close, and the Representative construction discipline this project follows (RCF Part Four) grounds a world's outer temporal edge in its own anchor figures' documented lives, not an arbitrary round date; (2) it closes the world on a real ecological rupture of the same kind that opens it — the Decian persecution (250) is external, state-organized pressure on this world's own congregational life at its beginning, and the Vandal invasion (crossing from Spain in 429, besieging Hippo as Augustine lay dying) is external, violent pressure on this world's own congregational life at its end. Both bookends are pressures the flock itself lived through, not events belonging to a different register of history. 430 sits comfortably inside the broader Phase One ceiling (451, Chalcedon) with no tension.

**Geographic Centers:** Carthage and Hippo Regius.

- **Carthage:** the metropolitan see of Africa Proconsularis and, through most of this world's own span, the most populous and consequential Latin Christian city outside Rome itself — the natural convening point for the African episcopate's own councils (Cyprian's own councils on the lapsed and on rebaptism; the councils of Carthage that Augustine's own generation continues to hold, including the 419 council on the reception of Donatist clergy). Cyprian's own see; the city where Augustine was ordained to preach even before Hippo formally became his own charge, and where much of his own preaching corpus (the *Sermones ad populum*) was in fact delivered.
- **Hippo Regius:** a substantial port city on the North African coast, west of Carthage, in the region generally described as Numidia — Augustine's own see from 395/396 (bishop) after ordination to the presbyterate there in 391. A real city with its own ordinary congregation, not a metropolitan seat of the kind Carthage was; this is itself part of this world's own character — an ordinary provincial bishop's flock, not a primatial see's own institutional apparatus.

**Cultural Environment:** Latin-speaking Roman North Africa, a province (or provincial cluster: Africa Proconsularis and Numidia together, loosely) with its own markedly vigorous Latin Christian literary and rhetorical culture — the tradition that produced Tertullian's own forged theological vocabulary (§6 below), Cyprian's episcopal letters, and Augustine's vast corpus, none of it derivative of the Greek East's own idiom. A Romanized provincial society with real underlying Punic and, further inland, Berber populations; how deeply that substrate culture shaped ordinary congregational life specifically (as against the literate, Latin-trained episcopal voice this world's surviving sources overwhelmingly preserve) is a genuine open question for Doc_02's own Source Asymmetry work, not resolved or assumed here. Predominantly urban in its surviving evidence (Carthage and Hippo both were significant cities), with a rural hinterland whose own Christianization this world's sources attest unevenly.

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Decian persecution (250 CE) — the first systematically enforced, empire-wide persecution, administered through the *libelli* (sacrifice-certificate) system, producing the lapsed crisis that defines much of Cyprian's own surviving correspondence.
- Recurring later persecution: the Valerianic persecution (257–258), under which Cyprian was martyred; the Great (Diocletianic) persecution (303–305), which falls within this world's own documentary gap (§4 below) and is more directly the founding crisis of Donatism (World #4) than of this world's own surviving voice.
- Epidemic disease: the so-called Plague of Cyprian (c. 249–262), a severe, well-attested pandemic Cyprian himself addresses directly in *De Mortalitate* — a real, lived pressure on ordinary congregational life, not merely a literary occasion.
- The Donatist schism, dominant across large parts of North African Christian life for most of the century between Cyprian and Augustine and a continuing, live pastoral problem throughout Augustine's own episcopate — this world's own defining external rival, per Step 0's boundary and the reciprocal Article 23 obligation established there (§6 below).
- Manichaeism, a live, organized rival religious system Augustine personally belonged to for roughly nine years before his conversion, and a recurring target of his own pastoral and polemical writing.
- The Pelagian controversy (410s–420s), a live doctrinal dispute over grace, sin, and human capacity that occupied a substantial share of Augustine's own later pastoral and theological output.
- The Vandal invasion of Roman North Africa (from 429), reaching Hippo itself as a besieging army in the final months of Augustine's life.

**Historical Catalysts:** Cyprian's conversion and rapid rise to the episcopate (c. 246–249); the Decian persecution and the lapsed crisis (250–251); the Novatianist and Felicissimus schisms breaking out after Cyprian's return from hiding (251), and *De Lapsis*'s own penitential reckoning; the Carthage plague and *De Mortalitate* (c. 252–253); Cyprian's councils on rebaptism (255–256) and his martyrdom under Valerian (258); [this world's own documentary gap, §4 below]; Augustine's conversion (386) and baptism (387); his ordination as presbyter (391) and bishop (395/396) at Hippo; the sack of Rome (410) and the composition of *City of God*; the Pelagian controversy (410s–420s); Augustine's death during the siege of Hippo (430).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** Ordinary territorial-congregational pastoral care, exercised through preaching, catechesis, penitential discipline, and sacramental administration to the same gathered flock — under recurring external crisis-pressure (persecution, epidemic, schism, invasion) that repeatedly tests whether that bond between bishop and congregation holds.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally, and named here only as candidates for Step 4 testing, not as confirmed findings: pastoral office as flock-keeping (a bishop's authority exercised as care for and responsibility toward a bounded local community, not as jurisdiction over other sees); penitential discipline and the reintegration of the failed (how a community that has suffered real, visible collapse — the lapsed under Decius, ordinary sin under Augustine's own catechetical care — is received back rather than permanently excluded or indiscriminately readmitted); preaching and catechesis as the primary mode of ordinary formation, rather than ascetic discipline (World #9) or philosophical schooling (World #2) or conciliar/juridical process (World #6).
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Formation and belonging dominate this world's own surviving evidence — the ordinary catechumen's and believer's own journey into and within the community. Authority is real and exercised throughout (both anchor figures are bishops), but its characteristic mode is pastoral — preaching, personal correspondence, penitential process — rather than juridical decree (World #6), ascetic exemplarity (World #9), or philosophical instruction (World #2). Worship is present but recovered mostly through what preaching and catechesis presuppose about it (baptismal practice, the Eucharist, the Creed taught to catechumens), not through separate liturgical treatises the way, for instance, World #9's ascetic literature more directly documents monastic horaria.
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §6 below.

## 4. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21: strand is "a finding, never a presupposed universal schema," accountable to evidence, and never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality. This determination is made here, for the first time — Step 0 correctly deferred it as Step 1's own work (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2, the century-gap discussion under A5), while flagging that Doc_01 must make an actual, reasoned determination rather than silently assume continuity or silently treat the century between Cyprian and Augustine as immaterial.

**The question this section must not confuse itself about, stated plainly first, on the model of IJC's own Doc_01 (§4 there):** a century of real historical development separating two figures is not, by itself, evidence of internal strand plurality. Article 15 (Historical Development & Instability) already establishes that formation worlds are "shaped through instability, adaptation, and incomplete continuity even within their own bounded timeframe" — that is the correct home for temporal development *as such*. Article 21's own test is narrower and different: does the evidence show *meaningfully distinct patterns of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation*, whether coexisting or successive, rather than the same pattern recurring at two removed points?

**Finding: strand-singular.** Tested directly against Article 21's own named criteria — formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, ecological orientation — Cyprian's church and Augustine's church do not diverge. Both are:

- **The same authority structure:** a single bishop, exercising monepiscopal office, embedded in a functioning African conciliar culture (Cyprian's own councils on the lapsed and on rebaptism; Augustine's own participation in the councils of Carthage and Hippo), holding territorial responsibility for one local flock rather than jurisdiction over other sees.
- **The same formation emphasis and practice:** preaching, catechesis, penitential discipline, and sacramental administration directed at the ordinary believer and catechumen in front of the bishop, not at doctrinal argument for its own sake (contrast World #2) or ascetic formation of a scholarly-patronage network (contrast World #9) or the consolidation of jurisdiction over other bishops (contrast World #6).
- **The same ecological orientation:** a bishop's authority is legitimated by, and exercised as, care for a bounded local community he is answerable to and for — visible in Cyprian's own agonized correspondence about the lapsed and in Augustine's own catechetical manual and sermons to his own congregation alike.
- **The same formation pathway into office:** both anchor figures are adult, rhetorically-trained converts who rise rapidly to the episcopate — not a coincidence this document treats as decorative (§2 above), but a genuine, checkable recurrence in how this world's own leadership is formed, independent of either figure's own awareness of the other's life.

What genuinely differs between Cyprian's phase and Augustine's phase — the specific external pressures each faced (state persecution versus a schism that had by then become the dominant regional rival; a plague versus a Vandal invasion; Novatianist rigorism versus Donatist separatism and Pelagian anthropology) — is real, substantial, and not minimized here. But it is a difference in the *forces pressing on* a single recurring pattern, which is precisely Doc_08's own subject matter (Forces Framework, initiating/ongoing/ending × external/internal), not a difference in the pattern itself. Treating differing historical pressure as strand plurality would misapply Article 21 in the same direction IJC's own Doc_01 warned against for a different reason (folding sequential development into strand language) — here the risk runs the other way: treating a real gap as manufacturing internal plurality where the underlying formation logic has not actually diverged.

**This finding does not by itself settle the harder, adjacent question Step 0 flagged and left open — Article 3's "sufficient historical coherence" test for the world as a whole, given the century's real gap and Donatism's own rival claim to the interval. Article 21 and Article 3 are different tests, and strand-singularity is not an answer to Article 3; it is the finding that clears the way to actually answer it, which this document now does.**

**The Article 3 question, addressed directly.** This world's "sufficient historical coherence" does not rest on continuous surviving self-documentation across the full span — none exists, and none is manufactured to supply it (Tier 5 is prohibited regardless). It rests instead on a checkable, in-world fact this world's own surviving corpus actually attests: Augustine's own church understood and documented itself as the same catholic communion Cyprian had led, not as a new community starting over. This is not a continuity claim this project imports from outside; it is Augustine's own, extensively documented, in his own corpus. His central anti-Donatist treatise, *On Baptism, Against the Donatists*, argues at length through the acts of Cyprian's own baptismal councils, treating Cyprian's own authority, teaching, and conciliar procedure as directly binding precedent for his own church a century later — not as admired antiquity, but as *his own* tradition's own prior act.

Honestly named, not smoothed over: the Donatists also claimed Cyprian as their own precedent, and on the narrow question of baptismal rigor specifically, Cyprian's own historical position (that baptism outside the true church's unity is invalid and must be repeated) sits closer to the Donatist position than to Augustine's own eventual, more accommodating one. This world's own boundary decision — Cyprian and Augustine together, Donatism excluded as the rival claimant occupying and contesting the interval (Step 0 §2, §3 B3; §6 below) — does not rest on adjudicating which side legitimately inherited Cyprian's own title, which is a contested historical-theological question this project does not resolve and which properly belongs to Donatism's own construction, not this world's. It rests on which *mode* of pastoral life recurs: ordinary, territorial, sacramental care of an entire local flock, in communion with the wider catholic world, versus a rigorist-separatist alternative that broke communion over ritual purity and formed its own parallel clergy. Augustine's church, whatever the Donatists themselves said about its legitimacy, is doing the former — the same mode Cyprian's own church did before the interval, on Augustine's own explicit, sourced understanding of his own inheritance. That is what this document takes as satisfying Article 3's coherence test for this world, and it is a narrower, more checkable claim than "the same institution persisted unbroken," which this document does not claim and does not need to.

**What the gap itself is, named precisely for Doc_02's own sake, not resolved here:** the interval between Cyprian's death (258) and Augustine's own arrival in ordained ministry (391) is not undocumented history in general — it includes the Great Persecution, the *traditio* crisis, and the rise of Donatism, all richly attested, but overwhelmingly through sources that are Donatism's own territory (World #4), not this world's. What is genuinely missing is a surviving voice, continuous with Cyprian's own ordinary-pastoral mode, from within this world's own boundary, for roughly 130 years. Doc_02's Source Ecology must name this precisely — an honest silence in *this world's own record*, not a general absence of evidence about the period — and Doc_05/the Representative's own eventual construction must hold it the way RCF Part Four requires: as natural quiet, never as meta-commentary about what survived to be documented.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation for Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8.

**What generated this world:** the maturation of North African Latin Christianity, by the mid-3rd century, into a settled, numerous, institutionally real communion — Tertullian's own generation had already forged the Latin theological vocabulary this world inherits (§6 below) and established a vigorous local Christian literary culture; Cyprian inherits a community large and organized enough to have real internal factions (the Felicissimus party) and enough clergy and structure to convene councils of dozens of bishops on short notice.

**What larger world was it embedded in:** two genuinely different larger worlds, which is itself the century's real content. Cyprian's own phase is embedded in a pre-Constantinian Roman North Africa under recurring, escalating state suspicion of an unlicensed religion. Augustine's own phase is embedded in a post-Constantinian, officially Christianized Roman North Africa whose own political and military integrity is visibly failing by his final years — a Christianized empire that can no longer reliably protect the very congregations it now formally recognizes.

**What was it responding to:** for Cyprian, the practical, unprecedented pastoral crisis of empire-wide, systematically enforced persecution testing whether the church could survive its own members' failure under pressure and still reconstitute itself; for Augustine, the practical pastoral crisis of a rival communion (Donatism) claiming to be the only true church, and the intellectual-pastoral crisis of Manichaean dualism and Pelagian anthropology, each requiring sustained catechetical and polemical response from the ordinary work of a bishop's own pulpit.

**What was it refusing:** for Cyprian, both extremes of the lapsed controversy at once — Novatianist rigorism, which refused any reconciliation to those who had sacrificed under persecution, and an opposite, undisciplined laxity that would readmit without any real penitential process; a refusal, in both directions, of letting a single test of purity decide who remains within the community. For Augustine, Donatist separatism's claim that a sacrament's validity depends on the personal purity of the minister administering it, and, on a different axis, Pelagian anthropology's claim that a person's own moral effort is sufficient without grace — in both cases a refusal to let one factor (a minister's own purity; a believer's own unaided will) become the sole determinant of the church's or the soul's own standing. Named here as a preliminary observation worth Doc_04's own testing, not asserted as a confirmed gravity: across the century's real gap, both anchor figures resist a purity-test that would exclude the compromised or the imperfect from the community's own ongoing life, in favor of a more patient, grace-dependent, communally-mediated model — a genuine thematic resonance that would, if it holds under Doc_04's six tests, be a strong candidate for this world's own cross-figure Primary gravity.

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits from prior worlds:** the Latin theological vocabulary Tertullian's own generation forged — Cyprian works directly within it and Augustine inherits it at one further remove (Step 0 §2, A2; §4 item 6 there) — without Tertullian himself being this world's own voice, a real dropped-voice cost this world's own shape creates, named again here rather than left only in Step 0's own record. The general monepiscopal office structure already visible in Post-Apostolic/Sub-Apostolic House-Church Christianity (World #1) is this world's own starting institutional form, developed and exercised here rather than invented here.

**What this world transmits to subsequent worlds/history:** substantially, and far beyond this project's own Phase One window — Augustine's own theology of grace, original sin, and the two cities becomes foundational for the entire subsequent Western theological tradition; Cyprian's own ecclesiology of the one episcopate and his conciliar procedure on readmitting the lapsed shape penitential practice for centuries. Neither continuation is this document's to reconstruct; noted for orientation, matching the restraint IJC's own Doc_01 (§6 there) applied to its own strands' later legacies.

**Distinctness from adjacent worlds, confirmed and, where owed, discharged here for the first time:**

- **World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, built).** Already confirmed distinct in the Step 0 Conclusion and reaffirmed on a live cross-document check in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3 B3 — non-overlapping authority structure (pastoral/sacramental/territorial-flock-care here; juridical/state-adjacent there), orthogonality to state power, temporal overlap rather than sequence. **Discharged here, per IJC's own Step 0 §4 item 3 and this world's own Step 0 §4 item 5, which named this obligation as now falling to this document since IJC "cannot itself hold the line from its side" no longer applies once this world is under construction:** stated from this world's own side, IJC's authority is exercised as jurisdiction *over other sees and, at moments, over emperors* — a claim about rank and precedence between office-holders who mostly never meet the ordinary believer directly. This world's authority is exercised as care *for the one flock physically in front of a given bishop* — Cyprian's and Augustine's own correspondence is overwhelmingly addressed to or about named individual members of their own congregations, not to peer bishops contesting precedence. The two do not compete for the same ecological territory even where (as with the fixed Confessions boundary, IJC's own item 16 in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md`) they cite an overlapping figure.
- **World #9 (Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity, built).** Already confirmed distinct by the original World #8/#9 split itself (Step 0 §1, §3 B3) — Jerome's own gravity (charismatic-scholarly authority, voluntary aristocratic patronage, textual and translation work) found genuinely distinct from Cyprian's and Augustine's episcopal, territorial, congregational gravity. **Discharged here, per Hieronymian's own Doc_01 §8.1 and this world's own Step 0 §4 item 5, which left this contrast's other half explicitly for this world's own construction to supply:** stated from this world's own side, this world's authority is *office-based and territorial* — a bishop answerable for one bounded flock by virtue of ordination and see, whether or not any individual member of that flock personally admires or seeks him out. Hieronymian authority, on that document's own account, is *charismatic and elective* — a scholar's household of aristocratic women who chose their own formation under Jerome specifically, sustained by patronage and personal devotion rather than territorial office. A bishop cannot be un-chosen by his own congregation the way a patron's household could, in principle, simply stop supporting him; that structural asymmetry, not merely a difference of subject matter, is this world's own half of the authority-mode contrast Hieronymian's Doc_01 asked for. The Augustine–Jerome correspondence itself (17 letters, double-placed as `tradition` in both worlds' own corpus-maps, Step 0 §4 item 2/§3 B3) is direct primary evidence for this contrast in both directions at once — two contemporaries, writing to each other, each fully embedded in his own world's own distinct mode of authority — and is flagged here as a load-bearing Doc_02 asset for exactly that reason, not merely as a shared-source bookkeeping matter.
- **World #4 (Donatism, Selected, Not Yet Built, in parallel construction).** The sharpest adjacency in this world's own portfolio, examined at length in §4 above and in Step 0 §2 (A5), §3 B3, and §4 item 2. Not redecided here: the reciprocal Article 23 obligation established at Step 0 (this world's own Doc_02 must reconstruct the Donatist schism as this world's own internal rival, from this world's own perspective) stands, as does the live, currently-unresolved reading divergence between this world's own Step 0 and Donatism's own Step 0 over how far the portfolio entry's "not the schism-crisis angle" parenthetical reaches (Step 0 §2 A5, §4 item 2(e)) and the open Optatus placement question (Step 0 §3 B3, §4 item 2(a)). Both are Doc_02's to resolve, not this document's; carried forward again at §7 below so they remain checkable rather than assumed settled by silence.
- **Antiochene Christianity (Chrysostom-centered; Possible Future World, not selected for this phase).** Per Step 0 §3 B3 and §4 item 7: the Step 0 Conclusion holds this world out as the standing comparison case for a future Antiochene candidate, "pending a validated primary-gravity contrast against world #8 that doesn't rely on geography or language." This document's own gravity-orientation work (§3, §5 above) is written with that contrast in view — this world's own recurring pattern is named as a *mode of pastoral office and formation practice* (territorial, penitential, catechetical, exercised on an ordinary congregation under recurring external crisis), not merely as "Latin, North African, third-to-fifth-century," so that a future Antiochene build's own genuine points of resonance (Chrysostom's own rich ordinary-lay ethical-formation material, per the Conclusion's own description) or divergence can be tested against substance rather than against geography and language alone. Doc_04's own gravity classification should keep this framing in view; not resolved here, since no Antiochene world yet exists to test it against.
