---

## Quick Meaning

For us, suffrage is the congregational voice that puts a man in office, characteristically over his own reluctance and sometimes over an organized faction's opposition.

---

## World Meaning

Cyprian's own word for what elected him — *your suffrage and God's judgment* — set against a rival presbyteral faction's *ancient venom*, in a letter written because that faction had made it impossible for him to come in person. A deacon who knew him reports the same thing from outside: *by the judgment of God and the favour of the people, he was chosen to the office of the priesthood and the degree of the episcopate while still a neophyte.*

The pattern recurs in the second phase but not identically, and the difference is kept rather than smoothed: Augustine was seized into the **presbyterate** at Hippo against his wishes; his **episcopate** came by his predecessor's designation and a consecration, with the people one of two things that persuaded him rather than the mechanism that appointed him.

---

## Ecological Function

*Suffrage* is the entry point of the flock: it is how a man acquires the charge he will then be answerable for, and it is why this world's authority carries an obligation running back toward the people. A participant who understands it understands that reluctance is part of the pattern rather than a pious flourish.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant will hear "suffrage" as the right to vote, and expect a franchise, a ballot and a procedure.

**World Hearing:**
No franchise and no procedure. A corporate acclamation that carries real constitutive force, in a community where the man acclaimed is expected to resist and where an organized faction can oppose without stopping it.
