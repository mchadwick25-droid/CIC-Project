## Quick Meaning

For us, reconciliation is not a private absolution but a public road: an examined, graded process, held by the bishop, that ends with a failed member standing inside the community again with the standing they had.

---

## World Meaning

Two names for one thing. The discipline is the road; the reconciliation is the arrival. What matters is that it is a road at all — that there is an order to it, that the order takes time, and that it is visible.

The visibility is not cruelty and it is not theatre. It is what makes the outcome mean anything. A failure that happened in public cannot be undone in private, because the people who watched the failure are the same people who have to receive the person afterwards. If the return is invisible, they are asked to accept something they never saw happen. If it is walked in the open, they have watched it, and the person walks back into a room that knows.

And the bishop holds it, which is the contested part. Not because he is more forgiving than anyone else — he is not — but because peace granted by whoever happens to feel moved to grant it is not the church's peace, it is a favour. A church that takes everyone back the same afternoon has no door. A church that takes no one back has no Master. The process is the shape of refusing both.

---

## Ecological Function

Reconciliation is the operative half of this world's second Primary gravity and the working end of the flock: it is what a shepherd actually does with a sheep that has failed. It presupposes the lapsed as its subject and generates the tension with the confessors, whose claim to grant peace directly is what forced the process into existence. It is also the concrete mechanism by which communion is restored, which is why communion in this world is a status rather than a feeling.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant is likely to hear either a private sacramental confession — a few minutes, a screen, a penance said alone — or a therapeutic notion of reconciliation as the repair of a damaged relationship, achieved when both parties feel differently.

**World Hearing:**
Neither private nor primarily about feeling. This is a public, staged, community-witnessed process with an examined entry, a duration, and a formal act of readmission at the end, controlled by one office. What it restores is standing, and the community can see it being restored.

## Key Sources

The Epistles (Registry row 1, Confidence A) carry the process in operation, including Epistles XX–XXI; *De Lapsis* (row 2, Confidence B) carries the argument for episcopal control of it; Pontius (row 7, Confidence A) reports Cyprian's conduct of the office more broadly. Doc_03's sweep returns 31 corpus-wide occurrences of *penitence*, 20 of them inside the Epistles alone.

**Author Gravity note:** Epistles XX–XXI are this world's rare exception to the episcopal-voice monopoly — **two lay confessors, neither yet ordained**, writing to each other in the first person. Even there the transaction is clerical and confessor-side; the reconciled believer does not speak.
