## 1. What this document does, and does not, do

**Does.** It converts `Doc_03_Lexicon_Candidate_List.md`'s eighteen candidate terms into a complete, deployable lexicon: a final tier for each term, argued against Doc_05's ecology rather than restated from Doc_03; contested-tradition tagging with the contest actually specified; eighteen self-contained deployment chunks written to the L4 template; and an index that can answer "which terms are contested," "which are runtime-critical," "which rest on a single voice" without opening a single chunk.

**Does not.** It does not revisit any gravity classification — Doc_04's spine is taken as given, including the conciliar-authority classification, which rests on the project lead's ruling of 2026-09-14. It does not characterize this world's opponents: Doc_02 §6's routing note places that with Article 23 and this world's eventual Representative, and three entries state the limit in terms. It does not add terms Doc_03 did not surface — **with one disclosed exception at §5 item 3**, which is flagged rather than added.

**Why Step 6 sits after Step 5, and what that is worth here.** CF V7.4 is explicit that full lexicon development follows ecological reconstruction because *"the ecology informs the depth of lexicon entries — a term's true ecological weight becomes clearest after the formation ecology is established."* That is not a formality in this world's case. Four of Doc_03's twelve proposed Tier 1 terms move down on ecological grounds that did not exist when Doc_03 was written, and one term's phase attribution is now settled by a check Doc_05 ran. A lexicon tiered at Step 3 and never revisited would have over-weighted this world's most textually abundant vocabulary and under-served its most dangerous.

## 2. Final Tier Classification — reasoned, not restated

Doc_03 proposed **twelve of eighteen** terms as Tier 1. LDF Part III names that shape as a warning sign in terms: *"a lexicon in which most entries are Tier 1 should be treated as a signal that tiering discipline has not actually been applied, since the Tiered Depth Principle exists precisely because most lexicon-worthy terms should not require full treatment."* Doc_03 was not wrong to propose generously — Part III also says tier assignment *"should be revisited as development proceeds"* and is proposed at the candidate stage, not fixed there. Revisiting it is this document's job.

**Final distribution: 7 Tier 1, 9 Tier 2, 2 Tier 3.**

### 2.1 Tier 1 — seven entries

| Term | Ground |
|---|---|
| the flock | Doc_04's **G1, Primary** — the ecological hub; Doc_05 §9.1 finds every lens passes through it |
| the lapsed | **G2, Primary** — the subject of this world's founding crisis |
| reconciliation / penitential discipline | **G2, Primary** — the operative half: what the world *does* with the lapsed |
| confessor | **G8, Tensional** — Doc_05 §9.2 names it the ecology's tension point, and it is load-bearing for the two entries above |
| communion | **G3, Primary** — the name of belonging itself in this world |
| heresy — validity outside the church | **G6, Primary** — the question on which communion is tested hardest |
| grace | **G7, Supporting** — the one gravity that is formation *content* rather than medium (Doc_05 §5.3), and the highest-frequency term in the corpus by a wide margin |

Six of the seven carry a Primary or Tensional gravity directly. The seventh, *grace*, is Supporting — admitted at Tier 1 on LDF Part III's own criterion, which names Supporting gravities alongside Primary, and on the ecological finding that it is the only term in this lexicon that a believer was formed *by* rather than formed *through*.

**`confessor` is the one Tier 1 entry attached to a Tensional rather than a Primary or Supporting gravity, and the reason is stated rather than assumed.** LDF Part III says Tier 1 *typically* corresponds to Primary or Supporting gravities. Two things carry it here: Doc_05 §9.2 identifies G8 as this ecology's named tension point, and Doc_03's own evidence makes it load-bearing for two other Tier 1 entries — the confessors' claim to grant peace *is* what forced the penitential process into existence. A term that two Tier 1 entries depend on cannot be explained at Tier 2 depth.

### 2.2 Four terms move down from Doc_03's proposal, each on a Doc_05 finding

- **preaching** and **catechesis** — Doc_03 flagged both Tier 1, preaching on 119 occurrences of its headword within one Registry row. **Now Tier 2.** Doc_05 §5.2 is the ground: G4 passes Repetition, Formation and Persistence cleanly and passes Dependency, Explanatory and Interaction only narrowly, because the evidence shows it functions as **the medium the other gravities are taught and enforced through** rather than as an independently organizing force. Raw frequency measured the volume of the channel, not the weight of what it carries. **This is the single largest divergence in this world between textual abundance and ecological weight, and the tiering follows the ecology.**
- **"bishop of bishops" (negated)** and **"plenary Council"** — Doc_03 flagged both Tier 1, on the ground that each is a verbatim, directly re-quoted formula on this world's most contested axis. Both are still exactly that, and both are still in the lexicon. **Now Tier 2**, on a bound Doc_04 states and Doc_05 §6.7 applies: there is **no evidence** that ordinary believers, catechumens, or most clergy in either phase were formed by, or even aware of, this question. Tier 1 is for terms that organize substantial portions of the ecology; a formula two bishops exchange across a century, which reaches no one else's formation on this document's evidence, does not. **This is not a suppression of the conciliar axis** — Doc_04 §7 item 2 forbids that, and the axis remains a named Supporting gravity with both formulas' existence Documented. It is a statement about lexicon depth, which is a different question from gravity strength.

### 2.3 Two terms move down to Tier 3, and one term Doc_03 flagged Tier 1 stays at Tier 2 against the pull of its own risk profile

***libelli*** and ***libellatici* / *sacrificati*** were already Tier 2 candidates in Doc_03 and are **Tier 3** here, on attestation rather than importance. A direct check finds *libelli*'s Latin headword **absent from this world's vendored English corpus altogether**, and the two-way classification reaches this record as the vendored edition's own 19th-century **editorial endnote**, attached to a different, Confidence-C text rather than to *De Lapsis*. Both are carried for completeness and runtime recognizability, which is precisely what LDF Part III reserves Tier 3 for.

**"compel them to come in"** is the hard case and it is worth stating why it resolves as it does. Doc_03 flagged it Tier 1; it carries [DR], [CT], [AS] and [PV]; Doc_01 binds this build explicitly against compressing its three-phase development; and it is, by a distance, **the single most dangerous term in this lexicon for a modern participant**. It is nonetheless **Tier 2**, on LDF Part III's Tier Assignment Discipline: *"A well-evidenced term that is ecologically peripheral remains Tier 2 or Tier 3."* Doc_04 §2 tested the illegal-to-established shift as a candidate gravity and **declined to advance it**, finding nothing in this ecology organizes *around* it. **What makes the answer safe rather than merely correct** is LDF Part III's other rule: *"Tier 2 entries should not omit sections present in Tier 1 entries. Depth should compress; structure should not fragment."* The full Modern Hearing / World Hearing pairing, the completed CT Contest Type, the three-phase sequence and the Author Gravity note are all present in that chunk. Nothing a Representative needs in real time is lost by the tier.

### 2.4 The result, checked against the Framework's own warning

Tier 1 is **7 of 18** — a minority, concentrated on the four Primary gravities plus the Tensional pole and the one content gravity. LDF Part III's signal that tiering discipline has not been applied does not fire. `Lexicon_Deployment_Index.md` §2 carries the count and the per-tier membership.

## 3. CT tagging — assigned, and the contest actually specified

Article 26 and LDF Part II restrict [CT] to **live scholarly contest within the relevant subfield**, not in-world disagreement. That distinction does most of the work in this world, because this world is *full* of in-world disagreement: two bishops answering the rebaptism question oppositely, confessors against their bishop, two incompatible conciliar formulas. **None of that is [CT].** Doc_03 drew the line correctly and this document confirms it rather than re-deriving it.

**Three terms carry [CT], as Doc_03 assigned: *grace*, *schism*, and *"compel them to come in."*** No term is added to or removed from that set.

**What this document adds is the thing Doc_03 did not supply: the contest type.** Doc_03 assigned the tag to three terms and specified the contest for none of them — which is the single most common gap in lexicon work, and is exactly why `Lexicon_Deployment_Index.md` §4 exists as a standing check sheet rather than a one-time note. All three are now completed:

- ***grace*** — **Relationship to present-day traditions**, secondarily **Meaning**. The contest is not principally about what Augustine wrote but about how that position stands to living traditions' use of the word, across the Reformation-era appropriations and the modern reassessment of whether the position Augustine attacks is the one Pelagius held.
- ***schism*** — **Application to this world**, secondarily **Historical scope**. Whether the heresy/schism distinction was as stable as later usage implies, and how far the category describes a real ecclesial situation as against encoding the eventual victors' account. The North African case is the field's central example, which makes this contest unusually close to home.
- ***"compel them to come in"*** — **Relationship to present-day traditions**, secondarily **Meaning**. The most-cited patristic warrant in the modern historiography of religious coercion; the argument runs over its relation to later practice, over how far the pastoral-correction framing should be credited, and over whether the three-phase development is a genuine change of mind or a retrospective self-presentation.

**Each of the three states what the entry therefore does not do.** That is the operative half of the tag: a contest named without a corresponding restraint on the entry's own claims is decoration.

## 4. Distortion Risk — the pattern named once here rather than eighteen times

Every entry carries its own paired Modern Hearing / World Hearing lines. Across the set they fall into four recurring shapes, worth naming once because a Representative meeting an unanticipated term will most often be meeting one of these four rather than a fifth:

1. **The word survives and the institution behind it does not.** *Communion*, *the flock*, *catechesis*. A modern participant has the word, attaches a thinner modern referent, and does not hear the structural weight — communion as standing rather than warmth, the flock as sentiment rather than answerability.
2. **The word has been captured by a later, narrower sense that points the wrong way.** *Confessor* is the sharpest: a modern participant hears one who *hears* confession; this world means one who *made* confession, under interrogation, and lived. *Suffrage* does the same — a franchise, where this world has acclamation without procedure.
3. **The word is heard as settled where this world found it open.** *Heresy* is heard as a foregone institutional verdict; here it names a genuinely open question the two anchor figures answer in opposite directions. *Plenary Council* is heard as centralized authority, when the same sentence makes that authority revisable.
4. **The word arrives pre-loaded from a later controversy.** *Grace* and *"compel them to come in"* both arrive through arguments that postdate this world by a millennium, and a participant is very often not asking this world's question at all.

**The fourth shape is the one that most needs naming** because it is the least visible: the participant's question sounds like the world's question and is not.

That entry currently rests on Augustine's own letters, and says so. Reading row 192 would strengthen it. Carried from Doc_05 §11 item 10.
3. **One term this lexicon does not contain, flagged rather than added.** Doc_05 §3 establishes that both of this world's defining crises are, at bottom, disputes about *rites*, and that the liturgical material has **never been read as liturgical evidence** (Doc_02 §9 item 9). If that reading is done, it will very likely surface at least one term — a name for the rite of reconciliation itself, or for the baptismal rite as administered — that Doc_03's candidate discovery could not have found, because it swept for doctrine rather than rite. **Adding a term on that expectation would be invention; naming the gap is not.** This is the highest-value single addition available to this world's lexicon and it is blocked on a reading, not on a decision.
4. **`Source_Registry.md` is returned to independent review**, and every chunk's Key Sources cites it. No row this lexicon depends on is among those corrected on 2026-09-13.
5. **Doc_03's corpus counts are reproducible but their matching rule is unstated.** Doc_05 §11 item 14 established that the flock/shepherd/pastor figure of 113 reproduces exactly under markup-stripped substring matching and returns 109 counting the pastoral nouns alone. Every frequency figure quoted in these chunks is Doc_03's, and the same caution applies to all of them. Doc_03 is Approved to proceed and another thread's — **flagged, not edited from here.**
6. **The editorial-apparatus problem is corpus-wide and this lexicon is where it bites hardest.** `Lexicon_Deployment_Index.md` §7 registers five entries that rest near 19th-century editorial matter printed inside or beside the primary text. One of those — the volume preface calling Augustine's coercion doctrine *"a false exegesis"* and *"least satisfactory to Protestant readers"* — is a **Protestant editor's theological verdict sitting in the same volume as the text it judges**, and is precisely the kind of material that could reach a participant as this world's own voice if a later pass were less careful. Doc_05 §11 item 11 routed the corpus-wide question to the project lead; this document registers the five local instances and excludes all five.

CF V7.4 Part VII notes that Step 7's integration lenses — the affective and conceptual ones especially — depend on Tier 1 entries having reached genuine depth. **The seven Tier 1 entries are written at that depth and are not placeholders.** Two specific handoffs:

Doc_04 §7 item 2 and Doc_05 §6.7 both bound it: the axis is a real Supporting gravity with both formulas Documented, **and** there is no evidence it reached ordinary formation. A lens built on it would describe two bishops rather than a world.
