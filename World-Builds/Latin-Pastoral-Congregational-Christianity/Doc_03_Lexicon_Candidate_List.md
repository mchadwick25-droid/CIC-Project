| *lapsi* / "the lapsed" | This corpus's own English rendering for Carthaginian Christians who sacrificed, offered incense, or obtained a certificate of compliance during the Decian persecution (250) without actually sacrificing, and who subsequently became the subject of Cyprian's own penitential process for readmission to communion — the founding subject of *De Lapsis* and the crisis dominating Cyprian's own surviving correspondence | Cyprian-phase. ## Notes on scope and process

- **Terms considered and not included, with reasoning, so the exclusion is visible rather than silent.** *Donatist-specific vocabulary* (e.g. *traditor*, the *traditio* charge, Circumcellion) — excluded per Doc_02 §6's own routing note: characterizing this world's own opponents is Article 23's future concern, not Step 3's, and Donatism is a separate, sibling world (World #4) with its own native vocabulary this world's own lexicon must not absorb, the same discipline Doc_01 §7 applies to the corpus-map boundary itself. *Manichaean- and Pelagian-specific technical vocabulary* — not included, for a different reason than the Donatist exclusion: Doc_02 names these corpora's own existence (§1) but does not evidence any specific recurring term from either corpus being quoted or re-verified within this document's own drafting, the way the lapsed, conciliar, and communion vocabulary above is. Their absence here is an evidentiary gap in this document's own work so far, not a scope exclusion on Article 23 grounds — a future candidate-discovery pass reading the anti-Manichaean and anti-Pelagian corpora directly, rather than only noting their existence, may find real candidates there. *Tertullian's own coined vocabulary* — excluded as a Named Comparandum (Registry row 29): Tertullian is credited with forging the Latin theological vocabulary this world inherits, but is not himself this world's own voice (Doc_01 §7); drawing his own terms into this list would blur exactly the boundary Doc_01 and Doc_02 §1 both take care to hold. *Modern analytical labels* ("orthogonality to state power," a Caesaropapism-style category) — not this world's own vocabulary, consistent with the same exclusion Imperial-Juridical's own Doc_03 applies to its own comparable case.
- **Author Gravity risk pattern, named directly rather than left to be inferred from twelve separate entries.** Unlike a world whose risk is concentrated in vocabulary coined by a single interested advocate, this world's own dominant pattern is the one Doc_02 §6 already names at the narrative level, recurring here at the vocabulary level: nearly every term above is known *only* through one or the other bishop's own regulating, disciplining, or arguing voice — never through the lapsed believer's own account of what *lapsi* meant to live through, never through a lay believer's own account of communion or reconciliation, never through a rival's own first-person defense of a position Cyprian or Augustine argues against. The two-conciliar-formula pair (`"bishop of bishops"` / `"plenary Council"`) carries a second, narrower pattern on top of that one: each formula is spoken by the very bishop whose own institutional interest it serves, the same interested-advocate pattern Imperial-Juridical's own Doc_03 names for its own primacy vocabulary.
- **Tier 1 flagging, proportioned rather than asserted.** Six of twelve candidate terms are flagged Yes for Tier 1 consideration at Step 6 — a slight majority, not the "small relative to total" proportion a finished lexicon should show (Interpretive Lexicon Development Framework V2.1, Part III). This reflects this document's own deliberately central-terms-first candidate selection at the discovery stage, not a finished tiering decision: per the Framework's own instruction, tier assignment is Doc_06's work, informed by Doc_04's gravity findings and Doc_05's ecological reconstruction, neither of which exists yet. Heavy pruning is expected before Step 6, and this list should not be read as pre-committing to six Tier 1 entries.
- **Phase attribution, honestly mixed rather than a clean two-way split.** Four terms sit cleanly in one phase (`*lapsi*`, `*libelli*`, reconciliation, and "the one episcopate" in Cyprian's; "plenary Council" and "compel them to come in" in Augustine's); one pair (the two conciliar formulas) is deliberately paired across the phases as the two poles of one still-open axis rather than placed in either phase alone; and four (communion, catechumen/catechesis, schism, heresy) are named cross-phase, on evidence of different strength — communion and heresy on Doc_01's own named axes directly, catechesis on ordinary shared practice attested in both phases, schism on one phase's own treatise whose concept later recurs differently. "Suffrage" is cross-phase as a pattern but not as a single word, a distinction this document states rather than smooths over. This mixed picture is not a strand-plural finding — Doc_01 §5's strand-singular determination is not reopened by it — but it is itself a candidate observation for Doc_04's own gravity testing, in the same spirit Imperial-Juridical's own Doc_03 names its comparable mixed picture as a candidate observation rather than a settled result.
- Per the Framework's own instruction, terms requiring full Tier 1 treatment are flagged above for Doc_06; this document assigns no final tier classification of its own, since tiering depends on Doc_04's gravity findings and Doc_05's ecological reconstruction.
