## Candidate terms

Organized into **five** thematic clusters reflecting this world's own conceptual groupings (Interpretive Lexicon Development Framework V2.1 Part I), rather than alphabetically: the office/calling material sits within section A, and coercion and grace are folded into one section E.

Tags follow Part II's own two independent dimensions — Origin ([AS] Signature Vocabulary, [SC] Shared Vocabulary) and Risk/Function ([DR] High Distortion Risk, [TC] Technical Concept, [RT] Likely Runtime Term, [PV] Plural Voices, [CT] Contested Tradition) — assigned on each term's own demonstrated function during this discovery pass, not by default or for even coverage across categories. Three points on the tags themselves:

- **[CT] marks live scholarly contest within the relevant subfield specifically** — Part II's own definition — not in-world disagreement between Cyprian and Augustine, which this table's own Phase Attribution column and paired-entry structure (section C especially) already carry without a tag. On that definition, [CT] is applied below only to "compel them to come in" (Augustine's coercion doctrine and its reception are a live subject in patristic and political-theological scholarship), to "grace" (the Augustinian grace/free-will debate is likewise live), and to "schism," for the genuine, named, unresolved textual-scholarship question at row 3 (*De Unitate* 4–5's two-recension question). It is not applied to "bishop of bishops," "plenary Council," "heresy," or "the one episcopate," each of which is a site of in-world disagreement between the two bishops, or a withdrawn citation — real facts, but not what Part II's [CT] flags.
- **[PV] marks a term whose evidence sits within one of this world's own two phases specifically, not across the whole reconstructed ecology** — Part II's own definition, applied below to every single-phase term; it does not sit on "suffrage," which is a cross-phase pattern rather than a single-phase term.
- Origin and Risk/Function tags are applied to all eighteen terms below, on each term's own demonstrated function; *libelli* carries an Origin tag ([AS]) — see the Notes below — and the coverage claim at the header above and in the Notes is true as written for the current table.
