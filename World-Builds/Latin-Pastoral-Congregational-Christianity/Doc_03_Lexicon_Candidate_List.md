## Notes on scope and process

- **Terms considered and not included, with reasoning, so the exclusion is visible rather than silent.** *Donatist-specific vocabulary* (e.g. *traditor*, the *traditio* charge, Circumcellion) — excluded per Doc_02 §6's own routing note: characterizing this world's own opponents is Article 23's future concern, not Step 3's, and Donatism is a separate, sibling world (World #4) with its own native vocabulary this world's own lexicon must not absorb. **Opponent-characterizing vocabulary generally** (Donatist, Manichaean, and Pelagian alike) is excluded on that same routing note, which names all three together: "How this world's eventual Representative characterizes this world's own opponents — the Donatists, the Manichaeans, the Pelagians... is Article 23's future concern, not this section's." The operative distinction is not Donatist-versus-the-others; it is *opponent's own vocabulary* (excluded, Article 23) versus ***Augustine's own* vocabulary responding to the controversy** (Native where it appears in his own corpus, in scope here): "grace," above, is exactly this second kind, drawn from Registry row 23's own Licensed-For language. The Manichaean half of the same gravity candidate (row 22) remains a real discovery gap rather than a scope exclusion — no comparably specific term has yet been surfaced from that corpus by this document's own drafting — carried forward for a future pass, alongside the epidemic/mortality gap named below. *Tertullian's own vocabulary* — the exclusion is of Tertullian **as a source** (Registry row 29, Named Comparandum), not of any word merely because he coined it first; a term is a candidate on the strength of its use in this world's own Native corpus, whatever its coinage history, per the Interpretive Lexicon Development Framework's own Three Sources Principle, whose second category — "shared vocabulary common across Christian tradition broadly" — this world's corpus draws on constantly through Cyprian's and Augustine's own inherited idiom. No term on this list is currently known to rest on Tertullian's own coinage specifically; if one is found in a future pass, it is a candidate on that basis, not excluded by association. *Modern analytical labels* ("orthogonality to state power," a Caesaropapism-style category) — not this world's own vocabulary, consistent with the same exclusion Imperial-Juridical's own Doc_03 applies to its own comparable case. *Epidemic/mortality vocabulary* — Doc_01 §6 places epidemic disease (the Carthage plague, c. 249–262) in the Ongoing/External force cell, and Registry row 5 is licensed "specifically for the epidemic gravity" (*De Mortalitate*); considered, but no specific recurring term has yet been surfaced from this document's own drafting, named here as an open discovery gap rather than filled without evidence to ground it.
- **Discovery coverage, named rather than left implicit.** This pass swept the Native rows directly cited in Doc_02's own §1–§6 narrative and in this document's own term entries above (rows 1–5, 7, 9, 11–13, 15, 18–19, 22–23, 26, 43). It did not systematically sweep every one of the Registry's 212 rows for further candidate vocabulary, and does not claim to have reached the diminishing-returns threshold Interpretive Lexicon Development Framework V2.1 Part I names for a complete Candidate List: two of that Part's three completion inputs (Doc_04's gravities, Doc_05's Integrated Ecology Analysis) do not exist yet at this step, and the third (primary sources) has been swept only as far as Doc_02's own already-completed narrative reaches. A further pass reading rows not yet drawn on directly for vocabulary — row 42's own Zonaras material, and the newly-vendored original-language witnesses at rows 191–212 — may surface further candidates, including for the two gaps named directly above (the Manichaean half of the grace/purity gravity; epidemic/mortality vocabulary).
- **Author Gravity risk pattern, named directly rather than left to be inferred from fourteen separate entries.** Unlike a world whose risk is concentrated in vocabulary coined by a single interested advocate, this world's own dominant pattern is the one Doc_02 §6 already names at the narrative level, recurring here at the vocabulary level: nearly every term above is known almost entirely through one or the other bishop's own regulating, disciplining, or arguing voice. This is not, however, an absolute — the reconciliation entry's own Epistles XX–XXI (Row 1) and Pontius (Row 7) are two lay-confessor and one non-episcopal-clerical exception this document names directly rather than erases, and the pattern is that such exceptions are few, occasional, and themselves clerically or confessor-framed, not that no exception exists. The two-conciliar-formula pair (`"bishop of bishops"` / `"plenary Council"`) carries a second, narrower pattern on top of that one: each formula is spoken by the very bishop whose own institutional interest it serves, the same interested-advocate pattern Imperial-Juridical's own Doc_03 names for its own primacy vocabulary.
- **Tier 1 flagging, proportioned rather than asserted.** Ten of fourteen candidate terms are flagged Yes for Tier 1 consideration at Step 6 — a high proportion, not the "small relative to total" proportion a finished lexicon should show (Interpretive Lexicon Development Framework V2.1, Part III). This reflects this document's own deliberately central-terms-first candidate selection at the discovery stage, not a finished tiering decision: per the Framework's own instruction, tier assignment is Doc_06's work, informed by Doc_04's gravity findings and Doc_05's ecological reconstruction, neither of which exists yet. Heavy pruning is expected before Step 6, and this list should not be read as pre-committing to ten Tier 1 entries.
- **Phase attribution, stated precisely rather than smoothed into a clean split.** Of the fourteen terms above, eight sit in a single phase: five in Cyprian's ("the lapsed," *libelli*, reconciliation/penitential discipline, "bishop of bishops," "the one episcopate") and three in Augustine's ("plenary Council," "compel them to come in," grace). Six are cross-phase, on evidence of different strength: the flock and preaching/catechesis on ordinary shared practice attested in both phases; communion and heresy on Doc_01's own named axes directly; schism on one phase's own treatise whose concept later recurs differently; and "suffrage," cross-phase as a pattern but attested through different figures, different offices, and different words, a distinction this document states rather than smooths over. This mixed picture is not a strand-plural finding — Doc_01 §5's strand-singular determination is not reopened by it — but it is itself a candidate observation for Doc_04's own gravity testing, in the same spirit Imperial-Juridical's own Doc_03 names its comparable mixed picture as a candidate observation rather than a settled result.
- **Tags.** Interpretive Lexicon Development Framework V2.1 Part I states plainly that candidate-list-stage entries "should be tagged for classification (Part II)." Origin and Risk/Function tags are applied to all fourteen terms above, on each term's own demonstrated function rather than for even coverage; no [CT]-tagged term's Contest Type is written up, since that is Doc_06's own work. Whether every Doc_03 across the portfolio should carry Part II tags at the candidate-discovery stage, or whether the Framework's Part I/II language is properly read as a Step 6 obligation despite its own "at this stage" wording, is a portfolio-wide methodology question this world's build thread does not settle for other worlds — logged as an open item for a coach/System-Hub pass, the same routing Doc_01 §8 item 9 already uses for a comparable cross-world process question.
