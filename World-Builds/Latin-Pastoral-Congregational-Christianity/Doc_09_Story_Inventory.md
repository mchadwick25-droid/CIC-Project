## Section 1 — World Identification

**World name:** Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity. **World code:** `lpc`.
**Time horizon:** c. 246–430 CE. **Geographic centres:** Carthage (Africa Proconsularis) and Hippo Regius (Numidia).
**Anchor figures:** Cyprian, bishop of Carthage 248/249–258; Augustine, presbyter at Hippo 391, bishop 395/396, died 430.

**The structural fact that governs this document more than any other.** This world is **two attested phases separated by a 133-year silence in its own record** (Doc_08 §1). **Every story below comes from one phase or the other; none comes from the interval, and none can.** Doc_02 §7 records that no primary source named at §1 and no row in `Source_Registry.md` dates from within it. §7 below treats that as an absence to be named rather than a gap to be filled.

**The gravity spine each story is assessed against (Doc_04 §4).** Primary — **G1** Pastoral Office as Territorial Flock-Keeping, **G2** Penitential Discipline, **G3** Collegial Communion Preserved Despite Disagreement, **G6** Sacramental and Ordination Validity. Supporting — **G4** Preaching and Catechesis, **G5** Conciliar Authority Theory, **G7** Grace and Human Incapacity. Tensional — **G8** Confessor-Authority vs. Episcopal-Regulated Peace.

## Section 2 — The Four Tiers, Re-Confirmed at Source

**Quoted from Construction Framework V7.4's own Part II text, opened for this document rather than carried from a sibling build.** The skill governing this step warns in terms that *"a skill that hardcodes last year's categories is exactly the kind of drift this project's own history has already been burned by,"* so the definitions below are the Framework's own words, and the confidence bands are its own too.

- **Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative.** *"Direct textual attestation within or close to the world's horizon. Named author with identifiable social location. Datable with reasonable confidence. The story claims to describe specific events or persons and the claim is historically credible with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective and genre."* Confidence: **Documented to Widely Accepted at the narrative level** — and CF continues, in a sentence an earlier version of this section cut: *"May carry Widely Accepted or Contested confidence for specific details within the narrative depending on the author's access and perspective."* Use: *"may be offered as historical narrative with the author and perspective named."* **[CORRECTED, 2026-09-15 — Round 2:** the trim removed CF's own instrument for stepping confidence within Tier 1, which is the route `lpcstory002` actually takes — and this document then escalated a governance question on the ground that CF offered no route.**]**
- **Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material.** *"Stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory… The collection itself is evidence of what the community remembered and valued."* Confidence: **Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction.**
- **Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition.** *"Material attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition rather than direct documentation… Hagiographic narrative is a specific type within this tier: stories shaped by recognizable hagiographic conventions — the idealized portrait of a saint's life, the miracle sequence, the death as completion of a formed life."* Confidence: **Contested for the general portrait *or attribution*; Inferential/Thin for specific details shaped by hagiographic convention** *or for events whose historical occurrence cannot be verified*. **[CORRECTED, 2026-09-15 — Round 1's C1:** the confidence line was trimmed at two points without ellipsis.**]** Use: *"The formation ideal is the evidence, not the biographical detail."*
- **Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** *"A composite reconstruction of what regularly happened within the ecology — not a story about a specific named individual… Every element must be traceable to attested evidence within the world's horizon."* Confidence: **Inferential/Thin regardless of how well-sourced the individual elements are.**
- **No Tier 5.** *"Generated or illustrative narrative — stories invented to illuminate formation truths but not traceable to the world's own evidence — is not permitted within this framework. If the evidence does not support a story, the story does not exist for this world. Silence in thin areas is the right response."*

**The distinction this world's evidence forces, and it is the central tier judgement in this document.** Pontius the Deacon was **an eyewitness with an identifiable social location** — Cyprian's own deacon, writing shortly after the martyrdom — which is Tier 1's shape. He was also writing **the first Christian biography, in hagiographic form**, which is Tier 3's. **Doc_02 §4 flags the genre risk by name**, and `Source_Registry.md` rows 99, 159 and 212 license Delehaye's genre scholarship precisely for it. **This build's resolution is to assign the tier per story rather than per source**: where Pontius reports something corroborated outside his own frame, or carries none of CF's three named markers, the story is Tier 1; where the narrative carries them — the death narrated as completion, a providential intervention at the climax — it is Tier 3, and says so. **A source is not a tier.**

**That rule is this build's construction, not the Construction Framework's, and it is escalated rather than asserted. [ADDED, 2026-09-15 — Round 1's M6; AMENDED at Round 2, and the amendment narrows it.]** An earlier version of this paragraph said CF *"does not state how to tier a source that satisfies one tier's author test and another's genre test simultaneously."* **That overstated the gap, and it did so because this document had trimmed CF's own text.** CF's Tier 1 definition requires historical credibility *"with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective **and genre**"* — genre is written into Tier 1 — and its confidence rule allows a Tier 1 narrative to carry stepped confidence *"for specific details… depending on the author's access and perspective."* **So CF does supply a route, and `lpcstory002` takes it.** What CF still does not state is the harder case: **where the genre shaping reaches the events themselves rather than the author's estimate of his subject**, as at `lpcstory006`, whose typology is applied to what physically happened. **That narrower question is what is escalated.** **The rule adopted here would govern how every world in the portfolio tiers an eyewitness hagiographer**, and Pontius is not a rare case — the genre is the normal form for a bishop's death in this period. **Routed to the project lead as a governance/methodology item at the Disposition; applied here because a tier had to be assigned, and disclosed as a local construction rather than a citation.**

### 3.1 By-Tier View

- **Tier 1 — six stories:** `lpcstory001`–`005`, `007`.

What it produced instead was **a correspondence** — and a correspondence is not a collection in the Framework's sense. **What Doc_08 §2B-5 does establish, from Cyprian's own words, is that he forwarded a thirteen-letter dossier of his own correspondence** (*"these thirteen letters sent forth at various times… which I have transmitted to you"*). **Whether the corpus as a whole was assembled in his lifetime or after it is not something this build has established, and the earlier wording asserted it. [CORRECTED, 2026-09-15 — Round 1's M7.]** The Tier 2 finding does not depend on the answer: what Tier 2 requires is a *community's* remembered collection with its own transmission history, and this world has no such collection either way. **The absence is a fact about what kind of world this was**: it transmitted itself by letter and treatise, not by remembered story.

## Section 7 — Absent Stories

**The question CF V7.4 requires: what stories can this world not tell, and why?** Article 20's affirmative duty governs this section — the absences below are named because naming them is owed, not because the list is short.

**1. There is no story from inside the 133-year silence, and there cannot be.** This is the absence that distinguishes this world from every other in the portfolio. Between Cyprian's martyrdom in 258 and Augustine's ordination in 391, **this world tells itself no stories at all** — not because nothing happened, but because nothing survives. Doc_08 §3B-2 records that the only thing crossing the gap is a text: Augustine reading Cyprian's conciliar acts and arguing with them. **A world whose middle third is narratively silent cannot produce a continuous story of itself**, and any Representative built on this material must be able to say so rather than bridging it.

**2. No story survives in the voice of an ordinary believer.** Every story above is told by a bishop, a bishop's deacon, or a confessor — men with a claim to authority in the community and a reason to write. Doc_02 §6 and Doc_07 §2B both find that what this world's record lacks is not a hostile mediator but **any ordinary congregant writing about ordinary congregational life as such.** `lpcstory005` comes closest and does not reach it: Celerinus and Lucian are confessors, a recognised status. **We have no story of a Sunday from someone who merely attended one.**

**3. The lapsed have no story of their own.** This world's central first-phase crisis is about people who sacrificed under persecution — and **not one of them left an account of why.** They appear as a category in Cyprian's letters, as petitioners at the confessors' doors, as a problem to be adjudicated. Celerinus's sister, in `lpcstory005`, is the closest this corpus comes to a named lapsed person with a history — **and she is described entirely by her brother, in the third person, as a grief he is enduring.** She does not speak. The world's most consequential formation question is documented exclusively from the side of those who did not fail it.

**4. Women appear in these stories and never narrate them.** Numidicus's wife burns; his daughter searches the ashes and finds her father alive. Numeria and Candida are discussed, weighed, and dispatched to peace by two men writing to each other. **All four are visible; none is audible — and all four are Phase One. [EXTENDED, 2026-09-15 — Round 1's L12.]** The second phase does not break the pattern, and the two strongest candidates for breaking it make the point sharper: **Doc_02 §6 records that Albina and the community of nuns at Hippo are known only through Augustine's own framing of them**, in letters he wrote about them rather than words they wrote themselves. **The absence is not an artefact of Cyprian's corpus. It holds across both phases and 180 years.** Doc_02 §6 records that named women do appear in this corpus — the absence named here is narrower and sharper: no woman in this world's horizon left a narrative of her own. **The one North African woman who did — Perpetua — sits outside the horizon by four decades**, which is a boundary fact with a real cost, and §6 item 1 is where it is felt.

**5. There is no story of an ordinary, uneventful pastorate.** Every story here is a crisis: an election under pressure, a plague, a torture, a ransom, a schism, an execution, a deathbed. **The daily work that Doc_04 makes this world's Primary gravity — a bishop keeping a flock, week after week, when nothing is happening — is the one thing no story records**, because nobody writes it down. This is the gap Tier 4 reconstruction exists for, and it is why this world's Tier 4 material sits in Doc_05 rather than here.
