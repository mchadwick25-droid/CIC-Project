## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing governs the order here: *"This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."*

### A1 — The floor

Article 4's floor tests whether a movement's own confession affirms, in its plain historical sense, the content drawn from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (381) — full divinity and consubstantiality of Christ, true humanity, the Passion/resurrection/ascension/return, and the Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified with the Father and Son.

Augustine (354–430) is squarely post-Constantinopolitan and is, on any account of the Latin theological tradition, among the two or three most consequential expositors of Nicene-Constantinopolitan orthodoxy the West produced — *De Trinitate* is the mature Latin statement of Nicene trinitarian theology; the Enchiridion, the Sermon to Catechumens on the Creed, and the mass of catechetical and preaching material vendored in this build's own source corpus (NPNF1, 8 volumes) affirm the full floor directly, repeatedly, and as the ordinary substance of his pastoral teaching, not as a contested or qualified position. **A1 clears directly for Augustine's own phase of this world.**

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements: Cyprian's own phase, tested on its own terms

Cyprian's episcopate (248/249–258 CE) falls entirely before Nicaea (325); he cannot be tested against a creed that did not yet exist for him, so A2's continuity test governs his own phase: does his surviving confession affirm, in the less technical language available to him, the substance Article 4 states, showing continuity with the strand that became the Nicene consensus rather than divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ecclesiastes-style subordinationist, or similar)?

Cyprian is about as clean a continuity case as A2 will ever see. *De Unitate Ecclesiae*, *De Dominica Oratione*, the *Testimonia*, and the letters (all vendored, ANF05) show a christology and theology proper continuous with — and directly formative of — the developing Western consensus: no denial of Christ's divinity, no dualist cosmology, no docetic Christ. His own theological vocabulary is a direct forerunner of the Latin tradition Augustine inherits and writes within (Augustine's own *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* argues at length through the acts of Cyprian's own baptismal councils, treating Cyprian's authority as a live, internal datum of the same tradition, not an outside or rival source). **A2 clears directly for Cyprian's own phase.**

### The century-gap question, honestly held open

This is the genuinely harder question this world's own portfolio entry already surfaced and did not resolve, and Step 0 confirms rather than pretends to close it. Unlike World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity), whose 312–451 span is one continuous trajectory later split for testing purposes only by the 325 date-line, this world's own two anchor figures are separated by a real, roughly century-long historical gap (Cyprian d. 258; Augustine b. 354) — an interval Donatism (World #4, Selected, Not Yet Built on this branch) actively occupies and contests, claiming its own inheritance from the same Carthaginian see and the same Cyprianic baptismal controversy.

The Step 0 Conclusion itself already named this precisely, without resolving it: *"Constitutional ambiguity flagged, not resolved. Article 3 (Formation-World Principle) requires 'sufficient historical coherence' but does not define whether a formation-type may recur across a temporal gap with a rival claimant contesting the interval (Cyprian/Augustine, with Donatism in between), or whether it requires continuous, unbroken community existence. Checked directly against the Constitution's actual text and found genuinely undefined. Logged as a standing interpretive question, likely to recur."*

Section A itself does not actually require this question to be answered before eligibility clears, on two independent grounds:

1. **Each phase clears its own applicable test on its own terms**, as shown above — A1 for Augustine, A2 for Cyprian — independent of whether the two phases are ultimately read as one continuous world, two strands of one world, or (a reading this document does not adopt, but names for completeness) two adjacent seeds that happen to share a Step 0 Conclusion entry. There is no confessional content in either phase that fails Article 4; the open question is about historical coherence (Article 3), not doctrinal floor (Article 4).
2. **A5 itself routes this exact shape of question downstream, not to Section A.** *"Where a movement contains multiple internal strands under Article 21, eligibility is assessed at the world level against its established strands as a whole... never strand-by-strand in a way that lets one divergent internal strand exclude an otherwise-eligible world."* Whether Cyprian's phase and Augustine's phase are formally two Article-21 strands of one world, or the Article 3 coherence question needs a different answer entirely, is Doc_01's own Strand Determination and World Continuity & Distinction work — explicitly Step 1's job per the Construction Framework's own text ("Strand is a finding, never a presupposed schema... This determination is made at Step 1"), not Step 0's.

**Resolution for this world:** Section A clears on both phases' own confessional terms; the standing Article 3 ambiguity the Step 0 Conclusion already logged is neither created nor closed by this confirmation — it is carried forward, explicitly, as binding on Doc_01 (§4 below), exactly as the Step 0 Conclusion itself already flagged it, and this document adds no new methodology question beyond re-confirming that existing one on the record.

### A3 — Contemporary movements and interpretive fidelity

Not applicable. This is a 3rd/4th/5th-century historical movement, not a contemporary confessional community reinterpreting Nicene vocabulary non-literally.

### A4 — Hand-selected adjacent inclusion

Not invoked and not needed. Both phases clear A1/A2 directly on their own confessional terms; no divergence from Article 4's five commitments needs to be waived.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

Beyond the strand-routing already discussed above, this section's other function — historical rivals are not separately screened, but appear within an included world's own reconstruction (Article 23) — bears directly on how this world must handle Donatism. Donatism is itself a Selected, Not Yet Built world in its own right (World #4), not a rival excluded from Section A; the two worlds share Carthage, share Cyprian's own baptismal-controversy inheritance as contested patrimony, and share the 3rd–5th century North African horizon. The Step 0 Conclusion's own boundary is explicit and binding here: this world (#8) holds "the ordinary pastor navigating persecution" and "ordinary sacramental administration," never "the schism-crisis angle," which belongs to World #4. This world's own Doc_02 will need to state this boundary in its own terms (not merely cite World #6's precedent), since — unlike World #6's relationship to the not-yet-built World #8 at the time IJC's Step 0 was drafted — Donatism is *also* not yet built on this branch (it is, per this session's own discovery, under active parallel construction on a sibling branch not visible to this working tree), so no live cross-document check is possible; the boundary is logged here as binding on Doc_01/Doc_02, to be reconciled against Donatism's own build when the two branches are eventually merged.

Article 20's affirmative duty (marginalized voices within an included movement) and Article 21's strand determination are both explicitly Step 1 work per the Framework's own text — flagged forward to Doc_01, not resolved here.

### Section A conclusion

**World #8 clears Section A.** Augustine's own phase clears A1 directly on its own confessional terms; Cyprian's own phase clears A2 directly on its own confessional terms; A3 is inapplicable; A4 is not needed; A5 correctly routes both the century-gap/strand question and the Donatism boundary into this world's own Doc_01/Doc_02 work rather than treating either as a Section A eligibility question.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Test: does enough independent, checkable evidence survive to support a defensible reconstruction at Documented or Widely Accepted confidence for its core claims, with the source-asymmetry named rather than hidden?

Exceptionally strong pass — among the very strongest in the entire nine-world portfolio, and arguably the strongest of any world confirmed so far, by sheer documentary volume:

- **Cyprian**: the complete surviving corpus — some 82 letters (the central documentary record of mid-3rd-century North African church life: the Decian persecution, the lapsed controversy, plague, rebaptism, schism), *De Unitate*, *De Lapsis*, *De Mortalitate*, *De Dominica Oratione*, *Ad Demetrianum*, *Ad Fortunatum*, the *Testimonia*, Pontius the Deacon's *Life and Passion of Cyprian* (often counted the earliest Christian biography), and the acts of his own councils on rebaptism (256 CE, the sententiae of 87 bishops) — all vendored in full (`cic/texts/anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml`), public domain, ANF standard edition.
- **Augustine**: the complete NPNF Series I set (8 volumes, all vendored: `npnf101`–`npnf108`) — the *Confessions*, *City of God*, *On Christian Doctrine*, *On the Holy Trinity*, the Enchiridion, *On the Catechising of the Uninstructed* (his own manual for catechetical formation), ~97 sermons preached to his own congregations at Hippo and Carthage, the *Enarrationes in Psalmos* (~695,000 words, the single largest body in the vendored corpus, preached and dictated over nearly three decades), 168 letters, the tractates on John, the homilies on 1 John, and the doctrinal/anti-Manichaean/anti-Donatist/anti-Pelagian treatises.
- A **pre-existing, detailed per-work source-assignment already exists** for this exact world (`cic/corpus-map/latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity.yaml`, produced 2026-08-26 during this project's own corpus-partition work, independent of and prior to this Step 0 confirmation), mapping every relevant work to its vendored file and locus, with confidence ratings and cross-world boundary notes already reasoned through for the shared Cyprian/Donatism material. This gives this world's own Doc_02 an unusually mature starting inventory.
- Pontius's *Life of Cyprian* and Cyprian's own letters together supply the same "formation narrative source" category (Framework Part II) that strengthened Hieronymian and Desert Monasticism's own B1 scores.

Source-asymmetry disclosure, carried forward as binding on Doc_02 rather than resolved here: both anchor voices are bishops — literate, elite, institutionally central. The world's own subject matter (ordinary congregational and lay life) is attested almost entirely *through* episcopal preaching and correspondence *about* that lay life, not through lay voices themselves — the general portfolio-wide skew (elite/literate/male/urban) applies here in a specific, sharpened form: even the "ordinary believer" material in this world is mediated entirely through the bishop's own pulpit and pen. Doc_02 must name this specifically.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

Test: can this candidate plausibly support most or all eight ecology lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only?

Strong pass, and — unlike World #6's own honestly-logged thinness in ordinary Formation Ecology — this is plausibly the *richest* ordinary-believer, ordinary-worship, ordinary-formation world in the confirmed portfolio to date. Augustine's sermons to his own congregation, his catechetical manual, his letters of pastoral consolation and administration, and Cyprian's letters written from hiding during active persecution to a frightened, plague-stricken, schism-torn congregation are direct, first-person, lived-community evidence — not institutional-power argument at one remove, and not elite scholarly formation at one remove either (contrast World #2 Alexandria and World #9 Hieronymian's own more textual-scholarly registers). Worship Integration (catechesis, the Creed taught to catechumens, baptismal practice, the Eucharist implicit throughout), Formation Ecology (the *De Lapsis* penitential process; *On the Catechising of the Uninstructed*), Meaning Transmission (preaching itself, as the world's own primary mode), Emotional/Affective reconstruction (Cyprian's *De Mortalitate* during plague; the *Confessions*' own interior register), and Authority Structures (episcopal office, exercised pastorally rather than juridically) are all richly attested. Likely thinner: Material Culture and daily domestic practice (as with most literary-source-dominated worlds), and non-elite voices in their own words (per B1's disclosure above).

**Passes B2 comfortably**, with the disclosure above logged forward as binding on Doc_02/Doc_05.

### B3 — Uniqueness (differentiation)

Test: judged relative to what is already selected or built for the phase, not in the abstract.

- **Versus World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, built):** already confirmed distinct in the Step 0 Conclusion and independently re-affirmed in IJC's own Step 0 confirmation (§3 of that document) — "non-overlapping authority structure, orthogonality to state power, temporal overlap rather than sequence." This world's bishops exercise authority through office, sacrament, and territorial care of an ordinary flock; World #6's actors exercise authority through courts, emperors, and imperial law. Live cross-check possible now that IJC is built: no material overlap found in a scan of IJC's own Doc_01/Doc_02 — Cyprian and Augustine (as pastoral, congregational figures) do not appear as IJC actors; Augustine appears in IJC's own source corpus only glancingly, via `Confessions` 9.7 (Ambrose's antiphonal singing, `ijc.source.augustine-confessions`), a single scene bearing on Ambrose's own liturgical innovation at Milan, not on Augustine's own pastoral office at Hippo — a different figure's story, not this world's. **Confirmed distinct, on a live check.**
- **Versus World #9 (Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity, built):** already confirmed distinct in the Step 0 Conclusion by the original World #8/#9 split itself (Jerome removed from this world precisely because his own gravity — charismatic-scholarly authority, voluntary aristocratic patronage, textual/translation work — was found genuinely distinct from Cyprian's and Augustine's episcopal, territorial, congregational gravity). This is the single most load-bearing differentiation finding in this world's own history, since it *is* the reason this world has its current shape. No live overlap check needed beyond noting it: Jerome does not appear in this world's own corpus-map assignment.
- **Versus World #4 (Donatism, Selected, Not Yet Built):** the sharpest, most consequential adjacency in this world's portfolio. Both worlds share Cyprian's own Carthage and, per the corpus-map's own provisional rows (the Council of Carthage under Cyprian on rebaptism; the anonymous anti-Novatianist and anti-rebaptism treatises), contested inheritance of the same 3rd-century baptismal controversy. The Step 0 Conclusion's own boundary — this world holds Cyprian "navigating the Decian persecution and its aftermath, not the schism-crisis angle" — is the load-bearing line and is carried forward as binding on Doc_01/Doc_02 (§2 above, §4 below). Not built yet on this branch (under active parallel construction elsewhere per this session's own discovery), so no live cross-document check is possible; flagged for reconciliation when branches merge.
- **Versus World #5 (Cappadocian Nicene Pastoral-Monastic Tradition, Selected, Not Yet Built) and World #2 (Alexandria, built):** distinct on language (Latin vs. Greek), region (North Africa vs. Anatolia/Alexandria), and formation logic (episcopal-congregational-pastoral vs. trinitarian-ascetic-pastoral for #5; exegetical-philosophical-catechetical for #2). No meaningful overlap risk; not elaborated further here since no adjacency this close was flagged in the Step 0 Conclusion for this pairing.

**Passes B3 clearly.** World #4 is the one adjacency requiring active vigilance during construction, named here so Doc_01/Doc_02 treat it as a live, currently-unreconciled boundary question.

### B4 — User needs (audience fit)

Test: does this candidate offer something distinctive to several of the Essential Experience document's named audiences, honestly noted rather than claimed universally.

Strong, broad fit — plausibly the broadest audience fit of any world confirmed to date, precisely because its register is ordinary pastoral life rather than institutional power (World #6) or elite ascetic-scholarly formation (World #9). *Curious seekers* and *thoughtful believers* have direct access to preaching, catechesis, and pastoral consolation amid plague and persecution — material that speaks to lived faith rather than doctrinal argument alone. *Pastors and teachers* have an unusually direct fit: this world's own anchor figures are, definitionally, working pastors, and Augustine's own catechetical manual and sermons are pastoral-formation material in the most literal sense. *Historically interested learners* and *academically minded users* are served by the sheer documentary depth (B1). *Deconstructing Christians* may find real material in Cyprian's and Augustine's own honesty about doubt, sin, and institutional failure (the *Confessions*; *De Lapsis*'s own reckoning with a church that failed under pressure) — not overstated, but a genuine point of contact this world's own sources support.

**Passes B4 strongly**, honestly scoped per the Methodology's own instruction not to maximize this criterion.

### B5 — Scale (tiebreak only)

Carthage was, for most of this world's span, the largest and most consequential Latin Christian see after Rome itself; Hippo Regius was a substantial provincial city. A modest, non-decisive positive factor per the Methodology's own instruction that Scale is a tiebreak note only.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Against the tiering rubric (Tier 1 — clears Section A directly, clears Sourcing and Ecology comfortably, offers genuine differentiation, serves multiple named audiences): this world clears Section A directly on both phases' own terms (§2), clears Sourcing exceptionally (B1, arguably the strongest sourcing base of any world confirmed to date), clears Ecology comfortably and with unusually broad lens coverage (B2), offers genuine differentiation with one adjacency (World #4) actively flagged for reconciliation rather than assumed settled (B3), and serves multiple named audiences strongly (B4). **World #8 confirms as Tier 1 — Strong seed.** This matches its de facto portfolio-level standing in the Step 0 Conclusion (selected outright, not deferred or excluded).
