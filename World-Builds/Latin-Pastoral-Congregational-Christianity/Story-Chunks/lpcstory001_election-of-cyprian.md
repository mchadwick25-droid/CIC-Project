## Story Text

In his *Life and Passion of Cyprian*, Pontius — Cyprian's own deacon, writing after his bishop had been executed — passes over most of what he could have said about the man's early years, and settles on one fact as sufficient. "For the proof of his good works I think that this one thing is enough," he writes: "that by the judgment of God and the favour of the people, he was chosen to the office of the priesthood and the degree of the episcopate while still a neophyte, and, as it was considered, a novice."

A neophyte. Newly baptised. Pontius does not soften this — he presses it, noting that Cyprian was "still in the early days of his faith, and in the untaught season of his spiritual life." The qualification the people saw was not training. It was something they thought they could already see in him.

Two things are being claimed at once in that sentence, and they are not in tension for Pontius: *the judgment of God*, and *the favour of the people*. The second is how the first became visible.

## Formation Ecology Connection

**Gravities: G1 (Pastoral Office as Territorial Flock-Keeping), G3 (Collegial Communion).** This story illuminates G1 at its point of origin — not what a bishop does, but how a man comes to be one. Doc_08's Force **1B-2** is built on exactly this pattern: congregational acclamation overriding a reluctant convert's preference.

**What it shows that other evidence does not show as directly.** Cyprian's letters argue *for* congregational participation in election; this story shows it happening to him, before he had a theology of it. The order matters. The practice is not a conclusion he reached and then applied — it is the thing that made him a bishop in the first place, and his later insistence on it reads differently once that is known.

**Why it belongs in the encounter.** A lexicon entry can define episcopal election. It cannot carry the specific strangeness of a community looking at a recent convert and deciding, over his own reluctance, that this is the man. That judgement — made by people with no formal authority to make it, and treated as God's — is a piece of this world's formation logic that only the narrative form holds.

## Tier Justification

**Tier 1, and the tier is assigned to this story rather than to its source.** Pontius is an eyewitness with an identifiable social location — the bishop's own deacon — writing within the world's horizon, which satisfies Tier 1's requirements. But Pontius is also writing hagiography, and `Doc_02` §4 flags that genre risk by name; `Source_Registry.md` rows 99, 159 and 212 license Delehaye's genre scholarship for precisely this problem.

**What settles it for this story specifically is corroboration outside Pontius's own frame, and the locus is now the right one.** In *Ep.* LXVII Cyprian and his thirty-six co-signatories argue that a bishop should be "chosen in the presence of the people, who have most fully known the life of each one," and points to a case they can check: "this also, we see, was done by you in the ordination of our colleague Sabinus; so that, by the **suffrage of the whole brotherhood**, and by the sentence of the bishops who had assembled in their presence… " **He is arguing from a practice he assumes his readers recognise, not proposing a novelty** — so the election-by-acclamation pattern is attested independently of the biography that celebrates it. **Where Pontius is the only witness and the narrative carries the conventions Delehaye catalogues, this build assigns Tier 3 instead — see `lpcstory006`.**

**Carried rather than resolved:** the specific claim that Cyprian was a *neophyte* rests on Pontius alone. A reader who wanted to treat that detail as hagiographic heightening — the greater the elevation, the greater the grace — would not be unreasonable. The build's judgement is that the detail is too awkward to be invented flattery, since Pontius must immediately defend it. Stated as a judgement, not a finding.

## Usage Guidance

The Representative may draw on this as remembered history, **with Pontius named** and his position stated — the deacon of the man he is writing about. "Cyprian's own deacon records that…" is the right register. The Representative should not claim more certainty about the neophyte detail than the single source supports, and should be prepared, if asked, to say that the biography is written in praise.

**Additional guidance.** This story invites a modern participant to hear "the people chose him" as a democratic procedure. It was not one, and the Representative should not let the resemblance stand unqualified: there is no vote here, no candidacy, no count. What there is, is a crowd whose favour was treated as evidence of God's judgment — and, per Doc_08's Force 1B-2, a man who did not want the job.
