## Story Text

*This is how this world's own tradition remembered the death of its first great bishop — an account written by his deacon, in the form of a saint's life, showing what the community believed a life fully given to the pastoral office could become. It is offered here as that portrait, not as a report of what a bystander would have seen.*

In Pontius's telling, the crowd that came out for the execution could not all see. The ground beyond was open, and "by the extent of the space beyond, the view was not attainable to the confused crowd" — so people climbed. "Persons who favoured him had climbed up into the branches of the trees."

Pontius tells the reader what that resembles. It was so, he writes, "that there might not even be wanting to him (what happened in the case of Zacchæus), that he was gazed upon from the trees."

Then the bishop bound his own eyes. "Having with his own hands bound his eyes, he tried to hasten the slowness of the executioner." The executioner could not do it — the man "with difficulty clasped the blade in his failing right hand with trembling fingers," and needed, in Pontius's account, help from above: "until the mature hour of glorification strengthened the hand of the centurion with power granted from above."

The tradition's portrait, then, is of a man more composed than the soldier killing him, in a crowd arranged like a scene from the Gospel — a life that ends the way the community believed a formed life ends: ready before anyone else in the clearing is.

## Formation Ecology Connection

**Gravities: G1 (Pastoral Office as Territorial Flock-Keeping), G8 (Confessor-Authority).** Doc_08's Force 3A-1 and the whole external column press through the bond between one bishop and one congregation. This account is that bond at its terminus — and the detail that carries it is not the composure, it is *the crowd*. They came. They climbed trees to see. The territorial flock is physically present at its shepherd's death.

**What it shows that other evidence does not show as directly.** Cyprian's letters argue that the bishop is answerable for the flock; Doc_04 makes that a Primary gravity. What no letter can show is **what the community did with the completed life afterwards** — and that is what a hagiography is evidence of. The formation ideal here is specific and unusual: not visions, not miracles at the grave, but *readiness*, and a shepherd watched by his own people to the end.

**Why it belongs in the encounter.** Per CF V7.4, the formation ideal communicated is the evidence; the events are not. What this world believed formation produced is visible here in a way no treatise states.

## Tier Justification

**Tier 3, and the tier turns on genre rather than on the author.** Pontius meets Tier 1's author test — eyewitness deacon, identifiable location, within the horizon — and `lpcstory001` and `lpcstory002` assign him Tier 1 on that basis. **This story is different, on the face of the text.**

**The conventions Delehaye catalogues are visible and Pontius points at one himself.** The Zacchaeus parallel is not the reader's inference; it is an authorial aside, the account telling us it is patterned on Scripture. The executioner's failing hand strengthened "with power granted from above" is a providential intervention at the climax. The death is narrated as the completion of a formed life — which is, in CF V7.4's own words, one of the three named markers of hagiographic narrative, alongside "the idealized portrait of a saint's life" and "the miracle sequence."

**`Source_Registry.md` rows 99, 159 and 212 license Delehaye's genre scholarship for exactly this assessment**, and row 159 records the further hazard — the later conflation of Cyprian of Carthage with Cyprian of Antioch — which is what an unguarded hagiographic tradition does to a figure over time.

**Tier disagreement carried at full strength rather than resolved quietly.** A reasonable builder could argue Tier 1 here: an eyewitness's account of a public execution in a known place on a known date, with the typology as ornament on a real event. **This build's judgement is that once the account tells you it is patterning itself on Scripture, the specific details can no longer be separated from the pattern by a reader who has no independent witness — and this build has no independent witness, because the *Acta* is unread.** Reading it would likely move this story to Tier 1. Until then, Tier 3 is the honest position, and it is a judgement rather than a finding.

## Absent Story Note

**What cannot be told is what the execution looked like to anyone who was not already devoted to him.** Every surviving account of Cyprian's death comes from inside his own community — Pontius, his deacon, writing in praise, and the *Acta Proconsularia*, a record kept by the court that killed him. **There is no bystander.** The crowd in the trees is described only by a man who believed what they had come to see; not one of the people in those branches left a word. **So the most public event in this world's first phase has no public witness**, and a participant who asks what an ordinary Carthaginian made of it should be told that the question cannot be answered from this world's record.

## Usage Guidance

The Representative may offer this as **the tradition's own account of what a formed life looks like**, with the hagiographic character acknowledged from the outset — "this is how Cyprian's own community remembered his death." **The specific events are not offered as historical fact.** The Representative does not claim that the executioner's hand was strengthened from above; it reports that this is how the tradition told it.

**Additional guidance.** If a participant asks "did that really happen?", the honest answer has three parts and the Representative should give all three: that Cyprian was certainly executed under Valerian in 258; that the account we have is written in praise by his own deacon, in a form that patterns deaths on Scripture; and that **a strictly documentary record of the trial does exist and this build has not read it.** Saying the third part is what keeps the first two honest.
