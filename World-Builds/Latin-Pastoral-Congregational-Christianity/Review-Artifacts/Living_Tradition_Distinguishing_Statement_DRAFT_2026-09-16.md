## 1. Why `lpc`'s case is structurally easier than Alexandria's

Alexandria has **one** living heir, the Coptic Orthodox Church, and the distinction has to separate a fourth-century world from a specific present-day communion that traces itself to it.

**`lpc` has no single heir, and that is the whole answer.** This world sits **upstream of every division in the Western church**. Datus is bounded to *c.* 246–430: he predates Chalcedon, the Great Schism, the Reformation, Trent and Vatican II. **He cannot be asked which side he is on, because no side he could be on had yet come into being.** The temporal boundedness does most of the work that Alexandria has to do by careful drafting.

The risk is therefore not that Datus will speak for a communion. It is the opposite: that a participant will **hear their own communion in him** and take his world as that communion's warrant. The statement below is built against that.

## 2. Draft statement — for adoption, redline or rejection

> ### Living Tradition Status — Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity (`lpc`)
>
> **Living tradition correspondence: YES.** This world's reconstruction touches traditions that continue into the present, diffusely and widely rather than through one nameable heir.
>
> **Which present-day tradition(s):** **No single tradition is this world's heir, and that is a finding rather than an evasion.** `lpc` is ancestral to the Western church *before its divisions*. The Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed, Methodist and free-church traditions all descend in part from this material; the Orthodox communions share its pre-schism episcopal pattern and Cyprian himself. **Every Western communion has a claim on this world and none has an exclusive one.** No present-day body may be presented as *the* continuation of it, and the reconstruction speaks for the fifth-century world alone.
>
> **Key divergences — where a present-day tradition does not follow this world:**
> - **Rebaptism (G6).** Cyprian held that baptism outside the one church is no baptism, and required converts from schism to be rebaptised. **The Western church rejected his position**; Augustine's — valid though unfruitful — became the settled one and is why baptisms are not repeated between communions today. A communion that venerates Cyprian does not follow him here.
> - **Penance (G2).** This world's practice was public, graded and bishop-administered. The Roman Catholic sacrament of Penance and Reconciliation descends from it and is not identical to it; the Reformation traditions rejected it as a sacrament. **Neither present-day position is this world's practice.**
> - **Grace (G7).** Augustine's doctrine is foundational to Catholic, Lutheran and Reformed self-understandings, **which do not agree about what it means.** This world is claimed by parties to a disagreement it predates.
> - **Conciliar authority (G5).** Cyprian's refusal that any bishop is *"a bishop of bishops"* is read very differently by communions that hold papal primacy and those that do not. **This world states the refusal; it does not settle the modern question.**
>
> **Figures and practices of contested standing (Article 29, second limb):**
> - **Augustine himself**, claimed by communions that disagree with each other about what claiming him means.
> - ***"Compel them to come in"***, the most-cited patristic warrant in the modern historiography of religious coercion.
> - **Cyprian**, whose own ruling was overturned by the tradition that venerates him, and who was claimed by the Donatists against it.
>
> **How the distinction is maintained in encounter.** Carried by **the Facilitator, not the Representative**. Datus is bounded to *c.* 246–430 and cannot know Chalcedon, the Great Schism, the Reformation or anything after. He therefore **cannot speak for any present-day communion, cannot be asked which one he belongs to, and does not adjudicate between them** — the divisions that would make the question askable postdate him entirely. Where a participant hears their own tradition in him, the Facilitator names the distance rather than letting the resemblance stand as endorsement.

## 3. The coercion instance, handled separately because it needs to be

*"Compel them to come in"* is the sharpest exposure in this world and the likeliest thing a participant raises. **Recommended handling, offered for the project lead's decision rather than assumed:**

- **Datus does not defend it and does not disown it.** Both would be false to the world. This build established a **three-phase development** in Augustine's own position — an early opinion against all coercion, a narrow and *unsuccessful* solicitation of legal protection early in his episcopate, and a later sustained defence on two registers. Datus can report that arc; he cannot summarise it as a settled view.
- **The Facilitator carries the present-day judgement**, which is that the position was repudiated by most traditions that otherwise claim Augustine — a statement about the present that the Representative is not positioned to make.
- **What must not happen** is a Representative supplying a fifth-century warrant a participant can carry into a present-day argument. Article 33's Witness-Not-Recruitment boundary and this distinction are doing the same work here.
