### H1 — The provision's antecedent is false on the document's own evidence: none of the six gravity tests yields an ambiguous result, and the sentence asserting otherwise is falsified by the clause after its own colon

**Sites:** line 3 (Status); **line 99 (§3 Classification)**; line 165 (§4 classification cell); `lpc_Decision_Log.md` line 764. **Standard:** CF V7.4 Part III, paragraph 311.

CF V7.4's trigger is a property of **results**:

> *"Where gravity **tests** yield ambiguous **results**, developers should: acknowledge the ambiguity · classify provisionally · revisit classification as ecological reconstruction progresses · accept that some candidates may resist clean classification."*

Doc_04's own six bullets, read at HEAD:

| Test | §3's verdict | Determinate? |
|---|---|---|
| Repetition | *"Passes narrowly and locally."* | **Yes** — a pass, with magnitude stated |
| Dependency | *"Passes narrowly."* | **Yes** |
| Formation | *"Does not clearly pass. This document **finds no evidence** in Doc_02 that ordinary believers, catechumens, or most clergy in either phase were formed by, or even aware of, this specific theoretical question."* | **Yes** — a finding of no evidence, with no search bound disclosed |
| Explanatory | *"Passes narrowly."* | **Yes** |
| Persistence | *"Does not pass at the world level, on a disclosed search bound rather than on an unqualified absence."* | **Yes as to the verdict**; the *evidence base* is disclosed incomplete |
| Interaction | *"Passes narrowly — see §6."* | **Yes** |

**Six verdicts, six of them stated flatly.** Line 99 then says:

> *"This candidate's six-test profile is **ambiguous**: narrow passes on Repetition, Dependency, Explanatory and Interaction; Formation does not clearly pass; Persistence does not pass at world level."*

The colon presents the six verdicts as the *evidence for* the ambiguity claim, and they are its refutation. Four passes and two non-passes is not an ambiguous set of results; it is a clear set of results that is **hard to classify**. Those are different things, and the Framework's antecedent names the first.

**The one genuinely indeterminate element in this candidate's case is not in the list.** It is the undischarged *Gesta* search bound at Open Item 6, which the document itself describes as *"open and unprejudiced: the source may strengthen Candidate 5's Repetition and Persistence, may leave them, or may bear on Doc_01 §8 item 10's reopening trigger."* **That is an ambiguity the Framework's provision fits — and the document never connects the two.** Neither line 99, nor line 165, nor the Status line, nor Open Item 2 mentions the search bound as the reason the classification is provisional. The document reached past a real ground to a manufactured one.

**Why this matters more than a wording point.** The pass's whole warrant is the provision. If the antecedent is not satisfied, the document is not "following the Framework rather than forcing a verdict"; it is declining a verdict it owes and citing a clause that does not reach it. And the error is invisible to every check the pass ran, because every one of them asks whether the provision is *stated consistently*, not whether it *applies*.

**Fix:** state the ambiguity where it actually is. *"Two of this candidate's six results — Repetition and Persistence — rest on a disclosed and undischarged search bound (§7 Open Item 6), which may move them in either direction. The classification is therefore held provisionally under CF V7.4 Part III's ambiguous-results provision and is to be revisited when that read is validly performed."* That is true, it is the document's own finding, and it satisfies the provision's antecedent as written.

### H2 — *"Ambiguous results"* is converted into *"ambiguous candidates"* at four sites, and *"should"* into *"directs"* — a predicate moved from results onto candidates, which is the operation that makes the provision appear to fit

**Sites:** line 3 (*"CF V7.4 Part III's own **direction for ambiguous candidates**"*); line 165 (*"CF V7.4 Part III **directs that such candidates** be classified provisionally"*); line 211 (Open Item 2: *"which the Framework **directs for ambiguous candidates**"*); line 216 (Open Item 7: *"per CF V7.4 Part III's **direction for ambiguous candidates**"*).

The Framework says *"Where gravity tests yield ambiguous results, developers **should**."* Two alterations run through all four sites.

**(a) Subject shift.** *Ambiguous results* is a property of the tests' output. *Ambiguous candidates* is a property of the candidate. The first has to be demonstrated test by test (H1 shows it cannot be, here); the second is a characterisation a document can simply assert. Once the provision is restated as being *about candidates*, the question "are the results ambiguous?" stops being asked — and it is never asked anywhere in this document. **This is structurally the same defect Round 8's H1 found: a predicate detached from the subject the source attaches it to and re-attached to a subject that makes the conclusion follow.** Round 8 found it done by ellipsis inside a quotation; here it is done by paraphrase outside one, which is harder to see and reaches four sites rather than one.

**(b) Modal shift.** *Should* becomes *directs* at three of the four. A recommendation restated as a direction is a stronger warrant than the text supplies, and it is the warrant §4's cell leans on: *"CF V7.4 Part III **directs** that such candidates be classified provisionally."*

Neither alteration is disclosed anywhere. Line 3 and line 99 quote the provision verbatim in the same breath as paraphrasing it into a different proposition, which is the arrangement that makes the paraphrase look sourced.

**Fix:** use the Framework's own subject at all four sites — *"CF V7.4 Part III's provision for where gravity tests yield ambiguous results"* — and *"should"* rather than *"directs."*

## Does the ambiguous-results provision actually apply?

This is the round's central question and it deserves its own answer, separate from the findings.

### What the provision says, read in position

Part III's Gravity Classification block runs: the three class definitions (paragraphs 304–306); the heading **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check** (307); the cross-check rule (308–309); the rule's rationale — *"gravity strength and evidential confidence are distinct properties that can diverge"* (310); **the ambiguous-results provision** (311–315); and a closing note that *"the number of gravities a world contains is not predetermined. Classification should reflect the ecology as evidence reveals it"* (316).

Three things follow from the position.

**First, the provision sits under the Cross-Check heading**, between the cross-check's rationale and the not-predetermined note. Its neighbourhood is about *the interaction of test results with evidential confidence* — cases where the two pull apart. That is a context in which "ambiguous results" most naturally means *results that do not resolve into a clean classification because the evidence and the tests disagree*, not *results that are missing*.

**Second, the provision is the last of three fallbacks, not the first.** The class definitions already absorb a great deal of imperfection: Primary passes *"all or nearly all"* tests; Supporting passes *"multiple"* tests and *"may not demonstrate the same breadth of dependency or persistence"*; Tensional *"may not organize as broadly as primary gravities."* **A candidate that fails named tests is not thereby ambiguous — it is a candidate the definitions were written to accommodate.** Persistence in particular is *named in Supporting's own concession*. On the Framework's text, a Persistence non-pass is closer to a positive fit indicator for Supporting than to a source of ambiguity.

**Third, the provision's four directions are all addressed to the developer's own act** — acknowledge, classify, revisit, accept. None of them is *"record that the question is unresolved."* The fourth, *"accept that some candidates may resist clean classification,"* is about the **fit** being imperfect after a classification is made, not about the classification being withheld.

### Does Candidate 5's profile satisfy the antecedent?

**On the document's own reporting: no.** Six tests, six determinate verdicts, four narrow passes and two non-passes, each stated flatly in its own bullet (H1). *"Narrow"* is a magnitude, not a hedge. *"Finds no evidence"* is a finding. *"Does not pass at the world level"* is a finding. Nothing in the six is indeterminate.

**Is "four narrow passes, two non-passes" instead a clear negative — a candidate that is unambiguously not Supporting?** No, and this needs saying plainly because the commission raised it. Supporting's definition names no Formation requirement (its sibling Primary's does, expressly), and its own second sentence contemplates lesser persistence. **Failing those two tests does not exclude Supporting on the Framework's text.** So the result is neither ambiguous nor a clear negative: it is a **narrow positive**, which is what "Supporting" is for.

**Is there real indeterminacy in this case? Yes — and it is somewhere else.** Two of the six results, Repetition and Persistence, rest on a disclosed and undischarged search bound, which Open Item 6 says may move them in either direction. **That is an ambiguity of results in the Framework's own sense, and it would satisfy the antecedent cleanly.** The document has it, states it three times in §3, carries it at Open Item 6 — and never once connects it to the provision it invokes.

### So what is the answer?

**The provision is available to this document and the document is not using it.** What it is using is a re-described version of it — *"a direction for ambiguous candidates"* — reached by moving the Framework's predicate from results onto candidates (H2), applied to a profile described as ambiguous in a sentence its own colon refutes (H1), to license a position the provision does not contain: that the developer need not classify (H3).

**On the commission's hypothesis — that selecting a provision which licenses deferral is the obvious third move after seven rounds of over-claiming and one of under-claiming, and that it would be invisible to every check the pass ran: confirmed.** The pass ran an enumeration and a by-hand sweep. Both test whether the new position is *stated everywhere*. Neither asks whether the provision's antecedent obtains — and nothing in eight rounds of remedies would, because every remedy so far has been about **propagation fidelity**, and this is a failure of **applicability**. The pass verified its quotation of the provision (correctly — CLEAN 3) and never verified that the provision reaches the case.

**One thing in the pass's favour, stated because it is true.** The move is *disclosed*. The document says what it is doing and cites what it thinks authorises it, which is why this finding is makeable at all. That is a real improvement on Round 1's fourth label and Round 2's unargued Tensional, both of which concealed the same difficulty. The failure here is of warrant, not of candour.

## Is this process converging?

Findings by round: **38, 27, 20, 20, 26, 31, 33, 32, 36.**

Candidates 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 have generated no HIGH finding since Round 4. §1, §2, §6 and Open Items 1, 4 and 5 are quiet. **Not one of this round's ten HIGH findings is about the world, the sources, the evidence, or seven of the eight candidates.** All ten are about Candidate 5's classification statement or about the record of previous attempts at it.

**And the apparatus is growing faster than the problem is shrinking.** Measured at HEAD: Doc_04 is 94,230 bytes against 87,310 at `b419357b`, when the ruling was first applied — **+8% while its substantive findings did not change**. `lpc_Decision_Log.md` went 290,943 → 320,756 bytes over the same span, **+29,813 bytes, all of it about this one candidate**. §8's Document Log is now **15.8%** of Doc_04; Candidate 5's §3 entry is **17.3%**; with the Status line and the Disposition, **about a third of this document is now about Candidate 5 and about the history of getting Candidate 5 wrong.** There are five 2026-09-14 Decision Log entries, five in-place correction notices, three entries in §8 each calling itself *"this pass"*, and supersession markers on markers.

**That is the mechanism, and it is self-sustaining.** Each pass writes a new layer of self-description — what it did, what it corrected, what it verified, what the last pass got wrong. Each layer is new text, in the live document, making checkable claims. The next review checks it and finds errors in it, because the claims are numerous, absolute, and about a record that has moved. Those findings generate another layer. **Seven of this round's ten HIGH findings (H6, H7, H8, H9, H10, and the propagation elements of H5) are defects in the correction apparatus, not in the gravity discovery.** The document is now generating most of its own review surface.

**A second mechanism, visible for the first time this round: the review stream is also generating error.** Four consecutive rounds carried a finding that is false at source and asked the document to introduce a defect (CLEAN 2). Round 8 certified a false tally as accurate (H10). A process in which both the fix side and the review side produce errors at a steady rate, and in which each side's output is the other's input, has no reason to converge — it has a fixed point, and the fixed point is roughly thirty findings a round.

**What would actually close it.** Not another review round, and not another propagation remedy. Three things, none of them review-shaped:

1. **A project-lead ruling on the one question Round 8 put and that has not been answered:** *is Supporting intended to rest on the ruling alone, with the profile recorded as ambiguous — or is it intended to rest on profile fit as well?* Every round since Round 5 has been the build thread guessing at this and being marked wrong. It is one sentence from the person with standing to say it, and until it is said the next pass will select a fifth basis and the round after will find it unsound. **This is the single highest-value action available.**
2. **Commission the *Gesta* read from an independent thread (Open Item 6, the document's own recommendation, twice unactioned).** It is the only thing that can move Repetition and Persistence, and it is the actual ground on which provisionality is defensible. Everything else is argument about a gap that a read would close.
3. **Move the correction history out of the live documents.** §8's Document Log and the Decision Log's five 2026-09-14 entries should be replaced by one dated *Superseded Claims* appendix, listing each withdrawn proposition once with its sites, and the live text should simply be correct. That removes at a stroke the surface that is now generating two-thirds of each round's findings, and it stops the compounding.

**Is the document adequate now? No — but not for the reason the finding count suggests.** Strip the apparatus findings and what remains is one unresolved substantive question (what Doc_04 may say about Candidate 5), one undischarged source read, and a short tail of genuine small defects (row 65, the §8 ordering, the file-line citation, the quotation of Primary). ## Has the failure mode migrated again, and what does enumerate-by-subject still not catch?

The remedy history: re-derive quotations at source (R2) → read the word-level diff (R3) → open every named destination (R4) → derive the destination set by search, not copy (R5) → widen the terms and assert contradictions (R6) → state the domain and check propositions rather than tokens (R7) → verify the proposition against the whole governing sentence before propagating (R8) → **enumerate by subject, by hand, over the record rather than the document (R9, this pass)**.

**The R9 remedy worked on exactly what it was pointed at, and better than its predecessors.** It found and fixed all three site classes Round 8 predicted: the bare provisional-classification line, Open Item 3's superseded vocabulary, and defects in the companion record. Two of the three had survived every token sweep and every propagation check in eight rounds. The record-as-unit widening is a genuine advance and should be kept.

**What it still does not catch — three things, of which the third will recur.**

**1. It verifies propagation and not applicability.** The pass's entire instrument set asks *is the new position stated everywhere it should be?* Nothing asks *does the authority the position cites reach this case?* That is this round's H1, H2 and H3, and no remedy in the ladder would have caught it, because every remedy in the ladder is about getting a proposition to the right places rather than about whether the proposition is licensed. **The ladder has been climbing a fidelity problem for eight rounds while the failures moved to a warrant problem.**

**2. A by-subject sweep is keyed on a sentence's *topic*, and load-bearing claims ride inside sentences about other topics.** This is the precise mechanism of H6 and H7. The pass enumerated *"every sentence about Candidate 5's classification or its basis."* Line 188 is, by its section's framing, a sentence about **whether Doc_01's preliminary reading survives independent derivation**; Candidate 5's classification is a parenthesis inside it. Line 190 is a sentence about **the shape of §5's overall result**. Line 244 is a sentence about **which CO-022 categories are occupied**. Line 231 is a sentence about **what Round 7 found**. Line 206 is a sentence about **why a matrix row is included**. Each of those five commits the document to a claim about Candidate 5's profile or label; none of them is *about* Candidate 5 in the sense a by-hand subject sweep uses. **Subject is a property of a sentence's frame; the defect lives in its predicate.**

**3. And the one that will recur: a sweep over the record still cannot see a claim whose falsity depends on a source outside the record.** CLEAN 2 and L2 are both of this kind. The Forces attribution and the `121764` citation are each a single clause, internally consistent, agreeing with every other site, and each was checkable only by opening a `.docx` and a 130,000-line text file. Eight rounds of remedies have all operated **inside the world folder**, and two of this round's verified items required leaving it. A ninth remedy scoped to the record will not reach the tenth round's version of this.

**Stated as the rule this round would add:** *the destination set is every sentence whose truth depends on the claim, not every sentence the claim is about — and a claim about a governing text is checked in the governing text, not in the record that cites it.*

**Predicted next migration, concretely.** The next pass will run the elimination, ground provisionality in the search bound, fix the five sites named in H6/H7/H8, and extend the two correction notices. It will enumerate by commitment rather than by subject and it will return clean. **And the round after will find its defects in the same three places this one did: in the new self-description layer it writes about having done all that; in a claim about a governing document that nobody re-opened; and in a number attached to a sentence about not trusting numbers.** The only remedy that reaches all three is the one in the convergence section: stop writing the self-description.
