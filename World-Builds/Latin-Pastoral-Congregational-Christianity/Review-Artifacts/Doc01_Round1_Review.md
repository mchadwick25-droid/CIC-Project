### H1 — Constitution Article 15 is quoted using words Article 15 does not contain, with a load-bearing qualifier silently deleted

**Where.** §4: *"Article 15 (Historical Development & Instability) already establishes that formation worlds are 'shaped through instability, adaptation, and incomplete continuity **even within their own bounded timeframe**' — that is the correct home for temporal development *as such*."*

**What's wrong.** Article 15's actual text, extracted from `CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx` this session, reads: *"historically developing ecclesial realities shaped, **to the degree the evidence attests**, through instability, adaptation, and incomplete continuity."* Two problems inside a single pair of quotation marks:

- The phrase **"even within their own bounded timeframe" does not appear in Article 15** — not in that sentence, not anywhere in the Article. It is the document's own gloss, presented as constitutional text.
- The qualifier **"to the degree the evidence attests" is silently deleted** with no ellipsis. That qualifier is not decorative: Article 15 goes on to state expressly that *"Where a formation world's evidence attests continuity and stability rather than dispute or upheaval, that finding is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment, never displaced by an assumption that instability must be present."* Deleting it converts a hedged, evidence-conditional principle into an unconditional one.

**Why it matters.** This is a fabricated citation in the CO-022 (c) sense, and it is load-bearing: the quotation is the pivot on which §4 routes temporal development away from Article 21 and toward Article 15. The document's Step 0 went through five rounds partly on this exact failure class (an A5 over-read achieved through a load-bearing elision, R1-H4). It recurs here, in the same build, one document later.

**Fix.** Quote Article 15 exactly, with the qualifier intact, and mark the gloss as a gloss: *Article 15 treats formation worlds as "historically developing ecclesial realities shaped, to the degree the evidence attests, through instability, adaptation, and incomplete continuity" — which this document reads as covering temporal development within a world's own bounded timeframe.* The argument survives intact; only the false quotation goes.

### H2 — *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* is characterized as doing the opposite of what it actually does, and the Article 3 coherence argument rests on that characterization

**Where.** §4: *"His central anti-Donatist treatise, On Baptism, Against the Donatists, argues at length through the acts of Cyprian's own baptismal councils, treating Cyprian's own **authority, teaching, and conciliar procedure as directly binding precedent** for his own church a century later — not as admired antiquity, but as *his own* tradition's own prior act."*

Propagated verbatim into §5's six-cell table, Cell 2B: *"Augustine's own explicit reliance on Cyprian's conciliar acts **as binding precedent** (§4 above) is itself the mechanism by which this world's own formation logic crosses the century-long gap."*

**What's wrong.** *De Baptismo* argues at length that Cyprian's council was **not** binding and that Cyprian's teaching on rebaptism was **wrong**. Augustine's own words, verified this session:

- Book VI: Cyprian's position *"was contrary to that which was afterwards brought to light by a decision, not of mine, but of the whole Church, confirmed and strengthened by the authority of a plenary Council."*
- Book II: the Cyprianic practice *"had no sanction from the ancient custom of the Church,"* and Cyprian and his colleagues acted *not "by the authority of any plenary or regionary Council."*

Augustine's actual move is the reverse of the one attributed to him: he concedes Cyprian's sanctity and his standing inside the same communion precisely in order to *detach* Cyprian's baptismal doctrine from binding force, and to show the Donatists that Cyprian's own conduct — remaining in unity while disagreeing — refutes their separation. Cyprian's *charity and refusal to break communion* are what Augustine treats as normative. His teaching and his council's decision are what Augustine treats as superseded.

**Why it matters.** Three ways, in ascending order.

1. This is the entire evidentiary basis of the Article 3 answer. §4 says so explicitly: the coherence claim "rests instead on a checkable, in-world fact this world's own surviving corpus actually attests," and that fact is this. The fact as stated is not attested; the treatise attests something adjacent to it.
2. **It contradicts the document's own next paragraph.** Two paragraphs later §4 concedes that "Cyprian's own historical position ... sits closer to the Donatist position than to Augustine's own eventual, more accommodating one." A treatise cannot simultaneously treat Cyprian's teaching as "directly binding precedent" and reach a conclusion contrary to it. The document knows the fact that falsifies its own sentence and does not connect them.
3. **Cell 2B's transmission claim is weakened by the same error and by an omission.** Augustine engages Cyprian's councils at length *because the Donatists cited them against him* — the transmission ran through the rival claimant, not through "one bishop's own deliberate citation of an earlier one." That is a more interesting and more honest Transmission finding, and it is the one this world's own §4 Donatist paragraph already implies.

**Fix.** Restate what the treatise actually does, which still supports the Article 3 argument and supports it better: Augustine treats Cyprian as *his own church's own father, whose acts he is obliged to argue with rather than admire from outside* — he disputes Cyprian's conclusion while claiming Cyprian's communion, which is a stronger continuity datum than deference would be, because you only argue this way with your own predecessors. Correct Cell 2B in the same pass, and name the Donatist citation as the proximate occasion for the engagement.

### H3 — The Strand Determination never engages the strongest available strand-plural candidate, and relabels it as a Forces question — which is the specific dodge Article 21's "authority structure" criterion exists to prevent

**Where.** §4: *"What genuinely differs between Cyprian's phase and Augustine's phase — the specific external pressures each faced (state persecution versus a schism ...; a plague versus a Vandal invasion; Novatianist rigorism versus Donatist separatism and Pelagian anthropology) — is real, substantial, and not minimized here. But it is a difference in the **forces pressing on** a single recurring pattern, which is precisely Doc_08's own subject matter ... not a difference in the pattern itself."*

**What's wrong.** The list of differences is curated so that everything on it *is* an external pressure. The one difference that is **not** an external pressure — and that goes directly to one of Article 21's four named criteria — is left off the list entirely:

> **Cyprian is a bishop of an illegal religion whose episcopal authority has no coercive backing whatsoever and is exercised against the state. Augustine is a bishop of the established religion who solicits, defends, and deploys imperial coercion against a rival communion.**

That is a difference in **authority structure** — Article 21's third criterion, and the Framework's Part I World Separation question "Has authority changed substantially?" — not a difference in the weather.

The evidence for it is not external to this build. It is:
- **In this world's own corpus-map**, at `confidence: assigned`: *The Correction of the Donatists* (Ep. 185), Augustine's sustained argument that the state should compel the Donatists back, resting on *"Compel them to come in"* (Luke 14:23). Also *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* and *On Baptism* itself, all assigned to this world.
- **In this document's own §5**, which names it plainly and calls it "the century's real content": *"Cyprian's own phase is embedded in a pre-Constantinian Roman North Africa under recurring, escalating state suspicion of an unlicensed religion. Augustine's own phase is embedded in a post-Constantinian, officially Christianized Roman North Africa..."*

So §5 identifies the candidate and §4 never tests it. That is not an oversight of an obscure fact; it is the document declining to look at the one thing its own adjacent section says is the century's real content.

**Why it matters.** The review question is whether "strand-singular" is well-argued against Article 21's real text or is a foregone conclusion dressed as analysis. As written it is closer to the latter, because the four "sameness" bullets are all true of things the two phases obviously share, and the one axis on which they visibly diverge is moved off the table by a categorical move ("that's Doc_08's subject") rather than by an argument. Both precedents in this portfolio set a higher bar and did so under review pressure: IJC's Doc_01 §4 was rebuilt in Round 1 precisely because it had not engaged Step 0's own flagged strand hypothesis; hal's Doc_01 §4 was rebuilt in Round 1 because "the strongest steelman for strand plurality had not actually been engaged." This document has not engaged its steelman.

Note also that §4 has, one paragraph earlier, *widened* the strand test to cover patterns "whether coexisting or successive" (see M9) — which makes a successive Cyprian-phase/Augustine-phase candidate squarely admissible under the test the document itself just set.

**Fix.** Engage the candidate directly and reach a reasoned finding, either way. A defensible route to strand-singular exists and is not hard: the *ground* of episcopal authority (ordination, see, territorial responsibility for a flock, answerability to and for it) is unchanged; what changes is the *external instrument available to it*, and Augustine's own justification for coercion is argued in pastoral-corrective terms rather than as a jurisdictional claim over other sees. But that argument has to be made, against the actual sources, with Ep. 185 named. If instead the evidence is judged to support two strands, say so — Article 21 is a finding, not a preference, in both directions.

### H6 — The IJC boundary is "discharged" on a claim about this world's own primary corpus that is false

**Where.** §6, World #6 bullet: *"This world's authority is exercised as care *for the one flock physically in front of a given bishop* — **Cyprian's and Augustine's own correspondence is overwhelmingly addressed to or about named individual members of their own congregations, not to peer bishops contesting precedence.**"*

**What's wrong.** For Cyprian this is wrong in both halves, and the counterexample is the most famous thing in his corpus.

- **Addressees.** Cyprian's ~82-letter body is dominated by inter-clerical and inter-episcopal correspondence: the Roman clergy, Cornelius, Stephen, Firmilian of Caesarea, Pompeius, the Carthaginian presbyters and deacons collectively, African episcopal colleagues, the confessors, the Spanish and Gallic churches. This world's own Step 0 §3 B1 already flags that "the corpus embeds letters by others (Cornelius, the Roman clergy, Firmilian of Caesarea, the confessors) under Cyprian's name" — an observation that only makes sense of a corpus that is largely an episcopal exchange.
- **"Not to peer bishops contesting precedence."** The rebaptism controversy *is* a precedence contest between the see of Carthage and the see of Rome. Stephen asserted his standing as Peter's successor; Cyprian and the African episcopate refused it; Firmilian wrote to Cyprian in support against Stephen. Cyprian's *De Unitate* and Ep. 71/72/74 belong to exactly that dispute, and Ep. 74 to Pompeius is a direct rebuttal of Stephen.

For Augustine the claim is also overstated. His 168-letter corpus, as this world's own corpus-map partitions it, contains a 17-letter exchange with Jerome (a peer, and the second-largest single block in the volume — which **this same §6 bullet's neighbouring paragraph relies on**), three separate controversy sub-corpora, and correspondence with imperial officials, Donatist bishops and Pelagian controversialists. The corpus-map describes the residual 138 as "pastoral advice, administration, consolation, friendship" — real, but not "overwhelmingly ... named individual members of their own congregations."

**Why it matters.** This is the evidence on which the document claims to discharge IJC's Step 0 §4 item 3 — the reciprocal boundary obligation from an already-built world. Discharging an obligation on a false evidentiary claim is worse than leaving it open, because it closes the file. And the specific falsity is the worst possible one for this boundary: the counterexample is a jurisdictional dispute over precedence between sees, which is IJC's defining subject matter, occurring inside this world's own anchor corpus.

**Why the boundary is probably still right.** The distinction survives on better grounds: Cyprian's argument against Stephen is that *no bishop holds jurisdiction over another's see* — the anti-primacy position — which is the opposite of IJC's Strand A claim, not an instance of it. That is a much stronger discharge than the one written.

**Fix.** Replace the correspondence claim with the argument above. State the Stephen dispute openly rather than writing as though it were not there, and hold the boundary on the *content* of Cyprian's position (episcopal collegiality without super-ordinate jurisdiction; *episcopatus unus est* held by all in common) rather than on a false claim about who his letters were addressed to.

### H7 — The Hieronymian boundary is "discharged" on a structural claim contradicted by this world's own vendored primary source and by the document's own §2

**Where.** §6, World #9 bullet: *"**A bishop cannot be un-chosen by his own congregation** the way a patron's household could, in principle, simply stop supporting him; **that structural asymmetry**, not merely a difference of subject matter, **is this world's own half of the authority-mode contrast Hieronymian's Doc_01 asked for.**"* Supported by: *"a bishop answerable for one bounded flock by virtue of ordination and see, **whether or not any individual member of that flock personally admires or seeks him out**."*

**What's wrong.** Cyprian says the opposite, in a letter vendored to this world in ANF05. Ep. 67, to the clergy and people of Spain concerning Basilides and Martial:

> *"a people obedient to the Lord's precepts, and fearing God, ought to separate themselves from a sinful prelate, and not to associate themselves with the sacrifices of a sacrilegious priest, especially since they themselves have the power either of choosing worthy priests, or of rejecting unworthy ones."*

That is Cyprian's own doctrine that a congregation both chooses and can reject its bishop — the exact proposition §6 nominates as this world's distinguishing structural asymmetry. It is not an obscure text: Ep. 67 is a standard locus for lay participation in episcopal election, and Cyprian's own episcopate began by the *suffragium* of the Carthaginian people over the opposition of five presbyters.

And the document contains its own counterexample. **§2 lists the Felicissimus schism among this world's Historical Catalysts** — a Carthaginian faction that refused Cyprian's authority and set up its own clergy, i.e. a congregation un-choosing its bishop. §5's Cell 1B likewise names "an already-substantial, organized Carthaginian church capable of sustained collective response."

**Why it matters.** This is the sentence the document nominates as its discharge of hal Doc_01 §8.1 — a standing, named, unmet request from an already-built world, on record since 2026-07-12. Discharging it with a claim this world's own sources contradict does not close it; it closes it falsely, and it does so in the mirror image of the error hal's own §8.1 was corrected for in its Round 1 (asserting facts about the neighbouring world "as though independently known").

### H8 — The Article 3 coherence argument's decisive move performs the adjudication it disclaims, and the "mode" distinction it substitutes does not actually distinguish the two claimants

**Where.** §4: *"This world's own boundary decision ... **does not rest on adjudicating which side legitimately inherited Cyprian's own title**, which is a contested historical-theological question this project does not resolve ... **It rests on which *mode* of pastoral life recurs: ordinary, territorial, sacramental care of an entire local flock, in communion with the wider catholic world, versus a rigorist-separatist alternative that broke communion over ritual purity and formed its own parallel clergy.** Augustine's church, whatever the Donatists themselves said about its legitimacy, is doing the former."*

**What's wrong.** Two things, and they compound.

1. **The stated distinction does not distinguish.** Donatism was not a sect that declined ordinary territorial pastoral care; for much of the fourth century it was the *majority* church across large parts of Numidia, with a full parallel episcopate holding territorial sees, catechizing, baptizing, celebrating the Eucharist, disciplining and readmitting, and caring for entire local flocks — 279 Donatist bishops attended the 411 Conference of Carthage, against 286 Catholic. On the axis actually named — "ordinary, territorial, sacramental care of an entire local flock" — both sides qualify. The only clause in the sentence that separates them is *"in communion with the wider catholic world."*

2. **That clause is the adjudication the paragraph just disclaimed.** Whether the Caecilianist communion or the Donatist communion was the one in legitimate catholic communion — and therefore which of them "broke communion" — is precisely the contested question of who inherited Cyprian's title. Donatism's own Step 0, which this build has read, states its side in exactly reciprocal terms: "a persecuted 'Church of the Martyrs' facing a state-favored rival." So the paragraph disclaims adjudicating, and then adjudicates in the Caecilianist direction inside the very sentence that does the work.

**Why it matters.** The review question was whether the honest acknowledgment of the Donatist claim is handled fairly or quietly tips the scale. It tips it. The concession two sentences earlier is real, and then the argument routes around it via a characterization ("broke communion," "rigorist-separatist," "over ritual purity") that only holds if the concession is set aside. That is circularity in the strict sense: the Article 3 test is passed by presupposing the boundary decision the test was invoked to justify.

A further structural problem: Article 23 does permit this world to speak of its opponents as it understood them — but Article 23 governs *inhabited prose*, and §4 is a construction-methodology argument in the project's own analytic voice. A partisan characterization is being used as the neutral ground on which a constitutional test is passed.

**Fix.** Two honest routes, either acceptable:
- **Narrow the claim.** State that Article 3 coherence is satisfied by *continuity of the mode of pastoral office* — territorial episcopal care of a whole local flock — and acknowledge frankly that this mode recurs in **both** the Caecilianist and Donatist communions, which is exactly why the two are separate worlds distinguished on their *primary gravities* (rigorist purity-boundary maintenance vs. ordinary pastoral flock-keeping) rather than on which one is "really" the church. This is stronger, not weaker: it concedes the shared mode and locates the distinction where Step 0 §4 item 8 says it belongs.
- **Or own the adjudication.** State plainly that this project's portfolio has, at Step 0, drawn the boundary along the Caecilianist/Catholic line, that this is a portfolio-level decision rather than an ecology-grounded finding, and label it as such per CO-022's second escalation category.

Either way, delete "broke communion over ritual purity" as a neutral premise, and cross-check the resulting text against Donatism's own Step 0, which this build has read.

### M4 — The Felicissimus schism did not break out after Cyprian's return from hiding
