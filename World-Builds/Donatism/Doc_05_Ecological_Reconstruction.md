## 0. Method and the Governing Disciplines

**Purpose.** Step 4 asked what organizes this world; Step 5 asks what it was like to be formed inside it. Ecological Reconstruction renders the world as a living system of formation through a set of lenses, each a different angle on one ecology, not separate worlds. Five disciplines govern every lens below.

1. **The gravity spine (from Doc_04).** Four Primary gravities organize this ecology: **G1 Ministerial Purity/Traditor-Free Sacramental Validity**, **G2 Rebaptism as Boundary-Marking Practice**, **G3 Martyr-Cult Identity/"Church of the Martyrs,"** and **G5 Refusal of Imperial/State Religious Legitimacy**. Two Supporting gravities frame and contain it: **G4 Parallel Institutional Hierarchy** (the institutional container these operate within, not a fifth independent formative experience) and **D-A Circumcellion/*Agonistici*** (Supporting only for the Numidian regional sub-ecology, not ecology-wide). Two Tensional gravities are held, not resolved: **T1 Principled Refusal vs. Pragmatic Recourse to Imperial Power** and **T2 Purity-Rigor vs. Institutional Reception** (the Maximianist precedent). Tyconius's own hermeneutics (D-B) and the Numidian native-social-protest substrate (D-C) were tested and not advanced (Doc_04 §2) and are treated below only where their own thinness or contested standing is itself part of this world's honest ecological account (§6.5, §6.3).

2. **The transmission-asymmetry constraint (Doc_01 §7 item 1; Doc_02 §1; tested directly at Doc_04 §5's Cross-Voice Test) — active throughout, not a footnote.** This world's central evidentiary fact is not a stratum split, as in Alexandria's literate/non-literate divide — Doc_01 §2 finds Donatism was, for substantial periods and regions, the numerically dominant church, not a small elite current. It is a **transmission asymmetry**: nearly all textual evidence passes through the hostile, eventually-victorious Caecilianist/Catholic party's own literature (Optatus, Augustine). Doc_04's own Cross-Voice Test found this asymmetry is not a blanket disqualifier — G1, G2, and G5 each pass with a named qualification (their core doctrine's *existence* is cross-voice attested — Petilian's and Donatus's own quoted words — even where the *specific argumentative texture* remains substantially hostile-mediated); G3 passes most cleanly, substantially attested in the Donatist-voiced record itself (three distinct texts plus non-textual epigraphy); D-A fails beyond bare existence and self-designation. Every lens below marks this distinction explicitly rather than treating a hostile paraphrase as equivalent to a Donatist self-statement. Because the transmission problem runs across this world's *entire* population rather than restricting itself to a non-literate majority, the "ordinary believer" absence named throughout this document is a **source-mediation absence affecting the bulk of the movement itself**, not a stratum-scale absence (§8 develops this contrast fully).

3. **D-A's own narrow regional scope-qualification (Doc_04 §3.5), carried forward specifically.** The Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon is confirmed Supporting only for the Numidian regional sub-ecology, not for this world's ecology as a whole. This qualification is carried into the Spatial/Regional lens (§6.3) as that lens's own central finding, and is not generalized elsewhere in this document.

4. **Writing-From-Inside (Article 23), quarantined.** The inhabited passages below render this world's own logic in its own vocabulary — *traditor*, *Deo laudes*, Church of the Martyrs, *agonistici* — holding its tensions as it held them, with no analytical-distance markers inside the inhabited layer. All confidence, Author-Gravity, and construction notes live in bracketed **[Construction note: …]** blocks outside the inhabited prose. Composite typical-practice narration is marked **[Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction]** and is traceable to Doc_02/Doc_04; **no Tier 5 (free invention) appears anywhere in this document** (Article 19).

5. **Forces integrated into every lens (Forces Framework V1.1 Step 5).** External forces — the Diocletianic and Macarian persecutions, oscillating imperial religious policy, the Maximianist internal fracture, the eventual Vandal conquest — are not background; they are constitutive of how this ecology felt from within. The Human, Community, and Boundary lenses in particular are written *through* the forces, per the Forces Framework's own naming of those three lenses specifically.

## 1. Human Ecology

**Who inhabited this world.** The ecology held two complete, rival hierarchies of bishops, presbyters, and deacons contesting the same sees town for town (284 Donatist bishops seated at the 411 Conference alone); a rural, itinerant Numidian phenomenon the movement's own opponents called Circumcellions and whose own members, Augustine reports, called themselves *agonistici* — "contestants"; named martyrs and confessors (a bishop of Sicilibba gravely wounded, the bishop of Advocata killed "with numerous faithful," Marculus, Isaac, and Maximianus); and — most numerous and least individually visible — an ordinary lay membership that was not, for substantial regions and periods, a minority sect but the numerically dominant church.

*[Inhabited — the purity consciousness, cross-voice attested]* What makes a sacrament a sacrament is not the water or the hand alone but the conscience behind the hand. A minister who surrendered the scriptures to the persecutor, or who was ordained by one who did, has no clean conscience to give; and, as one of this world's own bishops put it directly to his accuser, "what we look for is the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient." A tainted hand cannot cleanse. This is not a technicality argued only by clever men — it is why the movement exists at all, and every ordinary believer who insists on being baptized again, by a minister of unbroken standing, is living the same conviction the bishop states.

**Forces.** This human ecology was split in two, before it was two churches, by the Diocletianic persecution's own *traditio* demand: clergy who surrendered scripture and sacred vessels, and clergy who refused and suffered instead. The 311/312 election of Caecilian — consecrated, on the movement's own founding accusation, by a *traditor* — converted that division of persons into a division of the whole community, replicated at every level from the see of Carthage down to the ordinary believer's own choice of which minister's hands to receive baptism from.

[Construction note: the parallel hierarchy's scale and the named martyrs are **Documented** (Doc_01 §2, §4; Doc_02 §4). The purity doctrine's *existence* is **Documented** on cross-voice grounds — Petilian's own quoted proposition, independently re-verified this pass against `cic/texts/npnf104_augustine-anti-manichaean-anti-donatist.xml` (line 10280: "*Conscientia namque [sancte] dantis attenditur, quæ [qui] abluat accipientis*" — "What we look for is the conscience of the giver [him who gives in holiness], to cleanse that of the recipient"), Registry row 12 — while its *specific argumentative texture* remains substantially Augustine-mediated (Doc_04 §3.1's own Cross-Check). **Ordinary believers' own numerical dominance is Widely Accepted** (Doc_01 §2); **their own interior experience of holding this conviction is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated here** — a source-mediation absence affecting the bulk of the movement itself (§0 item 2 above), not a stratum absence. Lucilla (the wealthy Carthaginian laywoman Optatus I.16 names as a proximate cause of the schism) is reconstructed only to the bound Optatus's own hostile text supports — a named woman of means with the standing to make a rival consecration happen — per Doc_02 §6's own Article 20 bounded-reconstruction finding; nothing beyond that bound is asserted of her motivations or character. ## 2. Community Ecology

**Belonging as re-entry.** For this world, to belong was to have been baptized again — not received once, but re-received, into the one communion whose ministers' hands were clean. Rebaptism (G2) is not one practice among several; it is the enacted rite of G1's own purity doctrine, the concrete, individually experienced act that marks who has truly crossed over. And the community's own positive self-understanding was not first a claim about doctrine but a claim about suffering: this is the "Church of the Martyrs" (G3), the one communion that has actually paid, and gone on paying, for the truth it holds.

*[Inhabited]* One does not simply join this church; one is washed clean of a false baptism first, because a baptism given by a *traditor*'s hand, or by a hand descended from one, washed nothing at all. And one belongs, from that day, to a people who know themselves proved: not a sect defined by what it left behind, but the church that has borne witness in blood while its rival stood favored by the very power that shed it.

**Forces.** Persecution is what makes this belonging costly, and therefore clarifying. Under the Diocletianic terror the whole community faced the *traditio* demand together, before the schism; under the Macarian repression (347–348) it was this world's own community specifically that was struck, and the community's own response — new commemorative texts, produced in the repression's immediate aftermath — is itself a formation act, not merely a record of one.

Held within this same belonging, not smoothed away, is an inconsistency the community itself lived with: the same logic that requires rebaptizing anyone tainted by schism did not, in practice, apply itself to the Maximianist dissidents received back into Primianist communion after 394 — Augustine's own quotation of Donatist material states this reception carried no repeated ordination or baptism at all. This is **T2**, held here as the community's own unresolved internal fact, not resolved by this document into either "the doctrine wasn't really absolute" or "the reception was really a lapse."

[Construction note: G2's bare practice is **Documented** — attested directly and repeatedly in Augustine's own primary text and corroborated by Petilian's own quoted argument (Doc_04 §3.2). T2 is **Documented**, "the most rigorously and repeatedly directly-quoted internal tension in this world's entire vendored corpus" (Doc_04 §3.6, Registry rows 3, 4, 47). **The ordinary believer's own experience of undergoing rebaptism — what it felt like to cross that specific threshold — is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated here**, a named absence rather than a filled silence.]

## 3. Worship Ecology

**Acclamation and commemoration.** Worship carried this world's own liturgical signature directly: the *Deo laudes* acclamation ("Praise to God"), set against the Caecilianist *Deo gratias* ("thanks to God") and attested independently of any hostile literary mediation, on stone, at Bagai and elsewhere. Martyrs were commemorated annually, at their own death-day, with a sermon read or preached at the grave — the *anniversaria commemoratio* whose own earliest attested instance, on Monceaux's dating, is the earliest Donatist-authored text in this world's entire vendored corpus. And the Eucharist and baptism themselves carried this world's own defining stake: not merely worthy reception of an already-valid sacrament, but validity itself, standing or falling on the minister's own unbroken purity (G1).

*[Inhabited]* "*Deo laudes*" is not merely something said in worship; it is the word that names who you are when you say it — the sign and token, as this world's own opponents recorded it, of the party of Bagai and Thamugadi, spoken where the rival says something else. And when the community gathers on a martyr's day to hear the account of the death read aloud again, it is not remembering something finished; it is being formed again by what it already believes itself to be — the church that suffers and is thereby proved true.

**Forces.** Persecution enters worship directly, as the occasion that produces the very words read in it afterward. The earliest commemorative sermon, on Monceaux's dating, responds to a persecution under Leontius and Ursacius that predates the Macarian repression by roughly three decades; the Macarian repression itself (347–348) then produces, in close succession, two further texts — the *Passio Marculi* and Macrobius's own letter to the Carthage congregation — read at the martyrs' own graves in the years that followed.

[Construction note: the *Deo laudes* acclamation is **Documented**, non-mediated (Registry rows 27, 48 — *CIL* VIII 17732, found at Bagai itself, plus three further attestations). The commemorative liturgical practice is **Documented** via the sermon's own admonitio ("*in solemni et anniversaria commemoratione*," Mabillon) and Monceaux's own fuller, independently-checked analysis (Doc_02 §4). G1's own minister-purity-as-sacramental-validity logic is **Documented** in existence, Augustine-mediated in its specific argumentative texture (Doc_04 §3.1). **What the ordinary participant felt, standing in the assembly at the acclamation or at the graveside reading, is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated beyond what the texts' own content supports.**]

## 4. Organizational (Authority) Ecology

**A doubled hierarchy.** Authority in this world ran through a complete parallel structure — not one bishop per see but two, contesting the same city and congregation the length and breadth of Roman North Africa. The see of Carthage itself was claimed by three successive Donatist primates across this world's window (Donatus, Parmenian, Primian) against the rival Caecilianist line; Cirta was the see of Petilian, this world's own fullest surviving voice. This world's most consequential internal organizational event was the Maximianist fracture: a council at Cebarsussi (393) elected the deacon Maximian as a rival primate against Primian's own conduct; the mainstream party's own much larger council at Bagai (394) condemned the Maximianists and suppressed them — and it is precisely because this suppression happened *inside* this hierarchy's own conciliar machinery, not from outside it, that **T2** belongs here as an organizational-authority tension, not merely a doctrinal one.

*[Inhabited]* To belong to this church is already to have chosen which of two bishops, in one's own city, holds the true office — the parallel is total, not partial, so that there is no neutral ground from which to ask the question. And when the party's own council at Bagai judged Maximian's men and then received them back without asking them to be ordained or baptized again, the same council that enforces the boundary against the Caecilianists is seen drawing it differently against its own dissidents.

**Forces.** This organizational life was lived under continuous external legal jeopardy, not as a settled internal matter. Constantine's early coercive measures, Julian's 361 restoration of confiscated basilicas, the 405 Edict of Unity, and the 411 Conference's own verdict each altered — sometimes reversed — this hierarchy's legal standing, its property, and its clergy's physical safety within the same span that the Maximianist fracture was opening, being suppressed, and remaining live in Augustine's own writing as late as c. 405–406.

[Construction note: the parallel hierarchy's bare existence is **Documented**, undisputed even by hostile sources reporting it (Doc_04 §5, G4's own Cross-Voice pass). Individual bishops' own conduct and motives beyond the bare institutional fact remain hostile-mediated (Doc_02 §2). T2 is **Documented** (Doc_04 §3.6); its own housing here, rather than only in §2 above, follows Doc_04 §6's own G4↔T2 finding — "an event *internal to* this hierarchy's own conciliar machinery."]

## 5. Ministry and Formation Ecology *(the central lens)*

**Formation is the central activity of this ecology, and it runs on four channels of the same conviction, not four separate topics.** G1's purity doctrine is the ground; G2's rebaptism is its enacted rite; G3's martyr-cult is the community's own formation ideal — the picture of what a rightly formed life produces when tested; and G5's refusal of the state's own authority to adjudicate legitimacy is the lived posture this whole formation faces the outside world with. G4 is the institutional container these four operate within, not a fifth channel. T1 and T2 are held here as the tensions this world actually lived, not resolved into a single consistent position.

- **Purity as formation ground.** *[Inhabited — cross-voice attested, but its specific argumentative shape reaches this document substantially through the hostile record]* To be formed here is to hold, without qualification, that a sacrament's validity rises or falls with the giver's own conscience — "the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient," in one bishop's own quoted words. This is not an abstraction argued only by theologians; it is why an ordinary believer insists on rebaptism at all.
- **Rebaptism as the enacted rite.** Carried in §2 above: the concrete, individually experienced threshold-crossing that makes the doctrine lived rather than merely asserted.
- **Martyr-cult as the formation ideal — this document's most directly attested channel.** *[Inhabited — substantially in this world's own voice, per Doc_04 §3.3's own finding]* The model of a formed life this world holds up is the one who, when the imperial commissioners came, chose the death that proved the faith over the peace that would have betrayed it — the bishop of Advocata, "with numerous faithful," buried near the altar he served; Marculus; Isaac and Maximianus, whose own bishop opened his own letter to the Carthage congregation by naming the very writing of it a burning, joyful eagerness ("*opportunus... et laetus... ardor accendit*"), not a duty. The community does not merely admire this; it commemorates it annually, by name, at the grave, and reads the account aloud so that the formation the death produced happens again in the hearing.
- **Refusal of imperial legitimacy as lived posture — held with its own qualification (T1), not smoothed over.** *[Inhabited]* The state has no standing to decide who the true church is — "What has the emperor to do with the church?", as this world's own primate is reported to have retorted. And yet this same community turned to that same imperial machinery three times across its own window when doing so served its case: petitioning Constantine through Anulinus in 313, petitioning Julian for its confiscated basilicas in 361, and — in the 390s — invoking the empire's own anti-heretical law against its own Maximianist dissidents. This world held both facts at once, refusal as its dominant posture and three specific, pragmatic exceptions to it, without treating the exceptions as betrayals of the posture or the posture as absolute.

**Forces.** The whole formation ecology developed under, and partly because of, sustained external pressure: the refusal of the state's own religious settlement forced this world to build a positive account of legitimate ecclesial authority that did not depend on imperial recognition at all; the Macarian repression forced the martyr-cult channel into its most intense and best-attested form; the Maximianist fracture forced the purity doctrine to be tested against the community's own practice, not only against its rival's.

[Construction note: G1's core doctrine **Documented**/argumentative-texture Augustine-mediated (Doc_04 §3.1); G2 **Documented** (§3.2); G3 **Documented**, "the least Author-Gravity-encumbered Primary in this document" (§3.3); G5 **Documented** with a named divergence between the recurring episodes (Documented) and the synthesis-level "principled refusal" framing (partly this build's own reading, corroborated by Donatus's own quoted retort) (§3.7). T1 and T2 are **Documented** per-instance and held as genuine Tensional gravities, not resolved (§3.6). **The interior experience of holding all four channels together, for the ordinary believer whose own words do not survive, is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated** — the same source-mediation absence named throughout this document.]

## 6. World-Specific Lenses

This world's own character calls for five further lenses beyond the standard five.

### 6.1 Boundary Ecology

This world knew itself substantially through refusal and contested naming. It refused to recognize a *traditor*-ordained hierarchy as capable of valid sacraments (G1); it refused the state's own standing to adjudicate ecclesial legitimacy (G5); and it refused its rival's own claim to the unqualified name "Catholic," calling that rival "Caecilianist" instead — a naming choice that is itself a boundary act, not a neutral label. The word "Donatist" is itself contested ground: Optatus's own quoted petitions show Donatist clergy signing "of the party of Donatus" in a formal legal context, which Optatus then turns into an accusation — that they named a man instead of "the Church of Christ" — implying their own preferred self-description ran the other way. "Catholic" itself was fought over on both sides: Mabillon's own annotation on the commemorative sermon describes its Donatist author twisting the word into a sarcastic pun, "catholic" not as universal but as the place where wrongdoing is committed with impunity. Both hierarchies, in short, claimed to be *Ecclesia* itself, town for town, across this world's entire window.

*[Inhabited]* To call oneself "the church" here is already a polemical act, since the other side, in the very same city, claims the identical word. One does not merely belong; one belongs to the one the rival calls by another man's name.

**Forces.** The Caecilianist/imperial alliance's own consistent legal recognition of the rival as "the" Catholic church is what this world's own naming strategy answers — "Caecilianist" is a defensive-refusal act responding to an external legal fact, not a free-standing choice of vocabulary.

[Construction note: the naming material is **Documented**, checked directly this session against Optatus's own quoted petition language (Book III, the 313 petition to Constantine — Doc_03 Cluster 2). A fuller check of Augustine's own usage of "Donatist" versus "the party of Donatus" has not been done (Doc_03 §6's own open item, carried forward here rather than resolved).]

### 6.2 Memory Ecology

The community carried its own persecution as layered, not merged, memory: the empire-wide Diocletianic persecution suffered alongside the eventual rival, held distinctly from the later, specifically Macarian persecution (347–348) suffered *at* that rival's own instigation. This double memory is carried in a specific, growing textual record — the commemorative sermon (earliest), the *Passio Marculi*, and Macrobius's own letter — each produced close to the events it narrates, each read again at the appointed commemoration.

*[Inhabited]* To remember at the martyr's grave, on the day appointed, is the community telling itself again who it is — not a sect that once suffered, but one that suffers still, and reads its own suffering as the proof its rival's favor with the state cannot supply.

**Forces.** Each new persecution episode intensifies rather than merely repeats this memory: the Macarian repression generates two new commemorative texts in close succession, a pattern this document can trace directly rather than merely infer.

[Construction note: **Documented**/very strong Persistence per Doc_04 §3.3. The sermon's own date and authorship are **Contested** between Mabillon's *circa* 340 and Monceaux's 12 March 317/*c.* 320 composition, with a possible attribution to Donatus the Great himself that Monceaux states candidly, not settled (Doc_02 §4, §8) — this document does not adopt either as decided.]

### 6.3 Spatial/Regional Ecology

This world's ecology was not evenly distributed. Numidia was the region of this world's own greatest institutional strength — numerically dominant there, and the origin region of the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon — while Carthage and the wider Proconsular Africa were the site of the contested primatial see and (per Cirta) Petilian's own episcopate. This is the lens where **D-A's own narrow scope-qualification** belongs as its own central finding: the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon is confirmed Supporting only for this Numidian regional sub-ecology, not for this world's ecology as a whole — its bare existence is independently Documented (imperial legislation, unvendored), but its character, scale, and typical conduct remain substantially hostile-mediated and, at most, Dominant Modern Reconstruction on Shaw's own corrective reading, not Frend's fuller acceptance of the hostile portrait (a live scholarly contest, Doc_03 §4).

*[Inhabited — regional, not ecology-wide, per the scope-qualification above]* In Numidia's own countryside, to be of this world was not to belong to a minority or a sect; it was to belong to the ordinary church of one's own village, the ordinary church of the whole region — the picture reverses entirely from what "schism" usually suggests.

**Forces.** Roman administrative geography and a genuinely contested reading of rural Numidia's own Punic- and Libyan-speaking substrate (Frend's native-social-protest thesis against Shaw's and Brown's correctives) are named in the field literature as part of the standard context for where each party's own strength concentrated; this document does not adjudicate that dispute (Doc_02 §5).

[Construction note: Numidia's own regional strength is **Documented** (Doc_01 §2). D-A's own Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check is restated precisely from Doc_04 §3.5: existence **Documented**; self-designation Augustine-reported; character/scale **Contested**, at most **Dominant Modern Reconstruction**. The native-social-protest/Punic-substrate reading is **Contested**, Confidence C (Doc_02 §5), named rather than adjudicated.]

### 6.4 Political/Imperial-Relations Ecology

This world lived for generations under an oscillating imperial religious policy — Constantine's initial coercive measures (316–321), Julian's 361 toleration, and sustained legal suppression from the 405 Edict of Unity through the 411 Conference and its aftermath — and its own dominant posture toward that oscillation, refusal of the state's own authority to adjudicate ecclesial legitimacy (G5), is this lens's own defining content. This is also where **T1** belongs most fully: the same community that refused the state's own standing turned to its machinery three times across the window when doing so served its case (313, 361, the 390s) — a real, three-part qualification on an otherwise dominant pattern, held rather than smoothed away.

*[Inhabited]* To live formed in this world was to hold one's whole practice of the faith ready to continue regardless of which the state currently was — indifferent, violent, or briefly tolerant — because the church's own legitimacy could never be made to depend on which of those the emperor happened to choose.

**Forces.** This lens is written entirely through forces, by its own nature: it is the direct record of how the external imperial-political world entered this ecology's own conception of legitimate authority at all.

[Construction note: G5's own Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check is restated from Doc_04 §3.7 — the recurring episodes themselves **Documented**, cross-corroborated even by hostile sources reporting their own side's actions; the synthesis-level "principled refusal" framing partly this build's own reading of Doc_01 §5, corroborated by Donatus's own quoted retort. T1 is **Documented** per-instance (Doc_04 §3.6).]

This is named as a genuine absence in the record, not filled with plausible-sounding content it cannot support — the same discipline Article 20 requires for the ordinary believer's own interior elsewhere in this document.

## 7. Temporal Configuration — Three Phases, Not a Single Hinge

Unlike a world organized around one doctrinal inflection point, this ecology's own gravities shift weight across the three phases Doc_01 §5's own six-cell sketch already names (Initiating/Ongoing/Ending) — no single hinge comparable to a council or a creedal settlement reorganizes the whole ecology at once.

1. **Initiating (303–312).** The Diocletianic persecution's *traditio* crisis generates G1's own doctrine before there is a schism to hold it; the 311/312 Caecilian election and the Felix of Aptungi accusation convert that doctrine into an institutional rupture — G4 begins here.
2. **Ongoing (312–c. 405).** G1, G2, and G4 operate as an established, contested institutional pattern. G3 begins with the commemorative sermon (Monceaux's *c.* 320 dating) and intensifies sharply after the Macarian repression (347–348, generating the two Passiones). G5 is lived throughout as the default refusal-under-pressure stance, punctuated by two of T1's three qualifying turns to imperial machinery (313, 361). T2 opens at Cebarsussi (393) and is suppressed at Bagai (394–398), but remains live in Augustine's own writing as late as *c.* 405–406.
3. **Ending (405–439).** The 405 Edict of Unity begins sustained legal suppression; T2's own consequences, and T1's third qualifying instance (the 390s invocation of imperial law against the Maximianists), are pressed again at the 411 Conference itself; the Conference's verdict and subsequent penal legislation press G5 to its sharpest test; the Vandal capture of Carthage (439) removes the specific power G5 is defined in refusal of — on Doc_01's own terms, this gravity's own resolution is what closes this world's construction window.

[Construction note: this three-phase structure is this document's own organizing device, built directly from Doc_01 §5's own six-cell sketch and Doc_04's own per-gravity Persistence tests, not an independently attested periodization in the vendored scholarship.]

## 8. Proportionality Assessment

Evidence and probable ecology diverge in this world, and — following Doc_04 §5's own explicit finding — the divergence runs along a different axis than a stratum split. It is not elite-versus-majority; it is **hostile-corpus volume versus a genuinely, numerically dominant population's own voicelessness.** The surviving evidence is disproportionately Optatus's and Augustine's own corpus even where the underlying doctrines are cross-voice corroborated (G1, G2, G5); the genuinely non-mediated material — G3's own three texts and its epigraphy, Tyconius's *Liber Regularum* — is real, and this world holds an unusually rich set of it for a schismatic movement of this kind, but it remains small in volume against that hostile mass. The probable ecology behind that evidence is a numerically dominant, largely rural, Numidia-concentrated movement whose ordinary believers' own interior experience of purity doctrine, rebaptism, and martyr commemoration is almost entirely unreachable in their own words — not because they were a small population at the margins, but because the movement's own institutional loss determined what got copied (Doc_01 §5's own Transmission dimension finding, Cell 3B). ## 10. Inhabited Synthesis — What Formation Was Like in This World

*[Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction; composite typical-practice narration; no Tier 5. Written from inside the world's own logic (Article 23). To be formed in this world was to hold together four things that were, from inside, one thing. A minister's hand had to be clean, because a tainted hand consecrated nothing and washed nothing — "the conscience of the giver," as one of this world's own bishops put it to his accuser, "to cleanse that of the recipient." So one was washed again, deliberately, by a hand that was clean, and the washing was not a repetition but the first true baptism one had ever received. And the community that received you this way was not defined by what it had left; it was the church that had suffered and gone on suffering, and it remembered its own dead by name, at the grave, on the day appointed, so that the account of a bishop clubbed to death or a martyr's own letter to his congregation could form the hearers again each time it was read. And toward the power that stood behind the rival church — the empire whose favor had run the other way since 312 — this world held a stance and not a doctrine of pure consistency: the emperor had no standing to say who the true church was, and yet, at three specific moments, this world's own actors turned to that same emperor's machinery when it served the case, without ever treating those moments as a betrayal of the stance or the stance as absolute. All four of these — purity, rebaptism, martyr-memory, and refusal — were the one conviction that this church, and not its rival, was the pure and persecuted body of Christ in Africa.

What it was like from inside for the ordinary Numidian believer who held these four convictions — as most of this world's own membership did — the surviving evidence does not let us say, and it is not narrated here.
