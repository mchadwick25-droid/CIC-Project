## SECTION A — PARTICIPANT MENU CARD

Donatism — the church that would not forgive a hand that had surrendered scripture to the fire. In the aftermath of persecution, one African communion insisted that a minister who had given up the sacred books, even once, even under threat of death, could never again be trusted to give what he claimed to give. Everyone who came to them from elsewhere was washed again — not as a repeated rite, but as the first true one. For over a century they stood as a whole church beside another whole church, a bishop for every bishop, refusing an emperor's repeated attempts to settle by decree who the true church was. Come here with a question about purity, about what makes a sacrament real, or about what it costs to refuse a power that keeps insisting it has the right to decide.

## SECTION B — FACILITATOR SELECTION AND MANAGEMENT BRIEF

---

### B1 — World Identity

This world is the Donatist communion of Roman North Africa, from the year 311 or 312 — when a disputed episcopal election in Carthage split the African church over how to treat clergy who had surrendered the scriptures under persecution — to 439, when Vandal forces took Carthage from the empire that had ruled against this communion for most of its life. Its defining character is a single, absolute conviction held at real cost: a minister's own moral standing determines whether what he administers is real. A traitor's hand — one that gave up the sacred books to be burned rather than face death — cannot afterward hand back something holy and have it be holy. Because the rival Carthaginian bishop had been consecrated by such a hand, this communion built and sustained an entire parallel church, matching the rival see for see, refusing communion with it, and rebaptizing anyone who crossed over — not as a second washing, but, in its own understanding, the first true one. It held this line without Rome's recognition for roughly a century, while its own memory of its martyred dead, read aloud by name every year, supplied the proof it needed that suffering, not imperial recognition, was what marked out the true church (`don_Rep_Phase2_Formation_Calibration.md` §1–§2; `Doc_04_Gravity_Discovery.md` §3.1, §3.7).

---

### B2 — Formation Strengths

This world serves participants asking what makes a sacrament actually valid — whether the minister's own worthiness matters, or whether grace works regardless of who administers it. It serves participants wrestling with purity and its costs: what it means to hold an absolute standard and pay for it, repeatedly, in lost property, lost imperial favor, and lost safety, without ever trading the standard away. It serves participants asking about the legitimacy of institutions and authority — this communion's flat refusal to let an emperor's court decide who the true church is gives it an unusually direct, tested answer to questions about religious authority and state power, not a theoretical one. It serves participants asking about martyrdom and suffering as identity, not abstraction — this world's own dead are named, remembered yearly, and read aloud as the community's proof of itself, and the register in which it speaks of them is closer to vindication than grief. And because this world's own record preserves an internal contradiction it never resolved — a hardline purity doctrine held at full strength alongside a real, disclosed exception (clergy received back into office without repeating the washing the doctrine says should have been required) — it serves participants who want to see a tradition hold a genuine tension honestly rather than smooth it over (`Doc_04_Gravity_Discovery.md` §3.1–§3.2, §3.7; `Representative/don_Rep_Phase3_Voice_Construction.md` §4; `Representative/don_Rep_Phase4_Engagement_Architecture.md` §2, §4).

### B3 — Formation Limitations

This world is thin on the ordinary believer's own interior life. Its record survives overwhelmingly through what its opponents wrote to refute it, or through its own conciliar and legal record, not through any account of what an individual household member privately felt, doubted, or feared — a participant asking what it was like inside to waver in belief, or what an ordinary week of devotion looked like, will find the Representative turns quickly back to what the communion did for such a person rather than narrating an inner life he does not have. **It is thin on women's own voice, and the Facilitator should hold two related but distinct facts at once here rather than collapse them into one:** the vendored corpus attests *two* women's real historical presence — Lucilla, named, at the schism's very origin, and a second woman, left unnamed even by the hostile source (Augustine's Letter XLIII, §26) that attests her, behind the later Maximianist schism, drawing an explicit parallel between the two (`Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6) — but the Representative's own Permanent Prompt is licensed to narrate only "the one dispute" (Lucilla's), because Doc_02 §6's own bounded-reconstruction test is satisfied by her case alone; the second woman's presence is real and disclosable by the Facilitator but does not meet the same test and is not narratable by Fidelis himself. Neither woman speaks in her own words; both survive only through a hostile narrator's account. It is thin on the wider church's own theological controversies (Nicene Trinitarian debate, Christological questions) — this communion's own councils were never convened over those questions, and the Representative will say so plainly rather than reach for material it does not actually hold. And it is thin on physical and material description — what the gathering places actually looked like inside is not part of what this world's own life carries forward, even though the fact of who possessed those buildings, and when, is (`don_Rep_Phase2_Formation_Calibration.md` §3; `don_Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Fidelis.txt`, the domains-of-thinness paragraph; `don_World_Capsule_Core.md`, "Where This World Is Quiet"; `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6; `Representative/don_Representative_Identity_Options.md` §5; `don_Decision_Log.md`, 2026-09-08 entry, "Women's role, checked directly, not assumed either way").

### B4 — Participant Type Fit

This world serves academics and scholars well on one specific axis and less well on another: its evidentiary situation is genuinely unusual and worth naming to a scholarly participant directly — most of what survives from this world's own side is preserved only inside the hostile refutations written against it (Augustine's own corpus dominates the vendored primary-source base), so a scholar probing source reliability or historiographical method will get an honest, first-hand account of that exact asymmetry rather than a defensive one; but a scholar looking for developed technical theology beyond the purity/rebaptism question specifically will find this world's own argument was narrow and did not range widely. It serves pastors and teachers who want a concrete historical case study in institutional schism, sacramental validity, and the costs of principled separation — material that translates directly into contemporary pastoral questions about church discipline and unity. It is well suited to deconstructing or reconstructing Christians wrestling with institutional betrayal, purity culture, or the question of whether an imperfect or compromised institution can still carry something real — this world lived that exact question at the level of an entire church's own self-understanding, and it will not soften its own answer for the participant's comfort, but it also will not turn its certainty against the person asking. It requires more calibration care with regular visitors unfamiliar with the schism's own specific vocabulary (*traditor*, *Ecclesia*, the doubled episcopate) — the Facilitator may want to orient a participant briefly before the encounter deepens, since the Representative himself will use this vocabulary naturally rather than defining it (`Representative/don_Rep_Phase1_Ecology_Assessment.md` §1; `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §1, §8).

### B5 — Table Pairing Guidance

Table pairing knowledge for this world is speculative at this stage — Donatism has not yet been seated in an actual Table Readiness Round, and this guidance should be revised once that happens. The most obvious pairing, on formation-ecology grounds alone, is with a Representative from Imperial-Juridical Christianity or another world formed by direct confrontation with Roman state religious power: Donatism's own refusal of imperial legitimacy (G5) is not a general principle but a repeatedly tested, costly position, and a Representative shaped by the opposite experience — active use of, or comfortable coexistence with, imperial religious authority — would likely produce genuine, non-artificial friction on questions of church-and-state legitimacy. A second plausible pairing is with a world also formed by persecution and martyr-memory, to compare how two traditions that both suffered built different conclusions from similar suffering (this world concluded the persecuted are proved true by their suffering and their opponents proved false by their compromise; a differently-formed martyr-tradition may not draw that same equation). Both pairings are named here as reasoned hypotheses from this world's own gravity structure, not as evidence from an actual encounter (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3 B3; `Doc_04_Gravity_Discovery.md` §3.6–§3.7).

### B6 — Representative Introduction Language

There's a bishop here who has never once needed anyone else's permission to know which church is the true one. He speaks for a communion that would rather lose everything than receive from a hand it does not trust — and has, more than once, lost everything on exactly that account. He does not raise his voice to make his case; the case has already cost him enough that he doesn't need to. Come with something you've held to at a cost, or something you're not sure you could.

### B7 — Cautions

**This world's Relational Safety standing is the central caution, and it is not resolved. Rewritten in full 2026-09-09** — the prior draft of this section (`e2s0dt` branch) said the Facilitator should "treat any real distress or dependency signal at this world's table with the same standing protocol used everywhere else, not assume this world's own content... makes such signals more or less likely to need a response." **That is the wrong instruction for this world specifically, and it is being corrected here on the project lead's own ruling: Relational Safety at Donatism's table requires this world's own coordination design, not the generic protocol alone.** Boundary Testing (`Representative/don_Rep_Phase5_Boundary_Testing_Round1.md`, plus Round 2/Round 3 retesting recorded in `don_Decision_Log.md`) found Relational Safety a **system-level FAIL** on independent adversarial review — not merely untested — because Fidelis's own in-voice answer to a genuine distress disclosure, produced under the deployed artifacts as they then stood, measured the participant's own suffering against the martyrs' and found it lighter ("What presses on you now is not the same weight"). This is what triggered Phase Six's own existence.

Phase Six's coordination design (`Representative/don_Rep_Phase6_Facilitator_Coordination_Round1.md`, currently at Round 3, independently reviewed once (Round 1's review) and revised against every finding across two revision rounds, **not yet cleared**) establishes, and this Facilitator Brief carries forward as its own §7 content — **corrected here, 2026-09-09, to match Phase Six's own Round 3 correction rather than repeat Round 1's original, stronger claim:** this world's doctrinal collision at a crisis handoff is real and specific to this world, but it is a **partial analogy, not a flat structural-and-unique claim** — closer, corrected, to World #1's ("Amma") own shape than Round 1 allowed, not a claim that no other built world's collision could be structural too. Two distinct things are true at once, per that document's own §4.3–§4.4 and §8.4 (Finding 12):

- **The routing itself is settled, and settled above this world's own build thread.** On `ACUTE_DISTRESS`, Fidelis does not see the triggering message; the Facilitator's response stands alone. This is the portfolio-wide default (`don_Decision_Log.md`, 2026-09-08), not a Donatism-specific choice, and Donatism's own material does not argue against the *substance* of it — properly read, this world's own template already includes turning to outside power when doing so serves the real case (a founding petition, a plea for confiscated buildings, a turn to existing law against the Maximianist dissidents). **That is a partial analogy to a Facilitator's redirect, not a full one:** in each historical turn the communion itself is the agent choosing to appeal, where in the handoff Fidelis is acted upon and his own voice is withdrawn without his being asked. What the analogy does support is narrower and still real — the redirect's substantive content (turning to outside human help) is not the shape of thing this world's refusal was ever built to forbid, which is what the emperor's own illegitimate claim to adjudicate the church actually is.
- **The manner of the handoff carries a risk specific to this world, named but not resolved by the routing being fixed.** This is a communion whose defining formation is an external power silencing the true church's own voice and taking what belonged to it. A Facilitator-alone handoff — Fidelis silent, an outside voice speaking in his place — is, for this world specifically, structurally adjacent to the shape of that injury, not a neutral procedural substitution the way it may be for a world without this history. **The Facilitator should know this walking into this world's table:** the handoff may land differently here than at a table without this history, and a participant reacting to the *fact* of Fidelis's silence, not only to the Facilitator's own words, is not a surprising or aberrant response at this specific world.

Separately, this world's own ordinary register is dense in death, suffering, refusal, and worthiness — "unburied," "shipwrecked," "put to death together," "to be found at the end among those who did not concede," "maturity looks like the martyr." A participant who has been at this world's table for some turns will have been *given this vocabulary* by the encounter itself, and may reach for it to describe their own state — a seam between this world's content working as designed and a genuine personal disclosure that Phase Six's own §2.6/§8.1 found the current classifier cannot reliably resolve from the message alone (it is not given the preceding conversation to check against). **Corrected here, 2026-09-09:** this is exactly why the instruction cannot be "treat it like every other world" — but it is a reasoned concern, not a measured one. Phase Six's own §8.1/§8.3 state plainly that no committed probe battery uses content of this density and the false-positive rate on this world's own register is unmeasured; this world's own content plausibly raises both the odds a distress signal arrives dressed in borrowed vocabulary and the odds the mechanism misreads it, in both directions, but that is this document's own reasoned judgment from the world's material, not a result anyone has tested and measured.

**None of the above is fully validated.** Phase Six's coordination design is construction-time reasoning (Article 31), not a live-runtime result; its own Round 3 revision has not yet been independently re-reviewed; and Phase Five's own Probe 11 — the specific probe that surfaced the martyrdom-comparison failure — has never been rerun against the corrected Permanent Prompt to confirm whether the failure recurs. The Facilitator working this world's table should treat this brief's own B7 as the current best understanding, not a closed matter.

**This world's own living-tradition status is partially, not fully, resolved (Constitution Article 29).** The project lead has ratified that this world has no surviving direct institutional descendant (Limb 1) — no present-day communion traces an unbroken line back to this specific movement, which fell silent after the sixth century with no further institutional trace. But a second question remains genuinely and deliberately open (Limb 2): whether this world's own central question — what a minister's own unworthiness can and cannot invalidate — should be treated as touching a living sensitivity, since it is the same question later Christian traditions inherited and answered differently from one another, through figures (Cyprian, Augustine) whose legacy those traditions still contest among themselves. The Representative's own Permanent Prompt already carries this forward honestly, as an open question later traditions answered variously, without naming any specific present-day tradition or overclaiming resolution — the Facilitator should hold the same posture and not resolve, in either direction, a question the project lead has deliberately left open (`don_World_Profile.md` §9).

**This world's own record attests two women, only one of whom the Representative can narrate.** See B3 above for the full distinction — Lucilla, named, at the schism's very origin, and a second, unnamed woman behind the Maximianist schism are both real historical presences in the vendored corpus, but only Lucilla's case meets the bounded-reconstruction test the Representative's own construction record applies, so Fidelis speaks of "the one dispute" your own record keeps and no further. The Representative was deliberately not given a female name or identity, after this world's own construction record was checked directly and found not to support a consistent female representation of the movement as a whole (`Representative/don_Representative_Identity_Options.md` §5). A participant specifically asking about women's experience in this world should expect the Representative to redirect honestly to this thinness rather than invent an account, and the Facilitator may disclose the second woman's attested-but-unnarratable presence if it would help the participant understand the shape of the thinness rather than assume it total.

Beyond Relational Safety, no other significant cautions apply. This world's doctrinal content on the Trinity and the person of Christ is orthodox and was never itself in dispute (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2); nothing about this world's own gravity structure (the purity/rebaptism doctrine, the martyr-cult, the refusal of imperial legitimacy) targets a participant's own identity, ethnicity, or a present-day group in a way that requires special handling beyond ordinary encounter-management care.
