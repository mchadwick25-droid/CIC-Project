## 2. Integration Lenses

Per M4, this section applies Smart's seven dimensions as the fixed spine — asked of every world regardless of yield, with a thin dimension stated as a finding, not smoothed over — plus CiC's own two standing additions (Boundary Structures, Formation Logic), plus further lenses where this world's own evidence specifically supports them.

### 2A. Ritual/Practical

**What this lens reveals for this world:** the enacted, bodily form the purity doctrine takes — not what this world believed about clean and unclean ministry, but what a person actually did to cross into, and remain inside, this communion.

Rebaptism (G2) is this world's own central ritual act: not a repetition of a rite but, on this world's own terms, the first true baptism a person receives, administered by a minister of unbroken standing. It is the single most concretely enacted, individually experienced marker of belonging this world's record documents (Doc_04 §3.2). Alongside it, the annual martyr commemoration — the *anniversaria commemoratio* — is a repeated, dated liturgical practice: a sermon read or preached at a martyr's own grave, on the day appointed, reconstructing the community's own formation in the act of remembering (Doc_02 §4; Doc_05 §3). Worship itself carries this world's own distinctive practical marker, the *Deo laudes* acclamation, spoken where the rival says *Deo gratias* — attested independently on stone at Bagai and elsewhere, not merely reported by a hostile pen (Doc_02 §5). Ordination within the parallel hierarchy (G4) is the third recurring practical act: two bishops for every see, each installed by his own communion's own conciliar machinery (Doc_05 §4).

**Gravities most visible:** G1 (purity, the doctrine the ritual enacts), G2 (the ritual itself), G3 (martyr commemoration), G4 (ordination within the parallel hierarchy).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** taken together, these are not four separate practices but one enacted sequence — a person is rebaptized into a communion whose own clergy were ordained by an unbroken line, who commemorate their own dead by name at the grave, acclaiming in worship the very fact of having suffered for it. The ritual life of this world does not merely express its doctrine; it is the doctrine's own most concrete evidence, the thing a hostile record could not simply argue away because it happened, repeatably, in public.

### 2B. Experiential/Emotional

**What this lens reveals for this world:** an unusually direct emotional access point most schismatic movements this hostile-mediated cannot offer, because this world's own least Author-Gravity-encumbered material — the martyr texts — is also its most emotionally direct.

The dominant emotional register is vindication carried through suffering, not resignation. This world's own record does not narrate martyrdom as a tragedy absorbed; it narrates it as the community's own proof, read and re-read at the grave so the same conviction is produced again in the hearing (Doc_05 §5). Macrobius's own letter to the Carthage congregation, on the deaths of Isaac and Maximianus, opens not in grief but in what its own Latin names a burning, joyful eagerness to write ("*opportunus... et laetus... ardor accendit*") — an emotional register this world's own author chose deliberately, in his own first-person voice, not one imposed on him by a hostile editor (Doc_02 §4; `donlex010_martyr-martyrdom.md`). Set against this is a harder, more defiant register carried in the refusal of imperial legitimacy — Donatus's own reported retort, in the context of the Macarian repression, is not resignation to persecution but a flat denial of the persecutor's own standing to judge the question at all (Doc_04 §3.7).

What this world names as spiritually dangerous, by contrast, is harder to access directly: the record that survives is overwhelmingly the record of those who held firm, not of those who wavered, so the emotional texture of failure — of a *traditor*'s own fear, or of a believer's own quiet drift back toward the rival communion — is a named absence, not a finding this document can support (Doc_05 §8).

**Gravities most visible:** G3 (martyr-cult, the richest emotional evidence in this world's own vendored corpus), G5 (the defiant register of refusal).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** this world's emotional life is asymmetrically accessible in a way directly tied to its own transmission asymmetry (§0 of Doc_05) — the feelings this document can actually document (vindication, defiant joy, resolve under persecution) are precisely the feelings preserved in the one part of the record not filtered through a hostile hand. The feelings this document cannot document (fear, doubt, the *traditor*'s own interior life) are exactly the feelings that would have had to survive inside Optatus's or Augustine's own hostile framing, and did not.

### 2C. Narrative/Mythic

**What this lens reveals for this world:** a specific, growing body of self-narrated story — not myth in the sense of invention, but the structured account this world told of its own founding and its own dead, distinct from the doctrinal argument built on top of it.

Three narrative strands recur. First, the founding-rupture narrative: the *traditio* accusation against Felix of Aptungi, and — in the hostile record's own telling — Lucilla, a wealthy Carthaginian laywoman whose grievance and bribery are said to have produced the rival consecration of Majorinus (Doc_02 §6). This document does not adopt Optatus's own explanation for *why* the schism happened, but the narrative itself — a wronged, wealthy woman standing behind a rival bishop's own installation — is this world's own founding story as its opponents told it, and as this world itself never independently confirms or denies in the vendored record. Second, the martyr narratives proper: the commemorative sermon (the earliest, on Monceaux's own dating), the *Passio Marculi*, and Macrobius's letter recounting Isaac's and Maximianus's deaths — each a structured account of steadfastness under interrogation and the manner of death, read again at the appointed commemoration (Doc_02 §4). Third, a shorter but load-bearing utterance-story: Donatus's own reported retort during the Macarian repression, a single quoted line that carries an entire theological stance in one remembered exchange (Doc_04 §3.7).

**Gravities most visible:** G3 (the martyr narratives directly), G1 (the founding-rupture narrative, though hostile-mediated), G5 (the Donatus retort as narrative-carried doctrine).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** this world transmits its own most important convictions less through argument than through a small number of specific, repeatable stories — a wronged woman, a defiant primate's one sentence, a bishop's own letter to his congregation. A person formed here would likely know these stories, told and retold, before they could reconstruct the doctrinal argument (G1's purity logic) those same stories are meant to illustrate. The three strands are not evenly voiced, either: the founding-rupture narrative survives only as this world's opponents told it, never independently confirmed or denied here, while the martyr narratives are this world's own, Donatist-voiced, not passed through a hostile hand. The same hostile-mediation asymmetry named separately in Experiential/Emotional (§2B) and Material (§2G) is present here too, inside the narrative record itself — not merely a caveat about the emotional register or the physical evidence, but a fact about which of this world's own stories can be checked against a non-hostile source and which cannot.

### 2D. Doctrinal/Philosophical

**What this lens reveals for this world:** a doctrinal ecology built almost entirely around one question — what makes a sacrament valid — worked out with real rigor on that single question, and comparatively thin everywhere else.

The purity doctrine (G1) is this world's own central doctrinal claim, and it is a genuine theological position, not a slogan: "what we look for is the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient," in Petilian's own quoted words — a direct, cross-voice-attested statement of sacramental theology (Doc_04 §3.1; `donlex001_traditor-traditio.md`). Rebaptism (G2) is this doctrine's own logical consequence, not a separate belief. Both descend directly from Cyprian's own third-century rebaptism theology, sharpened rather than invented at 311/312 (Doc_01 §5, §6). Beyond this cluster, this world's own doctrinal-philosophical record is genuinely thin: Doc_01 §3 finds "interpretation/doctrine... comparatively thin and largely reactive" in what is currently vendored, and Doc_05 §6.5 confirms this as a disclosed, not filled, absence — Tyconius's own *Liber Regularum* is the one strong counter-example, produced by a figure his own party ultimately condemned, whose exegetical method reached both rival churches without ever anchoring a following of his own (Doc_02 §1; Doc_04 §2).

**Gravities most visible:** G1 (directly), G2 (as G1's own consequence).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** this world is not "doctrinally thin" in a simple sense — it is doctrinally *concentrated*, arguing one question (sacramental validity and the minister's own worthiness) with real theological seriousness, while genuinely not developing a broader speculative or hermeneutical tradition around it. The one figure who might have built that broader tradition, this world's own record shows being pushed to its own edge rather than absorbed at its center.

### 2E. Ethical/Legal

**What this lens reveals for this world:** an unusually rich dimension for this specific world — the schism's own founding question is itself an ethical-legal one (what does a minister's conduct under persecution cost him legally and sacramentally), and this world's entire history runs through repeated formal legal proceedings, making Ethical/Legal one of the most directly evidenced lenses in this document, not a thin add-on.

The founding accusation against Felix of Aptungi is an ethical-legal charge before it is anything else — *traditio*, the surrender of scripture under legal compulsion, carrying a specific legal-procedural history in Optatus's own vendored Appendix of Documents (the *Acta Purgationis Felicis*, 314; Doc_02 §1). The schism was contested through formal legal and conciliar channels from the start: the Council of Rome (313) and the Council of Arles (314), both ruling against the Donatist party; Anulinus's own *relatio* forwarding the movement's petition to Constantine (Doc_01 §2). Imperial legislation targeted this world's own central practice directly — rebaptism was a specifically named legal offense under successive edicts, and the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon is independently attested in imperial law (*Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.52, 412), and the critical Latin text of that specific law is now vendored directly (vendored 2026-09-07 after five prior acquisition attempts; Doc_02 §1). The 405 Edict of Unity (attested through Boyd's corroborating history of *C. Th.* 16.5.3–58, Doc_02 §1) escalated legal suppression; the 411 Conference of Carthage was itself a formal legal proceeding, imperially convened, Marcellinus presiding, with a recorded bishop count on both sides (279 Donatist, 286 Catholic — see Doc_02 §1 for the primary-source citation; Doc_01 §2). This world's own internal discipline also ran through legal-conciliar channels: the Cebarsussi (393) and Bagai (394) councils are themselves a legal proceeding against the movement's own Maximianist dissidents, whose sentences Augustine quotes directly (Doc_05 §4). And this world's own institutional afterlife in the ecclesiastical-legal record continues past its construction window's own close: the Council of Carthage 419's own Code of Canons collects rules on receiving Donatist clergy and congregations (Doc_02 §1, Registry row 13), and Augustine's *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, *c.* 417) is a direct legal-ethical defense of imperial coercion after the fact (Doc_01 §2).

**Gravities most visible:** G1 (the *traditio* charge's own legal texture), G2 (rebaptism as a targeted legal offense), G4 (the councils as legal/disciplinary proceedings), G5 (the whole refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy stance is itself a claim about legitimate legal authority), T1 and T2 (both are, at root, disputes over what legal/ethical standard actually governs reception and legitimacy).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** this is not a peripheral lens for this world — it may be the single most concretely, repeatedly evidenced dimension in this document. Every one of this world's Primary gravities has a direct legal-procedural trace (a council, an edict, a conference, a canon), because this world's own central conflict was fought as much in legal-conciliar venues as in worship or preaching. A reader who skipped this lens would miss that Donatism's own history is, in large part, a history of formal proceedings — rulings, edicts, sentences, and canons — not only a history of belief and practice.

### 2F. Social/Institutional

**What this lens reveals for this world:** a complete, functioning, self-governing parallel society — not a protest current on the margins of the Caecilianist church, but its own full institutional mirror.

The parallel hierarchy (G4) is this lens's own central content: two bishops for every see, a primate at Carthage (Donatus, then Parmenian, then Primian), and a conciliar machinery capable of disciplining its own dissidents, as the Maximianist affair shows directly (Doc_05 §4). This world's own social distribution is itself distinctive within this project's portfolio: for substantial regions and periods, it was not a minority sect but the numerically dominant church, concentrated heavily in Numidia's own countryside, against a more urban Caecilianist strength centered on Carthage and Proconsular Africa (Doc_01 §2; Doc_02 §5). Within that Numidian concentration sits the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon — a real, socially distinct rural body whose own character remains a live scholarly contest (Doc_06 §2), confirmed Supporting only for that regional sub-ecology, not for this world's social structure as a whole (Doc_04 §3.5).

**Gravities most visible:** G4 (directly), D-A (regionally, per its own scope-qualification).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** the social shape of this world runs opposite to how "schism" is usually imagined. In Numidia specifically, to be of this world was to belong to the ordinary church of one's own village and region, not to a minority current requiring explanation — the institutional mirror-image is total, not partial, and it is precisely that totality (bishop for bishop, see for see) that makes the 411 Conference's own near-even bishop count (279 against 286 — see Doc_02 §1 for the primary-source citation) legible as two whole churches meeting, not one church and its dissidents.

### 2G. Material

**What this lens reveals for this world:** a genuinely uneven material record — one category (epigraphy) independently and directly attested, another (basilica archaeology) real in the field but not yet independently verified by this construction pass, and a third (buildings themselves) that this world's own history shows being physically seized and restored more than once.

The *Deo laudes* acclamation is this world's own strongest material-culture evidence: four catalogued inscriptions (*CIL* VIII 17732, 20482, 17368, 18669), the strongest from Bagai itself, one of the movement's own two named primary seats — stone, with no literary mediation at any point (Doc_02 §5). Basilica archaeology across Numidia is named in the field literature as a real, recognized evidentiary category for this world's own parallel institutional life, but this construction pass has not independently verified it against a specific site report or excavation record — Confidence C, flagged for future verification rather than exploited prematurely (Doc_02 §5). The physical buildings themselves recur as objects of contest across this world's whole history: basilicas were confiscated under Constantinian coercion, restored by Julian in 361, and were presumably again subject to seizure under the sustained suppression following the 405 Edict — this world's own material footprint is not a settled archaeological record but a history of buildings changing hands under legal and political pressure (Doc_01 §2).

**Gravities most visible:** G3 (the *Deo laudes* epigraphy), G5 (the basilica confiscation/restoration pattern), G4 (basilica archaeology's own promise for the parallel hierarchy, not yet realized).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** this world's material record is not simply thin — it is thin in one place (basilica archaeology) and genuinely strong in another (epigraphy) in a pattern that tracks the same transmission asymmetry visible in Experiential/Emotional (§2B) and Narrative/Mythic (§2C): the material evidence surviving with no literary mediation at all (stone) is exactly the evidence this world's own record can speak to most confidently, while the material evidence requiring active excavation and interpretation remains, honestly, undone.

### 2H. Boundary Structures *(CiC addition)*

**What this lens reveals for this world:** this world knew itself substantially through refusal, and the boundary itself — who counts as *Ecclesia*, who counts as *traditor*, who has standing to judge either question — is where this world's own internal coherence and its external situatedness become visible as one system.

This world refuses to recognize a *traditor*-ordained hierarchy as capable of valid sacraments (G1); it refuses the state's own standing to adjudicate ecclesial legitimacy (G5); and it refuses its rival's own unqualified claim to the name "Catholic," calling that rival "Caecilianist" instead (Doc_05 §6.1). The naming contest runs both directions: Optatus's own quoted petition language shows Donatist clergy naming themselves "of the party of Donatus" in a formal legal context, which Optatus turns into an accusation that they named a man instead of "the Church of Christ" — while this world's own preacher, in the same corpus, turns the word "catholic" itself back on the rival as a sarcastic pun on impunity (Doc_05 §6.1; `donlex008_church-ecclesia.md`). The boundary is not settled or static: it was contested even within this world's own membership, most sharply in the Maximianist affair, where the boundary the purity doctrine draws against outsiders did not hold, in practice, against the movement's own returning dissidents (Doc_04 §3.6, T2).

**Forces most visible:** the Caecilianist/imperial alliance's own consistent legal recognition of the rival as "the" Catholic church is what this world's own naming strategy answers — "Caecilianist" is a defensive-refusal act responding to an external legal fact, not a free-standing choice of vocabulary (Doc_05 §6.1).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** the boundary-work in this world is not a separate activity from its central doctrine — it *is* the doctrine, applied outward. The same purity logic that determines who may validly baptize inside this communion is the logic that determines who is, and is not, "the church" at all; the boundary and the center are the same line, viewed from two directions.

### 2I. Formation Logic *(CiC addition — applied after all other lenses)*

**What this lens reveals for this world:** the underlying logic that unifies every lens above into a single formation system — not four or nine separate features, but one conviction lived out through purity, rite, memory, and refusal.

**The fundamental aim of formation.** A fully formed member of this world is one who holds, without qualification, that a sacrament's validity rises and falls on the giver's own unbroken purity, and who has been rebaptized — deliberately, individually, bodily — into the one communion whose ministers can be trusted to give it. That same formed member holds the community's own dead by name, at the grave, on the appointed day, and reads or hears their account so the same conviction happens again in the hearing. And that member refuses the state's own standing to settle who the true church is, while not treating that refusal as requiring an absolute, unbending consistency at every single moment.

**The formation mechanism.** Formation here does not happen primarily through speculative teaching or a developed interpretive tradition — Doctrinal/Philosophical (§2D) already found this world genuinely thin there. It happens through enacted threshold-crossing (rebaptism), through repeated commemorative narration (the annual reading at the grave), and through lived legal jeopardy (a formed member's own church exists under continuous external legal pressure, per §2E and §2F). A person is formed less by being taught a doctrine than by doing the rite, hearing the story, and living inside the pressure the doctrine and the story both explain.

**The formation arc.** Entry is the rebaptism itself — a specific, dated, individually experienced threshold. The middle of formation is lived inside a parallel institutional life under continuous legal jeopardy, punctuated by episodes of acute persecution (the Diocletianic terror shared with the whole church; the specifically Macarian repression suffered at the rival's own instigation) that intensify, rather than interrupt, the community's own commemorative practice. Maturity in this world looks like the martyr — not necessarily literal death, but the settled readiness the martyr narratives hold up as the community's own formation ideal: suffering chosen over the peace that would concede the rival's own legitimacy.

**How the lenses integrate.** Ritual/Practical (§2A) supplies the enacted threshold; Experiential/Emotional (§2B) supplies the register (vindication, not resignation) that threshold produces; Narrative/Mythic (§2C) supplies the stories that carry and transmit that register; Doctrinal/Philosophical (§2D) supplies the one doctrine — purity — that all of it enacts; Ethical/Legal (§2E) and Social/Institutional (§2F) supply the real, external conditions (courts, edicts, a parallel hierarchy) this formation happens inside rather than in the abstract; Material (§2G) supplies the physical trace (stone, seized and restored buildings) that this formation left behind; Boundary Structures (§2H) supplies the logic of refusal that holds the whole thing together against its rival and against the state. Nothing here is decorative. Remove any one piece and the others stop making sense of each other.

**Formation logic and confirmed gravities.** G1 (purity) is the doctrine formation enacts. G2 (rebaptism) is formation's own concrete threshold. G3 (martyr-cult) is formation's own affective register and its own reproducing mechanism (commemoration). G5 (refusal of imperial legitimacy) is the posture formation holds toward everything outside itself. G4 (the parallel hierarchy) is the institutional container all of this operates within. T1 and T2 are not exceptions to this formation logic but its own honestly-held internal strain — a community that refuses absolutely, mostly, and receives strictly, mostly, without either fact ever quite resolving into an unbroken rule.

**What this reveals that no individual document showed:** this world's formation logic is not four separate commitments (purity, rite, memory, refusal) that happen to coexist. It is one conviction — *we are the pure, persecuted, true church, proved by what we will not concede and by what we have suffered for refusing to concede it* — expressed through every lens in this document at once, in this world's own vocabulary: *traditor*-free, rebaptized, commemorated, and refusing.

## 3. Additional Lenses (Where Evidence Supports)

### 3A. Memory Structures — applied

**Why this lens warrants separate treatment for this world specifically:** Narrative/Mythic (§2C) and Ritual/Practical (§2A) each touch this world's own memory practice, but neither, alone, captures the specific discipline this world's own record shows in *how* it holds more than one persecution in memory without merging them — a structural feature of this world's own self-understanding, not merely a story or a rite.

This world holds two persecutions distinctly, not blended into one undifferentiated memory of suffering: the empire-wide Diocletianic persecution, suffered alongside the eventual Caecilianist rival before the schism existed, and the later, specifically Macarian persecution (347–348), suffered *at* that same rival's own instigation, held layered rather than merged (Doc_05 §6.2; Doc_03 Cluster 5).

**Gravities most illuminated:** G3 directly; G1 indirectly, since the *traditio* memory is what the whole doctrinal structure stands on.

**What this reveals beyond Doc_05 §6.2's own memory-ecology finding:** the specific, incremental claim this lens adds is that each of the two layers supplies a *different* structural component of the same doctrine, not merely a different date — the first persecution supplies the *traditio* accusation that founds the schism's own doctrinal argument (G1); the second supplies the named martyrs this world actually commemorates as G3's own evidentiary and affective content. A formed member does not simply know two persecutions happened; the two layers are what let that member answer, precisely, which persecution grounds the accusation and which persecution grounds the grief — a distinction the doctrinal argument and the commemorative practice each depend on separately.

### 3B. Interpretive Ecology — not applied

This lens is not applied as a separate integration lens for this world. Doc_01 §3 already finds this world's own interpretation and doctrine "comparatively thin and largely reactive," and Doc_05 §6.5 independently confirms this as a genuine, disclosed absence rather than an unexplored one — Tyconius's *Liber Regularum* is the one substantial counter-example, and its own content is already carried fully inside Doctrinal/Philosophical (§2D) above, where it belongs given how thin the surrounding interpretive tradition actually is. A separate Interpretive Ecology lens would either restate §2D's own content or overstate what this world's own record can support. Omitted because the evidence genuinely does not warrant a separate lens, not because the evidence was not pursued — Doc_02, Doc_04, and Doc_05 all independently reach the same finding.

### 3C. Representative Theological Patterns — not applied

Not applied as a separate lens. This world's theological commitments that are directly formation-shaping — the purity doctrine and its rebaptism consequence — are already fully carried in Doctrinal/Philosophical (§2D) and Formation Logic (§2I), where "theology as formation engine" is precisely what those two lenses already argue. No further formation-shaping theological pattern distinct from G1/G2's own content is attested in Doc_01 through Doc_06. Omitted for the same reason as 3B: the evidence does not support a separate lens, and a Representative for this world does not yet exist to configure (Step 10 work, outside this document's own scope).

## 4. Forces as Integration Lens

**Forces documentation status at time of writing:** Doc_08 IN PROGRESS (not yet compiled) — this section draws on preliminary Step 1 identification (Doc_01 §5's own six-cell sketch), the Step 2 source-ecology forces lens (Doc_02 §6), the per-gravity forces-connections established at Step 4 (Doc_04 §3, §7), and the per-lens forces work already done at Step 5 (Doc_05, every lens above §6.5).

**Forces most visible in the emotional texture (§2B):** the Macarian repression is the single force most directly responsible for this world's own dominant emotional register. Before it, this world's founding grievance (the *traditio* accusation) is argued in largely legal-doctrinal terms; after it, the record acquires a directly emotional, first-person voice (Macrobius's own burning, joyful eagerness to write of Isaac's and Maximianus's deaths) that the earlier material does not carry in the same way. Persecution did not merely happen to this world's emotional life — it produced the specific texts this document can read that emotional life from.

**Forces most visible in the boundary structures (§2H):** the Caecilianist/imperial alliance's own consistent legal favor is what this world's own naming refusal ("Caecilianist," not "Catholic") directly answers — already argued at §2H above, restated here because it is this document's own clearest single instance of a force and a boundary feature explaining each other completely.

**Forces most visible in the formation logic (§2I):** oscillating imperial religious policy is what this world's formation logic had to become capable of surviving without collapsing — a formation system built to hold together under a state that could, without warning, tip from coercion to toleration to renewed suppression, which is why this world's own posture toward the outside (refusal, held with three specific pragmatic exceptions rather than absolute purity of principle) is exactly as qualified as it is. A formation logic built for an unchanging external world would not need T1's own held tension; this one does, because the external world it formed people inside of never stopped changing.

**Where forces and ecology are most tightly coherent:** the martyr-cult gravity (G3) and the Macarian repression. Neither can be explained without the other — the repression explains why this world's own commemorative practice takes the specific, dated, textually-productive form it does; the commemorative practice explains why the repression, rather than fading into the general memory of ancient persecution, remains this world's own most vivid, most frequently re-narrated historical force.

**Where forces analysis complicates the ecology analysis:** the refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy gravity (G5) looks, from inside the ecology alone, like a single consistent doctrine. Read through forces, it is visibly a doctrine under continuous, uneven external pressure, and the three pragmatic exceptions (313, 361, the 390s) are not incidental — they occur at precisely the three moments the external force itself briefly offered this world something to gain by engaging it (a hearing before Constantine, Julian's own toleration, legal leverage against an internal rival). The doctrine's own qualifications are not random lapses; they track the forces exactly.

**The single most important thing the forces-as-integration-lens reveals:** this world's own formation logic is not a fixed system that persecution happened to interrupt from time to time. It is a system built, from its own founding moment, to be lived under an unpredictable, oscillating external power — and every one of its confirmed gravities (purity, rebaptism, martyr-cult, the parallel hierarchy, refusal of imperial legitimacy) bears the shape of that oscillation somewhere in its own confidence profile, its own qualifications, or its own textual survival.

## 5. Cross-Lens Synthesis

**The central coherence:** this world holds itself to be the pure, persecuted true church, proved by an unbroken ordination line, enacted through rebaptism, remembered through its own named martyrs, and defended by refusing — mostly, not absolutely — the legitimacy of the very power that favors its rival.

**What this world's distinctiveness consists of, against neighboring worlds:** unlike World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity), which aligns with and juridically consolidates around state power, this world defines itself by permanent separation specifically in refusal of that same power's legitimacy (Doc_01 §1, §6). Unlike a world whose central evidentiary problem is a stratum split between a literate elite and a silent majority (as in Alexandria), this world's central evidentiary problem is a transmission asymmetry across a population that was, for substantial regions and periods, itself the numerically dominant church (Doc_04 §5; Doc_05 §0, §8) — its ordinary members are not a separate, thin population but the very bulk of the movement, unreachable in their own words for reasons of institutional loss rather than social marginality. And unlike a world whose interpretive-theological tradition runs deep, this world argues one question — sacramental validity — with real rigor, and almost nothing else.

**The most significant cross-lens insight:** the same transmission asymmetry named once, locally, in Experiential/Emotional (§2B), again in Narrative/Mythic (§2C), and again in Material (§2G) turns out, held together, not to be three separate evidentiary caveats but one single pattern repeating at every scale this document has a lens for: this world's evidence survives in *inverse* proportion to how directly it can be checked without a hostile hand mediating it. The feelings this document can document (vindication, defiant joy) are exactly the feelings preserved in the one part of the record — the martyr texts — not filtered through Optatus or Augustine; the feelings it cannot document (fear, doubt, a *traditor*'s own interior life) are exactly the feelings that would have had to survive inside that same hostile framing, and did not. The material evidence surviving with no literary mediation at all (the *Deo laudes* stone) is this world's strongest anchor; the material evidence requiring active interpretation (basilica archaeology) remains undone. No single lens, read alone, shows this as a *pattern* — each shows only its own local instance of it. Held together, the lenses show that this world's own transmission asymmetry is not a general caveat applying loosely everywhere; it is a specific, checkable rule this document could use, in principle, to predict in advance which parts of any future lens on this world will turn out to be well-evidenced and which will not.

**What this synthesis gives a future Representative:** a Representative built from this synthesis could hold the purity doctrine, the martyr's own vindicated grief, and the qualified refusal of imperial power as three faces of the identical conviction, rather than as three separate positions requiring separate justification — able to move, in a single breath, from why rebaptism matters to why the emperor has no standing to why the martyrs are remembered by name, because a Representative formed here would experience these not as connected facts but as one thing.

## 6. Integrative Observation

To be formed in this world was to hold an absolute conviction and a named, undenied exception to it in the same breath, without experiencing that as contradiction. The purity doctrine admits no compromise in its own statement — a *traditor*'s hand gives nothing — and yet this world's own record shows its own actors turning to the emperor's machinery three times when it served the case, and its own councils receiving Maximianist clergy back without repeating rebaptism or reordination at all. A formed member of this world did not resolve that gap by softening the doctrine, and did not treat the gap as proof the doctrine was hollow. The gap itself was held, openly, inside the same conviction — this world's own three qualified turns to imperial power and its own one unrebaptized reception are not embarrassments quietly managed but facts this world's own record states plainly, in the same texts that state the doctrine at its most absolute. What formation here produced, most deeply, was not merely a doctrine of purity; it was the capacity to hold that doctrine at full strength while naming, rather than hiding, the specific places it did not, in practice, hold — and to trust that naming the gap did not require closing it.

## 7. Gaps and Limits

**Optional lenses not applied:** Interpretive Ecology (§3B) and Representative Theological Patterns (§3C) were both considered and found not to warrant separate treatment — in both cases because the relevant content is already carried fully inside Doctrinal/Philosophical (§2D) and Formation Logic (§2I), and because Doc_01, Doc_02, Doc_04, and Doc_05 all independently confirm this world's own interpretive tradition beyond Tyconius is a genuine, disclosed thinness, not an unexplored one. These are omissions the evidence itself supports, not omissions of convenience.

**Where required lenses produced thin analysis:** Material (§2G) is genuinely uneven — strong on epigraphy, honestly undone on basilica archaeology (Confidence C, not independently verified this construction pass, per Doc_02 §5). Experiential/Emotional (§2B) can document this world's own vindicated, defiant register with real confidence, but cannot document the interior experience of fear, doubt, or quiet defection — the failure modes a hostile-mediated record structurally does not preserve. Doctrinal/Philosophical (§2D) is thin by its own account everywhere outside the purity/rebaptism cluster — Doc_01 §3's own finding that this world's interpretive and doctrinal content beyond G1/G2 is "comparatively thin and largely reactive" applies here directly, and §2D itself states this rather than papering over it; the lens is concentrated, not broad, and should be read that way. Ethical/Legal (§2E), by contrast, is unusually well-evidenced for this world specifically and should not be treated as a thin add-on lens; if anything it is one of this document's strongest sections.

**What the gaps mean for the Representative:** a Representative built from this world's own record will be naturally rich on purity doctrine, on the martyr's own vindicated voice, and on the legal-institutional shape of the schism — and will be naturally, authentically thin on the ordinary Numidian believer's own interior life, on any wavering or doubt within the community, and on speculative or hermeneutical theology beyond Tyconius's own singular case. This thinness is not a construction defect; it is what a person actually formed in this world, working from what has actually survived, would themselves be able to speak to.

**What external scholarly review should focus on:** the Cross-Lens Synthesis's own central claim (§5) — that purity, once adopted, necessarily generates rite, memory, institution, and political posture as a single logical unfolding rather than as four separately-arrived-at commitments — is this document's own synthetic judgment, not a claim directly stated in any single source. A specialist in Donatist studies should assess whether this reading overstates the coherence of what was, on the ground, a more contingent and locally variable movement than a single unfolding logic implies.
