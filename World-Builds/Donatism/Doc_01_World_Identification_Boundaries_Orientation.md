## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** Donatism.

**Short Description:** The rigorist alternative communion that split from the North African Catholic church over the validity of sacraments and ordinations administered by clergy suspected of having surrendered scripture during the Diocletianic persecution — from the disputed 311/312 election of Caecilian as bishop of Carthage, through a full parallel episcopal hierarchy contesting the mainstream Caecilianist church town-for-town across Roman North Africa, to escalating imperial legal suppression after the 411 Conference of Carthage and the disruption of the Vandal conquest of Carthage (439), which closes this world's own construction window — within, but ending well before, this phase's own ceiling at Chalcedon (451).

**Core Identity:** This is a world of contested legitimacy and parallel institutional life, not a doctrinal heresy — a communion holding standard North African Latin Trinitarian and Christological confession while claiming to be the one true, pure "Church of the Martyrs" against a rival it regarded as *traditor*-tainted and, from 312 onward, state-favored. Its formation logic is boundary-and-purity-centered before it is doctrinal: rebaptism as the rite that marks who truly belongs, martyr and confessor memory as the community's own self-understanding, and clerical purity (a *traditor*-free ordination line) as the ground of sacramental validity, in a way distinct from any of the other confirmed worlds in this project's portfolio.

**Distinctive Contribution:** No other confirmed world in this project's portfolio reconstructs a sustained internal schism contesting the very definition of the true church from inside a shared confessional and cultural tradition, rather than encountering a rival tradition from outside it. World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity) reconstructs a communion's alignment with and juridical consolidation of state power; this world reconstructs the opposite and adjacent answer — permanent separation from the mainstream specifically in refusal of that same power's legitimacy. This world "is not [World #6's] rival for uniqueness so much as its clarifying contrast," in `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §1's own framing of the same pairing from its own side.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed. Per Constitution Article 29 and Formation World Blueprint V7.3 §17, Living Tradition Status confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform on its own behalf. Article 29's gate is itself conditional — it attaches "where a formation world's reconstruction touches a tradition that continues into the present, including where that contact is mediated through a specific figure, practice, institution, or lineage of contested standing among present-day traditions." Both limbs bear on this world: the first is addressed by the descendant question below; the second may bear on this world's own mediation through Cyprian, Augustine, and the rebaptism question, all of contested standing among present-day traditions — flagged for the project lead's own weighing at freeze, not resolved here (Blueprint V7.3 §17 separately provides for "a note to the external scholarly reviewer" identifying such contestation where a world's formation runs through a figure of contested standing). Distinct from several other confirmed worlds: unlike World #6's Roman and Constantinopolitan primacy strands, or Syriac Christianity's continuation into the modern Church of the East, this world's own preliminary assessment finds no clear surviving institutional descendant. The historical record shows the movement persisting for centuries beyond this world's own construction window, with the record falling silent after Gregory the Great's correspondence in the 590s and no attestation of an organized Donatist body thereafter — but no continuous line to a present-day communion is documented at any point. Whether Article 29's gate attaches at all — on either the no-documented-descendant question or the mediated-contact-through-a-contested-standing-figure question — is itself the project lead's call at freeze. **Status: PENDING.**

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** c. 311/312–439 CE. This is narrower than the phase's own ceiling (70–451 CE) and narrower than World #6's own window (312–451) — this world's own construction window closes at the Vandal capture of Carthage, not at Chalcedon, per the reasoning below.

- **Beginning point (311/312):** the disputed election and consecration of Caecilian as bishop of Carthage, and the *traditio* accusation against his consecrator, Felix of Aptungi, which produced the rival consecration of Majorinus by the rigorist party (succeeded, from c. 313, by Donatus, from whom the movement's name derives). Sources place the initiating dispute in either 311 or 312 depending on how the transition from persecution to episcopal election is dated; both years are carried here rather than resolved to a false precision the sources do not support. This is a defensible beginning point under the Framework's Temporal Scope test: it marks a genuine ecological rupture — before it, North African Christianity's rigorist and moderate readings of Cyprian's own third-century purity theology coexisted within one communion; after it, they never did again.
- **Ending point (439):** the Vandal capture of Carthage, the culmination of the Vandal invasion of Roman North Africa begun in 429. This is a defensible ending point on the Framework's own terms, distinct from — and earlier than — both this phase's ceiling (451) and World #6's own window. The two-party Donatist/Caecilianist-Catholic contest itself does not end in 439 — `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §1 records that the movement is "still a live problem in Gregory the Great's Register in the 590s"; Gregory's own correspondence — **now partially vendored** (`gregory-great_epistolae-selectae_turchi1907.txt`, Registry row 54, 2026-09-07; corrected 2026-09-08, Round 3 Opus review, from this line's own stale "not yet vendored" claim) — directly documents Donatist rebaptism of Catholics in Numidia specifically (his letter to Columbus, bishop of Numidia, 592), documenting that same contest continuing well past this world's own window, though Doc_04 §3.2 does not extend that into a claim about the movement's own scale or character at that date beyond the letters' own words. What 439 actually removes is the Roman imperial state as the Catholic-aligned adjudicating and coercing power — the condition this world's entire refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy pattern is defined against, and the condition every one of this section's own Historical Pressures depends on. That is the genuine ecological rupture this ending point marks. The two boundaries are distinct and must not be conflated: this world's own window (311/312–439) is a choice about where the Roman-imperial phase of this world's own story has its own natural rupture; the movement's actual historical persistence beyond that window, into the 590s and later, under different political conditions, is a separate fact.

**Geographic Centers:** Carthage, with the contest replicated town-for-town across Roman North Africa.

- **Carthage:** the seat of both rival bishoprics from the outset (Caecilian and Majorinus/Donatus), and the site of the decisive 411 Conference.
- **Numidia:** the region of Donatism's greatest institutional strength — numerically dominant there, and the origin region of several of the movement's most prominent figures and of the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon.
- **Cirta (Constantina):** the see of Petilian, one of the movement's own most prominent bishops and its fullest surviving voice in the vendored corpus. Milevis, by contrast, is not one of this world's own centers — it was the see of Optatus, the Catholic polemicist whose own opposing subject was Parmenian, Donatist bishop of Carthage; named here only for orientation, since Optatus's *Against the Donatists* is this world's own earliest substantial narrative source, hostile and external to it but contemporary and primary, not secondary.
- **Hippo Regius and the wider region of Augustine's own episcopate:** the specific local ground of the majority of the vendored Augustine corpus, and — not yet built as its own world — the same region World #8 (Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity) will reconstruct from its own, Caecilianist side.

**Cultural Environment:** Latin-speaking Roman North Africa (Africa Proconsularis, Numidia, Byzacena, Mauretania) — the same broad linguistic and cultural milieu World #8 will reconstruct, since these are, on the documentary record, two rival communions contesting the same region, language, and inherited Cyprianic tradition, not two separate cultural worlds. Participation in this world spans from bishops and clergy of the parallel hierarchy down to a genuinely broad lay base: unlike several other worlds in this portfolio, Donatism was for substantial periods and in substantial regions not an elite minority current but the numerically dominant church, which shapes how this world's Formation Ecology work (Doc_05) should read its own thinness — a source-mediation problem, not an elite-versus-ordinary-believer scale problem.

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Diocletianic persecution (303–305) and its *traditio* crisis: the demand that clergy surrender scripture and sacred vessels, and the community's own subsequent, unresolved argument over what should follow from having done so, or refused to.
- Escalating and oscillating imperial religious policy directed at the movement itself: Constantine's initial coercive measures (316–321, reversed under practical failure), toleration under Julian (361, including the restoration of confiscated Donatist basilicas), and sustained legal suppression from the 405 Edict of Unity through the 411 Conference of Carthage and its aftermath.
- Direct, sometimes lethal state coercion from within the window itself: the Macarian repression (347–348, imperial commissioners Paul and Macarius enforcing unification), which produced the movement's own martyr narratives central to §1 above (*Passio Marculi*, *Passio Isaac et Maximiani*).
- Competition with the Caecilianist Catholic party for legal recognition, property, and the right to be recognized as the legitimate North African church.

**Historical Catalysts:** the 311/312 Caecilian election and the Felix of Aptungi *traditio* accusation; the Council of Rome (313, under Pope Miltiades, convened at Constantine's order) and the Council of Arles (314), both ruling against the Donatist party; the Macarian repression (347–348); Julian's restoration of Donatist basilicas (361); the internal Maximianist schism (393, §4 below); the 405 Edict of Unity; the 411 Conference of Carthage (Marcellinus presiding; 279 Donatist against 286 Catholic bishops seated — corrected 2026-09-09 from an earlier, unverified 284 figure; see Doc_02 §1 for the primary-source citation); Augustine's *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, c. 417), defending the imperial coercion after the fact; the Vandal invasion (429) and capture of Carthage (439).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** An ecology of purity and contested legitimacy: rebaptism as the boundary-crossing rite, martyr and confessor memory as the community's own identity anchor, and an unbroken *traditor*-free ordination lineage as the ground of sacramental validity — recurring concerns not shared in this specific configuration by any other confirmed world.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally: ecclesial purity/rigorism (sacramental validity contingent on the minister's own holiness, inherited from and sharpened beyond Cyprian's third-century position); martyr-cult identity as the community's own self-understanding; resistance to state-backed religious coercion as a lived, recurring experience rather than a single episode. Named here as candidates for Step 4 testing, not as confirmed gravities.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Belonging and authority dominate — rebaptism and the parallel episcopal hierarchy are this world's most recurring formative material. Worship has genuine, documented content distinct from the Caecilianist party's own (the *Deo laudes* acclamation; martyr commemoration). Interpretation/doctrine is comparatively thin and largely reactive in what is currently vendored — Tyconius, **vendored 2026-09-01** (corrected 2026-09-08, Round 3 Opus review, from this line's own stale "pending acquisition"), is the strongest counter-example — and formation at the ordinary-believer level is structurally source-mediated, a limitation carried forward as binding on Doc_05 (§7 below).
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §6 below.

## 4. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21: strand is defined as "a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail" — a finding accountable to evidence, never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality. This determination is made here, for the first time — Step 0 correctly deferred it as Step 1's own work, flagging one specific candidate — Tyconius's own contested position — for testing here, not deciding it there.

**Tyconius does not, on current evidence, rise to strand status.** The standard account holds that Parmenian and a Donatist council condemned Tyconius (c. 380) over his universalist ecclesiology, and that he nonetheless never joined the Catholic church; no clerical office is attested for him (Gennadius, *De viris illustribus* 18, describes him as learned in sacred and secular literature and zealous in ecclesiastical affairs, but attaches no office — where the same chapter list gives his neighboring entries "the bishop" and "the presbyter"), and no separate following, parallel authority structure, or distinct communal practice is attested for his position. He appears in the record as a condemned individual dissenting voice, not as the head of a rival pattern of communal life — a determination that does not require, and does not take, a position on his own formal standing within the communion, which is left deliberately open in this world's own source ecology. This finding is provisional on Doc_02's own fuller research — the *Liber Regularum* itself is vendored (2026-09-01, corrected 2026-09-08, Round 4 Opus review, from this line's own stale "once... is acquired" framing) and available for that fuller research directly — and may be revisited if further evidence surfaces a following distinct from his own person.

**Finding: strand-singular.** A second candidate must be tested and is here rejected on the record, not overlooked: in 393, the deacon Maximian broke from the mainstream Donatist hierarchy — then led by Primian, bishop of Carthage — over disciplinary and procedural grievances against Primian's own conduct (the Cebarsussi sentence lists charges against Primian personally, with a regional dimension between Byzacena and Numidia). A council at Cebarsussi (393) elected Maximian as a rival Primate of Carthage; the mainstream party's own much larger council at Bagai (394) condemned the Maximianists and, over the following years, suppressed them, invoking existing imperial and proconsular anti-heretical legislation against Maximianist clergy — Augustine works through the proconsular acts at *Contra Cresconium* III–IV (**both this work and the *Contra epistulam Parmeniani* cited below are vendored, 2026-09-01** — corrected 2026-09-08, Round 4 Opus review, from this line's own stale "neither... is currently vendored" claim, which evaded three prior review rounds' own token-based sweeps because the negation used "neither...nor" rather than a searched phrase; Registry rows 17, 18, 39). Circumcellion involvement in the suppression is also reported by Augustine in his own polemic against the Donatists, alongside his quotation of the two councils' own sentences (*Contra epistulam Parmeniani*; *Contra Cresconium*) — all of it, like nearly the whole episode, a real and unusually specific documentary trace, but one that sits squarely inside this world's Author Gravity concentration (§7, item 1 below), not outside it.

Tested against Article 21's actual bar, the Maximianist episode does not clear it, on its own strongest evidence. By the finding's own terms, the two bodies differed in no formation emphasis, no practice, and no ecological orientation — the dispute was over who should hold the see, not over a distinct pattern of communal life. The ground of authority asserted was also not distinct: Maximian's own consecrated bishops asserted the identical Donatist ground of authority against a particular incumbent, unlike, for example, Ambrose's demonstrably different ground of authority in `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §4's own Strand C. Most decisively, the single fact that makes this episode significant for Doc_02 — that the mainstream party received returning Maximianist clergy back without reordination or rebaptism — is proof that the mainstream party itself recognized Maximianist orders and sacraments as valid: exactly a shared formation pattern, practice, and authority structure, not the distinct pattern Article 21 asks the evidence to show. Membership in this world, not Article 21's own distinctness test, is what separately excludes a case like the Caecilianist party from strand status — a different question from the one this finding turns on, and not needed to sustain it.

**Disposition of the Maximianist material:** it is not lost, only correctly classified. It belongs where the Forces Framework already places an internal fracture of exactly this kind — as an ongoing internal force at Cell 2B, whose suppression campaign (394–398, following the fracture's 393 opening) also has an ending-adjacent aspect the movement carries into the 411 Conference (§5 below) — and as binding material for Doc_02's own rebaptism-theology reconstruction, since Augustine's polemical use of it (this world's own rebaptism logic undercut by its own practice toward the Maximianists) bears directly on the gravity candidates named in §3 above.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation for Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8.

**What generated this world:** the Diocletianic persecution's *traditio* crisis (303–305) and the disputed 311/312 Caecilian election, which converted an unresolved communal argument over how to treat clergy who had surrendered scripture into a permanent institutional split.

**What larger world was it embedded in:** the North African Latin church's own third-century inheritance from Cyprian — a tradition on rebaptism and ecclesial purity this world's own founders read as continuous with, not a departure from. From 312 onward, this world was also embedded in, and increasingly disfavored by, the Roman imperial state, whose favor consistently ran to the rival Caecilianist party.

**What was it responding to:** the trauma of persecution and a felt need for an uncompromisingly pure leadership — a direct, sharpened continuation of Cyprian's own position (against Pope Stephen) that baptism outside the true church is no baptism — an ecclesiological claim about where the church is, which this world's own founders sharpened into a claim about the personal worthiness of the minister, applied now to the specific, immediate question of clergy suspected of *traditio*.

**What was it refusing:** episcopal legitimacy through office and imperial recognition alone, regardless of the office-holder's own conduct under persecution; later, and increasingly through this world's own window, submission to a religious settlement this world's own actors regarded as imposed by an illegitimately partisan state rather than reached in genuine ecclesial consensus. Donatus's own reported retort — "Quid est imperatori cum ecclesia?" ("What has the emperor to do with the church?"), from the context of the 347 Macarian repression — names this refusal directly, even though the movement itself, at three separate points across its own window, turned to the state's own machinery when doing so served its case: Anulinus's 313 *relatio* forwarding the movement's own petition to Constantine; the 361 petition to Julian for the restoration of confiscated basilicas; and, in the 390s, the mainstream party's own invocation of existing imperial and proconsular anti-heretical legislation against the Maximianist schism within Donatism itself (§4 above) — a real, three-part qualification on an otherwise dominant refusal-under-pressure pattern.

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits from prior worlds:** the Cyprianic rigorist tradition of the third-century North African church — rebaptism theology, ministerial-purity concerns — is this world's own direct doctrinal and institutional inheritance, not an invention at 311/312. Cyprian himself is not yet built as his own world's figure; he sits inside not-yet-built World #8, per the Step 0 Conclusion's own placement, and this world's own reliance on him is an inheritance from a shared root rather than a claim on him exclusively.

**What this world transmits to subsequent worlds/history:** genuinely narrower and less institutionally continuous than several other confirmed worlds — no clear living-tradition descendant is documented. This world's most durable transmission to later Christian history is substantially dialectical rather than a persisting tradition of its own: the rebaptism controversy it forced, and the church-purity question it raised at scale, is a major and lasting spur to the other side's own theological development — Augustine's mature doctrine of the objective validity of sacraments independent of the minister's own worthiness was substantially forged in direct response to this world's own challenge. That doctrine's own transmission belongs to World #8's future reconstruction, not this one's; noted here for orientation only, not developed.

**Distinctness from adjacent worlds, confirmed and carried forward as binding boundaries, not redrawn here:**

- **World #8 (Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity, not yet built — Cyprian and Augustine):** already confirmed distinct in the Step 0 Conclusion and reaffirmed in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3, which requires this document to state the operative axis explicitly. That axis is **external-management-within-unity versus internal-schism-into-division** — quoted in full from Coach3's own critique (`Syriac-Build/CiC_Coach3_Step0_Critique_2026-07-06.md`): "#8's Cyprian is defined by crisis pastoral management within one unified communion under external persecution; #4 is defined by permanent schism into two rival hierarchies over a different, later crisis (the Diocletian-era traditor controversy), with enduring martyr-cult identity as the organizing content. External-management-within-unity versus internal-schism-into-division — different gravity, not just different century." A genuine, evidenced shared-root adjacency remains (both worlds draw on Cyprian directly — see the inheritance paragraph above), which Coach3's critique names as a real, surviving risk the distinction nonetheless holds against, not a settled non-issue. This world's own account of the boundary is given here, per `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §4 item 4's binding instruction on this document specifically.
- **World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, c. 312–451):** already examined in `Syriac-Build/CiC_Coach3_Step0_Critique_2026-07-06.md` and adopted in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3 — "two sides of the same imperial-religious question: #6 is the church that aligns with and juridically consolidates around state power; #4 is the church that permanently splits from the mainstream specifically in refusal of that same power." No overlap risk; a point of productive contrast, adopted rather than re-derived here, matching the discipline `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §6 already applied from its own side to this same pairing. The three qualifications named in §5 above (this world's own bounded turns toward imperial or proconsular power in 313, 361, and the 390s against its own Maximianist dissidents) do not disturb this contrast — the pattern remains refusal-under-pressure on balance with respect to the Caecilianist/imperial axis this world defines itself against, not an absolute refusal at every point or against every target.
- **Standing question, named and not resolved:** the Step 0 Conclusion logs an explicitly unresolved constitutional question — whether a formation-type (here, World #8's own eventual claim to a single coherent pastoral-office tradition) may recur across a temporal gap with a rival claimant, this world, contesting the interval between Cyprian and Augustine. This document does not resolve it; it is named here, alongside this document's own boundary statement, because it bears on that boundary's shape. Whether and how it should also appear in World #8's own future Doc_01 is that document's own question to take up when it exists, not an obligation this document places on it.
