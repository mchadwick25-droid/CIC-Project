## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** Donatism.

**Short Description:** The rigorist alternative communion that split from the North African Catholic church over the validity of sacraments and ordinations administered by clergy suspected of having surrendered scripture during the Diocletianic persecution — from the disputed 311/312 election of Caecilian as bishop of Carthage, through a full parallel episcopal hierarchy contesting the mainstream Caecilianist church town-for-town across Roman North Africa, to escalating imperial legal suppression after the 411 Conference of Carthage and the disruption of the Vandal conquest of Carthage (439), which closes this world's own documentary window (§2 below) — within, but ending well before, this phase's own ceiling at Chalcedon (451).

**Core Identity:** This is a world of contested legitimacy and parallel institutional life, not a doctrinal heresy — a communion holding standard North African Latin Trinitarian and Christological confession (Step0 §2, A1) while claiming to be the one true, pure "Church of the Martyrs" against a rival it regarded as *traditor*-tainted and, from 312 onward, state-favored. Its formation logic is boundary-and-purity-centered before it is doctrinal: rebaptism as the rite that marks who truly belongs, martyr and confessor memory as the community's own self-understanding, and clerical purity (a *traditor*-free ordination line) as the ground of sacramental validity, in a way distinct from any of the other confirmed worlds in this project's portfolio.

**Distinctive Contribution:** No other confirmed world in this project's portfolio reconstructs a sustained *internal* schism contesting the very definition of the true church from inside a shared confessional and cultural tradition, rather than encountering a rival tradition from outside it. World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity) reconstructs a communion's alignment with and juridical consolidation of state power; this world reconstructs the opposite and adjacent answer — permanent separation from the mainstream specifically in refusal of that same power's legitimacy — a finding already examined and adopted, not re-derived here (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3, citing `Syriac-Build/CiC_Coach3_Step0_Critique_2026-07-06.md`). This world is not World #6's rival for uniqueness so much as its clarifying contrast, matching how `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §1 already frames the same pairing from its own side.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed. Per Constitution Article 29, Living Tradition Status confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform on its own behalf (Article 29 determination is G4 in this build's own launch process, at the freeze). Noted for the record, not decided here, and distinct from several other confirmed worlds: unlike World #6's Roman and Constantinopolitan primacy strands, or Syriac Christianity's continuation into the modern Church of the East, Donatism has **no clear surviving institutional descendant**. The historical record shows the movement persisting for centuries beyond this world's own documentary window (§2 below) — attested in Gregory the Great's correspondence into the 590s — but no continuous line to a present-day communion is documented; the tradition appears to end with the general collapse of North African Christianity following the Arab conquest of Carthage (698). Whether Article 29's determination should therefore resolve as "no living tradition, historical reconstruction only" rather than the more typical "confirmed" or "not confirmed pending further work" is itself a live question for the project lead at freeze, flagged here rather than presumed. **Status: PENDING.**

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** c. 311/312–439 CE. **This is narrower than the phase's own ceiling (70–451 CE) and narrower than World #6's own window (312–451)** — this world's own documentary boundary closes at the Vandal capture of Carthage, not at Chalcedon, per the reasoning below.

- **Beginning point (311/312):** the disputed election and consecration of Caecilian as bishop of Carthage, and the *traditio* accusation against his consecrator, Felix of Aptungi, which produced the rival consecration of Majorinus by the rigorist party (succeeded, from c. 313, by Donatus, from whom the movement's name derives). Sources place the initiating dispute in either 311 or 312 depending on how the transition from persecution to episcopal election is dated; both years are carried here rather than resolved to a false precision the sources do not support. This is a defensible beginning point under the Framework's Temporal Scope test: it marks a genuine ecological rupture — before it, North African Christianity's rigorist and moderate readings of Cyprian's own third-century purity theology coexisted within one communion; after it, they never did again.
- **Ending point (439):** the Vandal capture of Carthage, the culmination of the Vandal invasion of Roman North Africa begun in 429. This is a defensible ending point on the Framework's own terms, distinct from — and earlier than — both this phase's ceiling (451) and World #6's own window (which runs the full distance to Chalcedon): Vandal rule brought Arian (Homoian) governance to North Africa and redirected imperial-adjacent religious persecution toward Nicene Catholic Christianity specifically, a genuine ecological rupture that ends the specific two-party Donatist/Caecilianist-Catholic contest this world reconstructs, even though — per the phase-scope disclosure carried forward from Step 0 (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §1) — the Donatist communion itself is independently attested continuing for centuries after this world's own documentary close. **The two boundaries are distinct and must not be conflated:** this world's own window (312–439) is a documentary and ecological choice about where the two-party contest this world reconstructs has its own natural rupture; the movement's *actual historical persistence* beyond that window, into the 590s and later, is a separate fact, already disclosed and not reopened here.

**Geographic Centers:** Carthage, with the contest replicated town-for-town across Roman North Africa.

- **Carthage:** the seat of both rival bishoprics from the outset (Caecilian and Majorinus/Donatus), and the site of the decisive 411 Conference.
- **Numidia:** the region of Donatism's greatest institutional strength — attested numerically dominant there, and the origin region of several of the movement's most prominent figures and of the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon (Step0 §2, A5).
- **Cirta (Constantina)** and **Milevis:** episcopal seats of Petilian and Optatus's own opposing subject respectively, both directly evidenced in the vendored corpus (Step0 §3, B1).
- **Hippo Regius and the wider region of Augustine's own episcopate:** the specific local ground of the majority of the vendored Augustine corpus, and — not yet built as its own world (Step0 §3, B3; §4, item 4) — the same region World #8 (Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity) will reconstruct from its own, Caecilianist side.

**Cultural Environment:** Latin-speaking Roman North Africa (Africa Proconsularis, Numidia, Byzacena, Mauretania) — the same broad linguistic and cultural milieu World #8 will reconstruct, since these are, on the documentary record, two rival communions contesting the same region, language, and inherited Cyprianic tradition, not two separate cultural worlds. Participation in this world spans from bishops and clergy of the parallel hierarchy down to a genuinely broad lay base: unlike several other worlds in this portfolio, Donatism was for substantial periods and in substantial regions not an elite minority current but the numerically dominant church (Step0 §3, B2/B5), which shapes how this world's Formation Ecology work (Doc_05) should read its own thinness — a source-mediation problem, not an elite-versus-ordinary-believer scale problem.

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Diocletianic persecution (303–305) and its *traditio* crisis: the demand that clergy surrender scripture and sacred vessels, and the community's own subsequent, unresolved argument over what should follow from having done so, or refused to.
- Escalating and oscillating imperial religious policy directed at the movement itself: Constantine's initial coercive measures (316–321, reversed under practical failure), toleration under Julian (361, including the restoration of confiscated Donatist basilicas), and sustained legal suppression from the 405 Edict of Unity through the 411 Conference of Carthage and its aftermath.
- Direct, sometimes lethal state coercion from within the window itself: the Macarian repression (347–348, imperial commissioners Paul and Macarius enforcing unification), which produced the movement's own martyr narratives central to §1 above (*Passio Marculi*, *Passio Isaac et Maximiani* — Step0 §3, B1).
- Competition with the Caecilianist Catholic party for legal recognition, property, and the right to be recognized as the legitimate North African church.

**Historical Catalysts:** the 311/312 Caecilian election and the Felix of Aptungi *traditio* accusation; the Council of Rome (313, under Pope Miltiades, convened at Constantine's order) and the Council of Arles (314), both ruling against the Donatist party; the Macarian repression (347–348); Julian's restoration of Donatist basilicas (361); the internal Maximianist schism (393, §4 below); the 405 Edict of Unity; the 411 Conference of Carthage (Marcellinus presiding; 284 Donatist against 286 Catholic bishops seated — Step0 §3, B5); Augustine's *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, c. 417), defending the imperial coercion after the fact; the Vandal invasion (429) and capture of Carthage (439).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** An ecology of purity and contested legitimacy: rebaptism as the boundary-crossing rite, martyr and confessor memory as the community's own identity anchor, and an unbroken *traditor*-free ordination lineage as the ground of sacramental validity — recurring concerns not shared in this specific configuration by any other confirmed world.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally: ecclesial purity/rigorism (sacramental validity contingent on the minister's own holiness, inherited from and sharpened beyond Cyprian's third-century position); martyr-cult identity as the community's own self-understanding; resistance to state-backed religious coercion as a lived, recurring experience rather than a single episode. Named here as candidates for Step 4 testing, not as confirmed gravities.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Belonging and authority dominate — rebaptism and the parallel episcopal hierarchy are this world's most recurring formative material. Worship has genuine, documented content distinct from the Caecilianist party's own (the *Deo laudes* acclamation; martyr commemoration; Step0 §3, B2). Interpretation/doctrine is comparatively thin and largely reactive in what is currently vendored — Tyconius (pending acquisition, Step0 §3, B1) is the strongest counter-example — and formation at the ordinary-believer level is structurally source-mediated (Step0 §3, B2), a limitation carried forward as binding on Doc_05 (§7 below).
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §6 below.

## 4. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21: strand is "a finding, never a presupposed universal schema," accountable to evidence. This determination is made here, for the first time — Step 0 correctly deferred it as Step 1's own work (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2, A5), flagging one specific candidate — Tyconius's own contested position — for testing here, not deciding it there.

**Tyconius does not, on current evidence, rise to strand status.** Article 21 requires "meaningfully distinct patterns of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation" — not merely a distinct individual opinion. The standard account (Step0 §3, B1) holds that Parmenian and a Donatist council condemned Tyconius (c. 380) over his universalist ecclesiology, and that he nonetheless never joined the Catholic church; no separate following, parallel authority structure, or distinct communal practice is attested for his position — he appears in the record as a condemned individual dissenting voice within the mainstream hierarchy, not as the head of a rival pattern of communal life. This finding is provisional on Doc_02's own fuller research (particularly once the *Liber Regularum* itself is acquired, per Step0 §4 acquisition note) and may be revisited if further evidence surfaces a following distinct from his own person.

**Finding: strand-plural — the mainstream (Primianist) communion, and the Maximianist schism (393–c. 394/398) as a second, evidenced, shorter-lived internal strand.** In 393, a rigorist faction led by the deacon Maximian broke from the mainstream Donatist hierarchy — itself then led by Primian, bishop of Carthage — over disciplinary and procedural grievances against Primian's own conduct. A council at Cebarsussi (393) elected Maximian as a rival Primate of Carthage; the mainstream party's own much larger council at Bagai (394) condemned the Maximianists and, over the following years, forcibly suppressed them (Circumcellion involvement in that suppression is independently attested, and is itself a data point for the Circumcellion/*agonistici* question at Step0 §2, A5) and secured Roman imperial law classifying Maximianists alongside Donatists for penal purposes. This satisfies Article 21's own bar in a way Tyconius's case does not: a distinct, named ground of internal dispute (procedural/disciplinary grievance against Primian's conduct, not doctrinal divergence); a genuine rival authority structure, not merely a rival opinion (Maximian's own election as a competing Primate, with his own consecrated bishops); and direct primary evidentiary traces (the Cebarsussi and Bagai councils, and — a load-bearing detail for Doc_02, not developed here — Augustine's own polemical use of the episode: he argues that the mainstream Donatist party's willingness to receive returning Maximianist clergy without reordination or rebaptism undermines Donatism's own rebaptism logic against the Caecilianists). Named as its own strand, evidenced for the period it is attested, per the same discipline `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §4 applied to its own Strand C (a strand does not need to persist to the world's own close to qualify).

**Governing consequence for later steps:** per the Framework, cross-strand gravity testing occurs formally at Step 4. A candidate gravity that holds across both the mainstream and Maximianist strands (for example, the shared conviction that sacramental validity depends on ministerial worthiness, even as the two strands disagreed sharply about whose worthiness had failed) is a stronger candidate for this world's irreducible core than one that holds only within the mainstream. Doc_02 must also carry the Augustinian polemical use of the Maximianist episode forward, since it bears directly on the rebaptism theology at this world's own center.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation for Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8.

**What generated this world:** the Diocletianic persecution's *traditio* crisis (303–305) and the disputed 311/312 Caecilian election, which converted an unresolved communal argument over how to treat clergy who had surrendered scripture into a permanent institutional split.

**What larger world was it embedded in:** the North African Latin church's own third-century inheritance from Cyprian — a rigorist tradition on rebaptism and ministerial purity this world's own founders read as continuous with, not a departure from (Step0 §2, A2.5 discussion; §4). From 312 onward, this world was also embedded in, and increasingly disfavored by, the Roman imperial state, whose favor consistently ran to the rival Caecilianist party.

**What was it responding to:** the trauma of persecution and a felt need for an uncompromisingly pure leadership — a direct, sharpened continuation of Cyprian's own position (against Pope Stephen) that baptism and ordination outside a genuinely holy church are invalid, applied now to the specific, immediate question of clergy suspected of *traditio*.

**What was it refusing:** episcopal legitimacy through office and imperial recognition alone, regardless of the office-holder's own conduct under persecution; later, and increasingly through this world's own window, submission to a religious settlement this world's own actors regarded as imposed by an illegitimately partisan state rather than reached in genuine ecclesial consensus. Donatus's own reported retort — *"Quid est imperatori cum ecclesia?"* ("What has the emperor to do with the church?"), from the context of the 347 Macarian repression — names this refusal directly, even though the movement itself had also appealed *to* that same imperial power when it judged doing so served its case (Anulinus's 313 *relatio*; the 361 petition to Julian) — a real, bounded qualification on an otherwise dominant refusal-under-pressure pattern (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3).

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits from prior worlds:** the Cyprianic rigorist tradition of the third-century North African church — rebaptism theology, ministerial-purity concerns — is this world's own direct doctrinal and institutional inheritance, not an invention at 311/312 (Step0 §3, B1; §4). Cyprian himself is not yet built as his own world's figure; he sits inside not-yet-built World #8, per the Step 0 Conclusion's own placement, and this world's own reliance on him is named as an inheritance from a shared root rather than a claim on him exclusively (§4 item 4, `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md`).

**What this world transmits to subsequent worlds/history:** genuinely narrower and less institutionally continuous than several other confirmed worlds (§1 above — no clear living-tradition descendant is documented). This world's most durable transmission to later Christian history is substantially dialectical rather than a persisting tradition of its own: the rebaptism controversy it forced, and the church-purity question it raised at scale, is a major and lasting spur to the *other* side's own theological development — Augustine's mature doctrine of the objective validity of sacraments independent of the minister's own worthiness was substantially forged in direct response to this world's own challenge. That doctrine's own transmission belongs to World #8's future reconstruction, not this one's; noted here for orientation only, not developed.

**Distinctness from adjacent worlds, confirmed and carried forward as binding boundaries, not redrawn here:**

- **World #8 (Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity, not yet built — Cyprian and Augustine):** already confirmed distinct in the Step 0 Conclusion and reaffirmed in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3 — the schism-crisis axis (this world) versus ordinary episcopal pastoral office and sacramental care (World #8), with a genuine, evidenced shared-root adjacency (both worlds draw on Cyprian directly) that Coach3's critique names as a real, surviving risk the distinction nonetheless holds against, not a settled non-issue. This world's own account of that boundary is given here, in this document, per `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §4 item 4's binding instruction; World #8, when built, will need to state the same boundary from its own side, since it cannot yet hold the line here.
- **World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity, c. 312–451):** already examined in `Syriac-Build/CiC_Coach3_Step0_Critique_2026-07-06.md` and adopted in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3 — *"two sides of the same imperial-religious question: #6 is the church that aligns with and juridically consolidates around state power; #4 is the church that permanently splits from the mainstream specifically in refusal of that same power."* No overlap risk; a point of productive contrast, adopted rather than re-derived here, matching the discipline `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §6 already applied from its own side to this same pairing. The qualification named in §5 above (this world's own bounded appeals *to* imperial power in 313 and 361) does not disturb this contrast — it remains a refusal-under-pressure pattern on balance, not an absolute one throughout.
- **Standing question, named and not resolved (carried from `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3):** the Step 0 Conclusion logs an explicitly unresolved constitutional question — whether a formation-type (here, World #8's own eventual claim to a single coherent pastoral-office tradition) may recur across a temporal gap with a rival claimant, this world, contesting the interval between Cyprian and Augustine. This document does not resolve it; it is named here because it bears on this world's own §6 boundary statement above, and because, per Step 0 §4 item 4 (as amended), it should travel forward into World #8's own eventual Doc_01 rather than being rediscovered there.
