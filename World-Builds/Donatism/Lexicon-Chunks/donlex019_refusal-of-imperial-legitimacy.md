## Quick Meaning

The state has no standing to decide who the true church is — "What has the emperor to do with the church?", as our own primate is reported to have said — though we have, at three specific moments, turned to that same imperial machinery ourselves when doing so served our own case, without ever treating those moments as a betrayal of the conviction.

---

## World Meaning

When Constantine's own council at Rome, and the Council of Arles after it, ruled against us, we did not accept the verdict as settling anything — not because we reject the idea that a council can rule, but because a council convened and enforced by a state that had already thrown its favor behind our rival cannot be the church judging itself. The emperor's own recognition, or refusal of it, is simply not the same question as who the true church is. We hold this even now, generations later, under an empire that has consistently favored the other side since the very year our schism began. As our own primate put it to the emperor's own envoys, when they came pressing for unity during the worst of our suffering: what has the emperor to do with the church?

We do not pretend this has been an easy or an unbroken stance to hold. Three times across our own history, we ourselves turned to the very imperial machinery we otherwise refuse to recognize as having authority over us. We petitioned Constantine directly, through the governor Anulinus, in the earliest days of the dispute. We petitioned Julian, when he offered toleration, for the return of the basilicas that had been taken from us. And when our own house was divided by the Maximianist schism, we ourselves invoked the empire's own laws against heresy to bring our own dissidents back into line. We do not hide these three moments or explain them away as inconsistency. We hold them alongside our refusal, because a stance held on balance, under real and repeated pressure, is not the same thing as an absolute principle that breaks the moment it bends once. What we refuse, consistently, is the state's claim to settle who the true church is; what we have occasionally used is the same state's machinery, when it happened to serve a purpose that did not require conceding that claim.

Everything in our own experience of history is shaped by this refusal. The rulings we rejected at the very start; the repression that made our martyrs; the legal suppression that pressed harder as the years wore on; and, in time, the loss of the very power we had refused to recognize, when a different conqueror took Carthage from the empire that had spent a century enforcing unity against us. We do not read that loss as our own defeat. The specific power our whole stance was defined against was the one thing removed.

## Ecological Function

This refusal is the posture this world holds toward everything outside itself. It explains why the 313 and 314 rulings were rejected rather than accepted, why the martyrs die specifically because of this stance and not some other cause, and why the parallel hierarchy lives under continuous legal jeopardy rather than settled recognition. It is directly, and deliberately, qualified — not smoothed into a single unbroken principle — by the three pragmatic turns to imperial power this same entry holds alongside it, which is itself a genuine, held tension in this world's own record, not a flaw to be explained away.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Either a simple, consistent separatism (church and state kept entirely apart, without exception) or, if the three pragmatic exceptions are noticed, evidence of straightforward hypocrisy — a movement that claims a principle it does not actually hold.

**World Hearing:**
A dominant, genuinely held stance under real and repeated pressure, refusing the state's own standing to adjudicate ecclesial legitimacy — held together with, not despite, three specific, dated moments where this world's own actors turned to that same state's machinery because doing so served the case, without either fact canceling the other. This world's own record holds both as true at once, on balance, rather than resolving them into either pure consistency or pure hypocrisy.
