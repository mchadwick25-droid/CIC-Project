## Quick Meaning

A *traditor* is one who handed over the scriptures or the sacred vessels to the persecutor rather than suffer for keeping them; this world holds that such a man's hand can no longer validly ordain, baptize, or consecrate — which is why the whole schism turns on one specific accusation against one specific bishop's consecrator.

---

## World Meaning

When the persecutor came demanding that the scriptures be surrendered to be burned, some clergy handed them over, and some did not. We do not treat that difference as a private failure of nerve, forgivable once the danger passed and forgotten with it. What was surrendered was not paper — it was the very thing a minister exists to guard and hand on. A man who gave it up to save himself has shown, in the one moment that actually tested him, what his own hand is worth. And a hand that failed there cannot be trusted afterward to consecrate, to ordain, to baptize — not because we are vindictive toward him personally, but because a sacrament given by an unclean hand is not a sacrament withheld from you by accident; it is nothing given at all.

This is why the schism itself began exactly where it did. Caecilian was consecrated bishop of Carthage by a man accused of being a *traditor* — Felix of Aptungi — and that single fact is what made his own consecration impossible to accept. It was not a technicality invoked to justify a grudge; it is the same logic that governs every ordinary believer's own choice of which minister to be baptized by. If the accusation is true, everything that flows from that hand — every priest he ordained, every bishop those priests in turn helped consecrate, every baptism performed under that whole line — carries the same defect back to its root. That is why we could not simply overlook it and move on, the way our rivals ask us to. The line had to be broken and begun again, clean.

We know this is not a comfortable teaching to explain to someone who was not formed in it. It sounds, from outside, like an obsession with someone else's old failure. From inside, it is the opposite: it is our own insistence that what a minister does under real pressure is not separable from what he can give afterward. We ask it of our own clergy as much as anyone's — the purity this word demands runs through this whole church, not only through the men we accuse.

---

## Ecological Function

*Traditor* is the founding accusation this entire ecology stands on. Rebaptism exists because of it — you are rebaptized because the minister who first baptized you, or someone in his own line, may have been one. The parallel hierarchy (a second bishop for every see) exists because of it — a *traditor*-tainted line had to be replaced with a clean one, town for town. And the movement's own deepest internal difficulty, the Maximianist affair, is a direct test of how far this same logic can be pressed before it strains against the community's own practice. A participant who understands *traditor* understands why this world could not simply reunite with its rival once tempers cooled — reunion would mean accepting a line of sacraments this world holds to be no sacraments at all.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
An old grudge over who behaved badly during a long-past persecution — a historical dispute about individual cowardice that a reasonable person would expect both sides to have moved past.

**World Hearing:**
Not a grudge but a live sacramental question, asked fresh of every ordination and every baptism: is this minister's own hand clean enough to give what he claims to give? The accusation against one man in 311/312 is the reason a whole parallel church exists a century later — not because the grievance was never allowed to heal, but because, on this world's own terms, it cannot be waived without conceding that sacraments are valid regardless of the minister's own worthiness, which is precisely what this world was founded to deny.

## Key Sources

Optatus of Milevis, *Against the Donatists*, Appendix of Documents — the *Acta Purgationis Felicis* (314), the founding accusation against Felix of Aptungi. Petilian of Constantina's own quoted theological proposition, preserved in Augustine's *Answer to the Letters of Petilian*: "What we look for is the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient" (independently verified this build against `npnf104_augustine-anti-manichaean-anti-donatist.xml`).

Note: the Appendix's own selection and framing of the *traditio* documents is Optatus's act, even where the documents themselves are court records rather than his own argument — this world's own Author Gravity concentration applies to how this material reaches us, even at its most document-like (Doc_02 §1). Petilian's own words independently corroborate that the doctrine is genuine Donatist teaching, not a hostile invention, though his specific argumentative texture is preserved only inside Augustine's own refutation (Doc_04 §3.1).
