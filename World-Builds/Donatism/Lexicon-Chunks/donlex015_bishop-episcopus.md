## Quick Meaning

Not one bishop per city but two, contesting the same see and the same congregation — a complete rival hierarchy running the length and breadth of Roman North Africa, with its own primate at Carthage and its own council at every point of internal dispute.

---

## World Meaning

Wherever our rival has a bishop, so do we — not as a scattered protest movement but as a full, parallel church order, see for see, from Carthage itself down to the smallest town. This is not a metaphor for how divided the two sides are; it is a literal, daily fact of life in this world. Two men, each claiming the same office over the same people, each recognized by his own communion and refused by the other. When two hundred and eighty-four of our own bishops sat at the great Conference the emperor convened in Carthage, they sat opposite two hundred and eighty-six of theirs — proof, if any were needed, that this is not a fringe dissent but a whole church answering another whole church, bishop for bishop.

Our own bishops govern through councils, exactly as any legitimate church does — and it is precisely because our own conciliar machinery is real and functioning that our sharpest internal crisis was possible at all. When the deacon Maximian broke from Primian, our own primate at Carthage, a council of his own supporters at Cebarsussi elected him a rival primate; our own much larger council, meeting at Bagai, condemned that election and, in time, brought the Maximianist clergy back into communion with us. That we could discipline our own dissidents through our own councils is itself part of our claim to be a genuine church, not merely a protest against one. And that those same councils, once satisfied, received the Maximianist clergy back without repeating either their ordination or their baptism is a fact we do not hide, even where it sits uneasily beside how strictly we hold the same standard against our outside rival.

We do not experience our own authority as settled and safe. Every one of our bishops holds his see under continuing legal jeopardy — the same imperial power that recognizes our rival's bishops as the lawful church has, at different times, confiscated our own buildings, exiled our own clergy, and enforced unity against us by force. To be one of our bishops is to hold a real, functioning office that the state itself refuses to recognize as one.

---

## Ecological Function

The parallel hierarchy is the institutional container the purity doctrine and rebaptism operate within at scale — an "impure" line of ordination had to be replaced with a clean one, and that replacement is this office, doubled, everywhere. It is also where this world's sharpest internal tension is housed: the Maximianist affair is an event internal to this hierarchy's own conciliar machinery, not an outside attack on it, and its own resolution — reception without repeated ordination or baptism — tests the purity doctrine's own consistency from within the very structure meant to enforce it.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A loose or informal breakaway movement without real institutional structure — a protest current rather than an actual church, since "the" bishop of a given city is usually assumed to be a single, settled fact.

**World Hearing:**
A complete, functioning, self-governing rival church order — its own primate, its own councils, its own discipline for its own dissidents — matching the rival hierarchy office for office and see for see across the whole of Roman North Africa. Two hundred and eighty-four of our own bishops answering two hundred and eighty-six of theirs at one conference is not a metaphor for how contested this world was; it is the literal shape of it.

## Key Sources

Doc_01 §2's own record of the 411 Conference of Carthage (284 Donatist against 286 Catholic bishops seated). The Cebarsussi (393) and Bagai (394) council sentences, quoted directly in Augustine's *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* and *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* — the Maximianist clergy's reception without repeated ordination or baptism stated in Augustine's own words: "Felicianus... was not held by the Donatists themselves to have lost either the sacrament of baptism or the sacrament of conferring baptism."

Note: the bare institutional fact of the parallel hierarchy is undisputed even by hostile sources reporting it; individual bishops' own conduct and motives beyond that bare fact reach this document substantially through Optatus's and Augustine's own hostile characterization (Doc_04 §5).
