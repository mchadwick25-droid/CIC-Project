## Section 1 — World Identity

**World name (as the world would name itself):** Donatism — no single alternate self-name is attested; Donatist clergy named themselves "of the party of Donatus" in formal petitions (Optatus turns this into an accusation of naming a man instead of the Church), and both this world and its rival claimed the name "Catholic" (*Ecclesia*) for themselves alone. This world's own record has no surviving neutral or Donatist-coined alternative to the modern scholarly label.

**World code:** `don`

**Temporal scope:** *c.* 311/312–439 CE. The window opens with the disputed election and consecration of Caecilian as bishop of Carthage and the *traditio* accusation against his consecrator, Felix of Aptungi — the rupture point after which North Africa's rigorist and moderate readings of Cyprian's own third-century purity theology never again coexisted within one communion. It closes with the Vandal capture of Carthage (439), which did not end the two-party contest itself (Gregory the Great's Register still treats it as "a live problem" in the 590s) but removed the Roman imperial state as the Catholic-aligned adjudicating power — the specific condition this world's entire refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy pattern is defined against. A Representative formed in this world inhabits the schism at its height: a fully operative parallel church, under recurring but not yet final legal pressure, before the imperial power it refuses to recognize as legitimate is itself removed from the field.

**Geographic scope:** Roman North Africa (Africa Proconsularis, Numidia, Byzacena, Mauretania), Latin-speaking throughout. Carthage is the seat of both rival bishoprics from the outset and the site of the decisive 411 Conference. Numidia is this world's own region of greatest institutional strength — numerically dominant there, and the origin region of several of its most prominent figures and of the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon. Cirta (Constantina) is the see of Petilian, this world's own fullest surviving voice in the vendored corpus. The contest is replicated town-for-town across the whole region: wherever the rival has a bishop, so does this world.

**Community character:** This community understood itself as the one true, pure "Church of the Martyrs" — not a schismatic departure but the continuation of authentic North African Christianity against a *traditor*-tainted, state-favored rival. It held standard Trinitarian and Christological orthodoxy; this was never a doctrinal heresy in the credal sense. Its self-understanding organized around one conviction, worked out through every dimension of its formation life: *we are the pure, persecuted, true church, proved by what we will not concede and by what we have suffered for refusing to concede it.* It understood itself as a complete, legitimate, parallel church order — a bishop for every see, a full sacramental and institutional life of its own — not a protest movement on the margins of the mainstream church, while living under continuous external legal jeopardy it refused to treat as settling the question of legitimacy.

**Builder:** Donatism build thread

**Date of World Profile completion:** DRAFT, 2026-09-03

## Section 3 — Formation Logic

Drawn directly from Doc_07 §2I (Formation Logic, the current section under the M4 lens spine — see the Method Note above).

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

A fully formed member of this world holds, without qualification, that a sacrament's validity rises and falls on the giver's own unbroken purity, and has been rebaptized — deliberately, individually, bodily — into the one communion whose ministers can be trusted to give it. That same formed member holds this world's own dead by name, at the grave, on the appointed day, and reads or hears their account so the same conviction happens again in the hearing. And that member refuses the state's own standing to settle who the true church is, while not treating that refusal as requiring an absolute, unbending consistency at every single moment.

### The Formation Mechanism

Formation here does not happen primarily through speculative teaching or a developed interpretive tradition — this world is genuinely thin there (Section 4B/4D below). It happens through enacted threshold-crossing (rebaptism), through repeated commemorative narration (the annual reading at the grave), and through lived legal jeopardy (a formed member's own church exists under continuous external legal pressure). A person is formed less by being taught a doctrine than by doing the rite, hearing the story, and living inside the pressure the doctrine and the story both explain.

### The Formation Arc

Entry is the rebaptism itself — a specific, dated, individually experienced threshold. The middle of formation is lived inside a parallel institutional life under continuous legal jeopardy, punctuated by episodes of acute persecution (the Diocletianic terror shared with the whole church; the specifically Macarian repression suffered at the rival's own instigation) that intensify, rather than interrupt, this world's own commemorative practice. Maturity in this world looks like the martyr — not necessarily literal death, but the settled readiness the martyr narratives hold up as this world's own formation ideal: suffering chosen over the peace that would concede the rival's own legitimacy.

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

Not applicable — this world is strand-singular (Doc_01 §4). No cross-strand formation logic content exists to record.

### Formation Logic and the Confirmed Gravities

G1 (purity) is the doctrine formation enacts. G2 (rebaptism) is formation's own concrete threshold. G3 (martyr-cult) is formation's own affective register and its own reproducing mechanism (commemoration). G5 (refusal of imperial legitimacy) is the posture formation holds toward everything outside itself. G4 (the parallel hierarchy) is the institutional container all of this operates within. T1 and T2 are not exceptions to this formation logic but its own honestly-held internal strain — a community that refuses absolutely, mostly, and receives strictly, mostly, without either fact ever quite resolving into an unbroken rule.

**Usability test applied:** this section can be read directly into the World Capsule Core's "How This World Forms People" section without returning to Doc_07 — the fundamental aim, mechanism, and arc are each stated at the specificity Doc_07 §2I itself reached, not summarized down from it.

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary

Covers all nine of Doc_07's current M4 lenses (see Method Note above for why this expands past the template's own four-dimension count). The four dimensions the template names as required — Emotional Texture, Philosophical Ecology, Authority Structures, Boundary Structures — are each present below, correctly mapped to their current Doc_07 lens.

### 4A — Emotional Texture *(Doc_07 §2B, Experiential/Emotional)*

The dominant emotional register is vindication carried through suffering, not resignation. This world's own record does not narrate martyrdom as a tragedy absorbed; it narrates it as this world's own proof, read and re-read at the grave so the same conviction is produced again in the hearing — Macrobius's own letter opens not in grief but in what its own Latin names a burning, joyful eagerness to write. Set against this is a harder, more defiant register carried in the refusal of imperial legitimacy: Donatus's own reported retort is not resignation to persecution but a flat denial of the persecutor's own standing to judge the question at all.

The emotional arc of formation moves from entry (the rebaptism itself, a deliberate, individually chosen threshold) through a middle lived inside legal jeopardy and periodic acute persecution, toward a mature register of vindicated, settled readiness — the martyr's own disposition, held up as this world's own formation ideal. A named absence belongs here as much as the register itself: the emotional texture of failure — a *traditor*'s own fear, or a believer's own quiet drift back toward the rival communion — is a named absence, not a finding this world's own record can support, and this absence is not incidental. It is the same transmission asymmetry that shapes this whole world's own evidentiary base (Section 8 below): the feelings this document cannot document are exactly the feelings that would have had to survive inside a hostile framing, and did not.

### 4B — Philosophical Ecology *(Doc_07 §2D, Doctrinal/Philosophical)*

A doctrinal ecology built almost entirely around one question — what makes a sacrament valid — worked out with real rigor on that single question, and comparatively thin everywhere else. The central claim, in Petilian's own quoted words: "what we look for is the conscience of the giver, to cleanse that of the recipient." Rebaptism (G2) is this doctrine's own logical consequence, not a separate belief; both descend from Cyprian's own third-century rebaptism theology.

This world is not doctrinally thin in a simple sense — it is doctrinally *concentrated*, developing real rigor on its one central question while genuinely not developing a broader speculative or hermeneutical tradition around it. Tyconius is the one strong counter-example: an exegetical method that reached both rival churches without ever anchoring a following of his own (Doc_04 §2, D-B; Doc_09 donstory009).

### 4C — Authority Structures *(Doc_07 §2F, Social/Institutional)*

A complete, functioning, self-governing parallel society — not a protest current on the margins of the mainstream church, but its own full institutional mirror: two bishops for every see, a primate at Carthage (Donatus, then Parmenian, then Primian), and a conciliar machinery capable of disciplining its own dissidents, as the Maximianist affair shows directly. Authority is transmitted through consecration within the parallel line and exercised or renewed through councils (Cebarsussi, Bagai).

Socially, this world was not, for substantial periods and regions, a minority sect but the numerically dominant church — concentrated heavily in Numidia's own countryside, against a more urban rival strength centered on Carthage and Proconsular Africa. Within Numidia sits the Circumcellion/*agonistici* phenomenon (D-A, Supporting only regionally). It is precisely this world's own institutional totality — bishop for bishop, see for see — that makes the 411 Conference's own even bishop count (284 against 286) legible as two whole churches meeting, not one church and its dissidents.

### 4D — Boundary Structures *(Doc_07 §2H, CiC addition)*

This world knew itself substantially through refusal, and the boundary itself — who counts as *Ecclesia*, who counts as *traditor*, who has standing to judge either question — is where this world's own internal coherence and its external situatedness become visible as one system. Three refusals define it: refusing to recognize a *traditor*-ordained hierarchy as capable of valid sacraments (G1); refusing the state's own standing to adjudicate ecclesial legitimacy (G5); refusing the rival's unqualified claim to the name "Catholic," calling it "Caecilianist" instead. The naming contest runs both directions — this world's own clergy named themselves "of the party of Donatus" in petitions, which the rival's own literature turned into an accusation, while a Donatist preacher turned "catholic" itself into a sarcastic pun on impunity.

The boundary is not static: it was contested even within this world's own membership, most sharply in the Maximianist affair, where the boundary the purity doctrine draws against outsiders did not hold, in practice, against this world's own returning dissidents (T2). The boundary-work in this world is not a separate activity from its central doctrine — it *is* the doctrine, applied outward. The boundary and the center are the same line, viewed from two directions.

### 4E — Ritual/Practical *(Doc_07 §2A)*

The enacted, bodily form the purity doctrine takes: rebaptism (G2) — not a repetition of a rite but, in this world's own understanding, the first true baptism; the annual *anniversaria commemoratio* at the martyr's own grave; the *Deo laudes* acclamation, attested on stone, not merely reported; ordination within the parallel hierarchy. These are not four separate practices but one enacted sequence — this world's own ritual life does not merely express its doctrine; it is the doctrine's own most concrete evidence.

### 4F — Narrative/Mythic *(Doc_07 §2C)*

Three recurring narrative strands carry this world's own formation content: the founding-rupture narrative (the *traditio* accusation; Lucilla's own grievance and the rival consecration it produced — hostile-told, never independently confirmed or denied from this world's own side, Doc_09 donstory004); the martyr narratives proper (the commemorative sermon, the *Passio Marculi*, Macrobius's letter, Doc_09 donstory001–003); and Donatus's own retort, a shorter but load-bearing utterance-story (Doc_04 §3.6, T1). A person formed here would likely know these stories, told and retold, before they could reconstruct the doctrinal argument those same stories are meant to illustrate.

### 4G — Ethical/Legal *(Doc_07 §2E)*

An unusually rich dimension for this specific world — one of the most directly evidenced lenses in Doc_07, not a thin add-on. The founding accusation is itself ethical-legal (*traditio*); the schism was contested through Rome (313), Arles (314), and Anulinus's own *relatio*; imperial legislation targeted rebaptism directly; Cebarsussi and Bagai were legal-conciliar proceedings; the 411 Conference was a formal legal proceeding; the 419 Council of Carthage's own Code of Canons and Augustine's Letter 185 extend the legal record past this world's own construction window's close. Every one of this world's own Primary gravities has a direct legal-procedural trace.

### 4H — Material *(Doc_07 §2G)*

A genuinely uneven material record. The *Deo laudes* epigraphy (four catalogued inscriptions) is this world's own strongest material-culture evidence — stone, with no literary mediation at any point. Basilica archaeology across Numidia is real in the field but Confidence C, not independently verified this construction pass. Buildings themselves recur as objects of contest — confiscated under Constantine, restored by Julian in 361, presumably re-seized after 405. This world's own material record is not simply thin; it is thin in one place and genuinely strong in another, in a pattern that tracks the same transmission asymmetry named throughout this document.

### Cross-Lens Coherence

This world's central coherence, per Doc_07 §5: it holds itself to be the pure, persecuted true church, proved by an unbroken ordination line, enacted through rebaptism, remembered through its own named martyrs, and defended by refusing — mostly, not absolutely — the legitimacy of the very power that favors its rival. Unlike a neighboring world that aligns with and juridically consolidates around state power, this world defines itself by permanent separation specifically in refusal of that same power's legitimacy. Unlike a world whose central evidentiary problem is a stratum split between a literate elite and a silent majority, this world's central evidentiary problem is a transmission asymmetry across a population that was, for substantial regions and periods, itself the numerically dominant church.

The flagship cross-lens finding: the same transmission asymmetry, named once, locally, in Emotional Texture (4A), again in Narrative/Mythic (4F), and again in Material (4H), is not three separate evidentiary caveats but one single pattern repeating at every scale this world's ecology can be examined at — this world's evidence survives in inverse proportion to how directly it can be checked without a hostile hand mediating it. Held together, the pattern is a specific, checkable rule, not a general caveat applying loosely everywhere.

## Section 7 — The World's Tensions

From Doc_04's own Tensional gravities (T1, T2), held as this world held them, not resolved by hindsight.

---

**Tension:** Principled Refusal vs. Pragmatic Recourse to Imperial Power

**Nature:** What it costs to hold refusal at full strength: living under recurring, unrelieved legal jeopardy, refused every avenue of state relief on principle. What it costs to hold pragmatic recourse instead: undercutting the very claim — that the state has no standing over the true church — that gives the refusal its own moral weight. This world's own record shows both costs paid, at different moments, without resolving which one is the real position and which the exception.

**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; this tension runs across the whole ecology, not bound to any sub-part of it.

**How the world held it:** Not as hypocrisy quietly managed, but as a fact stated plainly in the same record that states the refusal at its most absolute — Donatus's own retort and the three documented turns to imperial power both belong to this world's own account of itself, not to a later critic's exposure of it.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed here carries both halves at once: the conviction that the emperor has no standing to judge the church, and the lived memory that this world's own leaders turned to that same emperor three times when it served the case. Formation here does not resolve this into a single consistent rule; it produces the capacity to hold the conviction at full strength while naming, rather than hiding, where it did not in practice hold.

---

**Tension:** Purity-Rigor vs. Institutional Reception (the Maximianist precedent)

**Nature:** What it costs to hold the absolute rebaptism/reordination rule: no exceptions, ever, for any returning cleric, however costly to institutional unity. What it costs to receive returning Maximianist clergy without repeating either rite: undercutting the very doctrine (G1/G2) that gives this world's own rebaptism practice its whole logic. Augustine seized on exactly this gap to build his own central argument against the doctrine as a whole.

**Cross-strand status:** A specific, historically bounded episode (393 Maximianist schism, 394 Bagai reception) rather than a tension running across the whole window — bound to Cell 2B specifically, not cross-strand in the Article 21 sense (this world being strand-singular).

**How the world held it:** The Bagai council's own decree describes the schismatics in the harshest possible terms — "shipwrecked... dashed by the waves of truth upon the sharp rocks... fail to find so much as burial" — and then receives the same men back without rebaptism or reordination. This world's own record states both facts plainly, in the same texts, without softening the decree's language to fit the reception that followed it, or hiding the reception to protect the decree's own absolutism.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed here understands that institutional survival and doctrinal purity are not always the same instinct, and that this world's own leadership, when the two pulled apart, chose continuity of office over the absolute application of its own stated principle — a genuinely uncomfortable inheritance to carry, not a triumph to narrate.
