# Source Acquisition Manifest — Donatism

**Companion to `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` and `Source_Registry.md`.** This is the G1 deliverable: the project lead's decision on which editions enter `cic/texts/` for this world, and on what rights and scope basis. Nothing in this file is a download; it is a set of real, identified candidates for the project lead to choose among (or reject) before any file is placed in the library. Once a source is chosen and the file is placed at its listed destination, the build verifies the file is present and matches its stated identity before Phase A continues past Doc_02 — a missing or substituted file halts, it is never worked around.

**A network-access limitation, disclosed rather than papered over:** this build session's outbound network access blocks direct fetch of the specific pages listed below (archive.org, Gallica, and general web domains all returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` when checked directly). Every `where` entry below is a real result returned by a live web search this session — a genuine title, publisher, series, and URL, not invented — but none has been opened and inspected page-by-page from inside this session. The project lead (or a future session with broader network access) should open each link and confirm it actually delivers what this manifest says it does — full text vs. preview, free download vs. controlled lending — before treating the rights-status column as final.

## 1. `gesta-carth-411` — *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis* (411 Conference acts)

- **Work/edition:** Serge Lancel's critical edition, published as *Actes de la Conférence de Carthage en 411* (Sources Chrétiennes 194, 195, 224, 373, Paris: Cerf, 1972–1991) and separately as *Gesta conlationis Carthaginiensis anno 411* (Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 149A, Turnhout: Brepols, 1974) — facing Latin/French text with critical apparatus. Registry row 14.
- **Rights status:** Sources Chrétiennes and CCSL are both modern, actively-copyrighted critical-edition series (Cerf; Brepols). Not public domain.
- **Where:** `https://archive.org/details/actesdelaconfere0003coun` — a 4-volume item (Lancel's SC edition: general introduction; text and translation of the general capitulation and first session; text and translation of the second and third sessions; critical apparatus, notes, and indexes). Access mode (free download vs. controlled digital lending) not confirmed from inside this session — Internet Archive items of in-copyright critical editions are frequently lending-only, and that should be assumed until checked.
- **Destination (if acquired):** `gesta-collationis-carthaginiensis_actes-conference-carthage_lancel1972.pdf` (or per-volume: `..._lancel1972-v1.pdf` through `-v4.pdf`, matching however the acquired item is actually structured).
- **Why this edition:** Lancel's is the standard modern critical edition of this text, and the only edition with a real, identified hosting location found this session. It also carries Donatist bishops' own recorded words at length (Emeritus of Caesarea), which is this source's whole evidentiary point for this world (Doc_02 §1).
- **Alternatives:** No public-domain full edition (Latin or English) was identified this session. No complete English translation of the *Gesta* appears to exist in any form, free or commercial, based on this session's research — if the project lead wants an English-readable text of the 411 Conference, a partial or excerpted translation would need separate research, or this source stays Latin-only in the library with translation done at the point of use.
- **Scope note:** If acquired via the archive.org item under controlled lending, the practical use case (repeated reference during construction, not one-time reading) may not fit a lending access model — worth confirming before relying on this as the acquisition path, rather than treating it as settled once the link is opened once.

## 2. `tyconius-lr` — Tyconius, *Liber Regularum* (Book of Rules)

- **Work/edition, option A (public domain):** F.C. Burkitt (ed.), *The Book of Rules of Tyconius, Newly Edited from the MSS., with an Introduction and an Examination into the Text of the Biblical Quotations* (Texts and Studies III/I, Cambridge University Press, 1894) — Latin critical text with an English scholarly introduction. Registry row 15.
- **Rights status (option A):** Published 1894 — public domain.
- **Where (option A):** Three separate Internet Archive items were found for this edition this session: `https://archive.org/details/bookofrulesoftyc00ticouoft`, `https://archive.org/details/rulesoftyconius00tico`, and `https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.167130` (a Digital Library of India scan of the same 1894 printing, 244 pages). Access mode not confirmed from inside this session, but public-domain-age Internet Archive scans (as opposed to in-copyright critical editions) are typically freely downloadable rather than lending-restricted.
- **Destination (option A):** `tyconius_liber-regularum_burkitt1894.pdf`
- **Work/edition, option B (modern English, not public domain):** William S. Babcock (trans.), *Tyconius: The Book of Rules* (Society of Biblical Literature, Texts and Translations 31, Early Christian Literature Series 7, 1989) — the modern standard English translation.
- **Rights status (option B):** Copyrighted, SBL 1989. Would require purchase or institutional/library access; no free or public-domain copy identified.
- **Why this edition:** Doc_02 §1 names Tyconius's own work as "the single largest available correction to this world's own Author Gravity concentration" if acquired — a genuinely Donatist-affiliated voice surviving independently rather than through hostile quotation. Option A is free and real but Latin-only; option B is the readable modern English translation this build would most want to work from directly, but is rights-constrained.
- **Alternatives:** none beyond A and B identified this session (an earlier partial modern English rendering by Karlfried Froehlich exists in an edited volume but was not independently verified this session and is, on the same publisher basis, unlikely to be public domain).
- **Scope note:** This is a genuine rights/language tradeoff for the project lead, not a case where one option is simply better: free-but-Latin-only versus readable-but-licensed.

## 3. `cth-16` — *Codex Theodosianus*, Book 16

- **Work/edition, option A (public domain, Latin):** Theodor Mommsen and Paul M. Meyer (eds.), *Theodosiani Libri XVI cum Constitutionibus Sirmondianis* (Berlin: Weidmann, 1905) — the standard critical Latin edition. Registry row 16.
- **Rights status (option A):** Published 1905 — public domain.
- **Where (option A):** `https://archive.org/stream/theodosianilibri02code/theodosianilibri02code_djvu.txt` — a direct plain-text stream of the full scanned volume, which (unlike a lending-interface item) should not require a borrow step; the same item's main page is `https://archive.org/details/theodosianilibri02code`. A second, separately-scanned copy was also found at `https://archive.org/details/theodosianilibri0002unse`.
- **Destination (option A):** `codex-theodosianus_book16_mommsen1905.txt`
- **Work/edition, option B (modern English, not public domain):** Clyde Pharr (trans.), *The Theodosian Code and Novels and the Sirmondian Constitutions* (Princeton University Press, 1952; reprinted by The Lawbook Exchange, 2001, 2012) — described in the secondary literature as likely the only full English translation ever made of this text.
- **Rights status (option B):** Still in active commercial print via reprint (Lawbook Exchange) — not public domain; would require purchase or institutional/library access. `earlychurchtexts.com`, a possible source of free English excerpts, is blocked by this session's own network policy and could not be checked; this remains a genuine open question for whoever next has broader network access, not a closed one.
- **Why this edition:** Mommsen's is the definitive scholarly edition of the Latin text, freely available in a directly downloadable format — the strongest acquisition candidate of the four sources in this manifest by rights clarity alone. It carries this world's independent imperial-legal attestation of the *circumcelliones* (16.5.52) and the 405 Edict of Unity, the evidentiary point Doc_01 and Doc_02 both need it for.
- **Alternatives:** Pharr's translation (option B) if the project lead wants English-language access and is willing to acquire it commercially or through a library; no free English alternative was identified this session.
- **Scope note:** Book 16 specifically is what this world needs — the full six-volume Mommsen edition (or the relevant single volume, if the archive.org item is already split by book) should be trimmed to that book at the point of vendoring, consistent with how other multi-book vendored volumes in this project's library are scoped.

## 4. `passio-macarian` — *Passio Marculi* and *Passio Isaac et Maximiani*

- **Work/edition:** as printed in J.-P. Migne (ed.), *Patrologia Latina*, vol. 8 — the *Passio Marculi* (headed *Passio Benedicti Martyris Marculi*) at columns 760–766; the *Passio Isaac et Maximiani* in the same volume's Donatist-adjacent material. A 19th-century compilation reprinting earlier editions (including Mabillon's *Analecta Vetera*); neither text has a modern critical edition, per the secondary literature checked this session. Registry rows 19, 20.
- **Rights status:** Migne's *Patrologia Latina* (compiled 1841–1855) is public domain.
- **Where:** An Internet Archive item hosting a Google Books scan of this volume, `https://archive.org/details/patrologiae_cursus_completus_lat_vol_008`, was found and described in this session's own search results as offering free download in addition to streaming/borrowing (Google-Books-sourced Internet Archive scans of out-of-copyright volumes are typically freely downloadable, unlike controlled-lending library scans). A Gallica (Bibliothèque nationale de France) digitization of the same Migne volume was also identified in earlier research this pass but its exact ark identifier was not re-confirmed in this session's searches and should be treated as unverified until re-found.
- **Destination:** `passio-marculi-isaac-maximiani_migne-pl8.pdf`
- **Why this edition:** the only edition of either text identified this session in any form, free or commercial — there is no modern critical edition of either Passio to compare it against.
- **Alternatives:** Maureen A. Tilley (trans.), *Donatist Martyr Stories: The Church in Conflict in Roman North Africa*, Translated Texts for Historians 24 (Liverpool University Press, 1996) — the modern standard English translation of the Donatist martyr-narrative corpus, including "The Martyrdom of Marculus" (Registry row 35). Copyrighted; would require purchase or library access. If the project lead wants English text rather than Latin, this is the edition to acquire instead of, or alongside, the Migne Latin.
- **Scope note:** Migne's text is Latin-only and not critically edited; if construction work downstream (Doc_04, Doc_09) needs to quote these Passiones in English or rely on their specific wording rather than their existence and general content, Tilley's translation is the source that licenses that use, not the Migne Latin.
