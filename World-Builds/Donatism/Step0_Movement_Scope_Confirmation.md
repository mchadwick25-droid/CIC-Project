## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing governs the order here: *"This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."*

### A1 — The floor

Article 4's floor tests whether a movement's own confession affirms, in its plain historical sense, the content drawn from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (381) — full divinity and consubstantiality of Christ, true humanity, the Passion/resurrection/ascension/return, and the Spirit as Lord and giver of life.

Donatism's defining dispute is not credal. It originates in a disagreement over the *validity of sacraments and ordinations administered by clergy suspected of having surrendered scripture during the Diocletianic persecution* (the *traditio* charge against Caecilian's consecrator, c. 311–312) — a question of ecclesiology and ministerial purity, never a question of Christ's nature or the Trinity. Every major scholarly treatment of the schism (Frend's foundational study among them) treats the Donatists as doctrinally orthodox by the standards of their own African Latin Christianity — the same confession Cyprian's own third-century communion held, unaltered. Donatist bishops attended councils, used standard Trinitarian language in their own surviving fragments, and Augustine's own extensive polemical corpus against them — the largest single body of surviving evidence about the movement — never once argues against a Donatist Christological or Trinitarian claim; every argument concerns the church's holiness, the validity of *traditor*-tainted sacraments, and the propriety of empire-backed coercion. **A1 clears directly**, on the movement's own plain confessional terms, for the entire life of the movement.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements: applicable to an approximately thirteen-year sliver, and it clears

Per the Methodology's Procedure section, "Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)," and following the resolution already reasoned through for World #6's own straddling start date (`Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2): this world's stated start date, c. 312 CE, precedes Nicaea (325) by roughly thirteen years, so both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of one continuous movement, rather than one displacing the other.

- **c. 312–325 (A2's proper scope):** does this world's own pre-Nicene confession show continuity with the strand that became the Nicene consensus, rather than divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar)? Yes, unambiguously — the schism's own founding actors on both sides (Caecilian and Majorinus/Donatus alike) were formed within, and never departed from, mainstream third/fourth-century North African Latin Christianity — the same confessional strand Cyprian represents earlier and Augustine represents later. There is no rival-strand doctrinal divergence to test for at all; this is, if anything, a cleaner A2 case than World #6's, since no complicating theological current is in play on either side of the dispute.
- **325–430s (A1's proper scope):** covered directly above.

**Resolution for this world:** both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of the same continuous movement, and both clear cleanly — a disciplinary/ecclesiological schism within a single, doctrinally continuous confessional tradition, not a credal departure at any point in its life.

### A3 — Contemporary movements and interpretive fidelity

Not applicable. This is a 4th/5th-century historical movement, not a contemporary confessional community reinterpreting Nicene vocabulary non-literally.

### A4 — Hand-selected adjacent inclusion

Not invoked and not needed. A1 clears this world directly; no divergence from Article 4's five commitments needs to be waived.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This is the section with the most substantive content for this specific world, and it runs in the opposite direction from World #6's A5 finding rather than merely echoing it.

**The Catholic/Caecilianist opposition as this world's own internal rival, per Article 23.** Per A5: a movement's historical rivals "are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under this section... they appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction." Donatism's own defining opponent — Caecilian's Carthaginian communion, and above all Optatus and Augustine, whose writings supply nearly the entire surviving documentary record — is not a separately screened candidate; it is (part of) the not-yet-built World #8's own territory, and Doc_02 must reconstruct that opposition from *inside* Donatism's own perspective (a persecuted "Church of the Martyrs" facing a state-favored rival), not merely cite Optatus and Augustine as neutral sources. This is the mirror image of World #6's A5 finding (Homoian Christianity as World #6's internal opponent): here, the not-yet-built World #8 is Donatism's internal opponent, and — looking forward, once World #8 is built — Donatism will symmetrically need to be named as *its* internal opponent, per the Primary-gravity-first history already on record (§1 above).

**Article 20's Affirmative Duty and the Circumcellion question.** A5 states this section "is never applied to exclude a marginalized voice within an already-included movement whose confession is harder to document than the mainstream's... such a voice is reconstructed and named as part of that world's own ecology." The Circumcellions — an itinerant, rural wing associated with the Donatist movement and with episodes of violence — are known to us almost entirely through hostile Catholic polemic (Augustine and Optatus), and the scholarly literature (Brent Shaw's work prominently) treats the category itself as partly a rhetorical construction of the movement's opponents rather than a stable self-description. This is flagged here as a binding Doc_02 Affirmative Duty item, not resolved: whatever this world's own materials can support about the Circumcellions must be reconstructed with the same source-critical caution Optatus's and Augustine's own Author Gravity entries require, never taken at the hostile sources' own valuation.

**Strand Determination (Article 21)** — whether this world contains internal strands (for instance, a rigorist-separatist mainstream against Tyconius's own more moderate, non-separatist exegetical strand, which stayed formally Donatist while arguing against the sect's ecclesial exclusivism) — is explicitly Step 1's own work, not Step 0's. Flagged forward to Doc_01, not resolved here.

### Section A conclusion

**World #4 clears Section A.** A1 applies directly across the movement's full span and is satisfied on its own plain confessional terms — the schism is disciplinary and ecclesiological, never credal; A2's continuity test governs only the pre-325 sliver and clears cleanly, with no complicating doctrinal current on either side; A3 is inapplicable; A4 is not needed; A5 correctly routes the Catholic/Caecilianist opposition into this world's own Doc_02 as its defining internal rival, and separately flags the Circumcellion question as a binding Article 20 item.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

Section B's own framing: "this is a phase-level process, run once when the project opens a new release phase" — this world was already scored, functionally, at the portfolio level. This confirmation re-applies the five criteria on the record against this world's own specific evidence.

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Test: does enough independent, checkable evidence survive to support a defensible reconstruction at Documented or Widely Accepted confidence for its core claims, with the source-asymmetry named rather than hidden?

Strong pass on volume, with a severe and unusually direct Author Gravity concentration that Doc_02 must name plainly rather than absorb into the general portfolio-wide caveat:

- **Optatus of Milevis, *Against the Donatists*** (c. 366–367, revised c. 385) — the earliest substantial Catholic polemical treatise against the schism, transmitting in its own appendix a body of contemporary documentary material largely independent of either side's advocacy: the *Acta Purgationis Felicis* (314), the *Gesta apud Zenophilum* (320), Constantine's own letters, the Council of Arles' 314 letter to Pope Silvester, the Acts of the Council of Cirta (305), and Anulinus's *relatio* (313). This documentary appendix is this world's closest analogue to World #6's imperial legal material — an evidentiary layer less shaped by either side's own rhetoric.
- **Augustine of Hippo's anti-Donatist corpus** — by far the largest body of surviving evidence: *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* (7 books, c. 400); *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* (3 books, quoting the Donatist bishop Petilian of Constantine's own letters clause by clause — the single most extensive surviving Donatist voice in the corpus, preserved entirely inside its own refutation); *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, c. 417, defending imperial coercion after the 411 Conference); and a substantial cluster of letters (Letters XXIII, XLIII, XLIV, LIII, LXXVI, LXXXVII, LXXXVIII, LXXXIX, XCIII, CXXXIX, CLXXIII).
- **Conciliar and institutional record** — the Council of Carthage's 419 canons on the reception of Donatist clergy and congregations, and (a required Doc_02 acquisition, not yet vendored — see G1) the *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis*, the acts of the imperially-convened 411 Conference of Carthage, at which Donatist bishops (Emeritus of Caesarea prominent among them) spoke and are recorded on their own side of the exchange.
- **Tyconius**, a Donatist exegete whose *Book of Rules* (a major hermeneutical treatise on scriptural interpretation, later influential on Augustine himself) survives substantially in its own right rather than only through hostile quotation — one of the very few points where this world's own intellectual voice is directly accessible rather than mediated. His own relationship to Donatist separatism is itself contested in the surviving record (he stayed formally within the communion while arguing against its exclusivist ecclesiology), which Doc_01's Strand Determination must take up rather than flatten.

**Author Gravity disclosure, carried forward as binding on Doc_02, not resolved here:** with the partial exception of Tyconius and the documentary appendix material in Optatus, this world's textual record survives almost entirely through the words of its opponents — Optatus and, overwhelmingly, Augustine. Even Petilian's own letters — the fullest surviving Donatist voice — reach us only as fragments embedded in, and selected by, Augustine's clause-by-clause refutation. This is a more severe and more direct case of Author Gravity risk than World #6's B1 disclosure (elite institutional sources reporting on their own institution's power): here, the movement's own primary voice is recoverable, where it is recoverable at all, only through the deliberate selection and framing of the side that eventually suppressed it. Doc_02 must name this as the world's central evidentiary problem, not a background caveat, and Author Gravity entries for Optatus and Augustine must apply the Transmission History dimension with particular weight.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

Test: can this candidate plausibly support most or all eight ecology lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only?

This world's evidentiary strength and thinness sit on an unusual axis. **Worship and memory ecology is unusually rich for this candidate specifically** — per the Step 0 Conclusion's own disclosure, martyr-cult and confessor devotion "is most substantively attested in world #4": the veneration of the Abitinian martyrs and the confessors of the Diocletianic persecution is not peripheral color but the movement's own self-understanding as the "Church of the Martyrs," directly recoverable through Passio literature (the *Acts of the Abitinian Martyrs* prominent among them — itself a text whose Donatist-versus-Catholic transmission history is scholarly-contested and must be named as such, not treated as a clean witness). **Formation Ecology at the ordinary-believer level is structurally at risk** for the reason named in B1 — the movement's own textual voice survives mainly through hostile mediation — but is partially mitigated by a fact distinct from World #6's case: Donatism was, for substantial stretches and substantial regions, not an elite minority current but the *numerically dominant* church in parts of Roman North Africa, meaning "ordinary believer" experience is not a separate, thinly-attested population but the bulk of the movement itself; the access problem is source-mediation, not scale.

**Passes B2, with this limitation (rich worship/memory ecology, structurally mediated ordinary-formation ecology) logged forward as a binding disclosure for Doc_02 and Doc_05, not treated as resolved by noting it here.**

### B3 — Uniqueness (differentiation)

Test: judged relative to what is already selected or built for the phase, not in the abstract.

- **Versus World #8 (Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity, Cyprian/Augustine):** already confirmed distinct in the Step 0 Conclusion itself, and this is the pairing that produced the Primary-gravity-first rule (§1 above). World #8's own entry states the boundary directly: Cyprian and Augustine's ordinary pastoral office and sacramental care, "not the schism-crisis angle, which belongs to world #4," with Donatism explicitly named as "occupying and contesting the interval" between them. This world's own primary gravity — a rigorist rival communion's claim to be the one pure church, contesting sacramental validity and clerical purity across a century-plus schism — is a different axis from World #8's ordinary episcopal pastoral care, not a variant of it. Not built yet, so no live cross-document check was possible; the boundary is logged here as binding on Doc_01 so it is not redrawn informally later, exactly as the IJC precedent did for its own not-yet-built neighbor.
- **Versus World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity):** genuine adjacency requiring active vigilance, not assumed-obvious distinctness. Donatism's later history is deeply entangled with imperial power — the 405 Edict of Unity, the 411 Conference of Carthage convened and adjudicated by the imperial commissioner Marcellinus, and Augustine's own *Correction of the Donatists* defending state coercion of the schism. The distinguishing axis: World #6 is built around a communion's own consolidation and exercise of state-adjacent institutional power; Donatism experiences imperial power as an external force pressed against it, from a position of eventual suppression rather than institutional ascendancy — an object of juridical action, not a wielder of it. This is a live boundary Doc_01/Doc_02 must state explicitly, not merely infer, particularly since World #6's own build (not yet reached this Step 0 confirmation's cross-check, but logged for both builds) will need Donatism as a named case within its own institutional-coercion material.
- **Versus World #1 (Post-Apostolic House-Church) and World #3 (Desert Monasticism):** distinct on period, region, and formation logic; no meaningful overlap risk. The Step 0 Conclusion's own disclosure that martyr-cult "is present in world #1" as well as "most substantively attested in world #4" is a difference of degree and center of gravity (a living identity built substantially on martyr memory, versus one strand among several), not a redundancy risk.

**Passes B3.** The one adjacency requiring active vigilance during construction (World #6, on the imperial-coercion axis) is named here so Doc_01/Doc_02 treat it as a live boundary question.

### B4 — User needs (audience fit)

Test: does this candidate offer something distinctive to several of the Essential Experience document's named audiences, honestly noted rather than claimed universally.

Honest assessment: this world's strongest and most distinctive fit is with *deconstructing Christians* and *thoughtful believers* wrestling with how institutional power, purity, and the definition of "the true church" have been contested and enforced historically — a rigorist movement persecuted into near-extinction by an empire-backed rival raises exactly the kind of hard question about coercion, orthodoxy, and who gets to decide that this audience is named for. It offers real material to *historically interested learners* and *academically minded users* studying schism, sacramental theology, and church-state relations in late antiquity. Its fit with *curious seekers* is real but narrower — a participant approaching Christian formation for the first time is less likely to reach for a schismatic rigorist minority church than for one of the portfolio's more devotionally central worlds, though the martyr-cult material (Passio narratives, confessor memory) gives this world genuine devotional texture distinct from a purely doctrinal-argument register. **Passes B4, honestly scoped rather than claimed universally.**

### B5 — Scale (tiebreak only)

Donatism was, for substantial periods, not a marginal sect but the majority church across large parts of Roman North Africa — a genuinely large-scale movement, not merely a well-documented small one. Per the Methodology, this is "a modest, non-decisive positive factor," not a filter, but it is worth recording precisely because B2 above leans on this same fact to mitigate the Formation Ecology thinness concern.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Against the tiering rubric (Tier 1 — clears Section A directly, clears Sourcing and Ecology comfortably, offers genuine differentiation, serves multiple named audiences): this world clears Section A directly (§2), clears Sourcing with a severe but well-bounded and explicitly named Author Gravity concentration rather than failing it (B1), clears Ecology with a named and bounded limitation rather than failing it (B2), offers genuine differentiation with one adjacency actively flagged for vigilance rather than assumed (B3), and serves multiple named audiences honestly rather than universally (B4). **World #4 confirms as Tier 1 — Strong seed.** This matches its de facto portfolio-level standing in the Step 0 Conclusion (selected outright, not deferred, excluded, or routed through A4).
