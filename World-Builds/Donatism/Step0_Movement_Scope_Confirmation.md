## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing governs the order here: *"This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."*

### A1 — The floor

Article 4's floor is stated in the Constitution and is not restated here in full (Methodology A1: *"The floor is stated in Article 4 of the Constitution... and is not restated here"*) — see Article 4, "On the Scope of 'Movement,'" for its complete five-commitment text.

Donatism's defining dispute is not credal. It originates in a disagreement over the *validity of sacraments and ordinations administered by clergy suspected of having surrendered scripture during the Diocletianic persecution* (the *traditio* charge against Caecilian's consecrator, c. 311–312) — a question of ecclesiology and ministerial purity, not a question of Christ's nature or the Trinity. The scholarly consensus (Frend's *The Donatist Church* the foundational study) treats the Donatists as doctrinally orthodox by the standards of their own African Latin Christianity — the same confession Cyprian's own third-century communion held. Donatist bishops attended councils and used standard Trinitarian language in their own surviving fragments, and Augustine's anti-Donatist corpus — the largest single body of surviving evidence about the movement — does not argue against a Donatist Christological or Trinitarian claim; every argument concerns the church's holiness, the validity of *traditor*-tainted sacraments, and the propriety of empire-backed coercion. **A1 clears directly**, on the movement's own plain confessional terms, for the life of the movement within this phase's window.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements: applicable to an approximately thirteen-year sliver, and it clears

Per the Methodology's Procedure section, "Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)," and following the resolution already reasoned through for World #6's own straddling start date (`Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2): this world's stated start date, c. 312 CE, precedes Nicaea (325) by roughly thirteen years, so both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of one continuous movement.

- **c. 312–325 (A2's proper scope):** does this world's own pre-Nicene confession show continuity with the strand that became the Nicene consensus, rather than divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar)? Yes — the schism's own founding actors on both sides (Caecilian and Majorinus/Donatus alike) were formed within, and never departed from, mainstream third/fourth-century North African Latin Christianity — the same confessional strand Cyprian represents earlier and Augustine represents later. **One genuine qualification, not present in the Round 1 draft:** the rebaptism question at the schism's root is not merely disciplinary — the Donatists revived Cyprian's own third-century position against Pope Stephen (that baptism outside the true church is no baptism), a real and long-standing sacramental-theological divergence within Latin Christianity, not a novel invention. It does not touch any of Article 4's five commitments and so does not disturb A1 or A2, but it is a genuine current, not "no complicating theological current... on either side," and is named accurately here rather than minimized.
- **325–430s (A1's proper scope):** covered directly above.

**Resolution for this world:** both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of the same continuous movement, and both clear — a disciplinary/ecclesiological and sacramental-practice schism within a single, credally continuous confessional tradition, not a departure from Article 4's content at any point.

### Criterion 2 — the person-defined-movement test (adopted portfolio-level, not codified in Section A; new in v2 — Round 1 M1, M2)

**Numbering note (v3 — Round 2 N7):** this test is deliberately not numbered "A2.5" or given any A-series label. It has no home in the formally codified Section A (A1–A5) text — it exists only in the Step 0 Conclusion's own prose — and giving it an A-series number inside a Section A walkthrough would itself be the precedent-hardening IJC's own §5/§6 warned against, arriving through formatting rather than through a claim. It is addressed here, adjacent to A2, because both concern the pre-Nicaea/founding period, but it is not part of the A1–A5 sequence and the Section A conclusion below lists it separately.

The Round 1 draft omitted this test entirely, an omission Round 1 correctly flagged as consequential: this is the first *eponymous* movement (named for Donatus) to reach a per-world Step 0 confirmation since the Step 0 Conclusion adopted the test: *"whether a movement's whole authority claim rests on a small number of named individuals' personal revelation or teaching, rather than a broader communal interpretive tradition. A movement can clear the doctrinal floor and still be excluded on this second ground alone."* It excluded Montanism and Novatianism; the Step 0 Conclusion separately notes *"Novatianism, excluded on the same new ground. Real redundancy risk against Donatism if both were ever built, independent of the criterion question"* — logged here rather than only in §3 B3.

Donatism clears this test on the same terms IJC's own §5 Finding 2 already named as the codification gap: the test itself has no home in the formally codified Section A (A1–A5) text — it exists only in the Step 0 Conclusion's own prose, applied there for the first time. Substantively, though, Donatism's own authority claim does not rest on Donatus's personal teaching or revelation at all: the movement's own argument, as its surviving fragments and the vendored corpus map's `role: antecedent` classification for Cyprian's *Epistles*, *De Lapsis*, *De Unitate*, and the 256 Council of Carthage on rebaptism show, is that it stands in continuity with a **communal, pre-existing North African rigorist tradition** — Cyprian's own — not that Donatus himself originated a new revelation. "Donatist" is substantially an opponents' label for a movement that called itself, and understood itself as, the church of the martyrs and confessors, in continuity with the wider African communion's own prior practice. On that basis, this world clears the person-defined-movement test on communal-tradition grounds — the same ground (a broader communal interpretive tradition, not a small number of named individuals' personal revelation) on which the test itself distinguishes an excludable movement from one that is not, applied here rather than asserted (**corrected in v3 — Round 2 N9**: the Round 2 draft drew a garbled analogy to "Cyprian's or Novatian's own doctrinal soundness," misassigning Cyprian, who founded no such movement and was never a Criterion 2 case; the actual excluded movements are Montanism and Novatianism, and the relevant point about them is authority-claim structure, not doctrinal soundness, which was never in question for either).

**This finding is offered on the same footing IJC's own A1/A2 resolution was offered** — a reasoned application of an adopted-but-not-yet-codified test, not a claim that Section A itself already contains it. **Corrected in v3 (Round 2 N4):** it is named to System Hub in §5 as the *first* world whose Step 0 confirmation has actually had to apply this test — IJC's own Finding 2 flagged the codification gap without needing the test itself (*"would not exclude a multi-figure, century-spanning institutional world like this one even if applied"*), so this is not a second instance of the same need, and §5 below is corrected to say so.
