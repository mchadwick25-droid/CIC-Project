## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing governs the order here: "This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."

### A1 — The floor

Article 4's floor is stated in the Constitution and is not restated here in full — see Article 4, "On the Scope of 'Movement,'" for its complete five-commitment text.

Donatism's defining dispute is not credal. It originates in a disagreement over the validity of sacraments and ordinations administered by clergy suspected of having surrendered scripture during the Diocletianic persecution (the *traditio* charge against Caecilian's consecrator, c. 311–312) — a question of ecclesiology and ministerial purity, not a question of Christ's nature or the Trinity. The scholarly consensus (Frend's *The Donatist Church* the foundational study) treats the Donatists as doctrinally orthodox by the standards of their own African Latin Christianity — the same confession Cyprian's own third-century communion held. Donatist bishops attended councils and used standard Trinitarian language in their own surviving fragments, and Augustine's anti-Donatist corpus — the largest single body of surviving evidence about the movement — does not argue against a Donatist Christological or Trinitarian claim; every argument concerns the church's holiness, the validity of *traditor*-tainted sacraments, and the propriety of empire-backed coercion. A1 clears directly, on the movement's own plain confessional terms, for the life of the movement within this phase's window.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements: applicable to an approximately thirteen-year sliver, and it clears

Per the Methodology's Procedure section, "Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)," and following the resolution already reasoned through for World #6's own straddling start date (`Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2): this world's stated start date, c. 312 CE, precedes Nicaea (325) by roughly thirteen years, so both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of one continuous movement.

- **c. 312–325 (A2's proper scope):** does this world's own pre-Nicene confession show continuity with the strand that became the Nicene consensus, rather than divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar)? Yes — the schism's own founding actors on both sides (Caecilian and Majorinus/Donatus alike) were formed within, and never departed from, mainstream third/fourth-century North African Latin Christianity — the same confessional strand Cyprian represents earlier and Augustine represents later. One genuine qualification: the rebaptism question at the schism's root is not merely disciplinary — the Donatists revived Cyprian's own third-century position against Pope Stephen (that baptism outside the true church is no baptism), a real and long-standing sacramental-theological divergence within Latin Christianity, not a novel invention. It does not touch any of Article 4's five commitments and so does not disturb A1 or A2, but it is a genuine current, named accurately here rather than minimized.
- **325–430s (A1's proper scope):** covered directly above.

**Resolution for this world:** both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of the same continuous movement, and both clear — a disciplinary/ecclesiological and sacramental-practice schism within a single, credally continuous confessional tradition, not a departure from Article 4's content at any point.

### Criterion 2 — the person-defined-movement test (adopted portfolio-level, not codified in Section A)

This test has no home in the formally codified Section A (A1–A5) text — it exists only in the Step 0 Conclusion's own prose. It is addressed here, adjacent to A2, because both concern the pre-Nicaea/founding period, but it is not part of the A1–A5 sequence and the Section A conclusion below lists it separately.

This is the first eponymous movement (named for Donatus) to reach a per-world Step 0 confirmation since the Step 0 Conclusion adopted the test: "whether a movement's whole authority claim rests on a small number of named individuals' personal revelation or teaching, rather than a broader communal interpretive tradition. A movement can clear the doctrinal floor and still be excluded on this second ground alone." It excluded Montanism and Novatianism; the Step 0 Conclusion separately notes "Novatianism, excluded on the same new ground. Real redundancy risk against Donatism if both were ever built, independent of the criterion question" — logged here and at §3 B3 below.

Donatism clears this test: its own authority claim does not rest on Donatus's personal teaching or revelation at all. The movement's own argument, as its surviving fragments and the vendored corpus map's `role: antecedent` classification for Cyprian's *Epistles*, *De Lapsis*, *De Unitate*, and the 256 Council of Carthage on rebaptism show, is that it stands in continuity with a communal, pre-existing North African rigorist tradition — Cyprian's own — not that Donatus himself originated a new revelation. "Donatist" is substantially an opponents' label for a movement that called itself, and understood itself as, the church of the martyrs and confessors, in continuity with the wider African communion's own prior practice.

This world's own Step 0 confirmation is the first to have to actually apply this test — Imperial-Juridical-Christianity's own Step 0 confirmation flagged the codification gap without needing the test itself, since the person-defined-movement ground would not have excluded a multi-figure, century-spanning institutional world. That is a data point for System Hub (§5 below), not a claim that this reading is already established methodology.

The Circumcellions — an itinerant, rural wing associated with the Donatist movement and with episodes of violence — are known to us substantially through hostile Catholic polemic (Augustine and Optatus), and the characterization the polemic gives them is partly a rhetorical construction (Brent Shaw's *Sacred Violence* is the standard treatment) — but the group itself is independently attested outside hostile polemic: imperial legislation names them directly (*Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.52, 412), and Augustine himself records their own self-designation, *agonistici* ("contestants," a term of the group's own, not merely the outsiders' label). This is flagged as a binding Doc_02 Affirmative Duty item, not resolved: this world's materials must reconstruct what can responsibly be said about the *agonistici* with the same source-critical caution Optatus's and Augustine's own Author Gravity entries require, distinguishing the independently-attested existence of the group from the polemically-shaped account of its character.

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Test: does enough independent, checkable evidence survive to support a defensible reconstruction at Documented or Widely Accepted confidence for its core claims, with the source-asymmetry named rather than hidden?

Strong pass on volume, with a severe and unusually direct Author Gravity concentration that Doc_02 must name plainly:

- **The Cyprianic antecedent corpus:** `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml` carries Cyprian's *Epistles*, *De Lapsis*, *De Unitate*, and the acts of the 256 Council of Carthage under `role: antecedent` — "the Donatists' standing proof that African tradition required rebaptism of those baptized outside the church." Cyprian is, on the corpus map's own framing, "the authority both sides argued FROM" — load-bearing for both this world's A1/A2 reasoning above and its B3 boundary with World #8 below.
- **Optatus of Milevis, *Against the Donatists*** (c. 366–367, revised c. 385) — the earliest substantial Catholic polemical treatise against the schism, transmitting in its own appendix a body of contemporary documentary material: the *Acta Purgationis Felicis* (314), the *Gesta apud Zenophilum* (320), Constantine's own letters, the Council of Arles' 314 letter to Pope Silvester, the Acts of the Council of Cirta (305 — a date genuinely disputed in the scholarship, with 307 and later argued by some), and Anulinus's *relatio* (313) — list per `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml`. This dossier's contents are court acts and imperial correspondence, but its selection, assembly, and transmission are a Catholic polemicist's act; the corpus map's own framing is the more careful one — `role: context`, "officials and emperors describing and adjudicating the parties, not either side's own voice" — and it does not recover the Donatist voice itself, so it does not mitigate the Author Gravity problem below the way a genuinely independent source would.
- **Augustine of Hippo's anti-Donatist corpus** — by far the largest body of surviving evidence: *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* (7 books, c. 400); *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* (3 books, quoting the Donatist bishop Petilian of Constantine/Cirta's own letters clause by clause — the single most extensive surviving Donatist voice in the vendored corpus specifically — preserved entirely inside its own refutation); *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, c. 417, to the tribune Boniface, defending imperial coercion after the 411 Conference); and a cluster of eleven letters (XXIII, XLIII, XLIV, LIII, LXXVI, LXXXVII, LXXXVIII, LXXXIX, XCIII, CXXXIX, CLXXIII).
- **Imperial legal material:** *Codex Theodosianus* Book 16 carries a substantial Donatist dossier independent of Optatus's and Augustine's own advocacy, including the 405 Edict of Unity and CTh 16.5.52 (412), which names the *circumcelliones* directly. **Vendored 2026-09-07** (`theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt`, after five prior acquisition attempts) and mapped in `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml` — corrected 2026-09-08, Round 2 Opus review, from this line's own stale "not currently vendored" claim, which reproduced the same directive Doc_01 §7 item 6 had already been corrected for. This is a second body of imperial-institutional evidence genuinely independent of both polemicists' own selection, and a present strength of the library as it stands, not merely a prospective one.
- **Conciliar and institutional record:** the canons on reception of Donatist clergy and congregations collected in the 419 African register (the *Code of Canons of the African Church*), mostly compiling African conciliar canons originating c. 397–407 rather than legislating them fresh; `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml` records this entry at `confidence: provisional`, carried forward here since B1's own test is confidence-sensitive. A required Doc_02 acquisition target, not yet vendored: the *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis*, the acts of the imperially-convened 411 Conference of Carthage, at which Donatist bishops (Emeritus of Caesarea prominent among them) are recorded speaking at length on their own side of the exchange.
- **Tyconius** — a Donatist-affiliated exegete whose *Book of Rules* (a major hermeneutical treatise, later influential on Augustine's own *De doctrina christiana* III) survives as a substantially complete, independently-authored work rather than only through hostile quotation. **Vendored 2026-09-01** (`tyconius_liber-regularum_burkitt1894.txt`, Mark, Manifest §1 G1) and mapped in `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml` (staged 2026-09-08) — corrected 2026-09-08, Round 2 Opus review, from this line's own stale "not currently vendored" claim. This mitigation is a present strength of the library as it stands, not a prospective one. His own formal standing within the Donatist communion is itself contested, not settled: the standard account holds that Parmenian and a Donatist council condemned him (c. 380) over his universalist ecclesiology, and that he nonetheless never joined the Catholic church — his formal status is the thing genuinely in dispute in the record, and is left open here rather than resolved in either direction, feeding directly into Doc_01's Strand Determination. Separately flagged for the census owner: `cic/texts/README.md` and `cic/engine/texts_registry.py` currently state that Donatist voices "survive at all... 'known only through Augustine's quotations'" — a claim the *Liber Regularum*'s independent survival contradicts, surfaced here for correction at the census level.
- **The material, epigraphic, and hagiographic record:** a body of Donatist-side and archaeological evidence exists that is the least mediated by either polemicist — basilica archaeology across Numidia, the *Deo laudes* acclamation attested epigraphically against the Catholic *Deo gratias*, and Donatist-side texts including the *Passio Marculi*, the *Passio Isaac et Maximiani*, and the *Liber genealogus*. None of this is yet inventoried in the vendored corpus map; flagged for Doc_02's own source-ecology work.

**Author Gravity disclosure, carried forward as binding on Doc_02:** the textual record that is currently vendored survives almost entirely through Optatus's and Augustine's own polemical framing. Even Petilian's own letters — the fullest Donatist voice in the vendored corpus specifically — reach us only as fragments embedded in, and selected by, Augustine's clause-by-clause refutation. ### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

Test: can this candidate plausibly support most or all eight ecology lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only? The Methodology's B2 criterion cites "the Formation Ecology and Integrated Ecology Analysis dimensions (eight co-equal analytical lenses)" — the Formation World Template's own Integrated Ecology Analysis section, which names exactly eight: Emotional Ecology, Philosophical Ecology, Interpretive Ecology, Authority Structures, Boundary Structures, Formation Logic, Memory Structures, and Representative Theological Patterns.

- **Authority Structures:** this world's strongest lens by any reading — a full parallel episcopal hierarchy, town-for-town against the Caecilianist sees, contesting who legitimately holds office at every level, is the movement's own defining institutional fact.
- **Boundary Structures:** equally strong — rebaptism is, literally, the mechanism by which this world draws and polices its own boundary of belonging; ministerial purity and *traditor*-taint are boundary questions in their most direct form.
- **Emotional Ecology:** unusually rich for this candidate specifically. Per the Step 0 Conclusion's own disclosure, martyr-cult and confessor devotion "is most substantively attested in world #4": the veneration of the Abitinian martyrs and the confessors of the Diocletianic persecution is the movement's own self-understanding as the "Church of the Martyrs," carrying the emotional register of persecution-memory, vindication, and grievance against the state-favored rival, recoverable through Passio literature (the *Acts of the Abitinian Martyrs* prominent among them — its Donatist-versus-Catholic transmission history is genuinely contested in the scholarship and must be named as such, not treated as a clean witness).
- **Philosophical Ecology:** thinner than the ecclesiological material but real — Tyconius's hermeneutics (pending acquisition) is this world's most substantial candidate content here; the rebaptism theology inherited from Cyprian is more sacramental-ecclesiological than philosophical in the narrower sense.
- **Interpretive Ecology:** carried substantially by Tyconius's *Book of Rules* itself (pending acquisition); otherwise thin in what is currently vendored, since interpretive habits are the kind of material most dependent on a movement's own surviving exegetical voice rather than a polemicist's report of it.
- **Formation Logic:** the *agonistici*/Circumcellion material and the rebaptism rite itself bear on how the movement formed and re-formed members crossing from the Caecilianist side; genuinely present but, per B1's Author Gravity disclosure, mediated by hostile sources for now.
- **Memory Structures:** overlaps substantially with Emotional Ecology above (martyr-cult as a memory practice, not only an affective one) and is likewise unusually rich for this world specifically.
- **Representative Theological Patterns:** thinner — the surviving pattern is largely reactive (defending sacramental rigorism against Catholic and, later, imperial pressure) rather than a fully independent constructive theology, though Tyconius (pending acquisition) is again the strongest counter-example.

Formation Ecology (the separate Template dimension, not one of the eight lenses) at the ordinary-believer level is structurally at risk for the reason named in B1 — the currently-vendored textual voice survives mainly through hostile mediation — but is partially mitigated by a fact distinct from World #6's own B2 case: Donatism was, for substantial stretches and regions, not an elite minority current but the numerically dominant church in parts of Roman North Africa (the 411 Conference itself seated 284 Donatist against 286 Catholic bishops), meaning "ordinary believer" experience is not a separate, thinly-attested population but the bulk of the movement itself; the access problem is source-mediation, not scale.

Passes B2 across all eight of the Template's own lenses, unevenly — strongest in Authority Structures, Boundary Structures, Emotional Ecology, and Memory Structures; genuinely present but currently source-mediated or acquisition-dependent in the remaining four — with the underlying Author Gravity concentration and the Formation Ecology mediation problem logged forward as a binding disclosure for Doc_02 and Doc_05, and the material/epigraphic evidence logged as support for Authority Structures, Boundary Structures, and Memory Structures specifically rather than a lens of its own.

### B4 — User needs (audience fit)

Test: does this candidate offer something distinctive to several of the Essential Experience document's named audiences, honestly noted rather than claimed universally.

This world's strongest and most distinctive fit is with *deconstructing Christians* and *thoughtful believers* wrestling with how institutional power, purity, and the definition of "the true church" have been contested and enforced historically. The framing is not a rigorist movement persecuted into near-extinction — what the sources actually support is a movement legally proscribed and progressively suppressed after 411–412, its clergy exiled and property confiscated under increasing imperial penalty, yet one that survived the Vandal conquest (429–439, whose persecution fell on Catholics rather than Donatists), persisted through the Byzantine reconquest, and remained a live pastoral problem for Gregory the Great in Numidia in the 590s — a slower, contested, and ultimately incomplete suppression. It offers real material to *historically interested learners* and *academically minded users* studying schism, sacramental theology, and church-state relations in late antiquity. Its fit with *curious seekers* is real but narrower, though the martyr-cult material (Passio narratives, confessor memory) gives this world genuine devotional texture distinct from a purely doctrinal-argument register. Passes B4, honestly scoped rather than claimed universally.
