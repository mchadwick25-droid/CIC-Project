> "the mainstream party's own much larger council at Bagai (394) condemned the Maximianists and, over the following years, suppressed them, invoking existing imperial and proconsular anti-heretical legislation against Maximianist clergy (Augustine works through the proconsular acts at *Contra Cresconium* III–IV, and **reproaches Emeritus with it at *Epistula* 87.8**)"

**Verified against the vendored text**, `cic/texts/npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml`, `div3 id="vii.1.LXXXVII"` — Letter LXXXVII, *To Emeritus*, a.d. 405 — extracted and read in full, all ten numbered sections:

- **§6** is the only Maximianist passage in the letter: "Have you come so to judge of things, as to affirm that the council of the followers of Maximianus who were cut off from you, as you were cut off from the Church, was of no authority against you, because their number was small compared with yours; and yet claim for your council an authority against the nations…" That is a reproach about *conciliar authority*, not about the invocation of imperial or proconsular machinery.
- **§8** is about the opposite matter — Catholic recourse to the civil powers *against the Donatists*: "You answer, perhaps, that Christians ought not to persecute even the wicked… Did you withdraw, some time ago, from communion with the deputy Flavianus…? Again, you will say that the Roman emperors are incited against you by us." The Maximianists are not mentioned in §8 at all.
- **Nowhere in Letter 87's ten sections** does Augustine reproach Emeritus with the Primianist party's use of proconsular or imperial legal machinery against Maximianist clergy. The nearest adjacent material is §5's reproach about tolerating Optatus of Thamugadi and his association with Gildo, which is a different charge.

The *Contra Cresconium* III–IV half of the citation is sound — Augustine does work through the proconsular acts on the Maximianist cases there. The Ep. 87.8 half supports nothing it is attached to.

**Provenance note, logged rather than absorbed.** This citation did not originate in the draft. It originated in **Round 1's own M3 fix text** ("the episode Augustine throws back at Emeritus (*Ep.* 87.8) and works through the proconsular acts in *Contra Cresconium* III–IV"). v2 adopted a reviewer-supplied citation into the document's own voice without verifying it. That is a real and distinct failure mode from the fabrications this build has logged before, and it is logged as a review-to-review disagreement in Part III.

**Fix.** Delete "and reproaches Emeritus with it at *Epistula* 87.8." If a reproach-to-Emeritus citation is wanted for the Maximianist affair generally, *Ep.* 87.6 is the correct locus for the conciliar-authority reproach, and *De gestis cum Emerito* is where Augustine presses Emeritus on the Maximianist case directly — but neither supports the proconsular-machinery claim, which *Contra Cresconium* III–IV carries on its own.

### N4. (Low) The transmission channel named for the Circumcellion detail is not the channel that carries it

### N5. (Low) §4's Caecilianist reductio equivocates, and overstates what Step 0 §2 A5 settles

**v2 §4:** "Run the other way, the same reasoning that would grant strand status here would grant it to the Caecilianist church itself — which Step 0 §2 A5 already settles otherwise: the Caecilianist party is this world's own opponent within Doc_02, not a strand of it."

Two problems.

**(a) The reductio equivocates between two different failure modes.** The Maximianists fail Article 21 (on v2's own three grounds) because they are *not distinct* — no separate formation emphasis, practice, ecological orientation, or ground of authority. The Caecilianists fail for the opposite reason: they are *not internal*. Article 21's test is for a distinct pattern "**within a single world**," and the Caecilianists are, by this project's own architecture, the rival communion this world defines itself against. On the distinctness criterion the Caecilianists would pass easily — different sacramental theology, different practice on rebaptism, different orientation to imperial power. So the comparison does not show that "rival hierarchy with its own consecrated bishops" is insufficient for strand-hood; it shows only that world membership is a separate gate. As an argument about Article 21 it proves nothing.

**(b) Step 0 §2 A5 does not settle strand status.** A5, re-read verbatim this round, settles *placement*: a movement's historical rivals "appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates," which puts the Caecilianist party inside this world's Doc_02 as its opponents. A5 says nothing about strands and does not address whether an opponent could be one. "Already settles otherwise" claims more than A5 does.

**This does not disturb the finding.** The first three grounds in the same paragraph are each independently sufficient and are correctly reasoned from Article 21's actual text. The reductio is a fourth, rhetorical flourish that should either be removed or restated as what it actually is — a note that world membership, not Article 21, is what excludes the Caecilianists.

**Provenance note:** this argument was supplied by Round 1's own H1 (point 4) and adopted into v2. Logged in Part III.
