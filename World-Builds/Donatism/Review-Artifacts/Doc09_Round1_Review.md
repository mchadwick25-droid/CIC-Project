## Verdict: SUBSTANTIAL REVISION REQUIRED

The tier discipline itself is sound and the No-Tier-5 audit holds: nothing in the eight chunks is invented, composited beyond its source, or extrapolated past what the cited text actually says. Six of the eight stories (donstory004 through donstory008, and the bulk of donstory003) were checked phrase-by-phrase against their cited primary sources and are accurate to a genuinely high standard — named participants, quoted phrases, sums of money, dates, and even minor procedural details (Ingentius "prepared for" rather than actually put to torture; the 310-bishop count at Bagai; the exact wording of the Bagai decree's "shipwrecked... sharp rocks... fail to find so much as burial") all independently verified against the underlying English translations. The Source Registry cross-reference in Doc_09 §5 is accurate row-for-row, including every Confidence letter grade. The document's own self-disclosed gaps (Section 8's two unregistered citations, Letter LXVI and Letter CV) are themselves accurate on independent verification, down to the specific detail that Letter CV is not present in the vendored file at all.

Against that, three High-severity findings mean this document is not ready to clear as drafted. **donstory001 states as its central narrative claim that the Passio Donati's annual commemoration falls "on the fourth of March" — this is a real date, checkable, and wrong: the sermon's own heading and Mabillon's own admonitio give the date as "4 Idus Martii," which converts to 12 March by the ordinary Roman ante diem convention, and this document's own antecedents already say so correctly** (Doc_02 §4: "delivered on the fourth of the Ides of March"; `Source_Registry.md` row 50, quoting Monceaux: "dates the persecution precisely to 12 March 317"). Doc_09 introduces an error its own cited sources do not contain. **donstory002's Source field misattributes an argument:** it credits "Optatus's own text, which Augustine quotes" with disputing whether Marculus's and Donatus's deaths were martyrdom or self-inflicted death, but that specific dispute is Augustine's own argument in a different work (*Answer to the Letters of Petilian* II.45–46), not anything Optatus's own text (*Against the Donatists*, Book III) argues — Optatus's actual argument there is a different one (that the deaths were deserved punishment, on the model of Phineas, Moses, and Elijah). The two arguments were conflated by treating a modern translator's footnote drawing an analogy between them as if it recorded an actual citation chain. **Most substantively: Doc_09 §7's central conclusion — that Tyconius's condemnation "does not fit Tier 1" because "no direct textual attestation... survives... within or close to this world's own horizon," only "Monceaux's 1920 secondary literary-historical synthesis, fifteen centuries removed" — is factually incorrect, and checkably so from material this document itself cites.** Monceaux's own footnote (which Doc_09 quotes from directly) cites Augustine's own *Contra Epistulam Parmeniani* I.1 for the condemnation; that work is not merely referenced but fully vendored, in Latin, at `cic/texts/augustini_scripta-contra-donatistas-pars-i-iii_petschenig1908-1910.txt` (Registry row 39), and this review located the exact sentence at line 2186: "*postea uero etiam concilio eorum perhibent esse damnatum*" ("afterward, however, it is reported that he was even condemned by their own council") — a near-contemporary (c. 400 CE) primary attestation, appropriately hedged with "*perhibent*" ("it is reported"), well within this world's own construction-window horizon. `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §9 item 11 had already flagged this exact gap ("both works are now vendored... and could be checked directly in a future pass") as an open item handed forward specifically for this stage of construction. Doc_09 did not take that check, and its Section 7 boundary-case reasoning is built on the false premise that no primary attestation exists.

None of this is a No-Tier-5 violation — nothing in the eight built stories is invented. But a document whose central "we cannot tell this story" argument turns out to be wrong on direct inspection of material the document's own footnote trail points to, alongside two further sourcing/citation errors in already-built chunks, is not ready to clear. Two further Medium findings (below) compound this: a quotation misattributed to a letter's own text when it is actually a modern editor's endnote, and a silent, undisclosed departure from a prior cleared document's own explicit tier-leaning handoff.

## HIGH SEVERITY

### H1. donstory001's central commemoration date — "the fourth of March" — is wrong, and contradicts this document's own cited sources

**Claim checked:** `Story-Chunks/donstory001_passio-donati-sermon.md`, Story Text, opening and closing lines: "Every year, on the fourth of March, this community gathers to remember..." / "This is what is read aloud, every fourth of March, to a congregation..."

**What I found:** The sermon's own heading in the primary source reads "**4. Idus Martii**" (`cic/texts/monumenta-vetera-donatistarum_migne-pl8.txt`, line 228: "4. Idus Martii Sermo de Passione SS. Donati et Advocaii"), and Mabillon's own admonitio states the same thing in running prose at line 123: "Sermo est habitus 4 Idus Martii in solemni et anniversaria commemoratione..." Under the ordinary Roman *ante diem* calendar convention, "the fourth day before the Ides of March" (the Ides of March being the 15th, counted inclusively) is **12 March**, not 4 March. This is not a marginal reading: `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §4 (a prior, Approved-to-proceed document Doc_09 explicitly claims to draw from — Section 1) already renders this correctly: "Mabillon's own admonitio records that the sermon 'was delivered **on the fourth of the Ides of March**...'" And `Source_Registry.md` row 50 — the very row Doc_09 §5 cites for this story — states, quoting Monceaux directly: "Monceaux... dates the persecution **precisely to 12 March 317**." Both of this document's own antecedents agree on 12 March (or "the fourth of the Ides of March," its correct English gloss); nothing in the corpus supports "the fourth of March."

**Why this matters:** This is not a peripheral detail — it is the narrative frame the entire Story Text is built around ("Every year, on the fourth of March..."), and it is directly, specifically contradicted by two sources this document itself cites as authoritative for this exact story (Doc_02 §4 and Registry row 50). A participant told this date by a Representative would be told something wrong by eight days, on a claim easily checked against sources already in this world's own build. This also constitutes a Section 4/5 cross-reference failure: Doc_09 §5 certifies row 50 as accurately cited for this story without noticing the date given in the Story Text contradicts what that very row says.

**Fix:** Change "the fourth of March" to "the twelfth of March" (or retain the Latin idiom, "the fourth of the Ides of March," as Doc_02 §4 does) in both instances in the Story Text. Re-check whether "the fourth of March" appears anywhere else in this document set as a result of this error propagating (it does not currently appear in Doc_09's master document text, only in the chunk).

### H2. donstory002 misattributes an argument: the martyrdom-vs-self-destruction dispute is Augustine's own, not "Optatus's own text, which Augustine quotes"

**Claim checked:** `Story-Chunks/donstory002_passio-marculi.md`, Source field: "Corroborated for the bare fact of Marculus's death (though not for the visionary or miraculous details) by **Optatus's own text, which Augustine quotes** disputing only whether the deaths of Marculus and Donatus of Bagai should be called 'martyrdom' rather than self-destruction." Repeated in the Formation Ecology Connection section: "Optatus (cited via Augustine, per this chunk's Source field) concedes Marculus was killed by Macarian soldiers but disputes calling it martyrdom at all." Also repeated in Doc_09's own master document, Section 4 (No-Tier-5 Audit), donstory002 row: "corroborated for the bare fact of Marculus's death... by Optatus's own text, which Augustine quotes."

**What I found:** The self-destruction-vs-execution dispute ("whether they threw themselves down a precipice... or whether they were thrown down by the true command of some authority") is Augustine's own argument, made in his own voice, in a **different work** — *Answer to the Letters of Petilian*, Book II, §§45–46 (`cic/texts/npnf104_augustine-anti-manichaean-anti-donatist.xml`, lines 16208–16213). Augustine is not quoting Optatus there; he is answering Petilian directly. Optatus's own text (`cic/texts/optatus_against-the-donatists.txt`, lines 2064–2078, "Against the alleged Donatist martyrs") does mention Marculus and Donatus's deaths, but makes a **different argument entirely**: that their deaths were deserved punishment for schism, on the model of Phineas, Moses, and Elijah slaying the wicked at God's command (lines 2080–2096) — not a claim about whether the manner of death was self-inflicted or externally imposed. The only textual link between the two passages is a modern translator's editorial footnote in the Vassall-Phillips Optatus edition (line 2668), which quotes Augustine's *Contra Litteras Petiliani* II.20 passage and comments, "This is precisely the statement and argument of St. Optatus in this Book" — an editor's own comparison across two different works by two different authors, not a citation chain in which "Augustine quotes Optatus." The comparison itself is arguably overstated (the two arguments are substantively different, as shown above), and Doc_09 treats it as an established fact rather than checking the underlying texts.

**Why this matters:** This is used as evidentiary support for the Formation Ecology Connection's own claim of "a documented, named tension with the Catholic side's own account" — a real and defensible finding on other grounds (Augustine's actual argument does dispute the martyrdom label), but sourced here to the wrong text and the wrong citation relationship. This is exactly the kind of citation the review brief asks not to take on faith: a claim resting on a translator's footnote, misread as a primary-source quotation chain.

**Fix:** Correct the Source field and Formation Ecology Connection to attribute the self-destruction dispute to Augustine's own *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* II.45–46 directly, not to "Optatus's own text, which Augustine quotes." If Optatus's own (different) argument is still worth citing, cite it separately and accurately (Optatus, *Against the Donatists* III.6, arguing the deaths were deserved). Propagate the fix to Doc_09 §4's own donstory002 row, which repeats the same error.

### H3. Doc_09 §7's conclusion that no primary attestation of Tyconius's condemnation survives is factually wrong — the primary source is vendored and directly locatable

**Claim checked:** Doc_09 §7 ("Tyconius's Condemnation: A Boundary Case"): "This does not fit Tier 1: no direct textual attestation of the condemnation itself survives within or close to this world's own horizon — what survives is Monceaux's 1920 secondary literary-historical synthesis, fifteen centuries removed, not a primary account."

**What I found:** Doc_09 §7 itself quotes Monceaux's chapter for the "*le chef d'une petite Église schismatique, où peut-être il était le seul fidèle*" line (verified verbatim at `cic/texts/monceaux_histoire-litteraire-afrique-chretienne-tome5_1920.txt`, line 8531–8532), but stops short of reading that same passage's own footnote three lines earlier. Monceaux's footnote 5 (line 8568) cites, and directly quotes in Latin, Augustine's own *Contra Epistulam Parmeniani* I.1: "*Parmenianus quidem primo eum per epistulam velut corrigendum putavit; postea vero etiam concilio eorum perhibent esse damnatum*" ("Parmenian at first thought he should be corrected by letter; but afterward, it is reported, he was even condemned by their own council"). This is not a citation to an unavailable work: *Contra Epistulam Parmeniani* is fully vendored, in a Latin critical edition, at `cic/texts/augustini_scripta-contra-donatistas-pars-i-iii_petschenig1908-1910.txt` (`Source_Registry.md` row 39, Confidence A, Native). This review located the identical sentence directly in that vendored file at line 2186. Augustine wrote this c. 400 CE, roughly two decades after the condemnation it reports (Doc_02 §4 dates it *c.* 380) — a near-contemporary primary attestation, squarely within this world's own 311–439 construction-window horizon, not "fifteen centuries removed." It carries an appropriate hedge of its own ("*perhibent*," "it is reported") that a story chunk could and should carry forward, the same way donstory007 carries forward the Council of Cirta's own disputed date rather than pretending to false precision.

Crucially, this gap was not undiscoverable: `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §9, Open Item 11 — a document Doc_09 §1 explicitly names as a source it draws from — states plainly: "Monceaux's own footnoted citations... Augustine's Epistle 93.10.43–45 and *Contra epistulam Parmeniani* I.1, offered as his biographical sources for Tyconius, have not been independently checked against the vendored corpus this session; both works are now vendored (row 39 above for the latter) and **could be checked directly in a future pass**." This is a standing, explicit invitation to exactly the check this review performed, and Doc_09 §7 does not show evidence of having taken it.

**Why this matters:** This goes to the heart of item 6 of this review's brief — is the reasoning for not building a story sound, or does it look like an excuse to skip real work? On direct inspection, the latter: a Tier 1 story (hedged for the "*perhibent*" reporting-verb qualification, the way donstory007 is hedged for its disputed date) is buildable from material already inside this world's own vendored corpus, without new source acquisition, and Doc_02 itself flagged exactly where to look. Section 7's own honest-silence framing ("the story does not exist for this world... because no primary narrative of the condemnation itself survives to tell") is not true on the evidence this document had within reach.

**Fix:** Re-open Section 6/7. Read `Contra Epistulam Parmeniani` I.1 directly (row 39; the passage is at line ~2088–2200 of the vendored file) and Monceaux's surrounding narrative (lines 8520–8642) in full, and reassess whether a ninth story chunk — Tyconius's condemnation, Tier 1 with an explicit "*perhibent*"-based confidence caveat, or Tier 3 if the fuller narrative detail is judged too thin for Tier 1 — should be built. At minimum, Section 7's own "no direct textual attestation... survives" claim must be corrected or withdrawn regardless of whether a full chunk is ultimately built.

### M1. Absent Stories item 3 attributes a quoted phrase to Letter LXVI's own text when it is actually a modern editor's endnote

**Claim checked:** Doc_09 §8, item 3: "`npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml`, Letter LXVI to Crispinus (lines 32092 onward)... alleges the forced rebaptism, 'notwithstanding their groaning and protesting,' of tenant peasants on a newly-acquired estate under Bishop Crispinus of Calama."

**What I found:** Letter LXVI's own body text (lines 32101–32106) says only: "in your work of rebaptizing the Mappalians..." The phrase "notwithstanding their groaning and protesting against this tyrannical act of their new landlord" is not Augustine's own wording — it is the NPNF **translator's endnote** (n. 1877, at line 32103–32106) glossing who "the Mappalians" were: "About eighty persons, on a property which he had acquired, were compelled by Crispinus to undergo submersion, notwithstanding their groaning and protesting..." Doc_09 presents this as something the letter itself "alleges," when it is a modern editor's paraphrase-with-detail, not Augustine's primary text.

**Why this matters:** Doc_09 is elsewhere careful to distinguish primary text from editorial apparatus — donstory001's Tier Justification, for instance, is explicit that "Mabillon's own admonitio" is external commentary, not the sermon's own words, and donstory004's Formation Ecology Connection is careful to mark Optatus's own hostile framing as such. This same discipline lapses here, in the one place a specific quoted phrase is offered in quotation marks as if it were the primary allegation.

**Fix:** Reword to attribute the quoted phrase to "the NPNF editor's own endnote on Letter LXVI" rather than to the letter itself, or drop the quotation marks and paraphrase the substance (which the endnote's content does support) without implying it is Augustine's own wording.

## LOW SEVERITY

### L1. donstory002's "all ten [bishops]" overstates the Latin's more equivocal phrasing

**What I found:** The Passio Marculi (`monumenta-vetera-donatistarum_migne-pl8.txt`, lines 967–992) states ten bishops were sent "*aut* [to] recall him from so great a crime with salutary warnings, *aut certe (quod contigit)* [or indeed, as it happened] themselves first leaped into the field of that devoted contest" — an either/or construction resolved narratively in favor of the second option, but not stated as literally all ten having joined. donstory002's "The tradition tells that all ten, instead, joined the resistance they had been sent to break" is a reasonable narrative gloss on "*quod contigit*" but slightly overstates the text's own precision.

**Fix:** Optional — soften to "the tradition tells that they, instead, joined the resistance" without the numerically precise "all ten," or note the either/or construction explicitly.

### L2. Ingentius's "confession under threat of torture" slightly elides that he stated he confessed before torture was applied

**What I found:** `optatus_against-the-donatists.txt` line 5713 records Ingentius's own words: "I have already confessed without torture." The apparatus (line 5921) confirms he was bound and prepared for torture (*suspendatur*) but was lowered and not actually tortured once his decurion status was established. donstory006's "Under questioning, and under the threat of torture, Ingentius broke" is defensible (he was genuinely under threat, bound and prepared) but does not surface Ingentius's own explicit claim to have confessed before, not because of, that threat.

**Fix:** Optional — no correction required; the story's own framing is not inaccurate, only slightly less precise than the fuller record allows.

### L3. The "no Tier 2 story exists" claim (§3.1) is reasonably argued but was not exhaustively tested against every candidate collected-material type

**What I found:** Doc_09 §3.1's argument — that this world's transmission asymmetry (no surviving ordinary-believer voice) precludes a Tier 2 collected-tradition story the same way it precludes a Tier 1/2 ordinary-believer story — is coherent and consistent with Doc_02 §6 and Doc_05 §10's own prior findings. This is noted as a limit on this review's own verification, not as a defect in Doc_09: the claim held up under the checking this review had time to do, but was not independently tested against every conceivable Tier 2 candidate type (e.g., conciliar-canon collections, considered and rejected implicitly rather than explicitly).

**Fix:** None required. Optional: a single sentence in §3.1 naming conciliar-canon collections explicitly and stating why they do not qualify (institutional record, not community-tradition transmission) would close this off explicitly rather than by implication.
