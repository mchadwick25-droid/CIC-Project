### H1. B5 attributes a quotation to the Step 0 Methodology that does not appear anywhere in it — the phrase is the IJC document's own prose

**Draft text (§3, B5):** *"Per the Methodology, this is "a modest, non-decisive positive factor," not a filter, but it is worth recording precisely because B2 above leans on this same fact..."*

**Why it's wrong:** the string "non-decisive" does not occur anywhere in `CiC_L3B_Step0_Movement_Scope_Methodology_V1.0.docx` (verified by extraction and grep of the full document text). The Methodology's actual B5 language is the heading *"B5. Scale (a modest tiebreak, not a filter)"* and the body *"where two candidates are otherwise comparable, the larger/more prominent movement gets modest priority as a tiebreak only; a small, thinly-known movement with genuinely strong evidence still screens in."*

The phrase the draft puts inside quotation marks and attributes to the Methodology is instead the *IJC build thread's own sentence*, from `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3 B5: *"This is a modest, non-decisive positive factor per the Methodology's own instruction that Scale is 'a tiebreak note only,' not a filter."* IJC correctly presented "a modest, non-decisive positive factor" as its own characterisation and reserved quotation marks for the Methodology's actual words ("a tiebreak note only"). This is precisely the failure mode the task of adversarial review exists to catch, and it compounds two standing project rules: the Step 0 Conclusion's own **Provenance-accuracy discipline** (*"A finding may not be described as 'confirmed against' a source... unless that is actually verifiable in the named source"*), and `cic-build-cycle`'s Draft instruction not to *"reach for content, sourcing, or characterizations that belong to a different formation world, even one that seems similar."*

**Fix:** delete the false quotation. Either quote the Methodology's actual words — Scale is "a modest tiebreak, not a filter," giving "modest priority as a tiebreak only" — or state the gloss unquoted as this document's own characterisation. Do not carry IJC's sentence across at all.

### H2. The Primary-gravity-first rule's origin is materially misstated (#8/#9 → #4/#8), and the error is load-bearing in three places

**Draft text (§1):** *"...was adopted* because *an earlier pass on the **World #4/#8 split** had used neighbor-protection (shielding Donatism's distinctiveness) as an unstated tiebreaker rather than reasoning to Cyprian's own primary characterization first. This world's own boundary with World #8 is therefore not a fresh question; it is **the specific case that produced the rule**..."*

**Why it's wrong:** the Step 0 Conclusion says: *"Adopted after review found the **world #8/#9 split** had used neighbor-protection (shielding Donatism's distinctiveness) as an unstated tiebreaker rather than reasoning to Cyprian's own primary characterization first."* The same document's Decisions list confirms it: item 3 is *"World #8/#9 split executed: Jerome removed and given his own world, built around the women's ascetic-patronage network. Cyprian retained with Augustine"*; item 4 is *"Primary-gravity-first rule adopted, directly to prevent the neighbor-protection reasoning found in decision 3 from recurring uncorrected."*

The split that produced the rule is **#8 vs. #9 (Cyprian+Augustine vs. Jerome)**. Donatism appears in that record only as the *thing being shielded* — a third party whose distinctiveness was improperly protected — never as one of the two worlds in the split. The draft has converted Donatism from the object of the error into a party to it, and then made "this is the specific case that produced the rule" the grounding for treating the #4/#8 boundary as already settled and not requiring fresh work. It is not that case.

The error propagates: §3 B3 (*"this is the pairing that produced the Primary-gravity-first rule (§1 above)"*) and §4 item 4 (*"this is also the pairing that produced the Primary-gravity-first rule and should be cited as such"*) both repeat it, and §4 item 4 instructs Doc_01 to cite it that way — so an uncorrected version propagates into the next document.

**Compounding:** the draft also drops the source sentence's own closing qualifier — *"— though partly mitigated there, since Cyprian's ecclesiology and pastoral crisis-management are historically fused rather than cleanly separable."* That qualifier is directly adverse to the draft's confident §3 B3 claim that #4 and #8 sit on cleanly different axes, and its omission is not marked.

**Fix:** correct all three occurrences to the #8/#9 split; restate Donatism's actual role (the neighbour whose distinctiveness was being shielded, which is what makes the rule relevant here, and is a *sufficient* reason to cite it without the false claim of authorship); and carry the "historically fused rather than cleanly separable" qualifier into §3 B3, where it bears directly.

### M1. "Criterion 2" (the person-defined-movement test) is never applied or mentioned, though this is the first eponymous movement to reach a per-world Step 0

Two aggravating points. First, the Step 0 Conclusion explicitly links Donatism to the Criterion-2 exclusions: *"Novatianism, excluded on the same new ground. **Real redundancy risk against Donatism if both were ever built**, independent of the criterion question."* §3 B3 does not mention this. Second, §5's affirmative claim that nothing beyond IJC's findings arose forecloses the question (see M2).

### M6. "Persecuted into near-extinction" is historically wrong

**Draft text (§3, B4):** *"a rigorist movement persecuted into near-extinction by an empire-backed rival raises exactly the kind of hard question about coercion, orthodoxy, and who gets to decide..."* Cf. §3 B1: *"the side that eventually suppressed it."*

Donatism was suppressed after 411–412; it was not brought near extinction. It survived the Edict of Unity and the post-Conference proscriptions, outlasted imperial enforcement when the Vandals took Africa (429–439) and directed their persecution at Catholics rather than Donatists, persisted through the Byzantine reconquest, and is still a live problem for Gregory the Great, whose Register complains repeatedly of Donatist activity in Numidia in the 590s. Frend's *The Donatist Church* devotes its closing chapters to exactly this survival, down toward the Arab conquest. The movement's disappearance is bound up with the general collapse of African Latin Christianity, not with the Catholic party's fifth-century victory.

This also sits badly beside B5's own claim of large-scale, majority-church status, and beside the world's stated "c. 312–430s" terminus (see M7).

**Fix:** replace with what the sources support — legally proscribed and progressively suppressed after 411, its clergy exiled and property transferred, while continuing as an attested body for two further centuries.

### M7. The world's stated end date is a phase-scope artifact, and the draft nowhere discloses that

Constitution Article 22 (Forces Principle) requires that a world's ending be *"recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur."* Passing an undisclosed truncation forward means Doc_01 and Doc_08 inherit a false ending rather than a disclosed boundary.

**Fix:** add one sentence to §1 or §4 recording that the 430s terminus is this phase's scope ceiling and not the movement's historical end, so Doc_01's boundary work and Doc_08's ending-forces cell start from an accurate statement.

### M8. A5 assigns the Caecilianist opposition to World #8's territory — an addition A5 does not make, and one that cuts against the Step 0 Conclusion's own boundary

**Draft text (§2, A5):** *"Donatism's own defining opponent — Caecilian's Carthaginian communion, and above all Optatus and Augustine... is not a separately screened candidate; it is **(part of) the not-yet-built World #8's own territory**, and Doc_02 must reconstruct that opposition from* inside *Donatism's own perspective..."*

### M9. B2 asserts an eight-lens pass while addressing two lenses, and omits the world's strongest non-textual evidence

The Methodology's B2 test is whether a candidate *"can plausibly support most or all eight lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only."* The draft's B2 discusses Worship/memory ecology and Formation ecology, names no others, and concludes **"Passes B2."** The other six lenses are neither named nor assessed.

The omission is not merely formal. North African Donatism has an unusually strong **material, epigraphic and archaeological** record — basilica archaeology across Numidia, the *Deo laudes* acclamation set against Catholic *Deo gratias* in inscriptions, and a body of Donatist-side texts the draft never inventories (the *Passio Marculi*, the *Passio Isaac et Maximiani*, the *Liber genealogus*, Donatist sermons). That is exactly the evidence that answers B2's question about lenses beyond doctrinal argument, and exactly the evidence least mediated by Optatus and Augustine — so it also bears on B1's Author Gravity finding.

**Fix:** at minimum name all eight lenses with a one-line disposition each; add the material/epigraphic record and the Donatist-side hagiographical corpus.

### M10. Tyconius's standing within the Donatist communion is stated as fact in the same sentence that calls it contested

**Draft text (§3, B1):** *"His own relationship to Donatist separatism is itself contested in the surviving record (**he stayed formally within the communion** while arguing against its exclusivist ecclesiology)."* Repeated in §2 A5: *"Tyconius's own more moderate, non-separatist exegetical strand, **which stayed formally Donatist** while arguing against the sect's ecclesial exclusivism."*

The standard account is that Parmenian and a Donatist council condemned Tyconius (c. 380) over precisely his universalist ecclesiology, and that he nonetheless never joined the Catholic church. His *formal standing inside the communion* is therefore the very thing in dispute — the draft names the contest and then resolves it in a parenthesis, in the direction least supported.

**Fix:** state the condemnation by Parmenian's council and the non-conversion, and leave his formal standing open, which is also the honest input to Doc_01's Strand Determination the draft flags.

### L4. Circumcellions: §4 states more strongly than §2, and both overstate the sourcing claim

§2 A5 says the category is *"partly a rhetorical construction"*; §4 item 2 hardens this to *"The category is substantially a hostile construction in the surviving sources."* Shaw's *Sacred Violence* is correctly attributed, but the position is that the *characterisation* is polemically shaped, not that the group is largely invented. And *"known to us almost entirely through hostile Catholic polemic (Augustine and Optatus)"* overlooks that *circumcelliones* are named independently in imperial legislation (CTh 16.5.52, 412), and that Augustine reports the Donatists' own term for them, *agonistici* — an outsiders' name for a real body with its own self-designation, which is a sharper and more useful finding for Doc_02 than "hostile construction." **Fix:** align §4 to §2's weaker phrasing and add the legislative attestation and *agonistici*.

### L5. Carthage 419 described as legislating what it largely compiles

The 419 register (the *Code of Canons of the African Church*) collects canons of the African councils from the 390s onward; the Donatist-reception canons in it mostly originate in the councils of c. 397–407. **Fix:** "the canons on reception of Donatist clergy collected in the 419 African register." Relatedly, the Council of Cirta's 305 date is genuinely disputed in the scholarship (307 and later dates have been argued); the draft states it flat, following the corpus map — acceptable, but worth a hedge.

### L6. "An object of juridical action, not a wielder of it" is one-sided

**Draft (§3, B3):** *"Donatism experiences imperial power as an external force pressed against it, from a position of eventual suppression rather than institutional ascendancy — an object of juridical action, not a wielder of it."*

The axis is broadly right but the absolute is not. The Donatists **initiated** the appeal to Constantine in 313 — Anulinus's *relatio*, which the draft lists in B1, is the document forwarding their own petition asking the emperor to appoint Gallic judges — and under Julian in 361 they petitioned successfully for and obtained the restoration of their basilicas by imperial rescript. Donatus's *"Quid est imperatori cum ecclesia?"* is a retort from within a movement that had itself opened the imperial door. **Fix:** state the asymmetry as predominant rather than absolute, naming 313 and 361 as the counter-cases — which strengthens rather than weakens the boundary claim.
