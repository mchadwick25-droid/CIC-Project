## MEDIUM

### M1. The H1 fix reaches the right conclusion by attaching one quotation to the wrong man — and Registry row 4 cites, as its warrant for the identification, two endnotes that do not make it, while the endnote that does sits unquoted in the same volume, in a row this Registry already holds at Confidence A.

**Doc_02 §1, new this revision:**

> "*Answer to the Letters of Petilian* describes the Donatist bishop of Thamugadi, Optatus Gildonianus — 'one of your colleagues,' Gildo's ally, **'whom you hold to be a priest'** — 'advancing with a military force' and 'sucking back Felicianus and Prætextatus once again within their pale' (Registry row 4)."

Two of the three quoted attributes are exact and correctly referred. "One of your colleagues" is *C. litt. Pet.* II.84, and the endnote on that very phrase reads "Optatus of Thaumugade (Thamogade), the friend of Gildo" — so both "colleague" and "Gildo's ally" are right. "Advancing with a military force" and "sucking back Felicianus and Prætextatus once again within their pale" are exact at II.23.

**"Whom you hold to be a priest" is not about Optatus. It is about Felicianus of Musti.** The full passage, *C. litt. Pet.* II.52.120:

> "In accordance with this true and catholic judgment, you too are free from pollution by the sacrifice of Optatus, if you disapproved of his deeds… But the evil involved in the schism of all your party makes this bread of mourners common to you all. **For, according to the judgment of your Council, Felicianus of Musti was a shedder of man's blood. For you said, in condemning them,** [endnote: *In the Council of Bagai*] **'Their feet are swift to shed blood.' See therefore what kind of sacrifice he offers whom you hold to be a priest, when you have yourselves convicted him of sacrilege.**"

The referent is fixed by the final clause, not by proximity. The Donatists **did** convict Felicianus — at Bagai, in the sacrilege language Doc_02 §1 itself quotes from *On Baptism* I.5.7 ("those whom they had **sacrilegiously**, to use the language of their own Council, baptized in schism") — and then held him a bishop again. They **did not** convict Optatus: three passages in the same work say so outright, including "what answer will they make about **Optatus, whom they were afraid to condemn** though they had long known his wickedness?" (II.9) and their own reported evasions, "'What did he do?' 'Who accused him?' **'Who convicted him?'**" (II.35). A reader who follows Doc_02's citation to check that Augustine calls Optatus a priest finds Augustine calling *Felicianus* a priest, in a sentence whose whole force depends on his having been condemned — which Optatus was not. This is the same class of defect as Round 2's H2(b): the source, opened, says something other than what the document reports of it.

**Registry row 4 compounds it by naming the wrong warrant.** Its Licensed-For now reads that Optatus Gildonianus is "identified as a Donatist colleague, not an imperial official, **by the same volume's own endnotes** ('Optatus Gildonianus is the person to whom he refers'; 'Optatus of Thaumugade (Thamogade), the friend of Gildo')." Both quotations are exact — but neither says "Donatist," neither says "bishop," and the first says nothing at all beyond fixing a pronoun. The endnote that makes the identification outright is a third one, in the same volume, attached to *On Baptism* **II.11.16**, and it is not quoted anywhere in either document:

> "**Optatus, a Donatist bishop of Thamogade in Numidia, was called Gildonianus from his adherence to Gildo, Count of Africa**, and generalissimo of the province under the elder Theodosius… He is not to be confounded with Optatus, Bishop of Milevis, the strenuous opponent of the Donatists."

That endnote hangs on Augustine's own sentence — "Thinking that they could annihilate the followers of Maximianus, **they pressed heavily on them through the agency of Optatus the Gildonian**" — which is itself a cleaner statement of the whole claim §1 is making than either passage the document currently uses, and it is inside **Registry row 3**, already at Confidence A, already quoted twice in the same paragraph. A fourth confirmation sits in `npnf101` Letter LXXXVII §5 ("your most famous and most scandalously notorious **Bishop of Thamugada**," endnote: "Optatus."), inside row 6, a letter this build has read in full five times.

**Why medium and not low.** It is a citation-warrant failure at two sites — the field prior rounds have graded medium every time (R1 M1, R2 M9, R2 M10, R4 M3, R4 H1(c)) — and it sits in the sentence written specifically to repair Round 4's only high finding, in the paragraph that discharges Doc_01 §4's binding.

**Why it does not make this revision substantial.** The claim is true, and the vendored corpus establishes it four separate ways. Nothing downstream reads differently once the correction lands.

**Fix — two edits, and both make the paragraph stronger.**
1. In Doc_02 §1, drop "whom you hold to be a priest" and put in its place what the volume actually says of him: *"the Donatist bishop of Thamugadi, Optatus Gildonianus — 'one of your colleagues,' Gildo's ally, through whose 'agency' the Primianist party 'pressed heavily on' the Maximianists (*On Baptism* II.11.16, row 3) — 'advancing with a military force' and 'sucking back Felicianus and Prætextatus once again within their pale' (row 4)."* If the priest/bishop point is wanted, take it from II.9's "**Why are those who were baptized by him** not said to have been baptized by one dead?", which is about Optatus and is unambiguous.
2. In row 4, replace the two endnote quotations with the one that carries the identification, and note that it sits at *On Baptism* II.11.16 (row 3) rather than in this work — or cite Letter LXXXVII §5 (row 6), which is inside a locus this Registry has already verified directly. **Do not paste my wording; open II.52.120 and II.11.16 and write what they say.**
