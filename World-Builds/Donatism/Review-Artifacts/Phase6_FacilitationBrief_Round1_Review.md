## Full findings

### Finding 1 (Medium) — B3's women's-voice claim undercounts this world's own established record

**Location:** B3 — "It is thin on women's own voice: only one individual woman carries any real presence in this world's own record — Lucilla, at the schism's very origin — and her portrayal survives only through a hostile narrator's account of her grievance, not her own words."

**What's wrong:** This world's own build record — `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6 and, more directly, `don_Representative_Identity_Options.md` §5 — establishes that **two** women carry individual narrative standing in the vendored corpus, not one. `don_Representative_Identity_Options.md` §5 states this explicitly: "only Lucilla (row 30) and **one unnamed woman behind the Maximianist affair** carry any individual standing in this world's own record, both single narrative moments rather than an office." `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6 independently corroborates this: Augustine's Letter XLIII §26 asks by "what woman's instigation Maximianus... withdrew himself from the communion of Primianus... precisely as Majorinus, under the influence of Lucilla, assembled a faction of bishops" — drawing a direct parallel between Lucilla and a second, real, instigating woman, whom the source leaves unnamed. Doc_02 §6 calls this asymmetry itself "the finding this bullet reports: a hostile source names Lucilla in the very act of comparing her to a second woman whose own name the source does not give" — i.e., the record attests **two individual women**, one named and one unnamed, not one.

B3's claim that "only one individual woman carries any real presence" conflates "named" with "has any presence at all," silently dropping the second, unnamed woman's attested role. This is the specific failure mode the assignment brief asked this review to watch for by name (incomplete disclosure — a second, relevant finding existing in the record and not being named alongside the first).

**Internal inconsistency, aggravating the finding:** the brief's own B7 states the equivalent claim correctly and more precisely: "This world's own record has no individually named female figure beyond Lucilla" — which is accurate, because it hedges on "named," exactly where B3 does not. B3 and B7 should be saying the same thing about the same evidentiary fact and currently say two different, inconsistent things.

**What I independently verified as correct:** the record attests two women with individual narrative standing — Lucilla (named, at the schism's founding) and a second, unnamed woman (attested only via Augustine's parallel reference, behind the Maximianist schism) — of whom only Lucilla is *named*. B3 should either match B7's "no individually named female figure beyond Lucilla" framing, or explicitly disclose both attested figures the way Doc_02 §6 and the Identity Options document both do.

**Practical impact:** limited but real. This is a Formation Limitations section meant to tell a Facilitator honestly where the world is thin; overstating the thinness here (by erasing a second attested figure) is a minor factual inaccuracy about the evidentiary record, not a safety-relevant error — B7, which is the safety/sensitivity-relevant section on this same topic, gets it right.

### Finding 2 (Low) — B1's "Rome's favor for a hundred years running" is unsourced and risks an inverted chronology

**Location:** B1 — "It held this line even when it cost the communion Rome's favor for a hundred years running..."

**What's wrong:** Neither of the two sources B1 cites at this point (`don_Rep_Phase2_Formation_Calibration.md` §1–§2; `Doc_04_Gravity_Discovery.md` §3.1, §3.7) states or supports a "hundred years" figure or frames the relationship with Rome as something "cost" over time. More substantively, the phrase risks implying a chronology in which the Donatist communion once held Rome's favor and then lost it by holding its line — but the actual sequence, per `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2 and `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §1, is that Rome ruled against the movement almost immediately (Council of Rome, 313, roughly one to two years after the 311/312 schism), so there was no extended period of "Rome's favor" the purity doctrine subsequently cost the communion. The 313-to-411/417 span (roughly a century) is a defensible approximation for how long the communion operated without Rome's recognition, but the sentence as written is more naturally read as describing a loss of previously-held favor.

**What I independently verified:** the schism-to-411-Conference (or Letter 185, c. 417) span is approximately 98–105 years, which the "hundred years running" figure reasonably approximates — but the underlying relationship was one of continuous non-recognition from very near the schism's own start, not a favor subsequently forfeited.

**Recommendation:** reword to something like "it held this line without Rome's recognition for roughly a century" rather than language implying a favor once held and then spent.
