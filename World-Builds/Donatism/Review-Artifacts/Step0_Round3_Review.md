## Part 3 — The escalation judgment, made rather than cited

The review was asked to judge, not merely check, whether §6's reasoning — that naming a pre-existing logged question is disclosure rather than a fresh category-4 trigger — is a legitimate application of `cic-build-cycle`'s four escalation categories or is itself a dodge.

**It is legitimate.** Four reasons, in order of weight.

1. **It matches the category's actual text.** Category 4 is *"Unresolved tensions the pipeline can't close on its own — two reviews disagreeing with each other, a contradiction between two already-cleared master documents, or a finding that cuts against an earlier decision."* None of the three obtains here. There is no review disagreement on this point. There is no contradiction between two cleared documents: the Article 3 ambiguity sits *inside* one governing document (the Constitution), and the only other document involved — the Step 0 Conclusion — does not contradict it but flags it. And naming the question does not cut against an earlier decision; the Step 0 Conclusion's Decisions item 5 *is* the decision, and it reads "Constitutional ambiguity on gapped/diachronic formation-types logged, not resolved." Naming the question is compliance with that decision, not a challenge to it.

2. **The document takes no position on the ambiguity, so there is nothing to escalate.** The dodge this project has actually been burned by — v1's H3 — was resolving a live question silently in the drafting document's own favour. v3 does the opposite: it quotes the question in full, states "This document does not resolve it," and gives the reason (the portfolio document that logged it declined to resolve it, and resolving it is not Step 0's job). A finding is escalation-triggering when the pipeline has to close it to proceed; this one does not have to be closed for this document to reach its result.

3. **The ambiguity does not bear on this world's own disposition.** Article 3's open question is whether a formation-type may recur across a temporal gap with a rival claimant contesting the interval — i.e. whether **World #8** (Cyprian + Augustine) coheres across its own gap. Donatism is the third party, the claimant contesting the interval. However that question resolves, World #4's Section A clearance, its B1–B5 dispositions and its Tier 1 placement are unaffected; if anything, a resolution against diachronic recurrence would make Donatism's distinctness clearer, not murkier. Escalating it from here would be re-escalating a portfolio-level question from a per-world document that does not depend on the answer.

4. **The same test has already been run and cleared on this project's record.** IJC's own §6 tested its Finding 2 against category 4 specifically and reached the same structure of answer, and IJC's Round 3 review states that it checked that reasoning "independently… against the actual `cic-build-cycle` escalation-category text, not just internal document logic," and cleared it. v3 applies the identical test to an analogous item. Consistency with an independently-cleared precedent is evidence of legitimacy rather than of rationalization. (That this precedent is used without being cited is R5 — a sourcing defect, not a reasoning one.)

**The one thing I would have the document add**, offered as an improvement rather than a finding: §4 item 4 binds Doc_01 to state the World #8 boundary, and the Article 3 question bears on exactly that boundary's shape. A one-clause cross-reference from item 4 to the standing question in B3 would carry the disclosure forward to the document that will actually have to work near it. As it stands the question is visible in §3 and §6 but not in the obligations list that Doc_01 will read.

I also checked the other three categories independently rather than accepting §6's answers. **Category 1** plainly does not apply. **Category 2:** re-verified that no forward-binding instruction to World #6 or World #8 survives anywhere in v3 (`git diff` and full-text scan); the only cross-world clauses are conditional and route to System Hub. **Category 3:** applying an adopted-but-uncodified portfolio-level test to a world that clears it comfortably, and reporting the codification gap to System Hub as a recommendation, is disclosure and not a rule change — and v3 no longer claims either the phase-split reading or Criterion 2 is "established," which was the v1 defect. §6's answers to all three hold.
