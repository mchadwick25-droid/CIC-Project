## 1. Primary Sources

This world's evidentiary situation is the inverse of a typical Author Gravity problem: the volume of surviving material is large, but it is concentrated almost entirely on one side of the schism it describes (Doc_01 §7, item 1). The following are Native per the Source Registry's Boundary Check; full classification detail (Type, Confidence, Licensed-For target) lives in `Source_Registry.md`.

**Optatus of Milevis, *Against the Donatists* (Books I–VII, c. 366–367, revised c. 385).** The earliest substantial narrative source on the schism, and the primary anti-Donatist source outside Augustine (`cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml`). Contemporary and primary in composition date, but hostile and external in perspective — Optatus describes the Donatists rather than speaking as one, "usable for Donatism only etically" (corpus map, verbatim). Transmits, in its own appendix, a body of contemporary documentary material of a different character: the *Acta Purgationis Felicis* (314), the *Gesta apud Zenophilum* (320), Constantine's own letters, the Council of Arles' 314 letter to Pope Silvester, the Acts of the Council of Cirta (305 — a date genuinely disputed in the scholarship, with 307 and later argued by some), and Anulinus's *relatio* (313). This appendix's *contents* are court acts and imperial correspondence; its *selection, assembly, and transmission* are still Optatus's own act, so it sits inside this world's Author Gravity concentration rather than outside it, even though it is a comparatively less rhetorically shaped layer than Optatus's own argumentative prose.

**Augustine of Hippo's anti-Donatist corpus.** By far the largest body of surviving evidence, and the dominant weight in this world's own Author Gravity concentration: *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* (7 books, c. 400); *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* (3 books) — quoting the Donatist bishop Petilian of Constantina's own letters clause by clause, and therefore the single most extensive surviving Donatist voice in the vendored corpus, though preserved entirely inside its own refutation; *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, c. 417, to the tribune Boniface, defending imperial coercion after the 411 Conference); and a cluster of eleven letters (XXIII, XLIII, XLIV, LIII, LXXVI, LXXXVII, LXXXVIII, LXXXIX, XCIII, CXXXIX, CLXXIII), split from the wider Augustine correspondence on Mark's 2026-08-26 ruling by the volume's own div3 markup (168 letters, cut at eight or more occurrences of Donatist vocabulary, plus Letter LXXVI by its title's own address to the Donatists as a body — `cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml`).

**The Cyprianic antecedent corpus.** Cyprian's *Epistles*, *On the Lapsed* (*De Lapsis*), *On the Unity of the Church* (*De Unitate*), and the acts of the Seventh Council of Carthage (256, on the baptism of heretics) are classified `role: antecedent` in the vendored corpus map, on Mark's 2026-08-26 ruling: Cyprian is neither a Donatist (he predates the schism by half a century) nor merely `context` describing it from outside — he is "the authority both sides argued FROM," a status measured rather than asserted (Augustine's *On Baptism* names Cyprian 306 times in roughly 92,000 words — corpus map, verbatim). **The 256 council is independently attested twice, from two different vendored volumes with two different classifications, and this document keeps the two distinct rather than treating them as one entry:** the Cyprianic corpus's own record of the council (`anf05`, `role: antecedent`, confidence: assigned) and a separate telling within the conciliar-history volume, *The Acts of the Council of Carthage under Cyprian* (`npnf214`, `role: tradition`, confidence: **provisional**). The provisional flag on the second is carried forward, not silently resolved, per this document's own confidence-sensitive test below.

**Conciliar and institutional record.** The Code of Canons of the African Church (Council of Carthage, 419), collecting canons on the reception of Donatist clergy and congregations that mostly originate in the African councils of c. 397–407, is vendored at `confidence: provisional` in the corpus map — carried forward here, not upgraded without independent verification. **Not currently vendored, and load-bearing for this world's own institutional self-understanding:** the *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis*, the acts of the imperially-convened 411 Conference of Carthage, at which Donatist bishops — Emeritus of Caesarea prominent among them — are recorded speaking at length on their own side of the exchange; Augustine's *Contra Cresconium* and *Contra epistulam Parmeniani*, this document's own sources for the Maximianist schism (§4 below); and the *Codex Theodosianus* Book 16, the imperial legal record of the movement's suppression, independent of either polemicist's own selection. All are flagged for Source Acquisition (§9 below and the manifest accompanying this document).

**Tyconius's *Liber Regularum* (Book of Rules).** A substantially complete, independently-authored hermeneutical treatise, later influential on Augustine's own *De doctrina christiana* III — not currently vendored. If acquired, this is the single largest available correction to this world's own Author Gravity concentration, since it is Donatist-affiliated material surviving in its own right rather than through hostile quotation. Doc_01 §4 found that Tyconius's own dissent does not rise to strand status (no attested following, parallel authority structure, or distinct communal practice); that finding does not depend on this acquisition and is not reopened here.

## 2. Author Gravity Assessment

Per Constitution Article 26 and Construction Framework Part II, every major voice is assessed across all five dimensions: Visibility, Representativeness, Influence, Limitations, and Transmission History.

**Optatus of Milevis.**
- *Visibility:* Over-visible for the schism's own documentary record — his appendix is this world's single richest concentration of contemporary legal and institutional material, and his own narrative frames how that material is read.
- *Representativeness:* Represents the Caecilianist/Catholic party's own case against the schism, not any Donatist perspective — he writes explicitly against "my brother Parmenian," the Donatist bishop of Carthage, a rhetorical fraternity that does not extend to representing Parmenian's own view.
- *Influence:* Foundational — Augustine's own later anti-Donatist corpus draws on and extends arguments Optatus first assembled, and Optatus's appendix is the earliest surviving collection of the schism's own founding documents.
- *Limitations:* A polemicist's own selection and framing govern which documents survive in the appendix and how they are read; the work's revision (c. 385, adding an eighth book responding to Parmenian's own reply) shows Optatus's own argument developing under pressure, not fixed from the outset.
- *Transmission History:* Preserved through the Caecilianist/Catholic side's own manuscript tradition, as the eventually-victorious party's literature; no adversarial-quotation problem of the kind affecting Petilian's letters below, since Optatus's own words are not filtered through a hostile source, but his selection of *what to include from the other side* is itself the transmission-shaping act this world's central evidentiary problem depends on.

**Augustine of Hippo.**
- *Visibility:* Overwhelmingly over-visible — the largest single body of evidence for this entire world, spanning three decades of his own episcopate (post-411 material comes disproportionately from the period after his own side's decisive institutional victory).
- *Representativeness:* Represents the Caecilianist/Catholic party at its most intellectually developed and most institutionally confident, writing after Rome, Arles, and (for his later works) the 411 Conference had already ruled against the Donatist party — not a contemporary, undecided voice.
- *Influence:* Enormous and lasting, both for how this schism is known at all and for the sacramental theology (objective validity of sacraments independent of the minister's worthiness) his engagement with Donatism substantially forged — a theological legacy that belongs to World #8's own future reconstruction (Doc_01 §6), not developed further here.
- *Limitations:* Every quotation of a Donatist voice in this corpus — Petilian's letters above all — survives only as much of it as Augustine chose to quote, in the order and selection he chose, for the purpose of refuting it clause by clause. This is not merely a general caution; it is the specific mechanism by which this world's own central evidentiary problem operates.
- *Transmission History:* Preserved through the Catholic institutional tradition's own manuscript transmission — the eventually-establishment side's literature, copied and circulated by the institutions that had every reason to preserve it. The letters cluster's own survival, split out of a 168-letter volume by markup rather than by a Donatism-specific manuscript tradition, is itself evidence of how thoroughly this world's own material is embedded inside a larger Catholic corpus rather than transmitted as its own body of literature.

**Petilian of Constantina.** Treated separately from the general Augustine entry because Petilian is, on the current vendored corpus, the fullest surviving *Donatist* voice — and because his own Author Gravity profile is therefore not Augustine's, even though his words reach us entirely through Augustine's own text.
- *Visibility:* Visible only to the extent Augustine's *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* chose to quote him, clause by clause, for refutation — a visibility entirely conditioned by his opponent's own selection.
- *Representativeness:* Represents a Donatist bishop's own articulate defense of the movement's rebaptism theology and ecclesiology, at a moment (his letters are generally dated after the Macarian repression, within living memory of Donatist martyrdom) when the movement's own grievance against the Caecilianist/imperial alliance was sharply felt — but one bishop's own voice, not a communal one, and not independently checkable against anything he might have said that Augustine chose not to quote.
- *Influence:* Substantial within this world's own reconstruction specifically, precisely because so little independent Donatist material survives — Petilian's quoted words carry disproportionate evidentiary weight for want of an alternative, a fact this document names rather than treats as a strength.
- *Limitations:* Everything about this entry is downstream of the same limitation: no direct manuscript of Petilian's own letters survives independent of Augustine's quotation. Where Augustine paraphrases rather than quotes, or characterizes rather than reproduces, this document treats the material as Augustine's own characterization, not Petilian's words, and Doc_02's own Registry entry marks this distinction row by row where it can be checked.
- *Transmission History:* The single sharpest instance of this world's central evidentiary problem: transmission and refutation are, for this figure, the same act. Petilian's words survive *because* Augustine needed them in front of a reader in order to refute them — a preservation mechanism that guarantees selection for refutability, not for completeness or fairness to Petilian's own case.

**Cyprian of Carthage (antecedent, not a Donatist voice).** Given a full entry despite predating the schism by half a century, because his material is load-bearing for this world's own A1/A2 reasoning (Doc_01, `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §2) and B3 boundary with World #8 (Doc_01 §6).
- *Visibility:* Very high within his own third-century context; his visibility *for this world specifically* is a function of how heavily both the Donatist and Caecilianist parties argued from him, not of anything Cyprian himself said about a schism he did not live to see.
- *Representativeness:* Represents the third-century North African church's own settled position on rebaptism (baptism outside the true church is no baptism) at a moment when that position was itself contested within the wider church (his dispute with Pope Stephen) but not yet a basis for schism within Africa itself.
- *Influence:* Decisive for this world's own founding logic — the Donatist position is a direct continuation and sharpening of Cyprian's own view, not a departure from it (Doc_01 §5).
- *Limitations:* Cyprian cannot be read as though he anticipated or endorsed the Donatist schism specifically; the corpus map's own antecedent classification exists precisely to prevent that anachronism, and this document does not use Cyprian to characterize any specific Donatist claim beyond what the schism's own participants (via Optatus's and Augustine's quotation of them) can be shown to have actually argued from him.
- *Transmission History:* Preserved through the same African, later broader Latin, manuscript tradition both the Donatist and Caecilianist parties drew on — a shared inheritance, not a source either side transmitted exclusively, which is itself the evidentiary basis for treating him as antecedent rather than as either side's own tradition (corpus map ruling, 2026-08-26).

## 4. Formation Narrative Sources

Per Construction Framework Part II, hagiographic and collected-narrative sources are assessed separately from theological treatises and institutional records, since they carry formation content — how the community understood itself — differently than argument does.

**The martyr and confessor Passiones.** Step 0's own disclosure (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §1) names martyr-cult and confessor memory as this world's own distinctive strength among the nine confirmed worlds — the community's self-understanding as the "Church of the Martyrs" is not peripheral color but this world's own central formation narrative. The *Passio Marculi* and the *Passio Isaac et Maximiani* — narratives of martyrs from the Macarian repression (347–348) — are the two most directly relevant texts, and are not yet inventoried in the vendored corpus map (a weaker, more provisional claim than "pending acquisition" — Step0 §3, B1). The *Acts of the Abitinian Martyrs* (from the earlier, 304 Diocletianic-persecution context) is this world's own account of what a faithfully formed life looks like under ultimate pressure, per the Framework's own Martyrology assessment category — but its Donatist-versus-Catholic transmission history is genuinely contested in the scholarship, and this document treats it as contested rather than as a clean witness to either side, consistent with the caution Step 0 already applied to it.

**Tyconius's own genre.** The *Liber Regularum* (§1 above) is a hermeneutical treatise, not narrative material, but its author's own contested standing (Doc_01 §4: condemned by Parmenian's council c. 380, never joining the Catholic church, formal status left open) makes it a formation-narrative-adjacent question in its own right — what a movement does with a dissenting intellectual figure it produces but does not fully embrace is itself formation evidence, flagged here for Doc_09's own Story Inventory work rather than resolved.

**No Tier 5 material identified.** Consistent with the Framework's prohibition, this document identifies no generated or illustrative narrative for this world; where the evidence is thin (ordinary lay experience of the schism, specifically), that thinness is named as a finding (§6 below), not filled.

## 6. Source Asymmetries and Missing Voices

Per Step 0 §4, item 1 (binding on this document): this world's Author Gravity concentration on the hostile (Optatus/Augustine) side is severe and unusually direct, and is named here as this world's central evidentiary problem, not a background caveat.

**A constitutional routing note, following the discipline `Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6 already applied to an analogous case:** Article 20's Affirmative Duty is scoped to voices marginalized *within* the community; it is explicitly distinct from the Writing-From-Inside Principle (Article 23), which governs how this world's own record represents its external opponents and combatants. The bullets below sort accordingly.

- **Elite/literate/male/urban skew (Article 20), with a genuine partial mitigation this world's own B5 finding already names (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B5):** this world's sourcing is not merely elite-skewed in the way several other confirmed worlds are — Donatism was, for substantial stretches and regions, the numerically dominant church, not a small elite current. The access problem is source-mediation (nearly everything currently vendored passes through hostile hands), not scale. This does not eliminate the skew; it changes its character — "ordinary believer" experience here is the bulk of the movement itself, currently unreachable in its own words, rather than a genuinely separate, thin population.
- **Gender (Article 20):** the vendored corpus preserves no documented female Donatist actor with anything approaching Justina's or Pulcheria's degree of individual attestation in World #6's own record (`Imperial-Juridical-Christianity/Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` §6, for comparison). This is named as an absence, not filled — no specific textual trace meeting the Framework's three bounding conditions for reconstruction (independent comparative evidence, material evidence, or a specific against-the-grain textual trace) has been identified this pass.
- **The Circumcellion/*agonistici* question (Article 20, internal to this world, per Doc_01 §2 A5 and Step0 §4 item 2).** Discharged in full here, not as a footnote. The characterization the vendored sources give this group — itinerant, rural, associated with episodes of violence — is substantially shaped by hostile Catholic polemic (Augustine, Optatus), and Shaw's *Sacred Violence* is the standard treatment of that shaping. But the group's *existence* is independently attested outside hostile polemic — imperial legislation names them directly (*Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.52, 412, not yet vendored) — and Augustine himself reports their own self-designation, *agonistici* ("contestants"), a term of the group's own rather than merely an outsiders' label. This document distinguishes the two claims throughout: the group's existence and self-designation are treated at the confidence the independent attestation supports; the group's *character* (their scale, their typical conduct, their relationship to the wider Donatist hierarchy) is treated as substantially Augustine's and Optatus's own framing, not independently checkable at present. The Maximianist suppression (Doc_01 §4) reports Circumcellion involvement through exactly this same channel — Augustine's own polemic — and is marked accordingly wherever it appears (§4 below; Doc_01 §5, Cell 2B).
- **The Caecilianist/Catholic party's own voice (Article 23, not Article 20):** per Doc_01 §2, A5, this world's defining opponent is reconstructed here from inside Donatism's own perspective — a persecuted "Church of the Martyrs" facing a state-favored rival — not cited as a neutral external source. Optatus and Augustine are used throughout as *this world's own opponents*, whose framing must be marked as such, consistent with the Writing-From-Inside Principle.

**Affirmative Duty (Article 20), applied to the genuinely internal-marginalization cases above:** the primary duty — naming structural absence — is discharged throughout this section. The secondary duty — bounded reconstruction — is not exercised in this document. No case meeting the Framework's three bounding conditions has been identified this pass for any specific ordinary believer's or marginalized voice's perspective beyond "structural absence, named." If later construction work (Doc_05, Doc_07) surfaces a specific textual trace meeting those conditions, it is added here under the Registry's own living-document discipline.

## 7. Required disclosure: the terminal record and this world's own construction-window boundary

Per Doc_01 §7, item 8 (binding on this document, alongside Doc_08): Donatism's own historical record does not end at this world's own 439 construction-window close. It continues, under different political conditions (Vandal, then Byzantine rule), for roughly two further centuries, falling silent after Gregory the Great's correspondence in the 590s, with no attestation of an organized Donatist body thereafter and no documented line to any present-day communion (Doc_01 §1). Gregory's own Register is not yet vendored; this document does not use it to support any specific claim about the terminal period, consistent with Doc_01's own corrected reasoning (Doc_01 §2) that the 439 boundary marks the removal of the Roman-imperial, Catholic-aligned adjudicating power this world's own refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy pattern is defined against — not the end of the underlying two-party contest, which the record shows continuing well past this world's own window.

## 8. Confidence Map

- **Documented / Widely Accepted:** the core sequence of dated events already established in Doc_01 (the 311/312 Caecilian election, the Council of Rome 313 and Arles 314, the Macarian repression 347–348, Julian's 361 restoration, the 405 Edict of Unity, the 411 Conference with its 284/286 bishop count, Augustine's Letter 185 c. 417, the Vandal invasion 429 and capture of Carthage 439); the existence and basic content of the major primary texts named in §1; Donatism's doctrinal orthodoxy on Trinity and Christology (Step0 §2, A1).
- **Dominant Modern Reconstruction:** the characterization of Homoian-style caricature ("Arianism") as inapplicable to this schism, which is disciplinary/ecclesiological rather than credal (Doc_01 §5); the reading of the Circumcellion category as substantially, though not wholly, a hostile rhetorical construction (§6 above).
- **Contested:** the exact date of the Council of Cirta (305 vs. 307 or later, §1 above); the authenticity and extent of the material/epigraphic record's specific attribution (§5 above, pending independent verification); Tyconius's own formal standing within the Donatist communion (Doc_01 §4, deliberately left open).
- **Inferential/Thin:** any claim about ordinary Donatist believers' own experience of the schism beyond the bulk-of-the-movement point named in §6; any claim about the Circumcellions'/*agonistici*'s own typical conduct beyond their independently-attested existence and self-designation; any claim about Petilian's own fuller argument beyond what Augustine's own selection preserves.
