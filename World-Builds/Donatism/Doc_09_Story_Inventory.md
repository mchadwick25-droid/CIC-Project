## Section 2 — Tier Categories, Re-Confirmed Against the Current Governing Text

Re-read directly from the Construction Framework V7.4 text (Part V) for this document, rather than assumed from a remembered list — the tier definitions have not changed since Doc_05 §11 last cited them, and no drift was found between the Framework's own text and the `Story_Repository_Chunk_Template.md` used to build the eight chunks below.

- **Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative.** Direct textual attestation within or close to the world's horizon; named author with identifiable social location; datable with reasonable confidence; a historically credible narrative claim, with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective and genre.
- **Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material.** Transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory; individual attribution may be uncertain, but the tradition itself is treated as authentic.
- **Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition.** Attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition rather than direct documentation; hagiographic narrative is the specific type named within this tier — the formation ideal it communicates is the evidence, not the specific events claimed.
- **Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** A composite reconstruction of typical practice, not about one named individual, every element traceable to attested evidence; reconstruction, not generation.
- **No Tier 5.** Generated or illustrative narrative — not traceable to this world's own evidence — is not permitted under any circumstance. If the evidence does not support a story, the story does not exist for this world.

## Section 7 — Tyconius's Condemnation: A Boundary Case

Doc_02 §4 and Doc_05 §6.5 both flag Tyconius's own condemnation by a Donatist council (*c.* 380) for dedicated treatment at this document, rather than developing it themselves. That flag is honored here with a considered judgment, not a story chunk: **this candidate does not currently support a story at any of the four permitted tiers**, and building one would risk manufacturing narrative texture the evidence does not actually supply.

What is known: a Donatist council condemned Tyconius; he never joined the Catholic side either; Monceaux's own dedicated chapter (`monceaux_histoire-litteraire-afrique-chretienne-tome5_1920.txt`, Source_Registry.md row 40) describes him afterward as becoming, in Monceaux's own words, "*le chef d'une petite Église schismatique, où peut-être il était le seul fidèle*" — the head of a small schismatic church, where perhaps he was its own only member.

This does not fit Tier 1: no direct textual attestation of the condemnation itself survives within or close to this world's own horizon — what survives is Monceaux's 1920 secondary literary-historical synthesis, fifteen centuries removed, not a primary account. It does not fit Tier 2 or Tier 3 either: both require the material to rest on this world's own collected tradition or community memory, however uncertain the specific attribution; here there is no ancient tradition being transmitted at all, only a modern historian's reconstruction from indirect evidence. It cannot be Tier 4, which requires a composite of multiple attested elements reconstructing typical practice — this is a claimed fact about one named individual, not a reconstruction of typical practice, and its own single element (the condemnation) is not independently attested beyond the one secondary source.

The honest conclusion is that this world can state, as a documented fact carried in its own build record (Doc_02 §4), that Tyconius was condemned and lived out his final years in an isolated remnant of a schism-within-a-schism — but it cannot yet tell this as a *story*, because no primary narrative of the condemnation itself survives to tell. This is named again, more fully, as an Absent Story in Section 8 below, rather than resolved by building a thin chunk to satisfy the letter of the flag while missing its point.

## Section 8 — Absent Stories

Answered with the specificity the governing skill requires, not left as a placeholder.

1. **No Tier 1 or Tier 2 story exists, or can exist, from the ordinary Numidian believer's own voice.** This is this world's central, already-established absence (Doc_05 §10, §11): the numerically dominant population across long stretches of this world's history is attested only through hostile or institutional intermediaries, never in its own words. This is not a gap this document can close by more searching — Doc_02's own transmission-asymmetry finding already establishes it as a structural feature of the surviving evidence, not an oversight of this build. Section 3.1's own Tier 2 finding (zero stories) is a second, independent symptom of this same underlying cause.

2. **The 411 Conference of Carthage cannot be told as a story.** This world's single largest and most consequential recorded event — 284 Donatist bishops meeting 286 Catholic bishops, with Emeritus of Caesarea speaking at length in the movement's own defense — has no story here, because the *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis* itself remains unvendored (Source_Registry.md row 14: "Confirmed unavailable in the public domain, recorded not requested"). Emeritus's own recorded words survive only as later scholarly reference, not as a text this world's own build has read directly. This is a different kind of absence than item 1: not a structural silence in the evidence itself, but a single, specific, nameable missing text blocking what would otherwise likely be this world's richest available Tier 1 courtroom-drama story — richer even than donstory006's Acta Purgationis Felicis, given the scale and the direct, extended presence of a named Donatist bishop's own voice. Should the *Gesta* become vendorable in a future acquisition round, this absence should be revisited directly rather than assumed permanent.

3. **No Donatist-side account of the Bagai-era Circumcellion violence against Catholics survives.** Hostile Catholic sources record specific, dated allegations: `npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml`, Letter LXVI to Crispinus (lines 32092 onward — **not currently a Source Registry row of its own; a registration gap disclosed here rather than silently cited as if a row existed**), alleges the forced rebaptism, "notwithstanding their groaning and protesting," of tenant peasants on a newly-acquired estate under Bishop Crispinus of Calama; Letter LXXXVIII from the clergy of Hippo (Source_Registry.md row 6) directly recounts, in its own body text, a converted presbyter dragged from his house, beaten, rolled in a miry pond, and held twelve days, with the Donatist bishop Proculeianus refusing to answer for it before the municipal courts; that same letter's own editorial endnote (not its body text) attributes a separate charge — that Crispinus attempted to kill the Catholic bishop Possidius — to Letter CV (itself likewise not a Registry row) and to Augustine's *Contra Cresconium* III.46–47 (Source_Registry.md row 17), which is registered and is the more citable primary attestation of that specific charge. Separately, *The Correction of the Donatists* (Letter 185, row 5) recounts the near-fatal attack on the Catholic bishop Maximianus of Bagai in 404. These are real, specific, named allegations from named hostile witnesses, not vague accusations — but this world has no surviving account of any of them in its own voice, and so cannot answer them, confirm them, contextualize them, or dispute their particulars from the inside. This document deliberately does not build a story chunk from the hostile accounts themselves: doing so would let this world's own Story Repository narrate an atrocity attributed to it entirely through its accuser's own telling, which is not the same evidentiary act as this document's other Tier 1 stories, several of which (donstory004, donstory007) are also hostile-authored but concern events this world's own later tradition would recognizably claim as its own history rather than events it would need to answer as an accusation. The absence named here is specifically the absence of this world's own side of that particular story, not the absence of the allegation itself, which the hostile record preserves in detail. The two unregistered citations (Letter LXVI, Letter CV) are named as an open item in Section 9 rather than left as silent, unverifiable references.
