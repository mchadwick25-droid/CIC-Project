## Section 1 — World Identity

**World name (as the world would name itself):** No single self-designation survives; this world's own participants would likely have described themselves by their location and discipline — "those at Bethlehem, by the manger" or "those given to the Scriptures" — rather than a school or movement name. Modern scholarly label: Hieronymian Ascetic-Literary Christianity.

**World code:** `hal`

**Temporal scope:** c. 382–420 CE. Beginning at Jerome's arrival in Rome and convergence with Marcella's established ascetic household (Doc_01 §1.1); ending at the close of a generational cluster of deaths (Marcella 410, Paula 404, Eustochium 418–420, Jerome 420) that ends the world's own attested formation ecology, on institutional-continuity rather than purely biographical grounds (Doc_01 §1.2).

**Geographic scope:** Bipolar — Rome (the Aventine Hill circle around Marcella; the aristocratic households of Paula, Eustochium pre-385, and Fabiola) and Bethlehem (the double monastery, the pilgrim hostel, proximity to the Nativity site), connected continuously by letter across the whole span, with the final decade (410–420) Bethlehem-concentrated once Marcella dies (Doc_01 §2, §1.3).

**Community character:** A community that understood its life together as one continuous scholarly-ascetic labor, offered outward in two linked forms: hospitality to travelers and a corrected biblical text for the wider Latin church. It did not understand itself as forming large numbers of new believers through structured initiation; it understood itself as a small, costly, textually-serious household — or rather two linked households — whose renunciation and whose scholarship were, in its own logic, a single discipline, not two (Doc_05 §11, Doc_01 §5.1).

## Section 3 — Formation Logic

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

A fully formed practitioner in this world was one for whom ascetic renunciation and scholarly-textual labor had become a single, undivided discipline — not a scholar who also fasted, and not an ascetic who also happened to read, but someone for whom the correction of a word and the correction of the appetite were the same kind of act, worked at from different ends (Doc_05 §1, §11). This world had no single settled term for this state; its own vocabulary approached it through the status categories of *virginitas* and *vidua* (for women) and through the practicing scholar's ongoing labor (for Jerome), rather than through one unifying title (Doc_06 entries 4, 5).

### The Formation Mechanism

Formation happened through two interlocking practices this world did not experience as separable: renunciation (the sale or redirection of inherited wealth, the closing-off of marriage, plain dress and fasting) and textual labor (Hebrew study, translation, correspondence, the *praefatio* practice of defending each finished work). Neither was preparatory to the other. The practices depended on a specific material condition — inherited aristocratic wealth, without which neither the mobility for translation work nor the funding for large-scale renunciation (a monastery, a hostel, a hospital) was possible (Doc_07 §2, §4). The relationship between practice and transformation was cumulative and public: renunciation was not a private disposition but a socially visible, family-disrupting, continuously re-affirmed act (Doc_05 §1; Doc_07 §3), and textual labor was not private study but argument conducted in public, against real, sometimes hostile, readers (Doc_07 §1).

### The Formation Arc

Entry into this world's formation was marked by a specific, costly renunciatory act — visible, public, and disruptive to prior family and social bonds (Doc_05 §1, §3). The middle of the journey deepened through sustained textual and ascetic labor under real external pressure: this world's participants did not experience formation as steadily accumulating; they experienced controversy (the Origenist rupture, the Augustine dispute) as periodically forcing them to re-argue and re-establish what they had already committed to (Doc_08 §1). Maturity in this world looked like the capacity to defend one's own textual-ascetic labor publicly and to hold real, unresolved tension (this world's own inherited Alexandrian interpretive method against its own later, urgent renunciation of Origen specifically) without collapsing it into a single, simplified account (Doc_05 §8).

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

World is strand-singular (Doc_01 §4); this sub-section is omitted per template instruction.

### Formation Logic and the Confirmed Gravities

*Hebraica veritas* is both produced and transmitted by the formation logic's textual half — the *praefatio* practice is where the gravity is continuously re-argued into being, not merely stated once (Doc_06 entry 13; Doc_08 force 2B-2). Ascetic self-impoverishment is the formation logic's material half, and it is what makes the textual half materially possible — patronage-authority is the hinge connecting the two, since it is what channels the wealth renunciation frees into the scholarship formation requires (Doc_07 §1, §2, §8's central cross-lens finding). The formation logic is, in the end, one structural fact — entanglement of intellectual authority with material dependency, held differently by different participants — refracted through every gravity this world confirms (Doc_07 §8).

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary

### 4A — Emotional Texture

This world's dominant emotional register is a fused grief-and-instruction — commemoration that teaches even as it mourns, most visible in the epitaph-letter genre (Ep. 108, Ep. 127) that is simultaneously this world's primary record of loss and its primary vehicle for showing what a well-formed life looked like (Doc_05 §8). Formation's emotional arc ran from a costly, publicly-grieved entry (family resistance and loss, Doc_05 §1) through a middle period genuinely marked by fear — of doctrinal error, of controversy turning violent, of a patron's death or favor undoing a life's labor (Doc_05 §6) — toward a maturity that could hold hope for a corrected text outlasting every dispute surrounding it, even without living to see that hope confirmed (Doc_06, Part A entry 1).

### 4B — Philosophical Ecology

This world organized reality around a layered, two-tier account of textual authority: the Hebrew scripture as nearer to inspired origin, the Greek as beloved and constantly used but secondary when the two disagreed (Doc_07 §1). Its understanding of the human person centered on the capacity for disciplined self-correction — the same capacity a scholar exercised on a mistranslated word, a renunciant exercised on inherited wealth and appetite. Formation's fundamental possibility, in this world's own logic, was not a single conversion moment but an ongoing, defended labor of correction, textual and ascetic at once (Doc_05 §1, §11).

### 4C — Authority Structures

Authority in this world was a single currency — demonstrated scriptural learning, personally verified through direct engagement — held in structurally different positions depending on whether the holder needed patronage's material function to exercise it (Doc_07 §2). Jerome held this currency in a materially dependent position, requiring continuous external funding; Marcella held the identical currency independently, requiring no patron (Doc_01 §4). This world had no episcopal or territorial authority structure of its own; its central scholar held the humblest clerical rank and never sought higher office (Doc_01 §3.3, §8.1).

### 4D — Boundary Structures

This world's boundary was the decision to renounce, evidenced through action rather than through formal admission or geography — easy to cross from a distance (Fabiola renounced without relocating) and genuinely costly to cross in practice (Doc_07 §3). A second, less visible boundary — literacy and language — was never named as a boundary by this world's own sources but structurally excluded anyone without substantial prior education from its core formation practices (Doc_07 §3, per Doc_02 §7.3's Affirmative Duty). The pagan aristocratic culture surrounding this world (the Symmachus circle) pressed on this boundary specifically, making renunciation more costly to cross during periods of backlash without changing its underlying logic (Doc_08 §1, force 2A-1).

## Section 7 — The World's Tensions

**Tension:** *Hebraica veritas* vs. the received Septuagint tradition
**Nature:** Holding to Hebrew priority cost this world real standing with the wider Latin church (the Oea incident, Augustine's continued resistance); abandoning it would have cost this world its own central scholarly identity.
**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; this tension runs across the whole world.
**How the world held it:** With persistent, combative defense — argued and re-argued, book by book, in the preface apparatus, never resolved by simply conceding to either side.
**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed here carried the discipline of holding a genuinely contested textual-authority position without either abandoning it or pretending it was uncontested.

**Tension:** Inherited Origenist interpretive method vs. urgent later renunciation of Origen
**Nature:** This world's own interpretive habits were partly formed by a tradition it later had to publicly and urgently disavow once the wider controversy made that inheritance untenable.
**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; this tension runs across the whole world, though it presses most visibly on Jerome's own scholarly output.
**How the world held it:** With genuine, unresolved discomfort — the community's interpretive method was reshaped by having to decide, under real pressure, what of its own inheritance it could keep.
**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed in maturity here could hold that a formative teacher's inheritance can be partly kept and partly urgently renounced, without this being experienced as pure hypocrisy or pure betrayal.

**Tension:** Marcella's independent exegetical authority vs. the world's dominant patronage-authority logic
**Nature:** The same recognition-currency that authorized Jerome (demonstrated scriptural learning) authorized Marcella too, but held in a materially independent position this world's dominant, patronage-dependent authority logic did not require of anyone else attested in this world.
**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; this tension is real but narrower — attested for one figure specifically, Rome-based, not organizing the whole ecology.
**How the world held it:** By not naming it as a tension at all in its own surviving voice — this world's own record (via Jerome) presents Marcella's standing as simply real, without framing it as anomalous relative to the dominant pattern; this document names the tension the world itself did not flag.
**Formation significance:** This is the clearest place this world's ecology cannot be honestly described as reducible to its Primary gravities alone — a genuine counter-current the Representative must be able to hold without either overclaiming it as a parallel authority structure or dismissing it as merely exceptional.
