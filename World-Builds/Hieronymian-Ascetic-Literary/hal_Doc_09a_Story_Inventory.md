## 1. Story Inventory

### Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative

**S1. Jerome and Paula's departure from Rome and journey to the Holy Land and Egypt (385–386)**
- **Source:** Ep. 108 (*Epitaphium Sanctae Paulae*), Jerome's own narration. **Wording corrected per Round 1 review, which found "corroborated" here misleading: Doc_01's own independent re-reading of Ep. 108 confirms what the letter says (reading-verification), not that the events it narrates are independently attested by a second source — this remains single-source material, like S2, S4, S5, and S6 below.**
- **Tier justification:** Named author (Jerome), datable, near-contemporary to events described (composed 404, describing 385–386), historically credible in its broad outline though authored by an interested party and single-sourced. Meets Tier 1's core test: direct textual attestation within the world's own horizon, with a named author and identifiable social location — independent corroboration is not a Tier 1 requirement (see S3/S3a below for the rarer cases where it exists).
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as historical narrative — this journey happened, in this general sequence, with the author's perspective (Jerome's, writing for Eustochium) explicitly named. Specific details within the narrative (the Nitria encounter's monk-roster, per Doc_01 §6's corrected finding) carry lower, Contested-to-Inferential/Thin confidence even though the narrative frame itself is Tier 1.
- **Confidence:** Documented-to-Widely-Accepted at the narrative level (per the Story Tier Cross-Walk).

**S2. The 384–385 Rome crisis: Blaesilla's death, loss of Damasus's protection, Jerome's departure**
- **Source:** Multiple Jerome letters referencing the episode; corroborated as a sequence by Doc_01 §6, §8.1 and Doc_08 §1 (force 2A-4).
- **Tier justification:** Named author, datable; historically credible specifically because the sequence (Blaesilla's death, Damasus's death, departure) is attested across multiple of Jerome's own letters rather than one isolated account, and the outer bounds of the sequence (that he did leave Rome in 385, after Damasus's December 384 death) are not seriously disputed even though the full causal interpretation is Jerome's own. Documented per Doc_08's confidence calibration.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as historical narrative; the claim that a formal "synod" reviewed Jerome's conduct is explicitly NOT part of this story at more than Inferential/Thin confidence (Doc_01 §9 Open Issue #4) and must not be added as embellishment.
- **Confidence:** Documented (sequence); Inferential/Thin (formal synod claim).

**S3. The Jerome–Augustine correspondence and the Oea "ivy/gourd" incident**
- **Source:** Ep. 112 (=Augustine's Ep. 75) and related correspondence; the Oea incident as reported within it.
- **Tier justification:** Two named authors, both sides of the exchange independently attested (unlike most stories in this inventory, this one is corroborated by a second author's own hand, not only Jerome's), datable.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as historical narrative, with both Jerome's and Augustine's perspectives nameable — the rare story in this inventory where the "other side" is not lost.
- **Confidence:** Documented.

**S3a. The Origenist controversy and the rupture with Rufinus (added per Round 1 review, which found this omission an unexplained coverage asymmetry — "this world's central internal fracture," per Doc_02 §2.1, was absent from both the inventory and Absent Stories, while comparable, less-central material (S3) was inventoried)**
- **Source:** Rufinus's *Apologia contra Hieronymum* (401) and Jerome's *Apologia adversus Rufinum* (401–403), addressed to Pammachius and Marcella (Doc_02 §2.1, §1.1).
- **Tier justification:** Two named authors, both sides independently attested in their own surviving works — like S3, and unusually for this world, this is a story where "the other side" is not lost. Datable, near-contemporary to the events described.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as historical narrative, with both Jerome's and Rufinus's perspectives nameable and explicitly not privileged over each other (Doc_02 §2.1's Author Gravity note: both accounts are equally polemical, in opposite directions). The relative doctrinal-vs-personal/political weight of the underlying dispute is itself Contested and CT-tagged (Doc_06 entry 8) — this story's narrative frame is Tier 1, but its interpretive substance carries that separate, named contest, which must not be resolved when this story is told.
- **Confidence:** Documented (occurrence and general sequence); Contested (relative weight of doctrinal vs. personal/political motivation).

**S4. The 416 Pelagian mob attack on the Bethlehem monastery**
- **Source:** Jerome's letter to Riparius.
- **Tier justification:** Named author, near-contemporary, historically credible for the bare occurrence (a violent attack happened; buildings burned) precisely because Jerome had no rhetorical motive to exaggerate an attack on his own community's safety upward — if anything the vagueness (Doc_08 §1, force 3A-2) suggests understatement or reticence rather than embellishment, though this document does not press that inference beyond noting it.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as historical narrative for the bare fact of the attack (occurred, buildings burned, at least one death reported); any more specific narrative detail (number of attackers, precise casualties, named individuals beyond what Jerome himself names) is not supported and must not be added.
- **Confidence:** Documented (occurrence); Inferential/Thin (details).

**S5. Marcella's death in the 410 Gothic sack of Rome**
- **Source:** Ep. 127, Jerome's obituary letter to Principia.
- **Tier justification:** Named author, near-contemporary (412, two years after the 410 event), but single-source with no independent corroboration (Doc_02 §1.4).
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as historical narrative for the bare event; the specific reported dialogue (soldiers demanding treasure, her reported composure) belongs to the epitaph genre's conventions and should be handled with the same genre-awareness applied to S6/S7 below, even though the story's core historical claim (she died as a result of the sack) is Tier 1.
- **Confidence:** Widely Accepted (via Jerome's account; no independent corroboration).

### Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition (including Hagiographic Narrative)

**S6. Paula's death and Jerome's epitaph-narrative of her life (Ep. 108 in full)**
- **Source:** Ep. 108, *Epitaphium Sanctae Paulae*.
- **Tier justification:** This is the clearest Tier 3 case in the inventory. The letter is attributed narrative in a recognized hagiographic-adjacent genre (Doc_02 §5) — its account of what a well-formed ascetic life looked like is itself formation-ecology evidence, but specific claimed details (the precise wording of her final words, the completeness of her self-impoverishment) follow genre conventions of idealization and cannot be treated as historical reporting of the specific events themselves. The bare facts of her death and burial location are Tier 1-adjacent (S1 above covers the journey; her death itself, 26 January 404, is Widely Accepted); the *narrative texture* of the letter — how her life is dramatized, what scenes are chosen, what words are put in her mouth — is Tier 3.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as the community's own account of what a formed ascetic woman's life looked like, with the epitaph framing explicitly acknowledged. Not usable as historical fact about specific reported scenes or quoted words. The formation ideal is the evidence; the biographical detail is not.
- **Confidence:** Contested (general portrait); Inferential/Thin (specific details, quoted words).

**S7. Marcella's reported exegetical standing among Roman clergy (Ep. 127)**
- **Source:** Ep. 127, Jerome's obituary letter to Principia.
- **Tier justification:** Attributed to a specific figure (Marcella) in a specific commemorative-genre account, authored after her death for Jerome's own partly self-vindicating purposes (Doc_02 §1.4, Doc_04 §2). **Corrected per Round 1 review: this document does not apply the formal title *epitaphium* to Ep. 127** — Doc_06 entry 20 itself confirms this formal title is attested for Ep. 108 but was not independently confirmed for Ep. 127, which is "epitaph-genre in function" only. This is the single most Author-Gravity-constrained story in the inventory (Doc_06 entry 11).
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as the community's own account of what independent female scriptural authority looked like within this world, with the attributed, single-source, post-mortem framing explicitly acknowledged every time it is used. **This is not usable as documented fact about the extent or frequency of Marcella's consultations** — only as this world's own remembered account of them, per the Tensional gravity classification (Doc_04 §3, §6).
- **Confidence:** Contested (per Doc_04's Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, explicitly not meeting the Primary threshold).

**S8. Jerome's "Ciceronianus es, non Christianus" dream (Ep. 22)**
- **Source:** Ep. 22, to Eustochium, *De virginitate servanda*.
- **Tier justification:** An autobiographical dream-vision embedded within an exhortatory letter — attributed to Jerome's own reported experience, genre-shaped (the dream-vision is a recognized late-antique rhetorical/spiritual device), datable but not independently verifiable as a literal reported event.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as this world's own account of the tension between classical literary formation and Christian ascetic renunciation — the tension it dramatizes is Widely Accepted as a real, lived formation-ecology tension (Doc_02 §5); the dream-as-literally-reported-event is Inferential/Thin and should not be presented as documented autobiographical fact.
- **Confidence:** Widely Accepted (the tension it dramatizes); Inferential/Thin (the dream as reported event).

**S9. The *Vita Malchi* and *Vita Hilarionis* (Jerome's ascetic-hagiographic romances)**
- **Source:** Jerome's own hagiographic works, composed at/near the Bethlehem period.
- **Tier justification, resolved here per Round 1 review, closing the ambiguity Doc_02 §5 explicitly deferred to this document ("to be formalized in Doc_09"):** explicit hagiographic romance genre with "acknowledged legendary and rhetorical elements, not straightforward historical reporting" (Doc_02 §5) — Tier 3, not Tier 3/4, and not "bordering Tier 4" as an earlier draft of this entry stated. Tier 4's defining criterion is that a story is *not about a specific named individual* — a composite of typical, unnamed practice. The *Vitae* are, structurally and by design, single-authored literary works entirely about specific named individuals (Malchus, Hilarion); they cannot border Tier 4 regardless of how thin their historical confidence is. The earlier hedge conflated confidence level (genuinely very thin, Inferential/Thin) with tier category (Tier 3, unambiguously, on the named-individual criterion) — these are corrected here as the distinct axes this document maintains everywhere else in this inventory.
- **Usage guidance:** May be offered as models of ascetic heroism this world's own literary output produced and valued — evidence of what this world believed formation could look like, not evidence of specific historical events involving the specific individuals named in the stories (Malchus, Hilarion).
- **Confidence:** Inferential/Thin (per Doc_02 §5) — a confidence-level statement only, not a tier ambiguity.

### Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction

**S10. A day of study, prayer, and manual labor at the Bethlehem double monastery**
- **Source:** Composite, drawn from attested elements only: the general fact of a structured common life (Ep. 108, Widely Accepted); the practice of Hebrew study under named teachers (Jerome's own prefaces, Documented); the *praefatio*/*epistula* correspondence practice (Doc_06 entries 7, 13, Documented); the general Egyptian/Palestinian monastic template this world adapted (Doc_08 force 2A-3).
- **Tier justification:** No single source narrates a specific day. This is explicitly a composite reconstruction of typical practice, not a story about a specific named individual on a specific day — meeting the Tier 4 discipline exactly: every element traceable to attested evidence, nothing invented or extrapolated beyond it. **Explicitly, per Doc_01 §9 Open Issue #5 and Doc_03 #8, this reconstruction does not include a specific liturgical horarium (which hours, which psalms) — that level of specificity is not attested and is not manufactured here.**
- **Usage guidance:** Appropriate in world documents (and, per Framework guidance, in Representative narration) when explicitly marked as reconstruction. The Representative voice may narrate typical practice in this mode but must not claim it as a specific known event or supply schedule-level detail beyond what is stated here.
- **Confidence:** Inferential/Thin (as all Tier 4 material is, regardless of how well-sourced individual elements are).

**S11. The process of translating a biblical book, from Hebrew consultation to finished preface**
- **Source:** Composite, drawn from: Jerome's own prefaces describing his method and defending his choices (Doc_06 entry 13, Documented); the attested (though Contested-extent) practice of consulting Jewish teachers (Doc_01 §3.1); the dedication practice to Paula/Eustochium/Marcella (Doc_02 §1.1, Documented).
- **Tier justification, with the named-individual tension confronted directly per Round 1 review, which found this undischarged:** no single source narrates one complete translation process start to finish as a unified scene; this reconstructs the typical shape from independently attested stages, and every element is traceable — nothing is invented, so this is not Tier 5. But unlike S10 (a reconstruction of genuinely communal, unnamed practice), this story's subject — biblical translation from Hebrew — was, on this world's own evidence, performed by exactly one person throughout the entire span (Doc_01 §3.1). Tier 4's "not about a specific named individual" criterion is therefore in real tension with this entry: it is composite in *method* (no single source narrates one complete instance) but not composite in *subject* (there is only one subject to reconstruct typical practice for). This document resolves the tension by retaining the Tier 4 classification on the composite-method ground, since the alternative (Tier 3, "attributed to a specific figure") would overstate the confidence of any single source actually narrating a translation scene, which none does — but flags this as a genuine boundary case, not a clean Tier 4 fit, and instructs that this story must never be narrated (by the Representative or in any deployment context) as though it depicts a specific, individually attested episode.
- **Usage guidance:** Appropriate as reconstruction of typical scholarly practice, explicitly marked as such, and explicitly marked as reconstructing Jerome's own single-person practice, not a generalizable community practice. Must not be used to assert Jerome's Hebrew fluency at higher confidence than Doc_01 §3.1/§9 establish (Contested).
- **Confidence:** Inferential/Thin.

## 4. Absent Stories (Constitution Article 20's Affirmative Duty, applied to narrative specifically)

This world **cannot tell**, and this document does not manufacture, the following stories, named explicitly rather than silently omitted:

1. **No story from the unnamed monastic multitude's own perspective** — the "countless multitudes" at Nitria (Doc_01 §6) and the unnamed members of the Bethlehem communities themselves are never individuated in any surviving source; there is no attested basis for a story centering any one of them, named or composite.
2. **No story of family resistance told in the resisting family member's own words** — Paula's grieving relative (Doc_02 §7.2) is known only through Jerome's account of the grief, never through an independent account of the resistance itself; this world cannot tell that story from the inside, and this document does not attempt to.
3. **No story of Jerome's Jewish teachers' own experience or perspective** — named only in passing in Jerome's own account, never given independent voice regarding what teaching Jerome meant to them or what their own religious life and circumstances were.
4. **No story independently authored by Paula, Eustochium, or Marcella** — per Doc_01 §0 and Doc_02's central finding, no text survives that was composed by any of the four women themselves; every story in this inventory that centers them (S5, S6, S7) reaches the participant only through Jerome's hand, a limitation this document has flagged at each relevant entry rather than only here.
5. **No story of household dependents or enslaved persons affected by their patrons' ascetic renunciation** — Doc_02 §7.2 names this absence at the source-ecology level; this document confirms no story-level material exists to fill it, and none is invented.
6. **No story of ordinary lay believers' formation** — this world's own evidence base does not support any story of catechesis, initiation, or ordinary congregational life, consistent with Doc_01 §8.2's differentiation finding that this world's formation medium was elite-textual, not mass-catechetical; there is accordingly no "ordinary believer" story to tell in this inventory, and this is a structural feature of the world, not an oversight of this document.
