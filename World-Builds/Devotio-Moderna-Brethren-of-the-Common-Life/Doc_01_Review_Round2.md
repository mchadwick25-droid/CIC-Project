## New findings introduced by Revision 2

### N1 — SUBSTANTIAL. A Documented rating is attached to a claim the cited Kempis passage does not make.

§2, Beginning point, says: "**Documented**, directly vendored, that the site already held a beguine community Groote had gathered before the deed." Cell 1A repeats it: "the existing lay-communal and beguine milieu already present on the Bagijnenstraat before 1374."

The cited passage (*Chronicle*, Part II, Ch. III, l. 3804–3806) reads: "his dwelling-house and homestead lie bequeathed for ever to the poor Sisters, or Beguines, whom he had gathered together in that same place." It says Groote gathered these women in that house and that the house is theirs. It gives no timing. The natural reading is that the "Beguines" Kempis names are the residents of the 1374 house itself, the women the deed was made for. Kempis calls them by the local term for unvowed religious women. The passage does not show a community living there *before* the deed.

What is supportable is narrower:
- **Documented (Kempis):** Kempis calls the house's residents "Sisters, or Beguines," gathered there by Groote. That is useful evidence that contemporaries saw the house through the beguine category.
- **Widely Accepted (standard scholarship, not vendored):** the Bagijnenstraat, the beguines' street, already existed, and Low Countries beguines had lived unvowed, self-supporting common lives for more than a century before 1374. The document already argues this point, but it currently cites no source for it.

*Fix:* re-rate the "before the deed" claim, and rephrase Cell 1A so it names the regional beguine milieu at Widely Accepted without attributing its pre-1374 presence on the street to the Kempis passage. The S7 narrowing itself stands. Only the evidence rating attached to it is wrong. This is substantial because it changes a confidence rating and a sourcing conclusion.
