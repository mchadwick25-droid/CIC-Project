## Findings by severity

### BLOCKING

**B1. Misattributed quote in the Strand B evidence: "indelible and perpetual vows" is from Bronope (1411), not Diepenveen (1408).**
§4, Strand B, quotes the Diepenveen investiture as: "the Order of Canons Regular... the habit and the order that followeth the rule of the Blessed Augustine... This investiture, with indelible and perpetual vows." The first two fragments are from *Chronicle*, Part II, Ch. XIV (l. 4276–4298, Diepenveen, 1408). The last fragment is from **Ch. XVIII** (l. 4400–4415): "*How the Sisters in Bronope were invested.* In the year of the Lord 1411... This investiture, with indelible and perpetual vows to live the life of the cloister..." The ellipsis joins two different events at two different houses, three years apart, into a single quote. The Ch. XVIII passage is still good Strand B evidence in its own right: it also records the General Chapter's oversight of Diepenveen. The fix is to cite it as Bronope 1411 in its own words, not as Diepenveen.
*Why blocking:* this is the misattributed-quote defect that CLAUDE.md names as recurring. It sits in the evidence the strand finding rests on.

**B2. Misattributed quote: "astrology and magic" (*Founders*, c. l. 955) is not about Groote.**
§2, Beginning point, cites Groote's "turn from a career including 'astrology and magic' (vendored, Founders, c. line 955)." Line 955 is in Arthur's introduction and describes mathematics in general: "Mathematical study, which was regarded with some suspicion, owing to its supposed connection with astrology and magic, had been pursued with success by Thomas Bradwardine..." The passage about Groote is Kempis's own, at l. 3493–3500: "It is said that Gerard had been skilled in astrology and necromancy, and before his conversion had been in the habit of displaying some few of the tricks of the art of magic: but I have learned from two of his pupils that more than these cannot be charged to him." Kempis reports this as hearsay and plays it down, so "a career including astrology and magic" overstates it. The census's own wording is milder still ("a taste for astrology").
*Fix:* cite l. 3493ff. with Kempis's actual words, and keep his hedge.

### SUBSTANTIAL

**S1. Source register mislabeled: Arthur's 1905 introduction is repeatedly presented as "directly vendored" primary evidence at Documented.**
These claims come from the **editor's introduction**, not from Kempis:
- The Mendicant-opposition reasoning (l. ~1195–1216). This is Arthur's paraphrase of Groote's deathbed instructions.
- The habit distinction, "dark grey cloth... white rochet with a black hood" (l. ~1281–1283).
- The Rule "summarized by Kettlewell (p. 173)" (l. 1229). This is a 19th-century secondary summary. The draft's wording, "summarized from an established source," also misdescribes what the passage says.
- The Western Schism narrative, including "Alexander died in 1410."
- The plague figure (see M3).
- "a desolate place between Zwolle and Deventer" (l. 1220).

The Schism is a well-established background fact and still stands. But the Mendicant claim (§2, Historical Pressures; Cell 2A) is rated **Documented** on the strength of a modern editor's paraphrase. Unless it can be traced to Kempis or Busch, it should be **Widely Accepted**, with the source register named.
This also makes §4's honest-limit sentence false: "Every piece of direct textual evidence cited above for Strand B... is Kempis's own account." The habit and Rule evidence is Arthur's and Kettlewell's. That puts it one step further from the canons' own voice than the draft says.

**S2. The Strand B evidence left out vendored material that bears directly on the strand question.**
The strand finding (two strands, based on a difference in authority structure) can be defended. Constitution Article 21 lists "authority structure" among the grounds for a strand, and the coexistence evidence (Diepenveen still drawing members from Master Geert's House in 1408) is real. But three vendored passages that a sound strand argument must deal with are not addressed:
- (a) Kempis's *Chronicle*, Part II, Ch. V (l. ~3891–3899): devout households "took upon them the habit of holy religion... and **converted their houses into Monasteries of the Order of Canons Regular**." This shows unvowed houses turning into professed houses, which pulls toward a developmental reading.
- (b) The same chapter (l. ~3909–3912): Groote himself planned "a monastery for Regulars who should take the habit which he had seen in Brabant in the house of John Ruesbroeck." Arthur's introduction (l. ~1193–1216) likewise has Groote directing the Windesheim foundation on his deathbed. So the professed wing was the founder's own design, not a later split.
- (c) The Arthur passage the draft cites for the habit distinction actually says "The habit adopted **by the Brothers** was of dark grey cloth, and **when they became** Canons Regular they wore a white rochet." That wording is sequential. It also contradicts the draft's own habit marker for Strand A ("wear no habit"), because it has the unvowed Brothers wearing a habit. The real dividing line is profession, vows, the Rule and General Chapter governance, not habit or no habit.

None of this overturns the finding, since the two structures do coexist over decades. But §4 has to show that it weighed this evidence, and it has to drop habit as the dividing marker. As written, the section heads off the "sequential" objection in the abstract while citing a passage that is itself sequential.

**S3. The gender finding is stated more firmly than its evidence allows.**
§4 says gender is "explicitly found not to be an independent strand axis." In the next paragraph, it concedes that no vendored text carries the Sisters' voice. It also asserts that "the same transition pattern [is] documented on the men's side" without citing anything; S2(a) above would be the citation. Evidence recorded entirely from outside the women's houses cannot rule out a gendered formation logic. It can only fail to show one.
*Fix:* restate this as a provisional finding, carried at **Inferential-Thin** and open for review once the Diepenveen sister-book is acquired. Tie it to the honest limit in the same section.

**S4. The rapiarium plank of the shared-formation argument has no vendored citation, although vendored evidence exists.**
§3 says the rapiarium is "directly attested as the practice out of which the Imitation of Christ itself grew." The only sources given are Step 0 §1 and the census. §4 then claims it is "a genre and practice that originated in the unvowed households," and that the professed houses "shared rather than replaced" Zerbolt's formation logic. No source is given for either claim. Busch's *Chronicon Windeshemense* is vendored and primary under the same library ruling as the Groote letters. It attests rapiaria among the Windesheim canons (l. ~12018, 12506, 12758–12760, 12996; index l. 45533, "Rapiarium. Notizbuch").
*Fix:* cite Busch for the cross-strand claim, which strengthens it. Either source the claim that the practice began in the unvowed households or remove it. Downgrade "directly attested" for the link to the Imitation's origin, since that rests on the census alone.

**S5. The claim about the Jesuit world's status is false.**
§1 says: "no world folder for it exists in this portfolio as of this drafting." `World-Builds/Society-of-Jesus/` exists. It holds a Step 0 with three review rounds and a Doc_01 draft dated the same day, 19:26, three minutes before this draft's own timestamp. The Jesuit Step 0 (§3 B2) and its Doc_01 draft (l. 26, 62, 108) already name the Devotio Moderna relationship from the Jesuit side, citing the same Coleridge anchor. So "presently one-directional in what can be checked" is inaccurate. The link is named on both sides, and only Wittenberg is silent.
*Fix:* correct the statement. Point to the Jesuit Step 0 and Doc_01 draft as named from that side, not yet approved to proceed. For the Jesuit thread only (flag it, don't touch it): its Doc_01 calls Devotio Moderna "a separate, already-built world," which is wrong. It also describes this world as "withdrawal into disciplined domestic devotion," which sits badly with this draft's town-embedded framing. This needs a cross-world reconciliation note.

**S6. Wrong date: Erasmus and Luther are placed in the "early-to-mid 16th century."**
§2, Historical Catalysts. Erasmus was schooled at Deventer c. 1478–1485, and Luther was at Magdeburg in 1497. Both fall in the late 15th century. As written, the entry also sits oddly with a window that closes in 1517. The census gives no dates, so this is an error the draft introduced.

**S7. The beginning-point "ecological rupture" claim goes too far.**
§2 says: "before it, no household anywhere lived this movement's own characteristic form (common life, self-support through manuscript work, deliberate non-monastic status) at all." The 1374 house stood on the Bagijnenstraat, the beguines' street. Low Countries beguines had lived unvowed common lives supported by their own work for more than a century. The census's own rule summary (a ban on anyone "who wanted the house as a convent") shows the house was defined against existing forms, not created where none existed. The 1374 date can still be defended, but on a narrower claim: it is the first house in this movement's own line, and "the model for dozens more" (census). It is not the first appearance of the form anywhere.
*Fix:* restate the defense on that narrower basis. §6 "What this world inherits" should also stop saying that no lineage is addressed (see S8).

**S8. The nearest sibling world is missing from the distinctiveness argument, and Kempis's own lineage evidence goes unused.**
The census entry for **Rhineland Mysticism & the Friends of God** (Atlas V.2, same era) says it "feeds Devotio Moderna," and its `legacy` field says "The Devotio Moderna in the Low Countries grew in its wake." Kempis's *Chronicle*, Part II, Ch. II–IV, records Ruusbroec and Groenendaal. It also records that Groote "translated two books of John Ruesbroeck from the Teutonic into the Latin tongue" (l. ~3824–3826) and modeled his planned Regulars on Groenendaal's habit (see S2b). The draft compares this world with Desert, Alexandria, Lollardy, the Hussites, Wittenberg and the Jesuits, but never with the one sibling the census names as a direct parent. §6 then says "no vendored source in this world's own corpus addresses that lineage directly," which the *Chronicle* contradicts.
*Fix:* add a distinctiveness argument against V.2, and restate §6's inheritance section using the Ruusbroec evidence. The finding is about scope and distinctiveness, so it is substantial.

**S9. Forces Cell 1A: the Western Schism (1378) is listed as an initiating force for a movement founded in 1374.**
The Schism began four years after the deed it is said to have initiated. It belongs in Cell 2A (Ongoing External). Cell 1A should name conditions that were present at the origin. The Forces template's Layer 1 wording says "present at this world's origin." Candidates already in the draft or the vendored texts include clerical laxity and the existing lay-communal and beguine milieu. §5's "embedded in" paragraph can keep the Schism as context.

**S10. The 1374 deed is carried at Documented on an unread source.**
The census's own `verification` field for "Geert Grote Gives His House Away" reads "from reference summaries; primary text not yet read." The draft rates the deed's date and substance **Documented** "on the strength of the census's own dated-document citation" (§2, §5). **S11. Plague as the cause of Groote's death is under-rated: vendored evidence exists.**
§2 says the vendored account "gives the date and his age but not the cause." Kempis's *Chronicle*, Part II, Ch. III (l. 3779–3787), says Groote "died at Deventer, in the time of the pestilence." That supports at least **Documented** for his death during the plague, as against **Widely Accepted** for plague as the cause. This is a change to a confidence rating.

### MINOR

**M1. Groote's age: the "discrepancy" does not exist.**
"In the forty-fourth year of his age" is an ordinal year of life, meaning he was 43. The *Chronicle* (l. 3787) confirms this: "the forty-fourth year of his age was nearly done." The census's "forty-three" agrees. The draft rightly disclosed the point rather than quietly picking one reading, but what it disclosed was not a real conflict. Replace the sentence with a note that the sources agree, citing both vendored passages.

**M2. Windesheim's founding year is in the vendored text.**
The draft settles for "the mid-1380s … since none is independently verified here." The *Chronicle*, Part II, Ch. VI (l. 3919–3935), gives **1387**: the church was consecrated "on the day before the Feast of St. Luke," and six Brothers were professed as Canons Regular that day. This also gives Strand B's coexistence argument a firm start date.

**M3. Truncated quote.**
§2 quotes "five millions died in France alone." The text (l. ~1117–1118) reads "According to Sismondi **between four and five millions** died in France alone." Quote it in full, and note that Arthur is citing Sismondi.

**M4. Wrong direction:** "It sits only six years inside the census's own 'c. 1380' label." 1374 is six years **before** 1380.

**M5. "Groote's own household (1374)" (§4).**
The 1374 house was for women (census; the draft's own §2 says so). It was not Groote's own household.

**M6. Census wording blurred.**
§1 puts in bold "a documented both-directions ancestor relationship" as though that were the census `relationsSummary` wording. The census says "Parent-influence on both Luther's schooling and Jesuit **pedagogy** - a rare both-directions ancestor." "Documented" is the draft's own word, and the draft itself rejects it two sentences later. Quote the census exactly.

**M7. The Desert contrast leans on vows when the stronger ground is elsewhere.**
Desert's own Doc_01 Strand A (anchoritic) has "no written rule, no formal office." "Most importantly, a vow structure" is not the clearest difference. The clearest difference is withdrawal versus staying in town, which the draft also names. Reorder the argument or qualify the vow point.

**M8. Layer 2 leakage in §5.**
"An era's own sense … that existing structures were not adequately forming ordinary Christians" is the world's own experience (Layer 2 in the Forces template), and the only source given is a 1905 editor. The same goes for "understood, by its own defenders, as part of an active contest." Cut both back to Layer 1 events, such as the friend's letter naming "heretics, usurers, and clerks that live in concubinage" (l. ~5110), or mark them as deferred to Doc_08. Cell 2B's reading of the rapiarium "as this world's own characteristic transmission form" edges toward Layer 3 and should also be kept as a Layer 1 description. The rest of §5 and the six-cell sketch stay at Layer 1.
