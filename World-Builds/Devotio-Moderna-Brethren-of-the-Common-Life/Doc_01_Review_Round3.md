- **Kempis passage re-verified.** *Chronicle*, l. 3804–3806 reads: "his dwelling-house and homestead lie bequeathed for ever to the poor Sisters, or Beguines, whom he had gathered together in that same place." The quotation is verbatim. The passage gives no timing.
- **Split is correct.** §2 now rates as **Documented (Kempis)** only the claim that Kempis calls the house's residents "Sisters, or Beguines," gathered there by Groote. It states that the passage "gives no timing" and "most naturally describes the women living in the 1374 house itself." It explicitly denies that the passage shows a community predating the deed.
- **Wider claim downgraded and cited.** The regional precedent is rated **Widely Accepted** and cited to the census entry "The Beguines" (Atlas IV.7, `dates` "c. 1190-1310"). The entry supports it. Its `why` field describes women "across the Low Countries and the Rhineland" living "with no vows, no rule and no enclosure," keeping their own property or working in cloth and nursing, in "walled courtyard communities called beguinages," beginning "in the late twelfth century." That gives more than a century of regional beguine life before 1374. The entry's window closes at 1310, so it does not itself show that the life continued up to 1374. The street name and Kempis's own use of the term in the 1374 context cover that continuity, and the claim is correctly worded as one about the wider movement. This is acceptable at Widely Accepted.
- **Cell 1A matches.** It names the regional milieu at Widely Accepted (Atlas IV.7) and Kempis's naming of the residents at Documented. It no longer places a beguine community on the Bagijnenstraat before 1374. Neither claim is overstated.
