## S1 — Strand Determination

**Resolved.** The pillars of the conclusion check out against the sources.

- **Badby.** Foxe, lines 19229–19332. Badby appears "personally" before the bishop of Worcester. He is examined on his own eucharistic view and then examined again before Arundel and a bench of bishops and lords. He answers in the first person, is "admonished and requested … oftentimes" (19294–95), and refuses: "he woidd never believe otherwise than before he had said, taught, and answered" (19329–30; quoted verbatim apart from collapsed spacing). This is the same individual, personally held mode as Brute and Thorpe. The only difference is that no composition of his own survives, which is a fact about what was preserved. The document now says exactly this.
- **Twelve Conclusions.** The Conclusions appeal to Scripture as authority: "sacra Scriptura … Novum Testamentum" (28427–28) and "excusamus nos per Bibliam" (28469). None of the twelve raises the question of Scripture in English or lay access to it. The claim holds.
- **Tanner / Norwich.** The archive.org metadata for `heresytrialsindi0020unse` returns `access-restricted-item: true`, with collections `inlibrary` and `printdisabled`. That confirms the item is lending-only. No Tanner or Norwich file is vendored in `cic/texts/`. The claim is accurate, and the document no longer draws any content from the volume.
- **Amersham.** Foxe, lines 32323–29, puts together readers ("having and reading certain books of Scripture"), hearers ("the others who but only heard them read, did bear faggots"), and children made to "set faggots unto their fathers". The document calls this single-sourced, not cross-checked, deferred by Foxe to a later volume, and a polemical aside rather than a trial record. That description is accurate.
- **Conclusion.** The document puts the individual/household difference down to register and carries it as internal plurality under Article 16. It does not base the finding on which records survived. It also brings §3 Q1, §5 Cell 2B, §5 Cell 3B and §10.4 into line with that finding. This is an evidence-based finding.
