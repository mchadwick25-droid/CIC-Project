## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas VI.3, "The Anabaptist Movements," 1525–1650, Swiss lands, Moravia, the Netherlands. The "radical Reformation": Conrad Grebel and Felix Manz among the Swiss Brethren founders (Zurich, 1525); Balthasar Hubmaier and Pilgram Marpeck as early theologians; Menno Simons and Dirk Philips leading the Dutch tradition from the 1530s. Believer's baptism, gathered/separatist church order, and (predominantly) pacifism, persecuted by Catholic and magisterial Protestant authorities alike. **The Münster Rebellion (1534–35) and its violent, polygamous, apocalyptic-kingdom episode is explicitly excluded from this candidate's own scope** — treated here, per the dossier, as outside "mainstream" Anabaptism represented by the Swiss Brethren and the Menno/Dirk Philips Dutch tradition.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears, with a real, disclosed, floor-adjacent complication unique to this candidate in the batch

The Swiss Brethren wing (Grebel, Manz, Hubmaier) shows no floor complication in any source checked this pass. **The Dutch/Menno wing carries a genuine, disclosable Christological complication that this document does not smooth over.** Menno Simons, following Melchior Hoffman, taught a "celestial flesh" Christology — that Christ's own flesh originated in heaven and merely passed through Mary "as through a channel," rather than being derived from her own substance. This bears directly on Article 4's third commitment: "Jesus Christ as truly human — incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary." Read plainly, Menno's own position is in real tension with the commitment's own "incarnate of... the Virgin Mary" language, on the same footing this project's own precedent documents treat comparable complications (Minucius Felix's Christological silence in the Latin Apologists' Step 0; Tertullian's later Montanist alignment) — **a disclosed, bounded complication, not treated as disqualifying by this document, but genuinely live and unresolved**, requiring Doc_01's own considered judgment rather than this document's own verdict.

**This is materially different in kind from VI.14's own anti-Trinitarian current — checked directly, not assumed.** VI.14 ("Anti-Trinitarian currents — Servetus, Polish Brethren," "Floor Question (register)" status) is a plain rejection of the Trinity itself. No source checked for this candidate — Swiss Brethren or Dutch — denies the Trinity. Menno's celestial-flesh Christology is a distinct, narrower complication about the *mode* of Christ's incarnation, not a denial of his full deity or of the Trinity. This candidate is not conflated with VI.14 and should not be treated as though it were.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: real, but genuinely thinner than every other candidate in this batch — stated honestly, not inflated

Per the dossier, directly host-verified: Menno Simons's complete works (Funk translation, 1871, two volumes), the *Martyrs Mirror* (Sohm translation, 1886 — the single richest lead, embedding Michael Sattler's trial record and Anneken Jans's prison testament), Dirk Philips's *Enchiridion* (Kolb 1910), and the Schleitheim Confession (in McGlothlin 1911 — itself once-removed, a translation of Zwingli's own Latin translation of a German original that survives only in manuscript, disclosed rather than presented as direct). **Real, disclosed gaps, not closed as dead ends but as genuinely unsourced:** Hubmaier's and Marpeck's own primary voices have no confirmed PD English translation anywhere; Grebel survives only in fragments. **Same methodological caveat as every sibling document: no figure below is word-extracted from vendored XML.** Qualitative read: thin in named-theologian range (two major early voices, Hubmaier and Marpeck, effectively unsourceable), unexpectedly strong in martyrological/testimonial bulk via the *Martyrs Mirror* alone.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on martyrological witness and personal testimony (the *Martyrs Mirror*'s embedded trial records and testaments — a genre this project's fleet has not built around before) and on pastoral/catechetical writing (Menno, Dirk Philips); genuinely thin on the movement's own founding-generation theological argument, since Hubmaier and Marpeck — the two Swiss/South German theologians most likely to carry that register — are unsourced.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no textual overlap, over a millennium's separation — but see §0 above for the deliberate thematic echo with Donatism, named as a reason for selection, not a source conflict. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** the persecution relationship with Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities (§2 A3); no figure or source overlap with the Jesuits, the Tridentine Church, or Lollardy.

### B4 — Audiences

The clearest "minority report" voice in the whole batch — direct appeal for anyone drawn to the already-built Donatism world's own question (does institutional size or state backing settle who the true church is?) asked again, independently, two centuries later and an ocean of theological difference away, with its own answer.

### B5 — Scale

The narrowest and most fragmented geography of any candidate in this batch — Swiss lands, Moravia, and the Netherlands are three genuinely distinct regional communities, not one continuous territory, a real disclosed complication for Doc_01's own coherence argument (the same shape of question, though different in specifics, that this project's own Gallic Monastic-Ascetic build had to resolve for its own Martin/Cassian relationship).

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A, with Menno's celestial-flesh Christology disclosed rather than smoothed over and correctly distinguished from VI.14's separate anti-Trinitarian question; clears Sourcing genuinely, though thinner than every batch-mate, with two real theological voices (Hubmaier, Marpeck) unsourced and disclosed as such; strong on martyrological/testimonial register, unusual among this project's built worlds; real, deliberate thematic echo of Donatism named as the reason for selection; narrowest, most fragmented geography in the batch, itself a disclosed open item.

**Tier 1, provisional — the strongest available echo in the batch, but with more real open conditions than any other candidate here.** Three binding items, not one: Menno's Christology (§2 A1), Hubmaier/Marpeck's unsourced voice (§3 B1), and the Swiss/Moravian/Dutch geographic coherence question (§3 B5). None is disqualifying on its own; together they mean this candidate needs more of Doc_01's own constructive work than its magisterial-Protestant batch-mates before its own scope is fully settled.
