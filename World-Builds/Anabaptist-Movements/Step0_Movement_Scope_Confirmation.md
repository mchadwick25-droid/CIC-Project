## 0. Purpose and relationship to the existing Step 0 record

Selected directly by Mark from the Era VII survey roster, 2026-09-15, on the same named criteria as its five batch-mates — explicitly as the batch's radical/dissenting voice, chosen with the acknowledged risk that its sourcing would be thinner in named-theologian range than the other candidates (§3 B1 below), against the deliberate payoff of a real thematic echo with this project's own already-built Donatism world (I.4, Era 2): a gathered, pure church separate from a state-sponsored one, a rebaptism/re-validation practice as its own boundary marker, willingness to suffer rather than blur the line.

Donatism (I.4) runs c. 311–439 CE; Anabaptism begins 1525 — the gap is roughly 1,100 years, over a millennium. The movement's own vendored voices do not accept the comparison as their own self-understanding — it is the charge their opponents made against them, and they reject it directly. Menno's own complete works: "we are opposed to the Donatists, Circumcelliones, Munsterites, and to the errors, abuses and abominations of all uproarious sects." Dirk Philips's *Enchiridion* protests persecutors who "liken us to the Donatists, Munsterites, and other turbulent, erring spirits... A great injustice is done to us." Real theological differences also separate the two cases: infant baptism, not believer's baptism, was at issue for the Donatists, whose own rebaptism concerned lapsed clergy's sacramental validity rather than a convert's first true baptism; the Donatists appealed to the Roman emperor for vindication, and Circumcellion violence has no real Anabaptist analogue. The echo named here as a reason for selection is real at the structural level this document names (gathered church, rebaptism as boundary marker, willingness to suffer) — but it is this document's own outside observation, not the movement's own self-understanding, and the movement's own sources actively reject being identified with the Donatists by name.

## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas VI.3, "The Anabaptist Movements," 1525–1650, Swiss lands, Moravia, the Netherlands. The "radical Reformation": Conrad Grebel and Felix Manz among the Swiss Brethren founders (Zurich, 1525); Balthasar Hubmaier, based from 1526 at Nikolsburg in Moravia, and Pilgram Marpeck as early theologians; Menno Simons and Dirk Philips leading the Dutch tradition from the 1530s. Believer's baptism and gathered/separatist church order hold across all three regions; pacifism is the majority but not universal position — Hubmaier's own vendored 1527 treatise *On the Sword* rejects Schleitheim's non-resistance on Christian magistracy (§3 B1, §4 item 4). All three regions were persecuted by Catholic and magisterial Protestant authorities alike. **The Münster Rebellion (1534–35) and its violent, polygamous, apocalyptic-kingdom episode is explicitly excluded from this candidate's own scope** — treated here, per the dossier, as outside "mainstream" Anabaptism represented by the Swiss Brethren and the Menno/Dirk Philips Dutch tradition.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears, on the movement's own affirmative confessions, with a real, disclosed, floor-adjacent complication reaching all three of its named-author pillars

Article 4's own required test is that "its own confession affirms, in its plain historical sense" the five commitments — not a test on absence. A test on absence ("no source checked... denies the Trinity") would be empty for the Swiss wing in particular, since Grebel and Manz have no vendored writings at all, and the Schleitheim articles never touch any of the five commitments. Hubmaier — based in Moravia rather than among the Swiss Brethren proper — has a partial vendored voice of his own (§3 B1), but it argues Christian magistracy and the sword, not any of Article 4's five commitments, so it supplies no affirmative evidence on the floor question either. The floor is grounded affirmatively instead, in the movement's own already-vendored confessions. The Dordrecht Confession (1632, reproduced in the *Martyrs Mirror*) Article I: "we confess with the mouth, and believe with the heart... in one eternal, almighty, and incomprehensible God, the Father, Son and Holy Ghost, and in none more, nor in any other." The Waterlander Confession (in McGlothlin, c. 1580) Article II: "This one God in sacred Scripture is revealed and distinguished into Father, Son and Holy Spirit... There are three (and yet) only one God." Dirk Philips's own *Enchiridion*: "The Father, Son and Holy Ghost, then, is the only true and living God and Lord... beside whom there is no other God and Lord." These affirm commitments (1) and (5) directly, in the movement's own words, not by absence of denial.

This affirmative floor evidence is Dutch-wing only. No affirmative parallel exists in the vendored corpus for the Swiss or Moravian wings: Grebel and Manz left no writings, Schleitheim never touches the five commitments, and Hubmaier's own vendored treatise argues a different question entirely. The floor verdict for the Swiss and Moravian wings therefore rests on §4 item 4's three-region coherence argument, not on affirmative evidence of their own — a dependency stated plainly here rather than left implicit. Two further leads exist for Doc_02 to weigh, neither settled by this document: McGlothlin's own editorial summary (pp. 13–18, his summary, not Peter Riedemann's own text) reports that Riedemann's Hutterite *Rechenschaft* (c. 1545) expounds "twelve articles of religion" and states a baptismal formula; and Hubmaier's own material in Vedder, though confined to the sword question, may bear further on this once read in full.

**The Dutch wing's own confessions carry a genuine, disclosable complication on commitment (3).** The phrase "as through a channel," sometimes used to describe Menno's Christology as teaching that Christ's flesh merely passed through Mary, does not appear anywhere in his vendored complete works (Funk, 1871). The actual source is the *Martyrs Mirror*: the friar Cornelis, prosecuting an imprisoned Anabaptist named Herman, *claims* Menno teaches Christ "merely passed through her body, as water through a sieve, or through a spout" — and Herman directly replies, "you do not understand what Menno Simons writes; for what you here say, cannot be found in his writings; but he shows with many Scriptures, that the Word became flesh (as John writes in his first chapter), and not the seed of Mary." This phrase is an opponent's caricature, not Menno's own wording, and the sieve/spout image is one Herman rejects outright — but Herman's own fuller reply affirms the doctrine underneath it: Christ's flesh is not "the seed of Mary." The complication is real, grounded in Menno's and Dirk Philips's own words below, and — through Herman's own testimony — in the *Martyrs Mirror* as well. Menno's *Reply to Gellius Faber* states that his opponents' own confession of Christ — that "the whole person, Christ Jesus, with body and soul, is the natural fruit of the flesh and blood of Mary" — gives rise to "weighty and intolerable improprieties and abominable errors," the first of which is "a divided Christ." Against this, Menno declares Christ "truly God and man... became a miserable, suffering and mortal man in Mary, the pure virgin... planted in her of the seed and Word of his Father, by the Holy Spirit of God; conceived of her through faith; fed and nourished in her virgin body" — language that consistently keeps Christ's flesh as *originating* with the Father and merely *nourished within* Mary, not derived from her own flesh and blood, which Menno elsewhere names and rejects as his opponents' position ("the man of Mary's flesh"). This bears directly on Article 4's third commitment: "Jesus Christ as truly human — incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary." Read plainly, this is real tension with the commitment's own "incarnate of... the Virgin Mary" language.

The complication reaches all three of the movement's named-author pillars, not Menno alone. Dirk Philips's *Enchiridion*, in its own section "The Incarnation of Jesus Christ," teaches the same doctrine outright, in his own words: "it is impossible for the flesh of Christ to be formed by Mary; for neither the seed of Mary, nor that of any earthly creature can by any means be the true living bread that came down from heaven." And the *Martyrs Mirror*'s own testimony — Herman's fuller reply to friar Cornelis, above — affirms the identical doctrine in the movement's own third pillar. Consistent with this same pattern, both the Dordrecht and Waterlander confessions above describe Christ as conceived or made flesh "in" the virgin Mary, not "of" her — Dordrecht: "the Word, Himself became flesh and man; that He was conceived in the virgin Mary"; Waterlander Article VIII: the Son "in the body of the virgin Mary was made flesh or man... born of Mary." This is a disclosed, bounded complication reaching Menno, Dirk Philips, the *Martyrs Mirror*'s own testimony, and the movement's own later confessional "in"-phrasing together — not treated as disqualifying by this document, but genuinely live and unresolved, requiring Doc_01's own considered judgment rather than this document's own verdict. The kind of complication is closer to the Latin Apologists' Step 0 treatment of Lactantius's own positive pneumatological and eschatological complications than to Minucius Felix's mere silence, since Menno's, Dirk's, and the *Martyrs Mirror*'s teaching here is positive and explicitly argued, not an absence — that document's own precedent bears on commitment (5) and on eschatology, not commitment (3); it is invoked here only for the *kind* of complication (argued teaching, not silence), not as though it addressed the same commitment.

**This is materially different in kind from VI.14's own anti-Trinitarian current — checked directly, not assumed.** VI.14 ("Anti-Trinitarian Currents (Servetus; Polish Brethren/Socinians; Transylvanian Unitarians)," "Floor Question (register)" status) is a plain rejection of the Trinity itself. No source checked for this candidate — Swiss Brethren or Dutch — denies the Trinity; Dordrecht, the Waterlander confession, and Dirk's own writing affirm it directly, as shown above. Menno's and Dirk's celestial-flesh Christology is a distinct, narrower complication about the *mode* of Christ's incarnation, not a denial of his full deity or of the Trinity. This candidate is not conflated with VI.14 and should not be treated as though it were.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing: real, thin in named-theologian range, fourth-largest by volume of this batch's six candidates

As of `cic/corpus-map/the-anabaptist-movements.yaml`'s 2026-09-25 state, the census calls the sourcing "RICH in ordinary inside voice: court testimonies, prison hymns, the Martyrs Mirror, Hutterite Chronicle" and records it as "Tiered Strong (Tier 1) on a rich base" — the same record adding its own qualification directly: "read with care for genre... Its court testimonies are coerced, not plain inside voice." By raw volume, summed directly from each of this batch's six candidates' own corpus-map `source_file` lists: Lollardy ~4.95M words, the Society of Jesus ~3.37M, the Tridentine Church ~2.91M, the Anabaptist Movements ~2.66M, the Reformed Cities of Zurich and Geneva ~2.30M, Lutheran Wittenberg ~0.96M. Anabaptist sourcing runs fourth of six by volume, not smallest and not second-largest. The defensible claim is thinness in named-theologian *range*, not in volume.

Eight works are vendored and assigned: Menno Simons's complete works (Funk translation, 1871, two volumes, ~531,000 words); the *Martyrs Mirror* (Sohm translation, 1886 — the single richest lead, embedding Michael Sattler's trial record and Anneken Jans's prison testament, ~1.19M words); Dirk Philips's *Enchiridion* (Kolb 1910, ~208,000 words); the Schleitheim Confession (in McGlothlin's *Baptist Confessions of Faith*, 1911, pp. 3–9 of the printed volume — itself once-removed, a translation of Zwingli's own Latin translation of a German original that survives only in manuscript, disclosed rather than presented as direct; the same compiled volume, ~134,000 words in full, also carries the Waterlander Confession quoted at §2 A1, under this project's own whole-volume assignment convention for a bounded primary excerpt inside a larger compilation); the Hutterite Brethren's *Geschicht-Buch* (Wolkan 1923, German original, ~352,000 words, covering 1517–1665), a garbled scan held to second-witness status under Mark's 2026-09-25 OCR ruling "a" (`worlds/_cross-world/LIBRARY-DECISION-LOG.md`) — scan quality, not language, is the reason; the Ausbund hymnal (Lancaster 1846 printing, ~137,000 words), the movement's own hymnody, still in liturgical use among the Amish, held to the same second-witness status under the same ruling; a standalone 1890 Elkhart edition of the Dordrecht Confession (1632) carrying the movement's own catechism and sacramental forms, independent of its reproduction inside the *Martyrs Mirror* (~24,000 words); and Balthasar Hubmaier's own primary voice, partly recovered: Vedder's 1905 biography (~86,000 words in full), whose Appendix (pp. 273–322) is a complete English translation of Hubmaier's 1527 treatise *On the Sword*, addressed from his base at Nikolsburg to the Margravate of Moravia's own chancellor and nobility. Total across all eight vendored files in full: approximately 2.66M words.

**Real, disclosed gaps:** Marpeck's own primary voice remains effectively unsourced. Grebel survives only in fragments. Hubmaier's own gap is only partly closed: *On the Sword* argues one specific question — Christian magistracy and the sword — not his wider sacramental and catechetical writing, and the *Martyrs Mirror*'s brief martyr account and notice of a Zwingli complaint against him remain the only other Hubmaier material. Two sources remain neither assessed nor formally closed this pass — the Täuferakten court-testimony series and Harder's *Sources of Swiss Anabaptism* — left open for Doc_02.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on martyrological witness and personal testimony (the *Martyrs Mirror*'s embedded trial records and testaments — a genre this project's fleet has not built around before), on pastoral/catechetical writing (Menno, Dirk Philips), and on liturgical/hymnic voice (the Ausbund), joined by a substantial Moravian institutional chronicle (the Hutterite *Geschicht-Buch*) — the chronicle and the hymnal both held to second-witness status as garbled scans, per Mark's 2026-09-25 OCR ruling "a," not for want of an English translation; genuinely thin on the movement's own founding-generation theological *argument* specifically. Marpeck's own primary voice remains effectively unsourced. Hubmaier's is now partly available: his complete 1527 *On the Sword* (Vedder's Appendix) is a real, if partial, primary voice — a specific argument on Christian magistracy, from his own Moravian base — not the clean zero the corpus previously carried, though it does not carry his wider theological register.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no textual overlap, over a millennium's separation — but see §0 above for the deliberate thematic echo with Donatism, named as a reason for selection, not a source conflict. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** the persecution relationship with Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities (§2 A3); no figure or source overlap with the Jesuits, the Tridentine Church, or Lollardy.

### B4 — Audiences

The clearest "minority report" voice in the whole batch — real appeal for anyone drawn to the already-built Donatism world's own question (does institutional size or state backing settle who the true church is?), asked again independently over a millennium later (§0, which gives the roughly 1,100-year figure directly) and an ocean of theological difference away, with its own answer — and, per §0, its own explicit rejection of being named alongside the Donatists, an added layer Doc_01 should present honestly rather than smooth into a clean lineage.

### B5 — Scale

The most fragmented geography of any candidate in this batch — Swiss lands, Moravia, and the Netherlands are three genuinely distinct regional communities, not one continuous territory, a real disclosed complication for Doc_01's own coherence argument (the same shape of question, though different in specifics, that this project's own Gallic Monastic-Ascetic build had to resolve for its own Martin/Cassian relationship). Lollardy, England only, is a narrower single region than this candidate's three; "most fragmented" is the defensible claim, not "narrowest."
