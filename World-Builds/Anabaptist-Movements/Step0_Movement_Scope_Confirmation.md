## 0. Purpose and relationship to the existing Step 0 record

Selected directly by Mark from the Era VII survey roster, 2026-09-15, on the same named criteria as its five batch-mates — explicitly as the batch's radical/dissenting voice, chosen with the acknowledged risk that its sourcing would be thinner in named-theologian range than the other candidates (§3 B1 below), against the deliberate payoff of a real thematic echo with this project's own already-built Donatism world (I.4, Era 2): a gathered, pure church separate from a state-sponsored one, a rebaptism/re-validation practice as its own boundary marker, willingness to suffer rather than blur the line.

Donatism (I.4) runs c. 311–439 CE; Anabaptism begins 1525 — the gap is roughly 1,100 years, over a millennium, as §3 B3 states. The movement's own vendored voices do not accept the comparison as their own self-understanding — it is the charge their opponents made against them, and they reject it directly. Menno's own complete works: "we are opposed to the Donatists, Circumcelliones, Munsterites, and to the errors, abuses and abominations of all uproarious sects." Dirk Philips's *Enchiridion* protests persecutors who "liken us to the Donatists, Munsterites, and other turbulent, erring spirits... A great injustice is done to us." Real theological differences also separate the two cases: infant baptism, not believer's baptism, was at issue for the Donatists, whose own rebaptism concerned lapsed clergy's sacramental validity rather than a convert's first true baptism; the Donatists appealed to the Roman emperor for vindication, and Circumcellion violence has no real Anabaptist analogue. The echo named here as a reason for selection is real at the structural level this document names (gathered church, rebaptism as boundary marker, willingness to suffer) — but it is this document's own outside observation, not the movement's own self-understanding, and the movement's own sources actively reject being identified with the Donatists by name.

## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas VI.3, "The Anabaptist Movements," 1525–1650, Swiss lands, Moravia, the Netherlands. The "radical Reformation": Conrad Grebel and Felix Manz among the Swiss Brethren founders (Zurich, 1525); Balthasar Hubmaier and Pilgram Marpeck as early theologians; Menno Simons and Dirk Philips leading the Dutch tradition from the 1530s. Believer's baptism, gathered/separatist church order, and (predominantly) pacifism, persecuted by Catholic and magisterial Protestant authorities alike. **The Münster Rebellion (1534–35) and its violent, polygamous, apocalyptic-kingdom episode is explicitly excluded from this candidate's own scope** — treated here, per the dossier, as outside "mainstream" Anabaptism represented by the Swiss Brethren and the Menno/Dirk Philips Dutch tradition.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears, on the movement's own affirmative confessions, with a real, disclosed, floor-adjacent complication reaching two of its three named-author pillars

Article 4's own required test is that "its own confession affirms, in its plain historical sense" the five commitments — not a test on absence. A test on absence ("no source checked... denies the Trinity") would be empty for the Swiss wing in particular, since Grebel, Manz, and Hubmaier have no vendored writings at all, and the Schleitheim articles never touch any of the five commitments. The floor is grounded affirmatively instead, in the movement's own already-vendored confessions. The Dordrecht Confession (1632, reproduced in the *Martyrs Mirror*) Article I: "we confess with the mouth, and believe with the heart... in one eternal, almighty, and incomprehensible God, the Father, Son and Holy Ghost, and in none more, nor in any other." The Waterlander Confession (in McGlothlin, c. 1580) Article II–III: "This one God in sacred Scripture is revealed and distinguished into Father, Son and Holy Spirit... There are three (and yet) only one God." Dirk Philips's own *Enchiridion*: "The Father, Son and Holy Ghost, then, is the only true and living God and Lord... beside whom there is no other God and Lord." These affirm commitments (1) and (5) directly, in the movement's own words, not by absence of denial.

**The Dutch wing's own confessions carry a genuine, disclosable complication on commitment (3) — and this revision corrects both the quotation used to characterize it and its scope.** Revision 1 attributed to Menno the phrase "as through a channel," describing Christ's flesh as merely passing through Mary. **That quotation is not in the vendored Menno corpus.** A direct search of Menno's complete works (Funk, 1871) found no such phrase. The actual source is the *Martyrs Mirror*: the friar Cornelis, prosecuting an imprisoned Anabaptist named Herman, *claims* Menno teaches Christ "merely passed through her body, as water through a sieve, or through a spout" — and Herman directly replies, "you do not understand what Menno Simons writes; for what you here say, cannot be found in his writings." It is an opponent's caricature, explicitly rebutted inside this project's own vendored source, not Menno's own wording — repeating it as though it were his own words would have been exactly the kind of quote-verification failure CLAUDE.md's source-fidelity section treats as a recurring, serious defect. **The underlying complication is nonetheless real, grounded here in Menno's own actual words instead.** Rejecting his opponents' Christology outright as one of several "abominable errors," Menno's *Reply to Gellius Faber* declares Christ "truly God and man... became a miserable, suffering and mortal man in Mary, the pure virgin... planted in her of the seed and Word of his Father, by the Holy Spirit of God; conceived of her through faith; fed and nourished in her virgin body" — language that consistently keeps Christ's flesh as *originating* with the Father and merely *nourished within* Mary, not derived from her own flesh and blood, which Menno elsewhere names and rejects as his opponents' position ("the man of Mary's flesh"). This bears directly on Article 4's third commitment: "Jesus Christ as truly human — incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary." Read plainly, this is real tension with the commitment's own "incarnate of... the Virgin Mary" language.

The complication is not Menno's alone — it reaches Dirk Philips too, on the same footing, in his own words. Dirk's *Enchiridion*, in its own section "The Incarnation of Jesus Christ": "it is impossible for the flesh of Christ to be formed by Mary; for neither the seed of Mary, nor that of any earthly creature can by any means be the true living bread that came down from heaven." Consistent with this same pattern, both the Dordrecht and Waterlander confessions above describe Christ as conceived or made flesh "in" the virgin Mary, not "of" her — Dordrecht: "the Word, Himself became flesh and man; that He was conceived in the virgin Mary"; Waterlander Article VIII: the Son "in the body of the virgin Mary was made flesh or man... born of Mary." This is a disclosed, bounded complication reaching Menno, Dirk, and the movement's own later confessional "in"-phrasing together — not treated as disqualifying by this document, but genuinely live and unresolved, requiring Doc_01's own considered judgment rather than this document's own verdict — on a footing closer to the Latin Apologists' Step 0 treatment of Lactantius's own positive Christological complications than to Minucius Felix's mere silence, since Menno's and Dirk's teaching here is positive and explicitly argued, not an absence.

**This is materially different in kind from VI.14's own anti-Trinitarian current — checked directly, not assumed.** VI.14 ("Anti-Trinitarian currents — Servetus, Polish Brethren," "Floor Question (register)" status) is a plain rejection of the Trinity itself. No source checked for this candidate — Swiss Brethren or Dutch — denies the Trinity; Dordrecht, the Waterlander confession, and Dirk's own writing affirm it directly, as shown above. Menno's and Dirk's celestial-flesh Christology is a distinct, narrower complication about the *mode* of Christ's incarnation, not a denial of his full deity or of the Trinity. This candidate is not conflated with VI.14 and should not be treated as though it were.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: real, thin in named-theologian range, but not the thinnest in the batch overall — corrected against the census and brought current

The census calls the sourcing "RICH in ordinary inside voice" and records it as "Tiered Strong (Tier 1) on a rich base." By raw volume, across the batch's vendored corpora, Anabaptist sourcing (roughly 2.3M words across Menno, the *Martyrs Mirror*, Dirk Philips, and the Hutterite chronicle below) runs second-largest, not smallest. The narrower, defensible claim: thinnest in named-theologian *range*, not in volume.

Per the dossier, directly host-verified and, as of 2026-09-24, five works vendored and assigned, not four as Revision 1 listed: Menno Simons's complete works (Funk translation, 1871, two volumes, ~531,000 words), the *Martyrs Mirror* (Sohm translation, 1886 — the single richest lead, embedding Michael Sattler's trial record and Anneken Jans's prison testament, ~1.19M words), Dirk Philips's *Enchiridion* (Kolb 1910, ~208,000 words), the Schleitheim Confession (in McGlothlin 1911 — itself once-removed, a translation of Zwingli's own Latin translation of a German original that survives only in manuscript, disclosed rather than presented as direct), and the **Hutterite Brethren's *Geschicht-Buch*** (Wolkan 1923, German original, ~352,000 words, covering 1517–1665) — omitted entirely from Revision 1, though Moravia is one of this candidate's own three named regions and this document's own B5 coherence question turns partly on it.

**Real, disclosed gaps, corrected in framing:** Hubmaier's and Marpeck's own primary voices have no confirmed PD English translation anywhere, though the *Martyrs Mirror* does carry a Hubmaier martyr account and a notice of a Zwingli complaint against him — partial material, not a full void; "effectively unsourceable" overstated a clean zero. Grebel survives only in fragments. Several census-named sources remain neither assessed nor formally closed this pass — the Täuferakten court-testimony series, Harder's *Sources of Swiss Anabaptism*, the Ausbund hymnal, and the Dordrecht Confession's own separate publication history (distinct from its *Martyrs Mirror* reproduction, already used above) — left open for Doc_02. The "no figure word-extracted from vendored XML" caveat from Revision 1 is stale: all vendored files here are `.txt`, directly word-countable, not XML.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on martyrological witness and personal testimony (the *Martyrs Mirror*'s embedded trial records and testaments — a genre this project's fleet has not built around before) and on pastoral/catechetical writing (Menno, Dirk Philips), now joined by a substantial Moravian institutional chronicle (the Hutterite *Geschicht-Buch*, German, not yet content-assessed); genuinely thin on the movement's own founding-generation theological *argument* specifically, since Hubmaier and Marpeck — the two Swiss/South German theologians most likely to carry that register — remain effectively unsourced in English beyond the partial *Martyrs Mirror* material noted above.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no textual overlap, over a millennium's separation — but see §0 above for the deliberate thematic echo with Donatism, named as a reason for selection, not a source conflict. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** the persecution relationship with Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities (§2 A3); no figure or source overlap with the Jesuits, the Tridentine Church, or Lollardy.

### B4 — Audiences

The clearest "minority report" voice in the whole batch — real appeal for anyone drawn to the already-built Donatism world's own question (does institutional size or state backing settle who the true church is?), asked again independently over a millennium later (§0; the gap is roughly 1,100 years, per §3 B3) and an ocean of theological difference away, with its own answer — and, per §0, its own explicit rejection of being named alongside the Donatists, an added layer Doc_01 should present honestly rather than smooth into a clean lineage.

### B5 — Scale

The most fragmented geography of any candidate in this batch — Swiss lands, Moravia, and the Netherlands are three genuinely distinct regional communities, not one continuous territory, a real disclosed complication for Doc_01's own coherence argument (the same shape of question, though different in specifics, that this project's own Gallic Monastic-Ascetic build had to resolve for its own Martin/Cassian relationship). Lollardy, England only, is a narrower single region than this candidate's three; "most fragmented" is the defensible claim, not "narrowest."

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A, on the movement's own affirmative confessions, with the celestial-flesh Christology — reaching Menno and Dirk together, grounded in their own words — disclosed rather than smoothed over and correctly distinguished from VI.14's separate anti-Trinitarian question; clears Sourcing genuinely, thin in named-theologian range but not thinnest in the batch by volume, with two real theological voices (Hubmaier, Marpeck) still effectively unsourced beyond partial *Martyrs Mirror* material, and disclosed as such; strong on martyrological/testimonial register, unusual among this project's built worlds, now joined by an unassessed Moravian institutional chronicle; a real thematic echo of Donatism named as a reason for selection, disclosed as this document's own outside observation rather than the movement's own self-understanding, which explicitly rejects the comparison; most fragmented geography in the batch, itself a disclosed open item.

**Tier 1, provisional — a genuinely strong candidate, but with more real open conditions than any other in this batch.** Three binding items, not one: the Christology, now reaching both Menno and Dirk (§2 A1); Hubmaier/Marpeck's still-effectively-unsourced voice (§3 B1); and the Swiss/Moravian/Dutch geographic coherence question (§3 B5).
