## Verdict

**SUBSTANTIAL REVISION REQUIRED.** Not ready to proceed.

The document is careful in form. It flags the sequential-development trap in advance, keeps Living Tradition Status at PENDING, and mostly stays at Layer 1 in §5. Its quotations are accurate. But its **Strand Determination rests on a factual premise contradicted by the vendored source it cites** (Vedder on Nikolsburg). It leaves the Moravian community's strand status undetermined, and it never tests the strongest divergence candidates. Its **distinctness argument makes two factual claims that the sources, including the sibling Step 0 documents it cites, contradict**: that Anabaptists had no civil settlement anywhere in the window, and that Lollardy was not an executable offence. It **does not engage the most adjacent built world** (Reformed Zurich and Geneva, `rzg`), whose own Doc_01 treats the Swiss Brethren as an internal schism from its Zurich strand. And it **moves a Step 0 obligation binding on Doc_01** (the celestial-flesh judgment) onto Doc_02.

Findings: **13 in total — 5 major, 3 moderate, 5 minor.** Every major finding and all three moderate findings are substantial under this project's definition, because each changes a claim's substance, a confidence rating, a sourcing conclusion, or a scope argument. This is Round 1, so the review cap has not been reached.

## Findings by severity

### MAJOR

**M1. The Nikolsburg/Hutterite history is wrong, and it contradicts the vendored source the document cites. This breaks the premise of the Hubmaier judgment call.** (§1 Short Description; §2 Geographic Centers; §4 second bullet)

The document makes three connected claims:
- the Moravian/Hutterite communities were "gathered around Nikolsburg (from 1526)" and "gathered from Hubmaier's Nikolsburg base";
- Nikolsburg was "later home to the Hutterite communities," which "came to occupy Nikolsburg after Hubmaier's 1528 execution";
- Hubmaier's sword position is attested "without a documented continuing party."

Vedder (`vedder_balthasar-hubmaier_1905.txt`, pp. 248–250, lines ~8966–9035), the source the document cites for this section, says the opposite on each point:
- "The party that had agreed with Hubmaier became known as the Schwertler, or men of the sword; while their opponents were named the Stabler, or men of the staff." That is a named party continuing Hubmaier's position after his death, in the summer of 1528.
- The Stäbler, led by Jacob Widemann (the Hut/Widemann side Hubmaier wrote against), **left** Nikolsburg. "They went their way to Austerlitz." There they began the community-of-goods settlements that Jacob Huter later organised: "by 1536 no fewer than eighty-six settlements."
- The Hutterites therefore descend from Hubmaier's **opponents** and formed **away from** Nikolsburg. They did not occupy it after him.

The document also cites "Step 0 §3 B1" for the Hutterites' communal-goods discipline. Step 0 §3 B1 says nothing about communal goods.

*Required:* Correct §1, §2 and §4 against Vedder. Re-argue the Hubmaier judgment call on true premises. The conclusion (no separate Hubmaier strand) may still hold: the Schwertler are not attested past 1528 in the vendored corpus, and Hubmaier has no vendored voice after his death. But the argument has to be rebuilt. It cannot rest on "no continuing party."

**M2. The Strand Determination is incomplete. It tests the weakest candidate and leaves the strongest ones untested.** (§4)

The section's framing paragraph is sound: it names and avoids the "X came from Y" trap. The finding itself has four defects:

1. **The Moravian community's strand status is never decided.** The finding reads "strand-singular for the two Dutch-and-Swiss communities the evidence most directly attests." Yet §4's closing bullet and §7 item 4 go on to claim a three-region coherence argument. Step 0 §2 A1 makes the Swiss and Moravian floor verdict depend on that argument. The phrase "not elevated to a fourth persisting strand" also mixes up regions and strands: under a singular finding, Hubmaier would be a candidate *second* strand.
2. **"Independently attested in each region's own confessional voice" overclaims.** The evidence given is Schleitheim (Swiss) plus Dordrecht and Waterlander, which are both Dutch. No Moravian confessional text is adduced. Step 0 §2 A1 already noted that Riedemann's *Rechenschaft* is known only through McGlothlin's editorial summary.
3. **The obvious candidates for strand-level divergence are never tested:**
   - *Hutterite community of goods.* Vedder p. 248: for the Stäbler it became "the doctrine of a standing or falling Church, since they were firmly convinced that a true Christian brotherhood could exist on no other basis." This organising conviction persisted across a sustained community from 1528 into the 1620s. By the document's own persistence criterion (the Ambrose/Imperial precedent), it is a far stronger candidate than Hubmaier.
   - *Dutch internal divisions over the ban and Christology.* McGlothlin's introduction (lines ~1440–1445) describes "serious divisions and much strife" over "discipline and the proper view of the person of Christ." He calls the Waterlanders "the most liberal wing." That is a named, persisting party within the window, divided on the very ban the document proposes as a preliminary gravity.
   - *The celestial-flesh Christology.* The dossier (§6) says it was "not present among the Swiss Brethren." It is a regional difference in organising conviction and bears directly on strand-hood.
4. **Polygenesis goes unengaged.** The census entry the document quotes elsewhere names Roth & Stayer as "the current standard reference on the movement's diverse, multiple origins ('polygenesis')." `rzg` Doc_01 relies on the same scholarship. This is the Dominant Modern Reconstruction on the exact question §4 must answer.

The claim that Dordrecht and Waterlander affirm the Schleitheim pattern "without textual dependence on it" has no source. It is also a non-derivation (lineage) argument in reverse, which recurring-ecology evidence does not need. McGlothlin (lines ~1466–1470) notes that the Dutch confessions show the influence of "Luther, Calvin, and other Reformers."

*Required:* Rebuild §4 so that each of the three communities, and each named divergence candidate, is tested against recurring-ecology evidence. Engage polygenesis explicitly. Reach a finding that actually covers Moravia.

**M3. The claims of criminalisation "across all three regions, 1525–1650" and "no comparable civil settlement anywhere in its 1525–1650 window" are contradicted by the vendored sources.** (§2 Cultural Environment; §3 distinctness from the Hussites; §5 Cell 2A, Cell 3B)

- *Martyrs Mirror* (`van-braght_martyrs-mirror_sohm1886.txt`, lines ~147865–148232): at Middelburg "the Prince of Orange, William I … had granted liberty of conscience, for the Anabaptists as well as for others." The volume then prints William's charge to the magistrates enforcing it.
- Vedder pp. 249–250 and 264–265: the lords of Austerlitz gave the Moravian brethren "entire liberty." The period 1564 to roughly the 1590s is called in the chronicles "the good time of the church." Their communities "flourished, under the protection of the Moravian" nobility.
- Vedder pp. 268–270: after the 1623 decree the Moravian communities were destroyed or dispersed, and survivors "found a home elsewhere" (Hungary and elsewhere). This contradicts Cell 3B's "Hutterite communal life in Moravia" as a settled mid-17th-century outcome.

The distinctness argument against the Hussites (criminalised versus settled) therefore rests on a false contrast. The six-cell sketch also leaves out Dutch toleration and Moravian noble protection, which are the most obvious external Ending/Transforming forces.

*Required:* Correct every instance. Restate the Hussite contrast on accurate grounds. Revise Cells 2A, 3A and 3B.

**M4. The distinctness argument misstates sibling worlds and skips the nearest ones.** (§1 Distinctive Contribution; §3 fourth bullet)

- **Lollardy.** The document says Catholic and magisterial authorities did not treat "Lollardy's underground preaching" as an executable offence. Lollardy's own Step 0 §1, the document the text says it is grounded in, states: "Burning for heresy was made law in England in 1401 … Oldcastle's failed 1414 rising made Lollardy treason as well as heresy." The claim is false.
- **Unity of the Brethren.** The document says the Hussite movement "became a tolerated national church, not a criminalized one," and that neither wing made adult (re)baptism defining. The Hussite Step 0 does not support this for the Unity. Standard scholarship records that the Unity was persecuted and that it rebaptised adult entrants from 1467 until about 1534. No vendored source settles the point. At minimum the claim must be tagged *Contested* or removed. It cannot be presented as "the one boundary marker none of these three siblings shares."
- **Batch attribution.** The document calls Devotio Moderna and the Hussites members of "this same six-world Era 6/7 batch." They belong to the separate three-candidate Era 6 batch of 2026-09-25 (Hussite Step 0 §0). Lollardy is the only one of the three in this world's 2026-09-15 batch.
- **Nearest neighbours omitted.** Reformed Zurich and Geneva (`rzg`, Built & Live) is the closest sibling of all. It shares the city, the year and the people. Its Doc_01 (§7, line 139) lists the 21 January 1525 baptisms as its own catalyst and calls the Swiss Brethren "an internal schism from this world's own Zurich strand." Lutheran Wittenberg (`witt`) and VI.14 (anti-Trinitarian currents) are also relevant. Step 0 §2 A3 asked Doc_01 to disclose the persecution relationship "from its own side." The distinctness section names three sibling worlds from another batch. It argues distinctness from none of the built worlds actually adjacent.

*Required:* Correct the Lollardy and Unity claims. Fix the batch attribution. Add an explicit distinctness argument against `rzg` (reconciled with its Doc_01's schism framing), against `witt`, and briefly against VI.14.

**M5. A Step 0 obligation binding on Doc_01 is moved to Doc_02 and left undischarged.** (§7 item 1)

Step 0 §4 item 1 is headed "binding on Doc_01 **and** Doc_02." It requires the build to "reach an actual considered judgment about how this world's own build should handle it." Step 0 §2 A1 says the complication requires "Doc_01's own considered judgment rather than this document's own verdict."

Doc_01 §7 item 1 restates the item as "binding on Doc_02" and says "this document does not resolve it either." That restates Step 0 differently and leaves a binding item undischarged. The judgment wanted is how the build handles the complication, for example as a disclosed Dutch-wing-specific floor-adjacent complication with named handling rules for later documents. It is not a verdict on the theology.

Related: §2 calls Dordrecht the affirmative-floor evidence for "the movement's Trinitarian and Christological commitments." Step 0 used Dordrecht affirmatively for commitments (1) and (5) only. It treated Dordrecht's "in the virgin Mary" wording as corroborating the *complication* on commitment (3).

*Required:* Make the considered handling judgment in Doc_01. Correct the §2 characterisation of what Dordrecht evidences.

### MODERATE

**Mo1. The 1650 endpoint defence is tagged with the wrong confidence level, is Dutch-centric, and leaves out window-relevant facts.** (§2 Ending point) The window itself is not reopened, since Step 0 and the census fix it. The defence of it needs repair:
- "*Dominant Modern Reconstruction*" names a scholarly-consensus level. The 1650 horizon is a project design choice taken from the census. Label it as such.
- Dordrecht is a Dutch consolidation. The defence never says what 1650 means for the Swiss or Moravian communities. By 1650 the Moravian communities had been destroyed or dispersed (Vedder, as in M3), and the Swiss persecution was still going on (for example the *Martyrs Mirror*'s Landis entries, line ~156905).
- The world's richest source, the *Martyrs Mirror* (1660), was compiled after the window closes, and so was the *Geschicht-Buch* (running to 1665). The census itself says the *Mirror* is "not a contemporaneous archive." Doc_01 should disclose that the compiler's framing lies outside the window.
- The claim that Dordrecht "remains in active liturgical and catechetical use" today is tagged *Documented* with no source. The Elkhart 1890 manual attests use in the 19th century only.

**Mo2. The Schleitheim evidence is misread.** (§1 Core Identity; §3 first bullet; §4 first bullet)

The document treats the translator's summary line, "Baptism, abstention, breaking of bread, avoidance of abominable pastors in the church, sword, swearing" (McGlothlin line 516), as though:
- it described the ban;
- it were evidence of congregational authority ("exercised by the gathered congregation itself");
- it were a list of "seven articles." It gives six headings.

The articles themselves separate excommunication (Art. 2), separation from "abomination" (Art. 4) and the church's own pastors (Art. 5). The summary line is a compression inside a translation of Zwingli's Latin translation. It is weak ground for a characterisation that carries evidential weight. Cite the articles themselves. Carry the once-removed caveat wherever Schleitheim bears weight in Doc_01, not only in §7.

**Mo3. The Münster exclusion is mis-tagged and under-explained.** (§2 Historical Pressures; §5)
- "*Documented* for the Münster exclusion" puts a confidence level on a scope decision.
- Step 0 §4 item 5 requires the document to "state plainly why." Doc_01 gives source absence and the dossier's "mainstream" label. It does not use the movement's own vendored repudiation: Menno, "we are opposed to the Donatists, Circumcelliones, Munsterites" (Funk line 44955); Dirk, "Donatists, Munsterites, and other turbulent, erring spirits" (Kolb line 4698).
- It also does not disclose that Münster arose in the same Melchiorite Dutch/North German milieu from which Menno later gathered congregations. Disclosing that is needed for an honest exclusion. Doing so is not a lineage claim.

## Confirmed accurate

- **Menno quote:** "we are opposed to the Donatists, Circumcelliones, Munsterites, and to the errors, abuses and abominations of all uproarious sects" is verbatim (Funk, line 44955). The ellipsis is fair.
- **Dirk Philips quote:** "liken us to the Donatists, Munsterites, and other turbulent, erring spirits … A great injustice is done to us" is verbatim (Kolb, lines 4697–4699; OCR reads "tous"). The ellipsis is fair.
- **Census quotations:** all five match `world-census.json` verbatim, allowing for capitalisation. They are "In January 1525, a small group in Zurich baptized one another as adults"; "Mennonites, Amish, Hutterites and Brethren all descend from it"; the free-church principle wording and "one of the most widely adopted Protestant ideas in the world"; "is still printed and still read aloud in Amish and Mennonite homes"; and the 1650/Dordrecht window sentence.
- **Anna of Rotterdam (Anneken Jans):** her testament to her son Isaiah is dated 24 January 1539, at Rotterdam (*Martyrs Mirror*, lines 63814–63820).
- **Sattler:** his letter to the church at Horb is present (*Martyrs Mirror*, line 58962).
- **Hubmaier:**
  - *On the Sword* is in Vedder's Appendix (p. 273), addressed to "Arekleb of Bozkowitz and Tzernehor of Trebitz, Chancellor of the Margravate of Moravia." The document's paraphrase is acceptable.
  - He was at Nikolsburg from 1526 to 1528, and it was his period of greatest literary activity (Vedder, chapter V).
  - He was burned at Vienna in March 1528 (Vedder, line 565).
- **Schleitheim:** at McGlothlin pp. 3–9, a "translation of his [Zwingli's] translation" (line 513). The once-removed provenance is correctly carried in §7.
- **Waterlander and Dordrecht:** both contain baptism articles.
- **Sibling Step 0 facts:**
  - Devotio Moderna is V.3, c. 1380–1517, and its houses dissolved under the Reformation.
  - The Hussite world is V.6, c. 1402–1517, with the 1436 Compactata.
  - Lollardy is V.5, England only.
- **Living Tradition Status:** handled correctly as PENDING. The distinction between historical continuity and present-day self-understanding under Article 28 is well drawn.
- **Donatist rejection:** disclosed honestly and not smoothed into a lineage, as Step 0 §4 item 5 requires.
- **Framing of §4:** it avoids the sequential-development trap explicitly.
- **§5:** outside Cell 3A, it stays at Layer 1 with no Layer 2 or 3 leakage.
- **Step 0 settled items:** window, Tier, sourcing figures and Hubmaier's Moravian placement are not reopened (but see M5 on the Christology item).
