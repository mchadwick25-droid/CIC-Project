## N1 in detail

**Swiss/Dutch.** §4 now tests the two regions directly against their own confessions. The Dordrecht claims check out verbatim in the Elkhart 1890 file:
- Art. XIV (Defense by Force): "Regarding revenge, whereby we resist our enemies with the sword... forbidden his disciples and followers all revenge and resistance."
- Art. XV (Swearing of Oaths): "all oaths, high and low, are forbidden."
- Art. XVI: "the ban, or excommunication, a separation or spiritual punishment by the church, for the amendment, and not for the destruction, of offenders."
- Art. XVII: "The Shunning of those who are Expelled."

The Schleitheim side also holds. The Second Article's ban is verbatim ("admonished twice in secret..."). The Fourth Article carries both the separation from "abominations" and the renunciation of "swords and arms of this kind... whether for enemies or for friends... 'You ought not to resist evil.'" The Seventh Article forbids "all swearing." Non-resistance, oath-refusal, the ban, and shunning or avoidance are therefore present in both regions' own confessional voice. Folding Swiss and Dutch into one Congregational strand is argued from shared practice, not from region. The finding explicitly names geography and leadership as the bases Article 21 excludes.

**Communal-Property (Moravian/Hutterite).** Vedder's quotations are verbatim (p. 248): the Stäbler were "men of the staff, i. c. [sic, OCR for i. e.], of peace and non-resistance"; community of goods "was at length with them the doctrine of a standing or falling Church, since they were firmly convinced that a true Christian brotherhood could exist on no other basis." "No fewer than eighty-six settlements" by 1536 is verbatim (Vedder frames it as "are said to have been"). The *Geschicht-Buch*'s 1517–1665 span matches the corpus-map and Step 0 §3. The strand is defined by the property structure alone, and §4 correctly says non-resistance does not separate it. The evidence is a secondary narrative and not the movement's own confession. §4 and §7 item 2 disclose that asymmetry plainly.

**Polygenesis.** It is now context only. §4 explicitly bars it from corroborating any strand count, and it names the reverse-origin move as forbidden. Resolved.

**Schwertler.** The exclusion now rests on Article 21's own word, "pattern," and not on persistence. The ijc citation is accurate. `worlds/ijc/Doc_01…` line 65 reads: "Article 21 tests for a meaningfully distinct pattern within the world, not for persistence through the world's full temporal span." Line 63 grounds Strand C in the 386 basilica standoff, Callinicum (388), and the Thessalonica response (390), exactly as this document reports. One overstatement remains (C-R4), but it does not change the conclusion.

## Required corrections (non-substantial; apply before promotion)

**C-R1. Misattributed quotation.** §4 ("Testing the Swiss/Dutch division") puts "the severity of the ban" in quotation marks and attributes it to "McGlothlin's introduction (pp. 24–25)." That phrase does not appear anywhere in the vendored McGlothlin. The source reads: "fell into disagreement and dissension, chiefly over questions of discipline and the proper view of the person of Christ." *Fix:* quote McGlothlin's actual words, or remove the quotation marks and the attribution. The argument, that the dispute is a variation in degree within one practice, still stands on "questions of discipline."

**C-R2. Schleitheim "Sixth Article" is not in the vendored file.** §4's Congregational-strand bullet cites Schleitheim's "Sixth Article (refusal of the sword)," and the Swiss/Dutch paragraph says Dordrecht matches "Schleitheim's own Sixth Article." The vendored McGlothlin skips from partway through the Fifth Article to the Seventh (raw lines ~625–645; OCR residue "ixrr rroc: raci"). Printed p. 7, which carries the sword article, is missing. *Fix:* cite the vendored Fourth Article's sword renunciation, which is present and says the same thing, and note the p. 7 lacuna. Doc_02 should record the lacuna as a source-condition item.

**C-R3. Amsterdam supplication.** §2 (Ending point) says Amsterdam sent the supplication "at the Swiss Anabaptists' own urgent request." The Mirror says it was sent "through the urgent request of the Anabaptists, at Amsterdam, with regard to the distress of the Swiss brethren" (Sohm ~156863–156865). *Fix:* attribute the request to the Amsterdam Anabaptists. (Also minor: the Mirror says Landis died after he was carried to church, not inside the prison. "Died of hunger in the Othenbach prison" follows the Mirror's own summary line and is acceptable. "Neglect" is the document's paraphrase of "want.")

**C-R4. Schwertler "attested exactly once."** Hubmaier's own position is attested more than once in the vendored corpus: *On the Sword* (1527, Vedder Appendix) and the 1528 Schwertler party. §2 itself cites both. The accurate statement is that the position is attested only within one local Nikolsburg controversy (1527–1528), with no attested community, practice, or teaching beyond it. That still falls short of the recurring pattern Article 21 requires, so the exclusion stands. *Fix:* reword "attested exactly once" and "no second instance... at all" to say this.
