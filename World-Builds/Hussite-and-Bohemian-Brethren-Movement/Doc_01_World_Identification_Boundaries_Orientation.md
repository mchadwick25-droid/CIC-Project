## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Hussite and Bohemian Brethren Movement.

**Short Description:** A university-based reform movement that began as Jan Hus's Czech-language preaching career at Prague's Bethlehem Chapel (1402), hardened into a national cause after his execution for heresy at the Council of Constance (1415), fought off five papal crusades under its own field commanders, and won a legal settlement — the 1436 Compactata — that no other medieval dissenting movement secured. Within this same window a stricter, pacifist offshoot, the Unity of the Brethren, formed at Kunvald in 1457. The window closes in 1517, before the movement's later, Reformation-era generations. — **Confidence: Documented** for the events named; **Widely Accepted** for their causal linkage into a single narrative arc.

**Core Identity:** Per the census's own framing, quoted directly: *"A functioning non-Roman national church a century before Luther."* This is a world of preachers who became a national cause, not (unlike Lollardy) a persecuted underground network, and not (unlike the Anabaptists) a movement defined by voluntary separation from state structures. Its formation logic runs through both a practical liturgical demand — that lay people, not only priests, receive the wine as well as the bread at communion (utraquism) — and a real institutional-political register: field armies, a papal-recognized legal settlement, and for a time a crowned Utraquist king (George of Poděbrady). — **Confidence: Widely Accepted**, carried forward from the census's own `relationsSummary` and Step 0 §2 A3.

**Distinctive Contribution:** This world's non-redundancy against Lollardy is not a claim of clean separation — the two are directly, textually connected, and this document engages that honestly rather than asserting otherwise. Per Schaff's own translator introduction to the vendored *De Ecclesia*, verified directly in the source file (`hus_de-ecclesia-the-church_schaff1915.txt`, lines 1514 and 1554): "Huss appropriated paragraph after paragraph from his [Wyclif], and transferred them often with little verbal change to his own pages," and "Never did a man owe more to mortal teacher than Huss did to John Wyclif." The vendored *Letters* corroborate a real, personal channel, not only a textual one: Hus's letter to the English Wycliffite Richard Wyche was read aloud in the Bethlehem Chapel (`hus_letters_workman-pope1904.txt`, lines 389, 2467–2853). Hus's own central ecclesiological treatise is, by its own translator's account, substantially Wyclif's doctrine in Hus's hand. — **Confidence: Documented** (direct textual citation, not inference).

What makes this a distinct world despite that dependency is not source-independence but outcome and register (Step 0 §3 B3–B4). Lollardy remained an underground, persecuted lay network with no institutional standing under the English crown. This movement fought and won: repeated crusades repelled in the field (1420s–30s), a formal legal settlement recognizing Utraquist practice (the 1436 Compactata), and — for a period under George of Poděbrady — a crowned head of state aligned with the Utraquist church. It is the institutional and political register this project's Era 6 batch does not otherwise have (Devotio Moderna is interior and devotional; Lollardy stayed underground). The two movements diverged sharply in what became of the same inherited doctrinal material. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the divergent outcomes; **Documented** for the 1436 Compactata as a real legal settlement.

This world is also distinct from — and chronologically prior to — the separate, already-registered census entry `czech-churches-last-century` (Atlas VI.24, Era 7, 1517–c. 1627, "The Czech Churches' Last Century (Utraquists and Unitas)"). That entry carries the same two bodies' (Utraquist church, Unity of the Brethren) *later* generations forward — the Kralice Bible, Comenius, the 1632/33 *Ratio Disciplinae*, and the Habsburg suppression of the 1620s. Worth noting as a structural, not merely narrative, signal that this boundary tracks a real discontinuity: the census itself reclassifies the successor entry into a different lane (`"Protestant & Evangelical"`, Era 7) from this world's own (`"Latin West & Catholicism"`, Era 6) — the project's own classification apparatus treats 1517 as a real seam, not only a convenient stopping point. — **Confidence: Documented** for the census's lane assignments as a fact of the project's own records; **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for what that seam represents historically.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed — **PENDING project-lead confirmation**, per Constitution Article 29, and not self-confirmed here. Noted for the record only: the census's own account traces this movement's remnant, after both this world's own 1517 close and the subsequent `czech-churches-last-century` entry's own c. 1627 close, to the Moravian Church, re-emerging at Herrnhut in Saxony in 1722 — meaning any living-tradition claim for *this* world's own material runs through two further historical hops this document does not reconstruct and is not the right document to adjudicate. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the Herrnhut lineage as a matter of record; **Status: PENDING.**

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Time Horizon: c. 1402–1517.** This is a specific, previously-ruled decision, defended here rather than re-derived:

- **Beginning point (1402), not 1415.** The window opens with Jan Hus's own preaching career, not his execution. Per the vendored *Letters* (`hus_letters_workman-pope1904.txt`, line 1440), Hus was appointed to the newly-founded Bethlehem Chapel on March 14, 1402, and preached there in Czech for over a decade before the Council of Constance burned him as a heretic in 1415. Moving the start back to 1402 is what brings Hus's own writings — the *Letters* (spanning June 1408 to his final letters of June–July 1415) and *De Ecclesia* (1413) — inside the window as this world's own-voice core, rather than treating them as background to a window that opens only at his death. This is the project lead's own explicit 2026-09-25 ruling (Step 0 §0), carried forward here, not re-argued from scratch. — **Confidence: Documented** for the 1402 appointment date; **Widely Accepted** for its significance as the movement's own generative starting point.
- **Ending point (1517).** The close keeps the Unity of the Brethren's *later* generations — including its 1632/33 institutional constitution, the *Ratio Disciplinae* (vendored, filed `role: context` in the corpus-map on exactly this basis) — with the separate `czech-churches-last-century` entry, rather than folding a further century of material into this world. No Unity of the Brethren tradition-role material from *inside* this window (1457–1517: a confession, a catechism, or the 1501 hymnbook itself) is currently vendored — a real sourcing gap named honestly in §7 below, not glossed over. — **Confidence: Documented** for the corpus-map's own filing rationale; carried forward per Step 0 §0/§4 item 5, not reopened here.

**Geographic Centers:** Prague and Bohemia, with Prague's Bethlehem Chapel and Charles University as this world's own institutional center of gravity, and southern Bohemia (Tábor, founded 1420) as a site of internal divergence discussed in §4 below. Bohemia only — a single, compact region, narrower even than Lollardy's England-only claim (Step 0 §3 B5). — **Confidence: Documented.**

**Cultural Environment:** A university-and-clerical milieu (Hus was rector of Prague's university as well as a preacher) that became, within a decade, a genuinely national and vernacular cause — Czech-language preaching at the Bethlehem Chapel reaching lay audiences directly, not only Latin disputation among clergy. This combination of an institutional-political register (crusades repelled, a legal settlement, a crowned Utraquist king) with a practical, popular liturgical demand (the lay chalice) is a register this project's Era 6 batch does not otherwise have. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted**, per Step 0 §3 B4.

**Historical Pressures:** Clerical wealth and moral corruption as an object of reform preaching; the practice of withholding the communion cup from lay people, which became the movement's own defining and unifying demand once adopted (see below); Rome's own claim to final ecclesiological authority, which Hus's own dispute directly contested — Hus's own quarrel with Rome is **ecclesiological** (can the church exist, and exercise real authority, without a corrupt or heretical pope), not creedal and not, for Hus personally, eucharistic-practical. Per Workman's own editorial note in the vendored *Letters* (Step 0 §2 A1, citing p. 177): "Hitherto, Hus had taken little interest in the matter [of the lay chalice] — in fact, in his *De Coena Domini*, written at a later date, he still practically concedes the Roman position"; he committed to it only late, from prison. Utraquism became the movement's defining practical demand only after Jakoubek of Stříbro introduced the lay chalice in the summer of 1414 (per the vendored *Letters*, corroborating Step 0's account at lines 8807 ff.), not from Hus's own original personal concern. — **Confidence: Documented** for the Workman citation; **Widely Accepted** for the ecclesiological-not-eucharistic characterization of Hus's own dispute.

**Historical Catalysts:** Hus's 1415 execution at Constance, which turned a university reform movement into a national one; the Four Articles of Prague (1420), the movement's programmatic statement; Utraquism (communion in both kinds), adopted 1414 and made the movement's emblem; the Compactata (1436), the legal settlement recognizing Utraquist practice — a settlement no other medieval dissenting movement secured. These four catalysts are attested directly in Hus's own voice (the 1415 execution and its build-up, via the *Letters*) and in the vendored context-role secondary histories (the Four Articles, Utraquism's later practice, and the Compactata specifically — Gillett's *Life and Times of Huss*, Vol. II, discusses the Compactata and the 1436–37 Council/Diet at Iglau directly, e.g. `gillett_life-and-times-of-huss-v2_1871.txt` at and around lines 20892, 21825, 22171). No tradition-role primary source currently vendored speaks in the movement's own voice for the Four Articles or the Compactata themselves (Step 0 §3 B1) — this document relies on the context-role secondary histories for those specific events, and names that reliance rather than presenting it as primary-sourced. — **Confidence: Documented** for the events; **Widely Accepted, secondary-sourced** for their narration in this document.

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** An unusual combination, for this project's portfolio, of a university/clerical formation register (Latin disputation, ecclesiological treatise, canon-law argument — Hus's own *De Ecclesia*) with a vernacular, popular-preaching register aimed directly at lay formation (Czech-language preaching at the Bethlehem Chapel) and an institutional-political register (field command, legal settlement, crowned monarchy). No other confirmed or candidate world in this project's Era 6 batch combines all three. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted**, per Step 0 §3 B2/B4.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Named here as candidates for Step 4 testing, not as confirmed gravities: ecclesiological authority exercised independent of, and in judgment upon, a corrupt or heretical pope; utraquism as an identity-defining practical demand; vernacular preaching and Scripture as the vehicle of lay formation; the conviction that a settlement with legitimate secular and ecclesiastical authority is worth fighting for and can be won, rather than only survived underground. — **Confidence: Inferential-Thin** — these are Step 1 candidates, not Doc_04 findings.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** **Utraquism — communion in both kinds — is the strongest and most specific recurring pattern**, attested as the movement's own emblem across its whole later history (from Jakoubek's 1414 introduction through the Compactata's own terms). Vernacular preaching at a single, named, physical site (the Bethlehem Chapel) is a second recurring pattern of formation and belonging. A third, more contested pattern of authority: appeal past ecclesiastical hierarchy directly to Christ — Hus's own recorded appeal language ("I have appealed to Christ," per Step 0 §4 item 4, in the vendored *Letters*) as a distinctive way of locating final authority when the institutional church's own authority is in dispute. — **Confidence: Documented** for utraquism and the Bethlehem Chapel; **Widely Accepted** for the appeal-to-Christ pattern as recurring rather than a single rhetorical instance.
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** From **Lollardy**: direct textual dependence (§1 above) but sharply divergent institutional outcome — underground and persecuted versus a legally-recognized national church; a different register (institutional-political, not only underground-devotional). From **`czech-churches-last-century`**: chronological sequence, not substantive difference in kind — that entry carries this same movement's own later generations (the Kralice Bible, Comenius, the 1632/33 *Ratio Disciplinae*, the Habsburg suppression) forward from 1517, and is classified in a different project lane (Protestant & Evangelical, Era 7) from this world's own (Latin West & Catholicism, Era 6). This world ends at the world-defining moment; that one begins there. — **Confidence: Documented** for both comparisons' factual basis; **Widely Accepted** for the framing of "sequence, not kind" for the `czech-churches-last-century` boundary.

## 4. Strand Determination (Article 21)

Per Constitution Article 21, strand is "a finding, never a presupposed universal schema," accountable to evidence, and never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality. It is also not a label for sequential or causal development — a temporal-development finding (Article 15) is a different question from whether a genuinely *simultaneous* divergence in authority-structure or ecological orientation exists within the world. This determination applies that test directly, twice, because this world's own evidence raises it twice.

### 4.1 Finding: not strand-singular. Two internal strands identified within the 1402–1517 window.

- **Strand A — Utraquist/mainstream Hussite.** Authority and belonging organized around the parish-based Utraquist church: lay chalice as the defining practice, working (if contested) relations with existing ecclesiastical and secular structures, culminating in the 1436 Compactata's legal recognition and, for a period, a crowned Utraquist king (George of Poděbrady). This strand persists to the window's own 1517 close and beyond (into `czech-churches-last-century`). — **Confidence: Documented**, per the census's own account and the vendored secondary histories (Gillett; Lützow).
- **Strand B — Unity of the Brethren.** Founded at Kunvald in 1457 under Gregory the Patriarch, under the direct doctrinal influence of the layman Petr Chelčický. Evidenced in the vendored context-role history *Bohemia: An Historical Sketch* (Lützow, 1920, `lutzow_bohemia-historical-sketch_1920.txt`, pp. 182–183, lines 9990–10060 in the source file): Chelčický is named there as "the intellectual originator of the 'Unity' of the Bohemian Brethren," holding "his most original doctrine, the one most completely opposed to the spirit of his age — that is, his belief in the absolute and unconditional sinfulness of bloodshed." This is a distinct ground of authority and belonging from Strand A's own — deliberate, principled pacifism and a stricter separatist discipline, not a variant of parish-based Utraquist practice — evidenced as simultaneous with, not merely subsequent to, Strand A: both bodies coexisted from 1457 through 1517 and beyond, and the same Lützow source records Chelčický's own explicit doctrinal distance from both the Taborites and (on the Eucharist specifically) from Hus, considering Wyclif rather than Hus his own teacher on that point. — **Confidence: Documented** for Chelčický's own recorded doctrine and the 1457 founding; **Widely Accepted** for characterizing this as a genuinely distinct authority-ground rather than a variant of Strand A.

**Real sourcing limit, named rather than smoothed over:** no Unity of the Brethren tradition-role primary text from inside this window (a confession, catechism, or the 1501 hymnbook itself) is currently vendored (Step 0 §3 B1). Strand B is evidenced here through context-role secondary history (Lützow) and the census's own account, not through the Unity's own voice. This is a genuine gap for Doc_02 to carry forward, not a reason to withhold the strand finding itself — Article 21 tests for a real, evidenced divergence, and the divergence itself is well-attested even though the Unity's own primary voice is not yet vendored.

### 4.2 Taborite radicalism: a genuine Article-21-qualifying divergence, but not treated as an internal strand of this world — flagged, not silently decided

The census carries **Taborite Radicalism** as its own, independently registered entry — Atlas ID V.11, 1420–1452, with its own dedicated primary sourcing (Taborite manifestos and chiliast articles; Nicholas of Pelhřimov's own confession and chronicle as "the movement's own bishop defending Taborite doctrine"; Jan Příbram's *The Lives of the Taborite Priests* as an intra-Hussite heresiology). On the documentary record this radical wing — founded 1420, holding property in common for a time under its own elected bishop, stripping worship to what it found in Scripture (no vestments, no purgatory, no cult of saints), fielding the wagon-fort armies that broke crusade after crusade — coexisted with, and was ultimately destroyed by, the mainstream Utraquist/Hussite party itself, at Lipány in 1434. This is a genuinely *simultaneous* divergence in authority-ground and ecological orientation (an elected, chiliastic, communal-property church versus a parish-based, legally-negotiating one), resolved by internal conflict rather than natural succession — precisely the kind of evidence Article 21 asks for, and precisely not a case of one phase causally yielding to the next.

That said, this document does **not** treat Taborite radicalism as an internal strand of this world's own downstream construction (Doc_02 onward), for a reason external to this world's own ecology: the project's own census architecture has already registered it as a separate, independently-built world (its own Atlas ID, its own window, its own dedicated primary-source track). Treating it as *also* an internal strand here would mean the same body of evidence is claimed by two separate world-builds at once, without that being a decision this document is positioned to make. Worth naming plainly: Step 0 itself, at §4 item 1, refers to "the movement's other strands (Taborites, Pikarts, the early Unity)" when naming what a full floor check should still cover — language that treats Taborite radicalism as this world's own strand, in some tension with the census's separate-world registration. **This document does not resolve that tension.** It records the honest state of the evidence (§4.1's test is met on the merits) and flags the scoping question — internal strand of V.6, or genuinely separate world V.11, or (least likely, given the real divergence found) both — as a portfolio-level classification question for the project lead, not a Doc_01 finding this thread can close alone (see §7 below).

**Governing consequence for later steps:** per the Framework, cross-strand gravity testing for Strands A and B occurs formally at Step 4. Taborite material is treated as external comparative context for this world's own construction unless and until the project lead redirects.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation for Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8.

**What generated this world:** Jan Hus's own 1402 appointment to preach in Czech at the newly-founded Bethlehem Chapel, and his sustained, decade-long preaching career there against clerical corruption — a generative act of vernacular, lay-facing reform preaching from within, not outside, an existing university and ecclesiastical structure. — **Confidence: Documented.**

**What larger world was it embedded in:** Western Latin Christendom, pre-Reformation — a still-unified, Rome-centered ecclesiastical order, a century before Luther, in which a national reform movement winning legal recognition from that same order (rather than breaking from it entirely or being permanently suppressed) was, per the census's own framing, without real precedent. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted.**

**What was it responding to:** Clerical wealth and corruption, as an object of reform preaching from Hus's own earliest Bethlehem Chapel years; and, once adopted in 1414, the practice of withholding the communion cup from lay people — a practical grievance that became, in Jakoubek's and then the wider movement's hands, the demand around which a diffuse reform current organized into a single, identifiable cause. — **Confidence: Documented.**

**What was it refusing:** Papal authority claims where a pope is corrupt or (in the movement's own later reckoning) heretical — Hus's own ecclesiological dispute, per Step 0 §2 A1, is precisely a refusal of unconditional obedience to defective ecclesiastical office, not a creedal or eucharistic-practical refusal on his own part. At the movement's own level (distinct from Hus's personal position), a refusal of the Roman sacramental practice restricting the cup to clergy — the utraquist refusal that became this world's own emblem. — **Confidence: Documented** for Hus's own ecclesiological refusal; **Widely Accepted** for utraquism as the movement's own, broader refusal.

**Preliminary six-cell sketch (Layer 1 only — Historical Event; Layers 2 and 3 belong to Doc_08's full compilation, not this preliminary pass):**

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits:** Directly and substantially, John Wyclif's own ecclesiology — per Schaff's own translator judgment (§1 above), Hus's central treatise "appropriated paragraph after paragraph" from Wyclif's writing, and the vendored *Letters* record a real personal channel (the Wyche correspondence) alongside the textual one. This is not a soft or general influence claim; it is a documented, substantial textual dependency that this document states plainly rather than minimizing. What this world's own actors did with that inheritance — organizing it into a popular, vernacular, and ultimately state-recognized movement — is what makes it a distinct world despite the dependency, not a claim that the dependency is small. — **Confidence: Documented.**

**What this world transmits forward:** Within this world's own window, to its two internal strands' own later histories: the Utraquist church continues as one of Bohemia's non-Roman churches into the 16th and early 17th centuries (per the census's own account, producing the Kralice Bible and, in Comenius, "a last bishop who became one of Europe's great educational reformers"), and the Unity of the Brethren likewise continues, both carried forward by the separate `czech-churches-last-century` entry (1517–c. 1627) rather than by this document. Beyond both windows' own close, the census records a further legacy: remnants of the Unity survived underground after the Habsburg suppression of the 1620s and resurfaced in 1722 at Herrnhut in Saxony as the Moravian Church, which still exists today. None of this later history is this document's own to reconstruct; it is named here for orientation, consistent with how the ijc exemplar (§6) treats forward transmission it does not itself develop. Living-heir mapping, per Article 28/29 discipline, is stated honestly as **pending** (§1 above) — this document names the candidate continuity chain without asserting the confirmation that chain would require. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the transmission facts as the census records them; **Status: PENDING** for any living-tradition confirmation.
