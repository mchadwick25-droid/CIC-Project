**Reviewer:** independent adversarial review agent (Opus), 2026-09-25, per `cic-build-cycle` discipline.

## Verdict

**Substantial revision needed.** The Section A result (clears the floor) is itself correct, but four findings each change a claim's substance, a sourcing conclusion, or a scope boundary.

## Findings

### Substantial

**S1. A1's content claim is false, and "host-verified" overstates what was checked.** A1 says Trent's canons "explicitly reaffirm the Nicene Creed and Chalcedonian Christology by name." Session III (Waterworth, c. lines 12320–12375) recites the full creed but calls it only "the Symbol of faith which the holy Roman Church makes use of" — "Nicaea" appears solely in a footnote citation. "Chalcedon" appears in the decrees exactly once, as a disciplinary citation on ordination (Session XXIII, ch. XVI) — never a doctrinal reaffirmation by name. The "host-verified" label reflects only a rights/metadata check, not a text read. **What stands:** the A1 verdict itself — the Session III creed recital matches Article 4's five commitments almost clause for clause, better evidence than the false claim as written. (This exact error was already flagged independently by the Society of Jesus review, which this document's own sibling repeats.)

**S2. A1/A5/Section A conclusion misdescribe the floor itself.** The document calls Trent "the reference point every other candidate in this batch is measured against" and "the institutional source of the floor test's own 16th-century Catholic articulation." But Article 4's floor is drawn from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (381, ELLC text, filioque-free) and states the test is "of belief, not institutional submission to any council's authority." Trent's own creed includes the filioque ("proceedeth from the Father and the Son," Waterworth c. line 12366) — a different text from the floor's own wording, not even identical to it. Trent must clear Article 4 like any other candidate (which it does, per S1's corrected evidence) — framing it as the batch's own doctrinal yardstick imports a Catholic-normative lens into a portfolio that includes built Protestant worlds. "No floor complication exists or could exist" also overclaims.

**S3. The Pole half of the "no personal voice" gap is false.** B1/B2/the Tier rationale/§4 item 1 all rest on "no PD English translation" of Pole's own writing existing "anywhere," described as "checked directly, not assumed." A direct archive.org creator search for "Pole, Reginald" returns *The Seditious... Oration of Cardinal Pole* (1560), English translation by Fabian Wythers — identifier `bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_the-seditious-oration_pole-reginald_1560`, no access restriction, ~10K words, confirmed as Pole's own oration to the Emperor in English (the same EEBO collection Sarpi and Barlow already come from — Pole's own words inside a hostile Protestant frame, the same pattern as Barlow). "Survives only in Latin" is also independently wrong — Pole's correspondence survives in Italian and English too, not only Latin. Pole's own role is also undersold: the Waterworth essay (c. line 3664) records that the Session II legates' exhortation was "composed by Cardinal Pole" — one of the three presiding legates at Trent's opening, not merely an "implementing figure." (Two further unverified leads: *A Treatie of Justification*, Louvain 1569 — PD text, but the only archive.org scan is a 1967, access-restricted facsimile; *Nine Historical Letters... by Reginald Pole*, 1871, metadata unresolved.)

**S4. The census-named liturgical books are silently dropped, and PD English versions exist.** The census names "the reformed liturgical books" (Missal, Breviary) alongside the Catechism; the dossier claims these were "checked and found to have no PD English translation," but its own §4 has no liturgical row to support that. Direct archive.org search found: Bute's English *Roman Breviary... reformed by order of the Holy Oecumenical Council of Trent* (1908 printing, `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`, though this text reflects later revision through Leo XIII — a caveat); *The Roman Missal Translated into the English Language for the Use of the Laity* (1843, `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`); several 19th-c. Holy Week/laity missals. Step 0 never assesses this strand — its "institutional/doctrinal/narrative-history... no pastoral or interior voice at all" framing ignores the world's own worship register, changing both the ecological conclusion and §4 item 1's binding framing.

### Moderate

- **M1.** The Borromeo claim overstates the dossier — Step 0 drops the dossier's own disclosed lead (a possible 1857 Wigley translation, "closed as not found, not assumed available") and upgrades it to "no PD English translation anywhere." Own archive.org search confirms no PD English Borromeo primary text was found, so the narrower claim holds, but the dossier's own caveat should be restored.
- **M2.** B5's "broadest single claimed jurisdiction in this batch" is false against the census (the Jesuits' own "Rome, then the world" claim is broader) — every sibling B5 claims the broadest reach, an internal batch contradiction.
- **M3.** B2's Jesuit contrast ("missing institutional document, the Constitutions") is stale — per the Society of Jesus review, the 1606 Latin Constitutions are now vendored; only the missing PD English translation is still true.
- **M4.** The Imperial Juridical Christianity precedent doesn't actually support "no personal voice at all" — `worlds/ijc/Doc_02` rests heavily on first-person voices (Eusebius, Ambrose's letters and *Sermo contra Auxentium*); it had no single founder, but it did not lack personal voice.
- **M5.** "Three widely separated sessions" (§1) is wrong — Trent had 25 sessions across three periods; the census's own "periods" wording is correct.
- **M6.** The window claim ("popes from Paul III... through the early 17th century") is looser than the actual 1517–1650 census/dossier window, leaving unaddressed the pre-Paul-III Luther response and 1600–1650 material (Propaganda Fide 1622, the Index, the Roman Inquisition, new active orders) and the `continuesAs: catholic-church-ancien-regime` boundary.
- **M7.** Sarpi is misdescribed as a "critical Venetian outsider" — he was a Servite friar and Catholic priest who never left the Church, an anti-Curial critic from inside it, publishing under a pseudonym in London (1619); the Optatus (rival-communion opponent) analogy is the wrong model, though the hostile/contested register itself is correctly flagged.
- **M8.** §0's batch arithmetic is internally inconsistent ("fifth candidate... of an original set of four" gives five, contradicting the "one of six"/"other five" language used elsewhere), and the detailed selection-rationale narrative has no findable record in `Ministry/`, `worlds/`, or `World-Builds/` — same class of unverifiable attribution flagged in the Society of Jesus review.
- **M9.** The scale claim ("several hundred thousand words... confirmed") sits inconsistently beside the boilerplate "no figure is word-extracted" caveat; actual raw counts are available (~1.22M words across all four files, though inflated by OCR noise and Latin columns).
- **M10.** The native/implementing split claimed in B3/§4 item 3 isn't reflected in the corpus-map — the Tridentine corpus-map carries the Society of Jesus's own Waterworth note verbatim (`role: context`, `provisional`), so neither world currently holds the text as "native." A corpus-map defect, flagged (also independently caught by the Society of Jesus review), not fixed here.

### Attribution/registry checks (the Barlow question)

Regnans in Excelsis is genuinely printed in parallel English/Latin columns (c. lines 2230–2600) — confirmed directly. Filing under `barlow` is the right call since Barlow authored the polemic, but the corpus-map's own stated reason ("compiler/printer slug") is imprecise — Barlow is the polemic's author, not its printer or compiler, and the `boero` precedent it cites is itself a biographer-author, not a compiler; the result is right, the reasoning is loose. Paul III's bull appears to be Latin-only in the file (sampled, moderate confidence) — the file header, REGISTRY.yaml, and corpus-map all claim "English and Latin" for both bulls, worth re-checking. The Barlow row's `locus: whole text` is too broad for a 117K-word file that's mostly hostile polemic around a short embedded bull text — the locus should narrow to the bull text itself. **REGISTRY.yaml has duplicate entries for the Catechism, Sarpi, and Barlow files** (approx. lines 2784–2812 and again at 2920–2948) — the same defect class independently confirmed by the Lollardy review for its own seven entries.

### Confirmed accurate

Article 4 quoted verbatim, matching the Constitution file word for word. Catechism facts (commissioned by Trent, published under Pius V 1566, 1923 translation PD by date). Waterworth's 1848 preface / c.1888 printing. Sarpi 1676 and Barlow 1681 PD by date; Regnans in Excelsis dates to 1570. Paul III convened the Council. A3's anathema claim holds (Session VI sola-fide canons present). Census fields for VI.22 correct. Pallavicino/Theiner closures consistent with the dossier.
