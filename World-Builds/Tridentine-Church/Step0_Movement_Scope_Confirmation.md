### A1 — The floor: clears, on corrected evidence and a corrected relationship to the floor test itself

**Correction (Revision 2): "Trent's own canons explicitly reaffirm the Nicene Creed and Chalcedonian Christology by name" is false** — caught on independent review, the identical error the Society of Jesus sibling document also made. Session III of the vendored Waterworth Canons/Decrees (c. lines 12320–12375) recites the full Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed but calls it only "the Symbol of faith which the holy Roman Church makes use of" — "Nicaea" appears solely in a footnote citation. "Chalcedon" appears in the decrees exactly once, as a disciplinary citation on ordination (Session XXIII, ch. XVI), never a doctrinal reaffirmation by name. The corrected evidence is better than the false claim: the Session III creed recital matches Article 4's five commitments almost clause for clause.

**Correction (Revision 2): this candidate is not "the reference point every other candidate in this batch is measured against."** Article 4's own floor is drawn from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (381, filioque-free) and tests "belief, not institutional submission to any council's authority." Trent's own Session III creed recital includes the filioque ("proceedeth from the Father and the Son") — a different text from the floor's own wording, not identical to it. Trent must clear Article 4 like any other candidate (which it does, on the corrected evidence above) — treating it as the batch's own doctrinal yardstick would import a Catholic-normative lens into a portfolio that includes built Protestant worlds. "No floor complication exists or could exist" also overclaimed; corrected to a considered clearance like every other candidate's.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: strong on doctrine and institutional narrative, with a real personal-voice gap narrower than previously described

Per the dossier, directly host-verified: the Council's own Canons and Decrees (Waterworth, preface 1848); the Roman Catechism (McHugh & Callan, 1923); Paolo Sarpi's *History of the Council of Trent* (English ed. 1676 — see §4 item 2 for a corrected characterization); and Thomas Barlow's *Brutum Fulmen* (1681), which prints Pius V's own bull *Regnans in Excelsis* in English and Latin. Word counts are now available directly (~1.22M raw OCR words across all four files, inflated by OCR noise and Latin columns), not the "no figure word-extracted" boilerplate Revision 1 carried.

**Correction (Revision 2): the "no PD English translation exists anywhere" claim for Pole is false.** Revision 1 said Pole's own writing "survives only in Latin" and that no PD English translation "exists" for either implementing figure. An archive.org creator search for "Pole, Reginald" turns up *The Seditious... Oration of Cardinal Pole* (1560), English translation by Fabian Wythers — item `bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_the-seditious-oration_pole-reginald_1560`, no access restriction, confirmed as Pole's own oration to the Emperor in English (the same EEBO collection Sarpi and Barlow already come from — Pole's own words inside a hostile Protestant frame, like Barlow). This is a verified lead, **not yet vendored** — real acquisition work for whoever takes this candidate into Doc_02, but the "exists nowhere" framing is corrected now. "Survives only in Latin" was also independently wrong — Pole's correspondence survives in Italian and English too. Pole's own role is undersold as merely "implementing": per the Waterworth essay (c. line 3664), the Session II legates' exhortation was "composed by Cardinal Pole" — one of the three presiding legates at Trent's opening.

Borromeo's own gap stands, with a caveat restored: no PD English translation of his own writing was found this pass either, but a possible 1857 London (Dolman) translation by G.J. Wigley, referenced in bibliographies, was not located on archive.org — "closed as not found, not assumed available" (the dossier's own original caveat, dropped in Revision 1's "no PD English translation anywhere" overclaim).

**Correction (Revision 2): the census-named liturgical books were never assessed, and PD English versions exist.** The census names "the reformed liturgical books" alongside the Catechism; Revision 1 never engaged this strand at all. Direct archive.org search found real PD leads, **not yet vendored**: Bute's English *Roman Breviary... reformed by order of the Holy Oecumenical Council of Trent* (1908 printing, though reflecting later revision through Leo XIII — a caveat) and *The Roman Missal Translated into the English Language for the Use of the Laity* (1843). See §3 B2 for how this changes the ecological picture.
