## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas VI.22, "The Tridentine Church," 1517–1650, Rome and Latin Christendom. The Council of Trent itself (1545–1563, in three widely separated sessions) and the institutional, doctrinal, and disciplinary reform of the wider Catholic Church that followed from it — popes from Paul III (who convened the Council) through the early 17th century, and the Council's own decrees, canons, and catechism as the movement's own central texts.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: strong on doctrine and institutional narrative, genuinely thin on personal/pastoral voice — both disclosed honestly

Per the dossier, directly host-verified: the Council's own Canons and Decrees (Waterworth, preface 1848); the Roman Catechism, commissioned by Trent and promulgated under Pius V (McHugh & Callan, 1923); Paolo Sarpi's contemporary *History of the Council of Trent* (English ed. 1676 — a hostile, critical Venetian account, disclosed as needing an etic/contested-source register rather than treated as this world's own native voice); and Thomas Barlow's *Brutum Fulmen* (1681), which prints Pius V's own bull *Regnans in Excelsis* in English. **Same methodological caveat as every sibling document: no figure below is word-extracted from vendored XML.** Several hundred thousand words available and confirmed — comparable to or larger than this project's existing built worlds — but see §3 B2 for the real ecological gap this scale doesn't resolve.

**A real, disclosed gap, checked directly and not assumed: no PD English translation exists for either of two major implementing figures' own primary voices.** Cardinal Reginald Pole's own letters survive only in Latin (Querini, 1744–57); Carlo Borromeo's own writings (his pastoral letters, sermons, the *Instructiones Fabricae*) have no PD English translation anywhere — the only two English translations found (Voelker 1976, Cihak & Santogrossi 2017) are both access-restricted and in copyright.

### B2 — Ecology

**This candidate's real gravity risk, stated plainly rather than smoothed over: it is the inverse of the Society of Jesus's own problem (§3 B1 of that sibling document).** Where the Jesuits have a strong personal-formation voice (Ignatius) but a missing institutional document (the Constitutions), this candidate has strong institutional/doctrinal/narrative-history material (the Canons, the Catechism, Sarpi's narrative) but no confirmed personal, pastoral, or interior voice at all — neither Pole nor Borromeo speaks in his own words anywhere in the available PD corpus. Doc_02 should name this as this world's own distinctive ecological shape, not attempt to paper over it with secondary paraphrase presented as primary voice.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no overlap, over a millennium's separation. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** a real, disclosed, non-disqualifying source overlap with the Society of Jesus (§2 A3 of that sibling document and here) — both would likely cite the Waterworth Canons/Decrees, this candidate with native/foundational weight (the body that convened and enacted them), the Jesuits with implementing/corroborating weight. Also a real, narrower overlap risk with the separate, not-yet-researched Borromean Milan (VI.12) census entry, lowered rather than raised by this pass's own finding: since Borromeo's own primary voice has no PD English translation at all, neither candidate can claim him as a native-voice source, and the real VI.22/VI.12 distinction is one of scale (whole-church versus one diocese) rather than competing sourced material. No figure overlap with Lutheran Wittenberg, the Reformed cities, the Anabaptists, or Lollardy, though see §2 A3 for the direct doctrinal-rival relationship with the first two.

### B4 — Audiences

The institutional counterweight to every other candidate's own more personal-voice-driven appeal in this batch — a distinct pull for anyone approaching the Reformation from the Catholic Church's own self-understanding and reform, rather than from any one reformer's own biography.

### B5 — Scale

Rome and Latin Christendom — the broadest single claimed jurisdiction of any candidate in this batch, reflecting the Council's own universal (for the Latin church) rather than regional scope.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A without qualification, the institutional source of the floor test's own 16th-century Catholic articulation; clears Sourcing strongly on doctrine and institutional narrative, with a real, disclosed gap on personal/pastoral voice (no Pole, no Borromeo in English) named as this world's own distinctive ecological shape rather than smoothed over; one real, non-disqualifying source-overlap item versus the Society of Jesus, already flagged for whichever Doc_02 runs first; broadest claimed jurisdiction in the batch.

**Tier 1 — Strong seed, one binding condition, ecological rather than evidentiary.** The missing personal/pastoral voice is a real limitation Doc_02 must name plainly, not a disqualifying complication — the institutional and doctrinal register available is itself a legitimate, distinctive world-shape, the same way this project's own Imperial Juridical Christianity world builds around institutional/legal material rather than a single founder's personal voice.
