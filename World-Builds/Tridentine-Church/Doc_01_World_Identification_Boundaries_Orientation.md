## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Tridentine Church.

**Short Description:** The Counter-Reformation Catholic Church as reformed, defined, and disciplined by the Council of Trent (1545–63) and its immediate institutional implementation across Rome and Latin Christendom — the conciliar decrees and canons, the Roman Catechism they produced, the reformed liturgical books, and the papal and episcopal machinery that carried the Council's program into parish life.

**Core Identity:** This is a whole-church world, not one order or movement within it. Its characteristic material is conciliar and catechetical: canons that settle contested doctrine by anathematizing its denial, decrees that reform clerical discipline and diocesan structure, and a catechism written expressly so that parish priests could teach what the Council had settled to ordinary believers. Where the Society of Jesus (a sibling Era VII candidate, built separately) is the world of one religious order's own founding and formation logic, this world is the institutional and doctrinal charter that order — among several — went on to implement. — **Confidence: Documented** that Trent produced binding conciliar canons, decrees, and a commissioned catechism (Waterworth *Canons and Decrees*; McHugh & Callan *Roman Catechism*, both vendored and role: tradition per the corpus-map); **Widely Accepted** for the periodization "Counter-Reformation Catholic Church" as the world's own organizing frame.

**Distinctive Contribution — versus the Society of Jesus specifically.** The corpus-map has already settled the native/implementing split between these two worlds, not as an open question this document reopens but as a finding it inherits and applies (Step 0 §4 item 4; §3 B3): Waterworth's *Canons and Decrees* is role **tradition** for this world — "this world's own defining conciliar voice" — and role **context** for the Society of Jesus, where the same text is held as the Jesuits' own implementation of a program that originated elsewhere, not the Society's own composed voice. This world is the deliberative and legislative body: the twenty-five sessions across three periods (1545–47, 1551–52, 1562–63) that debated, decided, and anathematized; the Society of Jesus is one executing order among several (alongside, notably, episcopal reformers such as Carlo Borromeo in Milan) that took Trent's settled program and enacted it in schools, missions, and confessionals. The distinction is institutional register, not chronological sequence or doctrinal disagreement — both worlds affirm the same Tridentine settlement; they occupy different positions relative to its authorship. — **Confidence: Documented** for the corpus-map role-split as a settled cataloguing fact; **Widely Accepted** for the historical native/implementing relationship it reflects.

**Distinctive Contribution — versus prior Catholic-world entries in the portfolio.** The current confirmed-world census, so far as this document can verify it, contains no earlier built Catholic world with which this candidate could overlap: the portfolio's confirmed worlds through Chalcedon (451 CE) predate this world's own 1517–1650 window by over a millennium, and the only other Catholic-adjacent candidate in this same Era VII batch is the Society of Jesus, addressed above. A separate, narrower census entry exists for Borromean Milan (VI.12, one diocese's own implementation) — not yet researched or built — which this world's own Step 0 explicitly declines to claim (Step 0 §0, §4 item 5). This document does not reopen that boundary; see §3 and §6 below for what it inherits from Step 0 on this point. — **Confidence: Documented** for the absence of temporal overlap with confirmed prior worlds; **Widely Accepted** for the VI.22/VI.12 scale distinction as Step 0 and the dossier state it (whole-church versus one diocese).

**Living Tradition Status: PENDING project-lead confirmation — not self-confirmed here.** The Roman Catholic Church is, without serious scholarly dispute, a still-living, present-day tradition of well over a billion adherents worldwide, with the papacy, the Roman Curia, and the diocesan/parish structure this world reconstructs continuing in direct, traceable institutional descent to the present day. That continuity is, if anything, more immediate and more visible than any other confirmed or candidate world in this portfolio's Living Tradition determinations to date — which makes the Article 28/29 distinction between the *historical formation world* (the Counter-Reformation Church of 1517–1650, as this document and its successors reconstruct it) and the *present-day Roman Catholic Church's own self-understanding* (which belongs to that living community and is not this project's to define) a matter requiring particular care, not less care because the continuity is obvious. Per Constitution Article 29 and this project's established practice, Living Tradition Status confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform on its own behalf. **Status: PENDING.** — **Confidence: Documented** that the Roman Catholic Church is a living, present-day tradition; the confirmation act itself is a governance step, not a historical claim, and is recorded here as outstanding rather than assumed.

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Time Horizon:** Step 0's own settled window is **1517–1650**, matching the census entry (`catholic-church-ancien-regime`) and the dossier — looser on both ends than the Council of Trent's own 1545–63 span, and deliberately so, given the vendored material this world's corpus now holds (Step 0 §1). This document adopts that window rather than narrowing it to Trent's own dates, because the vendored corpus itself extends past them in both directions: Bellarmine's genuine devotional works (late 16th/early 17th century), Borromeo's Milan *Acta* (1560s–80s, printed 1599), Pius V's apostolic letters (pontificate 1566–72, printed 1640), and Pius V's bull *Regnans in Excelsis* (1570) all postdate Trent's 1563 close, while the window's own 1517 start (Luther's 95 Theses) and its coverage of Leo X's and Clement VII's pre-Paul-III response to Luther (Step 0 §1) predate the Council's 1545 opening.

- **Beginning point (1517), defended.** 1517 marks Luther's 95 Theses and the opening of the controversy Trent exists to answer — a real ecological rupture in the same sense IJC's Doc_01 defends 312 CE: before 1517, the Catholic Church faced no organized, doctrinally systematic challenge of the kind Trent's own canons anathematize clause by clause; after it, the entire conciliar and disciplinary program this world reconstructs becomes necessary. Step 0 itself narrows its own *described* scope to Paul III (1534 accession) through the early 17th century while naming the full 1517–1650 census window as broader still (Step 0 §1; §4 item 7) — this document adopts the full window as the world's defended boundary, consistent with Step 0's own disclosure that the narrower description was incidental, not an argued boundary revision. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for 1517 as the conventional opening date of the Reformation controversy Trent answers; **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for adopting it, rather than 1545, as this world's own defended beginning.
- **Ending point (1650), defended provisionally.** The window's 1650 close reaches past this document's currently best-evidenced material: the richest vendored sourcing clusters between 1545 and 1599 (Trent's own sessions; the Roman Catechism, 1566; Borromeo's Milan reforms, printed 1599; Pius V's bull, 1570), with the 1600–1650 portion of the window — Propaganda Fide (1622), the developed Index and Roman Inquisition, the era's new active orders — named by Step 0 as **not yet engaged** by any vendored source (Step 0 §1). This document does not narrow the window on that basis; it flags the imbalance for Doc_02, which is the document responsible for testing whether the corpus actually supports reconstruction across the full span or only its earlier two-thirds. — **Confidence: Documented** that the window's later third is currently unevidenced in the vendored corpus; **Inferential-Thin** for any claim about what a fuller 1600–1650 corpus would show.

**Geographic Centers:**

- **Trent** — the Alpine prince-bishopric (in the Holy Roman Empire, not papal territory) chosen as a compromise site acceptable to the Empire, where the Council's twenty-five sessions across three widely separated periods (1545–47, 1551–52, 1562–63) actually convened, debated, and enacted canon and decree.
- **Rome** — the implementing center: the papacy (Paul III, who convened the Council, through Pius IV, who closed it, and Pius V, whose pontificate promulgated the Roman Catechism and the reformed liturgical books, and issued *Regnans in Excelsis*) and the Curia that carried Trent's decrees into binding, empire-wide instruments.
- **Milan** — named here because the vendored corpus gives it primary-voice weight (Borromeo's own *Acta Ecclesiae Mediolanensis*, 1599 Latin printing, role: tradition), evidencing one archdiocese's own concrete implementation of the Tridentine episcopal-reform program; not claimed as this world's own separate jurisdiction (see §6 on the Borromean Milan boundary).

— **Confidence: Documented** for Trent's own conciliar dates and sessions and for Rome's implementing role; **Widely Accepted** for Milan's status as a documented implementation case within this world rather than a separate world.

**Cultural Environment:** Latin Christendom's educated, clerical, and increasingly print-literate culture of the mid-16th through mid-17th century — conciliar Latin as the language of binding doctrine and law; vernacular catechesis, by contrast, as the medium Trent's own Roman Catechism was designed to enable at the parish level (McHugh & Callan, 1923 English translation of a text meant for priests to teach from, not for lay reading in the original). This world's formation logic runs through office and instrument — conciliar canon, papal bull, diocesan synodal act, catechism — in a register closer to IJC's own "office-holders, not congregants" framing than to a devotional or ascetic world's, though with a genuine catechetical and liturgical register (the Roman Catechism; the Missal; the Breviary) that IJC's own world largely lacks. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted.**

**Historical Pressures:**

- The Reformation's direct doctrinal and disciplinary challenge — Lutheran and Reformed teaching on justification, the sacraments, scriptural authority, and church governance, each of which Trent's own canons name and anathematize (see §5 below for the precise content, verified against the vendored canons rather than assumed from popular narrative).
- A genuine internal-reform pressure that predates and is not reducible to the Lutheran challenge: clerical discipline, episcopal residency, and the training of parish clergy were recognized problems inside the Catholic Church before 1517, and Trent's own disciplinary decrees (on ordination, residency, and the founding of seminaries) address them as such, not merely as a reactive answer to Protestant critique. Step 0 does not develop this point in depth, and this document does not overclaim a fully worked internal-reform genealogy here — it is named as a real pressure requiring Doc_02's own sourcing to substantiate further, not asserted as settled beyond what the canons themselves show. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the existence of a pre-Reformation internal-reform current in Catholic scholarship generally; **Inferential-Thin** for this document's own specific characterization of it, pending Doc_02.
- The need for doctrinal clarity in the face of a fracturing Western Christendom — the Council's own stated purpose, per its Session III preface, of "extirpating heresies" and "reforming manners" (verified verbatim against the vendored Waterworth text, lines 12320–12322).

**Historical Catalysts:**

- The three Trent sessions: 1545–47 (Paul III), 1551–52 (Julius III), 1562–63 (Pius IV) — twenty-five sessions in total.
- The Roman Catechism, 1566 (commissioned by Trent, promulgated under Pius V) — the text through which Trent's settlement reached parish clergy directly.
- Carlo Borromeo's Milan reforms — his own synodal acts and pastoral instructions (*Acta Ecclesiae Mediolanensis*, printed 1599), evidencing concrete diocesan-level implementation of Tridentine episcopal reform.
- The bull *Regnans in Excelsis* (Pius V, 1570) — excommunicating and purporting to depose Elizabeth I of England, verified directly against the vendored Barlow-transmitted parallel Latin/English text (opening "Regnans in excelsis, cui data est omnis in coelo et in terra potestas..."/"He that reigneth on high, to whom is given all power in Heaven and in Earth..."), a genuine primary papal act even though transmitted here inside a hostile 1681 Anglican polemical frame (Barlow's own commentary, role: context, distinguished from the bull text itself, role: tradition).

— **Confidence: Documented** for all four catalysts as dated events attested in the vendored primary sources.

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's four questions directly:

**What recurring formation ecology exists?** An ecology of conciliar definition and catechetical transmission: doctrine settled by anathema-bearing canon, discipline settled by decree, and both then carried to parish level through a commissioned catechism and reformed liturgical books — a formation logic that runs through *authoritative instrument* (conciliar text, papal bull, diocesan act, catechism) in a way distinct from a single founder's own devotional corpus or a single order's own formation rule. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted.**

**What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, deferred to Doc_04)?** Named here as candidates for later testing, not confirmed: doctrinal boundary-fixing through anathema (defining orthodoxy by naming and rejecting its denial, clause by clause); sacramental realism defended against reinterpretation (the Eucharistic canons, §5 below); hierarchical/conciliar authority as the proper locus of doctrinal settlement, defended against both individual private judgment and unilateral civil authority over the church. This document does not perform Doc_04's work; these are Step 1's own preliminary sketch only.

**What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Worship: the Mass, defended in its traditional Latin, ceremonial form against reformist critique (Session XXII canons, §5 below) and structured going forward by the Roman Missal (vendored, role: tradition, England 1843 translation, whole volume). Formation/catechesis: the Roman Catechism, written expressly for parish priests to teach from — this world's own doctrinal formation text, not a later commentary on it (corpus-map). Authority: episcopal reform and residency requirements, evidenced concretely in Borromeo's own Milan synodal acts. Interpretation: scriptural authority read alongside apostolic tradition and the Church's own magisterial teaching office, defended against a sola-scriptura hermeneutic (Session IV, named in Step 0's own floor-clearance discussion though not requoted here). Belonging: defined institutionally, through valid sacramental participation and communion with the hierarchical Church, rather than through a gathered-congregation or covenanted-community logic. — **Confidence: Documented** for the Mass/Missal, Catechism, and Borromeo's synodal acts as vendored primary evidence; **Widely Accepted** for the broader characterization of belonging and interpretation, pending Doc_02's fuller sourcing.

## 4. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

Per Article 21, strand is "a finding, never a presupposed universal schema," accountable to evidence, and never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality. This determination is made here for the first time; Step 0 did not perform it.

**The reasoning this section must not misuse:** the sequence conciliar-decrees → Roman-Catechism → Borromeo's-episcopal-implementation is a temporal and causal sequence — the Catechism was commissioned by and derives its authority from the Council's own decrees; Borromeo's Milan reforms implement, rather than independently originate, the disciplinary program Trent legislated. Per the discipline IJC's own Doc_01 applied to its three sequential phases (§4 there), sequential, causally-linked development of a single program is *not*, by itself, evidence of strand plurality — treating it as such would misuse Article 21 exactly as the Article warns against. That the Catechism came *after* and *because of* the Council's decrees, and that Borromeo's Milan reforms came after and drew on both, is a temporal-development fact (more properly an Article 15 matter), not a strand finding.

**The question Strand Determination must actually ask instead:** does this world, independent of that sequence, show a genuinely simultaneous or structurally distinct pattern of authority, voice, or formation logic — not merely "this text came before that one"?

**Finding, argued both ways on the actual evidence:**

*The case for a single, strand-singular conciliar/magisterial voice.* All three bodies of material — the canons and decrees, the Roman Catechism, and Borromeo's synodal acts — share one authority ground: magisterial definition exercised through hierarchical office, answerable to and derived from the Council's own settlement. The Catechism does not claim independent doctrinal authority; it exists to teach what the Council decided. Borromeo's Milan *Acta* does not claim independent doctrinal authority either; it is a metropolitan's own instrument for enforcing discipline the Council had already legislated (episcopal residency, seminary formation, parish visitation) within his own province. On this reading, what looks like three bodies of material is one authority structure operating at three points of the same implementation chain — conciliar legislation, catechetical transmission, diocesan enforcement — and Article 21's bar (a genuinely distinct *ground* of authority, not merely a distinct genre or a distinct author) is not met by any of the three against the others.

*The case for real strand variation worth naming.* Against that reading, the three bodies of material differ in genre-specific ways that plausibly carry ecological weight beyond mere sequence. The conciliar decrees speak in the voice of anathema — a canon-law register whose characteristic move is to name and reject a proposition, addressed to universal Latin Christendom. The Roman Catechism speaks in an instructional, positive-exposition register — addressed to parish priests, and through them to ordinary believers, explaining what to teach rather than what to condemn; its formation logic (patient exposition of Creed, sacraments, commandments, and prayer) is structurally different from the canons' own boundary-policing logic, even though both are magisterially authorized. Borromeo's *Acta* speaks in a third register again — administrative and visitational, addressed to one archdiocese's own clergy, concerned with concrete practice (church fabric, clerical residency, parish oversight) rather than doctrine or catechesis as such. If Doc_02's fuller sourcing shows these three registers producing meaningfully different formation experiences for their respective audiences (a conciliar father debating anathemas; a parish priest teaching the Catechism; a Milanese cleric receiving Borromeo's visitation), that would be evidence of genuine strand variation — a difference in formation logic, not merely genre.

**Working determination: strand-singular, provisionally, with the split named as a live candidate for Doc_04, not settled here.** The magisterial-authority-ground these three bodies of material share is the stronger and better-evidenced reading at Step 1: none of the three claims an authority independent of, or in tension with, the others, which is the substantive bar Article 21 sets (as IJC's own Doc_01 states it: a genuinely distinct *ground* of authority, not a distinct genre or moment). The genre difference named above is real but does not, on the evidence available at this stage, rise to a different ground of authority — it is better described as the same authority operating through different instruments for different audiences, which is a formation-ecology finding for Doc_05, not a Strand Determination for Doc_01/Doc_04. This finding is provisional in the same sense IJC's own strand work distinguished a single-development finding from a strand finding: if Doc_02's fuller source-ecology work or Doc_04's gravity testing surfaces a genuinely distinct organizing conviction in the catechetical or episcopal-implementation material — one not reducible to "the same doctrine, taught rather than legislated" — this determination should be revisited openly, not defended past the evidence. — **Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for the strand-singular determination itself; **Contested** (within this document's own reasoning, not in the secondary literature specifically) for whether the catechetical/pastoral register could yet be argued as a distinct strand — named, not resolved.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification (Forces Framework Step 1)

This is orientation for Doc_02's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Doc_08.

**What generated this world:** the Reformation's doctrinal and institutional challenge (Luther's 95 Theses, 1517, and the subsequent development of Lutheran and Reformed teaching and church polity), together with a genuine internal-reform pressure inside the Catholic Church that predates Luther — recognized problems of clerical discipline, episcopal absenteeism, and inadequate priestly formation that Trent's own disciplinary decrees address directly and that this document does not claim originated with, or are reducible to, the Protestant challenge (§2 above).

**What larger world was it embedded in:** Renaissance and early modern Latin Christendom — a Europe of contending confessional states, an Empire whose choice of Trent as council-site (rather than a papal city) itself reflects the political constraints on the Council's convening, and a print culture that made both Luther's challenge and Trent's own canons and catechism reproducible and transmissible at unprecedented speed and scale.

**What was it responding to:** Protestant doctrine and practice, named where the vendored canons themselves name it — not a generalized "Protestantism" but specific, citable positions. Session VI's canons on justification and Session XXI's and XXII's canons on the Eucharist and the Mass are addressed to named theological claims, verified directly against the vendored Waterworth text (see below).

**What was it refusing — precisely, verified against the canons rather than assumed from popular narrative:**

- **Justification by faith alone.** Session VI, Canon IX (verified verbatim, lines 13681–13686): "If anyone saith that by faith alone the impious is justified; in such wise as to mean that nothing else is required to co-operate in order to the obtaining the grace of Justification, and that it is not in any way necessary that he be prepared and disposed by the movement of his own will; let him be anathema." The canon's own precision matters: it anathematizes faith alone as *sufficient without any cooperation of the will*, not the broader claim that faith is necessary or central to justification.
- **Communion in one kind only, against the reformist demand for the chalice.** Session XXI (titled in the vendored text "On Communion in one kind, and the Communion of little children") lists, among the condemned reformist positions catalogued in its preparatory articles, item 8 (verified verbatim, lines 6179–6182): "That it is of divine right that communion be received under both kinds, even by the laity and children, and that they, therefore, are guilty of sin who cause the people to receive under one kind." Trent's own decree defends the Church's authority to withhold the chalice from the laity as a matter of ecclesiastical discipline, not a matter the reformers could claim as divinely mandated.
- **Mass in the vernacular only, and other liturgical reforms.** Session XXII, Canon IX (verified verbatim, lines 18702–18708): "If anyone saith that the rite of the Roman Church, according to which a part of the canon and the words of consecration are pronounced in a low tone, is to be condemned; or that the mass ought to be celebrated in the vulgar tongue only; or that water ought not to be mixed with the wine that is to be offered in the chalice... let him be anathema." The precise target is "in the vulgar tongue *only*" — the canon defends the Church's authority to retain Latin, not a blanket claim that vernacular liturgical elements were forbidden everywhere (Trent's own decrees elsewhere permit vernacular catechetical instruction alongside the Latin Mass, per §3 above).
- **Married clergy, specifically clerics in sacred orders or professed Religious under vow.** Session XXIV, Canon IX (verified verbatim, lines 20252–20261): "If anyone saith that clerics constituted in sacred orders, or Regulars, who have solemnly professed chastity, are able to contract marriage, and that being contracted it is valid, notwithstanding the ecclesiastical law or vow... let him be anathema." This is precisely scoped to clergy and religious under a prior vow of chastity, not a general condemnation of clerical marriage as such independent of that vow.

Each of these four refusals is verified here by direct grep and line-level reading of `council-of-trent_canons-and-decrees_waterworth1848.txt`, not asserted from general knowledge of the Counter-Reformation. — **Confidence: Documented** for all four, as the canons' own verbatim text.

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits:** medieval Catholic sacramental theology and ecclesiology, defended and clarified against reformist reinterpretation rather than reformed away — the Eucharistic realism, the seven sacraments, the authority of apostolic tradition alongside Scripture, and the hierarchical, Petrine-succession-grounded structure of church governance all continue from the medieval Church this world's own canons take as their starting, defended position, not as raw material to be redesigned. — **Confidence: Documented.**

**What this world transmits forward:** the entire early modern and modern Roman Catholic Church — its doctrinal settlement (the Tridentine profession of faith), its liturgical form (the Tridentine Mass, codified via the Roman Missal, in continuous use until the mid-20th century and beyond in some communities), its catechetical model (the Roman Catechism's own descendants, down to the modern *Catechism of the Catholic Church*), and its disciplinary structure (seminary formation, episcopal residency, parish-based pastoral care) — executed concretely, in this world's own vendored evidence, through orders such as the Society of Jesus and reforming bishops such as Carlo Borromeo, whose own Milan *Acta* stands as this world's most detailed primary case of implementation in practice. — **Confidence: Documented** for the broad transmission claim; **Widely Accepted** for the specific continuity of Tridentine liturgical form well beyond this world's own 1650 close.

**Living heir(s):** the Roman Catholic Church itself, in unbroken institutional continuity from this world to the present day — the most direct living-tradition continuity available to this portfolio (§1 above). Per Article 28, this document maintains, and every downstream document must maintain, a clear distinction between the historical formation world reconstructed here (the Counter-Reformation Church of 1517–1650, as its own conciliar, catechetical, and disciplinary texts present it) and the present-day Church's own self-understanding, which is not this project's to define and which has itself changed substantially since this world's own close (most consequentially at the Second Vatican Council, 1962–65 — named here only to mark that a genuine further discontinuity exists between this world's close and the present day, not to characterize it, which is outside this document's scope). — **Confidence: Documented** for the institutional continuity itself; the Article 28 differentiation duty is a governance requirement, not a historical claim, and is stated here as binding on all subsequent documents.
