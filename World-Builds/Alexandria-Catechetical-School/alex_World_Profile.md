## Section 1 — World Identity

The **Alexandrian Christian formation ecology, c. 150–400 CE**, centered on Alexandria and Egyptian Christianity, and anchored/named by its distinctive **catechetical-formation tradition** — a milieu of learned Christian teaching, allegorical-spiritual reading of Scripture, and confident synthesis with Greek intellectual culture. Its **formation axis is learned formation.** It is a distinct formation world (strand-singular internally), bounded before the Chalcedonian fracture (451); its living heir is the **Coptic Orthodox Church** (Section 9). *(Name/scope flagged for the project lead, OG-3; the institutional existence of the "catechetical school" as such is itself Contested — van den Broek 1995, van den Hoek 1997 vs. Scholten 1995 — so the world is named by the *tradition*, not the institution.)*

## Section 2 — Confirmed Gravities (Doc_04)

**Primary (2) — confirmed for the *literate-attested* ecology only (the central honest limit, Section 8):**
- **Scripture as Deep Formative Reality** — the Logos speaking now through a text of inexhaustible depth; the primary formation instrument.
- **Transformation of the Soul Toward God** — formation as genuine reorientation toward participation in the divine life, not self-improvement.

**Supporting (3):**
- **Divine Pedagogy** — the explanatory frame: God is always teaching (through Scripture's difficulty, catechesis, suffering).
- **Logos-Centered Unity** — the integrating center that makes the practices one ecology, not four.
- **Learning-Formation Integration** — learning *is* formation (temporally qualified: strong early/mid, attenuating post-c. 300).

**Tensional (4) — held, not resolved:**
- **Teacher–Bishop** (two authorities); **Learning–Community** (school-depth vs. communal-breadth = the stratum non-integration); **Speculative-vs-Doctrinal-Boundary** (freedom of inquiry vs. the drawn line; site = the Origen inheritance); **Martyrdom-vs-Contemplative-Ascent** (two competing pictures of a formed life).

## Section 3 — Formation Logic (Doc_05, Doc_07)

**Aim:** the transformation of the whole person toward God, culminating in real *participation* in the divine life (theosis as the horizon). **Mechanism:** the **Participation↔Perception dynamic** — participation deepens perception and perception deepens participation, a **cumulative spiral** (catechesis → illumination → gnosis → wisdom → participation, toward theosis), never stages left behind. **Two channels:** the *school-tradition* channel (guided depth-reading, the teacher relationship — the literate minority) and the *whole-community* channel (assembly proclamation, Eucharist, the Paschal fast — reaching the non-literate majority without literacy). **Strand:** strand-singular (no cross-strand formation logic; the desert/monastic attribution is held open, cross-build). Formation Logic and the gravities converge: Scripture (C1) and Transformation (C2) are *mutually constitutive* — Scripture the medium, transformation the capacity — with Logos-Centered Unity (C4) as the theological center and Divine Pedagogy (C3) as the reason it forms.

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary (Doc_05, Doc_07)

- **4A — Emotional texture:** early/formative confidence (Clement); mid intensity and interior seriousness under persecution (Origen); late embattled, doctrinally urgent resilience (Athanasius/Didymus); Nicaea felt as relief-and-burden. *(The majority's interior emotional life is a named absence.)*
- **4B — Philosophical ecology:** among the most philosophically engaged early-Christian worlds — the Philonic-Jewish and Platonic (Middle Platonist) inheritance kept for its account of reality's depth and the soul's ascent, refused where it could not hold the Logos-become-flesh; philosophy as preparation, never destination.
- **4C — Authority structures:** the Teacher–Bishop tension is the architecturally central feature; the post-Nicene shift reconfigures authority toward the office-pole (bishop as conciliar-orthodoxy enforcer).
- **4D — Boundary structures:** the Gnostic boundary (truth for all, not an elite) shaped the world's positive character; the Arian boundary (post-325) shifted the threat from external to internal; the internal Speculative-Doctrinal boundary runs through the Origen inheritance.

## Section 5 — Forces Summary (Doc_08)

Initiating: the Philonic synthesis and Septuagint (external), the apostolic tradition, Johannine Logos, and the knowing-impulse (internal). Ongoing: Gnostic and Platonic/Neoplatonic competition, episodic persecution, and the Arian controversy (external); Scripture-as-formative-force, the Teacher–Bishop tension, and **transmission** (internal). Ending/transforming: the Chalcedonian fracture (451, distal) and Arab conquest (641, distal terminal) (external); the Origen–Demetrius conflict, the post-Nicene authority reconfiguration, and the **transmission after-life** (internal). **The pivotal force is Nicaea (325)** — one structural shift producing multiple effects. **Transmission is a named force twice** (2B, 3B): the teacher-student channel is the most valuable and most fragile inheritance; the sacramental channel the most robust; the creedal the most precise-but-brittle. The world's most direct formation heir is the Desert Christianity world (attribution held open).
