## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document finalizes the catalog of formation stories the Alexandria world can tell, each classified into one of the four permitted tiers (Documented Historical Narrative / Collected and Traditional Material / Attributed-Hagiographic Tradition / Historically Grounded Reconstruction), with a specific source, a tier justification that engages the tier criteria, and usage guidance for what claim each story is licensed to support. It answers the required **Absent Stories** question (§3), and carries the **Validation Layer** (§5). Confidence follows the Story-Tier cross-walk (Framework Part II): Tier 1 → Documented/Widely Accepted; Tier 2 → Widely Accepted/DMR (attributions Contested); Tier 3 → Contested (portrait) / Inferential-Thin (specific events); Tier 4 → Inferential-Thin (labeled reconstruction). **Persecution-narrative stories may carry internally split confidence spanning these ranges by design** (`alexstory003`, `alexstory009`), where a documented event, a corroborated core claim, and a less-secure narrated particular are named separately rather than flattened to one label.

## 2. Story-by-Story Summary

### Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative
- **`alexstory001` — Gregory's Address to Origen.** *Source:* Gregory Thaumaturgus, *Address of Thanksgiving to Origen* (c. 238 CE; Nautin authenticity/dating caveat noted). *Tier justification:* direct first-person textual attestation, named author with identifiable social location, datable, historically credible; the genre (epideictic) idealizes but does not fabricate. *Usage:* remembered history of the teacher-student formation relationship — may be offered as an account of what studying under Origen was like, with the author and perspective named. — **Documented/Widely Accepted (narrative level).**
- **`alexstory002` — Palladius meets Didymus.** *Source:* Palladius, *Lausiac History* (c. 419–420), his own account of meeting Didymus the Blind. *Tier justification:* Palladius's own direct testimony (Tier 1 for his firsthand encounters, distinct from his received accounts, which are Tier 3). *Usage:* remembered history of the blind master of the late-horizon teaching tradition. — **Widely Accepted.**
- **`alexstory003` — Persecution shapes the school (Leonidas; Origen under Decius).** *Source:* Eusebius *HE* 6, in two distinct modes the tier justification must distinguish (Doc_02 §3.6): Leonidas's martyrdom is Eusebius's own institutional/biographical narration (HIGH Author-Gravity risk, no independent corroboration); Origen's Decian imprisonment is corroborated by Dionysius of Alexandria's own contemporary correspondence on the Decian persecution and the reintegration of the lapsed (Doc_02 §3.6, §5 — doubly-mediated by Eusebius's quotation but resting on a near-contemporary primary voice, not Eusebius's own construction), and independently by the empire-wide documentary record of the Decian persecution (the *libelli*). *Tier justification:* the persecutions themselves are documented events, corroborated well beyond Eusebius; Origen's own imprisonment rests on Dionysius's quoted contemporary testimony, not Eusebius's uncorroborated narration, and is Widely Accepted on that basis; the Leonidas particulars rest on Eusebius's own institutional narration alone and remain Contested-leaning. *Usage:* remembered history of how persecution pressed on the school tradition's own life — the Leonidas particulars should be carried more lightly than the Decian imprisonment, per the narrator-vs-quotation distinction above. — **Documented** for the persecutions as events; **Widely Accepted** for Origen's Decian imprisonment specifically (quoted-primary-source corroboration, not bare Eusebian narration); **Contested-leaning** for the Leonidas particulars (Eusebius's own uncorroborated narration). This is a **split-confidence composite story** (per §1's note on persecution-narratives), the same discipline applied at `alexstory009`.

### Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material
- **`alexstory004` — The Sayings of the Desert Fathers.** *Source:* the *Apophthegmata Patrum* (collected late 4th–5th c.; named abbas — Antony, Macarius, Poemen — plus anonymous material). *Tier justification:* a collected tradition with identifiable collection history, academically treated as authentic even where individual attribution cannot be verified; the collection is evidence of what the community remembered and valued. *Usage:* stories the tradition tells about itself — but **cross-build provisional:** the desert material's world-attribution (Alexandria vs. the distinct Desert Christianity world) is **held open** (Doc_01 §3.3); this world may reference it as material near its horizon, not claim it as its own formation logic. — **Widely Accepted/DMR** (attributions Contested).

### Tier 3 — Attributed / Hagiographic Tradition
- **`alexstory005` — The Life of Antony.** *Source:* Athanasius, *Life of Antony* (c. 356–362). *Tier justification:* a hagiographic account shaped by recognizable conventions (call narrative, temptation sequence, exemplary death) — the *formation ideal* it communicates is credible evidence of what the community believed formation produces; the specific episodes (visions, demonic combat, miracles) are not historical reporting. *Usage:* the community's own account of what a formed ascetic life looks like — **not** historical fact about specific events. **Cross-build (desert-formation substance) held open**; and Antony's own literacy is itself Contested (Rubenson's literate-Antony thesis). — **Contested (portrait) / Inferential-Thin (specific events).**
- **`alexstory006` — The Markan foundation of Alexandria.** *Source:* Eusebius, *HE* 2.16 (Mark the Evangelist as founder). *Tier justification:* an attributed apostolic-foundation narrative with no contemporary 1st–2nd-century corroboration — identity-bearing tradition, not documented history. *Usage:* the community's own foundation narrative (structurally like Rome's Peter-Paul or Edessa's Addai claim), offered as the tradition's self-account, not as history. — **Contested (as tradition) / Inferential-Thin (as event).**
- **`alexstory007` — The catechetical-school succession.** *Source:* Eusebius, *HE* 5.10–6.30 (Pantaenus → Clement → Origen → Heraclas → Dionysius). *Tier justification:* an attributed institutional tradition, Eusebius-mediated (HIGH Author-Gravity risk), whose *institutional form* is itself Contested (van den Broek/van den Hoek vs. Scholten; Doc_01 §1.2). *Usage:* the tradition's account of its own teaching lineage — offered with the institutional-form contest carried, never as a settled orderly institution. — **Contested.**
- **`alexstory008` — The Origen–Demetrius conflict.** *Source:* Eusebius, *HE* 6 (Origen's departure to Caesarea c. 231–234; Demetrius's condemnation). *Tier justification:* an attributed conflict-narrative, Eusebius-mediated (HIGH risk); the *structural fact* (a teacher-bishop conflict producing disruption) is more secure than the particulars. *Usage:* the paradigmatic teacher-bishop conflict — its structural meaning, never the contested particulars as fact. — **Contested / Inferential-Thin (particulars).**
- **`alexstory009` — The Coptic martyrs (the Great Persecution / Era of the Martyrs).** *Source:* the Coptic martyrological tradition; the Diocletianic persecution (303–311; the *Anno Martyrum* reckoned from Diocletian's 284 accession). *Tier justification:* the persecution is a documented event (Tier-1-level); the specific martyr acts are hagiographic (Tier 3) — what the community believed faithful witness under ultimate pressure looks like. *Usage:* the community's memory of martyrdom-as-formation (the one formation mode reaching every stratum; its interior held Inferential-Thin). — **Documented (persecution) / Contested (specific acts).**

### Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction
- **`alexstory010` — A typical catechumen's formation (composite).** *Source:* a composite typical-practice narration built **only** from attested elements — the staged catechumenate (Clement's *Paedagogus*), the daily prayer-and-Scripture rhythm (Clement's *Stromateis* VII.7, 12 — corrected 2026-09-08, independent maturity-gap audit: previously cited to "Origen's *On Prayer*," a work never actually vendored into this corpus despite being cited as if it were; see `Story-Chunks/alexstory010_typical-catechumen.md`), the festal/Paschal cycle (Athanasius's Festal Letters). *Tier justification:* a narration of *typical practice*, not a specific named individual, with every element traceable to attested evidence within the horizon (as at Doc_05 §10). *Usage:* typical-practice narration **explicitly marked as reconstruction**; may narrate the shape of a typical formation, not claim it as a specific known event. **This narration concerns the school-and-community *practice*, not the non-literate majority's interior** (that remains a named absence, §3). — **Inferential-Thin (labeled reconstruction).**

**No Tier 5.** No story in this inventory is invented, composited-beyond-evidence, or generated to illuminate a truth the world's own evidence does not support (Article 19). Where the evidence does not support a story, the story does not exist for this world (§3).

## 3. Absent Stories *(the required question — what stories this world conspicuously cannot tell, and why)*

This world's silence is structured, and naming it is a participant-facing obligation (Article 20), not a backstage note. The stories this world **cannot** tell:

- **The non-literate majority's own formation story, from the inside.** No first-person narrative of an ordinary, non-literate, Coptic-speaking, or rural believer's formation survives. The world can reconstruct the majority's *channels* (Eucharist, proclamation, the fast) but cannot tell a story *from inside* their formation — this is the story-level face of the stratum-bias limit (OG-4). The absence is not filled by a Tier-4 composite of the majority's interior; only *practice* is reconstructed, never interior.
- **A woman's formation story in her own voice.** Women appear as martyrs, as recipients of teaching, and as consecrated virgins and widows — almost never as narrators of their own formation. The world cannot tell a woman's formation story from inside; the household formation in which women were likely primary transmitters is largely unrecoverable.
- **The enslaved believer's story.** Enslaved persons in Christian households are mentioned occasionally and never narrated; the tension between proclaimed spiritual equality and social condition has left no story the world can tell.
- **The martyr's own interior.** The hagiographic *ideal* of the martyr survives; the martyr's own account of the experience does not. The world can tell what it *believed* martyrdom produced, not what the martyr felt.
- **A story from inside the desert as Alexandria's own.** Because the desert's world-attribution is held open (cross-build), this world cannot tell the desert's formation story *as its own* — only reference desert material near its horizon.

**Note on the catechumen's own voice.** Doc_02 §6 names catechumens among the structurally-suppressed voice-categories this world's record does not preserve in the first person — present only as recipients, never as authors. `alexstory010`'s composite narrates the *shape* of catechumenal practice from the outside; it does not, and cannot, supply the catechumen's own first-person account of undergoing it. This absence is not identical to the non-literate majority's interior above (not every catechumen was non-literate), but it is covered by the same discipline: named, not filled.

These absences are named, carried as named absences into any encounter, and never converted into invented narrative.
