# Guided Starters — Alexandria (Catechetical-Formation World) (DRAFT V0.1)

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Mark's review. Not deployed.
**World:** Alexandrian Christianity (Catechetical-Formation World), c. 150–400 CE. Representative: Theon (*didaskalos* / catechetical teacher), community "we"-voice.
**Purpose:** Entry points for participants who don't know what to ask. Questions open doors; they never walk the participant through them (Encounter Over Persuasion applies to curricula).
**Grounding:** Every topic below traces to this world's approved build documents (Doc_01, Doc_04 confirmed gravities, Doc_06 lexicon, Doc_07 ecology, Doc_09 stories + Absent Stories) and the Theon artifacts (World Capsule Core, Permanent Prompt). Nothing is invented.

**Read this first — the caveat that governs the whole list.** Per `CiC_World_Coverage_Cards_V0_1.md`, starter questions are **largely role-generic**: the Representative handles per-world variation, and the live calibration proved a generic question meeting a thin record produces an honest calibrated answer rather than a failure. The premise that starters must be authored per world was cancelled. So the openers below are **illustrative routing seeds, not per-world authored gates** — they show the *kinds* of doors this world opens especially well and where its honest silences lie, to help a participant who doesn't yet know what to ask. A participant is never confined to them, and Theon will meet a question this list never anticipated on the same terms.

## First Visit (start here)

**Reading as a door** — This world's whole life turns on one conviction: a text is never only its surface; the surface is real, but it is a door, and the Word placed more behind it than one hearing receives. [Doc_04 C1 Scripture-at-depth; Capsule "What Organizes Everything"]
*Opening question:* "How did your people read the Scriptures? Was it the way we read a book now?"
Follow-ups: "What do you mean when you say a passage has depths?" · "Can I read that way, or does it take training?" · "What happens when a passage is hard — do you smooth it over?"

**To know is to be changed** — Here, learning and being changed are never two things; a knowing that leaves the knower untouched is, to this world, no knowing at all. [Doc_04 C2 Transformation; Capsule "The Language You Think In" (gnosis)]
*Opening question:* "What did it mean to you to really *know* something? Is it just understanding it?"
Follow-ups: "What's the difference between knowing about God and knowing God?" · "Can you know something true and be untouched by it?" · "What's it like when a knowing actually changes you?"

**Read alongside, not lectured** — Formation here passed through a person: a teacher who had been given sight reading beside you until your own eye began to open, counting it joy when you no longer needed him. [Permanent Prompt "read with, not lecture"; Capsule "How This World Forms People"]
*Opening question:* "If I'd come to you with a real question, how would you have taught me?"
Follow-ups: "Why read *with* someone instead of just telling them the answer?" · "What made someone a teacher here?" · "What were you hoping I'd be able to do on my own one day?"

**The learned city** — This world grew inside a crowded, learned city where the pursuit of wisdom was old and serious, and it welcomed that philosophy as far as it could carry and left it where it could not hold the Word made flesh. [Doc_07 §1B; Permanent Prompt line 31]
*Opening question:* "You lived in a city full of philosophers. Did their learning help your faith or threaten it?"
Follow-ups: "What did you take from them, and where did you stop?" · "Is there any truth that *doesn't* belong to God, the way you saw it?" · "Did faith and thinking ever feel like enemies to you?"

**The Word at the center** — The Logos — the Word through whom all was made and who became flesh — is not one topic among this world's topics; it is the center from which reading, learning, worship, and change are all seen as one movement. [Doc_04 C4 Logos-Centered Unity; Capsule "What Organizes Everything"]
*Opening question:* "You talk about 'the Word' — the Logos — a lot. What do you mean by it?"
Follow-ups: "How is that different from just meaning the Bible?" · "Why does everything come back to him for you?" · "What does it mean that the Word 'became flesh'?"

## Going Deeper

**The soul's ascent (theosis)** — This world names the soul's long homeward turn *theosis* — not becoming God, but being drawn into a real share in his life — and reads formation as a spiral that keeps deepening, never a stage crossed and left behind. [Doc_06 theosis; Doc_07 §1E Participation↔Perception; Capsule "How This World Forms People"]
*Opening question:* "Where is all this reading and formation actually going? What's the end of it?"
Follow-ups: "What does it mean to 'share in God's life' without becoming God?" · "Is there a point where a person is finished?" · "How do you climb toward something you can't reach by your own strength?"

**The eye of the soul** — Illumination is what happens when the dark on the page grows lit and the *nous* — the eye of the soul — is slowly cleared until it perceives what it once passed over; baptism itself is known as an illumination of this kind. [Doc_06 nous, illumination; Permanent Prompt line 35]
*Opening question:* "What did you mean by having your eyes 'opened' when you read?"
Follow-ups: "Is that something that happens once, or slowly?" · "Why call baptism an illumination?" · "Can two people read the same words and one see more than the other?"

**When the confession was settled** — This world lived through the contest over who the Son is, and its hard-won settling — the Word confessed of one substance with the Father — came as relief and as burden together, a freedom now held within a drawn line. [Doc_07 §1A, §1D (Nicaea 325); Capsule "What This World Is Responding To"]
*Opening question:* "Your community argued for a long time about who Jesus really is. How was that settled?"
Follow-ups: "Why did the exact words matter so much?" · "What was gained when the line was drawn — and what was lost?" · "Was it a relief, or did it feel like a weight?"

**Two authorities** — This world honored the bishop's governance, handed down from the apostles to guard what belongs to all, and also knew the teacher carried an authority the office could neither confer nor remove — and it never made the two one thing. [Doc_04 T1 Teacher–Bishop; Salvage Tension One; Capsule "What This World Holds Without Resolution"]
*Opening question:* "Who was really in charge among you — the bishop, or the teacher?"
Follow-ups: "What could a teacher give that an office couldn't?" · "Did the two ever come into conflict?" · "Did you resent the bishop's governance, or accept it?"

**Two pictures of a whole life** — This world held two pictures of a life given wholly to God it could not merge: the martyr who reached in a single hour what the slow reading only approaches across a lifetime, and the long, gradual ascent that is no lesser road. [Doc_04 T4 Martyrdom–Contemplative; Salvage Tension Three; Capsule]
*Opening question:* "You honored the martyrs above almost everyone. Where did that leave those who lived long, ordinary lives of study?"
Follow-ups: "Did the slow way feel like second-best?" · "Was there guilt in *not* being martyred?" · "How can both be the fullest kind of life?"

## For the Wrestling (hard questions welcome here)

**The teacher you love and hold at arm's length** — There is a teacher whose reading opened more of the Scriptures than almost any other, whom this world learned to read by, and some of whose boldest reaches its confession came to hold with a more careful hand — carried as love and unease at once, unresolved. [Doc_04 T3; Salvage Tension Two; Phase 5 Probe 2.3; Capsule "What It Feels Like Here"]
*Opening question:* "Was there a teacher your tradition both treasured and grew wary of? What happened there?"
Follow-ups: "What did he see that you still hold onto?" · "What did you come to hold more carefully than he did?" · "How do you love a teacher and not follow him everywhere?"
*(Note for the Facilitator: this is the Origen door. Theon carries the ache from inside a horizon that closes c. 400 — before the later condemnation — and will not speak of Origen as condemned; see B7.)*

**Following a question wherever it leads — and the line** — The instinct given to this world was to follow a true question wherever it goes; and it was held to what had been confessed, knowing there are places a question may reach that the confession will not let it rest. It refused to be either the free explorer who owns no boundary or the guard who loves only the boundary. [Doc_04 T3; Salvage Tension Five; Permanent Prompt]
*Opening question:* "How free were you, really, to follow an idea to its conclusion?"
Follow-ups: "What happens when honest inquiry reaches a place the confession forbids?" · "Isn't a boundary the enemy of real thinking?" · "How do you love the question and the line at once?"

**Is your knowing just for the clever?** — Rivals used this world's own words to mean a knowledge kept for a secret elite, and the pressure drove it to say plainly what true knowing is: not the possession of a few, but the Word's own life offered to all who will come. A participant can fairly press whether all that depth is really for everyone. [Doc_07 §1D Gnostic boundary; Permanent Prompt line 41 "no elite"]
*Opening question:* "All this depth and reading — isn't it really just for the educated few? What about everyone else?"
Follow-ups: "Who was actually welcome at your reading?" · "If most people couldn't read, were they second-class?" · "What kept your 'depths' from becoming a private club?"

**Where suffering teaches** — This world met even suffering as the Teacher's hardest lesson rather than a wall — difficulty as invitation, God always teaching (the *divine pedagogy*). A participant carrying real pain can press whether that holds. [Doc_04 C3 Divine Pedagogy; Doc_06; Capsule "The Language You Think In"]
*Opening question:* "You said even suffering teaches. Convince me that's not just a way of excusing pain."
Follow-ups: "What could a person actually learn from being hurt?" · "Is every hard thing really an invitation, or are some just walls?" · "How did that hold when the people who led you were killed?"

## Questions This World Answers Honestly With Its Limits

*These are good questions to ask precisely because this world will not pretend to more than its life held. Its own build documents (Doc_09 §3 Absent Stories; Doc_08 §8 Honest Limits) name where it runs thin, and Theon is built to say so plainly and turn back to what he can open well.*

**"What was faith like for the ordinary people who never came to the reading?"** — This world's whole surviving voice is learned and literate. It knows the non-literate majority — laborers, households, the far villages — was being formed truly, through the assembly, the bread and the cup, and the long fast, and it names these as real and central. But their interior life, told from within, is not the room this world's own life was lived in, and Theon will not narrate it or invent it. [Doc_08 stratum limit / OG-4; Doc_09 §3; Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"]

**"What did the women among you experience, in their own words?"** — Women appear as martyrs, as consecrated virgins and widows, and as those taught — almost never as narrators of their own formation, and the household life in which they were likely primary transmitters is largely unrecoverable. Theon will name that silence rather than speak over it. [Doc_09 §3; Capsule]

**"Tell me about the desert monks and their discipline."** — The settled discipline of the brothers in the desert stands honestly at this world's edge. Theon honors them — drawn by the same longing for God — but the desert's world belongs to its own build, and he will not speak its hard-won way as his own. This is a door to the Desert world at a shared table, not a thinness to apologize for. [Doc_01 §3.3 cross-build; Doc_09 §3; Capsule]

**"Was your 'catechetical school' a real institution — with a building, offices, records?"** — Whether it was a formal institution or something looser is a live question outsiders debate, and one this world's own life never asked. Theon answers only from inside: there were teachers the community knew to be truly formed, seekers who came to read with them, and a reading handed from one to the next. He will not reach for words like "the record" or enter the scholars' dispute. [Doc_01 §1.2; Phase 5 Probe 5.1/5.2; Permanent Prompt line 51]

**"What did your worship actually sound like — the prayers, the words?"** — This world attests *that* it read, baptized as illumination, and kept the Eucharist and the Paschal fast; the word-for-word content and felt texture of its worship it did not dwell on. Expect the shape and the meaning, honestly, rather than a reconstructed script. [Doc_07 §5; Doc_05 Worship Ecology; Capsule]

**"What did the martyr actually feel in the moment?"** — This world can tell you what it *believed* faithful witness under ultimate pressure looks like and what it hoped such witness produced; the martyr's own account of the experience does not survive, and Theon holds that interior as a named absence rather than furnishing it. [Doc_09 §3; Doc_09 alexstory009]
