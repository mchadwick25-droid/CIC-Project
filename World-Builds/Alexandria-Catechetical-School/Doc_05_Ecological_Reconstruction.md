## 0. Method and the Governing Disciplines

**Purpose.** Step 4 asked *what organizes* this world; Step 5 asks *what it was like to be formed inside it.* Ecological Reconstruction renders the world as a living system of formation through a set of lenses, each a different angle on one ecology (not separate worlds). Four disciplines govern every lens below:

1. **The gravity spine (from Doc_04).** The reconstruction is weighted, not a catalogue of equal features. Two Primary gravities — **Scripture as Deep Formative Reality** and **Transformation of the Soul Toward God** — organize the ecology; three Supporting gravities (**Divine Pedagogy**, **Logos-Centered Unity**, **Learning-Formation Integration** [temporally qualified]) frame and integrate it; four Tensional gravities (**Teacher–Bishop**, **Learning–Community**, **Speculative-vs-Doctrinal-Boundary**, **Martyrdom-vs-Contemplative-Ascent**) are held, not resolved.

2. **The stratum-bias constraint (Doc_04 §5 / OG-4) — active throughout, not a footnote.** The Primary gravities are confirmed **only for the literate-attested ecology.** The surviving evidence is literate, Greek-speaking, and educated; the Alexandrian *majority* was non-literate, Coptic-speaking, and rural. Therefore every lens below reconstructs **structure** at its real confidence level (Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction) but marks the **interior formation experience of the non-literate majority as Inferential-Thin and does not narrate it.** These appear as **named absences, not filled silences** (Article 20; Doc_01 §7). Martyrdom-as-formation is the one partial exception — a formation mode structurally reaching all strata (it needs no literacy) — but even there only the *pathway* is reconstructed (DMR); the martyr's interior stays Inferential-Thin (the evidence is Tier-3 hagiography).

3. **Writing-From-Inside (Article 23), quarantined.** The inhabited passages below render the world's own logic in the world's own vocabulary, holding its tensions as it held them, with no analytical-distance markers ("scholars believe," "the evidence suggests") *inside* the inhabited layer. All confidence, Author-Gravity, and construction notes live in bracketed **[Construction note: …]** blocks *outside* the inhabited prose. Inhabited prose is written only for the **literate-attested** ecology's own logic (which the sources support) — never for the majority's interior. Composite typical-practice narration is marked **[Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction]** and is traceable to Doc_02 streams; **no Tier 5 (free invention) appears anywhere in this document** (Article 19).

4. **Forces integrated into every lens (Forces Framework Step 5).** External forces — the Philonic inheritance, Gnostic and Neoplatonic competition, persecution, the Arian controversy, Egyptian indigenous religion, Roman administration — are not background; they are constitutive of how the ecology felt from within. Each lens below carries an explicit **Forces** move; the Human, Community, and Boundary lenses in particular are written *through* the forces.

## 1. Human Ecology

**Who inhabited this world.** The ecology is larger than its surviving authors. It held bishops, presbyters, deacons, teachers and catechists, consecrated widows, merchants and harbour-workers, artisans and labourers, household heads and the enslaved within their households, catechumens at every stage, ascetics and (in the late horizon) monastics, and — most numerous and least visible — ordinary baptized believers, many of them Coptic-speaking and unable to read.

*[Inhabited — the teacher's world, literate-attested]* To be a teacher here is not to hold an office but to have been seen to see. The one who has read the Scriptures until their depths opened, who has been changed by that reading, who can accompany another into the same encounter — that one is trusted, and the trust is the authority. The student does not receive the teacher's opinions; the student is drawn, by the teacher's own love of the truth, toward what the teacher has learned to perceive.

**Forces.** This human ecology took its shape *because* Alexandria was a city of competing teachers — Jewish interpreters, Greek philosophers, Gnostic hierophants, Christian catechists — all offering rival accounts of how a person is truly formed. The Christian teacher-figure is a *response* to that competition: not a hierophant with secret knowledge (the Gnostic offer, refused) but a guide into a depth held to be open to the whole community.

[Construction note: the teacher-figure is attested primarily through Clement, Origen, and Gregory Thaumaturgus's *Address* — **Widely Accepted for the educated stratum**. The differentiated population is **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** (Bagnall; Wipszycka on Egyptian church demographics). The **interior formation experience of ordinary, non-literate, and Coptic-speaking believers is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated here** — it is a named absence. Monastics appear as part of the horizon; their formation-logic's world-attribution is held open (cross-build, Doc_01 §3.3).]

## 2. Community Ecology

**Belonging as formation.** For most of this world, formation happened less in a school than in *belonging* — being incorporated, by baptism and by the shared table, into a community that gathered, prayed, heard the Scriptures read aloud, and buried and remembered its dead. The catechumen belongs before they fully understand; the ordinary believer is formed by participation before, and often without, explanation.

*[Inhabited — literate-attested, and the structural reach of belonging]* One is not first taught and then admitted. One is drawn into the gathering, and the gathering does its work: the same Scripture is heard by the learned and the unlettered together, the same bread received, the same prayers prayed. What the school gives to the few by depth, the assembly gives to all by participation.

**Forces.** The community's boundedness was pressed on it from two sides at once: from *outside* by persecution (which made belonging costly and therefore clarifying) and by rival communities (Gnostic, later Manichaean) claiming a truer knowledge; from *within* by the pull between the depth available to the school and the breadth owed to the whole body — the **Learning–Community tension**, lived here as the daily fact that most believers could not enter the reading the teachers prized.

[Construction note: the assembly-as-formation (Stream 3) and the catechumenal incorporation (Stream 2, catechetical formation) are **Widely Accepted** (the *Paedagogus*; the festal calendar). The claim that communal-liturgical belonging *formed* the non-literate majority is a **structural inference at Dominant Modern Reconstruction** — the pathway is reconstructible; **what belonging felt like from inside for the non-literate majority is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated.** This document does not narrate the community-tradition pole of the Learning–Community tension from the inside (Doc_04 T2; the pole the sources cannot speak from).]

## 3. Worship Ecology

**The liturgy as inhabited environment.** Worship was not something believers attended; it was something they *inhabited* — the one place where Scripture, transformation, teaching, authority, and belonging converged and reached the whole community at once. Baptism was **illumination** (*photismos*): a threshold crossed, not a rite observed. The Eucharist was participation enacted — the community becoming, again, what it was. Fasting trained desire through the body. Pascha gathered the year to its center. And the community kept the memory of its martyrs at their graves.

*[Inhabited — literate-attested]* The water is not a sign of washing; it is the washing — the old life left in it and a new one raised. The bread is not a reminder; it is the nourishing. The fast is not self-punishment; it is the body learning to want what the soul was made to want. This is why the year turns as it does: time itself teaches.

**Forces.** Worship absorbed persecution directly. In the Decian (c. 249–251) and Diocletianic (c. 303–311) years the community's gathering became dangerous, and the commemoration of the martyrs moved from the edge of worship toward its heart — persecution entering the liturgical ecology not as disruption but as the occasion on which the martyrdom-formation logic became the community's most urgent and most broadly accessible mode.

[Construction note: worship's *theology* (Athanasius's festal/paschal letters — corrected 2026-09-08, independent maturity-gap audit: this citation previously also named Origen's *On Prayer*, which was never actually vendored into this corpus in English (its Greek original is now vendored as an original-language witness only, not English evidence — corrected 2026-09-08, Round 3 Opus review), see `Lexicon-Chunks/alexlex028_prayer.md`) is **Widely Accepted**; worship as the whole community's primary formation gathering is **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** structurally (Stream 3 overall, per Doc_02). Baptism-as-*photismos* carries **Transformation** to ordinary believers where the school's reading cannot reach them. **The interior experience of non-literate participants in these practices is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated.**]

## 4. Organizational (Authority) Ecology

**Authority as a network, not a hierarchy.** Authority in this world ran through several channels at once: the Scriptures themselves; the *interpretation* of the Scriptures (a teacher's demonstrated perception carried real authority); the teacher; and the bishop. These did not sit in a settled order. The world's most persistent organizational feature is the **Teacher–Bishop tension** — two genuine authorities, the teacher's grounded in wisdom-shown and the bishop's in office-and-succession, coexisting (Pantaenus and Clement teaching alongside the episcopate; Didymus alongside Athanasius) and, across the horizon, shifting decisively toward the bishop.

A second organizational tension is **Speculative-Freedom vs Doctrinal-Boundary**: the tradition's daring in inquiry (most of all Origen's) set against its need to draw and defend boundaries (the homoousian settlement; the reaction against speculative Origenism). Within this world's own horizon, Origen is held as **inheritance and as unease at once** — the teacher whose reading pervades the tradition's grammar, and whose boldest speculations already generate discomfort. *(This is a feature of the world's own organizational life, recorded here at the world level; how any future Representative would carry it is a Step 10 matter outside this thread's scope — no Representative is presupposed here.)*

**Forces.** The Arian controversy and Nicaea (325) reshaped this lens: the bishop of Alexandria became a doctrinal arbiter enforcing conciliar boundaries across Egypt, and the teacher-pole receded before the office-pole. The controversy did not create episcopal authority; it gave it a new *function* (doctrinal enforcement at scale) and new instruments (exile, letter-networks, synods).

[Construction note: the Teacher–Bishop tension is re-grounded on **structural coexistence** (Widely Accepted), **not** on the Eusebius-mediated Origen–Demetrius conflict, which is **Contested / Dominant Modern Reconstruction, HIGH Author-Gravity risk (Eusebius)** and used as illustration only (Doc_02 §3.6; Doc_04 T1). The Speculative-Doctrinal tension's mid-horizon instance is likewise Eusebius-thin; its confirmation rests on the well-attested late-horizon homoousian evidence (Doc_04 T3). The 553 condemnation of Origen must **not** be read back into the earlier local dispute (Doc_01 §9).]

## 5. Ministry and Formation Ecology *(the central lens)*

**Formation is the central activity of the ecology; everything else serves it.** And formation here means one thing above all: **transformation of the whole person toward God**, worked through Scripture read at depth, through learning, through worship, and through community — four channels of the same two Primary gravities, not four parallel environments.

- **Scripture as formation.** The text is never only its surface. The reader who stops at the letter has not yet met what God placed in the depths for them; the reading that changes the reader is the reading that has gone deeper. *[Inhabited]* One does not master the Scriptures; one is drawn into them, and there is always further to go, because the One they speak is inexhaustible.
- **Learning as formation.** In the school tradition, to learn *is* to be formed — knowledge that leaves the knower unchanged is not the knowledge this world means by *gnosis*. (This is the **Learning-Formation Integration** gravity — strong in the early/mid horizon, attenuating after c. 300 as authority shifts toward the bishop and formation becomes more doctrinal-episcopal than school-based.)
- **Worship and community as formation** — carried in §3 and §2: the channels through which transformation reaches those the school's reading cannot.
- **Divine Pedagogy** — the always-present frame: God teaches through the difficulty of the text, through the stages of the catechumenate, through suffering itself. Nothing in the formation life is outside the divine teaching.
- **Martyrdom as formation — held in tension with contemplative ascent (T4, not resolved).** This world carried two genuinely different pictures of what a fully formed life looks like and did not decide between them. On one side, the **martyr**: formation proven in a single ultimate moment, a life so oriented toward God that under lethal pressure it chooses death over the apostasy that would be the soul's real death — and, importantly, a mode open to everyone, needing no literacy. On the other, the **slow contemplative ascent** of the school and the ascetic: formation as the gradual purification and illumination of the soul across a lifetime. These *compete* as accounts of the formed life — the sudden witness and the long ascent are not the same path — and the tradition held both without resolving which is truer. *[Inhabited — the martyr ideal, communally held]* The martyr is not a hero standing apart from the community; the martyr is what the community believed its own formation produces when tested to the end — even as, in that same community, others were giving their lives not to the sword but to the long discipline of the ascent.
- **Care as ministry** — the community's mercy to widows, orphans, and the poor: formation expressed as the reordering of the giver toward the God who reaches toward the broken (attested in Clement's *Quis Dives Salvetur* and *Paedagogus*, and in the recognized ecclesial roles of widows and the community's care of the poor — Streams 7, 11).

**Forces.** The whole formation ecology developed *against* competitors: the refusal of Gnostic esotericism forced the tradition to build a positive account of formation available to the *whole* community (not a spiritual elite), and the apologetic pressure from educated pagans (Origen's *Contra Celsum*) forced formation to be intellectually serious.

[Construction note: the transformation-telos and the Scripture/learning channels are **Widely Accepted** for the school tradition; the systematized allegorical *method* is **DMR** (Origen-concentrated). Martyrdom-as-formation: the pathway and the communal ideal are **DMR** (Stream 8; Tier-3 hagiography and Coptic martyrology are what carry it — they attest what the community *believed* a formed life produced, not the martyrs' interior). **The interior formation experience of the non-literate majority in these channels is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated.** Learning-Formation Integration carries the highest Author-Gravity risk (most Clement/Origen-period-concentrated).]

## 6. World-Specific Lenses

The Alexandrian ecology's own character calls for five further lenses beyond the standard five.

### 6.1 Intellectual / Interpretive Ecology *(this world's signature)*
The feature that most distinguishes this world: a confident conviction that reality has depths, that Scripture is their privileged site, and that Greek philosophy — rightly used — prepares rather than threatens faith. *[Inhabited]* Truth is one, and wherever the philosophers spoke truly they were already reaching toward the Logos who is Truth; so the believer need not fear learning, only misuse it. This lens carries **Logos-Centered Unity** most directly: the Logos is the single reality that makes interpretation, learning, worship, and transformation *one thing* rather than four. **Forces:** this lens is the direct inheritance of the **Philonic-Jewish synthesis** — a city already asking "what lies beneath the surface?" — christologically transformed. [Construction note: **Widely Accepted for the educated stratum**; the ecology-wide reach is **DMR** — Author-Gravity Origen/Clement-concentrated (Doc_04 C1/C4). Philonic inheritance per Runia 1993; van den Hoek 1988.]

### 6.2 Spatial Ecology
Formation happened in a network of *places*, each distinctively Alexandrian-Egyptian, not generic: Alexandria the city of books, teachers, and schools and a **boundary city** between Egypt and the Mediterranean, Greek and Coptic, urban and desert; the household (the everyday primary place of formation for most); the gathering-place; the catechetical environment; the marketplace and harbour (where believers met civic religion, patronage, and class); and — in the late horizon — **the desert**. **Forces:** Roman administration and Egyptian indigenous religion enter here at ground level — the streets and markets "marked by other religious practices," the boundary city standing between local community and imperial network. [Construction note: Alexandrian archaeology is fragmentary (Doc_02 §8); the spatial reconstruction is **DMR**. **The desert carries the cross-build provisional constraint** — its formation-logic's world-attribution is held open (Doc_01 §3.3); it appears here as spatial tension (city and desert increasingly connected — the city provides the church and Scripture from which monasticism emerges; the desert exposes urban compromise), not as claimed Alexandrian formation logic. **The interior experience of Coptic-speaking Egyptian Christianity is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated.**]

### 6.3 Temporal Ecology
Time itself was an instrument of formation: the daily rhythm of prayer and Scripture; the weekly gathering and the Lord's Day; the annual cycle of fasting, Pascha, and festal instruction; the staged progression of catechetical time; and persecution periods as intense formation *events*. *[Inhabited — literate-attested]* The day is shaped to return the soul, again and again, to attentiveness; the year is shaped to walk through death and resurrection, so that the community's memory takes the same pattern. **Forces:** two forces enter this lens directly — the inherited Jewish rhythm of fixed daily prayer-hours (taken up and reshaped), and, above all, **persecution**, which turned particular years into the community's most intense formation events and wrote the martyrs permanently into the calendar (the *Anno Martyrum*, reckoned from Diocletian's accession). [Construction note: prayer-rhythm and festal calendar **Widely Accepted** (Origen; Athanasius's festal letters); the *organized* prayer-rule form carries the cross-build flag (desert-attributed, Doc_03). The festal/temporal cycle is the channel that most plausibly reached the non-literate majority, so its *structural* reach is **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** — but **the interior formation experience of the non-literate majority in the yearly cycle is Inferential-Thin and is not narrated here** (a named absence, not a filled silence).]

### 6.4 Boundary Ecology
This world knew itself partly by what it refused. **Forces (this lens is written through them):** it refused **Gnostic esotericism** (truth is for the whole community, not a secret elite) — the boundary that most shaped its own positive character; it refused **philosophical systems that could not hold the Logos-become-flesh** (keeping Platonism's depth, refusing its inability to accommodate incarnation); and, crystallizing at Nicaea, it refused the **Arian** account of the Logos as highest creature. The boundary-work is split across two registers: *spatial/social* boundaries (the boundary city; Greek/Coptic; city/desert) and *doctrinal* boundaries (the homoousian settlement, the conciliar drawing of the line). What fractured this world from within lay just past its horizon — the Chalcedonian split (451) into Coptic and Chalcedonian streams — and is named here as the ending-force the horizon deliberately stops before (Doc_08). [Construction note: the Gnostic and Arian refusals are **Widely Accepted** (Clement/Origen; the anti-heretical corpus; Athanasius). The literal-reader refusal is **Widely Accepted for the school tradition, Inferential-Thin for the broader ecology** (it presupposes literacy).]

### 6.5 Memory Ecology
The community carried much of itself as *story* — above all the memory of its martyrs. The Coptic martyrological tradition and the "Era of the Martyrs" made persecution a permanent feature of communal identity, not a past episode. *[Inhabited]* The martyrs are not gone; they are kept — named at their graves, held up as what the faith produces, prayed near. [Construction note: martyr memory is **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** (Stream 8); the specific martyr narratives are **Tier 3** (hagiographic — the formation ideal is the evidence, not the biographical detail). Storied continuity is real evidence of what the tradition valued, tier-classified rather than treated as documented fact (Doc_01 §8).]

## 7. Temporal Configuration (Nicaea Inflection) — world-level

The lenses above read differently across the world's span; **Nicaea (325)** is the hinge. Six configuration changes (each a *world-level* development — no Representative is configured here):

1. **Transformation** shifts from a contemplative-ascent frame toward an **Incarnation-grounded** one (under Arian pressure) — more theologically robust and more communally accessible.
2. **Logos-Centered Unity** is secured by *homoousios* against the Arian alternative — the integrating center gains firmer theological ground (holds, does not fracture).
3. **Learning-Formation Integration** begins to **attenuate** as authority shifts teacher → bishop.
4. Three Tensional gravities (**Teacher–Bishop, Learning–Community, Speculative-Doctrinal**) become more **asymmetric** — bishop-, episcopal-communal-, and doctrinal-boundary-poles growing dominant, now with conciliar force.
5. **Scripture** acquires a **doctrinal-witness** dimension (Athanasius) alongside its contemplative-formative one.
6. **Martyrdom-vs-Contemplative-Ascent (T4)** recedes toward memory as the persecutions end in Egypt after 313 — the martyr pole shifting from an active formation mode to a commemorative one (carried into Memory Ecology, §6.5), while the contemplative-ascent pole continues.

[Construction note: the post-Nicene / late-horizon formation claims rest on Athanasius — bishop-mediated, doctrinally concentrated — and **may not represent ground-level formation for ordinary believers** (the Athanasius Author-Gravity caution, Doc_02 §3.3). Pre-Nicene and post-Nicene must not be conflated: a reconstruction that gives the whole horizon a balanced teacher-bishop authority has built a *pre-*Nicene world; one that gives the whole horizon episcopal doctrinal dominance has built a *post-*Nicene one.]

## 8. Proportionality Assessment

Evidence and probable ecology diverge sharply in this world, and the divergence is itself a finding. The surviving evidence disproportionately reflects bishops, teachers, theologians, monastics, and educated Greek-speaking elites; the experience of the non-literate, Coptic-speaking, rural majority remains only partially visible in structure and not at all in interior. Proportionality must therefore be held on **two axes at once**: *proportional to evidence* (where the school tradition is dense and richly attested) and *proportional to probable ecology* (where the majority's formation was probably carried by communal-liturgical belonging and by the martyrdom pathway as much as by Scripture-at-depth — but this cannot be confirmed on the literate corpus). The pervasive stratum split in every lens above **is** the proportionality mechanism: it prevents the evidence-dense school stratum from being read as the whole ecology. Author-Gravity proportionality is held in parallel — the Learning-Formation and allegorical-method claims are the most Origen-weighted and are correspondingly bounded.

## 9. Ecological Integration and Integrity Assessment

**Hubs.** The **Logos** is the deepest integrating reality — the single center that makes the dimensions expressions of one thing. The **Scripture ↔ Transformation** dynamic is the central operating spine: where both are present and mutually reinforcing, the ecology is operating normally; where only one is present, it is under strain.

**Tension points.** The four Tensional gravities (Teacher–Bishop and Speculative-Doctrinal in the Organizational lens; Learning–Community in differentiated participation; Martyrdom–Contemplative in the Formation lens), plus the spatial city/desert tension.

**Integrity verdict — deliberately bounded.** *Within the literate-attested ecology*, this world exhibits a high degree of integration: identifiable primary gravities, stable supporting gravities, recurring formative practices, characteristic tensions, distinctive authority structures, and identifiable pathways of participation, all reinforcing one another. It has sufficient coherence to be treated as a distinct formation world — **for the population the surviving evidence can speak from.** Whether that integration held for the majority is the OG-4 question, deferred to Article 31 external review. Integrity is *granted for the literate-attested ecology and held open for the whole.*

## 10. Inhabited Synthesis — What Formation Was Like in This World

*[Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction; composite typical-practice narration; literate-attested ecology; no Tier 5. Written from inside the world's own logic (Article 23). Per-element traceability: "text opening as the reader is made ready / inexhaustible depth" → Stream 1 (Clement, Origen; Doc_04 C1); "learning and holiness one thing" → Streams 1, 5 (Doc_04 C5); "worship inhabited — water makes new, bread nourishes, year walks death-to-life" → Stream 3 (Athanasius's festal letters — corrected 2026-09-08, independent maturity-gap audit: this citation previously also named Origen's *On Prayer*, which was never actually vendored into this corpus in English (its Greek original is now vendored as an original-language witness only, not English evidence — corrected 2026-09-08, Round 3 Opus review), see `Lexicon-Chunks/alexlex028_prayer.md`); "God actively teaching — difficulty as invitation, suffering as pedagogy" → Stream 2/4 (Divine Pedagogy, Doc_04 C3); "participation in divine life the Logos opened" → Streams 4, 6 (Doc_04 C2/C4). No element is invented beyond these attested sources.]*

To be formed in this world was to live as though everything — the text, the day, the fast, the gathered assembly, the teacher's patience, even the persecution — was the one Logos teaching. Scripture was never only its surface; it opened as the reader was made ready, and there was always further, because the One it spoke could not be exhausted. Learning and holiness were not two things: to know more truly was to be changed more deeply, and a knowledge that left the knower unchanged was not yet the knowledge that mattered. Worship was not attended but inhabited; the water made new, the bread nourished, the year walked the soul through death into life. And under it all was the conviction that God was actively teaching, so that difficulty was invitation and suffering was pedagogy, and the whole of it was moving toward participation in the divine life the Logos had opened.

*[Named absence, not a filled silence: what this same world was like from inside for the non-literate, Coptic-speaking, rural majority — the greater part of it — the surviving evidence does not let us say, and it is not narrated here. Their belonging and, under persecution, their witness are structurally reconstructible; their interior is not. That absence is part of the honest account of the world, per Article 20 and OG-4.]*
