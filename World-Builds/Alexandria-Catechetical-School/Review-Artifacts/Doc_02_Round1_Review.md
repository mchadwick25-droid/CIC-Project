## SUBSTANTIAL FINDINGS

### Finding 1 — Eusebius (and the other §3.6 secondary narrative sources) lack the required five-dimension Author Gravity entry. [SUBSTANTIAL]

**Location:** §3.6 "Supplementary entries (secondary narrative and boundary voices)" — Eusebius of Caesarea entry (also Dionysius, Gregory Thaumaturgus, Palladius).

**What is wrong:** Framework V7.3 Part II, "Expanded Author Gravity Assessment," is explicit: *"Narrative Source Author Gravity entries for historians and hagiographers who function as secondary narrative sources for the world... These figures require their own Author Gravity entries applying all five dimensions (including transmission history)."* (Framework lines 259–262; see also line 225.) The four primary voices (§3.1–3.4: Origen, Clement, Athanasius, Didymus) each receive the full labeled five-dimension treatment (visibility / representativeness / influence / limitations / transmission history). Eusebius does not — he receives a single prose paragraph. Eusebius is the single most consequential distorting narrative source in this world (he is the scaffolding for the entire Pantaenus→Clement→Origen→Heraclas→Dionysius succession list, the source of the Origen–Demetrius conflict, and the source the document itself flags **HIGH risk for institutional/biographical claims**). He is precisely the figure the Framework's expanded requirement was written for. Dionysius, Gregory, and Palladius (a hagiographer/narrative source) are handled the same abbreviated way — each gets only a one-line "Risk:" tag.

**Why substantial:** This is a missing required deliverable component for the world's most important source-distortion vector. The prose paragraph touches representativeness, influence, and transmission obliquely but never systematically addresses visibility, and it does not present the five dimensions as the structured, auditable entry the Framework mandates — which is what makes the HIGH-risk conclusion methodologically load-bearing rather than asserted. It affects the rigor of a sourcing conclusion (check #1 and check #7 both call for the five dimensions to be *genuinely present* for the major voices, and Eusebius is a major voice for the Contested didaskaleion finding carried from Doc_01 §1.2).

**Evidence (fact of the requirement):** Framework V7.3 Part II lines 259–262 (extracted text). The HIGH-risk flag and its dependence on Eusebius are internal to the document (§3.6, §5.6, Stream 5, Stream 7). Web-verification of Eusebius's institutional role is not required — the defect is a method-completeness gap, not a factual error.

**Concrete fix:** Give Eusebius a full five-dimension Author Gravity entry in §3.6 (or promote him into §3 proper as a narrative-source entry), explicitly covering: **visibility** (the near-total dependence of the school's institutional record on him); **representativeness** (Caesarean, ~a century after Clement, apologetic aims, not an Alexandrian voice); **influence** (the succession scaffolding shapes all downstream institutional reconstruction); **limitations** (reconstructs origins from incomplete sources; orderly-succession picture questioned by modern scholars — van den Broek 1995, van den Hoek 1997, per Doc_01 §1.2); **transmission history** (textually sound; the risk is interpretive, not textual). At minimum, do the same for Palladius (a hagiographic/narrative source); Dionysius and Gregory may remain briefer as boundary voices, but should state which of the five dimensions are being waived and why.
