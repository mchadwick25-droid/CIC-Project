## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

This is the Catholic Church itself, in its most institutionally orthodox 16th-century form. Ignatius's own *Spiritual Exercises* and dictated *Autobiography* (both directly host-verified this pass) contain no Trinitarian or Christological deviation from any of Article 4's five commitments, and Trent's own canons (a text this candidate directly implements) explicitly reaffirm the Nicene Creed and Chalcedonian Christology by name. No floor complication found — the most straightforward A1 clearance of any candidate in this batch.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity

Not applicable.

### A3 — Contemporary movements

The direct, real rival relationship in this batch is Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities — the Jesuits were founded explicitly within, and as a response to, the same crisis those two candidates answer from the opposite direction. **One genuine scope-adjacent complication, correctly identified as out-of-window rather than silently omitted:** the Chinese and Malabar Rites controversies, in which later Jesuit missionary practice came into serious dispute with Rome, intensify in the mid-to-late 17th century and are formally condemned in 1704/1742/1774 — well past this candidate's own 1650 close. First-generation figures (Ignatius d. 1556, Xavier d. 1552, Nadal d. 1580) predate the dispute entirely. Noted here so a later reviewer does not mistake the omission for an oversight.

### A4 — Hand-selected inclusion: invoked

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

### Section A conclusion

**Clears Section A without qualification** — the cleanest floor clearance in this batch, no disclosed complication required.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: strong, with one real structural gap disclosed

Per the dossier, directly host-verified: the *Spiritual Exercises* (Mullan 1914), Ignatius's dictated *Autobiography* (O'Conor 1900), a first volume of his letters (O'Leary/Goodier 1914, 24 letters 1524–47 — a selection, not his full ~7,000-letter corpus), and Francis Xavier's own letters and life (Coleridge, Vol. 1, 1872). Plus the Waterworth Council of Trent Canons/Decrees, directly relevant as this order's own doctrinal charter. **Same methodological caveat as every sibling document: no figure below is word-extracted from vendored XML.**

**A real, disclosed structural gap, not smoothed over: no PD English translation of the Jesuit Constitutions exists anywhere** — the only English translation (Ganss, 1970/1996) is in copyright and access-restricted. This is the order's own governing document, and its absence from the available PD corpus is a genuine limitation on how fully this candidate's own institutional self-understanding can be sourced in its own words, distinct from Ignatius's own personal-formation voice (the Exercises, the Autobiography), which is well covered.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on personal spiritual formation (the Exercises, the Autobiography) and missionary/pastoral correspondence (Xavier's letters); genuinely thin on institutional self-governance (the Constitutions gap, §3 B1) and on other first-generation voices — Nadal's *Annotations and Meditations* and Faber's *Memoriale* are both closed leads per the dossier (§4 below).

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no overlap, over a millennium's separation, though a real thematic (not textual) echo with Desert Monasticism and Cappadocian worth naming in Doc_01 per §0 above — an interior-discipline parallel, not a source overlap. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** a real, disclosed, not-disqualifying source overlap with the Tridentine Church (also in this batch) — both would likely cite the Waterworth Trent Canons/Decrees, this candidate with implementing/corroborating weight, the Tridentine Church with native/foundational weight, the same shared-text pattern this project's existing corpus already handles for PAHC/Latin-Apologists' Tertullian case. No figure overlap with Lutheran Wittenberg, the Reformed cities, the Anabaptists, or Lollardy.

### B4 — Audiences

A genuinely distinct pull from every other candidate in this batch — the only entry with a real missionary/global dimension (Xavier's own letters run from Goa to Malacca to the Moluccas) and the only one whose core text (the Exercises) is a practiced spiritual discipline rather than primarily a doctrinal or polemical corpus.

### B5 — Scale

Rome, then worldwide by the window's close — the only candidate in this batch whose own stated geographic reach is explicitly global rather than regional-European, reflecting the real character of the order's own first-century expansion.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A without qualification, the cleanest in this batch; clears Sourcing strongly on personal-formation and missionary-correspondence material, with one real, disclosed structural gap (no PD Constitutions); clears Ecology on the same split; one real, non-disqualifying source-overlap item versus the Tridentine Church, already flagged for whichever Doc_02 runs first; distinct global audience and scale within the batch.

**Tier 1 — Strong seed, one binding condition, structural rather than evidentiary.** The missing Constitutions is a real gap Doc_02 must name plainly (this world's own governing self-understanding is under-sourced relative to its founder's personal voice), not a disqualifying complication — the same kind of honest, bounded disclosure this project's own precedent Step 0 documents already model for their own gaps.
