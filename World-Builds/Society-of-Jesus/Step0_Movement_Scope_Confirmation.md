## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears, on corrected evidence

This is the Catholic Church itself, in its most institutionally orthodox 16th-century form. Ignatius's own *Spiritual Exercises* and dictated *Autobiography* contain no Trinitarian or Christological deviation from any of Article 4's five commitments — positive evidence, not just absence: the Autobiography's own account of Ignatius's Trinity devotion at Manresa, and the Exercises' own Incarnation contemplation, both directly affirm commitments 2 and 3.

**Correction (Revision 2):** Revision 1 claimed Trent's canons "explicitly reaffirm the Nicene Creed and Chalcedonian Christology by name." False, caught on independent review (the identical error the Tridentine Church's own sibling Step 0 also made). Session III of the vendored Waterworth Canons/Decrees recites the full Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed but calls it only "the Symbol of faith which the holy Roman Church makes use of" — "Nicaea" appears solely in a footnote citation. "Chalcedon" appears in the decrees exactly once, as a disciplinary citation on ordination (Session XXIII, ch. XVI) — never a doctrinal reaffirmation by name. The corrected evidence is better than the false claim: the Session III creed recital matches Article 4's five commitments almost clause for clause. Trent is also not "this order's own doctrinal charter" (§3 B1 makes the same error) — the Society's actual founding charter is the 1540/1550 Formula of the Institute; Trent (1545–63, opening five years after papal approval) is a doctrinal anchor the order later implemented, and the corpus-map itself sets Trent's role as `context` for this world precisely because "it is not the Society's own composed voice."

### A3 — Contemporary movements

The direct, real rival relationship in this batch is Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities — the Jesuits were founded explicitly within, and as a response to, the same crisis those two candidates answer from the opposite direction.

**Correction (Revision 2): the Chinese/Malabar Rites controversy is not out-of-window — this was a factual error, caught on independent review.** Revision 1 claimed the controversy "intensifies in the mid-to-late 17th century and is formally condemned in 1704/1742/1774 — well past this candidate's own 1650 close," and that no cited condemnation date even matches the real record (Benedict XIV's actual Malabar condemnation is 1744, not 1774; 1773 is the Society's later suppression, apparently conflated). In fact the controversy sits inside this world's own 1540–1650 window: Ricci's China accommodation method (from the 1580s), de Nobili's Madurai accommodation (from 1606), Gregory XV's 1623 ruling on Malabar practices, the internal Jesuit Jiading conference (1627–28), and Propaganda Fide's 1645 decree condemning the Chinese rites as Morales presented them, are all within the window. "First-generation figures predate the dispute" is true of Ignatius/Xavier/Nadal individually, but the census defines this world as reaching China, Japan, India, Brazil, and Canada — a live, real controversy sits inside the window and needs treating as an actual scope boundary for Doc_01, not an exclusion (§4 item 4, corrected).

### B1 — Sourcing: substantially stronger than Revision 1 describes — this section was stale

**Correction (Revision 2): this document was drafted 2026-09-15, before a 2026-09-24 vendoring pass closed most of the gaps it describes below as open.** Per the dossier (updated 2026-09-24), REGISTRY.yaml, and the corpus-map, ten works are now vendored and assigned, not four: the *Spiritual Exercises* (Mullan 1914), Ignatius's dictated *Autobiography* (O'Conor 1900), Letters Vol. 1 (O'Leary/Goodier 1914, 24 letters 1524–47 — a selection, not his full ~7,000-letter corpus), Xavier's letters and life **both volumes** (Coleridge 1872 — Vol. 2 is now vendored, not just Vol. 1 as Revision 1 said), Canisius's *Summe of Christian Doctrine* (1622 English — a genuine first find, not previously checked), the 1606 Latin Constitutions (vendored as PRIMARY content, closing the gap this section originally called the binding condition), Nadal's 1595 Latin *Adnotationes* (also vendored as PRIMARY — Revision 1 called this a "closed lead"), and Boero's *Life of Peter Faber* (1873, context/provisional — a biography, not a translation of the Memoriale itself; how much of the Memoriale it quotes verbatim remains unchecked, so it only partly resolves the Faber gap, contra Revision 1's "closed"). **What remains genuinely open, narrower than Revision 1's "absent from the available PD corpus" framing:** no PD *English* translation of the Constitutions exists — the Ganss 1970/1996 translation is in copyright — so the order's own governing document is sourceable only in Latin. ### B2 — Ecology

**Correction (Revision 2):** strong on personal spiritual formation (the Exercises, the Autobiography), missionary/pastoral correspondence (Xavier's letters, both volumes now), catechetical writing (Canisius), and institutional self-governance (the Constitutions and Nadal's Adnotationes, both now vendored in Latin — §3 B1, corrected).
