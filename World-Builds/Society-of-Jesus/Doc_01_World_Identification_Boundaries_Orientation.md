## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Society of Jesus (Founding Century).

**Short Description:** The formation of the Society of Jesus from its 1540 papal approval through the close of its first century — a religious order organized around a repeatable technology of personal spiritual formation, the *Spiritual Exercises*, and deployed outward as a mobile body of teachers, confessors, and missionaries rather than a cloistered contemplative community. — **Confidence: Documented** for the 1540 approval and the order's basic institutional shape; **Widely Accepted** for "founding century" as a periodization.

**Core Identity:** This is a world organized around *formation as a portable discipline* before it is organized around a fixed place, liturgy, or rule of common life in the monastic sense. Its defining formative act is the individual, guided month of prayer Ignatius of Loyola designed — the *Spiritual Exercises* — a structured, repeatable technology for discerning and choosing a way of life, undergone first by Ignatius himself and then imposed as the entry-rite of formation for every man the order later admitted (Spiritual Exercises, Mullan 1914; Autobiography, O'Conor 1900). Around that formation-core sits an order built for mobility and deployment rather than stability: government by a Superior General with broad discretionary authority, an additional fourth vow of special obedience to the Pope specifically "regarding the missions" (documented at the level of the Formula of the Institute and the order's own governing tradition; the Constitutions and Nadal's *Scholia* explaining them are vendored for this world but the Constitutions text itself is held at second-witness status — see §7 below), and a founding generation that treated obedience itself as a formation discipline, not merely an administrative requirement — Ignatius's own 1542 letter to Giovanni Battista Viola works through what "blind obedience" does and does not require of a subject (Letters and Instructions, O'Leary/Goodier 1914, Letter XV). — **Confidence: Documented** for the Exercises' formative centrality and the fourth-vow provision as part of the order's self-understanding; **Widely Accepted** for obedience-as-formation-discipline as a recurring theme, evidenced directly in the one 1542 letter cited above rather than asserted as a blanket generalization.

**Distinctive Contribution:** This world's relationship to Tridentine Catholicism (Atlas VI.22, a sibling candidate in this same batch) requires honest, two-sided engagement rather than a comfortable answer in either direction, following Step 0's own framing (§2 A1, A4; §3 B3).

On one reading, the Society of Jesus is not a distinct world at all but an *executing instrument* of the Tridentine program: the corpus-map itself assigns the shared Waterworth Trent Canons/Decrees `role: tradition` for the Tridentine Church and `role: context` for this world specifically "since this is not the Society's own composed voice" (Step 0 §2 A1, §3 B3, item 3 of §4). Trent (1545–63) is this world's doctrinal anchor, not its own charter, and the Jesuits functioned historically as among Trent's "most vigorous executors" (dossier §5) — catechizing, teaching, and staffing the confessional in direct service of a program the Society did not itself convene or author.

On the other reading — and this is the reading this document adopts as the world's own distinctive ground, not merely an alternative — the Society's founding charism, spirituality, and institutional identity both *predate* and *exceed* Trent's own program. Ignatius's conversion (1521, at Pamplona; Autobiography, O'Conor 1900, ff. 361–400) and the composition of the core of the *Exercises* both occurred well before Trent convened in 1545. Papal approval of the new order (1540) preceded Trent's opening by five years, and the Society's actual founding charter is the 1540/1550 *Formula of the Institute* (Step 0 §2 A1) — a document of the Society's own self-organization, not a conciliar decree it later implements. The Exercises' own formation logic — an individually undergone, discerned choice of a state of life, oriented toward "finding God in all things" rather than fixed liturgical observance — has no equivalent within Trent's own conciliar output, which legislates sacramental discipline and doctrinal boundary rather than personal spiritual formation of this kind. And the order's mobile, globally deployed missionary reach (Rome, then India, Japan, China, Brazil, Canada — Step 0 §1, §3 B4–B5) exceeds anything Trent itself organized, even accounting for Trent's own missionary instrument, the later Propaganda Fide (1622; noted at Step 0 §3 B4 as the Tridentine Church's own comparable reach). — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the relative chronology (Ignatius's conversion and the 1540 approval preceding Trent); **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for the claim that the Exercises' formation logic is a genuinely distinct contribution rather than an application of Tridentine doctrine.

A second, separate distinctiveness question is this world's relationship to Devotio Moderna (a separate, already-built world in this project's portfolio). Devotio Moderna's own Step 0 record documents a real, "both-directions" influence relationship: the disciplined, interior, household-based spiritual reading it fostered fed forward into Jesuit formation. The vendored anchor for this, cited directly in Step 0 §3 B2, is a footnote in Coleridge's *Life and Letters of St. Francis Xavier* (Vol. I) citing Alcazar's *Chrono-Historia*: Ignatius gave each of the early companions gathered at Monte Cassino a copy of *De Contemptu Mundi* — the *Imitation of Christ* — and the same volume separately narrates the companions' regular practice of "spiritual reading in the Bible and the Imitation of Christ" alongside weekly confession and daily examination of conscience (Coleridge 1872, pp. 4531–36, 3506–09; directly re-verified against the vendored file for this document). But influence is not identity: Devotio Moderna was a lay, largely non-vowed household movement organized around communal manuscript copying and withdrawal into disciplined domestic devotion; the Society of Jesus is a vowed, centrally governed religious order organized for outward mission and public instruction. The Imitation's interior discipline was received into Ignatian formation and put to different institutional use, not continued as the same form of communal life. — **Confidence: Documented** for the Alcazar/Coleridge citation itself (directly re-checked against the vendored text); **Widely Accepted** for the broader characterization of Devotio Moderna's own communal form as distinct from the Society's.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed here — per Constitution Article 29 and this project's established practice, confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform on its own behalf. Noted for the record, not decided: the Society of Jesus is not merely a historical world with a living institutional descendant in the way most of this project's worlds are — it is itself a still-existing, globally active religious order and one of the largest in the present-day Catholic Church, a fact that makes the Article 28/29 distinction between the *historical formation world reconstructed here* (1540–1650) and the *order's own present-day self-understanding* especially load-bearing, not a formality. Nothing in this document's characterization of the founding century may be read as, or extended into, a claim about the contemporary Society of Jesus's own current identity, governance, or self-understanding. **Status: PENDING.**

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Time Horizon:** 1540–1650 CE. This is the window Step 0 already settled (Candidate identification, §1: "Atlas VI.11, 'The Society of Jesus,' 1540–1650, Rome then worldwide") and this document does not reopen it. What follows defends it on its own historical grounds; §7 below carries forward, honestly and separately, that the *vendored sourcing* inside this window is markedly uneven — a sourcing condition, not a boundary question.

- **Beginning point (1540):** papal approval of the new order under Paul III, formalizing the Formula of the Institute the first companions had already drawn up. This is a genuine ecological rupture, not an arbitrary date: before 1540 Ignatius and his companions were an unrecognized band of university-trained laymen and priests bound by personal vows taken at Montmartre (1534); after 1540 they were a canonically constituted religious order with the institutional standing to receive assignments directly from the Pope and to grow through its own recruitment and government. — **Confidence: Documented** for the approval as an event; **Widely Accepted** for its use as this world's own defensible starting point.
- **Ending point (1650):** this document defends 1650 as the close of the order's *founding century* — a period in which the Society's distinctive form (mobile deployment, the Exercises as universal formation-entry, government by a Superior General, rapid missionary and educational expansion) was established and had not yet been overtaken by the later crises (the 18th-century suppressions; the fully mature 17th–18th-century Rites condemnations) that mark a different, later phase of the order's multi-century institutional continuity. 1650 sits after the order's own first four Superior Generals (Ignatius, Lainez, Borgia, Mercurian) and after the 1599 *Ratio Studiorum* had standardized Jesuit education, and before the sharpest phase of the Chinese and Malabar Rites controversy — Benedict XIV's Malabar condemnation (1744) and the Society's 1773 suppression both lie well beyond this window (Step 0 §2 A3). This is a boundary argued on the order's own historical development, not a boundary set by what happens to be vendored: the Rites controversy's *earlier* phase is genuinely inside this window (below), and this document does not draw 1650 to exclude it.

  Honestly disclosed and carried forward rather than smoothed over: the *vendored primary corpus* for this world does not sustain equally rich documentation across the full 1540–1650 span. Institutional and administrative voice, via Ignatius's, Nadal's, Lainez's, and Salmeron's own letters, is documented directly to 1562 (Nadal's own letters) and, via Salmeron's letters alone, to c. 1585 — not via Lainez's own volume, which covers only 1536–1556 and ends before his own 1558–65 generalate, and not via Polanco's *Chronicon*, whose vendored volume (Tomus V) covers only the single year 1555. Missionary/global-reach material in the vendored corpus is concentrated in the founding generation, ending at Xavier's 1552 death; nothing is vendored on Ricci, de Nobili, or the Jesuit Relations. These are Step 0's own corrected figures (§3 B1–B5, §4 items 1, 5), restated here without re-derivation. The historical world extends to 1650; the documentary record this build can currently draw on thins well before that close, and Doc_02 inherits the obligation to state that plainly rather than let the world's real historical span imply a documentary richness the corpus does not yet have.

**Geographic Centers:** Rome — the order's government, the residence of the Superior General, and (per Step 0 §1) the seat from which Ignatius directed the order's rapid expansion by letter. But this world's geography cannot be honestly stated as Rome alone: the census (Atlas VI.11) and Step 0 both describe the order's own self-understanding as a body deployed "worldwide" within this same window — India and the Fishery Coast and Malacca/Moluccas missions (Xavier, 1541–48; vendored, Coleridge Vol. I), Japan and China (Xavier's final years to his 1552 death; vendored, Coleridge Vol. II), and, later in the window and outside the vendored corpus, further missions in China (Ricci, from the 1580s), south India (de Nobili's Madurai mission, from 1606), Brazil, and Canada (Step 0 §2 A3, §3 B2, B4–B5). Naming these missionary centers here, even where thinly or not-at-all sourced in the corpus currently in hand, follows the task's own instruction and Article 20's affirmative duty not to let a documentary gap silently narrow the world's own real geography. — **Confidence: Documented** for Rome's centrality and the founding-generation missions (India, Japan/China to 1552); **Widely Accepted** for the order's continued expansion into China, south India, Brazil, and Canada across the rest of the window, which this document states as historical fact while flagging (§7) that it currently rests on general historical knowledge and census framing rather than vendored primary sourcing.

**Cultural Environment:** Counter-Reformation Catholicism — a Catholic Church actively reorganizing its own doctrine, discipline, and formation in direct response to the Protestant Reformation, with Trent (1545–63) as its own defining conciliar articulation (Step 0 §2 A1, A3). The Society operated inside this environment as one of its most consequential instruments (teaching, catechesis, confession, missionary expansion) while also carrying a spirituality — personal, Ignatian, discernment-centered — that this environment did not itself generate (§1 above). — **Confidence: Widely Accepted.**

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Reformation itself: the Society was founded within, and substantially in response to, the same crisis Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities answer from the opposite direction — the sibling candidates in this same build batch with which the Society shares a direct rival relationship, per Step 0 §2 A3.
- The practical problem of forming a rapidly growing, geographically scattered body under a single coherent discipline — answered institutionally through centralized government under the Superior General and formationally through the Exercises as a portable, repeatable entry-rite (§1 above).
- The missionary encounter with non-European cultures and religious systems (Hindu, Confucian, indigenous American and Canadian), which by the later part of this window generated the Chinese/Malabar Rites controversy: whether Christian practice could be adapted to indigenous religious and social forms (ancestor rites, caste-linked custom) without compromising doctrine. Step 0 (§2 A3) establishes that this controversy is genuinely *inside* this world's 1540–1650 window, not a later development to be disclosed and excluded: Ricci's China accommodation method (from the 1580s), de Nobili's Madurai accommodation (from 1606), Gregory XV's 1623 ruling on Malabar practices, the internal Jesuit Jiading conference (1627–28), and Propaganda Fide's 1645 decree condemning the Chinese rites as one Jesuit missionary (Morales) had presented them, all fall within this window. This document treats the controversy as a real internal tension the world itself contains — a live disagreement, within the Society, over how far its own missionary method of *accommodation* (adapting presentation, language, and custom to reach a non-European audience) could extend before it ceased to be adaptation and became doctrinal compromise — rather than as an exclusion to note and set aside. Its texture cannot yet be written from the Society's own primary voice: no Ricci or de Nobili material is vendored, so this document states the controversy's existence and dates at **Documented** confidence (the councils, decrees, and conference dates themselves are well-attested historical events) while marking the internal Jesuit experience of the dispute — what accommodation felt like as a live, contested formation question from inside the order — as **Inferential-Thin** pending sourcing this build does not yet have (Step 0 §4 item 5; carried forward at §7 below).
- Regional political fragmentation and the wars of religion across 16th–17th-century Europe, within which the order operated as a genuinely transnational body answerable to Rome rather than to any one crown — a structural tension recorded but not developed here.

**Historical Catalysts:** 1540 papal approval and the Formula of the Institute (§2 above); Xavier's 1552 death, closing the vendored corpus's own richest missionary-voice material (Step 0 §3 B2, B5); Ignatius's 1556 death; Lainez's 1558–65 generalate as second Superior General (his own vendored letters, however, cover only 1536–1556 — see above); the 1599 *Ratio Studiorum*, standardizing Jesuit education across the order's rapidly multiplying colleges. — **Confidence: Documented** for the 1540, 1552, and 1556 dates and Lainez's generalate dates; **Widely Accepted** for the 1599 *Ratio Studiorum* as a genuine institutional catalyst. The *Ratio Studiorum* itself is not part of the vendored corpus for this world (§7 below) — it is stated here as a well-established historical fact governing this world's own institutional development, not as a source this build has directly examined.

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's four questions directly, per Step 0's own framing that these are Step 1's work to perform, not Step 0's:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** The *Spiritual Exercises* functioning as a repeatable formation technology: a structured, individually guided process of prayer and discernment, undergone by Ignatius himself, then formalized as the entry-rite through which every subsequent Jesuit was formed (Spiritual Exercises, Mullan 1914; Autobiography, O'Conor 1900). This is the world's most distinctive recurring ecology — a portable, repeatable discipline rather than a fixed liturgical or communal-life practice — and the feature Step 0 itself names as this batch's genuinely distinct pull (§3 B4: "the only entry whose core text is a practiced spiritual discipline rather than primarily a doctrinal or polemical corpus"). — **Confidence: Documented.**
- **What recurring gravities exist?** Deferred to Doc_04, per the Forces Framework's own governance (§4, Step 1 output is preliminary forces identification, not gravity discovery). Named here only as candidates for later testing, not as findings: the Exercises' discernment logic; obedience as itself a formation discipline (§1 above); accommodation/adaptability as missionary method (§2 above, and its own internal contestation via the Rites controversy).
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Authority and formation dominate, in a specific configuration: obedience to the Superior General, and (via the fourth vow) direct obedience to the Pope specifically regarding missionary deployment, are recurring, load-bearing authority structures documented in the order's own correspondence (Ignatius's 1542 letter on the two ways obedience can be "blind," §1 above) and in its own governing self-description (the Constitutions and Nadal's *Scholia* explaining them — both vendored, both held at second-witness status per §7 below, not yet quotable verbatim as primary text). Adaptability/accommodation as method — adjusting language, custom, and pedagogy to reach a given audience, whether European students or non-European converts — recurs across the founding generation's own missionary correspondence (Xavier's letters, vendored) and is also the exact feature the Rites controversy later contests (§2 above). Worship in the ordinary parish or monastic sense is comparatively less central to this world's own recurring self-description than formation and authority are — a limitation this document names rather than papers over, to be carried forward to Doc_05's ecology-lens proportionality.
- **What distinguishes this world from the Tridentine Church and from Devotio Moderna specifically?** Both engaged fully at §1 above: from the Tridentine Church, by the Society's own founding charism, spirituality, and institutional identity predating and exceeding Trent's own conciliar program, even while functioning historically as one of Trent's most vigorous executing instruments; from Devotio Moderna, by the documented spiritual-reading influence relationship (the Imitation of Christ at Monte Cassino) being received into and put to different institutional use within a vowed, centrally governed, outward-facing missionary order rather than continued as the same lay, household-based communal form.

## 4. Strand Determination (Constitution Article 21)

Per Article 21: strand is "a finding, never a presupposed universal schema," accountable to evidence, and never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality. This determination must not rest on sequential-development or causal reasoning — a later-appearing pattern is not, by virtue of appearing later, a "phase" rather than a strand, and an earlier-appearing pattern is not, by virtue of appearing first, automatically the whole world's own single throughline.

**The candidate split, named honestly:** a European/institutional-administrative wing (Rome-centered government, the Constitutions, catechesis and schooling within Europe) versus a missionary/accommodationist wing (Xavier's Asian missions; and, later in the window, Ricci's China method and de Nobili's Madurai mission) is the split Step 0 itself flags as worth naming, via its own disclosure of the sourcing gap after Xavier's 1552 death (§3 B2, B4–B5; §4 item 5).

**Finding: strand-singular, as a Step 1 working determination — the candidate split is named and held open, not resolved either way, for an evidentiary reason specific to Article 26, not a sequential-development reason.**

What would make this a genuine Article 21 strand finding is not that the missionary wing came *later*, or that it eventually diverged from Roman practice as history unfolded — that would be exactly the forbidden sequential-causal reasoning. The two wings were, in fact, substantially *simultaneous*: Xavier was already directing his own mission in Asia with real day-to-day latitude while Ignatius governed from Rome and Nadal traveled to explain the Constitutions to the provinces — contemporaneous activity, not a later offshoot of an earlier institutional core. A genuine strand claim would need to rest on a distinct, evidenced ground of authority or formation logic specific to the missionary wing, the way ijc's Ambrosian strand rests on Ambrose's own documented sacramental-leverage claim rather than merely on Ambrose acting later than Constantine.

That evidence is not yet in hand for this candidate split's most distinctive expression. Xavier's own vendored letters (Coleridge, Vols. I–II) attest founding-generation missionary adaptation — learning about local custom, working through interpreters, adjusting method to the audience — but this is continuous with, not yet clearly divergent from, the order's single shared formation logic (discernment, obedience to superiors, the fourth vow's own missionary orientation). The *developed*, most consequential form of accommodation this candidate strand would actually need to rest on — Ricci's scholar-mode engagement with Confucian literati, de Nobili's adoption of a Hindu ascetic's manner of life — has **no vendored primary material at all** (Step 0 §3 B2, B5; §4 item 5). Asserting a strand finding on that basis now would rest the finding on an unexamined, in fact wholly absent, source — precisely the condition Article 26's Author Gravity discipline forbids for a confirmed gravity, and this document extends the same discipline to a strand claim resting on the same missing evidence.

**Governing consequence for later steps:** this world is treated as strand-singular for internal-construction purposes at Step 1. The candidate split is not dismissed — it is carried forward as an explicit revision trigger: if the sourcing gap named in Step 0 §4 item 5 is closed (Ricci, de Nobili, or Jesuit Relations material acquired and vendored), Doc_04 must re-test whether the missionary/accommodationist wing shows a genuinely distinct, evidenced ground of authority or formation logic, rather than inheriting this document's strand-singular finding by default. — **Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for the strand-singular working disposition; the missionary/accommodationist split is **held open**, not assigned a confidence level of its own.

## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement (§4), this is orientation for Step 2's source-ecology scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Doc_08.

**What generated this world:** Ignatius's own convalescence and conversion after his leg was shattered by a cannonball at the 1521 siege of Pamplona, his subsequent reading and reinterpretation of his own interior experience at Manresa, and the composition there of the core of the *Spiritual Exercises* (Autobiography, O'Conor 1900) — the founding generative event, attested in Ignatius's own dictated first-person account. Around that core, the Paris student cohort — Xavier, Faber, Lainez, Salmeron, and the other early companions who bound themselves together at Montmartre in 1534 before the order's 1540 approval — supplied the order's first members and much of its earliest missionary and administrative leadership. — **Confidence: Documented** for the Pamplona wound and the Autobiography's own account of Manresa; **Widely Accepted** for the Paris cohort's formative role.

**What larger world was it embedded in:** Counter-Reformation Catholicism — per the census (Atlas VI.11) and Step 0's own framing, the broader 16th-century Catholic Church actively reorganizing itself in response to the Reformation, with Trent as its defining conciliar articulation (§1, §2 above). — **Confidence: Widely Accepted.**

**What was it responding to:** two things simultaneously, at different levels. Externally and doctrinally, the Reformation's challenge to Catholic doctrine and ecclesial authority — the same crisis this batch's Lutheran and Reformed candidates answer from the opposite direction (Step 0 §2 A3). Internally and personally, Ignatius's own spiritual crisis and convalescence — a founder's private formation experience that became, through the Exercises, the order's own shared and repeatable formation technology (§1 above; Autobiography, O'Conor 1900). — **Confidence: Documented** for Ignatius's own crisis as narrated in his own Autobiography; **Widely Accepted** for the Reformation-response framing.

**What was it refusing:** Protestant doctrine and ecclesial authority claims, in continuity with the wider Counter-Reformation project. This document names that refusal at only preliminary strength and flags, per Step 0's own disclosure, that the order's most systematic self-articulation of its own governing commitments — the Constitutions, and Nadal's *Scholia* explaining them — sits at **second-witness status**, not full primary access, under Mark's 2026-09-25 scan-quality ruling (both are vendored but flagged as garbled scans; Step 0 §3 B1, §4 item 1). This document's characterization of what the order refused therefore rests on the founder-generation letters and the Autobiography rather than on the order's own fullest institutional self-statement, and that evidentiary limit is named here rather than smoothed over. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the general Counter-Reformation refusal of Protestant doctrine; the evidentiary limit above is a construction discipline note, not itself a historical claim.

## 6. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits:** the documented spiritual-reading influence of Devotio Moderna on Ignatius and the early companions — the Imitation of Christ given to each of them at Monte Cassino, and their own ongoing practice of reading it alongside daily examination of conscience (Coleridge 1872; §1 above, directly re-verified against the vendored file for this document). This is a real, sourced inheritance relationship, not an assumed one, and Step 0 itself names it as a "both-directions ancestor" relationship on Devotio Moderna's own side (feeding both Luther's schooling and Jesuit pedagogy). — **Confidence: Documented** for the Alcazar/Coleridge citation itself.

**What this world transmits forward:** global missionary Catholicism and a distinctive model of Catholic education, both historically real and extending well past this world's own 1650 close — the order's later colleges, its continued missionary presence on multiple continents, and (far beyond this world's own horizon) its continued existence into the present day. This document does not reconstruct that later history; it is named here for orientation only, per the same discipline ijc and alx both apply to their own worlds' forward continuities. — **Confidence: Widely Accepted**, stated as orientation rather than as this document's own historical claim.

**Relationship to Trent:** implementing and executing, not originating — restated from §1 and §2 above rather than re-argued. The Society functioned as one of Trent's most vigorous executing instruments while carrying a founding charism and spirituality of its own that predates and exceeds Trent's own conciliar program.

**Living heir(s):** the Society of Jesus itself — still active, still globally present, still the largest religious order of men in the present-day Catholic Church. This is not a case where a reconstructed historical world's living descendant is a separate, later institution; the living tradition and the historical formation world reconstructed here share the same name and the same continuous institutional lineage. Article 28's requirement to distinguish historical reconstruction from a living tradition's present-day self-understanding therefore applies with particular force here, not as a formality: nothing in this document, or in any document built on it, may be read as a claim about the contemporary Society of Jesus's own present identity, governance, theology, or self-understanding. Article 29 confirmation of Living Tradition Status remains a project-lead act, not performed here (§1 above). — **Confidence: Documented** that the Society is a still-active, continuous institution; the Article 28/29 disposition itself is **PENDING**, not a historical claim this document can resolve.
