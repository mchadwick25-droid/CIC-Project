## 1. Candidate terms

Each entry: term (Greek/Coptic form, transliteration), preliminary definition, initial tier estimate (1 = most central/cross-strand; 2 = significant but more bounded; 3 = specialized or single-source), strand attribution, Author Gravity risk flag, and source anchor (from Doc_02).

### 1.1 ἀναχώρησις (*anachōrēsis*) — "withdrawal"

- **Preliminary definition:** the act of departing settled village or civic life for marginal or remote land, undertaken as the ascetic project itself rather than a change of residence (Doc_01, Section 1).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1 — Doc_01's own candidate gravity language names this the defining act across all three strands.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand (A, B, and C all instantiate withdrawal, differently — Antony's progressive withdrawal in stages; Pachomius's communal withdrawal into a rule-governed enclave; Nitria/Kellia/Scetis's semi-anchoritic dispersal).
- **Author Gravity risk:** low as a term, but the *specific form* withdrawal takes is heavily shaped by which author describes it (Athanasius's narrative staging of Antony's withdrawal vs. the Pachomian Rule's institutional framing).
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Sections 1.1–1.2.

### 1.2 ἀποταγή (*apotagē*) — "renunciation"

- **Preliminary definition:** formal renunciation of property, family ties, and worldly status as the entry act into ascetic life.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand, though most formally codified in Strand B (the Pachomian Rule specifies renunciation of personal property as a condition of entry; Strand A and C practice it without comparable codification).
- **Author Gravity risk:** low.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.2.

### 1.3 ξενιτεία (*xeniteia*) — "exile," "estrangement"

- **Preliminary definition:** self-imposed estrangement from homeland, kin, and familiar social bonds, treated as a distinct ascetic discipline in its own right, not merely a consequence of withdrawal.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2.
- **Strand attribution:** most strongly attested in Strand A/C material (the *Apophthegmata*); less prominent, though not absent, in Strand B's more settled communal framing.
- **Author Gravity risk:** moderate — concentrated in the *Apophthegmata* tradition (Doc_02, Section 1.5), itself already flagged as passing through later compilers' selection.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.5.

### 1.4 ἡσυχία (*hēsychia*) — "stillness," "quietude"

- **Preliminary definition:** interior and exterior stillness, cultivated as both precondition and fruit of ascetic discipline; later foundational to the Byzantine hesychast tradition, though that later development sits well outside this world's own c. 430 closing boundary (Doc_01, Section 2.3).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand, but structurally easiest to achieve in Strand A and C's more solitary settings than in Strand B's communal labor-and-liturgy rhythm — a genuine point of strand-differentiated texture, not merely of degree.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low as a general term; the specific theological elaboration of hesychia as a stage of practice is more concentrated in Evagrius (Section 1.6 below).
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Sections 1.1, 1.5.

### 1.5 λογισμοί (*logismoi*) — "the thoughts"

- **Preliminary definition:** the intrusive thoughts or temptations understood as the primary interior battlefield of ascetic struggle. Evagrius Ponticus systematized these into a set of eight — gluttony, lust (fornication), avarice, sadness (*lypē*), anger, *acedia*, vainglory, and pride — intended as a diagnostic taxonomy for identifying the process of temptation.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1 — this is the closest thing this world's own vocabulary offers to a systematic interior psychology, and it anchors Doc_01's "spiritual combat" candidate gravity.
- **Strand attribution:** the *systematized eight-part taxonomy* is Strand C-bound, specifically Evagrian (Kellia); the underlying experience of demonic "thoughts" as a category is attested more broadly across all three strands in less systematized form, especially in the *Apophthegmata*.
- **Author Gravity risk:** **high for the systematized form specifically** — the eight-part taxonomy rests on a single author (Evagrius), whose own transmission history (Doc_02, Section 1.4) is unusually fraught (post-553 condemnation, pseudonymous Greek survival). This document flags the taxonomy's precision as reflecting one author's systematizing project, not necessarily this world's own general vocabulary before Evagrius gave it that shape.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.4.

### 1.6 ἀπάθεια (*apatheia*) — "passionlessness," "freedom from disordered passion"

- **Preliminary definition:** the state of freedom from the disordered passions the *logismoi* provoke — in Evagrius's systematic scheme, the achieved goal of the *praktikē* (practical) stage of ascetic life, and the precondition for contemplation (*theōria*, Section 1.7).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2 — significant, but this document does not treat it as Tier 1 pending Doc_04's gravity testing, given how concentrated its systematic form is in one author.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand C-bound (Evagrian) in its systematized theological sense; a looser, non-technical sense of freedom from passion is attested more broadly, but this document does not yet have evidence distinguishing the two registers with confidence.
- **Author Gravity risk:** **high** — same concentration risk as *logismoi* (Section 1.5). Additionally flagged as a term with a documented cross-world collision risk (Section 3 below): "theosis"/deification-adjacent vocabulary is already substantially developed in World #2's own lexicon (per the project's existing multi-world vocabulary-isolation work), and *apatheia* sits in the same conceptual neighborhood without being identical to it — this document does not conflate the two but flags the proximity for explicit differentiation at Doc_06.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.4.

### 1.7 θεωρία (*theōria*) — "contemplation"

- **Preliminary definition:** in Evagrius's systematic scheme, the higher stage of ascetic life following *praktikē* and the achievement of *apatheia* — subdivided into "natural contemplation" (*physikē theōria*) and, at the highest stage, contemplation of God (*theologia*).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2, with the same Strand C/Evagrian concentration caveat as Sections 1.5–1.6.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand C-bound (Evagrian) in this systematized form.
- **Author Gravity risk:** **high**, same basis as Sections 1.5–1.6.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.4.

### 1.8 πένθος (*penthos*) — "mourning," "compunction"

- **Preliminary definition:** sorrowful, tearful awareness of one's own sin, cultivated as a positive ascetic discipline rather than treated as a state to be escaped.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2.
- **Strand attribution:** attested across strands, particularly prominent in the *Apophthegmata* tradition.
- **Author Gravity risk:** moderate, same compiler-mediation caveat as Section 1.3.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.5.

### 1.9 διάκρισις (*diakrisis*) — "discernment," "discretion"

- **Preliminary definition:** the capacity to distinguish rightly between competing courses of action, spirits, or thoughts — repeatedly treated across this world's teaching material as the master virtue governing all others, without which other disciplines become self-destructive.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand; a recurring theme across named elders in the *Apophthegmata* regardless of which settlement they are associated with.
- **Author Gravity risk:** moderate — concentrated in the *Apophthegmata*'s own compiled sayings (Doc_02, Section 1.5) more than in any single named author's systematic treatise.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.5.

### 1.10 νῆψις (*nēpsis*) — "watchfulness," "sobriety"

- **Preliminary definition:** vigilant attentiveness to one's own interior movements, closely related to *diakrisis* (Section 1.9) but denoting the ongoing act of watching rather than the resulting judgment.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand, though most systematically developed in Strand C (Evagrian) material. **Caveat** (consistency with the same caveat already given to *hēsychia*, Section 1.4): as a fully systematic technical term, *nēpsis*'s heaviest theological elaboration belongs to the later neptic/Philokalic tradition, well outside this world's own c. 430 closing boundary, even though its root (1 Peter 5:8) and Evagrius's own use of it are early.
- **Author Gravity risk:** moderate, similar basis to Section 1.9.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Sections 1.4–1.5.

### 1.11 γέρων (*gerōn*), ἀββᾶς/ἄββα (*abbas*/*abba*), ἄμμα (*amma*) — "elder," "father," "mother"

- **Preliminary definition:** honorific address terms for a spiritually authoritative elder, male (*abba*) or female (*amma*), whose sayings and example carry teaching authority without formal ecclesiastical office. *Gerōn* functions as a more general term for "elder" alongside the specifically parental honorifics.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1 — this vocabulary cluster names the entire authority structure of Strand A and C (Doc_01, Section 6).
- **Strand attribution:** primarily A and C, where authority is personal and elder-mediated rather than office-based; present but structurally secondary in Strand B, where the Rule's formal offices (housemaster, steward) supplement rather than replace elder-disciple address.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low as address terms; the *ammas'* own share of surviving material remains thin (Doc_02, Section 1.6) even though the honorific itself is well attested.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Sections 1.5–1.6.

### 1.12 κοινωνία (*koinōnia*) — "communion," "fellowship" — Pachomius's own name for his federation

- **Preliminary definition:** the New Testament term for fellowship/communion, adopted by Pachomius and his successors as the proper name for the federated network of monasteries under a single spiritual authority (by Pachomius's death, nine men's houses and two women's houses under one *koinōnia*).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1 for Strand B specifically; this document does not extend it to Tier 1 cross-strand status, since Strand A and C have no equivalent institutional referent for the term.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand B-bound as an institutional proper name; the underlying New Testament concept of fellowship is of course broader, but this document is flagging the term's specific technical use as Pachomius's own federation-name.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low — well attested across the Pachomian corpus's multiple recensions (Doc_02, Section 1.2), not dependent on a single voice.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.2.

### 1.13 σύναξις (*synaxis*) — "the gathering"

- **Preliminary definition:** the weekly (Saturday-to-Sunday) communal gathering for vigil, liturgy, and a shared meal, structurally central to Strand C's organizational pattern (Doc_01, Section 6).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand C-specific as the named weekly structure; Strand B has functionally comparable communal liturgical gathering but organized on a different (daily, Rule-governed) rhythm rather than under this specific name.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low, but the underlying evidence for *content* (as opposed to structure) is thin (Doc_02, Section 4) — this document flags that the term is well attested as a structural fact but should not be treated as carrying rich attested liturgical content.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 4.

### 1.14 κελλίον (*kellion*) — "the cell"

- **Preliminary definition:** the individual or small-group dwelling unit that is Strand C's basic architectural and organizational building block — also the namesake of the Kellia settlement itself (Doc_02, Section 5.1).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 2.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand C-specific as the term for the basic dwelling unit, though Strand A's Antony also occupies cell-like spaces at points in his career without the term necessarily being applied with the same technical weight.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low — independently corroborated by archaeological evidence (Doc_02, Section 5.1), a genuinely different evidentiary register from the literary sources.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 5.1.

### 1.15 χειρωναξία / ἐργόχειρον (*cheirōnaxia* / *ergocheiron*) — "handiwork," "manual labor"

- **Preliminary definition:** manual labor (chiefly rope- and basket-weaving) undertaken as both economic necessity and deliberate ascetic discipline against idleness (Doc_01, Section 4).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1 — cross-strand and directly load-bearing for this world's material self-sustenance and its own ascetic self-understanding simultaneously.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low — independently corroborated by papyrological evidence of actual monastic economic activity (Doc_02, Section 5.2), not dependent on literary portrayal alone.
- **Source anchor:** Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 5.2.

### 1.16 ἀπόφθεγμα (*apophthegma*) — "saying"

- **Preliminary definition:** the terse, memorable saying that is this world's primary vehicle of teaching — a form whose brevity is itself a formation technique (something to be turned over in the mind during solitary labor), not merely a container for content (Doc_02, Section 2.3).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 1.
- **Strand attribution:** cross-strand, though the collected form (the *Apophthegmata Patrum*) postdates this document's own closing boundary as a compiled written corpus even where the individual sayings' oral origin does not.
- **Author Gravity risk:** moderate — the compilers' own selection and arrangement is a distinct Author Gravity layer already flagged repeatedly (Doc_02, Sections 1.5, 2.3).
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Sections 1.5, 2.3.

### 1.17 ἀντίρρησις (*antirrhēsis*) — "talking back," "counter-speech"

- **Preliminary definition:** the specific ascetic technique of verbally countering a demonic *logismos* with a scriptural rebuttal at the moment of temptation, systematized by Evagrius in his *Antirrhetikos* (Doc_02, Section 1.4).
- **Initial tier estimate:** 3 — a specialized technique, not this world's general vocabulary.
- **Strand attribution:** Strand C-bound, single-author (Evagrian).
- **Author Gravity risk:** **high** — entirely dependent on one text by one author. The *Antirrhetikos*'s original Greek is lost; the complete text survives only in translation — Syriac (ed. Frankenberg), plus Armenian and Georgian — with additional fragmentary Sogdian material surviving from the Turfan finds. This fact is not yet present in Doc_02 and should be added there as a supplement at the next opportunity to revise that document, not cited as already established there (Section 4, open item).
- **Source anchor:** Doc_02, Section 1.4.

### 1.18 "Purity of heart" (Latin *puritas cordis*) — a translation-challenge entry, not a Greek/Coptic term

- **Preliminary definition:** John Cassian's Latin rendering of Evagrian *apatheia* (Section 1.6) for a Western Latin audience — not a literal translation but a deliberate substitution, adopted because *apatheia*'s Stoic-sounding claim to "passionlessness" had become theologically controversial by the early 5th century. Cassian anchors the substitution in Matthew 5:8 ("Blessed are the pure in heart"), giving Evagrius's psychological category a biblical register.
- **Initial tier estimate:** 3 — this term belongs to this world's *reception history* (via Cassian, writing in the 420s, at the very edge of Doc_01's c. 430 closing boundary) rather than to the world's own core vocabulary, and is included here specifically as a translation-challenge case study for Doc_06.
- **Strand attribution:** not strand-bound within this world — this is evidence of *this world's own vocabulary being deliberately altered* as it exits the world via Cassian's transmission to Gaul.
- **Author Gravity risk:** low as a documented translation choice, single-source (Cassian) by definition.
- **Source anchor:** verified this drafting pass; not yet present in Doc_02 and flagged as a research lead that should be added to Doc_02's Section 1 (Cassian as a primary transmission-adjacent voice) at the next opportunity to revise that document (Section 4, open item).

## 2. Terms considered and explicitly not included, with reasoning

- **Θέωσις (*theōsis*, "deification")** — deliberately not given its own candidate entry, despite appearing in ascetic-adjacent literature, because it is a heavily developed Tier 1 term in an earlier, archived Alexandria-world build (`Archive/Alexandria-Build-History/Alexandria-v7/Lexicon MD files/alexlex008_theosis.md`, confirmed Tier 1 this drafting pass; this file is archived, under an earlier world-name vintage ("Desert Christianity" was itself once used as a working label and the file references "Phase 3"), not a live `World-Builds/Alexandria/` folder, so this document's claim rests on an earlier-vintage citation and should be re-confirmed against whatever Alexandria's live rebuild eventually produces). This is consistent with the project's existing multi-world vocabulary-isolation concern (reflected in this branch's history, e.g. commit `bb0698c`, "strengthen vocabulary isolation between representatives at multi-world table"). **Notably, that same archived Alexandria file's own "Cross-build provisional constraint" states that Evagrius Ponticus's developed theosis theology "is most evidenced in the Desert Christianity world... Evagrian systematization: reserved for Desert Christianity"** — i.e., the neighboring world's own lexicon already points the Evagrian material back toward this world, which strengthens rather than weakens this document's claim to the *apatheia*/*theōria* cluster (Sections 1.6–1.7). Where this world's own material touches deification-adjacent ideas, this document routes that content through *apatheia* and *theōria* instead of *theosis*, and flags explicit cross-world differentiation, checked in both directions, as a required item at Doc_06.
- **Ἐρημίτης (*erēmitēs*, "hermit")** — not included as a separate candidate term because it functions descriptively rather than as this world's own operative technical vocabulary; Doc_01 and Doc_02 already use "anchoritic" (Section 1.1's *anachōrēsis* family) as the operative term for this pattern.

## 3. Language layer summary (per the Cultural Scope Author Gravity concern, Doc_01 Section 4 / Doc_02 Section 6)

Of the seventeen Greek/Coptic terms above, all seventeen reach this document through **Greek**-language sources (the *Apophthegmata*'s Greek collections, Evagrius's Greek corpus even where later retitled, Athanasius's Greek *Life*), even though Doc_01 and Doc_02 both document a predominantly Coptic-speaking participant base. This document has not identified a single candidate term above that survives to us primarily in a Coptic technical form independent of Greek mediation — a genuine gap, not a neutral finding. This is flagged as a priority research item for Doc_04 and Doc_06: Coptic-specific technical vocabulary, if it can be recovered (e.g., from the Coptic fragments of the Pachomian Rule and *Lives*, Doc_02 Section 1.2), should be actively sought rather than allowing this list's present Greek-only character to stand as if it were simply how this world spoke.
