## 1. Tier 1 Stories — Documented Historical Narrative

### 1.1 Antony's Call — Hearing Matthew 19:21

**Source:** Athanasius, *Life of Antony*, ch. 2.

**Story:** Athanasius records that Antony, not yet twenty years old and recently orphaned, entered church one day shortly after reflecting on how the apostles left everything to follow Christ, and heard the Gospel being read: "If you would be perfect, go, sell what you possess and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me" (Matthew 19:21). Athanasius records that Antony took the words as spoken directly to him, left the church, and gave away his inherited land and possessions, keeping only enough to provide for his sister.

**Formation ecology connection:** Directly generates gravity 1 (withdrawal) and gravity 7 (practical, personally-addressed scriptural engagement, Doc_08 Force 1B-ii). This is this world's own founding narrative for the specific interpretive posture Doc_05 §8.1–8.2 identifies as characteristic — scripture heard as immediate personal command, not general instruction.

**Tier justification:** Named author (Athanasius), specific text and chapter reference, composed within a generation of Antony's death (c. 356–362). Confidence: **Widely Accepted** as to the narrative's existence and general content within the *Vita*; **Contested** as to incident-level historical reliability specifically, per Doc_01 §10's carried-forward Rubenson/Athanasius tension and Doc_08 Force 1B-ii's own confidence label.

**Usage guidance:** May be told as remembered history with Athanasius named as the source and his own theological/political interests (Doc_02 §1.1) acknowledged where relevant — "Athanasius records that..." The Representative should not claim more certainty about Antony's specific interior experience than the source supports, and should be prepared to note, if asked, that Antony's own literacy and philosophical formation are independently contested (Doc_06 §2.2's [CT] tag).

### 1.2 Antony's Withdrawal to the Outer and Inner Mountain

**Source:** Athanasius, *Life of Antony*, chs. 3–14 (outer mountain); ch. 49–50 (inner mountain).

**Story:** Athanasius records Antony's staged withdrawal: first remaining near his village under an older ascetic's guidance, then to the tombs, then crossing the Nile to the "outer mountain" at Pispir (c. 286), where he remained in near-total seclusion for approximately twenty years before emerging to instruct the disciples who had gathered near him. Later, seeking greater solitude from the crowds his own reputation drew, he withdrew further to an "inner mountain" between the Nile and the Red Sea, where he spent most of the remainder of his life (Doc_01 §2.1).

**Formation ecology connection:** The paradigmatic instance of gravity 1 (withdrawal) as progressive intensification rather than single decisive act, and of gravity 3 (elder-mediated authority) as it takes shape once disciples begin gathering.

**Tier justification:** Same source and confidence basis as 1.1. The staged chronology (outer mountain c. 286–305/306; inner mountain from c. 311–313) is treated by Doc_01 §2.1 as the standard, well-established chronology, while incident-level narrative detail remains Contested on the same basis as 1.1.

**Usage guidance:** May be narrated as remembered history with the same author-named, distance-acknowledged framing as 1.1. Particularly useful for illustrating that withdrawal in this world's own logic is a lifelong, deepening practice rather than a single departure.

### 1.3 Pachomius's Founding of the Koinōnia at Tabennesi

**Source:** The Pachomian corpus — the *Lives of Pachomius* (Bohairic, Sahidic Coptic, and Greek recensions) and the Pachomian Rule.

**Story:** The *Lives* record that Pachomius, an early convert following military service, founded the first cenobitic community at Tabennesi in the Thebaid c. 320, initially joined by his brother John and subsequently by additional companions, establishing a written Rule, common property, and a hierarchy of housemasters and stewards. By his death in 346, the *koinōnia* had grown to nine houses for men and two for women.

**Formation ecology connection:** Directly generates gravity 6 (*koinōnia*, Doc_08 Force 1B-iii) and, in its ongoing coexistence with the elder-authority model, generates gravity 10 (the authority tension).

**Tier justification:** Named textual tradition (the Pachomian corpus) with a datable founding event, but the multiple, only partially overlapping recensions of the *Lives* carry a genuinely live, unresolved version-priority debate (Doc_01 §10; Doc_02 §1.2) — this document does not adjudicate that debate and holds this story at Tier 1 for the broad, cross-recension-consistent outline (founding date range, house count, membership scale at Pachomius's death) rather than for incident-level detail specific to any single recension. Confidence: **Widely Accepted** for the broad outline; **Contested** for recension-specific detail.

**Usage guidance:** May be told as remembered history, with the caveat — offered if the conversation reaches that depth — that the *Lives*' own textual history is complex and that this document does not claim priority for any single recension's specific wording.

## 2. Tier 2 Stories — Collected and Traditional Material

### 2.1 Abba Moses and the Leaking Jug

**Source:** *Apophthegmata Patrum*, Moses (Alphabetical Collection).

**Story:** The tradition tells that a brother at Scetis had committed a fault, and a council of elders was called to judge him. Abba Moses at first refused to attend, but when pressed, he came carrying a leaking jug filled with water, the water trailing out behind him as he walked. When the others asked what this meant, he said: "My sins run out behind me, and I do not see them, and today I am coming to judge the errors of another." Hearing this, the assembly forgave the brother and said no more to him.

**Formation ecology connection:** Directly illustrates gravity 5 (*diakrisis*) in its self-directed, humility-oriented register — discernment applied first to one's own condition before it is applied to another's — and the broader pattern Doc_05 §1 identifies of the tradition correcting toward moderation over judgment.

**Tier justification:** Attributed to a named figure (Abba Moses) but transmitted through the compiled *Apophthegmata* tradition (5th–6th century compilation, per Doc_02 §2.3's compiler-mediation caveat, carried forward at Doc_08 Force 3B-ii) rather than through a datable, single-authored text contemporary with the events. Confidence: **Widely Accepted** as to the saying's genuine place within the tradition; **Contested/Inferential** as to whether this specific incident occurred as narrated versus condensing a more general teaching pattern into a single memorable scene.

**Usage guidance:** Told as community tradition — "The tradition tells of Abba Moses..." — not as verified single-event history. The Representative should not claim the specific council scene as historically documented fact, only as the tradition's own preserved teaching.

### 2.2 Abba Arsenius's Call to Flee, Be Silent, Be Still

**Source:** *Apophthegmata Patrum*, Arsenius (Alphabetical Collection).

**Story:** The tradition tells that Arsenius, while still a tutor in the imperial court at Constantinople, prayed for guidance and heard a voice say: "Arsenius, flee the company of men and you will be saved." Having withdrawn to Egypt, he prayed again and heard: "Arsenius, flee, be silent, be still — these are the roots of sinlessness" (*fuge, tace, quiesce* in the Latin systematic collection).

**Formation ecology connection:** Directly illustrates gravity 1 (withdrawal), gravity 3 (the personal, directly-addressed mode of guidance this world's authority structure assumes), and *hēsychia* (Doc_06 §1.3) as a named, sought discipline.

**Tier justification:** Same basis as 2.1 — attributed to a named figure, preserved through the compiled tradition rather than a contemporary datable text. Arsenius's own historical existence and imperial-court background are independently well-attested in the broader tradition (Doc_01 does not treat this document's evidence base as extending to full verification of Arsenius's court career specifically, and this document does not overclaim it). Confidence: **Widely Accepted** for the saying's place in the tradition; **Inferential/Thin** for the specific court-tutor biographical frame.

**Usage guidance:** Told as tradition, not verified biography — "They say of Abba Arsenius that..." Particularly useful for illustrating gravity 1's own logic in its most compressed, memorable form.

### 2.3 Amma Sarah's Answer to the Visiting Elders

**Source:** *Apophthegmata Patrum*, Sarah (Alphabetical Collection).

**Story:** The tradition tells that when some elder monks came to Amma Sarah intending to test or humble her, saying, "Be careful not to become conceited, thinking to yourself, 'Look, anchorites are coming to see me, a mere woman,'" she replied: "According to nature I am a woman, but not according to my thoughts. It is I who am a man and you who are women."

**Formation ecology connection:** This world's single clearest direct textual evidence for the *ammas'* own teaching voice (Doc_02 §1.6; Doc_06 §1.6), directly illustrating both gravity 3 (elder-mediated authority, exercised here by a woman) and gravity 5 (*diakrisis*, deployed against a direct social challenge).

**Tier justification:** Same basis as 2.1–2.2. Confidence: **Widely Accepted** for the saying's place in the tradition; **Inferential/Thin** for the specific narrated encounter's historicity.

**Usage guidance:** Told as tradition. Given the genuine thinness of surviving material specifically attributed to named *ammas* (Doc_02 §1.6's own flagged asymmetry), this story should be used, not held back, when the conversation reaches questions of women's presence and authority in this world — but the Representative should be honest that this is one of only a small number of directly attributed sayings surviving for named *ammas*, not evidence of a fuller corpus that happens not to have been included here.

## 3. Tier 3 Stories — Attributed Tradition

### 3.1 Antony's Combat in the Tombs

**Source:** Athanasius, *Life of Antony*, chs. 8–10.

**Story:** The tradition, through Athanasius's own hagiographic account, portrays Antony shutting himself in an abandoned tomb for solitary combat, where he is assailed by demons in the form of beasts who beat him nearly to death, and later by a vision of demons in the form of wild animals filling the tomb. Antony is portrayed emerging from these confrontations progressively strengthened rather than destroyed, culminating in a vision of light that he understood as divine aid arriving once his own struggle had been sufficiently proven.

**Formation ecology connection:** This is this world's own paradigmatic portrait of gravity 2 (spiritual combat), told not as a neutral chronicle but as the tradition's own account of what total ascetic struggle looks like when carried to its furthest extremity — directly relevant to gravity 9's later Evagrian systematization (Doc_06 §§2.2–2.3) as the un-systematized, narrative-form precursor to that later psychological vocabulary.

**Tier justification:** This account carries clear hagiographic genre markers — physically embodied demons, a beast-form combat scene, a climactic vision of light — that distinguish it from the more restrained, incident-report register of Tier 1 stories (1.1–1.2) drawn from the same *Vita*. This document classifies it as Tier 3, the tradition's own account of what a formed life looks like at its most extreme, rather than Tier 1 documented history, precisely because the narrative register itself signals portrait rather than incident report. Confidence: **Contested** for the account as portrait of this world's own understanding of combat; **Inferential/Thin** for any claim about what specifically happened in the tomb.

**Usage guidance:** The Representative may offer this as the tradition's own account of what total spiritual combat was understood to look like, explicitly marked as such — "This is how the tradition remembers Antony's own struggle, what it believed total combat against the interior enemy could involve..." The Representative should not present the demons-as-beasts imagery as a claim about literal historical events, only as this world's own chosen register for representing interior struggle at its most extreme.

## 4. Tier 4 Stories — Historically Grounded Reconstruction

### 4.1 A Day in a Kellia Cell

**Story (reconstruction, explicitly marked):** In a typical day for a semi-anchoritic ascetic at Kellia, the day would open and close with recitation of the Psalter, continuous with manual labor — weaving rope or baskets — performed through the daylight hours in the cell's own workspace. On the sixth day, the ascetic would walk to the settlement's communal gathering point for the *synaxis* — a vigil, shared liturgy, and communal meal — before returning to the cell's solitude for the coming week.

**Confidence:** Inferential / Thin, per the Template's own rule that Tier 4 stories always carry this level regardless of individual element quality.

**Source Identification (required for Tier 4 — every element separately sourced):**
- *Continuous Psalter recitation:* Doc_02 §4 (liturgical evidence for constant/near-constant recitation as the substrate of prayer).
- *Manual labor in the cell, rope/basket weaving:* Doc_01 §4; Doc_02 §5.1–5.2 (textual, archaeological, and papyrological corroboration).
- *Cell as basic architectural unit, individual dwelling with attached oratory:* Doc_02 §5.1 (Kellia excavation findings).
- *Weekly Saturday-to-Sunday synaxis with vigil, liturgy, shared meal:* Doc_03 §1.13; Doc_06 §2.6.

**Formation ecology connection:** Synthesizes gravities 1, 4, and the Strand C-specific expression of gravity 5 into a single reconstructed daily rhythm, directly corresponding to Doc_07 §9's own architecture-based integration finding (the cell and the *synaxis* infrastructure jointly provisioning for both solitude and periodic communal accountability).

**Usage guidance:** The Representative must explicitly mark this as reconstruction from the outset — "In a typical day for someone formed in this tradition..." — and must be prepared, if asked, to acknowledge this is assembled from several independently attested elements rather than a single recorded account of one person's actual day.

## 5. Absent Stories

Per the Story Repository Chunk Template's Absent Story discipline: what might a participant expect this world to be able to tell, that this document cannot honestly tell, and why?

**A first-person, named woman's own extended narrative (as opposed to a preserved saying).** This world's evidence base offers named *ammas* and their sayings (2.3 above; Doc_02 §1.6) but no extended narrative account authored by, or centrally following, a named woman's own life comparable to the *Vita Antonii*'s treatment of Antony. This is not because women's ascetic practice was absent — Pachomius's own community included women's houses from an early stage (Doc_01 §2.1) — but because the surviving literary record was overwhelmingly produced by and about men (Doc_02 §6's Affirmative Duty discussion). This document does not invent such a narrative to fill the gap; the honest response is silence, named as such, per Doc_02 §6's own reasoning that this world's own valorization of anonymity may partially, but does not wholly, explain this absence.

**A Melitian ascetic's own first-person account.** The Nepheros archive (Doc_02 §5.2; Doc_08 Force 2A-ii) provides documentary evidence of a Melitian monastic community's ordinary business, but no narrative account in a Melitian ascetic's own preserved voice comparable to the Nicene-communion material this inventory otherwise draws on. This document does not construct one; the silence itself is informative about whose literary output this world's own dominant sources (produced substantially under Athanasius's own influence, Doc_08 Force 2A-ii) chose to preserve.

**A fully attested single-event narrative illustrating the authority tension (gravity 10) directly.** This document's evidence base establishes the tension itself clearly (Doc_04 §2, §6; Doc_07 §3) but offers no single preserved story in which a participant is shown directly navigating a conflict between elder-based and office-based authority within one narrated scene. The tension is well-evidenced structurally but not narratively dramatized in any surviving source this document has identified — this document does not manufacture such a scene to make the tension vivid, and flags this as a genuine limit on what a Representative can offer if a participant asks for a concrete story illustrating gravity 10 specifically.
