## (c) New Findings (fresh pass — all COSMETIC)

**New Finding 1 (cosmetic) — citation slip in new Section 2.3 material.** The Provisional Story Inventory note attributes the "Story Inventory Requirement" to "Framework **Part I**." It is in **Part II — Evidence Development.** Part I is "World Identification & Boundaries." Fix: change "Part I" to "Part II." (This is in content Round 1 never saw.)

**New Finding 2 (cosmetic) — prohibited-language / confidence mismatch, created by the Fix 3 downgrade.** Section 2.2 describes the Greek-priority position as "the current scholarly **consensus**," while the confidence line explicitly stamps the same claim "Widely Accepted (downgraded from Documented)." Framework lists "consensus" as **prohibited language unless the Documented level is genuinely met.** Having deliberately declined Documented, the prose should not call it a "consensus" (permitted language for Widely Accepted is "widely accepted"). Fix: replace "current scholarly consensus" with "the now-dominant scholarly view" or "widely accepted." This is the kind of drift the Fix 3 edit should have swept but left behind.

**New Finding 3 (cosmetic, lower confidence) — over-broad pseudonymity claim in §1.4.** Section 1.4 lists "including the *Praktikos* and *Chapters on Prayer*" among Evagrius's works transmitted "under pseudonymous attribution, chiefly to Nilus of Ancyra." The canonical pseudo-Nilus text is the *De oratione* (*Chapters on Prayer*). The *Praktikos*, however, is generally transmitted in Greek under Evagrius's **own** name (the basis for Guillaumont's critical edition). Including the *Praktikos* in the Nilus-attribution set slightly overstates the pattern. Fix: drop "*Praktikos*" from that specific clause or soften to "several of his ascetic-practical works."

**New Finding 4 (cosmetic, minor) — sixth label in the confidence map.** Section 9 introduces "**Speculative Territory, explicitly excluded from this document**" as a fifth bullet. This is not one of the Framework's five vocabulary levels; it marks content placed *outside* the confidence scale rather than assigning confidence to a retained claim, so it is arguably legitimate, but sits in mild tension with the document's own scrupulous five-level discipline. Optional: relabel as "Excluded (no claim made)" to avoid coining a pseudo-level.
