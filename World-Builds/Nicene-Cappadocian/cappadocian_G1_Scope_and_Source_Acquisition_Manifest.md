## Part A — World Scope: is the window and boundary right?

**[CORRECTED 2026-08-30] The independent review found this argument does not hold as presented — 15 blocking findings, not the "named risk, otherwise sound" picture given at first approval.** The three most consequential:

- **The world's own self-description is very likely factually wrong.** "The peace after the last martyrs" implies no martyrs died within this world's own span — but Eupsychius of Caesarea was executed under Julian in 362, inside the c.325–394 window, with his feast documented in Basil's own letters (which invite bishops to it). This is not a minor citation slip: the phrase is already the opening sentence of Eumathios's Permanent Prompt.
- **The document argues both that this world must not be equated with its three famous authors, and ends the world's timeline at those three authors' deaths** — an unresolved internal contradiction, not a subtlety. It also never canvasses whether the world is regional (in which case the region's own Homoian and Eunomian Christians are in-world, not "external weather") or confessional (in which case the "regional formation ecology" framing overreaches) — it is written as if it could be both without choosing.
- **A section the governing Construction Framework requires at this step — World Separation Criteria (does this world's own formation, worship, authority, or interpretation actually change enough to mark an ending) — is missing entirely.** Its absence is the direct cause of the third finding: the c.394 end date is argued from personnel dying and politics being won, not from any demonstrated change in the four things that are supposed to define a world's edge.
- Independently, both reviews separately caught the same underlying error — Basil's redated death year should read 377, not 378 (both wrote "September 378"; the actual redating literature argues 377) — for whatever it's worth as a confidence check on the diligence, catching the same thing twice from different angles is a good sign about the review, not about the document.

**This means Part A's scope is not actually confirmed yet, whatever the first approval said** — the underlying document needs real revision (the recommended repair order is in the ledger), and the revision could plausibly change the ending boundary and the strand-singular finding, not just its footnotes. The build thread's plan, unless Mark says otherwise: revise Doc_01 against the review's own repair order, run one more independent review round, and bring the corrected scope back here for Mark's actual re-confirmation before treating this as settled — because that is what G1 was supposed to be deciding in the first place.

Doc_01 as originally recovered (built under CF V7.3) argued the below; kept for reference, not as the settled position:

- **Self-description:** "the churches of Cappadocia and Pontus in the years the faith of Nicaea was fought for — from the peace after the last martyrs to the death of the last of the great household (c. 325 – c. 394)."
- **Beginning c. 325:** Widely Accepted (Nicaea; end of the Eastern persecutions).
- **Ending: two separate dates, per Doc_01's second revision.** The ecological break is **381** (the Council of Constantinople and the Theodosian settlement dissolve, by victory, the situational gravity this world organized around — Dominant Modern Reconstruction, argued from a stated six-question test). **c. 394** is a distinct, later choice — the evidentiary horizon for the founding generation's last securely-attested member (Gregory of Nyssa) — flagged as the softer, more arbitrary of the two judgments and open for external review. The alternative endpoints considered and declined: c. 400, or 398 (Eunomius' books burned) — both judged as aftermath, not the world's own life.
- **Strand finding:** strand-singular (Article 21) — the tempting episcopal/ascetic division declined because the same persons and families inhabit both; two fracture lines named instead (radical-ascetic/Gangra; the pneumatological rupture). Doc_01 itself flags this classification as defensible-but-contestable — a reviewer could argue the radical-ascetic line is a strand, not a fracture.
- **Geographic/cultural scope:** Cappadocia, Pontus (Iris valley), Armenia Minor (Sebaste), with education/mission extension arcs to Athens and Constantinople. Countryside and non-Greek-speaking church life named as thin throughout (Inferential/Thin), not filled in.
- **The world's own named risk, carried since Doc_01 and never retired:** this is a smaller, more author-concentrated evidence base than any world in the current set — three authors, one family, one friendship network. Doc_01 and Doc_02 both argue the regional-world framing holds; Critic Finding 2 (recovered, `Critic_Checkpoint_1_Simulated.md`) calls this the build's single deepest exposure and recommends a Cappadocia specialist judge it at Article 31 external review.

**What Mark is actually deciding here:** not whether the reasoning is competent (it is) — whether the boundary calls he'd have to defend to a specialist are the ones he wants standing: the soft 394 cut, the strand-singular classification, and building a regional "world" this concentrated in one household's archive. Any of the three can be revised now, cheaply; none can be revised later without reopening Doc_04 and Doc_08.
