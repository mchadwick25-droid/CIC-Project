## Section 2 — Preliminary Forces Identification

*(This records Doc_01's own Step 1 orientation, verbatim in substance, as the preliminary frame that governed Doc_02's scope — not a retrospective summary produced at this later stage. Per Doc_01, Section 7.)*

**What generated this world:** The spread of Christianity into Aramaic-speaking upper Mesopotamia via trade-route contact and a religiously plural local milieu (paganism, a significant Jewish community, and later Marcionite and Bardaisanite currents), taking root as a genuinely non-Greek theological and literary expression of the faith rather than a provincial extension of Greek patristic Christianity.

**What larger world it was embedded in:** The contested Roman-Persian Mesopotamian frontier zone; the broader pre-Christian Aramaic literary-wisdom tradition (e.g., Ahiqar); and a locally plural religious ecology in Edessa specifically that included paganism, Judaism, Marcionism, and Bardaisan's own following before any single "orthodox" identity had institutionally consolidated.

**What it was responding to at its origin:** Rival claimants to authentic Christian teaching circulating in the same Aramaic-speaking milieu and contested by this world's own primary voice directly: Marcion's rejection of the God of the Old Testament as maker of all things; Bardaisan's Nature/Fate/Free-Will cosmology and his popular hymnic mode of persuasion; and, later, Mani's fully independent universal religion, which itself emerged from a Jewish-Christian baptismal sect in nearby Mesopotamia and spread through this same zone in the 3rd century.

**What it was refusing:** Greek philosophical-categorical method as the necessary vehicle for theological legitimacy, in favor of a Semitic poetic-symbolic mode; and full assimilation into the Greek-language imperial-ecclesiastical apparatus centered at Antioch and Constantinople, in favor of a vernacular Aramaic register and locally rooted institutions (the qyama).

**How this preliminary identification governed Step 2 scope:** Doc_01's own framing directed Doc_02's search toward sources illuminating this world's actual engagement with Marcion, Bardaisan, and Mani as real contemporaries (not textbook heresiarchs), the differential persecution experience across the Roman/Persian frontier, and the silences around non-elite, non-literate qyama members' own voices — treated as a meaningful evidentiary gap to name, not fill by invention. Doc_01 additionally carried forward, from its own Strand Determination (Section 6), the differential intensity of state pressure between Roman Osroene and Sasanian Persia as a real, independently-documented systemic force distinguishing this world's Persian-side experience from its Roman-side experience — explicitly assigned to full Forces compilation at this document rather than to Strand Determination itself.

#### Force 1A-1: Edessa's Position as a Roman-Administered, Culturally Distinct Frontier Periphery Amid a Plural Local Religious Milieu

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
By the world's own c. 200 CE working beginning point, Osroene had been a Roman client kingdom since c. 132 BCE and was moving toward full provincialization (staged: Abgar IX's summons and execution under Caracalla c. 213, formal annexation 214 CE). Edessa itself sat inside the Roman provincial system for nearly this entire world's formative core, not outside it, while remaining linguistically and culturally Aramaic/Syriac rather than Hellenized — "a Roman-administered but linguistically and culturally distinct periphery" (Doc_01, Section 3, citing Millar and Griffith). At the same moment, Edessa's own religious ecology was genuinely plural and not yet consolidated around any single "orthodox" identity: paganism, a significant Jewish community, Marcionite Christianity, and Bardaisan's own following at Abgar VIII's court all occupied the same civic space (Doc_01, Sections 5, 7; Doc_02, Section 8). **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the political facts; **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** for "Roman-administered periphery" as the precise characterization (Doc_01, Section 3).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
This world's own surviving voice does not narrate its founding political condition as a discrete, named topic the way a modern historian would frame "provincial status." What can be rendered from within is narrower: this is a community that came to hold its own truth (*shrara*) as something spoken in Aramaic, sung in Aramaic, argued in Aramaic — a people who did not need Greek's categorical apparatus to say what needed saying, because the raza already bound word to reality without it. And in the same breath, this is a community formed among neighbors who were not distant heresiarchs but people one could meet in the street — Bardaisan's own circle, Marcion's adherents — so that knowing what one believed meant, from the very beginning, being able to say plainly how it differed from the belief of the person one could actually name and point to. **[Reported-Experience Status: this world left no text reflecting directly on its own political-administrative situation as such; the Aramaic-medium and plural-neighbor framing above is this document's own rendering, grounded in Doc_01 Sections 4 and 7 and Doc_02 Section 8, not a quotation or paraphrase of any surviving first-person testimony to this specific point.]**

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**
This force is the initiating condition for two of this world's confirmed gravities at once. It supplies the raw material for C1 (raza/shrara as the vehicle of doctrine, in Aramaic rather than Greek categorical form) and for C3 (heresiological self-definition against Bardaisan, Marcion, and later Mani) — a world whose earliest distinctive shape is inseparable from having real, locally-present, plausible-sounding neighbors to define itself against from the outset, not after the fact. See Section 4, Connection 1, and Section 5.

### CELL 1B — INITIATING / INTERNAL

#### Force 1B-1: Inherited Commitment to Symbolic/Typological Theological Method (Raza/Shrara) Over Greek Categorical Argument

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
This world's theological expression, from its earliest attested voices, took a poetic, exegetical, symbolic form rather than the scholastic/philosophical-categorical mode of Greek and Latin patristic writing — most visible in Ephrem, whose primary vehicle of theological argument is hymnody (*madrashe*) rather than treatise (Doc_01, Section 4, citing Brock; independently confirmed at Doc_02, Section 11, via Brock's own *The Luminous Eye* and *Harp of the Spirit*). Sebastian Brock's own documented argument is that Syriac *raza* ("symbol") is bound to *shrara* ("truth") and carries the truth's "hidden power" rather than merely representing it Platonic-allegory-style. **Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction** that this characterizes this world's own theological method generally; **Documented** for Ephrem's own textual practice specifically (Doc_04, C1).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
A raza is not a sign chosen to stand in for something else by convention; it is bound to the shrara it signifies and carries something of that reality's own hidden power. To read Scripture, or creation itself, rightly is to perceive connections already woven into the world by its Maker — not connections the reader invents. This is not a method adopted for lack of a better one; it is itself already the world "woven through with real connections that a properly formed eye can perceive" (Doc_07, Section 2B, citing Doc_06 `syrlex001_raza-shrara.md`). **[Documented for Ephrem's own corpus, per Doc_06 `syrlex001`; the interpretive claim that this constitutes an "inherited" disposition present from this world's own earliest period, rather than solely a mature achievement of Ephrem's own maturity, is this document's own synthesis and is offered at Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence.]**

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:**
This inherited disposition is the direct seed of Confirmed Gravity C1 (Primary). It later intensifies under the ongoing pressure of doctrinal rivalry (Force 2A-2 below) rather than remaining static — see Section 4, Connection 4, and Section 5.

#### Force 1B-2: Early Institutional Commitment to the Qyama Covenant-Order

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
The bnay qyama / bnat qyama ("sons/daughters of the covenant") — celibate ascetics bound by a vowed "covenant," living communally or semi-communally among their own kin rather than in desert withdrawal — are first clearly and firmly attested within this world's own window in Aphrahat's Demonstration 6 (337 CE), the earliest datable source for the institution (Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_04, C2). Aphrahat himself addresses the order as something already established and needing correction more than founding (Doc_05, Section 1.1) — internal evidence that the institution predates his own 337 attestation of it, though by how long is not established in the sources this project has verified. **Confidence: Widely Accepted** for the institution's existence and basic ascetic-communal character within this world's own window; **Inferential/Thin** for exactly how much earlier than 337 its own origin lies, since no source earlier than Demonstration 6 attests it directly (Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 2).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
To take the qyama — to become bar qyama or bat qyama — is to stand for a promise that does not end: not a season of youth to be outgrown, but a lifelong undertaking, lived among one's own kin rather than in flight from them. Aphrahat writes of this order as something already standing, needing correction more than founding — a discipline in the middle of its own life, not its first spring (Doc_05, Section 1.1). **[Documented core per Aphrahat, Demonstration 6, per Doc_02/03/04/05. #### Force 1B-3: Early Adoption of the Diatessaron as the Normative Harmonized Gospel

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
Tatian's Diatessaron (composed c. 172 CE, a single continuous-narrative Gospel harmony) functioned as the standard lectionary text in Syriac-speaking churches from a point early enough that it is already the shared narrative spine both Aphrahat and Ephrem attest independently (Aphrahat quotes it; Ephrem wrote a full Commentary upon it) — normative use that persisted until displaced by the Peshitta in the early 5th century, at or just past this world's own 410 boundary (Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_04, C5). **Confidence: Documented** for the Diatessaron's normative use and content within this world's own window; **Contested** for several downstream particulars (the Dura-Europos fragment's Diatessaronic identification; the original language of composition) that do not affect the normative-use finding itself (Doc_02, Section 2).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**
The Gospel, encountered in this world, is not four witnesses held in tension but one unbroken story — a single continuous narrative one hears, sings about, and hears commented upon as a whole (Doc_05, Section 3.1; Doc_07, Section 2B). Ephrem's own decision to devote a full commentary to this harmonized text signals how load-bearing the single-narrative experience of "the Gospel" was to this world's own devotional and exegetical life. **[Documented use per both authors, per Doc_02 Section 2 and Doc_05 Section 3.1; the framing of this as inhabited experience — "one hears... as a whole" — is this document's own rendering in the world's own vocabulary, not a direct quotation from either author.]**

#### Force 2A-1: Sasanian State Persecution Under Shapur II

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:**
Shapur II's persecution of Persian Christians, conventionally dated intensifying from the 340s and continuing in force through the 370s, produced the "Great Massacre" (c. 344–345) and a sustained Persian episcopal martyrological record: Simeon bar Sabbae (traditional martyrdom date 341, though Kosiński and Burgess argue for a redating to c. 344 — a live, unresolved dating dispute this document carries at full strength rather than resolving), Shahdost (341–343, martyred), Barba'shmin (343–346, martyred, followed by a roughly twenty-year vacancy in the primatial succession), and a wider martyrological record naming Milles of Susa, Acepsimas, Mareas, Bicor, and roughly twenty other bishops and 250 clergy (Doc_02, Section 11; Doc_04, C6). **Confidence: Widely Accepted to Documented** for the persecution itself and its effect on the episcopal succession; **Contested** at the level of exact dates (the Simeon bar Sabbae redating dispute, which would shift the entire downstream chain by two to three years).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:**

The fear does not resolve into simple resolve: a twenty-year silence follows Barba'shmin's death — no successor dares take a seat that means near-certain execution — and whatever hope sustained the community through that gap is not narrated in any surviving text this world's sources include. **[Widely Accepted to Documented for the outward facts, per Doc_02 Section 11 and Doc_05 Section 1.2. To hold the faith rightly here is, in part, an active, ongoing act of telling truth apart from a plausible-sounding neighbor, not a settled doctrine received once and set aside (Doc_05, Section 2.3; Doc_07, Section 2A). **[Documented, per Doc_01 Sections 5/7, Doc_02 Section 8, Doc_04 C3. ## Section 6 — Transmission as a Force Dimension — Synthesis

### Who Transmitted This World's Characteristic Material

Ephrem's corpus survives principally through British Library Add. 14571 (519 CE) and Edmund Beck's CSCO critical editions (1955–1979), which apply manuscript-age and internal-anachronism tests to separate authentic from later material. A substantial body of Greek pseudonymous material ("Ephraem Graecus") was preserved by a wholly separate, later transmission stream not continuous with the genuine Syriac corpus. Aphrahat's Demonstrations survive through British Library Add. 17182 (474/510 CE, incomplete) and BL Add. 14619 (the sole complete sixth-century witness), with only a single Demonstration (6) surviving in a Georgian translation itself mediated through an Armenian intermediary and transmitted under a misattribution to Hippolytus (Doc_02, Section 3).

### Under What Conditions Transmission Occurred

Both surviving corpora were preserved by and for institutionally embedded, clerical-ascetic communities with a direct liturgical and doctrinal stake in the material: Ephrem's own diaconate and qyama attachment, and the ongoing use of his hymns in worship, gave later generations reason to keep copying him; Aphrahat's more modest institutional profile and far thinner dissemination (Doc_02, Section 3) meant his own material had less of this self-sustaining transmission interest behind it, and survives correspondingly more thinly.

### What Selection Effects Shaped What Survives

What endured is precisely the clerical-ascetic, doctrinally combative material produced by figures with an institutional stake in transmission; what did not survive, structurally and near-completely, is any text authored by an ordinary lay believer, a bnat qyama woman, an independent Jewish voice responding to Aphrahat's polemic, or an enslaved or lower-status person (Doc_02, Section 7). This is not a gap this project can fill by inference — Doc_02's own Affirmative Duty finding (Article 20) names it as a structural silence rather than papering over it with plausible-sounding invented color.

### What the Transmission Pattern Reveals About Forces

Communities preserve what they fear losing or need to keep using. That Ephrem's liturgically useful hymn corpus survives in bulk while Aphrahat's less liturgically embedded prose demonstrations survive only in a handful of manuscripts, and that no lay or non-elite voice survives at all, shows that the forces most threatening to this community's own literary output were not primarily persecution or doctrinal rivalry directly, but ordinary non-use: material without an institutional home to keep re-copying and re-performing it simply stopped being carried forward, regardless of how central it may have been to unrecorded lived experience.

### Connection to Author Gravity Findings (Doc_02)

This connects directly to Doc_02's own Author Gravity Assessment (Section 3): Ephrem's dominant visibility in the surviving record is not a neutral reflection of his own relative importance within this world's own lived Christianity, but substantially a transmission-selection effect — his corpus dominates because it was institutionally embedded in ways that favored its preservation, not necessarily because his voice was proportionately more central to how ordinary believers actually experienced this world's formation. Separately, the later legendary material about Ephrem (his personal choir-leadership, attested only in 6th-century sources — Force 3B-2 above) shows this same transmission dynamic continuing to operate past this world's own boundary: successor communities did not merely preserve this world's material passively but actively reshaped Ephrem's own memory to serve their own later formational needs, a pattern this document flags for Doc_09's own future treatment of tiered, later-tradition material rather than resolving here.
