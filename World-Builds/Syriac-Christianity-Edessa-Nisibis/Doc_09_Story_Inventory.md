## 1. Purpose and Scope

Per Step 9 (Story and Narrative Sources Assessment, Part III), this document catalogs every story available to this world's Representative, classified into the Construction Framework's four-tier system, with no fifth tier: generated or illustrative narrative, however well it might fit this world's voice, is not permitted. The four tiers, per the Construction Framework's own text: **Tier 1**, Documented Historical Narrative (direct textual attestation, named author, datable, historically credible with caveats); **Tier 2**, Collected and Traditional Material (collected form, traceable to community memory, individual attribution uncertain); **Tier 3**, Attributed Tradition (attributed to specific figures, including hagiographic material — the formation ideal is the evidence, not the specific events); **Tier 4**, Historically Grounded Reconstruction (composite typical practice, every element traceable, no invented content, no named individual). No Tier 5 exists.

## 3. Absent Stories

This world's story repository is honest about what it cannot tell, consistent with the Affirmative Duty (Constitution Article 20) already applied at Doc_02 (Section 7) and Doc_08 (Section 6).

**No separate biographical or hagiographic tradition exists for Aphrahat, the Demonstrations' author.** Unlike Ephrem, whose legendary tradition is extensive (syrstory007, syrstory008, and the broader Vita complex), the standard Syriac hagiography catalog (syriaca.org's Syriac Biographical Dictionary, syri.ac — Source Registry #53) has no entry for Aphrahat despite listing many contemporaries. A rich anecdotal and miracle tradition does circulate under the name "Aphrahat" — flight from Zoroastrianism, healing the Emperor Valens's horse, a face-off with Valens, a parable involving a coat — but this project's research finds that scholarship (via Theodoret, *Historia Religiosa* ch. 8, and chronological argument) attributes this material to a different, later Persian-born hermit near Antioch, active under Valens (365–378) and dying c. 407–413, decades after the Demonstrations' author had finished writing (by 345). This same reference tool (Registry #53) has historically conflated the two figures. This repository does not include that anecdotal material under this world's own Aphrahat, precisely to avoid perpetuating that conflation. This world's Aphrahat is known only through his own discursive prose and the thin, contradictory external notices already carried in Doc_02 (Section 3) — no separate narrative tradition stands behind him at all.

**No individually narrated account exists for most of the Persian martyrological record.** Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles of Susa, and Acepsimas each have their own dedicated martyrdom Acts in the wider Syriac tradition (per this project's research), but this construction pass did not verify their specific narrative content with the same confidence achieved for Simeon bar Sabbae (syrstory005), and one source (Arthur Vööbus, *History of Asceticism in the Syrian Orient*, CSCO Subsidia 81 — Source Registry #52) explicitly flags the Acts of Miles as containing "fable and fantasy" requiring particular caution. Rather than construct story chunks on unverified specifics, this repository names their existence and leaves their detailed content for a future revision pass. Mareas and Bicor are attested only as names in the martyrological list (Doc_02, Section 11), with no individual Acts located at all.

**No first-person or eyewitness account exists of the 201 CE flood, or of any Christian community's own experience of it**, from the Christian side — the only surviving report (syrstory002) is a later, secular civic chronicle's brief notice, naming no Christian individual.

**No bnat qyama woman's own composed word survives**, in her own voice, from this world's own window. The richest available material about them (syrstory007) is Ephrem's own construction of their role, remembered a further century later by Jacob of Serugh — an account *about* these women, doubly mediated, never *by* one of them. This is the same silence Doc_02 (Section 7) and Doc_08 (Section 6) already name structurally; this document confirms it holds equally at the level of narrative, not only doctrine.

**No independent Jewish account exists** of the exchange Aphrahat's anti-Jewish Demonstrations respond to, and no enslaved or lower-status believer's story survives in any form — both already named in Doc_02 (Section 7) and carried forward here without alteration, since this construction pass located no new material bearing on either gap.

**The miraculous "image not made by hands" (the Mandylion) is not part of this world's own record.** It is first attested by Evagrius Scholasticus discussing the 544 CE siege of Edessa, nearly two centuries past this world's own 410 close — a later accretion this repository deliberately excludes from syrstory004 rather than folding in as though it belonged to the same textual layer as Hanan's ordinary painted portrait.

### 5.2 Findings

#### 5.2.1 The confirmed Malpana case (Doc_02 vs. Doc_03)

As already established and cited by the project: Doc_02, Section 3 (Author Gravity Assessment, Representativeness dimension) states as settled fact that "Ephrem was a deacon and *malpana* (teacher) embedded in the bnay/bnat qyama choir-order," citing Sidney Griffith. Doc_03, Section 3.1 — written later, explicitly governed by Doc_02, and performing independent verification — found that the claim of Ephrem holding the title/office "malpana" traces to later hagiographic and tertiary-source tradition, not to direct textual attestation within this world's own 200–410 window, and excluded the term from lexicon candidacy on that basis, further noting the "malpana of the School of Nisibis" attribution is chronologically impossible (the School of Nisibis was founded c. 489–496, over a century after Ephrem's death, using titles *mhaggyana*, *maqryana*, *mpashshqana* — not *malpana*). This is a genuine, unresolved contradiction between two approved documents on the same claim about the same figure. Neither document's own review rounds caught it; it was caught only later, by chance, during a downstream Phase One Ecology Assessment review. This audit confirms the characterization above is accurate on direct re-reading of both passages, and confirms — via a full-corpus search (see 5.2.2–5.2.3) — that the underlying Doc_02/Doc_03 contradiction was, at the time this audit was drafted, still uncorrected at the source. **RESOLVED 2026-07-08 — see 5.4.**

#### 5.2.2 The Malpana problem's propagation into Doc_09's own Story Inventory

Story-Chunks/`syrstory006_jacob-nisibis-deliverance.md`, Formation Ecology Connection section, stated: "It also illuminates the qyama-adjacent, non-episcopal teaching role this world's Representative construction has settled on (**a malpana** who urges and commemorates, rather than a bishop who acts with singular authority) — Ephrem's own place in this story is exactly that of the teacher who prompts and later sings of deliverance, not the one who works it."

This used "malpana" as an unhedged, settled descriptor of Ephrem's narrative role, with no cross-reference to Doc_03 Section 3.1's own finding that this specific title is not attested for Ephrem within this world's 200–410 window and was explicitly excluded from lexicon candidacy for that reason. This was the same underlying problem as 5.2.1, propagating into a third governing document (Doc_09, via its own deployment chunk) without correction or even acknowledgment of the term's contested status — a genuinely new, previously-uncaught instance, not a re-statement of the already-known Doc_02/Doc_03 case. It was not caught by any of Doc_09's own three independent review rounds above. **RESOLVED 2026-07-08 — see 5.4 and this document's own Revision Log.**

#### 5.2.3 A citation in Doc_02 Section 3 undercut by Doc_03's own later research, within the same sentence as the Malpana claim

The same Doc_02, Section 3 sentence discussed in 5.2.1 cited two sources for the compound claim ("Ephrem was a deacon and malpana... embedded in the bnay/bnat qyama choir-order"): Sidney Griffith, *Faith Adoring the Mystery* (1997), and Griffith's "Ephraem, the Deacon of Edessa, and the Church of the Empire" (1986). It was not caught by Doc_02's own two review rounds or by Doc_03's own two review rounds. **RESOLVED 2026-07-08 — see 5.4.**

#### 5.2.4 The School of Nisibis's founding date: 350-vs-489/496 drift — a second, independent instance of the Malpana pattern
