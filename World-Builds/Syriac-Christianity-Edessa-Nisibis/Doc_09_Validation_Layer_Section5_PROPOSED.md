## 5. Validation Layer

### 5.0 Purpose

This section is a pipeline-level, world-completeness cross-document consistency audit of Doc_01 through Doc_09's Story Inventory (Section 2 above) — confirming that the nine construction documents built for World #7 (Syriac Christianity, Edessa/Nisibis, c. 200–410 CE) are internally consistent with one another before this world is treated as complete and frozen, per the Representative Construction Framework V3.2, Part Nine's definition of Doc_09 as "Story Inventory, World Profile, and Validation." **This is not the same exercise as `Syriac_Phase5_Boundary_Testing_Validation_DRAFT.md`.** That document tests a *constructed Representative's* runtime conversational behavior against adversarial probe scenarios (anachronism handling, boundary maintenance, and so on) — it validates the Representative's performance. This Validation Layer does not touch the Representative or re-run any of Phase Five's probes; it validates the *construction record itself* — whether the nine documents that built this world's evidentiary, gravitational, ecological, and forces structure agree with each other, cite each other accurately, and apply the project's own confidence and tiering rules consistently. The two validations answer different questions about different objects, at different points in the pipeline.

### 5.1 Methodology

Five things were actually done to produce this section, not merely described in the abstract:

1. **Cross-reference verification.** Doc_01 through Doc_09 (including all nine Story-Chunks files) were read in full, not sampled or summarized from prior revision logs. Every citation encountered from a later document into Doc_04's gravity IDs (C1–C6) or Doc_08's Force IDs was checked against the cited location's actual text — not accepted on the strength of the citing document's own confidence. This included re-verifying, directly against Doc_04's Interaction Matrix (Doc_04, Section 3), the "no demonstrated relationship" (C3×C4) and "Reshaping" (C2×C4) findings that Doc_05 (Section 8), Doc_07 (Section 2C), and Doc_08 (Section 4, Connection 8; Section 5) each rely on; re-verifying Doc_07's own Round 1 citation fixes (the Papa bar Aggai/Miles of Susa contested-primacy claim's actual source, Doc_01 Section 6; the World #1 contrast language's actual source, Doc_01 Section 1) directly against Doc_01's text; and re-verifying Story-Chunks/syrstory006's corrected citation (Doc_08 Force 1A-2, not Force 2A-1) directly against Doc_08's own Force 1A-2 and Force 2A-1 entries. All eight non-006 story chunks' own citations into Doc_04 gravities and Doc_05/07 sections were likewise checked against the cited sections' actual content.
2. **Cross-document factual consistency.** Beyond the confirmed Malpana case (below), a systematic search was run across all nine documents plus all Story-Chunks and Lexicon-Chunks files for recurring claims, terms, and figures most likely to drift: the honorifics "malpana," "Mar Ephrem"/"Mar Aphrahat," and "Catholicos"; the term "Peshitta"; Jacob of Nisibis's death date (338 vs. 350); the Nisibis baptistery/cathedral date (313); and Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom date (341 vs. c. 344). Every occurrence was checked for whether it was characterized consistently with how the term/claim is treated elsewhere.
3. **Confidence-vocabulary consistency (Article 17).** Recurring claims most likely to drift in confidence rating — Aphrahat's episcopal status, the Diatessaron's vernacular name dating, the Nisibis cathedral's evidentiary status, Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom date, and Jacob of Nisibis's death date — were traced across every document in which they recur to confirm the same confidence label (or an explicitly-carried dispute) is used everywhere, not resolved quietly in one document and left contested in another.
4. **Doc_09 tier/story compliance (Article 19).** All nine Story-Chunks files were read in full. The syrstory006 fix Doc_09's own Round 1 review applied (correcting a mis-citation of Doc_08 Force 2A-1 to the actually-correct Force 1A-2) was independently re-verified against Doc_08's actual Force 1A-2 and Force 2A-1 text, not accepted on the revision log's own say-so. The remaining eight stories' own citations into Doc_04 gravities and Doc_08 forces were checked the same way.
5. **Forces Framework integration completeness.** Doc_01, Doc_02, Doc_04, Doc_05, Doc_07, and Doc_08 were each checked directly for substantive forces-integration content (not merely a section header gesturing at "forces"): Doc_01 Section 7 (Preliminary Forces Identification), Doc_02 Section 9 (Forces Lens Applied to Source Ecology), Doc_04's per-gravity "Forces-connection notation" field (present for all six candidates), Doc_05 Section 6 (Forces Integration Summary, with an explicitly self-named gap), Doc_07 Section 3 (Forces as Integration Lens), and Doc_08 (the full six-cell, three-layer Forces Document). All six were confirmed to carry genuine analytical content specific to this world's own evidence, not placeholder text.

Where a check could not reasonably be completed in full (see 5.4), that limitation is stated rather than papered over.

### 5.2 Findings

#### 5.2.1 The confirmed Malpana case (Doc_02 vs. Doc_03)

As already established and cited by the project: Doc_02, Section 3 (Author Gravity Assessment, Representativeness dimension) states as settled fact that "Ephrem was a deacon and *malpana* (teacher) embedded in the bnay/bnat qyama choir-order," citing Sidney Griffith. Doc_03, Section 3.1 — written later, explicitly governed by Doc_02, and performing independent verification — found that the claim of Ephrem holding the title/office "malpana" traces to later hagiographic and tertiary-source tradition, not to direct textual attestation within this world's own 200–410 window, and excluded the term from lexicon candidacy on that basis, further noting the "malpana of the School of Nisibis" attribution is chronologically impossible (the School of Nisibis was founded c. 489–496, over a century after Ephrem's death, using titles *mhaggyana*, *maqryana*, *mpashshqana* — not *malpana*). This is a genuine, unresolved contradiction between two approved documents on the same claim about the same figure. Neither document's own review rounds caught it; per the task brief, it was caught only later, by chance, during a downstream Phase One Ecology Assessment review. This audit confirms the characterization above is accurate on direct re-reading of both passages, and confirms — via a full-corpus search (see 5.2.2–5.2.3) — that the underlying Doc_02/Doc_03 contradiction itself has still never been corrected at the source; it has only been tracked and worked around in later, downstream Representative-construction documents (the Representative's title was moved away from "Malpana" specifically because of this unresolved tension). This audit does not resolve it; it is flagged again below (5.4) as still requiring project lead attention.

#### 5.2.2 New finding: the Malpana problem propagates uncaught into Doc_09's own Story Inventory

Story-Chunks/`syrstory006_jacob-nisibis-deliverance.md`, Formation Ecology Connection section, states: "It also illuminates the qyama-adjacent, non-episcopal teaching role this world's Representative construction has settled on (**a malpana** who urges and commemorates, rather than a bishop who acts with singular authority) — Ephrem's own place in this story is exactly that of the teacher who prompts and later sings of deliverance, not the one who works it."

This uses "malpana" as an unhedged, settled descriptor of Ephrem's narrative role, with no cross-reference to Doc_03 Section 3.1's own finding that this specific title is not attested for Ephrem within this world's 200–410 window and was explicitly excluded from lexicon candidacy for that reason. This is the same underlying problem as 5.2.1, propagating into a third governing document (Doc_09, via its own deployment chunk) without correction or even acknowledgment of the term's contested status — a genuinely new, previously-uncaught instance, not a re-statement of the already-known Doc_02/Doc_03 case. It was not caught by any of Doc_09's own three independent review rounds (confirmed by direct inspection of Doc_09's Revision Log, which discusses syrstory006 only for its unrelated Force 2A-1→1A-2 citation correction, never for this term). By contrast, Lexicon-Chunks/`syrlex008_mar.md` and Doc_03 Section 1.8 handle the comparable "Mar Ephrem" honorific correctly — explicitly noting no lifetime-contemporary attestation exists and flagging the risk of a participant or, by implication, a construction document assuming otherwise. syrstory006 does not apply the same discipline to "malpana."

**Suggested resolution (flagged for project lead, not resolved here):** either remove "malpana" from syrstory006's Formation Ecology Connection and replace it with an unadorned description of the narrative role ("teacher," "one who urges and commemorates"), or retain it with an explicit hedge matching Doc_03 Section 3.1's own finding (e.g., "in a role later tradition and this project's own Representative-construction discussion label 'malpana' — a title not itself attested for Ephrem within this world's own 200–410 record, per Doc_03 Section 3.1").

#### 5.2.3 New finding: a citation in Doc_02 Section 3 is undercut by Doc_03's own later research, within the same sentence as the Malpana claim

The same Doc_02, Section 3 sentence discussed in 5.2.1 cites two sources for the compound claim ("Ephrem was a deacon and malpana... embedded in the bnay/bnat qyama choir-order"): Sidney Griffith, *Faith Adoring the Mystery* (1997), and Griffith's "Ephraem, the Deacon of Edessa, and the Church of the Empire" (1986). Doc_03, Section 1.2 (qyama entry, Sources), while listing the genuine qyama-specific Griffith citations (his 1991 *The Harp* article and his 1993 *Eulogema* chapter), adds this note in passing: "*Faith Adoring the Mystery* (1997) is a real Griffith book but is not itself the qyama-specific citation." This means Doc_03's own independent verification pass found that one of the two sources Doc_02 attaches to its qyama-embedding claim does not, on inspection, actually support that specific claim — a real book by the right author, misattached to the wrong specific point. This is a distinct citation-accuracy problem from the Malpana title question itself, though it sits in the identical sentence and was evidently never carried back to correct Doc_02. It was not caught by Doc_02's own two review rounds (which cleared this Author Gravity Assessment passage without comment) or by Doc_03's own two review rounds (which noted the mismatch only as an aside inside the qyama entry's Sources list, not as a flag against Doc_02).

**Suggested resolution (flagged for project lead, not resolved here):** Doc_02, Section 3 should either substitute a citation that actually supports the qyama-embedding claim (Doc_03's own 1991/1993 Griffith citations are the candidates already verified for this purpose) or narrow the claim's citation to what *Faith Adoring the Mystery* actually supports, rather than leaving the mismatch standing uncorrected in an already-approved document.

#### 5.2.4 New finding: the School of Nisibis's founding date is uncorrected 350-vs-489/496 drift — a second, independent instance of the Malpana pattern

Doc_01 (Section 2 and Section 10) and Doc_02 (Section 12) both flag the School of Nisibis's founding date as an open question, carrying "350" only as the commonly-cited working figure still requiring a closer primary-source pass — not as a settled fact. Doc_03, Section 3.1, written and approved later, states flatly, as a load-bearing fact used to rule out a proposed "malpana of the School of Nisibis" title as chronologically impossible: "GEDSH's 'Nisibis, School of' entry confirms the School of Nisibis was founded c. 489–496 CE."

These are not the same open question answered progressively — Doc_01/Doc_02 still list 350 as an unresolved candidate requiring verification, while Doc_03 has already found and relied on a specific, sourced, radically different date (489–496) for what it treats as the same institution, without looping back to close out Doc_01/Doc_02's still-open flag or acknowledging the discrepancy. `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` and Doc_03's own review-artifact files do not track or resolve this. This is a genuine, previously-uncaught instance of exactly the defect pattern this audit exists to find (same claim, materially different treatment, never reconciled across two approved documents) — caught only on a second pass targeted specifically at this phrase, after this audit's first pass (5.1, item 2) checked a fixed list of recurring terms that did not include "School of Nisibis founding date" despite its recurring, unresolved presence in three documents.

**Suggested resolution (flagged for project lead, not resolved here):** either Doc_01/Doc_02's open flag should be closed out using Doc_03's own GEDSH-sourced 489–496 date (if that is in fact the same institution and the stronger-sourced figure), or, if there is a reason the two documents are describing different things or the dates are genuinely in tension, that tension should be named explicitly in both places rather than left as an unacknowledged discrepancy.

#### 5.2.5 What was checked and found clean

- **Peshitta and Catholicos anachronism discipline.** Every occurrence of "Peshitta" and "Catholicos" across all nine documents, all nine story chunks, and all lexicon chunks either explicitly excludes the term as anachronistic native-period vocabulary, flags it as a distortion-risk item, or presents it only as something outside this world's own attested horizon. No document uses either term as settled, native, in-window vocabulary. No violation found.
- **"Mar Ephrem" / "Mar Aphrahat."** Both compounds appear only in the context of Doc_03's own finding that no lifetime-contemporary attestation exists for either — never asserted elsewhere as settled usage. No violation found.
- **Jacob of Nisibis's death date (338 vs. 350).** Doc_01 and Doc_02 carry this as an explicit, unresolved primary-source conflict (*Martyrologium Hieronymianum* vs. *Chronicon Paschale*), and neither document quietly picks one date as settled. **Correction from an earlier version of this audit:** syrstory006 does not itself address Jacob's death date — its Tier Justification lists three candidate *siege* years (338, 346, or 350 CE), a related but distinct fact from the death-date dispute. syrstory006 leaves the siege year ambiguous, consistent with the source record; it was not, on closer reading, making a death-date claim to check for consistency against Doc_01/Doc_02 in the first place.
- **Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom date (341 vs. c. 344).** Consistently carried as a live, unresolved redating dispute (Kosiński and Burgess vs. the traditional date) everywhere it recurs — Doc_02 Section 11, Doc_04, Doc_05, Doc_08 Sections 3 and 7. No document flattens this to a settled year.
- **The "313 CE" figure.** Appears in exactly two places — Doc_02 Section 6 (the Nisibis baptistery's excavation date range, 313/320–359/360) and syrstory002 (the Chronicle of Edessa's own, separately-reported date for the Edessa church's founding). These are two different claims about two different buildings in two different cities; neither document cross-references the other's use of "313," and neither needs to, since they are not competing claims about the same fact.
- **Doc_09 story-to-gravity/force citations (the other eight stories).** syrstory001 (C2), syrstory003 (C4), syrstory004 (C4), syrstory005 (C6, Doc_08 Force 2A-1), syrstory007 (C1, C2, C3), and syrstory009 (C1, C2, C5, Doc_07 Section 2E) were each checked against the actual content of the cited gravity/section and found accurate. syrstory002 and syrstory008 make no gravity-ID claim and were checked for internal consistency with Doc_02/Doc_05 material instead, which held up.
- **The syrstory006 Doc_08 citation fix itself.** Independently re-verified, not merely trusted: Doc_08's Force 1A-2 entry does name Nisibis's "third, unstable position — contested repeatedly through the 3rd century, fixed as Roman only from 298 (Peace of Nisibis), and ceded to Persia in 363," matching syrstory006's corrected citation exactly; Doc_08's Force 2A-1 entry (Sasanian persecution, the poll tax, episcopal martyrdom) makes no mention of Nisibis or military sieges at all. The Round 1 fix is genuinely correct, not merely self-reported as correct.
- **Forces Framework's six required integration points.** Confirmed present with substantive, world-specific content (not template gesture) at all six: Doc_01 Section 7, Doc_02 Section 9, Doc_04's per-gravity forces-connection notation (all six candidates), Doc_05 Section 6, Doc_07 Section 3, and Doc_08 in full.
- **Doc_04 Interaction Matrix cross-references in Doc_05/07/08.** The C3×C4 "no demonstrated relationship" and C2×C4 "Reshaping" findings, both load-bearing for later documents' own integration claims, were verified directly against Doc_04 Section 3's matrix and found to be cited accurately everywhere they recur.
- **Doc_07's own self-corrected citations.** Doc_07's Revision Log records two citation-location fixes (the Papa bar Aggai/Miles of Susa claim's actual source, and the World #1 contrast language's actual source). Both corrected citations were independently checked against Doc_01's actual text (Section 6 and Section 1 respectively) and found accurate.

No further contradictions of the "same claim, different characterization" kind, beyond the Malpana complex (5.2.1–5.2.3) and the School of Nisibis founding-date drift (5.2.4), were found in this pass.

### 5.3 What this audit could not fully verify

This audit read all nine governing documents and all nine Story-Chunks files in full, and ran a targeted full-corpus search for the specific recurring terms and dates named in Section 5.1. It did not independently re-verify the underlying real-world bibliographic accuracy of every citation against the actual external scholarship cited (e.g., whether Griffith's, Brock's, or Kayaalp's works say precisely what they are quoted as saying) — that fact-checking is Doc_02 through Doc_08's own job, already carried through multiple independent adversarial review rounds each, and re-litigating it here would duplicate rather than validate that work. It also did not open and check every cell of the companion spreadsheets (Source_Registry.xlsx, Gravity_Index.xlsx, Force_Index.xlsx, Lexicon_Candidate_Index.xlsx, Lexicon_Deployment_Index.xlsx, Story_Index.xlsx) row by row; the narrative documents' own revision logs report several rounds of index-sheet regeneration and reconciliation against these spreadsheets, and this audit trusts that record for the spreadsheets specifically while independently re-verifying the narrative-level claims those spreadsheets are supposed to mirror. Given reasonable time, this audit checked more than twenty specific cross-references and recurring claims directly (enumerated in 5.1 and 5.2) rather than every citation in all nine documents; it is a substantial, genuine sample, not an exhaustive line-by-line re-audit of every citation in the roughly 370KB of narrative text across Doc_01–09.

**Sequencing disclosure.** This Validation Layer was produced out of the order the project lead originally set. On 2026-07-07 (recorded in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md`, item 3), the project lead decided to finish Representative Construction Phases Four through Seven, in order, before returning to the World Profile and Validation Layer. At that point only Phases Four and Five existed; Phases Six (Facilitator Coordination) and Seven (Encounter Ecology Mapping) still do not exist anywhere in this world's folder, and the World Profile component of Step 9 has also not been built. On 2026-07-08, prompted by this same investigation (Phase One's Ecology Assessment having drawn on an incomplete Doc_09, and its own review having caught the Malpana contradiction this Validation Layer was designed to systematically hunt for), the project lead explicitly directed building the Validation Layer now, ahead of Phase Six/Seven and the World Profile — a disclosed, deliberate re-sequencing, not a silent departure from the 2026-07-07 decision (see `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` for the full record). This audit's own scope is limited accordingly: it validates Doc_01–08 and Doc_09's Story Inventory against each other, and does not and cannot speak to whether the eventual, still-unbuilt World Profile or Phases Six/Seven will themselves surface anything further. "The world complete and frozen" (this project's own phrase for Step 9's endpoint) remains not yet reachable on process grounds alone until the World Profile and Phases Six/Seven exist, independent of this audit's own substantive findings below.

### 5.4 Completion Determination

Doc_01 through Doc_08, and Doc_09's own Story Inventory (Section 2 above), are **substantially internally consistent**: confidence-vocabulary ratings for recurring claims hold steady across documents; Doc_04's gravity classifications and Interaction Matrix are cited accurately everywhere checked; Doc_08's Force IDs are cited accurately everywhere checked, including the one place (syrstory006) where an earlier miscitation was caught and fixed; the Forces Framework's six required integration points are all substantively present; and no drift was found in this world's anachronism-discipline terms (Peshitta, Catholicos, Mar Ephrem/Aphrahat) or in its two most date-sensitive disputed figures (Jacob of Nisibis's death year, Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom year).

This audit originally found four specific content items requiring the project lead's attention before Doc_09 — and this world — should be treated as complete and frozen. **All four are now resolved, 2026-07-08, per the project lead's direction:**

1. **The confirmed Doc_02/Doc_03 Malpana contradiction (5.2.1) — RESOLVED.** Doc_02, Section 3 was corrected to drop the unhedged "malpana" claim, citing Doc_03 Section 3.1's finding that the title is not attested for Ephrem within this world's 200–410 window. See Doc_02's own Revision Log.

3. **The Doc_02 Section 3 citation-accuracy problem (5.2.3) — RESOLVED.** Doc_02, Section 3's qyama-embedding claim now cites Griffith's actual qyama-specific works (1991, 1993) in place of the mismatched *Faith Adoring the Mystery* (1997) citation. See Doc_02's own Revision Log.

Jacob of Nisibis's separate, still-genuinely-unresolved death-date question (338 vs. 350) was kept distinct and is unaffected. See Doc_01's and Doc_02's own Revision Logs.
