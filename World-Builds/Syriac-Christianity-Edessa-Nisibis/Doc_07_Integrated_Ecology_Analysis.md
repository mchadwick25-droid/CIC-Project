### 2A — Emotional Ecology [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Doc_05 (Sections 1.2, 5.2) already documented two distinct emotional registers side by side — a Persian-side fear held steady by defiance, a Roman-side anxiety of doctrinal crowding — without naming what they share. Reading both registers through Doc_06's Tier 1 vocabulary reveals that they are not two unrelated emotional facts about this world but two structurally different threats converging on the same achieved emotional destination: Iḥidaya, undividedness.

**Dominant emotional register:** This world's two anchor contexts do not share one dominant register; they run in parallel toward the same named endpoint. On the Persian side, per Doc_05 Section 1.2, the register is fear held steady by defiance — Aphrahat's later Demonstrations address a community that has already watched its bishops die and does not know how many more it will watch die (Doc_02, Section 3, Section 11; Doc_04 C6). On the Roman side, per Doc_05 Section 1.2, the register is the anxiety of the crowded room — not the fear of the executioner but the urgency of telling true faith from a plausible-sounding neighbor (Doc_02, Section 3; Doc_04 C3). Both registers are named, in this world's own vocabulary, as states a mature practitioner moves through toward Iḥidaya — undivided, single-minded standing — per Doc_06's `syrlex007_ihidaya.md`.

**Emotionally dangerous states:** For Ephrem's audience, the named danger is being deceived by a counterfeit raza — a symbol that mimics true typological binding but severs the raza from the shrara it should carry, the very accusation Ephrem's own refutations level at Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani (Doc_06 `syrlex001_raza-shrara.md`, Ecological Function; Doc_02, Section 8). For Aphrahat's audience, the named danger is less textually explicit but structurally clear from the evidence: losing coherence under sustained state pressure, without that danger ever being narrated from the inside in any surviving text (Doc_05, Section 1.2 — the twenty-year Persian succession silence is named as a silence the community's own surviving voice does not describe).

**Emotional arc of formation:** Entry into either qyama vow-taking or catechesis under the harmonized Gospel (Doc_06 `syrlex002_qyama.md`; `syrlex006_ewangeliyon-da-mhallete.md`); the middle of formation is confrontation with one of the two named dangers above, depending on which anchor context a practitioner is formed in; maturity is named directly by this world's own vocabulary as becoming Iḥidaya — the term itself names the achieved state formation moves a practitioner toward, on both sides of the world at once (Doc_06 `syrlex007_ihidaya.md`).

**Gravities most visible in the emotional texture:** C1 (raza/shrara) is most visible in the Roman-side register — the anxiety is precisely about correct versus counterfeit symbolic perception. C6 (persecution-endurance) is most visible in the Persian-side register. C2 (qyama/Iḥidaya) is visible in both, since it supplies the shared vocabulary for the achieved emotional destination underneath both registers.

**What Emotional Ecology reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** Doc_05 documented the two registers as parallel facts about two different anchor contexts. This lens shows they are not merely parallel — they are two different tests of the same underlying formation aim. A practitioner formed under doctrinal crowding and a practitioner formed under state persecution are, in this world's own terms, being formed toward the identical named state (Iḥidaya) by two structurally different roads. Doc_05 did not connect the two registers to a shared destination-vocabulary; this lens does.

### 2B — Philosophical Ecology [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** A single ontological pattern — binding, or undividedness — recurs across three separate places in this world's own vocabulary that Doc_05 and Doc_06 treated as distinct facts: how a symbol relates to the truth it signifies (raza/shrara), how the self relates to God (Iḥidaya), and how the Gospel narrative relates to itself (the harmonized Diatessaron, Ewangeliyon da-Mhallete). Once named, this pattern explains why 2A's emotional dangers felt as consequential as they did.

**A hedge stated here, not only in Section 6:** the pattern that follows is this document's own synthetic reading, proposed for the first time at this step — no source cited anywhere in Doc_01–06 (not Brock, not Murray, not Griffith, not any other scholarship this project has drawn on) states that this world's own writers treated raza's binding, Iḥidaya's oneness, and the Diatessaron's narrative unity as expressions of one underlying logic. The linguistic resonance is real (raza is fundamentally about non-arbitrary binding between sign and reality; Iḥidaya's root yḥd is literally "one"; the Diatessaron literally harmonizes four into one continuous narrative), but resonance across an English gloss of three distinct Syriac terms is not the same as an attested historical-theological connection this world's own participants drew. This document proposes the pattern as a genuine possible reading of the evidence, not an established fact, and flags it as the single claim in this document most needing outside scholarly scrutiny (see Section 6).

**Fundamental ontological assumptions:** This world holds a participatory rather than representational ontology of symbol and reality. Per Doc_06 `syrlex001_raza-shrara.md`, a raza is not a stand-in chosen arbitrarily to represent something else; it is bound to the shrara it signifies and carries something of that reality's own hidden power (hayla kasya). Reality itself, in this world's own terms, is "woven through with real connections that a properly formed eye can perceive" (Doc_06 `syrlex001`, World Meaning) — not invented by the interpreter, but already there.

**Understanding of the human person:** The human person's fundamental problem, in this world's own vocabulary, is dividedness; its fundamental possibility is undividedness. Iḥidaya carries this double duty as one word rather than two ideas — an ascetic designation for single-minded, undivided allegiance to God, and simultaneously the christological title for Christ as Only-Begotten (Greek monogenes), sharing the root yḥd, "one" (Doc_06 `syrlex007_ihidaya.md`). To be formed as Iḥidaya is to be told, in the name given to the practitioner, that single-mindedness participates in something larger than personal discipline — a small likeness of Christ's own undividedness from the Father.

**Temporal assumptions:** This world's normative Gospel text was not four discrete books held in tension but a single continuous harmonized narrative, the Diatessaron (Doc_06 `syrlex006_ewangeliyon-da-mhallete.md`; Doc_05, Section 3.1). The formation experience of encountering "the Gospel" in this world is the experience of a single unfolding story, not four-witness plurality requiring reconciliation. Ephrem's own decision to write a full commentary on this harmonized text (Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_05, Section 3.1) signals how load-bearing this single-narrative assumption was to his own typological reading practice.

**How philosophical ecology grounds the emotional ecology:** The same "bound-together, undivided" ontological pattern runs through all three of the facts named above, and this is what makes 2A's emotional dangers more than ordinary error. In a participatory ontology where a symbol is bound to the reality it signifies, misreading a symbol is not a neutral intellectual mistake — it severs a practitioner from real participation in truth, which is why Ephrem's refutations treat rival typologies as genuinely dangerous rather than merely wrong (Doc_06 `syrlex001`, Distortion Risk). In the same ontology applied to the person, dividedness is not simply poor behavior — it is a practitioner being ontologically less whole than the Iḥidaya ideal names as possible, which is part of why the stakes of enduring persecution without losing coherence (2A, Persian-side danger) carry the weight they do: what is at risk is not merely survival but the practitioner's own undivided nature.

**Gravities most visible in the philosophical structure:** C1 (raza/shrara, the participatory symbol-ontology), C2 (qyama/Iḥidaya, the person), and C5 (the Diatessaron, narrative wholeness) are the three gravities this lens draws together; none of the three, read separately, shows the shared pattern this lens names.

**What Philosophical Ecology reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** Doc_05 documented raza (Section 5.1), the qyama/Iḥidaya institution (Section 1.1), and the harmonized Gospel (Section 3.1) as three separate ecological facts, each in its own lens. This document proposes reading them as three surface expressions of one underlying onto-logic — a logic of binding and undividedness — as a candidate philosophical center of gravity for this world, prior to and beneath any one of the three facts individually. As stated above, this reading is this document's own synthesis, not an independently attested finding.

### 2C — Authority Structures [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Doc_04 and Doc_05 both documented this world's authority structure as genuinely ambiguous (C4, Tensional gravity) and declined to resolve that ambiguity. This lens does not attempt to resolve it either — it asks instead what it meant, formatively, for a practitioner to be formed inside a world whose own authority structure never settled into one clean answer.

**Primary sources of authority:** This world holds at least three, non-collapsing sources of authoritative standing, none of which cleanly subordinates the others within this world's own 200–410 evidentiary window. Ordained episcopal office is real but was itself structurally fragile — the Persian succession stood vacant for two decades after Barba'shmin's martyrdom, and Papa bar Aggai's own claim to primacy was fiercely contested by Miles of Susa and Aqib-Alaha of Karka d'Baith Slok (Doc_01, Section 6). Demonstrated vowed asceticism — qyama and Iḥidaya standing — functioned as a charismatic authority pathway running alongside, not merely beneath, ordained office (Doc_04, C2×C4 Interaction, "Reshaping"; Doc_06 `syrlex002_qyama.md`, Ecological Function). Textual-exegetical authority is visible in Aphrahat's own standing as a writer of taḥwyāṯā, addressed to and evidently received by his community, despite his own place within any formal office structure remaining genuinely unresolved by this project's own research (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_05, Section 4.1).

**Transmission of authority:** How authority was transmitted differs by which of the three sources is in view, and none of the three mechanisms is fully documented for this world's own period. Episcopal succession, where attested, ran through naming and (contested) claims to primacy, but with a demonstrated capacity to fail outright for two decades. Qyama/Iḥidaya standing was entered through vow rather than appointment (Doc_06 `syrlex002_qyama.md`, World Meaning). Aphrahat's own textual authority is not shown by the evidence to have been formally conferred at all — it appears to have functioned through his community's own reception of his taḥwyāṯā, which this project's research does not resolve into a named mechanism (Doc_05, Section 4.1).

**Sites of contestation:** Named at full strength rather than resolved: the twenty-year Persian succession vacancy itself (Doc_02, Section 11); Papa bar Aggai's contested primacy claim (Doc_01, Section 6); Aphrahat's own unresolved status, which this project's research leaves open in either direction rather than assuming non-episcopal by default (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_05, Section 4.1); and the qyama/Iḥidaya pathway's own status as an authority source running alongside ordained office, which Doc_04's Interaction Matrix records as a demonstrated relationship (C2×C4 "Reshaping") rather than a settled subordination.

**Authority and formation:** A practitioner forming in this world received formation from more than one authority pathway at once — text, ordained office where it held, and vowed ascetic example — without a single settled hierarchy resolving which pathway held final say. **[Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin, carried forward from Doc_05 Section 4.2's own established caveat:]** what this plurality actually felt like to negotiate day to day is not directly attested by this world's own surviving evidence; the claim that formation required a practitioner's own coherence to hold together what institutional order did not resolve is this document's own structural inference from the plurality of channels, offered provisionally and revisable at Step 8/9, not an established finding.

**Gravities most illuminated by the authority structure analysis:** C4 directly; C2 as the clearest alternative pathway documented in relation to it.

**What Authority Structures reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** Doc_05, Section 4.2, already named the plurality of authority channels and declined to resolve how it was lived, correctly flagging that claim as inferential. This lens carries that same honest limit forward rather than upgrading it, but adds one additional, evidence-grounded observation: the same plurality that Doc_04/05 treated as an evidentiary problem to be honestly disclosed is also, read as an integration lens, a formation condition — whatever this world's practitioners actually did with an unresolved authority structure, they did it without the option of falling back on one settled source, a structural fact independent of whether its lived experience is recoverable.

### 2D — Boundary Structures [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Doc_01 already found this world's Roman and Persian contexts differ in "intensity" of state pressure. Read through the forces work in Doc_01, Doc_02, and Doc_05 Section 6, this difference is not merely one of intensity — it is a difference in which force presses on which community, producing two structurally different boundary logics under one strand-singular world.

**Membership logic:** Entry into this world's core community ran through baptism and catechesis into the harmonized Gospel generally (Doc_06 `syrlex006_ewangeliyon-da-mhallete.md`), with qyama vow-taking marking a further, dateable threshold into this world's specific ascetic order (Doc_06 `syrlex002_qyama.md`, World Meaning) — a formal, communal, non-desert commitment distinct from ordinary lay membership.

**Relation to what is outside:** This world's two anchor contexts related to "outside" in two different registers. On the Roman/Edessene side, the outside was doctrinal — Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani, rivals close enough to be mistaken for the community itself, whose own circles and missionaries moved through the same streets in living memory (Doc_05, Section 2.3; Doc_01, Section 5/7). Boundary-work here is an active, ongoing act of distinguishing "us" from a plausible neighbor, substantially carried by one figure's own rhetorical labor (Ephrem) rather than an even, world-wide practice (Doc_04, C3 finding). On the Persian side, the outside was political-coercive — the Sasanian state under Shapur II — and, in a separate register, Jewish dialogue partners engaged through Aphrahat's own anti-Jewish Demonstrations, appearing only refracted through his polemic and never in their own voice (Doc_05, Section 2.3, Section 5.1).

**Forces most visible in the boundary structures:** This is the clearest force-to-boundary connection in the whole world. Doctrinal rivalry (a force with intellectual-competitive character) produces the Roman-side boundary logic; Shapur II's persecution (an external coercive force, intensifying from the 340s per Doc_02, Section 11) produces the Persian-side boundary logic. These are not the same force at different strengths — they are two different kinds of pressure, and the two boundary logics they produce differ in kind, not merely in degree.

**Boundary contestation:** On the Roman side, named at full strength: whether a Bardaisanite whose own circle still walked Edessa's streets counted as fully outside was a live, unsettled question in a way a text-book heresy category obscures (Doc_05, Section 2.3). On the Persian side, this project's own research does not document specific case material on contested apostasy or boundary-crossing under persecution threat — this document names that absence explicitly (see Section 6, Gaps and Limits) rather than inferring a parallel contestation pattern that the evidence does not show.

**Boundary logic and formation:** Being formed at this world's Roman-side boundary meant formation in constant, vigilant discernment — learning to detect a counterfeit raza among plausible neighbors. Being formed at its Persian-side boundary meant formation under visible, embodied risk, where the boundary between inside and outside could mean the difference between life and death for one's bishop. These are two different formative postures, not one boundary-logic applied at different intensities.

**What Boundary Structures reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** Doc_05 (Section 2.3) and Doc_01 documented the Roman/Persian difference as a difference of state-pressure intensity. This lens sharpens that finding: the difference is categorical (which force, not merely how strong), and it produces two different formative experiences of what it means to stand at this world's own edge — internal coherence and external situatedness become visible here as a single system in two different configurations, not one system under variable pressure.

### 2E — Formation Logic [REQUIRED — applied after 2A–2D]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Held together, the four lenses above converge on a single organizing aim: this world formed practitioners toward Iḥidaya, undividedness, using a participatory symbolic method (C1) and a vowed communal practice (C2), anchored in a single unbroken Gospel narrative (C5), made formationally necessary rather than optional by the absence of any one settled authority able to secure that coherence institutionally (C4) — and tested, in two structurally different ways depending on which force presses on a given community, by either doctrinal confusion (C3) or state violence (C6).

**The fundamental aim of formation:** In this world's own vocabulary, a fully formed practitioner is Iḥidaya — the Single One, undivided in allegiance, single-minded, participating in Christ's own undividedness from the Father (Doc_06 `syrlex007_ihidaya.md`). This is not "a holy person" in a generic sense; it is a named, specific achieved state that this world's own vocabulary supplies as its own telos, attested in both Aphrahat's and Ephrem's surviving text within this world's own 200–410 window (Doc_03, Section 1.7; Doc_06 `syrlex007`).

**The formation mechanism:** Transformation in this world happens through the combination of a hermeneutic (raza/shrara, trained and performed chiefly through sung madrasha, Doc_06 `syrlex001`, `syrlex004`) and a vowed communal practice (qyama, entered by lifelong promise and lived among kin rather than in desert withdrawal, Doc_06 `syrlex002`). Neither the method alone nor the practice alone is shown by this world's evidence to constitute formation; Doc_05's own account (Sections 1.1, 3.1, 5.1) treats them as operating together, worship being the site where the hermeneutic is performed rather than merely taught (Doc_05, Section 3.1).

**The formation arc:** Entry is through baptism and catechesis into the harmonized Gospel and, for those called to it, the qyama vow (Doc_06 `syrlex002`, `syrlex006`). The middle of formation is confrontation with one of the two named external tests (2A, 2D) — doctrinal confusion or state pressure — depending on anchor context. Maturity is named directly by this world's own vocabulary as Iḥidaya-hood: undivided perception (correctly reading raza), undivided practice (the qyama vow kept), and undivided endurance (standing firm under whichever test was faced).

**How the lenses integrate as a formation system:** The four required lenses converge on one organizing logic, and the convergence itself is a specific, evidence-grounded finding rather than an assumed harmony. 2A showed two different emotional threats converging on one named destination-state. 2B showed the ontological pattern (binding, undividedness) that runs beneath the method (C1), the person (C2), and the narrative (C5) alike, explaining why 2A's dangers carried the weight they did. 2C showed that this world's authority structure never resolved into one settled source — meaning the coherence that, in a differently-organized world, an institution might secure on a practitioner's behalf, had to be carried by the practitioner's own achieved undividedness instead. 2D showed that the specific test of that undividedness differed categorically by anchor context, depending on which external force pressed on a given community. The formation system is not a list of practices; it is this specific structural fact: undividedness is not one virtue among many in this world's formation, it is the load-bearing answer to what an unsettled authority structure could not otherwise secure.

**Cross-strand formation logic:** Not applicable in the plural sense — Doc_01, Section 6, determined this world is strand-singular, with the authority-structure question left open rather than resolved into two strands. The Roman/Persian variation documented throughout this analysis (2A, 2D) is accordingly treated as anchor-context variation within one formation logic, not as evidence of two distinct strands — consistent with Doc_03's own strand-attribution note (Section 0) that no lexicon term carries a strand split.

**Formation logic and confirmed gravities:** C1 (raza/shrara) supplies the perceptual half of undividedness — training in reading truly. C2 (qyama/Iḥidaya) supplies the practiced, vowed half — training in living undividedly. C4 (authority ambiguity) is not a gap alongside the formation logic but a structural reason the formation logic centers undividedness as heavily as it does: where institutions do not fully secure coherence, the practitioner must. C3 and C6 are the two different external tests of the same underlying undividedness, differing by anchor context rather than by formation aim. C5 (the harmonized Gospel) anchors the whole pattern in a single unbroken narrative that itself models undividedness at the level of the text a practitioner receives first.

**What Formation Logic reveals that no individual document showed:** No single document among Doc_01–06 states that this world's formation logic is organized around undividedness as such. Doc_04 classified six gravities without naming what, if anything, unifies them beyond co-occurrence in the same world. Doc_05 documented the ecology those gravities produce, lens by lens, without asking what single formation aim they jointly serve. This lens is the first place in this world's construction record where that aim is named directly, in the world's own vocabulary, and traced through all four required lenses as their shared organizing logic.

### 2F — Memory Structures [OPTIONAL — applied]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** This world's two anchor contexts remember differently, in a way that reinforces rather than merely parallels the boundary-structure bifurcation named in 2D — even the mechanism of communal memory itself differs by anchor context.

**What this world remembered and how:** On the Roman/Edessene side, the Chronicle of Edessa's report of the 201 flood and church destruction — this world's own earliest institutional trace — was itself already old, archivally mediated, before this world's own writers took it up; it is memory of memory rather than eyewitnessed communal recollection (Doc_05, Section 2.2; Doc_02, Section 5). On the Persian side, memory takes a martyrological cast: named bishops — Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles of Susa, Acepsimas, Mareas, Bicor — held in communal memory not as distant founders but as immediate, nameable losses a living generation could still recount (Doc_05, Section 2.2; Doc_02, Section 11).

**The formation function of communal memory:** On the Persian side, the specific naming of martyred bishops keeps persecution's cost un-abstracted, feeding directly into the "fear held steady by defiance" register named in 2A — a community remembering named losses rather than a generic category of suffering. On the Roman side, memory functions more as archival grounding for institutional identity than as a lived, formative pressure comparable to martyrological memory — a genuinely different memory-texture, not merely a thinner version of the same thing.

**Gravities most illuminated by the memory analysis:** C6 (persecution-endurance) is directly illuminated by the Persian-side martyrological pattern; no comparably direct connection exists between Roman-side archival memory and any single gravity, which is itself part of what this lens reveals.

**What Memory Structures reveals:** The Roman/Persian bifurcation already established in 2D and 2A (different forces, different boundary logics, different emotional registers) extends to how each context remembers its own past — a further, independent line of evidence for the same underlying finding rather than a redundant restatement of it, since memory-mechanism (archival-mediated versus martyrological-immediate) is a distinct ecological fact from boundary-logic or emotional register.

### 2G — Interpretive Ecology [OPTIONAL — applied]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Unlike the Roman/Persian bifurcations found in 2A, 2D, and 2F, this world's two hermeneutical traditions do not differ merely in intensity or immediacy — they differ because each answers a different opponent, a genuinely distinct kind of plurality from the boundary-structure finding.

**The hermeneutical assumptions of this world:** Ephrem reads by raza — the participatory typological method already developed in 2B, in which Scripture and creation carry a hidden power bound to the truth they signify (Doc_06 `syrlex001`; Doc_05, Section 5.1). Aphrahat's own interpretive practice runs alongside this with a heavier debt to Jewish exegetical method, his anti-Jewish Demonstrations constituting evidence of a live, textured argument with a real interlocutor tradition rather than a generic literary exercise (Doc_05, Section 5.1, citing Koltun-Fromm).

**How interpretation shaped formation:** A practitioner formed as a reader in this world does not inherit one uniform method of reading; the method itself depends on anchor context. Ephrem's raza-method is tuned against doctrinal rivals who claim their own competing symbolic readings (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani — C3). Aphrahat's Jewish-exegetical debt is tuned against an actual dialogue partner tradition his Demonstrations directly engage. The two hermeneutics are not variations in depth of the same method; they are two different reading-postures shaped by two different rhetorical opponents.

**Gravities most illuminated:** C1 primarily (Ephrem's raza-method); C3 secondarily, since Aphrahat's own anti-Jewish exegetical engagement is simultaneously interpretive method and boundary-work (Doc_05, Section 2.3, Section 5.1).

**What Interpretive Ecology reveals:** This is a subtly different kind of plurality than 2D's boundary bifurcation. In 2D, the same threat-category (external pressure) differed in kind by anchor context. Here, the interpretive method itself differs because the rhetorical opponent differs — meaning this world's practitioners were never trained in one single, portable "how to read" that traveled between contexts; how one read was itself shaped by whom one was reading against.

### 2H — Representative Theological Patterns [OPTIONAL — applied]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Doc_05, Section 5.2, already names two representative theological patterns without connecting either to the Formation Logic synthesis this document develops in 2E. This lens makes that connection explicit: the two patterns are two applications of the same underlying formation aim (undividedness), each tuned to its own anchor context's besetting threat.

**The theological patterns most formation-shaping:** Ephrem represents a poetic-typological pattern — doctrine argued through hymn, symbol, and polemical refutation of named rivals, addressed to a community whose most pressing formation task is telling true faith apart from plausible neighbors (Doc_05, Section 5.2). Aphrahat represents a pastoral-apologetic pattern — doctrine argued through sustained prose demonstration, addressed to a community whose most pressing formation task is enduring a hostile state without losing coherence (Doc_05, Section 5.2).

**How doctrinal commitments shaped practice and self-understanding:** Doctrine-as-argued-through-hymn trains an audience in the vigilant, aesthetic-typological discernment named in 2A and 2G — practitioners formed to hear a rival's plausible-sounding claim and recognize the severed raza within it. Doctrine-as-argued-through-sustained-demonstration trains an audience in patient, reasoned endurance — practitioners formed to hold a sustained argument's full weight across a hostile, uncertain period rather than react to an acute single threat.

**What Representative Theological Patterns reveals:** The two patterns are not two different formation logics coexisting in one world; they are two different applications of the single formation logic named in 2E, each shaped to answer its own anchor context's own named danger (2A) through its own genre (2G).

### 2I — Material Culture [OPTIONAL — not applied]
