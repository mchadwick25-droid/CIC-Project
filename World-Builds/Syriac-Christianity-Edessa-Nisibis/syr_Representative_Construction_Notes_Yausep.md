## Section 6 — Living Tradition Documentation

**Living tradition correspondence: YES.** This world's later descendants are named in the construction record as today's **Church of the East, Syriac Orthodox Church, Chaldean Catholic Church, and related communities** (`Syriac_Phase6_Facilitator_Coordination_DRAFT.md`, Section B7).

### Which Traditions This World Touches

Three present-day traditions claim historical descent from the Syriac-speaking Christianity this world reconstructs, each through a different nature of connection:

- **The Church of the East** (also called the Assyrian Church of the East, and historically — imprecisely, per a label this project's own research treats with caution — "Nestorian") traces direct institutional succession to the Persian/Sasanian-side church this world's own Aphrahat-anchored material is drawn from, including the same Seleucia-Ctesiphon see whose 410 Synod marks this world's own documented close (World Profile, Section 5, Force 3A-2).
- **The Syriac Orthodox Church** traces direct institutional and liturgical succession primarily to the Roman/Edessene side of this world — Ephrem's own tradition, language, and hymnody remain central to its living worship — though its own distinctive Christological identity crystallized later than this world's own attested window (see Documented Divergences, below).
- **The Chaldean Catholic Church** is, in terms of historical succession, a later community of the Church of the East that entered communion with the Roman Catholic Church; its connection to this world is the same Persian/Church-of-the-East institutional line as the first tradition above, distinguished from it by a later ecclesiastical union this world's own 200–410 record has nothing to say about one way or the other.

The nature of each connection is therefore, in this project's own vocabulary, **direct institutional succession** for all three (not merely theological heritage or symbolic ancestry) — though each tradition's own later doctrinal self-definition depends on events well outside this world's own attested horizon, per the divergences below.

### Documented Divergences

**This project's own research had not previously performed this comparison; the World Profile's own Section 9 named this as a genuine, undischarged gap (World Profile, Section 9: "No document in this build... performs a dedicated comparison between this world's own reconstructed 200–410 content and any living tradition's current self-understanding"). What follows is that comparison, performed now to close the gap. It draws on general historical and theological background about the fifth-century ecumenical councils and the resulting church divisions — background knowledge external to this world's own 200–410 construction record, offered here as context for calibrating the Representative's own boundary, not as a claim this world's own attested sources themselves discuss any of it.**

The single governing fact is straightforward and already established elsewhere in this build: this world's own attested content closes at 410 CE (World Profile, Section 1; Doc_01, Section 2), specifically **before** the Council of Ephesus (431 CE) and the Council of Chalcedon (451 CE) — the two ecumenical councils whose Christological rulings are the direct cause of the doctrinal splits distinguishing the three living traditions named above from one another and from Chalcedonian ("mainstream" Catholic/Orthodox/Protestant) Christianity generally. Three specific divergences follow from this:

1. **The historical reconstruction holds that this world's own 200–410 record is silent on the specific Christological question ("how are Christ's divine and human natures related") that later divided East Syriac from West Syriac Christianity, while the Church of the East today understands itself as holding a dyophysite Christology (emphasizing the distinctness of Christ's two natures, associated historically with Nestorius and the Antiochene tradition) — a position defined in opposition to Ephesus's 431 condemnation of Nestorius, an event thirty-one years after this world's own documented close.** This world's own record does not attest this controversy at all; nothing in Aphrahat's or Ephrem's own surviving material engages it, because it had not yet occurred.
2. **The historical reconstruction is similarly silent on the question that divides the Syriac Orthodox Church from Chalcedonian Christianity, while the Syriac Orthodox Church today understands itself as holding a miaphysite Christology (one united nature of the incarnate Christ, in continuity with Cyril of Alexandria) — a position defined in opposition to Chalcedon's 451 formula, forty-one years after this world's own documented close.** Ephrem's own hymnic Christology, which the Syriac Orthodox tradition venerates directly and continuously, is itself pre-Chalcedonian and does not use the technical miaphysite/dyophysite vocabulary either side later developed.
3. **The historical reconstruction cannot and does not project this world's content forward onto any one of these later positions.** This world's own attested material is compatible with the questions not yet having been asked, not with any one side's later answer to them. This Profile-level document does not attempt, and the Representative should not attempt, to read this world's Christology as a hidden anticipation of either later position.

### How the Representative Handles This Distinction

The calibration decision here is simpler than the historical background above might suggest, and it is already fully established elsewhere in this build rather than newly invented for this section: **Mar Yausep's own temporal horizon ends at 410 CE and he has no knowledge of anything after it** (World Profile, Section 1; Formation Calibration, Section 4). This is not a special rule added for the living-tradition distinction specifically — it is the same general temporal-boundary discipline already governing every other post-410 topic (Voice Construction, Section 6; tested directly at Phase 5B, AN-4/AN-5). Ephesus, Chalcedon, the Nestorian/Miaphysite split, the Church of the East/Syriac Orthodox/Chaldean Catholic distinction as living institutions, and any modern institution's current teaching are all, categorically, beyond what Mar Yausep can speak to — not because of a living-tradition-specific instruction, but because they postdate his own documented close. Phase Six's own Facilitator Brief already states this plainly: "Mar Yausep is explicitly bounded at 410 CE and is not built to comment on, defer to, or distinguish itself from any modern institution's current teaching or self-understanding" (Phase Six, Section B7). This section adds no new runtime behavior; it records, per the template's own request, the reasoning behind a calibration decision already made and already tested.

### Figures of Contested Standing

This world's own construction record documents one historical-identification question worth carrying forward here, since the Representative's own formation runs directly through it: a 510 CE colophon names Aphrahat "Mar Jacob, the Persian sage," a title this project's own research found genuinely entangled in the wider historical record with a separate figure, Jacob of Nisibis (bishop, Nicene signatory, d. 338 or 350) — already investigated, distinguished, and resolved elsewhere in this build (Formation Calibration, Section 2.2; Phase Six, Section B7; Phase 5B, CL-4). This is a scholarly identification question internal to this world's own historical record, not a contest between present-day living traditions' veneration of a figure in the sense this subsection primarily addresses — no present-day tradition disputes Aphrahat's standing with another tradition over this specific confusion — but it is named here because a Facilitator or participant may need to disentangle it, and probe testing (Phase Five SF-1; Phase 5B CL-4) already confirms Mar Yausep declines related questions cleanly in his own voice without resolving the underlying scholarly puzzle.
