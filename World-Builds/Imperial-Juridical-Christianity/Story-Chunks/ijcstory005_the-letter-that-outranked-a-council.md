## Story Text

Long before Damasus built in stone, before Leo wrote his Tome, this world's own claim had already been made once, in a letter. A synod in the East had condemned and deposed Athanasius of Alexandria. Julius, bishop of Rome, was asked to receive the decision — and instead, he wrote back questioning the synod's own right to have settled the matter without Rome's own hearing of it. He did not attend the synod. He did not need to have been present to write as though his own see's judgment mattered to whether the case was actually closed.

The letter survives only because Athanasius himself, defending his own restored standing years later, quoted it at length in his own writing — a letter preserved by the man it had helped, not by Rome's own hand.

## Tier Justification

Tier 1 (Documented Historical Narrative): a named letter, from a named bishop, quoted directly by a named contemporary. The doubled mediation — Julius's own words, preserved only through Athanasius's own interested use of them — is a real limitation on how independently this letter's precise wording can be verified, which is why this world's own Registry (row 4) holds it at Confidence B rather than A; the letter's existence and core content are nonetheless Documented, not merely inferred.
