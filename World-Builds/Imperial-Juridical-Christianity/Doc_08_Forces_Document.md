## Section 2 — Preliminary Forces Identification

Drawn directly from Doc_01 §5, this world's own Step 1 preliminary forces identification, not restated retrospectively:

**What generated this world:** Constantine's political and military victory at the Milvian Bridge (312) and the subsequent legal toleration of Christianity (Edict of Milan, 313), converting Christian episcopal office from a persecuted status into a form of state-recognized, and increasingly state-adjacent, authority.

**What larger world it was embedded in:** the late Roman imperial state itself — its legal, administrative, and military apparatus — and, within Christianity, the broader Nicene-trajectory church whose eligibility this world's own Step 0 confirmation tested. For long stretches of this exact window, the "larger world" this world was embedded in was not securely Nicene: under Constantius II and Valens, Homoian Christianity *was* the imperial church.

**What it was responding to at its origin:** the preceding era of persecution (the Diocletianic persecution, ending 311–313) and the unprecedented practical problem of maintaining doctrinal coherence across a newly-patronized, empire-wide church.

**What it was refusing:** for the Nicene actors this world centers, Homoian/Arian imperially-favored theology; for Ambrose specifically, unlimited imperial authority over the church; for Leo specifically, the reduction of ecclesiastical rank to a function of civil/political rank.

**How this preliminary identification governed Step 2 scope:** it directed Doc_02's own evidentiary work toward institutional-juridical sources (conciliar acts, decretals, imperial legislation) over devotional or ascetic material, and toward the Homoian-recentering disclosure obligation specifically — the expectation, confirmed at Doc_02, that this world's own record would underrepresent the losing side of its central conflict was itself part of what Step 1's own preliminary orientation anticipated.

## Section 3 — The Six-Cell Matrix

Per the From-Within Principle, Layer 2 below uses this world's own vocabulary; modern analytical categories govern Layer 1 only.

### CELL 1A — INITIATING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external conditions made this particular form of Christianity possible, necessary, or urgent at its origin?

#### Force 1A-1: Constantine's victory and the Edict of Milan

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Constantine's victory at the Milvian Bridge (312) and the subsequent legal toleration of Christianity (Edict of Milan, 313), ending the Diocletianic persecution and establishing imperial favor toward the church. Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** an emperor, once an enemy, now stands with the church rather than against it. Eusebius's own account renders this not as a political accident but as a hinge in the church's own story — the moment persecution's own long era gives way to something the church's own prior generations could not have expected: an emperor who asks the church's own bishops to gather, judge, and settle its own disputes, rather than an emperor who hunts it.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this force is the world's own beginning. Candidate 2 (Church-State Alliance and Its Limits) is its most direct product; Candidates 1, 3, and 4 all depend on the alliance this force establishes existing at all, though each connects to it through different, later intermediate forces rather than to this force directly (see Section 5).

### CELL 1B — INITIATING / INTERNAL

**Governing question:** What internal commitments, convictions, or inherited traditions gave this world its earliest distinctive shape from within?

#### Force 1B-1: Pre-existing proto-orthodox theological commitments and an already-functioning episcopal office

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** by 312, a proto-orthodox Nicene-trajectory church already existed with a functioning monepiscopal office structure, an inheritance this world shares with the earlier Post-Apostolic/Sub-Apostolic House-Church world (World #1) this project has already built. Confidence: Documented to Widely Accepted.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** the church did not wait for an emperor to know what it believed or how it was to be governed. What changed in 312 was not the church's own convictions but what those convictions could now do in the world — an office built to shepherd a persecuted flock is asked, almost overnight, to also settle imperial-scale disputes.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this is what made Force 1A-1 formative rather than merely permissive — an alliance offered to a church with no functioning office or settled confession would have produced a different world entirely. Connects to Candidate 2 (jointly with 1A-1) and to Candidate 4 and Candidate 6, whose own grounds (sacramental leverage; the sacramental/institutional tension) both root in this same inherited pre-alliance material.

### CELL 2A — ONGOING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external forces required this community's continuous response and adaptation throughout its active life?

#### Force 2A-1: Imperial religious policy oscillation

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** under Constantius II (337–361) and Valens (364–378), Homoian Christianity was the enforced imperial confession; under Theodosius I (Edict of Thessalonica, 380), Nicene Christianity was re-established as the empire's own confession. Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** imperial favor is not something granted once and then simply held. A bishop confirmed in office under one emperor's own confession could be exiled under the next, and restored under the one after that — the record of who rightly held an episcopal see at any given moment could not, in these decades, be settled by office alone, only by which confession the reigning emperor currently held.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this force intensified over time rather than remaining constant — its formative pressure sharpens with each reversal (359–360, then 380) rather than applying evenly across the whole window. Connects directly to Candidates 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6 (Section 5) — the single most connected force in this world's own matrix.

#### Force 2A-2: Western political and military fragmentation

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** the Western Empire's own administrative and military coherence weakened progressively across the later 4th and 5th centuries, visible within this world's own window at Alaric's sack of Rome (410). Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** the city whose bishop claims to guard an unbroken apostolic memory is, within living memory of that very claim's own consolidation, sacked by a foreign army — not experienced as background historical drift but as a direct, visible sign that the political order the church's own alliance had been built alongside is not permanent.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this force does not produce a new gravity so much as it transforms what an already-existing force (1A-1, the alliance) comes to mean for Strand A specifically over time — an alliance originally received as favor becomes, as this force intensifies, a resource Strand A must actively substitute for as its own political partner weakens. Connects to Candidate 1 (Section 5).

### CELL 2B — ONGOING / INTERNAL [includes Transmission dimension]

**Governing question:** What internal forces sustained this community from within, generated its characteristic internal tensions, and shaped how it transmitted itself?

#### Force 2B-1: Recurring internal contest over final doctrinal and disciplinary authority

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** across this world's own window, the question of where final authority to settle doctrine and discipline actually resides — the emperor, an assembled council, or the bishop of Rome — is never permanently closed; it is replayed at Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, and Chalcedon. Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** every settlement is provisional in a way not experienced as a defect but as the ordinary condition of the work itself — a council settles a question for now, for this dispute, and the next dispute reopens the same underlying question of who gets to settle it, not because the last council's own authority is doubted in principle, but because the same three claimants never stop existing side by side.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** directly tied to Candidate 5 (Doctrinal/Christological Precision-Seeking) and, more broadly, the standing condition Candidates 1 and 3 both operate within.

#### Force 2B-2: Transmission — archival and documentary preservation

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** conciliar acts, papal decretals, and episcopal correspondence were preserved disproportionately by the institutions with the strongest archival culture — Rome's own developing chancery, later canon-law compilers, and Damasus's own deliberate epigraphic-commissioning program (the Filocalian-lettered martyr inscriptions). Confidence: Widely Accepted (Doc_02 §2).

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** this world's own institutions do not experience their own record-keeping as a neutral act of preservation. Damasus's own program is a deliberate act of shaping what future generations will find and how they will read it — the church's own institutions know that what they write down and preserve is itself part of how a claim endures, not a passive byproduct of having survived.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this is the specific, named mechanism behind Doc_02 §6's finding that this world's own record structurally underrepresents ordinary-believer experience — the institutions with the archival capacity to preserve anything are the same institutions whose own documentary interest runs toward juridical self-presentation, not communal memoir (Doc_05 §1's own forces-integration finding). Full synthesis at Section 6.

### CELL 3A — ENDING/TRANSFORMING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external forces brought this world's distinct form to an end or transformed it into something different?

#### Force 3A-1: Chalcedon's own verdict fails to produce lasting empire-wide consensus

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Chalcedon's Christological settlement (451) is rejected immediately by miaphysite communities, particularly in Egypt, beginning a process of durable ecclesiastical division rather than the unified settlement the council itself intended. Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** the mechanism this world's own actors had relied on across the whole of this window — gather, argue, settle, and expect the settlement to bind — visibly fails to do what it had always been trusted to do, at the exact moment when the stakes of it working were highest.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this force does not itself organize a specific Doc_04 gravity — a boundary-condition force, like Force 3A-2 below, real and load-bearing without directly producing a confirmed gravity of its own. Its own formation impact runs through Force 3B-1: it is the direct external condition Rome's own rejection (below) reacts to (Section 4, Connection 5) — corrected after this document's first-drafted pass wrongly attached to *this* force the "closing event... primacy-claiming collision" quote that Doc_04 itself attaches specifically to Canon 28 and Leo's rejection (Force 3B-1's own territory), not to the miaphysite rejection this force documents.

#### Force 3A-2: Western imperial authority's continuing collapse

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** the process of Western imperial dissolution, already visible at Force 2A-2 (410), continues through this world's own closing decades, reaching its conventional terminus (476) a generation after this world's own window closes. Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** the alliance both Rome and Constantinople depend on is, by this world's own closing years, an alliance with a political partner whose own Western half is visibly failing — not yet the alliance's end, but the ground it was built on shifting under it.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this force is why this world's own boundary (312–451) is defensible as ending inside, rather than before, the process of Western collapse (Doc_01 §2), and why this world's own closing self-understanding cannot include confident knowledge of how the alliance would ultimately resolve. Not independently connected to a specific Doc_04 gravity — a boundary-condition force, named per the Proportionality Principle rather than force-fit onto a gravity it does not actually organize.

### CELL 3B — ENDING/TRANSFORMING / INTERNAL [includes Transmission dimension]

**Governing question:** What internal forces fractured this community, generated successor movements, or led to the dissolution of its characteristic form?

#### Force 3B-1: Leo's rejection of Canon 28 — the Strand A/B fracture

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Leo I refused to ratify Canon 28 of Chalcedon, which had granted Constantinople rank second only to Rome on political-geographic grounds; his own legate objected at the council itself, and Leo's own subsequent letters record the rejection directly. Confidence: Documented.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** from Rome's own side, this is not one more disputed canon among many but a direct challenge to the very ground Rome's own claim rests on — if rank can be granted by a council on the basis of a city's nearness to the throne, then rank is not, after all, secured by apostolic inheritance alone, and the response is not merely tactical disagreement but a defense of what the claim actually is.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this force is Doc_01's own Strand Determination finding made visible as an event — the two strands (A and B) Doc_01 §4 found operating on different, never-reconciled grounds throughout this world's own window collide directly, for the first time on the documentary record, here. This is Candidate 1's own direct closing evidentiary instance — Doc_04's own text names exactly this collision, not Force 3A-1's miaphysite rejection, as "the world's own closing event (Chalcedon, Canon 28, Leo's rejection)... itself a primacy-claiming collision" (corrected here from an earlier misattachment of this same quote to Force 3A-1).

#### Force 3B-2: Transmission — the 451 schism's own selection effect

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** the schism beginning at Chalcedon determines, from 451 forward, whose own records and self-understanding are transmitted as normative and whose as a rejected minority position — a selection effect with consequences extending well beyond this world's own 451 close (Doc_01 §5). Confidence: Widely Accepted.

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** the communities on the losing side of this fracture do not experience their own exclusion as a fair hearing concluded. This world's own record, being substantially the record of the side that prevailed institutionally, cannot itself supply the rejected communities' own account of what happened to them — that self-interpretation is not recoverable from surviving sources.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** this is the specific mechanism by which this world's own closing rupture becomes permanent rather than provisional like every earlier settlement (Force 2B-1) — transmission itself, not merely the doctrinal disagreement, is what makes this particular fracture durable. Full synthesis at Section 6.

## Section 6 — Transmission as a Force Dimension — Synthesis

### Who Transmitted This World's Characteristic Material

Named specifically, not generically: Rome's own developing episcopal chancery (visible from Damasus onward, 366–384); Damasus's own epigraphic-commissioning program specifically, using the Filocalian lettering workshop to produce durable, public inscriptions at existing martyr shrines (corrected to drop "dated" — not established as a general feature of the Damasan corpus); later canon-law compilers who preserved and organized conciliar acts; and, for the world's own narrative connective tissue, the three 5th-century continuator-historians (Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret, Doc_02 §2) whose own institutional position (Constantinopolitan lay lawyer; a lawyer with monastic sympathies; an Antiochene bishop) shaped what they each preserved. Eusebius stands apart from this same list, by design, not by omission: Doc_02 §2 characterizes his own transmission problem as authorial (his own court proximity shaping what he chose to write) rather than institutional-archival in the way the chancery, the epigraphic program, and the compilers are — he is not folded into "who transmitted" here because his own case is a different mechanism, treated in its own right at Doc_02 §2 rather than restated as though it belonged to this institutional list.

### Under What Conditions Transmission Occurred

Preservation tracked institutional interest specifically: material serving a see's own juridical self-presentation (Damasus's epigraphy, Leo's Tome and letters, conciliar canons) was preserved through deliberate institutional investment — commissioning, copying, archiving — not passive survival. Material without an institutional patron invested in preserving it (ordinary-believer testimony, the Homoian side of the central confessional conflict) survived only incidentally or not at all.

### What Selection Effects Shaped What Survives

This world's own Native Registry (Doc_02, Source Registry) shows the effect directly: elite, literate, institutional voices (Eusebius, Ambrose, Leo, Damasus) are extensively attested; Homoian Christianity's own self-testimony survives in exactly one substantial instance (Auxentius's letter on Ulfila), and only because a fellow Homoian happened to preserve it within his own dossier — even then surviving only as a later partly-erased marginal layer (Doc_02 §7; Doc_06's *homoios* entry). This is not a gap in what once existed evenly; it is a direct, traceable consequence of whose institutions had the resources and motive to preserve their own record.

### What the Transmission Pattern Reveals About Forces

Communities preserve what they judge worth defending. What survives from this world's own record — juridical, institutional, primacy-asserting material, overwhelmingly — reveals what this world's own strongest institutions understood themselves to be most at risk of losing: not devotional continuity, but the standing to make a claim that binds. This is consistent with, and further evidence for, this world's own dominant gravity (Candidate 1) — the pattern of what was preserved is itself an expression of the same organizing force that produced the material being preserved.

### Connection to Author Gravity Findings (Doc_02)

Doc_02 §2's own Author Gravity assessment already names this mechanism for each major voice individually — but not uniformly by the same mechanism, and this section does not flatten that distinction. Leo's and Damasus's own dominance traces to institutional transmission specifically, exactly as Section 6 describes above; Eusebius's own dominance traces to a different mechanism (authorial court-proximity, Doc_02 §2), not to institutional-archival transmission, and is not claimed as such here. What can be stated at the level of the whole world's own record, accurately: the thin or absent voices (Doc_02 §6 — ordinary believers, women beyond Justina and Pulcheria, Homoian Christians) are thin specifically because institutional transmission did not carry them, not because they left no impression on this world's own life — the absence side of this connection holds uniformly even where the presence side does not.
