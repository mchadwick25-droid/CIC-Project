## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing governs the order here: *"This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."*

### A1 — The floor

Article 4's floor (Constitution V2.3, "On the Scope of 'Movement'") tests whether a movement's own confession affirms, in its plain historical sense, the content drawn from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (381) — full divinity and consubstantiality of Christ, true humanity, the Passion/resurrection/ascension/return, and the Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified with the Father and Son.

This world's defining, identity-conferring phase — the phase the merge reasoning names as what the earlier phases exist to make possible ("Damasus-through-Leo's jurisdictional consolidation converts both the alliance and Ambrose's demonstrated leverage into durable, juridical Roman primacy claims") — is the direct institutional embodiment of Nicene-Chalcedonian orthodoxy. Damasus presided over Rome's own confirmation and assertion of Nicene-Constantinopolitan primacy claims (his own see was largely secure Nicene territory under Valentinian I and Gratian — the sustained Homoian imperial pressure of this period fell principally on the East, under Valens, not on Rome itself); Ambrose's confrontations with Theodosius are conducted explicitly as a pro-Nicene bishop asserting ecclesiastical authority over an emperor who had himself made Nicene Christianity the empire's establishment religion (Edict of Thessalonica, 380), and Ambrose's own direct standoff with a Homoian imperial court did occur, against Justina and the young Valentinian II over the Milan basilicas in 386; Leo I's Tome is a Chalcedonian Christological document, and Leo's own role at Chalcedon (451) is the world's own closing bookend. On its own plain confessional terms, at the point where this world is most fully itself, it affirms Article 4's content without qualification. **A1 clears directly.**

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements: applicable to a thirteen-year sliver, and it clears

The Methodology's own Procedure section governs which of A1/A2/A3 actually applies to a given candidate: *"Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)."* A2 itself is triggered by "a movement that predates 325," tested by continuity — because a movement wholly formed before 325 cannot be tested against a creed that did not yet exist for it — and its stated rationale tracks "the descriptive distinction scholars of the period draw between proto-orthodox Christianity — the strand whose beliefs became the Nicene consensus — and rival movements (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, and similar groups) whose beliefs diverged from it."

This world's stated start date, 312 CE (Constantine's alliance with the church, conventionally dated from the Battle of the Milvian Bridge), precedes Nicaea (325) by thirteen years. Read against the Procedure's own "as applicable" framing, the honest application is not to pick A1 *or* A2 for the whole world, but to recognize that both tests apply, to different phases of a single continuous trajectory, and both clear:

- **312–325 (A2's proper scope):** does this world's own pre-Nicene confession show continuity with the strand that became the Nicene consensus, rather than divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar)? Yes, straightforwardly — this sliver's defining actor, Eusebius of Caesarea, was not merely continuous with proto-orthodoxy but a direct participant in producing the 325 Creed itself thirteen years later. There is no rival-strand divergence to test for; continuity is about as strong as A2 could ask for.
- **325–451 (A1's proper scope):** does the movement's own confession, once the Creed exists, affirm it in the plain historical sense? Yes — this is the world's defining, world-naming phase (Damasus through Leo, ending at Chalcedon 451), and it clears A1 directly on the reasoning already given above.

**Resolution for this world:** both A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of the same continuous movement, and both clear on the text's own terms — not a forced either/or, and not resting on any rationale beyond what A2 and the Procedure section actually state.

**Narrower process observation, logged for System Hub (Section 5 below):** the Procedure's "as applicable" language is itself sufficient to route a straddling case like this one correctly, once actually consulted — this world's build did not initially consult it and instead reached for an invented rationale for excluding A2 outright, which was a drafting error in this document's first pass, not a real gap in the Methodology. What the Methodology does still leave unstated is how "applicable" is determined in the first place for a straddling start date — this document's resolution (apply both, to their respective phases) is a reasonable reading, but it is this build's own reading, not a rule the text spells out.

### A3 — Contemporary movements and interpretive fidelity

Not applicable. This is a 4th/5th-century historical movement, not a contemporary confessional community reinterpreting Nicene vocabulary non-literally.

### A4 — Hand-selected adjacent inclusion

Not invoked and not needed. A1 clears this world directly on its own confessional terms; no divergence from Article 4's five commitments needs to be waived. (Contrast Homoian Christianity itself, discussed under A5 below, which is excluded on the floor and was considered for, but not routed through, A4 at the portfolio level.)

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This is the section with the most substantive content for this specific world, because Imperial and Juridical Christianity's own temporal window (312–451) includes long stretches — under Constantius II (337–361) and Valens (364–378) — when **Homoian ("Arian") Christianity was the imperial church itself**, not a defeated rival operating from outside. A5 states plainly that a movement's historical rivals "are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under this section... they appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates."

This confirms, at the Step 0 level, what the Step 0 Conclusion already stated as a disclosure obligation: Homoian Christianity does not get its own Step 0 eligibility test inside this world's build (it was already tested and excluded at the portfolio level, on the doctrinal floor, as its own candidate world — a floor effect, not a judgment of unimportance). Its correct place *inside* this world's build is as Doc_02's own subject matter: the imperial-institutional rival this world's Nicene actors were actually contending with, for real, for real stretches of the exact period being reconstructed — not a footnote to an already-settled Nicene victory. This is carried forward as a binding requirement on Doc_02, not resolved here (Section 4 below).

Strand Determination (Article 21) — whether this world contains internal strands requiring separate tracking (e.g., an Eusebian/moderate-episcopal strand versus a Damasus-Leo/Roman-primacy strand versus Ambrose's confrontational-episcopal strand) — is explicitly Step 1's own work, not Step 0's. Flagged forward to Doc_01, not resolved here.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Test: does enough independent, checkable evidence survive to support a defensible reconstruction at Documented or Widely Accepted confidence for its core claims, with the source-asymmetry named rather than hidden?

Strong pass. This is among the best-documented candidates in the entire nine-world portfolio, because its actors are precisely the elite, literate, institutionally-central figures whose writing survival rate is highest:
- Eusebius of Caesarea: *Ecclesiastical History*, *Life of Constantine* — direct primary testimony on the founding alliance, by a direct participant and eyewitness to Nicaea itself.
- Ambrose of Milan: an extensive surviving corpus of letters, sermons, and treatises, including his own first-person account of the Theodosius confrontations (Letter 51 on the Thessalonica massacre; the Callinicum affair correspondence).
- Damasus through Leo I: conciliar acts (Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381, Chalcedon 451), papal letters and decretals, and — for Leo specifically — the Tome to Flavian, itself a primary theological-juridical document.
- Imperial legal material: the Theodosian Code, imperial edicts (Thessalonica 380 among them), giving this world an evidentiary layer — state legislation on religion — that most of the other eight candidate worlds simply do not have access to.
- Secondary scholarship, source-matrix additions already confirmed at the portfolio level: Neil McLynn (*Ambrose of Milan: Church and Court*), T.D. Barnes (*Constantine and Eusebius*), George Demacopoulos (*The Invention of Peter*, under consideration).

Source-asymmetry disclosure, carried forward as a binding Doc_02 requirement rather than resolved here: this world's evidentiary strength is itself a symptom of what it structurally excludes. Every one of the sources above is elite, literate, male, urban, and institutionally powerful almost by definition — this world's own subject matter (church-state power, juridical authority) selects for exactly the voices the portfolio-wide disclosure already names as over-represented across all nine worlds. Doc_02 must name this plainly, not merely gesture at the general portfolio-wide caveat.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

Test: can this candidate plausibly support most or all eight ecology lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only?

Genuine, honestly-named limitation, not a disqualifying one. This world's center of gravity is unmistakably the Organizational/Authority Structures lens and the Power, Influence & Historical Dynamics lens — it is, by its own merge reasoning, a world *about* institutional authority. Worship Integration has real material (Ambrose is credited with introducing antiphonal psalm-singing and Latin hymnody at Milan, directly tied to his standoff with the Homoian imperial court over the Milan basilicas in 386 — worship practice and institutional confrontation are, in this specific case, the same event). Formation Ecology and Meaning Transmission at the level of an ordinary believer's lived experience, by contrast, are thin: this world's surviving sources speak from and about bishops, emperors, and courts, not about how an ordinary Milanese or Roman layperson actually experienced formation day to day. This is a real, structural thinness this world shares with a "power and authority" subject matter, not a defect in the sourcing — but it means Doc_05's Ecological Reconstruction will need to name this proportionality honestly rather than manufacture ordinary-believer texture the sources don't support.

**Passes B2, with this limitation logged forward as a binding disclosure for Doc_02 and Doc_05 (Section 4 below), not treated as resolved by noting it here.**

**Passes B3.** The one adjacency requiring active vigilance during construction (World #5) is named here so Doc_01/Doc_02 treat it as a live boundary question, not an assumed-obvious one.

### B4 — User needs (audience fit)

Test: does this candidate offer something distinctive to several of the Essential Experience document's named audiences, honestly noted rather than claimed universally.

Honest assessment: this world's strongest fit is with *historically interested learners*, *academically minded users*, and *pastors and teachers* wrestling with how church and state power became entangled — a live question for anyone thinking about Christianity's relationship to political power today. It offers real material to *thoughtful believers* examining the institutional church's own history critically. Its fit with *curious seekers* and *deconstructing Christians* is weaker and should not be overstated: this is not a devotional or formational world in the way Desert Monasticism or Post-Apostolic House-Church Christianity is; a participant looking for lived spiritual formation will find less of that register here than in most of the other eight worlds. **Passes B4, honestly scoped rather than claimed universally** — matching the Methodology's own instruction not to maximize this criterion.

## 5. Process findings for System Hub (Step 0 methodology, first live per-world application)

Per this thread's own launch instructions, anything about the new process that was unclear, underspecified, or didn't work as described is reported here as a finding, not quietly worked around.

1. **A1/A2 applicability for a movement whose start date straddles 325 CE — largely resolved by the Methodology's own Procedure text, with one narrower residual gap.** See §2's A2 discussion in full. The Procedure section's "Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)" already establishes that a pre-325 start date does not mechanically and exclusively trigger A2 to the exclusion of A1 — both may apply, to different phases of one movement. The narrower thing actually left unstated: the Methodology does not spell out *how* a builder determines which of A1/A2/A3 is "applicable" for a straddling case in the first place — this document's resolution (apply both, to their respective phases, when a movement's pre-325 sliver and post-325 core are part of one continuous trajectory) is this build's own reasonable reading, not a rule the text states directly. Recommend Section A gain a short worked example of a straddling case, rather than leaving "as applicable" to be inferred.

2. **The Step 0 Conclusion's "Criterion 2" (person-defined-movement test) has no home in the formally codified Section A.** Confirmed directly against the Step 0 Methodology V1.0 text extracted for this build: Section A contains exactly five subtests (A1 floor, A2 pre-Nicene continuity, A3 contemporary interpretive fidelity, A4 hand-selected inclusion, A5 interaction with Articles 20/21/23) — no person-defined-movement test appears anywhere in it. The Step 0 Conclusion itself is transparent that this is "adopted here, in this pass, as a considered change to the survey — not asserted as something that was always true and merely being recalled." This does not affect World #6 (Criterion 2 was applied only to Montanism and Novatianism, and would not exclude a multi-figure, century-spanning institutional world like this one even if applied) — flagged here purely as a standing methodology gap: a real, adopted screening criterion is currently living only inside a portfolio-conclusion document's prose, not inside the Methodology document Section A itself governs. Recommend either formally adding it to Section A as an A6, or explicitly scoping it as portfolio-level-only and out of Section A's per-world text.

3. **No explicit Step 0 per-world output template exists.** The Framework V7.4 DRAFT's Step 0 stub and the Methodology V1.0 document both describe Step 0 as a phase-level survey producing a *seed list* — they do not describe what a later, individual-world *confirmation* pass (this document) should contain, since the Methodology assumes Step 0 happens once, before any specific world is chosen, not as a per-world gate re-run later. This document's structure (candidate identification → Section A walkthrough → Section B walkthrough → disclosure obligations carried forward → process findings) was built by analogy to the Methodology's own structure, not from an explicit template, because none exists for this use case. Recommend naming this pattern (or a revised one) explicitly if per-world Step 0 confirmations are expected to recur for future worlds selected from an existing seed list rather than freshly surveyed.
