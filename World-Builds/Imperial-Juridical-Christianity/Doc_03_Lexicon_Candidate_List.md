## Notes on scope and process

- **Terms considered and not included**, with reasoning, so the exclusion is visible rather than silent: *Caesaropapism* (a modern historiographical category, not this world's own vocabulary — belongs in the Facilitator's world-agnostic modern-term bridge, not in this world's own native lexicon, per this project's established glossary-ownership discipline); *Arianism* (the polemical umbrella term this document and Doc_02 §7 deliberately avoid in favor of the more precise *homoios*/Homoian vocabulary — its absence here is a disclosed choice, not an oversight).
- **Strand-attribution pattern worth naming, stated accurately rather than over-tidied:** most, not all, of the candidate list aligns with Doc_01's own three-strand finding (*primatus*/*sedes apostolica* and, more loosely, *Tomus*/*martyrium* for A; *presbeia*/*Nea Rhōmē* for B; the Ambrosian formula and *basilica* for C), but five terms don't fit that clean a picture: *homoios* sits at the world level, outside all three strands (it is this world's own required imperial-rival vocabulary, not any strand's own); *homoousios* is genuinely cross-strand, held by all three, most existentially by Strand C specifically; and *concilium*, *communio*, and *haeresis* are institutional vocabulary operating at the level of the world as a whole. This mixed picture — not a clean three-way split — is itself a candidate observation for Doc_04's cross-strand gravity testing, not a finding this document is positioned to resolve on its own.
- **Author Gravity risk is unusually concentrated in this world's own candidate list** relative to what a lighter-authority-structure world might show: five of twelve candidate terms carry a High risk flag — but, corrected after this document's first-drafted pass wrongly claimed a single unified cause, these five High-risk flags do not all share one underlying pattern. Three (*primatus*/*sedes apostolica*, *communio*, the Ambrosian formula) share the pattern already named in Doc_02 §2: this world's vocabulary is disproportionately coined or preserved by the very actors whose authority claims it exists to support. *homoios* is the structural opposite of that pattern — its risk comes from the vocabulary's own suppression by the winning side, not its promotion by an interested advocate (Doc_02 §7). *Tomus*'s risk is a third, distinct pattern again: single-instance genre-generalization, where one example (Leo's own Tome) does double duty as both the term's definition and its only evidence. Carrying all three patterns forward distinctly into Step 6, not flattened into one, is itself part of what this candidate list is for.
- **Tier 1 flagging, honestly proportioned:** ten of twelve candidate terms are flagged Yes for Tier 1 consideration at Step 6. This is a high proportion relative to the general expectation (a finished lexicon's Tier 1 list should be small relative to its total), and is not itself a finished tiering decision — it reflects this document's own deliberately central-terms-first candidate selection, not a claim that ten terms will actually reach full Tier 1 treatment. Heavy pruning against Doc_04's gravity findings and Doc_05's ecological reconstruction is expected at Step 6, and this list should not be read as pre-committing to ten Tier 1 entries.
- Per the Framework's own instruction, terms requiring full Tier 1 treatment are flagged above for Doc_06; this document assigns no final tier classification of its own, since tiering depends on Doc_04's gravity findings and Doc_05's ecological reconstruction, neither of which exists yet.
