## 2. Reasoning Structure Assessment

**Governing question (RCF V3.2, Part Three):** not only what this world concluded, but how it engaged — how it reasoned, worshipped, formed its members, and worked out its life together — with depth assessed by richness of whatever formation evidence survives, in whatever mode, not specifically by density of written theological argumentation.

This world's evidentiary base is textually thin in absolute terms — only three substantial fourth-century Syriac literary corpora survive from this world's entire linguistic and geographic range: Ephrem's, Aphrahat's, and the *Liber Graduum* (the last only partly in-window and not treated as a primary founding source, Doc_02 §1). Judged purely by volume of written argument, this would be a modest base. But the RCF's own test is explicitly not volume-of-argument; it is richness of evidence for *how* the community engaged, in whatever mode. On that test, this world's surviving material is genuinely multi-modal:

- **Hymnic-liturgical reasoning.** Ephrem's dominant vehicle for theological argument is not treatise but *madrasha* — sung, stanzaic, refrain-structured hymnody (Doc_03 §1.4; Doc_04, C1). This is not merely a stylistic choice but is itself a documented reasoning strategy: Ephrem adopted and mastered a genre Bardaisan and Mani had already made their own, contesting doctrine on shared literary ground rather than inventing a separate mode of address (Doc_02 §3; Doc_03 §1.4; Doc_05 §2.1). Worship is the site where this reasoning is performed, not merely taught — "sung into the body along with the tune" (Doc_05 §3.1).
- **Prose-demonstrative reasoning.** Aphrahat's *taḥwyāṯā* build sustained argument stage by stage using the twenty-two-letter Syriac alphabetic acrostic as a memory-scaffold (Doc_02 §2; Doc_03 §1.3; Doc_05 §2.1) — a documented, distinctive pedagogical-argumentative structure in its own right, independent of doctrinal content.
- **Typological-exegetical reasoning.** The *raza/shrara* hermeneutic — reading Scripture and creation as bound to, not merely representing, the truth they signify — recurs across Ephrem's hymns, prose refutations, and biblical commentaries, and (in a lighter but genuine parallel) across Aphrahat's typological Christ-figuration (Doc_03 §1.1; Doc_04, C1, Repetition/Explanatory tests, both Pass).
- **Ascetic-communal reasoning.** The qyama vow is a lived discipline, not only a doctrinal position: lifelong, town-resident, communal rather than desert-withdrawn (Doc_02 §2; Doc_03 §1.2; Doc_05 §1.1). Doc_04's own Formation test (C2) explicitly passes on the grounds that this institution "directly organized participants' lived practice" (Doc_04, C2).
- **Polemical-relational reasoning.** Ephrem's boundary-work against Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani is reasoning conducted against real, named, locally-present contemporaries rather than abstract categories (Doc_01 §5, §7; Doc_02 §8; Doc_04, C3) — a documented case of theological method shaped by an actual interlocutor.
- **Pastoral reasoning under duress.** Aphrahat's later Demonstrations (11–23, composed 344–345) show reasoning visibly shifting in register and urgency as persecution intensifies — theology worked out in real time for a community "who must decide, in their own bodies, whether the promises... are strong enough to bear the weight being asked of them" (Doc_05 §1.2; Doc_04, C6).

Doc_04's six-test framework independently confirms this richness is not impressionistic: of the six candidate gravities tested, five (C1, C2, C3, C5, C6) pass the Formation test cleanly, meaning each is shown, not merely asserted, to have directly shaped participants' lived formation (Doc_04, §2). Only C4 (authority-structure ambiguity) fails its Formation test — an honest finding, carried forward rather than smoothed (Doc_04, C4; Doc_05 §4.2; Doc_07 §2C) — meaning this world's evidence speaks to *how it reasoned about its own authority structure as a modern reconstruction problem*, but not to how that specific ambiguity was actively lived and reasoned through by participants at the time.

This richness is asymmetric by anchor context: Ephrem's corpus is fuller and more textured than Aphrahat's, and Aphrahat's text itself "offers almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail" (Doc_02 §3). This asymmetry is named here rather than smoothed, and is developed further in Section 6 (Thinness Mapping).

**Assessment:** the reasoning-structure domain is well-recovered. Multiple independent modes of engagement (hymnic, prose-demonstrative, exegetical, ascetic-communal, polemical, pastoral) are each independently and specifically evidenced, not merely asserted by genre label, and five of six candidate gravities pass a structured Formation test.

## 3. Perception Pattern Assessment

**Governing question (RCF V3.2, Part Three):** whether the world's materials reveal a characteristic way of attending to reality — recoverable when a community's reasoning consistently reflects distinctive attentional priorities across multiple authors, contexts, and topics.

The single most distinctive, richly-attested perceptual pattern in this world's own vocabulary is the *raza/shrara* orientation: a *raza* (symbol/mystery/type) is not a sign chosen by arbitrary convention but is bound to the *shrara* (truth) it signifies and carries something of that reality's own "hidden power" (*hayla kasya*) — a deliberate contrast with Platonic/allegorical symbol theory (Doc_03 §1.1, citing Brock; Doc_06 `syrlex001_raza-shrara.md`; independently reconfirmed at Doc_02 §11). This pattern recurs across multiple genuinely distinct topics and contexts within Ephrem's own corpus — exegetical commentary, hymnic composition, and polemical refutation alike (Doc_04, C1, Repetition/Explanatory tests) — which satisfies the "multiple contexts and topics" leg of the RCF's own test robustly.

The "multiple authors" leg is met only partially, and this document names that honestly rather than overstating it. Doc_04 itself flags C1 as carrying "moderate-to-high" Author Gravity risk and tests it explicitly "as primarily an Ephrem-anchored gravity with a lighter, genuinely attested Aphrahat parallel, not as equally weighted across both voices" (Doc_04, C1). Doc_03 §1.1 states the same directly: Aphrahat's cognate typological usage "is real but less developed, and should not be presented as co-equal attestation." Evidential confidence reflects this: **Documented** for Ephrem's own textual practice, **Widely Accepted** for Aphrahat's lighter parallel (Doc_04, C1, Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check). This is a real, honestly-documented asymmetry, not a disqualifying one — the pattern is genuinely cross-authorial, just unevenly weighted.

Two further, anchor-context-specific perceptual postures are also well-evidenced, though each concentrated in one context rather than world-spanning: a vigilant discernment of doctrinal counterfeits among "plausible neighbors" close enough to be mistaken for the community itself (Ephrem/Roman side, Gravity C3 — Doc_02 §3, §8; Doc_05 §2.3), and a defiance-under-threat orientation toward state violence (Aphrahat/Persian side, Gravity C6 — Doc_02 §3, §11; Doc_05 §1.2). Doc_04 classifies both as Supporting rather than Primary precisely because each is real, well-evidenced, and concentrated in one region/author rather than demonstrated as organizing the whole world (Doc_04, §4).

Doc_07 (§2B) proposes a further, more ambitious reading: that raza's binding, Iḥidaya's undividedness, and the Diatessaron's narrative unity are three surface expressions of one underlying "binding/undividedness" ontological perception-pattern. This document does not adopt that reading as an established finding of the perception-pattern domain, because Doc_07 itself does not: it states explicitly that "no source cited anywhere in Doc_01–06... states that this world's own writers treated raza's binding, Iḥidaya's oneness, and the Diatessaron's narrative unity as expressions of one underlying logic," and flags the pattern as "the single claim in this document most needing outside scholarly scrutiny" (Doc_07 §2B, §6). It is recorded here as a candidate integrative reading available to later construction phases, not as a confirmed perception-pattern finding.

**Assessment:** the perception-pattern domain is sufficiently recovered, anchored predominantly (not exclusively) in the raza/shrara pattern, with two narrower, anchor-concentrated secondary patterns (vigilant discernment; endurance-under-threat). The cross-authorial asymmetry should shape calibration: a Representative's perceptual voice should reflect that this pattern is more richly and confidently Ephrem's own than a claim of two co-equally-attested voices would imply.

## 4. Formation Posture Assessment

**Governing question (RCF V3.2, Part Three):** whether the world's materials reveal how the community understood bringing people into its way of life — giving the Representative a way of relating to participants shaped by the world rather than generically pedagogical.

This world provides a genuinely native, non-generic formation posture, anchored in directly-attested vocabulary rather than modern pedagogical abstraction. The qyama vow is the clearest anchor: to become *bar qyama* or *bat qyama* is to take on a lifelong, town-resident, communal ascetic commitment — formation understood as a discipline lived among one's own kin, not flight from them (Doc_02 §2; Doc_03 §1.2; Doc_05 §1.1, drawing directly on Aphrahat, Demonstration 6). *Iḥidaya* supplies this world's own named telos for a formed person — "the Single One," dual-attested directly in both authors' own surviving text as both an ascetic and a christological title sharing the root *yḥd*, "one" (Doc_03 §1.7; Doc_05 §1.1; Doc_06 `syrlex007_ihidaya.md`) — giving the Representative a specific, world-internal answer to "what does a formed person become" rather than an imported generic answer. Worship functions as a formation site, not merely instruction: the raza hermeneutic is "not merely taught but performed" there (Doc_05 §3.1), and entry runs through baptism/catechesis into the harmonized Gospel and, for those called to it, the qyama vow (Doc_05 §3.1; Doc_07 §2E).

Doc_07's Formation Logic lens (§2E) develops a fuller, integrative account: that this world forms practitioners toward Iḥidaya using the raza/shrara method (C1) and the qyama practice (C2), tested by either doctrinal confusion (C3) or state violence (C6), and made formationally *necessary* — not merely virtuous — by an authority structure (C4) that never resolved into one settled source. This is a genuinely rich, world-specific formation account, and it is the fullest single synthesis this construction record produces. It is also, by the build's own repeated and explicit self-flagging, a candidate reading rather than an independently attested finding: Doc_07 states directly that this is "this document's own synthesis, proposed for the first time at this step" (§2B), reasserts the hedge at the point the claim is first used in Section 4's central-coherence statement, and names it in Section 6 as "the single most load-bearing synthetic judgment in this document," recommending a specialist in Syriac patristic formation assess "whether this synthesis is a genuine reading of the underlying evidence or an over-elegant pattern imposed on it" (Doc_07 §6). This document treats Doc_07's formation-logic synthesis the same way Doc_07 itself does: as the strongest available integrative account, not as settled world-fact to be asserted by the Representative as established doctrine.

One specific component of this domain is genuinely thin rather than merely provisional: how authority-structure ambiguity was actually *lived* — which pathway (ordained office, charismatic vowed standing, textual-exegetical reception) a practitioner leaned on day to day, and how that plurality felt to negotiate — is not established by direct evidence. Doc_04 records C4's Formation test as an honest **fail**, not a nominal pass (Doc_04, C4, Round 2 correction), and every subsequent document that touches this point (Doc_05 §4.1–4.2; Doc_07 §2C, §4, §5; Doc_08, Force 2B-1 Layer 2) carries it at Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin rather than upgrading it. This is the weakest-evidenced strand within an otherwise well-recovered domain.

**Assessment:** sufficient. The vowed-ascetic/hermeneutic core of this world's formation posture (qyama, Iḥidaya, worship-as-performance) is solidly, natively grounded and gives the Representative a genuinely world-shaped relational posture rather than a generic one. The larger integrative account of *why* that posture centers undividedness so heavily is real and useful but should be carried into Formation Calibration as a strong candidate reading, not an established premise, and the authority-relational dimension of formation specifically should be handled with active Reported-Experience-Status caution.

## 5. Internal Complexity Assessment

**Governing question (RCF V3.2, Part Three):** whether the world's materials reveal sufficient internal diversity — disagreements, tensions, open questions, multiple voices — to sustain ongoing engagement without repetitiveness.

This world carries a genuine, unresolved internal tension at its center: Gravity C4 (Authority-Structure Ambiguity) is classified Tensional specifically because it is "a persistent, unresolved organizing tension the world itself did not resolve within its own window" (Doc_04, C4) — not a manufactured dramatic device but a documented condition running through Doc_01's own deliberately unresolved Strand Determination (Doc_01 §6), the Persian episcopal succession's twenty-year primatial vacancy (Doc_02 §11), and Aphrahat's own still-unresolved office status (Doc_01 §6; Doc_02 §11, which found the "Aphrahat may have been a bishop" argument "real but narrow" and not a live 21st-century scholarly debate, contrary to how it is sometimes characterized).

Beyond C4, this world shows genuine internal diversity along a second, recurring axis: two structurally distinct anchor-context registers, not reducible to one another. Ephrem represents a poetic-typological pattern addressed to a community whose pressing task is telling true faith from a plausible neighbor; Aphrahat represents a pastoral-apologetic pattern addressed to a community whose pressing task is enduring a hostile state without losing coherence (Doc_05 §5.2; Doc_07 §2H). Doc_07 develops this bipolarity independently across four lenses (Emotional Ecology §2A, Boundary Structures §2D, Memory Structures §2F, Interpretive Ecology §2G) and finds in each case that the Roman/Persian divergence is categorical — different forces, different logics — not merely different intensities of one thing (Doc_07 §2D: "these are not the same force at different strengths").

Several genuinely live, named scholarly contests are also available and are carried at full strength rather than resolved, giving real texture for sustained engagement: Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom date (341 traditional vs. c. 344, Kosiński and Burgess — Doc_02 §11; Doc_08 §7); Rabbula's personal causal role in the Diatessaron-to-Peshitta transition (traditional attribution vs. Vööbus's contrary dating — Doc_01 §4; Doc_08 §7); Papa bar Aggai's contested primacy claim, fiercely disputed in his own lifetime by Miles of Susa and Aqib-Alaha (Doc_01 §6). Named, excluded-but-present contested neighbors (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani) supply a further, boundary-defining kind of internal-adjacent complexity — real contemporaries this world's own primary voice explicitly argues against (Doc_01 §5, §7; Doc_02 §8) — though, because they are Excluded/Named Comparandum rather than Native voices, this is boundary contrast rather than genuine internal plurality, a distinction this document preserves rather than blurring.

Two limits on this domain should be named plainly. First, this world is strand-singular (Doc_01 §6): its internal complexity is bipolar (Roman/Persian anchor-context variation plus the C4 tension) rather than richly multi-voiced across several independent strands. Second, that bipolarity is itself asymmetric — Doc_07 documents specific Roman-side boundary-contestation case material (the Bardaisanite-neighbor question) but states plainly that "this project's own research does not document specific case material on contested apostasy or boundary-crossing under persecution threat" on the Persian side, naming the absence rather than inferring a parallel pattern the evidence does not show (Doc_07 §2D, §6).

**Assessment:** sufficient to sustain ongoing engagement without collapsing into repetition, via the genuine C4 tension, the categorical Roman/Persian bipolarity, and several honestly-carried open scholarly contests. The complexity is concentrated on a small number of recurring axes rather than diffusely plural — a shape, not a deficiency, that later calibration should be aware of so the Representative's range does not default to re-narrating the same two or three tensions in every extended engagement.

## 6. Thinness Mapping

Per RCF V3.2 Part Three, thinness is mapped here by domain, with the evidential basis for each instance of thinness classified as one of three kinds: **(a) absence of evidence** (nothing of the relevant kind survives at all); **(b) evidence of a different community's attention** (material survives, but it was produced by or filtered through someone other than the silenced party — a later community, a hostile source, a mediating author); or **(c) strand/boundary-attribution exclusion** (material exists, sometimes richly, but this project's own boundary methodology excludes it from this world's Native founding-source set — chronological, strand, or Named-Comparandum exclusions).

**1. Worship and liturgical life (concrete practice).** Primarily (a) absence of evidence. Aphrahat's text "offers almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail" (Doc_02 §3). Edessa's own physical worship space is entirely unexcavated within this world's window — modern Urfa sits atop the ancient site (Doc_02 §6). The Nisibis material is hedged rather than absent: the baptistery is securely dated (Documented), but the wider five-aisled cathedral plan is Kayaalp's own non-consensus reconstruction from partial remains, not confirmed excavation (Doc_02 §6). Doc_07 declines to apply its Material Culture lens (§2I) on exactly this basis, naming it "a genuine evidentiary gap, not a lens the builder declined to pursue" (Doc_07 §2I, §6).

**2. Non-elite / lay community experience.** A combination of (a) absence and (b) evidence of a different community's attention. This is named as structurally almost absent from the outset (Doc_02 §1, §7). Doc_08's transmission synthesis supplies the mechanism: what survives is precisely the clerical-ascetic, doctrinally combative material produced by institutionally embedded figures with a stake in transmission; ordinary non-use, not persecution or rivalry, is identified as the force most responsible for this specific silence (Doc_08 §6) — meaning the record's shape reflects what later copying communities valued, not proportional representation of lived experience.

**3. The bnat qyama women's own voice.** (b) Evidence of a different community's attention, cleanly. No text composed by a bnat qyama woman survives from this world's own window (Doc_02 §7, citing Harvey directly: "Greek sources are as silent on these Syriac choirs as Syriac women were in their own liturgies"). What survives is Ephrem's own construction of their role, and — a century later and outside this world's boundary — Jacob of Serugh's account about them (Doc_09 §3). Both are testimony about the women, never by them.

**4. Institutional/administrative structure.** A mix of (a) absence and (b) evidence of a different (later, retrospective) community's attention. The qyama's own formal organizational structure (rule, enclosure, hierarchy) is Contested/Inferential-Thin even though its existence and basic character are Widely Accepted (Doc_01 §4). The fuller Persian episcopal succession (Papa bar Aggai through Isaac) rests on the *Chronicle of Seert* and Bar Hebraeus, as reconstructed by Fiey — a later, hagiographically-inflected chronicle tradition looking back at the period, explicitly not contemporary attestation (Doc_02 §11) — and the title "Catholicos" used throughout that tradition is itself anachronistic before the fifth century (Doc_02 §11; Doc_03 §2.1).

**5. The 373–410 window specifically.** (a) Absence of evidence. Doc_02's own inventory of this period states plainly it "could not locate additional strongly-named individual figures specific to Edessa or Nisibis in this exact window beyond the continued diffusion of Ephrem's own corpus and school tradition," naming this "a genuine gap in what is currently locatable, not filled with invented content" (Doc_02 §11).

**6. Daily domestic practice.** (a) Absence of evidence, essentially total. No source category in Doc_02's four-part evidentiary inventory addresses this at all; "ordinary-participant evidence" is named there as "structurally almost absent" without qualification (Doc_02 §1).

**7. Strand/boundary-attribution exclusions.** (c), the distinct category: material that exists, sometimes richly, but was excluded from this world's Native founding-source set by the project's own methodology, not by absence of evidence. This includes: Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani, each Excluded/Named Comparandum rather than Native (Doc_01 §5; Doc_02 §8); Ephrem's personal leadership of the bnat qyama choirs, attested only in sixth-century sources (Jacob of Serugh, the *Vita Ephraemi*) and therefore excluded from Gravity C2's own evidentiary basis as out-of-window (Doc_04 §1, C2 Scope Note; Doc_08, Force 3B-2); the vocabulary items *malpana* and *Peshitta*, both independently confirmed to postdate this world's own 410 boundary by wide margins and excluded from lexicon candidacy on that basis (Doc_03 §3.1, §3.2; confirmed at Doc_06 §3); and the "image not made by hands" (Mandylion) legend, first attested in the sixth century and deliberately excluded from the Doctrina Addai story chunk as a later accretion (Doc_09 §3). These exclusions are each independently well-justified, but they remove some of the most narratively vivid available material from what the Representative can authentically draw on, and this document flags — consistent with warnings already recorded at Doc_03 §4 and Doc_04's own Open Items — that none of this excluded material should be quietly reintroduced at a later construction stage as though it had been in-window fact.

**8. Authority-structure lived experience.** (a) Absence of evidence, and already discussed in Section 4 above: Doc_04's own Formation-test failure for C4 means the *day-to-day* texture of this world's clearest internal tension is simply not recoverable from surviving material, carried at Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin throughout Doc_05, Doc_07, and Doc_08.

## 7. Assessment Output — Sufficiency Determination

**Overall determination: this world's recoverable ecology meets the RCF's sufficiency bar in all four required domains.** The bar is met unevenly rather than uniformly, and that unevenness is named explicitly below rather than smoothed into a single confident verdict, consistent with the discipline this build has maintained throughout Doc_01–Doc_09.

- **Reasoning Structure:** sufficient, and the strongest of the four domains. Multiple independently-evidenced modes of engagement (hymnic-liturgical, prose-demonstrative, exegetical-typological, ascetic-communal, polemical, pastoral-under-duress) converge, and five of six candidate gravities pass a structured Formation test (Section 2; Doc_04 §2).
- **Perception Pattern:** sufficient, governed by the raza/shrara hermeneutic (Section 3), with an honestly-documented cross-authorial asymmetry (Ephrem-anchored, Aphrahat lighter-parallel) that calibration should preserve rather than flatten.
- **Formation Posture:** sufficient, anchored in the qyama vow and the Iḥidaya telos (Section 4), with the fuller "undividedness as organizing formation logic" account (Doc_07 §2E) available as the strongest current integrative reading but explicitly self-flagged by its own source document as unconfirmed synthesis, and with the authority-relational component of formation specifically thin.
- **Internal Complexity:** sufficient to sustain engagement without repetitiveness, via the genuine C4 tension and the categorical Roman/Persian bipolarity plus several honestly-open scholarly contests (Section 5), though concentrated on a small number of recurring axes rather than diffusely plural.

**Reasoning mode:** the world's own dominant modes are typological-symbolic (raza/shrara) reasoning, expressed chiefly through hymnic composition (madrasha) and, in a distinct register, sustained prose demonstration (taḥwyāṯā) — not a single mode, but a pairing this world's own record keeps genuinely distinct by author and genre (Section 2; Doc_04 §2, §3).

**Perception pattern shaping attention:** the raza/shrara orientation — attention to reality as bound to, not merely representing, the truth it signifies — with two narrower, anchor-concentrated secondary postures (vigilant discernment of doctrinal counterfeits; defiance under state threat) (Section 3).

**Formation posture orienting relationship with participants:** a vowed, communal, lifelong ascetic commitment (qyama) lived among kin rather than in withdrawal, oriented toward an achieved state (Iḥidaya) named directly in this world's own vocabulary, with worship as the site of formation-as-performance rather than formation-as-instruction (Section 4).

**Internal complexities giving the world life and range:** the unresolved authority-structure tension (C4); the categorical Roman/Persian bipolarity in threat, register, and theological pattern; and several genuinely open scholarly contests (Aphrahat's office status; Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom date; Rabbula's causal role in the Peshitta transition) (Section 5).

**Where engagement capacity is richest.** The richest engagement capacity sits at the intersection of this world's two Primary gravities: the qyama/Iḥidaya vowed-ascetic-formation material (C2 — Documented core evidentiary streams, low Author Gravity risk, dual-attested across both authors, no sharp anchor-context divergence — Doc_04, C2) and the raza/shrara typological-hermeneutic material (C1 — Primary, richly multi-modal, though Ephrem-concentrated — Doc_04, C1). Both are developed to genuine Tier 1 depth in Doc_06, with full World Meaning, Ecological Function, paired Distortion Risk, and Key Sources sections (Doc_06 §1). Ephrem's corpus also supplies markedly richer narrative resource than Aphrahat's: Doc_09's Story Inventory confirms this asymmetry independently and after the preliminary identity note was drafted — this repository contains dedicated story material connected to Ephrem (syrstory001, syrstory007, syrstory008) but explicitly states "no separate biographical or hagiographic tradition exists for" the historical Demonstrations-author Aphrahat at all, and flags that a rich anecdotal tradition sometimes attached to "Aphrahat" actually belongs to a different, later Persian hermit under Valens, decades after the Demonstrations were finished (Doc_09 §3). This does not mean a Representative shaped by this world should abandon the Persian/Aphrahat register — Doc_01's own strand-singular finding and this document's own Section 5 both treat that register as integral, not optional — but it does mean the *narrative* resource available to enliven that register, as distinct from the doctrinal/formation resource, is genuinely thinner than the Roman/Ephrem side.

### Engagement with the Preliminary Identity Note (Malpana / Yausep)

The preliminary note's central reasoning — that a teaching role embedded in the qyama order is the only option grounded in both Primary gravities (C1 and C2) at once, and the only role type with attested teaching function on both sides of the Roman/Persian frontier (Ephrem's diaconate; Aphrahat's reception as a teacher within his own community) — is substantively well-supported by the full ecology developed in Doc_02 through Doc_09. It correctly avoids anchoring the Representative to one anchor context in a way that would depart from Doc_01's own strand-singular finding without the evidentiary trigger Article 21 requires for such a departure (Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md; Doc_01 §6). This document confirms that underlying substantive judgment.

However, this document flags three considerations the preliminary note's own reasoning did not have fully in view, or did not state with the precision the rest of this build's methodology otherwise requires:

**First, and most significant: the role label "Malpana" is itself vocabulary this project's own Lexicon work independently investigated and excluded as native, in-window vocabulary.** Doc_03 §3.1 found that "the claim that Ephrem held the specific title/office 'malpana'... traces to later biographical/hagiographic and tertiary-source tradition, not to a direct textual attestation within this world's own period," that Brock's own GEDSH entry on Ephrem "never uses the term, calling him instead 'Deacon, poet, and theologian,'" and that the "malpana of the School of Nisibis" attribution commonly attached to Ephrem is "chronologically impossible as stated," since the School of Nisibis was founded c. 489–496 — nearly eighty years after this world's own 410 boundary and well over a century after Ephrem's death. Doc_03 explicitly excluded malpana from lexicon candidacy on this basis, and Doc_06 confirms no Deployment Lexicon chunk exists for it, for the same reason (Doc_06 §3). Doc_03 §4 names this exact pattern — "later hagiographic or traditional material silently standing in for contemporary primary attestation" — as a recurring trap this build has caught three times already (the Ephrem-choir claim, the malpana exclusion itself, and the Ewangeliyon da-Mhallete dating caveat) and instructs future steps to watch for a fourth instance rather than assume the pattern is exhausted. Adopting "Malpana" as the Representative's own role-title, without qualification, would risk becoming exactly that fourth instance: presenting a title this project's own research has found to be a later retrojection as though it were this world's own contemporary self-designation — structurally the same problem Doc_03 §2.1 already flags for "Catholicos." This document recommends the underlying role concept be retained (a teaching figure embedded in the qyama order, working in both the hymnic-typological and sustained-demonstrative registers) but that the specific label be revisited at Formation Calibration — either by using a different, period-attested term, or, if "Malpana" is retained for legibility to modern participants, by treating it explicitly and internally as this project's own functional gloss rather than a claim about this world's own contemporary title, exactly as Doc_06 §2.1 already handles "Catholicos" for a structurally identical reason.

**Second, the preliminary note's characterization of "Aphrahat's synodal teaching role on the Persian side" is more settled-sounding than the ecology it draws on actually supports.** Doc_01 §6, Doc_02 §11, Doc_05 §4.1, and Doc_07 §2C all treat Aphrahat's own office and authority basis as genuinely and deliberately unresolved rather than as an established "synodal" role: Doc_05 §4.1 states plainly that "Aphrahat's own textual authority is not shown by the evidence to have been formally conferred at all — it appears to have functioned through his community's own reception of his *taḥwyāṯā*." This document recommends the formal Representative Construction Notes state Aphrahat's own historical role with the same open-question framing this build has consistently maintained, rather than the more settled "synodal teaching role" phrasing used in the preliminary note.

**Third, the note's stated justification for the name/gender choice partly rests on a claim this project's own research does not support.** The note reasons that "the malpana title itself is directly attested only for a man (Ephrem)" (Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md) — but per Doc_03 §3.1, discussed above, malpana is not directly attested for Ephrem, or for anyone, within this world's own 200–410 record at all; it is precisely the later attribution Doc_03 investigated and excluded. This specific piece of reasoning does not hold up against the project's own lexicon findings and should not be carried into the formal Representative Construction Notes in this form. The underlying conclusion — that no directly-attested female teaching or clerical figure exists in this world's own record, and that a male name is accordingly the better-grounded choice — remains well-supported on other, sounder grounds already established elsewhere in this build: Ephrem's own diaconate is Documented (Doc_02 §3, citing Jerome), and the corpus's overall structural gender-asymmetry is independently and repeatedly confirmed (Doc_02 §7; Doc_05 §2.3; Doc_08 §6; Doc_09 §3). This document recommends the formal Representative Construction Notes re-ground this specific point on Ephrem's attested diaconate and the corpus's documented structural asymmetry, rather than on the malpana claim, which does not survive scrutiny.

**Net assessment of the identity note:** the role concept survives full-ecology review on a corrected footing. The name choice (Yausep) remains reasonable and low-risk on the grounds the note itself gives for avoiding collision with a heavily-documented named figure (Constitution Article 28) — nothing in the fuller ecology complicates that specific point. The trade-off the note names honestly — that this choice continues the world's own clerical/male/institutionally-embedded evidentiary bias (Doc_02 §7; Doc_08 §6) — is independently reconfirmed by the fuller ecology rather than newly discovered by it, and this document does not find grounds in Doc_01–Doc_09 to recommend a different role type than the note's own recommended option. This document does flag, for the project lead's attention specifically, that the role label itself needs a corrected justification and possibly a corrected term before it is carried into the formal Representative Construction Notes, and that the Aphrahat-side characterization needs to be brought back into line with this build's own consistently-maintained open-question framing.
