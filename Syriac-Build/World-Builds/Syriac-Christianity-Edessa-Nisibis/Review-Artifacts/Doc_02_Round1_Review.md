## (c) Fact-check findings on new claims

This is where the document has real problems. Using independent web research:

**Ephrem works inventory:** Mostly sound, but contains a pattern of small fabricated/garbled precision layered onto otherwise-real sources: Mathews & Amar's *Commentary on Genesis and Exodus* translation is dated **1994**, not 2004 (2004 appears to be a reprint date presented as original); *Hymns on Paradise*'s actual CSCO volume is a combined *Paradise and Against Julian* edition, a detail the document omits; the Syriac-edition date for *Commentary on the Diatessaron* (1963) could not be corroborated. The Carmina Nisibena CSCO numbers, which Doc_02 itself flags as needing verification, actually check out fine — the self-flagged item turned out correct, while unflagged items contained the real errors.

**Aphrahat manuscripts/transmission:** Same pattern. Demonstration 23's date is stated as "winter 344/5" but sources give **August 345** — an invented seasonal specificity. Valavanolickal's translation is misdated "Gorgias 2005" when the Gorgias edition is **2011** (2005 was the original Kottayam printing). The claim that "Aphrahat" as a name appears "from the ninth century onward" is likely off by a century — sources point to the **tenth century** (Bar Bahlul, Elias of Nisibis). The "folio loss" causal explanation for Demonstrations 11–12 being missing is unsupported invention. The Georgian translation claim is misleadingly incomplete (it's Armenian-derived, covers only Dem. 6, and was itself misattributed — to Hippolytus, a detail Doc_02 omits entirely).

**Persian episcopal succession / 20-year vacancy:** The name sequence and the vacancy itself are real features of the traditional Church-of-the-East chronicle tradition (Chronicle of Seert, Bar Hebraeus, Fiey's reconstruction) — not fabricated. But Doc_02 states these dates with more confidence than the underlying sources warrant: every specific year (341, 343, 346, 363, 371, 377, 399, 410) is chronicle-derived and hagiographically inflected, not contemporary attestation, and current scholarship (Kosiński, Burgess) actively disputes Simeon bar Sabbae's traditional 341 date in favor of ~344 — which would shift the entire downstream chain by 2–3 years. Doc_02 never surfaces this live redating dispute. It also uses "Catholicos/Catholicosate" throughout, a title anachronistic before the 5th century per the same tradition it's drawing on — an internal-anachronism problem of exactly the kind Doc_02 elsewhere (Section 3, Beck/Epiphany example) is careful to flag in other contexts but fails to flag here.

**Diatessaron Dura fragment dispute:** The core claim is real — Joosten defends the identification; Parker, Taylor (David G.K.), Goodacre, and Mills reject it. But "Monier" is not fabricated — Mina Monier is real, co-author with **Joan E. Taylor** (a different Taylor than David G.K. Taylor) of the mediating JTS 2021 position. Doc_02's sentence ("Mills, Parker, Taylor, and Goodacre reject it; Monier and Taylor split the difference") uses "Taylor" twice for two different scholars without disambiguation — a real citation error that could mislead a downstream reader into thinking one person holds contradictory positions.

**Odes of Solomon dating:** Charlesworth and Butts citations check out. But the Lattke citation is wrong: the real article is "Dating the Odes of Solomon," ***Antichthon 27 (1993)***, not *Antichthon 49 (2015)* — a fabricated/garbled citation (wrong volume, wrong year by 22 years) attached to an otherwise-real scholar and thesis.

**Nisibis Cathedral (Kayaalp):** The article is real and correctly attributed (co-authored with Nihat Erdoğan). But Doc_02's characterization overstates certainty: Kayaalp's own abstract frames this as "an attempt to **reconstruct the possible cathedral**" — the five-aisled plan is inferred from partial remains (part of one wall, some piers), not a confirmed excavated structure. Only the baptistery is securely dated and standing. Doc_02's phrase "genuine, dated, in-window five-aisled cathedral" collapses a reconstruction/hypothesis into a fact, precisely the kind of unearned certainty this document elsewhere criticizes other sources for.

**Ramelli universalist/pro-Origen flag:** This one holds up well. Ramelli, the Apokatastasis book, and critical reviews of it (e.g., the "Origenes Vindicatus vel Rufinus Redivivus?" review) are all real, and the review's actual criticism (failure to distinguish Origen's views from later Origenist reception, selective harmonizing) substantiates Doc_02's characterization. The extrapolation from the Apokatastasis reviews to flag the separate Bardaisan monograph is an explicit, honestly-hedged inference, not a hidden overreach — Doc_02 says outright it couldn't find a dedicated review of the 2009 book and is inferring by analogy. This is a legitimate, well-executed caution, not a fabrication.
