## (c) Assessment of the central "undividedness" synthesis

This is a genuine, self-aware synthetic hypothesis, not a fabrication, and the document deserves credit for naming it explicitly as its own construction (§2B: "a single ontological pattern... that Doc_05 and Doc_06 treated as distinct facts"; §6: "not a claim any individual prior document states in these terms... a specialist... should be best positioned to assess whether this synthesis is a genuine reading... or an over-elegant pattern imposed on it"). Checking Doc_01–06 directly confirms: **no prior document anywhere connects raza/shrara's "binding," Iḥidaya's "undividedness," and the Diatessaron's narrative unity as one pattern.** Doc_05 documents all three as separate ecological facts in separate sections (1.1, 3.1, 5.1) with no cross-reference between them. Doc_07 is genuinely the first place this connection is drawn.

Substantively, the throughline is more than pure wordplay but less solid than it's made to sound. There is a real linguistic resonance — Iḥidaya's root is literally *yḥd*, "one"; raza is fundamentally about a symbol *not* being separate/arbitrary relative to what it signifies (a kind of non-division between sign and reality); the Diatessaron is literally a harmonization of four into one continuous narrative. These are not etymologically related roots, and Doc_07 never claims they are — it claims a shared *ontological pattern*, which is a different and more defensible kind of claim. But no source cited anywhere in Doc_01–06 (not Brock, not Murray, not Griffith, not Possekel) states that ancient Syriac writers themselves treated raza-binding, Iḥidaya-oneness, and Gospel-harmony as expressions of one underlying logic. This is a modern, retrospective pattern-match built on shared English-translation vocabulary ("bound," "undivided," "single," "one") across three distinct Syriac terms — exactly the risk the task's prompt names. The document's self-critique in Section 6 is honest and proportionate to the actual risk. The problem is that the confidence with which 2B, 2E, §4, and §5 *assert* the pattern (as "this world's actual philosophical center of gravity, prior to and beneath any one of the three facts individually," §2B) outruns the hedge that only shows up in Section 6. A reader who stops at Section 5 would come away thinking this is an established finding; only Section 6 clarifies it's the document's own unverified synthesis. **Recommendation: pull an explicit hedge (e.g., "this document's own synthetic reading, not independently attested") into 2B's own text where the pattern is first proposed, rather than leaving all the hedging to Section 6.**
