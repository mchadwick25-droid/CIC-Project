### 2.2 Role/Title: Mar (confirmed)

The Representative's role-title is **Mar**. This is a further change, made by the project lead in this revision round. The project lead had earlier resolved the Ecology Assessment's Malpana contradiction (Section 7, Point 2) by selecting "Deacon"; with the anchor context now confirmed Persian (Section 3 below), the project lead has replaced Deacon with Mar, because Deacon is Ephrem's own attested title, specific to the Roman/Edessene side, and does not fit a Representative whose lived, first-person formation is now anchored in Persia. Two further options were before the project lead and declined in favor of Mar: a fully generic label carrying no title at all, and retaining "Deacon" as a named analogy despite the anchor mismatch. This document records the Mar decision and grounds it in the actual construction record, including a documented complication that grounding surfaces honestly rather than smooths over.

**Grounding — Mar as a broadly attested Syriac honorific.** Doc_03 (Section 1.8) already treats "Mar" (ܡܪܝ, "my lord") as a candidate term in its own right: "Honorific title-prefix... for bishops, saints, and revered teachers, parallel to 'Saint.'" Doc_03's own sourcing for this is Sebastian Brock's *An Introduction to Syriac Studies*: "Mor (or Mar) is an honorific title used both for bishops and for saints" — a general statement about the term's use across Syriac Christianity, not tied to either anchor context specifically. This is precisely the feature that makes Mar fit a Persian anchor where "Deacon" did not: Mar is not a Roman/Edessene-side-specific attestation the way Deacon (Ephrem's own particular clerical office) was; it is a shared Syriac-wide convention, genuinely at home on either side of this world's own Roman/Persian bifurcation (Doc_01, Section 3; Doc_08, Force 1A-2). Mar's own breadth — covering bishops, saints, *and* revered teachers alike — also means it does not force a resolution of Aphrahat's own unsettled episcopal status (Doc_02, Section 11; Doc_04, C4) the way a more specific office-title would; it sits comfortably with C4's own Tensional, unresolved authority-structure finding rather than quietly deciding it.

Doc_03 (Section 1.8) also rates Mar its own Author Gravity risk and preliminary tier, and this document names that rating plainly rather than obscuring it: "**Preliminary tier estimate: Tier 3 (minor/supporting).** Real and low-controversy as a linguistic fact, but not itself world-organizing; useful mainly as a cross-reference note on other entries (e.g., noting Aphrahat's own attested honorific was 'Mar Jacob,' not 'Mar Aphrahat')" — a parenthetical worth quoting in full here since it is itself a naming cross-reference, not a confusion-risk flag, and does not pre-empt the fuller complication this document surfaces below. The construction record's own engagement with Mar as a candidate term was light — a minor-supporting entry, not one Doc_03 developed at length — so grounding a full Representative title decision in it draws on a thinner construction-record foundation than Deacon's own two-independent-citation grounding (Doc_02, Section 3; Doc_04, Section 1, Jerome) was. This is not a defect in the decision itself (Mar's own linguistic reality and broad attestation are, per Doc_03, "low-controversy"), but it is honestly a lighter evidentiary base, and this document says so rather than presenting Mar's grounding as equally developed as Deacon's had been.

**Grounding — the Aphrahat/"Mar Jacob" attestation, and a documented complication.** Doc_02 (Section 2) states plainly: "a 510 CE colophon calls him 'Mar Jacob, the Persian sage'" — the earliest datable attestation of any honorific-plus-name combination for Aphrahat at all, since "the name 'Aphrahat' appears only from the tenth century onward (Bar Bahlul, Elias of Nisibis)." Doc_03 (Section 1.8) repeats this citation and treats it, at face value, as Aphrahat's own attested honorific: "Doc_02 (Section 2) already confirms Aphrahat himself was called 'Mar Jacob, the Persian sage' in a 510 CE colophon." Both documents cite this colophon, at the point where each first states it, as though it were simply Aphrahat's own name-form, without further qualification.

This document's own check of the fuller construction record, performed specifically for this revision, finds a complication neither Doc_02 nor Doc_03 connects back to that colophon citation, even though both name it elsewhere in their own text. Doc_02 (Section 3, Aphrahat's transmission history) separately records: "a separate, fuller Armenian translation (misattributed to 'Jacob of Nisibis,' ed. Antonelli 1756, only 19 homilies) is separate from this single-Demonstration Georgian strand" — meaning that in one genuine strand of this world's own transmission history, Aphrahat's own Demonstrations were positively misattributed to **Jacob of Nisibis**, a distinct, independently attested fourth-century figure (a bishop of Nisibis, present at the Persian sieges, whose own death date — 338 or 350 — is a separately documented, unresolved primary-source conflict per Doc_02, Section 11, and not otherwise connected to Aphrahat anywhere in this construction record). Doc_08 (Force 2B-2) cites and carries this same fact forward, with attribution to Doc_02, in its own transmission-forces treatment: "a separate, fuller Armenian translation of nineteen homilies misattributed to 'Jacob of Nisibis' (Doc_02, Section 3)" — this is Doc_02's own finding repeated downstream with citation, not a separately-reached corroboration.

**This document states plainly what the prior record leaves unaddressed:** confusion between Aphrahat and Jacob of Nisibis is not a hypothetical risk this document is inventing — it is a documented, attested fact of this world's own transmission history, stated at Doc_02 (Section 3) and carried forward with citation at Doc_08 (Force 2B-2, which cites Doc_02 Section 3 directly rather than reaching the finding independently) — one fact, stated once at origin and repeated downstream with attribution, not two separately-arrived-at corroborations. What neither Doc_02 nor Doc_03 does, however, is connect that documented confusion back to the 510 CE colophon's own "Mar Jacob" designation for Aphrahat. Both documents record the colophon at face value, as an apparently independent, secure attestation of Aphrahat's own honorific-plus-name, while treating the Armenian-translation misattribution as a separate, later transmission-history fact about authorship confusion. This document does not know, and the prior record does not establish, whether the colophon's own "Jacob" reflects a genuine, independent second name or byname for Aphrahat himself — later separately entangled with Jacob of Nisibis only in the Armenian strand — or whether the colophon itself is already a product of the same underlying Aphrahat/Jacob-of-Nisibis conflation, in which case "Mar Jacob" would not be a clean, independent attestation of Aphrahat's own name at all. Resolving this would require primary-source work beyond this document's own scope. This document does not perform that check and does not resolve the question; it names the gap in the prior record honestly, since it bears directly on how solid "Mar Jacob" specifically is as a grounding data point for a title decision.

**How this shapes the actual title choice.** Precisely because of this unresolved complication, this document's own title grounding does not lean on the full "Mar Jacob" construction, and the Representative is not titled "Mar Jacob." It leans instead on Mar's own broad, generic attestation across Syriac Christianity (Doc_03, Section 1.8, citing Brock), independent of the specific "Jacob" name-component and its confusion risk — the safer, less specific title choice this revision was directed to check for. The Aphrahat/"Mar Jacob" colophon remains worth naming as confirmation that this world's own Persian-side record does attest a real person carrying this specific honorific (whatever the "Jacob" component's own tangled history), but it is cited here as corroboration of Mar's general currency, not as the load-bearing citation for the title itself. The Representative is titled **Mar Yausep** — Mar as this world's own broadly attested honorific convention (Section 2.1's already-confirmed name), not by importing "Jacob" or the specific "Mar Jacob" construction. This also avoids compounding a risk Section 2.1 and the Preliminary Decision already flag: attaching a specific, historically contested name-component to an invented Representative would risk exactly the "claiming a real, specific, otherwise-undocumented person's biography" problem Yausep's own selection was designed to avoid.

**This resolves the title question by the same avoidance discipline this document previously applied to Malpana, not by adjudicating the Jacob/Jacob-of-Nisibis question.** Whether the 510 CE colophon's "Mar Jacob" is a secure, independent attestation of Aphrahat's own byname, or is itself entangled with the Jacob-of-Nisibis confusion, remains open and unresolved by this document. It is not decided here, not decided by implication, and should be tracked as a standing item (Section 6 below) for a future Doc_02/Doc_03 cross-reference check, structurally similar to how the Malpana/Doc_02–Doc_03 gap is already tracked. Choosing "Mar" — not "Mar Jacob" — for the Representative's title removes the need to resolve that question for this purpose; it does not settle the question itself.

**The Malpana question itself is unaffected by this round's changes and remains exactly as before.** Unlike "Malpana," neither "Deacon" (the prior title) nor "Mar" (the confirmed title) carries the specific internal Doc_02/Doc_03 contradiction over whether "Malpana" is genuine 200–410 vocabulary. The Ecology Assessment's own Section 7 left three paths open for Formation Calibration on that question: (a) resolve the Doc_02/Doc_03 contradiction directly; (b) route around "Malpana" entirely using an uncontested alternative; or (c) separately flag the Doc_02/Doc_03 inconsistency to the project lead for a cross-reference correction, independent of whichever path (a) or (b) takes. **Conclusion on this test:** per Part Four's own instruction to say so when the breadth preference is not the deciding factor, this document records that it is not — Mar was chosen for the anchor-fit and project-portfolio reasons recorded in Section 1, not a breadth calculation. **What this gains.**

- **Title coherence.** "Deacon" is directly, cleanly attested *only* for Ephrem, on the Roman/Edessene side (Doc_02, Section 3; Doc_04, Section 1, Jerome citation; Doc_05, Section 1.1). - **Continues, without mitigating, the named representativity trade-off.** The Ecology Assessment (Section 7, Point 4) already confirms this cost is real regardless of anchor choice (a male, clerically-adjacent role continues this world's structural bias toward clerical, male, institutionally-embedded voices, per Doc_02 Section 7 and Doc_08 Section 6) — this is not a distinguishing cost of Option A specifically, but it is not reduced by choosing it either.
