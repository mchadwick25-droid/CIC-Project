## Section 1 — World Identity

**World name (as the world would name itself):** No single native self-designation for this community as a body distinct from "the church" generally is attested anywhere in the construction record (Doc_01–Doc_02). This world's own vocabulary names its practices and formation categories — qyama, raza/shrara, Iḥidaya — rather than supplying a proper institutional name for itself. This absence is preserved honestly rather than filled with an invented self-designation. This project's own working label, established at Doc_01 and used throughout Doc_01–Doc_09, is **Syriac Christianity (Edessa/Nisibis)**.

**World code:** syr (assigned at Step 6, per Doc_06, Section 0; back-annotated into Doc_01's header per that document's own housekeeping note)

**Temporal scope:** c. 200–410 CE. The beginning point is the turn of the 2nd/3rd century: a church building is attested at Edessa by 201 CE (recorded destroyed in that year's flood, per the *Chronicle of Edessa*), with Bardaisan's own career from the 170s–180s onward as the earliest individually attested activity in this world's own religiously plural milieu — Bardaisan himself is treated as boundary-adjacent, not this world's own founding native voice (Doc_01, Section 2, Section 5). The ending point is 410 CE, the Synod of Seleucia-Ctesiphon, chosen as the point of genuine institutional-structural change rather than a biographical marker (Doc_01, Section 2). Ephrem's death (373 CE) and the 363 CE cession of Nisibis to Persia are both significant internal hinges within this span, not the boundary itself: the 363 cession is treated as an internal transition — same tradition, same personnel, new political jurisdiction — because the formation logic continues unbroken across it (Doc_01, Section 2, Section 8).

**Geographic scope:** The Syriac-speaking Christian ecology across the Roman-Persian Mesopotamian frontier zone — not one city or one polity. Its anchor points are Edessa (Urhoy), capital of the former client kingdom of Osroene and Roman-administered for nearly this world's entire span, and Nisibis, a frontier fortress-capital, Roman from 298–363 CE and Persian thereafter; its scope also extends to Aphrahat's own Persian/Adiabene context under Sasanian rule from 224 CE — a distinct polity from Roman Osroene, joined to it in this world's own construction on linguistic-cultural rather than political grounds (Doc_01, Section 3). No single political label covers the whole: "Roman-administered periphery" holds for Edessa throughout, for Nisibis only 298–363, and not at all for Aphrahat's Persian context (Doc_01, Section 3, Section 9).

**Community character:** This world's own surviving voice does not supply a single confessional self-description of the kind a modern reader might expect. What its record does show, read across Doc_01–Doc_08, is a hymnic, symbolic, and exegetical Christian formation carried in Aramaic, organized institutionally around a covenant order of vowed ascetics (qyama) rather than around monarchical episcopal office as the primary formation structure; a community that read Scripture as raza bound to the shrara it signifies rather than as arbitrary illustration; gathered around a single harmonized Gospel narrative rather than four discrete witnesses; and understanding its own formation — per this world's own vocabulary, synthesized at Doc_07, Section 5 — as training toward Iḥidaya, undivided singleness of perception and practice, asked of practitioners in part because no single settled authority in this world ever secured that coherence institutionally on their behalf (Doc_01, Section 1; Doc_07, Section 2E, Section 5). This last characterization is Doc_07's own synthetic reading of the fuller ecology, carried at Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence, not an independently attested self-description (Doc_07, Section 2B, Section 6) — see Section 3 below.

## Section 3 — Formation Logic

*Drawn directly from Doc_07, Section 2E, cross-checked against Doc_05 and Doc_06. A hedge stated once here rather than repeated at every clause: the claim that this world's formation logic organizes around undividedness as a single named aim is Doc_07's own synthetic reading of Doc_01–06 together — proposed for the first time at that step, resting on real linguistic resonance across raza's binding, Iḥidaya's oneness, and the Diatessaron's narrative unity, but not independently attested by any prior document in these terms (Doc_07, Section 2B, Section 6). It is carried forward here at that same confidence (Dominant Modern Reconstruction), not upgraded. Its individual components — raza's binding, the qyama vow, the harmonized Gospel, and the authority structure's genuine non-resolution — are each independently Documented or Widely Accepted facts in their own right.*

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

In this world's own vocabulary, a fully formed practitioner is **Iḥidaya** — the Single One, undivided in allegiance, single-minded, participating in Christ's own undividedness from the Father. This is not "a holy person" in a generic sense; it is a named, specific achieved state this world's own vocabulary supplies as its own telos, directly attested in both Aphrahat's Demonstrations (especially 6:8, 7:20) and Ephrem's hymn corpus, within this world's own 200–410 window (Doc_03, Section 1.7; Doc_06, `syrlex007_ihidaya.md`; Doc_07, Section 2E).

### The Formation Mechanism

Transformation happens through the combination of a hermeneutic and a vowed communal practice — neither alone is shown by this world's evidence to constitute formation. The hermeneutic is **raza/shrara**: to read Scripture, or creation itself, by raza is to hold that reality is already woven through with real connections a properly formed eye can perceive, not connections invented by the interpreter; misreading a raza is not an intellectual slip but a severing from the truth it carries. This hermeneutic is trained and performed chiefly through sung **madrasha** — a genre in which the argument is carried through melody and refrain into the body, not merely stated in prose (Doc_06, `syrlex001`, `syrlex004`). The vowed practice is the **qyama**: entry by lifelong promise, lived among one's own kin in town rather than in desert withdrawal, a discipline in the middle of ordinary life rather than flight from it. Worship is the site where the hermeneutic is performed, not merely taught (Doc_05, Sections 1.1, 3.1, 5.1; Doc_07, Section 2E).

### The Formation Arc

**Entry** is through baptism and catechesis into the harmonized Gospel (the Diatessaron, this world's single unbroken narrative of "the Gospel") and, for those called to it, the qyama vow — a dateable, communal threshold distinct from ordinary lay membership (Doc_06, `syrlex002`, `syrlex006`). **The middle of formation** is confrontation with one of two named external tests, depending on anchor context: for the Roman/Edessene practitioner, learning to detect a counterfeit raza among plausible-sounding doctrinal neighbors (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani); for the Persian/Adiabene practitioner, enduring visible, embodied state violence without losing coherence. **Maturity** is named directly by this world's own vocabulary as Iḥidaya-hood: undivided perception (reading raza rightly), undivided practice (the qyama vow kept), and undivided endurance (standing firm under whichever test was faced) (Doc_07, Section 2A, 2E).

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

Not applicable in the plural sense — Doc_01, Section 6, found this world strand-singular, with the authority-structure question left open rather than resolved into two strands. What the Roman and Persian anchor contexts share as formation logic beneath their differing tests is real and load-bearing: the same raza method, the same qyama/Iḥidaya vocabulary, and the same harmonized Gospel are attested in both Aphrahat's and Ephrem's own surviving text (Doc_04, C1/C2/C5 all confirmed as not sharply regionally divergent). The Roman/Persian variation documented throughout this world's ecology (differing emotional registers, differing boundary logics, differing named tests) is anchor-context variation within one formation logic, not evidence of two distinct strands (Doc_07, Section 2E).

### Formation Logic and the Confirmed Gravities

**C1 (raza/shrara)** supplies the perceptual half of undividedness — training a practitioner to read truly. **C2 (qyama/Iḥidaya)** supplies the practiced, vowed half — training a practitioner to live undividedly, in a body that remains among its own kin. **C4 (authority-structure ambiguity)** is not a gap alongside this formation logic but, per Doc_07's own synthesis, a structural reason it centers undividedness as heavily as it does: where this world's own institutions did not fully secure coherence for a practitioner, the practitioner's own achieved undividedness had to. This specific claim — that the plurality of authority sources actually placed this weight on personal undividedness, as opposed to merely coexisting with it — carries Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin: the structural plurality is Documented, but how it was actually negotiated or felt is not directly attested (Doc_05, Section 4.2; Doc_07, Section 2C, Section 4). **C3 and C6** are the two different external tests of the same underlying undividedness, differing by anchor context rather than by formation aim. **C5 (the harmonized Gospel)** anchors the whole pattern in a single unbroken narrative that itself models undividedness at the level of the very text a practitioner receives first (Doc_07, Section 2E).

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary

### 4A — Emotional Texture

This world's two anchor contexts do not share one dominant emotional register; they run in parallel toward the same named destination. On the Persian side, the register is fear held steady by defiance — Aphrahat's later Demonstrations address a community that has already watched its bishops die and does not know how many more it will watch die. On the Roman side, the register is the anxiety of the crowded room — not the fear of the executioner but the urgency of telling true faith apart from a plausible-sounding neighbor. For Ephrem's audience, the named danger is being deceived by a counterfeit raza; for Aphrahat's audience, the named danger — structurally clear but never narrated from the inside in any surviving text — is losing coherence under sustained state pressure (Doc_05, Section 1.2; Doc_07, Section 2A).

Both registers are named, in this world's own vocabulary, as states a practitioner moves through toward the same achieved endpoint: Iḥidaya. Entry into either qyama vow-taking or catechesis under the harmonized Gospel begins the arc; the middle is confrontation with one of the two named dangers depending on anchor context; maturity is becoming Iḥidaya — undivided — on either road. This is not two unrelated emotional facts about two communities but two structurally different tests of one destination (Doc_07, Section 2A).

### 4B — Philosophical Ecology

This world holds a participatory rather than representational ontology of symbol and reality: a raza is not a stand-in chosen arbitrarily to represent something else but is bound to the shrara it signifies and carries something of that reality's own hidden power. Reality itself is understood as already woven through with real connections a properly formed eye can perceive, not connections invented by the interpreter (Doc_06, `syrlex001`; Doc_07, Section 2B).

The human person's fundamental problem, in this world's own vocabulary, is dividedness; the fundamental possibility is undividedness. Iḥidaya carries this as one word rather than two ideas — an ascetic designation for single-minded, undivided allegiance to God, and simultaneously the christological title for Christ as Only-Begotten, sharing the root yḥd, "one." This world's normative Gospel text was, correspondingly, not four discrete books held in tension but a single continuous harmonized narrative (Doc_06, `syrlex006`, `syrlex007`; Doc_07, Section 2B). **A hedge carried forward, not softened:** that these three facts (raza's binding, Iḥidaya's oneness, the Diatessaron's narrative unity) express one underlying ontological logic of binding and undividedness is Doc_07's own proposed synthetic reading — real linguistic resonance, not an attested historical-theological connection this world's own participants are shown to have drawn themselves (Doc_07, Section 2B).

### 4C — Authority Structures

This world holds at least three, non-collapsing sources of authoritative standing, none of which cleanly subordinates the others within its own 200–410 window. Ordained episcopal office was real but structurally fragile — the Persian succession stood vacant for two decades after Barba'shmin's martyrdom, and Papa bar Aggai's own claim to primacy was fiercely contested by Miles of Susa and Aqib-Alaha of Karka d'Baith Slok. Demonstrated vowed asceticism — qyama and Iḥidaya standing — functioned as a charismatic authority pathway running alongside, not merely beneath, ordained office. Textual-exegetical authority is visible in Aphrahat's own standing as a writer of taḥwyāṯā, evidently received by his community despite his own place within any formal office structure remaining genuinely unresolved by this project's own research (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_04, C2×C4 Interaction "Reshaping"; Doc_07, Section 2C).

How authority was transmitted differs by which of the three sources is in view, and none of the three mechanisms is fully documented: episcopal succession ran through naming and contested claims to primacy, with a demonstrated capacity to fail outright for two decades; qyama/Iḥidaya standing was entered through vow rather than appointment; Aphrahat's own textual authority does not resolve into a named conferral mechanism at all — it appears to have functioned through his community's own reception of his taḥwyāṯā (Doc_05, Section 4.1; Doc_07, Section 2C). A practitioner forming in this world received formation from more than one authority pathway at once, without a single settled hierarchy resolving which held final say — carried at Reported-Experience Status for what this plurality actually felt like to negotiate (Doc_07, Section 2C).

### 4D — Boundary Structures

Entry into this world's core community ran through baptism and catechesis into the harmonized Gospel generally, with qyama vow-taking marking a further, dateable threshold into this world's specific ascetic order (Doc_06, `syrlex002`, `syrlex006`). This world's two anchor contexts related to what lay beyond their own edges in two different registers, not merely at two different intensities of the same pressure. On the Roman/Edessene side, the outside was doctrinal: Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani, rivals close enough to be mistaken for the community itself, whose own circles and missionaries moved through the same streets in living memory. Whether a Bardaisanite whose own circle still walked Edessa's streets counted as fully outside was a live, unsettled question a textbook heresy category obscures (Doc_05, Section 2.3; Doc_07, Section 2D). On the Persian side, the outside was political-coercive — the Sasanian state under Shapur II — and, in a separate register, Jewish dialogue partners engaged through Aphrahat's own anti-Jewish Demonstrations, appearing only refracted through his polemic and never in their own voice; this project's own research does not document comparable Persian-side case material on contested apostasy or boundary-crossing, an absence named rather than filled by inference (Doc_05, Sections 2.3, 5.1; Doc_07, Section 2D).

Doctrinal rivalry (an intellectual-competitive force) produces the Roman-side boundary logic; Shapur II's persecution (an external coercive force) produces the Persian-side boundary logic. These are two different kinds of pressure producing two different boundary logics differing in kind, not the same force at different strengths (Doc_07, Section 2D).

**Tension:** Authority-Structure Ambiguity — Whose Standing to Teach and Lead Is Real

**Nature:** To lean on ordained episcopal office in this world is to lean on something structurally fragile: the Persian succession could stand vacant for two decades after a bishop's martyrdom, and even a bishop's own claim to primacy over his peers could be fiercely and publicly contested in his own lifetime.
