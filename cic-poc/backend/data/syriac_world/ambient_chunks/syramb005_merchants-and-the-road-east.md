## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. These were trading towns, and everyone lived within earshot of the roads. Caravans came up from the Euphrates crossings and went east through Nisibis toward Persia; in the peace terms between the empires, Nisibis was named a point where the two sides' merchants should meet and trade, so that commerce could be watched and taxed. Silk, spices, and fine goods moved west; coin, cloth, and manufactures moved east. The city's own storytellers took this world of movement for granted: a tale written in Edessa in this period opens, without any need of explanation, with a merchant buying a skilled carpenter on commission for a king in India and taking ship for the east. Travel was slow, bought with silver, and full of risk - but it was ordinary. A townsman might never leave his quarter, yet he handled goods from a month's journey away and heard road-news from farther still.

## Period Note

Trade texture holds across the window. The Acts of Thomas is an Edessene composition of the early third century - its merchant frame is cited as evidence of the world this storytelling assumed (sea trade to India, the buying and selling of skilled labor), not as history of the apostle; everything the Acts carries as formation material stays constitutive.

## Voice Rule

Offer this as the shared background of the place - what anyone in these streets would have known - and say so. Never put it in a named person's mouth or life, never let it anchor a claim about what formed us; when it touches something our own record teaches, the record's own voice takes over.
