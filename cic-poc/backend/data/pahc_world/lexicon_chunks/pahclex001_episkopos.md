## Quick Meaning

In this world, *episkopos* names the one who oversees the community's table and guards its unity — but whether every household has such a single figure, or whether presbyters share this trust together, is itself the question we are still living through.

---

## World Meaning

We know both patterns from real contact. In some households like ours, one episkopos stands at the center, and obedience to him is felt as the very shape of our unity — fitted together, as Ignatius of Antioch put it, the way a harp's strings are tuned to each other. Do nothing apart from the bishop, he writes to us, and we have heard this read aloud often enough that it has shaped how many of us think about order. In other households, a council of presbyters governs together, with no sense that anything is missing for want of a single presiding figure. The letter from Rome to Corinth speaks of presbyters removed from their office, not of a bishop deposed — and no one reading that letter feels they are being told of a church less complete than one with a bishop at its head.

What presses on both patterns alike, without settling which is right, is a felt and unpredictable possibility: a name and an accusation could be brought against any household, on any ordinary day. Under that pressure, the argument for a single visible figure — someone who can speak for all of us when speaking is demanded — carries real weight. But so does the argument for a council whose continuity does not depend on any one life.

## Ecological Function

This term anchors the Authority Consolidation question (G01) — the question of who leads and how leadership is secured now that those who walked with the Lord are gone from among us. A participant who understands episkopos also understands why this world's communities look different from one another on this very point, and why that difference has not broken communion between them.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern reader hears "bishop" and assumes an already-secured office with defined powers, territorial jurisdiction, and a place in a hierarchical structure.

**World Hearing:**
In this world, episkopos names a function some communities have concentrated in one person and others have not. The very question of whether every community should have such a figure is still being argued, not settled. To read back a later hierarchy flattens a live argument into an accomplished fact.

**Living Tradition Note:**
This entry describes this world's own formation ecology (c.70–200 CE). It makes no claim about how any present-day tradition currently defines, practices, or understands the office of bishop.

## Key Sources

Ignatius of Antioch, Letters to the Ephesians, Magnesians, Trallians, Philadelphians, Smyrnaeans, and to Polycarp (the seven authentic letters, Strand A). 1 Clement (Rome to Corinth, Strand B — presbyteral governance, no singular bishop). Polycarp's own self-designation as "one of the presbyters" (Letter to the Philippians).
