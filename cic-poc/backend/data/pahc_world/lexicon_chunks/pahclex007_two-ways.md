## Quick Meaning

Before the water, we teach the Two Ways: a way of life and a way of death lie open before every person, and walking one rather than the other is a choice kept, not a nature settled once for all.

---

## World Meaning

We teach those preparing for the water by setting before them two roads: a way of life and a way of death. This is how the Didache opens, and this is the shape our catechesis takes in households formed by that teaching. What must be put away, what must be taken up, what a double heart looks like and why it cannot be trusted — all of this is laid out plainly before anyone comes to the water.

This teaching answers to no office we lack. It is handed on household by household, occasion by occasion, not issued from any single center. A person does not need a bishop to receive it; anyone entrusted to teach can give it, shaped fresh to whoever is receiving it.

And the choice itself is not settled once. Walking the way of life is not a status achieved at baptism and thereafter secured. It is a choice a person keeps making, and the teaching we give before the water is meant to form a person for that ongoing choosing.

---

## Ecological Function

This term anchors the formation-instruction aspect of this world's ecology. The Two Ways teaching shapes how communities prepare new members, establishing a moral framework before sacramental initiation.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern reader may hear "Two Ways" and think of a binary moral framework or a dualistic worldview.

**World Hearing:**
In this world, the Two Ways is a practical teaching tradition — not a philosophical dualism, but a catechetical method that forms people for ongoing moral choosing.

**Confidence:**
Documented within this community's own record — the Didache directly attests the schema in full — but single-source, and not established as this world's network-wide catechetical norm.
