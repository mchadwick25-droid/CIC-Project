## Quick Meaning

For this world sin is not first a list of wrong acts needing forgiveness — it is *hamartia*, the soul's mis-orientation, the whole self turned away from God who is its created telos and pointed instead toward lesser goods that cannot fill the capacity it was made with; particular wrong acts express this condition rather than define it.

---

## World Meaning

The Greek word is *hamartia*, and it means missing the mark. Not breaking a law, not running up a debt, not violating a contract — missing the mark: failing to arrive at what we were aimed at. The picture is from archery, and we take it exactly. An arrow that misses is not guilty of anything. It has simply not landed where it was sent.

Take that picture seriously and the whole account follows. The soul is an arrow, and the image of God in us is what makes it an arrow pointed toward God — made for, capable of, aimed at real relationship with and participation in the divine life. Sin is the condition of that arrow no longer flying toward its mark: turned away from God, pointed at lesser goods, no longer moving the direction it was created to move. The image is not gone — the arrow is still an arrow — but its aim has been bent.

So sin is first a condition, not an act. The particular things we do wrong — lying, cruelty, greed, contempt, pride — are what a mis-aimed soul produces; they express where it is pointed. They are not nothing: they deepen the condition, habituate us further in the wrong direction, and make the turning-back harder. But to correct wrong acts one at a time without addressing the direction underneath is like nudging an arrow in flight while the bow still points the wrong way. The correction will not hold.

A soul turned from God is not turned toward nothing. It still has the capacity for God, and that capacity keeps desiring — only now it desires the wrong things: not the good that would satisfy it but goods that are real and lesser — pleasure, power, security, reputation, the approval of others who are themselves bent. These are genuine goods. They simply cannot fill what we were made to want, and the soul that chases them as though they were ultimate never arrives at what it is most deeply after.

This is the condition our whole formation answers — not mainly by cataloguing wrong acts, though that has its place, but by turning desire itself back toward its mark: toward God, toward the good we were made for, toward the participation the image was created to make possible. That is why fasting, prayer, belonging, and the encounter with Scripture are practices of re-orienting desire, not merely of regulating behavior.

And the turning-back is possible only because the damage is directional, not total. The image is wounded, not erased. A soul whose image had been destroyed could not be formed toward God — it would have to be made again from nothing. But the capacity sin misdirected is the same capacity grace can re-aim, and the freedom that turned away is the same freedom that, under formation, can turn home.

## Ecological Function

Sin sets the problem the entire salvation arc answers; without a precise account of it, none of what follows - death, resurrection, restoration, transformation - reads correctly. It anchors the entry on death (death is what the soul undergoes once it has turned from the source of its life), it gives the whole formation ecology its purpose (every practice is at some level the re-orientation of desire), it makes repentance intelligible (a directional condition answered by a directional turn, metanoia), and it ties directly to freedom (the soul turned away because it was free to, which is why formation works with the soul's freedom rather than over it). This is salvation seen from its problem-side.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
The forensic frame — sharpened after the Reformation and now migrated even into secular moral talk: sin is wrongdoing, breaking a rule, incurring guilt or debt before a divine judge who requires payment. God's answer is judicial — the debt forgiven, the guilt absolved, the account settled, the sinner acquitted. Healing and transformation, if present at all, come second to legal standing.

**World Hearing:**
The primary human problem is not legal guilt but directional mis-orientation — not a soul under sentence but a soul turned from God and so from its own deepest life. God's answer is not a verdict but a re-direction: the soul that turned from its source of life being turned back, healed, formed, and drawn into the participation it was always made for. The forensic frame is not wrong about everything; it mislocates the primary problem. A soul forgiven but not re-oriented is still aimed away from God — the record is clean, the aim unchanged.

## Key Sources

Genesis 3 (the paradigmatic turning away, read here as mis-orientation rather than first a legal violation) and Romans 3:23 ("all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" — sin as failing to reach the *doxa* the soul was made for). Clement of Alexandria, *Paedagogus* I and *Stromateis* II, IV (sin as the soul's turning from wisdom toward lesser goods; formation as the re-orientation of desire). Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–6 (the turn from contemplation of God; the dimming image; the soul drifting toward non-being). Origen, *On First Principles* III.1 (the soul's genuine self-determination as what makes sin possible).

Note: Origen ties sin to his speculative pre-cosmic fall — a primordial turning that preceded embodied life — and that speculative stratum carries the **Fall/Descent** contest (see the Fall/Descent entry, `alexlex074`, and Nous, `alexlex011`): whether it is Origen's own position or a later systematization, and how it relates to the 553 condemnations, is genuinely disputed, so the ecology-wide pre-existence claim is only **Dominant Modern Reconstruction**. This entry stays with what is **Widely Accepted** and independently attested in Clement and Athanasius: sin as the soul's directional mis-orientation, the image damaged but not destroyed, particular acts as expressions of the condition.
