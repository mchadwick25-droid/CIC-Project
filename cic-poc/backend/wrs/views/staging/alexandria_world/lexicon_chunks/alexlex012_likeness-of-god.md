## Quick Meaning

For this world the Likeness of God is what the Image of God becomes through formation — not a separate gift but the image progressively restored and fulfilled, visible in the quality of a person's perception, love, and life as formation shapes them toward the character of the God in whose image they were made.

---

## World Meaning

There are two words in the Genesis text, and they are not synonyms. "Let us make humankind in our image (*eikon*), according to our likeness (*homoiosis*)." The image is given. The likeness is the image's goal. The image is what every one of us is simply by being human — the indelible mark that makes us capable of God. The likeness is what we are being formed toward — the slow conforming of the whole person to the character and life of God that a lifetime of formation works.

This distinction carries weight. Confuse image and likeness and you collapse the given into the goal (as if formation manufactured our capacity for God rather than restoring it) or the goal into the given (as if the likeness were already ours and needed only to be noticed). Held apart, the two open the whole shape of our life: the image is already here, damaged but present, which is why formation is possible; the likeness is where the image is going, which is why formation has a direction.

The likeness is not a quality the soul holds statically. It shows in the *mode* of a person's life — in how they perceive, love, speak, suffer, serve. When we picture the one in whom the likeness has grown far, we picture someone who knows at depth and loves at depth, whose knowing and loving are one thing, whose clarity of sight and generosity of life are recognizably the character of the God they are being formed toward. This is not moral performance. It is what genuine formation makes of a whole person. And the stages name how it grows: catechesis opens the door; illumination deepens perception, and deeper perception of divine reality is a deepening of the image of the Logos, which is what the likeness is; gnosis increases real contact with God, and to be in contact with God is to become more like him; wisdom is the likeness once gnosis has had time to permeate a life; participation is the likeness enacted, the soul genuinely sharing the divine life and so growing like the life it shares; and theosis is the likeness completed, the horizon where image and likeness finally coincide.

So the likeness is not a goal standing apart from the practices. It is what they produce, stage by stage, what each deepens, what the whole sequence builds toward. And its possibility is grounded in the Incarnation: the Logos who is himself the Image of the Father entered our nature, so that our nature is now, from within, being restored toward the image and drawn toward the likeness by the very one whose likeness it was always meant to bear — genuinely like God, in the only way a creature can be: expressing, in creaturely form, the character, love, wisdom, and life of the one whose image we carry.

---

## Ecological Function

The Likeness of God is the formation goal of the whole anthropological stack — what image-bearers move toward through the sequence, and what that movement arrives at. It gives the formation sequence its direction (every stage is a stage of growing likeness, oriented toward the character of the God whose image the soul bears); it is what wisdom makes visible (the wise person is, in some recognizable way, like the God they know); it completes the image/likeness distinction that keeps the ecology from either presumption (the likeness already finished) or despair (the image too damaged to recover); and it connects to Theosis as its eschatological completion — what formation grows here, partial but real, is the same likeness theosis names as full, differing in fullness rather than in kind. Understand the likeness and one understands why this world's formation has both a ground and a goal.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Becoming like God" as moral imitation — trying to act as God would act, modeling one's conduct on the divine example. On this reading the likeness is a behavioral achievement imitated from outside, and the goal is conformity of conduct.

**World Hearing:**
The moral qualities the modern hearing notices — the formed person loves more freely, sees more clearly, serves more generously — are real, and we do not deny them. But they are the fruit of an ontological change, not the change itself. The likeness is not imitation laid over a self that stays the same; it is what the self looks like once genuine formation has altered what the self *is*. Two people may perform the same generous act — one who has decided to, and one whose desire has been genuinely reordered over years of formation. The modern hearing sees the acts rightly; it mislocates them as the goal rather than the fruit.

## Key Sources

Genesis 1:26 (the foundational text — "in our image, according to our likeness," the two words this entry holds distinct). Clement of Alexandria, *Stromateis* II.19, 22 and VII.1–3 (the likeness as what formation produces in the *gnostikos*: visible conformity to God's character through wisdom and love). Origen, *On First Principles* III.6 (the image given in creation, the likeness as the goal of the soul's journey; the gradual restoration of the image toward the likeness). Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–8 (the Logos entering human nature to restore the image and draw it toward the likeness — the ground of the likeness's possibility). 2 Corinthians 3:18 ("transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another").

Note: the image/likeness distinction as load-bearing, the likeness as the formation goal, and the likeness as grown from within (through genuine contact with divine life) rather than imitated — all Widely Accepted for the school tradition. Origen's speculative account of the soul's final restoration to likeness connects to the *apokatastasis* material: Dominant Modern Reconstruction, adjacent to the contested Apokatastasis and Fall/Descent entries, and carried there rather than in this chunk. "Likeness" is a technical term whose precise weight is calibrated within the school tradition; the broadly-shared core above is what this chunk asserts.
