## Quick Meaning

For this world the Image of God is the indelible mark in every human being that makes formation possible — not a quality earned or lost but the very ground of the soul's capacity for God, damaged by sin yet never destroyed, which the whole formation ecology exists to restore and fulfill.

---

## World Meaning

Everything we say about the human person — the soul's structure, the nous's reach, the freedom to turn, the possibility of being transformed — rests on one conviction: we are made in the image of God. This is not a graceful way of saying that we are impressive among the animals, or that we carry rights other creatures lack. It names something more exact and more astonishing. We are constituted by a real likeness to God, given in our making, and that likeness is what lets a creature genuinely receive and share in the life of its Creator.

The image is specifically the image of the Logos. We are not made in the image of God-in-general, as though God were an undifferentiated divine stuff; we are made in the image of the eternal Word, the one through whom all things were made and who is himself the Image of the Father. So our capacity for reason, for love, for wisdom, for the direct perception of divine reality — these are not extras added onto what we are. They are what the image of the Logos looks like in a creature. To be human is to be shaped toward the very reality the Logos is.

We read the Genesis words with care: "Let us make humankind in our image (eikon), according to our likeness (homoiosis)." Two words, and they are not the same word. The image is what we are by creation — the mark that cannot be scrubbed out. The likeness is what we are becoming through formation. The image is given; the likeness is that same image brought to its fullness. Hold the two apart and the whole shape of our life makes sense; collapse them and it does not.

When we turned away, the image was wounded, darkened, obscured — but not erased. This is the hinge of everything. A soul whose image had been wholly destroyed could not be formed toward God; it would have to be made again from nothing. But the image is damaged, not absent. The freedom with which the soul turned away is the same freedom with which, under grace, it can turn back; the reason that chased lesser goods is the same reason that can be illumined toward the highest. Formation is not the manufacture of something new. It is the restoring and fulfilling of what has been there, and hurt, all along. This is why we can speak of being brought back to our archetype — the image clarified until it reflects what it was always made to reflect — and why we can say the Logos entered our nature to begin, from inside, the healing our nature was made for but could not accomplish alone.

## Ecological Function

The Image of God is the foundational anthropological claim the whole cluster presupposes: without it the anthropology has no ground. It is what makes formation intelligible (the image is damaged but present, so it can be restored rather than created from scratch); it is why the soul is the subject of formation (the soul is the image-bearer); it is why the nous is the soul's highest faculty (the nous is where the image of the Logos is most legible, most wounded, and most directly restored); and it is what the likeness is the fulfillment of. It also carries the anthropological dimension of the Logos-Centered Unity (Doc_04): the ecology coheres around the Logos because human nature is itself Logos-shaped — every genuine human capacity for truth, love, and communion is a reflection of the Word. Grasp the image and one grasps why this world treats the human being as made-for-God rather than merely dignified.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Image of God" as a moral and political claim — human beings have intrinsic dignity and rights because they bear God's image. Not false, but it locates the image in moral *status*: what we are owed, how we should be treated.

**World Hearing:**
The image is first of all the ontological ground of the soul's capacity *for* God — the reason formation, illumination, gnosis, and real participation in divine life are possible rather than fictional. It says what we are in our deepest structure: constituted by a likeness to the Logos that makes us capable of genuine communion with God. The dignity is real, but it follows from this; it is not the point.

## Key Sources

Genesis 1:26–27 (the foundational text — "in our image, according to our likeness"). Clement of Alexandria, *Stromateis* II.19, 22 and V.14 (the image as the soul's constitution in the likeness of the Logos; formation as the image progressively restored). Origen, *On First Principles* I.1 and *Commentary on Genesis* (fragments) (the image as the ground of the soul's rational capacity for God). Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–5 (the image given in creation and restored from within by the incarnate Logos). 2 Corinthians 3:18 ("transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another").

Note: the core — the image as the ontological ground of formation, given in creation and damaged but not destroyed — is Widely Accepted, independently attested in Clement and Athanasius. Origen's speculative account of the image's pre-cosmic condition (the soul's fall from original contemplation, its eventual full restoration) is his distinctive systematization: Dominant Modern Reconstruction for ecology-wide claims, and connected to the contested Fall/Descent and Apokatastasis entries. This chunk stays with the broadly-attested core; the contested stratum is carried by those entries, not here.
