## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. There were no monasteries in these towns in this world's window. A household was the ordinary container of every kind of life: married couples and their children, unmarried kin, the old, servants where a family had means - and, woven among them, the men and women under vow. A vowed daughter might keep her father's house her whole life; vowed men lived in the town, worked, and were seen daily in its streets and congregation. The arrangements were close enough to ordinary life that the community's own teachers had to warn against households where vowed men and vowed women shared a roof - a warning that is itself evidence of how embedded in the household world the vowed life was. Women under vow served the congregation and sang; their place was public and audible, inside the town's ordinary week. Family, workshop, congregation, and vow all happened within a few streets of each other, under the same roofs.

## Period Note

Holds across the window. The qyama as institution - its vow, meaning, and formation weight - is Native-Constitutive (syrlex002 and its sources); this record carries only the MATERIAL shape the sources attest: vowed members living inside ordinary town households among married kin, not in separated communities, through this world's whole window.
