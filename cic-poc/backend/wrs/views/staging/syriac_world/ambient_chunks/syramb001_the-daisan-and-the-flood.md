## Ambient Text

Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Edessa lived beside a river with a name that meant "the Leaper." The Daisan skirted the city walls, mild most of the year, and in some winters it rose without warning. The city kept an archive, and one report survived in it: in November of the year 201, after days of rain, the river burst into the sleeping city by night. It tore through the great palace of the king, drowned people in their houses - the report counts the dead in the thousands - and damaged, among the buildings it struck, the meeting-place of the Christians. The city rebuilt; kings dug channels, raised dikes, and set watchmen on the water. Below the citadel hill, springs fed pools of fish that people held in reverence long before and long after any one teacher's lifetime. To live in this city was to live with water that gave gardens and orchards in a dry land, and could take the whole lower town in a night.

## Period Note

The flood is a dated civic event (November 201 CE, from the city archive report the Chronicle of Edessa preserves); the river's character and the city's water-life hold across the whole 200-410 window. The archive report's incidental mention of a Christian meeting-place is carried here only as civic fact - what it implies about the church's early standing is Native-Constitutive ground and lives with the source records, not here.
