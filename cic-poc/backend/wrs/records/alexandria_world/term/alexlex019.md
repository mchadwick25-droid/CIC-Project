quick_meaning: 'For this world resurrection is neither a corpse reanimated nor a soul surviving death
  — it is the genuine reversal of what death names: the soul''s reunion with God through the Logos who
  entered death from within, and the body''s transformation as a real participant in that salvation, because
  the Logos who sanctified bodily nature by entering it will not abandon it.'
world_meaning: 'Resurrection has to be heard against death. Death here has two modes, spiritual first:
  the soul''s separation from God, the dimming image, the *nous* closed to divine perception, with physical
  dissolution following as the outward seal. Resurrection answers both, in the same order — spiritual
  death primarily, physical death consequently — and it answers them by the same logic through which they
  came.


  The soul is separated from God because it turned from its source of life. The reversal is not a power
  imposed from outside — not a judge overturning a sentence, not a penalty absorbed by a substitute. The
  reversal is the Life itself entering what was dying and restoring it from within. Humanity was drifting
  toward non-being, toward the dissolution that follows from turning away from Being. The Logos, who is
  Being, through whom everything holds its existence, entered human nature — the flesh, the suffering,
  the mortality, the dying itself — and from inside that entry began to turn it back. What was dying from
  within begins to live from within, because Life has come inside it.


  That is resurrection''s first meaning: the soul''s return to its source through the Logos who has entered
  our nature and healed from within the connection sin severed. And it is not only future. The soul that
  participates in the Logos now — through catechesis, illumination, gnosis, the community''s practices,
  the recurring participation of the Eucharist — is a soul in which the resurrection is already at work.
  It is a present reality the formation ecology is deepening, not only a hope held for later.


  The body shares in this — and here we have to be plain about what is at stake. Every account that makes
  salvation the soul''s escape from the body agrees on one thing: the body is the problem, the prison,
  the weight to be shed. We hold the exact opposite. The Logos who made us — not souls accidentally lodged
  in bodies but embodied souls, the body the soul''s created partner — entered bodily human nature fully,
  and in entering it sanctified it. The body''s part in formation — the fasting, the vigil, the kneeling,
  the bread and cup received in hand and mouth — is not a concession to people who need physical props.
  It is the body doing what it was made to do: sharing in the formation of the whole person toward God.


  And because the Logos entered the body and sanctified it, he will not abandon it at the end. The resurrection
  of the body is not resuscitation — not the same organism switched back on in its present condition.
  It is transformation: the body, like the soul, genuinely changed by its participation in the Logos''s
  life, raised into what formation has been preparing it for. The body enters theosis as the soul does
  — the whole person restored and transformed in the completion of everything the formation sequence has
  been building toward.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  Resurrection is the reversal of death''s specific condition — spiritual separation and physical dissolution
  — inaugurated in Christ and operative now in the soul growing in participation. The soul being formed
  toward God is the soul in which resurrection life is already at work; the formation ecology is how the
  reversal takes hold in the living person. And the body is not left out: because the Logos entered and
  sanctified bodily nature, the body shares the reversal — not as resuscitation but as genuine transformation
  toward what the whole person is being prepared to become. The forensic frame thins it by making resurrection
  mainly the proof that a judicial penalty was paid; here it is the genuine reversal of death itself,
  the Logos entering what was dying and restoring it from within.'
