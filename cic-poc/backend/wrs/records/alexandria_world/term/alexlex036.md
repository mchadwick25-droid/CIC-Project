quick_meaning: 'For this world salvation is not primarily acquittal from legal guilt — it is the healing,
  restoration, and reorientation of the soul toward God: the Logos entering what was dying to restore
  it from within, so that real participation in the divine life becomes possible rather than merely longed
  for.'
world_meaning: 'The word "salvation" carries a legal freight this world does not share. In that frame
  sin is a crime, God is the judge, the problem is guilt, and the goal is acquittal. This world runs the
  account at a different level. Sin here is a *directional* condition, and the primary problem is not
  a verdict but a damage: the soul turned away from God, cut off from the life the Logos sustained, and
  unable on its own to turn back. If that is the problem, then the answer cannot be a not-guilty finding;
  the answer must match the wound. What is needed is restoration and reorientation — the Logos entering
  the damaged thing and healing it from the inside.


  This is exactly what this world sees in the incarnation. The Logos became human not to satisfy a legal
  demand but to enter what was dying and reverse it from within — to take on the very nature that was
  perishing and, from inside it, turn it back toward life. Consider the soul that has been acquitted but
  not reoriented: its record is clean, but its desires are still aimed where they were, still turned toward
  the lesser goods. The forensic account has solved the wrong problem. Salvation, as this world means
  it, is precisely the changing of what the soul is turned toward.


  So salvation is not a single moment but the whole arc — from repentance, through the formation ecology,
  through baptism and the Eucharist, toward the participation in God that theosis names. The relational
  and forensic dimensions are not denied; they simply are not the axis. What salvation primarily *is*,
  here, is the genuine reorientation and healing of the soul.'
