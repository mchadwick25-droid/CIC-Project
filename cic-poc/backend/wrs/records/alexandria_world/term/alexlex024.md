quick_meaning: 'For this world the Word of God is not first a name for the Bible — it is the name for
  the eternal divine speech through whom God creates, reveals, teaches, and restores; Scripture is where
  that Word most directly addresses the soul, not what the Word is, and confusing the two severs what

world_meaning: 'In the beginning was the Word.


  John''s Gospel does not open by saying "In the beginning was the Scripture." It opens with the Word
  — the divine speech, the eternal utterance of God, the one through whom "all things were made" and without
  whom "not one thing came into being." And the same prologue identifies this Word with Christ: "the Word
  became flesh and lived among us." The Word is not a property of a text. The Word is a person: the one
  we confess as genuinely God, the one we confess as the Anointed, the one the Logos entry names as the
  always-present integrating center of everything we do.


  So when we speak of the Word of God, we name this person first — not the text through which the person
  speaks, but the person who speaks. The distinction is not pedantic. It is the difference between a voice
  and the medium the voice travels through.


  Consider what is at stake in holding them together rightly. The soul that meets Scripture is not meeting
  a text that happens to contain true statements about God. It is meeting the Word — the one through whom
  all things were made, in whose image the soul was made, who now addresses that soul through the specific
  text we have gathered to read. Scripture does not speak; the Word speaks through Scripture. Prayer does
  not reach God on its own; the Word carries the soul''s address to the Father. Formation does not merely
  produce a better person; the Word forms the person toward the likeness of the one whose image they bear.


  This is the speech-character that "Word of God" names, and it is what marks the title off from our other
  names for the same one. Christ names the Anointed, who enacts the priestly, kingly, and prophetic offices.
  Son of God names the ontological reality, the one who is genuinely God rather than the highest creature.
  Word of God names the speech-character: the one whose very identity is constituted by speaking, revealing,
  addressing, teaching, calling, drawing. The Logos entry establishes this one as the always-present integrating
  center; the Word of God title names that same reality in the register of speech and address.


  That speech-character reaches back to creation and forward to Scripture at once. The Word is the one
  through whom God spoke creation into being. When Genesis says "God said, ''Let there be light,''" the
  Word is the speaking. Everything that exists has the Word''s address as its origin — which is why every
  genuine meeting with truth in creation, in honest reasoning, in natural knowledge, is already a meeting
  with the one whose speech made that inquiry possible and whose truth is what the inquiry finds.


  And Scripture is where this same Word addresses the soul most directly among us. Not the only place
  the Word speaks — the Word speaks through creation, through the community''s life, through the practices,
  through the suffering that divine pedagogy uses as instruction — but the primary instrument, because
  through the text the Word speaks with a specificity and directness the other channels do not carry.
  Scripture is the living address of the Word now, not merely the record of the Word having once spoken.
  The Word of God title names *who* it is that speaks through the text: the eternal one, through whom
  creation exists, speaking to this soul in this encounter through these words.


  The consequence is immediate. If the Word speaks through Scripture, then reading Scripture is meeting
  a person, not a text — and meeting the person through whom all things were made and in whose image the
  soul was made is a meeting that cannot leave the soul unchanged. This is why Scripture forms rather
  than merely informs. The Word who speaks through the text is the Word who made the soul and is even
  now forming it toward its end. The encounter is not a neutral transfer of information; it is the Word
  addressing what the Word made, and the soul that attends to that address is attended to by the one who
  knows it most completely.'

modern_hearing: '**Modern Hearing:**

  "The Word of God" now means, almost exclusively, "the Bible." The phrase is so fully claimed as a synonym
  for Scripture — "preach the Word," "the Word says," "take the Word seriously" — that hearing it and
  thinking "Bible" is not an error by ordinary usage. What is lost is threefold. The cosmic character:
  if "Word of God" means the Bible, then "in the beginning was the Word" becomes a puzzle — was the Bible
  present at creation? The personal character: a text does not address the soul; the Word who speaks through
  the text does. The formation ground: an authoritative document cannot ground formation power; who speaks
  through it can.'
