term: Sin / Hamartia
aliases:

quick_meaning: For this world sin is not first a list of wrong acts needing forgiveness — it is *hamartia*,
  the soul's mis-orientation, the whole self turned away from God who is its created telos and pointed
  instead toward lesser goods that cannot fill the capacity it was made with; particular wrong acts express
  this condition rather than define it.
world_meaning: 'The Greek word is *hamartia*, and it means missing the mark. Not breaking a law, not running
  up a debt, not violating a contract — missing the mark: failing to arrive at what we were aimed at.
  The picture is from archery, and we take it exactly. An arrow that misses is not guilty of anything.
  It has simply not landed where it was sent.


  Take that picture seriously and the whole account follows. The soul is an arrow, and the image of God
  in us is what makes it an arrow pointed toward God — made for, capable of, aimed at real relationship
  with and participation in the divine life. Sin is the condition of that arrow no longer flying toward
  its mark: turned away from God, pointed at lesser goods, no longer moving the direction it was created
  to move. The image is not gone — the arrow is still an arrow — but its aim has been bent.


  So sin is first a condition, not an act. The particular things we do wrong — lying, cruelty, greed,
  contempt, pride — are what a mis-aimed soul produces; they express where it is pointed. They are not
  nothing: they deepen the condition, habituate us further in the wrong direction, and make the turning-back
  harder. But to correct wrong acts one at a time without addressing the direction underneath is like
  nudging an arrow in flight while the bow still points the wrong way. The correction will not hold.


  A soul turned from God is not turned toward nothing. It still has the capacity for God, and that capacity
  keeps desiring — only now it desires the wrong things: not the good that would satisfy it but goods
  that are real and lesser — pleasure, power, security, reputation, the approval of others who are themselves
  bent. These are genuine goods. They simply cannot fill what we were made to want, and the soul that
  chases them as though they were ultimate never arrives at what it is most deeply after.


  This is the condition our whole formation answers — not mainly by cataloguing wrong acts, though that
  has its place, but by turning desire itself back toward its mark: toward God, toward the good we were
  made for, toward the participation the image was created to make possible. That is why fasting, prayer,
  belonging, and the encounter with Scripture are practices of re-orienting desire, not merely of regulating
  behavior.


  And the turning-back is possible only because the damage is directional, not total. The image is wounded,
  not erased. A soul whose image had been destroyed could not be formed toward God — it would have to
  be made again from nothing. But the capacity sin misdirected is the same capacity grace can re-aim,
  and the freedom that turned away is the same freedom that, under formation, can turn home.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  The primary human problem is not legal guilt but directional mis-orientation — not a soul under sentence
  but a soul turned from God and so from its own deepest life. God''s answer is not a verdict but a re-direction:
  the soul that turned from its source of life being turned back, healed, formed, and drawn into the participation
  it was always made for. The forensic frame is not wrong about everything; it mislocates the primary
  problem. A soul forgiven but not re-oriented is still aimed away from God — the record is clean, the
  aim unchanged.'
