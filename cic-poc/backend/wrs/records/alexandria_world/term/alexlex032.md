quick_meaning: 'For this world repentance is not feeling sorry — it is *metanoia*, a change of *nous*:
  the soul''s highest faculty actually turned from what it was pointed at back toward God; this turning
  is the foundational response that makes formation possible, and the whole formation sequence is the
  progressive deepening of what the turning begins.'
world_meaning: 'The word does the work if we hear it exactly. *Meta-noia*: a change of *nous*. Not a change
  of feeling, not a change of behavior — a change of *nous*, the soul''s deepest faculty of perception,
  the capacity by which we are oriented toward or away from God. *Metanoia* is that highest faculty being
  re-aimed: the *nous* that was pointed away from God beginning to point toward him.


  Sin, we have said, is first a directional condition — the soul turned away, the image damaged, desire
  misaimed at lesser goods that cannot satisfy it. Repentance is the turn back. Not the admission that
  the turn was wrong, not the sorrow the wrong turn produced: the actual, genuine, directional change
  — the soul beginning to turn toward the one it was made to face.


  This is why *metanoia* is the foundational response that makes formation possible. A soul that has not
  turned is still pointed away, and formation cannot form it toward God because it is not yet aimed the
  direction formation works toward. A soul that has turned — even initially, even partially — is a soul
  in which formation can now occur, because its orientation is at last aligned with what formation is
  deepening. The catechumen who has genuinely turned is in formation, however early. The person who moves
  through all the forms without genuinely turning is not, in the sense that matters.


  *Metanoia* is not a single event — that is the second thing the word requires. The soul turns, and that
  turn is real and identifiable, the thing the formation sequence begins from. But the turn deepens. Every
  stage is a deepening of what the turn began: catechesis forms the re-oriented desire more specifically
  toward God; illumination opens the perception the turn made possible, the *nous* beginning to see what
  it was always made to see; gnosis deepens that encounter; wisdom deepens what gnosis produces; participation
  deepens what wisdom enables. The soul is always at once the one who has turned and the one still turning
  — always deepening the change of direction that *metanoia* is.


  The turn is only ever the soul''s own. The soul turned from God by its own genuine freedom, not under
  compulsion, and *metanoia* is that same freedom exercised in the right direction. No one can be turned
  by force. The formation community cannot produce the turn in anyone; it can only make the conditions
  in which a person''s own turning can happen. A catechist who pressures or manipulates someone into the
  outward forms of turning, without the soul''s genuine response, has produced an imitation formation
  cannot deepen — there is no real turn to deepen.


  The sorrow that often comes with *metanoia* is real and not dismissed. To see clearly what the turned-away
  life has cost — in oneself and in others — is to grieve, and that grief is a fitting response to genuine
  turning. But the grief is what the turning produces, not what the turning is. A soul that feels the
  grief without turning has only the sorrow; a soul that turns without the grief may turn genuinely and
  find the grief follow as it begins to see clearly. The grief confirms the turn; it does not constitute
  it.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  *Metanoia* is a change of *nous* — a genuine change of direction in the soul''s highest faculty of orientation.
  The difference between feeling-sorry and *metanoia* is the difference between a person who regrets where
  they ended up and a person who has actually turned to walk a different way. Both feel the weight of
  where they have been; only the second has changed direction. Formation deepens a change of direction;
  it cannot deepen regret-without-turning, because there is no re-orientation to deepen. The test of genuine
  *metanoia* is not the intensity of feeling but the actual change of direction — visible over time in
  the soul''s orientation toward God and in the formation practices it now inhabits rather than merely
  performs.'

modern_hearing: '**Modern Hearing:**

  In modern English "repentance" usually means feeling sorry — the experience of remorse or regret for
  wrong acts, measured by the sincerity and intensity of the feeling, and typically tied to specific actions.
  The more sorry one feels, the more one has repented. This is not baseless: genuine *metanoia* often
  does produce sorrow. But it collapses the turn into the feeling, and that has a consequence — a person
  can feel intensely and sincerely sorry for particular acts while remaining aimed at the very goods that
  produced them. They are sorry for the expressions of the mis-orientation; the mis-orientation itself
  is unchanged. That is not *metanoia*.'
