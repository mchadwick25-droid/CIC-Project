quick_meaning: For this world the nous is the highest faculty of the soul — the capacity for direct, non-discursive
  perception of divine reality, the "mind's eye" that illumination opens and contemplation deepens; not
  ordinary step-by-step reasoning but the soul's deepest power to perceive what is real, most fully in
  the image of God and most directly restored by formation.
world_meaning: 'Beneath the discursive mind that reasons from one thing to the next, the soul has a faculty
  of a different kind: a capacity for direct apprehension, a seeing rather than a working-out. This is
  the nous. It is the highest part of the human person, the part illumination most directly reaches —
  the soul''s depth, where divine reality is *encountered* rather than *thought about*. And it is the
  part most fully in the image of the Logos: as the Logos is God''s own Reason, so the nous is what in
  the human most nearly answers to him. Formation, at its deepest, is the restoring of the nous to its
  proper orientation — turning the mind''s eye back toward the God it was made to perceive.


  This much is broadly the world''s conviction, and it holds it securely. But there is a further stratum
  of Origen''s account of the nous that the world holds far more tentatively. Origen extends it: the nous
  is, in some sense, what the soul *originally was* — rational beings existing first as pure contemplating
  intellects, the fall producing embodied psychic existence, and restoration returning all rational beings
  to that nous-like contemplation. This runs toward the pre-existence of the soul, and toward matters
  the tradition would later set outside its bounds. This further stratum the world holds as *contested
  inheritance* — carried and loved, but under real question rather than confessed as settled teaching
  (the specific dispute is set out below, under CT Contest Type). The deep conviction about the contemplative
  faculty it holds securely; Origen''s cosmology of the nous it holds with the weight of that unresolved
  question upon it.


  (A third, still more systematic account of the nous — its purification through *apatheia* into imageless
  prayer — belongs to Evagrius and the desert tradition. That is desert-attributed and reserved for the
  Desert Christianity build; it is not part of this world''s own attested vocabulary.)'

[CT Contest Type — parked at the S2.2-equivalent; home arrives with the contested_claim records (S2.6-equivalent) / S2.3 authoring] **Meaning.** The contest concerns what the term meant in its own context — specifically, whether Origen's *own* speculative account of the nous (the nous as the soul's original state; pre-existence; universal return) is the *same* theological position as the propositions condemned at the Second Council of Constantinople (553 CE). Patristics specialists are genuinely divided: some read the condemned "Origenism" as a later, distorting systematization not identical to Origen's own thought; others read meaningful continuity. The dispute is unresolved. Note precisely what is and is not contested: the *broadly-attested* account of the nous as the soul's contemplative faculty is **not** contested; only the continuity of Origen's speculative extension with the condemned propositions is. (This is the same *Meaning* contest carried by Apokatastasis, to which it is theologically linked.)
