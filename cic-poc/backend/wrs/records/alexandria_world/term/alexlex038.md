quick_meaning: 'For us love is not primarily a feeling — it is the fruit of genuine formation: what wisdom
  looks like in the soul''s relation to God and to others once transformation has done its work, inseparable
  from genuine knowledge of God, and the visible evidence that formation has actually occurred rather
  than merely been performed.'
world_meaning: 'Among us, love cannot be commanded into existence, and this is not a pastoral failure
  but a truth about what love is. The soul told to love God and neighbor, who then resolves to do so —
  to produce the acts of love by deliberate effort — has not yet understood what we mean by agape. Agape
  is not a series of acts the resolved will manufactures. It is a condition of the soul: what the soul
  naturally expresses once formation has genuinely done its work.


  The mature Christian holds knowledge and love together as one thing — not two separate achievements
  a formed person happens to possess, but the same reality approached from two sides. The soul that has
  come into genuine contact with God through formation knows God and loves God in a single movement toward
  the one it was made for. Knowledge without love is not genuine knowledge of God; love without knowledge
  is not genuine agape. They are inseparable because they are the soul''s one response to the same reality:
  the Logos, at once the truth the soul perceives and the good the soul desires.


  The formation sequence is what produces love — not as an optional final product but as what genuine
  formation always looks like when it has gone deep. Catechesis begins to reorient the soul''s desire
  toward God; illumination opens its perception to what it is being formed toward; gnosis deepens its
  genuine contact with God; and wisdom, which is what that contact looks like once it has soaked through
  the whole life, bears love as its most visible fruit. The soul in whom wisdom has taken hold loves freely,
  generously, without calculation, because its desire has been genuinely reordered toward what love is
  the expression of. Wisdom is a condition of the whole soul; agape is what that condition looks like
  in the soul''s relationships.


  This is why we can say with precision: love proves that formation has occurred. A soul genuinely transformed
  toward God does not show it first through doctrinal correctness or ritual observance or ascetic achievement,
  but through the quality of its love — love that is generous rather than calculating, that sees the other
  as bearing the image of the Logos, that serves without performing service, that endures without the
  bitterness unformed love cannot outlast. These are not virtues won by effort; they are what transformation
  produces in a soul genuinely formed.


  And what the soul''s love is finally aimed at is the Logos himself. The always-present center of our
  whole life is at the same time the object of the soul''s deepest desire — the one toward whom every
  stage of formation has been turning the soul. Love is the name for what the soul''s genuine orientation
  toward the Logos looks like once formation has deepened it enough. So agape is not aimed at an abstraction;
  it is aimed at the one already present in every genuine encounter our formation gives.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  Agape is neither a feeling nor a psychological capacity. It is what the soul naturally expresses once
  formation has genuinely reordered its desire toward God and toward the people who bear God''s image.
  The soul that loves freely and without calculation has not manufactured loving feelings by willpower
  or worked through its defenses; its desire has been genuinely reordered through formation, until what
  love expresses — the soul''s real desire for the one whose image the beloved bears — is now what the
  soul most deeply wants. This is why love can be commanded in the sense that matters: not as the production
  of a feeling but as the direction of formation. The community that catechizes, illumines, deepens knowledge,
  and grows wisdom is the community producing agape, which appears in genuinely formed souls as the natural
  expression of what formation is doing. We do not command feelings; we form souls, and souls formed toward
  God love.'

modern_hearing: '**Modern Hearing:**

  Love in modern usage is first an emotion — warmth, affection, attachment, care that rises toward particular
  people or things, that comes and goes and is not reliably under the will. On this hearing, loving God
  is having positive feelings toward God, and loving the neighbor is feeling warmly toward them; the trouble
  with commanded love is that you cannot produce the feeling by deciding to. A secondary, therapeutic
  hearing treats love as a relational capacity cultivated through psychological work — more securely attached,
  more capable of intimacy, less defended — closer in some ways but locating the change in psychological
  process rather than in the soul''s formation toward God.'
