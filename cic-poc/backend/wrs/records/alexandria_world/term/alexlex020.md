term: Restoration
aliases:

quick_meaning: For this world restoration is the conviction that God's work in creation is genuinely restorative
  — what sin damaged the Incarnation addresses, what death severed resurrection reunites, and the formation
  ecology is the ongoing work of the image being restored toward the likeness; this broadly shared conviction
  is separable from Origen's contested universalist *apokatastasis*, which the tradition does not require.
world_meaning: 'When the Logos entered human nature, the first thing that entry did was not to teach new
  truths or model a new morality. The first thing was restoration — Life re-entering what was dying, the
  image''s ground returning to what the image was losing, the reversal beginning of what sin had started
  and death was completing.


  God''s work in salvation is restorative before it is anything else, and this conviction is the backbone
  of the whole formation arc. What is being restored is the image of God in us — damaged by sin''s mis-orientation
  but never destroyed, present in every person as the ground of their capacity for God, obscured by the
  soul''s turning away and now being progressively cleared and fulfilled by the work of formation. Formation
  does not build a new human capacity from nothing. It restores and brings to fullness a capacity that
  sin damaged and the Logos''s entry is now healing from within.


  Restoration takes time because the damage did. The image was not destroyed in an instant by sin, and
  it is not restored in an instant by the Incarnation. A soul that turned from God by its own genuine
  freedom must be genuinely returned — not forced back but formed back, through the patient, graduated,
  community-sustained work of divine pedagogy. The Incarnation makes the restoration possible; the formation
  ecology is how it actually happens in real souls and lives, through the same freedom that sin required
  and that restoration must also work through.


  And restoration arrives further along than where we began. This is the point most easily missed. It
  is not a return to a prior condition — not the soul restored to what it was before sin, the way a painting
  is cleaned back to its original state. It goes past the starting point. The soul formed toward God,
  answering with its own genuine response, becomes what it was created to become — not merely what it
  was before it fell away, but the likeness the image was always aimed at and always meant to grow into.
  Restoration is completion, not reset.


  There is a place where this conviction has to be stated carefully. Origen extended it into a specific
  and more speculative proposal — *apokatastasis*: that in the end all rational natures, including in
  some formulations even those who have persistently refused God, are ultimately restored to him. That
  is not the same as the broadly shared conviction that God''s work is restorative; it is the further
  claim that the restoration is universal in scope, that no rational being is finally excluded. This universalist
  extension is not one we hold or require, and it sits uneasily among us — some are drawn to it, and others
  pull back from where it leads. The two are separable: one can hold a full restoration theology — the
  Logos restoring human nature, the Incarnation restoring the image, formation restoring the soul toward
  the likeness, resurrection restoring the whole person — without holding the universalist extension.
  Athanasius is the standing demonstration that this is so; he holds all of the former and none of the
  latter. That is why this restorative conviction is not itself contested: it is widely held and firmly
  grounded, and the disputed material belongs to *apokatastasis*, not to restoration as such.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  The restoration conviction here is forward-looking. What the Incarnation restores is not a prior condition
  but a created potential — the image progressively conformed to the likeness, the soul growing into what
  it was always meant to become rather than merely returning to what it was before it fell. It goes further
  than the starting point: not a reset but a completion. The forensic version of restoration is reinstatement
  to legal standing — acquitted, declared righteous, the relationship restored to its former legal condition.
  The restoration this world means is ontological and progressive: the soul genuinely changed, the image
  genuinely restored and fulfilled, the likeness genuinely grown into across a lifetime.'
