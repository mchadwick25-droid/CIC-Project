quick_meaning: 'For this world participation is what the whole formation sequence arrives at — not nearness
  to God but real sharing in the divine life itself: through grace and the Logos''s incarnation the soul
  genuinely takes part in the life of the one who made it, not by becoming God but by becoming truly what
  God intended a creature to be.'
world_meaning: 'When this world says the soul participates in the divine life, it is not reaching for
  a metaphor. Something real is shared. The word is placed carefully between two failures the ecology
  refuses. On one side is the soul that merely *approaches* God across a gap that never closes — near,
  but always outside. On the other is the soul *dissolved* into the divine, its creaturely nature burned
  away until there is no longer anyone there to share. Participation is a genuine third thing: the creature,
  remaining fully a creature, genuinely shares in God''s own life. Real contact, real sharing — and the
  creature is still itself.


  This is possible only because the Logos entered human nature and opened the sharing from the inside.
  As Athanasius puts it with deliberate audacity, God became human so that the human might become god
  — not god by nature, but by grace and participation, a creature drawn into the life of the Creator.
  That is why the incarnation is not decorative here: if the Logos who became flesh were himself only
  a creature, then to participate in him would be to participate in a creature, and the whole thing would
  collapse. Participation stands or falls with the *homoousios*.


  And because it is participation in the *Logos*, it is never solitary. The soul shares in Christ through
  the community and its life, not in a private mystical union sealed off from others. This is why the
  Eucharist stands at the center: it is the recurring, bodily enactment of participation — the community
  becoming, again, what it is, by sharing in the one whose life it partakes.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  vine, not a branch merely propped against it. Real life shared, not mere presence at an event.'
