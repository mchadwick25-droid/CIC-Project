quick_meaning: 'For this world *oikonomia* — literally "household management" — names God''s overall governance
  of the plan of salvation: the arrangement by which the Logos who creates all things also enters creation

  life, and the encompassing frame within which every part of our formation takes its place.'
world_meaning: '*Oikonomia* — literally the governance of a household (*oikos* and *nomos*, household
  law or management) — is our word for the encompassing plan that God''s saving work follows. Not a plan
  devised after something went wrong, as though God improvised a rescue. The divine *oikonomia* is the
  overall arrangement by which the Logos who creates all things also governs all things toward their restoration
  and fulfillment — the economy in which the whole of creation and of salvation history takes part.


  The word carries several related senses, and holding them together is part of what makes it a term of
  technical care. In its broadest sense *oikonomia* names God''s entire governance of creation toward
  its saving purpose — what our forebears called the "recapitulation" of all things in Christ: the Logos
  gathering up and restoring what sin had scattered and damaged, bringing creation back toward the share
  in divine life it was always aimed at. In this sense it spans the whole arc: creation, the long formation
  of Israel, the Incarnation, the resurrection, the completion at the end. In a narrower sense *oikonomia*
  names the particular arrangement of the Incarnation itself — the "plan" by which the Logos entered human
  nature at a particular time, in a particular way, to accomplish a particular restoration. The Incarnation
  is the *oikonomia*''s pivotal moment: not just any response to sin and death but the specific arrangement
  of the Logos''s entry into what was damaged, to restore it from within.


  Our formation takes part in the *oikonomia*. Every catechumenal encounter, every Scripture reading,
  every Eucharistic gathering, every act of prayer and fasting is a share in the one arrangement by which
  the Logos is restoring and transforming souls toward divine life. Our formation is not a human program
  running alongside God''s economy. It is the form the economy takes in the actual lives and communities
  of those who enter it.


  This is also where the integrating unity of everything we do gets its historical and saving depth. Because
  the Logos is the one through whom all things were made and through whom all things are being restored,
  our diverse practices — Scripture, sacrament, common life, catechesis — cohere as expressions of one
  arrangement rather than as a pile of separate activities. The *oikonomia* is why the unity the Logos
  gives is not only the center of our formation but the encompassing plan of all creation toward its end.


  And divine pedagogy is our formation''s share in the *oikonomia*''s graduated character. The economy
  does not restore all things in a single instant; it proceeds through the graduated teaching that God''s
  patience with creatures requires and God''s wisdom about them designs. The long arc from creation through
  the prophets through the Incarnation through our own formation toward the end is the *oikonomia* unfolding
  at the pace of genuine formation — the divine Teacher teaching at the rate that genuine reception, in
  genuine freedom, takes.'

    Note: *oikonomia* as God''s overall governance of the plan of salvation, the Incarnation as its pivotal

    **Widely Accepted**. Origen''s speculative account of the economy connects to his pre-existence and

    overall saving governance is attested independently of that speculation. How the precise scope of
    *oikonomia* is bounded, and how it relates to human freedom, was actively debated in the tradition
    and remains genuinely open — held here as an honest question, not a settled point.'

  real content in the term at all. A second hearing, familiar to those with some theological schooling,
  takes "economy" as the "economic Trinity" versus the "immanent Trinity" — God as revealed in salvation
  history versus God as God is in Godself. That is a real distinction, but far more technical than what
  *oikonomia* names here.'
