quick_meaning: 'For this world the Eucharist is not a memorial of something Christ did once — it is the
  community''s recurring, bodily, shared enactment of what he is still doing: giving himself to be received,
  so that every gathering at the table is a real participation in the divine life the resurrection opened
  and the whole of formation is reaching toward.'
world_meaning: 'The Eucharist tempts us to reach for the right words — "real presence," "the body and
  blood of Christ," "the sacrament of the altar" — and to believe the words have carried the meaning.
  They have not. The words are not wrong. But held on their own they say what the Eucharist is called
  without beginning to say what it does to the soul that receives it, week upon week, across a whole life.


  What it does begins with what it is: the community gathered around the one who gives himself to be shared.
  Not the memory of that giving. Not the doctrine that it once happened. The giving itself, again — as
  the one who gave himself in the incarnation and the cross and the rising gives himself still, in the
  bread and cup we receive together. We do not gather to recall a sharing that happened somewhere else.
  We gather to share — to take part, in the body, in the life of the one who offers himself to be taken
  part in. The bread broken is the Logos giving himself; the cup received is his life held out for us.


  This makes the table the most concentrated single act of formation in our recurring life. Every other
  practice we keep — Scripture, prayer, fasting, belonging, catechesis — is aimed at deepening the soul''s
  share in the divine life. The Eucharist enacts that share directly, in the gathered body''s act of receiving.
  The soul that has received a hundred times is not repeating a ceremony but going deeper each time into
  the participation the receiving is. And the table asks no literacy and no schooling of us; it asks the
  baptism that made a person a full member, and it asks genuine attention and desire. The believer who
  receives with real attention shares in the divine life as truly as the teacher grown old in Scripture''s
  depths — this is one of the main ways the life at the center of our world reaches the whole community,
  including those the school tradition cannot reach. The bishop who presides is doing what is most fundamental
  to that office: gathering us around the one who gives himself.


  The table also points forward. What it enacts is real but not yet full. We gather in hope as much as
  in sharing — receiving now what is given now, and knowing that the fullness of it is the horizon theosis
  names. Every Eucharist is the life of formation already enacted; every Eucharist waits for what formation
  is still reaching toward. We receive and hope, take part and wait, are formed and are not yet fully
  formed.'
distortion_risk: '**World Hearing:**

  The community''s present sharing in the life of the one who is always giving himself — not a memorialized
  Christ but a present Christ, whose life is genuinely available to be genuinely shared by those who gather
  and receive. It forms the soul because in it the soul takes part, bodily, in the life it was made for
  — not by conveying spiritual nutrition through an agreed symbol, and not merely by applying the correct
  label, but by genuine participation.'
