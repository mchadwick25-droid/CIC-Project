text: >-
  Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. These were trading towns, and everyone
  lived within earshot of the roads. Caravans came up from the Euphrates crossings and went east
  through Nisibis toward Persia; in the peace terms between the empires, Nisibis was named a point
  where the two sides' merchants should meet and trade, so that commerce could be watched and taxed.
  Silk, spices, and fine goods moved west; coin, cloth, and manufactures moved east. The city's own
  storytellers took this world of movement for granted: a tale written in Edessa in this period opens,
  without any need of explanation, with a merchant buying a skilled carpenter on commission for a king
  in India and taking ship for the east. Travel was slow, bought with silver, and full of risk - but
  it was ordinary. A townsman might never leave his quarter, yet he handled goods from a month's
  journey away and heard road-news from farther still.
