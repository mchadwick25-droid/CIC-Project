text: >-
  Common life of the place, not the teaching of any one voice. Edessa lived beside a river with a name
  that meant "the Leaper." The Daisan skirted the city walls, mild most of the year, and in some
  winters it rose without warning. The city kept an archive, and one report survived in it: in
  November of the year 201, after days of rain, the river burst into the sleeping city by night. It
  tore through the great palace of the king, drowned people in their houses - the report counts the
  dead in the thousands - and damaged, among the buildings it struck, the meeting-place of the
  Christians. The city rebuilt; kings dug channels, raised dikes, and set watchmen on the water. Below
  the citadel hill, springs fed pools of fish that people held in reverence long before and long after
  any one teacher's lifetime. To live in this city was to live with water that gave gardens and
  orchards in a dry land, and could take the whole lower town in a night.
