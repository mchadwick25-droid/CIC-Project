quick_meaning: In this world, *episkopos* names the one who oversees the community's table and guards
  its unity — but whether every household has such a single figure, or whether presbyters share this trust
  together, is itself the question we are still living through.
world_meaning: 'We know both patterns from real contact. In some households like ours, one episkopos stands
  at the center, and obedience to him is felt as the very shape of our unity — fitted together, as Ignatius
  of Antioch put it, the way a harp''s strings are tuned to each other. Do nothing apart from the bishop,
  he writes to us, and we have heard this read aloud often enough that it has shaped how many of us think
  about order. In other households, a council of presbyters governs together, with no sense that anything
  is missing for want of a single presiding figure. The letter from Rome to Corinth speaks of presbyters
  removed from their office, not of a bishop deposed — and no one reading that letter feels they are being
  told of a church less complete than one with a bishop at its head.


  What presses on both patterns alike, without settling which is right, is a felt and unpredictable possibility:
  a name and an accusation could be brought against any household, on any ordinary day. Under that pressure,
  the argument for a single visible figure — someone who can speak for all of us when speaking is demanded
  — carries real weight. But so does the argument for a council whose continuity does not depend on any
  one life.
