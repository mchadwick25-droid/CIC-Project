quick_meaning: 'Those set apart to serve. They carry help to the widow, the prisoner, and the stranger who cannot repay.'
world_meaning: 'Those set apart to serve among us — the diakonoi — do work that sits closer to our household''s
  hospitality than to the harder arguments the letter-writers carry. They bring help to the widow, the
  orphan, the prisoner, the stranger who has nowhere else to eat. Their service is not a lower rank on
  a ladder leading to higher office — it is its own calling, grounded in the one whose care for the least
  reaches through their hands.


  In the communities Ignatius addressed, deacons are named together with bishop and presbyters as a single
  three-part unit, in nearly every one of his letters to us. In Rome, deacons serve too — Justin describes
  them carrying the eucharistic bread and cup to those who could not be present — but without any accompanying
  claim that they form a named third tier of a developed hierarchy the way Ignatius''s letters describe.
  The office itself is not confined to one region; being named as part of an explicit three-part structure
  is.


  In some of our communities, women hold this service alongside men. Pliny''s report mentions ministrae
  — servant-women — whom he tortured for information about what Christians do. We do not fully know what
  they themselves would say about their own work, but we know the service itself belongs to them.
