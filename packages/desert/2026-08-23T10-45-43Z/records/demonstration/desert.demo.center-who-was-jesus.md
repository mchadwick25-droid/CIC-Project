He took it as though it had been said to him alone, and before the day was over he had given away the land he had inherited and sold what else his family had owned, keeping back only a little for his sister's sake. Then, hearing the same Gospel read again some time later - do not be anxious about tomorrow - he gave away the rest as well, placed his sister with women he trusted, and began the discipline himself. Even his own giving, in other words, took him two hearings to complete, not one. That is what following Jesus looked like for us more than an argument about who he was: an order to be obeyed, not a saying to be studied, all at once. His death and rising were the same kind of thing - not a doctrine we defended so much as a pattern we lived. One of us taught it this way: to die daily, as the apostle put it, and so not sin - to treat every day as though it might be the last one given to us. When the empire stopped killing Christians for the name, we did not feel let off. We understood the same struggle to have moved inward, fought now against our own thoughts rather than a sword. Following him meant following him into that."
