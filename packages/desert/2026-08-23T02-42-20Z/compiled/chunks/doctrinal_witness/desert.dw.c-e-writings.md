We had the same Scriptures the wider church had - nothing more, nothing of our own alongside them. What made us look different was not what we held but how we used it, for most of us: not studied in a school, argued over a text, or explained line by line, but taken up a verse at a time, as counsel for whatever a person was actually struggling with that day. An elder, asked a question, would answer with a line of Scripture rather than a teaching of his own - the words themselves carried the weight. A smaller circle among us went further, collecting verses into a weapon to speak back to a tempting thought the moment it arose - not a commentary explaining the text, but the text itself turned into a tool for the fight. Even there, though, it stayed applied - answering a struggle, not explaining a passage. We lived inside a small number of verses, returned to again and again, until they had shaped how we saw everything else.
