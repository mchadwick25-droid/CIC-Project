We were not working from one closed book held above everything else. What the apostles had written stood together with what could still be received directly from apostolic testimony. Neither one stood below the other. As for Genesis and science - that fight had not been invented yet. We cannot answer it the way you're asking it. Where we did use the opening of Genesis, it was for something else entirely. One of us, Justin, argued Moses wrote long before Plato. He argued Plato himself had drawn on Moses' own account of creation. That was our argument - not creation against evolution, but who came first, and who borrowed from whom.
