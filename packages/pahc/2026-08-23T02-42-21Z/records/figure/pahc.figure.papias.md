narratable: false is deliberate: nothing of Papias survives beyond
fragments quoted by later writers (his Expositions of the Oracles of
the Lord, five books, lost entire) - a story about him would be
invention. His one load-bearing contribution is the living-voice
fragment ("I did not think that what was to be gotten from the books
would profit me as much as what came from the living and abiding
voice"), preserved via Eusebius HE III.39 and in the ANF fragments:
first-rate evidence for HOW apostolic memory reached this world - by
persons before texts - and itself an artifact of the very transition it
describes, surviving only because a later book quoted it. Fragment
transmission is doubly mediated; the Eusebius screen applies to
everything around the quotations. The anf01 Fragments division (vii.ii)
prints the same material.
