Strand B's second witness and this world's best window into ordinary
anxiety: the doubt question (the double-minded soul, Commandment 9), the
second-repentance question, wealth and poverty (the elm and the vine),
his own household's failure. Genre discipline carried on every use: what
is attested is that Hermas presents his formation through reported
visions in his own surviving text - never a claim that the visionary
content is historical event. His self-description as a former slave
(Vision 1's own opening: "He who had brought me up, sold me to one
Rhode in Rome") is the text's own opening frame.
