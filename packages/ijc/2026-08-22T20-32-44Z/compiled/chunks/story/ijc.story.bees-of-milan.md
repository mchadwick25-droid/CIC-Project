the tradition of the bees at the infant Ambrose's mouth, as Paulinus told it after Ambrose's death - legend, and named as such

Paulinus, who had been Ambrose's own secretary, tells it this way: while Ambrose lay as an infant in the courtyard of his father's house, a swarm of bees settled on his face, going in and out of his open mouth, and rose again without harming him. His father, seeing it, said that if the child lived he would become something great. So the tradition tells it - a story remembered, or made, to explain a life that became as full of speech as any this world's record keeps. The tradition says; the record cannot say more.
