This world read its scriptures the way it read everything: toward what binds. The same Bible read for contemplation elsewhere in Christian history, this world read for warrant. "Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church" - Rome read that as a standing grant of office, not only a blessing. The century's deepest fight was over scriptural words: whether "like the Father," which scripture's language could carry, or "of one being," which scripture nowhere says, kept scripture's meaning better - both sides arguing from the same texts, at councils, for keeps. And the councils' own past rulings were read the way a court reads precedent: "let the ancient customs prevail." As for which writings counted, this world's window inherited the scriptures in near-settled form and fought over their interpretation, not their list - the fixing of the list belongs to other rooms than its councils. How someone who could not read received all this, the record barely says: through the assembly, the creed confessed, the psalms sung - the channels are visible even where the experience is not.
