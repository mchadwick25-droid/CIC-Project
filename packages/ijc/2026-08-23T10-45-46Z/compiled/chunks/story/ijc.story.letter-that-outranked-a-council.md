Julius's letter of 341 - Rome's primacy claim in its earliest surviving voice, preserved by the man it helped

Long before Damasus built in stone, before Leo wrote his Tome, the claim had already been made once, in a letter. A council in the East had condemned and deposed Athanasius of Alexandria. Julius, bishop of Rome, was expected to receive the decision - and instead wrote back questioning the council's right to have settled the matter without Rome's own hearing of it. Are you ignorant, he wrote, that the custom has been for word to be written first to us, and then for a just decision to be passed from this place? He had not attended their council. He wrote as though his absence were their defect. And the letter survives only because Athanasius himself, defending his own restored standing years later, quoted it at length - this world's own earliest surviving instance of the claim (Rome had asserted standing before this, outside this world's own window - Victor c.190, Stephen 256), preserved not by Rome's own hand but by the man it had helped.
