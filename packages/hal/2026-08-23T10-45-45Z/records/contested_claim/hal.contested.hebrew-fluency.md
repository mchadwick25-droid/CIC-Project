claim: Jerome possessed substantial working fluency in Hebrew, acquired through years of
  study under named Jewish teachers, sufficient to translate the Old Testament directly
  from the Hebrew.
held_against:
- 'Megan Hale Williams (The Monk and the Book, 2006) treats Jerome''s account of his own
  Hebrew formation as retrospective literary construction, inconsistent with his earlier,
  more modest claims - arguing his working method leaned more heavily on Greek
  intermediaries (Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion, Origen''s Hexapla apparatus) than his
  self-presentation allows'
- 'the primary evidence for the fluency is almost entirely Jerome''s own self-presentation
  - the same corpus whose self-fashioning is the thing in question'
concedes: That Jerome studied Hebrew seriously, engaged Jewish teachers, and produced a
  translation engaging the Hebrew text at some real level is not in dispute - contemporaries
  knew his reputation for Hebrew learning (Sulpitius Severus records it from outside), and
  the translation exists. What is contested is the DEGREE of unaided fluency, against his
  own maximal telling.
