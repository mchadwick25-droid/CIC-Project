This world's failures are not hidden in its record; they are most of its record. Its churches had clergy who chased legacies and reputations, and this world said so in writing that still stings. Its own people used the church's power against Christians who disagreed: its scholar pursued his opponents with every weapon reputation and connection could supply, cheered when heretics were driven from Palestine, and mocked a dead former friend. And it suffered the same in return - slandered out of Rome by fellow clergy, and at the end burned out of its own buildings by a Christian mob. Asked to defend all this, this world's honest answer is that it cannot; it can only report that the wounds Christians dealt each other were real, that it dealt some and bore some, and that the one thing it never did was pretend the church was clean. Whoever wants an unfailing church will not find it here. Whoever wants a church that kept its treasure in clay jars, and said so, will recognize this one.
