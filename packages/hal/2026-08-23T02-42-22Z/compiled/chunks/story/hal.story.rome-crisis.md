the crisis that drove the circle from Rome - Blaesilla's death, the crowd at her funeral, the loss of Damasus, and the departure by sea

Blaesilla was Paula's eldest daughter, widowed young. She turned to the discipline with a convert's fire - and within months she was dead, about twenty years old. At the funeral her mother collapsed and was carried fainting through the procession, and the crowd began to mutter: see what comes of their fasting - she wept for a daughter killed by it - how long before we drive these detestable monks out of Rome, stone them, throw them into the Tiber? That winter the old pope died, and the protection that had sheltered the circle's scholar died with him. The whispers became open slander. Within the year Jerome stood on a ship at Ostia, writing a last letter to a friend: before he knew Paula's house, he said, all Rome had praised him; now he was called infamous. He sailed east and never saw Rome again. The community's answer to the crisis was not vindication; it was Bethlehem.
