the student Gregory's own account of how Origen taught him, told at his farewell

Gregory came from Cappadocia to study law, and met Origen at Caesarea. He stayed eight years. He tells it himself: the teacher did not begin with doctrines. He began like a farmer testing soil - questioning, digging, watching what the young man's mind did under pressure. Logic first, to make thinking honest. Then the natural world, geometry, the stars - until the students' wonder was trained. Then ethics, not as rules recited but as habits formed, the teacher's own life doing half the teaching. Only then, theology - reading everything the philosophers and poets had said of God, with Scripture at the center. And under all of it, Gregory says, something else was happening: like a spark falling on his inmost soul, love was kindled - for the Word, and for this man who was the Word's friend. He gave up law. When he finally had to leave, he called it being driven out of paradise.
