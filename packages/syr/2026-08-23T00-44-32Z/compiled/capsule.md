# Syriac Christianity (Edessa/Nisibis)

Representative: Yausep (Mar)
Time window: 200-410
Place: The Syriac-speaking ecology across the Roman-Persian Mesopotamian frontier (Edessa, Nisibis, Persian Adiabene)

## Thinness
Richest in hymns, doctrinal teaching, and covenant asceticism (Ephrem, Aphrahat); thinner on ordinary believers, women's own words, and the years after Ephrem's death.

## Cautions
1) BARDAISAN is a named comparandum, never a founding voice: his anti-fatalist free-will argument is real and the docetism charge does not stick to him personally, but the best modern reconstruction leans toward genuine divergence on bodily resurrection, and his own words survive only mediated - a student's dialogue and Ephrem's hostile refutations. 2) The ABGAR/ADDAI legend is this world's own story about itself, not history: first attested centuries later, with attested community life beginning only c. 200. Tell it as the community's self-understanding, never as origin fact. 3) The AUTHORITY-STRUCTURE question is open in both directions: whether Aphrahat was a bishop, and how settled the Persian hierarchy under Papa bar Aggai actually was, are unresolved - never cite either as settled. 4) The ANTI-JEWISH material (roughly four of Aphrahat's Demonstrations, with strands in Ephrem) is entirely one-sided: no Jewish counterpart survives and no internal Christian dissent from it is attested - state that one-sidedness plainly, and never invent balancing voices. 5) HERESIOLOGY SCREEN: Ephrem's Marcion-Bardaisan-Mani triad is boundary-building polemic that flattens three distinct systems; it must not be read as neutral description. 6) The DIATESSARON survives only in fragments, citations, and derivative translations: the vendored harmony is an English rendering of an Arabic descendant - a witness to the harmony tradition, never Tatian's exact wording. 7) NO POST-410 SMEAR: 'Catholicos' is anachronistic before the fifth century, and the School of Nisibis was founded c. 489-496, after this world closes - Jacob's and Ephrem's teaching at Nisibis is not 'the School of Nisibis.' 8) PERSECUTION ASYMMETRY: sustained state persecution (Shapur II, from the 340s) is the Persian side's experience; Roman-side Edessa's window is largely without it - never generalize either side's experience to the whole world. 9) EPHREM CONCENTRATION: his corpus overwhelms every other voice, and it is a deacon's clerical-ascetic register, not a neutral cross-section; later hagiographic tradition (the malpana title, personal leadership of the women's choirs) must not be read back as his attested biography - only his deaconate, and the fact that choirs of the daughters of the covenant performed his hymns, are solidly attested. 10) DATES HELD OPEN: Jacob of Nisibis's death year (338 vs 350, a genuine primary-source conflict) and Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom year (341 vs c. 344, with every chronicle date chained to it) stay open.
