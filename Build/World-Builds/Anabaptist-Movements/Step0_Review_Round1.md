# Step 0 Review, Round 1 — The Anabaptist Movements

**Reviewer:** independent adversarial review agent (Opus), 2026-09-25, per `cic-build-cycle` discipline.
**Document reviewed:** `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` (Revision 1, drafted 2026-09-15).

## Verdict

**Substantial revision needed.** The headline conclusions (clears Section A, Tier 1 provisional) likely survive, but the reasoning offered for them is either wrong or not the test Article 4 actually specifies. Findings change the Donatism selection rationale's own substance, the scope boundary of the disclosed Christological complication, a sourcing conclusion, and the grounding of the A1 floor verdict.

## Findings

### High severity

**F1. Internal contradiction and factual error on the Donatism dating (§0, B4).** §0 says Donatism argued this case "two centuries earlier"; B4 says "two centuries later." The census dates Donatism (I.4) c. 311–439 CE; Anabaptism begins 1525 — the real gap is roughly 1,100–1,200 years. B3 of the same document correctly says "over a millennium's separation" — the document contradicts itself internally.

**F2. "Argues almost exactly this movement's own case" is contradicted by the movement's own vendored voices.** Menno's own complete works: "we are opposed to the Donatists, Circumcelliones, Munsterites, and to the errors, abuses and abominations of all uproarious sects." Dirk Philips's Enchiridion protests persecutors who "liken us to the Donatists, Munsterites, and other turbulent, erring spirits... A great injustice is done to us." In the 16th century, the Donatist comparison was the *opponents'* charge, and the movement's own sources reject it — the document presents the echo as though it were the movement's own self-understanding without disclosing that its own vendored sources repudiate the comparison. Real theological differences (infant vs. believer's baptism at issue for the Donatists; their rebaptism concerned lapsed clergy's sacramental validity, not believer's baptism; they appealed to the emperor; Circumcellion violence) are also smoothed over.

**F3. The "as through a channel" quotation attributed to Menno is not in the vendored source — it is an opponent's caricature that a vendored source explicitly rebuts.** A direct search of the vendored Menno Simons complete works (whitespace-normalized, for "channel," "conduit," "pipe," "tube," "sieve," "spout," "through her body," "through Mary") found no such passage. The actual source is the Martyrs Mirror (c. printed p. 795): the friar Cornelis *claims* Menno teaches Christ "merely passed through her body, as water through a sieve, or through a spout" — and the Anabaptist prisoner Herman directly replies, "what you here say, cannot be found in his writings." Menno's own vendored wording rejects Christ as "a man of Mary's flesh," affirms Christ "became a miserable, suffering and mortal man in Mary, the pure virgin," and affirms Christ as "truly God and man." **The substance of the complication stands** — Menno does deny Christ's flesh derived from Mary's own flesh, real tension with commitment 3's "incarnate of... the Virgin Mary" — but the characterization must be rebuilt from Menno's own words, not an inherited, unverified quotation. This is exactly the kind of quote-verification failure CLAUDE.md's source-fidelity section treats as a recurring, serious defect — the phrasing came from the dossier and was never independently re-checked against the vendored file before this document repeated it.

**F4. The complication is scoped too narrowly — it reaches Dirk Philips too, not only Menno.** Dirk Philips's Enchiridion teaches the same doctrine outright in its own section "The Incarnation of Jesus Christ": "it is impossible for the flesh of Christ to be formed by Mary; for neither the seed of Mary, nor that of any earthly creature can by any means be the true living bread that came down from heaven." §4 item 1 binds only "Menno's celestial-flesh Christology"; §4 item 2 lists Dirk as clean available register with no flag. The complication reaches two of the three named-author pillars, not one — the census's own floorNote already names "Menno Simons and the Dutch wing," not Menno alone.

**F5. A1 runs the wrong test — Article 4 requires affirmative evidence, not absence of denial, and the affirming evidence is already vendored but unused.** Article 4's own text requires that "its own confession affirms, in its plain historical sense" the five commitments; the document instead argues "no source checked... denies the Trinity." For the Swiss wing this is empty — Grebel, Manz, and Hubmaier have no vendored writings at all, and Schleitheim's articles don't touch any of the five commitments. The movement's own affirming confessions are already in the vendored corpus and were never cited: the Dordrecht Confession (1632, reproduced in the Martyrs Mirror's introduction — Art. I affirms the Trinity, Art. IV affirms the Word "conceived in the virgin Mary"); the Waterlander confession (in McGlothlin — Art. VIII, the Son "in the body of the virgin Mary was made flesh"); Dirk Philips's own direct Trinitarian affirmation. These also show the Dutch tradition's own later "in," not "of," phrasing — directly relevant to how live F3/F4's complication actually is.

### Medium severity

**F6. The sourcing record is out of date and incomplete.** The document lists four sources; the corpus-map and dossier (updated 2026-09-24) show five vendored and assigned — the Hutterite Geschicht-Buch (German original, Wolkan 1923, ~352K words, covering 1517–1665) is never mentioned, though Moravia is one of the document's own three named regions and B5's own coherence question turns on it. The "no figure word-extracted from vendored XML" caveat is outdated (files are .txt, directly countable: Menno ~531K words, Martyrs Mirror ~1.19M, Dirk ~208K). Several census-named sources (Täuferakten court testimonies, Harder's *Sources of Swiss Anabaptism*, the Ausbund hymns, Dordrecht) are neither assessed nor closed.

**F7. "Genuinely thinner than every other candidate in this batch" is unsupported and contradicts the census.** The census calls the sourcing "RICH in ordinary inside voice" and "Tiered Strong (Tier 1) on a rich base" — never engaged by the document. Raw word counts across the batch (Lollardy 3.81M, Anabaptist 2.41M, Reformed Cities 2.30M, Society of Jesus 1.39M, Tridentine 1.22M, Lutheran Wittenberg 0.96M) show Anabaptist is second-largest by volume, not thinnest. Only a narrower claim holds: thinnest in named-theologian *range* (the dossier's own qualitative read already says this correctly; the document's heading and conclusion overstate it).

**F8. "Narrowest... geography of any candidate in this batch" is false.** Lollardy is England only, per the census — narrower than this candidate's three regions. The document's own "most fragmented" framing is defensible; "narrowest" is not.

**F9. Precedent mis-citation.** The Latin Apologists' Step 0 treats Tertullian's New Prophecy alignment as "a discipline-and-authority complication," not a floor question, and the dossier only uses that precedent for rebaptism-as-practice. Menno's and Dirk's positive, explicitly-argued teaching is a different and arguably heavier kind of complication than Minucius Felix's mere silence — "the same footing" understates it. Lactantius's own positive complications in the same latap document would be a closer precedent.

### Low-medium

**F10.** Grouping Hubmaier under "Swiss Brethren wing... predominantly pacifism" is historically loose — his base moved to Nikolsburg, Moravia, and he explicitly rejected Schleitheim's non-resistance in *On the Sword* (1527); general historical knowledge, not independently verified against a vendored source. The Martyrs Mirror does carry a Hubmaier martyr account and a Zwingli-complaint notice — "effectively unsourceable" should say what partial material does exist.

**F11.** A3's instruction for Wittenberg/Reformed Cities to "disclose the persecution relationship" is stale — both worlds are now Built & Live, and Wittenberg's own Step 0 already discloses it. B1's "genuinely unsourced, not closed as dead ends" partially conflicts with the dossier's own "Checked and closed" table (Marpeck, Hubmaier, Grebel are in fact closed per the 2026-09-24 recheck).

## Confirmed accurate

Article 4 quoted verbatim; rights bases and archive.org IDs match REGISTRY.yaml; the Schleitheim once-removed-translation caveat is accurate; Martyrs Mirror contents (Sattler, Anneken Jans) confirmed present; the VI.14 distinction is sound (no vendored source denies the Trinity, and Dordrecht/Dirk affirm it); Zurich 1525, Münster 1534–35, Atlas ID/era/status all correct; the Münster exclusion is sound (though could be grounded more strongly in Menno's/Dirk's own explicit repudiations, per F2, rather than "per the dossier").
