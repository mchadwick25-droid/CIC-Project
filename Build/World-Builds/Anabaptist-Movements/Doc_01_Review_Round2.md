## New findings

### Substantial

**N1. The three-strand finding does not evidence a Swiss/Dutch divergence. The strand boundaries follow geography.** (§4 Finding, Swiss and Dutch bullets, Polygenesis bullet; carried into §5 Cell 2B, §7 item 4)

Article 21 defines a strand as "a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail." It is never to be assigned "to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality."

- The **Swiss strand's** stated organising conviction is "non-resistance and congregational discipline through the ban."
- The **Dutch strand's** stated organising conviction is "gathered congregational life under Menno's and Dirk Philips's own pastoral and catechetical leadership." That describes leadership and region. It is not a distinct pattern. It restates the shared ecology.
- The vendored Dordrecht Confession (Elkhart 1890) carries every element the document assigns to the Swiss strand: Article XIV (Defense by Force, rejecting "all revenge and resistance"), Article XV (the Swearing of Oaths), Article XVI (the ban) and Article XVII (the Shunning of those who are Expelled). The document itself says the Dutch confessions "independently affirm the same believer's-baptism, gathered-church logic." It also says the Dutch divisions concern "the *severity* of the ban," which is a variation in detail by the document's own account.
- The Stäbler were also non-resistant ("men of the staff, i. e., of peace and non-resistance," Vedder p. 248). So non-resistance does not distinguish the Swiss from the Moravians either.
- The only Dutch-specific distinctive the document names is the celestial-flesh Christology. The document does not argue that it is a distinct formation pattern, and §4 files it as a "regional theological distinctive."
- The Polygenesis bullet treats the three-strand result as "the outcome that scholarship would predict." Polygenesis is a claim about **origins**. Using it to corroborate strand boundaries is the causal-origin reasoning that §4's own framing paragraph warns against, run in reverse ("different origins, therefore different strands").

The only divergence the vendored evidence clearly supports is the Stäbler/Hutterite community of goods as "the doctrine of a standing or falling Church." That quotation is verbatim in Vedder p. 248.

*Required:* Either show, with vendored evidence, a meaningfully distinct Dutch pattern (not a variation in detail from the Swiss), or revise the finding to what the evidence supports. For example, the finding might be a Swiss–Dutch gathered-congregation/ban pattern plus a Moravian/Hutterite community-of-goods strand. Remove polygenesis as corroboration of the strand boundaries; it may remain as context on origins. Then update §4's governing consequence, Cell 2B and §7 item 4 to match.

*Method note for the rebuild.* §4 excludes the Schwertler because the position "does not persist." `Build/worlds/ijc/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §4 (Strand C) holds the opposite: "Article 21 tests for a meaningfully distinct pattern within the world, not for persistence through the world's full temporal span." Round 1 invoked that same precedent as a persistence criterion. Article 21's text supports ijc's reading. The rebuilt §4 should apply Article 21's own test to the Hubmaier/Schwertler question and state why it does or does not meet that test. It should not rest on persistence alone. The Hubmaier conclusion may still hold, for example on the ground that a single dispute of summer 1528 with no further attested pattern is a variation in detail. But the reason given must be the Article's test.

**N2. Felix Landis did not die "under torture."** (§2 Ending point; §2 Catalysts, c. 1642)

The *Martyrs Mirror* (Sohm, lines ~154173 and 156905–156930) records that Felix Landis "dies of hunger and want in prison Othenbach, A. D. 1642." Food was withheld "for many days," and when food was finally given he was too weak to take it. The Mirror dates the entry "about A. D. 1642," with a note that the dating is uncertain. The document tags "dies under torture at Zurich" as *Documented*. That misstates the source.

*Required:* Correct the wording to the source's account: death by starvation in the Othenbach prison, about 1642. The "undiminished" characterisation of Swiss persecution should also be softened. The same Mirror section prints an Amsterdam supplication to Zurich "for mitigation of the persecution" (A. D. 1642).

**N3. The Middelburg toleration is described as "reversible" and "ultimately reversed," which the source contradicts.** (§2 Cultural Environment; §2 Historical Pressures, second bullet; §3 Hussite distinctness; carried into the Hussite contrast)

The *Martyrs Mirror* Remark after William's charge (lines ~148230–148238) says that after William's death the harassment "began again," but that "it was terminated by a third prohibition, by his son." The vendored source therefore records a lapse that was itself reversed in the Anabaptists' favour. It does not record a toleration that was finally withdrawn. The Moravian protection did end in 1623, so "reversible" holds for Moravia only.

*Required:* Correct the Dutch case in each location. Restate the Hussite contrast on grounds that survive the correction. "Local and grant-dependent rather than a formal, movement-wide settlement of this movement's own church order" is still available. Add Dutch toleration to Cell 3A. Move the Dordrecht Confession to the internal column.

**N4. The Round 1 correction to §2's Dordrecht characterisation was not made.** (§2 Ending point; Round 1 M5, second paragraph)

§2 still says Dordrecht is the affirmative-floor evidence for "the Dutch wing's own Trinitarian and Christological commitments." That contradicts Step 0 §2 A1. It also contradicts this document's own §7 item 1, which treats Dordrecht's Christological "in Mary" wording as corroborating the complication.

*Required:* State that Dordrecht is affirmative evidence for commitments (1) and (5), and corroborating evidence only on the complication to commitment (3).

*Required:*
