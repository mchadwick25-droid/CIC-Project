## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas V.3, "Devotio Moderna / Brethren of the Common Life," c. 1380–1517, Low Countries. Geert Groote gave up a wealthy career to gather devout men and women into households that lived in common without ever taking vows, supporting themselves by copying manuscripts. Their practice of keeping a personal notebook of gathered readings (a *rapiarium*) produced Thomas à Kempis's *Imitation of Christ*, the most copied Christian book after the Bible. Their schools shaped Erasmus (educated at Deventer) and touched a year of Luther's own schooling (Magdeburg), and the same disciplined, interior devotion fed into Jesuit spiritual formation a century later. The houses themselves mostly dissolved during the Reformation; the *Imitation* never stopped circulating.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this project's Step 0 documents: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears, on an absence of complication and the movement's own relationship to authority

Quoted directly: floorNote "No question." *The Imitation of Christ* and Kempis's *Founders of the New Devotion* raise no Trinitarian or Christological complication. Groote's own *Publica protestatio* (Ch. XVIII of *Founders*, in his own first-person voice) is positive evidence of general Catholic orthodoxy, not merely an absence of complication: "in regard to those things that are of faith, I have steadfastly preached and defended that faith which is certain, pure, and Catholic, resting upon Jesus Christ Himself Who is the chief corner Stone... subject always to the judgement of the Holy Roman Church, to whom with all humility I everywhere and always submit myself." This is a general profession of Catholic orthodoxy and submission to Rome — it has no Trinitarian or Christological content in the specific sense Article 4's five commitments name. The floor clearance does not rest on the Protestatio for that content; it rests on the absence of any complication across the vendored corpus (the census's own "No question"), with the Protestatio standing as corroborating evidence of the movement's own orthodoxy in the broader sense.

The movement's relationship to church authority was not free of friction. Kempis's *Founders* records "many prelates of the Church were opposed to him... he was forbidden to preach by an edict craftily obtained" — Ch. XVIII is itself a defensive profession of faith written against accusations of unorthodoxy, and a friend's letter to the Bishop of Utrecht, written on Groote's behalf and preserved in the same volume, pleads for his suspended preaching license to be restored. The census confirms this: Groote was "forbidden to preach the year before for attacking the clergy's concubines." The movement submitted to church authority rather than breaking from it — a real distinction from Lollardy and the Hussites — but its relationship to that authority was genuinely contested, not absent of friction. Article 4 itself concerns Trinitarian and Christological commitments, not ecclesiastical authority; the floor clearance above does not rest on this authority relationship in either direction.

The reading behind this document's floor finding is sampling — "the opening pages and a mid-document sample," per the file headers themselves — not a full reading, across a corpus that has since grown well past the works originally sampled (see §3 B1). This pass checked Groote and Kempis only — the movement's own women's side (the Sisters' houses at Deventer and Diepenveen, named as its own voice by the census) was not checked at all. The *Chronicle of Mount St Agnes*, now vendored, does record the Diepenveen Sisters' own investiture in 1408 and other sisters' houses, but only from the canons' side; no vendored text carries the Sisters' own voice directly (see §3 B2).

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this project's Step 0 documents: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: an eleven-file base across four registers

Per the corpus-map, now 11 files spanning devotional, biographical, epistolary, and chronicle registers, host-verified: *The Imitation of Christ* (Benham, 1886); Kempis's own *Founders of the New Devotion* — his account of the movement's founder Gerard Groote and successor Florentius Radewin (Arthur, 1905); Kempis's *Chronicle of Mount St Agnes*, his own account of his home monastery (Arthur, 1906); *The Little Garden of Roses and Valley of Lilies* (1867); four volumes of Kempis's minor Latin works from Pohl's *Opera Omnia* (1902–18); Busch's *Chronicon Windeshemense*, the major chronicle of the Windesheim Congregation (Grube, 1886, Latin); Zerbolt of Zutphen's *The Spiritual Ascent*, the movement's own method text named by the census (Arthur, 1908); and an edition of Groote's own letters, Latin original (Acquoy, 1857).

Acquoy's own introduction (c. line 891) describes the source codex as containing 66 letters, of which 64 are attributable to Groote — the 19th and 20th are by William of Salvarvilla, cantor of Paris, not Groote. Acquoy's own edition, the one vendored here, covers 14 of them: 14 of the 64 letters attributable to Groote (of 66 total in the codex).

Groote's own voice is not Latin-only. The vendored *Founders* volume already contains his own writing in public-domain English: his *Publica protestatio* (Ch. XVIII, first person) and his "Resolutions and Intentions... not confirmed by vows" (the census's own named self-rule text). It also contains a letter to the Bishop of Utrecht pleading for Groote's suspended preaching license to be restored — written on his behalf, not by him: the heading itself reads "A Letter to the Bishop of Utrecht on behalf of Master Gerard Groote," and Acquoy's own introduction names the author as an unnamed friend — the scan reads "qoidam Girardi amicoa, cojos nomea non ad nos pervenit," OCR artifacts for the normalized Latin "quidam Gerardi amicus, cujus nomen non ad nos pervenit." Acquoy's introduction confirms the Protestatio and related texts were originally printed in Kempis's own *Vita*.

Groote's Latin letters are primary evidence, not a second witness. Mark ruled on 2026-09-25 (`Build/worlds/_cross-world/LIBRARY-DECISION-LOG.md`) that a clean public-domain original-language text can be primary evidence, on the same footing as an English translation, when it is primary to the world it's assigned to — language does not decide primacy; credibility and truth to the movement do. `cic/texts/INTAKE.md` states this as the current rule. The corpus-map's Groote entry applies the ruling directly: the letters are Groote's own surviving voice, not reported through Kempis's later biography, and the scan is not on OCR ruling "a"'s garbled list. Each quote is verified against the scan, and the editor's introductions are noticeably noisier than the letter bodies — unlike the 1606 Jesuit Constitutions and Nadal's 1595 *Adnotationes*, both of which are held to second-witness status under Mark's separate OCR ruling on scan quality, so the Jesuit-precedent framing an earlier revision leaned on no longer holds and isn't needed here regardless. Under this project's practice, a quote record holds the verified Latin text; the modern English a Representative speaks is an Opus rendering of the original, independently Opus-checked; and *Founders*' own English texts (the Protestatio, the Resolutions) serve as a cross-check where they overlap with the letters, not a precondition for using them.

The Imitation's own authorship is disputed in its own front matter. The vendored Benham preface itself (c. lines 490–640) calls Kempis's authorship "the erroneous notion that he was its author" and reports the case made by "the advocates of the Gersen authorship" for a 13th-c. "Abbot John," also identified in the manuscript record as Gersen, closing with "Whoever the author may be." Kempis authorship remains the mainstream modern view, but this world's own central vendored edition disputes the attribution in its own introduction — worth a `formation_confidence` tag at Doc_02, not silence.

The "documented" influence claims need separate confidence tags. The Luther/Erasmus schooling connection (§1, §3 B3) is contested in scholarship — R. R. Post's 1968 study rebutted Hyma's earlier thesis, arguing the Brethren mostly ran student hostels rather than schools and that the Reformation link is weak; the census's own wording is already careful ("a household connected with the Brethren"), and "documented" overstates it. The Jesuit direction has a real vendored anchor, though not in Xavier's own words: Coleridge's 1872 editorial narration in his *Life and Letters of St. Francis Xavier*, Vol. 1 (c. line 3509) records the early companions' spiritual reading including "the Imitation of Christ," and a footnote there (c. lines 4531–4536), citing Alcazar, records Ignatius giving away copies of "the book de Contemptu Mundi, i.e. the Imitation of Christ."

### B2 — Ecology

Devotional, biographical, epistolary, and chronicle registers together — a genuine multi-pillar structure across eleven vendored files, the strongest generic range of any Era 6 candidate in this batch. Arthur's own 1906 and 1908 translations of the *Chronicle of Mount St Agnes* and Zerbolt's *Spiritual Ascent* are both now vendored.

Real gaps remain even so. The census's own `sourcing`/`voices` fields name material still not vendored: customaries, the Sisters' houses' own lives (Deventer, Diepenveen) in their own voice, rapiaria beyond Kempis's own, and Van Engen's *Basic Writings* (in copyright). The women's side of the movement in particular is named by the census as its own voice ("no single founding figure holds the women's side... the Sisters' houses... recorded their lives collectively"). The *Chronicle of Mount St Agnes* now records the Diepenveen Sisters' own investiture (1408) and other sisters' houses, but only from the canons' side — no vendored text carries the Sisters' own voice directly. The movement's own institutional decline (per the census's `legacy` field) is likewise undocumented in the vendored corpus. Both are real, disclosed gaps for Doc_02: the earlier Tier 1 census finding (§0) rests in part on this wider material, most of it now vendored, but the women's-own-voice gap and the institutional-decline gap remain open.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no overlap, over a millennium's separation. **Versus this batch's other two Era 6 candidates:** no figure or text overlap with Lollardy or the Hussite movement. **The real, distinctive item: an influence relationship with two other worlds in this same overall batch.** The census's own `relationsSummary` states it directly: "Parent-influence on both Luther's schooling and Jesuit pedagogy - a rare both-directions ancestor." Lutheran Wittenberg is "Built & Live" (`Build/worlds/witt`), a live world, not merely a source-ready candidate. The Luther/Erasmus direction of this influence is contested in scholarship (§3 B1) — "documented" is too strong; "claimed by the census, contested in the wider literature" is more accurate. Neither Wittenberg's nor the Society of Jesus's own corpus-map entries currently cross-reference this candidate, and no shared vendored text links them directly — an influence relationship, not a text-sharing one — worth naming explicitly, with the Luther direction's own contested status disclosed, in whichever Doc_02 runs for any of the three worlds.

### B4 — Audiences

The interior, devotional register this Era 6 batch otherwise lacks. Lollardy and the Hussite movement are both, at root, protest movements against ecclesiastical authority. This candidate is not a movement that never challenged church authority — it submitted to that authority under real pressure (a suspended preaching license, prelates' opposition) rather than breaking from it, a real distinction in kind from open dissent, but not an absence of friction (see §2 A1). Its appeal remains distinct: personal spiritual formation and interior practice over institutional conflict as the movement's own center of gravity, even though real conflict with authority did occur.

### B5 — Scale

The vendored *Founders* itself (c. line 3793) records the communities spreading beyond the Low Countries to "Westphalia and Saxony" — and this document's own §1 places Luther's Brethren-linked household at Magdeburg, in Saxony. A single-region claim is too narrow; the movement's own core was the Low Countries, but its reach was not confined there.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A carrying forward the census's own existing Tier 1 finding (§0), on an absence of Trinitarian/Christological complication and a full account of the movement's own relationship to authority. Clears Sourcing with a genuine multi-pillar structure across eleven vendored files (devotional, biographical, epistolary, and chronicle registers), on an accurate Groote letter count, Groote's own English-language voice in *Founders*, primary status for Groote's Latin letters under Mark's own library ruling, and the Imitation's own disputed authorship — **with real disclosed gaps that remain even after the Zerbolt and Chronicle vendoring**: customaries, rapiaria beyond Kempis's own, Van Engen's in-copyright *Basic Writings*, and the women's side in its own voice, which the census's own earlier Tier 1 finding rests on in part. A real, distinctive cross-world relevance, reaching into a Built & Live world (Wittenberg, with the Luther direction contested) and a source-ready one (the Jesuits, with a real vendored anchor in Coleridge's Xavier volumes).

**Tier 1, carrying forward the census's own existing rating.** No unresolved floor-adjacent complication, and this document's own sourcing picture is genuinely multi-pillar and strong, across eleven vendored files. Not unconditional, though: the wider unvendored material (§3 B2 — customaries, the women's side in its own voice, Van Engen's *Basic Writings*) that the earlier census finding rests on in part is a real, disclosed ecology gap, not simply "one Groote translation item" — real, but not disqualifying, and it needs real acquisition/assessment work before Doc_02 rather than being assumed covered by what's vendored today.
