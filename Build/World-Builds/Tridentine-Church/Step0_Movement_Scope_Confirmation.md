## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears

Trent's own canons do not explicitly reaffirm the Nicene Creed and Chalcedonian Christology by name. Session III of the vendored Waterworth Canons/Decrees (c. lines 12320–12375) recites the full Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed but calls it only "the Symbol of faith which the holy Roman Church makes use of" — in Session III, "Nicaea" appears solely in a footnote citation. The decrees do name Nicaea in body text twice elsewhere: Session XIII, ch. VI cites "the age of the Council of Nicaea" on reserving the Eucharist, and Session XXV cites "the second Synod of Nicaea" on images — neither reaffirms the creed doctrinally by name. "Chalcedon" appears in the decrees exactly once, as a disciplinary citation on ordination (Session XXIII, ch. XVI), never as a doctrinal reaffirmation by name. This evidence still supports the floor clearance: the Session III creed recital matches Article 4's five commitments almost clause for clause.

This candidate is not the reference point every other candidate in this batch is measured against. Article 4's own floor is drawn from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (381, filioque-free) and tests "belief, not institutional submission to any council's authority." Trent's own Session III creed recital includes the filioque ("proceedeth from the Father and the Son") — a different text from the floor's own wording, not identical to it. Trent must clear Article 4 like any other candidate (which it does, on the evidence above) — treating it as the batch's own doctrinal yardstick would import a Catholic-normative lens into a portfolio that includes built Protestant worlds. This is a considered clearance like every other candidate's; it does not claim the floor question could never arise.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing: strong across doctrine, institutional narrative, and personal/pastoral voice

Per the corpus-map, directly verified against the vendored files: the Council's own Canons and Decrees (Waterworth, preface 1848, role: tradition — this world's own defining conciliar voice); the Roman Catechism (McHugh & Callan, 1923, role: tradition); Paolo Sarpi's *History of the Council of Trent* (English ed. 1676, role: context — see §4 item 2); and Thomas Barlow's *Brutum Fulmen* (1681), split into two corpus-map rows — the two embedded bull texts (Pius V's *Regnans in Excelsis* and Paul III's bull against Henry VIII) as role: tradition, Barlow's own dedicatory epistle, epistle to the reader, and running commentary as role: context (see §5 for the Paul III bull's actual language). Word counts across these four files run to ~1.22M raw OCR words, inflated by OCR noise and Latin columns.

A further ten files are now mapped to this world. Pole's own PD English oration is vendored: *The Seditious and Blasphemous Oration of Cardinal Pole*, trans. Fabian Wythers (1560, item `bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_the-seditious-oration_pole-reginald_1560`), role: tradition. The scan is a garbled black-letter 1560 print; it stays second witness under Mark's OCR ruling until a cleaner edition is vendored — a scan-quality classification, not a language one. Pole's correspondence also survives in Italian and English, not only in Latin. Pole's own role is more than merely "implementing": per the Waterworth essay (c. line 3664), the Session II legates' exhortation was "composed by Cardinal Pole" — one of the three presiding legates at Trent's opening. An independent check of the item's own archive.org metadata (queried directly, 2026-09-25) finds no `possible-copyright-status`, `licenseurl`, or `rights` field set, and no `access-restricted-item` flag (the item is not access-restricted); PD-by-date is sound on its own given the 1560 publication date. This is a positive rights check on this specific item, not the pending cross-world EEBO rights re-check, which Doc_02 should cite directly once it exists.

Borromeo's *Acta Ecclesiae Mediolanensis* (1599 Latin printing) is vendored, role: tradition — a clean scan. No PD English translation of Borromeo's own writing exists (the possible 1857 Wigley/Dolman lead remains unlocated on archive.org), but under Mark's 2026-09-25 Library ruling that an original-language source can be primary evidence directly, this Latin text is Borromeo's own primary voice, not a gap.

The census-named liturgical books are vendored: the Roman Breviary (Bute, 1908, role: tradition) — one of four seasonal volumes, reflecting the Breviary's later revision through Leo XIII, a caveat that still applies — and the Roman Missal, trans. England (1843, role: tradition), in full.

Pius V's own apostolic letters (*Apostolicarum ... Epistolarum*, five books, Latin, 1640, role: tradition) are vendored — real primary papal correspondence beyond the single Barlow-transmitted bull. Four Bellarmine works are vendored in English translation, role: tradition: *The Eternal Happiness of the Saints* (Dalton trans., 19th c.), *The Mind's Ascent to God by a Ladder of Created Things* (1925), *The Soul's Ascension to God, by the Steps of Creation* (Hall trans., 1703), and *The Joys of the Blessed* (Foxton trans., 1722, bound with an Addison essay that is context, not Bellarmine's own voice). A fifth vendored file, filed under the same filename slug (`bellarmine_notes-of-the-church_1688.txt`), is not Bellarmine's own work: it is a 1687-88 Church of England tract series refuting his *Notes of the Church* one by one, opponent voice (role: context), not this world's own tradition.

This world's corpus-map now carries fourteen mapped files.
