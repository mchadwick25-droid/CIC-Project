## S1 in detail — the replacement strand finding does not survive Article 21

Revision 2 replaces the Round 1 two-strand finding with a new partition. Strand A is "Individually-argued" (Brute, Purvey, Thorpe). Strand B is "Communal-access" (the Twelve Conclusions, Badby, Norwich, Amersham). It is framed as a difference in how authority is grounded, not in status or sequence. The disclaimer of sequential reasoning is sound. The evidence doesn't carry the finding.

1. **The criterion in practice is what kind of record survived, not how authority was grounded.** The document itself says the asymmetry "is a fact about which kind of record the trial process happened to produce and preserve." Badby is placed in Strand B because he "was briefly examined and burned … without leaving a sustained personal composition." But Foxe's account examines and condemns Badby individually, for his own individually held eucharistic view (19229 ff.). Lack of a surviving composition is a fact about preservation. It is not evidence that Badby's authority was grounded communally. This is the Round 1 S1 defect in a new form: a register or genre-of-record difference presented as divergent authority structure.
2. **Strand B's defining trait is evidenced by a document that does not address it.** Strand B is "authority grounded in … shared, direct access to Scripture in English." Its lead evidence is the Twelve Conclusions. §5 of this same revision correctly states that none of the twelve addresses vernacular Scripture (Round 1 S7). The Conclusions are also a sustained, argued doctrinal text. They are anonymous, but anonymity is not communal grounding in Scripture-access.
3. **Purvey's evidence is a recantation, described as a defense.** The vendored text is headed `CONFESSIO     ET    REVOCATIO … DE SUIS  ERRORIBUS  ET  H.F,RESIBUS` (30600–07). It is a formal abjuration, and Foxe lists Purvey among those who "did shrink … revolt and renounce" (20316–20). The document places it under "authority … defended directly before ecclesiastical authority" and says Purvey "personally composed" it. Nothing in the vendored text shows he composed the formula. Separately, "a priest who led the Wycliffite Bible's own revision through the 1390s" carries no confidence tag. Purvey's role in the Later Version is a traditional attribution that modern scholarship disputes, so at minimum it needs a Contested tag.
4. **Two of Strand B's four members rest on material the document says it has not checked.** Norwich is described as prosecuting "households and companies as such, examined and prosecuted collectively," yet §2 states that Tanner's volume "has not been vendored or independently content-checked." Amersham is a single forward-reference sentence (S3). The closing tag "Documented for each named figure's own … mode of engagement with authority" therefore overclaims for Strand B.

**Required fix:** re-run strand determination once more on recurring-ecology evidence (Art. 21). The finding must be supported by what the vendored record actually shows about how authority was grounded, not by which kind of document survived. As at Round 1, this review doesn't prescribe the outcome. A strand-singular finding, with the individual/communal and learned/household differences carried as internal plurality under Art. 16, is open. So is a two-strand finding, if the evidence supports it. Whichever is chosen, revise §4, the Strand references in §3, §5 Cells 2B and 3B, and §10 item 4 to match. If Revision 3 cannot close this on the vendored evidence, it is an unresolved tension the pipeline can't close on its own, and should be escalated rather than taken to a fourth round.

## New findings introduced by Revision 2

**N1 (substantial — sourcing conclusion). The two Arnold notes are one dating of one double tract, not two datings of two tracts.** §2 says the corpus corroborates the May 1382 dating "in two separate editorial notes, each dating a different tract," one a headnote to "Seven Werkys of Mercy Bodyly" and the other "a separate footnote to a tract on the friars' denial that tithes are pure alms." In the vendored volume, the headnote (10737–43) introduces "the interesting double tract which follows." The footnote at 11189 sits inside that same tract, before the "Gostly" half begins at line 11291. The passage it glosses is the tract's own Cap. VI discussion of the friars. The headnote's own cross-reference, "(See p. 175.)", points to the page (header at line 11149) that carries that footnote. So Arnold gives one dating, derived from one allusion in one work. The headnote relies on the footnote. The revision presents this as two independent datings of separate works, which overstates the corroboration. Fix: describe it as Arnold's dating of the double tract, grounded in its allusion to the earthquake council, with both line references.

(The cross-reference also shows that Round 1's own wording, "Arnold dates two tracts," was imprecise. The revision followed it. The defect is still in the document.)

No other new substantial defect was found. §5 stays at Layer 1: the four questions are answered, and the six-cell sketch is tagged Layer-1-only.
