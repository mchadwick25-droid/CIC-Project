## (c) New findings from fresh adversarial pass

**N1 — Cosmetic — Section 11 open-items list numbering is broken.** In source order the items are numbered **1, 7, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6**. The white-martyrdom item added under Finding 3 was labeled "7" but dropped into the second physical slot. Markdown auto-renumbering will paper over this on render, but the raw list is visibly disordered and the "7" is an artifact of the Finding 3 insertion. Renumber to 1–7 in sequence.

**N2 — Cosmetic — the Finding 6 spelling fix was applied to the schism but not to its eponym.** Sections 4 and 8.2 name the founder "Meletius of Lycopolis" (with an "e") while calling the movement "Melitian" (with an "i"). The standard Egyptian spelling that matches the corrected "Melitian" is "Melitius of Lycopolis" (this is the Wikipedia and common scholarly rendering for the Egyptian bishop; "Meletius" is the Antiochene spelling the document itself is trying to distinguish from). The eponym should read "Melitius of Lycopolis" for internal consistency with the very distinction Finding 6 was meant to establish. Sources do vary on the bishop's name, so this is defensible-but-inconsistent rather than outright wrong — hence cosmetic.

**N3 — Cosmetic — Rubenson original-title imprecision (same class as Finding 2).** Section 10 says the Rubenson work was "originally published as *Origenist Theology, Monastic Tradition and the Making of a Saint*, Lund University Press, 1990." The actual 1990 Lund edition's full title is *The Letters of St. Antony: Origenist Theology, Monastic Tradition and the Making of a Saint* — i.e., the main title "The Letters of St. Antony" was **identical** across both editions; only the subtitle changed (from "Origenist Theology, Monastic Tradition and the Making of a Saint" to "Monasticism and the Making of a Saint"). The document frames it as a whole-title change. This is precisely the bibliographic slip-class Round 1 flagged for Brakke and would ideally be corrected to "originally published with the subtitle *Origenist Theology, Monastic Tradition and the Making of a Saint*."

**N4 — Cosmetic/low — population-figure attribution.** Section 2.2 states "Palladius reports Nitria and Kellia together numbering in the range of five thousand ascetics." Palladius's ~5,000 figure (*Lausiac History* 7) is standardly given for **Nitria alone**; Kellia's own figure is much smaller. Attributing the 5,000 to the two settlements "together" is a minor imprecision (and errs conservatively). The claim is already heavily hedged as "an order-of-magnitude indicator from an interested witness," so the practical harm is negligible, but the attribution should read "Nitria" rather than "Nitria and Kellia together."

No substantial new findings. Strand Determination (three strands — anchoritic / cenobitic / semi-anchoritic — with authority-structure evidence for each) matches the Framework's Article 21 criteria and the standard scholarly typology; strand is presented as a finding, not a presupposed schema, per the Framework. Preliminary Forces covers all four Framework questions (generated / embedded-in / responding-to / refusing). Author Gravity discipline is handled unusually well and flagged forward.
