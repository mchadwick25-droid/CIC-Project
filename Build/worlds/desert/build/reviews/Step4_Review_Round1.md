## A. SUBSTANTIAL findings

**S1 — `desert.limit.f6-t-divorce-and-outsiders` declares a content gap that this corpus's own vendored Palladius fills at least five times over, in the compiled `statement` field, and never opened Palladius to check.** The record's `statement` (compiled verbatim into `build_prompt()`'s "Honest limit (F6-T)" segment):

> "And marriage is simply not a subject we speak to. Almost none of us who left a record had married."

and `why_sources_cannot_answer`:

> "…**no source addresses marriage's ending at all** — consistent with a corpus whose central figures are almost uniformly unmarried ascetics."

Its only `sources[]` entry is the *Vita*, with locus "the Vita's own silence on marriage's ending…". `desert.source.palladius-lausiac-history` is vendored, public-domain, registered, and cited by this very step (`desert.story.pachomius-founding`). I searched the whole file for every occurrence of `marri*`, `wife`, `husband`, `wedding`. Marriage is not merely "a subject we speak to" — it is the entire subject of one chapter and the hinge of several others:

- **ch. VIII (Amoun of Nitria)** is a sustained marriage narrative: Amoun is "constrained by his uncle to marry a wife," goes through "all the marriage rites," argues his bride into continence on the wedding night, "**lived in the same house with her eighteen years**," ate with her nightly, separated only at her own initiative, and afterwards "used to see that blessed lady his wife twice each year."
- **ch. XXII (Paul the Simple)** is a marriage *ending*, narrated in direct speech: Paul finds his wife with her lover and says "**By Jesus, I'll take her no longer. Go, you have her and her children, for I am going to become a monk**" — and then walks to Antony. This is the single closest piece of material this corpus holds to F6-T-03 ("What did your people hold about a marriage ending — could someone divorced belong…?"), and it involves this world's own founding figure.
- **ch. XLIV (Innocent)** — "he renounced the world, **leaving his marriage**, by which he had also a son."
- **ch. LXI (Melania the Younger)** — "married at thirteen and having lived with her husband seven years," her "great hatred of marriage," and the negotiated continence with Pinianus.
- **ch. XLVI/LIV (Melania the Elder)**, **ch. LXVI (Verus and Bosporia)**, **ch. LVI (Olympias)** all turn on marital status.

Two of the three sentences the record commits to compiled output are false against a file one directory away, and the third ("Almost none of us who left a record had married") is false against ch. VIII, ch. XXII, ch. XLIV and chs. LXI/LXVI. The narrow claim actually available — *the corpus preserves no ruling or teaching on remarriage after divorce* — is defensible and is not what the record says. This is the most serious finding in the round because an `honest_limit` is the one record type whose whole job is to be accurate about an absence, and because `classify_cell` treats F6-T as *covered* on the strength of it.
