## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

John Chrysostom's own doctrinal writing is squarely, uncontroversially Nicene-Chalcedonian-orthodox on every point the surviving corpus speaks to — his Trinitarian and Christological positions were never themselves the subject of conciliar condemnation, in his own lifetime or after. The Nestorian controversy itself erupts in 428–429 (Nestorius's own preaching against the title *Theotokos*, soon after his 428 elevation to Constantinople) and runs through Cyril's own Twelve Anathemas (November 430), both inside this world's own 350–430 window; only the Council of Ephesus's own formal condemnation of Nestorius (431) falls one year past the window's own close. This does not touch A1's own clearance for Chrysostom himself, who died in 407, well before any of this. **A1 clears directly** for Chrysostom and for the window's own Native corpus generally.

**A boundary caution, named rather than smoothed over:** Theodoret of Cyrrhus, already carried on this world's corpus-map at `role: tradition`, sits at this world's own outer edge in a doctrinally live way, not merely a chronological one — he died c. 457 and wrote his *Ecclesiastical History* c. 449, after the Nestorian controversy. The corpus-map carries *The Anathemas of Cyril with the Counter-statements of Theodoret*, and the Second Council of Constantinople (553) — vendored, `npnf214` — explicitly anathematizes, by name, "the impious writings of Theodoret, directed against the true faith and against the first holy Synod of Ephesus and against St. Cyril and his XII. Anathemas" (canon XIII, `cic:npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml:xii.vii`, quoted verbatim). Theodoret was himself personally rehabilitated at Chalcedon (451, outside this window); the Counter-statements specifically, carried on this world's own corpus-map, were condemned by name a century later. The corpus-map carries his *Eranistes* and *Letters* at `confidence: assigned`, and the *Ecclesiastical History* and the Counter-statements at `provisional`. None of this resolves Theodoret's own fit question. It stays a live boundary question for Doc_01 and Doc_02 (section 5 of the Source Readiness Dossier carries it), and it is stated with the condemnation disclosed, not denied.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

Per the Methodology, a movement's historical rivals are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under Section A. They appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates.

Chrysostom's own surviving corpus names several rivals, each routed here rather than separately screened:

- **The Anomoeans (Eunomians).** The corpus-map entry for Chrysostom's *Homily on the Paralytic* records that the homily proves the equality of Father and Son against the Anomoeans, and its note states that the census now carries an entry for exactly the party this material opposes. That is a separate, already-identified census candidate, not re-screened here. Per A5, this world's own material about the Anomoeans stays here, as Chrysostom's own opponents in his own reconstruction; the Anomoeans' own eligibility, if ever pursued, is that separate census entry's own question.
- **Marcionites and Manichaeans.** Chrysostom's own *Homily Against Marcionists and Manichaeans* (already assigned, corpus-map row) is, per that row's note, material about the two doctrinal-floor movements it argues against. Both fail Article 4's floor outright on their own confessions (Marcion's rejection of the Hebrew scriptures and denial of Christ's real humanity; Manichaeism's dualist cosmology), so neither is a live A1 question of its own; they appear here only as this world's own named opponents.
- **Jewish communities of Antioch, and "Judaizing" Christians.** This is not a rival Christian movement and is not routed by A5 at all — it is a real, serious, and separate disclosure question this document names here rather than deferring silently, because of its own ethical weight, not because A5 assigns it here mechanically. Chrysostom delivered a set of homilies at Antioch, commonly known by their traditional Latin title *Adversus Judaeos* ("Against the Jews"). This document states only what is independently, generally documented and confirmed directly against this corpus: the homilies exist and are addressed, on their traditional title, to the relationship between the Antiochene church and the city's own Jewish community. **Dating is Contested, not settled:** the `npnf109` apparatus (`cic:npnf109_chrysostom-priesthood-ascetic-homilies-statutes.xml:xix.ii`, verified) dates only one homily — "a certain discourse against the Jews" — to September 386, and its own text openly discusses a Paschal-table dating difficulty rather than treating that date as settled for the series as a whole. Separately, Socrates' own *Ecclesiastical History* (`cic:npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml:ii.ix.iv`, verified, the same chapter already drawn on for Chrysostom's education) states that "while he continued in the capacity of a reader" — that is, before his diaconate under Meletius — "he composed the book *Against the Jews*," which would place at least one work by this title earlier than 386. This document does not adjudicate between these two datings or assume they describe the same work. These homilies are a documented, historically significant, and troubling part of Chrysostom's own corpus and later reception — including a documented history of being invoked in later antisemitic rhetoric, a reception history this world's own build must not repeat by handling the material uncritically, in either direction. **Confirmed directly against this corpus: the primary text of these homilies is not currently vendored anywhere in `cic/texts/`** — the `npnf109` editorial apparatus discussed above, and Socrates' own brief historical mention that such a work exists (`ii.ix.iv-p2`, above), are both present, but neither is the primary text itself; no NPNF volume in this vendored set includes it (the 19th-century NPNF Chrysostom selection is documented to have omitted this material). This is named as a binding disclosure and acquisition obligation for Doc_02 (§4 below), not resolved here — this world's build must not proceed to characterize this material's specific content, tone, target, or theological argument beyond what is stated above until the primary text itself is vendored and can be read and quoted directly, per this project's own quote-verification discipline.

Strand Determination (Article 21) is Step 1's own work, flagged forward to Doc_01, not resolved here.

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Exceptionally strong, confirmed directly against the library package rather than assumed from the census note. `cic/corpus-map/antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml` currently holds 50 work-rows. The six vendored NPNF volumes (`npnf109`–`npnf114`) hold about 3.34 million words on disk, counted two ways: `wc -w` gives 3,339,085 under the `POSIX` locale (3,357,623 under `C.UTF-8`), and a whitespace split in Python 3 on the text with XML tags stripped gives 3,035,294. That is one of the largest single-figure holdings in this project's corpus. 34 of the rows are Chrysostom's own voice at `role: tradition`, overwhelmingly `confidence: assigned` — continuous homiletic commentary on Matthew (the largest single series: 535,125 words by `wc -w` under `POSIX`, 484,393 with tags stripped), John, Acts, Romans, the Corinthian and Pauline epistles, and Hebrews, together with the *Statues* homilies, the *De Sacerdotio*, and his exile correspondence.

Named honestly rather than left implicit: this is not the whole of Chrysostom's authentic output. The dossier's own §1 confirms this vendored set represents roughly 60–70% of his translatable corpus — missing the 67 Homilies on Genesis, the Psalms and Isaiah commentaries, and scattered freestanding moral homilies untranslated in NPNF, in addition to the *Adversus Judaeos* material named in §2 (A5) above. **Passes B1 comfortably**, with both the scale of what survives and the specific, named gaps in what's vendored carried forward as binding Doc_02 disclosures.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

The Step 0 Conclusion's own prior finding — "rich ordinary-lay ethical-formation material" — is, if anything, understated relative to what the vendored corpus actually shows. Chrysostom's homilies are not abstract doctrinal argument; they are sustained, practical moral exhortation addressed to an ordinary urban congregation's actual daily life — wealth and its use, family relationships, entertainment (the hippodrome, the theatre), civic behavior, almsgiving — genre after genre of it, across essentially the entire assigned corpus. This gives real, direct material to Formation Ecology and Meaning Transmission in a way this project's other elite-authored worlds (World #6, and `roman-church-gregorian`, both administrative/institutional in register) do not match. Worship Integration has real material (Chrysostom's own reputation and liturgical influence, and the *Statues* homilies' own occasion — a series of twenty-one sermons preached to a terrified congregation after the 387 riot, per the corpus-map's own note, a direct record of preaching as a communal, worship-embedded event). Organizational/Authority material is present but not this world's own center of gravity (contrast World #6): the *De Sacerdotio* treats priestly office theologically and pastorally, not primarily jurisdictionally, and the exile correspondence (to Innocent of Rome, to Olympias) evidences church-state conflict without this world's own build centering on it the way World #6's does. **Passes B2 comfortably**, arguably the strongest ecological fit of any candidate this document is aware of in the current portfolio for ordinary-believer formation material specifically.

### B3 — Uniqueness (differentiation) — the condition this document must actually resolve

This is where the Step 0 Conclusion's own deferral bites, and this section does the actual work the prior cycle left undone.

**The real test, run against `lpc`'s own Doc_04 — real, but not settled, and this document does not overstate it.** `lpc`'s own Doc_04 — the Framework's formal, six-test gravity-discovery process, revised across eleven rounds without ever clearing, disposed Approved to proceed on the project lead's own direct instruction, with open findings this document inherits rather than papers over — classifies eight candidate gravities for that world. Four are **Primary**: *Pastoral Office as Territorial Flock-Keeping*, *Penitential Discipline: the Reintegration of the Failed*, *Collegial Communion Preserved Despite Disagreement*, and *Sacramental/Ordination Validity Across the Boundary of the Church*. **Candidate 4, *Preaching and Catechesis as Primary Formation Mode*, is classified Supporting, not Primary** (tested in `lpc` Doc_04's own §3, classified in its own §4) — that document finds it passes the Repetition, Formation and Persistence tests but passes the Dependency, Explanatory and Interaction tests only narrowly, and it labels the classification provisional, not a final, closed finding (the project lead's ruling on that document concerned a different candidate, Conciliar Authority, not this one). This is the real, though not fully settled, fact this document's argument rests on: `lpc`'s own construction process tested exactly the material one might assume thin, found it real and substantial, and *provisionally* found it Supporting rather than Primary for that world — because what that document's own Primary gravities describe is territorial pastoral office under recurring crisis, penitential reintegration of the lapsed, collegial communion across schism, and sacramental validity, not the exegetical method by which any of that gets preached. **This document treats that finding as real, credible evidence for the contrast below — not as a closed, cross-checkable fact this world's own build can simply cite as settled.**

**`lpc`'s own Doc_01 (§7) anticipates and frames this exact test, rather than this document reaching past it.** Its §7 names that world's recurring pattern as a mode of pastoral office and formation practice (territorial, penitential, catechetical, exercised on an ordinary congregation under recurring external crisis, and oriented toward readmitting the compromised rather than policing a purity boundary), not merely as Latin, North African and third-to-fifth-century. It does so, in its own words, so that a future Antiochene build's points of resonance or divergence can be tested against substance rather than against geography and language alone. `lpc`'s own build left this test for a future Antiochene candidate to run; this document is the first attempt to run it, not an intrusion on settled territory.

**Run against that real standard — engaging, not omitting, the one real complication this world's own build itself surfaced.** Chrysostom's own ministry at Antioch (386–397) is not shaped by a Decian-persecution-scale lapsed-and-readmission crisis the way Cyprian's decade is; his own corpus shows no rebaptism or sacramental-validity controversy comparable to the Donatist schism Augustine's own ministry is permanently shaped by. **The collegial-communion-under-schism gravity requires more care:** Doc_01 §2 documents that Chrysostom himself lived through exactly this kind of episode — the Meletian schism at Antioch, in which he refused communion with the rival claimant Paulinus and spent three years in retirement rather than join either party. This is real, and this document does not pretend otherwise. **The schism's own ground is not only collegial:** Socrates' own *Ecclesiastical History* (`cic:npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml:ii.v.xliv`, verified directly) states that the homoousian party at Antioch would not communicate with Meletius's own adherents specifically "because Meletius had been ordained by the Arians, and his adherents had been baptized by them" — a validity-of-ordination-and-baptism-across-a-boundary dispute, not merely a personality or jurisdictional one. That is the specific ground `lpc`'s own Candidate 6 (Sacramental/Ordination Validity Across the Boundary of the Church) tests for, not only Candidate 3 (Collegial Communion). So this world's own Meletian material touches two of `lpc`'s own four Primary gravities, not one. What distinguishes it from either is that neither concern is, on the current evidence, an organizing concern of Chrysostom's own preaching corpus the way collegial communion is of Cyprian's own correspondence or ordination validity is of the Donatist controversy Augustine's own ministry is shaped by — it shapes his own biography, not the compositional structure or recurring subject matter of his homilies — but this is a claim this document's own future Doc_04 must actually test against the evidence, not one Step 0 can assert past a real counter-example it already knows about. What *does* organize Chrysostom's own corpus by its own compositional shape (§3, B1: essentially the entire assigned corpus is continuous, verse-by-verse exposition of one NT book after another) is the sustained exegetical-homiletic method itself. **This is offered here as a testable hypothesis for this world's own future Doc_04, not a finding this Step 0 or Doc_01 is positioned to make** — and that future Doc_04 must decide the specific question `lpc`'s own Candidate 4 finding actually turned on: whether sustained exposition functions here as an independently organizing force, the way `lpc` found its own four Primary gravities do, or as the medium through which some other, more organizing concern gets taught — `lpc`'s own stated reason for classifying Preaching and Catechesis Supporting rather than Primary. This document does not yet know which answer this world's own evidence supports; it names the test, rather than pre-judging it. What this document can responsibly claim now is narrower than "passes" any criterion outright: the same broad activity (preaching on scripture) is real and substantial in both worlds' own corpora, but `lpc`'s own provisional Doc_04 finding treats it as Supporting there, while this world's own compositional evidence makes it a serious, testable Primary-gravity candidate here — a genuine, real contrast worth building on, not a settled one, and independent of geography or language either way.
