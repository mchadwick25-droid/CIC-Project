## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** Antiochene Exegetical Christianity (Chrysostom-centered).

**Short Description:** The formation of ordinary Christian life at Antioch, and later Constantinople, through sustained, plain-sense exposition of scripture — a presbyter and later bishop expounding a biblical text verse by verse and drawing from it a concrete ethical demand, addressed to a real congregation's real conduct: wealth and its use, civic behavior, family life, and, at moments of acute crisis (a riot against the emperor's own statues in 387; his own later exile), the congregation's fear and grief directly.

**Core Identity:** This is a world formed by a method of reading, not primarily by an office or a jurisdictional claim. Its formation logic is Antiochene historical-grammatical exegesis — reading scripture in its plain, literal, historical sense rather than the allegorical mode associated with Alexandria — put to sustained, practical, ethical use for an ordinary urban congregation, across about 3.34 million words on disk in the six vendored NPNF volumes (counted in October 2026 over the six files `npnf109` to `npnf114`: `wc -w` gives 3,332,586 under the `POSIX` locale and 3,351,124 under `C.UTF-8`; a whitespace split in Python 3, after each XML tag is replaced by a space, gives 3,032,271; the `wc -w` counts include markup and every count includes editorial apparatus, so none counts Chrysostom's own prose alone), nearly all of it continuous scriptural exposition rather than administrative or doctrinal-controversial writing.

**Distinctive Contribution.** `Build/worlds/lpc/` (World #8, Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity) is a complete, nine-document build with Living Tradition Status confirmed. No other confirmed or in-progress world in this project's current portfolio reconstructs Christian formation through the specific mechanism of continuous, plain-sense scriptural preaching as a testable Primary-gravity candidate. This is a substantively different claim from World #8's own contribution: `lpc`'s own completed Doc_04 finds its four Primary gravities to be territorial pastoral office, penitential reintegration of the lapsed, collegial communion preserved across schism, and sacramental/ordination validity — with *Preaching and Catechesis as Primary Formation Mode* itself tested and found merely Supporting there. This world reconstructs formation organized, on its own corpus's own compositional shape, around the interpretive method itself, independent of the office that happens to deliver it — a hypothesis for this world's own future Doc_04, tested against `lpc`'s own real record, per `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3 (B3), which this document does not redecide. The escalation this finding requires (testing and potentially overturning an already-built sibling world's own portfolio-level deferral) is named at Step 0 §3/§6, not self-disposed here.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed. Per this project's established practice (`Build/worlds/ijc/Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` §1), Living Tradition Status confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform. Noted for the record, not decided here: John Chrysostom is venerated across Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Roman Catholic, and Anglican traditions alike, and is one of the three Ecumenical Teachers and Hierarchs of Eastern Orthodoxy (with Basil the Great and Gregory of Nazianzus) — an unusually broad, cross-traditional living continuity by this project's own standards, though whether and how to name it is the project lead's own call. **Status: PENDING.**

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** c. 350–430 CE.

- **Beginning point (c. 350):** the census's own window reaches back to roughly Chrysostom's own birth (c. 349, per the standard biographical record) and the generation of Antiochene Christian and civic life immediately preceding his formation — including, within this window, the emperor Julian's own residence at Antioch (362–363), already carried on this world's own corpus-map (`ammianus-marcellinus_roman-history_yonge1862`) as `context`: a pagan eyewitness's account of the burning of the Temple of Apollo at Daphne and Julian's retaliatory closure of Antioch's chief church, a formative civic memory of church-state hostility a generation before Chrysostom's own ministry there. This is a defensible beginning point because it captures the civic and religious climate that formed the Antioch Chrysostom was himself later formed by and preached in, not merely his own birth year in isolation.
- **Ending point (430):** twenty-three years after Chrysostom's own death (407), and one year before the Council of Ephesus (431) formally condemns Nestorius — the census's own stated boundary. This window does not, in fact, close before the Nestorian controversy itself, which erupts in 428–429 and runs through Cyril's own Twelve Anathemas (November 430), both inside this window; only Ephesus's own formal verdict falls one year past it. The boundary was not drawn to avoid the controversy — it simply ends where the census says it does, and Chrysostom himself (d. 407) predates all of it regardless.

**Geographic Centers:** Antioch, primarily; Constantinople, secondarily, during Chrysostom's own episcopate (397–404) and exile (404–407).

- **Antioch:** ranked third behind Rome and Alexandria on an older reckoning, though by this world's own 350–430 window Constantinople's own rapid growth as imperial capital is Widely Accepted to have overtaken it, a major center of Hellenistic urban culture, rhetorical education, and — directly relevant to this world's own required disclosure (Step 0 §2, A5) — a large, socially established Jewish community whose continued attraction for some Antiochene Christians is the documented occasion for the *Adversus Judaeos* homilies, a source this world's own build does not currently hold (Step 0 §4.2).
- **Constantinople:** the site of Chrysostom's own episcopate and the church-state confrontations (the Eutropius affair; the conflict with the Empress Eudoxia; his eventual deposition and exile) already correctly carried, per the existing corpus-map's own "second id" pattern, as material this world shares with `imperial-juridical-christianity` rather than this world's own primary geography.

**Cultural Environment:** Greek-speaking, rhetorically sophisticated urban late antiquity. Chrysostom's own formation is grounded directly in a vendored primary source: Socrates Scholasticus's own *Ecclesiastical History* (`npnf202`, already carried on this world's corpus-map as context, Book VI, Chapter III, "Birth and Education of John Bishop of Constantinople"), verified word for word (`cic:npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml:ii.ix.iv`): *"John was a native of Antioch in Syria-Cœle, son of Secundus and Anthusa, and scion of a noble family in that country. He studied rhetoric under Libanius the sophist, and philosophy under Andragathius the philosopher."* The same chapter, verified at `ii.ix.iv-p2`, records that two of Chrysostom's own fellow-students under Libanius (Theodore, later bishop of Mopsuestia, and Maximus of Seleucia) "entered upon the ascetic life, under the guidance of Diodorus" (Diodore of Tarsus), whose own exegetical method the NPNF editor's endnote describes as seeking a literal and historical meaning in preference to the allegorical and mystical interpretations that Origen and the Alexandrians attached to the Old Testament. **A careful distinction, not to be overstated:** Socrates' own text names Theodore and Maximus, not Chrysostom by name, as Diodorus's own direct ascetic disciples in this specific passage; Chrysostom's own association with Diodore's own school is Widely Accepted in the broader scholarship but is not independently re-verified against a primary text naming Chrysostom himself — named here as the honest boundary of what this specific vendored source establishes, not smoothed into a claim it does not make. This dual formation (classical rhetorical training, then Christian ascetic and exegetical vocation) is directly legible in the surviving homilies' own rhetorical craft.

**Historical Pressures:**
- **The Meletian schism.** Antioch's own church was divided for decades over rival claimants to its see — Meletius elected 360, the rival Paulinus consecrated 362, per the standard historical record. Chrysostom's own life is directly caught up in it, per Socrates' own *Ecclesiastical History* (`cic:npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml:ii.ix.iv`, verified): ordained a reader, then made deacon by Meletius himself; then, on Meletius's own death, deliberately refusing communion with the rival claimant Paulinus and spending three years in a self-imposed retirement rather than join either party; only later ordained presbyter by Evagrius, Paulinus's own successor, on Socrates' own specific account. **An alternative account exists, grounded directly rather than left vague:** the same vendored NPNF109 volume's own prolegomena (`cic:npnf109_chrysostom-priesthood-ascetic-homilies-statutes.xml:iii.vi`) states instead that Chrysostom was "ordained deacon by Meletius in 380 or 381, and a few years afterwards presbyter by Flavian (386)" — Flavian being the Meletian party's own eventual claimant, not Paulinus's successor Evagrius. This document does not adjudicate between Socrates' account and this one; both are cited to their own actual vendored sources, per this project's own Contested-confidence discipline.
- Julian's brief pagan restoration and residence at Antioch (362–363), and the city's own documented hostility to him (the *Misopogon*, Julian's own bitter response to Antiochene mockery, already carried as context via the Ammianus row).
- The "Statues" crisis of 387: an Antiochene riot against imperial tax policy resulted in the overthrow of the emperor Theodosius I's own statues, and the city awaited the emperor's judgment (possible destruction of the city itself) while Chrysostom preached the twenty-one *Homilies on the Statues* — this world's own most direct, sustained record of a congregation's fear meeting scriptural exhortation in real time.
- The ongoing tension, at Constantinople, between Chrysostom's own uncompromising preaching against luxury and injustice and the imperial court's own expectations of its bishop — directly evidenced in the Eutropius homilies and the conflict with Eudoxia, culminating in his deposition (the Synod of the Oak, 403) and final exile (404).
- The large Jewish community at Antioch and its continued attraction for some Christians, the documented occasion for the *Adversus Judaeos* material (Step 0 §2, A5) — named here as historical context for that disclosure obligation, not as license to characterize the homilies' own content before the primary text is vendored.

**Historical Catalysts:** Chrysostom's ordination as presbyter at Antioch (386) and the start of his major preaching career there; the Statues riot and its aftermath (387); his elevation to the see of Constantinople (397); the Synod of the Oak and his first deposition (403); his final exile and death (407).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** An ecology of homiletic exegesis: a congregation formed, week by week, through continuous exposure to scripture expounded in its plain sense and applied directly to conduct — the specific mechanism named in §1 above, evidenced by the sheer compositional shape of the surviving corpus (`Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` §3, B3).
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally: historical-grammatical exegesis as the primary vehicle of ethical formation; uncompromising moral exhortation addressed to wealth, status, and civic conduct, applied without regard to the hearer's own social rank (a documented, recurring theme across the Statues homilies, the Eutropius homilies, and the general moral-homiletic corpus); the tension between rhetorical eloquence (Chrysostom's own inherited Libanian training) and plain, direct address to ordinary hearers. Named as candidates for Step 4 testing, not confirmed gravities.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Interpretation and Formation dominate and are, in this world, substantially the same activity (§1). Worship has real, direct material (the Statues homilies, a series of twenty-one sermons preached after the 387 riot; the general homiletic setting itself as a liturgical event). Authority is present but not this world's own center of gravity in its Antiochene phase — it becomes more prominent, and more institutional/juridical in character, specifically during the Constantinopolitan phase (§6 below), material this world already correctly shares with World #6 rather than claims as its own primary register throughout.
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §6 below.

## 4. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21, strand is "a finding, never a presupposed universal schema," accountable to evidence, and "never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality."

**Finding: one primary strand — Chrysostom's own exegetical-homiletic-moral voice — continuous across a real change of office and city, not split by it.** Chrysostom's own ground of authority does not change between his Antiochene presbyterate (386–397) and his Constantinopolitan episcopate (397–404): in both settings, his own claim to speak is grounded in faithful exposition of scripture applied without concession to worldly rank — the same mode that produces the Statues homilies at Antioch produces the Eutropius homilies and the conflict with Eudoxia at Constantinople (§1, §2 above). Applying Article 21 honestly here, as `roman-church-gregorian`'s own Doc_01 did for a comparable single-figure case, means recognizing this as Article 15's temporal/geographic development of one continuous formation logic, not a division between two authority claims.

**Theodoret's own material is assessed here as an actual per-work boundary finding, not a pure strand-deferral.** A strand question presupposes the material is already inside this world's own boundary; whether it is at all is Doc_01's own job (per Step 0 §2, A1's own boundary caution), not something a strand-classification deferral to Doc_04 can substitute for. **One consistent criterion is applied throughout:** per the Source Registry Template's own Boundary Check (a source's status is assessed by what it speaks for, "its own subject, tradition, or evidentiary target — checked against Doc_01's stated boundary," and, separately, "Boundary Status is never assessed by the date a piece of scholarship happened to be written"), the test is each work's own **subject matter**, not when it was written, made here per work rather than as one undifferentiated question. **The Letters entry itself is not one uniform subject:** the 181-letter volume (`npnf203`, div2 3.10) is not uniformly 440s Eutychian-controversy material — it also carries, within the same volume, a run of letters directly from the Nestorian controversy of 430–431 (e.g. Theodoret's own Letter CL to John of Antioch on Cyril's anathemas, dated February 431 in the editor's note, `iv.x.cli`; his letter to Andreas of Samosata written from Ephesus, dated by the editor to late July or early August 431, `iv.x.clxiii`; the Commissioners of the East's first letter, dated by the editor to September 431, `iv.x.clxiv`, and their first petition to the Emperor, addressed from Chalcedon, `iv.x.clxvii`; the opening text of each of these four letters is present in the vendored file. John of Antioch's own letter to Nestorius, `iv.x.cl`, appears in the volume by its title alone, with an editor's note on its authorship; its text is not printed there). **Dating rule applied to every letter in this finding:** a letter is dated by its own text and by the editor's note printed with it, never by the volume that prints it. A letter dated to 431 is a live edge case on the grounds given below. A letter whose text refers to events after 431 is out of the window. Theodoret's letter to Nestorius (`iv.x.clxxiii`) is out of the window everywhere it is printed, in `npnf203` and in the *Synodicon* copy (Mansi tom. 5, file lines 88748-88766), because its text refers to Nestorius's deposition. The Mansi editor's note (file lines 88713-88724) dates it after the union of 433, which is Documented in that apparatus. The `npnf203` editor's note says only "not long after the receipt by Theodoret of the Chapters of Cyril" and gives no year. Applying the same subject-matter test consistently means splitting the Letters the same way the Counter-statements are already split from the *Eranistes*:

This is genuinely Native to this world's own boundary, but as a context-like historical witness, not as a second instance of this world's own core formation-mechanism (§1 above).

It anathematizes anyone who defends "the impious writings of Theodoret, directed against the true faith and against the first holy Synod of Ephesus and against St. Cyril and his XII. Anathemas, and [defends] that which he has written in defence of the impious Theodore and Nestorius". That description reaches the Counter-statements. On its wording it also reaches the Letters that attack the Ephesus proceedings of 431 or defend Nestorius: `iv.x.clxiii` says "Such is the mockery of a synod held by Egyptians, by Palestinians, by men from the Pontic and Asian dioceses, and by the West in their company", and `iv.x.clxxiii` tells Nestorius "to what has been done unjustly and illegally against your holiness, not even if one were to cut off both my hands would I ever assent". Condemned-content status is therefore disclosed for the Counter-statements and for those two letters (`iv.x.clxxiii` is out of the window, so its disclosure applies only if it is drawn on as context). ## 5. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation for Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8.

**What generated this world:** Chrysostom's own formation — classical rhetorical training under Libanius, followed by ascetic withdrawal and then ordination — combined with Antioch's own established culture of expository preaching (Diodore of Tarsus, Widely Accepted as an influence on this tradition though, per §2 above, not independently verified against a primary text naming Chrysostom himself as his direct pupil, and not himself part of this world's currently vendored Native base, per Step 0 §2 and §4.4) produced a preacher equipped to apply rhetorical skill to plain-sense scriptural exposition at unusual scale and sustained intensity.

**What larger world was it embedded in:** the Greek East of the later fourth and earlier fifth centuries — doctrinally, the Nicene-Constantinopolitan settlement of 381 (Widely Accepted). For the Antiochene church's rival factions, Socrates attests agreement in faith only for the time of Meletius's first episcopate, about 360 to 361: those attached to Meletius had separated from the Arian congregation, and the passage goes on, "those who originally embraced the homoousian opinion would not communicate with them, because Meletius had been ordained by the Arians, and his adherents had been baptized by them. Thus was the Antiochian church divided, even in regard to those whose views on matters of faith exactly corresponded" (Socrates, `cic:npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml:ii.v.xliv-p3`). The schism's own ground is therefore not merely "jurisdictional and personal": on that passage it is a validity-of-ordination-and-baptism dispute (see also Step 0 §3, which names this same ground against `lpc`'s own Candidate 6). This world's own actors are not simply outside the Trinitarian controversy the way World #6's earlier figures were: Chrysostom's own vendored corpus includes a homily proving the equality of Father and Son against the Anomoeans (Step 0 §2, A5), meaning the Trinitarian controversy's own later, Anomoean/Eunomian phase is directly present in this world's own Native material, not merely inherited as settled background. Politically, an empire whose Constantinopolitan court increasingly set the terms even for a see as prominent as Antioch's, a condition this world's own Constantinopolitan phase makes directly visible.

**What was it responding to:** at Antioch, ordinary congregational life under real but not acute political pressure, punctuated by the Statues crisis's own acute emergency; at Constantinople, an imperial court whose own expectations of its bishop's public conduct Chrysostom's own preaching repeatedly, deliberately violated.

**What was it refusing:** moral compromise with wealth, status, and imperial favor, applied consistently rather than selectively — the documented throughline connecting the Statues homilies (addressed to a whole frightened city, elite and poor alike), the Eutropius homilies (addressed to a specific fallen minister, at the literal altar), and the conflict with Eudoxia (addressed, implicitly, to the empress herself). Not a refusal of imperial power as such (contrast Donatism's own more total refusal, or World #6's own more equivocal accommodation), but a refusal to let imperial or elite status exempt anyone from the same scriptural standard applied to an ordinary hearer.
