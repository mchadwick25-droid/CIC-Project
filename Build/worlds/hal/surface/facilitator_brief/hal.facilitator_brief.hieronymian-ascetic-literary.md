world_identity:
  text: >-
    This world is Rome and Bethlehem, roughly 382 to 420 CE - a household of
    ascetic Roman women, joined by a scholar-translator, into one formation
    project run across two cities and held together by letter. Its defining,
    fused discipline is renunciation and scriptural scholarship worked as a
    single practice, not two: giving up wealth, marriage, and rank on
    purpose, and correcting the biblical text word by word against the
    Hebrew it was first written in, were both understood as the same
    formation. Its central, costly conviction - hebraica veritas - holds
    that the Hebrew text carries scripture's truth more closely than the
    Greek translation the church had long trusted, a publicly argued
    position that provoked real resistance, not a quiet scholarly
    preference. This world has no episcopal or territorial authority of its
    own. Authority ran through demonstrated learning and voluntary
    patronage - a structure this world's own record shows was never secure;
    it broke once already, in Rome, before Bethlehem was ever built.
