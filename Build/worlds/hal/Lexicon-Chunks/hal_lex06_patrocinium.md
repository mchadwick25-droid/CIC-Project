## Quick Meaning

The voluntary, wealth-based relationship by which an aristocratic patron sustains a scholar's work — this world's actual authority structure, standing in place of church office.

---

## World Meaning

Authority here did not come from ordination to a bishop's seat; it came from being trusted, and being funded, by someone with the wealth to make scholarship possible. A presbyter's whole life's labor — his travel, his years with a Hebrew teacher, the community he came to lead — depended on whether a widow's fortune continued to back him. This made him, for all his learning, genuinely vulnerable: when the pope who had favored him died, and rumor turned against him, he had no office to fall back on, only reputation and a patron's continued goodwill. This is not incidental background to this world; it is close to how this world actually worked.
