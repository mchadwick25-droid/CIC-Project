## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas VI.11, "The Society of Jesus," 1540–1650, Rome then worldwide. Ignatius of Loyola and the first generation of Jesuits — Francis Xavier, Peter Faber, Jerónimo Nadal — from the order's papal approval (1540) through the first century of its rapid missionary and educational expansion.

## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: this document's "clears" are a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### A1 — The floor: clears

This is the Catholic Church itself, in its most institutionally orthodox 16th-century form. Ignatius's own *Spiritual Exercises* and dictated *Autobiography* contain no Trinitarian or Christological deviation from any of Article 4's five commitments — positive evidence, not just absence: the Autobiography's own account of Ignatius's Trinity devotion at Manresa, and the Exercises' own Incarnation contemplation, both directly affirm commitments 2 and 3.

Trent's canons do not reaffirm the Nicene Creed or Chalcedonian Christology by name. Session III of the vendored Waterworth Canons/Decrees recites the full Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed but calls it only "the Symbol of faith which the holy Roman Church makes use of" — "Nicaea" appears solely in a footnote citation. "Chalcedon" appears in the decrees exactly once, as a disciplinary citation on ordination (Session XXIII, ch. XVI) — never as a doctrinal reaffirmation by name. The Session III creed recital nonetheless matches Article 4's five commitments almost clause for clause. Trent is not this order's own doctrinal charter — the Society's actual founding charter is the 1540/1550 Formula of the Institute; Trent (1545–63, opening five years after papal approval) is a doctrinal anchor the order later implemented, and the corpus-map sets Trent's role as `context` for this world "since this is not the Society's own composed voice."

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity

Not applicable.

### A3 — Contemporary movements

The direct, real rival relationship in this batch is Lutheran Wittenberg and the Reformed cities — the Jesuits were founded explicitly within, and as a response to, the same crisis those two candidates answer from the opposite direction.

The Chinese/Malabar Rites controversy is not out-of-window. Its later, better-known condemnations do come well after 1650 — Benedict XIV's Malabar condemnation dates to 1744, and 1773 marks the Society's later suppression, not a rites condemnation — but the controversy itself sits inside this world's own 1540–1650 window: Ricci's China accommodation method (from the 1580s), de Nobili's Madurai accommodation (from 1606), Gregory XV's 1623 ruling on Malabar practices, the internal Jesuit Jiading conference (1627–28), and Propaganda Fide's 1645 decree condemning the Chinese rites as Morales presented them, are all within the window. "First-generation figures predate the dispute" is true of Ignatius/Xavier/Nadal individually, but the census defines this world as reaching China, Japan, India, Brazil, and Canada — a live, real controversy sits inside the window and needs treating as an actual scope boundary for Doc_01, not an exclusion (§4 item 4).

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

**Framing note**, as for every candidate in this batch: Tier below is a considered signal, not a self-administered disposition.

### B1 — Sourcing

Per REGISTRY.yaml and the corpus-map, 19 works are vendored and assigned for this world. The founder-generation corpus: the *Spiritual Exercises* (Mullan 1914), Ignatius's dictated *Autobiography* (O'Conor 1900), his Letters Vol. 1 (O'Leary/Goodier 1914, 24 letters 1524–47 — a selection, not his full ~7,000-letter corpus), and Xavier's letters and life in both volumes (Coleridge 1872). Beyond that: Tomus I of the MHSI critical edition of Ignatius's own letters (*Epistolae et Instructiones*, volume 22 of the MHSI series; Latin, 1903); Faber's own *Memoriale* (Latin, 1873, role tradition, confidence assigned, clean scan) — a genuine primary Faber voice, alongside Boero's biography of him (context, provisional — how much of the Memoriale it quotes verbatim is still unchecked); Nadal's own letters (*Epistolae*, Tomus I, 1546–1562, Latin, 1898) and his *Scholia in Constitutiones* (Latin, 1883), alongside his *Adnotationes*; Lainez's own letters and acts (*Epistolae et Acta*, Tomus I, 1536–1556, Latin, 1912 — the volume ends before his own generalate of 1558–65); Salmeron's own letters (*Epistolae*, Tomus Primus 1536–1565 and Tomus Secundus 1565–1585, Latin, 1906 and 1907; the filenames read `v2` and `v3`, but the title pages read Tomus Primus and Tomus Secundus); Polanco's *Chronicon* (Tomus V, covering only the year 1555, Latin, printed 1897, role context — Ignatius's own secretary's historical chronicle; the filename reads `v1`, but the title page reads Tomus Quintus); and Ribadeneira's early Latin life of Ignatius (1572, role context, OCR-flagged). Canisius's *Summe of Christian Doctrine* (1622 English) and the Waterworth Council of Trent Canons/Decrees (an implementing/corroborating anchor, not this order's own doctrinal charter — §2 A1) round out the 19.

Two of these are vendored but not yet quotable verbatim. Under Mark's 2026-09-25 scan-quality ruling (`Build/worlds/_cross-world/LIBRARY-DECISION-LOG.md` point 5), scan quality, not a text's language, decides whether it can be cited as primary. The 1606 Latin Constitutions and Nadal's 1595 Latin *Adnotationes* are both flagged under that ruling as garbled scans and held at **second-witness status**, not PRIMARY — which closes a real sourcing gap but leaves them not yet quotable as the order's own words until a cleaner edition is vendored. The Constitutions gap also still has a language dimension of its own: no PD *English* translation exists (the Ganss 1970/1996 translation is in copyright), so the order's own governing document is not yet quotable verbatim in any language.

Word counts are available directly: 3,370,675 words across all 19 files (1,385,753 of that across the original ten; the nine works added on 2026-09-25 make up the rest).

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on personal spiritual formation (the Exercises, the Autobiography), missionary/pastoral correspondence (Xavier's letters, both volumes), catechetical writing (Canisius), and institutional self-governance and administration up to Ignatius's death in 1556 — the Constitutions, Nadal's *Scholia* explaining them, Nadal's own *Adnotationes* and letters, Lainez's letters and acts (Tomus I, 1536–1556), and Polanco's *Chronicon* (Tomus V, 1555 only) are all vendored. After 1556 the institutional voice thins in steps. Nadal's letters (Tomus I, 1546–1562) carry it to 1562. From 1562 to 1585 it rests on one correspondent, Salmeron (Tomus Primus 1536–1565, Tomus Secundus 1565–1585). Nothing vendored gives Lainez's own voice as General (1558–65), nor that of his successors Borgia (1565–72) and Mercurian (1573–80). A fair summary: strong to 1556, partial to the early 1560s, and a single correspondent's thread to 1585. What is thinner still: coverage past roughly 1585, and above all the order's missionary/global reach — nothing is vendored on Ricci's China accommodation method, de Nobili's Madurai mission, the 1599 *Ratio Studiorum*, the colleges, or the New France missions. The census names the Jesuit Relations (a plausible PD lead — Thwaites's 1896–1901 edition) as a core primary source for exactly this later, global dimension; it has not been checked — grep of `cic/texts/` confirms nothing vendored. The census's own sourcing note — "founder-corpus gravity needs standard discipline" — and its statusDescription — "a discipline for any account of it to apply, not a straightforward strength to claim" — are both worth carrying into Doc_02 rather than presenting the founder corpus as simply "well covered." Devotio Moderna's own Step 0 explicitly flags a "both-directions ancestor" influence relationship on Jesuit pedagogy: Coleridge's *Life and Letters of St. Francis Xavier* (Vol. I) narrates the early companions' spiritual reading in the Imitation of Christ, and a footnote there citing Alcazar's *Chrono-Historia* reports that Ignatius gave each monk at Monte Cassino a copy of *De Contemptu Mundi* — a real vendored anchor for it, worth naming in whichever Doc_02 runs for the Jesuits.

### B3 — Uniqueness

**Versus the six already-built patristic worlds:** no overlap, over a millennium's separation, though a real thematic (not textual) echo with Desert Monasticism and Cappadocian worth naming in Doc_01 per §0 above. **Versus this batch's other five candidates:** the native/implementing split is settled at the corpus-map level, not an open defect. The corpus-map assigns the shared Waterworth Trent Canons/Decrees `role: tradition, confidence: assigned` for the Tridentine Church — that world's own defining conciliar voice — and `role: context, confidence: assigned` for this world, since the text is not the Society's own composed voice (§2 A1). Both worlds would likely cite it regardless. No figure overlap with Lutheran Wittenberg, the Reformed cities, the Anabaptists, or Lollardy. See §3 B2 above for the real Devotio Moderna relationship.

### B4 — Audiences

A genuinely distinct pull within this batch — the only entry whose core text (the Exercises) is a practiced spiritual discipline rather than primarily a doctrinal or polemical corpus. It is not the only entry with a real missionary/global dimension: the Tridentine Church's own census entry includes Propaganda Fide (1622), the Roman office for the missions. Per §3 B2, this candidate's own missionary/global voice is itself concentrated in the first generation (Xavier, d. 1552); the newly vendored material extends the order's own institutional/administrative voice, not its missionary/global reach, which is still not sustained across the full window.

### B5 — Scale

Rome, then — per §3 B2 — global reach that the *vendored corpus* documents unevenly across the window: strong for the founding missionary generation (to 1552/1556) and for the order's own institutional administration to 1556, partial to 1562 (Nadal's letters), and carried after that by Salmeron's letters alone to c. 1585 (Lainez's volume ends in 1556 and Polanco's covers only 1555), but not for missionary/global reach itself past Xavier's death — nothing is vendored on Ricci, de Nobili, or the Jesuit Relations. The claim of eventual worldwide reach is historically accurate; the sourcing to back it as a *missionary* claim across the full window is not yet in hand.

### Section B conclusion — Tier

Clears Section A (§2 A1, A3); clears Sourcing on a vendored base of 19 works (§3 B1), with the Constitutions and Nadal's *Adnotationes* held at second-witness status under Mark's OCR ruling rather than treated as PRIMARY, and Faber's own *Memoriale* vendored directly alongside Boero's biography. The Trent/Tridentine-Church split is settled at the corpus-map level: tradition/native for the Tridentine Church, context/implementing for the Society of Jesus, both confidence assigned (§3 B3) — nothing here blocks either world's Doc_02. The global/missionary claim still needs a sourcing condition for the period after Xavier's 1552 death, including checking the Jesuit Relations (§3 B2, B5).

**Tier 1 — a strong seed, well sourced on institutional and administrative material to 1556, with three remaining conditions, all evidentiary rather than structural.** First, the Constitutions and Nadal's *Adnotationes* are vendored but not yet quotable verbatim (second-witness status, not absence from the corpus). Second, no PD *English* Constitutions translation exists. Third, and largest, is scale: the sourcing is strong for the founding generation and institutional administration to 1556, partial to the early 1560s (Nadal's letters), and a single correspondent's thread to 1585 (Salmeron). It is thin for the order's central voice after 1556 and for missionary/global reach across 1556–1650 — exactly the period its own global/missionary claims (§3 B2, B4, B5) depend on. Doc_02 should either narrow those claims to match the sourcing in hand, or treat acquiring Jesuit Relations material as a real precondition before making them.
