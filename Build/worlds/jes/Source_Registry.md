# Source Registry — The Society of Jesus

Per `Source_Registry_Template.md` V1.0. Co-equal Step 2 output with `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md`. **Living document: append-only.** A row is never renumbered or deleted. Doc_02 may not name a source in support of a specific claim unless that source has a row here. Rows are entered only for works that the corpus map assigns to this world, and for works that are not vendored. A vendored work assigned only to another world is recorded as a candidate in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (section E), not here.

**Status:** Draft. Not reviewed. The saturation statement at the end is not closed.
**Boundary checked against:** `Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` (1540–1650; Rome, then the Iberian kingdoms, Italy, the German lands, France, India, Japan, Brazil, and the missions beyond; strand-singular as a recommendation pending the project lead). Boundary Status is judged by what a source speaks for, never by the publication date of a modern edition.

**Type:** P (Primary) / S (Secondary) / M (Material/External) / L (Period Lexicon).
**Confidence (citation reliability):** A = the Licensed-For passages were read and matched in the vendored file by a structure marker (letter number, chapter or section heading, running-head page, or the edition's own title). For an Excluded row, A means that the passage which makes it a Named Comparandum was read and matched. B = a specific work or locus is named and exists in the vendored file, but the Licensed-For content was not itself re-read. C = a real author and work, no locus checked, not vendored. D = a body or genre of material, no single text named. No row is E; a source with no traceable basis is not entered. A C row for a work that has since been vendored keeps its letter, and its note names the row that holds the vendored file.
**Structure markers:** line numbers are lines of the vendored file in `cic/texts/`. "Letter" numbers are the numerals printed in the O'Leary and Coleridge editions. Latin scans are matched by the editors' own headings (for example "PRAEFATIO GENERALIS") and by dates printed in the text.
**Scan-quality note:** all vendored files are OCR text and carry OCR errors. Quotations in this Registry and in Doc_02 were matched against the files after whitespace and line-break-hyphen normalisation, and after reading long s (ſ) as s, which is the quote gate's own rule. No other letter of any quotation was corrected. The Latin scans' headers say that wording must be checked against the page image before it is quoted. No page image was available to this pass, so every Latin quotation is verified against the scan text only.
**Rights:** each vendored row states its rights basis from the file's own header. For a work that is not vendored, the row states the basis as far as the builder knows it. The Library must verify it before any acquisition.
**What an A attests where there is no Licensed-For:** a row with no Licensed-For content does not carry an A.

## Saturation statement

**Not closed.** The Registry holds every source the builder could identify from four channels: the corpus map and `cic/texts/` (the 87 assigned files, and a full-text search of the whole shelf), the census entry's own `sources`, `voices` and `documentedStories` fields, the source dossier, and the bibliographic footnotes and prefaces inside the vendored apparatus (the MHSI volumes, Coleridge, O'Leary, Salmeron, Nadal). The V7.4 field-bibliography sweep has **not** been run against any external instrument. Instruments not reached: Sommervogel's *Bibliothèque* itself (row 57), the bibliographies of O'Malley (row 54) and Schurhammer (row 55), the *Cambridge History* bibliographies (row 56), the *Archivum Historicum Societatis Iesu* and its bibliographic supplements, library catalogues, and archive.org, HathiTrust and Google Books full-text search.

**The shelf search.** The shelf holds 396 text files (358 `.txt` and 38 `.xml`) and 403 directory entries. A case-insensitive search for the words `Jesuit`, `Society of Jesus`, `Societatis Iesu` or `Jesu`, `Compañía de Jesús` and `Company of Jesus` matches 144 files. It matches 85 of the 87 assigned files. The two that do not match are Canisius's English of 1622, whose scan is garbled, and Trigault's Latin of 1615, whose scan has lost its word spaces. Of the other 59 matches, many are single mentions by editors of other worlds' texts, and some are false ones. The files that hold real Jesuit material and are assigned to other worlds are listed in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (section E). None is a row here. Searches on names found the following outside the assigned files. Xavier matches nine files, among them the Roman Breviary, which is assigned to the Tridentine Church, and the Boyd volume, which is assigned to another world. The rest were not read. Lainez matches five files and Salmeron two, among them Sarpi's history of Trent, which is assigned to the Tridentine Church. Borgia matches fourteen files, among them the Roman Breviary. Bellarmine matches 37 files outside the assigned ones, and his own devotional works are assigned to the Tridentine Church. The surname Ricci alone matches other people, for example an archbishop of Siponto in the MHSI volume. With whitespace normalised, the *Ratio Studiorum* matches thirteen files. Twelve are assigned to this world, and the thirteenth is a volume of Foxe, which is assigned to another world. The Institutum (row 76) prints the text of 1599, and row 45 records what the earlier assigned files say of it. Acosta matches files of this world and of others as a name, and was not read.

**Last productive searches:** the snowball from the MHSI Praefatio generalis and the Lainez Praefatio (rows 41, 46–50, 57, 58, 60, 64); the snowball from the Coleridge notes (rows 61, 62); the read of the census fields (rows 42–44, 51–56); the Library's acquisition against the requests in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (rows 74–92). **Last unproductive searches:** a search for de Nobili's own writings and for the documents of the rites controversy of 1623, 1627–28 and 1645, which the Library did not find (rows 52, 53); a search for Ricci's own text, which the shelf holds only as Trigault's second witness (row 89).
