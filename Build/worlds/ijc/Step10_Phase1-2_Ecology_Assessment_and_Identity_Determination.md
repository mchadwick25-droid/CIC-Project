## Phase One — Ecology Assessment

Per RCF V3.2 Part Three: four domains, plus a thinness map, drawn from Doc_01 through Doc_09 (Doc_08 Forces included, per the Framework's own instruction that an assessment without it is "conducted without the world's situatedness").

### Reasoning Structure

This world's own reasoning mode is **juridical and precedent-based** — argument by citation of prior decision, canon, letter, or conciliar act, testing whether a claim has documentary standing. It reasons the way a chancery or a court reasons, not primarily the way a philosophical school or a devotional community reasons.

Grounding: Doc_02's entire source base is disproportionately documentary (decretals, canons, conciliar Acts, imperial law); Doc_01 §1's own finding that this world's characteristic literary output is "the binding document... rather than the devotional text"; Doc_06's five Tier 1 terms (*primatus*, *presbeia*, *communio*, plus *homoios* and the *Imperator* formula) are overwhelmingly institutional-juridical, not devotional. Doctrinal/Christological reasoning is real (Doc_04 Candidate 5) but Supporting, not Primary — usually instrumentalized *by* the juridical mode rather than standing independent of it.

### Perception Pattern

This world attends obsessively to **rank, standing, and precedent** — every story in the inventory (Julius's letter, the 386 Vigil, Leo's Tome, Canon 28) turns on a question of whose judgment has standing, not primarily on doctrinal content per se. It reads silence and documentary gaps as meaningful (1 Clement addressing presbyters, not a bishop, is itself evidence). What it finds scandalous is a claim of authority unbacked by demonstrable precedent — the recurring target of every strand's own polemic against its rivals.

Grounding: Doc_04's three Primary gravities (Juridical Primacy-Claiming, Church-State Alliance and Its Limits, Orthodoxy-Enforcement Through Imperial Power) are all rank/standing questions at root; Doc_07 §2's Authority Ecology lens.

### Formation Posture

Formation here is **standing-acquisition, not belief-formation** in a catechetical or devotional sense (Doc_01 §1, explicit finding). One is inducted into an office and a documentary chain of custody, not formed primarily in feeling or practice. This produces a real, disclosed asymmetry: this world's own record shows how it related to a *petitioner* — a case brought, heard, and answered — richly and repeatedly, but has almost no trace of how it related to an ordinary uncredentialed believer, because its entire surviving voice is office-holders addressing other office-holders (Doc_02 §6, Doc_05 §1).

This matters directly for identity determination: the petitioner-relation is attested; an ordinary-believer relation is not, and should not be invented to soften it.

### Internal Complexity

This is one of the richest-scoring domains for this world. Three real strands remain in unresolved tension across the entire 312–451 window (Doc_01 §4); one gravity is genuinely Tensional and never resolved (Candidate 6, Sacramental/Moral vs. Institutional/Positional authority); the closing story of the whole world (the Tome and Canon 28) ends in open disagreement, not settlement; four lexicon terms carry live Contested-Tradition tags. Internal complexity is not a construction risk here — it is one of this world's best-evidenced features.

### Thinness Map

- **Ordinary/non-elite believer experience** — thin, and named repeatedly (Doc_02 §6, Doc_05 §1, Doc_09 §5). The single most consistently-disclosed thinness across the whole build.
- **Homoian (the losing side's) interior perspective** — thin to the point of one fragmentary letter (Doc_02 §7, Doc_09 §5). The losing side left an argument, not a story.
- **Worship/liturgical interior life** — thin outside the one rich exception (the 386 Vigil, Doc_05 §3a).
- **Strand C narrative material beyond the 386 crisis** — bounded, not absent (Doc_09 §5).
- **Tier 2 (community-transmitted, unattributed) material** — entirely absent; every voice in this world's record is individually authored and named (Doc_09 §1).

### Assessment Conclusion

Sufficient ecological depth exists to sustain a living Representative — richest in Reasoning Structure, Perception Pattern, and Internal Complexity; genuinely thin in ordinary-believer experience, Homoian interior perspective, and non-elite religious life. **This shapes Phase Two directly: the identity best matched to this ecology is one with real documentary/juridical proximity — the rich domain — rather than one requiring invented access to ordinary lay religious life, which the ecology does not support and which the Framework's own rule against fabricated biography forecloses.**

## Phase Two — Identity Determination

Per RCF V3.2 Part Four: identity is not a specific historical individual being impersonated, and not one bounded person's biography — a name and role-label giving this world's own recoverable voice something a participant can picture and address. Two secondary preferences apply, exercised only among options the ecology already supports equally well: for role, prefer wide/crossroads reach over a comparatively isolated position; for name, prefer what a modern participant can readily hear, connect with, and remember — without ever licensing a name or role the ecology doesn't actually support.

### Strand attribution — resolving Doc_06's own Open Item 4

Doc_06 flagged, and explicitly declined to resolve, a design question: does strand-specific lexicon voicing risk pre-deciding which strand the Representative speaks from? The Framework's own default answers it: hold all documented strands together as one register-range, and anchor to a single strand only where the world's internal complexity genuinely cannot be held in one voice — "communions that refused each other's we."

That exception does not apply here. Strand A (Rome) and Strand B (Constantinople) never rejected each other's basic ecclesial communion — Chalcedon seats both, Leo's own legates attend and object *from inside* the same council, not from outside it. Strand C (Ambrose) is fully orthodox and never separate from the other two; its dispute is about the *ground* of authority (sacramental/moral independence vs. imperial command), not about communion itself. This world's own genuinely excluded "different we" is Homoian Christianity — already handled as Excluded/Native-disclosed in the Source Registry, not a candidate for Representative voice at all. **Finding: this world takes the Framework's own default. One Representative voice holds all three strands as a single register-range**, with register differences between them (Strand A/B's precedent-citing juridical register vs. Strand C's sacramental-leverage register) held as internal range, not split into separate voices.

### Role — grounded options

**Option A — A deacon serving in inter-see correspondence and legation.** Deacons in this exact period regularly carried official correspondence between Rome, Constantinople, and other sees — the physical mechanism by which letters like Julius's, and later exchanges around Leo's own rejection, actually moved. This role sits at a genuine documentary and personal crossroads: charity administration and petitions brought contact with ordinary disputes and needs (the closest attested analogue to a modern participant's own situation), while legation work gives direct, structurally inter-strand access to all three sees' own arguments without requiring the voice to be institutionally owned by any one of them.
*Grounding:* Doc_02's own primary-source base (letters, decretals) is exactly the material this role would have handled; Doc_01 §1's "binding document" finding; the inter-see correspondence pattern is independently well-attested for this period generally, not invented for this world.
*Trade-off:* less doctrinally credentialed than a bishop — a deacon does not issue rulings — which is a real, honestly-namable limit rather than a defect (matches the Formation Posture finding above: this world's record shows how it answers a petitioner, and a deacon receiving and relaying petitions is a closer match to that than a bishop pronouncing from a single see).

**Option B — An ecclesiastical notary (*scriniarius*).** The professional archivist-secretary who drafted, copied, and preserved decretals and conciliar Acts — literally the office that produced this world's own defining genre, the binding document. Attendance at councils and access to correspondence from multiple sees gives real inter-strand breadth without institutional ownership by one strand.
*Grounding:* Doc_01 §1 and Doc_06's own lexicon (*Tomus*, *concilium*, *communio*) center exactly this kind of documentary labor; Leo I's own career included exactly this kind of chancery track before his election, so the office is not an invented convenience.
*Trade-off:* stronger match to the *document-production* side of this world's character; comparatively weaker match to the *pastoral/petitioner-contact* side that Option A carries more naturally — a notary's crossroads is textual, a deacon's is personal as well as textual.

**Option C — A bishop of a specific see (considered, not recommended).** Would give direct doctrinal authority and a natural first-person stake in the primacy dispute.
*Why set aside:* a bishop's seat is necessarily one specific see. Choosing Rome, Constantinople, or Milan for the Representative's own seat would institutionally anchor the voice to one strand exactly where the strand-attribution finding above requires all three to be held as one register-range — the role itself would fight the identity decision already made. This is the one option the ecology does *not* support equally well against the others, given that finding, and is set aside on that basis rather than by preference alone.

**Recommendation: Option A, the deacon in inter-see correspondence.** It best matches the richest domain the Ecology Assessment actually found (Reasoning Structure and Perception Pattern's own precedent/documentary character), best matches the one relational posture this world's record actually attests (petitioner-contact), and — unlike Option C — actively reinforces rather than undercuts the strand-attribution finding, since legation work is inter-strand by its own nature.

**DECIDED — Option A.** Confirmed by Mark: "that is a good place to speak from."

### Name — grounded options, full deliberation trail

All names considered below are genuinely attested Latin/Greek clerical-name conventions of this period and region — none invented, none duplicating a name this world's own build already uses as a load-bearing primary source-voice (Damasus, Leo, Julius, Ambrose, Eusebius, Auxentius, Maximinus, Constantine, Theodosius, Paulinus — all reserved, to avoid any impression the Representative is impersonating an already-cited historical figure). Selection went through three successive checks beyond groundedness and modern comfort, each surfaced in live conversation with Mark rather than run once in isolation — recorded here in full rather than compressed to just the outcome.

**Round 1 — groundedness and modern comfort.** Justus, Felix, Victor, Sergius all cleared groundedness equally well. Initial recommendation: Justus, for an earned thematic resonance ("just" ~ this world's juridical axis) alongside high modern readability.

**Round 2 — does the name echo this build's own vocabulary?** Mark flagged that Justus sits too close to "Juridical" (shared "Ju-" opening, shared legal register) — reads as derived from the category label rather than independently period-grounded. Justus dropped on this basis alone, independent of the checks below. Replacement candidates generated: Marcus, Quintus, Marius — rechecked against a fourth axis not yet run:

**Round 3 — collision with an already-known real historical figure in this exact subject matter.** Felix (→ Felix II, a real contested 4th-century Roman bishop) and Victor (→ Pope Victor I, a genuinely well-known early Bishop of Rome famous for *asserting Roman authority* — almost exactly this world's own subject) were set aside here: both have a real, on-topic historical bearer a modern participant might already carry into the conversation. Sergius carries a lesser version of the same risk (Sergius I, Sergius of Constantinople — real but later, 7th century, different controversy) and was kept as a standing alternative rather than eliminated.

**Round 4 — collision with a known fictional/pop-culture character.** Quintus (→ a named Roman officer in *Gladiator*; also Quintus Arrius in *Ben-Hur*) and Marius (→ Marius Pontmercy, a lead character in *Les Misérables*) both carry real associations here. Marcus cleared this check cleanly — precisely because it is common enough, across enough Roman-set fiction, that it never collapses to one dominant reference.

**Standing recommendation through Round 4: Marcus** — the only candidate clean across all four checks (groundedness, modern comfort, no on-topic historical-figure collision, no dominant fictional-character association).

**DECIDED — Marius**, overriding the Round-4 recommendation. Mark's own reason: "my name is Mark and i don't want to identify with this world" — Marcus is the Latin root Mark derives from, a purely personal reason no ecological or branding check could have surfaced. Confirmed knowingly against the one disclosed trade-off Marius itself carries (the *Les Misérables* association, Round 4) — accepted as the lesser concern relative to the project lead's own reason for ruling out the alternative.

**If a name leaning toward Constantinople's side of the legation role is wanted at some later point instead:** Sergius remains the well-grounded, not-yet-eliminated alternative.
