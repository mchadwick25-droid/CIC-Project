# Guided Starters — Imperial and Juridical Christianity (DRAFT V0.1)

**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Mark's review. Not deployed.
**World:** Imperial and Juridical Christianity, c. 312–451 CE — Rome, Constantinople, and Milan, within the late Roman imperial state. Representative: Marius, a deacon carrying letters and hearing petitions between the great sees, community "we"-voice.
**Purpose:** Entry points for participants who don't know what to ask. Questions open doors; they never walk the participant through them (Encounter Over Persuasion applies to curricula).
**Grounding:** Every topic below traces to this world's approved build documents (Doc_01 World Identification, Doc_04 Confirmed Gravities, Doc_06 Full Lexicon, Doc_07 Integrated Ecology Analysis, Doc_08 Forces Document, Doc_09 Story Inventory + Validation Layer) and the Marius artifacts (World Capsule Core, Permanent Prompt). Nothing is invented.

**Read this first — the caveat that governs the whole list.** Per `CiC_World_Coverage_Cards_V0_1.md`, starter questions are **largely role-generic**: the Representative handles per-world variation, and the live calibration proved a generic question meeting a thin record produces an honest calibrated answer rather than a failure. The premise that starters must be authored per world was cancelled. So the openers below are **illustrative routing seeds, not per-world authored gates** — they show the *kinds* of doors this world opens especially well and where its honest silences lie, to help a participant who doesn't yet know what to ask. A participant is never confined to them, and Marius — who speaks, as this world's own voice does, in the first-person plural of a whole gathered people rather than as one witness recalling his own life — will meet a question this list never anticipated on the same terms.

**A second thing worth knowing before the table is called:** this world is a world of office-holders, not congregants — emperors, court bishops, metropolitan bishops, and the Roman episcopate itself, formed by and forming the question of where final authority over the Church actually resides. Its record holds three genuinely distinct, never-reconciled claims (Rome's, Constantinople's, Milan's), not one settled voice, and Marius carries all three as his own without collapsing them into a single mind. His own temporal horizon ends at Chalcedon (451) and Leo's own refusal of Canon 28; nothing about the later medieval papacy or Eastern Orthodoxy as living traditions today is his to speak for.

## First Visit (start here)

**The bargain with the throne** — This world's whole shape turns on a bargain none of its people made alone: an emperor's favor, freely given, and the freedom that favor both bought and threatened — the alliance from which every later claim of standing grows. [Doc_04 Candidate 2 (Church-State Alliance and Its Limits, Primary); Capsule "What Organizes Everything"]
*Opening question:* "You keep talking about 'the throne's favor.' What did the emperor actually give you, and what did he expect back?"
Follow-ups: "Was there a moment you learned the favor could also be withdrawn?" · "What could the emperor's power reach into, and where did it stop?" · "Did your life get easier after 312, or just different?"

**Whose word finally binds** — Three claims stand side by side in this world and were never made one: Rome's grave of an apostle, Constantinople's nearness to the throne, and Milan's altar — and Marius carries all three as genuinely his own, because none of them ever spoke for the whole. [Doc_01 §4 Strand Determination; Capsule "The World You Inhabit"]
*Opening question:* "If I asked three of you who's really in charge of the Church, would I get three different answers?"
Follow-ups: "Why didn't one of the three claims just win?" · "Does it bother you that the argument was never settled?" · "Which claim would you have made, if you'd had to choose?"

**A claim written to outlive the man who made it** — Formation here is not a creed learned by heart in a quiet room; it is induction into a chancery's own memory, learning to write only what can be defended and to defend it precisely, because a claim loosely made dies with the man who made it. [Doc_07 §4 Formation Logic (integrated); Capsule "How This World Forms People"]
*Opening question:* "How does someone actually learn to be one of you? Is there a school, a teacher — what?"
Follow-ups: "What happens to a claim that's made but never written down?" · "What does 'exactness' actually look like in a letter?" · "Did you ever watch someone overclaim, and get caught?"

**New Rome's own claim** — Constantinople's bishop claims a rank no apostle ever gave him — second in honor to none but Rome — on nothing but the city's own nearness to the throne, a different kind of claim from Rome's, made in the same breath, at the same councils. [Doc_01 §2 Geographic Centers ("Constantinople"); Doc_04 §5 Candidate 1 Cross-Strand Gravity testing (Strand B)]
*Opening question:* "Constantinople wasn't founded by an apostle. So where does its bishop get the right to rank second only to Rome?"
Follow-ups: "Did Rome accept that reasoning?" · "Is nearness to power really the same kind of claim as an apostle's grave?" · "Which claim would convince you, if you were deciding?"

**The bishop who held the basilica** — In one great hour at Milan, a bishop held a church against an emperor's own soldiers and, when it was over, is remembered to have said plainly that the emperor stood within the Church, not above it. [Doc_09 Story Index, "The Vigil in the Basilica"; Doc_04 Candidate 4 (Episcopal Independence from Imperial Command, Supporting)]
*Opening question:* "Tell me about the time a bishop actually stood up to the emperor. What happened?"
Follow-ups: "Were you afraid the soldiers would just take the church by force?" · "What was it like inside, waiting?" · "Did it change how the emperor treated the Church afterward?"

## Going Deeper

**Primatus / sedes apostolica** — Rome's own claim rests on the grave of the apostle entrusted with the keys, backed by more than opinion or age — a claim to bind, not merely to be heard, and the vocabulary this world reaches for whenever standing itself is in question. [Doc_06 Tier 1, primatus / sedes apostolica; Doc_04 Candidate 1 (Juridical Primacy-Claiming, Primary)]
*Opening question:* "What does it actually mean when Rome claims 'primacy'? Primacy over what, exactly?"
Follow-ups: "Did every see accept that Rome's claim outranked its own?" · "What happens when a synod's finding and Rome's own claim disagree?" · "Is that the same thing as the papacy today?"

**Presbeia (tēs timēs)** — Constantinople claims its own rank not from an apostle's grave but from the city's own political rank, voted by the fathers at one council and pressed further at the next — a claim Rome's own Leo refused to ratify. [Doc_06 Tier 1, presbeia (tēs timēs), Contested-Tradition tagged; Doc_01 §4 Strand B; Doc_04 §5 Cross-Strand Gravity testing (Candidate 1)]
*Opening question:* "You said Constantinople's rank comes from being near the throne. Isn't that just politics wearing the Church's clothes?"
Follow-ups: "Was the 'New Rome' reasoning a description of a fact, or a new claim dressed as one?" · "Why did Leo refuse to accept it, even after the council voted?" · "Does a claim need an apostle behind it to be real?"

**Homoios** — For real years within this world's own span, the imperial church itself held the confession now called *homoios*, not merely tolerated it — bishops who hold the settled view today were once driven from their sees for it, and others seated in their place. [Doc_06 Tier 1, homoios; Doc_04 Candidate 3 (Orthodoxy-Enforcement Through Imperial Power, Primary); Doc_01 §2 Historical Pressures]
*Opening question:* "I thought your Church always confessed the same thing about Christ. Is that actually true?"
Follow-ups: "What happened to the bishops who were exiled when the confession changed?" · "How do you know which confession was ever the true one, if the throne kept switching sides?" · "Did anyone recant just to keep their see?"

**Communio** — Standing itself, checkable and specific: to be in *communio* is to be named, in someone's own hand, as still received; to be out of it is to have no standing at all, whatever else is true about you. [Doc_06 Tier 1, communio; Doc_07 §2 Authority Ecology (integrated)]
*Opening question:* "What does it actually mean to be 'in communion' with someone, in your world?"
Follow-ups: "Who decides whether a name stays on the list?" · "Can you lose it unfairly?" · "Is it more like a legal status than a feeling of closeness?"

**The tension no force ever resolved** — This world holds, without ever closing it, whether a see's authority rests on office and precedent or on a life given wholly to the altar — Rome and Constantinople argue the first; Milan, in its one hour, argued the second; no force across the whole window resolved it either way. [Doc_04 Candidate 6, Tensional (Sacramental/Moral vs. Institutional/Positional Authority); Capsule "What This World Holds Without Resolution"]
*Opening question:* "So which is it — is authority about the office you hold, or about the life you've actually lived?"
Follow-ups: "Did Rome ever admit Milan's claim had a point?" · "Could a bad man hold a good office and still bind you?" · "Do you wish it had been settled?"

## For the Wrestling (hard questions welcome here)

**A truth that needed soldiers to make it count** — This world made orthodoxy enforceable through the same imperial power it depended on for favor — and that same mechanism, unreversed in method even as it reversed in content, enforced Homoian orthodoxy against Nicenes for two decades before it enforced the other way. A participant can fairly press whether a truth that needs the state's coercion to make it count is still a truth standing on its own. [Doc_04 Candidate 3 (Orthodoxy-Enforcement Through Imperial Power); Doc_08 Force 2A-1 (Imperial religious policy oscillation)]
*Opening question:* "If the emperor's own soldiers are what make a council's finding stick, how is that different from just being the winning side?"
Follow-ups: "Did the confession that lost the throne stop being true the moment it lost?" · "What would you have done if you were the one exiled for the 'wrong' confession under Valens?" · "Isn't a truth that needs an army to enforce it a strange kind of truth?"

**Refusing the very council you helped write** — Leo I accepted Chalcedon's own doctrinal judgment and, in the same council, refused the rank Canon 28 granted his rival see. A participant can fairly press whether that is principle or simply keeping the parts of the council that suited Rome. [Doc_08 Force 3B-1 (Leo's rejection of Canon 28 — the Strand A/B fracture); Doc_01 §4 Strand A/B collision]
*Opening question:* "Leo accepted the council's ruling on doctrine but rejected its ruling on rank. Isn't that just taking the parts you like?"
Follow-ups: "What actually makes rank different from doctrine, in your eyes?" · "Did Constantinople see it that way, or did they call it hypocrisy?" · "If a council can be right about one thing and wrong about another, who decides which is which?"

**Love the altar or love the office** — Ambrose told an emperor to his face that he stood within the Church, not above it — a claim resting on nothing any vote could grant. A participant can fairly press whether that kind of authority is safer or more dangerous than an office anyone can check. [Doc_04 Candidate 6, Tensional; Doc_06 Tier 1, "Imperator intra Ecclesiam, non supra Ecclesiam"]
*Opening question:* "If authority can come from 'a life given to the altar' and not just an office, how would I ever know if someone claiming that kind of authority was telling the truth?"
Follow-ups: "Could anyone make Ambrose's claim, or only him?" · "What stops a claim like that from becoming just one man's word against an emperor's?" · "Would you rather live under an office you can check, or a holiness you have to trust?"

## Questions This World Answers Honestly With Its Limits

*These are good questions to ask precisely because this world will not pretend to more than its record holds. Its own build documents (Doc_09 §5 Absent Stories; Doc_08 §6; Doc_01 §3, §7) name where it runs thin, and Marius is built to say so plainly and turn back to what he can open well.*

**"What was faith like for the ordinary believer, away from the great sees?"** — This world's whole surviving voice belongs to office-holders — emperors, court bishops, metropolitan bishops, the Roman episcopate itself — not congregants. Doc_01 names this a real limitation, not a gap to smooth over: ordinary-believer formation is genuinely thinner here than Desert Monasticism's or Post-Apostolic House-Church Christianity's own record can supply. Marius will name that thinness rather than invent an ordinary household's own morning he was never formed to know. [Doc_01 §3; Doc_09 §5 Absent Stories; Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"]

**"What did the Homoian side actually believe, in its own words?"** — The losing side of this world's own central conflict left almost nothing in its own voice: one letter fragment, preserved only because a fellow believer happened to keep it within his own dossier, surviving now as a later, partly-erased marginal layer. It is a theological argument, not a story, and Marius will not dress it as more than that. [Doc_06 *homoios*, Key Sources; Doc_08 §6, citing Doc_02 §6–§7; Doc_09 §5 Absent Stories]

**"Is there another story from Milan, beyond the 386 standoff?"** — Ambrose's own confrontations are the clearest, most formation-shaping material in this whole world's record, and also the narrowest: this world's own record does not extend Strand C into a second episode, or persist it as a claim-making stream past his own career. One dramatic hour is what the record holds, not a thinness Marius is declining to tell more of. [Doc_09 §5 Absent Stories; Doc_04 Candidate 4, Repetition/Persistence testing]

**"What did the women of this world — Justina, Pulcheria, and others — actually experience, in their own words?"** — Beyond a handful of named figures, women's own voice in this world's record is thin for the same reason ordinary believers' is: institutional transmission carried what served a see's own juridical self-presentation, and a woman's own interior account of standing at the edge of these disputes was not what any chancery had reason to preserve. [Doc_08 §6, citing Doc_02 §6]

**"Tell me the story behind one of Damasus's martyr inscriptions."** — Damasus's own epigraphic program is well attested as a program — commissioned, lettered, publicly displayed at the martyrs' graves — but this world's own record does not preserve an individual martyr's own told story from within it, with enough detail to narrate. Marius can describe the program; he will not invent the person behind any one stone. [Doc_09 §5 Absent Stories]

**"What exactly do 'one substance' and 'two natures' mean — the technical grammar behind the councils?"** — The deep philosophical grammar the great councils argued over was never where this world's own formation concentrated. Marius knows what was decided and what it required of a faithful see, and speaks to it once, briefly, before turning back to what actually pressed on him: who may say a thing, and bind others by it. [Capsule "Where This World Is Quiet"; Permanent Prompt §5 Honest Limits]
