## 1. Candidate Gravity Generation

Every candidate below is traced to specific, named Doc_02/Source Registry evidence streams, not general impression or period-familiarity, per the Template's own structural requirement. Candidates were generated by looking for elements recurring across *multiple* evidence streams (not one source's own emphasis) and by examining Attention Presence/Absence patterns already named in Doc_02 (§6, §7) and Doc_01 (§5).

## 2. Candidates Considered and Not Advanced

**Institutional self-documentation / juridical record-keeping as its own organizing force** — considered and not advanced to full six-test treatment. Nearly every Registry row in the P (Primary) category (Rows 9–16) is itself a juridical-documentary genre (canon, decretal, conciliar act, legal code), which made this look, at first pass, like a strong candidate. On reflection it fails the Dependency Test at the point of generation: nothing in this world's own ecology depends on documentary production *for its own sake*; documentary production is instead the instrument through which Candidate 1 below (Juridical Primacy-Claiming) and Candidate 3 (Orthodoxy-Enforcement) actually operate. Treating it as its own gravity would double-count the same organizing force under two names. Not advanced; folded into Candidate 1's own evidence base instead.

## 3. Per-candidate testing

### Candidate 1 — Juridical Primacy-Claiming

*Generated from:* Rows 4 (Julius I), 12–13 (Leo), 14–15 (Damasus), 10–11 (Canons 3, 28), 29 (Demacopoulos), 31 (Pietri).

- **Repetition:** Passes strongly. Recurs across every decade of this world's own window with an unbroken evidentiary chain — Julius I (341) → Damasus (366–384) → Leo (440–461, active through 451).
- **Dependency:** Passes strongly. Strand A's entire evidentiary record (Doc_02 §1–2) organizes around this claim; the world's own closing event (Chalcedon, Canon 28, Leo's rejection) is itself a primacy-claiming collision.
- **Formation:** Passes. Shapes participants directly — it is the reason Damasus built a public epigraphic program (Row 14) rather than leaving primacy an internal clerical matter; it shapes how this world's own actors (Leo specifically) present themselves in their own writing.
- **Explanatory:** Passes strongly. Explains the Julius I/Athanasius episode, Damasus's building program, Leo's Tome, and Leo's rejection of Canon 28 — four otherwise separately-occurring events, one explanatory thread.
- **Persistence:** Passes. Visible in Rome specifically across the full 341–451 span; the Constantinopolitan counter-claim (Candidate for Strand B, tested separately below as part of this same gravity's cross-strand status) shows the same underlying logic (a see's rank matters enough to fight over) persisting on the other side of the same contest.
- **Interaction:** Passes. Reinforces Candidate 3 (Orthodoxy-Enforcement — Leo's Tome is simultaneously a primacy act and a doctrinal one); competes directly with Strand B's own imperial-proximity version of the same underlying drive (see Cross-Strand testing, §5); is reinforced by Candidate 4 (corrected from an earlier "reshapes Candidate 4" — the causal flow actually runs the other direction: Ambrose's own already-past confrontation feeds into and strengthens this gravity's later Strand A confidence, per Doc_01 §4's own "legacy, not identity" finding; this gravity does not reshape Candidate 4 back, since Ambrose's own conduct predates and is unaffected by Strand A's later use of it).
- **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidence reaches Documented for the existence and content of the primacy claims themselves (Julius's letter, Leo's Tome and rejection letters, the epigraphic corpus are all directly attested); Widely Accepted for the claim's actual reception/authority at the time (contested by Strand B's own counter-claim, which is itself part of the evidentiary record, not a gap in it). No divergence between apparent organizing strength and evidential confidence — both are high.
- **Forces-connection notation (Forces Framework Step 4):** Held and intensified under Cell 2A/2B's ongoing forces (Doc_01 §5) — Rome's own declining political centrality (Cell 2A) is precisely what makes this gravity *more* load-bearing over time, not less; the gravity is the community's own adaptive response to a genuine external force (loss of political centrality), not merely coexisting alongside it.

**Provisional classification: Primary.**

### Candidate 2 — Church-State Alliance and Its Limits

*Generated from:* Rows 1–2 (Eusebius), 16 (Theodosian Code 16.1.2), 5–7 (Ambrose), Doc_01 §5 (Preliminary Forces Identification, "what generated this world").

- **Repetition:** Passes strongly. This is the world's own initiating event (312–313) and recurs at every subsequent flashpoint (380 Edict of Thessalonica, 386 Milan standoff, Chalcedon's own imperial convening).
- **Dependency:** Passes strongly. All three strands (Doc_01 §4) presuppose this gravity's existence — Strand A and B both assume the alliance is real and durable enough to be worth claiming primacy within; Strand C exists only as a *limit* asserted against this same alliance.
- **Formation:** Passes. Directly shapes the world's own actors' self-understanding — Eusebius's own court theology (Doc_01 §4, Strand B's seed) is a formation response to this alliance's own novelty.
- **Explanatory:** Passes strongly. Explains why church office becomes a form of state-adjacent power at all (Doc_01 §5) — the single most load-bearing explanatory claim in this world's entire construction record to date.
- **Persistence:** Passes. Visible across the full window and across all three geographic centers (Rome, Constantinople, Milan).
- **Interaction:** Passes. Reinforces Candidate 1 (the alliance is primacy-claiming's own precondition); is directly reshaped by Candidate 4 (Ambrose's confrontation demonstrates the alliance has real limits, not unlimited imperial authority); competes with nothing identified so far — no candidate denies the alliance exists, only its scope.
- **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Documented — the alliance's basic existence and legal formalization (312–313) is as well-attested as anything in this world's record. No divergence.
- **Forces-connection notation:** This gravity *is* Cell 1A/1B's own direct formation-level consequence (Doc_01 §5) — not merely connected to those initiating forces but their most direct ecological product. Under Cell 2A's ongoing forces (Homoian imperial establishment, Doc_02 §7), this gravity does not hold in a simple, stable form — it fractures and re-forms repeatedly (the alliance persists, but which theological content it favors shifts with the reigning emperor), which is itself part of why Candidate 3 below (Orthodoxy-Enforcement) exists as its own distinguishable gravity rather than a mere restatement of this one.

**Provisional classification: Primary.**

### Candidate 3 — Orthodoxy-Enforcement Through Imperial Power

*Generated from:* Row 16 (CTh 16.1.2), Rows 9–11 (conciliar canons), Row 23 (Homoian material), Rows 33–34 (Hanson, Williams).

- **Repetition:** Passes. Recurs at Nicaea, under Constantius II and Valens (in reverse — Homoian orthodoxy enforced against Nicenes), at the Edict of Thessalonica, and at Chalcedon.
- **Dependency:** Passes, though more narrowly than Candidates 1–2. Doc_02 §7's entire required disclosure treatment depends on this gravity being real and named, not softened.
- **Formation:** Passes. Directly shapes which bishops hold office, are exiled, or are rehabilitated (Doc_02 §2, the Nicene historians' own careers under Valens).
- **Explanatory:** Passes. Explains why "the Roman imperial church" and "Homoian Christianity" could be, for two decades, the same institutional thing (Doc_02 §7) — an explanatory claim Candidates 1–2 alone do not fully supply, since neither is specifically about doctrinal-boundary coercion.
- **Persistence:** Passes, with an important qualification: it persists as a *mechanism* (state power enforcing a confessional boundary) even as *which* confession is being enforced reverses more than once (Nicene → Homoian under Constantius II/Valens → Nicene again under Theodosius). This reversal is itself evidence for, not against, the gravity's persistence — the mechanism outlives any single content it enforces.
- **Interaction:** Passes, with full pairwise coverage. Reinforces Candidate 1 (Leo's Tome, again, functions as both) and Candidate 2 (this gravity is one specific, recurring *use* of the alliance). Reshaped by Candidate 4: this gravity's own name and definition are "orthodoxy-enforcement *through* imperial power" — Ambrose's 386 confrontation is orthodoxy-defense run *against*, not through, imperial power, which does not extend this gravity into Strand C (see §5's corrected treatment) but does demonstrate, from within this same world, that the state-channeled mechanism is not the only route to the same substantive outcome, which is a genuine reshaping relationship, not a reinforcing one. No demonstrated relationship to Candidate 6 identified in this world's own Native record — named rather than assumed (see §6).
- **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Documented for the mechanism's existence and reversals (CTh 16.1.2, the exile record under Valens); the specific *content* of Homoian theology itself, per Doc_02 §7, rests more heavily on Hanson's modern reconstruction (Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction) than on directly-surviving Homoian self-testimony (Confidence C, Registry row 23). Flagged explicitly, not resolved by upgrading: this gravity's own *mechanism* is Documented, but full confidence in reconstructing *what, precisely,* was being enforced or resisted at any given moment inside the Homoian-establishment decades is lower than the mechanism's own attestation — a direct, disclosed instance of the Cross-Check's own governing concern.
- **Forces-connection notation:** Directly IS Cell 2A's own recurring content (Doc_01 §5) — imperial religious policy oscillation. Intensifies sharply under Constantius II and Valens specifically, and again, in reversed direction, under Theodosius I.

**Provisional classification: Primary**, with the Cross-Check divergence above carried forward explicitly (not resolved by this document) as an item for Doc_05/Doc_08's own attention.

### Candidate 4 — Episcopal Independence from Imperial Command (Strand C's own ground)

*Generated from:* Rows 5–7 (Ambrose), 27 (McLynn), 34 (Williams), 38 (Milan basilica complex).

- **Repetition:** Fails to pass strongly at the world level. Recurs robustly within Ambrose's own career (388, 390, 386) but Doc_01 §4 already found, at Step 1, that Strand C "does not persist as an independent claim-making stream to this world's 451 close" — this document does not reopen that finding, and the Repetition test here confirms rather than contradicts it: repetition is real but temporally bounded, not world-spanning.
- **Dependency:** Passes narrowly. Strand C's own later legacy (Doc_01 §4) depends on this gravity, though that legacy is "narrower and less institutionally continuous" than Strands A/B's own (Doc_01 §6).
- **Formation:** Passes strongly, within its own bounded window — Ambrose's own conduct is the clearest, most formation-shaping instance of any single gravity in this world's entire record (Doc_02 §2's own Author Gravity assessment of Ambrose already establishes this).
- **Explanatory:** Passes for its own specific episodes (Thessalonica, Callinicum, the 386 standoff) but does not explain the world's own broader institutional trajectory the way Candidates 1–3 do.
- **Persistence:** Does not pass at the world level, for the same reason Repetition does not — bounded to roughly one bishop's career (374–397) within a 140-year window.
- **Interaction:** Passes, with full pairwise coverage. Reshapes Candidate 2 (demonstrates the alliance's real limits) and, in the corrected direction, reshapes Candidate 3 (demonstrates that orthodoxy-defense can succeed through a non-state-channeled mechanism, which is what makes this world's own "through imperial power" gravity a named, bounded mechanism rather than the only conceivable one — see Candidate 3's own corrected Interaction test above). Reinforces Candidate 1, without being identical to it (Strand A's later confidence draws on this gravity's legacy, Doc_01 §4, without adopting its own specific ground) — an asymmetric relationship: 4 reinforces 1 (Strand A absorbs and legitimizes Strand C's demonstrated leverage into its own later confidence) without a comparable relationship running back from 1 to 4, since Ambrose's own conduct is already past and unaffected by Strand A's later use of it. Reinforces Candidate 6 (this gravity is Candidate 6's own clearest single-actor instance, per §3's own Candidate 6 treatment).
- **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidence reaches Documented (Ambrose's own letters, corroborated independently by Augustine's *Confessions* 9.7 for the 386 standoff specifically). No divergence — the gravity is both well-evidenced and, within its own bounded window, strongly organizing; its classification ceiling is set by Persistence and Repetition failing at the *world* level, not by any confidence problem.
- **Forces-connection notation:** Directly IS the ecological product of Cell 2A (the still-functioning Homoian imperial court, Doc_02 §7) meeting Cell 1B's inherited proto-orthodox commitments (Doc_01 §5) at one specific, high-stakes point of contact.

**Provisional classification: Supporting** — organizes a significant, well-evidenced portion of the ecology (Strand C specifically) but, per its own Repetition/Persistence results, functions within the context Candidates 1–2 establish rather than organizing the ecology as broadly as they do. This is not a downgrade forced by weak evidence (see Cross-Check above) — it is the correct classification for a gravity that is temporally and strand-bounded by the evidence itself, exactly the kind of case the Classification section anticipates ("the ecology as evidence reveals it").

### Candidate 5 — Doctrinal/Christological Precision-Seeking

*Generated from:* Rows 9–11 (Nicaea, Constantinople, Chalcedon canons/acts), Row 12 (Leo's Tome), Row 33 (Hanson).

- **Repetition:** Passes. Recurs at every council in this world's own window.
- **Dependency:** Partially passes. Some dependency is real (Leo's own primacy claim, Candidate 1, is asserted partly *through* a doctrinal document, the Tome), but this document does not find independent evidence that other ecological dimensions depend on precision-seeking *for its own sake*, as opposed to depending on the juridical/institutional gravities that make use of it.
- **Formation:** Passes narrowly — shapes which theological vocabulary this world's own actors could safely use (Doc_03's own *homoousios*/*homoios* candidate terms), but the formation effect is more accurately downstream of Candidate 3 (orthodoxy-enforcement makes precision consequential) than independent of it.
- **Explanatory:** Partially passes. Explains the content of the councils but does not, on its own, explain why this world's own actors cared about doctrinal precision *institutionally* rather than only theologically — that explanatory work is done more directly by Candidates 1 and 3.
- **Persistence:** Passes as a recurring activity, but see Interaction below for why this document does not read that persistence as evidence of an independent gravity.
- **Interaction:** Passes. The Framework's own Interaction Test text (checked directly) states the failure condition is a candidate that "sits beside other candidates without any traceable relationship to them" — mere coexistence, not reinforcement. Reinforcing relationships are one of the three passing relationship types the Framework names (reinforcing, competing, reshaping), not a disqualifying one. This candidate does reinforce Candidates 1 and 3 specifically (doctrinal precision is the vocabulary through which primacy is asserted and orthodoxy enforced) — a real, demonstrated, passing interaction, not an absence of one.
- **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Reached. Evidence for the conciliar record itself is Documented; no divergence between apparent organizing strength and evidential confidence.
- **Forces-connection notation:** Directly tied to Cell 2B (Doc_01 §5) — the recurring internal contest over final doctrinal/disciplinary authority, replayed at each council, is inseparable from the doctrinal-precision work each council actually does.

**Provisional classification: Supporting.** This candidate passes Repetition, Persistence, Interaction, and Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check cleanly, and passes Formation and Dependency and Explanatory only narrowly, in each case because the evidence itself shows this gravity "functions within the context established by primary gravities" rather than organizing independently — which is the Classification section's own definition of Supporting, not a disqualifying result. Classified Supporting on that basis, alongside Candidate 4, though for a different underlying reason (Candidate 4 is bounded in time and strand; Candidate 5 is bounded by its own dependency on Candidates 1 and 3 rather than by time or strand).

### Candidate 6 [Tensional] — Sacramental/Moral Authority vs. Institutional/Positional Authority

*Generated from:* Rows 5–7 (Ambrose), Row 14 (Damasus's own martyr-cult program, itself an appropriation of moral/sanctity-grounded authority into an institutional-positional project), Doc_01 §4 (Strand C's own distinct ground of authority).

- **Repetition:** Passes as a persistent tension, not a resolved position — visible in Ambrose's own direct assertion (sacramental leverage over positional imperial rank) and, differently, in Damasus's own epigraphic program, which does not resolve the tension but manages it by *annexing* martyr-sanctity (a moral-authority register) to serve an institutional-positional project (Strand A's own primacy claim).
- **Dependency:** Other dimensions depend on this tension remaining unresolved — Strand C's own distinct legacy (Doc_01 §4) exists specifically because this tension was never collapsed into Strand A's own institutional logic.
- **Formation:** Passes — shapes how this world's own actors have to keep re-justifying institutional authority in moral terms (Damasus's epigraphy) rather than resting on office alone.
- **Explanatory:** Passes — explains why this world, despite its own overwhelmingly institutional/juridical character, still produces a figure (Ambrose) whose authority claim is not reducible to office at all.
- **Persistence:** Passes as a persistent, unresolved counter-pressure across the window, exactly the Tensional classification's own defining character, rather than as a broadly world-organizing force in its own right.
- **Interaction:** Passes. Competes directly with Candidate 1 (a moral-sanctity-grounded authority claim is, in principle, independent of and potentially in tension with an office-grounded one); is reshaped into Candidate 1's own service by Damasus's program specifically (the tension gets managed, not resolved); reinforces Candidate 4 (this tension is Strand C's own deepest logic, named at the world level rather than only within Ambrose's own career).
- **Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Documented for both poles' existence (Ambrose's own sacramental-leverage claims; Damasus's own institutional appropriation of martyr memory); the claim that these represent a genuine, unresolved *tension* rather than a fully harmonized system is itself the Tensional classification's own proper content, not a confidence problem.
- **Forces-connection notation:** Held, rather than resolved, under every force named in Doc_01 §5 — this is precisely what makes it Tensional rather than Primary or Supporting: no force in this world's own record resolves it one way or the other within the 312–451 window.

**Provisional classification: Tensional.**

## 5. Cross-Strand Gravity testing (Constitution Article 21)

This world was found strand-plural at Doc_01 (Strands A, B, C) — this section therefore runs in standard form, not a declared substitute.

- **Candidate 1 (Juridical Primacy-Claiming):** holds directly within Strand A (it is that strand's own defining content) and, in a structurally parallel but substantively rival form, within Strand B (the same underlying drive — a see's rank is worth defending institutionally — organizes Strand B too, even though Strand B grounds that rank differently). Per Doc_01 §4's own governing consequence note, a gravity holding across strands on differing grounds is *more*, not less, evidence of centrality, provided the divergence in grounding is itself named rather than flattened — done here and in Doc_01 §4 directly. **Confirmed cross-strand.**
- **Candidate 2 (Church-State Alliance and Its Limits):** holds within all three strands as a shared precondition — Strand A and B both presuppose the alliance's durability; Strand C exists only in relation to it (as the strand asserting the alliance's limits). **Confirmed cross-strand.**
- **Candidate 3 (Orthodoxy-Enforcement):** holds within Strand A (Leo's own doctrinal-juridical documents) and Strand B (the councils Strand B's own primacy claim depends on are themselves orthodoxy-enforcement events). This gravity is defined by, and named for, enforcement *through* imperial power — Ambrose's 386 confrontation is orthodoxy-defense run *against* imperial power, which is the negation of this gravity's own defining mechanism, not an instance of it. Extending "holds across all three strands... in the same direction" would also directly contradict this candidate's own Persistence finding (§3), which establishes that the mechanism's content itself reversed (Nicene → Homoian → Nicene) — there is no single "direction of use" this gravity holds in even across the strands it does span. **Confirmed cross-strand to Strands A and B only.** Strand C's own relationship to orthodoxy-defense is real but is Candidate 4/6's own territory, not this gravity's — see the Interaction Test in §3 above, which states this as a reshaping relationship between Candidates 3 and 4 rather than treating Candidate 4 as a third instance of Candidate 3 itself.
- **Candidate 4 (Episcopal Independence):** holds only within Strand C, by Doc_01's own original finding and this document's own six-test results (Repetition/Persistence fail at world level specifically because this gravity does not extend into Strands A or B). **Strand-bound**, consistent with Doc_01's finding.
- **Candidate 5 (Doctrinal/Christological Precision-Seeking):** not independently tested for cross-strand status — its own §3 testing shows it operates as an instrument within whichever strand(s) Candidates 1 and 3 are themselves active in (Strands A and B), rather than having a footprint of its own independent of theirs. Recorded as such rather than run through a full standalone cross-strand test, since doing so would test a relationship this gravity's own Dependency result already establishes as derivative.
- **Candidate 6 (Tensional):** present in Strand C directly (Ambrose's own claim) and, in managed/appropriated form, in Strand A (Damasus's epigraphy). Not independently evidenced within Strand B specifically in this world's own Native record — flagged as an open item (§7) rather than asserted either way.
