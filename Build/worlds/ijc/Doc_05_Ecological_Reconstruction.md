## 1. Human Ecology — honestly thin, stated as such rather than manufactured

This world's Native record does not preserve an ordinary believer's own experience of any event this document reconstructs (Doc_02 §6). What can be said here is bounded to the actual participants the Registry attests: bishops, emperors, court officials, and — in two documented instances — royal women acting in their own political capacity (Justina, Pulcheria; Doc_02 §6).

For these actual attested participants, formation is inseparable from office. Ambrose's own case — a provincial governor acquiring the episcopate by popular acclamation while still a catechumen, not yet baptized, then baptized and consecrated within about a week — is the ecology's single most striking instance of *competency transfer*: the administrative and rhetorical skills that made him a capable governor (McLynn, 1994) were read directly onto episcopal office with no separate ecclesiastical formation track intervening at all. Stated carefully rather than generalized past what one case supports: Ambrose's own path is not evidence of a general world-level rule that ecclesiastical and civil formation were "a single track from the start" — the case, read honestly, shows the opposite about *paths* (his was a late, irregular substitution of one track for another, which is precisely why it was remarkable enough to be recorded) even as it shows something real about *competencies* (whichever track a bishop arrived by, the skills this world's authority structures actually reward are administrative and rhetorical ones). Doc_02 §2 already cautions that Ambrose "represents one bishop's own model of episcopal-imperial relations, not a settled Western consensus" — the same caution applies here: Damasus and Leo both rose through the ordinary Roman clergy, not a civil governorship, and this document does not have evidence to claim their own formation followed Ambrose's pattern.

**Forces integration, named explicitly per the Forces Framework's own requirement that this lens not be written without it:** this lens's own thinness is itself a forces-driven fact, not merely a transmission accident — Cell 2B's own transmission dimension (Doc_01 §5) means the institutions with the strongest archival culture (Rome's own chancery, conciliar record-keeping) are also the institutions least positioned or motivated to preserve ordinary-participant experience, since their documentary interest runs toward juridical self-presentation, not communal memoir. The force and the silence are the same fact seen from two sides.

**What this document does not do:** invent a devout layperson's experience of Damasus's martyr program, an ordinary Milanese Christian's experience of the 386 standoff, or a rank-and-file cleric's experience of Chalcedon. Per the Affirmative Duty (Constitution Article 20, discharged fully at Doc_02 §6), the honest posture is naming this absence here again, at the point where a fuller world's Doc_05 would ordinarily supply it, not silently filling the gap because a template section expects content.

## 2. Community Ecology — real but narrow: the episcopal network, not the congregation

This world's own "community," on its Native evidence, is not a congregation but a network of sees in contact, correspondence, and contest — Rome, Milan, Constantinople, and (at the world's closing moment) the assembled bishops at Chalcedon. Belonging in this ecology is belonging to *communio* (Doc_03) — a juridical, not primarily affective, category: to be in or out of communion with a given see is this world's own operative form of community membership, replacing the more intimate belonging-structures other worlds in this project's portfolio (household, congregation, ascetic community) can reconstruct in depth.

A conciliar assembly (Nicaea, Constantinople, Chalcedon) is this world's own closest equivalent to a community-forming event — hundreds of bishops physically gathered, a genuine communal experience in its own right, even though this document cannot reconstruct what any individual bishop *felt* in that gathering beyond what the conciliar acts and Leo's own letters directly attest.

**Forces integration, named explicitly per the Forces Framework's own requirement:** the *communio*-as-community-membership pattern is itself Cell 1A/1B's own direct product (Doc_01 §5) — the initiating alliance is what first makes "in or out of communion with a given see" a juridically consequential, state-recognized status rather than a purely internal ecclesiastical matter; without the alliance, communion status would carry sacramental weight but not the same institutional-political weight this world's own record shows it carrying.

## 3. Worship Integration — a real, if narrow, point of genuine ecological depth

Unlike Human and Community Ecology, worship is a lens where this world's evidence supports real texture, not thinness — Ambrose's introduction of antiphonal psalm-singing and Latin hymnody at Milan (Augustine, *Confessions* 9.7; Doc_02 §1) is not incidental color but is directly, causally tied to the 386 basilica standoff (Doc_01 Strand C; Doc_02 §7): worship practice *is* the contested ground, not merely its backdrop. Augustine's own testimony (used narrowly, per Doc_02's Boundary Check, as eyewitness to this event specifically) describes a congregation kept inside a besieged basilica overnight, singing to sustain morale under siege — worship as the actual, physical form Strand C's resistance to imperial pressure takes, not a separate devotional activity alongside it.

Damasus's own martyr-cult program (Doc_02 §1, §4; Doc_03's *martyrium*) is this world's second real point of worship-integration depth: pilgrimage to inscribed shrines, in Rome specifically, functioning simultaneously as devotional practice and as Strand A's own institutional self-presentation (§10 below). Worship here is not separable from the primacy-claiming gravity (Doc_04, Candidate 1) — it is one of that gravity's own delivery mechanisms.

## 3a. Emotional and Affective Ecology

The Framework's own Step 5 activities require an Emotional/Affective dimension. This is not a lens forced into thinness the way §1–2's own thinness is: affective material is directly present in this world's own Native record and had already surfaced, unlabeled, inside §3's own worship treatment.

The 386 basilica vigil (Doc_02 §7; Augustine, *Confessions* 9.7) is this world's own clearest affective-ecology evidence: a congregation kept inside a besieged basilica overnight, prepared, on the account this world's own record preserves, to die alongside their bishop rather than surrender the building — fear and solidarity under direct, physical, state-backed threat, sustained (per Augustine's own account) by the antiphonal singing Ambrose introduced in that same crisis. This is this world's one directly attested instance of what an ordinary congregant's affective experience of Strand C's own confrontation might have been, distinct from Ambrose's own leadership perspective (already covered under Author Gravity, Doc_02 §2) — genuinely different evidence from anything in §1's own honestly-thin Human Ecology treatment, because here a specific communal affective state is directly, not inferentially, attested.

The juridical vocabulary this world develops (*communio*, Doc_03) also carries a real, if less richly attested, affective register: to be placed outside communion is not merely a procedural status but, on the evidence this world's own actors' own language implies (their own use of exile, excommunication, and reconciliation as recurring plot points in the record — Doc_02 §1–2), a state this world's own actors treated as consequential enough to fight, negotiate, and write extensively to avoid or reverse. This document does not have direct testimony to an excommunicated person's own emotional state and does not manufacture one — the affective weight is inferred narrowly, from the sustained institutional effort this world's record shows being spent to avoid or reverse that status, not asserted as documented interiority.

## 4. Organizational and Authority Ecology — this world's own dominant lens

This is where this world's ecology is richest, and where the bulk of this document's own depth properly belongs, consistent with the binding proportionality constraint above.

Authority in this world is contested along exactly the three axes Doc_01's Strand Determination already found: apostolic-succession-grounded (Strand A), imperial-proximity-grounded (Strand B), and sacramental/moral-leverage-grounded (Strand C) — this is not a single organizational logic with local variations, but three genuinely different logics of legitimate authority operating within one institutional world, each producing its own characteristic organizational form: Rome's own decretal-and-epigraphic apparatus (Strand A — the decretal half carried at the qualified confidence Doc_02 §2 and Registry row 15 already establish, since much of the material transmitted under Damasus's own name specifically is of contested authenticity; the epigraphic half is on firmer ground); the conciliar-canon apparatus culminating in Canon 28 (Strand B); the individual bishop's own sacramental leverage over a congregation and, through it, over a ruler (Strand C).

The conciliar mechanism itself (*concilium*/*synodos*, Doc_03) is this world's own recurring organizational technology for attempting — and, per Doc_01 §5's own Cell 3A/3B forces sketch and Doc_04 Candidate 1's own treatment of Chalcedon as "a primacy-claiming collision," repeatedly failing to achieve lasting — resolution between these three logics (corrected from an earlier misattribution of this connection to Doc_04 Candidate 3, whose own forces-connection is Cell 2A specifically, not Cell 3A/3B). Chalcedon's own outcome (Canon 28 passed, Leo's own legate objecting, Leo's own later rejection) is the organizational ecology's own clearest self-portrait: a mechanism built to produce settled authority that, at this world's own closing moment, produces instead a durable, unresolved three-way contest.

## 5. Boundary Structures — policed doctrinally and juridically at once

Per the Forces Framework's own Step 5 requirement, this lens cannot be written without explicit forces integration (§11 below carries the full treatment); the ecological content itself is this: this world's boundaries are drawn and enforced through the same juridical instruments that constitute its authority structures (§4) — a council's canon, an emperor's edict (Theodosian Code 16.1.2), a bishop's decretal. Boundary-drawing and authority-assertion are, in this world, the same act viewed from two angles, not two separate ecological processes.

This world's own record shows at least two live boundaries, not one, and this document should not overstate their relative weight past what its own Registry actually supports: the boundary against paganism is real and, for this world's own central Strand C figure specifically, direct — Ambrose's own confrontation with Symmachus over the Altar of Victory (384) is a major church-state-pagan boundary episode in its own right, not a marginal one, even though this document's own Registry does not yet carry a dedicated row for it or for the Theodosian Code's own anti-pagan legislation (Book 16.10) — an evidentiary gap in this build's own Registry coverage, named here rather than papered over, not a claim that the pagan boundary was historically thin. What this document can say with more confidence, because the Registry does directly support it, is that the internal-to-Nicene-trajectory boundary against Homoian Christianity (Doc_02 §7) is the boundary this world's own record documents in the most sustained, load-bearing detail — a boundary this world's own actors had to draw and redraw as the empire's own official confession itself reversed twice within the window (Nicene → Homoian under Constantius II and Valens → Nicene again under Theodosius). Ambrose's own adult episcopate (374–397) spans the tail end of that reversal (Milan's own prior bishop, Auxentius, had held the see as a Homoian appointee for roughly two decades before Ambrose) into securely Nicene territory under Theodosius — a boundary the state itself enforced inconsistently, in opposite directions, within one bishop's own lifetime, and a genuinely unstable structure this document should not retroject settled stability onto.

## 6. Formation Logic — becoming a juridical actor, not a believer

What this world's own record shows being formed is not primarily belief but standing — the capacity to make a claim that binds. Julius I's letter (Doc_02 §1), Damasus's decretals (carried at the same qualified confidence noted in §4 above — much of this specific corpus is of contested authenticity, Doc_02 §2), Leo's Tome: each is a document produced by someone whose formation consisted in acquiring the standing to produce exactly this kind of document and have it taken seriously. Ambrose's own case (§1 above) shows this formation logic is transferable from civil administration with minimal modification — the same competence (command of Latin rhetoric, familiarity with imperial bureaucratic procedure, a governor's own accustomed authority) reads directly onto episcopal authority.

## 7. Memory Structures — actively constructed, not passively inherited

This world's own actors do not merely receive memory; they visibly construct it as an instrument. Damasus's epigraphic program (§3, §10) is memory-construction as a stated institutional strategy — commissioning verse inscriptions at existing martyr shrines to link Rome's own primacy claim to apostolic-era memory, in Damasus's own commissioned Filocalian lettering, meant to be read by pilgrims. Eusebius's own historiographical project (Doc_02 §2) is the same kind of act at the level of the whole alliance's own origin story — his *Ecclesiastical History* and *Life of Constantine* are not neutral records but active construction of how this world's own founding moment would be remembered, complete with documented silences (the Crispus/Fausta omission, Doc_02 §2) that are themselves part of the memory being constructed.

## 8. Interpretive Ecology — scripture and precedent read toward juridical ends

Where other worlds in this portfolio may show scripture read primarily for devotional or ascetic formation, this world's own interpretive habits read scripture and precedent toward institutional-authority ends specifically — the Petrine texts (Matthew 16:18–19) read by Strand A as a standing juridical grant rather than only a devotional or ecclesiological one (Demacopoulos, Doc_02 §3); conciliar precedent itself (Nicaea's own canons, cited and re-cited at every subsequent council in this world's window) read as binding juridical authority in the way a legal system reads prior rulings, not merely as venerated history.

## 9. Representative Theological Patterns — precedent-based, juridical argument as theological method

This world's own characteristic mode of theological argument is not primarily exegetical or ascetic-experiential but precedent-and-office-based: Leo's Tome argues Christological precision partly *through* the standing of the see issuing it, not only through scriptural exegesis in isolation from that standing (§8 above); Canon 3 and Canon 28 argue ecclesiastical rank through an explicitly political-geographic premise ("because it is New Rome," Doc_01 §4) rather than through a theological premise at all. This world's own theological patterns are, characteristically, arguments that a claim to truth and a claim to institutional standing reinforce each other — precisely Doc_04's own Candidate 1↔Candidate 3 reinforcing relationship, restated here at the level of argumentative form rather than institutional gravity.

## 10. Power, Influence, and Historical Dynamics — the ecology's own center of gravity

This lens is, on the evidence, not one lens among several but this world's own actual organizing center — consistent with Doc_04's own finding that Juridical Primacy-Claiming and Church-State Alliance and Its Limits are both Primary gravities. Three dynamics recur:

- **Political centrality as a substitute resource.** Rome's own declining political centrality (Doc_01 §5, Cell 2A) becomes the very condition under which ecclesiastical primacy-claiming becomes valuable — a see compensates in juridical/institutional currency for what it has lost in political currency. Constantinople's own rise runs the opposite direction: political centrality translated directly into claimed ecclesiastical rank (Canon 3, Canon 28).
- **Sanctity as convertible currency.** Damasus's own program (§3, §7) converts martyr-sanctity, a moral/devotional register, directly into primacy-supporting institutional capital — the clearest instance in this world's own record of Doc_04's Candidate 6 (the Tensional sacramental/institutional tension) being actively *managed*, not resolved, by one of the world's own Primary-gravity actors.
- **State coercion as an inconstant, borrowable instrument.** The same mechanism (imperial legal enforcement of a confessional boundary) serves Nicene ends under Theodosius and Homoian ends under Valens — power here is a real, load-bearing instrument this world's own actors depend on, but one whose direction is not this world's own to fully control, a genuine external-force dependency this lens must not paper over as a stable resource.

## 11. Forces Integration in Ecological Reconstruction

Per the Forces Framework's own Step 5 requirement, forces enter these lenses directly rather than as separate background:

- **Organizational/Authority Ecology (§4):** the three-strand contest is itself the ecological residue of Cell 1A/1B (Doc_01 §5) — the initiating alliance's own ambiguity about where final authority resides (emperor? council? bishop of Rome?) is never closed by any later force in this world's own window; Chalcedon's own outcome (§4) is Cell 3A/3B's own direct ecological product, not an independent development.

## 13. Ecological Integration Assessment

The lenses above are not independent categories that happen to co-occur; they are, on this world's own evidence, different facets of the same underlying structure. Authority (§4), boundary-policing (§5), memory-construction (§7), interpretive habit (§8), and theological argument-form (§9) all organize around the same core Doc_04 gravities (Primacy-Claiming, the Alliance and its limits, Orthodoxy-Enforcement) from different angles — this is the expected signature of a world with a small number of strong Primary gravities (Doc_04 §4) rather than a world with many co-equal, loosely-related ecological dimensions. **Worship (§3), stated narrowly:** both of this world's own worship-integration instances trace directly to Primary gravities — Ambrose's own hymnody to Candidate 2 (Church-State Alliance and Its Limits) and to orthodoxy-defense against the Homoian court, Damasus's own program directly to Candidate 1 (Primacy-Claiming) — so worship is not "the ecology's own least fully explained lens by the dominant gravities" in general. What can accurately be said is narrower: worship is the one lens that develops texture independently of the *primacy-claiming axis specifically* (Candidate 1 alone), because Ambrose's own instance runs through Candidate 2 and Strand C rather than through Candidate 1 at all — flagged for Doc_07's own closer integration scrutiny on that narrower basis.
