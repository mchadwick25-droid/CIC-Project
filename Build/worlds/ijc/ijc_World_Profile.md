## Section 3 — Formation Logic

*(Mapped from Doc_05 §6, "Formation Logic," and Doc_07 §4 — this world's own Doc_07 does not carry a "Section 2E"; see the structural note above.)*

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

What this world's own record shows being formed is not primarily belief but *standing* — the acquired capacity to make a claim that binds. A fully formed practitioner in this world is not one who has attained a devotional or ascetic ideal but one whose word, once spoken or written, has weight a challenger cannot simply set aside. This world has no single native term for this state comparable to a devotional world's own vocabulary for sanctity — the closest it comes is the office-titles themselves (*sedes apostolica*, the apostolic see, names the state achieved rather than describing a virtue).

### The Formation Mechanism

Formation here is standing-acquisition, transferable from civil administrative competence with minimal modification — Ambrose's own case (a provincial governor acquiring the episcopate) is this world's own clearest instance, carefully bounded rather than generalized past what one atypical case supports (Doc_05 §1's own corrected finding): the same administrative and rhetorical competence that makes a capable governor is read directly onto episcopal authority, with no separate ecclesiastical formation track required. This world's own actors did not always arrive by Ambrose's own irregular path — Damasus and Leo rose through the ordinary Roman clergy — but the underlying formation logic is the same: whichever track a bishop arrived by, the skills this world's own authority structures actually reward are administrative and rhetorical.

### The Formation Arc

Entry is acquisition of office, by whatever route (civil career, as with Ambrose; ordinary clerical advancement, as with Damasus and Leo). The middle of the journey is the office's own claim being tested under real pressure — a confrontation with imperial power, a conciliar dispute, a rival see's own counter-claim. Maturity is reached not by private spiritual attainment but by producing something durable: a document, a canon, a settled precedent, that the world's own later record treats as binding — or, for Strand C specifically, an act (a vigil, a refusal) that becomes formative even without generating a citable legal instrument of its own (Doc_07 §7's own documentation-genre finding).

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

Beneath their differing grounds (apostolic succession, imperial proximity, sacramental leverage), all three strands share the same underlying formation aim (standing sufficient to make a binding claim) and the same basic mechanism (administrative and rhetorical competence transferred into ecclesiastical office). What strand variation illuminates, rather than fragments, is *how* that standing is generated and *what kind of durable output* it produces — Strand A and B both generate citable, precedent-binding documents (decretals and Tomes; conciliar canons); Strand C generates real, transmitted documents too (Ambrose's letters, the *Sermo contra Auxentium*) but none that function as citable precedent for later claimants (Doc_07 §7) — a genuine, not merely apparent, difference in what "maturity" produces for this one strand relative to the other two.

### Formation Logic and the Confirmed Gravities

Primacy-Claiming (Candidate 1) is this formation logic's own most complete expression — Julius, Damasus, and Leo each represent successive instances of standing acquired and then exercised through binding documents. The Church-State Alliance (Candidate 2) is this formation logic's own precondition — without an alliance making ecclesiastical office state-adjacent power at all, "standing" in this world's own sense would not exist to be acquired. Orthodoxy-Enforcement (Candidate 3) is this formation logic's own instrument turned toward doctrinal boundary-policing specifically — the same standing-acquisition mechanism, applied to enforcing rather than merely claiming.

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary

### 4A — Emotional Texture

*(Source: Doc_05 §3a, added at that step's own review after an initial omission.)*

This world's own dominant emotional register is not devotional consolation but the specific texture of *standing under contest* — the felt weight of a claim that might be challenged, and the vigilance required to defend it. Its one directly-attested instance of communal affect is fear sustained into solidarity: the 386 basilica vigil (Doc_05 §3a; `ijcstory004`), a congregation prepared to remain through a night of genuine uncertainty, sustained by shared antiphonal singing rather than by private consolation. This world's own juridical vocabulary (*communio*) carries a real, if less richly attested, affective weight too — to be placed outside communion is a status this world's own actors spent extensive institutional effort avoiding or reversing, evidence of real emotional stakes even where direct testimony to any one person's interiority is not preserved.

### 4B — Philosophical Ecology

*(Source: Doc_05 §8–9, Interpretive Ecology and Representative Theological Patterns.)*

This world organizes reality around the conviction that a claim to truth and a claim to institutional standing are not separable categories requiring reconciliation — they reinforce each other by nature. Scripture and precedent alike are read toward institutional-authority ends (the Petrine texts as a standing juridical grant, not only a devotional or ecclesiological one; conciliar precedent read the way a legal system reads prior rulings). This world's own characteristic argument form is precedent-and-office-based: a document's own authority and the standing of the see that issued it are treated as mutually confirming, not as a bare theological claim standing on its own merits alone.

### 4C — Authority Structures

*(Source: Doc_05 §4, Organizational and Authority Ecology.)*

Authority in this world is contested along three genuinely different logics, not one logic with local variation: apostolic-succession-grounded (Strand A), imperial-proximity-grounded (Strand B), sacramental/moral-leverage-grounded (Strand C, Doc_01 §4). The conciliar mechanism is this world's own recurring, and recurringly failing, technology for attempting to resolve these three logics into one — Chalcedon's own outcome (Canon 28 passed, Leo's rejection) is this world's own clearest self-portrait of that mechanism's own limits.

### 4D — Boundary Structures

*(Source: Doc_05 §5, Boundary Structures; Doc_08 Cell 2A/3B.)*

This world's boundaries are drawn and enforced through the same juridical instruments that constitute its own authority structures — a canon, an edict, a decretal — rather than through a separate boundary-specific apparatus. Its sharpest internal boundary, on the evidence this world's record most richly documents, is not against paganism (real, but less fully captured in this build's own current Registry — Doc_05 §5's own disclosed gap) but the internal-to-Nicene-trajectory boundary against Homoian Christianity — a boundary this world's own actors had to draw and redraw as the empire's own official confession itself reversed twice within the window (Doc_02 §7).

## Section 7 — The World's Tensions

**Tension:** Sacramental/moral authority vs. institutional/positional authority
**Nature:** to hold authority by office and rank is to have a claim that outlives any one person but can be granted or reshaped by a council's own vote (as Constantinople discovered at Canon 28); to hold authority by personal sanctity and moral leverage (Ambrose's own model) is to have a claim no council can grant or take away, but one that does not survive its own holder's own career.
**Cross-strand status:** present in Strands A (managed, via Damasus's own appropriation of martyr-sanctity into an institutional project) and C (Ambrose's own direct claim); Strand B status genuinely open, not asserted.
**How the world held it:** not resolved — Damasus's own program shows this world's strongest institutions actively working to convert one kind of authority into the other, rather than treating them as reconcilable in principle.
**Formation significance:** practitioners formed in this world's own record are formed to use both registers as circumstance requires, without a settled account of how they relate.

**Tension:** Apostolic-succession rank vs. imperial-proximity rank
**Nature:** to ground primacy in an apostle's own grave is to have a claim independent of any emperor's own residence, but one a shifting political map cannot help; to ground primacy in nearness to the throne is to have a claim that tracks the empire's own actual center of gravity, but one that depends entirely on political facts a see does not control.
**Cross-strand status:** the defining tension between Strands A and B specifically.
**How the world held it:** unresolved past this world's own 451 close — Chalcedon's own Canon 28 and Leo's own rejection of it are this world's own last word on the question, and it is a standoff, not a settlement.
**Formation significance:** this world's own two most institutionally confident strands each formed their own actors to hold their own claim as self-evidently sufficient, with no shared adjudicating standard either strand accepted from the other.
