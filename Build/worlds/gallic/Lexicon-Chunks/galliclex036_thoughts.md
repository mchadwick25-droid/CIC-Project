## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, thoughts are the raw material of the interior struggle: "it is impossible for the mind not to be approached by thoughts, but it is in the power of every earnest man either to admit them or to reject them"; they come "from God, from the devil, and from ourselves"; and the mind is a mill-wheel that must grind *something* — the miller decides "whether he will have wheat or barley or darnel ground by it."

---

## World Meaning

Germanus asks the question a Gallic reader would ask: what can the mind do about its own thoughts? Abbot Moses answers on both sides. "It is impossible for the mind not to be approached by thoughts, but it is in the power of every earnest man either to admit them or to reject them. As then their rising up does not entirely depend on ourselves, so the rejection or admission of them lies in our own power." The mind is like a mill turned by a stream: it cannot stop turning, "but it is in the power of the man who directs it, to decide whether he will have wheat or barley or darnel ground by it." So the monk feeds it — the psalms by heart, meditation on Scripture, labour — because it will grind whatever it is given. Then the three origins: "there are three origins of our thoughts, i.e., from God, from the devil, and from ourselves. They come from God when He vouchsafes to visit us with the illumination of the Holy Ghost," lifting us higher or chastening us "with most salutary compunction"; "a whole range of thoughts springs from the devil, when he endeavours to destroy us," "deceitfully showing us evil as good, and transforming himself into an angel of light"; and a third range from ourselves. "We ought then carefully to notice this threefold order, and with a wise discretion to analyse the thoughts which arise in our hearts," like a good money-changer testing coin.

What follows is the whole southern practice. Because thoughts are admitted or rejected, the monk is answerable for them; because their origin is threefold and hard to tell, he shows them to the senior "as soon as ever such arise"; because the wheel never stops, the cell and the office keep it fed. The first fault is not a deed but a thought entertained.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Intrusive thoughts as symptoms to be managed; the mind as a private space where nothing is a fault; "distraction" as a productivity problem; or, oppositely, scrupulosity in which every thought is a sin.

**World Hearing:**
A wheel that cannot stop and must be fed, whose grain the monk chooses; thoughts of three origins to be tested and shown to an elder; responsible admission or rejection, not responsibility for their arising — Egypt's teaching, received as the first lesson of the southern life.
