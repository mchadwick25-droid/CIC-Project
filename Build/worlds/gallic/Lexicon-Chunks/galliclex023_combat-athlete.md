---

## Quick Meaning

For this world the monk's interior life is a sequence of single combats — "our first conflict ... against gluttony," "our sixth combat ... accidie" — fought in a fixed order like the trials of "the true Christian athlete who desires to strive lawfully in the lists of perfection," so that no one "is worthy to be tried in harder battles, who can be overcome in a slight skirmish."

---

## World Meaning

Cassian's fifth book turns from the customs of the house "to the struggle against the eight principal faults, i.e. first, Gluttony or the pleasures of the palate; secondly, Fornication; thirdly, Covetousness," and on through anger, dejection, accidie, vainglory, pride. Each is a combat with its number: "the first conflict we must enter upon is that against gluttony"; "our sixth combat is with what the Greeks call ἀκηδία." The order is a soldier's and an athlete's. The youth who wants "to enter the highest struggle in the contest, should first in the Olympic and Pythian games give evidence of his abilities"; "it is impossible for a full belly to make trial of the combat of the inner man: nor is he worthy to be tried in harder battles, who can be overcome in a slight skirmish." A fortress is "laid waste by the giving up of one postern however small." Accidie is to be expelled by "the true Christian athlete who desires to strive lawfully in the lists of perfection." And when the question of grace is raised, the same figure carries it: God "provided for in the case of Job His well tried athlete, when the devil had challenged him to single combat" — an athlete who fights "not with his own strength, but with the grace of God alone." The combat is real, ordered, and never won alone.

At Tours the word is used once, of the whole life, by the man who watched it: Sulpitius recalls "those daily struggles which he carried on against the various conflicts with human and spiritual wickedness," and answers his own question — could Martin be a martyr without persecution? — by counting the struggles as the martyrdom. The north has the fight; the south has its order of battle.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Spiritual warfare" as a loose metaphor for inner struggle; "athlete" as fitness or self-improvement; the sequence of faults as a psychology of vices to be understood rather than an order of battle to be fought.

**World Hearing:**
Numbered single combats in a fixed order, entered only after the lighter ones are won; an athlete's scrutiny before the great games; a fortress lost at one small gate; and, at the summit, a combat God's grace alone wins — with Tours counting the daily struggles as a bloodless martyrdom.
