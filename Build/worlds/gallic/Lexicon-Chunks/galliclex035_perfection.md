## Quick Meaning

For this world perfection is the summit of a graded ascent, not a state of being without fault — the *Institutes*' declared subject, "the attainment of the perfect life," reached "not ... simply by self-denial ... unless there is that charity," climbed "from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God," and, at the summit, confessed to be a thing "we cannot even make the effort to obtain ... without the mercy and inspiration of God."

---

## World Meaning

Cassian says in his first pages what his books are for: leaving aside the miracles, "which minister to the reader nothing but astonishment and no instruction in the perfect life," he will write "about the way to improve our character, and the attainment of the perfect life, in accordance with that which we received from our elders." Perfection is a road with stages. Abbot Moses ranks the practices beneath it — fasting, vigils, reading, "the abnegation of all possessions are not perfection, but aids to perfection" — and names its substance: none of these avails "unless there is that charity, the details of which the Apostle describes." The formation manual's ladder ends "by purity of heart the perfection of apostolic love is acquired." Abbot Paphnutius says the first renunciation "is not enough of itself to confer perfection on the renunciant"; Abbot Chaeremon sets three restraints on the road and only the last is perfection — "we must do our best to mount with perfect ardour of mind from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God"; Abbot Nesteros divides it, "this practical perfection depends on a double system; for its first method is to know the nature of all faults and the manner of their cure," and then the virtues. It has "lofty heights" the anchorite climbs, and "lists" the athlete strives in. And at the top the manual turns: "perfection cannot possibly be gained without these [efforts], but ... by these only without the grace of God nobody can ever attain it"; indeed "we cannot even make the effort to obtain perfection without the mercy and inspiration of God." The perfect monk is the one who knows he did not make himself.

At Tours the word is ordinary praise: Sulpitius uses it of how Martin did a thing, not of a doctrine of the perfect life, and Martin's disciples are formed by what they see rather than by a graded ascent anyone names.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
Perfectionism — flawlessness, sinlessness, an impossible standard producing anxiety; or "the perfect" as an elite; or a Pelagian claim to be without sin.

**World Hearing:**
A graded ascent with love at the top and charity as its substance, measured by a goal and not by faultlessness, whose practices are "aids," whose stages are fear, hope, and love, and whose summit is the confession that neither the effort nor the understanding of the effort is one's own; at Tours, simply a word of praise.
