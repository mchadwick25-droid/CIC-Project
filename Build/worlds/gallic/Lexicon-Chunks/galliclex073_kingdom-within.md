## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, Abbot Moses's reading of "the kingdom of God is within you": "nothing else can be 'within you,' but knowledge or ignorance of truth, and delight either in vice or in virtue, through which we prepare a kingdom for the devil or for Christ in our heart" — and Piamun's use of the same verse to deny that walls, desert, or the saints' company can guard an impatient heart, since "no one is more my enemy than my own heart."

---

## World Meaning

The end of the profession is the kingdom; Moses shows the two Gauls where it is. Since the Lord says "the kingdom of God is within you," "nothing else can be 'within you,' but knowledge or ignorance of truth, and delight either in vice or in virtue, through which we prepare a kingdom for the devil or for Christ in our heart." The heart is a territory ruled by one of two kings, and the whole interior discipline is the taking of it for Christ. Piamun turns the verse against the monk who trusts his surroundings: if patience and confidence rest "not in the strength of our inner man but in the doors of our cell or the recesses of the desert, and companionship of the saints," then in vain do we hope to escape the enemy, for "the kingdom of God is within you" — and "no one is more my enemy than my own heart which is truly the one of my household closest to me." The cell keeps the body still; it cannot keep the kingdom.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:** "the kingdom of God is within you" heard as a comforting interiority platitude, a general truism about spirituality residing inside a person.

**World Hearing:** the heart is read as contested territory, actively ruled by one of two kings; and (Piamun) the verse is turned into a warning that a cell's walls or the desert's distance cannot themselves secure a heart that remains its own worst enemy.
