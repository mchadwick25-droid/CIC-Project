## Quick Meaning

For this world the sign of the cross is a gesture with real force — the thing Martin will go into battle "protected by ... and not by shield or helmet," that halts a funeral crowd in its tracks, that turns a falling pine, and that Cassian's monk, armed with his staff, uses to "beat them off" — an act of power against demons and elements, not a devotional habit.

---

## World Meaning

At Tours the sign is a weapon and is called one. The discharged soldier offers to stand "before the line of battle tomorrow, and in the name of the Lord Jesus, protected by the sign of the cross, and not by shield or helmet," and to "safely penetrate the ranks of the enemy." Meeting a crowd of rustics carrying "the images of demons veiled with a white covering" — it is a funeral, not a procession, but he cannot yet tell — "lifting up, therefore, the sign of the cross opposite to them, he commanded the crowd not to move from the place in which they were," and they stand rooted until he releases them. When the heathen fell a sacred pine so that it will crush him, "simply holding up his hand against it, he put in its way the sign of salvation," and the tree spins away "after the manner of a spinning-top." Trapped by fire in a burning sacristy, he fails while he wrestles with the door, and "declared that as soon as he again sought assistance from the cross, and tried the weapons of prayer, the central flames gave way." Blessing Sulpitius, "amid the solemn words of benediction, he repeated again and again the name of the cross so familiar to his lips." The cross is the soldier's weapon after the discharge — what the *Life* says replaces shield and helmet.

At Marseilles the same act appears once and in the same idiom, as the last item of the monk's kit. Cassian's monk carries a staff because he must "never ... walk unarmed among so many barking dogs of faults and invisible beasts of spiritual wickedness"; "when they attack them they ought to beat them off with the sign of the cross and drive them far away." The Egyptian abbot who is asked to bless oil will "sign it with the sign of the cross." What Tours does before a crowd, the south does in the cell against the unseen — but it is the same weapon.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A devotional or liturgical gesture — a way of beginning prayer, a mark of piety or of denominational identity; or a superstition.

**World Hearing:**
A weapon that replaces shield and helmet, with visible effect on crowds, trees, fire, and demons; the north's exorcism and mission in one motion; and in the south, the last arm of the monk's kit against "invisible beasts."
