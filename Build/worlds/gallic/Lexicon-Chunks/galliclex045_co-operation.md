## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, the world's own verb for how grace relates to effort: "the grace of God always co-operates with our will for its advantage, and in all things assists, protects, and defends it" — sometimes even requiring "some efforts of good will from it that it may not appear to confer its gifts on one who is asleep" — so that God is named in prayer "not only ... our Protector and Saviour, but actually as our Helper and Sponsor."

---

## World Meaning

The verb is Paul's, and Chaeremon reads it as a monk reads a rule. "The grace of God always co-operates with our will for its advantage, and in all things assists, protects, and defends it, in such a way as sometimes even to require and look for some efforts of good will from it that it may not appear to confer its gifts on one who is asleep or relaxed in sluggish ease." When the Apostle says "Not I, but the grace of God with me," "he affirms that it cooperates with him when he was not idle or careless, but working and making an effort." Paphnutius says the same of the crown: no one can reach it "unless we have secured it by means of the co-operation of the Lord, and His directing our heart to what is right"; "the assistance and grace of God are necessary to it every day and hour." And the way it is felt from inside is given as a pair of names used in prayer: "in our prayers we proclaim God as not only our Protector and Saviour, but actually as our Helper and Sponsor. For whereas He first calls us to Him, and while we are still ignorant and unwilling, draws us towards salvation, He is our Protector and Saviour, but whereas when we are already striving, He is wont to bring us help," He is Helper and Sponsor. The formation manual gives the monk the sentence to say of every gain — "Not I, but the grace of God with me" — and insists that "God's mercy and grace are bestowed only upon those who labour and exert themselves."

Co-operation, then, is not a theory of shares — so much God's, so much mine — but the experience of a labouring monk who finds that his labour was helped before it began and will be crowned after it ends, and who names God twice for the two moments.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Synergism" as a confessional position (God does part, man does part) to be affirmed or condemned; "God helps those who help themselves"; or co-operation as a contract between equals.

**World Hearing:**
Paul's "with me" read by a labouring monk — grace that goes before, accompanies, requires effort, and crowns it, named twice in prayer for its two moments (Protector and Saviour; Helper and Sponsor), and confessed of every virtue as "Not I"; a word for an experience, not a share.
