## Quick Meaning

At Lérins the Doctor is the authorized teacher in the Church — "a Doctor in the Church, who is believed by his disciples or auditors to teach by revelation," the "doctors, who are now called Homilists, Expositors" — whose *consent* binds and whose singular opinion, "be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a martyr," is "a private fancy of his own."

---

## World Meaning

Vincent's whole apparatus of authority is built on teachers who agree. God "placed some in the Church, first Apostles," "secondly Prophets," "then doctors, who are now called Homilists, Expositors"; these, "remaining in the unity of communion and of the faith," are "approved masters," and what "all, or the more part, have supported and confirmed manifestly, frequently, persistently, in one and the same sense, forming, as it were, a consentient council of doctors," is to be held without doubt. But "whatsoever a teacher holds, other than all, or contrary to all, be he holy and learned, be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a martyr, let that be regarded as a private fancy of his own, and be separated from the authority of common, public, general belief." The Doctor's very gifts make him a trial when he errs; and he is addressed as the deposit's keeper — "O Timothy! O Priest! O Expositor! O Doctor!" — to polish what was received and add nothing. Cassian's "best masters of that work or science" are simply the experts a learner of any art follows.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:** "Doctor of the Church" heard as a later, formal honorific bestowed by canonization on a small fixed list of named saints.

**World Hearing:** at Lérins it names anyone recognized as an authorized teacher; what binds is not office, personal sanctity, or even martyrdom, but *consent* among such teachers — a solitary Doctor's own singular opinion, "be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a martyr," is "a private fancy of his own."
