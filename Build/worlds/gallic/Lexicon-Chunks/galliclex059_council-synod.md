## Quick Meaning

For this world a council is two things at once: for Vincent, the first recourse where the ancients disagree — "prefer the decrees, if such there be, of an ancient General Council to the rashness and ignorance of a few," with Ephesus "some three years ago" as the model in which the bishops "innovated nothing, presumed nothing" — and for Sulpitius, the assembly of bishops where the Priscillianists were handed to the sword, after which Martin "never again did he attend a synod, and kept carefully aloof from all assemblies of bishops."

---

## World Meaning

At Lérins the council guards antiquity. When a part opposes the whole, the Catholic prefers the whole; when a novelty infects the whole, he prefers antiquity; and when within antiquity itself the ancients disagree, "it will be his care by all means, to prefer the decrees, if such there be, of an ancient General Council to the rashness and ignorance of a few." Vincent's own proof is recent: "the holy council which some three years ago was held at Ephesus," where the bishops, though "beholden to make some determination on their own authority, yet they innovated nothing, presumed nothing, arrogated to themselves absolutely nothing, but used all possible care to hand down nothing to posterity but what they had themselves received from their Fathers." A council is legitimate exactly insofar as it transmits — the same test as everything else — and its decree is the memory of the whole Church set in writing.

At Tours the synod is where bishops do harm. "A synod, composed of bishops, was held at Nemausus," and Martin, who "had refused to attend," is told its decrees by an angel on shipboard. At Saragossa "a Synod was assembled ... at which even the Aquitanian bishops were present," and the Priscillianists were condemned in their absence; the affair ends at Trier with an emperor's sword, Martin insisting "that it was quite sufficient punishment that, having been declared heretics by a sentence of the bishops, they should have been expelled from the churches." Forced into one communion with the Ithacian bishops, "he lived sixteen years after this, but never again did he attend a synod, and kept carefully aloof from all assemblies of bishops." The bishop who was made bishop against bishops keeps away from the place where bishops gather.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Ecumenical council" as a settled constitutional organ with defined authority; or synods as church bureaucracy; or Martin's refusal as anti-institutionalism.

**World Hearing:**
At Lérins, the ancients' decree preferred to "the rashness and ignorance of a few," legitimate because it innovates nothing; at Tours, the assembly of bishops that hands heretics to the sword and that the saint will not enter again — two hearings that the world holds without reconciling.
