---

## Quick Meaning

For this world "merit" is spoken in two rooms: at Marseilles it is what human effort cannot claim over against grace — God receives us "from no antecedent merits of ours, but by the free grace of His pity," and salvation "is to be ascribed not to the merit of our own works but to heavenly grace" — and yet "the merits of the saints" are real; at Tours it is the saint's standing by which a miracle is asked — "loose, by his pious merits, her tongue."

---

## World Meaning

Cassian's teachers hold the word at both ends. Abbot Moses reminds the monk of "the call with which from no antecedent merits of ours, but by the free grace of His pity He receives us." Chaeremon: "the main share in our salvation is to be ascribed not to the merit of our own works but to heavenly grace"; and against the other error, "the profane notion of some who attribute everything to free will and lay down that the grace of God is dispensed in accordance with the desert of each man." But the same conference will not let the word go: "we must take care not to refer all the merits of the saints to the Lord in such a way as to ascribe nothing but what is evil and perverse to human nature." There are seeds of goodness in every soul; there are merits of the saints; and neither wins grace, which goes before them. The monk is taught to say "Not I" of every virtue and to know that God "is wont to bring us help" when "we are already striving." A generation later Faustus is remembered as teaching that whatever the will gains "is not its own desert, but the gift of grace."

At Tours merit is what one appeals to. Bishops beg Martin to heal a girl dumb from birth, "begging that the blessed man would loose, by his pious merits, her tongue"; Sulpitius hopes Rome may "learn the sacred merits of this man." A saint's *merita* are his standing before God, by which power is asked and given, and no one at Tours is arguing about whether they were earned.

## Ecological Function

G3's cluster (Doc_06 §2.5): the counter-term of grace (008) in the south's argument — what is refused to effort (no antecedent merits; not "the desert of each man") and yet kept for the saints — and, at Tours, a G6 word (the saint's *merita* as the ground of a miracle, beside blessing and *virtus*). The two referents must not be merged (Doc_03 5.7, cross-voice). Dependencies: grace (of God), free will (009), beginning of a good will (042), co-operation (045), Pelagians as foil (047, "according to the desert of each man"), virtus / power (010) and blessing (049, the northern sense's neighbours), humility (032). A participant who understands this term understands why a Massilian monk could deny that grace is earned and a Tours bishop ask a miracle "by his merits" without contradiction.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Works-righteousness" — earning salvation — as the thing this world stood for; or "merit" in the later scholastic sense; or a saint's merits as a treasury.

**World Hearing:**
At Marseilles, what effort cannot claim against a grace that goes first — while "the merits of the saints" are real and not to be denied to human nature; at Tours, the saint's standing by which a miracle is asked and by which Rome should know him.
