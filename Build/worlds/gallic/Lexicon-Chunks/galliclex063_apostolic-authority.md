## Quick Meaning

For this world "apostolic" is, at Tours, the highest thing said of Martin — "as being reckoned holy by all, he was also deemed powerful and truly apostolical," "in Martin alone, apostolic authority continued to assert itself" before the emperor, "a man clearly worthy of being compared to the Apostles" — grounded in power over death and demons; at Marseilles, "the perfection of apostolic love" at the end of the ascent and "the days of the preaching of the Apostles" as the coenobium's origin; at Lérins, the See.

---

## World Meaning

The word is given to Martin by what he does. When the catechumen rises from the dead, "from that time forward, the name of the sainted man became illustrious, so that, as being reckoned holy by all, he was also deemed powerful and truly apostolical." Before Maximus, where "the priestly dignity had, with degenerate submissiveness, taken a second place to the royal retinue, in Martin alone, apostolic authority continued to assert itself" — he would not sit at the tyrant's table until he had been asked "again and again." At Trier the Ithacians bring "the charge of heresy against Martin, who was at that time a bishop, and a man clearly worthy of being compared to the Apostles"; and Postumianus, hearing of "the works accomplished by his power," "justly compared him to the apostles and prophets." Apostolic, at Tours, means having the apostles' power and standing — over death, over demons, over emperors.

In the south the word points at love and at origin. The formation manual's ladder ends "by purity of heart the perfection of apostolic love is acquired" — the apostles as the measure of charity, not of wonders. Abbot Piamun traces the coenobites to "the days of the preaching of the Apostles," to the multitude of believers in Jerusalem who had all things common: the common life is apostolic because it is the first Church's. And Vincent's "Apostolic See" is a place with two Popes' authority. Nobody in the south calls a living man "apostolical."

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Apostolic succession" as a doctrine of validly ordained bishops; "apostolic" as an institutional or denominational adjective; or Martin's "apostolic authority" as jurisdiction.

**World Hearing:**
At Tours, the apostles' power and standing possessed by a living man, shown by raising the dead and confronting an emperor; at Marseilles, the perfection of love the ascent reaches and the antiquity of the common life; at Lérins, a See — three referents that never argue because they never meet.
