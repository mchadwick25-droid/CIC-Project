*Voice note:* two distinct usages by voice — Vincent's "Apostolic See" is Rome (Lérins); Cassian's "Pope" is a Gallic bishop (Marseilles). Written in close third person from inside the world, keeping the referents apart.

---

## Quick Meaning

For this world "Pope" is a bishop's title of honour, not Rome's alone: Cassian addresses Castor of Apta Julia as "most blessed Pope Castor" and Leontius as "blessed Pope, Leontius"; while Vincent's "Apostolic See" is Rome, whose "twofold authority" — Sixtus and Celestine — crowns his argument, and whose Pope Stephen, "Prelate of the Apostolic See," laid down "Let there be no innovation."

---

## World Meaning

Cassian's books are written to Popes who are bishops of Provence. The *Institutes* open to "most blessed Pope Castor," at whose request the work is written for his "new monastery"; the fifth book returns to him, "O most blessed Pope Castor, more than ever"; and the first Conferences honour "the commands of Pope Castor of blessed memory, and your wishes, O blessed Pope Leontius and holy brother Helladius." The word is what one calls a bishop one reveres; the sees are Apta Julia and, it seems, Fréjus. The same word in Vincent's mouth belongs to Rome, and to Rome as the keeper of antiquity. Pope Stephen "of blessed memory, Prelate of the Apostolic See, in conjunction indeed with his colleagues but yet himself the foremost," resisted rebaptism with the rule "Let there be no innovation—nothing but what has been handed down"; and Vincent closes his case by adding "the twofold authority of the Apostolic See, first, that of holy Pope Sixtus, the venerable prelate who now adorns the Roman Church," and then Celestine's. Rome's weight, for Vincent, is that it is the see "which as he exceeded all others in the authority of his place" was foremost in refusing novelty.

In the same decade, on the editor's account, the Marseilles party to whom Celestine's letter was addressed — "Venerius of Marseilles" first among the Gallican bishops — "clung to their views in spite of the authority of the Pope." Rome is invoked by the one Lérins voice as antiquity's guardian and resisted, on that account, by Cassian's city. Sulpitius, for his part, simply calls Liberius "bishop of the city of Rome," one bishop among those the Arians exiled.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Pope" as the bishop of Rome exclusively, with the later constitutional office read back; Vincent as a proof-text for papal supremacy, or Cassian's "Pope Castor" as an error.

**World Hearing:**
A title of honour for revered bishops in the West — Castor, Leontius — and, in Vincent, Rome as the see foremost in guarding what was handed down, its authority "twofold" because two of its bishops refused novelty; a Rome invoked at Lérins and, on the editor's account, resisted at Marseilles in the same years.
