## Quick Meaning

For this world "Catholic" is defined by a rule, not a confession: "that is truly and in the strictest sense 'Catholic,' which, as the name itself and the reason of the thing declare, comprehends all universally" — the faith "believed everywhere, always, by all" — so that "the true and genuine Catholic" is the one who "resolves that he will believe that, and that only, which he is sure the Catholic Church has held universally and from ancient time"; Cassian applies the same word to "the approved Catholic fathers" and to "the Catholic rule" of monastic custom.

## World Meaning

Vincent's whole book is an answer to one question: "how and by what sure and so to speak universal rule I may be able to distinguish the truth of Catholic faith from the falsehood of heretical pravity." The answer defines the word by its meaning. "In the Catholic Church itself, all possible care must be taken, that we hold that faith which has been believed everywhere, always, by all. For that is truly and in the strictest sense 'Catholic,' which, as the name itself and the reason of the thing declare, comprehends all universally. This rule we shall observe if we follow universality, antiquity, consent." A Catholic is therefore a kind of person: "he is the true and genuine Catholic who loves the truth of God, who loves the Church, who loves the Body of Christ, ... who ... resolves that he will believe that, and that only, which he is sure the Catholic Church has held universally and from ancient time; but that whatsoever new and unheard-of doctrine he shall find to have been furtively introduced by some one or another, besides that of all, or contrary to that of all the saints, this, he will understand, does not pertain to religion." The Pelagians call out "to Catholics, 'Take our word, follow our lead.'" And when a part cuts itself off "from the communion of the universal faith," the Catholic prefers "the soundness of the whole body."

Cassian uses the word of the same authority in a monk's room. The good money-changer tests whether a thought is "stamped by those who have the right to coin, i.e., the approved Catholic fathers"; the teaching on grace is "laid down by all the Catholic fathers who have taught perfection of heart not by empty disputes of words, but in deed and act"; Germanus's objections let "the value of the Catholic faith" be "better established." And of sackcloth and dress, the Egyptians reject what is "not in accordance with the Catholic rule. For the opinion of a few ought not to be preferred" to the many. "The need of the Catholic faith compelled me to visit" other provinces, Piamun says. At Tours the word is a party name: Priscillian's clergy "had lately adopted the cause of Priscillian, and revolted from the Catholics." One word, three rooms — a rule, a stamp, a side — and in each the same test of the many and the old against the few and the new.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Roman Catholic" — a confession, a denomination, or a communion under the bishop of Rome, contrasted with "Protestant" or "Orthodox."

**World Hearing:**
What "comprehends all universally" — believed everywhere, always, by all — tested by universality, antiquity, and consent; the stamp of "the approved Catholic fathers" on a thought; the "Catholic rule" of the monasteries against "the opinion of a few"; and the side the Priscillianists revolted from — a rule, not a confession.
