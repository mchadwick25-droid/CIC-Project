## Quick Meaning

In Cassian's Marseilles, Abbot Chaeremon's ladder: "three things which enable men to control their faults; viz., either the fear of hell or of laws even now imposed; or the hope and desire of the kingdom of heaven; or a liking for goodness itself and the love of virtue" — slave, hireling, son — with the command to "mount with perfect ardour of mind from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God."

---

## World Meaning

Chaeremon is asked how faults are overcome, and answers with an ascent. The first restraint is "the fear of hell or of laws even now imposed"; the second, "the hope and desire of the kingdom of heaven," which "is compared not to a slave but to a hireling, because it looks for the payment of its recompense"; the third, "a liking for goodness itself and the love of virtue," the son's. The prodigal, returning, "desires the condition of a hireling and thinks about the remuneration" — "How many hired servants ..." — and is received as a son. "Wherefore we must do our best to mount with perfect ardour of mind from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God, and the love of the virtues themselves." The formation manual ends the same way: humility "leads you on by a still higher step to love which knows no fear; and through this you begin, without any effort and as it were naturally, to keep up everything that you formerly observed not without fear of punishment; no longer now from regard of punishment or fear of it but from love of goodness itself, and delight in virtue." Fear is where the junior begins and where the son no longer lives.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:** fear of hell read either as the whole content of ancient piety, or dismissed as an embarrassing primitive stage to be explained away.

**World Hearing:** it is named, deliberately, as the first and lowest rung of a received ladder (Chaeremon's) the monk is formed to climb past — a starting restraint, not an endpoint, and not something the formation program is ashamed to name as where beginners actually stand.
