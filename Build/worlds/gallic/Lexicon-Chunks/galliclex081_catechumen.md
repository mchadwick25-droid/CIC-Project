---

## Quick Meaning

At Tours a catechumen is one enrolled but not yet baptized — Martin himself, who as a boy "begged that he might become a catechumen" and was still one when Christ appeared in his cloak saying "Martin, who is still but a catechumen, clothed me with this robe"; the disciple who dies unbaptized and is raised; and a whole heathen crowd "made ... all catechumens, by placing his hand upon the whole of them."

---

## World Meaning

Martin, "when he was ten years old ... betook himself, against the wish of his parents, to the Church, and begged that he might become a catechumen." Still a catechumen as a soldier, he gave half his cloak, and that night heard Christ say "to the multitude of angels standing round—'Martin, who is still but a catechumen, clothed me with this robe'"; and he "flew to be baptized." At his first monastery "a certain catechumen joined him," fell ill, and died "without receiving baptism"; Martin stretched himself on the body, and "Martin restores a Catechumen to Life" — the first of his powers made known. On the plain outside Tours he "made them all catechumens, by placing his hand upon the whole of them," and said "that, not without reason, were these made catechumens in that plain where the martyrs were wont to be consecrated." The catechumen is where the north's power and mission meet the individual body.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:** "catechumen" assumed to name a brief administrative waiting period before an expected, near-default infant baptism.

**World Hearing:** at Tours the status can last years into adult soldiering (Martin remains a catechumen well into his military service), and the state itself is where the north's literature locates the first breaking-through of Martin's power (the catechumen raised, not yet baptized), not friction to be resolved quickly and left behind.
