## Quick Meaning

For this world "the world" is what a monk has left — kin, property, honours, "the delights of this world," "secular business" — named from the far side of the renunciation as a warfare one has been discharged from and a sea one has come out of, and never as a place from which God is absent.

---

## World Meaning

Everyone in this world speaks of the world from outside it. Vincent begins his book by saying why: "whereas I was at one time involved in the manifold and deplorable tempests of secular warfare, I have now at length, under Christ's auspices, cast anchor in the harbour of religion." Abbot Moses greets the two Gauls as men who "despised the love of kinsfolk, and scorned your fatherland, and the delights of this world, and passed through so many countries" to reach him. The new monk is stripped so that he may know "that he has laid aside all worldly pride, and come down to the want and poverty of Christ" — and his worldly clothes are kept by the steward in case he cools. The worst thing accidie does is to send the soldier of Christ back: he "becomes a runaway from His service, and a deserter, and 'entangles himself in secular business.'" Martin's whole conversation with Sulpitius presses "that the allurements of this world and secular burdens were to be abandoned in order that we might be free and unencumbered in following the Lord Jesus." The world is a set of burdens and allurements; the words for it are a soldier's and a sailor's.

But the world is not godless. Abbot John marvels at a layman who "was living in the world and bound by the ties of marriage" and yet casts out a demon by "the utterance of his name" — the old man is "the more astonished at him because he saw that he had on" a layman's dress. The line the monk crosses is one of profession, not of power. And Salvian, writing to the Romans of Marseilles rather than to monks, describes the world the religious have left as a place where God's judgment is being executed now, in the ruin of the provinces — the world seen from a presbyter's window rather than a cell's, and under the same God.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
"Secular" as the non-religious sphere, or secular government versus the Church; "the world" as society to be despised or as the created order; monastic contempt of the world as misanthropy.

**World Hearing:**
What one has been discharged from and sailed out of — kin, property, honours, business, warfare — named always from the harbour; a place of burdens and allurements, not of God's absence, where a married man may still cast out a demon, and where, in one Marseilles voice, God's judgment is being carried out in the present.
