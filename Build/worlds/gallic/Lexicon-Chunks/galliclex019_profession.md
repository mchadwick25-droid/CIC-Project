## Quick Meaning

For this world the profession is the monastic undertaking as a binding public commitment with its own "goal and end" — what a soldier makes when he "professed himself a monk," what a false monk assumes "under the profession of a monk," and what a man "turned out a deserter or lukewarm" has betrayed.

---

## World Meaning

Cassian makes the word carry the whole life. "Our profession too has its own goal and end, for which we undergo all sorts of toils not merely without weariness but actually with delight" — the end is the kingdom, the goal purity of heart, and everything else is measured by them. Dress is chosen for "the humble character of our profession," not for novelty. The man pulled from his cell by accidie "begins to forget the object of his profession, which is nothing but meditation and contemplation of that divine purity." And the profession is what a monk is answerable for: Abbot Pinufius warns the newly received that if he had been "too easily admitted by us without realizing the responsibility of this profession," he might have "turned out a deserter or lukewarm." The Sarabaites are the counterfeit — "making their renunciation only as a public profession, i.e., before the face of men," and then living as they please. A profession is public, and the world knows it can be faked.

At Tours the word attaches to persons and to oaths. A soldier who "had renounced the military life in the Church, having professed himself a monk," pleads that "he was a soldier of Christ, and that she also had taken the oath of allegiance in the same service"; Martin answers, "Let not a woman enter the camp of men." Anatolius, "having, under the profession of a monk, falsely assumed every appearance of humility and innocence," is the north's Sarabaite — a profession worn as a disguise until Martin's presence strips it. The boy Martin "would have followed up that desire with the necessary vows, had not his as yet too youthful age prevented," and Sulpitius says of the dying bishop that "both by vow and virtues he was alike able and willing to be a martyr." At Tours the profession is a vow sworn like a soldier's oath; at Marseilles it is the undertaking whose "goal and end" order a whole life.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A career ("the profession"); or the later formal rite of solemn vows with canonical consequences; or "profession of faith" as a creedal declaration.

**World Hearing:**
A public undertaking of a whole way of life, with its own goal and end and its own "responsibility," sworn at Tours like a soldier's oath and betrayed as a soldier deserts; a thing that can be assumed falsely "before the face of men," which is why the world names its counterfeits.
