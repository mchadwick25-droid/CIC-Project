## SUBSTANTIAL FINDINGS

### S1. Twelve of the thirteen Tier-3 chunks omit Distortion Risk — the one section the Framework requires at *all* tiers — while including Key Sources, which the Template says to omit at Tier 3. Three of the twelve carry the [DR] tag.

**Locus:** `Lexicon-Chunks/galliclex069_sarabaite.md`, `070_brethren`, `071_the-religious`,
`072_fear-hope-love`, `073_kingdom-within`, `075_angels`, `076_antichrist`, `077_doctor-expositor`,
`078_anathema`, `079_commonitory-peregrinus`, `080_theotocos`, `081_catechumen`. (Only `074_massilians`
has the section.)

**What's wrong.** Lexicon Framework V2.1 Part VI, Content Sections, is explicit:

> Distortion Risk (all tiers — at minimum a single-line Modern Hearing / World Hearing pairing)

Part III repeats it: a Tier 3 entry contains "Quick Meaning, and, where applicable, a single-line
Distortion Risk note." The Chunk Template's Distortion Risk section carries **no** tier-limit note,
unlike Key Sources ("{Include for Tier 1 and Tier 2. Omit for Tier 3.}") and Ecological Function
("For Tier 3 terms, this section may be omitted"). The twelve chunks did the opposite of both
instructions: they dropped the required section and kept the optional-to-drop one.

Three of the twelve carry [DR] — High Distortion Risk, defined by the Framework as a gap large enough
that "encounter is likely to fail without explicit correction":

- **071 the religious / servants of God** — Tags `SC, RT, DR`. No pairing.
- **075 angels** — Tags `SC, DR, RT`. No pairing.
- **076 Antichrist** — Tags `SC, DR`. No pairing.

A [DR]-tagged term with no Modern Hearing / World Hearing pairing defeats the Distortion Risk
Principle and Part V's whole account of what the section is for ("to support a representative voice's
ability to correct a likely modern misunderstanding in real time"). "Antichrist" and "angels" are
precisely the terms a modern participant will import a foreign frame onto.

The omission is **undisclosed**. Each of the twelve ends: "Completed per
`L4-Templates/Deployment_Lexicon_Chunk_Template.md` V1.0. No brackets or builder notes remain. …
Ecological Function omitted (Tier 3)." Only the *permitted* omission is recorded.

There is a second-order problem. Doc_06 §2.5 keeps 069 Sarabaite at Tier 3 on the ground that
"Quick Meaning suffices," and the Framework warns that "a Tier 3 entry that begins to require [World
Meaning, Ecological Function, source citation] should be reclassified to Tier 2 rather than expanded
in place." Every one of the thirteen Tier-3 chunks carries a 148–246-word World Meaning **and** a Key
Sources list. They were expanded in place. That does not by itself force reclassification — I do not
think any of these thirteen belongs at Tier 2 on ecological weight, and I am not reopening the tier —
but the fix round should decide deliberately whether the Tier-3 chunks are Tier-3 entries with extra
prose, or Tier-2 entries mislabelled, rather than leaving the two spec deviations to cancel each other
out silently.

**How I verified.** Recount of `^## ` headings across all 81 chunk files, cross-tabulated against the
Tier field parsed from each chunk's own front-matter, then `grep -c "Modern Hearing"` per Tier-3 file
(result: 1 of 13, in 074), then read the Final Assembly Instruction of 069, 071, 075 and 076 in full.
Framework text read directly at `Lexicon_Framework_V2.1_extracted.txt` line 245 and Part III.

**Remedy.** Add a single-line Modern Hearing / World Hearing pairing to all twelve — non-negotiable for
071, 075 and 076, which carry the tag. Then either drop Key Sources at Tier 3 per the Template, or add
one clause to each Final Assembly Instruction stating the retention and why. Add a Distortion-Risk
presence column to the index's check sheets so the gap cannot recur unnoticed.

### S2. Doc_06 §5 item 2's Latin non-finding is wrong: *puritas* **is** attested in Hilary of Arles's own Latin (row 27), in the phrase *consentanea puritas pectoris* — and it is load-bearing for the "single-voice" claim on the term Doc_06 itself calls the most consequential single-voice term in the lexicon.

**Locus:** `gallic_Doc06_Full_Lexicon_Development.md` §5 item 2; and
`Lexicon-Chunks/galliclex006_purity-of-heart.md`, Key Sources, Latin-lemma line.

**What's wrong.** Doc_06 §5 item 2 reports the bounded Latin-lemma grep and states:

> **Not found in either:** *puritas*, *discretio*, *regula*, *antiquitas*, *miles*, *liberum arbitrium*.

*discretio*, *regula*, *antiquitas*, *miles* and *liberum arbitrium* are all genuinely absent from both
files — I confirmed each. ***puritas* is not.** It occurs once in
`cic/texts/hilary-arles_sermo-de-vita-sancti-honorati_migne-pl50.txt`, in Hilary's description of
Honoratus:

> …os illud congruens vitæ, in quo erat **consentanea puritas pectoris**…

That is not a bare cognate. It is *puritas* governing a heart-word (*pectoris*), in a funeral sermon for
the founder of Lérins, by his own successor at Arles — a Native row (27), a second Gallic voice, and
outside Cassian.

**Why it matters beyond the erratum.** Doc_06 §2.2 keeps 006 at Tier 1 as "Single-voice (Cassian) …
the most consequential single-voice term in the lexicon," and chunk 006's Author Gravity note repeats
"single-voice (Cassian)." Chunk 006's Latin line then says *puritas cordis* "is not attested in the
vendored translation, and not found in the bounded Latin grep of rows 26–27 or the Faustus file (row 24)
this pass." The narrower claim — that the exact phrase *puritas cordis* is absent — is true, and I
verified it. But the chunk presents the grep as having returned nothing, when the grep in fact returns a
near-cognate phrase in the very construction at issue, in a second Gallic voice. This is exactly the
evidence the §5 item 2 pass was designed to surface, and it is nowhere in the build: I grepped
`puritas` and `pectoris` across Doc_01–Doc_05, the Registry, all review files and all 81 chunks — the
only hits are the "unverified — no lemma in the vendored translation" notes.

I am **not** proposing the tier change. *Puritas pectoris* is a rhetorical phrase in a panegyric, not
Cassian's technical *scopos*, and the sense is unread. But "single-voice, and here is the one other
Gallic Latin occurrence of the lemma, in a different sense" is a materially different — and better —
disclosure than "not found."

**How I verified.** Whitespace-flattened, case-insensitive regex `purit[a-z]*` over the row-27 file
(1 hit, exact form `puritas`, context printed above); the same over the row-26 Eucherius file (0 hits);
word-boundary checks on `puritas / puritate / puritatem` in both; then `grep -rni "puritas\|pectoris"`
across the whole world-build directory.

**Remedy.** Correct §5 item 2's "Not found in either" list to drop *puritas*, and add it to the
"Attested in Hilary of Arles's own Latin (row 27)" list with the phrase and the sense-unread caveat.
Amend chunk 006's Latin-lemma line to say that *puritas cordis* is unattested but *puritas pectoris*
occurs once in row 27, sense not read. Amend chunk 006's Author Gravity note to "single-voice for the
technical term (Cassian); the bare lemma occurs once in a second Gallic voice (row 27), in a
non-technical sense." Carry as a Latin-pass item.
