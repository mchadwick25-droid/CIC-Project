## Step 1 — independent ten-item recall list (fresh subagent, blind)

Reproduced verbatim from the subagent's report:

1. **Sulpicius Severus, *Vita Sancti Martini*.** The foundational text of
   the whole field: the eyewitness-framed life of Martin of Tours that
   establishes Ligugé and Marmoutier as monastic sites.
2. **Sulpicius Severus, *Dialogi* (and the *Epistulae*).** Supplements the
   *Vita* with further material and Sulpicius's own defense of Martin's
   cult.
3. **John Cassian, *Institutes*.** Cassian's rule-like manual for cenobitic
   life at Marseilles, transmitting Egyptian ascetic practice to Gaul.
4. **John Cassian, *Conferences*.** Contains Conference 13, the text that
   triggered the grace controversy.
5. **Prosper of Aquitaine, *Contra Collatorem*.** Prosper's direct
   polemical attack on Cassian's Conference 13.
6. **Augustine, *De Praedestinatione Sanctorum* and *De Dono
   Perseverantiae*.** The late anti-Pelagian treatises addressed to
   Prosper and Hilary of Gaul.
7. **Vincent of Lérins, *Commonitorium*.** The Lérins community's own
   contribution to doctrinal method.
8. **Faustus of Riez, *De Gratia*.** The fullest surviving systematic
   "semi-Pelagian" treatise.
9. **Philip Rousseau, *Ascetics, Authority, and the Church in the Age of
   Jerome and Cassian*** (Oxford, 1978). The classic modern monograph on
   Cassian's monastic theology and institutional authority.
10. **Raymond Van Dam, *Leadership and Community in Late Antique Gaul***
    (California, 1985). The key modern social/political study of Martin,
    Sulpicius, and Gallic episcopal-monastic culture.

The subagent also named, as honorable mentions outside its own ten:
Stancliffe's *St. Martin and His Hagiographer* and Markus's *The End of
Ancient Christianity*.
