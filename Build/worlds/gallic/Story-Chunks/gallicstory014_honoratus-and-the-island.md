## Story Text

This is how the tradition of Lérins remembers its founder — in the words, as far as they can be read, of the disciple who succeeded him at Arles and preached his life at his death.

Honoratus, Hilary says, had been drawn from his homeland by desire for the desert, and Christ invited him "into a desert near this city" — *in eremum huic urbi propinquam* — an island that stood empty "because of the excess of its squalor" and was "unapproachable for fear of venomous creatures," lying not far under the Alpine ridge. Besides the opportunity of seclusion, he was drawn there by the nearness and love of the bishop Leontius, "a holy and most blessed man in Christ" — though many, in a new boldness, tried to hold him back, the neighbours telling him of "that terrible desolation," competing "in the ambition of faith" to keep him within their own borders. But he, "impatient of human society and longing to be cut off from the world even by the barrier of the sea," carried in heart and mouth, now to himself, now to his own, the psalm *Thou shalt walk upon the asp and the basilisk, and trample the lion and the dragon,* and the Lord's promise, *Behold, I have given you power to tread upon serpents and scorpions.* "So he goes in unafraid," Hilary says, "and scatters the fear of his own by his own security. The horror of the solitude flees; the crowd of serpents gives way." Hilary counts it among his master's miracles and merits — *inter miracula ac merita* — that the serpents, so often met in those parts (the OCR's word for the place, "aridilabus," is not securely legible and is not rendered), "as we have seen," stirred up especially by the sea's heat, were never a danger to anyone, nor even a fear.

"Why should I delay longer?" the sermon goes on. With Christ, "so to speak, co-operating," every adversity that had deterred men before was overcome, and "your Honoratus" pitched "a certain camp of God" there; the place that had long driven men from dwelling in it "is lit up by angelic offices." "The hiding-place is illuminated while the light is hidden."

And then the sentence this world's own construction has quoted at every step: "Here he was first bound to the long-avoided office of the clergy; here the priestly fillet fastens on its fugitive; and he who had refused to go to the dignity — the dignity came to him." *Hic primum illigatur diu evitati clericatus officio; hic refugam suum sacerdotalis infula innectit; et qui ire ad dignitatem detrectaverat, ad ipsum dignitas venit.* He appeared there a presbyter worthy of honour not twofold only but manifold — and "he kept a monk's humility as entire in the priesthood as, when a monk, he had fully possessed the merits of the priesthood."

## Tier Justification

Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition — by the governing text's own description (CF V7.4): "hagiographic narrative is a specific type within this tier" and, of Tier 3 generally, "the account of what a formed life looks like is itself genuine formation ecology evidence... The formation ideal communicated is credible evidence; the specific events claimed are not" (corrected, finding H1 — the prior draft quoted a sibling world's own paraphrase as if it were the Framework's own wording). This is a disciple's funeral sermon on his master, a genre that idealizes by design; its named markers are all present (the founder who flees rank; the waste tamed by a psalm; the serpents that yield; a "camp of God" lit by "angelic offices"; the office that overtakes the fugitive). Gennadius (ch. LXX, ancient text) independently attests that Hilary wrote "his Life of Saint Honoratus, his predecessor" — so the tradition's existence and attribution are secure — but the sermon's own claims about events are exactly what the tier says they are: the tradition's witness to what it believed a formed life could become.

Why not Tier 1, though the author is named, socially located, and close: because the genre is the decisive fact for this text as for gallicstory001 and 003, and because — a limit peculiar to this chunk — this build cannot yet read the whole sermon and cannot vouch for the sense of its own renderings. The Confidence field carries that: Contested for the portrait, and Inferential/Thin not only for events but for *wording*, the status Doc_04 §10 item 6 and Doc_06 §5 item 2 assign to every rendering from rows 26–27. This chunk's Latin is normalized from OCR whose own spellings differ (e.g. "iunectit," "dia evitati," "llonoratus"); the normalization is disclosed, per Doc_05's finding C11, rather than left for a reader to discover by grep.

Genuine uncertainty at construction, recorded: whether a story whose wording is Inferential/Thin should be built at all. It is built — bounded to the lines actually read, with nothing supplied from outside them — because the alternative would leave the world's own bishop-forming house with no story at all in a repository of fourteen, and because the *presence* of every element is checkable by grep at the lines given. A full read of row 27 with a stated translation method (Doc_05 §10D item 7) would let a future pass confirm, correct, or extend this chunk; until then its Story Text is the shortest a Tier 3 story can be while still being a story.

## Usage Guidance

The Representative may offer this story as the tradition's own account of what a formed life looks like, with the hagiographic character made explicit and — uniquely in this repository — the wording's status made explicit too: "This is how Hilary of Arles, at his master's death, remembered Honoratus coming to the island — as far as this world's record lets us read him." The serpents' yielding and the angelic offices are not offered as historical fact; the formation ideal — a man who fled rank, feared nothing on the island, and kept a monk's humility inside the priesthood that overtook him — is what the Representative carries.

Additional guidance specific to this story: the Representative must not quote this chunk's English as Hilary's words. The Latin phrases given may be cited as present in the text; their English is a rendering. If a participant asks what Lérins was like to live in — its rule, its office, its day — the Representative must say plainly that this story does not say and that nothing in this world's evidence does (Doc_05 §10A; the Absent Story Note below). If asked whether Honoratus's capture resembled Martin's, the Representative may point to gallicstory004 and gallicstory012 and state that the resemblance is this build's finding (Doc_07 §2C), since the sermon does not mention Martin, and Sulpitius never mentions Lérins.

## Absent Story Note

What a participant might reasonably expect here, and cannot have, is a story from *inside* Lérins — a day, a rule kept, a novice received, an elder's saying, a monk's prayer on the island. This chunk is the closest this world's Native corpus comes, and it is a story about the founder's arrival and his elevation, told at his funeral by the man who succeeded him as a bishop: it narrates the island's *edges* (the serpents at the shore; the fillet that took him off it), not its interior. Doc_05 §10A found Lérins to be "the ecology's most consequential institution … its least documented from inside"; Doc_07 §2A found "nothing ritual is attested" for the island; Doc_08 Force 3B-2 traces the thinness to transmission — "the English editorial projects that carried the three founders never selected the Lérins network's own Latin" — so that the story reaches this build as rough OCR of Migne. The silence is therefore partly a transmission gap (Hilary's sermon exists in full, unread here; Eucherius's *De Laude Eremi* is in the same condition) and partly the world's own: Vincent, the one Lérins voice this build reads in full, tells nothing of the island but that he dwells "in the seclusion of a Monastery" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]). The Representative's honest position is that it can tell how the founder came to Lérins and how he left it, and not what he or anyone did there in between.
