## 2. Integration Lenses

Smart's seven dimensions are asked in order, of each node; a thin yield is stated as a finding. Then the two CiC additions. Each lens states what it reveals, grounds every claim in Doc_01–Doc_06, names the gravities most visible as Doc_04 classified them, and closes with what it shows that Doc_05 alone did not.

### 2A. Ritual/Practical

**What this lens reveals for this world:** what a body actually did, repeatedly, in each house — and that the two repertoires sort onto one axis (display against concealment) while sharing exactly one ritual grammar.

**At Tours** the repertoire is public and bodily: exorcism performed prostrate in sackcloth and ashes, "the doors being bolted," in which Martin "touched no one with his hands, and reproached no one in words" (*Dial.* III.6; Doc_06 chunk 051); oil blessed, "such is the custom" (*Dial.* III.3; chunk 049); the sign of the cross that protects "not by shield or helmet" (*Vita* ch. IV; Doc_04 G8); a virgin consecrated by "placing upon her the dress characteristic of virginity" (*Vita* ch. XIX; Doc_05 §1.3); a heathen crowd made catechumens "by placing his hand upon the whole of them" (*Dial.* II.4; chunk 053). What Marmoutier sang, and at what hours, is not documented (Doc_05 §3). **At Marseilles** the repertoire is the *Institutes*' program, Egyptian by Cassian's own framing, read in a Gallic cell: the entrance rite, "prostrate at the feet of all the brethren that pass by," then stripping and reclothing (*Inst.* IV.3, IV.5; Doc_05 §2.1); twelve psalms at evening and night, "no appointment of man's invention" but "brought down from heaven to the fathers by the ministry of an angel" (*Inst.* II.4–5; chunk 007); daily disclosure of thoughts to the senior (IV.9); public penance on the ground and suspension from prayer (IV.16, II.16; chunk 067); meals "with their hoods drawn down over their eyelids" (IV.17; Doc_05 §2.3). **At Lérins** nothing ritual is attested: the *Commonitory* yields no practice beyond "the seclusion of a Monastery" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]), and whether the island kept any office is unknown (Doc_05 §10B). That is a finding: the world's best-documented house for producing bishops is its least-documented house for what its members did each day (Doc_05 §10A).

**Gravities most visible:** G6 *virtus* (Tensional — the north's repertoire is made of it), G7 the interior road (Supporting, node-bound southern — the office is its skeleton, Doc_05 §3), G8 the soldier of Christ (Supporting, cross-node — the idiom of both), G4 received-not-invented (Primary — the angel's number; *Inst.* I.2's reception criterion).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §1.2 named the sackcloth divergence and §2.2 tabulated the day; what appears when the whole repertoire is held together is that the two nodes' rites sort onto a single axis of *visibility*. Every characteristic northern act is done before witnesses in a conspicuous garment; every characteristic southern act is a discipline of not being seen or heard — the hood over the eyes, no groan at the synaxis (*Inst.* II.10), sackcloth refused "as being visible to all and conspicuous" (*Inst.* I.2; chunk 065). The axis holds across these three. The one rite the nodes share is the sanction. At Tours a single coerced communion leaves the saint with "a diminution of his power on account of the evil of that communion" (*Dial.* III.13); at Marseilles the monk suspended from prayer is "delivered unto Satan" (*Inst.* II.16; chunk 061). Ritual life is split at the level of display and unified at the level of exclusion — the shared grammar is the negative one.

### 2B. Experiential/Emotional

**What this lens reveals for this world:** the Tier-1 vocabulary lets the two affective economies Doc_05 §8 named be read as programs rather than temperaments — and shows that one affective sign, tears, is made to mark opposite curves at the two nodes.

The south's mature register has a name and a picture: "purity of heart," the "definite mark" at which the archer aims (*Conf.* I.4; chunk 006); its shape is Chaeremon's ladder, "from this fear to hope, from hope to the love of God" (*Conf.* XI.7; chunk 072); its named fear is not damnation but cooling — the renunciant who "grow[s] cold in regard to the rule of their institution" (*Inst.* IV.6; chunk 033), the monk become "a runaway from His service, and a deserter" (*Inst.* X.3); its trained affect at the moment of felt success is a sentence, "Not I, but the grace of God with me" (*Inst.* XII.9; chunk 008); and its measure of advance is weeping, since "their compunction for their faults increases day by day in proportion as their purity of soul advances" (*Inst.* XII.15; chunk 040). The north's register is wonder and its policing: the *Life* is written so that readers "shall be roused" (*Vita* ch. I); grief at the funeral is permitted and corrected in one breath (*Ep.* III; Doc_05 §8); the north's fear is "a terror of judgment, a fear of punishment" (*Ep.* II; Doc_04 G10). Vincent's affect is "a certain awful expectation of the approach of the divine judgment" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]); Salvian's is indignation (Doc_05 §8). No ordinary believer's or woman's affect is visible in any voice (Doc_05 §1.1, §1.3; Article 20), and none is reconstructed here.

**Gravities most visible:** G7 (the south's whole affective program), G3 the grace-and-effort argument (Supporting at world level; Primary within the southern node — the "Not I" affect is its felt form), G6 (the north's wonder), G10 judgment (Supporting, provisional).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §8 found two affective economies — the south trains out of wonder into compunction, the north forms through wonder into imitation. The Tier-1 entries, read together, add that *compunction marks an opposite curve at each node*. At Marseilles tears rise with purity: the holier the monk, the more he weeps. At Tours the one attested compunction is Martin's after the Ithacian communion, when he "cured some of the possessed more slowly and with less grace than usual" and confessed with tears — and the angel's verdict is "Justly, O Martin, do you feel compunction" (*Dial.* III.13; chunk 040). The southern monk weeps because power is not his; the northern saint weeps because his power has lessened. In the south compunction is the emotional form of G3 (felt progress disowned to grace); in the north it is the emotional form of G6 (felt loss of *virtus* grieved). A Representative that hears "compunction" as one feeling risks missing why the two nodes' formation logics diverge at exactly this point — **disclosed, not smoothed over:** this reading is in tension with chunk 040's own closing line, which states "In both places compunction is what a healthy heart does, and what a cooled one has stopped doing," treating the two nodes as one sense. The tension is over emphasis rather than a fact either document gets wrong — this section's own claim is about what *triggers* the tears (rising purity against lessening power), which chunk 040's "healthy heart" formulation is broad enough to hold without being written to foreground it — but it is named here as a live disagreement, left open for Doc_06's own record, rather than resolved by editing an Approved chunk from this document.

### 2C. Narrative/Mythic

**What this lens reveals for this world:** which stories this world told of itself, in what form — and that its founding stories at every house narrate the same event in the same shape.

The north's stories are *virtutes*: Doc_04 G6 finds the *Vita*, *Letters*, and *Dialogues* "built of *virtutes*," each guaranteed by a witness (*Dial.* II.4; Doc_04 G5), with the discharge before Caesar — "Hitherto I have served you as a soldier: allow me now to become a soldier to God" (*Vita* ch. IV) — as the biography's hinge. The south's stories are the elders' *exempla* reported in conference — Paphnutius, the cook and the "three lentil beans," Pæsius and John (*Conf.* XVIII.15; *Inst.* IV.20, V.27; Doc_05 §6A) — and one origin-story received from Egypt, the angel who sang and vanished (*Inst.* II.5). Lérins's narrative is the harbour after shipwreck (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]; Eucherius, row 26; Doc_04 G2) and Hilary's *Life* of his predecessor (Gennadius ch. LXX, ancient text — "his Life of Saint Honoratus, his predecessor" ("master" is not Gennadius's word; it is Doc_05 §6B's own gloss); Doc_04 G5). Vincent's narrative form is the catalogue of heresiarchs, each arising "under a definite name, at a definite place, at a definite time" (*Comm.* ch. 24 [62]; chunk 060) — error told as a story with a birthday, the faith as what has none.

**Gravities most visible:** G6, G5 formation by named example (Supporting, cross-node — the eyewitness frame of both literatures), G1 the monk-bishop (Primary — below), G2 Egypt as the measure (Primary, split-mode — "Europe will not yield to it," *Dial.* III.17).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §4.4 gave the monk-bishop's from-within shape: authority is what one flees and is overtaken by. The narrative lens adds that the two nodes, which never cite each other (Doc_05 §7.3), tell their founder's elevation in one identical story-shape: Martin is lured out by a citizen "pretending that his wife was ill," "under a kind of guard escorted to the city," and made bishop while bishops are "impiously offering resistance" (*Vita* ch. IX; chunk 013); Honoratus, in his disciple's Latin, is the fugitive the priestly fillet fastens — "he who had refused to go to the dignity — the dignity came to him" (Hilary, row 27, *ad ipsum dignitas venit*; Doc_04 G1, wording Inferential/Thin); the one Egyptian bishop Cassian carries west, Archebius, is "carried off from the assembly of anchorites" (*Conf.* XI.2). The world's most characteristic fact (G1) is narrated at every house as *capture* — which is how the story-layer reconciles what the gravity-layer holds as a competition (G1 × G9, Doc_04 §4): nobody in the stories wanted the office. The lens also yields an absence: the one story the wider Latin church told about this world — Prosper's appeal, Celestine's letter — has no narrative form in any Native voice and survives only in Gibson's prolegomena, an editorial layer (Doc_02 §1.2, §11). This world narrated its saints and did not narrate its own controversy.

### 2D. Doctrinal/Philosophical

**What this lens reveals for this world:** a doctrinal ecology concentrated at one node and split into two products — Cassian's grace-and-effort teaching and Vincent's rule — which, held together, are one intellectual move made twice; and a northern node whose only doctrine is a doctrine of power.

The south's first product is the two-sided grace teaching: "the grace of God always co-operates with our will for its advantage" (*Conf.* XIII.13); grace and free will "seem opposed to each other, but really are in harmony" (XIII.11; chunk 009); and its terminus is a declared limit — "how God works all things in us and yet everything can be ascribed to free will, cannot be fully grasped by the mind and reason of man" (XIII.18; chunk 008). Doc_05 §2.4(v) found the teaching occupying eleven chapters of *Institutes* XII and the work's closing sentence (XII.33), with *Conf.* XIII arising as a formation question at morning service (XIII.1): the doctrine sits inside the formation manual. **Its contested status is carried, not resolved.** Doc_06 §3 tags 008 grace and 009 free will [CT] on two types: *Meaning* — whether Cassian's "co-operates" and "precedes" amount to Augustine's prevenient grace or to the position Prosper's *Contra Collatorem* attacked, the Chadwick/Casiday contest bound to the Contested chronology of *Conf.* XIII (Doc_01 §7, §11 item 5); and *Application to this world* — whether "semi-Pelagian," a sixteenth-century coinage present in this build's files only in the editors' apparatus (Gibson's headnote "On the Semi-Pelagianism of this Conference"; Doc_01 §7), fairly names a position whose own reporters concede that these brethren hold that "men's wills are anticipated by God's grace" (Aug. *Praed.* ch. 2, row 15, context only) and whose later Lérins-formed witness, Faustus, is remembered as teaching that grace "always invites, precedes and helps our will" (Gennadius ch. LXXXVI, ancient text; Doc_02 §1.4). Whether Vincent or Lérins held the position at all is Heurtley's editorial reading, Contested (Doc_02 §1.3). The south's second product is the rule — "universality, antiquity, consent" (*Comm.* ch. 2 [6]; chunk 011) — with its own limit: against the consent of the ancients no single teacher counts, "be he a bishop, be he a Confessor, be he a martyr" (ch. 28 [72]), and progress is admitted only as "real progress, not alteration of the faith" (ch. 23 [54]; chunk 012). **Tours** has no doctrine of grace, by chronology (Doc_04 G3), and no rule; its one doctrinal claim is about *virtus* — power "present," measurable, diminishable (chunk 010) — and its one scruple is G4's (*Vita* ch. XI).

**Gravities most visible:** G3 (directly), G4 (Primary — the ground of both southern products), G7 (the argument exists to defend the received program, Doc_04 G3), G6 (the north's only doctrine).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §9A listed eight patterns and called the grace teaching "two-sided by design." Read as one doctrinal ecology, the grace teaching and the rule are the *same move in two registers*: each refuses to let a single principle settle the question (neither grace nor will; neither one teacher nor one's own opinion), each ends not in a system but in a stated boundary of what may be claimed ("cannot be fully grasped"; "not an author but a keeper," *Comm.* ch. 22 [53]), and each takes a non-treatise form — a reported conference, a memory-aid. This is a doctrinal ecology deliberately non-systematic, whose genre is what was heard or received rather than what was reasoned out. And that shape is what made it vulnerable to a systematic opponent: Prosper's method against *Conf.* XIII, per Gibson's prolegomena, was to extract twelve propositions and judge eleven erroneous (Doc_02 §1.2, editorial) — a conference turned into theses. The world's doctrinal form and its doctrinal fate explain each other.

### 2E. Ethical/Legal

**What this lens reveals for this world:** not a thin dimension but one split by *whose law binds* at each node — external and evaded in the north, internal and codified in the south — and unified by a single rule of recognition.

**At Tours** the law is external and the saint's ethics is a stance toward it. The Priscillianists, "declared heretics by a sentence of the bishops," should have been expelled and no more; that "a secular ruler should be judge in an ecclesiastical cause" is "a foul and unheard-of indignity" (*SH* II.50; Doc_04 G9; chunk 060). Martin goes to Trier "to protect even heretics themselves" (*Dial.* III.11) and afterwards "never again did he attend a synod, and kept carefully aloof from all assemblies of bishops" (*Dial.* III.13). His personal ethic is refusal to use his standing — as bishop "wronged by the lowest clerics with impunity" (*Vita* ch. XXVI; Doc_05 §4.2), as soldier "it is not lawful for me to fight" (*Vita* ch. IV). **At Marseilles** the law is the house's own code, Egyptian by Cassian's framing: three grades of sanction — public penance until the Abbot bids rise, suspension from prayer, "stripes" or expulsion for the graver faults including "familiarity with women" (*Inst.* IV.16; Doc_05 §4.1); the steward's cupboard holding the renunciant's clothes against failure (IV.6). Above the house, on the editor's account, Celestine's letter warns that "priests ought not to teach so as to invade the episcopal prerogative," and the Massilians "clung to their views in spite of the authority of the Pope" (Gibson's prolegomena, editorial; Doc_02 §1.2). **At Lérins** law is conciliar and Pauline: an ancient General Council's decrees preferred "to the rashness and ignorance of a few" (*Comm.* ch. 3 [8]; chunk 059), the heretic "separated, segregated, excluded" (ch. 8 [23]). **Salvian** supplies the one Native judgment on the empire's own law — "the rich who are murdering the poor by their so-called remedies" (*Gov.* IV.6, Sanford), the Bagaudae as men "whom we ourselves have forced into crime" (V.6; Doc_05 §1.4, one polemical witness, Named Tension).

**Gravities most visible:** G9 ambivalence toward authority (Tensional — largely this lens's content), G4 (below), G7 (the house code as the interior road's discipline), G1 (the bishop who declines to use his office), G10 (Salvian's indictment).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §4.3 stated the criterion of legitimacy for offices — authority is legitimate insofar as it transmits. The ethical/legal lens shows that this world had a *jurisprudence*, and that its rule of recognition for any binding rule is the same criterion. The office's number binds because it is "no appointment of man's invention"; Ephesus binds because the bishops "innovated nothing, presumed nothing" (*Comm.* ch. 31 [82]); the cook is suspended because "everything that they possess is consecrated to the Lord" (*Inst.* IV.20); sackcloth is ruled out because "a rule for the monasteries is not, therefore, to be passed by us" against "the ancient decrees of the holy fathers" (*Inst.* I.2). And the one law the world refuses outright is refused in this idiom: the secular judge is "unheard-of" — a G4 verdict, novelty as the mark of illegitimacy, pronounced on a court. The north's flight from synods and the south's graded penances look like opposite legal cultures; they share one theory of what makes a rule a rule, which is why Martin can accept a synod's sentence and refuse an emperor's sword in the same sentence.

### 2F. Social/Institutional

**What this lens reveals for this world:** an institution whose social intake runs downward and whose output runs upward, inside one house.

Every Native voice is a literate, Latin, male member of the provincial elite (Doc_05 §1.1; Doc_02 §9, Inferential/Thin). Marmoutier's eighty disciples include "many … such as are deemed of noble rank" who "forced themselves down to this degree of humility" (*Vita* ch. X); Cassian's noble junior must "come down to the want and poverty of Christ" (*Inst.* IV.5); Honoratus and his brother could not reach obscurity, because the more their life was hidden the more their fame shone out (Hilary, row 27; Doc_05 §1.1, wording Inferential/Thin). The same houses send men upward: "we have seen numbers of these afterwards made bishops. For what city or church would there be that would not desire to have its priests from among those in the monastery of Martin?" (*Vita* ch. X); Honoratus, Hilary, Faustus to Arles and Riez (Heurtley, editorial; Gennadius chs. LXX, LXXXVI, ancient); the *Conferences*' dedicatee turns from one "presiding as he does over a large monastery" to "a Bishop blessed in name as well as merits" between two prefaces (*Conf.* Prefs. II–III; Doc_04 G1). The institutional map: Ligugé and Marmoutier under a founder-bishop; Castor's province "at present without monasteries" receiving a book (*Inst.* Pref.); Cassian's two houses, "one for men and one for women, which are still standing" c. 495 (Gennadius ch. LXII); Lérins, linked to Marseilles by a dedication whose Lérins identification is the editorial apparatus's (Registry row 9), with a named tutelage chain for a bishop's sons (Doc_02 §13A; Doc_05 §2.1, Sanford's editorial paraphrase). Women's houses existed at both nodes under men's jurisdiction; no woman's authority or voice within them is attested (Doc_05 §1.3, discharged there). No documented traffic between Tours and the south (Doc_01 §2.3, undisturbed since).

**Gravities most visible:** G1 (directly), G9 (its counter-pressure), G5 (the exemplar as the house's hub, Doc_05 §10B), G2 (Egypt as the south's institutional authority).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §4–§5 described the office as destination and the drain it causes. Holding intake and output together shows a *two-directional social escalator inside one institution*: entry is a descent the noble must force on himself, exit is an elevation the Church forces on him, and the world's vocabulary makes the descent the only legitimate route to the elevation — Honoratus "kept a monk's humility as entire in the priesthood as, when a monk, he had fully possessed the merits of the priesthood" (Hilary, row 27; Doc_04 G1). That the elite enter to descend and leave elevated is what makes the monk-bishop one social type rather than two careers.

### 2G. Material

**What this lens reveals for this world:** a record entirely text-transmitted — no archaeology, no documentary material — and, within the texts, a node-split in *what objects are for*, with one physical fact shared.

Doc_02 §5 and §9 record "none vendored" for documentary and archaeological material; the Registry's search_record logs a live search for material culture at the three sites as "No results — coverage limit." This world reaches this build as literature. Within it, matter is dense. **At Tours** objects carry power outward: threads "plucked from the sackcloth which he wore" that "wrought frequent miracles," straw the virgins divide, a letter in a fevered girl's bosom, oil whose blessing "could not possibly perish" (*Vita* chs. XVIII–XIX; *Dial.* II.8, III.3; chunk 049); the psalter opened at random against Defensor (*Vita* ch. IX; Doc_05 §3); a wooden cell for the master and caves for the brethren, reached by "a very narrow passage" (*Vita* ch. X; chunk 016); camel's hair, sackcloth, ashes. **At Marseilles** objects are consecrated property whose mishandling is a fault: "three lentil beans" suspend the cook from prayer because "everything that they possess is consecrated to the Lord" (*Inst.* IV.20); a broken earthenware jar (which they call "baucalis") is expiated by public penance (IV.16; chunk 067); the renunciant's worldly clothes wait in the steward's cupboard (IV.6); each Egyptian garment is a mystery of the inner life, refitted because "the severity of the winter does not allow us to be satisfied with slippers or tunics or a single frock" (*Inst.* I.10; chunk 066); the heart, "fastened within the barriers of the cell," "may be shut up in some perfectly secure harbour" (II.14 — the text's own image is of the heart, fastened by the cell, being the thing harboured). **At Lérins** the island is the one material image, the harbour that takes in the shipwrecked (Eucherius, row 26; Doc_04 G2, wording Inferential/Thin). The one physical fact both nodes share is the cold — the force Cassian names in his own voice as changing what he transmits (Doc_05 §1.4), which Marmoutier's camel's hair and sackcloth answer without a word about climate. What the sexual body did is material this build cannot see: three body/sexuality texts are absent from the vendored English edition (Doc_05 §1.2, §7.3; row 11).

**Gravities most visible:** G6 (objects as conductors of *virtus*), G7 (objects as accountable property), G2 (the climate clause), G8 (dress as armour).

**What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show.** Doc_05 §1.2 catalogued the body and §6B named memory in objects. Across nodes, the *direction of accountability* between person and thing reverses: at Tours the thing answers for the saint (his straw heals because he lay on it); at Marseilles the monk answers for the thing (he lies on the ground because he dropped it). Matter is a conductor in the north and a ledger in the south — Doc_04 §4's structural G6 × G7 tension seen at the level of a lentil and a thread, which is the level at which a formed person actually met it. The thinness is itself the finding: a world attested only in writing, in an edition that removed the body, is one whose material culture a Representative can speak of only as its texts do, never from a site.

### 2H. Boundary Structures *(CiC addition)*

**What this lens reveals for this world:** written as the Forces Framework's three movements — and, held together, that the boundary-instrument this world used most firmly was the one turned back on it from outside, so that its boundary logic generated its own sharpest external pressure.

**What this world was responding to.** Doc_01 §7 names the initiating force as "the felt inadequacy, for a subset of committed Latin-West Christians, of ordinary institutional Christian life as a sufficient arena for radical formation," in a setting where formation could proceed "without severing a monk from institutional authority or even from eventual episcopal office." From inside, each founding narrative is a departure: Martin's discharge, Vincent's retreat from "secular warfare" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [2]), Honoratus's flight from rank (Hilary, row 27). Behind these: Egypt as the standard — received as authority in the south, matched in the north (Doc_04 G2, split-mode); the Arian violence and Hilary of Poitiers's exile that scattered Martin before Ligugé (*Vita* ch. VI; Doc_05 §1.4 — force only, nothing of that world imported, row 33); the unconverted countryside that gave the north its mission-boundary (chunk 053). **What pressed from outside.** From Africa and Rome: the report Prosper carried, Celestine's letter naming "Venerius of Marseilles" first, and *Contra Collatorem* — pressure on the formation manual's own summit (Doc_05 §2.5(b)), received against a passage the manual itself titles "How this faith concerning the grace of God was delivered to us by the ancient Fathers" and which states it as "the genuine faith of the ancient fathers" (*Inst.* XII.19) — the polemical use of that antiquity-claim against Africa and Rome's own charge of novelty is this document's own inference, carried from Doc_05 §7.3, and not a framing the chapter states for itself. *Named Tension:* whether *Conf.* XIII answers Augustine or shares his question is Contested (Doc_01 §7), and Doc_05 §10C found the teaching in the *Institutes* regardless. From the court: Trier, answered by withdrawal from synods (G9). From the climate: received as "the weakness of our constitution" (*Inst.* IV.11). From the fisc and the frontier: received, in the one voice that admits them, as "the ever-present judgment of God" (*Gov.* VII.12; chunk 054) — a force that left in this world's formation literature, per Doc_05 §1.4, one indignant book and one discharge scene. From episcopal recruitment: received by making the desire for office a fault (*Inst.* XI.14; *Conf.* I.20) and the office a destiny (Doc_05 §2.5(c)). **What fractured from within.** Cassian and Sulpitius never meet in any text, and each literature is made of what the other refuses: the north's saint is an exorcist; the south's fathers "never reckoned those as good monks or free from the fault of vainglory, who professed themselves exorcists among men" (*Conf.* XV.7; Doc_04 §6 point 3) — a fracture the world did not experience as one, since neither side addressed the other (Doc_05 §7.3). Inside the south, Cassian refuses the wonders Eucherius admires (Doc_04 G6). Inside Marmoutier, Brictio sneers at "ridiculous fancies about visions" (*Dial.* III.15). XIX, ancient; Contested, Doc_02 §1.1) would, if true, place him across the south's doctrinal line. And the world's names for its own failures are internal: the Sarabaite, the lukewarm elder, the monk who "has grown cold through pride" (*Inst.* XII.30, title).

**Gravities and forces most visible:** G4 (the boundary-instrument), G9 (Tensional — the fracture Doc_04 calls the world's boundary), G2 (the Egypt boundary, "permeable inward" in the south, "comparative outward" in the north, Doc_05 §7.2), G3 (the boundary drawn without naming the man across it, Doc_05 §7.1), G10 (the frontier's only entry point).

### 2I. Formation Logic *(CiC addition — applied after all other lenses)*

**What this lens reveals for this world:** the single logic that makes the eight lenses one formation system — a formation whose product is a person who can claim nothing of what he has become.

**The fundamental aim.** *At Marseilles*, a monk whose every practice is aimed at "purity of heart, which is charity" (*Conf.* I.7), who knows that "fastings, vigils, meditation on the Scriptures, self-denial, and the abnegation of all possessions are not perfection, but aids to perfection," who lays every thought bare to the senior, keeps the angel's number, and who, at the moment he feels progress, says "Not I, but the grace of God with me" — because "perfection cannot possibly be gained without these, but … by these only without the grace of God nobody can ever attain it" (*Inst.* XII.14). *At Tours*, a disciple "disciplined after the example of the saintly master" (*Vita* ch. X), formed by what he has seen power do, keeping sackcloth as a weapon, whom the churches will come to fetch. *At Lérins*, "not an author but a keeper" (*Comm.* ch. 22 [53]). Across all three: a renunciant the Church has not let go of — a bishop who keeps "the objects and virtues of a monk" (*Vita* ch. X), or a presbyter who lets no bishop's singular opinion count.

**The mechanism.** The north forms by a person seen; the south by a program read in the teacher's absence — Cassian's readers receive the fathers "into their cells" (*Conf.* Pref. III), and Cassian doubts any Gallic monk kept Egypt's perseverance "unbroken even for a year" (*Inst.* IV.2); Lérins by a named succession of teachers (Doc_05 §2.1). Doc_05 §10B called this "two formation ecologies with one skeleton"; §2A–§2G show the two fleshes differ on one axis throughout — display against concealment, wonder against compunction, eyewitness against reported conference, power present against grace argued, conductor against ledger.

**The arc.** Entry is severance: a discharge, a shipwreck, a stripping and reclothing. The middle is fervour under threat of cooling — the affective plot Doc_05 §8 named, in which the characteristic failure is temperature (chunk 033), and the south's sanctioned ascent runs from fear to hope to love. Maturity at Marseilles is a tranquillity of heart that weeps more, not less; at Tours it is power exercised for the countryside and grieved when it lessens; at both it is the office one did not seek — fled and overtaken (Doc_05 §4.4). The stalled are named: the Sarabaite, the lukewarm elder, Brictio, the proud monk who wants to rule.

**How the lenses integrate.** Ritual (§2A) supplies the enacted split and the one shared sanction; Experiential (§2B) the two curves of one tear; Narrative (§2C) the capture-story that reconciles G1 and G9; Doctrinal (§2D) the non-systematic form that refuses a single principle; Ethical/Legal (§2E) the jurisprudence of antiquity; Social (§2F) the escalator of descent and elevation; Material (§2G) the conductor and the ledger; Boundary (§2H) the loop by which consent-drawn boundaries invite contest. What runs through all eight is one grammar of *reception*: power is "present," not owned (*Vita* ch. VII, Roberts's note *adesse virtutem*); progress is "the grace of God with me"; teaching is "not mine but the fathers'" (*Conf.* Pref. I); office is seizure; custom is "no appointment of man's invention"; the rule is "a matter brought to thee, not put forth by thee." Every lens finds the formed person disowning what he has become.

**Formation logic and confirmed gravities.** G4 (Primary) is the grammar itself; G1 (Primary) its institutional outcome; G2 (Primary, split-mode) where the grammar's content comes from in the south and what it is measured against in the north; G7 and G3 (Supporting) the southern program and its own last word on why the program is neither optional nor sufficient; G5 (Supporting, the hub) the mechanism, a person seen or read; G8 (Supporting) the idiom. G6 and G9 (Tensional) are the two places the grammar strains — the north's power is *displayed* even as it is disowned, the office is *taken* even as it is fled — strains the world holds rather than resolves.

**What this reveals that no individual document showed.** Doc_04 found the Primaries; Doc_05 found two ecologies and one skeleton; Doc_06 §4 found the three importations a Representative must resist — Egypt's content heard as Gaul's invention, the Benedictine back-projection, the confessional binary. The formation logic shows these three risks are one: each would make the formed person the *author* of something this world formed him to *receive*. The formation logic is, in this world's own vocabulary, a training in *not I*.

## 3. Additional Lenses (Where Evidence Supports)

### 3A. Memory Structures — applied

**Why separately, for this world:** Doc_05 §6B found memory to be an institution against forgetting; what neither §2C nor §3B carries is the *chronological* shape of that institution, which is this world's own. Its inherited memory is old and received — the angel's twelve, the apostolic community of Acts, "a succession of fathers and their traditions" (*Inst.* II.3–5), the collated ancients (*Comm.* ch. 28 [72]). Its memory of *itself* is made within the founders' own lifetimes: "Martin is praised with the divine psalms" at his own funeral (*Ep.* III); threads and straw cure while he lives; the *Life* is written by a man who knew him "which shall serve in future as an example" (*Vita* ch. I); Hilary writes the *Life* of his own predecessor; Vincent writes a remembrancer to "provide against my forgetfulness" (*Comm.* ch. 1 [3]; chunk 079) and loses "the greater part of the second book" to a thief (Gennadius ch. LXV). **Gravities most illuminated:** G4, G6, G5. **What this reveals:** a world whose rule is that only the old is legitimate memorializes itself in the present tense — and resolves the tension by casting self-memory as *transmission*: a *Life* is "an example," a *Commonitory* is a keeper's aid written "with the fidelity of a narrator rather than the presumption of an author." Its own memory-institutions are all first-generation, and every one is framed so as not to look new.

### 3B. Interpretive Ecology — applied

**Why separately, for this world:** this world produced an explicit interpretive rule, and Doc_05 §6C found in it "the interpretive ecology's most consequential single move" — a finding Authority-type lenses (§2E, §2F) do not carry, since it concerns how texts are read, not who commands. Doc_05 §6C attests seven modes: the psalter as the day's substance and the mind's food (*Inst.* II.11, II.15); typological reading of the hours (III.3); the garment as mystery; the fourfold sense (present, not organizing); the rule of faith as the rule of reading, because "owing to the depth of Holy Scripture, all do not accept it in one and the same sense" (*Comm.* ch. 2 [4–6]; chunk 011); Scripture as oracle (*Vita* ch. ## 4. Forces as Integration Lens

Egypt's standard arrived as a critique (Doc_05 §2.5(a)): the first-person Gallic remarks in the *Institutes* — the hurried prostration, the loud Gloria the East "never heard," the return to bed after Mattins, the perseverance no Gallic monk kept "unbroken even for a year" — mean the southern program was received as a standing correction of what Gaul already did; the formation logic's "not I" is, under forces, also "not we." **The single most important thing the forces-as-integration-lens reveals.** This world's forces profile is inverted relative to its history. The force historians treat as the period's dominant one — the collapse of Roman Gaul — left one book and one discharge scene; the forces that actually shaped its formation were a foreign standard received as rebuke, a report carried to Africa and Rome, and the churches' hunger for its monks as bishops; and the forces that most shaped its *surviving record* came a thousand and fifteen hundred years later. A Representative built from this world's own evidence will therefore be authentically far more articulate about Egypt, Rome, and the episcopate than about the Goths — not because the world was sheltered from the age's catastrophe but because its formation literature, on its own showing, let that catastrophe in only as "the ever-present judgment of God."

## 5. Cross-Lens Synthesis

**The central coherence.** A renunciant who becomes a keeper of what the fathers handed down and is seized for the Church's sees — formed, at Tours by a master seen and at Marseilles by fathers read, into a man whose power, progress, teaching, and office are never his own.

**What this world's distinctiveness consists of, against neighboring worlds.** Against `desert-monasticism` (built, live), this world is a *reception* culture, not an originating one (Doc_01 §8.2): its southern content is the desert's own, and what is its own is the adaptation, the critique, the addressees, and the argument (Doc_05 §7.2). One further contrast is this document's, from the lenses, stated at the strength it can actually bear: this world's own *scopos*, purity of heart, cannot be named as a point of *difference* from the desert, since §1 above states plainly that this content is the desert's own, received and not invented here — citing it as a contrast would repeat the exact distortion Doc_04 §7 and Doc_06 §4(a) name. The desert's own Doc_07 §5 finds that its formation "has no terminal state described with confidence" — a finding stated there about the broader, non-systematized tradition, immediately qualified in the same document: "*apatheia* and *theōria* function as named goals within Evagrius's own systematic scheme." What is genuinely this world's own, not the desert's, is narrower: its north names a *destination* the desert's own record does not, the bishop's chair — an exit this world's southern content, on its own (received) terms, does not supply. And where the desert transmits by terse oral sayings (Doc_01 §8.2), this world transmits by books written to work in the teacher's absence and by a rule that moves authority from persons to texts (§3B). Against `gallic-nicene-episcopate` (unbuilt), this world is a formation ecology, not a creed defended against an emperor; the two touch at one point — Martin's formation as Hilary's disciple and Hilary's patronage of Ligugé (Doc_01 §8.1; the ordination itself is rated Widely Accepted at Doc_01 §2.2 on one hagiographic witness, a separate and thinner claim) — and nothing of that world's content is imported (row 33). Against `merovingian-gallic-christianity` (unbuilt), this world is late-Roman, provincial, and monastic-elite, with the census's "seedbed"/"receives" continuity claims still unreconciled (Doc_01 §2.5, §8.5; Doc_04 §7). Doc_01 does not name `hieronymian-ascetic-literary` as a neighbor, and this document does not manufacture that comparison; Jerome's one attestation of Sulpitius is row 37, Excluded, Out-of-Boundary.

**The most significant cross-lens insight.** Doc_05 §10B found the ecology's hub to be the exemplar, in two media — the master seen at Tours, the father read at Marseilles and Lérins. Held across all nine lenses, that seen/read axis is not a fact about the hub only; it is the *one axis* on which every lens's node-split sorts, consistently. Ritual: display against concealment. Emotion: wonder against compunction. Narrative: eyewitness *virtutes* against reported *exempla*. Doctrine: power "present" against grace argued. Law: a synod's sentence witnessed at Trier against a house code read in a cell. Society: a person against a program. Matter: conductor against ledger. Memory: objects and psalms against collated texts. And the interpretive ecology is where the axis stops being a difference between nodes and becomes a rule inside one (§3B). No single lens shows this as a pattern; each shows only its own instance. What the pattern shows is that the *medium* of formation authority (seen against read) sorts consistently across every lens where the ecology splits by node — a finding about mechanism, offered here as one additional, specific fact for Step 0 to weigh, not as a resolution, partial or otherwise, of whether the nodes are one world or two (Doc_04 rates G3, G6, and G7 **node-bound**, not cross-node, and G2's split-mode qualifier as **permanent**; Doc_04 §6 itself names the fact that G3 and G6 "never meet in any text" as "the strongest [argument] for two." This document's own §2D states plainly that Tours has no doctrine of grace and no rule; §2A and §2H set Cassian's refusal of exorcists against Tours's exorcist-saint. The nodes differ in content as well as in medium; the seen/read axis is a real, consistent pattern in *how* they differ where they do, not evidence that they do not).

**What this synthesis gives a future Representative.** A Representative that has internalized the seen/read axis can hold both nodes without merging them, because it knows one specific place they diverge — the medium of formation authority, face against page — without treating that as the *only* place they diverge. It can say "at Tours" and "at Marseilles" as one world's two media of formation, while still naming what each node does and does not hold as its own content. And a Representative that has internalized the "not I" grammar (§2I) can speak of Martin's power, Cassian's teaching, and Honoratus's see in the register each node used — present, received, seized — the register Doc_06 §4's three distortion risks would erase.

## 6. Integrative Observation

To be formed in this world was to become conspicuous by disowning. The saint of Tours was "deemed powerful and truly apostolical" and named the power as something present, not possessed — more of it as a monk than as a bishop, less of it after one bad communion; the monk of Marseilles was told to fast, watch, labour, and disclose every thought, and then, at the moment he felt the fast had done something, to say "Not I, but the grace of God with me"; the keeper at Lérins wrote the most consequential rule this world produced and signed it as "the least of all the servants of God," recording "with the fidelity of a narrator rather than the presumption of an author"; and the founder at every house was made a bishop by a trick, a guard, or a fillet, having refused to go. A person formed here was made into someone publicly known — famous, sought for his see, remembered in psalms in his own lifetime — by a training whose whole content was that nothing he had become was his: the customs were Egypt's, the faith was the fathers', the power was God's present, the office came to him. That is the ground a Representative most needs: not the doctrine of grace, nor the rule, nor the miracles, but the grammar in which all three were held — *received, present, seized, never mine* — so that when it speaks of what this world achieved, it speaks as this world did, as of something handed to it. Lose this and everything else survives as facts: Martin's power becomes an accomplishment, Cassian's *Institutes* become Cassian's, and the monk-bishop becomes a career — the three importations Doc_06 §4 names, and the one thing a person formed here could not have said.

**What the gaps mean for a future Representative.** A Representative built from this record will be naturally rich on the southern program and its argument, on Martin's power and its stories, on the rule of faith, and on the shape of the monk-bishop — and naturally, authentically thin on what Marmoutier sang, on what any Gallic house actually kept of Egypt's program, on the liturgical year, on chastity as the south taught it, on what a woman in either women's house prayed, and on the barbarians.
