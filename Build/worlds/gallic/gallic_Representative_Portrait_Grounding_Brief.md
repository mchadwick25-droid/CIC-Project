# Representative Portrait — Grounding Brief
## Renatus, GALLIC MONASTIC-ASCETIC CHRISTIANITY (the monasteries of Gaul, c. 360-450)

**Status:** DRAFT — a proposal for the project lead's own decision, in the same format as the other seven icons' own header comments and Cappadocian's own §35 brief (`CAPPADOCIAN_BUILD_LEDGER.md`), read directly before writing this rather than invented fresh. The object, dress, and appearance calls below are argued from this world's own record; the actual artwork, and the final lock, are the project lead's own act, matching every sibling world's precedent exactly. Eighth live world, eighth icon.

---

> CiC world icon - Renatus, a bishop raised from the monastery, GALLIC MONASTIC-ASCETIC CHRISTIANITY (the monasteries of Gaul, c.360-450). Eighth live world, eighth icon.
>
> ROLE (DOCUMENTED): a bishop who came up from the monastery - the office this world's own Gravity Discovery names its only Primary, cross-node-confirmed gravity (G1, monk-bishop): Martin at Tours, Honoratus at Lérins then Arles, the Egyptian counter-case of Archebius that this world received as its own measure. The role-label was deliberately left unlocated to any single see (`gallic_Representative_Identity_Preliminary_Decision.md` §3: "unlocated to a see... so that both Tours and Lérins-Marseilles remain equally available to the voice rather than the Representative reading as Martin specifically") - the single hardest constraint this brief has to hold visually, since "bishop raised from the monastery" is Martin's own documented shape most exactly. Distinguish deliberately from a portrait of Martin himself: no scene, no narrative attribute tied to one man's own story (no halved cloak, no raised catechumen, no serpent-island crossing) - a plain bust portrait of an office, not an illustration of an episode.
>
> OBJECT (DOCUMENTED): a psalter, held closed or half-open at the chest, same placement convention as the family's other objects (Chilo's loaf, Papnoute's jug, Chloe's cup, Theon's scroll). Grounded at both nodes, not one: at Tours, the psalter is the object of this world's own founding moment - at Martin's contested election, "the reader whose turn it was" failed to appear, and "one standing by seized the psalter" and read aloud the verse that fell open, taken as the moment's own sign (`gallic.story.election-at-tours`); at Marseilles and the island, twelve psalms sung at each of two daily offices, a number "received from an angel the fathers of Egypt heard" and kept as the fathers' own, not this world's invention (`gallic.story.the-angel-and-the-twelve-psalms`). A held book that is neither Theon's teaching-scroll nor an invented prop - the one object this world's own record hands both households at once.
>
> DRESS: the record is not silent here, but genuinely double, and the brief follows both sides rather than picking one. Cassian's own Institutes describe the monastic dress received from Egypt and refitted for Gaul's cold: a girdle ("the mortification of those members"), a small hood ("the simplicity and innocence of little children"), a tunic, no ornament, "novelty of colour or fashion" explicitly refused, adjusted from Egypt's lighter cloth to what "the severity of the winter" required (`gallic.term.the-monks-dress`, Inst. I.1-11). At Tours, Sulpitius describes Martin's own garment as camel's hair, worn as-is - "any dress approaching to softness was there deemed criminal." **"Undyed" is this brief's own inference, not a source word** - the source states plain, unshowy color refused as a matter of discipline, and Martin's own attested garment is a naturally-toned, unprocessed material; nothing in this world's own record uses the word "undyed" itself, and this brief should not have implied otherwise. This world's own record also states plainly, of the office itself, that a bishop raised from among the brethren "kept a monk's habits inside the bishop's office" and "the objects and virtues of a monk in a bishop's chair" - INFERENCE, but a documented theme, not a costuming guess: no mitre (a later medieval development, anachronistic to this world's own 360-450 window regardless of office), no ornate vestment, no pallium, no episcopal insignia of any kind. Plain wool in a flat, unsaturated, homespun tan or fawn (camel-hair's own natural tone, not a richer dyed-looking brown); the hood down or absent; a leather girdle; nothing marking the man as bishop except bearing and the psalter in his hands - the humility this world's own record insists on, kept as a visual rule rather than softened for legibility.
>
> APPEARANCE: SILENT (age, hair, complexion are absent from this world's own record for any composite figure, the same class of silence as `syriac.svg`'s Mar Yausep and `cappadocian.svg`'s Chilo). Kept emblematic, INFERENCE flagged: an older man, weathered rather than youthful (an office reached after years in the monastery, per the role itself), grounded in this world's own Gallic setting - the Loire valley and the Mediterranean coast of late-Roman Gaul - rather than Cappadocia's high plateau, the Levant, or Egypt's desert. A genuinely open question, flagged rather than resolved here, the same way Chilo's own coloring was left open against Cappadocia's climate: this world spans two real, different climates within itself (the Loire valley's own cold the sources dwell on directly, and the Mediterranean coast at Marseilles and the island) - a single composite figure cannot be textured to both literally, and the actual tone is the project lead's own call, the same way Mar Yausep's and Chilo's own were.
>
> TEMPLATE NOTE (flagged, not resolved): the retired `world-icons/*.svg` set's own "Era 1/Era 2 template" labeling already carries a naming mismatch pre-dating this brief (Cappadocian's own §35 found and named it, not fixed it). Gallic is census era 2 (matching desert, hal, ijc, cappadocian). By role-type the closest template match among the current seven live portraits is uncertain by object-family alone (no other Representative holds a book at the chest the way this brief proposes for the psalter) - worth checking directly against the seven actual portrait files, not the label text, when the art is made.
>
> Avoided: any of this world's own named figures' likeness or attributes (Martin's cloak-and-beggar scene, Cassian's Egyptian travels, Vincent's island seclusion, Honoratus's serpent-crossing) - a portrait of the office, never an illustration of any one man's own documented episode; any later medieval episcopal regalia (mitre, crozier-as-jeweled-staff, pallium); any Eastern/Coptic monastic register (Papnoute's own family, already claimed); any specific see or place-marker that would read as "Tours" or "Lérins" specifically rather than the whole composite span.

contrast alone. Not locked - the project lead's own call, the same way every
other world's final hex value was his.

## What is not decided here

The actual artwork (painterly/fine-art oil style, per the fleet's own
style ruling - visible canvas texture, explicitly not
photorealistic; bust-up composition; plain flat background; warm,
in-dialogue expression) is the project lead's own task, exactly as it was
for all seven prior portraits. This brief only proposes what a generator or
an artist would need grounded before drawing: role, object, dress,
appearance, and the one hard constraint (no likeness of Martin specifically)
this world's own identity decision already fixed. Once a first draft exists,
it should be checked against this brief - not assumed correct from the
brief alone. The color guidance above calls for a flat, homespun, unshowy
tone - not a richer, dyed-looking brown - matching the record's own plain,
unshowy material rather than a costuming guess.
