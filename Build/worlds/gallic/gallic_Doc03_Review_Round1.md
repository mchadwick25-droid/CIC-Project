## SUBSTANTIAL FINDINGS

### S1. `[AS]` Signature Vocabulary is applied against the Framework's own definition to roughly thirteen entries of inherited Egyptian desert-monastic vocabulary — and the document's own §9.1 concedes the point without revising the tag

**What's wrong.** Lexicon Framework V2.1 Part II defines `[AS]` as "Vocabulary **unique or substantially distinctive to this formation world, unlikely to carry equivalent weight in neighboring worlds**." Doc_01 §8.2 records `desert-monasticism` as a **built and live** neighbouring world, and says of exactly this material: "Cassian's own *Institutes* and *Conferences* are simultaneously desert-monasticism's own primary-source Latin transmission and this world's founding literature — **the same texts doing genuinely different work for each world**." Vocabulary that Cassian himself frames as received Egyptian teaching does not satisfy the `[AS]` test against that specific neighbour; it is the paradigm case of `[SC]` — shared vocabulary that this world uses with its own assumptions.

**Locus in Doc_03.** Origin tag `[AS]` at: **1.4** Sarabaite, **3.5** conference, **3.6** Institutes/customs/rule, **3.7** discretion, **3.8** disclosure of thoughts, **4.1** purity of heart, **4.2** goal and end, **4.4** thoughts and their three origins, **4.5** the eight principal faults, **4.6** accidie, **4.9** fear→hope→love, **4.10** contemplation/practical–theoretical, **8.6** the monk's dress (Appendix rows confirm all thirteen).

**Locus in the primary source that decides it.** The texts themselves label this material Egyptian in Cassian's own voice:
- *Conf.* XVIII.7 (`iv.vi.ii.vii`): "rightly named **in the Egyptian language** Sarabaites" — 1.4.
- *Inst.* Preface (`iv.ii`): "the customs of the monasteries **which we have seen observed throughout Egypt and Palestine**"; "the origin and causes of the principal faults, **of which they reckon eight**, and the remedies for them **according to their traditions**" — 3.6, 4.5, 4.6, 8.6.
- *Conf.* II.2–4 (`iv.iv.iii.ii`, `iv.iv.iii.iv`): discretion is the verdict of "the blessed Antony and the other fathers" in an Egyptian council — 3.7.
- *Conf.* Pref. I (`iv.iv.i`): "the Institutes **which are not mine but the fathers'**" — the framing Doc_03 itself quotes at 3.3.

**Doc_03 already knows this.** §9.1 says of exactly this block: "Doc_04 must ask, for each, whether it is a gravity of *this world* or a gravity of desert monasticism (Registry-excluded territory, Doc_01 §8.2) that this world *received* — the Institutes Preface's own framing … **suggests the latter** is how Cassian himself would put it." Having reached that conclusion, the document left the `[AS]` tag in place on all thirteen. Part II's closing rule — "Tags should be assigned based on the term's actual demonstrated function during candidate discovery, not assigned by default" — is the rule being breached: the demonstrated function is *reception*, and the tag says *signature*.

**Why it matters downstream.** The `[AS]`/`[SC]` distinction is not decorative. It is what tells Doc_06 and the deployment lexicon which words this world may be presented as owning. Carried forward unrevised, a Representative for Gallic monasticism would present *purity of heart*, *discretion*, *accidie* and the eight-fault scheme as its own signature vocabulary against a live sibling world whose founding literature is the identical text.

**Suggested fix (not binding).** Re-tag the Egyptian-derived block `[SC]`, and either (a) add a one-line "received from" note per entry, or (b) if the build wants to keep a visible marker for "distinctive within this world's own literature even though inherited", define that as a separate local flag in §0 rather than borrowing `[AS]`, whose definition is explicitly comparative across worlds. Terms that survive the `[AS]` test unchanged in my reading: **7.2** the rule of universality/antiquity/consent, **7.4** the deposit, **7.5** progress vs. alteration, **7.13** Commonitory/Peregrinus (all Vincent's own), **5.3** the beginning of a good will (the world's own formulation of its own argument), **5.6** co-operation, **8.8** Gaul, and **3.11** lukewarmness (arguable either way).

### S2. §9.1's "genuinely shared across all three voices" list contradicts its own entries on three terms — two of which §9.2 flags first for Tier 1

**What's wrong.** §9.1's final paragraph lists twelve terms as "**Genuinely shared across all three voices, both nodes (the safest vocabulary for a Representative)**". Three of the twelve are not attested in all three voices on the document's own evidence.

**Locus in Doc_03, per term:**

- **1.1 monk / solitary.** §9.1 lists it. Its own entry's Voices line reads "**Sulpitius, Cassian**; Gennadius corroborates." Appendix row 1.1 reads "**S C**". Vincent is absent, and Gennadius is a later corroborating witness, not one of the three voices. *Source check:* `iii.*` (the whole *Commonitory*) contains no monastic use of "monk"; Gennadius's "monk and bishop" is at `v.iv.xx` and is about Martin, not a Vincentine usage.
- **2.1 soldier of Christ.** §9.1 lists it, and §9.2 makes it Tier-1 flag **#1** on the ground that it is "cross-voice, cross-node". Its own entry reads "**Sulpitius, Cassian; Vincent supplies the antonym**." Appendix row 2.1 reads "**S C (V antonym)**". *Source check:* Vincent's contribution is "the manifold and deplorable tempests of secular warfare" at `iii.ii` = *Comm.* ch. I [2] — verified, and it is the contrast term, not the term. Two-voice attestation is still strong, and 2.1 may well deserve its Tier-1 flag; but the *stated* ground for the flag overstates the evidence.
- **1.6 brethren.** §9.1 lists it. Its entry claims "All three (Vincent: 'the holy brethren,' *Comm.* ch. 11 [29])." *Source check:* the locus is right — `iii.xii` = ch. XI [29] — but the sentence reads "who, **greatly beloved by the holy brethren**, and in high favor with the populace, expounded the Scriptures in public daily", and its subject is **Nestorius's popularity among the general body of the faithful**. That is not the referent 1.6 defines ("The monks of a house or circle as a body"). I enumerated every occurrence of "brethren"/"brother" in the *Commonitory*: the other four are Noah's brothers (ch. V), two verbatim Pauline quotations (Rom. 16:17 at ch. XXV; 1 Cor. 1:10 at ch. XXVIII), and "the brother of Basil" (ch. XXVIII). **Vincent has no monastic "brethren" in this text.**

**Why it matters.** §0's Discipline 3 calls the per-term voice attestation "the single most important classification in the document for Doc_04's purposes, and the one most likely to be wrong in detail". The §9.1 caption — "the safest vocabulary for a Representative" — is precisely the place where an over-claim converts into runtime behaviour.

**Suggested fix.** Move 1.1 and 2.1 to a "shared across both nodes, two voices" line; either drop 1.6 from the list or restate its Vincent evidence honestly as "the same English word, a different referent" — which is exactly what 1.10 conversion already does well and could be copied.
