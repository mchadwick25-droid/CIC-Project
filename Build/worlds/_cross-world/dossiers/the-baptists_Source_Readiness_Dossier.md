## 5. Open cross-world questions

- How much contact did Smyth's congregation have with the Waterlander Mennonites? Smyth's later confessions and Retractations are assigned to the-anabaptist-movements as provisional. The census calls this question contested.
- Liele and David George: the Black Baptist thread "carries forward into the next era, where it has its own named parent entry". The spec adds a provisional antecedent row for the-black-church-in-america.
- The General Baptist assembly's Socinian and Unitarian disputes (vol. II) run parallel to the Presbyterian drift after Salters' Hall. They are assigned provisionally to english-old-dissent as context.
- Bunyan's Relation of the Imprisonment covers the persecution Baptists shared with the ejected ministers. It is assigned provisionally to english-old-dissent.
- Roger Williams and John Clarke (Rhode Island) could serve this world or New England. Neither is vendored yet.
- Backus's Appeal: a provisional context row for the-evangelical-awakening-in-new-england, as a witness to the standing order.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The census's Tier 1 rating holds up in the open corpus. The world has the founder's own Amsterdam writings, two church books from the persecution years and the General Baptist assembly's minutes for a century and a half. It also has Bunyan, the 1792 hinge in both Carey's and Fuller's own words, and the confessions already vendored. The women's and Black voices the census names are present in their own first person: Agnes Beaumont (1674, printed 1760), Anne Dutton (1740), and George Liele and David George in Rippon's Register (1791-1793). The census reports no floor question, and everything is in English. The world stands apart from the Anabaptist movements and from Old Dissent through its own congregational records and the missionary turn. The open Waterlander question is a feature of the sources, not a gap. If more allowance is free later, Backus's History and Leland's Writings would deepen the American half, which is the thinnest part now.
