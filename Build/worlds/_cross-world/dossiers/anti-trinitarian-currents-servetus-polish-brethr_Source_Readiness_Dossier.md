What the files give, stated plainly. The movement speaks for itself in three genres: its confession (the Catechism, in the 1680 English and the 1609 Latin), its theologian (Socinus, in his own Latin, with one treatise and his Life in English), and its own history (Lubieniecki). Servetus speaks in both his books, the 1531 one only through a garbled scan. The Geneva process is in the French and Latin documents of the Corpus Reformatorum volume, with an English narrative beside it. Three things are not in the corpus at all: Ferenc Dávid in his own words, the 1568 Edict of Torda, and any Transylvanian source. The Transylvanian third of the entry has no voice in the library yet (§4).

Two cautions a build needs. Rees's English translates the 1680 recension, by his own footnote, so a quote from the English cannot be checked against the 1609 Latin without comparing the passage (`works_fragment.yaml` records the pair as two Expressions of one Work). The census says the same of Rees; the footnote confirms it.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Floor and register.** Same as §6: the library vendors this entry's own voice; a built world could treat it only from outside. Whether it is ever built is Mark's decision.
- **Anabaptists (VI.3).** The brief said to map to VI.3 only where a text names the Anabaptists. None of the new files was read for that; the Restitutio reprint's bookseller's notes call Servetus "Prince of the Anabaptists", which is a later English hand and not Servetus's. The only vendored texts that name both movements are the Beck and Vedder hits in §2. The VI.3 Step 0 already separates the two. Name left open.
- **Reformed cities (VI.2).** The Calvin volume and Rilliet are double-placed. VI.2 owns the Geneva view of the process (Calvin's own account and the council's records); VI.14 owns the hostile-record use. The Calvin volume also holds work that belongs wholly to VI.2 (De scandalis, the Consensus on predestination, the Quatre sermons).
- **Remonstrants and Dort (the-remonstrants-and-dort).** Socinianism is the charge laid against the Remonstrants by their Reformed opponents (Voetius, line 2101 "Socinus en de Remonstranten"). Whether the Rees Catechism or Socinus is a relevant context file for that world was not checked. Named, not decided.
- **English Unitarianism.** Biddle, Nye (1687) and Toulmin are English Unitarians after 1640. They sit in the window's last years or outside it and are not Polish or Transylvanian. Whether they belong to this entry or to a later English-dissent world is a mapping question the census does not answer.
- **Rees's recension.** The 1609 Latin and Rees's English are different recensions of one catechism. Doc_02 should cite both and say which text a quote comes from (`works_fragment.yaml`).
- **Author slugs.** `racovian-catechism`, `socinus`, `servetus`, `lubieniecki`, `przypkowski` and `rilliet` are new slugs equal to the file-name prefix; none needed an `authors_ruled` block. `przypkowski` is the slug for the 1653 Life, whose English title page names only "a Polonian Knight"; the attribution to Samuel Przypkowski rests on the host record of the 1636 Latin printing and is flagged in the header. If the lead prefers, the slug could be `socinus` with a note.
- **OCR apparatus.** The Murr Restitutio, the two Socinus volumes and Lubieniecki read the long s as `f` (the vendored files contain no ſ); the Calvin volume reads `v` as `y`. These are systematic substitutions of the kind `REGISTRY.yaml`'s `apparatus` mappings record. No mapping was written; each needs verifying against page images first.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor question (from the census floorNote).** "They rejected the Trinity and said so plainly in their own confessions of faith : a clear divergence from the Nicene Creed, declared in their own words rather than alleged by opponents. Noting that divergence is not a judgement on what was done to them: Servetus was burned at Geneva in 1553, and the Polish Brethren were expelled from Poland in 1658." The vendored Catechism confirms the first sentence in the movement's own text. The library vendors this entry's voice so the atlas can show it. A built world could treat it only from outside, with `register: etic`: the Representative would speak about a community whose confession rejects the Nicene Trinity, not as a community the project's own creed floor accepts. This section says so plainly; it does not decide the floor.

**Uniqueness and overlap with siblings.** The Reformed cities (VI.2) hold the persecutor and the shared Geneva record, so the process of 1553 is told from the bench there and from the dock here; the overlap is in the sources (the Corpus Reformatorum volume), not in the formation story. The Anabaptists (VI.3) are the nearest radical sibling; their Step 0 already keeps this current separate. Lutheran Wittenberg and the Tridentine church touch it only at the edges. Nothing else on the atlas tells the story of Raków or of the Transylvanian edict, but the corpus holds no Transylvanian voice, so the Transylvanian third of the entry is told by this library only through Rees's introduction and Socinus's letter to Dávid.

**Scale.** About 3.06 million words in eleven files, of which about two million are the two Latin Socinus volumes. The English part is small (the Rees Catechism at 161,000 words, the 1731 Argument, the 1653 Life, and Rilliet). The movement's confession and its theologian are in the corpus, which the census promised; the Servetus 1531 and the Lubieniecki scans are second witnesses until cleaner scans turn up.

**Recommendation, for Mark to decide, not a decision: atlas only.** The floor test (A1) is the barrier: the movement rejects the Nicene Trinity in its own confession, and the project's floor treats that as outside the build line, so the honest register for any build is etic and the entry stays a floor question until Mark rules. Sourcing (B1) is strong for the Polish Brethren and for Servetus and the Geneva process (the Catechism, Socinus, Lubieniecki, the Restitutio, Calvin's Defensio and the trial documents are all in), and absent for Transylvania (no Dávid, no 1568 edict). The ecology (B2) is lopsided: one confession, one theologian in volume, a hostile record in French and Latin, and almost nothing in English beyond Rees. Uniqueness (B3) holds; audiences (B4) and scale (B5) were not argued here. If Mark prefers to keep the question open until the Transylvanian sources are found, **open question** is the only other reading these sources support. Nothing here argues for a build.
