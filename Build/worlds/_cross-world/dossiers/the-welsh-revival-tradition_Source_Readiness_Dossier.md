## 5. Open cross-world questions

- Does the Moravian mission in Wales belong inside this world? The Welsh Zinzendorf (1760) is spec'd as Moravian tradition (assigned) and Welsh context (needs-ruling). Named, not decided.
- Should Whitefield's Welsh material sit in the Methodist world, here, or both? It touches the joint English and Welsh Calvinistic Methodist association of the 1740s.
- Griffith Jones's circulating schools could be an antecedent of this world or part of the Georgian Church of England. He stayed an Anglican rector and was Rowland's mentor.
- Edmund Jones and the Welsh Independents could belong here or with English Old Dissent, which covers "England, Wales". The spec puts Aberystruth in both, provisionally.
- The 1823 Confession and the 1834 English edition fall after the window's 1815 end. The spec marks them transmission.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The world's own voice is deep. It has Harris in his own words, Rowland's preaching, and the whole of Pantycelyn in Welsh: hymns, the long poems, and Drws y Society Profiad, the movement's own manual for the seiat. It also has Thomas Charles's diary and letters and the connexion's own rules of discipline. The census reports no floor question, and the corpus confirms that. The language limit is real but manageable. The core texts are Welsh originals, and public-domain English exists only for Harris, Rowland, the 1834 constitution and the hymns. No public-domain English of Theomemphus or Drws y Society Profiad is known. The gap that matters most is Ann Griffiths. Her voice reaches the corpus only in Pughe's free English verse of 1900, three hands removed from her, so every use needs that transmission named. Against its sibling worlds, the Welsh seiat, the hymn corpus and the Welsh language set this world apart from the Methodist Revival, while the Whitefield and Harris correspondence links the two.
