What kind of witness each is:

- **Eckhart.** Three texts, in three languages. Pfeiffer's edition (1857) is Eckhart's German sermons and tractates in Middle High German; the OCR carries letter errors, so a build checks the page images before quoting. Evans (1924) is a public-domain English of the same German text, made "with some omissions and additions". Daniels (1923) prints the Latin defence Eckhart wrote in his own case. Jostes (1895) adds unprinted texts of Eckhart and his circle; it is a second witness, because the text pages are partly garbled. The bull In agro dominico and the trial acts are not on the shelf.
- **Tauler.** English only. Winkworth's translation (reprinted 1907) gives a life and twenty-five sermons. No Middle High German Tauler is vendored.
- **Suso.** The critical German edition (Bihlmeyer, 1907) holds the Life of the Servant, the two Little Books and the Letters. Knox (1865) gives the Life in English. A 1910 English Little Book of Eternal Wisdom comes with Hilton's Parable of the Pilgrim; its translator is not named in the front matter.
- **Women's memory.** The Töss sister-book (Vetter, 1906, with the preface and the life of Elisabeth of Hungary) reads cleanly. The Engelthal sister-book (Schröder, 1871) and Adelheid Langmann's revelations (Strauch, 1878) are second witnesses with frequent letter errors. No English exists for any of them in the public domain.
- **Friends of God and the Theologia.** Merswin's Book of the Nine Rocks (Schmidt, 1859) is a second witness: long s is read as f. The Theologia deutsch is vendored in Pfeiffer's Middle High German (1851) and in Winkworth's English (1893), the translation being made from Pfeiffer's text.
- **Bridges.** Ruysbroeck's three works in English (1916) are the Brabant mysticism that links this world to the Devotio Moderna. Langenberg's 1902 study prints Low German texts and ends with a part on Low German mysticism and Eckhart. Mechthild of Magdeburg (Morel, 1869) is thirteenth-century and enters as the antecedent.

## 2. Cross-link opportunities

The shelf was searched for Eckhart, Tauler, Suso, Seuse, the Friends of God, Theologia Deutsch, Ruysbroeck and their variant spellings, in headers and bodies, and the hits were opened. Proposed rows are in `crosslinks.yaml`. All are `provisional`.

- **Luther, Works vol. 3 (`luther_works-v3-selected_various1930.txt`)** (bucket: Lutheran Wittenberg). The Leipzig Disputation of 1519 says Tauler "has much to say" about purgatory, and a note points to scholarship on Luther's relation to Tauler. Tier: out-of-window.
- **Calvin, Letters vol. 4 (`calvin_letters-vol4_bonnet1858.txt`)** (bucket: the Reformed Cities). An editor's note calls the Theologia Germanica "published by Luther, translated by Castalio" and says it might offend Calvin's orthodoxy. Tier: out-of-window.
- **Sudermann, Hohe geistreiche Lehren (`sudermann_hohe-geistreiche-lehren-hohes-lied-deu_heyden1622.txt`)** (bucket: the Schwenckfelders). The margins cite "M. Eckhart" and "Tauler". Tier: out-of-window.
- **Schwenckfeld corpus vol. 1 (`schwenckfeld_corpus-schwenckfeldianorum-v1-earliest-letters-deu-eng_hartranft1907.txt`)** (same bucket). Tauler is quoted and praised. Tier: out-of-window.
- **Wesley, Journal vol. 1 (`wesley-j_journal-v1_curnock1909.txt`)** (bucket: the Methodist Revival). Wesley reads Tauler's Life in Georgia. Tier: out-of-window.
- **Van Braght, Martyrs Mirror (`van-braght_martyrs-mirror_sohm1886.txt`)** (bucket: the Anabaptist Movements). Under the year 1400 the compiler quotes Tauler against the prelates. Tier: out-of-window.
- **Groote's letters, Kempis's Founders, Kempis's Chronicle, Busch's Chronicon (all in the Devotio Moderna bucket).** Each names Ruysbroeck; the Chronicon also names Suso. Groote went to Brabant to see Ruysbroeck, per Kempis. Tier: same time-different region. Proposed as `context`.
- **The Craft of Dying collection (`craft-of-dying_book-of-the-craft-of-dying-and-other-early-english-tracts_comper1917.txt`)** (bucket: the English Mystics). It prints a chapter by Henry Suso from the Horologium sapientiae in Middle English. Tier: same time-different region.
- Noted, no row proposed: Basil's Rules (the phrase "friends of God" is the Scriptural one), Herberstein's notes on Russia (a different Ruysbroeck, the friar William of Rubruck), and the Free Spirit paper by Haupt (no Eckhart hit on the shelf).

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census says Eckhart was condemned over how he said things, not over what the Creed says. Nothing in the vendored files was read against that claim. **Sourcing (B1).** The census says "Rich". The German side is. The thin parts are Tauler (English selection only), the Friends of God (one second-witness text), the Engelthal book and Langmann (second witnesses, German only) and the condemnation (not on the shelf).

**Ecology (B2).** Preachers and nuns sit side by side: Eckhart, Tauler and Suso preach, while Stagel, the Engelthal sisters and Langmann write. The ecology tests who is heard through whom. Suso's Life reaches us through a woman's editorial hand; the sermons survive as listeners' copies. The Friends of God speak through one banker's book, which Schmidt's preface says was written under the influence of the Friend of God from the Oberland.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Against the Devotio Moderna the difference is geography and tradition: Dominican preaching in German against Brabant and Zwolle. Against the Beguines (earlier) and the Free Spirit (condemned) the line runs through Eckhart's propositions. Against the English Mystics the shared ground is Suso and Hilton in one volume.

**Audiences (B4).** Eckhart has a large modern readership outside the church. The Theologia and Tauler reach Lutheran, Reformed, Anabaptist and Methodist readers on the shelf. The audience is large and reachable.

**Scale (B5).** About 13.9 MB in sixteen files, 13.8 million characters, and eleven cross-link rows for this world (the lead's `crosslinks.yaml` holds eighteen in all, across three worlds). No file is over the 10 MB line; the largest is Suso at 2.4 MB.
