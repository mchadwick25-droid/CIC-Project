What the census names and the table covers: Zinzendorf's corpus (eight files: five English printings of his discourses and catechism, Gambold's digest, and two German originals); the Losungen (one in-window year, German); the hymn corpus (the 1754 and 1789 English hymnals); the church's official self-definition (Acta 1749, Spangenberg's Idea Fidei Fratrum 1784); its official histories (Cranz 1780, Spangenberg's Life); the mission record (Oldendorp on St Thomas, Cranz on Greenland, Loskiel and Heckewelder on the Native congregations, La Trobe's survey, the Periodical Accounts); one printed Lebenslauf (Cennick); and the English controversy from both sides (Rimius, Wesley, the Brethren's own Acta and Discourses).

What the census names and the table does not cover: the Gemein-Diarium, the choir records and the manuscript Lebensläufe, including the women's and formerly enslaved members' memoirs. These are archival (Unity Archives, Herrnhut; Moravian Archives, Bethlehem) and the modern editions are in copyright. See §4.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Czech succession claim.** The Acta of 1749 and the first part of Cranz's history are the renewed church's documentary case for continuity with the old Unity. They are placed as `transmission`, provisional, in `czech-churches-last-century` (VI.24) and `the-hussite-and-bohemian-brethren-movement` (V.6). Whether the Czech worlds want the eighteenth-century claimant's narrative in their buckets at all is for those worlds' builders and the project lead.
- **Wesley's Queries (1755)** is assigned `tradition` for the Methodist Revival and `context` here. The Methodist dossier should carry the same row from its side.
- **The Welsh Sixteen Discourses (1760)** is a lead for VII.10 as much as for this world; recorded here so it is not re-found.
- **The Native American missions and VII.6.** Loskiel's Shekomeko chapters are placed provisional `context` in the New England Awakening world; the 1744–45 New York expulsion of the Moravian missionaries belongs to both stories.
- **Missions with no census home.** The Danish West Indies (Oldendorp), Greenland (Cranz) and Labrador (Periodical Accounts) missions are Moravian texts, but the enslaved and Inuit congregations they describe have no census entry of their own. The texts live here; the question whether a mission-born Christianity of the eighteenth century deserves an entry is portfolio-level.
- **Acrelius** is now linked from three Era VIII worlds (Brethren, Moravians, and by its subject the Swedish Lutherans on the Delaware, who have no census entry); the Brethren dossier already raised the missing world.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The own voice is the deepest in the era and almost all of it is in public-domain English of the period: Zinzendorf preaching, catechising and lecturing in five English printings made in his lifetime by his own followers, with two German originals to check them against; the church defining itself in the Acta of 1749 and in Spangenberg's Idea Fidei Fratrum; its own histories (Cranz, Spangenberg); two hymnals and one year of the Losungen; five mission narratives from the missionaries' side; and a member's printed Lebenslauf (Cennick). The controversy is readable from both sides (Rimius and Roche against; the Acta, the Discourses and the Maxims for; Wesley in between). The floor is clear (Step 0 A1: Trinitarian Lutheran-confessional Pietists under the Augsburg Confession). The census's uniqueness argument (literal renewal of the old Unity across 1627–1722) is documented in the church's own texts.

The one weakness is the one the census itself names as the world's strength: the congregational diaries, the choir records and the thousands of manuscript Lebensläufe, including the women's and formerly enslaved members' memoirs, are archival and their modern editions are in copyright. A Doc_02 must say plainly that the ordinary member's voice in this library is Cennick's printed life, the hymnals and the missionaries' diary extracts, not the Herrnhut memoir archive. The Sifting Time (1743–50) can be read from Rimius's hostile quotations and from the Seven Sermons of 1741, with the church's own correction in the 1789 hymnal's preface and Spangenberg's Exposition.
