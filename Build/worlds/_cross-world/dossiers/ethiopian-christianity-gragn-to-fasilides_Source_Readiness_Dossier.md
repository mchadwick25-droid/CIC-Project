What the files give a build, honestly stated.

- **The Ethiopian inside voice is thin and mostly in Ge'ez that the OCR cannot read.** The usable inside material is the French of the Sarsa Dengel chronicle (a royal chronicle of the reign from 1563), the German of the Galla history, and the Latin of the Confession of Galawdewos. The Ge'ez in each is noise in the OCR. Any quotation needs the page image.
- **Everything else is the view from outside:** Portuguese and Jesuit witnesses (Castanhoso, Bermudez, Paez, Mendes, Tellez, Lobo) and one Muslim enemy witness (Arab-Faqih). The census names the Jesuit century and the Gragn wars as the world's defining events, and these are the people who wrote them down in a language we can read.
- **The two Western-language editions that carry the Ethiopian royal chronicles for Lebna Dengel, Galawdewos, Minas and Susenyos are not on a reachable host** (§4). The window's other two reigns therefore have no royal chronicle in the corpus.
- **English availability.** Castanhoso, Telles and Lobo are in English. Everything else is in French, German, Latin or Portuguese, with no public-domain English found.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Society of Jesus (VI.11).** Paez, Mendes, Tellez and Lobo are placed to VI.11 as provisional rows (the Jesuit writing about his own mission) and to this world as context. The Society of Jesus thread decides their weight there. Borgia, Polanco and Ignatius are in its bucket already; the cross-link rows are mine to propose, theirs to accept.
- **Role of the outside witnesses in this world.** Every Western voice here describes the Ethiopian church from outside; the world's own voice is thin. A build may want to class Paez, Mendes, Tellez and Lobo as `context` (as assigned) and keep Galawdewos, Sarsa Dengel and the Galla monk as the only `tradition`. The role call is the lead's.
- **Galla history authorship.** The editions name no author (§1; the staging file holds the work as anonymous). The usual attribution to Abba Bahrey comes from the brief and the secondary literature. A ruling on the slug is needed before it is merged: `zenahu-la-galla` is used here because that is the work's own title in both editions.
- **Confession of Galawdewos: authorship and date.** The census says 1555. Ludolf's Commentarius itself has a section on whether the Confession is genuine (file line 27531). A build thread must read that section before treating the Confession as the emperor's own words.
- **Neighbouring Ethiopian worlds.** `the-ethiopian-monastic-flowering` (c. 1270–1517) is the antecedent; the royal chronicles of Lebna Dengel (the Gragn war's first half) are not in the corpus. `ethiopian-christianity-tewodros-to-menelik` (1855–1900) is later. Neither has a bucket.
- **Dates in `date_added`.** The registry fragment carries `date_added: '2026-10-16'` as the brief requires; the files were actually fetched on 2026-10-23 and the file headers say so.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor question.** The census floor note says "No question". Nothing in the files checked contradicts that: the Confession of Galawdewos (read only at its opening, which in Ludolf's Latin is "In nomine Patris, & Filii, & Spiritus Sancti, unici DEI") begins from the Trinity, and the Jesuit sources dispute rites and jurisdiction, not the doctrine of God. The Miaphysite Christology of the Ethiopian church (the Tewahedo teaching) is a difference in how Christ's divinity and humanity are described, as the census says of the Armenian church, and a build should state it the same way. The question of whether the Confession was written to satisfy the Jesuits is the one place the floor reading could be argued; Ludolf's discussion at file line 27531 is where to start.

**Uniqueness and overlap.** The Atlas has eight Ethiopian entries across the centuries, so this world's own territory is the sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries only. The overlap that matters is with the Society of Jesus (VI.11), which will cite the same Jesuit witnesses from the other direction, and with the Tridentine Church (VI.22) for the Counter-Reformation context. The census frames this world as the era's inversion: the Counter-Reformation arrives as outside pressure on an ancient church. No sibling carries that story from the Ethiopian side.

**Scale.** About 1.3 million words across nine files. The Ethiopian inside voice is a small part of that: the Sarsa Dengel volume (about 177,000 words, much of it Ge'ez the OCR cannot read), the German of the Galla history (about 10,000 words) and a few pages of the Confession inside Ludolf's 307,000-word commentary. The rest is Western testimony and commentary. A build has far less to quote from the Ethiopian side than from the Western side.

**Recommendation, for Mark to decide, not a decision: open question.** Floor (A1) clears. Sourcing (B1) is the weak test: the Ethiopian inside voice named in the census, the royal chronicles and the Confession, is present only as a Latin translation of the Confession, a French translation of one reign's chronicle (Sarsa Dengel, which follows the Gragn war and Wayna Daga) and a German or French translation of a short monk's report; the chronicles of Lebna Dengel, Galawdewos and Susenyos, three of the reigns the census's story turns on, are not on a reachable host. Ecology (B2) is lopsided toward Portuguese and Jesuit witnesses. Uniqueness (B3) holds, and audiences (B4) and scale (B5) are not in doubt. The question for Mark is whether to build now on a corpus where the Ethiopian church is described mostly by outsiders, with the Confession as the one major inside text, or to wait until the chronicles of Galawdewos and Susenyos can be found (Conzelman 1895 and Pereira 1892–1900 are both pre-1930 and probably public domain, but not on a host this session can reach). If Mark prefers a decision now, the next best option is **build** with Doc_02 stating the thin inside voice as an absence, as the Anabaptist dossier does for Marpeck and Denck; **atlas only** would be the conservative fallback. This is a recommendation for Mark, not a decision.
