## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Whitefield's allocation (VII.5, VII.6, VII.10).** Carried from the Methodist dossier: Whitefield's own texts are vendored under the Methodist Revival and placed provisional `context` here. The census's own open question, whether Britain and New England are one tradition or two, is the same question in another form; the project lead's ruling can be applied by changing roles.
- **The middle and southern colonies.** Tennent, Blair and Davies are placed here, Davies provisional, following the census's own inclusion of the Tennents and Hanover with the question left open. If the portfolio decides they are a separate movement, these rows move.
- **The Native missions and the Moravians (VII.4).** Brainerd's Delaware congregations and the Moravians' Shekomeko and Bethlehem congregations are neighbours in New Jersey and New York; Loskiel is linked here provisional, and Brainerd's Mirabilia may deserve a provisional row in the Moravian bucket. Not decided here.
- **The Black Church's parent traditions.** Wheatley and Occom are here as the census directs; Haynes and Hammon remain unfound. The same Era IX question raised in the Methodist dossier (a Black Church world) would take Wheatley with it.
- **The Separate Baptists (VII.16).** The Separates who became Baptists (Backus) are the Baptist world's; nothing here claims them.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The own voice is exactly what the census says it is, the era's own documentation project, and it is public domain in first printings: Edwards's narrative, defence, self-criticism and analysis of the revival (1737, 1741, 1742, 1746), with the case histories of Abigail Hutchinson, Phebe Bartlet and (unnamed) Sarah Edwards inside them; his Personal Narrative and Resolutions; Brainerd's Journal and Edwards's Life of him; the Christian History's weekly letters from the towns; a laywoman's forty years of diaries and letters; the first Black and Native authors in American print; and the opposition's own dossier. The floor is clear (census: no question). The two weaknesses a Doc_02 must state are the ordinary layman's manuscript voice (Cole, the Separates, the Haynes and Hammon broadsides) that the modern editions own, and the open boundary with Britain and with the southern colonies, which the library has placed provisionally rather than decided.
