What kind of witness each is:

- **Julian.** Three witnesses. Warrack's Long Text is a proofread Gutenberg transcription in modern spelling, so it is the cleanest. Cressy's 1670 text (reprinted 1843) is readable OCR in seventeenth-century English. Harford's 1911 edition gives the Short Text in modern spelling, from a different British Museum manuscript. All three are the editor's spelling, not fourteenth-century wording.
- **The Cloud of Unknowing.** Underhill 1912 is the Cloud alone in modern spelling. McCann 1924 adds the Epistle of Privy Counsel and Augustine Baker's seventeenth-century commentary. Baker is a later reader, not the author.
- **Hilton.** Underhill 1923 is edited from manuscripts. The Dalgairns printing keeps Cressy's seventeenth-century English. The two differ in wording, so a quote must name its edition.
- **Rolle.** Misyn's fifteenth-century Middle English of the Fire of Love and Mending of Life, and Horstman's large Middle English edition, are second witnesses because the Middle English OCR is noisy. Comper's and Hodgson's modern English renderings are readable and give the same works.
- **Margery Kempe.** She is the thin voice. The only Kempe text found that clears rights is the short printed extract inside Gardner's *Cell of Self-Knowledge*. Gardner says the extract is an eight-page quarto printed by Wynkyn de Worde and reprinted by Pepwell. The full Book is closed (section 4).
- **The Carthusian layer.** Nicholas Love's *Mirrour of the Blessed Lyf of Jesu Christ* is by the prior of the Carthusian house of Mount Grace (the title page says so). Its OCR reads the long s as f, so it is a second witness. No Carthusian manuscript text is in the library.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Lollardy and the Arundel record.** Arundel's Constitutions are the counter-pressure for this world and the central persecuting act for Lollardy. Foxe vol. III prints them. Which world quotes them as its own, and which as context? Proposed here: context for this world. Not decided.
- **Rolle's Psalter and the Lollard hand.** Arnold's volume argues that Rolle's English Psalter commentary was retouched in the Canticles by a Lollard writer and once ascribed to Wyclif. A Representative for Rolle must not quote that commentary as plainly his. The confidence tag is Contested. The Lollardy world meets the same text from the other side.
- **Devotio Moderna and the English Imitation.** The 1893 edition of the earliest English translation of the Imitation is staged as tradition / provisional for the Devotio Moderna world and as context for this one and for the parish world. Whether this world claims it at all is a Step 0 call.
- **Recusant English Catholicism.** Cressy's 1670 Julian and the Dalgairns Hilton are staged here as tradition and, for the Recusant world, as transmission (custody, not voice). The census says the books circulated among English exiles. Whether the Recusant world takes that custody claim is for that world's leads.
- **Carthusian Life.** The census names a Carthusian world in the Atlas (`carthusian-life`, from 1084). Love's *Mirrour* and the Carthusian copying belong to its subject. This pass did not open that world's bucket.
- **Rhineland and the continental mystics.** McCann's volume includes a translation of the *Hid Divinity* (Pseudo-Dionysius) and Gardner's holds Richard of St Victor's *Benjamin*, Catherine of Siena extracts and the Epistles of the Cloud group. Comper's *Craft of Dying* includes a chapter by Henry Suso. The Rhineland Mysticism world may want these as antecedent or context. Not opened for that.
- **The Pricke of Conscience.** The 1863 edition ascribes the poem to Rolle on its title page. Whether that ascription holds is a question for the build, not settled here.
