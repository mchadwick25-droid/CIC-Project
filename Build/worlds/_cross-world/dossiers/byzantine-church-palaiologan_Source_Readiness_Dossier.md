## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Palamas in this world or the next.** The census lists Palamas as a voice here and says the hesychast movement and its theology are treated separately. The two Palamas rows are `context` / `needs-ruling`. The lead should say whether the synodal tomes (assigned here as `tradition`) and Palamas's writings sit in both worlds, or only the tomes.
- **Gennadios, before and after 1453.** The Florentine orations (Migne 160) are staged here. The Confessions of faith for Mehmed II in the same volume are staged for the Ottoman world. The Defensio (Latin) is staged here, with an `antecedent` / `needs-ruling` row for the Ottoman world, because the vendored Ottoman files (Kimmel, the Historia politica) do not argue from it. The editors of Migne 159 give the Defensio to Joseph of Methone, not to Scholarios; the attribution of the Latin Defensio is open.
- **Syropoulos: church voice or context.** He was a church officer who signed and then rejected the union. The row is `tradition` / `provisional`. If the lead reads that as a hostile insider, the row becomes `context`.
- **Kantakouzenos and Gregoras across two worlds.** Both write on the church and the hesychast quarrel. Their rows are `context` in both buckets.
- **Bessarion and the Latin side.** Bessarion's Oratio dogmatica and the acts of Ferrara-Florence also serve the Latin papal church (V.14). Both are staged there as `provisional`, unread for that world.
- **Mark of Ephesus is in two printings.** The older Migne volume (tomus 160, vendored this pass) and Petit's edition inside the John of Ephesus volume (Patrologia Orientalis 17). A merge of the proposed cross-link row is enough for the second; the file does not need to be re-vendored. Which one a quote record should cite is for Doc_02.
- **Mansi's label.** Mansi calls the synod of 1347 a 'Pseudo-Synodus'. This is the Latin editor's frame, not the synod's own.
- **Greek-only Florence acts.** The Acta Graeca (Gill, 1953) is the modern edition and is in copyright. The Greek in Mansi 31 is garbled in the OCR. Petit's Patrologia Orientalis volumes give clean French and partly clean Greek for the debates on purgatory only. A Greek witness for the main debates is the gap.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census floor note is "No question (Chalcedonian-Orthodox)". Nothing read in this pass bears on the floor, and no floor analysis was attempted. The Florentine decree and the sessions were located and sampled, not read through.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says "Rich institutionally". That holds. The tomoi of 1341, 1347 and 1351 are now in Greek with Latin (Migne 151, Mansi 26). The Florentine acta, with Laetentur caeli, are in Mansi 31 and in Petit's fascicle on purgatory. Mark of Ephesus's corpus is in Migne 160 and, in Petit's fuller edition, inside Patrologia Orientalis 17. Bessarion is in Migne 161 with his refutation of Mark. Gennadios's pre-1453 voice is present in Greek with Latin (his Florentine orations in Migne 160) and in a Latin Defensio whose attribution is open. The patriarchal register is in two editions that print different selections: Migne 152 (1310–1372) and Miklosich and Muller volume 1 (1315–1402). The second volume of the register (1364–1402) was not found. Syropoulos gives the eyewitness record from the Greek side. The strongest gap is a Greek witness for Florence: the Greek in Mansi 31 is garbled, and the Acta Graeca is in copyright.

**Ecology (B2).** All four census voices are present: Palamas, Bessarion and the synods in vendored texts, Mark of Ephesus in Migne 160 and Petit's edition, and Gennadios in Greek and Latin. The opposing unionists (Mammas, Joseph of Methone, Isidore) and the Latin eyewitness of the fall (Leonard of Chios) are there too. The chronicles already in the bucket (Doukas, Sphrantzes, Anagnostes, Cananus, the Ecthesis) are lay and court voices and give the street and the court. The mix of church, court and eyewitness records is good for a conversation about a decision made under pressure.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Against the hesychast world the overlap is the 1341–1351 councils and Palamas; the census draws the boundary (this world receives the councils). Against the Ottoman world the overlap is Gennadios and the handoff in 1453. Against the Latin papal church the overlap is Florence. Each overlap is a boundary to state in Step 0.

**Audiences (B4).** The Ecumenical Patriarchate continues. Florence is a live memory in Orthodox–Catholic relations. The audience on both sides is large and reachable.
