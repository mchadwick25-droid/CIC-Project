## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor question (census floorNote): "No question."** This pass read nothing that disturbs that. The union is a communion with Rome that keeps the Byzantine liturgy; Nicene and Chalcedonian standing is not in doubt. The Filioque is the live doctrinal point in the sources, and the union's makers argued it as a matter of Roman primacy and authority. The floor test is A1 and clears.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says "moderate: synodal acts, brotherhood records, polemical literature". All three are now in hand to some degree. The acts are in Theiner's Latin versions and, since the second pass (October 2026), in their Ruthenian and Polish originals in Akty Zapadnoi Rossii vol. 4, with the brotherhood charters and the brotherhoods' protests of the 1590s in the same volume and the Vilnius brotherhood suits of 1605-1610 in AUZR vol. 2. The polemic of both sides is in RIB vol. 7 and the two Smotryc'kyj prints, with Skarga's Synod Brzeski now in a clean Polish witness and the 1621-1632 Vilnius pamphlets (garbled) in the Arkhiv vol. 7. The union's architects still speak only through the polemic and the acts: Potii's own writings are a gap (his 1605 publication of the Misail letter is in the Arkhiv vol. 7), and Josaphat's and Rutsky's own words are not in public domain. The saint is seen through hagiography written for his canonisation cause, now also in Susza's 1666 printing.

**Ecology (B2).** The shape is polemical and two-sided, which the census calls native. The main risks are named: (1) no English anywhere; every quote is an original-language quote and the voice a participant hears is a modern rendering made from Latin, Polish, Ruthenian or French; (2) three of the nine files are second witnesses, so the most characteristic Ruthenian and Polish voices cannot be quoted until the page images are checked; (3) the lives and histories are written from the union's side, and the Orthodox view comes through the *Threnos*, the *Apokrisis* and RIB vol. 4, which are partly garbled.

**Uniqueness and overlap (B3).** The overlap with Kyiv-Mohyla (VI.21) is large in sources and small in story. The two worlds share the polemical volumes and Smotryc'kyj, who stands on both sides. The Tridentine Church (VI.22) shares only the papal instruments. The two later Uniate entries continue this one. The census calls the Kyiv-Mohyla world its sibling-rival, which fits.

**Audiences (B4) and scale (B5).** Not argued here. The vendored corpus is about 2.1 million words, comparable to the Anabaptist set, with a higher share in original languages other than English. No Atlas world speaks for a communion that is Eastern in rite and Roman in obedience; the census says this "restores a whole absent communion family".

**Recommendation, for Mark to decide, not a decision: build.** Unchanged by the second pass, which strengthened it: the union's acts are now in their own languages as well as in Latin, and the Skarga texts read cleanly. Floor (A1) clears with no question. Sourcing (B1) is enough: the union's acts and both sides of the polemic are in the corpus, with Latin and Ruthenian text clean enough to quote after a page-image check. Ecology (B2) is workable but must be stated plainly in Doc_02, including the lack of Potii, Josaphat and Rutsky in their own voices and the need for page-image checks on the garbled files. Uniqueness (B3) holds because no sibling tells the story of a communion born inside an argument. If Mark reads the lack of any English and of the architects' own words as too heavy, the fallback is **part of another world**: Kyiv-Mohyla, as its two-sided partner. That fallback is weaker, since the two worlds' sources and stories point in opposite directions.
