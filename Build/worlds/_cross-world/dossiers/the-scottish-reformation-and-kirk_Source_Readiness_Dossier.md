## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Reformed Cities (VI.2, built and live) and this world's Genevan texts.** The Book of Common Order was made in Geneva in 1556 for the English exiles Knox served; the Scots Confession and the First Book of Discipline carry the Genevan order to a nation; Calvin's Catechism was the Kirk's own catechism. The rows written this pass give VI.2 `context` on each and give this world the native claim. Whether VI.2 wants to cite the Forme of Prayers of 1556 as its own text (the Geneva print is not vendored) is for that world's thread.
- **Puritanism (VI.8) and the Westminster Directory, Confession and Catechisms.** The Directory is vendored this pass inside a Scottish volume and placed as `tradition` for VI.8 (provisional). Second pass, October 2026: the Westminster Confession and Catechisms are now vendored by the Puritanism agent (`westminster-assembly_confession-catechisms-standards_nelson1877.txt`) and placed as `tradition` to both worlds, so the open question is no longer who vendors them but who holds native register. That 1877 file also prints the Directory and the National Covenant, so each of those now exists in two vendored files (Sprott and Leishman 1868 and Peterkin 1838 on this world's side); `works_fragment.yaml` records both as one Work in two Expressions. The Solemn League and Covenant (1643) is a joint Anglo-Scottish text; the cleanest PD print found is the 1820 Henderson volume (§3), and it is also in the 1877 standards file and in the Records of the Kirk.
- **Spottiswood's role (second pass).** The archbishop's history is vendored with `needs-ruling`: the first pass called him the outside witness, but he was primate of the Church of Scotland, and the first pass placed the Jacobean Assemblies' Rescinded Acts as this world's own voice in its episcopal phase. Whether the Representative speaks for the Kirk that included its bishops or for the presbyterian Kirk that rescinded them is for Doc_01 and Doc_04, and it decides whether Spottiswood is `tradition` or `context`.
- **English Reformation (VI.4).** Knox's English years (1549–1554) and the 1560 intervention are placed as `context` for VI.4, provisional. The Marian exiles' Frankfurt troubles (Knox vol. IV, not vendored) touch both worlds.
- **A later Scottish world.** `ATLAS-TARGETS.md` has no entry for the Covenanters of 1660–1690 or the Church of Scotland after 1690 (search on "scot", "covenant", "presbyter": only VI.5 and the Iona entry). The Records of the Kirk run to 1654 and Rutherford's letters to 1661, so the 1651–1654 rows are placed as `context` for this world. Whether the census needs a Restoration Covenanter entry is a portfolio question, not decided here.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census floorNote is "No question" and nothing found this pass disturbs it. The Scots Confession (1560) is plainly Nicene and Chalcedonian in its articles on God and on Christ. No floor-adjacent complication of the Anabaptist kind exists here.

**Sourcing (B1) and ecology (B2).** The Kirk's own voice is now in hand across every genre the census asked for: its confession and both books of discipline, the General Assembly's acts for 1560–1602 and 1638–1650 with the National Covenant, its service book, the densest surviving kirk-session register for 1559–1600, two first-person diaries (a minister in the Melvillian years, a lawyer in the covenanting years), and the letters that became the Kirk's devotional classic. Everything is public domain by date and most of it is clean OCR or proofread text. The census's claim that the session records are denser than Geneva's still rests on Todd's authority; the St Andrews register itself is vendored, so a build thread can judge that claim against the text. The ecology is unusually balanced for a Reformation world: institutional (Assembly, session), confessional, liturgical, narrative (Knox, Melville) and interior (Wariston, Rutherford), with one outside witness (Spottiswood) verified but not vendored. Second pass, October 2026: Spottiswood vol. III is now vendored (its role needs a ruling, §5), the Second Book of Discipline has a clean witness in Calderwood vol. III, and the Psalter of 1650 is in hand in a clean dated scan, so the devotional voice the census named is vendored and the one garbled passage of the first pass is covered. Baillie turned out to be long-s OCR and stays a lead.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Real, and testable from the texts: the national-covenantal logic (a whole kingdom bound by public oath, 1581 to 1638 to 1643) and the parish session as the unit of discipline are not Geneva's and not Puritanism's. The risk is the opposite one: Knox's voice is so strong that a Representative could collapse into him. The register, the diaries and Rutherford exist precisely to keep the world wider than Knox, and the census's own note ("the Kirk went on to outgrow Knox himself") should govern Doc_04 and Doc_10.

**Audiences (B4) and scale (B5).** A large, bounded corpus of about 17 MB, mostly in Scots, which raises a readability question for quotation that the NorthStar standard already handles for other non-modern voices. Presbyterian and Reformed audiences worldwide trace to this world, and the Covenant story is well known beyond them.

**Recommendation: build.** The floor is clear, the Kirk's own voice is vendored across every genre the census named, the ecology has institutional, narrative and interior registers in balance, and the uniqueness test (nation, covenant, session) can be argued from the texts in hand rather than from secondary claims. The two things a build thread should settle early are the Westminster-texts boundary with Puritanism (§5) and the Knox-dominance gravity risk (B3). This is a recommendation for Mark, not a decision. The second pass does not change it; it adds one ruling for the build (Spottiswood's role, which is really the question of whether the world's Kirk includes its bishops) and strengthens the sourcing on the two points the first pass flagged as thin.
