Before this pass the shelf held no hesychast text. The hits outside the files vendored for this world were:

- **Nikodemos the Hagiorite, Synaxaristes, volume 1 (September to February, Greek, 1900 printing of an 1868 text).** Bucket: the Kollyvades and Orthodoxy under the Ottomans. It names Palamas at nine places (file lines 942, 1218, 1276, 5950, 12241, 22697, 23474, 42635 and 48367); the note at 23474 says Palamas wrote a whole book against Barlaam. The Life of Palamas for 14 November was not located in the accent-garbled OCR. Tier: out-of-window (the text is the later church's custody of the saints' lives). Proposed row: `transmission`, `provisional`, in the lead's cross-link file.
- **Doukas, Sphrantzes, Anagnostes and the Ecthesis chronica (the Palaiologan church bucket).** They name the Kantakouzenos house and the civil war (Doukas on 178 lines) and nothing of the hesychast quarrel. Tier: same time-place. No row proposed for this world.
- **Georgirenes, Present State of Samos, Nicaria, Patmos and Athos (1678).** It describes the Athonite monasteries and says nothing of hesychasm in the lines searched. Tier: out-of-window (same place, three centuries later). No row proposed.

The census calls this world the parent of that line; the method tracts that the Philokalia gathered are now on the shelf in the Migne volumes (tomus 147 and 150), and the Philokalia itself is not (section 4).

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Palamas in the Palaiologan church.** The census lists Palamas among the voices of the Palaiologan church (V.15) but says that world treats the hesychast movement and its theology separately. His own writings sit here as `tradition`. The same files carry a `needs-ruling` row in the other bucket. The lead should say whether Palamas is that world's voice or only its context.
- **Nikephoros the Monk.** The census lists his text among the hesychast method texts, but he is usually placed before 1300. The row is `antecedent` / `needs-ruling`. The antecedent rule needs this world's own sources to argue from him, and that was not checked.
- **The Hesychast Revival.** The census calls this world the parent of the Philokalia line. The method tracts are now on the shelf in Migne, but the Philokalia is not. Whether that world needs the 1782 or the 1893 Greek text, and in what scan, is for the later build.
- **Kantakouzenos and Gregoras are in two worlds.** Both write on the Palaiologan church and on the hesychast quarrel. Their rows are `context` in both buckets, and the boundary between the two worlds is a Step 0 matter.
- **Cabasilas outside Thessalonica.** Cabasilas's Life in Christ is the census's lay-facing voice. Whether his lay audience puts him closer to the late-medieval lay piety worlds than to the monks is for the Doc_01 scope, not this dossier.
- **Modern English translations of Palamas.** Three short items carry a Public Domain Mark set by the uploader. The translator is unnamed and the translation is modern. The lead should decide whether that Mark is enough to vendor them, or whether a translator's own statement is needed.
- **Mansi's label.** Mansi calls the synod of 1347 a 'Pseudo-Synodus'. A Representative should not repeat that as a fact; it is the Latin editor's frame.
- **Dating of Bessarion's Apologia on Beccus and Palamas.** It belongs to the fifteenth century. The row is `context` / `provisional` here and not in the window.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census floor note is "No question". Nothing read in this pass bears on the floor, and no floor analysis was attempted. The synodal tomes were located and sampled, not read through.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says "Rich theologically; Cabasilas is a genuinely lay-facing formation voice". That holds, with one change: the rich part is now on the shelf in the original languages. Palamas speaks in his own Greek in two places (the Migne hesychast writings and the clean first edition of forty-one homilies). Gregory of Sinai, Kallistos and Ignatios Xanthopoulos, Telikoudes, Katafygiotes and Nikephoros the Monk give the method. Cabasilas is present in the Latin of the Life in Christ and the Greek and Latin of the Commentary, with the Greek of the Life in a garbled second witness. The synodal tomes are there in Greek with Latin and, again, in Mansi. The weak point is English: no public-domain English translation of any of these was found, so every English line a Representative speaks is an Opus rendering of the Greek or Latin, as the intake rule allows.

**Ecology (B2).** Both sides are on the shelf in their own words. Gregoras, Akindynos, Barlaam, Cyparissiotes and Calecas speak for the opposition, and Kantakouzenos gives the emperor's view. The census says the anti-Palamite side is largely untranslated. That holds, and here it is in Greek with Latin. The risk the sources show is a practical one: the Latin and Greek share one OCR file in most volumes, and a quote has to be checked against the page image.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Against the Palaiologan church the overlap is the same decades and the same councils, and the boundary is the one the census draws (the church receives the councils; this world is the movement). Against the Athonite and Comnenian monks (before 1300) and the Hesychast Revival (after 1746) the overlap is the tradition itself. Against the lay-piety worlds the link is Cabasilas.

**Audiences (B4).** The Palamite position is the Orthodox norm today. The audience is large and reachable. The Jesus Prayer is practised by many who know nothing of the fourteenth century.

**Scale (B5).** About 27.5 MB vendored for this world in eleven files, with about 20.6 million characters, and four more shared files (17.0 MB) that carry a second row here. The staging rows for this world number 28: 13 `assigned`, 14 `provisional` and 1 `needs-ruling`.

**Recommendation: build.** The movement speaks in its own voice in vendored texts: Palamas (twice), Gregory of Sinai, the Kallistos group of method writers with Nikephoros the Monk, and Cabasilas, plus both sides of the controversy in Greek and Latin and the synodal documents. The gap is a public-domain English leg, which was searched for and not found. A build can proceed on the intake rule that a clean original is primary, with every quote verified against the scan. The two things to settle before Doc_01 are the Palamas placement across the two worlds (section 5) and whether the three modern English translations of Palamas may be vendored.

This is a recommendation for Mark to weigh, not a decision.
