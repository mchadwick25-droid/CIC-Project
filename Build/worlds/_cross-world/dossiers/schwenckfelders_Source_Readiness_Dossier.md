## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor question (census floorNote, quoted).** "They diverge on one point: Schwenckfeld taught that Christ's humanity was heavenly rather than ordinary flesh, and his followers suspended the sacraments. Neither position denies Christ's full divinity, which is why their standing relative to the historic creeds is genuinely arguable rather than settled." The sources now in hand bear on this directly. The Formula of Concord (Epitome XII and Solid Declaration XII, vendored) lists the Schwenckfeldians' errors, among them that by his exaltation Christ's flesh took on all divine properties so that it belongs to the essence of the Trinity (Epitome XII, article 2, file line 27839 onward), and that the preached Word, baptism and the bread and wine are not means through which God works (articles 3 to 5). The Württemberg 1544 summary (Bossert, line 6522 onward) records an official's own paraphrase of a Schwenckfeld booklet that claims Christ, the eternal Son, was true God and man, and that the creaturehood of his humanity was "vergottet" (made divine) in the resurrection. This is the same kind of complication as Menno's celestial flesh in the Anabaptist dossier, though here the opponents call it a mixing of the natures. It is not an outright Trinity rejection, unlike VI.14. It is recorded here as a floor-adjacent complication, not a finding.

**Uniqueness and overlap.** Siblings are the Anabaptists (VI.3, with which Württemberg and the Formula group the Schwenckfelders), Lutheran Wittenberg (VI.1, the persecutor and the confessional frame), and Family of Love and Spiritualists (VI.16, the same Spiritualist wing). The distinct feature here is the "standing still" from the Lord's Supper (1526) kept by a house-meeting community for generations. No sibling carries it.

**Scale.** About 1.25 million words are now vendored, but only about 410,000 are Schwenckfeld-community voice (Corpus vol. I, Erläuterung, Sudermann, Heavenly Balm, Short Questions); the rest is the Lutheran frame (Jacobs, ~373,000) and scholarship (Kriebel). Of the voice, only the Erläuterung (about the Silesian period, written 1771 and printed 1830), Sudermann and the Anspach translation touch the window or near it. No Schwenckfeld text from 1540 to 1561 is in the corpus except through hostile summary in Bossert.

**Tests (Step 0).** Floor A1: arguable, not failed. Sourcing B1: thin for the window. The census says "modest but real", and the corpus confirms modest. Ecology B2: inside voice is mostly late (Pennsylvania, 18th-19th century) and secondary; the Silesian period reaches us through the community's own later history and through opponents. Uniqueness B3: holds. Audiences B4 and scale B5: not assessed here.

**Recommendation, for the project lead to decide, not a decision: open question.** The floor test is arguable by the census's own words, and the sourcing for the window is the weak point: the Great Confession and every post-1524 letter are not reachable. I recommend holding the entry as an held back until the project lead rules on the floor and until a later pass tries Google Books and the print holdings for Corpus volumes 2-8 (1911-1930, public domain by date). If Mark rules the floor clears and wants a world, the corpus can carry a short, honest build only on the Erläuterung, Sudermann and the Anspach text, with the absence of the 1540s voice stated plainly. Otherwise **atlas only** is the fallback. Neither choice is final.
