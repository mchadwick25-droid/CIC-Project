## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Canons of Dort and the 1840 standards volume.** The vendored 1840 file carries the Canons in English. Another agent owns the Remonstrants and Dort (VI.26). The Canons row is staged `tradition / provisional` for VI.26 and for this world; that agent should confirm, recast or replace it with a better witness. The Heidelberg Catechism cross-link to VI.26 is likewise left provisional.
- **Where the boundary with VI.26 runs.** The Remonstrant controversy (1610–1619) happens inside this world's place and window, and Voetius sat at Dort. Step 0 and Doc_01 must decide whether this world treats the controversy as context seen from the precisianist pulpit, and leaves its doctrine to VI.26. The staging rows assume that.
- **Taffin: Dutch or Huguenot?** A Walloon pastor writing French for the Low Countries. Staged as this world's own voice and as Huguenot context. The Huguenots agent may want him the other way round.
- **The English exiles (Ames, Robinson, the Amsterdam Baptists, the London stranger churches).** Primary home is Puritanism or the English Reformation; here they are refuge-culture context. The Puritanism agent should take Ames and Robinson (§3 leads).
- **Window edges.** Marnix's Bijenkorf is 1569, one year before the window opens; Voetius' lessons were printed in 1662 from an Utrecht ministry that began in 1634. Both are staged `assigned` on the author's place in the movement, with the dates stated in the notes; Doc_01 should say how it reads the window.
- **Dutch originals without English.** Voetius, the London minutes, the synod acts and Marnix have no public-domain English at all. A build will lean on Opus renderings from Dutch under the 2026-09-25 ruling, and the modern_rendering rule in `CLAUDE.md` applies. Say this plainly in Doc_02 rather than treating it as a gap to fill.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census says "No question", and nothing found disturbs that: the Belgic Confession, the Heidelberg Catechism and the Canons are the world's own floor texts, now in the corpus in English, and Voetius' lessons on the Trinity and the incarnation (vol. 1, questions on the creed) were sampled and are orthodox in the plainest terms.

**Sourcing (B1).** Rich, as the census says, but in a particular shape: four short English primary voices (two Teellinck tracts, Taffin, Schurman), a large Dutch mass (Voetius, Marnix, the London minutes, six synods) with no English, and the church's standards and liturgy in a nineteenth-century English. Udemans has no usable witness yet. The lead list in §3 can roughly double the Dutch mass (Hessels, the provincial synods) without adding English.

**Ecology (B2).** Two halves that the census name joins: the "further reformation" of personal piety (Teellinck, Taffin, Voetius, Schurman) and the "refuge" of institutions under pressure (London consistory, exile synods). The institutional half is strong and in-window; the piety half speaks in English only through translations made for English Puritans, which is itself a fact about the world. No single author dominates, so founder-corpus gravity is low; the risk is the opposite, a voice spread thin across catechism, minutes and tracts.

**Uniqueness (B3).** The census's own doubt stands: "a distinct tradition or simply the devotional intensity of Dutch Reformed church life". The sources answer it halfway. Nothing here is a separate church; everything is the Dutch Reformed church examining itself. What is distinct is the register: catechetical precision, examined daily life, refuge discipline. Overlap with VI.26 is real on place and window and must be ruled on at Step 0 (§5); overlap with VI.2 is doctrinal descent only.

**Audiences (B4).** Reformed and Presbyterian descendants, the Dutch diaspora in North America and South Africa, and readers of the English Puritans who met Teellinck and Taffin in print. Real and reachable.

**Scale (B5).** About 7.3 MB vendored in eleven files, thirty assignments; comparable in bulk to the Reformed Cities' first pass, smaller in English voice.

**Recommendation: build**, as one world that holds the piety and the refuge together, with its Step 0 ruling the VI.26 boundary before Doc_01. The reasons: the floor is clear (A1); the world's own confessions, liturgy, synods and consistory records are now in hand alongside four contemporary English voices (B1, B2); the distinct register answers the census's doubt well enough to try (B3); and the audience is large (B4). The cost to say up front is that most of the Representative's quoted matter will be Opus renderings from Dutch, and that Udemans is missing. This is a recommendation for Mark to weigh, not a decision.
