**Voices.** The census names five. Catherine of Siena speaks in her own Italian (clean text) and in two English books. Birgitta speaks in Latin (second witness) and in a fifteenth-century English version. Boniface VIII is in Mirbt's documents 307 and 309 (the bulls *Clericis laicos* and *Unam sanctam*) and nowhere else on the shelf. Martin V is in Mirbt's document 321 (the condemnation of Wyclif and Hus, 1418) and in the closing sessions of Mansi 27. Julius II speaks only through the acts of Fifth Lateran in Mansi 32. The shelf holds no private letter or sermon of Boniface VIII, Martin V or Julius II.

**Councils.** Finke vol. 2 is the best text in the set: the diaries of Cardinal Fillastre and of Cerretanus, the sermons preached at Constance and the reform papers, in clean Latin. Finke vol. 3 holds the three obediences (John XXIII with the articles against him, Gregory XII, Benedict XIII at Perpignan). Mansi 27 carries the decrees. Mansi 32 carries Lateran V.

**Conciliarists and opponents.** Gerson speaks in his treatises on the Schism (second witness: long s read as f). Marsilius is a contemporary opponent condemned at Avignon, so he is staged as context, not tradition. Richental is the layman's eyewitness chronicle of Constance.

**Court voices.** The Avignon popes' lives (Baluze, Mollat's text) and Burchard's ceremonial diary of the Rome of Alexander VI give the institution's own day-to-day record. Mirbt is a scholar's source book of official acts in their own languages; each passage needs a check against the page image and the act itself.

**Scan quality.** Witness grade follows the registry notes. Clean: the Italian Dialogo, both Catherine English books, Finke vol. 2. Readable, quote after checking the scan: Cumming, Marsilius, Mirbt, Richental, Baluze, Burchard, Höfler II. Second witness: the Latin Birgitta, Gerson, Finke vol. 3, Mansi 27 and 32.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Where the Gregorian entry ends and this one begins.** *Clericis laicos* (1296) is before the window and *Unam sanctam* (1302) is inside it. Mirbt's documents before 1300 are proposed for the Gregorian entry. The census names Boniface VIII among this entry's voices.
- **Franciscan worlds.** The Franciscan pass staged the Bullarium, Ehrle, Lea and Scholz here and on the Fraticelli and Franciscan entries. Scholz is `needs-ruling` on both. The poverty quarrel under John XXII belongs to the Franciscan entries; the papacy's side of it belongs here. Step 0 for each should say where it stops.
- **The Hussite entry.** Constance condemned Hus and the Hussite wars ended in Basel's Compactata. This entry holds the council's acts; the Hussite entry holds the defendant and the war. The trial of Hus is staged here as context only.
- **Lollardy.** Constance also condemned Wyclif (Mirbt document 321). Which entry reads that condemnation is open.
- **Conciliar voices as tradition or context.** Gerson is staged as tradition (assigned); Marsilius as context (assigned). The census says conciliarism is carried inside the story. Whether a defeated constitutional claim speaks as the church or about it is for Step 0.
- **Catherine and Birgitta.** The census calls them the Avignon-era prophetic women and lists them as voices. Their staging here is tradition. If a Dominican or a Brigittine entry is ever opened, the same files would serve it.
- **Mansi 32 and Trent.** The volume spans 1438 to 1549 and reaches the run-up to Trent. Only the Fifth Lateran and Pisan acts fall inside the window.
- **Converso context.** The census carries the 1391 pogroms, the 1478 Inquisition and the 1492 expulsion as context. This pass did not search for sources on them, and the shelf holds none for them.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census says no question (Chalcedonian-Latin). Nothing found disturbs that. Catherine's *Dialogo* and Birgitta's *Revelations* are devotional prophecy inside the Latin church; Gerson, Finke's sermons and the council's decrees are the church arguing about its own constitution.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says "Rich", and for the councils that holds. Constance is held in five files (Finke vol. 2 and 3, Mansi 27, Richental, Gerson), Lateran V in Mansi 32, and the Avignon court in Baluze's lives and the Bullarium. The weak points are the named popes. Boniface VIII, Martin V and Julius II speak only through official acts, and the Avignon registers themselves are not on the shelf. Basel is held only through the Hussite chronicle (Höfler II) and Palacký.

**Ecology (B2).** The set has an inside voice, an opposing voice and an outsider for the same events: the council's own diaries, Gerson and Marsilius against the plenitude of papal power, and Richental the townsman. Catherine and Birgitta add two women who address popes directly. The risk is that the clean texts (Finke II, the Dialogo) are not the ones that carry the constitutional argument; those are second witnesses (Gerson, Mansi).

**Uniqueness (B3).** Against the Gregorian entry the difference is the exile, the Schism and the conciliar claim. Against the Hussite entry the overlap is Constance and Basel; the difference is the side the document is written from.

**Audiences (B4).** The census says this is a continuous institution: the church is the Roman Catholic Church of today, and the conciliar claim resurfaced at the First Vatican Council. The audience on both sides of the conciliar question is large.

**Scale (B5).** About 32.0 million characters in fifteen vendored files, of which about 13.3 million are clean or readable first-witness text and about 18.7 million are second witnesses. Three more files from the Franciscan pass add about 11 million characters of papal bulls and polemic.

**Recommendation: build.** The floor is clear (A1). Catherine speaks in her own Italian with two English books beside her, Birgitta in Latin and Middle English, the council in its own diaries and sermons, and the court in its own lives and diary (B1, B2). The audience is large (B4). The cost to say up front is that the constitutional argument rests on second-witness Latin (Gerson, Mansi, Finke vol. 3) and that Martin V, Boniface VIII and Julius II have no voice but their official acts. This is a recommendation for Mark to weigh, not a decision.
