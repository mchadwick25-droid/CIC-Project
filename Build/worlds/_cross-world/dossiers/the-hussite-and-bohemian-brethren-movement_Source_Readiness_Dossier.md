## 5. Open cross-world questions

**Vs. Lollardy (V.5, vendored this same batch):** the census's own
`relationsSummary` for Lollardy names a "contested influence line to the
English Reformation," not directly to Hussitism — but the real, better-
documented historical connection runs the other way: Wycliffe's own
writings reached Prague (via university and court contacts between England
and Bohemia) and directly shaped Hus's own reform preaching before his
1415 execution. Neither vendored corpus here currently makes that link
explicit in a corpus-map note; worth flagging for whichever Doc_01/Doc_02
runs first for either world, though the two movements diverged sharply in
outcome (Lollardy stayed underground and persecuted; Hussitism won legal
recognition for a functioning national church in 1436) and are correctly
two separate candidates, not one.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes (for whoever drafts Doc_01/the Step 0 confirmation)

**Doctrinal floor:** not assessed in depth this pass — a real Step 0 pass
should check Hus's own Christology/Trinity commitments directly against
De Ecclesia and the Letters before assuming it clears, the same discipline
already applied to the Anabaptist and Jesuit candidates' own Step 0 notes.
Nothing in the material fetched this pass raised a visible complication,
but that is not the same as a completed check.

**Scale honestly stated:** thinner in named-voice range than a multi-figure
candidate like the Jesuits (Hus alone, not a movement of named theologians)
but each of the two vendored works is substantial and squarely his own
voice — closer in shape to the Anabaptists' Menno Simons pillar than to a
thin candidate. The movement's own institutional/legal-recognition story
(Bohemia's 1436 settlement) and its later Unity of the Brethren branch are
currently undocumented in the vendored corpus (see §4) — a real gap if
either becomes part of this world's own gravity, not filled here.
