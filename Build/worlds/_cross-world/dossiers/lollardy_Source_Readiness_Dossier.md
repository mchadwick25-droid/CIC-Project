**What the base is.** The earlier text of this dossier said the base was all native voice, with no hostile or outside material. That is no longer true.

- **The movement's own voice, English.** Wyclif's English sermons and tracts (Arnold, three volumes; Matthew; Vaughan), the Wycliffite Bible, and now the Lanterne of Light, the Apology for Lollard Doctrines, Jack Upland with Friar Daw's reply and Upland's rejoinder (Wright), and William Thorpe's Examination of 1407 (Pollard). The Apology's file is a second witness: thorn is read as f throughout.
- **Wyclif in Latin.** De Ecclesia, De Veritate Sacrae Scripturae and the Polemical Works vol. 2 were already on the shelf; this pass adds the Trialogus (Lechler), De Civili Dominio book 1 (Poole), the Sermones vol. 1 (Loserth) and De Officio Regis (Pollard and Sayle). All are primary Latin, with OCR caveats in each file's header. The only public-domain English of the Trialogus is the chapters of book 4 in Vaughan (already vendored).
- **The hostile and outside record.** The Fasciculi Zizaniorum (with the Twelve Conclusions) and Foxe vol. III were already staged as `context`. This pass adds Walsingham's Historia Anglicana vol. 2 (1381–1422), Knighton's Chronicon vol. 2, Pecock's Repressor (two volumes), Powell and Trevelyan's documents, and Foxe vol. IV (the Lollards of the early 1500s). Another worker staged the Lincoln registers, which hold the 1457 proceedings against the Lollard brothers Sparke and the Huntingdonshire Lollards.
- **What is still missing.** Hawisia Moone's confession and the Norwich trials (Tanner, 1977) are in copyright or borrow-only (§4). The census's named voice is therefore not on the shelf. Women's and artisans' stated beliefs reach the library only through Foxe, Lincoln and Powell.

The shelf was searched for Wyclif, Wiclif, Wycliffe, Lollard, Oldcastle and Wycliffite in headers and bodies, and the hits were opened. The earlier version of this section said none were expected; the Hussite side turned up several. Proposed rows are in `crosslinks.yaml`, all `provisional`.

- **Hus, Letters, Workman and Pope (`hus_letters_workman-pope1904.txt`)** (same bucket). About 150 mentions of Wyclif: the burning and defence of his books and the condemnation of his articles. Proposed as `transmission`. Tier: same time-different region.
- **Luetzow, Life and Times of Master John Hus (`lutzow_life-and-times-of-hus_1909.txt`)** (same bucket). Secondary prose with about 175 mentions. Proposed as `context`.
- **Knox, Works vol. 1 (`knox_works-v1_laing1895.txt`)** (bucket: the Scottish Reformation). An appendix, "On the Lollards in Scotland, during the fifteenth century", tells of the Lollards of Kyle. Proposed as `context`. Tier: same time-different region.
- **Staged by other workers for this world:** the Lay Folks' Catechism (EETS 118, `thoresby_lay-folks-catechism-enm-lat_simmons-nolloth1901.txt`; its host title lists a Wyclif version beside Thoresby's), Mansi vol. 27 (the Constance acts of 1409 to 1418, `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-27-1409-1418-lat_welter-facsimile1902.txt`), Love's Mirror (`love_mirror-of-the-blessed-life-of-jesus-christ-enm_powell1908.txt`, staged as `needs-ruling`) and the Lincoln registers (`lincoln-diocese_documents-1450-1544-enm_clark1914.txt`, the same edition this pass fetched). All four are consistent with this dossier.
- Noted, no row proposed: Foxe vol. VII and VIII on the shelf name the Lollards a few times (16 and 37 hits) but are Marian-era volumes. The Waldensian histories (Perrin, Leger, Morland) name Wyclif in passing. The Zwingli volumes and the Book of Concord have single mentions.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Hussite world (VI.12 on the census list).** Wyclif's works and Hus's use of them run both ways. The three Hus cross-links are proposed as `transmission` here. The Trialogus is staged as an `antecedent` for the Hussite world, provisional. Each world's Doc_01 should say what it takes from Wyclif.
- **The English Reformation (VI.4 on the Era VII list).** The census's `relationsSummary` calls the influence line "contested". Foxe vol. IV and the Lincoln registers are staged to that world as `antecedent`, provisional. Neither world's dossier decides the contest.
- **Late-medieval lay parish piety.** Pecock's Repressor and the Lincoln registers are staged there as `context`, provisional. Whether the hostile record belongs to Lollardy's ecology or to the parish world's is a question for each Doc_02.
- **Where Wyclif ends and the movement begins.** The census asks that Wyclif's own corpus be kept distinct from the movement's later texts. Eleven of the staged `tradition` rows are Wyclif's own; the Lanterne, the Apology, Jack Upland and Thorpe are the movement's. The Apology is attributed to Wyclif in its title, and Jack Upland's authorship is unknown.
- **The English Mystics and Devout.** Love's Mirror, staged by another worker as `needs-ruling`, belongs to the contrast between orthodox English devotion and the Lollard line.
- **Facsimile scans.** Three vendored Wyclif Society volumes are Johnson Reprint facsimiles (De Ecclesia, De Civili Dominio book 1) or are catalogued by the host with a facsimile's date. They are treated as public domain by the edition's date. This is flagged for the lead.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**The person-defined-movement floor question is already cleared** (census `floorNote`) — not re-run here. What the September pass added: two additional genuinely-PD 19th-century lead sources (Arnold 1869–71, Vaughan 1845) not named in the census at all, both stronger acquisition candidates than the four named-but-unverified/in-copyright sources it did list. Every source the census marked "Verified" turns out to require a rights-check it never actually ran; only the pre-1929 editions (Arnold, Matthew, Forshall & Madden, Buddensieg/Wyclif Society, Vaughan) are open leads.

**Carried forward from the census's own record, not re-litigated:** the contested influence-line from Lollardy to the English Reformation (`relationsSummary`: "Contested influence to the English Reformation") — the census's own `why` field states this candidate "carries the contest rather than the conclusion," and this dossier does the same. If the English Reformation (VI.4, Era VII) is ever added to this project's roster, Doc_01 for either world should engage this contested link directly rather than assume it.

**Floor (A1).** No creedal question, per the census. The files record Lollard positions on the sacrament, images, pilgrimage and church wealth. None was read against the Creed in this pass.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says hostile trial records plus real internal texts. The internal texts are on the shelf: Wyclif in Latin and English, the Bible, the Lanterne, the Apology, Jack Upland, Thorpe, the Twelve Conclusions. The hostile record is deep: two chronicles, Pecock, the Lincoln proceedings of 1457, Powell's documents, Foxe vols. III and IV. The thin part is the census's named voice: the Norwich trials with Hawisia Moone are closed.

**Ecology (B2).** An Oxford theologian, a movement of artisans, and a persecuting record. The risk the sources show: the movement's own English is mostly tracts and sermons by learned men, while ordinary believers speak only through bishops' registers and Foxe. A Representative drawn from the shelf would sound like the learned wing.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Against the Hussites the shared ground is Wyclif; the difference is place and the political break. Against the English Reformation and the English Mystics the lines are the contested descent and the orthodox contrast. Against the parish world the shared ground is the church records.

**Audiences (B4).** English-speaking Protestants claim the Lollards as forerunners, and Catholics read the same records as heresy trials. The audience is large and reachable.

**Scale (B5).** Fourteen files vendored this pass, 15.6 MB, 15.5 million characters, with the eleven earlier rows. Foxe vol. IV (3.2 MB) is the largest file. The earlier Wycliffite Bible file is about 14 MB and was vendored before this pass.

**Recommendation: build.** The floor is clear (A1). The movement speaks in its own English in four vendored texts, and Wyclif speaks in Latin and English in a dozen. The hostile record is deep, and a public-domain English leg exists for nearly everything. The cost to say up front: the Norwich trials with Hawisia Moone, the census's named voice, are not available, so ordinary believers' stated beliefs come only through Lincoln 1457, Powell's documents and Foxe. A Doc_02 should say so and keep Wyclif's own corpus distinct from the movement's texts. This is a recommendation for Mark to weigh, not a decision.
