## 6. Step 0 scope notes (for whoever drafts Doc_01/the Step 0 confirmation)

**Trinity/Christology vs. the anti-Trinitarian current (VI.14):** mainstream Anabaptism (Swiss Brethren, Hubmaier, Menno) is doctrinally distinct from and not conflated with VI.14's anti-Trinitarian current — no confirmed source above denies the Trinity. **But there is a genuine, disclosable floor-adjacent complication unique to the Dutch/Menno wing, not present among the Swiss Brethren:** Menno (following Melchior Hoffman) taught "celestial flesh" Christology — that Christ's flesh originated in heaven and merely passed through Mary "as through a channel," rather than being derived from her. This bears directly on the floor test's third commitment (Christ "truly human... incarnate of... the Virgin Mary") and needs the same bounded-disclosure treatment the Latin Apologists' Step 0 gives Minucius Felix's Christological absence — a real complication, not disqualifying, and distinct in kind from VI.14's outright Trinity rejection.

**Believer's baptism/rebaptism** is correctly a discipline/practice question, not a floor question — same shape as Tertullian's Montanism in the Latin Apologists precedent.

**Münster** should be excluded/flagged as outside "mainstream" — none of the sources above are Münsterite.

**Scale, stated honestly:** thinner than the patristic worlds in breadth of authorial voices — Hubmaier and Marpeck (two major early theologians) currently have no clean PD primary-source path, and Grebel survives only in fragments regardless. But the Martyrs Mirror alone is likely comparable in scale to a mid-size patristic corpus, and Menno's complete 1871 works plus Dirk Philips give a real three-pillar base (Swiss/martyrological, Dutch/Menno, Dutch/Philips). Overall: thin in named-theologian range, unexpectedly strong in martyrological/testimonial bulk.
