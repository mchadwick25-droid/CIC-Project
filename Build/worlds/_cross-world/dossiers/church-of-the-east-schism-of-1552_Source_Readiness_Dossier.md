**Time window:** 1517-1650 (the census). The core events run 1551-1555: the election of Yohannan Sulaqa, the journey to Rome, the return and his death. The two lines then run on through the whole window.
**Region(s):** Mesopotamia (Mosul, Alqosh, Amid, Gazarta), Hakkari, Rome

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Where does the schism belong?** The census gives it its own entry, but its causes (the hereditary succession from about 1450, the shrinking to Hakkari) sit in `church-of-the-east-timur-to-hakkari` (V.17, 1317-1517), and its long result (the two lines, the Chaldean recognition of 1830, the consolidation) sits in `church-of-the-east-chaldean-consolidation` (VIII.40, 1815-1906). The same sources (Assemani, Lamy's appendix, Wigram chapter VII, Malech) serve all three. The census also tags this entry with the `eastern-catholic-uniate` stream, which links it to the Union of Brest (dossier exists) and the Armenian Catholic and Maronite entries. This dossier names the question and decides nothing; see section 6.
- **St. Thomas Christians (VI.18 and V.23).** Abdisho's profession lists the Indian sees under his jurisdiction; Assemani chapter IX and Raulin Dissertatio V give the bishops Joseph, Abraham and Simeon sent out after 1552. The VI.18 dossier left the Chaldean connection undecided between the two worlds. Rows for both are staged as provisional context.
- **The Tridentine Church (VI.22).** Abdisho's 1562 oath to receive Trent is a Tridentine document too. One provisional context row is staged.
- **Dates disagree across the sources, and this dossier does not settle them.** The census has an election in 1552, consecration at Rome in 1553 and death in 1555. Assemani's 1725 list reads: arrival at Rome 18 November 1552, profession 20 February 1553, proclamation 9 April 1553, return to Amid 12 November 1553, death early 1555. Lamy's appendix reads the proclamation as 9 April 1552 and the return as 12 November 1553. Labourt dates the vacancy 1551. Wigram dates the old patriarch's death to 1552. Badger quotes Mosheim for 1553. Assemani's 1728 OCR is garbled on the dates. A build should read them off the page images.
- **Patriarch numbering and names.** Assemani and Lamy number the rival "Simeon VIII Barmama" (Denha) and call Sulaka "Joannes". Malech calls Sulaka "Shimon VIII" and the rival "Shimon VII Denkah". Wigram writes "Shimun Dinkha". The conflict is the sources' own and should be carried forward as such.
- **Who bribed the pasha.** Assemani says the rival Barmama caused Sulaka's death. Wigram says men believed it but there is no proof. Badger calls the Roman accounts "contradictory and improbable" and quotes a Jesuit story of a treasurer's second marriage. All three are in the corpus or listed.
- **Author slugs.** Four new slugs (`abdisho-iv`, `assemani`, `scher`, `malech`) carry `authors_ruled` (metadata-omission) with title-page evidence in their staging files. `wigram` is reused from the 1910 staging file with its own block. The lead should confirm the slugs at merge.
- **Reader-page rights.** Every vendored file is a whole text public domain by date; the registry trailers say so. The Malech file carries a few decorative plates as page-edge noise in the OCR.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census floorNote reads "No question - both patriarchal lines belong to the same ancient communion and share the same faith." The vendored sources do not contradict it. Abdisho IV's 1562 profession is the Roman creed sworn on the Gospels and Assemani quotes Sulaka's profession on the sacraments. Whether a Roman-formula profession changes what the plain-side line believed is the Roman and Assyrian sides' own dispute, not a floor problem. Step 0 should disclose two points: the Roman-side documents come from a party that wanted a submission, and the sources disagree on the dates and on the death of Sulaka (section 5).

**Sourcing (B1) and ecology (B2): the census calls it thin; the test.** The census is right about voices and too cautious about events.

- For events: the Roman side is documented in Latin (Assemani, Lamy, Le Quien listed) and in English by outsiders (Geddes, Hough, Wigram, Badger listed) and by one insider (Malech). The full text of one patriarch's profession (Abdisho 1562) is in hand. The colophons are present in summary.
- For voices: nothing in the first person survives from the Shimun line for 1551-1650 on any reachable host. Sulaqa's own profession is quoted but not found whole. Only Abdisho's 1562 profession is first person, and it is a Roman form. The Assyrian side appears through Malech (1910), Wigram and Badger, all later.
- The ecology would be Rome-heavy and outsider-heavy, as the census says. That is the entry's actual condition, not a search gap: Doc_02 would state it as an absence.

**Uniqueness (B3).** No sibling tells the 1551-1555 story. The same communion's Indian branch (VI.18) is a different ordeal with a different record. V.17 and VIII.40 are the chronological neighbours and share the sources and the figures; neither has a dossier yet.

**Audiences (B4).** Two living churches, the Chaldean Catholic Church and the Assyrian Church of the East, keep this memory and tell it differently. The census legacy field says so.

**Scale (B5).** About 4.3 million characters in five vendored files, but the passages on 1551 to 1562 are a few tens of thousands of characters: Assemani's account (a few pages), the 1562 profession (~11,000), Wigram's pages, Malech's paragraphs, and the colophon notes. The rest is context for the neighbours. Language: Latin and French for the primary and Roman material, English for the later narratives.

**Recommendation: part of another world, `church-of-the-east-timur-to-hakkari` (V.17), as its closing arc, with `church-of-the-east-chaldean-consolidation` (VIII.40) as the continuation.** This is a recommendation for Mark, not a decision. The schism is a four-year episode whose causes and whose outcome sit in the two neighbouring entries, and all three draw on the same sources; one build could hold the whole arc from the hereditary law of 1450 to the two lines, and keep the Roman-side documents as context. As a world of its own it would have only one first-person text in its window and no text in the Shimun line's own voice, so a Representative for either line would speak without a voice to draw on.
