What kind of witness each is:

- **The parish's own accounts.** Bristol All Saints' (three volumes) is clean English: the editor's translation of Latin and English records, under an open licence. Hobhouse's six parishes and the London parish of St Mary at Hill are early English with noisy OCR, so they are second witnesses. Morebath starts in 1520, three years after the window.
- **Wills.** The York wills (three volumes), the Bury wills, the Lincoln documents and Bristol Part 3 give lay will-makers in first person. All but Bristol are second witnesses. The Lincoln and Bury volumes run past the window.
- **Prayer books.** The Cambridge Prymer and the York Horae Eboracenses are the household's prayers. The Lay Folks Mass Book shows how the layman followed the Mass. All three are noisy OCR.
- **Teaching and preaching.** Mirk's Festial and his Instructions for Parish Priests, and the Lay Folks' Catechism. These are the clergy speaking to the laity, not the laity speaking. They are the nearest proxy for what the pew was told.
- **Fraternities.** Toulmin Smith's English Gilds gives the gilds' own ordinances. The returns date from 1389, before the window opens.
- **The outside record.** The Norwich visitations (Latin, no English translation vendored) and the Lincoln documents record how bishops saw the parishes.
- **Reading culture.** Love's Mirrour, the Pricke of Conscience, the Craft of Dying and the English Imitation are the books the literate lay reader could have had. These are placed as context or provisional.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Reformation boundary.** The window ends in 1517. Morebath starts in 1520, Hobhouse runs to 1560 and the injunctions of 1536 follow. The English Reformation world holds the same parishes after the break. A Step 0 should say which years each world reads.
- **Lollardy.** The Lollard attacks on pilgrimage, images and offerings are the hostile record of the same practice. The Lollard version of the Lay Folks' Catechism is printed in the edition vendored here, and the Lincoln volume prints proceedings against Lollards in 1457. Which of these the Lollardy world claims is for that world's leads.
- **The English Mystics.** The Prymer, Love's *Mirrour*, the Pricke of Conscience and the Craft of Dying are shared reading. They are staged here as tradition or context, provisional.
- **Borromean Milan.** The census names Borromean Milan as the later parallel. This pass did not open any Milan text.
- **No named voice.** The census says there is no founder or spokesman. The lay voice speaks in wills, accounts and gild rules, and in the clergy's sermons to the laity. A Representative for this world would have to be a composite or a named testator. That is a Representative identity decision, which the build-cycle rules send to Mark. This pass invented nothing.
- **Open licence.** The three Bristol volumes are CC BY-SA 4.0 on the host. Share-alike may bind anything built on them. The volumes also print "© Clive Burgess". The licence is the host's own statement. The project lead should confirm before whole-text republication.
- **Editor's English.** The Bristol volumes are the editor's modern English of Latin and English records. A quote record must say it quotes a translation.
- **The Norwich visitations.** Latin, with no English translation vendored. A build would read them with the Latin gap noted in its Doc_02.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census says "No question". Nothing found disturbs that. The texts were not read for doctrine in this pass, so the floor call stays the census's.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says "rich but oblique", and that holds. Practice is well documented: wardens' accounts from eight parishes (Hobhouse's six, which include Morebath in Devon; All Saints' in Bristol; and St Mary at Hill in London); will-makers from York, Suffolk, Lincoln and Bristol; two prayer books and a Mass book; the preaching and teaching the clergy gave; gild ordinances. Almost all of it is early English or Latin OCR, so most files are second witnesses that locate passages and need a page-image check before quoting. The clean legs are the three Bristol volumes (an editor's English under an open licence) and Hobhouse's preface. There is no interior writing by an ordinary parishioner. The nearest are the wills.

**Ecology (B2).** The parish speaks through its money and its wills. The clergy speak to it in Mirk and the Catechism. Bishops describe it in the visitations. Lollards attack it in the Wyclif tracts. That is a good shape for a world that tests whose account of a practice to trust. The risk is that the parish's own words come mostly through accounts and wills, which are lists.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Against the English Reformation world the overlap is place and the same parishes, with a date line at 1517 to 1536. Against Lollardy the overlap is the same practice seen from the hostile side. Against the English Mystics the overlap is the shared prayer books and reading. Each is a boundary to state, not a reason to merge.

**Audiences (B4).** The census says the great parish churches are still standing and the practice was built again in Borromean Milan. The audience is wide.

**Scale (B5).** 22 files, 19.3 MB, plus Morebath. Section 3 lists the open leads: Foster's Cambridge accounts, Part II of St Mary at Hill, the later York wills, the Caxton Golden Legend and the Wilkins Latin.

**Recommendation: build.** The depth is real: more than a dozen primary or near-primary records of the window in English or Latin, a clean open-licence English leg for one large parish, and a hostile record and an episcopal record beside them. The cost to say up front is that the lay voice is a set of wills and accounts, not a person, so the Representative identity is a decision for Mark under the build rules, and the Middle English and Latin files are second witnesses that need page-image checks. This is a recommendation for Mark to weigh, not a decision.
