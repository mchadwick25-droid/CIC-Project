What the new files change. Three of the five strands the census names are now in the corpus in the church's own words: the papal acts (the Bullarium from Pius IV to Innocent IX, Pius V's letters), the curia and the missions (Immensa; Propaganda's Inscrutabili and its Instruction of 1659), and the Index (Reusch's edition of the 1559 and 1564 texts). The new orders strand gains the Oratory's own Life of its founder. The Capuchins and Ursulines are not yet in (§3, §4). The lay devotional voice is Bellarmine plus the Roman Missal in English, and, since the second pass, Granada's Elizabethan English (§1, second-pass table).

**Non-English files and their English.** Bullarium, Collectanea, Reusch, the three Pallavicino parts: no public-domain English found for any of them. The Profession of Faith has English (the 1734 file) and, through the cross-link in §2, a Latin text with Schaff's English. The Latin dogmatic decrees of Trent are in Schaff (§2) beside Waterworth's English.

**Accuracy notes.** The Breviary file is one of four seasonal volumes and reflects later revision through Leo XIII, not a pure Pius V text. The Bullarium volume's imprint year is garbled in the OCR; the Internet Archive catalogue says 1727, the title page names Benedict XIV (pope 1740-1758), so the year is not confirmed from the page and either falls well before 1930. The Collectanea is a printed collection from the Congregation's archive, not the archive itself. Pallavicino writes after the Council and for the Curia; he is context, not the council's voice.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

**Vs. Society of Jesus (VI.11), unchanged.** Both worlds cite Waterworth; this world holds the native claim (tradition), the Jesuits the implementing claim (context). Step 0 §4 item 4 settles it.
