**What the corpus now holds, stated plainly.** The Synod of Diamper's own acts are present three ways: in English (Geddes 1694, and Hough 1839 in clean modern type), and in Latin (Raulin 1745). The Portuguese original (Gouvea, Coimbra 1606) is in the corpus since the second pass, October 2026, from the Biblioteca Nacional de Portugal's own OCR, rough and a second witness, so the three translations can be checked against it. The second pass also added one inside voice, Philipos 1869, which is the community's memory of these events two centuries on and not a text from the window, and Howard's 1864 English of the liturgies the separated party came to use. The 1653 events are told by Carmelite eyewitnesses in Italian (Sebastiani, Vincenzo Maria) and by Hough and Whitehouse. The oath's text survives, as the census says, only through tradition: the nearest the corpus comes is Whitehouse Appendix E, the Angamale document signed in 1787, which retells the oath at Mattancherry and the choice of Archdeacon Thomas in the signers' own words, a century and a half later and in translation. There is no contemporary text in the community's own voice for 1599–1665. The Syriac manuscripts and ola records the census names are not in the corpus and not reachable here.

**Witness quality.** Clean scans: Hough, Whitehouse. Readable, with the f-for-long-s pattern and broken words: Geddes. Rough: Raulin and all three Italian files. Under the OCR ruling of September 2026 the rough ones are second witnesses for exact wording until a passage is checked against the page images or a cleaner scan is found. Each header and staging note says so. For the acts, Hough's clean reprint of Geddes is the text to quote from; Raulin's Latin is the independent witness.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Society of Jesus (VI.11).** The Jesuit prelates of Malabar (Roz, Britto, Garcia) and the college at Cranganor are this world's antagonist. The Jesuit voice here is only through hostile pens (Geddes, Hough, Whitehouse) and the Carmelites, who were themselves the Jesuits' rivals. Whether the Jesuit entry owns any of the Diamper material is for the two Doc_02 threads; this dossier gives the Hough, Whitehouse and Geddes rows to VI.11 as provisional context only.
- **The Church of the East schism of 1552 (VI.30, `church-of-the-east-schism-of-1552`).** Malabar's bishops came from the Church of the East, and the census names the Chaldean connection. Only the Italian travel accounts touch that (the overland route through Baghdad). Whether the Chaldean connection belongs in this world or that one is undecided here.
- **Three Thomas Christian entries and their neighbours.** `st-thomas-christians-of-india` (849–1054), `st-thomas-christians-1300-1517`, this entry, then `northeast-india-revivals-and-the-mar-thoma-refor` and `the-malankara-churches-orthodox-and-jacobite`. Whitehouse spans all of them; the staging gives provisional rows to each. How the Whitehouse chapters divide among them is for the later builds.
- **Hough 1839 Appendix A and Geddes 1694 are the same translation.** The `works_fragment.yaml` lists them as one Expression (Hough reprints Geddes) beside Raulin's Latin. Whether the lead wants Hough tracked as a separate Manifestation is the lead's call.
- **Double-use of the Diamper acts.** The acts are tradition-role for this world and context-role provisional for the Society of Jesus and the 1300–1517 entry. The Tridentine Church (VI.22) could claim them as context too (the decrees apply Trent); not proposed.
- **Reader-page rights.** Every vendored file is whole-text public domain by date; the registry trailer says so. The Hough and Whitehouse Google scans carry "Digitized by Google" page markers left as found; whether a reader page strips them is a Library design call.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor question.** The census floorNote is "No question". The community worshipped in East Syriac under bishops from the Church of the East. That tradition's Christology is a live question elsewhere (Nestorius is named in its anaphora, and Neale's note at line 8361 calls "Mother of Christ" "the Nestorian heresy"), and the Synod of Diamper condemned it. But the entry's own subject is the community under Latin pressure, and the census rules the floor clear. Two points belong in Step 0: the Synod's charge of Nestorianism comes from the party imposing the Latin rite, and the census describes the 1653 oath as a refusal to obey the Jesuits, not as a rejection of any creed. Step 0 should state both as disclosure, not as a floor problem.

**Uniqueness and overlap.** No sibling carries this story. Neighbours on the same coast (`st-thomas-christians-of-india`, `st-thomas-christians-1300-1517`, the Mar Thoma and Malankara entries) cover other centuries. The Society of Jesus (VI.11) and the Tridentine Church (VI.22) are the pressure's source, not the community. The line from the 1653 oath to today's Kerala churches is the entry's own claim and no sibling makes it.

**Scale.** About 1.3 million words across seven files, of which roughly 480,000 are Italian and 237,000 Latin. The three English files (Geddes, Hough, Whitehouse) are about 590,000 words and carry the acts, the narrative of the revolt and two community documents. Raulin and the Italian files are second witnesses.

**Recommendation, for Mark to decide, not a decision: build.** Floor (A1) clears on the census note, with the Nestorianism charge disclosed as the Synod's accusation. Sourcing (B1) is moderate: the acts are in hand three ways and the Carmelite and Anglican narratives are rich, but the community's own voice is thin (one 1787 document, Whitehouse Appendix E, and the tradition as others report it), which Doc_02 can state as an absence rather than fill, as the census itself says the synod burned the books. Ecology (B2) is lopsided toward the conqueror's pen, which is the entry's own subject and makes a distinctive world: the community's practices survive as a list of things condemned. Uniqueness (B3) holds. Audiences (B4) and scale (B5) are plausible, with a readable English core. The gaps to carry into Step 0 are the Portuguese Gouvea, the rough Latin and Italian scans, and the Kerala manuscripts.

**Second pass, October 2026: recommendation unchanged (build).** Gouvea is now in the corpus, so the first of the three gaps named above is closed, though as a rough second witness. The community's own voice gains one text, Philipos 1869, which a build can use only as the Jacobite party's memory of 1599-1653, and Howard's English of the rite that memory kept. Nothing from inside the window in the community's own voice was found; Doc_02 should still state that as an absence.
