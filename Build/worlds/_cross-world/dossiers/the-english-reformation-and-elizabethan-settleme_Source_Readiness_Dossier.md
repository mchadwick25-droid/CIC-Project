## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Marian church, 1553–1558, between this world and Recusant English Catholicism (VI.7).** Frere and Kennedy Vol. II carries the Marian restoration's own orders (Queen Mary's articles 1554, Bonner's, Pole's 1556–57). They are inside this world's window and story, and they are also the English Catholic church's own institutional voice in the five years before the recusant window opens. The staging row to VI.7 is marked `needs-ruling`: tradition (the recusant church's immediate parent) or context (the establishment before the break). Not decided here.
- **Puritanism (VI.8) and this world share the same texts from opposite sides.** The 1559 Prayer Book, the Homilies, the 1566 Advertisements, Hooker and the Hampton Court account are this world's `tradition` and the Puritan world's `context`. Each was placed that way. When the Puritan dossier is written, its own voices (Cartwright, the Admonition, Perkins, Greenham, the diaries) should be read against these; the Zurich Letters, Bradford and Grindal (§3) will serve both worlds.
- **Foxe is one edition across three worlds.** Vol. III serves Lollardy and the Hussites; Vols. VII and VIII serve this world and (as context) the recusants. Nothing contested, but a build should know the same edition is split by volume, not by work.
- **Tyndale (1525–1536)** is placed nowhere. He precedes this world's "1530s" start and Lollardy ends c. 1520. Whether he is this world's prelude or belongs in a world of his own is for the lead.
- **Morebath's first decade (1520–1535)** is placed provisionally to `late-medieval-lay-parish-piety`, whose window closes at 1517. Substance says yes, dates say no by three years; left provisional.
- **Author slug collision:** the existing `barlow` is Thomas Barlow (1681). William Barlow is vendored as `barlow-william`. If `cic/texts/AUTHORS.md` or `AUTHOR-IDS.yaml` ever adds Wikidata keys, these are two people. `walton` (Izaak Walton) is ruled in the Hooker staging file as a metadata-omission author inside Hooker's volume.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census says "No question" and nothing found this pass disturbs that: the Articles of 1553 and 1571, the Prayer Book creeds and Jewel's Apology are plainly Nicene and Chalcedonian. The only floor-adjacent matter is internal to the Reformation's own quarrels (eucharistic doctrine, predestination in the Lambeth Articles), none of it a Nicene question.

**Sourcing (B1).** Rich, and now vendored: the liturgy ordinary England prayed (1549, 1552, 1559), the pulpit text it heard (the Homilies), the orders that reached its parishes (Frere and Kennedy), one parish's own ledger across the whole change (Morebath), the martyrs' examinations and letters (Foxe VII–VIII), and the leading voices (Cranmer, Latimer, Jewel, Hooker). Gaps that remain: no will series; Tyndale, Ridley, Bradford, the Zurich Letters and Grindal are verified leads not yet vendored; Jewel's Latin is not vendored; Morebath and Barlow are second witnesses for exact wording.

**Ecology (B2).** The ecology is distinctive: an unusually large share of corporate and institutional voice (liturgy, homilies, injunctions) beside a large martyrological voice and a single parish voice. The author-gravity risk is Foxe, whose hostile frame around the Marian material must be read as his, with the embedded examinations quoted as "as Foxe printed them". Cranmer's prose is the liturgy's own author speaking; a build should decide early whether the Representative is a parish voice (Morebath's Trychay is the one named ordinary person) or an institutional one.

**Uniqueness (B3).** Real overlap with Puritanism (VI.8) and Recusant Catholicism (VI.7), but as the thing they both stood against, not as a duplicate: this world is the Settlement itself, and its own texts are the other two worlds' context. Overlap with Lollardy and the Scottish Kirk is slight (Foxe's Lollard volume; Knox is not vendored).

**Audiences (B4).** Anglican and Episcopal participants have a direct line in; the Prayer Book's words are still in use. The Marian martyrs and Morebath give non-Anglican participants a human entry.

**Scale (B5).** Larger than any built world (about 23 MB vendored, more than two million words). A build will need to select, not read everything; the staging loci are written to make that possible.

**Recommendation: build.** This is a recommendation for Mark, not a decision. The world clears the floor without question, its sourcing is now the richest of any Era VII candidate in the corpus and its own voice (liturgy, homilies, parish, martyrs) is vendored rather than promised, and its uniqueness holds because it is the Settlement that the sibling worlds defined themselves against. The two things a build should settle first are the Representative's register (parish or institutional) and the treatment of Foxe's frame.
