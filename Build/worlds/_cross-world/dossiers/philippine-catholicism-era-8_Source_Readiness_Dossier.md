## 5. Open cross-world questions

- Murillo Velarde (1616-1716) and Francisco de San José's Tagalog book carry provisional rows into `philippine-catholicism` (VI.19); whether a later printing or a later chronicle may stand as evidence for the earlier window is the same ruling already pending on the 1760 pasyon.
- Vol. L's 'Events in Filipinas, 1764-1800' and the unvendored vol. LI run up to and past 1815, the hand-off to `philippine-catholicism-gomburza-to-aglipay` (VIII.39), whose native-clergy story starts with Archbishop Sancho's reforms here.
- The 1762-64 British occupation (vol. XLIX) is partly a military record; whether the British papers belong to the world at all, or only Rojo's and Anda's, is unnamed.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build, with an honest floor on the inside voice.** The friar-parish system, the visitation dispute (Camacho, Pardo, Sancho), the British occupation and the Jesuit expulsion are all now in English in eleven proofread Blair and Robertson volumes, with Murillo Velarde's Jesuit history in the original Spanish beside its English extract. That covers every strand the census names except the ordinary Filipino Christian's voice. Tagalog devotional print is present (the 1760 pasyon, garbled; the 1734 Quatro postrimerías, usable), but both are texts given to Filipinos rather than written by them, apart from the pasyon itself; its OCR still needs a clean scan or page-image checking before any quotation. The world is distinct from VI.19 and VIII.39 by its sources, not only by date: the volumes here do not overlap theirs.
