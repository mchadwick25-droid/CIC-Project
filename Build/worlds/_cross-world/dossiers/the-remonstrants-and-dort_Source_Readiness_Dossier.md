## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The Canons rows.** The VI.9 agent staged the 1840 Canons here as `tradition / provisional` and asked this agent to confirm. On reading, the placement is right: the Canons are the Dort half of this world and now have a second English Expression (Scott 1831) and a Latin one (Niemeyer). I recommend the lead raise the 1840 row to `assigned`. I could not edit the existing staging file. The Belgic Confession and Catechism rows stand as `context / provisional`.
- **Where the boundary with VI.9 runs.** The controversy of 1610–1619 happens inside the Dutch Further Reformation's place and window, and Voetius sat at Dort. The VI.9 dossier asks whether VI.9 treats the controversy as context seen from the precisianist pulpit and leaves its doctrine to this world. This pass agrees with that reading and has staged Dort as a tradition row here and as a provisional tradition row there. Step 0 for both worlds should say it once.
- **Arminius before the window.** He died in 1609. The census names him first among the voices, but his own works are the founder's, not the church's. The row is `needs-ruling` between `tradition` and `antecedent`. Doc_01 should say how this world reads the window.
- **The Methodist claim.** The census says Arminius is claimed by the Methodist Revival. The vendored Wesley files do not name him. The Methodist world's build should say what it takes from Arminius and from Calder's and Brandt's lives, which are Methodist-published in English (Calder's host record, Guthrie's 1857 Nashville printing).
- **The anti-Trinitarian boundary.** The census floor note says some Remonstrant thinkers drifted toward anti-Trinitarian positions later, and that this belongs to a different story. The opposing charge of Socinianism was live in the window: Calder's Chapter VII has Festus Hommius charging Episcopius with it, and Voetius names "Socinus en de Remonstranten" (line 2101 of vol. 1, per the anti-Trinitarian dossier). The Grotius defence of satisfaction against Socinus is a lead that sits across the boundary.
- **Partisan English.** Scott's footnotes take the Synod's side and Calder is written to defend Episcopius. Both are useful because they put documents into English, but neither speaks as the world. A Representative's quoted matter from either should be checked against the originals first.
- **Dutch and Latin originals without a clean witness.** The Remonstrants' Dutch and Latin originals that were reachable are second witnesses at best (§3, §4). Under the September 2026 ruling a clean original can be primary, but none of these is clean. Say this plainly in Doc_02.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor (A1).** The census says the movement held the historic creeds without dispute and its quarrel was about predestination. Nothing found disturbs that. The Confession's chapter 3 (the Holy and Sacred Trinity, line 5165) names three hypostases of one Godhead, with the Son and the Spirit "truly partakers of the same Deity or Divine Essence". It puts the stress on the Father as the fountain of the Godhead and on the Son's deity as communicated, which a Doc_04 test should read in context. The Declaration of Sentiments has a part on the divinity of the Son (p. 257 of the volume) and the Apology has a long article answering a charge of Sabellian speech (article 21). I did not read those through.

**Sourcing (B1).** The census says "Moderate", and that holds. The Dort half is well supplied in English: the Canons in two English forms, the Synod's own preface, the outside witness, and a Latin Canons. The Remonstrants' own voice was the thin half after the first pass: one 1676 English second witness, Arminius before the window, the Remonstrance of 1610 only through Calder's Chamberlayne. The second pass changed that. The party now speaks as a body in the Second Remonstrance of 1617 (Latin, with the Contra-Remonstrants' answer beside it), the banished Brotherhood speaks in its own acts and letters of 1619 to 1634 (Tideman, Dutch), its historian tells the Synod year in English (Brandt vol. 3), and Grotius is in clean English on the atonement.
