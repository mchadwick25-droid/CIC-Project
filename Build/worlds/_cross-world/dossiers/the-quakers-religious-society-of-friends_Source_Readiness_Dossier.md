What the census names and the table covers: Fox's Journal in both its texts (the manuscript text of 1911 and the Ellwood recension the Society read) with his Epistles to the meetings; Fell's Women's Speaking Justified in a 1667 printing and her Works of 1710; Woolman's Journal and Essays from the manuscripts; Ashbridge; the published journals corpus as a genre (Ellwood, Bownas, Churchman, Mary Penington, with Penn's 1677 travel journal); the Evans and Cheevers Malta narrative in its 1662 first printing; the meetings' own government (London's Book of Discipline of 1783, its Epistles 1675–1805, Philadelphia's Rules of 1797); Barclay's Apology and Penn's No Cross, No Crown as the doctrinal and devotional standards; Nayler for the first generation; and the Society's memorials of its ordinary dead (Piety Promoted).

What the census names and the table does not cover: Besse's Collection of the Sufferings (1753) and the Great Book of Sufferings; the separate women's meetings' minute books. See §3 and §4.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **Penn's 1677 journal and German Pietism (VII.2).** Placed provisional `context` there for the Frankfurt circle; whether VII.2 wants an English Quaker's view of Spener's milieu is for that world.
- **The Keithian schism and the Baptists (VII.16).** Morgan Edwards's Materials is linked here for the Keithian Friends who became Baptists; the Baptist dossier should carry the same row from its side.
- **The Anglican attacks** (Bennet 1705, lead above) belong to the Restoration and Georgian Church of England (VII.20) as much as here; recorded here so they are not re-found.
- **Puritanism (VI.8).** The census calls the Quakers a radical child of the Puritan ferment; nothing from the Puritan bucket is linked here, and the anti-Quaker writing of Baxter and Bunyan would arrive with those worlds' own vendoring. Named here, not decided.
- **Pennsylvania as a shared stage.** Acrelius is now linked from four Era VIII worlds (Brethren, Moravians, Quakers, and by its subject the Swedish Lutherans on the Delaware, who have no census entry). The Brethren dossier already raised the missing world.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The own voice is as deep as the census says and almost entirely in English of the period: Fox's Journal in two texts and his Epistles; Fell's defence of women's ministry in a contemporary printing and her collected Works; Barclay's theology and Penn's devotional and historical prose; Woolman's Journal and antislavery essays from the manuscripts; six more journals and life-accounts across the window, three of them women's (Fell, Mary Penington, Ashbridge) and one a prison narrative from the Inquisition (Evans and Cheevers); the meetings' own government in London's and Philadelphia's printed disciplines and 130 years of yearly epistles; and the Society's memorials of its ordinary dead. The outside witness is thin by design here (Acrelius, Asbury, Edwards, all in passing); the hostile Anglican literature is a lead, not vendored.

The floor question is answered from the vendored texts themselves, as the census asks: Barclay's Propositions and Penn's Brief Account affirm Christ's divinity in plain terms while refusing creedal subscription as a form, and a Doc_01 can cite both. The one gap a Doc_02 must state is the sufferings archive: Besse's two folios exist in three open copies and were held on size alone, and the Great Book of Sufferings is manuscript. The women's business meetings are documented only from the men's minutes and from Fell; no women's meeting minute book is in print.
