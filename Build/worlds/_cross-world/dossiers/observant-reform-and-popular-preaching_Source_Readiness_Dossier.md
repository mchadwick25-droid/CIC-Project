What kind of witness each is:

- **Bernardino's own voice.** The three Banchi volumes hold the forty-five Italian sermons of the Siena cycle of 1427. The OCR is clean. Banchi's introduction says a wool-shearer, Benedetto di maestro Bartolomeo, took the sermons down as Bernardino spoke. This is the near-transcript the census describes, vendored whole and ready to quote once checked against the page images. The only public-domain English is the 1920 selection by Orlandi and Robins. Its year rests on the host date alone, and which cycle it draws on was not checked against Banchi. The Italian needs an Opus rendering for a Representative; the 1920 English is a cross-check.
- **A Poor Clare's own book.** The 1536 print holds Caterina Vigri's book with the Seven Spiritual Weapons in part one. The OCR reads the long s as f, so it is a second witness for exact wording. Part two is Paleotti's life of her, which is not her voice. The 1926 English translation was not found (section 4).
- **The Observance's own chronicle.** Glassberger's chronicle in Analecta Franciscana II is a Latin year-by-year account written inside the Observance, in readable OCR.
- **Outside and institutional witnesses.** Bullarium Franciscanum, nova series I, holds the papal letters of 1431–1455, the years of Bernardino and Capistrano. Ehrle's Archiv Bd 4 has one section on how the Spirituals and the Observants related. Lea's third volume narrates how the Observantines replaced and suppressed the Fraticelli, and it holds a long treatment of Savonarola. Parsons's life of Colette is hagiography built on French lives. None of these is the Observants' voice.

- **Busch, Chronicon Windeshemense und Liber de Reformatione Monasteriorum (`busch_chronicon-windeshemense-lat_grube1886.txt`)** (bucket: the Devotio Moderna world). Tier: same time-different region. It is the shelf's one cross-order witness to the Observance: a Windesheim canon's account of reforming Saxon houses, with forms of observant- on about 109 lines. Proposed row: context / provisional.
- **Theiner, Vetera monumenta Poloniae et Lithuaniae, vol. 3 (`theiner_vetera-monumenta-poloniae-lithuaniae-v3-lat_1863.txt`).** It names the Observants on about 179 lines, but the volume covers 1585–1696. Tier: out-of-window. Not proposed.
- **Ignatius Loyola's Exercitia and the Reusch index of prohibited books** name Savonarola on 25 and 8 lines. Tier: out-of-window. Not proposed.
- **Within this batch.** Ehrle Bd 4, Lea vol. 3 and the Bullarium nova series are placed here as context and also serve the Order world and the Fraticelli world. Glassberger is placed on the Order world as well.

## 5. Open cross-world questions

- **The merge or split test against the mendicant traditions.** The census asks for it. The vendored texts are Franciscan, plus Busch on the shelf for the Augustinian and Benedictine houses. The Dominican Observance, Savonarola's side, has no primary text in the library; Lea's third volume is the only treatment. Whether the Observance is one world or a Franciscan world with an Observance chapter is for Step 0 to state.
- **The boundary with the Franciscan Order world (V.19).** The Observant wing is one of V.19's two named branches. Glassberger, the Bullarium nova series and Lea are placed on both. Step 0 for both worlds should say once where the line runs.
- **The Observants who hunted the Fraticelli (V.12).** The Bullarium nova series names Capistrano's powers against the Fraticelli, Lea has a section headed "The Observantines Replace and Suppress the Fraticelli", and Ehrle Bd 4 has a section on the Spirituals and the Observance. James of the Marches's dialogue against the Fraticelli, an Observant text, is excerpted in Döllinger's second volume (section 3, not vendored). Which world carries that quarrel is open.
- **Capistrano and Breslau, 1453.** The census says the Breslau trial records are testimony taken under coercion and are largely untranslated. Neither Capistrano's own writings nor those records were found (section 4). The vendored traces are papal grants, Glassberger's chronicle and Lea. Any treatment of Breslau would rest on secondary and institutional matter alone, and Step 0 should say so plainly.
- **Colette and the Poor Clares.** Only Parsons's life is vendored. Colette's constitutions were not found, and the French lives are listed in section 3.
- **The Spanish Observance and the converso crisis.** No Spanish text was found. The census carries the crisis as context with process honesty; the library has no source for it yet.
- **Trinci and the monti di pietà.** Trinci is named on about 13 lines of Ehrle Bd 4. No primary text on the monti was found; a Wadding volume (section 3) has an editor's note on them.
- **Rendering.** Bernardino and Vigri are in Italian and Glassberger in Latin. An Opus rendering is needed for a Representative, with the 1920 English as a cross-check only where it covers the sermon.

## 6. Step 0 scope notes and recommendation

**Floor.** The census says there is no creedal question. Nothing sampled disturbs that. The sermons were sampled at three points each and not read through. The Breslau persecution is a matter of conduct, not of the creed.

**Sourcing.** The census says "Rich: Bernardino's sermon-cycles taken down as spoken". That holds, and it holds for Bernardino alone. The movement does speak in its own voice in a vendored text: forty-five sermons in clean Italian, an Observant chronicle in Latin, and a Poor Clare's book as a second witness. The other four named voices are thin. Capistrano is present only through papal grants, the chronicle and Lea. Colette is present through a hagiography. Trinci is a name. There is a public-domain English leg for Bernardino only, and it is a selection.

**Ecology.** One great preacher with his hearers, an institutional chronicle, papal letters and a nun's formation book make a good shape for a conversation about mass lay formation. The dark side of the census, Breslau, has no record in the library, so a build cannot test it from sources.

**Uniqueness.** Against the Order world (V.19) the overlap is the Observant wing and the 1517 division; this world is the preaching and the reform, that world is the poverty quarrel and the split. Against the Fraticelli (V.12) the overlap is the suppression. Against the Devotio Moderna world the overlap is the idea of strict observance, in Windesheim's Augustinian form.

**Audiences.** The census names the Observants' heirs and the monti di pietà, one of which still trades as a bank in Siena. The audience for a sermon-based formation world is large and reachable.

**Scale.** 10 files are placed here (15.6 MB by characters), of which six are primary voices (the three Banchi volumes, the English selection, the Vigri book and Glassberger). The remaining four are context. The leads in section 3 would add little to the voice; the gaps in section 4 (Capistrano, Colette, Spain) are the ones that matter.

**Recommendation: build.** Bernardino's cycle is in the library whole, in clean Italian, with a public-domain English selection beside it (B1). The Observant chronicle and the nun's book give a second and third voice. The cost to say up front is that Capistrano, Colette, Trinci, the Spanish Observance and the Breslau record are not in the library, so a Doc_02 should run one more acquisition pass on those before the build counts the world's five named voices as secure. This is a recommendation for Mark to weigh, not a decision.
