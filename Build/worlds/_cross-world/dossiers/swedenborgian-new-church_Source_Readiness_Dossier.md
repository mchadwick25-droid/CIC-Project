## 5. Open cross-world questions

- Priestley's Letters (1791) serve this world as the outside witness and the Unitarian world as Priestley's own voice. Rows for `early-american-unitarians-universalists` and `english-old-dissent` are in the spec as provisional; where does English Unitarianism sit?
- The New Church grew partly from Methodist and Anglican readers (Clowes stayed in the Church of England; several Methodist preachers went over). Should the separatist and non-separatist wings both be in this world, and should Wesley's remarks cross-link from the Methodist world?
- Swedenborg's own corpus is pre-1772 and Swedish-Latin; the church is English and American, 1787 onward. Is the founder's corpus assigned here as `tradition` (as the spec does) or as `antecedent`? The census treats it as the world's primary source, so the spec follows that.
- The Boston New Jerusalem Magazine and the later General Convention liturgies belong to the nineteenth century; no later Swedenborgian world exists in the census to receive them.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The founder's corpus is complete in public-domain Latin and in the English the first readers used, and the church's own voice is present inside the window: the first Conference minutes, the liturgy with its catechism and hymns, a congregation's confession, two ministers' sermons and the controversy with Priestley from both sides. The floor question can be argued from the church's own documents, as the census asks: the liturgy confesses the Lord "in his Divine Humanity", and the Birmingham confession states how far its members held Swedenborg's writings to be authoritative. The weak point is scan quality for the London institutional record after 1789, and the absence of Hindmarsh's later memoir, so the build should name that gap. The world is distinct in the census: no sibling world carries a revelation-based, non-creedal one-person doctrine of God of this kind, and the Priestley controversy gives it a sharp, documented boundary with the Unitarian world.
