## 5. Open cross-world questions

- The Unitarian drift: Priestley's American years are assigned provisionally to early-american-unitarians-universalists. Where English Unitarianism after 1774 (Lindsey's Essex Street) belongs is not settled.
- Baxter's Part I (Civil War, Kidderminster) is assigned provisionally to puritanism. The Savoy Conference papers are assigned provisionally to restoration-georgian-church-of-england.
- Watts's hymns travelled into the evangelical revivals. The spec has a provisional transmission row for the Methodist Revival and for New England.
- The census region is "England, Wales". The Welsh Independents (Edmund Jones) and Welsh Old Dissent could belong here or with the Welsh revival world.
- The Gee and Hardy statutes serve Old Dissent, the Baptists, the Quakers and the Restoration church. It would be better as one portfolio vendoring than as one world's text.

## 6. Recommendation (portfolio decision for the project lead; does not block library work)

**Build.** The open corpus gives this world its own voice at each point the census names. Baxter's narrative of 1662 is in full, and the household of an ejected minister appears in both Philip Henry's diaries and his son's Life. Watts's hymns and psalms are in lifetime editions, and Doddridge's devotional classic appears in its first year. Salters' Hall is covered from both sides, and Priestley's own memoir traces the Unitarian drift. The census flags no floor question for the tradition. Its note about the drift is handled by vendoring the drift's own witness rather than leaving it out. Everything is in English. What sets the world apart from its siblings is the academies, the hymn and the subscription crisis, none of which the Baptist or Quaker worlds carry. The thinnest area is the congregational and academy record itself. Heywood's diaries, Doddridge's correspondence and Calamy's lists of the ejected are the natural next additions when the allowance allows.
