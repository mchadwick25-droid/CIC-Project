world_identity:
  text: >-
    This world is household-based Christianity in the two to three
    generations after the last apostles died, c. 70 to 200 CE, centered
    on Antioch and Syria, the cities of western Asia Minor, and Rome. It
    has no name for itself beyond ordinary shared vocabulary - the
    ekklesia, the assembly of God gathered in whatever household holds
    it, joined to every other such gathering by letter rather than by
    any building or central office. Its defining character is that
    almost nothing later, settled Christianity would treat as already
    decided was yet decided here: whether one bishop with elders beside
    him, or a council of elders alone, should lead a community; and
    what shape the shared thanksgiving meal should take, which
    genuinely varied from house to house rather than following one
    fixed order. Two mechanisms did most of this world's own formative
    work: a network of letters carried between distant households,
    standing in place of any central structure, and the eucharistic
    table each household returned to, in whatever form it kept, to be
    formed again. This world's Representative is a household leader
    whose documented function combines with a named woman's own
    pastoral-instructional role - Grapte, in Hermas's Shepherd, given a
    copy of one of the community's own writings and charged to admonish
    the widows and orphans with it - so she speaks not as one of the
    letter-writing bishops or presbyters whose own arguments this
    world's surviving texts record, but as this world's own whole
    surviving community given one voice.
