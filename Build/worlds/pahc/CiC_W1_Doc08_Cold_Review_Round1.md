**Judgment call worth testing (self-disclosed by the document, correctly):** Forces 3A-1 (Irenaeus/Tertullian) and 3A-2 (Alexandria) are classified "External" despite being generated by fellow Christian communities, not the Roman state. The document's own defense — "external to World #1's own three core regions" (Force 3A-1 Layer 1) — is a geographic rather than sociological reading of "External." This is defensible on internal-consistency grounds: Cell 2A-2 already treats Marcionite/Valentinian/Montanist movements (also Christian, also not the Roman state) as "External," so 3A-1/3A-2 is not introducing a new category logic, just extending an existing one from "rival, contemporaneous Christian movements" to "adjacent, successor Christian formation logics." But it is a genuine stretch relative to the template's own governing question for Cell 3A ("What external forces brought this world's distinct form to an end") if "external" is read as "outside Christianity" rather than "outside this world's own network" — Irenaeus and Tertullian are not rivals this world is drawing a boundary against (as 2A-2's movements are); they are the wider tradition's own successor mode, arguably closer in kind to an "Internal" development of Christianity generally than to a state-level "External" force. The document is right to flag this as a category judgment a reviewer should test rather than assert as settled — my own read is that it is defensible but not obviously correct, and a specialist reviewer could reasonably argue these two forces belong in 3B (Internal) instead, reframed as "a wider Christian tradition's own internal resolution of this world's still-open questions, arriving from outside this world's own three-region network."

## 3. Companion workbook — two real, checkable inconsistencies

Per this project's own established indexing discipline, I checked whether the workbook's index views can be trusted at face value without rereading the prose. Two cannot.

**(a) Section 7's Confidence Assessment silently omits two forces present in Section 3.**

Force 1B-1 (Apostolic Testimony Inheritance) carries an explicit confidence tag in Section 3 ("*Confidence: Widely Accepted that this commitment was present and inherited...*") and is cited by name in Section 5 ("1B-1 (Apostolic Testimony Inheritance): the inherited commitment that makes authority's grounding..."). It does not appear anywhere in Section 7 — not in the Documented/Widely Accepted paragraph, not anywhere else. I searched all four Section 7 confidence-tier paragraphs directly; Force 1B-1 is absent from all of them.

Force 2A-2 (Contemporary Rival Movements) carries an explicit split confidence tag in Section 3 ("*Confidence: Documented that these movements existed and were geographically overlapping; Contested on precise founding/break dates for each.*") and is cited by name in Section 4 (Connection 3) and Section 5 (as G05's direct external condition). It, too, is entirely absent from Section 7.

This means Section 9's own completion-checklist claim — "Confidence calibration present throughout Section 3 and summarized in Section 7" — is not fully accurate as currently written. Two of fourteen forces have Section 3 confidence tags that are never carried into the Section 7 summary.

**(b) The companion workbook's "By Confidence Level" sheet has the identical gap for Force 2A-2**, confirming this is a real, propagated omission and not just a prose oversight. I enumerated every Force ID appearing anywhere in the sheet's 17 rows: 1A-1, 1A-2, 1B-1, 1B-2, 2A-1, 2B-1, 2B-2, 2B-3, 2B-4, 3A-1, 3A-2, 3B-1, 3B-2 — thirteen of fourteen. **Force 2A-2 does not appear on this sheet at all.** (Force 1B-1 does appear on the workbook sheet, so the workbook is actually one force more complete than the prose Section 7 — meaning the prose and the workbook do not even fail in the same way, which is itself worth noting as a sign these two artifacts were not cross-checked against each other at the finish line.)

**(c) The Force Index sheet's own "Connected Gravities" column drifts from the workbook's own "By Connected Gravity" sheet and from Section 5's prose**, in two specific, checkable places:

- Force **1A-2**'s row in the Force Index sheet lists Connected Gravities as `G01` only. But Section 5's own G02 entry states explicitly: *"1A-2 (Neronian Persecution/Apostolic Deaths): the eyewitness-loss crisis this force initiates is what makes a correspondence-based (rather than direct-witness-based) unity mechanism necessary at all"* — a direct G02 connection. The workbook's own "By Connected Gravity" sheet gets this right (its G02 row lists "1A-1, 1A-2, 2B-2"). The Force Index sheet and the By Connected Gravity sheet, within the same workbook, disagree with each other about what 1A-2 connects to.
- Force **2B-3**'s row in the Force Index sheet lists Connected Gravities as `G04, G01` — omitting G03, even though the workbook's own "By Connected Gravity" sheet lists 2B-3 under G03's row explicitly ("2B-3 (via Connection 2)"), and Section 5 itself states G03's connection to G04/2B-3 is "Connection 2... the single most explicitly evidenced connection in this entire matrix."

A builder or reviewer who trusts the Force Index sheet alone — exactly the use case this project's own indexing discipline exists to support, per the `cic-forces-index` skill's own instruction that relationships should be "fast to verify against an index" rather than requiring a full reread — would get an incomplete picture of what 1A-2 and 2B-3 actually connect to. The "By Connected Gravity" sheet is correct and complete; the "Force Index" sheet's own per-force summary column is not fully consistent with it.

**What is not a problem:** the Force Index sheet's fourteen rows match the prose's fourteen forces exactly (no drift in force count or naming); the "By Connected Gravity" sheet correctly shows all six confirmed gravities with connected forces and correctly excludes G06 with a stated reason; the "Cross-Cell Connection Map" sheet's six rows match Section 4's six named connections exactly, including direction/type and the Connection 5 new-synthesis disclosure flag; the "Transmission Check" sheet correctly shows both 2B-2 and 3B-2 as their own dedicated rows with named mechanisms.

## 4. The document's own disclosed new-synthesis claims

**(a) Connection 5** — checked directly against Doc_04 and found accurate, as described above. This is the strongest-handled of the three disclosures: it is flagged inline, immediately, at the point of assertion, in Section 4, in Section 5 (under G05's connected-forces bullet), and in the Document Log — consistently and repeatedly, not just once at the end.

**(b) Force 3A-1/3A-2 "External" classification** — also flagged inline (at each force's own Layer 1 discussion) as well as in the Document Log. Assessed above (Section 2 of this review) as a genuinely defensible-but-contestable call, correctly surfaced for a reviewer to test rather than asserted as settled.

**(c) The Section 6 "downstream record of which... internal contests the wider tradition judged to have 'won'" claim — inconsistently disclosed.** This is the one place where the document's own disclosure discipline slips. The claim itself, in Section 6's "What the Transmission Pattern Reveals About Forces" paragraph, reads:

> "Communities and later copyists preserve what they judge worth the labor and risk of preserving... Transmission, read this way, is itself a downstream record of which of this world's own forces the wider Christian tradition eventually judged to have 'won.'"

Nothing in this paragraph, or anywhere else in Section 6, marks this sentence as the document's own interpretive extension rather than an established finding drawn from Doc_02. Compare this to how Connection 5 is handled: in Section 4 it is flagged in the connection's own header line ("this document's own new synthesis, not an inherited Doc_04 finding") and again inline in Section 5's G05 entry ("this document's own new cross-force synthesis (Connection 5), disclosed as an interpretive extension"). The Section 6 claim receives no comparable inline treatment — the only place it is actually identified as synthesis is the Document Log at the very end of the document ("the Section 6 claim that later transmission's institutional/apologetic selection interest is itself a downstream record of which of this world's own internal contests the wider tradition judged to have 'won' — this document's own synthesis, not a claim independently sourced to a named scholar"). A reader who stops at Section 6 — a perfectly normal way to read a synthesis section — would take this as an established conclusion on the same footing as the rest of the section's Doc_02-sourced content. This should be fixed by adding an inline disclosure directly in Section 6, matching the standard the document itself sets and meets elsewhere.
