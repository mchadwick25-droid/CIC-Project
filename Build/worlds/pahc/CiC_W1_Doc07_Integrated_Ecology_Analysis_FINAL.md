## Section 2 — Integration Lenses

### 2A — Emotional Ecology [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** This world's own Tier 1 lexicon (episkopos, presbyteros, ekklesia, eucharistia — Doc_06 Section 2) supplies no dedicated affective vocabulary; its emotional texture must be reconstructed from institutional and ritual terms rather than a native emotion-lexicon. Applied carefully, this lens shows that the same underlying external exposure (Doc_04's G03) was metabolized into two different internal dangers depending on which strand's own authority logic processed it — a pattern invisible in Doc_05's own presentation, which states both facts without naming them as two divergent responses to one shared force.

**Dominant emotional register:** Not constant terror but standing, unpredictable exposure: "near enough that a member of these communities organized some real part of their emotional life around it" (Doc_05 §1.2, grounded in G03/Supporting/cross-strand). For those formed most directly under Ignatius's own example (Strand A), this exposure could turn toward eager anticipation rather than dread — "a longing to be consumed rather than spared, framed as the truest possible imitation of the one this community followed" (Doc_05 §1.2, grounded in G04/Supporting/Strand-A-only; Ignatius, *Romans* 4, "food for wild beasts"). Doc_04 explicitly restricts this register's confidence to "these two specific people" (Ignatius and the community that produced the *Martyrdom of Polycarp*) — its Formation-test result reads "**Clearly** formation-shaping for the specific individuals in the record; far less certain for the ordinary, non-elite, non-imminently-threatened member" (Doc_04, G04). For Rome (Strand B), the sharper fear ran elsewhere: "not toward the possibility of arrest, but toward the possibility that one's own worst failure, committed after baptism, might already have used up whatever mercy remained" (Doc_05 §1.2, drawing on Hermas). Cutting across both strands: hope ran through correspondence — "a letter arriving from another *ekklesia* was never merely information. It was proof that the *ekklesia* a member belonged to was larger than the room" (Doc_05 §1.2, grounded in G02/Primary).

**Emotionally dangerous states:** For Strand A, doubt about the reality of Christ's bodily suffering — docetic denial — is the named danger, addressed through Ignatius's sustained anti-docetic argument (G05/Tensional/Strand-A-only) and reinforced ritually: adherence to "one eucharist" under one bishop functions as an embodied answer to that doubt (Doc_05 §4.1; Doc_04's G07 Dependency-test finding that the "one eucharist" instruction is "simultaneously a G01 and G05 move"). For Strand B, the named danger is despair that repentance no longer avails after a real post-baptismal failure — addressed through Hermas's own visionary instruction, which "dwelt at length" on exactly this question (Doc_05 §1.1).

**Emotional arc of formation:** Entry through Two Ways catechesis, framed as a stark moral choice between "a way of life" and "a way of death" (Doc_03 §2.7; Didache 1–6) → baptism (Didache 7's descending sequence of acceptable fallbacks, Doc_03 §2.11) → ongoing re-formation through the recurring eucharistic gathering, "the occasion a member... returned to, again and again, to be re-formed into the body they belonged to" (Doc_05 §1.1) → for a minority, direct testing through state exposure. No settled terminal state is evidenced anywhere in this world's own record; Doc_01's own closing-boundary discussion (§2.2) marks this world's *end* by the appearance of a formation logic that has settled (Irenaeus, Tertullian, Alexandria's teaching institution) — this world's own emotional arc, by definition, never itself resolves into settledness within its own lifetime.

**Gravities most visible in the emotional texture:** G03 (State Pressure, Supporting) as the shared undercurrent; G04 (Martyrdom, Supporting, Strand A only) as its most intensified individual expression; G02 (Translocal Network, Primary) as the hope-channel; G07 (Liturgical Practice, Primary) as the recurring re-forming rite.

**What Emotional Ecology reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** Doc_05 documents both strands' emotional content side by side without naming the relationship between them. Held together, they show that one shared external condition — real but intermittent legal exposure (G03) — did not produce one uniform emotional formation across this world. It produced two structurally parallel but substantively different economies of fear and hope, each shaped by which strand's own authority and boundary logic metabolized the same underlying pressure: Antioch/Asia Minor toward a fear of the body's unreality (answered by embodied martyrdom-readiness and bishop-presided eucharist), Rome toward a fear of mercy's exhaustion (answered by ongoing penitential self-examination). The exposure is shared; the danger it becomes is strand-specific.

### 2B — Philosophical Ecology [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Beneath the two strand-specific fears named in 2A lies one shared ontological commitment: nothing about a formed Christian life in this world — the reality of the body, the availability of forgiveness, the legitimacy of office — is treated as automatically secured by initiation or apostolic inheritance. Naming that commitment explains why each strand's specific danger is dangerous at all.

**Fundamental ontological assumptions:** Reality in this world is organized around the presence and fading of direct apostolic testimony rather than a closed textual canon — "scripture read alongside living apostolic testimony, not yet a closed canon" is named as one of this world's own Distinct World Criteria (Doc_01 §1). For Strand A specifically, the reality of the body is treated as ontologically load-bearing rather than incidental: Ignatius's sustained insistence that Christ "suffered truly and in the flesh, not merely in appearance" (Doc_05 §6.2) makes bodily suffering, not merely belief about it, the site where truth and falsehood are decided.

**Understanding of the human person:** The Two Ways schema frames the person as standing at a live, continuing moral fork — "a way of life" versus "a way of death" (Doc_03 §2.7) — not a nature settled once by initiation but an ongoing choice rehearsed in catechesis before baptism (Doc_05 §2.1). For Rome specifically, Hermas extends this provisionality past baptism itself: the person remains capable of a real moral failure serious enough to reopen the fundamental question of whether mercy remains available at all (Doc_05 §§1.1, 1.2) — personhood here is morally provisional, not secured once for all by the initiatory rite.

**Temporal assumptions:** This world understands itself as living inside the closing of the apostolic generation — "the dispersal of first-generation eyewitness and apostolic authority... created an urgent, lived problem" (Doc_01 §7) — not primarily an imminent-parousia urgency but an urgency of transmission before the last direct witnesses are gone. Continuity with the apostles is "not a settled inheritance quietly received; it was something actively, urgently claimed, precisely because the alternative... was close enough to be felt" (Doc_05 §6.3).

**How philosophical ecology grounds the emotional ecology:** Strand A's docetic anxiety (2A) is coherent only because bodily reality is ontologically load-bearing; if embodiment were not a site where truth was decided, denying the body's real suffering would threaten nothing. Rome's penitential anxiety (2A) is coherent only because the person remains morally provisional after baptism; if the initiatory rite secured a person's standing once for all, post-baptismal failure would threaten nothing either. Both strand-specific fears are downstream expressions of one shared conviction: nothing is automatically secured by inheritance; it must be actively held.

**Gravities most visible in the philosophical structure:** G05 (Strand A's ontological commitment to bodily reality); G01 (the still-argued nature of office mirrors the still-argued nature of personhood — "office was argued for, not assumed," Doc_05 §5.2); the household/*oikos* evidentiary gap underlying G06's own negative finding (Doc_03 §3; Doc_04, G06).

**What Philosophical Ecology reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** This world's institutional unsettledness (G01) and its emotional unsettledness (2A) are not two separate facts about this world but the same underlying philosophical commitment expressed twice — once institutionally, once affectively. This is a world that treats nothing about a formed Christian life as automatically secured by initiation or inheritance; everything (office, embodiment, forgiveness) has to be actively held, argued, or re-earned.

### 2C — Authority Structures [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** How the "actively argued, not automatically secured" logic named in 2B plays out institutionally — and specifically, how the same generative force produced two mutually exclusive but structurally parallel resolutions rather than one settled answer.

**Primary sources of authority:** Fading apostolic memory and testimony (Doc_01 §7); demonstrated, correspondence-recognized standing — Rome's intervention at Corinth is itself an exercise and public demonstration of Rome's own perceived authority to correct another *ekklesia* (Doc_02 §1.2); a singular office actively constructed and legitimated through argument, mapped by Ignatius onto pagan civic and mystery-cult models (Doc_01 §6, citing Brent's reading) rather than simply reported as already secured; and, in Strand B, a plural, interchangeable governing college whose legitimacy rests on collegial continuity rather than any single figure (1 Clement chs. 42, 44, Doc_02 §1.2; Hermas's plural "presbyters who preside over the Church," Doc_02 §1.5).

**Transmission of authority:** In Strand A, the single office is figured as fitted-together continuity — Ignatius's own image, newly surfaced as primary-text engagement at Doc_05's own step: "your justly renowned presbytery... is fitted as exactly to the bishop as the strings are to the harp" (Doc_05 §5.1, *Ephesians* 4; not yet a Source Registry row, flagged for escalation per Doc_05 §5.1 and Doc_06 §8, item 2). In Strand B, authority is transmitted as shared, interchangeable college membership, with "no felt lack created by the absence of any single presiding figure" (Doc_05 §5.1).

**Sites of contestation:** The clearest embodied contestation in this world's entire evidentiary base is a single person: Polycarp identifies himself only as one of "the presbyters" in his own letter, while Ignatius separately addresses him as Smyrna's bishop (Doc_01 §6; Doc_03 §2.2) — the same person carrying both descriptions simultaneously, held rather than resolved anywhere in this world's own record. Doc_04's own Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check on G01 found no defensible way to elevate this beyond Supporting: "the specific content that actually does this gravity's interaction and dependency work... is exactly the Contested part" (Doc_04, G01).

**Authority and formation:** In Strand A, obedience to a single bishop is "experienced as the very shape of unity" (Doc_05 §5.1) and is directly fused with liturgical formation: "attending that bishop's own eucharist and refusing any other was inseparably an act of worship and an act of belonging" (Doc_05 §4.1) — authority-formation and worship-formation are the same act, not two. In Strand B, a council of presbyters is "experienced as the ordinary, sufficient shape of oversight" (Doc_05 §5.1), with no comparable fusion evidenced.

**Cross-strand authority variations:** Per Doc_04's own finding: the *force* — that authority required active organizing at all — is cross-strand confirmed; its *resolution* is strand-bound (Doc_04, G01, Cross-strand status). Doc_03 identifies episkopos/presbyteros as "the single clearest strand-differentiating word in the whole source set" (Doc_03 §2.1).

**Gravities most illuminated by the authority structure analysis:** G01 (Supporting — this lens's direct subject); G07 (Primary — its Strand A fusion with liturgical formation).

**What Authority Structures reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show:** Doc_05 documents each strand's authority experience separately. Read together against Doc_01's own forces framing, both strand-specific resolutions turn out to answer the identical question — how should continuity with the apostles be secured once eyewitnesses are gone — with structurally parallel but substantively opposite claims to unbroken continuity: Strand A claims continuity through a single office fitted together like tuned strings; Strand B claims continuity through "an argument that ministry itself had passed down in an unbroken, if loosely conceived, line" (Doc_05 §2.2). This world's authority crisis was never actually about *whether* continuity mattered — both strands agree it does — but about *what form* continuity should take once direct witness is gone.

### 2D — Boundary Structures [REQUIRED]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** How this world's own still-unsettled internal authority question (2C) interacts with real external pressure to produce a boundary that is simultaneously about internal legitimacy and external danger — and a genuinely new integration finding not present in Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix, disclosed explicitly as this document's own synthesis below.

**Membership logic:** Carrying this world's name was "at once, a legal exposure and a claim about the truth, held together as a single thing rather than two" (Doc_05 §6.1) — membership was never purely a private or internal matter. Baptism (Didache 7's descending water-fallback sequence, Doc_03 §2.11) is this world's clearest internal entry rite, preceded by Two Ways catechesis (Doc_05 §2.1) — though both rest on the Didache alone within this world's Native evidentiary base and cannot be generalized network-wide with confidence (Doc_03 §§2.7, 2.11, Author Gravity risk: "Yes, maximally").

**Relation to what is outside:** Two distinct "outsides" mattered, experienced as different in kind rather than one undifferentiated threat. The Roman state's outside was legal-administrative — *hetaeria*, *pertinacia* (Doc_03 §§2.12–2.13) — improvised and intermittent rather than settled or systematic (Doc_01 §4; Trajan's rescript explicitly forbidding seeking Christians out). The rival-movement outside was doctrinal and, crucially, *not* external or already-settled either: Marcion, Valentinian/Gnostic teaching, and Montanism were "live, undefeated, contemporary neighbors," geographically overlapping this world's own three core regions in the same decades (Doc_01 §8.3) — not later heresies safely outside this world's own frame.

**Forces most visible in the boundary structures:** G03 (State Pressure, Supporting, cross-strand) — Doc_02's own "what silences reveal" finding reads this world's complete absence of institutional self-documentation as plausibly explained by the practical risk of durable, seizable records under unpredictable legal exposure, not merely accidental non-survival (Doc_02 §9). G05 (Boundary-Drawing vs. Rivals, Tensional, Strand A only) — Ignatius's anti-docetic argument. Doc_05 explicitly separates these into two boundaries, "not felt with equal intensity everywhere" — doctrinal anxiety sharpest nearest the rival teaching (Strand A); the state's boundary pressing on both regions, "intermittently and unpredictably rather than as a constant siege" (Doc_05 §3.1).

**Boundary contestation:** The doctrinal boundary is explicitly *not* settled within this world's own lifetime — Doc_04 classifies G05 Tensional specifically because its evidence is thin (essentially one voice) and its own Formation-test result reads only "Plausible... unestablished," never reaching the "Clearly" language G04 reaches even at its narrowest scope (Doc_04, G05 Cross-Check). The state's boundary is contested in a different sense: whether "the name itself" (*nomen ipsum*) or associated crimes constituted the actual chargeable offense remained genuinely unresolved even to Pliny, its own enforcer (Doc_03 §2.13).

**Boundary logic and formation:** In Strand A, refusing a rival's separate eucharist and holding to "the one eucharist" under the community's own bishop "were... the same act — belonging and boundary-marking were not experienced as two different decisions" (Doc_05 §3.1). In both strands, "learning where the edges of a safe public identity lay" was itself formative, "pressed home by a governor's own letter describing exactly what evidence he required and exactly what a simple denial would spare a person" (Doc_05 §3.1; Pliny 10.96).

**What Boundary Structures reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show, flagged explicitly as this document's own synthesis rather than an inherited Doc_04 finding:** Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix, after seven review rounds of pair-by-pair re-derivation, records **no demonstrated relationship** between G03 (State Pressure) and G05 (Boundary-Drawing) — the two gravities are evidenced by entirely different sources (Pliny; Ignatius) and Doc_04 never traces a direct textual connection between them. This document's own reading, going beyond what Doc_04 itself established, is that for a Strand A member specifically, these two independently-evidenced pressures converge on a single formative lesson at the level of lived experience even without a documented textual link between the gravities themselves: maintaining any boundary at all — against a rival teacher's eucharist, or against a magistrate's demand to curse Christ — was rehearsed as one continuous kind of refusal, learned first in the low-stakes doctrinal case before it might ever be tested in the high-stakes state case. This is Doc_07's own integrative claim, not a finding carried forward from Doc_04, and is named as such in Section 6 (Gaps and Limits) as a specific candidate for external scholarly review.

### 2E — Formation Logic [REQUIRED — applied after 2A–2D]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** The organizing principle beneath all four preceding lenses, and the reason Doc_04's own classification pattern — only two Primary gravities, several Supporting, one Tensional — is itself evidence for this world's coherence rather than evidence of a weakly-integrated ecology.

**The fundamental aim of formation:** Not to arrive at settled answers, but to become someone capable of holding real, community-defining, unresolved questions — who leads; whether the body's suffering was real; whether mercy still avails after failure; where the edge of a safe public identity lies — as the active substance of belonging, rather than as scandal or deficiency awaiting resolution. A mature practitioner in this world could write, or receive and act on, a letter arguing urgently for a still-contested position while simultaneously recognizing the community on the argument's other side — differently organized, differently answering its own live question — as still "the church of God" (1 Clement's own opening address, Doc_02 §1.2), not as a rival or an error to correct.

**The formation mechanism:** Two mechanisms did nearly all of this world's formative work, and they are precisely this world's only two Primary gravities. The correspondence network (G02) transmitted not settled doctrine from a stable center but arguments shaped by specific local trouble: "a member of one *ekklesia* rarely encountered the wider movement's teaching directly — they encountered it as a specific writer had already shaped it, in response to a specific, local argument" (Doc_05 §2.1). The eucharist (G07) did the parallel work ritually — repeated, re-forming, and, in Strand A specifically, simultaneously an authority-claim and a boundary-claim in the same act (Doc_04, G07 Dependency test; Doc_05 §4.1). Two Ways catechesis supplied the entry-point moral logic (Doc_02 §4), but this world's ongoing, adult formation ran through letters and liturgy — this world explicitly lacked any formal teaching institution of the kind that would define World #2's own formation logic just past this world's own close (Doc_01 §§2.3, 5; §8.2).

**The formation arc:** Entry via Two Ways catechesis and baptism → incorporation into a local *ekklesia*'s own already-argued answer to the authority question → ongoing re-formation through repeated eucharistic gathering and reception of translocal correspondence → for a minority, direct testing through state exposure, met in Strand A with an intensified, martyrdom-oriented formation logic modeled on one bishop's own self-presentation. No settled terminal stage is evidenced anywhere in this world's own record. Doc_01's own closing-boundary discussion marks this world's *end*, specifically, by the appearance of a formation logic that has settled (Irenaeus's succession argument, Tertullian's confident apologetic corpus, Alexandria's teaching institution, Doc_01 §2.2) — arguing was not a stage on the way to settling; for this world's own lifetime, arguing was the whole shape of formation.

**How the lenses integrate as a formation system:** The emotional register (2A) is the felt experience of living inside unresolved questions, in two strand-specific flavors. The philosophical ecology (2B) is the ontological conviction that makes that felt exposure coherent — nothing is automatically secured; everything must be actively held. The authority structure (2C) is the institutional expression of the same conviction — office argued into being rather than inherited as given. The boundary structure (2D) extends the identical logic outward — a boundary maintained by active, repeated refusal rather than a settled wall inherited from a previous generation. All four lenses, independently derived from Doc_01–06's own evidence, converge on one formation logic: this world formed people who could actively hold, argue for, and re-enact — rather than passively inherit — the very things a later, more settled Christian world would treat as already decided.

**Cross-strand formation logic:** The shared underlying logic — argue rather than inherit — runs through both strands even though its specific content diverges sharply. Strand A argues its way toward a single office and treats the doctrinal/bodily boundary as the sharper front; Strand B argues its way toward a plural college and treats post-baptismal mercy as the sharper front. Neither strand's argued, not-yet-settled character is itself in question; only the content each strand is arguing toward differs.

**Formation logic and confirmed gravities:** G02 (Primary) is the delivery mechanism through which every other gravity's argued, not-inherited character actually travels between communities. G07 (Primary) is the ritual site where the argued authority claim (G01) and the argued boundary claim (G05) become one embodied act, at least in Strand A. G01 (Supporting) is the clearest single institutional expression of "argued, not assumed." G03 (Supporting) supplies the external condition that makes active holding urgent rather than a scholastic exercise. G04 (Supporting, Strand A only) is the most extreme individual case of choosing rather than passively receiving one's own relationship to the community's central conviction. G05 (Tensional) is the least evidentially secured of all seven candidates — and for exactly that reason the clearest demonstration that even this world's boundary was actively defended, not inherited already-drawn.

**A distinction carried inline here, not only in Section 4, so it travels with this claim wherever it is cited or extracted:** "argued, not assumed" is not uniformly evidenced across G01, G03, G04, and G05. G01 and G05 fell short of Primary because the underlying question was genuinely, historically contested in antiquity — Doc_04's own Cross-Check language treats the coarse claim as Widely Accepted while only the specific resolution is Contested. G03 and G04 fell short for a different reason: modern reconstruction's own limited ability to responsibly generalize from scattered surviving episodes into a network-wide claim, not demonstrated proof that this world's own inhabitants argued over state pressure's severity or martyrdom's meaning the way they argued over office. Both are real instances of this world declining to settle a question, but they are not the same kind of unresolvedness, and this document's "argued, not inherited" formation logic should be read as most firmly established for G01 and G05, and more provisionally extended to G03 and G04.

**What Formation Logic reveals that no individual document showed:** Doc_04 classified only two Primary gravities and explicitly declined to elevate this world's authority resolution, martyrdom-meaning, or boundary-drawing beyond Supporting or Tensional — read in isolation, that classification pattern could suggest an ecology of unresolved fragments rather than a coherent formation system. This lens shows the opposite: the very fact that so much of this world's evidence resists confident, Primary-level classification is not a gap in the formation logic — it *is* the formation logic, directly and specifically. A world whose defining character is "argued, not assumed" will, when tested against Doc_04's own six-test discipline, produce exactly this pattern: two Primary gravities that are pure delivery mechanisms (network, ritual), and several genuinely Supporting or Tensional gravities that are the *contents* being delivered and argued over. The classification pattern is evidence for the coherence this document names, not evidence against it.

### 2F — Memory Structures [OPTIONAL — applied]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** This world visibly holds two structurally different modes of memory that map directly onto its strand division, adding a temporal dimension to the authority and boundary picture already built in 2C–2D.

**What this world remembered and how:** Strand A holds named-individual memory — a letter-writer's own voice, carried forward and read aloud after his death; the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* already, within this world's own lifetime, marking his *dies natalis* (Doc_01 §9; Doc_05 §2.2). Strand B holds corporate-continuity memory — "an argument that ministry itself had passed down in an unbroken, if loosely conceived, line," and a body of visionary instruction generating its own authority through revelation rather than a named chain of witnesses (Doc_05 §2.2).

**The formation function of communal memory:** The *Martyrdom of Polycarp*'s *dies natalis* commemoration (ch. 18, "more precious than the finest jewels") begins organizing time itself around a specific death-as-birth event (Doc_05 §4.1) — though Doc_04's own G04 entry treats this as something this world was only beginning to hand off to its successors, not something its own liturgical practice (G07) had yet fully organized around (Doc_04, G04 Dependency test).

**Gravities most illuminated by the memory analysis:** G04 (Strand A, named-individual memory); G01's own Strand B "unbroken line" argument.

**What Memory Structures reveals:** From a third independent angle, memory structures confirm the same strand-shaped fault line 2A (emotional register) and 2C (authority) already showed by different routes: Strand A externalizes continuity into named persons and their commemorated deaths; Strand B internalizes it into an argued, corporate, unbroken succession. Three lenses, independently derived, converge on one recurring division.

### 2G — Interpretive Ecology [OPTIONAL — applied]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** A question distinct from *who* held authority (2C): *how* legitimate interpretation itself worked. This world held at least three genuinely different interpretive postures side by side, with none displacing the others within its own lifetime (Doc_05 §6.1).

**The hermeneutical assumptions of this world:** Fixed-text interpretation (Two Ways/Didache, community instruction handed down as a stable teaching text); claimed-continuity interpretation (an individual leader's own claimed continuity with apostolic teaching, per Ignatius); and revelatory interpretation (instruction received through vision, per Hermas) — three postures existing side by side, "none of these subordinated to the others within this world's own lifetime" (Doc_05 §6.1).

**How interpretation shaped formation:** Which of these three postures a practitioner was formed to trust depended on their own community, and this unsettledness is inseparable from why a closed canon had not yet emerged: "scripture read alongside living apostolic testimony, not yet a closed canon" (Doc_01 §1).

**Gravities most illuminated:** G01 (differing legitimation logics for differing interpretive postures); G02 (each posture travels the network differently — text circulates, claimed-continuity is argued in letters, vision is recorded and read).

**What Interpretive Ecology reveals:** This world's failure to settle on one interpretive posture is not incidental to its authority question (2C) — it is the same unsettledness expressed at the level of *how* truth is known rather than *who* holds office. A world that has not settled who leads has, unsurprisingly, also not settled how legitimate teaching is recognized; the two open questions reinforce rather than operate independently of each other.

### 2H — Representative Theological Patterns [OPTIONAL — applied, thin evidence disclosed]

**What this lens reveals for this world:** Two theological commitments in this world are directly formation-shaping rather than merely doctrinal content, connecting the philosophical (2B) and emotional (2A) lenses to concrete claims each strand was prepared to argue for — though the underlying evidence is thin (two data points per strand) and this lens is applied with that thinness disclosed rather than smoothed over.

**The theological patterns most formation-shaping:** Strand A's insistence that Christ "suffered truly and in the flesh, not merely in appearance" (G05); Strand B's wrestling over "whether mercy remained available at all after a real failure committed once the water of baptism had already been received" (Hermas, Doc_05 §6.2).

**How doctrinal commitments shaped practice and self-understanding:** Strand A's commitment is inseparable from its eucharistic and boundary practice (2D) — refusing a rival's eucharist enacts, ritually, the same conviction argued doctrinally. Strand B's commitment shaped an ongoing practice of self-examination that Doc_05 names as a formative rhythm without developing its specific ritual detail beyond noting its presence (Doc_05 §1.1).

**What Representative Theological Patterns reveals:** "Neither community had settled the question so much as answered its own live pressure as that pressure was actually felt" (Doc_05 §6.2) — this world's theological content is not free-standing doctrine but a direct verbal register of each strand's own lived, still-open struggle. This is the same conclusion 2B reached through ontology and 2A reached through emotion, arrived at a third time through theological content specifically.

## Section 4 — Cross-Lens Synthesis

**The central coherence:** This world formed people to hold what had not yet been settled — who leads, whether the body's suffering was real, whether mercy still avails after failure, where a safe public identity ends — as the active substance of belonging to one *ekklesia*, transmitted and re-enacted through letters and a shared eucharist rather than resolved into a single inherited answer.

**What this world's distinctiveness consists of:** Compared to its two nearest neighbors, not to modern Christianity. World #2 (Alexandria) formed people through a teaching-relationship, exegetical-philosophical mode already assuming a stable teacher-student institution (Doc_01 §8.2). This world's own successor moment — Irenaeus's explicit apostolic-succession argument, Tertullian's confident apologetic corpus, c. 180–197 — argues *from* apostolic succession as an already-secured structure rather than one still being built (Doc_01 §2.2). A practitioner formed in this world had something a practitioner in either neighbor lacked: direct, personal stakes in an authority question that was not yet closed — the lived experience of writing, receiving, or arguing about which office model was legitimate, rather than inheriting the answer as settled fact. This world lacked something both neighbors had: a stable, reproducible teaching institution, or a systematic, treatise-length doctrinal apparatus. Its own formation ran entirely through occasional correspondence and repeated ritual, never through anything resembling a school.

**The most significant cross-lens insight:** Doc_04's own classification of only two Primary gravities (G02, G07) is not evidence of a weak or under-integrated ecology; it is the precise, formal signature of a world whose defining coherence is "argued, not assumed" — a claim first reached in 2E from the ecology lenses alone, and confirmed independently here from two directions 2E does not itself draw on. First, Section 3 (Forces) shows the pattern is not merely a classification artifact but a direct consequence of this world's own external situatedness: state pressure and the eyewitness-generation-loss force never became severe or centralized enough, within this world's own window, to force one strand's answer to displace the other's — the argued character of G01/G05's content is a condition this world's own forces made possible, not only a fact about how Doc_04 happened to score its evidence. Second, the optional lenses (2F, 2G) each independently rediscover the identical strand-shaped fault line 2A and 2C already showed — named-versus-corporate memory (2F), and fixed-text-versus-claimed-continuity-versus-revelatory interpretation (2G) — by routes that do not depend on Doc_04's classification apparatus at all. Three lenses converging on one fault line by three independent evidentiary routes is a stronger form of confirmation than the classification pattern alone.

**A disclosed limit on this claim's own evidentiary footing, not fully separated out in 2E:** the "classification pattern as coherence-signature" argument rests on firmer ground for G01 and G05 than for G03 and G04. G01's and G05's Supporting/Tensional status reflects Doc_04's own finding that the underlying question was **historically, actually contested in antiquity** — Doc_04's G01 Cross-Check states the coarse claim (authority required active organizing) is Widely Accepted while only the specific resolution is Contested, and G05 rests on Ignatius's own live argument against contemporary rivals. G03's and G04's Supporting status, by contrast, reflects a different kind of limit: **modern reconstruction's own inability to responsibly generalize** from scattered surviving episodes (Nero, Pliny, Ignatius's arrest for G03; two named individuals for G04) into a network-wide claim — a confidence and evidentiary-generalization limit, not demonstrated proof that this world's own inhabitants treated the severity of state pressure or the meaning of martyrdom as a live, argued question the way they treated ecclesial office. Doc_04 itself flags this distinction for G04 directly: "current classification reflects an absence of evidence, not a confirmed absence of the phenomenon in Rome" (Doc_04, Open Item 1). This document's own synthesis is accordingly better supported for G01/G05 than for G03/G04, and a reader should weight the central coherence claim above accordingly — the classification pattern is real and load-bearing, but not uniformly load-bearing across all four Supporting/Tensional gravities.

**What the cross-lens synthesis gives the Representative:** A Representative built from this integration can authentically hold — rather than resolve, minimize, or apologize for — a live disagreement about office, embodiment, or mercy, recognizing another community's differently-organized answer as still fully "the church of God" rather than as an error to correct or a rival to convert. A Representative built only from Doc_05 and Doc_06 without this synthesis might read this world's own institutional variety as a documentation gap or an unfinished construction rather than as this world's own defining and authentic formation logic. The synthesis licenses a Representative to inhabit unsettledness as formation, not as its absence.

## Section 5 — Integrative Observation

This world's members were not people waiting for their own *ekklesia*'s live questions to be settled before real belonging could begin — the argument itself, carried by letter from one *ekklesia* to another and re-enacted at one eucharist after another, was how they belonged. A member who wrote urging obedience to a single bishop and a member who addressed a plural *presbyterion* as sufficient in itself were both, in their own eyes and in each other's letters, holding the same table and answering to the same name — a name that was, in the same breath, a legal exposure and a claim about the truth. To have settled the question — which office, whether the flesh truly suffered, whether mercy still reached a real failure — would not have completed this world's formation; it would have ended it, and did, in fact, mark this world's own closing boundary when it finally began to happen. What must be carried, more than any specific answer this world reached, is that its people were formed to treat their own unresolved questions as the very shape of their belonging to one another, not as a wound in this ekklesia's own life.

**A note on evidentiary weight, carried forward from Section 2E and Section 4 so it travels with this observation wherever it is extracted or quoted rather than left behind in them:** this observation's central claim — that unresolved questions were themselves this world's formation, not a deficiency in it — rests on firmer ground for some of those questions than others. Which office to obey and whether the body's suffering was real were genuinely, actively contested within this world's own lifetime, in its own texts, by its own voices. Whether state pressure amounted to one standing condition, and how representative any one martyrdom is of the wider community's own experience, are unsettled for a different reason: modern reconstruction's own limited ability to generalize from scattered surviving evidence, not a demonstrated ancient debate over those questions' own terms. Both kinds of unresolvedness are real and both are named above; they are not the same kind, and this observation should not be read as claiming they are.
