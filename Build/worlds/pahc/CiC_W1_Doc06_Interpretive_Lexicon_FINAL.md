## 0. Purpose and Scope

Per the LDF's own Foundational Principles: "A glossary defines words. A lexicon reconstructs how a world's participants understood, categorized, and navigated reality through their own vocabulary." This document is the construction-record layer of that reconstruction — the full development of all thirteen candidate terms Doc_03 identified, now at the depth Step 6 requires because Doc_04's gravity testing and Doc_05's ecological reconstruction exist to inform that depth, per the LDF's own sequencing rationale ("a term's true ecological weight becomes clearest after the formation ecology is established").

This document does not itself contain each term's full entry text — per LDF Part VI, the full entry content lives in this world's **Deployment Lexicon chunks** (one self-contained file per term, thirteen files, filed alongside this document), so that the runtime retrieval system can surface any entry independently. This document is the construction-record companion: it states the methodology, resolves every open item Doc_03 carried forward to this step, documents the tier and tag decisions and why each was made, and provides the cross-reference and confidence-calibration audit that Part IV's Development Workflow requires. A companion index workbook (Section 7 below) makes the full set queryable without opening every chunk file, per this project's own indexing discipline.

**World-code decision, made explicitly here since Doc_01 did not assign one:** the LDF (Part VI) requires a short, lowercase, two-to-six-letter world identifier for chunk file naming, normally assigned during World Identification (Step 1). Doc_01 did not assign one. This document assigns **`pahc`** (Post-Apostolic House-Church) now, as the first point in the pipeline where the omission becomes load-bearing. This is a naming decision, not a substantive historical claim, and is disclosed as this document's own choice rather than treated as an established fact carried from Doc_01 — a reviewer who prefers a different code should say so, since nothing upstream depends on this specific string. All thirteen chunk files use this code: `pahclex001` through `pahclex013`.

## 1. Resolving Doc_03's carried-forward open items

Doc_03 carried **seven** open items to Doc_04/05/06 (Section 4 of that document). Four (items 1–4) bear directly on this step and are resolved here; two (items 5 and 7, about re-checking Author Gravity flags and Registry Flag Density against whatever Doc_04 developed) are addressed within each term's own entry below rather than as a single global resolution, since they are per-term questions. **Item 6 — "Doc_05 should develop Justin's *First Apology* ch. 61 (baptismal rite) and Ignatius's baptismal allusions directly from the primary texts" — was never carried out anywhere in the pipeline for six documents' worth of build history, then closed at Step 9's Validation & Testing pass (2026-07-08).** A dedicated primary-text extraction pass located and quoted Justin's *First Apology* 61 and 65–66 in full (a genuine, independent, comparably detailed second procedural account, Strand B/Rome) and searched all seven authentic Ignatian letters for baptismal content (yielding one substantive datum, *Smyrnaeans* 8:2's episcopal gatekeeping, Strand A/Antioch-Asia Minor, and correctly excluding several near-misses that were not actually about community baptismal practice). Doc_05 §4.2 (new), this term's chunk file (pahclex011), and Doc_09's Story 009 have all been updated accordingly. The improvement is real but modest, stated honestly rather than oversold: the general practice (water, triadic naming, pre-baptismal fasting, baptism-before-eucharist) is now independently corroborated in Strand B — Justin's account matches the Didache's basic shape without being identical in wording. Strand A (Ignatius) independently confirms only that baptism existed and required episcopal sanction, not the practice's specific shape — his evidence is institutional, not procedural, and should not be read as corroborating water method, formula, or sequence. Full account in `pahc_Validation_Testing.md` (Worship Integration Testing) and Doc_05's Document Log.

**Open Item 1 — should episkopos (2.1) and presbyteros (2.2) be one combined Tier 1 entry or two separate ones?** Doc_03 left this for Doc_04 to decide "based on how the terms actually function in gravity testing." Doc_04 generated and tested this question as a single gravity from the outset — **G01 Authority Consolidation ("the episkopos/presbyteros/diakonos question")** — rather than as separate candidates for each term; Doc_04's own text does not itself argue for or against splitting the terms into separate lexicon entries, so no finding is cited from it here beyond the classification itself. This document's own judgment, made fresh at this step, is that the terms should nonetheless be built as **two separate chunk files** (the LDF's chunk architecture is strictly one file per canonical term, and *episkopos* and *presbyteros* remain two distinct Greek words with two distinct — if entangled — semantic ranges) **but with each entry's World Meaning and Ecological Function sections built to make their shared dependency explicit rather than treating them as independently intelligible.** Concretely: *episkopos*'s entry carries the fuller Strand A/B institutional argument; *presbyteros*'s entry cross-references it rather than re-arguing it, and instead develops what is distinctive to *presbyteros* specifically — its Strand B interchangeability with *episkopos*, and Polycarp's own self-designation as "one of the presbyters" even while being addressed elsewhere as a bishop (Doc_03 2.2, Doc_01's own reading of this as counter-evidence against a fully-secured monarchical program). Both entries are Tier 1. Both list the other under Related Terms. This resolves Open Item 1 as this document's own editorial decision, consistent with (but not dictated by) Doc_04's classification and with the LDF's one-file-per-canonical-term architecture.

**Open Item 2 — is eucharistia (2.6) Tier 1 or Tier 2?** Doc_03 flagged this explicitly as a borderline case for Doc_04/Doc_05 to settle. It is now settled by both: Doc_04 classified **G07 (Liturgical Practice) as Primary.** Doc_04 names **two** Primary gravities, G02 and G07, presented as equals, and explicitly reserves the "strongest confidence footing of any candidate here" description for **G02**, not G07. G07's own stated confidence level is **Widely Accepted** that the underlying practices/texts exist and differ from one another (Contested only on whether any one is representative or network-wide) — not Documented. This does not change eucharistia's actual tier: G07 being genuinely Primary independently clears Tier 1's bar on its own, without needing to be the strongest Primary gravity or rest on Documented confidence. Doc_05 also stated directly that worship "did the heaviest lifting of any single practice in this world." A term anchoring one of this world's two cross-strand Primary gravities, treated by the world's own ecological reconstruction as its single most formative recurring practice, meets Tier 1's own bar ("terms that organize substantial portions of the ecology, typically corresponding to Primary or Supporting Historical Gravities") on these grounds. **Eucharistia is Tier 1.**

**Open Item 3 — the "house church"/*oikos* question.** Doc_05 performed the direct primary-text check Doc_03 deferred (Doc_05 Section 10, Open Item 2): Ignatius uses household-salutation language in two separate letters (*Smyrnaeans* 13:1–2; Letter to Polycarp 8) but never an explicit "the church that meets in this house" formula. Doc_05 recommended this be logged as a new Source Registry consideration before being treated as established, rather than entered as a lexicon term on its own. **This document does not add "house church" or *oikos* as a fourteenth candidate term** — the evidentiary base remains "household as recognizable social unit for salutation purposes," not "a native term for a meeting-place," and manufacturing a lexicon entry from the former to answer a question posed about the latter would overstate what Doc_05 actually found. This closes Open Item 3 without adding a new term; the Registry-escalation recommendation itself remains live and is not this document's to resolve (Doc_02 Registry ownership, per Doc_05's own disclosure).

**Open Item 4 — do the outside/etic terms (*ministrae*, *hetaeria*, *pertinacia*) and the single-community terms belong in this world's own Interpretive Lexicon at all, or should the etic ones move entirely to Doc_08's Forces Document?** Resolved here using the LDF's own Three Sources Principle, which was not yet in scope when Doc_03 raised this: a term belongs in the lexicon if "a representative situated within that world might naturally use it in conversation, and a modern reader would benefit from understanding how the term functions within that world's ecology" — and explicitly includes, as one of three equally legitimate sources, "ordinary human words whose meaning shifts significantly when heard through the world's own ecology." *Ministrae*, *hetaeria*, and *pertinacia* are exactly this: not this world's own self-description, but words a participant is likely to ask about ("were Christians tried as an illegal club?"), where the gap between modern and world hearing is real and specific. **All three remain in the lexicon, tiered per Doc_03's own proposal (Tier 2 for *ministrae*, Tier 3 for *hetaeria*/*pertinacia*), with each entry's Ecological Function section stating its direct relevance to Doc_08's Forces Document explicitly** rather than choosing between the lexicon and the Forces Document — this project's own practice elsewhere (Doc_05's own Forces Integration Summary cross-referencing gravities into ecology lenses) treats a term's relevance to more than one downstream document as normal, not as grounds for exclusion from either. Part VII's actual subject is how the *same term* should be treated when it recurs *across different formation worlds*, not a single term's relevance to multiple downstream documents *within* one world. The practical conclusion above stands on this document's own reasoning and Doc_05's precedent alone.

## 4. Confidence calibration audit (Part IV, Development Workflow step 6)

Every entry's World Meaning and Key Sources sections were checked against the constitutional five-level vocabulary (Documented / Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction / Contested / Inferential-Thin) rather than assumed settled because the term is well-attested as existing. The clearest calibration risk in this term set is exactly the one the LDF's own Tier Assignment Discipline names: a poorly-evidenced term that is ecologically central must stay at its true tier with the weakness disclosed, not be quietly downgraded to avoid disclosing it. Two terms in this list test that discipline directly:

- **episkopos** is Tier 1 (ecologically central — it anchors G01) despite Doc_04's own finding that its Strand A content is an Author Gravity risk resting overwhelmingly on Ignatius alone. This document keeps it at Tier 1 and states that dependency directly in its Key Sources section, rather than moving it to Tier 2 to make the disclosure smaller.
- **The Two Ways** and **prophetes** are both Tier 2 despite maximal Author Gravity risk (both rest on the Didache alone within this world's Native evidentiary base) — correctly *not* Tier 1, since neither organizes a Primary or Supporting gravity the way episkopos/presbyteros/ekklesia/eucharistia do; single-source dependency and ecological centrality are separate questions, and this document does not conflate them.

## 4a. Living Tradition and Scholarly Accountability (LDF Part VIII, Constitution Articles 28/29)

This document's own governance line invokes "LDF V2.1, Parts I–IX in full," which includes Part VIII. LDF Part VIII states: "where a formation world corresponds to a tradition with present-day adherents, lexicon entries for terms still in active use by that living tradition should distinguish explicitly between the term's reconstructed historical function within the bounded formation world and any claim about the term's current usage or meaning within the living tradition today. A lexicon entry is a historical-reconstruction artifact and should not be read, or written, as a claim about how a living community currently understands its own vocabulary."

Seven of this document's thirteen terms have direct, still-active correlates in present-day Christian vocabulary: **episkopos** (bishop), **presbyteros** (presbyter/elder), **diakonos** (deacon), **ekklesia** (church), **eucharistia** (eucharist/communion), **baptisma** (baptism), and the **"agape" label**, since "love-feast"/"agape meal" is an actively practiced present-day term in several living traditions (the Moravian Lovefeast; love-feast observances in Methodist and some Baptist/Anabaptist communities). Each of these seven chunk files now carries an explicit **Living Tradition note** at the end of its Distortion Risk section, stating plainly that the entry describes this world's own historical formation ecology (c.70–200 CE) and makes no claim about how any present-day tradition currently defines, practices, or understands the term. The remaining six terms (*presbyterion*, The Two Ways, *prophetes*, *ministrae*, *hetaeria*, *pertinacia*) are historically bounded institutional mechanics without a direct present-day terminological correlate, or (in *presbyterion*'s case) sufficiently narrow and Ignatius-specific that no separate disclosure was judged necessary — flagged again here as the more defensible of the two omissions a cold review raised, since *presbyterion*'s institutional function is narrower than a live office-title in a way "agape" was not. A reviewer who judges *presbyterion* otherwise should say so, since this remaining list is still this document's own judgment call, not a mechanically derived one.
