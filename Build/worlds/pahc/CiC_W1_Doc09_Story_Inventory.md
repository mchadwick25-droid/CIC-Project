## Section 1 — The Four-Tier System, As Governed by This World

Restated here for self-containedness, per Framework V7.3 Part II:

- **Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative.** Direct textual attestation within or close to the world's horizon; named author with identifiable social location; datable with reasonable confidence. Confidence: Documented to Widely Accepted at the narrative level.
- **Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material.** Stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory; individual attribution may be uncertain even where the tradition itself is authentic. Confidence: Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction; specific attributions Contested.
- **Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition.** Material attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition or hagiographic convention rather than direct documentation. Confidence: Contested for the general portrait; Inferential-Thin for specific details.
- **Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** A composite reconstruction of typical practice, not a story about one named individual or event; every element must be traceable to attested evidence within the world's own horizon. Confidence: always Inferential-Thin, regardless of source quality.
- **No Tier 5.** Generated or illustrative narrative — anything not traceable to this world's own evidence — does not exist for this world. Silence in thin areas is the correct response.

**A note on this world's own Tier 2 absence.** This world's evidence base produces no genuine Tier 2 candidate. Tier 2 requires "stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory" — a sayings-collection or anecdotal-tradition genre distinct from both direct correspondence (Tier 1) and hagiographic attribution (Tier 3). No such collected-narrative genre survives in this world's own Native evidentiary base: the Didache is a catechetical/church-order manual, not a narrative collection; no apophthegmata-style sayings collection exists for this period and these regions. This is stated here explicitly rather than left as a silent gap a reviewer might mistake for an oversight — see Section 4 (Absent Stories), Item 6.

**Story 008 — The Martyrdom of Polycarp**
*File:* `pahcstory008_martyrdom-of-polycarp.md`

- **Source:** *Martyrdom of Polycarp* (Registry P16, added during this document's own construction — see Document Log). Traditional dating c. 155–156 CE; Eusebius's *Chronicon* dates it 167 CE; concluding chapters (20–22) widely regarded as later redactional additions.
- **Confidence:** Contested (dating) / Inferential-Thin (specific details shaped by hagiographic and martyrological convention).
- **Strand:** A only.
- **Connected gravities:** G04 (Supporting, Strand A only — one of exactly two data points this gravity rests on).
- **Tier justification, in brief:** This is the clearest Tier 3 case in this world's evidentiary base. It is attributed to a specific figure and moment (Polycarp's arrest and death), transmitted by the receiving community (framed as a letter from the church at Smyrna to the church at Philomelium), and shaped by recognizable hagiographic and martyrological convention — the idealized courage, the theatrical arrest, the *dies natalis* framing of death as birth, the bone-collection scene (ch. 18) explicitly echoing relic-veneration language. The text's own concluding chapters are independently regarded as later additions, meaning even its own ending is not a clean single-layer witness. This is not treated as a defect that disqualifies the story — per the Framework's own discipline, "the account of what a formed life looks like is itself genuine formation ecology evidence," even where the specific details cannot be treated as historical reporting.
- **Usage guidance, in brief:** May be offered as the tradition's own account of what a formed life under martyrdom looks like — not as verified historical fact about the specific events. The formation ideal (courage, deliberate imitation of the founder, the community's own act of honoring him) is the evidence; the narrative's specific staged details are not.

**Story 009 — The Two Ways: A Catechumen's Path to the Water**
*File:* `pahcstory009_two-ways-catechumen.md`

- **Sources:** Didache chs. 1–7 (Registry P01) — every element traceable; see the chunk's own Source Identification section.
- **Confidence:** Inferential-Thin (Tier 4, always), though the underlying textual facts are Documented; the schema's specific *procedural* detail (water-type fallback sequence, threefold pouring) is not claimed beyond the Didache's own single (likely Syrian) community. The basic shape this story depicts — catechesis before baptism, water invoked under a triadic divine naming, pre-baptismal fasting, baptism as the gate to the community's shared meal — is now independently corroborated by Justin Martyr's *First Apology* 61, 65–66 (Rome/Strand B) and, more thinly, by Ignatius's *Smyrnaeans* 8:2 (Antioch-Asia Minor/Strand A, confirming the practice existed and was bishop-gatekept, though contributing no procedural detail). The *specific* sequence this story narrates remains Didache-only.
- **Strand:** Neither — single-community (Didache), distinct from both Strand A and Strand B. The general practice this story exemplifies is now known to have cross-strand analogues (see Confidence above); this specific composite remains a Didache-only account.
- **Connected gravities:** Feeds the philosophical/entry-point moral logic Doc_07 §2B names; not itself a Doc_04 gravity.
- **Tier justification, in brief:** No single named catechumen is attested — this is a composite reconstruction of typical practice (teaching the Two Ways, then baptism in running water with fallbacks, then fasting) built entirely from the Didache's own instructional text.
- **Usage guidance, in brief:** Must be told explicitly as reconstruction from the outset. Its specific procedural detail must not be extended beyond the Didache's own single-community evidentiary base — this cannot be offered as "exactly how baptism worked" across this world's network. It may now be paired with the broader, honestly-calibrated claim that the basic practice (water, triadic naming, pre-baptismal fasting, baptism-before-eucharist) is attested more widely, provided the two levels of claim — this story's specific sequence versus the general practice — are not blurred together.

## Section 4 — Absent Stories

This is not a placeholder. Every item below is a gap this build has already identified explicitly across Doc_01–08, tested rather than merely noticed, and is carried forward here rather than invented around.

**1. No Strand B (Rome) martyrdom account exists, and this was actively tested, not merely unnoticed.** Doc_05 §10 Open Item 6 explicitly checked whether Doc_05's own ecological reconstruction could develop new Strand B evidence for G04/G05 and "came up empty" — both gravities remain correctly Strand-A-only. Doc_08 (Section 5, "Where Forces Analysis Surfaced Gaps"; Section 9, "Outstanding items") carries this forward as still open: whether Rome's absence of martyrdom material reflects a genuine difference in lived experience, or an accident of which texts survived (transmission selection effects, per Doc_08 Force 2B-2/3B-2, make the latter a live possibility, not merely a hedge). This inventory cannot resolve it and does not manufacture a Strand B counterpart to Story 008 to appear symmetrical.

**2. G04 and G05 are both Strand-A-only, and both are severely thin even within Strand A.** G04 rests on exactly two data points (Story 001's martyrdom references within Ignatius's own letters, plus Story 008). G05 rests on exactly one voice, Ignatius alone. No third story can be added to thicken either without exceeding what Doc_04 itself found the evidence supports — the thinness is the honest finding, not a gap in this inventory's own research.

**3. This world's most narratively vivid material is overwhelmingly Strand A, and this inventory does not smooth that imbalance.** Compare the catalog above: Strand A supplies Stories 001, 008, 011 (Ignatius's journey, his martyrdom-adjacent successor's death, his eucharistic program) — vivid, high-stakes, individually attributed. Strand B supplies Stories 002, 006, 007 (1 Clement's institutional correction, Justin's liturgical report, Hermas's visions) — genuinely different in kind, institutional and visionary rather than narratively dramatic. Doc_07 §2A already names this: "This world's own Tier 1 lexicon supplies no dedicated affective vocabulary," and the emotionally vivid material clusters in Strand A specifically. Story 012 is this inventory's own attempt to hold both strands in explicit parallel rather than let the imbalance stand unaddressed, but it cannot manufacture narrative vividness Strand B's own evidence does not supply.

**4. No enslaved-member narrative exists in their own voice, anywhere in this world's evidentiary base.** The two *ministrae* Pliny tortured (Story 004) are the closest this world comes, and they remain unnamed, voiceless, and known only through their torturer's report (Doc_02 §7: "no surviving voice of their own"). Household codes address enslaved members but are "authored by free leaders instructing enslaved people toward obedience, never contesting owners' control over their bodies" (Doc_02 §7). Story 004's own Usage Guidance requires this be named plainly whenever the story is used, not left implicit.

**5. No ordinary/non-elite member's story exists at all, in either strand.** Doc_02 §§7–8 (Affirmative Duty): Keith Hopkins's estimate that only roughly 2% of members were fluent enough to have produced texts like these means no non-elite authorial voice survives from this world at all. Every story in this catalog is authored by, or filtered through, a literate leadership-tier voice or a hostile outside observer. Doc_05 §6.3 states this most sharply in inhabited voice: an enslaved member, or "a woman named only as somebody's wife rather than as a widow or a patron in her own right... had no comparable way to make their own voice carry."

**6. No genuine Tier 2 (Collected and Traditional Material) story exists for this world.** As stated in Section 1: this world's evidence base produces no sayings-collection or anecdotal-tradition genre distinct from direct correspondence and hagiographic attribution. This absence is itself worth naming as a structural feature of this world's own evidentiary base, not assumed away by stretching a Tier 1 or Tier 3 item into a Tier 2 shape it does not have.

**7. No household-based ("house church") story can be grounded in this world's own primary voices.** G06 (Household as Basic Unit) explicitly did not reach gravity status — the classic household-code texts (Colossians, Ephesians, 1 Peter) are not among this world's six primary voices and have no Registry row at all (Doc_04, G06). Doc_03 §3 independently confirmed the same gap: no primary voice uses *oikos* as a technical self-designation for a meeting unit. Story 012's household setting is a necessary narrative frame drawn from the primary voices' own domestic references, not a claim that "household as basic unit" is itself an attested gravity.

**8. Two further citations surfaced during Doc_05's own construction, and used in Story 012, still lack their own Source Registry rows** — the "lyre/harp" image at Ignatius, *Ephesians* 4, and the named household salutations at *Smyrnaeans* 13:1–2 and *Letter to Polycarp* 8 ("Tavia/Gavia," "the wife of Epitropus"). Both were flagged for Registry escalation at Doc_05 (§10 Open Item 2) and Doc_06 (§1 Open Item 3) and were not resolved before this document's own construction. Unlike the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* gap (Section 2, Story 008, and Document Log), these two are not fixed in this pass: *Ephesians* 4 and the *Smyrnaeans* 13/Polycarp 8 salutations both fall under the existing Registry row P03 ("Ignatius's Letters, middle recension, 7 letters"), which already covers all seven authentic letters as one Registry entry — these are specific passages within an already-registered text, not unregistered sources in their own right, and do not require new rows the way the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* (a wholly separate, unregistered text) did. This is stated explicitly here so a future builder does not mistake this for an unresolved citation-discipline gap of the same kind.

**9. The named household salutations ("Tavia/Gavia," "the wife of Epitropus") are the only place in this world's entire evidentiary base where an ordinary, non-leadership individual is named at all — and even there, nothing survives beyond a name and a greeting.** No narrative content exists about either person. This inventory does not construct one. Doc_05 §10 Open Item 2 found this "falls short... of an explicit 'the church that meets in this house' formula" — it establishes only that these were named individuals worth greeting, not a story.

**10. No material-culture or archaeological story is possible.** Doc_02 §5 and Doc_07 §2I: this world's window is "essentially archaeologically invisible" across all three regions. No institutional-record story is possible.** No membership rolls, council minutes, or administrative documents survive (Doc_02 §3); every institutional story in this catalog (002, 011, 012) is reconstructed from occasional, persuasive, or polemical correspondence, never from routine self-documentation.

**12. ## Section 5 — Confidence Assessment

**Tier 1 stories (001–007):** confidence ranges from Widely Accepted (the underlying textual/circumstantial facts) to Contested (specific dating or representativeness questions), consistent with the Framework's own Tier 1 cross-walk. Story 001 carries the heaviest disclosed authenticity dispute in this set (the three-way Ignatius dating/authorship split); Story 004 carries the heaviest content-care flag (torture-derived testimony); Story 007 carries an explicit genre disclosure distinguishing textual attestation from a claim about supernatural events.
