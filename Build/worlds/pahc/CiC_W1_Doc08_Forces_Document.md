## Section 2 — Preliminary Forces Identification

**What generated this world:** The dispersal of first-generation eyewitness and apostolic authority — through martyrdom under Nero, natural death, and geographic scattering — created an urgent, lived problem: how a translocal movement maintains unity, doctrine, and legitimate authority once its founding eyewitnesses are gone (Doc_01 §7).

**What larger world it was embedded in:** The Roman imperial Mediterranean — its road and sea infrastructure, its Greek linguistic koine, its voluntary-association (*collegia*) social forms, and its intermittent, locally improvised (not yet systematic) suspicion of Christian assembly (Doc_01 §7).

**What it was responding to at its origin:** The felt need to formalize authority (the presbyter-to-bishop question), to consolidate a shared textual and doctrinal memory before living apostolic testimony disappeared entirely, and to maintain communal cohesion across real geographic distance through a correspondence network rather than centralized administration (Doc_01 §7).

**What it was refusing:** Not yet a settled orthodoxy refusing settled heresy. Marcionite, Valentinian/Gnostic, and Montanist movements were live, contemporary, geographically overlapping rivals during this same window, not later or external threats. Ignatius's own anti-docetic polemic, already present by his conventional date of c. 107–115, shows a boundary being actively drawn in real time, not one inherited as already settled (Doc_01 §§7, 8.3).

**How this preliminary identification governed Step 2 scope:** Per Doc_01 §7, this orientation directed Doc_02's own research pass toward manuscript transmission histories (to test how the eyewitness-loss problem shaped what survived), primary liturgical evidence (to test how correspondence-borne unity was ritually enacted), and source asymmetry (to test whose voices the eyewitness-loss anxiety was actually recorded by). It specifically directed Doc_02 to treat institutional silence as potentially meaningful rather than merely accidental (Doc_02 §3's finding that no institutional records survive) and to depict Marcion, Valentinian Christianity, and Montanism as live, undefeated, contemporary neighbors rather than settled heresies (Doc_01 §8.3, developed in full at Doc_02 §9).

## Section 3 — The Six-Cell Matrix

### CELL 1A — INITIATING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external conditions made this particular form of Christianity possible, necessary, or urgent at its origin?

---

#### Force 1A-1: The Roman Imperial Mediterranean World

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** A single road and sea infrastructure, a shared Greek linguistic koine, and an established repertoire of voluntary-association (*collegia*) social forms connected Antioch/Syria, Asia Minor, and Rome into one navigable, mutually intelligible network by the late 1st century. Ignatius's own guarded journey from Antioch through Asia Minor to Rome (conventionally c. 107–115 CE) is the clearest single piece of evidence that this infrastructure could carry sustained contact across the whole network; Koine Greek is the uniform literary and administrative medium across every text in this world's evidentiary base (Doc_01 §3, §4). *[Confidence: Well-established/Documented for the infrastructure and linguistic facts themselves.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** A letter written in Antioch could be read, understood, and answered in Smyrna or in Rome without translation, and a traveler carrying it could expect the roads and the harbors to be there. The world beyond any one *ekklesia*'s own room was not a foreign country to be reached with difficulty; it was one connected reach, near enough that a letter from a sister *ekklesia* felt like proof of belonging to something larger than the room itself, not like news from somewhere else entirely (Doc_05 §1.2).

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This is the material precondition for G02 (Translocal Correspondence Network, Primary) — without a single connected, Greek-legible Mediterranean world, the correspondence-based unity this world's entire formation logic depends on could not have existed as a real, functioning practice rather than an aspiration. It also supplied the very roads Ignatius himself was marched along under guard, meaning the same infrastructure that enabled ordinary correspondence also carried this world's most intensive individual formation case (G04, Martyrdom).

---

#### Force 1A-2: The Neronian Persecution and the Deaths of Peter and Paul (c. 64–68 CE)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Tacitus's *Annals* 15.44 describes Nero scapegoating Christians for the Great Fire of Rome (64 CE) with brutal executions; Raymond Brown's standard periodization (*The Churches the Apostles Left Behind*, 1984) keys the "sub-apostolic age" beginning c. 67 CE to the deaths of Peter, Paul, and James under Nero. 1 Clement corroborates this by writing of the apostles' deaths as already past (Doc_01 §2.1). *[Confidence: the Tacitus passage's basic authenticity is Widely Accepted; a discrete, fire-linked, named-group persecution of Christians specifically is Contested (the Shaw/Jones debate, Doc_02 §6); the Petrine/Pauline martyrdom tradition underlying Brown's periodization is Dominant Modern Reconstruction.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** The people who had walked with the founder himself were dying, and no one could simply point to someone who had been there anymore. What replaced their direct witness was argument — a letter insisting its own writer stood in unbroken continuity with what had been handed down, a bishop insisting his own office carried that same weight forward. This was not a settled inheritance quietly received; it was something actively, urgently claimed, precisely because the alternative — the last living link simply running out — was close enough to be felt (Doc_05 §6.3).

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This is the single most direct trigger for this world's own defining formation logic. It produced the urgency behind G01 (Authority Consolidation) directly — Doc_01 §7 names the dispersal of eyewitness authority as what "created an urgent, lived problem" of maintaining unity and legitimate authority — and it is the generative condition Doc_07 identifies as what the entire "argued, not inherited" formation logic (Doc_07 §2E) is ultimately a response to.

### CELL 1B — INITIATING / INTERNAL

**Governing question:** What internal commitments, convictions, or inherited traditions gave this world its earliest distinctive shape from within?

---

#### Force 1B-1: The Apostolic Testimony Inheritance

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** This world inherited, rather than invented, the conviction that legitimate teaching and practice must trace to apostolic testimony. Scripture was read alongside living apostolic testimony rather than against a closed canon (Doc_01 §1, naming this as one of this world's own Distinct World Criteria); this inheritance predates this world's own 70 CE floor and was received from the apostolic generation directly. *[Confidence: Widely Accepted that this commitment was present and inherited; the precise mechanism of transmission from the apostolic generation itself is less securely documented than the commitment's presence in this world's own texts.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** What had been handed down by the apostles themselves was the measure against which everything else was tested — a visiting teacher's own words had to be weighed against what the community already held, and a letter's authority rested on how faithfully it carried forward what had been received. This was not treated as one tradition among several to be freely adapted; it was the inherited ground a community stood on, even while the community itself argued over exactly what standing on it required.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This inheritance is what makes eyewitness-generation loss (Force 1A-2) a crisis at all rather than a neutral fact — if apostolic testimony were not already the measure of legitimacy, its fading would not have generated the urgency behind G01. It is the philosophical ground Doc_07 §2B names as this world's own temporal assumption: living inside the closing of the apostolic generation, not primarily an imminent-parousia urgency but an urgency of transmission before the last direct witnesses are gone.

---

#### Force 1B-2: The Two Ways Catechetical Inheritance

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** The Didache's opening instructional material (chs. 1–6) structures entry into this world around a "way of life" and a "way of death," independently paralleled in the Latin *Doctrina Apostolorum* and Barnabas 18–20 — evidence the underlying schema circulated more widely than the Didache's own specific redaction of it, even though the Didache itself is plausibly a single (likely Syrian) community's own document (Doc_03 §2.7). *[Confidence: Documented that this schema exists and is used this way in the Didache; Inferential-Thin on how widely it was actually adopted as network-wide catechetical practice, given the Didache's own single-manuscript-dependent, single-community-attested evidentiary base (Doc_02 §1.1).]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** Before the water, a person preparing for baptism was taught that two ways lay open — a way of life and a way of death — and that which one a person walked was a live, continuing choice, not a nature settled once and for all (Doc_07 §2B). This teaching was inherited as a tool for shaping a new member, adaptable to a community's own occasion rather than issued from a single central authority.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This inherited catechetical schema is what Doc_07 §2B identifies as grounding this world's own philosophical understanding of the human person — someone standing at a live, continuing moral fork rather than a nature secured once by initiation. It supplies the entry-point moral logic for this world's entire formation arc (Doc_07 §2E), even though its own single-community evidentiary base means it cannot be generalized network-wide with confidence (Doc_03 §2.7, Author Gravity risk: maximal).

### CELL 2A — ONGOING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external forces required this community's continuous response and adaptation throughout its active life?

---

#### Force 2A-1: State Legal Precarity (G03)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Pliny's Letters 10.96–97 (c. 111–113 CE, Bithynia-Pontus) is the earliest Roman administrative document on Christians and shows no prior standing law; Trajan's rescript explicitly forbids seeking Christians out. Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44) and Suetonius corroborate earlier, scattered episodes. The first empire-wide persecution (Decius) does not occur until 249–250 CE, safely after this world's own close (Doc_01 §4). Doc_04's own testing of this as gravity G03 found it does not clear Primary confidence: the vivid impression of sustained persecution does not survive Doc_04's own discipline once "the generalized cross-episode synthesis" is separated from the narrow Documented core, since the specific episodes (Nero/Rome c. 64 CE; Pliny/Bithynia-Pontus c. 111–113 CE; Ignatius's own arrest, itself of disputed date) are geographically and temporally scattered, not one continuous policy (Doc_04, G03). *[Confidence: Documented that Pliny's letter is authentic and describes real legal action; Contested on whether these scattered episodes constitute one standing condition of persecution versus intermittent, locally variable exposure.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** A name could be given, an accusation made, and a choice demanded, without warning and without any way to know in advance whether it would happen to you. This was not constant terror — most days held no such demand — but it was near enough that a member organized some real part of their emotional life around it (Doc_05 §1.2). Carrying this world's own name in public was, at once, a legal exposure and a claim about the truth, held together as a single thing rather than two (Doc_05 §6.1).

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** Sustains G04 (Martyrdom as meaning-response) as its direct internal answer (Doc_04, G04: "this gravity is itself the meaning-making response to G03"); plausibly explains the near-total absence of institutional self-documentation across this world's evidentiary base (Doc_02 §3, §9 — durable, seizable written records carried their own risk in an environment of local, unpredictable legal exposure); and, per Doc_07 §3, its very *unevenness* — never becoming severe or centralized enough to force one strand's authority answer to displace the other's — is a condition of possibility for this world's own genuine two-strand plurality persisting as long as it did.

---

#### Force 2A-2: Contemporary Rival Movements (Marcion, Valentinian/Gnostic Christianity, Montanism)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Marcion's traditional break with the Roman church is conventionally dated c. 144 CE, though Sebastian Moll's reading suggests this may mark his arrival rather than expulsion; Valentinus moved to Rome c. 136 CE, remaining active into the 150s–160s, with a documented split into Western/Italian and Eastern (Asia Minor) branches; Montanism's Phrygian origin is archaeologically corroborated, with a start date disputed between c. 157 and c. 172, already a mature concern by 177 CE when the Lyons/Vienne martyrs wrote to Bishop Eleutherus about it (Doc_01 §8.3). All three were geographically overlapping with this world's own three core regions in the same decades this world's own texts were circulating. *[Confidence: Documented that these movements existed and were geographically overlapping; Contested on precise founding/break dates for each.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** These were not distant errors safely elsewhere or already defeated — they were near, contemporary, and undefeated neighbors, teaching within the same cities and sometimes drawing from the same communities this world's own members belonged to. A member did not experience this as a settled boundary already secured; the boundary against them was something that had to be actively argued and held, letter after letter, city after city (Doc_01 §8.3; Doc_05 §3.1).

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This is the external condition G05 (Boundary-Drawing, Tensional, Strand A only) is the internal response to — Ignatius's sustained anti-docetic argument exists because these rivals existed and pressed close. Doc_07 §2D's own new synthesis reads this pressure, combined with state legal precarity (Force 2A-1), as converging on a single formative lesson for Strand A members specifically: that maintaining any boundary at all, doctrinal or legal, was rehearsed as one continuous kind of refusal — though Doc_07 discloses this as its own interpretive extension, since Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix records no demonstrated relationship between G03 and G05 directly (Doc_07 §2D, §6).

### CELL 2B — ONGOING / INTERNAL [includes Transmission dimension]

**Governing question:** What internal forces sustained this community from within, generated its characteristic internal tensions, and shaped how it transmitted itself?

> **Transmission note:** addressed explicitly at Force 2B-2 below, per the Transmission Specificity Principle.

---

#### Force 2B-1: Authority Consolidation (G01)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** 1 Clement's interchangeable use of *episkopos* and *presbyteros* for a plural governing college (chs. 42, 44) and Hermas's plural "presbyters who preside over the Church" (Rome, Strand B) sit alongside Ignatius's explicit threefold-office program urged onto multiple Asia Minor churches (Strand A) — two mutually exclusive, contemporaneous, regionally-differentiated answers to the same organizational question (Doc_01 §6). Doc_04 classifies this Supporting rather than Primary: the coarse claim (authority required active organizing) is Widely Accepted, but the specific resolution is Contested, resting heavily on Ignatius's own contested authenticity/dating (Doc_04, G01). *[Confidence: Widely Accepted that authority organization was a live, contested question; Contested on Strand A's specific monarchical content.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** In one community, obedience to a single bishop, flanked by presbyters and deacons, was the very shape of unity — strings tuned together into one sound, as one of this world's own advocates put it. In another, a council of presbyters was the ordinary, sufficient shape of oversight, with no felt lack from the absence of any single presiding figure. The same person — Polycarp — could be addressed by one correspondent as a bishop and describe himself, in his own letter, only as one of "the presbyters" (Doc_05 §5.1; Doc_01 §6).

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This is the clearest single institutional expression of this world's own defining formation logic — office argued for, not assumed (Doc_05 §5.2). It is directly fused with G07 (Liturgical Practice) in Strand A, where attending the bishop's own eucharist and refusing any other was inseparably an act of worship and an act of belonging (Doc_05 §4.1), and it supplies the philosophical grounding Doc_07 §2C names: two structurally parallel answers to the identical question of how continuity with the apostles should be secured once eyewitnesses are gone.

---

#### Force 2B-2: Transmission — The Correspondence Network as Carrier and Filter

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** This world's characteristic material was carried forward by specific, named mechanisms, not an undifferentiated "tradition." Polycarp forwarded copies of Ignatius's letters onward to Philippi at the Philippians' own request (Doc_01 §3; Doc_02 §1.4). 1 Clement survives via Codex Alexandrinus (5th c., losing a leaf covering chs. 57–63) and Codex Hierosolymitanus (1056 CE), the latter also supplying nearly the entire text of the Didache (roughly 90% dependent on this single manuscript, undiscovered until 1873) (Doc_02 §1.1–1.2). Ignatius's own middle recension required a two-century critical dispute (Ussher/Vossius/Pearson vs. Daillé, then Cureton vs. Zahn/Lightfoot) to be authenticated as the genuine text at all (Doc_02 §1.3). The Shepherd of Hermas survives in no single complete Greek manuscript, an explicitly eclectic patchwork reconstruction with a documented forgery-adjacent complication (the Simonides episode) in its Athos transmission (Doc_02 §1.5). *[Confidence: Documented for the manuscript facts themselves throughout; the underlying selection mechanisms are Inferential-Thin, reconstructed from survivorship patterns rather than directly attested.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** A letter was copied and carried onward because a sister *ekklesia* asked for it, or because it answered a dispute that mattered enough to keep. What was worth the risk and labor of copying was not decided by an institution issuing instructions from a center — it was decided community by community, occasion by occasion, out of what a given *ekklesia* judged worth keeping and passing on.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** Doc_02 §9 reads the texts that survived best (Ignatius, eventually 1 Clement via biblical-codex inclusion, Justin) as precisely the ones with either strong institutional backing or apologetic value to later, more secure generations of the church — suggesting this world's own most vulnerable or marginal material (a hypothetical Didache-like text from Rome, or any text produced by or for an enslaved member) was structurally less likely to survive regardless of its original prevalence. This is the mechanism behind G02's own status as Primary and cross-strand confirmed (Doc_04, G02): the network did not merely carry unity claims, it determined which portions of this world's own self-understanding are recoverable today at all — a selection effect, not a neutral pipe.

#### Force 2B-3: Martyrdom as Formation-Shaping Meaning-Response (G04)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Ignatius's own letters (*Romans* 4, "food for wild beasts") and the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* (ch. 18, describing the community collecting Polycarp's bones as "more precious than the finest jewels" and marking his *dies natalis*) are, within this world's own Native evidentiary base, exactly two data points, both Strand A (Doc_01 §9; Doc_04, G04). Doc_04 classifies this Supporting, not Primary, on the strength of its own explicit test: "Clearly formation-shaping for the specific individuals in the record... far less certain for the ordinary, non-elite, non-imminently-threatened member" (Doc_04, G04). *[Confidence: the Formation-test claim itself reaches Documented-level confidence at its narrowest scope (these two individuals); representativeness beyond them is Contested/Inferential-Thin.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** For the one awaiting his own arrest, the exposure named in Force 2A-1 could turn toward eager anticipation rather than dread — a longing to be consumed rather than spared, framed as the truest possible imitation of the one this community followed (Doc_05 §1.2). For the community that received him, gathering annually at a leader's burial site to mark the day of his death as a birth into eternal life was itself a way of carrying his example forward into the community's own ongoing life.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** Sustains and intensifies in direct proportion to Force 2A-1 (Doc_04, G04: "This gravity is itself the meaning-making response to G03"); begins organizing time itself around a specific death-as-birth event (Doc_05 §4.1), though Doc_04's own entry treats this as something this world was only beginning to hand off to its successors, not something its own liturgical practice (G07) had yet fully organized around.

#### Force 2B-4: Boundary-Drawing Against Contemporary Rivals (G05)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Ignatius's anti-docetic argument, present across several of his letters, is — within Doc_02's own Native evidence streams — substantively developed by exactly one voice. Doc_04 classifies this Tensional: its Formation-test result reads only "Plausible... unestablished," never reaching the "Clearly" language G04 reaches even at its narrowest scope (Doc_04, G05). *[Confidence: Documented that Ignatius makes this argument; Inferential-Thin on its representativeness beyond his own direct addressees.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** A rival teaching that denied the reality of the body's suffering felt near and threatening enough to argue against at length, letter after letter, city after city. Refusing a separate eucharist from a rival teacher and holding fast to the one eucharist under the community's own bishop were the same act for a member shaped by this concern — belonging and boundary-marking were not experienced as two different decisions (Doc_05 §3.1).

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** Grounds this world's own ontological commitment, per Doc_07 §2B, that the reality of the body is not incidental but load-bearing — denying real bodily suffering threatens something real precisely because embodiment is where truth and falsehood are decided in this world's own philosophical ecology. Remains, per Doc_04's own classification, the least evidentially secured of this world's confirmed gravities — and, per Doc_07 §2E, for exactly that reason the clearest demonstration that even this world's boundary was actively defended, not inherited already-drawn.

### CELL 3A — ENDING/TRANSFORMING / EXTERNAL

**Governing question:** What external forces brought this world's distinct form to an end or transformed it into something different?

**Definitional note.** Forces 3A-1 and 3A-2 below are generated by fellow Christian communities (Irenaeus/Tertullian's regions; Alexandria) rather than the Roman state, so this document states directly what "External" means in its own usage, rather than leaving the term to be read geographically. A geographic reading — outside this world's own three core regions — cannot be what "External" means, since Force 2A-2 (Contemporary Rival Movements — Marcion, Valentinian/Gnostic Christianity, Montanism) is already classified External in Cell 2A above, despite those movements being Christian-identifying, not agents of the Roman state, and despite Force 2A-2's own Layer 1 stating directly that these movements were "geographically overlapping with this world's own three core regions in the same decades" — Marcion centered in Rome, Valentinian Christianity documented in both Rome and Asia Minor, Montanism spreading from Asia Minor to Rome (Doc_01 §8.3). Force 2A-2's own rivals operate substantially inside two of those three regions, so geography cannot be the operative test.

The actual rule Force 2A-2 already applies, and that Forces 3A-1/3A-2 can consistently follow, is narrower and does not depend on geography at all: **"External" names a force generated by a community, movement, or institution other than this world's own six-primary-voice evidentiary network** — the Didache, 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Hermas, and Justin, and the specific *ekklesiai* those six voices document (Doc_02 §1) — regardless of where that outside community happens to be physically located. Force 2A-2's rival movements meet this test on exactly this ground: they are doctrinally and organizationally distinct from this world's own six voices and the communities those voices speak for, even where a rival movement operated in the very same city as one of this world's own *ekklesiai*. Forces 3A-1 and 3A-2 meet the identical, non-geographic test on the identical ground: Irenaeus, Tertullian, and Alexandria's emerging teaching institution are none of them among this world's own six primary voices, and none of them write for or from one of this world's own documented *ekklesiai* — they are later or neighboring voices standing outside this world's own defined evidentiary community, independent of their geographic location. Classified consistently with 2A-2's own precedent under this corrected reading, 3A-1 and 3A-2's "External" classification is not an ad hoc call specific to Cell 3A — it follows the same rule this document already applied one cell earlier, now stated on the ground that actually supports it.

#### Force 3A-1: A Systematic, Succession-Argument Theological Mode Emerging in Adjacent Regions

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Irenaeus's *Adversus Haereses* (c. 180, written in Gaul) makes the first explicit four-Gospel, apostolic-succession argument; Tertullian's *Apologeticus* (197, Carthage) inaugurates a confident Latin apologetic corpus. Both figures write from regions genuinely outside this world's own three core regions (Antioch/Syria, Asia Minor, Rome), and both argue *from* apostolic succession as an already-secured structure rather than one still being actively built through pastoral correspondence (Doc_01 §2.2). *[Confidence: Well-established that these texts exist and argue this way; the characterization of this as a genuinely new mode relative to this world's own correspondence-and-catechesis pattern is this project's own comparative reading, Dominant Modern Reconstruction.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** Where sources allow: this world's own texts show no awareness of encountering a fully secured, systematized account of apostolic succession as a settled fact — the felt experience recoverable from this world's own core-region voices is of a still-argued, still-urgent question, not a doctrine already received from elsewhere. Whether or how a Strand A or Strand B member in this world's own final years began to encounter this newer mode is not recoverable from surviving sources.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** Marks, per Doc_01 §2.2, the point at which this world's own defining tension (collegial vs. monarchical authority; apostolic testimony vs. closed canon) becomes clearly resolved-in-direction rather than actively argued — the emergence of a formation logic that has settled, in adjacent regions, is what Doc_01 and Doc_07 both use to mark this world's own closing boundary, precisely because arguing rather than inheriting was this world's own defining character (Doc_07 §2E).

#### Force 3A-2: The Emergence of a Neighboring, Distinct Formation World (Alexandria)

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** The Step 0 Conclusion independently dates the Alexandrian Catechetical/Christian-Platonist Tradition (World #2) to c. 190–254 CE, built on a teaching-relationship, exegetical-philosophical formation logic distinct from this world's own household- and correspondence-based pastoral formation (Doc_01 §8.2). The two worlds' temporal ranges overlap only at this world's own closing edge (c. 190–200). *[Confidence: Documented that World #2's own gravity is independently emerging in these years, per the Step 0 Conclusion's own dating.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** Not recoverable from this world's own sources — this world's own core-region texts do not register Alexandria's emerging formation mode as a felt presence or pressure within this world's own lifetime.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** Doc_01 treats this overlap as a genuine, evidence-supported handoff rather than a contradiction requiring resolution (Doc_01 §8.2) — a different formation logic emerging in a different region at the precise moment this world's own logic is closing, rather than displacing or fracturing this world's own communities directly.

### CELL 3B — ENDING/TRANSFORMING / INTERNAL [includes Transmission dimension]

**Governing question:** What internal forces fractured this community, generated successor movements, or led to the dissolution of its characteristic form?

> **Transmission note:** addressed explicitly at Force 3B-2 below.

---

#### Force 3B-1: Monepiscopacy's Consolidation as the Dominant Emerging Pattern

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** Monepiscopacy appears earliest in Antioch and western Asia Minor via Ignatius, and latest in Rome, where Peter Lampe's "fractionated" reading finds no monarchical bishop until, on his account, possibly not before Victor I (189–199 CE). By c. 200, single-bishop governance is the dominant emerging pattern even in Rome — a real shift away from this world's own defining collegial-authority-in-formation gravity (Doc_01 §2.2). *[Confidence: Well-established for the general pattern per Sullivan and Lampe; Contested on the precise Roman dating, per Lampe's own specific account.]*

**Layer 2 — World's Own Experience:** Not recoverable as a single unified experience — this world's own texts do not record a moment of resolution, only the gradual, regionally uneven disappearance of the plural-college alternative as a live, argued option. Where this world's own sources are silent about how a Strand B member experienced the eventual adoption of a single bishop in Rome, this document does not construct an experience that is not attested.

**Layer 3 — Formation Impact:** This is the internal dissolution of this world's own characteristic form specifically — the resolution (not the mere continuation) of G01's own defining tension. Doc_01 §5 names this as one of three convergent fronts marking this world's own exit boundary; Doc_07 §2E treats this world's end as, by definition, the appearance of a formation logic that has settled where this world's own had not — meaning this world could not, by its own defining character, survive its own authority question actually being resolved.

---

#### Force 3B-2: Transmission at the Ending — Selective Canonization

**Layer 1 — Historical Event:** As monepiscopacy and apostolic succession became this wider tradition's own settled self-understanding, later transmission favored the texts that supported that settlement: Ignatius's corpus, the earliest sustained argument for monepiscopacy, was preserved, cited, and eventually required a two-century critical-scholarship effort to authenticate precisely because of its later canonical importance (Doc_02 §1.3); 1 Clement was folded into Codex Alexandrinus alongside the New Testament (Doc_02 §1.2). ## Section 6 — Transmission as a Force Dimension — Synthesis

### Who Transmitted This World's Characteristic Material

Named specifically, not generically: Polycarp of Smyrna, forwarding Ignatius's letters to Philippi at the Philippians' own request; the scribe Leon, copying Codex Hierosolymitanus in 1056 CE (the manuscript nearly the entire Didache and part of 1 Clement now depend on); the compilers of Codex Alexandrinus (5th c.) and Codex Sinaiticus (4th c.); Philotheos Bryennios, whose 1873 rediscovery of the Bryennios manuscript in Constantinople returned the Didache to circulation after roughly eighteen centuries; and, on the critical-scholarship side, James Ussher, Isaac Vossius, and John Pearson, whose 17th-century textual work is what established the middle recension of Ignatius's letters as authentic against Jean Daillé's challenge — a genuinely contested critical judgment call, not a passive inheritance (Doc_02 §§1.1–1.3).

### Under What Conditions Transmission Occurred

Selection favored texts with either strong institutional backing (canonical-adjacent status, eventual inclusion in biblical codices) or apologetic value to later, more secure generations of the church (Doc_02 §9). 1 Clement's inclusion in Codex Alexandrinus alongside the New Testament and Ignatius's own centrality to later monepiscopal argument both reflect this pattern directly; by contrast, no comparable institutional or apologetic interest protected a hypothetical Didache-like text from Rome, or any text produced by or for an enslaved member of this world's own congregations.

### What Selection Effects Shaped What Survives

The Didache survives essentially through a single 1056 CE manuscript, undiscovered for roughly eighteen centuries — a genuine single-point-of-failure transmission chain (Doc_02 §1.1). The Shepherd of Hermas survives in no single complete Greek manuscript at all, an explicitly eclectic reconstruction (Doc_02 §1.5). Both texts carried near-canonical status in antiquity (Hermas cited as scripture by Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria, and Origen) yet transmitted far more precariously than Ignatius's own corpus, which required active, centuries-long critical-scholarship defense but never came close to total loss. This directly traces to Doc_02 §1's own Author Gravity Assessment: representativeness and survival are shown to be two separate properties a text can hold in very different combinations.

### What the Transmission Pattern Reveals About Forces

**This document's own synthesis, not a claim independently sourced to a named scholar — disclosed inline here rather than only in the Document Log:** communities and later copyists preserve what they judge worth the labor and risk of preserving; what they chose to protect — Ignatius's monepiscopal argument, 1 Clement's corrective authority — reveals which of this world's own internal contests (Force 2B-1, Authority Consolidation) mattered enough to later custodians to actively secure, while material without a comparably useful later institutional home (Rome's own plural-college texts, the Didache's own Syrian-community catechesis) survived more by accident than by design. Transmission, read this way, is itself a downstream record of which of this world's own forces the wider Christian tradition eventually judged to have "won" — an inference this document draws from the manuscript-survival pattern Doc_02 documents, not a conclusion Doc_02 itself states.

### Connection to Author Gravity Findings (Doc_02)

Doc_02 §1's own Author Gravity Assessment already names this directly for each of the six primary voices: Ignatius's high, continuous visibility versus the Didache's near-total transmission dependency on a single medieval manuscript versus Hermas's fully eclectic reconstruction are not differences in how important these texts were to this world's own communities — they are differences in how well each text's own later usefulness protected it. The thin or absent voices this world's evidence base structurally suppresses (Doc_02 §8's Affirmative Duty disclosure — enslaved people, women outside a small named set of patrons, the non-literate majority) are thin for the identical underlying reason at an even more extreme degree: no later institutional or apologetic interest existed to protect material these members might have produced, even setting aside the prior question of whether they could produce durable texts at all.
