## Overall Verdict: QUALIFIED PASS

The document's central structural move — applying the L4 template's Strand Attribution default ("hold all strands as one register-range unless a world's strands refused each other's 'we'") to World #1 — is **verified accurate and soundly argued**. The template quote is verbatim, the application to World #1's "Held, not resolved" finding is a legitimate and honestly-flagged reframing of the Ecology Assessment's own open question, and the treatment of the Grapte peg (Section 1's closing paragraph) responsibly carries forward the specific risk the Ecology Assessment warned about (that the Grapte function might quietly imply a Strand A polity fit it doesn't have). This is real, careful work.

However, the citation-by-citation check surfaced a recurring pattern: several claims are over-specified beyond what their cited sources actually say, one quotation is presented in quotation marks as verbatim when it is a paraphrase, one supporting factual claim about Polycarp appears to be invented (no source found anywhere), and — most consequentially — the document silently reclassifies a domain the Ecology Assessment explicitly and repeatedly called "Thin" into "Moderate," without disclosing or justifying the change, in a document whose own stated discipline is "disclose the dependency, rather than let a downstream document discover it by accident." The document's single most load-bearing methodological claim (the §2E two-tier confidence distinction, used to instruct Phase Three not to voice hedged uncertainty about persecution) also overreaches beyond what its own cited source supports, in a way that is in tension with adjacent findings in the same source family (Doc_07 §2A, Doc_08 Force 2A-1).

None of these defects are fabrications that invert a source's meaning, and the document's overall reasoning architecture is sound. But there are enough of them, and one is significant enough, that this should not clear as an unqualified PASS. Recommended: fix the items below, particularly Findings 1 and 2, before this document is treated as settled input to Phase Three.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — SIGNIFICANT: The §2E "two-tier confidence" claim overreaches beyond what Doc_07 §2E actually supports, and is in tension with Doc_07 §2A / Doc_08 Force 2A-1

**What I checked:** The document's Section 3 (Moderate domains, "State legal precarity as lived risk") states: G03's "unresolved edges reflect *modern reconstruction's limited ability to generalize* from scattered episodes (Doc_07 §2E), **not ancient uncertainty Chloe's own community lived inside**." This is used as the basis for a specific instruction to Phase Three: "Chloe should not voice hedged uncertainty about 'how common' persecution was... she carries no in-world hedging at all." This claim is flagged in the document's own Section 4 as one of the four Ecology Assessment items being resolved, and the task instructions specifically asked me to scrutinize it against Framework Part Two's "Internal Uncertainty Only" principle.

**What I found:** I read Doc_07 §2E directly. The actual passage (quoted in full):

> "'argued, not assumed' is not uniformly evidenced across G01, G03, G04, and G05. G01 and G05 fell short of Primary because the underlying question was genuinely, historically contested in antiquity... G03 and G04 fell short for a different reason: modern reconstruction's own limited ability to responsibly generalize from scattered surviving episodes into a network-wide claim, **not demonstrated proof that this world's own inhabitants argued over state pressure's severity or martyrdom's meaning the way they argued over office.**"

§2E's actual distinction is narrower than the Calibration document's gloss: §2E says ancient people didn't *textually argue/debate* state pressure's severity the way they argued over office — it does not say ancient people felt no uncertainty about persecution at all. Doc_07 §2A explicitly documents the opposite: real, felt, ancient unpredictability — "near enough that a member of these communities organized some real part of their emotional life around it" (quoting Doc_05 §1.2) — and Doc_08 Force 2A-1 independently describes "a name could be given, an accusation made, and a choice demanded, without warning and without any way to know in advance whether it would happen to you." That is lived, ancient, in-world uncertainty — precisely the kind of thing Framework Part Two's "Internal Uncertainty Only" principle (verified directly: "tensions held without resolution... these are legitimate expressions of uncertainty") would actually *permit* the Representative to voice, not forbid.

**Assessment:** The underlying calibration instruction — don't have Chloe hedge about network-wide representativeness, since that is a modern historiographical problem she has no access to — is sound and correctly grounded in Framework Part Two's actual "Uncertainty generated by gaps in the historical record, by scholarly debate, or by reconstruction limitations does not exist for the Representative" language. But the supporting rationale ("not ancient uncertainty Chloe's own community lived inside") conflates two different things: (a) the absence of ancient *textual argument* about state pressure's severity (true, per §2E), and (b) the absence of ancient *felt* uncertainty about persecution risk (false, per Doc_07 §2A and Doc_08 Force 2A-1, which are part of this same document's own evidentiary base). The document's own following sentence ("vivid where her own community's experience was vivid, quiet where it was not") partially rescues the outcome, but the flat clause, read on its own by whoever builds Phase Three, risks the opposite of what the document intends: excising legitimate felt-anxiety texture instead of just excising modern meta-commentary.

**Severity:** Significant. This is the document's single most methodologically important claim (it resolves Ecology Assessment Item 4, the two-tier confidence distinction), and the phrasing as written could mislead downstream construction. Recommend rewording to something like: "the *scope/representativeness* of this risk across the network is a modern reconstruction problem Chloe has no access to — but the felt, unpredictable, personal exposure itself is squarely hers, per Doc_07 §2A and Doc_08 Force 2A-1."

### Finding 2 — SIGNIFICANT: "Household daily domestic practice and texture" is silently reclassified from the Ecology Assessment's explicit "Thin" to this document's "Moderate," without disclosure or justification

**What I checked:** Task instructions specifically asked me to check Section 3 (Depth Calibration) against the Ecology Assessment's own Section 5.6 "rich vs. thin" mapping for drift.

**What I found:** The Ecology Assessment's Section 5.6 places "Daily domestic practice in specific, textured detail" squarely in its **"Thin or displaced territory, requiring explicit, disclosed calibration rather than confident inhabitation"** list — not its Rich list. Section 5.3 goes further, calling it **"the single thinnest domain directly underneath Chloe's own role."** Section 7's Recommendation 3 explicitly instructs Phase Two to build this "given the genuine thinness of primary-voice household detail established in Section 5.3."

The Calibration document's Section 3 places "Household daily domestic practice and texture" in its **"Moderate domains"** category — not Thin — describing it as something Chloe "can engage" with, built from "the structural, secondary-scholarship argument," with no acknowledgment that the Ecology Assessment itself used the word "thinness" and "thinnest domain" for this exact material.

**Why this matters:** Framework V3.2 Part Four's Depth Calibration section (verified directly) defines these tiers with materially different consequences for Phase Three:
- **Moderate:** "the Representative can engage meaningfully but with less capacity for extended exploration. It will respond authentically but may reach its natural depth more quickly."
- **Thin:** "the Representative engages briefly and naturally... This manifests as brevity, as redirection toward richer territory... never as meta-commentary."

These are not cosmetically different instructions — "engage meaningfully" versus "brevity, or redirection toward richer territory" will produce different Phase Three voice-construction decisions. It is possible the Calibration document intends a deliberate, considered upgrade (translating the Ecology Assessment's binary Rich/Thin reporting, done under Framework Part Three's Thinness Mapping requirement, into Framework Part Four's finer three-tier Depth Calibration scale, where "well-argued structural fact + some documentation" genuinely fits "Moderate" better than "Thin"). If so, that is a defensible move — but the document does not say this is what it is doing. It cites the Ecology Assessment's own finding as its evidential basis while quietly changing the tier the Ecology Assessment assigned, in a build whose own stated discipline (the Ecology Assessment's own words, carried into this document's DNA) is: "disclose the dependency, rather than let a downstream document discover it by accident."

**Severity:** Significant. This is exactly the kind of "drift" the task asked me to check for, and it directly concerns the domain the Ecology Assessment called out as the single thinnest one underneath Chloe's own role. Recommend the document either (a) explicitly justify the Thin→Moderate reclassification against Framework Part Four's own tier definitions, or (b) move this domain back to Thin domains, consistent with the Ecology Assessment.

### Finding 3 — MODERATE: An unsupported factual claim about Polycarp receiving correspondence from Rome

**What I checked:** Section 1 states: "Polycarp of Smyrna — a Strand A figure by every measure — is the one who forwards Ignatius's letters onward to Philippi **and receives correspondence from Rome**; the network these two strands share (G02, Primary, cross-strand) is the very thing that makes them one world rather than two."

**What I found:** The letter-forwarding claim is well-attested (Polycarp, *Philippians* ch. 13, cited correctly elsewhere in both documents). But "receives correspondence from Rome" has no source anywhere in the build. I searched Doc_01, Doc_02, and Doc_08 directly (all discuss Polycarp) — none mention Polycarp receiving anything from Rome. The only Rome-linked Polycarp fact in the source base is that Irenaeus (who later worked in Gaul but knew Polycarp personally in Smyrna) provides cross-generational continuity — not correspondence from Rome. This detail appears to be invented to strengthen the "network connects the strands" argument.

**Severity:** Moderate. Not load-bearing to the core Strand Attribution argument (which stands fine on G02's independently-verified cross-strand Primary status without this detail), but it is presented as an established fact in a sentence adjacent to a genuine direct quotation, which risks a reader assuming it carries the same evidential weight. Should be removed or sourced.

### Finding 4 — MODERATE: Doc_04 G04 quotation is presented as verbatim but is a composite paraphrase (inherited from the Ecology Assessment)

**What I checked:** Section 3 (Thin domains) quotes: "Ignatius's own self-fashioning as a willing martyr, and the *Martyrdom of Polycarp*'s account, belong to specific named individuals (Doc_04, G04, **"explicitly restricted to these two specific people... not extended to the ordinary member"**)."

**What I found:** This exact phrase does not appear in Doc_04. The closest actual language in Doc_04's G04 entry: "**Clearly** formation-shaping *for the specific individuals in the record* (Ignatius, Polycarp); far less certain for the ordinary, non-elite, non-imminently-threatened member," and, separately, "narrow claim ('shaped these two individuals')." Doc_04 never uses the word "explicitly" or "restricted" in this context. This quotation-dressed-as-verbatim is inherited unchanged from the Ecology Assessment (which used the identical construction and was not flagged for it in that document's own Round 1 cold review) — so this is a pre-existing defect being carried forward, not new to this document, but it is repeated here without correction.

**Severity:** Moderate. Not a meaning inversion, but a quotation-fidelity problem that should be fixed at the source (both documents) — either de-quote it as a paraphrase or replace with Doc_04's actual language.

### Finding 5 — MODERATE: Letter-handling functions ("reads aloud, discusses, copies") are attributed to a citation that only supports "forwards"

**What I checked:** Section 3 (Rich domains): "Chloe receives, reads aloud, discusses, copies, and forwards such letters — a genuinely attested function (Polycarp's own act, Doc_08 §6)."

**What I found:** Doc_08 §6 ("Who Transmitted This World's Characteristic Material") names only Polycarp's act of *forwarding* Ignatius's letters to Philippi. It does not attest "reads aloud" or "discusses" anywhere. "Copies" appears elsewhere in Doc_08 (Force 2B-2) but as an unnamed, community-level practice, not something documented for Polycarp specifically, and not part of §6. Doc_05 contains no support for any of these three actions in connection with letter transmission either. "Receives" is a reasonable inference from the correspondence-network premise; "reads aloud, discusses, copies" are plausible ancient-letter-culture assumptions not actually established by this build's own evidentiary base, bundled under a citation that supports only one of the five listed actions.

**Severity:** Moderate. This inflates a citation's evidential reach in a "Rich domain" justification — precisely the kind of overclaim Framework Part Four's Depth Calibration is designed to prevent ("Emphasis-shape is a construction artifact with a named source, not a builder's discretionary judgment"). Recommend citing only "receives... and forwards" to Doc_08 §6, or separately sourcing "reads aloud/discusses/copies" to a plausible-inference basis if the project wants to keep them, disclosed as such.

### Finding 6 — MODERATE: "A gathering under one roof is exactly the site Pliny's letter shows state pressure reaching" is not supported by the cited sections

**What I checked:** Section 3 (Moderate domains, state legal precarity): "a gathering under one roof is exactly the site Pliny's letter shows state pressure reaching (Doc_02 §6, §9; Doc_08, Force 2A-1)."

Doc_08 Force 2A-1 likewise never mentions a gathering location. Note: a similar, slightly more hedged version of this claim already exists in the Ecology Assessment's own Section 6 ("A gathering in a home is the physical site this world's own outside evidence describes state pressure actually reaching"), so this is a pattern carried forward and stated more assertively here than in the prior document, not invented from nothing — but it remains uncited as given in both places.

**Severity:** Moderate. The underlying intuition is plausible but should be labeled as inference, not cited as though Doc_02/Doc_08 state it directly.
