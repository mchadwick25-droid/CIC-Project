## Findings

### Finding 1 — SUBSTANTIAL. `pahc.search.apostolic-fathers-anf01` falsely claims the prior Source Registry "named all these works."

The search record's body states: "The prior build's Source Registry (Doc_02, 73 rows) named all these works" — where "these works" is its `found_sources` list of eight, including `pahc.source.irenaeus-adversus-haereses`, `pahc.source.barnabas`, and `pahc.source.justin-dialogue`. I extracted every cell of every row of all five sheets of `CiC_W1_Source_Registry_FINAL_v2.xlsx` directly from the workbook XML: the strings "Irenaeus," "Barnabas," "Trypho," and "Dialogue" appear **nowhere in the entire workbook**. The registry's only Justin row is P06, "Justin Martyr, First Apology (chs.65-67 esp.)". This sentence also contradicts the record set's own internals: `pahc.source.barnabas` correctly describes the Two-Ways-parallel claim as previously "an unregistered citation," and the Irenaeus and Barnabas rows correctly say "registered at this build's step 2." The false sentence misstates the provenance of three of the eight found sources. Fix: restate as "named the Apostolic-Fathers core and Justin's First Apology (P01–P06, P16); Irenaeus, Barnabas, and the Dialogue are registered new at this step."

### Finding 2 — SUBSTANTIAL. `pahc.search.clement-complete-anf09` misstates the recovered portion of 1 Clement as "chapters 57:7–65."

The search record's `query` field describes "the ending recovered from Codex Hierosolymitanus (chapters 57:7-65 in modern numbering, incl. the long closing prayer) - absent from ANF vol. 1." Doc_02 §1.2 (approved) bounds the Alexandrinus lacuna at "57:7 through the end of ch. 63," and the `pahc.source.first-clement` record itself correctly says "loses a leaf (57:7-63)." Verified directly against the vendored files: anf01's own final chapter (LIX of its 59-chapter numbering) **contains the post-lacuna ending** — the "Send back speedily… Claudius Ephebus and Valerius Bito, with Fortunatus" material and closing benediction that is modern chs. 64–65 — so chs. 64–65 are neither Hierosolymitanus-recovered nor absent from ANF vol. 1. What anf01 lacks is 57:7–63, including the long prayer (modern 59–61), which anf09 div1 xii supplies (verified: 65 chapters, prayer beginning at its ch. LIX). The error is over-inclusive in a safe direction for quote-routing (citing anf09 for chs. 64–65 does no harm), but it is a factual transmission claim contradicting both the governing document and the sibling source record — exactly the class of internal contradiction this record set exists to prevent.

### Finding 3 — SUBSTANTIAL. `pahc.source.martyrdom-polycarp` quotes ch. 18 in wording that does not match the vendored edition the row claims verified-direct.

The record's body cites "the bone-collection scene at ch. 18 - 'more precious than the finest jewels'." The vendored anf01 text (div2 iv.iv, Chapter XVIII, read directly) reads: "we afterwards took up his bones, as being **more precious than the most exquisite jewels**, and more purified than gold." The record has inherited Doc_01 §9's wording ("more precious than the finest jewels"), which itself matches no line of the ANF translation — it appears to be Doc_01's own gloss of a different rendering. On a `verified-direct` source row whose stated function is verbatim-checkable citation against the named file, a quotation-marked phrase that fails against that file is a quote-discipline violation, however small. Fix: quote the ANF wording, or drop the quotation marks and attribute the phrase to Doc_01 §9's paraphrase.

### Finding 4 — SUBSTANTIAL. `pahc.source.eusebius-historia-ecclesiastica` omits its prior-build Registry row (P14 exists) and presents the source as newly registered.

Its `discovery_channel` reads "registered at this build's step 2 for a role the prior build exercised piecemeal (Eusebius citations appear across Doc_01/02/09 without their own row's screen being stated once)." But the Source Registry contains **P14, "Eusebius, Historia Ecclesiastica," author "Eusebius of Caesarea"** (added at Doc_02's round-1 fix pass, per the Doc_02 log "P14-P15, S41-S55"). Every other prior-registered source in this set cites its P-row (P01–P10, P15, P16 — all twelve verified against the workbook), including P15 and P16 which were late additions. The Eusebius row breaks that convention and makes a provenance claim ("registered at this build's step 2") that is at best misleading: the row existed; what is new is only the consolidated screen. The parenthetical about the screen never being "stated once" may stand; the registration claim may not. Fix: cite P14 in `discovery_channel` on the same pattern as the P15/P16 rows.

### Finding 5 — SUBSTANTIAL (narrow). The paraphrase-only consequence of the two not_found searches is stated on only two of the four affected source rows.

`pahc.search.pliny-english-pd` and `pahc.search.roman-historians-english-pd` both state the binding consequence: Pliny, Tacitus, Suetonius, and Lucian are paraphrase-only until a PD translation is vendored. On the source rows: **Pliny** carries it in a machine-readable `divergence_note`; **Tacitus** likewise; **Suetonius and Lucian have `divergence_note: null` and no paraphrase-only statement anywhere in the row** — their bodies say only "NOT VENDORED… pending acquisition." A builder reading the Suetonius or Lucian row alone (the normal retrieval unit) never sees the quote ban; it lives only in the search record and the manifest. Compounding this, all four rows carry machine-readable `rights_status: public-domain` with no vendored file — the body prose ("rights_status refers to the ancient text and the named translation candidate, not to a vendored file") corrects this for a human reader, but any automated consumer of `rights_status` alone (the rights gate reads exactly this field, `gates.py` line ~194) would read these rows as quote-licensed. This is a scope-boundary gap, not a wording preference. Fix: add the same paraphrase-only `divergence_note` to the Suetonius and Lucian rows (their differing `formation_confidence` values are legitimate and can stand; the note is additive).

### Finding 6 — COSMETIC. Justin translator attributions ("trans. Dods/Reith") cannot be confirmed from the vendored file, and blur which translator did which work.

Per the review instruction to verify translator claims against the volumes' own text: anf01 carries **no translator credit at the head of the First Apology (viii.ii) or the Dialogue (viii.iv)**. In-file "Translated by" credits exist only for the minor Justin works: viii.v Dods, viii.vi Dods, viii.vii Reith, viii.viii Dods, viii.ix Roberts, viii.xi Dods (mapped by position against the div structure). External bibliography supports Dods for the Apologies and Reith for the Dialogue, so the records are almost certainly not *wrong*, but the identical "trans. Dods/Reith" string on both rows is (a) not confirmable from the file itself and (b) imprecise about which man translated which work. Related, lower-grade notes, same class: "trans. Roberts-Donaldson" on the Ignatius, Polycarp, and Martyrdom rows rests on the volume-level credit ("Text edited by Rev. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson," plus the 1 Clement introductory note's "original editors and translators, Drs. Roberts and Donaldson" — the latter confirmed in-file at div ii.i, so the 1 Clement row is fully covered); and the Irenaeus row's "trans. Roberts and Rambaut" is in-file supported but the volume's own notice is more specific (books I–II Roberts; book III and part of IV groundwork Rambaut). Recommend: per-work precision where the file allows, and an explicit "per standard ANF bibliography, not stated in the file" hedge where it does not. No sourcing conclusion changes; hence cosmetic.

### Finding 7 — COSMETIC. The manifest's §3 mislocates its scholar list as "the named scholarship of Doc_02 §2."

Of the names in the manifest's parenthesis, Moll, Lieu, Thomassen, and Tabbernee are named in **Doc_01 §8.3** (not Doc_02 §2); Bagnall in **Doc_01 §3 / Doc_02 §10**; McGowan in **Doc_02 §4**. Lampe, Brown, Brent, Trevett, Bradshaw are genuinely §2 (Bradshaw §2/§4). The scholars are all real, all named in the governing documents, and all correctly consult-only — only the section citation is loose.

### Finding 8 — COSMETIC. The anti-Montanist row silently overrides the ANF volume's own dating conjecture without noting it.

`pahc.source.anti-montanist-fragments` correctly refuses the ANF's conjectural "Asterius Urbanus" attribution, but the same ANF introductory notice (read directly at anf07 div1 v) also dates the work "Circa a.d. 232" per Tillemont — forty years past this world's window. The row's own c. 192–193 dating is the modern consensus (and consistent with the fragments' internal "upwards of thirteen years since the woman died"), so nothing substantive is wrong; but a row whose whole discipline is "the ANF's framing of this material is conjectural and not adopted" would be complete if it noted the volume's date claim is rejected along with its name claim — otherwise a reader hitting the vendored file's "a.d. 232" header meets an unexplained apparent contradiction, the exact reader-experience failure Doc_02's round-5 Hartog clause exists to prevent.
