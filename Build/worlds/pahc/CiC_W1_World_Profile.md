## Section 1 — World Identity

**World name (as the world would name itself):** No self-designation survives in this world's own evidentiary base beyond ordinary shared vocabulary — *ekklesia* — which this world used but did not develop as a distinguishing proper name. Doc_01 records no passage in which this community names itself as something other than "the church of God" (1 Clement's own address) or an equivalent generic term also used by neighboring worlds. This document uses the working title assigned at Step 0: **Post-Apostolic/Sub-Apostolic House-Church Christianity**, with the modern scholarly label doing double duty as the world's own name in the absence of a distinguishing self-designation.

**World code:** `pahc`

**Temporal scope:** c. 70–200 CE, held with an explicit internal caveat carried forward from Doc_01. The floor (70 CE) is inherited from the Step 0 Conclusion's own constitutional scope decision (the destruction of the Second Temple) rather than from an internal marker specific to this world; Doc_01 discloses that this world's own lived sense of "post-apostolic-ness" more plausibly firms up gradually across 64–100 CE, keyed to the deaths of Peter, Paul, and James under Nero, than at 70 CE specifically. The ceiling (c. 200 CE) rests on three converging lines of evidence: monepiscopacy's emergence as the dominant institutional norm, a documented shift toward a settled, succession-argument theological mode (Irenaeus, Tertullian), and adjacency to World #2 (Alexandria, c. 190–254 CE). Doc_01 states this ceiling is directional, not universal — the point at which this world's defining tension becomes clearly resolved-in-direction, not the point at which every relevant development finishes everywhere at once.

**Geographic scope:** Antioch/Syria, Asia Minor (western Anatolia), and Rome — established through Ignatius's guarded journey connecting all three, the letter-exchange infrastructure (Polycarp forwarding Ignatius's letters to Philippi; 1 Clement Rome-to-Corinth; 1 Peter's circular address across five Asia Minor provinces), and uniform Koine Greek across the region. Egypt/Alexandria is explicitly excluded from this world's scope, resting on a single scholarly argument (Bagnall's papyrological-silence case) that this document's own Source Registry flags as a load-bearing single-source dependency.

**Community character:** This world understood itself as household-based gathering and catechesis, sustained by a translocal correspondence network rather than a fixed institutional center. It felt an active, urgent need to formalize authority, consolidate shared textual and doctrinal memory before living apostolic testimony disappeared entirely, and maintain cohesion across real geographic distance through correspondence rather than centralized administration. It was not yet a settled orthodoxy defining itself against a settled heresy — its boundary against contemporary rival movements (Marcionite, Valentinian/Gnostic, Montanist) was live and actively argued, not inherited already-drawn.

## Section 3 — Formation Logic

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

Not to arrive at settled answers, but to become someone capable of holding real, community-defining, unresolved questions — who leads; whether the body's suffering was real; whether mercy still avails after failure; where the edge of a safe public identity lies — as the active substance of belonging, rather than as scandal or deficiency awaiting resolution. A mature practitioner in this world could write, or receive and act on, a letter arguing urgently for a still-contested position while simultaneously recognizing the community on the argument's other side — differently organized, differently answering its own live question — as still "the church of God" (1 Clement's own opening address), not as a rival or an error to correct.

### The Formation Mechanism

Two mechanisms did nearly all of this world's formative work, and they are precisely this world's only two Primary gravities. The correspondence network (G02) transmitted not settled doctrine from a stable center but arguments shaped by specific local trouble — a member of one *ekklesia* rarely encountered the wider movement's teaching directly; they encountered it as a specific writer had already shaped it, in response to a specific, local argument. The eucharist (G07) did the parallel work ritually — repeated, re-forming, and, in Strand A specifically, simultaneously an authority-claim and a boundary-claim in the same act. Two Ways catechesis supplied the entry-point moral logic, but this world's ongoing, adult formation ran through letters and liturgy — this world explicitly lacked any formal teaching institution of the kind that would define the next world in this sequence's own formation logic just past this world's own close.

### The Formation Arc

Entry via Two Ways catechesis and baptism (running water preferred, with disclosed fallbacks) → incorporation into a local *ekklesia*'s own already-argued answer to the authority question → ongoing re-formation through repeated eucharistic gathering and reception of translocal correspondence → for a minority, direct testing through state exposure, met in Strand A with an intensified, martyrdom-oriented formation logic modeled on one bishop's own self-presentation. No settled terminal stage is evidenced anywhere in this world's own record. Arguing was not a stage on the way to settling; for this world's own lifetime, arguing was the whole shape of formation.

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

The shared underlying logic — argue rather than inherit — runs through both strands even though its specific content diverges sharply. Strand A argues its way toward a single office and treats the doctrinal/bodily boundary as the sharper front; Strand B argues its way toward a plural college and treats post-baptismal mercy as the sharper front. Neither strand's argued, not-yet-settled character is itself in question; only the content each strand is arguing toward differs. This shared logic is this world's irreducible core; strand variation illuminates it rather than fragmenting it.

### Formation Logic and the Confirmed Gravities

G02 (Primary) is the delivery mechanism through which every other gravity's argued, not-inherited character actually travels between communities. G07 (Primary) is the ritual site where the argued authority claim (G01) and the argued boundary claim (G05) become one embodied act, at least in Strand A. G01 (Supporting) is the clearest single institutional expression of "argued, not assumed." G03 (Supporting) supplies the external condition that makes active holding urgent rather than a scholastic exercise. G04 (Supporting, Strand A only) is the most extreme individual case of choosing rather than passively receiving one's own relationship to the community's central conviction. G05 (Tensional) is the least evidentially secured of all seven candidates — and for exactly that reason the clearest demonstration that even this world's boundary was actively defended, not inherited already-drawn.

**A distinction carried forward from Doc_07 §2E:** "argued, not assumed" is not uniformly evidenced across G01, G03, G04, and G05. G01 and G05 fell short of Primary because the underlying question was genuinely, historically contested in antiquity. G03 and G04 fell short for a different reason: modern reconstruction's own limited ability to responsibly generalize from scattered surviving episodes into a network-wide claim, not demonstrated proof that this world's own inhabitants argued over state pressure's severity or martyrdom's meaning the way they argued over office. Both are real instances of this world declining to settle a question, but they are not the same kind of unresolvedness.

The very fact that so much of this world's evidence resists confident, Primary-level classification is not a gap in the formation logic — it is the formation logic, directly and specifically. A world whose defining character is "argued, not assumed" will, when tested against Doc_04's own six-test discipline, produce exactly this pattern: two Primary gravities that are pure delivery mechanisms (network, ritual), and several genuinely Supporting or Tensional gravities that are the contents being delivered and argued over.

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary

### 4A — Emotional Texture

This world's Tier 1 lexicon carries no dedicated affective vocabulary — a structural feature of the evidentiary base, not an oversight in construction. The dominant emotional register is not constant terror but standing, unpredictable exposure: near enough that a member of these communities organized some real part of their emotional life around it, without that exposure amounting to sustained, active persecution. The two strands carry this shared exposure into different specific dangers. For those formed most directly under Ignatius's own example (Strand A), exposure could turn toward eager anticipation rather than dread — a longing to be consumed rather than spared, framed as the truest possible imitation of the one this community followed. For Rome (Strand B), the sharper fear ran elsewhere: not toward the possibility of arrest, but toward the possibility that one's own worst failure, committed after baptism, might already have used up whatever mercy remained.

Cutting across both strands, hope ran through correspondence: a letter arriving from another *ekklesia* was never merely information — it was proof that the *ekklesia* a member belonged to was larger than the room. The emotional arc runs from Two Ways catechesis through baptism into recurring eucharistic re-formation and, for a minority, state exposure — with no settled terminal emotional state ever evidenced. One shared external condition (state pressure, G03) produced two structurally parallel but substantively different economies of fear and hope by strand: the exposure is shared; the danger it becomes is strand-specific.

### 4B — Philosophical Ecology

Beneath the two strand-specific fears named above lies one shared ontological commitment: nothing about a formed Christian life in this world — the reality of the body, the availability of forgiveness, the legitimacy of office — is treated as automatically secured by initiation or apostolic inheritance. Reality is organized around the presence and fading of direct apostolic testimony rather than a closed textual canon. For Strand A, Christ's bodily suffering is ontologically load-bearing (Ignatius insists he "suffered truly and in the flesh, not merely in appearance"). The Two Ways schema frames personhood as a live, continuing moral fork, extended past baptism in Strand B by Hermas, where personhood is morally provisional rather than secured once for all by the initiatory rite. This world's temporal self-understanding is one of living inside the closing of the apostolic generation — an urgency of transmission before the last direct witnesses are gone.

Institutional unsettledness (Section 4C below) and emotional unsettledness (4A above) are not two separate facts about this world but the same underlying philosophical commitment expressed twice.

### 4C — Authority Structures

This is how the "actively argued, not automatically secured" logic named in 4B plays out institutionally. Sources of authority include fading apostolic memory, demonstrated correspondence-recognized standing (Rome's own intervention in the Corinthian dispute), a singular office actively constructed and legitimated through argument (Ignatius, mapped onto pagan civic and mystery-cult models for Strand A), and a plural, interchangeable college (1 Clement, Hermas, for Strand B). Transmission of authority is imagined differently by strand: Strand A as fitted-together continuity (Ignatius's own image of a harp's tuned strings); Strand B as shared, interchangeable college membership, with no felt lack created by the absence of any single presiding figure.

The sharpest point of contestation is embodied in a single person: Polycarp, addressed by Ignatius as bishop but self-identifying in his own letter as merely "one of the presbyters." This tension is held, not resolved, in this world's own record. In Strand A, obedience to the bishop is experienced as the very shape of unity and is fused with the eucharist — an inseparably worshipful and belonging-defining act. Both strands answer the identical underlying question — how to secure continuity once eyewitnesses are gone — with structurally parallel but substantively opposite claims.

### 4D — Boundary Structures

This is how this world's own still-unsettled internal authority question interacts with real external pressure. Carrying this world's name was, at once, a legal exposure and a claim about the truth, held together as a single thing rather than two. Baptism (per the Didache, the clearest internal entry rite this world's evidence documents, preceded by Two Ways catechesis) rests on a single-source evidentiary base and cannot be generalized with full confidence beyond that one community.

Two distinct "outsides" press on this world at once: the Roman state's boundary (legal-administrative, improvised and intermittent rather than systematic) and the rival-movement boundary (doctrinal, live and undefeated contemporary neighbors, not yet decisively answered). The doctrinal boundary is explicitly not settled within this world's own lifetime; the state's boundary is contested even for its own enforcer (Pliny himself was uncertain whether "the name itself" or associated crimes was the chargeable offense). In Strand A, refusing a rival's eucharist and holding the "one eucharist" were the same act — belonging and boundary-marking were not experienced as two different decisions.

This document's own new synthesis, disclosed explicitly rather than presented as an inherited Doc_04 finding: although Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix records no demonstrated relationship between G03 (state pressure) and G05 (boundary-drawing against rivals), Doc_07 proposes the two pressures converge for a Strand A member into one continuous kind of refusal at the level of lived experience — maintaining any boundary at all, against the state or against a rival teacher, was rehearsed as the same underlying formation habit. This is flagged as the single claim in this world's construction record a specialist reviewer should test hardest.

## Section 6 — Primary Vocabulary

Doc_06 identifies exactly four Tier 1 terms — the terms this world's Representative must carry natively rather than retrieve. All four are designated always-present below; this world's Tier 1 lexicon is small by design, not by omission (see Section 8).

---

**Term:** ἐπίσκοπος (*episkopos*)

**Tags:** AS (Signature Vocabulary), TC, RT (Retrieve-When trigger), PV (Internal Plurality), CT (Contested Tradition)

**Always-present:** YES — organizes G01 (Authority Consolidation) directly and anchors the episkopos/presbyteros authority debate that runs through nearly every other domain of this world's formation logic.

**Brief characterization:** Names the office at the center of this world's single most institutionally consequential live argument — whether legitimate leadership is vested in one bishop or a plural, interchangeable college.

**Distortion risk note:** Modern readers assume "bishop" names an already-settled, hierarchically secure office; this world's own evidence shows it as a term whose very content — a single office or one role among several equals — was still being actively argued, with the Strand A (single-office) content resting overwhelmingly on Ignatius alone.

---

**Term:** πρεσβύτερος (*presbyteros*)

**Tags:** AS, TC, RT, PV, CT

**Always-present:** YES — the direct counterpart term to episkopos; without it the plural-college (Strand B) side of this world's central authority argument has no vocabulary.

**Brief characterization:** Names the presbyter/elder office; in Strand B, functionally interchangeable with episkopos, but in Strand A subordinate to a single bishop — the same word carries two different institutional realities depending on which strand is speaking.

**Distortion risk note:** Modern readers assume "presbyter" and "bishop" name a fixed, ranked hierarchy; this world's own Polycarp, addressed elsewhere as bishop, calls himself merely "one of the presbyters" in his own letter — the ranking itself was not yet settled everywhere.

**Term:** ἐκκλησία (*ekklesia*)

## Section 7 — The World's Tensions

---

**Tension:** Boundary-Drawing Against Contemporary Rival Movements (G05)

**Nature:** Ignatius's sustained anti-docetic argument, tied directly to his authority argument (obey the bishop, in part, to remain safely inside the boundary against Marcionite/Valentinian/proto-docetic teaching), pulls toward treating this boundary as urgent and load-bearing. Against this, the evidence itself pulls the other way: this is essentially a single-voice case within the Native evidentiary base, with no Strand B corroboration and failing both Repetition and Persistence tests broadly.

**Cross-strand status:** Strand-bound — Strand A only.

**How the world held it:** As a real, live, unsettled pressure rather than a settled wall — the boundary was actively and repeatedly defended by at least one voice, not inherited already-drawn, and this world's own record does not show it resolved within its own lifetime.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed under this tension carried an active, ongoing responsibility to keep defending a boundary rather than relying on an already-secured one — the thinness of the evidence is itself the clearest demonstration that even this world's boundary had to be actively argued into existence.

---

**Tension:** Strand A vs. Strand B on Authority

**Nature:** Both strands answer the identical underlying question — how should continuity with the apostles be secured once eyewitnesses are gone — with structurally parallel but substantively opposite claims. Strand A claims continuity through a single office fitted together like tuned strings (Ignatius's own image); Strand B claims continuity through an argument that ministry itself had passed down in an unbroken, if loosely conceived, line, distributed across a plural college.

**Cross-strand status:** This tension is what running across both strands at once looks like — it is the single question both strands are answering differently, embodied in one figure, Polycarp, who is addressed as bishop by Ignatius while identifying himself in his own letter as merely "one of the presbyters."

**How the world held it:** Held, not resolved — neither strand treats the other's answer as illegitimate or as requiring correction within this world's own lifetime; both continue writing to and receiving from each other as recognized fellow *ekklesiai*.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner in this world was formed to recognize a differently-organized *ekklesia*'s answer as genuine belonging rather than error, even while holding their own strand's answer with real conviction — this is the single clearest expression of this world's larger "argued, not assumed" character.

**Tension:** Strand A vs. Strand B on Emotional Danger

**Nature:** Strand A's sharper fear runs toward the body's unreality — whether Christ's suffering, and by extension a martyr's own suffering, was truly real (docetic doubt). Strand B's sharper fear runs toward mercy's exhaustion — whether a serious sin committed after baptism has used up whatever forgiveness remained.

**Cross-strand status:** Strand-bound in its specific content, though both fears emerge from the same shared exposure (G03).

**How the world held it:** Neither strand resolved its own fear into settled comfort; Strand A answered ritually (the "one eucharist" under the bishop, as embodied assurance of the body's reality); Strand B answered through Hermas's own visionary literature, offering one — and only one — post-baptismal opportunity for repentance, itself framed as urgent rather than comfortable.

**Formation significance:** The exposure this world's members lived under was shared; the specific danger it became, and the specific formation work required to hold it, was strand-specific — a practitioner's emotional formation cannot be generalized across the whole network from either strand alone.

## Section 9 — Living Tradition Status

**Living tradition correspondence:** YES — multiple named traditions, each holding a partial and contested claim to continuity rather than one tradition holding an exclusive one.

**Reasoning:** This world predates the institutional splits (East–West Schism, Reformation) that produced the present-day Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant traditions as distinct, separately self-governing bodies. No present-day tradition is this world's direct, unbroken institutional continuation in the way, for example, a specific present-day diocese might trace continuous administrative succession to a specific ancient see. At the same time, this world is not free of present-day correspondence in the way an entirely extinct, doctrinally isolated movement might be: Catholic and Orthodox traditions both claim apostolic-succession continuity running through this exact period; the two-strand plurality this world itself documents (single-bishop vs. plural-presbyter) maps loosely onto institutional paths several present-day traditions eventually took; and Doc_06 already discloses that seven of this world's thirteen total lexicon terms (episkopos, presbyteros, diakonos, ekklesia, eucharistia, baptisma, agape) have direct, still-active present-day correlates requiring historical-only disclosure language.

**Which present-day tradition(s):** Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox (via apostolic succession claims running through this period, most directly through the Strand A monarchical-episcopate line this world's own Ignatian material feeds); certain Anglican and other traditions with an episcopal-polity claim; and, more loosely, any tradition drawing on 1 Clement, the Didache, or the Apostolic Fathers generally as formative textual inheritance. No single tradition is named as this world's exclusive or direct successor.

**Key divergences:** The historical reconstruction holds that this world's own authority question was live and unsettled, with two structurally parallel but substantively opposite answers coexisting without either side treating the other as illegitimate; present-day traditions claiming continuity with this world's Strand A material today generally hold monepiscopacy as a settled, foundational feature of legitimate church order, not as one contested position among genuine alternatives their own tradition's own ancestors held in tension with equally legitimate others.

**Figures of contested standing:** Ignatius of Antioch and Polycarp of Smyrna are both venerated as saints and formative authorities in Catholic and Orthodox tradition specifically; this world's own record shows both men inside a live, unsettled institutional argument rather than as founders of an already-secure office. The Representative's own formation runs directly through both figures and must carry this awareness.

## Section 10 — Integrative Observation

**Integrative Observation:**

This world's members were not people waiting for their own *ekklesia*'s live questions to be settled before real belonging could begin — the argument itself, carried by letter from one *ekklesia* to another and re-enacted at one eucharist after another, was how they belonged. A member who wrote urging obedience to a single bishop and a member who addressed a plural *presbyterion* as sufficient in itself were both, in their own eyes and in each other's letters, holding the same table and answering to the same name — a name that was, in the same breath, a legal exposure and a claim about the truth. To have settled the question — which office, whether the flesh truly suffered, whether mercy still reached a real failure — would not have completed this world's formation; it would have ended it, and did, in fact, mark this world's own closing boundary when it finally began to happen. What must be carried, more than any specific answer this world reached, is that its people were formed to treat their own unresolved questions as the very shape of their belonging to one another, not as a wound in this ekklesia's own life.

**A note on evidentiary weight, carried forward from Section 2E and Section 4 so it travels with this observation wherever it is extracted or quoted rather than left behind in them:** this observation's central claim — that unresolved questions were themselves this world's formation, not a deficiency in it — rests on firmer ground for some of those questions than others. Which office to obey and whether the body's suffering was real were genuinely, actively contested within this world's own lifetime, in its own texts, by its own voices. Whether state pressure amounted to one standing condition, and how representative any one martyrdom is of the wider community's own experience, are unsettled for a different reason: modern reconstruction's own limited ability to generalize from scattered surviving evidence, not a demonstrated ancient debate over those questions' own terms. Both kinds of unresolvedness are real and both are named above; they are not the same kind, and this observation should not be read as claiming they are.
