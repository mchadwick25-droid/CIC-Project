## 1. Candidate identification

Atlas I.35, "The Second-Century Greek Apologists," c. 124–200 CE. Athens, Rome, Sardis, Antioch. The census's own roster (`voices` field) names Quadratus, Aristides, Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, Melito, and the author of the *Epistle to Diognetus*. A body of individuals defending Christianity's reasonableness and legality to a skeptical or hostile Greco-Roman intellectual and political culture, in the vocabulary of Greek philosophy, roughly eighty years before the Nicene settlement.

Hegesippus and Dionysius of Corinth are not part of this candidate's own roster; both belong to PAHC's own corpus map entry (`post-apostolic-house-church`), and Hegesippus is additionally cross-mapped to Alexandria, Montanism, and Novatianism. ## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

### A1 — The floor

This candidate's entire window (124–200) predates the Creed itself by more than a century, so A1 is not the operative test in the primary sense; A2 is the real gate. IJC's own confirmation does not supply a same-reasoning precedent for an A1/A2 routing choice here: IJC held that *both* A1 and A2 apply, to different phases of one movement straddling 325, expressly rejecting a forced either/or — a conclusion that does not transfer to a window that never crosses 325 at all. Nor does LPC's own cleared Step 0 run "A2 as the sole governing test for Cyprian's wholly pre-Nicene phase": LPC's actual §2 addresses a different question entirely — whether Article 3's "sufficient historical coherence" is tested by Step 0 at all — and concludes it is outside Step 0's instrument altogether, not that A2 governs a pre-Nicene phase to the exclusion of A1. No cleared or on-record confirmation in this project supplies an exact precedent for a candidate whose entire window predates 325 outright, with no straddling phase at all. This document proceeds on the plain reading of A2's own text — it tests continuity toward the strand that became Nicene orthodoxy, which is coherently askable of a wholly pre-Nicene body of writing — without borrowing false authority from either IJC's or LPC's own, differently-shaped cases.

### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears, with one figure requiring honest disclosure rather than exclusion

This heading concerns one continuity-disclosure question — Tatian's later turn — not the two figure-*sharing* questions (Justin, Tatian) that B3 below addresses. Those are different tests: A2 asks whether a figure's own trajectory diverged from the strand that became Nicene orthodoxy; B3 asks whether a figure's voice is already claimed by a different built world. Tatian happens to raise both questions; Justin raises only the second. This section is scoped to the first.

A2 tests continuity with the strand that became Nicene orthodoxy, against divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar).

**Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, and Aristides clear cleanly and strongly.** Justin's Logos theology is not merely continuous with what became Nicene Trinitarian vocabulary — it is one of its direct historical sources; Athenagoras' *Plea* and *On the Resurrection* and Theophilus' *To Autolycus* are standard proto-orthodox texts with no divergence to test for.

**Tatian requires a disclosure, not an exclusion — and the disclosure this candidate would write already exists, in a built world, and is a better model than anything drafted here.** Tatian's *Address to the Greeks* is the pupil-of-Justin apologetic work vendored here — but Irenaeus reports directly (*Against Heresies* I.28) that after Justin's martyrdom Tatian went on to found or lead the Encratite current (rejecting marriage and animal food on ascetic-dualist grounds), a genuine later divergence Irenaeus traces from Saturninus and Marcion rather than from any Phrygian or Eastern source. The wine detail sometimes attached to this description belongs to the NPNF editorial apparatus on Eusebius' *HE* IV.29 — "these Encratites were heretics who abstained from flesh, from wine, and from marriage" — not to I.28 itself, which names marriage and animal food only; *HE* IV.29 is the same locus Syriac's own source record already cites as the charge's source. The *Address* is not merely "read as predating" that later turn on an unstated consensus: the citable warrant is Syriac's own source record, quoted immediately below, stating the work was "written before the events Eusebius describes" — a real citation, carried to §4 as a binding item, on the same footing as the sibling document's Commodian dating requirement. Tatian is not merely a census-level "contested figure" precedent to imitate — he is a **built, live figure**, `records/syr/figure/syr.figure.tatian.md`, and his source record `records/syr/source/syr.source.tatian-address-to-greeks.md` already carries a section headed "THE ENCRATITE CHARGE, HANDLED HONESTLY." That section does not state plainly that the charge is real — it deliberately stops short of asserting the charge's truth, naming it as **Eusebius's** report (not Irenaeus's — Irenaeus is this document's own source, earlier in this same paragraph, for the separate claim that the Encratite current itself derives from Saturninus and Marcion) and declining to adjudicate it, while noting the *Address* itself is not a confession of it and that the work predates the events Eusebius describes. Any Doc_02 disclosure this candidate writes should be built to the same standard that record already sets, not invented independently — and see A5 below and §3 B3 for the more consequential fact this same discovery exposes: Tatian is not only a disclosure question, he is a second figure-sharing question of the same kind as Justin's.

**A roster asymmetry worth naming rather than leaving implicit.** Tatian does not appear on Atlas I.35's own census `voices` field at all — the census names seven figures (Quadratus, Aristides, Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, Melito, and the *Diognetus* author) and mentions Tatian only inside the census's own `sourcing` prose. Tatian's presence in this document rests entirely on the corpus map's own assignment of the *Address to the Greeks*, not on the census's own roster-defining field. This does not disqualify the disclosure or the ruling above — the corpus map is the operative assignment instrument for B1/B3 purposes — but Doc_01 should treat Tatian's status as a corpus-map/sourcing question, not a census-roster question, when it resolves the binding items below, since the two carry different weight in this project's own practice.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Tatian's specific and bounded caveat carried forward as a binding Doc_02 disclosure (§4), modeled on Syriac's own existing treatment rather than drafted from scratch.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This candidate's own defining activity is arguing directly against named rivals — Justin against Marcion by name (1 Apol. 26, 58) and at length against Judaism's own claim on the Scriptures (the whole of the *Dialogue with Trypho*).

A5's full text, both sentences, governs how Marcion is handled here: *"A movement's historical rivals — movements it anathematized or was anathematized by — are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under this section. Per Article 23, they appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates."* The first sentence scopes the rule to *this candidate's own Section A pass* — it does not separately re-test a rival's own eligibility here. The second sentence does not say rivals may never have been screened elsewhere; it says how they appear when they are relevant to an including world's own reconstruction. Marcion was, in fact, already screened in his own right: census I.20, `Excluded - Doctrinal Floor (C1)`, whose own status note reads "Exclusion is not a judgment of unimportance." IJC's own confirmation states the same fact pattern correctly (Homoian Christianity "was already tested and excluded at the portfolio level... **as its own candidate world** — a floor effect, not a judgment of unimportance"). Marcion belongs inside this candidate's own reconstruction as Justin's named opponent, per Article 23, using exactly 1 Apol. 26 and 58 — though see §3 B3 below for the fact that PAHC's own records already cite both passages for the identical purpose, which narrows what is genuinely unclaimed here.

Trypho is a different case. Article 20 does not apply (it governs the suppressed *within* a community, not external opponents) — but Article 23 does apply, in full: "The Representative speaks about the world's opponents as the world understood them — honestly, without rehabilitating them into modern equals and without modern editorializing." Rabbinic Judaism as Justin's interlocutor is exactly the kind of external-opponent material Article 23 governs, and PAHC has already gone further than a general caution: `pahc.source.justin-dialogue.md` carries a named Doc_01 "Ways-That-Never-Parted" reading caution specifically for this material. Doc_02 should build on that standard, not invent a lighter one. There is also a further, real adjacency: Aristo of Pella's fragments are **triple**-assigned in the corpus map — to `greek-apologists-second-century`, `post-apostolic-house-church`, and `ebionite-nazoraean-current` (census I.24, `Contested — Evidentiary`) — on the map's own admission that "the Jewish-Christian current and pahc are both defensible; neither is demonstrated by the fragments." The *Dialogue*'s own corpus-map entry separately notes that its chapter 47 discussion of Torah-observant Christians is "one of the era's few direct witnesses to the ebionite-nazoraean-current material." Both are carried to §4 below.

None of Hegesippus, Dionysius of Corinth, Rhodon, Polycrates, Serapion, or Apollonius is on this candidate's own roster: direct inspection of `cic/corpus-map/greek-apologists-second-century.yaml` shows they are PAHC's, not this candidate's, and PAHC's own source record (`pahc.source.second-third-century-remains.md`) already runs a careful per-author disposition of that fragment collection. What the corpus map actually contains, and what genuinely does raise a live Article 21 question, is narrower and different in kind: three works transmitted under Justin's name and flagged `confidence: provisional`; Aristo of Pella's fragments (triple-assigned, as above); and the Ambrose *hypomnemata addressed to the Greeks*, itself `provisional` on a date question and dual-assigned with Syriac Christianity. Of the three pseudonymous Justin works, the note "transmitted under Justin, widely doubted" belongs specifically to the *Hortatory Address to the Greeks* and *On the Sole Government of God* ("same position as the *Discourse*"); the *Discourse to the Greeks* itself carries its own, differently-worded note — "printed under the JUSTIN MARTYR div1; authenticity has long been questioned... filed with its transmitted author per §6.2 - derive, don't assert - with the doubt recorded." Direct inspection of the corpus map entry (`ambrose-hypomnemata`) shows no connection between the Ambrose *hypomnemata* and Quadratus at all — the work is transmitted under the name Ambrose, dated as Greek apologetic surviving only in Syriac translation, and its `provisional` status concerns only its own undated composition, not any claim about Quadratus. The real Doc_01 question is whether these five items belong in this candidate's own figure roster as-is, or whether the pseudonymous-Justin works in particular should be held apart from Justin's own genuine corpus — a question that stands on its own regardless of Justin's own native status, which the ruling in §3 B3 below settles.

**Strand Determination (Article 21), Tatian specifically.** Tatian's own dual claim (pahc's disclosed Justin-pupil-in-Rome phase against Syriac's own claim on him as "the man who wove the four Gospels into the one story these churches read for two hundred years") is not resolved by this document and is carried to §3 B3 and §4 as a second binding figure question, alongside Justin's.

### B2 — Ecology

Real and uneven, in a specific and checkable way. The apologetic-argument register (engaging Greek philosophy, rebutting the atheism/cannibalism/sedition charges, arguing Christian ethics as superior) is exceptionally strong — this is what the genre *is*. Persecution and its stakes are load-bearing throughout (Justin's own martyrdom; the genre's whole occasion is legal jeopardy). Worship-practice texture is thinner than a typical built world's for a reason that must be stated precisely (see §3 B3 immediately below for what, specifically, is and is not already claimed by PAHC): some of the material a builder might reach for first — Justin's own baptism and Sunday-worship description, 1 Apol. 61 and 65–67 — is already the basis of PAHC's own worship-practice ecology. A build of this candidate should not re-derive that specific lens from that specific passage; its own worship-practice picture will correspondingly lean on what remains, and Doc_05 must name that honestly. Ordinary lay formation, women's voices, and enslaved persons' own testimony are essentially absent — the apologetic genre itself selects for classically-trained male rhetoricians writing to power, more narrowly than PAHC's own noted skew.

**Passes B2**, with the worship-practice thinness named as a structural, disclosed limitation.

Augustine is not precedent for "a single figure serving two Representatives through two different projects": checked directly, `records/hal/figure/hal.figure.augustine.md` states outright that Augustine is "STRICTLY AN OUTSIDE VOICE: Augustine belongs to his own (not-yet-built) world; this record draws on him ONLY as the other side of the correspondence" — and Donatism's own Step 0 places him among that world's named *opponents* (Article 23 material), reconstructed from Donatism's own perspective, not as a second native voice. The project has therefore actually built three distinct models for a figure or text touching more than one world: **outside voice** (Augustine in HAL), **opponent** (Augustine in Donatism), and **co-ownership with attribution held open** (Antony in Alexandria and Desert) — plus, separately, a ruling recorded in `cic/corpus-map/latin-apologists.yaml`, which states a fourth, different mechanism plainly: **"THE RULING SPLITS THE AUTHOR, WHICH IS WHAT `per work` MEANS"** — one author, different works, different worlds. That fourth mechanism is unavailable for Justin as currently recorded: PAHC's own figure record claims `locus: whole work` on both his *First Apology* and *Dialogue*, not a per-work split.

**Four options were drawn up from the models the project has actually built (above).** (a) Justin native to PAHC, held here only as a declared outside/boundary voice; (b) exclude Justin from this candidate's own figure roster entirely; (c) narrow PAHC's own `locus: whole work` claim to enable a genuine per-work split on the **`per work` model**; (d) co-hold Justin as native to both worlds on the **Antony model**, attribution held open.
