### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears, with one figure requiring honest disclosure rather than exclusion

This heading concerns one continuity-disclosure question — Tatian's later turn — not the two figure-*sharing* questions (Justin, Tatian) that B3 below addresses. Those are different tests: A2 asks whether a figure's own trajectory diverged from the strand that became Nicene orthodoxy; B3 asks whether a figure's voice is already claimed by a different built world. Tatian happens to raise both questions; Justin raises only the second. This section is scoped to the first.

A2 tests continuity with the strand that became Nicene orthodoxy, against divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, Ebionite, or similar).

**Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, and Aristides clear cleanly and strongly.** Justin's Logos theology is not merely continuous with what became Nicene Trinitarian vocabulary — it is one of its direct historical sources; Athenagoras' *Plea* and *On the Resurrection* and Theophilus' *To Autolycus* are standard proto-orthodox texts with no divergence to test for.

**Tatian requires a disclosure, not an exclusion.** Tatian's *Address to the Greeks* is the pupil-of-Justin apologetic work vendored here. Irenaeus reports (*Against Heresies* I.28.1) that Tatian "was a hearer of Justin's, and as long as he continued with him he expressed no such views; but after his martyrdom he separated from the Church." What he then taught, and the exact wording of the charge, are quoted in §4 item 3. The question for A2 is narrower: does the *Address* itself come before that turn or after it? The Syriac world's source record for the *Address* (`records/syr/source/syr.source.tatian-address-to-greeks.md`, lines 46–50) says the work was "written before the events Eusebius describes." That is a real citation, and this section tests it rather than repeating it. Tatian is also a **built, live figure**: `records/syr/figure/syr.figure.tatian.md`, with a source record whose section headed "THE ENCRATITE CHARGE, HANDLED HONESTLY" is the model for any Doc_02 disclosure (§4 item 3). See A5 below and §3 B3 for the more consequential fact this exposes: Tatian is not only a disclosure question, he is a second figure-sharing question of the same kind as Justin's.

**Dating the *Address*: the ancient evidence.** No ancient source dates the *Address*. Line numbers are those of the vendored files as on disk (`anf01` = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml`; `anf02` = `anf02_hermas-tatian-athenagoras-theophilus-clement-alexandria.xml`; `npnf201` = `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml`; `npnf203` = `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`).

- **Irenaeus, *AH* I.28.1** (`anf01`, 33656–33675). The break with the Church came "after his martyrdom", that is, after Justin's. Irenaeus gives no year for it and does not mention the *Address*.
- **Eusebius, *HE* IV.16.1–9** (`npnf201`, 23744–24077). Eusebius quotes *Address* 18–19 (24065, 24075) and says Justin died "in consequence of a plot laid against him by Crescens." McGiffert's note on IV.16.1 (23760–23782) says "The date of his martyrdom is very difficult to determine" and that Rusticus became prefect of Rome in 163. His note on the *Address* passage (23877–24034) observes that "Tatian puts himself on a level with Justin as the object of these machinations". IV.16 therefore does not date the *Address*.
- **Eusebius, *Chronicle*, 12th year of Marcus Aurelius (172)**, as quoted by McGiffert (`npnf201`, 26215–26222): "*Tatianus hæreticus agnoscitur, a quo Encratitæ.*" McGiffert sees "no reason to doubt that this represents with reasonable accuracy the date of Tatian's break with the Catholic Church." The *Chronicle* itself was not read; the 172 date rests on McGiffert's quotation.
- **McGiffert following Harnack (1882)** (`npnf201`, 26215–26222, 26493–26494): the *Address* was "probably written after Tatian had left Rome for the first time, but not long after his conversion", about 152–153. This is a modern reconstruction, not ancient evidence.
- **Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 29** (`npnf203`, 40084–40098). Tatian was "a follower of Justin Martyr" who founded a heresy; he "flourished in the reign of Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus". The gloss "Born about 130, died after 172" is the NPNF editor's.
- **The ANF02 introductory note** (`anf02`, 5528, 5636–5657) is headed "[a.d. 110-172.]" and says the sect was "established about a.d. 166". It is editorial and gives no source, so it is not evidence.

**Dating the *Address*: the text itself** (`anf02`). Chapter 4 (5823): "Does the sovereign order the payment of tribute, I am ready to render it" — one ruler (Zahn reads the Greek as singular; the Greek was not checked here, UNVERIFIED). Chapter 18 (6526): "the most admirable Justin", with no "martyr" and no "late", so ambiguous. Chapter 19 (6548–6554): philosophers "receive from the king of the Romans 600 aurei yearly", and Crescens "endeavoured to inflict on Justin, and indeed on me, the punishment of death" — one king, Crescens's attempt in the past, Justin's death not mentioned. Chapter 25 (6820): "like Proteus", with the ANF footnote "The Cynic Peregrinus is meant"; that identification is editorial, Peregrinus burned himself at Olympia in 165 (per Zahn, quoting the *Chronicle*), and Tatian says nothing of that death. Chapter 35 (7252): "when sojourning in the city of the Romans, I inspected the multiplicity of statues" — past tense, so probably not written at Rome. A search of lines 5700–7500 for *marri\**, *wedlock*, *abstain\** and *wine* finds no prohibition of marriage or wine in the *Address*.

**Dating the *Address*: public-domain scholarship.** Read from the Internet Archive copies named below; none is vendored. Page numbers are the running heads in the OCR.

- **Otto 1851**, vendored (`tatian_oratio-ad-graecos-grc-lat_otto1851.txt`, 1374–1375): composed at Rome "paullo post Iustini Martyris mortem" (a little after Justin's death).
- **Zahn 1881** (*Forschungen zur Geschichte des neutestamentlichen Kanons* I, pp. 275–280; a Google Books scan, not vendored): the *Address* is Tatian's first Christian writing, written soon after his conversion. The singular "king" excludes the joint reign, so it falls before March 161, "etwa um 150", perhaps at Rome.
- **Harnack 1882** (as reported by McGiffert, `npnf201`, 26224–26233, 26493–26494): 152–153, written in the East.
- **Harnack 1897** (*Chronologie* I, `b1geschichtederalt02harn`, pp. 284–288): before Justin's death, "nicht später als c. 155", written outside Rome; the break with the Church in March 172. He leaves a later date open ("nicht zwingend … aber wahrscheinlich ist sie nicht").
- **Bardenhewer 1908** (*Patrology*, `patrologyliveswo00bardrich`, pp. 57–58): published "probably outside Rome (c. 35), and about 165, when Justin had already passed away (cc. 18. 19)"; the break "probably in 172". His bibliography note says Harnack, in *Geschichte der altchristlichen Litteratur* II.1, 284 ff., "has more or less completely withdrawn his earlier views concerning the date of Tatian."
- **Puech 1912** (*Les apologistes grecs*, `lesapologistesgr00puec`, p. 151): Justin was already dead; the *Address* dates from c. 170–172, "dans les deux ou trois années antérieures" to the 172 schism. He rejects Harnack's c. 155.
- **Rendel Harris 1890** (*The Diatessaron of Tatian*, `diatessaronoftat00harr`, pp. 2–3) gives no date for the *Address*. He holds that Tatian's fellowship with Justin was unbroken "up to the close of Justin's earthly career" and that the Encratite turn came "after his return to the East".
- Overbeck, Funk, Hilgenfeld and Möller argue a later date (named in Harnack 1897, p. 285 n. 1; not read). Schanz, Schaff, and all post-1930 scholarship were not consulted: UNVERIFIED context only.

**Judgment, in three claims, each tagged.**

1. **Absolute date of the *Address*: c. 150–172 CE. Contested.** Three public-domain schools disagree: before Justin's death in the 150s (Zahn, Harnack 1882 and 1897); just after it, c. 165 (Otto, Bardenhewer); c. 169–172 (Puech).
2. **The *Address* comes before Tatian's break with the Church: Dominant Modern Reconstruction.** Every public-domain scholar read places it so, and the text contains no marriage or wine prohibition. No ancient source states the order, so this is an inference and is not tagged Documented.
3. **The *Address* relative to Justin's martyrdom (c. 165): Contested.**

Best-supported minimal wording for Doc_01 and Doc_02: "written after his conversion at Rome and, on the dominant reading, before his break with the Church (c. 172); its date relative to Justin's death is disputed."

**What the Syriac record says, and what the evidence supports.** The record's sentence sits inside its paragraph headed "THE ENCRATITE CHARGE, HANDLED HONESTLY" (`syr.source.tatian-address-to-greeks.md`, lines 46–52): the *Address* "is an apology addressed to Greeks, written before the events Eusebius describes". The paragraph is about the Encratite charge, and the companion figure record cites "Eusebius IV.29" for it (`syr.figure.tatian.md`, lines 38–39). Nothing in `records/syr/` mentions Justin's death or *HE* IV.16. Read in its own paragraph, then, "the events Eusebius describes" are the Encratite events, and the record does not claim anything about Justin's death. So read, the claim is supported: Irenaeus puts the break with the Church after Justin's martyrdom, the *Chronicle* gives 172, and every public-domain scholar read places the *Address* earlier (claim 2 above). The evidence supports it as a Dominant Modern Reconstruction, not as Documented; the record tags the source `formation_confidence: Documented`, which fits what it says about the text of the *Address* and is stronger than a relative-date claim can carry. Out of its paragraph the clause could be misread as also covering Justin's death; on that question the evidence is Contested (claim 3). The record's other defect is its accuser: "Eusebius accuses him of it". The earliest source for the charge is Irenaeus, whom Eusebius quotes (§4 item 3). Neither record is edited by this build. The Library decision log's entry "Correction: the Syriac Tatian record's clause is about the Encratite charge, not Justin's death" records the same reading and supersedes the earlier slate entry's word "over-broad".

**A roster asymmetry worth naming rather than leaving implicit.** Tatian does not appear on Atlas I.35's own census `voices` field at all — the census names seven figures (Quadratus, Aristides, Justin, Athenagoras, Theophilus, Melito, and the *Diognetus* author) and mentions Tatian only inside the census's own `sourcing` prose. Tatian's presence in this document rests entirely on the corpus map's own assignment of the *Address to the Greeks*, not on the census's own roster-defining field. This does not disqualify the disclosure or the ruling above — the corpus map is the operative assignment instrument for B1/B3 purposes — but Doc_01 should treat Tatian's place in this roster as resting on the corpus map, not on the census roster; the two carry different weight in this project's own practice.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Tatian's specific and bounded caveat carried forward as a binding Doc_02 disclosure (§4), modeled on Syriac's own existing treatment rather than drafted from scratch.

### A5 — Interaction with Articles 20, 21, and 23

This candidate's own defining activity is arguing directly against named rivals — Justin against Marcion by name (1 Apol. 26, 58) and at length against Judaism's own claim on the Scriptures (the whole of the *Dialogue with Trypho*).

A5's full text, both sentences, governs how Marcion is handled here: *"A movement's historical rivals — movements it anathematized or was anathematized by — are not separately assessed for inclusion as their own worlds under this section. Per Article 23, they appear, where relevant, as an included world's own opponents, within that world's own reconstruction, not as independently screened candidates."* The first sentence scopes the rule to *this candidate's own Section A pass* — it does not separately re-test a rival's own eligibility here. The second sentence does not say rivals may never have been screened elsewhere; it says how they appear when they are relevant to an including world's own reconstruction. Marcion was, in fact, already screened in his own right: census I.20, `Excluded - Doctrinal Floor (C1)`, whose own status note reads "Exclusion is not a judgment of unimportance." IJC's own confirmation states the same fact pattern correctly (Homoian Christianity "was already tested and excluded at the portfolio level... **as its own candidate world** — a floor effect, not a judgment of unimportance"). Marcion belongs inside this candidate's own reconstruction as Justin's named opponent, per Article 23, using exactly 1 Apol. 26 and 58 — though see §3 B3 below for the fact that PAHC's own records already cite both passages for the identical purpose, which narrows what is genuinely unclaimed here.

Trypho is a different case. Article 20 does not apply (it governs the suppressed *within* a community, not external opponents) — but Article 23 does apply, in full: "The Representative speaks about the world's opponents as the world understood them — honestly, without rehabilitating them into modern equals and without modern editorializing." Rabbinic Judaism as Justin's interlocutor is exactly the kind of external-opponent material Article 23 governs, and PAHC has already gone further than a general caution: `pahc.source.justin-dialogue.md` carries a named Doc_01 "Ways-That-Never-Parted" reading caution specifically for this material. Doc_02 should build on that standard, not invent a lighter one. There is also a further, real adjacency: Aristo of Pella's fragments are **triple**-assigned in the corpus map — to `greek-apologists-second-century`, `post-apostolic-house-church`, and `ebionite-nazoraean-current` (census I.24, `Contested — Evidentiary`) — on the map's own admission that "the Jewish-Christian current and pahc are both defensible; neither is demonstrated by the fragments." The *Dialogue*'s own corpus-map entry separately notes that its chapter 47 discussion of Torah-observant Christians is "one of the era's few direct witnesses to the ebionite-nazoraean-current material." Both are carried to §4 below.

None of Hegesippus, Dionysius of Corinth, Rhodon, Polycrates, Serapion, or Apollonius is on this candidate's own roster: direct inspection of `cic/corpus-map/greek-apologists-second-century.yaml` shows they are PAHC's, not this candidate's, and PAHC's own source record (`pahc.source.second-third-century-remains.md`) already runs a careful per-author disposition of that fragment collection. What the shelf actually contains, and what genuinely does raise a live Article 21 question, is narrower and different in kind: three works transmitted under Justin's name and flagged `confidence: provisional`; Aristo of Pella's fragments (triple-assigned, as above); and the Ambrose *hypomnemata addressed to the Greeks*, `provisional` on a date question. **The window slate.** On the work-by-work slate shown to him, Mark ruled (LIBRARY-DECISION-LOG.md, "Window slate for the Greek and Latin Apologists approved"): "yes, approve the slate and proceed".
