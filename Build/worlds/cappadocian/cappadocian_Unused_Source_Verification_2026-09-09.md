## 1. Candidate 1 — CTh 16.1.3, the imperial communion law of 381

### 1.1 What was verified, and how

The Latin text was read directly in two independent public-domain witnesses: **Mommsen–Meyer 1905** (*Theodosiani libri XVI*, book 16, at the rubric `XVI, 1, 3 (381 Iul. 30)`, with its critical apparatus) and **the Latin Library's plain text** of the Code (at the label `CTh.16.1.3`). Both carry an identical clause and an identical bishop list. An independent English-language discussion, **W. K. Boyd, *The Ecclesiastical Edicts of the Theodosian Code* (1905)**, was also read at its own citation of this law (p. 46 n. 1).

The law, addressed *ad Auxonium proconsulem Asiae*, orders that all churches be handed to bishops who confess Father, Son and Holy Spirit of one majesty and power, of the same glory, one splendour — and it identifies those bishops **by naming whose communion they must be in**:

> …quos constabit communioni Nectari episcopi Constantinopolitanae ecclesiae nec non Timothei intra Aegyptum Alexandrinae urbis episcopi esse sociatos; quos etiam in Orientis partibus Pelagio episcopo Laodicensi et Diodoro episcopo Tarsensi: in Asia nec non proconsulari adque Asiana dioecesi **Amphilochio episcopo Iconiensi** et Optimo episcopo Antiocheno: in Pontica dioecesi **Helladio episc. Caesariensi** et **Otreio Meliteno** et **Gregorio episc. Nysseno**, Terennio episc. Scythiae, Marmario episc. Marcianopolitano communicare constiterit.

Those who dissent from the communion of the men "whom the special enumeration has expressed" are to be expelled from the churches as manifest heretics.

### 1.2 Three corrections to the finding as it was handed over

1. **The vendorings do not exist on the default branch.** The three files named — `codex-theodosianus_latinlibrary.txt`, `theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt`, and `boyd_ecclesiastical-edicts-theodosian-code_1905.txt` — are **not in `cic/texts/` on `main`**. They exist only on the unmerged branch `donatism-lpc-integration`, which is where this verification read them. Source Registry row 79's own statement ("the Mommsen–Meyer Latin text is public domain but not acquired into `cic/texts/` this session") is therefore still accurate as written. No builder working from the default branch alone can open this text today.
2. **The law names eleven bishops, not four.** Nectarius of Constantinople and Timothy of Alexandria head the list; Pelagius of Laodicea and Diodore of Tarsus stand for Oriens; Amphilochius of Iconium and Optimus for Asiana; Helladius of Caesarea, Otreius of Melitene, Gregory of Nyssa, Terennius of Scythia and Marmarius of Marcianopolis for Pontica. Four of the eleven belong to this world's own circle. Doc_02 §2's existing phrasing — "naming, **among** the East's touchstone bishops" — is the accurate one and needed no change; the discovery pass's framing ("names… *by name* as the legal standard for who counts as orthodox… in the empire") overstates it in two directions at once, since the instrument is also addressed to one proconsul for the eastern dioceses and names no western bishop at all.
3. **This is not unused material.** The law is already carried at Doc_01 §4a, Doc_02 §2/§7/§9, Source Registry rows 79 and 80, `records/cappadocian/source/cappadocian.source.imperial-communion-law-of-381.md`, and — the point that matters most for the structural question — **Doc_04's own forces notation for the situational gravity**, which already reads the Theodosian settlement as the force that ends this world by fulfilling its central fight (§3.3, first and eighth entries).

### 1.3 The Doc_04 judgment: supplemental, and the classification question left open on both sides

Doc_04 classifies **Gravity 3, "the contested church under the contested empire," as Primary with a situational annotation** (candidate 8 at §1), and carries at Open Item 1 the standing alternative that it should be **Supporting**. The question put to this pass was whether imperial legal recognition, by name, of these bishops should move that classification.

**The elevation case fails.** The Formation test is the only place Doc_04's own reasoning locates the doubt about this gravity — §3.1 calls its moderation "the decisive moderation," and §3.2 and §4 both name it as the reason the Supporting alternative is flagged at all. (That is not the same as saying classification is a Formation-score threshold; the Framework's classification is a whole-battery judgment. What is true is narrower: for *this* candidate, with the other five tests already strong, the Formation score is where the question actually sits.) It is moderated to *moderate* at §3.1 on the reasoning that the ordinary Christian's becoming ran through household teaching, baptism, psalmody, festival, brotherhood and alms — instruments *conditioned* by the imperial contest but not *constituted* by it. The other five tests are already **strong**, so corroboration cannot raise them, and CTh 16.1.3 is an instrument of recognition and property rather than a formation instrument. Nothing here argues for Primary being *more* secure than it was.

**The downgrade case is the one that was actually open, and the first draft of this document never asked it.** The independent review caught that, and it is right: Doc_04 Open Item 1 asks whether Gravity 3 should be *Supporting*, and the "recognition and property, not formation" reading is itself a downgrade argument. If this gravity's culminating documentary act touches no formation practice, that is evidence it organizes the world's *situation* rather than its *formation* — which is the substance of the Supporting case. So the honest finding is:

> **CTh 16.1.3 makes the Supporting case marginally stronger, and is not close to decisive. Open Item 1 stays open, and this pass does not close it.**

Two reasons it is not decisive. First, Doc_04's "situational annotation within Primary" exists precisely to hold "organizes the situation, not the formation instruments" *while* keeping Primary on the strength of the other five tests. A fact that sharpens "this is situational" therefore lands inside the annotation as easily as inside a reclassification, and one imperial constitution cannot adjudicate between two readings that both already accommodate it. Second, Open Item 1 is explicitly "flagged for external review" in Doc_04's own text — it is not a build thread's to close from inside the build, in either direction.

**The baptism-and-festival objection, engaged rather than routed around.** The review's sharpest point: two of the six formation instruments the Formation moderation rests on — baptism and festival — happen in the buildings this law reassigns, administered by clergy whose communion it makes the test. Does that make the contest *constitutive*? On the evidence: no, but by less margin than Doc_04 §3.1's phrasing implies. What the law changes is the *personnel and the premises* — who baptizes, who preaches at the panegyris, under whose communion the basilica is held. The baptism, the psalmody and the feast happen either way; the plateau's formation does not wait on the proconsul's letter. That is conditioning, in Doc_04's own sense. But it is conditioning that reaches directly into the sites and ministers of two of the six instruments, which is a firmer form of the word than "conditioned by the contest" suggests, and it is part of why the Supporting case gets marginally stronger rather than not moving at all. **Recorded as a finding for whoever takes up Open Item 1, not resolved here.**

**The Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, re-run because its input changed.** The `cic-gravity-index` skill requires that the Cross-Check be applied to every Primary classification specifically. Gravity 3's Cross-Check (§3.2) rates its evidence *Documented*, under Doc_04 §9 Open Item 9's blanket caveat that "nothing in this build has been verified against editions or external scholarship." **That caveat is not wholly true, and this pass is not the first thing to make it untrue** — 62 records under `records/cappadocian/` already carry `verification_state: verified-direct`, some since 2026-08-31, so Open Item 9 has been stale for over a week and this pass merely adds to the pile. What is new here is narrower: an instrument *of this particular gravity* has now been checked against two Latin editions and an independent English discussion. Re-running it honestly means saying what it can and cannot show. The Cross-Check compares organizing strength against evidential confidence on the project's fixed five-level vocabulary. Gravity 3's evidential confidence was *Documented* and remains *Documented*: edition-verification of one instrument makes the grounding firmer within that level, it does not move the level, and the five-level vocabulary has no finer gradation to move it to. Organizing strength is unchanged. So the two still do not diverge and no flag is raised — **but that outcome was not in doubt, and it would be dishonest to present it as a test that could have failed.** What the re-run actually establishes is only that the changed input does not disturb the existing finding. **Outcome: no change, and Gravity 3 remains eligible for Primary on the evidence axis.** What does need recording is that **Doc_04 §9 Open Item 9 — "nothing in this build has been verified against editions or external scholarship" — is simply out of date**, and has been since well before this pass; it should be narrowed by whoever next revises Doc_04.

**Disposition: supplemental. Doc_04 is not revised here, and no gravity classification changes — but two items go back to Doc_04's own open list** (Open Item 1 marginally re-weighted toward Supporting without being resolved; Open Item 9's systemic caveat now inaccurate in one respect). Under the `cic-build-cycle` discipline both belong to **escalation category 4** — a finding that cuts against, or re-weights, an earlier decision the pipeline cannot close on its own — and are escalated rather than actioned.

### 1.4 The one new evidential fact, stated smaller than the first draft claimed

Because the law is an imperial chancery document rather than a text this world's circle wrote, it is **external, non-circle attestation that four of this world's own men — two of them primary voices — held publicly recognized authority in their own lifetimes.**

The first draft credited that against **Doc_04 Open Item 2**, "the circle-versus-world exposure." The independent review rejected that, and the rejection holds. Open Item 2 is a **representativeness** problem — whether the circle's surviving record stands for the world beyond the circle — and an imperial list naming four circle members confirms only that the circle was prominent, which the exposure already presupposes. If anything it *tightens* the exposure: a group written into the empire's own communion test is exactly the kind of unusually well-placed group whose record is least likely to represent the plateau. Open Item 2 also names Gravities 6 and 8 as where the exposure bites, and this law bears on neither. **The Open Item 2 attribution is withdrawn**, here and from the compiled source record that carried it. What the attestation is genuinely good for is narrower and worth keeping: external confirmation of the circle's public standing, from outside the circle's own record.

## 4. Candidate 5 — Macrina the Elder

**Verified, with three corrections and one addition.**

Both letters Doc_02 §1.4 names are present in `cic/texts/npnf208_basil-letters-select-works.xml` and both carry the datum. **Epistle 204** (printed "Letter CCIV.", with "Placed in 375." and "To the Neocæsareans." as adjacent apparatus rather than one printed line; element id `ix.ccv` — the printed-vs-`div` off-by-one this build has already recorded once, checked again rather than assumed), section 6, is the substantive one. **Epistle 223** (`ix.ccxxiv`) carries the shorter corroborating form.

**A translation fact found only by reading the letter's own prefatory note:** this volume prints Letter CCIV as a braid of two English versions, and states that "the passages in brackets are Newman's version." The Macrina passage falls wholly inside one such bracketed stretch. So it is Basil's own text in **J. H. Newman's** rendering, not the NPNF translator's. A translation question, not an authenticity one — but not the sort of thing to discover after quoting it.

**Epistle 210 is a stronger third witness than the first draft allowed.** `ix.ccxi`, "To the notables of Neocæsarea," carries both halves of the datum *separately*: its unnumbered opening section has the upbringing ("here I was brought up by my grandmother," with the volume's endnote supplying "Macrina, at her residence at Annesi"), and its §3 independently carries the Thaumaturgan transmission as sharply as anywhere in the corpus — the Neocaesarean movement is disloyal "to the tradition of Gregory the truly great, and of his successors up to the blessed Musonius, whose teaching is still ringing in your ears" — followed by a discussion of Gregory's own statement of faith cross-referring to Ep. 204 itself. What Ep. 210 does *not* do is join the two: Macrina is not named as the link there, and that join is what makes the datum load-bearing. The first draft said 210 "establishes the upbringing but not the transmission," which understates a source this pass had just read. Corrected.
