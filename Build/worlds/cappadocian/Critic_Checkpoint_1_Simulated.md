### Finding 1 — SYSTEMIC, HIGHEST WEIGHT: the entire build rests on unverified model memory
Every primary and secondary citation in Doc_02 through Doc_08 was produced from the builder's internal knowledge, with no verification against editions, databases, or web sources during the run. The sources named are genuine and identifiable (no invented authors or works detected on re-read; passage-level loci were deliberately kept sparse), but several **specific factual claims that carry structural weight are plausible-from-memory, not checked**: the imperial communion law of 381 naming Helladius of Caesarea, Gregory of Nyssa, and Otreius of Melitene; Eunomius' Cappadocian birth; the Forty-of-Sebaste relics at the family estate shrine; the "koinōnikon zōon" phrase's locus in the Asketikon; the Gangra canon-content summary; the famine's dating to c. 368/9; the detail-texture of the 372 provincial division. All are believed sound; none is verified. Anti-fabrication was held at "no invented sources"; it cannot be claimed at "every claim verified." Disposition: every passage-level and named-detail claim must be independently verified before any external, participant-facing, or scholarly use. This is the build's largest single caveat and must appear in the final report unsoftened.

### Finding 2 — WORLD-IDENTIFICATION RISK, deeper than the Early Latin analogue: is "the world" actually "the circle"?
Early Latin faced an Augustine-dominance problem inside an evidently broad world (councils, two communions, four provinces of evidence). This build's problem is structurally worse: three authors, one family, one friendship network constitute nearly the entire evidence base, and Doc_01 asserts a *regional formation world* around them. The mitigations are real but modest — the canonical letters (village discipline), Gangra read against the grain (the radicals' actual practices), Eunomius' surviving Apology, the embedded questions in the Rules, the festal-crowd asides — and the build uses all of them. But a hostile reviewer could still argue that "Nicene-Cappadocian formation world" is an extrapolation from one household's library, and that the honest object of reconstruction is "the Basilian circle and what can be glimpsed around it." The build's own transmission analysis (Doc_08 2B-2/3B-2) supplies the strongest statement of this problem — which is honesty, but not a solution. Disposition: carried as the build's standing identification risk; Doc_01's world-coherence claim (Widely Accepted) should be a named target for external scholarly review — a Cappadocia specialist should judge whether the regional-world framing overreaches the circle-centric evidence. The Representative construction must not compound this by making the voice a circle-member in disguise.

### Finding 3 — OVER-UNIFICATION RISK: the eusebeia synthesis and the "refusal to grasp" epigram
Doc_06 §5 flags "one seamless posture" as a construction synthesis; Doc_07 then builds the Integrative Observation on it ("reverence is the trained refusal to grasp"), unifying dogmatics, economics, office, and grief under one scheme. The epigram is rhetorically strong and each limb is separately evidenced — but eusebeia is common Greek Christian (indeed common Greek) vocabulary, and a reviewer could charge that the build has dressed period koine as a world-signature and then made the costume load-bearing. The defense on the record (the cross-genre accusation-pattern: asebeia charged against both Eunomius and the hoarders) is genuine evidence, but it is a *usage observation*, not a demonstrated organizing structure. Disposition: carried; the Integrative Observation is flagged for reviewer stress-testing in Doc_07 §6 (confirmed present); the Representative's voice should treat the unification as its lived instinct, not recite it as a doctrine — and validation testing should probe whether the voice over-systematizes.

### Finding 4 — TEMPLATE-ECHO RISK: convergence with the Early Latin build's solution-shapes
Three structural moves in this build mirror Early Latin's: the situational-Primary annotation (Gravity 3), the persecution-inheritance-transposed pattern (martyr memory), and the ending-by-transformation window. Doc_04 discloses the first convergence explicitly and claims evidence-first ordering; the disclosure is honest. But the risk is real and systemic: a second world built by the same builder in the same methodology may converge on the first world's *shapes* rather than discover this world's own — and the reader of both builds will notice that even the document rhetoric ("the world's own weather," transposition language) rhymes. Substantive differences that argue against mere echo: this world's situational gravity dissolves by *victory* not collapse; its martyr memory is fresh-and-familial not century-old-and-civic; its coercion asymmetry runs the opposite direction. Disposition: carried as a standing risk with the counter-evidence named; the genuine Critic pass should specifically test whether any Cappadocian finding exists *only* because its Early Latin counterpart existed.

### Finding 5 — OUTLIER-INFLATION RISK: the anti-slaveholding homily
Nyssen's fourth homily on Ecclesiastes appears in Doc_02, Doc_05, and inside Tier 1 entry eikōn (Doc_06 §8). Each appearance carries the "outlier protest, not the world's policy" caveat — checked and confirmed present. But frequency itself is emphasis: a text with no documented in-world reception history appears three times in a bounded build, which reflects the modern builder's (and modern scholarship's) enthusiasm more than the world's own weighting. The world's actual documented relation to slavery is the Asketikon's amelioration-and-return counsel and a slave-exporting regional economy. Disposition: partially resolved in place — Doc_06 §8's Distortion Risk section already warns against reading the world as abolitionist; carried constraint: the Representative must know the homily exists without wielding it as the world's self-image, and the Facilitation Brief must name the temptation.

### Finding 7 — INNOCENCE-BY-UNDERDOG RISK: "pressed, not pressing" needs its own counter-ledger

Disposition: resolved in part at Doc_09 (the Sasima entry carries the wound *and* the power-politics reading); carried constraint: the Representative must be able to speak the world's own uses of power without deflection to its sufferings.
