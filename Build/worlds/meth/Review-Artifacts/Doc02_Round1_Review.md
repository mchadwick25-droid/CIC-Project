## Findings

**M (medium) — none.**

**L (low) — three, all fixed directly in this same pass:**

- L1. §1's opening line originally stated "Eight corpus-map rows across seven vendored files." Recounting directly against `cic/corpus-map/the-methodist-revival.yaml` (generated by `corpus_map_merge.py`, re-read for this review) finds **eleven** rows: two from the Journal file (the Aldersgate entry plus Curnock's own editorial introduction, kept as separate rows precisely because §1 of the Registry itself flags them as different voices), three from the Sermons file, one from the Large Minutes, two from the hymnal (the Preface and the hymns themselves, again kept separate as different authors/voices), and three from Asbury (one per volume). Seven files is correct; eight rows was not. Corrected directly to "Eleven corpus-map rows across seven vendored files." This is a pure counting error, not a claim about what any specific row says — cosmetic, not substantial.
- L2. Re-ran `python -m engine.m9.cli holdings meth` directly (not merely trusted Doc_02's own paraphrase of the earlier failure) to confirm the header disclosure's own claim. Confirmed: identical `FileNotFoundError` for `records/meth`, for the identical reason stated (no Phase-B record store exists yet for any world at the Library stage). The header's own account is accurate; no revision needed. This check itself is worth logging per `cic-build-cycle`'s own rule that a dismissal ("this is a known, expected tooling gap, not a real defect") is never accepted without independent re-verification — this is that re-verification, not a repetition of the original build thread's own unchecked claim.
- L3. Checked §2's own Author Gravity findings against Doc_01 §4/§5 for silent contradiction: §2 states Wesley's corpus is "front-loaded toward 1738 and doctrine, thin on 1739–1790," while Doc_01 §5's own Strand Determination cites Wesley's "personal authority over Asbury until 1791" as a bridging fact between strands. Checked whether these are actually in tension (a thin-evidence-for-1739–1790 finding could seem to undercut a specific claim about Wesley's ongoing authority across that same span) — confirmed they are not: Doc_01's own claim about Wesley's authority over Asbury is explicitly flagged "Widely Accepted, not independently vendor-verified this pass" rather than drawn from this pass's own thin 1739–1790 corpus, so the two documents are not making the same claim from the same evidence base. No contradiction; no revision needed, confirmed clean rather than assumed clean.
