## Answer to the brief's central question

**§4, Whitefield as a separate movement: the conclusion partly survives, but the argument does not. It was reached to get a clean answer.**

The Framework's actual test (Construction Framework V7.4, "World Separation Criteria") has six questions: (1) Has a new gravity emerged? (2) Has a major gravity disappeared? (3) Has formation changed substantially? (4) Has worship changed substantially? (5) Has authority changed substantially? (6) Has interpretation changed substantially?

Doc_01 §4 folds these into four bullets. It answers them: gravity **No**, formation **Yes**, authority **Yes**, worship/interpretation **No**. Then it claims "four of six live tests … favor" separation, counting "the absence of any shared gravity-emergence" as a point *for* separation. That is backwards: a "No" on the gravity questions points toward one world. By the document's own answers the tally is 2 of 6.

Its gravity answer also contradicts Doc_01 §3, which lists "free grace, offered to all rather than limited by predestination (directly counterposed to Whitefield's own Calvinism)" and entire sanctification as this world's candidate gravities. Whitefield rejected both.

My own run of the test from the evidence:

| Question | My answer |
|---|---|
| Gravity (emerged / disappeared) | **Yes.** Two of this world's four candidate gravities are absent or contradicted in Whitefield. |
| Authority | **Yes.** The 1743 Calvinistic Methodist Association, then the Huntingdon line. |
| Interpretation | **Arguably yes.** The predestination dispute is an interpretive one. |
| Formation | **Partial, not "decisive."** Whitefield's Tabernacle society, the 1743 Association with Whitefield as moderator, and the Welsh *seiat* (which census VII.10 itself calls "comparable to Methodism's class meetings") refute "never built anything resembling." |
| Worship | **No.** |

So "Whitefield's Calvinistic organization is not a strand of this world" holds, and on stronger grounds than the document gave. But three things written around that conclusion do not hold:
- **"Article 21 does not apply"** and **"touches no other census entry's boundary."** The census names Whitefield as one of this world's six `voices`. Two of its three `documentedStories` feature him, and one ("Whitefield Asks for Slaves") is entirely about him. It cites his Journals and Works as primary sources. He is also a named voice of VII.6 (the Evangelical Awakening in New England), and VII.10 (the Welsh Revival Tradition) is the Calvinistic Methodist line.
- **The "corroborated by the census" claim is false** (details at P0-2).
- **The fairest reading** is Step 0's own option 3. Whitefield the person belongs inside this world's 1738–41 opening, since the census's doorway story is his Kingswood preaching. His post-1741 Calvinistic organization is a contemporary-movement boundary whose census homes (VII.6, VII.10, and no entry for the English Calvinistic Methodists/Huntingdon) are a cross-world allocation. That needs Mark's ruling, not self-disposition.

**§5, British and American as two strands: holds up.** Authority structure differs (episcopal church from 1784 vs. bishop-less Conference); ecological orientation differs (frontier vs. parish, urban and industrial). Either alone meets Article 21's definition. The census's own "Britain, then America" framing supports one world.

The argument still needs repair: it never runs the six-question test on this pair, although the Round 1 review (L3) says it did. Its bridging claim ("Wesley's personal authority … until 1791") is overstated — in 1787 the American Conference declined Wesley's direction to appoint Whatcoat and dropped his name from the Minutes. §5.2's "genuinely different practical solutions, not the same solution applied in two places" is wrong: itinerancy, circuits and the class meeting were British-connexion mechanisms Asbury carried over. It ignores in-window divisions: O'Kelly (1792), the Methodist New Connexion (1797), the Primitive Methodists (1807–11, census VIII.34), plus overlap with VIII.2 (Asbury is a named VIII.2 voice).

**Consistency between §4 and §5.** On the document's own answers, the two relationships have the same profile: authority and formation differ, everything else shared. Yet they reach opposite results. The only honest discriminator is divergence at the level of gravity, which is exactly what §4 denied. Fix §4's gravity answer and the two sections become consistent.
