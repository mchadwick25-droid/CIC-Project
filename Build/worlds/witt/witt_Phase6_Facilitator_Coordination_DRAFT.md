### B1 — World Identity

This world is Lutheran Wittenberg and its congregations, 1517 to 1580 — a territorial, confessional church, not a single central hierarchy. Princes and city councils carried the reform into their own lands; Wittenberg's own university theology faculty advised them, rather than an independent church government ruling from above (World Profile §1). Its defining character is a single settled conviction, restated in every register this world speaks in: a promise spoken plainly into a person's ears, not earned by any work, is the only thing that makes a frightened conscience sure (World Profile §10, Integrative Observation). This world understood itself as a restoration of the Gospel, not an invention of something new (World Profile §1). **This specific Representative, Nikolaus, is a sexton-schoolmaster** — a lay office that ran the town's German school, taught the catechism to the young, and assisted at worship, sitting at the point where household, school, parish, and pulpit meet, but never holding the pulpit's own authority (Doc_10 §1). He is not a pastor, and his construction record deliberately excludes a pastor's own first-person voice, since no pastor's own voice survives anywhere in this world's library (Doc_10 §1, citing Doc_02 §12.2 and Doc_09 §5).

### B2 — Formation Strengths

This world serves participants asking what it actually costs to stop trying to earn your own peace of mind. Its richest, most fully worked-out material is the terrified-and-comforted conscience — the claim that guilt and fear are not solved by trying harder, but only by a promise spoken from entirely outside the person carrying them (World Profile §2, Gravity G5; World Profile §4A, "certainty after engineered terror, not serenity"). It serves participants curious what a lived, disciplined household faith actually looked like: the three-part catechism recited at rising, at table, and at night, examined weekly by a father, all of it Documented to the hour as prescription (World Profile §2, Gravity G4; Formation Logic, World Profile §3). It serves, with real directness, participants wrestling with the tension between rules and freedom — Nikolaus lives daily inside a genuine, never-resolved tension between what must be kept and what must be left free, paced to whoever "can only creep" (World Profile §2, Gravity G8; §7, this world's own sharpest named tension). It also serves participants asking about congregational song: German hymn-singing inside a retained, not abolished, Mass is this world's one clearly generated (not merely inflected) formation practice, with the widest non-founder attestation in the whole construction record (World Profile §2, Gravity G11; Doc_08 §6).

### B3 — Formation Limitations

This world is honestly thin in several specific places, named plainly rather than softened. Whether any actual household or parish carried out the catechism program the way it prescribes is the single largest reception gap in this world's own record — Nikolaus can describe the program in full, but not any village's own report of receiving it (World Profile §8, domain 1). Women's own formation and voice is thin to the point of absence: no text written by a woman survives in this world's library at all, and Nikolaus will not invent one, including a real and tempting one — Katharina Schütz Zell's own writings from Strasbourg exist, but belong to a different city's own record and must never be borrowed as this world's own woman's voice (`Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` OG-1; Phase Five, Section 3.7, CL-3). ### B4 — Participant Type Fit

For the **regular visitor**, this world is warm and accessible, built in a plain, repeated, catechetical register rather than an elevated literary one (Doc_10 §2, Language and Register) — a visitor may need a moment's orientation to a handful of terms ("must and free," "the three parts," "the congregation of saints") that are not generic Christian vocabulary but carry specific weight here. For the **pastor or teacher**, this world offers directly usable material on household formation, weekly examination, and how a program of daily repetition is meant to serve, not replace, a promise received by faith — material grounded in Nikolaus's own richest domain (B2 above). For the **academic or scholar**, this world is a genuinely rich fit on its own confessional terms — the Approved Source List traces every anchor image to a verified primary-source locus (Doc_10 §2A) — but Nikolaus is built never to discuss "sources," "scholars," or the historiography of his own tradition in his own voice (Constitution Article 23; RCF V3.2 Part Eight, Violation Indicator VI-1) — corrected 2026-09-28 (Phase Six Round 3 recheck, non-blocking note): the deployed system's own live output on the 1543 treatise does, in fact, produce the word "sources" (B7 below, CL-1), so this is a built discipline, not an absolute guarantee; a scholar wanting that kind of discussion needs the Facilitator's own mediation, and a scholar pressing a specific modern reading (for instance, Heiko Oberman's apocalyptic-Reformation thesis) should be told up front that Nikolaus will not adjudicate it, since this construction's own record marks that reading as unread and not built on (World Profile §2, Gravity G12; Phase Five, Section 3.5, SF-1).
