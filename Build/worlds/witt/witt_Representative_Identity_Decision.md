## The decision

**Name: Nikolaus**

**Role: Sexton-schoolmaster** (Küster/Schulmeister) — a lay office standard to Lutheran territorial churches across the whole 1517–1580 window: ran the town's German school, taught the catechism to the young, assisted at worship (where hymnody — this world's own distinctively generated gravity, G11 — lives), and stood at the point where household, school, parish, and pulpit all actually met.

---

## How the decision was reached

A three-option packet was prepared and presented for review (`witt_World_Profile.md`'s companion decision doc; the full options analysis, including the five-test check for each, ran in a shared Claude Docs page — content preserved below since that page is not part of this world's own build folder):

- **Option 1 — Kaspar**, lay household-catechist/elder. The build thread's own original recommendation: sits directly on the world's own Primary formation gravity (G4, the household catechism).
- **Option 2 — Barbara**, the same household-catechist role held by a woman, per the Small Catechism's own textual warrant for "a master or a lady of the house." Presented as a genuine, evidentially real option, not a token one — discussed at length (see below).
- **Option 3 — Nikolaus**, sexton-schoolmaster. Broader institutional reach across household, school, and parish; the strongest "crossroads" fit of the three per the naming discipline's own test 3 (role must span the whole movement, not one office or moment).

All three cleared the naming discipline's first four tests (real-not-confusable name; lands with a modern reader; spans the whole movement; name and image the only fabrications). Two considered-and-dropped candidates were named for the record: a pastor (the library never captures a pastor's own first-person voice — a real absence, per Doc_02 §12.2 and Doc_09 §5) and a printer (fits the material/print gravity well but lacks natural doctrinal standing to speak across the whole ecology).

### The Test-5 discussion (women's leadership), in full, for the record

Before settling on a male name, the question of a woman's name was taken seriously and discussed at length, not waved past:

- Barbara, under the same household-catechist role as Option 1, was checked against Test 5 and found to be **the strongest "authorization without a named voice" case surfaced anywhere in this project's built portfolio** — the Small Catechism's own confession script names "a master or a lady of the house" directly, and household catechesis is this world's own Primary formation gravity (G4). This is real textual warrant, not an imported modern assumption.
- A comparative survey across all 18 currently-built formation worlds (run 2026-09-19) found only two worlds where a woman was actually chosen as Representative (Hieronymian/Albina, Post-Apostolic House-Church/Chloe) — both grounded in richer, *named* female presence (four documented women for Hieronymian; a documented cultural opening for household female authority for PAHC) than witt's own case, which rests on textual authorization rather than a named woman's own voice or leadership. Two worlds' naming records (Cappadocian, Desert-Monasticism) show the same question weighed seriously and set aside for reasons distinct from evidentiary weakness; two (Donatism, Syriac) show the identical test applied and returning a clean evidentiary no, for the same structural reason witt's case falls short of the "leader" bar: real female presence in the record, but no named woman holding the office or public doctrinal authority the role would need.
- A further, sharper question was raised directly: not just whether a woman *could* faithfully carry this world's doctrine (yes — Lutheran theology itself holds that faith and formation don't differ by sex, and the household register is fully hers), but whether her *scope of public voice* — as opposed to her doctrinal knowledge — could be built without quietly erasing a real historical constraint. The honest answer reached: a faithfully-built Barbara would need her authority to speak framed the way this world itself framed it — full confidence on the catechism and the household, a different and more mediated relationship to the confessional-political register (Augsburg, the papal and imperial confrontations) that this world's own library shows almost entirely as men's public business. That's not a diminishment properly built; it's fidelity. But it is a real construction cost the male options don't carry.
- **A separate, non-evidentiary consideration decided the question: living-tradition reception.** Mark's own reasoning, stated directly: even where the historical record could support a woman's voice, a Representative choice can itself be read as a present-day statement about a living tradition that spans a wide range of confessional positions today — from the ELCA to the LCMS and confessional Lutheran bodies worldwide, on exactly this question. Choosing a male name for this world isn't a concession to thin evidence (the evidence, while thinner than the two worlds that chose women, was real and had already cleared serious consideration); it's declining to make a statement this project isn't positioned to referee on behalf of a living tradition it doesn't speak for. **This is a distinct, standing consideration beyond the naming discipline's own five evidentiary tests, and Mark named it explicitly as a general principle, not a witt-specific one** — flagged for possible addition to the governing Naming & Role Discipline document as a sixth consideration (not yet formalized; a separate governance decision, pending Mark's word).

### Between the two male options

Mark's own words: **"lets do nikolaus, it is unique and broader."**

Two things named directly: distinctiveness (Nikolaus stands apart from the other two options and from every name already carried by a documented figure in this world's own library — Luther, Melanchthon, Katharina, Karlstadt, Jonas, Walter, Praetorius, Köpfel, and the rest) and breadth (the sexton-schoolmaster's institutional reach across household, school, and worship, spanning the whole 1517–1580 window as this office became standard Lutheran territorial-church infrastructure, over Kaspar's tighter but narrower fit to the household gravity alone).

## What this decision does and doesn't settle

- **Settled:** the Representative's name (Nikolaus) and role (sexton-schoolmaster). Per the naming discipline, these are the *only* two fabrications permitted — no backstory, biography, family, or personal history beyond what this world's completed ecology (Doc_01–Doc_09, the World Profile) directly supports.
- **Not settled here, and not this document's job:** the actual voice construction (reasoning mode, register, native vocabulary, boundary calibration) — that is Doc_10's work (Representative Construction Notes + Permanent Prompt), per `Representative_Construction_Notes_Template.md` Section 1, which this decision directly feeds.
- **Open, flagged above, pending Mark's separate word:** whether "living-tradition reception risk" becomes a formalized sixth consideration in the standing Naming & Role Discipline document, alongside the existing five evidentiary tests. This is a governance/methodology change and is not enacted by this document.
