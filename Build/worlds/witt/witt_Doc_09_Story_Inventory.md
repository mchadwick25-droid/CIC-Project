## 1. The governing tier framework, quoted directly

CF V7.4 states the four tiers as follows (quoted verbatim; re-extracted from the current `.docx` this pass rather than assumed from memory, per this project's own recurring caution about drift in remembered methodology text):

**Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative.** "Direct textual attestation within or close to the world's horizon. Named author with identifiable social location. Datable with reasonable confidence... Confidence level: Documented to Widely Accepted at the narrative level... How to use: may be offered as historical narrative with the author and perspective named."

**Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material.** "Stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory... Academic consensus treats the tradition as authentic even where individual attribution or detail cannot be verified... Confidence level: Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction. The tradition is authentic; specific details and attributions carry Contested confidence... How to use: may be offered as stories the tradition tells about itself, with the collection tradition named."

**Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition.** "Material attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition rather than direct documentation... Hagiographic narrative is a specific type within this tier... Confidence level: Contested for the general portrait or attribution; Inferential/Thin for specific details... How to use: may be offered as the community's own account of what a formed life looks like or what a specific figure represented, with the attributed or hagiographic framing acknowledged. Not used as historical fact about specific events."

**Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** "A composite reconstruction of what regularly happened within the ecology — not a story about a specific named individual... Every element must be traceable to attested evidence within the world's horizon... Confidence level: Inferential/Thin... How to use: Tier 4 narration is appropriate... when it is explicitly marked as reconstruction... The representative voice may narrate typical practice in Tier 4 mode but should not claim it as a specific known event."

**No Tier 5.** "Generated or illustrative narrative — stories invented to illuminate formation truths but not traceable to the world's own evidence — is not permitted within this framework."

### witt-S01 — The letter to Albrecht and the circulation of the Ninety-Five Theses (October–December 1517)

**Source:** Luther's own dated letter to Archbishop Albrecht of Mainz [R2]; the editor's bibliography of the Theses' printed editions.

**Tier: 1, split.** The letter itself is Tier 1 without qualification — a named author, a datable, directly attested document. The Theses' further circulation and the traditional door-posting detail are a different evidentiary matter: Doc_02 §10 already flagged this as **[Contested]**, per the Iserloh historiographical debate over whether the theses were physically posted on the Wittenberg castle church door. That contest is a matter of documented modern scholarly dispute over a specific claimed event, not a question of genre (it is not hagiographic convention, so it does not fall to Tier 3); it remains Tier 1 material with a named, disclosed confidence split within the tier, exactly as the Cross-Walk allows Tier 1 "specific details within the narrative" to carry Contested confidence while the narrative itself is Documented to Widely Accepted. **The evidentiary basis for that split, stated plainly (review finding, this revision):** this library's own vendored text for the posting narrative is not a period document at all — the Registry's own R2 entry records it as "the editor's, 1915" ("It was not night, but mid-day," v1 473–474), a twentieth-century editorial narrative assertion with no primary citation of its own. The Iserloh reference this document's "Contested" tag ultimately rests on (R76) is itself unread by this build, verified only as existing by a web search of its title (Registry line 92, confidence **B**). "Contested" here therefore means: an unread secondary source is understood to dispute a modern editor's own unsourced 1915 narrative claim — not two primary accounts in tension. That is evidentially thinner than "a documented dispute over a specific event" can sound, and a scholar checking this entry should be told so directly rather than left to infer it.

**Confidence:** Documented (the letter) / Contested (the posting).

**Formation ecology connection:** The world's own founding act of address — a protest addressed upward, through channels, to an ecclesiastical superior, not a public act aimed at popular agitation. Connects to G1 (Doc_04) as the movement's earliest self-understanding of its own protest as loyal correction, not rebellion.

**Usage guidance:** Per Tier 1's own rule — offered as historical narrative with the author and perspective named. Tell the letter as the documented act; the posting detail, if used at all, must carry its historiographical contest explicitly and never be narrated as settled fact ("the hammer" itself is not attested at Documented confidence and should not be dramatized as though it were).

**Registry cross-reference:** R2, Native, Primary.

### witt-S03 — Worms (1521), in Luther's own retelling

**Source:** Table Talk [R31].

**Tier: 2.** Identical evidentiary basis and identical reasoning to witt-S02: a collected, later reminiscence of a documented event, not a contemporaneous record of the event's own words.

**Confidence:** Widely Accepted (the Diet, the summons, the refusal to recant) / Contested (specific wording — including the famous "Here I stand" sentence, which this library's own Table Talk source gives in a different form; see the usage guidance below).

**Usage guidance:** Per Tier 2's own rule, offered as the tradition's own story of itself, collection tradition named. **A specific, disclosed constraint carried forward from the Registry, corrected this revision (review finding: the quotation previously printed here was misattributed — see below):** this library's own Table Talk text reads, "Although I was somewhat astonished at the news, yet I answered the herald, and said, although in Worms there were as many devils as there are tiles on the houses, yet, God willing, I will go thither" (TT 3507–3509) — not "Here I stand — I cannot do otherwise." Any use of either Carlyle-transmitted phrase — "Here I stand" or "these tile roofs" — must carry the Bacon/Carlyle provenance and disclose that its verbatim authenticity is contested in the wider scholarship (R69).

### witt-S04 — The Brussels martyrs (1523)

**Source:** Luther's own ballad, *Ein neues Lied wir heben an*, hymn V [R30].

**Tier: 1 (the event) / 3 (the narrative details and formation portrait).** The martyrdom itself — two named Augustinian-house monks, John and Henry, burned at Brussels on 1 July 1523 for their evangelical convictions — is Tier 1: datable, attested by a near-contemporary author who wrote the ballad within months of the event (the Introduction's own dating argument, Hy 696–699). The ballad's own telling of the martyrdom, however, is composed in a recognizably devotional-commemorative register — stanza form, direct address to the reader, the martyrs' last words rendered as reported speech ("They won the crown of martyrs," Hy 1767) — which is exactly what CF V7.4 names as **hagiographic narrative, the named subtype within Tier 3**: "the idealized portrait... shaped by recognizable hagiographic conventions... the account of what a formed life looks like is itself genuine formation ecology evidence... even if the specific details are shaped by genre." This is the sharper justification Discipline 4 above flagged as owed: Doc_02 §10 correctly split the tier but did not name hagiographic convention as the reason; this entry supplies it.

**Confidence:** Documented (the martyrdom occurred, in 1523) / Contested to Inferential/Thin (the ballad's specific reported details and dialogue, per Tier 3's own rule).

**Formation ecology connection:** The library's only martyrology, and the clearest single statement of the formation ideal the world holds up under ultimate pressure — "true priests of God's own making" (Hy 1799–1800) — a phrase that states in one line what the whole ecology's doctrine of vocation argues at length elsewhere (G1, G7, Doc_04).

**Usage guidance:** The event may be told as historical narrative (Tier 1: author and perspective named — Luther, writing within the same year, from Wittenberg, not Brussels). The ballad's own dramatized details — the martyrs' words at the stake, the specific sequence of the Louvain theologians' proceedings — are Tier 3 and must be framed as the tradition's own commemorative portrait, not narrated as verified reportage. Per Doc_02 §10's own instruction, carried forward unchanged: "the formation ideal... is the evidence, not stanza 11" — the theological claim the hymn makes about what these deaths meant is usable at full confidence; the narrative texture around it is not.

**Registry cross-reference:** R30, Native, Primary. The vendored text names the two monks "John and Henry" (Doc_02 §7 item 1 and §11).

### witt-S11 — A household catechism lesson (typical practice)

### witt-S12 — Luther's prayer for rain during the 1532 drought (new to this document; read this pass)

**Source:** Table Talk, "Of Luther's Prayer for a gracious Rain" [R31], TT lines 3154–3192.

**Tier: 2.** The same evidentiary class as witt-S02/S03/S08 — a later, collected, student-recorded account (Table Talk's own compilation history), not a contemporaneous document of the event. It is dated with unusual precision for a Table Talk entry (9 June 1532, stated twice within the passage itself), which is a mark in favor of the tradition's own care in preserving it, but the precision of an internal date does not convert a collected reminiscence into a Tier 1 document — the account still reaches this library through Table Talk's compilers, not through a contemporaneous record of the day itself.

**Confidence:** Widely Accepted (that Luther led a public prayer for rain during a documented 1532 drought, before the assembled church) / Contested (the specific wording of the prayer itself, as recorded, and the precise sequence and timing of the rain that is said to have followed "within the space of half an hour").

**Formation ecology connection:** This is the only story in the entire inventory in which the formation subject is the **gathered congregation itself**, not Luther alone or Luther among students — "Luther called together the whole assembly into the church" (TT 3159). Every other Tier 1/2 story in this inventory is a founder-corpus scene; this one, uniquely, shows the whole community assembled and addressed, even though it still reaches this library only through Luther's own recorded prayer, not through any congregant's voice (the same founder/congregation asymmetry Doc_02 §11 names at the ecology's widest scale is visible again here, in miniature, inside the one scene where the congregation is most visibly present). The prayer's own content is doctrinally dense and formation-relevant in its own right: it names "the covetousness of the rich farmers" and the movement's own anxiety that evangelical liberty had been mistaken for license ("they are unbridled, they think it free for them to live and do what they please... 'I believe, therefore I shall be saved'") — a documented instance of G13 (Doc_04's coldness/negligence gravity) voiced as a *public, congregational* complaint rather than only a private one, and of G8's "must"/"free" tension turned outward into pastoral anxiety about a whole town's conduct, not just an individual household's.

**Usage guidance:** Per Tier 2's own rule — offered as a story the tradition tells about itself, with Table Talk named as the collection. The prayer's text may be quoted at Widely Accepted confidence as to its having been prayed in substance; the specific reported timing of the rain ("within the space of half an hour... in a most miraculous manner") should be carried with its own genre awareness — Table Talk records this as the community's own experience of answered prayer, and per the From-Within test this build has applied throughout (Doc_08 Discipline 5), that is a fact about the world's self-understanding worth carrying honestly, not a claim this document independently verifies as meteorological fact.

**Registry cross-reference:** R31, Native, Primary. **Not previously entered in Doc_02 §10** — flagged there only as a seed (Open_Gaps_Tracking.md OG-9 item 12, "not read past 3161") and completed by this document's own bounded reading pass (§0 Discipline 2).

### witt-ABS-01 — A village congregation's ignorance at visitation

**What is missing:** A scene of an ordinary rural congregation being examined at a Saxon church visitation (the inspections that ran from 1527 onward) and found lacking in its grasp of the catechism — the very failure the Small Catechism's own preface says provoked its writing.

**Why it cannot be told:** No vendored source reports it. The Large Catechism's short preface is the founder's own complaint about negligence, in his own voice, not a visitor's field report of any actual parish (Doc_02 §10's own determination, carried unchanged: "the LC preface is the founder's complaint, not a visitor's report; narrating it as a scene would be Tier 5"). The actual visitation protocols exist — Doc_02 §12.2 names them specifically: "The Saxon church inspected its own parishes from 1527 and wrote down what it found; those reports exist, mostly untranslated" — but they are not in this library, for rights and language reasons, not because they were never made.

**What this absence means, stated plainly, per Article 20:** The world that most needed its ordinary congregations' own voice to be heard — a movement whose central complaint was that its own people were not learning what they were taught — has left this library nothing in those people's own words about whether the complaint was true of them. A Representative built from this world can speak with real depth about the founder's anxiety that formation was failing, and can state exactly why: because the founder said so, repeatedly and in his own name. It cannot speak, from this library, as anyone who was examined and found wanting, or as anyone who examined them.

### The wider Absent Stories pattern, drawn from Doc_02 §11–§12

Named here as a set, because Article 20 asks that absence be named where it is structural, not only where a single candidate story was tested and refused:

- **No woman's own story.** Doc_02 §12.1: "the library contains no text written by a woman." Katharina's one recorded question (witt-S08) is the library's single speaking trace, and it is explicitly not expandable. There is no version of any story in this inventory that can be retold from a woman's own perspective without crossing into invention.
- **No peasant's story, and no story of 1525 at all.** The single most consequential event inside this world's own dated window — the Peasants' War — is absent from this library on both sides: the movement's sympathetic 1521 tract is vendored (Doc_02 §12.4), but its harsh 1525 response is not, and no peasant voice is present in either. This inventory accordingly tells no story of 1525, and none should be built for Phase B by inference from the surrounding years.
- **No Roman Catholic contemporary's story.** Doc_02 §1.3, §13: Rome appears in this library only as quoted and answered, never in its own extended voice. Any story of a Roman Catholic figure's own formation or motivation is untellable here.
- **No Reformed contemporary's story.** The silence is total and, per Doc_02 §13, chronological rather than suppressive — but total regardless. No Zwingli, no Marburg, no Reformed voice at any tier.
- **No story from *On the Jews and Their Lies* (1543) or the peasants' 1525 tract, in either direction.** Doc_02 §12.3–§12.4 bind this build to disclosure, not narration, of these two texts. Neither is a source for any story in this inventory, and Phase B must not build one from either, per those sections' own binding instruction, carried forward here unchanged.
- **No parish Sunday, no ordinary pastor's own voice.** Every worship-practice story in this inventory (S07, S09, S11) is either institutional, founder-adjacent, or reconstructed from prescription. No single parish's actual Sunday, from any actual worshipper's own account, is tellable from this library.

**What building on this inventory must not do:** treat any of the above as a gap to quietly fill at Phase B by extrapolation from what the library does hold. Doc_02 §12's own instruction stands: these absences are Facilitator-facing disclosures, not construction problems this or any later document should try to solve from inside the library it has.
