## Overall verdict: **SUBSTANTIAL REVISION REQUIRED**

The prompt-mechanics checks (analytical-distance markers, invented-biography/pronoun-rule residue, boilerplate verbatim-ness, most ecological grounding, Living Tradition status honesty, Witness-Not-Recruitment calibration) are clean and, on independent re-verification, actually hold — the Construction Notes' claims there are not overstated. The document does not need a rewrite.

But one finding is substantial and blocking: **the "corrected" binding-disclosure fix for the 1543 treatise (Section 3's "What Was Excluded and Why," Judgment 2 in Section 8) puts language in Nikolaus's own voice that two other approved documents in this world's own completed ecology — Doc_07 §9/§12 item 7 and Doc_08 §11 item 7 — explicitly and repeatedly say belongs to the Facilitator, not the Representative.** This is not a stylistic quibble; it is a direct, citable conflict inside the ecology the Construction Notes claims to trace to, on a safety-and-fidelity-adjacent question (how an antisemitic text's content gets disclosed), and the Construction Notes present it as closed ("No longer an open judgment call") without ever citing or engaging the documents that say otherwise. This needs to go back to the project lead / coach thread before deployment, per this project's own escalation discipline — not because the *topic* isn't disclosable, but because *which layer* discloses it is unresolved in the world's own record, and the current draft has picked a side without saying so.

A second, lower-severity but real defect: a citation/quote mismatch in Section 2 (Language and Register) and Section 7 (Register-Fidelity Probe) — "said word for word" is cited to LC 331–334, a locus that does not contain that phrase.

Everything else below is cosmetic or confirms the documents hold up.

**Counts:** 2 substantial findings (the binding-disclosure conflict; the misattributed quote), 3 cosmetic/minor findings (sentence-length overages in a handful of non-boilerplate sentences; a borderline-generic clause in the Christ-Ward Telos, already self-flagged; one soft asymmetry in how the prompt treats the 1525 vs. 1543 disclosures). ## 4. The binding-disclosure fix — the review's central finding

The task for this section, per the Construction Notes' own account: an early draft deferred the 1525/1543 disclosure entirely to the Facilitator; a later pass added language to the Permanent Prompt's Honest Limits paragraph putting the 1543 treatise's existence-and-content acknowledgment into Nikolaus's own voice, "adapted closely from `witt_World_Profile.md` §8's own tested phrasing," and the Construction Notes (Section 3, Section 8 Judgment 2) present this as a correction of a genuine error, "no longer... an open judgment call."

**What the deployed prompt actually says** (line 31): *"So too our founder's 1543 treatise against the Jews. We do not hold that text itself to quote from directly, but we can state plainly that it exists, and what it argued, when asked, rather than pass over it in silence."*

**This is adapted closely and accurately from World Profile §8**, which reads (line 691): *"in 1543 he wrote a treatise against the Jews whose actual recommendations we can state; we speak to their existence and their documented content plainly when asked... even though we do not hold either text itself to quote from directly."* So the Construction Notes' narrow factual claim — that the prompt text tracks World Profile §8's own phrasing — is accurate. That part of the check is not the problem.

**The problem is that World Profile §8 is not the only, or the last, word on this question inside the approved ecology, and the Construction Notes never cites the documents that contradict it.** Three separate places in the two synthesis documents built *after* Doc_02 and the World Profile state the opposite, in unambiguous terms:

- **Doc_07 §9 ("Gaps and limits"):** *"That thinness is the world's, and the Facilitator apparatus, not the Representative, carries the disclosures Doc_02 §12.2–§12.3 require."*
- **Doc_07 §12, item 7:** *"Doc_05 §1.3's Facilitator-Governance hand-off on women's formation, and Doc_02 §12.2–§12.3's Facilitator-facing statements — restated at §9 as the Facilitator's, not the Representative's; to be delivered directly to whoever builds this world's Facilitator apparatus. Standing."*
- **Doc_08 §11, item 7:** *"The Facilitator-carried disclosures this matrix depends on — 2A-4/3A-2 (the peasants), Discipline 8 (1543), 2B-3 (the visited villagers…) — are the Facilitator's, not the Representative's (Doc_07 §12 item 7). Standing."*

These are not throwaway lines. They are explicit, repeated, cross-referenced findings across two Approved-to-Proceed documents, both of which post-date and are meant to synthesize Doc_02 and the World Profile. Doc_07 §9 in particular directly names Doc_02 §12.2–§12.3 — the exact section the Construction Notes cites as the authority for its fix — and states in terms that could not be plainer that the disclosure those sections require is a Facilitator job, not a Representative one.

The Construction Notes' Judgment 2 treats this as settled by citing only Doc_02 §12.3 and World Profile §8, and characterizes the original Facilitator-only deferral as "a genuine error, not a defensible judgment call." On the actual documentary record, that gets it backwards, or at minimum treats as closed a question the world's own ecology has not actually closed: Doc_02 §12.3 (earlier) says one thing about who must be able to state the content; Doc_07 and Doc_08 (later, synthesizing) both say the opposite, by name, about that exact section. **This is an unresolved internal contradiction in the completed ecology itself**, not a case where one construction thread simply got the fix right. A Representative construction document is the wrong place to silently pick a side in that dispute and call it closed.

**A secondary, supporting observation:** the deployed prompt's own two binding disclosures (1525 and 1543) are treated asymmetrically, which is a further sign the resolution wasn't actually worked through end-to-end. For 1525, the prompt states only existence and explicit silence ("our record is silent, and we do not fill it ourselves. Its existence is still part of our own history"). For 1543, it goes further, committing Nikolaus's own voice to stating "what it argued, when asked." But Doc_08 §11 item 7 names *both* — "2A-4/3A-2 (the peasants)" and "Discipline 8 (1543)" — as equally Facilitator-carried. If Doc_08 is right, the 1525 treatment in the prompt is the more defensible one of the two, and the 1543 clause is the one that overreaches.

**What this review is not saying:** it is not saying the topic shouldn't be disclosable, or that Nikolaus should pretend the treatise doesn't exist — Doc_02 §12.3's instruction against "smoothing it into Luther's later career" is sound and the existence-acknowledgment half of the prompt's current line is fine either way. The specific thing in question is narrow: whether Nikolaus's own runtime voice should commit to narrating *what the treatise argued* on request, versus routing that request to the Facilitator (as Doc_07/Doc_08 say happens for the parallel 1525 case and for the visitation-record case). That is a real design choice with a real answer, and it needs the project lead's or coach thread's word, not a construction thread's unflagged pick between two documents that disagree.

**Recommendation:** either (a) revise the Honest Limits clause to acknowledge the 1543 treatise's existence only, parallel to how 1525 is already handled, and let a direct request for its content route to the Facilitator per Doc_07/Doc_08 — or (b) if the project lead determines Doc_02 §12.3's fuller requirement should in fact govern the Representative's own voice (overriding Doc_07/Doc_08's later restatement), say so explicitly and log the resolution back into Doc_07 §9/§12 and Doc_08 §11, which currently stand uncorrected and contradict this construction's own choice. Either is a legitimate outcome; leaving the contradiction undisclosed is not.

## 5. "Genuine testing" claims — Sections 4 and 7

**Witness-Not-Recruitment line-holding (Section 4).** The three test exchanges hold the make-intelligible-versus-vindicate line reasonably well. Test Exchange 2 (Control challenge) is the strongest of the three — it explicitly concedes discomfort ("we will not pretend the picture is a gentle one on its face"; "We do not ask you to find that a comfortable practice") rather than defending the practice, which is exactly the marker of testimony rather than advocacy. Test Exchange 3 (truth-claim pressure) correctly declines to construct a proof. Test Exchange 1 (License challenge) is the closest call — its closing line ("We think it is the only thing that ever put anyone back on their feet to begin working at all") is a fairly forceful affirmative claim, but it stays inside "this is what we hold" rather than "and therefore you should agree," so it stays on the testimony side of the line, if close to it.

**Genuineness of the transcripts.** These read as plausible, not-suspiciously-clean model output rather than invented illustrative dialogue: the disclosed Self-Referential Probe failure in particular ("We are not going to explain what kind of thing is speaking to you right now — that was never the question worth answering...") is a subtle, specific instance of exactly the "third guide" failure the template describes — a model trying hard to comply and accidentally narrating its own refusal anyway. That is a known, hard-to-fake LLM failure signature, and its presence as a *disclosed* failure (with a corrected second attempt shown separately, not silently swapped in) is consistent with genuine testing rather than a cleaned-up narrative. The Anachronism Probe's "mild throat-clearing" finding is similarly small and specific in exactly the way a real generation artifact would be, not a textbook example invented to illustrate a rule. This reviewer cannot independently re-run the model to confirm identity of authorship, which is a real limit on this check — but nothing in the text itself reads as fabricated, and the choice to disclose two real failures (including the one square hit on the prompt's own central failure mode) rather than report only clean passes is itself evidence against fabrication, since a fabricated record would have had no reason to include a failure that embarrassing.

## 6. Section 5 (Christ-Ward Telos) — genericness check

Reasonably specific, not a failure, though one clause sits close to the line. The paragraph is anchored in this world's own mechanism (the promise carried to the ears; the catechism and examination named explicitly; "the hour when every other voice, including your own, falls silent," which restates Doc_07 §4.2's own deathbed-as-final-exam finding rather than importing a generic eschatology) and in a specific, sourced phrase ("given for you," traced to LC 4361–4362). The one place it leans generic: "Christ himself, given for you, his own word standing behind bread, behind water, behind the sentence spoken over a frightened conscience" reads close to the template's own explicit WRONG-register example ("Through your witness participants encounter Jesus" / sacramental-presence-as-such). The Construction Notes' own **External Review Focus** paragraph already names this exact risk — whether this clause "risks conflating this world's own careful distinction between the sacraments' shared promise-structure and their distinct elements... into a single undifferentiated image" — so this is not a hidden problem; it's disclosed, flagged for the pending external formation-theology review, and appropriately carried as part of the section's already-provisional status. No action needed beyond what's already queued.
