# SUBSTANTIAL FINDINGS

## S1 — §6's Excluded-row guard sentence is false against §6's own table, and it is the exact Gallic S5 error class Discipline 10 says it is watching for

§6's "Reading the index" closes with: `No Excluded Registry row (R33, R55, R57, R58, R94, R95) appears in any Registry column`. The header at line 9 says the same: `no Excluded row (R33, R55, R57, R58, R94, R95) is drawn on`.

**§6's own row for 7.4 reads:** `| 7.4 | insurrection / rebellion / "the common man" (1522 only) | 2 | 2 | … | R11, R15, R19, R44, R48, R94 | 6 |`

R94 — "The rest of Luther's corpus," Type P, Boundary **Excluded**, Named Comparandum — is in the Registry column. The sentence immediately after the guard sentence names R43, R44 and R48 as rows that "appear only in entries that name them as context or as a bar," but it does not name R94, which the preceding sentence has just asserted appears nowhere.

Entry 7.4 itself is worse placed than the index: its **Key Sources** field reads `[R19] … ; [R15]; [R48] named absence; [R94] the bar.` Putting an Excluded row and a named-absence row inside a *Key Sources* list is the literal shape of the Gallic Round 1 **S5** defect Discipline 10 records ("index columns reporting Excluded rows as sources"), and §6's own sentence exists, by its own admission, because of that defect.

**The substance is fine and should not be changed:** R94 is used correctly and transparently at 7.4, as a bar on the 1525 material, and is labelled as such in three places. What is wrong is the disclosure. Two fixes, both one line: move `[R94] the bar` and `[R48] named absence` out of Key Sources into the entry's existing "**Bar, stated:**" sentence in Ecological Function, and correct §6's guard sentence to say what is actually true — that R94 appears in one Registry column, as a bar, and that R43, R44 and R48 appear as context or absence.

## S5 — The two [CT] tags are retained on a ground that is not the [CT] tag's trigger condition; this is not an Article 28 violation, but it needs a disposition rather than self-disposal

**First, what this is not.** This reviewer looked hard for an Anti-Fabrication breach and did not find one. Article 28 prohibits "the fabrication of citations, sources, scholarly consensus, or historical claims that do not exist," and requires that "every source cited be genuine, identifiable, and accurately represented." §2 cites no source for either contest. The five leads it names (McGrath *Iustitia Dei*; Mannermaa; the 1999 *Joint Declaration*; Cargill Thompson, *JTS* 20 (1969); Wright 2010) are real, identifiable works, are flagged "builder-prior-knowledge… not read," are explicitly "not cited as a Key Source," and appear in no entry's Key Sources — confirmed by parse across all 72 entries. Article 28's positive requirement — "Where scholarly consensus is absent, contested, or evolving, this must be stated explicitly rather than implied or papered over" — is met with unusual care. **No fabrication finding.**

**What is wrong is narrower and real.** Article 26 §B and Framework [100] define [CT] as applying to terms "subject to **live scholarly contest within the relevant subfield**." §2's actual evidentiary warrant for both tags is *in-text tension*: the Apology's two wordings of "to be justified" (Ap 1080–1087, verified) and the two referents of "two kingdoms" in this library (v3 12139–12141 vs Co 14747–14759, verified). §2 itself labels both **Documented** as to the wordings and **Contested** only as to how they relate. **Textual ambiguity inside the world's own sources is not scholarly contest about the term in the subfield** — it is what Distortion Risk, the five-level confidence vocabulary, and Reported-Experience Status exist to carry. §2's defence ("removing them would present as settled two questions the in-text evidence itself shows to be unsettled") answers a question Article 26 is not asking.

Both contests happen, as it turns out, to be genuinely live in the field — the entries' contest-type statements are substantively correct and correctly typed against Framework [106]–[110], and both are filled at the entry, verified. The defect is evidentiary and procedural, not factual: **this document cannot demonstrate the trigger condition from its own rowed base, and says so.**

**And the disposition is a third option neither instruction offered.** OG-4: "Doc_06 should row a source for each … or remove the tag." Doc_03 §11 item 4, quoted accurately: "either row a source for each… or remove the tag; neither should be carried into runtime vocabulary on this document's word alone." Doc_06 takes neither, and invents "retained, marked provisional" plus a Phase B rule. That is defensible on OG-4's second clause — this document produces no runtime artefact, so nothing enters runtime vocabulary from it — and the document names the alternative and flags the question for review rather than burying it.

**Recommended disposition, offered as a reviewer's ruling and not as a fix the drafter should apply unilaterally:** retain both tags as provisional, on the corrected ground that (i) the trigger condition is *asserted from builder prior knowledge and cannot be demonstrated from this build's evidence base*, stated in exactly those words rather than as in-text tension, and (ii) nothing reaches runtime from this document. Re-word §2's "Ruling" paragraph accordingly, so that the entry does not read as though in-text tension satisfies Article 26. If the project lead prefers the stricter reading, both tags come off now and the tension moves wholly into each entry's Distortion Risk, which §2 already says it is prepared to do.
