# SUBSTANTIAL FINDINGS

## S1 — §3's "private masses 'for the most part they go so far'" quotes a phrase that is nowhere in this corpus, and its real source is about people who stay away from the Sacrament

§3, "How worship functions," reads: `"we hold only Public or Common Mass [at which the people also commune, not Private Mass]" (Ap 8526–8527); private masses "for the most part they go so far" (per Doc_04 §3 G3's arc) are the thing abolished.`

The quoted phrase does not exist. I searched every vendored Luther and Melanchthon file: `go so far` occurs once in the whole corpus (v3 1429, "I go so far as to say…", unrelated). The phrase's actual provenance is Doc_04, but not at G3 — it is at **Doc_04 §3 G13**, quoting **LC 4244–4248**: people who *"pretend that it is a matter of liberty and not necessary… go so far that they become quite brutish."* That is the 1529 complaint about communicants who stay away. It has nothing to do with private masses, with G3's 1520/1522/1530 arc, or with what was abolished.

So a phrase in quotation marks, in this document's own voice, (i) is not verbatim from any source, (ii) is carried on a pointer to a section that does not contain it, and (iii) is attached to a subject its true source contradicts. This is the Gallic Round 1 **S1/S4** class — a prior-document locus reused without re-reading, landing on the wrong subject — occurring in a document whose Discipline 2 says this is "the rule that did the most work this pass." It is in the analytical layer rather than an inhabited passage, which is the only thing that keeps it from being worse.

**Remedy.** Delete the phrase. The sentence does not need it: Ap 8526–8527 already says what is abolished, and v2 14850 ("the private mass must be abolished, as I have said in my writings") says it in the sermons' own voice at a locus inside a declared read-range.

## S2 — "a title the sermons' sidenotes use" is false, and it is the whole licence for "the Doctor" in three of the four inhabited passages

Passage 1's layer note states that the passage uses `"the Doctor" for the speaker's own pastor-founder, a title the sermons' sidenotes use, not a claim about how any household addressed him.` Passage 4's layer note then inherits it verbatim: `"the Doctor" as at the earlier passages.`

I dumped every `[Sidenote: …]` in the Eight Sermons range (v2 14634–15700). There are thirty-five of them. **Not one contains "Doctor."** The one that names Luther says `[Sidenote: Luther's Method]` (v2 14920). Across the whole sermon range the only `Dr.` is *Dr. Carlstadt* (v2 14997) — an opponent. In the Table Talk, "Doctor" occurs six times and names Ussinger, Jonas, and two unnamed doctors, never Luther. The **only** place in this library where Luther is called Doctor is Walter's letter, `Doctor Luther` (Hy 770) — which passage 3's own note cites correctly.

So the document's stated evidence for a form of address it uses in three inhabited passages is a statement about a vendored text that is not true of that text. Passage 3's use survives on Hy 770; passages 1 and 4 have no anchor at all, and the note that purports to supply one is wrong. In a household passage set in 1529 in no named place, "the Doctor" additionally does the quiet work of making the speaker a Wittenberger with access to Luther's table speech.

**Remedy.** Either re-anchor "the Doctor" honestly (Hy 770 only, which places it in Walter's mouth and in Wittenberg, not in an unnamed household), or drop it from passages 1 and 4 and correct both layer notes. Do not correct one and leave the other — that is the Gallic Round 2 **R2** class.

## S3 — Passage 1 expands the one household scene Doc_02 §10 says "do not expand it," by transposing Katharina von Bora's sentence into a generic household's own mouth

Passage 1 closes: *"My wife says we are cold at it, compared to how they used to pray under the pope. The Doctor says the devil drives his people on, and we are ice-cold and negligent."*

The source (TT 3146–3151, verified) is a single reported exchange between two named people: Katharina asks Luther why "we are very cold and careless in praying," and Luther answers. Doc_02 §10's tier table gives this scene its own row — *"Luther catechizing his household; Kate on prayer," Tier 2, Table Talk [R31], Contested for specifics* — with the usage note, in full: **"The only household scene; do not expand it."**

Passage 1 expands it. It takes one filtered, Contested-as-verbatim, once-only utterance about one specific household — the founder's — and renders it as a generic 1529 household's own reported speech, spoken by the father about his own wife. The anchor note discloses that the saying is Contested and that it is "the one place a Contested-as-verbatim saying enters an inhabited passage"; it does **not** disclose that the utterance has been moved from one house to another. The Tier 4 rule the passage invokes (`every element cites R25, R26 or R31; no named individual`) is satisfied on its face — but satisfying it by stripping the name is exactly how the expansion happens. A secondary problem sits underneath: a household father in 1529 could not have heard a private table saying first printed in 1566.

This is the Gallic Round 1 **S2** class as Discipline 11 itself names it: *"an inhabited voice claiming as its own house's practice what its source says is prescribed or foreign."*

**Remedy.** Either cut the last two sentences of passage 1 — the passage loses nothing structurally — or keep them and mark the transposition explicitly in the layer note, disclosing that the library attests this exchange of one house only and that placing it in a reconstructed house is a licensed generalisation, not a datum. I recommend the cut, because Doc_02 §10's instruction is not a confidence tag that can be discharged by labelling; it is an instruction.

## S4 — Passage 3 is a parish-practice reconstruction, which Doc_02 §14 bars categorically; §10C's discharge line says the document contains none; and the passage is an undisclosed composite of 1526–1543

Doc_02 §14, read at source, instructs: *"Doc_05 may reconstruct household practice at Tier 4 and may not reconstruct parish practice at all."* The bar is on **reconstructing**, not on **asserting as fact** — it does not have a confidence escape hatch.

Passage 3 is written in the first-person plural present tense of someone inside the Wittenberg parish: *"We hear the Epistle sung on the eighth tone… No one goes up who has not first been examined and absolved; we go up together, those of us who are fit for it… Afterwards the boys sing Vespers… When one of us dies we sing over the grave no dirge…"* Whatever its label, that is a reconstruction of parish practice. Labelling it "as the movement itself claimed it" describes the *evidence*; it does not change what the *form* does. And §10C then states the discharge as: `passage 3 is the movement's claim, not a parish reconstruction, and says so; no parish scene anywhere` — the last four words are contradicted by the passage two hundred lines above them.

Two aggravating specifics, each checked at source:

1. **Privileged access.** *"the Doctor set the tones himself and sang them over to Walter before the first German Mass was sung here"*; *"because Paul is an earnest apostle… because our Lord Christ is a good friend"*; *"because the Doctor will not have them singing only German songs in the streets."* All three come from Walter's letter (Hy 752–778, verified), a private reminiscence written "in his old age" some forty years later, reporting a three-week consultation and Luther's own spoken reasons — `and sung them over to me to get my judgment thereon` (Hy 765). None of it is available to an anonymous Wittenberg congregant on a Sunday. The passage's anchor note correctly flags the *layer* (R45, three hands) but not the *standpoint*.
2. **Undisclosed chronological composite.** The passage's anchors span AC/Ap 1530–31, LC 1529, the first German Mass of 1526, Aurifaber's 1532 drought, the 1542 burial preface (Hy 975–987) and the 1543 hymnal preface (Hy 1009–1019) — presented as one Sunday, with no join disclosed. Passage 4 discloses its 1522/1529 join in one clause and is the better model. This is the HAL Round 1 **S2** class (a chronological error inside a passage), in its milder, compositional form.

**Remedy.** The honest options are (a) cut the passage and let §3's analytical treatment carry the worship lens — it already does, thoroughly; or (b) rebuild it in the third person as the confession's own "our churches" voice (which is what AC XXIV actually is: `the Mass is retained among us`), which keeps the movement's claim without manufacturing a congregant; or (c) escalate to the build's owner, since reading Doc_02 §14's bar as permitting a labelled parish scene is a change to an approved document's binding instruction, not a Doc_05 drafting choice. Whichever is chosen, §10C's "no parish scene anywhere" must be corrected.

## S6 — "Twelve editions of one treatise within a year" is editorial-apparatus data used unflagged, and "within a year" is not in the source

The datum appears twice, at §5 (`to print — twelve editions of one treatise (v1 12304–12309 [R85])`) and §9B (`Twelve editions of one treatise within a year (v1 12304–12309 [R85])`).

At source (v1 12301–12309, verified) this is the Philadelphia editors' bibliographical note: *"Going to press in May, the book was completed on the 26th of June. The twelve known editions are all quartos and range in size from twenty-two to thirty-two leaves."* Two problems:

1. **Layer.** Discipline 3 requires the Philadelphia editors to be "named as layer wherever drawn on and never counted as a voice." Doc_02 §6 classifies this exact datum as *"Print as the movement's own medium — documented inside the editorial apparatus"* and rates it *[Widely Accepted] as to the editors' bibliographical data*; Registry R85 types it **M/S**. Doc_05 uses it twice with no layer marking, in a list of what the world did. This is Discipline 11's own **S6/S7** class ("a de-licensed or editorial claim re-imported as documented"). The `[R85]` pointer mitigates but does not discharge — R85's Verification Note also does not say "within a year."
2. **An added claim.** The source gives a completion date and an edition count, and no time-bound on the editions. "Within a year" is the document's, not the editors'.

**Remedy.** Restate as `twelve known editions of one treatise recorded by the Philadelphia editors (v1 12304–12309 [R85], editorial)`, and drop "within a year."

## S7 — §2.6's Dependency test is decided against a criterion stricter than the Framework's own, without saying so, and two of the six tests carry no result code

The §2.6 prayer result is well argued and I do **not** think it is wrong. Three things about how it is argued need fixing, all disclosure rather than reversal — and the reviewer is asked to adjudicate the document's own named weak point (1), so this is that adjudication.

1. **The criterion is swapped mid-test.** CF para 293 states the Dependency Test in five words: *"Do other dimensions depend upon it?"* Doc_04 §10 item 5 asks whether prayer "shows prayer organizing other practices rather than sitting inside G4/G12." §2.6 answers with a third, stricter criterion: *"No practice was found that depends on prayer for its **content** the way examination depends on the catechism or the Supper on the promise."* On the Framework's own wording, §2.6's own evidence reads as a pass — prayer as the stated precondition of orderly reform (v2 14764–14766), as a third sign of forgiveness set beside the two sacraments (LC 3613–3615), as the community's whole defence (*"all our shelter and protection rest in prayer alone,"* LC 3185–3186, verified). The content-dependency criterion may well be the right one; it is not the one the Framework states, and the substitution is not declared.
2. **Two tests get no code.** §2.6 opens "The six tests, run as Doc_04 ran them," and Doc_04's declared format scores each test **P / p / F**. Repetition, Formation, Explanatory and Persistence carry P. **Dependency and Interaction carry prose only** — which happen to be the two that decide the outcome.
3. **The Interaction Test's polarity is inverted.** CF para 301–302 makes a fully-related row a *pass* and an isolated candidate the warning sign. §2.6 reads a uniformly-reinforcing row as evidence *against* gravity status. It does so on a real in-build precedent (Doc_04 §5's reading of G12's row, which I verified verbatim), so this is legitimate — but it is now the second place in this build where a uniformly-R row is read against the Framework's stated polarity, and it should be named as a reading rather than applied silently.

**Remedy.** State the content-dependency criterion as a declared narrowing with its reason; assign explicit codes to Dependency and Interaction; add one clause naming the Interaction polarity as this build's own reading. The result — fold holds, destination adds G3 and G5, flag lowered — needs no change, and it is correctly delivered as input rather than applied (Discipline 8 verified clean: no Doc_04 classification is reopened or contradicted anywhere in §2.6).
