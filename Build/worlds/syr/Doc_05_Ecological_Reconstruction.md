Per Step 5, this document reconstructs this world's formation ecology — how participants were actually shaped, not merely what they believed in the abstract — integrating forces directly as constitutive of how the ecology felt from within (Forces Framework Step 5), applying the Writing-From-Inside Principle (Article 23) throughout inhabited prose, and closing with a Proportionality Assessment and Ecological Integration Assessment.

## 1. Human Ecology

### 1.1 Formation Ecology — Practices, Habits, Disciplines

To take the qyama — to become *bar qyama* or *bat qyama*, a son or daughter of the covenant — is to stand for a promise that does not end: the vow is not a season of youth to be outgrown, but a lifelong undertaking. This is a town's own askesis, not the desert's: the *bar qyama* and *bat qyama* live among their kin, unmarried, keeping the fast and the watch, present at the vigil and the reading, formed less by flight from the world than by refusal within it. Aphrahat writes of this order to his own community as something already established and needing correction more than founding — a discipline in the middle of its life, not its first spring. **[Documented core: Aphrahat, Demonstration 6, per Doc_02/03/04. Note: the specific moral weight of what breaking the vow would mean to a member — beyond the bare fact that the vow is lifelong — is this document's own plausible inference, not something Dem. 6 itself is cited as stating; it has been trimmed from the passage above so the inhabited prose does not carry more specific dramatization than the sourcing bears.]**

Alongside the qyama stands the *iḥidaya* — the Single One, the one who has made themselves undivided for the sake of the undivided God, and who therefore stands near enough to Christ, the *Iḥidaya* in the deeper sense, that the same word can hold both without strain. To be formed as *iḥidaya* is to be told, in the very name given you, that your single-mindedness participates in something larger than your own discipline — that your undividedness is itself a small likeness of the Only-Begotten's own undividedness from the Father. **[Documented dual-attestation, both authors, per Doc_03 1.7.]**

Ephrem's own formation is harder to render in the same first-person register this document uses for the qyama, because so much of what later ages said about his path to it — his ordination, his teaching seat, his personal governance of women's choirs — rests on testimony that arrives a century or more after his death (Doc_04, Section 1). What can be said from within his own lifetime, on his own evidence, is narrower and more modest: a deacon of Edessa's church, embedded in this same qyama-adjacent order, whose formation is not narrated by him so much as performed in the sheer discipline of the corpus he left — nine major works, a full commentary on the harmonized Gospel, refutations answering three named rivals by name. If this document tried to narrate more of his personal formation than that, it would be writing legend as though it were memory, and it does not do that here. **[Documented: deacon, per Jerome; Reported-Experience Status only for anything beyond this — no inhabited narration of Ephrem's interior formation is attempted where evidence does not support it.]**

### 1.2 Emotional/Affective Reconstruction — Hopes, Fears, Emotional Weighting

On the Persian side, the register is fear held steady by defiance. Aphrahat writes his later Demonstrations — the ones on wars, on the resurrection of the body, on the true meaning of persecution — into a community that has already watched its bishops die and does not know how many more it will watch die before the killing ends. This is not abstract theology answering an abstract question; it is pastoral address to people who must decide, in their own bodies, whether the promises Aphrahat sets before them are strong enough to bear the weight being asked of them. **[Widely Accepted to Documented, per Doc_02 Sec.3, Sec.11; Doc_04 C6.]**

The fear does not resolve into simple resolve. A twenty-year silence follows Barba'shmin's death — no successor dares take a seat that means near-certain execution — and whatever hope sustained the community through that gap is not narrated in any surviving text this world's sources include. This document does not supply the missing interior account. It names the silence and lets it stand as a silence: the community endured something for two decades that its own surviving voice does not describe from the inside. **[Reported-Experience Status: the outward fact of the vacancy is Documented; its inward emotional texture is not attested and is not invented here.]**

On the Roman side, the emotional register Ephrem's corpus makes audible is different in kind: not the fear of the executioner but the anxiety of the crowded room — the sense that truth is contested by rivals close enough to be mistaken for it. Ephrem's hymns against Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani carry real heat, real urgency, but it is the urgency of a teacher who fears his own hearers might wander into a plausible-sounding error next door, not the urgency of a community counting its dead. **[Documented, per Doc_02 Sec.3; Doc_04 C3.]**

## 2. Community Ecology

### 2.1 Meaning Transmission — Teaching, Storytelling, Apprenticeship

Meaning moves in this world mostly by ear before it moves by eye. Ephrem's madrashe belong to a sung, stanzaic genre that Bardaisan and Mani had already made their own before him — a form he takes up and masters rather than invents, so that the genre a rival's hymns had already made familiar now carries orthodox words instead. To transmit meaning here is, in part, to contest the same genre-ground a rival already occupied — not to invent a wholly separate mode of address but to answer a neighbor within a shared literary form. **[Genre-level adoption Documented, per Doc_03 1.4 — this is the specific, narrower claim the sourcing supports; a stronger claim about matching a rival's specific meter or refrain-structure would overstate what Doc_03 establishes and is not made here. Doc_04 C1/C3 Interaction.]**

Aphrahat transmits differently: through *taḥwyāṯā*, sustained demonstrations built on the twenty-two-letter acrostic, each one working systematically through a topic the way a teacher walks a student through an argument stage by stage, letter by letter, so that the very alphabet becomes a scaffold for holding doctrine in the memory. **[Documented, per Doc_03 1.3.]**

### 2.2 Memory Structures

Memory here is contested memory. The *Chronicle of Edessa*'s own report of the 201 flood — the church destroyed, later remembered as this world's own earliest trace — was itself already old before this world's writers took it up; it is memory of memory, archivally preserved rather than eyewitnessed. **[Documented as archivally-derived, per Doc_02 Sec.5, 9.]** And on the Persian side, memory takes a martyrological cast: named bishops — Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles of Susa, Acepsimas, Mareas, Bicor — held in a community's memory not as distant founders but as immediate, nameable losses whose deaths a living generation could still recount. **[Documented per Doc_02 Sec.11.]**

### 2.3 Boundary Structures

To be formed in this world is, in part, to be formed against three names: Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani. A hearer of Ephrem's refutations does not encounter these as distant heresiarchs from a textbook but as real, remembered neighbors — Bardaisan a figure whose own circle still walked Edessa's streets in living memory, Mani's missionaries still moving through nearby Mesopotamia in the same generation. The boundary is not a settled doctrine so much as an active, ongoing act of distinguishing "us" from a rival close enough to be mistaken for us. **[Documented, per Doc_01 Sec.5/7; Doc_02 Sec.8; Doc_04 C3.]** This boundary-work is, per Doc_04's own finding, substantially one figure's own rhetorical achievement rather than an even, world-wide practice — a fact this document preserves rather than smooths into a claim that every believer in this world drew these same lines with the same intensity.

The record is honest about whose boundary-drawing this mostly is, and equally honest about whose voices never appear at all: no enslaved or lower-status believer's own account survives from this world in any form; no bnat qyama woman's own composition survives; Jewish dialogue partners appear only refracted through Aphrahat's own polemic, never in their own voice. **[Documented, per Harvey, already carried in Doc_02 Sec.7.]** This document does not manufacture what these absent voices might have said. It names the absence as itself part of this world's own ecology — a community whose surviving self-portrait is clerical, ascetic, and male, and whose fuller life is simply not recoverable from what remains.

## 3. Worship Ecology

### 3.1 Worship Integration

Worship here is where doctrine, memory, belonging, and time are held together rather than kept in separate rooms. The harmonized Gospel — read, sung about, commented upon — is the shared narrative spine both anchor communities gather around: not four books held in tension with one another but a single continuous story, so that the formation experience of hearing the Gospel in this world is materially different from a community reading four separate accounts. **[Documented use, per Doc_01 Sec.4; Doc_02 Sec.2; Doc_04 C5.]** Ephrem's own decision to write a full commentary on this harmonized text — an unusual investment of a teacher's labor — signals how central this single-narrative experience of the Gospel was to worship's own shape here.

Worship also carries the raza: to hear Ephrem's madrashe in worship is to be trained, hymn by hymn, in a way of hearing Scripture and creation itself as bound to the truth they signify rather than standing apart from it as mere illustration. Worship is where this hermeneutic is not merely taught but performed — sung into the body along with the tune. **[Documented, per Doc_02 Sec.2/3; Doc_04 C1.]**

Worship shapes belonging concretely through the qyama and bnat qyama choirs: to sing in that choir is to occupy a body-visible place inside worship's own structure, distinct from ordinary lay presence. **[Documented, per Doc_02 Sec.3.]** Worship shapes time through the liturgical calendar's own disputed points — Nisibis, in Ephrem's lifetime, kept a single combined Nativity-Epiphany observance rather than the later separated feasts, meaning worship's own calendar was still settling its shape within this world's own window. **[Documented, per Beck's finding, per Doc_02 Sec.3.]**

## 4. Organizational Ecology

### 4.1 Authority Structures

This is the dimension where this document must be most honest about the limits of what it can render from within, because Doc_04's own testing (C4) found the evidence here speaks more to modern reconstruction difficulty than to lived formation experience — C4 failed its own Formation test rather than passing it nominally. This document does not manufacture the missing inward account.

What can be said: on the Persian side, a real episcopal structure existed — Papa bar Aggai, Simeon bar Sabbae, and successors through Isaac — but its own continuity was fragile enough that a twenty-year gap opened after Barba'shmin's martyrdom, during which no successor is named. **[Documented/Widely Accepted at the level of the structure's existence; Contested/Inferential-Thin at the level of exact dates, per Doc_02 Sec.11.]** Aphrahat's own place within any such structure is itself unresolved — he may have held real authority without holding episcopal office, or may have held a title now lost to the record; this document does not resolve what Doc_01 and Doc_02 both left open, and repeats their finding rather than quietly picking a side.

On the Roman side, Ephrem's own position — deacon, embedded in the qyama order — is comparatively legible, but this document does not extend that legibility into a claim that the wider community's own sense of "who has standing to teach" was equally settled; Doc_04's own Interaction finding (C2×C4) suggests the qyama order's own charismatic, vow-based standing may itself have functioned as an authority pathway running alongside, not merely beneath, ordained office — a live tension this document names rather than resolves. **[Reported-Experience Status: the structural ambiguity is Documented; how it was actually negotiated day to day is not.]**

### 4.2 Formation Logic

This subsection must carry forward, not quietly drop, Section 4.1's own concession that C4 failed its Formation test: this world's evidence establishes that more than one channel of teaching authority existed structurally — ordained office, charismatic vow-holding, and (on the Persian side) an episcopal seat that could stand vacant for two decades — but it does not establish how believers actually experienced or reconciled that plurality day to day. The claim that formation "tolerated" or "required" living without one clean answer is this document's own structural inference from the plurality of channels, not a lived-experience finding Doc_01–04 actually support. **[Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin: the plurality of authority channels is Documented; any claim about how that plurality was actually negotiated, tolerated, or felt by a believer forming within it is this document's own inference, offered provisionally and revisable at Step 7, not an established finding.]**

## 5. Ministry Ecology

### 5.1 Interpretive Ecology

To interpret Scripture and the world in this ecology is to read by raza: every text, every event, every created thing potentially bears a hidden power pointing toward the truth (shrara) it signifies, most fully realized in Christ. This is not allegory in the Alexandrian sense of a code to be cracked once and set aside — the symbol keeps its own reality even as it points beyond itself. **[Documented for Ephrem; Widely Accepted lighter parallel in Aphrahat, per Doc_02 Sec.2; Doc_03 1.1; Doc_04 C1.]** Aphrahat's own interpretive practice runs alongside this with a heavier debt to Jewish exegetical method — his anti-Jewish Demonstrations are evidence of a live, textured argument with a real interlocutor tradition rather than a generic literary exercise. **[Widely Accepted, per Koltun-Fromm's reading, Doc_02 Sec.3/4.]**

### 5.2 Representative Theological Patterns

Two representative patterns emerge, neither reducible to the other. Ephrem represents a poetic-typological pattern: doctrine argued through hymn, symbol, and polemical refutation of named rivals, addressed to a community whose most pressing formation task is telling true faith apart from plausible neighbors. Aphrahat represents a pastoral-apologetic pattern: doctrine argued through sustained prose demonstration, addressed to a community whose most pressing formation task is enduring a hostile state without losing its own coherence. **[Documented, per Doc_02 Sec.3; Doc_04 C1/C3/C6.]**

### 5.3 Power, Influence, and Historical Dynamics

History's impact on this world is asymmetric and concrete: Roman-administered Edessa experiences pressure as doctrinal crowding — real neighboring claimants to the same religious space — while Persian-administered territory experiences pressure as the state's own violence, intensifying under Shapur II from the 340s and continuing in force through the 370s, fracturing the episcopal structure itself for two decades. **[Documented, per Doc_01 Sec.7; Doc_02 Sec.11; Doc_04 C3/C6.]** The world's own impact on history is narrower but real: Marutha of Maiperqat's embassies, drawing on trust built through this world's own final decades, help secure the 410 Synod and the Persian church's first formal state toleration. **[The embassies, the Synod, and the toleration are Documented, per Doc_02 Sec.11; framing this as "this world's own closing act is itself a hinge point for what follows it" is this document's own interpretive synthesis, not a status Doc_02 itself assigns, and is offered at that lower confidence.]**

## 6. Forces Integration Summary (Forces Framework Step 5)

Per the Forces Framework's own requirement, external forces are treated above as constitutive, not as background: Shapur II's persecution is integrated directly into Human Ecology (1.2, the fear/endurance register), Community Ecology (2.2, martyrological memory), and Organizational Ecology (4.1, the succession's fracture) rather than named once and set aside. Doctrinal rivalry (Bardaisan/Marcion/Mani) is integrated into Community Ecology (2.3, boundary-work as lived practice) and Ministry Ecology (5.2, Ephrem's representative pattern) rather than treated as a one-time boundary determination already settled in Doc_01. No force is invoked in a lens where this world's own evidence does not actually connect it — Roman political administration, for instance, is not forced into the Persian-side persecution narrative, and vice versa.

**Gap named rather than left silent:** Worship Ecology (3.1) and Interpretive Ecology (5.1) as currently written contain essentially no forces-constitutive material, even though Doc_04's own C1 forces-connection notation supplies the connective tissue — the raza/shrara method's polemical intensification under doctrinal-rival pressure appears specifically in hymn-genre works (the *Contra Haereses* madrashe), meaning worship itself was, at least in this one respect, a contested space shaped by external pressure, not only a serene one. This document's current 3.1/5.1 treatment does not render that contested dimension and should be revisited at Step 7 rather than left as an unflagged gap.

## 7. Proportionality Assessment

Attention given above is proportional to evidence, not to probable-but-unattested ecology, and the two are named separately where they diverge — but this proportionality is uneven across lenses rather than uniform, and this section says so specifically rather than generalizing. Worship Ecology (Section 3) is genuinely and justifiably Ephrem-heavy: Doc_02 (Section 3) states plainly that Aphrahat's text "offers almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail," so the imbalance there is proportional to a real evidentiary asymmetry. Human Ecology (Section 1) is not uniformly Ephrem-heavy in the same way — on inspection, the Persian/Aphrahat register (1.2) is the fuller, more vivid passage, appropriately reflecting the richer emotional evidence Doc_02/04 supply for the persecution-endurance gravity (C6), while Ephrem's own formation passage (1.1) is long chiefly because of hedging about what cannot be said (the choir-leadership exclusion) rather than because of richer positive content. Ministry Ecology (Section 5) is mixed: 5.1 leans Ephrem-heavy, while 5.2 and 5.3 are close to balanced. This document does not claim a single blanket asymmetry where the actual pattern is more particular to each lens. Separately, and applying regardless of which side of the world is fuller in a given lens: ordinary lay experience, women's own voice, and non-elite formation are structurally absent from both anchor contexts almost equally (Doc_02 Sec.7) — this document names that gap explicitly rather than letting either author's fuller textual record stand in for a fuller reconstructed life.

## 8. Ecological Integration Assessment

The strongest relationships in this ecology, checked directly against Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix rather than asserted independently, are Worship↔Interpretation (raza is performed in worship, not merely taught about it — consistent with C1's reinforcing relationships to C3 and C5) and Human↔Organizational (the qyama/Iḥidaya institution, C2, reshapes C4's own authority-ambiguity by supplying a charismatic authority pathway alongside episcopal office — matching Doc_04's own C2×C4 "Reshaping" finding exactly). This document does not assert a Community↔Organizational relationship between boundary-work (C3) and authority-ambiguity (C4): Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix records the C3×C4 cell as "No demonstrated relationship," and this document does not assert a relationship its own governing prior document tested for and found absent. The clearest ecological hub is the raza/shrara method (C1), reaching into Worship, Interpretation, and Community boundary-work alike, consistent with Doc_04's own Interaction Matrix. The clearest tension point is Organizational Ecology's authority ambiguity (C4) — a condition this world seems to have lived with rather than resolved, per Doc_01's own strand-singular finding. The qyama/Iḥidaya institution (C2) functions as connective tissue between Human Ecology (formation) and Organizational Ecology (an alternative authority pathway), a relationship this document renders explicitly rather than leaving implicit.

What remains most uncertain is precisely what Doc_04 already flagged: how the authority ambiguity was actually experienced day to day, and what the interior life of the Persian community's twenty-year succession gap actually felt like from within. This document declines to fill either gap with invented texture.
