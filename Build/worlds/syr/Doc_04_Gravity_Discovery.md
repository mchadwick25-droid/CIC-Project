## 0. Purpose and Scope

Per Step 4 of the Construction Framework, this document identifies the organizing gravities of this world's formation ecology — the commitments, practices, and tensions the community actually centered on, as distinct from topics that merely recur without organizing anything. Every candidate below is generated from traceable Doc_02 (Source Ecology) evidence streams, not general impression, and cross-referenced against Doc_01's Preliminary Forces Identification and Doc_03's candidate vocabulary. Every candidate is tested against the six named gravity tests (Repetition, Dependency, Formation, Explanatory, Persistence, Interaction), cross-checked against the fixed five-level confidence vocabulary (Constitution Article 17), tested for cross-strand status (Article 21), given a forces-connection notation per the Forces Framework's own Step 4 requirement, and classified Primary, Supporting, or Tensional.

**Note on "gravity" versus "force":** these are distinct categories in this project's methodology. A force (Article 22) is an external or internal pressure acting on the world — persecution, political status, rival movements. A gravity is the community's own organizing response or commitment. This document does not list forces as candidate gravities; where a force (e.g., Sasanian persecution) shapes a gravity, that relationship is recorded in the forces-connection notation, not as a separate gravity in its own right.

**Strand status:** Doc_01, Section 6, found this world strand-singular, with the authority-structure question deliberately left open rather than resolved into two strands. Per Article 21, cross-strand testing is therefore not applicable in the plural sense (there is one strand to test within); however, where a candidate's evidence diverges sharply between Ephrem's Edessa-context and Aphrahat's Persian-context material, that divergence is recorded explicitly as an Author Gravity / regional-emphasis consideration, consistent with Doc_01's own decision not to split this world into strands on the strength of such divergence alone.

## 1. Candidates Considered and Not Advanced to Testing

Per this project's transparency discipline, the following were considered and explicitly not advanced, with reasons:

- **Source-record asymmetry / silenced voices (Doc_02, Section 7).** This characterizes a limitation of the evidence base itself — what is missing from the record — not an organizing commitment the community centered its own formation on. It remains load-bearing for the Affirmative Duty (Article 20) but is not a gravity candidate.
- **Edessa's Roman-administered political status (Doc_01, Section 3).** This is background/force context (an external political fact), not an internal organizing commitment. Where relevant, it is carried into the forces-connection notation below rather than tested as its own gravity.
- **The Diatessaron-to-Peshitta transition itself (Doc_01, Section 4).** This is a temporal/textual-transition event at the world's own closing edge, not a sustained organizing gravity within the 200–410 window; the Diatessaron's normative use *during* the window is tested below as Candidate 5.
- **Ephrem's own personal leadership of the *bnat qyama* choirs.** Real and worth naming, but excluded from candidacy — and, on closer inspection, excluded from C2's own evidentiary basis (see C2 below) — because its primary attestation (Jacob of Serugh's 6th-century panegyric on Ephrem; the 6th-century Syriac *Vita Ephraemi*) falls outside this world's own 200–410 temporal boundary (Doc_01, Section 2). This is not merely a lower-confidence in-window claim; it is a later reception-history claim about this world, more properly the concern of Doc_09 (Story Repository) than of this document's own gravity testing.
- **Ephrem's attested clerical office (deacon).** Real and well-sourced (Doc_02, Section 3; Jerome's *De Viris Illustribus* 115, calling him "deacon of the church of Edessa"). Not advanced as an independent candidate gravity because it functions here as a comparandum data point *within* C4's own authority-structure analysis (Ephrem's institutional position is comparatively unambiguous, in contrast to Aphrahat's) rather than as a distinct organizing commitment in its own right — it describes one figure's personal standing rather than a community-wide practice or commitment the way C1's method or C2's ascetic order do.
- **Formation-narrative/civic memory (Doctrina Addai, Chronicle of Edessa).** Doc_02 (Section 5) already frames these as this world's own foundation myth and archival record respectively, not as attested organizing practices — the Doctrina Addai's historical claims are explicitly non-historical per Doc_01's own finding. Considered and declined as a gravity candidate on this basis, consistent with Doc_02's own characterization.

## 2. Candidate Gravities

### C1 — Symbolic/Typological Theological Method (raza / shrara)

**Generated from:** Doc_02, Section 2 (Ephrem's full corpus, Aphrahat's typological usage) and Section 3 (Author Gravity Assessment, Influence dimension); Doc_03, Section 1.1 (Tier 1 candidate).

**Author Gravity risk flagged at generation:** Moderate-high. The fullest articulation ("hidden power," *hayla kasya*) is substantially Brock's synthesis of Ephrem specifically; Aphrahat's cognate usage is real but lighter (Doc_03, 1.1). This candidate is tested as *primarily an Ephrem-anchored gravity with a lighter, genuinely attested Aphrahat parallel*, not as equally weighted across both voices.

**Six-test results:**
- *Repetition:* Recurs across Ephrem's madrashe, prose refutations, and biblical commentaries (Doc_02, Section 2), and in Aphrahat's typological Christ-figuration (lighter). Present across multiple evidence streams. **Pass.**
- *Dependency:* Doc_01's own cultural-scope finding (Section 4) and Doc_02's Author Gravity Assessment both treat this as the substrate other claims about Ephrem's "doctrinal method" depend on. Other dimensions (Ephrem's heresiological rhetoric, C3 below) draw on this same hermeneutic. **Pass.**
- *Formation:* Directly shapes how participants encountered doctrine — through symbol and type rather than syllogism (Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 3). **Pass.**
- *Explanatory:* Explains Ephrem's choice of genre (madrasha, Doc_03 1.4), his exegetical commentaries, and his approach to Old Testament material in both authors. **Pass.**
- *Persistence:* Attested across Ephrem's entire surviving corpus (350s–373) and in Aphrahat's earlier Demonstrations (336/7–345) — persists across the bulk of this world's own window. **Pass.**
- *Interaction:* Reinforces C3 (the polemical use of typology against Bardaisan/Marcion/Mani draws on the same raza/shrara logic) and C5 (typological reading is how the Diatessaron's harmonized narrative was itself interpreted). No competing relationship identified. **Pass — see Interaction Matrix.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidential confidence is **Documented** for Ephrem's own textual practice (his corpus directly attests it) and **Widely Accepted** for Aphrahat's lighter parallel usage. Organizing strength and evidential confidence align — no divergence to flag.

**Cross-strand status:** Not applicable in the plural sense (strand-singular, Doc_01 Section 6). Regional/author-gravity note: heavily Ephrem-anchored; Aphrahat's attestation is real but should not be presented as co-equal (per Doc_03, 1.1).

**Forces-connection notation:** Doc_02's Forces Lens (Section 9) names Ephrem's rhetoric as partly *constructed against* Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani — real external doctrinal rivals in the same milieu (Doc_01, Section 5, 7). This gravity **intensified** under that external pressure: the typological/symbolic method became sharper and more explicitly polemical in works like the *Prose Refutations* and *Contra Haereses*, which use the same raza/shrara logic to argue against rival cosmologies. It does not appear to have fractured or shifted in kind — it held under pressure and was deployed more pointedly.

**Classification: Primary.**

### C2 — Covenanted Ascetic Life (qyama / bnay-bnat qyama / Ihidaya)

**Generated from:** Doc_02, Section 2 (bnay/bnat qyama inventory) and Section 7 (bnat qyama as missing-voices case); Doc_03, Sections 1.2 and 1.7 (both Tier 1 candidates).

**Scope note:** This candidate's evidentiary basis is deliberately narrowed to what is directly attested within this world's own 200–410 window in both authors' own surviving text: Aphrahat's Demonstration 6 (the institutional core) and the *Ihidaya* material (dual-attested, Doc_03 1.7). The claim that Ephrem personally organized or led the *bnat qyama* choirs is **excluded from this candidate's evidentiary basis**, not merely flagged within it — its own primary attestation (Jacob of Serugh, *Vita Ephraemi*, both 6th-century) falls outside this world's temporal boundary entirely (see Section 1). This narrowing is what allows C2's classification below to be cleanly earned rather than borrowed from an attached weaker claim.

**Author Gravity risk flagged at generation:** Low. Both remaining evidentiary streams (Aphrahat/Demonstration 6; *Ihidaya* in both authors) are direct, firsthand, contemporary, and independently Documented — a genuinely low-risk candidate once the out-of-scope Ephrem-choir claim is removed.

**Six-test results:**
- *Repetition:* Attested in Aphrahat (Dem. 6, direct) and in the *Ihidaya* material (both authors, directly, Doc_03 1.7). **Pass.**
- *Dependency:* This world's cultural/institutional scope (Doc_01, Section 4) is partly defined by this institution; Doc_02's own missing-voices analysis (Section 7) depends on qyama's existence as the frame for the bnat qyama silence finding, even though that finding itself concerns women's voices generally rather than resting on the excluded Ephrem-choir claim specifically. **Pass.**
- *Formation:* Directly organized participants' lived practice — vow-taking, town-resident celibacy, communal identity distinct from desert monasticism. **Pass.**
- *Explanatory:* Explains the ascetic-clerical register of the surviving corpus (Doc_02, Section 3) and the structural absence of lay/ordinary voices (Doc_02, Section 7). **Pass.**
- *Persistence:* Attested from Aphrahat's earliest Demonstrations (336/7 CE) onward, within this world's own window. **Pass.**
- *Interaction:* Reinforces C1 (the *iḥidaya* material's dual ascetic/christological register connects to Ephrem's own symbolic-method voice, Doc_02 Section 3) and reshapes C4 — the qyama order's own charismatic/ascetic leadership is itself one of the two authority-legitimation pathways at stake in C4's own ambiguity (an alternative to episcopal consecration). No demonstrated relationship to C6 was found: Doc_02's persecution/martyrological record (Section 11) centers specifically on the episcopal hierarchy (Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles, etc.), and no evidence was located of qyama members being targeted as a distinct group, as opposed to bishops and clergy generally — this absence is reported honestly rather than assumed by analogy. **Pass — see Interaction Matrix.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidential confidence is **Documented** for both remaining evidentiary streams (Aphrahat/Demonstration 6; *Ihidaya* dual-attestation). No divergence to flag — this is the direct result of narrowing this candidate to its in-window evidentiary streams, which removes the one sub-claim that previously created a divergence.

**Cross-strand status:** Not applicable in the plural sense. No sharp regional divergence — both anchor contexts attest the (narrowed) institutional core.

**Forces-connection notation:** No direct forces-test evidence was located showing this gravity shifting under external pressure within the 200–410 window — it appears to have **held** steadily as a stable institutional form across the period covered by both authors. This is itself worth naming: not every gravity is forces-reactive, and this document does not manufacture a forces connection where the evidence does not support one.

**Classification: Primary.** (Now cleanly earned — every remaining evidentiary stream independently reaches Documented confidence, per the Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check's own requirement.)

### C3 — Heresiological Self-Definition Against Named Rivals (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani)

**Generated from:** Doc_01, Sections 5 and 7 (Bardaisan boundary case; Preliminary Forces Identification); Doc_02, Sections 3 (Ephrem's Influence dimension) and 8 (Boundary Cases); Doc_03, Section 1.4 (madrasha genre's polemical origin).

**Author Gravity risk flagged at generation:** High — this gravity's evidentiary base is almost entirely Ephrem's own corpus (*Prose Refutations*, *Contra Haereses*), which Doc_02 (Section 3) already found to be substantially Ephrem's own rhetorical construction (per Ruani, who calls him "the founder of Syriac heresiology outright"). Aphrahat's Demonstrations do not engage Bardaisan, Marcion, or Mani by name. This candidate is tested as an Ephrem-specific gravity, not a world-wide one.

**Six-test results:**
- *Repetition:* Recurs across multiple Ephrem works: the *Prose Refutations* (dedicated to all three), *Contra Haereses*, and — per Doc_03's madrasha entry — the very choice of genre itself (adopted partly to out-compete Bardaisan's and Mani's own use of madrashe, per Brock). **Pass, within Ephrem's corpus specifically.**
- *Dependency:* Doc_01's own Section A task (Bardaisan clearance test) and Strand Determination both depend on this boundary-work being real and load-bearing. **Pass.**
- *Formation:* Shaped how Ephrem's audience understood orthodoxy — not as an abstract position but as defined against specific, named, locally-known rivals. **Pass.**
- *Explanatory:* Explains the *Prose Refutations*' existence, the triad's recurring co-mention (Doc_02, Section 8), and Doc_01's own finding that this world sits close to Bardaisan's floor-proximity risk precisely because it had to argue against him at length. **Pass.**
- *Persistence:* Attested across Ephrem's career but not demonstrated in Aphrahat at all, and no evidence located that this specific triad-focused polemic continued as a live organizing concern into the 373–410 window (Doc_02, Section 11's inventory of that window does not surface it). **Partial pass** — persists within Ephrem's own lifetime and corpus, not demonstrated as persisting world-wide across the full 200–410 span.
- *Interaction:* Reinforces C1 (same typological logic deployed polemically) and competes, in a documented sense, with a hypothetical "ignore heterodox neighbors" alternative that this world's own record shows it did not take. No relationship to C6 (Persian persecution) was found — these operate in different registers (doctrinal rivalry vs. state pressure) and different regions. **Pass, with one no-demonstrated-relationship noted honestly (C3–C6) rather than invented.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidential confidence is **Documented** for the fact that Ephrem wrote against this triad; **Widely Accepted** for the specific claim that this shaped his choice of genre (Brock's own argument, per Doc_03). No divergence between organizing strength and evidential confidence — both are solid, but solid specifically for Ephrem, not the whole world.

**Cross-strand status:** Not applicable in the plural sense. This is the clearest case of regional/author-gravity concentration in this document: this gravity is real and strong for Ephrem's Edessa context and essentially unattested for Aphrahat's Persian context. Per Doc_01's strand-singular finding, this is recorded as an Author Gravity concentration, not elevated into a strand split.

**Forces-connection notation:** Per Doc_01 (Section 7) and Doc_02 (Section 3), this gravity exists *because of* the external pressure of real, contemporary rival movements occupying the same religious milieu — Bardaisan's own continuing circle, Marcion's dissemination, and Manichaean missionary activity in nearby Mesopotamia (Doc_02, Section 8). Under that pressure, this gravity **intensified**: Ephrem did not merely note these rivals but built sustained, genre-spanning literary campaigns against them. There is no evidence of this gravity later fracturing or shifting into a different form within this world's own window — Ephrem's death (373) is simply where the direct textual record of it ends, not a documented fracture point.

**Classification: Supporting.** (Real, well-evidenced, and load-bearing for Doc_01's own boundary-work — but concentrated in one author/region rather than demonstrated as organizing the whole world, which keeps it below Primary.)

### C4 — Authority-Structure Ambiguity (Charismatic-Teacher Standing vs. Episcopal Legitimacy)

**Generated from:** Doc_01, Section 6 (Strand Determination, authority-structure question left deliberately open); Doc_02, Section 11 (Persian episcopal succession development; Aphrahat's own episcopal status still unresolved).

**Author Gravity risk flagged at generation:** Moderate. The clearest evidence is Aphrahat-side (his own contested status) and Persian-succession-side (the chronicle-derived, hagiographically-inflected episcopal list, Doc_02 Section 11). Ephrem's own institutional position (deacon, embedded in the qyama order — Doc_02, Section 3) is comparatively well-attested and less ambiguous, so this candidate's "ambiguity" character is itself unevenly distributed across the two anchor contexts.

**Six-test results:**
- *Repetition:* Recurs across Doc_01's own Strand Determination reasoning, Doc_02's Persian succession material (a multi-see structure existing but severely disrupted, including a twenty-year primatial vacancy), and the unresolved Aphrahat-episcopal-status question. **Pass.**
- *Dependency:* Doc_01's entire Strand Determination finding depends on this ambiguity being real and unresolved rather than resolved either way — the strand-singular conclusion explicitly rests on bracketing this question rather than answering it. **Pass.**
- *Formation:* The evidence base speaks to modern reconstruction difficulty (how confidently this project can characterize the world's authority structure) rather than to how the ambiguity was actively lived and experienced by participants at the time. **Fail, carried forward as an open item rather than smoothed into a nominal pass.** A test failure here is consistent with, not disqualifying for, this candidate's Tensional classification — a persistent condition the world itself did not resolve is not required to clear every test cleanly to be named as a real, unresolved organizing tension.
- *Explanatory:* Explains why Doc_01 could not resolve Strand Determination cleanly and why Aphrahat's own title ("Mar Jacob," not a clearly episcopal title) remains a live scholarly question (Doc_03, Section 1.8). **Pass.**
- *Persistence:* The ambiguity persists across the documented Persian succession's full span (Papa bar Aggai through Isaac, 280s–410) and across Aphrahat's own career — not a momentary gap but a sustained condition. **Pass.**
- *Interaction:* Reshapes how C6 (persecution) is best understood — the succession's twenty-year vacancy (Doc_02, Section 11) is itself a *product* of persecution-era disruption, meaning C4 and C6 stand in a reshaping relationship (persecution reshaped the authority structure's own continuity) rather than mere co-occurrence. **Pass — see Interaction Matrix.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** This is the clearest case of intentional divergence in this document. Evidential confidence for the *underlying facts* (a multi-see structure existed; Aphrahat's status is unresolved; a vacancy occurred) is **Widely Accepted to Documented**. But confidence in *how this ambiguity actually functioned as a lived, organizing reality* — as opposed to a gap in the modern record — is **Contested/Inferential-Thin**. This divergence is named explicitly, not resolved by upgrading: the gravity is real as an evidentiary and methodological finding, but weaker as a claim about lived formation experience.

**Cross-strand status:** Not applicable in the plural sense — and this is precisely the candidate Doc_01 itself declined to resolve into a strand split. This document does not revisit that decision; it tests the ambiguity as a documented condition rather than attempting to settle it now.

**Forces-connection notation:** Per Doc_02 (Section 11), the Persian episcopal succession's own continuity **fractured** under Shapur II's persecution (the twenty-year vacancy after Barba'shmin's martyrdom is a direct, named instance of a structure fracturing under external force, not merely shifting). This is one of the clearest forces-connections in this document precisely because the fracture is independently documented rather than inferred.

**Classification: Tensional.** (A persistent, unresolved organizing tension the world itself did not resolve within its own window — exactly the kind of finding the Tensional category exists for.)

### C5 — The Diatessaron as Normative Harmonized Gospel

**Generated from:** Doc_01, Section 4 (cultural scope, Diatessaron); Doc_02, Section 2 (Diatessaron detailed inventory); Doc_03, Section 1.6 (Ewangeliyon da-Mhallete candidate term).

**Author Gravity risk flagged at generation:** Low-moderate. Both anchor authors attest the Diatessaron's use (Aphrahat quotes it; Ephrem wrote a full Commentary on it), which is a genuine strength — but the vernacular name's own dating is unsettled (Doc_03, 1.6), so this candidate's *textual content and liturgical use* is well-attested while its *specific vernacular naming* should not be leaned on for dating precision.

**Six-test results:**
- *Repetition:* Attested in both Aphrahat and Ephrem, independently. **Pass.**
- *Dependency:* Ephrem's entire *Commentary on the Diatessaron* depends on this text's normative status; the later Diatessaron-to-Peshitta transition (Doc_01, Section 4) is only a meaningful boundary marker because the Diatessaron was normative before it. **Pass.**
- *Formation:* Shaped how this world's participants encountered the Gospel narrative — as a single harmonized text rather than four discrete Gospels, a materially different formation experience than the post-410 world. **Pass.**
- *Explanatory:* Explains why Ephrem produced a dedicated Commentary (an unusual textual investment) and why the eventual shift to the "separated" Gospels (post-410, Rabbula-era) counts as a real world-transition rather than a minor textual variant (Doc_01, Section 4). **Pass.**
- *Persistence:* Persists across the bulk of this world's window (attested by Aphrahat in the 330s–340s and still the text Ephrem comments on in the 360s–370s) but is understood to end at or near this world's own 410 boundary. **Pass, bounded — persistence is real but time-limited to this world's own span, which is appropriate rather than a weakness.**
- *Interaction:* Reinforces C1 (the Diatessaron's harmonized narrative was read through the same typological/symbolic method) and stands in a documented "ending" relationship with the world's own boundary (its supersession by the Peshitta tradition is part of what defines this world's closing edge, per Doc_01). **Pass — see Interaction Matrix.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidential confidence is **Documented** for the Diatessaron's use and content within this window; **Contested** for the specific vernacular name's own dating (Doc_03, 1.6). No divergence between organizing strength and confidence in the *use* claim; the naming caveat is a separate, narrower issue already flagged and does not undercut the gravity's own classification.

**Cross-strand status:** Not applicable in the plural sense. No sharp regional divergence — both anchor contexts attest it.

**Forces-connection notation:** No clear evidence the Diatessaron's own normative status shifted or fractured under external force *within* this world's own 200–410 window — its supersession happens at or just after the boundary (early 5th century, under/after Rabbula), which is precisely why Doc_01 treats that transition as marking this world's own ending edge rather than an internal dynamic to explain. This gravity **held** steadily throughout the window and its eventual replacement is better understood as this world's own closing transition than as a mid-window forces-response.

**Classification: Supporting.** (Real, well-attested, world-spanning — but narrower in explanatory reach than C1 or C2, since it organizes scriptural engagement specifically rather than the fuller range of doctrinal and communal life.)

### C6 — Endurance Under State Persecution as a Formation Ideal

**Generated from:** Doc_02, Section 3 (Aphrahat's Influence dimension — Demonstrations 11–23 composed during Shapur II's persecution) and Section 11 (Persian martyrological record: Shahdost, Barba'shmin, Milles of Susa, Acepsimas, Mareas, Bicor, and roughly twenty other bishops and 250 clergy).

**Author Gravity risk flagged at generation:** High. This candidate's evidentiary base is almost entirely Aphrahat/Persian-side; no comparable Ephrem/Edessa-side attestation of state persecution as an organizing formation pressure was located in Doc_01 or Doc_02 (Edessa's own pressures, per Doc_01/02, were doctrinal-rivalry-shaped, not state-persecution-shaped, within this window). This candidate is tested as a Persian-context-concentrated gravity, explicitly.

**Six-test results:**
- *Repetition:* Recurs across Aphrahat's Demonstration 5 ("On Wars"), Demonstrations 21–23 (composed 344–345, during active persecution), and the Persian martyrological record spanning multiple named bishops and clergy over several decades. **Pass, within the Persian-context evidence stream specifically.**
- *Dependency:* Doc_02's own framing of Aphrahat's "defensive, apologetic register" (Section 3, Influence dimension) depends on this pressure being real and sustained, not incidental. **Pass.**
- *Formation:* Directly shaped Aphrahat's own pastoral address to his community — Doc_02 names this explicitly as shaping "the Persian side of this world as reconstructed here." **Pass.**
- *Explanatory:* Explains the content and tone shift between Aphrahat's earlier (336/7) and later (344–345) Demonstrations, and explains the twenty-year Catholicosate vacancy (Doc_02, Section 11) as a structural consequence rather than an unexplained gap. **Pass.**
- *Persistence:* Persists across a multi-decade span (Shapur II's persecution is conventionally dated from the 340s and continues in force through the 370s), longer than a momentary crisis. **Pass, within the Persian-context stream.**
- *Interaction:* Reshapes C4 (the authority-structure ambiguity, via the vacancy) and stands in no demonstrated relationship to C3 (Ephrem's doctrinal-rivalry gravity) or C5 (the Diatessaron) — these operate in different registers and, on the evidence available, do not reinforce, compete, or reshape one another. This absence of relationship is recorded honestly rather than manufactured. **Pass, with two honest no-relationship findings.**

**Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check:** Evidential confidence is **Widely Accepted to Documented** for the persecution itself and its effects on the episcopal succession (Doc_02, Section 11); **Widely Accepted** for its shaping effect on Aphrahat's own text. No divergence between organizing strength and confidence — both are solid, but solid specifically for the Persian-context stream.

**Cross-strand status:** Not applicable in the plural sense — but this is the second-clearest case (after C3) of a gravity that is strong in one anchor context and essentially unattested in the other. Recorded as an Author Gravity / regional concentration per Doc_01's strand-singular framework, not elevated into a strand split.

**Forces-connection notation:** This gravity *is itself the community's organizing response to* the external force of Sasanian state persecution (already named in Doc_01, Section 7, and developed further in Doc_02, Section 11) — making the forces-connection here unusually direct rather than inferential. Under that pressure, the gravity **intensified** through the 340s (Aphrahat's later Demonstrations) and the surrounding episcopal structure **fractured** (the twenty-year vacancy), while the underlying formation ideal of endurance itself did not disappear — Tomarsa's restoration (363–371) and the eventual 410 toleration under Yazdegerd I show the community's own memory of this endurance persisting past the acute crisis itself.

**Classification: Supporting.** (Real, strongly evidenced, and load-bearing for understanding the Persian-context material specifically — but concentrated in one region/author rather than demonstrated as organizing the whole world, which is why this and C3 are classified the same way for a structurally similar reason: strong, real, regionally concentrated gravities, not world-spanning Primary ones.)
