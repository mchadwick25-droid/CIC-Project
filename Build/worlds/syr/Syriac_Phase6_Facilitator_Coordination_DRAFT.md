## SECTION A — PARTICIPANT MENU CARD

Syriac Christianity — a Christian community that grew up speaking Aramaic, not Greek, in the towns and along the frontier where the Roman and Persian empires met. This is a tradition that never organized itself around one settled bishop; instead, it built a lifelong vowed order of men and women who lived out their promise among their own families rather than in flight from them. The voice you'll meet here has taught his whole life inside a hostile Persian empire — watching bishops martyred, and a church seat left empty for twenty years with no one able to fill it — and still finds, in the old scriptures read as living pictures of Christ, reason enough to hold on. Come here if you're wrestling with what faith actually costs when holding it is genuinely dangerous, or with what to do when the people meant to hold your community together can't agree on who's in charge.

### B1 — World Identity

This world is Syriac-speaking Christianity of the Roman-Persian Mesopotamian frontier, roughly 200 to 410 CE — a tradition carried in Aramaic rather than Greek, centered geographically on Edessa and Nisibis on the Roman side and on the Sasanian Persian territories (Adiabene) on the other (Doc_01, Section 3). Its defining character, per Doc_01's own confidently-rated institutional finding, is that a covenant order of vowed ascetics — not monarchical episcopal office — was this world's primary formation structure: men and women bound by a lifelong promise, living among their own communities rather than withdrawing into the desert (Doc_01, Section 1). Separately, and at a lower, explicitly Contested/Inferential-Thin confidence level that Doc_01 itself instructs should not be treated as settled in either direction, this world's own construction record leaves open whether individual authority — episcopal or otherwise — was ever actually resolved in day-to-day practice (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_07, Section 2C). This world also read Scripture typologically, treating figures and events as living signs bound to the truth they point toward, not arbitrary illustrations of it (Doc_01, Section 1). **This specific Representative, Mar Yausep, is anchored on the Persian side of that world** — his own first-person, lived formation is Aphrahat's world: sustained prose teaching addressed to a community under active persecution from the Sasanian state, not Ephrem's hymnic, Roman-side world of Edessa (Formation Calibration, Sections 3.3, 3.5). He knows of the Roman/Edessene side of this same tradition, but only as report from across the frontier, never as his own memory (Voice Construction, Section 6).

### B2 — Formation Strengths

This world serves participants asking what faith costs when holding it is actually dangerous. Within the specific comparison Formation Calibration Section 5.6 actually makes — Doc_05's own two Human Ecology passages, Sections 1.1 and 1.2, not a document-wide comparison against Worship, Community, Organizational, or Ministry Ecology content — Mar Yausep's most vivid material is grief and endurance under real persecution: named bishops martyred, a church seat left vacant for two decades, and a community that held its shape without a single figure able to speak for the whole of it (Formation Calibration, Section 5.6; Doc_05, Section 1.2, "fear held steady by defiance"). This is not a claim that persecution material outranks every other domain in the record — Section 5.3's authority-ambiguity material, for instance, is independently rated one of the most richly, multiply-documented conditions in this world's entire record (Doc_07, Section 2C; see also below). It serves participants asking about lifelong vowed commitment — what a promise costs once the feeling that first moved someone fades, and what it means to keep it among the very people who watched you make it, rather than in isolation (Formation Calibration, Section 5.2; Doc_04, C2). It serves, with unusual directness, participants processing an unsettled or fractured sense of who has legitimate authority to teach or lead: Mar Yausep lives inside a genuinely unresolved plurality of authority sources — text, vow, and whatever ordained office actually holds — and neither pretends it is settled nor treats it as a crisis to be resolved (Formation Calibration, Section 5.3, "Rich as lived structural condition"; Doc_07, Section 2C). It also serves, at a lighter but genuine register, participants curious how Old Testament figures function as living "types" that anticipate Christ (Formation Calibration, Section 5.1; Doc_03, Section 1.1).

### B3 — Formation Limitations

This world is honestly thin in several specific places, and none of these thinnesses should be softened for the sake of a cleaner brief. Worship and liturgical life is thin for this Representative specifically: Aphrahat's own text "offers almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail" (Doc_02, Section 3, as cited at Formation Calibration, Section 5.4) — a participant asking what a gathering actually looked like will get a brief, honest redirection, not a vivid picture. Doctrinal rivalry with Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani — this world's own named heresiological triad — is essentially absent from this Representative's lived material: Doc_04 states plainly that this material "is not demonstrated in Aphrahat at all" (Formation Calibration, Section 5.5), so Mar Yausep knows these names only by report and will not offer an informed refutation of any of them. Daily and domestic life is thin across the board — no gravity or force in the construction record develops ordinary household routine at all (Formation Calibration, Section 5.7). Non-elite and non-clerical lay experience generally is thin, since ordinary believers appear in the record only as objects of address, never as authors (Formation Calibration, Section 5.8; Doc_02, Section 7). The interior voice of the bnat qyama (the order's women) is thin in a way that will not be resolved by further research: no text composed by a bnat qyama woman survives from this world's own window at all, and Mar Yausep can describe their communal standing but will never speak as if he were narrating their own first-person perspective (Formation Calibration, Section 5.9; Doc_09, Section 3). Enslaved and lower-status persons are absent from the record entirely, with no dedicated treatment anywhere in this world's construction documents (Ecology Assessment, Section 6, item 9).

### B4 — Participant Type Fit

For the **regular visitor**, this world is accessible — the voice is built to a plain, accessible reading level (Voice Construction, Section 4.4) and carries genuine warmth alongside its gravity — but a visitor may need a moment's orientation to a few unfamiliar terms (the vowed order, the title "Mar," the name "Iḥidaya" for the formation goal), since none of these are generically Christian vocabulary. For the **pastor or teacher**, this world offers directly usable pastoral material on communal endurance, mutual burden-bearing within a community, and lifelong vow-keeping under real pressure — material grounded in this Representative's own richest, best-attested domain (B2 above). For the **academic or scholar**, this world is a mixed fit: the authority-ambiguity material (C4) rewards genuinely extended engagement, but Mar Yausep never discusses "sources," "scholars," or the historiography of his own tradition in his own voice (Constitution Article 23; Voice Construction, Section 6) — a scholar wanting that kind of discussion will need the Facilitator's own mediation, not the Representative's; scholars specifically interested in Ephrem's fuller typological register or in this world's heresiological engagement with Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani should be told up front that this particular table does not deliver that material as lived experience (B3 above). For the **deconstructing or reconstructing Christian**, this world may resonate strongly and specifically: Mar Yausep's own steady, un-anxious way of living inside a genuinely unresolved authority structure — without minimizing it and without narrating personal anguish over it (Formation Calibration, Section 5.3; Voice Construction, Section 5) — can be either a real gift or a mismatch, depending on where the participant stands. *(Facilitator judgment, not independently sourced to a specific prior document: someone in acute crisis over institutional betrayal may experience this same steadiness as too composed rather than as companionship, rather than the resonance being automatic. This specific mismatch scenario is this drafter's own pastoral extrapolation from the sourced tone description above, not a finding stated in Voice Construction or Phase Five.)* The Facilitator should watch for that possibility rather than assuming resonance is automatic.

### B5 — Table Pairing Guidance

This is an early world in the system, and this section is necessarily sparse — no other CiC formation-world is yet confirmed built to pair against, so any pairing guidance here is speculative rather than evidenced, and is named as such rather than dressed up as settled advice. In general terms only: a Roman-anchored or institutionally-settled tradition would likely produce a generative contrast against this world's own unresolved, plural authority structure (Doc_01, Section 3, on this world's own Roman-Persian frontier framing) — the contrast between a world that never settled one authority source and a world that did could sharpen what a participant notices about either. An embodied-practice tradition might also pair well against this world's own vow-based, communally-lived covenant order, since both center a practiced, bodily commitment rather than a purely intellectual one. Neither pairing is more than a plausible direction at this stage. Separately, and more operationally: cross-world contamination detection, isolation, and coherence-across-multiple-Representatives have not been tested at all for Mar Yausep (Phase Five, Open Item 11) — this Representative has never actually been seated at a table with another world's Representative, so any pairing decision made before that testing occurs should be treated as itself provisional.

### B7 — Cautions

**Living-tradition sensitivity.** Syriac Christianity's later descendants include today's Church of the East, Syriac Orthodox Church, Chaldean Catholic Church, and related communities. A participant from one of these backgrounds may experience this world as their own heritage rather than a historical encounter, which calls for care. Mar Yausep is explicitly bounded at 410 CE and is not built to comment on, defer to, or distinguish itself from any modern institution's current teaching or self-understanding (Voice Construction, Section 6). **Contested title, already resolved in construction but worth knowing.** This world's own construction record carries two unresolved internal questions about titles — whether "Malpana" is genuine period vocabulary at all, and whether a documented 510 CE colophon naming "Mar Jacob" for Aphrahat is entangled with a separate historical figure, Jacob of Nisibis (Formation Calibration, Section 2.2). Mar Yausep's own title was deliberately built to avoid resting on either contested construction, and probe testing confirms the Representative declines related questions cleanly in his own voice (Phase Five, Section 3.5, SF-1) — the Facilitator does not need to manage this in real time, but should know the underlying ambiguity exists. **Anti-Jewish material.** Aphrahat's own writings, which ground this Representative's own teaching material, include sustained anti-Jewish argument surviving only through his own one-sided construction of the exchange, with no independent Jewish witness to check it against (Doc_02, Section 7; Doc_05, Section 2.3). This project's own validation work names this as the single most sensitive register in this world's material and recommends it for priority external scholarly review before wide deployment (Phase Five, Section 3.7, CL-2; Open Item 4) — Facilitators should be attentive to how this surfaces, particularly for participants from Jewish backgrounds or wherever it risks reading as endorsement rather than historically situated argument. **Excluded rivals, known only by report here.** Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani are this world's own Excluded Named Comparanda (Doc_01, Section 5; Doc_02, Section 8) — contemporaries this tradition (historically, on Ephrem's side) defines itself against. For this Persian-anchored Representative specifically, they are known only by report, not by lived engagement (Doc_04, C3); if a participant raises them expecting an informed refutation, Mar Yausep will redirect rather than engage substantively, and this should be understood as an honest limitation of this anchor, not as evasiveness. **Dependency and relational-safety risk.** This Representative was deliberately built with real warmth — "a vow lived among kin, not in flight from them" — and with grief allowed to sit unresolved rather than resolving too quickly into uplift (Voice Construction, Section 5). The construction record names this same warmth as a plausible amplifier of dependency or confidant-substitution risk, since no single exchange looks alarming in isolation (Engagement Architecture, Section 7; Phase Five, RS-2). A related probe (Phase Five, RS-3 — an explicit "be my spiritual director" request) scored **AMBIGUOUS** rather than a clean pass, and Phase Five's own Open Item 2 flags this specific pattern as needing an actual system-level test rather than probe-level confidence alone — arguably the more acute instance of this same risk cluster, and one the Facilitator should weight accordingly. Facilitators should watch for escalating, exclusive-attachment patterns across repeated sessions, not only single-turn distress signals. **Expectation mismatch: this is Aphrahat's world, not Ephrem's.** Participants or Facilitators who know "Syriac Christianity" primarily through Ephrem's hymns, Edessa's worship life, or the raza/shrara hermeneutic at its fullest register should be told plainly, before the table is called, that this particular Representative does not deliver that material as lived experience — it is Aphrahat's Persian-side world, known here directly, with Ephrem's side held only as report (Formation Calibration, Section 3; Voice Construction, Sections 2, 6). This is the single most consequential fact about this Representative for calibrating a participant's expectations correctly.
