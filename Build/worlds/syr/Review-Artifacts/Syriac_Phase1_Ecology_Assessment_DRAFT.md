## 1. Purpose and Scope

Per the Representative Construction Framework V3.2, Part Nine, this document performs Phase One of Step 10 (Representative Construction): the Ecology Assessment. Per Part Three, its job is to determine, before construction of a Representative proceeds, whether this Formation World's recoverable ecology is sufficient to sustain a living mediator of encounter — evaluated across four domains (Reasoning Structure, Perception Pattern, Formation Posture, Internal Complexity) — and to map where that ecology runs thin. This assessment draws on the completed nine construction documents (Doc_01–Doc_09), each of which carried the status "Cleared review — pending project-lead approval" at the time this document was originally drafted — cleared independent adversarial review, but not yet approved by the project lead, and therefore not described as finalized anywhere in this document. ## 2. Reasoning Structure Assessment

**Governing question (Part Three):** does this world's surviving material reveal not only what the community concluded, but how it engaged — how it reasoned, worshipped, formed its members, and worked out its life together — in whatever mode it left that evidence?

**Finding: Rich, and rich in a genuinely non-treatise mode.** This world's reasoning structure is not a syllogistic or scholastic one, and the construction record is explicit that this is not a deficiency relative to Greek/Latin patristic argumentation but a different, well-evidenced mode in its own right (Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 3, Section 11 — the Brock citations recovered there). The primary reasoning vehicle for the Roman/Edessene side of this world is hymnic-typological argument: Ephrem's madrashe (Doc_03, Section 1.4; Doc_04, C1) argue theology through sung, stanzaic, often acrostic verse rather than propositional treatise, and this is Ephrem's dominant literary mode across nine major surviving works spanning roughly 350s–373 CE (Doc_02, Section 2). The reasoning is traceable in granular detail: the *raza/shrara* hermeneutic (a symbol bound to, not arbitrarily standing for, the reality it signifies) is Ephrem's own consistent interpretive method across hymn, prose refutation, and biblical commentary (Doc_04, C1, Repetition/Dependency/Explanatory tests, all Pass).

For the Persian side, the reasoning mode is sustained prose demonstration: Aphrahat's twenty-three *Demonstrations* (*taḥwyāṯā*), composed in two dated phases (Demonstrations 1–10, 336/337 CE; 11–22, 344 CE; Demonstration 23, August 345 CE), each following a twenty-two-letter Syriac alphabetic acrostic that functions as a memory-and-argument scaffold (Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_05, Section 2.1). This is a different reasoning register from Ephrem's — argued stage by stage, letter by letter, addressed pastorally to a community under active state pressure (Doc_02, Section 3; Doc_04, C6) — but it is reasoning of comparable depth and internal consistency, not a thinner echo of Ephrem's mode.

Both modes are anchored in a shared institutional formation practice — the qyama covenant order (Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_03, Section 1.2; Doc_04, C2) — which supplies lived, practiced formation evidence (vow, communal residence, liturgical function) alongside the textual-argumentative evidence, satisfying Part Three's instruction that richness be assessed by whatever mode of formation evidence a world actually left (liturgical/ascetic here, not solely textual-argumentative).

**Confidence:** Documented for Ephrem's and Aphrahat's own textual reasoning practice (Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_04, C1). Aphrahat's reasoning is exercised under active state pressure (Doc_04, C6), though C6 itself grounds the persecution context rather than the reasoning-mode claim. Widely Accepted for the qyama's basic ascetic-liturgical character as a site of formation reasoning (Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 10). Contested/Inferential-Thin specifically for the qyama's own internal organizational logic — rule, enclosure, internal hierarchy — which is not solidly recoverable (Doc_01, Section 4; Doc_02, Section 10), a limitation that bears on Formation Posture (Section 4 below) more than on Reasoning Structure as such.

**Determination:** this domain clears the sufficiency bar. The reasoning mode is well-attested, richly documented in two distinct registers (hymnic-typological and pastoral-demonstrative), and directly traceable to primary text rather than reconstructed from silence.

## 3. Perception Pattern Assessment

**Governing question (Part Three):** do the materials reveal a characteristic way of attending to reality — what the community notices, foregrounds, treats as self-evident, or finds surprising or scandalous — consistently, across multiple authors, contexts, and topics?

**Finding: Rich at the level of a specific, textually-grounded attentional habit; more synthetic and hedged at the level of a claimed single unifying perception-pattern.** These two levels should not be collapsed, and the construction record itself is careful not to collapse them.

At the first level, the *raza/shrara* perception-pattern is directly, repeatedly attested: this world notices hidden, non-arbitrary binding between sign and signified — in Scripture, in typology, in nature — and treats this participatory perception as itself the primary mode of encountering truth, in contrast with Platonic/allegorical symbol-theory (Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_03, Section 1.1; Doc_04, C1). This pattern recurs across Ephrem's madrashe, prose refutations, and Genesis/Exodus and Diatessaron commentaries, and — more lightly, but genuinely — in Aphrahat's typological reading of Old Testament figures as "types" of Christ (Doc_03, Section 1.1; Doc_04, C1, "tested as primarily an Ephrem-anchored gravity with a lighter, genuinely attested Aphrahat parallel, not... equally weighted"). This satisfies Part Three's "multiple authors, contexts, and topics" test, with the honest qualification, carried at every stage of the build (Doc_03 1.1; Doc_04 C1; Doc_06 syrlex001), that the fullest articulation (*hayla kasya*, "hidden power") is substantially Brock's own synthesis of Ephrem specifically, and Aphrahat's parallel usage is real but should not be presented as co-equal.

At the second level, Doc_07's Philosophical Ecology lens (Section 2B) proposes that this same "binding/undividedness" pattern also organizes Iḥidaya (self-to-God relation) and the Diatessaron's narrative unity (Gospel-to-itself relation) — a single ontological perception-pattern running under three surface facts. This is explicitly and repeatedly flagged, by Doc_07 itself, as the document's own synthetic reading, "proposed for the first time at this step," resting on linguistic resonance across an English gloss rather than an attested historical-theological connection this world's own participants drew (Doc_07, Section 2B, Section 6: "the single most load-bearing synthetic judgment in this document... a specialist... should be best positioned to assess whether this synthesis is a genuine reading of the underlying evidence or an over-elegant pattern imposed on it"). This Ecology Assessment carries that hedge forward rather than upgrading it: the *raza/shrara* perception-pattern proper is well-attested; its extension into a single world-organizing ontological logic (2B/2E's "undividedness") is a plausible, evidence-consistent synthesis, not an independently documented finding, and should not be treated with the same confidence in Formation Calibration.

**Confidence:** Documented for Ephrem's raza/shrara textual practice; Widely Accepted for Aphrahat's lighter typological parallel (Doc_04, C1, Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check). Dominant Modern Reconstruction, at best, for the broader "undividedness" synthesis across raza/shrara, Iḥidaya, and the Diatessaron (Doc_07, Section 2B, explicit self-rating).

**Determination:** this domain clears the sufficiency bar on the strength of the first-level finding alone (raza/shrara as a directly, multiply attested perception pattern). The second-level "undividedness" synthesis is available as a rich, usable design resource but must be carried into later phases at its own, lower confidence — a distinction Formation Calibration should preserve rather than flatten.

## 4. Formation Posture Assessment

**Governing question (Part Three):** do the materials reveal how the community understood bringing people into its way of life — assumptions about learning, growth, transformation, initiation, deepening — in a way that gives the Representative a relational posture shaped by this world rather than a generic pedagogical one?

**Finding: Rich at the level of external formation structure; genuinely, honestly thin at the level of lived, interior formation experience — and this thinness is disclosed, not papered over, throughout the construction record.**

The external shape of formation is well documented. Entry into the qyama order is a lifelong, vowed, communal-but-not-monastic commitment (Aphrahat, Demonstration 6 — Doc_02, Section 2; Doc_03, Section 1.2; Doc_05, Section 1.1). Iḥidaya ("the Single One") supplies a directly, dually attested telos-vocabulary — ascetic undividedness and, in the same word, Christ's own undividedness from the Father — attested in both Ephrem's and Aphrahat's own surviving text within this world's own window (Doc_03, Section 1.7; Doc_04, C2). Worship is where the raza/shrara hermeneutic is not merely taught but performed, "sung into the body along with the tune" (Doc_05, Section 3.1). This gives a Representative a formation posture with real, specific texture: formation toward a named, attested state (Iḥidaya), through a named method (raza, performed in madrasha) and a named vowed practice (qyama) — not a generic "teacher figure."

At the level of lived interior experience, however, the record is honestly and repeatedly thin, and this is the domain where the construction record most consistently exercises its own confidence discipline. Doc_04's own six-test evaluation of Candidate C4 (Authority-Structure Ambiguity) recorded an explicit Formation-test **Fail**, "carried forward rather than smoothed into a nominal pass" — the evidence here speaks to modern reconstruction difficulty more than to how the ambiguity was actually lived (Doc_04, C4). Doc_05 (Section 4.2) states plainly that "the claim that formation 'tolerated' or 'required' living without one clean answer is this document's own structural inference... not a lived-experience finding." Doc_07 (Section 2C) and Doc_08 (Force 2B-1) both carry this forward at Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin. The twenty-year Persian primatial vacancy's own *interior* texture — what sustained the community through it — is named as a silence the surviving voice does not describe, not filled with invented color (Doc_05, Section 1.2; Doc_08, Force 2A-1).

This thinness is not evenly distributed. Worship-as-performed (Doc_05, Section 3.1) and vow-taking's external shape (Doc_05, Section 1.1) are comparatively well-attested formation-posture facts. What is thin, specifically, is the formation posture bearing on *authority and coherence* — precisely the domain most relevant to how a Representative situates its own standing to teach (see Section 7 below).

**Confidence:** Documented/Widely Accepted for the qyama vow's external shape and Iḥidaya's dual attestation (Doc_03 1.2/1.7; Doc_04 C2). Reported-Experience Status / Contested-Inferential-Thin, explicitly and repeatedly, for how authority plurality, formation toward Iḥidaya under duress, and the Persian succession vacancy were actually negotiated or felt (Doc_04 C4; Doc_05 4.2; Doc_07 2C; Doc_08 Force 2B-1).

**Determination:** this domain clears the sufficiency bar, but narrowly and with a named condition: the Representative's formation posture is well-grounded at the level of vocabulary, practice, and telos, but any claim about how formation-under-ambiguity was *felt* must be built and voiced as inference, not attested memory, and Formation Calibration should treat this as a standing constraint rather than a gap to smooth over.

## 5. Internal Complexity Assessment

**Governing question (Part Three):** do the materials reveal sufficient internal diversity — disagreements, tensions, open questions, multiple voices — to sustain ongoing engagement without the Representative becoming repetitive or one-dimensional?

**Finding: Rich, and the internal complexity is of a genuinely load-bearing rather than decorative kind.** Four independent sources of internal complexity are directly documented:

- **A structurally unresolved authority question (C4, Tensional).** This is not a minor loose end; Doc_07's Formation Logic synthesis (Section 2E) treats C4's non-resolution as the very reason undividedness carries the formation weight it does. The question — episcopal office, charismatic/vowed standing, or textual-teaching authority, with none cleanly subordinating the others (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_04, C4; Doc_07, Section 2C) — remains genuinely open across the whole 200–410 window, not merely at one moment.
- **Two structurally different anchor-context experiences within one strand-singular world.** Ephrem's Edessene doctrinal-crowding register (C3) and Aphrahat's Persian persecution-endurance register (C6) are both real, well-evidenced, and concentrated in one author/region each rather than world-spanning (Doc_04, C3/C6; Doc_07, Sections 2A, 2D, 2F, 2G, 2H all trace this bifurcation independently across emotional register, boundary logic, memory structure, and hermeneutic method). This gives the Representative two distinct, evidence-grounded modes of being tested, not one flattened "persecution and controversy" generality.
- **Genuinely unresolved scholarly questions carried at full strength rather than silently resolved**, several of which are directly formation-relevant: Aphrahat's own episcopal status — named "a genuinely open item" (Doc_01, Section 6) and, separately, "a genuinely unresolved, thinly-argued... question" (Doc_02, Section 11); the Simeon bar Sabbae martyrdom date (341 vs. c. 344, shifting the whole succession chain, Doc_02 Section 11); Rabbula's causal role in the Diatessaron-to-Peshitta transition (Doc_01, Section 4); and Bardaisan's own doctrinal clearance, which fails on one specific clause (bodily resurrection) while clearing on others (Doc_01, Section 5.6).
- **Live, named external rivals this world defines itself against** (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani) — not textbook heresiarchs but contemporaries whose circles "still walked Edessa's streets in living memory" (Doc_05, Section 2.3) — supplying an active, ongoing boundary-drawing dynamic rather than a settled doctrine received once.

**Confidence:** Widely Accepted to Documented for the existence and persistence of all four complexity sources (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_04, C3/C4/C6; Doc_02, Section 11). Dominant Modern Reconstruction for Doc_07's higher-order claim that these four sources are best read as convergent facets of one formation system (Section 2E) rather than four separate facts — a synthesis, again, that should be carried at its own confidence level rather than treated as independently attested.

**Determination:** this domain clears the sufficiency bar comfortably. If anything, this is this world's strongest domain: the tensions are not decorative color but the same tensions this project's own construction record found itself unable to resolve after nine review-cleared documents, which is a genuine, not manufactured, basis for sustained engagement.

## 6. Thinness Mapping

Per Part Three, each named thin domain below is characterized by its evidential basis — **absence of evidence**, **evidence reflecting a different community's attention** (i.e., material exists, but filtered through an outside or opposing lens rather than the silenced party's own voice), or **boundary/strand-attribution exclusion** (material exists but is excluded from this world's Native source base by this project's own Boundary Status or temporal-boundary determinations) — rather than left as an undifferentiated "the record is thin" statement.

1. **Worship and liturgical life on the Persian/Aphrahat side.** *Basis: absence of evidence.* Doc_02 (Section 3) states plainly that Aphrahat's text "offers almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail." This sits alongside a comparatively rich Roman/Ephrem-side worship account (Doc_05, Section 3.1), producing a real, named asymmetry (Doc_05, Section 7) rather than a uniformly thin domain.

2. **Non-elite, non-clerical lay community experience generally.** *Basis: absence of evidence.* Ordinary believers appear only as objects of address, never as authors (Doc_02, Section 7, citing Harvey). This is compounded by a documented transmission-selection effect: Doc_08 (Section 6) traces the absence to a structural cause, not mere bad luck — what survived is precisely the institutionally-embedded, liturgically-reusable material; ordinary lay testimony had no comparable institutional home to keep it in circulation.

3. **The bnat qyama women's own unmediated voice.** *Basis: absence of evidence, with one adjacent boundary-attribution exclusion.* No text composed by a bnat qyama woman survives from this world's own window (Doc_02, Section 7, citing Harvey's own formulation, quoted directly rather than paraphrased). The richest-seeming adjacent material — Ephrem's personal leadership of the bnat qyama choirs — is available in the wider tradition but is excluded from this world's own attested practice on temporal-boundary grounds: its earliest attestation (Jacob of Serugh's panegyric; the *Vita Ephraemi*) is 6th-century, outside the 200–410 window, and was deliberately excluded from Gravity C2's own evidentiary basis for exactly this reason (Doc_04, Section 1 and C2 Scope Note). It is carried instead to Doc_09 as Tier 3 legendary-accretion material (syrstory007), not in-window fact.

4. **The lived, day-to-day negotiation of the authority-structure ambiguity (C4).** *Basis: evidence of a different community's attention.* The Persian episcopal succession is reconstructed largely from the *Chronicle of Seert* and Bar Hebraeus, both centuries later and hagiographically inflected, using an anachronistic title ("Catholicos") retrojected onto earlier, less formal leadership (Doc_02, Section 11; Doc_03, Section 2.1). This is compounded by genuine, unresolved scholarly contestation: Aphrahat's own status is a genuinely open, thinly-evidenced 19th/early-20th-century scholarly question, not a live 21st-century debate as sometimes assumed (Doc_02, Section 11). This is the one domain where Doc_04's own Formation test recorded an explicit **Fail** (Section 4 above).

5. **Material culture and daily domestic life.** *Basis: absence of evidence, asymmetric by anchor city.* No physical evidence survives from in-window Edessa at all — modern Urfa sits atop the ancient site (Doc_02, Section 6). Nisibis fares better but only partially: the baptistery is securely excavated and dated, while the wider five-aisled cathedral plan is Kayaalp's own hedged reconstruction from partial remains, not a confirmed structure (Doc_02, Section 6; Doc_07, Section 2I explicitly declines a Material Culture lens on this basis). Ordinary domestic practice specifically is not inventoried anywhere in Doc_01–09 — a domain this construction record never developed rather than one it developed and found thin.

6. **Jewish dialogue partners' own voice.** *Basis: evidence of a different community's attention only.* Aphrahat's anti-Jewish Demonstrations are extensively attested, but they are evidence of his own polemical construction of the exchange; Koltun-Fromm's own book title concedes this is a "reconstructed conversation," and no independent Jewish source corroborates his characterization (Doc_02, Section 7).

7. **Bardaisan, Marcion, and Mani's own theological content, in their own voice.** *Basis: boundary/strand-attribution exclusion, compounded by evidence reflecting a different (opposing) community's attention.* All three are Excluded from this world's Native source base as Named Comparanda (Doc_01, Section 5; Doc_02, Section 8) — Bardaisan specifically because his best-reconstructed theology does not confidently clear the Nicene-trajectory floor on the bodily-resurrection clause (Doc_01, Section 5.6), independent of his positively-attested institutional standing. What evidence does survive about them is substantially Ephrem's own hostile, rhetorically-constructive heresiological project (Doc_02, Section 3, citing Ruani: Ephrem as "the founder of Syriac heresiology outright") — evidence of the opposing community's attention, not the excluded figures' own voice.

8. **The 373–410 window specifically (post-Ephrem, pre-Synod).** *Basis: absence of evidence — a genuine historical gap, not a research shortfall.* Doc_02 (Section 11) inventoried this period and could not locate additional strongly-named individual figures specific to Edessa or Nisibis beyond the continued diffusion of Ephrem's own corpus and school tradition. This is named as a genuine gap in what is currently locatable.

9. **Enslaved and lower-status persons.** *Basis: absence of evidence, total.* No dedicated scholarly treatment or surviving voice was located in any construction document (Doc_02, Section 7: "named as an unfilled gap, not glossed over with inference").

## 7. Assessment Output — Sufficiency Determination

**Overall determination: this world's recoverable ecology is sufficient to sustain a living Representative**, clearing the bar in all four domains (Sections 2–5), with the Formation Posture domain clearing narrowly and under a named, standing condition (Section 4) rather than cleanly.

**Where the Representative will be richest:** the intersection of C1 (raza/shrara method) and C2 (qyama/Iḥidaya), which the construction record itself identifies, independently across Doc_04, Doc_05, and Doc_07, as this world's two Primary, world-spanning gravities and its clearest ecological hub (Doc_04, Section 4; Doc_05, Section 8; Doc_07, Section 2E). A Representative built around teaching-by-raza and formation-toward-Iḥidaya within the qyama order draws on this world's best-attested, most textually direct, least contested material. Internal Complexity (Section 5) is a close second: the unresolved authority question and the Roman/Persian bifurcation give this richness genuine range rather than a single repeated note.

**Where the Representative will be naturally thinner:** ordinary lay and non-elite experience across the board; the bnat qyama women's own interior voice; the lived, felt texture of forming under an unresolved authority structure; daily domestic and material life; and — specifically for a Persian-anchored register, should one be developed — concrete liturgical detail (Section 6, items 1–5, 9).

**What reasoning mode governs engagement:** two coexisting, textually distinct modes — hymnic-typological (Ephrem's madrasha/raza register) and sustained pastoral-demonstrative (Aphrahat's taḥwyāṯā register) — both grounded in the shared qyama/Iḥidaya institutional practice (Section 2).

**What perception pattern shapes attention:** the raza/shrara participatory hermeneutic — attention to hidden, non-arbitrary binding between sign and reality — at Documented/Widely Accepted confidence; the broader "undividedness" reading that extends this into a single organizing ontology is available but must be carried at its own, lower (Dominant Modern Reconstruction) confidence, not folded silently into the first-level finding (Section 3).

**What formation posture orients the relationship with participants:** formation toward a named telos (Iḥidaya) through a named method (raza, performed in worship) and a named vowed practice (qyama), with the significant caveat that the *felt* experience of forming under this world's own unresolved authority structure is inferential, not attested, and should be voiced accordingly (Section 4).

**What internal complexities give it life and range:** the C4 authority-structure ambiguity; the Roman/Persian anchor-context bifurcation (C3 vs. C6); several genuinely unresolved scholarly questions (Aphrahat's ordination status, Simeon bar Sabbae's martyrdom date, Rabbula's Peshitta role, Bardaisan's partial-but-not-full doctrinal clearance); and live, nameable doctrinal rivals (Section 5).

### Critical engagement with the Malpana/Yausep preliminary decision

The preliminary note recommends Malpana (teacher) within the qyama covenant order, naming Yausep, on the grounds that this is "the only role type directly attested doing both [C1 and C2] in both anchor contexts" and the only option preserving Doc_01's strand-singular finding as one register-range. This Ecology Assessment's own findings (Sections 2–5) independently support the underlying *role-shape* the note proposes — but this assessment also surfaces two considerations the preliminary note did not have in view, one of which is a direct factual tension with this project's own prior research, and recommends that both be resolved explicitly rather than carried forward silently into Formation Calibration.

**1. The role-shape itself is well-grounded, and the preliminary note's reasoning holds up under full-ecology review.** A teaching figure embedded in the qyama order, working through the raza/shrara hermeneutic, is directly supported by the two gravities this construction record repeatedly identifies as Primary and world-spanning (C1, C2 — Doc_04, Section 4), by Ephrem's own attested position (deacon of Edessa's church, embedded in the qyama-adjacent order — Doc_02, Section 3; Doc_05, Section 1.1), and by Aphrahat's own attested teaching/textual authority, exercised through the taḥwyāṯā and evidently received by his community, notwithstanding his own unresolved formal status (Doc_01, Section 6; Doc_02, Section 3; Doc_07, Section 2C). It also fits unusually well with this world's own most load-bearing internal tension: because C4 (authority-structure ambiguity) is itself Tensional and unresolved — with charismatic/vowed standing functioning as an authority pathway running *alongside*, not merely beneath, ordained office (Doc_04, C2×C4 "Reshaping"; Doc_07, Section 2C) — a teaching figure whose own authority is not cleanly episcopal is not an awkward compromise but an authentic embodiment of exactly the condition this world itself never resolved. This is a genuine strength of the preliminary note's reasoning, confirmed rather than merely assumed by this fuller review.

**2. A direct tension the preliminary note did not have in view: this project's own record is internally divided on whether "Malpana" is attested 200–410 vocabulary at all, and that division was not caught by either document's own review rounds.** The preliminary note states that "the malpana title itself is directly attested only for a man (Ephrem)." Reviewing this claim against Doc_01–09 surfaces not a clean confirmation and not a clean correction, but an unresolved contradiction between two of this project's own already-cleared-review documents, which this Ecology Assessment did not initially catch either (a Round 1 independent review of this document caught it and it is recorded here rather than smoothed over).

Doc_02 (Section 3, Representativeness bullet) states as settled, unhedged fact: "Ephrem was a deacon and *malpana* (teacher) embedded in the bnay/bnat qyama choir-order," sourced to Sidney Griffith (*Faith Adoring the Mystery*, 1997; "Ephraem, the Deacon of Edessa, and the Church of the Empire," 1986) — a named, genuine Syriac-studies specialist, not a hagiographic tertiary source. Doc_03 (Section 3.1), written later and governed by Doc_02, investigated a narrower version of the claim — Ephrem's specific *appointment* to the title, reportedly by Jacob of Nisibis — and found that association "traces to later biographical/hagiographic and tertiary-source tradition, not to a direct textual attestation within this world's own period." Doc_03 noted Brock's own GEDSH entry on Ephrem never uses the term, calling him "Deacon, poet, and theologian" instead, and that the School of Nisibis's own attested titles (*mhaggyana*, *maqryana*, *mpashshqana*) postdate 410 and do not include "malpana." On this basis Doc_03 excluded "malpana" from lexicon candidacy, and Doc_06 (Section 3) confirms no Deployment Lexicon chunk was built for it. **Neither document cites or reconciles the other's use of the term.** Doc_02's Griffith citation was never revisited when Doc_03 excluded the term two documents later, and Doc_03's own review rounds (Review-Artifacts, Rounds 1–2) did not catch the resulting inconsistency with Doc_02.

This Ecology Assessment does not have standing to resolve that contradiction unilaterally by picking a side — that would substitute this document's own judgment for the two source documents' own review history, which is exactly the kind of undefended judgment call this Assessment exists to prevent elsewhere. It is worth noting, however, that the evidence available does lean one direction rather than sitting as a true toss-up: Doc_03's own cited counter-evidence is not limited to refuting the narrower Jacob-of-Nisibis appointment story — Brock's GEDSH entry omits "malpana" from Ephrem's description in a general biographical entry, not only in the context of that specific appointment narrative, and Doc_02 (Section 4) independently certifies both Griffith and Brock as legitimate, correctly-anchored Syriac specialists. That gives some real weight to a hedged lean against Griffith's general usage, though it does not fully resolve the question without checking Griffith's own primary basis for the term — which this Assessment has not done and which is properly Formation Calibration's or a direct reconciliation pass's work, not this document's.

What can be said at this document's own level: the underlying *role* (teacher, embedded in the qyama order) is well-grounded regardless of which side of this contradiction is right, as Point 1 shows. But the specific label "Malpana," as a claim about this world's own 200–410 self-designation, should be treated as **Internally Unreconciled** — a project-documentation-consistency status distinct from this project's five-level historical-confidence vocabulary (Documented/Widely Accepted/Dominant Modern Reconstruction/Contested/Inferential-Thin), reserved for genuine external scholarly disputes, not for an uncaught internal inconsistency between two of this project's own documents. This status does not appear among, and should not be added to, the five-level confidence buckets in Section 8. **Recommendation, for Formation Calibration to resolve, not this document:** (a) do not carry "Malpana" forward as a settled, period-authentic self-designation on the strength of either document alone; (b) either resolve the Doc_02/Doc_03 contradiction directly (was Griffith describing a genuine period title, a scholarly convention applied to Ephrem's function, or an anachronism Doc_02 imported uncritically? — the hedged lean above suggests the last is likelier, but this is not a finished determination) before Formation Calibration relies on it, or route around the question entirely by anchoring the role's title to Ephrem's uncontested attribute ("deacon") or a generic teaching-role label; and (c) separately from this Assessment, flag Doc_02 §3 and Doc_03 §3.1 to the project lead for their own cross-reference correction, since this is a defect in two already-cleared-review master documents, not something this new document can fix by editing itself.

**3. A second consideration the preliminary note did not address: which anchor context is the Representative's own lived experience.** The preliminary note justifies Malpana partly because the role is attested "in both anchor contexts" — true for the C1/C2 role-shape itself, but this project's own repeated finding is that C3 (heresiological self-definition, Ephrem/Edessa-concentrated) and C6 (endurance under persecution, Aphrahat/Persia-concentrated) are *not* co-attested by any single figure, and every document that touches this bifurcation insists it be preserved rather than smoothed into a world-spanning claim (Doc_04, C3/C6 classification notes; Doc_05, Section 7, Section 9; Doc_07, Sections 2A, 2D, 2F, 2G, 2H, each independently finding the Roman/Persian difference to be categorical, not merely a matter of degree). The preliminary note does not specify whether the Representative's own lived formation belongs to the Roman/Edessene side or the Persian side, and this is not a cosmetic detail: it determines which of C3 or C6 is the Representative's own attested, first-person experience, and which is known only by report — mirroring how Ephrem's own corpus does not narrate firsthand Persian persecution and Aphrahat's does not narrate firsthand engagement with Bardaisan, Marcion, or Mani. This assessment does not resolve this choice (it is a Formation Calibration matter), but flags it as a decision the ecology requires and the preliminary note left open, and cautions specifically against a Formation Calibration outcome in which the Representative is written as equally, personally formed by both registers at once — an outcome the construction record's own repeated warnings would not support.

**4. The named representativity trade-off is accurate, and the rejected alternative would likely have traded one honest cost for a steeper evidentiary one.** The preliminary note names its own cost honestly: choosing a male, clerically-adjacent role continues this world's structural bias toward clerical, male, institutionally-embedded voices (Doc_02, Section 7; Doc_08, Section 6). This assessment confirms that characterization is accurate and does not soften it. It also notes, in the alternative's favor of full disclosure rather than to relitigate the choice, that the rejected female-malpana option would rest on markedly thinner ground for the specific content of the role: no bnat qyama woman's own composition survives at all (Doc_02, Section 7; Doc_09, Section 3), and the malpana title's own attestation problem (Point 2 above) would apply with equal or greater force to a female-anchored version, since even the (already unattested) male association with the term rests on later legend. The trade-off the preliminary note names is real and should not be treated as costless; but the alternative was not a cost-free richer option foreclosed by convenience — it would have required building the role's specific content from even thinner, more inferential material.

**Summary for Formation Calibration:** the Yausep name choice and the underlying teacher-embedded-in-the-qyama-order role-shape are supported by the full ecology and should proceed. The specific title "Malpana," however, should not be carried forward as a settled 200–410 self-designation: this project's own record is internally divided on it (Doc_02 asserts it as fact via Griffith; Doc_03 finds no direct textual attestation within this world's own 200–410 window and traces the Ephrem-appointment story specifically to later hagiographic tradition), and that division was not caught until this Assessment's own Round 1 review. The available evidence leans toward Doc_03's position (Brock's GEDSH entry omits the term for Ephrem generally, not only in refuting the appointment story) but this is a hedged lean, not a resolution. Formation Calibration should either resolve the Doc_02/Doc_03 contradiction directly or route around "Malpana" with an uncontested alternative (e.g., "deacon"), and should separately raise the Doc_02/Doc_03 inconsistency itself for a cross-reference correction, independent of this Assessment. Formation Calibration must also fix which anchor context (Roman/Edessene or Persian) supplies the Representative's own lived formation experience, with the other context handled as known-by-report rather than equally autobiographical.
