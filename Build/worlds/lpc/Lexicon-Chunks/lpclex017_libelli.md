---

## Quick Meaning

The certificates issued to those who complied with the Decian edict's sacrifice requirement — the administrative mechanism that created the category of the lapsed, including for people who never sacrificed but paid to be recorded as having done so.

---

## World Meaning

The empire asked for a sacrifice, and then it wrote down that you had made one.

That is the whole mechanism, and it is why the crisis took the shape it did. A demand you could satisfy with money rather than incense produced a class of people who had not, in their own minds, worshipped anything — and who nonetheless held a document saying they had. The paper is the problem. Without it, failure under pressure is a private matter between a person and God. With it, failure is filed.

---

## Ecological Function

This is the administrative fact underneath the lapsed, and therefore underneath penitential discipline and the whole confessor-authority tension. A participant who understands this term understands why the crisis was documentary rather than merely moral: the community was not guessing who had complied.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant may picture a formal identity document or licence.

**World Hearing:**
A compliance record produced by an empire-wide administrative demand, whose existence is precisely what made failure documentary and public rather than private.

---

## Key Sources

Cyprian uses the word himself. *De Lapsis* §27: *"libellis tamen conscientiam miscuerunt"* ("yet have defiled their conscience with certificates"); §28: *"nullo sacrificii aut libelli facinore constricti"* ("bound by no crime of sacrifice to idols or of certificate"). In the letter to Antonianus (ANF Epistle LI §13; Hartel letter 55 §13) he writes of *"those who receive certificates"* and argues they are not to be put on a par with those who sacrificed. Registry rows 1 and 2, with the Latin at rows 291 and 296. Doc_01 §2's own italicized use — *"administered through the* libelli *(sacrifice-certificate) system"* — is the gloss this entry follows.

**Evidentiary disclosure.** The English translation renders *libelli* as "certificates", so the Latin headword is absent from the vendored English volume apart from one occurrence in the Introductory Notice to the anonymous treatise against Novatian (Registry row 8). The Latin witnesses carry it. Latin *libellus* in Cyprian also names the martyrs' petitions (Hartel letter 15 §4; letter 27 §1), so the split between this entry and `lpclex019` is a way of keeping the English word apart. It is not a distinction the Latin makes.
