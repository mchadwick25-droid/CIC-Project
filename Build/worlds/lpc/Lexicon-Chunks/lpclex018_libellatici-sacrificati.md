---

## Quick Meaning

The two-way classification of the lapsed — those who bought exemption from sacrificing, and those who actually sacrificed — carrying different degrees of discipline, with all finally admitted to pardon.

---

## World Meaning

Not everyone who failed failed the same way, and the community knew it.

One person handed over money and walked away with a paper. Another stood at the altar and did the thing. Both are outside, and both are asking to come back, and it would be false to pretend the two acts weigh the same. So they are not treated as if they do — the road back is longer for one than the other.

What does not change is where the road ends. The distinction governs how long and how hard, never whether.

---

## Ecological Function

This is the graduation inside penitential discipline: the reason reconciliation is a process with stages rather than a single yes or no. A participant who understands it understands that this world's refusal to let one act decide a life was not a flattening of degrees but a judgment about the end point.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant may assume a formal canonical taxonomy fixed by the church itself and applied uniformly.

**World Hearing:**
A real practical distinction in how gravely a failure was reckoned, inside a process whose outcome was the same in the end: a road back that all of them could walk.

---

## Key Sources

**Cyprian names both classes in his own words.** *Epistle LI* (Registry row 1): *"Neither must you think, dearest brother, as some do, that those who receive certificates are to be put on a par with those who have sacrificed"*, and later *"it was decided, dearest brother, the case of each individual having been examined into, that the receivers of certificates should in the meantime be admitted, that those who had sacrificed should be assisted at death"*. In Hartel's Latin (Registry row 39; Ep. 55.13 and 55.17) the class names are *"libellaticos cum sacrificatis aequari"* and *"libellaticos interim admitti, sacrificatis in exitu subueniri"*; Ep. 55.14 and 55.26 use *libellatici* again. The Latin edition is a cross-check on the English reading, and Registry row 39 is held at Confidence C.

**The editor's endnote is a separate summary.** The vendored English prints the class names only in the American editor's endnote in the Introductory Notice to the anonymous treatise against Novatian (Registry row 8, Native, Confidence C): *"(1) Libellatici, those who had compounded with the heathen, and bought off from offering sacrifice; and (2) Sacrificati, those who had actually offered sacrifice to idols. Different degrees of discipline were awarded, but all were admitted to pardon finally."* Its closing clause is the editor's summary.
