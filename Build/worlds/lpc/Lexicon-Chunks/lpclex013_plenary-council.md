---

## Quick Meaning

For us, this is the counter-principle: local councils yield to wider ones, and even the widest are corrected by those that come after them as things previously hidden come to light.

---

## World Meaning

*The Councils themselves, which are held in the several districts and provinces, must yield, beyond all possibility of doubt, to the authority of plenary Councils which are formed for the whole Christian world; and... even of the plenary Councils, the earlier are often corrected by those which follow them, when, by some actual experiment, things are brought to light which were before concealed.*

Two moves in one sentence, and the second is the surprising one. Narrower yields to wider — that is a hierarchy. But the widest is itself revisable, because the church learns. Which means a past ruling is authoritative and correctable at the same time, and that is precisely what is needed by a man who intends to honour a predecessor while overturning what he decided.

---

## Ecological Function

This is the hinge of this world's interpretive ecology: it makes the tradition's own prior rulings into a body of arguable precedent rather than fixed deposit. It is deployed specifically against the earlier ruling on heresy and rebaptism, and it stands in direct contradiction to the paired *bishop of bishops* formula. A participant who understands it understands how this world can dispute its own authorities without repudiating them.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant is likely to hear a straightforward claim of centralized institutional authority — Rome or a general council laying down the law — and miss that the same sentence makes that authority revisable.

**World Hearing:**
A layered and self-correcting account: wider bodies outrank narrower ones, and the widest are corrected by later ones as understanding develops. Authority and revisability are asserted together, by a man using both halves at once.

## Key Sources

*On Baptism, Against the Donatists* II.3, Registry row 13 — directly quoted and re-verified at source; Doc_03's sweep returns 31 occurrences of *plenary* within the treatise itself, scoped to its own boundaries.

**Author Gravity note.** Augustine's formula is spoken in defence of overturning Cyprian's specific ruling. His institutional interest in the argument runs opposite to Cyprian's in the paired entry, which is why this build does not treat the two formulas as reconcilable restatements of one theory.

**Ecological bound**, as for the paired entry: the phrase *plenary Council* does not appear in Augustine's preaching to his congregation. He put the wider question of who judges a bishop to ordinary people in his Psalm against the Donatists (about 393, Registry row 309), but not as this formula.
