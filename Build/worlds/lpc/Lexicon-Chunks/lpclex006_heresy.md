## Quick Meaning

For us, the question named by this word is not which opinions are wrong but whether a baptism or an ordination given outside the one communion is anything at all — and this world answers it twice, oppositely, a century apart.

---

## World Meaning

Someone arrives who was washed elsewhere, by people who are not us. Is that person baptized?

One answer: no. What is given outside is not given, because there is nothing outside to give it — *he can no longer have God for his Father, who has not the Church for his mother* — and so bring them to the water. That answer is not a technicality; it follows from taking the one body seriously, and it is ruled on in council and defended against Rome at real cost.

The other answer, a century and a third later, from a man arguing against that ruling: what was given outside was truly given, and does them no good where they stand. *As the baptized person, if he depart from the unity of the Church, does not thereby lose the sacrament of baptism, so also he who is ordained, if he depart from the unity of the Church, does not lose the sacrament of conferring baptism. For neither sacrament may be wronged.* Bring them in, and what they already carry will begin to work.

The question does not go away between the two answers. It gets harder, because by the second time it is not one convert at the door but an entire rival hierarchy holding the same towns — and because that rival appeals to the first answer, in the first bishop's own name, against the second.

---

## Ecological Function

This is the question on which communion is tested hardest, and the one that makes the boundary of the church concrete rather than notional. It bears directly on reconciliation — whether a returning person is received or re-made — and on the one episcopate, since a rival consecration's validity decides whether the rival's bishops are bishops. A participant who understands this term understands why a disagreement about water became the defining structural question of North African Christianity.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant is likely to hear "heresy" as settled, malicious deviation from an agreed orthodoxy — a charge levelled by an institution confident of its own correctness, with inquisitorial overtones and a foregone conclusion.

**World Hearing:**
A genuinely open question, argued at book length by both sides, about whether a specific act performed outside one communion is valid. The two most respected figures in this world's own record answer it in opposite directions, and the later one is arguing against the earlier one's ruling while treating him as unquestionably inside the church.

## Key Sources

Cyprian: the Epistles' rebaptism correspondence (Registry row 1) and the 256 Council's ruling (row 4); *De Unitate* supplies the ecclesiology beneath it (row 3, Confidence B) — *"He can no longer have God for his Father, who has not the Church for his mother."* Augustine: *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* (row 13), where the contrary position is argued at length and the ordination passage quoted above is re-verified at source; Doc_03's sweep returns 687 occurrences of *baptism* within that treatise alone, markup stripped and scoped to its own boundaries.
