---

## Quick Meaning

For us, the lapsed are our own people who complied with the order to sacrifice — some by sacrificing, some by paying for a certificate saying they had — and who now want to come back.

---

## World Meaning

They are not strangers and they are not enemies. That is the whole difficulty. They sat where you sit. Some of them went up and burned the incense; some never went near the altar but bought a paper saying they had, and thought the difference would hold. In the reckoning afterwards the difference does matter, but it does not make either of them clean.

What a persecution of this kind does is not kill a church — it empties one at a table, one certificate at a time, and then leaves the survivors in a room with the people who did not hold. Two demands then arrive at once and both are serious. The ones who stood, and especially the ones who were interrogated and did not deny, say the church cannot simply take the others back as if nothing was weighed. And the fallen themselves, who have not gone anywhere, are asking to be let back in.

To say they are finished would be to say that a single hour under pressure decides a life. To let them straight back would say the hour was nothing, and the ones who held it would have held it for nothing. So neither is said. What is said instead is that there is a road, it is walked in the open, and it ends inside.

---

## Ecological Function

This term generates the whole penitential apparatus: reconciliation exists because the lapsed exist, and the confessors' rival claim to grant peace exists because there is peace to be granted. It is also the concrete instance of communion as a status — a thing lost and regained rather than possessed or not. *Libelli* names the mechanism that created the category, and *libellatici / sacrificati* the distinction drawn within it. A participant who understands this term understands why this world will not let a single test of purity decide who stays.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A modern participant is likely to hear a category of lapsed or lapsed-Catholic believers — people who drifted away, stopped attending, lost interest. Gentle, gradual, largely a matter of private conviction.

**World Hearing:**
Nothing gradual and nothing private. This is a documented act under state compulsion, on a specific occasion, with a certificate as evidence, and it carried a public consequence for standing in the community. The lapsed did not drift off; they stayed, and asked to be readmitted, and the church had to decide in public what to do with them.

## Key Sources

*De Lapsis* (Registry row 2, Confidence B) is the founding document of the controversy and is directly quoted throughout Doc_05 §1.1. The Epistles' lapsed-crisis correspondence (row 1, Confidence A) carries the working detail, including Epistles XX–XXI, in which two lay confessors discuss a specific reconciliation in their own first-person voices. Doc_03's sweep returns 96 occurrences of the exact phrase *the lapsed* in Cyprian's vendored corpus and 114 of the bare word.

**Author Gravity note:** the regulating, disciplining voice throughout is Cyprian's own — as presiding bishop, and as the party whose middle position ultimately prevailed. Doc_02 §2 records that his corpus is *"occasional rather than systematic."* **No lapsed believer's own account of undergoing the process survives anywhere in this world's corpus** (Doc_02 §6), and this entry does not supply one.

**Provenance of the two-way distinction, disclosed here because the World Meaning above turns on it.** The split between those who sacrificed and those who only bought the paper is Cyprian's own: *Epistle LI* (Registry row 1; Hartel's Latin, row 39, Ep. 55.13 and 55.17) names both classes and treats them differently, and `lpclex018` carries the quotations. The vendored English prints the Latin class names only in the American editor's **endnote** to a different, Confidence-C text, and that endnote's closing clause is the editor's summary. **What the World Meaning rests on beyond the split is the refusal to let either group's failure be final**, which is *De Lapsis*'s argument throughout.
