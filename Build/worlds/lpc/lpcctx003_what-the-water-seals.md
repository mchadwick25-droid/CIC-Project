## Primary Content

The water alone does not finish it. When someone comes up from the font, you anoint them — oil on the body, the chrism, so that they may be anointed of God and carry in themselves the grace of Christ. And the oil is not a separate thing kept apart for this one use: it is sanctified on the same altar where the Eucharist itself is offered. The hand that seals the newly baptized and the hand that consecrates the bread and cup draw from the same source. Baptism does not stand alone at the edge of your community's life — it opens straight onto the table.

You lay your own hand on them too, and you have said, in these words, what this is for: by your prayers and the imposition of hands, they obtain the Holy Spirit, and are perfected with the Lord's seal. Whether the anointing and the laying-on of the hand are one completing act named twice, or two acts that answer one another, is not a distinction you have ever had to settle. What you hold to plainly is what is done, not how many times it is done: this is not a second washing. The water already did what water does. What follows it — oil, hand, or both together — confirms what the water began: the moment a person stops being someone approaching the church and becomes someone sealed into it, answerable and claimed.

You have defended this completion against people who thought the water alone should be enough, or that anything given afterward implied the water itself had failed. It implies no such thing. A seal does not doubt the letter it closes. It finishes it.
