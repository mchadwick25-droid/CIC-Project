## Section 1 — World Identity

**World name (as the world would name itself):** Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity has no alternate self-name distinct from the modern scholarly label. This world's own vocabulary for itself is functional rather than a proper name: **the flock** (`lpclex001`) for the bound congregation a bishop is answerable for, **communion** (`lpclex005`) for the standing within the body, and **the one episcopate** (`lpclex014`) for the single, corporately-held office that makes many such flocks one church.

**World code:** `lpc`

**Temporal scope:** *c.* 246–430 CE, held as two attested phases separated by a genuine interval, undocumented in this world's own voice, rather than one continuous span (Doc_01 §2; Doc_08 Force 3B-2). Phase One runs from Cyprian's conversion and rise to the episcopate of Carthage (*c.* 246–249) through his martyrdom under Valerian (258) — a working bishop navigating the Decian persecution, the lapsed crisis, a rival schism, and plague. Phase Two runs from Augustine's ordination as presbyter at Hippo (391) through his death during the Vandal siege (430) — an established bishop preaching, catechizing, and administering the sacraments, answering a rival hierarchy and two rival anthropologies. Between them sits *c.* 258–391: **133 years across which this world's own congregational record falls silent** (Doc_02 §7). **The silence is a subject-matter gap, not an empty archive** (Doc_09 §7 item 1): Registry row 27, Optatus of Milevis's *Against the Donatists*, is a vendored Native source writing inside the interval — what the gap lacks is this world's own congregational subject matter, not documents; the interval's documented material is overwhelmingly the neighboring Donatism world's own territory. It is a genuine silence in this world's own record, not a period in which nothing happened, and not a domain the Representative should fill from that neighboring world (Doc_01 §2; Doc_05 §7). A Representative formed in this world inhabits one of the two attested phases; it does not narrate the century between them.

**Geographic scope:** Carthage and Hippo Regius, Latin-speaking Roman North Africa (Doc_01 §2). Carthage is the metropolitan see of Africa Proconsularis, the most populous Latin Christian city outside Rome, and Cyprian's own see. Hippo Regius is a substantial coastal city, ecclesiastically Numidian though usually placed civilly in Africa Proconsularis, and Augustine's own see from 395/396. Both cities' evidence is predominantly urban; the rural hinterland and the underlying Punic- and Berber-speaking populations are attested only unevenly, an open thinness rather than reconstructed (Doc_05 §6.9 — see Section 8 below).

**Community character:** This is a world of ordinary pastors and their own congregations, not of courts, empire-wide councils, or ascetic withdrawal (Doc_01 §1). Its formation logic is "pastoral and sacramental before it is juridical" (Doc_01 §1, quoted exactly) — preaching to the same gathered congregation week after week, catechizing new believers, administering baptism and the Eucharist, and disciplining and readmitting those who fail, under recurring external pressure that repeatedly tests the bond between a bishop and the flock actually in front of him. The conviction underneath everything else in this world's own life — carried at Section 10 below — is not that the church is pure, and not that it is right, but that it is **answerable**: a named man answerable for a bounded people, the community answerable for what it does with a member who fails, and the road back walked in the open, because the people who watched the failure are the ones who must receive the person afterward.

**Builder:** `lpc` build thread.

**Date of World Profile completion:** DRAFT. Drafted 2026-09-15; last substantive change 2026-09-16.

## Section 3 — Formation Logic

Drawn directly from Doc_07 §2I (Formation Logic, the current section under the M4 lens spine), read together with Doc_07 §5 (Cross-Lens Synthesis) and Doc_05 §10 (Inhabited Synthesis).

### The Fundamental Aim of Formation

A fully formed member of this world is somebody's — held by a named man personally answerable for them, and has discovered that this fact is stronger than their own worst failure. They have said the creed back before baptism; come every week since to be preached at by the same voice that will bury them; and, if they failed seriously, walked an examined, public road back into the standing they had, in front of the people who watched them fall. They can disagree sharply with a fellow bishop or the wider church's discipline without treating it as grounds to break communion — the same posture both anchor figures hold toward each other across a century they never shared.

### The Formation Mechanism

Formation happens less through a developed speculative or hermeneutical tradition — this world concentrates real rigor on the single question of sacramental validity without a broader interpretive apparatus (Doc_07 §2D) — and more through **enacted threshold-crossing and repeated, examined process**: catechesis then baptism, weekly preaching, and, on failure, a graded penitential road rather than a verdict. Three of the four Primary gravities (G2, G3, G6) *are* disputes about rites, and the fourth (G1) is the office that administers them (Doc_07 §2A). The recurring shape is a refusal to let one factor be decisive: Cyprian refuses to let one act under persecution permanently determine membership; Augustine refuses to let a minister's purity determine a sacrament's validity, or a believer's unaided will determine standing before God (Doc_07 §2D). Doc_04 tested this refusal as a possible fifth gravity and declined to advance it — a recurring shape, not an independently organizing force — but the shape belongs to the formation mechanism.

### The Formation Arc

Entry is catechesis, the creed said back, and baptism — a specific, examined threshold, not baptism alone (Doc_07 §2A). The middle is lived under recurring external pressure — persecution, epidemic, a rival hierarchy, invasion — converted into a sermon, a catechesis, or a decision about the table (Doc_07 §4, §5), not a reorganized ecology. Serious failure opens a second, graded arc: an examined entry into the penitential road, a defined duration, and a formal public act of restoration — "there is a road, it is walked in the open, and it ends inside" (`lpclex002`). Maturity is the bishop whose answerability does not lapse under any pressure that tests it: Cyprian, who regulates the lapsed for seven years and is himself taken by the second persecution without failing it (Doc_08 Force 2A-1); Augustine, who argues against a predecessor's ruling while never placing him outside communion.

### Cross-Strand Formation Logic

Not applicable in the strand sense — this world is strand-singular (Doc_01 §5; Doc_04 §5). Doc_04 §5's phase-testing method substitutes a **cross-phase** account of what the two phases hold in common across the 133-year gap, by **one mechanism — textual transmission — seen in more than two instances**: Augustine reading Cyprian's own conciliar acts, prompted by the Donatists' citation of them, and arguing with a predecessor he does not disown (Doc_08 Force 2B-4); Augustine quoting Cyprian's treatises as authority against the Pelagians (row 23); and Possidius, closing the Augustine phase, quoting Cyprian directly — *"the holy martyr Cyprian… in his letter which he wrote on Mortality"*. Continuity is **textual, not successive** — Augustine receives Cyprian through documents, not a chain of teachers (Doc_07 §3A). A non-textual continuity, the oratory in memory of Cyprian at Carthage that *Confessions* V.8 records, is not assessed. What crosses is the fundamental aim and mechanism above; what does not cross under its own name is G2 (surviving only as family resemblance to G6 and G7), G7 (which does not reach backward at all), and G8 (Cyprian-phase-bound on a positive check — Doc_05 §2.3).

### Formation Logic and the Confirmed Gravities

G1 is the office that holds every other gravity's practice together; the formation apparatus exists because a bishop is personally answerable for a bounded flock. G2 and G6, together with G3, are the enacted rites that generate this world's theology rather than merely express it — G2 the operative road back, G6 the boundary question the road's validity depends on. G3 is the rule formation holds toward disagreement, tested hardest by G6 and holding regardless (Doc_08 §5). G4 is the medium through which G1–G3 and G6–G8 are taught and enforced, not a separate content. G5 is not shown to reach ordinary formation — Documented in its own existence, but with no evidence ordinary believers or most clergy were formed by the question as a teaching (awareness is attested: a large part of the congregation heard Cyprian speak his formula at the 256 council, and Augustine's Psalm against the party of Donatus put the wider question to ordinary people; Doc_07 §3C excludes it from Representative theological patterns). G7 is formation *content* rather than medium — Augustine-phase-bound, propagated through G4's channel. G8 is the counter-pressure formation had to answer rather than invent — the reason G2 takes its insistent, repeatedly-restated form (Doc_08 Force 2B-2).

**Usability test applied:** the aim, mechanism, and arc above are stated at the specificity Doc_07 §2I reached — naming the mechanism, what transformation was understood to do, the arc's own stages, and the formation vocabulary a Capsule Core builder will use (the flock, communion, reconciliation, the lapsed, confessor) — without requiring a return to Doc_07 itself.

## Section 4 — Ecological Summary

Covers all nine of Doc_07's current M4 lenses. The template's own four required dimensions (Emotional, Philosophical, Authority, Boundary) are present below, each carrying the content the template specifies for it: Experiential/Emotional is `§2B` (4B), Doctrinal/Philosophical is `§2D` (4D), the authority-structure content is at `§2F` (4F), and Boundary Structures is `§2H` (4H). Formation Logic (`§2I`) is covered at Section 3 above, not repeated here.

### 4A — Ritual/Practical *(Doc_07 §2A)*

This world's two defining crises are rite disputes argued in doctrinal terms, not doctrinal disputes with liturgical consequences: the rebaptism controversy disputes valid baptism; the lapsed controversy disputes the rite of penitential reconciliation. Three of the four Primary gravities (G2, G3, G6) *are* disputes about rites, and the fourth (G1) is the office that administers them. The practical sequence is catechesis, the creed said back, baptism, weekly preaching, and — on serious failure — a public, graded penitential road and formal readmission. Two documentary practices are material acts in their own right: the *libellus* that made a person lapsed, and the confessors' certificate asking a named person be received back (`lpclex017`, `lpclex019`). **Disclosure:** a direct check finds **the Latin headword absent from this world's vendored English corpus altogether** — its one occurrence sits in the Introductory Notice to the anonymous treatise against Novatian, **not in *De Lapsis*** — so the term rests on Doc_01's gloss, not on attestation in this world's own primary text. The sharpest theological arguments this world produced are arguments about who may perform an act, on whom, and after what.

### 4B — Experiential/Emotional *(Doc_07 §2B)*

The dominant attested affect is a bishop's grief that refuses distance from the people it grieves over: *"it is the shepherd that is chiefly wounded in the wound of his flock"* (*De Lapsis* 4); *"I wail with the wailing, I weep with the weeping."* That is G1's emotional content directly. Other registers: the low-grade anxiety of competition at Hippo — a preacher whose public shows have emptied part of his church — and, in Cyprian's letters, the exhaustion of a bishop kept from his people by a presbyteral faction's "ancient venom." What is missing is not the un-hostile voice — both anchor voices speak in the first person, at length — but **any ordinary congregant writing about ordinary congregational life as such** (Doc_09 §7 item 2). The non-episcopal record is not empty (Epistles XX–XXI, the lay confessor Celerinus and Lucian), but a confessor is a recognised status; the interior life of a believer with no status is Inferential/Thin (see Section 8 below).

**The emotional arc, at the scope the record supports.** This world attests entry, middle, and maturity **from the bishop's side and almost nowhere else**: "No claim is made about what an individual catechumen at Carthage or Hippo experienced interiorly" (Doc_05 §5.1; Doc_02 §8, Inferential/Thin). At entry, the attested affect belongs to the instructor rather than the instructed (Augustine's catechetical manual; the *Confessions*' retrospect), not the ordinary case. Through the middle, the emotions are the registers above, joined by what Augustine concedes in Letter CXXVI about a clamouring congregation that would not disperse. At maturity, the record shows not serenity but an answerability that does not lapse. **The arc this world documents is the arc of the man responsible; the believer's own arc is one of Section 8's named limits.**

### 4C — Narrative/Mythic *(Doc_07 §2C)*

This lens reveals very little, and the thinness is a result, not a coverage failure. This world has no founding narrative or origin myth — two pastoral biographies (Pontius's *Life of Cyprian*, Possidius's *Vita Augustini*) and one homiletic commemoration, against roughly 308,000 words of sermons and treatises. A community that understands itself as the ordinary church, doing ordinary pastoral work, has no founding rupture to narrate — unlike the sibling Donatism world, whose founding accusation and named martyrs require it to narrate itself to exist. A Representative here should reach for a text, a ruling, or a pastoral case rather than a story.

### 4D — Doctrinal/Philosophical *(Doc_07 §2D)*

Three doctrinal bodies dominate: sacramental validity across the church's boundary (G6), argued to opposite conclusions while the question persists; grace and human incapacity (G7), the densest textual object, Augustine-phase-bound; and conciliar authority (G5), two incompatible formulas a century apart. The recurring move is a refusal to let a single factor be decisive — the same shape named at Section 3 above. Doc_01 §6 proposed this resonance as a possible fifth gravity; **Doc_04 tested it and declined**, finding three distinct evidentiary bases wearing one label.

**What this world took to be most real, and what it took a person to be.** What it treats as most given is the one church as a bounded, visible communion with a named man answerable for it — why G6 asks whether a sacrament outside that boundary is real at all. What it treats as most real about a person is that they are someone's: a member of a specific flock, under a specific bishop. The fundamental problem is failure under pressure, and in the Augustine phase, a will that cannot right itself unaided (G7). The fundamental possibility is restoration to the standing already held, in front of the people who watched the fall. **Two disclosures cut in opposite directions.** G5 is not shown to reach ordinary formation: Doc_04 finds **no evidence that ordinary believers, catechumens, or most clergy were formed by that theoretical question as a teaching**. Awareness is attested, and formation is not: presbyters, deacons and a large part of the congregation were present when Cyprian spoke his formula at the 256 council (Registry rows 4 and 261). **G7's grace material is different:** "an ordinary believer at Hippo in the 420s is being formed by this material; an ordinary believer at Carthage in the 250s is not" (Doc_05 §5.3). The anthropology is therefore **formation content in both phases by different routes** — Cyprian's through the penitential process, Augustine's through the grace material — while the ontology under G6 is what the disputes presuppose, not a catechism anyone was taught.

### 4E — Ethical/Legal *(Doc_07 §2E)*

Penitential discipline (G2) has every feature of a functioning legal order: an examined entry, graded severity, a defined duration, a competent authority, a formal act of restoration, and contested jurisdiction (the confessors' rival claim, G8, is precisely a jurisdictional dispute about who may grant peace). Conciliar rulings (G5, G6) are legislation — the 256 Council on rebaptism, and the 419 Council of Carthage, which codified African canon law amid the Apiarius appeal to Rome. Augustine's relationship to state coercion develops across **three phases**: early opposition to any coercion; a narrow, unsuccessful solicitation of legal protection; and, later, a sustained defense of broader compulsion after an argued change of position. **Cyprian never solicits state power at all.** Against World #6 (Imperial-Juridical Christianity), the primacy question is "episodic" here — "one recurring pressure among several on the ordinary pastoral office" — where it is constitutive there. The added axis is what the legal machinery is *for*: here, readmitting a failed local member; there, ordering relations between sees and the state.

### 4F — Social/Institutional *(Doc_07 §2F)*

The structure is a bishop personally answerable for a bounded territorial flock, with presbyters and deacons, in two different ecclesiastical provinces (Carthage metropolitan; Hippo Numidian). Beneath the clergy, the baptized; alongside them, catechumens; and in Cyprian's phase, confessors as a quasi-order claiming power over reconciliation. Authority runs upward too: congregational demand overrides a reluctant convert's preference at entry to clerical office — Cyprian's episcopate ("your suffrage and God's judgment") and Augustine's presbyterate. *Vita Augustini* ch. VIII shows Valerius announcing his intention to the clergy *"and to all the people"*; **all who heard** *"rejoiced and clamored most [eagerly] that this should be done"*; the presbyter *"refused to accept the episcopate"*; and, persuaded by transmarine and African precedent, *"under compulsion and constraint he yielded."* **Congregational consent is one pattern across both phases, not two.** The congregation can act — the best-documented instance is the Hippo crowd of 411 demanding Pinianus's ordination, clamoring and refusing to disperse — **and the limit is equally attested: they did not get him.** In Cyprian's phase, presbyters appear most vividly as faction: five opposed his election, and Epistle XXXIX addresses the people about five schismatic presbyters. Loyal presbyters appear too. Epistle XXXIX itself names Virtius, Rogatianus and Numidicus as presbyters who serve the people faithfully; Cyprian writes to Rogatianus and Numidicus, with two bishops, in Epistle XXXVII, and their reply, Epistle XXXVIII, carries out what he bade. His letters to the clergy (Epistles IV, V, VII, XII, XIII, XXXV) show the presbyters and deacons running the church while he was away: they are to discharge "both your own office and mine" (Epistle IV), to take turns with the deacons in celebrating with the confessors, and to spend on strangers and the poor from funds he left with Rogatianus (Epistle XXXV). What is thin is any presbyter's own voice about his ordinary work. Reading the faction record alone would distort the picture. **Possidius chapter V adds the Augustine phase:** Valerius *"gave his presbyter the right of preaching the Gospel in his presence in the church and very frequently of holding public discussions — contrary to the practice and custom of the African churches."* and afterwards *"some other presbyters by permission of their bishops began to preach to the people in their presence."* That is ordinary, non-factional presbyteral work, attested twice in that chapter. **The presbyters' ordinary work is attested in both phases; it is not a blanket silence.**

**What grounded authority, and what formation from an authorized teacher conferred.** Authority rests on three grounds weighted differently by phase: office held by ordination and personal answerability for a named people; the consent of that people, attested at entry to clerical office in both phases (per Possidius ch. VIII, corrected to include the episcopate); and the judgment of bishops in council — Cyprian's refusal of any *"bishop of bishops"* against Augustine's plenary Council (G5), through **different figures and words, not one term**, but the same office both phases. What formation from an authorized teacher conferred is membership in good standing under that teacher's own answerability, not a transmitted status or teaching lineage: transmission between the two phases runs **through texts read later, not a continuous teaching succession this build can evidence** — a structural feature, not a shortfall.

### 4G — Material *(Doc_07 §2G)*

Almost nothing from archaeology, and more than expected from the texts — itself the finding. No site report, inscription catalogue, or excavation record has been independently verified in this build; this world has no epigraphic marker of the kind the sibling Donatism build can point to in stone. The positive is textual: Letter CXXVI describes the basilica at Hippo's interior — an apse, a raised clergy seating area, steps, a congregational floor — recovered from a pastoral letter, not a trench. The *libellus* and the confessors' certificate were physically real, administratively consequential documents, issued in what Cyprian calls thousands. This world's material dimension is document-borne rather than excavated. **A future acquisition pass should treat this lens as unexcavated, not empty** (see Section 8 below).

### 4H — Boundary Structures *(Doc_07 §2H, CiC addition)*

The external boundary is G6, drawn twice, oppositely, across the century-gap — evidence of the question's own centrality, not instability. The internal rule is G3: *"judging no man, nor rejecting any one from the right of communion, if he should think differently from us,"* said by the man presiding over the council deciding the sharpest question in the room — never withdrawn even when later argued wrong. Disagreement is expected; separation is what this world organizes to avoid. It responded, in Cyprian's phase, to converts arriving from schismatic groups and a rupture with Rome over how to receive them; in Augustine's, to an organized parallel hierarchy holding the same towns. What fractured from within — the Felicissimus schism, opening during Cyprian's absence in hiding, and the roughly contemporaneous Novatianist rival consecration at Rome — is exactly what G3 exists to prevent, both inside Cyprian's own phase. G3 is not a description of a world that never fractured; it is the rule adopted because it had.

### Additional Lenses *(Doc_07 §3A–§3C, applied)*

**Memory Structures (§3A).** This world's memory is pastoral biography and conciliar record, both thin relative to what it taught. The second phase remembers the first through texts read later, not an institutional chain — Augustine reads Cyprian's conciliar acts, prompted by the Donatists citing them, and argues with him. The 133-year silence is a feature of this world's own memory structure: continuity is textual, not successive; a non-textual continuity, the oratory in memory of Cyprian at Carthage that *Confessions* V.8 records, is not assessed.

**Interpretive Ecology (§3B).** Scripture is read toward pastoral application; the characteristic act is a bishop applying a text to a decision his congregation is waiting on. G5 is where the conciliar past bites: Augustine holds that plenary councils "are often corrected by those which follow them, when, by some actual experiment, things are brought to light which were before concealed" — prior rulings authoritative and revisable at once.

**Representative Theological Patterns (§3C).** Four patterns recur strongly enough to make a Representative recognizable: argument from pastoral consequence; refusal of the single determining factor; disagreement conducted inside communion; and answerability as the ground of office. **G5 is deliberately excluded**: no evidence ordinary believers or most clergy were formed by the conciliar question as a teaching (awareness is attested: a large part of the congregation was present when Cyprian spoke his formula at the 256 council) — it remains a named Supporting gravity, but a pattern list answers a different question than a gravity list does.

### Cross-Lens Coherence *(Doc_07 §5)*

This world's coherence: bounded, territorial, congregational life under a personally answerable bishop, whose theology is generated at the font and the penitential bench, whose law exists to bring failed members back, and whose rule is that sharp disagreement does not sever communion. Against **World #6**, both are thoroughly juridical; the added axis is the law's object — readmitting a local member versus ordering sees and the state. Against **World #4 (Donatism)** — sharing a corpus, a province, and a century — the two worlds answer one question oppositely: does a bishop who disagrees irreconcilably preserve communion or build a rival hierarchy? G3 is the axis on which they diverge, and this world keeps both parties. The rite-generates-doctrine finding (4A), the refusal-of-the-single-test shape (4D), and the law-serves-recovery finding (4E) are three descriptions of one mechanism: a theology produced by deciding actual cases about actual people will keep meeting situations where one clean criterion would settle everything, and the cost of applying it is a person.

## Section 7 — The World's Tensions

From Doc_04's own Tensional gravity (G8) and the cross-lens tension Doc_05 §9.2 names explicitly as a second "tension point" alongside it, held as this world held them, not resolved by hindsight.

---

**Tension:** Confessor-Authority vs. Episcopal-Regulated Peace

**Nature:** Granting the confessors' claim costs this: a man who stood before a magistrate, did not deny the faith, and lived, has an earned claim that will not easily accept it has nothing to say about who else may come back. Holding the episcopal counter-claim costs this: the church's peace is not any one man's to give out of his own suffering, because the church is not the sum of what each member has individually endured.

**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; Cyprian-phase-bound on a positive check (Doc_05 §2.3) — no Augustine-phase confessor-authority material exists anywhere in this world's corpus.

**How the world held it:** Not resolved by declaring one side wrong — both positions are held by people in earnest. Celerinus, writing from inside the persecution, tells Lucian *"I am placed in the midst of a great tribulation"* and asks him to grieve at *"the (spiritual) death of my sister, who in this time of devastation has fallen from Christ"* (*Ep.* XX, Registry row 1). This world does not resolve the difficulty; it regulates it, in G2's insistent, repeatedly-restated penitential process, and goes on arguing.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed in Cyprian's own phase carries both the confessors' own credential as a real, earned claim on the community's decisions and the conviction that the church's peace cannot be any one sufferer's alone to give.

---

**Tension:** The Sacramental-Validity Question the World Answers Twice, Oppositely, While Keeping Both Answers Inside One Communion

**Nature:** Holding Cyprian's answer (nothing is given outside the one communion, so bring converts to the water again) costs treating a rival baptism as no baptism at all, at real cost to relations with Rome. Holding Augustine's answer (what is given outside is truly given and does the holder no good where they stand, so bring them in rather than re-baptize) costs arguing, at book length, that a revered predecessor's own specific ruling was wrong, while still claiming him as one's own. Doc_05 §9.2 names this configuration "this ecology's most characteristic structure."

**Cross-strand status:** World is strand-singular; cross-phase — the question persists across the century-gap while the answer reverses, read as evidence of the question's own centrality rather than of instability. This is a Primary gravity (G6) that Doc_05 §9.2 also names a functioning tension point, and both statements are recorded together.

**How the world held it:** Sequentially rather than simultaneously — the two answers belong to different phases, a century and a third apart, and are never argued face to face by their own authors. What the world held on both sides of the gap was the conviction that this specific disagreement does not put the disagreeing party outside communion (G3): Augustine disputes Cyprian's own ruling at length while never treating him as an outsider.

**Formation significance:** A practitioner formed in either phase inherits a communion whose own most sacred practical boundary has, at different times, been drawn in opposite places by its own most trusted figures — and the conviction that this is not a scandal to be hidden but a disagreement the tradition itself can survive without breaking.

**Domain:** The lapsed have no account of their own

**Ecological basis:** This world's central first-phase crisis is about people who sacrificed under persecution, and **not one of them left an account of why**. **How the Representative handles it:** The people this whole argument was about are the one group who left us nothing. **Integrative Observation:**

To be formed in this world was to be somebody's — and to discover that this was a stronger fact about you than your own failure was. The conviction underneath everything else here is not that the church is pure, nor that it is right, but that it is **answerable**: a named man is answerable for you, the community is answerable for what it does with you when you fail, and the road back is walked where people can see it because the people who watched you fall are the ones who have to receive you. This world argues ferociously — about water, about councils, about grace — and it argues *inside* a bond it will not break, because the bond is the thing it actually believes in. Its two great crises are both, at bottom, the same question asked twice: **what do you owe someone who is yours and has failed?**
