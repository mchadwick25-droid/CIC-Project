## 2. The governing constraint

The Naming and Role Discipline sets the bar, in the project lead's words: a real name that would not be mistaken for a significant figure in this world or a close world, one a modern reader can remember, and a role that positions the voice to represent the entire movement. The name and an image are the only fabrications. No backstory, family or personal history is built.

The Decision Log entry states the same limit for Datus: the role is "a register-shaping label", not a position that limits what the voice can know.

## 3. Name

**Decided: Datus.**

**Grounding as recorded.** *Datus* occurs zero times across the 38 vendored corpus files, checked with editorial notes stripped. It is a real African cognomen of the period: *Dativus*, its extended form, is one of the Numidian confessor-bishops Cyprian addresses in the mines (*Ep.* LXXVI–LXXVII). The entry notes two syllables and no stress ambiguity.

**Names the record says were ruled out, and why.**

| Name | Reason recorded |
|---|---|
| *Restitutus* | The Catholic presbyter murdered by Circumcellions in Augustine's diocese. The entry calls it the worst possible choice beside the Donatism world. |
| *Sedatus, Quietus, Castus, Dativus, Polianus, Litteus, Tenax, Verianus, Libosus* | All appear in the 256 Council of Carthage sententiae, which Augustine reproduces verbatim in the anti-Donatist works. Encumbered in both of this world's phases and in the adjacent world. |
| *Privatus* | A condemned heretic (*Ep.* LIX). |
| *Tertullus* | A specific figure who keeps the martyrs' death-dates. |
| *Celerinus, Numidicus, Crementius, Saturus* | Already used in this build's own story chunks. |
| *Fortunatus, Felix* | Crowd Fidelis's F-register. |
| *Fructuosus* | Tarragona, 259; well known for exactly this period. |

## 4. Role and role label

**Decided.** Role: bishop within an urban African congregation. Role label: **Bishop of the Kept Flock.**

**Role grounding as recorded.**
- G1 is this world's Primary gravity (Doc_04, *Pastoral Office as Territorial Flock-Keeping*). Doc_05 §4 describes the authority as "pastoral in mode and territorial in scope… a bounded local community he is personally answerable for and to — not jurisdiction over other sees."
- The role is urban, not rural. Doc_05 §6.9 says the verified evidence is urban (Carthage, Hippo) and that any claim about rural or Punic-speaking congregational life "would rest on nothing this build has verified."
- RCF V3.1's breadth criterion prefers a crossroads position. The entry says an urban bishop holds one, and that a presbyter or deacon holds it less.

**Alternatives the record names.**

| Alternative | Reason recorded |
|---|---|
| A bishop of a small country see | An earlier draft of the brief placed him there. Corrected, because Doc_05 §6.9 gives no verified evidence for rural life. |
| Deacon | "Additionally unavailable": it is Marius's role, and this world's own deacon voice (Pontius) is a biographer. |
| Presbyter | Named only as holding less of a crossroads position. No further reason recorded. |

**Role label grounding as recorded.** Doc_03 counts 113 combined occurrences of *flock*, *shepherd* and *pastor* in Cyprian's corpus alone (57 *flock*, 39 *shepherd*, 17 *pastor*). The entry calls the label this world's own dominant vocabulary, not a coinage. It follows the portfolio pattern `[office] of the [defining community]`, as with Albina, Yausep, Marius, Chilo and Fidelis. It contrasts with Fidelis, "Bishop of the Unbroken Line": that label claims a succession, this one describes custody.

**Naming Discipline check on the label.** The label names no see and no date. The Phase Two document (`lpc_Rep_Phase2_Formation_Calibration.md`) sets the temporal horizon at c. 246–430. The label itself is unlocated, in line with the practice at Fidelis, Renatus and Theophilus.

**Not recorded:** any label alternatives (no other wording is named); the project lead's own words on the role or label; who corrected the rural draft to urban. What would settle it: the 15 September 2026 session record.

## 5. Object

The object was part of the 15 September 2026 package and was changed later, by a separate decision.

**15 September 2026.** The decision table named a certificate of peace (*libellus pacis*) with names written on it, grounded in *Ep.* XV. The entry records these as rejected as weaker: a purse (the ransom of `lpcstory004`, described as reading "generic almsgiving") and the commemoration record of death-dates (*Ep.* XXXVI, which is Tertullus's object).

**Constraint found the same day.** The entry's item 5 records that `donatism/Fidelis_Portrait_Prompt.md` bars any flat written-text object, because it would collide at icon scale with Theon's scroll and Albina's tablet. It lists three options (keep the certificate; substitute the purse; no object) and closes "Not decided here."

**24 September 2026.** The project lead chose the ransom purse, grounded in *Ep.* LIX (one hundred thousand sesterces for captives' ransom; G3 and G1). The reasons recorded: it removes the Theon and Albina collision, and unlike dropping the object it keeps the held-object versus empty-hands contrast with Fidelis. The certificate was marked superseded, not rejected on source grounds. The record also corrects the earlier "generic almsgiving" dismissal of the purse as inaccurate to `lpcstory004`.

## 6. Image

**Decided 15 September 2026:** painterly oil bust portrait, wide landscape, warm cream ground, under the locked standard of 24 July 2026. Held object at chest height in both hands. Solid and opaque, no glow or halo. No jewellery. No legible writing.

**Dress and appearance are INFERENCE, not DOCUMENTED.** The entry says so in its item 6. They are: hooded coarse wool cloak (*birrus*) over an ochre-brown tunic; no insignia; mid-to-late forties, dark hair and beard with first grey. The age was corrected from an earlier sixty-year-old draft, on the project lead's observation that the portfolio already reads old, and on Doc_05 §1's description of middle-aged bishops. Complexion is silent in the record and was deliberately not differentiated from Fidelis.

**Differentiation.** From Fidelis by age, hood, palette and the held object. From Chilo by beard, garment colour, belt and object; the entry records this as "judged different enough by the project lead" and as a known overlap, not a closed question. From Theon by object.

**Participant-facing text.** The alt text and caption are in the Decision Log entry. The alt text was updated after 24 September 2026 to describe the purse. The caption reads: "Datus, Bishop of the Kept Flock. A representative voice for the town churches of Roman Africa — who learned, under persecution and plague, that a church which takes everyone back the same afternoon has no door, and one that takes no one back has no Master."
