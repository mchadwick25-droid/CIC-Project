## Section 1 — World Identification

**World name:** Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity. **World code:** `lpc`.

## Section 2 — The Four Tiers, Re-Confirmed at Source

**One transcription convention, disclosed once.** Where a quotation begins mid-sentence in the source, this document and its chunks capitalise the first letter without an opening ellipsis — *"Was found half dead…"* for the source's *"…was found half dead."* **Four** such sites exist across **four** chunks. **No wording is altered and no meaning changes**, but a reader running a literal string search against the vendored text will miss them, and this repository corrects quotations at finer granularity than this elsewhere.

**Quoted from Construction Framework V7.4's own Part II text, opened for this document rather than carried from a sibling build.** The skill governing this step warns in terms that *"a skill that hardcodes last year's categories is exactly the kind of drift this project's own history has already been burned by,"* so the definitions below are the Framework's own words, and the confidence bands are its own too.

- **Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative.** *"Direct textual attestation within or close to the world's horizon. Named author with identifiable social location. Datable with reasonable confidence. The story claims to describe specific events or persons and the claim is historically credible with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective and genre."* Confidence: **Documented to Widely Accepted at the narrative level** — and CF continues: *"May carry Widely Accepted or Contested confidence for specific details within the narrative depending on the author's access and perspective."* Use: *"may be offered as historical narrative with the author and perspective named."*
- **Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material.** *"Stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world's own community memory… The collection itself is evidence of what the community remembered and valued."* Confidence: **Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction.** — and CF continues: *"The tradition is authentic; specific details and attributions carry Contested confidence."*
- **Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition.** *"Material attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition rather than direct documentation… Hagiographic narrative is a specific type within this tier: stories shaped by recognizable hagiographic conventions — the idealized portrait of a saint's life, the miracle sequence, the death as completion of a formed life."* Confidence: **Contested for the general portrait *or attribution*; Inferential/Thin for specific details shaped by hagiographic convention** *or for events whose historical occurrence cannot be verified* — and CF continues: *"The formation ideal communicated is credible evidence; the specific events claimed are not."* Use: *"The formation ideal is the evidence, not the biographical detail."*
- **Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** *"A composite reconstruction of what regularly happened within the ecology — not a story about a specific named individual… Every element must be traceable to attested evidence within the world's horizon."* Confidence: **Inferential/Thin regardless of how well-sourced the individual elements are.**
- **No Tier 5.** *"Generated or illustrative narrative — stories invented to illuminate formation truths but not traceable to the world's own evidence — is not permitted within this framework. If the evidence does not support a story, the story does not exist for this world. Silence in thin areas is the right response."*

**The distinction this world's evidence forces, and it is the central tier judgement in this document.** Pontius the Deacon was **an eyewitness with an identifiable social location** — Cyprian's own deacon, writing shortly after the martyrdom — which is Tier 1's shape. He was also writing **the first Christian biography, in hagiographic form**, which is Tier 3's. **Doc_02 §4 flags the genre risk by name**, and `Source_Registry.md` rows 99, 159 and 212 license Delehaye's genre scholarship precisely for it. **This build's resolution is to assign the tier per story rather than per source, and to mark inside a story any detail that only convention-shaped text supports.** Where Pontius reports something corroborated outside his own frame, or carries none of CF's three named markers, the story is Tier 1. Where he alone gives a detail shaped by convention, the story marks the detail as his. `lpcstory006` shows the method: it is told at Tier 1 on what Pontius and the *Acta Proconsularia* both attest, with the details only Pontius gives marked as his and the one contradiction stated. **A source is not a tier. This reverses Doc_02 §9 item 10**, which describes Pontius's *Life* *"in terms matching the Framework's own Tier 3 definition."* That reading holds for the convention-shaped details, which `lpcstory006` marks as Pontius's alone. It is rejected for the stories Pontius reports corroborated outside his own frame (`lpcstory001`) or whose idealising portrait attaches to the man and not to the events (`lpcstory002`). **The reversal is named here rather than left for a reader to notice**, and Doc_02's item stands as written: it described the source, and this document tiers the stories.

**That rule is this build's construction, not the Construction Framework's, and it is disclosed rather than asserted.** CF's own Tier 1 definition requires historical credibility *"with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective **and genre**"* — genre is written into Tier 1 — and its confidence rule allows a Tier 1 narrative to carry stepped confidence *"for specific details… depending on the author's access and perspective."* **So CF supplies a route, and `lpcstory002` takes it.** What CF does not state is the harder case: **where the genre shaping reaches the events themselves rather than the author's estimate of his subject**, as in Pontius's account of the execution. `lpcstory006` takes the route of telling what a second text also attests and marking the rest as Pontius's. **That narrower question was escalated, and CF answered it; the escalation is withdrawn, and the Disposition records why.** CF classifies *"where on that spectrum **each story** sits"* and calls itself a *"four-tier **story** classification framework"* — so assigning the tier per story is what CF prescribes, not a departure from it. **The rule adopted here would still govern how every world in the portfolio tiers an eyewitness hagiographer**, and Pontius is not a rare case — the genre is the normal form for a bishop's death in this period, so the pattern is recorded rather than escalated. **Applied here because a tier had to be assigned, and disclosed as a local construction rather than a citation.**

### 3.1 By-Tier View

- **Tier 1 — seven stories:** `lpcstory001`–`007`.
- **Tier 2 — zero stories. Not an oversight, and the reason is this world's own transmission shape.** Tier 2 requires *"stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history."* This world produced no apophthegmata-style collection, no sayings anthology, no *Vitae Patrum*. What it produced instead was **a correspondence** — and a correspondence is not a collection in the Framework's sense. **What Doc_08 §2B-5 does establish, from Cyprian's own words, is that he forwarded a thirteen-letter dossier of his own correspondence** (*"these thirteen letters sent forth at various times… which I have transmitted to you"*). **Whether the corpus as a whole was assembled in his lifetime or after it is not something this build has established.** The Tier 2 finding does not depend on the answer: what Tier 2 requires is a *community's* remembered collection with its own transmission history, and this world has no such collection either way. **The absence is a fact about what kind of world this was**: it transmitted itself by letter and treatise, not by remembered story.
- **Tier 3 — zero stories.** The hagiographic details in Pontius's account of Cyprian's death are marked as his inside a Tier 1 story at `lpcstory006`, and no story rests on convention-shaped detail alone.
- **Tier 4 — zero stories built here.** CF V7.4 states that Tier 4 narration *"is appropriate in world documents (particularly ecological reconstruction sections) **when it is explicitly marked as reconstruction in the construction notes**,"* and that *"the construction notes must identify the specific attested sources each element draws from."* **This world's Tier 4 material already exists, marked as reconstruction, in `Doc_05_Ecological_Reconstruction.md`**; duplicating it as story chunks would create a second, un-derived copy of material that is already disclosed where the Framework places it. Recorded as a placement decision, not an absence.

## Section 6 — Candidates Considered and Not Built

1. **The Passion of Perpetua and Felicitas (Carthage, 203).** Vendored at row 204, and the most famous Christian story North Africa produced. **Not built, because it is outside this world's boundary. But the interesting question is not whether Perpetua belongs to this world; it is that this world kept telling her story.** Augustine preached on Perpetua, and on the Scillitan martyrs (`Source_Registry.md` rows 122 and 171 license the scholarship on exactly this). **A story about this world's own use of an inherited martyr-story would be in-boundary where the martyr-story itself is not** — and it would bear directly on G4. **It is not built here because the sermon texts have not been read for this document.** They are vendored in Latin: Sermons 280, 281 and 282 on Perpetua and Felicitas are in the Latin of PL 38 (`augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38.txt`, row 216, from *Sermo* CCLXXX at line 89935), and the Scillitan martyrs' day is in Morin's 1917 volume (`augustine_sermones-guelferbytani-lat_morin1917.txt`, row 267, "De natale sanctorum Scillitanorum", lines 1361 and 8466). These sermons are inside the world, which includes every sermon of Augustine in a public-domain edition held in the Library (Doc_01 line 30), so building the story is a reading task under the sermon scope, subject to the OCR limits Doc_02 states.
2. **The *Acta Proconsularia Sancti Cypriani*** (rows 41, 194) — **read at source and used.** It is an early Christian compilation built around the hearing records, its compiler unnamed, in one edition (Hartel's) printed three times (Hartel, Gebhardt no. XI, Knopf no. 13), and vendored in Latin. `Source_Registry.md` row 41 calls it *"the one strictly documentary (as opposed to hagiographic) record"*, a phrase that row's own provenance column marks as builder prior knowledge and that the vendored text does not support as worded: the text carries Christian framing, such as *"beatum Cyprianum"* and a closing doxology. `lpcstory006` is told at Tier 1 on what Pontius and the *Acta* both attest (§3, §8 item 1).
3. **Augustine's *Confessions*** (row 9, Native, vendored) — **in `Source_Registry.md` row 9's own words, *"the founding first-person document of this world's own Augustine half"*, and no story is built from it.** Not built this pass because the work is a conversion narrative of the years *before* the episcopate, and this inventory's Phase Two stories are congregational and pastoral; a story drawn from it would be about a man becoming a Christian, not about a bishop keeping a flock. **That is a scope judgement, not an absence** — and it is a thin ground for leaving out the one extended first-person text this world has. Carried at §8 as the strongest Phase Two candidate for the next pass.
4. **The healing miracles at Hippo** (*City of God* XXII.8) — Augustine as bishop, recording cures in his own congregation and having the accounts read publicly. Congregational, pastoral, and in-boundary. **Not built this pass** because the chapter has not been read whole, and a miracle sequence read in part is exactly the material most likely to be mis-tiered. The sermon scope bears on this candidate too, since Augustine preached on the same miracles.
5. **The 411 Conference of Carthage.** The *Gesta* has not been read in full. Doc_04 §7 item 6 is closed as a persistence question, and the *Gesta* is kept for narrow, independently checkable facts only (Doc_08 §9). No story is built from it.

## Section 7 — Absent Stories

**The question CF V7.4 requires: what stories can this world not tell, and why?**

**Absent Story Note rollout, stated once here rather than repeated in every chunk.** The L4 template provides the section *"where a story that might be expected cannot be told because evidence is insufficient."* At first, no chunk carried one; **six of the seven now do. `lpcstory001` does not**, because its own absence — that the neophyte detail rests on Pontius alone — is carried in its Tier Justification rather than as a story that cannot be told. Article 20's affirmative duty governs this section — the absences below are named because naming them is owed, not because the list is short.

**1. There is no story from inside the 133-year silence that this build has assessed.** That claim is Contested: nine tractatus in Morin 1917 are not yet assessed (OG-54; Doc_01 §5). This is the absence that distinguishes this world from every other in the portfolio. Between Cyprian's martyrdom in 258 and Augustine's ordination in 391, **this world tells itself no stories at all** — not because nothing happened, and not because the century is undocumented. **Row 27's Optatus of Milevis writes inside the gap and is Native to *this* world** — Doc_02 §1 records him as `tradition` here and `context` on the Donatism side, *"native to one, etic evidence for the other."* **What the gap lacks is not documents but a voice continuous with Cyprian's ordinary-pastoral mode.** Doc_02 §7 states it on Doc_01's narrower test: some Registry rows date from the gap or record its events (Optatus, rows 27, 64 and 300; the council canons; the *Codex Theodosianus*), and none of them is that voice. Optatus writes polemic against an opponent, and not a bishop's ordinary address to his own congregation. **The silence, as far as this build has assessed it, is a silence in the surviving record for this world specifically, and not an empty archive.** Doc_08 §3B-2 records that what crosses the gap is a text, and the crossing is attested more than once: Augustine reading Cyprian's conciliar acts and arguing with them, Possidius quoting Cyprian's *De Mortalitate* to judge how a bishop should face his own death, and Augustine's anti-Pelagian works citing Cyprian on grace. **A world whose middle third is narratively silent, as far as this build has assessed, cannot produce a continuous story of itself**, and any Representative built on this material must be able to say so rather than bridging it.

**2. No story survives in the voice of an ordinary believer.** Every story above is told by a bishop, a bishop's deacon, or a confessor — men with a claim to authority in the community and a reason to write. Doc_02 §6 and Doc_07 §2B both find that what this world's record lacks is not a hostile mediator but **any ordinary congregant writing about ordinary congregational life as such.** `lpcstory005` comes closest and does not reach it: Celerinus and Lucian are confessors, a recognised status. **We have no story of a Sunday from someone who merely attended one.**

**3. The lapsed have no story of their own.** This world's central first-phase crisis is about people who sacrificed under persecution — and **not one of them left an account of why.** They appear as a category in Cyprian's letters, as petitioners at the confessors' doors, as a problem to be adjudicated. **People whose standing after the persecution was disputed are named** — *Ep.* XX names **Numeria and Candida**, and records what was argued about them — **but not one of them speaks.** They are discussed, vouched for and dispatched to peace by two confessors writing to each other. Celerinus's own sister is not even named: she is described entirely by her brother, in the third person, as a grief he is enduring. The world's most consequential formation question is documented exclusively from the side of those who did not fail it.

**4. Women appear in these stories and never narrate them.** Numidicus's wife burns; his daughter searches for his body and finds him alive. Numeria and Candida are discussed, weighed, and dispatched to peace by two men writing to each other. **All four are visible; none is audible — and all four are Phase One.** The second phase does not break the pattern, and the two strongest candidates for breaking it make the point sharper: **Doc_02 §6 records that Albina and the community of nuns at Hippo are known only through Augustine's own framing of them**, in letters he wrote to them and about them rather than words they wrote themselves. Doc_02 §6 records that named women do appear in this corpus — the absence named here is narrower and sharper: **no text a woman wrote herself survives in this world's corpus. That is not an artefact of Cyprian's corpus. It holds across both phases and 180 years.** One martyr text does report a woman's own spoken account of a vision, in her own first person. In the *Passion of Montanus and Lucius* 8, Quartillosia, held in prison with the martyrs after her husband and son had suffered, sets out what she saw: *"Vidi, inquit, filium meum"* (`cic/texts/knopf_ausgewaehlte-maertyrerakten-lat-grc-deu_krueger1929.txt` lines 5771–5783; Registry rows 269 and 270, Native; no English version is vendored). That is a single narrated voice inside a text men wrote, not a body of women's writing, and no story here is built from it. **The one North African woman who did write a narrative of her own — Perpetua — sits outside the horizon by four decades**, which is a boundary fact with a real cost, and §6 item 1 is where it is felt.

**5. There is no story of an ordinary, uneventful pastorate.** Every story here is a crisis: an election under pressure, a plague, a torture, a ransom, a schism, an execution, a deathbed. **The daily work that Doc_04 makes this world's Primary gravity — a bishop keeping a flock, week after week, when nothing is happening — is the one thing no story here tells**, and the reason is **this build's, not the record's. Possidius, *Vita Augustini* XIX–XXVII, is nine chapters of exactly that** — seven of the nine printed headings are *"Augustine as judge," "How he interceded for prisoners," "His frame of mind when attending councils," "Augustine's use of food and clothing," "His use of the church revenues," "Household affairs," "Household discipline"* — the list is a sample, not the full range. It is **row 192, Native and vendored**, in the same file `lpcstory007` is built from, immediately before that chunk's declared XXVIII–XXXI span. **Doc_05's own item 10 (tracked at `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` OG-8) records the *Vita* as *"vendored and unread beyond one identification"* — unread, not unwritten**, which is the distinction §6 item 1 states as a rule. **Read at source** — `Review-Artifacts/Possidius_XIX-XXVII_Read_2026-09-15.md`. **The absence is answered, and the tier expectation inverts.** This document routed the ordinary pastorate to **Tier 4**, on the reasoning that it would have to be reconstructed. **Possidius XIX–XXVII is not Tier 4 material**: it is a named individual observed by a named eyewitness with an identifiable social location, carrying none of CF's three hagiographic markers in these chapters. **It is Tier 1 material.** The daily work this world makes its Primary gravity was never a gap requiring reconstruction — it was attested, in a Native vendored source, and unread. **A Tier 1 story of an ordinary pastorate is now buildable**; the strongest candidates are XXIV's account of the key, the ring and the annual audit, and XXIII's offer to hand the church's possessions back to the people. **Not built in this pass.** This is the gap Tier 4 reconstruction exists for, and it is why this world's Tier 4 material sits in Doc_05 rather than here.
