## 0. Method and the Governing Disciplines

**Purpose.** Step 4 asked what organizes this world. Step 5 asks what it was like to be formed inside it. Ecological Reconstruction renders the world as a living system of formation through a set of lenses — different angles on one ecology, not separate worlds. Five disciplines govern every lens below (§0.2–§0.6); §0.1 states the gravity spine they operate on, and §0.7 is a coverage index rather than a discipline.

**0.1 The gravity spine (from `Doc_04_Gravity_Discovery.md` §4).** Four **Primary** gravities organize this ecology: **G1 Pastoral Office as Territorial Flock-Keeping**; **G2 Penitential Discipline — the Reintegration of the Failed** (Cyprian-phase-grounded; its own Persistence test finds it does *not* continue into Augustine's phase under its own name); **G3 Collegial Communion Preserved Despite Disagreement**; and **G6 Sacramental and Ordination Validity Across the Boundary of the Church**. Three **Supporting** gravities operate within them: **G4 Preaching and Catechesis as Primary Formation Mode** (the medium the others are taught through); **G5 Conciliar Authority Theory — Egalitarian vs. Hierarchical**; and **G7 Grace and Human Incapacity** (Augustine-phase-bound). One **Tensional** gravity is held rather than resolved: **G8 Confessor-Authority vs. Episcopal-Regulated Peace** (Cyprian-phase-bound). Two candidates were considered and not advanced — the illegal-to-established shift in state coercive capacity, folded into G3's and G6's own forces notation, and Doc_01 §6's "purity/sufficiency-test refusal" taken as one unified cross-phase candidate, which Doc_04 §2 finds is not one candidate at all but three distinct evidentiary bases wearing a single label. Neither is smuggled back in below as an unnamed organizing force.

**0.2 G5 is carried exactly as Doc_04 §7 item 2 directs, and that direction is quoted rather than paraphrased.** Doc_04 tells this document what to do with its most contested gravity: *"treat it as a Supporting gravity; do not suppress it, since both formulas' existence is Documented and the disagreement is real; treat the Cross-Check divergence at Open Item 1 as a statement about evidential confidence, which CF V7.4 distinguishes from gravity strength, not as a finding that the candidate fails to organize; and note that Open Items 6 and 8 may both bear on the classification."* All four clauses are honored. G5 appears in the Organizational lens (§4) as a real, named structure of this world's authority ecology; its evidential thinness is stated there as a confidence statement and not converted into an ecological verdict; and §11 carries Open Items 6 and 8 forward unresolved. **This document does not re-litigate the classification and does not treat the project lead's ruling as something it may revisit.**

**0.3 The two-phase constraint and the 133-year silence (Doc_01 §2, §5; Doc_02 §7) — active in every lens, not a preface.** This world is two bishops' phases with a documentary silence between them: Cyprian at Carthage (episcopate 248/249–258) and Augustine at Hippo (presbyter 391, bishop 395/396, dead 430). **The interval of roughly 133 years is counted from Cyprian's martyrdom to Augustine's ordination, as Doc_01 fixes it. The texts written at and just after Cyprian's martyrdom stand at the very start of that interval: the *Acta Cypriani* (rows 41, 194, 231 no. 13 and 232 no. XI), Pontius's *Life* (rows 7, 40, 194 and 205), and the two martyr acts of rows 231 and 232 (nos. 15 and 16, or XIII and XIV). They belong to his phase and do not fill the silence. Their full sequence is not fixed: Pontius already cites the Acts of the proconsul's hearing (*Life* 11), Harnack (row 205) argues that the *Acta* in their compiled form came after the *Life* and calls the two martyr acts contemporary with it, and no source gives the whole order. From them to Augustine's ordination, no source in `Source_Registry.md` supplies a bishop's ordinary pastoral or congregational voice, or a congregation's voice, that continues Cyprian's. The Registry texts dated inside the interval include Optatus of Milevis (rows 27, 64 and 264) and the appendix documents (row 265); none continues Cyprian's voice, and this world draws on none of them for a claim. The licensed exceptions (Carthage council canons in rows 26, 59 and 202; Augustine's writings from before his ordination in rows 11, 22 and 25; Theodosian Code constitutions in rows 44 and 88) are named at Doc_02 §7.** Doc_02 §7 binds this document specifically: the gap is an honest silence *in this world's own record*, not a general absence of evidence about the period — the interval is richly attested, but overwhelmingly through sources that are Donatism's territory, World #4's, not this world's. Two consequences run through every section. First, a claim attested in one phase is **not** extended to the other without saying so; where a lens can only be written for one phase, it says which. Second, per Doc_01 §5's own instruction carried into Doc_02 §7, the silence is **never** made an occasion for meta-commentary about what did or did not survive to be documented — so no inhabited passage below remarks on its own evidence.

It is narrower and different in kind — **two named, elite, male, clerical voices carry nearly the entire surviving record across a 184-year span.** Doc_02 §6 also establishes that the negative is not blanket: a deacon's extended first-person account survives (Pontius, Registry row 7); two lay confessors write to each other in their own voices (Epistles XX–XXI, row 1, neither yet ordained); and named women carry real narrative weight inside row 11 (Letter CXXVI to Albina; Letter CCXI to the Nuns of Hippo). ## 1. Human Ecology

**Who inhabited this world.** Two bishops whose surviving words carry almost the whole record — a rhetorician converted in middle life and made bishop of Carthage within two or three years, over five presbyters' recorded opposition; and, a century and a third later, a bishop of a substantial Numidian port city who had been seized by that city's congregation and ordained presbyter against his wishes. Beneath and around them: presbyters, some of them factional and named as such; deacons, one of whom wrote the first surviving Latin account of a bishop's life; catechumens under instruction toward baptism; consecrated virgins addressed as a class; whole households; and — most numerous, least individually visible — the ordinary baptized, who appear in this record overwhelmingly as the body a bishop addresses, grieves over, disciplines, and is elected by, rather than as people who left words of their own.

**1.1 The two phases hold different populations under different pressures, and are not merged.** Cyprian's Carthage is a large, organized, illegal congregation inside a city under the first systematically enforced empire-wide persecution, administered through the *libelli* system, and then inside a severe pandemic. Augustine's Hippo is a legally established congregation inside a Christianized empire whose military integrity is visibly failing, contending for its own members against an organized rival communion that claims to be the only true church.

*[Inhabited — the bishop's own account of what a persecution does to a congregation]* We were not decimated by an enemy at the gate; we were emptied at a table, one certificate at a time. Some went up and sacrificed. Some never went up but paid to have it written that they had. Both are ours, and both are outside, and the grief of it is not theirs alone: *it is the shepherd that is chiefly wounded in the wound of his flock.* I grieve with you; I do not stand at a distance from this and call it your failure. I wail with the wailing, I weep with the weeping, and I count myself thrown down with those who are thrown down.

[Construction note: **Documented.** Cyprian, *De Lapsis*, Registry row 2 — re-verified at source in `cic/texts/anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml`: *"I grieve, brethren, I grieve with you; nor does my own integrity and my personal soundness beguile me to the soothing of my griefs, since it is the shepherd that is chiefly wounded in the wound of his flock… I wail with the wailing, I weep with the weeping, I regard myself as prostrated with those that are prostrate."* The *libellatici*/*sacrificati* distinction rendered here as *"some went up… some never went up but paid"* is **the vendored edition's own 19th-century editorial note**, attached to Registry row 8 (the anonymous treatise against Novatian) and not to *De Lapsis* — per Doc_03's own disclosure, and marked here as editorial, not as Cyprian's own two-way classification. Row 2 sits at Registry Confidence **B**.]

**Forces (Cyprian's phase).** The Decian edict is not background to this human ecology; it is what produced its central category. There were no *lapsed* before there was a certificate to obtain, and no *confessors* with a claim on anything before there was an interrogation to survive. The persecution created, in a single year, two new classes of person inside one congregation — those who had failed the test and those who had passed it — and the whole of G2 and the whole of G8 are the community's response to having both in the room at once. The Carthage plague (c. 249–262) then pressed on the same congregation from a direction no discipline could sort, and Cyprian addressed it directly in *De Mortalitate*.

*[Inhabited — the bishop's own account of a congregation that has come to hear him]* You are here, and the shows are on today, which means the ones who are not here chose otherwise, and I am asking you to be anxious for them with me. But you came. So I am not speaking to hearts that are deaf, and to minds that will disdain the word; this waiting of yours is itself a prayer for me.

[Construction note: **Documented.** Augustine, Sermon I.1, Registry row 19 — re-verified at source in `cic/texts/npnf106_augustine-sermon-mount-harmony-gospels-homilies.xml`: *"I am not speaking to hearts that are deaf, and to minds that will disdain the word, but this your longing expectation is a prayer for me. There is a further consideration; for the day of the public shows has dispersed many from hence, for whose salvation I exhort you to share my great anxiety."* The rival draw of the games is the sermon's own stated occasion, not supplied here. Row 19 sits at Confidence **B** for its specific loci, Documented for existence and basic content per Doc_02 §8's bracket.]

**Forces (Augustine's phase).** The pressures on this human ecology are of a different order and are named as different: an organized rival communion with its own bishops in the same towns; Manichaeism, a system Augustine had belonged to for roughly nine years; Pelagian anthropology, which occupied a thirteen-work corpus; and, at the very end, a Vandal army outside the walls. The public shows in the passage above are a fifth, smaller, and entirely ordinary pressure, and it belongs in this lens precisely because it is ordinary: the competition for an ordinary believer's Sunday is a real force in this ecology and is attested in the preacher's own opening sentence.

**1.2 The stratum question, answered in this world's own shape rather than borrowed.** Alexandria's Doc_05 works with a literate/non-literate divide; Donatism's works with a transmission asymmetry running through a hostile victorious party. **Neither describes this world.** Here both anchor voices are the tradition's own, speaking in the first person, and the record's asymmetry is one of *social position and office*, not of hostility or of literacy strata: bishops wrote, bishops were copied, and the tradition that copied them was the one that won every dispute they engaged. Doc_02 §6 puts the survivorship mechanism plainly — each was, by the time of writing or of later transmission, *"on the institutionally dominant and eventually canonized side of every dispute he engaged."* The effect is that this world's evidence is unusually *full* and unusually *single-angled* at the same time, which is a different problem from thin evidence and is treated as such throughout.

**1.3 Named absences, not filled.** No ordinary lay believer's own account of ordinary congregational life survives anywhere in this corpus. Cyprian's interior motivations beyond Pontius's hagiographically-shaped account are Inferential/Thin and are not narrated. How deeply the Punic- and Berber-speaking rural substrate shaped ordinary congregational life is, per Doc_01 §2 and Doc_02 §5–§6, a genuine open question this document does not answer and does not gesture at answering; it is carried at §6.9 and §11. The material conditions of worship at Carthage and Hippo specifically — basilica form, inscription, furniture — rest on no site report independently verified in this build (Doc_02 §5, §9 item 2), and §3 is written under that limit explicitly.

**1.4 Article 20's secondary prong, exercised once — the Hippo congregation of 411.**

The Framework admits bounded reconstruction of a marginalized voice on one of three conditions: comparative evidence from similar communities, material evidence, or **the world's own sources read against the grain**. The first two are unavailable here — Doc_02 §9 item 2 records that no site report, inscription catalog, or excavation record has been independently verified in this build, and no comparative-community instrument has been run. The third is available, at exactly one place, and it is used here — **but the fit has to be argued rather than assumed, because the Framework's own illustration of the condition does not obviously cover this genre.** The Framework glosses against-the-grain reading as *"a polemic that presupposes a practice, a prohibition that implies the prohibited behavior — never inference from silence alone."* Letter CXXVI is neither a polemic against a practice nor a prohibition; it is a personal letter in which a self-interested witness minimises his own congregation's conduct to a correspondent accusing it. **The argument that it satisfies the condition is this:** what the Framework's two examples have in common is that the *source's own purpose runs against the content being recovered*, so the content cannot have been supplied by the source's interest — a polemist does not invent a practice he is attacking, and a legislator does not invent the behaviour he is banning. A concession against interest has exactly that structure and arguably a stronger form of it: Augustine's purpose in this letter is to make the crowd's conduct look smaller than Albina alleges, so every element of that conduct he nonetheless admits is admitted against the grain of his own reason for writing. **What is excluded by the same test is stated so the licence cannot be stretched:** nothing Augustine asserts *in his own favour* in this letter is usable under this condition, and nothing is inferred from what he does not say. **This is an interpretive extension of the Framework's condition, not a case it names, and it is flagged for the project lead to confirm or reject rather than treated as self-evident** (§11 item 13).

Letter CXXVI (a.d. 411) is Augustine writing to Albina to rebut her charge that her son-in-law Pinianus was coerced under threat of death by the people of Hippo. Augustine is the hostile witness to his own congregation in this letter: his purpose is to minimize what they did. **What he nonetheless concedes, against his own interest in conceding it, is the bounded trace.** He concedes that the people clamoured; that they *"utter[ed] many opprobrious and unworthy reproaches"* against Alypius; that when he told them he was bound by promise not to ordain Pinianus against his will and left the crowd for his own seat, they *"began to be much more warmly excited"*; that they calculated he might break his word or that another bishop might be found to ordain; that they would not believe him when he said an unwilling ordinand would simply leave; and that they gathered in front of the steps *"with terrible and incessant"* clamour. He also concedes his own fear of what a crowd can do.

*[Inhabited — Tier 4, Historically Grounded Reconstruction, bounded strictly to what the bishop concedes]* We wanted the man. He was here, he was ours by living among us, and a church does not get many such and does not let one walk out. The bishop said he had given his word. We did not think a word given to a rich man's family outweighed a church's need, and we said so, loudly, and we said worse to Alypius than we should have. He went up and sat down. We stayed at the steps and kept calling.

[Construction note: **Inferential/Thin**, and presented as such — Article 20's secondary prong is admitted *"only on independent positive evidence, never inferred from silence,"* and is *"presented at inferential confidence and never as equivalent to documented material."* Every element above is a concession Augustine makes in his own rebuttal (Letter CXXVI §1, Registry row 11, re-verified at source in `cic/texts/npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml`); **nothing is supplied about the congregation's motives beyond the one Augustine himself names — that they wanted Pinianus as their presbyter.** The single word *"ours by living among us"* renders Augustine's own account of Pinianus's residence at Hippo and nothing further. **What this trace does not license:** any claim about what ordinary Hippo believers thought about grace, the sacraments, the Donatists, or their own baptism. It is a trace of a congregation *acting*, not of a congregation *believing*, and it is not generalized past that.]

**The verdict on the three comparators Doc_02 §9 item 5 raised, which is not the verdict that item anticipated.** Doc_02 asked whether Letters CXXVI (Albina) or CCXI (the Nuns of Hippo), or Sermons 280–281 on Perpetua and Felicitas (row 122), meet this world's load-bearing threshold comparable to the sibling Donatism build's Lucilla finding. **On this document's assessment: none of the three does, and the reason is instructive rather than disappointing.** Lucilla is load-bearing in Donatism because a named laywoman's act is a *proximate cause of the schism itself* — she is inside that world's central causal chain. Albina is a Roman aristocrat corresponding from outside the congregation about her son-in-law, and her own position reaches this record only as the charge Augustine answers; the nuns of Hippo are inside the world but their grievance reaches it only through the letter correcting them; and row 122 is flagged at Confidence **C** and has not been independently re-read in this build. **What is load-bearing here is not any of the three named individuals but the crowd in Letter CXXVI** — and that is a finding about this world's own shape: its best against-the-grain trace of a suppressed voice is *corporate and anonymous*, which is exactly what a world whose record is two bishops' pastoral correspondence would be expected to yield. Doc_02 §9 items 4 and 5 are **discharged** by this section, with that correction stated.

## 2. Community Ecology

**Belonging as standing within a body that can be lost and regained.** To belong in this world is to be in communion — a formal, structural status of standing-within-the-body, not a warmth and not only a rite. Doc_03 marks the distortion risk on the word directly: a modern reader's default sense is materially narrower than this world's operative one. The status can be forfeited by failure and restored by a process (**G2**), and it survives sharp disagreement between those who hold it (**G3**). Those two facts together are this world's community ecology.

**2.1 What the community does with its failures — G2, Cyprian's phase.** The lapsed are not expelled and not waved back in. Between Novatianist rigorism, which would readmit no one who had sacrificed, and an undisciplined laxity that would readmit without process, Cyprian's church builds a penitential discipline: an examined, graded, bishop-regulated path back to communion. Doc_03's counts register how much of this corpus is that one subject — *"the lapsed"* 96 times as the exact phrase (114 for the bare word), *"penitence"* 31 times corpus-wide with 20 of those inside the Epistles alone.

*[Inhabited]* No one here is beyond return, and no one returns by simply walking back in. What was done was done in public and is undone in public; there is an order to it, and the order is not cruelty, it is the only way the door can stay a door. A church that takes everyone back the same afternoon has no door. A church that takes no one back has no Master.

[Construction note: **Documented** for the discipline's existence and content (Registry rows 1 and 2; Doc_04 §3 Candidate 2, which finds Repetition, Dependency, Formation, and Explanatory all passing strongly). Row 1 sits at Confidence **A**, row 2 at **B**. The Novatianist/lax framing is Doc_01 §6's own "what was it refusing" statement. **The lapsed believer's own experience of undergoing the discipline is Inferential/Thin and is not narrated here** — a named absence, not a filled one.]

**2.2 The counter-claim the community lived with — G8, Tensional, and held rather than resolved.** The confessors had survived interrogation, and on the strength of that they granted peace to the lapsed directly, in their own right, without the bishop. This is not a fringe irritation: *"confessor"* is, on Doc_03's sweep, the single most frequent crisis term in Cyprian's corpus — roughly 150 occurrences, more than *communion* (~120), *schism* (103), or *the lapsed* (96) individually, with 128 inside the Epistles alone. Epistles XX–XXI are two confessors writing to each other about a specific reconciliation, in the first person, neither yet ordained.

*[Inhabited — both poles, held together]* I stood before the magistrate and I did not deny Him, and I have a letter from a brother who did the same, and between us we say this man may come back. — And I say the peace of the church is not any man's to give from his own suffering, however real his suffering was, because the church is not the sum of what each of us endured. Both of us are in earnest. That is the difficulty.

[Construction note: **Documented** for both poles' existence (Registry row 1, Confidence **A**, the confessors' own first-person correspondence; row 2, Confidence **B**, *De Lapsis*'s regulating argument). Doc_04 §3 Candidate 8 classifies this **Tensional** — passing all six tests as a persistent, bounded counter-force — and the tension itself, not a confidence gap, is the finding. **This document does not resolve it in either direction.**]

**2.3 A positive check this document runs, discharging Doc_04 §7 item 5 in the negative.** Doc_04 carries forward, as an open item, that a Doc_05 pass finding Augustine-phase confessor- or ascetic-authority material would bear directly on whether G8's Tensional classification and phase-boundedness should be revisited. **The check was run and is reported whichever way it came out.** A full-text count across all eight vendored Augustine volumes (`npnf101`–`npnf108`) returns **twenty occurrences** — against roughly 150 in Cyprian's single volume. **The sweep's rule is stated so it can be re-run:** regex `(?i)confessors?`, applied to the tag-stripped, whitespace-collapsed text of each volume. Per volume: npnf101 **2**, npnf102 **4**, npnf103 **2**, npnf104 **1**, npnf105 **3**, npnf106 **1**, npnf107 **1**, npnf108 **6**. **All twenty were opened and read**, and all twenty are accounted for below.

**None of the twenty carries the Cyprianic technical sense** — a survivor of interrogation holding a standing claim to dispose of the church's peace. They sort into six kinds, and the enumeration reconciles to the per-volume counts above:

- **(a) Generic martyrological**, of the dead or the tortured — 6: *"martyrs and holy confessors"* in the heavenly city (npnf101); *"no confessor of a holy poverty could be tortured"*, *"confessors of the faith"*, Valentinian who *"stood forth as a confessor of the Christian faith"*, and that name's matching index line (npnf102); *"light were the chains to the confessors"* (npnf108).
- **(b) Confession of sin, not of Christ under interrogation** — 4: *"a sincere and humble confessor of his own sins"* (npnf105); *"the one was a despiser, the other a confessor, of his sins"*, *"if love were not in the confessor?"*, and *"so is he a confessor, who does not wish that to be seen which himself is"* (npnf108).

## 3. Worship Ecology

**Worship is recovered here mostly through what preaching and catechesis presuppose about it, and that limit is stated before anything is claimed.** Doc_01 §3 says so directly: worship in this world is visible through *"what preaching and catechesis presuppose about it (baptismal practice, the Eucharist, the Creed taught to catechumens), not through separate liturgical treatises."* Doc_02 §5 adds the second half of the limit — the liturgical material has **not yet been read as liturgical evidence**, for what it shows about the rite's own form rather than the doctrine argued from it, and no site report, inscription, or excavation record has been independently verified in this build. **This section therefore reconstructs worship's ecological role, not its choreography.**

**3.1 Both of this world's defining crises are, at bottom, disputes about rites — which is the central fact of its worship ecology.** Doc_02 §5 establishes it and Doc_04 §1 generates candidates from it: the rebaptism controversy is a dispute over the valid administration of **baptism**; the lapsed controversy is a dispute over the rite of **penitential reconciliation**. Neither is doctrine with liturgy as its backdrop. In this world, the sharpest theological arguments on record are arguments about who may perform a rite, on whom, and after what. **That is an unusual ecological configuration and it deserves naming as such:** worship here is not a separate dimension that the gravities act upon — three of the four Primary gravities (G2, G3, G6) *are* disputes about rites, and the fourth (G1) is the office that administers them.

**3.2 What the corpus does directly attest.** *On the Lord's Prayer* (row 5) is catechetical exposition of the church's own set prayer. The creedal works (row 18) preach the baptismal creed to catechumens directly. *On the Catechising of the Uninstructed* (row 15) is a manual for instructing those preparing for baptism — evidence of the practice's centrality stronger than word-frequency would be, since it is a book written to train the trainers. The *Confessions* (row 9) narrates a catechumenate and a baptism from the inside. And **"baptism" occurs 687 times inside *On Baptism* alone**, markup stripped and scoped to its own boundaries — the length at which one sacrament's validity is argued.

*[Inhabited]* We teach you the prayer before you are given the table, and we teach you the creed before you are given the water, and the order is not an accident: you are told what you are joining before you join it, and you say it back before anyone washes you.

[Construction note: **Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction.** The catechetical sequence rendered here is what rows 5, 15 and 18 presuppose and what Doc_02 §5 names; **the setting, gestures and full order of the rites at Carthage or at Hippo are not attested in anything this build has verified** (Doc_02 §5, §9 items 2 and 9), and none of it is supplied. **3.3 Worship's ecological functions, assessed against CF V7.4's own seven questions.** *How worship forms participants:* through catechesis into the creed and prayer before admission, and through weekly preaching after it (**G4**). *How it shapes belonging:* decisively — communion is the name of belonging itself (§2), and the rite of readmission is the mechanism by which belonging is restored (**G2**). *How it shapes theology:* this world's theology is largely generated by rite disputes rather than the reverse (§3.1). *How it shapes authority:* the bishop's authority is at its most concrete in the administration of the rites and in the power to withhold and restore them (**G1**, **G2**). *How it shapes memory:* see §6.3. *How it shapes time:* attested only glancingly and honestly — Augustine's Sermon I refers to *"the matins of the festival of the Lord's Nativity"* and to a day of public shows that emptied the church; that is a real liturgical calendar pressing against a civic one, and it is as much as this build can verify. *How worship functions overall:* as the site where this world's central questions are actually contested, not as its devotional wrapper.

**Forces on the worship lens.** Persecution attacks worship at the point of the participant's own body — the sacrifice test is a rival rite, and the *libellus* is a certificate that one has performed it. Plague presses on the gathered assembly as such. In Augustine's phase the rival communion's existence makes the validity of a rite performed *outside* the church into the live question (**G6**), and the ordinary civic calendar of games competes for the same Sunday.

## 5. Ministry and Formation Ecology *(the central lens)*

**This is the lens the world exists for.** Doc_01's Distinctive Contribution claims that among this project's confirmed worlds, none reconstructs ordinary congregational and lay formation as richly as this one — a claim Doc_01 deliberately keeps as a hedge rather than sharpens, because Step 0 §3 B3 flags a live, unresolved Antiochene contrast against exactly it. **That hedge is kept here too and is not converted into a finding.**

**5.1 The ordinary shape of formation.** A person enters as a catechumen and is instructed — in the creed, in the Lord's Prayer, in the basic content of belief — by clergy working from what is, in Augustine's case, a manual written for the purpose. They are baptized. Thereafter they are formed week by week by preaching to the gathered, already-baptized congregation: *"preach"* occurs **119 times inside the Sermons' own bounds alone** (row 19, ~97 sermons, ~308,000 words). If they fail seriously, they enter a public, graded penitential process and return by it. The bishop who preaches to them, baptizes them, disciplines them and readmits them is the same man, personally answerable for them, and they may well have had a hand in putting him there.

*[Inhabited — Tier 4, Historically Grounded Reconstruction]* You are taught before you are washed, and you are preached to for the rest of your life afterwards, and if you fall you are not finished, you are set on a road back that everyone can see you walking. The same hands do all of it. That is not an arrangement anyone designed; it is what it means for one man to be answerable for one people.

## 6. World-Specific Lenses

### 6.1 Boundary Structures

**The boundary in this world is drawn at the sacrament, and it is drawn twice, oppositely, by the two anchor figures — which is G6.** Cyprian holds that no valid baptism, and by the same logic no valid ordination, exists outside the church; hence rebaptism. Augustine holds that a rival consecration is valid but unfruitful: *"as the baptized person, if he depart from the unity of the Church, does not thereby lose the sacrament of baptism, so also he who is ordained, if he depart from the unity of the Church, does not lose the sacrament of conferring baptism. For neither sacrament may be wronged."*

[Construction note: **Documented**, re-verified at source (row 13, *On Baptism*). Doc_04 §3 Candidate 6 passes all six tests, with the *question* persisting across both phases while the *answer* reverses — itself evidence of the question's centrality. Doc_03 marks the distortion risk on *heresy*: this world's operative question is **whether a specific sacrament administered outside one specific communion is valid**, argued at book length by both sides, not a settled deviation from an agreed orthodoxy.]

*[Inhabited — both phases, in their own order]* What is given outside is not given at all; bring them to the water. — What is given outside is truly given and does them no good where they stand; bring them in, and what they already have will begin to work.

**Forces.** In Cyprian's phase the boundary question arrives through individual converts from schismatic or heretical groups and ruptures his relations with Rome. In Augustine's it arrives through an organized parallel hierarchy holding the same towns, and it is intensified by the Donatists' own appeal to Cyprian's authority for their rebaptism doctrine — the mechanism, named at Doc_02 §2 and Doc_04 §3, by which an internal theological question became externally and institutionally consequential across the century.

**A Doc_04 item that bears on this lens and is named here rather than buried.** Doc_04 §7 item 6 records that the Migne printing of the *Gesta Collationis Carthaginiensis* — the record of the 411 Conference, the most obvious place in this corpus where inter-episcopal authority structure would be visible among clergy other than the two anchor figures — **has not been validly read**; two attempts were withdrawn. ### 6.2 Formation Logic

**What a person is being formed into, in this world, is a member of a body that can hold them through their own failure.** That is the logic underneath G1, G2, G3 and G6 together, and it is stated at Doc_01 §1 as this world's core: its formation logic is *"pastoral and sacramental before it is juridical."* The contrast is structural, not rhetorical: this world does not form juridical actors (contrast Imperial-Juridical-Christianity, whose Doc_05 names exactly that), nor ascetic exemplars, nor philosophical students. It forms people who belong to a named bishop's congregation and are answerable within it.

**The logic has a characteristic move, visible on both sides of the century gap: refuse the single test.** Cyprian refuses to let one act under persecution permanently decide membership; Augustine refuses to let a minister's purity decide a sacrament's validity, and refuses to let a believer's unaided will decide their standing before God. **Doc_01 §6 named this resonance as a possible fifth gravity, and Doc_04 §2 tested it and did not advance it as stated** — finding it is not one candidate but three distinct evidentiary bases wearing one label, with no shared demonstrated base beyond the resonance itself. **This section therefore names the resonance as a shape this world's formation logic repeatedly takes, and explicitly not as an organizing force**, which is the distinction Doc_04 drew and this document is bound by.

### 6.3 Memory Structures

**This world's memory is pastoral biography and conciliar record, and both are thin compared to what it preaches.** Pontius's *Life of Cyprian* is the first surviving Latin account of its kind and is the whole of this world's hagiographic memory of its first phase; Possidius's *Vita Augustini* is its counterpart for the second and **has been read in full** (`Review-Artifacts/Possidius_Full_Read_2026-09-16.md`, all thirty-one chapters, 2026-09-16). Council acts survive as prefaces and rulings. Against these sit ~308,000 words of sermons and a vast treatise corpus — so this world remembers itself far more through what it *taught* than through what it *narrated about itself*.

**One memory practice is directly attested and is worth naming:** Augustine preached on Perpetua and Felicitas by name (Sermons 280–281, row 122) — an African congregation's own martyrs, remembered homiletically within this world's boundaries, distinct from the *Passion* itself, which predates this world's c. 246 start. **Row 122 sits at Confidence C and has not been independently re-read in this build**, so it is named as an attested practice and nothing is built on its content.

**The gap is itself a memory structure, and the discipline about it holds here.** This world's second phase does not remember its first through an institutional chain of transmission; it remembers it through **texts** — Augustine reads Cyprian's conciliar acts, prompted by the Donatists citing them, and argues with him. Doc_01 §6 places that engagement as an internal force with an external prompt, flagged there as a placement judgment rather than a clean case, and it is carried that way here. Per §0.3, **no inhabited passage in this document remarks on what did or did not survive.**

### 6.4 Interpretive Ecology

**Scripture is read in this world toward pastoral application, and precedent is read as arguable.** The characteristic interpretive act on record is not exegesis for its own sake but a bishop applying a text to a decision his congregation is waiting on. Augustine's own exegetical preaching (the Tractates on John, row 21) and Cyprian's use of the shepherd texts to describe his own answerability (§4) are the same move at a century's distance.

**The world's distinctive interpretive object is its own conciliar past**, and this is where G5 actually bites. The 256 Council's acts are, for Augustine, a text to be interpreted and — on his own explicit principle — corrected: earlier plenary councils *"are often corrected by those which follow them."* **This is an interpretive ecology in which the tradition's own prior rulings are authoritative and revisable at once**, and the two anchor figures hold incompatible accounts of how that works. Doc_03's citation caution is carried: *De Unitate*'s *episcopatus unus est* predates the Stephen controversy and is **not** evidence for that dispute; this document uses *De Unitate* for its general ecclesiology only, and notes that the treatise survives in two recensions of chapters 4–5, one more favourable to Roman primacy, on which nothing here depends.

### 6.5 Emotional and Affective Ecology

**The dominant affect on record is a bishop's grief that refuses distance from the people it grieves over.** *De Lapsis* does not describe the congregation's failure from above; it insists the shepherd is the one chiefly wounded, and says *I wail with the wailing, I weep with the weeping.* That is not a rhetorical posture incidental to the ecology — it is G1's emotional content: if a bishop is personally answerable for a bounded flock, its failure is his injury.

**A second, quite different affect is attested at Hippo and is not the same emotion at all:** the *anxiety of competition* — a preacher who knows the games have emptied part of his church and asks the ones who came to be anxious for the ones who did not. And a third, in Cyprian's letters: the exhaustion of a bishop kept from his own people by a presbyteral faction's *"ancient venom,"* writing because he cannot come.

*[Inhabited]* I am not ashamed to say I am afraid of what you might do, and I am not ashamed to say I want the ones who are not here. Both of those are the same thing. A man given a people does not get to be calm about them.

### 6.6 Meaning Transmission

**Meaning moves in this world through five channels, in rough order of attested weight:** the weekly sermon to the baptized (G4); catechetical instruction to those not yet baptized (G4); the penitential process, which teaches by enacting on a named person in public what the community holds about failure (G2); episcopal correspondence, which carries rulings between sees and constitutes most of what survives from phase one; and conciliar assembly, where the bishops say in turn what they hold (G3, G5).

**The channel that is missing is the one most worlds have:** there is no attested institutional chain carrying this world's meaning across its own 133-year gap. Transmission between phases happens through **texts read later**, not through a continuous teaching succession this build can evidence (§6.3). That is a genuine structural feature of this world's meaning transmission and is recorded as a finding rather than as a shortfall.

### 6.7 Representative Theological Patterns

**Four patterns recur strongly enough that a Representative of this world would be recognizable by them.** *(1) Argument from pastoral consequence:* positions are defended by what they would do to actual people — the lapsed, the rebaptized, the congregation waiting on a ruling. *(2) Refusal of the single determining factor:* the shape named at §6.2, present on both sides of the gap and deliberately **not** treated as a gravity. *(3) Disagreement conducted inside communion:* the willingness to argue a colleague's ruling at book length while never placing him outside — G3 as a theological method, not merely a social courtesy. *(4) Answerability as the ground of office:* authority described in the idiom of watch-keeping and of being answerable if found neglectful, rather than of jurisdiction.

**One pattern is deliberately excluded from this list, and the exclusion is stated.** Neither anchor figure's conciliar-authority theory (**G5**) is a Representative theological pattern on this document's evidence, because Doc_04 finds no evidence that ordinary believers, catechumens, or most clergy in either phase were formed by the question as a teaching. Awareness of it is attested (the 256 council preface, row 4, records presbyters, deacons and a considerable part of the congregation present), and formation by it is not. **Per Doc_04 §7 item 2 this is not a suppression of G5**: it remains a real Supporting gravity, named in this world's authority ecology at §4.2, with both formulas' existence Documented. It is excluded from *this* list, on this list's own criterion — what a Representative would be recognizable by — and the two things are not the same judgement.

### 6.8 Power, Influence, and Historical Dynamics

**History's impact on the world.** Decisive at both ends and in the middle: the Decian persecution creates this world's first crisis and its two crisis categories; the plague presses on the same congregation; the Donatist schism dominates the intervening century and remains a live pastoral problem throughout phase two; Manichaeism and Pelagianism occupy a large share of phase two's output; the Vandal invasion closes the world with an army outside Hippo as Augustine died.

**The world's impact on history.** Disproportionate, and asymmetric between the phases. Cyprian's penitential settlement shaped Western practice on post-baptismal sin; his conciliar egalitarianism was cited against Roman claims long after him; and — with real irony this document states rather than smooths — **his rebaptism doctrine became the Donatists' own warrant**, which is what forced Augustine to argue against him. Augustine's corpus became, per Doc_01 §6, *"the foundational inheritance of the subsequent Western theological tradition"* far beyond this world's close.

**Position, power, and influence.** Carthage is the metropolitan see of Africa Proconsularis and the most consequential Latin Christian city outside Rome for most of this world's span. Hippo is a substantial port city, ecclesiastically **Numidian** — so this world's second anchor is a provincial bishop answerable within a different provincial structure than the primatial see whose wider African councils he nonetheless attended. **That is not a footnote; it is part of this world's character**, and it is why this world's authority ecology reads as territorial-pastoral rather than jurisdictional.

**The coercion question, stated at Doc_01's own three-phase precision and not compressed.** Doc_01 is explicit that Augustine's relationship to state power develops across **three** phases, not two: an early opinion, by his own retrospective account, against any coercion (Letter XCIII §17); a real but narrow solicitation of legal protection, argued but in the event not granted, early in his episcopate; and, later, a sustained defense of broader compulsion after an argued change of position (Letter 185 §§25–26). **Cyprian never solicits state power at all.** Doc_03 flags the doctrine's author-gravity risk precisely: it is known in this world's corpus only through Augustine's own advocacy, in his own defense, **with no Donatist first-person answer surviving in this world's Native record.** This document states it at that bound and does not characterize the Donatists here — Doc_02 §6's routing note places that with Article 23 and this world's eventual Representative, not with this document.

**Effects on formation, interpretation, and theology — the Framework's fourth required question under this dimension, treated explicitly rather than left to the three above.** The power dynamics of this world reach ordinary formation by a single, traceable route, and it is the one §5 names: a bishop's answer to an external pressure arrives at a believer as a sermon, a catechesis, or a decision about the table. Concretely — *formation:* the coercive capacity of phase two changes what membership can mean, because a believer's return from a rival communion may now be a matter the civil power has touched, which is not a condition any phase-one believer's reconciliation was under. *Interpretation:* this world's prior rulings become revisable precedent (§6.4), and the engine of that revision is a power dispute — Augustine reads Cyprian's conciliar acts at all because the Donatists cited them against him. *Theology:* **G6 and G7 both exist in the form they do because of a rival system's pressure**, and §6.1's whole doctrinal apparatus is generated by a boundary question that only an organised rival makes urgent. **The negative belongs here too:** Doc_04 §2 declined to advance the illegal-to-established shift as its own gravity, finding that nothing in this ecology organises *around* it. So the honest statement of this fourth question is that power changed this world's **instruments and occasions** decisively and its **organising forces** not at all — which is a finding, not a hedge.

### 6.9 Spatial and Regional Ecology

**Two cities, two provinces, one world — and the difference is real.** Carthage: metropolitan, large, the convening point for African councils, with a congregation big enough to hold organized factions. Hippo: substantial, coastal, secondary, Numidian by ecclesiastical province. **Both are urban, and that is this lens's central finding:** this world's surviving evidence is predominantly urban, while the rural hinterland's Christianization is attested unevenly, and the Punic- and Berber-speaking substrate's effect on ordinary congregational life is, per Doc_01 §2 and Doc_02 §5–§6, **a genuine open question this build has not answered.** It is named here as an absence and not reconstructed; no comparative-community instrument has been run, and no inscription or site report has been independently verified (Doc_02 §9 items 2 and 3, and rows 34, 35 and 38's own flags).

**Consequence for downstream work, stated plainly:** any claim this world's Representative makes about rural or Punic-speaking congregational life would rest on nothing this build has verified. That is a bound on Doc_07 and on Step 10, and it is recorded at §11.

## 10. Inhabited Synthesis — What Formation Was Like in This World

*[Inhabited — Tier 4, Historically Grounded Reconstruction. Composite; every element traceable to §1–§7 above.]*

You are somebody's. That is the first thing and it does not stop being the first thing. There is a man in this city who is answerable for you — not for the province, not for the church at large, for you and the people who sit where you sit — and he did not entirely choose it either; the people called for him and he came, and if he is found neglectful of you it will be said to him.

So you are taught before you are washed. You learn the prayer and you learn the creed, and you say it back, and then you are brought to the water, and after that you come every week and are preached at by the same voice that will bury you. Some weeks the shows are on and half the benches are empty and he says so from the front, and asks the ones who came to be anxious for the ones who did not.

And then something goes wrong, because something always does. A magistrate wants a certificate. A rival church down the road says the water you were given was not water at all. You fail, or you are told you never really began. And here is what this church does with that, which is the whole of it: you are not finished and you are not waved through. There is a road back and it is walked in public and everybody watches you walk it, and at the end of it you are in again, properly, with the same standing you had. The door is a door. It opens and it can be opened again.

The bishops argue. They argue hard, about the water and about who may give it and about what a council is and whether one bishop may correct another. One of them says plainly that no man among them sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, and that every one of them may say what he holds without being put outside for it. A hundred years later another one says the earlier councils are corrected by the later ones — and says it while arguing that the first one got it wrong. They do not break over it. That is not softness. That is the point: a church that cannot hold two men who disagree this sharply is a church that will lose one of them, and this one keeps both.

And under all of it, all the way through, one thing does not shift. Whatever is happening — the edict, the plague, the rival hierarchy, the army on the road — it reaches you as a sermon, or as an instruction, or as a decision about whether you may come to the table. It arrives through the man who is answerable for you. That is what this world is: not a court and not a school and not a desert. A man, a people, and everything that happens to them arriving in that order.

Per §0.3 and Doc_01 §5, the passage makes no reference to documentation, survival, or gaps.]
