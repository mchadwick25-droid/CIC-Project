## 2. Section A — Movement-Scope Eligibility

Section A's own framing governs the order here: *"This section is the procedural home for the Constitutional Movement-Scope Principle. It runs first, before Section B's five screening criteria — it is a threshold gate, not a scored criterion."*

### A1 — The floor

Article 4 states: *"This is the authoritative statement of the floor's content; the Construction Framework's Step 0 operationalizes it procedurally and must not restate it independently — where the two differ, this Article governs."* This document does not restate the floor's content on its own authority; it applies it, and quotes it verbatim below because the first commitment is directly at issue for this world (immediately following). Article 4's five commitments, quoted exactly, not paraphrased or abridged: "One God, the Father, the Almighty, maker of heaven and earth, of all that is, seen and unseen." "Jesus Christ as the only Son of God, eternally begotten of the Father, God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten not made, of one Being with the Father." "Jesus Christ as truly human — incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, 'became truly human.'" "Christ's death under Pontius Pilate, burial, bodily resurrection on the third day, ascension, and his return in glory to judge the living and the dead." "The Holy Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified together with the Father and the Son."

Augustine (354–430) is squarely post-Constantinopolitan; on his own corpus's own terms, *De Trinitate* is the mature Latin statement of the second, third and fifth commitments together, and the Enchiridion and the Sermon to Catechumens on the Creed teach the whole floor directly, repeatedly, as the ordinary substance of his pastoral practice. The **first** commitment — one God, maker of all that is, against any rival principle — is not incidental for this world specifically: Augustine's own anti-Manichaean corpus (a substantial share of `npnf104`, assigned to this world by the corpus-map) is exactly the sustained argument that this commitment excludes, made by a bishop who was himself a Manichaean auditor for nine years before his own formation reversed it. **A1 clears directly for Augustine's own phase of this world, on all five commitments, not four.**

### A2 — Pre-Nicene movements: Cyprian's own phase, tested on its own terms

Cyprian's episcopate (248/249–258 CE) falls entirely before Nicaea (325); he cannot be tested against a creed that did not yet exist for him, so A2's continuity test governs his own phase: does his surviving confession affirm, in the less technical language available to him, the substance Article 4 states, showing continuity with the strand that became the Nicene consensus rather than divergence toward a rival current (Gnostic, Marcionite, **Ebionite**, or similar)?

Cyprian is about as clean a continuity case as A2 will ever see. *De Unitate Ecclesiae*, *De Dominica Oratione*, the *Testimonia*, and the letters (all vendored, ANF05) show a christology and theology proper continuous with — and formative for — the developing Western consensus: no denial of Christ's divinity, no dualist cosmology, no docetic Christ. Cyprian's own theological vocabulary works *within*, and directly transmits into the Latin pastoral tradition, the vocabulary Tertullian himself forged — the Step 0 Conclusion's own separate disclosure (§1 above) credits Tertullian, not Cyprian, with forging it, and the corpus-map documents Cyprian's own dependence on it directly (*De Bono Patientiae* "reworks Tertullian's *De Patientia*"; *De Dominica Oratione* is "dependent on Tertullian's *De Oratione*"; *De Habitu Virginum* follows "Tertullian's dress treatises"). Cyprian is the vocabulary's pastoral transmitter and applier, not its forger — and that is, if anything, the more direct continuity argument A2 asks for: an unbroken chain of transmission into the strand that becomes Nicene consensus, not an independent origin. Augustine's own *On Baptism, Against the Donatists* treats Cyprian's authority as a live, internal datum of the same tradition, arguing at length through the acts of Cyprian's own baptismal councils. **A2 clears directly for Cyprian's own phase.**

### The century-gap question, honestly held open

This is the genuinely harder question this world's own portfolio entry already surfaced and did not resolve, and Step 0 confirms rather than closes it. Unlike World #6 (Imperial and Juridical Christianity), whose 312–451 span is one continuous trajectory later split for testing purposes only by the 325 date-line, this world's own two anchor figures are separated by a real, roughly century-long historical gap (Cyprian d. 258; Augustine b. 354) — an interval Donatism (World #4) actively occupies and contests, claiming its own inheritance from the same Carthaginian see and the same Cyprianic baptismal controversy.

The Step 0 Conclusion itself already named this precisely, without resolving it: *"Constitutional ambiguity flagged, not resolved. Article 3 (Formation-World Principle) requires 'sufficient historical coherence' but does not define whether a formation-type may recur across a temporal gap with a rival claimant contesting the interval (Cyprian/Augustine, with Donatism in between), or whether it requires continuous, unbroken community existence. Checked directly against the Constitution's actual text and found genuinely undefined. Logged as a standing interpretive question, likely to recur."*

The plain reading of Section A's own text is that this question is simply outside Section A's instrument, and no routing rule, elision, or interpretive stretch is needed to reach that conclusion. A1 tests the floor. A2 tests pre-Nicene continuity to the floor. A3 tests contemporary interpretive fidelity to the floor. A4 is a bounded waiver *of the floor*. A5 governs how the floor interacts with Articles 20/21/23 — its strand clause specifically concerns *"eligibility... assessed at the world level against its established strands as a whole — including whichever strand(s) meet the floor"* (quoted in full, including the clause that fixes A5's subject as the floor). The Procedure's own screening instruction is "Test each candidate against A1 (and A2 or A3 as applicable)" — nothing else. None of Section A, and none of Section B's five criteria (Sourcing, Ecology, Uniqueness, User Needs, Scale), tests Article 3's "sufficient historical coherence." **The Article 3 question is outside what Step 0 tests at all**, not answered by Step 0 and deferred, but genuinely a different Article's own open question.

What Section A does establish, independently of that open question: each phase of this world clears its own applicable test on its own confessional terms, as shown above — A1 for Augustine, A2 for Cyprian. There is no confessional content in either phase that fails Article 4. Whether the two phases are ultimately read as one continuous world, two Article-21 strands of one world, or some other coherent framing is Doc_01's own Strand Determination and World Continuity & Distinction work — explicitly Step 1's job per the Construction Framework's own text ("Strand is a finding, never a presupposed schema... This determination is made at Step 1"), not Step 0's, and not decided here.

**Resolution for this world:** Section A clears on both phases' own confessional terms; the standing Article 3 ambiguity the Step 0 Conclusion already logged is neither created nor closed by this confirmation — it is carried forward, explicitly, as binding on Doc_01 (§4 below), exactly as the Step 0 Conclusion itself already flagged it.

### Disposition of the Step 0 Conclusion's "Criterion 2" (person-defined-movement) for this world

The Step 0 Conclusion states that Section A screening "now applies a second, separate test alongside the doctrinal floor: whether a movement's whole authority claim rests on a small number of named individuals' personal revelation or teaching, rather than a broader communal interpretive tradition." This test has no home in the formally codified Section A text (IJC's own Step 0 §5 Finding 2 already logged this gap; carried forward and not re-litigated here — see §5). Disposed of on the merits for this world, on the record, as IJC's Step 0 did for World #6: Cyprian's and Augustine's authority claim rests on episcopal office itself — apostolic succession, sacramental office, the collective conciliar practice both men worked within (Cyprian's own councils of African bishops; Augustine's participation in the councils of Carthage and Hippo) — the paradigm case of "a broader communal interpretive tradition," not personal revelation. This is, if anything, the polar opposite of the Montanist/Novatianist pattern the criterion was built to exclude. **Clears, were the criterion applied.**

## 3. Section B — Seed Screening and Tiering

### B1 — Sourcing (evidentiary rigor)

Test: does enough independent, checkable evidence survive to support a defensible reconstruction at Documented or Widely Accepted confidence for its core claims, with the source-asymmetry named rather than hidden?

Exceptionally strong pass — among the very strongest in the entire nine-world portfolio by sheer documentary volume:

- **Cyprian**: some 82 letters (the central documentary record of mid-3rd-century North African church life: the Decian persecution, the lapsed controversy, plague, rebaptism, schism) — noted qualification: the corpus-map's own entry records that "the corpus embeds letters by others (Cornelius, the Roman clergy, Firmilian of Caesarea, the confessors) under Cyprian's name," so the 82-letter body is not uniformly his own voice; *De Unitate*, *De Lapsis*, *De Mortalitate*, *De Dominica Oratione*, *Ad Demetrianum*, *Ad Fortunatum*, the *Testimonia*; Pontius the Deacon's *Life and Passion of Cyprian* (often counted the earliest Christian biography); the acts of his own council on rebaptism (September 256 CE, the sententiae of 87 bishops — the only one of his several councils whose acts survive; his 255 and earlier-256 councils are known only by reference, not by surviving acts). A further qualification worth naming rather than leaving silent: the corpus also carries a body of four treatises "attributed to Cyprian on questionable authority," two of them commonly given to Novatian in modern scholarship — kept under Cyprian's name because that is how the corpus transmits them, per the corpus-map's own ruling, but not uniformly authentic. All vendored (`cic/texts/anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml`), public domain, ANF standard edition.
- **Augustine**: the complete **Augustine** portion of NPNF Series I — volumes 1 through 8 of that fourteen-volume series, all vendored (`npnf101`–`npnf108`; volumes 9–14 are Chrysostom, not part of this world) — the *Confessions*, *City of God*, *On Christian Doctrine*, *On the Holy Trinity*, the Enchiridion, *On the Catechising of the Uninstructed* (his own manual for catechetical formation), ~97 sermons preached to his own congregations at Hippo and Carthage, the *Enarrationes in Psalmos* (~695,000 words, the single largest body in the vendored corpus, preached and dictated over nearly three decades), 168 letters, the tractates on John, the homilies on 1 John, and the doctrinal/anti-Manichaean/anti-Donatist/anti-Pelagian treatises.
- A **pre-existing, detailed per-work source-assignment already exists** for this exact world (`cic/corpus-map/latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity.yaml`, produced during this project's own corpus-partition work, independent of and prior to this Step 0 confirmation), mapping relevant works to their vendored files and loci, with confidence ratings and cross-world notes for some of the shared Cyprian/Donatism/Novatianism material — real and useful, though §3 B3 and §4 below name specific gaps and corrections in it rather than treating it as settled.
- Pontius's *Life of Cyprian* is a formation-narrative source in the Construction Framework's own sense (CF V7.4, Step 2 activities: "Assess Formation Narrative Sources" — a Doc_02 task, not a Step 0 scoring category; noted here as a real Doc_02 asset, not as evidence toward any other world's own score — the only other per-world Step 0 confirmations, IJC's and the parallel Donatism draft, each score only their own world, never another world's B1, and neither Hieronymian nor Desert Monasticism has a Step 0 confirmation of this kind at all).

Source-asymmetry disclosure, carried forward as binding on Doc_02 rather than resolved here: both anchor voices are bishops — literate, elite, institutionally central. The world's own subject matter (ordinary congregational and lay life) is attested almost entirely *through* episcopal preaching and correspondence *about* that lay life, not through lay voices themselves — the general portfolio-wide skew (elite/literate/male/urban) applies here in a specific, sharpened form: even the "ordinary believer" material in this world is mediated entirely through the bishop's own pulpit and pen. Doc_02 must name this specifically.

### B2 — Ecology (does this candidate support a full, lived world)

Test: can this candidate plausibly support most or all eight ecology lenses, or does what survives mostly amount to one lens only?

Strong pass, and — unlike World #6's own honestly-logged thinness in ordinary Formation Ecology — this is plausibly the *richest* ordinary-believer, ordinary-worship, ordinary-formation world in the confirmed portfolio to date. Augustine's sermons to his own congregation, his catechetical manual, his letters of pastoral consolation and administration, and Cyprian's letters across the distinct phases of his own episcopate — written from hiding during the Decian persecution (c. early 250–spring 251, addressing the persecution, the lapsed, and the confessors' *libelli*); then, after his return, the Felicissimus and Novatianist schisms and *De Lapsis*'s own penitential reckoning (251); then the Carthaginian plague and *De Mortalitate* (c. 252–253) — are direct, first-person, lived-community evidence across three distinct crises, not one compressed one, and not institutional-power argument at one remove (contrast World #6), nor elite scholarly formation at one remove either (contrast World #2 Alexandria and World #9 Hieronymian's own more textual-scholarly registers). Worship Integration (catechesis, the Creed taught to catechumens, baptismal practice, the Eucharist implicit throughout), Formation Ecology (*De Lapsis*'s own penitential process; *On the Catechising of the Uninstructed*), Meaning Transmission (preaching itself, as the world's own primary mode), Emotional/Affective reconstruction (Cyprian's *De Mortalitate* during plague; the *Confessions*' own interior register), and Authority Structures (episcopal office, exercised pastorally rather than juridically) are all richly attested. Likely thinner: Material Culture and daily domestic practice (as with most literary-source-dominated worlds), and non-elite voices in their own words (per B1's disclosure above).

**Passes B2 comfortably**, with the disclosure above logged forward as binding on Doc_02/Doc_05.
