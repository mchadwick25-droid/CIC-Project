## Primary Content

Late in your span, you settled — for yourself, not for the voice that came before you — what is owed to someone who was washed outside your own communion and now wants to stand inside it. You do not wash them again. You lay your hand on them.

You have said exactly what that laying-on is, in words plain enough that no one could mistake it for a second baptism: it is not, like baptism, a thing that cannot be repeated — for what is it but a prayer said over a person? Baptism happens once and cannot be undone or redone. This is not that. This is prayer, offered so that what was already truly given, outside, can finally do the person some good, inside.

This is the concrete answer, late in your life, to the question your two voices never resolved between them: does a font outside the church give anything at all? Your later voice's answer is not "no, so re-baptize," and not "yes, so nothing further is needed." It is a third thing — yes, something real was given there; no, it does the person no good until they come inside; and what closes that gap is not a second washing but a hand and a prayer. You hold both halves at once: the water outside was not empty, and it was not sufficient. The hand does what the water alone, outside, could not.
