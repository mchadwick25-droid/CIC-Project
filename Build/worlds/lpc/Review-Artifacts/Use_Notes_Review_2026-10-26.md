# VERDICT: REVISE

**lpc use notes: independent adversarial review, round 1, 2026-10-26.**

- **Scope:** commit `ecf4c60a` on `claude/lpc-use-notes-2026-10-25`. That is the stage `Build/worlds/lpc/scripts/wb_lpc_s2u_use_notes.py` and the 117 records it changed.
- **Reviewer:** Opus 5.5, independent of the drafter. No model API was called.
- **Counts:** 6 P1 and 17 P2.

---

## What was checked, and how

- **Coverage.** All 117 notes were read in full against their own records: 52 quotes, 18 witnesses, 19 terms, 7 stories, 9 limits, 4 contested claims and 8 gravities. For each one I compared the note with the record's `text`, `statement`, `claim`, `description`, `plain_meaning` and `modern_lens_note`. I also checked it against the record's `claim_guards`, `divergence_note`, `formation_confidence`, `held_against`/`concedes`, `tensions` and `absent_detail`. About 60 notes got a close read and the rest were skimmed for patterns. Every type was covered.
- **Sources.** Two claims were checked against the vendored texts:
  - the Latin of Ep. 20.2 in both Hartel files under `cic/texts/`;
  - the sources each `years` span rests on, listed per record from its `sources`.
- **The stage.** It was imported and its `with_note()` was re-applied to every record in memory:
  - **Idempotent:** a second application leaves 0 of 117 files changed.
  - **Reproducible:** applying it to the parent commit's text gives exactly the committed files.
  - **Insert-only:** no line of the parent text was removed or altered. The stat shows only insertions.
- **Gates.** `use-note-present` and `use-note-shape` both run clean on the real lpc corpus.
- **Readability.** Scored with the engine's own `engine.m1.gates.grade_text`. For comparison, the `don` and `rzg` notes were scored the same way.

## Patterns

1. **The quotes are the strongest type.** The 52 quote notes follow their `modern_lens_note` and `claim_guards` closely. Each passes the record's guards through as not_for lines, adds the false-friend gloss ("'publican' means a keeper of a pub"), and keeps Pontius-only details apart from the Acta. I found no invention in any quote note. The quote problems are a variant guard (UN-14), a word choice (UN-15), and the readability shared by all notes (UN-21).
2. **Paraphrase drifts toward settled outcomes.** All four P1s on content (UN-02, UN-03, UN-04, UN-06) follow one pattern: the record's careful scope ("said", "counts as apostolic", "under this name", "ordinary") is shortened into a broader claim. Compression is where these notes go wrong. It happens most in witnesses, gravities and limits, where the source sentence is the build's own synthesis.
3. **Not_for lines name real overreaches but sometimes use build words to do it.** Most lines are good: realistic, phrased as claims, and drawn from the record. The weak ones speak in build vocabulary about the build itself (UN-12), or are not overreaches a listener would make (the cross-check line). In one case a line repeats an error from its record (UN-01).
4. **Years are mostly honest, with a few loose spans.**
   - Correct as the drafter chose them: `chosen-while-still-a-neophyte` and `election-of-cyprian` (248 to 249), `days-before-the-sun` (413 to 430), the Acta and Pontius death quotes (258), `clamour-and-tears` (391), and the Possidius deathbed quotes (430). The phase spans for undated Cyprian material (246 to 258) are also right.
   - Wrong: `the-one-episcopate` over the whole window (UN-05), `grant-me-chastity` over the Augustine phase (UN-09), and the Constantine witness ending at 413 (UN-08). The undated-sermon span also covers sermons that can only date from 395/396 or later (UN-10).
5. **Emic framing is lost in the term notes.** The term records open "For us". The notes restate them as plain definitions, which reads as the project's own doctrinal claim (UN-19).
6. **No AI tells of the usual kind.** There is no filler, hedging boilerplate or assistant-voice cadence. The defect is length and clause-stacking, not tone.

## Verdict

**Revise.**

Six P1 findings need fixing before this stage is approved to proceed:
- UN-01, a false claim about Cyprian's text that contradicts a sibling record;
- UN-02, UN-03, UN-04 and UN-06, content claims widened past what their records support;
- UN-05, a years span its sources do not support.

The P2s should land in the same revision. UN-21 (length and readability) and UN-12 (build vocabulary) are the ones a participant would notice. UN-22 is outside this world's scope and goes to Mark as an engine and governance decision.

Round 2 should be a targeted recheck covering:
- the 23 findings above;
- a re-score of every revised means with `grade_text`;
- one more idempotency check of the stage.

UN-01 and UN-06 also need entries in `Build/worlds/lpc/Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` for the record-level defects behind them: the certificates `divergence_note`, and the Optatus contradiction as it reaches this note.
