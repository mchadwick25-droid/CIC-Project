## HIGH

### H1 — The newly-added "grace" entry attributes to Cyprian a refusal Doc_01 §6 assigns entirely to Augustine, and the reassignment contradicts Doc_01 §5's own hardest-won concession about Cyprian's rebaptism rigor

**Location:** line 56, the "grace" entry, Preliminary Definition column.

Doc_03 writes that grace is

> one half of Doc_01 §6's own preliminary observation that both anchor figures resist letting a single factor (**a minister's own purity under Cyprian**; a believer's own unaided will under Augustine) become the sole determinant of the church's or the soul's own standing

Doc_01 §6's paragraph, read whole rather than at the parenthetical:

> **for Cyprian**, both extremes of the lapsed controversy at once — Novatianist rigorism… and an opposite, undisciplined laxity…; a refusal, in both directions, of letting a single test of purity decide **who remains within the community**. **For Augustine**, Donatist separatism's claim that a sacrament's validity depends on the personal purity of the minister administering it, and, on a different axis, Pelagian anthropology's claim that a person's own moral effort is sufficient without grace — **in both cases** a refusal to let one factor (a minister's own purity; a believer's own unaided will) become the sole determinant of the church's or the soul's own standing.

Both items in that parenthetical are **Augustine's** two refusals — Donatist minister-purity and Pelagian unaided will. Cyprian's refusal, in the preceding sentence, is about *who remains within the community* (the lapsed), not about the minister. Doc_03 splits the parenthetical across the two bishops and hands the minister-purity half to Cyprian.

This is not a citation slip; it inverts a finding Doc_01 fought for. Doc_01 §5 states directly, and calls it a correction of its own earlier draft:

> on the narrow question of how to treat converts arriving from *outside* the church, Cyprian's own historical position sits closer to the Donatist position than to Augustine's own eventual, more accommodating one — Cyprian required rebaptism of converts from schism or heresy, the same rigor Donatism itself later revived

Doc_03's own "heresy" entry (line 43) says the same thing two sections earlier: "Cyprian holds no valid baptism, and by the same logic no valid ordination, outside the church (hence his own rebaptism requirement)." So the document contradicts itself across two entries, and the version that reaches the reader in the *grace* entry is the one Doc_01 §5 and Doc_01 Round 2's own H3 exist to prevent.

The mechanism is a splice, which is why Round 1's quotation checks did not catch it: the words "both anchor figures resist" come from a *later*, differently-scoped sentence in §6 ("across the century's real gap, both anchor figures resist a purity-test that would exclude the compromised or the imperfect from the community's own ongoing life"), and are welded onto Augustine's own two-item parenthetical. Neither half is inside quotation marks, so a quotation-accuracy pass reads clean.

The stakes are downstream and specific. If Doc_06 or Doc_04 carries "Cyprian refuses a minister's-purity test," the World #4 boundary Doc_01 §5 built — and rebuilt twice — is reversed at the vocabulary level.

**Fix:** State the observation as Doc_01 §6 states it. Augustine's parenthetical is Augustine's, on two axes (Donatist minister-purity; Pelagian unaided will). Cyprian's refusal is of a purity test deciding *who remains within the community* — the lapsed — and Doc_01 §5's concession that Cyprian's own position on converts from outside was the rigorist one must travel with it, not be silently reversed. The "both anchor figures" resonance is real and is Doc_01's own; it is a resonance across two *differently scoped* refusals, which is exactly why §6 calls it "a preliminary observation worth Doc_04's own testing."
