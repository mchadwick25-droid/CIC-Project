## 1. Is there an English *Acta* in `anf05`?

**No.** `anf05` holds Pontius's *Life* in English. It has no translation of the *Acta*. A search for the *Acta*'s own proper names finds none of them in any Cyprian context: no *Paternus*, *Galerius*, *Curubis*, *spiculator*, *Macrobius*. The only hit for "Sextus" is Sextus Empiricus, in Hippolytus. This matches Doc_02 §1 and Doc_09 §6 item 2: there is no English *Acta* in this corpus. Everything below is read from the Latin.

## 2. The three *Acta* texts are one edited text, not three witnesses

- **G** states its source at line 254: *"Die Acta S. Cypriani (XI) gebe ich im Wesentlichen nach Hartel"* — "I give the Acta S. Cypriani essentially after Hartel".
- **K** states its source at lines 4961–4962: *"Text: Cypriani opera ed. G. Hartel (Corpus Script. Eccles. Lat. 3, 3), Wien 1871, CX—CXIV; v. Gebhardt, Acta, 124—128."*

**So agreement among H, G and K shows that the editions transmit Hartel's text faithfully. It is not three independent confirmations of the events.** At every passage this note relies on, the three agree word for word, apart from OCR and spelling (*exilium/exsilium*, *quotidie/cotidie*, *comperisset/conperisset*).

**Differences found among the three:**

- **G 6616** reads *"sacris legibus"* where **H 41569** and **K 4903** read *"religionibus sacris"*. G records this as its own departure, in its note at line 6650: *"2 sacris legibus] religionibus sacris H."* It is not in a passage the story uses.
- **K 4922** reads *"eidem speculatori"* at the second mention of the executioner. **H 41589** and **G 6637** read *"spiculatori"*. Harnack's quotation of the passage also has *"speculatori"* (`harnack_…1913.txt` line 1731). So this may be a real reading, not an OCR slip. It changes nothing in substance.

**Behind Hartel's text is a tradition in more than one form, and his own volume shows it.** His apparatus quotes manuscripts *E M* opening differently: *"Incipit passio eiusdem (eiusdem passio E) Cypriani ex die qua martyrium Cyprianus tulit"* (H 41441–41444). They give different wording at several places, for example *"in uico omnes ante ianuas hospicio principis iaccbant EM"* (H 41547) and *"in atrio sedentis. obuoluto (obuoluato M) et sauciolo oblatus est EM"* (H 41553). His Praefatio quotes one codex with yet another form of the trial: *"Aput cartagine die XVIII. kl. octobris inducto Cypriano eadem inter cetera Galerius uc. procons. affricae ei dixit : tu es Tascius Cyprianus…"* (H 36988–36994). None of these variants touches the execution details below. But "the *Acta*" means Hartel's edited text.

## 3. What the vendored files say about the *Acta*'s genre and date

Only what the files say. No claim here comes from outside them.

**The text's own form is mixed.** It reads partly as court record, partly as Christian narrative.

- **Record-like:** consular dates (*"Imperatore Valeriano quartum et Gallieno tertium consulibus"*, H 41415; *"idil)us Septembris Tusco et Iksso consulibus"* [idibus … Basso], H 41474). Places (*"in secretario"*, H 41416; *"in atrio Sauciolo"*, H 41521). Question and answer in direct speech under fixed formulas (*"Paternus proconsul dixit … Cyprianus episcopus respondit"*). A sentence read *"ex tabella"* (H 41576).
- **Christian narrative, in the text's own voice:** *"beatum Cyprianum"* (H 41463). *"sauctus martyr electus a Deo"* [sanctus] (H 41468). A revelation: *"sicut illi osteusum fuerat"* [ostensum] (H 41473). A burial *"cum uoto vt triumplio magno"* [et triumpho] (H 41599). A closing doxology, *"regnante uero Domino nostro lesu Christo, cui est honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum. amen."* (H 41625–41626).

**So the files do not support calling it a bare court record.** `Source_Registry.md` row 41 calls the *Acta* *"the one strictly documentary (as opposed to hagiographic) record"*. That row's own provenance column says the description is *"builder-prior-knowledge / field knowledge"*. The text as vendored is a Christian compilation built around hearing records. It contains no miracle, and nothing in it is shaped like a scriptural type.

**Bardenhewer** (line 375ff.) calls them *"sog. Acta proconsularia S. Cypriani"* ("the so-called …"). He says they contain *"Protokolle über die Verhöre Cyprians und die Vollstreckung des Todesurteils"* — records of Cyprian's hearings and of the carrying out of the death sentence.

**Harnack, 1913, is the only vendored file that discusses date and relation to Pontius:**

- Line 377ff.: Pontius *"die 'Acta proconsularia' in der Zusammenstellung, in der wir sie lesen, noch nicht vorgelegen haben können. Diese Zusammenstellung ist aber auch sehr alt."* That is, Pontius cannot yet have had the *Acta* in the compiled form we read, though that compilation is also very old. He dates the *Vita* to 259 and calls that *"die herrschende Ansicht"* (the prevailing view).
- Lines 1829–1836, reporting Monceaux: Pontius did not know the *Acta* as we have them, *"sondern nur den Bericht über das erste Verhör im J. 257 und den Wortlaut des im zweiten Verhör gefällten Spruchs"* — only the report of the first hearing in 257 and the wording of the sentence passed at the second. Both texts *"in allen wesentlichen Punkten teils übereinstimmen, teils sich ergänzen, und Differenzen nur an einer unwesentlichen Stelle übrig bleiben"* — they agree or complement each other on every essential point, with differences left at only one unimportant place. **Harnack does not name that place in the passages read for this note.** Footnote 2, line 1847: *"Obgleich die Redaction auch sehr bald nach den Ereignissen er- ;olgt sein muß"* [erfolgt; OCR ";" for "f"] — although the redaction must also have been made very soon after the events.
- Lines 1855–1858: where the two narrate the same events, the *Acta* are *"der 'Vita' überlegen"* (better than the *Vita*). Line 1739, after quoting *Acta* 5: *"Wieviel besser hat dieser Augenzeuge beobachtet und berichtet als Pontius"* — how much better this eyewitness observed and reported than Pontius. **The *Acta*'s compiler is unnamed. "Eyewitness" is Harnack's judgement, not a name.**

**K's bibliography** (lines 4964–4972) lists studies whose titles show the sources of Cyprian's death were argued over in 1913–1919: Reitzenstein, *Die Nachrichten über den Tod Cyprians* (1913) and its *Nachträge* (1919); Corssen, *Das Martyrium des Bischofs Cyprian* (1914–1917); Franchi de' Cavalieri (1914). **Their contents are not vendored.** This note cannot say what they argue, only that the question was debated.

**Two facts from Pontius's own side that bear on independence:**

1. **§11 points to the *Acta*.** ANF 28001–28004: *"Banishment followed these actions … And what God's priest replied to the interrogation of the proconsul, there are Acts which relate."* *Acta* 1 is indeed the 257 hearing before Paternus, ending in exile *"ad urbem Curubitanam"* (H 41415–41461). The Absent Story Note's pointer is sound.
2. **Pontius knew the wording of the sentence.** §17: *"in qua dictus est sectae siguifer et inimicus is deorum, et qui suis futurus esset ipse documento et quod san- guine eius inciperet disciplina sanciri"* (H 41315–41317; *"is"* is a fused line number). *Acta* 4: *"cum sis nequissimorum criminum auctor et signifer deprehensus, eris ipse documento bis quos scelere tuo tecum adgregasti : sanguine tuo sancietur disciplina"* [his] (H 41573–41575). This fits Harnack's report. **Where the two agree on the sentence, they are not independent. Where they agree on the execution scene, the vendored scholarship treats them as separate accounts.**

## 5. Re-tiering against CF V7.4, as quoted in Doc_09 §2

**What the reading settles.**

- **The details the story is built on** are rows 2–4 and 6–8: the trees, the Zacchaeus parallel, the executioner's failing hand, and the strengthening "from above". **All of them are Pontius alone.** The independent witness does not confirm any of them.
- **One detail is in tension with the independent witness.** Row 5: Pontius says *"per manus suas"*. The *Acta* names two clerics who tied the cloths because he could not.
- **The story's Formation Ecology centre is attested, and better than Pontius attests it** (rows 1, 9 and 10). The flock is present overnight and at the execution. It asks aloud to die with him. Its own clergy tie the blindfold, and it carries the body to burial by torchlight.

**How the tier tests apply.**

- **Tier 1** needs *"Direct textual attestation … Named author with identifiable social location. Datable with reasonable confidence"* and a claim that is *"historically credible with appropriate caveats for the author's perspective and genre."* Its details *"May carry Widely Accepted or Contested confidence."*
  - The *Acta* alone does not pass the author test, because the compiler is unnamed.
  - Pontius passes it, as `lpcstory001` and `lpcstory002` already find.
  - Where Pontius is confirmed by the *Acta*, the claim is Tier 1 on the same pattern as `lpcstory001`: Pontius, corroborated by a second text.
- **Tier 3**'s own confidence rule is *"Inferential/Thin for specific details shaped by hagiographic convention"*. That still describes the trees, Zacchaeus and the strengthened hand exactly. Harnack's Johannine glosses and his Perpetua parallel (row 8) add support from vendored scholarship to the story's own reading of these as convention-shaped. Against that, Harnack's line 2574 calls the tree-climbing and similar touches *"kleine unschuldige Hervorhebungen"* (small, innocent emphases), and the case for Tier 1 should be heard at that strength.

**So the Tier Justification's prediction is half right.** Reading the *Acta* does make a Tier 1 story available. It does not make *this* story text Tier 1, because the text is built on exactly the details the *Acta* leaves unconfirmed.

**Options for the project lead.**

- **A — Keep Tier 3 and keep the text, with corrections.** Rewrite the Tier Justification: the independent witness is now read, and it confirms the setting but none of the convention-shaped details. Correct row 5. Remove every "the *Acta* is unread" statement. *Cost:* the strongest material in the world's record about this death goes unused, and the story keeps leaning on a sentence (row 7) whose subject is disputed.
- **B — Retell at Tier 1 on the corroborated core (recommended).** Build the story around what Pontius and the *Acta* both attest: the overnight vigil at the door, the crowd following, *"Deo gratias"*, the kneeling, the cry *"et nos cum ipso decollemur"*, his own hand over his eyes, the two Juliani tying the cloths, the body carried by night with candles and torches. Pontius stays the named author. The *Acta* is the corroboration, attributed as an unnamed early compilation. The trees and Zacchaeus can stay as Pontius's own stated comment, at Contested detail confidence. The strengthening "from above" is either dropped or reported only as Pontius's reading, with the dispute over who trembles disclosed. **Why recommended:** it puts the story's G1/G8 centre, the flock at its shepherd's death, on its best evidence. For this story, it also makes the Doc_09 §8 item 7 escalation (genus clause against convention clause) moot, because the story no longer rests on the convention-shaped details. *Cost:* it is a real rewrite, not a relabel. Whether a Tier 1 story may keep a typological aside even when attributed is close to the item 7 question, and is Mark's call.
- **C — Two stories.** Keep `lpcstory006` as the Tier 3 portrait, corrected as in A. Build a new Tier 1 story from the *Acta* core. *Cost:* two chunks for one death, and a reader has to keep them apart.

**What the story text has to change under any option:**

1. **"Then the bishop bound his own eyes."** This follows Pontius against the *Acta*. At minimum say it is Pontius's version. Better, give both: his own hand over his eyes, his clergy tying the cloths (H 41592–41594).
2. **"The executioner could not do it — the man 'with difficulty clasped the blade…'"** This rests on ANF's reading of a sentence Harnack reads the other way (row 7). Disclose that, or drop the clause.
3. **Source field, Do-Not-Retrieve-When, Usage Guidance part three, and Absent Story Note.** Each says the *Acta* *"has not been read"* or *"has not been opened"*. These must go.
4. **"The court's own record of the proceedings"** (Absent Story Note) and the Registry row 41 phrase *"strictly documentary"*. The vendored text supports neither as worded (§3). Say instead: an early, unnamed Christian compilation built around the hearing records. Fixing row 41 is a Doc_02 matter for that document's owner.
5. **"What cannot be told is what the crowd made of it."** This needs narrowing. The crowd left no individual account. But the *Acta* records what it said as a body: *"et nos cum ipso decollemur"*.
6. **`records/lpc/story/lpc.story.the-death-of-cyprian.md` mirrors the chunk.** It needs the same changes: the second `sources` locus (*"not read in this build"*), `prefer_instead`, `narrative_tier`, `narrative_tier_justification`, `divergence_note`, and the `text` sentence on the eyes. The record says *"Then the bishop bound his own eyes"* and *"the man needed, in Pontius's account, help from above"*.

**The story's ANF quotations were re-checked verbatim against `anf05` lines 28256–28266, and all match.** The one exception is the transcription convention Doc_09 §2 already discloses: *"Persons who…"* and *"Having with…"* are capitalised mid-sentence starts, and *"by the extent…"* drops the source's leading *"But as,"*.
