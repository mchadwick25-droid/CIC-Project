## HIGH-3. Section 4B and Section 8 Domain 1: the non-episcopal silence is asserted at a scope the corpus refutes and Doc_09 explicitly qualifies

**Where.**

- Section 4B: *"what is missing is not the un-hostile record but the **non-episcopal** one. The interior life of any ordinary believer in this world is Inferential/Thin and is narrated nowhere in this build."*

**Evidence.** Doc_09 §7 item 2, in full:

> **2. No story survives in the voice of an ordinary believer.** Every story above is told by a bishop, a bishop's deacon, or a confessor — men with a claim to authority in the community and a reason to write. Doc_02 §6 and Doc_07 §2B both find that what this world's record lacks is not a hostile mediator but **any ordinary congregant writing about ordinary congregational life as such.** `lpcstory005` comes closest and does not reach it: Celerinus and Lucian are confessors, a recognised status. **We have no story of a Sunday from someone who merely attended one.**

`Source_Registry.md` row 1, *Licensed-For* column: *"**Epistles XX–XXI (\"Celerinus to Lucian\" / \"Lucian Replies to Celerinus\") specifically, as this world's own lay-confessor first-person voice, licensed for Doc_02 §6's Article 20 discharge**"*.

I resolved both by `title=` on their `<div>` in `anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian.xml` rather than by position — *"Celerinus to Lucian."* and *"Lucian Replies to Celerinus."* — and read Epistle XX. It is first-person, non-episcopal, and interior:

> In writing this letter to you, my lord and brother, I have been rejoicing and sorrowful,—rejoicing in that I had heard that you had been tried on behalf of the name of our Lord Jesus Christ… but sorrowful, in that from the time when I was in your company I have never been able to receive your letters.

> Know, nevertheless, that I am placed in the midst of a great tribulation; and, as if you were present with me, I remember your former love day and night, God only knows. And therefore I ask that you will grant my desire, and that you will grieve with me at the (spiritual) death of my sister, who in this time of devastation has fallen from Christ…

Celerinus was not yet ordained when he wrote this — Epistle XXXIV, *"To the Clergy and People, About the Ordination of Celerinus as Reader,"* is the later event. Doc_03's own entry calls Epistles XX–XXI *"this world's own rare lay first-person voice."*

**The document is also internally inconsistent about it.** Section 4B says the *non-episcopal* record is what is missing. Section 8 Domain 1, four pages later, says accounts are told by *"a bishop, a bishop's deacon, or a confessor"* — two of those three are not bishops. Section 4F names confessors as *"a quasi-order"* beneath the clergy. The document lists its own counterexamples without noticing them.

**Why this is HIGH rather than a scoping quibble.** Section 8 *"directly governs the Representative's Honest Limits section in the Permanent Prompt"* (template, Section 8 preamble). The handling line *"their own voice did not come down to us"* is written in the Representative's own voice for deployment. Deployed as written, the Representative will deny having a lay voice it demonstrably has, in the one part of its corpus this world's Article 20 discharge was built on.

**Fix.** Restore Doc_09 §7 item 2's own scoping. Section 4B: the missing record is the *ordinary congregant writing about ordinary congregational life as such*, not the non-episcopal record. Section 8 Domain 1: name Epistles XX–XXI as the one surviving lay first-person exception, and rewrite the handling line so the Representative can say what it actually has — two confessors' letters — before saying what it does not.

## HIGH-4. Section 7, Tension 1: a fabricated quotation presented as a confessor's own words

**Where.** Section 7, first tension, *How the world held it*:

> Both positions are held by people in earnest — *"I stood, and my brother stood, and between us we say this man may come back"* is a claim made by men who genuinely paid what they claim to have paid…

**What is wrong.** The sentence is in quotation marks, attributed to no source, and framed as something confessors said. It is an invention.

**Evidence.** It occurs in exactly one place in the project outside this Profile: `Lexicon-Chunks/lpclex004_confessor.md` line 33, where it carries **no quotation marks** and is transparently the chunk author's own illustration of what such a letter would contain:

> They were there. They know what the hour cost. And so they write — *I stood, and my brother stood, and between us we say this man may come back* — and letters like that go to bishops, and the people named in them expect to be received.

A case-sensitive and case-insensitive search of `cic/texts/` returns zero hits for *"and my brother stood"*. The string exists nowhere in the vendored corpus.

This is the false-quotation-marks defect class this portfolio already tracks by name — the Donatism build logged it at Doc_09 (*"a false-quotation-marks risk in the Isaac material, converted to indirect speech with the caveat disclosed"*), and Doc_06 Round 2 caught one in this world's own chunks. Here the direction of travel is the reverse: an unquoted authorial illustration has acquired quotation marks in transit to the canonical world description.

**Fix.** Remove the quotation marks and the *"is a claim made by men who genuinely paid"* framing, or replace the illustration with an actual confessor utterance from Epistles XX–XXI, which are Registry-licensed and available.
