### H1 — The rewritten Layer 2 at 2B-5 attributes to Cyprian a directive written by the Roman clergy to his church, in the one entry whose subject is transmission-mediated attribution

**Site:** `Doc_08_Forces_Document.md` line 203 (Force 2B-5, Layer 2). Propagated to line 378 (§8, "Cyprian directing that his letters be copied and forwarded"); `lpc_Force_Index.md` line 109 (same paraphrase); `lpc_Decision_Log.md` line 1165 (same quotation, same attribution).

**What I found.** Doc_08 line 203 opens the evidence for this world's transmission-consciousness with: *"Cyprian asks his readers "to send a copy of this letter to whomsoever you are able, as occasions may serve, or make your own opportunities.""*

Those words occur **exactly once** in ANF05. They are in **Epistle II**, whose own heading in the vendored edition reads *"From the Roman Clergy to the Carthaginian Clergy, About the Retirement of the Blessed Cyprian"* (Oxford ed. Ep. viii), and whose own Argument reads *"The Roman Clergy Had Learnt from Crementius the Sub-Deacon, that in the Time of Persecution Cyprian Had Withdrawn Himself. Therefore… They Remind the Carthaginian Clergy of Their Duty."* The "we" of the sentence is the Roman presbytery. Cyprian is the letter's **subject**, not its author; the letter is in part a rebuke of him, and the edition's own note on the passage says so (*"so these clergy argue"*). Verified twice, on two files and two code paths.

Three things make this worse than an ordinary misquotation:

1. **Doc_02 §2 warns against precisely this, in these words.** The Cyprianic corpus, it records, *"embeds letters by others (Cornelius, **the Roman clergy**, Firmilian of Caesarea, the confessors) under Cyprian's own name, per the corpus map's own note on CCEL's whole-body attribution — a transmission fact this Registry (row 1) discloses rather than treats as though every letter in the body is demonstrably Cyprian's own composition."* The defect is the one the build's own source ecology names, committed in the build's own transmission entry.
2. **Cyprian's own words about this very letter are quoted two sentences later in the same paragraph, as the fourth item of evidence.** The letter he returns for collation — *"neither the person who wrote nor the persons to whom it was written were plainly declared… the matter, and even the paper itself, gave me the idea that something had been taken away, or had been changed from the original"* — **is Epistle II**, identified as such by the edition's own note (*"The foregoing letter, Ep. ii."*). Doc_08 quotes, as Cyprian's own instruction, a sentence from the one letter in the corpus that Cyprian says he cannot attribute and suspects of tampering.
3. **The standing method Doc_08 records at §2B-5 item 2 does not catch this.** "Mark `<note>` spans before stripping tags" returns a clean result here: the sentence is continuous primary text, not apparatus. The failure mode is intra-corpus attribution, not editorial interleaving, and the recorded remedy is blind to it.

**What survives.** The Layer 2 claim itself does not collapse. The other three quotations verify exactly, in Cyprian's own voice, from works he wrote: the thirteen-letter dossier (Epistle XIV, *To the Presbyters and Deacons Assembled at Rome*), the read-aloud line (Epistle LIV, *To Cornelius*), and the Crementius collation (Epistle III, Cyprian's reply to Rome). "Copied, forwarded, collated and read aloud" is still carried by "forwarded," "collated" and "read aloud." Only "copied" loses its witness. I state this so the fix is scoped correctly and the entry is not rewritten further than the defect requires.

**Why this matters.** Round 1's H4 was closed by a research pass whose whole claim is that the material *was there in the vendored sources and was not looked for*. A pass that makes that argument and then misreads the voice of its own lead exhibit has not closed the finding on the terms it claims. And this is a Layer 2 entry: under Article 22 and 23 and the Forces Framework's governing test, it is the one place the document speaks as the world. Speaking as Cyprian in words another church wrote about him fails that test at the point where it is strictest.

**Fix.** Remove the attribution. Either drop the first quotation and let "copied" rest on the three sound witnesses, or re-attribute it accurately — *the Roman clergy, writing to Carthage during Cyprian's withdrawal, ask that their letter be copied onward* — and say what that shows (a copy-and-forward expectation shared across the correspondence network, not Cyprian's own directive), noting that Doc_01's world boundary makes the Roman presbytery an interlocutor rather than this world's own voice. Correct the same claim at Doc_08 line 378, `lpc_Force_Index.md` line 109 and `lpc_Decision_Log.md` line 1165. Add the intra-corpus attribution problem to §2B-5 item 2's standing method: marking `<note>` spans is necessary and not sufficient; the containing work's own heading must be recovered too.
