## MEDIUM SEVERITY (new)

### M2-new. §6.5's new Forces paragraph asserts a specific transmission mechanism for Tyconius's survival that contradicts this document's own §8 and misstates Doc_01 §5 / Doc_02 §2

**Claim checked:** §6.5, Forces paragraph, second sentence (line 143): "And the same transmission asymmetry named throughout this document (Doc_01 §5, Cell 3B) determined which of this world's own intellectual products got copied at all: Tyconius's work survives mainly because it was influential enough on the eventually-victorious Caecilianist/Catholic side (shaping Augustine's own *De doctrina christiana* III) to be preserved through *that* side's own manuscript tradition, not through a continuing Donatist interpretive community of the kind this lens would otherwise need to reconstruct."

**What I found:** This sentence asserts, as settled fact, that the *Liber Regularum*'s survival mechanism was preservation through the Caecilianist/Catholic side's own manuscript tradition. Three independent checks show this is not supported and is actually contradicted by the documents it cites or sits alongside:

1. **Doc_05 contradicts itself.** §8 (Proportionality Assessment, line 163) states: "the genuinely **non-mediated** material — G3's own three texts and its epigraphy, **Tyconius's *Liber Regularum*** — is real, and this world holds an unusually rich set of it for a schismatic movement of this kind." §6.5's new sentence says the opposite in substance: that the *Liber Regularum* survives *because of* preservation through the hostile/Catholic side's transmission — i.e., that it is mediated by that side's manuscript custody, not non-mediated. A single document cannot correctly hold both characterizations of the same text's transmission at once.

2. **Doc_01 §5 Cell 3B does not group Tyconius's work with the hostile-mediated material.** Cell 3B reads: "Donatist literature survives almost entirely as quotation embedded inside its own refutations (Optatus, Augustine), with a **small independently-surviving remainder** — **Tyconius's *Liber Regularum*** (pending acquisition)... and a material/epigraphic record that survived largely because it was never textual to begin with." Doc_01 places the *Liber Regularum* explicitly in the *independently*-surviving category, in contrast with the embedded-in-refutations category the new sentence effectively assigns it to.

3. **Doc_02 §2 explicitly declines to explain Tyconius's transmission mechanism, and its language for Optatus/Augustine is deliberately not reused for him.** Doc_02 §2's Transmission History entries for Optatus and Augustine each use the specific phrase "preserved through the Caecilianist/Catholic side's own manuscript tradition" / "the Catholic institutional tradition's own manuscript transmission." Tyconius's own entry pointedly does not use this framing; instead it reads: "Preserved and condemned by different mechanisms at different points... yet his exegetical work survived well enough to shape Augustine and to reach the modern critical edition (Burkitt 1894)... **This document does not resolve, and does not need to resolve, exactly how a work by a figure his own party condemned came to survive as well as it did; that transmission puzzle is named rather than explained.**" §6.5's new sentence takes the exact transmission-mechanism language Doc_02 reserves for the hostile-authored corpus and applies it to Tyconius specifically — the one figure Doc_02 goes out of its way to say that mechanism is *not* established for.

His influence on Augustine's *De doctrina christiana* III is itself accurately sourced (Doc_02 §1/§2 both state it), but influence-on-a-later-reader is a fact about reception, not a fact about *how the manuscript itself survived* — the new sentence conflates the two and, in doing so, manufactures a specific answer to a question Doc_02 explicitly leaves open, on a document whose primary strength (per Round 1) is precisely its discipline about not doing this.

**Why this matters:** This is the same fidelity-slip pattern Round 1 caught at M1 (a document's own restatement of a prior document's finding overclaiming precision beyond what that prior document actually established), reintroduced by the M2 fix itself while closing the previous gap. It does not touch any gravity classification or confidence rating, and it sits inside a lens the document elsewhere handles with real discipline (§6.5's own body text correctly declines to narrate what it cannot support). But left as written, a reader would come away believing Doc_02 has resolved a transmission puzzle it explicitly says it has not, and would see Doc_05 contradict its own §8 in the same document.

**Suggested fix:** Drop the specific "preserved through *that* side's own manuscript tradition" causal claim and replace it with language that matches what Doc_02 §2 and Doc_01 §5 actually support — e.g.: "...His work's influence on Augustine's own *De doctrina christiana* III (Doc_02 §1–§2) is well attested, but exactly how a text by a figure his own party condemned came to survive independently at all is a transmission puzzle Doc_02 §2 itself names rather than explains; whatever the mechanism, it left no continuing Donatist interpretive community of the kind this lens would otherwise need to reconstruct." This keeps the paragraph's genuinely supported point (the transmission asymmetry left no following to reconstruct) without asserting a specific, contradicted mechanism for how the text physically survived.
