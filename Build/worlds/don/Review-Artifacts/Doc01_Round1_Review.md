## High-severity findings

### H1. The Strand Determination's Maximianist finding does not clear Article 21's own definitional test as reasoned — and the evidence adduced for it is evidence against it

**Draft text (§4):**

> "**Finding: strand-plural — the mainstream (Primianist) communion, and the Maximianist schism (393–c. 394/398) as a second, evidenced, shorter-lived internal strand.**"

> "This satisfies Article 21's own bar in a way Tyconius's case does not: a distinct, named ground of internal dispute (procedural/disciplinary grievance against Primian's conduct, not doctrinal divergence); a genuine rival authority structure, not merely a rival opinion (Maximian's own election as a competing Primate, with his own consecrated bishops); and direct primary evidentiary traces..."

**What Article 21 actually says** (`CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx`, Art. 21, verbatim):

> "Strand is defined as a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail."
> "Strand determination is a construction finding accountable to evidence. It is never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality or to impose a diversity schema onto a world that the evidence does not support."

**Why this fails as written:**

1. **The draft substitutes a distinct ground of *dispute* for a distinct pattern of *formation*.** Article 21 asks for a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation. The draft's own stated ground is "procedural/disciplinary grievance against Primian's conduct, **not doctrinal divergence**" — i.e. by its own account the Maximianists differed from the Primianists in no formation emphasis, no practice, no ecological orientation, and no theory of authority. They differed about *who should hold the see*. That is the textbook shape of what Article 21 excludes as "merely a variation in detail."

2. **The "genuine rival authority structure" is a rival *instance* of the same structure, not a distinct one.** Compare the precedent the draft itself invokes. IJC Doc_01 §4 retained Strand C precisely because Ambrose's **ground of authority** was different in kind: "Ambrose's own authority claim, on the documentary record, is not grounded in Petrine succession at all... it rests on sacramental and moral leverage... That is a different ground of authority than either of the primacy-among-sees claims below, not a variant of one of them." The Maximianists asserted no different ground of authority. They asserted the *same* Donatist ground against a particular incumbent.

3. **The single most load-bearing fact the draft cites is evidence against its own finding.** The draft records, correctly, that "the mainstream Donatist party's willingness to receive returning Maximianist clergy **without reordination or rebaptism**" is what Augustine turns against Donatist rebaptism logic. But that fact means the two bodies recognised each other's orders and each other's sacraments as valid — the strongest available proof that they shared one formation pattern, one practice, and one authority structure. Augustine's whole point is that the Maximianist affair shows the Donatists behaving as if the split made **no** ecclesial difference. Cited as evidence *for* strand-hood, it is exactly backwards.

4. **The reasoning proves too much.** If a rival hierarchy claiming the same ground, with its own consecrated bishops, is an internal "strand," then the Caecilianist church is a strand of this same world — which the project's whole architecture denies. Step 0 §2 A5 settles that the Caecilianist party sits inside this world's own Doc_02 **as this world's opponents**, not as a strand of it.

5. **The Framework's own sibling test is never run.** Construction Framework V7.4 Part I gives World Separation Criteria ("Has a new gravity emerged? Has a major gravity disappeared? Has formation changed substantially? ... authority ... interpretation?") alongside Strand Determination. Nothing in §4 applies it, and the Maximianist episode is the obvious case for it.

**Note that the document already houses this material correctly elsewhere.** §5 Cell 2B lists "the internal Maximianist fracture (393, §4 above)" as an ongoing internal force — which is precisely where the Forces Framework puts it ("A theological fracture that split the community into rival successor communities," Cell 3B; internal contests and tensions, Cell 2B). The research is good. The classification is the problem.

**Fix.** Either (a) re-reason the finding to Article 21's actual criterion — which requires showing a distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation, and would have to confront the reception-without-reordination fact head-on rather than cite it as support; or (b) record **strand-singular**, and carry the Maximianist schism where it belongs: as a named internal force at Cells 2B/3B, as binding Doc_02 material (its polemical use bears directly on this world's rebaptism theology), and as a documented internal fracture under Article 22. Option (b) loses nothing this world needs.

**Dependencies to fix with it:** §4's "Governing consequence for later steps" paragraph and §7 item 5 (Doc_04 cross-strand testing) both fall away or change if the finding is restated. §7 item 2's addition (carrying the Maximianist suppression's Circumcellion involvement into the Article 20 reconstruction) survives either way and should be kept.

### H2. The Maximianist episode is presented as independently and primarily evidenced — contradicting the Author Gravity disclosure this same document twice restates as binding

**Draft text (§4):**

> "(Circumcellion involvement in that suppression is **independently attested**, and is itself a data point for the Circumcellion/*agonistici* question at Step0 §2, A5)"

> "...and **direct primary evidentiary traces** (the Cebarsussi and Bagai councils, and — a load-bearing detail for Doc_02, not developed here — Augustine's own polemical use of the episode..."

**Why this is wrong.** Substantially everything we know about Cebarsussi, Bagai, the anti-Maximianist campaign, and Circumcellion participation in it reaches us **through Augustine**, who quotes the two councils' own sentences at length (*Enarrationes in Psalmos* 36, sermon 2; *Contra Cresconium* III–IV; *Contra epistulam Parmeniani*) as ammunition against the Donatists — and who is the very author this world's binding disclosure names as the problem. The Maximianist affair is arguably the single most heavily Augustine-mediated episode in the whole Donatist record, because Augustine had the strongest possible polemical motive to preserve it.

That collides with three things the draft itself carries:

- §5 Cell 2B: "nearly all textual material currently vendored for this world passed through Catholic hands (Optatus, Augustine) before reaching us, a severe selection effect already named as this world's central evidentiary problem."
- §7 item 1: "Doc_02 must treat the Author Gravity concentration on the hostile (Optatus/Augustine) side as the world's central evidentiary problem."
- Step 0 §3 B1's Author Gravity disclosure, which is binding and not reopenable.

Worse, **"independently attested" is a term of art in this build.** Step 0 §2 A5 and §4 item 2 use it in one specific sense: attestation *outside hostile polemic* — "imperial legislation names them directly (*Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.52, 412)." Applying that exact phrase to a detail known only through Augustine inverts the discipline the phrase exists to enforce, inside the very sentence that cross-references A5.

**Fix.** State the transmission plainly: the councils of Cebarsussi and Bagai are known through their own acts and sentences **as quoted by Augustine**, which is a real and unusually specific documentary trace but sits squarely inside this world's Author Gravity concentration, not outside it. Delete "independently attested" for the Circumcellion involvement, or replace with an accurate statement of what attests it. This is not a reason to drop the material — it is a reason to mark it, which is what Step 0 §4 item 1 requires of every claim drawn from a hostile source.

## Medium-severity findings

### M1. §2 misidentifies Milevis, and misrepresents the source it cites for it

**Draft text (§2):**

> "- **Cirta (Constantina)** and **Milevis:** episcopal seats of Petilian and Optatus's own opposing subject respectively, both directly evidenced in the vendored corpus (Step0 §3, B1)."

Petilian/Cirta is correct (`cic/corpus-map/donatism.yaml`: "Petilian of Constantine"). Milevis is not. **Milevis was Optatus's own see** — he is Optatus *of Milevis*, the Catholic polemicist, exactly as the cited source states: Step 0 §3 B1 reads "**Optatus of Milevis**, *Against the Donatists*." His "opposing subject" was **Parmenian**, Donatist bishop of **Carthage** (*Contra Parmenianum Donatistam*). The sentence gets the see wrong, gets the person wrong, and misreports the source it cites in the same clause.

There is a second problem underneath it: listing a Catholic polemicist's see as a **geographic center of this world** conflates "places our sources come from" with "regions that meaningfully participate in this world" (Framework V7.4, Geographic Scope). If Milevis belongs in this list it needs a stated reason of its own.

**Fix.** Correct to Petilian of Cirta/Constantina, and either drop Milevis or justify it explicitly as the see of this world's principal early Catholic opponent rather than as one of its own centers.

### M2. §4's "a rigorist faction led by the deacon Maximian" mischaracterizes the Maximianists — and contradicts the draft's own next paragraph

**Draft text (§4):** "In 393, **a rigorist faction** led by the deacon Maximian broke from the mainstream Donatist hierarchy..."

The Maximianist break was not a rigorist movement. The scholarship (Frend, *The Donatist Church*, on the Maximianist schism; Shaw; Tilley) treats it as a revolt against Primian's high-handedness and disciplinary conduct — the Cebarsussi sentence lists charges against Primian personally — with a strong regional (Byzacena vs. Numidia) and familial dimension (Maximian being of Donatus's own family). If anything the rigorist charge ran the other way: one of the accusations against Primian was his communion with the rigorist Claudianists. The draft's own next paragraph gets it right — "procedural/disciplinary grievance against Primian's conduct, not doctrinal divergence" — leaving the two sentences in contradiction.

**Fix.** Delete "rigorist."

### M3. §4's "secured Roman imperial law classifying Maximianists alongside Donatists for penal purposes" is not supportable as stated

**Draft text (§4):** "...forcibly suppressed them ... and **secured Roman imperial law classifying Maximianists alongside Donatists for penal purposes**."

Two distinct facts are compounded into one that is not attested. What *is* attested: the Primianist party used **proconsular authority and the existing anti-heretical imperial legislation** to evict Maximianist clergy from their basilicas — the episode Augustine throws back at Emeritus (*Ep.* 87.8) and works through the proconsular acts in *Contra Cresconium* III–IV. That the Maximianists were later swept up with Donatists in imperial penal legislation is a separate, imperial-side fact, and not something Primian's party "secured."

This is not a small loss. **Accurately stated, it is the sharpest detail in the whole finding for this world's own subject**: the communion whose founder said *"Quid est imperatori cum ecclesia?"* turning imperial machinery on its own dissenters. See M4.

**Fix.** Restate as the invocation of existing imperial/proconsular legal machinery against Maximianist clergy, with the Augustinian citation trail; drop the "secured ... law" claim.

### M4. §5 and §6's enumeration of this world's appeals *to* imperial power is left incomplete by §4's own new finding

**Draft text (§5):** "...even though the movement itself had also appealed *to* that same imperial power when it judged doing so served its case (**Anulinus's 313 *relatio*; the 361 petition to Julian**) — a real, bounded qualification on an otherwise dominant refusal-under-pressure pattern."

**Draft text (§6):** "The qualification named in §5 above (this world's own bounded appeals *to* imperial power **in 313 and 361**) does not disturb this contrast..."

Both passages are carried accurately from Step 0 §3 B3, which named exactly those two. But **§4 has since added a third, in-window instance** — the mainstream party's recourse to imperial/proconsular authority against the Maximianists in the 390s (see M3) — which is the one Augustine actually deploys against the Donatists' anti-imperial rhetoric. The document introduces the fact in §4 and then leaves §5 and §6 asserting a two-item "bounded" list, and §6 affirming the World #6 contrast on that basis.

This is the same class of defect IJC Doc_01's own Round 2 found: a §4 rewrite not propagated into §5 and §6.

**Fix.** Add the Maximianist episode to §5's qualification and to §6's parenthetical, and re-check whether "bounded" is still the right word once a third instance — internal, in-window, and against fellow Donatists — is on the list. The World #6 contrast can survive it; it just has to survive it honestly.

### M5. §2's stated reason for the 439 ending point is contradicted by the document's own disclosure

**Draft text (§2):**

> "Vandal rule brought Arian (Homoian) governance to North Africa and redirected imperial-adjacent religious persecution toward Nicene Catholic Christianity specifically, a genuine ecological rupture **that ends the specific two-party Donatist/Caecilianist-Catholic contest this world reconstructs**..."

439 as a boundary is defensible and I would not disturb it. The **reason** is not. The two-party contest did not end in 439 — the draft's own §1 says the movement is "attested in Gregory the Great's correspondence into the 590s," Step 0 §3 B4 says it "survived the Vandal conquest (429–439, whose persecution fell on Catholics rather than Donatists), persisted through the Byzantine reconquest, and remained a live pastoral problem for Gregory the Great in Numidia in the 590s," and Gregory's complaint is *specifically* about Donatists rebaptizing Catholics. That is the two-party contest, continuing.

What 439 actually removes is the **Roman imperial state as the Catholic-aligned adjudicator and coercer** — the condition against which this world's entire refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy pattern (§1, §3, §5) is defined, and the condition every one of §2's own Historical Pressures depends on. That is a real ecological rupture, cleanly stated, and consistent with the document's own disclosures.

**Fix.** Reground the ending point on the removal of the imperial-Roman frame, not on the end of the contest.

### M6. §1's terminal claim about the Arab conquest is new, unsourced, and not supported by the record

**Draft text (§1):** "...but no continuous line to a present-day communion is documented; **the tradition appears to end with the general collapse of North African Christianity following the Arab conquest of Carthage (698)**."

698 is the right date for the final Arab capture of Carthage. But nothing places an organized Donatist body anywhere near it. The Donatist record goes **silent after Gregory the Great's Register (c. 590s–600)**; there is no attestation across the seventh century, and the standard treatments do not carry the movement to the conquest. Attributing its end to 698 supplies a cause for a disappearance the sources do not narrate.

Constitution Article 22 is directly on point: a world's ending "is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — **never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur**." This claim also appears nowhere in Step 0, which stops at Gregory.

**Fix.** State what is actually the case: the record falls silent after Gregory's Register, with no attestation of an organized Donatist body thereafter and no documented line to any present-day communion. If the Arab conquest is mentioned at all, mention it as the terminus of North African Christianity generally, explicitly not as an attested end for this movement.

### M7. §5 misstates Cyprian's position against Stephen, and drifts from Step 0's own accurate wording

**Draft text (§5):** "a direct, sharpened continuation of Cyprian's own position (against Pope Stephen) that **baptism and ordination outside a genuinely holy church** are invalid..."

Cyprian's position in the 255–256 controversy (the Carthage councils, esp. September 256) was that baptism administered **outside the one church** — by heretics and schismatics — is no baptism. It is an ecclesiological claim about *where the church is*, not a claim about the personal holiness of the minister, and it concerned baptism, not ordination.

The difference is not pedantic: the move from "outside the church" to "by an unworthy minister" **is the Donatist development**. Collapsing it erases the thing §3 correctly calls "inherited from and **sharpened beyond** Cyprian's third-century position."

Step 0 §2 A2 states it correctly: "the Donatists revived Cyprian's own third-century position against Pope Stephen (**that baptism outside the true church is no baptism**)." `cic-build-cycle`'s cross-document fact-consistency rule applies — two documents in the same build stating the same claim from the same source material should match or disclose why they differ.

**Fix.** Restore Step 0's formulation and keep §3's "sharpened beyond" framing consistent with it.

### M11. §1 invents resolution categories for Article 29 and presents a non-question as a live one

**Draft text (§1):** "Whether Article 29's determination should therefore resolve as '**no living tradition, historical reconstruction only**' rather than the more typical '**confirmed**' or '**not confirmed pending further work**' is itself a live question for the project lead at freeze..."
