## Verdict: SUBSTANTIAL REVISION REQUIRED

This document's primary-source citation work is, on direct verification, careful and accurate — every specific quotation I checked against the actual vendored files exists, verbatim, in the place and context claimed. That is a genuine, non-trivial finding given this project's own history and is recorded in full below. But the document has a structural defect at its center: a fully-generated candidate gravity (G5) is tested nowhere, classified nowhere, and yet is treated throughout the rest of the document as an established, operative organizing force — including a dangling cross-reference to a testing subsection that does not exist. This is compounded by a companion-workbook Interaction Matrix that consequently drifts from the document's own prose, an inflated evidentiary count for one of the three Primary gravities, and a Primary gravity's headline persistence claim resting on a citation that the very document it cites explicitly disclaims supporting. These are not cosmetic issues; they bear directly on Framework Part III's own core requirements (six-test discipline applied to every candidate; Interaction Matrix fidelity; Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check integrity) and should be corrected before this document proceeds.

**Counts:** 4 High-severity findings, 4 Medium-severity findings, 2 Low-severity findings.

## HIGH SEVERITY

### H1. G5 (Refusal of Imperial/State Religious Legitimacy) is generated, then never tested, never classified, and never disposed of — yet is treated throughout the rest of the document as an established, confirmed gravity

**Claim checked:** That every candidate gravity generated in §1 receives the Framework's required six-test discipline, Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, Cross-Voice (Article 21 substitute) testing, and a final classification (Primary/Supporting/Tensional/not advanced) — the explicit Step 4 requirement ("Test candidates using six gravity tests... **What I found:** §1's candidate-generation table lists eight candidates: G1, G2, G3, G4, **G5**, D-A, D-B, D-C — G5 ("Refusal of Imperial/State Religious Legitimacy") is fully sourced (Doc_01 §3, §5; Doc_02 §6; Doc_03 Cluster 5) and given an AG-risk flag at generation ("Moderate"), exactly as the Framework requires at this stage.

G5 then simply disappears from the document's disposition apparatus:
- It has no `### 3.x` testing subsection (§3 runs G1 → G2 → G3 → G4 → D-A → the two Tensionals; there is no G5 subsection anywhere).
- It is not named in §2, "Candidates Considered and Not Advanced" (which covers only D-B and D-C).
- It is absent from §4's Classification Summary and from the "Required index table (every tested candidate)" — a table whose own heading claims to cover every tested candidate, yet has no G5 row.
- It is absent from §5's Cross-Voice Test, which runs its per-candidate review over G1, G2, G3, G4, D-A, T1, and T2 — no G5.
- It is absent from every sheet of the companion `Gravity_Index.xlsx` (Candidates, Interaction Matrix, By Classification, By Cross-Check Flag) — nine rows, none of them G5 (see H2 below).

Yet the rest of the document writes as if G5 already exists as a confirmed, operative gravity with its own settled evidentiary base:
- §3.2 (G2 testing): "the specific target of **G5's own imperial legislative attention**."
- §3.3 (G3 testing): "Reinforces G1... and **G5** (the martyrs die specifically *because of* the state-legitimacy refusal **G5 names**)."
- §3.3's Cross-Check line explicitly ranks G5 alongside a confirmed Primary: "...escapes the hostile-mediation problem that qualifies G1 and (to a lesser extent) **G5**."
- §3.4 (G4 testing): "**G5's** imperial legislative attention targets this hierarchy's own clergy and property specifically."
- §3.5 (D-A testing): "Interacts with **G5** (the direct target of specific imperial legislation)."
- §6 (Interaction Matrix, prose): five separate named pairwise relationships for G5 (**G2↔G5**, **G3↔G5**, **G4↔G5**, **G5↔T1**, **D-A↔G5**), each argued as if G5 were a classified peer of G1–G4.
- §7 (Open Items to Doc_08): "3A the 411 Conference verdict, penal legislation, and the Vandal capture of Carthage (G4, **G5's own resolution** and this world's own construction-window close)."

Most tellingly, §3.6's own testing of T1 contains a cross-reference to a section that does not exist: *"Interaction PASS (directly qualifies G5, **§3.7 below**)."* The document's own section numbering runs `## 3.6` → `## 4.` — there is no `## 3.7`. This is direct internal evidence that a G5 testing subsection was intended (and possibly drafted) immediately after §3.6, and was dropped, without anyone going back to correct the now-dangling reference or the half-dozen other places that assume G5's existence as settled.

The arithmetic confirms the same gap from a different angle: §1 generates 8 candidates; §4's own closing tally is "3 Primary, 2 Supporting, 2 Tensional, 2 not advanced" = 9 disposed items. The 9 are G1, G2, G3, G4, D-A, T1, T2, D-B, D-C — netting out G5 (generated but never disposed) against T1 and T2 (disposed but never listed as generated candidates in §1 — see M1 below).

**Why this matters:** This is not a paperwork gap. G5 is the document's own name for the "Refusal of Imperial/State Religious Legitimacy" gravity that Doc_01 §3 already flagged as a preliminary candidate for Step 4 testing, and it is load-bearing for at least three other candidates' own Interaction tests and forces-connections. A reader relying on §4 (the Classification Summary, which is the section a reviewer or downstream document is entitled to trust as complete) would never learn G5 was even proposed; a reader of §3 or §6 would reasonably conclude it had been confirmed. Framework Part III requires every generated candidate to be tested and classified — G5 received neither, and the document does not disclose this as an open item, a deferral, or a "not advanced" finding anywhere. This must be fixed by either (a) writing the missing §3.7 G5 testing subsection, running all six tests, the Cross-Check, and the Cross-Voice Test, and giving G5 a real classification, entered into §4's table and into every workbook sheet; or (b) formally retracting G5 as a candidate (with reasons, in §2) and then rewriting every one of the passages listed above that currently treat it as real.

### H3. G3's "four Donatist-voiced texts" claim double-counts a single letter as two separate texts, inflating the evidentiary base for one of the three Primary gravities

**Claim checked:** Task item 1, third bullet — the "four Donatist-voiced texts" claim underlying G3's evidentiary base, checked against Doc_02 §4/§5 and Source Registry rows 19, 20, 27, 48, 50, and directly against the vendored file.

**What I found:** Doc_02 §6 reaches "four" texts correctly and transparently: the *Passio Donati* sermon, the *Passio Marculi*, Macrobius's letter to the Carthage congregation (noting explicitly that this one letter appears in the vendored file *twice*, once as the *Passio Isaac et Maximiani* and once as a separately-titled *Fragmentum epistolae Macrobii Donatistae* — but counting it once), **plus Tyconius's *Liber Regularum*** (from the G1 acquisition) as the fourth, genuinely independent text. Doc_04, however, applies the "four" figure specifically to G3 (martyr-cult), where Tyconius's hermeneutics have no relevance (Tyconius is tested separately as D-B and correctly not advanced — he has nothing to do with martyr-cult). To still reach "four" without Tyconius, Doc_04 instead counts Macrobius's one letter twice:

This is the same underlying letter, counted twice under its two in-file titles, presented as if it were two independent pieces of corroborating evidence. This should be corrected to state the accurate count and, if the classification still holds on three texts plus epigraphy, argue that directly rather than padding to four.

### H4. G2's strongest persistence claim ("outlives the construction window... single most persistent element... Documented at the strongest level") relies on a citation that Doc_02 explicitly disclaims supporting that specific claim

**Claim checked:** Task item 5's underlying discipline (accurate characterization of prior-document findings) applied to G2's Persistence test and Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check, cross-checked against Doc_01 §2 and Doc_02 §3/§7.

**What I found:** Doc_04 §3.2 (G2 — Rebaptism):

The "Numidia specifically" detail traces to Doc_01 §2, which states it as unsourced background: *"Gregory's own correspondence, not yet vendored, is **independently known** to concern Donatist rebaptism of Catholics in Numidia specifically"* — no citation to Frend or to any Registry row is given there; it is explicitly flagged as resting on unvendored material.

Doc_02 §3 licenses Frend generally for "the terminal-record material (Gregory the Great's Register, §7 below)" — but Doc_02 §7, the section that actually governs that license, states in terms that directly foreclose the specific use Doc_04 makes of it:
> "Gregory's own Register is not yet vendored; **this document does not use it to support any specific claim about the terminal period beyond fixing the terminus of attestation**, consistent with Doc_01's own precise reasoning (Doc_01 §2) that the 439 boundary marks the removal of the Roman-imperial, Catholic-aligned adjudicating power this world's own refusal-of-imperial-legitimacy pattern is defined against — not the end of the underlying two-party contest..."

Doc_02, in other words, deliberately declines to use Gregory's Register (even via Frend) for anything more specific than "the record continues past 439 and goes silent in the 590s." Doc_04 nonetheless cites "(via Frend, Doc_02 §3)" as authority for the much more specific and vivid claim that this material "concerns Donatist rebaptism of Catholics in Numidia specifically" — dressing up Doc_01's own hedged, unsourced, unvendored aside as though it were a cleared, Frend-licensed finding, when the very document that would have to license it explicitly refuses to. The companion workbook's G2 row repeats the substantive claim ("corroborated by Gregory's 590s correspondence, outside this world's own Author Gravity concentration") without the specific misattribution, but the underlying over-reliance on unvendored material for a Primary gravity's headline persistence and Cross-Check claim is present in both.

This does not necessarily threaten G2's Primary classification overall — the practice is independently well-attested elsewhere (Augustine's direct text, Petilian's quoted argument) — but the specific superlatives ("single most persistent element in this world's entire evidentiary record," "Documented at the strongest level this world's record offers," "single highest-confidence Primary gravity") lean on a citation that should not be doing that work, and the "(via Frend, Doc_02 §3)" attribution should be removed or corrected to accurately reflect that this detail is Doc_01's own unvendored aside, not independently corroborated secondary scholarship.

## MEDIUM SEVERITY

### M1. T1 and T2 (the Tensional Gravities) are introduced without appearing in §1's candidate-generation table

Every other candidate in this document — G1 through G5, D-A, D-B, D-C — is listed in §1's generation table with traced sources and an AG-risk flag assigned at the point of generation, per the Framework's explicit instruction ("A candidate proposed by only one major voice... should be explicitly flagged as an Author Gravity risk at the point of generation, before it is tested, not discovered as a concern afterward"). T1 and T2 instead appear for the first time in §3.6, fully tested and classified, with their own sourcing given there rather than at a generation stage. Unlike G5 (H1), T1 and T2 do go on to receive genuine six-test treatment and classification, so this is a process/documentation gap rather than a missing disposition — but it means two of the document's seven classified candidates bypassed the generation-stage discipline the Framework requires of all candidates, and a reader checking §1 against §4 would (correctly) notice the candidate list has silently grown.

### M2. Doc_03's sharpened existence/self-designation/character distinction for Agonistici is flattened back into a two-way split in Doc_04

Doc_03 §2 explicitly corrects Doc_02 §6 for bundling the Circumcellion group's independently-attested bare existence together with its Augustine-reported self-designation term under one "independently attested" heading, and states plainly that the self-designation term "reaches this record only through Augustine's own report of it and **should not be treated as evidentially on par with a genuinely non-mediated source**." Doc_04's own AG-risk column (§1) and Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check (§3.5) for D-A instead group "bare existence and self-designation" together as one **Low**-risk / **Documented** bucket, distinct only from "character/scale" (HIGH-risk / DMR-at-best) — repeating the same two-way bundling Doc_03 was specifically written to correct, rather than carrying Doc_03's three-way distinction forward. This does not change D-A's ultimate classification (Supporting, scope-qualified — driven by the character/scale problem either way), but it is a fidelity slip in exactly the vocabulary-level discipline Doc_03 §2 told Doc_04 to "keep... active."

### M3. D-A's "Supporting, scope-qualified (Numidian regional sub-ecology)" classification is not run through region-specific six-test verdicts for most tests

Only the Persistence test in §3.5 is explicitly split by region ("FAILS for ecology-wide claims; PASSES for the Numidian sub-ecology"). Dependency, Formation, and Explanatory are each reported as a flat ecology-level "PARTIAL," with no explicit argument that they would PASS if re-run confined to the claimed Numidian sub-ecology. The "Supporting, scope-qualified" classification is also a category the Framework's own three-way Primary/Supporting/Tensional scheme does not explicitly provide for (a regionally-bounded Supporting tier, as distinct from a world-level Supporting gravity like G4). The document is transparent about this being a compromise disposition and names it as "the sharpest divergence in this document" rather than hiding it — which mitigates the severity — but the region-specific case for "Supporting" status, rather than a lower disposition, would be stronger if the other tests were actually re-argued at the regional scope rather than left at their ecology-wide PARTIAL verdicts.

### M4. The declared Cross-Voice Test substitute tests a different axis than the one Article 21 cross-strand testing is built to catch

The Framework states Article 21's purpose plainly: "A gravity that holds across all confirmed strands is more likely to represent the world's irreducible core than one that holds within only one strand" — the risk is that a gravity real for one population/formation-pattern sub-group gets mistaken for a gravity of the whole population. Alexandria's own declared substitute (the Cross-Stratum Test, literate vs. non-literate populations) is a genuine parallel on that same axis — it asks whether a different *population* within the ecology might organize around different gravities. Donatism's declared Cross-Voice Test instead asks whether the *evidence* for a gravity is hostile-mediated or self-voiced — a source-corroboration question, closely related to (and in G1's case explicitly convergent with) the Author Gravity discipline already governing this entire document, not a population-generalizability question. Doc_04 §5 argues the analogy in good faith and applies it seriously (it is the reasoning that keeps D-A from being classified ecology-wide), so this is not "labeled and left unapplied" — but the document should more explicitly acknowledge that it is substituting a source-mediation test for a population-mediation test, and should address whether any genuine population/formation-pattern variation (e.g., Numidian rural vs. Carthage urban formation, which D-A's own regional scope-qualification gestures at from a different angle) needs its own check independent of the Cross-Voice Test, rather than letting the Cross-Voice Test stand in as if it covered that ground too.
