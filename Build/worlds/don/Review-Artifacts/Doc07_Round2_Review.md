## MEDIUM SEVERITY (new this round)

### M-R2-1. §5's new "most significant cross-lens insight" claims the same transmission-asymmetry pattern is independently named in §2B, §2C, and §2G — but §2C's own stated finding is a different phenomenon, and the word "transmission" does not appear in §2C at all

**Claim checked:** Doc_07 §5: "the same transmission asymmetry named once, locally, in Experiential/Emotional (§2B), again in Narrative/Mythic (§2C), and again in Material (§2G) turns out, held together, not to be three separate evidentiary caveats but one single pattern repeating at every scale this document has a lens for."

**What I found, reading §2B, §2C, and §2G directly rather than accepting §5's own characterization of them:**

- **§2B** explicitly names it: "this world's emotional life is asymmetrically accessible in a way directly tied to its own transmission asymmetry (§0 of Doc_05)... The feelings this document can actually document... are precisely the feelings preserved in the one part of the record not filtered through a hostile hand." A clean, direct match.
- **§2G** explicitly names it, in language §5 itself echoes: "this world's material record is not simply thin — it is thin in one place... and genuinely strong in another... in a pattern that tracks the same transmission asymmetry running through every other lens in this document." A clean, direct match — though note this sentence's own claim ("every other lens in this document") already overreaches, since it predates this round's fix and §2C, as shown below, does not in fact carry this finding as its own stated point.
- **§2C** does not. Its own "What this reveals that Doc_05 alone did not show" paragraph states: "this world transmits its own most important convictions less through argument than through a small number of specific, repeatable stories... A person formed here would likely know these stories, told and retold, before they could reconstruct the doctrinal argument." This is a claim about *how conviction passes from the community to a person* (narrative before argument) — a genuinely different sense of "transmission" than the hostile-mediation evidentiary-survival asymmetry §2B and §2G name. I grepped §2C in full: the words "transmission" and "asymmetry"/"asymmetric" appear nowhere in it. The closest §2C comes is the Gravities line's two-word gloss, "G1 (the founding-rupture narrative, though hostile-mediated)," and the body's observation that the Lucilla narrative is told only "in the hostile record's own telling" and "never independently confirm[ed] or den[ied]" — real material that *could* support an asymmetry reading (the founding narrative survives only hostile-mediated; the martyr narratives are more independently voiced, per §2B and §5's own distinctiveness paragraph) but that §2C itself never draws out as its own finding the way §2B and §2G explicitly do.

**Why this matters:** This is the exact class of check this round's task specifically instructed — "read those three lens sections directly and confirm each really does contain a transmission-asymmetry finding that the new §5 paragraph can legitimately claim to be synthesizing" — and on independent inspection, one of the three does not, as stated. This is not a claim that the underlying substance is false: a hostile-mediation asymmetry plausibly does run through §2C's own body material (the founding-rupture narrative is unconfirmable outside hostile telling; the martyr narratives are not). But §5, as the document's own flagship "analytical heart" (per the L4 template's own Section 4 instruction), asserts that the pattern was independently "named... locally" in three places when it demonstrably was in two, and the third lens's own stated conclusion is a different, if related, claim. A specialist checking §5 against §2C directly — exactly the kind of check this document invites at §7's own "What external scholarly review should focus on" — would find the citation does not support the claim as characterized.

**Fix:** Either (a) add a closing sentence to §2C's own "What this reveals" paragraph naming the asymmetry explicitly — there is real textual room, since the body already establishes that the founding-rupture narrative survives only in hostile telling while the martyr narratives are comparatively independently voiced — so that §2C genuinely does state what §5 says it states; or (b) revise §5's own characterization of §2C's contribution to something §2C actually says (for instance: within the narrative record itself, the story that is hostile-told-only and unconfirmed sits alongside stories that are comparatively independently voiced, which is a narrower but accurate parallel to §2B's and §2G's own asymmetry findings). Also tighten §2G's own pre-existing "running through every other lens in this document" phrase once §2C is fixed, since that claim is what first over-generalizes the pattern.
