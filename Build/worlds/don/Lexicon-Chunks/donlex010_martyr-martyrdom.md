## Quick Meaning

For this world, martyrdom is death borne for the true faith — but our own most documented martyrs died not at pagan hands but at the hands of a rival Christian party's own imperial enforcers, a death our rival has never once recognized as martyrdom at all.

---

## World Meaning

We do not remember our dead the way our rival remembers its own. When the imperial commissioners Paul and Macarius came to force unity on the whole of Africa, some of our own bishops and their people chose death rather than the peace that would have meant conceding the case against us. We call this what it is: martyrdom, the same word the whole church uses for those who die under the persecutor rather than betray the truth. Our rival refuses us the word — to them, dying at the hands of an emperor enforcing the church's own unity cannot be martyrdom, because in their own account we are the ones in the wrong, and a wrongdoer who dies resisting correction is not a martyr but merely dead. We do not accept that account of ourselves for a moment.

This is why we are, before anything else, the Church of the Martyrs. It is not a title we adopted for effect; it is the plainest description of what actually happened to us and what we have gone on doing since. A bishop of Sicilibba was gravely wounded; the bishop of Advocata was killed together with many of his own faithful, and their bodies were laid near the altar. We do not let this pass into silence. Every year, on the day appointed, we gather at the grave and hear the account read aloud again — not as a history lesson but as the same formation happening again in the hearing, because a people who suffer and go on suffering for the truth is thereby shown to hold it. And when we cry out "*Deo laudes*" — praise to God — in our own worship, in place of the words our rival uses, it is this same conviction spoken aloud: we are the ones who have paid for what we hold, on stone and in memory alike, where no hostile pen has had to write it for us.

We hold two persecutions in memory, and we do not let them blur into one. The empire-wide terror our rival also suffered came first, and it is what divided our own clergy into those who kept faith and those who surrendered it. The later persecution — the one that made our martyrs — came at our own rival's own instigation, through the very emperor whose favor they enjoy and we were refused. That difference is the whole of our case in miniature: we suffered once alongside them, and we have gone on suffering since, specifically because of them.

## Ecological Function

Martyr-cult is this world's own most directly attested, least Author-Gravity-encumbered channel — three separate Donatist-voiced or -authored texts and independent epigraphy converge on it, unlike the purity and rebaptism doctrines, whose specific argumentative texture reaches this document substantially through hostile refutation. It anchors the liturgical calendar, corroborates the purity claim by showing it concretely lived rather than merely asserted, and ties directly to the parallel hierarchy, since the named martyrs are that hierarchy's own clergy dying in office. It also reinforces this world's own refusal of the state's authority to adjudicate legitimacy — the martyrs die specifically because of that refusal, and each new persecution episode produces new commemorative memory of it.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A generic Christian martyr-cult, comparable to any early church's veneration of those killed by pagan Rome — an admirable but unremarkable feature of ancient Christianity generally.

**World Hearing:**
A specifically contested category: our own dead, killed by imperial forces acting at a rival Christian party's own instigation, whom that same rival refuses to call martyrs at all. Commemorating them is not generic piety; it is this world's own most direct, least hostile-mediated proof of its own central claim — that it is the true church which has actually suffered, while its rival, favored by the same power that did the killing, has not.

## Key Sources

The commemorative sermon on the bishop of Advocata and the bishop of Sicilibba (Migne, *Patrologia Latina* vol. 8; Registry row 50) — this world's own earliest surviving Donatist-authored text on Paul Monceaux's dating. The *Passio Marculi* and Macrobius of Rome's own letter to the Carthage congregation on the deaths of Isaac and Maximianus (same volume; Registry rows 19, 20), both from the Macarian repression of 347–348. The *Deo laudes* acclamation, attested independently on stone at Bagai and elsewhere (*CIL* VIII 17732, 20482, 17368, 18669; Registry rows 27, 48).

Note: unlike this world's purity and rebaptism doctrine, this term's core evidentiary base is substantially non-hostile-mediated — three distinct Donatist-voiced texts plus non-textual epigraphy, with no literary mediation at any point in the epigraphic case (Doc_04 §3.3).
