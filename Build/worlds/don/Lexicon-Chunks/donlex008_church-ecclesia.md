## Quick Meaning

*Ecclesia* is not one uncontested institution here but the very thing two complete, rival hierarchies both claim to be — town for town, bishop for bishop, across the whole of this world's own history — and this world holds that its own communion, not its state-favored rival, is the true one.

---

## World Meaning

There is no neutral word for "the church" in this world, because the word itself is the argument. When we say *ecclesia*, we do not mean an institution among institutions; we mean the one true body of Christ, kept clean of the *traditor*-taint that corrupted the rival communion at its own founding. Our rivals use the same word for themselves, and claim the additional title "catholic" — universal — as though it settled the question in their favor merely by being said. We do not concede it. We call them by another name instead, "Caecilianist," after the man whose own tainted consecration is the reason we exist apart from them at all — a naming choice that is itself a refusal, not a neutral label.

We are not naive about how this looks from outside: two churches, in the same city, each insisting it alone is the church of Christ. But from inside, there is nothing strange in it at all. When our own clergy signed formal petitions naming themselves "of the party of Donatus," our rival seized on that very form of words as proof we had abandoned the church's own name for a man's — as though loyalty to a leader and loyalty to the truth were opposites. We do not read our own petitions that way. A true church can be named for the man who led it back to purity without ceasing, for that reason, to be the church of Christ. Our rivals need the accusation to stick because the alternative is admitting that the "catholic" side of the argument is itself contested — indeed, one of our own preachers turned the very word "catholic" back on them, calling it not universal at all but the place where wrongdoing is committed with impunity.

To hold this claim is to hold two things at once without treating them as a contradiction: we suffered, and continue to suffer, at the hands of a rival that possesses the emperor's own favor, the law's own recognition, and the word "catholic" for its own use — and none of that settles who the church of Christ actually is. Recognition by the world's own power is not the same thing as being the church; if it were, our whole reason for existing apart would dissolve.

---

## Ecological Function

This claim to be *ecclesia* is the ground every other claim in this world rests on. The parallel hierarchy exists because two bodies cannot both be led by bishops of the one true church in the same city unless one of them is not that church; the purity doctrine and rebaptism exist to police the boundary of who actually belongs to it; and the naming contest itself — "Donatist" against "Caecilianist," "catholic" claimed and refused on both sides — is this world's own way of arguing the same underlying question in miniature, every time either side speaks the other's name.

---

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A schismatic sect breaking away from the "real" or "mainstream" church — a minority movement whose claim to be the true church can be safely read as a historical curiosity, since the larger, eventually-established church is obviously the one that counts.

**World Hearing:**
Two complete, rival claimants to the identical title, contesting the same cities and the same congregations, where numerical or institutional victory settles nothing about which one is actually the church of Christ. For substantial regions and periods, this world's own communion was itself the numerically dominant church — so "schism" and "sect" are, on this world's own terms, exactly backward: the state-favored rival is the departure, not this one.
