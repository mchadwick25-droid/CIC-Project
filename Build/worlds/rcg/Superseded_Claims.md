# Superseded Claims — The Roman Church in the Age of Gregory the Great

Companion to `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md`, `Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md`, `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md`, and `Source_Registry.md`. It holds the claims those documents have withdrawn that a reader of the current text might otherwise expect to find in them, so the live documents themselves can state current claims only, per CLAUDE.md's "keep the live/canonical surfaces clean" rule (the same treatment `Build/worlds/lpc/Doc_04_Superseded_Claims.md` established for this project).

Entries record what was claimed, what was wrong with it, and which review round established that. They are not an account of any round's own full work — see the review files in `Review-Artifacts/` for that, and each document's own Document Log for the review sequence. Nothing here is a live claim.

## 1. Step 0

**1.1 The census `why` field misquoted,** attributing to it dossier-framing language ("whether these decades are a world or the opening of the medieval papacy... and whether an entry can properly be built around a single person at all") that does not appear in the live `world-census.json`. Withdrawn: an early draft sourced this from a prototype file via the Source Readiness Dossier, not a verbatim census quote. The live census `why` field is quoted in full at §1.

**1.2 The Moralia's own word-count ratio to the Register stated as "roughly four times."** Withdrawn: corrected by direct count to roughly six times (1,022,290 words against the Register's ~160,000).

**1.3 An unsourced claim that the Pastoral Rule "remains in active use in seminary formation today."** Withdrawn: not independently verified; B4's case now rests on documented historical influence only.

**1.4 The Nicaea-Chalcedon-to-window arithmetic stated imprecisely.** Withdrawn: corrected to 235 years after Nicaea, 109 after Chalcedon.

**1.5 World #6's own distinctive contrast with this world framed as one of imperial "absence."** Withdrawn: Gregory's own election required imperial confirmation, he spent years as papal legate at Constantinople, and the "Universal Bishop" dispute is itself a petition to the emperor — not evidence of imperial irrelevance. Restated, narrower: Byzantine *military and administrative* incapacity in Italy specifically, layered onto continuing imperial-church partnership, not absence of one.

## 2. Doc_01

**2.1 The Short Description and Distinctive Contribution described imperial power as in "near-total practical absence."** Withdrawn: this is the same overstatement Step 0 §1.5 already corrected, not yet propagated here. Restated in the corrected, narrower form.

**2.2 The Reccared conversion and its ratification given a single, imprecise "589" date.** Withdrawn: his own personal conversion from Homoian Arianism dates 587; its public ratification at the Third Council of Toledo is 589 — both now stated separately.

**2.3 The Istrian schism dated as originating in 553 itself.** Withdrawn: 553 (Second Council of Constantinople) is the underlying conciliar condemnation; the actual rupture in communion followed Popes Vigilius's and Pelagius I's own later assent to it, c. 554–557, and involved Liguria (under Milan) as well as Aquileia/Istria-Venetia.

**2.4 The World #6-adjacency list in §6 substituted `donatism` for `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism`.** Withdrawn: the early-benedictine adjacency is treated in its own right elsewhere in the same section; surviving Donatism's own link is via a separate source (the Turchi letters), not this list.

**2.5 Eastern Orthodoxy's own veneration of Gregory described imprecisely.** Withdrawn: Orthodoxy has no formal "Doctor of the Church" category equivalent to the Roman Catholic one; Gregory is venerated there as a saint, known as Gregory the Dialogist — stated as such, not elided with the Catholic designation.

**2.6 Gregory's own self-designation "servant of the servants of God" read as a deliberate rebuke of the "Universal Bishop" claim, tagged Dominant Modern Reconstruction.** Withdrawn: the formula heads Gregory's very first extant letter as pope (September 590), five years before the 595 dispute it was said to be answering, and had already been used by Damasus and Augustine before him. Now carried as a self-designation of Contested significance. An untraced "much modern scholarship" / R.A. Markus attribution for this specific reading was removed, not repeated.

**2.7 The formula described as "recurring," found "at multiple loci."** Withdrawn: the file's own endnote records only four attestations across the whole Registrum (Ep. I.1, I.36, VI.51, XIII.1) — a real but sparse pattern, not a recurring habit.

**2.8 The Equitius sequel (Dialogues, Book I) reported, in one revision, as unverified, then asserted anyway in the same document — a self-contradiction.** Withdrawn: resolved by directly quoting the verified passage (the pope's own vision, the summons withdrawn).

**2.9 The primary-gravity distinction from World #6 assigned as binding on Doc_02 rather than Doc_01.** Withdrawn: corrected — Step 0 §4.2 binds this specifically on Doc_01, which discharges it directly; Doc_02 draws on it as established.

## 3. Doc_02 + Source Registry

**3.1 The "Universal Bishop" letter's own "barbarians... in the regions of Europe" language cited as Lombard-specific evidence.** Withdrawn: this is general imperial rhetoric, not a Lombard-specific statement; the Register evidences the Lombard crisis directly elsewhere, and this document no longer cites this passage for that purpose.

**3.2 The Reccared/Leander letter identified as "Book IX."** Withdrawn: it is Book I, Epistle 43 (591), confirmed against the file's own Book-division headings. Same error corrected in Source Registry row 1.

**3.3 Gregory's turn from the storm metaphor to the Reccared news described as "the very next sentence."** Withdrawn: it is the next paragraph, after two further sentences of the metaphor.

**3.4 The *servus servorum Dei* formula read as a rebuke of "Universal Bishop," tagged Dominant Modern Reconstruction; the untraced R.A. Markus attribution for this specific claim.** Withdrawn: matching Doc_01 §2.6 above. Now Contested; the Markus attribution removed, not repeated (Secondary Scholarship §3, and Registry row 11).

**3.5 The formula described as appearing at "multiple loci," recurring.** Withdrawn: matching Doc_01 §2.7 above — four attestations only, per the file's own endnote.

**3.6 The Istrian-schism apparatus passage said to name Pelagius I and a specific c. 554–557 date.** Withdrawn: the apparatus passage itself names only Vigilius specifically ("as did his successors... including Gregory"); the fuller dating remains a reasonable general-history claim (Doc_01 §2) but is not this specific source's own statement.

**3.7 The Book II Epistle XLVI quotation (the emperor's own order restraining Gregory over the Istrian bishops) cited at locus `iii.v.i.xxiv-p8`.** Withdrawn: this is a Book I Seneca endnote locus; the correct locus is `iii.v.ii.xxiv-p8`.

**3.8 The "ineffectual... in vain" Istrian-schism apparatus framing, and the emperor's-order quotation, both called Confidence A.** Withdrawn: only the Book II Ep. XLVI quotation is Gregory's own words at Confidence A; the "ineffectual... in vain" framing is apparatus (editorial) text, Confidence B.

**3.9 §9's own summary claiming "two Confidence A primary quotations" for the Istrian material.** Withdrawn: only one (Ep. II.46 itself) is Gregory's own words at that confidence.

**3.10 Queen Theodelinda's own characterization paraphrased inexactly ("esteemed highly"), and the apparatus editor's own inference that her refusal was "distressing to him" repeated as a fact Gregory states.** Withdrawn: the exact wording ("esteemed so highly," "cordially") is now quoted, and the "distressing" clause is marked as the editor's own inference, not Gregory's own documented feeling.

**3.11 The surviving-Donatism material's locus given as `NPNF212` ~line 22462.** Withdrawn: the correct locus is ~line 23072 (the same Book I summary sentence as the Istrian material).

**3.12 The Moralia dedicatory epistle's own OCR-normalization disclosure described generically as "letter-spacing and long-s artifacts."** Withdrawn: the file's actual artifacts at this locus are footnotes interleaved directly into the main text flow, stray misrecognized characters, and scattered extra spacing — described precisely, not generically.

**3.13 The Moralia's own dedication said to "predate the Register letter" (Book I, Ep. 43, 591), and its dedicatory epistle called this world's "earlier" statement of the contemplative/active-pastoral tension.** Withdrawn: the epistle's own text says the opposite — Gregory already describes himself as burdened with "the Pastoral charge," meaning he is already pope. The finished, dedicated Moralia postdates the Register letter; its own oral origins predate the pontificate (at Constantinople), but its dedication is later. Registry row 4 corrected to match.

**3.14 The Register's own transmission-history claim (relative completeness for a document of its kind) attributed to file locus ~line 22132.** Withdrawn: line 22132 is the NPNF volume's own Prefatory Note on its own selection method, not a statement about sixth-century archival practice; the actual transmission statement sits further on, around line 22971, not independently re-traced to a specific paragraph.

**3.15 The holdings-tool `engine.m9.cli holdings rcg` described as requiring converted, per-record files as a later-build-stage precondition.** Withdrawn: the tool only requires an empty `records/rcg/` directory to exist.

**3.16 Source Registry row 1a scoped only to Ep. 16 and Ep. 74,** while the same introductory apparatus was already being cited elsewhere in Doc_02 for several further claims (the Lombard-truce survey, the Bertha correspondence, the Register's own transmission history) with no row of its own covering them. Withdrawn: row 1a's own Licensed For was widened to cover all of it.

**3.17 Source Registry row 1 had no locus covering the Book II Ep. XLVI quotation directly.** Withdrawn: row 1 now covers it, at the corrected locus.
