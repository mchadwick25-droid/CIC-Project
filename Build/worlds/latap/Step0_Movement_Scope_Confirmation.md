### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears for all five core figures, with five honest complications disclosed rather than smoothed over

The body below discloses five complications, one per author: Minucius Felix (Christological absence), Lactantius (pneumatology and eschatology), Commodian (disputed date), Arnobius (anthropology), and Tertullian (his own later New Prophecy/Montanist alignment, immediately below) — matching the Resolution sentence, the Section A conclusion, and §4 items 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8.

**Tertullian clears, with his own later-life complication disclosed on the same footing as the other four, not treated as a floor question.** Tertullian's central theological achievement is not merely continuous with what became Nicene Trinitarian and Christological vocabulary — like Justin's Logos theology in I.35, it is one of its direct historical sources: *Against Praxeas* supplies much of Western Christendom's own working vocabulary for one substance in three persons, and *The Prescription Against Heretics* and the *Apology* are proto-orthodox on every one of Article 4's five commitments. The real complication is later and different in kind: in the later part of his writing life Tertullian aligned himself with the New Prophecy out of Phrygia, a fact the corpus map itself discloses plainly and repeatedly. Seven works — *On Monogamy*, *On Modesty*, *On Fasting*, *On the Veiling of Virgins*, *On Exhortation to Chastity*, *De Fuga in Persecutione* and *Against Praxeas* — carry an explicit `montanism-the-new-prophecy` cross-assignment alongside their home assignment here. Three are `assigned` there (*On Fasting*, *On Modesty*, *On Monogamy*) and four `provisional` (the other four). The markers differ by work. *On Monogamy*, *On Modesty* and *On the Veiling of Virgins* appeal to "the Paraclete." *On Fasting* names Montanus. *On Exhortation to Chastity* cites an oracle of "the holy prophetess Prisca" as authority. *De Fuga* has no explicit Paraclete vocabulary in the vendored translation, and its assignment rests on its rigorist position and its dating. *Against Praxeas* is placed in his New Prophecy period by the map's own note. That last work is also the source of the Trinitarian vocabulary credited to him above. Both facts hold, and Doc_02 must state both (§4 item 8). **Checked directly against the census's own Montanism entry (I.25) rather than assumed:** its `floorNote` states plainly that "what the New Prophecy confessed about God, Christ and the Spirit is not what its opponents objected to — as far as the surviving evidence shows, it held the same doctrine as the churches that condemned it. The dispute was about authority and discipline." This is a materially different case from I.35's Tatian, whose own later Encratite turn Irenaeus traces to a doctrinal source (Saturninus and Marcion) outside proto-orthodoxy entirely; Tertullian's later rigorism is a discipline-and-authority dispute on an otherwise shared doctrinal floor, not a rival-current divergence, and this document does not treat it as one. It is nonetheless a real complication requiring honest, bounded disclosure, not a silent pass: Tertullian's own later works argue for stricter fasting, against second marriage, against a bishop's own authority to admit adulterers back to communion, all on the authority of ongoing prophecy — a genuinely idiosyncratic later-career position, on the same disclosure standard this document already applies to Minucius Felix's Christological silence, Lactantius' pneumatology, and Arnobius' anthropology.

**Minucius Felix clears, with a disclosure stated precisely.** The *Octavius* is not merely thin on distinctively Trinitarian or Christological content — word-level inspection of the vendored text (`anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml`, div2 iv.iii, 23,819 words including editorial apparatus) finds it is, in Minucius' own prose, **absent**: zero occurrences of "Jesus," zero of "Son of God," zero of any incarnation language. "Christ" occurs **twice** — the ch. XXXVII *Argument* heading ("Confession of Christ's Name") and an editorial footnote citing Athenagoras — and "crucifi-" occurs twice more (the ch. IX *Argument* heading, "They Worship a Crucified Man," and an editorial gloss at ch. XXIX). All four occurrences are ANF editorial apparatus — chapter headings and footnotes — not Minucius' own prose, so there is no positive Christological statement anywhere in the work itself. The one place the text meets the charge directly, ch. XXIX, reads "you wander far from the neighbourhood of the truth, in thinking either that a criminal deserved, or that an earthly being was able, to be believed God" and moves immediately to mocking Egyptian god-kings. Read directly, that sentence rejects the pagan's own characterization of Christ as "a criminal" rather than straightforwardly denying his divinity — it declines to deny Christ's divinity rather than denying it outright, and this document adopts that reading consistently. This is a live, long-running scholarly question, not a settled feature of persuasive strategy that this document is entitled to explain away, and it is named as such rather than resolved (see §4 item 3 below).

**Lactantius requires a real, disclosed complication, not an unqualified clearance.** His biography is accurate — rhetor at Nicomedia, tutor to Constantine's son Crispus at Trier — but he does not clear without theological qualification. Jerome (*Ep.* 84.7, to Pammachius and Oceanus) charges that Lactantius denies the Spirit's substance. The vendored translation (`cic/texts/npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml`) reads: "Lactantius in his books and particularly in his letters to Demetrian altogether denies the subsistence of the Holy Spirit, and following the error of the Jews says that the passages in which he is spoken of refer to the Father or to the Son" — a charge against exactly Article 4's fifth commitment, "The Holy Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified together with the Father and the Son," quoted here in full rather than paraphrased — leveled at Lactantius' **books generally**, with the letters to Demetrianus named only as its sharpest instance, not its sole target. The letters to Demetrianus are genuinely lost and not among the vendored texts in `cic/texts/`, and that is worth disclosing — but the charge does not attach to them *rather than* to the *Divine Institutes*; Jerome's own very next sentence asks who can forbid him to read the *Institutes* "simply because this opinion of his is to be abhorred," confirming the charge reaches Lactantius' surviving, vendored work. The *Divine Institutes* also carries a chiliastic reading of the end times (Book VII) and, read directly, a two-spirits cosmology at II.8–9 — God producing a second being "in whom the disposition of the divine origin did not remain," who "passed from good to evil" — precisely the binitarian-with-a-fallen-second-spirit structure the ancient charge concerns, not a structure outside its reach. Doc_02's disclosure must say plainly that no primary text of the charge's sharpest instance (the lost letters) can be shown, while the charge itself is leveled at the corpus this candidate does hold. None of this is a rival-current divergence — it is the same kind of disclosed, bounded idiosyncrasy this document already grants Minucius Felix and Arnobius. Jerome also names Lactantius among those who follow the chiliast view (*De viris illustribus* 18, on Papias), and the vendored Latin of Book VII carries that view in Lactantius' own words (§4 item 5). Which of the dualist passages in the *Divine Institutes* are Lactantius' own is itself disputed (§4 item 5).

**Commodian and Arnobius each carry a real, disclosable complication, with Arnobius' correctly attributed:**

- **Commodian's date is genuinely disputed in the scholarship.** The census says scholars have placed him "anywhere between the third century and the fifth." The public-domain literature reviewed in §4 item 1 puts most proposals wholly inside this candidate's window (mid-third century to 313). The upper end of Harnack's range (c. 260 to c. 350) crosses the window's 320 close. One minority thesis, Brewer's fifth-century one (1906), lies wholly outside it, and outside Era 1. This is a scope question, not a floor question, and this document does not conflate the two: A2 tests continuity for a figure who belongs to this window; whether Commodian belongs to this window at all is prior to that test, not a complication within it. The cited dating judgment is now at §4 item 1, tagged `Contested`, and the approved slate keeps him on this shelf, with the minority thesis disclosed.
- **Arnobius' theology carries real, disclosed idiosyncrasy, with its own biographical attribution stated carefully.** Arnobius did not himself write, anywhere in *Against the Heathen*, that he wrote it "by his own admission," "at his bishop's insistence," or that his freshly-converted, uncatechized status is established "on his own text's own evidence" — all three belong to Jerome's report alone, and *De viris illustribus* 79 itself states only that Arnobius was "a most successful teacher of rhetoric at Sicca in Africa during the reign of Diocletian" who "wrote volumes Against the nations which may be found everywhere" — no dream, no bishop, no refusal, no pledge. The dream-and-bishop account is the *Chronicle*'s alone. Jerome's entry stands under the year 2342 (a.d. 326) in the ANF06 editor's account and under 2343 (a.d. 327) in Harnack's citation. The *Chronicle* itself is not among the vendored texts in `cic/texts/`. That is a disclosure of a different kind from the lost Demetrianus letters named at §2 A2/§4 item 5 above: the *Chronicle* survives and is simply not vendored. The ANF06 introductory notice does quote the passage in English: Arnobius was "led by visions to the faith; and not being received by the bishop as hitherto a persistent enemy to Christ, composed very excellent books against his former belief" (`anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml`, lines 39070–39080). Arnobius' **bishop refused to receive him**, and Arnobius wrote the books — the reverse of "at his bishop's insistence." The Latin, as Harnack quotes it, closes "et tandem veluti quibusdam obsidibus pietatis foedus impetravit," and Harnack summarises the story as Arnobius having written "zum Erweise seiner jüngst gewonnenen Christlichkeit" (Geschichte der altchristlichen Litteratur II.2, pp. 414–415). This document glosses the Latin clause no further. Nothing in the *Adversus Nationes* itself describes its author's own biography this way. The census's own entry carries the appropriate hedge, "if Jerome is to be believed," which this document adopts. The theological content itself is accurately described: Book II argues the soul is of a *media qualitas*, not naturally immortal, receiving immortality as a gift through Christ — a genuinely idiosyncratic position, disclosed here rather than laundered into settled orthodoxy.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Minucius Felix's Christological absence, Lactantius' pneumatological and eschatological complications, Commodian's dating, Arnobius' anthropology (and its correctly attributed provenance), and Tertullian's own later New Prophecy alignment, all carried forward as binding, disclosed items in §4 — none smoothed over, and none, on the evidence available, disqualifying.

North African dating holds and wrong if the 5th-c. Gallic one does. Nothing in the corpus settles it." The *Carmen apologeticum* carries the same role and confidence for the same reason. This bears directly on §4 item 1. Arnobius' seven books *Against the Heathen* (140,826 words, `anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml`); Lactantius' *Divine Institutes* (242,005 words, Books I–VII and the *Epitome*), *On the Anger of God* (20,397 words), *On the Workmanship of God* (18,853 words), and fragments (3,932 words) are vendored (`anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml`). The *Epitome* also carries no row of its own on `cic/corpus-map/latin-apologists.yaml`. **464,840 words in total for the original seven works.** The census's sentence "his surviving work totals just under half a million words" is written of Lactantius alone; Lactantius' four works here total 285,187 words, and the figure that matches the census's phrase is the original seven's total (§4 item 1 lists this for the census owner).

**Tertullian's own corpus, recounted by the same method — direct text extraction between each work's own div2 boundary in the vendored XML, not the corpus map's own rounded figures.** All 32 of his works are vendored, across `anf03_tertullian.xml` and `anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml`. The largest single items: *The Five Books Against Marcion* (187,134 words, in agreement with the corpus map's own rounded "187k words" note), his largest surviving work by a wide margin; *A Treatise on the Soul* (49,425 words); *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* (46,242 words); *Ad Nationes* (34,919 words); the *Appendix of poems ascribed to Tertullian* (32,001 words — pseudonymous verse, filed under his name per the corpus map's own ruling, "IT DOES, AND IT ALSO FILES AS WHAT IT IS," and also dual-assigned to `apocryphal-and-pseudepigraphal-literature`); *Against Praxeas* (31,933 words); the *Apology* itself (38,163 words); *On Modesty* (26,183 words); *Against Hermogenes* (23,066 words); *An Answer to the Jews* (22,346 words); *The Prescription Against Heretics* (20,936 words); *On the Flesh of Christ* (20,148 words). The remaining twenty works range from *Ad Martyras* (2,677 words) to *On Idolatry* (15,121 words). **All 32 works together: 731,618 words.** Adding the *Passion of the Holy Martyrs Perpetua and Felicitas* (7,325 words, `author: passion_of_perpetua`, transmitted with his corpus and already carried on this candidate's own corpus map, per §2 A5 above) — the fuller figure for "everything the merger added" — **738,943 words added.**

**Combined total: 1,203,783 words** (464,840 plus 738,943), for the 40 works counted — a corpus two and a half times its pre-merger size. This document has not run a census-wide word-count comparison and does not claim this is among the largest corpora in the census generally; that would require recounting every other candidate and built world by the same method. **Method, exactly.** For each work, take the text of the vendored ANF XML file from the work's own opening `<div2 …>` tag to the next `<div2 `, `<div1 ` or `</div1>` tag. Replace every XML tag with a space. Split on whitespace. Count the tokens. The script is a regular-expression pass in Python with no XML parser, run over `anf03`, `anf04`, `anf06` and `anf07`. It counts everything inside the division: the work's heading, the ANF chapter headings and arguments, editorial footnotes and brackets, and Greek and Hebrew words. It is a count of the ANF text as printed, not of the ancient author's own words.
