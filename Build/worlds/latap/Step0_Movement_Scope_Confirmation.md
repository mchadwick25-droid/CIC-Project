### A2 — Pre-Nicene continuity: clears for all five core figures, with five honest complications disclosed rather than smoothed over

The body below discloses five complications, one per author: Minucius Felix (Christological absence), Lactantius (pneumatology and eschatology), Commodian (disputed date), Arnobius (anthropology), and Tertullian (his own later New Prophecy/Montanist alignment, immediately below) — matching the Resolution sentence, the Section A conclusion, and §4 items 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8.

**Tertullian clears, with his own later-life complication disclosed on the same footing as the other four, not treated as a floor question.** Tertullian's central theological achievement is not merely continuous with what became Nicene Trinitarian and Christological vocabulary — like Justin's Logos theology in I.35, it is one of its direct historical sources: *Against Praxeas* supplies much of Western Christendom's own working vocabulary for one substance in three persons, and *The Prescription Against Heretics* and the *Apology* are proto-orthodox on every one of Article 4's five commitments. The real complication is later and different in kind: in his final years Tertullian aligned himself with the New Prophecy out of Phrygia, a fact the corpus map itself discloses plainly and repeatedly (six works — *On Monogamy*, *On Modesty*, *On Fasting*, *On the Veiling of Virgins*, *On Exhortation to Chastity*, *De Fuga in Persecutione* — carry an explicit `montanism-the-new-prophecy` cross-assignment alongside their home assignment here, on internal markers: repeated appeals to "the Paraclete," and to Montanus and Priscilla by name in some). **Checked directly against the census's own Montanism entry (I.25) rather than assumed:** its `floorNote` states plainly that "what the New Prophecy confessed about God, Christ and the Spirit is not what its opponents objected to — as far as the surviving evidence shows, it held the same doctrine as the churches that condemned it. The dispute was about authority and discipline." This is a materially different case from I.35's Tatian, whose own later Encratite turn Irenaeus traces to a doctrinal source (Saturninus and Marcion) outside proto-orthodoxy entirely; Tertullian's later rigorism is a discipline-and-authority dispute on an otherwise shared doctrinal floor, not a rival-current divergence, and this document does not treat it as one. It is nonetheless a real complication requiring honest, bounded disclosure, not a silent pass: Tertullian's own later works argue for stricter fasting, against second marriage, against a bishop's own authority to admit adulterers back to communion, all on the authority of ongoing prophecy — a genuinely idiosyncratic later-career position, on the same disclosure standard this document already applies to Minucius Felix's Christological silence, Lactantius' pneumatology, and Arnobius' anthropology.

**Minucius Felix clears, with a disclosure stated precisely.** The *Octavius* is not merely thin on distinctively Trinitarian or Christological content — word-level inspection of the vendored text (`anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml`, div2 iv.iii, 23,819 words including editorial apparatus) finds it is, in Minucius' own prose, **absent**: zero occurrences of "Jesus," zero of "Son of God," zero of any incarnation language. "Christ" occurs **twice** — the ch. XXXVII *Argument* heading ("Confession of Christ's Name") and an editorial footnote citing Athenagoras — and "crucifi-" occurs twice more (the ch. IX *Argument* heading, "They Worship a Crucified Man," and an editorial gloss at ch. XXIX). All four occurrences are ANF editorial apparatus — chapter headings and footnotes — not Minucius' own prose, so there is no positive Christological statement anywhere in the work itself. The one place the text meets the charge directly, ch. XXIX, reads "you wander far from the neighbourhood of the truth, in thinking either that a criminal deserved, or that an earthly being was able, to be believed God" and moves immediately to mocking Egyptian god-kings. Read directly, that sentence rejects the pagan's own characterization of Christ as "a criminal" rather than straightforwardly denying his divinity — it declines to deny Christ's divinity rather than denying it outright, and this document adopts that reading consistently. This is a live, long-running scholarly question, not a settled feature of persuasive strategy that this document is entitled to explain away, and it is named as such rather than resolved (see §4 item 3 below).

**Lactantius requires a real, disclosed complication, not an unqualified clearance.** His biography is accurate — rhetor at Nicomedia, tutor to Constantine's son Crispus at Trier — but he does not clear without theological qualification. Jerome (*Ep.* 84.7, to Pammachius and Oceanus) charges that Lactantius denies the Spirit's substance. The vendored translation (`cic/texts/npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml`) reads: "Lactantius in his books and particularly in his letters to Demetrian altogether denies the subsistence of the Holy Spirit, and following the error of the Jews says that the passages in which he is spoken of refer to the Father or to the Son" — a charge against exactly Article 4's fifth commitment, "The Holy Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified together with the Father and the Son," quoted here in full rather than paraphrased — leveled at Lactantius' **books generally**, with the letters to Demetrianus named only as its sharpest instance, not its sole target. The letters to Demetrianus are genuinely lost and not among the vendored texts in `cic/texts/`, and that is worth disclosing — but the charge does not attach to them *rather than* to the *Divine Institutes*; Jerome's own very next sentence asks who can forbid him to read the *Institutes* "simply because this opinion of his is to be abhorred," confirming the charge reaches Lactantius' surviving, vendored work. The *Divine Institutes* also carries a chiliastic reading of the end times (Book VII) and, read directly, a two-spirits cosmology at II.8–9 — God producing a second being "in whom the disposition of the divine origin did not remain," who "passed from good to evil" — precisely the binitarian-with-a-fallen-second-spirit structure the ancient charge concerns, not a structure outside its reach. Doc_02's disclosure must say plainly that no primary text of the charge's sharpest instance (the lost letters) can be shown, while the charge itself is leveled at the corpus this candidate does hold. None of this is a rival-current divergence — it is the same kind of disclosed, bounded idiosyncrasy this document already grants Minucius Felix and Arnobius.

**Commodian and Arnobius each carry a real, disclosable complication, with Arnobius' correctly attributed:**

- **Commodian's date is genuinely disputed in the scholarship** — proposals range across roughly three centuries, and some of that range would place him well after this candidate's own 320 close, outside Era 1 entirely. This is a scope question, not a floor question, and this document does not conflate the two: A2 tests continuity for a figure who belongs to this window; whether Commodian belongs to this window at all is prior to that test, not a complication within it. Not resolved here; Doc_01 needs an actual, cited dating judgment before Commodian can be treated as belonging to this candidate at all.
- **Arnobius' theology carries real, disclosed idiosyncrasy, with its own biographical attribution stated carefully.** Arnobius did not himself write, anywhere in *Against the Heathen*, that he wrote it "by his own admission," "at his bishop's insistence," or that his freshly-converted, uncatechized status is established "on his own text's own evidence" — all three belong to Jerome's report alone, and *De viris illustribus* 79 itself states only that Arnobius was "a most successful teacher of rhetoric at Sicca in Africa during the reign of Diocletian" who "wrote volumes Against the nations which may be found everywhere" — no dream, no bishop, no refusal, no pledge. The dream-and-bishop account is the *Chronicle*'s alone (ad ann. 327, itself not among the vendored texts in `cic/texts/`, a disclosure symmetric with the lost Demetrianus letters named at §2 A2/§4 item 5 above): Arnobius' **bishop refused to admit him**, doubting a conversion prompted by a dream, and Arnobius wrote the books as a pledge to obtain baptism — the reverse of "at his bishop's insistence." Nothing in the *Adversus Nationes* itself describes its author's own biography this way. The census's own entry carries the appropriate hedge, "if Jerome is to be believed," which this document adopts. The theological content itself is accurately described: Book II argues the soul is of a *media qualitas*, not naturally immortal, receiving immortality as a gift through Christ — a genuinely idiosyncratic position, disclosed here rather than laundered into settled orthodoxy.

**Resolution: A2 clears**, with Minucius Felix's Christological absence, Lactantius' pneumatological and eschatological complications, Commodian's dating, Arnobius' anthropology (and its correctly attributed provenance), and Tertullian's own later New Prophecy alignment, all carried forward as binding, disclosed items in §4 — none smoothed over, and none, on the evidence available, disqualifying.

### B2 — Ecology

Strong on the same apologetic-argument register as I.35, in Latin rhetorical and legal idiom — Arnobius, Lactantius, and Tertullian are all trained rhetoricians, sharpened by Lactantius writing during and immediately after the empire's most severe persecution effort. Worship-practice texture, thin for the original four authors alone ("none of these four texts primarily describes Christian worship practice"), is substantially strengthened by Tertullian's own corpus: his *On Baptism*, *On Prayer*, and *On Repentance* are each a substantial, dedicated treatise on a specific practice — *On Baptism* is, on the corpus map's own note, "the earliest surviving treatise devoted to baptism" anywhere in the vendored library — and *On Fasting* and *On Modesty* add real texture on penitential discipline (the latter also a key early witness to the practice the Cyprianic and Donatist disputes later fought over, per the corpus map's own note, "noted here, not assigned there"). This is a genuine strengthening, not a cosmetic one, and Doc_02 should build the candidate's own worship-practice lens on this material rather than describe it as absent. Lay formation, women's voices, and enslaved persons' testimony remain essentially absent — Tertullian's own *On the Apparel of Women* and *To His Wife* address women as an object of disciplinary address, not as sources of their own voice, and do not change this — the same skew as I.35 and IJC.

**Passes B2**, with the worship-practice thinness corrected and the lay-voice thinness unchanged.
