## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Latin Apologists.

**Short Description:** Christians who defended their faith in Latin, between the Carthage of Tertullian and the court of Constantine. Tertullian of Carthage wrote the *Apology* in 197. Minucius Felix, a Roman advocate, set his dialogue the *Octavius* on the beach at Ostia (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:iv.iii). Arnobius taught rhetoric at Sicca in Africa under Diocletian (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lxxxi). Commodian wrote *Instructions* in acrostic verse (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:v.ii). Lactantius taught at Nicomedia and, in old age, tutored Crispus, a son of Constantine, in Gaul (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lxxxii). The *Passion of Perpetua and Felicitas*, a martyrs' record printed with Tertullian's works in the ANF volume and transmitted alongside them on the census's account, sits on the same shelf. The census window is c. 197–320.

**Core Identity:** A world of argument made in Latin. Its authors were trained speakers. They wrote to outsiders who held that Christians were atheists, criminals or the cause of the empire's troubles. They also wrote to insiders about heresy, discipline and the body. Arnobius states the charge he answers in one sentence: "if it should appear that to us are attributable causes by reason of which the universe has deviated from its laws" (Book I ch. 1, cic:anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml:xii.iii.i). Tertullian states what is worshipped: "The object of our worship is the One God, He who by His commanding word, His arranging wisdom, His mighty power, brought forth from nothing this entire mass of our world" (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:iv.iii.xvii).

**Distinctive Contribution:** The Latin vocabulary of Christian argument. The census's legacy field says that Tertullian "coined most of the technical words Western Christians still use for the Trinity and the sacraments". This document does not adopt that claim in the word "coined". The shelf can show a word in use. It cannot show first use. In the Latin of *Against Praxeas* the words are in use: *trinitatis*, *substantiae*, *personas* (cic:tertullian_opera-pars3-lat_kroymann-csel47.txt:line11686, the treatise opening). The world is also the one candidate of its batch where the same pages answer pagan critics and Christian rivals. Section 3 sets out what holds that together and what does not.

**Living Tradition Status:** The census records `living: false`. This document makes no Article 29 claim of its own and does not ask the project lead to confirm one.

**Naming of the candidate.** The census's informal name for this entry is "The Case Made in Latin". The Step 0 document §1 records an objection that stays live: five authors who are not contemporaries, spanning more than a century, are not obviously one community. §4 and §5 below treat that objection as unresolved.

## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** c. 197–320.

- **Beginning (197).** 197 is the date of Tertullian's *Apology*. Harnack (1904) puts it at the end of 197 (cic:harnack_geschichte-der-altchristlichen-litteratur-chronologie-band2-deu_1904.txt, the Tertullian table at printed pp. 295–296), and Step 0 §2 A5 calls it the best-anchored date in the roster. Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction, the tag Step 0 §4 item 8 gives to Tertullian's absolute years, resting here on Harnack alone. It is a fixed boundary of this world's window. It is not the opening of the Latin case. On Harnack's own table *Ad Martyras* (early 197) and *Ad Nationes* (summer or autumn 197) come before the *Apology*, and his note allows that some works he places in 198 to 202/3 may be from c. 195 to 197. On one of the two datings of the *Octavius*, that work is also earlier (Step 0 §4 item 1; below). Nothing in this document says that the *Apology* "opens" the Latin case.
- **What lies before the window.** The Greek apologists of the second century (Atlas I.35) are an earlier act of the same project, written in Greek. No Latin text on this shelf is shown to be earlier than 197 on the evidence read, except the *Octavius* on its earlier dating.
- **Ending (320).** 320 is the census's window close. The last datable works on the shelf are Lactantius's. Harnack places the *Epitome* after 313 and the move to Gaul at about 317, and Jerome says Lactantius tutored Crispus in Gaul (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lxxxii). No source read for this document shows an event that closes the world in 320. Doc_08's Ending cell must say so.

Correcting the census belongs to its owner. (b) The *Octavius* is dated as two Contested ranges, never one year. Pole A, c. 160–192, lies before Tertullian's *Apology* and outside the window. IX that the author was chief magistrate of Cirta rests on inscriptions that name a Caecilius Natalis, the namesake of the pagan speaker in the dialogue, and not the author.

**Geographic Centers:**

- **Carthage.** Tertullian's city: Jerome says he was from Carthage (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lv). Monceaux dates Cyprian's *Address to Demetrianus* to the plague at Carthage (cic:monceaux_histoire-litteraire-afrique-chretienne-tome2_1902.txt:line15403). The translator's note to the Passion says the more general opinion puts the martyrdom at Carthage, and that some think it was at Tuburbium (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:vi.vi.i). The place of the martyrdom is not settled on the shelf.
- **Rome and Ostia.** The setting of the *Octavius* (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:iv.iii). Jerome calls Minucius a distinguished advocate of Rome (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lx).
- **Sicca in Numidia.** Arnobius's city. Jerome places Sicca in Africa; the census places it in Numidia.
- **Nicomedia and Trier.** Lactantius at Diocletian's eastern court, and later in Gaul. The Step 0 document §3 B5 names the two capitals of the persecution and the settlement.

**Cultural Environment:** Latin rhetoric, law and Roman civic religion. The authors argue in the genres of the schools. Lactantius says Minucius Felix "was of no ignoble rank among pleaders" (Book V ch. 1, cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.v). The listeners are educated pagans, Christian communities, and, in the later authors, an empire turning from persecution to toleration. Greek was the older Christian language at Carthage on Harnack's argument that the church there had at first a considerable Greek element (printed p. 297). Harnack's table lists Greek as well as Latin versions of three Tertullian works (*The Shows*, *On Baptism* and *On the Veiling of Virgins*).

**Historical Pressures:**

- **The charge of causing disasters.** Arnobius answers it at the start of his first book (the quotation in §1). Cyprian's *Address to Demetrianus* answers the same accusation, in the plague years.
- **Persecution.** The Passion belongs to the persecution under Severus (7 March 202 or 203, Widely Accepted; Step 0 §4 item 9). The Great Persecution begins in 303 with edicts. Harnack reads Lactantius as giving up public teaching when the edicts came. The census also carries a documented story of the destruction of the church at Nicomedia on 23–24 February 303. This document does not rest on that story.
- **Heresy.** Tertullian's *Prescription Against Heretics*, the five books *Against Marcion*, *Against Hermogenes*, *Against the Valentinians* and *Against Praxeas* come to 278,083 of the 1,203,783 words Step 0 counts, about 23 percent. That count includes the editorial apparatus in the ANF text, and Step 0 does not claim a second independent run. The heresy treatises are the largest block within Tertullian's corpus. Lactantius's four works come to 285,187 on the same count (Step 0 §3 B1), so they are not the largest share of the whole.
- **Tertullian's break with the church.** Jerome reports that Tertullian "lapsed to the doctrine of Montanus, and mentions the new prophecy in many of his books" (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lv). Harnack puts the break at 207/8 and Step 0 §4 item 8 reports Monceaux at 213. The date is Contested.

**Historical Catalysts:** Tertullian's conversion and the *Apology*; the Severan persecution; the plague at Carthage; the edicts of 303; the toleration of 311; Lactantius at Constantine's court.

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** Rhetorical training is the common ground. Beyond that the shelf shows three practices. First, the school and the dialogue as ways to form a Christian mind (the *Octavius*, the *Divine Institutes*). Second, worship and discipline, in Tertullian's dedicated treatises on baptism, prayer, repentance, fasting, modesty, monogamy and the veiling of virgins. The Step 0 document §3 B2 treats this as a genuine strengthening of the world's worship texture. Third, martyrdom, in the Passion, *To the Martyrs* and *Scorpiace*. Lay formation, women's voices and the voices of the enslaved remain thin. The treatises addressed to women (*On the Apparel of Women*, *To His Wife*) address women as an object of discipline. They are not women's own voices. The Passion contains the visions of Perpetua and Saturus, which are their own writing (Widely Accepted; Step 0 §4 item 9).
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** These are candidates for testing. Each carries an Author Gravity risk, and the risk is large here. On Step 0's count, Tertullian's thirty-two works, the pseudonymous *Appendix* of poems included, are 731,618 of 1,203,783 counted words, about 61 percent. Without the *Appendix* (32,001 words) his thirty-one works are 699,617 words, about 58 percent. *Against Marcion* alone is 187,134 words, about 16 percent. The count includes the ANF apparatus and was made by one method (Step 0 §3 B1). Any gravity that rests on Tertullian alone is an author finding before it is a world finding.
  1. The one God against the many gods of Rome. Tertullian, Minucius, Arnobius and Lactantius all argue it. Risk: it is the common ground of every apologist, Greek or Latin.
  2. Christians as loyal subjects who are wrongly blamed. Arnobius, Cyprian and Lactantius argue it. Risk: Cyprian's *Address* is shared with another world.
  3. The rule of faith and the church's claim to Scripture against heretics. Almost wholly Tertullian. Risk: one author.
  4. Moral and bodily discipline. Tertullian's later treatises. Risk: he wrote the most rigorist of them in the New Prophecy period (§4).
  5. Endurance in persecution. The Passion, Tertullian's martyr treatises, Lactantius. Risk: the voices are few.
  6. Speaking Christian in Latin. The vocabulary question in §1. Risk: the shelf shows use and cannot show origin.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Worship: baptism, prayer and repentance as practised in Carthage, read from Tertullian. Authority: the church's rule of faith as Tertullian states it in *Against Praxeas*, and the bishop in the disputes that follow. Belonging: the martyrs' communion in the Passion. Interpretation: the argument from Scripture and from the prophets, at length in the *Divine Institutes*, Book IV. The evidence for belonging beyond the elite is thin.
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §7.

## 4. World Separation Criteria

The Framework asks when one world becomes another. The six questions are run below against three periods: Tertullian's years (197 to c. 222/3), the middle years (c. 223 to c. 295), and the years of the Great Persecution and the settlement (c. 295 to 320).

- **Has a new gravity emerged?** In the middle period the shelf is thin. It holds the *Octavius* if Pole B is right, Cyprian's *Address*, *On the Vanity of Idols* and, on the mainstream dating, Commodian. In the third period the argument to power becomes the main business: Arnobius and Lactantius address the educated class, and Lactantius writes during the persecution.
- **Has a major gravity disappeared?** The heresiological argument against named Christian rivals is Tertullian's. It does not continue on this shelf after him, apart from Lactantius's brief remarks. That is a change in the shelf's content. It is not shown to be a change in the tradition.
- **Has formation changed substantially?** Tertullian's disciplinary treatises have no counterpart in the later authors on this shelf.
- **Has worship changed substantially?** The shelf cannot say. Worship texts are Tertullian's.
- **Has authority changed substantially?** Not on the evidence read. Tertullian's last works attack a bishop's authority to forgive (Step 0 §2 A2). Only two works ascribed to Cyprian stand on this shelf, and both are shared with another world.
- **Has interpretation changed substantially?** No evidence read shows it.

**Finding.** The evidence supports a cluster of authors who share a language, a genre family and a persecution. It does not show a community of teachers and pupils. Step 0 §1 notes that the one asserted teacher-pupil link, Arnobius to Lactantius, rests on Jerome alone. *De viris illustribus* 80 says "a disciple of Arnobius" (cic:npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml:v.iii.lxxxii), and Letter 70 says "his pupil Lactantius" (cic:npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml:v.LXX). Tertullian is linked to none of the four. This document adds that the authors cite each other. Lactantius names Minucius Felix and Tertullian (cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.v), and *On the Vanity of Idols*, which is ascribed to Cyprian, copies the *Octavius* in chapters 1–9 and the *Apology* in chapters 10–15 on Harnack's report (cic:harnack_geschichte-der-altchristlichen-litteratur-chronologie-band2-deu_1904.txt, printed pp. 336–337). Those links show readership. They do not show one community. The objection from Step 0 §1 stands as live and unresolved.

## 5. Strand Determination

Article 21 defines a strand as "a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail." It adds that strand determination "is never assigned to satisfy an architectural preference for plurality". Strand is a finding that must answer to evidence. Article 21 also gives the default: "A world without established strands is treated as strand-singular throughout construction."

**Finding (the builder's, for the project lead to confirm or change).** The world is treated as strand-singular. This is a recommendation. It is not a ruling, and no later document should rest on it as settled until the project lead has confirmed it. Doc_04 tests it. The recommendation rests on Article 21's default, because the shelf shows no established second strand. It does not rest on proof that there is only one.

**The case for strand-singular.**

- One genre family. Every author on the shelf argues, and argues in Latin rhetoric.
- The same opponents recur. Roman religion is the target in Tertullian's *Apology* and *Ad Nationes*, in the *Octavius*, in Arnobius and in Lactantius.
- The same loyalty claim recurs. Christians are not the cause of disaster, and they pray for the empire.
- The authors read one another (§4).

**The case for two strands, at full strength.**

- *An outward strand and an inward strand.* The apologetic works address outsiders: the *Apology*, *Ad Nationes*, *To Scapula*, the *Octavius*, Arnobius, the *Divine Institutes*. The heresiological and disciplinary works address Christians: the *Prescription*, the five books *Against Marcion*, *Against Praxeas*, *On Modesty*, *On Fasting*, *On Monogamy*. The audience, the argument and the authority differ. In the inward works the authority is the church's rule of faith and, late in Tertullian's life, the Paraclete. In the outward works it is reason and the antiquity of the prophets.
- *Size.* The inward strand is the larger share of the counted corpus, because Tertullian wrote most of it.
- *Orientation.* The inward works treat other Christians as the opponent. Marcion and Praxeas are rivals inside the faith, screened at their own census entries (Step 0 §2 A5).

**What weighs against two strands.**

- Tertullian wrote both kinds. The *Apology* and the *Prescription* have one author, and the *Prescription* itself argues from the church's claim on Scripture, which is an apologetic move in its own way.
- Only one author supplies the inward strand on this shelf. A strand held by one man is an author, not a strand.
- The outward strand's later authors do not engage Christian rivals, but that is a limit of what survives.

**Why the recommendation is one strand.** The two patterns differ in audience more than in orientation, and the inward pattern is a single author's. Article 21's default applies unless a strand is established, and none is. If Doc_04 shows that the inward works carry a gravity the outward works do not share, it should reopen the finding. Confidence: Inferential-Thin. It rests on the shape of the shelf and on the default.

**What this document does not decide.** Whether two strands exist is not decided here. Nor is Representative identity, which belongs to Doc_10 and to the project lead.

## 6. Preliminary Forces Identification

Constitution Article 22 requires that a world be "situated against the forces that shaped it across both external and internal dimensions". The sketch below is a preliminary identification. Each cell carries its confidence and its source. The complete analysis is Doc_08.

**What generated this world:** the need to answer. The pagan charge that Christians cause disaster is stated by Arnobius at the opening of his first book (§1). In the *Octavius* the pagan speaker Caecilius makes the charge that Christians worship a crucified man (ANF ch. IX), and the Christian speaker Octavius answers it at ch. XXIX: "in that you attribute to our religion the worship of a criminal and his cross, you wander far from the neighbourhood of the truth" (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:iv.iii). Tertullian's *Apology* (197) and the Severan persecution belong to the same pressure.

**What larger world it was embedded in:** the Roman empire of the Severi, the third-century crisis and the tetrarchy. The church in Africa and the church at Rome. The schools of rhetoric.

**What it was responding to:** three things. First, the charge of atheism, disloyalty and crime. Second, persecution, from the Severan years to the edicts of 303. Third, rival Christian teachers: Marcion, Hermogenes, the Valentinians, Praxeas.

**What it was refusing:** the worship of the civic gods and of the emperor's image, and, in Tertullian, military and theatrical life. *On Idolatry*, *The Shows* and *The Chaplet* are the treatises. These refusals are Tertullian's. No other author on the shelf is shown to share all of them.

## 7. World Continuity & Distinction

**What this world inherits:**

- **Scripture and the Greek apologists.** The Greek apologists of the second century are an earlier act of the same project (Atlas I.35). The Step 0 document §3 B3 found no figure overlap between the two shelves.
- **Roman rhetoric and law.** The authors are trained speakers. The census says Tertullian was trained as an advocate; Jerome calls Minucius an advocate. This document uses the census's word and Jerome's, and no more.

**What this world transmits:**

- **To the Latin theological vocabulary.** The words appear in *Against Praxeas* (§1). Who first used them is outside what the shelf shows.
- **To the later imperial church.** Through Lactantius, whose pupil is Constantine's son, and whose *On the Deaths of the Persecutors* belongs to another world. Harnack holds that the work is Lactantius's and Brandt denied it, as Harnack reports (Step 0 §4 item 6).
- **To the Latin pastoral world.** Through the two works ascribed to Cyprian that both shelves hold.

**Continuity and change.** *What shared material functions differently here than in adjacent ecologies?* Tertullian's *Apology* and *Against Marcion* are on another world's shelf as outside corroboration (below). Here they are the world's own voice. *Where does continuity mask change?* The name "Tertullian" covers a presbyter of the church and, later, a writer in the New Prophecy. Two of his seven New Prophecy–associated works, *On Exhortation to Chastity* and *On the Veiling of Virgins*, are placed before the break on Harnack's table, and Step 0 §4 item 8 reports the same placement on the other two tables it cites. The shelf must not present them as post-break works. *Where does discontinuity mask continuity?* The step from the heresy treatises to the later apologists looks like a change of world. Lactantius's Book IV on Christ and the prophets covers ground that Tertullian's *Apology* ch. XXI also covers. Whether Lactantius continues Tertullian's argument is the builder's reading, tagged Inferential-Thin, and neither author cites the other on that point in the passages read.

**Distinctness from adjacent worlds** (checked directly in Step 0 §3 B3 and §4 item 7, and the built worlds' records searched again for this document; the search method is Step 0 §4 item 7's):

- **Post-Apostolic House-Church Christianity (built).** Holds two Tertullian works, the *Apology* and *Against Marcion*, as outside corroboration. Step 0 found its records need no change. The search found one further use: `pahc.source.lucian-peregrinus` triangulates with Tertullian's *Apology* 39 on the communal fund. That is the same work PAHC already cites as outside corroboration. One PAHC record also uses the wording Step 0 §4 item 1, rule (a), does not adopt: `pahc.force.systematic-theological-mode-elsewhere` says that the *Apology* opens a body of confident Latin defense (its `description` field). It is carried as a cross-world wording item (§9 item 11) and is not edited.
- **Latin Pastoral-Congregational Christianity (built, `records/lpc/`).** Shares Cyprian's *Address to Demetrianus* and *On the Vanity of Idols*, which is ascribed to Cyprian. Its Registry excludes Tertullian as a Named Comparandum, but its records now cite his *On Modesty*, chapter XXII, as comparandum evidence: the source record `lpc.source.tertullian-de-pudicitia` (register `etic`, `evidentiary_weight: corroborating`) is cited by `lpc.force.decian-persecution-libelli-system` and `lpc.gravity.confessor-authority-vs-episcopal-peace` for the sentence that the confessors' claim to grant peace was not new in the Decian crisis. *On Modesty* is a work on this shelf, co-assigned to Montanism, and the identity of the bishop it attacks is Contested (Callistus or Zephyrinus). LPC's `lpc.force.inherited-latin-theological-vocabulary` also names the Latin vocabulary that Tertullian is credited with forging. LPC's records also use Monceaux's first volume, which this shelf assigns as scholarship. No LPC record is edited here. The shared use of *On Modesty* XXII is carried as a cross-world question (§9 item 11).
- **Imperial-Juridical Christianity (built).** Holds Lactantius's *De mortibus persecutorum*, which the corpus map splits from this shelf. The work's authorship is disputed: Harnack holds that it is Lactantius's and Brandt denied it, as Harnack reports (Step 0 §4 item 6). IJC's records are not edited here.
- **The Roman Church in the Third Century (I.33, unbuilt).** Its census story for Callistus lists Tertullian's *On Modesty* as a second hostile witness to the edict on readmitting adulterers, usually taken to be Callistus's. The identity of that bishop is Contested (Callistus or Zephyrinus). I.33's census entry does not mention Minucius Felix.
- **Montanism (I.25).** Seven of Tertullian's works carry a co-assignment there. The census's own floor note says the New Prophecy shared the churches' doctrine and differed on authority and discipline.

## 8. The Article 4 check (Step 0 §2 A2 and §4 item 3)

Step 0 cleared Section A for all five core figures, with a complication disclosed for each. This section extends the check to the vendored text of each author's own voice. The five commitments are quoted from Constitution V2.3, Article 4, in the same words as the Step 0 header. Article 4 also says: "A movement need not have recited the Creed, formally adopted it, or existed at a date when doing so was possible." So the test is plain sense. It asks whether a commitment is affirmed. It does not ask whether the Nicene words appear.

**1.** "One God, the Father, the Almighty, maker of heaven and earth, of all that is, seen and unseen."
- *Shown.* Tertullian: "The object of our worship is the One God, He who by His commanding word, His arranging wisdom, His mighty power, brought forth from nothing this entire mass of our world" (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:iv.iii.xvii). Arnobius: "we who worship God the Father of all things" (Book I ch. 28, cic:anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml:xii.iii.i).
- *Shown in substance.* Commodian speaks of "the Almighty" in the *Instructiones* (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:v.ii.xliii). Minucius Felix argues for one God and declines to call him Father: "If I were to call Him Father, you would judge Him to be earthly" (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:iv.iii.xviii). "Almighty" does not occur in his *Octavius* on a count of the text with notes removed.

**2.** "Jesus Christ as the only Son of God, eternally begotten of the Father, God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten not made, of one Being with the Father."
- *Shown.* Tertullian: "Thus Christ is Spirit of Spirit, and God of God, as light of light is kindled." (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:iv.iii.xxi). Lactantius: "He became both the Son of God through the Spirit, and the Son of man through the flesh" (Book IV ch. XIII, cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.iv).
- *Partly shown.* Arnobius: "Christ alone has had given into His charge and entrusted to Him by God the Father" (Book II ch. 65, cic:anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml:xii.iii.ii). He also answers an objector who asks why Christ, if God, appeared in human shape (Book I ch. 60, cic:anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml:xii.iii.i). "Begotten not made" and "of one Being" are not found in his words. Lactantius also says that God produced a first spirit like himself and then made another being, "in whom the disposition of the divine origin did not remain" (Book II ch. IX, cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.ii). Step 0 §2 A2 ties this passage to the floor question. It stands in Brandt's constituted text (Step 0 §4 item 5). Only the fuller dualist additions in some manuscripts are manuscript-only. Whether it bears on the second commitment is not settled here. Step 0 §2 A2 also ties it to Jerome's charge about the Spirit, which is the fifth commitment (below).
- *Not shown in the author's own prose.* Minucius Felix. The *Octavius* has no occurrence of "Jesus", "Son of God", or incarnation language. "Christ" occurs twice and "crucifi-" twice, all four in ANF apparatus. Two independent counts agree: a count of the text elements of the division with the editorial notes removed, and a count over the raw XML of the division, read hit by hit. The raw count also finds the two headings a second time in their attributes, and the footnotes, so the hit-by-hit reading is what gives four. The four are the ch. XXXVII *Argument* heading, an editorial footnote citing Athenagoras, the ch. IX *Argument* heading, and an editorial gloss at ch. XXIX. Minucius's own prose has none. The one place the text meets the charge directly is ch. XXIX: "in that you attribute to our religion the worship of a criminal and his cross, you wander far from the neighbourhood of the truth" (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:iv.iii.xxix). That sentence rejects the pagan's characterization. It does not state a Christology. The clearance for Minucius is an argument from silence and genre, as Step 0 §2 A2 says.
- *Commodian.* "The Almighty Christ descends to His elect" (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:v.ii.xliii). Commodian's *Instructiones* use "Christ" 42 times and "Jesus" never, on a count of the text with notes removed. No sentence in them is read here as stating begottenness.

**3.** "Jesus Christ as truly human — incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, 'became truly human.'"
- *Shown.* Tertullian: "descending into a certain virgin, and made flesh in her womb, is in His birth God and man united" (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:iv.iii.xxi). In *Against Praxeas*: "Him we believe to have been sent by the Father into the Virgin, and to have been born of her" (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:v.ix.ii). Lactantius names the Holy Spirit as the one who filled the prophets (Book IV ch. XI, cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.iv), and then: "Therefore the Holy Spirit of God, descending from heaven, chose the holy Virgin, that He might enter into her womb." (Book IV ch. XII, cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.iv).
- *Not shown.* Minucius Felix and Commodian. Arnobius is silent on the Virgin's conception in the passages read.

**4.** "Christ's death under Pontius Pilate, burial, bodily resurrection on the third day, ascension, and his return in glory to judge the living and the dead."
- *Shown.* Tertullian: Pilate and crucifixion, the empty tomb on the third day, ascent "He was encompassed with a cloud and taken up to heaven", and the return: "is he not rather up in the heavens, thence about to come again" (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:iv.iii.xxi; cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:iv.iii.xxiii). Lactantius: "they took and brought Him before Pontius Pilate" (Book IV ch. XVIII, cic:anf07_lactantius-apostolic-constitutions-didache-liturgies.xml:iii.ii.iv), the third day (Book IV ch. XIX, same locus), and "when He shall come again with majesty and glory to judge every soul" (Book IV ch. XII, same locus).
- *Partly shown.* Arnobius says Christians worship one who died an ignominious death (Book I ch. 41, cic:anf06_gregory-thaumaturgus-dionysius-julius-africanus-methodius-arnobius.xml:xii.iii.i). "Pilate" does not occur in his seven books. Commodian: "If thou hast not adored the crucifixion of the Lord, thou hast perished." (cic:anf04_tertullian4-minucius-felix-commodian-origen1-2.xml:v.ii.xxxiii).

**5.** "The Holy Spirit as Lord and giver of life, worshiped and glorified together with the Father and the Son."
- *Shown.* Tertullian: "three Persons—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost: three, however, not in condition, but in degree; not in substance, but in form" (cic:anf03_tertullian.xml:v.ix.ii). That is the strongest statement on the shelf.
- *Shown in part, and contested for one author.* Lactantius names the Holy Spirit as the one who filled the prophets and who conceived Christ in the Virgin (the Book IV passages in item 3). Jerome charges that "Lactantius in his books and particularly in his letters to Demetrian altogether denies the subsistence of the Holy Spirit" (cic:npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml:v.LXXXIV). The letters are lost, and the charge reaches the *Divine Institutes*. The Book II passage on a second being (item 2 above) is the structure Step 0 §2 A2 ties to that charge. The check here does not settle it. Whether Lactantius states the Spirit as worshipped with the Father and the Son is Contested between Jerome and the passages above.
- *Not shown.* Minucius Felix, Arnobius and Commodian. "Holy Spirit" and "Holy Ghost" do not occur in Arnobius's seven books or in the *Octavius* or the *Instructiones*, on a count of the text with notes removed.

**The other voices.** The Passion of Perpetua is a martyrs' record. It was not read for this check. Cyprian's *Address* and the ascribed *On the Vanity of Idols* are on the shelf of another world and were checked there. The Tertullian works associated with the New Prophecy were not read clause by clause here. The census's Montanism entry says the New Prophecy shared the churches' doctrine.

**Result.** Nothing on the shelf denies any of the five commitments. Tertullian affirms all five in the passages read. Lactantius affirms the first four in the passages read and the Spirit's part in the incarnation, with the bearing of the Book II passage on the second and fifth commitments unsettled, and Jerome's charge on the fifth unsettled. Arnobius affirms Christ's divinity in substance and is silent on the Spirit. Minucius Felix and Commodian are silent on most clauses. Silence is not denial. The floor holds on the evidence read, at the strength stated clause by clause. It is not a completed check of every work, because the Montanism-period treatises and the Passion were not read for it.
