# Source Registry — The Hussite and Bohemian Brethren Movement

Per `Source_Registry_Template.md` V1.0. Co-equal Step 2 output with `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md`. **Living document: append-only.** A row is never renumbered or deleted. Doc_02 may not name a source in support of a specific claim unless that source has a row here. Rows are entered only for works that the corpus map assigns to this world, and for works that are not vendored. A vendored work assigned only to another world is recorded as a candidate in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (section E), not here. Numbers 25 to 28 are not used, and they are not reused. A row whose work has since been vendored is marked superseded, carries no Confidence letter, and points to the rows that replace it.

**Status:** In revision, awaiting independent review. It is not approved to proceed until a review clears it. The saturation statement at the end is not closed (see "Saturation statement").
**Boundary checked against:** `Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md` (c. 1402–1517; Bohemia and Moravia; three strands: Utraquist, Taborite, Unity). Boundary Status is judged by what a source speaks for, never by the publication date of a modern edition.

**Type:** P (Primary) / S (Secondary) / M (Material/External) / L (Period Lexicon).
**Confidence (citation reliability):** A = the Licensed-For passages were read and matched in the vendored file by a structure marker (letter number, chapter heading, running-head page, or chapter title). For an Excluded row, A means that the passage which makes it a Named Comparandum was read and matched. B = a specific work or locus is named and exists in the vendored file, but the Licensed-For content was not itself re-read. C = a real author and work, no locus checked, not vendored. D = a body or genre of material, no single text named. No row is E; a source with no traceable basis is not entered.
**Structure markers:** line numbers are lines of the vendored file in `cic/texts/`. "Letter" numbers are Workman & Pope's own roman numerals. "p." numbers for *De Ecclesia* are read from the running heads and were checked against the printed chapter headings.
**Scan-quality note:** all vendored files are OCR text and carry OCR errors. Quotations in this Registry and in Doc_02 were matched against the files after whitespace and line-break-hyphen normalisation only. No OCR correction was applied to any quoted word, and a Latin or Czech quotation is given as the file prints it, misreads included.
**Original-language rows and witness status:** a work in Latin, Czech or German is primary evidence when its scan is clean (`cic/texts/INTAKE.md`, the ruling of 2026-09-25). An A row for such a work means the passage was read in the vendored file and matched there by a structure marker (letter or document number, running head, chapter heading). No page image was available to the builder, so a quotation from any of these rows must still be checked against the printed page before a quote record is written. Each row's Verification Note opens with its **Witness**. *First* means a legible original that can be quoted once the page image is checked. *Second* means a scan whose long s, misread letters or interleaved notes make its wording unreliable. A second witness supports what a passage says in outline and is not quoted.
**Rights:** each vendored row states its rights basis from the file's own header. For a work that is not vendored, the row states the basis as far as the builder knows it. The Library must verify it before any acquisition.

**The corpus, counted two ways.** Character counts use `LC_ALL=C.UTF-8`; under the shell's default POSIX locale `wc -m` counts bytes and gives the wrong figure. The corpus map assigns 24 works to this world in 23 files (Brown's *Fasciculus* carries two entries, one as context and one as tradition). Method 1 is Python `len()` on each file decoded as UTF-8. Method 2 is `wc -m` under `LC_ALL=C.UTF-8` on the same 23 files, with `wc -c` for bytes. Both give **35,341,097 characters (36,047,134 bytes)**. Three files are assigned whole but concern this world only in part: Foxe vol. III (3.99M characters) counts for lines 30742–46807 (1,018,510 characters), Van Braght (6.60M) for lines 47370–47660 (10,416) and Brown's *Fasciculus* (3.07M) for lines 17311–19520 (162,022). Both methods agree on each of those three figures. Counting only those parts, the corpus is **22,880,482 characters**. The tradition role holds 12,852,753 of them (56 per cent). Of those, 8,919,310 (39 per cent of the corpus) are in eight files that the corpus map assigns as quotable after a page check: Hus's *Letters* and *De Ecclesia* in English, Novotný, Palacký's *Documenta*, Erben's three volumes and Chelčický. The other 3,933,443 are second-witness or attributed: Březová, the Brethren's Latin items and the *Tractatus responsivus*. Hus's two English works together are 1,408,217 characters (6 per cent). Palacký's and Novotný's volumes include editors' prefaces and documents by other hands, so tradition role does not mean Hus's own words throughout. The Library's `REGISTRY.yaml` lists 396 text files for the whole shelf, which matches the 396 `.txt` and `.xml` files in the directory (358 and 38; 403 entries in all, with seven files of metadata, the index database and the intake folder).

**The shelf search.** All searches were run on the 396 files, on whitespace-normalised text with Python regular expressions and again with `grep -l` line by line. A whole-word, case-sensitive search for "Hus" or "Huss" matches 73 files by both methods; all 23 assigned files are among them and 50 are not assigned to this world. A case-insensitive search matches 148 by both methods, and the extra matches were not read. Many matches are false ones, such as the word "Hus" in Goodspeed's apparatus to the Greek apologists and in Canisius's English "Hus much haue we saide". A search for Hussite, Utraquist, Calixtine or Taborite matches 25 files by both methods, 15 assigned here and 10 not. A search for "Bohemian Brethren", "Unity of the Brethren", "Unitas Fratrum" or "Bohemian Confession" matches 12 files across line breaks and 7 line by line. Of the 12, the real ones are the assigned Seifferth, Comenius, Brown's *Fasciculus*, Gillett, Lützow's *Bohemia* and *Life & Times*, and Van Braght, and, in files assigned elsewhere, Luther's *Works* vols. 2 and 3, Vedder on Hubmaier, Wyclif's *Polemical Works* vol. 2 (in a preface) and Zwingli's *Latin Works* vol. 3. Files with real Hus or Hussite material beyond the assigned works are all assigned to other worlds: the Loserth introduction, Buddensieg's *Polemical Works* vol. 2 and *De veritate* and the *Fasciculi Zizaniorum* (Lollardy); Luther's *Works* vols. 2 and 3, the Wace–Buchheim volume and the Bacon–Allen hymns (Lutheran Wittenberg); Vedder on Hubmaier and Menno Simons's *Works* (Anabaptist movements); Kempis (Devotio Moderna); Sarpi and Waterworth's *Canons and Decrees of Trent* (the Tridentine church); Polanco's *Chronicon*, Lainez's letters and Canisius (the Society of Jesus, after the window); and Calvin's *Letters* vol. 4 and Zwingli's *Latin Works* (Reformed cities). They are recorded as candidates in `Open_Gaps_Tracking.md` (section E and entry 27), and none is a row here. Only the first two contexts of each were read.
