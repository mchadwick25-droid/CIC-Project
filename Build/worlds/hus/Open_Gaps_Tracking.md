## Section B — Source requests to the Library thread (no source has been fetched)

A garbled scan stays a second witness. A clean public-domain original in Latin or Czech counts as primary evidence under the 2026-09-25 intake ruling. The items below are leads from the builder's own knowledge and from the bibliographic footnotes in the vendored apparatus. **None has been rights-checked or verified to exist as a public-domain scan; the Library thread should verify each.** Priority order.

- **R1. Assign Foxe, *Acts and Monuments*, Vol. III to this world** (`foxe_acts-and-monuments-v3_cattley-townsend1837.txt`), role `context` (or as the Library decides). Already vendored; assigned only to Lollardy. Its contents list (lines 462–560) and body show about a hundred pages on Hus: his public notices, the testimonial of the papal inquisitor, the nobles' and the university's petitions, his answer to 26 articles on *De Ecclesia*, his appeal, and (pp. 58–88) his testimony about Wyclif's articles. Registry row 25. This is the quickest gain: it needs assignment, not acquisition.
- **R2. The Latin original of *De Ecclesia*.** Schaff's translation was made from the *Historia et Monumenta J. Hus* (Frankfurt, 1715, a reprint of the 1558 edition), which Schaff says he compared paragraph by paragraph. A scholarly edition by Flajšhans is also named in Schaff's apparatus. Lead type: builder prior knowledge plus Schaff.
- **R3. Hus's Czech works and the Latin and Czech originals of the *Letters*.** Leads from builder knowledge: Erben's edition of Hus's Czech writings (19th century); Novotný's *Korespondence* of Hus (1920); the letters printed in Palacký's *Documenta* (below). These would let the Czech-language letters be quoted in the language Hus wrote them.
- **R4. Palacký, *Documenta Mag. Joannis Hus* (1869)**, cited by Lützow (*Hussite Wars*, footnote near the archbishop's letter). It prints the Constance documents and, on the builder's knowledge, the Latin *Relatio* of Peter of Mladoňovice. Registry row 30.
- **R5. Lawrence of Březová's chronicle of the Hussite wars** (a critical Latin edition of the 19th or early 20th century), cited by Lützow. He is the main source for Tábor and the Adamites, and hostile to Tábor. Registry row 34.
- **R6. The Four Articles of Prague and the Taborite articles in Latin or Czech.** Leads from builder knowledge: the documents printed in Palacký's *Urkundliche Beiträge* (1873) and in the *Archiv český*. Gillett prints the Calixtine articles and describes the Taborite twelve in English only. Registry row 31.
- **R7. Unity of the Brethren texts from 1457 to 1517.** Named in the vendored Seifferth Introduction: the Brethren's 1504 Confession to King Vladislav and their 1508 letters to Dr. Augustine, said to be first printed in the *Fasciculus rerum expetendarum et fugiendarum* (Cologne, 1535; the title is legible in the scan at line 8268 of the file). From the census and builder knowledge: Petr Chelčický's *Síť víry* (Czech), the Unity's Czech hymnbook of 1501 or 1505, and Goll's *Quellen zur Geschichte der Böhmischen Brüder* (1878–82), cited by Lützow. Registry rows 32, 33, 35.
- **R8. A cleaner witness of Piccolomini's *Historia Bohemica*.** A second printing or edition whose scan does not interleave the marginal notes. See the Piccolomini scan and file header entry of 2026-09-30.
- **R9. Later Unity histories, lower priority** (all named in the Seifferth Introduction and post-window in date): Comenius, *Historia Fratrum Bohemorum*; Camerarius, *Historica narratio*; Lasitius on the Brethren's discipline. They would give the Unity's later account of the window at first hand.
