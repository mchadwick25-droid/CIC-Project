## Section A — Gaps and open questions

1. **The Unity's in-window voice is almost absent (Step 0 §4 item 2), 2026-09-30.** No confession, catechism, complete letter of Gregory the Patriarch, discipline ordinance or hymnbook by the Unity from 1457 to 1517 is on the shelf. The Seifferth volume quotes two short passages of the Brethren's 1508 letters to Dr. Augustine (Registry row 43). They are fragments, at two removes or more, and they must not be read as a summary of the 1504 or 1508 documents. The only other Unity text vendored is the *Ratio Disciplinae* (1632/33), outside the window and Excluded as a Named Comparandum (Registry row 24). Any claim about the Unity before 1517 rests on these fragments or on later or secondary voices. Requests R7 and R9 below. Owner: Library thread for acquisition; `hus` build for disposition.

2. **The Taborite strand has no voice of its own, 2026-09-30.** It is known only from a hostile Italian chronicler, Utraquist chroniclers seen through 19th-century historians, and Gillett's summary of the Taborite articles. Lützow says of the chronicler Březová that his "hatred of the Taborites was even intenser than his hatred of the adherents of the Church of Rome". Gillett's account of the assemblies is, in his words, "the Calixtine narrative". Doc_01 §5 names it a strand on the evidence of distinct practice and authority. Doc_04 must decide whether it can carry a gravity. Requests R5 and R6. A vendored 1660 Anabaptist martyrology prints what it calls a Taborite confession of 1431 (candidate E2 below).

3. **Piccolomini's scan and its file header, 2026-09-30.** The corpus-map note says the scan "is clean, not flagged as garbled under the Library's own OCR-quality rule". ## Section B — Source requests to the Library thread (no source has been fetched)

A garbled scan stays a second witness. A clean public-domain original in Latin or Czech counts as primary evidence under the 2026-09-25 intake ruling. The items below are leads from the builder's own knowledge and from the bibliographic footnotes in the vendored apparatus. **None has been rights-checked or verified to exist as a public-domain scan; the Library thread should verify each.** Priority order.

- **R1. Not used.** The Foxe volume that R1 once named is vendored and assigned to another world. It is recorded as candidate E1 in section E.
- **R2. The Latin original of *De Ecclesia*.** Schaff's translation was made from the *Historia et Monumenta J. Hus* (Frankfurt, 1715, a reprint of the edition of 1558), which Schaff says he compared paragraph by paragraph. Schaff says the earlier edition was printed at Frankfurt; a note in Loserth's Wyclif volume says Nuremberg. Schaff does not name a critical edition. He hopes that Dr. Flajšhans "will add to his other editions of Huss's writings a new edition of this" (Schaff lines 2265–2267), so none existed in 1915. Lead type: Schaff's own apparatus. Registry row 48.
- **R3. Hus's Czech works and the Latin and Czech originals of the *Letters*.** Leads: editions by Erben (named by Workman; Registry row 50); Novotný's *Korespondence* of Hus (1920; from builder knowledge; row 51); the letters printed in Palacký's *Documenta* (below). These would let the Czech-language letters be quoted in the language Hus wrote them.
- **R4. Palacký, *Documenta Mag. Joannis Hus* (1869)**, cited by Lützow (*Hussite Wars*, footnote near the archbishop's letter; many places in *Life & Times*). It prints the Constance documents and, on the builder's knowledge, the Latin *Relatio* of Peter of Mladoňovice. Registry rows 30 and 49.
- **R5. Lawrence of Březová's chronicle of the Hussite wars** (*Historia Hussitica*, ed. Goll, *Fontes rerum Bohemicarum* V, as Lützow's bibliography lists it), cited by Lützow. He is the main source for Tábor and the Adamites, and hostile to Tábor. Registry row 34.
- **R6. The Four Articles of Prague and the Taborite articles in Latin or Czech.** Leads from builder knowledge: the documents printed in Palacký's *Urkundliche Beiträge* (a title in Lützow's bibliography) and in the *Archiv český*. Gillett prints the Calixtine articles and describes the Taborite twelve in English only. Registry row 31.
- **R7. Unity of the Brethren texts from 1457 to 1517.** Named in the vendored Seifferth Introduction: the Brethren's 1504 Confession to King Vladislav and their 1508 letters to Dr. Augustine, said to be printed in the *Fasciculus rerum expetendarum et fugiendarum* of 1535 (the title is legible at line 8267 of the file, with one misread letter; the place of printing is garbled). From the census and builder knowledge: Petr Chelčický's *Síť víry* (Czech), the Unity's Czech hymnbook of 1501 or 1505, and Goll's *Quellen zur Geschichte der Böhmischen Brüder* (1878–82), cited by Lützow. Registry rows 32, 33, 35 and 43.
- **R8. A cleaner witness of Piccolomini's *Historia Bohemica*.** A second printing or edition whose scan does not interleave the marginal notes. See the Piccolomini scan and file header entry of 2026-09-30.
- **R9. Later Unity histories, lower priority** (all named in the Seifferth Introduction and post-window in date): Comenius, *Historia Fratrum Bohemorum*; Camerarius, *Historica narratio*; Lasitius on the Brethren's discipline. They would give the Unity's later account of the window at first hand.

## Section E — Candidates on other worlds' shelves (no Registry row; the project lead's authorisation is needed before any is placed in this world)

These vendored works are assigned to other worlds in the corpus map. Each holds Hus or Hussite material. None is evidence for this world, none has a Registry row, and Doc_02 does not rely on any of them. Placing one here (as a `context` work, or as an Excluded Named Comparandum) is a cross-world decision for the project lead.

- **E1. John Foxe, *Acts and Monuments*, Vol. III** (`foxe_acts-and-monuments-v3_cattley-townsend1837.txt`; assigned to Lollardy). The contents list (from about line 462) names a Hus section headed "The Entry of the memorable History of the Bohemians": Hus's coming to Constance and the process against him, the nobles' supplications, the University of Prague's testimonial, his answer to twenty-six articles taken from *De Ecclesia*, and his appeal. The testimonial of the papal inquisitor, Nicholas, bishop of Nazareth, says he found Hus "a faithful and a catholic man" (line 34383). The corpus-map note for Lollardy says the volume "was not checked for Hus/Bohemian content". Existence and structure were checked; the section was not read. It is a 16th-century Protestant martyrology (here in the 1837 edition) that presents Latin documents in English, a second witness and a partisan one.
- **E2. Van Braght, *Martyrs Mirror*** (`van-braght_martyrs-mirror_sohm1886.txt`; assigned to the Anabaptist movements). A Hus and Hussite section runs from about line 47370 to line 47660. It says Hus took from Wyclif the view that "it does not become a Christian to swear". It gives a notice of Hus's followers taken from Mehrning, and a note that the Hussites divided into "Praguers" and "Taborites" (from Lydius). It prints what it calls a confession of the Taborites of 1431, on baptism (from Mehrning and Lydius). It also carries a note that the name "Bohemian brethren" was sometimes given to the Hussites (lines 47774–47777). This is a 1660 Anabaptist appropriation of Hus into a Waldensian and non-swearing lineage, at three removes from any Taborite text. If authorised, it would be an Excluded Named Comparandum row, and a warning against reading its claims back into Hus.
- **E3. Loserth's Introduction to Wyclif's *De Ecclesia*** (`wyclif_de-ecclesia-lat_loserth1886.txt`; assigned to Lollardy). The apparatus discusses Hus's use of Wyclif (for example lines 136–142 and 653–676). It bears on the Hus–Wyclif question in Doc_01 §7. The Latin text itself is Wyclif's and is not this world's.
- **E4. Luther, the Address to the Christian Nobility and other passages** (`luther_first-principles-reformation_wace-buchheim1885.txt`; `luther_works-v2-selected_jacobs-spaeth1916.txt`; `luther_works-v3-selected_various1930.txt`; and the Bacon–Allen hymn volume `luther_hymns_bacon-allen.txt`; assigned to Luther worlds). Doc_01 §7 cites the Address as a later witness to the tradition's reception and mentions that Luther's hymn *Jesus Christus unser Heiland* is printed as "Improved" from a Communion Hymn of John Huss. The stanzas are Luther's revision and must not be given to Hus. If authorised, the hymn would be a Named Comparandum row and the Address an Out-of-Boundary row.
- **E5. Lower priority, passing mentions of Hus or the Brethren.** Vedder on Balthasar Hubmaier (Hus's influence on Hubmaier); Kempis, *Founders of the New Devotion* (the Devotio Moderna's response to Hus's condemnation); the *Fasciculi Zizaniorum* (Lollard material, Hus named in passing); Sarpi's *History of the Council of Trent* (the Calixtines); Zwingli, *Latin Works*, vol. 3 (Heller, 1929; the Bohemian Brethren, in the editor's note). None was read beyond the mentions.
