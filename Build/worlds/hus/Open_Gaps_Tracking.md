## Section A — Gaps and open questions

1. **The Unity's in-window voice is almost absent (Step 0 §4 item 2), 2026-09-30.** No confession, catechism, complete letter of Gregory the Patriarch, discipline ordinance or hymnbook by the Unity from 1457 to 1517 is on the shelf. The Seifferth volume quotes two short passages of the Brethren's 1508 letters to Dr. Augustine (Registry row 43). They are fragments, at two removes or more, and they must not be read as a summary of the 1504 or 1508 documents. The only other Unity text vendored is the *Ratio Disciplinae* (1632/33), outside the window and Excluded as a Named Comparandum (Registry row 24). Any claim about the Unity before 1517 rests on these fragments or on later or secondary voices. Requests R7 and R9 below. Owner: Library thread for acquisition; `hus` build for disposition.

2. **The Taborite strand has no voice of its own, 2026-09-30.** It is known only from a hostile Italian chronicler, Utraquist chroniclers seen through 19th-century historians, and Gillett's summary of the Taborite articles. Lützow says of the chronicler Březová that his "hatred of the Taborites was even intenser than his hatred of the adherents of the Church of Rome". Gillett's account of the assemblies is, in his words, "the Calixtine narrative". Doc_01 §5 names it a strand on the evidence of distinct practice and authority. Doc_04 must decide whether it can carry a gravity. Requests R5 and R6. A vendored 1660 Anabaptist martyrology prints what it calls a Taborite confession of 1431 (candidate E2 below).

3. **Piccolomini's scan and its file header, 2026-09-30.** The corpus-map note says the scan "is clean, not flagged as garbled under the Library's own OCR-quality rule". ## Section E — Candidates on other worlds' shelves (no Registry row; the project lead's authorisation is needed before any is placed in this world)

These vendored works are assigned to other worlds in the corpus map. Each holds Hus or Hussite material. None is evidence for this world, none has a Registry row, and Doc_02 does not rely on any of them. Placing one here (as a `context` work, or as an Excluded Named Comparandum) is a cross-world decision for the project lead.

- **E1. John Foxe, *Acts and Monuments*, Vol. III** (`foxe_acts-and-monuments-v3_cattley-townsend1837.txt`; assigned to Lollardy). The contents list (from about line 462) names a Hus section headed "The Entry of the memorable History of the Bohemians": Hus's coming to Constance and the process against him, the nobles' supplications, the University of Prague's testimonial, his answer to twenty-six articles taken from *De Ecclesia*, and his appeal. The testimonial of the papal inquisitor, Nicholas, bishop of Nazareth, says he found Hus "a faithful and a catholic man" (line 34383). The corpus-map note for Lollardy says the volume "was not checked for Hus/Bohemian content". Existence and structure were checked; the section was not read. It is a 16th-century Protestant martyrology (here in the 1837 edition) that presents Latin documents in English, a second witness and a partisan one.
- **E2. Van Braght, *Martyrs Mirror*** (`van-braght_martyrs-mirror_sohm1886.txt`; assigned to the Anabaptist movements). A Hus and Hussite section runs from about line 47370 to line 47660. It says Hus took from Wyclif the view that "it does not become a Christian to swear". It gives a notice of Hus's followers taken from Mehrning, and a note that the Hussites divided into "Praguers" and "Taborites" (from Lydius). It prints what it calls a confession of the Taborites of 1431, on baptism (from Mehrning and Lydius). It also carries a note that the name "Bohemian brethren" was sometimes given to the Hussites (lines 47774–47777). This is a 1660 Anabaptist appropriation of Hus into a Waldensian and non-swearing lineage, at three removes from any Taborite text. If authorised, it would be an Excluded Named Comparandum row, and a warning against reading its claims back into Hus.
- **E3. Loserth's Introduction to Wyclif's *De Ecclesia*** (`wyclif_de-ecclesia-lat_loserth1886.txt`; assigned to Lollardy). The apparatus discusses Hus's use of Wyclif (for example lines 136–142 and 653–676). It bears on the Hus–Wyclif question in Doc_01 §7. Its note at line 676 reads "Norimbergae" for the place of the 1558 *Historia et Monumenta*, where Schaff says Frankfurt (Registry row 48, R2); the disagreement is a lead for the Library once the file is authorised. The Latin text itself is Wyclif's and is not this world's.
- **E4. Luther, the Address to the Christian Nobility and other passages** (`luther_first-principles-reformation_wace-buchheim1885.txt`; `luther_works-v2-selected_jacobs-spaeth1916.txt`; `luther_works-v3-selected_various1930.txt`; and the Bacon–Allen hymn volume `luther_hymns_bacon-allen.txt`; assigned to Luther worlds). Doc_01 §7 cites the Address as a later witness to the tradition's reception and mentions that Luther's hymn *Jesus Christus unser Heiland* is printed as "Improved" from a Communion Hymn of John Huss. The stanzas are Luther's revision and must not be given to Hus. If authorised, the hymn would be a Named Comparandum row and the Address an Out-of-Boundary row.
- **E5. Lower priority, passing mentions of Hus or the Brethren.** Vedder on Balthasar Hubmaier (Hus's influence on Hubmaier); Kempis, *Founders of the New Devotion* (the Devotio Moderna's response to Hus's condemnation); the *Fasciculi Zizaniorum* (Lollard material, Hus named in passing); Sarpi's *History of the Council of Trent* (the Calixtines); Zwingli, *Latin Works*, vol. 3 (Heller, 1929; the Bohemian Brethren, in the editor's note). 29. **Workman's "conceived" against Mladoňovic's Latin, 2026-09-30.** Workman's English of the burning gives Hus's third clause as "Who was conceived of the Virgin Mary" (*Letters*, line 13349). Mladoňovic's Latin, as Palacký prints it from the Mladoňovic manuscript, reads *Qui natus es ex Maria virgine* (Registry row 56, line 17394). The Latin word means "born". No voice may give "conceived" as Hus's last words. Open: the Vienna manuscripts' variants, which Palacký records, were not collated, and Workman's source for his wording is not named in the passage. Owner: `hus` build.

30. **The cup letters and Hus's early interest in the cup, 2026-09-30.** Cross-refers to entry 10. Erben's note in vol. 3 (Registry row 63) called two letters on the cup spurious: the letter to Hawlik and the letter to Wenceslas of Duba and John of Chlum. Palacký (1869) and Novotný (1920) print both in Latin from manuscripts, among them the so-called Mladoňovic manuscript, and Novotný gives the Hawlik letter as no. 141 (rows 55, 58). Workman's "little interest" in the cup (row 3) is set against Novotný no. 95, a letter dated by the editor to November 1414 that urges communion in both kinds. Its date, addressee and, on Palacký's judgment, authorship are uncertain. Doc_04 must treat the cup gravity's Author Gravity risk with this in view. Open: read Novotný's introduction on no. 95; find Hus's tract *De sanguine Christi sub specie vini a laicis sumendo* (named in Novotný's note to no. 141), which Novotný cites as "Op I 42–44" (the printed *Opera*) and which is not on the shelf. Owner: `hus` build.

31. **The Four Articles: two Latin witnesses differ in order, and a clean edition is still wanted (R6), 2026-09-30.** A papal legate's reply of July 1420 quotes the four points in legible Latin, in the order cup, preaching, clergy, sins (Registry row 74, Palacký, *Urkundliche Beiträge* vol. 1, no. 34). Březová's copy, in Latin and Czech with proofs, has preaching, cup, clergy, sins, in a garbled scan (row 68). Lützow's order is preaching first. The wording was not collated. The Taborite twelve articles of August and the Taborite priests' articles are in Březová as a hostile chronicler gave them. Palacký's *Urkundliche Beiträge* gives only German summaries of the Taborite diet's declaration of 6 February 1426 and of other Taborite documents, with pointers to the *Archiv český*, which the Library did not find on archive.org (entry 26). Still wanted: the Articles as the Praguers issued them, and Taborite texts in their own voice. Owner: Library thread.

32. **The Brethren's Latin confession and the Augustine documents, 2026-09-30.** Cross-refers to entry 1. Brown's 1690 print (Registry row 70) gives the Brethren's Latin *Professio fidei* to King Vladislav and their *Excusatio*. Its contents list names the second item as the Brethren's reply against two letters of Dr. Augustine to the king. Seifferth calls the 1508 document letters to Dr. Augustine (row 43). The print's text carries no year, so 1504 against 1508 is not settled. Its headings call the Brethren *Fratres Waldenses*. The Latin of the second quotation of row 43 (the priests' manual labour) is in the *Excusatio*, and the first quotation was not located. Comenius's *Historia* (row 73) describes a drawing of twelve sealed slips in 1467, which is not shown to be the census's Lhotka lot story (row 39). Open: a clean witness of the confession; the 1535 printing is the earlier text. Owner: Library thread.

33. **Piccolomini's two printings, 2026-09-30.** Cross-refers to entry 3. The 1524 printing (Registry row 72) has no interleaved marginal notes and keeps the long s as ſ, but misreads many letters. On the passages compared, Poggio as the source of the burning narrative and the martyr's honours, it agrees in substance with the 1592 printing. Neither is clean. Whether the pair together meet the Library's rule for a clean witness is the Library's ruling. The 1592 file's header and the corpus-map note are unchanged. Owner: Library thread.
