## Held-Object Comparative Reasoning

Per the standing rule (Papnoute's water-jar correction; Fidelis's relic-rejection): no object tied to one named historical individual's own particular story is admissible for a composite Representative who speaks as "we" across a whole tradition, and no object may collide in silhouette with an object a live Representative already holds. Current inventory, checked directly rather than assumed: Chloe — a cup; Theon — a scroll; Yausep — a closed codex (the Diatessaune); Fidelis — cupped bare hands, no object; Papnoute — a jug, corrected to palm-frond rope-work; Marius — a document case; Albina — a wax tablet; Renatus (Gallic, pending) — a psalter, closed or half-open.

**Option A — RECOMMENDED: the single signed page of the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), held flat and open.** This is the one document this world's own record itself calls out as belonging to the whole tradition rather than to one man: "the single page our own ministers signed together... calling it nothing grander than a faithful record of what had actually been conferred" (Doc_05 §6.1; `rzg_Representative_Permanent_Prompt_Theophilus.txt`). It is the literal, physical object that embodies this Representative's own defining structural fact — one voice holding both strands — since it is the one document both the Church of Zurich and the Church of Geneva actually signed together. It is composite-safe by its own nature: multiple signatories, not one author's letter or one bishop's relic. It is also genuinely silhouette-distinct from every object above: a flat, unbound single sheet held open in both hands, showing plain handwriting and more than one block of signatures, is a different shape from a rolled scroll (Theon), a bound codex (Yausep) or psalter (Renatus), a cylindrical case (Marius), or a wax tablet (Albina).

**Option B — considered and set aside: an open Bible/Scripture volume.** Directly grounds G3 (Scripture as sole and sufficient authority), this world's own single deepest structural fact — a real, defensible case. Set aside for two reasons, not one: first, Fidelis's own comparative reasoning already found "open book vs. closed book" too weak a distinction to avoid colliding with an existing bound-book object (Yausep's codex) — the same problem would recur here against both Yausep's codex and Renatus's psalter, since all three are the same object-family (a bound volume) regardless of open/closed state. Second, an open Bible is the object almost any Christian-formation-world Representative could plausibly hold; it grounds this world's own core conviction but does not distinguish this world's own record from its neighbors the way the signed 1549 page does. Recommend against, not because it is ungrounded, but because a stronger, more distinctive, equally-grounded option exists.

**Decision needed from the project lead:** which option, before art is generated. The prompt below is built for Option A; if Option B is preferred instead, the object clause is the only paragraph that needs to change.

## Role, Dress, and Appearance — Grounding Notes

> ROLE (DOCUMENTED): a pastor — a minister of the Word — holding both the Zurich and Geneva strands as one voice, per the identity decision already made. Deliberately unlocated to either city, following the same discipline as Fidelis's "no diocese named" and Renatus's "no node named": no scene, no narrative attribute tied to one of the four named historical figures' own documented story (no Sixty-Seven Articles disputation-hall scene for Zwingli, no Institutes writing-desk for Calvin, no organ-silencing for Bullinger, no *Tabula* for Beza) — a plain bust portrait of an office, not an illustration of any one man's own episode.
>
> OBJECT (Option A, RECOMMENDED): the single signed page of the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), held flat and open at the chest in both hands — not rolled, not bound, not closed. See Held-Object Comparative Reasoning above for the full argument and the rejected alternative.
>
> DRESS: this world's own vendored record documents worship's own austerity in detail (Zurich's organ silenced, "no ornament unwarranted by Scripture" — Doc_05 §3) but does not itself describe clerical dress in the same detail. Step two of the standing three-step order therefore applies: the plain black preaching gown of a 16th–17th-century Reformed minister — the historically well-attested convention (the "Geneva gown") that grew directly out of the Reformed rejection of Catholic Mass vestments (chasuble, stole, alb) as unwarranted by Scripture — is time- and geography-bound to exactly this world's own setting and its own documented theme of refused ornament, and is proposed as INFERENCE from general Reformation-era historical convention, not from this world's own specific text. A plain white collar, unadorned — not an ornate ruff, and not the later "Geneva bands" convention, which is a 17th–19th-century Puritan/Presbyterian development that postdates this world's own core span and should not be assumed without further checking. No vestment, no stole, no clerical insignia of any kind — the same "nothing marking the office except bearing and the object in his hands" discipline Renatus's brief already applied to a bishop's own habit.
>
> APPEARANCE: this is not the same class of question Fidelis's, Papnoute's, or Yausep's briefs faced — those worlds required a genuine, disclosed ethnicity judgment because their own geography was contested or silent. This world's own geography is not silent or contested: Zurich and Geneva are, without dispute, Central and Western European cities, German-speaking and French-speaking respectively. A Central/Western European appearance is therefore a direct, geography-bound grounding, not a "defensible diversity" call requiring the same disclosure Papnoute's or Fidelis's did. Age and build remain SILENT in this world's own record for a composite figure, exactly as for every prior Representative — kept emblematic, INFERENCE flagged: a man in settled middle age, neither young nor elderly, matching this world's own formation ideal of "settled comfort... taught and handed to the next generation without drift" (`rzg_World_Profile.md` §3) rather than the fire of a young disputant. A modest, close-cropped beard is proposed as INFERENCE from general period portraiture convention for northern European scholarly/clerical men of this era (distinct from the clean-shaven late-antique Roman civic convention Fidelis's and Marius's briefs used for their own, much earlier spans) — flagged as a period-convention inference, not a documented fact about any specific figure.

## Ready-to-use Gemini prompt (Option A — Consensus Tigurinus page)

> A fine-art oil portrait, painterly and richly textured, of a Reformed pastor of 16th-century Switzerland, painted in the same style as a Rembrandt-period Old Master character study: visible canvas weave and grain throughout, fine cracquelure across the paint surface, warm layered brushwork rather than any photographic or digital smoothness. He is a man in settled middle age, of Central/Western European appearance, with a plain, unremarkable complexion, dark or greying hair worn short and unstyled with no side part of any kind, and a modest, close-cropped beard. His expression is warm and open, as though caught mid-conversation with someone he trusts, gently engaged rather than stern, solemn, or mid-speech with an open mouth. His head, shoulders, and body face directly forward, squared straight on to the viewer at eye level — not turned three-quarters or angled to either side — and he looks directly out of the canvas at the viewer, making direct eye contact, so the whole composition sits symmetrically on a single central point of perspective. He wears a plain black wool preaching gown, unadorned, over a simple shirt with a plain white collar — no ruff, no lace, no embroidery — and absolutely no clerical vestments of any kind: no stole, no chasuble, no alb, no ecclesiastical insignia, and no jewelry of any kind, no rings, no chains, no ornamented clasp. He holds a single sheet of paper, flat and fully open, in both hands at his chest — not rolled into a scroll, not bound into a book, and not folded shut — angled slightly so the viewer can see it is covered in plain handwriting with more than one distinct block of signatures near the bottom, though the text itself should read as generic period handwriting, not as any specific legible words. The background is a flat, plain, softly neutral dark tone with no scenery, no architecture, and no other figures, in keeping with an isolated character-study portrait. Compose the image as a wide horizontal landscape-oriented bust-up portrait, framed from roughly waist height up and centered directly on the figure, so both hands and the full sheet of paper are clearly and fully visible at the chest rather than only hinted at along the frame's edge — do not crop tightly to the face or shoulders alone.
