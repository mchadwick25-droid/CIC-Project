## 1. Formation World Identity

**World Name:** The Reformed Cities — Zurich & Geneva.

**Short Description:** The Swiss branch of the magisterial Reformation, beginning with Ulrich (Huldrych) Zwingli's reform preaching at Zurich's Grossmünster from January 1519, continuing through Heinrich Bullinger's four-decade Zurich pastorate (1531–1575) after Zwingli's death at the Battle of Kappel (fought during the Second War of Kappel, 11 October 1531), and joined — not founded — by John Calvin's Geneva ministry from 1536, consolidated under the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances. The two cities' own traditions are formally bridged by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), a doctrinal agreement on the Lord's Supper mediated by Bullinger's own Zurich and Calvin's Geneva — not evidence of prior contact between Zwingli and Calvin, who never met and whose ministries did not overlap (Zwingli died in 1531; Calvin's Geneva ministry began in earnest in 1536), a distinction this document states plainly per Step0 §4 item 1's own binding instruction. Window: 1519–1650, per the census's own already-decided continues-cap convention (Era 7 Freeze, Mark, 2026-08-02) — an administrative closing point for a still-living tradition, not a historical rupture (§2 below). **One world with two internal strands, not two separate worlds — confirmed by the project lead, 2026-09-15, per this document's own §4 reasoning and named options; record filed at `CiC_Reformed_Zurich_Geneva_Doc01_Scope_Confirmations_2026-09-15.md`.**

**Core Identity:** A world of civic, city-governed reform — church order remade by a city's own council and, at Geneva, by a Consistory of pastors and lay elders exercising disciplinary authority over the whole population's ordinary conduct — built on a theology in which predestination carries load-bearing doctrinal weight (Article 4 commitments (1)–(3), quoted in full in `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md` header, are directly verified against this world's own confessional texts per Step0 §2 A1; commitments (4)–(5) are affirmed by the same confessional documents but were not individually checked against the text in that pass, a distinction this document carries forward rather than upgrades) and in which the Lord's Supper is read as spiritual rather than corporeal — the doctrine that, once developed on both sides, separated the mature Reformed tradition from Wittenberg's own Lutheran Reformation at Marburg (1529, §6 below).

**Distinctive Contribution:** No other confirmed or in-build world in this project's portfolio reconstructs a Reformed presbyterian/consistorial church order in which lay elders, not only clergy, hold formal disciplinary authority over a whole city's ordinary conduct, week by week — the specific texture the census's own selection rationale for this world names directly (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`, this entry's `"why"` field: "one of the best ordinary-life archives in Christian history"). Set against this batch's own Lutheran Wittenberg (VI.1): both are magisterial Protestant reforms of a Latin-Christian civic and princely order, but Wittenberg's reform runs through a prince's territorial church and retains a corporeal real-presence Eucharistic theology, where this world's reform runs through republican city-councils and consistorial courts and holds a spiritual-presence or memorial Eucharistic theology. This world's own church-government vocabulary is Reformed and non-episcopal throughout: no figure in this world holds a bishop's *see*. Zwingli held the office of *Leutpriester* (people's priest) at the Grossmünster; Bullinger, his successor, held the title *Antistes*, Zurich's senior pastoral office answering to the city council — the two titles are not interchangeable and this document does not apply *Antistes* back onto Zwingli's own tenure. Geneva was governed by the Company of Pastors and the lay-inclusive Consistory; the Palatinate church at Heidelberg was a Reformed territorial church under its Elector. Abolishing episcopal jurisdiction is itself part of what this world reconstructs — the census's own `"legacy"` field names "church government by elected elders rather than by bishops" as part of what this world transmits.

**Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed, and squarely triggered rather than merely possible. Article 29 attaches "where a formation world’s reconstruction touches a tradition that continues into the present" — quoted here verbatim from `Build/reference/L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Constitution_V2_2.docx` (Article 29, whose internal text is V2.3 despite the filename). This world's own case is stronger than Donatism's PENDING finding (no documented descendant): the census's own `"legacy"` field names four traditions this map already carries as separate, not-yet-built entries — the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritanism. ## 2. Historical Boundaries

**Approximate Time Horizon:** 1519–1650 CE, adopted directly from the census's own already-decided window (`"start": 1519, "end": 1650`, Era 7 Freeze, Mark, 2026-08-02). This window differs in kind from Donatism's own 311/312–439: this world's ending point marks no comparable historical rupture — the census's own `"dateRationale"` field states plainly that 1650 is a "continues-cap" convention applied because this is a `"living": true` tradition, replacing an earlier 1600 boundary that "matched nothing." Doc_02 and Doc_08 should treat 1650 as an administrative construction-window close, not a historical ending event.

**What developments remain internal to this world's own window, and what indicate transition:** internal — the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), the Bolsec and Perrinist controversies, the Second Helvetic Confession and Heidelberg Catechism (1563, 1566). Transitional — the Synod of Dort (1618–19) is the one development inside this window whose own status as internal-to-this-world versus a hand-off to a neighboring, differently-scoped world required a real decision (§7 below), precisely because Dort marks the point at which the Reformed movement's own doctrinal self-definition becomes an international and specifically Dutch-political affair. **Confirmed answer (§7 below):** the international Reformed dimension of Dort (the Canons, this world's own delegate participation, the Beza throughline) remains internal to this world's own transmission; the specifically Dutch domestic controversy is the actual point of hand-off, to VI.26.

- **Beginning point (1519):** Zwingli's appointment as *Leutpriester* at the Grossmünster, Zurich, and his decision — beginning 1 January 1519 — to preach in continuous exposition through the Gospel of Matthew rather than follow the fixed lectionary.
- **Ending point (1650):** adopted from the census as an administrative, not historical-rupture, closing point. The Reformed pattern this world reconstructs continues unbroken past 1650 into the four descendant traditions named in §7 below, a finding Doc_08's own Ending/Transforming cell must record honestly, per Article 22's own instruction that "where a world's formation continues unbroken into a living tradition... that finding is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur" (Constitution V2.3 Article 22, quoted verbatim).

**Geographic Centers:**

- **Zurich:** Zwingli's and then Bullinger's own city, German-speaking, an independent city-state within the Swiss Confederacy; the site of the Prophezei (from 1525, a public communal exposition of scripture in the original languages, meeting on most weekdays rather than daily, that also produced the Zurich Bible, completed 1531) and of the First and Second Zurich Disputations (January and October 1523).
- **Geneva:** Calvin's city from 1536 (with a 1538–1541 interruption, below), French-speaking. Geneva's *combourgeoisie* alliance with Bern and Fribourg dates from 1526; the city's actual break from the Prince-Bishop of Geneva and the Duchy of Savoy dates from 1536 (the bishop had already left the city in 1533, and Savoyard authority ended with Bern's own 1536 military intervention and Vaud campaign, alongside Geneva's own 21 May 1536 reformation vote) — a decade after the alliance, not the same year. Geneva was not itself a Swiss Confederacy member in this period. The seat of the Consistory established under the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances and of the Genevan Academy Calvin founded in 1559.
- **Strasbourg:** not this world's own center, but the site of Calvin's 1538–1541 exile after his and Farel's expulsion from Geneva, under Martin Bucer's own pastoral influence.
- **The Palatinate (Heidelberg):** the territorial Reformed church associated with Zacharias Ursinus (traditionally, and per the corpus map's own attribution, joint author with Caspar Olevianus of the Heidelberg Catechism, 1563; current scholarship treats Olevianus's own share as disputed, with Ursinus generally read as principal author — carried here as the traditional attribution the corpus map itself uses, not independently adjudicated). Directly vendored and corpus-mapped (`cic/corpus-map/the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva.yaml`) as "a distinct pastoral/catechetical voice alongside Calvin's Geneva Catechism, from the Reformed tradition's German wing."
- **Bern:** not a center of original reform but the Confederacy's decisive early ally — the 1528 Bern Disputation brought Switzerland's most powerful canton into the Reformed camp.

**Cultural Environment:** Two distinct vernaculars inside one reforming movement — German-speaking Zurich (and Bern, and German Switzerland generally) against French-speaking Geneva, itself substantially populated by French religious refugees, of whom Calvin, a Frenchman by birth, was the most consequential. Both cities sit within the Swiss Confederacy's own decentralized cantonal political structure — sovereign city-councils rather than a single prince or territorial ruler — a real point of contrast with Wittenberg's own princely-patronage model, drawn here as this document's own comparative observation rather than located in a specific Step0 citation (Step0 §0 and §3 B4 discuss this world's general distinctness from Wittenberg but do not themselves name princely patronage, territorial church structure, or Eucharistic theology as the specific axis). Geneva became an international print and refugee center over this window — French Huguenots and, under Mary I, English Marian exiles.

**Historical Pressures:**
- The Swiss Confederacy's cantonal structure itself, which made reform a city-by-city political decision and also meant Reformed and Catholic *cantons* went to war directly over it — the Wars of Kappel.
- Catholic-canton military resistance: the First War of Kappel (1529, resolved without battle) and the Second War of Kappel (1531), in whose fighting Zwingli was killed (11 October 1531).
- Geneva's own political situation: the 1526 alliance with Bern and Fribourg, the 1536 break from Savoy and the Prince-Bishop, and recurring internal factional resistance to consistorial discipline (the Bolsec controversy over predestination, 1551; the Perrinist faction's resistance to the Consistory's independent excommunication authority, substantially resolved in Calvin's favor by 1555).
- Refugee inflow to Geneva that both stressed and internationalized the city's own institutions.
- The Counter-Reformation as an external pressure across the whole window, sharpest at Michael Servetus's 1553 execution for anti-Trinitarian teaching by Geneva's own council (**a separate, already-registered census entry, VI.14, "Floor Question (register)" — not re-litigated here; Calvin's own theology is not in question, his conduct toward a rival current is a different matter belonging to that document**, per Step0 §2 A1, quoted here in substance) and, at the window's far end, the Synod of Dort (1618–19), whose scope this document confirms in part as this world's own (§7 below).

**Historical Catalysts:** Zwingli's 1519 preaching start; the 1522 "Affair of the Sausages"; the First Zurich Disputation (January 1523, presenting Zwingli's Sixty-Seven Articles, now directly vendored and corpus-mapped) and Second Zurich Disputation (October 1523); the 21 January 1525 first believer's baptisms in Zurich, at Felix Manz's house — Conrad Grebel baptizing Georg Blaurock, who then baptized the others present, including Manz (Grebel and Manz were both formerly members of Zwingli's own reform circle; Blaurock, a former priest from Chur who joined Zurich's radical circles in 1524–25, is not established on the standard accounts as having been part of Zwingli's own circle specifically, and this document does not extend that claim to him — §7 below); the Bern Disputation (1528); the Marburg Colloquy (1529, §6, §7 below); the Wars of Kappel (1529, 1531) and Zwingli's death; Geneva's 1526 alliance with Bern and 1536 break from Savoy, and Calvin's arrival the same year; Calvin and Farel's 1538 expulsion from Geneva and Calvin's Strasbourg exile (1538–1541); Calvin's 1541 recall and the Ecclesiastical Ordinances; the Consensus Tigurinus (1549); Theodore Beza's *Tabula praedestinationis* (1555, §7 below); the Bolsec controversy (1551); Servetus's execution (1553); the Perrinist crisis's 1555 resolution; the Second Helvetic Confession (1566, Bullinger's own mature statement, directly vendored); the Heidelberg Catechism (1563); and, at the window's close, the Synod of Dort (1618–19, §7 below).

## 3. Distinct World Criteria

Applying the Framework's own four questions directly:

- **What recurring formation ecology exists?** A civic-reform ecology in which church order is remade through a city's own governing instruments — Zurich's council acting on the outcome of public disputations, Geneva's Consistory exercising direct weekly disciplinary oversight of ordinary conduct — joined to a theology in which the sovereignty of God in predestination and the rejection of any corporeal presence in the Supper are load-bearing commitments distinguishing this world from its Lutheran counterpart.
- **What recurring gravities exist (preliminary, subject to Doc_04)?** Provisionally: the sovereignty of God, expressed as predestination as a systematized, load-bearing doctrine (most fully in Calvin and Beza, present in seed form in Zwingli's own providential theology); a spiritual or memorial reading of Christ's presence in the Supper; scripture as the sole and sufficient authority for reforming both doctrine and civic order; church discipline as a formal, institutionalized function of the visible church. Named here as candidates for Step 4 testing.
- **What recurring patterns of worship, formation, interpretation, belonging, and authority exist?** Authority and belonging are the most developed in the vendored corpus so far. Worship shows a real, documented internal difference at this world's own outset: Zurich under Zwingli suppressed instrumental music and congregational singing as unscriptural (§5 below), while Geneva under Calvin and then Beza built congregational worship around the metrical Genevan Psalter (complete 1562) — neither the Psalter nor the 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances is itself yet vendored (§8 below), so this characterization draws on the census's own descriptive notes and general knowledge, not text-verified quotation. Interpretation and formation are strong in the Institutes and the catechisms but still asymmetric between the two cities (§8 below).
- **What distinguishes this world from adjacent worlds?** See §7 below.

## 4. World Separation Criteria

Part I of the Construction Framework requires this test — "When does one world become another?" — as its own named element, prior to Strand Determination. This section runs the Framework's own six named sub-questions honestly, including where the evidence cuts against a single-world reading, rather than reaching for the reading that keeps the world intact — the specific failure Round 1 review named and Round 2 review found recurring in this section's own first attempt.

- **Has a new gravity emerged (Geneva versus Zurich)?** No support found for one. Calvin's and Beza's own systematized double predestination is a development in degree and rigor of a conviction already present in seed form in Zwingli's own providential theology.
- **Has a major gravity disappeared?** No. Bullinger's Second Helvetic Confession (1566) affirms, on this document's own reading, the same core commitments the Sixty-Seven Articles state in 1523 — not independently re-verified against both vendored texts side by side this pass, flagged at Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence rather than certainty.
- **Has formation changed substantially?** **Yes, and this document does not minimize it here the way its own prior revision did.** The Prophezei's public scholarly exposition and the Genevan Catechism's fixed instruction backed by Consistory discipline are genuinely different formation logics, not merely different mechanisms serving one shared logic — §5 below reaches the identical finding, and this section no longer states the opposite of it. This is real, substantial separation-supporting evidence, weighed rather than explained away.
- **Has worship changed substantially?** Yes, sharply, at this world's own outset — Zurich's suppression of congregational song against Geneva's Psalter-centered practice is a real, documented divergence, not a minor stylistic variance. By most accounts this particular divergence did not remain permanent on the Zurich side — congregational psalm-singing was adopted across the Swiss Reformed churches generally, including Zurich, within this world's own window (not confirmed against a vendored primary source; Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence) — but a later convergence does not erase the reality of the founding-period divergence itself, which is live evidence on both sides of this document's own argument (§5 below carries the same divergence forward as strand evidence without needing to deny the later convergence).
- **Has authority changed substantially?** Yes, and this did **not** converge within the window on Zurich's own side: Zurich's council-led governance persisted throughout 1519–1650, while Geneva's Consistory is a genuinely distinct disciplinary institution Calvin fought to keep independent of civil-council control. The wider Reformed world's later convergence toward a Geneva-influenced consistorial pattern — in the French, Scottish, and Dutch Reformed churches this world transmits to (§7 below) — is a fact about this world's own descendants, not about Zurich and Geneva as they actually stood within this window, and this document does not treat it as answering this question for the two cities themselves.
- **Has interpretation changed substantially?** No evidence found of a divergent hermeneutic — both hold sola scriptura and the same humanist philological method both Zwingli and Calvin were trained in.

**A further consideration, not among the Framework's own six named questions but within its own non-exhaustive "Questions include:" framing, and the single strongest fact cutting against a single-world reading:** Zurich's and Geneva's reforms began independently, five years apart, with no contact between their initiating figures — Zwingli's 1519 Erasmian-humanist civic reform and Geneva's 1526/1536 political break from Savoy are two separate beginnings, connected only retrospectively by the 1549 Consensus Tigurinus. None of the six named questions directly tests for this, and this document does not let that omission stand as if the fact were absent from the record.

**Finding, offered as a disclosed close call in Revision 3 and confirmed by the project lead rather than self-resolved by this document:** two of six named tests (gravity emergence, gravity loss) plus interpretation clearly favor treating Zurich and Geneva as one world. Formation and authority both show real, substantial, and — for authority — permanently unconverged-within-window divergence that favors treating them as two separate worlds; worship diverged sharply at the outset, converging only later and only partially. Independent origins, five years and no contact apart, is real separation evidence no single one of the six named tests captures directly. Historical and theological convention treats Zwinglian/Bullingerian Zurich and Calvinist/Bezan Geneva as one "Reformed" confessional family rather than two — real corroboration for a single-world reading, but it describes how later scholarship classifies the outcome rather than settling whether this world's own separation test clears, and is named here as corroboration, not as the test's own result. This document's own tentative reading favored one world, on the strength of the gravity-continuity and interpretation findings plus the historical-convention corroboration; **the project lead confirmed this reading directly, 2026-09-15, in this build thread's own session, in response to the named options this section presented — record filed at `CiC_Reformed_Zurich_Geneva_Doc01_Scope_Confirmations_2026-09-15.md`, reproducing the options and trade-offs exactly as presented and the selection exactly as given.** (This document reads the six questions as diagnostic together rather than as a simple majority vote; the Framework itself states no aggregation rule for them — "Questions include:" and nothing further — so that reading is this document's own interpretive choice, disclosed as such rather than attributed to the Framework.)

**Confirmed architecture: one world, two strands.** §5 below applies Article 21 on this now-settled premise, not a provisional one.

## 5. Strand Determination

Per Constitution Article 21: strand is "a meaningfully distinct pattern of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world, not merely a variation in detail" — a finding accountable to evidence. Step 0 flagged the underlying continuity complication that motivates this question (§2 A2) though it did not itself invoke Article 21 — Step0 §2 A5 treats Articles 20/21/23 as not applicable in the developmental-tradition sense that section tests for pre-Nicene candidates; this document's own Strand Determination is Step 1's own first application of Article 21 to this world, not a continuation of a test Step 0 began. §4 above confirms the single-world premise directly (project-lead confirmation, 2026-09-15, record filed at `CiC_Reformed_Zurich_Geneva_Doc01_Scope_Confirmations_2026-09-15.md`); this section applies Article 21 on that now-settled premise.

**Finding: two strands — the Zurich strand (Zwingli, succeeded by Bullinger) and the Geneva strand (Calvin, succeeded by Beza) — bridged doctrinally, not merged institutionally, by the Consensus Tigurinus (1549), itself mediated by Bullinger's own Zurich rather than by any direct contact between Zwingli and Calvin, who never met.**

The same divergence §4 weighs as separation evidence is, within one world, strand evidence, on more than one of Article 21's own named dimensions:

1. **Practice.** Zurich's own worship, under Zwingli's direct instruction, suppressed instrumental music and congregational singing as without scriptural warrant — by most accounts the Grossmünster's own organ was silenced in the mid-1520s (sources vary on the exact year; not vendored; Dominant Modern Reconstruction confidence). Geneva's worship, by direct contrast, built congregational singing into the center of its own devotional life through the metrical Genevan Psalter (Marot and Beza, complete 1562, not itself vendored — §8 below).
2. **Authority structure.** Zurich's reform was enacted through the city council acting on the outcome of public clerical disputation. Geneva's 1541 Ecclesiastical Ordinances (also not vendored — §8 below) created a distinct institution, the Consistory, holding disciplinary authority Calvin fought to keep independent of civil-council control, substantially resolved only by 1555.
3. **Formation emphasis.** Zurich's own formation instrument was the Prophezei; Geneva's was the fixed Genevan Catechism paired with the Consistory's own direct, individualized moral oversight — genuinely different formation logics, the same finding §4 above now reaches rather than contradicts.

**What bridges, rather than separates, the strands:** the shared confessional core — predestination, scriptural sole authority, rejection of the Mass and of image veneration, and, after 1549, a formally agreed spiritual reading of the Supper. The Consensus Tigurinus is the documented evidence of that bridge, and its own mediating party is Bullinger's Zurich, not any relationship between Zwingli and Calvin themselves: Zwingli died in 1531, five years before Calvin's Geneva ministry began in earnest, and the two men never met. This world's own coherence rests on Bullinger's mediation specifically, per Step0 §4 item 1's own binding instruction, stated here without qualification.

**Cross-strand implication for Step 4 and Step 10:** per Article 21, "where strands exist, convergence across them is a test of a gravity’s centrality" — the sovereignty-of-God/predestination gravity and the scripture-sole-authority gravity are visible in both strands and should be tested for convergence at Doc_04. This finding also directly bears on the Representative-construction question named as a live choice for this build — a single Representative versus a genuine multi-figure Representative — by establishing, on properly-run and honestly-weighed evidence, that the two-strand structure is real and substantial, not a rhetorical framing of one figure's dominance. This document does not decide the Representative question itself; per this build's own escalation categories, that decision is reserved for the project lead directly, at Step 10.

## 6. Preliminary Forces Identification

Per the Forces Framework's Step 1 requirement, this is orientation governing Step 2's scope, not the complete forces analysis compiled at Step 8. Constitution Article 22 states: "Every formation world is situated against the forces that shaped it across both external and internal dimensions, and across the duration of its life... Where a world’s formation continues unbroken into a living tradition, or where no internal fracture is attested by the evidence, that finding is recorded honestly as the result of the assessment — never inferred, never supplied to satisfy the requirement that the assessment occur. These forces are documented from within the world’s own formation logic — as the world experienced and understood them — never as an external analytic overlay imposed on it, consistent with the Writing-From-Inside Principle (Article 23)." The sketch below is preliminary and explicitly Layer-1 (Historical Event) only. The Forces Framework's own Section 5 (Governing Principles), verified directly against `Build/reference/L3A-Shared-Methodology/CiC_L3A_Forces_Framework_V1.1.docx`, states the register this permits at Layer 1 specifically: "Modern analytical categories are appropriate at Layer 1, where the historical event is documented using scholarly vocabulary. They must not govern Layer 2, where the world’s own consciousness is the subject." The external, orienting register below is licensed by that Layer 1 provision, not — as this document's own prior revision mis-cited — by Article 23, which governs the opposite: Layer 2's inhabited voice, Doc_08's own eventual compilation, and the Representative's own future speech.

**What generated this world:** two independent local triggers, not one shared cause — Zwingli's own Erasmian-humanist biblical scholarship, applied to Zurich's own civic reform context and catalyzed by the 1522 Sausage Affair; and, wholly separately, Geneva's 1526 alliance with Bern and 1536 political break from Savoy and the Prince-Bishop, which created the civic opening Calvin's own arrival and 1541 recall filled. The two triggers are connected only retrospectively, by the Consensus Tigurinus (§5 above).

**What larger world was it embedded in:** the same late-medieval Latin-Christian civic and clerical order Wittenberg's own Reformation contested, read through the Swiss Confederacy's own decentralized, republican city-state structure; and, more distantly, the Augustinian tradition on predestination and grace — the census itself already records this transmission: "Calvin's Institutes draws explicitly and heavily on Augustine's anti-Pelagian writings (De Praedestinatione Sanctorum, Contra Julianum) for total depravity, unconditional election, and irresistible grace -- Calvin named Augustine his primary authority next to Scripture itself. A direct theological line across eleven centuries with no institutional continuity -- influence, not identity."
