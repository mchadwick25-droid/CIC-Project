## Quick Meaning

God has, from eternity and by his own free choice alone, determined who will be saved — a conviction both Zurich and Geneva held, though Geneva's own Calvin worked it into a full system while Zurich's own founders stated it more briefly and pastorally.

---

## World Meaning

Salvation does not begin with anything in the one who is saved — not their own effort, not their own foreseen faith, not anything God looked ahead and found worthy. It begins, entirely, in God's own free decision, made before the world existed. We call the decree itself predestination; we call the persons it names, from their own side, the elect. One of our own founders at Zurich put the whole order plainly, working through Paul's own letter to the Romans: God's election, predestination or marking out, calling, beatifies — each step follows the one before it, and none of them rests on anything we ourselves supply.

This is not a private opinion argued only by theologians in a study. It is stated as the church's own confession, meant to comfort rather than to torment. Our own confession at Zurich says it directly: we reject those who seek out of Christ whether they are chosen, and instead point the anxious soul back to Christ himself — let Christ be the mirror in which we behold our own predestination. Whoever is joined to Christ by true faith already has, in that very faith, the only evidence of election anyone is given or needs.

Geneva took the same conviction further, into a fuller architecture: how election, calling, faith, and glorification connect to one another, worked out at the length a whole systematic theology allows rather than the length a confession or a disputation allows. The difference is not a disagreement between the two cities — it is the same doctrine, held with the same seriousness, given two different registers by two reforms that never had the chance to compare notes on how to say it.

---

## Ecological Function

This doctrine grounds Sola Scriptura's own most consequential application: if salvation rests on God's decree alone, then no human tradition, penance, or merit can add to or subtract from it, which is exactly what licenses this world's own rejection of the Mass's own sacrificial claims and of any doctrine of merit. It also stands behind this world's own catechetical urgency — if assurance comes only from faith in Christ, then teaching people to hold that faith rightly, early and repeatedly, is not optional instruction but the church's own most serious work.

## Distortion Risk

**Modern Hearing:**
A grim, fatalistic doctrine — the idea that some people are simply doomed no matter what they do, offered as an excuse for cruelty or indifference toward the "unchosen."

**World Hearing:**
Not a doctrine of doom but of comfort: since salvation was never something we could lose by our own failure, it was never something we earned by our own success either. Zurich's own confession states this pastoral intent directly — anxiety about one's own election is answered by looking to Christ, not by searching one's own worthiness. The doctrine's own harder, more systematized edges (a fully worked scheme of double predestination) are a later, largely Calvin/Beza-specific development, not the way either city's founders first stated the conviction.

## Key Sources

Zwingli's own 1527 *Refutation of the Tricks of the Baptists* (`zwingli_selected-works_jackson1901.txt`, lines 9591–9762, within the collective row-7 "Selected Works" volume, dated to Zurich, 31 July 1527) — a sustained, first-person exposition working through Romans 8–9, independently re-verified this pass: "God's election, predestination or marking out, calling, beatifies, you will ever say right" (lines 9677–9678, verbatim). The Second Helvetic Confession's own dedicated Chapter X (Bullinger, 1566), independently re-verified: "We reject those who seek out of Christ whether they are chosen" (line 667) and "Let... Christ be the mirror in which we behold [our] predestination" (lines 684–685, correcting the source's own OCR-garbled "onr" to "our," disclosed rather than silently cleaned). Calvin's Institutes Books II–III (the fullest, most systematized statement).

Note: the core conviction is cross-strand attested in each strand's own independent voice — not carried by inference from Calvin's corpus alone. The specific *systematized* form (a fully worked order of salvation, and the harder double-predestination scheme Beza develops further) is substantially Calvin/Beza-concentrated; the Second Helvetic Confession's own register is notably more pastoral and Christocentric by comparison. This divergence is disclosed, not smoothed into an undifferentiated "both sides teach predestination" claim (Doc_04 §3.1).
