### Finding 1 — HIGH — Doc_09's claim that no vendored source narrates Zwingli's death at Kappel is false; a genuine Tier-1-caliber contemporary account exists in this world's own already-vendored, Native corpus and was missed

**Checked:** Doc_09 §6 ("Zwingli's death at Kappel (1531)... no vendored source gives a narrative account of the battle or Zwingli's own death within it — the fact is carried at the level of Doc_01's own general historical orientation, not from a primary narrative text in this world's Registry") and §7 Absent Stories item 3 ("No narrative account survives of Marburg (1529), Zwingli's death at Kappel (1531), the Bolsec controversy (1551), or the Perrinist crisis (1555)... in a form meeting Tier 1's own 'direct textual attestation... named author' bar"). Also checked against §8 item 4's self-certification that a "citation-accuracy self-check... independently verified the exact line ranges for every quotation in both story chunks directly against the vendored files... All quotations checked exact on this pass; no defect found requiring correction before this document went to review."

**Found:** `cic/texts/zwingli_latin-works-correspondence-vol1_jackson1912.txt` — Source_Registry.md row 8, Type P, Confidence B, Boundary Status Native, already licensed for this world's own use since Doc_02 — contains, embedded within it, a distinct work: **Oswald Myconius's "The Original Life of Zwingli"** (the file's own section header at line 681; a footnote at line 684 identifies it explicitly: "[The life of Huldreich Zwingli by Oswald Myconius, written in Latin..."). Myconius was Zwingli's own close friend and a fellow Reformed pastor (later Basel's own reformer), a named author with an identifiable social location, writing a first-person memorial letter to a named correspondent ("Agathius"). Its own Section XII, headed "**Zwingli perishes in the Second Cappel War**" (line 1550), gives a genuinely first-person, eyewitness-adjacent narrative of the day of Zwingli's death (11 October 1531):

> "When I saw him, I could scarcely stand for sudden pain of heart presaging the worst. We passed the day [in Zurich] in continual sighs and prayers to God... As, then, I saw them going out in the morning, so at night I heard the news, — the fight had been sharp yet unsuccessful, and our Zwingli had perished. It was reported that three times he had in the shock of arms been prostrated, but each time he had struggled to his feet; a fourth time he was struck under the chin by a spear, and, fallen upon his knees, said, 'What evil is there in this? They are able, it is true, to kill the body but not the soul.' And having so spoken he fell asleep presently in the Lord."
(lines ~1550–1600, verified directly against the vendored file; the passage continues with the account of Zwingli's body being found, quartered, and burned by the enemy, and his heart reportedly found unburned in the ashes — presented by Myconius as a report he received and did not personally witness, honestly hedged as such.)

This is not a hostile or later account: Myconius was in Zurich, personally close to Zwingli, and wrote from direct, same-day experience of Zurich's own side of the event, exactly the kind of "named author with identifiable social location... datable... historically credible narrative claim" Doc_09 §2 itself defines as Tier 1's own bar — arguably a stronger, more affecting eyewitness-adjacent account than Hegenwald's own preface, which Doc_09 correctly built into rzgstory001.

The same file also contains, earlier (line 1343, within the same "Original Life" narrative), a brief mention of Zwingli's own journey to Marburg: "He made also the long journey to Marburg, to the colloquy with Luther and Melanchthon upon the Lord's Supper. He feared nothing there, for the place was suitable." This is much thinner — a single third-person sentence, not a narrative of the colloquy itself — so Doc_09's specific claim about Marburg ("no vendored source... gives a first-person or contemporary eyewitness narrative of the Colloquy itself") is more defensible on its own narrow terms, but the document still should have disclosed this passage's existence rather than treating Marburg's absence as total.

Neither the Bolsec nor the Perrinist-crisis claim in Section 6/7 was contradicted by this review's own broad corpus search (a `grep -i` sweep of every vendored `.txt` file for "Bolsec," "Perrin," "Marburg," and "Kappel" surfaced only two further RZG-corpus hits, both false positives on inspection: a "Bolsec" mention in Beveridge's own editorial introduction to *Institutes* vol. 1, discussing a dispute over which *edition* of the Institutes is authentic — unrelated to the 1551 predestination controversy — and a "Perrin" hit that is the name of a Geneva printer in a bibliographic colophon, not the Perrinist faction).

**Why this matters:** this is the identical failure mode Donatism's own Doc_09 was caught on at Round 1 review (Finding H3 there: "Section 7's conclusion that no primary attestation of Tyconius's condemnation survives was false" — corrected there by building a ninth story chunk). The review brief that commissioned this pass named this exact risk directly ("Spot-check the claim that no vendored primary narrative source exists for Marburg, Kappel, Bolsec, or the Perrinist crisis"). Doc_09's own §8 item 1 states this document's research pass found "both built stories were previously unmined for this specific purpose" inside `zwingli_selected-works_jackson1901.txt` and the Consensus Tigurinus file — but the parallel, equally-available, equally-Native Zwingli volume (row 8) was evidently never opened and read for narrative content at all, despite the Source Registry's own note that this row is, like row 7, an unitemized "collective bucket" whose "individual items within this row [are] not separately re-verified" (a caution the Registry states explicitly for row 8, and which Doc_04 already demonstrated the risk of by mining row 7's own unitemized *Refutation* for new election-doctrine content at Round 1 review). The Absent Stories section is the one part of a Story Inventory the governing skill specifically warns is "most likely to get skipped under deadline pressure, precisely because it's the one item that isn't about adding content" — here it was not skipped, but its central factual premise for one of its four named absences is wrong.

**Fix required:** Doc_09 must be revised to (1) disclose Myconius's "Original Life of Zwingli" and its Kappel-death narrative directly, citing Source_Registry.md row 8 and the specific line range; (2) properly assess it against the Tier definitions in Doc_09 §2 (this reviewer's own reading is that it clears Tier 1 cleanly); and (3) either build a third story chunk (`rzgstory003`) from it, or state a specific, evidence-based reason not to that is not simply a repetition of the now-falsified "no vendored source" claim. Section 6, Section 7 item 3, and Section 8 item 4's own self-certification all need correcting to reflect what was actually found. The brief Marburg mention in the same file should also be disclosed in Section 6, even if the document's own judgment remains that it is too thin to carry a full story chunk.

### Finding 2 — HIGH — rzgstory001's own Tier Justification cites an incomplete line range for the title-heading date quotation: the range given never reaches the year the quotation claims it contains

**Checked:** rzgstory001, Tier Justification: "this vendored volume's own title heading states the disputation was held 'ON THE 29TH DAY OF JANUARY... 1523' (lines 1513–1516)."

**Found:** Direct inspection of `zwingli_selected-works_jackson1901.txt` shows the title block reads, by line: 1513 "III. ACTS OF THE CONVENTION HELD IN THE PRAISE-"; 1514 "WORTHY CITY OF ZURICH ON THE 29TH DAY OF"; 1515 "JANUARY, ON ACCOUNT OF THE HOLY GOSPEL-"; 1516 "BEING A DISPUTATION BETWEEN THE DIGNIFIED"; ...continuing through 1522: "THE ASSEMBLED COUNCIL IN THE YEAR 1523.*" The cited range (1513–1516) does contain "ON THE 29TH DAY OF JANUARY," but it does **not** contain "1523" — that word appears six lines past the cited range's own stated end, at line 1522. The elided quotation ("...1523") implies the omitted middle content sits inside the cited range; it does not. This is exactly the kind of line-range-doesn't-actually-cover-the-quoted-material defect the review brief asked to be checked for by name.

**Why it matters:** this is a load-bearing citation in the story's own Tier Justification section, the section specifically responsible for establishing "datable with reasonable confidence" — and it sits inside a paragraph explicitly built to demonstrate rigor about this exact disputation's own disputed dating. A citation range that does not actually reach the year it claims to quote is a defect of the identical shape (wrong/incomplete line span for an elided quotation) already caught and fixed at this world's Doc_04, Doc_05, Doc_07 (twice), and Doc_08.

**Fix required:** correct the citation to "lines 1513–1522" (or otherwise expand the range to actually include "IN THE YEAR 1523"), or shorten the quoted material to end at what lines 1513–1516 actually contain and cite the "1523" separately at its own correct line.

### Finding 3 — HIGH — rzgstory001's characterization of "Jackson's own introduction (lines 1529–1598)" as entirely editorial framing is inaccurate: an interleaved fragment of Hegenwald's own primary letter sits inside that exact range, an OCR/page-layout artifact this document's own citation-accuracy pass did not catch

**Checked:** rzgstory001, Tier Justification, second caveat: "Jackson's own introduction (lines 1529–1598) is his own 1901 editorial framing, not Hegenwald's primary text."

**Found:** the vendored file's own OCR extraction interleaves two printed pages' worth of main text and footnote out of their original reading order. Lines 1529–1552 are genuinely Jackson's own 1901 editorial prose (cut off mid-sentence at "...one of the ablest disputants on the Roman Church" at the bottom of a printed page). Lines 1558–1567, however — squarely inside the claimed 1529–1598 "Jackson editorial framing" range — are the **opening of Hegenwald's own primary dedicatory letter**: "To the worthy ecclesiastical Lord and Father Sir John Jacob Russinger... Master Erhart Hegenwald offers his willing service and wishes peace in Christ. Worthy ecclesiastical Lord and Father: I understand how your dignity and grace is inclined to read and further the Gospel doctrine... That Your" — cut off mid-sentence at the bottom of the next printed page, where the OCR text then resumes Jackson's own interrupted footnote (lines 1568–1595, continuing "side, bore the brunt of the attacks upon that church... the official beginning of the Reformation in German Switzerland"), before Hegenwald's letter itself resumes on the following page at line 1603 ("Grace undertook to come to the meeting..." — i.e., "[Your] Grace undertook...," continuing directly from the interrupted "That Your" at line 1567).

The specific facts the story actually draws from this range ("six hundred persons," line 1547; Faber's identity, lines 1552/1569; the council's ratification, lines ~1587–1590) do all fall inside the genuinely-Jackson-editorial portions before and after the interleaved fragment, so the story's own substantive sourcing for those three facts is not wrong. But the blanket claim that "lines 1529–1598... is his own 1901 editorial framing, not Hegenwald's primary text" is not accurate as a description of that range — roughly ten lines inside it are Hegenwald's own primary words, not Jackson's — and this is precisely the sort of thing Section 8 item 4's "independently verified the exact line ranges for every quotation... All quotations checked exact on this pass" self-certification should have caught and disclosed.

**Why it matters:** the Jackson-editorial-versus-Hegenwald-primary distinction is the central evidentiary argument Doc_09 §6 uses to justify *not* building a Second Disputation story from the Jackson intro at lines 5047–5099 — a genuinely correct call there (that range was independently checked this pass and contains no comparable interleaving; it is cleanly Jackson's own prose throughout). But if rzgstory001's own worked example of "how to tell Jackson's editorial framing from Hegenwald's primary text" mischaracterizes its own cited range, that weakens confidence in the discipline being applied consistently, and is exactly the self-certification-versus-actual-text gap this world's build has been caught on repeatedly (e.g., Doc_08 Round 1 Finding 2).

**Fix required:** correct the range description to disclose the interleaved primary fragment (e.g., "lines 1529–1552 and 1568–1598 are Jackson's own editorial framing; lines 1558–1567, though numerically inside this span, are the opening of Hegenwald's own primary letter, out of sequence in the OCR text due to the source page's own footnote/body interleaving") rather than describing the whole numeric range as uniformly editorial.

### Finding 6 — MEDIUM — rzgstory001's narrative reframes the "tinker's day" mockery's own timing in a way the cited source does not clearly support

**Checked:** rzgstory001, Story Text: "Before the day even arrived, word had already reached the enemies of the new preaching, who mocked the whole gathering in advance: they called it a 'tinker's day,' and said nothing but tinkers would attend."

**Found:** Hegenwald's own preface places this mockery differently: "although in addition to all the clergymen, preachers and priests... there were invited and summoned to this praiseworthy meeting also many other foreign nobility and common people, prelates, doctors, masters... when these had appeared at Zurich before the Council in session certain enemies of the Gospel truth (as I hear) ridiculed the matter, announcing and saying that a tinker's day was being held at Zurich, and that nothing but tinkers were attending" (lines 1610–1619). The plain reading is that the mockery occurred as/when the invited attendees converged at Zurich for the Council session — contemporaneous with the gathering itself, not clearly "before the day even arrived" in the sense of advance mockery circulating ahead of the event. The story's framing invents a specific temporal sequence (advance mockery, before the day arrived) the cited text does not itself state.

**Why it matters:** this is a Tier 1 story whose own Usage Guidance tells the Representative it may be drawn on "as remembered founding history, with real confidence" — a bar that depends on the narrative connective tissue between quotations not adding invented specificity the source doesn't support, which is exactly what the task brief's fabrication-check item asks reviewers to catch. This is a minor sequencing embellishment, not an invented fact, but it is a concrete, checkable instance of narrative "connective tissue" going beyond what the cited source actually shows.

**Fix required:** soften to a formulation the text actually supports — e.g., "as the invited crowd gathered at Zurich, enemies of the new preaching mocked the whole affair, calling it a 'tinker's day'" — removing the "before the day even arrived... in advance" framing unless a different, more specific vendored passage supports it.
