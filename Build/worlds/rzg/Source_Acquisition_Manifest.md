## §1 OPEN requests

**G3. Geneva's consistory registers, any legitimately usable substitute or excerpt.**
- Why it matters: the census's own stated selection rationale for this world (`"why"` field: "one of the best ordinary-life archives in Christian history"); the single largest disclosed source-ecology gap in this build (Doc_02 §5). Source Registry row 13.
- Checked this pass: the only identified English edition (Kingdon/Lambert/Watt, trans. McDonald, Eerdmans 2000, vol. 1 only, covering 1542–44) is confirmed copyrighted per the Source Readiness Dossier §1. No PD alternative or excerpt located this pass. **Status: open.** Given the edition's own narrow date range (1542–44 only, even in copyright), a genuinely PD 19th-century French edition of the registers' own original-language text (if one exists and is judged worth pursuing as a second-witness, non-English source per this project's own original-language intake path) may be worth a dedicated search in a future pass — named here as a direction, not pursued this pass.

**G4. Theodore Beza's own works — letters, the *Tabula praedestinationis* (1555), or his own share of the Genevan Psalter. PARTIALLY CLOSED, 2026-09-29.**
- Why it matters: Calvin's own successor at Geneva; the direct theological throughline to the confirmed Dort transmission (Doc_01 §7); Source Registry row 16.
- Checked this pass: real network access to archive.org was tested and confirmed working in this session (contrary to `Build/worlds/_cross-world/CORPUS-USE.md`'s note that "the agents' sandbox blocks every patristic text host" — that note evidently does not describe every session's environment; disclosed here rather than assumed silently true or false for future sessions). Searched and found *Job Expounded by Theodore Beza* (Cambridge: John Legatt, 1589), a genuine, direct English translation of Beza's own commentary/paraphrase on Job with a dedicatory epistle to Elizabeth I — vendored as `beza_job-expounded_legatt1589.txt`, Source Registry row 26, rights-verified directly (1589 imprint, unambiguously PD by date). Also identified but NOT vendored this pass: *Theodore Beza, the Counsellor of the French Reformation, 1519-1605* (Baird, archive.org `theobeza00bair`) — a secondary biography whose appendices reportedly print many of Beza's own letters; not checked this pass whether those letters appear in English translation or the original Latin/French, and not vendored without that check. His own share of the Genevan Psalter is already vendored (Source Registry row 19, closing part of this item independently). **Status: partially closed** — the commentary and his Psalter share are real, vendored, direct Beza text; his own letters and the *Tabula praedestinationis* remain unacquired. The Baird biography's own letters appendix is a live lead for a future pass, not pursued further here.

**G5. Marie Dentière's own works — the 1539 *Epistre très utile* and/or the 1561 signed preface to Calvin's sermon on women's apparel.**
- Why it matters: Article 20's Affirmative Duty (Doc_02 §6) — a genuine missing-voices case, not merely a completeness gap; Source Registry row 17.
- Checked this pass: actively searched, using the same confirmed network access as G4 above. Findings: (1) the only known English translation is Mary B. McKinley's 2004 University of Chicago Press scholarly edition (*Epistle to Marguerite de Navarre and Preface to a Sermon by John Calvin*, "The Other Voice in Early Modern Europe" series) — confirmed in copyright (2004), not usable. (2) The original 1539 French text (*Epistre tresutile faicte et composée par une femme Chrestienne de Tornay*) is transcribed at French Wikisource, but no underlying archive.org or Gallica scan was located to vendor directly per this corpus's own standing practice (a direct scan of a PD physical object, not a wiki transcription's own separate rights layer); Gallica's own search interface returned HTTP 403 to this session and was not reachable to check further. (3) Archive.org's own full-text search for "Dentiere" surfaced only two modern, in-copyright anthologies (Stjerna's *Women and the Reformation*, 2009; Janz's *A Reformation Reader*, 2008) that might excerpt her in translation — neither checked further, since both are plainly still in copyright. **Status: still open.** This remains a genuine, confirmed Article 20 case: an actual English PD translation does not currently exist to be found, not merely one this pass failed to locate. A future pass could pursue the Wikisource French text's own rights basis more carefully (is a Wikisource transcription of an unambiguously PD 1539 work itself vendorable under this project's PD-only rule, distinct from the transcription's own CC-BY-SA wiki license?) — a real, disclosed open question, not resolved here.

**G6. Calvin's Biblical Commentaries (Calvin Translation Society series, ~22 vols., covering nearly the whole Bible). PARTIALLY CLOSED, 2026-09-25 (reconciled 2026-09-29).**
- Why it matters: named as a verified, host-confirmed acquisition lead in the Source Readiness Dossier §3 (CCEL, `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`-class per the dossier's own PD-by-date verification). **Not currently needed:** no gravity or lexicon candidate confirmed through Doc_04 depends on Calvin's exegetical commentaries specifically — the confirmed doctrinal content (predestination, the Supper, sola scriptura) is already grounded in the Institutes, the Geneva Catechism, and the Consensus Tigurinus.
- **Status: two volumes vendored (Genesis, complete, Vols. I–II, Source Registry rows 20–21), rights-verified directly against archive.org item commentariesonfi01calvuoft (`NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`). This is a deliberate one-work sample of the ~22-volume series, not a closure of the full lead — the remaining ~20 volumes (Psalms, the Gospel harmony, Paul's epistles, and others) remain open, low priority relative to G3–G5, since nothing confirmed through Doc_04 depends on them.**
- **G7. Letters of John Calvin, 4 vols. (Bonnet, 1855–58). MOSTLY CLOSED, 2026-09-25 (reconciled 2026-09-29).**
- Why it matters: also named as a verified, host-confirmed lead in the Source Readiness Dossier §3. Bears directly on Calvin's own Limitations dimension in the Author Gravity Assessment (Doc_02 §2 names his own conduct as a civic/disciplinary actor as under-documented relative to his doctrinal writing — correspondence is exactly the kind of source that could partially close that gap) and on the Beza/Dort throughline (Doc_01 §7).
- **Status: Vols. I, II, and IV vendored (Source Registry rows 22–24), rights-verified directly against archive.org items lettersofjohncal01calv / 02calv / 04calv (all `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`). Vol. III could not be located under this identifier family, the parallel 1855–57 Edinburgh/Constable printing, or Project Gutenberg (which holds only Vols. I–II) — searched and confirmed genuinely missing, not merely unsearched. This item is closed as far as this set's own availability allows; Vol. III is not carried forward as an active open request, since no further lead toward it has been identified.** Whether the now-vendored letters actually close the Limitations-dimension gap in the Author Gravity Assessment, or merely supply more material without changing that assessment's own finding, has not been tested — a real, disclosed open question for whoever next revisits Doc_02 §2.
