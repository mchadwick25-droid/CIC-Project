# Source Acquisition Manifest — The Reformed Cities: Zurich & Geneva

**World file-code:** `rzg`. **Produced:** 2026-09-15, alongside `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md` and `Source_Registry.md`.
**For:** the project lead's operational source-acquisition role.
**Scope authority:** the approved Doc_01 boundary (1519–1650, Zurich/Geneva Reformed; the confirmed Dort scope in Doc_01 §7) — treated as settled here, not reopened.
**Vendoring rule this manifest is built to satisfy:** per `cic/texts/README.md`, public-domain-only; in-copyright editions are referenced by source record and never vendored.

Per this project's own per-world convention (not a `cic-build-cycle`-defined gate — the numbering below is this manifest's own item labels, following the same pattern other worlds' manifests use, e.g. Donatism's G1–G7).

## §1 OPEN requests

**G1. Calvin's 1541/1561 Ecclesiastical Ordinances, any confirmed-PD English edition.**
- Why it matters: licenses the Authority-structure limb of Doc_01's own Strand Determination (§5) directly from primary text rather than from general knowledge; Source Registry row 14.
- Checked this pass: Schaff's *History of the Christian Church* Vol. VIII (CCEL, `ccel.org/ccel/schaff/hcc8/hcc8.iv.xiii.vii.html`) — confirmed to be Schaff's own paraphrase and summary of the Ordinances' contents, not a direct translation; not usable as a primary-text substitute. An archive.org item, `calvino-theological-treatises` (1977), appears to be the Library of Christian Classics volume (likely J.K.S. Reid's translation) and carries an archive.org "Public Domain Mark" this manifest does not accept at face value — a 1977 publication does not clear this project's own PD-by-date rule, and an uploader's own applied mark is not a rights determination. **Status: open.** A dedicated search for an actual 19th-century English translation (if one exists) or confirmation that none does is still owed.

**G2. The Genevan Psalter (Marot & Beza, complete 1562), any confirmed-PD English or French edition.**
- Why it matters: licenses the Practice limb of Doc_01's own Strand Determination (§5); Source Registry row 15.
- Checked this pass: not searched beyond the general-knowledge citation already in the census's own `sources` field. **Status: open, not yet actively searched.**

**G3. Geneva's consistory registers, any legitimately usable substitute or excerpt.**
- Why it matters: the census's own stated selection rationale for this world (`"why"` field: "one of the best ordinary-life archives in Christian history"); the single largest disclosed source-ecology gap in this build (Doc_02 §5). Source Registry row 13.
- Checked this pass: the only identified English edition (Kingdon/Lambert/Watt, trans. McDonald, Eerdmans 2000, vol. 1 only, covering 1542–44) is confirmed copyrighted per the Source Readiness Dossier §1. No PD alternative or excerpt located this pass. **Status: open.** Given the edition's own narrow date range (1542–44 only, even in copyright), a genuinely PD 19th-century French edition of the registers' own original-language text (if one exists and is judged worth pursuing as a second-witness, non-English source per this project's own original-language intake path) may be worth a dedicated search in a future pass — named here as a direction, not pursued this pass.

**G4. Theodore Beza's own works — letters, the *Tabula praedestinationis* (1555), or his own share of the Genevan Psalter.**
- Why it matters: Calvin's own successor at Geneva; the direct theological throughline to the confirmed Dort transmission (Doc_01 §7); Source Registry row 16.
- Checked this pass: not searched. **Status: open, not yet actively searched.**

**G5. Marie Dentière's own works — the 1539 *Epistre très utile* and/or the 1561 signed preface to Calvin's sermon on women's apparel.**
- Why it matters: Article 20's Affirmative Duty (Doc_02 §6) — a genuine missing-voices case, not merely a completeness gap; Source Registry row 17.
- Checked this pass: not searched. **Status: open, not yet actively searched.**

**G6. Calvin's Biblical Commentaries (Calvin Translation Society series, ~22 vols., covering nearly the whole Bible).**
- Why it matters: named as a verified, host-confirmed acquisition lead in the Source Readiness Dossier §3 (CCEL, `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`-class per the dossier's own PD-by-date verification) but not yet vendored or dispositioned in this world's build — Doc_02's own §1 "what this list does not contain" paragraph should disposition this candidate (rowed or excluded with a reason) and does not yet. **Not currently needed:** no gravity or lexicon candidate confirmed through Doc_04 depends on Calvin's exegetical commentaries specifically — the confirmed doctrinal content (predestination, the Supper, sola scriptura) is already grounded in the Institutes, the Geneva Catechism, and the Consensus Tigurinus. **Status: open, low priority relative to G1–G3** — a genuine, large acquisition (nearly the whole Bible) that would primarily deepen this world's own exegetical-practice evidence (currently thin, per Doc_02 §4's own formation-narrative disclosure) rather than close an active gap in a confirmed claim.
- **G7. Letters of John Calvin, 4 vols. (Bonnet, 1855–58).**
- Why it matters: also named as a verified, host-confirmed lead in the Source Readiness Dossier §3, also not yet vendored or dispositioned. Would bear directly on Calvin's own Limitations dimension in the Author Gravity Assessment (Doc_02 §2 names his own conduct as a civic/disciplinary actor as under-documented relative to his doctrinal writing — correspondence is exactly the kind of source that could partially close that gap) and on the Beza/Dort throughline (Doc_01 §7). **Not currently needed** for any confirmed gravity or lexicon candidate through Doc_04. **Status: open, low priority relative to G1–G3.**

## Decision from the project lead

**Not yet presented.** This manifest is submitted alongside Doc_02 and the Source Registry for the project lead's own decision on which of G1–G7 to pursue, and in what priority. Recommended priority, reasoned from what each unlocks: **G3** (the consistory registers) first, since it is both the census's own named selection rationale and this build's single largest ecology gap; **G1** (the Ecclesiastical Ordinances) second, since it directly backs an already-load-bearing Doc_01 finding, and its acquisition (or confirmed non-acquisition) directly determines whether Doc_04's own G4 gravity classification should be revisited (`Doc_04_Gravity_Discovery.md` §7); **G5** (Dentière) third, as a genuine Article 20 case; **G4** (Beza) and **G2** (the Psalter) after, as real but lower-stakes gaps; **G6** (the Biblical Commentaries) and **G7** (Calvin's Letters) last — both real, verified leads, but neither currently blocks any confirmed gravity or lexicon candidate, and G6 in particular is a large acquisition (~22 volumes) whose priority against this project's other worlds' own open requests is itself worth the project lead's own judgment. This document does not decide the priority itself — only recommends it — consistent with acquisition decisions being the project lead's own operational role in this project's established practice (Donatism's own G1 gate precedent).
