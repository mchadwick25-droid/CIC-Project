# Facilitator Brief Backlog

**Status:** Living backlog. Not yet built into the runtime Facilitator system — this document exists so that content and reasoning developed during design conversations isn't lost before the Facilitator Brief architecture is actually built. When that work begins, entries here are the starting material, not an exhaustive spec.

**Why this exists:** Participants will ask for things the architecture doesn't provide in the form they expect (e.g., "let me talk to Augustine," or "why isn't there an Augustine world the way there's an Alexandria world"). These moments need an honest, prepared response, not an improvised one. This backlog captures those moments as they're identified, in a reusable two-part format.

---

## Format: Facilitator Interjection Note

Every entry in this backlog has two parts, serving two different needs:

**1. In-conversation interjection (1–2 sentences).** Spoken by the Facilitator function, stepping briefly into the foreground to supply modern context — never spoken by the Representative, who stays fully inside their world and never breaks character to explain the project's own architecture. Short, plain, low-friction; answers the immediate question without derailing the encounter.

**2. Expandable deep note (clickable, with sources).** The full reasoning, available to anyone who wants to go further, structured so it can carry citations and not just assertions. Does not interrupt the conversational flow for participants who don't open it.

## Taxonomy: why a well-known early-Christian voice may not appear as its own world

Participants will repeatedly ask "why isn't [famous name] here." There isn't one reason — there are several distinct ones, and facilitators should give the *right* one, not a generic one. Recorded here so this reasoning isn't reconstructed from scratch each time a new name comes up.

1. **Eponymous exclusion.** The movement's whole identity is one person's specific claimed teaching, revelation, or personal authority (Marcion, Montanus, Novatian, Priscillian, Pelagius, Meletius, Mani). Excluded regardless of how much evidence survives, because the project doesn't center worlds on individual founders' claimed authority.
2. **Insufficient independent community evidence ("singular voice").** A real, orthodox, important figure — but what survives is overwhelmingly their own writing, without an independently documented community around them (Augustine — see the Augustine entry below).
3. **Outside the Nicene trajectory.** The position is defined by rejecting what became mainstream Christian orthodoxy, not merely predating it (Marcionite rejection of the creator God, Valentinian/Sethian cosmology, Homoian/"Arian" rejection of the Nicene formula).
4. **Dominant surviving content is doctrinal argument, not lived ecology.** A real, non-eponymous, orthodox tradition exists, but what mostly survives is theological or conciliar argument rather than descriptions of daily practice (the generic "Arian controversy" as a subject in itself; Constantinople's court politics). Sometimes resolved by including the figure *inside* a broader world rather than building a standalone one.
5. **Evidentiary base too thin overall.** A real, distinct, doctrinally-fine candidate exists, but there isn't enough surviving evidence of any kind — insider or outside witness — to construct a defensible, rigorously-graded ecology yet (early Armenian Christianity, Aksumite Christianity, the Meletian schism). Different from reason 2: this is "we don't know enough," not "we only have one person's account."
6. **Redundant with an already-selected world.** A valid, well-evidenced candidate whose distinctive content substantially overlaps a world already in the release, so including both would dilute differentiation rather than add a new dimension (Cappadocian material overlapping several already-selected worlds; the Persian Church of the East sharing Syriac Mesopotamia's textual spine).
7. **Resolved by inclusion, not exclusion.** The figure is a genuine part of a larger community that *is* being built as a world, so they appear naturally without their own entry or special explanation (Origen and Clement within Alexandria; Jerome within Jerusalem/Palestine; Tertullian and Cyprian within North Africa).
8. **Deferred for construction difficulty, not invalidity.** A candidate that passes every substantive test but whose insider voice is unusually hard to recover independent of hostile sources, making it a harder first-release build — recommended for a later phase once the methodology is proven elsewhere, not because it fails any test (this was Donatist Africa's original classification before the power-differentiation criterion changed the calculus and moved it into Phase One).

## Category: Significant historical voices without a corresponding formation world

**The profile:** a figure whose later influence is enormous — large enough that participants will assume a rich, multi-voiced community must stand behind them — but whose actual surviving evidence is overwhelmingly their own writing, without independent outside witnesses describing the same community. This is a distinct failure mode from an *eponymous exclusion* (a movement literally named for and defined by one person's claimed teaching or authority, like Novatian or Pelagius, excluded under a different criterion entirely). Facilitators should not reach for the same explanation in both cases — see the distinction noted below.

Entries in this category don't need to reference any other figure to make sense (a figure who *is* included, like Origen, needs no special note at all — it's resolved by inclusion, not by explanation). Each entry here stands alone.

### Augustine

**Trigger context:** A participant asks to encounter Augustine directly, or asks why there's no "Augustine's world."

**In-conversation interjection:**
> Quick note: Augustine, though enormously influential on later Christianity, is known to us almost entirely through his own writing — his sermons, letters, and autobiography — rather than through an independently documented community around him. So there's no "Augustine's world" built the way some other worlds are.

**Expandable deep note:**
> A formation world requires a genuine, multi-voiced community — not just one brilliant writer, but independent evidence of other people describing or participating in the same lived ecology. Augustine doesn't clear that bar, and that's worth being direct about — not because he matters less, but because of what kind of evidence survives around him. Hippo, where he served as bishop for nearly forty years, wasn't a major imperial see; its historical weight comes almost entirely from the fact that its bishop happened to be an extraordinary writer. What survives is overwhelmingly his own voice, without an independent outside witness describing Hippo the way other cities in this era are described from multiple angles. Building a "formation world" from that would mean dressing up one man's testimony as a documented community, which this project's evidentiary discipline exists to avoid.
>
> Augustine's voice isn't absent from the system, though. His anti-Donatist letters and his presence at the 411 Carthage conference are genuine primary sources used in constructing the North African parallel-church world, and his correspondence with imperial officials would inform an imperial-court world if built. Participants encountering those worlds meet Augustine's actual words and actions there — what they won't get is a formation world centered on him, because the evidence doesn't support one, however much later Christian history rests on his shoulders.

**Sources to attach when built:** *Confessions*; sermons to the Hippo congregation; correspondence re: the Donatist controversy; the *Acts* of the 411 Council of Carthage; Possidius' *Life of Augustine* (the one meaningful outside-ish witness, though friendly rather than independent).

### Athanasius of Alexandria

Athanasius is a plausible candidate for this same profile. His significance rests partly on the Arian controversy (excluded territory, doctrinal-debate-centered) and partly on his 45-year Alexandrian episcopate and five exiles, which do have some independent documentation (imperial correspondence, conciliar records, opponents' accounts) — more than Augustine has. He resolves by inclusion if Alexandria's world scope extends late enough to cover his episcopate, or if an imperial-court world is built. Otherwise he needs his own "singular voice" entry. Which applies depends on the actual scopes of Alexandria's world and the imperial-court world.

### Reference: figures already resolved by inclusion — no special note needed

Origen and Clement (inside the Alexandria world); Jerome, Paula, and the Melanias (inside the Jerusalem/Palestine world, where they already appear in the source base); Tertullian and Cyprian (inside the North Africa world); Ambrose (inside the imperial-court world, if built). None of these need a facilitator interjection — participants meeting these worlds will meet these figures naturally.

### Reference: the other failure mode — eponymous exclusions

Pelagius, Novatian, Marcion, Montanus, and similar figures are excluded for a *different* reason than Augustine: their movements are, by definition, one person's specific claimed teaching or authority, not a broader community independent of them. If a participant asks about these, the honest answer isn't "not enough independent evidence around them" — it's "this project deliberately doesn't center formation worlds on one founder's individual teaching or revelation, regardless of how much evidence survives." A separate entry for this failure mode should be drafted when the Facilitator Brief work begins, distinct from the Augustine-profile entries above.
