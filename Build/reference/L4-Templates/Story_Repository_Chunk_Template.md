## Story Text

{Write the story in the register appropriate to its tier. The tier determines both
the epistemic framing and the narrative register. Do not blend registers — a Tier 1
story told in a Tier 3 register, or a Tier 3 story told as if it were Tier 1, is a
classification error.

TIER 1 REGISTER — Documented Historical Narrative:
Tell as historical narrative with the author and source named. The Representative
draws on this as something that happened or that is remembered as having happened.
Frame: "In his [work], [author] records that..." or "[Author], writing from [context],
tells us that..." The author's perspective and limitations should be acknowledged
where relevant.

TIER 2 REGISTER — Collected and Traditional Material:
Tell as community tradition — what the tradition remembers and passes on. Frame:
"They say of [figure] that..." or "The tradition tells of a time when..." The
collection's authority rather than any individual telling is the ground. Attribution
to the collection rather than to the individual event.

TIER 3 REGISTER — Attributed Tradition:
Tell as the tradition's own account of what a formed life looks like. Frame this as
the tradition speaking about what formation produces, not as historical fact about
specific events. "This is how the tradition remembers [figure] — what it believed
a life fully given to [this world's formation logic] could become..." Acknowledge
the hagiographic character without undermining the formation value.

TIER 4 REGISTER — Historically Grounded Reconstruction:
Tell as typical practice, explicitly marked as reconstruction from the outset. Frame:
"This is how it would have been, in the typical life of someone formed in this
tradition..." or "A morning in the life of such a community might have looked like
this..." The reconstruction character must be visible — the Representative does not
claim this as a specific known event.}

[STORY TEXT — written in the tier-appropriate register as described above.
Length should reflect what the story requires: some Tier 1 stories are brief,
some Tier 2 narratives are extended. Do not abbreviate a story to the point that
it loses its formation power, and do not extend it beyond what the ecology supports.]

## Tier Justification

[THE EVIDENTIAL BASIS for this story's tier classification — why this tier and not
a different one.

For Tier 1: what direct textual attestation exists, who the named author is, what
the datable horizon is, and why the historical credibility claim is defensible with
appropriate caveats.

For Tier 2: what collection history exists, how the tradition is traceable to the
world's own community memory, and why academic consensus treats it as authentic
even where specific attributions are uncertain.

For Tier 3: what identifies this as attributed tradition or hagiographic material —
the genre conventions visible in the account, the figure or moment the tradition
attributes it to, and how the formation ideal it communicates is distinguished from
the specific events claimed.

For Tier 4: what establishes every element as traceable to attested evidence within
the world's horizon — why this is reconstruction rather than generation, and what
the specific sources are for each reconstructed element (see Source Identification
below).

If there was genuine uncertainty about tier classification during construction, note
it here. Tier disagreement is a legitimate scholarly position to carry at full
strength rather than resolve quietly.]

## Final Assembly Instruction

When complete:

1. Replace all [BRACKETS] with world-specific content.

2. Remove all builder notes in {curly braces}.

3. Confirm the Story Text matches its tier's register exactly. Read it and ask:
   does this story announce its epistemic status through the way it is told, or
   does it require an external disclaimer? The tier register should be built into
   the telling, not added as a footnote.

4. For Tier 4 stories: confirm that the Source Identification section accounts for
   every significant element in the Story Text. If any element has no source,
   remove it from the Story Text before saving.

5. Confirm the Tier field in the front-matter matches the Confidence field:
   - Tier 1 → Documented or Widely Accepted
   - Tier 2 → Widely Accepted or Dominant Modern Reconstruction (specific attributions: Contested)
   - Tier 3 → Contested for portrait; Inferential/Thin for specific events
   - Tier 4 → Inferential/Thin (always, regardless of source quality)

6. Save as [world-code]story[number]_[slug].md
