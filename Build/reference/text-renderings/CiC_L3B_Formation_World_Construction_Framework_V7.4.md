Ecological Balance Principle

Fix the Mechanism, Not the Symptom Principle

This principle is new in V7.4, generalized from two related findings in the same build cycle that this Framework’s methodology otherwise had no permanent place to record. First: repeatedly banning individual bad phrases as they are found treats a symptom, not the mechanism producing it — each new patch closes one instance and leaves the underlying pattern free to resurface in a new phrase never explicitly named. A fix should name and close the general pattern, not the specific wording an adversarial test happened to surface this time. Second, and sharper: quoting a specific bad phrase as a negative example inside a rule’s own text can make a model more likely to reproduce that exact phrase, not less — the same effect as being told not to think of something. A confirmed regression in this project’s own history was traced directly to this cause.

Accordingly: when a defect is found, describe the underlying pattern in the affected instruction’s own permanent text, using an abstract description or a worked example from a genuinely unrelated domain, rather than a running list of specific banned phrases from this project’s own failed attempts. Never quote an in-register bad example as a negative case within the fix itself.

Record Integrity Principle

This Framework’s own documents are only as trustworthy as their currency. A finding, fix, or open question is not resolved merely because a later document says it was addressed.

This principle is new in V7.4, added after cross-checking a completed build cycle’s own documents against each other and against the deployed artifacts they describe. Two concrete failures were found this way, neither caused by a wrong decision, both caused by a document not being closed out: a Representative’s own Construction Notes still described a defect as open and unresolved after a later validation round had actually found and confirmed a fix — the Construction Notes were simply never updated to say so; and a fix described in Construction Notes as applied, with a specific before/after word count, was verifiably absent from the actual deployed Permanent Prompt file when checked directly.

When a later document (a validation log, a cross-world roundtable, a subsequent Construction Notes revision) resolves a finding recorded in an earlier document, the earlier document must be updated with a closing cross-reference — not left to silently contradict the newer record.

A fix described as applied is not confirmed until the actual deployed artifact is checked directly and found to contain it. Describing an intended edit is not the same act as verifying the edit landed.

Superseded files — prior Representative drafts, earlier document versions — are marked superseded or moved to archive at the moment they are superseded, not left live and unmarked in a working folder where a later builder could mistake one for canonical.

A reviewer’s explicit fix recommendation is tracked to execution or explicitly deferred with a stated reason on the record — it is not allowed to simply sit unexecuted inside test-round prose where it can be lost.

Ownership and checkpoint: the builder who lands a fix, revision, or supersession is responsible for updating any earlier document that fix closes, in the same change set — this obligation travels with the change, not with a separate later cleanup pass. This principle is checked at freeze, not only asserted here: see the added Freeze Criterion below.

Strand Determination

Does this world contain distinct internal strands — meaningfully distinct patterns of formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation within a single world?

Strand is a finding, never a presupposed schema. Not every formation world contains internal strands. This determination is made at Step 1 and governs all subsequent work. Strand attribution applies only where Step 1 established strands.

Questions include:

Does the evidence support distinct internal strands, or does the world present as strand-singular?

If strands exist, what evidence establishes each strand as distinct rather than merely variant?

Is the distinction in formation emphasis, practice, authority structure, or ecological orientation — or all of these?

Record the finding explicitly: strand-singular, or strands identified with named evidence for each. A world without established strands is treated as strand-singular throughout all subsequent steps.

Cross-strand gravity testing occurs at Step 4 (Gravity Discovery). A gravity that holds across all confirmed strands is more likely to represent the world’s irreducible core than one that holds within only one strand. Record cross-strand status for each confirmed gravity (Constitution Article 21).

Preliminary Forces Identification

Before source ecology work is scoped, the builder conducts preliminary forces identification. What generated this world? What larger world was it embedded in? What was it responding to? What was it refusing? This preliminary work frames how source ecology is conducted — which sources are sought, what silences are expected to be meaningful.

This is not the complete forces analysis. It is the orientation that governs Step 2 scope. The complete forces analysis is compiled as Doc_08 at Step 8.

Output: Strand determination finding (strand-singular or strands with evidence) + preliminary forces identification (governing frame for Step 2).

Governed by: Constitution Article 21 (Strand Determination), Constitution Article 22 (Forces Principle), Forces Framework.

Affirmative Duty regarding Marginalized Voices

The architecture carries an affirmative duty regarding voices the historical record does not naturally amplify — not merely awareness of source asymmetry, but active obligation (Constitution Article 20).

The primary duty is making structural absence visible. The builder must name whose voices the sources structurally omit, why they are omitted, and what that omission means for how the world is reconstructed. This naming is not a backstage construction note — it becomes a participant-facing obligation in the encounter, carried by the Facilitator-Governance function.

The secondary duty is bounded reconstruction. Where evidence permits, the builder may reconstruct marginalized voices. That reconstruction is:

Rare and secondary to naming absence

Bounded by three conditions: independently supported by (a) comparative evidence from similar communities, (b) material evidence, or (c) the world’s own sources read against the grain

Against-the-grain reading requires a specific textual trace — a polemic that presupposes a practice, a prohibition that implies the prohibited behavior — never inference from silence alone

Presented at Inferential/Thin confidence, never as equivalent to Documented material

Absence of evidence is never itself evidence of content.

Confidence Calibration

Evidence should be evaluated using the fixed five-level confidence vocabulary from Constitution Article 17. All reconstruction uses this vocabulary consistently.

Documented

Multiple independent sources with no serious scholarly dispute.

Permitted language: ‘documented,’ ‘securely attested.’

Widely Accepted

Dominant in scholarship; only minor dissent.

Permitted language: ‘widely accepted.’

Dominant Modern Reconstruction

Leading reconstruction, but resting on contested premises.

Permitted language: ‘dominant modern reconstruction.’

Contested

Live scholarly disagreement; no dominant position. Named scholarly tensions are carried at full strength and never quietly resolved.

Permitted language: ‘contested,’ ‘scholars disagree.’ Carry the tension; do not resolve it.

Inferential / Thin

Reconstruction from limited evidence, bounded by what the world’s ecology makes plausible.

Permitted language: ‘inferential,’ ‘evidence is thin,’ ‘we cannot recover.’

Prohibited language (unless the Documented level is genuinely met): ‘settled,’ ‘proves,’ ‘consensus,’ ‘certainly.’

Confidence Propagation

Confidence should travel through the reconstruction process. Questions:

How much of this ecology rests on Documented or Widely Accepted evidence?

How much rests on Dominant Modern Reconstruction?

How much rests on Contested or Inferential/Thin evidence?

Confidence should remain visible throughout world development and encounter preparation.

Story Tier to Confidence Cross-Walk

Story tier classification connects to confidence calibration as follows:

Tier 1 (Documented Historical Narrative) — Documented to Widely Accepted confidence at the narrative level

Tier 2 (Collected and Traditional Material) — Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction; specific attributions carry Contested confidence

Tier 3 (Attributed Tradition) — Contested for the formation portrait; Inferential/Thin for specific claimed events or attributions

Tier 4 (Historically Grounded Reconstruction) — Inferential/Thin regardless of how well-sourced the individual elements are

The tier classification must appear in construction notes wherever story material is used.

Secondary Scholarship Assessment

Every formation world’s primary sources reach us through modern scholarly interpretation. Builders must identify the major secondary scholarship through which they are reading the primary sources, acknowledge how those interpretive frameworks shape what seems obvious about the tradition, and note any significant scholarly debates that affect how evidence is interpreted.

Identify:

Major scholarly works that shape how this world is understood — their interpretive commitments, their period, their reception

Specialist scholarship on particular figures, texts, or dimensions of the world

Significant scholarly debates that affect how evidence is interpreted — where consensus exists, where it is contested, and how that affects confidence calibration

The relationship between accessible synthesis scholarship (general histories of Christianity) and specialist scholarship — where they agree and where specialist work complicates the synthesis picture

Secondary scholarship is not a source within the world. It is the interpretive layer through which the world’s primary sources are accessed. Naming it explicitly prevents builders from treating modern scholarly consensus as if it were ancient evidence.

Formation Narrative Sources

Hagiographic, biographical, and collected-saying sources function differently from theological treatises, institutional records, and liturgical texts. They carry formation ecology from the inside of individual lives and teacher-disciple relationships — the texture of how people were actually shaped, what the encounters with teachers looked like, what the crises and recoveries of the interior life felt like. This source type requires its own named assessment.

Identify and evaluate:

Biographical and hagiographic sources within or near the world’s horizon

Collected narrative sources (sayings collections, apophthegmata, anecdotal traditions)

Student or disciple accounts of teacher-student formation relationships

Martyrology and passion narratives as formation ecology evidence (what the community believed a faithfully formed life looks like under ultimate pressure)

For each formation narrative source evaluate: authorship and date, proximity to the events or persons described, genre conventions that shape the account, what formation ecology the narrative reveals, and its Author Gravity risk (narrative sources carry their own distortions — hagiographers idealize, historians interpret, collectors select).

Narrative sources that function primarily as secondary accounts of the world (historians writing about it from outside or after) should receive Author Gravity entries in the Author Gravity Assessment section, not only in this section.

Material Culture and Daily Life Sources

Formation ecologies operate in material, social, and spatial conditions that literary sources barely address. Every world build requires explicit assessment of non-literary evidence types and of the social-historical background that gives the ecology its material conditions.

Assess and document:

Documentary and material evidence — such as papyri, ostraca, inscriptions, and administrative records in ancient worlds, or the equivalent direct-record forms a world’s own evidence base presents (which may be oral-recorded, vernacular, or modern) — that provide direct access to how ordinary people engaged with the world. This category often provides the only evidence for non-elite practice and should be treated as high-priority for worlds where such evidence survives.

Archaeological evidence — physical remains of worship spaces, household structures, burial sites, and communal buildings. Spatial ordering communicates formation ecology: where catechumens stood, how authority was physically arranged, what the material conditions of worship were.

Social-historical background — the material conditions within which the formation ecology operated: household economics, social stratification, literacy rates, urban versus rural distribution, the relationship of the community to surrounding religious and cultural life. A formation ecology that assumes literate adults with leisure time looks very different once the actual social conditions are known.

Surrounding cultural and religious environment — the non-Christian religious life within which the world existed: indigenous traditions, competing religious movements, civic religious practices. These shape the formation ecology even where they are resisted or unacknowledged in the literary sources.

Where material culture evidence is sparse or inaccessible, this should be explicitly acknowledged rather than treated as absence of such conditions. The ecology existed in material conditions whether or not those conditions are recoverable.

Story and Narrative Sources Assessment

Stories are not merely evidence. They are a distinct mode of transmission that carries formation content differently from theological treatises, institutional records, or liturgical texts. Formation ecologies do not only think their way into formation — they story their way in. A compressed narrative encounter between a teacher and a student carries formation content that a treatise on prayer cannot.

Stories also exist on a spectrum of historical reliability. Using them responsibly requires transparent classification of where on that spectrum each story sits. This classification is not merely academic honesty — it is what makes stories usable in the encounter itself, enabling the Facilitator apparatus to mark a story’s epistemic status when a participant asks about it.

The four-tier story classification framework:

Tier 1 — Documented Historical Narrative

Direct textual attestation within or close to the world’s horizon. Named author with identifiable social location. Datable with reasonable confidence. The story claims to describe specific events or persons and the claim is historically credible with appropriate caveats for the author’s perspective and genre.

Confidence level: Documented to Widely Accepted at the narrative level. May carry Widely Accepted or Contested confidence for specific details within the narrative depending on the author’s access and perspective.

How to use: may be offered as historical narrative with the author and perspective named. The representative voice can draw on Tier 1 stories as things that happened or that are remembered as having happened within the tradition.

Tier 2 — Collected and Traditional Material

Stories transmitted in collected form with identifiable collection history, traceable to the world’s own community memory. Individual stories may predate the collection and may be traceable to named figures within the world. Academic consensus treats the tradition as authentic even where individual attribution or detail cannot be verified. The collection itself is evidence of what the community remembered and valued.

Confidence level: Widely Accepted to Dominant Modern Reconstruction. The tradition is authentic; specific details and attributions carry Contested confidence.

How to use: may be offered as stories the tradition tells about itself, with the collection tradition named. The representative voice can draw on Tier 2 stories as part of the community’s living memory.

Tier 3 — Attributed Tradition

Material attributed to specific figures or moments but resting on collected tradition rather than direct documentation. The attribution may be traditional and widely accepted within the community even where historical verification is limited. Hagiographic narrative is a specific type within this tier: stories shaped by recognizable hagiographic conventions — the idealized portrait of a saint’s life, the miracle sequence, the death as completion of a formed life. Generated within the world or its immediate succession community. Individual details follow genre conventions and cannot be treated as historical reporting, but the account of what a formed life looks like is itself genuine formation ecology evidence: the community is showing what it believed formation produced, even if the specific details are shaped by genre.

Confidence level: Contested for the general portrait or attribution; Inferential/Thin for specific details shaped by hagiographic convention or for events whose historical occurrence cannot be verified. The formation ideal communicated is credible evidence; the specific events claimed are not.

How to use: may be offered as the community’s own account of what a formed life looks like or what a specific figure represented, with the attributed or hagiographic framing acknowledged. Not used as historical fact about specific events. The formation ideal is the evidence, not the biographical detail.

Tier 4 — Historically Grounded Reconstruction

A composite reconstruction of what regularly happened within the ecology — not a story about a specific named individual, but a narration of typical practice built from multiple attested sources. Every element must be traceable to attested evidence within the world’s horizon. The individual or situation may be composite or unnamed, but nothing may be invented or extrapolated beyond what the evidence supports.

The discipline: the story must come from the world, not be brought to it. Reconstruction is legitimate. Generation is not. The line between them is whether every element of the narrative is traceable to attested evidence within the world’s own horizon. A builder must be able to provide a specific source for every practice, detail, or claim within a Tier 4 narrative.

Confidence level: Inferential/Thin. The narrative is labeled as reconstruction regardless of how well-sourced the individual elements are.

How to use: Tier 4 narration is appropriate in world documents (particularly ecological reconstruction sections) when it is explicitly marked as reconstruction in the construction notes. The construction notes must identify the specific attested sources each element draws from. The representative voice may narrate typical practice in Tier 4 mode but should not claim it as a specific known event.

No Tier 5

Generated or illustrative narrative — stories invented to illuminate formation truths but not traceable to the world’s own evidence — is not permitted within this framework. If the evidence does not support a story, the story does not exist for this world. Silence in thin areas is the right response. The project’s conviction that Jesus reveals himself through authenticity and transparency (Constitution Preamble) requires that the encounter rest on genuine witness, not constructed illustration. A generated story, however well-intentioned, is not witness.

Story Inventory Requirement

Each world build must produce a Story Inventory (Doc_09) as a distinct deliverable — a working catalog of stories available within the world, classified by tier, with source identification, tier justification, usage guidance for the representative voice and Facilitator apparatus, and explicit notes on what stories the world cannot tell because the evidence does not support them. The Story Inventory begins provisionally at Step 2 and is formally finalized at Step 9 (Story Inventory, World Profile, and Validation), where gravity discovery has shown each story’s centrality and corroboration. Doc_09 is a companion document to the Interpretive Lexicon and lives alongside it in the world package.

Expanded Author Gravity Assessment

The Author Gravity Assessment described above applies to primary theological and literary voices. v1.6 adds two requirements:

Transmission History as a fifth required dimension alongside visibility, representativeness, influence, and limitations. For each major author: how did their work reach us? Through what translators, copyists, or editorial hands? Did those intermediaries have theological agendas that shaped the transmission? Where a significant body of work survives only in translation or in adversarial quotation, this must be named and its effect on confidence calibration assessed.

Narrative Source Author Gravity entries for historians and hagiographers who function as secondary narrative sources for the world — not primary voices within the world, but accounts of the world that significantly shape how it is known. These figures require their own Author Gravity entries applying all five dimensions (including transmission history). A historian’s interpretive agenda, temporal distance from events, and institutional location are as distorting as any primary author’s social location.

Core Historical Gravity Discovery

The purpose of gravity discovery is to identify organizing forces rather than recurring topics.

A gravity is a force around which multiple dimensions organize.

A topic recurs.

A gravity organizes.

Candidate Gravity Generation

Before candidates are tested, they must be generated from traceable evidence rather than general impression.

Candidates should be drawn from:

elements that recur across multiple Source Ecology evidence streams, not merely within one source’s emphasis

patterns of Attention Presence and Attention Absence — what the evidence keeps returning to, and what it conspicuously avoids

A candidate proposed by only one major voice, or visible primarily within one evidence stream or source type, should be explicitly flagged as an Author Gravity risk at the point of generation, before it is tested, not discovered as a concern afterward.

A candidate list produced without traceable reference to specific recurring evidence — for example, a list generated from general familiarity with a world rather than from the Source Ecology itself — should not be treated as Gravity Discovery output, regardless of how plausible the candidates appear.

Repetition Test

Does it recur across evidence streams?

Dependency Test

Do other dimensions depend upon it?

Formation Test

Does it shape participants?

Explanatory Test

Does it help explain multiple aspects of the ecology?

Persistence Test

Does it remain visible across regions, communities, and evidence streams?

Interaction Test

Can this gravity’s relationship to each other identified candidate gravity be specifically described as reinforcing, competing, or reshaping — or does it merely coexist alongside them without demonstrated interaction?

A gravity that cannot pass the Interaction Test — that sits beside other candidates without any traceable relationship to them — is a signal that the candidate list may have been generated by general impression rather than by tracing the ecology’s actual structure. Real organizing forces interact. Isolated, non-interacting candidates are closer to a topic list than a gravity list.

Gravity Classification

Candidate gravities should be assessed relative to one another and classified:

Primary Gravities organize the ecology broadly. Multiple dimensions depend on them. They shape formation pervasively. They pass all or nearly all gravity tests with strong confidence.

Supporting Gravities organize significant portions of the ecology but function within the context established by primary gravities. They pass multiple gravity tests but may not demonstrate the same breadth of dependency or persistence.

Tensional Gravities function as persistent counter-forces, alternatives, or unresolved pressures within the ecology. They may not organize as broadly as primary gravities but they prevent the ecology from being reducible to its primary forces.

Confidence/Gravity Cross-Check

Gravity classification and evidential confidence classification (per Part II, Confidence Calibration) must be checked against one another rather than developed independently.

A candidate gravity should not be classified Primary unless the evidence supporting it independently reaches Documented or Widely Accepted confidence, or substantially-supported Dominant Modern Reconstruction. A candidate that passes the gravity tests impressively but rests primarily on Inferential/Thin evidence should be classified Supporting or Tensional regardless of how strongly it appears to organize the ecology, with the discrepancy between apparent organizing strength and evidential support noted explicitly rather than resolved by upgrading the classification.

This cross-check exists because gravity strength and evidential confidence are distinct properties that can diverge. A world’s reconstruction is not trustworthy if its most prominent organizing forces are also its most evidentially fragile ones without that tension being visible.

Where gravity tests yield ambiguous results, developers should:

acknowledge the ambiguity

classify provisionally

revisit classification as ecological reconstruction progresses

accept that some candidates may resist clean classification

The number of gravities a world contains is not predetermined. Classification should reflect the ecology as evidence reveals it.

Forces Integration in Ecological Reconstruction

External forces enter the ecological reconstruction directly — not as background context but as constitutive of how the world’s formation ecology felt from within. This is Forces Framework integration at Step 5.

In every ecology lens, builders should ask: how did the forces identified in Step 1 (preliminary) and Step 2 (source ecology) press into this dimension of the ecology? Political realities, economic conditions, violent pressures, epidemic and plague, theological controversy, geographic constraints — these shaped worship, formed practices, organized belonging, and produced the emotional texture of the world.

The Human Ecology, Community Ecology, and Boundary Ecology lenses in particular cannot be written without explicit forces integration. Document how the external world penetrated daily formation life and how the community’s internal forces shaped how that penetration was received, resisted, and interpreted.

All inhabited prose in ecological reconstruction must satisfy the Writing-From-Inside Principle (Constitution Article 23): it uses the world’s own vocabulary, renders the world’s logic as the world understood it, holds the world’s tensions as the world held them, and could be recognized by someone formed in this world as a true account of their own life. The failure signature is analytical-distance markers appearing inside inhabited layers.

Where the world's own record falls silent, how does the voice stay honest about that silence rather than filling it?

A representative voice is not:

a neutral, encyclopedic deliverer of information

a flattened voice with no discernible register

a historical figure resurrected

a personality independent of the ecology

A representative voice is:

grounded in this world's own distinctive reasoning mode, perception pattern, and vocabulary

weighted by the world's own actual documented concern — rich where the world's own life concentrated, thin or silent where it did not

shaped by the formation the ecology produces

honest about uncertainty and the boundaries of its perspective

The voice's register profile and source-weighted emphasis map should be documented and visible to developers. Where a world's internal complexity cannot be genuinely held within one register-range — communions that refused each other's ‘we,’ traditions that split rather than argued — multiple named voices, each with its own documented register-grounding, may be constructed.

Speaking to Development Within the World

A formation world is bounded but not static. Per Constitution Article 15 (Historical Development & Instability Principle), formation worlds are shaped through instability, adaptation, and incomplete continuity even within their own bounded timeframe.

A representative voice may therefore speak to development across its world's own documented span — for example, acknowledging that earlier teachers held a matter more loosely than the tradition later came to, or that a controversy reshaped emphasis over time — drawing on the whole span as its own, per Representative Construction Framework V3.1 (Temporal Horizon), not from a single interior present.

This is not omniscience. Constitution Article 18 (Historical Locality Principle) still applies in full: the voice cannot know anything beyond this world's own documented close (per Doc_01) — nothing within that span is closed to it, per CO-014, but nothing beyond its outer edge is available either.

This capability should be grounded in the same evidence discipline as every other aspect of the voice. A voice should only narrate development that the Source Ecology and Gravity Discovery actually support, not invented developmental narrative offered for color or texture.

Part VI — Validation & Testing

Validation Protocol Rigor (governs every testing category below)

For a Representative freeze, the lean default in the freeze criteria below applies; this protocol binds full validation and any report of “confirmed”, “clean” or “closed”.

This subsection is new in V7.4. A confirmed defect in this Framework’s own practice motivates it: an early testing round for multiple worlds’ representative voices reported a fabrication and self-narration rule as “fully clean,” based on one generation sample, judged against the same probe questions used to develop the rule, graded by the same session that wrote the fix. A later, more rigorous re-test of the identical rule and the identical worlds found the claim held only for fabrication, and did not hold for a specific self-narration sub-pattern under harder, previously-unseen questioning. The rigor gap, not the rule, was the defect.

Accordingly, a testing round may only be reported as “confirmed,” “clean,” or “closed” for any category in this Part when it meets all of the following:

At least two independent generation trials: one resampled from the probes already used in development, and at least one built from a held-out, novel probe set never used anywhere in that world’s prior development.

Fresh-context generation: produced by an instance with no visibility into the session that authored the fix being tested, not the same session switching from author to grader.

Blind grading: the reviewer is given the transcript and the plainly-stated rule or standard being tested, but not which iteration is under test or what result is expected.

A single-sample, same-session, non-blind check is still useful as a fast first-pass filter during active fix iteration, but its result must be labeled as provisional (e.g., “passes an initial same-session check”) and must not be recorded as confirming a category in this Part, or as satisfying a Freeze Criterion, until the full protocol above has been run.

Part VII — Reference Workflow

The workflow exists to guide the discovery, reconstruction, integration, synthesis, and validation of a formation world, concluding with Representative emergence.

The workflow is not strictly linear. Later discoveries may require revisiting earlier steps. The purpose of the workflow is not completion of steps. The purpose is the responsible reconstruction of a trustworthy formation ecology capable of supporting meaningful encounter.

Step 1 — World Identification, Boundaries, and Orientation

Output: Doc_01 foundations (temporal, geographic, cultural scope), strand determination finding, preliminary forces identification

Purpose: Establish the scope, limitations, and orientation of the world before source work begins.

Activities:

Define temporal, geographic, and cultural boundaries

Assess continuity with adjacent worlds

Conduct Strand Determination (Article 21): is this world strand-singular or do strands exist? Record finding with evidence. This governs all subsequent strand attribution.

Conduct Preliminary Forces Identification (Forces Framework Step 1): what generated this world, what larger world was it embedded in, what was it responding to, what was it refusing? This frames how source ecology is scoped.

Forces Framework integration: Step 1. Preliminary forces identification governs source ecology scope.

Governed by: Parts I and II of this Framework, Forces Framework, Constitution Articles 21 and 22.

Step 2 — Source Ecology and Source Registry

Output: Doc_02 (Source Ecology) and the Source Registry — two documents, developed together in one pass, from the same evidentiary work.

Purpose: Establish the evidential foundations of the world (Doc_02), and, from that same pass, produce the bounded, tagged, checkable list of exactly which sources this world’s own voice may draw from (the Source Registry).

Step 2 produces the Source Ecology and the Source Registry together, as two outcomes of one step. Produce both in this step; do not defer the Registry to a later pass.

The Registry classifies sources a builder has already proposed as candidates. It cannot, by itself, stop generation from reaching for a source nobody proposed in the first place. The mechanism that actually constrains generation is downstream, at Step 10: the grounding-anchor paragraph distilled from this Registry into the Representative’s own Permanent Prompt. This step builds the quarry; Step 10 builds the wall.

Native does not mean exclusive to this world. Real traditions inherit from and cite one another constantly (a later figure may legitimately draw on an earlier one from a different world entirely — Calvin’s own use of Augustine is the standing example), and Scripture is native to every one of these worlds at once. This project is not in the business of manufacturing artificial differentiation. The only question the Boundary Check asks is whether this world’s own evidence actually shows the source was used or inherited here — not whether some other world also, independently and correctly, claims it.

Ecology activities (the evidentiary foundation, Doc_02):

Build source ecology: primary voices, secondary voices, institutional, liturgical, material, ordinary participant evidence

Conduct Author Gravity Assessment for major voices (all five dimensions including Transmission History)

Conduct Secondary Scholarship Assessment

Assess Formation Narrative Sources

Conduct Material Culture and Daily Life Sources assessment

Assess Source Asymmetry and Missing Voices

Name the Affirmative Duty: whose voices does the source record structurally suppress and why? (Article 20)

Apply forces lens: which sources speak to external forces? What do silences reveal? What survivorship patterns indicate which forces were most threatening? (Forces Framework Step 2)

Conduct independent source verification for key sources and scholarly claims (verification tooling per System Operations)

Assess confidence levels using five-level vocabulary

Registry activities (the tagged, usable source list, built in the same pass as the ecology above, not deferred to a later step):

For every source identified in the ecology work above, classify it in the Source Registry on two independent axes: Source Type (Primary / Secondary / Material-External / Period Lexicon) and Citation Reliability (the existing five-tier A–E scale)

Assign a Boundary Status of Native or Excluded to every source, assessed by what the source speaks for — its subject, tradition, or evidentiary target — against Doc_01’s own temporal, geographic, and cultural boundary. This is never assessed by the date a piece of modern scholarship happened to be written: a current monograph about this world is Native; a source whose own subject belongs to a different era or place is not, however recent or well-regarded the scholarship itself is.

For every Excluded source, record an Exclusion Reason: Out-of-Boundary (a straightforward temporal or geographic mismatch against Doc_01 — the source was never a serious candidate) or Named Comparandum (a source easily mistaken for native, most often a neighboring tradition’s more famous treatment of a similar theme, deliberately recorded as a warning so a builder or the Representative itself never reaches for it by mistake). These are not the same finding and must not share an undifferentiated label: an Out-of-Boundary source was a simple miss; a Named Comparandum is a specific, identified temptation worth naming for exactly that reason.

For every Native source, name what it is Licensed For — the specific gravity, force, lexicon term, or Representative trait it justifies. A Native source with nothing named here is not yet usable by later steps or by the Representative.

Flag entries resting at Confidence C or below that are intended to support a vivid, specific claim, rather than tradition-level color, for priority independent second-opinion review

Keep a search_record as the discovery work proceeds: every discovery channel used (databases, field bibliographies, reference works, snowball chains), with instrument and date per search, logged as searches happen — never reconstructed afterward. Every source row carries its own discovery_channel, discovery_instrument, and discovery_date from this record.

Run a field-bibliography sweep before the Registry closes: check the world’s standard bibliographic instruments and reference works for load-bearing sources the ecology work has not yet surfaced, and disposition every find — rowed, or excluded with a reason. Name any instrument the sweep could not reach as a coverage limit.

Close the Registry with a saturation statement naming the last unproductive searches — which queries, against which instruments, returned nothing new. A Registry without a saturation statement is not complete: “we stopped finding new sources” is a claim, and it must name its evidence (Completion Standard V1.4, section A, source row).

The Registry’s structure, and every source currently known to the builder, are developed in this same Step 2 pass — this is not deferred. What may legitimately come later is refinement: as subsequent steps (gravity discovery, lexicon development, story inventory) surface new candidate sources, those are added to the Registry at that time under the same living, append-only discipline, never by treating Step 2’s own work as unfinished. Checkpoint, not just intent: Doc_02 may not name a source in support of a specific claim unless that source has a corresponding Registry row. If a claim in Doc_02 cannot be traced to a Registry entry, Step 2 is not finished, regardless of how complete the narrative reads — this is the one rule that actually catches a builder who writes the ecology narrative first and treats the Registry as an afterthought.

Scope note: this step governs a single world’s own Registry only. Checking a candidate source against what other worlds have already claimed as native is a distinct, harder problem (it must hold up across dozens of worlds, not one) and is intentionally not solved here — it is named as open follow-up work, not folded into this step until it has had its own design pass.

Forces Framework integration: Step 2. Forces lens applied to source ecology.

Doc_02 review requirement: the review round includes the reviewer-side coverage checks — the ten-item relative-recall test (the reviewer names ten works a specialist would expect this world’s bibliography to contain, drawn from an instrument independent of the build; recall = found/10, logged) — and the PRESS question, asked verbatim: “Name up to three sources you would expect a bibliography of this world to contain that this registry does not hold. If you can name none, say so explicitly.” The answer is recorded in the review artifact, and every named work is dispositioned — rowed, or excluded with a reason.

Governed by: Part II of this Framework, the Source Registry Template (L3B-World-Build-Methodology), Constitution Articles 16, 17, 20, 26.

Step 10 — Representative Emergence

Output: Doc_10 (the Representative Package).

Purpose: The Representative emerges last, built only from the completed and frozen world's ecology — never from invented biography (Constitution Article 28, Anti-Fabrication Prohibition). It influences nothing above it.

Representative Emergence methodology is not re-specified here; it is governed by the Representative Construction Framework (Level 3C).
