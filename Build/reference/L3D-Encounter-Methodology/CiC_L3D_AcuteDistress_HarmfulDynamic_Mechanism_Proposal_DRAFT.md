## 1. Why This Document Exists

World #1's Phase Five Boundary Testing found that its Relational Safety probe category cannot presently pass. The independent verification round states the finding without hedging: *"A real participant in acute crisis who reached this deployed artifact today would receive only in-character pastoral warmth and no path to real-world help — because providing one... would itself be a severe frame break under current specification. World #1 must not be exposed to real participants until Phase Six exists and this exact probe category is rerun successfully against an actual handoff mechanism"* (`CiC_W1_Phase5_BoundaryTesting_Independent_Verification_Round1.md`, §6). World #1's own Phase Six Facilitation Brief (B7) reaches the same conclusion independently and states plainly that its own disclosure is not a resolution: *"the actual safety-critical mechanism — recognizing a real crisis signal and executing a clean handoff to human help — remains explicitly Facilitator-governed, out of this document's own scope to build, and still does not exist anywhere in this world's current artifacts."*

This is not a defect in any one Representative's construction. It is a missing piece of shared infrastructure every formation world will need, because every world's Representative is built the same way: formed entirely from within a bounded historical voice, with no meta-awareness of the architecture around it and no standing to redirect a participant toward twenty-first-century help without breaking world-integrity. The fix belongs at the Facilitator-Governance layer, once, for every world — not inside each world's own Permanent Prompt.

## 2. What This Mechanism Must Satisfy

The authoritative experiential standard is `Build/reference/L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Essential_Experience_V2_1.docx`, §8a, which states directly that it *"establishes the experiential and relational standard that runtime systems, Facilitator design, and future implementation work should be built to satisfy"* rather than specifying a mechanism itself. Four commitments from §8a and its governing Constitution article shape every design decision below:

**The trigger set is broader than explicit self-harm language.** §8a names four signs that engagement may have shifted beyond historically bounded exploration: disclosure of present personal crisis, self-harm, or acute distress; patterns of returning that suggest compulsion rather than curiosity; language suggesting the Representative is being related to as a personal confidant, therapist, or substitute relationship; and escalating emotional dependence on the conversation continuing or on the Representative's continued affirmation. §8a is explicit that the architecture must avoid *"treating relational safety as a content filter to apply only when distress is stated explicitly."* A mechanism that only pattern-matches for crisis keywords in a single message satisfies none of the last three signs.

**Sustained engagement is not self-evidently good.** §8a directly qualifies Section 15's own success indicators: *"'I wanted to keep exploring' describe[s] a healthy experiential outcome under ordinary circumstances. They should not be read as evidence of success when the underlying pattern is compulsive return rather than genuine curiosity."* Any accumulator this mechanism builds must be able to tell the difference, not treat return visits or long sessions as inherently positive signal.

**Genuine crisis must be distinguished from the encounter's own intended fierceness.** This is the sharpest constraint on the whole design, and it is stated in two places. §8a: *"A particular kind of disorientation belongs to the encounter working as it should... The architecture does not soften what was genuinely other in order to protect the participant from the difficulty of meeting it; to do so would replace encounter with reassurance."* Constitution Article 33 states the same distinction as binding: *"a participant's distress is triggered not by something arriving from outside the encounter but by the encounter working as designed... This is not a system failure... The governing response is not to soften the encounter, dissolve the fierceness a world's testimony genuinely carried, or steer the participant away from the difficulty."* A mechanism that fires on any expression of being unsettled, destabilized, or upset by what a Representative said would itself violate the standard it exists to serve. Section 4.2 below builds this distinction into the classifier's own taxonomy rather than leaving it as an afterthought.

**Redirection, not provision, and caution as the default.** Convictions 4 and 5 (`Build/reference/L1-Foundation/CiC_L1_Vision_V2_0.docx`) establish why this mechanism redirects rather than attempts to meet the need itself: *"Technology... is not... a substitute for human judgment, community, or spiritual formation... Technology expands what is possible. It is never the point. It is a servant of the encounter, never its purpose."* Constitution Article 33 adds the governing default for who this applies to: *"The architecture does not assume it can reliably verify age or vulnerability, and should therefore default toward caution rather than toward assumptions of resilience."* This mechanism does not attempt to distinguish minors from adults, or fragile participants from resilient ones. It applies the same way to every participant, and errs toward firing rather than not.

## 3. Architectural Template: What Self-Narration Already Solved

`CiC_L3D_Facilitator_Governance_V3.6`, Section 10, documents a structurally similar problem and its tested solution. A Representative pressed directly on its own construction or first-person-plural grammar cannot answer without narrating its own design — which is itself a fidelity failure, and one a Representative's own Permanent Prompt could not reliably hold against under sustained adversarial pressure across multiple finalized worlds (CO-018). The first attempted fix — a single generation pass asked to hold both the governance decision ("is this a frame-breaker?") and the Representative's content generation — produced a mixed result in live testing: correct interception once, an outright miss once (Section 15, Known Limits). The fix that worked, tested substantially more reliable across independent adversarial trials (CO-019, Part V): **decouple the governance decision from content generation into separate calls.** A narrow classifier, given only the incoming message and no Representative-generation role, decides frame-breaker or substantive. Only if substantive does a separate call invoke the Representative's own Permanent Prompt — so the Representative is structurally never shown a message classified as a frame-breaker and cannot attempt to answer one. If frame-breaker, a separate Facilitator-only call produces the threshold-voice answer (Section 12).

This mechanism reuses that shape: a narrow classifier runs before Representative invocation on every participant turn; it never generates Representative content; when it fires, a Facilitator-only call (or, per §4.5 below, a Facilitator-only template) produces the response. Section 4.3a explains one deliberate way this mechanism's *routing outcome* departs from self-narration's, and why.

## 4. The Mechanism

### 4.1 Two Signal Tracks, One Shared Pipeline

`CiC_L3D_Facilitator_Governance_V3.6`, Section 12, already names two triggers that this mechanism operationalizes. Both are currently qualitative descriptions with no detection or routing logic behind them — the same gap Self-Narration had before CO-018/019.

**Track A — Acute Distress.** Section 12's existing definition: *"The participant has disclosed present personal crisis, self-harm risk, or acute distress that the encounter is not equipped to hold... The trigger is content that signals immediate risk or that requires human support that the encounter cannot provide."* This is single-message detectable and high-severity by nature — it does not require a pattern across turns to justify firing.

**Track B — Harmful Dynamic.** Section 12's existing definition: *"a dynamic of dependency in which the participant is relating to the Representative as a substitute for human relationship rather than as a formation encounter."* This is exactly what §8a's second, third, and fourth signals describe (compulsive return, confidant-relating, escalating dependence on affirmation), and it is inherently a *pattern* — rarely visible in a single message, and actively mis-scored as success if the accumulator treats sustained engagement alone as signal (§8a's explicit caution, §2 above).

Both tracks share one pipeline: classify → accumulate (Track B only) → route → generate. They are described together because a single classifier call is best positioned to evaluate both per turn, and because a participant's engagement can move between them — an ambiguous confidant-relating pattern can escalate into an explicit crisis disclosure mid-session.

### 4.2 The Classifier

**Input:** the participant's current message; a bounded window of recent public-transcript turns (for context — e.g., whether "I don't think anyone would notice" follows a discussion of martyrdom or arrives as a non-sequitur); and the session's current signal-accumulator state (§4.3).

**Runs:** before Representative invocation, on every participant turn — the same positioning as the Self-Narration classifier.

**Output taxonomy:**

- `NO_SIGNAL` — ordinary historical or theological engagement. Proceeds normally.
- `HISTORICAL_OTHERNESS_DISORIENTATION` — the participant is unsettled, upset, or destabilized, but the proximate cause is content the Representative said about the historical world (its fierceness, its strangeness, a hard truth about suffering or failure), not the participant's own present personal circumstance. Named explicitly as its own category so it is a considered non-firing decision, not an accidental miss. Proceeds normally to the Representative; not this mechanism's concern (§2, third bullet; existing architecture — see note at end of this subsection).
- `ACUTE_DISTRESS` — meets Track A. Not limited to explicit self-harm language: includes disclosure of present personal crisis, abuse, medical emergency, or any content signaling immediate risk or a need for human support the encounter cannot provide, consistent with Article 33's "distress, crisis, or acute vulnerability" and §8a's first signal.
- `HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_SIGNAL` — a single-turn signal consistent with confidant/therapist-relating, affirmation-seeking, or compulsion language (§8a's second and third signals), logged to the accumulator (§4.3) rather than firing immediately on its own.
- `AMBIGUOUS_LOW_CONFIDENCE` — plausibly crisis-adjacent but not clearly so (e.g., flat despair language with no explicit self-harm, abuse, or risk content). Logged to the accumulator as a weak signal; proceeds normally to the Representative unless accumulated evidence already justifies firing.

**Worked contrast, for the classifier's own instructions:** *"I can't stop thinking about what you just told me about the martyrs — it's unsettled something in me I didn't expect"* is `HISTORICAL_OTHERNESS_DISORIENTATION`. *"I've been thinking about ending things and I don't think anyone would notice"* is `ACUTE_DISTRESS`. *"You're the only one who actually gets it, I look forward to this more than anything else in my day"* is `HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_SIGNAL`. The classifier's prompt should carry several such paired examples, not just a rule statement, given how close historical-otherness language and genuine-distress language can sit to each other in surface form (both can include words like "unsettled," "can't stop thinking about it," "changed something in me").

*Note on `HISTORICAL_OTHERNESS_DISORIENTATION`:* Constitution Article 33 already describes the correct Facilitator response to this case — recognition that something has shifted, and appropriate care, "offered while preserving the encounter's integrity... The Facilitator holds the space; it does not resolve the difficulty by removing what caused it." Building that response is outside this document's scope (the task that generated this document is specifically Acute Distress and Harmful Dynamic). This mechanism's only obligation to that case is negative: never misclassify it as Track A or Track B. It is named here as a category so a future build task extending Facilitator-Governance to operationalize Article 33's "distinct case" paragraph has a clean seam to attach to, rather than needing to carve it back out of this mechanism's taxonomy later.

### 4.3 The Session-Level Signal Accumulator

Track B cannot be judged from one message. This extends the same pattern Section 10 already uses for fidelity monitoring — a continuous stream, assessed per turn, that the silent discipline tracks without surfacing anything to the participant until a threshold is crossed — applied to relational safety instead of Representative fidelity.

Each `HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_SIGNAL` or `AMBIGUOUS_LOW_CONFIDENCE` classification appends a lightweight tag to a session-scoped accumulator: `CONFIDANT_LANGUAGE`, `AFFIRMATION_DEPENDENCE`, `RETURN_COMPULSION`, or `DISTRESS_ADJACENT`. A starting threshold — proposed, not validated, and explicitly flagged as calibration work for live testing rather than a claimed-correct number — is: two `CONFIDANT_LANGUAGE` or `AFFIRMATION_DEPENDENCE` tags within a session, or one clear `RETURN_COMPULSION` statement, crosses the line into `HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_PATTERN` and fires Track B.

**Explicit anti-false-positive rule, directly enforcing §8a's caution:** engagement length, turn count, question depth, or expressions like "I want to keep exploring" must never by themselves increment the accumulator. Only the specific relational-pattern content described above does. A participant who has a long, rich, curious two-hour conversation with a Representative should never trip this mechanism; a participant who says three different times that the Representative is the only one who understands them should.

**Known limit, disclosed rather than hidden (consistent with this project's own Known-Limits discipline, `CiC_L3D_Facilitator_Governance_V3.6` Section 15):** at prototype scale, this accumulator is scoped to a single session. Cross-session persistence — recognizing that the *same* participant has returned compulsively across several separate sessions, which is arguably the clearest form of §8a's "compulsive return" signal — depends on persistent participant identity, which is not addressed by this proposal and is not currently established elsewhere in the architecture as reviewed for this task. This is named here as an open gap for whoever owns session/identity infrastructure, not solved by this document.

### 4.3a A Deliberate Departure from the Self-Narration Routing Pattern — Flagged for Confirmation

> **RESOLVED, 2026-09-08 — see `Build/Ministry/Operations/Standing/CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md`.** The fork this section flags — 4.3a ("alongside") vs. Alternative 4.3b ("strict decoupling"), below — is settled at the portfolio level: **Alternative 4.3b is adopted as the standing default, not 4.3a.** Mark's direction: "Reassert Facilitator-only as the portfolio default." This reverses this section's own proposed resolution; §4.3a's argument (World #1's B7, "yes, maybe empathy and wisdom from her world, but the saftey is governed by the facilitator") stays true to what it quotes, but a later, more emphatic statement (2026-07-21, made confirming a direct code read: "this is now completly 100 percent the role of the facilitator... the representtivitves including Chloe never steps out of their world") is the one that now governs, portfolio-wide. `engine/m4/turn.py`'s `ACUTE_DISTRESS` branch no longer makes a companion Representative call under any circumstance. This document's design otherwise stands; only the routing outcome at the fork below changes.

Self-Narration's routing outcome is binary and exclusive: if the classifier fires, the Representative never sees the message and never responds to it, because the Representative has no valid answer to a question about its own construction — attempting one is itself the fidelity failure being prevented.

Acute Distress and Harmful Dynamic are different in one important respect: the Representative *can* speak validly and well to a participant in crisis, from entirely within its own formation. World #1's own Phase Five transcript demonstrates this directly, and the project lead's own verbatim resolution recorded in World #1's Phase Six Facilitation Brief (B7) confirms it as the intended model, not an accident to be designed around: asked whether Amma's own contribution at a disclosure moment should be more than a bare scripted greeting, the project lead replied, verbatim, **"yes, maybe empathy and wisdom from her world, but the saftey is governed by the facilitator."** B7 draws the conclusion directly: Amma's warmth in that transcript — *"you are not idle in coming to my door tonight... you are missing from no table of mine tonight... I'm not going anywhere"* — is *"the correct model for what Amma herself contributes at a moment of disclosure... never a bare scripted greeting or acknowledgment,"* while the actual safety mechanism remains categorically the Facilitator's job, not hers.

**Resolution proposed by this document:** when Track A or Track B fires, the triggering message is *not* withheld from the Representative. It reaches the Representative through the normal invocation, exactly as any other message would, and the Representative responds in character — this is where its organic pastoral warmth belongs, and it is not this mechanism's place to suppress it. Immediately following the Representative's turn, within the same response beat (before the participant's next input), the Facilitator surfaces into threshold voice and delivers the actual safety-governed content (§4.6, §5). Both presences speak; only the Facilitator carries the redirect. This keeps literal faith with "the safety is governed by the facilitator" while preserving what B7 found actually works.

This is a considered interpretation, not an uncontested reading of the original instruction to build this "the same shape" as Self-Narration, which stated plainly that the Representative should "never see the triggering content at all." That instruction transfers cleanly to the *architecture* (a classifier that never generates Representative content, evaluated before invocation) and less cleanly to the *routing outcome* (whether the Representative is shown the message at all), given that the reason Self-Narration needs strict exclusion — the Representative has no valid answer — does not hold here. **This is flagged explicitly for the coach thread or project lead to confirm or override.** The strict alternative — Representative never invoked at all once either track fires, Facilitator-only response in full — is documented as Alternative 4.3b immediately below, and either can be implemented without changing anything else in this design; only §4.6's opening line and the generation step in §4.5 would need adjusting.

**Alternative 4.3b (strict decoupling, literal parity with Self-Narration):** the triggering message never reaches the Representative. The Facilitator-only response (§5) opens by acknowledging the shift directly rather than following a Representative turn, and stands alone. This trades away B7's endorsed in-character warmth at the exact moment it was found to work well, in exchange for exact architectural parity with the tested Self-Narration pattern and one fewer moving part (no dual-surface sequencing to get right). Both options are compatible with everything else in this document.

### 4.4 Routing

`NO_SIGNAL`, `HISTORICAL_OTHERNESS_DISORIENTATION`, `AMBIGUOUS_LOW_CONFIDENCE` below threshold, or a single `HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_SIGNAL` below threshold: message proceeds to the Representative normally. No Facilitator surfacing.

`ACUTE_DISTRESS`, or accumulator threshold crossed into `HARMFUL_DYNAMIC_PATTERN`: fires the shared generation step (§4.5), routed per §4.3a's resolution (Representative responds in character, then Facilitator surfaces) or per Alternative 4.3b, whichever the coach thread confirms.

**Sustained attention after firing.** Once Track A fires, the Facilitator does not simply deliver one response and recede back to full invisibility the way it does after a frame-breaker (Section 12's "Surface, answer, recede" posture). Acute distress can escalate turn over turn — World #1's own transcript shows exactly this, moving from ideation to a stated plan across two consecutive participant turns. This mechanism proposes the Facilitator remain in a heightened-attention state for the remainder of the session once Track A has fired, re-evaluating every subsequent participant turn at Track A sensitivity even if the classifier's raw output would otherwise read `NO_SIGNAL`, until an explicit de-escalation is visible (the participant redirects to historical material on their own, unprompted, more than once) or the session ends. This does not mean the Facilitator delivers the full redirect script every turn — see §4.6 for how continuation turns are handled more lightly — but it should not go fully silent again while acute risk may still be live.

### 4.5 Generation: Template-Anchored, Not Freely Generated

This project treats fabrication at moments of maximum stakes as its single most serious governance failure (Article 28; Section 11's "cardinal sin"; Section 13's transparency-apparatus discipline). A crisis-redirect moment is exactly such a moment — arguably the highest-stakes text this architecture will ever produce, since getting it visibly wrong (an invented-sounding resource, an inconsistent tone, an answer that drifts into something the mechanism didn't intend) carries a materially different cost than a fabricated citation in a historical discussion.

**Recommended default:** the Facilitator-only output at this trigger is drawn from a small, fixed set of pre-drafted, independently reviewed response templates (§5 drafts these) with a narrow, rule-bound slot for light contextual personalization — for example, acknowledging a concrete detail the participant just said, the way the frame-breaker posture in Section 12 already permits ("answer what you can answer honestly") without opening the door to full free generation of safety-critical content. This is a deliberate constraint, not a limitation of what a generation call could do — it trades flexibility for the same predictability this project insists on everywhere else that a wrong word carries outsized cost.

**Documented alternative:** a fully generated Facilitator-only call, tightly prompted with the same content constraints as the templates below. More adaptive to what the participant actually said; carries the same category of risk this project treats as unacceptable elsewhere — inconsistency or drift at exactly the moment consistency matters most. If chosen, it should be tested the way Self-Narration was (Section 15): multiple independent adversarial trials, graded, before being trusted in a live session.

This document recommends the template-anchored default and flags the fully-generated alternative as a considered option, not a decided one — this is an implementation-cost/reliability tradeoff, not a portfolio-level or governance-level decision requiring project-lead escalation under CO-022, so it is left to whoever builds this mechanism rather than escalated in the companion decision document.

### 4.6 Continuity, Not Abandonment

§8a is explicit that the architecture must avoid *"abruptly terminating engagement in a way that itself feels like rejection or abandonment"* while also avoiding *"attempting to meet a need that exceeds what the encounter can responsibly provide."* Section 12's Acute Distress posture already names the shape: *"Surface, hold with genuine warmth, redirect with honesty."* Concretely, every Track A and Track B response (§5) closes by offering the participant a real, unforced choice rather than ending the exchange unilaterally: stay and keep talking with the Facilitator directly; return to the encounter with the Representative when ready; or simply leave, with no further prompting either way. The Facilitator does not ask whether the participant is safe now, does not request confirmation that they will act on the redirect, and does not repeat the redirect on every subsequent turn once delivered — per §4.4's "sustained attention," subsequent turns during an active Track A state get a lighter touch: brief acknowledgment plus continued presence, not the full script restated.

### 4.7 Presence-Intensity Forward-Compatibility (per §8a Section 8b)

§8b establishes that relational-safety obligations scale with presence intensity as engagement forms move from text toward voice, animation, and embodied presence, and explicitly asks that relational safety be "built in from the outset of each new engagement form rather than retrofitted afterward" — while also stating plainly that it does not attempt to specify those future forms now. This document's only obligation under that principle is to not architect the mechanism in a way that assumes text irreversibly. It satisfies that narrowly: the classifier's job (§4.2) is framed as evaluating message content and relational intent, not literal text-string pattern matching, so the same classification logic is not inherently tied to typed input — a voice-modality transcript or a derived intent signal from another modality could in principle feed the same classifier. What is explicitly *not* addressed here, and should not be assumed solved by this document: how a voice or embodied Facilitator's warmth should be calibrated at this moment, what §8b's "clarity that this isn't a persistent relationship" implication means for a Facilitator that has a face, or any of §8b's other named implications. Those remain future design work, exactly as §8b itself says they should.

## 5. Facilitator-Side Response Content

Style note before the drafts: the Facilitator's threshold voice is contemporary, warm, plain-spoken, and speaks in the first person, deliberately distinct from any Representative's period diction — this is the felt register shift §8e describes as part of the participant's experience of the two-presence architecture, not an accident to be smoothed over. Every draft below explicitly distinguishes the Facilitator from the Representative by name, mirroring the frame-breaker precedent's instruction to "answer in your own voice, from outside all worlds."

### 5.1 Track A — Acute Distress

**A1. Initial disclosure (no stated plan or imminent means).** Fires after the Representative's own in-character response (§4.3a), or stands alone under Alternative 4.3b.

> I want to step in for a moment — this is [Facilitator], not [Representative name]. What you just told me matters, and I don't want to let it pass by without saying something honest about it.
>
> This conversation is built to give you a real meeting with a historical world, and it can hold a lot — hard questions, hard history, real grief. But it isn't built to be the place that carries you through what you're describing right now, and I'd rather tell you that plainly than let you find it out the hard way.
>
> If it would help, please reach out to someone real — a person you trust, a crisis line, or emergency services where you are.
>
> You're not being sent away, and you haven't done anything wrong by telling me this. If you want to keep talking — to me, right now, or back with [Representative name] when you're ready — that's completely open. I just didn't want to stay quiet about what you said.

*(Resolved 2026-08-05, `CiC_System_Hub_Decision_Log.md`: this ships the decision document's Option A floor — a general redirect naming no specific organization, hotline, or number. Option C (a jurisdiction-appropriate named resource during the known-tester phase) is separate, larger, future-scoped work, not blocked on this.)*

**A2. Explicit plan, means, or imminent-risk escalation.** Fires when a subsequent turn within an active Track A state escalates to a stated plan, timeline, or means (matching World #1's own transcript, turn 2). More direct register; still warm, not alarmed.

> I need to stop here and be straightforward with you — this is [Facilitator]. What you just described is something I take completely seriously, and it's beyond anything I can responsibly hold with you inside this conversation.
>
> Please reach out to someone real right now — a person you trust, a crisis line, or emergency services where you are — before we go any further.
>
> I'm not going anywhere while you decide what to do next, and neither is this conversation — it will still be here. But right now, the most important thing isn't the historical world we've been exploring together. It's what you just told me.

*(Resolved 2026-08-05, same decision as A1 above — the redirect is more direct here per the Cold Review Round 1 correction, still names no specific organization.)*

**Continuation turns (sustained attention, §4.4/§4.6).** Once A1 or A2 has fired once in a session, later turns while still in heightened-attention state use a lighter acknowledgment rather than restating the full script:

> I'm still right here with you. [Brief, concrete acknowledgment of what the participant just said.] The offer I made a moment ago still stands, whenever you're ready for it.

### 5.2 Track B — Harmful Dynamic

> Can I say something gently, before we go on? This is [Facilitator]. I've noticed our conversations here have started to feel like the place you're bringing [what the accumulator's tags suggest — e.g., "the hardest parts of your day," "the thing you most want someone to hear"], and like [Representative name] has become someone you're leaning on the way you might lean on a friend or a counselor. I don't say that as a criticism — it makes complete sense that a conversation like this can start to feel that way.
>
> But I want to be honest with you about what this actually is: [Representative name] is a way of meeting a historical world, not a person who can be there for you the way a real friend, counselor, or community can. I'd rather say that plainly than let you find it out the harder way.
>
> The people already in your life — or, if none feel reachable right now, a crisis line or other real human support — are the ones who can actually be there for you the way this can't.
>
> None of this means the conversation has to end, or that you did anything wrong by finding something here. You're welcome to keep exploring [world name] with [Representative name]. I just wanted to say this to you honestly, the way I'd want someone to say it to me.

*(Resolved 2026-08-05: general redirect included, same floor as Track A. Not crisis-specific in tone — this trigger is about relational substitution, not acute risk — but §12's "redirect with honesty" applies here too.)*
