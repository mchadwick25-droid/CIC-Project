# S6.2 M decision — fleet migration order

**Decided by Mark, 2026-07-27: ALEXANDRIA FIRST** (the cost-weighted
recommendation — largest corpus, largest R7 retrieval-economics win;
the ≈$0.020 → ≈$0.0012 headline becomes measurable for real).

Remaining order (PAHC, Syriac, Hieronymian, Imperial-Juridical) to be
decided as each world completes — Mark picks per completion, not all
at once.

**Decided by Mark, 2026-07-28, world 3: SYRIAC** — picked on size,
complexity, and maturity, per Mark's own criteria, checked directly
rather than assumed:

- **Size:** 9 lexicon terms, ties Desert exactly — the smallest of the
  four remaining candidates, nowhere near Alexandria's 45.
- **Complexity:** real but already-resolved — spans the Roman/Persian
  frontier, a strand-singular determination and three named boundary-
  case figures (Bardaisan, Marcion, Mani) all argued through in the
  source documents already, not complexity the migration has to
  discover fresh. PAHC was ruled out here specifically: its lexicon is
  embedded in Doc06 prose rather than discrete chunk files, a format
  mismatch against the migration tooling that risks unpredictable
  tooling-friction cost on top of content cost.
- **Maturity:** the most reviewed world in the project — 50 review
  artifacts (vs. 25–28 for the other three), with visible multi-round
  self-correction on its own hardest call (Doc_01's strand
  determination: Round 1 wrong, Round 2's fix itself found overstated,
  Round 3 correct) — the profile most likely to surface few new
  discoveries during S2.1a/S2.1b, not many.

**Context this decision was made under (Session Contract note six):**
this is explicitly a budget-measurement run, not a confirmed-sufficient
one — Fable's own weekly allocation sat at 82% used (of its up-to-half-
of-total cap) when this was decided. If it completes, the actual
per-world cost becomes plannable for the last two worlds. If it
doesn't, a clean rule-7 stop and a Friday resume is the expected,
accepted outcome — not a failure to avoid at the cost of rigor.
