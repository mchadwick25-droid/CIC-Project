# S6.2 — length-ceiling retry: cost investigation and options memo

**Date:** 2026-07-31. **Branch:** `retry-cost-investigation-2026-07-31`.
**Subject:** the buffered first-draft / silent-single-regeneration mechanism in
`stream_representative_turn` (`cic-poc/backend/app/graph/nodes.py`, the
`HARD_CEILING_WORLDS` branch).

**Verdict up front.** The measurement is done and it settles the disputed
number: **23.9% of a Desert conversation's spend is retry spend, and 38.8% is
the same fact scoped to `main_response` only. The 13.6% figure is wrong.** The
mechanism is doing its job and doing it well — on the measured evidence I
recommend **not** changing its protective behaviour. Every cost-reduction
option I found that is worth real money also changes the words a participant
reads, which is Mark's call, not mine. So the code shipped on this branch is
**observability only, provably behaviour-identical**; the cost options below
are a memo awaiting a decision, not an implementation.

## 1. What was measured, and how to re-derive it

Source: the committed raw log
`Build/Ministry/Technology/Pass2/baselines/cost_baseline_2026-07_raw.jsonl` (733
records; run 2026-07-26 against the live system; 40 participant turns across 4
fixed conversations). Everything below re-derives offline with no API key:

```
cic-poc/backend/venv/bin/python cic-poc/backend/scripts/cost_baseline_runner.py \
    --report Build/Ministry/Technology/Pass2/baselines/cost_baseline_2026-07_raw.jsonl
cic-poc/backend/venv/bin/python Build/Ministry/Technology/Pass2/gates/S6.2_length_ceiling_observability_gate.py
```

The raw log records neither "this call was a retry" nor "this call was world
X". Both had to be recovered. Both recoveries are now code
(`split_retry_calls`, `attribute_worlds` in `cost_baseline_runner.py`) and both
are asserted by the gate, so nobody has to take my word for either.

### 1a. Identifying a retry — the +51 signature

A retry re-sends the identical prompt plus the discarded draft plus the
corrective message. So the second call's `input_tokens` must exceed the
first's by exactly *the first call's `output_tokens`* (the draft, re-tokenised)
plus the corrective's own token cost, and both calls must read the **identical
cached prefix**.

Across the whole log that surplus is **exactly 51 tokens on all 15 detected
retries** — one value, no spread (gate check 2c). The detected count is also
flat at 15 across surplus windows of 51, 80, 120, 250 and 400 tokens (gate
check 2b), so the detector is not tuned to the corrective's current wording.

### 1b. Identifying the speaker — cached-prefix arithmetic

A representative's cached prefix is its own static system prompt, optionally
plus one conditionally-present guidance block that carries its own cache
breakpoint (`_cached_system_message`). Each single-world conversation therefore
pins exactly one world's static size:

| world | static prefix (tokens) | pinned by |
|---|---|---|
| post-apostolic-house-church | 14,453 | C1 (all 8 calls identical) |
| desert-monasticism | 13,718 | C2 (all 18 calls identical) |
| alexandria-catechetical | 15,737 | C3 (all 10 calls identical) |
| syriac-edessa-nisibis | 15,103 | residual (see below) |

Every prefix in the 3-world C4 decomposes as one of those statics plus 0, 374
or 2,982:

`14,827 = HC+374` · `17,435 = HC+2982` · `14,092 = Desert+374` ·
`16,700 = Desert+2982` · `15,477 = 15,103+374` · `18,085 = 15,103+2982`

374 and 2,982 are real, shared guidance blocks, not coincidences: measured
directly against `app/prompts/table_discourse.py`,
`OPENING_TURN_LARGE_TABLE_GUIDANCE` is 1,390 chars (≈386 tokens) and
`REACTIVE_TURN_GUIDANCE` is 10,516 chars (≈2,921 tokens). The two leftover
prefixes both reduce to a single unexplained static of 15,103, and Syriac is
the single world in the set not otherwise pinned — hence the assignment. All
64 `main_response` calls attribute; none are left unknown (gate check 3a).

**Independent corroboration:** this attribution yields 21 representative turns
in C4 across 8 participant turns. `app/message_cap.py`'s
`CONVERSATION_TURN_CAP = 20` counts *representative* turns, and the run notes
record C4's turns 9–10 as cap turns with zero LLM calls. A count of 21 is
exactly what makes the cap land where it landed. A miscount of 19 or 23 would
not.

## 2. Three of the four worlds are not measured by this log at all — and a zero here does not mean zero

This is the single most important caveat in the document, and it is the reason
no prior analysis produced numbers for the other worlds.

- **`hieronymian-ascetic-literary` is not in the fixed conversation set.** It
  is never seated in any of the four conversations. The log contains literally
  zero evidence about it. Its 180 @ 1.2 ceiling has never been measured in
  production traffic by anything committed to this repo.

- **`alexandria-catechetical` and `syriac-edessa-nisibis` were not in
  `HARD_CEILING_WORLDS` when the log was collected.** The run notes date the
  run 2026-07-26. Alexandria's `160 @ 1.2` was set in commit `83eeda8`
  (2026-07-28) and Syriac's `165 @ 1.2` in `16cab54` (2026-07-28) — two days
  *after* the run. Their 0% is an artefact of the mechanism not existing for
  them yet, not a measurement of it.

  The data says the same thing independently. Converting output tokens to
  words with a measured estimator (3.32 chars/token from 20 single-
  representative turns; 5.27 chars/word from 68 committed representative turns
  in the TRRs — so words ≈ tokens × 0.629):

  | world | first-draft words in this log | its trigger | over trigger |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | alexandria-catechetical (solo) | 217–299, median 259 | 192 | **10/10** |
  | syriac-edessa-nisibis (at the table) | 303–350, median 314 | 198 | **5/5** |

  Every one of those turns would have fired a retry had the ceiling been in
  force. It was not. That is the whole explanation of their zero.

**Self-correction, recorded rather than hidden:** my first pass estimated
Alexandria's solo turns at 218–470 words from `streamed_chars`. The 470 is
wrong for this purpose — C3 turn 1 also streamed a
`modern_term_bridge_facilitator_turn` (256 output tokens of *Facilitator*
prose) into the same turn, which `streamed_chars` cannot separate from the
representative's own words. The 217–299 range above is derived per-call from
the `main_response` row alone and is the figure to use.

**Consequence for the record:** the deployed ALX ceiling comment states
"Ceiling 160 = the cleared solo register's own measured max
(alexvoice001.native_measure: responses 123–166, mean ~141) **so solo answers
never trigger**." On 2026-07-26 the deployed app's solo Alexandria turns ran
217–299 words — well above the 192 trigger and well above the record's stated
123–166 native measure. If that gap survived the two 2026-07-28 fix sessions,
the ALX ceiling is firing on single-world Deep Interview turns, which the
design explicitly did not intend, at an unbudgeted cost and with a silent
compression of solo answers nobody decided to apply. **I cannot settle this
offline** — the deployed Theon prompt was amended twice after the run, and a
post-fix solo measurement does not exist. It is a named, testable hypothesis
and it is the first thing the re-run in §5 will answer.

## 3. The disputed cost figure, settled

**The correct figures are 23.9% and 38.8%. They are not two competing
measurements — they are one measurement at two scopes. The 13.6% is wrong.**

Derivation for `C2_desert`, standard rates, using the runner's own `dollars()`
(which correctly subtracts cache-read and cache-creation tokens back out of
LangChain's inflated `input_tokens` before pricing the uncached span):

| rowset | n | $ std |
|---|---|---|
| C2 all calls (whole conversation) | 153 | 0.5964 |
| C2 `main_response` only | 18 | 0.3664 |
| — of which first drafts | 10 | 0.2241 |
| — of which **ceiling retries** | **8** | **0.1423** |

- retry ÷ whole conversation = 0.1423 ÷ 0.5964 = **23.86% → 23.9%**
- retry ÷ `main_response` only = 0.1423 ÷ 0.3664 = **38.83% → 38.8%**

The marginal definition is the right one: without the mechanism the first
draft would simply have streamed, so the extra spend the mechanism causes is
the second call, not both.

**Why 13.6% must be wrong, without needing to know how it was produced.** The
same analysis reported 13.6% (whole-conversation) *and* 38.8%
(`main_response`-scoped). Those two can only both hold if `main_response` were
0.136 ÷ 0.388 = **35.1%** of C2's spend. It is measurably **61.4%**
($0.3664 ÷ $0.5964). Run the consistency the other way and 38.8% × 61.4% =
**23.8%** — which lands on the first analysis's 23.9% to within rounding. The
second analysis's own companion figure therefore corroborates the first
analysis's headline and refutes its own. This is asserted as gate check 4d.

I could not reproduce 13.6% from any natural formula over the raw log (I swept
numerator/denominator combinations across scopes, pricing tables, token-vs-
dollar bases, per-turn averaging, and naive cache-double-counting; nothing
landed within 0.4pp). With the correct $0.1423 numerator, 13.6% implies a
denominator of **$1.0464** against C2's real $0.5964 — i.e. a denominator
roughly 1.75× too large, of the size you would get by summing two
conversations' totals. That diagnosis is inference and is labelled as such;
the refutation in the paragraph above is not, and does not depend on it.

**Wider scopes, for proportion:**

| scope | retry spend | total | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| C2_desert (solo Desert) | $0.1423 | $0.5964 | 23.9% |
| C4 (3-world table incl. Desert) | $0.1765 | $1.5846 | 11.1% |
| whole 40-turn baseline | $0.3188 | $3.3924 | **9.40%** |
| whole baseline, `main_response` only | $0.3188 | $1.9482 | 16.36% |

Per Desert participant turn this is **$0.0142 of $0.0596**. In absolute terms
the whole mechanism cost **$0.32 across a 40-turn baseline**.

### Where a retry's dollars actually go

This decomposition decides which options in §4 are worth anything:

| component | $ | share of retry spend |
|---|---|---|
| uncached input (dynamic prompt + transcript + discarded draft) | 0.2282 | **71.6%** |
| cached-prefix reads/writes | 0.0664 | 20.8% |
| output (the shortened answer the participant reads) | 0.0242 | 7.6% |

Mean retry: 14,762 cached-prefix tokens, **5,071 uncached input tokens**, 107
output tokens. **Nearly three-quarters of the cost is re-sending context the
model has already been given once in the same turn.** Shortening the retry's
*output* can never save more than 7.6% of it.

Separately, the discarded first drafts throw away 3,468 output tokens =
**$0.0520**, which is 1.53% of whole-baseline spend.

## 4. Is the mechanism working? Yes — measurably

Before proposing anything, the honest question is whether the money buys
anything. It does.

`C2_desert`, ceiling 60, trigger 90 (draft words estimated per §2; final words
measured directly from `streamed_chars`, which is exact for these turns because
only the representative streamed):

| turn | draft words (est) | retried | final words (measured) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 91 | YES | 57 |
| 2 | 67 | no | 65 |
| 3 | 123 | YES | 42 |
| 4 | 92 | no | 83 |
| 5 | 115 | YES | 36 |
| 6 | 155 | YES | 90 |
| 7 | 159 | YES | 69 |
| 8 | 189 | YES | 82 |
| 9 | 106 | YES | 53 |
| 10 | 142 | YES | 33 |

- Post-retry lengths: 33, 36, 42, 53, 57, 69, 82, 90. **0 of 8 remain over the
  90-word trigger; 5 of 8 reach the 60-word ceiling outright.**
- The two turns that never fired measured 65 and 83 words — both genuinely
  under the trigger. The mechanism is not firing spuriously.
- The 189-word draft at turn 8 independently corroborates the freeze record's
  "Papnoute's uncorrected drafts landed around 175–180 words."

Against the acceptance bar in the gate reports — Alexandria 82%→70% dominance,
Syriac 79%→63% with table turns falling from 1,053 words to 139–246 — this is a
mechanism that is earning its cost. The design record is also explicit that the
cheap alternatives were already tried and failed: the static prompt's own
wording, then a `check_length_ceiling`-queued correction for the *next* turn,
both tested, both insufficient under real multi-world topical pressure. The
fleet-level investigation names the general root — "a finding is detected,
queued, delivered once, and no mechanism checks whether the next turn
complied… `HARD_CEILING_WORLDS` exists precisely because advisory guidance did
not bind on length."

**A 79% fire rate is therefore not evidence of a broken mechanism. It is
evidence that for Desert the ceiling and what a reactive turn is required to do
are in genuine structural conflict, exactly as the code comment says.** The
mechanism is not an exception path; for Desert it is the normal path, and
Desert turns cost ~1.79 generations each by design.

## 5. Options for reducing the cost — each with its dominance-reintroduction risk

Ordered by (money saved) ÷ (risk). None are implemented. Every one that saves
real money changes the words a participant reads.

### Option A — restrict what the retry re-sends (saves up to ~70% of retry spend)

The retry re-sends the full dynamic prompt: retrieved lexicon/story context,
the reroot correction, the transcript. But the retry is a *restatement* of a
draft it is being shown verbatim — the substance is already in the draft. Drop
the dynamic (uncached) portion from the retry's system message only, keeping
the cached static prompt so the voice and the world's discipline are fully
intact and the cache prefix stays valid.

- **Money:** targets the 71.6% uncached-input slice. Best case ≈ $0.10 of the
  $0.32 baseline; ~7% of whole-baseline spend if the fleet looks like Desert.
- **Dominance risk: essentially none.** Output stays bounded by the same
  ceiling logic; nothing about the trigger or the ceiling moves. This cannot
  push Theon back toward 77–82%.
- **Quality risk: real, and it is a fabrication risk, not a length risk.**
  Stripping the retrieved evidence from the call that produces the
  *participant-visible* text means the regeneration is no longer grounded in
  the records at the moment it speaks. In a project whose whole discipline is
  record-grounded speech and whose freeze batteries specifically test claim-
  laundering and parroting, that is not a side effect to accept quietly. It
  needs the ALX/SYR freeze batteries re-run as the regression standard.
- **Cheaper, weaker variant:** keep retrieval, drop only the transcript. Less
  saving, less risk. Needs the same battery.

### Option B — a smaller model for the retry pass only (saves ~2/3 of retry spend)

"Say this again, shorter" is a compression task; Haiku is ~3× cheaper on both
input and output.

- **Money:** ≈ $0.21 of the $0.32 baseline.
- **Dominance risk: low.** Length compliance is easy; if anything a smaller
  model will hold the ceiling tighter.
- **Quality risk: high and unmeasured, and it lands on the visible turn.**
  The retry's output *is* what the participant reads on ~79% of Desert turns.
  Every voice, parroting, pushback, anachronism and held-position battery in
  S5.6/S6.2 was run against Sonnet. Swapping the model for the majority of a
  world's visible speech invalidates that entire evidence base until re-run.
  My honest read: the saving is real but this is the option most likely to
  quietly degrade voice fidelity, which is the thing the whole build exists to
  protect.

### Option C — abort the discarded draft early (saves ~1.5%, plus real latency)

The first draft is buffered, never streamed. Once its running word count
crosses the trigger, it is already certain to be discarded — the stream could
break there instead of generating to completion.

- **Money:** small. The discarded drafts are $0.0520 total (1.53% of baseline)
  and an early abort recovers only part of that, plus a little of the retry's
  input (the draft is re-sent).
- **Latency:** this is the real prize. A ceilinged turn currently costs the
  participant two full generations of waiting before the first token appears.
- **Dominance risk: none.**
- **Quality risk: real but modest.** The retry would be shown a truncated
  draft and told to "say the same thing again" — it may omit content the
  truncated draft had not yet reached. Needs a short battery, not a full one.

### Option D — few-shot length calibration in the prompt (saves by firing less often)

The record shows advisory *instruction* was tested twice and failed. It does
not show that a worked **exemplar** — an actual in-voice answer at the ceiling
— was ever tried. Different mechanism: exemplars calibrate length far more
reliably than numeric instructions.

- **Money:** potentially the largest of all, because it attacks frequency
  rather than unit cost. Every avoided retry saves a whole call.
- **Dominance risk: none — it moves in the protective direction.** Shorter
  first drafts *are* the goal the ceiling serves.
- **Quality risk: real and specific.** An exemplar in the prompt is a
  parroting vector, and parroting is measured at near-zero (mean .005–.006)
  across both freeze batteries — a number this build has worked hard for.
  Needs the parroting battery specifically.
- **Cannot be evaluated offline at all.** It is an empirical question about
  live generation. Requires an API key and a re-run.

### Option E — widen the trigger multiples

**Rejected, and I am not putting a number on it.** This is the one move that
directly trades away the protection the mechanism exists to provide, and the
gate reports are the acceptance bar. Note also that the trigger was *tightened*
to 1.2 for Albina/Theon/Yausep on measured evidence precisely because 1.5 left
them permanently in the dead zone. Widening would undo a fix that was already
made deliberately, in the wrong direction, to make a cost number look better.

### Option F — streaming truncation instead of regeneration

**Rejected.** Cutting a representative mid-sentence is a straightforward
quality regression, and the code's existing comment on empty streams already
records how badly malformed representative text corrupts later turns.

## 6. What was implemented on this branch, and why only this

Not a cost fix. **The instrument that makes any of §5 decidable.**

Every option above needs the same input, and nobody has it for 3 of the 4
worlds: a per-world distribution of *first-draft* word counts, plus the real
fire rate and dead-zone rate on ordinary traffic. Today the only route to those
numbers is the token forensics in §1 — which works, but only for a log
collected while the world was already ceilinged, and only because the
corrective happens to cost a constant 51 tokens.

Committed here (all purely additive, participant-visible behaviour proved
unchanged):

1. **`cic-poc/backend/app/length_ceiling_logging.py`** — a dedicated
   `cic.length_ceiling` logger, mirroring `usage_logging.py` and
   `over_settling_logging.py` exactly. One structured line per ceilinged turn
   carrying world, ceiling, trigger multiple, first-draft word count, outcome,
   and retry word count.

2. **Three call sites in `stream_representative_turn`**, covering all three
   outcomes — including the **under-ceiling** case, which was never logged at
   all and is why the fire rate has had no denominator in any committed
   artefact. The existing `print()` lines are left byte-for-byte alone. The
   **dead-zone** branch — deliberately uncorrected, and present because a prior
   Opus review asked for observability there *before* anyone touches the
   trigger again — now emits a line something can actually count, instead of a
   `print()` to stdout that no harness captures.

3. **`scripts/cost_baseline_runner.py`**: captures the new lines into the raw
   log as a `length_ceiling` record kind; `--report` gains a Length-ceiling
   section that works both from those records and, for older logs, from the
   forensic detector; and it now prints an explicit warning that a 0% fire rate
   in a record-less log **cannot** distinguish "stayed under trigger" from "the
   world had no ceiling yet" — the exact trap §2 describes.

4. **`--run` mode repaired.** It has been broken since the session-ownership
   change: `/api/session/{id}/message/stream` now requires the
   `X-Session-Token` minted by `/api/session/start`, and the runner never sent
   it, so every turn 403s. **The B-COST baseline has not been re-runnable.**
   Fixed here (2 lines).

5. **`gates/S6.2_length_ceiling_observability_gate.py`** + committed run
   output — 22 checks, all green, offline, no API key.

**Verification performed:**

- Gate: **22 passed, 0 failed** (`gates/S6.2_length_ceiling_gate_run.txt`).
- **Differential behaviour test:** the real `stream_representative_turn` driven
  with a stub LLM through all three branches, on this branch and on `main`,
  produced **identical** participant-visible output — under-ceiling 40w draft →
  40w first draft; dead-zone 77w → 77w first draft; retried 175w → 58w retry
  text. Exactly one structured line per turn.
- `--report` on the committed raw log reproduces every pre-existing section
  **byte-for-byte**; the new section is strictly appended (verified against the
  pre-change committed report at implementation time, then the report
  regenerated). *Self-correction: the gate initially asserted this against the
  committed report file and correctly failed once I regenerated that file; the
  check was rewritten to assert the determinism contract that actually holds
  going forward.*
- `S1.1a_instrumentation_gate.py`: still **PASS, 38/38 sites, 0 uninstrumented**.
- pyflakes: warning set on `nodes.py` **identical** before and after (5 pre-
  existing warnings, none in the changed region); no new warnings anywhere.

**Also found, not fixed (out of scope, flagged):** the S4.1 replay-parity suite
(`app/graph/replay/parity_suite.py replay`) is **red on `main`** for the same
session-token reason — it 403s on its first case. It is the natural harness for
verifying any of §5's changes and it should be repaired before that work
starts.

## 7. What is needed from Mark

**Nothing on this branch is a product decision.** It is instrumentation; it
changes no prompt, no model, no ceiling, no trigger, and no word any
participant reads. It wants an ordinary code review.

The decisions are these, in order:

1. **Authorise a B-COST re-run** (needs an API key; `--run` now works again).
   One 40-turn run produces the per-world first-draft distributions, the real
   fire rates, and the dead-zone frequency for every ceilinged world at once.
   Without it, §5 cannot be chosen between on evidence, and three of four
   worlds stay unmeasured.

2. **Consider adding `hieronymian-ascetic-literary` to
   `scripts/cost_baseline_conversations.json`** before that run — or accept
   that its 180 @ 1.2 ceiling stays permanently unmeasured. Note the tension:
   that file is marked FIXED because the baseline's comparability depends on it
   not changing. Adding a fifth conversation rather than editing the four
   preserves comparability and is my suggestion, but it is Mark's file.

3. **Then, and only then, pick from §5.** My recommendation if forced today:
   **Option A (restrict the retry's context), gated on the ALX and SYR freeze
   batteries re-run as the regression standard.** It targets the 71.6% slice,
   carries no dominance risk, and its one real hazard — an ungrounded
   regeneration — is precisely what those batteries already test for. Option B
   saves more and risks the voice; Option D could save the most and cannot be
   evaluated at all without live runs.

4. **Answer the Alexandria solo-register question in §2** — is the ALX ceiling
   firing on single-world Deep Interview turns, against its own stated design
   intent? The re-run answers it for free.

**And the thing not to do:** do not widen the trigger multiples to make the
23.9% smaller. That number is the price of a fix that measurably moved
dominance from 82% to 70% and from 79% to 63%, and moved Syriac's table turns
from 1,053 words to 139–246. It is a fair price. If it must come down, it
should come down through §5's unit-cost work or through better first drafts —
not by letting the representatives dominate the table again.
