## The tradition
- Name: Gallic Monastic-Ascetic Christianity
- Era: 360-450
- Region: Roman Gaul (Tours, Lérins, Marseilles)

This was a monastic movement in Roman Gaul, now France, from about 360 to 450. It began with Martin, who founded communities near Tours. It continued on the island of Lérins and at Marseilles, where Cassian founded houses for men and women. Its people were monks, abbots and future bishops writing in Latin. They adapted Egyptian desert teaching to Gaul. They argued over grace and human effort, and Vincent set out a test for judging church teaching. Whether the northern and southern groups form one tradition is unsettled. The year 450 is a working limit; the real shift came in the 470s, when bishops took over the debate.
