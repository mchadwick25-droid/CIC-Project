## The tradition
- Name: Imperial and Juridical Christianity
- Era: 312-451
- Region: Rome, Constantinople and Milan

This tradition tells how Christianity became tied to the power of the Roman state. It begins in 312, when Constantine won a battle and then favored the church, and it ends at the Council of Chalcedon in 451. Its people were emperors, court officials and bishops in Rome, Constantinople and Milan, not ordinary church members. They argued over who had the last word in the church: the emperor, a council of bishops, or the bishop of Rome. Rome claimed authority from Peter, Constantinople from nearness to the emperor, and Ambrose of Milan from a bishop's spiritual power over rulers.
