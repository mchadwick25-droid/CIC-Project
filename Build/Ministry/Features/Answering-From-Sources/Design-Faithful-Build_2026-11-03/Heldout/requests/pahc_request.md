## The tradition
- Name: Post-Apostolic House-Church Christianity
- Era: 70-200
- Region: Antioch/Syria, western Asia Minor, and Rome

This is the Greek-speaking Christianity of the generation after the apostles, from about 70 to 200 CE. It lived in Antioch, western Asia Minor, and Rome, all inside the Roman Empire. Its people met in private homes, and the household was the basic unit of church life. With no central office, letters and travelling messengers held far-apart groups together. Members were formed through baptism, a shared meal, and a simple teaching about two ways of living. Leadership moved unevenly from groups of elders toward a single bishop. The tradition is bounded around 200, when bishops had become the norm and writers like Irenaeus and Tertullian began building systematic arguments.
