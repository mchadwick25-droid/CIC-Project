## The tradition
- Name: Syriac Christianity
- Era: 200-410
- Region: Edessa and Nisibis, on the Roman-Persian frontier in upper Mesopotamia

Syriac Christianity grew in Edessa and Nisibis, in upper Mesopotamia, from about 200 to 410. Its people spoke Syriac, a form of Aramaic, and lived along the frontier between the Roman and Persian empires. They read the gospel as one continuous story, in a harmony made by Tatian. Their theology came through hymns and symbols, most famously in Ephrem, who died in 373. Celibate men and women called sons and daughters of the covenant shaped community life. The tradition defined itself against rivals such as Marcion, Bardaisan, and Mani. It is bounded in 410, when a synod organized the Persian churches into one formal church structure.
