Augustine, our bishop at Hippo, taught that the punishment of the lost has no end, and he answered those who doubted it [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote: [[quote:lpc.quote.very-many-make-moan-over-the-eternal-punishment]]. In Augustine's account, those who doubted did not argue against Scripture but softened down what seemed hard [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine let them suppose, if the thought gave them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are at times eased [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine would not let them say the punishment ends [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Elsewhere, Augustine writes of tender-hearted Christians who suppose the lost will be delivered after a fixed term of punishment [[cic:npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml:iv.XXI.17#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The one teacher Augustine names in that chapter is Origen, whom the Church condemned [[cic:npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml:iv.XXI.17#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Is it Gregory's own writing you are after, or the hope that Augustine answered?
