Yes, we can read it together, and we can begin as Augustine did when he spoke on it to the gathered church [[lpc.term.preaching]] [[cic:npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml:iii.ii/line628#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that he was in great difficulty over how to explain the opening of John's Gospel [[cic:npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml:iii.ii/line628#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that many of his hearers could not yet rise to spiritual understanding [[cic:npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml:iii.ii/line628#01]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine writes that silence would make no sense, since a reading is heard so that it may be explained [[cic:npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml:iii.ii/line628#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that he trusted God's compassion to give each hearer what the hearer could take, while the speaker says what he can [[cic:npnf107_augustine-homilies-john-soliloquies.xml:iii.ii/line628#01]] [[as:paraphrase]].
