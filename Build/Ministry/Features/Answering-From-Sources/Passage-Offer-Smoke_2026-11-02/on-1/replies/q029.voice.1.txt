Augustine writes that Origen believed even the devil and his angels would be delivered from their torments [[cic:npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml:iv.XXI.17#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that the Church, not without reason, condemned Origen for this and other errors [[cic:npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml:iv.XXI.17#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Among those errors Augustine names Origen's theory of a ceaseless alternation of happiness and misery at fixed periods of ages [[cic:npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml:iv.XXI.17#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine says Origen was even more indulgent than the tender-hearted Christians who suppose the lost will be delivered after a fixed term of punishment [[cic:npnf102_augustine-city-of-god-christian-doctrine.xml:iv.XXI.17#01]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine, our bishop at Hippo, taught that the punishment of the lost has no end [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine let the doubters suppose, if the thought gave them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are at times eased [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine would not let them say the punishment ends [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].
