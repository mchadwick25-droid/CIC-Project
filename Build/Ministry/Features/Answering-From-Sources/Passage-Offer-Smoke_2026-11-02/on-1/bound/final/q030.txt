Augustine, our bishop at Hippo, taught that the punishment of the lost has no end, and he answered those who doubted it. Augustine wrote: “So it is in vain that some, indeed very many, lament the eternal punishment and the endless, unbroken torments of the lost. They say they do not believe it will be so. They do not set themselves directly against Holy Scripture. But, prompted by their own feelings, they soften everything that seems hard. They give a milder sense to sayings they think were meant more to frighten than to be taken as literally true.”

In Augustine's account, those who doubted did not argue against Scripture but softened down what seemed hard. Augustine let them suppose, if the thought gave them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are at times eased. Augustine would not let them say the punishment ends.
