Record lpc.witness.punishment-without-end [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 391 to 430]
(1) Augustine taught that the punishment of the lost has no end.
(2) After the final judgment, he wrote, there are two kingdoms.
(3) The life of one and the death of the other "shall both be without end." The lost suffer in degrees, "one being more endurably miserable than another." Many around him found this hard to bear.
(4) He wrote that "some, indeed very many, make moan over the eternal punishment." He said they did not argue against Scripture.
(5) They "soften down everything that seems hard." He called their moan "in vain." He let them suppose, "if the thought gives them pleasure," that the pains of the damned are at times eased.
(6) He would not let them say the punishment ends.
(7) God's mercy, he said, is shown "not in putting an end to their eternal punishment." It is shown, he said, "in mitigating, or in granting them a respite from, their torments." In the next chapter he wrote that the death of the wicked "shall abide for ever." Augustine taught that the punishment of the lost has no end.
(8) After the final judgment, he wrote, there are two kingdoms.
(9) The life of one and the death of the other "shall both be without end." The lost suffer in degrees, he said, "one being more endurably miserable than another." Many around him found this hard to bear.
(10) He wrote that "some, indeed very many, make moan over the eternal punishment." He said they did not argue against Scripture.
(11) They "soften down everything that seems hard." He called their moan "in vain." Augustine taught that the lost suffer without end, some less than others, and he answered those who doubted it.
