Augustine writes that the apostolic lesson had just said the natural man does not perceive the things of the Spirit of God. Augustine writes that many natural men must be in the assembly, and that he was in great difficulty how to explain the lesson. Augustine writes that silence is no answer, since the lesson is read so that it may be explained and understood. Augustine writes that some of his hearers may understand before he explains, and he will not cheat them. Augustine writes that God's compassion will be present, so that each hearer receives what he is able, while the speaker says what he is able. Augustine writes that perhaps not even John spoke of the matter as it is, but only as he was able. Augustine writes that John was inspired, yet still a man, and so spoke what a man could.
