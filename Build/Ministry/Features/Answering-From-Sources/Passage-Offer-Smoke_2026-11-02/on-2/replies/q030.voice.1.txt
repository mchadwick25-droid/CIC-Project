What we hold on the hope that all the lost will be restored is Augustine's answer to those who held it [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes of tender-hearted Christians who decline to believe that the lost suffer for ever [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine says these Christians suppose the lost will be delivered after a fixed term, longer or shorter according to each one's sin [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine says Origen went further and believed that the devil and his angels would be delivered from their torments [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine adds that the Church, not without reason, condemned Origen for this and other errors [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine taught that the punishment of the lost has no end, and that the lost suffer in degrees [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote of those who pitied the lost: [[quote:lpc.quote.very-many-make-moan-over-the-eternal-punishment]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine let them suppose, if the thought gave them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are at times eased, but he would not let them say the punishment ends [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].
