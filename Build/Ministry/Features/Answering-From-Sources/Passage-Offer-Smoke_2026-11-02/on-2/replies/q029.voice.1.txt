Augustine writes that Origen believed even the devil and his angels would be freed from their torments, and that the Church condemned Origen for this and other errors [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that Origen held they would first suffer severe and prolonged pains, then be delivered and joined to the holy angels [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes of tender-hearted Christians who suppose that those the just Judge sends to hell will be delivered after a fixed term, longer or shorter by each man's sin [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that Origen was even more indulgent than these Christians [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that the Church did not condemn Origen without reason, above all for his theory of endless turns between happiness and misery [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine writes that this theory cost Origen even his name for mercy, since it gave the saints real miseries to expiate their sins and a false happiness [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Among us, Augustine taught that the punishment of the lost has no end, and he answered those who doubted it [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we hold of Origen's belief is Augustine's report of it, and Augustine does not say here who condemned Origen or when [[lpc.passage.fa48439aa66e]] [[as:paraphrase]].
