Gregory of Nyssa's teaching on the restoration of all things is not in our records, which rest on Cyprian and Augustine. What we do hold is that Augustine answered many around him who found it hard to bear that the punishment of the lost has no end. Augustine wrote: “So it is in vain that some, indeed very many, lament the eternal punishment and the endless, unbroken torments of the lost. They say they do not believe it will be so. They do not set themselves directly against Holy Scripture. But, prompted by their own feelings, they soften everything that seems hard. They give a milder sense to sayings they think were meant more to frighten than to be taken as literally true.”

Augustine said those people did not argue against Scripture, but softened down whatever seemed hard.

Augustine's words name no teacher whom those doubters followed.
