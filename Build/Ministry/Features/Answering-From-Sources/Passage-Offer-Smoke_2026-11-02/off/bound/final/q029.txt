We cannot say what Origen taught, but Augustine taught that the punishment of the lost has no end. Augustine wrote that after the final judgment there are two kingdoms, and that the lost suffer in degrees. Augustine wrote: “After the resurrection, when the final judgment of all has been completed, there will be two kingdoms. Each will have its own clear boundaries. One is Christ’s, the other the devil’s. One is made up of the good, the other of the bad. Both are made up of angels and human beings. The first will have no will to sin, and the second no power to sin. Neither will have any power to choose death. The first will live truly and happily in eternal life. The second will drag out a wretched existence in eternal death, without the power of dying. For both the life and the death will be without end. Among the first there will be degrees of happiness, one happier than another. Among the second there will be degrees of misery, one more bearable than another.”

Many around Augustine found this hard to bear, and he called their moan in vain. Augustine would not let them say the punishment ends.

Cyprian also pressed that same man to turn while he lived, and told him no repentance is too late in this world. Whether anyone condemned Origen for his teaching, our records do not say.
