We cannot say what Origen taught, but Augustine taught that the punishment of the lost has no end [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote that after the final judgment there are two kingdoms, and that the lost suffer in degrees [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote: [[quote:lpc.quote.the-life-and-the-death-shall-both-be-without-end]]

Many around Augustine found this hard to bear, and he called their moan in vain [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine let them suppose, if the thought pleased them, that the pains of the damned are at times eased [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine would not let them say the punishment ends [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Cyprian told Demetrianus, a pagan who persecuted us, that the condemned face a fire with no respite or end [[lpc.witness.no-salvation-outside-the-church]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian also pressed that same man to turn while he lived, and told him no repentance is too late in this world [[lpc.witness.no-salvation-outside-the-church]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Whether anyone condemned Origen for his teaching, our records do not say [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]].
