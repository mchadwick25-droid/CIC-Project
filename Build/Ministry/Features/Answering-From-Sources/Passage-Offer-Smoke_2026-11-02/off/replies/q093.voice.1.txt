Among us, communion is a formal standing within the one body, which a person can lose and be given back [[lpc.term.communion]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At the council of 256, Cyprian said no one would be put out of communion for thinking differently [[lpc.term.communion]] [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A century and a half later, Augustine argued at book length that the ruling of that council was wrong [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine never put Cyprian outside our communion [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote: [[quote:lpc.quote.apart-in-heart-united-in-body]]. Is it the rite of the table you are asking about, or the standing we call communion?
