Gregory of Nyssa's teaching on the restoration of all things is not in our records, which rest on Cyprian and Augustine [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we do hold is that Augustine answered many around him who found it hard to bear that the punishment of the lost has no end [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote: [[quote:lpc.quote.very-many-make-moan-over-the-eternal-punishment]]

Augustine said those people did not argue against Scripture, but softened down whatever seemed hard [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine let them suppose, if the thought pleased them, that the pains of the damned are at times eased [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. God's mercy, Augustine said, is shown in easing their torments or granting a respite, and not in ending the punishment [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine's words name no teacher whom those doubters followed [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].
