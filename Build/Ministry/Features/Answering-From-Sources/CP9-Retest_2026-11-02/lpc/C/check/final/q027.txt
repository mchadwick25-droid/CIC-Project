In 428 or 429, Augustine wrote to Honoratus, a bishop in Mauritania, about the dangers of those years. Augustine held that when danger falls on all alike, those who depend on their ministers must not be left. When danger reaches its extreme, Augustine wrote, a crowd gathers in the church, some urgently asking baptism, others reconciliation, others even the doing of penance. Augustine wrote that if the ministers are at their posts, all are aided, and none are defrauded of the communion of the Lord's body. The lapsed are our own people, not strangers and not enemies; they sat where we sit, and now they ask to come back. In Cyprian's time we took a lapsed person back and did not shut that lapsed person out for good. The way back for the lapsed was a public road, walked in steps under the bishop.
