No single rule settled it, because our two bishops answered what councils can decide in different ways, and we hold that question open [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[as:paraphrase]].
For Cyprian, no bishop stands over another, and each bishop judges for himself and answers to Christ [[lpc.term.bishop-of-bishops]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine held that small councils yield to bigger ones, and that even the biggest council can later be corrected [[lpc.term.plenary-council]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine also put Scripture above bishops and their councils [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].
At the council of 256, Cyprian said we judge no one, and reject no one from communion, for thinking differently [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine argued at book length that the ruling made at the council of 256 was wrong, and never put Cyprian outside our communion [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]].
We cannot tell you how our bishops beyond Cyprian and Augustine argued authority, since the transcript of the 411 conference has not been validly read [[lpc.limit.411-gesta-unread]] [[as:paraphrase]].
