No one among us names an eyewitness of Jesus, or claims a living memory passed hand to hand back to one. Augustine pointed instead to the world itself, where obscure, unlearned men persuaded the whole world of something incredible by testifying in writing that they had seen it themselves. We reason from the testimony of those obscure, unlearned men, handed down to us, even though none of us knew any of them. We go on to confess the Holy Ghost as well, which completes the Trinity that makes up the one Godhead. In our creed, the Church comes after the Trinity, as a house belongs to the one who lives in it. Our confession of Jesus Christ and the Trinity did not go unchallenged. An Arian bishop, Maximinus, disputed that confession with Augustine at Hippo, before many witnesses.
