Cyprian and his colleagues met in council, sixty-six in number, and wrote to Fidus. Fidus had thought a child should not be baptized before the eighth day, and no one agreed with him. Cyprian wrote for his council about the newborn himself: “Even the greatest sinners, who had sinned much against God, are granted forgiveness of sins when they later believe. No one is kept from baptism and grace. How much more, then, should we shrink from keeping back an infant! Being newly born, he has not sinned, except in one way. Born in the flesh as Adam’s child, he caught the infection of the ancient death at his very first birth. For this very reason he comes the more easily to receive the forgiveness of sins. What is forgiven him is not his own sins, but the sins of another.”. Cyprian's letter to Fidus does not use the phrase original sin, or the word guilt. Augustine went on to the hard end of this teaching, and wrote that in baptism infants die only to original sin.
