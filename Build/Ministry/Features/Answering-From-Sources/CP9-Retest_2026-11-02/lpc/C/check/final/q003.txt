We tell such a person that the trouble is real, because one of our own knew a trouble close to that person's before he was received among us. As a young man, Augustine prayed for chastity and continency, but not yet, afraid that God would answer him before he was ready to let go of what he still wanted. Near his own conversion, Augustine wrote that the truth had become certain to him, yet the old burden had not lifted. Being persuaded, Augustine found, was not the same as being free. We do not tell the struggle away, or promise the struggle ends on any set schedule. We say the struggle was real in one of our own, and was not the end of Augustine's story. Augustine opened the story of his own life with a restless heart. Augustine pointed to the world itself, where obscure, unlearned men persuaded everyone of something incredible by testifying in writing that they had seen it themselves. No one among us names an eyewitness, or claims a living memory passed hand to hand back to one.
