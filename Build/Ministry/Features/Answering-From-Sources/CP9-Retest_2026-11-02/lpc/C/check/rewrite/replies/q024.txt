Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A marriage compact, Augustine wrote, is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Separation does not make a marriage compact void [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So long as her husband lives, Augustine held, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine's teaching is about what marriage itself is, and what marriage binds [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
