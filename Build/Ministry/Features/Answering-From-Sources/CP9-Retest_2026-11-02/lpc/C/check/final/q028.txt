Among us, Cyprian taught that none are saved outside the Church. Cyprian spoke of heretics, people who confessed Christ but stood outside the Church, when he taught that none are saved outside the Church. Cyprian wrote: “Can the power of baptism be greater or worth more than confession and suffering? That is when someone confesses Christ before others and is baptized in his own blood. Yet even this baptism does not help a heretic, though he confessed Christ and was put to death outside the Church. Only the defenders and advocates of heretics say otherwise. They declare that heretics killed in a false confession of Christ are martyrs. They give them the glory and crown of martyrdom against the word of the apostle. He says it will profit them nothing, even if they were burned and killed. Not even the baptism of public confession and blood can help a heretic to salvation, because there is no salvation outside the Church. How much less will it help him in a hiding-place and a den of robbers, stained with the infection of adulterous water! There he has not only failed to put off his old sins. He has piled up still newer and greater ones.” In his book on the Church's unity, Cyprian called anyone who leaves the Church a stranger, profane, and an enemy. Cyprian gave the ark of Noah as his picture of leaving the Church. Heresy raised one sharp question among us: whether a baptism given outside our one church counts at all. Schism is the tear made when someone leaves the church's bishop, clergy and people, and sets up another altar and another bishop. Cyprian's words went to heretics, to those who split from the Church, and to a pagan who heard and would not believe.
