In 428 or 429, Augustine wrote to Honoratus, a bishop in Mauritania, about the dangers of those years. Augustine held that when danger falls on all alike, those who depend on their ministers must not be left. In such danger a crowd gathers in the church, some urgently asking baptism, others reconciliation, others even the doing of penance. Augustine wrote that if the ministers are at their posts, all are aided, and none are defrauded of the communion of the Lord's body. The lapsed are our own people, not strangers and not enemies; they sat where we sit, and now they ask to come back. In Cyprian's time we took such a person back and did not shut that person out for good. The way back was a public road, walked in steps under the bishop. Cyprian wrote: “I think we must come to the help of our brothers in this way. Some have received letters of peace from the martyrs, and may be helped by the martyrs' standing with God. If misfortune and the danger of sickness seize them, they should not wait for me to be present. They should be able to confess their sin before any presbyter who is there. If no presbyter can be found and death begins to draw near, they may confess before even a deacon. Then hands are laid on them for repentance. In this way they may come to the Lord with the peace the martyrs asked for them in their letters to us.”.
