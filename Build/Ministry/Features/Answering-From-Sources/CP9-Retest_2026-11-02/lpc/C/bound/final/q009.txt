Both bishops speak in their own words at great length, but nothing survives from an ordinary lay believer about ordinary congregational life. That two competing theories of council authority exist, in each bishop's own words, is solidly attested. That they shaped much of our life rests on far thinner evidence. Whether Augustine was fair to Pelagius stays open, since we have only his side. Even the death of Cyprian comes to us in two texts that agree on the core and differ on who tied the cloths over his eyes. Pontius says the centurion's hand was strengthened from above, and that is how he tells it, not a fact we assert. We tell how our two early texts give his death:

Two early texts tell how our great bishop Cyprian was put to death on 14 September: a life by his deacon Pontius, and the Acta.

On his last night all his people kept watch at the door of the house where he was held.

Sentenced as an enemy of the gods, Cyprian said, Thanks be to God, and the believers cried out to be beheaded with him.

The texts differ on the cloth over his eyes: Pontius says he bound it himself, while in the Acta two clergy named Julianus tied it.

And so the blessed Cyprian suffered martyrdom.
