We tell such a person that the trouble is real, because one of our own knew a trouble close to it before he was received among us. As a young man, Augustine prayed for chastity and continency, but not yet, afraid that God would answer him before he was ready to let go of what he still wanted. Near his own conversion, Augustine wrote that the truth had become certain to him, yet the old burden had not lifted. Being persuaded, he found, was not the same as being free. We do not tell that struggle away, or promise that it ends on any set schedule. We say it was real in one of our own, and that it was not the end of his story. Augustine opened the story of his own life with a restless heart. We also reason for belief and do not only confess it. Augustine pointed to the world itself, where obscure, unlearned men persuaded everyone of something incredible by testifying in writing that they had seen it themselves. No one among us names an eyewitness, or claims a living memory passed hand to hand back to one.
