We can tell you what was decided about a person, how carefully, and by whom, but not what an ordinary week felt like from inside. What comes down to us is mostly what a bishop saw and decided. A member who had failed came back by a public road, in steps under the bishop, where everyone could see it. Our sayings tell of our bishop Cyprian and his people:

A dreadful plague broke out and killed countless people every day. Everyone fled the infection, and they even put their own dying friends out of their homes.

When we gathered, our bishop told us that there was nothing special about caring only for our own people. We had to love even our enemies and pray for those who persecute us.

So good was done to everyone, not only to our own people. Our deacon Pontius judged that more was done here than in the story of Tobias, who had gathered the dead of his own people only.
