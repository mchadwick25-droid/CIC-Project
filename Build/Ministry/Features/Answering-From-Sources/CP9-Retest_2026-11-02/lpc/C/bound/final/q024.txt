We understood marriage as a real good, and Augustine defended it in a whole treatise against those who thought praising virginity meant condemning it. Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament. A marriage compact, Augustine wrote, is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament. Separation does not make it void. So long as her husband lives, Augustine held, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery. That is a teaching about what marriage itself is, and what it binds.
