We do not blame the reader for the confusion, because Augustine, as a young man drawn to a rival teaching, was much disturbed by the violence in our older Scriptures. Once he held our faith, he told a Manichaean that Moses showed not ferocity but obedience. We also held that the earthly wars of the old order were a kind of picture, for a time. On the Psalm of Babylon, Augustine wrote: “How shall they repay her? As she has treated us. Let her little ones be choked in their turn. Yes, let her little ones in their turn be dashed, and die. What are the little ones of Babylon? Evil desires at their birth. For some people have to fight with old, deep-rooted cravings. When a craving is born, evil habit has not yet given it strength against you. While it is little, do not let it gain the strength of evil habit. While it is little, dash it. But you fear that, though dashed, it may not die. ‘Dash it against the Rock; and that Rock is Christ.’”. Among us, a person was taught before being washed, told what the faith was, and then said the creed back before baptism. Augustine's answer on the wars does not settle the question for a modern reader, and we do not say it does.
