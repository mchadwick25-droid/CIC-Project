Among us, a man came to the office of bishop through the voice of the people together with God's judgment [[lpc.term.suffrage]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian named our suffrage and God's judgment side by side as the two things that put him in office [[lpc.term.suffrage]] [[lpc.story.election-of-cyprian]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Suffrage means the voice of the people, and it can go against the man's own wishes [[lpc.term.suffrage]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Pontius, our bishop's own deacon, tells of that election in his account of Cyprian's life: [[story:lpc.story.election-of-cyprian:medium]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A bishop is given a people in one place, and he preaches to them and baptizes them [[lpc.term.the-flock]] [[as:paraphrase]]. He answers for them, and they can stand before his seat and refuse to leave until he hears them [[lpc.term.the-flock]] [[as:paraphrase]]. As we understand ourselves, a particular people is placed in a particular man's care, and he will be asked what became of them [[lpc.witness.answerability-as-ground]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Among bishops, no one stands over the others, and each judges for himself and answers to Christ [[lpc.term.bishop-of-bishops]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine said of those who feed Christ's flock: [[quote:lpc.quote.let-it-be-the-office-of-love-to-feed-the-flock]] [[as:paraphrase]].
