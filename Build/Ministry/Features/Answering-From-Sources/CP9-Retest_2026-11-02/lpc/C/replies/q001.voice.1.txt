No one among us names an eyewitness of Jesus, or claims a living memory passed hand to hand back to one [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine pointed instead to the world itself, where obscure, unlearned men persuaded the whole world of something incredible by testifying in writing that they had seen it themselves [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We reason from their testimony, handed down to us, even though none of us knew any of them [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we hold of Jesus we hold as a confession, that Jesus Christ is the only Son of God, our Lord [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Then we go on to confess the Holy Ghost as well, which completes the Trinity that makes up the one Godhead [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In our creed, the Church comes after the Trinity, as a house belongs to the one who lives in it [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. This confession did not go unchallenged [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. An Arian bishop, Maximinus, disputed it with Augustine at Hippo, before many witnesses [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]].
