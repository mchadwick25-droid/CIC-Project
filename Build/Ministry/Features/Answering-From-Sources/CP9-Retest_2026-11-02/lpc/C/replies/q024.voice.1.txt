We understood marriage as a real good, and Augustine defended it in a whole treatise against those who thought praising virginity meant condemning it [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A marriage compact, Augustine wrote, is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Separation does not make it void [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So long as her husband lives, Augustine held, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That is a teaching about what marriage itself is, and what it binds [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
