We held that God's mercy and grace are not to be refused to anyone born of man, as Cyprian's council judged [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That council wrote to Fidus, who had thought a child should wait until the eighth day, and no one agreed with him [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In 428 or 429, Augustine wrote to Honoratus, a bishop in Mauritania, about the dangers of those years [[lpc.witness.the-dying-and-the-pastors-post]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine held that when danger falls on all alike, those who depend on their ministers must not be left [[lpc.witness.the-dying-and-the-pastors-post]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In such danger a crowd gathers in the church, some urgently asking baptism, others reconciliation, others even the doing of penance [[lpc.witness.the-dying-and-the-pastors-post]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote that if the ministers are at their posts, all are aided, and none are defrauded of the communion of the Lord's body [[lpc.witness.the-dying-and-the-pastors-post]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The lapsed are our own people, not strangers and not enemies; they sat where we sit, and now they ask to come back [[lpc.term.the-lapsed]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In Cyprian's time we took such a person back and did not shut that person out for good [[lpc.gravity.penitential-discipline]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The way back was a public road, walked in steps under the bishop [[lpc.term.reconciliation-penitential-discipline]] [[as:paraphrase]].
