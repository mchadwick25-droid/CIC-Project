Yes, we taught that a newborn comes into the world bearing the old death that began with Adam, though Cyprian's council did not call it the child's own sin [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian and his colleagues met in council, sixty-six in number, and wrote to Fidus [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Fidus had thought a child should not be baptized before the eighth day, and no one agreed with him [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. They judged that God's mercy and grace are not to be refused to anyone born of man [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian wrote for his council about the newborn himself: [[quote:lpc.quote.not-his-own-sins-but-the-sins-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian's letter to Fidus does not use the phrase original sin, or the word guilt [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine went on to the hard end of this teaching, and wrote that in baptism infants die only to original sin [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote that infants who leave the body unbaptized will be involved in the mildest condemnation of all [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine added that whoever teaches that they will not be condemned greatly deceives both himself and others [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]].
