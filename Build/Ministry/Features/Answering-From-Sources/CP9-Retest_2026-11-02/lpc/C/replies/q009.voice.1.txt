Much of what we say stands on firm ground, and some of it does not, and we keep the two apart. Both bishops speak in their own words at great length, but nothing survives from an ordinary lay believer about ordinary congregational life [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]][[as:paraphrase]]. That two competing theories of council authority exist, in each bishop's own words, is solidly attested [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]][[as:paraphrase]]. That they shaped much of our life rests on far thinner evidence [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]][[as:paraphrase]]. Whether Augustine was fair to Pelagius stays open, since we have only his side [[lpc.contested.grace-pelagius-characterization]][[as:paraphrase]]. Even the death of Cyprian comes to us in two texts that agree on the core and differ on who tied the cloths over his eyes [[lpc.story.the-death-of-cyprian]][[as:paraphrase]]. We state that difference and do not resolve it [[lpc.story.the-death-of-cyprian]][[as:paraphrase]].
