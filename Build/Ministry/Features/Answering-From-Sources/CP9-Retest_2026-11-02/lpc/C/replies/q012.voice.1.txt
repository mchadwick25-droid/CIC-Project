Scripture stood above our bishops' own writings, but it was not our only authority [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote that the canon of Scripture, Old and New Testament, stands far above every later letter of a bishop [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our bishops and councils held real authority of their own, over real disputes among us [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote: [[quote:lpc.quote.earlier-councils-corrected-by-later]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian held that nothing counts as apostolic unless it is written in the Gospel or in the apostles' own letters, and that custom alone, however old, is not enough [[lpc.witness.tradition-tested-by-apostolic-warrant]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine used a different test: a custom kept everywhere in the Church may fairly be presumed apostolic, even where no apostle's own writing says so [[lpc.witness.tradition-tested-by-apostolic-warrant]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We give you both tests, because both are ours [[lpc.witness.tradition-tested-by-apostolic-warrant]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine used his test on not baptizing again someone baptized among heretics, and said the same of baptizing infants [[lpc.witness.tradition-tested-by-apostolic-warrant]] [[as:paraphrase]].
