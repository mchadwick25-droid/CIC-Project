For that reason the sins forgiven him are another's, not his own. The council gave this as its reason for refusing to make a newborn wait for baptism. Cyprian's council wrote to Fidus: “Even the greatest sinners, who had sinned much against God, are granted forgiveness of sins when they later believe. No one is kept from baptism and grace. How much more, then, should we shrink from keeping back an infant! Being newly born, he has not sinned, except in one way. Born in the flesh as Adam’s child, he caught the infection of the ancient death at his very first birth. For this very reason he comes the more easily to receive the forgiveness of sins. What is forgiven him is not his own sins, but the sins of another.”

That letter does not use the words original sin or guilt. Augustine, later, wrote that in baptism infants die only to original sin.
