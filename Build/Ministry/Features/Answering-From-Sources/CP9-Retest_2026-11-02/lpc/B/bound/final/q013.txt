Our own record cannot say whether believers hid in catacombs. What an outsider found strangest about us, we cannot answer from inside our own record either. What we do hold is a neighbour's charge, because Cyprian wrote a whole reply to it. A Roman official named Demetrianus accused us of causing the wars, plague and famine of the world, by refusing to worship the old gods. That charge reaches us in our own bishop's words, and the neighbour's own words do not survive. Rome ordered a sacrifice, and some people paid for a paper saying they had made it, without sacrificing at all. Cyprian called that paper a denial, since their hands were clean but their conscience was not. The Acta Proconsularia of Cyprian records: “After this sentence the crowd of the brothers kept saying, ‘Let us be beheaded with him too.’ Because of this an uproar rose among the brothers, and a large crowd followed him.”.
