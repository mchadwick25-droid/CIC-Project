To us, Jesus's death and rising are the pattern our own life is modelled on, in reality and not only in mystery. We crucify our own flesh, and that is his cross in us. We are buried with him in the water, and that is his burial in us. We walk in a life made new, and that is his rising in us. We set our affection on what is above, and that is his ascension and his sitting at God's right hand, worked out in an ordinary life. Augustine wrote of the Lord when he was struck: “For many have learned how to offer the other cheek, but do not know how to love the one who strikes them. But in truth the Lord Himself was surely the first to keep the commands He taught. Yet He did not offer the other cheek to the high priest’s servant who struck Him there. Far from it, He said, ‘If I have spoken evil, bear witness of the evil; but if well, why do you strike me?’ Yet He was not, for that reason, unready in heart for the salvation of all. He was ready not only to be struck on the other cheek, but even to have His whole body crucified.”
