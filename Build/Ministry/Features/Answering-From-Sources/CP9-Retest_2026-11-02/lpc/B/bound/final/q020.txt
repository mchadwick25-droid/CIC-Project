Yes, we speak of baptism as a birth, and Cyprian gave us the plainest words for it. Cyprian wrote to Jubaianus about people who come to the church from heresy. Cyprian held that they ought to be baptized, so that divine regeneration would prepare them for the kingdom of God. Cyprian rested this on Scripture, on the saying that a man must be born of water and of the Spirit to enter that kingdom. So for our bishop, baptism was a regeneration, a birth. Cyprian does not say in that letter what the two sacraments of the birth are. What a baptized infant or a catechumen understood by the birth, our record does not give. We can tell you what was decided at the font, and by whom, but not what an ordinary believer felt standing there.
