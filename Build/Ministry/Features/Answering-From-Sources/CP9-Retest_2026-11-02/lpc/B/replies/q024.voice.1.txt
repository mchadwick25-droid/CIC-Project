Among us, Augustine taught that marriage is a real good, and he named three things it is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote a whole treatise defending marriage against those who thought that praising virginity meant condemning it [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Of the third good, Augustine wrote that a marriage compact is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine held that separation does not make that compact void [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So long as her husband lives, Augustine taught, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For us this is a teaching about what marriage itself is, and what it binds [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
