Yes, we speak of baptism as a birth, and Cyprian gave us the plainest words for it [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian wrote to Jubaianus about people who come to the church from heresy [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian held that they ought to be baptized, so that divine regeneration would prepare them for the kingdom of God [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian rested this on Scripture, on the saying that a man must be born of water and of the Spirit to enter that kingdom [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So for our bishop, baptism was a regeneration, a birth [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian does not say in that letter what the two sacraments of the birth are [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What a baptized infant or a catechumen understood by the birth, our record does not give [[lpc.witness.baptism-as-new-birth]] [[lpc.limit.ordinary-interior-life]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We can tell you what was decided at the font, and by whom, but not what an ordinary believer felt standing there [[lpc.limit.ordinary-interior-life]] [[as:paraphrase]].
