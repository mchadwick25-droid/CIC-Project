Augustine, reading Paul's letter, preached: [[quote:lpc.quote.no-one-has-the-love-of-god-from-himself]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine taught on prayer that goes unanswered from Paul's own words about his thorn [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Paul asked God three times to take it away, and God did not take it away [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. God answered instead that his grace was sufficient, and that strength is made perfect in weakness [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine made this a rule for the saints, who are always heard in what concerns their salvation, even when they are not heard as they wished [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The violence in some of our older Scriptures once troubled Augustine, before he held our own faith [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Later he answered a Manichaean who pressed that charge, and wrote that Moses showed obedience and not ferocity [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In giving that command, Augustine held, God acted not in cruelty but in righteous retribution, giving to all what they deserved [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine did not stop at that answer, and he held that the earthly wars of the old order were themselves a kind of picture, for a time [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]].
