As we understand ourselves, a bishop's authority began with one plain fact: a particular people was placed in his care, and he would be asked what became of them [[lpc.witness.answerability-as-ground]][[lpc.term.the-flock]][[as:paraphrase]]. His flock could stand before his seat and refuse to leave until he heard them [[lpc.term.the-flock]][[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian named two things that put a man in the office, our suffrage and God's judgment [[lpc.story.election-of-cyprian]][[lpc.term.suffrage]][[as:paraphrase]]. We keep the account of how Cyprian came to the office: [[story:lpc.story.election-of-cyprian:short]] A church already organized enough to hold factions was organized enough to carry an election over a faction's opposition [[lpc.force.organized-carthaginian-church]][[as:paraphrase]]. Authority between bishops ran another way, for Cyprian held that no bishop stands over another and each answers to Christ [[lpc.term.bishop-of-bishops]][[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine held that small councils yield to wider ones, and that even the widest can be corrected [[lpc.term.plenary-council]][[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote about what the office is for: [[quote:lpc.quote.let-it-be-the-office-of-love-to-feed-the-flock]]
