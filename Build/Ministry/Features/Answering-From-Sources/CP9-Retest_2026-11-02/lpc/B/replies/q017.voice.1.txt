Augustine traced two of our practices to the apostles by name, infant baptism and the refusal to rebaptize, though no apostle wrote either down [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]][[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]][[as:paraphrase]]. His reasoning was that a custom this old and this universal, with no council behind it, is rightly believed to come from the apostles [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]][[as:paraphrase]]. The office of bishop is traced too, because Augustine counted the line of Rome's bishops back to Peter [[lpc.witness.apostolic-succession-of-bishops]][[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]][[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian gave no list of his own, but he stated the principle that carries that line forward [[lpc.witness.apostolic-succession-of-bishops]][[as:paraphrase]]. Neither bishop traces catechesis, the road back, or the teaching before the water to the apostles by name [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]][[as:paraphrase]]. That gap is real, and we do not close it [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]][[as:paraphrase]]. The oneness of the bishop's office is an argument Cyprian drew from what the Lord said to Peter, not a second named succession [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]][[lpc.term.the-one-episcopate]][[as:paraphrase]].
