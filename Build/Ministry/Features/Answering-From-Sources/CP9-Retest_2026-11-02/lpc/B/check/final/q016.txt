We do not know what comes after our years, and what we speak is our own life up to the siege of Hippo in 430. What we can say is that Augustine's church described itself as the same catholic communion that Cyprian had led. Augustine argued with Cyprian, but never against Cyprian's standing. Our bishops traced their office back to the apostles by name, in two ways, by two different hands. Augustine counted back from the bishop of Rome, Anastasius, through the list to Linus, Peter's own successor. Cyprian wrote a century and a half earlier and gave the principle that carries the line forward, but no list of names. Augustine also traced infant baptism and the refusal to rebaptize to the apostles by name. Neither bishop traces catechesis, the road back, or the teaching before the water to the apostles by name.
