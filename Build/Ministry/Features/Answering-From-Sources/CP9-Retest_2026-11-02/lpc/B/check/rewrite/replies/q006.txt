For us, God gives first, and grace is our word for that [[lpc.term.grace]] [[as:paraphrase]]. No effort of ours is enough on its own, and any good a person does was given to the person before the person did it [[lpc.term.grace]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine taught on prayer that goes unanswered from Paul's own words about his thorn [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Paul asked God three times to take away the thorn, and God did not take the thorn away [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. God answered Paul instead that God's grace was sufficient, and that strength is made perfect in weakness [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine made Paul's case a rule for the saints, who are always heard in what concerns their salvation, even when they are not heard as they wished [[lpc.witness.heard-for-salvation-not-for-wish]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The violence in some of our older Scriptures once troubled Augustine, before Augustine held our own faith [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Later Augustine answered a Manichaean who pressed the same kind of charge, and wrote that Moses showed obedience and not ferocity [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In giving the command for the wars of Moses, Augustine held, God acted not in cruelty but in righteous retribution, giving to all what they deserved [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine did not stop at the answer about Moses, and held that the earthly wars of the old order were themselves a kind of picture, for a time [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]].
