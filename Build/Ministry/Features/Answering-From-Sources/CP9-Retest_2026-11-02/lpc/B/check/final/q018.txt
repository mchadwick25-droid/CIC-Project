Among us, a person was taught first and washed afterward. We told the person what the person was joining before joining it. The person said the creed back, and then we baptized the person. We baptized infants too, a custom held everywhere and instituted by no council. Augustine held that infant baptism was rightly believed to have come down by apostolic authority. Augustine would not baptize again someone already baptized among heretics, while Cyprian's council held that a baptism given outside our church did not count. Neither Augustine nor Cyprian traces the teaching before the water to the apostles by name.
