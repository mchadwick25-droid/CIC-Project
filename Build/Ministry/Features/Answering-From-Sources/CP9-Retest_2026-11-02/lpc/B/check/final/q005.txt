Among us no single seat settled it, for Cyprian opened the council of 256 by saying that no bishop sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, and that each bishop has his own right of judgment. A century and a half later, Augustine held that small councils yield to wider ones, and that even the widest can be corrected later. Scripture stood above all of them, yet our bishops and councils still held real authority over real disputes. The evidence that we worked this way is in what the bishops did with each other. At the council of 256 Cyprian said we judge no one, and reject no one from communion, for thinking differently. A century and a half later, Augustine argued at book length that the ruling made at Cyprian's council was wrong, and never put Cyprian outside our communion. The font shows how far our disagreement went, since Cyprian's council said a baptism given outside the church does not count, and Augustine said the baptism was given, though the baptism did no good there.
