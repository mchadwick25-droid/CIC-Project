Among us, Augustine taught that marriage is a real good, and he named three things it is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament. Augustine wrote a whole treatise defending marriage against those who thought that praising virginity meant condemning it. Of the third good, Augustine wrote that a marriage compact is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament. Augustine held that separation does not make that compact void. So long as her husband lives, Augustine taught, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery. For us this is a teaching about what marriage itself is, and what it binds.
