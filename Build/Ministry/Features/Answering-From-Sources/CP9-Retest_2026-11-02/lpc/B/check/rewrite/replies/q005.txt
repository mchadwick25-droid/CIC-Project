Among us no single seat settled it, for Cyprian opened the council of 256 by saying that no bishop sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, and that each bishop has his own right of judgment [[lpc.term.bishop-of-bishops]] [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A century and a half later, Augustine held that small councils yield to wider ones, and that even the widest can be corrected later [[lpc.term.plenary-council]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Scripture stood above all of them, yet our bishops and councils still held real authority over real disputes [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The evidence that we worked this way is in what the bishops did with each other [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At the council of 256 Cyprian said we judge no one, and reject no one from communion, for thinking differently [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A century and a half later, Augustine argued at book length that the ruling made at Cyprian's council was wrong, and never put Cyprian outside our communion [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The font shows how far our disagreement went, since Cyprian's council said a baptism given outside the church does not count, and Augustine said the baptism was given, though the baptism did no good there [[lpc.term.heresy]] [[as:paraphrase]].
