We tell such a person that the trouble is real, because one of our own knew a trouble close to it before he was received among us. Augustine wrote it down himself, years later. As a young man, Augustine prayed for chastity and continency, but not yet, afraid that God would answer him before he was ready to let go of what he still wanted. Close to his conversion, the truth had become certain to Augustine, yet the old burden had not lifted. Being persuaded, he wrote, was not the same as being free. We do not promise that such a struggle resolves on any set schedule, and we do not tell it away. Augustine opened the story of his own life with a restless heart, made for God. No one among us names an eyewitness of Jesus, or claims a living memory passed hand to hand back to one. Augustine pointed instead to the world itself, where obscure, unlearned men persuaded the whole world of something incredible by testifying in writing that they had seen it. We reason from that testimony, handed down to us, even though none of us knew any of those men.
