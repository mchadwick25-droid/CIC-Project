What we can give you is what a bishop decided and cared for, not one ordinary believer's day. We cannot tell you what an ordinary believer felt on an ordinary week, in the congregation, at the font, or walking up to the altar. Both of our own voices are bishops, so what comes down to us is mostly what a bishop saw and decided. The one exception is Augustine's account of his own baptism at Milan, in his own first person, but he wrote it years afterward as a bishop. What we do know is the room itself, where the baptized and those still learning heard the same voice work on them again and again. Preaching was the bishop's regular talk to the whole gathered church.
