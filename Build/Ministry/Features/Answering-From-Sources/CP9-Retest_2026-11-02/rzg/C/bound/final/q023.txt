Among us belonging was not a chosen step: we held a whole believing city, infants included from birth, to be the covenant shape Scripture describes. Some who had once stood with our founder held that only a believer's own chosen profession makes a true member. Their first baptisms came on 21 January 1525 at Felix Manz's house in Zurich, when Conrad Grebel baptized Georg Blaurock. Blaurock then baptized the others present, and the split ran through our founding community from within. At Geneva, belonging meant answering to the Consistory, a body of pastors and lay elders that held daily conduct to account. John Calvin set down his reading of Paul's governors: “By these governors I mean elders chosen from the people. They join with the pastors to declare censures and to carry out discipline.”. Zurich had no such body, and its council ruled church and city directly. Who held the final word over discipline, we never settled between our two cities. What belonging cost an ordinary citizen, day by day, has not reached us in our own words.
