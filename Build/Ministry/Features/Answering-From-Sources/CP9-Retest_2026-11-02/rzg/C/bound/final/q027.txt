We held that no one finds their standing by searching their own heart, but by looking to Christ, held out in the promise. Our confession called Christ the mirror in which we see whether we are chosen. We offered that as settled ground, not as a threat. At Geneva, a body of pastors and lay elders held the whole population's ordinary conduct answerable to what we profess. Its harshest step was excommunication, which answered sin that was not repented, and readmission stayed open to genuine repentance. What we refused was other people's claims, not persons: we refused Rome's repeated sacrifice, Wittenberg's body in the bread, and the profession-only church. How a Consistory summons felt to the person who received it has not reached us.
