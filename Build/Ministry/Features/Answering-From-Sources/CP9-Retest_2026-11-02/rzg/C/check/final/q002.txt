To us Jesus is what the whole church says he is, and that was never our own quarrel, not with Rome and not between our two cities. What we have of Christ is the text, not a living chain of memory, and we held the text to be enough on its own terms. Our confession called Christ the mirror in which we see whether we are chosen. So we turned a troubled mind outward toward Christ, once, and not inward toward the self, endlessly. At the Supper we held that Christ is truly given, by the Spirit, to whoever receives him believing. We refused two claims about it: that the Supper repeats his sacrifice, and that his body sits in the bread. In 1549 Zurich and Geneva agreed words that tell the bread apart from the reality it points to, and never pull the two apart. What is distinctly ours is not a new picture of Christ, but what we were taught about who is chosen in him. How an ordinary believer felt before him at a service has not reached us in our words.
