Authority was established differently in our two cities, and we never agreed between them who holds the final word over church discipline. At Zurich the council governed church and city together, and the chief pastor's title, Antistes, answered to that council. Bullinger held the title of Antistes after Zwingli died. At Geneva, Calvin's 1541 recall produced the Ecclesiastical Ordinances, which created a Consistory of pastors and lay elders with the power of censure. An elder there was a layperson, not a pastor, and held real power on the Consistory. Geneva's Consistory fought for its independence from the magistrate, and only by 1555 substantially won it. Calvin wrote of Paul's governors in the church: “By these governors I mean elders chosen from the people. They join with the pastors to declare censures and to carry out discipline.”

Calvin, in Institutes of the Christian Religion, Book IV, writes that the election of pastors does not belong to one individual, since other pastors should preside and the people consent and approve. Under both arrangements stood one conviction, that Scripture alone may bind a church, and what it does not warrant, no one may require of us.
