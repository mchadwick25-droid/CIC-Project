Scripture alone was our authority, and no pope, council, or custom could set aside what Scripture yields. Scripture alone as our authority did not mean each believer decided doctrine alone from private study. In today's English, Huldrych Zwingli says: “I, Ulrich Zwingli, declare that I have preached the articles and opinions below in the good city of Zurich. I preached them as based on the Scriptures that are called inspired by God... Where my present understanding of these Scriptures is not correct, I will let myself be taught better. But I will be taught only from these same Scriptures.” At Geneva the conviction that Scripture alone binds the church was built up article by article and taught to everyone through the Catechism. We read Zurich and Geneva as one authority, enacted twice, and not as one arrangement. Zurich's council governed church and city together, and Geneva's Consistory fought for its independence until about 1555.
