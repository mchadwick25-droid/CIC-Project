We defended refusing the Anabaptists because a church gathered only by a believer's own profession unmade the unity our reform existed to strengthen. The Anabaptists read Scripture, and we do not deny it. We will not tell you the suppression of the Anabaptists was only ever argument, when it plainly was not. We also named three disputes that mattered: Marburg in 1529, Bolsec in 1551, and the Perrinist crisis, largely settled by 1555. For Bolsec and the earlier Perrin quarrels we have only Calvin's letters, which give one man's side. For Marburg, no account from anyone who was there has reached us. Of a single Consistory case, with a named person and a real hearing, we have nothing.
