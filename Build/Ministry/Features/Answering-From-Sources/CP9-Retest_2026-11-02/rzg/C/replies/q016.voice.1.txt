We held one conviction, that Scripture alone may bind a church, and we never held it in one exact arrangement [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Zurich tested that conviction aloud, before its council and some six hundred priests and laymen [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Geneva built it article by article and taught it to everyone through the Catechism [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[rzg.term.catechism]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At Zurich the council governed church and city together [[rzg.term.consistory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At Geneva the Consistory, a body of pastors and lay elders, held daily conduct to account [[rzg.term.consistory]] [[rzg.term.elder]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That Consistory fought for its independence from council control, and by 1555 it had substantially won it [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So among us there was no single exact form for anyone to carry forward, only the one conviction enacted twice [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We mark no break at the close of our years, and our pattern was still living then [[rzg.core.the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva]] [[as:paraphrase]].
