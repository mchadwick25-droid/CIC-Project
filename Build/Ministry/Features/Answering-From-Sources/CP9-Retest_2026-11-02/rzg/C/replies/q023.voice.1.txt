Among us belonging was not a chosen step: we held a whole believing city, infants included from birth, to be the covenant shape Scripture describes [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Some who had once stood with our founder held that only a believer's own chosen profession makes a true member [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Their first baptisms came on 21 January 1525 at Felix Manz's house in Zurich, when Conrad Grebel baptized Georg Blaurock [[rzg.force.anabaptist-schism]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Blaurock then baptized the others present, and the split ran through our founding community from within [[rzg.force.anabaptist-schism]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At Geneva, belonging meant answering to the Consistory, a body of pastors and lay elders that held daily conduct to account [[rzg.term.consistory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. John Calvin set down his reading of Paul's governors: [[quote:rzg.quote.seniors-selected-from-the-people]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Zurich had no such body, and its council ruled church and city directly [[rzg.term.consistory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Who held the final word over discipline, we never settled between our two cities [[rzg.witness.what-we-have-not-agreed]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What belonging cost an ordinary citizen, day by day, has not reached us in our own words [[rzg.limit.ordinary-daily-practice]] [[as:paraphrase]].
