What unsettles us is that our own council moved against the Anabaptist movement with civil force in the years after 1525, and not only with argument [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We defended refusing them because a church gathered only by a believer's own profession unmade the unity our reform existed to strengthen [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. They read Scripture, and we do not deny it [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We will not tell you the suppression was only ever argument, when it plainly was not [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The record does not narrate in detail how that suppression was carried out [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We also named three disputes that mattered: Marburg in 1529, Bolsec in 1551, and the Perrinist crisis, largely settled by 1555 [[rzg.limit.marburg-bolsec-perrinist-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For Bolsec and the earlier Perrin quarrels we have only Calvin's letters, which give one man's side [[rzg.limit.marburg-bolsec-perrinist-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For Marburg, no account from anyone who was there has reached us [[rzg.limit.marburg-bolsec-perrinist-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Of a single Consistory case, with a named person and a real hearing, we have nothing [[rzg.limit.consistory-case-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]].
