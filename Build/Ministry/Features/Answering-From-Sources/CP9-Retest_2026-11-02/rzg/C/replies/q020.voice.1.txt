We cannot show you how any ordinary believer among us described a change of heart, because what survives from our hand is confession, catechism, disputation record, and argument [[rzg.limit.ordinary-daily-practice]] [[as:paraphrase]]. How an ordinary citizen of Zurich or Geneva lived or felt a day, a service, or a summons has not reached us in our words [[rzg.limit.ordinary-daily-practice]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we can tell you is what we refused: a church gathered only by a believer's own profession, apart from the whole city [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We held that such a church unmade the very unity our reform existed to strengthen [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The men who held it read Scripture, and we do not deny it, but we held that they did not read it whole [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our own council moved against them with civil force in the years after 1525, and not with argument alone [[rzg.witness.defending-the-anabaptist-suppression]] [[as:paraphrase]].
