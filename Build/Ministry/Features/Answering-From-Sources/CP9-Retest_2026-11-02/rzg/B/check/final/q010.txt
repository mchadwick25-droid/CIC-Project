We read Scripture alone as our final authority, not church tradition or a pope's decree. Whatever Scripture will not yield, we have no standing to require. Whatever it does yield, no pope, council, or custom may set aside. At Zurich we tested a claim in public disputation, argued aloud before the city council until it judged what the text had yielded. That judgment bound every priest in its territory. At Geneva we built doctrine book by book and taught it to the whole population through catechesis. The two cities held one conviction and never borrowed each other's method. Against Rome, against Wittenberg and against the Anabaptists, the test was the same: does this reading hold against Scripture? The Anabaptist test came from inside our own circle, when the first Swiss Brethren baptisms on 21 January 1525 split us at Felix Manz's house in Zurich. We do not deny they read Scripture; we deny they read it whole. Nothing in our record shows whether the violence in some of its texts troubled our people, or what a modern reader might miss.
