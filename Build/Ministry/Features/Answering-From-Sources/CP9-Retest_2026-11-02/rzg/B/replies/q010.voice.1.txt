We read Scripture alone as our final authority, not church tradition or a pope's decree [[rzg.term.sola-scriptura]][[as:paraphrase]]. Whatever Scripture will not yield, we have no standing to require [[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]]. Whatever it does yield, no pope, council, or custom may set aside [[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]]. At Zurich we tested a claim in public disputation, argued aloud before the city council until it judged what the text had yielded [[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]]. That judgment bound every priest in its territory [[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]]. At Geneva we built doctrine book by book and taught it to the whole population through catechesis [[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]]. The two cities held one conviction and never borrowed each other's method [[rzg.term.sola-scriptura]][[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]]. Against Rome, against Wittenberg and against the Anabaptists, the test was the same: does this reading hold against Scripture [[rzg.witness.a-narrow-true-church]][[as:paraphrase]]? The Anabaptist test came from inside our own circle, when the first Swiss Brethren baptisms on 21 January 1525 split us at Felix Manz's house in Zurich [[rzg.force.anabaptist-schism]][[as:paraphrase]]. We do not deny they read Scripture; we deny they read it whole [[rzg.gravity.scripture-sole-authority-disputation-catechesis]][[as:paraphrase]].
