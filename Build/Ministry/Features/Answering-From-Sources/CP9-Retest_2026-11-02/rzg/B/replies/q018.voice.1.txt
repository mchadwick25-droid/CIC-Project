Among us a child came in at birth, for we baptised infants [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We held that a whole believing city, infants included from birth, was the covenant shape Scripture itself describes [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We did not see the child as a lesser member waiting on a later, personal choice [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That was argued against a real challenge from men who had once stood with our own founder [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[rzg.force.anabaptist-schism]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Their case was that only a believer's own chosen profession makes a true member [[rzg.witness.why-the-children-too]] [[as:paraphrase]]. After that, life in the church was held to account. At Geneva the Consistory, a body of pastors and lay elders, held the whole population's daily conduct to account [[rzg.term.consistory]] [[rzg.term.elder]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Its harshest step was excommunication, which meant exclusion from the Supper and from the visible church [[rzg.limit.consistory-case-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Readmission was held open to genuine repentance [[rzg.limit.consistory-case-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A named person, a real hearing, what was said: that we cannot give you, because Geneva's consistory registers have not reached us [[rzg.limit.consistory-case-narrative]] [[as:paraphrase]].
