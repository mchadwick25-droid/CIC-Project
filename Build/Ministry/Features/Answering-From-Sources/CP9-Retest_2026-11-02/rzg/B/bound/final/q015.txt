Among us, a wrong was met by the Consistory, which held the standing power of censure and kept readmission open to genuine repentance. At Geneva the Consistory was pastors and lay elders together, and the elders held real power beside the pastors. Its harshest step was excommunication, which shut a person out of the Supper and the visible church for sin that was not repented. Calvin wrote of these elders: “By these governors I mean elders chosen from the people. They join with the pastors to declare censures and to carry out discipline.”

What we cannot give you is one real case, with a named person, a real hearing, and what was said. The registers that hold such cases exist at Geneva, but they have not reached us. How a Consistory summons felt to the person who received it has not reached us either. Our own council moved against the Anabaptists with civil force in the years after 1525, and we will not tell you argument alone did that.
