Authority ran differently in our two cities, and we never agreed between them who holds the final word over church discipline. At Zurich the council governed church and city together, from our first days. Zurich's own title for its chief pastor was antistes, which Bullinger held after Zwingli, and the antistes answered to the city council. At Geneva, the Consistory was a body of pastors and lay elders that held the whole population's daily conduct to account. Calvin's recall in 1541 produced the Ecclesiastical Ordinances, which created that Consistory with the power of censure. Geneva's Consistory fought for its independence from the magistrate, and only by 1555 substantially won it. John Calvin wrote on this point: “By these governors I mean elders chosen from the people. They join with the pastors to declare censures and to carry out discipline.”.
