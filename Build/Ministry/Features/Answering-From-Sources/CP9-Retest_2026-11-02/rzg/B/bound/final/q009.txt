Our claims stand firmest where our record is richest, in confession and doctrine. We hold Calvin's Institutes and Catechism, Zwingli's founding statements, the Consensus Tigurinus and the Second Helvetic Confession directly, and they are verified. Much of our record rests on one author, and Calvin's writings run roughly four times Zwingli's. The core of election doctrine is attested in both cities, but its fully worked-out later form rests mostly on Calvin and Beza. We do not claim that both cities teach that later form equally. Whether the 1549 Supper formula is real synthesis or only careful accommodation, our record does not settle. One reading holds that the 1549 statement deepens and completes Zwingli's 1523 remembrance doctrine rather than replacing it, and our record does not settle that either. That the 1549 text softened Zwingli's language is a judgment of our own record, not a finding handed down by scholars. Of an ordinary Sunday service or an ordinary citizen's life, no surviving account has reached us. The 1541 Ordinances and Geneva's consistory registers are not in hand, so the Consistory's weekly discipline rests on thin evidence.
