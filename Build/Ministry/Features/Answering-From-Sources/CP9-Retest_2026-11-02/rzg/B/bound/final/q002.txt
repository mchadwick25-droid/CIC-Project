To us, Jesus is what the whole church says he is, and that was never our own quarrel, not with Rome and not between our own two cities. What is distinctly ours is what we were taught about who is chosen in him. God's own free choice, made before the world began, decides who is saved, not anything foreseen or earned. We hold that as a settled ground, not a threat. Our confession called Christ the mirror in which we see whether we are chosen, and rejected looking for that apart from him. For a mind that would not settle, we turned attention outward to Christ, once, rather than inward, endlessly. On the Lord's Supper, Zurich and Geneva agreed in 1549 to distinguish the bread from the reality it points to, and never to disjoin the two. Our side claims that formula is a genuine synthesis, and our record does not settle whether it was that or only words both parties could sign. How an ordinary believer lived with Christ from day to day has not reached us in that believer's own words.
