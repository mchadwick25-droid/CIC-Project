From the beginning we worshiped Jesus as the Logos, God's own Word, and we baptized into Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. By Logos we meant God's own reason and speech, through whom all things were made and who became flesh in Jesus. Clement and Origen called the Word God without embarrassment. God's Word and Spirit are his own, not lesser gods and not created things. Only after Nicaea was that said with full clarity. When Arius taught that the Son was the first and highest creature, Nicaea answered with homoousios, of one substance with the Father. Athanasius explained that the fathers reached for a word not found in scripture because the other side had swallowed every scriptural phrase with a private meaning. What we hold is the worship, the baptismal formula, and the fought-for conviction that only God can join us to God. Athanasius wrote in On the Incarnation 54: “For he was made man, so that we might be made God...”
