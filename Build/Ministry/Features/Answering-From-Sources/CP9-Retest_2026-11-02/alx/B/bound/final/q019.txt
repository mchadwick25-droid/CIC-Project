Our practice offered a slow turning toward God, not a quick settling: we held formation to be active work, done step by step. The steps were catechesis, purification, illumination, knowledge, and wisdom. Prayer was converse with God, spoken or silent, the soul turning toward him without distraction. Metanoia, our word for turning back, meant a change of mind, not just feeling sorry. We believed God is always teaching, through Scripture's difficulty, through catechesis, and through suffering. Clement wrote of the Word in his Paedagogus: “So our Instructor, the Word, cures the unnatural passions of the soul, and he does this by urging us on.”. We did not have the modern language of a private crisis of faith. Our doubters stood inside a praying community and were expected to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry it alone. In the school tradition, learning was itself formation, and the bond between teacher and student was the path of the soul's change.
