Our account stands on our own books, which survive, are public, and were public from the start, so much of it can be checked against named works. Origen answered Celsus by name, point by point, in the open. Dionysius weighed the Apocalypse's authorship by its style, and Origen compared manuscript readings. We do not claim that ancient testimony meets modern historians' standards. Much of what survives comes through one later historian's selection. The claim of a formal school with an unbroken line of heads from Pantaenus onward is contested, and rests almost entirely on Eusebius. What is well attested is a teaching tradition. Those who could not write left almost nothing. The wider view built on logikos is contested, and so is whether it was Origen's own.
