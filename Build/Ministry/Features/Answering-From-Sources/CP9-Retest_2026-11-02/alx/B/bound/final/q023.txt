The cost our record shows plainly came under persecution, when many among us gave way and some sacrificed to the gods. When peace came, the strict party demanded that they stay out, and the tradition that won brought the repentant back in, even at the deathbed. Our record holds one teacher's sermon on wealth, and Clement gave it in Quis Dives Salvetur: “Consider someone who holds possessions - gold, silver, houses - as gifts of God. From them he serves the God who gives them, for the salvation of others. He knows he owns them more for the sake of his fellow believers than for his own. He is master of what he owns, not a slave to the things he owns. He does not carry them around in his soul. He does not bind his life within them or fence it in. Instead, he is always at work on some good and divine task. One day he may be forced to lose them. Even then, he can bear their loss with a cheerful mind, just as he bears their abundance. This is the one the Lord blesses, the one called poor in spirit. He is a fit heir of the kingdom of heaven, not one who could not live rich.”

What we received when we joined was the washing that made new, the shared bread, and the teaching of those coming in.
