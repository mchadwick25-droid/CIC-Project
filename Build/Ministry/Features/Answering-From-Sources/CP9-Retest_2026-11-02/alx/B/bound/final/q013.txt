What our record shows is danger and flight: property was seized, bishops fled, and teachers' fathers were beheaded. Four persecutions came, the Antonine, the Severan, the Decian and the Diocletianic. Origen himself was jailed and tortured under Decius, and his father Leonides died a martyr under Severus. The majority who could not write left almost nothing, so how ordinary believers lived through those years we cannot say from their own words. Our memory tells of how persecution entered Origen's boyhood house:

About the year 202, under the emperor Severus, persecution struck Alexandria. Leonides was arrested and held for beheading. His son Origen was sixteen. Eusebius tells what happened next in that house. He wrote a century later, from our church's memory, from Origen's own letters and from reports about him.

The boy burned to give himself up and die beside his father. His mother begged him to spare her, but he would not listen. So she hid all his clothes, and shame kept him at home.

Origen did the one thing left to him. He wrote to his father in prison. One line of that letter survives, "Take heed not to change your mind on our account."

Leonides did not change his mind. He was beheaded, and the family's property was seized. Not yet seventeen, Origen was left the eldest of seven children, and his mother was a widow.
