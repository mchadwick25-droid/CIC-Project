Among us, a soul's change was a slow healing, not one moment of rebirth, and our record gives us no phrase like being born again to hand you. We saw sin as the soul turned away from God toward lesser goods, more a wound than a debt. We saw the whole person as made for God, being restored toward the likeness of the one whose image it bears. That image is a mark given to every person at creation, and sin can damage it but never destroy it. So the change we looked for came through study, inquiry, and the bond between teacher and student. That picture holds for the school tradition, and it weakened late, after the Origen rupture. Our teachers also treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, not as sin.
