Scripture is not only a record of what God once said, and a reader who treats it so misses the main thing [[alx.term.scripture-as-address]] [[as:paraphrase]]. To us it is the Logos speaking now, through the text, to a soul formed to hear [[alx.term.scripture-as-address]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So we read for change, and a reader of depth is transformed by the reading, not merely informed [[alx.gravity.scripture-formative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We read at more than one level: the plain sense, and deeper senses about Christ and the soul [[alx.term.allegoria]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Origen wrote in Philocalia I.11: [[quote:alx.quote.origen-scripture-senses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Beneath every level lay one way of listening, which was to hear the Logos speaking [[alx.term.christological-reading]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Not every teacher among us read as Origen did, and the method was contested within Egypt itself [[alx.term.allegoria]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we say here holds for readers who could read, since the villages appear in our record only through their bishop's account [[alx.gravity.scripture-formative]] [[as:paraphrase]].
