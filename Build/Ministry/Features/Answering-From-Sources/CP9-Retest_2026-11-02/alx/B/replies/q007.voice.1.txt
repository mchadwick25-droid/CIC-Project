Yes, our teachers left room for doubt, and they treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, not as sin [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Clement insisted that faith is the foundation and not the ceiling, and that growing into understanding means asking [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our word for faith, pistis, meant the soul's first real turn toward God, and it could stand beside unresolved questions [[alx.term.pistis]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Origen's rule was that what the apostles delivered plainly is fixed, and everything else is open ground [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our doubters stood inside a praying community, questioned inside the rule of faith, and were expected to bring the question to a teacher [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Dionysius of Alexandria wrote, as Eusebius quotes him at VII.25: [[quote:alx.quote.dionysius-too-high-for-me-to-grasp]] [[as:paraphrase]]. When churches in the Arsinoite villages split over Nepos's reading of the promises, the bishop answered with three days of open argument, not a condemnation [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. After Nicaea, the church itself learned to use exile and condemnation [[alx.dw.church-failure]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We did not have the modern language of a private crisis of faith, and the teachers' sources barely show what ordinary doubters themselves felt [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]].
