Our practice offered a slow turning toward God, not a quick settling: we held formation to be active work, done step by step [[alx.gravity.soul-transformation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The steps were catechesis, purification, illumination, knowledge, and wisdom [[alx.gravity.soul-transformation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Prayer was converse with God, spoken or silent, the soul turning toward him without distraction [[alx.term.prayer]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Metanoia, our word for turning back, meant a change of mind, not just feeling sorry [[alx.term.metanoia]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We believed God is always teaching, through Scripture's difficulty, through catechesis, and through suffering [[alx.gravity.divine-pedagogy]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Clement wrote of the Word in his Paedagogus: [[quote:alx.quote.clement-instructor]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We did not have the modern language of a private crisis of faith [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our doubters stood inside a praying community and were expected to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry it alone [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In the school tradition, learning was itself formation, and the bond between teacher and student was the path of the soul's change [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[as:paraphrase]].
