Among us, a soul's change was a slow healing, not one moment of rebirth, and our record gives us no phrase like being born again to hand you [[alx.term.psyche]] [[alx.term.hamartia]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We saw sin as the soul turned away from God toward lesser goods, more a wound than a debt [[alx.term.hamartia]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We saw the whole person as made for God, being restored toward the likeness of the one whose image it bears [[alx.term.psyche]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That image is a mark given to every person at creation, and sin can damage it but never destroy it [[alx.term.eikon]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So the change we looked for came through study, inquiry, and the bond between teacher and student [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That picture holds for the school tradition, and it weakened late, after the Origen rupture [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our teachers also treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, not as sin [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]].
