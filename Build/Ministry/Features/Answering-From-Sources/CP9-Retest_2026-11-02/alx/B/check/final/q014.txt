Who decided disputed belief changed across our history: first the teachers, then the bishop, and at the end the council. Early on, teachers argued with books, lectures, and letters, and authority went to proven wisdom. A didaskalos was a teacher whose authority rested on visible wisdom, not on a title. The bishop governed how the community was formed and guarded what it received. In the middle years the bishop's judgment grew, and Demetrius condemned Origen's ordination. Two things were true at once and could not be made one: the teacher was trusted because others saw that he saw, and the bishop held the office handed down. At Nicaea, bishops from everywhere ruled on the disputed confession for all, and the emperor called the council and backed the council with force.
