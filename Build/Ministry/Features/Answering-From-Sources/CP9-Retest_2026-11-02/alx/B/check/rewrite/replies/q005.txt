Among us, who settled a dispute changed over our years: first the teachers, then the bishop, and at the end the council [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Early on, teachers argued with books, lectures, and letters, and authority went to proven wisdom [[alx.dw.councils]] [[alx.term.didaskalos]] [[as:paraphrase]].

In the middle years, the bishop's judgment grew, and Demetrius condemned Origen's ordination [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

At the end, bishops from everywhere met at Nicaea and ruled on the disputed confession for all, with an emperor who called the council and backed it with force [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Our best picture of deciding well is Dionysius at Arsinoe, and our harder picture is the same power with an empire behind it [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].
