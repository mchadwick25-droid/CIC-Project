We did not hide our failures, and our record leaves the wounds visible. Our greatest teacher was driven out by his own bishop, not by pagans, and the church remembered both men rather than erasing either. Under persecution many gave way, and some sacrificed to the gods. When peace came, the community fought bitterly over those who gave way. The strict party demanded that those who gave way stay out. The tradition that won brought the repentant back in, even at the deathbed, and Dionysius defended doing so. After Nicaea, the church itself learned to use exile and condemnation. Some of what was done with the power of exile and condemnation, our own sources report without pride. Where the restoring side won, we refused two easy exits: we did not pretend the failure away, and we did not make the failed unforgivable.
