The steps were catechesis, purification, illumination, knowledge, and wisdom. Prayer was converse with God, spoken or silent, the soul turning toward him without distraction. Metanoia, our word for turning back, meant a change of mind, not just feeling sorry. We believed God is always teaching, through Scripture's difficulty, through catechesis, and through suffering. We did not have the modern language of a private crisis of faith. Our doubters stood inside a praying community and were expected to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry it alone. In the school tradition, learning was itself formation, and the bond between teacher and student was the path of the soul's change.
