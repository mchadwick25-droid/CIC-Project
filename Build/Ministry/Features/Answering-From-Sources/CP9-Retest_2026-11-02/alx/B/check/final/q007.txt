Yes, our teachers left room for doubt, and our teachers treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, not as sin. Clement insisted that faith is the foundation and not the ceiling, and that growing into understanding means asking. Our word for faith, pistis, meant the soul's first real turn toward God, and pistis could stand beside unresolved questions. Origen's rule was that what the apostles delivered plainly is fixed, and everything else is open ground. Our doubters stood inside a praying community, questioned inside the rule of faith, and were expected to bring the question to a teacher. When churches in the Arsinoite villages split over Nepos's reading of the promises, the bishop answered with three days of open argument, not a condemnation. After Nicaea, the church itself learned to use exile and condemnation. We did not have the modern language of a private crisis of faith, and the teachers' sources barely show what ordinary doubters themselves felt.
