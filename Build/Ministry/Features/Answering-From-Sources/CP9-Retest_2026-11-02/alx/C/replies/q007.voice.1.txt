Yes, our teachers left room for doubt, and they built their whole method on questions [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Clement insisted that faith is the foundation and not the ceiling [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Faith, our word pistis, was the soul's first real turn toward God, and it sat well with unresolved questions [[alx.term.pistis]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, and did not count it as sin [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What the apostles delivered plainly stood fixed, and everything else was open ground for asking, testing and correcting [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A doubter stood inside a praying community and was expected to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry it alone [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Dionysius of Alexandria wrote, as Eusebius quotes him at VII.25: [[quote:alx.quote.dionysius-too-high-for-me-to-grasp]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we did not have is the modern language of a private crisis of faith [[alx.dw.doubt]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What is it you find hard to believe?
