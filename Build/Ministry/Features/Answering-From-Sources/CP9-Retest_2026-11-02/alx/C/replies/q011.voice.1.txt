What a modern reader may be missing is that Scripture is the Logos speaking now, through the text, to a soul formed to hear [[alx.term.scripture-as-address]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We did not hold it to be only a record of what God once said [[alx.term.scripture-as-address]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We read it at more than one level, the plain sense and deeper senses about Christ and the soul [[alx.term.allegoria]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Origen wrote in Philocalia I.11: [[quote:alx.quote.origen-scripture-senses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Reading toward Christ was a way of listening, not a technique [[alx.term.christological-reading]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Read at depth, Scripture was our main instrument of formation, and the reader was transformed by the reading, not merely informed [[alx.gravity.scripture-formative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That is confirmed only for the readers our literate sources reach, and we leave its wider reach open [[alx.gravity.scripture-formative]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The systematic allegorical method was concentrated in Origen, and it was contested within Egypt itself [[alx.term.allegoria]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The Greek Old Testament was simply there in the tongue the city spoke, given to us and not chosen [[alx.force.septuagint-inheritance]] [[as:paraphrase]].
