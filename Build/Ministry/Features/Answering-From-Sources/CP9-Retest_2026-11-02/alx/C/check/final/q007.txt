Yes, our teachers left room for doubt, and our teachers built their whole method on questions. Clement insisted that faith is the foundation and not the ceiling. Faith, our word pistis, was the soul's first real turn toward God, and pistis sat well with unresolved questions. We treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, and did not count it as sin. What the apostles delivered plainly stood fixed, and everything else was open ground for asking, testing and correcting. A doubter stood inside a praying community and was expected to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry the question alone. What we did not have is the modern language of a private crisis of faith. What is it you find hard to believe?
