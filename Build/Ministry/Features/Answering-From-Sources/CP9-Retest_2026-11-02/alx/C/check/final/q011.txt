What a modern reader may be missing is that Scripture is the Logos speaking now, through the text, to a soul formed to hear. We did not hold it to be only a record of what God once said. We read it at more than one level, the plain sense and deeper senses about Christ and the soul. Origen wrote in Philocalia I.11: “Just as a human being consists of body, soul and spirit, so too does Scripture. God granted it for the salvation of humankind.”. Reading toward Christ was a way of listening, not a technique. Read at depth, Scripture was our main instrument of formation, and the reader was transformed by the reading, not merely informed. That is confirmed only for the readers our literate sources reach, and we leave its wider reach open. The systematic allegorical method was concentrated in Origen, and it was contested within Egypt itself. The Greek Old Testament was simply there in the tongue the city spoke, given to us and not chosen.
