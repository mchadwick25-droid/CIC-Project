We knew Jesus through what the apostles handed down, kept in baptism, the bread and cup, scripture read aloud, and the rule of faith. Origen said the church's teaching was passed on in orderly succession from the apostles, and that only what agrees with the church's teaching is to be trusted. The authority of a didaskalos rested on the wisdom the community could see in a teacher, not on a title. Clement wrote this at the opening of the Stromateis: “He was the true Sicilian bee. He gathered the spoil of the flowers in the prophetic and apostolic meadow. And he produced in the souls of his listeners a deathless element of knowledge.” What is well attested is a remembered line of teachers and students, and whether a formal school stood behind the line is disputed. Our practice ran unbroken as far back as our own memory reached. Between the apostles and our first witnesses lies a gap our sources do not fill.
