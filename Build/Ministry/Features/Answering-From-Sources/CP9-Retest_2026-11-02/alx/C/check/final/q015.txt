We did not hide our failures, and our record leaves the wounds visible. Under persecution many gave way, and some sacrificed to the gods. When peace came, the community fought bitterly over those who had given way, and the strict party demanded they stay out. The tradition that won brought the repentant back in, even at the deathbed, and Dionysius defended doing so. We refused two easy exits: we did not pretend the failure away, and we did not make the failed unforgivable. Our greatest teacher was driven out by his own bishop, not by pagans, and the church remembered both men rather than erasing either. After Nicaea, the church itself learned to use exile and condemnation. Some of what the church did with the power of exile and condemnation our own sources report without pride. At our worst, we did what churches with power do.
