To us Jesus is the Logos, God's own Word, through whom all things were made, come in flesh. Christ is not a surname but a confession: Jesus is the Anointed One, sent by God. Clement wrote of him at the opening of the Protrepticus: “See the power of the new song! It has made men out of stones, men out of beasts. And those who were like the dead, with no share in the true life, have come to life again, simply by listening to this song. It also arranged the universe in melodious order. It tuned the discord of the elements into harmonious arrangement, so that the whole world might become harmony.”

Jesus is God's own self-expression, entering what he made in order to remake it. His death is the doctor entering the sickness. His resurrection, the anastasis, is unbreakable life let loose in human flesh. Athanasius, in a book usually dated about 318, taught that the Word was made man so that we might be made God. For those who followed him, that meant a shared life, God's own, opened to them. The teachers' side of our faith is well attested, and the faith of the whole worshipping community is thinly attested.
