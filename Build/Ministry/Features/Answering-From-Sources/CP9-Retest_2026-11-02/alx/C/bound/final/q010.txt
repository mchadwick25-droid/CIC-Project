We read Scripture as God's main lesson, in a school that was the whole of creation. We heard it as the Logos speaking now, through the text, to a soul formed to hear. So reading was a practice of formation: meeting the Logos at the depth the reader's formation allowed. We read at more than one level, the plain sense and deeper senses about Christ and the soul, a method contested within Egypt itself. What the apostles delivered plainly stood fixed, and everything else was open ground, where asking questions was itself a way of loving God. In the school tradition, study and the bond between teacher and student were the very path of the soul's change. Clement opened the Stromateis with a debt to his last teacher: “He was the true Sicilian bee. He gathered the spoil of the flowers in the prophetic and apostolic meadow. And he produced in the souls of his listeners a deathless element of knowledge.”. The school side of this is well attested, while the community side is thinly attested. The villagers appear only in their bishop's own account of persuading them, never in their own voice.
