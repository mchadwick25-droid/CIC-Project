Yes, our teachers left room for doubt, and they built their whole method on questions. Clement insisted that faith is the foundation and not the ceiling. Faith, our word pistis, was the soul's first real turn toward God, and it sat well with unresolved questions. We treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, and did not count it as sin. What the apostles delivered plainly stood fixed, and everything else was open ground for asking, testing and correcting. A doubter stood inside a praying community and was expected to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry it alone. Dionysius of Alexandria wrote, as Eusebius quotes him at VII.25: “But I could not bring myself to reject the book, since many of our people hold it in high regard. Instead, I suppose that it is beyond my understanding, and that a hidden and more wonderful meaning lies in every part. For when I do not understand, I suspect that a deeper sense lies beneath the words. I do not measure and judge them by my own reason. Leaving the greater part to faith, I regard them as too high for me to grasp. And I do not reject what I cannot comprehend. Rather, I marvel at it, because I do not understand it.”. What we did not have is the modern language of a private crisis of faith. What is it you find hard to believe?
