We kept a real boundary around what we belonged to: one baptism, one bread, the scriptures, and the rule of faith. The boundary was kept by argument and exclusion from communion, and after Nicaea by harder tools. Clement wrote in Stromateis I.5: “Perhaps, too, philosophy was given to the Greeks directly and in the first place, until the Lord should call the Greeks. For philosophy was a guardian to bring the Greek mind to Christ, as the law was for the Hebrews. So philosophy was a preparation. It paved the way for the one who is made perfect in Christ.”. Whether Origen held the restoration of all things as settled doctrine is contested, since his texts survive through Rufinus and Jerome. Our teachers spoke of both faith and works, and said we must love God and our neighbour and do the Father's will. The soul has a real power to turn toward God or away, and God does not compel it. For our own who fell under persecution, the tradition that won here brought the repentant back in, even at the deathbed.
