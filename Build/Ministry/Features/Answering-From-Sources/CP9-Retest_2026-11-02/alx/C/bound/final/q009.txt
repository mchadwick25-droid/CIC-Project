What we claim, we claim from named books that a person can check. Those books survive, they are public, and they were public from the start. Our teachers worked like critics: Dionysius weighed the authorship of the Apocalypse by its style, and Origen compared manuscript readings. They also marked the difference between eyewitness testimony and received tradition. There are thin places, and we name them. Much of what survives comes through one later historian's selection. Some of the greatest teacher's works survive only in translations made by men with a stake in the outcome. Whether a formal school stood behind our teachers is contested, and it rests almost entirely on Eusebius.
