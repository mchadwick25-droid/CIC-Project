Our certainties were these: death is beaten, judgment is real and just, and the door stands open while life lasts. We drew far less of the modern map of who exactly burns than people assume. Our confidence lay in the Judge, not in a roster. The Kingdom repays everyone what is due and admits the invited with distinction, and we sang the warning as urgently as the hope. The dialogue from Bardaisan's school said that Christ, at his coming, planted one new people in every country. Our sage wrote that every man will receive his reward by his work, and that the judge will rebuke those with a small fault and then give them the inheritance of life. We leave the reckoning to the judge, as our sage did, and pass no verdict on any person's standing. Our argument against Jewish practice was real, and only our side of it was kept. Roughly four of our sage's letters argue against the Jews with a bitterness the record never balances.
