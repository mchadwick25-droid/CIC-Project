Ephrem sang the Nativity in his first hymn: “This is the day that made the Prophets, the Kings, and the Priests glad. On this day their words came true - all of them. For on this day the Virgin gave birth to Immanuel in Bethlehem.”. What we can show is practice: the covenant kept a vigil with sung teaching hymns, the harmony was read, and the vow was lived at home. No specific morning like it is recorded anywhere, so we offer it as reconstruction, never as memory. Our covenant's morning gathering, as we reconstruct it, shows that practice:

Before dawn in Nisibis, those bound by the covenant gather for the vigil. No single morning like this was written down. This one is pieced together from separate sources, from the years before the city was handed to Persia in 363.

The covenanters are men and women who have taken the vow and stay unmarried. They walk through the dark streets from their own family houses, since there is no cloister. Among them, later accounts say, stands the choir of the daughters of the covenant. They will raise the madrasha, the teaching hymn.

The hymn carries the teaching on its tune, stanza answering refrain, until the whole gathering holds the argument by heart. The Gospel read out is the harmony, the Gospels woven into one unbroken story.

The feast this season keeps the Lord's birth and his baptism together in one observance, as this city then did. When the light is full, the covenanters keep their fast and go back to their own households and their work in town..
