To us, Jesus is the Only-Begotten of God, the Ihidaya, our own name for him. Ihidaya is also our word for the vowed celibate, and the two senses share one root. Jesus left his place with God and made his home in the Virgin, and by being born as we are born he became the brother of many. In Jesus the words of the prophets, kings, and priests came true, and the Nativity hymns sing the day that made them glad. The death and rising of Jesus are one rescue, for Jesus went down into Sheol, the world of the dead, and came out into the Kingdom. The rising of Jesus is our promise that Jesus will bring us out after him. To those who followed Jesus, he is the Physician, and his coming is medicine for a wounded people. Jesus is God, we said, in our own way of speaking, through a chain of Scripture's own names rather than a philosopher's definition. Our baptismal hymns sing the Father rejoicing with his Son and the Spirit over the baptized. Our native tongue for this faith was praise, and our teachers warned against prying scrutiny into the Godhead. The Persian sage set down our confession in Demonstration XVII: “It is a sure thing with us that Jesus our Lord is God, the Son of God, the King, and the King's Son. He is Light of Light, Creator, and Counsellor. He is Guide, the Way, Redeemer, and Shepherd. He is Gatherer, the Door, the Pearl, and the Lamp.”.
