What the modern reader misses is the hidden truth that each word of Scripture carries. We read each word as a symbol, a raza, bound to the truth it shows, the shrara. The symbol is not a stand-in that a reader picks at will. So Eden was read toward the cross, Adam toward Christ, and the ark toward the church. A whole chapter's worth of meaning was found in a single pearl. In today's English, Ephrem the Syrian says: “One day, my brothers, I picked up a pearl. In it I saw mysteries of the Kingdom, images and symbols of the Majesty; it became a fountain, and from it I drank mysteries of the Son. I set it, my brothers, on the palm of my hand so I could examine it. I went to look at it from one side, and it showed faces on every side.”

We did not read Genesis as science, and that was because we read for something else. Our teaching also came in song, because doctrine was taught by singing it. The Gospel reached us as one continuous story, woven from four accounts.
