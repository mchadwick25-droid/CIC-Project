Authority came to a person in one of three ways among us: by being made a bishop, by taking the vow, or by being found trustworthy over time. Bishops stood first, and Nisibis sang of Jacob and Babu and Valgash as the city's fathers, who prayed for it. The Persian church had enough bishops to quarrel over precedence. The covenant's vowed men and women carried a standing of their own. They promised a celibate life for good, stayed in town, and served the congregation in the fast, the watch, the vigil and the singing. A teacher whose word held could also write to bishops in a synod's name. Aphrahat did so, and no one now knows his rank. The Persian church's one claimed head, Papa, was contested by his own bishops. No settled titled head stood over the Persian bishops in our years. Which path finally outranked the others, we never settled while we lived. What it felt like to move among these paths day to day is not attested. The covenant's formal inner structure is also thinly documented.
