No, Scripture was supreme and everywhere among us, yet it always reached us through others. It came through the church's worship, the harmony that gave the Gospel its shape, our teachers, and the covenant's practice. Asked whether the Bible was our only authority, we would have found the question strange. The book and the community that sang it were never rivals. We read each word of Scripture as a raza, a symbol bound to the truth it carries, which we called the shrara. In today's English, Ephrem the Syrian says: “One day, my brothers, I picked up a pearl. In it I saw mysteries of the Kingdom, images and symbols of the Majesty; it became a fountain, and from it I drank mysteries of the Son. I set it, my brothers, on the palm of my hand so I could examine it. I went to look at it from one side, and it showed faces on every side. I discovered that the Son cannot be fully grasped, since he is wholly Light.”. Among persons, no one authority stood alone either. Covenant standing and episcopal office ran side by side, and this never resolved inside our years. Aphrahat drafted a synodal letter in the bishops' name, and his own rank is unknown. Papa, the one claimed head of the Persian hierarchy, was contested by his own bishops.
