We would have met you as someone still raising a house, because faith among us was a building that goes up piece by piece. What we distrusted was not the struggling believer. It was the prying scrutinizer, who would take the Godhead apart like a problem to be solved. Aphrahat, the Persian sage, wrote in Demonstration I: “In the same way the true Stone, our Lord Jesus Christ, is the foundation of all our faith. On him, on this Stone, faith is laid; and resting on faith, the whole structure rises until it is complete.”. Aphrahat gave an order, not a short list. First a man believes, and when he believes, he loves. When he loves, he hopes; when he hopes, he is justified, and then he is made perfect. Fasting, prayer, alms and meekness are built on that faith. For the one who falls, Aphrahat named penitence as the medicine for the wound. We name no single act that secures life, and we pass no verdict on you, for Aphrahat leaves the reckoning to the judge.
