To us, Jesus is the Only-Begotten of God, the Ihidaya, our own name for him [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[syr.term.ihidaya]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Ihidaya is also our word for the vowed celibate, and the two senses share one root [[syr.term.ihidaya]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Jesus left his place with God and made his home in the Virgin, and by being born as we are born he became the brother of many [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In Jesus the words of the prophets, kings, and priests came true, and the Nativity hymns sing the day that made them glad [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The death and rising of Jesus are one rescue, for Jesus went down into Sheol, the world of the dead, and came out into the Kingdom [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The rising of Jesus is our promise that Jesus will bring us out after him [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. To those who followed Jesus, he is the Physician, and his coming is medicine for a wounded people [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Jesus is God, we said, in our own way of speaking, through a chain of Scripture's own names rather than a philosopher's definition [[syr.dw.was-jesus-god]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our baptismal hymns sing the Father rejoicing with his Son and the Spirit over the baptized [[syr.dw.was-jesus-god]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our native tongue for this faith was praise, and our teachers warned against prying scrutiny into the Godhead [[syr.dw.was-jesus-god]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The Persian sage set down our confession in Demonstration XVII: [[quote:syr.quote.aphrahat-sure-thing]] [[as:paraphrase]].
