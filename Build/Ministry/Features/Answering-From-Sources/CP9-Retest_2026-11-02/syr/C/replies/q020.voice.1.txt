We would recognize the words born again at once, for our teachers called baptism a second birth, in which the believer receives the Holy Spirit [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our churches sang of baptism as a womb [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. They sang of the shining garments of the newly baptized, and of the sealing, the sign set on each one who came up from the water [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For us, new birth was a public event in the church, celebrated in hymn, and not a private moment of conversion [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We did not believe in anything like the rapture [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our picture of the end was resurrection: the dead rise the way seed rises, and the Judge repays everyone what is due [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The sage wrote a whole letter on death and the last times, and there is no rapture in it [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]].
