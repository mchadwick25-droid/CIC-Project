At Nisibis, a baptistery still stands where we worshipped, and its own inscription dates it securely to the years of our bishop and the siege [[syr.dw.remains]] [[as:paraphrase]]. It is the only building of ours from those years that can be dated securely [[syr.dw.remains]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At Edessa, almost nothing remains, because the modern city sits directly on the ancient one [[syr.dw.remains]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The church that the flood destroyed lives only in a chronicler's line [[syr.dw.remains]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The Chronicle of Edessa records in its entry for the year 513 of the Greeks: [[quote:syr.quote.chronicle-flood-line]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That chronicle was compiled in the sixth century from the city's archives, and it is not an eyewitness account [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The flood line is our oldest dated word, though we cannot be sure it stood in the first record and was not added later [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[as:paraphrase]].
