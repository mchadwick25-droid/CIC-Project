To us Jesus is God, the Only-Begotten, whom we call the Ihidaya, and we said so in our own way of speaking. The Only-Begotten left his place with God and made his home in the Virgin. Asked about the Trinity, we answered from our font, where our baptismal hymns sing the Father rejoicing with his Son and the Spirit over the baptized. Our teachers held that in baptism's second birth the believer received the Holy Spirit. Our teachers also warned against prying scrutiny into the Godhead, because what God is, is better sung than dissected. Our own tongue for that faith was praise. Our tradition keeps a memory of Jacob, our bishop of Nisibis: Jacob, bishop of Nisibis, is remembered among the bishops at the great council of Nicaea. In the year 325, bishops from the whole world gathered there to answer Arius. Our tradition holds Jacob to be one voice from our Syriac-speaking frontier. He stood with those who confessed the Son as true God. Nothing survives of what Jacob said or did at the council. The lists of its signers were pieced together by later hands, and they carry his name among the assembled bishops. Theodoret of Cyrus, a Greek church historian of the next century, also counts him among them. No document from the fourth century itself confirms that he was there. At home in Nisibis, Ephrem praised Jacob as the city's wall.
