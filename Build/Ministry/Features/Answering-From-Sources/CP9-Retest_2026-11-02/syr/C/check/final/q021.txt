At Nisibis, a baptistery still stands where we worshipped, and its own inscription dates it securely to the years of our bishop and the siege. It is the only building of ours from those years that can be dated securely. The church that the flood destroyed lives only in a chronicler's line. The Chronicle of Edessa records in its entry for the year 513 of the Greeks: “And they destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians.”. The flood line is our oldest dated word, though we cannot be sure it stood in the first record and was not added later. What is known of us comes from letters that date themselves to the year, hymns copied by later hands, and that short city chronicle.
