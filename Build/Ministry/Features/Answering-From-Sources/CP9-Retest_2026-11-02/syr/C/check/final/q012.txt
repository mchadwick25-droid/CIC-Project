No, Scripture was supreme and everywhere among us, yet Scripture always reached us through others. Scripture came through the church's worship, the harmony that gave the Gospel its shape, our teachers, and the covenant's practice. Asked whether the Bible was our only authority, we would have found the question strange. The book and the community that sang the book were never rivals. We read each word of Scripture as a raza, a symbol bound to the truth it carries, which we called the shrara. Among persons, no one authority stood alone either. Covenant standing and episcopal office ran side by side, and the question of authority never resolved inside our years. Aphrahat drafted a synodal letter in the bishops' name, and Aphrahat's own rank is unknown. Papa, the one claimed head of the Persian hierarchy, was contested by his own bishops.
