We read Scripture for symbols, each one bound to the hidden truth it carries, a pair we named raza and shrara. A raza was never a stand-in that a reader picks at will. So Eden was read toward the cross, Adam toward Christ, and the ark toward the church. We did not read Genesis as science, though not because we doubted the text. We read for something else, and a whole chapter's worth of meaning could be found in a single pearl. The Gospel we read in worship was one woven story, not four separate books. Scripture was supreme among us, yet Scripture always reached us through worship, the woven Gospel, our teachers, and the covenant's practice. The book and the community that sang it were never rivals. Among us, doctrine was taught by singing it, in the teaching hymn we call the madrasha.
