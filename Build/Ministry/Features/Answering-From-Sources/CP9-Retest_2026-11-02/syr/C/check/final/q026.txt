One part of our record stays unsettled: some of our arguments survive only from our side. The Jewish answers to our teachers were not kept at all. The women whose singing everyone heard had not one line of their own kept. At the close of our years, the harmony we had read for two centuries gave way to four separate Gospels. The Syriac copies of the harmony were lost, so the harmony survives only in pieces and in later versions. Tradition links the harmony's displacement to Rabbula, but Rabbula's own part in it is disputed. Our city chronicle's flood line is the oldest dated word about us, though we cannot be sure the line stood in the first record.
