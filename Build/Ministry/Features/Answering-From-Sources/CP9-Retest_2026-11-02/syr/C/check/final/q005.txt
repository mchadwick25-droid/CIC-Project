No standing court settled disputed belief among us, because the right to decide was still being worked out. Bishops, occasional synods and teachers whose word held each carried weight. In practice, we met disputed belief with teaching, with answers in writing, and with our own boundary. The sage himself drafted a letter in the name of assembled bishops and clergy. The Persian bishops fought over their own chief's claim to first place. The persecution left the chief seat empty for twenty years. Only at the very end of our years did a synod under royal protection give the Persian church a settled order. As for evidence, our letters date themselves to the year. Our hymns were copied by later hands into books that still exist. A short city chronicle was drawn out of real archives.
