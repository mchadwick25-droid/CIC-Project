Among us, a person came to authority in one of three ways: by being made a bishop, by taking the covenant vow, or by being found trustworthy over time. Authority lay first with shepherds, and Nisibis sang of its bishops Jacob, Babu and Valgash as the city's fathers who prayed for it. The sage wrote that the good shepherd gives himself for the flock. The sons and daughters of the covenant promised a celibate life for good, yet stayed in town among their kin and served the congregation in the fast, the watch, the vigil and the singing. A teacher whose word held could write to bishops in a synod's name, as the sage did, and no one now knows his rank. The Persian church had bishops enough to quarrel over precedence, and Papa's claim to be head was contested by his own bishops. Which of these paths finally outranked the others, we never settled in our own years. How members lived this plural authority from day to day is not attested.
