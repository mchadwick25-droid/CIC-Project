We cannot give you the steps in order, because no order of worship survives from us. What we kept are the pieces: the font sung as a womb, the sealing set on each one, and the covenant vow taken before the water. Who stood where, and what was said first, our record never wrote down. Our churches sang of baptism as a womb, and of the shining garments of the newly baptized. Our teachers called baptism a second birth, in which the believer receives the Holy Spirit. For us, new birth was a public event in the church, celebrated in hymn, and not a private moment of conversion. In today's English, Aphrahat says: “For this reason it is right for the preachers of the Church, who sound the trumpet, to warn everyone in God's covenant before baptism. They are also to warn those who choose for themselves virginity and holiness: the young men, the virgins, and those wishing to become holy. The preachers are to warn them and say this. If your heart is set on marriage, then marry before your baptism. Otherwise you may fall in the fight and be killed. And if you are afraid of this part of the fight, turn back now, so that you do not break your brothers' hearts along with your own. And if you love your possessions, turn back from the army too. Otherwise, when the battle goes against you, you may remember your possessions and turn back to them. For there is disgrace for the one who turns back from the fight.”
