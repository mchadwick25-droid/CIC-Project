Our practices are older than our proof: when the sources begin, first-day assembly, fasting, the covenant, and the one Gospel are already there. We lived our inheritance as apostolic, but our written record shows it only from the point where the sources begin. Our story of Addai the apostle is a cherished legend, written down late, and it is not a document from the beginning. Tatian wove the four accounts into a single narrative around 172, and for some two hundred years that was the Gospel in our churches. The flood line in our city chronicle is the oldest dated word about us, though we cannot be sure the line stood in the first record and was not added later. At Nisibis a baptistery still stands, and its own inscription dates it securely to the years of our bishop and the siege. No order of worship survives from us, so the pieces are real, but how the pieces fit together on an actual morning is not known.
