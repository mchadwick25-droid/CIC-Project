We would know the words "born again" at once, though we meant something other than a private moment of conversion. Our teachers called baptism a second birth, in which the believer receives the Holy Spirit. Our churches sang of baptism as a womb. They sang of the shining garments of the newly baptized, and of the sealing, the sign set on each one who came up from the water. For us, new birth was a public event in the church, celebrated in hymn. Our hope for the end was resurrection and judgment, and we held nothing like a rapture.
