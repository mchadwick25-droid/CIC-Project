We were not hiding in catacombs, because that is another city's story. In Edessa a church building stood early enough to be destroyed in the great flood. The city's own chronicle records that loss as plainly as it records palaces. On the frontier, believers gathered openly on the first day of the week. On the Persian side the danger was of another kind. When the persecution came there, being known as a Christian made you a suspect, then you were taxed, then hunted. When Rome's emperor became a Christian, the court of the King of Kings accused Persia's bishops of loving Rome. Being Christian began to sound like being the enemy's friend.
