What we had of Jesus was the Gospel woven into one continuous story, and our churches read it in worship. Beside it stood the Law and the Prophets, read as one long promise pointing to him. We claimed no living eyewitnesses, since a century and more stood between us and the events. We confessed him as the Only-Begotten of God, the Ihidaya, our own name for him. Ephrem sang of the Nativity: “This is the day that made the Prophets, the Kings, and the Priests glad. On this day their words came true - all of them. For on this day the Virgin gave birth to Immanuel in Bethlehem.”. Our own account of the path is a story of an apostle sent to our king, and that is our cherished legend, not our evidence. Our cherished story of our founding names King Abgar and the apostle Addai:

This is how we tell our own beginning.

King Abgar of Edessa was sick when his envoys came home from Jerusalem with news of what Jesus was doing. The king wrote Jesus a letter. He had heard, he wrote, that Jesus healed without medicines or herbs and opened the eyes of the blind by his word.

Abgar called Jesus either God come down from heaven or the Son of God, and he asked him to come and heal him. He had heard that some were seeking Jesus' life, so he offered to share his own small and beautiful city.

Jesus answered, the story says, that the work he was sent for was now finished and he was going up to his Father. After that he would send one of his disciples to heal the king, and the city would be blessed.

After the ascension Addai came, one of the seventy-two, and stayed with a Jewish man named Tobias. The story says he healed the king and preached to the city, calling it away from Nebo, Bel and Taratha to the one true God.

Before he died, Addai made Aggai leader in his place. Years after Abgar died, one of his sons had Aggai's legs broken as he sat teaching in the church. Aggai died too quickly to ordain Palut. So Palut went to Antioch and was ordained by its bishop, Serapion. Serapion had himself been ordained by Zephyrinus, bishop of Rome, in the line of Simon Peter.. What the record actually shows is practice older than proof: when our sources begin, the first-day assembly, the fasting, the covenant, and the one Gospel are already there.
