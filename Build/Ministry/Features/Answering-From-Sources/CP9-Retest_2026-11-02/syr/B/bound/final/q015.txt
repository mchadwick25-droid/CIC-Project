Our tools for the fallen were real: confession, the medicine of penitence, and restoration. Our teachers spoke like field surgeons, and said the only fatal mistake is hiding the wound until it gets worse. The one who helps must not turn you away, or tell others what you show. The fallen fighter, once healed, goes back into the line. Our argument with the Jews was real and local, and it survives entirely one-sided. We saved our own harsh words and nothing of our neighbors' answers. Our greatest teacher's gift for poetry was also a gift for scorn, and his attacks flattened three different rivals into one deception. Mostly, we did not see our failures as failures. We had far fewer ways to see our own blind spots than ways to heal the fallen.
