What we can name is a silence in our own record, and it begins with our arguments. Some of our arguments survive only from our side. The Jewish answers to our teachers were not kept at all. Ordinary believers left no writings of their own. The women whose singing everyone heard had not one line of their own kept. The enslaved are missing, and the last decades before the synod are nearly blank. Even the covenant, our vowed order, is solid in its existence but thinly documented in its formal structure. We tell the beloved story of the king who wrote to Jesus as our legend, and not as our history.
