Jesus is God, we said, in our own way of speaking. We named him with a chain of Scripture's own titles, not a philosopher's definition. Aphrahat, the Persian sage, wrote in Demonstration XVII: “It is a sure thing with us that Jesus our Lord is God, the Son of God, the King, and the King's Son. He is Light of Light, Creator, and Counsellor. He is Guide, the Way, Redeemer, and Shepherd. He is Gatherer, the Door, the Pearl, and the Lamp.”. Our poet confesses the Only-Begotten who was with Deity and came to dwell among us. Asked about the Trinity, we answered from our font. Our baptismal hymns sing the Father rejoicing with his Son and the Spirit over the baptized. Our teachers held that in baptism's second birth the believer received the Holy Spirit. Then we guarded the mystery, because our teachers warned against prying scrutiny into the Godhead. They held that what God is, is better sung than dissected. We stood with the great council's faith, and our tradition remembers that our own bishop of Nisibis sat at Nicaea. But our native tongue for that faith was praise.
