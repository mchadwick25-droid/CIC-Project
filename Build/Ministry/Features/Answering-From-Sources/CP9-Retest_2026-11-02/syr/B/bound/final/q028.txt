We left the fate of those outside us to the Judge, and our confidence lay in him, not in a roster of names. Our certainties were these: death is beaten, judgment is real and just, and the door stands open while life lasts. We drew far less of the map of who exactly burns than people assume. The dialogue from Bardaisan's school said that Christ, at his coming, planted one new people in every country. Our hymns show Death himself trembling, because Jesus has plundered his house. Aphrahat wrote that every man will receive his reward by his work. Those with a small fault the judge will rebuke, and then give them the inheritance of life. No single act that secures that inheritance is named in the writings we keep, and Aphrahat leaves the reckoning to the judge. Our harshest words went against rivals and against the Jews, and only our side of that argument was kept.
