Yes: for the one who cannot quiet their own head, our pastoral craft was the medicine of penitence. Our teachers talked like field surgeons, and they said everyone in this war gets wounded. The only fatal mistake is hiding the wound until it gets worse. So we taught people to confess to one who can help. The physician must not turn you away, or tell others what you show him. Aphrahat, the Persian sage, wrote in Demonstration VII: “For all diseases there are medicines, and there is healing when a skilled physician finds them. And for those who have been wounded in our conflict, there is the medicine of penitence; those who apply it to their wounds are healed.”. The fallen fighter, once healed, goes back into the line. For the one who prays into silence, we knew that silence at its harshest. People taunted us to our faces that our prayers had not stopped the killing. We kept praying, and answered that God's faithfulness is longer than one generation's rescue. We did not pretend the silence away, and we endured inside it.
