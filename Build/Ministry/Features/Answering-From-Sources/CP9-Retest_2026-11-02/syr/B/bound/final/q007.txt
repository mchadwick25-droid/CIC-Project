Yes, there was room, because we taught that faith is a building that rises piece by piece, and not a certainty held from the first day. It rests on one foundation stone, and fasting, prayer, and love are added like courses of stone. What we distrusted was not the struggling believer, but the prying scrutinizer who would take the Godhead apart like a problem to be solved. Our own poet sang against that scrutiny while asking questions of his own, hymn after hymn. Aphrahat closed his tenth Demonstration with these words: “These things are not enough by themselves. Hear them from me without quarreling, and ask about them among brothers who are open to persuasion. Whatever you hear that truly builds up, receive. Whatever builds up strange teachings, overthrow and demolish completely. For quarreling cannot build anything up. But I, my beloved, like a stonecutter have brought stones for the building. Let wise architects carve them out and set them in the building. Everyone who labors on the building will receive a reward from the Lord of the house.”

Hard challenges were not run from either. A debater once taunted our sage that his prayers had not stopped the persecution, and the sage says the taunt troubled him, and he answered it from Scripture. For the wounded believer, our teachers talked like field surgeons: the only fatal mistake is hiding the wound until it gets worse. They said to confess to one who can help, and that the physician must not turn you away or tell others what you show him. As for prayer that goes unanswered, we knew that silence at its harshest, and we endured inside it. A private inner crisis of doubt is not something we wrote about.
