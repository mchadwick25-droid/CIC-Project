Among the sources we can check, none is a record taken under torture: they are our sage's letters, our poet's hymns and a short city chronicle [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our sage's letters date themselves to the year, and our poet's hymns survive in ancient copies [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The chronicle was drawn from real archives and records that a flood destroyed a church building in Edessa [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[syr.story.edessa-flood-201]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The chronicle was compiled later, so it is no eyewitness account, and we cannot be sure the church line stood in the first record [[syr.story.edessa-flood-201]] [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Theodoret, an outside historian, tells of Jacob's prayer that brought gnats on the siege of Nisibis, and we hold that as hagiographic tradition, not attested event [[syr.story.jacob-deliverance]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Even the Persian church's memory of its bishop Simeon's death is a hagiographic account with a historical core [[syr.story.simeon-martyrdom]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Which record do you have in mind?
