We would know the words "born again" at once, though we meant something other than a private moment of conversion [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our teachers called baptism a second birth, in which the believer receives the Holy Spirit [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our churches sang of baptism as a womb [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. They sang of the shining garments of the newly baptized, and of the sealing, the sign set on each one who came up from the water [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For us, new birth was a public event in the church, celebrated in hymn [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our hope for the end was resurrection and judgment, and we held nothing like a rapture [[syr.dw.born-again-endtimes]] [[as:paraphrase]].
