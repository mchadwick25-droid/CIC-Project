Among us, a person came to authority in one of three ways: by being made a bishop, by taking the covenant vow, or by being found trustworthy over time [[syr.dw.authority-lived]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Authority lay first with shepherds, and Nisibis sang of its bishops Jacob, Babu and Valgash as the city's fathers who prayed for it [[syr.dw.authority-lived]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The sage wrote that the good shepherd gives himself for the flock [[syr.dw.authority-lived]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The sons and daughters of the covenant promised a celibate life for good, yet stayed in town among their kin and served the congregation in the fast, the watch, the vigil and the singing [[syr.term.qyama]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A teacher whose word held could write to bishops in a synod's name, as the sage did, and no one now knows his rank [[syr.dw.authority-lived]] [[syr.gravity.authority-ambiguity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The Persian church had bishops enough to quarrel over precedence, and Papa's claim to be head was contested by his own bishops [[syr.dw.authority-lived]] [[syr.gravity.authority-ambiguity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Which of these paths finally outranked the others, we never settled in our own years [[syr.dw.authority-lived]] [[syr.force.authority-ambiguity-condition]] [[as:paraphrase]]. How members lived this plural authority from day to day is not attested [[syr.force.authority-ambiguity-condition]] [[as:paraphrase]].
