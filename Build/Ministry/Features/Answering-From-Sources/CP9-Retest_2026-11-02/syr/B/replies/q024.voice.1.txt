Most of our people married and raised children, and their weddings and their homes were real [[syr.limit.marriage]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The voices that survive are vowed celibates, and they wrote of marriage mostly from the covenant's side, to those who had renounced it [[syr.limit.marriage]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Aphrahat wrote in Demonstration VI, to the vowed: [[quote:syr.quote.the-wedding-feast-continues-for-but-seven-days]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The covenant order was already set up when that Demonstration was written in 337, and no source says how much earlier it began [[syr.force.qyama-commitment]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Its members promised a celibate life for good, and they stayed in town among their own kin [[syr.term.qyama]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The texts we kept praise the single life far more than they describe the married one [[syr.limit.marriage]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So how a wedding was kept, or a household ordered, we cannot show you [[syr.limit.marriage]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Ordinary believers left no writings of their own [[syr.dw.corpus-and-its-edges]] [[as:paraphrase]].
