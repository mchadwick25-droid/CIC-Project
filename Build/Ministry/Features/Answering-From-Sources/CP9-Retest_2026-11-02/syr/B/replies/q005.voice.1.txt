No standing court settled disputed belief among us, and the right to decide was still being worked out [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We had bishops, occasional synods, and teachers whose word carried because their teaching held [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The sage himself drafted a letter in the name of assembled bishops and clergy [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The Persian bishops fought over their own chief's claim to first place, and the persecution left the chief seat empty for twenty years [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Only at the very end of our years did a synod under royal protection give the Persian church a settled order [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In practice, we met disputed belief with teaching, with answers in writing, and with our own boundary [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our tradition remembers that one of our bishops sat at the great council of Nicaea [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our tradition keeps that memory of Jacob of Nisibis, and the lists that name him are late reconstructions, not a document of his own day: [[story:syr.story.jacob-nicaea]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What we hold as evidence for all of this is teaching texts and a later chronicle, not a record of how any one decision was made [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]].
