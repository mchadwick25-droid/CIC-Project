## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0008
1. Chapter XIV: Augustine rejects the view that a word against the Son of man is forgiven and one against the Holy Spirit is not because Christ's flesh is lesser, since the Spirit is equal to the Father and the only Son in substance.
2. Chapter XV: Augustine says the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit all forgive sins, as the Lord's Prayer and the Son's own words show, and that the Son cast out demons in the Spirit.
3. Chapter XVI: Augustine says the Spirit is given by the Father and the Son but the power and work of the Trinity are one, with scriptural examples of naming the means of an action; the Spirit divides gifts as he wills.
4. Augustine says the work of the Trinity is inseparable, so when one person acts the others act too; the Father is the origin of works because the Son is born of him and the Spirit proceeds from him.
5. Section 27: Augustine says the voice from the cloud saying this is my beloved Son is attributed to the Father, though the Son and the Spirit cooperated, since the Son was also in the Father's bosom as the Word.
6. Chapter XVII: Augustine says sins are not forgiven outside the Church, since forgiveness is by the Spirit who gathers the Church into one, so repentance outside the Church is of no use; the Spirit is the Spirit of adoption.
7. Augustine says the tongues of all nations at Pentecost signified the society of God's children among all nations, and that one held by the bond of peace in the Church spread over the nations knows he has received the Spirit, who belongs to both Father and Son.
8. Section 30: Augustine says those separated from the Church do not have the Spirit, citing the apostle Jude on those who separate themselves, and Paul on the natural man not perceiving the things of the Spirit.
9. Augustine says Paul calls the Corinthians still carnal because of strife between followers of Paul and Apollos, and that not yet does not mean hopeless, since the two ministers were united in the Spirit.
10. Section 31: Augustine says little ones in Christ, still carnal and natural, have the Spirit though they cannot understand what they have, as Paul tells the Corinthians that they are God's temple, which could not be said to those cut off from the Church.
11. Chapter XIX: Augustine says false Catholics, heretics and schismatics do not have the Holy Spirit, and those baptized among schismatics are like Ishmael, born of the flesh and not of the promise, until they come to the Catholic Church.
12. Chapter XX: Augustine says forgiveness of sins is given only in the Church that has the Holy Spirit, so that the unclean spirit no longer rules and we become the Spirit's temple; at Pentecost the apostle called for baptism and the gift of the Spirit.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0009
1. Augustine says the Holy Spirit dwells in no one without the Father and the Son, since their dwelling and work are inseparable; the Son is not the Son of both, but the Spirit is the Spirit of both.
2. Chapter XXI: Augustine argues from Luke that the unforgivable blasphemy means persisting in impenitence against the Spirit through whom the Church is united, and that one who shuts himself out of the Church shuts out forgiveness.
3. Section 35: Augustine says the other two evangelists show the same, since the saying arose when the Lord cast out demons in the Spirit, who is not divided against himself, against the unclean spirit divided against itself.
4. Chapter XXII: Augustine says Matthew makes clear that the blasphemy is resisting the unity of the Church with an unrepentant heart, because those separated from Christ's gathering do not have the Holy Spirit.
5. Chapter XXIII: Augustine says all assemblies that call themselves churches of Christ but are divided from one another and from the true Church scatter rather than gather, and have no Spirit through whom sins are forgiven.
6. Augustine says one who opposes the truth by speaking against the Son of man will be forgiven if he repents, but one who resists by impenitence the gift of forgiveness itself, the Spirit, has no forgiveness; the Church holds the bond of peace.
7. Chapter XXIV: Augustine closes his treatment of this most difficult question with humility, saying any failure is his weakness and not the truth's, and giving thanks to God; a heading follows for Sermon LXXII on making the tree good.
8. Chapter I of Sermon LXXII: Augustine says the Lord warns us to be good trees bearing good fruit, that a bad man cannot do good works, and that the man must change first for his works to change.
9. Section 9: Augustine says the Lord found all of us bad trees, since Christ died for the ungodly, yet gave believers the power to become God's children; he put off the axe and gave forgiveness first.
10. Chapter II: Augustine says John speaks of the axe laid to the root, and the parable of the owner and the gardener shows God's patience, the three years being the time before the Law, under the Law and now the time of grace.
11. Augustine says God visited mankind before the Law, as Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob show; after the Law the Lord came, suffered, rose and gave the Spirit; digging round the tree is the humility of penitence.
12. Chapter III: Augustine says each should be a good tree, since good fruit comes only from a good tree, that covetousness is the root of evil and charity the root of good, and asks what is good, since gold is good only for the good.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0010
1. Chapter IV: Augustine says man wants all good things for himself but should turn his eyes inward and examine himself, rooting out what displeases, since one who lacks the better goods is foolish to crave outward ones.
2. Chapter V: Augustine says the true good is what you cannot lose against your will, unlike gold, a house, honours or bodily health, and asks how long people will have heavy hearts and seek vain things.
3. Chapter VI: Augustine says the world is scourged hard because it now knows the Lord's words, since a servant who knew his master's will and did not do it is beaten much; a heading follows for Sermon LXXIII on the sower.
4. Chapter I of Sermon LXXIII: Augustine recalls the parable of the sower and the parable of the enemy who sowed weeds among the good seed, and tells the hearers not to be angry that the wicked are mixed with the good until the harvest.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0011
1. Section 2, on being gentle: Augustine says to be gentle in affliction and secure against stumbling, since trouble itself is not a stumbling-block, as a press makes the oil flow and the apostles in chains sang a hymn to God.
2. Augustine describes Job on the dung heap, stripped of everything yet praising God, and says his stumbling-block came when his wife told him to speak against God and die; his gentleness answered that if we accept good from God we must accept evil.
3. Section 5: Augustine defines the gentle as those who are pleased with God alone in all the good they do and displeased with God in nothing they suffer, and says that God gives the increase.
4. Section 4: Augustine says a stumbling-block comes from a loved one, a wife or friend, who persuades to evil, so that the Gospel says to cut off the offending eye, hand or foot; he applies it to being asked to give false testimony.
5. Augustine goes on that a powerful man may press you to bear false witness and a friend may urge you to do it, but a lie kills the soul; the tempter quotes the law, as Satan did to the Lord.
6. Augustine says a powerful enemy can only kill the flesh, which would die anyway, while a lie kills the soul, so he will not give false testimony; the Lord says not to fear those who kill the body.
7. Section 6: Augustine says every man is a liar, as the Psalm says, but God gave the power to become sons of God, and Christ, God and man, stands between God and man, so through the man we ascend to God.
8. Section 7: Augustine says pagans take offence at the troubles of the world, and tells Christians that they are pilgrims passing through, seeking the city in heaven, and that these pressures are not stumbling-blocks.
9. Augustine says the boat in which Christ sleeps is the heart where faith sleeps; Christians are told the world will fail, so they should wake Christ and not fear that the world is perishing, for their youth will be renewed like the eagle's.
10. Section 9: Augustine answers the pagan complaint that Rome is perishing in Christian times, saying Rome is its people and not its walls, and recalls the Trojan gods carried from Troy.
11. Augustine continues that Rome was founded with false gods brought from Troy, citing a Roman historian that Trojans founded the city; a proverb on rebuking openly follows.
12. Chapter I of a sermon on the speck and the beam: Augustine says the Lord tells us not to overlook each other's sins, and that the speck in the eye is anger and the beam is hatred, which grows from the speck.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0012
1. Chapter II: Augustine distinguishes between the sin of one who is angry and the cruelty of one who hates, saying we are angry with our children but do not hate them; hatred is murder, and the beam blinds while the speck only troubles.
2. Section 3: Augustine says hatred harms the hater more than the one hated, and that one must first stop hating before correcting a brother, since only without the beam can one see his eye; the hater has lost innocence while the other lost only money.
3. Chapter III: Augustine says a brother should be corrected out of love, for his sake and not out of pain at being wronged, as the Lord says to correct privately so as to win him; to sin against a brother is to sin against Christ.
4. Section 5: Augustine says sin against a brother is not slight but is cured quickly by making amends; the Gospel's warning of hell for calling a brother a fool is answered by being reconciled before offering the gift at the altar.
5. Chapter VI: Augustine says people are quick to wrong others and slow to seek peace, and that the one who has done wrong should ask pardon, since whoever humbles himself will be exalted.
6. Section 7: Augustine says the one wronged should correct the offender privately, since to neglect his wound is worse than the wrong; he forgets his own injury but not his brother's wound.
7. Augustine reconciles the commands to rebuke privately and to rebuke openly, saying the two Testaments do not contradict; Solomon says one who reproves openly makes peace, and the Lord says to rebuke in private.
8. Chapter VI, section 9: Augustine says some sins need private rebuke and some public, and that with each applied at the right time the Scriptures agree; the question is when to do each.
9. Chapter VII, section 10: Augustine says a sin done in secret must be corrected in secret, otherwise the one rebuking becomes a betrayer, as Joseph showed mercy to his wife; sins done in public are rebuked before all.
10. Chapter VIII, section 11: Augustine says we must correct in secret so as not to give the sinner away to an enemy who wants to punish, and urges the hearers to rebuke out of charity; he also cites the apostle on marriage being honourable.
11. Chapter IX, section 12: Augustine tells the hearers to amend their lives now, fearing God's judgment and not only an informer, since the last day of each person is near and uncertain; he speaks publicly to all but reaches the conscience of some.
12. Chapter X, section 13: Augustine says sins of the flesh are not to be treated lightly, since the body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, as Paul says to those who were bought at a price.
