## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0019
1. Cyprian argues that Christ is to be seen in the captive brethren, and that those he redeemed should be ransomed from the barbarians with money, as the Lord will count it done to himself at the judgment.
2. Cyprian thanks the Numidian bishops for letting the church at Carthage share in this work, says he has sent one hundred thousand sesterces collected there, and asks that the givers be remembered in their prayers.
3. Epistle LX, Cyprian to Euchratius about an actor: he forbids an actor who goes on teaching the theatrical art to others to take communion, and says the church can support him if he is poor.
4. Editor's footnote to Epistle LX, giving the Oxford edition number and a date about 249 and a remark on church music.
5. Cyprian says a man has not given up the stage if he trains others to take his place, and that if he pleads poverty the church can keep him in food and clothing, so he must not teach deadly things.
6. Epistle LXI, Cyprian and his colleagues to Pomponius about virgins found sleeping in the same bed with men: those still found to be virgins are to be received into communion, the others made to do full penance.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXI, with the Oxford edition number and a date about 249, on keeping virgins to their duty and on the power of excommunication, with scriptural references and a note on pagan manners.
8. Cyprian says overseers and people must see that virgins do not live with men, since their weak sex and age need guarding, and that if they cannot persevere it is better that they marry than fall into the fire.
9. Cyprian says virgins must not scandalize the brethren and that an examination by midwives cannot clear them, since sin may lie elsewhere; those who teach continence must not themselves teach sin.
10. Cyprian approves Pomponius for excommunicating the deacon and others who had slept with virgins, and says virgins found intact by the midwives may be received into communion, with a warning against returning to the same men.
11. Cyprian says disobedience to priests was once punished by the sword and is now punished by casting the proud out of the Church, outside which there is no salvation, and closes with a farewell.
12. Epistle LXII, to Cæcilius on the sacrament of the cup: Cyprian teaches, against those who used water alone in the Lord's supper, that wine mixed with water must be offered, and that water stands for the people.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0020
1. Cyprian, writing to Rogatianus about a deacon who contended with his bishop, says the Lord honoured true priests and that deacons were appointed by the apostles as ministers of the bishops, so they should not dare against them.
2. Epistle LXV, Cyprian to the clergy and people at Furni about Victor, who named the presbyter Faustinus as guardian in his will against a council of bishops: Cyprian forbids offering for him and argues that clerics should not take on secular cares.
3. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXV, with the Oxford edition number and date 249, notes on Roman law, a scriptural reference, and a note that prayer for the dead meant rest until the resurrection.
4. Cyprian says clergy are ordained so as not to be drawn away from the altar and are supported by the gifts of the brethren, like the tithes given to the Levites.
5. Epistle LXVI, Cyprian to Father Stephanus about Marcianus, bishop of Arles, who had joined Novatian: Cyprian asks Stephen to announce his excommunication so that another bishop can be chosen at Arles.
6. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXVI, with the Oxford edition number and date 254, a note that some manuscripts omit the letter but its style is Cyprian's, and a remark on how Cyprian addresses Stephen.
7. Cyprian says Marcianus boasts of following Novatian, who was long ago excluded, and that Novatian was told by a council in Africa that he had excluded himself by setting up a profane altar.
8. Cyprian compares the case to a harbour or an inn that has become dangerous, which travellers avoid, and says the church should receive the lapsed with the medicine of paternal care.
9. Cyprian says that though they are many shepherds they feed one flock, so suppliant brethren must not be trodden down by proud men, and asks Stephen to send letters naming a successor to Marcian.
10. Epistle LXVII, Cyprian to the clergy and people in Spain about Basilides and Martial: bishops who lapsed and were stained by certificates of idolatry have been replaced by Sabinus and Felix, which Cyprian and his colleagues approve.
11. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXVII, with the Oxford edition number and date 257, scriptural references, and a remark that Stephen, far away and ignorant of the facts, should not meddle in the African province.
12. Cyprian and his colleagues reply to the Spanish letters, which said the lapsed bishops should not keep the priesthood, and begin to cite Scripture that priests must be without blemish.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0021
1. Cyprian says divine precepts cannot be set aside for any person, warns with the Lord's words through Isaiah against those who honour him with their lips, and says only men whom God will hear should be chosen priests.
2. Cyprian tells the people not to think themselves free of sin while they share communion with a sinful priest and consent to an unlawful bishop, for the prophet says all who eat of such sacrifices are polluted; they have power to choose worthy priests and refuse unworthy ones.
3. Cyprian says the priest must be chosen in the presence of the people under all eyes, as the Lord commanded in Numbers, and that ordinations that are not lawful are displeasing to God.
4. Cyprian says apostolic practice, kept almost throughout the provinces, is that the neighbouring bishops gather with the people when a bishop is ordained, and the people, who know his life, take part in choosing him; the Spanish people did this for Sabinus.
5. Cyprian says Basilides and Martial are stained by certificates of idolatry, Basilides had also blasphemed in sickness and laid down his episcopate, and lapsed bishops may be admitted to repentance but not to the priesthood.
6. Cyprian tells the Spanish people not to be troubled by wavering faith in these last times, which was foretold, and says brave bishops still keep to evangelic discipline.
7. Cyprian recalls Mattathias, Elias, Daniel and the three youths who stood firm when others gave way, and says God's servants must forsake human error and keep the Lord's precepts.
8. Cyprian says some colleagues who rashly hold communion with Basilides and Martial need not trouble their faith, since Scripture says those who consent to wrongdoers share in their sin; he closes with a farewell.
9. Epistle LXVIII, Cyprian, also called Thascius, to Florentius Pupianus on calumniators: he clears himself of the crimes Florentius accused him of and chides him for trusting slanderers.
10. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXVIII, with the Oxford edition number and date 254, a note that Cyprian had been bishop six years, and a note on his heathen name Thascius.
11. Cyprian argues from the Lord's saying that a man's testimony of himself is not enough, so servants should welcome the judgment of others, and cites scriptural warnings against despising priests.
12. Cyprian says the Lord and the apostle did not revile the high priest, teaching that priestly dignity should be honoured, and that when the one bishop who rules the Church is despised by the proud, schisms and heresies arise.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0022
1. Cyprian answers the demand of Pupianus to be cleared by his judgment, calling it arrogant to say that for six years the brotherhood has had no bishop unless Pupianus gives judgment.
2. Cyprian says Pupianus fell into his scruple through irreligious credulity in believing wicked reports against his brother and priest, and cites Scripture against listening to a wicked tongue.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0023
1. Cyprian argues that after the resurrection the apostles were sent to baptize the nations in the name of the Father, Son and Holy Ghost, so baptism outside the Church in the name of Jesus Christ alone cannot remit sins.
2. Cyprian says that if even a son defends his father's honour, Christians should honour the Father, and that admitting heretics without baptism makes the admitters partakers in their sins.
3. Cyprian says it is perverse to tell repentant heretics who come to the Church that they already had remission of sins when they themselves confess that they have sinned.
4. Cyprian argues that if baptism by blood cannot save a heretic outside the Church, baptism in a robbers' cave cannot either, so baptism cannot be common to the Church and heretics, who must be born of water and the Spirit.
5. Section 22: Cyprian answers the objection about the catechumen put to death for confessing Christ before being baptized, saying such catechumens hold the sound faith and are not to be confused with heretics.
6. Section 23: Cyprian asks what becomes of those formerly received from heresy without baptism, and answers that the Lord can show mercy to those who fell asleep in the Church, but that error need not continue once the truth is plain.
7. Section 25: Cyprian says the baptism of John gives no ground for accepting heretics' baptism, since John was the Lord's forerunner and not his adversary; he also cites Esau, who lost his birthright.
8. Section 26: Cyprian closes his letter to Jubaianus, saying he prescribes to no one, leaves every bishop free to judge, keeps concord with his colleagues, and sends his treatise on patience.
9. Epistle LXXIII, Cyprian to Pompeius against the letter of Stephen on the baptism of heretics, with an argument noting that Augustine records Stephen disagreeing with Cyprian.
10. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXXIII, with the Oxford edition number, a quotation from Augustine that Cyprian kept unity with those who thought differently, and a note on which heretics Stephen meant.
11. Cyprian says Stephen forbade baptizing anyone coming from heresy, and so held the baptism of all heretics to be lawful, yet called Cyprian the innovator; the Lord commanded the nations to be baptized.
12. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXXIII, with scriptural references and remarks that Cyprian does not believe in the mere working of the water and that the unity he means is not agreement with Stephen.
