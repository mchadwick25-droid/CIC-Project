## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0029
1. Cyprian pictures Mother Church receiving the confessors, men, women, virgins and boys, as they return from the battle bearing trophies over the defeated enemy.
2. Section 3: Cyprian says no one should belittle the glory of those who stood firm, for confessing the Lord openly is the first title to victory, and withdrawing cautiously to be reserved for the Lord is the second.
3. Section 4: Cyprian says one cause of grief remains, the lapsed, a part of our own bowels torn away; he needs tears rather than words, and shares the sorrow of his fallen brethren.
4. Section 5: Cyprian says finding the cause of the disaster gives the remedy: a long peace had corrupted discipline, so the Lord allowed this trial, and among the priests there was no devotion, among the ministers no faith, and in the works no mercy.
5. Cyprian describes the failings before the persecution: beards defaced, faces painted, frauds on the simple, marriage with unbelievers, false oaths and quarrels, and bishops who ought to have taught failing in their duty.
6. Editor's footnotes to the treatise On the Lapsed, with scriptural references, a note that infant baptism and infant communion now seem general, and a remark on Savonarola.
7. Section 7: Cyprian says these things were foretold, that the lapsed betrayed their faith at the first words of the threatening foe, and that Christ had appointed punishment for those who deny him and rewards for those who confess him.
8. Section 8: Cyprian condemns those who denied before they were even seized, running to the market-place of their own accord and sacrificing as if they had long wished to.
9. Section 9: Cyprian says many urged others to ruin, pledging each other in the deadly cup, and that infants carried in their parents' arms lost what they had gained at birth and will bear witness against them at the judgment.
10. Section 10: Cyprian says no reason excuses the lapsed, since a man must lose his country and estate sometime anyway, and the Spirit calls the faithful to depart; one who leaves for a while does not deny his faith, while the fallen stayed and denied it.
11. Section 11: Cyprian says love of property deceived many, who were bound by their wealth like a chain, and that the Lord taught freedom from it, as the apostles did, and others who forsook their goods and clung to Christ.
12. Section 12: Cyprian asks how those held by wealth can follow Christ, calling them slaves of their money, and quotes the apostle on the snare of riches and the Lord's blessing on those persecuted for his sake.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0031
1. Cyprian says Christ made a short summary of his teaching so that the learned and unlearned could easily remember it, giving examples such as the saying that eternal life is to know the one true God and Jesus Christ.
2. Section 29: Cyprian says the Lord taught prayer by example, praying often and all night alone, so sinners should pray all the more, and that he also prayed for unity.
3. Section 31: Cyprian tells those at prayer to be watchful and to put away worldly thoughts, as the priest's call to lift up our hearts reminds the people, so the heart is closed to the adversary.
4. Section 32: Cyprian says prayer must bear fruit and be joined with fasting and almsgiving, since God hears the merciful, as the angel told Cornelius that his prayers and alms had gone up before God.
5. Section 33: Cyprian says prayers rise quickly when backed by good works, citing Tobias and the angel Raphael, and the prophet on loosing the bonds of wickedness and breaking bread for the hungry.
6. Cyprian continues with the promise that those who clothe the naked and give alms among God's household will be heard, and says whoever pities the poor lends to God.
7. Section 34: Cyprian says the three youths with Daniel prayed at the third, sixth and ninth hours as a sign of the Trinity, and that at the ninth hour the Lord on the cross washed away our sins.
8. Section 35: Cyprian says Christians should also pray in the morning for the Lord's resurrection and in the evening for his coming, citing the Psalms.
9. Cyprian says Christ is the true sun and day, so no hour is excepted from prayer, and whoever has light in his heart is never without the day.
10. Section 36: Cyprian says Christians who are always in the light should not cease praying even at night, as the widow Anna served with fastings and prayers, and should keep giving thanks.
11. Treatise V, An Address to Demetrianus: Cyprian answers the proconsul of Africa, who blamed Christians for wars, famine and plague because they did not worship the gods, and begins by saying he has long ignored such railing.
12. Editor's notes to the address to Demetrianus, written in 252, summarizing how Cyprian shows the powerlessness of the gods, who confess what they are when Christians cast them out of possessed bodies, with scriptural references.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0032
1. Section 2: Cyprian says he has often held his tongue, but when Demetrianus says Christians cause wars, plague, famine and drought, he must answer, since truth can move a man to good.
2. Section 3: Cyprian answers that the world has grown old and weaker and the seasons less fruitful, which would be so even without Scripture.
3. Cyprian continues that marble, gold and silver are running out and farmers, sailors, soldiers and justice are failing, as everything decays when it grows old.
4. Section 4: Cyprian compares the world to old men who might blame Christians for their weakness, and says no one should wonder that everything fails when the world itself is ending.
5. Section 5: Cyprian says wars, famine, plague and disease were foretold for the last times as the day of judgment draws near, and happen because God is not worshipped, not because the gods are neglected.
6. Section 6: Cyprian cites God's commands to worship only the Lord and the prophet's warning of anger, with drought for those who do not turn to him.
7. Section 7: Cyprian says the Lord is angry and threatens because men do not turn to him, and that scarce rain, hail, drought and disease are consequences of sin which God sends to restrain men.
8. Section 8: Cyprian tells Demetrianus that while he demands service from his slave and from nature, he does not serve God, who ordained all things for him.
9. Editor's footnotes to the address, noting variant readings, a remark that a phrase on equal law might have furnished ground for the American Declaration of Independence, and a comparison with Minucius Felix.
10. Section 9: Cyprian says that if God's scourges do not convert people, the eternal dungeon and fire remain, where the groans of the suppliants will not be heard.
11. Section 10: Cyprian tells Demetrianus to judge himself, listing pride, greed, anger and lust, and describes people who tend the dying only to inherit their estates.
12. Section 11: Cyprian describes the havoc of the plague, with robbery and seizure everywhere and no shame, and urges each to consider the wounds of his conscience and stop complaining about God and the Christians.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0033
1. Section 12: Cyprian says Demetrianus persecutes innocent Christians though his own life is stained with crimes and false worship, and devises new tortures.
2. Section 13: Cyprian asks Demetrianus whether being a Christian is a crime; if it is, he should put the confessor to death, and if it is not, he should not persecute an innocent man.
3. Section 14: Cyprian says Demetrianus should contend with the mind and faith and not the body, and that if his gods have any power they should defend themselves instead of needing his protection.
4. Section 15: Cyprian says Demetrianus should see the demons adjured by Christians, howling and confessing the judgment to come as they are driven from possessed bodies, and so believe even the gods he worships.
5. Section 16: Cyprian calls it blind madness to refuse to come from darkness to light, warns that those who sacrifice to other gods will be rooted out, and urges Demetrianus to stop persecuting God's servants.
6. Section 17: Cyprian says Christians do not resist when arrested, being patient in the certainty that God will avenge them, and that the troubles come for their sakes.
7. Section 18: Cyprian says Christians are avenged by the present troubles though they share them, because worldly adversity hurts only those whose joy is in this world.
8. Section 19: Cyprian contrasts the pagans' clamorous impatience with the Christians' quiet and grateful patience, which waits for the divine promise.
9. Section 20: Cyprian says hope and faith stay firm among Christians even amid the ruins of the world, and that they pray night and day for the peace and salvation of their persecutors.
10. Section 21: Cyprian says that though Christians and pagans share worldly troubles, the wrath of God was foretold to fall on the unjust and vengeance will defend God's servants.
11. Cyprian says only those new-born and signed with the sign of Christ will escape, citing the mark placed on the faithful when God sends his angels to destroy the world.
12. Section 23: Cyprian urges Demetrianus to look to eternal salvation while there is time, since the end of the world is at hand, and warns of the fire of Gehenna for those who do not believe.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0034
1. Section 17 of On the Mortality: Cyprian answers those who grieve that the plague deprives them of martyrdom, saying martyrdom is God's gift, and that God sees the will of the faithful.
2. Section 18: Cyprian says it is absurd to pray that God's will be done and then resist when God calls us from this world, being dragged to the Lord in sadness.
3. Section 19: Cyprian tells of a fellow-priest who, near death, prayed for a respite and was rebuked in a vision of a youth, which Cyprian reads as a lesson for all.
4. Section 20: Cyprian says it has often been revealed to him that brethren called away are not to be mourned, since they are sent before us, and that we should not wear black while they wear white.
5. Section 21: Cyprian cites the apostle's rebuke of grieving for those who sleep, and says Christians, who hope for the resurrection, should meet death with glad security.
6. Section 22: Cyprian says death is a passage to immortality and eternal life, and that no one who longs to be changed into the likeness of Christ should lament departure.
7. Section 23: Cyprian cites Enoch, who pleased God and was translated, and Solomon on the righteous being taken early lest the world pollute them.
8. Section 24: Cyprian asks why Christians should love a world that hates them, quoting John against loving the world, and says we should come without resistance when the Lord calls.
9. Section 25: Cyprian says that now the world is collapsing, it is an advantage to depart quickly, like leaving a house whose walls are about to fall.
10. Section 26: Cyprian says we are guests and strangers here and should greet the day that takes us home to paradise, as a traveller longs for his own country.
11. Treatise VIII, On Works and Alms: Cyprian urges that faith be shown by works and that offerings to the Church and giving to the poor are the best use of a Christian's wealth, beginning with God's mercy in sending the Son and with almsgiving as a way to wash away later sin.
12. Editor's notes to On Works and Alms, giving the Oxford edition number and date 254, textual variants, a remark on Cyprian's skill as an orator, and scriptural references on mercy, almsgiving and riches.
