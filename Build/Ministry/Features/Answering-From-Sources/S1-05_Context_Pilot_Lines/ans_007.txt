## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0024
1. Cyprian argues that if the Gospel or Acts said that those from heresy need only have hands laid on them, that tradition should be kept, but heretics are called adversaries and antichrists and stand condemned by themselves.
2. Cyprian says heresies grew more numerous and worse over time, that there is only one baptism, within the Church, and that it is obstinate to put human tradition above divine ordinance.
3. Cyprian answers Stephen's remark that heretics do not rebaptize one another, saying the Church should not borrow discipline from darkness, and that baptism, which cannot be separated from the Church and the Holy Spirit, cannot be among heretics.
4. Cyprian asks why, if baptism in the name of Jesus Christ is effective anywhere, hands are not also laid on in that name for the Holy Spirit, and says there is no baptism where the Spirit is not.
5. Cyprian argues that only the Church, joined to Christ, bears sons spiritually; regeneration is in the washing, so heresy, which is not the spouse of Christ, cannot bear sons to God.
6. Cyprian says one is born in baptism and not by the laying on of hands, and receives the Spirit afterward, as Adam was formed before being breathed into; remission of sins cannot be granted where blasphemy is uttered against the Father.
7. Cyprian asks whether a priest can give a good account at the judgment who approves the baptism of blasphemers, and says Stephen, a friend of heretics, wrongly thinks priests who defend unity should be excommunicated.
8. Cyprian says that if this is the way to give glory to God, the Church should lay down its arms and yield to heretics, light to darkness and truth to falsehood.
9. Cyprian urges that, if the fear of God prevails, the camp entrusted to us be kept with valour, and that a custom which crept in must not prevent the truth from prevailing.
10. Cyprian says a stubborn love of one's own position keeps men from agreeing with the truth, cites the apostle that a bishop must be teachable and not contentious, and says practice must return to the Lord and to apostolic tradition.
11. Cyprian says there is one God, one Christ, one Church and one baptism, ordained only in the Church, so whoever leaves this unity stands with heretics; he compares the Church to an enclosed garden and to the one ark.
12. Cyprian concludes that all converts from any heresy must be baptized with the Church's one lawful baptism, except those first baptized in the Church who strayed, who return by the laying on of hands alone.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0025
1. Epistle LXXIV, Firmilian, bishop of Cæsarea in Cappadocia, to Cyprian against the letter of Stephen, in the year 256: he thanks Cyprian for his letter on the baptism of heretics and praises unity.
2. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LXXIV, with the Oxford edition number, a remark that it shows Ante-Nicene unity, a note that Stephen refused communion with those who disagreed, and scriptural references.
3. Firmilian says unity brings joy to the angels, who are saddened by divided wills, and relates Stephen's conduct to the discord.
4. Firmilian says Stephen's audacity and pride should be passed over for now, rejoices that Cyprian has settled the question by the rule of truth, and recalls the Lord's prayer that they may be one.
5. Firmilian says they received Cyprian's writings as if they were their own and committed them to memory, and that adding to them does not mean too little was said.
6. Firmilian says Cyprian has abundantly answered Stephen's claim that the apostles forbade baptizing converts from heresy, since heresies such as Marcion's arose later, and that confirming heretics' baptism makes one a partaker with them.
7. Firmilian says the Romans do not keep everything handed down from the beginning, for they differ from Jerusalem over Easter and other matters, and so cannot pretend apostolic authority for upholding the baptism of heretics.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0026
1. Editor's elucidations at the end of Cyprian's letters, noting that the church of Cornelius had forty-six presbyters and more than fifteen hundred widows and sufferers to support, and turning to Stephen.
2. Elucidation XVII on the quotation attributed to Tertullian and on the old Latin texts before Jerome, with remarks on whether Cyprian cited a verse of the first epistle of John.
3. Elucidation XVIII on Cyprian's appeal to return to the Lord, the source, and apostolic tradition, and the start of Elucidation XIX on Firmilian's letter to Cyprian.
4. Elucidation on Firmilian's letter: he faults Stephen for claiming the privileges of a successor of Peter and for betraying unity, and the editor contrasts Cyprian's forbearance with Stephen's coercion.
5. Elucidations XX and XXI: Cyprian's moderation about baptism of the sick by sprinkling, and the persecution of senators and Roman knights as Christianity spread among the upper classes.
6. Elucidation XXII on the Lord's saying that the Spirit speaks in those brought to trial, and the close of the life of Cyprian.
7. Heading of the Treatises of Cyprian in this volume.
8. Treatise I, On the Unity of the Church: written at the time of Novatian's schism to keep the Carthaginians from him, it argues that heresies arise because Christ the head is not looked to, and that the Church and the episcopate are one.
9. Editor's notes to the treatise on unity, written in 251, calling it the key to all Cyprian's writings and noting that it has been tampered with to favour Rome.
10. Section 2 of the treatise on unity: Cyprian urges keeping the commands of Christ, which drive out death, and warns that whoever strays from the truth makes no progress towards salvation.
11. Editor's footnotes to the treatise on unity, noting that Cyprian knows of no centre of unity or one see as the test of communion, and that a Roman view has been written into the text.
12. Section 3: Cyprian warns of the enemy's subtler craft after Christ's coming, deceiving under the name of Christ, which prevails as long as people do not return to the source of truth.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0027
1. Section 4 of the treatise on unity: Cyprian cites the Lord's words to Peter about the rock and the keys, and the apostle's statement of one body, one spirit, one faith and one baptism.
2. Section 5: Cyprian says the bishops must hold the episcopate to be one and undivided, and that the Church is one, like many rays of one light and many branches of one tree.
3. Section 6: Cyprian says the Church, the spouse of Christ, cannot be adulterous, and that whoever is separated from her cannot have God for his Father who does not have the Church for his mother.
4. Section 7: Cyprian says the unity of the Church is shown by the Lord's coat, which was not torn but woven in one piece and kept whole.
5. Editor's footnotes to the treatise on unity, calling one reading obscure and giving many scriptural references on peace, unanimity and charity.
6. Section 8: Cyprian says no one should think God's unity can be divided, citing one flock and one shepherd and the apostle's plea that all speak the same thing.
7. Section 9: Cyprian says the Holy Spirit came as a dove, a simple creature that lives in concord, and that this simplicity should be found in the Church; those who went out were never of us.
8. Section 10: Cyprian says heresies arise and continue through discord, that the Lord permits them so the approved may be shown, and that heretics' speech spreads like a cancer.
9. Section 11: Cyprian quotes the Lord against false prophets who promise peace, and says those who break the Lord's peace by discord lose the grace of faith.
10. Section 12: Cyprian warns against misusing the Lord's saying about two or three gathered in his name, which refers to those who agree in the Church and not to those who set up separate meetings.
11. Cyprian says the promise to two or three applies to those who are simple, peaceable and keep God's commandments, and that prayer is heard more through the agreement of a few than the discord of many.
12. Section 13: Cyprian says the law of prayer requires forgiveness, that one who comes to the altar in strife must first agree with his brother, as Cain's offering was not accepted, and that without charity even giving to the poor and burning one's body profit nothing.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0028
1. Section 16 of On the Dress of Virgins: Cyprian argues that dyeing the hair falsifies what God made, and cites the Lord's saying that one cannot make a hair white or black.
2. Section 17: Cyprian warns that on the day of resurrection the Maker may not recognise a woman who has painted her skin and dyed her hair, and says such persons should be kept apart from the flock of virgins.
3. Section 18: Cyprian warns virgins against attending marriage feasts, where lewd talk and drunken banquets can lessen the virtue of one who remains a virgin in body.
4. Section 19: Cyprian warns against mixed bathing, saying that exposing oneself in the baths leads others to lust and cannot be excused by the plea that one only wishes to wash.
5. Section 20: Cyprian says the Church mourns over her virgins who fall through adornment and liberty and become adulterous to Christ, and tells them to avoid gold and dye and to bathe among women.
6. Cyprian tells virgins to avoid shameless marriage feasts, to overcome dress and gold as they overcome the flesh, and to walk the strait and narrow way that the martyrs and the just take.
7. Section 22: Cyprian urges virgins to hold fast to continence, which frees them from the sentence of sorrow in childbearing laid on women, and to keep their eyes on heaven.
8. Section 23: Cyprian says the first command was to multiply but now that the world is full continence is urged though not commanded, and that those who choose it are made eunuchs for the kingdom.
9. Section 24: Cyprian closes his address to virgins, telling the older to teach the younger and all to encourage one another, and asking them to remember him when virginity is rewarded.
10. Treatise III, On the Lapsed: Cyprian rejoices at the unexpected peace of the Church and the constancy of the confessors, laments the lapsed, blames neglect of discipline for the persecution, and warns against the Novatians.
11. Editor's notes to the treatise On the Lapsed, written in 251: Cyprian keeps his promise to write once peace came, rebuking the lapsed who forced communion from presbyters without the bishop's judgment.
12. Section 2: Cyprian greets the confessors with joy, praising their resistance and their example, and saying their heads refused the veil and the crown of the idols.
