## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0015
1. Cyprian tells Cornelius that the unity delivered through the apostles must be kept and the wandering sheep drawn off by faction gathered back into the Church, and says he sends copies of his letters by the sub-deacon Mettius and the acolyte Nicephorus.
2. Epistle XLII, Cyprian to Cornelius: he has written a short letter to the confessors at Rome who were led away by Novatian and Novatus, urging them to return to their Mother, the Church.
3. Editor's footnote to Epistle XLII, giving the Oxford edition number and date 251 and a note on the frequent confusion of the names of Novatian and Novatus.
4. Epistle XLIII, Cyprian to the Roman confessors Maximus and Nicostratus and the others: he urges them to return from the faction of Novatian and Novatus to the Church, their Mother.
5. Editor's footnote to Epistle XLIII, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, remarking that nobody in Cyprian's circle imagined an infallible bishop of Rome as the centre of unity.
6. Epistle XLIV, Cyprian to Cornelius about Polycarp of Adrumetum: he explains why letters from that colony went to the Roman clergy rather than to Cornelius, and says the senders were urged to hold to the root of the Catholic Church.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XLIV, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, a note on the words root and matrix, and a note on letters addressed only to presbyters and deacons.
8. Cyprian explains that his province is wide and includes Numidia and Mauritania, so all the bishops sent letters approving the ordination of Cornelius, lest a schism in the city confuse distant churches.
9. Epistle XLV, Cornelius to Cyprian: he reports with joy the return to the Church of the confessors who had been misled by Novatian and who now acknowledge their error.
10. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XLV, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, a note on the terms for episcopal ordination, and the principle that there is one episcopate.
11. Cornelius tells Cyprian that the returning confessors asked that their error be forgotten, that he called the presbytery together with five bishops present, and that all wept for joy.
12. Cornelius gives the confessors' own words: they acknowledge Cornelius as bishop of the Catholic Church, admit they were deceived, and say they always kept faith in one God, one Christ and one Holy Spirit.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0016
1. Epistle XLVI, Cyprian answers Cornelius, congratulating him on the return of the confessors from schism and thanking God that the unity of the Church is protected.
2. Editor's footnote to Epistle XLVI, giving the Oxford edition number and date 251, variant readings, and a reference to Luke 15.
3. Cyprian says the people and the brotherhood rightly received the returning confessors with joy, and that good soldiers of Christ could not long be kept outside the Church by the deceit of others.
4. Epistle XLVII, Cornelius to Cyprian about the faction of Novatian: after Cyprian's rejection of his messengers Novatian has risen again, and Cornelius names others who have gone to Carthage.
5. Editor's footnote to Epistle XLVII, with the Oxford edition number and date 251 and two cross-references.
6. Epistle XLVIII, Cyprian answers Cornelius about the crimes of Novatus: he thanks Cornelius for the warning, says Novatian cannot be a confessor of Christ having denied the Church, and begins to describe Novatus and his earlier schism in Africa.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XLVIII, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, a scriptural reference to Ephesians, and a remark that Rome is called a root and matrix of the Church, not the one apostolic see.
8. Cyprian cites the apostle's words on Christ and the Church as one flesh to argue that whoever is not with the spouse of Christ cannot be with Christ or rule the Church.
9. Cyprian charges Novatus with first sowing discord and schism at Carthage, appointing Felicissimus a deacon without leave, and then going to Rome to do the same there.
10. Cyprian continues his charges against Novatus, saying he feared being turned out of the presbytery and welcomed the persecution as a way to escape the investigation of his case.
11. Epistle XLIX, Maximus and the other confessors write to Cyprian that they have returned to the Church and made peace with Cornelius, their bishop, and the whole clergy.
12. Editor's footnote to Epistle XLIX, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, noting that the Roman clergy speak of our bishop with no sense of any special position over other churches.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0017
1. Epistle L, Cyprian to the confessors Maximus, Urbanus, Sidonius and Macharius, congratulating them on their return from schism and replying to their letter.
2. Editor's footnotes to Epistle L, with the Oxford edition number and date 252, a scriptural reference, and a remark that Cyprian teaches the Roman clergy the principle of Catholic unity.
3. Cyprian tells the returned confessors how much their peaceful return pleased him, and how he grieved while they were in schism and he could not hold communion with them.
4. Cyprian says no servant may claim what the Father gave the Son alone, to separate all the tares from the wheat, and calls such presumption proud and sacrilegious; those who do so perish from the Church.
5. Epistle LI, Cyprian to Antonianus about Cornelius and Novatian: Antonianus had begun to waver after letters from Novatian, and Cyprian confirms him in holding communion with his bishop and the Catholic Church.
6. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LI, with the Oxford edition number and date 252, a summary that Cornelius was lawfully elected and Novatian was not, and a remark on Cyprian's idea that union with one's lawful bishop is union with the whole Church.
7. Cyprian answers Antonianus's second letter, which asked him to explain Novatian's heresy and why Cornelius communicated with Trophimus and those who had sacrificed, and says peace for the lapsed was not given lightly.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0018
1. Cyprian's letter to Cornelius in exile about his confession says the enemy of the Church passes by those he has already drawn away and harasses those in whom he sees Christ, and that confession outside the Church gives no one credit.
2. Epistle LVII, Cyprian and his colleagues to Lucius, bishop of Rome, congratulating him on his return from banishment and foretelling Cyprian's own coming martyrdom among his own people.
3. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LVII, with the Oxford edition number and date 252, noting that the episcopate of Lucius lasted under six months, with a reference to Eusebius.
4. Cyprian compares Lucius to the youths who were ready to be burned rather than worship the king's image, and says the adversary persecutes Christ's camp and passes over heretics.
5. Cyprian wishes he could be present at the return of Lucius, picturing the brethren running together to embrace him, and says an example of courage and faith ought to be offered up when the brethren are present.
6. Epistle LVIII, Cyprian to Fidus on the baptism of infants: against those who said an infant may not be baptized before the eighth day, he and his fellow-bishops decree that it may be baptized as soon as it is born; he also lets Victor keep the communion granted him.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LVIII, with the Oxford edition number, a note that the letter followed the synods on the lapsed, and lines of verse by George Canning.
8. Cyprian reports that the council unanimously rejected the idea that infants must wait until the eighth day to be baptized, as the old circumcision required, and held that God's mercy and grace are refused to no one born of man.
9. Cyprian argues that all are equal before God whatever their age, since the Holy Spirit is given alike to all, and that the eighth-day rule belonged to a figure that ended when the truth came.
10. Cyprian concludes that no one should be kept from grace by the old law of circumcision, noting that Peter was told to call no man common or unclean, and that if sins were a bar, grown sinners would be barred before infants.
11. Epistle LIX, Cyprian to the Numidian bishops about the redemption of their brethren captured by barbarians: he deplores the captivity and sends a hundred thousand sesterces collected from brethren, sisters and colleagues.
12. Editor's footnotes to Epistle LIX, with the Oxford edition number and date 253, a suggestion that the captivity was the work of the barbarians Decius fought, a note on the size of the gift, and a note on the diptychs, the lists of benefactors read at the eucharist.
