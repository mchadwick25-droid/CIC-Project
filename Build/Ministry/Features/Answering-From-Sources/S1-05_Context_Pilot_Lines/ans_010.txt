## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0001
1. Chapter XV of a sermon by Augustine, headed on the four dimensions of the cross: it likens good works, perseverance in them, hope of heavenly things and God's hidden grace to the breadth, length, height and depth of the Lord's cross.
2. Opening of a sermon by Augustine on two commands of the Lord that seem to conflict: to let one's light shine before men so that they see good works, and to take care not to do one's justice before men.
3. Augustine says the apostle fulfils both commands, citing Paul on commending himself to every conscience, providing what is good before God and men, and pleasing all, yet not serving men.
4. Section 3 reconciles the two commands by the intention of the heart: whoever wants his good works seen for his own glory breaks the command, whoever wants the Father glorified keeps it.
5. Section 4: Augustine says those who truly keep both commands count no righteousness as their own but God's, citing Paul on not having his own righteousness but that which comes by faith in Christ; the heading of the next sermon follows.
6. Chapter I of a sermon on the Gospel's warning against calling a brother a fool: Augustine says the reading rightly terrifies those who have faith, and that fear is useful now so as to have security without end.
7. Chapter II: Augustine asks what to do, since no man can tame the tongue, tells the hearers to take counsel together as one in Christ, and prays that God be their refuge.
8. Chapter III: Augustine says God must be the one who gives the tongue its mastery, that we take refuge in God because we are evil to ourselves, and that a man who tames beasts by reason should let God tame him as his image.
9. Chapter IV: Augustine says God is the tamer and we must bear his scourge, as a man uses the whip on his animals, and that in him are our hope, our shepherd, our drink, our honour and our riches.
10. Chapter V: Augustine asks to what hope man is tamed, quotes the letter to the Hebrews that God disciplines every son, and says it is better that God scourge us and receive us than spare us and abandon us.
11. Chapter VI: Augustine says God is our refuge in every generation, at birth, at rebirth and in guidance; a heading follows for Sermon LVI on the Lord's Prayer, addressed to the Competentes.
12. Chapter I of the sermon on the Lord's Prayer: Augustine says the apostle foretold that all nations would call on the Lord's name, and that the Symbol and the Prayer are given in order, belief before prayer.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0002
1. Chapter II: Augustine says one who prays must avoid two errors, asking for what he should not and asking from whom he should not, such as the devil, idols and demons, and he warns that wishing evil on enemies turns prayer into sin.
2. Chapter III: Augustine says the cursing Psalms are prophecy and not a wish, and urges the hearers not to pray evil against anyone, since the person may become better, as Paul once persecuted the Church and then preached the faith.
3. Section 4: Augustine says the Lord first cut off wordy prayer, since the Father knows what we need, and that the words he taught in the Lord's Prayer are the pattern of our desires.
4. Chapter IV, first petition: Augustine tells the hearers to remember they have a Father in heaven by rebirth in the font, and says that praying for God's name to be hallowed asks that it be held holy, to our own benefit.
5. Section 6, second petition: Augustine explains that praying for the kingdom to come asks that we be found fit for the kingdom promised at the end of the age, since God always reigns.
6. Chapter V, third petition: Augustine says that praying for God's will to be done asks that it be done in us and that we not resist it, since God is almighty and does not need our prayer.
7. Section 8: Augustine reads heaven and earth as mind and flesh, so that God's will is done when the flesh agrees with the mind, and prays that enemies be made friends.
8. Chapter VI, fourth petition: Augustine says that from the bread petition on we pray for ourselves, that all of us are God's beggars, and that daily bread comes from God's mercy and not from wealth.
9. Section 10: Augustine says God gives daily bread to the good and the wicked alike, but believers also ask for the bread of the altar, the Eucharist, so as not to be separated from it.
10. Chapter VII, fifth petition: Augustine says all, even bishops, are debtors who need forgiveness of sins, quotes that if we say we have no sin we deceive ourselves, and urges alms, which pray for us.
11. Chapter VIII: Augustine says that after the washing of regeneration the daily cleansing of prayer, alms and forgiveness is still needed, and small sins must not be despised since they add up.
12. Chapter IX: Augustine says we often pray with wandering thoughts, forgetting before whom we stand, and that small sins pile up like drops filling rivers or grains of sand weighing like lead.
