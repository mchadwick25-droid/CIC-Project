## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0004
1. Section 19: Pontius ends his account, saying Cyprian was the first bishop in Africa to shed his blood in martyrdom after the apostles, and that he regrets staying behind himself.
2. Opening of Epistle I, Cyprian's letter to Donatus, with its summary: he keeps his promise to speak of divine things, praises the grace given in baptism, and urges Donatus to scorn the world and to read and pray.
3. Editor's footnotes to the letter to Donatus, with textual notes, a note on the vintage festival and a remark on the modern theatre and opera.
4. Section 2: Cyprian tells Donatus that plain speech suits talk of God better than eloquence does, and that he will say what he can.
5. Section 3: Cyprian recalls that before baptism he thought it impossible to be born again and put off his old self, held down by the vices of the world.
6. Section 4: Cyprian says that after the water of new birth washed away his old sins, light entered his heart and he began to live by the Spirit of holiness.
7. Cyprian tells Donatus that all power comes from God and not from human virtue, that not sinning is the beginning of faith, and that assurance must not breed carelessness.
8. Section 5: Cyprian tells Donatus that if he keeps the way of righteousness the gifts of the Spirit will grow without limit, including healing the sick and making peace between enemies.
9. Cyprian goes on to the Spirit's power to drive unclean spirits out of the people they torment, a work that is unseen but plain in its result.
10. Section 6: Cyprian asks Donatus to picture himself on a high mountain looking down on the turmoil of the world, and to pity its crimes, which pass unpunished when done on a grand scale.
11. Section 7: Cyprian describes the gladiatorial games in the cities, where men are fed up and killed for the crowd's pleasure, and says crime is now taught as well as done.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0005
1. Epistle VIII, Cyprian to the martyrs and confessors: he praises the African martyrs for their constancy and urges them to hold firm, citing the example of their colleague Mappalicus.
2. Editor's footnotes to Epistle VIII, giving the Oxford edition number and the date 250, scriptural references, and a note on pictures of martyrs' sufferings in a church at Rome.
3. Cyprian praises the martyrs who endured torture to the end, saying the sufferings gave way to them and the crown ended what the torments could not.
4. Cyprian says Christ was present at their contest and still conquers death in his servants, recalling the promise that the Spirit speaks in those handed over for trial.
5. Cyprian recalls the apostle's words about the race and the incorruptible crown, and says Christ himself wrestles in his servants and is crowned with them.
6. Cyprian tells those whose contest may be spared by peace that a sound will and a good conscience still count, since the Lord sees hidden hearts, so they need not feel less than earlier martyrs.
7. Epistle IX, Cyprian to the clergy about certain presbyters who rashly gave peace to the lapsed before the persecution ended and without the bishops' knowledge; he begins by saying he has been patient.
8. Editor's footnotes to Epistle IX, with scriptural references, a note on the limits of presbyters' powers, and a comment on the word for open confession of sin.
9. Cyprian warns that whoever withholds the Lord's words from the fallen deceives them, since truly repenting people should satisfy God by prayers and works, and he faults those who set the bishop aside.
10. Cyprian says the presbyters gave the eucharist to the lapsed before the persecution was over and before his return, despising the bishop's honour and the martyrs' request that the Lord's law be kept.
11. Epistle X, Cyprian to the martyrs and confessors who sought peace for the lapsed: he urges them to keep the Lord's law and honour the bishop, since certain presbyters are relaxing it.
12. Editor's notes to Epistle X, quoting Cyprian's remark that certificates were being granted by the thousand without examination, with remarks on how he uses the word for the bishop.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0006
1. Cyprian says certain presbyters, against the Gospel law and the martyrs' own petition, offer the eucharist to the lapsed before penitence is complete, confession made, or hands laid on by the bishop.
2. Cyprian asks the martyrs to weigh each petitioner's conduct and the kind of sin, so that the Church is not shamed before the Gentiles by hasty promises.
3. Epistle XI, Cyprian to the faithful people: he grieves with them over the fall of their brethren and warns against communicating with the lapsed before the proper time.
4. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XI, with the Oxford edition number, scriptural references, and a note that the laity shared in decisive action.
5. Cyprian argues that if lesser sins need a set penitence and the laying on of hands before communion, the gravest sins call for far more care, and that the presbyters should have warned the people.
6. Epistle XII, Cyprian to the clergy about the lapsed and the catechumens, who must not be left without oversight; he marvels that they have not answered his many letters.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XII, quoting Cyprian on lapsed Christians holding certificates from the martyrs who are dying, with a note on the word for catechumens.
8. Cyprian says that lapsed Christians holding certificates from the martyrs, if they fall gravely ill, may confess their sin to a presbyter, or to a deacon if none is present, and so receive peace.
9. Epistle XIII, Cyprian to the clergy about those in haste to receive peace: peace comes through penitence and keeping the commandments, and the sick holding the martyrs' certificates may be admitted.
10. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XIII, with the Oxford edition number, a note on a word for ill-will, and a gloss that those without certificates must wait.
11. Cyprian says those without certificates must wait for the public peace of the Church, which the bishops and clergy will settle together before the people who stood firm.
12. Epistle XIV, Cyprian to the presbyters and deacons at Rome: he gives an account of his withdrawal and what he did in it, since reports reaching Rome were garbled, and sends copies of his letters.
