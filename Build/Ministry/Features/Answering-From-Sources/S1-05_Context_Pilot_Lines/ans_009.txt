## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0035
1. Section 2: Cyprian cites Scripture that sins are purged by almsgiving and faith and that almsgiving quenches sin as water quenches fire; baptism gives remission once, and almsgiving cleanses those who become defiled again after baptism.
2. Section 3: Cyprian says everyone has some wound of conscience, no one should claim a clean heart, and God provides remedies in works of mercy for wounds that appear even after healing.
3. Section 4: Cyprian says Scripture, old and new, always urges works of mercy, and cites Isaiah that sharing bread with the hungry and clothing the naked brings God's hearing.
4. Section 5: Cyprian says God's own words give the remedies for sins, citing Solomon that alms shut up in the heart of the poor intercede for us and that one who stops his ears to the weak will not be heard, and the angel Raphael on alms.
5. Section 6: Cyprian says souls are delivered by almsgiving even from the first death, citing Tabitha, who was raised after widows pleaded for her because of her works.
6. Section 7: Cyprian says the Lord commands nothing more often than almsgiving and laying up treasure in heaven, citing the command to sell goods and give alms and the merchant who sold all to buy a pearl.
7. Section 8: Cyprian says the Lord calls those who aid the poor children of Abraham, citing Zacchæus, who gave half his goods and received salvation.
8. Section 9: Cyprian answers the fear of poverty, saying that what serves Christ cannot run out, and promises on the faith of Scripture that the giver will never lack.
9. Section 10: Cyprian tells the rich man who fears for his estate that he is losing life and salvation, loving mammon more than his soul, and cites that covetousness is a root of all evils.
10. Section 11: Cyprian says the righteous do not lack, citing Elias fed by ravens and Daniel fed in the lions' den, and asks why a servant of the Lord should fear want.
11. Section 12: Cyprian says one who feeds Christ will be fed by Christ, calls the faithless rich Pharisees, and recalls the Lord's advice to make friends of earthly wealth by good works.
12. Section 13: Cyprian calls the excuse of fear for the future a vain pretext of avarice, says the rich are slaves of their money, and urges them to share their gains with Christ and become his fellow-heirs.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0036
1. Editor's footnotes to On Works and Alms, with scriptural references, a rabbinic saying about an eagle and a coal, and notes on the word corban and on readings.
2. Section 14: Cyprian cites the Lord's words in the Apocalypse to those who say they are rich and need nothing but are really wretched and poor, urging them to buy true gold and anoint their eyes with Christ's eye-salve.
3. Section 15: Cyprian tells the rich who come to the Lord's Supper without bringing a sacrifice that they take part of the sacrifice the poor man offered, and holds up the widow who gave two mites.
4. Section 16: Cyprian says no one should be excused from good works for the benefit of his children, since the Lord must be loved more than father, mother, son or daughter.
5. Section 17: Cyprian recalls the widow who gave Elias her last meal and oil before feeding her own children, and says one who prefers himself and his children to Christ sins in the Church.
6. Section 18: Cyprian answers the excuse of many children by saying a father of many must give more alms, since there are more souls to be redeemed.
7. Section 19: Cyprian tells fathers to make God the guardian and trustee of their children's inheritance, since property kept with God is safe from the state, and warns against teaching children to love property more than Christ.
8. Section 20: Cyprian tells fathers to be like Tobias, who commanded his son to serve God, do righteousness and give alms, which deliver from death and Gehenna.
9. Section 21: Cyprian says a gift made to the poor is seen by God, Christ and the angels, a glory greater than the games and consulships of the Gentiles, and brings eternal life.
10. Section 22: Cyprian imagines the devil challenging Christ, saying his own people did nothing for him yet are almost equalled by Christ's people, to shame the stingy and indolent.
11. Section 23: Cyprian asks what excuse the rich can offer, since Christ has given precepts and set out the judgment, and quotes the Lord's words about feeding the hungry and visiting the prisoner.
12. Cyprian continues with the judgment scene: those who fed the hungry and clothed the naked did it to Christ, those on his left who gave nothing go to everlasting fire, and Christ abides in the one who is despised.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0037
1. Elucidation VII on Peter the rock: Launoi found only seventeen Fathers taking Peter to be the rock against forty holding otherwise, and the editor says no Catholic doctrine makes the modern bishops of Rome the rock.
2. Elucidation VIII on the eucharist: the modern practice of the priest placing the wafer in the mouth dates from about 880, set against Cyril of Jerusalem's teaching on receiving the bread in the hand.
3. Elucidation IX on who should be greatest: the editor argues that the Lord gave supremacy to no one apostle and that Peter knows nothing of it.
4. The editor refers to Bishop Sage's treatise and begins Elucidation X on Cyprian's reading of the lion image in the old Latin text.
5. The editor continues on the lion image by the Jordan and the old African Latin text, which Cyprian reads as Easter imagery fulfilled in the New Testament.
6. Elucidation XI on Quirinus, Cyprian's son in the Gospel, who may have been a catechumen of Jewish birth, and a note on the later withholding of the chalice in 1415.
7. Elucidation XII on good works and mercy: the first Christian hospital founded about 350 and hostels for travellers in the Nicene period.
8. The editor lists the moral rules in Cyprian's treatise: works of mercy, almsdeeds, brotherly love, forgiveness, love of enemies, avoiding usury and avarice, care of widows and orphans, and visiting the sick.
9. An elucidation on the numbering of the Psalms, noting the differences between the Hebrew, the Greek Septuagint and the Vulgate and how the Fathers cite them.
10. Heading of the Seventh Council of Carthage under Cyprian, concerning the baptism of heretics.
11. Introduction to the Seventh Council of Carthage: after Stephen of Rome condemned the African decrees on the baptism of heretics, Cyprian called eighty-seven bishops to Carthage in September 258 to decide again.
12. Editor's notes on the Seventh Council, with Augustine as the source, noting the judgments of the eighty-seven bishops on the nullity of baptism by heretics and identifying the bishops' cities.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0038
1. Cyprian opens the council by saying each bishop should state his view, judging no one and refusing communion to no one for differing, since none sets himself up as a bishop of bishops.
2. Cæcilius of Bilta says there is one baptism, in the Church and none outside, and that among heretics, where the faith is false, Antichrist baptizes in the name of Christ.
3. Primus of Misgirpa, Polycarp of Adrumetum and Novatus of Thamugada each give their vote that those coming from heresy must be baptized.
4. Nemesianus of Thubunæ says the baptism given by heretics is not the true one, quoting Scripture on false prophets, and that those who come from heresy must be born again in the Catholic Church by both sacraments.
5. Another bishop's opinion continues: one body, one Spirit, one baptism, and what heretics do is carnal, so they cannot be saved without baptism in the Catholic Church.
6. Januarius of Lambesis and Lucius of Castra Galbæ vote that heretics must be baptized, citing the salt that has lost its savour and the apostles' commission to baptize all nations.
7. Crescens of Cirta says heretics and schismatics who wish to come to the Church must first be exorcised and baptized, unless they were baptized in the Catholic Church before; another bishop says they obtain no remission among sinners outside.
8. Munnulus of Girba says heretics have neither Father, Son nor Holy Spirit, so when they come to the Church they must be born again and baptized.
9. Editor's footnotes to the council's votes, with scriptural references and notes identifying the bishops' cities in Numidia, Mauritania and Byzacena, and a remark that none of them suspected Stephen to be the rock.
10. Felix of Bagai, Polianus of Mileum, Theogenes of Hippo Regius and Dativus of Badis vote that heretics must be baptized, since the blind leading the blind fall together and heretics cannot give the Holy Spirit.
11. Fortunatus of Tuccaboris and Sedatus of Tuburbo vote that those outside the Church cannot baptize, since Christ built his Church on a rock and gave the power of baptizing to bishops, and that water infected by heretical discourse adds sins.
