## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0008
1. Cyprian's letter to Moyses, Maximus and the other confessors pictures their prison as the wine-press of the autumn vintage, where they shed blood for wine, and says death is overcome by the reward of immortality.
2. Cyprian praises the confessors' lofty minds, asks to be remembered in their prayers, and says their martyrdoms have led others to fear God.
3. Epistle XVI, the confessors to Cyprian: a short certificate written by Lucianus in the martyrs' name, granting peace to all the lapsed whose account of themselves has satisfied Cyprian.
4. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XVI, giving the Oxford edition number and date 250 and noting that the confessors address Cyprian with the title meaning pope.
5. Epistle XVII, Cyprian to the presbyters and deacons: certificates from the martyrs are not to count before the peace of the Church is restored, and the lapsed must show true penitence and humility.
6. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XVII, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, a scriptural reference, and a note that early bishops sought the agreement of the brethren.
7. Epistle XVIII, Caldonius to Cyprian: Caldonius asks whether peace should be given to lapsed Christians who confessed Christ again and went into exile.
8. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XVIII, with the Oxford edition number, a note on the term presbyters, and a remark on a touching incident in the letter.
9. Epistle XIX, Cyprian replies to Caldonius: he agrees that peace should be granted to lapsed Christians who have earned it by true repentance and by confessing the name of Christ.
10. Editor's footnote to Epistle XIX, giving the Oxford edition number and date 250, a guess that it refers to the treatise on the lapsed, and praise for Cyprian's obedience.
11. Epistle XX, Celerinus to Lucian: Celerinus rejoices that Lucian confessed Christ but grieves, and asks the Carthaginian confessors to give peace to his lapsed sisters at Rome.
12. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XX, with the Oxford edition number, comments on a corrupt reading, and a note on Candida, who turned back on her way to sacrifice.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0009
1. Celerinus tells Lucian of his distress at his sister's spiritual death in sacrificing, saying he spends the days in sackcloth and ashes, and asks that whichever confessor is crowned first should forgive his sisters Numeria and Candida.
2. Celerinus says one sister gave gifts so as not to sacrifice, so he knows she did not, and that the rulers ordered the sisters to stay as they are until a bishop is appointed; he asks the confessors to be indulgent.
3. Epistle XXI, Lucian replies to Celerinus, answering warmly and saying the confessors, by common agreement, have given peace to all.
4. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXI, noting the emperor Decius, a confused passage in the text, and a guess that the letter was written by poorly educated confessors.
5. Lucian reports what Paulus directed and the confessors' sufferings in prison, shut in two cells to die of hunger and thirst, and says Numeria and Candida shall have peace; he ends with greetings.
6. Epistle XXII, Cyprian to the Roman clergy about the confessors, the forwardness of Lucian and the modesty of Celerinus: he sends his own letters and theirs, and calls the lapsed demand for peace seditious.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXII, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, a reference to Galatians, and an editor's remark about the Marian dogma.
8. Cyprian says Lucian wrote in the name of all the confessors that peace had been given to all, loosening every bond of faith and discipline, and that he sent Rome a copy of the letter.
9. Cyprian contrasts the modest Celerinus with Lucian, who is unskilled in the Lord's word and grants pardon in a martyr's name, though past sins are done away in baptism; he adds that Rome agrees with him.
10. Epistle XXIII, Cyprian to the clergy: he sends copies of the letters exchanged with Rome and tells them that Saturus has been appointed reader and Optatus sub-deacon.
11. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXIII, noting a numbering error in Migne's edition and that teaching presbyters were a distinct class who judged the fitness of teachers.
12. Epistle XXIV, Cyprian to Moyses, Maximus and the other Roman confessors: a letter of congratulation praising their confession and urging them to stand firm against those who weaken the Lord's commands.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0010
1. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXIV, noting that the see of Rome was vacant after the death of Fabian in 250, with scriptural references and textual variants.
2. Cyprian recalls the Lord's charge to the apostles to teach and baptize all nations, and the saying that whoever claims to know him but does not keep his commandments is a liar, then ends with a farewell.
3. Epistle XXV, Moyses, Maximus, Nicostratus and the other Roman confessors answer Cyprian, thanking him for his consolation, calling martyrdom a happiness, and accepting his judgment on the lapsed.
4. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXV, noting that Cyprian's retreat was wise, that the persecution reached everywhere, and how Scripture shaped the martyrs' spirit.
5. The Roman confessors tell Cyprian that one who exhorts is no less worthy of reward than one who suffers, and begin to list the blessings of confession.
6. The confessors go on listing the blessings of confession: to stand free before God, refuse laws against the faith, bear public witness and overcome torture, and they cite the apostle's words that we are more than conquerors.
7. The confessors say that reading the Gospel feels like torches placed under them to inflame their faith, that they are ready to shed their blood, and that Cyprian has always been present with them in spirit.
8. The confessors praise Cyprian for rebuking those who extorted peace from the presbyters in his absence and gave what is holy to dogs, and say this grave sin must be treated with caution.
9. The confessors say hands stained by sacrifice must be cleansed by good works and mouths by words of true penitence, and that the penitent must weep again and again; they end with greetings.
10. Epistle XXVI, Cyprian to the lapsed: he answers those who claimed peace as already given because Paulus gave it to all, and describes the honour of a bishop and the order of the Church.
11. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXVI, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, a note that Cyprian's view of the bishop's chair was not peculiar to one bishop, and references to Matthew 16.
12. Cyprian says that if the lapsed claim to be the Church, there is nothing left but to ask them to admit us, so they should be humble and write to the Church, not in its name.
