## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0003
1. Section 13 of the sermon on the Lord's Prayer: Augustine says the prayer is a pact with God, forgive and be forgiven, and tells those about to be baptized to forgive anyone they hold anything against, so that all their debts may be forgiven.
2. Chapter X: Augustine urges love of enemies, who can harm only one's property or flesh, whereas hatred harms the hater, and tells the hearers to wish the enemy well so that his malice ends and he is no longer an enemy.
3. Augustine continues that an enemy shares our human nature and is our brother, with God as Father and the Church as mother, even if he is pagan, Jew or heretic; what should die is his malice, not the man.
4. Chapter XI: Augustine admits that loving enemies is for few, yet all say the prayer, and warns that God may ask why they ask him to forgive when they do not do what he commanded.
5. Augustine says that when an enemy asks pardon you must forgive at once, since refusing harms yourself, and recalls the Lord's prayer for those who did not know what they did; the text passes into Sermon LVII.
6. Augustine says to forgive hatred from the heart, not discipline, as a father still punishes his son; one who cannot yet love an enemy who rages can still forgive one who asks pardon.
7. Section 18, sixth petition: Augustine says forgive us concerns past sins, which cannot be undone, while lead us not into temptation but deliver us from evil concerns sins we might fall into.
8. Chapter XIV: Augustine says the first three petitions concern eternal life, where God's name is always hallowed and his will done, while bread and forgiveness concern present need; a heading for another sermon on the Lord's Prayer follows.
9. Chapter I of another sermon on the Lord's Prayer: Augustine says one must learn first what to believe and then what to ask, quoting the apostle that everyone who calls on the Lord's name will be saved.
10. Chapter II: Augustine says the Son of God taught us to pray and, though the only-begotten, wished to have brothers, so that we may call God our Father and live as children of such a Father.
11. Chapter III: Augustine says we ask our heavenly Father for rain and for a little more time before death, with groaning, and should cry out much more to reach the place where we never die.
12. Chapter IV, first petition: Augustine says we ask that God's name be sanctified in us, since it is always holy; he makes us holy through it, and we pray for ourselves and not for God.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0004
1. Chapter V, second petition: Augustine says God's kingdom is eternal and will come at the resurrection of the dead, when the faithful redeemed by the blood of the only Son will themselves be his kingdom; we watch now and will reign without end.
2. Chapter VI, third petition: Augustine says God's will is that the good reign and the wicked be condemned, and that this petition can be understood in many ways, which he sets out.
3. Chapter VII, fourth petition: Augustine says daily bread is both bodily and spiritual, naming the Eucharist, sermons, readings and hymns as daily bread for this pilgrim life; in heaven we will not need to ask for it.
4. Augustine continues that the Eucharist is our daily bread, which should feed the mind as well as the body, and that the unity of the Church as Christ's body is what we receive.
5. Chapter VIII, fifth petition: Augustine says debts are sins, which are all forgiven in baptism, yet the prayer is still needed in this life, and those about to be baptized must forgive others as they are forgiven.
6. Chapter IX, sixth petition: Augustine says temptation is of two kinds, citing the book of Job and the apostle's letter that God tempts no one to evil, and that desire brings forth sin and death.
7. Augustine goes on that after baptism sins are forgiven but desires remain to be fought, that the real enemy is within and not the outside tempter, and that God leads into temptation by abandoning.
8. Chapter X: Augustine says deliverance from evil belongs with the same petition, since God by freeing us from evil does not lead us into temptation.
9. Chapter XI: Augustine says the great temptation is the wish for revenge, because it takes away the forgiveness by which other falls are healed.
10. Augustine continues that the Lord singled out this one petition, forgiving our debtors, for comment after the prayer, and that to lose it is to be lost; chapter XII follows on love for enemies and daily debts.
11. Chapter XIII: Augustine urges the hearers to fight their own heart and anger, asking God to make them victors over themselves, and says God wants us to ask for this more than for rain; a heading for Sermon LVIII on the Lord's Prayer follows.
12. Chapter I of Sermon LVIII: Augustine says the Creed has been given back and now the Lord's Prayer is given, since one cannot call on one in whom one has not believed; Christ is our advocate and later our judge.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0006
1. Chapter II, section 3: Augustine says some could reach the Creator through creation, citing Paul in the Acts of the Apostles quoting the Athenians; the truth about God was plain to them, yet they are without excuse.
2. Chapter IV: Augustine says to glorify God is to give thanks to God, and that those who knew God but were not thankful are without excuse; only those whose heart is lifted up give thanks.
3. Chapter V: Augustine says the mysteries are hidden from the proud and revealed to the little ones, since those who claimed to be wise became fools; a heading follows for Sermon LXIX on the Lord's call to all who labour.
4. Chapter I of the sermon: Augustine says we all labour as mortal men, and that the Lord rejoiced in spirit that the Father hid these things from the wise and revealed them to the little, with the invitation to come to him.
5. Augustine says the promise is the sight of the true living God, who hears and sees, whereas false gods have eyes and do not see, and that many do evil because they think God does not see.
6. Chapter III: Augustine says the sinner cannot escape God by fleeing, since God fills heaven and earth, so the sinner must flee to him; Christ saw Nathanael under the fig tree.
7. Augustine explains the fig tree as the leaves of Adam's first sin and the desire of the flesh, and says God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, born of a virgin by faith and not by lust; a heading follows for another sermon on the same Gospel.
8. Chapter I of the next sermon: Augustine says it seems strange that the Lord calls his yoke easy, since the apostle Paul endured many hardships.
9. Chapter II: Augustine says the apostle bore all these hardships because the Holy Spirit renewed his inner self day by day, so the yoke of Christ was sweet and the burden light, while worldly pursuits cost people much more toil.
10. Chapter III: Augustine says love makes hard things gentle, so that those who love bear the same labours without finding them heavy, and that the sufferings of this time are not worth comparing to the coming glory.
11. Augustine says nothing is so easy to a good will as the good will itself, that the angels sang peace to men of good will, and that God does not allow us to be tempted beyond our strength; a heading follows for a sermon on blasphemy against the Holy Spirit.
12. Chapter I of a sermon on a difficult saying of the Gospel about the Spirit: Augustine asks the hearers to pray for him as he takes up the great question raised when the Lord healed a man who was possessed, blind and mute, and the Pharisees said he cast out demons by the prince of demons.

## augustine_sermones-1-340-lat_migne-pl38/0007
1. Augustine says the Lord's disciples, who learned no evil arts from their good master, will be judges of those who accused him of casting out demons by the prince of demons, and that the kingdom of God has come upon them.
2. Section 3: Augustine says deliverance from the devil comes by grace and not by merit, since the sons of the Pharisees were sinners in the devil's house until the strong one was bound; he cites Paul, a persecutor and blasphemer, who was rescued.
3. Chapter II: Augustine says that despite heresies and schisms the kingdom of Christ is not divided, since he who is not with Christ is against him, and those who gather under his name without being with him scatter.
4. Chapter III: Augustine states the difficulty that every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven but not blasphemy against the Spirit, and asks what hope is left for those the Church wishes to win.
5. Augustine says the Sadducees denied the Holy Spirit and the Pharisees defended him but denied he was in Christ; Jews and heretics who deny the Spirit is in Christ's body, the one Catholic Church, are like them, as are the Photinians.
6. Section 6: Augustine says that pagans, Jews and heretics blaspheme the Holy Spirit and yet cannot be written off, since all converts to the Church came from among them and are forgiven.
7. Chapter IV, section 7: Augustine rejects the view that only the baptized who sin gravely, as by adultery, murder or leaving the Church, sin against the Spirit, since penance is open to all crimes and heretics are to be corrected.
8. Chapter V, section 8: Augustine calls this the most difficult question in all Scripture, one he has avoided in his sermons, but today's Gospel moved him to speak, and he asks his hearers for their attention.
9. Section 9: Augustine points out that the Lord did not say every blasphemy against the Spirit is unforgiven but whoever speaks a word, otherwise the Church could win no pagan, Jew or heretic.
