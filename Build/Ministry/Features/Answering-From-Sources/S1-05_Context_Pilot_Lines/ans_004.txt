## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0011
1. Epistle XXVII, Cyprian to the presbyters and deacons: he approves their decision, with his colleagues present, not to communicate with Gaius, presbyter of Didda, and his deacon, who rashly admitted the lapsed to communion.
2. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXVII, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, notes on alms and offerings at the eucharist, scriptural references, and a variant reading.
3. Cyprian says the true remedy of the skilful physician cannot help while sweet enticements deceive, and tells the clergy to deal with the lapsed together with his colleagues and to keep the matter until he returns.
4. Epistle XXVIII, Cyprian to the Roman clergy: he tells them what has happened since his last letter, in which the lapsed rashly demand peace, and says he will act as the Gospel directs if they will not yield.
5. Editor's footnote to Epistle XXVIII, giving only the Oxford edition number and the date 250.
6. Epistle XXIX, the Roman presbyters and deacons to Cyprian: they agree with the Carthaginian decrees, saying peace for the lapsed must follow the Gospel law, and they put the demand of Felicissimus down to faction.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXIX, giving the Oxford edition number and noting that the word pope was then only the fatherly address given to all bishops, with a remark on sister churches.
8. The Roman clergy argue that a privilege of communion that is contrary to the Gospel cannot give true communion, and that those who ask peace for the lapsed should keep the law themselves.
9. The Roman clergy argue that the martyrs themselves referred the lapsed to the bishops, and warn against those who demand a premature communion, a fatal poison, instead of the remedy of delay.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0012
1. Epistle XXXI, Cyprian to the clergy at Carthage: he sends copies of the letters exchanged with the Roman clergy and with Moyses, Maximus and the other confessors, and asks that they be read.
2. Editor's footnote to Epistle XXXI, giving the Oxford edition number and date 250 and a remark on the Roman clergy administering jurisdiction while the see was vacant.
3. Epistle XXXII, Cyprian to the clergy and people: he reports that Aurelius, a confessor, has been ordained a reader, and praises his courage and modesty.
4. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXXII, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, a note that Cyprian acted before consulting because divine approval came first, and that Aurelius read for Cyprian during the persecution.
5. Cyprian says Aurelius is both dignified and humble and deserves higher ordination, but for now reading suits a voice that has confessed the Lord.
6. Epistle XXXIII, Cyprian to the clergy and people about the ordination of Celerinus as reader, praising how Celerinus confessed the faith through great suffering.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXXIII, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, references to the emperor Decius and to Ussher, and a note on the memorial offerings for martyrs.
8. Cyprian describes Celerinus lying nineteen days in a dungeon, racked and in irons, starved and thirsty, yet free in spirit.
9. Cyprian notes that the church offers sacrifices for the martyrs on their yearly anniversaries, and says Celerinus came from a family of martyrs, a nobility greater than any worldly rank.
10. Cyprian says Celerinus and Aurelius are equals, humble in their glory and fit for both conflict and peace, and that the Lord rejoices in such confessors.
11. Epistle XXXIV, Cyprian to the clergy and people: he tells them that Numidicus has been appointed a presbyter at Carthage and briefly commends him.
12. Editor's footnote to Epistle XXXIV, with the Oxford edition number and date 250, a variant reading, and a note on Cyprian's need to fill vacant places among his presbyters in a terrible time.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0013
1. Epistle XXXV, Cyprian to the clergy about the care of the poor and strangers: he explains that he stays away so that his presence will not provoke the heathens, and warns them not to neglect widows, the sick, the poor or strangers.
2. Editor's footnote to Epistle XXXV, giving the Oxford edition number and a date about 251, and a note on the form of address.
3. Epistle XXXVI, Cyprian to the clergy, telling them to show every kindness to the confessors in prison and to note the days of those who die there so that their memory can be kept each year.
4. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXXVI, with scriptural references and a note on Ussher's tract about the yearly commemorations of martyrs.
5. Cyprian reminds the clergy that the confessors did what the Lord asked, confessing, enduring and staying faithful unto death, and urges care for the poor as well.
6. Epistle XXXVII, Cyprian to Caldonius, Herculanus and others about excommunicating Felicissimus, who has been working wickedness and fraud and heading a sedition.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXXVII, with the Oxford edition number and date 250 and a note on where Felicissimus held his gathering.
8. Cyprian says Felicissimus has rushed out as the leader of a faction, that several brethren have rejected his boldness and stayed with the Church, their mother, and that the names of his followers are to be sent.
9. Epistle XXXVIII, Caldonius, Herculanus and others report that they have excluded Felicissimus, Augendus and several others from communion, as Cyprian had asked.
10. Editor's notes to Epistle XXXVIII, with the Oxford edition number and date 251 and a long note on the unknown meaning of a trade name.
11. Epistle XXXIX, Cyprian to the people about five schismatic presbyters of the faction of Felicissimus, who stirred up sedition and schism and granted peace to the lapsed without distinction.
12. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XXXIX, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, remarks on the clergy of Rome acting with sister churches, and on the one chair that Cyprian calls Peter's chair.

## anf05_hippolytus-cyprian-caius-novatian/0014
1. Cyprian says the five presbyters had earlier urged certain confessors to resist their bishop and are now trying to ruin the lapsed by keeping them from healing and setting up a new, sacrilegious practice.
2. Cyprian reminds the people that it had been agreed with the confessors, the city clergy and the bishops that nothing new would be done about the lapsed until all met together, and says the five have broken this.
3. Cyprian says those offering peace have no peace themselves, that there is one God, one Christ, one Church and one chair founded on the rock, and that no other altar can be set up.
4. Cyprian warns the people to reject those who put their own tradition above God's command, prays that the lapsed may be raised up, and asks the people to join their tears and prayers to his.
5. Cyprian warns the people to avoid the wolves who divide the sheep from the shepherd, and the devil who promises peace and salvation so that the sinner never gains them.
6. Epistle XL, Cyprian to Cornelius: he rejects the messengers sent by Novatian to announce his ordination as bishop, since the ordination was unlawful and made against the Catholic Church.
7. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XL, noting that Cornelius has become bishop of Rome, the terms for ordination, and that no universal bishop is heard of, the Roman bishop being treated like any other.
8. Cyprian says that when Novatian's messengers burst into the assembly demanding a public inquiry, he refused to let the honour of Cornelius, already chosen and approved, be questioned by rivals, and urges them to return to the Church.
9. Epistle XLI, Cyprian to Cornelius, approving his ordination and explaining that he accepted it once the letters of his colleagues Caldonius and Fortunatus showed it was lawful, and refused Novatian's accusations.
10. Editor's footnotes to Epistle XLI, with the Oxford edition number and date 251, textual variants, and a remark on how a modern pope would be addressed.
11. Cyprian says he has told his colleagues throughout the province and the people of Carthage of the ordination of Cornelius, and that he rejected what the other party sent against him.
12. Cyprian quotes Scripture against slander and says he would not let calumnious letters against Cornelius be read in Carthage, since the brethren have approved his priesthood.
