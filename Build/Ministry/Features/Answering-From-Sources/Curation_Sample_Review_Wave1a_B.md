## Recurring patterns

- A printed heading was declared as furniture (Optatus 2294). Furniture should be checked against the notes before a line is called a running head.
- A locus named a finer level than the division actually covers (CTh `pr`). When the edition prints a finer level, that level should be the division.
- Headings missed because a margin line number sits in front of the numeral (`15 XVII.`, `10 2.`, `; . IV.`), or because the numeral is in Greek capital letters (`ΙΧ.`). The worst cases are hand-listed `lines` for a rule, where a missed line gives no warning (Gebhardt 1902 Pionius, Knopf, Possidius).
- A dropped `I` in OCR read as a real numeral (Gebhardt 1902 Greek Perpetua, 4128). A cross-check against a second edition of the same text catches it.
- A running head used as a division start to get under the 5,000-word limit (second Gebhardt scan, 5619).
- Locus labels that describe the text rather than restate a printed heading (Robinson).
- `check` passed all eight files. None of these defects is something the checker can see. The checker's `number` gaps do flag the missed headings, but drafters left the gaps unexplained.

## Tooling observations

- `CDiv.start` in `resolve()` is 0-based, but YAML and evidence lines are 1-based. A reviewer script reading `resolved.divisions` has to add one.
