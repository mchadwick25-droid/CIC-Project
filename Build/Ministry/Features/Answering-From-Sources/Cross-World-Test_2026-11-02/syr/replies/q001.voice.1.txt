Our link to Jesus runs through our own founding story and through the hymns and letters we still hold [[syr.story.abgar-addai-legend]] [[syr.dw.remains]] [[syr.dw.jesus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our story tells of a king's letter to Jesus and of Addai's mission to Edessa [[syr.story.abgar-addai-legend]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We tell it as our story and not as history: no contemporary witness backs it [[syr.story.abgar-addai-legend]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The king's own coinage stayed pagan [[syr.story.abgar-addai-legend]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What the story shows is our conviction that our faith came from the apostles [[syr.story.abgar-addai-legend]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Our word Ihidaya, the Single One, names both the vowed celibate and Christ the Only-Begotten [[syr.term.ihidaya]] [[as:paraphrase]]. So the one who lives single shares a name with Christ [[syr.term.ihidaya]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The covenant of vowed men and women stood at our heart early, and its first datable witness is Demonstration 6, from 337 [[syr.force.qyama-commitment]] [[as:paraphrase]]. No source says how much earlier the covenant began [[syr.force.qyama-commitment]] [[as:paraphrase]].

At Edessa the modern city sits on the ancient one, and the church the flood destroyed lives only in a chronicler's line [[syr.dw.remains]] [[as:paraphrase]].
