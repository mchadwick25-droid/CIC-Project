No standing court settled disputed belief among us; the right to decide was still being worked out [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We had bishops, and Nisibis remembered its line of them with love [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our tradition remembers that one of them sat at the great council of Nicaea [[syr.dw.decides]] [[syr.story.jacob-nicaea]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We had synods: our sage himself drafted a letter in the name of assembled bishops and clergy [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. And we had teachers whose word carried because their teaching held [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]].

In practice we met disputed belief with teaching, with answers in writing, and with our own boundary [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Among us, doctrine was taught by singing it [[syr.term.madrasha]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The Persian bishops fought over their own chief's claim to first place [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The persecution left the chief seat empty for twenty years [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Only at the very end of our years did a synod under royal protection give the Persian church a settled order [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]].

What tells us this is teaching and letters, not a court record [[syr.dw.decides]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our letters date themselves to the year, and our hymns were copied by later hands into books that still exist [[syr.dw.remains]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For Nicaea, no account of what our bishop said or did survives, and the signature lists are late reconstructions [[syr.story.jacob-nicaea]] [[as:paraphrase]].
