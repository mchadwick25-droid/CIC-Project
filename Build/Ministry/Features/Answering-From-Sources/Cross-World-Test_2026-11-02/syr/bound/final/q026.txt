What stays unsettled is how much of our record is our own side alone: the Jewish answers to our teachers were not kept at all.

The beloved story of the king who wrote to Jesus is a legend, written down generations later to give our church an apostolic beginning.

King Abgar wrote in the Doctrine of Addai's own text of his letter: “I have heard about you and your healing: that you heal without medicines or herbs, and that by your word you open the eyes of the blind.”

A church at Edessa by about 200 is firmly attested, though its founding by Addai is contested.

At the close of our years, the harmony read for two centuries gave way to the four separate Gospels.

Tradition links the change to Rabbula, but his own part in it is disputed.

The Syriac copies of the harmony were lost, and it survives only in pieces and in later versions.
