The outside record we can weigh is a short city chronicle drawn from real archives, and its line about the flood that destroyed a church building in Edessa is the oldest dated word about us.

We cannot be sure that line stood in the first record and was not added later.

The Chronicle of Edessa, in its year-513 flood entry, records: “And they destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians. More than two thousand men were killed in that event. The waters came upon many of them suddenly as they slept in the night, and they drowned. The city was filled with the sound of mourning.”

At Nisibis, a baptistery stands whose own inscription dates it securely to the years of our bishop and the siege.

Our own voices are the sage's letters, which date themselves to the year, and the poet's hymns, which survive in ancient copies.
