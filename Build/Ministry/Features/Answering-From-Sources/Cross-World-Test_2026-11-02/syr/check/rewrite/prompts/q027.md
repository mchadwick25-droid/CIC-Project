Record syr.dw.doubt [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 337 to 373]
(1) Our word for the life of belief was not certainty but faith.
(2) Faith, the sage taught, is a building.
(3) It rests on one foundation stone and goes up piece by piece.
(4) Fasting, prayer and love are each added like courses of stone.
(5) A building is not finished in a day.
(6) What we distrusted was not the struggling believer.
(7) It was the prying scrutinizer, the one who would take the Godhead apart like a problem to be solved.
(8) Our own poet sang against that scrutiny while asking questions of his own, hymn after hymn.
(9) And we did not run from hard challenges.
(10) The sage records a debater's taunt: if your faith were true, would your prayers not have stopped the persecution?
(11) He says the taunt troubled him, and he answered it from Scripture.
(12) A private inner crisis of doubt is not something we wrote about.
(13) Our faith was questioned, and it kept growing.
(14) faith is a structure that grows over a life - incompleteness is its normal condition hard questions were engaged in the open, including the hardest, why God does not stop this what is warned against is prying dissection of God, not the struggling questioner This world's voice says faith was a building rising over a lifetime, that hard outside questions were answered openly, and that only prying scrutiny of the Godhead was warned against.
means: This world's voice says faith was a building rising over a lifetime, that hard outside questions were answered openly, and that only prying scrutiny of the Godhead was warned against.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a record of private inner crises of doubt, which the sources do not describe; a claim that all questioning was discouraged; a claim that open-ended uncertainty was valued as an end in itself

Record syr.dw.adam-grace [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 337 to 373]
(1) We said that from Adam's transgression, death has ruled over everyone - even over those who had not sinned the way Adam did.
(2) What people inherit from Adam, we described as death's rule and sin's wounding, a sickness in the human race.
(3) And we preached that our Lord took sin and nailed it to his cross.
(4) The sage taught faith with a picture.
(5) Christ was the foundation stone, and faith rested on him.
(6) On faith the whole house was raised: fasting, prayer, love, almsgiving, meekness.
(7) And a house is built FOR someone.
(8) The believer becomes a dwelling-place for Christ, and a king's dwelling is not left bare.
(9) As for the bread and cup, we received them with reverence as the Lord's own gift; we asked no deeper questions about how they were his gift.
(10) death rules from Adam over all - the race is wounded, and the wound is inherited Christ took sin and nailed it to the cross; salvation is healing as much as pardon faith is the one foundation, and the works are the house built on it - neither without the other the world's sacramental reverence predates the later analytical categories entirely Aphrahat teaches that death has ruled all since Adam as an inherited wound, that Christ nailed sin to the cross, and that works rise on faith.
means: Aphrahat teaches that death has ruled all since Adam as an inherited wound, that Christ nailed sin to the cross, and that works rise on faith.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a statement of original guilt or transubstantiation, which are later frames; a claim of faith without works, or of works without faith; a detailed eucharistic theology, which the record deliberately does not claim

Record syr.limit.what-must-one-do [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 337 to 345]
(1) You ask what a person must do to inherit eternal life.
(2) Aphrahat gave an order, not a short list, and he set it down in the first of his Demonstrations.
(3) First a man believes, and when he believes, he loves.
(4) When he loves, he hopes.
(5) When he hopes, he is justified, and then he is made perfect.
(6) The whole house is raised on Christ the Stone, with fasting, prayer, alms and meekness built on faith.
(7) For the one who falls, he named penitence as the medicine for the wound.
(8) He also wrote that every man will receive his reward by his work.
(9) Those with a small fault the judge will rebuke, and then he will give them the inheritance of life.
(10) No single act that secures it is named in the writings I can speak from.
(11) Aphrahat leaves the reckoning to the judge.
(12) Aphrahat teaches an order of believing, loving, hoping, being justified and being perfected, with works raised on faith and penitence as medicine, but no single act.
means: Aphrahat teaches an order of believing, loving, hoping, being justified and being perfected, with works raised on faith and penitence as medicine, but no single act.
