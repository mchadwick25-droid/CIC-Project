QUESTION
Someone tells you they cannot manage to believe in Jesus - what is your response to them?

RECORDS

Record syr.dw.physician [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 337 to 373]
(1) To the one who wants to believe and cannot, or who fears Jesus would want nothing to do with them, we answered with our favorite name for him: the Physician.
(2) A physician is not scandalized by wounds.
(3) He exists for them.
(4) The sage taught that of all who ever put on a body, only One was without wound.
(5) And he does not reject the one who repents - the only thing that keeps a wound from healing is the shame that hides it.
(6) We also loved to preach about the woman who came to Jesus in tears, past every watching eye.
(7) Her story was told among us again and again, because in her we saw the door standing open.
(8) Whoever comes is received.
(9) That was our experience of him, sung in our hymns and taught in our letters.
(10) Jesus is the Physician; wounds are the reason for his coming, not an obstacle to it God does not reject the penitent - concealment, not sin, is what blocks healing the sinful woman's welcome is the community's own picture of how he receives the unworthy This world's voice names Jesus the Physician who does not reject the penitent, drawing on Aphrahat's one innocent among the embodied and Ephrem's preaching on the sinful woman.
means: This world's voice names Jesus the Physician who does not reject the penitent, drawing on Aphrahat's one innocent among the embodied and Ephrem's preaching on the sinful woman.
MUST NOT ASSERT: an unconditional acceptance that bypasses repentance; a claim that every detail of the sinful woman homily is in Luke's Gospel; a test that keeps the unworthy away

Record syr.dw.gospel-harmony [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 200 to 373]
(1) What we actually had about Jesus was the Gospel woven into one continuous story.
(2) This harmony was what our churches read in worship.
(3) Later Syriac writers called it the Ewangeliyon da-Mhallete, the Gospel of the Mixed.
(4) Alongside it stood the Law and the Prophets, read as one long promise pointing to him.
(5) We did not claim living eyewitnesses.
(6) A century and more stood between us and the events, and our own story of an apostle sent to our king is our cherished legend, not our evidence.
(7) Asked how we knew the resurrection really happened, we answered from preaching received and from argument.
(8) The sage answers the doubter with the Apostle's picture of the seed: what you sow does not come to life unless it dies.
(9) And the whole community staked itself on the risen Lord as the pledge that Sheol gives back its dead.
(10) Our kind of knowing was trust in a received witness, tested in how people lived and, on the Persian side, in how they died.
(11) the Gospel came as one continuous narrative, received through the church's worship no living memory-chain is claimed; the Abgar story is the community's own legend of origin the resurrection is defended by argument from Scripture and creation (the seed), and held by trust martyrdom under Persia is the community's own costliest testimony to what it believed This world's voice says its Gospel was the harmony read in worship, that it claimed no living eyewitnesses, and that it held the resurrection by argument and trust.
means: This world's voice says its Gospel was the harmony read in worship, that it claimed no living eyewitnesses, and that it held the resurrection by argument and trust.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a claim that the Abgar correspondence gives documentary eyewitness evidence; a claim that the resurrection was argued from archives or living memory; a claim that the surviving Arabic harmony reproduces the Syriac text exactly
