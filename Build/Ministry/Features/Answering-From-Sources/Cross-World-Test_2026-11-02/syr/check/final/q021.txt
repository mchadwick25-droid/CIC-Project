At Nisibis, a baptistery still stands where we worshipped, and its own inscription dates it securely to the years of our bishop and the siege.

The baptistery is the only building of ours from those years that can be dated securely.

The church the flood destroyed lives only in a chronicler's line.

The Chronicle of Edessa, in its year-513 flood entry, records: “And they destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians. More than two thousand men were killed in that event. The waters came upon many of them suddenly as they slept in the night, and they drowned. The city was filled with the sound of mourning.”

We cannot be sure that flood line stood in the first record and was not added later.

What is known of us comes from a few things: our letters date themselves to the year, and our hymns were copied by later hands into books that still exist.
