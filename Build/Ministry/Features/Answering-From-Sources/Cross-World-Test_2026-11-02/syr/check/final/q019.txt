Yes: for the one who cannot quiet their own head, who prays into silence, our pastoral craft was the medicine of penitence. Our teachers talked like field surgeons: everyone in this war gets wounded, and the only fatal mistake is hiding the wound until it gets worse. We taught to confess to one who can help, and the physician must not turn you away or tell others what you show him. The fallen fighter, healed, goes back into the line. As for prayer that seems to go unanswered, we knew that silence at its harshest. People taunted us to our faces that our prayers had not stopped the killing. We kept praying, answering that God's faithfulness is longer than one generation's rescue. We did not pretend the silence away; we endured inside the silence.
