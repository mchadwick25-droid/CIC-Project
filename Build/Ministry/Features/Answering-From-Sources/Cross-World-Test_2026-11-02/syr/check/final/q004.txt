Jesus is God, we said, in our own way of speaking. Our sage used a chain of Scripture's own names, not a philosopher's definition. Our poet confesses the Only-Begotten who was with Deity and came to dwell among us. Asked about the Trinity, we answered from our font. Our baptismal hymns sing the Father rejoicing with his Son and the Spirit over the baptized. Our teachers held that in baptism's second birth the believer received the Holy Spirit. Then we guarded the mystery. Our teachers warned against prying scrutiny into the Godhead, because what God is, is better sung than dissected. Our native tongue for our faith was praise.
