We will not tell you the suppression of the Anabaptists was only ever argument, when plainly the suppression was not. We held that a church gathered only by a believer's own profession, apart from the whole city, unmade the unity our reform existed to strengthen. The Anabaptists read Scripture, and we do not deny it; we held that the Anabaptists did not read Scripture whole. At Geneva our Consistory had excommunication as its harshest step, which shut a person out of the Supper and the visible church. What we cannot give you is a single real case, because Geneva's consistory registers have not reached us. The Perrinist faction contested the Consistory's independence from civil-council control, and the fight was substantially resolved in Calvin's favor only by 1555.
