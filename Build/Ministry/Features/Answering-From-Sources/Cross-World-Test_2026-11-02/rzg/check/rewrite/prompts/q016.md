Record rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 1523 to 1555]
(1) We hold one conviction, tested two ways.
(2) Scripture alone may bind a church; what it does not warrant, no one may require of us.
(3) At Zurich, that conviction was argued aloud, before the council and some six hundred priests and laymen, until the council itself said what the text had yielded.
(4) At Geneva, it was built up article by article and taught to everyone through the Catechism.
(5) We do not read these as two churches that happen to agree.
(6) We read them as one authority, enacted twice by two cities that did not need to share a method.
(7) What we will not tell you is that the two methods ever became one arrangement.
(8) Zurich's council governed church and city together.
(9) Geneva's Consistory fought for its independence from that kind of council control, and only by 1555 substantially won it.
(10) We hold that unresolved, in the same breath as the conviction itself, because it is also true.
(11) Scripture alone tests what a church may require; what it does not warrant, no council or custom may bind on us.
(12) That is the one conviction underneath everything else we hold, and we did not arrive at it by agreeing in advance to work the same way.
(13) At Zurich, we test a claim aloud, before the whole assembled city, until the council itself judges what the text has yielded -- and there is no second court to appeal to, because none is wanted.
(14) At Geneva, we build the same conviction up a different way: article by article, taught to the whole population through the Catechism, and tested against a believer's own daily conduct by the Consistory.
(15) Two cities, two methods, the same authority behind both.
(16) We do not read this as two churches that happen to agree.
(17) We read it as evidence the conviction is real: the same scriptural authority, argued for aloud in one city and built up in fixed sequence in the other, produced doctrine neither city could have dictated to the other, and neither had to.
(18) That is why, when we did put our names to one page together in 1549, it changed nothing about how either city tests doctrine day to day.
(19) We did not need to become one method to already share one authority.
(20) Zurich's public disputation and Geneva's catechesis enact one conviction, that Scripture alone binds the church, without becoming one arrangement.
means: Zurich's public disputation and Geneva's catechesis enact one conviction, that Scripture alone binds the church, without becoming one arrangement.

Record rzg.witness.one-supper-two-poles [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 1523 to 1549]
(1) To us the bread and cup were not a sacrifice repeated, and not Christ's own body, confined to what you can chew.
(2) Our own founder said this first: the mass is a remembrance, not a sacrifice.
(3) Twenty-six years later, both our cities spoke together on the same Supper.
(4) We distinguished the sign from what it points to, but we did not pull them apart: all who take the promise in faith receive Christ spiritually.
(5) We hold both statements as our own.
(6) We will not tell you the later one quietly replaced the first, as though our own founder had been corrected and we were too polite to say so.
(7) Nor will we tell you nothing changed.
(8) Our own record does not decide which is the last word, and neither do we.
(9) The Supper is not a sacrifice repeated, and Christ's own body is not confined to the bread.
(10) That much we have always held.
(11) Our own founder said it first, and plainly: 'the mass is not a sacrifice, but is a remembrance of the sacrifice.' Two cities, one page, said it again twenty-six years later, in fuller words: 'though we distinguish, as we ought, between the signs and the things signified, yet we do not disjoin the reality from the signs.' Christ is truly given, by the Spirit's own power, to whoever receives him believing -- not carnally, not through the bread itself, but truly given all the same.
(12) We hold both statements as our own, because both are.
(13) What we will not tell you is which one finally speaks for us, as though the second had quietly corrected the first.
(14) Perhaps the later word only says the earlier one more fully.
(15) Perhaps it says something the earlier word did not yet reach.
(16) Our own record does not decide this, and we do not decide it for you.
(17) We hold both Zwingli's 1523 remembrance language and the 1549 sign-and-reality formula, refusing both sacrifice and Christ's confinement to the bread.
means: We hold both Zwingli's 1523 remembrance language and the 1549 sign-and-reality formula, refusing both sacrifice and Christ's confinement to the bread.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a claim that this world held transubstantiation or a repeated sacrifice; a claim that the Supper was a bare memorial with nothing truly given; a claim that the 1549 Consensus corrected or replaced Zwingli's teaching; a claim that nothing changed between the 1523 and 1549 statements
