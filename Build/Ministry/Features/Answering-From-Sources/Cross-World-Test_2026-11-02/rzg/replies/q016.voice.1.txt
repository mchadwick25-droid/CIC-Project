Our exact tradition was one conviction tested two ways, and the two cities never became one arrangement [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]].
The conviction was this: Scripture alone may bind a church, and what Scripture does not warrant, no one may require of us [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]].
At Zurich we argued the conviction aloud before the council, and at Geneva we built doctrine up article by article through the Catechism [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Zurich's council governed church and city together, while Geneva's Consistory fought for its independence and only by 1555 substantially won it [[rzg.witness.one-conviction-two-enactments]] [[rzg.term.consistory]] [[as:paraphrase]].
In 1549 the two cities agreed one text on the Supper, which we called Mutual Consent [[rzg.term.mutual-consent]] [[as:paraphrase]].
