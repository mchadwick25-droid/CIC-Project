Scripture alone was our final authority for doctrine and church order, not church tradition or a pope's decree.

Scripture alone may bind a church, and what Scripture does not warrant, no one may require of us.

Whatever Scripture does yield, no pope, council, or custom may set aside.

Huldrych Zwingli declared: “I, Ulrich Zwingli, declare that I have preached the articles and opinions below in the good city of Zurich. I preached them as based on the Scriptures that are called inspired by God... Where my present understanding of these Scriptures is not correct, I will let myself be taught better. But I will be taught only from these same Scriptures.”

Zwingli began continuous, book-by-book exposition at Zurich's Grossmunster from 1519, rather than following the fixed lectionary.

He brought his Sixty-Seven Articles to the First Zurich Disputation in 1523.

At Zurich the city council judged what the text had yielded, and the council bound every priest in its territory.

At Geneva, doctrine was taught to the whole population through the Catechism.

We held more than one catechism, in more than one voice.

We hold that the 1525 Swiss Brethren baptisms misread Scripture, for a church gathered only by adult profession unmakes the unity of church and city.
