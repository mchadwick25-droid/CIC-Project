We joined people to the church by baptising infants, from birth, as part of a whole believing city. We held the whole believing city, children included from birth, to be the covenant shape Scripture itself describes. We did not see a baptised child as belonging to a lesser church waiting on a later, personal choice. Against us stood men who had once stood with our own founder and first baptised each other as believing adults. Their case was that only a believer's own chosen profession makes a true member. Their first baptisms came on 21 January 1525 at Felix Manz's house in Zurich, with Conrad Grebel baptizing Georg Blaurock. Once inside, a member at Geneva stood under the Consistory, where pastors and lay elders held daily conduct to account. Someone shut out by excommunication could return, because readmission stayed open to genuine repentance.
