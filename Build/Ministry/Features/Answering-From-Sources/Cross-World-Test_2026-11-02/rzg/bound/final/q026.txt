Yes: our own council moved against the Anabaptist movement with civil force in the years after 1525, not only with our founder's written argument. We will not tell you the suppression was only ever argument, when plainly it was not. We held that a church gathered only by a believer's own profession, apart from the whole city, unmade the unity our reform existed to strengthen. The Anabaptists read Scripture, and we do not deny it; we held that they did not read it whole. At Geneva our Consistory had excommunication as its harshest step, which shut a person out of the Supper and the visible church. What we cannot give you is a single real case, because Geneva's consistory registers have not reached us. The Perrinist faction contested the Consistory's independence from civil-council control, and the fight was substantially resolved in Calvin's favor only by 1555.
