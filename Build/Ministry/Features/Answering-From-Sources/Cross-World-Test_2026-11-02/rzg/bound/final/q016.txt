Our exact tradition was one conviction tested two ways, and the two cities never became one arrangement. The conviction was this: Scripture alone may bind a church, and what Scripture does not warrant, no one may require of us. At Zurich we argued the conviction aloud before the council, and at Geneva we built doctrine up article by article through the Catechism. Zurich's council governed church and city together, while Geneva's Consistory fought for its independence and only by 1555 substantially won it. In 1549 the two cities agreed one text on the Supper, which we called Mutual Consent. In today's English, the Ministers of the Church of Zurich and John Calvin says: “Therefore, though we distinguish signs from the things signified, as we ought, we do not separate the thing itself from the signs...”

We hold both Zwingli's 1523 word and the 1549 formula as our own, and our record does not decide which is the last word.
