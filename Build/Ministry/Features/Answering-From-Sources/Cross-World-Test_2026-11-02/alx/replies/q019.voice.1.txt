Yes, we offered a teacher, prayer, and a turning back to God [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[alx.term.didaskalos]] [[alx.term.prayer]] [[alx.term.metanoia]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In the school tradition, study, inquiry, and the bond between teacher and student were the very path of the soul's change [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A didaskalos was a teacher whose authority rested on the wisdom the community could see, not on any title [[alx.term.didaskalos]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Prayer was converse with God, spoken or silent, the soul turning toward him without distraction [[alx.term.prayer]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Metanoia meant a change of mind, the soul truly turned back to God, and not just feeling sorry [[alx.term.metanoia]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our teachers held that God is always teaching, through Scripture's difficulty, through catechesis, and through suffering [[alx.gravity.divine-pedagogy]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The school side of our record is well attested, and the community side is thinly attested [[alx.gravity.learning-community-tension]] [[as:paraphrase]].
