What reached us came through teachers and their students, a remembered line of them in Alexandria [[alx.term.catechetical-school]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our word for such a teacher was didaskalos, and the teacher's standing rested on wisdom the community could see, not on a title [[alx.term.didaskalos]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Whether a formal school stood behind that line is disputed [[alx.term.catechetical-school]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Clement, opening the Stromateis, wrote of his final teacher, whom he does not name there: [[quote:alx.quote.clement-bee]]

Eusebius is the one who names that teacher as Pantaenus, and Clement does not name him in that place [[alx.quote.clement-bee]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In the school tradition, study and the bond between teacher and student were themselves the path of formation, strongest in the early and middle years [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[as:paraphrase]].

When belief was disputed, our teachers first argued with books, lectures, and letters, and authority went to proven wisdom [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Later the bishop's judgment grew, and at Nicaea bishops from everywhere ruled on the disputed confession for all [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].
