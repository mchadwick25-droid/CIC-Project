Two kinds of authority stood side by side among us: authority from wisdom, and authority from office [[alx.force.teacher-bishop-ongoing]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The teacher was trusted because others saw that he saw, and the bishop held the office handed down [[alx.force.teacher-bishop-ongoing]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Early on, teachers argued with books, lectures, and letters, and authority went to proven wisdom [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

In the middle years the bishop's judgment grew, and Demetrius condemned Origen's ordination [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At Nicaea, bishops from everywhere ruled on the disputed confession for all [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. After Nicaea, the bishop of Alexandria became the enforcer of conciliar orthodoxy across Egypt, with exile, letter-networks, and synods as his tools [[alx.force.post-nicene-authority-shift]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Authority came to mean the drawn confession as well as the office handed down, and the teacher's kind of standing gave way [[alx.force.post-nicene-authority-shift]] [[as:paraphrase]]. After the Origen rupture and under the post-Nicene shift, formation's center of gravity moved toward the episcopal-doctrinal channel [[alx.gravity.learning-formation]] [[as:paraphrase]].
