We would have met you as one who asks, and asking was how a soul was actually formed. We treated doubt aimed at understanding as hunger, not as sin. We expected a questioner to bring the question to a teacher rather than carry the question alone. Questions stayed inside the rule of faith, and what the apostles delivered plainly stayed fixed. Our word mysterion meant a depth known only from inside through formation, not a secret kept for an inner circle. We owed breadth-formation to the whole worshipping community, as well as depth-formation to the few. Our teachers' sources barely show how ordinary doubters themselves lived through their questions.
