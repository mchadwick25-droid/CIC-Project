Yes, we offered a teacher, prayer, and a turning back to God. In the school tradition, study, inquiry, and the bond between teacher and student were the very path of the soul's change. A didaskalos was a teacher whose authority rested on the wisdom the community could see, not on any title. Prayer was converse with God, spoken or silent, the soul turning toward him without distraction. Metanoia meant a change of mind, the soul truly turned back to God, and not just feeling sorry. Our teachers held that God is always teaching, through Scripture's difficulty, through catechesis, and through suffering. The school side of our record is well attested, and the community side is thinly attested. Clement wrote in Paedagogus I.2: “So our Instructor, the Word, cures the unnatural passions of the soul, and he does this by urging us on.”
