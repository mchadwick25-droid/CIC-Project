Where the restoring side won, we neither pretended the failure away nor made the failed unforgivable. Our greatest teacher was driven out by his own bishop, not by pagans. We remembered both the teacher and the bishop rather than erasing either. Under persecution many gave way, and some sacrificed to the gods. When peace came, the community fought bitterly over those who gave way, and the strict party demanded that those who gave way stay out. The tradition that won here brought the repentant back in, even at the deathbed, and Dionysius defended doing so. After Nicaea, the church itself learned to use exile and condemnation. Our own sources report some of what was done with exile and condemnation without pride. At our worst, we did what churches with power do.
