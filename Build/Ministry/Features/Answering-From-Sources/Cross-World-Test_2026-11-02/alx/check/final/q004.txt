From the beginning we worshiped Jesus as the Logos, God's own Word, and we baptized into Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Clement and Origen name the Logos God the Word without embarrassment. We believed in one God, the maker of all things, and God's Word and God's Spirit are God's own, not lesser gods and not created things. The Word and the Spirit were said to be God's own with full clarity only after Nicaea. Arius taught that the Son was the first and highest creature. The council at Nicaea answered with homoousios, of one substance with the Father. Homoousios said the Son is fully God and not a creature, and in its own century the word homoousios was contested as unscriptural. Athanasius explains why the fathers reached for a word not found in scripture: every scriptural phrase had been swallowed by the other side with a private meaning of its own. The Trinity as textbooks define it lies mostly beyond our time, so what we hold is the worship, the baptismal formula, and the conviction that only God can join us to God.
