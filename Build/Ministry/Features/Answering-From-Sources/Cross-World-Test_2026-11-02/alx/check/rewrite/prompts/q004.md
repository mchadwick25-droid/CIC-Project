QUESTION
Explain your community's understanding of Jesus's divine status and its relation to God the Father and the Spirit.

Record alx.dw.was-jesus-god [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 180 to 369]
(1) From the beginning we worshiped Jesus as the Logos, God's own Word, and we baptized into Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
(2) Clement and Origen say 'God the Word' without embarrassment.
(3) But the precise wording was the work, and the wound, of our last years.
(4) When Arius taught that the Son was the first and highest creature, the council at Nicaea answered with homoousios: of one substance with the Father.
(5) Athanasius explains why the fathers reached for a word not found in scripture: every scriptural phrase had been swallowed by the other side with a private meaning of its own.
(6) 'Trinity' as later textbooks define it lies mostly beyond our time.
(7) What we hold is the worship, the baptismal formula, and the fought-for conviction that only God can join us to God.
(8) Jesus was worshiped as God's own Word from the tradition's beginning Nicaea's word homoousios put a fence around worship already in use.
(9) It did not create a new object of worship.
(10) The baptismal faith names the Spirit from the start.
(11) Clearer teaching about the Spirit only begins late in the window.
(12) This witness says Jesus was worshiped as God's Word from the start, and Nicaea's homoousios fenced that worship rather than voting in a new God.

Record alx.dw.god [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 180 to 254]
(1) We believed in one God, the maker of all things.
(2) He is good, and beyond every image and every clever description.
(3) And he is never alone: his Word and his Spirit are his own - not lesser gods, and not created things.
(4) (Only after Nicaea was that last part said with full clarity.) God is known the way light is known.
(5) You do not stare at the sun; you see everything else by it.
(6) We argued about much: where the soul - the psyche - comes from, the shape of the end, how far philosophy could walk with faith.
(7) Our teachers drew one careful line.
(8) What the apostles delivered plainly stood fixed.
(9) Everything else was open ground, where asking questions was itself a way of loving God.
(10) Our deepest habit was to speak of God as a teacher.
(11) The whole creation is his school, and scripture is his main lesson.
(12) The slow healing of a soul is his patient method.
(13) There is one God, creator and good.
(14) The church refused the Gnostic split between a creator and a redeemer.
(15) the Word and Spirit belong to God's own being The answer sets apart the fixed apostolic ground and the open questions.
(16) Inquiry is free inside the rule of faith.
(17) God's relation to the world is pedagogical: he teaches, through everything This witness describes one good creator God beyond every image, whose Word and Spirit are his own, and who teaches through his whole creation.
means: This witness describes one good creator God beyond every image, whose Word and Spirit are his own, and who teaches through his whole creation.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a claim that the Word's and Spirit's full divinity was stated with clarity before Nicaea; a claim that teachers' speculations on the soul's origin or the end were fixed doctrine; the dedicated answer on doubt, which sits in alx.dw.doubt

Record alx.term.homoousios [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 325 to 373]
(1) 'Of one substance': the word the council at Nicaea used to say the Son is fully God, not a creature.
(2) The Nicene word: the Son is fully God, not made.
(3) homoousios Homoousios was the Nicene council's word for the Son as fully God and not a creature, contested in its own century as unscriptural.

MUST NOT ASSERT: projecting it into any period before 325; describing it as a word found in Scripture; saying the council voted Jesus into being God
