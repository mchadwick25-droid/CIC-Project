Who settled a dispute changed across our history: first the teachers, then the bishop, and at the end the council [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Early on, teachers argued with books, lectures, and letters, and authority went to proven wisdom [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

In the middle years the bishop's judgment grew, and Demetrius condemned Origen's ordination [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

At Nicaea, bishops from everywhere ruled on the disputed confession for all, and the emperor who called the council backed the council with force [[alx.dw.councils]] [[as:paraphrase]].

The rule of faith, the short summary of what the apostles handed down, set the bounds within which inquiry into Scripture could range [[alx.term.kanon-pisteos]] [[as:paraphrase]].

After Nicaea, the bishop of Alexandria became the enforcer of conciliar orthodoxy across Egypt, with exile, letter-networks, and synods as the bishop's tools [[alx.force.post-nicene-authority-shift]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Our best picture of deciding well is Bishop Dionysius's own account of his work with the village churches [[alx.story.arsinoite-conference]] [[as:paraphrase]].

The arguments exchanged in the villages survive only in Dionysius's summary, and Nepos's book is lost, known only through the man who refuted Nepos's book [[alx.story.arsinoite-conference]] [[as:paraphrase]].
