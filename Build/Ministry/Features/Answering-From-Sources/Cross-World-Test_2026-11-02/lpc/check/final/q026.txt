We answered the question of baptism outside our one church twice, in two different ways, a century and a half apart. Cyprian's council said a baptism given outside our one church did not count, while Augustine said the baptism was given, though it did no good there. Augustine argued at book length that the ruling of Cyprian's council was wrong. Yet Augustine never put Cyprian outside our communion. We do not pretend that being answerable for someone makes the question of baptism outside our one church easy. The confessors held that their suffering gave them standing to ask peace for the lapsed. The church answered that a name must be set down, then examined before the whole congregation. We hold both as real, and we have never found the place where the confessors' claim and the church's process become one settled rule.
