Among us a bishop was given a particular people, and the bishop answered for what became of the people. The people of a bishop's flock could stand before the bishop's seat and refuse to leave until the bishop heard them. Pontius, writing in praise of Cyprian, says God's judgment and the people's favour made Cyprian bishop. Later Cyprian named our suffrage and God's judgment side by side. Pontius tells of Cyprian's election. Our deacon Pontius wrote our bishop Cyprian's life and chose one fact as proof enough of the good he did. In 248 Cyprian was chosen bishop of Carthage "by the judgment of God and the favour of the people." He had only recently been baptized, and he stepped back from the office, but a crowd of believers crowded outside the house where he was. For us, the people's favour was the way we saw God's judgment at work. In 251 Cyprian wrote to us that some presbyters had long hated him as bishop, which meant hating our choice and God's judgment. Pontius alone says Cyprian was a new convert, and no other witness of ours confirms that Cyprian was a new convert. A confessor was someone who was questioned for the Name, did not deny the Name, and lived. In Cyprian's time a confessor's standing could rival a bishop's own. Confessors wrote notes asking that named lapsed people be taken back, an informal claim to grant peace. Cyprian's penance, which the bishop ran and controlled, answered the confessors' rival claim. The road back came in public steps under the bishop. Our church was already organized enough to hold factions, and to call councils of dozens of bishops.
