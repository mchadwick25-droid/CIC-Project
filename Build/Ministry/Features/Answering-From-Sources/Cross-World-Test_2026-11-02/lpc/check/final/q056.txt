Yes, our church sent a hundred thousand sesterces to ransom our captive brethren.

Cyprian wrote: “Finally, we give you our warmest thanks. You wanted us to share in your concern, and in so great and needed a work. You offered us fruitful fields in which to sow the seeds of our hope. We expect a harvest of the richest fruit from this heavenly work that brings salvation. So we have sent you a sum of one hundred thousand sesterces. It was collected here in the Church over which, by the Lord's mercy, we preside. It came from the gifts of the clergy and people who live among us. You will hand it out there as carefully as you can.”

Our letters tell of this ransom:

Eight bishops wrote to Cyprian, who led our church, that barbarians had carried off our brothers and sisters, and he read their letter with tears.

He answered that we must pay to set them free, not only out of love but because our religion called us to it. Paul says that everyone baptized into Christ has put on Christ, so we were to see Christ himself in our captive brothers and sisters.

Our clergy and people gave quickly and freely. We sent the bishops one hundred thousand sesterces to use for the captives as carefully as they could.

We do not know whether the ransom worked, because Cyprian never wrote again about the outcome. No source of ours gives the sum in modern money, and specialists contest any such comparison.
