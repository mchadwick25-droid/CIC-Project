Among us, a bishop is given a particular people in one place, and he answers for them. A bishop preaches to the people given him and baptizes them. When the flock fails, our bishop carries the failure as a wound, and does not manage it as a problem.

The charge runs both ways, for the flock can stand before the bishop's seat and refuse to leave until the bishop hears them. As we understand ourselves, a particular people is placed in a particular man's care, and the man will be asked what became of them.

Augustine told his people: “You share communion with the members of the Apostles. You share communion with the shrines of the holy martyrs, who are spread over the whole world. And you belong to my care, so that I may give a good account of you. You know what my whole account is. ‘Lord, You know that I have spoken. You know that I have not kept silent. You know in what spirit I have spoken. You know that I have wept before You when I spoke and was not heard.’”
