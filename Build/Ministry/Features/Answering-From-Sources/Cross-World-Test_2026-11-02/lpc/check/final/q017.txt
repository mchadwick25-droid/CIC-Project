Augustine traced two of our practices back to the apostles, though none of the apostles wrote either practice down. The two practices are baptizing infants and refusing to rebaptize someone already baptized among heretics. For the refusal to rebaptize, Augustine said no one could find a later age that had invented the custom. Baptizing infants, Augustine said, was held everywhere and instituted by no council. Augustine held that a custom so old and so universal, with no other explanation for its origin, is rightly believed to come from the apostles. Augustine names the line of Roman bishops from Peter down to the man sitting then, and Cyprian states the principle that carries the line forward. Neither Augustine nor Cyprian traces catechesis, the road back, or the teaching before the water to the apostles by name. We also hold that the office of bishop is one, and each bishop holds his part of the office for the whole. Cyprian argues that the office of bishop is one from what the Lord said to Peter, as an argument from scripture and not as a second named succession.
