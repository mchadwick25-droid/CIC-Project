Among us a person who failed was met with a road back, walked in public and in steps under the bishop. The road had an order, the order took time, and everyone could see the road. The Decian persecution made failure common, through certificates that recorded the holder had sacrificed. A person could get a certificate by performing the sacrifice, or by paying to have the sacrifice recorded. The Decian persecution sorted the congregation into those who had stood and those who had not, and both groups still belonged. Cyprian wrote that when the flock is wounded, the shepherd is wounded most. Confessors issued written requests that named lapsed persons to be received back. The contest over the lapsed had to be settled by a formal process, not by a bishop's word alone. Augustine wrote to Honoratus, a bishop in Mauritania, in 428 or 429. When danger fell on all alike, Augustine held, ministers must not leave those who depend on them, so that the dying can be baptized and reconciled. Augustine also held that when someone who has sinned against you asks forgiveness, and you forgive from the heart, that much fulfils what the Lord's Prayer asks. We can tell you what was decided about a person, how carefully, and by whom. What an ordinary believer felt walking up to the altar is not ours to give you.
