As a young man drawn to a rival teaching, Augustine was much disturbed by one question. The question was whether men who had many wives, and did kill men, could really be called righteous. Once Augustine held our faith, Augustine answered a Manichaean who pressed the same kind of charge. Augustine wrote that Moses showed not ferocity but obedience. God, in commanding the wars of Moses, acted not in cruelty but in righteous retribution, Augustine wrote. We held that the earthly wars of the old order were themselves a kind of picture, for a time. Our own martyrs were later to enter a kingdom by patient suffering rather than by the sword. On the first three days of Genesis, which come before the sun, Augustine named the difficulty honestly rather than explaining it away.
