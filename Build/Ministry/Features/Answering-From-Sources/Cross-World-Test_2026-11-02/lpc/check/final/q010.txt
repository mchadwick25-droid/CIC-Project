Augustine took up how to read the days of Genesis directly, in City of God. Ordinary days have morning and evening only by the sun's rising and setting. But Genesis reports the first three days before the sun existed, since the sun was made only on the fourth day. What kind of light made those days, and by what movement the light made evening and morning, is beyond the reach of our senses. We cannot understand how it was, Augustine wrote, and yet we must believe it. Some histories claimed many thousands of years for the world, and Augustine called those documents highly mendacious. Augustine held instead that by Scripture's own reckoning, not 6,000 years had yet passed since the world began. Augustine read the ruling on rebaptism made by Cyprian's council of 256, and argued at length that the ruling was wrong. Cyprian was a predecessor who belonged to us, and we did not disown him.
