Augustine wrote his treatise on marriage against those who thought that praising virginity meant condemning marriage. Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament. Of the third good, Augustine wrote that a marriage compact is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament. Separation does not make the marriage compact void. So long as her husband lives, Augustine taught, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery. Augustine's teaching on the separated wife is a teaching about what marriage itself is and what marriage binds.
