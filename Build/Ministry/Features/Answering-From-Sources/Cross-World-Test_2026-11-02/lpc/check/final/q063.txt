Augustine reasoned that ordinary days have morning and evening only by the sun's own rising and setting. Yet Genesis reports the first three days before the sun existed, since the sun was made only on the fourth day. Augustine named the difficulty of the days before the sun honestly rather than explaining the difficulty away. What kind of light made the first three days, and by what movement the light made evening and morning, Augustine called beyond the reach of our senses. We cannot understand how it was, Augustine said, and yet we must believe. On the age of the world, Augustine rejected histories that claimed many thousands of years for the world, and called the documents of the histories plainly untrue. By Scripture's own count, Augustine held, not 6,000 years had yet passed since the world began.
