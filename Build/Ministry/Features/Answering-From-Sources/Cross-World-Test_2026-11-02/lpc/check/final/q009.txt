Some of our claims stand firm, and some we hold open. That each bishop held a theory of council power, in his own words, is solidly attested. Cyprian's formula stands at length in the preface to the council of 256. Cyprian states the same freedom of each bishop in his letters to Jubaianus and Magnus, and in the letter Cyprian and his council sent to Stephen. The evidence that the theories of Cyprian and Augustine shaped much of our life is much weaker. Both theories appear only in bishops' writing, and nothing shows either one at work among ordinary clergy. The lack of any sign among ordinary clergy is a limit of the search so far, not a proven absence. Whether Augustine was fair to Pelagius stays open, since we have only Augustine's side. Pelagius's own words do not survive among our sources.
