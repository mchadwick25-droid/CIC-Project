Augustine wrote his treatise on marriage against those who thought that praising virginity meant condemning marriage [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Of the third good, Augustine wrote that a marriage compact is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Separation does not make the marriage compact void [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
So long as her husband lives, Augustine taught, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine's teaching on the separated wife is a teaching about what marriage itself is and what marriage binds [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
