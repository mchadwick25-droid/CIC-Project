Record lpc.story.election-of-cyprian [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 248 to 257]
(1) How we came to see a new convert's election, against his own reluctance, as God's own judgment made visible
means: Pontius says God's judgment and the people's favour made Cyprian bishop, and later Cyprian named our suffrage and God's judgment side by side.
MUST NOT ASSERT: That the people's favour was how God's judgment became visible to us is our own reading (Inferential-Thin). Pontius section 5 pairs the two but does not say how they relate. Do not present that relation as Pontius's own statement. The same holds for any claim that for Pontius the two were not in tension. It also holds for the line about what we saw in him in Cyprian.; a claim that it was a vote with a candidacy and a count; a claim that any witness besides Pontius confirms that Cyprian was a neophyte; a claim that Pontius, writing in praise, is a neutral witness
NOT KNOWN: The specific claim that Cyprian was a neophyte rests on Pontius alone; no other witness of ours confirms or corrects it, and we do not claim more certainty about it than that one source supports. Pontius is writing in praise, and he says so himself.

Record lpc.limit.ordinary-interior-life [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 246 to 430]
(1) We can tell you what was decided about a person, and how carefully, and by whom.
(2) We cannot tell you what an ordinary believer actually felt walking up to the altar, or standing at the font, or sitting in the congregation on an ordinary week.
(3) Almost never, that is.
(4) There is one real exception, and it is not really ordinary.
(5) Augustine's own Confessions describes his own baptism at Milan, before he was a bishop, in his own first person -- "how greatly did I weep in Thy hymns and canticles." But he wrote that down years afterward, already a bishop, about a conversion he examined at book length.
(6) Both of our own voices are otherwise bishops.
(7) What comes down to us is mostly what a bishop saw and decided, not what an unremarkable person in front of him was feeling.
(8) Ask us what was decided, and we will not run short.
(9) Ask us what an ordinary believer felt from inside, and that -- with this one exception -- is still not ours to give you.
(10) We show what bishops ruled but almost never what plain people felt, save the tears Augustine shed at his baptism, told long after.
means: We show what bishops ruled but almost never what plain people felt, save the tears Augustine shed at his baptism, told long after.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a claim that Augustine's account of his baptism shows ordinary believers' experience; a claim that an ordinary believer's own account survives; a claim about what believers felt at the font or in the congregation

Record lpc.story.the-death-of-cyprian [formation confidence: Widely Accepted, limited to the years 258 to 258]
(1) How two early texts tell the death of our great bishop Cyprian, and where they differ
means: Cyprian died on 14 September, and Pontius and the Acta agree on the core but differ on who tied the cloths.
MUST NOT ASSERT: this account must not be presented as historical fact that the centurion's hand was strengthened from above -- we report only that this is how Pontius told it; the two texts differ on who tied the cloths over Cyprian's eyes -- we state the difference and do not resolve it; a claim that the centurion's hand was strengthened from above, which is only how Pontius tells it; a claim that the two texts agree on who tied the cloths; a claim that the Acta is a court transcript; a claim that any spectator's own account survives
NOT KNOWN: What cannot be told is what any one person in the crowd made of it, because no spectator left an account of his own. What the crowd said as a body, the Acta records: Et nos cum ipso decollemur.
