Augustine traced two of our practices back to the apostles, though none of the apostles wrote either practice down [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].
The two practices are baptizing infants and refusing to rebaptize someone already baptized among heretics [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].
For the refusal to rebaptize, Augustine said no one could find a later age that had invented the custom [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Baptizing infants, Augustine said, was held everywhere and instituted by no council [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine held that a custom so old and so universal, with no other explanation for its origin, is rightly believed to come from the apostles [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Augustine names the line of Roman bishops from Peter down to the man sitting then, and Cyprian states the principle that carries the line forward [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Neither Augustine nor Cyprian traces catechesis, the road back, or the teaching before the water to the apostles by name [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]].
We also hold that the office of bishop is one, and each bishop holds his part of the office for the whole [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]].
Cyprian argues that the office of bishop is one from what the Lord said to Peter, as an argument from scripture and not as a second named succession [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]].
