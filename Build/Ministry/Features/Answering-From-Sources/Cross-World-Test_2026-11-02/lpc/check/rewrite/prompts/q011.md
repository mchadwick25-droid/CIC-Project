Record lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 413 to 430]
(1) Augustine took up how to read the days of Genesis directly, in City of God.
(2) Ordinary days have morning and evening only by the sun's own rising and setting.
(3) But Genesis reports the first three days before the sun existed at all, made only on the fourth day.
(4) He named the difficulty honestly rather than explaining it away.
(5) He wrote that what kind of light made those first days, and by what movement it made evening and morning, is beyond the reach of our senses.
(6) We cannot understand how it was, he wrote, and yet we must believe it.
(7) Elsewhere in the same work he took up a related question.
(8) Some histories claimed many thousands of years for the world or the human race.
(9) He called those documents "highly mendacious," and held instead that by Scripture's own reckoning, not 6,000 years had yet passed since the world began.
(10) Augustine took up how to read the days of Genesis directly, in City of God.
(11) Ordinary days have morning and evening only by the rising and setting of the sun.
(12) But Genesis reports the first three days before the sun existed, made only on the fourth day.
(13) Augustine said the days before the sun are past our senses, and that by Scripture's count less than 6,000 years had passed.
