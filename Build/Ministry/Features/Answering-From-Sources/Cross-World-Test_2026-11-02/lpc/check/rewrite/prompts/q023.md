(1) The Decian Persecution and the *libelli* System (250) The Decian persecution was the first empire-wide persecution to be enforced in a planned way.
(2) It worked through certificates recording that the holder had sacrificed.
(3) It did not mainly demand that Christians give up their faith.
(4) It demanded a recorded act of compliance.
(5) A person could get one by performing the sacrifice, or by paying to have it recorded.
(6) This is documented in Cyprian's crisis correspondence, which carries the higher citation grade (A), and in his De Lapsis, which carries the lower grade (B).
(7) It was felt less as an attack from outside than as a table emptied one certificate at a time.
(8) The demand reached each person alone.
(9) It sorted the congregation into those who had stood and those who had not.
(10) Both groups still belonged, still in the room.
(11) Cyprian wrote that when the flock is wounded, it is the shepherd who is wounded most.
(12) This force made common the group that the whole penitential system exists to deal with.
(13) In one year, many lapsed and many confessors stood in one congregation.
(14) The certificate was a new way to fall.

(16) Tertullian, in On Modesty, already tells of sinners who ask imprisoned martyrs for peace.
(17) So the force produced two things at once.
(18) One was the ongoing contest over members who had failed, and the discipline for restoring them.

(20) It also sharpened the bishop's answerability, which is sharpest when his people fail.
