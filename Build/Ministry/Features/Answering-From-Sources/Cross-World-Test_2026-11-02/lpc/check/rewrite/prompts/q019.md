Record lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 246 to 258]
(1) In our earliest years, those who had confessed Christ and suffered for it made a claim of their own.
(2) Their suffering, they held, gave them standing to ask peace for some who had failed the test they passed.
(3) We took that claim in earnest.
(4) Their suffering was real, and so was what it carried.
(5) And it still had to be answered by something steadier than one man's word, however real his suffering had been: a name set down, then examined before the whole congregation and with its judgment.
(6) We hold both as real: the claim that pressed hard enough to demand a hearing, and the process that had to govern what the hearing decided.
(7) We have never found the place where these become one settled rule, and we do not expect to.
(8) In our earliest years, a confessor's suffering carried a claim of its own.
(9) Those who had held firm under questioning, and suffered for it, asked that some who had failed the same test be given peace.
(10) We took that claim in earnest.
(11) It was not a pretense, and it was not treated as nothing.
(12) And yet the road back is examined, weighed, and walked in the open, under one office's care.
(13) It is not handed out on anyone's certificate, however real their suffering was.
(14) We hold both convictions as genuine.
(15) One is the process that must govern the return.
(16) The other is the claim that pressed hard enough, from inside our own life, to require a process at all.
(17) Confessors said they could ask peace for the lapsed, the church's rules answered them, and the two never became one rule.
