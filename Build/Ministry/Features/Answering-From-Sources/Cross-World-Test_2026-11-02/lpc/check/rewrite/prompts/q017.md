QUESTION
What evidence connects your rituals to the earliest followers, rather than later invention?

Record lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 391 to 430]
(1) Augustine traced two of our practices back to the apostles in plain words.
(2) He would not rebaptize someone who had already been baptized among heretics.
(3) That custom, he said, no one could find to have been invented by a later age.
(4) It was "rightly believed to have been handed down from the apostles." He said the same of baptizing infants.
(5) It was held everywhere and instituted by no council.
(6) It too was "rightly held to have been handed down by apostolical authority." Both rest on the same reasoning.
(7) A custom this old and this universal, with no other explanation for its origin, is rightly believed to have come from the apostles.
(8) Augustine says plainly that two of our practices came down from the apostles.
(9) The first is not rebaptizing someone baptized among heretics.
(10) No one could find that this custom began later.
(11) It "is rightly believed to have been handed down from the apostles." Augustine held that infant baptism and no rebaptism came from the apostles, though none of them wrote it.
means: Augustine held that infant baptism and no rebaptism came from the apostles, though none of them wrote it.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a claim that a written apostolic text gives either practice; a claim that Augustine traces catechesis or the road back to the apostles by name; a claim that Cyprian shared Augustine's test of apostolic origin

Record lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended [formation confidence: Documented, limited to the years 246 to 430]
(1) How do we know our own practices go back to the apostles, and are not later inventions?
(2) I will not stretch the truth just to answer that well.
(3) Two of our practices, infant baptism and the refusal to rebaptize, Augustine traces to the apostles by name.
(4) The office of bishop is traced too.
(5) Augustine names the line of Roman bishops from Peter down to the man sitting now.
(6) Cyprian states the principle that carries that line forward.
(7) Neither of them traces catechesis, the road back, or the teaching before the water to the apostles by name.
(8) That gap is real.
(9) One more thing is not a traced lineage either.
(10) We believe the office of bishop is one.
(11) Each bishop holds his part of it for the whole.
(12) Cyprian argues that from what the Lord said to Peter.
(13) It is an argument from scripture, not a second named succession.
(14) Augustine ties infant baptism and the bishops to the apostles, but not teaching or the road back.
means: Augustine ties infant baptism and the bishops to the apostles, but not teaching or the road back.
MUST NOT ASSERT: a claim that catechesis, the road back or teaching before the water is traced to the apostles; a claim that the oneness of the episcopate is a traced lineage
