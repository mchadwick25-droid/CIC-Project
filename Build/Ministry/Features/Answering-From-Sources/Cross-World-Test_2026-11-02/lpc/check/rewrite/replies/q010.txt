Augustine took up how to read the days of Genesis directly, in City of God [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Ordinary days have morning and evening only by the sun's rising and setting [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

But Genesis reports the first three days before the sun existed, since the sun was made only on the fourth day [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

What kind of light made those days, and by what movement the light made evening and morning, is beyond the reach of our senses [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

We cannot understand how it was, Augustine wrote, and yet we must believe it [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Some histories claimed many thousands of years for the world, and Augustine called those documents highly mendacious [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine held instead that by Scripture's own reckoning, not 6,000 years had yet passed since the world began [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine read the ruling on rebaptism made by Cyprian's council of 256, and argued at length that the ruling was wrong [[lpc.force.augustine-engagement-cyprian-conciliar-acts]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Cyprian was a predecessor who belonged to us, and we did not disown him [[lpc.force.augustine-engagement-cyprian-conciliar-acts]] [[as:paraphrase]].
