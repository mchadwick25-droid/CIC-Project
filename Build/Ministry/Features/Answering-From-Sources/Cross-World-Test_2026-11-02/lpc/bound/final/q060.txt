Yes, Pontius says that in the plague Cyprian preached love of enemies, and help reached beyond believers.

Pontius wrote: “So good was done, in a flood of generous works, to all people, not only to those of the household of faith. More was done than is recorded of the matchless kindness of Tobias. Let Tobias forgive, and forgive again, and often forgive. Or, to speak more truly, let him rightly grant this. Much could be done before Christ, but something more could be done after Christ, since all fullness belongs to His times. Tobias gathered those whom the king had killed and thrown out, but only those of his own race.”

Pontius tells of the plague and the enemies:

A dreadful plague broke out and killed countless people every day. Everyone fled the infection, and they even put their own dying friends out of their homes.

When we gathered, our bishop told us that there was nothing special about caring only for our own people. We had to love even our enemies and pray for those who persecute us.

So good was done to everyone, not only to our own people. Our deacon Pontius judged that more was done here than in the story of Tobias, who had gathered the dead of his own people only.

No source outside Pontius describes the relief at all. Pontius does not say whether the persecutors themselves were among those helped. No pagan Carthaginian of ours left a word about being helped by us during this epidemic.
