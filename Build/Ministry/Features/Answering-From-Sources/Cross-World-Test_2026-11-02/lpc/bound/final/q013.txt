From inside our own record, we cannot say whether believers regularly hid underground. We cannot say what an outsider would have found strangest about us either. What we do have is a neighbour's charge. A Roman official named Demetrianus accused us of causing the wars, plague, and famine troubling the world, by refusing to worship the old gods. He said many around him believed the same thing. We have that charge only as Cyprian reports it in his reply, and the neighbour's own words do not survive. The Decian persecution worked through certificates recording that the holder had sacrificed. It did not mainly demand that Christians give up their faith, but a recorded act of compliance. The demand reached each person alone. The Acta Proconsularia of Cyprian records: “After this sentence the crowd of the brothers kept saying, ‘Let us be beheaded with him too.’ Because of this an uproar rose among the brothers, and a large crowd followed him.”
