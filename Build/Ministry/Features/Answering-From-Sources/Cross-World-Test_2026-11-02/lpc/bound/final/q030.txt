What we hold on the end of the lost is Augustine's teaching that the punishment of the lost has no end.

Many around Augustine found the endless punishment hard to bear, and Augustine said they softened everything that seemed hard, without arguing against Scripture.

Augustine wrote: “So it is in vain that some, indeed very many, lament the eternal punishment and the endless, unbroken torments of the lost. They say they do not believe it will be so. They do not set themselves directly against Holy Scripture. But, prompted by their own feelings, they soften everything that seems hard. They give a milder sense to sayings they think were meant more to frighten than to be taken as literally true.”

Augustine allowed the doubters to suppose, if the thought gave them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are at times eased. Augustine would not let the doubters say that the punishment ends. In the next chapter Augustine wrote that the death of the wicked will abide for ever.
