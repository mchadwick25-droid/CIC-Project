Among us, Cyprian became bishop when the people of Carthage chose him by acclamation, over the recorded opposition of five presbyters. Cyprian was a trained public speaker who had become a Christian in middle life, roughly two or three years before.

Cyprian later wrote that certain presbyters kept up an old venom against his office as bishop. Cyprian said that venom was aimed at his people's choice and God's judgment.

Pontius writes in praise of Cyprian, and says so himself. The claim that Cyprian was new to the faith when chosen rests on Pontius alone, and no other witness of ours confirms it. The pattern came back with Augustine, who was seized at Hippo in 391 and made a presbyter against his wishes.

Pontius tells how Cyprian came to the office:

Our bishop Cyprian was executed by the sword, and afterward his deacon, Pontius, wrote the story of his life. There was too much to tell, he said, so he chose a single fact that was proof enough of the good Cyprian did.

In 248 Cyprian was chosen bishop of Carthage "by the judgment of God and the favour of the people." Yet he had only recently been baptized, and people considered him a novice, a beginner in the faith.

When the whole people rushed forward to honour him, he humbly stepped back and gave way to older men. He considered himself unworthy, but a crowd of believers still pressed at the doors of the house where he was.

Pontius named two things side by side, the judgment of God and the favour of the people. In our own reading, the people's favour was the way we saw God's judgment at work.

By 251 certain presbyters were renewing their old attack on him, so Cyprian wrote to us, the people. These men had long hated him as bishop, he wrote, and to him that meant they hated our choice and God's judgment as well.

In 257 Cyprian and his fellow bishops wrote to the clergy and people in Spain. A bishop, they wrote, should be chosen in the presence of the people, who knew each man's life best. That was how Sabinus had been made bishop there. The whole community of believers gave their voice for him, and the assembled bishops gave their judgment.
