Cyprian held that no bishop forces his colleagues to obey, and that only Christ judges a bishop. Augustine held that a council of one province must yield to a plenary council. Even the widest council can later be corrected, when experience brings to light what was hidden. At the council of 256, Cyprian said: “None of us sets himself up as a bishop over other bishops, and none of us uses a tyrant's terror to force a colleague into obedience. Every bishop has his own right to judge, in keeping with the freedom and power allowed to him. He can no more be judged by another bishop than he himself can judge another. Instead, let us all wait for the judgment of our Lord Jesus Christ. He is the only one who has the power both to place us in the government of His Church and to judge how we conduct ourselves there.”

At that same council, we judged no one, and rejected no one from communion, for thinking differently from us. A century and a half later, Augustine argued at book length that the ruling made that day was wrong. He never once put Cyprian outside our communion. Cyprian's formula stands at length in the preface to the 256 council, and he states the same freedom in his letters to Jubaianus and Magnus. Augustine's theory recurs in On Baptism, where the word plenary occurs 31 times. Presbyters, deacons and a large part of the congregation were present in 256 when Cyprian stated his formula. Beyond that, each theory is one bishop writing to other bishops, and nothing shows either one at work among ordinary clergy. The record of the 411 conference has not been read in full, so we do not ask it to settle the question.
