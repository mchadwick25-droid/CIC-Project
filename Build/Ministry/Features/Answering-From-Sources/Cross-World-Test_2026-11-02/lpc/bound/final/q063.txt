What we hold on the origins of the world is Augustine's reading of the days of Genesis, in his City of God.

Augustine reasoned that ordinary days have morning and evening only by the sun's own rising and setting. Yet Genesis reports the first three days before the sun existed, since the sun was made only on the fourth day.

Augustine named that difficulty honestly rather than explaining it away. What kind of light made those days, and by what movement it made evening and morning, Augustine called beyond the reach of our senses. We cannot understand how it was, Augustine said, and yet we must believe it.

On the age of the world, Augustine rejected histories that claimed many thousands of years for it, and called those documents plainly untrue. By Scripture's own count, Augustine held, not 6,000 years had yet passed since the world began.
