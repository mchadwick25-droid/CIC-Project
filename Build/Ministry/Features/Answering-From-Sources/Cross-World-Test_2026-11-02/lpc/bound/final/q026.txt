What we never settled was whether a baptism given outside our one church counts at all. We answered that question twice, in two different ways, a century and a half apart. Cyprian's council said such a baptism did not count, while Augustine said it was given, though it did no good there.

Augustine argued at book length that the ruling of Cyprian's council was wrong. Yet Augustine never put Cyprian outside our communion. We do not pretend that being answerable for someone makes this question easy.

 The confessors held that their suffering gave them standing to ask peace for the lapsed. The church answered that a name must be set down, then examined before the whole congregation. We hold both as real, and we have never found the place where they become one settled rule.
