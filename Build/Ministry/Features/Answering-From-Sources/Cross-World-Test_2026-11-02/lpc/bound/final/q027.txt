We held a person who had failed as one of our own, to be taken back and not shut out for good. The lapsed were our own people, not strangers and not enemies, and they sat where we sit.

The lapsed came back by a public road, walked in steps under the bishop. The road had an order, it took time, and everyone could see it.

Our letters tell of Celerinus and Lucian:

Celerinus, who had confessed Christ, wrote to Lucian, another confessor, who was in prison. His sister had fallen away from Christ, and it seemed plain that she had sacrificed. He asked Lucian to grieve with him, for to him she was dead in spirit.

He also asked the martyrs to win pardon for two women, Numeria and Candida, who had repented.

Lucian granted that pardon, called peace, in the name of the martyr Paulus and the other martyrs. Each case was to be set before the bishop and confession made, and then the women would have peace.

Taking back the failed was central in Cyprian's time, and our record places that concern in his phase alone. Augustine held that where the ministers stay at their posts, the dying are baptized or reconciled to the Church.
