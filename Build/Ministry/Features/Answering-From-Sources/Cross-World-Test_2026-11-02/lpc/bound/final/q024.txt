We held marriage to be a real good, and Augustine wrote a whole treatise to defend it. He wrote it against those who thought that praising virginity meant condemning marriage.

Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament. Of that third good he wrote that a marriage compact is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament. Separation does not make that compact void.

So long as her husband lives, Augustine taught, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery. That is a teaching about what marriage itself is and what it binds.
