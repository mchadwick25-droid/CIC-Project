In today's English, The Acta Proconsularia of Cyprian says: “After this sentence the crowd of the brothers kept saying, ‘Let us be beheaded with him too.’ Because of this an uproar rose among the brothers, and a large crowd followed him.”

No spectator left an account of his own, so what any one person in the crowd made of the day cannot be told. Pontius and the Acta agree on the core of the death, and the two texts differ on who tied the cloths over Cyprian's eyes.

Two early texts tell the death of our great bishop Cyprian:

Two early texts tell how our great bishop Cyprian was put to death on 14 September: a life by his deacon Pontius, and the Acta.

On his last night all his people kept watch at the door of the house where he was held.

Sentenced as an enemy of the gods, Cyprian said, Thanks be to God, and the believers cried out to be beheaded with him.

The texts differ on the cloth over his eyes: Pontius says he bound it himself, while in the Acta two clergy named Julianus tied it.

And so the blessed Cyprian suffered martyrdom.
