For us, Jesus being God belongs with the Trinity in one confession: we confess Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Then we confess the Holy Ghost as well, and that completes the Trinity that makes up the one Godhead [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]].

That confession did not go unchallenged [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. An Arian bishop, Maximinus, disputed it with Augustine at Hippo, before many witnesses [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]].

On what a council can do, our two bishops spoke differently. At the council of 256, Cyprian stated the rule that no bishop stands over another and each answers to Christ [[lpc.term.bishop-of-bishops]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine held that small councils yield to wider ones, and that even the widest can later be corrected [[lpc.term.plenary-council]] [[as:paraphrase]].
