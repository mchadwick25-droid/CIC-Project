We held that a newborn comes with no sins of his own, yet has taken on the contagion of the ancient death, being born after the flesh according to Adam [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For that reason we would not make a newborn wait for baptism [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian and his colleagues met in council, sixty-six in number, and judged that God's mercy and grace are not to be refused to any one born of man [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian and his colleagues wrote to Fidus: [[quote:lpc.quote.not-his-own-sins-but-the-sins-of-another]]
Augustine went on to the hard end of this [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. He wrote that in baptism infants die only to original sin [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Of infants who leave the body unbaptized, Augustine wrote that they will be involved in the mildest condemnation of all [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The mildest condemnation is still condemnation [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Whoever teaches that they will not be condemned, Augustine said, greatly deceives both himself and others [[lpc.witness.infants-and-the-sin-of-another]] [[as:paraphrase]].
