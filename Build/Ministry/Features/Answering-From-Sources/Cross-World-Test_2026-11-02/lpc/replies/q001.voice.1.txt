No one among us names an eyewitness of the risen Jesus, or claims a living memory passed hand to hand back to one [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. What reached us is testimony in writing, and none of us knew the men who wrote it [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine pointed to the world itself [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Obscure, unlearned men persuaded the whole world of something incredible, by testifying in writing that they had seen it themselves [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We reason from their testimony as it was handed on to us, and we argue for the resurrection's credibility, not only confess it [[lpc.limit.no-chain-of-witnesses]] [[as:paraphrase]]. For us, Jesus being God and the Trinity belong to one confession [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We confess Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord, and then the Holy Ghost, which completes the Trinity of the one Godhead [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That confession did not go unchallenged, since an Arian bishop, Maximinus, disputed it with Augustine at Hippo before many witnesses [[lpc.witness.the-only-son-and-the-trinity]] [[as:paraphrase]].
