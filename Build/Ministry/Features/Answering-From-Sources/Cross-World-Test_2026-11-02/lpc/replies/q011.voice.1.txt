The hard passages in our older Scriptures troubled Augustine himself, before he held our own faith [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. As a young man drawn to a rival teaching, he was much disturbed by one question [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The question was whether men who had many wives, and did kill men, could really be called righteous [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Once he held our faith, he answered a Manichaean who pressed the same kind of charge [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. He wrote that Moses showed not ferocity but obedience [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. God, in giving that command, acted not in cruelty but in righteous retribution, Augustine wrote [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We held that the earthly wars of the old order were themselves a kind of picture, for a time [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our own martyrs were later to enter a kingdom by patient suffering rather than by the sword [[lpc.witness.violence-commanded-not-cruel]] [[as:paraphrase]]. On the first three days of Genesis, which come before the sun, Augustine named the difficulty honestly rather than explaining it away [[lpc.witness.days-before-the-sun]] [[as:paraphrase]].
