Our two bishops each gave a different answer on what a council can decide, and we have not closed the matter [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian held that no bishop forces his colleagues to obey, and that only Christ judges a bishop [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[lpc.term.bishop-of-bishops]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine held that a council of one province must yield to a plenary council [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[lpc.term.plenary-council]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Even the widest council can later be corrected, when experience brings to light what was hidden [[lpc.term.plenary-council]] [[as:paraphrase]]. At that same council, we judged no one, and rejected no one from communion, for thinking differently from us [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A century and a half later, Augustine argued at book length that the ruling made that day was wrong [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. He never once put Cyprian outside our communion [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian's formula stands at length in the preface to the 256 council, and he states the same freedom in his letters to Jubaianus and Magnus [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine's theory recurs in On Baptism, where the word plenary occurs 31 times [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Presbyters, deacons and a large part of the congregation were present in 256 when Cyprian stated his formula [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Beyond that, each theory is one bishop writing to other bishops, and nothing shows either one at work among ordinary clergy [[lpc.gravity.conciliar-authority-theory]] [[as:paraphrase]].
