What we never settled was whether a baptism given outside our one church counts at all [[lpc.term.heresy]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We answered that question twice, in two different ways, a century and a half apart [[lpc.term.heresy]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian's council said such a baptism did not count, while Augustine said it was given, though it did no good there [[lpc.term.heresy]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine argued at book length that the ruling of Cyprian's council was wrong [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Yet Augustine never put Cyprian outside our communion [[lpc.witness.communion-over-separation]] [[lpc.witness.answerability-as-ground]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We do not pretend that being answerable for someone makes this question easy [[lpc.witness.answerability-as-ground]] [[as:paraphrase]].

A second tension never became one rule. The confessors held that their suffering gave them standing to ask peace for the lapsed [[lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace]] [[lpc.term.confessor]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The church answered that a name must be set down, then examined before the whole congregation [[lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We hold both as real, and we have never found the place where they become one settled rule [[lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace]] [[as:paraphrase]].
