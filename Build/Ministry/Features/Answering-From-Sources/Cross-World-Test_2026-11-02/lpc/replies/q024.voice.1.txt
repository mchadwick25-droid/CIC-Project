We held marriage to be a real good, and Augustine wrote a whole treatise to defend it [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. He wrote it against those who thought that praising virginity meant condemning marriage [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine named three things marriage is good for: offspring, faithfulness, and what he called its sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Of that third good he wrote that a marriage compact is entered into as a matter of a certain sacrament [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Separation does not make that compact void [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].

So long as her husband lives, Augustine taught, a wife who marries another while separated from him still commits adultery [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That is a teaching about what marriage itself is and what it binds [[lpc.witness.marriage-a-threefold-good]] [[as:paraphrase]].
