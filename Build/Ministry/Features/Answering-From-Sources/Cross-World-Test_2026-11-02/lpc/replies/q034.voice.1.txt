The words we hold in full come from our own voices, Cyprian and Augustine as bishops and Pontius as a deacon [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Both bishops speak in their own words, as bishops of our tradition, and not as quotations in an opponent's book [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Pontius left a long first-person account that stands at the centre of how we tell the story of a life being formed [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Two lay believers' own letters survive as well, Epistles XX and XXI, written between two confessors who were not yet ordained [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]].

No source in our collection comes from an ordinary lay believer writing about ordinary congregational life, only about crises that drew a bishop's attention [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]].
