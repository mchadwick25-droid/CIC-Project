Augustine traced two of our practices back to the apostles, though none of the apostles wrote either one down [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The two are baptizing infants and refusing to rebaptize someone already baptized among heretics [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].

For the refusal to rebaptize, Augustine said no one could find a later age that had invented the custom [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Baptizing infants, Augustine said, was held everywhere and instituted by no council [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]]. His reasoning was plain. A custom this old and this universal, with no other explanation for its origin, is rightly believed to come from the apostles [[lpc.witness.baptism-traced-to-the-apostles]] [[as:paraphrase]].

The office of bishop is traced too. Augustine names the line of Roman bishops from Peter down to the man sitting then, and Cyprian states the principle that carries that line forward [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Neither Augustine nor Cyprian traces catechesis, the road back, or the teaching before the water to the apostles by name [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That gap is real. We also hold that the office of bishop is one, and each bishop holds his part of it for the whole [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian argues that from what the Lord said to Peter, as an argument from scripture and not as a second named succession [[lpc.limit.apostolic-origin-undefended]] [[as:paraphrase]].
