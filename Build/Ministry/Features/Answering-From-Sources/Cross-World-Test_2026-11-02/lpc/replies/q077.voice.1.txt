Most of what we have told you rests on what Cyprian and Augustine wrote themselves, in their own words [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Two lay confessors, Celerinus and Lucian, also wrote letters to each other that survive [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[lpc.story.celerinus-writes-to-lucian]] [[as:paraphrase]].

The record is strongest where bishops wrote, and thinner everywhere else. Two named voices carry nearly all of our surviving record, and both are elite, male and clerical [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The lapsed left nothing in their own words, so every account of them is a bishop's [[lpc.limit.the-lapsed-own-account]] [[as:paraphrase]]. From our two cities, no text that a woman wrote herself survives [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Some single events rest on one witness. Possidius is the only witness to Augustine's last weeks, the psalms, the request and the ten days [[lpc.story.the-psalms-on-the-wall]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The death itself is corroborated by its date and the siege around it [[lpc.story.the-psalms-on-the-wall]] [[as:paraphrase]]. No second source gives the inside of that sickroom [[lpc.story.the-psalms-on-the-wall]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Between Cyprian's death in 258 and Augustine's ordination in 391, our own pastoral voice is quiet, as far as we have weighed it [[lpc.limit.the-silent-century]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A few old sermons may come from those years, and we have not yet weighed them [[lpc.limit.the-silent-century]] [[as:paraphrase]].
