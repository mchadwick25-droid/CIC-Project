Among us, Cyprian became bishop when the people of Carthage chose him by acclamation, over the recorded opposition of five presbyters [[lpc.force.congregational-acclamation-overriding-preference]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian was a trained public speaker who had become a Christian in middle life, roughly two or three years before [[lpc.force.congregational-acclamation-overriding-preference]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Cyprian later wrote that certain presbyters kept up an old venom against his office as bishop [[lpc.force.congregational-acclamation-overriding-preference]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian said that venom was aimed at his people's choice and God's judgment [[lpc.force.congregational-acclamation-overriding-preference]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Pontius writes in praise of Cyprian, and says so himself [[lpc.story.election-of-cyprian]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The claim that Cyprian was new to the faith when chosen rests on Pontius alone, and no other witness of ours confirms it [[lpc.story.election-of-cyprian]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The pattern came back with Augustine, who was seized at Hippo in 391 and made a presbyter against his wishes [[lpc.force.congregational-acclamation-overriding-preference]] [[as:paraphrase]].
