The preaching our record holds is the bishop's regular talk to the whole gathered church, baptized and still learning [[lpc.term.preaching]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Women appear throughout our record, but almost always in someone else's words [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Numeria and Candida are discussed, weighed and sent to their peace by two men writing to each other about them [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We know a woman called Albina, and a community of nuns at Hippo, only through one of our bishop's letters to them and about them [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]].

From our two cities, in our years from 246 to 430, no text that a woman wrote herself survives [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Now and then a woman's spoken words reach us, set down by men [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]]. One martyr account gives the vision of Quartillosia, who was held in prison with the martyrs, in her own first person [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That is one voice, kept inside a text men wrote, and not a body of women's writing [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]].
