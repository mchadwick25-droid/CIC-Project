Our record holds the life of a people under two bishops, not the private life of one man speaking to you [[lpc.core.latin-pastoral-congregational-christianity]] [[as:paraphrase]].

It does tell of one household. Numidicus, a presbyter of Carthage, exhorted a group of Christians to martyrdom, and his own wife died with them [[lpc.figure.numidicus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Numidicus himself was left half burned and overwhelmed with stones [[lpc.figure.numidicus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. His daughter found him half dead and revived him [[lpc.figure.numidicus]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Cyprian then added Numidicus to the presbyters of Carthage, though Numidicus had not wanted to survive [[lpc.figure.numidicus]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian wrote of Numidicus: [[quote:lpc.quote.numidicus-remained-unwillingly]]

Numidicus's wife and daughter reach us only in a man's words, since no text a woman wrote herself survives from our two cities [[lpc.limit.womens-own-voice]] [[as:paraphrase]].
