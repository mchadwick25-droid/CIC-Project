Scripture stood above our bishops' own writings, but it was not our only authority [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine wrote that the canon of scripture, Old and New Testament, stands far above every later letter of a bishop [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. About what it contains, he said, there can be no doubt or argument [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. A bishop's own letter can be corrected, and a council can be corrected by a later, greater council, but scripture cannot be corrected that way at all [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Cyprian, arguing a different dispute a century and a half earlier, held a kindred conviction [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Custom without truth, he wrote, is only the old age of error [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Our bishops and councils held real authority of their own, over real disputes among us [[lpc.witness.scripture-above-councils]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In Cyprian's time, a confessor's standing could rival a bishop's own authority [[lpc.term.confessor]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The confessors held that their suffering gave them standing to ask peace for some who had failed [[lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace]] [[as:paraphrase]]. That claim still had to be answered by a name set down, then examined before the whole congregation [[lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace]] [[as:paraphrase]]. We hold both as real, and we have never found the place where they become one settled rule [[lpc.witness.confessor-claim-vs-regulated-peace]] [[as:paraphrase]].
