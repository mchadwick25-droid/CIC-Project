We held a person who had failed as one of our own, to be taken back and not shut out for good [[lpc.gravity.penitential-discipline]] [[lpc.term.the-lapsed]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The lapsed were our own people, not strangers and not enemies, and they sat where we sit [[lpc.term.the-lapsed]] [[as:paraphrase]].

The lapsed came back by a public road, walked in steps under the bishop [[lpc.term.reconciliation-penitential-discipline]] [[as:paraphrase]]. The road had an order, it took time, and everyone could see it [[lpc.term.reconciliation-penitential-discipline]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Taking back the failed was central in Cyprian's time, and our record places that concern in his phase alone [[lpc.gravity.penitential-discipline]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In 428 or 429 Augustine told Honoratus, a bishop in Mauritania, that people who depend on their ministers must not be left in a time of danger [[lpc.witness.the-dying-and-the-pastors-post]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine held that where the ministers stay at their posts, the dying are baptized or reconciled to the Church [[lpc.witness.the-dying-and-the-pastors-post]] [[as:paraphrase]].
