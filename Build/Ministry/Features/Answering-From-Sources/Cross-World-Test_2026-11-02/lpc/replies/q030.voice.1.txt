What we hold on the end of the lost is Augustine's teaching that the punishment of the lost has no end [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. After the final judgment, Augustine wrote, two kingdoms stand, and the life of one and the death of the other are both without end [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Among the lost there are degrees of suffering, one being more endurably miserable than another [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Many around Augustine found the endless punishment hard to bear, and Augustine said they softened everything that seemed hard, without arguing against Scripture [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].

Augustine wrote: [[quote:lpc.quote.very-many-make-moan-over-the-eternal-punishment]]

Augustine allowed the doubters to suppose, if the thought gave them pleasure, that the pains of the damned are at times eased [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. Augustine would not let the doubters say that the punishment ends [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. God's mercy, Augustine said, is shown in mitigating the torments or granting a respite, not in ending the eternal punishment [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]]. In the next chapter Augustine wrote that the death of the wicked will abide for ever [[lpc.witness.punishment-without-end]] [[as:paraphrase]].
