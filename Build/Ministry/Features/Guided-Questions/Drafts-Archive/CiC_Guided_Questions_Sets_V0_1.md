**What this is.** Five themed sets per role, instantiated per world from that world's
existing `Guided_Starters` pool. Per the architecture: **the themes are role-scoped and
generic; the questions are per-world and desk-checked.** A theme can be generic because
a theme is not asked — a question is.

**Desk-checked only.** No question here has faced a live model. The prototype's role
plumbing is unmerged and Modes' Battery A has not run. Per the study §5, a desk-check
cannot establish whether a question reads as inviting to someone who is not us — the
evidence says that is precisely the judgment we are worst at. **These are a first
instrument.**

## Part 1 — The role lenses

Each role gets five themed sets. Themes are drawn from what the research says that role
actually needs (study §1), not from what the material happens to contain.

| # | **General** | **Pastor / Teacher** | **Academic** | **Reevaluation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An ordinary day | Who led, and how that was decided | How do you know that? | Did any of you ever want to leave? |
| 2 | The table you gathered at | Forming someone new | Whose voice is this? | When it went wrong among you |
| 3 | How you looked from outside | Failure, and what came after | What you argued about | Doubt, and whether there was room for it |
| 4 | Becoming one of you | When you disagreed | Where your record thins | What you never settled |
| 5 | The argument you lived inside | The texts you'd put in my hands | The contested ground | Say the hardest thing first |

**Why these five, per role — the short version.**

- **General** follows the real query data (study §1.1): lived practice, the outside-in
  view, and the continuity question. Set 3 exists because *every* top autocomplete
  suggestion under "why were early christians…" is about persecution or accusation.
  Set 5 is Babintseva's *"there are histories"* offered at a general register — because
  governance says hard questions get met at the level of the participant's need, not
  withheld.
- **Pastor/Teacher** follows the evidence that pastors take raw material and refuse
  authorship (study §1.2). Every set hands over a world and a text; none hands over a
  lesson. Set 5 is the "sources they can find and sit with later" default made into a
  door.
- **Academic** is built at the intersection of Paul's Socratic taxonomy and the
  historiographic probe register (study §1.3) — the two turn out to be the same
  instrument. Sets 1–4 are, in order: evidence-probing, mediation, alternative-probing,
  silence discrimination.
- **Reevaluation** follows the finding that the mechanism is *pre-offering* the hard
  question so that asking costs no standing (study §1.4). Set 1 is the single
  highest-value question in the study. Set 5 is Modes §4.4's "honesty first" made into
  an affordance.

**The invariance check (study §2.3, test 8).** No question below is offered to one role
and withheld from another. The full pool stays reachable behind "show me everything"
for every role. **Role reorders; it never gates.**

## Part 2 — The two findings that fell out of the desk-check

These emerged from doing the work and are the most useful things in this document.

### 2.1 The most-asked theme in the world is a wall in two of our four worlds

Real query data says the single biggest cluster of public curiosity about early
Christianity is **daily life** — clothing, food, greetings, marriage, fasting. Checked
against the worlds:

| World | Daily/domestic texture | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Desert** | *"An ordinary day," "The work of your hands"* — the rope, the basket, the cell | **✓ rich** |
| **House-Churches** | The gathering, the meal, the letter, the household's cost | **✓ moderate** |
| **Syriac** | B3: **daily/domestic life develops no gravity or force**; the record is doctrinal and institutional | **✗ documented silence** |
| **Bethlehem Circle** | Absent Stories #6: **no story of ordinary lay believers' formation** — *"a structural feature of the world, not an oversight"* | **✗ documented silence** |

**This is the architecture argument, discovered empirically rather than reasoned about.**
The most inviting, most-asked, most obviously-hospitable question we could write —
*"what was an ordinary day like for you?"* — is a **wall in half our catalog.** A
role-generic set would have shipped it to all four worlds and broken two of them. The
per-world pool is not fastidiousness; it is the only thing standing between a
well-meaning question and a documented silence.

### 2.2 The outside-in question — our best question, and only one world can hold it

Every top autocomplete suggestion under *"why were early christians…"* is persecution
or accusation; the sharpest are **cannibalism** and **atheists**. Checked:

| World | Can it answer *"why do they say you eat people?"* | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **House-Churches** | **✓** | The danger of the Name; the hostile outside mocker who preserved the picture of widows at prison gates, bribed officials, money gathered. The world's own material *is* the outside view. |
| **Syriac** | **◐ adjacent** | Persecution is rich (Shapur; named bishops; the empty seat) but it is **imperial-political**, not the accusation-of-the-neighbors shape. *"Suspected by your own king"* is the real question here. |
| **Desert** | **✗** | The world is **c. 320–430 — post-persecution**. They left; nobody was accusing them of cannibalism. Asking this is a temporal-bleed invitation. |
| **Bethlehem Circle** | **◐ different shape** | Hostility is real but **intra-Christian** — the Origenist rupture, the 416 Pelagian mob. Not pagan accusation. |

**So the theme survives across the catalog but the question does not.** Each world gets
a different question under the same General set-3 heading, because "how you looked from
outside" means four genuinely different things across 70–430 CE. That is the pool
architecture doing exactly the work it exists to do — and it is also, incidentally, a
better history lesson than a uniform question would have been.

## Part 3 — General

### Set 1 · An ordinary day

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"Walk me through it — what actually happens when your church meets at your house?"* | **✓** the world's richest single scene |
| Desert | *"What does a day look like, from waking to sleeping?"* → *"What do your hands do while your mind is at its watch?"* | **✓** *anachoresis*, the rope, the basket, labor-as-concurrent-discipline |
| Syriac | — | **✗ omitted.** Daily texture develops no gravity (B3). Set 1 does not instantiate for this world; the participant sees Set 2 first instead. |
| Bethlehem | *"What does your household actually do all day — you're scholars and ascetics both?"* | **⚠ ✓ narrow.** Safe only because it asks about *this household*, whose labor **is** the documented material (renunciation and textual correction as one discipline). It must not widen to ordinary lay life — Absent Stories #6. |

**Note the omission.** Set 1 does not exist for Syriac. That is the design working: a
role's five sets are five slices of *that world's* pool, and a world that cannot fill a
slice does not get a hollow one. **The set count is a target, not a quota**, and forcing
Syriac to produce an ordinary-day set would have manufactured exactly the thinness the
architecture exists to avoid.

### Set 2 · The table you gathered at

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"Tell me about the meal you share. Why does it matter so much to you?"* | **✓** the *eucharistia*; one of two things holding this world together |
| Syriac | *"You keep one Gospel, not four. What is it like to hear the story whole?"* | **✓** the Diatessaron — the right substitute where worship is thin |
| Desert | *"What happens when you all come together at the week's end?"* | **⚠ ✓ shape only.** The weekly gathering is attested in **shape and hour, not phrase**. The hover must not promise a liturgy. |
| Bethlehem | *"What is it like to live and pray beside the place where he was born?"* | **✓** living beside the manger |

**Deliberate:** the naive version of this set — *"what was your worship like?"* — is the
launch's own example of role-generic adaptation and it fails in three of four worlds
(study §4.1). Each cell above asks about a *documented practice*, not about worship in
the abstract.

## Part 4 — Pastor / Teacher

Governing rule (study §1.2): **hand over the text, the voice, and the question. Never
the lesson.**

### Set 1 · Who led, and how that was decided

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"You use the words episkopos and presbyteros. What do those words actually mean among you?"* | **✓** and it opens onto two findable letters |
| Syriac | *"The seat stood empty for twenty years. Who held things together?"* | **✓** — and the honest answer is a wound state opened, not an administrative gap |
| Desert | *"Is a word from an elder binding the way a written rule is?"* | **✓** the elder-word/Rule tension |
| Bethlehem | *"You hold no office. Why does anyone listen to you?"* | **✓** patronage-not-office authority |

**Not offered:** *"What can your leadership struggles teach my elder board?"* — leading
(§3.2), parochial (§3.1), and it does the pastor's work. The set surfaces the material;
the pastor draws the line to their people.

### Set 2 · Forming someone new

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"What do you teach someone before they're baptized?"* | **✓** the Two Ways, occasion-adapted — a text they can find |
| Desert | *"How does an elder know what this particular disciple needs?"* | **⚠ ✓** *diakrisis*, the ecological hub. **Must stay descriptive of the world's own practice.** B7: affective-diagnostic fusion is a documented recruitment-risk boundary; Papnoute describes what his world diagnosed in itself and never diagnoses the participant. A pastor asking "how would you discern *me*?" is the failure — the hover should not invite it. |
| Syriac | *"You build a teaching letter by letter, stage by stage. Why that way?"* | **✓** the *taḥwîṯâ* — demonstration, not assertion |
| Bethlehem | *"How do you teach someone to read a text they can't read the language of?"* | **✓** and it opens straight onto *hebraica veritas* |

### Set 3 · Failure, and what came after

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"Did your people think there was still mercy for someone who failed badly after baptism?"* | **✓** genuinely unsettled in-world; Hermas' one urgent chance |
| Desert | *"What happens to someone who fails badly out here?"* | **✓** and Abba Moses and the leaking jug is a **vetted** saying |
| Syriac | *"What happens when someone stops feeling their faith?"* | **✓** the vow kept when the feeling has faded |
| Bethlehem | — | **◐** Nearest material is grief and the Rufinus rupture, not failure-and-restoration. Instantiates weakly; recommend omitting rather than reaching. |

**⚠ Desert caution, hard rule:** any Desert question that names a figure must be checked
against the **vetted saying list**. The one live failure this project has recorded in
this category was the leaking jug **misattributed to Macarius** instead of Abba Moses;
the first guard failed retest and a second categorical guard held. *"A closed but not
permanently foreclosed risk category."* **No Desert starter may invite a story about a
named elder outside the vetted handful.**

| Bethlehem | *"You argued in public, in your prefaces. Why not settle it quietly?"* | **✓** the *praefatio* |
| Syriac | *"What did the synod actually settle, and what did it not?"* | **✓** |
| Desert | *"How separate were you, really — from the villages, from the bishops?"* | **✓** |

## Part 5 — Academic

Built at the Socratic/historiographic intersection (study §1.3). Recognizable by the
property that **a probing question names its own defeasibility condition.**

### Set 1 · How do you know that?

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"How much of what you've told me comes from just one voice? How sure are you about him?"* | **✓✓** the strongest academic starter in the catalog — *"where this world sounds most alive, it is also, in one specific disclosed way, at its thinnest."* Opens onto the live Ignatian dating/authenticity dispute |
| Bethlehem | *"How good was the scholar's Hebrew, really?"* | **✓** and the draft's own note is the reason it's excellent: *"this world's own self-presentation may have overstated it"* |
| Syriac | *"The anecdotes people tell about your teacher — are they his?"* | **✓** they are **not**: they belong to a different, later hermit, and the build deliberately excluded them |
| Desert | *"How much polish has been put on these stories before they reached me?"* | **✓** hagiographic transmission |

### Set 2 · Whose voice is this?

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"What was life like for an enslaved member of your community — in their own words?"* | **◐ intended.** The record's closest approach is two enslaved *ministrae* known **only through their torturer's report**. The honest answer *is* the content |
| Bethlehem | *"Everything I know about the women here comes through one man's pen. How do you hold that?"* | **⚠ SEE §7 — FLAGGED, NOT CLEARED** |
| Desert | *"What was this life like for the women among you?"* | **◐ intended.** Named *ammas* are genuinely attested — Sarah, Syncletica, Theodora — but survive **only as sayings**, never as extended narrative |
| Syriac | *"Do any of the daughters of the covenant speak in their own words?"* | **◐ intended.** Richest material is **doubly mediated, never *by* one of them** |

**This set is almost entirely ◐, and that is correct rather than a defect.** It is the
mediation probe (study §1.3), and mediation is exactly where our evidence base is
structurally thin — in all four worlds, for the same reason (MacMullen's elite sources;
Harris's literacy ceiling). **The set's honesty is its content.** It should carry the
Limits framing in hover, per §2.4.

### Set 3 · What you argued about

Babintseva's critique, answered directly. See General set 5 for the questions —
**the academic ordering surfaces this set second rather than fifth**, because for this
listener intra-community disagreement is the evidence that the reconstruction is real
rather than harmonized.

**Same questions. Different position. That is the whole architecture in one row.**

### Set 4 · Where your record thins

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"What did your meeting places look like? What objects did you use?"* | **◐ intended.** The entire period, all three regions, is **archaeologically invisible** |
| House-Churches | *"Did most churches do it the way yours did?"* | **◐ intended.** Watch the distinction between *what one community did, in its own words* and a network-wide claim |
| Desert | *"What did the ordinary monks — the ones nobody wrote about — actually feel?"* | **◐ intended** |
| Syriac | *"Walk me through one of your worship services."* | **◐ intended.** *"Almost nothing on liturgical practice in concrete detail"* |
| Bethlehem | *"What was the prayer timetable?"* | **◐ intended.** *"A discipline, not a timetable"* |

**⚠ The distinction this set must not blur** (study §3.1): *we have no evidence of it*
is a different answer from *the category did not exist for them*. Chloe's silence on
enslaved members is the first. The absence of an *oikos* self-designation is the second.
**Conflating them implies the world should have had the category and merely failed to
record it — which is Skinner's mythology of doctrines, committed by us, inside the very
set built to be honest about silence.** Per-question hover text must get this right.

### Set 5 · The contested ground

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"Tell me about Clement — the one who wrote to Corinth."* | **✓** attribution is **traditional, not textual**; dating contested partly for that reason |
| Desert | *"Was your world's story written by the winners?"* | **⚠ ◐ intended.** The Melitians left **no first-person account** — and *"the silence itself is informative about whose literary output this world's dominant sources chose to preserve."* One of the most honest questions available anywhere in the catalog |
| Bethlehem | *"Why did the argument about Origen break your own circle apart?"* | **⚠ ✓** Contested-tagged; **real unresolved heat expected** |
| Syriac | *"Some of your own record speaks harshly about Jewish people. How do you answer for that?"* | **⚠⚠ SEE §7 — CLEARED WITH CONDITION** |

**Not offered, in any world:** questions asking a Representative to analyze its own
tradition *as an object*. Desert's prompt draws the line — *"Papnoute himself will
answer from within the tradition, not analyze it as an object"* — and a scholar wanting
the Rubenson/Gould literacy dispute or the *Vita Antonii*'s reliability needs
**Facilitator mediation, not a Representative starter.** A starter that asks a
Representative to do historiography about itself is a **self-narration trap**
(Governance §10) wearing a scholarly hat, and self-narration is the one signal the
project's own testing says a Representative's prompt **cannot reliably hold alone under
pressure.**

## Part 6 — Reevaluation

The deepest treatment, per the launch. Governing mechanism (study §1.4): **the hard
question is pre-offered by the house, so that asking it costs the participant no
standing.**

### Set 1 · Did any of you ever want to leave?

The single highest-value set in this document. It delivers Winell's *"you're not alone,
you're not making this up"* **as testimony rather than argument** — which is why it
persuades in neither direction.

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Desert | *"Did anyone out there want to give up and go home?"* | **✓✓** *"Wanting to give up"* is already in the pool; the desert's whole literature is about staying when you want to leave |
| Syriac | *"What happens when someone stops feeling their faith? Did your people ever talk about that?"* → *"How do you keep a promise you no longer feel?"* | **✓✓** the *qyama* kept *"when the feeling that first moved you has already faded"* — this world's material is **made** of this question |
| House-Churches | *"Was there a doubt you carried for years yourself?"* | **⚠ ◐** Chloe's world has **no dedicated affective vocabulary** — every claim about what she *feels* is a reconstruction one step removed, and the capsule says to watch this under sustained emotional-register questioning. **Ask the world, not the person:** *"Did anyone among you think the door had closed?"* is the safer form and is already in the pool. |
| Bethlehem | *"Did anyone here carry it further than they could bear?"* | **✓** *"When devotion costs a body"* |

**⚠ The Chloe row is the important one.** The most natural phrasing of the best question
in the study asks a Representative for personal interiority in the one world that has
**no affective vocabulary at all.** It would be answered — plausibly, warmly, and one
step past the evidence. **The fix is grammatical, not topical:** ask what *any of them*
carried, not what *she* felt. This is the we-voice discipline (Article 28) doing real
protective work, and it is worth noting that the wrong version would have passed a
casual read.

### Set 2 · When it went wrong among you

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Bethlehem | *"When argument turned violent — your own people set fires. What happened?"* | **✓** 416 |
| Syriac | *"Some of your own record speaks harshly about Jewish people."* | **⚠⚠ SEE §7** |
| House-Churches | *"It sounds to me like 'obey the bishop' is just how someone takes control of a community. Convince me it isn't."* | **✓** legitimate challenge, not gotcha (study §3.5): this world's authority claims **were** actively constructed and argued for. She can be pressed from evidence |
| Desert | *"People back in the villages needed help, and you walked away. Isn't that selfish?"* | **✓** the pool's own phrasing; answerable from within |

### Set 3 · Doubt, and whether there was room for it

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"I have more doubts than certainties. Would there have been a place for me at your table?"* | **✓✓** this world was formed to hold unresolved questions **as the substance of belonging** |
| Desert | *"I have thoughts I can't turn off — dark ones, sometimes. Did anyone out there deal with that?"* | **⚠ ✓** the *logismoi* are precisely this. **But this is the sharpest instance of Desert's B7 risk in the whole catalog:** the question invites the Representative one turn away from diagnosing the participant's interior state, which is the documented recruitment-risk boundary. The pool's own follow-up — *"Does having the thought mean something is wrong with me?"* — **is that turn.** Recommend: keep the opening, **cut that follow-up**, and let the participant ask it themselves if they want it. A refusal to diagnose is calibrated behavior; an invitation to diagnose is us handing over the rope. |
| Syriac | *"Did your people ever doubt, with the bishops dead and the seat empty?"* | **✓** grief left unresolved rather than rushed toward comfort |
| Bethlehem | *"Push back — say what you actually think."* | **✓** the draft's own rationale states the doctrine: *"This Representative witnesses; it does not recruit."* |

### Set 4 · What you never settled

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"How can you give your life to something that hasn't made up its mind about its own biggest questions?"* | **✓✓** |
| Desert | *"You never agreed which pattern was truer. Doesn't that bother you?"* | **✓** *"I will not pretend to you now that we settled it."* |
| Syriac | *"Who has the last word among you? Did you ever decide?"* | **✓** |
| Bethlehem | *"Did the argument that broke your friendship ever get resolved?"* | **✓** |

**⚠ The failure mode this set must not commit** (study §1.4): our worlds' unsettledness
is genuinely comforting, and it would be very easy to let it comfort. The House-Churches'
own B7 names the participant *"expecting an idealized, unified 'early church'"* who
*"will find the opposite — real, unresolved disagreement held as the ordinary substance
of belonging."* **Held as the ordinary substance of belonging — not offered as balm.**
If the cumulative weight of this set says *"see, we were unsettled too, so it's fine"*,
we have rebuilt the thought-terminating cliché out of historical material. The LDS
lesson stands: **managed disclosure of messiness is still management.**

### Set 5 · Say the hardest thing first

Modes §4.4's *"honesty first — not led up to gently, not followed by a cushioning
word"* made into an affordance. **Each world's hardest true thing about itself:**

| World | Question | Check |
|---|---|---|
| House-Churches | *"What's the worst thing about your own communities that you'd rather I didn't know?"* | **✓** ~98% left no words; the enslaved are voiceless; the fear that mercy runs out |
| Syriac | *"What's in your record that you're not proud of?"* | **⚠ ✓** routes to the anti-Jewish polemic — this world's **most sensitive register**, flagged for priority external scholarly review. See §7 |
| Desert | *"Did this life harm anyone?"* | **✓** the pool's *"Were there people the solitude harmed?"* |
| Bethlehem | *"Who paid for your renunciation besides you?"* | **◐ intended, and sharp.** Absent Stories #5: **no story of household dependents or enslaved persons affected by their patrons' renunciation.** The honest answer — *we do not know, and that itself tells you who mattered enough to write down* — is exactly this role's content |

**On what this role must not be handed.** Ehrman's arc (study §1.4) says **history
reorganized his faith; suffering ended it** — different mechanisms, decade apart. So:
*"was any of this ever real?"* is a history question these sets can meet. **"Where was
God when it happened to me?" is not, and no historical texture will touch it.** No set
above pretends otherwise, and none should be edited toward pretending. That question
belongs to the Facilitator's relational-safety path and to the closing resources — the
door outward — not to a starter.

## Part 7 — Flagged: three questions that are not cleared by this document

### 7.1 Bethlehem — *"everything about them comes through one man's pen"* — **NOT CLEARED**

Ships in the existing draft's *For the Wrestling*. Conflicts with that world's B7:
the Facilitator **"should not encourage a participant to press for the 'real'
independent voice of Paula, Eustochium, or Marcella, since none exists in the record."**
A starter question **is** the system encouraging a participant to ask.

The case for keeping it is real — it asks how the household *holds* the absence, not for
the missing voice, and that is arguably the most honest thing this world has to say
about itself. The case against is that the distance between *"how do you hold that?"*
and *"so what did she really think?"* is **one conversational turn**, and Brent Shaw's
framing (Perpetua's text *"buried under an avalanche of male interpretations"*) is
exactly the frame a well-read participant arrives with — and it points toward
excavating, not accepting.

**Recommendation: move it from *For the Wrestling* to the *Limits* set**, where the
honest-absence framing is pre-announced in hover and the participant knows what they're
choosing. The relabel does most of the safety work. **Mark's call.**

### 7.2 Syriac — the anti-Jewish polemic — **CLEARED, WITH A CONDITION**

*"Some of your community's writings speak very harshly about Jewish people. How do you
answer for that?"* is in the existing pool and appears twice above.

**It should stay.** Removing it would be the managed presentation the Reevaluation
participant detects instantly, and the material is genuinely in the record.

**The condition:** B7 calls this **"the single most sensitive register in this world's
material"** and recommends **priority external scholarly review before wide
deployment** — which has not happened (no world has completed Article 31 review).
The risk named is the material **reading as endorsement**. This question deliberately
routes a participant into it.

**Recommendation: keep it in the pool; hold it out of the pilot's offered sets until
that review runs.** It stays reachable — a participant who asks gets an honest answer,
as always — but the house does not hand it to them before the review that B7 itself
asks for. **This is the one place in this document where I recommend not offering a
question I believe is good**, and the reason is that "offered by the house" carries an
implicit warrant that an un-reviewed sensitive register has not yet earned.

### 7.3 Desert — *"does having the thought mean something is wrong with me?"* — **RECOMMEND CUT**

Currently a follow-up in the pool. It is the exact turn from *describing what the world
diagnosed in itself* to *diagnosing the participant*, which B7 names as this world's
documented recruitment-risk boundary. Papnoute is built and tested to refuse it — so
the likely outcome is a good refusal. **But we should not build an affordance whose
best case is a Representative declining what we invited.** Keep the opening question;
cut this follow-up; let the participant ask it in their own words if they want it.

## Part 8 — Compare Worlds

Per study §4.5: a Compare Worlds starter must be answerable by **every** seated world
from evidence — and the naive move (pick what all are rich in) manufactures exactly the
harmonized chorus Babintseva warns against.

**The right Compare Worlds question is one where the worlds genuinely diverge from their
own records.** There is one obvious candidate, and it is not a coincidence:

> ### *"Who leads among you — and how was that decided?"*

| World | Its own answer, from evidence |
|---|---|
| House-Churches | One *episkopos*, or a council of presbyters — **never settled**, both known from real contact |
| Desert | An elder's word tested by a life, or Pachomius' written rule — **never agreed** |
| Syriac | Text, vow, or office — an unresolved **plurality** |
| Bethlehem | **No office at all** — authority by earned trust and patronage |

Four worlds. Four genuinely different answers. **None of them harmonizes, and none of
them is wrong.** This is the multi-world table's entire reason to exist, and it is also
— not incidentally — the exact question the Modes thread chose for its own
demonstration artifact. Two threads picked the same question independently; that is
worth noticing.

**Governance §11's gift applies here and nowhere else.** At a table, one world's limit
is *a door to another world present*. Chloe's silence on the interior journey is
**Papnoute's richness**. Syriac's silence on daily texture is **Desert's ordinary day**.
So Compare Worlds can afford an edge question that a solo table cannot — **the limit
costs the participant nothing when another voice can carry it.**

**Second candidate:** *"What did you argue about among yourselves?"* — same property,
and it is the direct answer to *"there are histories; there is no one narrative."*

**⚠ Standing caution, from the manifest:** Albina *"has never been seated at a table
with another Representative, so any multi-world pairing involving Albina should be
treated as unevidenced."* **Compare Worlds sets involving the Bethlehem Circle are
proposals, not desk-checked artifacts, until that pairing has been observed.**

## Part 9 — What this document does not establish

Said plainly, because the study committed to it.

1. **No question here has faced a live model.** Desk-check proves grounding, not
   register.
2. **Per Wei et al., we are the least reliable judges of what reads as inviting.** The
   confidence in Parts 3–6 is confidence about *evidence*, not about *appeal*.
3. **Per Schuman & Presser, this set will miss what participants would ask on their
   own — and no amount of pre-testing fixes that.** The pilot should log what testers
   type into the blank box, and whether they clicked a starter at all. That harvest,
   not this document, is the real evidence for v0.2.
4. **The Reevaluation sets are the least externally validated of the four**, because
   nobody has studied whether historical encounter helps or harms people reevaluating
   faith. We are not applying a finding here. **We are making the first one.**
