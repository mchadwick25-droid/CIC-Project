## 0. What governs this re-test

- **The four marks** (change order, 2026-11-08, "a defined movement, not just a trend"): a
  recognizable beginning; identifiable carriers; a name or self-understanding as a movement; and
  recognition as a movement by historians.
- **The evidence rule for recent movements** (change order, 2026-11-08, "Change order: evidence
  for recent movements"; Mark: "we have to be able to tell an accurate story, but use different
  criteria"). The movement's own dated founding texts show marks 1–3. A standard scholarly
  reference work or academic study shows mark 4. Both are required for a "defined movement"
  verdict. Neither needs to be vendored in the Library.
- **Verification.** "Verified" means the item's bibliographic details (or a founding text's date
  and publisher) were confirmed in this run against a search result or page whose address is
  recorded. Everything else is [S], and [S] never counts as verified. Wikipedia is [S] throughout,
  even when its claims are stated correctly. Repo text is counted apart from verified items.
- **Mark 4, one standard for every candidate (after Round 1).** Mark 4 is met when a scholarly
  study or reference work treats the candidate as a movement. It does so when it names the movement,
  or tells it as one story across more than one carrier. A study of a single carrier counts only if
  that study itself places the carrier inside the named movement, and the place where it does so is
  quoted or cited. A history written by the movement's own leaders or press is an own text: it may
  show marks 1 to 3, never mark 4. A study that tells a larger story, such as all of Christian
  history, does not by that alone treat this candidate as a movement. Every verdict below was
  re-judged by this standard.
- **Copy.** Every sentence of draft copy traces to a verified item that supports it. A sentence
  that rests only on an [S] item, or on none, is cut, and the gap is left.
- **Window and living mark.** Era 10 runs 1906 to c. 2010; nothing after 2010 is claimed in the
  history. The living mark means alive today, graded CD or CR on its newest dated evidence.
- **How it was made.** Three Opus researchers, one per group below, each with web search and its
  own scratch space. No metered model calls.

### The mark 4 standard (added at assembly, after Round 1)

- **G-79. How much recognition by historians mark 4 needs.** The change order asks that
  historians recognize a candidate "as a movement". Round 1 found it judged two ways, so this
  revision applied one reading, set by this thread, not by Mark: a study counts when it treats the
  candidate as a movement, naming it or telling it as one story across more than one carrier. A
  study of a single carrier counts only if it places that carrier inside the named movement. Under
  that reading, the evangelical student movement (G-62) and frontier missions (G-58) fall one mark
  short. (a) keep this reading, as applied; (b) a looser reading: scholarly studies of a
  movement's principal carriers suffice when its own texts name the movement, which would restore
  both as entries; (c) keep (a) and send both to the source-research thread to look for a study
  of the whole movement. **Rec. (c)** It holds the bar that kept the other verdicts consistent,
  and these two are well-documented movements likely to find such a study.
