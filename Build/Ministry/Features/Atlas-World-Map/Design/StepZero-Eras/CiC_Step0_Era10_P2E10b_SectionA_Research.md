## IX.54 — The rescue-mission movement (IUGM, 1913; later AGRM; Citygate Network)

**Title:** "Statement of Faith" (Citygate Network)
**URL seen:** https://www.citygatenetwork.org/statement-of-faith/ (live page, read 2026-11-08) — **verified**
**Date:** not stated on the page. The earliest Wayback capture of this URL is 2025-08-23 **[S]**. Earlier captures exist at older URLs, with contents unseen **[S]**: citygatenetwork.org/agrm/Statement_of_Faith.asp (2019-07-20) and agrm.org/agrm/Statement_of_Faith.asp (2012-01-11). The page says members affirm it "in alignment with our bylaws", but it gives no bylaw date. **The only copy seen is post-2010, and it is undated.**
**Verbatim lines:**
> "We believe there is one God, eternally existing as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit."
> "We believe that the Lord Jesus Christ is Deity, that He was born of a virgin, that we are redeemed by His atoning death through His shed blood, that He bodily resurrected and ascended into heaven, and that He will come again in power and great glory."

On the floor: the Trinity and Christ's divinity are explicit. The resurrection is explicit and bodily. The incarnation is carried by "Deity ... born of a virgin", not stated in its own words.
No IUGM-era (1913 onward) statement was found. A Citygate member mission (Orlando Union Rescue Mission, ourm.org) says it subscribes to Citygate's doctrinal statement **[S]**, not read.

**Section A reading: clears directly on content, on Citygate Network's "Statement of Faith" (undated; read live 2026; earliest capture seen 2025).** Grade: post-2010 and undated. It evidences the present network's confession, not the IUGM's. The 2012 AGRM capture is the first lead for an earlier date.

## IX.55 — The Christian homeschool movement (HSLDA, 1983)

**Title:** "What We Believe" — sub-heading "What does it mean when HSLDA says we're a Christian organization?"; the doctrinal section is headed "What does HSLDA as an organization believe?"
**URL seen:** https://hslda.org/post/what-we-believe — **verified**
**Date:** byline "HSLDA, April 22, 2020" on the page (**verified**). Earliest Wayback capture of the URL: 2020-06-29 **[S]**. An hslda.org/about page was captured from 2001-04-08, contents unseen **[S]**. **The only copy seen is post-2010 (2020).**
**Verbatim lines:**
> "There is one God, who exists eternally in three Persons (the Trinity): Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Because human beings are sinful by nature and inherently in need of salvation, God became a flesh-and-blood human to save us—Jesus Christ, the Son, who was conceived by God in a virgin woman, Mary. In His death, Jesus received the consequences for sin that we deserved. He then rose bodily from the dead and will literally come again to earth in the Second Advent."

On the floor: the Trinity is explicit. Christ's divinity and the incarnation are carried by "God became a flesh-and-blood human ... Jesus Christ, the Son". The resurrection is explicit and bodily.
Caveat for the row: the statement binds HSLDA's "leaders, directors, and employees". The same page says "We do not require members of HSLDA to be Christian or religious." So this is the carrier's confession, not a membership test of the movement.

**Section A reading: clears directly on HSLDA's "What We Believe" (dated April 22, 2020).** Grade: post-2010; 37 years after the 1983 founding. It evidences the carrier's confession now, not at founding.

## IX.57 — The Third Wave and the Vineyard (Association of Vineyard Churches; John Wimber)

**Title:** "Statement of Faith" (Association of Vineyard Churches; now Vineyard USA)
**Date:** adopted by the Association of Vineyard Churches Board of Directors, "as of the Board meeting of November 1994." That adoption line was seen only on third-party reproductions, not on the publisher's own page:
- https://bible.ca/cr-vinyard.htm — **verified** (the reproduction): "Until such a time, the Association of Vineyard Churches Board of Directors has formally adopted this document as our official statement of faith as of the Board meeting of November 1994."
- https://religionfacts.com/vineyard-churches — **verified** (the same sentence, quoted).
- So the 1994 date rests on third-party copies **[S as to the publisher]**. The publisher's own early copies exist only as Wayback captures with contents unseen **[S]**: vineyardusa.org/about/beliefs/ (2002-02-17) and vineyardusa.org/site/files/about/statement-of-faith.pdf (2009-02-06). Wikipedia says the statement was "released in 1994" after work begun in 1983 **[S]**.

**Publisher's own text, seen:** Vineyard USA, *Core Values & Beliefs* booklet, section "Our Statement of Faith" — https://vineyardusa.org/Vineyard-Core-Values-Beliefs.pdf — **verified** (PDF metadata creation date 2023-02-23; no adoption date in the booklet).
**Verbatim lines (publisher's booklet):**
> "God The King And The Holy Trinity — We believe that God is the Eternal King. He is an infinite, unchangeable Spirit, perfect in holiness, wisdom, goodness, justice, power, and love. From all eternity He exists as the One Living and True God in three persons of one substance, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, equal in power and glory."
> "Christ The Mediator And Eternal King — ... sending His Son, Jesus, into the world. Conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary, as fully God and fully human in one person ..."
> "After dying for the sins of the world, Jesus was raised from the dead on the third day ..."

The 1994-labelled third-party copy (bible.ca) has the same Trinity sentence. Its Christ line reads "as fully God and fully man in one person" ("man", not "human"). Neither wording changes the doctrine.

On the floor: the Trinity is explicit ("three persons of one substance ... equal in power and glory"). Christ's full divinity and the incarnation are explicit ("fully God and fully human in one person"). The resurrection is explicit.

**Section A reading: clears directly on the Vineyard "Statement of Faith" (AVC Board, November 1994).** Grade: the content is verified on the publisher's own text (2023 booklet). The November 1994 adoption date is seen only on third-party copies, and the publisher's own dated copy is still to be checked (the 2002 and 2009 Wayback captures).
