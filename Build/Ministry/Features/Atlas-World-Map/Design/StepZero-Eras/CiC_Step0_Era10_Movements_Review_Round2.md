## SUBSTANTIAL findings

### S-1. The homeschool entry is scoped "Christian", but no cited item shows its carriers are Christian, and Section A says no creed was seen

**Location:** M13 verdict ("defined movement: the Christian homeschool movement"), mark 3, Section A line ("HSLDA is a legal body with no creed seen"), draft name, G-64.

**Evidence:** The own texts cited for marks 1 to 3 (items 6 and 10) name "the modern homeschooling movement", that is, the movement as a whole. Mark 3 itself says "Shown for homeschooling". No cited item states that HSLDA, the Moores or the families are Christian. The Farris tribute I read does not label HSLDA or the movement Christian; it mentions only "Christian radio". The Christian scope rests on Stevens's lineage (item 1), a reference work, so in the record as it stands the unit "Christian homeschool movement" is not shown by any own text. HSLDA's own site does describe it as a Christian organization ("What does it mean when HSLDA says we're a Christian organization?", https://hslda.org/post/what-we-believe). That contradicts the Section A line, and it is the own-text item the entry needs. This is the same precision about the unit that held M2. The fact is recoverable, but as written the defining word of the entry traces to nothing in the record's own texts.

**Fix (mechanical):**
1. Add HSLDA's "what we believe" / Christian-organization page as an own-text item, with its date (or [S] date), as verified once seen.
2. Rewrite Section A to cite it. If the page carries a statement of faith, say so; otherwise say "self-described Christian organization; statement of faith not seen".
3. In mark 3, state plainly that the own-text name is for the homeschooling movement as a whole. The Christian unit rests on HSLDA's self-description plus Stevens's Christian lineage (reference work).
4. Make the Summary row, G-64 and the Count match.

If no dated own item can be cited, change the verdict wording to say that the Christian scope rests on a reference work, and offer it in G-64.

## MINOR findings

**M-1. Homeschool longDescription S7 overstates the source.** "In March 1983 two of those fathers, the lawyers Michael Farris and J. Michael Smith, launched..." makes both men among the families who heard the broadcast and began to homeschool. Item 10 says Vickie Farris and Mike Smith heard it. It says the Smiths began "right away" and does not say when the Farrises began. *Fix:* "In March 1983 two homeschooling fathers, the lawyers Michael Farris and J. Michael Smith, launched the Home School Legal Defense Association with their wives" (items 6, 10). Separately, S1 ("traces one root of Christian homeschooling to the Christian day-school movement") should read "traces one root of homeschooling", as Stevens's two lineages are of homeschooling as a whole (item 1).

**M-2. Frontier longDescription S3 drops a qualifier.** "Still had no strong church of their own" renders Maust's "still do not have a strong indigenous evangelical church". Dropping "evangelical" widens the claim to peoples with Orthodox, Catholic or other churches. That is a modern sense that misleads. *Fix:* "...that still had no strong evangelical church of their own" (E12). The draft is held, but it is kept "so the path to an entry is ready".

**M-3. Count sentence: "7 are recommended as entries".** It subtracts only the Oxford Group (Section A) and new monasticism. G-72 recommends holding the healing revival's floor assessment until a dated statement of faith and Weaver's account are checked, so that entry also waits on Section A. *Fix:* "Of the 9, the Oxford Group and the healing revival wait on Section A (G-69, G-72)...", with the resulting number. Alternatively, say explicitly that the healing revival is recommended in form (G-71) with its floor held (G-72).

**M-4. G-79 option (b) omits Bible translation.** (b) is "scholarly studies of a movement's principal carriers suffice when its own texts name the movement", and the record says it "would restore both". M1 has carrier studies (Svelmoe, Aldridge) and an own-text name ("the Bible translation movement", Franklin 2013). *Fix:* add one clause saying what (b) does to M1. For example: it would not restore M1 on its own, because M1's mark 3 is only partly shown and its own-text name dates from 2013. Or it would, and G-56 changes too.

**M-5. Copy sentences resting on repo text alone, and one shifted rendering.**
- (a) Third Wave S1 (and Why S1) traces only to "census IX.45".
- (b) Liturgical S8 ("prepared much of what the Second Vatican Council did with worship") traces to item 8 (repo) and item 6, whose cited content is a chapter title, which does not support "prepared".

  §0 says copy traces to a verified item and counts repo text apart. *Fix:* either add one §0 line allowing the census's own approved text to carry a sentence, or cite a verified item. For the Third Wave that is the Vineyard's or Miller's dating; for the liturgical movement, a Fenwick and Spinks passage.
- (c) Anglican S5: "Where there was a clear moral duty to do so" renders the resolution's "clearly felt moral obligation". That turns a subjective test into an objective one, which is the very point of the resolution's contested reading (the ECU report calls its import extremely difficult to determine). *Fix:* "Where a couple clearly felt a moral obligation to do so, they said, the method must be decided on Christian principles" (E9).
