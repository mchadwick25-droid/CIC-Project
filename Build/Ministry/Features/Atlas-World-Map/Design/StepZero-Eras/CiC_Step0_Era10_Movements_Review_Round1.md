## SUBSTANTIAL findings

### S-1. Mark 4 judged two ways: student movements admitted, Bible translation held, on the same kind of study

**Location:** "Student and campus movements (M2)", mark 4, verdict, and G-62. Compare "M1 Bible translation", mark 4, and G-56.

**Evidence:** For M1 the document says mark 4 is "not shown" because Svelmoe and Aldridge "study one carrier" and "none was seen calling 20th-century Bible translation a movement of its own". For M2, mark 4 is called "shown, with a limit" on Turner (one carrier, Campus Crusade, read as part of "the renewal of evangelicalism") and Bramadat (one InterVarsity chapter). The document itself says: "No academic study of the student movement as a whole was found." I confirmed both books: Turner, UNC Press, March 2008 (https://uncpress.org/book/9780807858738/bill-bright-and-campus-crusade-for-christ), and Bramadat, OUP, 13 July 2000 (https://academic.oup.com/book/40920). Neither framing names an evangelical student movement as the unit. The two candidates stand on the same footing for mark 4, yet one is admitted and the other is held. Under the both-kinds rule, the admitted verdict does not follow.

**Fix:** Make the two verdicts agree. Either (i) set M2 mark 4 to "not shown as a movement (studies of carriers only)", change the verdict to "one mark short", and move G-62's recommendation to (c), with (a) as the path once a whole-movement study is staged; or (ii) argue explicitly why carrier studies count for M2 but not M1, and re-test M1 on that same reading. Then update the Summary row, the Count paragraph ("11 movements", "9 are recommended as entries") and G-62 to match.

### S-2. Christian day schools admitted inside a family entry although the strand fails the both-kinds rule

**Location:** "Christian day schools and the homeschool movement (M13)", marks 1 and 3, draft dates "1960s-c. 2010", G-64 recommendation.

**Evidence:** The document says that for day schools "no dated founding text of the day-school movement was seen", and that the 1960s start "rests on Stevens's account". Mark 3 for both strands is shown only through reference works ("Stevens ... names both"; "Gaither's last five chapters"), not through the movement's own texts, as the change order requires for marks 1 to 3. G-64 then recommends the family entry partly *because* "the day-school strand alone lacks a dated founding text". That uses the family form to carry a strand past the rule. It is the same gap that keeps Word of Faith out (G-74: "the rule needs both kinds of evidence, and the own-text half is unverified"). Stevens's two-lineage account is real; I confirmed that his book traces home education to "the liberal alternative school movement of the 1960s and 1970s and one stemming from the Christian day school movement of the same era" (https://press.princeton.edu/node/62152). But that is mark-4 evidence, not own-text evidence.

**Fix:** Recommend (c), homeschooling only, 1975 or 1983 to c. 2010, with the day schools as the disclosed prelude Stevens names. Or hold the day-school half in the way Word of Faith is held, until a dated own text is staged (for example ACSI's 1978 founding documents). Cite an own text for homeschooling's self-understanding for mark 3, or say plainly that mark 3 rests on reference works. Re-date the draft from "1960s" to a dated own act.

### S-3. Healing revival: the floor reading claims more than the dated texts show, and the gate recommendation decides a route that is Mark's

**Location:** "Healing revivals and the Latter Rain", Section A line, G-71 (C1) and its recommendation.

**Evidence:** Section A says "Most evangelists stood in Trinitarian Pentecostal bodies", but cites only [S] items for it (E3). It names no carrier's dated statement of faith. It then applies A5 / Article 21 to set aside Branham's non-Trinitarian teaching. The methodology's A5 clause covers "multiple internal strands" and "one divergent internal strand" (method doc, A5). Branham is not a minor strand here. By the document's own E1, he is the named publisher of the revival's founding magazine, and the copy calls him "its best-known early figure". Whether A5 applies, or whether the A4 hand-selected path (the project lead's alone, logged, never waiving Christ's divinity) is the right one, is a floor decision. The census already puts the non-Trinitarian Pentecostal body on the register as a floor question (IX.15, "Register (Floor Question)"). G-71 also stacks two decisions (one entry or two, and the floor route), and its recommendation (a) pre-selects A5. Compare the Oxford Group, where the same lack of a dated statement leads to (d), hold.

The Latter Rain half has the same gap as Word of Faith. Its own texts (E2, E3) are both [S], so its marks 1 to 3 rest only on Riss, a reference work. I confirmed the reference works: Harrell, Indiana UP, 1975 (https://cambridge.org/core/journals/church-history/article/abs/all-things-are-possible-the-healing-and-charismatic-revivals-in-modern-america-by-david-edwin-harrelljr-bloomington-indiana-university-press-1975-xi-304-pp-1095/795C07D76DD0C6F0E5130C5106022F99); Riss, Pneuma 4/1 (Spring 1982), 32-45 (https://calmview.bham.ac.uk/rdf/catalog/XHT/I/1/179); Weaver, Mercer UP, 1987, with "The decline of the healing revival 1955-1965" (https://archives.northwestu.edu/handle/nu/65437); and The Voice of Healing, April 1948, Branham as publisher.

**Fix:** Split G-71 into two questions: (1) form: healing revival as the entry with the Latter Rain as a named line (now (b)), one entry, or two; (2) floor: A5 world-level assessment versus A4 hand-selected inclusion versus hold, with Branham's divergence stated from Weaver once checked. Recommend (d) hold on question 2 until a carrier's dated statement of faith and Weaver's account of Branham's teaching are cited, consistent with G-69. Delete "Most evangelists stood in Trinitarian Pentecostal bodies" from Section A unless a dated source is cited. Recommend (b) on question 1 so the Latter Rain is not admitted as a co-equal half on [S] own texts.

### S-4. Faith missions: the entry's opening claim does not trace to its cited evidence

**Location:** "M5 faith missions", mark 1, Why S1, longDescription S1-S2 and S5 (trace "S1-S2 E2,E3; S5 E2,E3").

**Evidence:** The copy opens: "In 1917 the main North American mission conference let only denominational boards vote." Neither E2 (Wheaton guide) nor E3 (CT 1965), as recorded, says this. My checks of both found the 1917 March and September meetings and "popularly termed 'faith missions'" (https://christianitytoday.com/1965/01/interdenominational-foreign-mission-association). They did not find a voting rule. The Wheaton archives instead describe the founders as agencies "that no longer felt compatible with the growing ecumenical movement" (https://archives.wheaton.edu/repositories/4/resources/194), which is a different cause. S5, "The association grew fastest in the 1940s and 1950s", is also not in E2 or E3 as recorded. The stated cause of a founding is the kind of claim the trust rule protects.

**Fix:** Quote the exact passage of E2 or E3 that supports the voting rule, or replace the sentence with what the Wheaton guide says (the founding agencies' sense of distance from the ecumenical movement), cited to its URL. Cut S5 or cite a source that dates the growth. While here, note that "Its members called themselves faith missions" rests on CT's "popularly termed" wording, whose page is [S]. Either soften it to "were known as faith missions" or cite an own text.
