Decision 1 of 3 · the unit of access

What does a visitor get when they use a free allowance or buy a pack?

All options share the same protections, which the review approved to proceed to design. A unit is spent only when the first Representative reply lands. Errors and cut-off replies cost nothing. Refresh, back or a dropped connection never loses it, and an unfinished conversation resumes for at least 7 days. Purchases do not expire.

Recommended by research

Whole conversation

One unit is one conversation: an opening question and the follow-ups in it. It has a length ceiling built as an ending, with a gentle cue near the end. Expert Q&A services frame a unit this way.

The ceiling must come from pilot logs. The engine already caps sessions at 10 exchanges, so the first question is whether paying buys more conversations or longer ones. Who voices the close is also open. Today it is a Facilitator template.
Layer, not a unit

Church or class pool

A shared pool of whole conversations for a congregation or class, sold once. Sits on top of A or B.

Fits the faith-sector pattern (FORMED, Hallow). Do not decide it first.
Not recommended

Turns, credits or minutes

Per-message counts, an abstract currency, or timed passes. Visitors watch a meter, cost is hard to cap, and these draw the most complaints in the research.

Cursor, Duolingo Energy and Cambly are the cautions.

Your direction on this decision: one offer that buys more conversations, each able to go deeper than a free one. Still open: how much deeper. Cost per turn rises as a conversation grows, so the paid length cap has to come from pilot logs and the cost measurement, not a guess. Free conversations keep today's 10-exchange cap until then.

Decisions 2 and 3, still to come: the free allowance, then the end-of-free moment. Both depend on this one.

Decided so far

Recorded in the workstream decision log, Build/Ministry/Features/Access-Monetization/.

  • Safety rule. No balance, cap or paywall sits between a message and the safety gate. Purchase prompts stay hidden after a safety event.
  • Voice. The Facilitator speaks the close and the offer. The Representative never speaks the ask.
  • No auto-reload at pilot, so no recurring billing in any form.
  • Church and class offer is a one-time shared pool, never an annual licence.
  • Donations stay separate from paid access at pilot.
  • Tracking. The free allowance uses the existing signed visitor cookie only. This is a change order to the 2026-08-06 no-tracking decision.
  • Buyers are adults. A parent may buy for a youth. Youth use needs its own safety and COPPA review.
  • Privacy. A payment is never linked to conversation text.
  • Counsel and an accountant review before any live checkout. Everything runs in Stripe test mode until then.

What makes a charge feel fair

"I got what I paid for"

  • A whole thing, not a counted thing
  • Priced in dollars, not a currency
  • Never expires
  • Not charged when a reply fails
  • Ends on purpose, not at a wall

"I got robbed"

  • A counted thing with a visible meter
  • Priced in credits or gems
  • Unused time or credits expire
  • Charged for failed or cut-off output
  • Ends mid-thought at a wall

Basis: Prelec and Loewenstein, Soman and Gourville, Raghubir and Srivastava, plus product cases (Cambly, Duolingo, Cursor, Suno, Audible). A separate fairness study has not been run.

Structure and margin, without prices

  • Stripe fees punish small tickets. $1 costs 32.9% in fees, $5 costs 8.9%, $10 costs 5.9%, $20 costs 4.4%. The research sets $10 as the practical floor.
  • The 5-times markup target is not reliable. The review found the planning model assumed free use costs 20% of paid use. At 2% to 5% conversion the free tier could cost several times paid use, which would turn margin negative. Markup has to be modeled against the free-to-paid ratio, and the dispute fee ($15) outweighs small packs. Prices wait for that.
  • Structure the research proposed: three packs in a non-expiring balance, and a gift pack. Build a Table is a separate early-access item, priced by Representatives plus one until its cost is measured. Opt-in auto-reload, a church offer and a donation round-up are open questions for you, not recommendations. A church licence billed annually would be a subscription.
  • Checkout stays on the web. Apple and Google take 15% to 30% in-app. Start US-only. EU buyers owe VAT from the first sale, so a merchant-of-record service comes later.

The end-of-free moment: six directions

The research recommends D2 and D3 together. The Representative never speaks the ask in any of them.

D2
1st

Closing Page. Every conversation ends on a summary page with the offer on it. Whether a transcript is kept or emailed depends on privacy decisions still open. The ordinary end of every conversation, with no interruption.

D3
1st

Threshold Sheet. A dismissible sheet that appears only when someone reaches for more after the free allowance is used. Plain cost reason, three packs, per-conversation price, a real "Not now" with the refresh date.

D1
3rd

Closing Card. A short card inline at the end of the conversation, with no separate page.

D5
phase 2

Sponsored Seat. A donor-funded conversation for visitors who cannot pay. Precedents are Hallow, Lichess and the suspended-coffee idea.

D4
signed-in

Quiet Ledger. A balance view for signed-in visitors. A complement, not a first screen.

D6
last

Degrade to Shorter. Keep going with shorter replies instead of stopping. Only worth it if data shows people leave.

Rules the evidence supports. One calm notice at the start of the last free conversation, with no running counter. The refresh shown as a plain date, not a clock. Dollars and a per-conversation price. "Never expires" and "a failed reply never counts" on screen. No pre-checked boxes, no countdowns, no discount-on-dismiss, no untrue "best value" badge. Support sits apart from the packs. What the best products share

The benchmark covers 15 products, including Wikipedia, the Guardian, Kagi, Hallow, Audible, Steam and Khan Academy.

  • The whole experience comes before any ask.
  • The limit is explained in plain words in advance.
  • The wall falls between units, never inside one.
  • Returners pay in one tap. Newcomers check out as a guest, wallet first.
  • On return, the balance shows, the conversation is restored, and there is one thank-you with no upsell.
  • Refunds are lenient enough to be a trust signal.

Public benchmarks: a wallet such as Apple Pay lifts checkout by about 22% where it is shown, and about double when shown early. Link adds 7% to 14%. Lenient refunds raised net sales on Steam. Wikipedia's honest copy beat threat copy. Hallow is the commercial high-water mark for a faith app, yet its reviews are dominated by trial-billing and support complaints. A one-time model removes the trial trap by design. The full report has a 28-item pass or fail checklist to measure CiC against.
