## 2026-10-19 — Direction B: a hand dealt from a deck

**Decision (Mark).** "B, hand dealt from a deck." Chosen over A (all eleven in one fan) and C (a row and grid).

**Mark's question that shaped it.** "If we go with a hand of five, how do they get access to the others, especially when we will have 100?" The answer, now in the design: the hand is the front door, never the whole chooser. Behind it sit the deck (the set the hand is dealt from, narrowed by era or by a place or a name the visitor knows, with the count always written) and the library (the Church Family Tree, every tradition on one map, one step away).

**Why B and not A or C.** A hides half the cards at any moment, and the evidence from Apple's collection guidance, Google's own carousel research and the Oslo carousel studies runs against it. C is the pattern the standards describe, but it is the least like the hand Mark likes, and at a hundred worlds it needs the same deck anyway. B keeps the hand at the count where nothing hides, honours Theme D's rotating few, and scales through the deck and the Tree.

**The heart of it.** Mark wants the first screen to feel like being dealt a hand, simple and a little surprising, with the depth one turn away. The deck and the Tree keep that feeling honest at scale: the hand never pretends to be everything.
