## 2026-10-19 — Direction B: a hand dealt from a deck

**Decision (Mark).** "B, hand dealt from a deck." Chosen over A (all eleven in one fan) and C (a row and grid).

**Mark's question that shaped it.** "If we go with a hand of five, how do they get access to the others, especially when we will have 100?" The answer, now in the design: the hand is the front door, never the whole chooser. Behind it sit the deck (the set the hand is dealt from, narrowed by era or by a place or a name the visitor knows, with the count always written) and the library (the Church Family Tree, every tradition on one map, one step away).

**Why B and not A or C.** A hides half the cards at any moment, and the evidence from Apple's collection guidance, Google's own carousel research and the Oslo carousel studies runs against it. C is the pattern the standards describe, but it is the least like the hand Mark likes, and at a hundred worlds it needs the same deck anyway. B keeps the hand at the count where nothing hides, honours Theme D's rotating few, and scales through the deck and the Tree.

**The heart of it.** Mark wants the first screen to feel like being dealt a hand, simple and a little surprising, with the depth one turn away. The deck and the Tree keep that feeling honest at scale: the hand never pretends to be everything.

## 2026-10-19 — Change order (Mark): the card is a collector's card

**Mark's words.** "I want each tradition to have a collectors card, square with the era band across the top, the tradition name a dates, picture and the taglines we just developed in the front and the longer detail to fit on the back of the card, patterned after like baseball cards or pokemon cards, but with a church history aesthetic that matches the portraits." **What changes in the converged design.** The card front now carries the tagline (the intro line) as well as the portrait, the tradition name and dates, and the era band; the back carries the longer summary, a stat block (years, place, shaped by) and the actions. **Still open from this change.** The portrait thumbnails are 192 px; the build uses the full portraits. The back's summary texts are drafts under the same review as the taglines.

## 2026-10-19 — Refinements (Mark): shuffle, find, and a scroll of the whole deck

**Mark's questions.** "Do we have a way to shuffle the deck or find the cards they want in the hand?" and "Is there a way to select from a scroll of all of them, not the hand?" Then: "Yes, do that."

**What changed.** "Deal another hand" is now "Shuffle". A search brings the matching cards into the hand the visitor already holds, first match to the front, instead of replacing the hand; the hand holds up to eight. "See all" opens the whole deck as a sideways scroll under the hand (direction C's row, with buttons and a visible scrollbar); tapping a card there takes it into the hand and brings it to the front. Cards already in the hand are marked in the scroll. The build plan's component row should carry these three behaviours.

**Why.** The hand is what the visitor holds; the scroll is the deck laid out on the table. Both are needed at a hundred worlds, and the scroll is the pattern the standards favour.

## 2026-10-19 — The hand stays as it is (Mark)

**Mark's question.** "What is the 5 hand doing, it looks great, but is it adding real value to a typical user?"

**The assessment given.** The value is mostly in three plainer things the fan carries: five cards instead of eleven or a hundred, so the visitor starts; a fresh deal that puts an unfamiliar tradition in front of someone who came for a familiar name; and a first screen that reads as an invitation without instructions. The costs: the fan hides parts of four cards, a returning visitor must search for a card they held before, and shuffle, deck and "See all" are three more ideas before a conversation. The fan earns its place only if the list fallback and the one-tap path to "Talk with" are flawless and it does not push Theme D's stories down the page.

**Decision (Mark).** "Let's keep it as it is."

**Carried into the build plan.** The build records which control launched each room (hand, scroll, search, Tree), so after the pilot the hand can be judged against a row of five on evidence, not taste.
