## 2026-10-19 — Direction B: a hand dealt from a deck

**Decision (Mark).** "B, hand dealt from a deck." Chosen over A (all eleven in one fan) and C (a row and grid).

**Mark's question that shaped it.** "If we go with a hand of five, how do they get access to the others, especially when we will have 100?" The answer, now in the design: the hand is the front door, never the whole chooser. Behind it sit the deck (the set the hand is dealt from, narrowed by era or by a place or a name the visitor knows, with the count always written) and the library (the Church Family Tree, every tradition on one map, one step away).

**Why B and not A or C.** A hides half the cards at any moment, and the evidence from Apple's collection guidance, Google's own carousel research and the Oslo carousel studies runs against it. C is the pattern the standards describe, but it is the least like the hand Mark likes, and at a hundred worlds it needs the same deck anyway. B keeps the hand at the count where nothing hides, honours Theme D's rotating few, and scales through the deck and the Tree.

**The heart of it.** Mark wants the first screen to feel like being dealt a hand, simple and a little surprising, with the depth one turn away. The deck and the Tree keep that feeling honest at scale: the hand never pretends to be everything.

## 2026-10-19 — Change order (Mark): the card is a collector's card

**Mark's words.** "I want each tradition to have a collectors card, square with the era band across the top, the tradition name a dates, picture and the taglines we just developed in the front and the longer detail to fit on the back of the card, patterned after like baseball cards or pokemon cards, but with a church history aesthetic that matches the portraits." Then: "squarish, dimensions of standard cards."

**What changes in the converged design.** The card front now carries the tagline (the intro line) as well as the portrait, the tradition name and dates, and the era band; the back carries the longer summary, a stat block (years, place, shaped by) and the actions. The hand, the deck, the tray, the fallbacks and the hand-off are unchanged. The build plan's component and card-words rows are updated.

**Why.** The first prototype card looked like an app tile, not a card. A collector's card gives each tradition a thing to hold: the era band and the stat block are the baseball-card grammar, and the parchment, gold rules and serif are the project's own manuscript finish, which the portraits already use.

**Still open from this change.** The portrait thumbnails are 192 px; the build uses the full portraits. The back's summary texts are drafts under the same review as the taglines.

## 2026-10-19 — Refinement (Mark): a current collector's card, not a property card

**Mark's words.** "Really good, but looks a little too much like a Monopoly card, can we make it a little more current, collector cards."

**What changed.** The parchment face and flat band gave way to the grammar of a current collector's card: a full-bleed portrait under a slim era bar, the tradition name and dates over the image, a dark name plate at the foot carrying the Representative's name, title and tagline, a gold-foil edge, and a set number ("06 / 11"). The back keeps the stat block, the longer summary, the record line and the actions on the same dark face. Proportion and square corners unchanged. The prototype template in `Prototype/` carries it.
