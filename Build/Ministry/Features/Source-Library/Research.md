# Research: how the field links citations to primary texts

**Research pass for the Source Library design, 2026-10-14.** Four research streams
(passage-addressing standards; free text databases and their licences; page images and
persistent links; reader design and scholarly trust), synthesised into one report. Evidence
grades are carried on every claim. Most patristic, library and standards hosts were blocked
from the research sandbox, so a large share of the licence wording is snippet-grade and must
be re-read live before it enters a policy document. The design that rests on this is
`Design.md` in this folder.

# Address the Edition, Anchor the Quote, Show the Leaf

Every mature text program that scholars actually cite does the same three things, and none of them is expensive: it names the **work** separately from the **edition**, it addresses a passage by the edition's own divisions (book, chapter, section) rather than by page or by a database row, and it keeps the quoted words verifiable by freezing versions of the text rather than by any cleverness in the citation itself. CTS URNs (2014), the Distributed Text Services API (1.0 released **13 February 2026**), TEI `citeStructure`, Sefaria's `Ref`, and the Bible world's book.chapter.verse all converge on this shape ([CTS URN spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cite-architecture/ctsurn_spec/master/md/specification.md); [DTS site](https://github.com/distributed-text-services/distributed-text-services.github.io)). For Church in Conversation's two vendored corpora the news is mixed but actionable: the CCEL ThML files already carry the edition's own labels in `@type`/`@n` on every `div`, so a canonical address can be computed today, while the archive.org `_djvu.txt` plain text has **zero page boundaries** and can never by itself say which page a quotation sits on; the hOCR and `_page_numbers.json` files on the same items can (verified by direct probe). The strongest free, re-hostable texts are First1KGreek, the Patristic Text Archive, the Digital Syriac Corpus, Coptic SCRIPTORIUM, EEBO-TCP, the World English Bible and open-access CSEL; CCEL and New Advent are link-only unless asked. What scholars demand before citing a digital passage is well documented and short: source edition with page, who made the digital text and how, a quality status word, a licence, a stable identifier with a version or date, and a cite-this template; the empirical literature says editions that omit these are simply cited in print instead. For lay readers the evidence is thin but points one way: trust transfers from the page's visible responsibility and plain framing, and the blockers are hidden features and poor glossaries, not the presence of the primary text.

*Evidence grades carried from the notes: **(verified)** = read directly from the host, repository or by HTTP probe; **(snippet)** or **(secondary)** = taken from a search-engine extract or a third-party page because the host was unreachable from the research sandbox; **(unverified)** = plausible but unconfirmed, test before relying on it; **(inference)** = the notes' own reasoning. Most patristic, library and standards hosts were blocked, so a large share of the licence wording below is snippet-grade and should be re-read live before it enters a policy document.*

## Mature programs address the edition's own divisions, never the page

The Canonical Text Services URN is the clearest statement of the pattern. Its work component is a dotted hierarchy `TEXTGROUP.WORK.VERSION.EXEMPLAR`, where WORK is the abstract text and VERSION is a specific edition or translation, and its passage component is the edition's own citation hierarchy such as `10.1-10.10`, never a page number; word-level subreferences (`10.4@Atreus[1]`) are legal only at version level "because they are inherently language-specific" ([CTS URN spec, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cite-architecture/ctsurn_spec/master/md/specification.md)). Scaife simply puts the URN into the URL path (`scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0011.tlg002.perseus-eng2:1-3/?right=perseus-grc2`), so the URL *is* the citation and a parallel edition is a query parameter ([Scaife Viewer](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0011.tlg002.perseus-eng2:1-3/?right=perseus-grc2)). Version identifiers in the wild increment (`perseus-grc1` to `perseus-grc2`, `1st1K-eng2`) rather than overwrite, which is how a new issue of a text avoids silently changing what an old citation points at (inference from live URNs). The CTS *API* is being retired at Perseus ([scaife-viewer issue #590, verified](https://github.com/scaife-viewer/scaife-viewer/issues/590)), but the URN remains the identifier of record in the Perseus Catalog, Open Greek and Latin, the Patristic Text Archive and Coptic SCRIPTORIUM.

DTS 1.0 is what replaces the CTS protocol, and it deliberately drops the URN requirement: any URL-safe string can identify a resource, a text declares one or more **citation trees** (`citeStructure` with `citeType`), passages are addressed by `ref` or an inclusive `start`/`end` pair over `CitableUnit` identifiers, and partial documents come back as TEI wrapped in `<dts:wrapper>` recording what was asked ([DTS v1.0 spec, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/distributed-text-services/specifications/master/versions/v1.0/README.md)). The feature that matters most here is multiple citation trees: a resource can carry a structural tree as default and a page tree alongside it (`tree=pages&ref=5`), which is the specification's own answer to "internal divisions versus page numbers" — both coexist, and the structural one wins by default. Adoption caveat: the spec repository's `CITATION.cff` still reads "1-alpha" and half the listed implementations are on "1.0 Draft" dialects, so a 2026 adopter should target 1.0 and expect partners on older forms ([CITATION.cff, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/distributed-text-services/specifications/master/CITATION.cff)). Whether the Patristic Text Archive runs a DTS endpoint today could not be confirmed; its roadmap lists "CTS/DTS-API" for 2026 (snippet) ([PTA roadmap](https://pta.bbaw.de/pta/project/roadmap)).

TEI supplies the header-level declaration that teaches software to turn "Matt 5:7" into an XPath. The newer `citeStructure` nests steps with `match`, `use` and `delim`, resolving `Matt 5:7` to `//div[@n='Matt']/div[@n='5']/div[@n='7']` ([TEI source, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TEIC/TEI/dev/P5/Source/Specs/citeStructure.xml); [TEI SA chapter, snippet](https://tei-c.org/release/doc/tei-p5-doc/es/html/SA.html)); the older `refsDecl/cRefPattern` does the same with regular expressions, and CapiTainS makes a TEI file CTS-addressable by requiring exactly one thing, a `refsDecl n="capitains"` whose `xml:base` is the edition URN ([Capitains guidelines, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Capitains/guidelines/master/README.md)). Both assume the citable nodes carry an `@n` equal to the edition's own label. Sefaria's `Ref` is the same idea with the human citation as the key: `Genesis 1:3`, `Rashi on Genesis 1:3`, `Genesis 1:3-2:4` are parsed against a per-text schema and normalised into URLs (`Genesis.1.3`), with editions as separate `Version` records whose `versionTitle` is part of the primary key and selected by query parameter ([Sefaria text.py, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sefaria/Sefaria-Project/master/sefaria/model/text.py)). Bible infrastructure converged decades ago on the same split: OSIS `Gen.1.3`, API.Bible `GEN.1.1` under a separate `{bibleId}` path, STEP `version=ESV|reference=John.3.16-20`, Bible Gateway `search=John+3&version=NIV` ([API.Bible help](https://care.api.bible/article/404-how-api-bible-handles-verse-queries); [STEP URL integration](https://stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TYNSTEP/pages/36569090/Integration+via+the+URL); [Bible Gateway support](https://www.biblegateway.com/support/KA-01444)). Projects whose documents have no canonical divisions — papyri, inscriptions, Syriac works — key on document or work identifiers instead (`trismegistos.org/text/1234`, `syriaca.org/work/694`, `syriaccorpus.org/430`) and lean on shared authority files ([Syriaca URIs, snippet](https://syriaca.org/documentation/uris.html); [Digital Syriac Corpus history, snippet](https://syriaccorpus.org/history.html)).

Two findings bear directly on the vendored CCEL files. First, the ThML `id` values (`iv.viii.i.ii`) are **positional** Roman-numeral sequences, while the edition's own labels live in `@type="Book" n="I"` and `@type="Chapter" n="I"`; the local `npnf203` file shows `<div3 type="Book" n="I" ... id="iv.viii.i">` and `<div4 type="Chapter" ... n="I" id="iv.viii.i.ii"><p id="iv.viii.i.ii-p1">` (verified by reading the vendored file). A citation "Theodoret, *HE* I.1" should therefore resolve through `div3[@type='Book'][@n='I']/div4[@type='Chapter'][@n='I']`, treating the ThML id as a snapshot-local key — exactly what a TEI `citeStructure` expresses. Second, the ThML id still earns its place because it produces the external link: CCEL's own URI note documents `server/ccel/authorID/bookID[.version].format|ID#ID` (snippet) and live pages follow `ccel.org/ccel/<author>/<book>.<divid>.html`, so `iv.viii.i.ii-p1` in `npnf203` resolves to `https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf203.iv.viii.i.ii.html#iv.viii.i.ii-p1` (a well-supported inference, not a CCEL statement) ([CCEL URIs, snippet](https://www.ccel.org/ThML/ccel-URI.htm); [live CCEL URL](https://ccel.org/ccel/anonymous/bcf.vi.iii.html)). ThML itself is dormant — last specification **1.04, January 2003** — so its ids are a stable local key, not a cross-institution convention ([Wikipedia, ThML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theological_Markup_Language)).

On drift, none of the standards reviewed carries a content hash inside the citation. Drift control everywhere is operational: Perseus warns that GitHub file URLs "should NOT be used as Permanent Stable Identifiers" and publishes auto-tagged releases whose notes are a "PASSING FILES" list ([PerseusDL/canonical, verified](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical); [canonical-greekLit releases, verified](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit/releases)); First1KGreek deposits each release on Zenodo tied to a GitHub tag ([Zenodo 2591371](https://zenodo.org/records/2591371)); PTA offers "versioned permalinks" beside the URN permalink (snippet) ([PTA About](https://pta.bbaw.de/en/project/about)); CapiTainS exists so that every push re-validates the citation tree ([Clérice, DHQ 2017, snippet](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/11/4/000350/000350.html)). A small library can match this with a new version id whenever a vendored file changes materially, a tagged snapshot per release, and a check that every stored citation still resolves to the same `@n` path and the same quoted text.

## Archive.org's leaf index is the only page-image key verified end to end

Of the six scan hosts examined, only archive.org could be probed fully, and it turns out to be the best fit for a zero-cost nonprofit. Page URLs of three shapes all return 200: `details/<id>/page/n14` (leaf index, zero-based), `details/<id>/page/15` (printed page label) and `details/<id>/page/leaf14`, with `/mode/2up` and `?q=` to prefill search-inside ([probe, verified](https://archive.org/details/originofspecies00darwuoft/page/n14/mode/2up?q=natural)). The legacy `/stream/<id>#page/n14` form now 301-redirects to `details/<id>?view=theater`, and because `#page` is a client-side fragment whether the reader still honours it is **unverified**. No official help page documenting this URL grammar was found (the obvious help URL 404s), so the shapes are verified by HTTP status only, not by rendering.

The decisive detail is which key ties everything together. The leaf index is shared by the hOCR `ppageno`, the `_page_numbers.json` `leafNum`, the IIIF canvas id (`iiif/<id>$12/canvas`), the DjVu XML `OBJECT` order and the search-inside `page` field — the sample item has **568 OBJECT elements and 568 IIIF canvases** ([`_djvu.xml` probe, verified](https://archive.org/download/originofspecies00darwuoft/originofspecies00darwuoft_djvu.xml); [v3 manifest, verified](https://iiif.archive.org/iiif/3/originofspecies00darwuoft/manifest.json)). The printed-page form is friendlier but rests on IA's automatic page-number detection, which `_page_numbers.json` exposes as probabilistic (`pageProb`, `wordConf`, per-leaf `confidence`, item-level 99 on the sample) ([probe, verified](https://archive.org/download/originofspecies00darwuoft/originofspecies00darwuoft_page_numbers.json)). The plain-text download that CiC has vendored, `_djvu.txt`, is **1,499,250 characters with zero form-feed characters and no other page markers**; `_hocr_searchtext.txt.gz` likewise has none ([probe, verified](https://archive.org/download/originofspecies00darwuoft/originofspecies00darwuoft_djvu.txt)). IA's own developer page says `_djvu.xml` is a modified standard and "the recommendation is to instead parse the hOCR files", which carry one `ocr_page` div per leaf with word-level bounding boxes and confidence, plus a `_hocr_pageindex.json.gz` of byte offsets for seeking ([archive.org/developers/ocr.html, verified](https://archive.org/developers/ocr.html)). The BookReader's internal `fulltext/inside.php` endpoint does the whole mapping server-side and returns bounding boxes, but it lives on a per-item storage host and is not a documented public API ([probe, verified](https://ia800501.us.archive.org/fulltext/inside.php?item_id=originofspecies00darwuoft&doc=originofspecies00darwuoft&path=/35/items/originofspecies00darwuoft&q=natural+selection)).

IIIF makes the page image embeddable without copying it. Manifests at `iiif.archive.org/iiif/<id>/manifest.json` (v3) and `/iiif/2/<id>/manifest.json` (v2) send permissive CORS headers, `info.json` answers with `access-control-allow-origin: *`, the Image API is level 2 with 512-px tiles, and the manifest's `seeAlso` links the page-number JSON and hOCR index ([manifest probe, verified](https://iiif.archive.org/iiif/3/originofspecies00darwuoft/manifest.json); [info.json probe, verified](https://iiif.archive.org/image/iiif/3/originofspecies00darwuoft%2Foriginofspecies00darwuoft_jp2.zip%2Foriginofspecies00darwuoft_jp2%2Foriginofspecies00darwuoft_0015.jp2/info.json)). Loading another institution's manifest into Mirador, Universal Viewer or Clover is routine, documented practice, and the only technical prerequisite is the CORS header the Image API tells servers to send ([IIIF training, secondary](https://training.iiif.io/iiif-1-day-workshop/presentation/view_manifest/)). IA's bots page requires a descriptive User-Agent and back-off on 429 for any server-side fetching, states no numeric cap, and says nothing against embedding ([archive.org/developers/bots.html, verified](https://archive.org/developers/bots.html)); IA "does not assert any new copyright" over its database ([developers portal, verified](https://archive.org/developers/index.html)). The Terms of Use page could not be read, so its linking and embedding clauses are unverified.

The alternatives are weaker for this use. HathiTrust retired its Data API on **17 July 2024** and will cease funding HTRC at the end of 2026; its page viewer sits behind a Cloudflare challenge that returned 403 to scripted requests, HathiTrust itself says the babel URL "is not necessarily permanent" and recommends the Handle `hdl.handle.net/2027/<htid>` with a `urlappend` to jump to a page, and non-US visitors are blocked from "pdus" items — most non-US imprints from roughly 1899 onward, which covers much of the 19th-century European patristic editions (all secondary except the 403 probe and the live Bibliographic API) ([HathiTrust Data API retirement](https://hathitrust.org/press-post/retirement-data-api); [CDL rights explanation](https://cdlib.org/services/consortial-partnerships/hathitrust/hathitrust-rights-copyright-issues/); [Bib API probe, verified](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/api/volumes/brief/oclc/424023.json)). Google Books page links (`books?id=<12 chars>&pg=PA<N>`) have no identifier scheme, no IIIF and no documented persistence policy, and community documentation warns the target "is much more likely to vary over time" when parameters combine; the Books API refused anonymous requests with a zero per-day quota ([Wikipedia template doc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template:Google_books/doc); [API probe, verified](https://www.googleapis.com/books/v1/volumes?q=intitle:%22origin+of+species%22+inauthor:darwin&filter=full&maxResults=3)). Gallica (ARK plus `f<N>.item`, IIIF manifest per ARK, credit "Source gallica.bnf.fr / BnF", non-commercial reuse free), MDZ (`api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/presentation/v2/<bsb id>/manifest`, viewer `?page=`, OCR downloads capped per 24 hours) and e-rara (DOIs `10.3931/e-rara-NNNNN`, IIIF at `i3f/v20/<id>/manifest`, licence per title) are all usable for Reformation-era scans, but none could be reached, so every URL shape there is secondary ([Wikisource:Gallica, verified](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Wikisource:Gallica); [MDZ interfaces, snippet](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/interfaces); [ZB Zürich e-rara IIIF, secondary](https://data.zb.uzh.ch/map/books/data-map-der-zentralbibliothek-zurich/page/e-rara-iiif/export/pdf)).

## A quotation anchor needs exact text plus context; a permalink needs a version

The address above locates a section; it does not prove the quoted words are there. The W3C Web Annotation Data Model's `TextQuoteSelector` is the standard anchor for that: it copies the `exact` text and "some of the text immediately before (a prefix) and after (a suffix) it to distinguish between multiple copies", counted in Unicode code points, and can be paired with a `TextPositionSelector` (start/end offsets) and chained under a `FragmentSelector` naming a paragraph via `refinedBy` ([W3C Annotation Model §4.2.4, verified from the w3c GitHub copy](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/#text-quote-selector)). Hypothesis's production client shows the proven pattern: `describe()` stores a RangeSelector, a TextPositionSelector and a TextQuoteSelector together; `anchor()` tries them in order from simple to complex and throws `'quote mismatch'` when a range or position result does not equal `quote.exact`; `matchQuote()` then falls back to approximate matching scored with **quote weight 50, prefix 20, suffix 20** and returns null below a threshold ([hypothesis/client html.ts, verified](https://github.com/hypothesis/client/blob/main/src/annotator/anchoring/html.ts); [match-quote.ts, verified](https://github.com/hypothesis/client/blob/main/src/annotator/anchoring/match-quote.ts)). For the shareable link itself, URL Text Fragments (`#:~:text=`) are now cross-browser — **Chrome 81, Edge 83, Safari 16.1, Firefox 131 (1 October 2024)** — but remain a WICG draft and fail silently rather than re-anchoring when the text changes, so they should be generated from the stored selector, never hand-written ([caniuse data, verified](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fyrd/caniuse/main/features-json/url-scroll-to-text-fragment.json); [Firefox 131 notes](https://www.mozilla.org/firefox/131.0/releasenotes)). The exact fragment syntax was not read from MDN and should be checked (unverified).

For external pages the Robust Links convention (Hiberlink/LANL) decorates `<a>` with `data-versionurl` (an archived snapshot) and `data-versiondate`, so a rotten or drifted target can fall back to the archived copy (secondary) ([Robust Links spec](https://mementoweb.org/robustlinks/spec/)). The Wayback Machine supplies the snapshots: the Availability API returns the closest capture, Save Page Now 2 accepts authenticated POSTs with a free account, and third-party summaries give limits of roughly 100,000 captures per day authenticated and 10 per URL per day, with a September 2026 report of widespread 429s from new anti-automation protections (all secondary, limits unverified) ([Wayback API](https://archive.org/help/wayback_api.php); [SPN2 public doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Nsv52MvSjbLb2PCpHlat0gkzw0EvtSgpKHu4mk0MnrA/mobilebasic)).

The zero-cost persistent-identifier stack is well defined. An ARK NAAN is free, requested by form and approved manually, with **1,720 ARK organisations** by end-2025 (secondary) ([ARK FAQ](https://arks-stg.cdlib.org/about/ark-faq-en/); [ARK Alliance news](https://arks.org/news/2025-12-15-wrapping-up-2025-with-1720-ark-organizations/)); w3id.org redirects are free by pull request and host nothing ([perma-id/w3id.org README, verified](https://github.com/perma-id/w3id.org)); Zenodo mints a concept DOI plus a DOI per version at no cost (secondary) ([Zenodo versioning help](https://support.zenodo.org/help/en-gb/1-upload-deposit/97-what-is-doi-versioning)). Paid routes are Crossref's new lowest tier at **US$200/year from January 2026**, Handle prefixes with an annual fee whose current amount could not be confirmed, and Perma.cc, free only through a registrar such as an academic library and otherwise from US$10/month on 2021 figures (all secondary) ([Crossref blog](https://crossref.org/blog/some-things-are-big-because-they-are-small-the-new-fee-tier-for-crossref-members-takes-effect/); [Perma FAQ](https://perma.cc/docs/faq)). The TEI Consortium's own citation advice is the honest limit of all of this: cite version number and last-modified date, and "for purposes where a stable reference to specific words is crucial, citing the print version (if any) may be preferable" (secondary) ([TEI: Licensing and Citation](https://old.tei-c.org/?p=21848)). No system examined publishes per-passage checksums; adding one would be a differentiator, not a norm.

Three consequences follow. First, for the Fathers in English, the 19th-century Schaff/Wace translations are public domain but both popular hosts wrap them in their own claims: CCEL's "non-profit purposes" permission arguably covers display by a nonprofit, yet its "republishing requires contacting CCEL for permission" clause means re-hosting CCEL's *files* needs a written ask, and New Advent's blanket LLC notice makes it link-only ([CCEL policy, snippet](https://ccel.org/about/copyright.html); [New Advent, snippet](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3505.htm)). Since ThML files are half of CiC's corpus, that letter to CCEL is a real action item, and the cleaner legal route for any passage CiC wants to display verbatim is to anchor it to a PD scan on archive.org or to Pearse, Wikisource or Gutenberg — noting that Gutenberg blocks deep links by referrer, so passage links into gutenberg.org files will break ([Gutenberg permission page, verified](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html)). Second, the Latin Fathers' PL exists freely only through Corpus Corporum under non-commercial, provider-restricted terms whose authoritative licence page could not be read, so verification quotes for Augustine, Jerome or Cyprian should anchor to open-access CSEL PDFs and OGL's EpiDoc instead, with Corpus Corporum as an external link. Third, ShareAlike on First1KGreek, Perseus and some PTA and Coptic files obliges derivative *texts* to stay CC BY-SA; it does not pull the whole site under that licence, but any corrected text CiC publishes from them must carry it (inference). The persistent *work* identifiers for all of this are already free: Brepols's Clavis Clavium gives CPG/CPL numbers in open access ([Brepolis, snippet](https://about.brepolis.net/clavis-clavium)), Syriaca.org gives `work/NNN` URIs under CC BY 4.0 ([syriaca.org/about, verified](https://syriaca.org/about.html)), and Fribourg's BKV already keys its Latin texts to CPL (`cpl-46`) (snippet) ([BKV](https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpl-46/versions/ad-demetrianum)). Monumenta.ch and Documenta Catholica Omnia's rights remain entirely unverified; DCO has a documented 2009 history of carrying TLG rights notices (snippet) ([Pearse 2009](https://www.roger-pearse.com/weblog/2009/11/18/troubles-at-documenta-catholica-omnia/)).

## Scholars cite what names its source, version and licence; lay readers trust the container

The review criteria, the TEI header and the citation studies agree on one short list. The IDE's "Criteria for Reviewing Scholarly Digital Editions" (v1.1, 2014) and its separate "Criteria for Reviewing Digital Text Collections" (v1.0, 2017, the catalogue that fits a library rather than a critical edition) both put bibliographic identification and citability on the checklist (secondary; hosts blocked) ([IDE criteria](https://www.i-d-e.de/publikationen/weitereschriften/criteria-version-1-1/); [Digital Classicist wiki: RIDE](https://wiki.digitalclassicist.org/RIDE)); the MLA Committee on Scholarly Editions names "transparency, accuracy, appropriateness of method, clear and responsible documentation" (secondary) ([infoDOCKET summary](https://www.infodocket.com/2015/09/18/white-paper-from-a-mla-committee-considering-the-scholarly-edition-in-the-digital-age/)); the TEI header puts the same facts in `publicationStmt/availability`, `editionStmt` and `editorialDecl` ([TEI ref-publicationStmt](https://guidelines.tei-c.de/en/html/ref-publicationStmt.html)). The empirical warning is specific. Blaney and Siefring (2017) found a "culture of non-citation": scholars who used British History Online and EEBO-TCP cited the print instead, and the effect "is particularly prevalent where a print version of the source also exists" ([DHQ 11.1, PDF](https://dhq-static.digitalhumanities.org/pdf/000282.pdf)). Bürgermeister (2023) found roughly **one in four** digital edition projects uses any versioning, judged on "creation of versions for each change, documentation of changes, and availability of previous versions" (secondary) ([Bürgermeister, SIDE 16](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/70452/1/09_Buergermeister.pdf)); Bleier (2021) surveyed editions' permalink and citation strategies and argued that clarity about what to cite "is vital" (secondary; the sample is reported as 100 or 670 in different summaries) ([DHQ 15.3](https://digitalhumanities.org/dhq/vol/15/3/000561/000561.html)).

The corpora nearest CiC's material show what the list looks like on a page. Coptic SCRIPTORIUM's document pages carry title, translator, annotators, manuscript, licence, "version number with date" (e.g. 6.2.0) and a "Cite this Document" block, and its guidelines demand version and date precisely because URNs "resolve to the latest edition of the data" ([citation guidelines, snippet](https://copticscriptorium.org/citation-guidelines); [AP 027 metadata page, snippet](https://data.copticscriptorium.org/urn:cts:copticLit:ap.27.monbeg/norm/html)). PTA pairs a URN permalink with a versioned permalink and commit date in a Reuse tab, and says plainly that some editions are "preliminary ... may contain errors" (snippet) ([PTA documentation](https://pta.bbaw.de/project/documentation)). The Digital Syriac Corpus labels each text "Status: Uncorrected Transcription" with a type ("Print Transcription") and a Corrections button, and its Syriaca entries cite the print edition pages the transcription rests on (snippet) ([syriaccorpus.org/175](https://syriaccorpus.org/175); [Syriaca work 8501](https://syriaca.org/work/8501)). New Advent's "About this page" line is the closest existing model for reused 19th-century translations — "Translated by Frederick Crombie. From Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 4. ... (Buffalo, NY: Christian Literature Publishing Co., 1885.) Revised and edited for New Advent by Kevin Knight" — but it neither says in plain words that the translation is old nor versions anything (snippet) ([New Advent 0412](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0412.htm)). The mass-digitisation projects state accuracy as method rather than per page: TCP's keying target is **99.995%**, DTA's double-keying "99.9%+" (snippet) ([TCP instructions](https://textcreationpartnership.org/docs/dox/instruct3.html); [TEI-C DTA page](https://www.tei-c.org/activities/projects/deutsches-textarchiv-the-german-text-archive/)). For OCR, no standard wording exists; the recurring pattern is HathiTrust's "In most cases, a human hasn't corrected or checked the OCR" and Papers Past's "automatically generated and may contain errors", Trove's visible original/corrected pairing where humans have fixed text, and machine confidence (IA's `wordConf`, hOCR `x_wconf`) that no library surfaces to readers (secondary) ([hathitrust.org/accessibility](https://hathitrust.org/accessibility); [Papers Past](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/AS19000502.2.2); [GLAM Workbench, Trove corrections](https://glam-workbench.net/trove-newspapers/csv-newspapers-corrections/)).

Reader-interface practice adds four transferable patterns. Sefaria separates three things a small project would conflate — UI language (site setting), which text language is shown (reader menu: source, translation, bilingual) and which *version* is shown, each version with its own title, source of acquisition and licence under "About This Text"; it also names its editable modern renderings "Community translations" (CC0) as a different class from "printed editions", though the export shows community versions carry no date or named responsibility (snippet) ([Sefaria licensing help](https://help.sefaria.org/hc/en-us/articles/18490043237148); [Sefaria developers FAQ](https://developers.sefaria.org/docs/general-faq)). The consumer Bible apps converge on a default that keeps a first page readable: Bible Gateway turns footnotes, verse numbers and headings on and cross-references off, with a per-page toggle (snippet) ([Bible Gateway KA-01430](https://search.biblegateway.com/support/KA-01430)); STEP refuses interlinear display on untagged versions and discloses alignment granularity, an honest-labelling move worth copying (snippet) ([STEP Bible Displays](https://stepweb.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SUG/pages/9797667/Bible+Displays)); Logos is the only mainstream tool that shows *how* two renderings differ, with struck-through base text against coloured variants (snippet) ([Logos support](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054863171-Three-Tools-to-Compare-Bible-Translations)). Digital Loeb keeps "content and pagination ... identical to the print" so a page cited from the site is the printed page — a trust device a PD reader can copy cheaply by preserving the source edition's page breaks as visible markers (secondary) ([DTS Loeb guide](https://library.dts.edu/Pages/RM/DBM/Other/loeb-online.pdf)). Scaife's reviewers on three independent surfaces said the same thing about its weakness: powerful functions "somewhat hidden" and "they never wrote any help pages" ([SCS review](https://classicalstudies.org/scs-blog/stephensansom/review-perseus-digital-library-scaife-viewer); [B-Greek thread](https://www.ibiblio.org/bgreek/forum/viewtopic.php?p=30005)).

Direct evidence on lay readers is thin and the notes say so. Pew (2023) finds **21% of US adults** use apps or websites as aids for scripture reading, 47% among Historically Black Protestants and 39% among evangelicals, but measures no trust ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/06/02/use-of-apps-and-websites-in-religious-life/)). The supported claim is indirect: readers judge an embedded source partly by the trustworthiness of the publishing document, and better after training in source evaluation (n=131 undergraduates) ([source-evaluation study, PDF](https://repositorio.uca.edu.ar/bitstream/123456789/16275/1/effect-document-source.pdf)); archive users "judged the authenticity of information by their level of trust in the archivist" ([Yakel](https://www.yakel.people.si.umich.edu/admin.htm)); Perseus's beginners "badly need concise and consistently accurate dictionary entries" and struggle with navigation, and humanities graduate students found usability problems "can seriously hinder use" of digital editions ([Dickinson Classics blog](https://blogs.dickinson.edu/dcc/?p=1346); [Caria & Mathiak, PDF](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/9120/1/12_caria_mathiak.pdf)). No study was found that tests whether showing a primary patristic source with or without plain framing raises or lowers lay trust; that is a genuine hole, not a search failure. On accessibility, WCAG 2.2 (October 2023) is the AA floor and its only reading-level criterion, 3.1.5, sits at AAA and permits a plain-language supplement rather than requiring plain text throughout, so CiC's own B2 target for interface chrome is stricter than any conformance rule ([W3C Understanding 3.1.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/reading-level); [Deque WCAG 2.2](https://dequeuniversity.com/resources/wcag-2.2)).

## Conclusion

The decision in front of CiC is not "which host to link to" but "what a citation record *is*". The field's answer, consistent across CTS, DTS, TEI, Sefaria and the Bible APIs, is a record with four separable parts: a work identifier drawn from a free authority file (CPG/CPL via Clavis Clavium, Syriaca work URIs, OSIS book codes), an edition identifier that increments rather than overwrites when a vendored file changes, a passage address in the edition's own labels (for ThML, `@type` plus `@n` walked as a `citeStructure`, with the positional ThML id kept only as the snapshot-local resolver and the generator of the `ccel.org/.../<book>.<divid>.html#<divid>-p<n>` link), and a quotation anchor stored Hypothesis-style as exact text with prefix and suffix. Everything the app renders — the CCEL deep link, a Text Fragment URL, a Scaife or PTA URN link, an archive.org page link, a cite-this box — is then a view computed from that record, and a nightly re-resolution of every record against the tagged corpus release is the drift check no standard provides syntactically. This also settles the modern-rendering question: a `modern_rendering` is a separate *version* of the same passage, labelled as its own class with its author and date, exactly where Sefaria's "Community translation" labelling is strongest and its export is weakest.

Two things change immediately. The archive.org half of the corpus cannot do page-level linking from the files CiC holds: `_djvu.txt` carries no page boundaries, so the vendored plain text should be rebuilt from `_hocr.html` (seeking with `_hocr_pageindex.json.gz`) and joined to `_page_numbers.json`, giving every quotation a leaf index — the one key shared by the viewer URL, the IIIF canvas and the printed-page map — and letting the passage page show the page image through IA's CORS-enabled IIIF rather than a copied scan. And the CCEL half needs a letter, because "non-profit purposes" and "republishing requires permission" are both in the policy; until that answer comes, CCEL is a deep-link target and the verbatim passage CiC displays should be verified against a PD scan. The passage page itself should carry the seven items the specialist corpora already agree on — print citation beside the permalink, who made the digital text and how, a status word ("digitised from print; uncorrected" or "checked against the page image on <date>"), licence, identifier with version and date, cite-this, and a report-an-error route — written for the lay reader in plain words no surveyed project yet uses ("an 1885 translation"), which would put CiC ahead of the field rather than behind it. The licence wording that drives these choices is largely snippet-grade; before any of it enters a policy document, the CCEL, New Advent, Corpus Corporum, TCP Phase II and Internet Archive terms pages should each be read once in a browser.
