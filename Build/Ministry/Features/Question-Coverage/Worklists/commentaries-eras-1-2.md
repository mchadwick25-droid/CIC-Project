**How the priority is counted.** One point per world-by-sub-question cell that the finished maps (`search/C3`, `C4`, `C5`, `C8` coverage reports) show as a gap, among the ten Era 1 and 2 worlds with records, for the sub-questions the work actually treats. Gap means loaded-only, nothing, only a guard or honest limit (C3, C5, C8), or thin (C4). `p` = cells in a world that voices the author; `c` = cells where the author is context only. The sum is an upper bound. Worlds with no records yet (aec and others) are named but not scored.

## Answer in short

- Origen on Romans goes first. The Library holds only Rufinus's preface and peroration (npnf203). Latin text is in PG 14; every English and critical edition is in copyright.
- The biggest other gaps are the Latin commentaries of Jerome (the Library has his prefaces, not his commentaries, even though a whole world is named for him), Ambrosiaster, Pelagius, and Origen's homilies on Genesis to Leviticus.
- The biggest cheap win is not a download: Chrysostom's full verse-by-verse set on Matthew, John, Acts and every Pauline letter is already vendored in English, but is mapped only to the Antiochene world, which has no records.
- Public-domain English exists for almost none of the Latin and Greek candidates. Cyril of Alexandria on John and Luke is the exception.

## Notes on edges and drift

- The brief says Jerome's commentaries are in npnf206. They are not: that volume has his letters, treatises and the prefaces to his commentaries and translations.
- `augustine_in-ioannis-evangelium-tractatus-lat_migne-garnier1902-t3-pt2.txt` is the whole of PL 35 (Questions on the Gospels, Matthew, 124 Tractates, 1 John, Romans inchoata, Galatians). The queue row for "Migne PL 35" is stale.
- The earlier acquire list asks for Tyconius's Rules; the Latin is vendored (Burkitt 1894). Only English is missing, and it is in copyright.
- Outside the window and excluded: Gregory the Great (Moralia, vendored), Bede, Primasius, Junilius, Cassiodorus, Caesarius, Apringius, Oecumenius, Andrew of Caesarea, Procopius of Gaza, Narsai, Jacob of Serug, Ishodad of Merv. Chromatius of Aquileia (Era 2) has no public-domain edition: the Tractatus on Matthew was found in 1969 and is edited in CCSL 9A (1974).
- Didymus the Blind (Genesis, Zechariah, Psalms, Ecclesiastes) survives in the Tura papyri, edited after 1941, all in copyright. Evagrius's Scholia likewise. Neither is scored.
- Sealed probes and the three-round review cap were not touched. Nothing here changes a canonical record.
