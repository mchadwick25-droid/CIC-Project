# Commentaries and homily series, Eras 3 to 6 (451-1517): what the Library holds, what is queued, what exists in public domain

Read-only research, 26 October 2026. Nothing in the repo was changed, no model call, no download. Editions come from my own knowledge; where I was not sure of an edition, a host or a licence I wrote **unverified**. Hosts were not tested (the sandbox blocks them), so every URL-type claim for a work not already vendored or queued is from memory.

## Counts

- 119 works listed. Vendored in full: 8. Vendored in part (a commentary sits inside a larger file, or only one volume): 11. Not vendored: 100.
- 17 of the 119 have a public-domain English text (vendored or findable). For most others only the Latin, Greek or Syriac is public domain and the modern English is in copyright.
- No record in any world cites any of the vendored Era 3-6 commentary files. Era 3-6 has no built world; the three with documents are rcg (Gregorian Rome), wsyr (West Syriac) and hus (Hussites), none with records yet.
- WANTS-REGISTER (154 sources, 11 worlds, all Eras 1-2 or 7) holds no Era 3-6 commentary. The download queue holds 3 Era 3-6 commentary rows (Wyclif Opus evangelicum; Hus in Flajshans; Gerson in Du Pin) plus 4 adjacent sermon or study rows.

## Method for the priority score

Priority = worlds served x sub-questions. **Worlds served**: the 90 worlds in ATLAS-TARGETS for Eras 3-6, counted when the work belongs to the world's tradition (Latin West, Byzantine Greek, East Syriac, West Syriac, Armenian, Slavic and so on) and its reception window overlaps the world's dates. A window runs from composition to last documented use, and to 1517 only for school-standard books such as the Glossa and Lyra. I narrowed regional works by hand (for example Aelfric serves only late Anglo-Saxon England). **Sub-questions**: ids in `07-subquestions.yaml` (129 in all) that the books covered can speak to. Example: Romans carries C4.S1 to C4.S6 (faith and works, original sin, election, assurance, baptism, the unevangelised), C9.S3, C11.S1, C11.S5, C8.S6, C5.S8. Whole-Bible works carry 63. The built-world gap maps (C1 to C16) cover only Eras 1-2 and Era 7 worlds, so no Era 3-6 world could be scored from a finished map; every score is the unbuilt-world count.

Read the score as an upper bound on cells the text could speak to, not as cells it has been shown to answer. It favours Latin works because 38 of the 90 Era 3-6 worlds are Latin, against 10 Greek and 6 or fewer for each Eastern tradition. The second list below ranks the English-ready ones separately for that reason.

## Corrections to the brief

- **Gregory of Narek's Lamentations is not a commentary.** The Book of Lamentations is 95 prayers. His commentary is on the Song of Songs. I list both and flag the first.
- **Hus on Romans:** I could not find a Hus commentary on Romans. His exposition work is the Czech Postilla (vendored, Erben vol. 2), the expositions of Creed, Decalogue and Lord's Prayer (vol. 1) and the Song of Songs exposition (vol. 3). The Romans lecturer of this era is Colet (1497-99); Lefevre d'Etaples wrote on Paul in 1512.
- **Lombard's Sentences gloss:** I read this as the Magna Glossatura on the Psalms and Paul plus the Sentences. The Sentences themselves are theology.
- **Erasmus up to 1517:** only the 1516 Novum Instrumentum and Annotations, and the 1517 Paraphrase on Romans, fall in Era 6. The rest is Era 7.
- **In copyright in English:** Ibn at-Tayyib (no PD text found at all), Theophylact (Stavropoulos), Andreas and Oecumenius (FOTC), Aquinas on Matthew, John, Romans and Job (Magi, Damico), Bede's commentaries, Rupert, Lombard, Cassiodorus, Gregory on Ezekiel. For these only the original is PD. Barhebraeus Scholia on the OT (Sprengling and Graham 1931) is not PD until 2027.
- **Named in the brief but not found in the Library or the queue:** Theophylact, Euthymius, Oecumenius, Andreas, Arethas, Photius' Amphilochia, Ishodad, Bar Salibi, Jacob of Edessa, Narsai's OT homilies, Barhebraeus' Storehouse, Vardan, Ethiopic commentaries. All appear in the table as not vendored.
