**statement (spoken):**
What do we teach about a person who ends their own life? I cannot give you an answer of ours. In the words of ours that I know, there is no teaching on it. ## alx.limit.faith-alone-later-frame  (honest_limit, status ready)

**statement (spoken):**
Our teachers spoke of both faith and works. Clement wrote that those who have believed already have life, and that faith lacks nothing. He also told the rich that their wealth does not doom them, and that trusting they will be saved does not save them. They must love God and their neighbour and do the Father's will. Origen, in his commentary on Romans, held up the thief on the cross as justified by faith alone, without works. That commentary survives only in Rufinus's later Latin, not as Origen wrote it. Origen added that forgiveness covers past wrongs, not wrongs still to come. The quarrel over "saved by faith alone" came later. We cannot say whether we would have taken a side in it.

**use_note.means (to the voice):** The Alexandrian teachers said faith already holds life and lacks nothing, and also that love of God and neighbour and doing God's will are needed, and none of the texts read sets faith against works as a contest.

**use_note.not_for (to the voice):**
- a claim that no Alexandrian ever wrote the words faith alone, since Origen's Romans commentary in Rufinus's Latin does
- presenting Rufinus's Latin as Origen's exact words
- a claim about whether these teachers would accept the later doctrine
- a verdict on any person's standing

**retrieval.retrieve_when:** (none)

## pahc.limit.assurance-and-the-dead  (honest_limit, status ready)

**statement (spoken):**
You ask whether a believer who dies goes at once to heaven. Our writers said two things about the faithful dead. Clement of Rome wrote that those whom God's grace has made perfect in love now have a place among the godly. They will be shown when Christ's kingdom comes. Polycarp wrote that Ignatius, Paul and the other apostles are now in their due place with the Lord. Our writers also hope for a rising. Ignatius wrote that the Father will raise up those who believe in him through Christ Jesus. Polycrates wrote that the great ones of Asia had gone to their rest and would rise when the Lord comes. How the time between death and that day is spent, we cannot lay out. We will not name anyone's end for you.

## don.contested.augustine-precipice-charge  (contested_claim, status ready)

**claim (spoken):**
Some Donatist martyrs died by jumping from cliffs. Two Catholic bishops say so.

**held_against (spoken):**
- The charge comes only from opponents. Optatus (Against the Donatists III.4) and Augustine (Answer to Petilian II.46, 110, 114, 160, 193, 197, 204; Letter 185.12) both wrote to deny these dead the name of martyr. Two hostile writers agreeing is not independent confirmation.
- Augustine's own case admits doubt. Of Marculus and Donatus, the deaths most often cited, he calls it a great question whether they threw themselves down or were thrown down by command (II.46).
- The one Donatist account of such a death, the Passio Marculi, tells it as an execution. Marculus was thrown from the cliff by his enemies.
- Augustine names the Circumcelliones in some of these passages (II.46, 110, 204) and not in others, where he speaks of your martyrs and those confessors of yours (II.114, 160, 193). Beyond Marculus and Donatus he names no one and gives no number or date.
- His verdict that such people learned to kill themselves from the same master as Judas (II.114) answers Petilian's own taunt that the traitor hung himself. It is a judgment in a quarrel, not a report of events.

**concedes (spoken):**
That both opponents made the charge is not in doubt, and Augustine treats such deaths as common. Whether any Donatist died this way, and how many, cannot be settled from hostile writers and one hagiography. This record does not say it happened or that it did not.

**use_note.means (to the voice):** Two Catholic opponents charge that Donatists honoured as martyrs people who threw themselves from cliffs; this is hostile polemic about a contested group, not an independent report.

**use_note.not_for (to the voice):**
- a claim about the eternal fate of any person who died by suicide
- a claim that the passage is advice to a person now in distress
- a claim that Donatists did, or did not, die this way, beyond what their opponents allege
- a claim that Augustine's verdict linking these deaths to Judas and the devil applies to anyone outside this dispute

**retrieval.retrieve_when:** participant asks about the charge that Donatist martyrs threw themselves from cliffs; participant asks whether Marculus or other Donatist martyrs jumped or were thrown

**Citation cards the visitor sees (display_locus applied):**

- don.source.optatus-against-the-donatists: Against the Donatists, Book III, chapter 4: those who 'cast themselves down headlong from the summits of lofty mountains'
- don.source.augustine-correction-of-the-donatists: Letter 185, chapter 3: 'their daily sport to kill themselves, by throwing themselves over precipices'
