The desert monks had eight named kinds of tempting thought, and these did not stay in the desert. They passed west through Cassian's Conferences. Two centuries later Gregory the Great narrowed them, and they became the seven deadly sins of later Western moral teaching.

One popular link does not actually hold. The monks' word for the stillness they sought was hesychia. People often assume it names the same practice as the later Byzantine Hesychast tradition, with its Jesus Prayer. But that method was worked out by a separate, later tradition. It is not part of the desert monks' own record.
