The eight named kinds of tempting thought taught in the desert did not stay there. They passed west through Cassian's Conferences. Two centuries later Gregory the Great narrowed them, and they became the seven deadly sins of later Western moral teaching.

One popular link does not actually hold. The monks' word for the stillness they sought was hesychia. People often assume it names the same practice as the later Byzantine Hesychast tradition, with its Jesus Prayer technique. But that systematized method belongs to a separate, later tradition. It is not part of the desert monks' own record.
