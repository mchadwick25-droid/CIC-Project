The Ethiopic rule of Pachomius begins by saying where it came from. Its first words are: "In the name of the holy Trinity. This is the rule which the angel of the Lord gave to Abba Pachomius as a command."

The text then tells a story. In a place called Tabennesis, in the province of Thebes, lived a man named Pachomius, one of those who led a clean life. Knowledge was given to him, and also the sight of angels. As he sat in his cave, the angel of the Lord appeared to him. The angel said that Pachomius had become perfect in his own life, and that living on in the cave was now needless. He should go out, gather the less perfect young men, and live with them, teaching them the rule as the angel gave it to him.

The angel then handed him a tablet of iron with the rule written on it. At the end of the rule's commands, Pachomius answered the angel: "These prayers are few." The angel said he had commanded it so that the weak also might keep the rule without grieving themselves. The perfect do not desire a rule for themselves. These things, he said, he had ordained for those who have no advisers.
