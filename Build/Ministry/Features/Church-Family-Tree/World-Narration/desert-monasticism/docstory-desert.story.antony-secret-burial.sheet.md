# Sheet: desert-monasticism / docstory-desert.story.antony-secret-burial

## Original text (as narrated now)

Athanasius writes that Antony visited the monks of the outer mountain and told them it was his last visit. He said he would be surprised if they met again in this life, and that he was near a hundred and five years old.

The monks begged him to stay and die among them. He would not, and above all for one reason. The Egyptians wrapped the bodies of good men in linen and kept them on couches in their houses. Antony had often urged the bishops to command the people against this, and he had taught that it was neither lawful nor holy.

He went back to the inner mountain, where he was used to living, and a few months later he fell sick. Two men had stayed with him there for fifteen years, serving him because of his age. He called them and told them to let no one take his body into Egypt. "Bury my body and hide it underground yourselves," he said. "Keep my words, so that no one may know the place but you alone."

He told them to divide his garments. One sheepskin and the garment he lay on were for the bishop Athanasius. The other sheepskin was for the bishop Serapion, and the two men were to keep the hair garment. Then he said farewell, and died.

They buried him as he had commanded. Athanasius writes that to this day no one knows where he lies, except those two.

### desert.story.antony-secret-burial (records/desert/story/desert.story.antony-secret-burial.md)
- formation_confidence: Widely Accepted
- divergence_note: Widely Accepted for the narrative's existence and general content within the Vita, composed within a few years of Antony's death - matching desert.story.antony-call's own basis for material from the same Vita - by an author who appears in the account himself: SS91 has Antony order one sheepskin and the garment he lay on given to "Athanasius the bishop." SS91-92 do not say that the author received them or that he heard the account from the two attendants; SS92 says only that no one but those two knows the burial place. Contested as to incident-level historical reliability specifically. This record does not treat the later, out-of-horizon relic traditions (a 561 'discovery' and translation, later moves to Constantinople and France) as continuous with what Antony himself asked for - the Vita itself already states no one but the two attendants knew the site when it was written.
- absent_detail: The two attendants are never named, and no account survives in their own words of how they experienced carrying out this instruction, or of the years afterward when they alone knew where the desert's most famous monk was buried. Later traditions claim the site was 'discovered' in 561 and the body eventually moved to Alexandria and then France - traditions that fall outside this world's own c. 320-430 window and that the Vita's own instructions would seem to rule out.
