# Sheet: desert-monasticism / docstory-desert.story.antony-withdrawal

## Original text (as narrated now)

Antony left the world in stages. First he lived near his own village, as all did who wished to train in those days. He had seen an old hermit in the next village and copied his devotion.

Later he found a fort on the far side of the river. It had stood empty so long that it was full of creeping things. Antony crossed over and lived there, and the creatures, Athanasius wrote, fled at once as if someone were chasing them. He built up the entrance completely and stored six months of loaves. He found water inside. He stayed alone, never going out and never looking at anyone who came, and loaves were let down to him from above twice a year.

Friends who came were not let in. They often spent days and nights outside, and they heard what sounded like crowds clamoring within.

Nearly twenty years later he came out, and in time, Athanasius says, he was beset by many people and could not be still. Then a voice came to him from above and told him that, if he truly wanted quiet, he should go into the inner desert. He journeyed three days and three nights with Saracens, desert nomads, who were going that way. He came to a very lofty mountain, with a clear spring at its foot, a plain, and a few uncared-for palm trees. He recognized the place as his own home and stayed.
