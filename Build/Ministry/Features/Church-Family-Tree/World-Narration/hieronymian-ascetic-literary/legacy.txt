The circle's own record does not follow Jerome's central labor past his lifetime. It shows an argument that was still open when he died.

Within a few years, a respected fellow scholar, Augustine, made the strongest case for the other side, in writing. He agreed with every point of the Hebrew scholarship, yet he still refused the conclusion. The churches of Christ, he wrote, should trust the old Greek translators, not one modern scholar's own work, however sound.

Did the Hebrew-based translation ever become the circle's own settled Bible? Its surviving writings cannot answer that question. They end with the argument still unresolved.
