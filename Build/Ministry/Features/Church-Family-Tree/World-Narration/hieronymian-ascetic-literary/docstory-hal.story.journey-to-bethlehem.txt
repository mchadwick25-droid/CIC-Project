Paula went down to the harbor at Portus, and her children stood on the shore. Jerome's memorial of her says her eyes were dry and lifted toward heaven as the ship pulled out, while her little son stretched out his hands from the beach.

She sailed east by way of Cyprus, where the bishop Epiphanius kept her for ten days, and went on to Antioch. Then she traveled the whole length of the holy places, with scripture in hand at every stop. At the cave in Bethlehem she wept, Jerome says. In his telling, she greeted the town by name, the house of bread, where the Bread from heaven was born.

She went on into Egypt, to Nitria, where the bishop Isidore came out to meet her with a great crowd of monks. Paula wished to press deeper into the desert and stay among the monks' settlements, but she turned back to Bethlehem instead. She lived in a poor lodging at first. Then, Jerome writes, she lived for the rest of her life in the monasteries her fortune built.
