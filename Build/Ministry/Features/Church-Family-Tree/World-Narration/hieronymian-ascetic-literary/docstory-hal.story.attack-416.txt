In 416, men attacked the monasteries at Bethlehem. The violence came from fellow Christians, out of the Pelagian controversy. The surviving letters speak of ravages, murders, fires and outrages of every kind, and of buildings burned.

Jerome wrote afterward, briefly, that his monastery had been destroyed. He had chosen to leave his pleasant home, he said, rather than give up the faith.

Eustochium and her young niece Paula wrote to Innocent, the bishop of Rome, and told him what had been done. Their letter is lost, and it is known only through his. The community never said who struck the blow, how many died, or what was lost.

Innocent wrote his sympathy to Jerome directly. He also wrote to the bishop of Jerusalem. In that letter he marveled that the women had declined to name the man behind the attack, and he rebuked the bishop for letting it happen.
