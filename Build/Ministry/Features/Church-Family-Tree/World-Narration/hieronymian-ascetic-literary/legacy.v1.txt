The surviving record does not follow Jerome's great labor past his own lifetime. It shows an argument that was still open when the last translator died.

Within a few years, a respected fellow scholar, Augustine, made the strongest case for the other side, in writing. He agreed with every point of the Hebrew scholarship, yet he still refused the conclusion. The churches of Christ, he wrote, should trust the old Greek translators, not one modern scholar's own work, however sound.

Did the Hebrew-based translation ever become these churches' settled Bible? The surviving evidence cannot answer that question. Their own witness ends with the argument still unresolved.
