A widow on the Aventine hill in Rome once turned down a rich old suitor. If she had wished to marry again, she told him, she would have looked for a husband, not an inheritance.

Her standing comes down to us in one man's memorial. Jerome wrote it when she was two years dead, and her letters to him are lost.

When Jerome came to Rome, she pressed him with questions on scripture until his reluctance gave way. She never took an answer without disputing it, he wrote. Her aim, he said, was to learn what could be said against his answers. His own letters show that she asked hard questions, and more than a dozen of his surviving answers were written to her.

After he left for Bethlehem, the memorial says, people turned to her when a dispute about scripture arose in Rome, and she settled it. Many men brought her hard questions, he adds, and priests were sometimes among them. She gave her opinions as if they came from him or from someone else, not from herself. In this, he says, she was mindful of the apostle's rule.

Later, in his telling, Marcella was the one who set in motion the condemnation of the heretics in Rome.
