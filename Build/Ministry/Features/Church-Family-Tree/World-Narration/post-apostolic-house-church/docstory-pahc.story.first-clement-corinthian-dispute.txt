The church at Corinth had removed some of its presbyters, its elders, from office, and the church at Rome answered with a long letter. The letter names no author, though later tradition says Clement wrote it. It is usually dated around 96, though it may be as late as 140.

Rome's letter says the presbyters had served blamelessly and with honor. Only Rome's letter survives, so Corinth's own side of the dispute is lost.

The letter takes up the removal directly. It gives example after example of jealousy and strife destroying what unity had built. Rome wrote as one church to another, out of concern, and it could only appeal. It gave no orders the way a later church authority would.

Corinth's action, the letter argues, broke proper order, and it calls on those who started the revolt to submit to the presbyters.
