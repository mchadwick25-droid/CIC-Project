In the letters that carry his name, Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, says that he was condemned and sent under armed guard on the long road to Rome. There he expected to be thrown to the beasts in the arena. It is not certain that he wrote the letters himself. They may have been written later, in Rome, under his name.

He says he was bound to ten leopards, by which he means his band of soldiers. They only grew worse, he says, when they were treated kindly. Churches came out to meet him along the way, and at Smyrna he was met by delegations from Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles.

On the road he wrote at least seven letters that survive. He urged each community to gather around one bishop and one eucharist, and he pressed the point hard because that unity was still being argued over. He believed his coming death gave his words unusual weight.

He asked Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, to write for him to the nearby churches. He thanked the church at Smyrna for welcoming his companions and for not being ashamed of his chains. He sent a letter ahead to the church at Rome, asking them not to step in to save his life. He called himself the wheat of God, and he asked to be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, so that he might be found the pure bread of Christ.
