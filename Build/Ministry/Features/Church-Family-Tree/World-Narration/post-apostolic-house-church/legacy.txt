The question of who held authority in the churches did not stay open forever. By around 200 CE, one bishop had become the common pattern almost everywhere, including in Rome. Rome had once been led by a council of elders. No text says how or when that change was actually felt. All that can be seen is that the older way quietly dropped out of the argument.

The texts of these churches did not survive evenly. Ignatius argued for the single bishop, and it took a two-century scholarly fight to settle which of his letters were genuine. Some scholars still doubt them. First Clement was copied into a great Bible manuscript. The Shepherd of Hermas was once read as scripture by three major later teachers, but it was later moved to the shelf newcomers were given to read. That was a real demotion, though not the outright ban later memory sometimes assumes. The Didache, a teaching manual from one of these communities, disappeared from use for roughly eighteen centuries. What survived best was not always what mattered most at the time. It may have been what a later settlement found useful to keep.
