Years later, at his own table, Luther told his students how he was summoned to Augsburg in the summer of 1518. He had attacked the sale of indulgences, and the Pope's legate, Cardinal Cajetan, was to hear his case. Frederick, the Elector of Saxony, gave him a strong escort and a safe-conduct, and he was warned not to trust the Italians.

He waited three days for the Emperor's own safe-conduct. Meanwhile an Italian led him to Cardinal Cajetan, urging him the whole way to say one word, Revoco, which means I recant. Say it, the man promised, and the Cardinal would commend him to the Pope's favor.

Before the Cardinal, Luther said, he knelt and then went all the way down to the ground. He stayed there until Cajetan told him three times to rise. The Cardinal was pleased, he said, and hoped Luther would think better of things.

The next day Luther came again and would not recant anything. Writing to Carlstadt, he named two points. An indulgence, he said, is not the treasure of Christ's merits. And whoever comes to the Lord's table must believe.

In Luther's memory, the Cardinal asked whether he thought the Pope cared for Germany, or that princes would raise armies to defend him. Where, Cajetan asked, would he be safe then?

"Under Heaven," Luther answered.
