# Sheet: lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations / docstory-witt.story.worms-1521

## Original text (as narrated now)

At Erfurt, on the road to Worms, a herald asked Luther whether he still meant to go on. Word had spread that he was condemned.

So Luther told it, years later, to the students at his table. He had been summoned to the Diet under an imperial safe-conduct, and within a day of his leaving it was broken. His books were burned at Worms before he arrived. He answered the herald that he would go, "although in Worms there were as many devils as there are tiles on the houses."

Before the Emperor and Electors, Dr. Eck asked whether the books on the table were his. He said they were his. Some were books of doctrine, and those he could not recant. He asked for time to consider, and was given one day and one night.

The next day the Bishops pressed him. God's Word, he told them, was not his word, so he did not know how to give it away. In whatever did not touch the Holy Scriptures, he would obey.

Word reached the Emperor, wrongly, that he had recanted. A Bishop came to ask if he had left his cause to the Emperor. He had not, Luther said, and never meant to.

### witt.story.worms-1521 (records/witt/story/witt.story.worms-1521.md)
- formation_confidence: Widely Accepted
- divergence_note: Widely Accepted that the Diet, the summons, and the refusal to recant occurred substantially as Luther later described them; Contested for specific wording, including the sentence most people know this story by. This library's own Table Talk text has Luther say, of the journey itself, "although in Worms there were as many devils as there are tiles on the houses, yet, God willing, I will go thither" (verified at this locus) - not "Here I stand, I cannot do otherwise." That famous sentence, and a second, differently-worded "tile roofs" rendering of the journey saying, both reach this library only as quoted together, at one remove, inside Bacon's Introduction to the hymns, attributed there to Carlyle's transmission - not this library's own Table Talk text. Any use of either Carlyle-transmitted phrase must carry that provenance and disclose that its verbatim authenticity is contested in the wider scholarship.
- absent_detail: This library's own text of Luther's retelling does not include the words "Here I stand, I cannot do otherwise" in any form, at the moment of his final refusal or anywhere else - it does go on to quote his actual answer the next day at length ("God's Word is not my word..." and the exchange that followed), just not that particular sentence, in that particular wording. The famous sentence reaches this library only through a much later writer's translation, quoted inside a hymn-book editor's introduction, and its own authenticity is disputed by scholars this library has not independently read.
- tellable_as: Warned on the road that he is already condemned at Worms, Luther answers that he will go anyway, "although in Worms there were as many devils as there are tiles on the houses." Warned again that he may be burned, he goes on. Before the Emperor and the assembled Empire he says he cannot give up his books of doctrine. The next day, before the Bishops, he says he will accept instruction in everything except what touches the Holy Scriptures.
