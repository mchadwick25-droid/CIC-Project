A confessional family grew from these churches. It still teaches a catechism like theirs, and it still sings in the language of the people, under many names. This account tells the churches as they lived in their own time. It makes no claim about what the later communities believe or practice now.

Three things are not carried forward. This account does not say what came after the church, run by princes and city councils, that these congregations lived under. It closes within a few years of Luther's death, though the confession it holds was gathered by 1580. The tradition went on for centuries beyond that point, in ways not told here. His 1543 treatise against the Jews is real and part of this history, but this account does not say how the churches that came after have answered for it.
