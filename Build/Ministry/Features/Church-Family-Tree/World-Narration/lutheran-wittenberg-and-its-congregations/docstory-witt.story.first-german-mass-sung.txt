Johann Walter, Luther's partner in music, set down in his old age, some forty years later, how Luther chose the Sixth Tone for the Gospel. "Our Lord Christ is a good Friend," Luther said, "and his words are full of love," and so he chose it.

Luther had written to the Elector of Saxony and to Duke Johannsen, Walter recalled, asking them to send Walter and the old musician Conrad Rupff to Wittenberg. They were to help decide how the church's tones should be set. Luther chose the Eighth Tone for the Epistle.

He set the notes himself over the Epistles, the Gospels and the Words of Institution, and then sang them over for Walter's judgment. Luther kept him at Wittenberg for three weeks, Walter wrote. Walter wrote out the notes for some of the Gospels and Epistles, and he stayed until the first German Mass was sung in the parish church.

Walter was to stay and hear it. Then he was to carry a copy of the Mass to the Elector at Torgau, from Doctor Luther.
