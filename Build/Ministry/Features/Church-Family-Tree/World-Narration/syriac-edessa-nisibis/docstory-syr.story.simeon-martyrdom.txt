"As time went on, the Christians grew in number. They began to form churches, and they appointed priests and deacons." So Sozomen, a Greek historian of the fifth century, opens his account of the persecution in Persia. The Magi, the priestly guardians of the Persian religion, grew deeply angry.

Simeon was bishop of Seleucia and Ctesiphon, the royal cities of Persia, and he was accused of being a friend of the Roman emperor. King Shapur laid heavy taxes on the Christians, hoping poverty would force them to give up their faith. The year of what followed is disputed: 341, or about 344.

The tradition tells that Simeon was brought before the king in chains and would not bow down. He said he stood there now in defense of godliness and of the Christian faith. Shapur offered him gifts and then threatened to destroy him and all the Christians, but each time Simeon refused to worship the sun or betray his religion.

Usthazanes, the king's aged foster-father, rose to honor Simeon as he was led to prison. Usthazanes had been a Christian but had given way and worshipped the sun, and the bishop rebuked him and turned his face away. Usthazanes then confessed himself a Christian before the king, and Shapur had him beheaded.

The next day Simeon again refused to worship the king or the sun. That day a hundred other prisoners were put to death while he watched, and Simeon was killed last of all, with two aged priests of his own church.
