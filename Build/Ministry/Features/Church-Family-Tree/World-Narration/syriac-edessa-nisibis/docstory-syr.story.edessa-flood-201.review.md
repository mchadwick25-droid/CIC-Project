## Findings

- MINOR. "King Abgar stood on the great tower and watched the water by torchlight, and that same hour the waters broke down the western wall and swept through the city, overthrowing the king's palace." At 33 words this is the longest sentence in the piece, and the join was not needed. Replace with the original's two sentences: "King Abgar stood on the great tower and watched the water by torchlight. That same hour the waters broke down the western wall and swept through the city, overthrowing the king's palace."
- MINOR, on the quoted span (asked to check): "And they destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians." This follows Cowper word for word (cic/texts/chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt, line 53), is identical to the original, and is the `text` field of both quote records named (syr.quote.the-temple-of-the-church-of-the-christians, syr.quote.chronicle-flood-line). It is not their modern_rendering ("And they destroyed the church building of the Christians."), and syr.quote.chronicle-flood-line's own lens note warns that "temple" may be heard as a non-Christian shrine. It may stand: Kit Part 1 lets older wording stand when the narrator gives its plain sense alongside, and the narrator does so ("a church building stood in Edessa to be destroyed"), while "of the church of the Christians" in the span itself rules out a pagan temple. The cost is that the entry is then heard in two Englishes, Cowper's for the first line and the modern rendering for the rest. If Mark prefers one register, the exact swap is: "And they destroyed the church building of the Christians."
