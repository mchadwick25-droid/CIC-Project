The Paradise is the Syriac version of a book by Palladius of Galatia. Its chapter on Ephrem opens with one dream and closes with another, though the Greek-based text of the same chapter has neither. The Syriac chapter does not name the first dreamer.

The Paradise says one of the holy fathers saw a dream in which the holy angels came down from heaven at God's command. One of them held a roll written on the inside and on the outside, and the angels asked each other who was fit to be trusted with it.

Some angels named one man, some another, and each time the answer was that these were holy and righteous, but not enough for this. They counted up many names of saints. At last they said that no one was fit for it except Ephrem, and they gave him the roll.

When the dreamer got up in the morning, he heard people saying that Ephrem was teaching. His words, they said, flowed from his mouth like water from a fountain. Then, it says, the old man knew within himself that the words coming from Ephrem's lips were of the Holy Spirit.

The second dream is one Ephrem himself is said to have had as a youth. In it, a vine grew out of his tongue and filled the whole space beneath the heavens, loaded with clusters of grapes. All the birds of heaven came and ate from it, and the whole time they ate, the clusters grew in number and in size.
