Ephrem's fame reached the Greek church in his own century. Sozomen, a Greek historian of the next century, writes that Basil greatly admired him and was astonished at his learning. Basil later became bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia. Palladius of Galatia and Jerome both name Ephrem a deacon of the church at Edessa.

A later age built the story of a meeting on a passage in Basil's own writings. There Basil credits an explanation of Scripture to an unnamed Syrian, who, he says, knew little of worldly wisdom but much of the knowledge of the Truth.

Later tradition took that Syrian to be Ephrem. But Basil never names him, and he may well be another man, Eusebius of Emesa. The meeting itself is a later legend.

The later Life of Ephrem tells the story with love for both men. In it, a vision prompted the poet to travel to Caesarea to see the great Basil. The two holy men had no common language, yet they recognized each other in the Spirit. And Basil himself, the Life says, ordained Ephrem a deacon.
