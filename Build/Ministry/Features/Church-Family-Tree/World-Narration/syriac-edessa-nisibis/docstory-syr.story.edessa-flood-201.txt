In November of the year 513 of the Greeks, around the year 201, the river Daisan rose against Edessa in the night. King Abgar stood on the great tower and watched the water by torchlight. That same hour the waters broke down the western wall and swept through the city, overthrowing the king's palace.

The Chronicle of Edessa sets down this account. It was compiled about 340 years later from the archives where the royal scribes had filed it, and no bishop or believer is named in it.

One line among the losses names the church. "And they destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians."

The entry goes on. "More than two thousand men were killed in that event. The waters came upon many of them suddenly as they slept in the night, and they drowned. The city was filled with the sound of mourning."
