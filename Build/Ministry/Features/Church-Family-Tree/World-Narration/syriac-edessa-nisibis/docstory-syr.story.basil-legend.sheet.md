# Sheet: syriac-edessa-nisibis / docstory-syr.story.basil-legend

## Original text (as narrated now)

Ephrem's fame reached the Greek church in his own century. Sozomen, a Greek historian of the next century, writes that Basil greatly admired him and was astonished at his learning. Basil later became bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia. Palladius of Galatia and Jerome both name Ephrem a deacon of the church at Edessa.

A later age built a meeting on a passage in Basil's own writings. There Basil credits an explanation of Scripture to an unnamed Syrian. He says the Syrian knew little of worldly wisdom but much of the knowledge of the Truth.

Later tradition took that Syrian to be Ephrem. Basil never names him, and he may well be another man, Eusebius of Emesa. The meeting itself is a later legend.

The later Life of Ephrem tells the story with love for both men. A vision prompted the poet to travel to Caesarea to see the great Basil. The two holy men had no common language, yet they recognized each other in the Spirit. And Basil himself, the Life says, ordained Ephrem a deacon.

### syr.source.basil-hexaemeron (records/syr/source/syr.source.basil-hexaemeron.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: AN OUTSIDE WITNESS, IN GREEK, WHO DOES NOT NAME HIS SOURCE. Basil says the best reading of Genesis 1:2 is not his own but "that of a Syrian", and never says which Syrian. The volume's own nineteenth-century apparatus records the disagreement rather than settling it - Tillemont takes him for Eusebius of Samosata, the Benedictine editors prefer Ephrem Syrus - and syr.story.basil-legend, on modern scholarship, names a third man, Eusebius of Emesa. Three candidates, no evidence in the text. This world may use the passage for what Basil says about Syriac and about the man's learning; it may not use it to identify him.

### syr.quote.basil-credits-a-syrian (records/syr/quote/syr.quote.basil-credits-a-syrian.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: The Syrian is unnamed and stays unnamed. No record may resolve this from the passage, which supplies no name.
