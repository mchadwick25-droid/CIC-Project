This is the story these churches told of their own beginning.

King Abgar of Edessa was sick when his envoys came home from Jerusalem with news of what Jesus was doing. The king wrote Jesus a letter. He had heard, he wrote, that Jesus healed without medicines or herbs and opened the eyes of the blind by his word.

Abgar called Jesus either God come down from heaven or the Son of God, and he asked him to come and heal him. He had also heard that some were seeking Jesus' life, so he offered him his own small and beautiful city.

Jesus answered, the story says, that the work he was sent for was now finished and he was going up to his Father. After that he would send one of his disciples to heal the king, and the city would be blessed.

After the ascension, Addai came. He was one of the seventy-two, and he stayed with a Jewish man named Tobias. The story says he healed the king and preached to the city, calling it away from Nebo, Bel and Taratha to the one true God.

In the story, Addai made Aggai leader in his place before he died. Years after Abgar died, one of his sons had Aggai's legs broken as he sat teaching in the church. Aggai died too quickly to ordain Palut, so Palut went to Antioch and was ordained by its bishop, Serapion. Serapion had himself been ordained by Zephyrinus, bishop of Rome, in the line of Simon Peter.
