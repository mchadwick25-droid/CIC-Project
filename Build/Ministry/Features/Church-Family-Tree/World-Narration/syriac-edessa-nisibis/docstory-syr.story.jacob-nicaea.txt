Jacob, bishop of Nisibis, is remembered among the bishops at the great council of Nicaea. In the year 325, bishops from the whole world gathered there to answer Arius. The tradition of these churches holds Jacob to be one voice from their Syriac-speaking frontier, and says he stood with those who confessed the Son as true God.

Nothing survives of what Jacob said or did at the council. The lists of its signers were pieced together by later hands, and they carry his name among the assembled bishops. Theodoret of Cyrus, a Greek church historian of the next century, also counts him among them. Still, no document from the fourth century itself confirms that he was there.

At home in Nisibis, Ephrem praised Jacob as the city's wall.
