A council in the East had condemned Athanasius of Alexandria and removed him from office, and Julius, bishop of Rome, was expected to accept that decision. Instead he wrote back, questioning the council's right to settle the matter before Rome had heard it.

He put it to them as a question. "Are you unaware that the custom has been to write to us first, and then for a just decision to be passed from this place?" Julius had not attended their council, and those he wrote to disputed how old and settled that custom was.
