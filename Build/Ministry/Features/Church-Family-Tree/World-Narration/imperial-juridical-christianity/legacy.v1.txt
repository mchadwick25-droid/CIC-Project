The last council of this period did not close in agreement. Chalcedon's settlement on Christ's two natures was final for those who accepted it, but whole regions rejected it at once, starting in Egypt, at the council itself. This rejection did not fade the way earlier disputes had. It hardened into lasting division. The record used here ends just as that fracture opens, and it says nothing of how the fracture would end.

The two rival claims these churches argued over have been argued over ever since. Does the record from this period already amount to a settled papacy, or is it a claim still being worked out? Later traditions still argue that question, from these same documents.

Constantinople's rank raises a second, related fight. The council at Chalcedon granted that rank on one ground: nearness to the throne, not an apostle's grave. The tradition that received the rank reads this one way, and the see that refused it reads it another way. The record opens that disagreement and does not close it.
