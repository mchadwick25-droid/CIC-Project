The altar of Victory stood in the Senate house in Rome. Constantius had it removed, Julian put it back, and Gratian removed it again.

In the year 384, Symmachus petitioned the young emperor to bring the altar back. He was prefect of Rome and the most eloquent voice of the old religion. Rome's ancient worship, he said, had guarded Rome's ancient greatness, and so great a secret could not be reached by one road alone.

Ambrose answered him twice in writing. A Christian emperor's state, Ambrose argued, may not fund what the faith knows to be false. What Symmachus called reverence for the past was, to Ambrose, the past's own long error.

The altar stayed removed.
