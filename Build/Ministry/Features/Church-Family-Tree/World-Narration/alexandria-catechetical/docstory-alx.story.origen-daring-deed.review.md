## Findings

1. PRE-EXISTING (a "so" that narrows the cause). "Eusebius says Origen wanted no one to accuse him of wrong, so he took a hard verse from Matthew in the most literal sense." Eusebius (npnf201, lines 33196-33202) says Origen took the verse "in too literal and extreme a sense", and acted "in order to fulfill the Saviour's word, and at the same time to take away from the unbelievers all opportunity for scandal", since he taught women as well as men. The original makes fear of accusation the reason for the literal reading and drops the first purpose, so the act sounds driven by reputation alone. The rewrite carried it at the original's strength. Tree wording (replacing that sentence and the next two):
   "Eusebius says Origen took a hard verse from Matthew in the most literal sense. It speaks of those who "have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake." By this account he acted on it himself, both to fulfill Christ's word and so that no one could accuse him of wrong, since he taught women as well as men. He tried to keep it secret, but he could not."
   The record's owner should be told: alx.story.origen-daring-deed's text and telling give only the second purpose.
