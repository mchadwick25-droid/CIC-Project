Two things from the Christians of Alexandria lived on past their own time, in different ways. The first was a settled inheritance. The church there passed on the Nicene confession, the hope of sharing God's own life, and Scripture read for a deeper meaning beneath its surface. Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus, teachers of a later generation, gathered an anthology of Origen's own writing before that age had ended.

The second thing was a live argument, not a settled one. Origen's boldest ideas kept being read, loved and contested by turns, and translators who had taken a side carried them forward, on both sides of the argument. The argument finally broke into open controversy right around the year 400.
