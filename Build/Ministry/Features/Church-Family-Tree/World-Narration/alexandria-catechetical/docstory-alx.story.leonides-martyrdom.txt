About the year 202, under the emperor Severus, persecution struck Alexandria. Leonides was arrested and held for beheading, and his son Origen was sixteen. Eusebius tells what happened next in that house. He wrote a century later, from the Alexandrian church's memory, from Origen's own letters and from reports about him.

The boy burned to give himself up and die beside his father. His mother begged him to spare her, but he would not listen. So she hid all his clothes, and shame kept him at home.

Origen did the one thing left to him: he wrote to his father in prison. One line of that letter survives, "Take heed not to change your mind on our account."

Leonides did not change his mind. He was beheaded, and the family's property was seized. Origen, not yet seventeen, was left the eldest of seven children, with a widowed mother.
