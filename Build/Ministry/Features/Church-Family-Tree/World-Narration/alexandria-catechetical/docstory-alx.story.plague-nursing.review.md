## Findings

1. PRE-EXISTING (narrator carries the bishop's claim, and the image misleads). "The church buried its dead with open hands." The rewrite moved this from the church's own voice ("Our church") to the narrator's, so the narrator now asserts as fact what only Dionysius reports, in a letter the record calls a bishop's rhetorical frame for his own churches. Heard alone, "with open hands" also sounds like a figure for generosity. In the source it is literal: "they took the bodies of the saints in their open hands and in their bosoms, and closed their eyes and their mouths; and they bore them away on their shoulders and laid them out ... and they prepared them suitably with washings and garments" (lines 41112-41116). Tree wording: "He wrote that they took the bodies of the dead in their open hands, washed and dressed them, and carried them away." The record's owner should be told: alx.story.plague-nursing's text states the line as fact.
