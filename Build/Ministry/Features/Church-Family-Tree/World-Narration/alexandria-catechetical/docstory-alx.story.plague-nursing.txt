A great sickness came to Alexandria about the year 260, and every house had its dead. Bishop Dionysius wrote to the churches at festival time, while the plague still raged.

Most of the brothers and sisters did not run, he wrote. They visited the sick without fear, tended them day after day and served them in Christ.

Many caught the sickness from those they nursed, and they died of it. He wrote that they died most joyfully, as if drawing their neighbors' pain onto themselves. He wrote that they took the bodies of the dead in their open hands, washed and dressed them, and carried them away.

In the same letter, he said the pagans thrust away their own sick at the first sign of illness, and fled even from their dearest.
