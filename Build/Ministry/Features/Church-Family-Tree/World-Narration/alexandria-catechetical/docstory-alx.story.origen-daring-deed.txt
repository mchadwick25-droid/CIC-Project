Origen was still young and teaching the faith in Alexandria, where he taught men and women together, day and night.

Eusebius alone tells what Origen did then, and no account from Origen himself describes it. Eusebius says Origen took a hard verse from Matthew in the most literal sense. It speaks of those who "have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake." By this account he acted on it himself, both to fulfill Christ's word and so that no one could accuse him of wrong, since he taught women as well as men. He tried to keep it secret, but he could not. Eusebius calls the act immature, and in the same breath a proof of unusual faith.

When his bishop, Demetrius, learned of it, he admired the young man's daring and faith and urged him to keep teaching. Later, once Origen had grown famous beyond Alexandria, the same Demetrius turned that youthful act against him. He wrote to bishops everywhere calling it foolish, and he also attacked Origen's ordination by the bishops of Caesarea and Jerusalem.

Some have doubted that it happened at all, and thought the report grew from Origen's own figures of speech. Others see no reason to doubt it.

Origen himself later judged the verse differently. He wrote against reading it so literally.
