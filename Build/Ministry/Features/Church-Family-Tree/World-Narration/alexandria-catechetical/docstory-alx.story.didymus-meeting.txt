Didymus lost his sight at four, he told Palladius, too young ever to have learned to read. Palladius met him four times over ten years and wrote down what he saw.

By then Didymus was old. What others took in with their eyes, he had taken in through his ears and held on to, and he interpreted Scripture word by word. Palladius calls him the blind author. Jerome, who studied with him briefly, wrote in 392 or 393 that Didymus had already passed his eighty-third year.

Later tradition called him the seer, a name set against his blindness. Most of the books he wrote are lost, and only fragments were recovered, centuries later.

He died at the age of eighty-five.
