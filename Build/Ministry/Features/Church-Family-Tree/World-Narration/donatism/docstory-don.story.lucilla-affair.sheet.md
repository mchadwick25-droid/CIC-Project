# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.lucilla-affair

## Original text (as narrated now)

She kept the bone of a martyr with her, and she kissed it before she took the cup at communion. Lucilla was a wealthy laywoman of Carthage, and the archdeacon Caecilian rebuked her for it. Optatus wrote against us about fifty years later. He adds that the dead man was not yet a recognized martyr, if he was one at all. She left, he says, full of anger.

Around then, persecution broke over Carthage, and the bishop, Mensurius, was summoned to the emperor's court. He left the church's gold and silver with elders he trusted. He gave an old woman a written list of it, to hand to whoever sat in the bishop's chair if he did not come home.

He did not come home. Botrus and Celestius had both expected the office, but Caecilian was elected instead. Optatus says the elders had kept back more of the treasure than they should have. In his telling, the elders, the two passed-over men and Lucilla now found a common cause.

At a crowded meeting in the basilica, Caecilian demanded that his accusers state their charge. Purpurius only mocked him. Let his head be well beaten in penance, he said. No proof was produced. Instead Majorinus, a member of Lucilla's household, was made bishop in Caecilian's place. It was done at her urging and through her bribes, Optatus writes.

## Grounding records

### don.story.lucilla-affair (records/don/story/don.story.lucilla-affair.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the sequence of events - the rebuke, the treasury entrusted and mishandled, the two passed-over candidates, the contested election, the public assembly, the rival consecration. NOT documented, and not adopted here, is the motive Optatus assigns: that personal spite on Lucilla's part was the schism's true cause. That is a hostile characterization of a named woman's intentions by the party she is blamed for splitting, written roughly five decades after the events, and it is treated throughout as his framing rather than as a finding. No account of these events by anyone on our own side survives to set beside it.
- absent_detail: Lucilla left no text and no one asked her what she thought she was doing. Everything known about her reaches us inside the hostile narrative that blames her for the schism; her own account of the rebuke, of the money, and of Majorinus does not exist. Nor does any account of these events from our own side.
- tellable_as: A rich woman is rebuked for kissing a martyr's bone before communion. Years later the man who rebuked her becomes bishop of Carthage. A rival bishop is made against him.

### don.figure.lucilla (records/don/figure/don.figure.lucilla.md)
- formation_confidence: Contested
- divergence_note: Contested, and the banding is strict. What the record supports is a named woman of means at Carthage with the standing to make a rival consecration happen: a fortune, a household, a devotional practice she was rebuked for, and money that a court six years later traced to the founding of a rival altar. Any claim about her own motivations, private character, or theological commitments beyond what Optatus's hostile narrative attributes to her is Inferential-Thin and is not made anywhere in these records. She left no text. Everything reaches us inside the narrative that blames her for the schism.

### don.figure.caecilian (records/don/figure/don.figure.caecilian.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the office, the contested election, and the consecration by Felix of Aptungi that the whole dispute turns on. His own conduct and character reach us almost entirely through writers on his own side, and the accusations against him reach us only inside those same writers' refutations of them. No text of his survives.

### don.figure.mensurius (records/don/figure/don.figure.mensurius.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the episode that matters here - the treasury entrusted to the seniors against his expected exile, the written inventory left with an old woman, and his failure to return. He dies before the schism exists, so he belongs to neither communion; everything recorded of him reaches us through the narrative that treats his succession as the origin of the rupture.

### don.figure.majorinus (records/don/figure/don.figure.majorinus.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the one act that matters - his consecration as bishop of Carthage against Caecilian, which begins the parallel line of ordination this communion held for more than a century. Almost nothing else about him is recorded. He wrote nothing that survives, held the see only briefly, and reaches us mainly as the name in a sentence about someone else's money.

### don.figure.purpurius (records/don/figure/don.figure.purpurius.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented, and unusually well for a figure on our side of the line, because he appears in two independent kinds of evidence: a narrated council scene and his own letters, read into a court record in 320 and preserved verbatim in that record. The letters are the strongest thing here and the hardest - they are his own words, not an opponent's paraphrase, and in them he writes that the accusation against a fellow bishop is true and urges that no one be allowed to find out.

### don.figure.optatus-of-milevis (records/don/figure/don.figure.optatus-of-milevis.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented as author, office and adversarial position. Everything he transmits about this communion is transmitted in the course of arguing against it, roughly five decades after the founding events he narrates; his narrative richness and documentary specificity must never be converted into independent corroboration. The dossier appended to his work contains genuine court and conciliar acts, but the selection of them is his own act, and every one of them tells against us.
