# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.council-of-cirta

## Original text (as narrated now)

Before the quarrel over Caecilian began, the bishops of Numidia met at Cirta to make a bishop of their own. The year is disputed, 305 or 307 or later. Optatus tells the story of that meeting in his books against us.

Secundus of Tigisis presided. He asked whether any of them had handed over the scriptures in the persecution. One by one, several said they had. It was the same charge we would soon bring against Felix, the bishop who made Caecilian.

Then Purpurius turned the question back on Secundus. He taunted him for being let go only after a long time among the soldiers. The room began to mutter that Secundus too had given up the sacred books to buy his release.

Secundus asked advice from his brother's son, called Secundus the Less, who told him to leave the whole affair to God. Secundus then asked the three bishops who had not been accused for their judgment. They were Victor of Garba, Felix of Rotarium and Nabor of Centurio. They said a case like this should be reserved to the Lord.

"Sit down, all," Secundus said. "Thanks be to God," the assembly answered, and sat. No one was found guilty, and no one was cleared.

## Grounding records

### don.story.council-of-cirta (records/don/story/don.story.council-of-cirta.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented at the narrative level, with the council's own exact date disputed in the surviving record - 305, or 307, or later - and left disputed here rather than resolved by choosing. The larger caveat is interpretive rather than chronological: this account reaches us only through Optatus, who tells it in order to argue that the movement's founders were self-interested traditores absolving one another. The bare facts are treated as Documented; his framing of what "reserved to the Lord" meant to the men who said it is treated as his own hostile reading, and no account of this council from our own side exists to set against it.
- absent_detail: No record of this council from our own side survives - no minute, no later defense, no explanation of what the phrase "reserved to the Lord" was understood to mean by the men who said it. Whether the admissions were what Optatus takes them to be, and what the assembly thought it was doing when it sat down, cannot be checked against anything.
- tellable_as: The bishops who later ruled that a traditor could never serve once faced that same question about themselves. They agreed to leave it to God, and sat down.

### don.figure.purpurius (records/don/figure/don.figure.purpurius.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented, and unusually well for a figure on our side of the line, because he appears in two independent kinds of evidence: a narrated council scene and his own letters, read into a court record in 320 and preserved verbatim in that record. The letters are the strongest thing here and the hardest - they are his own words, not an opponent's paraphrase, and in them he writes that the accusation against a fellow bishop is true and urges that no one be allowed to find out.

The dossier appended to his work contains genuine court and conciliar acts, but the selection of them is his own act, and every one of them tells against us.
