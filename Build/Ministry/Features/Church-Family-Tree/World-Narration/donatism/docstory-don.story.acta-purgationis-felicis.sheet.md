# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.acta-purgationis-felicis

## Original text (as narrated now)

At the center of the case lay a letter to Felix, the bishop of Aptungi who had made Caecilian bishop of Carthage. Our side said Felix had handed over the scriptures in the persecution. If that was true, he had no right to make anyone a bishop, and Caecilian's office was void from its first day. The letter's closing lines seemed to prove it.

The emperor Constantine ordered the charge looked into, and the proconsul Aelianus heard named witnesses on the record. Their words survive in the dossier of documents Optatus kept. The letter came from a magistrate, Caecilianus. He told the court he had never dictated its closing lines.

Then a town official named Ingentius was questioned. Under the threat of torture, he broke. He admitted that he had added to the letter himself, out of grief over Maurus, his guest. A counsel named Apronianus charged that he had traveled through Numidia and Mauritania as our party's agent. Ingentius denied that he had been to Numidia.

The proconsul ruled that nothing had been proved against Felix, and he cleared him.

## Grounding records

### don.story.acta-purgationis-felicis (records/don/story/don.story.acta-purgationis-felicis.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented: a formal judicial verdict, ordered by the emperor, conducted by a named proconsul, with named deponents on record and a confession obtained under threat of torture. The confession was extracted under that threat, which is stated here rather than left implicit - a modern reader is entitled to weigh what that does to its reliability, and this record does not treat torture-adjacent testimony as though it were free testimony. The proceeding is preserved inside Optatus's own appendix and its outcome is unfavorable to our founding accusation, which is the honest position and is not reframed.
- absent_detail: Nothing survives of what Felix himself said, or of what Ingentius said before the threat was made. No account of this inquiry from our own side exists at all - not a defense of the accusation, not a challenge to the proceeding, not an explanation of who sent Ingentius or on whose authority.
- tellable_as: The emperor orders an inquiry. Felix, who made Caecilian a bishop, is said to have given up the scriptures. A town councilor admits, under threat of torture, that he added false lines to a letter against him. The inquiry clears Felix.

### don.figure.felix-of-aptungi (records/don/figure/don.figure.felix-of-aptungi.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the accusation, the imperially ordered inquiry, the named deponents, the confessed forgery and the verdict of vindication. Two things are held open rather than closed: the confession that cleared him was obtained under threat of torture, which a modern reader is entitled to weigh; and no account of the inquiry from our own side survives, so the case for the accusation as its own advocates would have put it cannot be recovered.

### don.figure.caecilian (records/don/figure/don.figure.caecilian.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the office, the contested election, and the consecration by Felix of Aptungi that the whole dispute turns on. His own conduct and character reach us almost entirely through writers on his own side, and the accusations against him reach us only inside those same writers' refutations of them. No text of his survives.

### don.figure.constantine (records/don/figure/don.figure.constantine.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the acts that bear on this world - the referral of the dispute to a hearing at Rome in 313 and a council at Arles in 314, the order to investigate the accusation against Felix of Aptungi, and the coercion that followed. His own words reach us only through documents preserved in the dossier of the party that won, and no Donatist chronicle of Arles or of the imperial rulings survives at all. The 313 approach to him was, on our side, one of three named exceptions to a stance otherwise of refusing the state's standing to judge the church.

### don.figure.optatus-of-milevis (records/don/figure/don.figure.optatus-of-milevis.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented as author, office and adversarial position. Everything he transmits about this communion is transmitted in the course of arguing against it, roughly five decades after the founding events he narrates; his narrative richness and documentary specificity must never be converted into independent corroboration. The dossier appended to his work contains genuine court and conciliar acts, but the selection of them is his own act, and every one of them tells against us.
