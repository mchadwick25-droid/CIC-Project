# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.passio-marculi

## Original text (as narrated now)

We remember Marculus as a man who had already turned from worldly advancement and given his life to the church. Our account of his suffering, the Passio Marculi, names no author. It gives a day but no year.

When Macarius's persecution came into Numidia, ten of our bishops were sent to meet him, and Marculus was among them. He was seized at Vegesela, bound to columns and flogged. The account says he bore it without visible pain and praised God the whole time. Then he was paraded through several towns of Numidia and held four days at a cliff called Novapetra.

There, the account says, he fasted and was given a vision. A cup, a crown and a palm were shown to him together. Before dawn he was thrown from the cliff.

We hold that his body did not break in the fall. It came down gently, we say, as though something carried it. A bright cloud covered it, and flashes of light showed the brothers where he lay, so that they could take him for burial.

## Grounding records

### don.story.passio-marculi (records/don/story/don.story.passio-marculi.md)
- formation_confidence: Contested
- divergence_note: Contested for the formation portrait; Inferential-Thin for the visionary and miraculous details specifically, which are offered as the tradition's own testimony and never as verified events. The bare fact of Marculus's death under Macarius is corroborated from two separate hostile directions - Optatus argues the deaths were deserved punishment for schism, and Augustine, separately and on a different question, disputes whether they qualify as martyrdom at all - but neither hostile writer accepts our own account of what happened, so the corroboration covers the killing and nothing beyond it. The Passio's own heading gives a day and no year: the 347-348 Macarian dating rests on the standard field literature, not on the text.
- absent_detail: The Passio names no author and gives no year - only a day. Nothing survives of what Marculus himself said under interrogation in his own words, and no independent witness confirms the vision, the fall, or the light. What the hostile record supplies is only that he was killed, and an argument about whether that killing counts.
- tellable_as: On the last night of his four days at Novapetra, after a day of fasting, Marculus is shown a cup, a crown and a palm. Before dawn he is thrown from a cliff. The tradition says his body came down gently, and a bright cloud showed the brethren where he lay.
