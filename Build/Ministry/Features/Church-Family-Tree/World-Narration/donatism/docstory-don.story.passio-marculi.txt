The Donatists remembered Marculus as a man who had already turned from worldly advancement and given his life to the church. Their account of his suffering, the Passio Marculi, names no author. It gives a day but no year.

In the Passio's telling, when Macarius's persecution came into Numidia, ten Donatist bishops were sent to meet him, and Marculus was among them. It says he was seized at Vegesela, bound to columns and flogged, and that he bore it without visible pain and praised God the whole time. Then he was paraded through several towns of Numidia and held four days at a cliff called Novapetra.

There, the account says, he fasted and was given a vision. A cup, a crown and a palm were shown to him together, and before dawn he was thrown from the cliff.

The Donatists held that his body did not break in the fall. It came down gently, they said, as though something carried it. A bright cloud covered it, and flashes of light showed the brothers where he lay, so that they could take him for burial.
