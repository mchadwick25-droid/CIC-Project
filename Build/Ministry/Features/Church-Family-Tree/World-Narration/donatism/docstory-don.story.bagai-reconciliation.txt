In 393 a deacon named Maximian was made rival primate at Cebarsussi, against Primian, the Donatist primate at Carthage. In miniature, it was the act the Donatist founders had once taken against Caecilian.

The answer came the next year at Bagai. Three hundred and ten Donatist bishops met there and condemned the followers of Maximian in the harshest terms they had. Augustine quotes the decree word for word in his books against the Donatists. It calls them shipwrecked men, dashed by the waves of truth onto the sharp rocks. Their bodies cover the shore, it says, and they cannot even find a grave.

Later the Donatist party took back two of those condemned bishops, Felicianus of Musti and Praetextatus of Assuris. Neither was baptized again or ordained again. Optatus Gildonianus, one of the Donatist bishops, used soldiers to enforce their return.

These two had baptized while in schism, and Augustine made this the center of his case against the Donatists. If their baptisms could stand on Donatist authority, he argued, then the Donatist rule did not hold even for the Donatists themselves.
