# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.bagai-reconciliation

## Original text (as narrated now)

In 393 a deacon named Maximian was made rival primate at Cebarsussi, against Primian, our primate at Carthage. In miniature, it was the act our founders had once taken against Caecilian.

The answer came the next year at Bagai. Three hundred and ten of our bishops met there and condemned the followers of Maximian in the harshest terms they had. Augustine quotes the decree word for word in his books against us. It calls them shipwrecked men, dashed by the waves of truth onto the sharp rocks. Their bodies cover the shore, it says, and they cannot even find a grave.

Later our party took back two of those condemned bishops, Felicianus of Musti and Praetextatus of Assuris. Neither was baptized again or ordained again. Optatus Gildonianus, one of our bishops, used soldiers to enforce their return.

These two had baptized while in schism. Augustine made this the center of his case against us. If their baptisms could stand on our own authority, he argued, then our rule did not hold even for us.

## Grounding records

### don.story.bagai-reconciliation (records/don/story/don.story.bagai-reconciliation.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented, and the most repeatedly and directly quoted internal tension in this entire corpus - corroborated across two separate works by the same author, with the Bagai council's own decree quoted rather than paraphrased. The caveat is single and must be stated every time: the whole episode reaches us through Augustine, who quotes the decree specifically in order to build his central argument against our rebaptism logic. His selection and framing serve that purpose. That does not cast doubt on the decree's own wording, which he is quoting; it does mean the story arrives already inside an argument, and no account of the affair from our own side survives.
- absent_detail: No account of the Bagai council from our own side survives beyond the decree Augustine quotes, and nothing at all survives of the reasoning by which the same council that wrote those words received Felicianus and Praetextatus back unrepeated. We do not know what was said in the room, or whether anyone present named the inconsistency out loud.
- tellable_as: Three hundred and ten of our bishops condemn a breakaway party. They say its people are shipwrecked men who cannot even find a grave. Later our party takes two of those bishops back. It repeats neither baptism nor ordination.

### don.figure.maximian-of-cebarsussi (records/don/figure/don.figure.maximian-of-cebarsussi.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the rupture that bears his name - a deacon deposed, elected rival primate at Cebarsussi in 393, condemned at Bagai in 394. The whole episode reaches us almost entirely through Augustine's own quotation, and he quotes it to build his central argument against our rebaptism logic; that mediation must be marked and never converted into independent corroboration. Of Maximian himself, beyond the office and the condemnation, almost nothing is recorded, and no text of his survives.

### don.figure.augustine (records/don/figure/don.figure.augustine.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented as author, office and position, and by far the largest single body of evidence for this world - which is itself the risk rather than a reassurance. His anti-Donatist corpus spans three decades of his episcopate and weighs disproportionately toward the years after his own side's institutional victory. He preserves more of our own words than anyone else, in the form of clauses selected for answerability. No world-level claim should rest on him alone without that being said.
