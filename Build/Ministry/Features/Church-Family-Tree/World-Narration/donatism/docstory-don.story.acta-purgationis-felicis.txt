At the center of the case lay a letter to Felix, the bishop of Aptungi who had made Caecilian bishop of Carthage. The Donatists said Felix had handed over the scriptures in the persecution. If that was true, they held, he had no right to make anyone a bishop, and Caecilian's office was void from its first day. The letter's closing lines seemed to prove it.

The emperor Constantine ordered the charge looked into, and the proconsul Aelianus heard named witnesses on the record. Their words survive in the dossier of documents kept by Optatus, an opponent of the Donatists. The letter came from a magistrate, Caecilianus. He told the court he had never dictated its closing lines.

Then a town official named Ingentius was questioned. Under the threat of torture, he broke. He admitted that he had added to the letter himself, out of grief over Maurus, his guest. A counsel named Apronianus charged that Ingentius had traveled through Numidia and Mauritania as the Donatist party's agent. Ingentius denied that he had been to Numidia.

The proconsul ruled that nothing had been proved against Felix, and he cleared him.
