# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.conference-of-carthage-411

## Original text (as narrated now)

The judge sat, and our bishops stood. Flavius Marcellinus offered them chairs, and said it weighed on him to sit while so many venerable men stood. Petilian thanked him and refused. We do not blush and we do not fear, he said. Christ himself stood before the governor, so how could we refuse to stand?

This was Carthage in the summer of 411. The emperor had ordered our whole quarrel settled at law, and imperial notaries took down what was said.

Emeritus of Caesarea carried our argument. He would not let the court touch the case itself until it had settled the day, the mandates and who was bringing the charge. When the other side claimed the name Catholic, Petilian answered that it meant nothing until the case was decided. Marcellinus agreed that the name belonged to whichever side the truth was found with. Then he said he was bound by the emperor's written order, which had already given them that name.

On the third day Augustine argued from the wheat and the weeds that the church holds good and bad together. Emeritus answered him verse for verse from the same books. He signed the notary's record of most of what he said. At times he added that he kept his right to appeal.

In the end Marcellinus ruled against us, though the acts we have break off before his sentence.

### don.story.conference-of-carthage-411 (records/don/story/don.story.conference-of-carthage-411.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the proceeding itself and for every intervention narrated below, each of which was read directly in the vendored Migne printing at a named line range rather than taken from any secondary paraphrase. Three limits are held open rather than closed. (1) OCR: this scan carries this corpus's worst OCR on record for a text of its significance, so the Latin renderings below are a careful reading of a difficult scan, not a settled critical-edition text -- the modern critical editions (Lancel, SC 194/195/224/373; CCSL 149A) are in copyright and were not consulted. (2) Extent: roughly nineteen hundred lines of a 144,733-line volume were read, selected by direct search on the Donatist bishops' own recorded names, so this is a systematic reading of their interventions and not of the whole transcript. (3) The two bishop-counts do not agree and neither is suppressed here: the volume's own editorial summary gives 286 Catholic against 279 Donatist, while the acts' own roll-call tally, read at its own location, records the Catholic subscriptions at two hundred and sixty-six with twenty further bishops present who stated they had not yet subscribed. The verdict is NOT part of what was read: the numbered acts in this printing break off before it.
- absent_detail: The verdict is not here. In the printing read for this record the numbered acts break off before any sentence is pronounced, and what follows is a later editor's own narrative history of the conference, not the court's own words. Nothing survives of what our bishops said to one another between the sessions, or on the road home; the transcript preserves only what was said inside the room, on the record, to the judge.
- tellable_as: Our bishops face the Catholic bishops before an imperial judge. They refuse the chairs he offers, because Christ stood before his own governor. For three days they argue that a court which has already been told which side is Catholic cannot be the one to find out.

### don.figure.petilian (records/don/figure/don.figure.petilian.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented, and unusually, through two structurally different channels rather than one. His letters survive only as quoted clause by clause inside the refutation written against them, where the selection effect is not incidental to the source but is the source. His interventions at the 411 Conference survive instead in a court transcript taken down by imperial notaries, where nothing was chosen by an adversary for refutation. Any claim about the fuller shape of his argument, beyond what the refutation preserves, is Inferential-Thin. Monceaux's dedicated chapter on him is vendored and remains unread.

### don.figure.augustine (records/don/figure/don.figure.augustine.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented as author, office and position, and by far the largest single body of evidence for this world - which is itself the risk rather than a reassurance. His anti-Donatist corpus spans three decades of his episcopate and weighs disproportionately toward the years after his own side's institutional victory. He preserves more of our own words than anyone else, in the form of clauses selected for answerability. No world-level claim should rest on him alone without that being said.
