Before the quarrel over Caecilian began, the bishops of Numidia met at Cirta to make a bishop of their own. The year is disputed, 305 or 307 or later. Optatus tells the story of that meeting in his books against the Donatists.

Secundus of Tigisis presided. He asked whether any of them had handed over the scriptures in the persecution, and one by one, several said they had. It was the same charge the Donatists would soon bring against Felix, the bishop who made Caecilian.

Then Purpurius turned the question back on Secundus. He taunted him for being let go only after a long time among the soldiers. The room began to mutter that Secundus too had given up the sacred books to buy his release.

Secundus asked advice from his brother's son, called Secundus the Less, who told him to leave the whole affair to God. Secundus then asked the three bishops who had not been accused for their judgment. They were Victor of Garba, Felix of Rotarium and Nabor of Centurio, and they said a case like this should be reserved to the Lord.

"Sit down, all," Secundus said. "Thanks be to God," the assembly answered, and sat. No one was found guilty, and no one was cleared.
