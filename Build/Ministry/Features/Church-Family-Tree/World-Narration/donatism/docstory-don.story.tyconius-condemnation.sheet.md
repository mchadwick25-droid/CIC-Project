# Sheet: donatism / docstory-don.story.tyconius-condemnation

## Original text (as narrated now)

Tyconius argued at length, with strong support from Scripture, that the Christians of Africa belonged to a church spread across the whole earth. Anyone who followed the case could see where it led. If he was right, the African churches still in communion with the rest had lost nothing by staying. Those who had left that communion, his own party among them, had broken away from something real.

Parmenian, our bishop at Carthage, saw where the argument pointed, and he did not follow it. Augustine, writing against us some twenty years later, tells what came next. Parmenian wrote to Tyconius and rebuked him for preaching that the church spread across the whole earth. He warned him never to dare do it again.

Tyconius did not take it back, and he did not leave. He went on arguing from inside the party that had told him to stop. It is reported, Augustine says, that a council of his own communion then condemned him. He never went over to the other side.

## Grounding records

### don.story.tyconius-condemnation (records/don/story/don.story.tyconius-condemnation.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for Parmenian's letter of rebuke and for the fact of a subsequent condemnation. The specific mechanism - a conciliar sentence rather than some other form of exclusion - carries Augustine's own explicit reporting hedge, "perhibent," it is reported, and that hedge is preserved here rather than smoothed into unqualified fact. The account is Augustine's, written around 400 about events of around 380, and no account from our own side survives. Tyconius's own formal standing within this communion is deliberately left open and is not settled by this story.
- absent_detail: Parmenian's letter itself does not survive; what survives is Augustine's report of it. No act of any council condemning Tyconius survives, which is why the report of one carries a hedge. Nothing survives of Tyconius's own answer to the rebuke, of who if anyone stood with him, or of how his book got out of Africa and into the hands that made it famous.
- tellable_as: A man argues from Scripture that the church spreads across the whole world. His own bishop tells him never to preach it again. He neither gives in nor leaves.

He preserves more of our own words than anyone else, in the form of clauses selected for answerability. No world-level claim should rest on him alone without that being said.
