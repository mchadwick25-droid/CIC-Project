## Patterns

- **Naming the two churches.** The originals name the other church only from the Donatist side ("the rival church", "the other church", "its rival", "the other side"). The rewrite kept these. A listener new to the history cannot tell whose rival is meant, and the narrator speaks from one bank of the schism. The proposed name is "Caecilian's church", after its founding bishop, as the Donatists are named after theirs, and heard in the story before it is used. Both other names carry a side: "Caecilianist" was the Donatists' own label for the other party (don.term.caecilianist), and "Catholic" is the name both claimed and the imperial rescript gave one side (don.dw.the-word-catholic-and-no-door-today). Where the label "Catholic" appears it stays credited to the judge and the rescript, which the rewrite already does. Mark may want to confirm "Caecilian's church" as the tree's term across this world.
- **Words from one side.** "Persecution" for 347-348 is the Donatists' word; Optatus held these deaths deserved punishment for schism (don.story.passio-marculi). Where the narrator speaks, the proposed wording says what happened (the commissioners forced union, at the other church's urging; men were flogged, thrown down and drowned) and credits the word "persecution" to the Donatists. The force of the violence is kept. Polemic labels elsewhere stay credited: Isaac's "traditores" and "madness of unity", the Bagai decree's shipwrecked men, Optatus's bribery charge against Lucilla.
- **Present tense from the emic voice.** "We remember" became "The Donatists remember": the tense now implies a living church. Past tense is proposed.
- **Coercion on both sides.** Where the originals hold it, the rewrites keep its force: the torture threat in 314, the Macarian deaths, Optatus Gildonianus's soldiers at Bagai, the Donatists' own recourse to imperial law against the Maximianists, the penal laws after 411. One gap for the record's owner, not for this rewrite, since Mark's ruling keeps content: the world's Story itself tells violence only as done to the Donatists, plus the recourse to law in the 390s. The records also hold the Donatist side's own coercion (don.dw.what-we-did-with-the-power-we-had, Widely Accepted: a bishop with soldiers enforcing the Bagai reconciliation, and the law against heretics used on the Maximianists) and the Contested Circumcellion material (don.contested.circumcellion-character). The Story names none of it beyond the 390s recourse.
