# Review: story

**Verdict: REVISE**

Score: grade 8.2, reading ease 70.4 (names out). Passes the hard limits. With the wording below applied: grade 8.5, ease 70.5.

## One hearing

Clear and adult. The schism, the founding charge, the rite, the 347-348 deaths, the parallel church, the court of 411 and the end all land in order. Two things pull the narrator onto one side. The other church is never given a name a listener can hold: it is "the rival church", "its rival", "the other church", "their rival", "rivals" and "the other side". Each is the Donatist vantage, and a listener cannot tell, on one hearing, whose rival is meant in "the rival church's urging". And the narrator itself calls 347-348 "persecution", the Donatists' own word for it (Optatus held these deaths deserved punishment for schism: grounding record don.story.passio-marculi, divergence note).

## Fidelity

Changes checked against the original: "but they split" (contrast in the original); "That side saw itself"; "the Donatists held" for "they held"; "though the Donatists did not count it" and "In their view" (attribution made explicit, good); "There, it says" (good); "the Donatists read such accounts"; "the Donatist church was"; "In their view, a council called..."; "and in the 390s"; "But then he said"; "This story ends in 439" became "In 439 a Vandal army took Carthage" with "but the contest went on" (supported by don.force.vandal-capture-of-carthage: 439 removes the Roman power, it does not end the contest). Nothing added; every qualifier holds ("Whether they used that name among themselves is not known"; "Under threat of torture"). The witness for the forged evidence, the torture threat, the Donatists' own recourse to imperial law against the Maximianists, and the penal laws after 411 all keep their force.

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The other church is named only from the Donatist vantage.** Name it plainly after its founding bishop, as the Donatists are named after theirs. "Caecilian's church" is a description a listener can follow, since Caecilian is already in the story. It avoids "Caecilianist" (the Donatists' own label: don.term.caecilianist) and "Catholic" (the name both claimed and the rescript gave one side: don.dw.the-word-catholic-and-no-door-today, and the story's own 411 paragraph).
- "For a century, anyone who crossed over from the rival church was washed again" → "For a century, anyone who crossed over from Caecilian's church was washed again"
- "with its own bishops, basilicas and councils in the same towns as its rival." → "with its own bishops, basilicas and councils in the same towns as Caecilian's church."
- "In Numidia it far outnumbered the other church," → "In Numidia it far outnumbered Caecilian's church,"
- "a council called and enforced by a state that already favored their rival" → "a council called and enforced by a state that already favored Caecilian's church"
- "faced a slightly larger number of rivals." → "faced a slightly larger number of bishops from Caecilian's church."
- "an imperial rescript that had already given the name to the other side." → "an imperial rescript that had already given the name to Caecilian's church."

**BLOCKING 2. "Persecution" in the narrator's voice carries one side's view.** "Persecution came again in 347 and 348. This time it came at the rival church's urging, through the emperor's commissioners." Replace with the record's plain account (don.force.macarian-repression: the commissioners "forced union", at the urging of the rival) and credit the word to the Donatists. The deaths that follow keep their full force:
→ "In 347 and 348 the emperor's commissioners came to force the two churches into one, this time at the urging of Caecilian's church. The Donatists called it persecution. Their own account says that Bishop Marculus was held four days at a cliff called Novapetra."
(This replaces the two sentences quoted and "The Donatists' own account says that Bishop Marculus was held four days at a cliff called Novapetra.")

**MINOR 1.** "There, it says, he fasted and was shown a vision of a cup, a crown and a palm. Then he was thrown down." Standing alone, "Then he was thrown down" leaves the account's frame, and how Marculus died is Contested (don.story.passio-marculi: neither hostile writer accepts the Donatist account of what happened). → "There, it says, he fasted and was shown a vision of a cup, a crown and a palm, and then he was thrown down."
