# Sheet: donatism / legacy

## Original text (as narrated now)

Tyconius wrote the Book of Rules. A council of his own party is reported to have condemned him, but the book lasted longer than that party. It passed to the rival church, and it shaped how Augustine read Scripture.

The contest between the two churches did not end in 439. It went on under Vandal and then Byzantine rule for about another century and a half. In the 590s Pope Gregory the Great wrote letters pressing for an inquiry into Donatist rebaptism still going on in Numidia. No line from the Donatists to any church a person could visit today has ever been shown.

## Grounding records

### don.term.liber-regularum (records/don/term/don.term.liber-regularum.md)
- formation_confidence: Widely Accepted
- divergence_note: Thin in a specific and unusual way, and the record says so. The text itself is vendored -- Burkitt's 1894 critical edition -- and is the single largest available correction to this world's Author-Gravity concentration, since it is Donatist-affiliated material surviving in its own right rather than through hostile quotation. But Doc_02 SS2 states plainly that the vendored Liber Regularum has NOT been read in depth by this build, so everything said about its content here runs through Paul Monceaux's 1920 chapter rather than through the Latin. Beyond that: Tyconius's hermeneutics was tested at Doc_04 SS2 and failed to advance as a gravity (single voice, no following, failing Persistence and Dependency); Doc_01 SS4 leaves his formal standing in the communion deliberately open; his own party condemned him c. 380; and Monceaux reports that afterwards he 'became the head of a small schismatic church, where perhaps he was its only member'. Even the spelling of his name is unsettled across six attested manuscript forms.

### don.figure.tyconius (records/don/figure/don.figure.tyconius.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for the man, the work, the rebuke and the fact of a subsequent condemnation. Two hedges are kept rather than smoothed. The mechanism of the condemnation - a conciliar sentence rather than some other exclusion - carries Augustine's own "perhibent," it is reported. And Tyconius's own formal standing within this communion is deliberately left open: he was tested against the bar for a distinct strand and rejected on the record, as a condemned individual voice with no attested following and no distinct communal practice, which is a different finding from settling what his membership status actually was.

### don.story.tyconius-condemnation (records/don/story/don.story.tyconius-condemnation.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented
- divergence_note: Documented for Parmenian's letter of rebuke and for the fact of a subsequent condemnation. The specific mechanism - a conciliar sentence rather than some other form of exclusion - carries Augustine's own explicit reporting hedge, "perhibent," it is reported, and that hedge is preserved here rather than smoothed into unqualified fact. The account is Augustine's, written around 400 about events of around 380, and no account from our own side survives. Tyconius's own formal standing within this communion is deliberately left open and is not settled by this story.
- absent_detail: Parmenian's letter itself does not survive; what survives is Augustine's report of it. No act of any council condemning Tyconius survives, which is why the report of one carries a hedge. Nothing survives of Tyconius's own answer to the rebuke, of who if anyone stood with him, or of how his book got out of Africa and into the hands that made it famous.
- tellable_as: A man argues from Scripture that the church spreads across the whole world. His own bishop tells him never to preach it again. He neither gives in nor leaves.

### don.force.vandal-capture-of-carthage (records/don/force/don.force.vandal-capture-of-carthage.md)
- formation_confidence: Documented

This world's own participants' interpretation of the ending is NOT recoverable from surviving sources, and Doc_08 SS7 assigns that Layer 2 no confidence level at all rather than substituting an inference. Two further limits belong here. What 439 closes is precise and must not be widened: it removes the Roman imperial state as the Catholic-aligned adjudicating and coercing power the refusal pattern is defined against - it does NOT end the two-party contest, which persists under Vandal and then Byzantine rule for roughly a further century and a half, falling silent only after Gregory the Great's 590s correspondence. And the superlative Doc_04 SS3.7 attaches here belongs to this force and the 411 verdict IN COMBINATION, not to either alone.
