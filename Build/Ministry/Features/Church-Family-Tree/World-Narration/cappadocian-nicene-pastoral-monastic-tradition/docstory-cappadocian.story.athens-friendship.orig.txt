Basil and Gregory of Nazianzus met as students, most likely at Athens. Both had gone there for the fullest education their world offered.

Gregory's memory of Athens comes from the great funeral speech he gave for Basil, years afterward. He wrote it after the friendship had met real trouble, the wound of Sasima among it. That trouble had not been simply resolved before Basil died. When he rose to praise his dead friend, Gregory chose to remember Athens first.

What grew between them, Gregory said, was one soul lodged in two bodies. They were housemates, messmates and close friends. Worldly advancement was not their aim, he says. The sole business of both was virtue, the fullest possible turn toward God.

Of Basil's earlier schooling, Gregory used the image of the busy bee. It gathers what is most useful from every flower.

When their studies ended, both men went home to Cappadocia.
