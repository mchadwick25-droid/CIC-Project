The teachers of these churches did not stay local. Their names and arguments became curriculum, liturgy and law for later traditions. Those traditions trace their roots through the councils these churches lived through and helped write. Orthodox, Catholic and, more distantly, Protestant teaching all still argue in vocabulary this circle fought to place.

One thing, though, is not so simple. Did the three teachers ever share one settled scheme, or did they pursue three related projects that later tradition flattened into one tidy story? Historians now dispute this. Even the letter most often cited to prove their agreement is in doubt, since many scholars now think it was written not by Basil but by his brother Gregory.
