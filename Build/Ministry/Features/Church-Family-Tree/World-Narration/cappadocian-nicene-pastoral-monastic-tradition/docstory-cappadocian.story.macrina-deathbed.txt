In 379, Gregory of Nyssa traveled to see his sister Macrina. She was the eldest of the family's children, and her circle called her simply the Teacher. He found her already dying.

She lay on a plank set on the ground instead of a couch, wrapped in a rough garment, with no possessions around her.

As she was dying, brother and sister talked about the soul, about the resurrection, and about whether death should be feared. Everything that survives of this comes through Gregory's own pen. He wrote a Life of Macrina soon after, and he also shaped their last talk into a full dialogue, On the Soul and Resurrection. He staged it at her deathbed, in the manner of Plato's account of the last hours of Socrates. It is not known how much of her part is her own and how much is his.

Macrina died that evening. Gregory says plainly that he wept, and that he struggled through the funeral rites. A crowd gathered for her burial at the family chapel at Annisa, where earlier generations already lay. Gregory says the company of virgins she had gathered and led came with the crowd.
