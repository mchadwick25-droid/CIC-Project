# Sheet: cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition / docstory-cappadocian.story.sasima-wound

## Original text (as narrated now)

Sasima was a small posting, a dusty village where the mail changed horses.

In 372 Cappadocia was divided into two provinces. A rival bishop claimed authority over the new province's land. Basil moved to secure his authority as metropolitan, the chief bishop of the province. He created new sees and consecrated allies to hold them. Sasima was one, and Basil named his close friend, Gregory of Nazianzus, to it.

Gregory felt the appointment as a wound. Gregory's bitter letters and poems tell it, and so does the long poem about his life that he wrote years later. No record from Basil's side survives.

Gregory had been pressed into an office he had not sought, in a miserable little village. As Gregory understood it, he was sent there more for Basil's need in the fight than for Gregory's own good.

Gregory in effect refused the post. He probably never governed it. His aging father, then bishop of Nazianzus, begged him to stay and help.

## Grounding records

### cappadocian.story.sasima-wound (records/cappadocian/story/cappadocian.story.sasima-wound.md)
- formation_confidence: Widely Accepted
- divergence_note: Widely Accepted that Gregory of Nazianzus experienced and recorded this appointment as a wound - that much is Documented as his own telling. What is genuinely Contested is Basil's own side of it (essentially unrecorded, so his motives are argued from context, not from his own words) and how much of the friendship's rupture was jurisdictional necessity versus personal failure. The Sasima letters are vendored in the npnf207 edition (Letters XLVIII, XLIX, L and LVIII) and the editor's account of the province split, Anthimus and the new sees is vendored there too. This story keeps named-not-rechecked because it also names the long poem De vita sua, which is held here only as unchecked OCR Greek; the description of Sasima as a place rests on the vendored English alone.
- absent_detail: Basil's own account of why he made this appointment, and what if anything he understood himself to owe his friend for it, is not recorded anywhere in this world's evidentiary base. This is entirely Gregory's telling.
- tellable_as: Basil appoints his own close friend to a backwater see in a jurisdictional fight, and the friendship never fully recovers.
