# Sheet: cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition / docstory-cappadocian.story.forty-sebaste

## Original text (as narrated now)

It was around the year 320, in one of the last persecutions. At Sebaste in Lesser Armenia, forty soldiers were condemned for being Christians.

As our church remembers it, they were stripped and made to stand through a winter night on a frozen pond. A warm bath steamed on the shore. It was offered to any man who would deny the faith and step out of the cold.

The tradition says one of the forty broke before the night ended and went to the warm water. It says a guard set to watch them was moved by what he saw. He took off his own clothes and joined the rest on the ice. He confessed the faith the other man had just abandoned, so the martyrs still numbered forty. All of them died.

Basil and Gregory of Nyssa both preached on the feast of the Forty. Their mother, Emmelia, is remembered for building the family's chapel at Annisa for the Forty, where the relics were placed.
