## Fidelity: did lowering the level change a claim?

Each claim was checked against the original and the grounding records (rzg.force.selective-reception-four-descendant-traditions, rzg.force.no-external-ending-force, rzg.force.synod-of-dort-international-dimension, rzg.figure.beza). Most survive: the unbroken passing on, the four named heirs, no ending force from outside or inside, Dort in 1618-19, an international delegation, the "sharpest" form, "at one remove" through Beza from Geneva, "sharpens, does not close or complete", and thirty-two years before the end. One claim changed and two shifted slightly.

**BLOCKING.** "Those dates are a boundary drawn by editors around a living tradition." The original says only the *close* is an editorial boundary ("its own administrative close is an editorial boundary for a still living tradition"). The record agrees: "The window's own 1650 close is the census's own administrative continues-cap for a still-living tradition". "Those dates" stretches this to 1519 as well. That implies the start was arbitrary too, but the story tells 1519 as the real start of Zurich's reform. "Drawn by editors" also brings in people the record does not name, and "still" is lost. Replacement for that sentence and the next: "The end date, 1650, only marks where this account stops, for a tradition that is still alive. It is not a historical ending."

**MINOR.** "...passed on, unbroken, to four later churches: the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritans." The original and record say "descendant traditions" and "English Puritanism". English Puritanism was a movement inside the Church of England, not a separate church, so "four later churches" slightly misnames it. Replacement: "The beliefs of these two cities passed on, unbroken, to four later traditions: the Scottish Kirk, the Huguenots, the Dutch Reformed, and English Puritanism."

**MINOR.** "...pressed the teaching on election to its sharpest form yet." The original says it was *this world's own* doctrine of election. The next sentence ("from Geneva") partly restores this, but the claim is clearer if the owner is named. Replacement: "At the Synod of Dort in 1618 and 1619, Reformed delegates from many countries pressed these cities' teaching on election to its sharpest form yet."
