## Fidelity

Every claim in the original is present, in the original's order. The rewrite only joins sentences. Each new link was checked.

- "Zurich's reform began on 1 January 1519, when Zwingli preached straight through the Gospel of Matthew..." The original puts the two sentences side by side. The force record (rzg.force.zwinglis-1519-preaching-sausage-affair) makes the preaching the start of the reform. Supported.
- "In 1549 people feared that Geneva and Zurich taught different things about the Supper, so Calvin went to Zurich himself with William Farel." **The causal "so" is supported.** The grounding record rzg.story.calvins-journey-to-zurich says "He thought a friendly conference was the best way to end the offense. So he went to Zurich himself", and its `use_note` says "because others doubted...". Calvin's own letter says the same (cic/texts/calvin-zurich-pastors_consensus-tigurinus-mutual-consent-sacraments_beveridge1844.txt, lines 101-110): "very many are offended because my doctrine seems ... to differ from yours ... Thinking no means better fitted to remove this offence than a friendly conference ... I for this purpose paid you a visit". The cause is Calvin's own stated motive.
- "Zurich's council ruled church and city as one, but Geneva's Consistory fought to stay free of that control." The contrast is the original's own point. Supported.
- "The Perrinist party opposed the Consistory, and by 1555 the fight was mostly settled in Calvin's favor." Additive "and", no cause implied. Supported.
- "as a field chaplain" is held in rzg.witness.not-a-death-he-sought ("muster to the Second War of Kappel as a field chaplain"). "though the two men never met" is held in rzg.core.the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva ("Zwingli and Calvin never met, and their ministries did not overlap"). Both supported; not findings.
