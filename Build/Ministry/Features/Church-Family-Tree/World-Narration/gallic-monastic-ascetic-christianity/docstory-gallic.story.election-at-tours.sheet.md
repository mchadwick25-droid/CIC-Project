# Sheet: gallic-monastic-ascetic-christianity / docstory-gallic.story.election-at-tours

## Original text (as narrated now)

The people of Tours wanted Martin as their bishop, but Sulpitius says he could not easily be drawn out of his monastery. So a citizen named Ruricius knelt before him and pretended that his wife was sick. When Martin came out to help, citizens lining the road led him into the city as if under guard. A huge crowd from Tours and the nearby towns had gathered to vote, and nearly all of them wanted Martin.

A few people objected, among them some of the bishops called in to consecrate him. Led by a bishop named Defensor, they said Martin looked contemptible, with his shabby clothes and disgusting hair. The crowd only laughed.

That day the reader could not get through the crowd. While the officials stood confused, someone picked up the psalter and read the first verse he found. Out of the mouths of babes and infants, it said, God had perfected praise, to destroy the enemy and the avenger. The crowd roared, and Defensor's side fell silent, for people believed God had chosen the verse as a witness against him. Martin became our bishop, and as bishop he kept the dignity of the office and the plain habits of a monk.

The Representative must not quote the English here as Hilary's words - only as a rendering. Verification is direct as to presence (the lines were read directly at Doc_09), not as to sense.
- absent_detail: What a participant might reasonably expect here, and cannot have, is a story from inside Lerins - a day, a rule kept, a novice received, an elder's saying, a monk's prayer on the island. This is the closest our Native corpus comes, and it is a story about the founder's arrival and his elevation, told at his funeral by the man who succeeded him as a bishop: it narrates the founder's coming (the serpents at the shore) and his being made a presbyter there, not the island's interior. The silence is partly a transmission gap - Hilary's sermon exists in full, unread here beyond these lines; Eucherius's In Praise of the Desert is in the same condition - and partly our own: Vincent, the one Lerins voice read in full, tells nothing of the island but that he dwells "in the seclusion of a Monastery." The honest position is that we can tell how the founder came to Lerins and how he was made a presbyter there, and not what he or anyone did there in between. One OCR word for the place where serpents were met is left unrendered.
- tellable_as: Hilary tells how Honoratus went unafraid onto an island shunned for its filth and its serpents, with a psalm on his lips. The serpents gave way, and a camp of God rose there. There, as far as his sermon can be read, the priest's headband caught the man who had run from it.
