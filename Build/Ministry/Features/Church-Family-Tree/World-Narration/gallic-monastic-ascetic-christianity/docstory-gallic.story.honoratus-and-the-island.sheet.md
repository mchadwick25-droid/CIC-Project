# Sheet: gallic-monastic-ascetic-christianity / docstory-gallic.story.honoratus-and-the-island

## Original text (as narrated now)

When our founder Honoratus died, his disciple and successor Hilary preached his life. Honoratus, Hilary says, left his homeland because he longed for the desert. There was an island that stood empty, because it was filthy and no one could go near it for fear of poisonous snakes. He was drawn there by the chance of solitude, and by the nearness of the holy bishop Leontius, whom he loved. Many people tried to hold him back by warning him how terrible the place was. But he wanted to be cut off from the world, even by the sea.

He carried a psalm in his heart and on his lips. It promised that he would tread on the asp and the basilisk, and trample the lion and the dragon. So he went in unafraid, Hilary says, and his calm scattered the fear of his own people. The horror of the empty place fled, and the snakes gave way. Snakes were often met in those parts, Hilary adds, yet they never harmed or even frightened anyone, and he counts this among his master's miracles.

There Honoratus set up a camp of God. And there, as far as Hilary's sermon can be read, the priesthood caught up with the man who had run from it. He was made a presbyter, a priest, and he kept a monk's humility inside the priesthood.

- formation_confidence: Contested
- divergence_note: Contested for the formation portrait (the founder who flees rank, tames a waste, and is overtaken by the priesthood while keeping a monk's humility inside it); Inferential-Thin for the specific events - and, unlike every other story in this repository, Inferential-Thin for the wording itself: the English rests on the Doc_09 builder's own rendering of rough OCR of the Latin (Migne PL 50, vendored file lines c. 660-722), with the presence of every rendered phrase checkable by grep at the lines given and its sense not verified against a critical edition. One OCR word ("aridilabus") is left unrendered and said so. The Latin is normalized from OCR whose own spellings differ ("iunectit," "dia evitati," "llonoratus"). The sermon's existence and attribution are independently attested by Gennadius ch. LXX (ancient text: "his Life of Saint Honoratus, his predecessor"). The Representative must not quote the English here as Hilary's words - only as a rendering. Verification is direct as to presence (the lines were read directly at Doc_09), not as to sense.
- absent_detail: What a participant might reasonably expect here, and cannot have, is a story from inside Lerins - a day, a rule kept, a novice received, an elder's saying, a monk's prayer on the island. This is the closest our Native corpus comes, and it is a story about the founder's arrival and his elevation, told at his funeral by the man who succeeded him as a bishop: it narrates the founder's coming (the serpents at the shore) and his being made a presbyter there, not the island's interior. The silence is partly a transmission gap - Hilary's sermon exists in full, unread here beyond these lines; Eucherius's In Praise of the Desert is in the same condition - and partly our own: Vincent, the one Lerins voice read in full, tells nothing of the island but that he dwells "in the seclusion of a Monastery." The honest position is that we can tell how the founder came to Lerins and how he was made a presbyter there, and not what he or anyone did there in between. One OCR word for the place where serpents were met is left unrendered.
- tellable_as: Hilary tells how Honoratus went unafraid onto an island shunned for its filth and its serpents, with a psalm on his lips. The serpents gave way, and a camp of God rose there. There, as far as his sermon can be read, the priest's headband caught the man who had run from it.
