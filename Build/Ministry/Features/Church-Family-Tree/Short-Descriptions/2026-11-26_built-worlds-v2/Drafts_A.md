### 1. post-apostolic-house-church

**Name:** Post-Apostolic Household-Church Christianity

**Dates · place:** late 1st – 2nd c. · Antioch · Asia Minor and beyond
(census: 70–200 CE. No held line fixes 70 or 200. The start rests on Clement of Rome's letter to Corinth, Widely Accepted as c. 96; the end on Justin's trial before the prefect Rusticus, Widely Accepted as c. 165, the latest dated witness the card draws on. The letter and the trial are held: `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml` 519–520 and 29298–29315.)

**Card:**
> Each Sunday, believers from town and country met to hear the writings of the apostles and prophets read, to pray, and to share bread and wine. Churches in different cities wrote to each other, and messengers carried the letters.

Homes are left to the name above the card, since the held line on meeting places (Justin's trial, 29303–29311, "I live above one Martinus") is one man's answer in Rome. Pliny and the women deacons are left out: no room to explain them.

### 2. alexandria-catechetical

**Name:** Alexandrian Catechetical / Christian-Platonist Tradition

**Dates · place:** late 2nd – 4th c. · Alexandria · Egypt
(census: c. 150–400 CE. No held line reaches back to 150. The start is Pantaenus in charge of "the school of the faithful" under Commodus, Eusebius, Church History V.9–10, `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` 28461–28465 (chapter "The Bishops under Commodus"), 28538–28539 ("had charge of the school of the faithful in Alexandria") and 28574 ("Pantænus was at that time"). The end is Eusebius's own day: "which continues to our day", 28562, written in the early 4th c. (Widely Accepted). The year 400 is not set by any held line, so the end is told as "4th c.")

**Card:**
> Christian teachers taught the faith to newcomers, and students of Greek philosophy came to learn from them. One teacher, Origen, said Scripture is like a person, with a body, a soul and a spirit: a plain sense, and higher ones for those who grow.

**Scores:** 44 words, FK grade 9.3, reading ease 71.0 (names out). Pass.

**For the checker:** The card avoids "school", since how formal the teaching was is Contested (the census sources note this; Eusebius's "catechetical school" is his own frame). Origen is named because the card says what he taught. "Higher ones for those who grow" is a plain sense of Origen's "soul" for "he who has ascended a certain way" and "spirit" for "the perfect man" (33476–33477). The newcomers line is Eusebius retelling Origen ("as he tells us himself", 32671); the philosophers line has Origen's own words behind it (35260–35262). Clement and Nepos are left out: no room to explain them.

### 3. syriac-edessa-nisibis

**Name:** Syriac Christianity (Edessa/Nisibis)

**Dates · place:** c. 200–410 · Edessa · Nisibis
(census: 200–410 CE. The start is the Chronicle of Edessa's flood of "the year 513" (Seleucid; A.D. 201/2), which "destroyed the temple of the church of the Christians", `chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt` 53; "c." because the year is an era conversion. The end is the synod of the Persian church at Seleucia-Ctesiphon in 410, a Widely Accepted event; no held line opened here fixes it.)

**Card:**
> Churches that spoke Syriac sang their faith in hymns by Ephrem, with a refrain that came back after each verse. Before baptism, some young men and women chose to live unmarried and holy.

**Scores:** 33 words, FK grade 6.6, reading ease 80.9 (names out). Pass, with the scorer's note that grade 6.6 is under 8 (for the listening read to check it is not flat).

**For the checker:** "Sang ... hymns by Ephrem" rests on Sozomen, a Greek historian writing some seventy years after Ephrem; it is a plain fact of practice, not a wonder or praise, so it is told without naming him. The refrain is shown from Ephrem's own hymns, in the held English. "Sons of the covenant" (Hallock 62) is left out: the card has no room to explain it, so the vow is told as Aphrahat's plain words "virginity and holiness". Ephrem is named because the card says what he did, and is given no title. The Abgar story, the Diatessaron and the Persian persecution are left out for length. The end date 410 is the one point not opened in a held line.
