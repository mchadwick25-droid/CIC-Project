# Sheet: kongo-christianity-afonso-to-1650

Working file for the tradition brief. Census entry: `kongo-christianity-afonso-to-1650`, 1491-1650. Files are in `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines (spacing in the OCR is noisy; the words are not changed).

**Scope of what the vendored sources hold.** Afonso's own letters (1512-1540) and the Lopes report of 1591 cover Afonso's reign and the first Christian court. Nothing vendored and read for this brief tells the years 1543-1650 in Kongo's own voice. The brief therefore tells 1491 to about 1540 and says so. It does not tell the later kingdom.

## Witnesses

- **W1. Afonso I himself.** Letters in Portuguese, written by scribes in his name, collected by Paiva Manso (1877), `paiva-manso_historia-do-congo-documentos-por_1877.txt`. His own words, contemporary. Confidence for what they say he wanted: Documented. The letters are the king's words as a scribe wrote them down (a scribe is named at the end of two of them: Rui Godinho, 1517; D. Joao Teixeira, 1526).
- **W2. Duarte Lopes, written up by Filippo Pigafetta, 1591**, `lopes_report-of-the-kingdom-of-congo_hutchinson1881.txt` (Hutchinson's English, 1881). About a century after the events, from an outsider's record, in Rome. Confidence: Widely Accepted for the outline; Contested for details (names differ from Afonso's own letters, see Do not say).

## Claims

**C1. Afonso's father was baptized in 1491 and took a Portuguese name; Afonso followed.** Lopes, lines 3522 ("where he was baptized, with his infant son", about the Prince of Sogno, called Emmanuel) and 3670-3672 (the king takes the name Dom Joao, the queen Leonora). Lopes dates the Portuguese ships' arrival 1491 (line ~3512). The son is named Afonso, after the prince of Portugal (about line 3689). Witness: W2, late (about a century). Confidence: Widely Accepted. Afonso's own 1512 letter to the pope says Portuguese religious men taught the kingdom and that they took "a agoa do sancto baptismo" (lines 538-545 in `paiva-manso`): W1, Documented.

**C2. Afonso's own account of conversion, to the pope, 1512.** Lines 533-562. Paiva Manso's footnote at line 551 gives the text as taken from Damiao de Gois's Chronica de D. Manuel, part III: the letter reaches us through a Portuguese chronicler (about 1567), not from an archive copy. Tell it as 'a letter a Portuguese chronicler later printed'. Lines 534-562: the kingdom was "enganados pelo demonio, adorando idolos", and through the Portuguese religious men it left that; sends embassy including his son Dom Henrique, who had been sent to Portugal to be taught Scripture. Witness: W1 as printed by Gois, later. Confidence: Widely Accepted.

**C3. He sent kin to learn in Portugal; Manuel's reply was that it was doing no good; Afonso answers (17 May 1517).** Lines 1921-1945: sends relatives "pêra aprenderem o que for serviço de noso senhor Jeshu Christo", "por alumiar os cegos que sam em meus Reynos". Prevented from going, it would be "grandysyma vergonha antre as minhas gentes" (a great shame before his people). W1. Documented.

**C4. Succession struggle.** Lopes lines 3700-3830: Afonso defended the Christian faith and swept the idols from his province; his brother Pango opposed him and enlisted nobles whose wives disliked the one-wife rule (lines 3703-3714); a false charge that Afonso wanted the throne cost him his province, restored after inquiry; on the old king's death the queen mother concealed it for three days so Afonso could return (about lines 3755-3770); Pango came with a very large army. Witness: W2. The numbers (200,000 and 10,000) are Lopes's. Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction for the struggle; the army figures and the battle itself are Lopes's late account only (Inferential-Thin as numbers). A battle won by a miraculous vision is told by Lopes and later writers; no source here independent of him. Leave the vision out, or credit it to Lopes as his account.

**C5. The 1526 letter (6 July 1526, to D. Joao III).** Lines 2339-2430. W1. Documented as the king's words.
- Portuguese factors let merchants settle with goods "por nos deffessas" (forbidden by Afonso) which spread through his kingdoms; vassals break from him because others have more goods than he (lines ~2386-2394).
- The merchants carry off "nossos naturaes filhos da terra e filhos de nossos fidallgos e vassallos e nossos parentes" (line 2400); thieves and men of bad conscience seize them to sell (lines 2401-2404); "nosa terra se despouoa toda" (line 2405).
- He asks the king of Portugal for "padres e algumas poucas pessoas pêra emsynarem nas escollas", and for wine and flour for the sacrament (lines 2406-2410), and no merchants or merchandise.
- "nossa vontade he que nestes Reynos nam aja trato de esprauos nem sayda pêra elles" (line 2412).
- The same letter says wine and flour for Mass were short (lines ~2349-2356) and that this came from Portuguese officials forgetting Kongo.

**C6. The 1540 letter, 17 December 1540 (lines 3386-3440).** Afonso writes that Friar Alvaro arranged for seven or eight white men to fire on him at Easter Mass; the shot passed "polas nossas fraldas" (by the skirts of his robe) and killed a man and wounded two others; the king takes the motive to be a plan to make "outro Rey que quiser" (lines 3425-3433). A second man, Alvaro Pessanha, is accused of a plot (lines ~3434-3440). W1. His accusation: Documented that he wrote it; the guilt of the men named is his claim only, no second source read here. Tell as his claim.

**C7. The 1540 plea for money (4 December 1540, lines 3298-3380).** He asks D. Joao III for five thousand cruzados for his brother Dom Manuel, his ambassador to Rome, and recalls "ao conhecimento da santa fee catholica viemos". W1. Documented. (Not used unless needed.)

**C8. Writing as a king to a king.** Afonso's letters address Manuel and Joao III as "irmao" (brother) and sign "ElRey+ dom afonso". Documented, visible on every letter (for example lines 2339, 3298, 3392).

## Quotes (renderings by Opus, pending)

Q1. 1526, lines 2411-2412: "nossa vontade he que nestes Reynos nam aja trato de esprauos nem sayda pêra elles". Opus rendering: "It is our will that in these kingdoms there be no trade in slaves, and no outlet for them." (OCR: 'esprauos' = escravos, sense certain.)
Q2. 1517, lines 1931-1941. Opus rendering: "I send them for nothing else but to learn what serves our Lord Jesus Christ, and to spread our holy Catholic faith. They are to bring light to the blind in my kingdoms. Then, after my death, they can uphold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ." (OCR 'calolyqua' = catolyqua.) Source wording: "pêra aprenderem o que for serviço de noso senhor Jeshu Christo e pêra acrecentamento de nosa santa fee calolyqua por alumiar os cegos que sam em meus Reynos".

## Who tells it

- Almost everything about Kongo's Christians that was written down is the pen of Portuguese or Italian outsiders. Afonso's letters are the exception: the king speaking for himself, through scribes, to the Portuguese crown.
- Lopes writes in 1591 from what the Portuguese told in Rome. He is the only vendored source for 1491 and for the succession struggle.
- The letters do not tell what Kongo's ordinary Christians believed or did. Nothing read here does.

## Do not say

- Not "Afonso converted in 1491". Lopes (line 3670) names the king as Dom Joao, who was baptized, and Afonso as the son who followed. The Portuguese named the son after the prince of Portugal. His own letters sign "Afonso".
- Not that the vision or the miracle that won the battle happened. Lopes and later writers tell it; keep it out or credit it to Lopes by name.
- Not "he ended the slave trade" or "he stopped it". He asked for it to end and for Portugal to send fewer merchants. What the letters hold is the plea. The sheet holds no source for its result.
- Not "Portugal enslaved the kingdom". The letters speak of merchants and thieves taking Kongo's people to sell; they do not say Portugal's king ordered it. Keep "his letter says".
- Not that Kongo's Christianity was "the mission's creation alone". The letters show a king asking for teachers and sending his own kin to learn.
- Not "the attack on Afonso at Easter proved" anything. It is his accusation. A second source was not found in the vendored files.
- Not any date after 1543 as if the sheet held it.

## Leave out

- The battle's numbers (200,000 against 10,000) and the vision: Lopes's late report.
- The later kingdom (1543-1650): no source read for this brief. It goes to the card, not the audio.
- The 1540 plea for money.
- Cavazzi (1687) and the later Capuchin record: not used; a late mission pen.

## Connection to carry

What passed on: a Kongo king who took the faith, built it into the kingdom's order, asked Portugal for priests and teachers, and in his own letters told the Portuguese king that the slave trade he had brought was emptying his land. What the listener must understand: the same Portuguese contact brought the faith and the trade, and Afonso set the one against the other in writing.

## Ending

Afonso's own words in 1526 (Q1), or the 1540 Easter Mass scene. Preferred: the Easter scene, told as his accusation, then close on the letter of December 1540 ending with his own sign-off as "brother" to the king of Portugal.
