# Sheet: kirishitan-japan

Working file for the tradition brief. Census entry `kirishitan-japan`, 1549-1650. Files are in `cic/texts/`; line numbers are file lines (OCR spacing is noisy).

**Scope.** The brief tells the church from 1549 to the 1610s through what Japanese Christians themselves wrote and what one outside witness saw. It does not tell the hidden Christians (the "250 years without a priest" is a later study; no vendored text read here holds it) or the years after about 1617.

## Witnesses

- **W1. Letters of Japanese Christians in French (Pages, 1870)**, `pages_histoire-religion-chretienne-japon-annexes-fra_1870.txt`. Pages translated and edited letters and relations; footnotes for some letters cite earlier printed accounts (for example "Ureman, p. 89, ed. de Naples", lines 5693 and 5779). The letters are therefore a convert voice carried through missionaries' hands, then translated twice. The Jesuit vice-provincial received several of them; a missionary's note at the end of Ingoro's letter says the letter breaks off. Confidence for what they say the writers felt and chose: Widely Accepted as their words; none is an original-language text. Tell as "in a French translation of 1870".
- **W2. Francis Xavier's letters**, `francis-xavier_life-and-letters-v2_coleridge1872.txt` (Letter LXXIX, "Cagoxima, Nov. 5, 1549", about line 896-903 in the contents). Documented: Xavier was at Kagoshima in November 1549.
- **W3. Richard Cocks, diary** (English East India Company trader at Hirado), `cocks_diary-v1_thompson1883.txt`. An outside witness, contemporary. Entry of 12 August 1615 (lines 2549-2590): an embassy from the king of Spain with a present "in respect of his favour to Christians"; Cocks remarks the king "did littell know how he hath formerly banished all Christians out of his dominions: I meane all fryres, monkes, jesuists, and pristes" (lines 2587-2590). Documented as Cocks's remark.

## Claims

**C1. The mission began in 1549.** Xavier writes from Kagoshima on 5 November 1549 (W2). Documented. The size of the early church is not given in the sources read here; do not give a number.

**C2. Jean Ingoro before the governor (letter, undated in the file; lines 3006-3137).** A governor, Cacouzayemon, tells him his profession of the forbidden religion is a capital offense but that he can be safe if he renounces (3008-3011). Ingoro answers that nothing could give him more joy than to shed his blood for God (3012-3018). The governor is astonished: "je n'ai jamais contemplé quelqu'un d'aussi heureux" (3019-3020) and asks where such joy comes from (3021-3022). Ingoro tells how he read the Japanese sects' writings and found nothing about a Creator (3024-3039), heard preaching, and was baptized after Diego Mimasdca Conichi, back from Korea, converted most of the people and nobles of Yachchiro (3050-3054). The governor says he doubts the Fathers' motives (3068-3073) and has him jailed (3116). W1. The letter stops there; then a note printed after it adds (3131-3137; unsigned, so not called the editor's): the governor accused him of stealing the bodies of four crucified Christians, "Madeleine, Ines, Jean, et le petit Luis", to sell; he answered he had gathered bones that had fallen, to bury them with Christian rites. This addition is a note, not Ingoro's letter. Confidence: Widely Accepted.

**C3. Michel Fieoyemon in prison.** Letter of 7 January 1609 to the vice-provincial: for four years in prison "pour la confession de son tres-saint Nom", with so much joy "qu'il me semble n'etre prisonnier que depuis un seul jour" (4147-4154). W1. Widely Accepted.

**C4. The exiles to Tsugaru, 1614.** Letter of the exiles: they sailed from Tsuruga on 8 May 1614 and reached Tsugaru on 17 June; the next day the lord, by order of "Daifousama" (the shogun), told them to clear unworked land and asked if they would obey; they answered they would obey in everything provided they could keep the divine law, and wrote a pledge to farm (5690-5713). They ask for a priest to visit next year (5718-5721). W1. Widely Accepted. The cause of the exile (a ban of 1614) is not stated in the lines read; say "ordered into exile" and no more.

**C5. Maria and Jean Pioyemon.** Maria writes that word has spread he is condemned to death and asks for his reliquary and some hair, and urges him to humility before the crowd of Christians and non-Christians who will watch; she hopes to be present and to give and receive the last farewell (5745-5757). Jean answers that being judged worthy of martyrdom is far above his merits, mentions the confessors of Sourounga who had their fingers cut off, hopes at least for the same, and says "ne me parlez pas des cheveux" but she will have the gift she asks if God grants him death (5759-5786). W1. Widely Accepted as their words. The date of the letters is not given in the lines read.

**C6. Cocks, 12 August 1615.** W3. As above; his remark shows an English outside witness knew all friars and priests had been banished by the emperor's order.

## Who tells it

All the Japanese voices here are Christians who were, or expected to be, punished, writing to missionaries. They wrote to Jesuit priests; the letters were later printed by missionaries and translated by a French editor. No Japanese-language text and no Japanese official's own record is held. The governor's words are reported by Ingoro.

## Do not say

- Not "the church was crushed" or "ended in 1614". These sources show prison, exile and condemnation, not the end.
- Not "hidden Christians kept the faith 250 years without a priest" as a source claim. Later studies say so (census); none of these texts does. Leave to the card.
- Not that Ingoro died, or how. The letter breaks off; the editor adds the accusation about the bodies. No outcome is in the lines read.
- Not that the governor was cruel in his words. Ingoro reports him as astonished and offering safety if he renounced; he also had Ingoro jailed.
- Not a date for Ingoro's letter, Maria's letter or the Pasio letter. They are undated in the lines read.
- Not "the shogun banned Christianity in 1614" as read in these lines. The exiles' letter says the order came in the name of Daifousama (the shogun) and tells nothing more of a ban.
- Not that these letters speak for all Japanese Christians. They are a few voices, picked and printed by missionaries.
- Not Xavier's later fame, or his years in Japan (1549-1551). Only the 1549 arrival.

## Leave out

- The hidden Christians (later studies).
- Apostasy registers (coerced evidence, not read).
- Letters from the vice-provincial, the bishop and the Daimyo of Wochou (missionary and diplomatic record).
- Anything after 1617.

## Connection to carry

What passed on: Japanese Christians who wrote in their own voice to the missionaries about joy under arrest, exile and the expectation of death, while a few outsiders (Cocks) saw the priests banished. The listener must understand that nearly all of what we have of them was filtered through the mission, and that the brief names what the sources do and do not say.

## Ending

Maria's request for a last farewell and Jean's reply (C5): close on Jean's wish for his fingers, and Maria asking to be present and say goodbye. The scene is in their own words (in translation).

## Opus renderings (done)
Q1 (lines 3019-3020): "I have never seen anyone as happy as you seem to be." (the governor's words as Ingoro reports them)
Q2 (lines 4150-4154): "I have borne them with such keen joy and comfort that I seem to have been a prisoner for only one day." (from the sentence about four years in prison "for confessing his most holy Name"; "confession" rendered "confessing" because "confession" now suggests admitting sin)

## Added after Review round 1 (Do not say)
- Not that the governor had the power to kill Ingoro. He led the inquiry; the law was the lord's, and he offered to plead with the lord (3060-3061, 3106-3118).
- Not that Yachchiro was Ingoro's home town (Mouro was), and not that he was baptized after others were converted: Diego converted him with them (3039-3054).
- Not "the French editor adds". The note after Ingoro's letter is unsigned: say "a note printed after it" (3131).
- Not "a relic" (she asked for his reliquary and some hair); not "hoped only" (he asked "at least"); not "would not give her his hair" (he said not to speak of the hair, and offered to exchange reliquaries) (5745-5786).
- Q1 range is 3019-3020 (the governor's remark). "Daifousama" is glossed only as "the country's ruler". Cocks says the emperor "hath formerly banished"; no order is named.
