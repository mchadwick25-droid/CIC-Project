## 1. One hearing, as a new listener

- **Who or what:** the Christians of Japan, from Xavier's arrival in 1549 to the 1610s, heard through letters they wrote themselves.
- **What happened:** under a ban they faced prison, exile and death, and wrote of joy in it: Ingoro before a governor, Fieoyemon after four years in prison, exiles sent north to farm, a husband and wife facing his death. An English trader noted that the priests had been banished.
- **Why the connection matters:** we have their own words, but carried, chosen and translated by the mission they wrote to.

I could write all three lines. The essence lands by about word 145 ("so we hear these Christians through the mission"), inside the first minute.

Where I got lost: at "Ingoro's letter tells how he came to believe". After the essence, I took it for a jump back to before the opening scene. Only at "The governor then had him jailed" did I see that the scene had resumed and the conversion was what he told the governor (see M2). The last paragraph also confused me: "He would not give her his hair" and then "she would then receive the gift she asked of him" seem to contradict each other (B4). Nothing reads as a children's book. The voice is plain and adult. The exiles and the last paragraph run as strings of short one-act sentences (see M5).

No event is told twice. The order after the essence is Ingoro (undated, but before 1609 in the telling), 1609, 1614, 1615, then the undated Maria and Jean exchange, which the brief does not date. That holds.
