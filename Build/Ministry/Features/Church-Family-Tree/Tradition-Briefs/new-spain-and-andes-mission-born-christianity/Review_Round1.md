# Review, round 1: new-spain-and-andes-mission-born-christianity

Reviewer: Opus, independent of the writer. Brief read: `new-spain-and-andes-mission-born-christianity.brief.txt` (454 words by the scorer). Sources checked by opening the lines myself in `cic/texts/`: `motolinia_historia-de-los-indios-de-la-nueva-espana-spa_sanchez-garcia1914.txt` (Motolinia; accents dropped, OCR noisy) and `acosta_natural-and-moral-history-of-the-indies-v2_grimston-markham1880.txt` (Acosta, English of 1604).

## Verdict: REVISE

Four BLOCKING findings, each with wording below. Both quotes match their recorded renderings word for word, and the 1536 date, the twelve friars, January 1523, the smallpox figures, the providence claim and the river scene all hold in the lines. The errors are in what the plagues paragraph leaves out, how the three counts are framed in time, the two "library" sentences, and the river scene told after a paragraph set fifty years later.

## 1. One hearing, as a new listener

- **Who or what:** the Christianity born when Franciscan friars baptized the native peoples of Mexico after the Spanish conquest, heard through one friar, Motolinia, and later a Jesuit, Acosta.
- **What happened:** smallpox and war had struck the land; the friars came in 1523 and counted millions of baptisms; Motolinia read the plagues as God's work; Acosta later saw native rites as near the Gospel but mixed with abomination; people crossed a flooded river so their children could be baptized.
- **Why the connection matters:** the people baptized are heard only through those who baptized them, and the counts say nothing of belief.

I could write all three lines. The essence (a mission among a conquered people, heard only through the friars) is there by about word 115, but it is thinned by two sentences about the record rather than the story (B3), and the brief never says plainly what the friars came for. The Andes, half the tradition's name, get one sentence that is about the library, not about the Andes (B3).

Where I got lost: at "then close to five million, then more than nine million in fifteen years", I heard three counts made at three later times, rising, and so could not see why they "do not agree" (B2). At "Motolinía also told a smaller scene", after a paragraph set around 1590, I was carried back to the 1520s (B4). Nothing reads as a children's book. The voice is plain and adult; the plagues paragraph runs as short one-act sentences.

Told twice: "more than four million" is in the opening quote and again in the counts paragraph. Here the repeat sets up the disagreement, so it is MINOR (M5). The point that we hear only the friars is made three times (M4).

The four faults (Kit Part 1):
- **One witness's claim told as fact:** no. Motolinia is credited at the twelve, the death toll, the providence and the raft; "War killed many" and "many houses stood empty" sit inside his credited paragraph. The counts are told as his.
- **An editor's view given to the source:** no. The brief does not make Motolinia say he was one of the twelve (the editor's line, 1328).
- **A date or detail moved:** the empty houses onto the first two plagues (B1); the counts onto three times (B2); the gourds onto this raft (M7).
- **Force left out:** yes (B1). Motolinia's own ten plagues are mostly Spanish rule: tribute, slavery, the mines. Cutting them to smallpox and war leaves the friar's witness against his own side out, and that witness is the strongest thing his book says about the land the church was born in.
