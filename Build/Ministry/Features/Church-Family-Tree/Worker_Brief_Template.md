# Who worker brief

Approved to proceed (Mark, 2026-11-15). The first paragraph below goes, word for word, into the brief of every worker who drafts or revises a Who profile. The second goes into the brief of every worker who reviews one; its wording follows Mark's "Yes" of 2026-11-15 to the three parts of the review process. Both replace any instruction to copy an earlier batch. The decision is in `Decision-Log.md`, "The Who writing standard restored to the eight early-church briefs" (2026-11-15).

## The paragraph for a writer

Before you draft, read `Who_Profile_Pattern.md` in full and all eight briefs in `Who-Profiles/bar/`. They are the standard. Do not open an earlier batch to see how a brief is written, and do not match its length, its shape or its phrasing; earlier batches are being rewritten to this same bar. Mark's definition of craft is the test: "good story telling and flow, no ai tells hanging sentences and everything is relevant to the story." Write each brief as a story an adult would hear on BBC News or read in National Geographic. Open in a sourced moment, follow the life, put the reason in the sentence with the act, and say in a sentence or two why this person mattered. Leave no sentence standing alone, and end where the story ends. A last line picked because it is quotable is a fault. Name ancient witnesses inside the story with their leaning in a few words. Keep editors, editions and dating arguments for the card's Sources and What is uncertain. A date, count or judgment that only a modern scholar gives is named to him in a clause or left to the card, and is told flat nowhere. Invent nothing, check every quote against the vendored file, and tell a dispute as a dispute. Aim at about two minutes; go shorter where the sources are thin, and longer only where the story needs it and your batch note says why. When you finish a draft, read it beside two of the eight and apply Mark's five craft tests from checklist item 10 in your batch note, quoting your own sentences. Then score it: grade 8.0 to 10.0 on the full text, reading ease 60 or above with names left out. If it scores below grade 8, join and explain; do not reach for longer words. In review, a fix may join sentences as well as split them.

## The paragraph for a reviewer

Read `Who_Profile_Pattern.md` in full and all eight briefs in `Who-Profiles/bar/` before you read the batch. Compare every brief to the eight, never to an earlier batch. First do the blind pairing you are given: say which text of the unlabeled pair is stronger and why, and record it in the review file before you learn which is which. If you cannot tell them apart, or you prefer the new brief without a concrete reason, say so; that is a finding for the coordinator. Then run the checklist. For item 10, apply Mark's five craft tests to the whole brief: for each test, quote the brief's weakest sentence for that test beside the model passage from the eight, and say plainly whether the brief reaches that level. No test passes without quoted evidence. Look at the signals (joining words per 100, the share of source talk, a close on a quotation, an opening on "The X was Y", runs of short sentences). A signal tells you to read harder; it does not pass or fail a brief. A failed craft test is a blocking finding, and its fix is a rewrite of the passage. A fix may join sentences as well as split them. The batch does not go live until Mark has read its sample, which the coordinator picks.
