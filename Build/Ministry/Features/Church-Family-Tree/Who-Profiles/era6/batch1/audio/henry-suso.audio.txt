[calmly] A woman in poverty and suffering drew from a friar how his inner life began. His Life says she wrote it down "by stealth, so that he knew not of it." When he found out "this ghostly theft", he made her give up the pages and "burnt all of it that was there." A message from God, the Life says, kept him from burning the rest. What follows stayed "for the most part just as she had written it with her own hand."

The friar is Henry Suso, a Dominican of Constance, whose Life tells his story in the third person, as the Servitor of the Eternal Wisdom. A later chapter names the woman as Elsbeth Stagel, a Dominican nun at Töss. Thomas Francis Knox, who put the Life into English in 1865, says Suso was born in 1300. Karl Bihlmeyer, who edited his German works in 1907, reckons about 1295.

The prologue says Suso added teaching of his own after her death, in her name. Bihlmeyer writes that the extent of his reworking can no longer be fixed.

The Life tells that Suso, burning with love, went into his cell and bared his breast. With a pointed stylus, he cut "the Name of Jesus" into the flesh above his heart. The wound healed, but the letters stayed. Later, the Life says, Stagel stitched the Name in red silk on little cloths, in the form IHS.

He had been told that his entry into the order came with a gift of worldly goods. The Life says this woke in him an inordinate fear of the sin called simony. For about ten years he saw himself as damned. Then he went to Meister Eckhart, a Dominican master of theology, and "the holy man delivered him". Later, at a chapter in the Netherlands, two men of high rank charged that his books taught false doctrine. He was harshly rebuked and threatened with punishment, though the Life holds he was guiltless.

Bihlmeyer writes that Suso died at Ulm on 25 January 1366 and was buried inside the friars' church. Knox gives the same day in 1365, with burial in the cloister.

[warmly] In a 1910 English text of his Little Book of Eternal Wisdom, he seeks rest in created things. Every image warns him away, "I am not what thou seekest!" At last he cries, "Tender loving Wisdom! And is it Thou I have so long been seeking for?"
