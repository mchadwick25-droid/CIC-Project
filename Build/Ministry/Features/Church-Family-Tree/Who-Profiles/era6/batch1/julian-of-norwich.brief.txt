A woman lay sick and believed she would die. Her curate came and set a cross before her face. "I have brought thee the Image of thy Maker and Saviour", he said. The room went dark "as if it had been night, save in the Image of the Cross". Then, she writes, all her pain was taken from her, and the showings, as she calls her visions, began.

She dates them to 13 May 1373. She was Julian of Norwich, and this is her own account, in Grace Warrack's modern spelling of 1901. She never names herself in it. A copyist's heading on a shorter version calls her "a devout woman, and her name is Julian, that is recluse at Norwich". The heading adds that she is still alive. A recluse, or anchoress, lived shut in a cell beside a church. Dundas Harford, who printed that shorter text after the British Museum bought it in 1909, reads its year as 1413. Francis Blomefield, an eighteenth-century historian of Norfolk, read 1442, and Warrack follows him. Harford calls that "a curious mistake".

She was "thirty years old and a half" when the sickness came, she says. Before this, she had asked God for three gifts. They were to have Christ's Passion in mind, a sickness near to death at thirty, and three wounds, of contrition, compassion and longing toward God.

In one showing she saw "a little thing, the quantity of an hazel-nut, in the palm of my hand". She wondered what it might be, and was answered: "it is all that is made." She had often wondered why God did not prevent sin. The answer came: "It behoved that there should be sin; but all shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well."

In Warrack's text she calls herself "a simple creature unlettered". In the shorter text, in Harford's spelling, she refuses to be called a teacher, for "I am a woman unlearned, feeble and frail". "But truly charity stirs me to tell you it," she goes on. Being a woman, she says, is no reason to keep silent about God's goodness, since she saw that God wills it known.

In the long version, which Harford holds grew out of the short, she writes that she often longed to learn the Lord's meaning. "Fifteen years after, and more," an answer came: "Learn it well: Love was His meaning."
