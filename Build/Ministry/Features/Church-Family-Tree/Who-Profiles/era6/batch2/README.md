# Who profiles: era 6, batch 2

Eight profiles, one id each: `catherine-of-siena`, `bridget-of-sweden`, `bernardino-of-siena`, `angelo-clareno`, `william-of-ockham`, `gregory-palamas`, `gregory-of-sinai`, `nicholas-cabasilas`.

These are eight WHO rows of `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-12_who-scan-era6/Who_Scan_Era6.md`, the second era 6 batch (1300 to 1517) under Mark's ruling of 2026-11-14 that eras 4 to 7 are built in full. None was written before, and none is a live census person entry; the eight appear in their traditions' voices, people lists and texts (Ockham is a Franciscan Order voice but not on its people list). Many of the scan's years rest on a Library file header or an editor's reckoning, so the cards give the years the held sources give and say whose they are.

The coordinator marked all eight major on 2026-11-14, as the scan's Major column says "yes". The tradition caps hold: Hesychasm & the Palamite Synthesis 3 (Palamas, Gregory of Sinai, Cabasilas), The Latin Papal Church 2 (Catherine, Bridget), Observant Reform 1 (Bernardino), Fraticelli 1 (Clareno) and The Franciscan Order 1 (Ockham). The river caps do not all hold. Greek East & Orthodoxy holds 3 era 6 majors. Latin West & Catholicism held batch 1's eight, and with this batch's five it holds 13 against the 5 the map shows. Which five the river shows is Mark's pick, as for eras 4 and 5 (Open-Gaps 263); an era 6 entry is registered with this batch, and the coordinator asked for the count to be noted here. The briefs run 403 to 406 words, about 2:30; the 2:30 is a cap, not a target. `grade_text` scores them at grade 6.6 to 8.6 and Flesch Reading Ease 61.4 to 72.5.

- **Catherine of Siena** opens on Raymond of Capua's account of the Avignon audience, from Mirbt's extract of his Life, and is told from Vida Scudder's letters and chronology, Thorold's Dialogue and Fiorilli's notes. Her part in the pope's return is told through Scudder and Duranti, with Gobelinus's credit to Bridget's revelations in the brief, and Fiorilli, Platina and Scudder's note of the pope's later coolness on the card, and tagged Contested. The Dialogue's date (Thorold's 1370 against the 1378 colophon) and the stigmata (Scudder's chronology, and the bull of Sixtus IV against pictures of them, which the Bollandist note reports with its later softening) are tagged Contested. Her birth day and canonization each rest on one witness.
- **Bridget of Sweden** opens on the Naples revelation of early 1373, from the Latin of 1628. How her Swedish became Latin is tagged Contested: Cumming's two accounts, Duranti's two accounts, Torquemada's two Peters and Wilkens on her Latin as rhetorical are set side by side, and none is settled. Her death day and canonization day differ within the witnesses, and the card shows both.
- **Bernardino of Siena** opens on his own story of the sow-thistle, in Robins's English. The Siena sermons are said plainly to be Benedetto's report. Glassberger's accusation before Martin V, Bernardino's own words on his hearing at Rome with Banchi's note, and Eugenius IV's letter of 1432 are told as separate records, and the card says no witness joins them. His place and age at death differ by witness.
- **Angelo Clareno** opens on the Avignon hearing told in the History of the Seven Tribulations, and the book's authorship is tagged Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Ehrle's argument, followed by Eubel and Oliger), with Affò, Richard and Tocco on the card. His letter of defense is told in modern words from Ehrle's 1885 text. His death year is Ehrle's reckoning, using Papebroch's list of the years the weekday fits, and the card says so; the Bollandists give 14 June for the day.
- **William of Ockham** opens on the night flight of 1328, from Carlini and Lea, and is told through John XXII's own letters in Eubel's Bullarium. The 1323 Bologna friar is told with Eubel's doubt, and the heresy of his writings, his death year and the 1349 reconciliation are tagged Contested. His death on 10 April 1347 is the Munich book of the dead's and Glassberger's, with Wadding, Lea and the Quaracchi editors against it. No held line has "Ockham's razor", and it is not told.
- **Gregory Palamas** opens on Kantakouzenos's account of his release from the palace prison in 1347, with Kantakouzenos marked as his partisan. Philotheos's Encomium is called a speech in his praise. The essence and energies dispute is tagged Contested, with Gregoras's charge of many gods named. No held source text gives his birth or death year; Philotheos gives his age, and the 1857 editor's reckoning points to 1362.
- **Gregory of Sinai** rests on the Philokalia's notice, reprinted by Migne, with Meyer, Rački, Basil of Poiana Marului and Paisius as later witnesses. The brief tells his teaching in his own words, in modern English. Paroria as a place, Meyer's Crete and his thirteenth-century dating are on the card with their witnesses.
- **Nicholas Cabasilas** opens on the patriarchal election of 1353, where Kantakouzenos calls him still a layman. How he was Nilus's nephew, whether the "Kabasilas of the sakellion" is he and which anti-Latin writings are his are tagged Contested. That he became archbishop of Thessalonica is tagged Widely Accepted, on Fabricius, Gass and a Thessalonica record; no held line gives the date. Gregoras's old friend is Boivin's identification, followed by Gass.

Held witnesses beyond those the scan cites, among them:

- **Bridget.** Torquemada's prologue; Boniface IX's bulls; Duranti's notes and treatise on visions; Scudder's chronology; Lea; Rodrigo Alvarez's note; Mansi 26 on Gobelinus; her daughter's Life in the Acta Sanctorum for March; Wilkens; Richental; the Declaratio printed with Gerson; Glassberger on Basel; Olavus Petri; the York wills.
- **Bernardino.** Raynaldus's annals, with Sigonius's Bologna story and Surius's account of the death; the Bullarium nova series, with Eugenius IV's letter of 1432, the letters of 1440 and 1447 and the bull of canonization; Vespasiano da Bisticci's Life in Banchi's first volume; Lea.
- **Clareno.** Ehrle's 1885 Archiv volume, with the full letter of defense and the letters; Döllinger's extract of the Chronicon; Rader's preface in Migne PG 88.
- **Ockham.** Carlini, with the Munich book of the dead and the 1338 protest; the 1328 to 1330 letters in Eubel's Bullarium; Konrad of Megenberg quoting Clement VI; Shirley; Gerson; Luther; Reusch's Index of 1564.
- **Palamas.** Kantakouzenos; Gregoras, volumes 2 and 3; Miklosich and Müller's patriarchal acts; the Philokalia; Mansi 26 with Combefis's notes; the Bollandists after Allatius; Covel.
- **Gregory of Sinai.** The Philokalia of 1893; Meyer; Rački; Paisius Velichkovsky's volume with Basil of Poiana Marului's prefaces; Nil Sorsky.
- **Cabasilas.** Kantakouzenos, volumes 2 and 3; Miklosich and Müller; Gass; Phrantzes; Symeon of Thessalonica; Pallavicino on Trent; Petit in Patrologia Orientalis 15.

Corrections to the scan found in drafting:
- **Catherine.** Her death line is Scudder's chronology at l.1394-1395. Cumming's l.980-990 never names Catherine; the line that pairs the two women is Duranti's note. Fiorilli's file was opened and holds the dictation witnesses.
- **Bridget.** Cumming gives both 23 and 28 July 1373, and the bulls set the feast on 23 July. Cumming's line that Torquemada led Gregory XI's commission is against Torquemada's own prologue, which places his examination at Basel in 1435. The birth year has a Cumming line, so it is tagged Contested.
- **Bernardino.** The Bullarium nova series lines 575 and 1109 name only Capistrano. Glassberger's l.16242-16267 gives the plague service at twenty and the entry in 1402 at twenty-two. Raynaldus was missing from the scan.
- **Clareno.** Ehrle's 1885 volume was missing from the scan. The birth year c.1255 is the file header's. The 1334 bull does not name him; the name is Eubel's bracket. The authorship case is at Ehrle II l.5678-6150, and the hearing at l.7669-7740.
- **Ockham.** His death and burial are held. The 1323 Bologna letter's friar may be William of Alnwick, by Eubel. The Mansi 25 "Willielmus de Okam", a parish rector and doctor in 1311, is a namesake and is not used.
- **Palamas.** The scan's dates rest on file headers only. PALH 3584, 5885 and 10142 are running page heads, and PALH 10569 is Neilos's, not Philotheos's. The PG151 Latin note is taken from Oudin and is hostile.
- **Gregory of Sinai.** The life notice is the Philokalia's, reprinted by Migne. PG150 l.60950-60975 is the head of the Ascetica and Palamas's notice; Gregory's chapters begin at l.69298.
- **Cabasilas.** The embassy urging Kantakouzenos to step down is 1343 in his history and names only "Kabasilas of the sakellion"; the 1346 embassy that names Nicholas went to Manuel at Berroia. The Life in Christ is in PG150 in Greek as well as Latin. "Lay theologian" and "nephew of Nilus" are told with their witnesses, since Fabricius makes him Nilus's successor in the see.

Names and ids, the worker's calls:
- **Catherine of Siena**, `catherine-of-siena`, the census voice name. Catherine Benincasa is Scudder's form. Caterina Vigri of Bologna and Bridget's daughter Catherine of Sweden are different women.
- **Bridget of Sweden**, `bridget-of-sweden`, the census voice name. Cumming holds Birgitta to be her correct name, and the brief gives it. Brigid of Kildare is a different woman.
- **Bernardino of Siena**, `bernardino-of-siena`, the census name. Bernardino Ochino and the Carmelite Bernardinus de Senis of the order's general chapters are different men, and so is Bernardino of Aquila, the chronicler.
- **Angelo Clareno**, `angelo-clareno`, the census name. Peter of Fossombrone was his earlier name, and the card gives it in its Latin form, Petrus de Forosempronio. Angelo Tancredi is a different man. Whether the Angelo of the Spoleto valley of the 1331 bull is Clareno is open: Raynaldus seems inclined to think so and Tocco holds it, Eubel doubts it, and Oliger leaves it unsettled.
- **William of Ockham**, `william-of-ockham`, the census voice name. The Mansi rector of 1311 and Nicholas of Ockham are kept apart.
- **Gregory Palamas**, `gregory-palamas`, the census name. Gregory Akindynos and Gregory of Sinai are named in full, and Philotheos is named as patriarch of Constantinople at his first mention in the brief and in the card.
- **Gregory of Sinai**, `gregory-of-sinai`, the census name; the sources call him the Sinaite. The patriarch Kallistos is kept apart from Kallistos Xanthopoulos.
- **Nicholas Cabasilas**, `nicholas-cabasilas`, the census name. His uncle Nilus Cabasilas is named at once in the brief, since Fabricius warns the two are often confused.

Card traditions are the census's names. All eight fit the scan's placings:
- Catherine and Bridget read "The Latin Papal Church (Avignon to Fifth Lateran)" (1300 to 1517). Both are among its voices, and their lives in the papal cities fall inside its years.
- Bernardino reads "Observant Reform & Popular Preaching" (c. 1368 to 1517). He is one of its voices.
- Clareno reads "Fraticelli (Spiritual Franciscan rigorists)" (1300 to 1466), where he is the first voice; he is also a voice of the Franciscan Order entry. His years after 1294 fall inside both.
- Ockham reads "The Franciscan Order — Spirituals Crisis to the Great Division" (1300 to 1517), where he is a voice. The scan's "also Fraticelli" is not used: that entry names him in its text among the friars who fled the papal court, but not among its voices.
- Palamas, Gregory of Sinai and Cabasilas read "Hesychasm & the Palamite Synthesis" (c. 1300 to 1400). All three are among its voices. Palamas is also a voice of the Byzantine Church, Palaiologan Era.

Lines held only in Latin, Greek, Italian, German, Croatian or Church Slavonic are told in modern words with the source named. Quotation marks are kept only for lines held in English: Scudder's and Thorold's Catherine, Cumming's Middle English Bridget, Robins's Bernardino, Lea's and Shirley's English, and the 1931 English Luther. Quotations keep their own spelling, including "sleer of soules" and "doth". Where an OCR slip falls inside a quoted line, the quotation is given as the editor printed it ("Holy Chureh" in the Robins file is read as "Holy Church").
