[calmly] Before he became a friar, Bernardino of Siena decided to live in a wood, on grass and water. He told the story himself, preaching at Siena in 1427. He bought a Bible, and chamois skin to keep it dry. He went looking for one near Massa, could not choose a hill, and came home. Outside a city gate he tried a meal of sow-thistle. "Chew, and chew, it would not go down!" One mouthful, he said, rid him of the whole temptation.

He was born in 1380 and joined the Franciscans in 1402, at twenty-two. For about forty-four years, writes Nazareno Orlandi, editing his sermons in 1920, he preached more often in town squares than in churches.

The Siena sermons survive because a cloth-shearer, Benedetto, son of Master Bartolomeo, stood in the Campo with wax tablets. He took them down as he listened and wrote them out in his shop. Luciano Banchi printed this listener's report in 1880.

Bernardino preached devotion to the name of Jesus. The historian Carlo Sigonius, quoted in the church annals of Odoricus Raynaldus, tells a story from Bologna. After his preaching, games were burned, and a painter of playing cards complained he had lost his living. Bernardino told him to paint this instead. He drew a sun, and inside it the name of Jesus in a new form of letters.

Nicolaus Glassberger, an Observant Franciscan who wrote a chronicle about 1508, says Dominican preachers accused him before Pope Martin V of leading people toward a kind of idolatry. The pope heard him, found nothing wrong, and let him preach everywhere. In a letter of January 1432, Pope Eugenius IV quashed another charge, brought by Michael, a Prague priest, and called Bernardino a fierce rooter-out of heresy.

In 1439, Glassberger writes, Bernardino was made vicar general over all the Observants of Italy, the friars who kept the rule strictly. In May 1444, on his way to preach at L'Aquila, he fell ill and died. His friend Vespasiano da Bisticci, a Florentine bookseller who wrote his Life, says he died before reaching the town; Glassberger places his death in its friary. Pope Nicholas V made him a saint at Pentecost 1450.
