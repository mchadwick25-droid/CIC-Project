Gregory of Sinai came to Mount Athos and went round its monasteries and hermitages. He found many wise and serious monks who did not know guarding of the mind, stillness or contemplation even by name. At the skete of Magoula, opposite the monastery of Philotheou, he met only three who gave a little time to contemplation: Isaiah, Cornelius and Macarius.

The story comes from a short notice by the editors of the Philokalia, a Greek collection on prayer first printed in 1782. They say he became a monk on Mount Sinai, and so was called the Sinaite. They place him about 1330, under an emperor Andronikos. His disciple Kallistos, later patriarch of Constantinople, wrote his Life at length. Paisius Velichkovsky, whose community copied it, says a Greek copy that reached them lacked the account of his death. Citing the Life in 1894, the German scholar Philipp Meyer says Gregory first learned the prayer of the mind on Crete, from a hermit named Arsenios.

On Athos, the notice says, he taught hermits and the monks in communities alike about watchfulness, guarding the mind and prayer of the mind. Basil of Poiana Marului, in a preface to Gregory's book, says the most learned fathers of Athos at first opposed him.

The Fathers said the prayer whole, Gregory wrote: Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me. Others said half, Jesus, Son of God, have mercy on me, which is easier for a weak mind. Some say it with the mouth and some with the mind, he added; he set down both. Sit on a stool, he told the monk, bend low, gather your mind into your heart and call on the Lord Jesus. When your shoulders hurt and your head aches, hold on, seeking the Lord in your heart.

He built three large lauras, communities of monks, at Paroria, near the borders of Macedonia. Migne's Latin takes Paroria to mean the borders, but the 1893 Philokalia prints it as a place name. Franjo Rački, a Croatian historian, retold in 1869 a Life of Theodosius of Trnovo written by Kallistos. There, monks of Greek and Slavic birth gather round Gregory. Theodosius comes to him at Paroria, and Gregory sends him to Tsar Alexander of Bulgaria to seek protection from the Turks.

The working of the Holy Spirit, Gregory wrote, is given in baptism to every believer. It lies buried under the passions and waits for our repentance.
