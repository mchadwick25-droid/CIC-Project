In 306 the Roman persecution of the church was in its fourth year. Peter was bishop of Alexandria in Egypt, in the church of Alexandrian Christianity. Many Christians had given way under torture, and now they asked to come back.

That Easter Peter set the terms. Those who had fallen only after prison and torture, he wrote, "did not come to this by their own choice, but were betrayed by the weakness of the flesh." They were to fast forty more days and then be received. He also forbade anyone to accuse those who had fled to save their lives.

While he made room for the fallen, another Egyptian bishop, Meletius of Lycopolis, was ordaining clergy in other bishops' districts, and four bishops in prison wrote to protest. An old account kept with their letter says he then came to Alexandria and replaced priests Peter had appointed. Peter told his people to hold no communion with Meletius until he could meet him.

Why the two broke is disputed. Athanasius, a later bishop of Alexandria and the Meletians' enemy, says a council under Peter deposed Meletius for sacrificing to idols, among other crimes. Epiphanius of Salamis says the two were prisoners together and split over the fallen. Peter pleaded for mercy, Meletius wanted them kept waiting until peace came, and most of the clergy there sided with Meletius.

In the ninth year of the persecution Peter was seized without warning and beheaded, but the split outlived him. In 325 the council of Nicaea left Meletius the name of bishop with no power to ordain. Athanasius says the Meletians then spread charges against him, as they had against Peter, and still bore Meletius's name in his day.

Peter's rules lasted too. In 692 the council in Trullo set its seal on his canons, making them church law.
