Polycarp of Smyrna was an old bishop whom Irenaeus of Lyon calls a man "instructed by apostles." Once he met Marcion of Pontus face to face. Marcion asked him, "Dost thou know me?" Polycarp answered, "I do know thee, the first-born of Satan." Almost everything we know of Marcion comes, like this story, from his opponents. None of his own books survives.

Justin Martyr, writing in Rome while Marcion was still alive, says he was teaching his followers "to believe in some other god greater than the Creator." Irenaeus says he took up the teaching of a man named Cerdo and flourished while Anicetus was bishop of Rome, in the middle of the second century. Tertullian of Carthage dates him later, to the time of Bishop Eleutherus. Tertullian calls him "that shipmaster of Pontus," and says Marcion gave money to the church at Rome, which rejected the money along with the man. Later, Tertullian says, Marcion offered to repent, but he died before he could return.

On the core of his teaching the opponents agree. Justin, Irenaeus and Tertullian all say he set another god above the Creator, the God of the law and the prophets. Irenaeus adds that Marcion called the Creator "the author of evils," and that the higher God was the Father of Jesus. In Tertullian's words, "Marcion's special and principal work is the separation of the law and the gospel." Irenaeus says he cut down the Gospel of Luke and the letters of Paul, removing the passages that called the Maker of the world the Father of Jesus.

His following spread. Justin says many believed him "as if he alone knew the truth." Tertullian says, "Even wasps make combs; so also these Marcionites make churches."

Two later writers tell another story. A book Against All Heresies, printed with Tertullian's works, calls Marcion "son of a bishop, excommunicated because of a rape committed on a certain virgin." Epiphanius of Salamis, two centuries after Marcion, tells it at more length. He adds that the bishop was Marcion's own father, at the city of Sinope, a claim that is his alone. Karl Holl, who edited Epiphanius's Greek, notes that the one other early writer to name Sinope copied him. Holl doubted the story, because Tertullian never tells it. Tertullian, who wrote five books against him, speaks of "the continence of Marcion."
