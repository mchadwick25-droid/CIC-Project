[calmly] In Rome, the teacher remembered as Justin Martyr stood before the city prefect, Rusticus, with six other Christians. An account of the trial survives. Rusticus asked where the Christians met. Justin said he lived "above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath," and knew of no other meeting. Then Rusticus asked him, "Are you not, then, a Christian?" Justin answered, "Yes, I am a Christian."

Justin was the son of Priscus, from Flavia Neapolis in Palestine. He tells his own story at the opening of his Dialogue with Trypho. As a young man he went from teacher to teacher, looking for God. A Stoic could tell him nothing about God, and a Peripatetic asked for his fee too soon. A Pythagorean turned him away because he knew no music, astronomy or geometry. A Platonist gave his mind "wings." Then, walking alone in a field near the sea, he met an old man who pointed him to the Hebrew prophets. "But straightway a flame was kindled in my soul," Justin wrote. He still called himself a philosopher: "Thus, and for this reason, I am a philosopher."

At Rome he taught, and he wrote two Apologies, or defences, of the Christians. The first is addressed to the emperor Antoninus Pius and his sons. In it he argues that Christ is the Word of God, and that every race of people has had some share in that Word. So, he writes, "those who lived reasonably are Christians, even though they have been thought atheists," and he names Socrates of Athens and Heraclitus among the Greeks.

Justin expected to die for this. In his Second Apology he wrote that he looked to be "plotted against and fixed to the stake," perhaps by Crescens, a Cynic philosopher who called Christians atheists. Eusebius of Caesarea says a plot by Crescens brought about his death. Arthur McGiffert, who translated Eusebius, points out that Crescens is known only from Justin and Justin's pupil Tatian. They were, in McGiffert's words, Crescens's "bitterest enemies." The trial account does not name Crescens at all. McGiffert dates the trial to 163 or later, when Rusticus was prefect of Rome.

The account says Rusticus had Justin and the others scourged and beheaded. Before the sentence, Rusticus asked whether he really thought he would go up to heaven. [warmly] "I do not suppose it," Justin said, "but I know and am fully persuaded of it."
