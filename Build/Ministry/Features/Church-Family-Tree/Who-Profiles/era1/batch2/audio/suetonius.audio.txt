[calmly] Around 120 Suetonius was secretary to the emperor Hadrian in Rome. He was not a Christian, and his lives of twelve Caesars, from Julius Caesar to Domitian, give the Christians two short notices.

The first, in the life of Claudius, reads: "The Jews were constantly causing disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus, and he expelled them from Rome." The book of Acts also says that Claudius ordered all the Jews to leave Rome, so the expulsion has a second witness. Who Chrestus was is disputed, because Suetonius says nothing more about him. The line reads as if Chrestus were in Rome under Claudius, and Tacitus puts the death of Christ earlier, under Tiberius. So Suetonius may mean another man of that name, or he may have a confused report of quarrels over Christ among Rome's Jews. Romans did get the name wrong. Tertullian of Carthage complained that they said Chrestian for Christian, and Lactantius later wrote that the ignorant changed one letter and called Christ Chrestus.

The second notice is in the life of Nero, in a list of abuses he put down, between a rule on what taverns could sell and a curb on chariot drivers. "The Christians were punished, a group of people given to a new and harmful superstition." Elsewhere Suetonius says Nero set fire to Rome, but he does not connect the Christians with the fire, and he gives no reason for the punishments.

The sources we hold show no early Christian writer quoting either notice. [warmly] Yet near the end of the fourth century Jerome wrote that he had been urged to follow the example of Suetonius and do for the church's writers what he had done for the famous authors of the pagans.
