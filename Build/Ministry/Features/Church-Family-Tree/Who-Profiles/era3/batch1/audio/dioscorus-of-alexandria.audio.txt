[calmly] At the Council of Chalcedon in 451, the minutes of Eutyches's trial were read. Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, broke in. The acts record him saying, "I receive 'the of two;' 'the two' I do not receive." He added, "I am forced to be impudent, but the matter is one which touches my soul."

He had followed Cyril of Alexandria as bishop. Charles Lett Feltoe, who translated Leo of Rome's letters, says Leo wrote to him in 445 to lay down church rules. Leo told him, "you and we ought to be at one in thought and act."

In 449, Feltoe says, Dioscorus presided at a council at Ephesus by the emperor's order. Its minutes, in S. G. F. Perry's English, give his sentence on Flavian, bishop of Constantinople, and Eusebius of Dorylaeum. He judged them "alien from all the Honor of the Priesthood and of the Episcopate." Flavian answered, "I repudiate you. I protest." Leo later called that meeting one "not of judges but of robbers."

Evagrius of Epiphania, a historian born some eighty years later, gives the charges. Chalcedon convicted Dioscorus of hiding Leo's letter, and of having bishops sign a blank paper to depose Flavian. Called three times, he did not come, and the council wrote that he was deposed on the thirteenth of October. Evagrius says he was sent to live at Gangra in Paphlagonia.

The Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene, from the side that rejected Chalcedon, tells it another way. It says he was deposed for teaching that Christ became incarnate from two natures, and for refusing two after the union. Asked to sign and regain his throne, he said, "Sooner would Dioscorus see his own hand cut off, and the blood falling on the paper, than do such a thing as that." In Egypt, it says, priests, monks and many of the people broke with his successor.

A History of him goes under the name of his disciple Theopistus. Its editor, François Nau, does not vouch for many details. In it he tells the empress Pulcheria she may take his throne of wood, but not the one the Messiah has prepared for him in heaven. Nau and Feltoe date his death in exile to 454.

[warmly] The History of the Patriarchs of the Coptic Church says, "He preserved the orthodox faith, which persists in the see of the evangelist Saint Mark to this day and for ever."
