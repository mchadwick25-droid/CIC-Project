Zachariah Rhetor came to Berytus, now Beirut, to study civil law, and he expected trouble. In his Life of Severus of Antioch he says new students were teased by the others, and he feared Severus most of all. On his first day in the law school of Leontius, he found Severus among the students. Severus greeted him kindly.

Zachariah came from the port of Gaza, as he says in that Life. He had studied beside Severus at Alexandria, hearing the same teachers. E. W. Brooks introduced the English translation of the chronicle that bears Zachariah's name. From the Life, he places their studies in the emperor Zeno's reign and says Zachariah later worked as an advocate in Constantinople.

He wrote the Life to defend his friend, who was by then bishop of Antioch. It opens with a friend who comes to him from the bookstalls of the Royal Portico in Constantinople, upset by a pamphlet that mocks Severus. Brooks says enemies charged that Severus had followed pagan rites in his youth. Brooks finds Zachariah's answer "a very lame one."

Zachariah also wrote a church history in Greek for Eupraxius, who served at the imperial palace, ending in 491 by Brooks's reckoning. Its preface attacks the Council of Chalcedon. A later Syriac compiler used the history in a longer chronicle. Brooks says the compiler followed Zachariah in books three to six only, and that later writers wrongly gave him the whole work. Land, its editor of 1870, gives him book seven as well.

Brooks also takes him for the Zachariah Scholasticus who, by the heading of a Greek dialogue, later became bishop of Mitylene. A bishop of that name was at a synod in 536, Brooks says. Brooks suggests, with a "perhaps," that he had come over to Chalcedon's side when he became bishop.

In that preface Zachariah says the council "tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ, woven from the top throughout."
