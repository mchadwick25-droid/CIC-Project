[calmly] After a siege of Arles in Gaul, the Goths came back into the city with a great crowd of captives. Caesarius, bishop of Arles, gave the church's silver to buy them free. His disciples report what he often said to clergy who would not do the same. In modern words, he said God would not mind the vessels of his service buying people back, since Christ gave himself to redeem us.

His Life was written by the bishops Cyprian of Toulon, Firminus and Viventius, with the priest Messianus and the deacon Stephen. They wrote for Caesaria, the abbess who then led the convent he founded. It is a saint's Life by men who called him father.

He came from near Chalon, they say. At about eighteen he asked its bishop, Silvester, to take him into the church's service, without his family knowing. Later he joined the monks on the island of Lérins. Fasting wore down his body, and his abbot sent him to Arles to recover. Eonius, bishop of Arles, ordained him and chose him as his successor. The Life says Caesarius hid among the tombs to escape it.

Arles was then under Alaric, the Arian king of the Visigoths. One of Caesarius's own notaries, the Life says, accused him of plotting to give the city to the Burgundians, and he was exiled to Bordeaux. After Arles passed to the Ostrogoths, he was taken under guard to their king, Theodoric, at Ravenna. The king gave him a silver dish, and he sold it to free more captives.

His teaching on grace had rivals in Gaul, the Life says. At a meeting of bishops in Valence, Cyprian of Toulon argued for him. No one can make any progress toward God by himself, Cyprian said, unless God's grace first calls him. Boniface, bishop of Rome, upheld Caesarius. He signed first at a council in Orange, whose chapters on grace and free will came from Rome. Friedrich Maassen, who edited its acts, dates Orange to 529 and leaves open which council came first.

He wrote rules for monks and for nuns. He sent sermons as far as Italy and Spain for priests to preach, and led the church of Arles for forty years.

[warmly] His Rule for the nuns opens with a request. When they entered the kingdom with the wise virgins, he asked, they should pray that he would not be left outside with the foolish.
