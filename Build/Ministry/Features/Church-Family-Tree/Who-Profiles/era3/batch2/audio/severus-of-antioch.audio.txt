[calmly] In the first year of his reign, the emperor Justin ordered the arrest of Severus, bishop of Antioch. Evagrius of Epiphania, a later historian on the side of the Council of Chalcedon, says some reported that Severus was to lose his tongue. He fled his see in September. E. W. Brooks, the English translator of his letters, dates the flight to 518.

Severus was born at Sozopolis in Pisidia. His fellow student Zachariah Rhetor wrote a Life of him, which says his widowed mother sent him and his two older brothers to Alexandria to study grammar and rhetoric. Later he studied law at Berytus, now Beirut. Brooks says Zachariah wrote to answer enemies who claimed Severus had followed pagan rites in his youth. In Brooks's view, "the answer is a very lame one."

Zachariah and Evagrius agree he was baptized at the shrine of the martyr Leontius at Tripolis. He became a monk between Gaza and Majuma, where Peter the Iberian had lived, Evagrius says. Nephalius, who had once shared his view and then taken Chalcedon's side, drove him and other monks out. Severus pleaded their cause at Constantinople and won the notice of the emperor Anastasius.

He replaced Flavian as bishop of Antioch in 512, Brooks says. Evagrius says his letters as bishop condemned Chalcedon. A letter Evagrius quotes, from Chalcedon's side, says they carried a curse on the council and on all who taught two natures in Christ.

After his flight he lived mostly in hiding in Egypt. There, Brooks says, he wrote against Julian of Halicarnassus, who held that Christ's body could not decay. Evagrius says the empress Theodora, who favored the single nature, persuaded Justinian to send for Severus. Brooks says he and Anthimus of Constantinople were expelled from the city in 536 and condemned at a synod that May. Evagrius says Justinian then cursed him by law. Brooks gives his death in Egypt as 8 February 538.

To the chamberlain Misael he wrote that, when he was raised to the see, he wished "not to know even the very name" of interest. Then he saw the church's debts. "I forgot the spiritual laws," he admits.

Brooks reckons his letters once numbered at least 3,759; a Syriac version made in 669 keeps 123. [warmly] In the letter to Misael, Severus says God alone is "able to heal our church that is groaning under many wounds, and to change need into abundance."
