The empress Theodora wrote to the duke of the Thebais, John of Ephesus says. She and her husband Justinian had each planned an embassy to the Nobadae, a people beyond the Thebais, at the edge of Egypt. She was sending a blessed man named Julian, and her envoy had to arrive first. If the duke let the emperor's envoy through before hers, she warned, "your life shall answer for it; for I will immediately send and take off your head." So the duke held back the emperor's envoy, saying he still needed pack animals and men who knew the deserts.

Julian was a presbyter and an old man, one of the clergy of Theodosius of Alexandria. He longed to make Christians of this people, John says. He took the plan to Theodora. Justinian disliked it, John writes, because Julian opposed the Council of Chalcedon, and he told bishops of his own side in the Thebais to go and teach them.

All that is known of Julian comes from John. John's English translator, Payne Smith, notes that he belonged to the party that refused Chalcedon, and John tells the story from Julian's side. The later chronicle of Michael the Syrian shortens John's account.

The king of the Nobadae received Julian with joy. He and his nobles gave up the gods of their fathers. Julian then told them of Chalcedon, and of Theodosius, who had lost his throne rather than accept the council. When the emperor's envoy came, they took the gifts and refused the faith. They would follow "the example of pope Theodosius," they answered.

Julian stayed two years. He used to say that "from nine o'clock until four in the afternoon" he sheltered in caves full of water, wearing only a linen cloth like the local people. Even so he taught, and baptized the king, his nobles and many people. He left them in the care of Theodore, an old bishop from the Thebais, whom John later calls bishop of Philae.

Julian returned safely to Constantinople, where Theodora received him with honour. He died a short time before Theodosius. John gives no year for any of it.

Afterwards a priest named Longinus was made bishop for the Nobadae, as Theodora had ordered. John calls Julian "their teacher." He says that Julian taught "both the king and his princes, and most of the tribes in his dominions, for a period of two years."
