About three hundred students walked out of the school of Nisibis, the Chronicle of Seert says. They carried Gospels and crosses on black veils, with censers, and sang the hymns of the Rogations. Only twenty people stayed behind, and hardly as many children. They were leaving in protest against their master, Henana of Adiabene.

Henana led the school of Nisibis, the great school of the Church of the East in the Persian empire. Addai Scher, who edited the chronicle, reckons he became its master in 572. His teaching is known only through his opponents. A pupil's praise of the man survives.

The chronicle comes from his opponents' side. It says Henana had long read unorthodox books and explained Scripture against Theodore of Mopsuestia, whom his church called the Interpreter. Abdisho of Nisibis, listing Henana's books, says many were rejected because of Theodore. Gregory of Kashkar, metropolitan of Nisibis, condemned his books, the chronicle says. Henana wrote to the catholicos Sabrisho, who took his side. Gregory shook the dust off his sandals at the city gate and left, and the students followed him.

The sharpest charges come from Babai the Great, abbot on Mount Izla, in his Life of the martyr George. In it a monk says Henana taught that fate rules everything and denied the resurrection of the body. Babai calls him a Chaldean, which the German scholar Oskar Braun reads as a charge of astrology, and a follower of Origen. Braun calls Babai passionate and full of exaggerations. J. Labourt calls Babai Henana's antagonist, and cannot say whether the charge of fatalism is slander.

Synods of his church in these years defended Theodore. They never name Henana, though their editors read some canons as aimed at him.

Mari ibn Sulayman, writing in the 1100s, says he had eight hundred students. His pupil Barhadbeshabba, later bishop of Halwan, wrote while Henana still taught, Scher says. Scher thinks the pupil hid the truth to praise his master.

Barhadbeshabba calls him gentle, merciful and patient, a man who does not seek his own glory. He says his master worked day and night at reading and explaining the Scriptures, and he prays that God will lengthen his days.
