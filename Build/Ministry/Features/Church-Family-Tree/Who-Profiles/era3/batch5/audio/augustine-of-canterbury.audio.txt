[calmly] King Ethelbert of Kent would not meet the strangers indoors. Bede of Jarrow says he feared they might work magic on him, so he sat in the open air on Thanet. Augustine of Canterbury and his companions came singing a litany. They bore a silver cross for a banner and a picture of Christ painted on a board. The king called their words fair but new, and would not leave the faith his people had long kept. He let them stay and preach.

Augustine had been provost of Pope Gregory the Great's own monastery, Gregory's letters say. Bede says Gregory sent him to the English in 596, and the mission landed the next year. On the way, Bede tells, the monks lost heart at the thought of a fierce people whose language they did not know. They sent Augustine back to ask to be excused. Gregory sent him on with a letter: "When Augustine, your chief, returns, whom we also constitute your abbat, humbly obey him in all things."

At Canterbury the king believed and was baptized. Gregory wrote to Eulogius of Alexandria that more than ten thousand English were reported baptized one Christmas. Bede says Augustine then went to Arles to be made archbishop of the English. In Bede's telling, Aetherius of Arles ordained him; the editor Nicola Turchi names Vergilius.

Augustine sent questions to Rome, and Bede copied them with Gregory's answers. One asks: "Whereas the faith is one and the same, why are there different customs in different churches?" Gregory told him to choose what was good from every church, "for things are not to be loved for the sake of places, but places for the sake of good things." Paul Ewald and Ludwig Hartmann, editing Gregory's letters, say these answers have often been doubted, and judge them genuine.

Gregory also put all the bishops of Britain in his care. Bede tells of two meetings with British bishops, the first at Augustine's Oak. Before the second, a hermit told them to follow him if he rose to greet them. Augustine stayed seated, and they refused him. If they would not preach to the English, Augustine is said to have warned, they would suffer death at English hands. Bede, no friend of the Britons, calls them "that perfidious nation".

[warmly] Augustine died on the twenty-sixth of May. His epitaph gives the day but not the year, and calls him the first archbishop of Canterbury.
