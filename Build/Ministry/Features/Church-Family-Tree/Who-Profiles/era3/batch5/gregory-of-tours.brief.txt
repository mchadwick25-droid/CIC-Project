Gregory of Tours lay ill with fever and sores, unable to eat or drink. He thought only of his burial, he writes. Still he set out for the tomb of Martin of Tours. In a forest the fever came back, and his friends begged him to go home. "Carry my dead body there and bury it," he told them, in Ernest Brehaut's English. After a third night's vigil at his church, Gregory slept and woke with all weakness and pain gone.

Gregory later became bishop of Tours, in Frankish Gaul, and wrote the History of the Franks. Brehaut says he was born at Clermont. In his own Latin he calls Gregory, bishop of Langres, his great-grandfather. W. Arndt, editor of the History, reckons from Gregory's own words that he was born in 538 or 539.

Gregory says he became bishop in the 172nd year after Martin's passing, which his editors set in 573.

King Chilperic, who held Tours, ordered that the Trinity be called only God, with no distinct Persons. "This is my will, that such be thy faith," he told Gregory, in O. M. Dalton's English. Gregory answered that the Father is different from the Son, and the Son from the Holy Ghost. Whoever followed the king, he said, "will never be a wise man, but a fool." The king gnashed his teeth, but held his peace.

In 580, by Arndt's date, Leudast, once count of Tours, went to the king. He said Gregory claimed Queen Fredegund lived in adultery with Bertram, bishop of Bordeaux. Before a council of bishops, Gregory denied it. He said Mass at three altars and cleared himself by oath. The account is the accused man's own.

Arndt reckons he died on 17 November 594. Bruno Krusch, in the same volume, argues for 593.

Closing his History, Gregory charges the bishops of Tours after him to leave his books "complete and intact in your time just as I myself have left them."
