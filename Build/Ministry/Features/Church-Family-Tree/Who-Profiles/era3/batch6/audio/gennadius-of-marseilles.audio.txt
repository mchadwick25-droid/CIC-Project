[calmly] After the people of Alexandria killed their bishop, Proterius, Timothy Aelurus was made bishop in his place by a single bishop, willingly or not. To defend himself, Gennadius of Marseilles writes, Timothy sent the emperor Leo a very persuasive book. It claimed that Pope Leo, the Council of Chalcedon and all the Western bishops were Nestorians at heart. At the brothers' request, Gennadius put the book into Latin "for learning's sake", and "prefixed a caveat".

Gennadius was a priest of Marseilles, in Gaul, late in the fifth century. Little more is known of his own life. He carried on the Lives of Illustrious Men by Jerome, a list of Christian writers. Ernest Cushing Richardson, who translated both into English, judges most of Gennadius's part was written about 480.

Gennadius also put Greek works into Latin, among them those of the monk Evagrius, a disciple of Macarius the Egyptian.

Richardson finds in him glimpses of a point of view that he calls semi-Pelagian. Gennadius credits Prosper of Aquitaine with an unsigned book against works of John Cassian, "which the church of God finds salutary, but which he brands as injurious". Some editions add a line on Augustine of Hippo. Because he said so much, it runs, Solomon's saying came true of him, "In the multitude of words there wanteth not sin." The line drew much comment on his bias, Richardson says. He thinks it almost certainly does not go back to Gennadius.

"I Gennadius, a presbyter of Marseilles, have written eight books Against all heresies," begins a notice at the end of the work. It also names a letter on his creed sent to Gelasius, bishop of Rome. Richardson thinks this notice is a later hand's. He and Blomfield Jackson, in the same volume, give 496 for his death.

[warmly] Of Faustus of Riez he wrote, "There are still other works by him, but as I have not read, I do not care to mention them."
