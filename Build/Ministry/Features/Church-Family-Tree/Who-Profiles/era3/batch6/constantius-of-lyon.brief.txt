Bishop Patiens of Lyon built a new church, and verses were written for its walls beside the altar. Constantius of Lyon wrote some of them. Wilhelm Levison, who edited Constantius's work, reports this from the letters of Sidonius Apollinaris, later bishop of Clermont.

Constantius lived at Lyon in Gaul in the later fifth century, and little else is known of his own life. Levison cannot say whether he was a cleric or a layman given to the religious life. Constantius had urged Sidonius to publish his letters, and Sidonius dedicated them to him. About 473, by Levison's date, Constantius helped make peace in a quarrel at Clermont. Sidonius praised him as a man grave with age and venerable in religion.

Patiens, Constantius writes, ordered him again and again to set down the Life of Germanus, bishop of Auxerre. So much time had passed, he says, that what was known of it could scarcely be gathered. He sent it to Patiens and to Censurius, a later bishop of Auxerre. When Censurius ordered it sent beyond a close circle, it made him, Constantius says, the accuser and the betrayer of his own fault. Levison dates the Life about 480 or a little later.

Levison judges Constantius wrote less as a biographer than as a writer of saints' Lives. Wonders of little weight fill much of the book, as in the Life of Martin of Tours by Sulpicius Severus, which Constantius knew. With so few other witnesses, Levison holds that the book should be used with care and must not be scorned. Bede, in England, copied large parts of it almost word for word into his History of the English.

At its close Constantius asks the reader's pardon for his rough words and for the book's length. Then he writes, in modern words, "I call God, who knows all secrets, to witness that I have kept silent about many deeds of my lord Germanus that I knew and had proved."
