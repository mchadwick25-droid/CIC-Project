A student fell asleep reading in the hospital at Nisibis. He heard a terrible voice, the Chronicle of Seert says, ordering him to go to Abraham, head of the monks on Mount Izla. The student, Babai, later called the Great, gave away his wealth but sixty gold coins for the monks.

Babai was a monk of the Church of the East, in the Persian empire, in the late 500s and early 600s. His life comes from later admirers, in this Arabic chronicle and in the Book of Governors by Thomas of Marga, which E. A. Wallis Budge dates to 840.

After Abraham and Dadisho, Babai became the third head of the monastery on Izla. Thomas calls him learned and wise, but somewhat hasty of speech and harsh in command. He tells how Babai drove out a holy monk named Jacob, and the other fathers rebuked him. Many left and founded monasteries across the East, among them Jacob's Beth Abe, Thomas's own house.

When the church's head died, King Khosrau Parwez let no successor be chosen. Three metropolitans asked Babai to visit the monasteries and drive out heretics, Thomas says, and he did so until the king was murdered. Babai wrote against Henana of Adiabene, master of the school of Nisibis. His Life of the Izla martyr George charges Henana with teaching fate and denying the resurrection of the body. Those charges rest on Babai, whom the German scholar Oskar Braun calls passionate.

In his Book of the Union, which J. Labourt quotes, Babai wrote of how Christ is God and man. "We say: Christ died, the Son died, through the Son all was created and made," he writes, in modern words. The death belongs to the humanity and the making to the divinity, he explains. Yet in the union there are not two Sons.

The chronicle says he died at seventy-five, in the king's thirty-eighth year, before the king's death, as its editor Addai Scher reads it. But Thomas says that after the king's murder the fathers begged him to lead the church, and he refused.

Thomas writes that an angel with a fiery sword came to his cell. It had served him as stand-in for the church's head, and must now serve the new one. "If I had known that thou wast with me," Babai said, "I would, with all care, have accepted this great work; but now depart in peace, and pray for me."
