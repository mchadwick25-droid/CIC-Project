In the royal city of Constantinople, John of Biclaro writes, a plague of groin sores broke out. "We saw many thousands of people die of it," he says. That same year, envoys of a people he calls the Maccurritae came to the city. They brought the emperor Justin the Younger elephant tusks and a giraffe as gifts.

John was a Goth, born at Scallabis in Lusitania, Isidore of Seville writes. His life is known mainly from Isidore's short notice. As a young man John went to Constantinople and learned Greek and Latin there. He came home to Spain after seventeen years, Isidore says. Some copies of Isidore read the seventh year. Theodor Mommsen, who edited John's Chronicle, holds to seventeen, since its first ten years report the news of Constantinople.

King Leovigild was then pressing the Arian teaching. John refused it, and the king exiled him to Barcelona, where he suffered ten years of plots from the Arians. He later founded a monastery called Biclaro and wrote a rule for its monks. Isidore says he became bishop of Gerona. Mommsen takes him to be the John of Gerona who signs the acts of a council at Barcelona and a decree of King Gundemar.

His Chronicle carries on from that of the African bishop Victor of Tunnuna. It runs from Justin's first year as emperor to King Reccared's fourth. John tells the revolt of Leovigild's son Hermenegild as a seizure of power, urged on by Leovigild's queen, Goiswinth. At the Third Council of Toledo, he says, Reccared's presence renewed what Constantine had done at Nicaea. Mommsen notes that John writes of Leovigild, the king who exiled him, with great admiration.

In his preface John says he wrote down the deeds of his own times for those who would come after. He set down "partly what we saw with our own eyes, and partly what we learned from the reports of faithful men."
