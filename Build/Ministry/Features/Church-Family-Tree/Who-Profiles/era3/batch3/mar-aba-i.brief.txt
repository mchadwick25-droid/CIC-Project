Mar Aba and his bishops came into the district of Kashkar. Two men there had each claimed to be its bishop in the years of quarrel. At the town of Zizwarda, the record of his synod says, the people of Kashkar agreed and chose one bishop, Samuel. The two rivals lost their titles and gladly served under Samuel as priests.

Mar Aba was catholicos, head of the Church of the East in the Persian empire. The French scholar J.-B. Chabot dates his election to January 540, from the synod's own dates.

Mari ibn Sulayman, who wrote his church's history in the 1100s, says Aba had been a magus, a follower of the old Persian religion. Mari tells his conversion as a wonder. Aba refused a Christian scholar named Joseph a place in his boat on the Tigris. Each time he refused, winds held the boat back, until he took Joseph aboard.

A Life of Mar Aba survives, which Chabot says needs careful critical study. It has him baptized in the village of Akda, a student at Nisibis, a learner of Greek at Edessa, and later a teacher.

Before him, his church had two rival patriarchs, Narses and Elisha. Mari says each ordained his own bishops, so that two altars stood in every church. The synod's record opens by saying that split had wholly ended. A letter in Mar Aba's name calls marriage a bond of love in the chaste and lawful union of a man and a woman. It forbids marriage to a father's wife and other close kin, as the magi did.

The magi turned against him, Mari says, because he had been one of them and changed their customs. He spent seven years in prison in Adorbigan, Mari writes, and kept caring for his flock. He told the king, Mari says, to kill him if the king wanted him dead.

How he died is told two ways. The Life has him regain the king's favour and die of illness, as head of his church. Bar Hebraeus, of the rival Syriac Orthodox church, says the king chained him when he returned to Seleucia, and he died in prison. Chabot gives the year as 552.

The Life has him buried in the monastery of Seleucia. Mari says his disciple Qyura carried his body to Hira and built a monastery over the grave. Chabot notes that his church honours him as a saint.
