John, archbishop of Jerusalem, called the monks into the city by night, Cyril of Scythopolis writes. Those who counted the crowd put it at ten thousand. Anastasius, the duke of Palestine, had come to make John accept Severus of Antioch and condemn the Council of Chalcedon. On the Sunday, in the church of Saint Stephen, John climbed the pulpit with the abbots Theodosius and Sabas beside him. For hours the people shouted for him to condemn the heretics and confirm the council. The three condemned Severus and others who refused the council, and the duke, afraid of the monks, fled to Caesarea.

Cyril tells this in his Life of Sabas. The Library holds only its passages on Severus, in French by M.-A. Kugener. Kugener says Cyril was born at Scythopolis in Galilee about 523 and died after 557. He calls the Life of Sabas a source of the greatest value for the church of Jerusalem and the monks of Palestine. Cyril writes for the side that accepted Chalcedon, and these passages tell of the struggle against Severus.

His Life of Euthymius is held whole, in Jean-Baptiste Cotelier's Latin translation of 1681. In it Cyril says he often saw Euthymius's tomb flowing with miracles, and wondered how the saint had won such confidence with God. He sought out old monks of the desert who had heard of Euthymius from the saint himself or from Sabas. He wrote down what each one told him. He names the anchorite Cyriacus as the source of some stories.

Still he could not begin the book, he says. Then Euthymius and Sabas appeared to him. Sabas told Euthymius that Cyril, his son, held the paper but could not set down even a first word. Euthymius put a spoonful from a silver flask into Cyril's mouth. Cyril woke with its sweetness still on his tongue.

Then he began the Life of Euthymius, and he says he was eager to write the deeds of Sabas in another work.
