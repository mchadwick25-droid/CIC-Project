John, archbishop of Jerusalem, called the monks into the city by night, Cyril of Scythopolis writes. Those who counted the crowd put it at ten thousand. Anastasius, the duke of Palestine, had come to make John accept Severus of Antioch and condemn the Council of Chalcedon. On the Sunday, in the church of Saint Stephen, John climbed the pulpit with the abbots Theodosius and Sabas beside him. For hours the people shouted for him to condemn the heretics and confirm the council. The three condemned Severus and others who refused the council, and the duke, afraid of the monks, fled to Caesarea.

Cyril tells this in his Life of Sabas. Its passages on Severus were put into French by M.-A. Kugener. Kugener says Cyril was born at Scythopolis in Galilee about 523 and died after 557. He calls the Life of Sabas a source of the greatest value for the church of Jerusalem and the monks of Palestine. Cyril writes for the side that accepted Chalcedon.

Jean-Baptiste Cotelier printed his Life of Euthymius the Great with a Latin translation in 1681. In it Cyril says he often saw Euthymius's tomb flowing with miracles, and wondered how the saint had won such confidence with God. He met many old monks of the desert who had heard the story of Euthymius, some of it from Sabas. He wrote down what each one told him. He names the anchorite Cyriacus as the source of some stories.

Still he could not begin the book, he says. Then Euthymius and Sabas appeared to him. Sabas told Euthymius that Cyril, his son, held the paper but could not set down even a first word. Euthymius dipped a small spoon in a silver jar and put it in Cyril's mouth. Cyril woke with its sweetness on his tongue.

Then he began the Life of Euthymius, and says he was eager to write the deeds of Sabas in another work.
