On a Sunday in Constantinople, two men named Marinus and Plato stood in a church pulpit and changed the hymn of the Trinity. Marcellinus Comes says they did it at the order of the emperor Anastasius. Many of those he calls orthodox kept singing the old words and shouted the new singers down. They were cut down in the church, he writes, and others died in prison.

Marcellinus tells what came next in his Latin chronicle. More were killed the next day in the court of the church of Saint Theodore. Then crowds filled the forum of Constantine. They threw down the emperor's statues and called for a man named Areobindus to rule. On the third day Anastasius sent them home with what Marcellinus calls his usual false oaths. He promised them everything and did nothing. Theodor Mommsen, who edited the chronicle, puts this in 512.

Marcellinus wrote at Constantinople, but Latin was his own tongue, Mommsen says. Cassiodorus, a Latin writer of the same age, calls him Marcellinus of Illyricum. He says Marcellinus is reported to have served Justinian as a court clerk before Justinian became emperor.

In his preface Marcellinus calls himself a count and a man of high rank. He says he followed the Eastern empire only. His book carries on the chronicle of Eusebius of Caesarea and Jerome. It runs from 379 to 518. Later he added sixteen more years, up to 534. He calls it "meum rusticum opus", my rustic work.

Mommsen says hatred of heretics breathes from every page, above all against the followers of Nestorius. He also notes the writer's warm support for Justinian. One manuscript goes on to 548. Mommsen judges that part to be by another hand. Cassiodorus does not know it, and the preface does not mention it.

In his guide to Christian reading, Cassiodorus names Marcellinus right after Eusebius and Jerome among the writers of chronicles.
