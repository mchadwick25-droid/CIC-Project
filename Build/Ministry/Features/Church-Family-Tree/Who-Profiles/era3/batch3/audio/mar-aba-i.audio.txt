[calmly] Mar Aba and his bishops came into the district of Kashkar. Two men there had each claimed to be its bishop in the years of quarrel. At the town of Zizwarda, the record of his synod says, the people of Kashkar agreed and chose one bishop, Samuel. The two rivals lost their titles and gladly served under Samuel as priests.

Mar Aba was catholicos, head of the Church of the East in the Persian empire. The French scholar J.-B. Chabot dates his election to January 540, from the synod's own dates.

Mari ibn Sulayman, who wrote his church's history in the 1100s, says Aba had been a magus, a priest of the old Persian religion. Mari tells his conversion as a wonder. Aba refused a Christian scholar named Joseph a place in his boat on the Tigris. Each time he refused, winds held the boat back, until he took Joseph aboard.

Chabot knows a longer Life of Mar Aba and says it needs careful critical study. His own outline has Aba baptized at Hira, a student at Nisibis, a learner of Greek at Edessa, and later a noted teacher at Nisibis.

Before him, his church had two rival patriarchs, Narses and Elisha. Mari says each ordained his own bishops, so that two altars stood in every church. The synod's record opens by saying that split had wholly ended. A letter in Mar Aba's name, in Chabot's French, calls marriage a bond of love in the chaste and lawful union of a man and a woman. It forbids marriage to a father's widow and other close kin, as the magi did.

The magi hated him, Mari says, because he had been one of them and changed their customs. He spent seven years in prison in Adorbigan, Mari writes, and kept caring for his flock. He told the king, Mari says, to kill him if the king wanted him dead.

How he died is told two ways. Chabot reports that the Life has him regain the king's favour and die in peace as head of his church. Bar Hebraeus, a historian of the rival Syriac Orthodox church, says the king had him chained when he returned to Seleucia, and he died in prison. Chabot gives the year as 552.

[warmly] Mari says his disciple Qyura carried his body to Hira and built a monastery over the grave. Chabot notes that his church honours him as a saint.
