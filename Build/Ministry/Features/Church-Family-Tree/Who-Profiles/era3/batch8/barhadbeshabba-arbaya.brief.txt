The bishops of the Church of the East met at the Persian king's court to choose a new head. J.-B. Chabot, who edited their acts, dates the meeting to April 605. The acts say King Khosrau the Second had the distant bishops brought on his own beasts. They chose Gregory of Prat. The twenty-seventh to sign is Bar Hadbeshabba, bishop of Halwan.

Addai Scher and François Nau take this bishop to be Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia, who wrote the Cause of the Foundation of the Schools. The title Scher prints calls its author bishop of Halwan. His epithet marks him as a man of Beth 'Arbaye, Scher says, and he studied at the school of Nisibis under Henana of Adiabene.

The Cause tells of the schools through which God taught angels and people, from Adam down to the schools of Edessa and Nisibis.

The Cause speaks of Henana as a living master. It praises his humility and prays that God will lengthen his days. Scher says Henana's own church always charged him with leaving Theodore of Mopsuestia's reading of Scripture, and thinks the author probably hid this to praise him. The Chronicle of Seert names a Hadbeshabba 'Arbaya among the students who walked out of the school in protest. It says he was later made metropolitan of Halwan.

Part of a history of the holy Fathers persecuted for the truth also survives. Nau gives it to Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaia, a priest of the school of Nisibis, and dates it to the end of the 500s. He thinks it not impossible that this writer is the bishop of Halwan.

A chronicle that Ignazio Guidi edited says that after Gregory's death, Bar Hadbeshabba of Halwan won fame for writing books.

Of the school that Narsai of Nisibis founded, the Cause says, in R. H. Connolly's English, "Edessa was darkened, but Nisibis shone forth; and the dominions of the Romans were filled with error, but those of Persia with the knowledge of true religion".
