[calmly] Chararic, a king in Galicia, had a gravely ill son, Gregory of Tours writes. The king sent to the tomb of Martin of Tours for relics. A man named Martin, from a far country, came into a Galician port with the relics. The king's son was healed, and the king confessed the unity of Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

The newcomer was Martin of Braga, bishop of the monastery of Dumio and later of Braga. Gregory says he went East to visit the holy places, and was held second to none in learning. The Sueves kept their own kingdom in Galicia until the Gothic king Leovigild made it a province of the Goths, John of Biclaro says.

Once the Sueves turned from the Arian teaching to the Catholic faith, Isidore of Seville says, Martin set down a rule of faith for them. He also founded monasteries. Isidore credits the change to King Theudemir, backed by Martin, while Gregory names Chararic.

At the Second Council of Braga, Martin spoke first, as bishop of Braga. Nothing in this province was in doubt about the faith, he told the bishops. They should correct whatever in themselves was at fault, by the Scriptures and the old canons.

Polemius, bishop of Astorga, asked him to write on the origin of idols, for country people who still honored demons more than God. Martin sent a sermon for him to preach, On the Correction of Peasants, in country speech. Some who renounced the devil at baptism, it says, now light candles at stones, trees, springs and crossroads, and that is the devil's worship.

Gregory tells of Martin's death among events that C. P. Caspari and the Bollandists date to 580, and says his people mourned him greatly.

[warmly] In his epitaph Martin speaks to Martin of Tours. Born in Pannonia, it says, he crossed vast seas to Galicia by God's will. Following his patron, he rests in the peace of Christ, the same in name but not in merit.
