[calmly] In the year Theodor Mommsen counts as 563, Marius of Avenches writes, the mountain of Tauredunum in the Valais suddenly fell. It buried a nearby fort and villages with everyone in them. It shook the whole lake, so that the water broke over both shores. The flood swept away old villages with their people and cattle. It carried off the bridge at Geneva with its mills and men, then burst into the city and killed many.

Marius was bishop of Avenches, in the Burgundian lands, in the later 500s. His Chronicle carries on that of Prosper of Aquitaine from 455 to 581.

At the council of Mâcon, in King Guntram's twenty-fourth year, Marius signed as bishop of the church of Avenches. His epitaph comes down through Conon, provost of Lausanne, who wrote in 1235. It says he was of noble birth and a cleric from his early years. He made sacred vessels for the church and worked his lands with his own hands. He fed the poor and ate sparingly himself.

His years are disputed. From the epitaph and a church Marius dedicated, Mommsen reckons that he became bishop in 574 and died in 594. Conon dates the start of his rule to 581, and later annals of Lausanne put his death in 601. Mommsen rejects both. Andrea Gallandi, after Pierre-François Chifflet, gives 596.

The see of Avenches later moved to Lausanne. Mommsen says no one knows when, and thinks it moved after Marius. Gallandi reports Charles Le Cointe's view that Marius made the move himself.

Mommsen says Marius looked most to Agaune and the Valais. He tells of King Sigismund founding the monastery at Agaune. Its monks, he says, later rose one night against their bishop Agricola. A single manuscript keeps his Chronicle, and Mommsen says no one used it in the Middle Ages.

[warmly] A book of the dead from Lausanne cathedral, written about 1510, still marked his day, Mommsen notes. On that day a procession went to the church of Saint Marius.
