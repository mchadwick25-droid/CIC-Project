Cassiodorus remembered a monk who could take any Greek book in his hands and run through it in Latin without a stumble. He could read Latin books back again in Greek. You would think, Cassiodorus says, that the words pouring from his mouth had already been written down.

That monk was Dionysius Exiguus, who worked at Rome in the early 500s. Cassiodorus says he was a Scythian by birth but wholly Roman in his ways. The two read dialectic together. Dionysius never set himself above the lowest servants, Cassiodorus writes, though he was fit to speak with kings.

Bishop Stephen of Salona asked him to put the church's canons into Latin from Greek copies. Dionysius set the canons called the Apostles' first, and warned that many did not readily accept them. Cassiodorus says the Roman church used his canons widely, and he told his own monks to read them often.

For Julian, a Roman priest, Dionysius gathered the rulings of past popes. He also put Gregory of Nyssa's book on the making of man into Latin for the priest Eugippius. Some points in the book could rightly be faulted, he warned, and no reader should think he agreed with them. He had set them out as a translator, he said, and had not approved them as a censor.

Bishop Petronius had long urged him to set out the rule for Easter. Dionysius followed the bishops of Nicaea, he wrote, and their nineteen-year cycle, which Cyril of Alexandria had kept. Cyril counted his years from the emperor Diocletian. Dionysius would not tie his tables to the memory of a wicked persecutor, he wrote. He chose to number the years from the Incarnation of Jesus Christ. That way the start of our hope would be better known, and the Redeemer's passion, the cause of our renewal, would shine more clearly.

Whether the count of years from Christ spread from him is a later judgment. William Cave says Dionysius was the first to reckon years this way. Johann Wilhelm Janus agrees, but notes that some give the credit to Bede. Charles Plummer says Bede was the first chronicler to date events from Christ's birth, and so brought the Dionysian era into Western Europe.

Cassiodorus wrote after his death that Dionysius used to pray with them. He asks him now to plead for them, so that those once upheld by his prayers may be helped by his merits.
