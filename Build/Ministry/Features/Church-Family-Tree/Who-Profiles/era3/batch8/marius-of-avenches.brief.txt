In the year Theodor Mommsen counts as 563, Marius of Avenches writes, the mountain of Tauredunum in the Valais suddenly fell. It buried a nearby fort and villages with everyone in them, and shook the whole lake until the water broke over both shores. The flood swept away old villages with their people and cattle. It carried off the bridge at Geneva with its mills and men, then burst into the city and killed many.

Marius was bishop of Avenches, in the Burgundian lands, in the later 500s. His Chronicle carries on that of Prosper of Aquitaine from 455 to 581.

At the council of Mâcon, in King Guntram's twenty-fourth year, Marius signed as bishop of the church of Avenches. His epitaph comes down through Conon, provost of Lausanne, writing in 1235. It says he was of noble birth and a cleric from his early years. He made sacred vessels for the church and worked his lands with his own hands, it says. He fed the poor and ate sparingly himself.

His years are disputed. From the epitaph and a church Marius dedicated, Mommsen reckons that he became bishop in 574 and died in 594. Conon gives 581 for his becoming bishop, and a later hand in the annals of Lausanne puts his death in 601. Mommsen rejects both. Andrea Gallandi, after Pierre-François Chifflet, gives his death as 596.

The bishop's seat later moved from Avenches to Lausanne. Mommsen thinks this came after Marius. Gallandi reports Charles Le Cointe's view that Marius moved it himself.

Marius wrote most about Agaune and the Valais, Mommsen says. He records that King Sigismund of Burgundy built the monastery at Agaune, and that its monks tried one night to kill their bishop, Agricola. A single manuscript keeps the Chronicle, which Mommsen says went unused in the Middle Ages.

A Lausanne cathedral book of the dead from about 1510 marked his day, Mommsen notes, with a procession to the church of Saint Marius.
