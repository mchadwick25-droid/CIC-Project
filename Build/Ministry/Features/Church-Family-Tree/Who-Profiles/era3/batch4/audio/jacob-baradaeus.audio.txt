[calmly] Horsemen were hunting a bishop across the roads of the empire. Often they met him on the way, and did not know him. "Have you heard any secret news of that deceiver James anywhere there?" they asked. He named a place well behind him. "If you set out," he told them, "perhaps you will catch him." John of Ephesus, whom James made a bishop, tells the story.

Jacob Baradaeus, whom John calls James, was the bishop who built up the church that rejected the council of Chalcedon in Syria and far beyond. Born at Tella, John says, he trained in the monastery of Fsiltha. He went up to Constantinople, where the empress Theodora gave him a house. He lived alone in a cell for about fifteen years. He cut one thick, ugly cloak in two and made his clothing from the halves. From this, says E. W. Brooks, who edited John, came his nickname, Burd'ana, the man of the patchwork garment.

Bishops of his party had grown few. Harith bar Gabala, king of the Saracens, asked Theodora to have two or three made. In the sixteenth year of Justinian, John says, James was made bishop for Edessa, and Theodore for Hirtha of the Saracens.

Then James went on foot through Syria, Armenia, Cappadocia, Asia and the islands, as far as Alexandria. He would finish his work in a district in a night and a day, then move thirty or forty miles on. The emperor paid gold to anyone who promised to catch him. John counts twenty-seven bishops he ordained, and two patriarchs. If anyone says he ordained a hundred thousand clergy, John adds, "I do not think he will depart from the truth."

In old age he was drawn into a bitter quarrel over Paul of Antioch, a patriarch he had made. John calls him "a simple man", swayed by the violent men around him. Michael the Syrian, a twelfth-century patriarch of the same church, says those who stood with the old man were called Jacobites. Brooks thinks the name arose from that quarrel.

[warmly] He died on the road to Alexandria, at the monastery of Cassianus on the border of Egypt, by John's first account. John also gives a report that their ship sank, and says, "which of these stories is true, God knows." Michael gives the day as the thirtieth of Tammuz, which is July, and Brooks the year as 578.
