[calmly] King Gundobad of the Burgundians came to believe that the Son and the Holy Spirit are equal to the Father. He asked the bishop of Vienne to anoint him in secret. Gregory of Tours, writing later in the same century, gives the bishop's answer. The king should not fear his people, he said, but lead them. "For thou art the head of the people; the people is not thy head." Gregory says the king never confessed it in public.

That bishop was Avitus of Vienne, in Gaul. An old Life of him says he followed his father Isicius as bishop. His editor, Rudolf Peiper, puts his birth about 460. Gundobad's church was Arian, and the Life says Avitus fought that teaching with great labor.

When Clovis, king of the Franks, was baptized, Avitus wrote to him in Latin. He had not been there in body, he said, but had shared the joy. In choosing for yourself, he told the king, you judge for everyone, and your faith is our victory. Peiper dates the letter to 496 or 497. The year and place of the baptism are disputed. O. M. Dalton, Gregory's English translator, follows those who hold Reims in 496, against Bruno Krusch's Tours in 507 or 508.

At Gundobad's request, Gregory says, Avitus wrote against the teaching of Eutyches. Agobard of Lyon, writing three centuries later, says he argued with the king in person and by letter. After Gundobad died, Agobard says, Avitus turned his son and heir Sigismund to the Catholic faith. Peiper dates Avitus's letter to the pope about that conversion between 513 and 516, while Gundobad still lived. In 517 Avitus signed first at the council of Epaone.

No source used here gives the year he died. His Life says it was under the emperor Anastasius; Peiper corrects this to Justin.

[warmly] Gregory wrote of his letters that "as they once quelled heresy, so now they edify the Church of God."
