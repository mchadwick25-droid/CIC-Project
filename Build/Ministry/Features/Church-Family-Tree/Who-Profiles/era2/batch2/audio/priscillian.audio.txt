[calmly] At the court of the emperor Maximus, the prefect Evodius tried a Spanish bishop named Priscillian in two hearings. Sulpicius Severus, a chronicler from Aquitaine, tells it. He says Priscillian did not deny that he had taught lewd doctrines, held meetings of women by night, and prayed naked. Evodius found him guilty. The emperor ordered that Priscillian and his friends be put to death.

Priscillian was a layman of Spain, "a man of noble birth, of great riches," Sulpicius says. He could go without sleep, food and drink, and cared little for money. Sulpicius thought him a heretic and his teaching the heresy of the Gnostics. He disliked the bishops who hunted him just as much. "The accusers were as distasteful to me as the accused," he writes. He calls Ithacius of Sossuba, one of the accusers, bold, loud and greedy for pleasure.

A council at Saragossa condemned Priscillian in his absence, Sulpicius says. Two bishops who backed him then made him bishop of Abila. Ydacius of Emerita, another enemy, won an order from the emperor Gratian driving heretics out. Priscillian went to Rome to clear himself before Damasus, bishop of the city, but was turned away.

In 1885 Georg Schepss found eleven treatises in a manuscript at Würzburg. They carry no name, but Schepss judged them Priscillian's. In his book to Damasus, Priscillian says that at Saragossa none of his group was accused, convicted or condemned. He reminds Damasus that his own letter had forbidden judging people absent and unheard. In another treatise he curses anyone who does not condemn Mani, founder of the Manichaeans.

Orosius, a Spanish priest writing to Augustine of Hippo, says Priscillian taught that souls came down from God through circles of evil powers into bodies. A council at Braga in 561 cursed souls cast down into bodies and bound to the stars, naming Priscillian. "He is still accused by some, of being tainted with Gnosticism," wrote Jerome of Strido. His defenders, Jerome adds, deny it.

Martin of Tours had begged Maximus not to shed blood, saying a secular judge should not decide a church case. Later Ithacius alone of the bishops was put out of his see, Sulpicius says. The bodies were carried back to Spain and buried "with great pomp." His followers now honoured him as a martyr. [warmly] "It came to be thought the highest exercise of religion to swear by Priscillian."
