An old monk stood before a synod of bishops at Constantinople. He was Eutyches, a priest and the head of a monastery in the city. Eusebius, bishop of Dorylaeum, had accused him of heresy. As the minutes record his answer, he said, "I confess that our LORD was of Two Natures before the Union, but after the Union I confess Him to be of One Nature." Flavian, bishop of Constantinople, deposed him. S. G. F. Perry, who translated the Syriac acts of the later council, dates that synod to November 448.

Eutyches told his own story another way. In a petition to the council that met at Ephesus the next year, he says he was dedicated to God from his birth. To Leo, bishop of Rome, he wrote that he had spent "seventy years of life in continence and all chastity." He took refuge with Leo as "the defender of religion," cursing Apollinaris, Nestorius and the other heretics.

He also said the record was false. He told the emperors that "what he said to me was not put on paper, and what I did not say, they put into the Documents." He said he had appealed to Rome at the trial. Flavian wrote to Leo that "this he certainly never did."

At Ephesus in 449, under Dioscorus of Alexandria, the minutes were read again. Basil of Seleucia denied a sentence written as his, though he then said he did not remember. Juvenal of Jerusalem declared, "This sentence, therefore, was altered." Juvenal voted that Eutyches be restored "to his Monastery and to his Grade," and the council agreed.

Leo had already given his verdict. His letter to Flavian, known as the Tome, calls Eutyches "very unwary and exceedingly ignorant." In 451 the Council of Chalcedon received the Tome, its definition says, "for the removal of the false doctrines of Eutyches." Later, as Charles Lett Feltoe summarises one of Leo's letters, Leo urged that he be sent further into exile. No held line tells when or where he died.

The Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene, from the side that rejected Chalcedon, says the council "introduced and increased the heresy of Nestorius." Yet it also says Eutyches "affirmed an inaccurate dogma," not having been well taught.

In his letter to Leo, before the council of 449, he had written, "I stand in jeopardy of my life as a heretic."
