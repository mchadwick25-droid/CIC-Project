Around the year two hundred, in Alexandria, Origen's father was arrested for his faith. Origen was a boy, and Eusebius of Caesarea says he was seized by such a longing for martyrdom that he rushed toward danger. His mother hid his clothes to keep him home, so he wrote to his father in prison. Eusebius kept one sentence of that letter: "Take heed not to change your mind on our account."

His father died a martyr, and the state took the family's property. Origen was not yet seventeen. In his eighteenth year he took charge of teaching the faith in Alexandria, because everyone else had fled. Jerome, who put the Bible into Latin, says he was so devoted to the Scriptures that he learned Hebrew, against the habit of his time and his people. He then set the Hebrew text beside the Greek translations, in a work called the Hexapla.

He taught that Scripture, like a person, has body, soul and spirit. In On First Principles he says the plain story builds up simple believers, its soul those who have advanced, and its spiritual law the perfect. The book taught that souls existed before birth. It says the Son's generation is as eternal as brightness from the sun, but we read that line in the Latin of Rufinus of Aquileia, who Jerome said changed the book. Origen also wrote eight books in answer to Celsus, who had attacked Christians in a book called the True Discourse.

On a journey through Palestine, the bishops of Caesarea and Jerusalem ordained Origen a priest. Jerome says this so enraged his own bishop, Demetrius, that he wrote everywhere to ruin Origen's name. In 231 Origen left Alexandria for Caesarea. He later wrote that he had come through a storm and been brought out of Egypt. About twenty years later, under the emperor Decius, he was stretched in the stocks. Jerome says he died at Tyre, aged sixty-nine.

Gregory of Nazianzus later sent a friend the Philocalia, a book of passages chosen from Origen. Socrates of Constantinople, who defended Origen, says Athanasius often cited him in support of his own faith. Yet three centuries after his death, the acts of a council at Constantinople list his name among condemned heretics.
