Around the year 300, Methodius was a bishop in Lycia, in the south of Asia Minor, in the church the tree calls the Anatolian church of the third century. Origen had been dead some fifty years, and his books still had critics and defenders. Methodius wrote against him, and two of Origen's admirers wrote about what he did.

Socrates of Constantinople says Methodius attacked Origen in many places and then, as if taking it all back, praised him in a dialogue. Eusebius of Caesarea, in a defense of Origen that Jerome quotes, asked how Methodius dared to attack Origen now, after all he had said of his teaching. Socrates called Origen's critics worthless, and both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness.

Little else about Methodius is certain. Jerome says he was bishop of Olympus and later of Tyre, and that he died a martyr at Chalcis at the end of the last persecution. He also wrote a Banquet of the Ten Virgins, in which ten women in turn praise the chaste life.

His chief quarrel with Origen was over the body. Origen had taught, Photius of Constantinople reports, that the soul was given a body as a chain after its fall. In On the Resurrection, Methodius answered that the body is no chain, and that a person is soul and body joined. He pictures sin as a wild fig tree grown into a temple's stones. Death tears the tree out so the temple can stand again. Then the flesh is raised, he writes, "with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed." Decades later, Epiphanius of Salamis wrote against Origen and chose to rest on Methodius instead of arguing it himself. He copied him word for word, as a learned man who fought hard for the truth.

Methodius also answered Porphyry, who had written against the Christians. That reply is lost, but Jerome counted it among the answers that had met Porphyry well, and said it ran to ten thousand lines. A century and more later, Theodoret of Cyrrhus kept a passage from his sermon on the martyrs. He named him among the bishops and martyrs whose words witness to the church's faith in Christ.
