In 312 the church had lived through nearly ten years of persecution. That year Constantine marched on Rome against his rival emperor Maxentius, and the tree's story of Church and Empire begins with him. On the march, Eusebius of Caesarea says, he prayed that God would show him who he was, and years later Constantine told Eusebius the rest under oath. At midday a cross of light stood above the sun, with the words "Conquer by this," and that night Christ came to him in sleep. Lactantius, who wrote in Constantine's own day, knows only a dream that told him to mark the sign of Christ on his soldiers' shields.

Maxentius was driven into the Tiber at the broken bridge. Soon Constantine and his fellow emperor Licinius met at Milan and decreed that Christians and all others should be free to follow the religion each thought best, and that the Christians' meeting places be given back. Eusebius says churches rose again from their foundations.

At Alexandria the presbyter Arius argued that if the Father begat the Son, there was a time when the Son was not. Constantine wrote to Arius and his bishop that the quarrel was trifling, then called the bishops to Nicaea and sat among them, "your fellow servant," as he wrote. Eusebius told his church that the emperor urged one added word, homoousios, meaning of the same essence, and an imperial edict then sent Arius into exile.

The council did not end the quarrel. Years later Arius gave the emperor a written creed, and Constantine ordered Athanasius, by then bishop of Alexandria, to receive him back, because, Socrates of Constantinople says, he did not wish to see the church torn. Athanasius refused, his enemies brought charges against him, and Constantine sent him into exile in Gaul.

Only when he knew he was dying, Eusebius says, did he seek baptism. Sozomen calls him "the first of the emperors who began to be zealous for the Church". He was buried in the Church of the Apostles at Constantinople, and from then on, Sozomen says, later Christian emperors were buried there too.
