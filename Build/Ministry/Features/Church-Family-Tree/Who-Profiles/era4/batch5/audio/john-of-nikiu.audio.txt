[calmly] When the Muslim army took the town of Nakius, in Egypt, it found no soldiers to stop it, a Coptic chronicle says. The army put to the sword all it found in the streets and in the churches, men, women and infants, "and they showed mercy to none." Then the chronicler breaks off: "Let us now cease, for it is impossible to recount the iniquities".

The chronicle is by John of Nikiu, a later Coptic bishop of that same town. It survives only in an Ethiopic version made in 1602 from an Arabic one now lost. R. H. Charles, who put it into English in 1916, says it was first written in Greek. He thinks John was born about the time of the Arab conquest.

John's own life is told in the History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria, a later Arabic history. Its writer says he was the spiritual son of the patriarch John III. John of Nikiu was among the bishops at that patriarch's deathbed, and met with them to choose the next. The patriarch Simon later gave him charge of the monasteries, since he knew the monks' life and rules.

Then some monks carried a virgin off from her convent. John had the guilty monk beaten, and ten days later the monk died. The bishops deposed John, the History says, for going beyond what humanity required of him. He was silent while they did it. Then he cried out that the Lord would make them "strangers to your sees". Eusèbe Renaudot, retelling the story in Latin, says he bore it impatiently.

Charles dates the election after John III to 690. The History heads the life of the patriarch then chosen, Isaac, 686 to 689.

In his Chronicle, John writes that people blamed the conquest on the emperor Heraclius, and on his persecution of the Orthodox through the patriarch Cyrus. Of the Arab general 'Amr he says both that he "had no mercy on the Egyptians" and that "he took none of the property of the Churches".

[warmly] In his preface he calls himself "wanting in eloquence beyond all authors." He adds, "I have been honest".
