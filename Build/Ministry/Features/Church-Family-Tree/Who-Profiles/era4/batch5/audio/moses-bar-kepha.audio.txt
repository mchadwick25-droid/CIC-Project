[calmly] Someone may ask, a Syriac bishop wrote, in what part of the world paradise lies. Is it toward the east or the west, the south or the north? Basil, the brother of Gregory of Nyssa, said we face east to pray, to train our eyes on paradise. Others, the bishop added, think church builders prove it, since they lay out churches to face the sun rising in the month of Nisan. In that month, they say, the sun rises over paradise. Ephrem said paradise circles the whole earth beyond the Ocean, as a ring circles the moon.

The bishop was Moses bar Kepha, of the Syriac Orthodox Church. The historian Bar Hebraeus calls him Severus, bishop of Beth Raman, also called Moses bar Kepha, a commentator on the holy Scriptures.

Bar Hebraeus's editors, J. B. Abbeloos and T. J. Lamy, add more from an anonymous Syriac life that Joseph Assemani reports. He lived about ninety years, it says. For forty years he held three sees in the region of Nineveh: Beth Raman, Beth Ceno and Mosul. For ten years he was also visitor of the church of Tagrit, which then had no bishop of its own.

Bar Hebraeus puts his death on the twelfth of Shebat, in February, and his burial at the monastery of Mar Sergius, called Moallak. One part of his chronicle gives the year 913, and another gives 903. His editors take 903, and call 913 a slip in the numeral.

His book on paradise, addressed to Ignatius, whom he calls his brother, survives in the Latin of Andreas Masius, printed in 1569. He meant it to be exact and clear, he wrote, with each chapter set apart, so that readers would understand it all and forget nothing.

In his book of church law, Bar Hebraeus lists Moses's commentaries among the books of the teachers read in the church.

[warmly] J. Rendel Harris and Alphonse Mingana quote a line from his tract on baptism, as a Mr. Aytoun gives it. It tells the one baptized that "thou goest down to baptism one person, and comest up another instead, the new instead of the old".
