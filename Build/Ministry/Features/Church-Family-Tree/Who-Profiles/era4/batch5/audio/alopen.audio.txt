[calmly] "Observing the azure clouds, he bore the true sacred books; beholding the direction of the winds, he braved difficulties and dangers." So a stone set up in China in 781 tells of a man from Syria. In 635 he reached the Tang capital, Chang-an. The emperor Taitsung sent his prime minister, Duke Fang Hiuen-ling, to the west border to bring the guest in. The books were translated in the imperial library. The emperor studied them in his own rooms, and ordered the religion spread.

The man was Alopen, of the Church of the East. His story is told on that stone, the Xi'an stele. His own church raised it nearly a century and a half after he came. Its Chinese text names its author as King-Tsing, a priest. A. Wylie put it into English, and calls him Olopun.

Workmen dug the stone up in 1625, Henry Yule writes. Jesuits and their opponents argued warmly over whether it was genuine, S. Wells Williams says. To him the weight of evidence leaves no doubt.

In 638, the stele says, the emperor issued a decree. "Right principles have no invariable name, holy men have no invariable station," it begins. It names "the Greatly-virtuous Olopun, of the kingdom of Syria", who had brought "his sacred books and images" to the capital. The emperor found the teaching "purely excellent and natural". He ordered a Syrian church built in the capital, "which shall be governed by twenty-one priests."

The next emperor, Kautsung, built churches in every province, the stele says. He confirmed Alopen's honor, "making him the great conservator of doctrine for the preservation of the State." The Latin that Athanasius Kircher published, as Joseph Assemani gives it, names a different title. It calls him bishop of the great law that governs China.

Wylie's English of the stele gives 635 for Alopen's arrival, and so does Yule. Assemani gives 636. Yule thinks the name is probably a Chinese form of Rabban, meaning monk. F. Hirth suggests Ruben, and Assemani the Syriac Jaballaha.

[warmly] The stele's closing verse begins: "When the pure, bright Illustrious Religion / Was introduced to our Tang dynasty, / The Scriptures were translated, and churches built".
