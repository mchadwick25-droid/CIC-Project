# Who profiles: era 4, batch 5

Eight profiles, one id each: `sophronius-of-jerusalem`, `athanasios-the-athonite`, `sebeos`, `antony-of-choziba`, `moses-bar-kepha`, `alopen`, `thomas-of-marga`, `john-of-nikiu`.

These are eight eastern WHO rows of `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-11_who-scan-era4/Who_Scan_Era4.md`, built under Mark's ruling of 2026-11-14 that eras 4 to 7 are built in full. None is a live census person entry, and none was written before. Adam (Jingjing), the author of the Xi'an stele, is a separate scan row and is named here only as the stele's author, King-Tsing.

The coordinator called two of the eight major (2026-11-14): Athanasios the Athonite, who founded the first great house on Mount Athos, and Alopen, who brought the Church of the East to China. Athanasios's brief runs 405 words, about 2:30. Alopen's runs 362 words, about 2:14: everything the held sources say of him goes back to the one stele, and the brief runs to what it carries (coordinator, 2026-11-14). The other six run 347 to 358 words, about 2:09 to 2:13. Sebeos, Thomas of Marga and John of Nikiu are recorders and stay at standard length, as Palladius, John of Ephesus and Gregory of Tours did. Antony of Choziba is a recorder too, known only from his own writings, the Life of George of Choziba and the Miracles printed after it under his name.

Names and ids, the worker's calls:
- **Sophronius of Jerusalem**, the form the census already uses on the Theodosius I of Alexandria card. The census and the Library hold other men named Sophronius, among them Jerome's friend and a ninth-century Sophronius of Alexandria.
- **Athanasios the Athonite**, the census voice-line form, with "Athanasius" kept under other names.
- **Moses bar Kepha**, the census form. The Latin sources spell him Bar-Cepha and Barcepha, and Bar Hebraeus gives his name as bishop, Severus.
- **Alopen**, the census form. A. Wylie's English of the stele spells him Olopun, and the card keeps the other spellings the sources use.
- **Sebeos**, **Antony of Choziba**, **Thomas of Marga** and **John of Nikiu** stand as the census and the scan name them. Thomas of Kashkar and Thomas of Hedhodh, in the same book, are other men; John III of Samannud, the patriarch, is not John of Nikiu.

Card traditions are the census's names. Five are the worker's calls, to be logged in Open-Gaps:
- Sophronius reads "The Byzantine Imperial Church (Heraclian & Iconoclast Eras); Chalcedonian Monasticism - Judean Desert & Gaza". His patriarchate and his stand against the one-operation teaching fall in the first, though its entry does not name him. His years as a monk of Theodosius with John Moschus fall in the second, whose people list holds Moschus.
- Athanasios reads "The Byzantine Imperial Church (Macedonian Era); Athonite & Comnenian Byzantine Monasticism". Both name him as a voice. The second is an era 5 entry, but it begins in 963 with his Lavra.
- Sebeos reads "Armenian Christianity before the Bagratids". The scan places him in "Armenian Apostolic", which is not a census movement name. That movement runs from about 554 to 884 and covers his years; no era 4 Armenian entry does, since Bagratid Armenian Christianity begins about 884.
- Antony of Choziba reads "Chalcedonian Monasticism - Judean Desert & Gaza". The entry ends in 614, and its long description closes with the Persian sack of the desert houses and those who lived and wrote down what was lost. His Life is one of those accounts. No era 4 entry covers the Judean desert monasteries.
- Thomas of Marga reads "The Church of the East on the Silk Road; The Church of the East under the Abbasids". He came to Beth 'Abhe in 832 and wrote between 832 and 850, in the first. Mari ibn Sulayman places him as metropolitan while his brother Theodosius, patriarch from 852, was in prison, in the second.

The others: Moses bar Kepha in "Syriac Orthodox Christianity under Islam"; Alopen in "The Church of the East on the Silk Road"; John of Nikiu in "Coptic Christianity under Early Islam".
