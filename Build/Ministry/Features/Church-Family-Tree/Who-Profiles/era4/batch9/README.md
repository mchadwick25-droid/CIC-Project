## John of Damascus, the live entry

The census lists him in Melkite Arabic Christianity and in Iconophile Byzantine Monasticism, both with the year 730. The Iconophile entry quotes the Exposition of the Orthodox Faith (Salmond's English, `npnf209` l.36233-36234); the Melkite entry points to the same held file. The profile agrees with the entry's names and traditions, and quotes the same chapter of the Exposition. It differs on the year. In the held sources, 730 is Michel Le Quien's date for the second speech on the images, when Patriarch Germanus was driven out (the scan reads 750). Le Quien ties it to Germanus's fall, which Theophanes puts in the year of the world 6221, that is 729/30. Johann Fabricius dates the Dialectica, the first part of the work that holds the Exposition, after 743. The census year is not changed here; the difference is logged in Open-Gaps, "Era 4 Who batch 9" (2026-11-15), for the census fix.

## Who the sources are

- **John of Damascus.** His own speeches on the images (Greek and Latin) and the Exposition (English). His life is told in a Life headed as by a John, patriarch of Jerusalem, which Fabricius dates after the middle of the tenth century; the brief says so and tells the cut-hand story as the Life's, with Fabricius and Leo Allatius on either side. The early witnesses are Theophanes the Confessor, the council of 754 and Stephen the Deacon's Life of Stephen the Younger (808).
- **Theodore the Studite.** His letters, Testament and Antirrhetici, in Patrologia Graeca 99; two Lives that carry Michael the monk's name, which Angelo Mai says hardly differ in what they tell; Naukratios's letter on his death; Theophanes. The years are Fabricius's and the Sirmond chronology's; the Lives give ages.
- **Cyril and Methodius.** No own work is held. The Italian Legend, which the Bollandists, and Johann Ginzel after them, think very probably Gauderich of Velletri's; the Life of Methodius, which Ernst Dümmler takes as a pupil's and Ginzel, who writes against the Greek schism, calls a schismatic's concoction; the hostile Salzburg account of about 873; Pope John VIII's letters of 879 and 880; the Russian Primary Chronicle in S. H. Cross's English.
- **Antony and Theodosius.** The Russian Primary Chronicle (Sylvester's colophon of 1116), in Cross's English, and the Caves Paterik, whose base text is the redaction of 1462, with Nestor's Life of Theodosius and a founding account headed as Nestor's. Where the accounts differ (Antony's journeys, Theodosius's tonsure year, who chose him as prior, who built the monastery above ground, the source of the Studite rule), the card gives both. On the last three, the Paterik's founding account agrees with the Chronicle, and Nestor's Life differs. It also gives the Chronicle's two accounts of where Antony ended his life.
- **Anskar.** Rimbert's Life, in Georg Waitz's Latin and C. H. Robinson's English; his own letter to the bishops in Mansi's Latin; Adam of Bremen as a later witness; the Royal Frankish Annals and the Annals of Saint-Bertin.
- **Rimbert.** A recorder, with a thin own life flagged. His Life of Anskar and his letter to the nuns of Heresi are held. His own life rests on the anonymous Life of Rimbert, which G. H. Pertz dates to Adalgar's lifetime. The held scan ends in its chapter 23, so his death rests on Adam of Bremen.
- **Clement of Ohrid.** One Life, on the re-score's "needs-ruling" list. Its heading names Theophylact, archbishop of all Bulgaria (died 1107). Franz Miklosich holds it is a tenth-century disciple's work, Ginzel that Theophylact reworked tenth-century notes. Migne's editors leave it to the reader in Patrologia Graeca 126, while Patrologia Graeca 111 lists it as anonymous, of about the middle of the tenth century, and ascribed to Theophylact only by some. The dispute is told in the brief's second paragraph and the card's first uncertain item. Mark ruled, through the coordinator (2026-11-15), picking "Keep as Who, dispute told (Recommended)": Clement stays a Who, and the needs-ruling is closed (Decision-Log, "Who profiles, era 4 batch 9", 2026-11-15).

## Corrections to the re-score found in drafting

- **John of Damascus.** Lines 616-621 of the Patrologia Graeca 94 file are Fabricius's words, not Le Quien's; "post medium saeculi decimi" dates the Life's author, and the death "not before 756" carries Fabricius's "videtur". Anastasius the Librarian's Latin makes John Mansur's grandson where Goar's Latin of Theophanes says son. The image speeches are held in Greek and Latin, not English.
- **Theodore.** "Cum ipso vixit" (l.3725) is the Sirmond preface's claim about the Sirmond Life, which Mai denies is Michael's. Naukratios wrote a letter on the death, not a Life.
- **Cyril and Methodius.** The Salzburg account is at l.13130-13722 of the Ginzel file (l.864-866 is the contents); Industriae tuae (880) is at l.13878-13982. No held line names Methodius's jailer or calls him "of Sirmium", though an editor's note to the Salzburg account says John VIII restored his diocese in place of the old see of Sirmium (Ginzel l.13166-13173).
- **Clement.** The Monitum (PG 126 l.96725-96749) reports Miklosich's argument and leaves the judgment to the reader; it does not argue it in its own voice. Patrologia Graeca 111 (l.243-244, l.36942-36947) places the Life about the middle of the tenth century, which the re-score does not cite. The Life dates his burial, not his death, to 27 July 6424 from the creation. The Saint Panteleimon dedication is not in the Life; only Nikodemos the Hagiorite's late book of saints gives it.
- **Antony and Theodosius.** No held line gives Antony's death year. The Paterik's base text begins only partway into its seventh discourse.
- **Rimbert.** His birthplace at Turholt is an unsigned note in Waitz's edition; Robinson says only "near". The priest Rimbert of Danish descent sent to Sweden may be another man (Dahlmann; Robinson). The held Life of Rimbert ends in chapter 23, before his death.

## Language and quotations

Lines held only in Greek, Latin, German or Slavonic are told in modern words with the source named, without quotation marks. Quotation marks are kept for held English: Percival, Salmond, Cross and Robinson. Quotations keep their own spelling ("favour", "succour"). Two quotations in the briefs cross scan marks in their files, and the words are otherwise exact: a footnote mark in Robinson's "cloakf" (l.2652), and in Cross's "Who ... your prior" (l.11853-11860) a running head and a stray quote mark before "your".
