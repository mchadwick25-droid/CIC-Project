[calmly] A young man walked to Kyiv to become a monk. Every monastery turned him away, for his clothes were poor. Then he heard of Antony, who lived in a cave, and threw himself at his feet. Child, do you see this cave, Antony said. It is a grim, cramped place, and you are young. The young man answered that Christ had brought him there. Here is the place, said Antony. Stay in it.

The young man was Theodosius. With Antony he founded the Caves monastery of Kyiv. Their story is told in the Russian Primary Chronicle, which Sylvester, prior of Saint Michael's, says he wrote in 1116, and in Nestor's Life of Theodosius.

The Chronicle says Antony was a layman from Lyubech who became a monk on Mount Athos. His prior sent him home to Rus, so that other monks might follow his example. On a hill outside Kyiv he dug a cave and prayed, in S. H. Cross's English, "Oh Lord, strengthen me in this place." After 1054, under Prince Izyaslav, brothers gathered to him. Wanting to live alone, he moved to another hill. When the brothers asked him for a prior, he answered, "Who among you is more obedient, more modest, and more mild than Theodosius? Let him be your prior."

Theodosius was born at Vasilev, near Kyiv, Nestor says. As abbot, the Chronicle says, he gathered a hundred monks. In 1062, by Nestor's account, he moved them from the cave to buildings above ground. He took the rule of the Studion monastery of Constantinople.

Later Izyaslav turned against Antony, and Antony fled by night to Chernigov, where he dug another cave. No source used here gives the year he died.

In 1073 Prince Svyatoslav drove his brother Izyaslav out of Kyiv. Nestor says Svyatoslav invited Theodosius to dinner, and Theodosius would not go to what he called Jezebel's table. He wrote that the blood of the prince's brother cried out to God against him, as Abel's had against Cain. Told he might be exiled, he said he was ready for exile or death.

Theodosius died on 3 May 1074. He told Stephen, the next abbot, "Change not the traditions and the institutions of the monastery."
