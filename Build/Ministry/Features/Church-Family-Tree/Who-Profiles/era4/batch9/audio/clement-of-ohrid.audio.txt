[calmly] In a Moravian prison, Clement and his companions sat bound in iron chains. They sang the hymns of the Third Hour of prayer, then the psalms. Then, his Life says, a great earthquake came, and the chains fell off.

Clement was a priest who had followed Methodius from his youth. His story is told in one Life. Its heading names Theophylact, archbishop of all Bulgaria, who died in 1107. The scholar Franz Miklosich argued that a Bulgarian disciple of Clement wrote it in the tenth century. Its narrator says he was always with Clement. The historian Johann Ginzel thinks Theophylact reworked older notes. Migne's editors leave it to the reader in one volume, and in another date the Life to about the middle of the tenth century.

After Methodius died, the Life says, the Frankish clergy won over Prince Svatopluk. Methodius's younger priests were sold to Jews. Soldiers drove Clement and others out naked toward the icy Danube. They crossed the river on three logs tied with lime bark. At Belgrade they were sent on to Boris, prince of the Bulgarians, who longed to talk with them.

Boris sent Clement to teach in Kutmichevitsa. The Life counts 3,500 picked pupils. We never saw him idle, the narrator writes. He showed some children their letters, explained Scripture to others, and shaped others' hands for writing, by night and day. He did this for seven years.

Symeon, the first to be called king of the Bulgarians, made him bishop of Dremvitsa. So Clement became the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue. Many priests there barely understood Greek, so he wrote simple, clear sermons for every feast. He had built a monastery at Ohrid before he became bishop, and added a church that later became the archbishop's seat.

Old, he asked Symeon to let him go. Give me these few days, he said, to speak with myself and with God, and let me die in my monastery. Symeon refused. Clement fell ill, finished the hymns sung from the Sunday after Easter to Pentecost, and died. He was buried on 27 July 916.

[warmly] The Life ends with a prayer to him. Through you, it says, all the land of Bulgaria came to know God.
