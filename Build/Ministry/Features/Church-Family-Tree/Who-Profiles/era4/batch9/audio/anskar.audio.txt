[calmly] Pirates suddenly surrounded the town of Hamburg. Anskar, its archbishop, saw it could not be held, and escaped, in C. H. Robinson's English of his Life, "without even a cloak to cover his body." The church and the monastery burned. So did the Bible the emperor had given him. Anskar said again and again the words of Job: the Lord gave, the Lord has taken away, blessed be the name of the Lord.

Anskar was a monk of Corbie, near Amiens, who carried the faith to the Danes and the Swedes. His Life was written by Rimbert, his disciple and successor, with help from a fellow disciple. Nearly all agree, says its editor Georg Waitz, that it was written at Hamburg before 876.

In 826 Harald, a Danish king driven from his kingdom, was baptized, with the emperor Louis as his sponsor. The abbot of Corbie named Anskar to go back with him, and Anskar was willing, though many reproached him. A few years later he set out for Sweden. Pirates took his ships and nearly forty books, and his party walked the rest of the long way to Birka. There Herigar, the town's prefect and the king's counselor, was baptized and built a church on his own land.

Waitz and Robinson date the sack of Hamburg to 845. The Annals of Saint-Bertin say that in 845 Horic, king of the Northmen, sent 600 ships up the Elbe, and Waitz thinks the city they then took was Hamburg. Yet Anskar later visited Horic often, became his friend and counselor, and won leave to build a church at Schleswig.

On a second journey to Sweden, an old man spoke for the faith at the people's assembly at Birka. "It is good for us," he said, "to have the help of this God who is always, and under all circumstances, able and willing to succour those who cry to Him."

Anskar died in 865. He prayed that God would forgive whoever had wronged him. When his breath failed, he told a brother to go on saying the prayers for him. That brother, says the Life of Rimbert, was Rimbert himself.

[warmly] When people spoke of the healings done through him, Anskar told a friend, "Were I worthy of such a favour from my God, I would ask that He would grant to me this one miracle, that by His grace He would make of me a good man."
