[calmly] An officer came to Theodore the Studite in exile with orders to give him fifty lashes. Theodore praised God, stripped off his clothes and said, My son, do what you were ordered to do to me. Struck with sudden fear, his Life says, the man threw himself at Theodore's feet and asked for his blessing.

Theodore was abbot of the Stoudios monastery in Constantinople. His story is told in two Lives that carry the name of Michael the monk. Angelo Mai, who edited one, says they hardly differ.

He was born in the city in 759, by Johann Fabricius's count. At 22, the Life says, he left the world with his family, led by his mother's brother Plato, for Sakkoudion, an estate outside the city. At 35 he became abbot there, against his will.

The emperor Constantine VI then put away his wife and married Theodote, a kinswoman of Theodore's. Theodore and Plato broke off communion with him. The emperor went to the hot springs at Prusa, expecting the two abbots to come and greet him. They stayed away. His men flogged Theodore and sent him with ten monks into exile at Thessalonica.

Recalled, he took charge of Stoudios. Theophanes the Confessor, a chronicler of his own time, says up to 700 monks served under him there. In his Testament Theodore told the abbot after him to own no slave, for a slave is a man made in God's image. Among the brothers all should be shared, nothing anyone's own, down to a needle.

He was exiled twice more. The second exile came in 809, after he refused to accept the restored priest Joseph, who had married the emperor to Theodote. The third came when he stood against the emperor Leo V's ban on images. When Christ suffers persecution in his image, he wrote to all the monks, even the disciple must speak the truth with a free and fearless voice.

He died on Sunday the 11th of November 826, with his disciple Naukratios, the next abbot, beside him. The brothers were singing a psalm, and at the words I will never forget your precepts, for by them you have given me life, he died.

[warmly] More than two centuries later, Theodosius of the Kyiv Caves learned the Studion rule from a monk named Michael. In S. H. Cross's English of the Russian Primary Chronicle, he "copied it out, and established it in his own monastery."
