In a Moravian prison, Clement and his companions sat bound in iron chains. They kept the hours of prayer and sang psalms. Then, his Life says, there came a great earthquake, and the chains fell off.

Clement was a priest who had followed Methodius from his youth. His story is told in one Life. Its heading names Theophylact, archbishop of Ohrid, who died in 1107. Franz Miklosich argued that a Bulgarian disciple of Clement wrote it in the tenth century, and its narrator says he was always with him. Johann Ginzel thinks Theophylact reworked older notes. Migne's editors leave it to the reader.

After Methodius died, the Life says, the Frankish clergy won over Prince Svatopluk. Methodius's younger priests were sold to Jews. Soldiers drove Clement and others out naked toward the icy Danube. They crossed the river on three logs tied with lime bark. At Belgrade they were sent on to Boris, prince of the Bulgarians, who longed to talk with them daily.

Boris sent Clement to teach in Kutmichevitsa, and gave him houses at Devol and places to rest at Ohrid. The Life counts 3,500 picked pupils. We never saw him idle, the narrator writes. He showed some children the shapes of the letters, explained Scripture to others, and shaped others' hands for writing, by night as well as by day. He did this for seven years.

Symeon, the first to be called king of the Bulgarians, made him bishop of Dremvitsa, also called Velitsa. So Clement became the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue. Many priests there barely understood Greek, so he wrote simple, clear sermons for every feast, plain enough for the dullest Bulgarian. At Ohrid he built a monastery, and a church that later became the archbishop's seat.

Old now, he asked Symeon to let him go. Give me these few days, he said, to speak with myself and with God, and let me die in my monastery. Symeon refused. Clement fell ill, finished the hymns sung from the Sunday after Easter to Pentecost, and died on 27 July 916.

The Life ends with a prayer to him. Through you, it says, all the land of Bulgaria came to know God.
