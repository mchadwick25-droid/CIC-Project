Cedd chose a site for a monastery, on land given by King Ethelwald. Bede says its crags and distant hills looked fit only for robbers and wild beasts. First Cedd meant to cleanse it by prayer and fasting. Through Lent he fasted each day but Sunday until evening, and then ate only "a little bread, one hen's egg, and a little milk mixed with water."

The place was Lastingham, in the north of England. Cedd and Chad were brothers, two of four who all became priests, which Bede calls "a rare thing to be met with." Bede tells their lives together, as he learned them from the monks of the one house they shared.

King Oswy sent Cedd to preach to the East Saxons, and Finan of Lindisfarne made him their bishop. He built churches and gathered monks at Ithancestir and at Tilbury, on the Thames. At the synod of Whitby in 664 he stood with the Irish party on Easter. Bede says he was also "a most careful interpreter for both parties." Afterward he accepted what Bede calls "the Catholic observance of Easter." He fell sick and died at Lastingham in a time of plague.

He left Lastingham to Chad, one of Aidan's disciples. Oswy then sent Chad to be made bishop of York, while Wilfrid was overseas. Chad went about on foot, Bede says, "after the manner of the apostles." When Archbishop Theodore told him he had not been properly consecrated, Chad offered to resign, "for I never thought myself worthy of it." Bede says Theodore completed his ordination instead. Stephen, who wrote Wilfrid's Life and takes his side, says Theodore ordered Chad removed from another man's see. Even so, Stephen calls him a true and most gentle servant of God.

At Theodore's request, Chad became bishop of the Mercians, at Lichfield. When Chad would not ride, Theodore lifted him onto the horse with his own hands. He died there during a plague, on the second of March.

Trumbert, one of Bede's teachers, had been a monk under Chad. He said that when a strong wind rose, Chad prayed for mercy for all mankind. If the wind grew stronger, "he closed his book, and prostrating himself on the ground, prayed still more earnestly."
