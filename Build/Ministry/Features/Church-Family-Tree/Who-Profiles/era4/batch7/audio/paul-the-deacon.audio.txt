[calmly] A Lombard named Lopichis, carried off to the Avars as a boy, fled as a grown man with only a quiver, a bow and a little food. Then, his great-grandson writes, "a wolf came to him and became the companion of his journey and his guide." When his bread ran out, Lopichis tried to shoot the wolf for food, and it slipped away. At last he reached Italy and the house where he was born. It had no roof and was full of brambles. Inside its walls grew a large ash tree, and he hung his quiver on it.

The great-grandson was Paul the Deacon, who tells this family story in his History of the Langobards, the Lombards. He was born in Friuli between 720 and 730, by the reckoning of W. D. Foulke, who translated the History in 1907. His father was Warnefrit and his mother Theudelinda, Paul writes. He became a monk, in all likelihood at Monte Cassino, Saint Benedict's house. The one sure point, Foulke says, is that it was before 782.

Around then Paul went to the court of Charlemagne, king of the Franks. Foulke says he taught Greek there and gathered a book of sermons by the king's order. He also wrote a history of the bishops of Metz. After a few years he went home to his monastery.

There he wrote the History of his people. He tells how Queen Theudelinda won gifts for the church, and how Pope Gregory sent her his books. Through her, Paul says, King Agilulf held the Catholic faith. Foulke thinks Paul is probably mistaken in this. Paul also says that under King Rothari almost every city had two bishops, one Catholic and one Arian, which Foulke's notes call doubtful.

He tells how the Lombards sacked Monte Cassino by night, and how Petronax of Brescia brought monks back about a hundred and ten years later.

[warmly] Foulke says Paul died on the thirteenth of April, but the year is unknown. His History stops after the death of King Liutprand. Franks, he writes, carried off the bones of Benedict to their own country. Even so, Benedict's "venerable mouth, sweeter than all nectar, and the eyes beholding ever heavenly things" remained, Paul says, "to us."
