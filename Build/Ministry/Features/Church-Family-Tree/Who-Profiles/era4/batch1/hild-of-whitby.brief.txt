One night a man of Hild's monastery, who could not sing, left a feast before his turn came. In his sleep, Bede says, someone told him to sing the beginning of created beings, and he woke with verses in praise of God. The steward took him to the abbess. Before many learned men she heard his dream and verses. She had him become a monk and learn the whole of sacred history. He was Caedmon, and Bede says no one in England could compare with him in religious song.

The abbess was Hild of Whitby, in Northumbria, in northern England. Most of what is known of her comes from Bede, writing some fifty years after her death. Bede says she was the daughter of Hereric, a nephew of King Edwin of Northumbria. She took the faith with Edwin, whose baptism at York Bede dates to 627.

She lived thirty-three years in the world. Then she went to the East Angles, meaning to join her sister at the monastery of Cale in Gaul. After a year, Bishop Aidan of Lindisfarne called her home. She was made abbess of Heruteu, which L. Cecil Jane gives as Hartlepool, and Aidan often came to teach her.

Later she built or set in order a monastery at Whitby, for men and women. Bede says she taught peace and charity above all. As in the first church, no one there was rich and no one poor. Kings and princes asked her advice. Five bishops came from her house, Bede says, and all who knew her called her Mother.

In 664 the kings met at Whitby to settle the date of Easter. Hild and her followers held with Bishop Colman and the Scots, the people who had sent Aidan. King Oswy ruled for the Roman side, which Wilfrid of Ripon had argued. Stephen, Wilfrid's biographer, also places the meeting at Whitby, in Hild's presence. He copies a letter of Pope John saying that men sent by Archbishop Theodore of Tarsus and by Hild went to Rome to accuse Wilfrid. Bede is silent on it.

Hild suffered a fever for six years and kept teaching. She died on 17 November 680, aged sixty-six, urging her people to keep peace among themselves and with all. Bede says her mother dreamed of a jewel whose light spread through all Britain. He writes that it was fulfilled in her daughter, "whose life was a bright example."
