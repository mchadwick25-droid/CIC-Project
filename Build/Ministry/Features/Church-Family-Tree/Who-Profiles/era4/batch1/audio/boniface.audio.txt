[calmly] In 754 Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, sailed down the Rhine toward Frisia for the last time. Edward Kylie, who translated his letters in 1911, says he had a winding-sheet packed in the chest with his books. On the fifth of June at Dokkum, he writes, a pagan band surrounded his tents, and he forbade his companions to resist. The Royal Frankish Annals record that pagans killed him there as he preached.

He was English, born Wynfrith between 675 and 680, by Kylie's reckoning. For his early life, Kylie's modern account is the main witness. At Nursling, near Winchester, he lectured on the Bible. A first mission to Frisia in 716 met resistance. In Rome in 718, Kylie says, his English name took a Latin form.

In Hesse, Kylie writes, he felled the oak at Geismar sacred to Thor and built a chapel to Saint Peter from its ruins. No missionary before him had called women to his aid, Kylie says. To Lioba, Tecla and Cynehild he wrote that he would "beseech, nay all but command" them to pray for him.

About 737, by Kylie's date, he asked all the English to pray that God would turn the hearts of the pagan Saxons. The Saxons themselves, he reminded them, were wont to say, "We are of one blood and one bone."

In 742 Carloman, prince of the Franks, called a synod. His decree sets Archbishop Boniface, the envoy of Saint Peter, over the bishops of the cities, and orders a synod every year. In a letter Alfred Boretius prints with the decree, Boniface told Pope Zacharias that Carloman had promised to set right the church's order. It had been trodden down, he wrote, for no less than sixty or seventy years.

After a synod in 747, he sent its decrees to Cuthbert, archbishop of Canterbury. He likened himself to a watchdog that sees robbers breaking in, but with no one to help him "only complains and grieves." When Pippin was made king of the Franks, the Royal Frankish Annals say, Boniface anointed him.

Cuthbert later wrote to Boniface's helper Lul that an English synod had fixed the day of his martyrdom as a yearly feast.

[warmly] Kylie quotes Boniface's greeting to a new pope, Stephen. "If I have accomplished anything for the good of the Church during the thirty-six years of my Roman mission I still wish to complete and perfect it."
