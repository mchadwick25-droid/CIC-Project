[calmly] On Saint Andrew's day in 841, a noblewoman at Uzès began writing to her elder son. She saw many in this world rejoicing with their children, she wrote. She, Dhuoda, was far from her son William, so she sent him a book in her name. Édouard Bondurand, her editor in 1887, dates it from her closing lines.

Her preface says she married William's father in the palace at Aachen in 824. Léopold Delisle, quoted by Bondurand, names him as Bernard, duke of Septimania. William was born two years later, and a second son at Uzès in March 841. Before the baby was baptized, Bernard had him brought to Aquitaine by Elefant, bishop of Uzès, while her lord's orders kept her at Uzès.

She had heard Bernard had placed William in the hands of King Charles the Bald. The historian Nithard says when Bernard learned Charles had won the battle of Fontenoy, he sent William to him. The boy was to commend himself to Charles, if the king would grant him Bernard's holdings in Burgundy.

Dhuoda taught in writing, for her sons. Her book teaches William to love and seek God, to honor his father and to keep faith with Charles. You will have teachers who teach you more and greater things, she tells him, but none with a heart burning like your mother's. Once his little brother, whose name she did not yet know, was baptized, William was to teach him and later show him the book.

She told William to pray often that God would give his father peace with all, if it could be, and bring him to heaven. She had borrowed much, from Christians and from Jews, to keep up her service to Bernard. William was to pay any debt owed at her death. She wanted verses on her grave whose first letters spell her name, and which ask all who pass to pray for her.

The Annals of Saint-Bertin say the Franks judged Bernard guilty of treason, and he was put to death on Charles's order in 844. In 850 William took two counts by a trick, was taken by a craftier one, and was killed at Barcelona. No source used here gives the year of Dhuoda's death, though Bondurand thinks she died soon after the book was finished.

[warmly] She told William to keep coming back to this little book. Farewell, she wrote, and be strong, noble boy, always in Christ.
