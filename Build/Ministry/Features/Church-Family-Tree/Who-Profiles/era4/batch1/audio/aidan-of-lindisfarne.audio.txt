[calmly] Bede passes on a report of a council among the elders of the Scots, as he calls the Irish. King Oswald of Northumbria had asked them for a bishop, but the first man they sent came home calling the English stubborn and barbarous. Aidan, a monk of Iona, told him he had been too severe, and had not first given his hearers "the milk of more easy doctrine". The elders made Aidan a bishop and sent him.

He came to the English from Iona, which Bede calls Hii, in the 630s. Oswald gave him a see on Lindisfarne, an island at high tide. Aidan spoke little English, so the king, who had learned the Scots' tongue in exile, interpreted for him. Nearly all we know of Aidan comes from Bede, writing some eighty years later, from those who had known him.

Bede says Aidan taught no otherwise than he lived. He walked through town and country, urging people to believe and to give alms. Gifts from the rich went to the poor, or to ransom people wrongly sold as slaves. Many of those he freed he taught and made priests.

King Oswin of Deira gave him a fine horse, and Aidan gave it to a beggar. When the king complained, he answered, "Is that foal of a mare more dear to you than the Son of God?" Oswin later knelt for his pardon, and Aidan wept and told his priest the king would not live long. Oswin was killed soon after, and Aidan died twelve days later, on 31 August 651, Bede says.

Aidan kept Easter Sunday by his country's custom, between the fourteenth and twentieth day of the moon. Bede, who followed the Roman Easter, says he detests this. Charles Plummer, who edited Bede in 1896, calls it "sad" that Bede breaks off his praise of Aidan to say so. Yet Bede grants that Aidan always kept it on a Sunday. While Aidan lived, he adds, all bore the difference patiently.

An unnamed Lindisfarne monk wrote a saint's Life of Cuthbert of Lindisfarne. He says the young shepherd Cuthbert saw a soul carried to heaven, and days later heard that Aidan had died that hour. Bede's Life of Cuthbert says he learned it the next morning.

[warmly] Bede praises Aidan's love of peace and his care for the afflicted and the poor. "These things I much love and admire in the aforesaid bishop," he writes.
