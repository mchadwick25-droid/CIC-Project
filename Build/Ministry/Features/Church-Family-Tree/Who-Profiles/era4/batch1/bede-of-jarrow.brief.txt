On his last evening, Bede was dictating to a boy named Wilbert. The boy said one sentence was still unwritten. "Well, then, write it," Bede said. When the boy said it was written, Bede agreed, "it is finished." He asked the boy to hold his head while he sat facing the place where he used to pray. He died on the floor of his cell singing "Glory be to the Father."

The scene comes from a letter by his pupil Cuthbert to a fellow pupil, Cuthwin. Charles Plummer, who prints it, holds that Bede died on the eve of Ascension Day in 735, though the Saxon Chronicle gives 734.

Bede was a monk and priest of Wearmouth and Jarrow, in northern England. At the close of his History, finished in 731, he tells his own life. He was born on the monastery's land. At seven he was given to Abbot Benedict Biscop, and afterwards to Ceolfrid, to be educated. He became a deacon in his nineteenth year and a priest in his thirtieth. "I always took delight in learning, teaching, and writing," he wrote.

An anonymous History of the Abbots tells of a plague at Jarrow. It killed every monk who could read or sing the services, except Abbot Ceolfrid and one small boy he had raised. The two kept up the psalms with their antiphons until others were trained. Plummer dates the plague to 686, and says the boy can hardly be anyone but Bede.

His own list names commentaries on many books of the Bible, drawn from the Fathers. It also lists saints' lives, hymns, and books on nature and the reckoning of time. Bede wrote that rowdy peasants at their drink charged him with heresy over his book On the Times, which Plummer dates to 703. He answered in a letter to Plegwin, a Hexham monk, on the six ages of the world.

Bede names his sources in the History. Nothelm, a London priest, searched the papal archives and found letters of Gregory the Great. For Northumbria, Bede leaned on "the faithful testimony of innumerable witnesses." L. Cecil Jane, who edited the 1903 English edition, calls the History practically the only authority for England from the Saxon invasion to 732.

He closed the History with a prayer to "good Jesus." He asked that he, who had been given "the words of thy wisdom," might "come to thee, the fountain of all wisdom."
