# Who profiles: era 4, batch 8

Six profiles, one id each: `eadburga-of-thanet`, `abbess-bugga`, `yahya-ibn-said-of-antioch`, `elias-of-nisibis`, `adam-jingjing`, `al-baladhuri`.

This is the last era 4 batch under Mark's ruling of 2026-11-14 that eras 4 to 7 are built in full. Four rows come from the era 4 table of `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-12_who-scan-supplement/Who_Scan_Supplement.md`: Eadburga, abbess of Thanet; Bugga, abbess; Yahya ibn Said of Antioch; and Elias of Nisibis. Two come from `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-11_who-scan-era4/Who_Scan_Era4.md`: Adam (Jingjing), the author of the Xi'an stele, and al-Baladhuri, "WHO (OUTSIDE WITNESS)". The final sweep found al-Baladhuri in no batch, and the coordinator added him here (2026-11-14). None of the six has a census person entry or was written before. Mennas, Benjamin I's brother, is a STORY row and is not written here.

The coordinator marked none of the six major. Mark ruled on length during this batch (2026-11-14, relayed by the coordinator): "no stop, how much of the story will we loose, i would rather have a 2;15 good story than a 2;00 cramped but flat card". Non-majors run to about 2:15, about 365 words, at most. The briefs run 350 to 372 words, about 2:10 to 2:18.
- **Eadburga and Bugga** are Whos under the women ruling: Eadburga led a house and taught Leoba; Bugga governed a monastery, by the letter of Ethelbert the Second, king of Kent. Both lives are thin and their cards say so. The two are kept apart, as Edward Kylie keeps them. The Bollandists' report that Eadburga was also called Bugga is told in both briefs and tagged as contested.
- **Yahya ibn Said and Elias of Nisibis** are recorders and stay at standard length, under the Palladius, John of Ephesus and Gregory of Tours rulings. Yahya's life rests on his own preface alone. Elias's life rests on a note that later hands added to his Chronography and on Joseph Assemani; Bar Hebraeus and Mari ibn Sulayman give the setting of the Isho'yahb affair and of the peace with Elias I.
- **al-Baladhuri** is an outside witness, as Procopius and Agathias are in era 3: a Muslim historian of the conquests. His card's Tradition line reads "Outside the church". His life rests on Philip Hitti's introduction, from al-Fihrist, Yakut and al-Kutubi.
- **Adam** is a name on a text. His brief says what the stele says of him and how little that is. It quotes his own text, in A. Wylie's English, and agrees with the Alopen profile (era 4 batch 5) on the stele.

Held witnesses beyond those the scans cite:
- **Eadburga.** The Bollandists' note on Hildelitha (Acta Sanctorum, March, volume 3); Charles Plummer's notes to Bede on her as a copyist; a note in Migne's Alcuin guessing that she built a church of Saint Peter at Thanet; Louis Duchesne on Lul's gift.
- **Bugga.** Bregwin of Canterbury's letter to Lul, in Patrologia Latina 96, on an abbess Bugga's burial day, 27 December; Eadmer's Life of Bregwin, for his see; the index of Migne's Alcuin, which asks whether a Bugga who built a church is the same woman.
- **Yahya.** His editors' preface; Gustave Schlumberger, quoted by Frédéric Macler in his Stepanos of Taron; A. Vasiliev in his Agapius.
- **Elias.** His own opening to the second part of the Chronography; his preface and chapters on inheritance, which Ebedjesu of Soba copied into his collection of canons, in Angelo Mai's Latin, with Mai's description of a Vatican codex holding his letter on Isho'yahb; Assemani's notes to Ebedjesu's catalogue; Bar Hebraeus and Mari on Isho'yahb son of Ezekiel; J.-B. Chabot's Synodicon and Frédéric Macler on his death; M.-A. Kugener on the date of the Chronography; J. Parisot; the Chronica Minora; George David Malech.
- **al-Baladhuri.** Richard Gottheil's foreword to Hitti.
- **Adam.** Henri Cordier's note in Yule's Marco Polo, joining King-tsing to Adam and reporting J. Takakusu on a Buddhist sutra; Assemani's reading of the Syriac lines; T. J. Lamy, as J.-B. Chabot quotes him.

Corrections to the scans found in drafting:
- **Elias's death year.** The Library header of the Chronography file gives 975-1046, and the supplement row repeats it. No held line gives 1046. Assemani puts his death after the catholicos Elias I, who died in 1049, and Chabot soon after; Frédéric Macler gives about 1056. The card gives all three and tags the death year as contested.
- **Elias's own words.** Besides the Chronography, his preface and chapters on inheritance are held, in Mai's Latin of Ebedjesu's canons.
- **Elias's life.** The supplement row says the editors place his life on pp. 228-229 of the file, not opened. Those pages are the note after the end of his canon, which E. W. Brooks marks as added later by others. It gives his birth in 975 and his ordinations in 994, 1002 and 1008, reckoned from its Greek and Arab years.
- **Eadburga's Thanet.** "Abbess of Thanet" is in Kylie's headings and introduction, not in the letters themselves.

Names and ids, the worker's calls:
- **Eadburga of Thanet**, `eadburga-of-thanet`. The Library holds other Eadburgas, among them a West Saxon queen in Asser and a daughter of King Edward in Wulfstan's Life of Ethelwold. Thanet is Kylie's place for her.
- **Abbess Bugga**, `abbess-bugga`. No held line gives her house a name or a place, so the title carried by Boniface's headings and Ethelbert's letter identifies her. The Alcuin volume's Bugga, a king's daughter, and a Westphalian Bugga in the Acta Sanctorum are other women or unproven matches.
- **Yahya ibn Said of Antioch**, `yahya-ibn-said-of-antioch`, as the scan names him. Other Yahyas in the Library include the Abu Salih Yahya of Egypt and Baladhuri's informant Yahya ibn Sa'id.
- **Elias of Nisibis**, `elias-of-nisibis`, with Elias bar Shinaya as an other name. The catholicos Elias I, earlier bishop of Tirhan, is a different man, and so are the other bishops named Elias in Mari.
- **Adam (Jingjing)**, `adam-jingjing`, the census form. Adam the deacon, son of Jazedbuzid, named on the same stone, is a different man.
- **al-Baladhuri**, `al-baladhuri`, as the scan names him; Hitti gives him as Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir al-Baladhuri.

Card traditions are the census's names. All six fit the scans' placings:
- Eadburga and Bugga read "Anglo-Saxon Christianity" (597 to 793).
- Yahya reads "Melkite Arabic Christianity" (700 to 1009). He wrote in Arabic as a sequel to the history of Sa'id ibn Bitriq, patriarch of Alexandria, whose flock his history calls Melkites. His move to Antioch in 1014 or 1015 falls after the entry's end; "The Byzantine Imperial Church (Macedonian Era)" covers that, but no held line ties him to it, so it is not added.
- Elias reads "The Church of the East under the Abbasids" (845 to 1220).
- Adam reads "The Church of the East on the Silk Road" (635 to 845), as Alopen does.
- al-Baladhuri reads "Outside the church", by the outside witness rule; the scan places him with the Muslim chroniclers of the conquests.

Lines held only in Latin or French are told in modern words with the source named. Quotation marks are kept for held English: Kylie's Boniface letters, Wylie's stele, Yule and Hitti. Quotations keep their own spelling, including "honoured" and "thy".

Every WHO row of the era 4 scan (46 rows) and of the supplement scan's era 4 table (14 rows) was checked against `Who-Profiles/era4/batch1/` to `batch8/`.
