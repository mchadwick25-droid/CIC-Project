The Christians of Damascus kept the church of Saint John beside the mosque. Mu'awiyah wanted it added to the mosque, and they refused. 'Abd al-Malik offered them money, and they refused again. Then the caliph al-Walid threatened to tear it down. Someone warned him that whoever "tears down a church will lose his wits". Angered, al-Walid called for a spade. He began breaking the walls with his own hand, in "a robe of yellow silk", and workmen pulled the rest down.

Al-Baladhuri tells this story. He was a Muslim historian of the ninth century, and his book is the Futuh al-Buldan. Philip Hitti put it into English in 1916 as The Origins of the Islamic State. From older Arab lives, Hitti says, he was Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir, a native of Baghdad of Persian descent, and a friend of caliphs. He died in 892, Hitti writes, his mind gone from drinking the juice of the anacardia, in Arabic baladhur, which gave him his name.

In his account of how the Muslims won their lands, the church appears in the terms they made. When the Christians of Najran came to the Prophet, he reports, their written terms secured "their persons, religion, lands and possessions". "No attempt shall be made to turn a bishop from his office as a bishop," the terms go on. Under the terms he reports with the Nubians, they gave slaves each year and received food in exchange.

His Damascus story goes on. The caliph 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz ordered the part taken from the church returned, and the Muslims of the city objected. In the end the Christians gave up their claim to Saint John's. In return they received the churches of al-Ghutah that had been taken by force.

Hitti weighs how far he can be trusted. He says al-Baladhuri tries, wherever he can, to rest each fact on its own chain of named reporters. Hitti finds the book "free from exaggeration", but says the author scarcely judges what he reports. The book is also a shortened form of a larger work, now lost.

Hitti quotes the later historian al-Mas'udi: "We know of no better work on the history of the Moslem conquests".
