The Christians of Damascus still held their church of Saint John. The caliph Mu'awiyah wanted to add it to the city's mosque, and they refused. The caliph 'Abd al-Malik offered them money, and they refused again. Then his son, the caliph al-Walid, threatened to tear it down. Someone warned him that whoever "tears down a church will lose his wits". Angered, al-Walid called for a spade. He began breaking the walls with his own hand, in "a robe of yellow silk", and workmen pulled the rest down.

Al-Baladhuri tells this story in his Futuh al-Buldan. He was a Muslim historian who died in 892, well over a century after Mu'awiyah's reign. Philip Hitti, who put the book into English in 1916, draws his life from al-Fihrist and the lives by Yakut and al-Kutubi. He was Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir, a native of Baghdad of Persian descent. He died deranged, Hitti writes, from drinking the juice of the anacardia, in Arabic baladhur, which gave him his name.

Years later the caliph 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz ordered the part taken from the church returned, and the Muslims of the city objected. In the end the Christians gave up their claim to Saint John's. In return they received the churches of al-Ghutah, an orchard district of Damascus, that had been taken by force.

During the siege of Damascus, he reports, the commander Khalid wrote terms at the bishop's request. They promised the people "security for their lives, property and churches". He also gives the terms the Christians of Najran made with Muhammad. "No attempt shall be made to turn a bishop from his office as a bishop," they say.

Hitti weighs how far al-Baladhuri can be trusted. He says al-Baladhuri tries, wherever he can, to rest each fact on its own chain of named reporters. Sometimes, as he says himself, he pieces reports together into one account. The Saint John's story is one he opens only with "It is reported". Hitti finds the book "free from exaggeration", but says the author scarcely judges what he reports.

Hitti quotes the later historian al-Mas'udi: "We know of no better work on the history of the Moslem conquests".
