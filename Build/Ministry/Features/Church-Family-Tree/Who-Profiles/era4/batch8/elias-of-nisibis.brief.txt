Under the Arab year 397, which began in 1006, Elias of Nisibis recorded a split among Christians. The Christians of the West and of the East did not agree on how to reckon the fast before Easter and the feasts. The West set the start of the fast on 17 Shebat. The East set it on the 24th.

Elias was metropolitan of Nisibis in the Church of the East. He set this down in his Chronography, which E. W. Brooks, who put its first part into Latin, dates to 1019. It runs year by year, and for almost every year it names its sources, Greek, Syriac and Arabic works, most of them now lost. For its last years it names none. There, Brooks writes, Elias tells what he remembered or had heard.

A note added after Elias's own entries tells his life, and Brooks marks it as the work of later hands. It says he was born in 975. John, bishop of Senn, ordained him priest in 994 at the monastery of Father Simeon. John later became catholicos, the head of the Church of the East. He made Elias bishop of Beth Nuhadra in 1002, and metropolitan of Nisibis in 1008.

In 1020 Isho'yahb son of Ezekiel was made catholicos. Bar Hebraeus, a later Syriac historian, says Isho'yahb paid the governor five thousand denarii. The governor ordered that all who would not consent be drowned, and many bishops refused to proclaim him. Joseph Assemani, writing in 1725, says Elias stood firm against him. In a letter to the people of Baghdad, Assemani says, Elias called Isho'yahb a buyer of his office.

Elias gathered rules on inheritance from a work of the catholicos Elias I. Ebedjesu of Soba, which is Nisibis, copied them into his book of canons. In his preface, Elias prays for the soul of Elias I, who died in 1049. Assemani puts the metropolitan's death after that. J.-B. Chabot says he died soon after. Frédéric Macler gives about 1056.

The second part of the Chronography, on reckoning years and feasts, opens with a prayer for its reader, his beloved brother. May the Lord guide you, Elias wrote, to please him in your thoughts, words and deeds.
