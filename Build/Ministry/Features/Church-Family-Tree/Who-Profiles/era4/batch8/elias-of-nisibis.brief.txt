In the Arab year 397, which began in 1006, a churchman of Nisibis noted a split among Christians. The Christians of the West and of the East did not agree on how to reckon the fast and the feasts. The West set the start of the fast on 17 Shebat. The East set it a week later, on the 24th.

The churchman was Elias of Nisibis, metropolitan of that city in the Church of the East. He set this down in his Chronography, which E. W. Brooks, its Latin translator, dates to 1019. It runs year by year, and for almost every year it names its sources, Greek, Syriac and Arabic works, most of them now lost. For its last years it names none. There, Brooks writes, Elias tells what he remembered or had heard.

A note added after Elias's own entries tells his life, and Brooks marks it as the work of later hands. It says he was born in 975. John, bishop of Senn, ordained him priest in 994 at the monastery of Father Simeon. John later became catholicos, the head of the Church of the East. He made Elias bishop of Beth Nuhadra in 1002, and metropolitan of Nisibis in 1008.

In 1020 a new catholicos was made, Isho'yahb son of Ezekiel. Bar Hebraeus says he paid the governor five thousand denarii, and the governor ordered that all who would not consent be drowned. Many bishops refused to proclaim him. Joseph Assemani, writing in 1725, says Elias stood firm against him. Elias wrote to the people of Baghdad that Isho'yahb had bought his office. From the synods and the Fathers, he argued that the ordination was void.

When Elias died is not settled. Assemani reasons that he outlived the catholicos Elias I, who died in 1049, since one of his prefaces prays for that patriarch's soul.

The second part of the Chronography, on reckoning years and feasts, opens with a prayer for its reader, his beloved brother. May the Lord guide you, Elias wrote, to please him in your thoughts, words and deeds.
