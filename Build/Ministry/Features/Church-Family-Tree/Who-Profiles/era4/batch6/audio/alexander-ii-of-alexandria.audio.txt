[calmly] Alexander II, the Coptic patriarch of Alexandria, went to Misr to greet the new governor, Abd Allah, as patriarchs did. "What is this man?" Abd Allah asked. He held the patriarch until he paid three thousand dinars. Then George, a deacon from Dimru, went to the governor. "Dost thou desire the life of the patriarch or money?" he asked. "I wish for the money," Abd Allah answered. George took Alexander round the North for two months, begging, until they had the sum.

The History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria, a later Arabic history of the Coptic church, tells it this way. Alexander was a monk of the monastery of Az-Zajaj. He was consecrated on the feast of Saint Mark, after the see had stood empty three years. B. Evetts, the History's translator, gives his years as 705 to 730.

The next governor, Kurrah, sent by the caliph al-Walid, arrested him as well. "Our Law bids us not to lay up treasure," Alexander told him. Alexander asked leave to beg in Upper Egypt, and Kurrah let him go. "Since the time of the Father Benjamin we have not seen a patriarch in Upper Egypt," the people said.

Kurrah took the church's gold and silver vessels. At Easter, the people and clergy of Alexandria demanded their dues. He told them they now used chalices of glass and patens of wood. They reviled him, the History says, and he bore it.

The History says that under the governor Usamah, monks were branded on the left hand with a ring-shaped iron. Eusèbe Renaudot, a Catholic scholar writing in Latin in 1713, says instead that they had to wear an iron ring on the right hand. The governor Ubaid Allah had the Christians counted and marked, and tried to brand Alexander too. Alexander refused. He asked for three days, and he prayed to die rather than be branded. On the third day he fell sick.

[warmly] "Prepare the boat at sunset that we may depart, for to-morrow the Lord Jesus Christ will visit me," he told the brothers. They fled down the river and reached Tarnut by morning, and there he died. Shamul, bishop of Wasim, was the only bishop with him.
