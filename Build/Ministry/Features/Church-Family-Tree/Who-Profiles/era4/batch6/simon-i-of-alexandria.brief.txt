John, head of the monastery of Az-Zajaj, was handsome, and the Amir Abd al-Aziz agreed to make him patriarch. Then one bishop stood up, says the History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria. "This man shall not be our patriarch," he said. "Simon is worthy of this degree."

Simon I of Alexandria was a Syrian, from the East. The Amir asked why they could not choose a man of their own country. "The matter belongs to God, and in the second place to thee," the bishops replied. The Amir had him ordained.

The History is a later Arabic history of the Coptic church. B. Evetts, who translated it, dates Simon's patriarchate 689 to 701. The History says Simon's parents had given him to the church as a boy, like Samuel, at John's monastery. John the deacon, who wrote the History's life of Michael I, says Simon's archdeacon George wrote the history of these years.

Some of Simon's own priests poisoned his cup, but it did him no harm. Then they gave him poisoned figs while he fasted, and he lay forty days in pain. When the Amir ordered the poisoners burned, Simon fell on his face. He could not stay patriarch if they died, he said, and the priests were set free. The Ethiopic Synaxarium, a book of saints' days, says instead that they were sent into exile.

A priest came from India asking for a bishop. Simon would not ordain one without the Amir's command. The Gaianites, a rival sect, ordained a man anyway. The caliph Abd al-Malik had the hands and feet of the new bishop and his two priests cut off. He wrote to the Amir that the patriarch had sent news of Egypt to India. The Amir questioned Simon at night, and Simon said: "If a voice came from heaven, bidding me deviate from the truth, I would say no otherwise." The Amir gave him three days. At sunset on the second day, Simon's spiritual son found the Indian priest. The next morning the priest told the Amir all that had happened, and the Amir saw that Simon had told the truth.

Before he died, he went to see the monks of Wadi Habib. "I shall not see them again after this time in the body," he said.
