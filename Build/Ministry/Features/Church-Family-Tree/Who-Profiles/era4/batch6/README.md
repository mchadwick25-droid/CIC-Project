# Who profiles: era 4, batch 6

Eight profiles, one id each: `agathon-of-alexandria`, `john-iii-of-samannud`, `isaac-of-alexandria`, `simon-i-of-alexandria`, `alexander-ii-of-alexandria`, `michael-i-of-alexandria`, `joseph-i-of-alexandria`, `severus-ibn-al-muqaffa`.

These are the eight Coptic WHO rows of `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-11_who-scan-era4/Who_Scan_Era4.md`, the sixth batch under Mark's ruling of 2026-11-14 that eras 4 to 7 are built in full. None of the eight is a census person entry or was written before. Benjamin I (era 4 batch 2) and his brother Mennas, a separate STORY row, are not in this batch.

The coordinator marked none of the eight major. Coptic Christianity under Early Islam has Benjamin I as its major, and these patriarchs are told mostly through one History. All eight briefs run about 340 to 360 words, about 2 minutes. Severus ibn al-Muqaffa is a recorder and stays at standard length, under the Palladius, John of Ephesus and Gregory of Tours rulings. His own life is thin, and his card says so.

Whose account each life rests on:
- **The History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria**, in B. Evetts's English (Patrologia Orientalis 1 and 5), for Agathon, John III, Isaac, Simon I, Alexander II and Michael I. The briefs call it a later Arabic history of the Coptic church and do not call its lives Severus's. Evetts holds that its lives from the seventh century are by writers of their own time.
- **The writers inside it.** John III's life is told by an unnamed writer who calls himself John's spiritual son. Michael I's life is told by John the deacon, an eyewitness who names himself. He calls Moses, bishop of Wasim, his spiritual father. John says the archdeacon George, Simon's secretary, wrote the history down to the days of Alexander II, and that he himself told the rest of Alexander's chapter, down to the death of the patriarch Theodore. No held line names the writer of the lives of Agathon or Simon.
- **Isaac** also has a long Coptic Life by Mina, held in E. Porcher's French. Porcher says it was written to edify.
- **Joseph I.** The History's life of him is not held. His profile rests on Eusèbe Renaudot's Latin retelling of it, the Arabic and Ethiopic synaxaria, and Bar Hebraeus's quotation of Dionysius of Tel Mahre.
- **Severus ibn al-Muqaffa.** His own voice is the History's third preface. The held lines show a dispute over how much of the History is his, and over his century. Both are tagged as contested.

The held witnesses go past those the scan lists:
- **Simon I.** Basset's Arabic synaxarium notice, 24 Abib, bound into the PO 17 John of Ephesus file.
- **Agathon.** His own first-person account of Benjamin's consecration of the church of Saint Macarius, in Benjamin's life. A cleaner copy of that life is bound into the Basset Thout to Babeh synaxarium file.
- **John III.** Budge's Ethiopic synaxarium, 1 Takhshash, and Abu Salih's monastery of the Brothers at Sailah.
- **Isaac.** Mina's Life is held twice, in the Toubeh to Amshir synaxarium file and bound into the Evetts PO 1 file.
- **Michael I.** Basset's Arabic synaxarium for Barmahat (PO 16), and the notice of the bishop of Ousim, 11 Mesore, bound into the PO 17 John of Ephesus file.
- **Joseph I.** Renaudot's full chapter; Bar Hebraeus's Latin, with Dionysius's report on the Coptic church; Budge's 23 Tekemt; Abu Salih on the Nubian prince Georgios.
- **Severus.** Renaudot's notice under the patriarch Abraham, with his list of works; Budge's and Sylvain Grébaut's Ethiopic synaxaria, 6 Takhshash; Joseph Assemani's catalogue.

Corrections to the scan found in drafting:
- John III was pressed for a hundred thousand denarii, not drachmae. Drachmae appear only in his answer.
- Simon's teacher was John, head of the monastery of Az-Zajaj, not the patriarch John III. The men who kept mistresses were laymen, and the bishops, not Simon, shut them out.
- Alexander II's life gives twenty-four and a half years on the throne, not twenty-five.
- Michael I's life runs from line 4414 of the PO 5 file, not 4979.

Many lines are held only in Latin or French. The briefs tell those lines in modern words, with the source named, and keep quotation marks for held English (Evetts's History, Budge's Ethiopic synaxarium). Quotations keep Evetts's wording, including "to-morrow" and "thou".

Names and ids, the worker's calls:
- **Agathon of Alexandria**, as B. Evetts spells him and as the approved Benjamin I card names him. The scan's "Agatho", Abu Salih's form, is an other name. Pope Agatho of Rome is a different man.
- **John III of Samannud**, as the scan heads him; "of Samannud" already marks him off from John of Nikiu.
- **Joseph I of Alexandria**, an English id, as asked. The census names him Yusab, and Renaudot heads him "Yusab, sive Josephus". Yusab is an other name, and the brief says "also called Yusab".
- **Severus ibn al-Muqaffa**, as the census names him. He is kept apart from Severus of Antioch.

Card traditions are the census's names. All eight read "Coptic Christianity under Early Islam" (642 to 1013), as the scan places them. All their dates fall inside it, Severus's included, whichever century is taken.
