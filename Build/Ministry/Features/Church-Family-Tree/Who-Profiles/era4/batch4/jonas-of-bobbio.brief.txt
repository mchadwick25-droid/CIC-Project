For three years, Jonas of Bobbio wrote, he was carried along the shores of the Ocean. He sailed the river Scheldt, and the slow marsh of the Elnone often soaked his feet. He had gone to help Bishop Amand, who was curbing the old errors of the people there with the blade of the Gospel.

Jonas says so in the preface to his Life of Columbanus, the Irish monk who founded Luxeuil in Gaul and Bobbio in Italy. Jonas was a monk of Bobbio in the seventh century. His translator, Dana Munro, says he entered it three years after Columbanus died. Some three years before the preface, Jonas had promised at Abbot Bertulf's command to write the saint's deeds. Many who had seen them were still alive, Jonas says, and he learned more from Attala and Eustasius, the abbots who followed Columbanus.

It is a saint's Life, and its miracles are many, as Munro notes. Bruno Krusch, an editor of the Frankish saints' Lives, dates it to about 640. Jonas often names the man who told him a story. "Theudegisil himself told us of this", he writes of one wonder, in Munro's English.

In the third year of King Clothar, which Krusch reckons as 659, Jonas was an abbot himself. On a journey ordered by the king and his mother, Queen Balthild, he rested a few days at the monastery of Réomé. Its monks begged him to write down what the disciples of their founder, John, and those after them had passed on, and he did so. Krusch says two forgers of the Carolingian age later reworked the book, and one claimed to have lived before Jonas.

Krusch believes Jonas also wrote the older Life of Vedast, bishop of Arras, since its style and one of its wonders match his.

If readers found him praising someone still living, Jonas wrote, they should not call him a flatterer. He meant only to commend to memory what deserved it.
