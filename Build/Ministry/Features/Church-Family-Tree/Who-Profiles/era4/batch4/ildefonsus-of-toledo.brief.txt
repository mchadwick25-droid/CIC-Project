The boy Ildefonsus had run away to the monastery of Agali to become a monk. His father came after him in a fury. Only a single wall stood between them. The father pushed on into the inner rooms and passed the place where the boy was hiding. Then he attacked the monastery, sword in hand, found nothing, and went home to mourn a lost son.

Ildefonsus became bishop of Toledo, in Visigothic Spain, in the seventh century. The story comes from Julian of Toledo, who lived in his time and later became bishop of the city. Julian's account is a short tribute to him. Julian says the boy came back to Agali once his father had given up, and lived there as a monk for many years. He built a convent for women and endowed it from his own wealth. Then he became abbot of Agali. An abbot Ildefonsus signed the acts of two councils of Toledo in the 650s.

The king's force brought him back to Toledo, Julian says, and he became bishop after Eugenius the second. He was made bishop in the ninth year of King Reccesvinth and held the office for nine years and about two months. He died on 23 January and was buried in the church of Saint Leocadia, at the feet of the bishop before him.

Julian lists many books. Among them is On the Virginity of Holy Mary, written, Julian says, against three unbelievers. Ildefonsus also carried on the list of famous church writers that Jerome began and Isidore of Seville continued. He wrote it, he says, so that silence would not hide the memory of the great men of his church.

A Life written later by Cixila, a bishop of Toledo under Muslim rule, adds a wonder. Leocadia, the virgin saint of the city, rose from her tomb before the king and the people. She is said to have thanked God that her Lady lived through the life of Ildefonsus.
