On Saturday, 16 July 1054, the clergy of Hagia Sophia in Constantinople were ready for Mass. Three envoys from Rome came in and laid a sentence of excommunication on the high altar. They went out, shook the dust from their feet, and cried out that God should see and judge.

Their own account names Humbert, cardinal bishop of Silva Candida, first. Pope Leo IX had sent him with Peter, archbishop of Amalfi, and Frederick, the chancellor. Cornelius Will, who edited the account, believes Humbert wrote it.

In 1053, Will says, Leo, archbishop of Ohrid, wrote against the Latins at the urging of Michael Keroularios, patriarch of Constantinople. The envoys reached the city on 24 June. There Humbert wrote a Dialogue between a Roman and a man of Constantinople, against the Greek charges.

A monk of the Studios monastery, Nicetas, had written a book on the bread of the Mass, the Sabbath and married priests. Before the emperor, the account says, Nicetas cursed his own book, and it was burned. The next day he came to the envoys and became their friend. A fierce Latin reply calls Nicetas more foolish than an ass. Will gives it to Humbert, and Wibert, Pope Leo's biographer, gives it to Frederick.

The patriarch would not meet them. Their sentence likens his party to the heretics of old. They sell the gift of God, it says, and they have cut from the creed the Spirit's going forth from the Son. It curses Michael, Leo of Ohrid and all who follow them.

The edict of Michael's synod calls the envoys men from the darkness of the West, who burst into the city like a wild boar. The pope never sent them, it says. They came on the advice of Argyrus, the Greek governor in Italy, and forged the seals on their letters. On 20 July, by Will's reckoning, the synod cursed the paper and its makers.

The envoys' account says Michael falsified the Greek version of their sentence to turn the crowd against them. Will takes their side and says the edict heaps lie on lie. Leo of Ostia, the chronicler of Monte Cassino, says the pope had died by the time they made ready to go home.

In 1059 Humbert signed Pope Nicholas II's decree on how popes were to be chosen.

Will wrote that this curse made the split between the churches complete, and that it has lasted.
