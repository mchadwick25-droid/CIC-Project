[calmly] On a Saturday in July, the clergy of Hagia Sophia in Constantinople were ready for Mass. Three envoys from Rome came in and laid a sentence of excommunication on the high altar. They went out, shook the dust from their feet, and cried out that God should see and judge.

Their own account names Humbert, cardinal bishop of Silva Candida, first. Pope Leo IX had sent him with Peter, archbishop of Amalfi, and Frederick, the chancellor. Cornelius Will, who edited it, believes Humbert wrote it. Will reads its date as 16 July 1054, and Carl Mirbt gives 13 July.

In 1053, Will says, Leo, archbishop of Ohrid, wrote against the Latins at the urging of Michael Keroularios, patriarch of Constantinople. Will dates the embassy to January 1054. Wibert, Pope Leo's biographer, says Humbert wrote a Dialogue in the city, against the Greek charges.

A monk of the Studios monastery, Nicetas, had written a book against the Latins, on unleavened bread at Mass, the Sabbath and married priests. On 24 June, the account says, Nicetas cursed his own book before the emperor, and it was burned.

The envoys' account says the patriarch avoided them. Michael wrote that they came to him but would not greet him, bow, or sit below his bishops. Their sentence likens his party to the heretics of old. They sell the gift of God, it says, and they have cut from the creed the Spirit's going forth from the Son. It curses Michael, Leo of Ohrid and their followers, unless they repent.

The edict of Michael's synod calls the envoys men from the darkness of the West, who burst into the city like a wild boar. The Greeks will not corrupt the creed, it says, for they hold that the Spirit proceeds from the Father. The pope never sent the envoys, the edict adds. They came on the advice of Argyrus, the Greek governor in Italy, and forged the pope's letters. On 20 July, by Will's reading, the synod cursed the paper and its makers.

The envoys' account says Michael falsified the Greek version of their sentence to turn the crowd against them. Will takes their side and says the edict heaps lie on lie. Will dates Pope Leo's death to 19 April, before the sentence.

[warmly] Will wrote that the sentence of 1054 made the split between the churches complete, and that it has lasted.
