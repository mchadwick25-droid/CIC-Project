[calmly] One winter, Theophanes writes, the Black Sea froze far out from its northern shores. In February the ice broke into blocks like mountains, which the wind drove into Constantinople's waters. He saw it, he says, and with about thirty boys his age he climbed onto one block in play.

This was Theophanes the Confessor, who wrote the Chronographia, a year-by-year history of the empire and the church. From that winter, its Dominican editor Jacques Goar dates his birth to about 751 or 752, or to 744 by Theophanes's own figures.

His life is told in a Life and Eulogy whose author and date are disputed. Goar names Symeon Metaphrastes, who retold saints' Lives, or, following the church annalist Baronius, Theodore the Studite.

On his wedding night, the Life says, he urged his bride to give up married life for God, and she agreed. Her father turned the emperor Leo IV against him, and Leo threatened to blind him. Within three years both men were dead. Theophanes became a monk in Sigriane and bought a farm there called Agros, the Field, where others joined him.

Later the emperor Leo the Armenian attacked the holy images and sent for Theophanes, old and too ill with kidney stones to walk. The Life, which calls Leo the wickedest of men, gives Theophanes's answer. What I refused when young, he said, I will not take when old. Light the fire, and I who cannot walk will leap into it.

Leo held him two years, then exiled him to Samothrace, where he died after twenty-three days. Goar puts the year at 818, and the Bollandists, editors of saints' Lives, at about 820.

[warmly] His preface says George, an abbot who had once served the patriarch Tarasios, wrote a chronology up to the emperor Diocletian. Dying, he left it to his friend to finish. Theophanes searched many books and added nothing of his own. Where something is missing, he asks the reader to forgive him, for love of the Lord.
