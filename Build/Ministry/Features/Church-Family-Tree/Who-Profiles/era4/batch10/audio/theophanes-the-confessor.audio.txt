[calmly] One winter, Theophanes writes, the Black Sea froze far out from its northern shores. In February the ice broke into blocks like mountains, which the wind drove into Constantinople's waters. He saw it, he says, and with about thirty boys his age he climbed onto one block in play.

This was Theophanes the Confessor, the monk and abbot who wrote the Chronographia, a year-by-year history of the empire and the church. From that winter, Jacques Goar, its Dominican editor, dates his birth to about 751 or 752. Theophanes's own figures would give 744.

His life is told in a Life and Eulogy whose author and date are open. Goar names Symeon Metaphrastes, who retold saints' Lives, or, following the church annalist Baronius, Theodore the Studite.

The Life says that on his wedding night he urged his bride to give up married life for God, and she agreed. Her angry father turned the emperor Leo IV against him, and Leo threatened to blind him. Within three years both men were dead.

He became a monk in Sigriane and bought a farm there called Agros, the Field, where others joined him. When the bishops met at Nicaea in 787, he rode there on a donkey in his goat-hair sack.

The emperor Leo the Armenian later attacked the holy images of Christ, his Mother and the saints. Then he sent for Theophanes, who was old and too ill with kidney stones to walk.

The Life gives his answer to Leo's offers. What I refused when young, he said, I will not take when old. Light the fire, and I who cannot walk will leap into it. Leo held him for two years, then exiled him to Samothrace, where he died after twenty-three days. Goar puts the year at 818, and the Bollandists, editors of saints' Lives, at about 820.

[warmly] His preface says that George, an abbot who had once served the patriarch Tarasios, wrote a chronology up to the emperor Diocletian. Dying, he left it to his friend to finish. Theophanes searched many books and added nothing of his own. Where something is missing, he asks the reader to forgive him, for love of the Lord.
