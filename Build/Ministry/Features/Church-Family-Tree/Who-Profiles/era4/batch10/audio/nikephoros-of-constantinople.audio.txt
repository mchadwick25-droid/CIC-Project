[calmly] In 813 Patriarch Nikephoros crowned the new emperor, Leo the Armenian, in the Great Church. As he set the crown on Leo's head, his Life says, he seemed to be putting his hand into thorns and thistles. He said openly that it pained him.

Nikephoros led the church of Constantinople from 806 to 815. His Life is by his disciple Ignatius the Deacon. Ignatius confesses that for a time he took communion with the iconoclasts, the party that broke the images.

Ignatius says the patriarch's father, an imperial secretary, was flogged and exiled for honoring the images. Nikephoros became an imperial secretary too. At the council of Nicaea in 787, which restored the images, he spoke for them before holding any church office.

The emperor Nikephoros I, a different man, had him chosen patriarch while still a layman. Theodore the Studite objected, says Theophanes the Confessor, a chronicler of the time. Soon a synod restored the priest Joseph, who had married the emperor Constantine VI to Theodote. The chronology in Jacques Sirmond's edition of Theodore says Nikephoros agreed, fearing the emperor, and Theodore broke communion with him until 811.

When Leo moved against the images, Ignatius writes, he told Nikephoros to debate the iconoclasts. Nikephoros answered that no council stood with them. The best emperor, he said, makes peace out of war, and you have brought war on a church at peace.

One night in March 815, Leo sent soldiers to take the sick patriarch into exile. Ignatius says he rose with a servant's help and lit two candles in the church where he often prayed all night. He lay face down in prayer, then let them carry him off.

In exile, in monasteries he had built, he wrote against the iconoclasts. Who ever heard of a body without limits? he asks in one. A body without limits would be in no place, and what is in no place is no body at all.

[warmly] He died in exile in 828. Nineteen years later, says Theophanes the priest, Patriarch Methodios opened the tomb and found the body whole. It was laid in the Church of the Holy Apostles on 13 March, the day of his exile.
