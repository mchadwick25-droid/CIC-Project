Prince Yaroslav of Kyiv gathered many priests at his church in Berestovoye. Among them, the Russian Primary Chronicle says, was a presbyter named Ilarion, "a virtuous man, learned and ascetic." He would walk to a wooded hill by the Dnieper. There he dug a little cave, two yards deep, to chant the hours and pray in secret.

This was Ilarion of Kyiv. In 1051, the Chronicle says, Yaroslav gathered the bishops and made him metropolitan, the chief bishop of Rus. Its Laurentian copy, in Old Russian, calls him a Rusin, a man of Rus. Later Antony of the Caves came home from Mount Athos and settled by Ilarion's cave. The Caves monastery grew there.

The 1844 Moscow edition of his works quotes the Chronicle's two passages and says this is all that is known. No source used here gives his birth. The Chronicle names another metropolitan, George, by 1072.

The chronicler does not say why Ilarion was not sent to Constantinople to be consecrated, the foreword notes. It holds that Yaroslav did not mean to cut the Kyiv church's tie to Constantinople. A. N. Mouravieff, a Russian official writing in 1838, blames Yaroslav's anger at the emperor, who had blinded Rus prisoners.

Ilarion left a confession of faith in his own name. He signs it as a monk and presbyter, made metropolitan in Kyiv in 1051 to be its shepherd and teacher.

The foreword argues that he also wrote the Sermon on Law and Grace. In one manuscript the same hand runs it on into the Creed and his confession. It prays for Yaroslav and his wife Irina as living, so the foreword places it before her death in 1050.

The sermon pictures the law as Hagar the slave and grace as Sarah the free woman. The law passed, it says, as moonlight fades when the sun rises. Rome praises Peter and Paul, it says, so let us praise our teacher, Vladimir, Yaroslav's father.

Near its end the sermon calls Vladimir from his tomb. Rise, honored head, it says, and shake off sleep. See your son George, Yaroslav's Christian name. See the city shining in its greatness, the churches in flower, Christianity growing.
