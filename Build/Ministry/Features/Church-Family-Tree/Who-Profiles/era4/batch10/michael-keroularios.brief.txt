Michael Psellos says he watched the patriarch being led away in disgrace, pulled about and mocked. I saw him then, Psellos writes, and wept. The patriarch did not rebuke the men who took him, he adds, or chafe at the insult.

The prisoner was Michael Keroularios, patriarch of Constantinople from 1043. In 1054 the envoys of Rome had excommunicated him. Psellos praised him in a funeral speech, but he is a partisan witness. He served the emperor who arrested Michael. Konstantinos Sathas, its editor in 1874, says Psellos was also forced to write the charge against him.

Under Emperor Michael IV, Psellos says, plotters chose Michael as emperor without telling him. He was exiled, kept apart from his brother, and made a monk. His brother could not bear the parting and died suddenly. Later, Constantine Monomachos made him patriarch.

Psellos says Michael quarreled with Rome over the Latin teaching that the Holy Spirit comes in some way from the Son.

His own letters to Peter, patriarch of Antioch, tell of the envoys. They walked into the palace with a cross and staffs, he writes, and would not greet him or bow. They handed him a sealed letter, and he found the seal tampered with. He would not debate them, since he was sure they could not be moved and the other patriarchs were not with him.

In July the envoys, led by Humbert of Silva Candida, laid a sentence on the altar of Hagia Sophia. The hostile sentence curses Michael and his followers. His synod of bishops cursed the paper and its makers.

Psellos's history says Michael once spoke too boldly to Isaac Komnenos, made emperor by a revolt in 1057. Isaac held his anger, then suddenly sent him out of the city.

Psellos says he was brought from the island of Imbros for trial in Thrace. He died before it, forgiving the emperor and his judges. Sathas says instead that he heard the charge in silence before the court, forgave them and fell dead.

The envoys, Michael told Peter, said they had come to teach us and to make us hold their teachings. They had not come to learn, they said, or to talk.
