When Leo the Armenian took the throne, Patriarch Nikephoros crowned him in the Great Church. As he set the crown on Leo's head, his Life says, he seemed to be putting his hand into thorns and thistles.

Nikephoros's Life is by his disciple Ignatius the Deacon. Ignatius confesses that he once took communion with the iconoclasts, the party that broke the images.

Ignatius says the patriarch's father, an imperial secretary, was flogged and exiled for honoring the images. At the council of Nicaea in 787, which restored the images, Nikephoros spoke for them before holding any church office.

The emperor Nikephoros I, a different man, had him chosen patriarch in 806, while still a layman. Theodore the Studite objected, says Theophanes the Confessor, a chronicler of the time. A synod then restored the steward Joseph, who had illegally married the emperor Constantine VI to Theodote. A chronology printed with Theodore's works says Nikephoros agreed, fearing the emperor, and Theodore broke communion with him until 811.

When Leo moved against the images, Ignatius writes, he told Nikephoros to debate the iconoclasts. Nikephoros answered that no council stood with them. The best emperor, he said, makes peace out of war, and you have brought war on a church at peace.

One night Leo sent soldiers to take the sick patriarch into exile. The Bollandists, editors of saints' Lives, give the year as 815, and in one note 814. Jacques Goar, Theophanes's editor, gives 816. Ignatius says the patriarch rose with a servant's help and lit two candles in the church where he often prayed all night. He lay face down in prayer, then let them carry him off.

In exile he wrote books against the iconoclasts. Who ever heard of a body without limits? he asks in one. A body without limits would be in no place, and what is in no place is no body at all.

He died in exile in 828 by the Bollandists' count, or 829 by Goar's. Nineteen years later, says Theophanes the priest, Patriarch Methodios opened the tomb and found the body whole. It was laid in the Church of the Holy Apostles on 13 March, the day of his exile.
