- **Theophanes the Confessor.** Named in the person entries for Philoxenus (the longDescription and card, among them the slave, Calandion, images and name items), Sophronius, Maximus the Confessor, Martin I, John Climacus and Pisentius; in the Melkite story's caveat and sources; in two Heraclian-era stories; and in the sources of the Stephen story. Photius's entry names "the chronicle that continues Theophanes", and the Paulicians story names "the continuator of Theophanes" in its text and "Theophanes Continuatus" in its caveat and sources. Most report what he says of others. The two that place him, "writing in the early ninth century" and "writing for Chalcedon's side", agree with the held lines: the first fits a chronicle that Baronius ends in 813 (PG108 l.3826-3830). No difference.
- **Stephen the Younger.** In Iconophile Byzantine Monasticism, `why`, `longDescription` and `voices[1]` say he was killed in 765, the first two "his Life says". The Life gives no year, only 28 November in his fifty-third year (PG100 l.97215-97217). Fabricius gives 766 (l.88570-88571) and the PG108 editors 28 November 767 (THEO l.131105-131109). Theophanes gives 20 November under Goar's margin 757 (THEO l.78706-78707), and the Short History's marker 766 (PG100 l.81476). No held line gives 765. The story "Stephen the Younger Dragged Through the Mese" (marked "primary text not yet read") differs too. Its `when` and text give 28 November 765, the text as the Life's account. The Life has him take the habit at 16 and shut himself in at 31, against "thirty years on Mount Auxentius". It names a public road and the Forum of the Ox, not the Mese (l.96963, l.97134), and a crowd on its way to the empress's feast, not "a mob from the palace faction" (l.96917-96955). Fabricius dates the Life to 808, not "c. 809". The 342 monks and the pit agree. The coin differs. In the Life he gets it from a devout man before he is brought in (PG100 l.95535-95536), where the story has him ask for it before the emperor. He asks only whether he would be punished (l.95704, l.95722); no held line has "for insulting metal or for insulting the emperor". The story's Theophanes source gives AM 6257 as 764/5. The held annal is AM 6257 (the scan's Greek heading reads 6237, PG108 l.78764), which Goar's Latin numbers 757 (l.78706); no held line gives 764/5. Its Life is Auzépy's edition (1997), which is not held.
- **Nikephoros of Constantinople.** `voices[4]` of the same entry, deposed and exiled and a writer against iconoclasm, and the edge `iconophile-byzantine-monasticism--byzantine-imperial-church-macedonian`, where Photius reads and praises his Historical Epitome, the Short History (photius_library-v1_freese1920.txt l.5383-5384, l.5401-5402). Both agree. The edge spells him "Nicephorus", which is on the card. The John Climacus card (`uncertain[1]`) also names him as "Nicephorus", one of three writers whose years for Peter of Jerusalem Philippe Labbe uses. The held notice gives the 20 years (PG88 l.41478-41479; the scan reads "Nicephorus Coust."). It says nothing of his life, and agrees.
- **Nicholas Mystikos.** In the Macedonian story "The Emperor Turned Away at the Door on Christmas Day". No held line has a palace priest crown Zoe; Nicholas says Leo crowned her himself (PG111 l.14009-14018). No held line gives a procession or an imperial door; de Boor's dating gives refusals at Christmas 906 and Epiphany 907 at the church door (DEBOOR l.6611-6616, l.9246-9248). The account before the Tomus of Union, in the lines read, says nothing of Constantine VII's legitimacy. The story cites "Letters 32 and 117"; in Mai's numbering, Letter 117 is to Leo, bishop of Sylaeum (PG111 l.25241). The census may follow an edition not held. The story says Leo VI himself had legislated against a fourth marriage. No held line has Leo forbid a fourth marriage: Baronius, in Henschen, has his law forbid a third (PG111 l.423-425), and Mansi's note says Basil voided fourth marriages and Leo forbade the third (MANSI l.93963-93965). The census's February 907 is de Boor's; he reports Krug and Muralt for 908 (DEBOOR l.5795-5802), and Mai puts the exile nine years after the election of 895, or eleven by Leunclavius (PG111 l.662-664). Its source line names the pope of Letter 32 as Anastasius III; Mai reports Baronius for Lando and Pagi for Anastasius III (PG111 l.862-865). It says the eastern patriarchs allowed a dispensation; de Boor holds that their legates came, and names Mansi among those who doubted it (DEBOOR l.8640-8656). It dates the council to 920; Mansi prints the year of the world 6428 (MANSI l.103092), and de Boor puts the agreement in 921 (DEBOOR l.4693-4694). It also has the dispensation come before the deposition. Nicholas has Rome's legates arrive before his arrest (PG111 l.14275-14277); in the Life, as de Boor reads it, they had not yet come on the day of his exile, and de Boor prefers the Life (DEBOOR l.8546-8561, l.8599-8611). The rest agrees.
- **Peter of Sicily.** In Paulicians and two links. The census cites Hamilton and Hamilton's English (1998), which is not held. Its source note lists "adoptionism" among the charges; it is not among Peter's six marks (l.1318-1517). The link `paulicians--bogomils` has him at Tephrike "around 870", as Rader and Gifford give; Gieseler dates the embassy to 868 (l.154). The other link agrees.
- **Ilarion of Kyiv.** In both Kievan Rus' Christianity entries, their link, and a story's sources. No held line gives "around 1049" or "c. 1049"; the foreword places the Sermon after 1037 and before 1050 (l.224-230), and the edition's note says about 1050 (l.4202). The Laurentian calls him a Rusin (PSRL1 l.6897), the foreword Russian by birth (l.761), and Mouravieff names him and Clement of Smolensk as the native Russian metropolitans (MOUR l.2076-2078). No held line calls the Sermon the first or earliest Rus' theology. The 1054-1240 voice spells him "Hilarion of Kyiv", and no held source dates an act of his after 1054. The same entry's voice for Anthony has him dig "the first cave"; the Chronicle has Ilarion dig the crypt that Antony came to (CROSS l.11751, l.11774-11775); the Caves Paterik has Antony first live in a cave the Varangians had dug (PAT l.1091). - **Michael Keroularios.** In The Byzantine Church after the Schism and six other places: the Macedonian entry's voices, The Greek Church and the Ecumenical Patriarchate in the Twentieth Century, the Niketas Stethatos and Humbert of Silva Candida profiles, and two links. ## Who the sources are

- **Theophanes the Confessor.** A recorder. His Chronographia is held in Greek with Jacques Goar's Latin (Patrologia Graeca 108). His life rests on a Life and Eulogy whose author and date are disputed. Goar names Metaphrastes or, by Baronius, Theodore the Studite (l.318-319); Fabricius says some give it to Theodore. Anastasius the Librarian already knew a written Life while Ignatius led the church of Constantinople. The 818, and the birth about 751 or 752 (744 by Theophanes's own figures), are Goar's reckonings. The Bollandists, in Patrologia Graeca 100, put the death about 820 (the scan reads 720; PG100 l.1641-1647).
- **Stephen the Younger.** No own work is held. His life rests on one Life, by Stephen the Deacon, written 42 years after the death; Fabricius dates it to 808. Theophanes and Nikephoros's Short History tell the death on their own. Both say he was accused of drawing people into the monastic life; the Life puts the images at the center. The brief tells both.
- **Nikephoros of Constantinople.** His Antirrhetici, Apologeticus, letter to Pope Leo III and Short History, in Greek and Latin (PG100). His life rests on Ignatius the Deacon's Life, by a disciple who confesses he once took communion with the iconoclasts, with the Bollandists' commentary; a sermon on the return of his body by Theophanes the priest; Theophanes the Confessor; and the Sirmond chronology of Theodore in PG99.
- **Nicholas Mystikos.** His 163 letters (PG111), above all Letter 32 to the pope on the fourth marriage, which Carl de Boor dates to 912; Mansi prints a shorter Latin version. The other side is the Life of Euthymius, the man who replaced him, which de Boor calls partisan and dates no earlier than 921. Its Greek is held only as garble, so the Life is told through de Boor's German. The chronicles are Theophanes Continuatus and Leo the Grammarian, which de Boor says draw on one older chronicle.
- **Peter of Sicily.** A recorder and hostile witness, with a thin own life flagged. His History of the Paulicians is held in J. C. L. Gieseler's Greek and Latin (1846), with Matthaeus Rader's preface of 1604. His own account of the embassy to Tephrike carries the life; Gieseler dates it to 868 and says that is all that is known for certain. On the teaching, the profile sets Peter's own report that the Paulicians curse Mani, and John of Odzun, an earlier hostile Armenian witness, beside Gieseler.
- **Ilarion of Kyiv.** His signed confession of faith (1051) and the Sermon on Law and Grace, in the Church Slavonic of the 1844 Moscow edition, with its Russian. The edition's unsigned foreword argues the Sermon is his. It quotes the two passages of the Russian Primary Chronicle, citing the Königsberg copy, and says they are all that is known of him. The profile reads them in Cross's English and the Laurentian copy in Old Russian. A. N. Mouravieff (in R. W. Blackmore's English) gives one view of why he was consecrated in Kyiv. Simon of Vladimir's letter, in the Caves Paterik, says Polycarp had read in a Life of Antony that Antony tonsured him. The same Paterik's founding account gives the cave passage again, with an earlier stay of Antony's in Kyiv before it (PAT l.1086-1111). Jacob Susza, a bishop in union with Rome, quotes Meletius Smotrytsky in a book of 1666. Smotrytsky says Rus would not take its metropolitan's consecration from Michael Keroularios (SUSZA1666 l.5698-5702; SUSZA1864 l.4485-4489). Michael Harasiewicz's Annales Ecclesiae Ruthenae (1862) gives 1031 for the election and 1063 for George I (l.1672, l.1749). Nothing held gives his birth or states the year of his death; Cross's report of Shakhmatov ties the 1051 entry to his death.
- **Michael Keroularios.** His two letters to Peter of Antioch and his synod's edict of July 1054, in Cornelius Will's Acta et scripta (1861). The envoys' account and sentence, the hostile side, are in the same volume. His life is told by Michael Psellos, in a funeral speech and in the Chronographia on Isaac Komnenos, in Konstantinos Sathas's edition (1874). Psellos is partisan: he served Isaac, who exiled the patriarch, and Sathas says Psellos was forced to write the charge against him. Sathas's prologue is a third telling, sharply critical of him.

## Corrections to the re-score found in drafting

- **Nicholas.** PG111 l.1108-1135, cited for "own letters", is the start of Letter 1, to the emir of Crete. Letter 32 is at l.13723-15700. More than his own voice and one hostile source is held: Theophanes Continuatus, Leo the Grammarian, Eutychius and the Tomus. The row gives the exile as 907, which is de Boor's; Krug and Muralt give 908, and Mai nine years after the election, or eleven by Leunclavius (DEBOOR l.5798-5800; PG111 l.662-664). - **Peter.** The embassy runs l.787-798, and the Bulgaria boast, the brief's opening, continues on l.835-839. - **Ilarion.** "All that is known" is at l.741-759, not l.178-190. The Sermon's file lines are l.1136-2497, with "Rise, honored head" at l.2239-2262; the confession is l.2503-2611. The re-score's numbers are the host OCR's. The editor is not named in the file; only the Library record names A. V. Gorskii, and the card says so. The re-score does not cite the Laurentian entry, Mouravieff or Simon of Vladimir.
- **Keroularios.** The speech's heading is at PSELL l.20514-20515. The heading calls it an encomium, and Sathas both an epitaph and an encomium; the card says "funeral speech". The re-score misses Psellos's Chronographia passage (l.18041-18060). The death needs the dispute: Psellos's speech has him die on the way to trial in Thrace, his history within the bounds of his exile, and Sathas before the court.

## Language and quotations

Lines held only in Greek, Latin, German, Church Slavonic, Old Russian, Russian, Armenian or Croatian are told in modern words with the source named, without quotation marks. Quotation marks are kept for held English. The one quotation in the briefs is Cross's "a virtuous man, learned and ascetic." in Ilarion's (CROSS l.11747-11748). The cards add Cross's "Metropolitan of Rus" (l.11742) and "to connect it with the death of the metropolitan Hilarion" (l.5192-5193), Blackmore's "sat till 1058" (MOUR l.16006), Gifford's "boldly conjectures" (NPNF7 l.9963) and, on Nikephoros's card, Photius's "he throws into the shade all historians who have preceded him" in Freese's English (l.5401-5402). No quotation in a brief or card crosses a scan mark. The records quote Greek, Latin and German as the scan reads them, and every quoted string was checked by script against its file.
