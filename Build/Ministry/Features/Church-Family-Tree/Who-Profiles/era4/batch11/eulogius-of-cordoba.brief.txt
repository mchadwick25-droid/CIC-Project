In the palace at Cordoba, one of the emir's counselors knew Eulogius well and pleaded with him. Fools had rushed to this death, he said, but Eulogius was wise. Let him say one word now and keep his faith afterward wherever he could. No one would look for him. Eulogius smiled and preached the Gospel, and they ordered his death.

His boyhood friend Paulus Alvarus tells this in a Life of Eulogius, a tribute to a saint. For most of the story he is the only witness. Eulogius of Cordoba was a priest of the church of St Zoilus in the 800s, when Muslim emirs ruled the city.

When Christians began to die as martyrs, Eulogius writes, nearly the whole city turned against them for fear of the emir. He began his Memorial of the Saints to record them. Critics said the martyrs worked no miracles. He answered that miracles are not given to everyone, or at every time.

Alvarus says Bishop Reccafred jailed every priest he could, Eulogius among them. A letter from his cell is dated November 851. In prison he wrote his Instruction for Martyrdom for Flora and Maria, two women held with him for their faith. Others, he says, had nearly talked them out of it. They died on 24 November.

Leocritia, a young woman whose parents were not Christians, fled to Eulogius and his sister Anulo. When her hiding place was betrayed, he was arrested with her. He told the judge that a priest must give the light of faith to all who ask.

He was beheaded on a Saturday, 11 March, Alvarus writes. Ambrosio de Morales, his editor in the 1500s, worked the year out as 859 from that Saturday. The city's Christians bought back his head and buried his body at St Zoilus.

In a letter to Alvarus that opens the Memorial, Eulogius says he wrote to leave a witness for later generations. From them he would earn either the shame of a liar or the name of praise.
