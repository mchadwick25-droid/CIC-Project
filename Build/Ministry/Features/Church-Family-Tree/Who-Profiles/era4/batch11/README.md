## River and tradition counts

**The river counts do not change.** The batch adds no majors. Release 44's major flags on the census's era 4 Who rows give Latin West & Catholicism 10, Greek East & Orthodoxy 5, Syriac East & Asia 4, Caucasus 1 and Africa 1. Which rule counts is for the map build slice (Open-Gaps, "Map Who build slice: caps, major mark, placement", 2026-11-11).

## Who the sources are

- **Stepanos of Taron.** A recorder, with a thin own life flagged. His Universal History is held in two translations: Heinrich Gelzer and August Burckhardt's German of all three books (1907), and Frédéric Macler's French of Book III (1917), an OCR scan, with Macler's introduction and notes. No Armenian text is held. His life rests on a few lines of his own (a Lent at Khladsor, the old teachers he saw in his youth, the order of the catholicos Sargis, the colophon's Armenian year 453, which Macler reckons as 1004 to 1005) and on modern editors whom Macler reports and weighs. Death and burial rest on a manuscript notice whose age Alishan does not give, Emin's reading of a letter of Gregory Magistros, and a tradition of his tomb at Mush; Brosset said no one knows when he died.
- **Eulogius of Cordoba.** His own Memorial of the Saints, Instruction for Martyrdom, Apology for the Martyrs and letters, in Patrologia Latina 115. His life rests on his friend Paulus Alvarus's Life of Eulogius, a tribute to a saint, in the same volume, with the notes of Ambrosio de Morales, his editor in the 1500s. Alvarus is the only witness for most of the life.
- **Paulus Alvarus.** His Confession, letters, poems and the Luminous Index (Indiculus luminosus), with Nicolás Antonio's notice, in Patrologia Latina 121; his Life of Eulogius and the letters between the two men in Patrologia Latina 115. His own life is thin. His ancestry, his marriage and the Index's authorship are argued by his editors.
- **Theodore Abu Qurrah.** His Arabic works in Georg Graf's German (1910), and his Greek works in Patrologia Graeca 97. Graf's introduction is the only held life, and most of it is Graf's reconstruction. Michael the Syrian's account of the debate in Armenia, Abu Ra'ita's letters and the later Syriac and Armenian notices are held only through Graf's German; Michael's Book XII is not in the Library.
- **Stephen of Mar Saba.** A recorder, with a very thin own life flagged. His eyewitness account of the raid on Mar Saba, the Passion of the Twenty Martyrs, is held in Greek with the Bollandists' Latin in the Acta Sanctorum (March, volume 3). The account never names its author. The name Stephen rests on the Bollandists' heading, which they take from a Life of Stephen the Wonderworker that is not held.
- **Ishoyahb III.** His letters, in Rubens Duval's Latin (1905), with two in Alphonse Mingana's English (1926). His life rests on Thomas of Marga's Book of Governors (840 by Budge's title; Labourt, 832 to 850; Assemani, early ninth century; Budge's English), Amr ibn Matta and Mari ibn Sulayman's lives of the patriarchs, Elias of Nisibis, Bar Hebraeus (a rival church's historian), the Chronicle of Seert, an anonymous chronicle edited by Ignazio Guidi, and Assemani.
- **al-Tabari.** An outside witness. Two volumes of his Annals in Arabic, edited at Leiden under M. J. de Goeje (1890, 1893); the held Arabic runs from late in the year 11 to the year 32 of the hijra. His life rests on F. K. Ginzel (1887) and Philip Hitti (1916). His card also cites al-Maqrizi in Étienne Quatremère's French (1811) on the copies in the caliph Aziz's library, and Michael the Syrian in J.-B. Chabot's French (1901) for Yazdegerd as the last Persian king.
- **Ibn Ishaq.** An outside witness. His life of Muhammad, held only in Ibn Hisham's reworking, in Gustav Weil's German (1864) and, for the first part, in Ferdinand Wüstenfeld's Arabic (1858). His life rests on Weil, Hitti, a line of al-Baladhuri's in Hitti's English on his grandfather Yasar, and a note by B. T. A. Evetts (1895). Axel Moberg (1924) weighs his Najran account against the Syriac.

## Language and quotations

Lines held only in Latin, Greek, German, French, Arabic or Syriac are told in modern words with the source named, without quotation marks. Quotation marks are kept for held English. The quotations in the briefs are Thomas of Marga's "having many eaglets upon his back" and Mingana's "this country has since sat in darkness" (Ishoyahb), Hitti's "We only transmit to others what has been transmitted to us." The records quote Latin, Greek, French, German and Arabic as the scan reads them, marked "(scan)" where garbled, and every quoted string was checked against its file.
