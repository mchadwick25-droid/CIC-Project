# Kind kit: an era

**Purpose.** Set the stage: give a listener the bigger picture of one era, so the traditions on the map in it make sense.

**The test, for an era.** What was happening, to the church and in the world; where; and what questions it left for the traditions in it.

## The heart

- **The essence:** what turned in this era, for the church and around it.
- **What was happening to the church:** the turns that shaped it, such as a persecution ended, a council called, a split or a mission.
- **What was happening in the world:** the empires, wars, plagues, trade and laws that pressed on Christians.
- **Where:** where Christians lived and where the faith was moving, and which regions the era's traditions belong to.
- **Setting the stage:** the questions this era left open, which the traditions on the map answered in their different ways.

## The form: a story of the age

Open on one moment that holds the era, then widen to the bigger picture, told through events and the voices of the time, in order. Hand on to the traditions without telling each one's story.

## Sources

An era is a survey of its time, so it draws on the broad historical scholarship for the events, trends and geography of the age, as well as the old sources. Documented, Widely Accepted and Dominant Modern Reconstruction claims are told plainly as the picture of the age. Where historians genuinely disagree, the era says so in a clause, or leaves it out. Inferential-Thin claims stay out. No historian is named in the narration; the era card lists the works under Modern works, within the rights rules. No speculation, and no verdict on the age as golden or declining.

## Length

Aim at about three minutes. Mark rules on each era's length with its heart.

## Traps

A list of dates; a textbook summary; retelling the traditions' own stories; judging the age. The era's title on the map is our label, not a claim the story makes.
