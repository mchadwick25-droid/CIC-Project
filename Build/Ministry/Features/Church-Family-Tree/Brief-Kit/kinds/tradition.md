# Kind kit: a tradition

**Purpose.** Let a listener meet one community of Christians in its own time and place: who they were and how they lived their faith, so they come away having met something they had not met before. Every tradition on the tree, built world or not, uses this kit.

**The test, for a tradition.** Who these people were, what happened to them, what their faith was about, and why the connection matters.

## The heart

- **The essence:** who they were, when and where, and the one or two things everything else turned on.
- **Who they were:** the community, by a name a narrator can say aloud.
- **When and where:** their time and place, brought in a clause at a time.
- **What they faced:** what brought them into being, what pressed on them, and what ended or changed them.
- **What they believed:** what they held and what they fought over, told as they held it, in their own words where the record gives them.
- **How they practiced:** worship, prayer, daily life, and how they formed their people.
- **What held them together:** the one or two things everything else turned on. For a built world, its gravities, in plain words.
- **What came of them:** who carries it on, or what was handed down.

## The form: a story

The heart is what the story carries, never a list of sections. Open on one moment from the record: a gathering, a trial, a hymn sung, a letter sent. Follow the community through time, through real people and what happened to them. What they faced is the story's tension. What they believed and how they practiced come through what people did and said. End on the community itself.

## Sources

A built world brings its own records: its forces, gravities, terms, stories and honest limits, each traced to its source lines. Every other tradition draws on the census entry and the Library, at the confidence they bear. Either way, the heart comes to Mark on the hearts page.

## Length

Aim at about three minutes (about 450 words), up to about four and a half (675) where the content calls for it.

## Traps

An encyclopedia entry; a founder's biography; a list of beliefs with no people in it; only the leaders and none of the ordinary believers; settling what the record leaves open.
