# The standard

A story that lets an interested non-specialist meet one person, council or event from the church's history. It is heard once, by adults, many of them listening in a second language. The outline sets its length: about two minutes, up to three for a major figure or a story that needs the room, shorter when the record is thin.

**The test.** After one hearing, the listener can explain who or what this was, what happened, and why the connection matters. Meet it without making the story simpler.

## Craft

- A well-sourced story, told by an adult documentary narrator who knows the record well, in the register of a BBC or National Geographic documentary. Never quotations linked by commentary, never a report about the record, and never talking down.
- Open on something that draws the listener in. Within the first few sentences, give the time, the place and the tradition in the outline's spoken words.
- Tell what was at stake, not only what happened: what an act meant in its own time, what it risked and what it changed.
- Link ideas the way an adult narrator does, with cause, contrast and time. Let a longer sentence carry a chain of events and a short one mark a turn. No sentence past about 30 words, and no run of short sentences that each tell one act.
- Give the outline's connection enough room to become understandable, carried through what people did: what they left the church and what they defended. End there, on an event or in the record's own words. No moral, no summing up.

## Rigor

- Tell only what the outline holds, transitions included: a "so" or "because" that implies motive or cause must be in the sources. Add no feeling or detail for effect. Honour the outline's Do not say list.
- Use only the outline's quotes, word for word, and only where their particular words do something the narrator's cannot. The narrator carries the story.
- Name a witness only when the listener needs to know who is speaking: praise or blame, a wonder, a much later source, a hostile view, or a link on the tree. A wonder stays in its witness's voice. A hostile view is told openly and credited to its source; the narrator never carries it forward.
- Say plainly what is uncertain. Do not present it as settled, and do not bury it in hedging.

## Accessibility

- Plain modern words: simplify the language, never the thought. Use the precise words a grown listener knows without stopping to define them, and work any puzzling word or custom into the sentence as part of what happens.
- The audio tells the story; the card carries the references, fuller qualifications and debates.
- Aim for reading grade 8 to 9. Grade 10 and reading ease 60 are hard limits; below them, a score is a warning for the listening review, never a goal. Never simplify to move it.
