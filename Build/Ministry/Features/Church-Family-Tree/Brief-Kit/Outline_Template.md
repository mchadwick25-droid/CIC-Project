# The outline

The researcher fills this in for one person, from the source sheet. One page, no prose. Every line points to its place in a held source text (file and line). The writer gets this outline, `Shapes.md` and `Standard.md`, and nothing else.

- **Length:** 2 minutes (about 300 words), or a stated reason to stretch toward 3 (up to about 450 words): a major figure, or a story that needs the room. As short as 1 minute when the record is thin. Never fill to reach a length.
- **Setting, in words to say aloud:** the century or year, the place and the tradition, in plain spoken words, with the lines that support them.
- **Thread:** one sentence on what this person left the church.
- **Shape:** one of the shapes in `Shapes.md`.
- **Openings:** one or two things from the record that could draw a listener in: a moment, an image, their own words, someone else's words about them, or a question the record raises.
- **Story beats:** 5 to 7 events in order, in plain paraphrase. Each beat gives the reason for the act, the meaning of any word or custom that would puzzle a newcomer, its source, and its confidence level.
- **What came of them:** ranked heaviest first, where weight is what later witnesses carried on and used, not what is easiest to tell. What they defended is marked. This part carries about a third of the story.
- **Quotes, 2 or 3 at most:** each with its modern rendering, its source, and the job it does in the story.
- **Connection to carry into the story:** what specifically passed from this person or event to another person, community, text, practice or decision. Give the supporting passage, and state what the listener needs to understand about that relationship. Naming a later person or event is not enough: say what the writer must explain where a listener would otherwise have to infer it. It should come through source-supported action, never a forced "this matters because" sentence or an appended moral.
- **Links:** each tie to another person on the tree, labelled by kind: met, heard him teach, wrote to, kept his letters, quoted his book, wrote his life, kept his feast.
- **Who tells it:** for each main source, who wrote it and whether in the person's own time or later; where it matters, one line the writer can say aloud.
- **Do not say:** the wordings a writer could easily get wrong from this outline, each with the right wording beside it. These include a wonder that must stay in its witness's voice, a qualifier that must not be dropped ("partly", "forty or more"), an outcome or motive the record does not give, and a detail a writer might add for colour. When a later check or review finds a writer's error that the outline left room for, it is added here.
- **Leave out:** what the record holds that this story should not carry, and why: doubtful claims, side stories.

Confidence uses the project's five levels: Documented, Widely Accepted, Dominant Modern Reconstruction, Contested, Inferential-Thin.
