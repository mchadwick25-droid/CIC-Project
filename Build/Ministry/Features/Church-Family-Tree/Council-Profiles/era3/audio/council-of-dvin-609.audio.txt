[calmly] Abraham, the catholicos or head of the Armenian church, did all he could to win the Iberians to his teaching. When he failed, he drew the spiritual sword and cursed them, with the assent of all the Armenians. So Stephen of Taron, an Armenian historian writing around the year 1000, tells it.

Malachia Ormanian, a former Armenian patriarch of Constantinople, calls these Iberians the Georgians. Their own church head, Kyrion, had been made catholicos by Movses, who led the Armenian church before Abraham. Shortly before Movses died, Stephen says, Kyrion came to doubt the orthodox faith and accepted the Council of Chalcedon. Ormanian says he did it to win the emperor's favour.

No record of the council is held in the Library, and no letter between the two church heads. Our witnesses are Armenian writers and modern historians. The Georgian chronicle, in Marie-Félicité Brosset's French, does not name Kyrion. His side reaches us only through his foes.

James Issaverdens, a priest of the Armenian academy in Venice, wrote in 1875 that Abraham sent Kyrion three letters. He gives Kyrion's reply: "I am astonished that you are at variance with all other churches of Christ." Issaverdens names no source for it.

Issaverdens says Abraham then called a synod of bishops at Dvin, the chief city of Persian Armenia. They cursed Kyrion "and all those who accepted it," meaning Chalcedon. No held source names the bishops or counts them.

The year is disputed. Ormanian has Abraham elected on 30 April 607 and the council at Dvin in 609. Brosset, in a note of 1876, puts it in 596.

Ormanian says the Georgian church, with Kyrion at its head, "became part of the Greek Church." Sebeos, an Armenian bishop of the 600s, tells of a debate before the Persian king. In it the catholicos of Georgia and Albania took Chalcedon's side. Issaverdens adds that Kyrion complained to the emperor Maurice, who called bishops of both sides to Constantinople. No other held source tells of that meeting.

[warmly] Brosset, the French historian of Georgia, wrote in 1849 that the Georgians parted from the Armenians in religion for ever.
