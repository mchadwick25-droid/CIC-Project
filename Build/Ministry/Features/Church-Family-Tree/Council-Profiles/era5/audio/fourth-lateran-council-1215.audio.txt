[calmly] When the bishops began to leave for Rome and the Lateran council, Dominic went with Fulk, bishop of Toulouse. Dominic had worked almost alone for nearly ten years. Together they meant to ask Pope Innocent III to confirm an order of Preachers. Jordan of Saxony, who led the order after Dominic, tells it so.

No account from inside the sessions is among our sources. Its canons survive in extracts printed by two modern editors, Carl Mirbt and Heinrich Denzinger. Mirbt dates the council from 11 to 30 November 1215. Hethum of Korykos, an Armenian chronicler, counts 422 bishops and 72 archbishops. The Maronites kept Latin customs as a sign of obedience, Jacques de Vitry writes, and so their patriarch came.

Innocent held the council, Hethum says, to tear Jerusalem from the hands of unbelievers. The canons reached much further.

The first canon set out the faith. There is one universal church, outside which no one at all is saved. In the sacrament of the altar, Christ's body and blood are truly present under the forms of bread and wine, the bread changed in substance into the body. Another canon bound every believer past the age of discretion to confess at least once a year to their own priest, and to receive communion at Easter. Rulers were to swear to drive out heretics, and preachers without leave from pope or bishop were excommunicated.

The fourth canon rebuked the Greeks. They washed altars where Latin priests had said Mass, it said, and baptized again those the Latins had baptized. Nearly six centuries later, the Greek canonist Nikodemos of Mount Athos called this council a local synod.

The second canon condemned a tract by Joachim of Fiore, who had called the theologian Peter Lombard a heretic and a madman. In another work, Joachim warns against picturing the three persons as branches on one root, which brings in a fourth. The council spared his monastery, because Joachim had submitted all his writings to Rome in a letter signed with his own hand.

After the council, Innocent told Dominic to choose a rule already approved, and the brothers took Augustine's.

[warmly] Innocent died at Perugia the next summer. Jacques de Vitry found his body there, stripped of its robes in the night and left almost naked in the church. He saw, he wrote, how short and empty is the glory of this world.
