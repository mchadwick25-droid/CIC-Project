[calmly] In October 451 the bishops met in the church of the martyr Euphemia at Chalcedon, near Constantinople. Paschasinus, a legate of Leo of Rome, stood up first. Leo had ordered, he said, that Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria, must not sit among them: "let him be expelled or else we leave." Dioscorus sat down in the middle, as a man on trial.

In 449 Dioscorus had led a council at Ephesus. It restored Eutyches, a monk of Constantinople who spoke of one nature in Christ after the union. It also deposed Flavian, bishop of Constantinople, who had condemned him. When the emperor Theodosius II died in 450, his successor Marcian called a new council. Charles Joseph Hefele, the historian of the councils, says the empress Pulcheria acted with him.

The council's own minutes survive. The losing side tells its story in a later Syriac chronicle, known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene. It was built on a Greek history that ran to 491. It counts 567 bishops. The council's letter to Leo says 520.

The bishops reread the acts of 449 and cleared Flavian. Leo's letter to Flavian, known as the Tome, was read. Of Christ's two natures it says, "the one of these shines out in miracles, the other succumbs to injuries." The bishops cried, "Leo and Cyril taught the same thing." Dioscorus, called three times, did not come. The council deposed him on 13 October.

The bishops wanted no new statement of faith, but the emperor's officials pressed for Leo's wording. "Which would you follow, the most holy Leo or Dioscorus?" they asked. The definition they signed says Christ is one person, known "in two natures," without mixture and without division.

Later the bishops gave Constantinople rank "next after" Rome. Leo's legates protested, and Leo wrote that he would "dis-annul" it.

Alexandria would not accept Dioscorus's fall. Evagrius of Epiphania, a historian on Chalcedon's side, quotes Priscus, who was in the city. Priscus says a crowd there burned soldiers alive in the old temple of Serapis. Zachariah's chronicle says the council "tore into ten thousand rents the perfect Robe of Christ."

[warmly] Malachia Ormanian, a former Armenian patriarch of Constantinople, says a synod at Dvin rejected Chalcedon's acts in 506. The Greek and Latin churches, he says, came to count it the fourth council of the whole church. His own church "would have nothing to do with" it.
