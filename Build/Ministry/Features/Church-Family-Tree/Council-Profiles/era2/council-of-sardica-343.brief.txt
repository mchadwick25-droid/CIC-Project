At Sardica, the accusers of Athanasius of Alexandria came to the church where Ossius of Cordoba was staying. Ossius later told the emperor Constantius his offer. If they would not speak before the whole council, they could speak "before me alone." If Athanasius was guilty, the bishops would reject him. If he was innocent and they still shunned him, Ossius would take him home to Spain. Athanasius agreed. They refused.

Bishops of the East had deposed him. Julius, bishop of Rome, had received him into communion, as he had Marcellus of Ancyra. Constans, emperor of the West, pressed his brother Constantius, and the two called a council at Sardica.

Socrates of Constantinople and Sozomen date it by a consulship that their editors take as 347. Charles Joseph Hefele, the historian of the councils, works from Athanasius's Easter letters and puts its start late in 343.

Most of the record comes from one side. Athanasius kept the Western letters, and Ossius's, in his own books. The Eastern bishops' letter survives in Latin, in papers gathered by Hilary of Poitiers, who held to Nicaea.

Athanasius says about 170 bishops came. The Eastern letter speaks of eighty from the East, and Hefele puts the West at about ninety-five.

The Eastern bishops would not sit with men they had already condemned. Their letter says they found the two men sharing the sacrament with Ossius. It says Marcellus would "set bounds to the perpetual, eternal, and timeless kingdom of our Lord Christ." The West would not shut them out, so the Easterners left. Socrates says they met on their own at Philippopolis in Thrace.

Each side then condemned the other. The Westerners cleared both men. They deposed their accusers, among them Valens of Mursa and Ursacius of Singidunum. They kept the creed of Nicaea. Under Ossius they also made rules for appeals. A bishop who lost his case could write to Julius, to "honour the memory of Peter the Apostle." The Easterners deposed Julius and Ossius, Sozomen says.

Socrates says East and West shared communion only up to a mountain called Soucis, "which divides the Illyrians from the Thracians." Constans threatened his brother with war, and Athanasius went home in 346, by the index to his Easter letters. Later, Pope Zosimus quoted a Sardica rule to the bishops of Africa as a rule of Nicaea. They could not find it in their copies.
