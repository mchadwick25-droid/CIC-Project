## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

1. **Why they met.** Good. Palladius's request to Gratian for a general council and Ambrose's reduction of it to an Italian synod (npnf210 1385-1393; Palladius's own complaint that the Easterners were ordered to come and then were not, Kauffmann 4210, 5124-5126; the Gesta on the emperor's letter, Mansi 3 59848-59857). The legal background in CTh 16.5.5 and 16.1.2-3.
2. **Who came and who was missing.** Good. The roll of some thirty-two bishops of Italy, Gaul, Illyricum and Africa in the Gesta (Mansi 3 59656-59679 and the subscriptions, not read in full); Valerian of Aquileia presiding in name, Ambrose in fact (59772 on); Palladius and Secundianus present; the Eastern bishops absent, which is Palladius's whole case (Kauffmann 4210).
3. **What was argued.** Strong. The reading of Arius's letter and the demand that Palladius condemn it clause by clause (Mansi 3 59772-59857; Kauffmann 1963-1998 summarises the sequence); Palladius's refusal to answer a court he did not recognise and his appeal to the Easterners (59827, 59848; Kauffmann 1998-2028).
4. **What was decided, quoted from the source.** Strong. The sentence on Palladius and Secundianus (Mansi 3 60958 region; the exact sentence line not checked); the synodal letters to the emperors asking that the two be removed and the Danubian churches given to others (61228; 61343-61350).
5. **Who disagreed and why, in their own terms.** Strong: see §4.
6. **Legacy.** Good. Theodosius's promise and withdrawal of an Eastern council for Palladius, the Constantinople meeting of 383 to which Wulfila was summoned (Kauffmann 3583-3702; Auxentius 15283); Valentinian II's Homoian law of 386 and Ambrose's Letter XXI against Auxentius of Milan (CTh 6889; npnf210 41833); the Danubian Homoian church's long memory in Maximinus (Kauffmann 3232-3273).
7. **Stories from inside the sources.** Strong. The Gesta are a verbatim exchange: Ambrose pressing "Qui sunt consortes vestri?" and Palladius answering "Orientales episcopi" (Mansi 3 59826-59827); Palladius's protest that the emperor had summoned the Easterners and Ambrose had prevented them (Kauffmann 4210, 5124-5126); the demand that the stenographers record every word so that no one could later deny his own profession (Mansi 3 59760-59775). A rare chance to tell a council as a transcript.
8. **How each tradition remembers it.** Thin outside the Latin West: see §6.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

- **Palladius of Ratiaria** (Homoian bishop, Dacia Ripensis): his words as the Gesta record them (Mansi 3 59827, 59848 and the exchanges around them), that is, as Ambrose's stenographers and editor transmitted them (Kauffmann 2232 calls the protocol "das von Ambrosius redigirte"); and his own written reply, surviving in the Arian scholia that Maximinus compiled from it (Kauffmann, Latin text 3982-5610; German analysis 2420-2427, 3210, 3263-3270). The second is the dissent in its own transmission, fragmentary, in a single manuscript.
- **Secundianus:** only his answers in the Gesta (Kauffmann 2792-2793 quotes them from the acts: "illud dico quod Christus dixit", "veritatis me servum profiteor").
- **Maximinus** (Homoian bishop, c. 383 and after): the compiler's own framing, including his charge that Ambrose's record was partisan (Kauffmann 2406, 2010-2028).
- **Auxentius of Durostorum:** the Gothic Homoian aftermath (15283 on).
- **Missing.** Palladius's reply survives only in Maximinus's excerpts; no complete Homoian account of the day exists. The Eastern (Homoian) bishops whom Palladius invoked left nothing held.

## 5. Legends

No well-known legend attaches to Aquileia. Contested, not legendary:

- **Whether the Gesta are a fair record.** Maximinus charged that Ambrose edited them (Kauffmann 2010-2028, 2232, 2406). Tag Contested; present both the acts and Palladius's complaint.
- **Whether Gratian had promised a general council.** Palladius said so (Kauffmann 4210); Ambrose's party said the emperor "ordered none to come who were not willing" (Mansi 3 59848-59857, sense not checked to the sentence). Contested in the sources.

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Latin, Milanese and Italian:** the Gesta and Ambrose's letters (Mansi 3); Ambrose's De fide (npnf210 20228) as the doctrinal ground; Paulinus's Life of Ambrose is silent on the council (checked).
- **Latin, Danubian Homoian and Gothic:** Palladius, Maximinus and Auxentius (Kauffmann). The only non-Nicene memory of a Western council held in its own manuscript transmission anywhere in the Library.
- **Latin, Aquileian:** Rufinus is silent (27937 names the city for Gratian's death only); Jerome's Chronicle ends in 378 and so cannot carry it.
- **Greek/Byzantine:** silence, documented (npnf202 15259; Theodoret searched).
- **Syriac Orthodox:** none located. Michael the Syrian tome I names Ambrose only for the Theodosius episodes (60557-60957); Bar Hebraeus vol. I has Gratian recalling the exiled bishops (7515-7516) and no council (searched 'Aquilei', 'Pallad').
- **Church of the East:** none located. The Chronicle of Seert's chapters on Valentinian and Gratian and on Theodosius (12748, 12790) were searched for Aquilée and Palladius without result.
- **Coptic, Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopian:** none located (Severus al-Muqaffa, John of Nikiu, PO 16, Moses of Khoren, the Ethiopian Synaxarium vols. I-II searched).
