## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

1. **Why they met.** Strong. Tyre: the Melitian charges (Ischyras's chalice, Arsenius) and Constantine's summons (Apol. c. Ar. 22508-22679, 25637; Socrates 5052; Sozomen 30273; Theodoret 8089; Mansi 2 123239). Rome: Athanasius's and Paul's appeal and Julius's letter (Apol. 22880; Socrates 6287; Mari 15466 for the Eastern memory). Sardica: the two emperors' summons and the Eastern bishops' refusal to sit with Athanasius (Apol. 23998; Theodoret 8621; the Eastern encyclical CSEL 65 10432).
2. **Who came and who was missing.** Good. Hosius presiding (HA 38256-38319; Sozomen 31511); the Western subscriptions (Mansi 3 1689-1966; CSEL 65 series B table 1159-1208); the Eastern bishops who withdrew to Philippopolis, about sixty, with Euticius of Philippopolis among them (Mansi 3 14471-14543). Tyre's membership by name only in part (Apol. c. Ar., not checked to the line).
3. **What was argued.** Strong on both sides. Western: Julius's letter (22880) and the Sardica letters (23687-23998). Eastern: the Philippopolis encyclical (CSEL 65 10432; PL 10 Fragmentum III 53435). Western dissent from the Western line: Lucifer, "Quia absentem nemo debet iudicare" (5463-9342), written later about Milan 355 but arguing the Tyre principle.
4. **What was decided, quoted from the source.** Strong. The Sardica canons in Greek, Latin and English (Lauchert 3865-4365; Mansi 3 1689; npnf214 30409); the Western letters (23687-23998); the Eastern deposition of Julius, Hosius and others in the encyclical (CSEL 65 10432 on). Tyre's deposition of Athanasius is known from its opponents and from Constantine's letter (25637, 26047).
5. **Who disagreed and why, in their own terms.** Strong: see §4.
6. **Legacy.** Good. The Sardica appeal canons cited as Nicene at Carthage 419 (Bruns 6481-6513); the dispute whether Sardica counts as ecumenical (npnf214 930-932; Hefele 10874); Ammianus's pagan verdict on Athanasius (4181-4207); the Liberius lapse ten years later as the sequel the sources themselves draw (HA 38917-38961; CSEL 65 13390-14423).
7. **Stories from inside the sources.** Strong. Arsenius produced alive before the judges, "hic est Arsenius" (Rufinus 28120; Athanasius 22341-22355; Socrates 5082; Sozomen 29752; Theodoret 8164-8208); the Ischyras chalice (22508-22679); Athanasius's flight from Tyre to Constantinople and Constantine's letter to the council (25637; 26047); the Eastern bishops' refusal to enter Sardica and their letter from Philippopolis (CSEL 65 10432); Julius's complaint that the Easterners "wrote to me what they ought not" (22880, not checked to the sentence).
8. **How each tradition remembers it.** Good: see §6.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

- **The Eastern bishops of Sardica/Philippopolis (343):** their encyclical, surviving only in Hilary's Collectanea (CSEL 65 10432 on; Maurist text PL 10 Fragmentum III 53435; Mansi 3 prints it from Hilary, 5369, 8192-8236). Their own words, inside a hostile Nicene compilation; the dossier's framing notes are Hilary's.
- **The Melitians:** Epiphanius, Panarion 68 (43599-43666), a hostile outsider's account that is nonetheless the fullest; Ischyras's and the Melitians' own letters survive only as Athanasius quotes them (22508-22679). Opponents' quotation.
- **The Eusebian party at Tyre and Antioch:** their letters to Julius are known only through Julius's reply (22880) and through Sozomen and Socrates. No Eusebian document of 335-341 survives in its own transmission in any held file.
- **Valens and Ursacius:** their recantation to Julius (Mansi 3 table: "Epistola pacifica Ursacii et Valentis ad Athanasium", "libellus poenitentiae ad Julium", 388-402; the texts themselves not checked to the line) and later career in Hilary's fragments.
- **A pagan outsider:** Ammianus XV.7 (4181-4207).
- **Missing.** The acts of Tyre; any Melitian text in its own transmission; Eusebius of Nicomedia's own words after 325.

## 5. Legends

- **The severed hand of Arsenius.** What it claims: Athanasius murdered the Melitian bishop Arsenius and used his hand for magic; the hand was shown in a box; Arsenius was then produced alive. This is not a later legend. It is the charge as the earliest sources record it: Athanasius's own defence (22337-22440), Rufinus (27947-28125), Socrates (5082), Sozomen (29752), Theodoret (8164-8208). The later memory elaborates it: Michael the Syrian has the accusers themselves cut the hand from a corpse (57275); Bar Hebraeus likewise ("abscissa cadaveris dextera manu", 5794-5801). A note should say: contemporary charge, recorded by the accused and by the fifth-century historians; the corpse detail first appears in the held files in the twelfth-century Syriac chronicles, and we do not know where it began.
- **"Constantine's handwriting."** The Coptic History of the Patriarchs has Athanasius refuse to absolve Arius because the excommunication was "in the handwriting of Constantine ... and the members of the holy council" (`severus-al-muqaffa_history-patriarchs-coptic-church-v1_evetts1904.txt` 29244-29251, 29360). No fourth-century source held says this; the earliest text we hold is this tenth-century Coptic compilation. Say "no source from the time records it".
- **Contested, not legendary:** the date of Sardica (343 against the 347 of the older reckoning: Hefele 5582; npnf202 note 1500); whether Sardica was ecumenical (npnf214 930-932; Hefele 10874); the "Sardican creed" (Hefele 451 "pretended creed"; Theodoret 8621).

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Greek/Byzantine:** Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret (§1), fifth century, Nicene; Epiphanius (374-377) on the Melitians and Arius.
- **Latin:** Rufinus (402-403) in Latin; Hilary's dossier (356-360s); Lucifer (355-361); Augustine not checked; Ammianus (c. 390), pagan; Jerome's Chronicle (vendored this pass) has Athanasius entries not read in this pass.
- **Syriac Orthodox:** Michael the Syrian, twelfth century (Tyre 57275-57284; Sardica 58048-58056); Bar Hebraeus, thirteenth century (5794-5801; 6458-6499); Zachariah Rhetor's sixth-century Miaphysite text anathematises any doctrine "defined elsewhere, whether in Rimini, or in Sardica, or in Chalcedon" (`zachariah-rhetor_chronicle_hamiltonbrooks1899.txt` 5805): Sardica rejected as a non-ecumenical council.
- **Church of the East:** the Chronicle of Seert (tenth century; 12800: the Antioch council that exiled Athanasius and Paul, named while dating the "concile des 150"); Mari ibn Sulayman (twelfth century; 15466: Athanasius and Paul appeal at Rome to the emperor's brother, who writes to Constantius; Constantius orders Athanasius killed, Athanasius flees and writes a defence of his flight). Elias of Nisibis (vendored by worker C) names Athanasius only in a list (223).
- **Coptic:** the History of the Patriarchs, Athanasius chapter (28113-28136: "endured many trials, and was sent into exile", no council named; 29244-29251 the handwriting story); John of Nikiu (`john-nikiu_chronicle_charles1916.txt`, chapter summaries 1101-1111: "the tribulations which the holy Athanasius ... had to endure at the hands of the Arians. And the exile of Liberius"; text 4722-4776); the Copto-Arabic Synaxarium, 7 Bachons (PO 16 18317-18421).
- **Armenian:** none located. Moses of Khoren (Italian, held) has Nicaea only; Langlois's Collection des historiens de l'Arménie tome II (1869, checked in scratch, not vendored) has no Tyre or Sardica; the Armenian Synaxarion for Tré (PO 16) was not read for this.
- **Ethiopian:** the Synaxarium vols. I-II (Budge 1928, vendored by another worker) have no entry for these events; Athanasius's day (7 Genbot) falls in vol. III, not located on archive.org.
- **Georgian:** none located.
