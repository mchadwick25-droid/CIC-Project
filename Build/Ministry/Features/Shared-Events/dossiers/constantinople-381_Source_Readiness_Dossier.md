## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

**1. Why they met.** Strong. Theodosius's law of 380 (CTh 16.1.2, latinlibrary 6883) and his expulsion of Demophilus (Socrates V.7, npnf202 14976; Sozomen VII.5, 40630) set the scene. The Antiochene schism is documented from both ends: Basil's letters (npnf208 27824 on) and Sozomen's account of the Meletius-Paulinus oath (40529). Gregory's own account of why he came and what he found is in Oration 42 and in the poem on his life (PG 37, 85705; English of the oration in npnf207 40106). The synodical letter of 382 states the council's own purpose (npnf203 15898).

**2. Who came and who was missing.** Good. Socrates V.8 lists the leading bishops and the Macedonian leaders (npnf202 15063-15095); Theodoret V.8 adds the Egyptians' late arrival (15769 on). The Pedalion's prologue states that no Westerner and no legate of Damasus was present (16923-16935). CTh 16.1.3 gives eleven names as the imperial standard of communion (Mommsen 83672-83676). A subscription list in a legible form is not held: Mansi 3 prints the council but the Greek column is garbled (29706 on). Hefele Sec. 95 (20847) discusses the membership from the documents.

**3. What was argued.** Partial. The theological case against the Pneumatomachians is carried by the winners: Basil's De Spiritu Sancto (npnf208 8580), Gregory of Nyssa's treatise against the Macedonians (npnf205 27918), Gregory of Nazianzus's Theological Orations (PG 36; English in npnf207). The quarrel over Maximus the Cynic and over Antioch is told by Gregory himself (Or. 42; PG 37 c. 90264) and by Theodoret V.8 (15814-15880) and Sozomen VII.9 (40905). The debates on the floor are not recorded anywhere; the poem on his life is the nearest thing to minutes, and it is one angry man's verse.

**4. What was decided, quoted from the source.** Strong. The creed in English (npnf214 13342), in Latin with garbled Greek (Mansi 33218), and in the Pedalion's Greek commentary context (16996 on); the seven canons in English (npnf214 13957-14684 and beyond) and in Greek (Pedalion 17002 on); the synodical letter of 382 in English (npnf203 15898-16065). The caution for the story: the creed is not quoted by any contemporary as this council's work. npnf214's excursus states the dispute (13305) and the Chalcedonian acts of 451 are the earliest held text that recites it as the faith of the 150 (20250). This is a `Dominant Modern Reconstruction` claim about authorship, not a `Documented` one.

**5. Who disagreed and why, in each side's own terms.** Thin for most parties; see section 4 below.

**6. The legacy.** Good. Canon 3 and Rome: Leo's Letter CVI (npnf212 9285), Hefele Sec. 100 (22664); canon 28 of Chalcedon is in npnf214. The creed's later life: Chalcedon's recitation (npnf214 20250) and the Filioque excursus (13522). The 383 synod of the sects (Socrates V.10, 15245; Sozomen VII.12, 41057) shows the settlement being enforced. The Pedalion (16923 on) shows how Greek canon law reads the council.

**7. Stories from inside the event's own sources.** Good. Gregory's farewell, in his own words (npnf207 40106 on; Greek PG 36 38916). Meletius's death during the council and Gregory of Nyssa's funeral oration (npnf205 45711; Socrates V.9, 15196; Sozomen VII.10, 40970). The attempt to ordain Maximus the Cynic at night in Constantinople (Theodoret V.8, 15814-15880; Sozomen VII.9, 40905; Gregory's own version, PG 37 c. 90264). Nectarius chosen while still unbaptized (Socrates V.8, 15128; Sozomen VII.8, 40843). The Egyptian bishops' late arrival and their quarrel with Gregory (Theodoret V.8). All of these are told by named sources of the period; none needs a legend note.

**8. How each tradition remembers it.** See section 6 below.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

- **Pneumatomachians (Macedonians).** No text of their own survives in the Library, and none is known to this worker in a public-domain edition. Their position is recoverable only from opponents: Basil's De Spiritu Sancto and his letters on Eustathius of Sebaste (npnf208 8580, 39055, and the faith Eustathius once subscribed, 31449); Gregory of Nyssa against the Macedonians (npnf205 27918); Socrates V.4 and V.8 on their withdrawal (npnf202 14820, 15095); the npnf214 excursus (14016). A story must say that their words come through their adversaries.
- **Eunomians (Anomoeans).** The one party with its own voice held: Eunomius's Apology (eunomius_first-apology_whiston1711.txt), written before the council. His confession presented to Theodosius in 383 is not held; neither Whiston volume on archive.org carries it (section 8). Philostorgius gives the Eunomian memory of Theodosius's reign but his epitome skips the council (368-388).
- **Apollinarians.** Now held in their own words: Lietzmann's collection (8381, 8983, 12118). Gregory's Letters 101-102 (npnf207 44566; Greek PG 37 13148) and Damasus's letter (npnf203 16066) give the opposing reading.
- **Homoians (Demophilus's church).** No text of their own. Their expulsion is told by Socrates V.7 (14976) and Sozomen VII.5 (40630); Philostorgius (350-356) is an Anomoean, not a Homoian, witness.
- **Maximus the Cynic and the Alexandrian party.** No text of Maximus survives. The Egyptian case is reported by Theodoret V.8 and by Gregory, who was its target (PG 37 c. 90264). Damasus's letters to Acholius about Maximus were not located by grep in the held Mansi 3 (the Damasus letters at 42149-44739 are other letters); their Latin is in PL 13, not located this pass.
- **The West.** Ambrose's Epistles 13-14 to Theodosius are described but not included in npnf210 (1385-1407). Rome's refusal of canon 3 is in Leo's own words (npnf212 9285).
- **Gregory of Nazianzus** is himself a dissenter from the council's course. His side is the best documented of all: Oration 42, the poem on his life, the poem on the bishops.

## 5. Legends

- **"Gregory resigned to keep the peace."** Not a legend: it is his own account, in Oration 42 (npnf207 40106-41100) and the poem on his life (PG 37 85705 on). The note for a story is that this is the loser's self-presentation; Socrates V.7 (14976) and Sozomen VII.7 (40769) give the canonical objection (his translation from Sasima) as the stated reason.
- **"The 150 fathers wrote the Nicene-Constantinopolitan creed."** Contemporary silence checked: the synodical letter of 382 (npnf203 15898-16065) speaks of a "tome" and of the Nicene faith but quotes no new creed; Socrates V.8, Sozomen VII.7-9 and Theodoret V.8 (lines above) report canons and elections and no creed text; Gregory's Oration 42 and the poem on his life do not mention composing one. Earliest held text that attributes the creed to the 150: the Acts of Chalcedon, 451 (npnf214 20250, with the editorial heading at 13342). The npnf214 excursus (13305) and the npnf205 note on Epiphanius's Ancoratus of 374 (28727) state the scholarly problem. Tag: `Contested`.
- **"Gregory of Nyssa composed the creed."** No held text carries it. The first appearance is not located in a citable public-domain text we hold.
- **"Damasus confirmed the council."** The Pedalion asserts later Western agreement (16923-16935). The contemporary texts show the opposite for canon 3 (Leo, npnf212 9285) and show Rome's own synod of 382 going its own way (Theodoret V.10-11, npnf203 16066, 16132; Hefele Sec. 102, 23095). Tag the Pedalion's claim as that tradition's own reading.

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Greek/Byzantine:** the Pedalion's canons and prologue (16923-17789; 1800, printed 1886), reading the council as ecumenical and anti-Macedonian; the Synaxaristes for 25 January (53739) and 30 January (55655), 1819 edition; Socrates and Sozomen in Greek (socrates tomus 2, 6315-6744; sozomen tomus 2, 18760 on), fifth-century Constantinople.
- **Latin:** Leo's Letter CVI, 452 (npnf212 9285), refusing the see of Constantinople's rank; Hefele's history, 1855/1876 (20847-23269), Roman Catholic; Mansi's collection, 18th century (29706 on).
- **Syriac Orthodox:** Bar Hebraeus, Chronicon Ecclesiasticum I, 13th century (7749-7754 on Meletius and his successors; 7946 on Nectarius), Latin translation only.
- **Church of the East:** none located in the held files for this council; the East's adoption of Nicaea in 410 is another worker's event.
- **Coptic:** John of Nikiu, 7th century (5512-5666), who makes Timothy of Alexandria the honoured voice and has Gregory sent away; Severus ibn al-Muqaffa, 10th century (31594-31597), one sentence.
- **Armenian:** none located in the held files.
- **Georgian:** none located.
- **Ethiopian:** the Synaxarium's one-line commemoration of Gregory the Theologian (Budge vol. II, 12779), a late liturgical memory.
- **Eunomian:** Philostorgius, early 5th century, through Photius (368-388).

## 9. Gaps and escalations

- Parts 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 are well covered. Part 3 (what was argued on the floor) rests on one eyewitness in verse (Gregory) and on the winners' treatises; no minutes exist and none will.
- Part 5 is thin for the Pneumatomachians and Homoians, who have no surviving voice of their own; the story must say so plainly. The Apollinarian and Eunomian sides are now held in their own words.
- No PD English of the poem on his life or of the poem on the bishops is known. Any quotation will be an Opus rendering from the PG 37 Greek, which is a second-witness scan; the page image must be checked first (INTAKE §2).
- Nothing here needs Mark's ruling. One item to flag, not decide: whether the authorship of the creed is presented as `Contested` (this worker's reading of the held evidence) or `Dominant Modern Reconstruction`.
