## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

**1. Why they met.** Carried. The 410 acts open with the peace: the king of kings' edict, Marutha's embassy, the letter of the Western bishops read to the synod, and the saying "L'Orient et l'Occident forment une seule puissance" (Synodicon, French 16090-16100 and 16311-16330; Syriac from 1883; German, Synhados 282-300). The Chronicle of Seert gives the same chain from the Persian side: the Roman emperor's letter, Marutha treating the sick king, Isaac using Marutha's presence to call the bishops (15501-15518). Socrates VII.8 gives the Roman view (npnf202 19136-19175). For 424 the acts say why in their heading: Dadisho wished to resign the catholicate after imprisonment and slander, in the fourth year of Warahran, at Markabta of the Tayyaye (18551-18575; captivity noted at 18853). Labourt 7162 and Wigram 6027 narrate it.

**2. Who came and who was missing.** Carried, with a caveat. The 410 acts say forty bishops were listed (16405, 16521) and the canon on the metropolitan sees names the provinces (17743); Chabot's note warns that the surviving signatures do not match forty (16457). The 424 acts list the metropolitans and bishops present at Markabta (18575 onward). Missing: the Western (Antiochene) bishops, present at 410 only by letter (16311), and at 424 the deposed rebels, present only as the accused. The Arabic of Amr gives "forty bishops and metropolitans in the eleventh year of Yazdgard" (4813-4818). No source names those who stayed away in 410.

**3. What was argued.** Carried for 410 from the acts: the reading of the Western letter, Marutha calling for the book of the canons, the reception of "the council of the 318" (16587-16619), the demand that the ministry follow the Western pattern (17203). Carried for 424 from the long speech in the acts that recalls every Western intervention from Papa to Isaac and Yahballaha, the Papa affair, and the rebels' deposition by the Western Fathers (18895-19157). Braun's 1898 volume shows what "the canons of the Western Fathers" contained in Syriac (creed at 1086; canons after). Thin: no record of debate at 410 beyond acclamation; the acts are decisions, not minutes.

**4. What was decided, quoted from the source.** Carried in three languages. 410: the creed as received (16819), the anathema clause (16659), the twenty-one canons (16963-17455 in the raw numbering plus 12; the deposition canons at 16933 and 17353), the sentence on Batai (17809-17824); Syriac 1883-3150; German 282-1790. 424: the definition that Easterners may not complain of their patriarch before the Western patriarchs and that unresolved cases are "réservée au tribunal du Christ" (19304-19313; German 2602-2617), and Dadisho's acceptance (19370). Any quotation of the Syriac must first be checked against the page image; the French and German are quotable now and the Syriac is the original behind them.

**5. Who disagreed and why.** Partly carried; see section 4 below.

**6. The legacy.** Carried by the Church of the East's own later record: the synod of Acacius (486) and its successors in the same Synodicon (19437 onward), Elias's dated entry (5089), Mari (14915), Amr (14185-14188, Arabic 4813-4818), the Chronicle of Seert (15433-15548), and the Ebedjesus citation of the 424 definition noted by Chabot (19335-19337). Syriac Orthodox memory: Bar Hebraeus (held, 2838-3044, 3405-3510); Michael (161-228, Marutha only). Modern reception: Hefele (held, 27296-27349), Labourt (5728, 7162), Wigram (3882-6254).

**7. Stories from inside the event's own sources.** Carried. (a) Marutha heals the king: Socrates VII.8 (npnf202 19151), the Seert chronicle (15501-15503), Amr's Arabic (4841, the Persian physicians fail), Braun's 1898 introduction collecting the versions (531-598). (b) The Papa scene inside the 424 acts: Papa sees justice leave the assembly, lays his hand on the Gospel "comme un homme qui cherche du secours", and is struck (18960-18969). (c) Dadisho imprisoned and wishing to resign, and the bishops begging him to govern "selon le précepte du Christ à Pierre" (18551-18575, 19192-19201). (d) Marutha has the book of canons brought in (16587). Each is told by the source itself; the modern telling is the story thread's work.

**8. How each tradition remembers it.** See section 6.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

- **410.** The acts name the dissent: Batai of Mashmahig, "censuré et repoussé", is censured again, excommunicated and deposed by the whole synod, with anyone he ordains (17809-17824); "les autres téméraires et perturbateurs qui se sont attribué par violence le nom d'évêque" follow (17810-17813). Their own words do NOT survive: only the synod's sentence. Chabot's note at 17292 (raw) explains the term used for them. Synhados 1790 region gives the German.
- **424.** The acts quote the rebels against Dadisho in direct speech: "nous n'acceptons point la sentence de Dadisho, car il n'a pas reçu le catholicat; un homme qui s'intitulait de lui-même évêque lui a imposé les mains" and what they wrote to the magi (18819-18822). This is the opponents' position in the winners' document: embedded voice, not an independent text.
- **The Western (Antiochene) side of 424.** No source. The renunciation is told only by the Persian synod; nothing from Antioch about Persian appeals survives in any vendored text, and this worker found no public-domain edition of such a text. The "letter of the Western Fathers" is itself preserved only inside the Persian acts (Chabot's introduction, 1187 raw plus 12; the Dadisho acts at 18936 raw plus 12 call one such letter "prétendue").
- **Papa's opponents (fourth century).** Only through the 424 acts (18960-19037). Aphrahat's letter (npnf213 13308-13352) shows the division existed; it does not give the opponents' case. Braun showed the "letters of Papa" to be forgeries (Chabot's note, 18921); they are not held and would not be evidence.

## 5. Legends

- **Marutha cures Yazdgard and exorcises the prince.** Not a legend by the project's rule: a near-contemporary source carries it (Socrates VII.8, c. 440, npnf202 19151-19169; Greek 15446-15449). Later tellings elaborate it (Seert 15501-15503; Amr Arabic 4841; Braun 1898 at 531-598 compares them). Tell it as Socrates' story with its date.
- **Papa strikes the Gospel and is paralysed.** Earliest text we hold: the acts of 424 (18960-18969), a century after the alleged event, told by Papa's partisans. Contemporary silence checked: Aphrahat, Demonstration XIV (npnf213 13308-13352), searched for "Papa", "Gospel", "strik": no hit. Bar Hebraeus (held) carries the catholicoi list; his Papa entry was not read for this. "No source from the time records it" is the honest note; the Papa correspondence that also carries it is a forgery (above) and not held.
- **Forty bishops at 410.** A number, not a legend; the acts say forty (16521), Chabot shows the signatures do not match (16457). Tag as a contested count.
- No other legend attached to these synods was found in the vendored texts or in Labourt and Wigram.

## 9. Gaps and escalations

- No eyewitness narrative exists outside the acts; parts 1-4 rest on the acts themselves and on later chronicles. This is the shape of the evidence, not a gap a search can close.
- The dissenting side of 410 (Batai and the "perturbateurs") survives only as the synod's sentence; the Antiochene side of 424 survives not at all. Part 5 must say so.
- The Syriac OCR is a second witness until checked against page images; the Arabic of Mari is not recovered at all; the Arabic of Amr is recovered but error-prone.
- The Chronicle of Seert's surviving text stops before Dadisho; 424 has no compilation witness.
- The Chronicle of Arbela is contested; nothing may rest on it alone.
- **For Mark (Library rule):** archive.org uploaders sometimes attach a CC BY-NC-ND licence URL to a scan of a public-domain printing (PO 5, 7, 9, 13 here). This worker treated the printed edition's date as decisive but avoided those scans where a clean alternative existed. A standing rule is needed.
- **For Mark (corpus-map role):** Marutha's Nicene dossier is staged as `tradition` for `syriac-edessa-nisibis` and `transmission` for `persian-church-of-the-east-early`; the Church of the East received it as its own law. Which role fits is a project-lead call.
