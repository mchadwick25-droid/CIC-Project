## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

**1. Why they met.** Carried. Arius's letter to Eusebius of Nicomedia and Eusebius's to Paulinus (npnf203, about 6000 and 5776); Alexander's encyclical (Socrates I.6, npnf202, chapter only); Constantine's letter to Alexander and Arius (Life of Constantine II.64-72, npnf201, chapter only); Philostorgius's claim that Alexander and Hosius settled homoousios at Nicomedia beforehand (98). The Thalia fragments (npnf204, De Synodis 15) give Arius's teaching as his opponent quoted it.

**2. Who came, and who was missing.** Carried, with the counts shown as the sources give them. Eusebius: more than 250 (Life III.8, chapter only). Eustathius: about 270 (npnf203 6387). The round number 318 is Athanasius's and later tradition's (Ad Afros 2; the Armenian text at 10462 has it); the dossier treats 318 as reception, not record. The subscription lists (Gelzer file, Latin lists from about 4780) name who signed, by province; Hosius, and Vitus and Vincentius for the bishop of Rome (Socrates I.8). Missing: Silvester of Rome in person; Papa bar Aggai of Seleucia-Ctesiphon, whose absence from the lists Bar Hebraeus's editors note (1700). The Syriac tradition's Jacob of Nisibis and the Armenian tradition's Aristakes are in the lists' later recensions; the Latin lists must be checked before either is stated as signatory.

**3. What was argued.** Carried, from three angles. Eusebius of Caesarea's letter home (npnf204 19554; also Socrates I.8 and Theodoret I.12) is the only account of the creed's wording being negotiated, by a participant who had to explain his own signature. Athanasius, De Decretis 19-20 (npnf204 from 26384), on why "of the essence" and "homoousios" were chosen. Eustathius's fragment (npnf203 6289): the Arian creed read out and torn up. Gelasius (from 4093) supplies a long philosopher disputation that no earlier source has: fifth-century memory, to be named as such.

**4. What was decided, quoted from the source.** Carried in full. Creed (npnf214 2394; Greek in the Pedalion and in Mansi 2 at 624; Latin versions in Turner). Twenty canons (npnf214 2812-5497; Greek Pedalion; Latin Mansi 2 at 626 and Turner). Synodal letter on Arius, the Melitians and Easter (npnf214 2326; Socrates I.9). Constantine's letter on Easter (npnf214 6419; Life III.17-20). Nothing on the biblical canon: see §5.

**5. Who disagreed, and why, in each side's own terms.** Carried, with a limit. Arius's own words survive only as quoted by opponents (Theodoret I.5; Athanasius, De Synodis 15-16 and Orations I.5-6; Epiphanius 69, not held in Greek). Eusebius of Nicomedia's letter to Paulinus (npnf203 about 5776) is the most articulate statement of the other side before the council. The Eunomian memory of the council is Philostorgius I.7-10 (98-106). The two bishops who refused to sign, Secundus and Theonas, have no surviving words of their own. The later Homoian rejection of homoousios is in Hilary (npnf209) and Auxentius (Kauffmann file). The limit: no text from the Arian side survives except through hostile transmission; a story must say so.

**6. The legacy.** Carried. Athanasius, De Decretis and De Synodis; Hilary; the creed of 381 and its relation to 325 (npnf214); CTh 16.1.2 (380) making the Nicene faith the imperial standard (held in both Theodosian Code files); the Latin West's canon collections (Turner; Caecilian's text at Carthage 419, 18788); the Church of the East's adoption of the creed and canons at Seleucia-Ctesiphon in 410 (the Synodicon Orientale is being vendored under the church-of-the-east-synods-410-424 dossier); the Armenian canons (10462-10470); Hefele's reconstruction (vol. I from 492) as a nineteenth-century reading.

**7. Stories from inside the event's own sources.** Carried, each with its earliest witness:
- Constantine's entry and speech, and the banquet for the bishops: Eusebius, Life III.10-15 (eyewitness; chapter only).
- The petitions the bishops brought against each other, burned unread by the emperor: Socrates I.8 (2829), Sozomen I.17, Rufinus X.2 (Latin; locate in the GCS file before citing). Not in Eusebius.
- Paphnutius against a rule of clerical continence: Rufinus X.4 (26727) is the first written source, c. 402; Socrates I.11 (Greek 6225, 9952) and Sozomen I.23 follow him. Eusebius and the canons are silent.
- The simple confessor who silences a philosopher: Rufinus X.3 and Sozomen I.18; elaborated in Gelasius (from 4093).
- Eustathius and the torn Arian paper: Theodoret I.8 (6289).
- Constantine kisses the scars of the confessor-bishops (Paphnutius's eye): Theodoret I.11 (chapter only; locate before citing).
- Acesius the Novatian and the emperor's "set up a ladder and climb to heaven alone": Socrates I.10 (chapter only; locate before citing). This is the one Nicaea story in which a schismatic bishop speaks for himself.
- Spyridon the shepherd-bishop's miracles: Socrates I.12, Sozomen I.11 (not set at the council), Gelasius chapter on Spyridon (6243).

**8. How each tradition remembers it.** See §6 below.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

Who dissented: Arius and his Alexandrian clergy; Eusebius of Nicomedia, Theognis of Nicaea, Maris of Chalcedon; Secundus of Ptolemais and Theonas of Marmarica, who refused to sign; the Melitians, whose settlement the synodal letter records; the later Anomoean tradition that read the council as a defeat.

In their own words, as the Library holds them: Arius to Eusebius of Nicomedia (npnf203, about 6000); Arius and his clergy to Alexander (npnf204 18983, in De Synodis 16); the Thalia fragments (De Synodis 15; Orations I.5-6); Eusebius of Nicomedia to Paulinus (npnf203, about 5776); Eusebius of Nicomedia and Theognis, letter of recantation (Socrates I.14; Sozomen II.16, chapter only). All of these survive only inside hostile works. The Greek of the Athanasian quotations is in PG 26 (second witness, verify against the page image). The Eunomian reading is Philostorgius (98-106), itself surviving only in Photius's hostile summary.

Missing: any Arian text transmitted by Arians; any Melitian text on the settlement (the Melitian papyri held in `bell_jews-christians-egypt-meletian-papyri_1924.txt` are later and about Athanasius, not the council); Epiphanius's Panarion 69 in Greek (§7).

## 5. Legends

**Nicholas of Myra strikes Arius.** Claim: Nicholas slapped Arius during the council. Silence checked: `npnf201`, `npnf202`, `npnf203`, `npnf204`, `npnf214` (searches for Nicholas and Nicolas find Pope Nicholas I, Theodore of Myra at Nicaea II, and a list of "pillars" at npnf203 11120 only); Gelasius, the fullest ancient narrative of the council, has no Nikolaos at all (search Νικόλα, 0 hits); Rufinus X; Hefele vol. I (only Pope Nicholas). The subscription lists are the place to check whether a Nicholas of Myra signed; the Latin lists in the Gelzer file can be searched by a build thread. Earliest text we hold that carries it: none. The Greek Synaxarion entry for 6 December (36919-36925) says Nicholas "was part of" the council and then passes to the three men saved in Constantinople; it tells no slap. Where the slap first appears is not located in any citable public-domain text held or found this pass; do not assert a first appearance.

**Spyridon's brick (fire, water, clay) demonstrating the Trinity.** Silence checked: Socrates I.12 and Sozomen I.11 tell Spyridon's miracles (the thieves, his daughter's deposit) and set none at the council; Gelasius has a Spyridon chapter (6243) and the words for brick or tile occur nowhere in the file (κέραμ, πλίνθ: 0 hits); the Synaxarion's 12 December entry (from 16548) puts him at Nicaea as "champion of piety" and the following hundred lines name no brick (the full entry was not read). Earliest text we hold: none. First appearance not located.

**Constantine burns the bishops' petitions.** Not a legend in the project's sense: recorded by Socrates I.8 (2829), Sozomen I.17 and Rufinus X.2 within a century of the event. Eusebius, the eyewitness, does not mention it; a story should say "the fifth-century historians record" rather than "it happened".

**Paphnutius and clerical marriage.** Likewise a reported scene, first in Rufinus (26727), about seventy-five years after the event; Eusebius and the canons are silent. State the gap.

**"Nicaea decided the books of the Bible."** No canon, letter or ancient narrative held makes any ruling on the biblical canon (npnf214 Nicaea section; the Mansi 2 syllabus at 624-626; Eusebius; Socrates; Gelasius). Earliest text we hold that says it: none. This legend is modern; a note can only show the silence.

**"318 fathers."** Reception, not record: Eusebius says more than 250, Eustathius about 270 (6387). The 318 appears in Athanasius and becomes the fixed number in later tradition (Armenian text 10462). A note can show the three numbers side by side from the files.

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Greek and Byzantine:** Gelasius of Cyzicus (c. 475, pro-Nicene compilation); the Pedalion's canons and commentary (Nikodemos, 1800); the Synaxarion entries for 6 and 12 December (Nikodemos, 1819). The Byzantine commemoration of the Fathers of the First Council (seventh Sunday of Pascha) is not in the files held.
- **Latin:** Rufinus X (402-403, Aquileia); Hilary, On the Councils (359, Gaul); the Latin canon versions (Turner; Carthage 419); Mansi's collection (eighteenth century, Lucca); Hefele (1855, Tübingen, Roman Catholic). Leo and the fifth-century West are in `npnf212`.
- **Syriac Orthodox:** Chronicle of Edessa (sixth century, 69); Bar Hebraeus (thirteenth century, 1700, 31265); the Jacob of Nisibis tradition told by `syr.story.jacob-nicaea`. Marutha of Maipherqat's Syriac account of Nicaea (Braun 1898) is a lead under the church-of-the-east-synods dossier.
- **Church of the East:** the synod of 410 received the creed and canons (Synodicon Orientale, being vendored in this batch); Bar Hebraeus's Eastern section (1700). Standpoint: a church outside the empire adopting the empire's council a century later.
- **Coptic:** Severus al-Muqaffa, History of the Patriarchs (tenth century) is held but this pass could not confirm its Nicaea passage by search; read the Alexander I entry. No other Coptic witness is held.
- **Armenian:** Moses of Khoren II.89-90 (10462-10470): Aristakes at Nicaea, the 318, the twenty canons brought home, Gregory's additions. Standpoint: a national church placing its founder's son at the council. Italian translation (1850); the Armenian text is not held.
- **Eunomian / Anomoean:** Philostorgius (fifth century, via Photius).
- **Georgian, Ethiopian, Nubian, Indian:** none located in a public-domain edition reachable from this sandbox. The Ethiopian Senodos and the Georgian Great Nomocanon exist only in editions not checked here.

## 9. Gaps and escalations

- The Arian side speaks only through its opponents. That is the nature of the record, not a sourcing failure; a story must say it.
- No clean Greek of Theodoret or Epiphanius is held; the English of Theodoret (npnf203) carries the letters.
- Athanasius in Greek is held only as a second-witness scan (PG 25-26).
- Coptic, Ethiopian and Georgian memory of Nicaea: not held in any public-domain edition reachable here.
- Legends: the first appearance of the Nicholas slap and the Spyridon brick is not located in any text held; the notes can show silence only, which is what the legend rule requires.
- Nothing here needs a ruling from Mark. The event clears the bar: every one of the eight parts has at least one source that carries it, and the dissenting side is present in quotation.
