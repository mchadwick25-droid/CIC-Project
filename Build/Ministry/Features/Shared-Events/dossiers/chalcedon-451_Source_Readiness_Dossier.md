## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

**1. Why they met.** Carried. Eutyches's trial at the Home Synod of 448 and his appeal (npnf212, letters from Eutyches and Flavian, 4657; Zachariah III.1, 1229-1253; the acts read back at Chalcedon, Mansi 6 within Actio prima); Ephesus II and its sentences (Perry; Flemming; Evagrius I.10, 1780; Chronicle of Edessa 63, 169); Leo's protest and the word Latrocinium (npnf212 editor 663-672); Theodosius's death and Marcian's accession (John of Nikiu 6762-6778, from the Coptic side; Zachariah). The anti-Chalcedonian reason, that Nestorianism was being restored, is in Timothy Aelurus and in the Plerophories (612 ff.).

**2. Who came, and who was missing.** Carried. The subscription lists and the imperial commissioners in the acts (Mansi 6-7); Leo's legates presiding, Dioscorus moved to the accused's place (Evagrius II.4, 3000-3012; npnf214 Session I); the "six hundred and thirty" of later tradition against the acts' own counts (Ethiopic Synaxarium 7346 gives 630); Leo absent; the Egyptians who would not sign without a patriarch (acts, session IV, title read); the Armenian bishops absent because of the Avarayr war of 451, a fact the Armenian historians Elishe and Lazar report without naming the council (Langlois vol. 2 checked: "Chalcédoine" occurs only in the editor's introduction, raw line 3093). The Church of the East sent no one.

**3. What was argued.** Carried. Session I's reading of the Ephesus II acts and the Home Synod acts, with the bishops' recantations (Mansi 6, 77925; npnf214); the Tome and the Egyptians' objections; the dispute over "from two natures" against "in two natures" at session V (Mansi 7 from 9189; npnf214 Session V); the Egyptian case in Dioscorus's own words as the acts preserve them, and as Timothy Aelurus later argued it; Theodoret's reluctant anathema of Nestorius (npnf214 Session VIII, title only; locate before citing).

**4. What was decided, quoted from the source.** Carried in full. The Definition of Faith (npnf214; Greek and Latin at Mansi 7, 10861); the deposition of Dioscorus (npnf214 "The Condemnation Sent to Dioscorus"; Mansi 6 session III); the twenty-eight canons including canon 28 (npnf214; Pedalion 19422; Mansi 7 index 238); the decrees on Jerusalem and Antioch, Ephesus and Nicomedia (titles read); the council's letter to Leo and Leo's qualified reply (npnf212, titles read).

**5. Who disagreed, and why, in each side's own terms.** Carried, both before and after the council. Before: Dioscorus and Ephesus II in their own acts (Perry, Flemming; Mansi 6, 77925); Eutyches's own confession in his letter to Leo (npnf212). At the council: the Egyptian bishops' refusal (acts). After: Timothy Aelurus's refutation (Armenian, whole file); Zachariah III-IV; the Plerophories; the Life of Peter the Iberian; Theopistus's Dioscorus; Severus's letters and homilies; Philoxenus; Michael the Syrian. Rome's own dissent from canon 28: Leo's letters to Anatolius and Marcian (npnf212, titles read; `ijc.contested.canon-28-meaning` already works this). The limit: Dioscorus's own words at Chalcedon survive only in the acts the winners kept; Eutyches has one letter.

**6. The legacy.** Carried. The Codex Encyclius of 457-458 (Mansi 7, 396); Proterius's murder and Timothy Aelurus's consecration (Evagrius II.8, 3636-3649; Zachariah IV, 2517-2530; Raabe's preface 191); the Palestinian revolt against Juvenal (Life of Peter the Iberian 2726-2806; Plerophories); Severus and Philoxenus; the Chalcedonian monks of Palestine (Cyril of Scythopolis); the Henotikon and the Acacian schism (Era 3 dossier); Justinian's diptychs (Chronicle of Edessa 88, 219); the Church of the East's own two-natures creed of 486 (Synodicon Orientale, sibling file, 1244) as a parallel settlement; the Coptic and Ethiopian liturgical memory of Dioscorus (Severus al-Muqaffa 34868; Ethiopic Synaxarium 7346).

**7. Stories from inside the event's own sources.** Carried, each with its witness:
- Eusebius of Dorylaeum's petition read at the first session, "I have been wronged by Dioscorus; the faith has been wronged; the bishop Flavian was murdered": Evagrius II.4 (3006), quoting the acts; the acts themselves (Mansi 6; npnf214 Session I).
- The bishops who signed at Ephesus II crying that they signed under force, "we were beaten", and Dioscorus's reply: the acts (npnf214 Session I, title read; Mansi 6).
- Dioscorus summoned three times and refusing to come; his deposition "for contempt" at session III: npnf214 Session III and the condemnation; Mansi 6.
- The Egyptian bishops begging not to be made to sign without a patriarch, "we shall be killed when we go home": npnf214 Session IV (title read; locate before citing).
- The Definition read in the martyr's church before Marcian and Pulcheria, the acclamations: npnf214 Session VI; Mansi 7.
- Theodoret made to anathematize Nestorius before he is restored: npnf214 Session VIII (title read).
- Dioscorus at Gangra telling the council from his side: Theopistus (388), Coptic memory, to be dated as such.

**8. How each tradition remembers it.** See §6 below.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

Who dissented: Dioscorus and the Egyptian bishops; Eutyches; the monks of Palestine who rose against Juvenal; Timothy Aelurus and the Alexandrian church that made him patriarch; the Syriac-speaking bishops and monks who became the Syriac Orthodox church; the Armenian church, which rejected the council at Dvin in 506; the Coptic, Ethiopian and Nubian churches that kept Dioscorus's memory. From the other side, Rome dissented from canon 28.

In their own words, as the Library now holds them: Dioscorus's council in its own acts (Perry; Flemming; Mansi 6 at 77925); Eutyches to Leo (npnf212); Timothy Aelurus, the whole refutation (Armenian; no translation held; the German preface is a lead); Zacharias of Gaza's history (Zachariah III-VI, English); John Rufus's Plerophories (French); the Life of Peter the Iberian (German); Theopistus's Dioscorus (French); Severus's letters (English) and homilies 58-69 (French, PO 8.2); Philoxenus (English); the Coptic History of the Patriarchs (English); the Ethiopic Synaxarium (English, sibling file); Michael the Syrian (French, sibling file). Leo on canon 28 (npnf212).

Surviving only in opponents' record: Dioscorus's own words at Chalcedon (the acts); the Egyptian bishops' plea (the acts). Missing: Dioscorus's letters and sermons (lost except fragments); the Armenian Book of Letters (not located; see §8); Timothy Aelurus in any modern language; Zachariah's Syriac (the only scans recovered no Syriac, §8).

## 5. Legends

**The rival statements of faith laid in St Euphemia's tomb.** Claim: the orthodox and the heretics each wrote their confession; both were sealed in the martyr's coffin; after prayer the heretics' tome lay at her feet and the orthodox one in her hand or on her breast. Silence checked: the council's own letters and acts in `npnf214` (the Euphemia passages at 20081-20486 name the church and say, in the letter to Leo, that the martyr delivered the definition "as her own confession of faith", 20356, a rhetorical phrase, no tomb test); the Greek of Evagrius II.3-4 (4379 ff.) and the English (2861-2945, searched for tomb, coffin, book, definition: only the coffin's structure and its aperture, 44-68 of that span), which describes the shrine and its miracles and tells no test; Zachariah (2475, 6748, 9448: the church as venue only); Leo's letters (npnf212, no hit for a tomb test by title or search). Hefele's note on the "very obscure passage" (npnf214 20356) and the Ballerini note reprinted in Mansi 6 (14481: "hunc locum, qui hactenus fuit obscurus, et ex quo orta creditur historia quam enarrant Zonaras aliique posteriores Graeci") say the story grew out of that phrase and name Zonaras (twelfth century) as its teller; we do not hold Zonaras, so that is a lead, not a citation. Earliest texts WE HOLD that carry it: (a) the Chronicle of Edessa 83 (209), c. 540, Syriac, pro-Chalcedonian: a different form, in which the emperor Anastasius (491-518) orders the synod's book taken from Euphemia's coffin and burnt, and fire strikes the faces of those who try; this attests a book in the coffin and a miracle, not the two-tome contest; (b) Nikodemos's Synaxaristes, 11 July (vol. 2, 30848-30862), the two-tome contest in full, in a Byzantine liturgical compilation of 1819 (1868 edition, 1900 printing), a late witness to a much older synaxarion tradition. A note can show the silence of the acts and of Evagrius, cite the Chronicle of Edessa as the earliest held form, and give the Synaxarion as the tradition's own telling; it must not assert where the two-tome form first appears.

**Flavian's death from his beating at Ephesus II.** Not a legend; a reported event with uncertain details. Dated witnesses: the acts of Chalcedon, session I (451), where Eusebius of Dorylaeum says Flavian "was murdered" and Barsumas is called his killer (Evagrius II.4 quoting them, 3006; npnf214 20860 for Barsumas); the Syriac acts of 449 (Perry; Flemming) record the sentence, not the death; Leo's letters of 449-450 and the Latin Breviculus make him die in exile some days or weeks later (Hefele's weighing of Prosper, the Breviculus and Nicephorus, 13750-13856; npnf212 editor "variously recorded", 663-672); Zachariah III.3 (1264-1312) records the ejection, not the death; the Chronicle of Edessa 63 (169) records the anathema only; Michael the Syrian VIII (sibling file). A story should say he was beaten, deposed, exiled and died within days, as the sources from 449-451 allow, and name the uncertainty.

**Proterius killed in the baptistery.** Not a legend: a reported event. Evagrius II.8 (3636-3649: run through with a sword, dragged through the city, burnt) from a Chalcedonian standpoint, late sixth century; Zachariah IV.2 (2517-2530) from the anti-Chalcedonian side, c. 500, which blames Proterius's own violence and the Roman soldiers; the Life of Peter the Iberian (3111) and the Plerophories call him Judas and apostate; Michael the Syrian has the murder foretold three days before (sibling file, 5574). The baptistery as the place is in Evagrius (check the exact wording at 3636-3649 before citing). Say so, with both standpoints.

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Greek and Byzantine:** Evagrius (late sixth century, Greek now held); Cyril of Scythopolis (sixth century, Palestinian Chalcedonian); the Pedalion (1800); the Synaxaristes (1819). The Codex Encyclius (457-458) is the imperial church's first collective reception.
- **Latin:** Leo's letters (npnf212); Hefele (1855). Rome receives the faith and rejects canon 28.
- **Syriac Orthodox:** Zachariah (c. 500); Philoxenus (c. 500); Severus (early sixth century; letters held, homilies 58-69 now held in PO 8.2); John of Ephesus (sixth century); Michael the Syrian VIII (twelfth century, sibling file). Standpoint: the council as a Nestorian revenge and an imperial imposition.
- **Church of the East:** the Synodicon Orientale (sibling file): the synods of 486 and after adopt a two-natures Christology independently and later cite Chalcedon's canons (1085, 36338). Standpoint: a church outside the empire for which Chalcedon vindicated what Ephesus 431 had condemned.
- **Coptic:** Severus al-Muqaffa (tenth century, 34868-35213); John of Nikiu (seventh century, 6762-6778); Theopistus's History of Dioscorus (Coptic legend-history in Syriac, now held). Standpoint: Dioscorus the confessor, Marcian and Pulcheria the persecutors.
- **Ethiopian:** the Ethiopic Synaxarium, 7 Maskaram (sibling file, 7346): Dioscorus's death at Gangra, Marcian "the denier of Christ". Fourteenth century and later, in a 1928 translation.
- **Armenian:** Timothy Aelurus's refutation survives only because the Armenians translated it (now held, Armenian). The fifth-century Armenian historians (Elishe, Lazar) do not name the council (Langlois vol. 2 checked); the formal rejection is at Dvin (Era 3). The Book of Letters is not held (§8).
- **Georgian:** the Life of Peter the Iberian (now held, German) is a Georgian prince's anti-Chalcedonian Life written in Gaza; the Georgian church's own later Chalcedonian memory (Kyrion, c. 600, a census site story) is not held in any public-domain text located here.
- **Palestinian Chalcedonian monasticism:** Cyril of Scythopolis (held); the Plerophories and the Life of Peter give the opposing Gaza monks' view.
- **Nubian, Melkite:** none located.

## 9. Gaps and escalations

- The dissenting side is present in its own words, in five languages: Timothy Aelurus (Armenian), Zachariah, Severus, Philoxenus, the Coptic History (English), the Plerophories, Theopistus, Michael (French), the Life of Peter (German), the 449 acts (English and German). Nothing here needs a ruling.
- Timothy Aelurus is held only in Classical Armenian with no translation; a quote from him needs an Opus rendering checked against the page image, under the project's original-language rule.
- Zachariah's Syriac and the Syriac of the 449 acts, the Plerophories and the Life of Peter are on the page images but not in any OCR found; the translations stand as the quotable text.
- The Euphemia legend's two-tome form is attested in the files only in the 1819 Synaxarion; the Chronicle of Edessa (c. 540) has an earlier, different form. Zonaras is named as the teller by the Ballerini note but is not held.
- Armenian and Georgian Chalcedonian-era memory beyond Timothy and Peter is not held; the Book of Letters is not in any public-domain edition located.
- Mansi 6-7 total 7.3 MB and their Greek columns are only partly recovered; the Latin is the quotable column.
