## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

1. **Why they met.** Strong. The Donatist petition for Gallic judges (Optatus English 265; PL 8 305-330); the Rome judgment of 313 under Miltiades (Augustine Ep. 43, npnf101 27637-27649; Latin 11663); the Donatists' complaint that Rome judged "shut up somewhere" and the emperor's decision to hear the case again at Arles (Constantine to Aelafius, Optatus English 7099; Latin 1054); Constantine to Chrestus (npnf201 50627).
2. **Who came and who was missing.** Partial. The summons names the provinces and the travel route (Aelafius letter 7099) and Chrestus's summons gives the opening date (npnf201 50627). The letter to Silvester opens "Marinus vel coetus episcoporum qui adunati fuerunt in oppido Arelatensi" (Lauchert 2660), so Marinus presided and Silvester was absent, and the council told him so (Optatus English 7115 "would ... that you had deemed it well to be present"). No subscription list is held; the Candidates row's "none" stands. The Donatist attendance is known only from Constantine's and Augustine's statements that both parties came (petschenig 2695; 31584).
3. **What was argued.** Thin. No acts survive. The letter to Silvester calls the Donatists "troublesome men ... of undisciplined mind" rejected because "they neither have reasonableness in their argument, nor any moderation in their accusations" (Optatus English 7115). The substance of the Caecilian case must be reconstructed from the Felix acta (5467; Latin 711) and the Cirta acta (7228), which the Arles judges would have known.
4. **What was decided, quoted from the source.** Strong for the canons, thin for the verdict. The letter to Silvester and the canons on Easter, ordination, rebaptism of heretics by the Africans (canon 8), soldiers, charioteers and actors, deacons, and the traditores (Lauchert 2660-2672; English 7112-7115; Latin 1161-1189). The verdict on Caecilian is known only by report: Constantine's letter after Arles (7123; Latin 1257) and Augustine (npnf101 27649; petschenig 2695).
5. **Who disagreed and why, in their own terms.** Partial. See §4.
6. **Legacy.** Good. The Donatists' appeal to the emperor himself after Arles and the Milan hearing of 316 (Constantine's letters 7123-7193; npnf104 note 11465; Augustine Ep. 43 and 88); the coercion of 317-321 remembered in the Donatist Sermo de passione Donati et Advocati (PL 8 excerpt 90-94); Augustine's use of "Rome and Arles" as the two episcopal judgments the Donatists first sought and then refused (npnf104 17722; 19378; petschenig 31584); canon 8 on rebaptism as the Catholic answer to African practice (npnf104 11819; Turner 5810); the canons' afterlife in Gaul (Turner 40162-40164).
7. **Stories from inside the sources.** Good, but from the dossier not the council: Constantine's "Judgement of Christ" reply (Optatus English 271); the Donatist bishops asking for judges from Gaul because Gaul had no traditores (265; PL 8 305-330); the itinerary the African bishops were ordered to take through Mauretania and Spain (7099, with the translator's defence of it 7091); the proceedings before Zenophilus in 320 with the grammarian Victor and the deacon Nundinarius proving Silvanus a traditor (5943-6184); the Felix acta. The council itself has no narrated scene; any story set in the council chamber would be invention.
8. **How each tradition remembers it.** See §6. Thin outside the Latin West.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

- **The Donatist petition to Constantine** (before Arles): the bishops of Majorinus's party ask for Gallic judges; in their words, with the subscribing names Lucianus, Dignus, Nassutius, Capito, Fidentius (PL 8 305-330; English in Optatus I.22 at 265). Preserved by Optatus, a Catholic.
- **Parmenian** (Donatist bishop of Carthage, c. 362-391): his admission that both parties and the judges met at Arles, and his charge that the judges and then the emperor were "corrupt" (petschenig 2695-2700). Survives only as Augustine's paraphrase in Contra epistulam Parmeniani.
- **Petilian** (Donatist bishop of Constantina, c. 400): his letter is quoted in pieces throughout Contra litteras Petiliani (npnf104 15292 on; Latin petschenig 24196 on); on the emperors, the only direct words recovered in this pass concern Julian (npnf104 17879). Opponent's quotation.
- **Cresconius** (Donatist grammarian): his account of the appeals, answered at Contra Cresconium III (petschenig 40275-40280). Opponent's quotation.
- **The Donatist sermon De passione Donati et Advocati** (c. 340, PL 8 excerpt 90-148): a Donatist author's own voice on the imperial coercion that followed the judgments. It does not mention Arles by name in the lines read.
- **Missing.** No Donatist text states the Arles proceedings themselves. The Donatists' written appeal after Arles is known only from Constantine's reply (Optatus English 7123). Part 5 can be written, but every Donatist argument about the council itself comes through Catholic hands, and the story must say so.

## 5. Legends

No well-known legend attaches to Arles 314. Two contested points should be tagged rather than told as settled:

- **The authenticity of Constantine's letter to Aelafius** (Optatus appendix III). The translator's note (Optatus English 7089-7091) records the doubt and Duchesne's answer. `formation_confidence`: Widely Accepted that it is genuine; the note should say the doubt existed.
- **The number and date.** The letter to Silvester gives no bishop count; the opening date of 1 August 314 rests on the Chrestus and Aelafius letters (npnf201 50627; Optatus English 7099). Hefele's discussion is at 11702.

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Latin African (Catholic):** Optatus (c. 365) and Augustine (Epp. 43, 88, 93, 185; On Baptism; Contra Petilianum; Contra Cresconium), all held in Latin and English (§1).
- **Latin African (Donatist):** Parmenian, Petilian and Cresconius via Augustine; the Donatist sermon and martyr acts in PL 8 (§4).
- **Gallic:** the canons' reuse by the second council of Arles (Turner 40162-40164); Jerome's Chronicle records Donatus and the Donatians (45125, 45669) but not the council.
- **Greek/Byzantine:** Eusebius HE X.5 preserves Constantine's summons to Chrestus (npnf201 50627); Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret are silent (checked by search). No Byzantine memory located.
- **Syriac Orthodox:** none located. Michael the Syrian tome I (vendored this pass) has no Arles or Donatus (searched 'Arles', 'Donat'); Bar Hebraeus vol. I likewise (searched).
- **Church of the East:** none located for the council. Mari ibn Sulayman's Latin (vendored by worker C) carries a line "Romae schisma fecit Donatus" (raw scan line 15825; not read in context in the vendored file), a confused memory of the African schism as Roman, worth a note and nothing more.
- **Coptic, Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopian:** none located (Severus al-Muqaffa, John of Nikiu, the Ethiopian Synaxarium vols. I-II, Moses of Khoren, PO 16 searched for Arles and Donatus without result).
