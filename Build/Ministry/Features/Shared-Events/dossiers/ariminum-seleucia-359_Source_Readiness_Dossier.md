## 3. What the sources can carry, part by part

1. **Why they met.** Strong. Constantius's plan for a double council after Sirmium 357-359 (Athanasius 56338-56623; Sozomen 32282; Hilary De Synodis 7545-7603; Philostorgius 226; Sulpicius 11197).
2. **Who came and who was missing.** Good. "Four hundred and more" Western bishops at Ariminum, and the Aquitanians, Gauls and Britons who refused the imperial rations (Sulpicius 11205; Latin 5388); about 160 at Seleucia with Hilary present (Hilary intro 1626; Sulpicius 11253); the Western subscriptions are not held as a list. Athanasius was absent and says so (56336 on).
3. **What was argued.** Strong on all three sides. Nicene: Phoebadius (15614), Hilary De Synodis (7740), Athanasius De Synodis part III (58501). Homoian: the Dated Creed and the Niké formula (56623, 58362-58377; Mansi 3 22034-22123; CSEL 65 series A), Germinius's letter (CSEL 65 series table 1159-1208), Auxentius's confession (Kauffmann 15283 on). Anomoean: Philostorgius IV-V (226-234); Acacius's creed at Seleucia (Socrates 9264).
4. **What was decided, quoted from the source.** Strong. The Niké-Constantinople creed in English and Latin (58362-58377; Mansi 3 33121); the Ariminum letters to Constantius (Theodoret 9926-9941; CSEL 65 series A; Mansi 3 18865); the depositions at Constantinople 360 (Socrates 9616; Philostorgius 234); the law of 360 on clerical privileges "in Ariminensi synodo" (CTh 6930).
5. **Who disagreed and why, in their own terms.** Strong: see §4.
6. **Legacy.** Strong. Hilary's Contra Constantium (46205) and the Gallic repudiation (Jerome 45885; Sulpicius 11419); the Luciferian schism (Lucifer; Avellana 5622-7443; Jerome's Dialogue 32331-32448); Damasus's synodical act annulling Ariminum (Theodoret 10196); Valentinian II's law of 386 making the Ariminum-Constantinople faith the legal one (CTh 6889; Mommsen 83733); Wulfila's Gothic church as the Homoian legacy (Kauffmann 15283); Zachariah Rhetor's Miaphysite anathema on Rimini (`zachariah-rhetor_chronicle_hamiltonbrooks1899.txt` 5805).
7. **Stories from inside the sources.** Strong. The bishops who would not eat the emperor's bread (Sulpicius 11205; Latin 5388, the census site story); the legates at Niké deceived and the night of the signing (Sozomen 34451; Jerome 32331-32448; Athanasius 58362); Hilary at Seleucia hearing the Easterners' creed and finding it worse than he feared (intro 1836-1843; Contra Constantium 46205, not checked to the sentence); Constantius keeping the Western legates waiting at Constantinople (56836); the earthquake that moved the Eastern council from Nicomedia (Philostorgius 228; Sozomen 33610).
8. **How each tradition remembers it.** Good: see §6.

## 4. The dissenting side in its own words

Three dissents, two of them in their own transmission:

- **Homoian (the winners of 359-360, later the dissent from the Nicene settlement):** the Dated Creed, the Niké formula and the Constantinople creed (Athanasius 56623, 58362-58377; Mansi 3 33121); Germinius's letter and the Arian party's letter from Ariminum (CSEL 65 series table 1159-1208); Auxentius of Durostorum on Wulfila's faith (Kauffmann 15283 on) and the Dissertatio Maximini (Latin 3982-5610), the Danubian Homoian voice of 381-383 that still called the Rimini faith its own (Kauffmann 16848, 16935). Partly their own transmission (Kauffmann's Paris manuscript), partly embedded in Nicene dossiers.
- **Anomoean/Eunomian:** Philostorgius IV.10-V.2 (226-234), his own history through Photius's hostile epitome; the Acacian creed of Seleucia via Socrates (9264). Mediated.
- **Hard-line Nicene refusal of the settlement:** Lucifer (2628-17490), Phoebadius (15614), Hilary's Contra Constantium (46205), the Luciferian Libellus precum (Avellana 5622-7443). Their own transmission.
- **Missing.** The acts of Ariminum and Seleucia themselves (Jerome says he read "the acts of the Synod of Ariminum", 32395; they are lost); any Homoian narrative of the councils in its own words; Acacius of Caesarea's own writings.

## 5. Legends

- **"The whole world groaned and was astonished to find itself Arian."** What it claims: a contemporary cry of despair at Ariminum. What the files show: the sentence is Jerome's own, written about 379 in the Dialogue against the Luciferians, section 19 (npnf206 32413), twenty years after the event, as a rhetorical summary; the NPNF editor quotes it again in a note on Letter LXIX (15904-15905). No source from 359-360 says it (Athanasius, Hilary, Sulpicius and the Ariminum letters were searched for "groan"). It is a true quotation of Jerome and a false quotation of 359; the note should say so and give the date and standpoint.
- **"Four hundred" or "four hundred and fifty" bishops.** Sulpicius: "quadringenti et aliquanto amplius" (Latin 5388; English 11205); the NPNF note: "about 450" (15899-15905). Contested at the level of the sources; tag Widely Accepted for "over four hundred".
- **The lapse of Liberius.** Whether and what Liberius signed in exile is contested in the sources themselves: Hilary's dossier preserves letters of capitulation (CSEL 65 13390-14423; PL 10 Fragmenta); Athanasius says he gave way after two years (HA 38917-38961); Jerome's Chronicle says "taedio victus exilii" (45536); Sozomen IV.15 and Theodoret II.17 (33240, 9854) soften it; the Avellana Gesta tell the Roman side (5040). `contested_claim` material; `formation_confidence`: Contested.
- **The lapse of Hosius at Sirmium 357.** Athanasius HA (Hosius at 38256-38702, the Sirmium passage not checked to the line); the Libellus precum's hostile account (Avellana 5796-5853); Hilary De Synodis (7740 on, not checked to the line). Contested in degree, documented in fact.

## 6. Reception and memory, by tradition

- **Greek/Byzantine:** Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret (fifth century, Nicene); Athanasius (contemporary, absent); Philostorgius (Eunomian).
- **Latin, Gallic and Aquitanian:** Hilary (eyewitness at Seleucia, 359-361), Phoebadius (participant), Sulpicius (c. 403), Jerome's Chronicle (380-381) and Dialogue (c. 379), the councils of Paris 360-361 (Hefele 16939; the Paris letter is in Hilary's fragments, CSEL 65, not checked to the line).
- **Latin, Roman:** Damasus's synodical act (Theodoret 10196); the Avellana (383-384 and after); Liberius's letters.
- **Latin, Danubian and Gothic Homoian:** Auxentius of Durostorum and Maximinus (Kauffmann); the law of 386 (CTh 6889).
- **Syriac Orthodox:** Michael the Syrian (58036-58212); Zachariah Rhetor's anathema on Rimini (5805), sixth century. Bar Hebraeus vol. I: "Seleuciae" at 6885 is a single search hit not read in context.
- **Church of the East:** the Chronicle of Seert on Macedonius (13724). Mari: nothing on 359-360 found by search.
- **Coptic:** John of Nikiu's chapter on "the exile of Liberius and the holy bishops who were with him through the evil devices of the Arians" (1101-1111; text 4722-4776); the History of the Patriarchs on Athanasius's exiles without naming the councils (28113-28136); the Synaxarium, 7 Bachons (PO 16 18317-18421). None names Ariminum.
- **Armenian, Georgian, Ethiopian:** none located (Moses of Khoren, Langlois tome II, the Ethiopian Synaxarium vols. I-II searched).

## 9. Gaps and escalations

- The acts of both councils are lost; no vendored text can restore them. The story must build the proceedings from the letters, the creeds and the five narratives.
- No Homoian narrative of the councils in its own words exists; the Homoian side speaks through its creeds, Germinius's letter, Auxentius and Maximinus. Enough for part 5.
- Nothing for Mark.
