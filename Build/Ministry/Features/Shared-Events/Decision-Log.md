# Shared Events — decision log

Append-only. Newest last.

## 2026-10-17 — Why this workstream exists

Reading the Atlas stories and family tree, Mark saw that the stories of councils and
decision points outside a single tradition were missing. A survey found that only 12 of
292 census movements are built, and that most events between traditions are told
nowhere. The Great Schism, Ephesus, the Crusades, Marburg, Westminster, Azusa Street and
Vatican II are among them. Ephesus and Chalcedon are missing even from the census
keyEvents.

A correction is on record. The site-editing thread first quoted the Atlas log as Mark
saying the Great Schism was "not for us to worry about as this is a map". The log
(`Build/Ministry/Features/Atlas-World-Map/Decision-Log.md`, 2026-08-02 entry) has him
saying the schism's lived depth belongs to world-building, not to the map thread. Mark:
the Great Schism "is an essential story to tell".

## 2026-10-17 — How an event is told (Mark)

- **Option C, told outside the traditions.** Mark chose a fuller account outside the
  worlds, placed like the map's existing gap stories, plus how each tradition remembers
  it. Options A (each tradition's own account side by side) and B (one plain story only)
  were set aside.
- **A new map mark.** It shows the event involved or changed more than one tradition.
- **The same bar.** The same academic rigor, source documents and craft as everything
  else.
- **A new voice.** The voice is made after the events are chosen.
- **Pattern.** The account follows why, who, topics, decisions, disagreements and
  legacy.
- **Stories from the sources.** Mark asked to add unique stories from inside the council
  sources.
- **Legends.** Mark asked that a few legends with no source be addressed, such as
  Nicholas striking a heretic at Nicaea. A legend is never told as a true story. It is
  explained in a note on a story, honestly and rigorously.
  - Rules proposed by the thread:
    - Show the silence of the contemporary sources from our own files.
    - Name where the legend first appears only when we hold and cite that text.
    - Say "no source from the time records it", not "it never happened".
    - Treat the tradition's devotion with respect.
  - Open question: legend notes only where a legend is well known, or a search in every
    event.
- **Open question:** whether "how each tradition remembers it" is the last part, as in
  the README.

## 2026-10-17 — The list and the sourcing (Mark)

- **All 77 candidates stay.** Built in order of source readiness.
- **Pilot.** The Great Schism was the first pilot proposed, but no source for it is
  vendored. Nicaea was then recommended: its sources are strong, and the Nicholas legend
  tests the legend rule.
- **A Fable research library thread** identifies sources for every event, with the same
  depth, breadth and standards as the tradition builds.
- **Later sources are welcome** where they show how an event was applied or remembered.
  Their date and standpoint are named; they never stand in for evidence of the event
  itself.

## 2026-10-17 — Non-English sources are primary material (Mark)

As with the other Library sources, non-English documents that are public domain or openly
licensed can be used as primary source material. That covers critical editions and
original-language texts: Greek, Latin, Syriac, Coptic, Armenian, Georgian, Ge'ez, Arabic,
German, Dutch, Czech and others. The sourcing thread searches for them actively, especially
for the non-Western events and the dissenting voices, which English translations often leave
out. The Library's existing rules for language tags and mixed-language files apply
(`cic/texts/README.md`, `cic/texts/INTAKE.md`). Relayed to the sourcing thread by the
site-editing session on 2026-10-17; it restates the Library ruling of 2026-09-25 in
`Build/worlds/_cross-world/LIBRARY-DECISION-LOG.md` for this workstream.

## 2026-10-17 — Era 2 sourcing opened; dossier shape for events

The source-research thread began with Era 2 (Imperial Church, 312-451), Nicaea first as
the pilot. Each event gets a Source Readiness Dossier at
`Build/Ministry/Features/Shared-Events/dossiers/<event-id>_Source_Readiness_Dossier.md`,
adapted from the Library's dossier template to the eight parts of an event story, with
fixed sections: what the Library held; what was vendored; what the sources can carry part
by part; the dissenting side in its own words; legends (contemporary silence shown from our
files, earliest text held that carries the legend, or "not located"); reception and memory
by tradition; verified leads not fetched; checked and closed; gaps and escalations;
corpus-map notes. The Nicaea dossier is the exemplar. Rights are verified from the host
record or the file's own title page; what each OCR actually recovered (which scripts) is
stated in every header, since several Google scans recover only the Latin-script column.

## 2026-10-17 — Two open questions closed (Mark)

Both open questions in the entry "How an event is told" are settled.

1. **Order of the parts.** "How each tradition remembers it" is the last part of each event
   story, after the stories from the sources. The README's order stands.
2. **Legend notes.** Only where a legend is well known. No hunt for obscure legends in every
   event. For each well-known legend the sourcing stays as the rules require: the
   contemporary sources that show the silence, and the text where it first appears if one
   can be cited.

Relayed to the sourcing thread by the site-editing session on 2026-10-17.
