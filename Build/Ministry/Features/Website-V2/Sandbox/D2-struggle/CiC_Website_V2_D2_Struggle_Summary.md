# Website V2 — D2 Struggle Summary

**What this is:** the close of D2. Five directions, five independent Opus
adversarial reviews, five independent Fable defenses (one per direction,
each author seeing only its own review). This is the record the charter
asks for at this gate — survivors, kills with reasons, what the fight
taught — for Mark to read before he picks. It does not pick for him.
Sandbox artifact, not a deliverable.

---

## 1. The verdicts, direction by direction

Every direction died as a whole-site homepage. Every direction's own
author, given one honest defense, conceded that verdict in substance —
none argued the whole-site kill was wrong. What differs is what each
one asks to carry forward, and each ask is specific, named, and
independently re-verified (mostly by the defending author re-measuring
their own files in the same headless browser the reviewer used).

### 01 — Editorial / Long-Form Storytelling
**Dies as the spine.** Cause of death, stated precisely by its own
defense: front-matter ordering (chronology, not conversation-readiness)
and one wrong door (the primary CTA points at an unbuilt app surface
while the one working link is styled invisible at 1.196:1 contrast).
**Survives:** the Representative chapter page as an inner-page
pattern — provenance → "Where we are quiet" → essay → the tradition's
own hardest starter questions first → interview door — plus the
sidenote/citation implementation whole, the "we" register, and the
verified-against-data discipline.

### 02 — Atlas-First Spatial
**Dies as a homepage** — "the map is the doorway," and the bet lost:
a map answers *where/when*, Mark's heart audience arrives with a
*what/why*. **Survives:** a sharpened, twelve-item rework brief for the
real `atlas-v3.html` page (not the homepage) — including a "never dim a
portrait" rule that also indicts the live Atlas's own 18% dimming — plus
one narrow homepage component (a built-traditions map band) proposed
for *under* whatever question-first screen D3 builds, not in place of it.

### 03 — Institutional-Professional / Scholarly Authority
**Dies as a homepage direction** — its own section order ranks the
academic above Mark's stated heart audience, and no resequencing closed
that gap. **Survives, and its author argues this is not salvage but the
direction's actual right home:** `tradition.html` as a *second-screen*
thesis — the collection → assembly → trust → chair sequence is real and
well-built, it was just placed first when it belongs one click deeper.
Also survives: the AI-disclosure block, the confidence-glyph set, the
contrast table, and the "declared limits, stated with dignity" pattern.

### 04 — Quiet-Liturgical
**Dies outright, including its premise** — the most decisive concession
of the five. Its own author found, unprompted, that the Brand
Guidelines' Never list already forbade "Christianese" and that its own
rubric line ("Silence is kept") is a verbatim liturgical phrase — this
direction broke a brand rule that predates Mark's non-religious-feel
ruling. Also found that the constitution's stillness rule and its
door-first S0 rule were never actually in tension in the source text;
the direction invented a conflict that wasn't there, and neither of its
two pages carries a "Start with your question" door at all.
**Survives:** not as a visual identity, but as an extractable
*discipline* — no reveal-on-scroll, no hover lift, no gratuitous
transitions, a measured contrast floor, one action per screen — any
other direction could adopt, plus its seven-place chronological list
and its seat-picker mechanic as reusable parts.

### 05 — Product-Led Feature Clarity
**Dies in its D1 form** — its own "refusal table" is convicted by its
own thesis; the actual page skeleton is a SaaS-marketing homepage in
full, including a fake chat composer in the hero. **Survives, argued
for a broader scope than "extract the method":** the inner-page
anatomy (`meet-chloe.html` as a template for all seven traditions) and
the homepage skeleton itself, reordered under a real question-first
door — its author points out the existing UX constitution *already
specifies* "Start with your question" as a co-equal S0 door, and this
direction only ever organized around the other one. Also surfaced a
live-app fact for Mark to rule on: the fleet's real personal starter
questions (23 of them, including two naming doubt directly) are already
served to every visitor inside a live conversation, but are all flagged
`canon_status: seed` in the app's own schema — unresolved whether
seed-status material is cleared for a public marketing surface.

## 2. What the fight taught

**One finding, independently reached five separate times, unprompted in
four of the five cases:** every single direction's own defense — not
just the reviews attacking it — concluded that the homepage's real,
missing first move is a fast, personal way to ask a hard question,
before chronology, before a map, before a method, before a threshold
ritual, before a feature grid. This isn't a synthesis I'm imposing after
the fact: it is what five independently-reasoning authors, defending
five different philosophies against five different attacks, converged
on anyway. And it isn't a new idea — the existing UX constitution
(`CiC_Full_UX_Design_V1_0.md` §5.1) already designs a co-equal "Start
with your question" door at S0. **None of the five D1 directions built
it as a real, prominent homepage entry point.** That is the single
clearest, most load-bearing finding of the whole D2 phase.

**Two cross-direction technical findings, true regardless of what wins:**
the D1 text-contrast finding (several brand colors are unsafe as *text*)
extends to dark-mode *graphical* elements too (rings, underlines) — at
least three directions independently measured the same category of
failure; and every direction that touched dark mode found real,
specific breaks in it, which the brand record has always flagged as
deferred and unshipped — D3 needs to either commit to a real dark
register with its own audit, or design the whole site to not need one
yet, not inherit five different ad hoc attempts.

**One process finding:** the defense round was worth doing in full, not
as a formality. Every single defense found something the review missed
— usually against itself. That is what "authors get one defense" is
for, and it held up in five-for-five here.

## 3. The real choice in front of Mark

**No direction survives whole.** That is not a failure of D1 or D2 —
five genuinely different, well-built bets were each tested hard and
each fell in a different, specific way, exactly what a real divergent/
struggle process is supposed to produce. But it does mean the D2→D3
handoff isn't "pick one and freeze it." There are, honestly, a few real
paths from here, and this coordinator is not picking among them:

**A. Authorize a hybrid direction**, per the charter's own D2 rule
("hybridization is allowed only after critique — a hybrid is a new
direction with a named parentage, and it too gets an Opus review").
Fable would synthesize one new homepage direction from the named
survivors above — most obviously a question-first S0 door (the
convergent finding), routing into an inner-page pattern (institutional's
`tradition.html`-as-second-screen or editorial's Representative-chapter,
or a merge of the two), the Atlas rebuilt separately per its own
twelve-item brief rather than as the homepage, and quiet-liturgical's
restraint discipline governing motion/pacing throughout. This new
hybrid gets its own Opus review before D3 converges it — one more
round, not skipping the struggle.

**B. Pick the single direction whose remaining shape you find most
compelling** and ask for a targeted repair pass on just its measured
defects plus a real question-first door — narrower than A, faster, but
built on one bet rather than the field's best pieces.

**C. Treat this as real information and re-diverge** — commission a
fresh D1 round, this time informed by everything D2 found (especially
the question-first finding), rather than assembling from what already
exists. Most expensive, truest to "not a jump to decisions," and the
charter does explicitly permit sending directions back to be remade
rather than only forward to hybrids.

**My recommendation, with the reasoning, both craft and heart:** (A).
The five-for-five convergence on a question-first door is too clean a
signal to set aside, and it isn't a compromise pieced together by a
committee — it's what your own charter already designed at S0, that
none of these five happened to build. Re-diverging (C) would very
likely rediscover the same thing at real additional cost, since it's
already this legible in the record. And a single-direction repair (B)
throws away real, independently-verified craft in the other four —
institutional's `tradition.html`, editorial's chapter pattern, and
quiet-liturgical's discipline are not consolation prizes, three
different reviewers and three different defenses converged on their
value. The heart reasoning: your stated heart is the person with a
hard question who wants to put it down, not explore a map or read a
method first. A hybrid built around a real question-first door, tested
once more before it freezes, is the most direct way to build a site
that actually opens with that person rather than asking them to wait.

But this is a real decision, not a formality — you may see something in
the five reviews or defenses I haven't weighed correctly, or want a
sixth voice, or want to sit with the record longer before choosing.
That is exactly what this gate is for.
