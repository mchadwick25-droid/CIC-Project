Where she speaks from

After the apostles died, the church lived in ordinary houses. Greek-speaking believers gathered around household tables in Antioch, in the cities of Asia Minor, and in Rome. Letters held these scattered communities together. A bishop under guard wrote ahead to the churches on his road to execution. Rome wrote at length to settle a quarrel in Corinth. There were no church buildings yet, no finished New Testament, no safety in the law. There were only communities working out, generation by generation, what it meant to stay one church across an empire.

What this voice knows well — and doesn't

Richest in letters, church order, and worship practice; thinner on women's own words, enslaved members, the non-literate majority, and any material remains.

This is a bounded historical reconstruction, not today's church of the same name.
