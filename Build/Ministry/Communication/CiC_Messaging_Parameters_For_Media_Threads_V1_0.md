# Messaging Parameters — For Any Thread Producing Participant-Facing Copy

**V1.0 · 2026-07-19 · From the Branding & Messaging thread, at Mark's direction, following a real catch on a demonstration-video draft.** Read this before writing script, voiceover, on-screen text, or any copy describing the experience of using Church in Conversation. It's short on purpose — the full reasoning lives in the Kit and QuickRef if you want it.

## The catch that produced this

A recent draft read: *"You're talking with them, not texting."* Two separate problems in one short phrase, and both are binding rules now:

### 1. Say what it is. Don't deny what nobody asked about.

*"Not texting"* answers a question the copy itself raised by asking it. This is the brand's standing rule (positive framing): **state the fact, once, and stop.** *"You're in conversation with them"* needs no negative tail.

**The only exception:** a direct question actually gets a direct answer (an FAQ, a Q&A moment) — "no" is honest when someone asked. The rule targets *unprompted* denial, not honest answers to real questions.

**One thing this does NOT touch:** clarifying contrast that names what kind of thing something is — *"a doorway, not a home"* — is fine. That's a category, not a denial. Nobody suspected CiC was trying to be a home; the line just says what it is. If your copy is naming a role by contrast, keep it. If it's denying a suspicion the copy just created, cut it.

### 2. Say "conversation," not "talking." Don't over-humanize — or under-humanize.

"Talking" leans toward literal spoken human presence. "Conversation" is the right word because it's **medium-agnostic** — it covers text and voice, live and asynchronous, without claiming or denying either. Use it as the default noun for the interaction.

The goal is a **bridge**, not a retreat to clinical language:

| Too human — avoid | The bridge — use this | Too clinical — also avoid |
|---|---|---|
| "talking with them" | "in conversation with them" | "querying the system" |
| "hearing them say…" | "the conversation covers…" | "the model generates…" |
| "listen to their answer" | "read what they say" | "the output returns…" |
| "you're talking to a real voice" | "you're in conversation with a representative voice" | "you're interacting with an interface" |

You don't need to say "AI" or "computer" to avoid overclaiming — just don't reach for words that imply live human speech when the honest word ("conversation") already does the job.

### 3. Watch the shape, not just the words: minimize "not X, it's Y"

Even when the content is fine, stacking negation-then-correction ("it's not just a chat, it's a real encounter") is a recognizable tic of AI-generated writing. For a brand built on human craft and historical rigor, writing in that pattern undercuts the very claim being made — it reads as generated, not crafted. If a sentence pairs a denial with a correction, try cutting the denial first and see if the correction alone is stronger. It usually is.

## Quick self-check before you publish

1. Does any sentence deny something nobody would have suspected? Cut the denial, keep the fact.
2. Does the copy say "talking" where "conversation" would be more accurate? Swap it.
3. Count the "not X, it's Y" constructions. If there's more than one in a script, at least one is probably cuttable.
4. Read it once more for the Kit's five voice traits (witness not recruiting, rigorous not distracting, accessible not shallow, technology not seen, relational not human) — full detail in `CiC_Messaging_Branding_Kit_V0_1_DRAFT.md`.
