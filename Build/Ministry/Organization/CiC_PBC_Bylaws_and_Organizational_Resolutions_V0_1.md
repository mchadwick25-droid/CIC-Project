## Part A — Bylaws of Faithways Studio, Inc.

### Article 1 — Offices
The principal office of the corporation shall be as designated in the Articles of Incorporation,
or as the board may from time to time determine.

### Article 2 — Shareholders
2.1. The corporation shall have a single class of stock — one class of common stock, held
initially by Mark Chadwick and Susan Chadwick in equal shares.
2.2. Shareholder action may be taken at a meeting or, in lieu of a meeting, by unanimous written
consent signed by all shareholders — the ordinary and expected method for a two-shareholder
company.
2.3. An annual shareholder meeting is not required to be held in person; a written consent
satisfying applicable requirements of the Colorado Business Corporation Act suffices.

### Article 3 — Board of Directors
3.1. **Number.** The board shall consist of not fewer than one and not more than five directors,
the exact number fixed from time to time by resolution of the shareholders. **Initially, two
directors: Mark Chadwick and Susan Chadwick. Mark Chadwick serves as Chair of the Board; in the
event the directors cannot reach majority agreement on a board-level matter, the Chair's vote is
decisive.**
3.2. **Meetings.** The board may act at a meeting (in person, by phone, or by video) or by
unanimous written consent in lieu of a meeting.
3.3. **Quorum and voting.** A quorum consists of a majority of directors then in office. Except
where the Articles of Incorporation require a different vote (see Article IV of the Articles,
the 4/5-of-shares mission-lock protection — a shareholder-level vote, not a director-level one),
action of the board requires the affirmative vote of a majority of directors present at a meeting
at which a quorum exists.
3.4. **Deadlock resolution.** Deadlock on an ordinary board matter is resolved by the Chair's
tie-breaking vote (Article 3.1). This does not extend to any matter requiring a shareholder-level
vote under Article IV of the Articles of Incorporation or the companion Shareholder Agreement —
including any sale, merger, or other change-of-control transaction — which always requires both
shareholders' agreement regardless of who chairs the board.
3.5. **The public-benefit balancing duty.** Per C.R.S. § 7-101-506 and Article IV of the
Articles of Incorporation, the board shall manage the corporation's affairs in a manner that
balances shareholder pecuniary interest, the interests of those materially affected by the
corporation's conduct, and the corporation's specific public benefit.

### Article 4 — Officers
4.1. The officers of the corporation shall be a President, a Treasurer, and such other officers
(including a Secretary) as the board may from time to time appoint. One person may hold more than
one office.
4.2. **The President** is the chief executive officer, responsible for general management and
day-to-day operations, and may sign contracts and other instruments on the corporation's behalf
unless the board directs otherwise.
4.3. **The Treasurer** is responsible for the corporation's funds and financial records — bank
and payment-processor account management, bookkeeping, and preparation or oversight of tax
filings — and may sign checks and authorize transactions in the ordinary course of business.
4.4. Officers serve at the pleasure of the board and may be removed, with or without cause, by
board action at any time.

### Article 5 — Indemnification
The corporation shall indemnify its directors and officers to the fullest extent permitted by the
Colorado Business Corporation Act for actions taken in good faith on the corporation's behalf.
*(Standard protective language — worth keeping even for a small, closely-held company.)*

### Article 6 — Amendment
These bylaws may be amended by a majority vote of the board or by shareholder written consent,
except that no amendment may alter or circumvent Article IV of the Articles of Incorporation
(the Foundational Commitments / mission lock), which is amendable only as that Article itself
provides.

initial board), then the newly seated board acts (to adopt bylaws, elect officers, and issue
stock).*

### Step 2 — Action of the Board of Directors (Mark Chadwick and Susan Chadwick), in lieu of the first board meeting

**RESOLVED**, that the Bylaws set forth in Part A above are adopted as the Bylaws of the
corporation.

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that the following officers are elected, to serve at the pleasure of the
board:

- **President:** Mark Chadwick
- **Treasurer & Secretary:** Susan Chadwick

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that the corporation is authorized to issue **3,500,000 shares of common
stock to Mark Chadwick and 3,500,000 shares of common stock to Susan Chadwick** (an equal,
50/50 split, issuing 7,000,000 of the 10,000,000 shares authorized under Article II of the

first), in exchange for **cash payment of $20.00 each ($40.00 total)**, payable to the
corporation upon issuance.

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that the corporation's shares shall be uncertificated; the corporation
shall maintain a share register reflecting ownership, and no paper stock certificates shall be
issued unless the board later determines otherwise, in which case any certificate issued shall
bear the legend required by C.R.S. § 7-101-505.

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that the President and Treasurer are each authorized, acting individually,
to open bank and payment-processing accounts (including with Stripe) in the corporation's name,
and to execute such documents as are reasonably necessary to give effect to the foregoing
resolutions.

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that the board has determined that $40.00 total ($20.00 from each of Mark
Chadwick and Susan Chadwick), or approximately $0.0000057 per share for the 7,000,000 shares
issued, is a fair price for stock in a newly formed corporation with no
operating history, revenue, or material assets; upon receipt of this amount, the shares shall be
validly issued, fully paid, and non-assessable, and this issuance date establishes the original
issuance date for purposes of the holding period under IRC §1202 and for federal and state tax
reporting. *(Paying cash for the shares, rather than issuing them for services, avoids any
question about the fair-market-value of services rendered under IRC §83. This is a separate
transaction from, and does not address, the separate question of formally assigning CiC's
pre-incorporation IP — code, content, methodology, and related assets, presently owned by Mark
Chadwick personally — into the corporation, which is addressed in the companion IP Assignment
Agreement as a capital contribution, not part of this cash purchase.)*

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that shares are issued subject to, and with notice of, the corporation's
status as a public benefit corporation under C.R.S. § 7-101-503; the President and Treasurer are
directed to send each shareholder the written notice of uncertificated share issuance required
by C.R.S. § 7-106-207, including the public-benefit-corporation disclosure required by C.R.S.
§ 7-101-505, within a reasonable time after issuance.

**FURTHER RESOLVED**, that the corporation's fiscal year shall end **December 31**.

*(Signatures: Mark Chadwick, Director; Susan Chadwick, Director — both required, since board
action under these bylaws needs majority-of-quorum agreement and with two directors that means
both, absent a formal meeting.)*
