ADDITIONAL PROVISIONS
Faithways Studio, Inc. - Colorado Public Benefit Corporation
Attachment to Articles of Incorporation


ARTICLE III - PURPOSE AND SPECIFIC PUBLIC BENEFIT

The corporation may engage in any lawful act or activity for which corporations may be organized under the Colorado Business Corporation Act.

Pursuant to C.R.S. Section 7-101-503, the corporation is a public benefit corporation, and its specific public benefit -- a religious and educational public benefit within the meaning of that section -- is to create safe spaces to explore faith and the story of Jesus. This specific public benefit includes, and is defined together with, the Foundational Commitments set forth in Article IV, which are integral to it.

The corporation may pursue this benefit directly, through products it builds and operates (presently Church in Conversation), or by licensing its systems, technology, or methodology to others who share this purpose.


ARTICLE IV - FOUNDATIONAL COMMITMENTS (MISSION LOCK)

The following commitments are constitutive of the corporation's specific public benefit -- applying equally to products and experiences the corporation operates directly and to any systems, methodologies, or frameworks it develops, licenses, or otherwise makes available to others in service of the same purpose -- and the board of directors shall manage or direct the business and affairs of the corporation in a manner that balances the pecuniary interests of the shareholders, the best interests of those materially affected by the corporation's conduct, and this specific public benefit, as required by C.R.S. Section 7-101-506.

These are principles to guide the board's good-faith judgment, not a mechanical checklist -- each names something the corporation takes seriously and is accountable to, while trusting the board to apply it reasonably to circumstances as they actually arise. Nothing in this paragraph limits the enforceability of the amendment-protection provision below, or affects the status of these commitments as constitutive of the corporation's specific public benefit under Article III.

1. Genuine exploration, not persuasion. The corporation's products and experiences shall be designed to support honest exploration and understanding, and to be safe places to ask real questions -- including questions of doubt -- without pressure toward any predetermined conclusion, position, or outcome.

2. Transparency and accountability. The corporation will never represent its work as more certain, more reviewed, or more validated than it actually is. Whatever grounds a product's claims -- its sources, its confidence, its limitations -- shall be visible to every participant; and where independent review is claimed, it shall be real, appropriate to the product's own content, and honestly disclosed.

3. No engagement optimization. No product or system of the corporation shall be designed or configured to maximize engagement duration, return frequency, or emotional dependency as an objective in itself.

4. Outside funding never alters the corporation's purpose or convictions. The board decides what the corporation builds and how; funding may support that choice, but is never, by itself, a reason to make it. No gift, payment, sponsorship, or investment shall alter, soften, or override the corporation's purpose (Article III) or the commitments of this Article IV.

Amendment protection. This Article IV may be amended only by the affirmative vote of at least four-fifths (4/5) of the outstanding shares entitled to vote, voting together as a single class. This four-fifths requirement applies to any amendment of this Article IV or Article III, and to any merger, conversion, share exchange, sale of all or substantially all of the corporation's assets, or other transaction or corporate action that would eliminate, alter, circumvent, or render inoperative the specific public benefit described in Article III or the commitments set forth in this Article IV, in addition to any approval of the board of directors required by law.


ARTICLE V - DIRECTORS

The number, terms, election, and qualifications of directors shall be as set forth in the bylaws. The corporation is not required by statute to maintain independent directors or a designated benefit officer.

Pursuant to C.R.S. Section 7-101-506(3), a disinterested director's failure to satisfy the duty set forth in Section 7-101-506(1) shall not, by itself, constitute a violation of that director's duties under Sections 7-108-401 and 7-108-402 or Article 109 of the Colorado Business Corporation Act.

To the fullest extent permitted by the Colorado Business Corporation Act, no director of the corporation shall be personally liable to the corporation or its shareholders for monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty as a director, except for liability that may not be eliminated or limited under applicable law.
