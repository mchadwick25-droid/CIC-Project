# Attorney Review Package — Faithways Studio, Inc. (Round 2)

**Cover memo for the Colorado corporate/tax attorney consult** · 2026-07-21

> This is the second review pass. Round 1 (two independent reviews — one attorney-lens, one
> mission/business-development-lens) found the Articles were "facially fileable but not
> filing-ready to do what they're meant to do," and surfaced a specific, fixable gap: the
> mission-lock (Article IV) didn't survive a merger, conversion, or asset sale, only a direct
> "amendment." All identified fixes have now been drafted and incorporated. This package bundles
> the four documents as they now stand, plus what changed and why, so a reviewer isn't starting
> from zero.

## The four documents in this package

   includes Articles I–IX (a new Article IX, Severability, was added this round).
2. **`CiC_PBC_Bylaws_and_Organizational_Resolutions_V0_1.md`** — governance rules + the
   incorporator/board resolutions that seat the board, adopt bylaws, and issue stock.
3. **`CiC_PBC_IP_Assignment_Agreement_V0_1_DRAFT.md`** — new this round. Assigns CiC's

4. **`CiC_PBC_Shareholder_Buy-Sell_Agreement_V0_1_DRAFT.md`** — new this round. Addresses the
   50/50, no-tie-breaker deadlock risk between the two shareholders (Mark and Susan Chadwick),
   including a mediation-first escalation ladder and a "shotgun" buy-sell backstop.

## What changed since Round 1, and why

**Article III (Purpose).** The prior language named the corporation as "a center of research,
methodology, and expertise for others... through licensing, advisory, or collaborative
relationships." Both Round 1 reviewers independently flagged this as language that could
disqualify QSBS (IRC §1202) treatment, since §1202 excludes consulting businesses and businesses
whose principal asset is the reputation/skill of individuals. **Replaced** with: *"The corporation
may pursue this benefit directly, through products it builds and operates (presently Church in
Conversation), or by licensing its systems, technology, or methodology to others who share this
purpose."* Reframes the future licensing option as a product/IP model rather than an
advisory/services model. **Open question for this round: does this fully resolve the §1202 concern,
or does any residual risk remain given the corporation's likely small size and founder-dependent
operations?**

**Article III + Article IV (the merger/conversion survivability fix).** The attorney reviewer's
central finding: Colorado already requires two-thirds-of-each-class approval for a PBC-status
conversion, cash-out merger, or asset sale (C.R.S. § 7-101-504(4)) — but that protection attaches
to the Article III "specific public benefit" statement, not to Article IV, and runs at two-thirds,
not the four-fifths the founders chose. Two changes were made together to close this: (a) Article
III now states the specific public benefit "includes, and is defined together with, the
Foundational Commitments set forth in Article IV"; (b) Article IV's amendment-protection clause
now extends the four-fifths requirement beyond "amendment" to "any merger, conversion, share
exchange, sale of all or substantially all of the corporation's assets, or other transaction or
corporate action that would eliminate, alter, circumvent, or render inoperative" the specific
public benefit or Article IV's commitments — plus a clause confirming this is in addition to, not
in place of, any board-approval step otherwise required by law. **Open question: does this drafting
actually achieve the intended effect under Colorado law, and does it interact correctly with the
statutory § 7-101-504 framework rather than creating an ambiguity between the statutory 2/3 and the
charter's 4/5?**

**Article IV (the "checklist" framing).** Added one sentence after the existing "principles to
guide the board's good-faith judgment, not a mechanical checklist" language: *"Nothing in this
paragraph limits the enforceability of the amendment-protection provision below, or affects the
status of these commitments as constitutive of the corporation's specific public benefit under
Article III."* Intended to prevent that framing from later being read as evidence Article IV was
never meant to bind.

**Article IV (pronoun consistency).** Commitment 4's heading changed from "our purpose or
convictions" to "the corporation's purpose or convictions" for consistency with the rest of the
Article.

**Article V (Directors).** Two optional additions: the §7-101-506(3) safe harbor (a disinterested
director's failure to satisfy the balancing duty is not, by itself, a breach of the duty of
loyalty), and a director personal-liability limitation to the fullest extent Colorado law permits.

**Article IX (new — Severability).** Standard severability clause added, given Article IV's
novel drafting.

**Share issuance (Bylaws/Resolutions).** Changed from issuing all 10,000,000 authorized shares
(5,000,000/5,000,000) to issuing 7,000,000 (3,500,000/3,500,000), leaving 3,000,000 shares (30%)
authorized-but-unissued for a future hire, advisor grant, or investor without amending the
Articles. Also added: an explicit board determination that the shares issued for "services
rendered to date" have $0.00 fair market value given the corporation's lack of operating history,
revenue, or assets at issuance — with a note that this determination is scoped to the services
issuance only and does not resolve the separate IP-contribution question below. Also added the
§7-101-503(4)-required written notice of PBC status to share recipients before issuance.

**New: the IP assignment gap.** Both Round 1 reviews (the BD review explicitly, the attorney
review implicitly via the stock-for-services discussion) flagged that CiC's actual IP —
methodology, code, content, domains — was built by Mark personally before the corporation existed
and has never been formally transferred in. A first-pass IP Assignment Agreement has been drafted,
structured as a §351 contribution effective at incorporation. **Open question, explicitly flagged
in the draft itself: is this contribution properly separated from the "services rendered" stock
issuance, or does folding both into one $0-FMV determination create an inconsistency — i.e., can a
corporation simultaneously claim its founder-services stock is worth $0 while also receiving a
functioning, demonstrated product as a contribution?** This is the single most tax-technical open
item in the whole package and the one most in need of real scrutiny.

**New: the shareholder deadlock gap.** Both Round 1 reviews flagged the 50/50-board,
no-tie-breaker structure as riskiest exactly at an exit decision. A first-pass Shareholder
Agreement has been drafted: ROFR on transfers, a mediation-first escalation ladder, a shotgun
buy-sell mechanism as the deadlock backstop, and death/disability buyout provisions — with an
explicit subordination clause stating it cannot be used to circumvent Article IV.

## What was deliberately NOT changed, and why

- **The federal trademark registration for "Church in Conversation"** remains a separate,
  not-yet-executed action item (tracked outside this package) — the CO trade-name filing doesn't
  substitute for it, but it isn't part of the entity's formation documents themselves.
- **The S-corp vs. C-corp election** remains an open, separate IRS filing (Form 2553), not
  addressed in the Articles either way. Working assumption on record: stay a straight C-corp to
  preserve QSBS eligibility.
- **CiC's own product-level commitments** (always-free core access, external scholarly review)
  remain deliberately absent from Article IV — they were moved to a not-yet-formalized
  product-level document (the Organizational Covenant) so future products aren't bound to CiC's
  specific mechanics. Not resolved in this package.

## What this review should focus on

1. **Verify the Article III/IV fix actually works** — does it correctly fold Article IV into the
   statutory "specific public benefit" and correctly raise the § 7-101-504 floor, or did the
   drafting introduce a new ambiguity?

3. **Review the two new documents (IP assignment, shareholder agreement) on their own merits** —
   they did not exist at Round 1.
4. **Specifically weigh in on the stock-for-services-vs-IP-contribution valuation question** raised
