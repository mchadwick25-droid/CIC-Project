# Shareholder Agreement (Buy-Sell / Deadlock Provisions)

**Faithways Studio, Inc., a Colorado public benefit corporation** · Between Mark Chadwick and
Susan Chadwick, its two shareholders · Draft **V0.1** · 2026-07-21

> **Status: working agreement between Mark and Susan Chadwick.** Companion to the Articles of
> Incorporation and Bylaws/Organizational Resolutions. Kept deliberately simple and low-cost for
> a two-person, family-owned company. Ordinary board deadlock is resolved directly by the Chair's
> tie-breaking vote (Bylaws Article 3.1) — no outside mediation or arbitration process is needed.
> This Agreement's real job is the one thing the Bylaws' tie-breaker doesn't cover: what happens
> with a sale, and what happens with the shares if either of you dies. Not a substitute for
> personal estate planning (wills, and a trust later if the company becomes valuable), but nothing
> here is blocked on hiring an attorney.

---

## 1. Purpose

This Agreement supplements the Bylaws and does not modify or weaken Article IV of the Articles
of Incorporation (the Foundational Commitments / mission lock) in any way. Nothing in this
Agreement authorizes any action that Article IV's amendment-protection provision would otherwise
require a four-fifths (4/5) vote of shares to approve; where this Agreement and Article IV
conflict, Article IV controls.

For purposes of this Agreement, "change-of-control transaction" means any merger, conversion,
share exchange, sale of all or substantially all of the corporation's assets, dissolution, or
other transaction or corporate action described in Article IV of the Articles of Incorporation.

This Agreement governs only for so long as Mark Chadwick and Susan Chadwick are the sole
shareholders of the corporation. Any issuance or transfer of shares to a third party is
conditioned on that person executing a joinder to this Agreement, or on the shareholders amending
this Agreement to address the resulting ownership structure, before the issuance or transfer is
completed.

## 2. Transfer restrictions — right of first refusal

Neither shareholder may sell, gift, pledge, or otherwise transfer any shares to a third party
without first offering them to the other shareholder on the same price and terms ("**ROFR**").
The other shareholder has **45 days** to accept the offer, in whole or in part; if
declined or only partly taken up, the transferring shareholder may complete the transfer to the
originally proposed third party, on the same terms, within **90 days**, or the ROFR process
restarts.

Transfers to a spouse, child, or a trust for estate-planning purposes do not trigger a full
ROFR, but the transferee must agree in writing to be bound by this Agreement.

## 3. Deadlock resolution

Mark and Susan resolve ordinary disagreements directly, as they always have. For a board-level
matter, the Chair's tie-breaking vote (Bylaws Article 3.1) resolves any deadlock. This does not
extend to, and cannot be used to force, any sale, merger, dissolution, or other change-of-control
transaction, or any matter that would itself require the four-fifths vote under Article IV of the
Articles of Incorporation — those are governed exclusively by Section 4 below.

## 4. Change-of-control default, and buyout events

A sale, merger, dissolution, or other change-of-control transaction shall occur only with the
mutual consent of both shareholders. If mutual consent is not reached, the corporation shall
continue to operate under its current ownership;
neither shareholder may be compelled to sell their shares, and the corporation may not be
compelled to pursue such a transaction, as a result of this Agreement. A buyout of a
shareholder's shares under this Agreement occurs only upon the specific events set forth in
Section 5 (death or permanent disability), using the valuation method set forth in Section 6.

## 5. Death or permanent disability

This Agreement does not require a buyout on the death or permanent disability of either
shareholder. If one shareholder dies, that shareholder's shares pass to the surviving spouse, and
the corporation continues to operate as before. If both shareholders die, their shares pass to
their children (or as otherwise directed by their estate plans), remain subject to this
Agreement, and the corporation continues to operate. If the corporation's shares become
significantly valuable — comparable to how the shareholders may hold other significant assets,
such as their home, in a trust — the shareholders, or after both have passed their children, may
establish a trust to hold the shares, at whatever point they judge it worthwhile. Neither a trust
nor any other estate-planning arrangement is required by this Agreement, and none needs to be
decided now.

## 6. Valuation method

If the shareholders ever voluntarily agree to a buyout under this Agreement, share value is
whatever the shareholders agree to at that time, or, absent agreement, the value set by a single
independent appraiser both shareholders select. No valuation formula needs to be fixed in advance,
since no buyout is required or automatic under this Agreement.

## 7. Confidentiality and non-disparagement

Both shareholders agree to keep the terms of any dispute, mediation, or buyout confidential, and
not to disparage the corporation, its mission, or each other in connection with any transfer
under this Agreement.

## 8. Governing law and amendment

This Agreement is governed by Colorado law. It may be amended only by written agreement of both
shareholders, and no amendment may weaken or circumvent Article IV of the Articles of
Incorporation.
