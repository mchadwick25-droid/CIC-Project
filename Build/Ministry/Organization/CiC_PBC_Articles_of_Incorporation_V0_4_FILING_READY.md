## Article I — Name

The name of the corporation is **Faithways Studio, Inc.**

*(A corporate designator is legally required here, unlike the nonprofit path — C.R.S. § 7-90-601
requires a for-profit corporation's name to contain "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Company,"
"Limited," or an abbreviation (Corp., Inc., Co., Ltd.). "Inc." is the standard, least
brand-competing choice. Colloquially and in the trade-name/public-facing sense, the entity is
still "Faithways Studio" — the designator is a formal legal requirement, not something that needs
to appear in everyday use, marketing, or the "Church in Conversation" trade name itself.)*

*("Studio" chosen deliberately over "Technologies" — it fits the project's own voice (careful,
craft-oriented, not corporate-generic) and stays open to future formats beyond conversational AI
(e-learning, video) without narrowing the identity to "technology" specifically. If a different
permanent name ever emerges from further reflection, Colorado corporate name changes are a
simple, low-cost Articles of Amendment, available any time — nothing about filing under this name
now creates lock-in. Confirm this name's own availability with the same quick CO SOS check
already run for "Church in Conversation" before filing — a different, fresh check, since this is
a distinct candidate name.)*

*(Colorado no longer requires "PBC," "Public Benefit Corporation," or similar in the name
itself — that requirement became optional in 2017. Because the name omits it, the corporation
must give written notice of its public benefit corporation status to any person to whom it
issues shares, before the shares are issued. This is a one-line notice on the stock issuance/
subscription paperwork, not something that goes in the Articles themselves — flagged in Part D
of the filing-ready package for when shares are first issued.)*

**Trade name.** Once formed, the corporation will register **"Church in Conversation"** as a
Colorado trade name (d/b/a) — a separate, simple filing distinct from this entity-name
registration — and operate its current flagship product under that name. The existing CO SOS
name reservation for "Church in Conversation" (made earlier this thread) carries over cleanly to
this purpose; it doesn't need to be the entity's own legal name to remain useful. This is what
lets the flagship product's brand, and eventually its trademark, be sold or spun off later without
ever requiring the parent corporation itself to change its name.

## Article II — Type, Duration, and Authorized Shares

The corporation is a **public benefit corporation** organized under the Colorado Business
Corporation Act, C.R.S. § 7-101-501 *et seq.*, with perpetual duration.

The corporation is authorized to issue **10,000,000 shares of common stock**, no par value.
*(A standard, arbitrary founder-stage figure — authorized shares are a ceiling, not a count of
what's actually issued, and cost nothing extra to set higher than needed now. Attorney may
adjust; this figure is not load-bearing.)*

## Article III — Purpose and Specific Public Benefit

The corporation may engage in any lawful act or activity for which corporations may be organized
under the Colorado Business Corporation Act.

Pursuant to C.R.S. § 7-101-503, the corporation is a public benefit corporation, and its
specific public benefit — a religious and educational public benefit within the meaning of that
section — is to create safe spaces to explore faith and the story of Jesus. This specific public
benefit includes, and is defined together with, the Foundational Commitments set forth in
Article IV, which are integral to it.

The corporation may pursue this benefit directly, through products it builds and operates
(presently Church in Conversation), or by licensing its systems, technology, or methodology to
others who share this purpose.

## Article IV — Foundational Commitments (Mission Lock)

The following commitments are constitutive of the corporation's specific public benefit —
applying equally to products and experiences the corporation operates directly and to any
systems, methodologies, or frameworks it develops, licenses, or otherwise makes available to
others in service of the same purpose — and the board of directors shall manage or direct the
business and affairs of the corporation in a manner that balances the pecuniary interests of the
shareholders, the best interests of those materially affected by the corporation's conduct, and
this specific public benefit, as required by C.R.S. § 7-101-506.

These are principles to guide the board's good-faith judgment, not a mechanical checklist — each
names something the corporation takes seriously and is accountable to, while trusting the board
to apply it reasonably to circumstances as they actually arise. Nothing in this paragraph limits
the enforceability of the amendment-protection provision below, or affects the status of these
commitments as constitutive of the corporation's specific public benefit under Article III.

1. **Genuine exploration, not persuasion.** The corporation's products and experiences shall be
   designed to support honest exploration and understanding, and to be safe places to ask real
   questions — including questions of doubt — without pressure toward any predetermined
   conclusion, position, or outcome.
2. **Transparency and accountability.** The corporation will never represent its work as more
   certain, more reviewed, or more validated than it actually is. Whatever grounds a product's
   claims — its sources, its confidence, its limitations — shall be visible to every participant;
   and where independent review is claimed, it shall be real, appropriate to the product's own
   content, and honestly disclosed.
3. **No engagement optimization.** No product or system of the corporation shall be designed or
   configured to maximize engagement duration, return frequency, or emotional dependency as an
   objective in itself.
4. **Outside funding never alters the corporation's purpose or convictions.** The board decides what the
   corporation builds and how; funding may support that choice, but is never, by itself, a reason
   to make it. No gift, payment, sponsorship, or investment shall alter, soften, or override the
   corporation's purpose (Article III) or the commitments of this Article IV.

*(Deliberately stated at the level of principle, not mechanism — see the 2026-07-21 revision
note below. CiC's own specific operating choices, including always-free core access and
external scholarly review of its historical reconstructions specifically, remain real and
serious commitments; they now live in the Organizational Covenant / product-level commitments
for "Church in Conversation" specifically, rather than being hard-locked at the parent-entity
level for every future product to inherit.)*

**Amendment protection.** This Article IV may be amended only by the affirmative vote of at least
four-fifths (4/5) of the outstanding shares entitled to vote, voting together as a single class.
This four-fifths requirement applies to any amendment of this Article IV or Article III, and to
any merger, conversion, share exchange, sale of all or substantially all of the corporation's
assets, or other transaction or corporate action that would eliminate, alter, circumvent, or
render inoperative the specific public benefit described in Article III or the commitments set
forth in this Article IV, in addition to any approval of the board of directors required by law.
*(This is the PBC-appropriate equivalent of the nonprofit draft's director-supermajority
protection — since governance here runs through shareholders, not a mandated independent board.
Extending the requirement beyond "amendment" closes a gap the independent attorney review
identified: Colorado's own statutory backstop for mergers/conversions/asset sales (C.R.S.
§ 7-101-504(4)) runs at two-thirds of each class, not four-fifths, and attaches to the Article III
specific-public-benefit statement — which is why Article III now explicitly folds Article IV's
commitments into that statement. While the corporation has two shareholders at 50/50, this
provision is self-binding rather than externally enforced; its real protective value activates
once a third holder or an unequal split exists — a future investor, dilution, or a transfer of
shares. Attorney: confirm enforceability and drafting mechanics under Title 7.)*

## Article V — Directors

The number, terms, election, and qualifications of directors shall be as set forth in the
bylaws. The corporation is not required by statute to maintain independent directors or a
designated benefit officer; **whether to build a voluntary advisory board or independent
governance structure beyond the sole founder is a deliberate choice for the corporation to make,
not one imposed by this entity form** — see the companion PBC structure analysis for that
open question.

Pursuant to C.R.S. § 7-101-506(3), a disinterested director's failure to satisfy the duty set
forth in § 7-101-506(1) shall not, by itself, constitute a violation of that director's duties
under §§ 7-108-401 and 7-108-402 or Article 109 of the Colorado Business Corporation Act.

To the fullest extent permitted by the Colorado Business Corporation Act, no director of the
corporation shall be personally liable to the corporation or its shareholders for monetary
damages for breach of fiduciary duty as a director, except for liability that may not be
eliminated or limited under applicable law.

| | Nonprofit draft (V0.2) | This PBC draft |
|---|---|---|
| IRS-required limitations (Article V, private inurement, lobbying, political limits) | Required | **Removed — not applicable.** A PBC is a normal taxable corporation; none of the 501(c)(3) restrictions apply. |

### Article III broadened: the durable purpose, and the think-tank/methodology function (2026-07-21 revision)

Mark's direction: not a rename — "Faithways Studio" stands — but a check that the purpose and
mission-lock actually deliver a durable, non-limiting mission statement as technology and the
financial environment change over the next five-plus years, and that the corporation isn't
accidentally boxed into only building and operating its own products. Article III now states the
core benefit directly — a safe, unbiased place to explore faith and ask real questions,
encountering a bigger picture of Jesus and His Church than previously known, using technology and
built experience as the means — and explicitly adds a distinct activity type: **the corporation
may act as a center of research, methodology, and expertise for others building similar systems**,
via licensing, advisory, or collaborative relationships, not only through its own direct
offerings. This is a genuinely different kind of activity from operating a product (a think-tank/
methodology function, not just a builder), and needed to be named explicitly rather than left to
an implied reading of "other means." **Article IV's opening was broadened to match** — its five
principles now apply equally to systems or methodologies the corporation licenses or makes
available to others, not only to products it operates directly, so the same integrity
commitments travel with the methodology wherever it goes.

**Wording iteration and final sign-off (same day).** Several phrasings were proposed and talked
through in chat before anything was written into this document — a process correction Mark asked
for directly mid-conversation, after noticing edits were being locked in and published before he'd
approved the exact words. Going forward on this document: proposed language is shown in chat only;
nothing is written here, republished, or logged as decided until Mark gives explicit sign-off on
the exact wording.

The reasoning behind minimizing Article III as far as it went: the statutory bar (C.R.S.
§ 7-101-503) only requires identifying *one or more specific public benefits* — "specific" means
identifiable, not detailed — so there was real room to strip this down. Two structural facts made
that safe to do: (1) **at the time this reasoning was written, the 4/5-of-shares amendment lock
applied only to Article IV, by its own express terms** — Article III was amendable through
Colorado's ordinary corporate process, so it did not yet carry the same high-stakes "get it right
because it's permanent" pressure Article IV did. **This has since changed: the 4/5-of-shares
supermajority protection, as revised 2026-07-21, now extends to Article III as well as Article
IV** — see the amendment-protection paragraph in Article IV, which locks any amendment of either
Article III or Article IV, and any merger, conversion, asset sale, or other transaction that would
circumvent either. This history is preserved here to explain why Article III was minimized in the
first place, not because the lock still applies only to Article IV.
(2) Article III's own first sentence — "the corporation may engage in any lawful act" — already
protects the corporation's practical capacity regardless of how narrow or broad the
specific-benefit language reads. Two explicit choices were made along the way, not assumed: Mark
and Susan confirmed they want Jesus named at the heart of the purpose (the fully generic "faith"
alternative, opening the parent's stated purpose to any future faith tradition, was offered and
declined); and Mark confirmed "using technology" should be dropped from the purpose statement
itself — the current method is already visible in the next paragraph ("presently... by building
and operating Church in Conversation," self-evidently a technology platform), so naming the method
in the timeless purpose sentence would only have re-created the same kind of mechanism-lock this
whole revision pass was working to remove.

**Final, explicitly signed-off wording:** *"is to create safe spaces to explore faith and the
story of Jesus."*

### Why Article IV was rewritten at a higher level (2026-07-21 revision)

Raised directly by Mark, reading the draft closely: the original Article IV was written as CiC's
specific operating playbook — "always free," "historical reconstructions," "external scholarly
review" — hard-locked at the *parent entity* level via the 4/5-of-shares amendment protection.
That meant every future product built under Faithways Studio would inherit CiC's specific
mechanics whether or not they fit, and Mark was not prepared to commit every future product to
"always free" specifically. The same "frame vision, not program" principle already applied to
Article III's purpose statement had not yet been applied to Article IV — this revision closes
that gap. **Since revised further and finalized (2026-07-21, later the same day):** transparency
and independent review were merged into one commitment at Mark's request ("we will not represent
something that it is not"), a "guidance, not a mechanical checklist" framing sentence was added
so the board's good-faith judgment governs application rather than a rigid rulebook, and the
funding commitment was reworked twice — first to make explicit that funded development is fine
so long as it aligns with mission and convictions, then simplified to Mark's own words, "outside
funding never alters our purpose or convictions." **Four commitments now state *principles***
(honest exploration, transparency and accountability, no engagement optimization, funding never
alters purpose or convictions) that would sensibly bind any product this company ever builds,
without hard-locking a specific business model or review mechanism. **CiC's own specific
commitments — always-free core access foremost among them — remain real and are not abandoned;
they relocate to a product-level commitment (the Organizational Covenant, or an equivalent
document specific to "Church in Conversation") rather than the parent's hardest-to-amend
article.** Whether and how to formalize
that product-level document with its own real teeth is a follow-on decision, not yet made.

### Why the entity/product name split matters (2026-07-21 addition)

Raised directly by Mark: if the corporation itself were named "Church in Conversation" and the
flagship product were later sold, the remaining company would be stuck legally wearing a name
it no longer describes — an awkward rename exactly at the moment of a sale, with real risk of the
name conflicting between the seller's shell and the buyer's ongoing use of it. Separating the
**entity name** (Faithways Studio) from the **product trade name**
(Church in Conversation) solves this at the source: selling the program means selling the trade
name and trademark rights along with it — the parent corporation keeps its own name throughout,
no amendment needed, no overlap period. It also means a second spiritual-formation product, if
one is ever built, has a natural home under the same parent without forcing a rename either way.

### What this does NOT decide (deliberately left open, per the PBC structure analysis)

- **S-corp election vs. staying a straight C-corp** — a separate IRS filing (Form 2553), made
  after incorporation, not stated in these Articles either way. **Working default: don't file
  it — stay a straight C-corp**, which is what happens automatically if no election is ever made.
  No cost, no CPA consult required to make this call; filing as a PBC and electing
  S-corp status are not alternatives to each other — S-corp is a tax election layered on top of
  whichever entity exists, never a substitute for incorporating. **Decided: stay a straight
  C-corp, don't elect S-corp** — this is simply not filing Form 2553, so it costs nothing and
  needs no CPA visit; it also keeps QSBS eligibility open if a sale ever happens down the road,
  without committing to any particular timeline for one.
- **Whether to build a voluntary advisory board** — Article V leaves this open on purpose; it's
  Mark's call, not something the entity form decides for him.
