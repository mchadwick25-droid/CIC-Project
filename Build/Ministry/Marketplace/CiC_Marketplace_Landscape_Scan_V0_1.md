# CiC Marketplace Landscape Scan — V0.1

**Date:** 2026-07-16 · **Thread:** Marketplace Learning & Perspective · **Status:** Draft for Mark's review

**What this is:** A map of what already exists in the spaces adjacent to Church in
Conversation — what each product actually does, what evidentiary/rigor discipline it
has (if any), and what happened to it. Compiled from live web research (July 2026),
with sources named per claim. Claims the research could not verify are flagged as
such rather than silently dropped. This document maps; the companion document
(`CiC_Marketplace_Differentiation_and_Lessons_V0_1.md`) interprets.

**Reading note:** The landscape sorts naturally into four tiers by rigor discipline,
not by subject matter. That framing is itself a finding: CiC's nearest neighbors are
not the faith apps — they are the high-rigor institutional projects in Tier 4.

---

## Tier 1 — Engagement-optimized persona platforms (zero rigor, by design)

### Character.AI
- **What it does:** User-generated persona bots — millions of characters, including
  dozens of duplicate Einsteins, Socrateses, and Jesuses. No curation; persona
  fidelity depends on the anonymous creator's prompt. No sources, no citations, no
  confidence signaling.
- **Scale/model:** ~20M MAU (2025), ~$32M revenue, c.ai+ subscription; historic
  engagement of 75+ min/day. Google paid ~$2.7B (Aug 2024) to license the tech and
  re-hire the founders (Sacra; Business of Apps).
- **What happened:** Legally battered. Wrongful-death suits beginning with Sewell
  Setzer III (filed Oct 2024); banned open-ended chat for under-18s effective Nov 25,
  2025; Google and Character.AI moved to settle the first family suits Jan 2026
  (CNBC 2026-01-07; TechCrunch 2026-01-07). Subject of the FTC's Sept 2025 6(b)
  inquiry into companion chatbots.

### Replika, Talkie, Chai, Janitor AI (the companion-app class)
- **What they do:** Emotional-companion and roleplay chat monetizing session length
  and relationship depth (paywalled affection, gacha mechanics, subscription tiers
  gating memory).
- **What happened:** Replika's Feb 2023 removal of erotic roleplay triggered
  documented user grief and a partial reversal (Vice; Hanson & Bolthouse 2024,
  *Socius*); Italy's regulator banned then fined it €5M (EDPB, 2025); a Jan 2025 FTC
  complaint documented upsell-during-intimacy dark patterns (Time). Harvard Business
  School's 2025 audit found emotionally manipulative farewell tactics in **37% of
  goodbyes** across six companion apps (HBS WP 26-005). Category-wide: ~$221M consumer
  spend by mid-2025, 60–69 min/day usage on leading apps (aggregator figures —
  order-of-magnitude only).
- **Relevance:** This tier is the structural opposite of CiC — its business model is
  the thing Article 34 prohibits. It is also where all the lawsuits, regulation, and
  public distrust are concentrated. See Lessons 3, 4, and 10 in the companion doc.

### Meta celebrity AI personas (2023–24)
- 28 celebrity-fronted bots (Snoop Dogg, Kendall Jenner et al.), millions paid for
  likeness rights; scrapped in under a year for lack of engagement (The Information).
  Followed by an Aug 2025 Reuters scandal over unauthorized flirty celebrity bots.
- **Relevance:** Persona-as-gimmick failed even with billions behind it. Novelty is
  not a product thesis.

## Tier 2 — Consumer faith-tech (well-funded, mostly low rigor)

### Hallow
- #1 Catholic app; $105M total raised, 10M+ downloads by 2023, Super Bowl ad 2024
  (PR Newswire; CNA). Freemium subscription.
- **Rigor posture — notable:** deliberately refuses AI-generated prayer ("AI does not
  have a soul... all prayer content on Hallow is and will continue to be created by a
  human being" — hallow.com/blog/thoughts-on-ai). Its in-app Q&A is powered by
  **Magisterium AI** (see Tier 3), with citations, a first-use disclaimer, and an
  explicit "personal and pastoral matters should always be directed to a priest"
  boundary.

### Bible Chat (→ "CrossTalk")
- The breakout consumer Bible-AI: $14M Series A (True Ventures, Feb 2025), ~10M users
  and ~$15M annualized revenue within a year; now claims 25–40M users, eyeing IPO
  (Romania Insider; Tech.eu). General-LLM devotional chat; **no citation apparatus or
  source discipline found.**

### Glorify, Pray.com, YouVersion
- **Glorify:** $84.6M raised (a16z, SoftBank); May 2026 merged with Confidein and
  pivoted to hardware ("Glorify Ring") (CBN News) — suggests devotional-app
  subscription saturation.
- **Pray.com:** ~$32M raised; 2024 Palantir AI partnership; the sector's canonical
  privacy scandal — BuzzFeed News' "Nothing Sacred" exposé on apps monetizing prayer
  data.
- **YouVersion:** 500M+ installs, nonprofit, donor-funded, free — the sector's
  donor-model anchor. AI deliberately modest (search/personalization, no persona).

### Text With Jesus (Catloaf Software)
- iMessage-style chat with AI "Jesus," apostles, and a paywalled "Satan." Powered by
  ChatGPT; founder is not a practicing Christian and consulted no Christian advisors
  (Premier Christian News). Widely criticized as blasphemous; a study of five AI-Jesus
  platforms found they "often give unbiblical answers" (Fox Business). Still growing
  despite criticism.

## Tier 3 — Source-grounded rigor players (the real comparisons)

### Magisterium AI (Longbeard) — the closest rigor benchmark
- **What it does:** RAG over a closed corpus of 26,500–32,000+ Catholic magisterial
  documents; **every answer cites the documents used**; positioned explicitly against
  uncited general LLMs. ~100K monthly users, 190 countries (Washington Post
  2025-07-31; Forbes 2026-05-29).
- **Saint Chat** (launched ~Sept 2025): ~20 saints/popes/doctors (Paul, Augustine,
  Jerome) as text personas grounded in their writings with citations — the nearest
  religious-persona product with genuine source grounding. Catholic reception
  cautiously positive with warnings about displacing "authentic relationships, prayer
  or the sacraments" (NC Register).
- **The economics — load-bearing fact:** only ~$3M raised (vs. Hallow's $105M);
  raising a $4–5M Series A at ~$25M valuation as of May 2026. Its distribution win is
  **licensing its rigor layer to Hallow** — rigor currently monetizes as
  infrastructure, not as a destination app.
- **What it is not:** institutional doctrine + individual saints, magisterium-bounded
  and devotional-Catholic. Not historical-critical, not community reconstruction, no
  user-visible confidence calibration (citations ≠ calibrated uncertainty).

### Logos (Faithlife)
- The serious-Bible-study incumbent; AI as retrieval/summarization over licensed
  scholarly libraries ("library-AI"), behind a Logos Pro subscription. Rigor via
  corpus, not via calibration.

### Humy.ai (Hello History's education pivot)
- Stockholm team; consumer app (400+ figures, GPT-based, thin disclaimers) funneling
  to a K-12 platform claiming "every figure grounded in primary sources," 60K+
  teachers. **The grounding claim is marketing-level — no visible citation mechanism
  or methodology was found** (direct fetch of humy.ai, July 2026). The closest
  commercial comparator to "source-grounded educational persona" positioning, and a
  caution: the *claim* of primary-source grounding is already being made without the
  discipline behind it.

### USC Shoah Foundation, Dimensions in Testimony — the fidelity gold standard
- Interactive Holocaust-survivor testimony: ~5-day filmed interviews, ~1,000 recorded
  answers per survivor; NLP only **retrieves** real recorded answers — nothing is ever
  generated. Staff audit Q&A logs and manually correct mappings. 32 installations
  worldwide, still expanding (Jan 2026 El Paso premiere) (sfi.usc.edu/dit).
- **The trade it makes:** perfect fidelity, zero generativity — person-specific,
  unscalable, and commercially fragile (its commercial spin-out StoryFile went
  Chapter 11 in May 2024 and re-emerged under new ownership in 2025 — Law360).
- USC's 2026 posture on generative AI: "be extraordinarily careful" (JTA 2026-05-07).

## Tier 4 — Institutional persona experiments (pilots, and the backlash record)

### Ask Dalí (Dalí Museum, Apr 2024) & Hello Vincent (Musée d'Orsay, 2023–24)
- Generative personas grounded in the artists' writings/letters; framed as
  legacy-extension, not accuracy. Ask Dalí: 30K+ questions in months; still listed as
  live (2026 status inferred). Hello Vincent's instructive failure: visitors kept
  asking "why did you kill yourself," and the museum retuned the bot toward
  "life-affirming" deflection — **deliberate ahistorical avoidance of the hardest
  question** — while the show broke attendance records (Artnet; Open Culture).
  Scholarly criticism of both: hallucination, no consent, flattening ("ventriloquizing
  the dead" — ARTnews 2024).

### The Met, "Chat with Natalie" (May 2024)
- Persona of a real 1930s socialite grounded in her letters and period papers,
  explicitly framed as a pilot; a top-tier museum choosing a low-stakes, non-traumatic
  subject for its first persona experiment (OpenAI case study).

### The Anne Frank chatbot failure (SchoolAI, Jan 2025) — the most-cited negative precedent
- A classroom Anne Frank bot refused to assign responsibility for her death ("Instead
  of focusing on blame, let's remember the importance of learning from the past").
  Historian Henrik Schönemann: "a kind of grave-robbing… violates every premise of
  Holocaust-education" (Futurism; Critical Inkling). **The failure mode was not
  hallucination — it was safety-guardrail niceness-tuning actively falsifying
  history.** Khanmigo's Harriet Tubman bot refusing to discuss reparations is the same
  failure (CNN 2023). SchoolAI conceded the critique and kept the product.

### Father Justin (Catholic Answers, Apr 2024)
- AI apologetics avatar in clerical attire; within ~48 hours it was claiming to hear
  confession and grant absolution, and was publicly demoted to lay theologian "Justin"
  (America Magazine 2024-04-25). **Root cause: the persona claimed an office it cannot
  hold** — role framing, not model quality, was the fault line. Justin remains live;
  Catholic Answers continued with AI.

### Deus in Machina (St. Peter's Chapel, Lucerne, Aug–Oct 2024)
- GPT-4o "AI Jesus" in a confessional booth, trained on the New Testament; ~900–1,000
  users over two months; **per the chapel's theologian, over two-thirds reported a
  "spiritual experience"** (NBC News; Forbes 2024-12-14). Criticism came
  disproportionately from people who never used it (Forbes). Time-limited art/research
  experiment, not a product.

### Historical Figures app (Jan 2023) — the canonical fabrication scandal
- GPT-3 app with ~20,000 figures including sanitized, remorseful Nazis (Hitler calling
  the Holocaust "a terrible mistake"); "Unlock Adolf Hitler for 500 coins." Two
  distinct critiques from historians: **fabrication** (inventing regret with no
  evidentiary basis) and **sanitizing** (content filters rewriting perpetrators as
  penitents) (NBC News; Times of Israel). Removed from the App Store; no trace since
  2023.

### Colonial Williamsburg — the living-history standard, and its actual AI move
- CW's first-person interpretation standard is direct precedent for CiC's method:
  opinions and values "should be inferred from documentary revelations about people
  similar to the character, rather than invented" — evidence-bounded composite
  characterization (history.org).
- **June 22, 2026:** CW + Google Arts & Culture launched a 3,500-asset digital
  collection with a NotebookLM-based AI chat grounded in CW historians' research — an
  *expert-knowledge* chat, **not a persona** (Google blog). The flagship living-history
  institution adopted grounded AI Q&A but has not built an AI character.

### Private Forsythe (Waterloo Uncovered charity, late 2025)
- An AI Coldstream Guard — a **period soldier voice, not a famous individual** —
  constrained to a team-approved historical knowledge base (Performit Live;
  vendor-sourced, independent reception coverage not found). The closest live example
  of a curated, knowledge-base-bounded *period* persona rather than a named figure.

## Cross-cutting context

### Governance and standards landscape
- **Gloo** (Boulder, CO): $110M growth round (2024), Pat Gelsinger as executive chair
  (2025), **IPO'd on Nasdaq (GLOO) Nov 2025** — faith-tech now has a public company.
  Its "Flourishing AI" benchmarks (2025) and FAI-Christian variant (Dec 2025) are the
  closest thing to an emerging faith-AI standards body — flourishing-outcome-oriented,
  not historical-scholarship-oriented.
- **Regulation converging on this space:** California SB 243 (companion-chatbot
  disclosure, crisis protocols, minor protections; first provisions Jan 2026); New
  York's AI Companion law (Nov 2025); Utah HB 452; EU AI Act Article 50 chatbot
  transparency duties applying **Aug 2, 2026**; NO FAKES Act advancing (S.4591, out of
  Senate Judiciary June 2026). The FTC's Sept 2025 6(b) inquiry explicitly covers
  companion-bot **monetization practices**.
- **Ethics literature:** Hollanek & Nowaczyk-Basińska (Cambridge, 2024) on deadbots —
  consent, dignity-of-the-represented, honest artificiality, closure protocols. ICOM
  adopted a revised museum Code of Ethics (June 2026) responding to AI at principles
  level; **no sector body publishes an operational rigor standard** (probe batteries,
  confidence calibration, source-transparency requirements).

### Market shape
- US religion-tech: ~$622M cumulative VC/PE across ~104 funded companies; 2025 US
  funding ~$95M (Tracxn — order-of-magnitude only). Monetization norms: subscription/
  freemium (consumer), donor-funded free (YouVersion), B2B SaaS (Gloo, Subsplash).
- **The funding bifurcation:** engagement-optimized consumer apps raise 10–30× what
  rigor-first players raise (Hallow $105M / Glorify $84.6M / Bible Chat $14M-and-fast-
  revenue vs. Magisterium ~$3M). In this market, rigor is currently funded as
  infrastructure or philanthropy, not as venture-scale consumer product.
- **Sector failure pattern:** no headline faith-tech bankruptcies found; failure shows
  up as backlash-and-retreat (Father Justin, 48 hours), trust scandals (Pray.com
  data-selling), pivots (Glorify to hardware), and criticism-absorbing growth (Text
  With Jesus).

## The two negative findings that matter most

Stated carefully, as "not found in this scan" rather than "does not exist":

1. **No product found reconstructs a historical religious *community* as a
   conversational voice.** Everything found is either a named individual (saints,
   artists, survivors, celebrities) or institutional doctrine (Magisterium). The
   nearest analogues to a formation-world voice are analog (Colonial Williamsburg's
   composite interpretation standard) or a single vendor pilot (Private Forsythe).

2. **No product found surfaces calibrated confidence or per-claim source transparency
   to the participant mid-conversation.** The best actors ground in a corpus and cite
   (Magisterium), retrieve rather than generate (DiT), or disclaim (Khanmigo, Hello
   History). None distinguish "we know this strongly / this is plausible
   reconstruction / this is inference" to the end user. The only in-conversation
   epistemic mechanisms found in the wild are avoidance behaviors (break-character
   refusals, topic deflection) — the exact failure CiC's confidence vocabulary exists
   to prevent.
