## 2026-07-16 — Core positioning finding: two genuinely unoccupied positions

**Decided (as a finding, not a strategy commitment):** Two positions were found
empty across the entire scanned landscape, stated as "no product we could find,"
never as "the only":
1. Reconstructing a historical religious **community** (vs. a named individual or
   institutional doctrine) as a conversational voice.
2. **Participant-visible, calibrated confidence** mid-conversation. Citations exist
   (Magisterium AI); calibration does not. The field's substitutes — deflection and
   topic-refusal — are its documented failure modes (SchoolAI's Anne Frank, Orsay's
   Van Gogh, Khanmigo's Tubman).

**Reasoning, including the heart:** These two are not just market gaps — they are the
two places where the Convictions forced CiC off the industry's path years before the
industry's path produced its scandals. The community architecture exists because of
Conviction 1 (no single witness exhausts Christ's richness), and it happens to
sidestep the consent/dignity/impersonation wounds that produced Father Justin, the
deadbot ethics literature, and the NO FAKES Act. Visible calibration exists because of
Conviction 4, and it happens to be the exact discipline whose absence produced the
Anne Frank whitewashing. The market findings are downstream confirmation of the
Convictions, not a reason for them.

**Also decided:** CiC positioning must explicitly exit the "AI companion" and "AI
chatbot" categories (both now carry lawsuits, FTC scrutiny, and fabrication baggage),
by naming the structural refusals — not by tone. Operationalized in the brief's
category-exit table.

**Next action:** Mark reviews the three V0.1 documents; Communication thread takes the
brief from there.

## 2026-07-16 — Founder question: real potential, realistic scale, and the path past "that's cool"

**What was asked:** Mark asked directly whether CiC truly has the potential to meet
its mission and vision, what the realistic scale is, and what would make it more than
a "that's cool" program (naming ideas like tours of worlds).

**Perspective given (a finding of this thread, not a decision):**
- *Potential:* Yes, conditionally. Strongest external evidence: Deus in Machina
  (two-thirds of ~1,000 users of a far shallower source-bounded religious AI reported
  a "spiritual experience") plus the durability of DiT and living-history
  interpretation. The condition: the market shows comfort scales effortlessly and
  encounter doesn't — encounter scales through structure, institutions, and story,
  never through app-store habit loops (which the Constitution rightly forbids anyway).
- *Scale:* The realistic north star is BibleProject (nonprofit, donor-funded, free,
  scholarly-accessible, a decade to millions of users), not Hallow or Character.AI.
  Three tiers named: faithful boutique (5–50K/yr), institutional ministry (100K–1M/yr),
  BibleProject-scale (millions). Marginal serving cost is trivial; the true scale
  constraints are world-build throughput and institutional distribution.
- *Past "that's cool" (in leverage order):* (1) guided journeys / "tours of worlds" —
  the blank-prompt problem killed most persona products; structured arcs are the
  biggest product lever, and the L3D table processes are the seed; (2) group use as
  the primary container (small groups, classrooms) — programs, not apps, are what
  institutions adopt, and it embodies the funding model; (3) inter-world encounters as
  the signature un-copyable experience (Conviction 1 embodied); (4) tie-ins to
  existing practice (Lent, Advent, curricula) — doorway-not-home makes CiC the only
  product a pastor can wholeheartedly recommend; (5) content-cadence retention
  (catalog depth), never habit loops; (6) eventual physical embodiment (installations,
  a traveling Table).
- *Conditions for scale under the Convictions:* the pipeline must become teachable
  (train other builders under Mark's gates — the governance docs were written to be
  internally complete for this); distribution via institutions, not paid acquisition;
  formation stories as the donor-model currency; Prototype 1's real demand signal is
  unprompted return plus articulable encounter, not satisfaction scores.

**The heart of it:** The "that's cool" fear is the fear that encounter can't compete
with comfort. It can't, on comfort's terms — and never has. Encounter has always
scaled through structure, community, and testimony. The Constitution didn't
accidentally forbid the shortcuts; it forced the project onto the only road that
could carry the mission anyway.

**Open questions put to Mark (everything downstream branches on these):**
1. Which ambition tier is he actually called to — faithful boutique, institutional
   ministry, or BibleProject-scale?
2. Is he willing to train other builders soon (worlds he didn't personally build,
   under his gates), or does the pipeline stay his through Phase 1?
3. Who is the first institutional partner archetype for the next 18 months — a
   seminary, a denominational formation office, or a local-church network? (One.)

**Handoffs staged (pending Mark's direction):** guided journeys / group mode /
inter-world signature experience → front-end thread; seminary-partnership and
ambition-tier implications → funding thread.

**Next action:** Awaiting Mark's answers to the three questions; they determine which
handoffs fire.
