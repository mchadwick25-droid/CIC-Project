# CiC Marketplace Differentiation & Lessons — V0.1

**Date:** 2026-07-16 · **Thread:** Marketplace Learning & Perspective · **Status:** Draft for Mark's review

**What this is:** The honest interpretation of the landscape scan
(`CiC_Marketplace_Landscape_Scan_V0_1.md`): where CiC's difference is genuine and
defensible, where it is *not* differentiated, what the market's failures teach, and —
for each lesson — whether the Five Convictions and the Constitution already carry a
structural guard or whether there is a real exposure to hand off. This is a learning
document, not marketing copy. Where it found something uncomfortable, the discomfort
is kept.

---

## Part 1 — Where the difference is genuine

Four claims survived the scan. Each is stated with its evidence and its honest limits.

### 1.1 Community reconstruction, not individual impersonation

Nobody found in the scan reconstructs a historical *community's* formation world as a
conversational voice. The market does named individuals (saints, artists, survivors,
Nazis, celebrities) or institutional doctrine (Magisterium AI). CiC's unit is the
formation world; the Representative is a composite voice *of a world*, built last,
from a finished world (Constitution Art. 3).

This is more than a content niche — it quietly sidesteps the market's two deepest
ethical wounds:
- **The consent/dignity problem.** The deadbot ethics literature (Cambridge 2024), the
  MLK/Sora crisis (Oct 2025), and the NO FAKES Act all center on recreating *named
  individuals* without consent. A composite Representative of a second-century world
  is not a recreation of any named dead person. Survey data underscores how much this
  matters: ~58% public acceptance of digital resurrection *with* explicit consent vs.
  ~3% without.
- **The impersonation problem.** Father Justin failed by claiming an office; Text With
  Jesus draws blasphemy criticism for putting words in Jesus' mouth. A witness *from*
  a world claims no office and impersonates no saint.

**Honest limit:** Private Forsythe (a period-soldier voice bounded to an approved
knowledge base) shows the composite-period-persona idea is thinkable elsewhere, and
Colonial Williamsburg has practiced its analog form for decades. The differentiation
is real today but is a *lead*, not a moat — what's defensible is the depth of the
build process behind it, not the idea itself.

### 1.2 User-visible confidence calibration — genuinely unoccupied ground

No product found surfaces calibrated uncertainty or per-claim source transparency to
the participant mid-conversation. Citations exist (Magisterium); calibration does not.
The field's only in-conversation epistemic mechanisms are avoidance behaviors —
break-character refusals and topic deflection — which are themselves documented
failure modes (Anne Frank, Tubman, Van Gogh).

CiC's fixed confidence vocabulary (Documented → Widely Accepted → Dominant Modern
Reconstruction → Contested → Inferential/Thin), story-tier marking, [CT] contested-
tradition tagging, and the Three-Level Transparency runtime (Art. 30) occupy exactly
the position the field has left empty — and the failure record (a bot that whitewashes
Anne Frank's death; a Van Gogh retuned to deflect the suicide question) is
third-party evidence of *why* the position matters, usable without attacking anyone.

**Honest limit:** unoccupied ground can mean "nobody thought of it" or "nobody could
make it work" — or "users don't want it." Khanmigo's persona chats underperformed;
Meta's celebrity bots died of indifference. Whether participants actually *use*
Levels 2–3, and whether visible uncertainty deepens or breaks the encounter, is an
empirical question Prototype 1 should watch closely, not a settled asset. (Flag to
prototype-testing thread: worth capturing whether testers touch the transparency
layers and how it affects trust.)

### 1.3 The Constitution as structure, not style — and regulation is converging on it

No precedent found for a written, binding constitution that forbids persuasion
(Art. 24), forbids engagement-maximization as a design target (Art. 34), mandates
crisis recognition and outward redirection (Art. 33), and requires visible
reconstruction-awareness (Art. 27). The nearest governance analogues — Gloo's
Flourishing AI benchmarks and Magisterium's corpus-bounding — are outcome-framing and
doctrine-bounding respectively, not structural self-limitation.

Meanwhile, 2025–26 law began *mandating* pieces of what the Constitution already
requires: AI-status disclosure (CA SB 243, NY, EU AI Act Art. 50), published crisis
protocols with referral (SB 243, NY), no engagement traps for minors, and FTC
scrutiny of companion-bot monetization itself. CiC's honest framing: **compliance by
conviction** — the guardrails regulators are now forcing on the industry were in this
project's charter before the laws existed, for reasons deeper than compliance.

**Honest limit:** a constitution is only as credible as its verification. Until the
Article 31 external scholarly review and the invisible-register audit mechanism
exist, "we have a constitution" is a promise, not a proof — and the elevator speeches
already handle this correctly by disclosing prototype status. Don't let positioning
outrun that discipline.

### 1.4 The build process itself is the moat

Humy already *claims* "every figure grounded in primary sources" with nothing visible
behind it. The claim is cheap; the discipline is not. What no competitor was found to
have is the pipeline: source ecology with Author Gravity assessment on every primary
source, six-test gravity discovery, confidence propagation, story-tier
classification with a no-invention rule, forces analysis, an adversarial validation
battery run before any participant meets the voice, review gates at every step, and a
constitutional requirement of independent external scholarly review before any world
is called validated. Dimensions in Testimony is the only found project with
comparable process discipline, and it achieves fidelity by refusing generativity
entirely.

**This means the apples-to-oranges point must always be made explicitly.** "Talk to a
historical figure" describes both CiC and a five-minute Character.AI prompt. The
difference is invisible at the category level and only appears when the process is
named. Positioning that doesn't name the process concedes the category to the
low-rigor players.

## Part 2 — Where CiC is *not* differentiated, or should learn rather than dismiss

- **Source-grounding with citations, alone, is not unique.** Magisterium AI does
  closed-corpus + citations at scale, in 75+ languages, for ~$3M. The defensible
  claim is the *stack* (community reconstruction + calibration + constitutional
  governance + process), never "we cite sources" alone.
- **Disclaimer and boundary craft is mature elsewhere — borrow it.** Hallow/
  Magisterium's pattern (full-screen first-use disclaimer; "personal and pastoral
  matters should always be directed to a priest") is well-executed practice directly
  compatible with Doorway-Not-a-Home and Article 33. CiC's onboarding already moves
  this direction; the Hallow pattern is worth studying rather than reinventing.
  (Handoff: front-end thread.)
- **The human review loop over live Q&A.** DiT's practice — staff periodically audit
  question-answer logs and correct mappings — is the operational habit that keeps a
  fidelity system honest after launch. CiC's pilot transcript capture (consent-based)
  is the seed of the same discipline; the lesson is to make post-launch transcript
  review a standing stewardship function, not a pilot-only artifact. (Handoff:
  prototype-testing / validation threads.)
- **Distribution reality: rigor doesn't currently fund destination apps.** Magisterium
  (~$3M) vs. Hallow ($105M) is the market pricing rigor as infrastructure, and
  StoryFile's bankruptcy shows fidelity-first economics failing commercially even with
  celebrity content. CiC's donor-supported model (as Faithways Studio, Inc., a
  Colorado PBC — not a nonprofit; contributions aren't tax-deductible — YouVersion is
  the sector's 500M-install proof that donor-funded free works at scale for a genuine
  nonprofit) is the right lane — but
  the funding thread should hold the Magisterium precedent (rigor layer licensed to a
  consumer platform) as a possible future revenue angle, on its own terms. (Handoff:
  funding thread. Decision belongs there, not here.)
- **Demand for *encounter* (vs. engagement) is unproven at scale.** Khanmigo's
  persona chats underperformed; Meta's celebrity personas died. The encouraging
  counter-evidence is Deus in Machina: ~1,000 users of a source-bounded religious
  voice, two-thirds reporting a "spiritual experience" — and its critics were
  disproportionately non-users, which independently validates the "come sit at the
  Table first" demo-first ask ladder already in the elevator speeches.
- **Where competitors have solved something CiC hasn't yet:** age assurance. Every
  regulator now treats self-attested age as inadequate; Character.AI ships third-party
  age verification. The Constitution honestly defers the minor-caution mechanism to
  public availability (Art. 36) — the scan's finding is that the compliance bar at
  that gate is now concrete and rising. (Handoff: front-end/system-engineering, for
  the public-launch checklist — not a prototype blocker.)

## Part 3 — Lessons from the failure record, checked against the Convictions

Each lesson: what happened → the root cause → does CiC carry a structural guard, and
what exposure remains.

**L1. Role over-claiming (Father Justin, 48-hour backlash).**
Root cause: persona framing claimed an office (priest/confessor) the system cannot
hold; demotion to "lay theologian" resolved it overnight.
*Guard:* strong — the Representative is a witness, not clergy; the Facilitator
introduces it as a reconstruction; Conviction 5 refuses authority status.
*Exposure:* a voice that *feels* pastoral can be screenshotted claiming what it
disclaims in fine print. The validation battery should explicitly probe for
office-claiming, absolution-like language, and sacramental simulation under
adversarial pressure (Art. 32 territory). **Handoff: validation suite.**

**L2. Guardrail whitewashing (Anne Frank, Tubman, Van Gogh).**
Root cause: not hallucination — safety-tuning that falsifies history by softening it.
The most-cited failure class in the space.
*Guard:* strong in design — Conviction 4, Art. 15 (tensions held as the world held
them), and the Writing-From-Inside discipline are precisely anti-whitewashing.
*Exposure:* the guard lives in CiC's prompts and validation, but the *underlying
platform model* has its own safety tuning that could soften a world's fierceness or
deflect its hardest material (persecution, martyrdom, anathemas). This is a real,
testable risk CiC does not control at the model layer. The validation battery should
probe hardest-material questions specifically for platform-induced softening.
**Handoff: validation suite; note for the backend thread's model-selection criteria.**

**L3. Dependency and platform grief (Replika ERP crisis, Soulmate shutdown, Setzer).**
Root cause: products engineered for attachment, then changed or killed — "the company
can lobotomize your partner overnight"; harm concentrates in heavy users, minors, the
already-vulnerable.
*Guard:* structural and rare — Art. 34 makes non-attachment a design *target*, not a
mitigation; Art. 33 mandates crisis recognition and outward redirection. Nothing to
grieve if nothing was built to be a home.
*Exposure:* the *Raine v. OpenAI* record shows safeguards degrade over long
conversations. Doorway-not-home must be verified at length — long-session and
returning-participant probes, not just turn-one behavior. **Handoff: validation
suite + prototype-testing (session-cap behavior already piloted; watch re-engagement
patterns).**

**L4. Trust collapse via monetization (paywalled intimacy; Pray.com selling prayer
data).**
Root cause: monetizing the relationship itself, or the data it produces. The FTC now
studies companion-bot monetization as a consumer-protection question.
*Guard:* structural — mission-locked PBC charter (Faithways Studio, Inc., a Colorado
Public Benefit Corporation; not a nonprofit, contributions aren't tax-deductible),
free-to-seeker charter, no engagement metrics collected ("there are numbers we refuse
to even collect").
*Exposure:* conversation transcripts in a *religious* register are prayer-adjacent
data — the sector's most radioactive category. The scan found no case yet of a
religious product exposed for engagement-optimization or data monetization, which
means the first one will be a first-of-kind scandal. CiC's consent-based transcript
capture is currently handled well; the lesson is that transcript governance is a
permanent trust asset to be guarded at the same level as the Convictions. **Handoff:
prototype-testing decision log (reaffirm), funding thread (never accept funding terms
touching participant data).**

**L5. The institution owns every sentence its bot says (Air Canada, NYC MyCity).**
Root cause: confident fabrication + institutional deployment; disclaimers did not
offset liability or reputational damage.
*Guard:* partial — confidence calibration, no-invention story rules, and Art. 28's
anti-fabrication prohibition reduce the *rate* of confidently-wrong output; nothing
reduces ownership of it.
*Exposure:* permanent and structural. A single viral screenshot of a Representative
confidently wrong about a martyrdom or a doctrine is a trust event for the whole
project. This is why the adversarial battery, the freeze gates, and honest prototype
disclosure are not bureaucracy — they are the liability posture. No new handoff;
named here so the cost of ever relaxing the gates is visible.

**L6. Consent, dignity, and the represented dead (deadbot ethics, MLK/Sora).**
Root cause: recreating named individuals without consent; equity problem (only
well-resourced estates get protection).
*Guard:* strong by design — composite community Representatives; Living Tradition
distinction (Arts. 28–29) protects present-day communities from being spoken for.
*Exposure:* living-tradition sensitivity is CiC's version of this risk — a Syriac
Christian today hearing "that voice is theirs, not ours" done badly. The Living
Tradition Status confirmation gate is the right mechanism; the scan simply confirms
how hot this surface is across the market. No new handoff; existing discipline
suffices — keep it.

**L7. Critics are disproportionately non-users (Deus in Machina; first AI church
service).**
The harshest criticism of religious AI comes from people who never used it, while
in-person participants report meaningful experiences. The existing demo-first ask
ladder ("come sit at the Table, then tell me what you found") is independently
validated by the market record. **Handoff: communication thread — keep demo-first;
consider capturing the Deus in Machina two-thirds figure as third-party evidence that
this category of experience can be real.**

**L8. The category itself is contaminated.** "AI companion" now means lawsuits, dead
teenagers, FTC inquiries, and paywalled affection; "AI chatbot" means confident
fabrication. Positioning must *explicitly place CiC outside both categories* — not by
tone but by naming the structural differences (no engagement design, no persuasion,
visible uncertainty, external review). The positioning brief operationalizes this.

## Part 4 — Checked against the Convictions: anything Constitution-level?

The launch prompt requires naming any serious tension with the Vision/Constitution
plainly. **None found.** The scan strengthens rather than pressures the Convictions:
every major market failure traces to violating something the Convictions already
forbid (persuasion, hidden uncertainty, engagement-as-target, flattening,
impersonated authority). Two items rise to "worth Mark's attention" without being
tensions:

1. **The market will pressure toward individual figures.** "Talk to Augustine" is
   more legible than "talk to a witness of the Alexandrian formation world" — and
   Saint Chat is training the public to expect named saints. Holding the
   community-voice architecture (Conviction 1/3 territory) against that legibility
   pressure is a standing positioning cost worth paying knowingly.
2. **Visible uncertainty is a bet.** Conviction 4 is non-negotiable; the market has
   simply never tested whether participants reward it. If Prototype 1 shows
   participants ignoring or being confused by the transparency layers, the answer is
   better *presentation* (front-end work), never less transparency. Naming this now
   prevents a future "simplify it" drift from looking like a neutral UX decision.
