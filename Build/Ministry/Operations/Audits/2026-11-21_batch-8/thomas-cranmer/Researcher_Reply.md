# Thomas Cranmer: researcher's reply

**Essence.** Archbishop of Canterbury from 1533 under Henry VIII (Documented). Under Edward VI he led the making of the Prayer Books of 1549 and 1552 (Widely Accepted: the census and his editor), which put the services into English in one form for the realm (Documented). Under Mary he signed recantations, then withdrew them in public and was burned at Oxford in 1556 (Documented, from his enemies' print and register); Foxe tells that he put the signing hand into the fire first (Documented as Foxe's account).

**Also told.** He judged the king's first marriage unlawful (his own letter). He sought the king's leave for the English Bible, most of it Tyndale's (link). He argued against Lambert, burned in 1538. The 1549 rising against the book; Elizabeth's 1559 book made from his 1552 one.

**Shape and length.** Founder or leader. About 4:00, about 600 words, about 195 of them quotes, glosses and credited witnesses. Opening: the 1549 preface; the fire told once, at the end.

**Connection.** The English Prayer Book: one shared service in a known tongue, imposed, resisted, and outliving him through 1559.

**Quotes** (none lifted, none on the tree).
1. "My hand did wrong when it wrote against my heart. So my hand shall be punished for it first. If I come to the fire, it shall be burned first."
2. "For many years the service in this Church of England has been read to the people in Latin, which they did not understand. So they have heard with their ears only. Their hearts, spirit and mind have not been built up by it."
3. The rebels' "like a Christmas game".

**Where sources differ.** Bonner's printed speech ends on the Catholic faith he was to declare; Foxe's ends on the renunciation and the hand. Bonner's own register records the withdrawal. Foxe dates Waltham 1529, Cox 1528. Editors differ on how many recantations he signed and who drafted them.

**Gaps.** Quote 1's original is held only in a damaged Foxe scan; Perkins (1600) confirms the reading. The hand rests on Foxe alone; the Catholic eyewitness letter is not held. The census story's "watched Ridley and Latimer burn" is not held and Foxe says otherwise: it is in Do not say. The corpus-map's AUTHOR-IDS, WORKS and RETRIEVAL-HINTS have no Cranmer entry.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught "this unworthy right hand" (Foxe: "his"), the editors' share of Tyndale credited to them, the watching claim, and line numbers.
