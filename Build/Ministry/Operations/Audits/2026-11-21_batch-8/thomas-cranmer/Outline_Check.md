# Outline check: Thomas Cranmer

## My heart, written before reading the outline

**Essence.** Thomas Cranmer, archbishop of Canterbury from 1533 under Henry VIII, led the making of the Book of Common Prayer (1549, revised 1552), which put the whole of England's worship into English and into one book for the whole realm. Under Mary he signed a series of submissions, then at Oxford in March 1556 publicly withdrew them before he was burned; he is on the tree as the chief architect of the English Reformation's worship, the book that outlived him and shaped worship in English.

**Also told.**
- He served Henry VIII: wrote that the pope could not dispense with God's word on the king's marriage, and in May 1533 gave sentence that the king's marriage to Katherine was unlawful (own letter; Documented).
- He asked the king to license the 1537 English Bible, most of it Tyndale's work (own letter, Documented; Tyndale's share Widely Accepted).
- The record's force: he argued against John Lambert, burned as a heretic in 1538 (editor and Foxe; Documented).
- The 1549 book: English, "one use" for the realm, the Preface on a service the people did not understand (Preface unsigned, attributed to him: Widely Accepted); the 1549 rising in Devon and Cornwall against it, and his answer (Documented).
- The 1552 revision: "Mass" dropped from the title, the new words at the bread; attendance made a legal duty (Documented texts; his leading it Widely Accepted).
- Under Mary: Tower, Oxford, condemned 1554, degraded 1556; pressed and persuaded, he signed (why: Contested; his own speech in Foxe, "for fear of death"); six submissions in Bonner's hostile printed book, how many and whose words Contested.
- The last day: the speech renouncing the papers, "my hand shall first be punished"; Bonner's book prints a different, prepared ending; Bonner's own Register says he publicly revoked (Documented); the hand in the fire is Foxe's account (Documented as Foxe's; Foxe partisan, not present).
- What came: the 1559 book made from 1552, and the Prayer Book's long life (Documented / Widely Accepted); Foxe made his death the memory of the Marian burnings.
