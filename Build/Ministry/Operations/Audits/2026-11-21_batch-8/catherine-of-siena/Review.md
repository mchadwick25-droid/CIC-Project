## The three lines (from one hearing)

1. **Who:** Catherine of Siena, a dyer's daughter in fourteenth-century Siena. She wore the habit of the Dominican Sisters of Penance but lived at home, and she gathered a circle of followers who wrote down the letters she dictated to popes, cities and rulers.
2. **What happened:** she pressed Pope Gregory XI to take the papal court from Avignon back to Rome. She went to Avignon in 1376 to make Florence's peace, and that peace failed. That September the pope left for Rome. In 1378 she dictated the Dialogue, then served Urban VI in Rome once the schism began, and she died there in 1380.
3. **Why the connection matters:** her voice reached the church only through the people around her. They wrote down her letters and her book, then put the book into Latin and print. The claim that she brought the pope home comes from that same circle, through Raymond, while her own letters show that he had already decided.

All three lines are clear from one hearing.

## The heart

**My own, written before comparing.** Essence: a Sienese dyer's daughter who joined no convent, gathered a circle, and dictated letters to popes and cities. She pressed Gregory XI to bring the papacy from Avignon back to Rome, and in 1376 he went, though how far she moved him is disputed. Also told: the lay life under the Dominican habit; the dictation; what her letters pressed for (reform, the return, a crusade, peace with Florence); the failed peace at Avignon; the Dialogue; the schism and her work for Urban; and what came of her (the dispute, the Dialogue's Latin, print and English, Raymond's Life, her canonization).

**Where the essence is complete:** at "disputed" ("How far she moved him is disputed."), which is word **184** by the scorer's count. This is past about 150. See B1.

## Verdict: REVISE

Two BLOCKING findings. Both have small fixes, and with every fix below applied the story counts about 676 words, at the cap.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The essence finishes past the first minute (word 184 by the scorer; the limit is about 150).** The cause is the opening. It runs eight sentences and 122 words, and Q1 alone is 52 of them. Keep Q1 whole and cut the setup around it. In paragraph 1 replace

> "Catherine of Siena stands before Pope Gregory XI. She knows no Latin and he no Italian, so her confessor, the priest who hears her confessions and guides her, interprets. He is Raymond of Capua. She says the papal court reeks of vice, and the pope asks how she can know it after a few days. Raymond records her answer:"

with

> "Catherine of Siena stands before Pope Gregory XI, and her confessor, Raymond of Capua, the priest who hears her confessions and guides her, interprets. When the pope asks how she can know the ways of his court after a few days, Raymond records her answer:"

In paragraph 2, change "That September he left for Rome." to "That September he left."

Measured with the scorer, the essence then finishes at word **168**. Her complaint about vice is still heard in Q1's own "stench of the sins". "Interprets" still tells the listener the two did not share a language. The languages themselves (E8) go to the card. Q1 cannot be cut, so 168 is close to the floor for this opening. If a strict 150 is wanted, take the outline's opening 2 instead (the letter, Q2) and tell the audience in its place at beat 3. That is a larger change, and I do not recommend it.

*Do not say, to add to the outline:* "With Q1 whole, the opening stays at about three sentences: no separate sentence for the languages or for Raymond's name. 'She knows no Latin and he no Italian, so …' → '… her confessor, Raymond of Capua, … interprets' (the languages go to the card)."

**B2. "Urban called her to Rome, and she dictated his letters."** This is unsupported. It says she wrote Urban's own letters. The sources say she wrote her own letters on his behalf, to kings, a queen and cardinals (E14; LET-ed l.1371-1381). The outline's wording is "dictated letters for him". Change "she dictated his letters" to "she dictated letters for him".

*Do not say, to add to the outline:* "'she dictated his letters' → 'she dictated letters for him' (her own letters, written on Urban's behalf)."

### MINOR

**M1. The length is at the cap.** The scorer counts 679 words, about 4:32. That is "about 4:30" and not clearly over, so it is not blocking. The fixes here net about −3 words, to about 676. One cut is not needed for anything: "came late and" in "Florence's envoys came late and would not deal with her" (−3 words). The envoys' refusal is the point, and "late" is detail. Nothing else in the story is surplus. Every other part serves the heart, the record's force, a gloss, a credited witness or the connection.

**M2. The writer cut past the cut-first list.** Mark's ruling of 2026-11-21 says writers drop from the list "and not by their own choice". The story drops all nine listed items, which is allowed. It also drops three things the list does not name: the drawn sword at Florence (the outline says "The drawn sword stays"), Gregory's death in March 1378, and the July 1378 peace. None of these is part of the approved heart or a credited disagreement, so none is blocking.
- Gregory's death is needed for one hearing. Without it, "the new pope, Urban VI" comes with nothing before it. In paragraph 7, change "In 1378, back in Siena, she dictated" to "Gregory died in March 1378. Back in Siena that year, she dictated" (+5 words).
- The sword would add about 25 words and take the story past 4:30. Under the batch 7 rule, a story that cannot fit comes back to Mark. **For Mark:** does the sword stay on the card, or does the story go a little past 4:30?

**M3. The bishop's retort lacks its claimed effect, and the rebuke has no agent.** The outline credits the later biographer with saying the retort "hastened the return". Without that clause the retort reads as an anecdote rather than as a cause. Change "A later biographer of the popes said a bishop, rebuked for living away from his own diocese, asked why the pope did not go back to his own church." to "A later biographer of the popes said a bishop, rebuked by the pope for living away from his own diocese, asked why the pope did not go back to his own church. The answer, the biographer says, hastened the return." (+10 words; each sentence stays under 30 words.)

**M4. Two names are heard once and neither acts nor links.** Both are witnesses whose role is enough, and the outline's Names list gives both by role.
- "One of them, Cristofano Guidini, wrote" → "One of them wrote" (−2 words).
- "A secretary who was with her, Barduccio, wrote" → "A secretary who was with her wrote" (−1 word).

**M5. One sentence runs past 30 words** (33 words): "Pressed by her family to marry, … but lived in their own homes." Split it: "… the Sisters of Penance of St Dominic. They were laywomen who wore the dress of the Dominican order but lived in their own homes." (+2 words.)

**M6. "The schism" is first heard in paragraph 9, after its plain sense was given in paragraph 8 without the word.** The listener has to connect the two. In paragraph 9, change "with the schism unhealed" to "with the split between the two popes unhealed" (+4 words). Alternatively, end paragraph 8's sentence "so two men claimed to be pope, a split called the schism".

**Note (not a finding).** "Dictated" is first heard in paragraph 2 and explained in paragraph 3. A grown listener knows the word, so this is fine.
