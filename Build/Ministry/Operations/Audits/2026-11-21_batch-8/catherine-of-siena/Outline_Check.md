# Outline check: Catherine of Siena

Checker, batch 8, 2026-11-21.

## My own heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

**The essence.** Catherine, a dyer's daughter of Siena, took the habit of the Dominican Sisters of Penance (the Mantellate) and lived it at home rather than in a convent. Though she could hardly write, she dictated letters to popes, cities and rulers, and in 1376 went to Avignon for Florence and pressed Gregory XI, face to face and in letters, to make peace and bring the papacy back to Rome. He went that September. How much she moved him is disputed: her confessor Raymond says she led him to it; her own letters say the resolve was already his; later writers credit Bridget's revelations or a bishop's retort. She is on the tree as the tradition's voice for the return from Avignon. (Documented for the acts; Contested for her share in the return.)

**Also told.** A circle (her "famiglia") gathered round her and called her mother; her secretaries took down her letters (Documented). Her letters press reform of the clergy, the return and a crusade (Documented). The Avignon peace mission for Florence failed when Florence's envoys would not deal with her (Documented). Sent by Gregory to Florence in 1378, she was nearly killed there and stayed until peace was made under Urban VI (Documented; the stopping of the attacker in Raymond's voice). In 1378 she dictated the Dialogue, a book of four petitions and God's answers (the Son as bridge), to her secretaries, who say she was in ecstasy (Documented; the trance in the witnesses' voice). After the schism she went to Rome and worked for Urban VI; she died there in 1380, aged about 33 (Documented / Widely Accepted). What came: the return and the argument over it (Contested); the Dialogue put into Latin, printed, carried to England (Documented); her circle and Raymond's Life carried her on; canonized by Pius II (Widely Accepted).

Shape: Founder or leader (she founded nothing formal; the "built" thing is her circle and the voice of her letters).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **Her death date: held sources differ, and the outline states one as settled.** Setting ("died 30 April 1380") and beat 6 ("She died in Rome on 30 April 1380") give Scudder's date (LET-ed l.1394-1395) alone. Raymond gives another: "anno domini millesimo trecentesimo octuagesimo, die vicesimo nono mensis Aprilis, quae fuit Dominica dies circa horam Tertiarum" (RAY l.211676-211678: 29 April 1380, a Sunday). Barduccio, who was there, also says she died on a Sunday ("die Dominica sub horam sextam", BARD l.214957), and the account before his letter counts her suffering "usque ad penultimam diem Aprilis" (RAY l.214480-214481). Under Mark's ruling of 2026-11-19 this must be credited or not stated. **Correction:** Setting: "died at the end of April 1380, in Rome (Raymond, RAY l.211676-211678: 29 April, a Sunday; Scudder, LET-ed l.1394: 30 April)". Beat 6: "She died in Rome at the end of April 1380, aged about thirty-three, with the schism unhealed." Add to **Do not say**: "died on 30 April 1380" (or "29 April") as settled → "at the end of April 1380"; the card gives both dates with their witnesses. Add to **For the card**: the two dates, and that Raymond, who was in Genoa (RAY l.211679), puts it at about Terce, Barduccio at about the sixth hour.

2. **The Connection field says more than the sources.** (a) "she could not have written her letters or her book; she spoke them" contradicts the outline's own Do not say and the four-fault note: Scudder says she learned to write three years before her death and still dictated (LET-ed l.518-522); Fiorilli that she could barely read and less write (LIB-ed l.14603-14607). (b) "The same circle then put the Dialogue into Latin, copied it, and gave it to the printers and translators who took it to Italy, France and England" makes her circle the source of the prints: no held line says so. The circle's own acts are Maconi's and Guidini's Latin, Guidini's copies, and the French bishop who took Guidini's copy home to preach from (LIB-ed l.14407-14422, 14467-14472); the 1472 print and the English of 1519, made from Raymond's Latin printed at Brescia in 1496, are told by the editors only (LIB-ed l.14503-14511; GARD l.488-504). **Correction:** replace the first sentence's clause with "she did not write her letters or her book herself; she spoke them, and her secretaries wrote them down (E2, E13, Q3)", and the second with "The same circle put the Dialogue into Latin and had it copied, and a French bishop who had met her at Avignon took one copy home to preach its teaching (I2). Later it was printed, by 1472, and put into English in 1519 from Raymond's Latin (the editors)."

### MINOR

3. **Bridget "then living in Rome".** Beat 4 and Names give the visionary as "then living in Rome", which a writer can hear as "living in Rome in 1376". Mansi says the revelation had come to her "tunc adhuc Romae degenti" and was passed to the pope a second time through a hermit (MANSI l.53404-53408). **Correction:** beat 4: "credited revelations that had come to a Swedish visionary while she lived in Rome, passed on to the pope through a hermit". Names: drop "noblewoman" (no cited line gives it): "a Swedish visionary".
4. **Words to explain: three gaps.** (a) "see", in beat 4 ("living away from his see"): add "see: say 'his own diocese' or 'his own church'". (b) "habit" (beat 1, the Mantellate gloss): add "habit: the dress of a religious order". (c) "Carthusian" (What came 3, optional): add "the Carthusian monks: an order of monks who live mostly alone in silence", or drop the word. The gloss on "dictated / secretaries" is needless but harmless.
5. **Q1 rendering, last sentence.** My rendering from the Latin: "For the honour of almighty God I dare to say that, while I was in my own city where I was born, I smelled more of the stench of the sins committed in the papal court than those who have committed them and commit them every day." The outline's is verbatim in sense, but "I smelled it more than the men who have committed them" can be heard as comparing the men with the stench. **Correction:** "I smelled it more than the men who have committed them, and commit them every day, smell it themselves." The Latin follows Mirbt ("quae"; RAY prints "qui"): note that it is Mirbt's reading.
6. **Ending (a): the quotation marks and "the Gospel's" words.** Barduccio's Latin is a translation and the scan is damaged, with no other held print, so the outline rightly plans paraphrase, but then sets "Blood, blood" and "Father, into your hands I commend my soul and my spirit" in quotation marks, and calls them the Gospel's words. The Gospel line has "spirit" only; "soul and spirit" is Barduccio's. **Correction:** "she cried out for the blood, again and again, and then, as Christ did on the cross, gave her soul into her Father's hands, bowed her head and died (Barduccio, a secretary who was there; paraphrase, no quotation marks)."
7. **Source sheet E5** says "incarnate demons" was written "to the Florentine side"; it is in a letter to Gregory XI (LET l.5038-5039, under the heading at l.4889). The outline has it right; no outline change.
8. **Library sweep, one addition for the card.** Teresa of Ávila's own Life names Catherine twice, among saints who had revelations and who rejoiced in Christ's humanity (`teresa-of-avila_life_lewis1904.txt` l.2257-2261, 11588-11592). Add beside Alvarez in Links (`teresa-of-avila`, card only).
