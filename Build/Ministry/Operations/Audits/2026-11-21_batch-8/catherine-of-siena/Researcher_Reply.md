# Catherine of Siena: researcher's reply

**Essence.** A dyer's daughter who never entered a convent (Documented), gathered a circle and dictated letters to popes, cities and rulers (Documented). In 1376 she went to Avignon and pressed Gregory XI to return to Rome, and that September he left (Documented). How far she moved him is disputed (Contested).

**Also told.** She wore the habit of the Dominican Sisters of Penance, laywomen who lived at home (Documented; "at home" Scudder's, Widely Accepted). She pressed for clergy reform, the return, peace with Florence and a crusade (Documented). At Avignon the pope put the peace terms in her hands, but Florence's envoys would not deal with her and it failed (Documented). The Dialogue was dictated in 1378 (Documented; the trance is her secretaries' account). After: her circle put the Dialogue into Latin; printed by 1472, English by 1519; Pius II canonized her (Widely Accepted).

**Shape and length.** Founder or leader. About 4:05, about 610 words; quotes, glosses and credited witnesses about 330. Opening: Raymond's account of the Avignon audience, where the pope fell silent.

**Connection.** She could not write, so her voice passed through her secretaries, who wrote her letters and book and carried the Dialogue into Latin, print and English. The claim that she brought the pope back comes above all from that same circle.

**Quotes** (none lifted; none on the tree).
- "While I was still in the city where I was born, I smelled the stench of the sins committed in the papal court…" (from Raymond's Latin; the Acta Sanctorum scan is damaged, Mirbt's clean print confirms it).
- "I tell you: come, come, come. Do not wait for time, because time does not wait for you."
- "To hear it told, this seems a thing past believing. But to those who wrote it down and heard it, it does not seem so. And I am one of them." (Guidini, a secretary)
- Three short phrases in narration; "with her leading him" is held only in the damaged scan, so the card prints the English alone.

**Sources differ.** Raymond: she led the pope to return; her own letters: he had already "decided on your return and announced it"; Gobelinus credits Bridget's revelations; Platina a bishop's retort. Arrival in Rome 17 January 1377 (Scudder) against 13 January "1376" (Mansi). The Dialogue 1378 (manuscripts) against 1370 (Thorold's title page).

**Gaps.** Not held: the Italian of her letters, contemporary Lives of Gregory XI, any canonization bull or year. The Who profile lacked Raymond's full Life; it is now held, in a damaged scan. The tree's documented story on her tradition entry makes claims nothing held supports ("nearly four hundred" letters, the needle in her foot). Teresa is a link for the card, not a sibling.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught "excommunication", which no held source says (replaced by the pope's sentences, the interdict and war); a fourth Raymond quote hidden in beat 3; "she could not have written it" (overstated); line numbers. All fixed.
