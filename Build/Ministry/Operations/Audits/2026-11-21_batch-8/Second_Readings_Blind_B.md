## B8-17
Source: `cic/texts/catherine-of-siena_libro-della-divina-dottrina-ita_fiorilli1912.txt`, lines 14405-14407 (Italian).

Read: "Questo a udire pare che sia cosa da non crédare; ma a coloro, che lo scrissero e udîro, nollo pare cosí; e io so' uno di quegli."

Rendering: To hear of this, it seems a thing beyond belief. But it does not seem so to those who wrote it down and heard it, and I am one of them.

## B8-18
Source: `cic/texts/philokalia_ton-ieron-neptikon-v2-grc_tzelatis1893.txt`, lines 42167-42170 (Greek).

Read (OCR normalized): "οἱ δ᾽ ὀμφαλοψύχους τούτους ὀνομάζοντες, πρὸς τῷ συκοφαντεῖν σαφῶς ὧν κατηγοροῦσι· τίς γάρ ποτε τούτων ἐπ᾽ ὀμφαλοῦ λέγει τὴν ψυχήν;"

Rendering: But those who call these men "navel-souls" are plainly slandering the people they accuse, besides everything else. For which of these men ever says that the soul is in the navel?

Scan: the OCR has stray quotation marks (a closing mark after "κατηγοροῦσι" and one before "ἣν ὄψιν" in the line above), and "χα-τηγοροῦσι", "ὀμφα-λοῦ" are broken across lines with Latin-looking "χ" for κ. The reading above is my normalization. The Greek sentence is not complete inside the 20 words: "πρὸς τῷ συκοφαντεῖν" ("besides slandering") sets up a second charge that only comes after the question ("πρὸς γοῦν τῷ γενέσθαι δήλους…"). So "besides everything else" stands in for a clause that continues past the passage. The question itself reads as an aside inside the sentence.

## B8-19
Source: `cic/texts/palamas_opera-omnia-pg150-grc-lat_migne1865.txt`, lines 67527-67533 (Greek).

Read (OCR normalized): "Καὶ τοίνυν ὅταν ἀκούσῃς τῶν Πατέρων, ἀμέθεκτον λεγόντων τὴν οὐσίαν τοῦ Θεοῦ, ἐκείνην μοι νόει τὴν ἀνεκφοίτητόν τε καὶ ἀνέκφαντον· ὅταν δ᾽ αὖθις μεθεκτὴν, νόει μοι τὴν φυσικῶς προσοῦσαν τῷ Θεῷ πρόοδον, καὶ τὴν ἔκφανσιν, καὶ τὴν ἐνέργειαν, καὶ οὕτως στέργων ἀμφότερα, ὁμόλογος ἔσῃ τοῖς Πατράσι."

Rendering: So, when you hear the Fathers say that the essence of God cannot be shared in, please understand them to mean the essence that does not go out of itself and is not revealed. But when you hear them say again that it can be shared in, understand them to mean the going-out that belongs to God by nature, and the revealing, and the energy. If you hold to both in this way, you will be in agreement with the Fathers.

Scan: damaged in several words, all readable from context. "προποῦσαν" I read as "προσοῦσαν" (belonging to); "ἔχφανσιδ" as "ἔκφανσιν" (revealing, manifestation); "ὁμό- / Àoyo;" as "ὁμόλογος" (in agreement). "μοι" ("for me", "I ask you") is a conversational particle, rendered as "please". The second "ὅταν δ᾽ αὖθις μεθεκτὴν" has no verb of its own; "you hear them say" is carried over from the first clause.

## B8-20
Source: `cic/texts/gregoras_byzantina-historia-v3-grc-lat_bekker1855.txt`, lines 552-553 (Latin; this is the edition's Latin version of Gregoras's Greek, and the request quotes the Latin).

Read: "dogmata polytheismi simulque atheismi parturiret"

Rendering: [he] was in labour with teachings of many gods and, at the same time, of no God.

Scan: clean. The five words sit inside a longer sentence: the speaker recalls that Palamas, "pregnant with these disasters", was giving birth to these teachings. "Polytheismi" is "of polytheism" and "atheismi" "of atheism"; I have put them as "many gods" and "no God" to keep the contrast plain. The birth image (parturiret, "was in labour with") is the text's own.

Rendering: I say that we have judges here who cannot be wrong and who take no side: Holy Scripture, which can neither lie nor deceive. We have it here in Hebrew, Greek and Latin. Let both sides accept these as fair and just judges.

## B8-22
Source: `cic/texts/kidd_documents-illustrative-of-the-continental-reformation-lat-deu-fra-eng_1911.txt`, lines 26134-26139 (Latin; Zwingli to Bern, 30 May 1529).

Read: "Nam ista pax quam quidam tantopere urgent, bellum est non pax. Et bellum cui nos instamus pax est, non bellum; non enim sitimus cuiusquam sanguinem, neque etiam per tumultum hauriemus; sed in hoc sumus, ut oligarchiae nervi succidantur."

Rendering: For this peace that some people press for so hard is war, not peace. And the war we are pressing for is peace, not war. We do not thirst for anyone's blood, and we will not shed it in an uprising either. Our aim is this: to cut the sinews of the oligarchy.

Scan: clean. The 38 words end at "succidantur"; the next sentence ("Id nisi fiat…") is not rendered. "Hauriemus" is literally "we will drain / draw off" (blood), here "shed". "Per tumultum" is "through a riot / uprising". "Nervi succidantur" is "the sinews be cut", that is, its strength be crippled.
