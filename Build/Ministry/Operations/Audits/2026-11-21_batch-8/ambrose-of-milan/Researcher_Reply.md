# Ambrose of Milan: researcher's reply

**Essence.** In 374 the crowd at Milan shouted for their governor, Ambrose, not yet baptized, to be bishop; eight days after his baptism he was (Documented). He held that the church's buildings and communion were not the emperor's to command: he would not give a church to the empress's party, and he kept Theodosius from communion until he did public penance for the massacre at Thessalonica (Documented).

**Also told.** Hymn-singing began at Milan while the people held the church, and spread to almost all the West (Documented by Paulinus and Ambrose; the spread Widely Accepted). He took Augustine in "like a father" and baptized him (Documented). Force on both sides: the court's fines, prisons and soldiers, and his crowd seizing a priest of the court's party, whom he rescued; and Callinicum, where Christians had burned a synagogue (Documented).

**Shape and length.** Founder or leader. About 4:05, about 610 words, never past 675; about 240 already go to quotes, plain-sense glosses and credited witnesses. Opening: the acclamation in the church.

**Connection.** Milan's singing became the West's custom, and his refusal of church and communion became the remembered moment an emperor bowed to church discipline.

**Quotes** (none lifted).
- "They also say that the people have been led astray by the songs of my hymns. I do not deny it. … So they have all become teachers, who could hardly be pupils."
- "I dare not offer the sacrifice if you mean to be there. What is not allowed after the blood of one innocent person is shed, is it allowed after the blood of many? I do not think so."
- "I have not lived among you in such a way that I am ashamed to live. Nor am I afraid to die, because we have a good Lord."
- The phrase "The palaces belong to the emperor, the churches to the bishop."

**Sources differ.** The election: Paulinus, a child's voice began the shout; Rufinus, one shout from the crowd. The basilica year: the editor 385, Augustine's count 386. The penance: Ambrose's private letter against Theodoret's later scene at the church door. The seven thousand dead is Theodoret's alone ("it is said").

**Gaps.** No Latin of Ambrose's letters or sermons is held: the first two quotes and the phrase are from the English alone. The last words are held only in OCR scans, but two prints agree. Barred, as `imperial-juridical-christianity` uses them: "within the church, not above it", Augustine's "prepared to die with their bishop", and his lines on the hymns.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught a promise to change the Callinicum order told as a cancellation, a motive put on the hymns, and the editor's "lawyer by training"; and that leaving out Paulinus's torture claim softened the record (now told, credited to him).
