# Source sheet: Ambrose of Milan

Researcher's sheet for batch 8 (2026-11-21). The job (Mark's, 2026-11-21): the governor acclaimed bishop of Milan before he was baptized, who held the basilicas against the empress's Arian party, made the emperor Theodosius do public penance after the massacre at Thessalonica, brought hymn-singing to the West, and baptized Augustine. Shape: Founder or leader. New card. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

## Setting

S1. **Before he was bishop.** AMB-ed: "about a.d. 370 he was appointed by the Prætorian Prefect Probus governor of Liguria and Æmilia, with the rank of consular" (AMB 930–932). Probus: "Go and act, not as a judge, but as a bishop" (AMB 935–936; also PAUL 3309–3312, Paulinus's own telling). Birth: "probably St. Ambrose was born, as seems most likely, a.d. 340 … the birth year of St. Ambrose might be 333 or 340" (AMB-ed 914–921); his father "prefect of the Gauls", chief residence Trier (AMB-ed 911–914). Witness: editor (birth), Paulinus (Probus). Confidence: governor, Documented (also Rufinus, RUF 30231–30232 "consularis eiusdem provinciae fasces regebat"; Socrates, SOC 14242–14248); birth year Contested (editor: 333 or 340). Plain words: "the governor of the province around Milan".

S2. **Milan and the tradition.** CEN `ambrosian-milan-standalone`: name "Ambrosian Milan (standalone)", shortName "Ambrose's Milan", informalName "The Milan of Ambrose", dates "c. 374-397", place Milan, Italy. CEN `ambrose-of-milan`: dates "374-397", placeLabel Milan, era 2. The spoken name is "Ambrose's Milan". The tradition is his own city church; the story need not name it more than once. Milan as the court's seat: Socrates, Justina "going to Milan while her son was still young" (SOC 15410–15411); Augustine came "to Milan" when the prefect of Rome sent a teacher there (CONF 8515–8521). Plain words: "Milan, in northern Italy, where the western emperors then held court" (the court's residence: AMB Letter XX throughout; CEN tradition `relationsSummary` "the seat of the western court").

S3. **Dates.** Election 374 (AMB-ed 624–626). Consecrated "December 7, a.d. 374" (AMB-ed 964–966). Basilica crisis: AMB-ed dates Letter XX to "Easter, a.d. 385" (AMB 41448–41449) and the chronology puts the Holy Week persecution in 385 (AMB-ed 703–706); Augustine puts it "about a year, or not much more" before his own baptism at Easter 387 (CONF 13750–13752; CONFL 14234–14236), which points to 386; CEN tradition says 386. Callinicum 388 (AMB-ed 725–726, Ep. XL). Thessalonica 390 (AMB-ed 732–737; THDT-ed 16515). Death: "Good Friday night, April 4–5, a.d. 397" (AMB-ed 1116–1117); Paulinus: before daybreak, his body lay in the church "the same night in which we kept the vigil of Easter" (PAUL 5761–5763).

## Events

### Acclaimed bishop, 374

E1. **The election, by Paulinus.** "At the same time, Auxentius being dead, a bishop of the Arian heresy … Ambrose, since the people in seeking a bishop were rising to revolt and it was his care to quell sedition … proceeded to the church; and there while he was addressing the populace, the voice of a child is said to have suddenly cried out among the people: 'Ambrose Bishop.' At the sound of this voice the mouths of all the people were changed, all crying: 'Ambrose Bishop.' Thus those who disagreed most violently before, because both the Arians and the Catholics wished the other party to be defeated and a bishop of their own to be appointed, suddenly insisted on this one man with miraculous and incredible harmony" (PAUL 3191–3204; OCR "miractilous"). Latin: "subito uox fertur infantis in populo sonuisse: 'Ambrosium episcopum.' ad cuius uocis sonum totius populi ora conuersa sunt adclamantis: 'Ambrosium episcopum'" (PAUL 3144–3147). Witness: insider, about 38 years later. Confidence: the acclamation Documented (E2, E3 agree); the child's voice Contested (Paulinus alone, "is said", *fertur*).

E2. **The election, by Rufinus.** "Interea defuncto apud Mediolanium Auxentio haereticorum episcopo utriusque partis populi diversis studiis ferebantur … Ambrosius tunc consularis eiusdem provinciae fasces regebat. is cum perniciem civitati videret inpendere, pro loco atque officio suo confestim ecclesiam seditionem populi mitigaturus ingreditur. cumque inibi multa secundum leges et publicam disciplinam pro quiete et tranquillitate perorasset, pugnantis inter se et dissidentis populi subito clamor et vox una consurgit Ambrosium episcopum postulantes: baptizari hunc protinus clamant, erat enim catechumenus" (RUF 30228–30238; OCR "defuneto", "tune", "eonsularis" corrected by eye). In plain English: the people of both parties were divided; Ambrose, then governor of the province, went into the church to calm them; after he had spoken at length, "suddenly a shout and one voice rose up" from the quarrelling people, demanding Ambrose as bishop; they shouted that he be baptized at once, "for he was a catechumen". Witness: later, about 28 years. Documented. No child.

E3. **The election, by Socrates.** The people "suddenly came to an unanimous agreement, crying out 'that Ambrose was worthy of the bishopric' … having baptized him—for he was then but a catechumen—they were about to invest him with the episcopal office. But although Ambrose willingly received baptism, he with great earnestness refused to be ordained"; the emperor Valentinian said "his choice was by the voice of God rather than by the votes of men" (SOC 14253–14267). Witness: later, Greek, about 65 years. Documented as his account.

E4. **He resisted, in his own words.** Letter LXIII.65: "How I resisted my ordination, and lastly, when I was compelled, endeavoured that it might at least be deferred, but the prescribed rule did not prevail against the popular eagerness" (AMB 45416–45419). *On the Duties of the Clergy* I.1.4: "For I was carried off from the judgment seat, and the garb [*infulis*] of office, to enter on the priesthood, and began to teach you, what I myself had not yet learnt. So it happened that I began to teach before I began to learn" (AMB 1844–1855, note cut). Paulinus adds that he fled the city at night and later hid on an estate, and was given up (PAUL 3299–3324); and that, to put the people off, he had torture applied to persons, and had public women brought openly into his house (PAUL 3205–3210 English; the second act in Latin only, PAUL 3239–3241, "publicas mulieres publice ad se ingredi fecit", its English lost at a page break of the scan). Witness: own words (resistance); Paulinus (the flight and the stratagems). Confidence: resistance Documented; the stratagems Documented as Paulinus's account (AMB-ed calls them "some questionable expedients", AMB 959–962).

E5. **Baptized and consecrated within eight days.** Paulinus: "he demanded that he should not be baptized save by a Catholic bishop; for he carefully guarded against the heresy of the Arians. And so on being baptized he is said to have fulfilled all the ecclesiastical offices and on the eighth day was consecrated bishop" (PAUL 3322–3328; Latin PAUL 3266–3271, "octavo die episcopus ordinatus est"). AMB-ed: "Eight days later, December 7, a.d. 374, he was consecrated Bishop" (AMB 964–966). Witness: Paulinus; editor (the day). Documented (the eighth day, Paulinus); the calendar date editor's.

E6. **The predecessor.** Auxentius, "the intended Arian Bishop of Milan" after Dionysius was banished in 355; "in 369 Auxentius was excommunicated in a synod at Rome, but succeeded in maintaining his post" (AMB-ed 942–953). Sulpicius: at Milan Martin set up a monastery; "There, too, Auxentius, the originator and leader of the Arians, bitterly persecuted him; and, after he had assailed him with many injuries, violently expelled him from the city" (SULP 950–953). The live `damasus-of-rome` story tells that a council at Rome under Damasus condemned "Auxentius of Milan" (CEN, from Theodoret). Witness: Sulpicius (Martin), editor (dates). Documented.

### The basilicas, mid-380s

E7. **What the court asked, and who asked.** Justina, mother of the boy emperor Valentinian II, "was still an Arian … Justina led him to support a demand of the Arians for the use of the Portian basilica, situated outside the walls of Milan. This being refused, a second application was made for the large and newer basilica within the city" (AMB-ed 993–1002). Ambrose: "this time it was not the Portian basilica, that is the one outside the walls, which was demanded, but the new basilica, that is the one within the walls, which is larger. First of all some great men, counsellors of state, begged of me to give up the basilica … I replied, of course, that the temple of God could not be surrendered by a Bishop" (AMB 41455–41463). Augustine: Justina "persecuted Thy servant Ambrose in the interest of her heresy, to which she had been seduced by the Arians" (CONF 13751–13765, note cut). Rufinus: Justina threatened the bishops with exile unless they restored the decrees of Ariminum (RUF 30442–30443) and sent armed men to break the doors and drag the bishop into exile (RUF 30452–30454). Witness: own words; Augustine; Rufinus. Documented. Plain words for the court's party: "the Homoians, Christians who would not say the Son is of one being with the Father; their opponents called them Arians". The census uses "Homoian" (CEN `ambrose-of-milan` floorNote; tradition `relationsSummary`).

E8. **His answer: the palaces and the churches.** Letter XX.19: "At last the command was given: Surrender the Basilica. My reply was, it is not lawful for me to surrender it, nor advantageous for you, O Emperor, to receive it. … It is asserted that everything is lawful for the Emperor, that all things are his. My answer is: Do not, O Emperor, lay on yourself the burden of such a thought as that you have any imperial power over those things which belong to God. … The palaces belong to the Emperor, the churches to the Bishop. Authority is committed to you over public, not over sacred buildings" (AMB 41676–41693, the editor's note at 41684–41687 cut). XX.8: "if he asked of me what was mine, that is, my land, my money, or whatever of this kind was my own, I would not refuse it … but that those things which are God's are not subject to the imperial power" (AMB 41528–41531). XX.22: "I cannot surrender the basilica, but I may not fight" (AMB 41754–41755). Witness: own words (his report of his own answers). Documented.

E9. **The force on both sides.** XX.5: "I heard that a certain Castulus, who, the Arians said, was a priest, had been seized by the people … I sent priests and deacons and rescued the man from violence" (AMB 41496–41504). XX.6: "Thereupon the heaviest sentences were decreed, first upon the whole body of merchants … two hundred pounds' weight of gold was required within three days' time … The prisons were full of trades-people" (AMB 41506–41514). XX.11: "Before day when I left the house the basilica was surrounded by soldiers" (AMB 41559–41560). XX.13: the soldiers "began to come to our assembly … the soldiers themselves said that they had come for prayer not for fighting" (AMB 41578–41583). XX.22: the notary: "If you are a tyrant I wish to know it, that I may know how to prepare against you" (AMB 41747–41749). XX.26: "the Emperor had commanded the soldiers to retire from the basilica, and that the sums which had been exacted of the merchants should be restored" (AMB 41802–41805). XX.27: the emperor: "If Ambrose bade you, you would deliver me up to him in chains" (AMB 41818–41819). XX.28: the chamberlain Calligonus: "I will take your head from you" (AMB 41825–41826). *Sermon Against Auxentius* 1: he had received an order "that I was to go hence, whither I would" (AMB 42099–42101). Paulinus: soldiers were sent to guard the doors so that no one could enter, but turned and let the people in (PAUL 3610–3621). Augustine: "The pious people kept guard in the church, prepared to die with their bishop" (CONF 13766–13767; **barred**: used in `imperial-juridical-christianity`). Witness: own words for both sides' acts; Paulinus; Augustine. Documented as his report; the court's words survive only in his report.

E10. **The hymns, in his own words.** *Sermon Against Auxentius* 34: "They declare also that the people have been led astray by the strains of my hymns. I certainly do not deny it. That is a lofty strain, and there is nothing more powerful than it. For what has more power than the confession of the Trinity which is daily celebrated by the mouth of the whole people? All eagerly vie one with the other in confessing the faith, and know how to praise in verse the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. So they all have become teachers, who scarcely could be disciples" (AMB 42628–42642, note at 42629–42636 cut). Witness: own words. Documented.

E11. **The hymns began then, and spread: Paulinus and Augustine.** Paulinus 13: "At this time antiphons, hymns, and vigils began first to be practiced in the church of Milan. The devotion to this practise continues even to this very day not only in the same church but almost through all the provinces of the West" (PAUL 3622–3625; Latin PAUL 3570–3573: "hoc in tempore primum antiphonae, hymni et uigiliae in ecclesia Mediolanensi celebrari coeperunt. cuius celebritatis deuotio usque in hodiernum diem non solum in eadem ecclesia uerum per omnes paene prouincias occidentis manet"). Augustine IX.7.15: "At this time it was instituted that, after the manner of the Eastern Church, hymns and psalms should be sung, lest the people should pine away in the tediousness of sorrow; which custom, retained from then till now, is imitated by many, yea, by almost all of Thy congregations throughout the rest of the world" (CONF 13770–13775; CONFL 14240–14245). The Augustine passage is already told in paraphrase in `imperial-juridical-christianity`; this story credits Paulinus for the spread and does not quote Augustine's lines. Witness: Augustine eyewitness; Paulinus later insider. Documented as their witness; that Ambrose brought congregational hymn-singing to the West, Widely Accepted.

E12. **Which hymns are his.** Augustine, *Confessions* IX.12.32: "there came into my mind those true verses of Thy Ambrose" and quotes "Deus creator omnium" (CONF 14346–14363; CONFL 15068–15080: "recordatus sum ueridicos uersus Ambrosii tui: tu es enim, deus, creator omnium polique rector uestiens diem decoro lumine, noctem sopora gratia, artus solutos ut quies reddat laboris usui mentesque fessas alleuet luctuque soluat anxios"). AMB-ed: "some hymns, probably from four to twelve of those ascribed to him being genuine, and in use to the present day" (AMB 1082–1084). The *Te Deum* is "ascribed by some to Ambrose" (CONF-ed 13719). Witness: Augustine (that "Deus creator omnium" is his), editor (the count). Confidence: Deus creator his, Documented; how many, Contested.

E13. **The martyrs' bodies (not in the job).** Letter XXII and Paulinus 14; told in CEN tradition `documentedStories`. Set aside (Leave out).

### Augustine at Milan

E14. **Augustine heard him and was baptized by him.** *Confessions* V.13.23: "And to Milan I came, unto Ambrose the bishop … That man of God received me like a father" (CONF 8520–8527; CONFL 8568–8572: "ad eum autem ducebar abs te nesciens, ut per eum ad te sciens ducerer, suscepit me paterne"). *On Marriage and Concupiscence* I.40: "the blessed Ambrose, bishop of Milan, by whose priestly office I received the washing of regeneration" (AUGP 13134). AMB-ed: baptism "at Milan by St. Ambrose at Easter" 387 (AMB 719). The live sibling `augustine-of-hippo` tells the baptism ("at Easter Ambrose baptized him"). Witness: Augustine's own words. Documented. A clause here.

### Callinicum, 388, and Thessalonica, 390

E15. **Callinicum.** Christians burned a synagogue at Callinicum; Theodosius ordered the bishop to rebuild it (AMB-ed summary, AMB 43055–43060; the emperor's own words as Ambrose reports them, Letter XLI.27: "I had indeed decided too harshly about the repairing of the synagogue by the bishop, but that has been rectified", AMB 43971–43973). Ambrose, Letter XL.8: "I declare that I set fire to the synagogue, or at least that I ordered those who did it, that there might not be a place where Christ was denied" (AMB 43204–43206). Letter XLI.28: he would not go to the altar until the emperor promised to amend the edict and end the inquiry: "I went to the altar, whither I should not have gone unless he had given me a distinct promise" (AMB 43979–43990). Paulinus tells it too (PAUL 4249–4276). Witness: own words. Documented. (The tradition story `imperial-juridical-christianity` tells it as "the hardest evidence" against that tradition; the story here gives it one clause, told plainly.)

E16. **The massacre.** Theodoret: at Thessalonica a sedition; magistrates stoned; the emperor's anger "slew innocent and guilty together. No trial preceded the sentence … It is said that seven thousand perished" (THDT 16508–16527); THDT-ed (after Hodgkin): the people killed Botheric, the Gothic general, over a jailed charioteer; "This was in 390" (THDT-ed 16510–16515). Sozomen: Buthericus killed; "the wrath of the emperor was excited immediately, and he commanded that a certain number of the citizens should be put to death" (SOC 42169–42178). Rufinus: after "quidam ex militaribus vir", a military man, had been killed by the furious people (RUF 30564–30565), the people were invited to the circus games, and soldiers surrounded them and cut down whoever they met (RUF 30565–30567). Paulinus: the emperor had promised Ambrose to pardon the city, but the counts consulted secretly with him, "the city was given to the sword for almost three hours and many innocent were slain" (PAUL 4278–4283, 4358–4360). Augustine: he punished it "compelled by the urgency of certain of his intimates", after "at the prayer of the bishops he had promised to pardon" (CD 11211–11214). AMB-ed: "some 7,000 persons" (AMB 1039; from Theodoret). Witness: several, later; the number only "it is said" (Theodoret). Confidence: the massacre Documented; seven thousand Contested (one later witness, "it is said").

E17. **His letter, in his own hand.** Letter LI: he had avoided meeting the emperor on his arrival (AMB 44019–44022) and pleads "bodily sickness" (AMB 44069–44072). LI.6: "There was that done in the city of the Thessalonians of which no similar record exists, which I was not able to prevent happening … When it was first heard of, a synod had met because of the arrival of the Gallican Bishops. There was not one who did not lament it" (AMB 44074–44085). LI.11: "Sin is not done away but by tears and penitence" (AMB 44163–44164). LI.13: "I dare not offer the sacrifice if you intend to be present. Is that which is not allowed after shedding the blood of one innocent person, allowed after shedding the blood of many? I do not think so" (AMB 44183–44186). LI.14: "Lastly, I am writing with my own hand that which you alone may read" (AMB 44188–44189). Witness: own words. Documented. The letter asks for repentance and says he will not offer the eucharist in the emperor's presence; it does not itself name a public penance.

E18. **The public penance.** Augustine, *City of God* V.26 (Latin CDL 15519–15524): "quando in Thessalonicensium grauissimum scelus, cui iam episcopis intercedentibus promiserat indulgentiam, tumultu quorundam, qui ei cohaerebant, uindicare conpulsus est et ecclesiastica cohercitus disciplina sic egit paenitentiam, ut imperatoriam celsitudinem pro illo populus orans magis fleret uidendo prostratam, quam peccando timeret iratam?" English: "being laid hold of by the discipline of the church, did penance in such a way that the sight of his imperial loftiness prostrated made the people who were interceding for him weep more than the consciousness of offence had made them fear it when enraged" (CD 11214–11218). Augustine does not name Ambrose here. Rufinus: rebuked "a sacerdotibus Italiae" (by the bishops of Italy), he admitted the crime with tears and did public penance "in conspectu totius ecclesiae", in the sight of the whole church, for the time set him (RUF 30568–30571). Paulinus 24: Ambrose "refused the emperor any opportunity of entering the church … before he should do public penance"; the emperor pleaded that David had committed adultery and murder; Ambrose: "You who have followed him as he sinned, follow him as he corrected himself"; the emperor "did not scorn public penance" (PAUL 4359–4371; Latin 4303–4314). Sozomen: Ambrose met him at the church doors, took hold of his purple robe and said "Stand back!"; Theodosius "publicly confessed his sin in the church, and during the time set apart for penance, refrained from wearing his imperial ornaments" (SOC 42160–42166, 42196–42198). Theodoret: Ambrose met him outside the porch and forbade him the threshold; after "eight months" at Christmas Rufinus the official interceded; Ambrose required a law delaying sentences thirty days; the emperor lay "prone upon the ground", plucked his hair, wept (THDT 16529–16659). Witness: Augustine (contemporary, unnamed bishop), Rufinus, Paulinus, Sozomen, Theodoret (later). Confidence: the public penance Documented; the door scene, the eight months and the dialogue Contested (later Greek witnesses; see D3).

E19. **The thirty-day law.** Rufinus: Theodosius made a law that princes' sentences of punishment be delayed thirty days, so that there might be room for mercy or repentance (RUF 30572–30575). Sozomen the same (SOC 42198–42206; SOC-ed "Not extant", 42199). Theodoret makes it Ambrose's demand (THDT 16633–16649). Witness: later. Documented as their accounts; Ambrose's part Contested (Theodoret alone). Leave out of the story (card).

### Death, 397

E20. **His last words.** Paulinus 45: count Stilicho sent the city's nobles to beg him to pray for a longer life; "When he heard this from them he answered: 'I have not so lived among you that I am ashamed to live nor do I fear to die, because we have a good Lord!'" (PAUL 5575–5653, the words at 5649–5651; Latin PAUL 5600–5602: "Non ita inter uos uixi ut pudeat me uiuere nec timeo mori, quia Dominum bonum habemus"). Possidius, *Life of Augustine* 27, tells the same reply heard by Augustine: "Non sic vixi, ut me pudeat inter vos vivere: sed nec mori timeo, quia bonum Dominum habemus. Et in his noster Augustinus senex elimata ac librata admirabatur et laudabat verba" (POSS 3772–3775, OCR "nee" for "nec"; English POSS 3816–3820: "I have not so lived that I should be ashamed to live among you, yet I do not fear to die, for we have a Lord who is good." And our Augustine, in his later days, used to admire and praise these well-weighed words). Weiskotten: "These dying words of Ambrose … are also recorded in almost exactly the same form by Paulinus" (POSS 5818–5821). Witness: two, independent of each other in wording; both written after his death. Documented as their report. Both prints are OCR scans, legible; they confirm each other's reading.

E21. **His death.** Paulinus 47: from about the eleventh hour "he prayed with his arms extended in the form of a cross. We indeed saw his lips move but his voice we did not hear"; Honoratus, bishop of Vercelli, offered him the Body of the Lord, and "when he took and swallowed it he breathed forth his spirit" (PAUL 5672–5755; Latin 5697). Paulinus 47 (Latin PAUL 5699–5701): "quia ut Elias nunquam regibus uel ullis potestatibus ita nec iste pro Dei timore loqui ueritus est" (because like Elijah he never feared to speak to kings or any powers, for the fear of God; English PAUL 5757–5759). Paulinus 48: the body lay in the great church on the night of the Easter vigil; the crowd at the funeral was "not only of Christians but also of Jews and pagans" (PAUL 5761–5782). The wonders (infants seeing him, a star, demons crying out) are Paulinus's (PAUL 5764–5774). Date: AMB-ed "Good Friday night, April 4–5, a.d. 397" (AMB 1116–1117). Documented (death, Easter); wonders in Paulinus's voice only.

## What came of him (impact, heaviest first)

I1. **A bishop who held the church's line against the emperor, remembered so.** His own rule (E8, and *Sermon Against Auxentius* 36, "the Emperor should be called the son of the Church", AMB 42661–42662). The penance was told again and again by later writers in Latin and Greek: Rufinus, Paulinus, Augustine, Sozomen, Theodoret (E18). Theodoret: "Both to me are admirable; the former for his brave words, the latter for his docility" (THDT 16690–16693). Augustine counted the penance among Theodosius's good works (CD 11210–11218). Witness: the later historians. Documented as each one's telling. (The census already gives his phrase "within the church, not above it" in the tradition story; this story tells his rule through the palaces line and the acts, not by that phrase.)

I2. **The people's hymns.** Paulinus: hymns and vigils began at Milan then and the practice remains "almost through all the provinces of the West" (E11). Augustine: copied by almost all congregations (E11). His hymns still sung, some four to twelve genuine (E12, AMB-ed). Documented (the witnesses); Widely Accepted (the spread).

I3. **Augustine.** Received him "like a father" (E14); baptized him (E14). Augustine later quoted him as an authority against the Pelagians: chapter heading "Refutation of the Pelagians by the Authority of St. Ambrose, Whom They Quote to Show that the Desire of the Flesh is a Natural Good" (AUGP 13129), and the text: "the blessed Ambrose, bishop of Milan, by whose priestly office I received the washing of regeneration, briefly spoke on this matter" (AUGP 13134) — both sides quoted him. Augustine kept his rules of conduct and praised his last words (POSS 3753–3775). Witness: Augustine's own words; Possidius. Documented.

I4. **The rite of Milan.** AMB-ed: in the Ambrosian basilica "the Ambrosian liturgy and rites, differing considerably from the Roman use of the rest of the churches of Italy, continue to this day, though doubtless with many modifications subsequent to the time of St. Ambrose" (AMB 1118–1122). CEN `ambrose-of-milan` legacy: "Milan still keeps the rite that bears his name, though how much of it goes back to him is unclear." Witness: editor and the tree's own entry. Inferential-Thin as to his own part; the rite's survival Documented. Optional, one clause.

## Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **How the acclamation began.** Paulinus: a child's voice "is said" to have cried "Ambrose bishop", and the crowd took it up (E1). Rufinus: "a shout and one voice" rose from the quarrelling people (E2). Socrates: all came suddenly to one mind (E3). Tell: the crowd shouted for him; Paulinus, his secretary, writing years later, says a child's voice began it. Credit each; do not tell the child as fact.

D2. **The year of the basilica crisis.** AMB-ed dates Letter XX to Easter 385 (AMB 41448–41449, 703–706); Augustine's count points to 386 (CONF 13750–13752); CEN says 386. Say "in the mid-380s"; the card gives the dispute. For the card: the court's own law, CTh 16.1.4, gives the right of assembly to those who hold the faith of Ariminum, threatens with death anyone who disturbs them, and is dated at Milan on 23 January 386 (`codex-theodosianus_latinlibrary.txt` 6889; `theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt` 83728–83741); its date fits Augustine's count, against the editor's 385.

D3. **How the penance came about.** Ambrose's own letter: he kept away when the emperor returned to Milan, wrote privately in his own hand, and said he would not offer the eucharist with the emperor present (E17). Rufinus: "the bishops of Italy" rebuked him (E18). Paulinus: Ambrose refused him entry to the church until public penance (E18). Sozomen: he met him at the church doors and held his purple robe (E18; Sozomen places it "after the death of Eugenius", 394, SOC 42152–42154). Theodoret, Greek, two generations later: the door scene, eight months, the official Rufinus, the speech, the emperor prostrate (E18). Augustine: the church's discipline, the people weeping, Ambrose not named (E18). Tell: his own letter, then the public penance (Augustine and the others); then that later Greek historians told a scene at the church door, which his own letter does not tell. Do not tell the door scene as fact.

D4. **How many died at Thessalonica.** "It is said that seven thousand perished" (Theodoret, THDT 16526–16527); AMB-ed repeats 7,000. Paulinus: "many innocent", "almost three hours" (E16). Say "thousands, a later historian says" or "a later Greek historian puts it at seven thousand"; never "seven thousand" as fact.

D5. **Two men named Auxentius.** His predecessor at Milan (died 374, E6) and the later Homoian bishop at the court whom the *Sermon Against Auxentius* answers (AMB 42069–42094; AMB-ed 705–706 "Auxentius claims the see of Milan"; CEN `auxentius-of-durostorum`). Name neither in the story.

D6. **Did Augustine call the penance Ambrose's doing?** In *City of God* V.26 he names only "the discipline of the church" (E18). Do not say Augustine credits Ambrose there.

D7. **The editor's and his translator's judgments.** AMB-ed on the election stratagems ("questionable expedients", AMB 959–962), on Augustine owing "his conversion even more to the life and character than to the teaching" (AMB 1069–1070); SOC-ed (Hartranft) that Sozomen calls Justina's acts persecution "but Ambrose's intolerant procedures against the Arians are not even noticed" (SOC 23167–23170). Editors' views; the story does not carry them.
