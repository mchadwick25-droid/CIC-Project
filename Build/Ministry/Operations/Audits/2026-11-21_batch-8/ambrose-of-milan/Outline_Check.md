# Outline check: Ambrose of Milan

## My own heart, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (not its `longDescription`), the batch README job and `Source_Sheet.md`. (The tool that listed the folder showed the first lines of the outline's essence before I had written this; the account below is built from the sheet's items, not from that preview.)

- **The essence.** Ambrose, the governor of the province around Milan, was acclaimed bishop by the city's divided crowd in 374 while still a catechumen, and was baptized and made bishop within eight days (E1–E5). In the city where the western court then sat, he held for twenty-three years that what belongs to God is not the emperor's: he would not hand a basilica to the court's Homoian ("Arian") party in the mid-380s (E7–E9), and after the massacre at Thessalonica in 390 he would not offer the eucharist in Theodosius's presence, and the emperor did public penance (E16–E18). That is why he is on the tree: the bishop remembered, by Latin and Greek writers alike, for telling an emperor where his power stopped (I1).
- **Also told.**
  - The hymns: his people sang hymns, some his own, while holding the basilica; Paulinus says the custom began then at Milan and spread through almost all the West (E10–E12; Documented as witness, the spread Widely Accepted).
  - Augustine: received "like a father" and baptized by him at Easter 387 (E14); a clause, since `augustine-of-hippo` tells the baptism.
  - The force in the record, told plainly: the court's fines and soldiers (E9), and Callinicum in 388, where he made the emperor drop the order to rebuild a burned synagogue (E15).
  - His death on Good Friday night 397, with the last words Paulinus and Possidius both keep (E20–E21).
  - Optional: Milan's rite that bears his name, his own part uncertain (I4).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The penance: Paulinus's own account of the church refusal is left out, and the Do not say line credits it to the wrong sources.** Paulinus, Ambrose's own secretary and a Latin witness, writes that Ambrose "refused the emperor any opportunity of entering the church … before he should do public penance" (PAUL 4354–4360; Latin 4302–4304, "copiam imperatori ingrediendi ecclesiam denegauit"). Sozomen says the same, "forbade him to enter the church" (SOC 42193–42194).

Beat 6 sets "his own letter" against "a Greek historian two generations later, Theodoret". The Do not say line calls "barred the door" "Theodoret's (and Sozomen's) telling". Told that way, the listener would take the church refusal to be only a late Greek legend, and that is misleading. What only the later Greek writers tell is the face-to-face meeting at the doors: Sozomen's purple robe and "Stand back!", and Theodoret's porch and speech.

Correction:
- **Beat 6**, after Q2 and before the public penance, insert: "Paulinus, his secretary, says Ambrose would not let the emperor into the church until he had done public penance (PAUL 4354–4360; Documented as Paulinus's account)."
- **Beat 6**, then change the later telling to: "Later Greek historians, Theodoret among them, tell of Ambrose meeting the emperor face to face at the church door; neither his own letter nor Paulinus tells that scene (D3)."
- **Do not say**, replace the door line with: "Not 'Ambrose met the emperor at the church door' as fact. The meeting at the door is Sozomen's and Theodoret's. That he refused the emperor entry to the church until penance is Paulinus's (and Sozomen's): credit it. His own letter says only that he kept away, wrote, and would not offer the eucharist with the emperor present."
- **Who tells it**, Paulinus: add "the refusal of entry to the church (beat 6)."

### MINOR

**M1. Words to explain.** These words are missing a gloss or need a plain word instead:
- **"notary"**: today the word means a legal official. Use "a court secretary".
- **"the prefect"**: use "the emperor's chief minister" (AMB-ed note at 41465–41467, "one of the four great officers of the Empire").
- **"counts and tribunes"**: give them by role, "the court's high officers".
- **"Pelagians"** (What came 3): drop the name and say "in a later dispute over whether children are born bearing Adam's sin, where his opponents quoted Ambrose too" (AUGP 13128–13134).
- **"eucharist"**: it is used inside the gloss for "offer the sacrifice". Prefer "lead the communion service".
- **"tyrant"**: the outline makes the notary's question a must-hear. Gloss it once as "a man setting up his own power against the emperor's". Ambrose's own answer supports this sense: bishops never seized imperial power, and "priests had never been tyrants, but had often suffered from them" (AMB 41761–41776).

**M2. The hymns are told twice.** What came 1 opens "began at Milan in those days, Paulinus says", which repeats beat 3. Correction: What came 1 begins with the spread only: "That custom, Paulinus says, still went on in his own day, some thirty years later, almost through all the provinces of the West." The phrase "in his own day" is Paulinus's day, so it should not read as Ambrose's.

**M3. Q1 rendering.**
- Restore "certainly": "I certainly do not deny it." The rendering should keep every clause.
- "Songs of my hymns" is clumsy. Use "the tunes of my hymns" (de Romestin's "strains").
- In the short form, "So" loses what it points back to. Add to Q1's job: "The narrator first gives the plain sense of the cut middle: the whole people sing their faith in the Father, Son and Holy Spirit every day."

**M4. Citations for beat 2's must-hear line.** "He had no arms but his own body to offer" is not in E9. Cite AMB 41761–41763 ("I had arms, but only in the Name of Christ, that I had the power of offering my own body") and AMB 41531–41533 ("if my body, I will go at once").

**M5. Do not say: add these lines.**
- "Not 'a priest of the court's party' bare. Ambrose writes 'a certain Castulus, who, the Arians said, was a priest' (AMB 41496–41497). Say 'a man the court's party called a priest'."
- "Not 'two hundred pounds of gold from each merchant'. It was demanded of the merchants as a body, within three days (AMB 41506–41511)."
- "Keep 'almost' in 'almost three hours' (PAUL 4354–4355)."
- "No motive for the massacre in the narrator's voice. Theodoret, Sozomen and Rufinus give the emperor's anger (THDT 16516–16521; SOC 42174–42176; RUF 30564). Augustine and Paulinus say he had promised pardon and was pushed by men around him (CD 11211–11213; PAUL 4278–4283). If a motive is told, credit both."
- "The emperor who approved the election (beat 1) is not the boy emperor of beat 2, and he is not Theodosius. Give him as 'the emperor of the day', or leave him out."

**M6. Sibling field.** Add `ambrosian-milan-standalone`, the census `toldWithin` tradition, which is live and tells the acclamation, the basilicas, the penance, the baptism and the rite. It shares no quote with this outline. Its job is the city church's story; this story's job is the man. Note for the coordinator only, since that text is not this thread's to touch: it says "within a week" and "in 386" where this outline says "within eight days" and "the mid-380s".
