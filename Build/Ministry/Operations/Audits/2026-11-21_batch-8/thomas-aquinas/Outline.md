# Outline: Thomas Aquinas

Sheet items (S, E, I, L, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each. ASM is the Acta Sanctorum for March I (the Bollandists, William of Tocco's Life, the sworn inquiry of 1319); ST4 and ST8 the Leonine Latin of the Summa; GER Gerard de Frachet's Latin; ASJ the Acta Sanctorum for July III; BUL Pius V's bull; AAS Pius X; TYN Tyndale.

- **The essence:** Thomas Aquinas, a Dominican friar who taught theology at Paris in the thirteenth century, wrote the Summa of Theology, a teaching book that sets out the case against each point and then answers it (E7, E9; Documented, his own words). He held that faith and reason cannot truly conflict: the opposite of a truth can never be proved, so every argument against the faith can be answered (E8; Documented, his own words). The church made him a saint in 1323 and ranked his feast with those of its great teachers (I1, I2; Documented).
  Where the job says he wrote the Summa "to show that faith and reason do not conflict": his own stated aim is to teach beginners in good order (E7). The no-conflict claim is his teaching inside the Summa (E8), and Tocco says the like of another book, the Contra Gentiles (E11, Tocco's judgment; that book is not held). Tell it so.
- **Also told:**
  - **His family held him to stop him joining.** As a young man at Naples he joined the friars; his kin seized him on the road and held him in a castle, and could not make him give up the habit. The held sources differ on who did it and for how long (E1–E4, D1–D3; Documented as each one's account).
  - **He stopped writing months before his death.** Around December 1273, after Mass at Naples, he wrote no more; to his companion he said everything he had written seemed to him straw. This rests on a sworn witness three steps from the event; Tocco tells it differently (E13, E14, D4; Documented as each one's account).
  - **He died on the road to a council** at the monastery of Fossanova, on 7 March 1274 (E16, E17; Documented).
  - **Canonized in 1323**, by Pope John XXII (I1; Documented; one held note says 1318, in error, D6).
- **Length:** about 4:30 at most (about 675 words by the scorer), aiming at 4:00. Counted first: the three quotes, about 110 words (Q1 about 55, Q2 about 42, Q3 about 13); Augustine's answer in the narrator's words, about 30; the plain sense of the words to explain, about 60 (friar and Order of Preachers, master of theology, Summa, article, habit, canonized, Doctor of the Church, penance, council); the witnesses credited, about 75 (Gerard, Tocco, the chain behind the straw line, the pope as Tocco reports him, Tyndale). That is about 270 words before any event. The rest carries six beats and what came of him. Why past three minutes: the job names four turns (the family's force, Paris and the Summa, the end of the writing, the canonization), and the force and the straw line each need their witnesses named. What does not fit is in **For the card, not the story**.
- **Cut first, in order** (the writer drops from here, top down, if the draft runs past 675 words; each cut item is kept in **For the card, not the story**). Together the items free about 30 counted words, past the 13 by which the count puts the outline over 640.
  1. Students crowding his classes at Paris, as Tocco says (beat 4): about 8 words. "He became a master of theology at Paris" stays.
  2. The Dumb Ox, and Albert's answer, by Tocco's "it is said", that this ox would one day bellow so loud the whole world would hear (beat 3): about 22 words. "The order sent him to study under Albert the Great at Cologne" stays.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: born around 1225 (S2; the Bollandists' reckoning; never a year as fact); joined the friars as a young man (S4; no year); a master of theology at Paris in the 1250s (E6; the year is disputed, D5); stopped writing around December 1273 (E13; the month is the witness's, the year follows from his order of events); died 7 March 1274 (E17); canonized 1323 (I1).
  - Place: the lands of the counts of Aquino, on the border between the kingdom of Sicily and the pope's lands, south of Rome (S1; "between Rome and Naples" is enough); the monastery of Monte Cassino (S3); Naples (S4, E13); Cologne (E5); Paris (E6); Fossanova, a monastery near Terracina, south of Rome (S6).
  - Tradition: the tree's name is "The Dominican Order" (census). Say "the Dominicans, the Order of Preachers" once.
  - Language: he wrote in Latin.

**Words to explain** (the first time each is heard; plain word or short gloss, worked into the sentence)

- **friar / the Dominicans / Order of Preachers**: "the Dominicans, the Order of Preachers: poor, travelling friars trained in theology to preach" (census `the-dominican-order`, why). Do not say "mendicant" bare.
- **habit**: "the friars' robe". Gerard and Tocco's point is that he would not take it off.
- **master of theology**: "a master of theology, a full teacher in the university". The year is not given (D5).
- **Summa of Theology**: "his Summa of Theology, a summary of the whole faith, written as questions". Say once how an article runs: it opens with the case against, then answers.
- **article**: one question of the Summa, argued through; or say 'one question'.
- **penance**: "the part on penance, the sacrament of confession and forgiveness".
- **council**: "a council of the whole church, which the pope had called at Lyon".
- **canonized**: "declared a saint".
- **Doctor of the Church**: say what the bull did: "ordered his feast kept like those of the four great Doctors, the church's chief teachers". Do not say "made him a Doctor of the Church" bare.
- **heretic**: no gloss; the beat says what he wrote of them.
- **Duns**: "Duns Scotus, a Franciscan teacher" in the Tyndale clause.

- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The question he tried to answer: how can the whole faith be taught in order, so that every argument against it is heard and answered? What he taught: that faith and reason do not truly conflict, shown in his own method, case against first, then the answer. Who learned it: his order, the schools, and the popes who made his teaching a standard. The trap is a list of books; show the idea through one article in his own words (Q1), and his rule for it (Q2).
- **Sibling:** none in the batch. On the tree:
  - `bonaventure` (live, read; job: the Franciscan minister general and his Life of Francis). It does not mention Thomas. Here: one clause on the road to Lyon (beat 6), through a line both sources hold: he too was bound for the council. The "same day at Paris" story is a sixteenth-century Life's and is doubted (L1, D5): for the card, not the story.
  - `augustine-of-hippo` (live, read; job: grace, the Confessions, the Donatists). Its quotes are not used. Here: Thomas answers the case against God in Augustine's words from his Handbook, the Enchiridion (E9), which that story does not use.
  - `boethius` (live, read). Its "if possible, reconcile faith and reason" is barred, and its definition of a person is on its card. Here: not told, or one optional clause without quoting (L3).
- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen.** His own words, Q1: the case against God, as the Summa states it. In about three sentences: the quote; "So begins one article in the Summa of Theology of Thomas Aquinas, a Dominican friar, who went on to answer it"; then Augustine's answer in the narrator's words (E9), and straight into the essence. It reaches ahead to the Summa; beat 4 then tells the book and Q2, not this article again.
  2. Not chosen: the straw line (Q3). Strong, but it is the end of the writing and belongs at beat 5.

**Story beats** (the opening, the essence and beat 1 make the first minute, about 150 words; beats 2 to 6 follow the life once, in order)

1. **Aquino, Monte Cassino, Naples.** Born around 1225 into the family of the counts of Aquino (S1, S2). As a small boy his parents gave him to the monastery of Monte Cassino, Tocco says, at five (S3). He studied at Naples, and there joined the Dominicans, the Order of Preachers (S4). One clause; do not say why the family later objected (Do not say).
2. **The family holds him.** On the road toward Paris his kin seized him and shut him in a castle (E1). Tell the force plainly, crediting each: Gerard, writing in Thomas's lifetime, says his kinsmen held him about a year and tried every means to make him leave, but could not make him put off the habit (E1); Tocco, writing for his canonization decades after his death, when he pressed the cause in 1319, says his brothers did it at his mother's order, tore his habit, and sent a woman to tempt him, whom he drove out with a burning stick from the fire (E2, E3); a royal official told the inquiry of 1319 it was his father, for more than a year (E4). Then: they let him go (one clause; how differs, D3). The angels and the girdle are left out (Leave out).
3. **Cologne and Albert.** The order sent him to study under Albert the Great at Cologne (E5). He said so little that the friars called him the Dumb Ox; Tocco says Albert answered that this ox would one day bellow so loud the whole world would hear (E5; paraphrase; Tocco's "it is said": credit it). Keep it short.
4. **Paris, and the Summa.** He became a master of theology at Paris, and students crowded his classes, Tocco says (E6). He wrote the Summa, he said, to teach beginners in good order (E7; paraphrase). Every article opens with the case against, as in the opening. Then Q2, his rule: the opposite of a truth can never be proved (E8). He used the philosophers where reason had found the truth (E8; one clause). The same method, with its case against and its answer, also led him to write that heretics who would not turn back could be left to the rulers to be put to death; corrupting the faith, he said, was worse than forging money (E10; paraphrase; his own words; tell it plainly, without comment).
5. **Naples, December 1273: he stops.** Back at Naples, by the royal official's sworn account, after Mass in the chapel of Saint Nicholas he wrote and dictated nothing more (the whole beat is his account, credited once at its start, and the chain told at its end); the Summa stopped in its part on penance (E13, E15). His companion Reginald begged him to go on. Q3. To Reginald alone he added that it was straw beside what he had seen and what had been shown to him (E13; paraphrase). Then the witness, inside the beat: Reginald told it on his deathbed to another friar, who told a royal official, who swore to it in 1319 (E13). Required, one clause: Tocco tells it at his sister's castle, and has him say what he had written seemed small (E14, D4). What he saw is not known; say so plainly, once.
6. **The road to Lyon, and Fossanova.** The pope called him to a council at Lyon (E16). Bonaventure, the Franciscan leader, was also bound for the council; Thomas never reached it (E16–E17; the tree's `bonaventure` story). He fell ill, and asked to be carried to the monastery of Fossanova, the royal official says, where he died on 7 March 1274 (E17; the death and date Documented in both witnesses). Optional, cut first: at communion he said that if he had said anything wrongly, he left it all to the correction of the Roman Church (E17; paraphrase).

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **His order bound to his teaching.** Within forty years his own order bound its teachers to teach by his teaching and works, and to train their students in it; the chapters of 1309 and 1313 threatened punishment for those who taught against it (I0; Documented, the chapter acts).
2. **Declared a saint, as a teacher.** Sworn witnesses were heard at Naples in 1319 (I1). Tocco, who pressed the cause, reports that Pope John XXII said Thomas had lit up the church more than all the other teachers, and that he would gladly declare him a saint, provided some miracles could be found (I1; credit Tocco; paraphrase). He was declared a saint in 1323 (I1).
3. **Ranked with the great teachers.** In 1567 Pope Pius V ordered his feast kept like those of the four great Doctors of the Church (I2).
4. **The schools took his name.** In the age of the Reformation the English reformer William Tyndale complained that preachers quarrelled, "one followeth Duns, another St Thomas" (I3; short phrase, Tyndale's own English; link).

Defended: the friars' way of life, by refusing to give up the habit (E1–E4); the faith, by holding that no argument against it is a true proof (E8). Also, in the Summa, the putting to death of heretics who would not turn back (E10).

**Quotes** (3)

1. **Summa I q.2 a.3, the first case against** (ST4 5795–5809; Latin, rendered from it; light OCR damage in "contrariorum", "aliud" and "nomine", readings certain from the sentence; no second print of the Summa is held). Latin: "Videtur quod Deus non sit. Quia si unum contrariorum fuerit infinitum, totaliter destruetur aliud. Sed hoc intelligitur in hoc nomine Deus, scilicet quod sit quoddam bonum infinitum. Si ergo Deus esset, nullum malum inveniretur. Invenitur autem malum in mundo. Ergo Deus non est." Rendering: "It seems that God does not exist. For if one of two opposites were without limit, the other would be wholly destroyed. But by the name God we mean a good without limit. So if God existed, no evil would be found. But evil is found in the world. So God does not exist." Job: the opening; shows his method in his own words. The card prints the English alone; the Latin's damaged letters ('*rum', 'jaliud', 'P mine') are not confirmed by a second print.
2. **Summa I q.1 a.8** (ST4 4099–4104; Latin, rendered from it; one OCR mark only). Latin: "Cum enim fides infallibili veritati innitatur, impossibile autem sit de vero demonstrari contrarium, manifestum est probationes quae contra fidem inducuntur, non esse demonstrationes, sed solubilia argumenta." Rendering: "Faith rests on a truth that cannot fail. And the opposite of a truth can never be proved. So it is clear that the proofs brought against the faith are not true proofs. They are arguments that can be answered." "True proofs" renders demonstrationes, proof that settles a question; "proofs" renders probationes, what is offered as proof. Job: beat 4, the job's faith-and-reason claim in his words.
3. **The inquiry of 1319, Bartholomew of Capua's testimony** (ASM 187860–187861, damaged OCR "palez"; the 1735 print of the same volume, ASM35 117763–117764, reads alike, also damaged). Latin: "Raynalde, non possum: quia omnia, quae scripsi, videntur mihi paleae." Rendering: "Reginald, I cannot. For everything I have written seems to me straw." Job: beat 5, the end of the writing. If the card needs it, it prints the Latin as read in both prints; the English alone is safe.

Narrator's paraphrase, not a quote: Augustine's answer as Thomas cites it (E9, ST4 6017–6023): a God supremely good would let no evil into his works unless he were so powerful and good that he could bring good even out of evil. Credit "as Augustine wrote".

Short phrase in the narrator's sentence: Tyndale's "one followeth Duns, another St Thomas" (TYN 11871–11872). His own English; the narrator says who Duns was.

**Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from Thomas was a way of teaching the faith: state the case against in full, then answer it, using Scripture, the earlier teachers such as Augustine, and the philosophers where reason had found the truth, on his rule that the opposite of a truth can never be proved (E8, E9). The listener must be told two things. First, what the Summa is and how one article runs, so that Q1 is heard as his method and not as his belief: he wrote the case against God himself in order to answer it. Second, how it passed: his order ruled in 1309 and 1313 that its teachers must teach by his works (chapter acts); his order pressed for him to be declared a saint as a teacher, and the pope, as Tocco reports him, said he had lit up the church more than all the others (I1); popes later ranked his feast with the great Doctors (I2), and by the Reformation the schools were divided into those who followed him and those who followed others (I3). Carry it through these acts, never as a "this matters because" sentence.

**Ending:** close on the person. Either (a) the opening image seen again: the Summa he left: its third part still breaks off in its questions on penance, where he stopped, and others finished it from his earlier books (E15; say "others finished it", no name); or (b) his death at Fossanova with his words at communion (E17), told last, after What came. Recommended: (a), one or two sentences, after What came; the death itself stays at beat 6. Do not retell the stopping scene. No moral.

**Do not say**

- Any word in "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard.
- Not "Thomas believed God does not exist" or "argued that God does not exist". Q1 is the case against, which he goes on to answer.
- Not "he wrote the Summa to prove that faith and reason agree" as his stated aim. His stated aim was to teach beginners in good order (E7); the rule that nothing true can be proved false is his teaching inside it (E8).
- Not 'Thomas proved God exists' in the narrator's voice. In this same article he holds that God's existence 'can be proved in five ways' (ST4 5825–5826), and that it comes before the articles of faith rather than being one of them (ST4 5685–5689). What he held reason cannot prove is the articles of faith themselves; against them it can only answer arguments (ST4 4095–4104). If the five ways are mentioned at all, say 'he held it could be proved, and gave five ways'. In the opening, Augustine's words answer this first argument, about evil (ST4 6017–6023). They are not the article's whole answer, so say 'he answered that argument', not 'he answered the question'.
- Not "his mother imprisoned him", "his father imprisoned him" or "his brothers kidnapped him" in the narrator's voice. Credit each account (D1).
- Not "for two years" or "for a year" as fact. Credit each length (D2).
- Not "because they wanted him to be abbot of Monte Cassino" as the reason for the seizure. No held source says so.
- Not "he escaped" in the narrator's voice; say "they let him go", and credit any detail (D3).
- Not the angels or the "girdle of chastity" (E3). Not "he was tempted and resisted" as the narrator's fact; it is Tocco's.
- Not "Gerard names Thomas". Gerard does not name him; the Bollandists identify him (E1).
- Not "Albert said" as fact. "Tocco says Albert said" (E5).
- Not "he became a master in 1256", "1257" or any year (D5). Not "on the same day as Bonaventure" (D5).
- Not "he had a vision" or "a mystical experience" as fact. Bartholomew says he was "moved by a wonderful change" at Mass; Thomas says, by that account, that something had been shown to him. What it was is not known.
- Not "he never wrote again" except inside Bartholomew's account (E13).
- Not "he called the Summa straw". He said "everything I have written" (E13).
- Not "Reginald told the inquiry". Reginald was dead; the chain is Reginald, another friar, the royal official (E13).
- Not the place or the word as settled: credit 'straw' and Naples to the royal official, and 'small' and the sister's castle to Tocco.
- Not "on the feast of Saint Nicholas" as a fixed day; "around the feast of Saint Nicholas" (E13).
- Not "the Summa was finished by Reginald". Others finished it from his earlier books; who, the editors dispute (E15).
- Not 'death kept him from finishing' as fact; say 'others finished it from his earlier books'. The fourteenth-century note the editors print says "morte preventus non complevit" (ST12 75705), but Bartholomew has him stop months before his death; and name no finisher.
- Not "he died on the way to the council of Lyon, poisoned". The poisoning tale is not in any held witness (sheet, Library sweep).
- Not "he died at fifty". Tocco says he had completed his forty-ninth year (E17); say "not yet fifty" or give no age.
- Not "canonized in 1318" (D6).
- Not "the pope said he worked as many miracles as he wrote articles". That saying is not in a held source. Use Tocco's report only (I1).
- Not "Pius V made him the fifth Doctor of the Church" or "named him a Doctor". Say the feast was ordered kept like those of the four Doctors (I2).
- Not "the Angelic Doctor" as a title he was given at a date: for the card.
- Not "the whole church adopted his teaching" for the chapter acts of 1309 and 1313: it was the order's rule for its own teachers (I0).
- Not "the church still teaches..." or "the greatest theologian" in the narrator's voice.
