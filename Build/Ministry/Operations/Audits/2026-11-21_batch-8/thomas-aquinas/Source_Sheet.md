# Source sheet: Thomas Aquinas

Census id `thomas-aquinas` (new person entry; told within `the-dominican-order`, era 5, spoken name "The Dominican Order"). All paths are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are the files' own. OCR spellings are kept as printed; a bracket gives the reading where the OCR is damaged.

**Files**
- **ASM**: `acta-sanctorum_martii-tomus-1-lat_palme1865.txt`. Acta Sanctorum, March I (Palmé reprint, 1865). Holds the Bollandists' commentary (from 174198), William of Tocco's Life (from 174858) and the sworn inquiry of 1319 at Naples (from 181781). Latin. OCR fair, some damage.
- **ASM35**: `acta-sanctorum_martii-tomus-1-lat_1735.txt`. The same Bollandist volume, the 1735 print. Latin, heavier OCR damage. Used only to confirm ASM readings.
- **ST4**: `thomas-aquinas_opera-omnia-t4-summa-theologiae-lat_leonine1888.txt`. Summa theologiae I, qq. 1–49, Leonine edition. Latin. OCR fair; stray marks in some lines.
- **ST8**: `thomas-aquinas_opera-omnia-t8-summa-theologiae-lat_leonine1895.txt`. Summa II-II, first part.
- **ST12**: `thomas-aquinas_opera-omnia-t12-summa-theologiae-lat_leonine1906.txt`. Summa III, end, with the Supplement.
- **GER**: `gerard-de-frachet_vitae-fratrum-lat_reichert1896.txt`. Gerard de Frachet, Lives of the Brethren, Latin (Reichert). **GERc**: `gerard-de-frachet_lives-of-the-brethren_conway1924.txt`, Conway's English.
- **ASJ**: `acta-sanctorum_julii-tomus-3-lat-grc_palme1868.txt`. Acta Sanctorum, July III: the Bollandists' commentary on Bonaventure and Pietro Galesini's sixteenth-century Life of Bonaventure.
- **BUL**: `bullarium-romanum_magnum-bullarium-tomus2-pius-iv-to-innocent-ix-lat_cherubini.txt`. Pius V's bull of 1567. Two-column OCR, interleaved and damaged.
- **AAS**: `acta-apostolicae-sedis_v2-lat_1910.txt`. Pius X's motu proprio of 1910, quoting his encyclical Pascendi (1907).
- **TYN**: `tyndale_doctrinal-treatises_walter1848.txt`. Tyndale, Obedience of a Christian Man, with Henry Walter's notes (1848).
- **ACG**: `dominican-general-chapters_acta-capitulorum-generalium-v2-1304-1378-lat_reichert1899.txt`. Acts of the Dominican general chapters, 1304–1378 (Reichert, 1899). Latin. OCR fair, with slips.
- **DEN2**: `ehrle-denifle_archiv-literatur-kirchengeschichte-mittelalters-bd2-deu-lat_1886.txt`. Denifle on early Dominican writers' lists.

**Witness kinds.** Thomas's own words (the Summa). Gerard de Frachet: contemporary; the Bollandists say he died in 1271, before Thomas, and names him not (ASM 174212–174219). William of Tocco: a Dominican who saw and heard Thomas teach, and who pressed his canonization, presenting letters at Naples in 1319 (ASM 174240–174254); his Life is later by some 45 years and hagiographic. Bartholomew of Capua: royal chancellor (logotheta) of Sicily, who as a young man saw Thomas for years at Naples; sworn witness in 1319, mostly from hearsay (ASM 187559–187576). The Bollandists: editors, 1668 onward.

## Setting

- **S1. Family and birthplace.** Bollandist commentary: Thomas "de Aquino" drew his origin from the counts of Aquino; father Landolfo, mother Theodora (ASM 174682–174686, 174715). Tocco: his mother was at the castle of "Rocchae-Siccae [Roccasecca] in confinio regni Siciliae et Campaniae" when pregnant (ASM 175370–175371). The Bollandists print a notary's act of 1650 that Roccasecca held by "traditione antiqua" that he was born there (ASM 174746–174757). Witness: later hagiographer and editors. Confidence: family Documented (Bollandists from charters); birthplace Widely Accepted (tradition and Tocco's setting).
- **S2. Date of birth.** Bollandists: "Natus est S. Thomas sub initium anni MCCXXV, saltem ante diem vii Martii, uti ex tempore obitus et aetate ejusdem certo constat" (ASM 174758–174760), reckoned from Tocco's "quadragesimum nonum annum suae vitae perficiens" at death (ASM 180053–180054). Walter's note to Tyndale says "born in 1224" (TYN 11886). Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction ("around 1225").
- **S3. Monte Cassino.** Tocco: the parents "quinquennem ad monasterium praedictum montis Casini ... transmittunt" (ASM 175562–175564); in the hermit's prophecy they hope he will rise to its "magnos ... reditus" as its head (ASM 175378–175381). Bartholomew, from hearsay and "communi praeeloquio": the father "monachavit dictum Fr. Thomam puerum, disponens eum Abbatiae praeficere Casinensi" (ASM 187589–187591). Confidence: Widely Accepted that he was a child oblate at Monte Cassino; the intent to make him abbot rests on Bartholomew's hearsay and Tocco's prophecy (Contested as motive).
- **S4. Naples, entry into the order.** Tocco: the abbot advised he be sent "Neapolim ad studendum" (ASM 175601–175607); there Brother John of San Giuliano drew him to the Order of Preachers (ASM 175644–175650). Bartholomew: at Naples he entered the order "nondum ... pubes adhuc" (ASM 187598–187600). The Bollandists date entry 1243 at age 19 (ASM 174815; editors' reckoning). Conway's note puts the seizure in 1243 and release in 1244 (GERc 9408). Confidence: entry at Naples Documented; year Contested (editors).
- **S5. The tradition.** Bollandist heading: "Ordinis Praedicatorum" (ASM 174198–174200). The census `the-dominican-order` gives Toulouse, Paris and Bologna, and its legacy names "the university theology that Albert the Great and Thomas Aquinas produced inside the order" (census, legacy).
- **S6. Fossanova.** "In monasterio Fossae-novae prope Tarracinam" (Roman Martyrology, ASM 174669–174670); its abbot is "Abbas monasterii Fossae novae, Ordinis Cisterciensis" (ASM 182341–182342). Documented.

## Events

- **E1. The seizure: Gerard's account.** "Fuit frater quidam valde nobilis de partibus Romanis, quem cum secum duceret magister ordinis, frater Iohannes, Parisius ad proficiendum, consanguinei eius ipsum rapuerunt in via, confidentes de domino Frederico, quondam inperatore, cum quo ipsi tunc erant; ducentes autem eum ad castrum remotum, ibique tenentes eum quasi per annum et caventes, ne frater aliquis vel littera fratris posset ad eum devenire. Induxerunt eum per amicos et modis quibus poterant ad resiliendum a proposito suo; sed Dei virtute in eo agente, nec ad dimittendum habitum vel faciendum contra ordinem in aliquo eum flectere potuerunt. Desperantes autem de mutacione animi eius dimiserunt eum. Et ipse ad fratres rediens missus est Parisius et factus est magister in theologia" (GER 12148–12166; OCR marginal glosses and note marks removed). Contemporary. He is unnamed; the Bollandists identify him (ASM 174212–174214). Conway renders "quasi per annum" as "for more than a year" (GERc 9404–9420), which the Latin does not say. Confidence: Documented (contemporary); identification Widely Accepted.
- **E2. The seizure: Tocco's account.** His mother came to Naples "volens eum ... maternis monitis confirmare" (ASM 175776–175778); the friars sent him on to Rome, to be sent to Paris (ASM 175778–175796); she sent to her sons "qui erant cum Frederico Imperatore in castro Aquae-pendentis", ordering them to seize him (ASM 175798–175809); they found him resting by a spring with four friars and "non ut fratres, sed ut hostes irruerunt" (ASM 175812–175816); he was held "in monte S. Joannis et Rocchae praedictae" (ASM 175827–175828). Innocent IV, told by the friars, ordered the emperor to punish the brothers, and he had them seized (ASM 175834–175848). Later hagiographer, about 45 years on. Confidence: Documented as Tocco's account; the mother's kindly motive is Tocco's own framing (Contested).
- **E3. The prison: Tocco.** He read through the Bible there (ASM 175861–175862); "fecerunt sibi dilaniari habitum" (ASM 175887); they sent to his room "puellam pulcherrimam, cultu meretricio perornatam" (ASM 175906–175908); he drove her out with a burning brand (ASM 175994–175996); angels then girded him (ASM 176001–176012; a wonder, Tocco's voice). Held "fere per duos annos"; his mother let him be lowered "de fenestra per funem" to waiting friars (ASM 176072–176080). Confidence: Documented as Tocco's account; the girl Inferential-Thin beyond Tocco (he alone tells it in the held sources; the 1319 inquiry repeats it at ASM 186330–186336 from a later witness).
- **E4. The prison: Bartholomew.** "Sed factum est favore et potentia dicti patris, quod idem Fr. Thomas captus, fuit in castro paterno detentus, in quo plusquam per annum extitit arcte detrusus: et licet idem suus genitor sibi monachales vel laicales vestes offerret, eas numquam recipere voluit ... Demum cum ejus constantia duritate et asperitate paterna convinci non posset, pater idem multis precibus uxoris suae ... dictum Fr. Thomam a carcere liberavit" (ASM 187604–187629). Sworn hearsay, 1319. Bollandists: "Fuit autem S. Thomas in carcere plus anno, uti in Processu Canonizationis num. 76 indicatur, quod tempus duos circiter annos aliqui asserunt" (ASM 174823–174826). Confidence: Documented as Bartholomew's account.
- **E5. Cologne and Albert.** Sent to Paris and then Cologne, "ubi sub Fratre Alberto ... florebat studium generale" (ASM 176151–176155). He was silent, and "coeperunt eum Fratres vocare Bovem mutum" (ASM 176243–176246). After he answered a hard question, "fertur Magistrum Albertum dixisse ...: Nos vocamus istum bovem mutum; sed ipse adhuc talem dabit in doctrina mugitum, quod in toto mundo sonabit" (ASM 176310–176313; also ASM35 110380). Tocco, "fertur" (it is said). Confidence: Documented as Tocco's report; Albert's saying Inferential-Thin (one later witness, hearsay).
- **E6. Paris, master.** Albert urged the master of the order by letter to make him bachelor at Paris (ASM 176368–176385). Gerard: "factus est magister in theologia" (GER 12165–12166). As master, Tocco says, "tanta multitudo scholarium ejus scholas intrabat, ut vix eos locus scholarum caperet" (ASM 176743–176745); his way of teaching was "compendiosum, apertum et facilem" (ASM 176747–176748). Year: Bollandists, "Anno MCCLIII aut sequente Doctor" (ASM 174833); the July Bollandists report Echard: "non ante annum 1257" (ASJ 157684–157685). Confidence: master at Paris Documented; year Contested.
- **E7. The Summa: his stated aim.** Prologue: "Quia catholicae veritatis doctor non solum provectos debet instruere, sed ad eum pertinet etiam incipientes erudire ... propositum nostrae intentionis in hoc opere est, ea quae ad Christianam religionem pertinent, eo modo tradere, secundum quod congruit ad eruditionem incipientium" (ST4 861–870). Beginners were hindered by "multiplicationem inutilium quaestionum" and by matter not taught "secundum ordinem disciplinae" (ST4 874–883). Their own words. Documented.
- **E8. Faith and reason (his teaching).** "Cum enim fides infallibili veritati innitatur, impossibile autem sit de vero demonstrari contrarium, manifestum est probationes quae contra fidem inducuntur, non esse demonstrationes, sed solubilia argumenta" (ST I q.1 a.8; ST4 4099–4104). "Cum enim gratia non tollat naturam, sed perficiat, oportet quod naturalis ratio subserviat fidei" (ST4 4128–4131). Sacred teaching uses philosophers "ubi per rationem naturalem veritatem cognoscere potuerunt" (ST4 4134–4138). Truth about God found by reason would come "a paucis, et per longum tempus, et cum admixtione multorum errorum" (ST I q.1 a.1; ST4 1051–1054). "Fides praesupponit cognitionem naturalem, sicut gratia naturam" (ST I q.2 a.2 ad 1; ST4 5688–5690). Documented, his own words. That this was the purpose of the Summa is not his stated aim (E7); it is his teaching inside it.
- **E9. The method: the case against God.** "Videtur quod Deus non sit. Quia si unum contrariorum fuerit infinitum, totaliter destruetur aliud. Sed hoc intelligitur in hoc nomine Deus, scilicet quod sit quoddam bonum infinitum. Si ergo Deus esset, nullum malum inveniretur. Invenitur autem malum in mundo. Ergo Deus non est" (ST I q.2 a.3 obj. 1; ST4 5795–5809; light OCR damage: "*rum", "jaliud", "P mine"). Answer: "sicut dicit Augustinus in Enchiridio: Deus, cum sit summe bonus, nullo modo sineret aliquid mali esse in operibus suis, nisi esset adeo omnipotens et bonus, ut bene faceret etiam de malo. Hoc ergo ad infinitam Dei bonitatem pertinet, ut esse permittat mala, et ex eis eliciat bona" (ST4 6017–6023). Documented.
- **E10. The same method on heretics.** Objections for tolerating heretics, among them the parable of the weeds left to grow "usque ad messem" (ST8 22157–22161). Answer: heretics deserve "non solum ab Ecclesia per excommunicationem separari, sed etiam per mortem a mundo excludi. Multo enim gravius est corrumpere fidem, per quam est animae vita, quam falsare pecuniam" (ST8 22172–22177); the Church, after a first and second warning, if he is still obstinate, "relinquit eum iudicio saeculari a mundo exterminandum per mortem" (ST8 22183–22194). Documented, his own words (ST II-II q.11 a.3).
- **E11. Contra Gentiles: Tocco's reading.** "Scripsit etiam librum, qui intitulatur Contra Gentiles ... Per quod manifeste ostendit aliis gentibus, fidei non subjectis, quanta sit veritas fidei Christianae; quae si humanam rationem transgreditur, rationibus tamen naturalibus ... verisimiliter suadetur ... dum advertunt, quod fidem nostram rationibus non possunt impugnare, quibus nequeant defensores fidei rationibus respondere" (ASM 176773–176792). Tocco's judgment. The Contra Gentiles itself is not held.
- **E12. Refusals.** Tocco: he refused the archbishopric of Naples offered by bull of Clement IV (ASM 178634–178639); excused himself from King Louis's table "propter opus Summae in Theologia, quod dictaret tunc temporis" (ASM 178656–178660). Optional; Documented as Tocco's.
- **E13. He stops writing: Bartholomew's account.** "Cum dictus Fr. Thomas celebraret Missam in dicta capella S. Nicolai Neapoli, fuit mira mutatione commotus, et post ipsam Missam non scripsit, neque dictavit aliquid, imo suspendit organa scriptionis in tertia parte Summae in tractatu de Poenitentia" (ASM 187842–187847). Reginald asked why he had left so great a work; "Cui respondit dictus Fr. Thomas: Non possum" (ASM 187850–187853); pressed again: "Raynalde, non possum: quia omnia, quae scripsi, videntur mihi paleae" (ASM 187860–187861, OCR "palez"). Reginald told the countess his sister: "A festo B. Nicolai circa fuit in isto statu, ex tunc nihil scripsit" (ASM 187871–187873). Under oath of secrecy Thomas added: "Omnia, quae scripsi, videntur mihi paleae respectu eorum, quae vidi et revelata sunt mihi" (ASM 187886–187888, OCR "paleee"). The chain: Reginald, dying years later, confessed it to John del Giudice, who told Bartholomew at Anagni "paullo antequam Dominus Papa Bonifacius caperetur" (ASM 187893–187905). Confirmed in the 1735 print: "suspendit organa scriptionis in tertia parte Summae" and "Raynalde, non possum: quia omnia, quae scripsi, videntur mihi paleae" (ASM35 117740, 117763–117764, damaged OCR "palcx"). Witness: third-hand, sworn 1319. Confidence: Documented as Bartholomew's testimony; what Thomas saw is not known.
- **E14. He stops writing: Tocco's account.** At San Severino, "castro sororis suae", in ecstasy; to Reginald: "Venit finis scripturae meae, quia talia sunt mihi revelata, quod ea, quae scripsi et docui, modica mihi videntur, et ex hoc spero in Deo meo, quod sicut doctrinae, sic cito finis erit et vitae" (ASM 178936–178953). Tocco also ties it to a voice from the crucifix at Naples, "Thoma bene scripsisti de me", while he wrote the third part of the Summa, after which "parum scripsit" (ASM 178115–178120; a wonder, Tocco's voice). Confidence: Documented as Tocco's.
- **E15. The Summa breaks off.** The Leonine Tertia pars ends with q.90, "De partibus poenitentiae in generali" (ST12 63940–63943), followed by the "Supplementum tertiae partis" (ST12 72717). A fourteenth-century note the editors print: "Quia b. Thomas morte preventus non complevit hanc ultimam partem summe", others completed it "de libris eiusdem doctoris" (ST12 75705–75710); who compiled the Supplement the editors dispute (ST12 75697–75760). Agrees with E13 on where he stopped. Documented (the edition's structure); compiler Contested.
- **E16. The road to Lyon, and Bonaventure.** Bartholomew: going to the Council of Lyon at Gregory X's call, he struck his head on a fallen tree (ASM 187786–187791); Reginald said, "Vos et Fr. Bonaventura eritis Cardinales, et exaltabitis Ordines vestros"; Thomas: "In nullo statu possum esse ita utilis Ordini nostro, sicut in isto ... Sis securus, quod ego numquam in perpetuum mutabo statum" (ASM 187805–187813), from Abbot Roffredo, who was there (ASM 187814–187816). Tocco: he was carrying the book he had written against the Greeks at Urban IV's order (ASM 179459–179463). Confidence: Documented as Bartholomew's (hearsay from an eyewitness).
- **E17. Death at Fossanova.** Bartholomew: falling ill at Maenza, he asked to be carried to the monastery of Fossanova; at the door, "Haec est requies mea in seculum seculi" (ASM 187912–187919). At communion: "Sumo te pretium redemptionis animae meae, sumo te viaticum peregrinationis meae, pro cujus amore studui, vigilavi et laboravi et praedicavi et docui; nihil unquam contra te dixi ... nec sum pertinax in sensu meo; sed si quid male dixi, totum relinquo correctioni Ecclesiae Romanae" (ASM 187928–187936). Tocco has the same words, "si quid male dixi de hoc Sacramento" (ASM 179655–179662). Tocco: "Obiit ... anno Domini millesimo ducentesimo septuagesimo quarto ... septimo die Martii, hora matutinali" (ASM 180043–180046). Confidence: death and date Documented; the words Widely Accepted (two later witnesses agree).

## Impact (heaviest first)

- **I0. His order bound to his teaching.** General chapter of Saragossa, 1309: "Volumus et districte iniungimus lectoribus et subiectoribus universis, quod legant et determinent secundum doctrinam et opera venerabilis doctoris fratris Thome de Aquino, et in eadem scolares suos informent"; those found to have notably done the contrary and, when warned, unwilling to retract to be punished "quod sint ceteris in exemplum" (ACG 2550–2552, 2573–2582; OCR slips mended, as "secundura doctrinara"). General chapter of Metz, 1313: "Cum doctrina venerabilis doctoris fratris Thome de Aquino sanior et communior reputetur, et eam ordo noster specialiter prosequi teneatur", no friar teaching, determining or answering may hold assertively the contrary of what is commonly held to be his opinion (ACG 3971–3973, 4081–4086); one found to have done so, if already noted for it before, is to be removed "a lectoratus officio vel studio ... in penam", and punished more sharply if scandal follows (ACG 4104–4110). The order's own acts, before the canonization. Confidence: Documented.
- **I1. Canonized, as a teacher.** Tocco: John XXII told the friars, "Nos credimus, quod Fr. Thomas est gloriosus in coelo: quia vita sua fuit sancta, et doctrina ejus non potuit esse sine miraculo"; and to the cardinals, that they would count it a great glory "si istum Sanctum possumus catalogo Sanctorum ascribere, dummodo aliqua miracula possint de eo inveniri: quia ipse plus illuminavit Ecclesiam, quam omnes alii Doctores, in cujus libris plus proficit homo uno anno, quam in aliorum doctrina toto tempore vitae suae" (ASM 180781–180812). Tocco was the promoter of the cause (ASM 174240–174254). An inquiry heard sworn witnesses at Naples, ending 18 September 1319 (ASM 174368–174369). Canonization by John XXII in 1323, bull dated xv Kal. Aug. (ASM 174371–174374); Denifle gives 16 July 1323 (DEN2 10346); Walter's note to Tyndale says "canonized in 1318" (TYN 11894), against every other held source. The bull itself is not held. Confidence: 1323 Documented; the pope's words Documented as Tocco's report.
- **I2. His feast kept as a Doctor's.** Pius V, 1567: the feast of "Divi Thomae" to be kept "quemadmodum Sanctorum quatuor Ecclesiae Doctorum festivitates" (BUL 62527–62534, damaged two-column OCR); the bull speaks of the "certissima Christianae regula doctrinae, qua S. Doctor Apostolicam Ecclesiam infinitis confutatis haeresibus illustravit" (BUL 62599–62602, damaged). The Bollandists agree: "ut de quatuor Ecclesiae Doctoribus solet fieri" (ASM 174654–174656). Documented.
- **I3. The schools divided by his name.** Tyndale: "one holdeth this doctor, and another that; one followeth Duns, another St Thomas" (TYN 11867–11872, with a marginal gloss in the OCR). Foxe: in the dispute on Mary's conception, "some holding one part with Scotus, some the other part with Thomas Aquinas" (`foxe_acts-and-monuments-v4_cattley-townsend1837.txt` 13136–13138; the Minorites with Scotus). Documented as each one's words.
- **I4. Prescribed for the training of priests.** Pius X, Pascendi (dated 8 September 1907, AAS 34655), reprinted 1910: scholastic philosophy is to be the foundation of sacred studies, "eam praecipue intelligimus quae a sancto Thoma Aquinate est tradita"; teachers are warned that to depart from Aquinas even a little, "praesertim in re metaphysica", is not without great harm (AAS 34695–34715; it cites Leo XIII's Aeterni Patris, AAS 34714, not held). Documented.
- **I5. Carried into Greek.** The PG editors' catalogue lists Greek versions by Gennadius Scholarius of Thomas's De ente et essentia and his commentary on Aristotle's De anima (`gennadius-scholarius_opera-omnia-pg160-grc-lat_migne1866.txt` 25555–25565). Editors' catalogue. For the card.

## Links

- **L1. Bonaventure.** Reginald's words on the road to Lyon (E16). Galesini's sixteenth-century Life of Bonaventure: on the same day Thomas gained his chair at Paris, Bonaventure gained his and yielded first place "S. Thomae prae humilitate" (ASJ 163286–163290); both called by Gregory X to Lyon, "Hic adfuit, ille per obitum non potuit", and "ambo in scholis Parisiensibus condiscipuli ... in munere publice interpretandi socii" (ASJ 164891–164899). The July Bollandists doubt it: "Recte sane, si haec omnia ex idoneis auctoribus probarentur, apud quos de SS. Bonaventurae et Thomae studiorum communicatione nihil reperitur" (ASJ 157673–157676).
- **L2. Augustine of Hippo.** Answers the case against God in Augustine's words (E9).
- **L3. Boethius.** ST I q.29 a.1 tests "definitio personae quam Boetius assignat ... Persona est rationalis naturae individua substantia" (ST4 59308–59311). Already on Boethius's card; not to be quoted.
- **L4. Albert the Great.** His teacher at Cologne (E5). Census spelling, from the Dominican legacy.
- **L5. William Tyndale.** I3.
- **L6. The Dominican Order.** E1–E6; the census tradition.

## Where sources differ, or come late

- **D1. Who seized him.** Gerard: his kinsmen (E1). Tocco: his brothers, at his mother's order (E2). Bartholomew: by the father's favour and power (E4). Walter's note: "against the will of his widowed mother" (TYN 11887–11888).
- **D2. How long.** Gerard: about a year (E1). Bartholomew: more than a year (E4). Tocco: nearly two years (E3). Conway's English: "more than a year".
- **D3. How he went free.** Gerard: they despaired and let him go. Bartholomew: his father freed him at his mother's pleas. Tocco: his mother let him be lowered from a window by a rope.
- **D4. The end of the writing.** Bartholomew (third-hand): after Mass at Naples, around the feast of Saint Nicholas, "straw". Tocco: at his sister's castle, "small". Where, and the word, differ; both have him stop and say it to Reginald.
- **D5. Master's year.** 1253–1254 (Bollandists), 1257 (Echard, in ASJ). Galesini's "same day as Bonaventure" is doubted by the Bollandists.
- **D6. Canonization date.** 1323 (Bollandists; Denifle 16 July; the Bollandists' xv Kal. Aug. is 18 July); 1318 (Walter's note, in error).
- **D7. Late and partisan witnesses.** Tocco wrote for the canonization; the girl, the angels, the crucifix's voice and Albert's saying are his. Bartholomew's testimony is mostly hearsay, given 45 years after the death.

## Gaps

- Not held: the canonization bull of 1323; Bernard Gui's and Ptolemy of Lucca's accounts (the Bollandists give only Gui's chapter titles); Thomas of Cantimpré; the Summa contra Gentiles; Leo XIII's Aeterni Patris; the Paris condemnations of 1270 and 1277; Dante.
- No held source gives the year he began the Summa.
- Damaged scans: E9's objection (ST4) is legible but marked; no second print of ST I is held. E13 "paleae" is damaged in both prints of ASM but reads alike in both. BUL is badly damaged; Pius V's phrase is confirmed in sense by the Bollandists (ASM 174654–174656).
