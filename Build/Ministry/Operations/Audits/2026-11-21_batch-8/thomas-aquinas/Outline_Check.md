# Outline check: Thomas Aquinas

## My heart, written before reading the outline

**Essence.** A Dominican friar who taught theology as a master at Paris and wrote the Summa of theology, a book for beginners (his own stated aim, ST4 861–870) that takes each question by first stating the case against the faith's answer and then answering it (ST4 5795–5809, 6017–6023). Inside it he taught that faith and reason cannot truly conflict: nothing true can be proved against the faith, so arguments against it can be answered (ST4 4099–4104), and grace does not destroy nature but perfects it (ST4 4128–4131). He is on the tree because that way of teaching, and that confidence in reason under faith, became the method of the schools and of his order, and later popes made him the standard for training priests.

**Also told.** His noble family seized and held him to stop him being a friar, told differently by each witness (Gerard: kinsmen, about a year; Bartholomew: by the father's power, more than a year; Tocco: brothers at the mother's order, nearly two years). At the end of 1273 he stopped writing, leaving the Summa unfinished in the treatise on penance, and said what he had written seemed to him straw (Bartholomew, third-hand, sworn) or small (Tocco). He died at the Cistercian abbey of Fossanova on 7 March 1274, on the way to the Council of Lyon; canonized by John XXII in 1323, the pope praising his teaching. Pius V kept his feast as a Doctor's (1567); Pius X made his teaching the foundation of priests' studies (1907). Also his own words that heretics may be put to death (ST II-II q.11 a.3), the same method applied: the record's force, which a fair account does not leave out.

**Compared with the outline.** The outline's heart matches this one in substance: the Summa's method (case against, then answer), his rule that nothing true can be proved false, Paris, the family's force, the end of the writing, Fossanova, 1323 and the feast. It keeps the researcher's essence, as Mark ruled (Hearts.md, item 6), and it tells the heretics passage plainly (beat 4). One word in its essence goes past the sources and the approved heart (B1). No major contribution is left out of the heart, but the order's own rulings that its teachers must follow his teaching are missing from What came and the Connection (B2).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Essence: "the strongest case against".** No held source says the objections are the strongest case. The prologue (ST4 861–883) and the article (ST4 5795–5809) give only the form: objections first, then the answer. The approved heart reads "states the case against each point" (Decision-Log, "Batch 8 hearts approved", item 6).
*Correction:* in the essence, change "sets out the strongest case against each point of the faith and then answers it" to "sets out the case against each point and then answers it".

**B2. The order's own rulings on his teaching are missing (Library sweep, What came, Connection).** `cic/texts/dominican-general-chapters_acta-capitulorum-generalium-v2-1304-1378-lat_reichert1899.txt` is held, and the sheet does not use it or set it aside (only v6, 1601–1628, is set aside). The general chapter of 1309 at Saragossa: "Volumus et districte iniungimus lectoribus et subiectoribus universis, quod legant et determinent secundum doctrinam et opera venerabilis doctoris fratris Thome de Aquino, et in eadem scolares suos informent" (lines 2552, 2573–2579), with those who teach against it to be punished (2576–2579). The chapter of 1313: "Cum doctrina venerabilis doctoris fratris Thome de Aquino sanior et communior reputetur, et eam ordo noster specialiter prosequi teneatur", no friar may hold the contrary when he teaches (3973, 4081–4086). This is the order's own act, before the canonization, and it is the plainest held support for the Connection's "carried on by his order". At present the Connection rests that claim only on Tocco pressing the cause.
*Correction:* add a sheet item (I0, Documented, the chapter acts). Add What came 1, ahead of the canonization: "Within forty years his own order bound its teachers to teach by his teaching and works, and to train their students in it; the chapters of 1309 and 1313 threatened punishment for those who taught against it." In the Connection, after "how it passed:", add "his order ruled in 1309 and 1313 that its teachers must teach by his works (chapter acts)". Add to Who tells it: "The order's general chapters, 1309 and 1313: their own decrees (What came 1)". Add a Do not say: not "the whole church adopted his teaching" here; it was the order's rule for its own teachers. This adds about 15–20 counted words. Offset them by dropping the needless gloss entries (M5) and by moving Pius X (What came 4) from optional to Leave out.

**B3. Do not say gives the writer a false correction on proving God.** The line "Not 'Thomas proved God exists' ... He held that reason cannot prove the articles of faith, only answer arguments against them (ST4 4095–4104)" implies he held God's existence cannot be proved. In the same article as Q1 he writes "Deum esse quinque viis probari potest" (ST4 5825–5826). In q.2 a.2 he holds that God's existence "demonstrabile est per effectus nobis notos" and is "non ... articuli fidei, sed praeambula ad articulos" (ST4 5682–5689). What reason cannot prove, against someone who believes nothing, is the articles of faith (ST4 4095–4098), and reason is not used "ad probandum fidem" (ST4 4109–4111). A writer following the present line could say he held that God's existence cannot be proved, which is false.
*Correction:* replace the line with: "Not 'Thomas proved God exists' in the narrator's voice. In this same article he holds that God's existence 'can be proved in five ways' (ST4 5825–5826), and that it comes before the articles of faith rather than being one of them (ST4 5685–5689). What he held reason cannot prove is the articles of faith themselves; against them it can only answer arguments (ST4 4095–4104). If the five ways are mentioned at all, say 'he held it could be proved, and gave five ways'. In the opening, Augustine's words answer this first argument, about evil (ST4 6017–6023). They are not the article's whole answer, so say 'he answered that argument', not 'he answered the question'."

**B4. Beat 5: Tocco's differing account is optional.** Bartholomew has Naples, after Mass, and "straw". Tocco has his sister's castle and "small" (ASM 178936–178953; D4). Mark's ruling (Decision-Log, "Batch 6: where sources disagree", 2026-11-19) is that where two held sources differ, the story says so and credits each. The hearts ruling (item 2) also says the reviewer checks that every disagreement the outline must credit survived. Also told already says "Tocco tells it differently", but beat 5 makes that clause optional.
*Correction:* in beat 5, change "Optional, one clause: Tocco tells it ..." to "Required, one clause: Tocco tells it at his sister's castle, and has him say what he had written seemed small (E14, D4)." Add to Do not say: "Not the place or the word as settled: credit 'straw' and Naples to the royal official, and 'small' and the sister's castle to Tocco."

**B5. Beat 6: Reginald's "you and Bonaventure will be cardinals" clashes with the tree's live Bonaventure story.** That story says "In 1273 Pope Gregory X named him a cardinal and bishop of Albano" (census `bonaventure`, Quaracchi editors). Told on the road to Lyon in 1274, Reginald's line implies Bonaventure was not yet a cardinal, against the tree's own sourced story. The outline neither credits nor notes the difference.
*Correction:* move cut-first item 2 (Reginald's cardinals line and Thomas's answer) to **Leave out** / the card, noting the clash. Keep Bonaventure as a link through a line both sources hold: "Bonaventure, the Franciscan leader, was also bound for the council; Thomas never reached it" (Bonaventure's live story: he went with the pope to Lyon; E16–E17). Update Links and Names to match. Renumber cut-first.

### MINOR

**M2. Who tells it / Names: Tocco "knew him".** The held line says only that he "ipsum B. Thomam in vita sua vidit et audivit docentem et praedicantem" (ASM 174245–174247).
*Correction:* "a friar who had heard him teach and preach, and who pressed for his canonization".

**M3. Who tells it: "about forty-five years after his death".** Beat 2 says "the Bollandists' reckoning", but the cited lines (ASM 174240–174254) give no date for the Life. They say only that he pressed the cause and presented letters at Naples in 1319.
*Correction:* in beat 2 and Who tells it, "decades after his death, when he pressed the cause in 1319"; strike "the Bollandists' reckoning".

**M4. Gloss for the Dominicans says more than its source.** "Friars who owned nothing, begged and studied in order to preach" has no held line cited. The census tradition says "friars trained in theology, poor and mobile".
*Correction:* "the Dominicans, the Order of Preachers: poor, travelling friars trained in theology to preach" (census `the-dominican-order`, why).

**M5. Needless or empty gloss entries.** "Mass" ("no gloss needed") and "heretic" (a common word; the beat itself says what happened to them) need no gloss. "Objection" is never heard.
*Correction:* delete the Mass and objection entries. Make heretic "no gloss; the beat says what he wrote of them". Recount the glosses.

**M6. Missing gloss: "article".** The opening says "one article in the Summa". In this sense the word is not known at once, and a listener may hear a newspaper article.
*Correction:* add "article: one question of the Summa, argued through; or say 'one question'". Or change the opening line to "So begins one question in the Summa of Theology".

**M7. Gerard does not name him.** The Bollandists identify him (ASM 174212–174214; E1, identification Widely Accepted).
*Correction:* add to Who tells it: "Gerard does not name him; the Bollandists identify him". Add to Do not say: "not 'Gerard names Thomas'". The card carries it.

**M8. Ending (a): the fourteenth-century note says death prevented him.** The note the editors print reads "morte preventus non complevit" (ST12 75705). Bartholomew has him stop months before his death.
*Correction:* add to Do not say: "Not 'death kept him from finishing' as fact; say 'others finished it from his earlier books'." Also: that note names one finisher, and the editors dispute who compiled the Supplement. So "others" is safe, but no name.
