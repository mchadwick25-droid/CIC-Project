# Thomas Aquinas: researcher's reply

**Essence.** A Dominican friar who taught theology at Paris (Documented) and wrote the Summa, which states the case against each point of the faith and then answers it (Documented, his words). He held that nothing true can be proved false (Documented). His stated aim was teaching beginners; faith and reason is his teaching inside the book, not the book's stated aim.

**Also told.** His family seized and held him to stop him joining (Documented as each account). About December 1273 he stopped writing (Documented as testimony; the year is reckoned). Died at Fossanova 7 March 1274 (Documented); canonized 1323 (Documented).

**Shape and length.** Teacher or thinker. About 4:10, 625 words; about 270 quotes, glosses and named sources. Opening: his own article "It seems that God does not exist", answered with Augustine's words.

**Connection.** A way of teaching (state the case against, then answer it), carried on by his order, the popes and the schools.

**Quotes** (none on the tree; none lifted).
1. "It seems that God does not exist … So God does not exist."
2. "Faith rests on a truth that cannot fail … They are arguments that can be answered."
3. "Reginald, I cannot. For everything I have written seems to me straw."

**Sources differ.** Who held him (kin, his mother's order through his brothers, or his father) and how long (one to two years); where he stopped and his word for his writing ("straw" or "small"); the year he became master; 1323 against 1318 for the canonization.

**Gaps.** The 1323 bull and the Contra Gentiles are not held. The straw line's Latin is a damaged scan, but both prints read alike. No sibling.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught two unattributed claims, now credited.
