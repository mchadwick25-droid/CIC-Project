# Second readings, blind reader C (B8-29 to B8-41)

## B8-29
Source (English, NPNF): "The palaces belong to the Emperor, the churches to the Bishop."

Rendering: The palaces belong to the emperor. The churches belong to the bishop.

## B8-30
Source (English, NPNF): "I certainly do not deny it."

Rendering: I don't deny it at all.

## B8-31
Source (English, NPNF): "So they all have become teachers, who scarcely could be disciples."

Rendering: So they have all become teachers, people who could hardly have been pupils.

## B8-32
Source (English, NPNF): "I dare not offer the sacrifice if you intend to be present."

Rendering: I do not dare to offer the sacrifice if you mean to be there.

## B8-33
Source (English, NPNF): "Is that which is not allowed after shedding the blood of one innocent person, allowed after shedding the blood of many? I do not think so."

Rendering: If something is not allowed after one innocent person's blood has been shed, is it allowed after the blood of many has been shed? I do not think so.

## B8-34
Source (Latin, Paulinus, Vita Ambrosii 45; the asterisks in the scan are apparatus marks, not text): "Non ita inter uos uixi ut pudeat me uiuere nec timeo mori, quia Dominum bonum habemus."

Rendering: I have not lived among you in such a way that I am ashamed to live. And I am not afraid to die, because the Lord we have is good.

## B8-35
Source (English, NPNF): "I began to learn my letters anew and to study to pronounce words both harsh and guttural."

Rendering: I started learning my alphabet all over again, and worked at pronouncing words that were harsh and came from the throat.

## B8-36
Source (English, NPNF): "Who but you would have laid hands upon the divine gift and the inheritance of the Apostles?"

Rendering: Who except you would have laid hands on God's gift and on what the apostles handed down?

## B8-37
Source (Latin, Augustine, Ep. 71.5, CSEL 34): "factus est tantus tumultus in plebe maxime Graecis arguentibus et inflammantibus calumniam falsitatis, ut cogeretur episcopus — Oea quippe ciuitas erat — Iudaeorum testimonium flagitare"

Rendering: Such an uproar broke out among the people, with the Greeks above all accusing him and stirring up the charge that the text was false, that the bishop was forced (the city was Oea) to ask the Jews for their testimony.

Note: the word count of 25 seems to take in the next two words, "utrum autem" ("but whether ..."), which open the following question. They are left out here because they begin a new sentence that runs past the lines given.

## B8-38
Source (English, NPNF): "But I thank the Lord that from this seed of learning sown in bitterness I now cull sweet fruits."

Rendering: But I thank the Lord that from this learning, sown like a seed in bitterness, I now gather sweet fruit.

## B8-39
Source (Latin, Leo's Tome as printed in Hefele): "Esurire, sitire, lassescere atque dormire, evidenter humanum est."

Rendering: To be hungry, to be thirsty, to grow tired and to sleep is plainly human.

## B8-40
Source (English, NPNF): "nor is the need to put down in the pages of a letter all that was allowed to be perpetrated in that meeting, not of judges but of robbers, at Ephesus;"

Rendering: Nor is there any need to set down in a letter everything that was allowed to be done at Ephesus, in that meeting of robbers, not judges.

## B8-41
Source (English, NPNF): "Yet things secular stand on a different basis from things divine: and there can be no sure building save on that rock which the Lord has laid for a foundation."

Rendering: But worldly things rest on a different footing from divine things. And nothing can be built securely except on the rock the Lord has laid as the foundation.
