# Outline check: Huldrych Zwingli

Checker, batch 8, 2026-11-21. Written before `Outline.md` was opened, from the census tradition entry (`the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva`), `Source_Sheet.md` and what Zwingli is known for.

## My own heart (written first)

**The essence.** From 1519 Zwingli, Zurich's people's priest, preached straight through whole books of the Bible, and in January 1523 he had his teaching tested in a public debate before the city's council with Scripture as the only judge. The council ruled for him and ordered every preacher to preach only what Scripture could defend; over the next two years the city removed its images and ended the Mass. That made Zurich the first of the Reformed churches, and his break with Luther at Marburg in 1529 over how Christ is present in the Lord's Supper split the Protestant reform into two families, Lutheran and Reformed.

**Also told.**
- The method: the council, not a bishop, decided; dissent was to be punished (decree of 29 January 1523; mandate of October 1523).
- The force of it: the same principle, pushed further by Grebel and Manz, became believers' baptism; the council made rebaptism a drowning offence (7 March 1526) and drowned Manz (5 January 1527); Zwingli reported the decree with approval and with regret.
- Marburg: brought by Philip of Hesse; agreement on fourteen articles and most of the fifteenth, disagreement on the bodily presence; each side's account of how it ended (Luther refused to call them brothers; Zwingli's tears are Luther's report).
- War: he urged war on the Catholic Five Cantons (1529, "this peace is war"), the First Peace of Kappel; the blockade of 1531; his death on the field at Kappel, 11 October 1531, as the city's chief pastor with the army.
- What came of it: Bullinger took his place at Zurich; Luther read his death as God's judgment, Foxe as a martyr's; Geneva's agreement with Zurich in 1549 (Consensus Tigurinus) bridged the Reformed cities.

Shape: Founder or leader.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The Library sweep misses Myconius's Life of Zwingli, and the Kappel beat rests on Foxe and editors where it would carry the claims.** The sheet says "Myconius's Life of Zwingli ... **not held**" (Library sweep, last bullet; also D3 and E12). It is held: JK12 681-1650, "The Original Life of Zwingli", by Oswald Myconius, "written in Latin in 1532" (JK12 684-690). Myconius was Zwingli's friend and wrote at Zurich the year after Kappel. It is the source Foxe draws on. It contradicts four lines of the outline:
- Why he went (beat 5, D3). JK12 1523-1533 says that in the first war he went "of his own motion" to the deliberations. In the second "he was elected chaplain, and although he told himself as well as others that he would not go, nevertheless he went."
- The blockade (beat 5, D4). JK12 1533-1546: "The cause of war never met his approval, i.e., the cutting off of provisions", a plan "accepted by the reluctant Zurichers ... even though the Zurichers had preferred a war".
- Armed, and his fall (Do not say, "Zwingli fought"). JK12 1570-1571 has him "following in the rear on horseback and armed according to our custom". JK12 1584-1594 has a report ("It was reported") that he was struck down three times, then hit by a spear, and said, "They are able, it is true, to kill the body but not the soul."
- His body and heart (Also told, beat 5, Do not say, Leave out). JK12 1596-1614: the body "was found, tried and condemned, cut into four parts, thrown into the fire". Friends found "his heart ... whole and uninjured". Myconius would not look at a piece of it.

So "what was done to his body is Foxe's later account", "the heart is a later legend" and "an editor says he went armed" are wrong as to who holds the claims.

## MINOR

- **M1.** Also told, "from 1526 it drowned Anabaptists". The law was made on 7 March 1526 and the first drowning was on 5 January 1527 (E6). Say: "in 1526 it made rebaptism a crime punished by drowning, and in 1527 it drowned the first".
- **M2.** Beat 1: the reason "to let Zurich hear the whole Gospel and how it spread" is Zwingli's reason for adding Acts (JK12 10763-10764), not for Matthew. For Matthew, Myconius says he told the canons he would teach "the history of Christ the Saviour as Matthew the Evangelist describes him", expounded "not according to human reason" but by collating the Scriptures (JK12 993-1004). Credit that to his friend. The start date also differs: Kidd has him admitted on 1 January 1519 (KD-ed 21198), and Myconius has "Christmas day, 1519" (JK12 1010). Say "at the start of 1519" and give no day, or credit both on the card.
- **M3.** Beat 3: Manz "bound hand and foot". The sentence binds his hands, pulls them over his knees and pushes a stick between his arms and legs (EG 33747-33751). Say "bound, his arms pulled down over his knees", or just "bound".

Luther writes that they "could never bear this word from me", and that they flared up whenever they heard it (DW 2670-2672). It was something he said again and again, not a reply to the tears. Say: "Luther wrote that Zwingli asked in tears to be counted brothers, and that he kept telling them they had a different spirit" (paraphrase; credit Luther).
