# Source sheet: Huldrych Zwingli

Researcher's sheet for batch 8 (2026-11-21). The job (Mark's, 2026-11-21): Zurich's people's priest from 1519, who tested each church practice in public debate before the city council, broke with Luther at Marburg in 1529 over the Lord's Supper, and died in battle at Kappel in 1531; the first of the Reformed churches. Shape: Founder or leader. New entry and card, under `the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva` (era 7). The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

## Setting

- **S1. Birth.** KD-ed 21130-21131: "Huldreich Zwingli was born, 1 Jan. 1484, at Wildhaus, in the Toggenburg, of peasant but well-to-do parents." JK01-ed 150-153 gives the same day and place. Editors' statement; no held primary gives it. *Widely Accepted (editors).* Card only.
- **S2. Before Zurich.** KD-ed 21159-21177: parish priest of Glarus 1506-16; "Zwingli thrice accompanied them as chaplain" with the Glarus contingents in papal service; then people's priest at Einsiedeln 1516-18. JK01-ed 162-171 adds that his attacks on mercenary service forced him from Glarus. *Widely Accepted (editors).* Card only.
- **S3. People's priest at Zurich, 1519.** KD-ed 21194-21202: Myconius "pressed him on the chapter for the office of people's priest ... Zwingli was elected 11 Dec. 1518 ... and admitted 1 Jan. 1519. He began to preach not, as at Einsiedeln, on the liturgical Gospels, but in continuous exposition of 'the Gospel of Matthew, the Acts of the Apostles, the Epistles to Timothy, to the Galatians and both the Epistles of Peter'". JK01 footnote 371-373: the "bishops" of the city are "the people's priests or preachers of the three parish churches in Zurich, viz., the Great Minster, Minster of our Lady, and St. Peter's." *Documented (date, editor from the chapter's records); the book list is Zwingli's own (E1).*
- **S4. The city's councils.** JK01 footnote 325-332: "This body was in two parts, the Small Council ... and the Great Council, also called the Council of the Two Hundred ... The Great Council was the deciding body on all legislative matters of importance". KD-ed footnote 21702-21705 agrees ("the Great Council, or Council of Two Hundred (actually 212), was the real legislature"). *Widely Accepted (editors).*
- **S5. Tradition.** CEN `the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva`: name "The Reformed Cities - Zurich & Geneva", dates 1519-1650; `voices`: "Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich's people's priest from 1519, whose preaching and public disputations carried the city into reform"; `people`: {"name": "Huldrych Zwingli", "year": 1519, "voice": 0, "author": "zwingli"}. Spoken name, as `john-calvin`'s relationsSummary has it: "the Reformed cities of Zurich and Geneva". *The tree's own entry.*
- **S6. Kappel.** JK01-ed 257: "Cappel, only 10 miles south of Zurich". *Editor.*

## Events

- **E1. He preached straight through the books.** Zwingli's own words, *Archeteles*, August 1522, JK12 10755-10764: "For three years ago now (to give you an account of the preaching I have done at Zurich), I preached the entire Gospel according to Matthew, and at that time I had not even heard the name of those persons to whose faction you accuse me of belonging. I added the Acts of the Apostles to the Gospel immediately, that the Church of Zurich might see in what way and with what sponsors the Gospel was carried forth and spread abroad." The same list in his sermon on foods, 1522, JK12 5232-5234: "the Gospel of Matthew, the Acts of the Apostles, the Epistles to Timothy, to the Galatians, both Epistles of Peter, on which you all heard me preach". *Documented (his own words). That he owed nothing to the faction named is his own claim; the passage does not name it.*
- **E2. The council's call to debate, 3 January 1523.** KD 23003-23006 (Latin): "Quod si vero quis post haec quoque contumacius sese sanae doctrinae opponere coeperit, nec dignis sacrae Scripturae argumentis sua probarit, in eum pro rei indignitate graviori iudicio animadvertemus". JK01 1700-1710 (English): "After this no one shall continue to preach from the pulpit whatever seems good to him without foundation in the divine Scriptures. ... But if any one in the future opposes this, and does not base his opposition upon the true Holy Scriptures, with him we shall proceed further according to our knowledge in a way from which we would gladly be relieved." *Documented (the council's own text).* Jackson's bracket dates it "[Jan. 3, 1522.]" (JK01 1714); Kidd's heading has 1523 (KD 22964-22965); see D1.
- **E3. The First Disputation, 29 January 1523.** JK01 1716-1720: "there were in the Great Council room at Zurich more than six hundred assembled, counting the local and foreign representatives". The burgomaster, JK01 1730-1765: Zwingli "has frequently offered to give the causes and reasons for his sermons ... in case a public discussion before all the clergy and the laity were granted him ... he can prove the truth of the same before my Lords ... and show him at once his error by means of the Scriptures". The bishop of Constance's vicar (Johann Faber) proposed the universities as judges (KD 23452-23454; his interruption, footnote 23457-23460). Zwingli's answer, JK01 2184-2187 and 2214-2216: "Now finally, since reference is made to the judges which my Lord Vicar thinks cannot be found outside the universities, I say that we have here infallible and unprejudiced judges, that is the Holy Writ, which can neither lie nor deceive. These we have [footnote 2188-2212] present in Hebrew, Greek and Latin tongues; these let us take on both sides as fair and just judges." The Latin version, KD 23572-23578: "Habemus hic iudices, qui nec personarum respectu errare, neque ullo alio affectu excaecati falli possunt, Dei nimirum Scripturas quae nec fallere nec mentiri norunt. Hae nobis in Hebraea simul Graeca et Latina lingua praesentes sunt. Has utrinque aequi et iusti iudicis loco recipiemus." *Documented (his words in a record edited by his own side, JK01-ed 1547-1552). Quote 1.*
- **E4. The council's decree, same day.** JK01 3733-3754: "the aforesaid mayor, council and great council of this city of Zurich ... have decided ... that Master Ulrich Zwingli continue and keep on as before to proclaim the holy Gospel and the correct divine Scriptures ... Furthermore, all your secular clergy, spiritual guides and preachers ... shall undertake and preach nothing except what they can defend by the Gospels and other right divine Scriptures ... Those which seem contrary and do not obey will be restrained in such manner that they must see and discover that they have committed wrong. Done the Thursday after Carolus, in the city of Zurich, on the 29th day of January, in the year 1523." Latin, KD 23654-23672 ("eas de se poenas sumi sentient"). Jackson calls it "the official beginning of the Reformation in German Switzerland" (JK01-ed 1590-1594). *Documented (decree); the verdict is the editor's.*
- **E5. Images and the Mass, October 1523 to April 1525.** KD-ed 22930-22941: "the [No. 197] Second Disputation, 26-8 Oct., was held ... Leo Jud leading the attack on Images ... and Zwingli challenging the Mass ... The Council then placed Zurich beyond his [the bishop's] jurisdiction". The council's mandate of 27 Oct 1523, KD 24470-24500: for now no one may move an image without the parishioners' consent; on the Mass "things shall ... remain as they are"; "whosoever, in word or deed, shall behave himself unmannerly or disobediently, our Lords will punish him severely". KD-ed 24635-24647: in June 1524 organs and relics cast out, 2-17 July images put away; 5 Dec 1524 the religious houses dissolved and, "at Zwingli's suggestion", their revenues given "to education and to relief of the poor". The last Mass: EG 19592-19609, council decision of 12 April 1525 that the Supper be held "nach der gedruckten Anleitung" and "doch soll keine Messe mehr gehalten ... werden", with Egli quoting Edlibach's chronicle: "Auf ... Mitwuchen hielt man die letzte Messe zu Zürich und ward der tisch Gotts ufgericht(et) ... und alle büecher uss dem cor genommen und verwüestet. Das gefiel einem wol, dem andren nüts. Das Mehr für Abschaffung der Messe betrug wenig Hände." English of the chronicle, KD 24739-24748: "On Wednesday in Holy Week the last Mass was celebrated in Zurich; and God's table was set up ... Yet what pleased one man well did not please his neighbour." *Documented (council and chronicle); "a few hands" is the chronicler's (Kidd: "by a small majority", KD-ed 24652).*
- **E6. The Anabaptists, 1523-1527.** KD-ed 25131-25171: at the Second Disputation "a radical party made its appearance ... led by Conrad Grebel ... and Felix Manz ... Their aim was to apply consistently the principle which governed the official reforms, and admit nothing without explicit warrant of Scripture." 18 Jan 1525 the council ordered infant baptism; adult baptism began "before 7 Feb., at Zollikon"; "on 7 March 1526 ... the Council decreed ... that Anabaptists should be drowned ... Manz was the first so to suffer, 5 Jan. 1527 ... two countrymen, 23 March 1532, the last". The mandate, EG 28360-28374: "wer also witer den anderen toufte, zuo dem wurdent unser Herren grifen und (in) nach iro jetz erkannter urteil on alle gnad ertränken lassen. Darnach wüsse sich mänklich zuo hüeten, und dass im selbs zuo sinem tod niemans ursach gebe." (English, KD 25373-25391.) Zwingli's own letter to Joachim Watt (Vadian), burgomaster of St. Gall, 7 March 1526, KD 25395-25409: "Est, honoratissime Consul, hoc ipso die per Senatum Διακοσίων decretum ut Catabaptistarum coryphaei in eam turrim, in qua prius delituerunt, coniiciantur, pane et aqua deliciantur, donec aut spiritum reddant aut manus. Additum quoque, ut qui posthac tingatur, prorsus mergatur, lata iam sententia. Sic tandem tentata satis patientia erumpit. ... Mihi vehementer incorrigibilis hominum audacia tum dolet, tum displicet. Nollem crepundia renascentis Christianismi huiusmodi exemplis inaugurari; sed Deus non sumus". Manz's sentence, EG 33679-33753: the council had its preachers instruct him from Scripture (33686-33689); he had baptized a woman after escaping (33707-33710); the executioner is "der im sin händ binden, (in) in ein schiff setzen ... und in also gebunden in das wasser werfen und in dem wasser sterben und verderben lassen" (33747-33752). *Documented (council records, Zwingli's own letter). The law and sentence are the council's; Zwingli's part held here is his report and approval, with regret.*
- **E7. Philip of Hesse brings them to Marburg, 1529.** KD 26209-26222, Philip to Zwingli, 22 April 1529: "Nos id agitamus ut Lutherum, Melanchthonem et alios quoque qui de Sacramento in sententia tua sunt loco opportuno cogamus ... Nam in hoc conventu Principum Papistae ... nequeunt nisi ut dicant nos ... inter nos ipsos de religionis doctrina non consentire". KD-ed 14369-14380: the colloquy met at Michaelmas 1529 and "turned upon the Eucharistic presence". *Documented.*
- **E8. Marburg: what was said.** Collin, a Zurich professor who went with Zwingli (KD-ed 14377-14379), KD 14491-14495: "Lutherus, priusquam inchoaret colloquium, in mensa sua scripserat, disputaturus contra Zuinglium et Oecolampadium, 'Hoc est corpus meum': ut ne ab his verbis se abduci pateretur." Zwingli's argument, KD 14746-14752: "Ergo finitam habet humanitatem. Si corpus sursum, ergo in uno loco ... Conclusio: 'Corpus Christi in uno loco; ergo non potest esse in multis locis.'" Luther, to his wife, 4 October 1529, CU 12747-12763: "we have agreed upon nearly all points, except that our opponents maintain that only the bread and wine are present in the sacrament, although admitting Christ's spiritual presence in the elements. To-day the Landgrave is making every effort to unite us, or at least to make us consider each other brethren ... But although we object to be brethren, we wish to live at peace and on good terms ... Say to Herr Pommer that Zwingli's argument was the best: 'Corpus non potest esse sine loco, ergo Christi corpus non est in pane'". Luther to Jacob Probst, 1 June 1530, DW 2566 (heading), 2658-2676: "cum Zwinglio unum et totum argumentum fuerit, corpus non posse esse sine loco et dimensione ... ut eos fratres dignaremur appellare ... hoc quod Zwinglius palam lachrymans coram Landgravio et ordinibus rogabat, dicens in haec verba: Es sind keine Leut auf Erden, mit denen ich lieber wollt eins sein, denn mit den Wittenbergern ... ita ut hanc vocem nunquam ex me possent ferre: Vos habetis alium spiritum quam nos ... Tandem id concessimus, ut articulo ultimo ponitur, ut fratres quidem non essent, sed tamen charitate nostra, quae etiam hosti debetur, non spoliarentur." **The German words in this letter are held only in a damaged scan** (DW 2668-2669 reads "Es (inb feim £eut auf Erden, mit denen ich lieber mollt eins fen, benm mil ben XOittenbergern"); no other held print gives them. The Latin around it is legible. *Documented as each witness's account: Collin for Zwingli's side, Luther for his own; the tears are Luther's report only. Paraphrase only.*
- **E9. The Marburg Articles, 3 October 1529.** Article 15, the German, WA 12420-12544 (print D column, with the editors' apparatus between; the closing sentence 12535-12544): "Und wie wol aber wir uns, ob der ware leib und blut Christi leiblich ym brod und wein sey, dieser zeit nicht vergleicht haben, So sol doch ein teyl gegen dem andern christliche liebe, so fern ydes gewissen ymmer leiden kan, erzeigen, und beyde teyl Gott den almechtigen vleissig bitten, das er uns durch seinen geist den rechten verstand bestetigen wölle, Amen." The same article first agrees that both kinds are to be used, that the Mass is not a work that wins grace for another, living or dead, that the sacrament is "ein Sacrament des waren leibs und bluts Jhesu Christi", and that the spiritual eating of that body and blood is needed by every Christian (WA 12420-12534; Latin, KD 14881-14892). Latin version of the closing sentence, KD 14892-14899 ("Etsi autem an verum corpus et sanguis Christi corporaliter in pane et vino Coenae Domini praesens sit hoc tempore non concordavimus ..."). Signatures, KD 14907-14909: "M. Lutherus, I. Ionas, P. Melanchthon, A. Osiander, I. Brentius, I. Oecolampadius, H. Zuinglius, M. Bucerus, C. Hedio." Collin, KD 14873-14877: articles drawn up "ut orbi Christiano notum fieret eos in omnibus fidei capitibus consentire, unico excepto, de modo praesentiae corporis et sanguinis Christi in Coena". *Documented (the signed text). Quote 2; German held in a fair black-letter scan, confirmed by Kidd's Latin version.*
- **E10. His own teaching on the Supper, 1530.** HK 2660-2670, *Account of the Faith*, to the emperor: "I believe that in the holy Eucharist, i. e., the supper of thanksgiving, the true body of Christ is present by the contemplation of faith ... But that the body of Christ in essence and really, i. e., the natural body itself, is either present in the supper or masticated with our mouth and teeth, as the Papists or some who look back to the fleshpots of Egypt assert, we not only deny, but constantly maintain to be an error". *Documented (his own words).* For the writer's plain sense of his view only.
- **E11. War with the Five Cantons, 1529.** KD-ed 26034-26046: Zurich seized the abbey of St. Gall's property; "Not content with this act of aggression which he had supported ... on 30 May Zwingli wrote to Bern ... to stiffen its resolution for war"; he drafted the reasons for war and the proposals that ended in the First Peace of Kappel, 25 June 1529. Zwingli to Bern, 30 May 1529, KD 26134-26145: "Nam ista pax quam quidam tantopere urgent, bellum est non pax. Et bellum cui nos instamus pax est, non bellum; non enim sitimus cuiusquam sanguinem, neque etiam per tumultum hauriemus; sed in hoc sumus, ut oligarchiae nervi succidantur. Id nisi fiat, neque Evangelii veritas, neque illius ministri apud nos in tuto erunt. Nihil crudele cogitamus". The First Peace, KD 26147-26152: "Inasmuch as no man ought to be forced in matter of faith, the Cantons and their domains shall not be put under compulsion therein". Zwingli to Conrad Sam, 30 June 1529, KD 26191-26206: "nosque docuit nos vincere posse vel nulla caede facta ... neque altera pars alteri ullum hominem vulneravit." *Documented (his letters, the treaty); "act of aggression" is Kidd's verdict. Quote 3.*
- **E12. Kappel, 11 October 1531.** KD-ed 26087-26096: Zwingli "was all for renewal of the war with the Five Forest Cantons ... Bern thought it sufficient, 15 May, to stop their supplies ... But even this meant war. In straits for food the Foresters crossed the border: defeated Zurich 11 Oct. ... and left Zwingli dead, on the field of Cappel ... On 20 Nov. ... the second Peace of Cappel ... transferred the advantage to the Catholic side". FX 26243-26305 (Foxe): the Zurich and Bern men "would permit no corn nor victual to pass unto them"; the Five Cantons "armed themselves secretly"; the Zurich relief force came down a hill "with more haste than order ... and overmatched of the multitude; which was on the 11th of October ... Among the number of them that were slain, was also Uldricus Zuinglius, the blessed servant and saint of God." FX 26346-26349: "When he was slain, great cruelty was shown upon the dead corpse: such was their hatred toward him, that their malice could not be satisfied, unless also they should burn his body being dead." FX 26351-26355: "The report goeth, that after his body was cut first in four pieces, and then consumed with fire, three days after his death his friends came ... where they found his heart in the ashes whole and unburned". JK01-ed 254-265: "Zwingli, as chief city pastor, went to the field as a non-combatant, although armed for defense, and perished the same day." EG 26695-26696 (EG-ed, list of canons): "Huldrich Zwingli, Schulherr († 11. Oct. 1531 zu Cappel ...)". *Death on the field: Widely Accepted (Kidd, Jackson, Egli, Foxe). The blockade: Kidd and Foxe. The burning of the body: Foxe, late and partisan (Inferential-Thin as fact; Documented as Foxe's account). The heart: a report Foxe repeats (late legend).*

## Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

- **I1. Zurich kept his church; Bullinger took his place.** EG 48393-48413 (council record, March 1532): "in den händlen der leer, lectionen, schuoler etc. sind bestimpt M. Heinrich Bullinger, an M. Uorichs (Zwingli) statt ... und dazemal angenommen den wolgelerten M. Theodorum Buochmann von Bischofzell, theologum, an M. Uorichs seligen statt in die lection der bibli ... und demselben verordnet M. Uorichs seligen hof zuo siner wonung; doch hat er sich güetlich begeben, durch M. Uorichs und siner kinden willen, ein zit mitliden mit inen ze han und bi im lassen wonen, bis dass die frow ein ander herberg überkommen". SHC-ed 82-91: Bullinger, "the pupil, friend, and successor of Zwingli ... was chosen his successor as chief pastor (Antistes) at Zurich, Dec. 9, 1531." KD-ed 26094-26095: "Zwingli was succeeded by Bullinger ... as antistes at Zurich". CEN tradition `entry.tile`: Bullinger "held Zurich's own pastorate for four decades after him". *Documented (council); Widely Accepted (editors, tree).*
- **I2. Marburg's split became two Protestant families.** KD-ed 14380-14390: Luther revised the articles "against 'the Sacramentaries'" into the Articles of Schwabach, which "took rank as the first of the Lutheran Symbolical Books", the condition of "a strictly Lutheran league"; "thus Philip's wider combination failed." JK01-ed 264-266: "the Reformed churches, as contrasted with the Lutheran churches, look to him as one of their great founders." Calvin, `john-calvin` relationsSummary (CEN): the Reformed tradition "began under Zwingli at Zurich in 1519." *Widely Accepted (editors, tree).*
- **I3. Geneva joined Zurich on the Supper, 1549.** CT 1-8 and 146-152: the agreement "between the Ministers of the Church of Zurich and John Calvin", Geneva, 1 August 1549, answered from Zurich. Calvin's exposition (dated CT 641, Geneva, 28 November 1554), CT 583-589: "all whom he styles Sacramentarians have one same faith and confess it as with one same mouth, and even if the two excellent doctors, Zuinglius and Œcolompadius, who were known to be faithful servants of Jesus Christ, were still alive, they would not change one word in our doctrine." CEN tradition `entry.tile`: the Consensus Tigurinus is "what formally bridges the two cities". *Documented (Calvin's claim, in English from the French; the French is not held). Paraphrase.*
- **I4. Luther's judgment on his death.** CU 16744-16749, Luther to Wenzel Link, 3 January 1532: "We are now seeing God's judgments for the second time — first on Munzer and now on Zwingli. I prophesied that God would not long suffer such blasphemies, calling us cannibals, blood-drinkers, and other horrible names. They have brought it upon themselves." Latin, DW 22231-22234: "Iudicium Dei nunc secundo videmus, in Munzero, nunc in Zwinglio." CU 16706-16708 (another letter, 1532): "whom they now declare to be one of Christ's martyrs". *Documented (Luther's words; hostile).*
- **I5. Remembered as a martyr by Protestant England.** FX 26304: "the blessed servant and saint of God". *Documented as Foxe's view.*
- **I6. Calvin's private weighing, 1539.** CL1 5163-5178, to Farel: the Zurich men "flame up into a rage if any one dares to prefer Luther to Zuingli ... if the two men are compared with each other, you yourself know how much Luther has the preference ... it would be impiety in a high degree, in our thoughts of such a man, not to entertain sentiments of honourable esteem." *Documented (Calvin's letter).* Card.
- **I7. Bible readings in place of the choir office.** KD-ed 24664-24667 and footnote 25133-25135: on 19 June 1525 the choir office gave way to Bible readings, "Prophesyings", which "were the sources of the Reformers' commentaries, and of the Puritan 'prophesyings' in England". *Editor.* Card.
- **I8. Today.** CEN tradition `experienceToday`: "The Grossmünster, Zurich — Zwingli's own church, still open to visitors". CEN `legacy`: "Reformed and Presbyterian churches around the world descend from these two cities." *The tree's own entry.*

## Where sources disagree or come late

- **D1. Dates of the First Disputation.** The council's decree is dated "the 29th day of January, in the year 1523" (JK01 3753-3754; KD 23670-23672). Jackson's own introduction says the day set "was the 23d of January, 1523" (JK01-ed 1545), and his bracket dates the summons "[Jan. 3, 1522.]" (JK01 1712), though the summons is of 3 January 1523 (KD 22964-22965). Use 29 January 1523.
- **D2. How Marburg ended.** Collin (Zurich side): on the third day articles were written to show agreement on every head of faith but one, the manner of Christ's presence (KD 14873-14877). Aurifaber's Luther-side summary: the talks were broken off on the Landgrave's order because plague had begun at Marburg, and they agreed to stop all strife and pray, "Et ita quidem amice discessum fuit" (SCH 1151-1163). Luther to his wife: agreed on nearly all points; "although we object to be brethren, we wish to live at peace" (CU 12749-12757). Luther to Probst: Zwingli asked in tears to be called brothers; Luther would not (DW 2667-2676). The signed Article 15 itself: not yet agreed whether Christ's body and blood are bodily in the bread and wine, but each side to show Christian love (WA 12535-12544). Credit each; the article is the shared text.
- **D3. Why he went to Kappel.** Foxe, from Sleidan: "an old received manner among the Zurich men, that when they go forth in warfare, the chief minister of their church goes with them", and Zwingli would not stay home while others fought (FX 26319-26330). Foxe, from Oecolampadius: "he went not out as a captain of the field, but as a good citizen with his citizens, and as a good shepherd ready to die with his flock"; "neither did he go out of his own accord, but rather desired not to go ... But the senate, being importunate upon him, would have no nay" (FX 26331-26343). Jackson: "as chief city pastor, went to the field as a non-combatant, although armed for defense" (JK01-ed 262-263). Kidd: he "was all for renewal of the war" (KD-ed 26087-26088). No held primary of 1531 tells it.
- **D4. Who blockaded.** Kidd: Bern thought it enough to stop the Five Cantons' supplies (KD-ed 26088-26090). Foxe: "the Tigurines and the Bernates, stopping all passages and straits, would permit no corn nor victual to pass unto them" (FX 26259-26261). Both: the blockade drove the Five Cantons to march.
- **D5. Kidd's verdict.** "act of aggression" (KD-ed 26039) is the editor's view of the St. Gall seizure, not a source's. Zwingli's own letter says "Nihil crudele cogitamus" (KD 26141).
- **D6. Late and wrong details.** Foxe makes him "younger than Martin Luther by four years" (FX 26367); Kidd and Jackson give 1 January 1484 (KD-ed 21130; JK01-ed 150-153), and the Luther card gives 1483. Luther wrote in 1532 that "Carlstadt has succeeded Zwingli in Zurich" (CU 16706-16707); the council record names Bullinger in his place (EG 48403). Leave both out.
- **D7. Whether the drowning law was his.** The mandate and the sentence are the council's (EG 28360-28374; EG 33679-33753). Zwingli's letter reports the decree the same day, with grief at the Anabaptists' "audacia" and the wish that the new faith had not begun with such examples (KD 25395-25409). Kidd's "as if to suit the penalty to the crime" (KD-ed 25166-25167) is the editor's comment.
