## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who.** Bernard, a young Burgundian noble who led his brothers and more than thirty companions into the small, struggling monastery of Cîteaux in 1113, then became abbot of Clairvaux and a voice that popes and kings consulted.
2. **What happened.** He preached on the Song of Songs as the love between God and the soul. He moved against Abelard, whom the pope condemned. At the pope's order he preached the crusade, wrote against killing the Jews while calling their scattering a just penalty, and took the blame when the armies turned back from Damascus. He wrote the pope a book of advice, and died with 160 monasteries under his care.
3. **Why the connection matters.** He wrote *On Consideration* as a friend's counsel to a pope he had trained. Peter never went in gold, and a pope who does follows Constantine. In 1413 Jan Hus copied those lines to argue against papal pomp.

**My own heart, written before comparing.** Essence: a young noble who brought his brothers and companions into the poor new house of Cîteaux in 1113. As abbot of Clairvaux, through his sermons and letters, he became the churchman Europe listened to most, and the order spread with him. Also told: the Song of Songs sermons; the case against Abelard; the Second Crusade he preached, and its failure, with the Jews of the Rhine; his book of advice to Eugenius; the 160 houses at his death. This matches the approved heart (`Hearts.md`; outline lines 5 to 13).

**Where the essence is complete.** It is complete at "made him their abbot, the head of a monastery" (about word 153). The pieces before that are Cîteaux with more than thirty companions (about word 85) and "Popes and kings would come to ask his word" (about word 124). That falls inside the first minute. Every part of what is also told is present and has its weight. That includes the eighty-six sermons left unfinished, Berengar, the Rhine with both halves of the letter, and the witnesses who differ on the failure.

**One line through the life.** The life is told once, in order. Abelard in 1140 is told after the Song sermons of 1135, and *On Consideration* after Damascus, as the outline's self-check intends. The story ends on the person: his death, Geoffrey's count in "that valley", and the sermon broken off by death.

**Where I got lost.** "William, who heard him preach, says the house was so small it had almost lost hope of lasting. Popes and kings would come to ask his word." With no link between them, the jump from the tiny house to popes and kings is abrupt (MINOR 6). It never reads as a children's book, and it never talks down.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. "took the cross" has no plain sense.** Words to explain lists it (outline line 35; gloss from Letter 363, "placed on a devout shoulder", E2 24037–24040). A listener in a second language will not hear that it means joining the crusade.
Change "In 1146 he preached it, promising pardon for every sin confessed with a contrite heart to all who took the cross." to:
"In 1146 he preached it, promising pardon for every sin confessed with a contrite heart. That pardon was for all who took the cross, putting its sign on their shoulder as the mark of those going east." (+13 words)
*Do not say (add to outline):* "'took the cross' bare → with its plain sense in the same sentence: putting the sign of the cross on their clothes, at the shoulder, as the mark of those going east (E7)."

**B2. "vicar of Peter" has no plain sense where it is heard.** Words to explain lists it (outline line 39: "the one who holds Peter's place"). It is the key word of the connection, and to most listeners "vicar" means a parish priest. "Peter, whose place the pope held", two sentences earlier, does not gloss it where it is heard. The fix also carries what the connection says the listener must be told (outline line 99): Hus turned the words against the popes of his own time.
Change "to argue that a pope who lives in pomp is no true vicar of Peter." to:
"to argue that the popes of his own day, living in pomp, did not truly hold Peter's place." (+3 words; Hus, HUS 6158–6164: "a true vicar of him whose place he fills")
*Do not say (add to outline):* "'vicar of Peter' bare → 'holds Peter's place' (HUS 6163–6164)."

### MINOR

**M1. "his older brothers went with him" (opening) is narrower than the source.** William says Bernard won his brothers over first, all but the youngest. That includes Bartholomew, "younger than the other brothers", and Andrew, "Bernardo etiam ipse junior" (VP 15960–15980). "Older" suggests that only his elders went. Change "his older brothers went with him" → "his brothers went with him" (−1). The outline's opening carries the same word (outline line 46).
*Do not say (add to outline):* "'his older brothers went with him' → 'his brothers went with him': younger brothers went too (VP 15974–15980)."

**M2. "Nivard soon followed." comes before the entry into Cîteaux.** It suggests he came in with the thirty. William says he stayed home and followed "a little later" (VP 16252–16256). Move the sentence to after the Cîteaux sentence: "…the old Rule of Saint Benedict strictly. Nivard followed a little later." (+2)

**M3. William is credited three times** ("his friend William says", "William, who heard him preach, says", "William says"). The kit says to name each source once, and outline line 98 says to credit William once. Change "his friend William says" → "his friend William, who heard him preach, says". Change "William, who heard him preach, says the house was so small" → "He says the house was so small". Change "William says they lived on barley bread" → "They lived on barley bread". (−6 net)

**M4. A monk "of his own order" rests on a summary, not a held text.** Bernard's letter calls Ralph only "a monk and a hermit" (E2 24262–24264), and the editor calls him "a certain monk" (E1-ed 4098–4100, 4123). "Of his own order" comes from the tree's Cistercian documented story, which is marked "from reference summaries; primary text not yet read". It is widely held and not misleading, so the wording stays. The card should say what the held letter says.

**M5. Sentences past about 30 words.**
- "Two years after he entered, the abbot of Cîteaux sent him … the head of a monastery." (31) → begin "Two years later, the abbot of Cîteaux…" (−3)
- "At a meeting of bishops at Sens there was no debate: Bernard writes that he read out the charges, …" (33) → "At a meeting of bishops at Sens, Bernard writes, he read out the charges, and Abelard walked out and appealed to the pope in Rome, over their heads." (−4; the walk-out already shows there was no debate)
- "Peter, whose place the pope held, … he said; and in such things the pope followed not Peter but the emperor Constantine." (32) → "…went in gold and silk, he said. In such things the pope followed not Peter but the emperor Constantine." (−1)

**M6. A bare jump to popes and kings.** "Popes and kings would come to ask his word." → "In time, popes and kings would come to ask his word." (+2). It keeps the reach-ahead and makes it a contrast with the tiny house.

**M7. Missing one required clause on what came of him.** What came 3 (outline line 63) marks one clause as required: his Song sermons were read for centuries (CEN `cistercian-monasticism` `legacy`). It is not part of the approved heart, so it is not blocking. After "…I love in order to love.\"", add "His Song sermons were read for centuries." (+7)

**M8. Cuts the story does not need** (none is a must-keep, the record's force, a credited disagreement or a plain sense):
- "an old robbers' den, once called the Valley of Wormwood" → "an old robbers' den" (−6). The ending's "that valley" still lands.
- "In the eighty-third sermon he said," → "He said," (−4). The number goes to the card.
- "a poor new monastery in Burgundy" → "a poor new monastery" (−2). Burgundy is already heard in the first sentence.
- "His monk Geoffrey counts" → "Geoffrey counts" (−2). He has already been credited as "his monk Geoffrey".

The total with B1, B2 and M1 to M8 is about 670 words, under the 675 limit. Getting near 4:00 would mean cutting must-keeps (the three quotes, the credited witnesses on Sens and on Damascus, the plain senses), so the story should stay near 4:25.
