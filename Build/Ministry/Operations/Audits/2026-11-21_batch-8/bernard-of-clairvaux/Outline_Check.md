# Outline check: Bernard of Clairvaux

## The checker's own heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the slate job, the tradition entry `cistercian-monasticism`, the source sheet and what Bernard is known for.

**Essence.** Around 1113 a young man of a Burgundian knightly family entered the struggling house of Cîteaux with more than thirty companions, his brothers among them (William of Saint-Thierry). Made abbot of the new house of Clairvaux, he became the voice the Cistercians spread through: by his death, by Geoffrey's count, some seven hundred monks at Clairvaux and a hundred and sixty houses under his care. Through his sermons on the Song of Songs and his letters, the tree calls him the most listened-to churchman in Europe. He is on the tree as the Cistercians' most famous voice.

**Also told.**
- The Song of Songs sermons (from 1135, 86, unfinished at his death): love its own reward, "I love because I love" (Documented).
- The papal schism of 1130: he backed Innocent (Documented; one clause).
- Sens, 1140: he pressed charges against Abelard; his account (Abelard walked out and appealed) and Berengar's hostile satire both credited; the pope's sentence (Documented as each witness's).
- His monk elected Eugenius III, 1145; Bernard's book to him, *On Consideration*: "not Peter but Constantine" (Documented), later carried by Hus (link).
- The Second Crusade: preached on the pope's order (Geoffrey, William of Tyre); his letter's promise of pardon; his letter that the Jews must not be killed, with its contempt in the same lines, and the monk Ralph stopped; Joseph ha-Kohen's later Jewish memory (Documented as each).
- The failure before Damascus, 1148; William of Tyre's rumours; Bernard's own answer, the shield line, and his blame of the crusaders' sins; Geoffrey's view (each credited; cause Contested).
- The Templars' praise (one clause, link).
- Death 1153 (Documented).

Shape: Founder or leader. What came, heaviest first: the order spread (Geoffrey's figures; the tree); the book to Eugenius read on by Hus; the Song sermons read on (Calvin, Hus; the tree's legacy line).

## BLOCKING

**B1. Nivard is told twice, and the opening puts him in the 1113 entry.** The Opening ends "he followed them, and with more than thirty companions they entered the poor new monastery of Cîteaux", and beat 1 then adds "Nivard followed his brothers soon after". William says the opposite of the opening's order. When they had gone, the boy "tunc quidem domi cum patre remansit, sed modico post evoluto tempore fratres secutus" (VP 16252–16256: he stayed at home with his father, and only a little later followed). So the opening implies Nivard went in with them, and the same event then comes twice.
*Correction:* end the Opening: "Nivard answered with Q1. Then straight into the essence: in 1113 Bernard and more than thirty companions, his older brothers among them, entered the poor new monastery of Cîteaux." Keep Nivard's following only in beat 1: "A little later the boy followed his brothers, and neither his father nor his kin could hold him (E1)". In the opening's first sentence, change "his brothers went with him" to "his older brothers went with him".

**B2. Beat 2 tells his death early.** "The sermons ran to 86, and he had not finished them when he died (E2, editor)" reaches ahead to his death. Beat 3 then goes back to 1140, and beat 7 tells the death again. Under the kit this is an event in two beats and the ending told early.
*Correction:* beat 2 keeps "the sermons ran to 86 over the years that followed"; move "he had not finished them when he died; the 86th breaks off where death stopped him (E4-ed 33259–33262, the editor's)" to beat 7, beside his death. Ending option 2 already allows this.

**B3. A word the connection needs has no gloss: "vicar of Peter".** What came 2 and the Connection turn on "no true vicar of Peter" / "not Peter's true vicar". Words to explain has no entry for it. A general listener will not know "vicar" in this sense, or why Peter and Constantine stand against each other.
*Correction:* add to Words to explain:
- "**vicar of Peter**: the one who holds Peter's place. Bernard's own word is 'heir' ('lest he whose seat you hold deny you are his heir', PL182 78509–78510). Hus: 'a true vicar of him whose place he fills' (HUS 6163–6164)."
- "**Peter / Constantine**: 'the apostle Peter, whose place the pope held' and 'the emperor Constantine'. One clause each, the first time they are heard."

Add to Names: "Peter (link, by title: the apostle Peter)" and "Constantine (link: `constantine-the-great`, 'the emperor Constantine')".

## MINOR

**M1. The sibling field misses a live story on the same event.** `cistercian-monasticism` has a documented story, "Bernard Rides to the Rhineland to Stop Radulf" (CEN `movements` [87] `documentedStories`[0]). It tells beat 5's event. Its only quoted wording, "the apple of the Lord's eye", is not in this outline, so no quote is shared. But the field should name the story and give the job difference. Its spelling of the monk is "Radulf", not "Ralph".
*Correction:* add to Sibling: "`cistercian-monasticism` documented story on Radulf (1146): it tells the Rhine as the order's story, with Ephraim of Bonn (not held). Here it is one beat of his crusade preaching, from his own letters, and does not retell the Mainz confrontation." In beat 5 and Names, give him by role ("a monk of his own order who had left his cloister"). If he is named, use "Radulf", as the tree spells him.

**M2. "He refused to lead the army" rests on the editor. The letter it cites is held, and its date is in doubt.** Letter 256.4 is in E2 (15602–15620). Bernard himself writes that at Chartres "I was chosen as general and leader of the expedition … this was not of my seeking, and was and is against my will", and he begs the pope "give me not over to the will of man". "Refused" is the editor's word (E2-ed 24198–24200). The editor heads the letter 1146, but its own text speaks of "the losses of the former army" (E2 15559). Mabillon's chronology also places a new expedition in 1150 (E1-ed 5151–5154). So the 1146 setting is uncertain in the held sources themselves.
*Correction:* take the clause out of beat 4. Move Letter 256.4 to "For the card", with the editor's 1146 and the letter's "losses of the former army" side by side. Add to Do not say: "'he refused to lead the crusade' → not in the story; the card gives his letter's own protest, with the date doubt."

**M3. Bernard's qualifier on the pomp.** Straight after "not Peter, but Constantine", Bernard writes "Consulo toleranda pro tempore, non affectanda pro debito" (PL182 78517–78518: "I advise these be borne for the time, not sought as a due"). What came 2's "held to Peter's poverty" and the Connection's "to call him back from pomp" (Schaff's wording, HUS-ed) say more than Bernard did.
*Correction:* in What came 2, write "**What he defended**: the pope's office as a shepherd's, after Peter's pattern." Add to Do not say: "'Bernard told the pope to give up his gold and horses' → he wrote that Peter never went so, and that such things could be borne for the time but not sought as the pope's due (PL182 78517–78518)."

**M4. "In Prague" is not held.** Schaff says Hus wrote during "his semi-voluntary exile from Prague" (HUS-ed 744, 14539).
*Correction:* in What came 2, write "In 1413 Jan Hus copied Bernard's words …". Drop "in Prague".

**M5. The 160 houses are "under his care", not "founded".** Geoffrey's words are "qui sub ejus cura in centum sexaginta monasteriis … sunt adducti" (VP 24345–24349). The Shape line ("What he built: … the 160 houses") and the Connection ("the houses he founded carried his sermons across Europe") push a writer toward "founded 160". The clause about carrying his sermons has no held source.
*Correction:* in Shape, write "What he built: Clairvaux; at his death, by Geoffrey's count, 160 monasteries under his care." In the Connection, replace the last two sentences before "Tell it" with "The order's spread (I1) is told with its numbers as Geoffrey's." Add to Do not say: "'he founded 160 monasteries' → 160 monasteries under his care, Geoffrey says."

**M6. Ranking: the Song sermons' later readers are thin.** Calvin, Hus (Sermon 12), and the tree's own legacy line all carry the Song sermons on: Rolle and Hilton "still quoted Bernard by name" (CEN `cistercian-monasticism` `legacy`; also tree edge 263). In the outline they appear only as an optional Calvin clause.
*Correction:* in What came 3, make one clause required: "his Song sermons were read for centuries; two hundred years on, English writers still quoted him by name (CEN legacy)". Keep Calvin optional (cut item 4).

**M7. The 700 count is told twice.** What came 1 and the chosen Ending both give Geoffrey's 700 and 160.
*Correction:* in What came 1, add "told once, at the ending".

**M8. The "take the cross" gloss says more than its source.** "Sew a cloth cross on their clothes, the sign that they had vowed to go": "sew" and "vowed" are not held. Letter 363 gives the cross "placed on a devout shoulder" (E2 24037–24040). Joseph ha-Kohen's *Chronicles* say "they put the cross upon their clothes for a sign" (`joseph-ha-kohen_chronicles-of-rabbi-joseph-ben-joshua-ben-meir-v1_bialloblotzky1835.txt` 5308–5309).
*Correction:* change the gloss to "put the sign of the cross on their clothes, at the shoulder, as the mark of those going east".

**M9. The pardon qualifier.** Also told says "promising pardon of sins to all who took the cross". The letter limits it: "pardon for every sin that you confess with contrite heart" (E2 24034–24036).
*Correction:* add to Do not say: "'pardon to all who took the cross' bare → 'pardon for every sin confessed with a contrite heart', his letter says."

**M10. The schism clause goes back in time.** Beat 2's optional clause (1130) comes after the Song sermons (1135).

**M11. The essence and Do not say pull against each other.** The essence has him "became the most listened-to churchman in Europe" as narration, while Do not say bars it as fact.
