# Bernard of Clairvaux: researcher's reply

**Essence.** In 1113 Bernard, a young Burgundian noble, brought his brothers and more than thirty companions into the poor new monastery of Cîteaux (Documented: William, who knew him). From 1115, as abbot of Clairvaux, his sermons and his letters to popes made him the most listened-to churchman in Europe (Documented; "most listened-to" is the tree's own judgment, Widely Accepted).

**Also told.** The Song of Songs sermons, from 1135 (Documented). Abelard, 1140: Bernard's charges; at Sens, by Bernard's account, Abelard walked out and appealed; Innocent II ordered him confined and his books burned (Documented). On Consideration, to Eugenius III (Documented). The crusade preached at the pope's order in 1146, with the Rhine letter on the Jews and the turn back from Damascus in 1148 (Documented); why it failed is Contested. About 160 houses and nearly 700 monks at Clairvaux at his death in 1153 (Geoffrey's figures).

**Shape and length.** Founder or leader. About 4:00, about 600 words; about 200 quotes, glosses and credited witnesses. Opening: the youngest brother, Nivard, in the street, the essence straight after.

**Connection.** Hus in 1413 copied Bernard's warning to Eugenius ("not Peter but Constantine") to argue that a pope living in pomp is no true vicar of Peter; told in paraphrase.

**Quotes** (none lifted).
- "So heaven is for you, and earth for me? This is not a fair division."
- "Love seeks no cause beyond itself, and no fruit. Its fruit is the loving itself. I love because I love; I love in order to love."
- "If one of the two must happen, I would rather people grumble against me than against God. It is good for me if he will use me as a shield. I gladly take on myself…"

**Sources differ.** Thirty or more than thirty companions; two Lives word Nivard's reply differently; Sens: Bernard's account against Berengar's satire of drunken bishops; the crusade's failure: Bernard blames sins, Geoffrey says heaven was filled, William of Tyre suspects treachery but is unsure.

**Gaps.** The Nivard Latin is held only in a damaged scan: the card prints the English alone unless the page image is checked. Not held: Otto of Freising, Odo of Deuil, any English of De consideratione or the Lives, the canonization year. The Hus outline was not done, so the shared line was not checked against it.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught Abelard's place of death (Saint-Marcel, not Cluny), an unsupported age at death, editors' dates to mark, and one added word in the shield rendering.
