## Quoted spans

1. Nivard, 15 words: **awaiting second reading**. William's Latin (VP 16242-16256) is a damaged scan whose words for heaven, fairly and this are faulty, and no other held print confirms them, so by the batch 6 ruling it is left out. No English of the Life is held. Printed instead: Alan's clean Latin fragment ut vos coelum habeatis, ego terram (VP 34743), which carries the heaven-and-earth sense but is not William's speech. The entry says so.
2. Sermon 83, 26 words: **awaiting second reading**. Printed in Eales's English (E4 32161-32164). The Latin (PL183 105304-105306) has a fault in praeter ("prster"), no held print confirms it, so it is not printed.
3. The shield, 53 words: **awaiting second reading**. No English is held. Only the first clause is printed, from PL182 75607-75608, because the rest of the passage stands in two scans (PL182 and Geoffrey's quotation) that each carry faults (PL182: feri, digne-mr, detra-nent'mm, maledieas, blaspüemorum; VP: necessc, inc, mc, blasphemurum, Heri). The rendering follows the Latin throughout.

## Differences from the research files, found on re-reading

- The sheet says Geoffrey quotes the shield passage word for word as PL182 has it. Geoffrey begins Si necesse sit where PL182 begins Etsi necesse sit. The sense the rendering takes is the same; the entry says so.
- Line numbers in the sheet and review for VP in this region are about ten lines early: the shield is at VP 20932-20937 (sheet 20922-20928), Geoffrey's heaven filled at VP 20915-20918 (sheet 20909-20913), the blind boy at VP 20940-20952 (sheet 20929-20950). The card uses the lines as found.
- The review puts the letter that calls Ralph a monk and a hermit at E2 24262-24264; those lines are page furniture. The words stand at E2 24272.
- The sheet's PL182 shield passage begins at 75604; the Etsi clause begins at 75606.
- E1-ed 4425-4429 has the house's name garbled in the scan; the card does not rely on it.

## Conflicts with existing fields

The person has no entry yet. The tradition entry `cistercian-monasticism` differs from the held texts in these places; none is changed here.
- Its story of the Rhine has Bernard's letter say that whoever touches the Jews touches the apple of the Lord's eye, and calls them living words of Scripture. The held Letter 363 says living signs, and the apple-of-the-eye line stands in Joseph ha-Kohen's account, not in Bernard's letters (E2 24062-24075; JHK 3744-3762).
- It says Radulf was a monk of Bernard's own order. Letter 365 calls him a monk and a hermit and the editor a certain monk; the order is not in the held texts.
- It says Bernard traveled to Mainz and ordered him back. The held editor, from Otto of Freising who is not held, says Bernard brought him to go back to his monastery (E2-ed 24253-24262).
- It names Ephraim of Bonn, who is not held.
- Its `voices` line calls Bernard the most listened-to churchman in Europe; the card says only that popes and kings asked his word, as the brief does.

## Claims not found

None on the card is unsupported. Not found in the held texts: the tradition entry's present-day line that the order is still here, and the year of Bernard's canonization. The Latin of William's Nivard speech cannot be printed clean from any held file.
