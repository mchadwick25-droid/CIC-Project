# Teresa of Ávila: researcher's reply

**Essence.** More than twenty years in a large, easy-going convent at Ávila; in 1562 she founded St Joseph's, a small, poor house of no more than thirteen nuns, which the city council tried to close (Documented, her own words). Then more, seventeen houses of nuns by 1582 by her editors' count, which became the Discalced Carmelites (count Widely Accepted).

**Also told.** Her confessors ordered the Life (1562 to about 1565; dates Dominant Modern Reconstruction). In 1574 a princess denounced it, and her confessor Báñez handed it to the Inquisition himself; Báñez found it not a deceiver's work, and she never saw it again (Documented as each one's account). The Interior Castle, 1577 (Documented). The 1575 order to stop founding (her account; who issued it Contested).

**Shape and length.** Founder or leader. About 4:10, about 620 words. Opens on quote 1.

**Connection.** The old rule, kept in house after house until it became an order; her teaching, written for confessors, held by the Inquisition, printed only in 1588.

**Quotes** (none lifted).
- "I crossed this stormy sea for almost twenty years, with these falls, and rising again, and badly, for I would fall once more."
- "People came to me in great fear… This struck me as funny, and made me laugh, for in this I was never afraid."
- "I thought of the soul as like a castle, made of a single diamond or of very clear crystal…" (English only)

**Sources differ.** Her entry (1533 or 1535); years in the convent (20, 22 or 25); how many houses (17 or 32); how the Life reached the Inquisition; the censure's date (6 or 7 July 1575).

**Gaps.** Her Spanish is held only for the Life. The tree's own tradition entry disagrees with the held texts on Báñez's report: for Mark.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught a jailing placed in the wrong year and an unheld year and age (removed); restored Báñez's "writings of women" testimony.
