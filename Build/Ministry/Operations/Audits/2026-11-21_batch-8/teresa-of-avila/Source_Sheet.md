# Source sheet: Teresa of Ávila

Researcher's sheet for batch 8 (2026-11-21). The job (Mark's, 2026-11-21): the Carmelite nun who, after twenty years in a large convent, founded a small strict house at Ávila in 1562 and then many more, wrote her Life for her confessors under the Inquisition's eye, and in the Interior Castle mapped prayer as rooms of a castle. Shape: Founder or leader. New person entry and new card, told within `carmelite-reform-and-spanish-mystics` (era 7). ## Setting

S1. **Birth and place.** BREV 79187–79193: "THE virgin Theresa was the daughter of a father and mother, equally honourable on account of their birth and of their godliness, [and was born] at Avila [in the kingdom of Old Castile] in Spain, [on the 28th day of March, in the year of our Lord 151 5.]" FOUND-ed 1035–1038: the Incarnation's first mass was said "in 1515, 4th April, the day on which S. Teresa was baptised." Liturgy and editor; Documented (1515, Ávila). Plain words: "born in 1515 at Ávila, a walled city of Castile in central Spain". CEN home: region "Castile, then Catholic Europe"; modern country Spain.

S2. **The tradition.** CEN `carmelite-reform-and-spanish-mystics`: name "The Carmelite Reform and the Spanish Mystics", dates "1562-1622", era 7; `people` lists "Teresa of Ávila", year 1562. Spoken name: "the Carmelite Reform and the Spanish Mystics". The order itself: CEN `the-carmelites` ("The Carmelites", era 5).

S3. **The Incarnation: a large convent outside the walls.** FOUND-ed 1034–1046: "This famous monastery had been built in the year 1513, by Doña Elvira de Medina … It stood outside the city, and was a fine and handsome house, with large gardens … In 1550, according to the history of Fray Francis de Santa Maria, bk. i. ch. ix. 1, the house held one hundred and ninety nuns ; and the Saint herself, in a letter written by her in March or April 1581, says that she lived for five-and-twenty years in a monastery wherein there were a hundred and eighty nuns." Footnote 1047: "In 1567 there were more than a hundred and fifty". Her own words in the Life: "the house was large and pleasant" (LIFE 16733; Life XXXII.12), and the nuns "left it very often" because it "was very poor"; "The rule also was kept, not in its original exactness, but according to the custom of the whole Order, authorised by the Bull of Mitigation" (LIFE 16718–16726). Own words (Documented) for large, pleasant, poor and relaxed; the number 180 is her letter as the editor reports it (letter not held; Documented as reported).

S4. **Entering the Incarnation.** LIFE-Z 677–735: Yepes, the Bollandists and Lewis "assume that she entered the convent of the Incarnation on November 2nd, 1533, and made her profession on November 3rd, 1534"; Zimmerman: "As to the profession we have overwhelming evidence that it took place on the 3rd of November, 1536, and her entrance in the convent a year and a day earlier," resting on the deed of renunciation and dowry dated 31 October 1536. BREV 79223–79225: "In the twentieth year of her age she withdrew herself among the nuns of St Mary-of-Mount-Carmel." See D1. Say "about 1535, at about twenty".

S5. **Language.** She wrote in Spanish (VIDA; title "Libro de la Vida", line 55).

## Events

### The Incarnation and the long struggle

E1. **Leaving her father's house.** LIFE 3508–3512 (Life IV.1): "I remember perfectly well, and it is quite true, that the pain I felt when I left my father's house was so great, that I do not believe the pain of dying will be greater— for it seemed to me as if every bone in my body were wrenched asunder". Own words; Documented. Optional color only.

E2. **Nearly twenty years of struggle in prayer.** VIDA 8724–8728 (Vida VIII): "Pasé este mar tempestuoso casi veinte años con estas caídas, / y con levantarme y mal, pues tornaba a caer; y en vida tan baja / de perfeción, que ningún caso casi hacía de pecados veniales". LIFE 5439–5441: "I passed nearly twenty years on this stormy sea, falling and rising, but rising to no good purpose, seeing that I went and fell again." VIDA 8729–8730: "porque ni yo gozaba de Dios, ni traía contento en el mundo" (LIFE 5447–5448: "I had no sweetness in God, and no pleasure in the world"). Own words; Documented. The "twenty years" is her time of struggle in prayer inside the convent, not the length of her stay there (D2).

E3. **The turn before the wounded Christ.** LIFE 5804–5817 (Life IX.1): "It came to pass one day, when I went into the oratory, that I saw a picture … It was a representation of Christ most grievously wounded … I threw myself on the ground beside it, my tears flowing plenteously, and implored Him to strengthen me once for all, so that I might never offend Him any more." Own words; Documented. No year in her text, and the annals' OCR (LIFE-L 2640–2668) loses the alignment of years to events, so no date is held for it. VIDA-ed 13640: the Jesuits founded their college at Ávila "en 1554". Say "years later"; no year, no age.

E4. **Called deluded; sent to the Jesuits.** LIFE 12346–12352 (Life XXIII): her two advisers concluded "in the opinion of both, I was deluded by an evil spirit ; that the best thing for me to do was to apply to a certain father of the Society of Jesus". LIFE 12008–12013: "the Fathers of the Society of Jesus had come hither ; and I, though I knew none of them, was greatly attracted by them, merely because I had heard of their way of life and of prayer" (VIDA 13618–13625: "porque habían venido aquí los de la Compañía de Jesús"). LIFE-L 1838–1855: the Jesuit Juan de Prádanos heard her general confession and judged "her way of prayer was sound"; then "she told everything to St. Francis de Borja", who told her not to resist further. LIFE-ed 12427–12428: Ribera says Prádanos "gave in part the Exercises of St. Ignatius to the Saint" (editor reporting Ribera). Own words (Documented) for the verdict and the Jesuits; Borja's part is Lewis's preface (Widely Accepted, editor).

### The Inquisition's shadow and the small house

E5. **The books taken away, 1559.** VIDA 14816–14820 (Vida XXVI): "Cuando se quitaron muchos libros de ro- / mance, que no se leyesen (1), yo sentí mucho, porque algunos me / daba recreación leerlos, y yo no podía ya por dejarlos en latín, / me dijo el Señor: No tengas pena, que Yo te daré libro vivo." VIDA-ed 14825–14827: "Don Fernando de Valdés, gran Inquisidor de España, publicó en 1559 un Índice prohibiendo la lectura, no sólo de libros que contenían herejías, sino también muchos de devoción escritos en romance". LIFE 13502–13507 agrees. Own words for the grief; the date and the Inquisitor General are Silverio's (editor; Widely Accepted). The voice she heard stays in her voice.

E6. **"The times were harsh": the Inquisition laughed off, before the house opened.** VIDA 17928–17941 (Vida XXXIII): "También comenzó aquí el demonio, de una persona en otra, / procurar se entendiese que había yo visto alguna revelación en / este negocio, y iban a mí con mucho miedo a decirme que / andaban los tiempos recios, y que podría ser me levantasen algo / y fuesen a los inquisidores. A mí me cayó esto en gracia, y me / hizo reir, porque en este caso jamás yo temí, que sabía bien de / mí que en cosa de la fe, contra la menor cerimonia de la Iglesia / que alguien viese yo iba, por ella u por cualquier verdad de / la Sagrada Escritura, me por ni a yo a morir mil muertes; y dije / que de eso no temiesen, que harto mal sería para mi alma, si en / ella hubiese cosa que fuese de suerte que yo temiese la Inquisi- / ción (2). Que si pensase había para qué, yo me la iría a buscar". In Vida XXXIII, among the troubles before St Joseph's opened in August 1562; no year is given in the text. LIFE 17111–17126 agrees ("people came to me in great terror, saying that the times were dangerous, that something might be laid to my charge, and that I might be taken before the Inquisitors. I heard this with pleasure, and it made me laugh"). Own words; Documented. Context: LIFE-ed 8882–8885: the Valladolid group of 1559, "Cazalla strangled, and his body burnt" (editor).

E7. **The idea of a small house.** LIFE 16745–16749 (Life XXXII.13): "It occurred once to a person with whom I was speaking to say to me and the others that it was possible to find means for the foundation of a monastery, if we were prepared to become nuns like those of the Barefooted Orders." LIFE-ed 16772–16773: the speaker was her niece María de Ocampo (editor, citing Ribera). Her own reluctance: "I took the greatest delight in the house in which I was then living" (LIFE 16758–16760). A voice in prayer then commanded the house, in her account (LIFE 16765–16775): her voice only. Own words; Documented.

E8. **Poverty: the Rule before it was relaxed.** LIFE 18142–18145 (Life XXXV.2): "Until I spoke to her, I never knew that our rule, before it was mitigated, required of us that we should possess nothing ; nor was I going to found a monastery without revenue". The woman was María de Jesús, who had walked to Rome barefoot (LIFE 18126–18131). FOUND-ed 21995–22003: the rule of Albert was corrected by Innocent IV in 1247; "In 1432 Eugenius IV mitigated the rule again, and the rule as explained by him was in force when S. Teresa made her profession in the monastery of the Incarnation." Own words (Documented); the 1247/1432 history is the editor's (Widely Accepted).

E9. **Why so strict: her own reasons.** WAY 1354–1362 (Way I.2): "Just at this time I heard of the miseries France was suffering, of the havoc the Lutherans were making there … I determined to do what little was in my power, which was to follow the Evangelical counsels as perfectly as I could and to see that the few nuns here should do the same." WAY 1344–1347: she did not at first intend such austerity "nor that it should possess no income". English only. Own words; Documented. WAY-ed 1367–1371: "Luis de Leon relates … The first and chief reason for her founding the houses of Discalced Carmelites was to repair, to some extent, these wrongs done by the heretics" (D11).

E10. **St Joseph's founded, 24 August 1562.** VIDA 19300–19305: "fué el Señor servido que, día de / San Bartolomé, tomaron hábito algunas (2), y se puso el San- / tísimo Sacramento, y con toda autoridad y fuerza quedó hecho / nuestro monesterio de el gloriosísimo Padre nuestro San Josef, / año de mil y quinientos y sesenta y dos." LIFE 18626–18631 agrees; LIFE-L 1891–1893: "On the 24th of August, the feast of St. Bartholomew". Own words; Documented.

E11. **No more than thirteen.** VIDA 17655–17656: "nunca queríamos fuesen más / de trece, por muchas causas." VIDA 20029–20031: "solas con El solo, y no ser más de trece". LIFE 19343–19345: "This is to be the perpetual aim of those who are here, to be alone with Him alone. They are not to be more in number than thirteen". Own words; Documented.

E12. **The city tries to close it.** VIDA 19569–19579: "juntáronse algunos de los regido- / res y corregidor, y de el cabildo, y todos juntos dijeron que en / ninguna manera se había desconsentir [sic], que venía conocido daño / … a la república, y que habían de quitar el Santísimo Sacramento, / … En fin, con- / cluyeron que luego se deshiciese. Sólo un Presentado (2) de la / Orden de Santo Domingo, aunque era contrario, no de el mo- / nesterio, sino de que fuese pobre, dijo que no era cosa que / ansí se había de deshacer". LIFE 18941–18960: "the governor, certain members of the council of the city and of the Chapter, came together, and resolved that the new monastery should not be allowed to exist … in the end, they resolved that the monastery should be broken up. Only one … of the Order of St. Dominic, and objected, not to the monastery itself, but to the foundation of it in poverty—said that there was no reason why it should be thus dissolved". LIFE-ed 18975–18979: Báñez's own margin note, "This was at the end of August, 1562. I was present, and gave this opinion". Before that, her Provincial "commanded me to explain my conduct before the nuns" (LIFE 18926–18927); she had been ordered back to the Incarnation (VIDA-ed 19601–19604, Julián de Ávila). Own words and Báñez's note; Documented. VIDA-ed 19600–19604 adds the chaplain Julián de Ávila's account: the governor went to order the nuns out or break the doors; the novices said they would leave only "por la mano que allí las había metido" (later witness via editor; card only).

### The Life, for her confessors

E13. **Written at her confessors' order.** LIFE-L 1885–1901: the Dominican Pedro Ibáñez "ordered her to give him in writing the story of her spiritual life", finished "in the month of June, 1562"; García de Toledo then "bade her 'write the history of that foundation, and other matters.'" LIFE-Z 1182–1183: "It was begun in spring, 1563, and completed in May or June, 1565." Prologue, LIFE 2758–2763: "As I have been commanded and left at liberty to describe at length my way of prayer … I could wish that I had been allowed at the same time to speak distinctly and in detail of my grievous sins". Own words (Documented); dating is the editors' (D4).

E14. **"Burn it at once."** VIDA 9387–9393 (Vida X): "Y ansí lo que fuere más de decir simplemente el discurso de / mi vida, tome vuestra merced para sí, … si fuere conforme a las verdades de nuestra / santa fe católica; y si no, vuestra merced lo queme luego, que / yo a esto me sujeto". LIFE 6331–6336: "if it be not, your reverence must burn it at once—for I give my consent." VIDA 9386: "basta ser mujer para caérseme las alas, cuanti más mujer y ruin" (LIFE 6326: "it is enough that I am a woman to make my sails droop"). Own words; Documented. Closing letter, VIDA 22138–22139: "aunque vuestra merced luego lo queme."

E15. **Juan de Ávila reads it, 1568.** LIFE-L 1929, 1952, 2095: "The book is not fit to be in the hands of everybody"; "The doctrine of prayer is for the most part sound"; signed "Montilla, 12th Sept., 1568." Contemporary; Documented. For the card.

### The foundations, and the order's resistance

E16. **Seventeen houses of nuns.** FOUND-ed 766–772: "She founded and directed seventeen monasteries of nuns in the course of twenty years, yet of those years five years all but nine days were spent tranquilly in her first monastery of S. Joseph in Avila"; the list ends "17. Burgos … April, 1582" (FOUND-ed 976–978). Friars' houses: two she founded under the General's licence, Duruelo (first mass Advent 1568) and Pastrana (FOUND-ed 700–703, 757–758), and "the other houses which were founded afterwards, and which were thirteen in number" (FOUND-ed 760–763). Editor; Widely Accepted. BREV 79252–79253: "enabled to build two-and-thirty houses" (D9).

E17. **The young friar.** FOUND-ed 696–699: "she had two friars, or, as she said, a friar and a half, for Fray Antonio was a portly personage, while S. John of the Cross was thin and low of stature." By role only in the story (John of the Cross not in the census).

E18. **The order's decree to stop, 1575.** FOUND-ed 2065–2072 quoting Foundations XXVII.18: "'there was brought to me,' saith the Saint (ch. xxvii. 18), 'from the general chapter, which I think ought to have highly considered the increase of the order, a decree, made by the deputies assembled, enjoining me not only to make no more foundations, but also on no account whatever to leave the house I should choose to dwell in". Her own words (English only); Documented. FOUND-ed 2034–2040: "nothing is said either against the Reform as such or against the Carmelite nuns, or S. Teresa in particular. The order she received to withdraw … did not proceed from the chapter ; it was probably a measure taken by the General personally" (D10). FOUND-ed 2226–2237: the nuncio Sega imprisoned leading friars of the reform. Give the decree in her voice, credited.

E19. **Pastrana and the Princess of Eboli; the Life denounced, 1574.** LIFE-L 2102–2123: the Princess "in 1569, obtained a copy from the Saint herself, after much importunity"; it reached her servants, and its visions "were said to be of a like nature with those of Magdalene of the Cross, a deluded and deluding nun." LIFE-L 2154–2161: "The nuns were withdrawn from Pastrana in April, 1574, and then the anger of the Princess prevailed ; she sent the Life of the Saint … to the Inquisition, and denounced it as a book containing visions, revelations, and dangerous doctrines". VIDA-ed 6191–6193, Gracián: denounced to the Inquisition by "una Señora / Princesa por hacer daño' a la Madré, a causa de cierto enojo". Contemporary (Gracián) and editors; Documented that it was denounced; the Princess's motive is Gracián's word ("cierto enojo") and the editors' reading (D5).

E20. **Báñez's part, and his wish to burn it.** LIFE-Z 1135–1158, Báñez's deposition: "It was not convenient that this book should become public during her lifetime, but rather that it should be kept at the Holy Office (the Inquisition) until we knew the end of this person … I told her she ought to burn the original because it would never do that the writings of women should become public property ; to which she answered she was quite aware of it and would certainly burn it if I told her to do so ; but knowing her great humility and obedience I did not dare to have it destroyed but handed it to the Holy Office for safe-keeping". VIDA-ed 6195–6200: hearing of the denunciation, Báñez "llevó él mismo a los inquisidores el manuscrito denunciado". Contemporary (Báñez, deposition 1591); Documented as his account.

E21. **Báñez's censure, July 1575.** LIFE-L 2214–2219: "This book … has been examined by me, and with much attention, and I have not found anywhere in it anything which, in my opinion, is erroneous in doctrine." LIFE-L 2276–2279: "this woman, even though she may be deceived in something, is at least not herself a deceiver". LIFE-L 2365–2374: "I am of opinion that this book is not to be shown to every one, but only to men of learning, experience, and Christian discretion. … Of one thing I am very sure, so far as it is possible for a man to be,—she is not a deceiver … For within the last thirteen years she has, I believe, founded a dozen monasteries of Barefooted Carmelite nuns". LIFE-L 2383–2386: "Given in the College of St. Gregory, Valladolid, on the sixth day of July, 1575. 'FRA DOMINGO BANES.'" English only (D6). Also in the same censure: visions "always to be afraid of, especially in women" (LIFE-L 2244–2246): the force of the record, said in his words.

E22. **She never saw it again.** LIFE-L 2389–2399: "The book remained in the keeping of the Inquisition, and the Saint never saw it again. But she heard of it from the Archbishop of Toledo, Cardinal Quiroga, President of the Supreme Court of the Inquisition … he had read it himself, and regarded it as containing sound and wholesome doctrine." Ana de Jesús, deposition (VIDA-ed 6161–6191): Teresa told her she had written it "tan sin temor" that she did not care for herself, "porque / bien sabía Dios la verdad y sinceridad con que había dicho lo que / en aquel libro estaba". Editor and a later deposition; Documented as reported.

E23. **Denounced herself at Seville, 1576?** FOUND-ed 15384–15412: a dismissed postulant "denounced S. Teresa to the Inquisition", officials searched the house, and "The inquisitors, satisfied with their search, severely reprimanded the poor priest". VIDA-ed 17946–17948: "no parece que se la denunciase a ella misma, como lo fueron tantas otras personas de su tiempo" (D8). Card only.

### The Interior Castle, 1577

E24. **Written under obedience, at Toledo, in the reform's worst year.** CASTLE 1687–1693: "So I begin this work on the Feast of the Blessed Trinity in the year 1577, in the Convent of St. Joseph of Carmel at Toledo, where I am living, and I submit all my writings to the judgment of those learned men by whose commands I undertake them." CASTLE 1644: "Rarely has obedience laid upon me so difficult a task as this of writing about prayer". CASTLE-ed 464–467: begun 2 June 1577, finished 29 November 1577. CASTLE-ed 503–522: ordered by Gracián and the canon Velázquez; in October the Incarnation's nuns elected her (during the five-month break in the writing, CASTLE-ed 467–470), "fifty-five electors recorded their votes in favour of the Saint and were immediately declared excommunicated. The whole work of the Reform seemed on the brink of ruin". Own words (Documented); the troubles are the editor's (Widely Accepted). CASTLE-ed 615–621: a nun's deposition that Gracián ordered it and she begged off because "there remained nothing for a woman to write" (later deposition; card).

E25. **The castle.** CASTLE 1752–1761, 1778 (First Mansions I.1–2): "While I was begging our Lord to-day to speak for me, since I knew not what to say nor how to commence this work which obedience has laid upon me, an idea occurred to me … I thought of the soul as resembling a castle, formed of a single diamond or a very transparent crystal, and containing many rooms, just as in heaven there are many mansions." CASTLE 1843–1846 (I.1.4): "in the centre, in the very midst of them all, is the principal chamber in which God and the soul hold their most secret intercourse." CASTLE 2017–2018: "the gate by which to enter this castle is prayer and meditation." The seven dwelling places: the book's divisions, First to Seventh Mansions (CASTLE 440, 1732, and corpus-map loci). English only. Own words; Documented. Title: VIDA-ed 1320 "sus Moradas o Castillo Interior".

E26. **Ignatius named in the Castle.** CASTLE 6929–6956 (Fifth Mansions IV): "of how many victims the evil one was deprived by St. Dominic, St. Francis, and other founders of religious orders ! How many more he loses, even now, through Father Ignatius [Loyola], who founded the Company [of Jesus] !" Own words (brackets the translators'); Documented. VIDA-ed 4208–4220: Luis de León's 1588 edition replaced the words on Ignatius with "y otros fundadores de Ordenes" (editor; Documented as his comparison). See L1.

E27. **Her own judgment of the Castle.** CASTLE-ed 738–741, her letter to Gracián: "The book I have written since seems to me superior [to the Life] ; at least I had more experience when I wrote it." Letter quoted by editor; Documented as reported. Card.

### The separation and her death

E28. **A province of their own, 1580.** FOUND-ed 2378–2384: "the Sovereign Pontiff, Gregory XIII, in a brief dated 22nd June 1580, confirmed and sanctioned the severance of the mitigation from the reform. All the friars and all the nuns under the primitive rule and of the reform of S. Teresa were to form one province under a provincial of their own, but under the father-general of the whole order." The Latin act is in `bullarium-romanum_magnum-bullarium-tomus2-pius-iv-to-innocent-ix-lat_cherubini.txt` (corpus-map: from line 125228; running-head year needs checking, not read here). Editor; Documented. FOUND-ed 3677–3678: "1593. Separation of the Orders of Calced and Discalced Carmelites."

E29. **Death at Alba de Tormes, October 1582.** FOUND-ed 2490–2494: she went from house to house "till she reached Alba de Tormes, 20th September 1582, being then on the point of death." FOUND-ed 2524–2533: on receiving the sacrament "she added, 'But I am a child of the Church'" and "again gave God thanks that she was a child of the Catholic Church." FOUND-ed 2537–2549: on the feast of St Francis, "Between nine and ten o'clock that night God took her to Himself for ever." BREV 79312–79314: "on the 15th day of October in the year 1582, New Style, being then 67 years of age"; BREV 79377: "She died in the year and at the day in which the Kalendar was changed, about 9 P.M." ## What came of her (impact, heaviest first)

I1. **The reform outlived her as an order.** E16 (seventeen nuns' houses, the friars' houses), E28 (the province of 1580; separate order 1593). CEN home `legacy`: "The Discalced Carmelites are still a worldwide order of friars and nuns." CEN home `livingEvidence`: the Order's General Definitory letter of 14 December 2025. Editor and the tree's sourced entry; Documented.

I2. **Her books were printed after her death, from copies.** LIFE-L 2415–2428: "Anne of Jesus, in 1586, founding a monastery of her Order in Madrid,—the Saint had died in 1582,—made inquiries about the book, and applied to the Inquisition for it, for she was resolved to publish the writings of her spiritual mother. The Inquisitors made no difficulty … Fra Luis de Leon, the Augustinian, to edit the book … It was published in Salamanca, A.D. 1588." LIFE-Z 1191–1199: Luis de León, "professor at Salamanca", "did not know the autograph". CASTLE-ed 752–757: at the Salamanca seminary the Castle "was read publicly after dinner". VIDA-ed 4196–4220: León's edition cut her naming of the Jesuits and of Ignatius. Editors; Documented.

I3. **Declared a saint, 1622; her feast in the church's daily prayer.** FOUND-ed 3681–3690: "1614. 24th April. Beatification … 12th March. Canonisation of S. Teresa" (the year line is lost in the OCR); JCL 9265–9267: a friar "saw not the beatification only, but the canonisation also of St. Teresa, in the year 1622". BREV 79325–79327: "was numbered by Gregory XV. among the Saints". BREV 79250–79253 sums her life in the lessons read on 15 October. Documented.

I4. **Readers who carried her on.** PASC 15024–15029 (Pensées 868): "we generally look upon Saint Athanasius, Saint Theresa, and the rest, as crowned with glory … But at the time when he was persecuted, this great saint was a man called Athanasius; and Saint Theresa was a nun." PASC 15554: "Saint Theresa having always followed her confessor". LIG 2659: Liguori heads his first letter (Naples, 29 October 1730) "Praised be Jesus, Joseph, Mary, and Teresa !"; LIG 2688–2689: "the feast of my patroness St. Teresa". LIGM 11853: "humility is truth, as St. Theresa says". GUY 6781: "like St. Theresa, 'Hither to suffer or to die!'". ASS 14102–14108 (Latin): the Carmelite order, "per S. Teresiam a Jesu renovari", so "inflamavit" devotion to St Joseph "unaque cum monasteriis suis toto orbe Christiano propagavit". Own words of each; Documented as their words.

I5. **Doctor of the Church.** CEN home `legacy`: "Teresa, named in 1970, was the first woman to hold that title." Not in a held text (after the Library's cutoff); the tree's own entry only. Optional, one clause, credited to nothing in the story; better on the card.

## Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **When she entered the Incarnation.** 1533 (Yepes, Bollandists, Lewis) against 1535, a year and a day before profession on 3 November 1536 (Zimmerman, from deeds dated 31 October 1536) (LIFE-Z 677–735). BREV: "in the twentieth year of her age" (79223–79225). Say "about 1535, when she was about twenty", crediting no one; the card gives both.

D2. **How long before the new house.** "Nearly twenty years" is her time of struggle in prayer (E2). The Breviary: "There she dwelt for two-and-twenty years, tormented by grievous sicknesses and divers temptations" (BREV 79225–79229). Her letter of 1581, as the editor reports it: "five-and-twenty years" in a house of 180 nuns (FOUND-ed 1043–1046), which counts her later years there as prioress (1571–1574, FOUND-ed 1060–1072). From about 1535 to 1562 is about twenty-seven years. The job's "twenty years" is safe only as "more than twenty years".

D3. **How many nuns.** 190 in 1550 (Francisco de Santa María, via FOUND-ed 1041–1042); 180 (her letter, via FOUND-ed 1044–1046); "more than a hundred and fifty" in 1567 (FOUND-ed 1047). Say "well over a hundred and fifty nuns", or "a hundred and eighty, she later wrote".

D4. **When the Life was finished.** May or June 1565 (Zimmerman, LIFE-Z 1182–1183); "nearly six years in the writing" from 1562 (Lewis, LIFE-L 1906–1910). Zimmerman also doubts that García de Toledo's command came in 1562 (LIFE-Z 1218–1224). Say "between 1562 and about 1565".

D5. **How the Life reached the Inquisition.** The Princess denounced it in anger (Gracián, VIDA-ed 6191–6193; LIFE-L 2154–2161); Báñez himself carried it to the Inquisitors to protect it (VIDA-ed 6195–6200; his deposition, LIFE-Z 1150–1158). Lewis: "Thus the book was in a sense denounced twice,—once by an enemy, the second time by a friend, to save it" (LIFE-L 2189–2190). The year: 1574 (Silverio, VIDA-ed 6150–6154) against 1570 (Andrés de la Encarnación, ibid.). Tell both routes, credited.

D6. **The date of Báñez's censure.** "the sixth day of July, 1575" (LIFE-L 2383–2385, printing de la Fuente) against "a 7 de Julio de 1575" (VIDA-ed 6231, from the autograph). Say "in July 1575".

D7. **What Báñez wanted done with it.** In the censure: shown "only to men of learning, experience, and Christian discretion" (E21). In his deposition: kept at the Holy Office "until we knew the end of this person" (E20). The tree's home story says "shown to no one" (L3). Give the censure's words.

D8. **Was she herself denounced?** Yes, at Seville in 1576, and cleared (FOUND-ed 15384–15412); "no parece" (VIDA-ed 17946–17948). Card only.

D9. **How many houses.** Seventeen houses of nuns, and friars' houses (two plus thirteen) (FOUND-ed 760–772); "two-and-thirty houses" (BREV 79252–79253); Báñez in 1575: "a dozen" in his censure (LIFE-L 2373–2375) but "nine" in his margin note of May 1575 (LIFE-ed 18977–18978). Say "seventeen houses of nuns, her editors count, and houses of friars too"; the Breviary's thirty-two on the card.

D10. **Who stopped her in 1575.** Her words: a decree of the general chapter (E18). Zimmerman: the chapter's text says nothing against her; probably the General's own order (FOUND-ed 2034–2040). Credit her account as hers.

D11. **Why she founded.** Her Life: the relaxed rule, the going out, the call she heard in prayer (E7, S3). Her Way: the news of the Lutherans in France, and the wish to do the little she could (E9). Luis de León, as Zimmerman reports him: that was the "first and chief reason" (WAY-ed 1367–1371). Tell her own two reasons, as hers.

D13. **Did the first editor see the original of the Life?** Zimmerman: Luis de León "did not know the autograph of the 'Life'" (LIFE-Z 1198–1199). Lewis: "He was allowed to compare the copy furnished him with the original in the keeping of the Inquisition" (LIFE-L 2424–2426). Card only.

D12. **The day she died.** The night of 4 October 1582, the feast of St Francis (FOUND-ed 2537–2549); 15 October 1582, New Style (BREV 79312–79313). Not a dispute: the calendar changed that night (BREV 79377). Say "in October 1582".
