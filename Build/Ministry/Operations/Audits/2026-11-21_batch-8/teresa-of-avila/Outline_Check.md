## My heart, written before reading the outline

**Essence.** A Carmelite nun who spent more than twenty years in the large, relaxed convent of the Incarnation at Ávila (Documented, her own words; the number of years differs among held sources), and in 1562 founded St Joseph's, a small, poor house of no more than thirteen nuns under the order's old unrelaxed Rule, over the opposition of the city council (Documented). From it she founded house after house, seventeen of nuns by her editors' count, with friars' houses too, and the reform became the Discalced Carmelites, a province of its own in 1580 (Widely Accepted for the counts; Documented for 1580). She is on the tree as the founder of that reform and its teacher of prayer.

**Also told.** Her Life, written at her confessors' order (about 1562–1565, Dominant Modern Reconstruction), in a time when the Inquisition had banned devotional books in Spanish (1559) and people warned her she might be reported; she laughed (Documented, own words). The Life reached the Inquisition in two ways told by held witnesses: a princess denounced it (Gracián; editors) and her confessor Báñez carried it there himself to keep it safe (his deposition); Báñez found she was "not a deceiver" (July 1575), and she never saw the book again (Documented as each one's account). The order's 1575 order to stop founding (her account; who issued it contested by Zimmerman). The Interior Castle (1577): the soul as a castle of crystal or diamond with many rooms, God in the centre, prayer as the gate (Documented). Death at Alba de Tormes, October 1582; printed 1588; canonized 1622 (Documented).

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 3 tells Quote 2 before the idea of the house it is about, and leaves out why people were afraid.** In VIDA 17928–17931 (Vida XXXIII) the warnings come while the foundation is being planned. The devil, she writes, spread it "de una persona en otra" that she had had a revelation "en este negocio" (in this business, the new house). That comes after the idea of a small house (Life XXXII.13, LIFE 16745–16749). The outline places Quote 2 straight after the 1559 ban and before "A friend's idea of a small house…". That is a jump back in time, and it ties the warning to the book ban rather than to the house. **Correction:** reorder beat 3 to: the 1559 ban (one clause) → the idea of a small house (E7) → "As word went round that she had had a revelation about the new house, people warned her…", then Quote 2 (E6, VIDA 17928–17931) → the barefoot pilgrim and poverty (E8) → 24 August 1562 → the city council. In "Also told", change "friends warned her" to "people warned her" (the source says "de una persona en otra", from one person to another).

**B2. The Life's writing dates state one editor's reading as settled.** Beat 4 says "say 'over the next three years'", and Do not say gives "over the next three years" or "by about 1565" as the right wording. Those are Zimmerman's dates (LIFE-Z 1182–1183). Lewis, in the same held volume, says the Life "must have occupied her nearly six years in the writing of it" (LIFE-L 1906–1910). The sheet records this as D4. **Correction:** in beat 4 and in "Also told", say "she wrote it in the years after 1562", with no end year. Change the Do not say line to: Not "she finished the Life in 1565", and not "over three years" or "over six years" as fact. Say "in the years after 1562". The editors differ: Zimmerman says by mid-1565, Lewis says nearly six years. Both go on the card.

**B3. The Connection states one editor's reading as settled.** It says the Life "was printed only after her death, from copies (E22, I2)". Zimmerman says Luis de León "did not know the autograph" (LIFE-Z 1198–1199). Lewis says "He was allowed to compare the copy furnished him with the original in the keeping of the Inquisition" (LIFE-L 2424–2426). The outline's own Do not say already bars "printed it from copies only" (D13). **Correction:** in the Connection, delete ", from copies". It should read "was printed only after her death, at Salamanca in 1588 (E22, I2)".

**B4. Beat 5 leaves out the Inquisition's commission to Báñez, and Do not say bars it as if no held source gave it.** Lewis's preface is held, and it says that after the princess's denunciation "The book was forthwith given to theologians for examination, and two Dominican friars, of whom Bañes was one, were delegated censors of it by the Inquisition" (LIFE-L 2159–2162). It also says that after Báñez sent his own copy to Madrid, "The Inquisitor Soto returned the copy to Fra Bañes, desiring him to read it, and give his opinion" (LIFE-L 2194–2196). As written, beat 5 runs "Báñez took the manuscript … himself … In July 1575 he reported". A listener would hear the friend's own opinion, not the examination the Inquisition ordered. Mark's ruling (hearts item 4) stands: both routes are told and credited, and the tradition entry's wording goes to the census pass. But the held text supports part of the tradition entry's version. **Corrections:**
- Beat 5, before "In July 1575 he reported": add "The Inquisition then gave it back to him to judge, her editor Lewis writes (LIFE-L 2194–2196)" (about 14 words; Documented as Lewis's account).
- Do not say: replace "Not 'the Inquisition took the book and gave it to Báñez' (the home tradition entry's version), and not either road alone" with "Not either road alone, and not 'the Inquisition took the book and gave it to Báñez' without Báñez's own carrying of it there".
- Sheet L3 and the Researcher_Reply's gap for Mark say the tradition entry "disagrees with the held texts on Báñez's report". Add to that note, for the census pass, that Lewis (LIFE-L 2159–2162, 2194–2196) supports the Inquisition's giving the book to Báñez to examine. The held texts differ from the entry only on the tribunal (Madrid, LIFE-L 2189), the date (D6) and "shown to no one" (D7).

**B5. The gloss for the Castle's title says more than its source.** Words to explain gives "the book she called the Dwelling Places, known in English as the Interior Castle". Her own heading says "This treatise, styled the Interior Castle, was written by Teresa of Jesus" (CASTLE 1639). The autograph's title is "El Castillo Ynterior ó Tratado de las Moradas" (CASTLE 1519). "Interior Castle" is her own title, not an English label. **Correction:** "**the Interior Castle**: her own title; its parts she called dwelling places (*moradas*). Never 'mansions' bare (the modern sense misleads)" (CASTLE 1519, 1639).

## MINOR

1. **Báñez's qualifier.** "Not a deceiver" comes with "even though she may be deceived in something" (LIFE-L 2276–2277). Add to Do not say: Not "Báñez said she was not deceived". He wrote that she might be deceived in something but was not herself a deceiver. In beat 5, "found nothing in it against the faith" is closer to the source as "found nothing in it erroneous in doctrine" (LIFE-L 2216–2217).
2. **Báñez in 1562.** Add to Do not say: Not "her confessor Báñez defended the house". He did not yet know her (LIFE-ed note to Life XXXVI.15; LIFE-L 2163–2165). Beat 3 already says he "later became her confessor". Keep that.

6. **"A friend's idea".** Beat 3 says "a friend". LIFE 16745 says "a person with whom I was speaking", and LIFE-ed 16772–16773 (citing Ribera) names her niece María de Ocampo. Say "someone she was talking with" or "a young relative".

9. **Ending credit.** "Those with her said" names witnesses that the held text does not name. FOUND-ed 2524–2533 is Zimmerman's narrative, with no witness given. Credit "her editors tell" or "the account of her death says".
10. **"A young friar who became the reform's poet".** No cited held line gives "young" or "poet". The support is the tree's home entry (CEN `carmelite-reform-and-spanish-mystics` `longDescription`: "a young friar, John of the Cross … its greatest poet"). Cite CEN beside E17.

12. **Quote 2 rendering.** "Someone might bring a charge against me" loses the falseness in "me levantasen algo" (raise something false against me; Lewis: "something might be laid to my charge"). "Someone might bring a false charge against me" is closer. It adds one word.
