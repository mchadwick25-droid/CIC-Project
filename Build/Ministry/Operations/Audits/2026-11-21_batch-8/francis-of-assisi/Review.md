## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who.** Francis of Assisi, a trader's son from Assisi in central Italy. In his early twenties he gave up his family's wealth and founded the Friars Minor, brothers who were to own nothing.
2. **What happened.** His father chained him, and he stripped before the bishop. He served the lepers, took to the roads, won the pope's confirmation of a short Rule, and later a stricter one. He made the Canticle of Brother Sun. Before dying in 1226 he dictated a Testament: no privileges from Rome, strayed brothers held like prisoners, the Rule read simply.
3. **Why the connection matters.** The order lived by his Rule of owning nothing. Four years after his death the pope, asked by the order's leaders, ruled that the Testament did not bind them. For a century the brothers fought over the Rule's poverty, and in 1323 their lawyer was still quoting its words to a pope.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

**B2. The 1223 Rule is made to say what the Testament says.** "When no wage came, it said, the brothers should go for alms". The Rule's chapter 6 says only that they go confidently for alms (OPU 2577–2582; ROB 4774–4795). Chapter 5 lets them take necessities as the reward of their work (ROB 4756–4767). The condition "when the price of labor is not given" is the Testament's (ROB 5476–5512; outline beat 5, E9). Change to: "It said the brothers should go for alms, gifts of food or help begged from door to door." (−3). The essence's "beg when no wage came" stays; it is the Testament's, and correct.
- *Do not say, to add to the outline:* Not that the 1223 Rule says to beg "when no wage came". That condition is the Testament's (ROB 5476–5512). The Rule says they go confidently for alms (OPU 2577–2582).
