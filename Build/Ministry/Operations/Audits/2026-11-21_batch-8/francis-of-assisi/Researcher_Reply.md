# Francis of Assisi: researcher's reply

**Essence.** A trader's son of Assisi gave up his family's wealth in the early 1200s, served the lepers he had loathed, and preached on the roads (Documented: his Testament, Celano). His Friars Minor owned nothing, worked, and begged only when no wage came (Documented: his Rule and Testament; Jacques de Vitry, 1216).

**Also told.** His father's chains and the stripping before the bishop (Celano; "inheritance" is Bonaventure's word). Innocent III's confirmation about 1209 (year Contested), and the 1223 Rule. The Canticle (Documented; the added death verse Contested). The Testament's commands, including holding strayed brothers like prisoners. Gregory IX's 1230 ruling and the 1323 appeal (Documented).

**Shape and length.** Monk or ascetic. About 610 words, 4:05. Opening: his Testament's leper lines.

**Connection.** The order lived by his Rule, and fought over it before the popes for a century.

**Quotes** (none lifted, none used in another story's text).
- "The Lord gave me, Brother Francis, to begin to do penance in this way… turned into sweetness of soul and body."
- "Let the brothers make nothing their own, not a house, not a place, not anything…"
- "Be praised, my Lord… Sir Brother Sun…"; "…for our Sister Bodily Death, from whom no one living can escape."

**Sources differ.** The lepers before or after leaving home; his words before the bishop; the speaking crucifix (a later Life only); 1209 or 1210.

**Gaps.** The Three Companions, the Assisi Compilation and the 1220 letter are not held. The Canticle's Italian is in a damaged scan; other manuscript readings and Robinson's English confirm it. For Mark: the tradition entry's `documentedStories` (not a `longDescription`) already prints the leper lines.
