# Outline check: Francis of Assisi

## My own heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census tradition entry (`the-franciscan-movement`: `why`, `longDescription`), the batch job, the source sheet, and what Francis is known for.

- **Essence.** Francis, a trader's son of Assisi (born about 1182), gave up his family's wealth in the first years of the 1200s, went among the lepers he had loathed, and took the Gospel's sending-out of the disciples as his rule: no money, one tunic, preaching on the roads. The brothers who joined him (the Friars Minor) were to own nothing, work and beg, and live among the townspeople rather than behind monastery walls; Innocent III confirmed their short rule about 1209. He is on the tree as the founder of the Franciscan Movement, whose poverty, in his own Rule and Testament, became the order's lasting quarrel.
- **Also told.** The leper turn in his own words (Testament); the break with his father and the stripping before the bishop (Celano; "inheritance" is Bonaventure's word); the Gospel at the Portiuncula; Rome, the cardinal's advice to become a monk or hermit refused, the Pope's approval (year 1209 or 1210); how they lived (Testament, the 1223 Rule's "nothing as their own", the outsider Jacques de Vitry's 1216 letter); the women of San Damiano under Clare (by role); the Canticle of Brother Sun, made ill at San Damiano, his one Italian song; the Testament of 1226 with its commands (no glosses, no letters from the curia, brothers who fail held as prisoners); his death at the Portiuncula, October 1226, "Welcome, Sister Death" credited to later witnesses; the stigmata at most as a wonder in Celano's voice. What came: the Rule and Testament fought over (Gregory IX's ruling of 1230 that the Testament did not bind; the Spirituals; Bonagratia before John XXII in 1323); his life written and rewritten by his brothers (Celano, Bonaventure); Clare's Rule keeping his words; the spread of the order.

## BLOCKING

**1. Quo elongati is held in full, and the outline rests the pope's ruling on a later report instead.** Sabatier's *Speculum perfectionis* (SPECL, already used) prints Gregory IX's bull *Quo elongati* (28 September 1230) whole in its appendix, from line 31815 ("Quo elongati a sæculo") to line 32284 ("Datum Anagniæ IV. Kal. Octobris"). SPECL 28888 says so: "reproduite ci-après intégralement". The bull says three things:
- The order's ministers, in general chapter, sent envoys, and the minister general came in person, to ask whether they were bound by the Testament (31887–31890, 31906–31909).
- The Testament forbade glosses on the Rule and letters from the Apostolic See, and added "other things which could not be kept without much difficulty" (31899–31905).
- The pope believed Francis had "a pious intention" in the command, but ruled "ad mandatum illud vos, dicimus non teneri", that they are not bound by that command. Without the consent of the brothers, and above all of the ministers, Francis "could not bind them, nor did he in any way bind his successor, since an equal has no command over an equal" (31933–31943).

So the outline makes the ruling rest on a later report, Ubertino's of 1310 with Ehrle's note on which pope, when the pope's own text is held. Its Gaps line ("held only as reported by Ubertino ... and identified by Ehrle") is wrong, and so is the sheet's ("full texts ... of *Quo elongati* (1230): not held").

Corrections:
- **What came 1**, first sentence: "In 1230, four years after his death, the order's own leaders asked the pope whether the Testament bound them. Gregory IX, the cardinal the Testament had named, answered in a bull that they were not bound by its command. He believed Francis had meant well, he wrote, but Francis could not bind them without the ministers' consent, or bind the pope who came after him (SPECL 31887–31890, 31933–31943; own text of the pope, contemporary; Documented)." Drop "as a leader of the strict friars reported about eighty years later" and "Ehrle's identification of the pope".
- **Connection**, second point: "and that four years later, at the request of the order's own leaders, the pope, the very cardinal he had named as protector, ruled in a bull that this command did not bind them (SPECL 31887–31943)".
- **Who tells it**: replace the "A leader of the strict friars, about 1310 (Ubertino)" line with "**Gregory IX's bull *Quo elongati*, 1230**, the pope's own words, contemporary: the ruling on the Testament (What came 1)". Ubertino goes to the card.
- **Names**: remove "a leader of the strict friars (Ubertino of Casale)" from the by-role list.
- **Do not say**, replace the "overturned / betrayed" line with: "Not 'the pope overturned Francis' or 'betrayed him', and not that the pope acted on his own. The order's leaders asked him. He wrote that Francis meant well, and ruled that the brothers were not bound by the command (SPECL 31887–31943)."
- **Gaps**: replace the *Quo elongati* line with: "*Quo elongati* (1230) is held in full in Sabatier's appendix (SPECL 31815–32284). Not held: the full text of *Solet annuere* (1223)." Remove *Quo elongati* from the first Gaps line.
- **Leave out / For the card**: add "Ubertino of Casale's report of the ruling, and his view that Francis meant by it to explain his intention in the Rule (EHR3 3080–3087)".

**2. The lepers: where the Testament and Celano differ, the outline tells Celano's order without saying so (D1; Mark, 2026-11-19).** The Testament (Q1, the opening) puts the lepers first: "the Lord himself led me among them ... After that I waited a little, and I left the world". Beat 2 then tells the break with his father and the bishop, and only beat 3 tells the leper service (Celano, CEL 1478–1485, placed after the bishop scene). The outline leaves out Celano's own earlier meeting, which falls before the break: "while still in the habit of the world, he met a leper ... went near and kissed him" (CEL 1497–1500; CELL 7382–7385). A listener hears Francis say "lepers, then I left the world", and then hears the narrator put the lepers after he left. That states Celano's order as settled against the Testament's. The sheet's own D1 said: "Tell the Testament's order in his voice; credit Celano for the kiss."

Corrections:
- **Beat 1**, last sentence (or the first sentence of beat 2): "Celano writes that one day, while he still dressed as a man of the town, he met a leper, went up to him and kissed him (CEL 1497–1500; Celano's account; Documented as his)." About 20 words.
- **Beat 3**, first sentence: "After the break he went to live among the lepers and served them, washing their sores, Celano writes (E3)."
- **Do not say**, add: "Not that he first went near a leper only after the bishop scene. Francis puts the lepers before he left the world, and Celano puts the first meeting, the kiss, before the break and the service after it (D1)."
- Leave out the kiss only in Bonaventure's version, where the leper vanishes. That line stands.

**3. "Letters of privilege" has no gloss, and the listener needs it for the Connection.** Beat 6 and the Connection's second point ("forbidding privileges from Rome") rely on it. A general listener does not know what a papal privilege is, and Do not say only bars listed words.

Correction, in **Words to explain**, add: "**letters of privilege** (beat 6, Connection): 'letters from the pope's court granting the brothers special rights, for a church, a place or leave to preach' (ROB 5523–5529; OPU 2912–2918)."

## MINOR

1. **Ending, the quoted phrase.** The outline has "Welcome, Sister Death". The source reads "Welcome, my Sister Death!" (SPEC 8978–8979; SPECL 27500–27501, "Bene veniat soror mea mors"). Correction: "Welcome, my Sister Death".
2. **Words to explain, "gloss".** "Notes that explain a text away" adds a motive. The Testament's own words are glosses "saying: Thus they ought to be understood" (ROB 5618–5621). Correction: "notes saying how his words ought to be understood"; or say "simply, as he wrote them".
3. **Words to explain, further items.**
   - "the Office" (beat 6): "the daily prayers set by the Rule".
   - "bull", if the optional heading in beat 4 is kept: "a sealed papal letter".
   - "seraph", if the optional clause in beat 6 is kept: "a six-winged angel" (CEL 4231–4234).
4. **Beat 2, "When he began to give money away, townsmen threw mud and stones."** This implies a cause that Celano does not give. Celano says they pelted him because he was "changed from his former ways and greatly worn by maceration of the flesh" (CEL 1288–1296). Correction: "When he came back into the town changed, and thin from fasting, townsmen threw mud and stones at him as a madman."
5. **What came 1, "Defended".** It credits the strict friars alone. But the 1323 lawyer, Bonagratia, spoke for the order's majority (the Community), which had set him against Ubertino (EHR3 2795–2799).
   - Correction: "Defended: his poverty, with his own words: by the order itself before the pope in 1323."
   - Do not say, add: "Not that the order's lawyer of 1323 was one of the strict friars. He spoke for the order, and had opposed their leader."
6. **Cut-first marks off the list.** Beat 4 marks the cardinal's advice "optional, cut first", but the essence and Also told carry it, and a Do not say line relies on it. Remove the mark. Beat 6 marks the marks of the wounds "Optional (cut first)", but they are not on the numbered list. Put them at the head of the cut-first list as item 1, about 25 words, so the writer drops them first.
7. **Q3, the sun line (damaged scan).** Other held manuscripts in the same file read the verb "giorna / iorna", "who brings the day" (SPECL 30207, 30309, 30382). Robinson has "The which lights up the day" (ROB 8603–8604). The outline's "who is the day" follows Assisi 338 (SPECL 27175) and is a fair rendering. Add these readings as the confirming prints for the sun line, and note the alternative for the card. The line is cut-first item 7.
8. **Names, Jacques de Vitry, "a French bishop at the papal court".** No held text cited says "French", and he was bishop of Acre visiting the curia (JDV 5858–5860, 5992). Correction: "Jacques de Vitry, a bishop on a visit to the papal court in 1216", or "the new bishop of Acre".
9. **What came: the spread.** The shape puts the impact in "the pattern others copied". The outline drops the order's spread (I4), which is held. Add, optional, one clause: "Jacques de Vitry, writing later, says there was hardly a Christian province without its brothers (JDVH 19384–19386; contemporary, long-s OCR)". It can be paid for by the cut-first list.
10. **Q1, a variant for the card.** Some manuscripts read "feci moram cum illis", "I made a stay with them", for "I showed mercy to them" (ROB-ed 5385–5388). OPU, ROB and CELL all print *misericordiam*, so the rendering stands. Add the variant to "For the card".
11. **Who tells it, the Mirror of Perfection.** "A later book of his companions' memories" settles an attribution that is contested (SPEC header, line 9; sheet SPEC). Correction: "a later book said to gather his companions' memories". Apply it in the Ending and the Canticle item too.
12. **Words to explain, the Portiuncula.** "Below Assisi" says more than the cited lines. Celano has "another place ... called Portiuncula", near the city (CEL 1621–1629). Correction: "a little church of St Mary near Assisi".
13. **Source sheet (for the record, no edit asked here).** The sheet's Gaps and Library sweep both list *Quo elongati* as not held (see BLOCKING 1).
