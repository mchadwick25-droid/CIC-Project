# Outline check: Francis of Assisi

## My own heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census tradition entry (`the-franciscan-movement`: `why`, `longDescription`), the batch job, the source sheet, and what Francis is known for.

- **Essence.** Francis, a trader's son of Assisi (born about 1182), gave up his family's wealth in the first years of the 1200s, went among the lepers he had loathed, and took the Gospel's sending-out of the disciples as his rule: no money, one tunic, preaching on the roads. The brothers who joined him (the Friars Minor) were to own nothing, work and beg, and live among the townspeople rather than behind monastery walls; Innocent III confirmed their short rule about 1209. He is on the tree as the founder of the Franciscan Movement, whose poverty, in his own Rule and Testament, became the order's lasting quarrel.
- **Also told.** The leper turn in his own words (Testament); the break with his father and the stripping before the bishop (Celano; "inheritance" is Bonaventure's word); the Gospel at the Portiuncula; Rome, the cardinal's advice to become a monk or hermit refused, the Pope's approval (year 1209 or 1210); how they lived (Testament, the 1223 Rule's "nothing as their own", the outsider Jacques de Vitry's 1216 letter); the women of San Damiano under Clare (by role); the Canticle of Brother Sun, made ill at San Damiano, his one Italian song; the Testament of 1226 with its commands (no glosses, no letters from the curia, brothers who fail held as prisoners); his death at the Portiuncula, October 1226, "Welcome, Sister Death" credited to later witnesses; the stigmata at most as a wonder in Celano's voice. What came: the Rule and Testament fought over (Gregory IX's ruling of 1230 that the Testament did not bind; the Spirituals; Bonagratia before John XXII in 1323); his life written and rewritten by his brothers (Celano, Bonaventure); Clare's Rule keeping his words; the spread of the order.
