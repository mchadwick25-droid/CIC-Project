# Outline check: Jan Hus

## My own heart, written before reading Outline.md

Disclosure: a tool preview showed me the outline's first lines (the essence and the first two Also-told items) before this section was written. What follows comes from the census job line, the source sheet and what Hus is known for.

- **Essence.** A Prague priest and university master who preached to the city in Czech at the Bethlehem Chapel (the 1408 bull for the chapel's purpose, Documented; from 1402, Lützow, Widely Accepted). He read Wyclif's books and would not call him a heretic (his own words, Documented). Under King Sigismund's safe-conduct he went to the Council of Constance, was arrested, would not abjure articles he said he never taught, and was burned there on 6 July 1415 (Peter of Mladoňovice, eyewitness, Documented). His death turned a university quarrel into a nation's movement: within two months 452 lords sealed a protest (the letter, Documented; the turn, Widely Accepted, the tree's entry).
- **Also told.** The university fights: the 1403 ban on Wyclif's articles, the 1409 decree that made the university Czech, the 1410 burning of Wyclif's books and his excommunication. The 1412 indulgence dispute, aggravated ban, appeal to Christ, interdict and his leaving Prague; *De Ecclesia* (the Church as the whole number of the predestined, Christ its only head), much of it drawn from Wyclif. Constance: the arrest within weeks, the dispute over the safe-conduct (credited each way), the king at the hearing, his defence of the cup for lay people from prison, the last refusal and the death, with Richental's hostile account credited beside Peter's. What came: the lords' protest, the movement and its chalice, his book read by the Bohemians and by Luther, who in 1520 called his death unjust and the safe-conduct broken.
- **Why on the tree.** The first voice of the Hussite and Bohemian Brethren movement; the bridge from Wyclif to the Bohemian church and, through his book, to Luther.

**Against the outline.** The outline's essence and Also told match this in substance: the Czech preacher, Wyclif, the safe-conduct, the arrest and refusal, the burning, the lords' protest and the turn to a nation's movement, with the cup and Luther. It leaves out no major contribution. The 1409 decree is a side story, and leaving it out is sound. The heart Mark approved holds, and the sources hold it.

## BLOCKING

**B1. The interdict gloss says more than its source, and it is wrong for the period.** The outline glosses it as "a ban that stopped church services, baptisms and burials in the whole city". Lützow, the only cited witness, lists what stopped: masses, sermons, all religious functions, burial with Christian rites, last rites for the dying, confession and communion (LUT 7054–7058). He does not list baptism, and an interdict commonly allowed infants to be baptized.
- *Correction (Words to explain, interdict):* "a ban that stopped Mass, sermons and church burials across the whole city (LUT 7054–7058)."

**B2. "The first demand" is unsupported, and it is wrong.** What came 2 reads: "for which lay people's right to the wine at communion was the first demand (E13, I2; census entry)." The census entry says the movement "was built around one demand" (`why`) and that the chalice "stood for the demand" (`longDescription`). It never says "first", and the first of the Four Articles of 1420 was free preaching, not the cup. The census also credits Jakoubek of Stříbro with restoring the cup to lay people in 1414 (`voices`), so a writer could wrongly make Hus its originator.
- *Correction (What came 2):* "The cup he defended from his cell became the emblem of the movement that grew from his death, the Hussite and Bohemian Brethren movement, which was built around one demand: that lay people, not only priests, receive the wine at communion (E13; census `why`)."
- *Add to Do not say:* "Not 'Hus began giving the cup to lay people' or 'the cup was his idea'. Others in Prague began it in 1414 (census `voices`); he defended it from prison (E13)."

**B3. The recommended ending jumps back a century.** Ending (a) tells the lords' letter (September 1415) last, "after Luther" (1520). What came's header also lets the writer move the lords' letter to the end. That is a jump back after the essence, which the bar counts as a failure to understand on one hearing (Kit Part 4, "The bar"; Part 1, "never the ending and then back").
- *Correction (What came header and Ending):*
  - Tell What came in time order. First the lords' letter with Q3 (1415). Then the cup as the movement's emblem. Luther (1520) comes last. The ranking stays as listed.
  - Replace Ending (a) with: "Close on the opening image seen again. The king's letter had promised he could 'return freely'. A century later Luther wrote that Hus was burned against a sworn safe-conduct, and that he had no wish to make a saint of him (LUA 6960–6964, 6992–6995). No moral."
  - Keep (b) only if (a) is not used.

**B4. The Library sweep missed held council-side witnesses. As a result the arrest is told only by Hus's partisan, where a held contemporary source differs.** The corpus-map bucket lists `council-of-constance_acta-concilii-constanciensis-bd2-lat_finke1923.txt` (Finke, *Acta Concilii Constanciensis* II), "placed ... for the trial of Hus". It is neither used nor set aside on the sheet. It holds two contemporary diaries of the council:
  - the papal official Cerretanus (Liber gestorum);
  - Cardinal Fillastre (Gesta).

What they record:
  - **The reason for the arrest (Cerretanus, 9542–9558).** He was held because he went on teaching Wyclif's doctrine to visitors at his lodging and would not stop when warned.
  - **The pope's denial (same lines).** A knight protested that the pope had given Hus a safe-conduct, and the pope denied it.
  - **The proof against him (Fillastre, 2172–2178).** He was convicted by witnesses and by his own book on the Church. He balked at abjuring because he said he had not said the things charged, "but the contrary was proved".
  - **His refusal, in the council's own words (Cerretanus, 2578–2586).** He would not recant because he believed he would offend God, and did not wish to mislead the people he had preached to in Bohemia. This is a second witness for E16.

Under the ruling of 2026-11-19, the story must credit the council's own reason beside Peter's account.
- *Corrections:*
  - **Sheet:** add these lines as E9a (the arrest's reason, and the pope's denial) and as a second witness for E15 and E16. Add the pope's denial to D5. Move Finke II into Used.
  - **Beat 4, after the arrest:** "A papal official's diary of the council gives its reason: he had gone on teaching Wyclif's doctrine to visitors at his lodging after he was warned to stop (Cerretanus, Finke II 9542–9558; credit by role)."
  - **Who tells it:** add "A papal official at the council (Cerretanus), in his diary: the reason for the arrest (beat 4). A cardinal's diary (Fillastre), for the card: the charges were proved by witnesses and his book."
  - **For the card:** add the pope's denial that he had given a safe-conduct.

## MINOR

1. **The Sibling field misses a story already on the tree.** The tradition's documented story "The Sixth of July at Constance" (`the-hussite-and-bohemian-brethren-movement`, `documentedStories`) tells the same day from the same Relatio. It quotes the hymn: "Christ, son of the living God, have mercy on me."
   - *Sibling:* add "On the tree: the tradition's documented story 'The Sixth of July at Constance' (the death, told from Peter). Here the death is one beat inside the life; its hymn quote is not used."
   - *Do not say:* add "Not the hymn's words as a quote ('Christ, son of the living God, have mercy'). Say 'he sang a prayer to Christ' (REL 17391–17394)."
2. **Do not say needs the number of devils on the crown.** Peter says the paper crown bore three devils (REL 17299–17301). Richental says two (RIC 4330–4332). Add: "Not 'three devils' or 'two devils' as fact. Say 'painted with devils', or credit each."
3. **The birth year leans toward one reading.** "Born about 1370" sits nearer Palacký's 1369 than Lützow's "between 1373 and 1375" (LUT 3007–3013). It also contradicts D1, "Leave the year out of the story".
   - *Setting, beat 1 and Do not say:* "No birth year; the card credits each reading (D1)."
4. **The heart.** Peter writes that the executioners broke his head apart with clubs and fixed his heart on a sharpened stick and roasted it (REL 17406–17410); "burned his heart apart" is not his wording.
   - *Beat 6 and Leave out:* "The executioners broke up his bones and roasted his heart on a stick, Peter writes, and his ashes were thrown into the Rhine."
5. **The reason he left Prague is incomplete.** Lützow says first that the king asked him to leave (the queen probably pressed the same), and "also" that the people's suffering moved him (LUT 7165–7179).
   - *Beat 3 and cut-first item 5:* "At the king's request, and moved by the city's suffering, his biographer writes" — or tell only that he left.
6. **The indulgences gloss says more than its source.** Lützow says "plenary indulgence" (LUT 6563). "Papal pardons for sin" is more than that, and the tree's Luther story glosses the word differently.
   - *Gloss:* "papal pardons said to cancel the punishment for sin, offered to all who fought in, or paid for, a pope's war against the king of Naples."
7. **Q3's rendering.** "Laudabiliter conversatus et comprobatus" means "lived in a way worthy of praise, and proven"; "praised" shifts the sense. *Rendering:* "Master John Hus was truly a good, just and catholic man. For many years he lived among us in our kingdom in a way worthy of praise, proven in his life, his conduct and his good name."
8. **Beat 4: who heard his reply at the arrest.** He said he would rather die than deny a truth to the bishops who came to his lodging, before he went to the cardinals (REL 13657–13663). *Beat 4 and cut-first item 10:* "he told the bishops sent to fetch him".
9. **Bethlehem gloss.** The bull says the citizen (the merchant) named it Bethlehem (DOC 18196). *Gloss:* "named Bethlehem by the Prague merchant who gave the ground".
10. **Beat 1's reason narrows the bull.** The bull says none of the city's churches was set aside for preaching at all, so Czech preachers wandered (DOC 18176–18181). *Beat 1:* "none was set aside for preaching, so preachers in Czech, the people's own tongue, were pushed into houses and hidden corners."
11. **The 452.** The seals are those of barons, nobles and knights, on eight copies in the same words (DOC 30063–30076). *Essence and Do not say:* "the lords and knights of Bohemia and Moravia set 452 seals"; not "452 lords signed".
12. **Schaff's line is already on the tree.** The `john-wyclif` card quotes his "appropriated paragraph after paragraph" (an editor's text, barred by the README). *Do not say:* "Not Schaff's words as a quote. Paraphrase: much of the book was taken from Wyclif's, his translator writes."
13. **A gloss is missing.** Do not say uses "the secular power" without a gloss. *Words to explain:* "secular power: the lay rulers, here the king's officers (REL 17303–17306)."
14. **The king's own claim about the safe-conduct.** At the hearing he said he gave it before Hus left Prague (REL 15438–15441; D3). It is missing from For the card. *Add it there* beside the Speyer date of 18 October, so each claim is credited.
15. **A held witness is missing from the sweep.** Lützow's appendix translates a contemporary Czech account of the death (LUT 16025–16460), which Patera found. Patera doubts its author was an eyewitness. *Sheet:* list it under D6, set aside for the story and kept for the card.
