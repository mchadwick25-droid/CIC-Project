# Outline check: Jan Hus

## My own heart, written before reading Outline.md

Disclosure: a tool preview showed me the outline's first lines (the essence and the first two Also-told items) before this section was written. What follows comes from the census job line, the source sheet and what Hus is known for.

- **Essence.** A Prague priest and university master who preached to the city in Czech at the Bethlehem Chapel (the 1408 bull for the chapel's purpose, Documented; from 1402, Lützow, Widely Accepted). He read Wyclif's books and would not call him a heretic (his own words, Documented). Under King Sigismund's safe-conduct he went to the Council of Constance, was arrested, would not abjure articles he said he never taught, and was burned there on 6 July 1415 (Peter of Mladoňovice, eyewitness, Documented). His death turned a university quarrel into a nation's movement: within two months 452 lords sealed a protest (the letter, Documented; the turn, Widely Accepted, the tree's entry).
- **Also told.** The university fights: the 1403 ban on Wyclif's articles, the 1409 decree that made the university Czech, the 1410 burning of Wyclif's books and his excommunication. The 1412 indulgence dispute, aggravated ban, appeal to Christ, interdict and his leaving Prague; *De Ecclesia* (the Church as the whole number of the predestined, Christ its only head), much of it drawn from Wyclif. Constance: the arrest within weeks, the dispute over the safe-conduct (credited each way), the king at the hearing, his defence of the cup for lay people from prison, the last refusal and the death, with Richental's hostile account credited beside Peter's. What came: the lords' protest, the movement and its chalice, his book read by the Bohemians and by Luther, who in 1520 called his death unjust and the safe-conduct broken.
- **Why on the tree.** The first voice of the Hussite and Bohemian Brethren movement; the bridge from Wyclif to the Bohemian church and, through his book, to Luther.
