# Jan Hus: researcher's reply

**Essence.** A Prague priest and university master who preached in Czech at the Bethlehem Chapel (Documented, papal bull of 1408; his start there in 1402 is Lützow's, Widely Accepted). He read Wyclif and would not condemn him (Documented, his own words). He went to Constance under King Sigismund's safe-conduct, was arrested, refused to abjure, and was burned on 6 July 1415 (Documented, eyewitnesses). Within two months 452 lords sealed a protest (Documented). That his death turned a quarrel into a nation's movement is Widely Accepted (the tree's entry).

**Also told.** The 1403 ban and 1410 book-burning; the 1412 ban, interdict, exile and his book on the Church (Widely Accepted); the cup he defended from prison (Documented); Luther in 1520 (Documented, English only).

**Shape and length.** Martyr, about 4:00 (about 600 words; quotes, glosses and credited witnesses about 295). Opens on the safe-conduct's own words: "pass, stay, remain and return freely".

**Connection.** The lords and the cup carried his cause from a university quarrel into a nation's movement.

**Quotes.**
1. "I do not know where John Wyclif's soul is. I hope that he is saved, and I fear that he may be damned. Yet in hope I would wish my soul to be where John Wyclif's soul is." The last sentence is lifted from `john-wyclif`; the first two are new to the tree.
2. "God is my witness that I never taught or preached the things falsely charged to me, laid on me by false witnesses. The chief aim of my preaching, and of all my other acts and writings, was only to turn people from sin. I wrote, taught and preached the truth of the gospel from the words and teachings of the holy teachers. In that truth I will gladly die today."
3. "Master John Hus was truly a good, just and catholic man. For many years he lived in our kingdom, praised and proven in his life, his conduct and his good name."

**Sources differ.** His birth year; when he first said the Wyclif words; when he got the safe-conduct and what it covered; Richental's hay-cart arrest, which Peter calls false; his death (Peter: he sang; Richental: he cried out).

**Gaps.** Spinka, Fudge and Kaminsky not held. Luther's "all Hussites" held only in Lützow's English. Piccolomini and Mansi damaged with no confirming print: card only. The job says "emperor", but Sigismund was crowned emperor only in 1433: the job line is corrected to "King Sigismund". The Constance council profile's draft uses two lines this outline avoided.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught an unsourced motive for leaving Prague (now credited to Lützow), "eight months" (now seven), and the bones and heart left out (restored).
