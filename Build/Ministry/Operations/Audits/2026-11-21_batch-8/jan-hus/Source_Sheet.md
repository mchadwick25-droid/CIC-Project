## Setting

- **S1. Birth.** LUT 3007–3016 (editor's survey, 1909): "We are unable to state positively in what year Hus was born. The oldest traditions stated that he was born on July 6, 1373. More recently such great authorities as Palacky and Tomek gave July 6, 1369 ... the exact year of his birth cannot be affirmed, but it undoubtedly took place in the period between 1373 and 1375. The day is quite uncertain." Later, secondary. Year Contested; say "born about 1370" at most, or leave out. See D1.
- **S2. Place of birth.** LUT 3018–3019: "John Hus, or, 'of Husinec,' was born in the village of Husinec near the small town of Prachatice". Peasant parents, LUT 3045–3047. Later, secondary. Widely Accepted.
- **S3. University career.** LUT 3363–3366: "In 1394 he became a bachelor of divinity, and in 1396 a master of arts. In 1402 he became ... for the first time rector of the university. It was probably in 1400 that Hus was ordained a priest". Rector again after the Germans left in 1409: LUT 5079–5082. Secondary. Widely Accepted. In the safe-conduct of 1414 the king calls him "sacrae theologiae baccalaureum formatum et artium magistrum" (DOC 13090–13091; Documented).
- **S4. Prague and the Czech tongue.** The papal bull of Gregory XII, Lucca, 15 May 1408 (DOC 18147–18233), confirming the chapel's foundation. Contemporary document. Documented. DOC 18178–18183: "nullus locorum eorundem privilegiatus ad praedicationis verbi dei ministerium ibidem specialiter esset deputatus, sed praedicantes in vulgari Bohemico, quod ibidem usuale sive naturale existit, plerumque per domos et latebras cogerentur divagari". (No place in the city was set aside for preaching, so preachers in Czech, the common and native tongue there, were mostly forced to wander through houses and hidden corners.) Founders: "Crucis Institoris, civis Pragensis" and "Joannes de Mulheim, miles" (DOC 18175–18176); the place "quem Bethleem nuncupari censuit, pro usu praedicationis verbi dei" with "duobus rectoribus ... et praedicatoribus in dicto vulgari" (DOC 18196–18200).
- **S5. Founding date and Hus's appointment.** LUT 3429–3431: "It was due to the great fame of Hus as a preacher that he obtained in 1402 the important appointment of preacher at the Bethlehem chapel." LUT 3463–3474: the founding document "(dated May 24, 1391)" regrets that there was no place "where sermons could be preached in the national language. Bohemian preachers were therefore generally obliged to seek shelter in houses or hiding-places." Secondary for both dates. Widely Accepted. The bull (S4) is the primary witness for the purpose.
- **S6. Sigismund's titles.** Safe-conduct, DOC 13107: "regnorum nostrorum anno Hungariae ... XXXIII, Romanorum vero quinto": king of Hungary and King of the Romans. He calls Hus taken "in nostram et sacri imperii protectionem" (DOC 13092–13093). He was crowned emperor only in 1433: LHW 13098–13100, "King Sigismund, who, in the course of the year 1433, was crowned as Roman Emperor". He was brother of King Wenceslas of Bohemia: REL 15452–15453, Sigismund says "propter nos et honorem nostrum et fratrem nostrum et regnum Bohemiae". Documented.
- **S7. Date of death.** REL 17086–17089: "Die vero sequenti, quae erat sexta dies Julii, alias sabbato post Procopii, in octava beatorum Petri et Pauli apostolorum". Palacký's note 17125 says the manuscript reads "septima dies Julii", and his note 17072 corrects the day before. RIC 4286: "Uff samßtag nach sant Uolrichs tag" (Saturday after St Ulrich's day) 1415. Documented: Saturday 6 July 1415.

## Events

- **E1. The first Wyclif quarrel, 1403.** LUT 3654–3697 (secondary): a German master, John Hübner, brought twenty-one articles drawn from Wyclif's works, with twenty-four others condemned in London, before the university; at a meeting "on May 28, 1403", the majority declared that "no one should teach, repeat, or affirm these articles either privately or publicly." Hus protested. SCH 565–571 (editor): "In spite of Huss's protest ... the writings were forbidden to be read or taught. Huss declined to accept the decision". REL 15221–15227 (the charge and his answer at Constance): he resisted the condemnation "unacum aliis magistris". Widely Accepted.
- **E2. The university made Czech, 1409.** LUT 4706–4724 (secondary, paraphrasing the decree): the king's decree of Kutná Hora "(January 18, 1409)" gave the Bohemian nation three votes in the university and the foreign nations one. LUT 4912–4918: "on May 16 ... a large number of German magisters and students left Prague for Leipzig", "about 2000 men" with servants. LUT 5079–5082: the Bohemians elected Hus rector. Widely Accepted.
- **E3. Wyclif's books burned; Hus excommunicated, 1410.** LUT 5554–5569 (secondary): on July 16 Archbishop Zbyněk had Wyclif's books burned in his palace court, "and all present sang the Te Deum"; from his castle of Roudnice "he there pronounced the sentence of excommunication against Hus." LUT 5579–5596 quotes a contemporary chronicle: "Some took the part of the canons and some that of Hus. Henceforth there was great discord among the people." Widely Accepted.
- **E4. Hus will not condemn Wyclif: his own words.** WP 4489–4494, Hus to the English master John Stokes (1411; English only here): "you judge me, and in your book you condemn the soul of Wyclif. Where is revelation, or Scripture, or personal acquaintance, that you condemn a man who stands at the bar of God?" WP 4517–4520 (Hus's reply, quoted by the editors): "I will not grant that Wyclif is a heretic, I will not affirm a negative, but I hope that he is not, since in doubtful matters one ought to choose the better part. Wherefore, I hope that Wyclif is among the saved." His own words. Documented. Latin of his written reply: DOC 9052–9056, "Confiteor enim me dixisse, quod vellem esse in spe, ubi est anima Wikleff".
- **E5. Indulgences, the ban and the interdict, 1412.** LUT 6630–6641: a papal preacher of indulgences reached Prague in 1412; Hus disputed them publicly. LUT 7000–7046: in August 1412 a bull aggravated his excommunication; "all the faithful were forbidden to give him food or drink or to speak to him"; Hus "appealed from the sentence of the Roman pontiff to Jesus Christ, the supreme judge"; a further bull ordered the Bethlehem chapel, "a nest of heretics", "destroyed and levelled to the ground"; "The interdict was again proclaimed". Secondary. Widely Accepted. Hus's own defence of the appeal at Constance: REL 17183–17192, "non est tutior appellatio, quam ad Jesum Christum dominum, qui non flectitur pravo munere, nec fallitur falsa attestatione" (no appeal is safer than to the Lord Jesus Christ, who is not bent by a bribe nor deceived by false witness). Documented.
- **E6. Exile and the book on the Church, 1412–1414.** LUT 7176–7179: moved by the sufferings of the city under the interdict, "He therefore left Prague, probably in October 1412." LUT 7305–7306: he stayed away until "October 1414". SCH 2648–2650 (Hus): "the holy catholic — that is, universal — church is the totality of the predestinate ... present, past, and future." SCH 3654: "Christ alone is the head of the universal church". SCH 1507–1517 (Schaff, editor): "Huss appropriated paragraph after paragraph from his predecessor and transferred them often with little verbal change to his own pages", as Loserth showed. His own words for the doctrine, Documented; the borrowing, Widely Accepted (editor, with Loserth). Czech works of these years: LUT 8210–8225 (the Výklad and others; secondary).
- **E7. The safe-conduct.** Text: DOC 13083–13110 (in REL), dated "Datum Spirae ... XVIII die Octobris" 1414 (DOC 13105–13106). The king takes Hus into "nostram et sacri imperii protectionem ... et tutelam" (13092–13093) and orders all to let him "transire, stare, morari et redire libere" (13102–13103: pass, stay, remain and return freely), and to provide him "de securo et salvo ... conductu" (13104). Documented. REL 13063–13068 (Peter): Sigismund had promised to send "suos speciales salvos conductus ... libere ut Constantiam veniens, e converso redire ad Bohemiam possit", and afterwards "eosdem salvos conductus direxit obviam" (sent them to meet him on the way). Documented as Peter's account.
- **E8. He sets out.** WP 7455–7460 (editors): "Hus set off without even waiting for the safe-conduct. As soon as he had received Sigismund's official promise ... dated Rothenburg, October 8 — Hus started (October 11, 1414)". LUT 8202: "On October 11, 1414, Hus left Prague". Hus's Czech letter to the Bethlehem congregation, WP 7516–7518 (English only here): "Now, however, I have started on my journey, without safe-conduct, into the midst of many of my greatest enemies". His own words. Documented. Arrival: LUT 9227, "on November 3, 1414". Secondary.
- **E9. The arrest, 28 November 1414.** REL 13621–13690. Contemporary eyewitness (Peter, from Chlum's side). Documented as Peter's account. The cardinals sent two bishops and the burgomaster to his lodging; Chlum protested that the king had ordered him to bring Hus under safe-conduct, and told the burgomaster "si diabolus veniret ad videndum causam suam, ipse deberet veraciter audiri" (13640–13641). Hus: "potius volo mortem eligere, antequam veritatem ex scripturis vel alias mihi cognitam denegarem" (13661–13663: I would rather choose death than deny a truth known to me from Scripture or otherwise). The house had been surrounded by armed men (13667–13668). He was kept under guard (13684–13686). WP 8410–8414 (editors, from REL): on 6 December he was moved "to a dark cell hard by the latrines" in the Dominican house. LUT 9629 agrees on the date. See D4 for Richental's other account.
- **E9a. The council side on the arrest.** Finke II 9542–9558 (Cerretanus, *Liber gestorum*, papal official, contemporary): Hus was confined more strictly "quia in domo, in qua Constancie hospitatus erat, doctrinam reprobam Wyclef docmatizabat ad eum accedentibus ... et monitus super hoc desistere nolebat" (because in the house where he lodged at Constance he taught Wyclif's condemned doctrine to those who came to him, and when warned would not stop), after the pope's deliberation, with the king's envoys and the city's burgomaster and council consenting; a knight who had brought him to Constance protested that the pope had given Hus a safe-conduct, "quod quidem papa negavit" (which the pope denied). Documented as the council side's account.
- **E10. The council's side on the safe-conduct.** REL 14284–14290: the council's commissioners had been told that "post captivitatem dicti M. J. Hus sui fautores et amici sibi salvum conductum die XV procurarunt" (his friends got the safe-conduct for him fifteen days after his arrest). The Bohemian lords answered that it was shown and read to many on the day of arrest (14291–14309). RIC 4214–4218 (hostile eyewitness): the king wanted to help, thinking it a great shame if his safe-conduct were broken, "Do antworten im die gelertien: Es könd und möcht in kainen rechten nit sin, das ain kätzer gelait haben solt" (the learned answered him that by no law could a heretic have a safe-conduct), and when he heard their earnestness he let it be. WP 8095–8097 (editors): "In reality the Pope's safe-conduct could alone have guaranteed his immunity from the Inquisition. Sigismund's safe-conduct did not reach the spiritual sphere." Each Documented as its witness's claim; what the safe-conduct covered, Contested.
- **E11. Sigismund at the hearing, 7 June 1415.** REL 15436–15458 (Peter, present). Documented as Peter's report. The king said: some say he gave the safe-conduct only fifteen days after the arrest, "ego dico, quod non est verum ... ego dedi tibi salvum conductum etiam prius quam de Praga exivisti" (15438–15441), so that Hus might have a public hearing; "licet dicant alii, quod ego non possem dare salvum conductum haeretico" (15447–15448); he counselled Hus to submit to the council's grace; "nullum volo haereticum defendere, imo si unus vellet in sua haeresi esse pertinax, ego solus vellem succendere et comburere ipsum" (15456–15458: I want to defend no heretic; if one would stay stubborn in his heresy, I alone would light the fire and burn him).
- **E12. The Wyclif words at the hearing, 7 June 1415.** REL 15228–15250. Charge: he had said to the people, "Utinam anima mea esset ibi, ubi est anima Joannis Wiclef!" (15241–15242). His answer: twelve years before, when Wyclif's theological books were not yet in Bohemia and he knew only of his good life, he had said: "Nescio, ubi est anima ipsius Joan. W.; spero, quod sit salvatus et timeo, ne sit damnatus; vellem tamen in spe, quod anima mea esset ibi, ubi est anima Joannis W." (15246–15249). "Et cum haec in concilio dixisset, deriserunt eum valde, moventes capita sua" (15249–15250). Then (15254–15258) he said he had defended no error of Wyclif. Documented, Peter's record of his words.
- **E13. The cup.** Hus's letter from prison after 18 June 1415. Latin DOC 7360–7369; English WP 11870–11884. His own words. Documented. DOC 7360–7366: "O quanta dementia: evangelium Christi ... et factum Christi ... ut errorem condemnare! scilicet de communione sacramenti calicis domini pro omnibus adultis fidelibus constituta. Ecce errorem dicunt, quod laicis fidelibus bibere licet de calice domini". WP 11753–11755 (editors): the council's decree "forbidding the cup ... was formally passed on June 15, 1415." The census tradition entry gives the chalice as the movement's emblem (census `longDescription`).
- **E14. 5 July: the last offer.** REL 17043–17066. Lord John of Chlum to Hus: "nullomodo facias contra conscientiam tuam, nec in conspectu dei mentiaris, sed potius sta usque ad mortem in ea, quam cognovisti, veritate" (17056–17059). Hus, weeping: if he knew he had written anything against the law and the church, he would recant, "deus mihi testis est"; but let them show him better Scriptures (17059–17065). Documented, Peter's record.
- **E15. 6 July: the sentence.** REL 17086–17225; council text MAN 78630–78666. The session in the cathedral, the king presiding crowned (REL 17089–17091). The sentence declared him a heretic and condemned his books, "et praesertim libellus de Ecclesia" (REL 17217–17221). MAN 78636–78643 (council's own text, damaged OCR): it judged that he had "seduced" the Christian people, "maxime in regno Bohemie", and was "non veracem predicatorem evangelii Christi ... sed ... verius seductorem", "pertinacem et incorrigibilem", to be deposed and degraded and "curie seculari relinquendum" (MAN 78644–78666). Hus prayed aloud for his enemies (REL 17236–17242). Documented. Second witness, Finke II 2172–2178 (Fillastre, *Gesta*, cardinal): he was convicted "tam per testes, quam per librum per eum compositum, quem intitulavit: de ecclesia", and made difficulty about abjuring "quia dicebat non dixisse, set probabatur contrarium" (because he said he had not said it, but the contrary was proved).
- **E16. 6 July: why he would not recant.** REL 17250–17258: dressed in priest's vestments and urged to abjure, he said from the platform, weeping: "timeo hoc facere, ne sim mendax in conspectu domini, et etiam ne conscientiam et dei veritatem offendam, cum numquam istos tenui articulos, qui contra me false testantur ... et etiam ideo ne tantam multitudinem, cui praedicavi, scandalisem". Documented, Peter's record. Second witness, Finke II 2578–2586 (Fillastre, *Gesta*): he said aloud he would not recant "quia crederet Deum offendere et sanctos et quod nolebat scandalizare tantum populum, cui illam doctrinam predicaverat in Boemia" (because he believed he would offend God and the saints, and did not wish to scandalize so many people to whom he had preached that doctrine in Bohemia). Documented.
- **E17. 6 July: degradation and the paper crown.** REL 17262–17305: the bishops took the chalice from his hands, cursing him as Judas (17262–17268); stripped each vestment; cut his tonsure (17281–17289); before setting the paper crown on him said "Committimus animam tuam diabolo" (17292). The crown was round, about a cubit high, painted with three devils, titled "Hic est haeresiarcha" (17299–17303). The king told Duke Ludwig "Vade, accipe eum" (17303–17306). RIC 4330–4333 agrees on the mitre with two devils and "Heresiarcha". Documented.
- **E18. 6 July: the road and the stake.** REL 17309–17405. His books burned in the churchyard as he passed, and he smiled (17310–17313); nearly the whole city went armed (17315); the place a meadow between the gardens outside the gate (17328–17331); tied to a post with a sooty chain at the neck, wood and straw to his chin (17356–17373). The imperial marshal and the duke asked him a last time to abjure (17375–17379). His answer, REL 17380–17388: "Deus mihi, inquit, testis est, quod ea, quae false mihi ascribuntur, per falsos testes mihi imposita, numquam docui nec praedicavi, sed de principali intentione meae praedicationis et omnium aliorum meorum actuum vel scriptorum exstitit, ut solum homines retrahere possem a peccatis; in ea vero evangelii veritate, quam scripsi, docui et praedicavi ex dictis et ex positionibus sanctorum doctorum hodie laetanter volo mori." He sang "Christe fili dei vivi miserere nobis", then "miserere mei", and as he began "Qui natus es ex Maria virgine" the wind blew the flame into his face (17391–17396). Documented, eyewitness.
- **E19. The ashes.** REL 17400–17415: the executioners broke the bones, burned the heart apart, threw his coat into the fire "ne Bohemi illud pro reliquiis haberent", and threw the ashes into the Rhine. RIC 4382: "Damach fürt man die äschen gentzlichen, was da lag, in den Rin." Documented, two eyewitnesses agree.

## Impact, heaviest first

- **I1. The lords' protest: a nation takes up his cause, 2 September 1415.** DOC 30063–30260. Contemporary document. Documented. Heading (Palacký, 30063–30076): letters of the barons, nobles and knights of Bohemia and Moravia, "sigillis 452 additis", reproaching the council for the condemnation and death of Hus; "Pragae, 1415, 2 Sept." Text: they had written to Sigismund, and those letters, "ut refertur", the council had thrown into the fire (30143–30148). DOC 30155–30158: "quod ipse M. Joannes Hus fuit vir utique bonus, justus et catholicus, a multis annis in regno nostro vita, moribus et fama laudabiliter conversatus et comprobatus". DOC 30165–30168: "numquam audivimus, nec intelligere potuimus ... quod praefatus M. Joannes Hus quenquam errorem vel haeresim in suis sermonibus docuisset". The vow, DOC 30256–30260: "legem domini nostri Jesu Christi ipsiusque devotos et humiles ac constantes praedicatores usque effusionem sanguinis, omni timore et statutis humanis in contrarium editis posttergatis, defendere volumus et tueri."
- **I2. The movement.** Census tradition entry (`the-hussite-and-bohemian-brethren-movement`, `longDescription`, `why`): "Then in 1415 the Council of Constance burned him as a heretic. The shock turned a university movement into a national one. Its emblem was the chalice"; the lay cup; the Four Articles of 1420; legal recognition in 1436. This is the tree's own sourced entry; the turn itself is an interpretation, Widely Accepted. Hus's own defence of the lay cup (E13) is the bridge.
- **I3. His book read by the Bohemians and the reformers.** SCH 1795–1806 (Schaff, editor): "what Wyclif's Bible was to the small company of dissenters in England, Huss's Treatise on the Church was to the large body of Bohemians"; Hussites sent a copy to Luther, and "in 1520, an edition was printed in Mainz". "Wyclif was not quoted by the Reformers. They knew him through Huss." Editor's view. Widely Accepted.
- **I4. Luther, 1520.** LUA 6960–6974: "John Huss and Jerome of Prague were burnt at Constance in violation of the Papal, Christian, and Imperial oath and safe conduct". LUA 6980–6982: "It is not my intention here to judge John Huss's belief and to defend his errors; although my understanding has not been able to find any error in him". LUA 6992–6995: "I have no wish to make a saint or a martyr of John Huss (as some Bohemians do), though I own that he was treated unjustly, and that his books and his doctrines were wrongfully condemned". Later witness, about a century; English translation only. Documented as Luther's words. LUT 12563–12566 gives Luther's "We have all been Hussites without knowing it" in Lützow's English only, without the Latin: editor-carried; not to be quoted.
- **I5. A hostile humanist's memory, about 1458.** PIC 4052–4062 (damaged OCR; no other held print confirms it, since the 1524 printing could not be read at this place): "Pertulerunt ambo constanti animo necem, et quasi ad epulas invitati ad incendium properarunt, nullam emittentes vocem, quae miseri animi posset facere indicium. Ubi ardere coeperunt, hymnos cecinere ... Nemo philosophorum tam forti animo mortem pertulisse traditur, quam isti incendium." Of Hus and Jerome together. Later hostile witness, about forty years. Documented as his words, reading uncertain; card only, or paraphrase credited.
- **I6. The day kept.** Census tradition `legacy`: "6 July, the day Hus was burned, is a public holiday in the Czech Republic." Tree's own entry. Not held elsewhere.

## Where sources disagree or come late

- **D1. Birth year.** The oldest traditions 1373; Palacký and Tomek 1369; Lützow, after Flajšhans, between 1373 and 1375 (S1). Contested. Leave the year out of the story.
- **D2. When he first spoke the Wyclif wish.** Peter's record of the hearing: Hus said he had said it twelve years before (about 1403), before Wyclif's theological books reached Bohemia (E12). Workman and Pope: in a sermon before the synod, probably June 1408 (WP 4509–4512: "In a sermon before the Synod and in the presence of Zbinek (probably in June 1408), Hus had stated: 'I could hope that my soul should be where rests the soul of Wyclif.'"). The `john-wyclif` card gives "probably 1408". Credit each; the story tells it at Constance, where both agree he repeated it.
- **D3. When the safe-conduct was given.** The document is dated Speyer, 18 October 1414 (E7), a week after he left on 11 October (E8, editors and Lützow). Hus wrote that he set out without it (E8). Peter says it was sent to meet him on the road (E7). Sigismund told him at the hearing that he gave it before Hus left Prague (E11). Some at the council said it was got fifteen days after the arrest (E10). Documented as each one's claim; the exact sequence Contested.
- **D4. The arrest.** Peter: arrested 28 November when called to the cardinals; he names the rumour that Hus had fled hidden in a hay cart and says "sed falsum fuit" (REL 13600–13615). Richental, the Constance burgher: Hus hid in a knight's hay wagon with bread and a flask of wine to get away, was found, and was then handed to the pope (RIC 3262–3283; again RIC 4177–4200, where he dates the attempt to the Sunday Oculi in Lent and the arrest after it). Richental is hostile and inconsistent on the date. Contested; Peter's date stands with Lützow and the editors (E9). The story does not tell the hay wagon (Leave out); the card may credit both.
- **D5. What the safe-conduct covered.** Hus and his lords: a promise broken (WP 11197–11203; DOC 14095–14103). The learned at Constance, per Richental: no law gives a heretic a safe-conduct (E10). Sigismund: he gave it so Hus could have a public hearing, and some said he could not give it to a heretic (E11). The editors: it did not reach the church's courts (E10). Luther: a broken oath and safe-conduct (I4). The pope, per Cerretanus: he denied he had given Hus a safe-conduct (E9a). Contested; tell each credited.
- **D6. His death.** Peter: he prayed and sang aloud until the wind blew the flame into his face, and died praying silently (E18). Richental: when the fire was lit, "Do gehüb er sich mit schryen vast übel und was bald verbrunnen" (RIC 4373–4374: he cried out very badly and was soon burned). Piccolomini, decades later and hostile: they went to the fire as if to a feast, letting out no word of a wretched mind, and sang hymns (I5). Credit each; the narrator does not settle it. Also held: a contemporary Czech account of the death, found by Patera, in Lützow's English (LUT 16025–16460); Patera doubts its author was an eyewitness, though the writer claims to be (LUT 16053–16058). Set aside for the story; kept for the card.
- **D7. Confession.** Richental: a priest offered to hear his confession if he would renounce his heresy; Hus said "Es ist nit not, ich bin kain todsünder nit!" (RIC 4366–4368: there is no need, I am no mortal sinner). Peter: a priest on horseback said he should not be heard or given a confessor, as a heretic; Hus had confessed in prison to a monk and been absolved, as he wrote in his notes (REL 17335–17341). Leave out; card at most.
- **D8. Did he hold Wyclif's teaching on the bread?** He was charged with holding that the bread remains after consecration (SCH 570–571, editor). He answered at Constance that he had defended no error of Wyclif (E12). His teaching on this point is Contested; the story must not say he denied that the bread becomes Christ's body.
- **D9. The movement's turn.** "Turned a university quarrel into a nation's movement" is the census entry's reading (I2); the held primary witness for it is the lords' letter (I1). Widely Accepted as interpretation; Documented as the letter.
