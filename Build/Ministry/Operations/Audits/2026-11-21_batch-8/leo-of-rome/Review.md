## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Leo, bishop of Rome from 440 to 461, a Latin writer who never went east, whose letter on Christ was read to a council of Greek-speaking bishops.
- **What happened:** his letter (the Tome) was kept from being read at Ephesus in 449, which he called a robbery; at Chalcedon in 451 it was acclaimed and, after doubts, adopted. He rejected the council's rule ranking Constantinople next to Rome and declared it void by Peter's authority, but it stood. In 452 he went to meet Attila, and three witnesses tell why Attila left.
- **Why the connection matters:** the Tome's faith became the faith of the Greek and Latin churches and divided them from Egypt and Syria, and his claim to speak for Peter set Rome against Constantinople's rank for centuries.

No line is blank or wrong.

**My essence, written before reading the approved heart.** Leo wrote the Tome, a letter saying Christ is one person in two natures, which the Council of Chalcedon took as its rule of faith; and he claimed that Peter spoke through the bishop of Rome, which he used to refuse Constantinople's rank. Also told: Ephesus 449, the robbery; the doubts at Chalcedon; the canon refused and standing; Attila and the three accounts; how each side later read him.
