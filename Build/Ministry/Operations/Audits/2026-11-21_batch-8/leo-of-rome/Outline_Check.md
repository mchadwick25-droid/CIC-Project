# Outline check: Leo of Rome

## My own heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry (teaser: "Bishop of Rome whose letter to Flavian of Constantinople, the Tome, set out the two natures of Christ and was received at the Council of Chalcedon in 451"), the batch job, and the source sheet.

**The essence.** Leo, bishop of Rome from 440 to 461, wrote the letter to Flavian called the Tome (June 449), which held Christ one person, truly God and truly human, each nature doing what is its own; it was read at Chalcedon in 451, hailed with "Peter has spoken thus through Leo", and taken into the council's faith. He is on the tree because that letter became the West's and Chalcedon's statement of the two natures, and because he spoke as Peter's heir with a care over the whole church, a claim he pressed against the East.

**Also told.**
- He was elected in 440 while away in Gaul as a deacon making peace between generals; Rome waited more than forty days (Prosper, Documented).
- His view of his office: Peter honoured in his person, his unworthy heir (Sermon III, Documented, own words).
- The Manichaeans hunted out in Rome, their books burned, those who would not recant banished by the emperor's decree (Prosper 443, Feltoe 444; Documented; the force told plainly).
- Ephesus 449: the Tome not read, Flavian deposed and roughly used, his deacon's escape; Leo's plea "with groans and tears" for a council in Italy; later his verdict "not a trial but a robbery" (Documented, told from Leo's side).
- Chalcedon 451: his legates presided; the Tome read and cheered at session II; some bishops doubted it and it was accepted only at session IV (Documented).
- Canon 28: he refused the rank given to Constantinople and declared the bishops' assent void by Peter's authority; the canon stood (Documented).
- Attila 452: he went to the king with two high officials and Attila withdrew; why is told three ways (Prosper, Jordanes citing Priscus, Hydatius) (Contested).
- Geiseric 455: he met the Vandal king outside the gates; Rome was spared fire and slaughter but plundered for fourteen days (Prosper, Documented). Optional.
- Death late 461, buried in St Peter's (Documented).

What came of him, heaviest first: the Tome as the received statement of the two natures; his claim to speak for Peter, which outlasted the dispute over canon 28; his memory as the saviour of Italy (later Roman witnesses); the churches that refused Chalcedon read him as the enemy (hostile Syriac chronicle).

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beats 4 and 5 jump back in time.** Beat 4 ends with Quote 2, from the letter of 20 July 451, and then adds the excommunication of spring 450. Beat 5 then opens with Theodosius's death in July 450 and the calling of the new council. The listener goes 449, then 451, then 450, then back to 450 again. That is a jump back after the essence (Kit Part 3, Story beats; the bar in Part 4).
*Correction.* Beat 4 ends at the October 449 plea for a council in Italy, followed by the optional clause on the spring 450 excommunication. Move Quote 2 into beat 5, after "called a council, but in the East, not in Italy as Leo had asked". Write: "In July 451, writing to the empress Pulcheria to accept the council and send his envoys, he said: Quote 2 (E12, LEO 8610–8616, 8637–8640)." Then continue with "His envoys presided". In the Sibling field and in Quote 2's job, change "the hinge between the defeat at Ephesus and the reversal at Chalcedon" to: "his own verdict on Ephesus, given as he accepted the new council and sent his envoys". This also keeps Q2's job apart from its use in `dioscorus-of-alexandria`. Leave D8 ("two years later") as it stands.

**B2. Beat 6 states Feltoe's reading of the end of the quarrel as settled, where another held source differs.** Beat 6 says Leo "broke off contact with the bishop of Constantinople until that bishop gave way (E22a; Feltoe)". Feltoe says Anatolius "at last yielded" (LEO 818–821). The editors of the councils say "the breach was made up ... by an apparently insincere letter on the part of the latter", and that "Leo never gave over his opposition" (NC 22430–22432).
*Correction.* Replace it with: "He broke off contact with the bishop of Constantinople until that bishop wrote to make peace (E22a; NC 22430–22432)." Add to the card list: "Feltoe: Anatolius 'at last yielded'; the editors of the councils: the breach was made up by 'an apparently insincere letter'." Add to Do not say: "'the bishop of Constantinople gave way' or 'submitted' → 'wrote to make peace'".

**B3. "Close to Leo" is stated as fact, but it rests on a Contested report.** Beat 7 says "Prosper, who wrote in those years and was close to Leo". Who tells it says the listener "must hear once that he was close to Leo". The only support is Gennadius's "are also thought to have been dictated by him" (GEN 43673–43676, Contested on the sheet, E7e) and a bracketed manuscript variant. Prosper's own chronicle shows praise ("beatissimus papa Leo", PROS 54952–54953; "viro sancto", 54715). It does not show closeness.
*Correction.* In beat 7, replace the clause with: "Prosper, who wrote in those years and praises Leo throughout". In Who tells it, change "The listener must hear once that he was close to Leo" to: "The listener must hear once that he praises Leo. If the Gennadius clause is kept, say only that a writer of the next generation thought Prosper had drafted Leo's letters." Add to Do not say: "'Prosper, Leo's friend' or 'close to Leo' as fact → 'who praises Leo throughout'".
