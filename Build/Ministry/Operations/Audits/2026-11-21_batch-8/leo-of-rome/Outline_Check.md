# Outline check: Leo of Rome

## My own heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry (teaser: "Bishop of Rome whose letter to Flavian of Constantinople, the Tome, set out the two natures of Christ and was received at the Council of Chalcedon in 451"), the batch job, and the source sheet.

**The essence.** Leo, bishop of Rome from 440 to 461, wrote the letter to Flavian called the Tome (June 449), which held Christ one person, truly God and truly human, each nature doing what is its own; it was read at Chalcedon in 451, hailed with "Peter has spoken thus through Leo", and taken into the council's faith. He is on the tree because that letter became the West's and Chalcedon's statement of the two natures, and because he spoke as Peter's heir with a care over the whole church, a claim he pressed against the East.

**Also told.**
- He was elected in 440 while away in Gaul as a deacon making peace between generals; Rome waited more than forty days (Prosper, Documented).
- His view of his office: Peter honoured in his person, his unworthy heir (Sermon III, Documented, own words).
- The Manichaeans hunted out in Rome, their books burned, those who would not recant banished by the emperor's decree (Prosper 443, Feltoe 444; Documented; the force told plainly).
- Ephesus 449: the Tome not read, Flavian deposed and roughly used, his deacon's escape; Leo's plea "with groans and tears" for a council in Italy; later his verdict "not a trial but a robbery" (Documented, told from Leo's side).
- Chalcedon 451: his legates presided; the Tome read and cheered at session II; some bishops doubted it and it was accepted only at session IV (Documented).
- Canon 28: he refused the rank given to Constantinople and declared the bishops' assent void by Peter's authority; the canon stood (Documented).
- Attila 452: he went to the king with two high officials and Attila withdrew; why is told three ways (Prosper, Jordanes citing Priscus, Hydatius) (Contested).
- Geiseric 455: he met the Vandal king outside the gates; Rome was spared fire and slaughter but plundered for fourteen days (Prosper, Documented). Optional.
- Death late 461, buried in St Peter's (Documented).

What came of him, heaviest first: the Tome as the received statement of the two natures; his claim to speak for Peter, which outlasted the dispute over canon 28; his memory as the saviour of Italy (later Roman witnesses); the churches that refused Chalcedon read him as the enemy (hostile Syriac chronicle).
