# Source sheet: Leo of Rome

Census id `leo-of-rome`; told within `imperial-juridical-christianity`, spoken name "Church and Empire" (census `informalName`), era 2. Card: `Card.existing.json` in this folder (used as a starting list only).

File keys. All paths are under `cic/texts/`.
- **LEO**: `npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml`, Leo's Letters and Sermons in Charles Lett Feltoe's English (1895), with Feltoe's Introduction (lines 386–992). Clean transcription.
- **NC**: `npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml`, the acts and canons of the councils in English (H. R. Percival, ed.), with Percival's notes and excursus.
- **M5**, **M6**: `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-5-431-441-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt` and `...-tomus-6-441-451-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt`. Mansi's Latin (and Greek) of the Tome, Leo's letters and the acts of Chalcedon. **Damaged scan**: raw OCR of a 1901 facsimile, long s read as f, Greek column mostly garbled.
- **PROS**: `prosper_chronica-minora-1-lat_mommsen1892.txt`, Prosper of Aquitaine's *Epitoma Chronicon* in Mommsen's Latin (1892), entry numbers his. Raw OCR, readable, with letter errors; no wording below depends on a doubtful letter.
- **CM2**: `chronica-minora-2_marcellinus-victor-tunnunensis-biclaro-isidore-mgh-aa-11-lat_mommsen1894.txt`, Hydatius, Cassiodorus, Victor of Tunnuna in Latin. Raw OCR.
- **JOR**: `jordanes_origin-and-deeds-of-the-goths_mierow1908.txt`, Jordanes, *Getica*, in Mierow's English (1908). Clean transcription.
- **LP**: `liber-pontificalis_book-of-the-popes-v1_loomis1916.txt`, the Book of the Popes in Loomis's English (1916). Clean English OCR.
- **GEN**: `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`, Gennadius, *Lives of Illustrious Men*, in Richardson's English.
- **ZAC**: `zachariah-rhetor_chronicle_hamiltonbrooks1899.txt`, the Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene, in Hamilton and Brooks's English (1899).
- **NES**: `nestorius_bazaar-of-heracleides_driver-hodgson1925.txt`, Nestorius, *Bazaar of Heracleides*, Syriac in Driver and Hodgson's English (1925).

## Where sources disagree or come late

- D1 **Attila, 452: why he turned back.** Prosper (contemporary, close to Leo by Gennadius's report) credits God's help and the king's pleasure at the bishop's presence (E23). Jordanes, a century later, citing Priscus, says Attila's own men feared Alaric's fate, and Attila still demanded Honoria (E23a). Hydatius, writing in Spain in the same years, names famine, disease and Marcian's army, and no Leo (E23b). Feltoe adds rumours of invasion at home, "it is thought" (E23c). Leo himself says nothing of it (E23c).
- D2 **Attila: where.** Prosper gives no place. Jordanes: the ford of the Mincius in the Ambuleian district. Feltoe: Lake Benacus. The card notes Benacus is Feltoe's.
- D3 **Attila: who went.** Prosper: Leo with Avienus and Trygetius, sent as envoys by the emperor and senate's counsel. Cassiodorus: sent by Valentinian. Jordanes: "Pope Leo himself came". Liber Pontificalis: Leo alone "delivered all Italy".
- D4 **The Tome's origin.** Gennadius, about forty years later: Leo's letters against Eutyches thought dictated by Prosper (E7e). The Syriac chronicle: Leo wrote it after Theodoret won him over with a gift (I2), an order of events that conflicts with the letter's own date of June 449 (Theodoret was deposed at Ephesus in August 449, per the `theodoret-of-cyrrhus` story and ZAC 2210–2213).
- D5 **Manichaeans: the year.** Prosper sets it under 443 (E4); Feltoe under 444 (E4a).
- D6 **Birthplace.** Liber Pontificalis: a Tuscan; Prosper: Rome his *patria*; Feltoe: uncertain (S2–S2b). Card only.
- D7 **Number in the succession.** Prosper 43rd; Feltoe 47th (E2). Card only.
- D8 **"Robbers": when.** The phrase is in a letter of 20 July 451 (E12), two years after the meeting; Feltoe says "afterwards" (E12b). Not "at once".
- D9 **Ephesus 449 is told here only by its opponents** (Leo, Prosper, Feltoe). The Syriac acts are held but set aside (below); the `eutyches` and `dioscorus` stories carry them.
