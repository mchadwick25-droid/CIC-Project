**B1. Q3's rendering misleads on one hearing (outline fault).** Fixed where it starts, in the outline, then in the story. The rendering is newly authored from Augustine's Latin (AUGL 20553–20556), under the project's rendering rule, and checked against Cunningham's English (AUG 32546–32550). Q3 in `Outline.md` and in the story now reads, word for word:

> "The Greeks above all found fault with the reading, and fanned the charge that it was false. Such an uproar broke out in the congregation that the bishop was forced to demand the Jews' testimony. The town was Oea."

Clause by clause:
- *maxime Graecis arguentibus* → "The Greeks above all found fault with the reading". *Arguere* is to charge with error or censure; "the reading" supplies the object the Latin leaves implied, as Cunningham does ("correcting what had been read"). The reviewer's proposal had "denounced"; "found fault with" is the closer sense of *arguere*, and the force of the outcry is carried by the next clause and by "uproar".
- *et inflammantibus calumniam falsitatis* → "and fanned the charge that it was false". *Inflammare* is to set alight or fan; "stirred up" was weaker than the image.
- *factus est tantus tumultus in plebe* → "Such an uproar broke out in the congregation".
- *ut cogeretur episcopus … Iudaeorum testimonium flagitare* → "that the bishop was forced to demand the Jews' testimony". *Flagitare* is to demand or press for; the earlier "ask the Jews to bear witness" softened it.
- *Oea quippe ciuitas erat* → "The town was Oea."

The Greeks' outcry now comes first, as the cause it is in the Latin's ablative absolute, and the uproar and the summons follow it. Nothing is added beyond the object "the reading". Each sentence holds one thought; the longest is 18 words. Q3 is now 39 words, not 43.
