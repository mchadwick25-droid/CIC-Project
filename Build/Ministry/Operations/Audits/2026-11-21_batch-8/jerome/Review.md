## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Jerome, a Latin scholar and monk of the late 300s and early 400s, who learned Hebrew as a young man and spent his later life at Bethlehem.
- **What happened:** At Rome he corrected the Latin Gospels against the Greek for Damasus. Rome turned on him and he sailed east, by his own account at a time he chose. At Bethlehem, with Paula, he put the Old Testament into Latin from the Hebrew. Rufinus and Augustine attacked the new version, and a congregation at Oea rioted over one word in Jonah. He and Augustine ended as allies, and he died in 420.
- **Why the connection matters:** His Latin Bible made from the Hebrew was resisted in his lifetime but became the Western church's standard text. Trent declared the "old and Vulgate edition" authentic in 1546, and that Bible was built largely on his work.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

## 5. Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Q3's rendering misleads on one hearing (outline fault).** In Augustine's Latin (AUGL 20553–20556), the Greeks' outcry is the cause of the uproar and comes before the summons: "factus est tantus tumultus in plebe maxime Graecis arguentibus et inflammantibus calumniam falsitatis, ut cogeretur episcopus … Iudaeorum testimonium flagitare". Cunningham's English (AUG 32546–32550) keeps that order: "especially among the Greeks, correcting what had been read, and denouncing the translation as false, that the bishop was compelled to ask the testimony of the Jewish residents". The outline's rendering puts "The Greeks above all denounced it" after "the bishop was forced to ask the Jews to bear witness". Heard once, the "it" lands on the Jews' witness, and the Greeks seem to act after the summons. That misreads both who was denounced and the order of events, inside Augustine's own quoted words. The writer quoted the outline exactly, so the fix starts in the outline (Kit Part 4, "fix it where it starts").
- **Change in the outline, Q3** (the rendering goes to its Opus renderer under the project's rendering rule; proposed): "The Greeks above all denounced the reading, and stirred up the charge that it was false. Such an uproar broke out in the congregation that the bishop was forced to ask the Jews to bear witness. This was at Oea." Every Latin clause is kept, nothing is added ("the reading" makes plain the object of "arguentibus", which Cunningham gives as "correcting what had been read"), and the order now follows cause and effect.
- **Then in the story:** replace the Q3 span with the outline's new wording, word for word.
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** "The Greeks above all denounced it", placed after the bishop's summons → the Greeks denounced the new reading first, and their outcry is what forced the bishop to call the Jews (AUGL 20553–20556; AUG 32546–32550).

**B2. Part of the approved heart is dropped: why Augustine held back.** The approved heart ("Also told", Outline line 8) and beat 4 read: "unless he marked where he differed from the old Greek, which Augustine held in the highest esteem". The story stops at "the old Greek." That clause is the only place the story says why the Greek mattered to its defenders. The outline's connection (point 2) requires it: going back to the Hebrew meant "setting aside a Greek text many held" to have authority. Without it, Augustine's plea and his later refusal to have the Hebrew read in church come with no reason. Source: AUG 25155–25157, "that of the LXX., whose authority is worthy of highest esteem".
- **Change:** "unless he marked where he differed from the old Greek." → "unless he marked where he differed from the old Greek, which he held in the highest esteem." (+7 words; paid for by M7 and M8 below.)
- **Do not say (add to the outline):** "Augustine begged him … unless he marked where he differed from the old Greek", with the clause dropped → keep "which he held in the highest esteem" (E16, AUG 25155–25157). The clause carries the stake.
