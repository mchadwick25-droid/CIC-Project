## Conflicts with the census entry's own fields, and claims not found

- `why` says he put the Old Testament into Latin from the Hebrew "in the work later called the Vulgate". The card agrees that the Vulgate is built largely on his work, and adds Fremantle's note that the Psalter the churches sang was his revision from the Greek, and that the Trent decree does not name him. This qualifies the wording and does not contradict it. Flagged.
- `legacy` (Trent declared the Vulgate authentic) agrees with the decree's "held as authentic". The card does not carry the Cyprian and Tertullian line.
- `floorNote` (a combative defender of the Nicene faith): no held line is cited for it in the Sheet or Outline, so it is not on the card. Not contradicted. Not found.
- `voices` and `relationsSummary` (Rufinus a friend from student days; the break over Origen; he wrote to Augustine for years; he attacked Jovinian): the card has the friendship and the break (JR 46247-46262) and the letters to Augustine. His attack on Jovinian is named only among the controversies left out of the story, with no line, because the Sheet cites none.
- `dates` "374-420" and `longDescription` (not in scope): the long description says born about 345 (Fremantle's date); the card gives all four dates. Noted only.
- The brief calls Augustine a bishop at his first plea (heading dates it 394 or 395). The headings of Letters 28, 71 and 82 all address Jerome as "fellow-presbyter", early and late, so they do not settle Augustine's own rank at that date. The card gives the letter's date and says nothing of Augustine's rank. Noted for the story's owner; the brief is unchanged.
- Not found, so left off the card: Marcella (the Outline's link says "card" and the Sheet cites no line); Ildefonsus of Toledo continuing the list (the Sheet cites only the live story); Jerome's teachers Didymus, Gregory of Nazianzus and Apollinaris as a card claim (their lines are lifted into other stories; Rufinus-answer JR 51621 names Apollinarius, Didymus and Gregory but the card does not rest a claim on it).
