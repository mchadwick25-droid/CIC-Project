# Jerome: researcher's reply

**Essence.** Jerome learned Hebrew so that he could put the Old Testament into Latin from the Hebrew itself, not from the Greek the churches read; before that, at Rome, Damasus had set him to correct the Latin Gospels against the Greek (Documented, his own words; the 391 to 404 dates are the editor's). His Bible was fought over in his lifetime, then became the West's standard: Isidore praised it, and Trent declared the "old and Vulgate edition" authentic in 1546 (Documented; that this is mainly Jerome's work is the editor's, Widely Accepted).

**Also told.** The Roman women he taught (Documented, his account). Rufinus's and Augustine's quarrels with him, and the later reconciliation with Augustine (Documented, both sides). Rome turning on him (his account).

**Shape and length.** Teacher or thinker. About 615 words (4:05); about 215 carried by quotes, glosses and witnesses before any beat. Opening: his own words on learning Hebrew.

**Connection.** A Latin Old Testament made from the Hebrew, first resisted by the churches, then adopted.

**Quotes** (none on the tree; none lifted).
- "I began to learn my letters all over again, and worked at sounding out words that were harsh and throaty." / "…from this seed of learning, sown in bitterness, I now gather sweet fruit."
- Rufinus: "Who but you would have laid hands on God's gift, and on the inheritance of the apostles?"
- Augustine, rendered from his Latin: "Such an uproar broke out in the congregation…"

**Sources differ.** Paula: Jerome says she studied with him and learned Hebrew; Palladius says he held her back out of jealousy. Oea: Augustine and Jerome differ on the Jonah word and the local Jews. Leaving Rome: Jerome says he left when he chose; Rufinus hints at more. His birth year and the day of his death.

**Gaps.** The Latin of his letters, his Bible prefaces and Rufinus's Apology is not held: two quotes are rendered from Fremantle's English. One Isidore line is only in a damaged scan: not used. The "Old Testament from the Hebrew" line in On Illustrious Men is in some manuscripts only. For Mark: the Paula "fresh questions" detail may matter to her later rebuild.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught a line nearly read as about the Hebrew translation (it is his earlier Latin version from the Greek); that the Damasus commission was the Greek Gospels only; and that the Trent decree never names Jerome.
