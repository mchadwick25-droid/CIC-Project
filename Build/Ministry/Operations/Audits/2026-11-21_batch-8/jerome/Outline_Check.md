# Outline check: Jerome

Checker, batch 8 (2026-11-21). Written before reading `Outline.md`.

## The checker's own heart (before reading the outline)

**The essence.** Jerome learned Hebrew, against the habit of a Latin church that read the Old Testament at third hand (Hebrew to Greek to Latin), and at Bethlehem turned it into Latin from the Hebrew itself; before that, at Rome, Damasus had set him to correct the old Latin Gospels against the Greek. That Bible was attacked in his lifetime (Rufinus, Augustine's fears, the uproar at Oea), and centuries later became the Latin West's standard text, the Vulgate, declared authentic at Trent in 1546 (the decree does not name him). He is on the tree as the man who gave the West its Bible from the original languages, and as the hand through which the Roman women's circle is known. (Documented for his own words and the decree; Widely Accepted that the Vulgate is chiefly his.)

**Also told.** His letters and the Roman women he taught (Paula, Eustochium, Marcella's circle), Paula's foundations at Bethlehem, and the dispute over Paula's study (Jerome: she learned Hebrew; Palladius: he held her back); Rome turning on him after Blaesilla's death and his leaving in 385, his account as his; the break with Rufinus over Origen and Rufinus's attack on the translation; the correspondence with Augustine (from "do not translate from the Hebrew" through Oea to the reconciliation of 418); the old scholar at his books (Sulpicius); his death at Bethlehem in 420.

## BLOCKING

**B1. "His translations were made for and dedicated to them" (Also told, the Roman women) and "dedicating his prefaces to Paula and her daughter" (beat 4) say more than the record.** E12 supports one preface, Samuel and Kings, addressed to Paula and Eustochium (JW 46798–46805). The editor's headings in the same file show others went elsewhere: Genesis to Desiderius (JW 46750), Chronicles to Chromatius of Aquileia (JW 46956), Ezra and Nehemiah to Domnio and Rogatianus (JW 46966), Job with no dedication. Isaiah, Daniel and the Minor Prophets went to Paula and Eustochium (JW 47155, 47181, 47280). The editor says the version was made "at the request of private friends" (JW 46735–46741), not for the women. Correction to Also told: "Many of his Bible prefaces were addressed to Paula and her daughter (E12; JW-ed headings 46804, 47155, 47181, 47280; Documented)." Beat 4: "dedicating many of the prefaces to Paula and her daughter". Add to Do not say: "he made his Bible for Paula" / "all his prefaces were for Paula and Eustochium" → "many of the prefaces were addressed to them".

**B2. Palladius on Paula: held texts differ, and the sheet's Library sweep missed one.** The outline credits only *Lausiac History* 41.2 (PAL 475), "he hindered her by his jealousy". Three held points are not on the sheet. (a) Clarke prints that passage in square brackets, and his note 275 (PAL 1189) says such passages are "translations of Butler's Greek text, which is here a critical reconstruction". (b) Chapter 36 of the same book gives a second charge about Paula, told as Posidonius's prophecy: "The noble Paula, who looks after him, will die first and be freed from his bad temper" (PAL 435). (c) The Syriac version, held as `palladius_paradise-v1-syriac_budge1907.txt` and listed in the tradition's corpus-map bucket (`cic/corpus-map/hieronymian-ascetic-literary.yaml`, row at line 506), makes it harder: Paula "departed from this world before her time in order that she might escape from his envy" (Budge 1233; editor's summary at 83). The tradition's own `sourcing` field, which the researcher read, flags this same split. The sheet says every file in the bucket was opened, but this one is in neither its Used nor its Set-aside list. Correction: (1) add E11a to the sheet with PAL 435, PAL 1189 and Budge 1233, and add the Syriac file to the sweep's Used list. (2) Extend D3: Palladius's Greek says Jerome held her back by jealousy (41.2, a reconstructed text) and reports a prophecy that she would die first and be freed of his temper (36); the Syriac says she died before her time to escape his envy. Each is credited; Contested. (3) The story keeps the 41.2 charge as the outline has it, credited to Palladius. The card credits the other two forms. (4) Add to Do not say: "Paula died to escape Jerome" / "Palladius says Jerome hastened her death" → card only, credited to the Syriac version and to Posidonius as Palladius reports him.

**B3. The year of death: a held source differs, and the outline treats 420 as settled.** Beat 7 and D2 give 420, from Prosper (PRO 53897–53902) and Fremantle (JW-ed 3447). Schaff's note in the City of God volume, which the outline uses for I2, says "He died at Bethlehem, 419" (CIV 36733–36738, note 1217). Correction: add to D2: "Schaff (CIV note 1217): 419." The card gives Prosper's 420 (the ancient witness, with Fremantle) and Schaff's 419, each credited. The story may keep "in 420", credited in Who tells it to Prosper's chronicle, as now. Change beat 7's confidence to "Documented (Prosper); one editor gives 419 (card)."

## MINOR

**M1. Beat 6 steps back in time.** Beat 5 is dated 400. Beat 6 then opens with Augustine's first letter of 394 or 395 (E16). Correction: move Augustine's first plea to the end of beat 4, after the Hebrew work begins (about 391), as one clause. Beat 6 then opens with Oea in 403, so the beats run 391, 394/5, 400, 403 to 418.

**M2. Augustine's first plea drops its qualifier.** Ep. 28.2 (AUG 25145–25157) asks Jerome not to translate the canonical books "unless you follow the method in which you have translated Job, viz. with the addition of notes", showing where his version differs from the Septuagint. Beat 6 (moved per M1) and Also told: "Augustine begged him not to translate the Scriptures afresh unless he marked where he differed from the old Greek, which Augustine held in the highest esteem." Add to Do not say: "Augustine told him not to translate from the Hebrew at all" → keep the "unless" (E16).

**M3. The "old man" line, as placed, reads as Jerome's answer to Oea.** Ep. 72.3 (AUG 32660–32663) answers Augustine's earlier letter, which had reached Italy before it reached Jerome (AUG 32699–32700). Correction to beat 6: "Over an earlier letter of Augustine's that had gone round Italy before it reached him, he told Augustine to stop troubling an old man in his monastery cell." Or cut the line to the card.

**M4. Rufinus's charge softened.** Beat 5 has "a new translation made with the help of Jews". Rufinus wrote "under the influence of the Jews" (JR 50263–50264), which is an accusation, not a neutral fact. Correction: "by a new translation made, he charged, under the influence of the Jews".

**M5. The Septuagint gloss says more than its source.** "The one the apostles' churches read" is, in the held texts, Rufinus's and Augustine's claim: "approved by the Apostles" (JR 50273); "the seal of approbation was given by the apostles" (AUG 35741–35742). Correction: "the old Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures, which the Greek churches read and from which the Latin churches' version was made" (CIV 36726–36730). The apostles' approval is told only as Rufinus's and Augustine's view.

**M6. Ep. 195 compressed.** Beat 6 has "all the world knew Augustine as the restorer of the ancient faith". The letter says "You are renowned throughout the whole world; Catholics revere and look up to you as the restorer of the ancient faith" (AUG 54052–54055). Correction: "that Augustine was known throughout the world, and that Catholics revered him as the restorer of the ancient faith".

**M7. "Late in life Augustine" (What came 2) has no held support.** The sheet notes the City of God is "not dated in the file". Correction: "In his City of God, Augustine called him..."; drop "late in life".

**M8. Q3's last sentence misleads when heard once.** "For it was the town of Oea" sounds like a reason. *Oea quippe ciuitas erat* is an aside: "the town, you see, was Oea." The checker's own rendering, made before comparing: "Such an uproar broke out among the people, the Greeks above all accusing it and fanning the charge of falsehood, that the bishop was forced (the town was Oea) to demand the testimony of the Jews." The outline's rendering keeps every clause and adds nothing; only its last sentence needs a change. Correction: "This was at Oea." Q1a, Q1b and Q2 match the checker's own renderings of Fremantle in sense, word for word.

**M9. Words to explain: "the Greeks" in Q3, and Oea's place.** A listener may not know who "the Greeks" are in an African town. The held files never place it. Mark it Inferential-Thin in Setting, or say "a town where one of Augustine's fellow bishops served".
