# Gregory Palamas: researcher's reply

**Essence.** A monk of Athos who defended the hesychasts, the monks of stillness, when Barlaam mocked them as "navel-souls" (Kantakouzenos's account, his ally; Documented). He taught that God cannot be shared in his essence but gives himself in his uncreated energy (Documented, his own words; contested by his opponents).

**Also told.** Held for years in the civil war (Documented; why is Contested). The councils of 1341, 1347 and 1351 upheld him (Documented; the winning side's records). Gregoras's charge of "many gods and no god" (the charge Documented, its truth Contested). The Synodikon and his feast (the 1857 editor's account); the Philokalia (Documented).

**Shape and length.** Teacher or thinker. About 4:05, about 615 words; quotes, glosses and credits about 256. Opening: Barlaam posing as a pupil (Kantakouzenos).

**Connection.** His answer to how a person at prayer meets God became the church's teaching through three councils, read aloud each Lent.

**Quotes** (none lifted, none on the tree).
- "Those who call them navel-souls are, to begin with, plainly slandering the men they accuse. For which of them ever says that the soul is in the navel?"
- "So when you hear the Fathers say God's essence cannot be shared, understand the essence that does not go out and is not shown. When they say again it can be shared, understand the going-out that belongs to God by nature, his showing and his energy. Holding to both in this way, you will agree with the Fathers."
- "The heavenly to the heavenly."
- Gregoras (short phrase): "in labor with the teachings of many gods, and at the same time of no god".

**Sources differ.** Why he was held (the patriarch, Kantakouzenos and Gregoras each say). Barlaam charged "two gods" first; both sides used "many gods".

**Gaps.** No early year of birth or death. Barlaam's own writings and Palamas's captivity letters are not held. The Symeon link is thin: card only unless Mark rules otherwise.

**Four-fault check.** Run. Caught an unsupported "emperor from 1347", years resting on an editor's margin, a Library-header date, force cut from 1351, and "excommunicated".
