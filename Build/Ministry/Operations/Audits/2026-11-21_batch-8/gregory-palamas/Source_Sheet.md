# Source sheet: Gregory Palamas

Researcher's sheet for batch 8 (2026-11-21). The job (Mark's, 2026-11-21): the monk of Athos who defended the hesychasts' silent prayer when the scholar Barlaam mocked it, taught that God is unknowable in his essence yet truly given in his energies, was imprisoned for it, and was upheld by councils of 1341, 1347 and 1351; the charge of teaching many gods is told and credited to Gregoras. Shape: Teacher or thinker. New entry and card. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

**Conventions.** Line numbers are each file's own. Greek is given as the OCR prints it, with obvious OCR letters (χ for κ, ϑ for θ, Cyrillic х/а) left as found. "[…]" marks a cut.

**Language.** Palamas and every early witness wrote in Greek. No English translation of any of them is held. Every rendering in the outline is mine, from the Greek, checked against Migne's Latin where Migne prints the same passage.

**Witness kinds.**
- OWN = Palamas: the Triads (nine treatises, written 1338-41 by the census sources note; in extract in PHK2 and as De Hesychastis in PG150); the 150 Chapters (PHK2, PG150).
- CONTEMP, synodal = the tome of July 1341 (MM 9665ff.; PG151 14301ff.); the tome of 1351, which recites those of 1341 and 1347 (PG151 15418ff.). The winning side's records (CEN sources note).
- CONTEMP, hostile = Patriarch John Kalekas, letter of November 1344 (MM 11280-11460). Nikephoros Gregoras, historian, condemned in 1351, writing his history through the 1350s (GRE2, GRE3).
- CONTEMP or soon after, partisan = John Kantakouzenos, emperor 1347-1354, Palamas's ally, writing his own history later (KAN1-3). Philotheos Kokkinos, patriarch, his friend, in a speech of praise written after his death (PHIL; it gives his age at death, 8243).
- LATER = the 1857 editor (PHIL-ed); the Philokalia's compilers (1782, per the PHK1 Library header); Paisius Velichkovsky (OPT, 1849 print).
- Not held: Palamas's full Triads in a critical text; his letters from captivity (CEN documentedStories); Barlaam's letters in a located text (PG151 lists his opuscula, the body not found, corpus-map row); Russell's and Meyendorff's books (CEN sources). No held early witness gives his year of birth or death.

## Setting

S1. **Family.** Philotheos: his parents were well born, from the east, and settled in Constantinople, where he was born their first child: "τὴν δὲ μεγίστην ταυτηνὲ χαὶ βασιλεύουσαν, τὴν τοῦ θαυμαστοῦ Κωνσταντίνα φημὶ πόλιν σχόντες εἰς μετοιχίαν, ἐνταῦθα χαὶ παίδων πατέρες […] ὧν ὁ θαυμαστὸς Γρηγόριος πρῶτος χατὰ γένεσιν" (PHIL 1411-1417). Kantakouzenos: "ἐξ ᾿Ασίας ὢν τὸ γένος καὶ βασιλείοις αὐλαῖς ἐντεθραμμένος" (from Asia by descent and raised in the imperial palace; KAN1 32724-32725). Witness: Philotheos, partisan, after his death; Kantakouzenos, partisan, later. Confidence: Documented as their accounts. No birth year in any held early witness.

S2. **Leaving for Athos.** Kantakouzenos: with his two brothers, "ἐπὶ πάνυ παιδικῇ τῇ ἡλικίᾳ", at a very young age, he left property, parents and the emperor's honors to follow Christ alone, and went to the holy mountain (KAN1 32725-32734). Philotheos: he took his two brothers with him, "δύο δὲ ἦσαν οὗτοι", and they left Constantinople "φθινούσης ὁπώρας ἤδη", in late autumn, for Athos (PHIL 2140-2144). Confidence: Documented as their accounts; his age is not given as a number (the card's "past his boyhood" for Philotheos was not found and is not used).

S3. **The Lavra of Athanasios.** Philotheos: "εἰς τὴν μεγίστην μεταθαίνει Λαύραν ᾿Αθανασίου τοῦ πάνυ φημὶ" (he moved to the greatest Lavra, that of the famous Athanasios; PHIL 2572-2573). Later, after raids drove him off, he came back "πρὸς τῇ μεγίστη Λαύρᾳ τοῦ θαυμαστοῦ καὶ πάλιν ᾿Αθανασίου", and set his cell of quiet outside it, at the house of Saint Sabbas, coming into the Lavra only for the liturgy (PHIL 3130-3145). Philotheos also has him at "τὸν Ἄθω χαὶ τὴν ἱερὰν Λαύραν διατρίβων ἐν ἡσυχίᾳ" when he heard of Barlaam (PHIL 4006-4007). Confidence: Documented (Philotheos). This is the link to `athanasios-the-athonite`, whose live story tells the founding of the Lavra (CEN).

S4. **Thessalonica.** The new patriarch Isidore consecrated "Γρηγόριον τὸν Παλαμᾶν Θεσσαλονίκης" archbishop; the city's governors would neither obey the emperor's letters nor receive him (KAN3 6209-6221). Gregoras: he got into Thessalonica "μετὰ πολλοὺς τινας δρόμους βίᾳ καὶ μόλις", after many journeys, by force and barely (GRE3 4380-4381). Confidence: Documented. No year in the lines held (KAN3's page margin is not a source).

S5. **Death and age.** Philotheos: "τρία μὲν ἔτη καὶ ἑξήκοντα τὰ πάντα συγγεγονυῖαν τῷ συμφύτῳ σαρκίῳ, δυοκαίδεκα δ᾽ ἐκ τούτων αὐτῶν κατὰ τὴν τῆς Ἐκκλησίας θαυμαστὴν προστασίαν" (his soul lived sixty-three years in all with its body, twelve of them in his leadership of the church; PHIL 8243-8253). The 1857 editor: the feast was set "ἐν ἔτει σωτηρίῳ ͵ατξη', ἓξ δηλονότι ἔτεσιν ὕστερον τῆς τιμίας αὐτοῦ ἀναλύσεως" (1368, six years after his death; PHIL-ed 510-514). The editor's figure points to 1362; twelve years from 1347 would point to 1359. Neither is a held early witness's year. Confidence: age at death Documented (Philotheos); year Inferential-Thin.

S6. **The tradition.** CEN `hesychasm-and-the-palamite-synthesis`: name "Hesychasm & the Palamite Synthesis", `informalName` "The Hesychasts and Gregory Palamas", dates c. 1300-1400, region "Athos, Thessalonica". The spoken name contains his own name; the story can say "the hesychasts, the monks of stillness" and need not say the tradition's name. Its `people` list holds him (year 1338, voice 0, held file PHIL); its first `voices` line: "Gregory Palamas — Athonite monk and archbishop of Thessalonica, who gave the practice its theological defense".

## Events

### Barlaam and the mockery

E1. **Who Barlaam was** (Kantakouzenos). "μοναχὸς γάρ τις ὁρμώμενος ἐκ Καλαβρίας τοῖς Λατίνων ἤθεσι καὶ νόμοις ἐντραφεὶς, ὄνομα Βαρλαὰμ, ἄλλως δὲ νοῆσαί τε ὀξὺς […] καὶ τὰ Εὐκλείδου καὶ Ἀριστοτέλους καὶ Πλάτωνος ἐκμελετήσας" (a monk from Calabria raised in Latin ways and laws, named Barlaam, sharp of mind, who had studied Euclid, Aristotle and Plato; KAN1 32600-32605). He enjoyed the emperor's favor and the grand domestic's (Kantakouzenos's own) care (KAN1 32614-32617). Confidence: Documented (KAN, partisan).

E2. **The mockery** (Kantakouzenos). Barlaam went, as if wanting to learn, to one of the hesychasts, a man Kantakouzenos calls unlearned, and asked him to teach the way of stillness (KAN1 32621-32661). The monk told him how those who pray without distraction feel an unspeakable joy "καὶ φῶς ὁρῶσι τοῖς σωματικοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς ἀστράπτον περὶ αὐτούς" (and see with their bodily eyes a light flashing around them; KAN1 32665-32668). Hearing of light seen with bodily eyes, Barlaam "κοινήν ἐποιεῖτο τῶν ἡσυχαζόντων καταδρομήν, ἀπατεῶνας καλῶν καὶ ψευδομένους καὶ Μασσαλιανοὺς καὶ Ὀμφαλοψύχους" (made a general attack on the hesychasts, calling them deceivers, liars, Messalians and navel-souls; KAN1 32670-32673; Latin 32700-32701 "impostores, mendaces, Massalianos et Omphalopsychos"). He wrote it down too, "ἵνα ἐς ἀεὶ ἀνάγραπτος ἡ ὕβρις εἴη" (so the insult would stand written for ever; KAN1 32717-32719). It happened at Thessalonica (KAN1 32719-32720). Many hesychasts there took it hard, "καὶ μάλιστα Γρηγόριος ὁ Παλαμᾶς" (KAN1 32721-32723). Philotheos tells the same approach "ἐν προσχήματι ταπεινώσεως" (under a show of humility; PHIL 3966-3986), after which Barlaam called the monks' teachers heretics. Confidence: Documented as Kantakouzenos's and Philotheos's accounts, both partisans of Palamas.

E3. **Barlaam's own words** (quoted in the 1341 tome and again in Kalekas's letter). On the prayer: Barlaam wrote against "the guide of this teaching" for trying "διὰ τῶν εἰσπνοῶν" (by breathings-in) to overturn the Christians' mysteries; "ἐμβρόντητε καὶ μιαρὲ, τίς πώποτε ἐκείνων νῆψιν καὶ φυλακὴν καρδίας καὶ προσοχὴν, τοιαύτην τερατείαν, οἵαν σὺ παραδίδως, ὠνόμασεν;" (you thunderstruck, foul man, which of them ever called watchfulness, guarding of the heart and attention such a monstrosity as you hand on?) and mocked the rule to pray always "κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ, υἱὲ τοῦ θεοῦ, ἐλέησόν με" (MM 10106-10116, the tome of July 1341; the same at MM 11379-11386, Kalekas's letter, damaged). On the light of the Transfiguration: it "οὐκ ἀπρόσιτον ἦν, οὐδὲ κατὰ ἀλήθειαν ὑπῆρχε φῶς θεότητος", but lower than our own thinking (MM 11351-11356, Kalekas). Confidence: Documented as Barlaam's words in his opponents' records. For the card; the story uses Kantakouzenos's report (E2) and Palamas's answer (Q1).

### Palamas answers: the Triads

E4. **Called from the mountain.** The monks at Thessalonica, with Isidore leading, wrote begging him to come from his retreat and the Mountain (PHIL 4060-4079); he came, unwillingly, judging there is a time for quiet and a time for war (PHIL 4080-4086). He first tried friends and then talk face to face, urging Barlaam to keep to outside philosophy and leave prayer and stillness alone (PHIL 4108-4165). Confidence: Documented (Philotheos).

E5. **His reply to Barlaam, as Philotheos quotes it.** "αἱροῦμαι δὲ μᾶλλον συνυβρίζεσθαι ταῦτα πάσχουσι τοῖς διὰ τὸν Θεὸν πτωχοῖς χαὶ εὐτελέσιν ἀδελφοῖς, ἢ πρόσκαιρον ἔχειν εὐφημίας χαὶ δόξης ἀνθρωπίνης ἀπόλαυσιν" (I choose rather to be insulted along with the brothers who are poor and lowly for God's sake and suffer this, than to enjoy for a time good report and human glory; PHIL 4246-4251). Confidence: Documented as Philotheos's quotation. For the card.

E6. **Nine treatises.** "Ἐννέα μὲν οὖν οἱ πάντες […] συντάττονται λόγοι, κατάγε τῶν ψευδῶν λόγων […] τοῦ Βαρλαάμ […] εἰς τρεῖς διηρημένοι τριάδας" (nine treatises in all, against Barlaam's false writings, divided into three triads; PHIL 4252-4258). Confidence: Documented (Philotheos). CEN calls them the Triads.

E7. **What the Triads say: the navel charge.** Palamas, in the treatise "Ὑπὲρ τῶν ἱερῶς ἡσυχαζόντων" (running head PHK2 41673, 41949): "οἱ δ᾽ ὀμφαλοψύχους τούτους ὀνομάζοντες, πρὸς τῷ συκοφαντεῖν σαφῶς, ὧν κατηγοροῦσι· τίς γάρ ποτε τούτων ἐπ᾽ ὀμφαλοῦ λέγει τὴν ψυχήν;" (PHK2 42167-42170). Migne's Latin of the same passage confirms it: "qui vero eos vocant omphalopsychos; non solum evidenter calumniantur quos accusant (quis enim horum in umbilico reponit animam?)" (PG150 64679-64683). In the same treatise: Elijah set his head on his knees in prayer and ended the long drought (PHK2 42147-42151); the body is not evil, "μηδὲ τὸ σῶμα πονηρόν" (PHK2 41676-41678). Confidence: Documented, his own words. **Quote 1.**

### The teaching: essence and energy

E8. **Three in God** (150 Chapters, ch. 75). "Τριῶν ὄντων τοῦ Θεοῦ, οὐσίας, ἐνεργείας, Τριάδος ὑποστάσεων θείων" those found worthy to be one with God are not united by essence, for "κατ᾽ οὐσίαν εἶναι τὸν θεὸν ἀμέθεκτον"; "λείπεται κατ᾽ ἐνέργειαν ἑνοῦσθαι", and the one joined to God is "τὴν ἄκτιστον τοῦ πνεύματος ἐνέργειαν […] ἀλλ᾽ οὐ τὴν οὐσίαν τοῦ Θεοῦ" (PHK2 44974-44991; PG150 66797-66803 and 66839, with Latin 66829-66840 "Cum tria sint in Deo, essentia, operatio, et sacrosanctae Trinitatis personae"). Plain sense: in his essence God cannot be shared; people are united with him through his uncreated energy. Confidence: Documented, his own words.

E9. **How to read the Fathers** (150 Chapters, end of ch. 107 in Migne's numbering). "Καὶ τοίνυν ὅταν ἀκούσῃς τῶν Πατέρων ἀμέθεκτον λεγόντων τὴν οὐσίαν τοῦ Θεοῦ, ἐκείνην μοι νόει τὴν ἀνεκφοίτητόν τε καὶ ἀνέκφαντον· ὅταν δ᾽ αὖθις μεθεκτὴν, νόει μοι τὴν φυσικῶς προσοῦσαν τῷ Θεῷ πρόοδον, καὶ τὴν ἔκφανσιν, καὶ τὴν ἐνέργειαν· καὶ οὕτως στέργων ἀμφότερα, ὁμόλογος ἔσῃ τοῖς Πατράσι." (PG150 67527-67533; PHK2 45852-45860, the same words). Two held prints agree. Confidence: Documented, his own words. **Quote 2.**

E10. **The Athonite monks' tome.** "HAGIORITICUS TOMUS DE QUIETISTIS" (PG150 68790-68793); CEN edge: the hesychasts' case "went out from Athos, as the Tome of the Holy Mountain". For the card.

### The council of 1341

E11. **What the synod heard** (tome of July 1341). Heading: "XCVI. (6849—1341) iulio" (MM 9665-9668). Barlaam accused the monks of saying God's very essence can be shared; when they answered "οὐ τὴν οὐσίαν, ἀλλὰ τὴν ἄκτιστον καὶ θεοποιὸν χάριν τοῦ πνεύματος", he "διθεΐας αὐτοῖς ἐντεῦθεν ἔγκλημα προστρίψασθαι ἐπεχείρησεν" (tried to lay on them the charge of believing in two gods), and came to the church accusing above all Palamas (MM 9708-9714). Palamas was called to make his defense (MM 9793-9797). Confidence: Documented, the synod's record. The first charge of more than one God was Barlaam's: two gods, in 1341.

E12. **What it decided.** The monks "ἀπεδείχθησαν ἀνώτεροι τῆς κατηγορίας αὐτοῦ"; Barlaam, condemned, asked pardon; if he truly repents, well; if not, he will be cut off from the Church (PG151 14790-14826, damaged two-column OCR; the same tome in MM from 9665). The tome's title: the senate and the judges were present, "προκαθημένου καὶ τοῦ θειοτάτου βασιλέως ἕως οὗ περιῆν" (with the emperor presiding while he lived; PG151 14304-14307). Kalekas, in 1344: Barlaam was told to say what he had against Palamas and instead raised dogmatic questions; canons were read forbidding public teaching without the office; his words on the light and the prayer were judged blasphemous (MM 11294-11398). Confidence: Documented. The emperor died soon after (KAN1 Latin 32619-32622, "paulo post […] vita excedit").

### The civil war and prison

E13. **The setting.** Gregoras: from the time the empress Anna turned against Kantakouzenos, she used the patriarch as her partner and adviser in all she did (GRE2 12644-12650; Latin 12669-12676). Confidence: Documented (Gregoras).

E14. **Three accounts of why he was held.**
- **Kalekas** (the patriarch, his opponent, November 1344): after the synod Palamas went on writing and speaking; summoned, "οὔτε ἦλθεν ὡς ἡμᾶς, καὶ ἀπέδρα εἰς τὴν Ἡράκλειαν" (he did not come, and ran off to Heraclea); brought back, asked to show his writings and give a confession, he did neither; so it was judged with the empress and the rulers "κρατηθῆναι τοῦτον καὶ κωλυθῆναι […] τοῦ μὴ τοῖς πολλοῖς ἀναμίγνυσθαι" (that he be held and kept from mixing with the many) until he gave proof that his mind was sound (MM 11416-11436, OCR damaged but legible). Confidence: Documented as Kalekas's account.
- **Kantakouzenos** (his ally): the patriarch envied Palamas, suspecting he wanted the patriarch's throne; he first told him to join in the censures and excommunication of Kantakouzenos; when Palamas openly refused, the patriarch "δυσσεβείας ἐδίωκε μεταβαλών" (changed tack and prosecuted him for impiety; KAN2 33660-33671), siding with Akindynos, who took up Barlaam's case, and held Palamas and those who thought as he did "ἐν δεσμωτηρίοις […] καὶ ἐκάκου ὅσα δυνατά" (in prisons, and harmed them all he could; KAN2 33671-33712). Elsewhere: he had openly opposed the war on the emperor from the start, "δι᾽ ἃ καὶ δεσμωτήριον οἰκεῖν κατεκρίθη ἄχρι τέλους ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς" (for which he was sentenced to live in prison from beginning to end; KAN3 1447-1451). Confidence: Documented as Kantakouzenos's account.
- **Gregoras** (his opponent): letters from the empress with the patriarch, and from her son the young emperor, to the monks of Athos said that no one should think Palamas was held "εἱρκταῖς καὶ φρουραῖς" (in prisons and under guard) for siding with Kantakouzenos, but for teaching "θεοτήτων ἀκτίστων […] ἀπειράκις ἀπείρων ἑσμὸν" (a swarm of infinitely many uncreated divinities), some higher and invisible, some lower and visible (GRE2 12652-12664; Greek cut at the left edge; Latin 12676-12684 confirms, "ne quis vinculis custodiisque ideo Palamam coerceri existimaret, quod is animum ad Cantacuzenum adiecisset"). Confidence: Documented as Gregoras's report of the letters.
Confidence that he was held: Documented (all three agree). Why: Contested.

E15. **Released, 1347.** The empress ordered Andronikos Asan and "τὸν Παλαμᾶν Γρηγόριον αὐτοῦ που κατακεκλεισμένους ἐν δεσμωτηρίοις ἔνδον βασιλείων" (shut up in prisons inside the palace) brought to her, and sent them to Kantakouzenos to treat for peace (KAN2 34246-34252; margin A.C. 1347). Kantakouzenos then "Παλαμᾶν δὲ ἐπήνει καὶ πολλὰς ὡμολόγει χάριτας, ὅτι πολλὴν ὑπὲρ αὐτοῦ δικαίου καὶ τῆς ἀληθείας κάκωσιν ὑπενεγκὼν, οὐδὲν ἀγεννὲς οὔτε ἔπραξεν, οὔτ᾽ ἐφθέγξατο ἄχρι τέλους" (praised Palamas and thanked him, because he had endured much suffering for justice and truth on his behalf and to the end had done or said nothing base; KAN2 34298-34304; Latin 34322-34324). Confidence: Documented (Kantakouzenos). Not the opening: the 2026-11 Who profile opened on this release.

### The council of 1347

E16. The bishops deposed the patriarch John in writing and set out a tome: Akindynos and his party think wrongly; "Παλαμᾶς δὲ καὶ ὅσοι τῶν ὁμοίων ἔχονται, ἀκόλουθα τοῖς θεοσόφοις θεολόγοις καὶ λέγουσι καὶ φρονοῦσιν" (Palamas and those like him speak and think in line with the wise theologians; KAN3 1383-1392). The 1351 tome recites it: the tome of 1347 carried "ὑπογραφαῖς ἀρχιερέων τριάκοντα" (thirty bishops' signatures) and cast out Akindynos and the former patriarch (PG151 15500-15509). Confidence: Documented. Then the archbishopric (S4).

### The council of 1351

E17. **The meeting** (Kantakouzenos). The synod met "ἐν βασιλείοις τοῖς τῶν Βλαχερνῶν λεγομένοις, καὶ βασιλέως τοῦ Καντακουζηνοῦ παρόντος", for four days; the debate was above all with Palamas of Thessalonica, against the followers of Akindynos, whose heads were the metropolitans of Ephesus and Ganos, "καὶ Γρηγορᾶς καὶ Δεξιὸς" (KAN3 10399-10410; Latin 10425-10431). The emperor had summoned "τὸν Θεσσαλονίκης Γρηγόριον τὸν Παλαμᾶν" by letter (KAN3 10296-10299). The tome of 1351 names among the opponents "Γρηγορᾶν τε καὶ Δεξιόν" (PG151 15509-15511). Confidence: Documented. (The card's "Kantakouzenos presiding" is not in these lines: KAN3 says "present".)

E18. **The meeting** (Gregoras, who was there). By common vote of emperor and patriarch it was confirmed that the essence may in a way be called divinity, but that the energy is properly uncreated divinity, quite different from the divine essence (GRE2 27457-27462, left edge cut). "Ταῦτα δρώντων τοίνυν καὶ ἀκουόντων ἡμῶν, ὦ δίκη καὶ ἀνοχὴ θεοῦ, κατὰ ποταμοὺς ἐχώρει τὰ δάκρυα, σιωπῇ κυπτόντων εἰς γῆν" (as we saw and heard these things, O justice and patience of God, the tears ran like rivers, as we bowed silently to the ground); it was far into the night, torches had been brought in, "ὅτε καὶ χεῖρας ἐπιβάλλειν ἤρξαντο μετ᾽ ὀργῆς βαθείας ἀναπηδήσαντες σύν γε τῷ πατριάρχῃ οἱ ἐπίσκοποι καθ᾽ ἡμῶν" (when the bishops with the patriarch, leaping up in deep anger, began to lay hands on us; GRE2 27536-27544). Confidence: Documented as Gregoras's eyewitness account, hostile.

### The charge of many gods

E19. **Gregoras's charge, in his own voice.** "Παλαμᾶν κύοντα μὲν ἔτ᾽ ἐν γαστρὶ μοχθηρὰ, καὶ ὠδίνοντα τὰ τῆς πολυθεΐας ἅμα καὶ ἀθεΐας δόγματα" (Palamas, still pregnant with wicked things, and in labor with the teachings of many gods and at once of no god; GRE3 528-531, Gregoras speaking, "ἔγωγε ἔφασκον", 528; Latin 551-554 "quum praegnans […] dogmata polytheismi simulque atheismi parturiret"). Also GRE2 23025-23035 (he divides the light, named energy, into "ἀπειράκις ἀπείρους θεότητας, ἀκτίστους", power, wisdom, life, will, truth, sleep and drunkenness; damaged, left edge cut; Latin 23062-23068 confirms); GRE2 28518-28523 (men made into numberless uncreated gods without being, "ταὐτὸν δ᾽ εἰπεῖν μηδένων", that is to say, none). Confidence: Documented as Gregoras's charge; that Palamas taught many gods: Contested (the synods of 1347 and 1351 declared him sound).

E20. **The charge thrown both ways.** Philotheos calls Barlaam "the leader" of "τῆς κακίστης τῶν ἀνενεργήτων τούτων, ἢ πολυθέων τε καὶ ἀθέων αἱρέσεως" (the worst heresy of these men who deny the energies, of many gods and of no god; PHIL 4025-4027). Palamas, as Gregoras quotes him: when they vote him guilty of many gods, he votes them guilty of one god, "οὐ γὰρ Ἰουδαίων ἢ Περσῶν εἰμὶ παῖς" (GRE2 24565-24572, damaged); Gregoras again: charged with many gods, he said he was neither a Jew nor an Ishmaelite, "ἵν᾽ ἕνα σέβῃ θεόν" (GRE3 4441-4443, clean). Letters from Cyprus against "τῶν ἀθέων καὶ πολυθέων Παλαμιτῶν" (MM 18975-18997). Confidence: Documented as each side's words. For the card (see the outline's Leave out).

### Death

E21. **His last words** (Philotheos). As he lay dying, he kept whispering, those present heard, the words "τὰ ἐπουράνια εἰς τὰ ἐπουράνια" (the heavenly to the heavenly), again and again "ὥσπερ ἐν περιόδῳ τινὶ καὶ στροφῇ κύκλου" (as if in a circling round), showing where he was going (PHIL 8219-8243; the phrase at 8235-8236, broken by the OCR across the line as "τὰ éns- ράνια εἰς τὰ ἐπουράνια", and again at 8243 "καὶ τὰ ἐπουράνια"). Then the age (S5). Philotheos adds a light that filled the room and his face at death (PHIL 8256-8263): a wonder, his voice only. Confidence: Documented as Philotheos's account. One print only; the OCR is clean apart from the broken word, which the repetition confirms. **Quote 3.**

## What came of him (impact, heaviest first)

I1. **The councils made his teaching the church's.** 1341 (E12), 1347 (E16), 1351 (E17-E18). CEN tradition `longDescription`: the hesychast view "became the standard of Orthodox theology". CEN `byzantine-church-palaiologan` (live text): the synods "made Gregory Palamas's theology the standard of Orthodoxy". Confidence: Documented (the tomes); "standard" is the tree's own sourced tradition text.

I2. **Read aloud every year.** The 1857 editor: the Synodikon read on the first Sunday of Lent, the Sunday of Orthodoxy, gives "eternal memory" to Gregory of Thessalonica, who brought down Barlaam and Akindynos and defended the doctrine of God's natural energy and the unapproachable light (PHIL-ed 455-502); the next Sunday was set every year for his memory (PHIL-ed 502-508); the feast was made general by synod under the patriarch Philotheos in 1368 (PHIL-ed 509-514). Confidence: Documented as the editor's account (the Synodikon itself is not held; the editor cites the Triodion, 494). CEN `legacy`: "The second Sunday of Great Lent is still kept in his memory across the Orthodox world." (who keeps him today: the tree's own tradition text).

I3. **His prayer writings carried on.** The Philokalia, printed at Venice (PHK1 38; 1782 by the Library header), prints him under "ΤΟΥ ΕΝ ΑΓΙΟΙΣ ΠΑΤΡΟΣ ΗΜΩΝ ΓΡΗΓΟΡΙΟΥ ΤΟΥ ΘΕΣΣΑΛΟΝΙΚΗΣ" (PHK2 39954); Paisius Velichkovsky's Slavonic, printed by Optina in 1849, holds "Григорія Паламы Архиепископа Оессалонитскаго, о молитвѣ" (OPT 9835-9836). Confidence: Documented (CEN edges cite the same lines).

I4. **His opponents' charge kept alive.** Gregoras's history (E18-E20); the Cyprus letters (MM 18975ff.). The card's later Latin and English judges (the Bollandists after Allatius, Covel in 1722) are card, not rechecked: for the card only after a recheck.

Defended: the hesychasts' way of prayer, and their claim to see a light that was God's, not a created one (E7-E9; PHIL-ed 469-474).

## Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **Why he was imprisoned**: Kalekas (defiance of the synod's ban, flight, refusal to show his writings), Kantakouzenos (the patriarch's envy and his refusal to condemn Kantakouzenos; his open opposition to the war), Gregoras (the empress's letters: his teaching, not his politics). E14.

D2. **Many gods.** Barlaam charged two gods (1341, MM 9710-9711); Gregoras charged many gods and no god (E19); Philotheos threw the same words back at Barlaam's side (E20); the synods of 1347 and 1351 declared Palamas in line with the theologians (E16-E17). The synods are the winning side's records (CEN sources note).

D3. **1341: what was judged.** The tome and Kalekas say the monks were found above Barlaam's charges and Barlaam's words judged blasphemous (E11-E12); Kalekas also says the synod forbade anyone to raise such questions further and urged the same on one other man, whose name the OCR garbles ("τῷ lane", MM 11410; probably Palamas, not certain). The card's note that Combefis and Gregoras say no doctrine was debated in 1341 is card, not rechecked.

D4. **Month of the 1341 decision**: MM 9665 heads it July; the card says the Jassy text gives August (card, not rechecked).

D5. **Death year**: none in a held early witness (S5).

D6. **Origin and age at leaving**: Philotheos, family from the east settled in Constantinople; Kantakouzenos, "from Asia by descent", raised at court, and very young when he left (S1-S2). Compatible; no ages as numbers.

D7. **Late or partisan**: Kantakouzenos and Philotheos are his partisans; Kalekas and Gregoras his opponents; the 1857 editor writes five centuries later for the church that keeps his feast.
