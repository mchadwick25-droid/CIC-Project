# Outline check: Gregory Palamas

## My heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census tradition entry `hesychasm-and-the-palamite-synthesis` (he has no person entry yet), the source sheet and what he is known for.

**Essence.** A monk of Athos who, when the Calabrian scholar Barlaam mocked the hesychasts (monks of stillness who prayed the Jesus Prayer and said they saw a divine light), defended their prayer and taught that God cannot be shared in his essence yet truly gives himself in his uncreated energies; councils of 1341, 1347 and 1351 upheld him, and his teaching became the standard of Orthodox theology.

**Also told.**
- Born in Constantinople to a well-born family from the east, raised at court; very young, left with his two brothers for Athos; lived at and beside the Lavra of Athanasios.
- Barlaam's mockery (deceivers, Messalians, "navel-souls") after a monk told him of light seen with bodily eyes; Palamas called down from the Mountain; his nine treatises, the Triads, answered him: no one puts the soul in the navel; the body is not evil.
- The teaching: three in God (essence, energy, the three persons); union is by energy, not essence; how to read the Fathers on both.
- 1341: Barlaam charged two gods; the synod cleared the monks and condemned Barlaam.
- Civil war: under Patriarch Kalekas and the empress Anna he was held in prison; three accounts of why (Kalekas, Kantakouzenos, Gregoras), credited each.
- 1347: released, the council upheld him and deposed Kalekas; made archbishop of Thessalonica, a city that would not receive him at first.
- 1351: Blachernae council upheld him; Gregoras, who was there and was condemned, tells it in tears and anger; Gregoras's charge of many gods (and no god), told and credited to him.
- Death at sixty-three (Philotheos), whispering "the heavenly to the heavenly".
- What came: councils made his teaching the church's; remembered on the second Sunday of Lent and in the Synodikon; his prayer writings carried by the Philokalia and Paisius.

Shape: Teacher or thinker.
