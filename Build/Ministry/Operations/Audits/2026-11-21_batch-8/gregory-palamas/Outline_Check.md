# Outline check: Gregory Palamas

## My heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census tradition entry `hesychasm-and-the-palamite-synthesis` (he has no person entry yet), the source sheet and what he is known for.

**Essence.** A monk of Athos who, when the Calabrian scholar Barlaam mocked the hesychasts (monks of stillness who prayed the Jesus Prayer and said they saw a divine light), defended their prayer and taught that God cannot be shared in his essence yet truly gives himself in his uncreated energies; councils of 1341, 1347 and 1351 upheld him, and his teaching became the standard of Orthodox theology.

**Also told.**
- Born in Constantinople to a well-born family from the east, raised at court; very young, left with his two brothers for Athos; lived at and beside the Lavra of Athanasios.
- Barlaam's mockery (deceivers, Messalians, "navel-souls") after a monk told him of light seen with bodily eyes; Palamas called down from the Mountain; his nine treatises, the Triads, answered him: no one puts the soul in the navel; the body is not evil.
- The teaching: three in God (essence, energy, the three persons); union is by energy, not essence; how to read the Fathers on both.
- 1341: Barlaam charged two gods; the synod cleared the monks and condemned Barlaam.
- Civil war: under Patriarch Kalekas and the empress Anna he was held in prison; three accounts of why (Kalekas, Kantakouzenos, Gregoras), credited each.
- 1347: released, the council upheld him and deposed Kalekas; made archbishop of Thessalonica, a city that would not receive him at first.
- 1351: Blachernae council upheld him; Gregoras, who was there and was condemned, tells it in tears and anger; Gregoras's charge of many gods (and no god), told and credited to him.
- Death at sixty-three (Philotheos), whispering "the heavenly to the heavenly".
- What came: councils made his teaching the church's; remembered on the second Sunday of Lent and in the Synodikon; his prayer writings carried by the Philokalia and Paisius.

Shape: Teacher or thinker.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 2 states Philotheos's account of how Palamas came to Thessalonica as settled, but Kantakouzenos's account differs.** Philotheos says Palamas was on Athos at the Lavra when he heard of Barlaam, and that the monks of Thessalonica, led by Isidore, wrote begging him to come down from the Mountain (PHIL 4004-4007, 4060-4086). Kantakouzenos says something else. After his first spiritual father died, Palamas spent more than eight years under a second one, then ten years in a cell near Veroia, and then time in the Athos houses. Then, "τῆς περὶ τὸ σῶμα νοσοκομίας ἐπειγούσης, ἦλθεν εἰς Θεσσαλονίκην" (when he needed care for his body, he came to Thessalonica; KAN1 32760-32787). So by Kantakouzenos's account he was already in the city when Barlaam began, and "μάλιστα Γρηγόριος ὁ Παλαμᾶς" (above all Gregory Palamas) took it hard there (KAN1 32719-32723). The two witnesses agree on one point: he first sent friends to ask Barlaam to stop (KAN1 32788-32794; PHIL 4108-4126). The outline credits only Philotheos (beat 2; Who tells it, "the call from the Mountain"), and cut item 7 keeps only his version. Corrections:
- Beat 2, replace the second sentence with: "How he came to Thessalonica is told two ways. Philotheos, in his speech of praise, says the monks there wrote begging him to come down from the Mountain, and he came unwillingly (E4; PHIL 4060-4086). Kantakouzenos says he had already come to the city, for care when he was ill, after years on Athos and in a hermitage near Veroia (KAN1 32760-32787). Both say he first sent friends to urge Barlaam to stop; Philotheos adds that he then spoke to him face to face (PHIL 4108-4126; KAN1 32788-32794). Tell both, each credited; the narrator does not choose."
- Cut first, item 7: "Beat 2, his first tries through friends, and Philotheos's face-to-face meeting: keep the two accounts of how he came, each in one clause (about 12 words)."
- Who tells it, Kantakouzenos: add "his coming to Thessalonica for treatment (KAN1 32784-32787)". Philotheos: change "the call from the Mountain" to "the call from the Mountain (Kantakouzenos differs: beat 2)".
- Do not say: add "Not 'the monks called him down from Athos' or 'he was already in Thessalonica' in the narrator's voice. Credit each: Philotheos, the call from the Mountain; Kantakouzenos, already in the city for care when ill."
- For the card: add "the two accounts of how he came to Thessalonica, and Kantakouzenos's years under two spiritual fathers and at Veroia (KAN1 32760-32787)".

**B2. "Also told", What came of him: "the Philokalia carried his writings on prayer into Slavonic and Russian" is not what the held lines say.** The Philokalia held here is the Greek one, first printed at Venice (PHK1 38 and its header). The Slavonic is Paisius Velichkovsky's own collection, "Ears of grain plucked" (OPT 9835-9836, whose header says it is in Church Slavonic). That is not the Philokalia, and it is not Russian. The census `legacy` says only that Optina printed it "for Russian readers". Correction: "the Philokalia, a Greek book of the old teachers of prayer printed at Venice in the eighteenth century, gathered his writings; Paisius Velichkovsky put his piece on prayer into Slavonic, and the monks of Optina printed it (I3; CEN `legacy`)." Do not say: add "Not 'the Philokalia was translated into Slavonic and Russian' or 'his writings reached Russia through the Philokalia': the held lines give the Greek Philokalia and Paisius's own Slavonic collection."

## MINOR

**M1. Q3's source note is wrong about how the reading is confirmed.** PHIL 8243, "μετέστησε καὶ τὰ ἐπουράνια", is Philotheos's own narration: grace moved his soul "to heaven and the heavenly things". It is not the dying man's phrase said again. At 8235 the first word of the phrase is broken by the OCR ("éns-ράνια"), so it could be ἐπουράνια or οὐράνια. The English is the same either way. Correction: "One print only. The first word is broken by the OCR at 8235-8236 (ἐπου]ράνια or οὐράνια); the English holds either. 8243 is Philotheos's echo, not a repetition. The card prints the English alone, or the Greek with the first word marked uncertain."

**M2. The opening's place.** Kantakouzenos sets Barlaam's approach to the simple monk at Thessalonica (KAN1 32719-32720). Philotheos sets the same approach at Constantinople, after Barlaam left Thessalonica (PHIL 3962-3986). Philotheos then has him back at Thessalonica, attacking the monks there (PHIL 4029-4031). The opening already credits Kantakouzenos, so it may keep "at Thessalonica" only inside his credit, or leave the place out. Do not say: add "Not 'at Thessalonica' in the narrator's own voice for Barlaam's visit to the monk: it is Kantakouzenos's place; Philotheos puts it at Constantinople." For the card: both places.

**M3. Beat 4 places the "two gods" charge at the council.** The tome tells Barlaam's accusation, the monks' answer and his "two gods" charge as things that happened before he brought the case to the church (MM 9700-9715). Rephrase: "Barlaam had accused the monks of saying God's very essence could be shared. When they answered that what is shared is the uncreated grace of the Spirit, he charged them with believing in two gods, and he brought the case to the patriarch. At the council, with the emperor presiding, Palamas made the defense (MM 9788-9797)…"

**M4. Beat 5: the patriarch's reason needs its content.** "Defying the synod" ("Also told") means this, by Kalekas's letter: the synod had forbidden anyone to raise such questions again, and Palamas went on speaking and writing (MM 11405-11415, 11436-11440). Add one clause to beat 5's account from the patriarch: "that the council had forbidden anyone to raise these questions again, and he went on writing".

**M5. Do not say on the length of the prison: "When it began is not in a held line" is not right.** Kantakouzenos says the patriarch turned on him "right after the war began" (KAN2 33660-33671). He also says Palamas "was sentenced to live in prison from beginning to end" of the war (KAN3 1447-1451). The patriarch's letter of November 1344 gives the order to hold him (MM 11416-11436). Replace with: "Say 'held for years'. Kantakouzenos says he was held from the war's start to its end; the patriarch's letter of November 1344 gives the order; no held line gives a year for the start."

**M6. Beat 7, "cut his opponents off" (cited as E17, which does not hold it).** The 1351 tome strips the bishops of Ephesus and Ganos of all priestly office, and sends the others away under sentence, except a few who asked pardon (PG151 15655-15671). Reword: "It ruled for Palamas, stripped two bishops of his opponents' side of their office, and condemned the rest (PG151 15655-15671)."

**M7. Connection, first point, and beat 3: "a created thing, or a delusion".** Barlaam's held words concern the light at the Transfiguration. He said it "was not unapproachable, nor truly the light of the Godhead", but lower than our own thought (MM 11351-11356). In Kantakouzenos's report, he called the monks deceivers, people who "deceive and are deceived" (KAN1 32670-32681). Correct the Connection to: "Barlaam said the light the monks spoke of was not truly God's light but lower than human thought, and the monks deceivers who were themselves deceived." Beat 3's optional clause should be reworded the same way, and it should say this was Barlaam's word on the light at Christ's Transfiguration.

**M8. Words to explain.**
- Add **Calabria**: "Calabria, in the south of Italy" (opening).
- Move **the Fathers** into the list from the Q2 note ("the great teachers of the early church").
- If the Paisius clause is kept, add **Slavonic**: "the old church language of the Slavs".
- **Mount Athos**: the gloss is plain geography and is fine. But the census region it cites (S6) reads only "Athos, Thessalonica", so mark it as the narrator's gloss and not as a census line.
- **Synodikon**: the gloss holds (PHIL-ed 455-466: anathemas and "eternal memory" read on the first Sunday of Lent).
- No common word is glossed needlessly.

**M9. "Beyond all knowing" (Do not say, Shape).** No held Palamas line says "unknowable". Q2 has "not made manifest" (ἀνέκφαντον) and "cannot be shared". The phrase comes from the census tradition text. Prefer "never shown, beyond all sharing". If "beyond all knowing" is kept, it must be in the narrator's sentence about the teaching only, as the outline already says.

**M10. "Philotheos, his friend" (Names, Who tells it)** has no supporting line. Cite one from PHIL, or say "Philotheos, who later became patriarch, in a speech of praise after his death".

**M11. Q1 has only one held Greek print.** PG150's Greek column for this sentence is lost in the OCR (the Greek resumes at 64686 with the Elijah passage; ὀμφαλοψύχ is not found in the file). PHK2 is the only Greek print, and Migne's Latin confirms the sense. The card prints the PHK2 Greek with this note. As for damaged scans generally: Q2 has two Greek prints, Q1 one print backed by the Latin, Q3 see M1, and the Gregoras phrase is clean, with the Latin confirming it. The 1351 night (GRE2 27536-27544, the left edge cut) is paraphrased, not quoted, and the Latin at 27564-27571 confirms it.
