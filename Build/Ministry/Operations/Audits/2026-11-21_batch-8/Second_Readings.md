# Second Readings (batch 8, B8-01 to B8-41)

## Method

For each id I set our wording (`Second_Reading_Key.md`, as it stands in the brief) beside the blind rendering (`Second_Readings_Blind_A.md`, `_B.md`, `_C.md`). I first confirmed that each key wording stands word for word in its brief and equals its card entry's `modern`: all 41 do. Where the readings differed, I opened the cited lines in `cic/texts/` and read the story's card `quotationOriginals` entry (`printed`, `original`, `uncertain`). Where the blind reader rendered past the end of our span, or only part of it, I judged the words we use. Following Mark's guidance (Decision-Log, "Batch 6: where sources disagree", 2026-11-19), where two held sources differ, a wording held by either is credited; where the card's English is made from a Latin the Library holds, I read that Latin.

- **SAME**: same meaning.
- **MINOR**: a small difference a listener would not take differently.
- **MEANING**: a listener would understand something different.

The thirteen CARRIED spans (checked at card) were not read again. `constantine-the-great` has no span on the list.

## The two rulings

**Jerome's Oea quote (B8-37).** The Latin puts the uproar first. The main clause is *factus est tantus tumultus in plebe*; the Greeks stand inside it, in an ablative absolute, *maxime Graecis arguentibus et inflammantibus calumniam falsitatis*, and the result clause follows, *ut cogeretur episcopus ... Iudaeorum testimonium flagitare* (lines 20552-20556). The card checker read it rightly. Our order does not mislead. The ablative absolute tells what drove the uproar and who led it, and ours says the same: the Greeks, above all, attacked the reading as false; the uproar followed; the bishop had to call on the Jews. A listener takes away the same people, the same charge and the same outcome. MINOR, no rendering change. The card's `uncertain` already records the order.

**B8-37's `original`.** It does not run into the next sentence. The card's `original` ends at *flagitare*, as the print does before *utrum autem* (line 20555). The request's "25 words" counts the two dashes around *Oea quippe ciuitas erat* as words (`str.split()` in `tools/list_second_readings.py`); the Latin itself is 23 words. No correction.
