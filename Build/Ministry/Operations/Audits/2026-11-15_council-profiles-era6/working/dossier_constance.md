## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The pope's carriage in the snow (Richental, RICH 1503-1521).** On the way to Constance, John XXIII's wagon overturned on the Arlberg.
> "do viel sin wagen umm und lag in dem schnee under dem wagen." (1504-1505) / "Do antwurtt er: „Ich lig hie in dem namen des tüfels"" (1518-1519) / "do sprach er: „Sic ca- piuntur wulpes", das ist ze tütsch: „Also werdent die fuchs ge- fangen!"" (1520-1522)
> Mine: "his wagon overturned and he lay in the snow under it." "He answered: 'I lie here in the devil's name.'" Seeing the land below: "Thus are foxes caught!"
Caveat: Richental is anecdotal; Lützow (LUTZ 9291-9293) quotes it and calls him "very entertaining, though very mendacious". A good scene, but tell it as Richental's story.

**B. The council that could not start (Fillastre, FIN2 912-927; Cerretanus FIN2 9307-9318).**
> Fillastre: "Cum autem iam progrederetur et papa iam exivisset cameram in pontificalibus, arripuit eum egritudo et opportuit eum retrocedere" (917-919) ... "Die lune sequenti quinta Novembris papa sanato fuit facta dicta processio et missa sollemnis ... Et ita inceptum est concilium." (923-927)
> Mine: "When the procession was already moving and the pope had left his room in his vestments, sickness seized him and he had to turn back." "On the Monday following, 5 November, the pope being well, the procession and solemn mass were held ... And so the council was begun."
Cerretanus says it was "accidens ex indigestione causatum" (9310). Partisan note: both men were curial.

**C. The paper crown (Mladoňovice, PAL-DOC 17292-17304).** At the degradation on 6 July 1415:
> "Committimus animam tuam diabolo. Et ipse manibus complosis et erectis in coelum oculis dixit: Et ego eam committo piissimo domino Jesu Christo." (17292-17294) ... "titulus in eadem corona causae ipsius inscriptus erat: Hic est haeresiarcha." (17302-17303)
> Mine: "'We commit your soul to the devil.' And he, hands joined and eyes raised to heaven, said: 'And I commit it to the most merciful Lord Jesus Christ.'" "On that crown the title of his case was written: 'This is an arch-heretic.'"
Mladoňovice is Hus's companion and partisan; the acts (MANSI27 77872-77877) confirm Hus was set on a high bench in the middle of the council "ita quod omnis homo eum sufficienter videre poterat".

**D. "We have a pope" (Richental, RICH 6690-6699; cf. Walsingham WALS 28074-28085).**
> "glich dozemal do warend sy all dry und fünfzig in der capell in dem kofhus ... do schray man und ruft usser [COLI] dem conclavi: »Wir habend ainenbaupst: Otto- nem de Columpna!« Und luff menglich für das kofhus, wol ob lxxx tusend personen, frowen und man." (6692-6699; "Ixxx" in the OCR)
> Mine: "Just then all fifty-three were in the chapel in the warehouse ... the cry went up out of the conclave: 'We have a pope: Oddone Colonna!' And everyone ran to the warehouse, well over eighty thousand people, women and men."
The eighty thousand is Richental's count. Fillastre, an elector, gives a quieter scene: children in surplices singing Veni creator outside, "plures ad lacrimas fuerunt commoti" (8126-8131).

## 3. Issue

- **Three popes.** Fillastre (FIN2 790-832): the schism had lasted thirty years ("Cum autem dictum scisma triginta annis durasset", 801); the cardinals of both obediences called the Council of Pisa, "in quo dicti Benedictus et Gregorius fuerunt depositi de summo pontificatu" (824-825), and elected Alexander V; but "dicta depositione non obstante" Spain obeyed Benedict and Naples, Carlo Malatesta, the dukes of Bavaria and Trier obeyed Gregory (827-831). Alexander died; John XXIII was elected, "Quam electionem fuisse vitiosam fama est" (840-841). Laurence of Březová (BREZ 35779-35786, Hussite) gives the same three obediences.
- **Pisa as background (held witnesses).** MANSI27 holds the Pisa acts (sessions from 13207; the session to set the definitive sentence named "dies quinta Junii", 41630-41640). The Monk of Saint-Denis in Mansi says the cardinals elected a pope "circa finem Junii" and on 7 July he was called Alexander V (2162-2174). Fillastre's account is the cardinals' side; the Pisa rulings are what Fillastre's Italian nation wanted confirmed at Constance (FIN2 965-968), and d'Ailly opposed it (968-974).
- **Reform "in head and members".** Fillastre names the two aims: "de pace et unione perfecta ecclesie, secundo de reformacione illius" (FIN2 946-947). Haec sancta adds faith (MIRBT 13922-13923).
- **Heresy.** Fillastre: while the union matter lay silent, "actum est contra quendam hereticum Boemum dictum Johannem Hus et de erroribus Jobannis Wiclef Angüci" (FIN2 975-977).
- **Hus's side:** he came to answer in public. The barons' letter of 12 May 1415 (PAL-DOC 28720-28727, Latin version of a Czech original) says he went "propter falsum crimen, quod ei innocenti totique regno ac nationi Bohemicae objiciebatur". Hus's own words: he came "libere, habens salvum conductum domini regis hic praesentis, volens meam innocentiam ostendere et de fide mea reddere rationem" (Mladoňovice reporting Hus on 6 July, PAL-DOC 17200-17203).

## 4. Called by

- John XXIII convoked it, at Sigismund's demand. Fillastre: Sigismund asked the pope for power to call a council; the cardinals pressed; Challant and Zabarella "elegerunt Constantiam et diem kalendarum Novembris" 1414 with the king's consent; at Lodi "papa in generali consistorio rege presente generale hoc concilium Constantiense ... convocavit" (FIN2 856-876). The bull of convocation was read at session I (FIN2 937-939). Fillastre's closing judgment: John XXIII "illud refugiebat ... et quasi coactus fecit" (8622-8625).
- Březová (BREZ 35776-35784): John XXIII convoked it "votis ... serenissimi principis Sigismundi ... annuens".
- Hussite memory names Sigismund: Prague to Venice, 1420: "in Constantiensi concilio, cujus ipse rex autor, patronus et rector erat" (URK1 2292-2293).
- Gregory XII's line: on 4 July 1415 his cardinal Giovanni Dominici "auctoritate d. sui Gregorii istud concilium convocavit et auctorizavit" (FIN2 2439-2440); at that moment the king sat in the president's chair (2416-2419; MANSI27 76535-76540 has the king then move to his own seat, with Jean de Brogny presiding).
- Contested how to name the caller: see §11.

## 5. Attended

Counts, each with its witness:
- **Richental's recapitulation** (RICH 11624-11686): John XXIII came with 600 persons; 5 patriarchs; "Cardinal xxxiii"; 47 archbishops; "Bischoff hundert und xxxxv"; 93 suffragans; 132 abbots and 155 provosts "all mit ir nammen geschriben"; doctors of theology 2xx ("ii^xyli", OCR unclear); 37 universities; "Offen huren ... dero waren ob vii°, on die haimlichen, die laß ich belibnen" (11660-11662: over 700 open prostitutes, "not counting the secret ones, whom I leave out"); envoys of over 83 kings; 472 imperial-city embassies. **"[Eine spätere band:] personen 72460." (11686)**: Buck marks the 72,460 total as a later hand, not Richental's.
- Richental on the opening: "allen cardinälen, der was do zemaul xxiii und alle erzbischofen, dero was by xxxvii" (1722-1724).
- **Fillastre**: the French nation had "quadringentas personas honestas" (FIN2 1333-1336), "the largest in number and merit".
- **Acts**: session IV, "in qua fuerunt ducenti patres" (MANSI27 60337; margin "Al. vel circa in ms. Goth.": a manuscript adds "or about").
- **Conclave**: Fillastre "Erant enim LIII electores et tres servabantur pro absentibus" (FIN2 7818-7819); Glassberger "XXIII Cardinales et XXX viri nationum, qui iuncti simul faciunt LIII" (GLASS 18876-18877); Richental "dry und fünfzig" (RICH 6692). The Quaracchi editors note Ciaconius's figure of 28 cardinals and Oldoini's correction to 23 of 26 then living (GLASS 18964-18967).
- **Walsingham**: 140 mitred at Martin V's first mass (WALS 28087-28088).

Where from: four nations (Italian, French, German, English; Fillastre passim, e.g. 7583-7587 with a Spanish nation by 1417). England: bishops of Salisbury, Bath, Hereford, abbot of Westminster, prior of Worcester, Earl of Warwick (WALS 26581-26586). Poland: archbishop of Gniezno, bishop of Płock, Paulus Wladimiri (MANSI27 129359-129369); Polish envoys arrived 20 Jan 1415 (Finke note, FIN2 19717-19718). Scandinavia: learned men of Sweden, Denmark, Norway (RICH 3030-3032). Byzantium: "zwen hertzogen von Tropi, uß Kriechenland, in bottschaft des kaysers von Constantinopel" (RICH 2722-2723). Rus': "Gregorius archiepiscopus ... Ruthenus de ordine sancti Basilii, qui dicitur esse metropolitanus tocius Rossie" with six priests, 25 Feb 1418 (FIN2 8391-8397; Richental's version RICH 7165-7175 and 7337-7341, dated 19 Feb 1418). Richental also lists envoys of Witold of Lithuania, the "Despot" of Serbia, Wallachia, "zwain küngen uß Türggen", "wißen Russen", "der mertail under inn recht haiden, ettlich scismatici" (RICH 2725-2731).

**Lists of names held:** Richental's long lists (RICH ~8280-11600, e.g. 10092 "Johannes Huß, Jeronirnns, heretici"); Glassberger's list of all 53 electors by name (GLASS 18880-18945); Mansi's names of those who swore the Narbonne articles (index MANSI27 ~817). No signed subscription list to Haec sancta or the main decrees was found (see Absences).

## 7. Decided (from the acts where held)

1. **Haec sancta** (session V, 6 April 1415; first pronounced in session IV with an article left out). MIRBT 13920-13929: "Haec sancta synodus Constantiensis .. primo declarat, quod ipsa in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, concilium generale faciens et ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans, potestatem a Christo immediate habet, cui quilibet, cuiuscunque Status vel dignitatis, etiamsi papalis existat, obedire tenetur in his, quae pertinent ad fidem et exstirpationem dicti schismatis et reformationem ecclesiae in capite et in membris." (OCR in MIRBT garbles "obedire tenetur in his" as "che dir e tenetiir in Ms"; MANSI27 60767-60772 has "obedire tenetur inbis que per- tinent ad fidem"). Mine: "...has power immediately from Christ, which everyone of whatever state or rank, even the papal, is bound to obey in what concerns the faith, the rooting out of this schism, and the reform of the church in head and members." It was read in session V by "electum Posnaniensem Polonum, quia cardinalis Florentinus illas noluit pronunciare" (FIN2 1570-1572).
2. **Frequens** (session XXXIX, 9 Oct 1417): councils five years after this one, then seven, then every ten "perpetuo" (MIRBT 13931-13938).
3. **Wyclif**: 45 articles condemned; 260 further articles (Fillastre 1864 says "CCLXVI"); memory condemned; "corpus & ejus ossa, si ab aliis fidelibus corporibus discerni possint, exhumari, & procul ab ec[clesiae sepultura projici]" (MANSI27 65559-65562, 4 May 1415). Fillastre on 6 July: "Primo de Wiclef condempnavit XLV articulos ... et ducentos sexaginta in alia parte approbavitque sentenciam Romani concilii novissimi" (FIN2 2573-2576). First article: "Substantia panis materialis ... remanent in sacramento altaris" (DENZ 15787; MIRBT 14030).
4. **Communion in one kind** (session XIII): though Christ instituted it "sub utraque specie", the custom that laymen receive "tantummodo sub specie panis" stands; to call it sacrilege "censeri debet erroneum, et pertinaciter asserentes oppositum ... tanquam haeretici arcendi sunt" (DENZ 15960-16010).
5. **Hus** (session XV, 6 July 1415): 30 articles condemned (DENZ nos. 627-656; art. 1 "Unica est sancta universalis Ecclesia, quae est praedestinatorum universitas", 16017-16018). Sentence: Hus "hereticum fuisse & esse"; to be degraded by six named bishops (Milan, Feltre, Asti, Alessandria, Bangor, Lavaur); "judicio seculari ... relinquendum fore decernit" (MANSI27 78633-78666). Fillastre: "de heresi condempnavit, degradavit et tradidit curie seculari" (FIN2 2577-2579).
6. **Tyrannicide**: the proposition defended in France after the murder of Louis of Orléans condemned the same day (FIN2 2598-2603; DENZ index no. 690).
7. **Safe-conducts** (session XIX, 23 Sept 1415): no safe-conduct to heretics prevents a competent church judge from proceeding, "etiamsi de salvo conductu confisi ad locum venerint iudicii, alias non venturi" (MIRBT 13944-13955, citing Mansi 799). And a decree that Sigismund, "non obstante memorato salvo conductu ex iuris debito fecisse quod licuit et quod decuit regiam maiestatem" (MIRBT 13957-13982; Mirbt cites v. d. Hardt IV 521, no session given; unsure of its session).
8. **Popes**: John XXIII deposed 29 May 1415 and "sentencie acquievit" (FIN2 2160-2169); Gregory XII's resignation received 4 July 1415 (FIN2 2412-2440); Benedict XIII deposed 26 July 1417 (FIN2 6645-6680).
9. **Observants**: a vicar general for the Observant friars of France, Burgundy and Touraine (GLASS 18533-18555).
10. **Election rules**: cardinals plus six from each nation (Fillastre 7814-7819; Glassberger 18875-18877).
11. **Close**: next council at Pavia (RICH 7770-7776; MANSI27 129205-129212); Martin V's answer to the Poles on Falkenberg (MANSI27 129305-129315): "quod omnia & singula determinata, con- clufa & decreta in materiis fidet per prefens Con- ciliam conciliariter , tenere O° inviolabiliter obfer- ware volebat ... Ipjaque fic conciliariter fatta approbat © ratificat, & non aliter , nec alio modo". Mine: "that he would hold and keep inviolably everything determined, concluded and decreed in matters of faith by the present council conciliarly ... and approves and ratifies what was so done conciliarly, and not otherwise, nor in any other way."
12. **Bridget of Sweden**: Richental says a full session on Candlemas eve 1415 heard the Scandinavians and "ward da canonisiert" (RICH 3027-3058). Her Who card already records Cumming's view that this confirmed a canonization of 1391 (batch2 bridget-of-sweden.card.json lines 46-48).
13. **Bull Inter cunctas** (Martin V, 22 Feb 1418): Wyclif's and Hus's articles again; interrogations for suspects, incl. whether Constance "universalem Ecclesiam repraesentet" and whether the condemnations of Wyclif, Hus and "Hieronymi de Praga" were "rite et iuste factae" (DENZ 16190-16241).

## 8. Outcome

- **Schism ended**: one pope. Fillastre: "Laudetur ... qui dedit hec in pace fieri et ecclesiam sub uno capite constitui" (8162-8164). Benedict XIII did not accept: he held Peñíscola and said "non posse esse generale concilium, quoniam nulli sunt cardinales, nulli episcopi ... omnes, qui sunt hie, sunt scismatici et excommunicati" (FIN2 4826-4831, Fillastre's summary of his written reply).
- **Hussite reaction**: 12 May 1415 letter with 250 seals asking Hus's release (PAL-DOC 28592-28637); 2 Sept 1415 protest of the barons, knights and nobles, "sigillis 452 additis", that they will defend preachers of God's law "usque effusionem sanguinis" (PAL-DOC 30060-30075, 30258-30262; Palacký tallies the seals over eight letters: 35, 100, 102, 84, 24, 26, 27, 54, at 30264-30269). They call Hus "vir utique bonus, justus et catholicus" (30155-30156).
- **The chalice**: begun in Prague in 1414 by Jakoubek of Stříbro (BREZ 35607-35614); Hus from prison defends "the communion of the sacrament of the cup" against the council's decree (HUSLET 11872-11880, his letter after 18 June 1415).
- **The wars**: Prague to Venice, 10 July 1420: Sigismund at Constance had the chalice-communicants condemned, raised "illam impiam crucem Antichristi" against Bohemia, and had Hus condemned "sub suo salvo aut potius calvo conductu" (URK1 2292-2333). The census carries the wars and the Compacts (Hussite entry; edge ...--2).
- **Reach beyond the Latin West**: the Greek-rite metropolitan Gregory says "serenissimus dominus meus, d. imperator Constantinopolitanus ... patriarcha eciam illius urbis" desire union "per congregacionem concilii" (FIN2 8475-8497), a line toward Florence. Envoys of the emperor present (RICH 2722-2723, 6198-6203).
- **The papacy and the council**: Bannwart (DENZ 16208-16224, modern editor): the Holy See "nunquam confirmavit omnia illius decreta"; Eugene IV in 1439 rejected the sentence of sessions IV and V "tanquam impiam et scandalosam" and in 1446 received Constance "absque tamen praeiudicio ... sanctae Sedis Apostolicae". Fifth Lateran, Pastor aeternus (19 Dec 1516): the pope alone has "auctoritatem super omnia Concilia" (DENZ 17495-17511; Constance not named).
- **Windesheim**: the prior came home honored; Martin V later confirmed the chapter's privileges (BUSCH 20455-20470, 20481-20500).
- **Observants**: the 1415 decree became the base for later papal rulings (census edge; GLASS 19434).

## 9. The condemned and refusing parties' own voices

- **Hus (two independent witnesses: his own writings and letters, and Mladoňovice):** De Ecclesia: "the holy catholic — that is, universal — church is the totality of the predestinate — omnium predestinatorum universitas" (HUSCH 2648-2650; Schaff's English with Hus's Latin). Before the council, 5 July: "sed semper desidero, quod ostendant mihi scripturas meliores et probabiliores ... et si ostensae mihi fuerint, volo paratissime revocare" (PAL-DOC 17061-17064). On 6 July: "Numquam fui, sed nec Sum pertinax, sed semper desideravi et die hodierna desidero informationem efficaciorem ex scripturis" (17213-17215). Letter "to the Father", mid-June 1415 (HUSLET 11689-11700, translators' English): he cannot abjure because he would "have to give up many truths", be "a perjurer by admitting the errors which have been falsely laid to my charge", and "a stumbling-block to many of God's people". Letter of 26 June 1415 (Czech; HUSLET 12588-12645, translators' English): "the Council — proud, avaricious, and defiled with every crime — hath condemned my Czech books, which it hath never either seen nor heard read". Hus's own report of the arrival: "venimus sine salvo conductu" (HUSKOR 14828; Novotný's note: the safe-conduct reached him only on 5 Nov, 14872-14873).
- **Wyclif**: dead (d. 1384); his teaching is held in his own works (e.g. `wyclif_de-ecclesia-lat_loserth1886.txt`, `wyclif_trialogus-lat_lechler1869.txt`), but I did not match the 45 articles to his own words (see Absences). At Constance no one defended him: "Nullus vero comparuit, qui eumdem five ejus memoriam defenfaret" (MANSI27 65542-65543).
- **Jerome**: only through others. Fillastre (hostile) reports his revocation of his recantation: he had abjured "per metum ignis", "vocans semper dictum Hus bonum virum" (FIN2 3247-3251). Poggio (eyewitness, admiring but disclaiming his views): "Stabat impavidus, intrepidus, mortem non contemnens solum, sed etiam appetens, ut alterum Catonem dixisses" (PAL-DOC 32706-32708); at the stake: "Huc (inquit) accede et in conspectu accende ignem; si enim illum timuissem, numquam ad hunc locum ... accessissem" (32735-32738).
- **Benedict XIII**: through Fillastre (hostile): angered at being called heretic, he spoke "per unam horam et amplius"; his written reply denied the council was general (FIN2 4807-4831). Finke III may hold Benedict-side texts (not read).
- **John XXIII**: through Fillastre: he said he fled "pro timore regis Romanorum" (FIN2 1397-1399); at deposition "nichil videre volebat ... Ipse staret iudicio concilii" (2155-2158).

## 11. Contested points (each side with file:line)

1. **The flight's day and manner.** Fillastre: night 20/21 March, after midnight, by water (FIN2 1386-1397). Walsingham: "infirmitate simulata, nocte repente recessit" (WALS 26600-26601). Richental: 20 March, St Benedict's eve, "ain stund nach mittag", on a small horse in a grey cloak and hood "das man in nit kennen mocht" with a crossbow, while Duke Frederick jousted outside the town (RICH 3436-3459).
2. **Haec sancta: session IV or V, and what was read.** Fillastre: on 30 March Zabarella read the decrees but omitted the article on the council's power over the pope in reform, holding it "non erat verus de iure"; the full text was pronounced on 6 April by the elect of Poznań "quia cardinalis Florentinus illas noluit pronunciare" (FIN2 1539-1572). Mansi's session IV text prints the clause in brackets "( & reformationem generalem ecclefie Dei in capite & in membris. )" (60413-60416). MIRBT gives "Sessio V, 6. April 1415" (13919). The Gallican Articles cite "sessione quarta et quinta" (DENZ 23513); Bannwart's note cites both (16213-16214). Its binding force is contested: Bannwart says Rome never confirmed all the decrees and Eugene IV rejected this one (DENZ 16208-16217); the Gallican clergy said it stands "a Sede Apostolica comprobata" and must not be restricted "ad solum schismatis tempus" (DENZ 23505-23516). Martin V's "conciliariter ... et non aliter" (MANSI27 129311-129315) is the text each side reads its own way; I found no held writer arguing it either way (unsure).
3. **When Wyclif was condemned.** Acts: session VIII, 4 May 1415 (MANSI27 64789; DENZ 15778). Fillastre: the 4 May sentence was read, but he himself objected and it "non transivit in rem iudicatam" (FIN2 1867-1870); on 6 July the council condemned the 45 articles and his memory (2570-2576). The census edges use 6 July (Fillastre) and the Wyclif card uses May (Mansi). Both are held. Number of further articles: 266 (FIN2 1864) vs 260 (FIN2 2575; MANSI27 65500).
4. **Did Hus try to escape?** Richental: Hus hid in a hay wagon to flee and was caught; "Maister Hanns, warumb haben ir üwer gelait selber brechen?" (RICH 3261-3283). Mladoňovice: a rumour, "sed falsum fuit" (PAL-DOC 13603-13620). Lützow (modern) rejects Richental's tale and suggests he confused Hus with Jerome (LUTZ 9435-9449, 14102-14104).
5. **The safe-conduct.** The text, dated Speyer 18 Oct 1414 (PAL-DOC 13085-13106). Hus: "venimus sine salvo conductu" (4 Nov) and "Veni sine salvo conductu pape" (6 Nov) (HUSKOR 14828, 14939); Novotný: the king's safe-conduct was brought on 5 Nov (14872-14873); Workman-Pope: Hus set off without waiting for it (HUSLET 7451-7462, editors). John of Chlum to the pope: "sub salvo conductu domini mei Romanorum regis magistrum Hus adduxi" (PAL-DOC 13787-13789). The council: no safe-conduct binds against a heretic (MIRBT 13944-13955); Sigismund did "quod licuit et quod decuit" (MIRBT 13967-13970). Luther 1520: Hus and Jerome "were burned at Constance in violation of the papal, Christian, imperial safe-conduct and oath" (LUTH2 4307-4309); the 1916 editors say Luther errs in giving Jerome a safe-conduct (LUTH2 6104-6106).
6. **Who presided at Hus's sentence.** Acts: Jean de Brogny, bishop of Ostia, "prefidente nomine collegii cardinalium", with the king present in regalia (MANSI27 77806-77850). Mladoňovice: "praesidente Romanorum et Hungariae rege sub diademate" (PAL-DOC 17090). Note: the census story says "the emperor Sigismund presiding"; held lines call him king of the Romans.
7. **How many bishops degraded Hus.** Mladoňovice: "septem episcoporum" (PAL-DOC 17246). The sentence names six (MANSI27 78655-78661).
8. **Jerome's burning in Richental.** Richental tells it in his 1415 sequence (after "sonntag vor des haiigen crütz tag im herbst", ending "Acta sunt haec anno MCCCCXV", RICH 4722-4757), and again in his May 1416 sequence: "Dazwischen da ward Jeronimus verbrannt ... als vor geschriben stät" (5194-5195). Fillastre and Poggio date it 30 May 1416 (FIN2 3256; PAL-DOC 32751). He says Jerome "lebt in dem für vast lenger, dann der Huß und schrayt vast grülich" (RICH 4749-4750); Poggio says he began to sing a hymn that smoke and fire cut off (PAL-DOC 32733-32734).
9. **Richental's "three popes deposed" on 29 May 1415** (RICH 3964-3971) vs Fillastre: John deposed 29 May; Gregory resigned 4 July 1415; Benedict deposed 26 July 1417 (FIN2 2160, 2412, 6645). Walsingham says Gregory renounced first (WALS 26589-26591). The census line "deposed the rivals" fits John and Benedict, not Gregory.
10. **Who first acceded to Colonna.** Fillastre: "statim cardinalis Pisanus ... accessit"; then seven cardinals; he and Foix last (FIN2 8098-8121). Walsingham: on St Martin's morning Richard, bishop of London, said "Ego, Ricardus Episcopus Londoniensis, accedo ad Dominum meum, Cardinalem de Columna" and all agreed at once (WALS 28073-28078).
11. **Day of the election in Fillastre.** His held text has the scrutiny on Wednesday with no accession that day (FIN2 8058-8073), then the accessions without a new day named. Cerretanus and Richental say Thursday 11 Nov. Unsure whether Fillastre's text simply omits the day.
12. **Opening date.** 1 Nov (fixed day; Cerretanus heading; Březová) vs 5 Nov (procession; Fillastre, Cerretanus body; Mansi) vs 16 Nov (first session; Fillastre). Richental's "sixth day ... Sunday before Martinmas" (RICH 1721-1722).
13. **Chalice decree's day**: Fri 14 June (Fillastre FIN2 2218) vs Sat 15 June (MANSI27 75430; DENZ 15956).
14. **Martin V's consecration day.** Fillastre: Sunday 21 Nov (FIN2 8166). Richental: deacon Friday, priest Saturday, bishop "momends an dem Sonnentag" after St Martin (RICH 6760-6767, i.e. 14 Nov). Walsingham: priest next day, bishop the day after (WALS 28085-28087).
15. **Who called it** (§4): John XXIII's bull vs Sigismund's demand vs Gregory XII's later convocation.
16. **Gregory Tsamblak's arrival**: Fillastre "Die veneris sequenti" before 25 Feb 1418 (FIN2 8387); Richental 19 Feb 1418 with five bishops (7165-7168) and another entry "xxi tag ... Januarii" with eight bishops (2733-2738); the year of the second entry is not stated in the lines read (unsure).
