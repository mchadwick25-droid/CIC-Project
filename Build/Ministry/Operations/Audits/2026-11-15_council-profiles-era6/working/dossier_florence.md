## 1. Held sources

### Acts (the council's own texts)

- **MANSI31, the Greek acts with a Latin version ("Acta Graeca & Latina")**, l.46129-98040. A first-person Greek narrative ("ingressum vero nostrum", l.46050s) from the voyage to Venice to the return home: arrival in Venice and Ferrara, the opening, the sessions at Ferrara (on the addition to the Creed) and Florence (on the procession), the patriarch's death and note, the signing and the reading of the decree. Author not named in the file. **Louis Petit** (PO15, introduction, 1920) cites the printed Greek acts (Rome 1577) as "[Dorothée de Mitylène]", in brackets, as his attribution (PO15 l.327, 431, 435). Kind: acts (a delegate's record), Greek, pro-union in outcome.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The foot-kiss at Ferrara, March 1438 (Syropoulos).** The patriarch's barge waits at the river bank below Ferrara. Six bishops come from the pope and tell him how he must greet Eugenius. He refuses to kiss the pope's foot:
- SYR l.17865-17875 (Creyghton's Latin): "Quæ Synodus antiquorum Patrum, quis canon hunc morem ei indulsit? ... Num reliqui Apostoli ad S. Petri pedes osculandos in genua procubuere? quis hoc unquam audivit?" (my tr.: "Which synod of the ancient Fathers, which canon granted him this custom? ... Did the other apostles fall on their knees to kiss Saint Peter's feet? Who ever heard of it?")
- SYR l.17885-17889: he would embrace him "ficut fratres, fraterno more" ("as brothers do, in brotherly fashion").
- SYR l.17901-17905: unless the pope remits the kiss, "nunquam ex hac scapha in Terram descendere" ("never to step from this boat on to land").
- The pope gives way and receives him in his chamber, before the cardinals only (l.17967-17989). The Greek acts tell the same entry without the quarrel: "patriarcha quidem stantis papae genam osculatus est" ("the patriarch kissed the cheek of the pope, who stood") (MANSI31 l.46755). Petit dates the patriarch's entry to 8 March (PO15 l.300-301); the acts say they reached the port "die vero Martii septima" and entered next morning (MANSI31 l.46709-46716, 46750).

**B. "Then we achieved nothing", Florence, July 1439 (Syropoulos).** The pope looks over the Greek signatures, signs standing, and asks after Mark:
- SYR l.47962-47970: "cum subscriptiones nostrorum oculis circumlustrasset, subscripsit & ipse, stans arrecto corpore ... & interrogavit, num Ephesius Definitioni subscripsisset: cumque audiisset Ephesium non subscripsisse: Ergo, inquit, nihil praestitimus" (OCR "nibil preftitimus"). (my tr.: "When he had run his eyes over our people's signatures, he too signed, standing upright ... and asked whether the man of Ephesus had signed the Definition; and when he heard that he had not: 'Then', he said, 'we have achieved nothing.'")
- Hostile witness; the census already uses it (edge byzantine-church-palaiologan--latin-papal...).

**C. The reading in the cathedral, Monday 6 July 1439 (Greek acts).** After Mass the definition is read "Latine quidem a cardinali sanctae Sabinae Juliano: Graece vero a Bessarione archiepiscopo Niceno" (MANSI31 l.96261-96272); the Greeks kiss the pope's knee and hand, embrace one another, and the cantors sing "Laetentur caeli, & exultet terra" (l.97359-97372). The church is called "sancta Maria Liberata" in this Latin (l.96265-96266), so I would not name the church from this line.

**D. The return, Constantinople, February 1440 (Doukas).** Townsfolk greet the bishops off the galleys: "how did the synod go? did we win?"
- DOUK l.12133-12137 (Latin): "ut se res vestrae habent? quomodo transacta est synodus? vicimusne nos? responderunt illi: fidem nostram vendidimus, pietatem impietate mutavimus, Azymitae facti sumus" (Greek garbled at l.12108-12112: "πεπράκαµεν τὴν πίπ[στιν] ἡμῶν, ἀντηλλάξαμεν τῇ ἀσεβεία τὴν εὐσέβειαν"). (my tr.: "'We have sold our faith, we have traded piety for impiety, we have become men of the unleavened bread.'") Date of arrival from the notes at DOUK l.32667 ("Cpolim redierunt Graeci anno [1440], Februarii die 1"); the year digit is garbled in the OCR ("rjjo").

My recommendation: A (puts the listener at the river, and the issue of primacy is in the room at once) or B as the turn.

## 3. Issue: what brought them together

- **Basel's own programme.** Basel was to reform the church and end the Hussite heresy. Glassberger: Eugenius confirmed Cardinal Julian (Cesarini) "in legatione ... pro concilio Basileensi celebrando, permaxime ad exstirpandam haeresim Hussitarum" (GLASS l.20471-20472). The Taborite chronicle: Basel invited the Bohemians to a hearing on the Four Articles, held in 1433 (HOF2 l.35975-35990, 36062-36068).
- **Council versus pope.** Glassberger: the Basel fathers held "quod secundum decreta concilii Constantiensis, Basileae renovata, concilium universale legitime congregatum potestatem immediate a Christo haberet et maius esse Papa; et quod idcirco Papa illud sine eius consensu nec transferre nec dissolvere posset" (GLASS l.20991-20994). Eugenius rejected the Constance thesis on 4 Sept 1439 "tanquam impiam et scandalosam" (Denzinger's editor's note, DENZ l.16214-16215).
- **Union with the Greeks and the place.** The Greeks would only meet where the pope could come. Bissipatus, envoy of the emperor and patriarch, at Basel, 1437: the place must be one "ex nominatis in decreto et habilis nobis Graecis et domino nostro papae, quia praesentia sua ... supramodum est huic negotio necessaria" (RAYN l.31551-31556). Basel's majority wanted Basel, Avignon or a town of Savoy; Eugenius and the Greeks wanted Italy (RAYN l.31303-31312; Eugenius's letter l.31340-31365).
- **The Turkish threat.** The anonymous Roman Historia: "Turcarum enim vi ac tyrannide in majores quotidie angustias orientalis imperii fines redigebantur" (MANSI31 l.45849-45851). The chronicle under Sphrantzes's name has Manuel II tell his son that the Turks fear above all a union of Greeks and Latins (SPHR l.10235-10241). Chalkokondyles: at Ferrara the pope asked the emperor to help him "ἐς τὴν πρὸς Γερμανοὺς διαφοράν" (PG159 l.18083; Latin l.18129-18131: "in contentione, quae intervenisset sibi cum Germanis"), i.e. against Basel.
- **The doctrinal points.** The Historia lists: the procession and the Filioque, azymes, purgatory and the vision of the saints, the Roman primacy (MANSI31 l.45904-45911). Petit: purgatory was taken first, at private meetings from 13 April 1438 (PO15 l.308-338). Mark's own account: the first public question was the addition to the Creed, then the doctrine at Florence (PO17 l.37436-37440, 37755-37766).
- **Sides.** Latin: Eugenius, Cesarini, the curia. Greek: John VIII, Joseph II, the bishops (Mark and Bessarion as the two spokesmen on purgatory, PO15 l.359-362; Mark as "ἔξαρχος" for the Greeks and Cesarini for the Latins per Doukas, DOUK l.11957-11963, Latin l.11979-11991). Basel: the majority under the Cardinal of Arles (RAYN l.31505-31507, Mansi's note; l.42114-42118).

## 4. Called by

- **Basel**: called under Martin V and Eugenius IV. The acts quoted by Raynaldus: Palomar and John of Ragusa, Cesarini's delegates, "inchoaverunt Concilium legitima potestate eidem cardinali a felicis recordationis domino Martino et domino Eugenio praesenti" (RAYN l.14194-14210). Glassberger: Martin V sent Cesarini (GLASS l.20457-20459). Felix V's bull says Basel was begun "juxta generalium conciliorum Constantiensis & Senensis statuta, ordinationesque fel. rec. Martini V. ac Gabrielis, tunc Eugenii IV." (MANSI31 l.25969-25975).
- **Ferrara**: Eugenius IV transferred the council and indicted the synod with the Greeks at Ferrara (RAYN l.33176-33183: "Synodum OEcumenicam cum Graecis habendam Ferrariae indixit. Quas litteras Encyclicas XIV kal. Octobris consignat"). Eugenius's own letter to the citizens of Basel: "transtulimus Concilium Basileense ad civitatem Ferrariensem" (RAYN l.33318-33320). The Greek acts: the synod was proclaimed by common consent of pope and Greeks (MANSI31 l.46849-46858); Eugenius's decree of 9 April 1438 names the emperor and patriarch as consenting (l.47552-47556).
- **Florence**: Eugenius's decree, "consentientibus ... Joanne Palaeologo ... et ... Josepho patriarcha Constantinopolitano, sacro approbante Concilio" (RAYN l.38422-38440).
- **Rome**: Eugenius, by a session at Florence (RAYN l.51818-51834, quoting Agostino Patrizi).

## 5. Attended

**Counts (each with its witness; none from a full roll of Basel):**
- Basel's opening, 23 July 1431: "convocatis omnibus Ecclesiasticis personis" (RAYN l.14204-14206, the Basel acts as Raynaldus quotes them). Mansi's note: at first no bishops, only "rari quidam abbates" (RAYN l.14459-14460). No count held.
- Ferrara's first session, 8 Jan 1438, under Albergati: "quinque archiepiscopi, episcopi octodecim, quatuor alii designati, tum plures religiosorum Ordinum praelati" (Raynaldus, RAYN l.35141-35144).
- Basel's deposition session (25 June 1439): Raynaldus: "septem tantummodo pseudoepiscopi" (RAYN l.42626-42628). Aeneas Silvius, "oculatus testis" as Raynaldus quotes him: no Aragonese or Spanish prelates, from Italy only the bishop of Grosseto and an abbot, "ex duabus aliis nationibus mutuati fuerunt solum viginti" (RAYN l.42121-42131). Two counts, kept apart; neither is a signed list.
- Greek delegation: Syropoulos names the sees of the bishops who came: Ephesus, Trebizond, Heraclea, Cyzicus, Sardis, Nicomedia, Nicaea, Tornovo, Monemvasia, Lacedaemonia, Amaseia, Mitylene, Stauropolis, Moldo-Wallachia, Rhodes, Melenikon, Drama, Ganos, Distra, Anchialus, plus Isidore of Russia "uno Episcopo comitatus" (SYR l.11452-11459). My count of his list: 20 sees plus Isidore and one bishop. Officers named: great sakellarios, skeuophylax, chartophylax, ecclesiarch (himself), protekdikos, nomophylax (l.11469-11475). Also proxies of the three eastern patriarchs (Historia, MANSI31 l.45894-45896: "affuerunt itidem Alexandrinae, Antiochenae & Hierosolymitanae ecclesiae legati").
- Isidore's train: "insimul 100 hominibus" (Harasiewicz, from a Polish source he translates, HARAS l.4825-4830 and note l.4834-4836).
- Georgians: "duos Episcopos & unum Legatum" brought from Iberia (SYR l.9401-9403); later "Epifcopus unus, alter Princeps secularis" (SYR l.43934-43936). The two counts differ within Syropoulos.

## 7. Decided (from the acts where held)

1. **Ferrara declared the ecumenical synod (9 April 1438).** "decernimus & declaramus, consentientibus dictis imperatore, & patriarcha ... sacram oecumenicam synodum esse in hac urbe Ferraria" (MANSI31 l.47550-47557); both sides answered "Recipiemus, & approbamus" (l.47697-47701).
2. **Against Basel.** Ferrara decreed that those staying at Basel "sub praetenso Concilii nomine, quod verius conventiculum dici debet" had incurred the penalties of the transfer (RAYN l.35526-35537). *Moyses* (4 Sept 1439) condemned Basel's three propositions and the attempt to depose (RAYN l.42615-42635).
3. ***Laetentur caeli* (6 July 1439):**
   - Opening: "Laetentur coeli & exultet terra: sublatus est enim de medio paries, qui occidentalem orientalemque dividebat ecclesiam" (MANSI31 l.96338-96345). (my tr.: "Let the heavens rejoice and the earth be glad: the wall that divided the western and the eastern church has been taken away.")
   - Procession: the Spirit "ex utroque aeternaliter tanquam ab uno principio et unica spiratione procedit", and the Greek "per Filium" is read in that sense (DENZ l.16436-16444).
   - Addition: the Filioque was added "licite ac rationabiliter" (the decree, MANSI31 l.96693; the phrase also in *Exultate Deo*, l.98408-98411).
   - Bread: "in azymo sive fermentato pane triticeo corpus Christi veraciter confici; sacerdotesque in altero ipsorum ... iuxta suae ecclesiae sive occidentalis sive orientalis consuetudinem" (MIRBT l.14276-14278).
   - Purgatory and the vision of God (MIRBT l.14283-14290).
   - Primacy: "sanctam apostolicam sedem et Romanum pontificem in universum orbem tenere primatum ... verum Christi vicarium, totiusque ecclesiae caput et omnium christianorum patrem ac doctorem existere" (MIRBT l.14290-14300), with the patriarchs' order and "salvis videlicet privilegiis omnibus, & juribus eorum" (MANSI31 l.96860-96866). The Greek acts tell how that last clause was fought over: the Latins wanted to drop "omnibus" (l.96245-96253).
4. ***Exultate Deo* (22 Nov 1439), Armenians:** gives them the Creed with the Filioque, Chalcedon, the doctrine of two wills, the seven sacraments, the Athanasian creed, *Laetentur caeli*, and Latin feast dates (MANSI31 l.98380-99070); the envoys accept it "nomine suo & sui patriarchae & omnium Armenorum" (l.99049-99062).
5. ***Cantate Domino* (4 Feb 1442), Copts ("Jacobites"):** "nondum exacto triennio ... trium magnarum nationum unionem" (RAYN l.51859-51862): Greeks, Armenians, and now "Jacobini, magni etiam per Aegyptum populi" (l.51870-51871); Andrew, abbot of St Antony, accepts for patriarch John (l.51922-51935). Its §15 on no salvation outside the church: MIRBT l.14487-14495.
6. ***Multa et admirabilia* (30 Sept 1444), Syrians:** Abdallah, archbishop of Edessa, for Ignatius "patriarcha Syrorum", accepts the faith, "praesertim decreta Graecorum, Armenorum atque Jacobitarum in sacro OEcumenico Concilio Florentino edita: quae cum in Arabico traducta ipse Abdalem archiepiscopus accurate legisset" (RAYN l.59100-59111, 59290-59316).
7. ***Benedictus sit Deus* (7 Aug 1445), Chaldeans and Maronites of Cyprus:** Timothy, metropolitan of the Chaldeans, and Isaac for Elias, bishop of the Maronites (RAYN l.62415-62436); no one is to call them heretics (Assemani's quote, per the census edge); they may worship, be buried and marry with Catholics (RAYN l.62655-62668).
8. **Basel's own decrees after the split** (not held as acts, except in quotation): the three "truths" on conciliar supremacy (session XXXIII), the deposition (XXXIV), the Immaculate Conception definition (session XXXVI, "XV kalendas Octobris" 1439, GLASS l.21183-21185; Quaracchi editors: never a binding decision "quia non erat definitio universalis concilii legitime uniti", l.21235-21238).

## 8. Outcome

- **At once, in Florence:** Bessarion and Isidore made cardinals (Chalkokondyles PG159 l.18222-18235; MOUR l.3843-3845).
- **The Greek bishops' return and rejection:** Doukas's quayside scene (§2 D) and his charge that bishops took money to sign ("nisi pecunias, quae nobis sufficiant, praebueritis, non subscribemus", DOUK l.12145-12146, hostile). HPPC: union made "sed exitu nullo bono, eo quod subscribere recusaret Ephesius" (HPPC l.537-539). The anonymous Roman Historia: "Graeci numquam hoc generale concilium receperint, quod octavum ab ipsis appellatur" except those who sat in it (MANSI31 l.46010-46015).
- **Mark's campaign** (§9) and Petit: the union was promulgated in the islands "en juin et juillet 1440, par le nouveau patriarche Métrophane" (PO17 l.17900-17903; Petit).
- **The eastern patriarchs:** MANSI35 prints a 1443 synod of the patriarchs of Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem against Metrophanes and Florence (l.6063-6175), which Mansi thinks forged (§11). Mouravieff asserts it as fact (MOUR l.3836-3838).
- **Russia:** Isidore read the Florence decree in the Dormition cathedral, Moscow; Grand Prince Basil called him a traitor, the bishops rejected the pope as Vicar of Christ and the Filioque; Isidore was confined in the Chudov monastery, escaped to Rome (MOUR l.3845-3866). Harasiewicz: on his way back, "Redux Russiam ... anno 1440 venit 5 martii", the sentence running on after a footnote to "Budam, dein Sandeciam, Cracoviam, ... Kijeviam et Moscoviam" (HARAS l.4831, 4847-4849; the place of 5 March is not clear from the broken text); "In Moscovia vero opposuit sese unioni Florentinae Magnus Dux Basilius" (l.5210-5212). After Isidore, eight years "in widowhood" and the choice of Jonah (MOUR l.3888-3897).
- **Constantinople 1452-53:** the union celebrated "secundo idus Decembris, Spiridionis episcopi sancti die" (12 Dec 1452), Leonard of Chios (PG159 l.54517-54518). Doukas: Scholarios posted his warning on his cell door ("miseri Romaei, quo aberrastis?", DOUK l.14224-14234); the people shunned Hagia Sophia "ut Iudaeorum synagogam" (l.14730-14731).
- **The Armenians:** the catholicos (Constantine) sent four vardapets "who accepted and subscribed to the unity" (ISSAV l.7340-7352). CHAMICH has only that "the Greeks and Armenians became united with the Latins" (CHAMICH l.15230-15233). The 1441 move of the catholicosate to Etchmiadzin is in the census entry; Mansi notes that the Etchmiadzin catholicos took the patriarchal insignia "paulo post concilium Florentinum" (MANSI35 l.2406-2412). No held line links the two causally; do not.
- **Ethiopia:** Ethiopian envoys at Florence and Rome, 1441 (RAYN l.49225-49310); the Ethiopian chronicles say nothing of it (ZY l.668-674).
- **The Basel schism to its end** (§6) and later Roman memory (§13).
- **Reach beyond one tradition:** the decrees of union with Armenians (1439), Copts (1442), Syrians (1444), Chaldeans and Maronites of Cyprus (1445) are held (RAYN, MANSI31, DENZ); the Russian rejection (MOUR, HARAS); Ethiopian envoys (RAYN).

## 9. The refusing party's own voice

**Mark of Ephesus (held in his own words, Latin version in PO17 and Greek in PG160):**
- His refusal: "meam et ipse scripturam tenui ... Post haec illi res suas egerunt, atque ad conscribendam definitionem ... [ego] segregatus ab illis et mihi ipsi vacans, ut sanctis meis Patribus ac doctoribus perpetuo adhaeream" (PO17 l.38174-38192). (my tr.: "I kept my written statement to myself ... Then they went about their business and the drafting of the definition ... while I stood apart from them, attending to myself, so as to hold for ever to my holy Fathers and teachers.")
- On the others: "pollicitationibus et muneribus pellecti" ("lured by promises and gifts", PO17 l.38158-38159); on Joseph: "esset iam pridem et ipse miser corruptus" (l.38163-38164). Hostile to the patriarch; Mark's view.
- Encyclical: those who joined "vocandi merito Graeco-Latini, usitato autem nomine vulgo appellati Latinizantes ... iuxta fabulosos hippocentauros semiferi" (PO17 l.38519-38522); "Quae ergo hic unio, ubi nullum patens ac manifestum apparet signum? et quomodo uniti sunt, qui sua retinere volunt?" (l.38779-38781).
- To Scholarios (Greek, PG160 l.99432-99434): "Οὐδέποτε διὰ μεσότητος, ἄνθρωπε, τὰ ἐχχλησιαστιχὰ διωρθώθη· μέτον [μέσον] ἀληθείας καὶ ψεύδους οὐδέν ἐστιν" (Migne's Latin l.99458-99461: "Nusquam, mi homo, res ecclesiasticae per mediationem correctae sunt. Nihil inter veritatem et mendacium medium interjacet"). (my tr.: "Never, man, have the church's affairs been put right by middle ways. Between truth and falsehood there is nothing in between.") And the synod "ψευδώνυμος σύνοδος" ("the falsely named synod"), with the city guarded "διὰ τῶν χρυσίων τοῦ Πάπα" ("by the Pope's gold") (PG160 l.99645-99666).
- Mark to the pope, as Syropoulos tells it (a second, friendly witness): "Ego privatam doctrinam aut meam publice nunquam docui" (SYR l.49346-49360). The emperor refused to let the pope try Mark: "quando noster est Praesul, ad nostros pertinet censura" (SYR l.48709-48714).

**The Georgian envoy** (through Syropoulos only): "Nostra vero Ecclesia ... nihil addidit; nihil subtraxit ... At Romana Ecclesia addidit" (SYR l.44093-44105). Neither Georgian signed: "utlbericorum neuter legatorum unioni faederandae suffragium praebuerit, uterque enim subterfugerat" (l.44068-44070).

**Isaias of Stauropolis** (through Syropoulos): he slipped away from Florence when the signing began (SYR l.47672-47681).

**The Basel party (its own voice, held):** the Lausanne decree of 1449: "sancta haec Lausanensis synodus, dum Basileae in Spiritu sancto legitime congregata perseveraret, ne assentiret tunc Eugenio papae IV. Ferrariae congregationem sub nomine concilii generalis erigenti" (MANSI32 l.6991-6996). (my tr.: "this holy synod of Lausanne, while it continued lawfully gathered at Basel in the Holy Spirit, would not consent when Pope Eugenius IV set up at Ferrara a gathering under the name of a general council.") Felix V: Eugenius "Romano pontificio privatus, & ejectus extiterit, nosque per ministerium ipsius S. synodi ad Sacram Petri sedem advocati fuerimus" (MANSI31 l.25984-25987).

## 10. Short quotable lines

1. *Laetentur caeli* (acts): "Laetentur coeli & exultet terra: sublatus est enim de medio paries, qui occidentalem orientalemque dividebat ecclesiam" (MANSI31 l.96338-96345). "Let the heavens rejoice and the earth be glad: the wall that divided the western and the eastern church has been taken away."
2. *Laetentur caeli* (acts), the bread: "in azymo sive fermentato pane triticeo corpus Christi veraciter confici" (MIRBT l.14276). "Christ's body is truly made in wheat bread, unleavened or leavened."
3. *Cantate Domino* (acts): "nondum exacto triennio ... trium magnarum nationum unionem" (RAYN l.51859-51862). "Before three years have passed ... the union of three great nations."
4. Eugenius at Ferrara (Greek acts, reported): "Ubi ego sum cum imperatore & patriarcha, ibi est universa Christianorum synodus" (MANSI31 l.48664-48666). "Where I am with the emperor and the patriarch, there is the whole synod of Christians."
5. Joseph II's note (Greek acts): "Omnia igitur, quae sentit, & quae dogmatizat catholica & apostolica ecclesia ... senioris Romae, ipse quoque sentio" (MANSI31 l.94455-94461). "Everything that the catholic and apostolic church of elder Rome holds and teaches, I too hold."
6. Joseph at the barge (Syropoulos): "Num reliqui Apostoli ad S. Petri pedes osculandos in genua procubuere?" (SYR l.17873-17875). "Did the other apostles go down on their knees to kiss Saint Peter's feet?"
7. Eugenius on Mark (Syropoulos): "Ergo, inquit, nihil praestitimus" (SYR l.47970). "'Then', he said, 'we have achieved nothing.'"
8. Syropoulos on signing: "cum lacrymis & suspiriis nostrorum plurimi subscripsimus" (SYR l.47655-47656). "With tears and sighs most of us signed."
9. Mark (own letter): "μέσον ἀληθείας καὶ ψεύδους οὐδέν ἐστιν" (PG160 l.99433-99434). "Between truth and falsehood there is nothing in between."
10. Doukas: "fidem nostram vendidimus, pietatem impietate mutavimus" (DOUK l.12136-12137). "We have sold our faith; we have traded piety for impiety."
11. John of Ragusa to Basel: "Juvenem enim me & imberbem misistis; & ecce senem, & albatum canitie, atque Graece barbatum suscipitis" (MANSI31 l.26128-26131, right column; OCR "Grace barbatum"). "You sent me young and beardless; and see, you get me back old, white-haired and with a Greek beard."
12. The Armenian envoys: "Christi vicarius es ... Venimus ad caput nostrum. Venimus ad pastorem ... Via tantum retinuit nos; quare tardavimus. Servi tui sumus" (MANSI35 l.2386-2394; text in two OCR columns). "You are Christ's vicar ... We have come to our head, to our shepherd ... Only the road held us back; that is why we were late. We are your servants."
13. The chronicle under Sphrantzes's name: "utinam fieret conciliatio ecclesiarum!" (SPHR l.10227-10228, Latin version). "If only the churches were reconciled!"

## 11. Contested points

1. **How many Greeks refused.** Doukas: all agreed "πλὴν Μάρκου" / "praeter Marcum" (DOUK l.11998, l.12035). Census: Mark "alone among the Greek bishops present refused to sign" (byzantine-church-palaiologan.why). But Syropoulos says Isaias of Stauropolis slipped away rather than sign (SYR l.47672-47681), and both Georgian envoys fled (l.44065-44070). The printed list lacks Stauropolis (MANSI31). Tell it as: Mark refused; others avoided signing by leaving (Syropoulos).
2. **The patriarch's death date: 9 or 10 June 1439.** Greek acts 9 June (MANSI31 l.94631, l.94684); Syropoulos 10 June (SYR l.45238); the notes to Doukas 10 June (DOUK l.32661).
3. **Did the patriarch die before or after the union?** Doukas: "μετὰ τὴν ἕνωσιν" / "post sancitam unionem obiit patriarcha" (DOUK l.12064, l.12088). The Greek acts and Syropoulos: he died before the signing (SYR l.45228-45232: "mortuus ante subscriptionem"); the notes to Doukas correct him (l.32660-32664).
4. **The patriarch's note.** The Greek acts give it as found in his room after his death (MANSI31 l.94419-94440). Mark calls him long since "corruptus" (PO17 l.38163-38166). **The census claim that Syropoulos "suspects it was written for him" is not supported by any held Syropoulos line I found** (see §12).
5. **Who sent the Ethiopians.** Eugenius's letter calls Andrew and Peter the deacon envoys "magni principis Constantini imperatoris Aethiopum, sive ... presbyteri Joannis" (RAYN l.49243-49246); Patrizi names "Zara Jacob" (l.49288-49289, Mansi). Dillmann (as Perruchon reports, ZY l.578-582): the monks were sent "non par Zar'a Yâ'eqôb ni par son ordre, mais par Nicodème, supérieur du couvent abyssin de Jérusalem, à l'insu de ce roi". Mansi also argues that Raynaldus's "three" Ethiopian embassies come down to one (RAYN l.49276-49306).
6. **The 1443 synod of the eastern patriarchs against Florence.** Held in MANSI35 l.6063-6175; Mouravieff treats it as fact (MOUR l.3836-3838). Mansi: "In eam sententiam propendo, scriptum hoc esse plane commentitium, a Graeculo quodam schismaticarum partium studioso confictum, cum nec Phranza ejus meminerit" (MANSI35 l.6077-6080). Contested; do not tell as fact.
7. **Year of the transfer to Rome:** Patrizi 1442, Mansi 1441 (§6).
8. **Date of the transfer bull to Ferrara:** 18 Sept (XIV kal. Oct.) vs 1 Oct (kal. Oct.) (Mansi, RAYN l.31588-31596).
9. **Date of the Armenian decree:** 22 Nov (decree, MANSI31, DENZ, RAYN l.52413) vs 10 Dec (Raynaldus's narrative, RAYN l.39765). The acts should be preferred; tell 22 Nov with "the decree's own date".
10. **The Coptic decree year:** 1441 (DENZ, MIRBT, Florentine style) vs 1442 (modern style per Mansi). Not a real disagreement; a year-reckoning difference to explain or to give as 1442 "by our reckoning".
11. **Number of Armenian envoys:** four vardapets (ISSAV l.7348-7352) vs two named in the catholicos's mandate, "Sarchis Yertebet [vardapet] et Joachim episcopo Pone" (RAYN l.39752-39755). Not necessarily contradictory (mandate vs party).
12. **Count at Basel's deposition session:** "septem ... pseudoepiscopi" (Raynaldus) vs Aeneas's description (Raynaldus's quotation). Both on one side (papal); Basel's count not held.
13. **Who wrote the Greek acts:** Petit's bracketed "[Dorothée de Mitylène]" (PO15 l.327); the file and Mansi do not name him.
14. **Was Basel a council after the transfer?** Glassberger's editors: "concilium Ferrariense solum gaudebat vera et legitima auctoritate, dum Basileense esset conciliabulum sine capite" (GLASS l.21093-21095); Lateran V: "conciliabulo seu potius conventicula" (MIRBT l.15137-15139); against them Basel/Lausanne's own claim to have remained "legitime congregata" (MANSI32 l.6992-6993) and Felix V (MANSI31 l.25975-25979). Eugenius himself in 1446 received Basel "ab eius initio usque ad translationem per nos factam" (DENZ l.16218-16220, editor's note quoting Eugenius). Contested by party.
15. **Motive: aid against the Turks vs doctrine.** The census says "in exchange for military help". Held: Mark's "guarding the city with the Pope's gold" (PG160 l.99663-99666), Manuel's advice (SPHR l.10235-10243), the Roman Historia's Turkish pressure (MANSI31 l.45849-45851). The decrees themselves give no exchange; HPPC says no help came ("ea causa fuit nullius postea summissi auxilii", l.539-540). Tell the bargain as a witness's account.
