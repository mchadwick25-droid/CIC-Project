Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y credits each side where the sources differ: "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead..." X credits its main source well ("as others told it to him"), but its siege story contradicts itself.
2. Y's opening image ("a medicine chest") explains itself and carries through to the close ("in his chest of cures: the church"). X opens on a line it never makes clear: "sending out... old women, infants and old men." Later it says "feed only men and youths" and "all not needed for the fighting, men or women." A listener who hears it once cannot follow the trick.
3. Y tells its story once and in order. X gives away the ending ("used the council's vote to slip out almost all the besieged") and then tells it again ("Under cover of that decree he saved almost all").

X faults: "hunger was harder to bear than the enemy outside" (the narrator adding drama); "urged peace with Rome, but they grew angry. He argued instead" (the cause is muddled); "made him a second bishop there, to take over at his death" (whose death is unclear); "whose bishop, the same deacon, had died" (a knotted sentence, and the deacon was never said to be bishop); the Easter paragraph is too dense to follow by ear; "an Easter past the moon's twentieth day" is unclear; "Picts" is not explained; too many names (Paul of Samosata, Hild, Colman, Gregory); it ends on Bede, a later writer.

Y faults: "from Augustine to a council at Nicaea" is told early and again in paragraph 15; "four centuries after" then "Four centuries on" repeats itself; the heresy gloss is odd ("people led astray ever since Adam"); the Jerome-and-curtain paragraph is overloaded; the "Long Brothers" are never really explained; "A book written to answer the council's argument" is vague.

Grades: X C, Y B+.
