Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on one turning moment in Basil's own words ("like a man roused from deep sleep") and states the essence plainly. X opens on a long quote, then gives its ending early ("put to death about 310") and tells it again twice.
2. Y credits each source where they differ ("The old writers differ on what turned him... Gregory of Nyssa says... Rufinus says"). X does this once well (Eusebius vs Photius on the Defense), but its many "Eusebius says / Jerome says" lines start to sound like quotations linked by commentary.
3. Y explains what it names ("Valens, who favored the party that ranked Christ, the Son, below God the Father"). X's Defense paragraph is too dense to follow heard once.

Faults in X:
- The Defense, the torture and the two years in prison are each told twice (paragraph 2, then later).
- "He refused and ignored his threats": unclear who "his" is.
- "had his sides scraped": awkward.
- The quote "the command to become approved money-changers is wholly shut out" and "drench him in the shame of the heretics" are opaque on one hearing.
- "Eusebius took Pamphilus's name as a surname": no reason given.
- The order jumps back: "Some ninety years after," then "Jerome had also found."
- It ends on Jerome, a later writer.

Faults in Y:
- "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit": hard to follow once.
- "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself" and "The rich gave to it at his urging" are praise told in the narrator's voice, with no witness named.
- "When the province was divided" and "making more bishops" are not explained.
- The council is told twice (paragraph 3 and the close).
- It ends on Benedict, not Basil.

X: C+. Y: B+.
