Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y tells one life in order, and its question (the emperor's pressure, the Spirit dispute) runs through to the point where it resolves. X reads more like a report about a source. "His account runs to a few sentences, about 140 words of Latin" is a comment on the record, not part of the story.
2. Y credits each witness where the sources differ: "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says their sister Macrina turned him, while the historian Rufinus says..."; "out of envy, Gregory of Nazianzus says".
3. X tells things twice and gives the ending before going back. Paragraph 2 previews Pilate, Tertullian and Sulpicius, and all three return later ("named Christus... put to death under Pontius Pilate", then the full quote).

Faults, X:
- The paragraph 2 preview gives the ending first.
- The Tertullian donkey-head section strays off the subject: "His quarrel was with another of Tacitus's histories."
- Sentences that only say what is missing: "naming no source for it"; "he names none of them"; "though he names no source".
- Clumsy repetition: "That account of the Jewish people... close to the Jewish people".

Faults, Y:
- The essence paragraph sets out the lodging, the rules and the council, then tells each one again later ("he died before a council... declared it").
- It ends on a later writer: "Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule by pointing his monks to Basil's".
- A listener cannot follow this on one hearing: "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit."
- The opening sentence is overloaded: "defending himself in a letter against men who, he wrote, were spreading lies about him".

Grades: X C, Y B.
