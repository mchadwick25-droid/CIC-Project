Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a strong image and names its subject at once ("called his book the Panarion, which means a medicine chest"). It then returns to that image at the end ("in his chest of cures: the church"). X opens on Noetus and leaves its subject unnamed until the second paragraph.
2. Y credits sources where they differ: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead". Its scenes run on what people did ("hold his mouth shut"; "He had not, and had judged them by report").
3. X is dense and out of order. Its second paragraph summarizes the whole life, so the quarrel, the book and the exile are each told twice.

X faults: "In 235 he was sent to Sardinia" (para 2) repeats "In 235... were deported to Sardinia"; "Rome kept his day among its martyrs" comes back as "kept them both on 13 August"; the narrator corrects the source ("Yet that split came later"), and the shift from "Novatus" to "Novatian" will confuse a listener; "The high priesthood meant..." is a tacked-on gloss; "some keeping the moon's fourteenth day" is never explained; "stood against them for the truth" is close to praise told in the narrator's voice; the opener "He said that Christ was the Father" leaves "He" unclear; the story ends on a later writer (Damasus).

Y faults: paragraph 3 tells the ending first ("Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea... In old age he carried the same fight") and paragraph 15 then tells it again; "took his book as their list" is the narrator's own judgment; "about 375" may rest on a modern reckoning; "held by people led astray ever since Adam" is awkward; the preface quote's "fall into it" has no clear referent.

Grades: X C, Y B+.
