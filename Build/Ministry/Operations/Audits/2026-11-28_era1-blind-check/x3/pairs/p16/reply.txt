X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X opens on a scene from the record, its own words ("like a man roused from deep sleep..."), and then states the essence plainly. Y opens on a report ("Tatian says he had been initiated..."), so there is no moment to hold the listener.
2. X credits sources where they differ ("His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says... while the historian Rufinus says..."). Y credits too, but it adds narrator guesses: "so he meant the writings of Moses and the prophets"; "Eusebius, about 150 years after the break."
3. X mostly tells one story in order. Y jumps around: his conversion, then his birth, then the ending in paragraph 2 ("In the 400s one bishop put away the copies"), which comes back in the last paragraph.

Faults, X:
- The council is told twice: "he died before a council... declared it in its creed," then "In 381, after his death, a council..."
- The lodging is told twice (paragraphs 2 and 7).
- "held them equal": unclear what "them" refers to.
- The narrator gives Gregory's praise and scenes as fact: "greeted them as brothers and tended them himself"; "The whole city poured around Basil as he died."

Faults, Y:
- Too many names: Rhodon, Crescens, Valentinus, Marcion, Epiphanius, Ephrem, Homer.
- "A later Roman teacher gives Tatian's reason": this witness is never named.
- "Theodoret, bishop of Cyrrhus under Antioch": "under Antioch" is not explained.
- The long quote ("I was won over..." three times) does work the narrator could do.
- It ends on a later writer, not on Tatian.

Grades: X B+, Y C-.
