Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y has the stronger opening. It starts on a real line from the record: "like a man roused from deep sleep, I turned my eyes..." and then states the essence plainly. X opens well too, but its first sentence carries a long clause about Eusebius ("who tells most of Origen's life about two generations after his death...") that slows it down.
2. Y credits disagreement cleanly in one place: "The old writers differ on what turned him... Gregory of Nyssa says... Rufinus says." X handles Demetrius well ("Here the old sources disagree") but loads up names: Chaeremon, Cornutus, Philip, Gallus, Pamphilus, Socrates and the Arians, well past six.
3. Y's quotes do work the narrator can't: "Whom, then, will you wash?" X's On First Principles quote is long and abstract for one hearing.

Faults, X: told twice ("He set the Hebrew text beside the Greek translations" in paragraph 2, then the columns again in paragraph 7). "and they say he often stood by" gives a vague witness. The Philocalia paragraph is a detour. It ends on a later council, not on Origen. Paragraph 9 is dense.

Faults, Y: told twice ("he died before a council... declared it" in paragraph 3, then "In 381, after his death, a council..."). "held them equal" and "attacked these" have unclear referents. "His accusers" are never explained. "tended them himself" is uncredited praise. The two prayer forms are hard to follow by ear. Two Gregorys invite confusion. It ends on Benedict, a later writer.

Grades: X C, Y B.
