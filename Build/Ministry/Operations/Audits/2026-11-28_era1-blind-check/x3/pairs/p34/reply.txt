Y is better, by a clear margin.

Reasons:
1. Y tells one life in order, with a live question carried through: the official's threat, then "a man who owns only rags and a few books cannot be robbed." X goes back and forth between a cosmology, a quarrel and a list of books.
2. Y credits each witness where they differ ("The old writers differ on what turned him... Rufinus says") and explains what it names ("the party that ranked Christ, the Son, below God the Father"). X is mostly crediting, so it reads as a report about the record: "Irenaeus wrote five books against them... Tertullian wrote a book... and Hippolytus a long work."
3. Y's quotes do work the narrator's words cannot ("Whom, then, will you wash?").

X faults: things told twice: the cosmology (paragraphs 2 and 9), the two branches ("carried the school on in two branches" / "the school split into an Italian branch"), and "faith in one God who made the world" twice. "Under one bishop and stayed until the time of a third" is vague. "A bud of Valentinus's" is unclear. The list-of-books paragraph. The why-it-matters link stays thin.

Y faults: the ending and the lodging are told twice (the council in paragraphs 3 and 17, the lodging in 3 and 13). "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself" and "The whole city poured around Basil" are praise given in the narrator's voice, with no witness named. The doxology sentence ("with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit") is hard to follow in one hearing. It ends on Benedict, a later writer, not on Basil.

Grades: X C, Y B.
