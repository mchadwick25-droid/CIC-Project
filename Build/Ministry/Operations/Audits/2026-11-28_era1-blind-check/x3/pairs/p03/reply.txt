Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a scene, the fugitive "trembling under the altar" while John preaches, and ends on John himself: "Glory to God for all things." X opens well on the "marks of Jesus", but it ends on a later council: "in 692 a council set its seal on the canons".
2. Y's story moves forward through cause: the grudge, the Oak, the statue, exile. X's middle is a list of rulings: "The tortured would fast forty more days... those jailed without torture would wait a year more... Those who paid..."
3. X is stronger on rigor, which narrows the gap. It credits all three accounts of the Melitian split (the bishops' letter, Athanasius, "Epiphanius... some sixty years later").

Faults in X:
- It goes back to the Easter writing: "In that Easter writing, a sermon on repentance".
- Melitius is introduced twice: "one of his own bishops, Melitius" and then "Melitius, a bishop of Upper Egypt".
- The year 311 is given twice.
- "about 300" may rest on a modern reckoning.
- The quote "as if Maximinus had ordered it" is unclear heard once.
- The clergy sentence runs past 30 words.

Faults in Y:
- The essence gives away the ending, which is then told again: "came back only when his body came home" / "those who had left the church... came back".
- "He preached plainly about the poor" is vague.
- One sentence is nested: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk".
- "they tell it differently": the "they" is unclear.
- No reason is given for "Eutropius made Theophilus... ordain him".
- The Gothic general thread leads nowhere.
- "John was silenced" is unexplained.

Grades: Y B, X C.
