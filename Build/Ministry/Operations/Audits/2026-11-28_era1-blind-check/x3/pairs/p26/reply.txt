X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X opens on the subject's own words, "medicine chest... antidotes," then states the essence plainly and follows one quarrel, against Origen, through to its end: "He sailed for Cyprus and died at sea." Y has no throughline. It is a file of remarks about two lines, broken up by biography ("His father, in his own telling, was an army officer").
2. X credits each witness where they differ: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead..." It keeps the charges in their witnesses' voices ("he acted from the fear of God, he explains").
3. Y tells things twice. Paragraph 2 gives away Tertullian ("Christians complained... 'Chrestians'") and Bede ("A Christian chronicle set that line down") before telling each again in full at the end.

Faults in X: "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea..." is told in the essence and again in the second-last paragraph. The curtain episode, "In a village church he found a curtain," has no time or place, so the order blurs. "Then Theophilus" is unclear in time. "He wrote the Panarion about 375" may rest on an editor's reckoning.

Faults in Y: the opening sentence runs about 45 words. There are absence-only sentences: "He names no charge, number or kind of punishment," and "there he names no Christians." The Tertullian line is muddled: "names him on other matters... without naming Suetonius." "Chrestus... was also a real name" is left hanging. The ending on Bede is a later writer, not the subject. The essence is never stated plainly.

X: B+
Y: C-
