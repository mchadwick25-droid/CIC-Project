X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X has a story question it carries through: the Spirit dispute, from "he defended the Holy Spirit's place" to the 381 creed. Y has no thread. It is a set of "he wrote" reports.
2. X credits sources where they differ and keeps it short: "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says their sister Macrina turned him, while the historian Rufinus says..." It also keeps blame in its witness's voice: "out of envy, Gregory of Nazianzus says."
3. Y tells things twice. The fear of philosophy comes up three times ("answered Christians who... feared Greek philosophy"; "feared philosophy as children fear masks"; "His answer to the believers who feared philosophy"), and Jerome comes up twice (paragraphs 2 and 15).

X faults: the paragraph "The old writers differ on what turned him" breaks the order of the story. The lodging is told twice ("built a great lodging"; "building a lodging for strangers"). "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself" is praise with no witness named. It ends on a later writer: "Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule..." "The emperor's highest official" is vague.

Y faults: after the essence it goes back in time ("was trained in the Scriptures at Alexandria under his teacher"). The Valentinus passage is a side trip, and "who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning" is clumsy. "Jerome's critic" and "Alexander, a bishop" are never placed or explained. The close is a later writer's praise: "What in them is unlearned?" "Some asked, he wrote" reads as a report about the record. The story's risk under Severus is dropped.

Grades: X B+, Y C.
