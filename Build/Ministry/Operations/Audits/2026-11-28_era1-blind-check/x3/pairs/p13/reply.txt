X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X opens on a scene in the subject's own words, with his name in: "like a man roused from deep sleep, I turned my eyes..." Y opens on Noetus, and Hippolytus first appears in the third sentence.
2. X credits its sources where they differ, and does it cleanly: "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says their sister Macrina turned him, while the historian Rufinus says..." Y credits too ("In Hippolytus's telling, the only account..."), but it uses about ten names (Noetus, Zephyrinus, Callistus, Pontianus, Eusebius, Jerome, Damasus, Novatus, Novatian), which is too many for one hearing.
3. X explains what it names in plain words: "rhetoric, the art of public speaking"; "The first set the Spirit beside the Father and the Son."

Faults in X: it tells things twice. The lodging comes in paragraph 2 and again in "building a lodging for strangers...". The council comes in "died before a council... declared it" and again in "In 381... a council at Constantinople". The rules for monks are also told twice. It ends on a later writer ("Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule...") rather than on Basil. "Basil answered by making more bishops" does not say why that answered the rival.

Faults in Y: the exile is told twice ("In 235 he was sent to Sardinia" and "In 235, Rome's list of bishops records..."). The quarrel is spread across paragraphs 3, 5, 9 and 11, and the Easter table breaks the order. "The split of Novatus" against "Novatian's party" will confuse a listener. "The high priesthood meant..." is a gloss added after the quote instead of worked into it. "A later bishop there" is vague.

X: B. Y: C.
