Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a vivid line, the "medicine chest" with "venom and poison... antidotes", and closes on the same image ("in his chest of cures: the church"). X opens well, but it ends on Theodoret, a later writer, not on Tatian.
2. Y credits each source where they differ: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead". X stacks witnesses into a list of citations ("Irenaeus says... Eusebius... makes... Epiphanius... says") and loses the thread of one story.
3. Y tells one quarrel in order, from Jerusalem to Theophilus to Constantinople to his death at sea. X jumps from the conversion to a summary, then back to his birth, then to Crescens, then to the break.

X faults: the essence paragraph tells the break and the ending ("left the church after Justin died", "put away the copies"), and both are told again later. There are too many names: "Rhodon" does nothing, and nor do Ephrem or Homer. One line is the narrator's guess: "so he meant the writings of Moses and the prophets". One sentence only says what is missing: "did not know how Tatian combined the Gospels". The essence paragraph reads as a run of short, single-act sentences. "A later Roman teacher" is left vague.

Y faults: the preview tells things twice ("from Augustine to a council at Nicaea" comes back in full at the end). "He had not, and had judged them by report" is told as fact, with no witness named. "Led astray ever since Adam" is murky. The essence paragraph jumps ahead before the story is told in order.

Grades: X C, Y B+.
