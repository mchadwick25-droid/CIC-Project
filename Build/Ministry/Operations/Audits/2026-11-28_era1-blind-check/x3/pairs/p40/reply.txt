Verdict: Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y's opening holds the listener. The hunger and the shocking council speech ("old women, infants and old men") raise a real question, and the deacon's "like a father and a doctor" pays it off. X opens on a letter's reflection ("like a man roused from deep sleep"), which is quieter.
2. Y gives credit where witnesses differ and lets them speak: Colman against Wilfrid, then "Bede doubted the Irish claim." X credits Nyssa, Rufinus and Nazianzus well on the conversion, but it does so in a list.
3. X tells events twice and jumps around. The lodging comes in paragraphs 2 and 6, and the council in paragraphs 2 and 7.

X faults: "he died before a council ... declared it" and then "In 381, after his death, a council" (told twice); "a great lodging for the poor" repeated; "Gregory says" is unclear with two Gregories; "The whole city poured around Basil" and "greeted them as brothers and tended them himself" are praise not credited to a witness; the Valens sentence is tangled; "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit" is too dense to follow on one hearing; it ends on a later writer (Benedict).

Y faults: "used the council's vote to slip out almost all the besieged" gives the outcome before the siege is told (told twice); "Of the siege, Eusebius tells one of his deeds, as others told it to him" is a stock how-we-know line; the siege is never explained (who, why); "whose bishop, the same deacon" is confusing; there are too many names (Hild is not needed); "must not be counted from a new moon before the spring equinox" is dense.

Grades: X C, Y B-.
