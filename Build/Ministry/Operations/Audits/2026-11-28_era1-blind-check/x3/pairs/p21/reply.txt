Y is somewhat better overall.

Reasons:
1. Y does the rigor work Part 1 asks for most clearly. Where sources differ, it credits each one: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." It also keeps the enemy's charges in full: "sold church property, insulted the clergy, struck a man and eaten greedily alone."
2. Y carries a real question, the bishop against the court, through to its end. X's three messages are arranged by theme, so its sense of time is weaker.
3. Both openings are strong. Y's "As he lay trembling under the altar" scene grabs a listener faster than X's quote.

Faults, X:
- It tells the afterlife twice: "Polycarp of Smyrna gathered the letters... and sent them on," and then again in the second-to-last paragraph.
- The quote "Even when they are treated well, they grow all the worse" is unclear and has no plain sense next to it.
- The gloss inside the harp image is awkward: "the elders, the council who led a church with its bishop, were fitted to him."
- "They thought the same of his own sufferings and those of others like him" is muddy.
- "which he had left in chains" is ambiguous.
- "later bishops quoted them for centuries" reads like a summing-up.

Faults, Y:
- It tells the ending early and then again: "the followers who left the church over his fall came back only when his body came home."
- "his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile" is the narrator's own causal summary, which the councils complicate.
- "they tell it differently" has an unclear subject.
- It carries a heavy load of names, at least seven.
- No reason is given for "Eutropius made Theophilus... ordain him."

Grades: X: B-. Y: B+.
