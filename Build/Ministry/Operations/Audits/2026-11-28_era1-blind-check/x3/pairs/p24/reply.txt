X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X tells one life in order, with stakes that build (Eutropius, the Oak, Eudoxia, exile, death). Y spends much of its length reporting on the record ("about 140 words of Latin", "the details of these deaths rest on him alone") and drifts into Tertullian's donkey-head quarrel, which is not Nero's story.
2. X credits its witnesses where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently"; "Palladius, who wrote John's life and took his side, says".
3. X's quotes do work the narrator can't ("Do not neglect him when he is naked"; "Glory to God for all things").

X faults: paragraph 2 gives the ending early and then tells it again at the close ("came back only when his body came home" / "those who had left the church... came back"). "His sermons were read for centuries" is a legacy line. "Both kinds were kept and read" is flat. "where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body" is clumsy. "they tell it differently" has an unclear "they". "John was silenced" is vague. Eutropius is dropped after the opening.

Y faults: paragraph 2 previews Christus, Tertullian and Sulpicius, then tells each again later. It has three missing-detail asides: "naming no source for it", "and he names none of them", "though he names no source". "Hatred of the human race" and "deadly superstition" are never explained. "The Jewish people" comes four times in two sentences. "Later, one Christian... and another" reads as a how-we-know sentence.
