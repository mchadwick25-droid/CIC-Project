Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a scene, names its subject at once ("Basil of Caesarea looked back"), and then states the essence plainly. X opens on an unnamed governor and only later says what Lactantius was.
2. Y credits each source where they differ: "The old writers differ on what turned him... Gregory of Nyssa says... Rufinus says". It also credits praise and blame: "out of envy, Gregory of Nazianzus says". X tells Jerome's report "In extreme old age, Lactantius taught Constantine's son Crispus" as plain fact.
3. Y tells one story in order. X drifts in its second half through the disputed book, Jerome, Lucifer and then Jerome again. It ends on a later writer: "Jerome called Lactantius the most eloquent man...".

X faults: the same idea told twice ("religion cannot be forced" / "Religion cannot be compelled"). Meta line: "He is the last on the tree of the Latin Apologists". Unclear speaker: "he says, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown'" (Jerome or Lactantius?). A muddy line: "answered a pagan with Scripture the man thought false". Too many names (Tertullian, Cyprian, Lucifer, Constantius, Crispus). The Lucifer paragraph is long, about a later writer, and pulls focus.

Y faults: the ending is given early ("he died before a council... declared it") and told again at the close. The lodging is described twice. "Moved against the churches that held them equal" has an unclear "them". Gregory's praise is told as narrator fact: "tended them himself", "The whole city poured around Basil". It ends on a later writer (Benedict).

X: C. Y: B+.
