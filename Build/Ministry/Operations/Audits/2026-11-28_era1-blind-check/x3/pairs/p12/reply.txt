X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X opens on one vivid moment ("As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit") and carries one conflict through to its end. Y has no story question; it drifts between themes.
2. X credits sources where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." It keeps the soldiers' motive in Palladius's voice ("In his telling").
3. Y tells things twice and out of order. Fear of philosophy comes up in paragraphs 2, 7 and 9, and Jerome in paragraphs 2 and 15. It ends on a later writer ("Jerome asked...") when that is not the point.

Faults in X:
- The essence paragraph gives away the ending early: "came back only when his body came home," which is then told again at the close.
- "He preached plainly about the poor, and his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile" squeezes the causes more than the record does.
- One nested sentence: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk."
- The Arian episode leads nowhere.
- "Since two councils had condemned him" reads as the narrator's reasoning.

Faults in Y:
- Repetition, as above, and "Many believers, he wrote, feared philosophy" restates paragraph 2.
- "under his teacher" leaves the teacher vague. "a teacher he opposed... by Clement's reckoning" is clumsy.
- The one-witness sentence ("In Eusebius's account, which holds most...") comes late and stands as its own sentence.
- "saved by their very nature" is not explained.
- "set Christian and philosophers' views" is awkward.

Grades: X B+, Y C-.
