Y is somewhat better overall.

Reasons:
1. Y follows one quarrel from start to end: the sanctuary law, enemies made, the Oak, the statue, exile, death, return. X's story question fades after Cyprian dies, and the closing paragraphs drift back into the book's purpose and how it begins.
2. Both credit sources where they differ, and Y does it with more at stake: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." X's "But Cyprian's own last letter says..." is good too, but it is a smaller point.
3. Y's opening scene carries the essence with it: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop... preached against him."

Faults, X:
- Paragraph 2 lists events it tells again later: "heard Cyprian's dream there, and was with him on his last night."
- "almost all we know of him is his own book" reads as a stock how-we-know line.
- "His book never gives his name" is unclear: whose "his"?
- "he sends readers to the court record" never says what that record is.
- The order breaks after the death, going back to "He began where Cyprian's faith began."

Faults, Y:
- The essence paragraph gives away the ending, which is then told again: "came back only when his body came home" / "those who had left the church... came back."
- "his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile" is the narrator's own causal summary.
- "His sermons were read for centuries" partly repeats "Both kinds were kept and read."
- "trembling" may be a detail added for effect.
- "Some thought" credits no source.
- In "They decked the altar," it is unclear who "they" are.

Grades: X C+, Y B.
