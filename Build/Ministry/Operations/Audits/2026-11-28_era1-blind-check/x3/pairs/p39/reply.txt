Y is somewhat better overall.

Reasons:
1. Y carries one question, the blunt bishop against the powerful, from the Eutropius scene to the exile. X drifts into a list of later readers ("Pamphilus... Socrates... Basil... Gregory also sent a friend the Philocalia").
2. Y credits its disputes cleanly: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." X does too ("Here the old sources disagree... Jerome, writing over a century later"), but sets it inside denser material.
3. Y is easier to follow by ear. X's paragraph on On First Principles is a long chain of abstractions followed by a long quote.

Faults in X:
- Told twice: "He set the Hebrew text beside the Greek translations" and later "He set them side by side in columns with the Hebrew."
- Too many names. "Chaeremon, a Stoic philosopher, and of Cornutus" are not needed.
- "His judge did all he could not to let him die" is ambiguous and could mislead, because the judge kept him alive to prolong the torture.
- The second sentence of the opening runs past 30 words.
- "fought over them for centuries" is a summing-up phrase.
- The ending piles reception onto reception.

Faults in Y:
- It tells the ending, then goes back to it, and so tells it twice: "came back only when his body came home" and later "those who had left the church over John's fall came back."
- "They decked the altar" has an unclear "they."
- "they tell it differently" has an unclear "they."
- The Gothic general episode never says how it bore on John's fall.

X: C+
Y: B
