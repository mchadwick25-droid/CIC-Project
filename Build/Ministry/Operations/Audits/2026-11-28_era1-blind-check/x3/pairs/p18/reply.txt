Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a real scene from the record and lets its question (the church's right of shelter, a bishop against power) run through to the end: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit." X opens on a claim, then a vague line: "He turned inward and asked how he might find the truth."
2. Y credits its sources where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." It also flags Palladius's bias ("took his side"). X stacks witnesses into a list of who-said-what (Irenaeus, Eusebius, Epiphanius, Theodoret).
3. Y keeps to about six named people. X names fourteen or more. Crescens and Rhodon ("At Rome Tatian taught Rhodon, a pupil from Asia") do nothing in the story.

Faults in X: paragraph 2 tells the end first and then goes back to his birth; the Diatessaron is named and the copies are "put away" before either is explained, and the copies are then told again in full at the end. "So he meant the writings of Moses" is the narrator guessing. "Irenaeus, his opponent" is loaded. "Eusebius... did not know how" is murky. The long quote reads as a chain of lists. The Ephrem line may misstate the record.

Faults in Y: "came back only when his body came home" gives away the ending, which is then told twice. "Some thought he was attacking" credits no source. "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk" is nested, and its "They" is unclear. "His sermons were read for centuries" is a later-legacy claim.

X: D+. Y: B+.
