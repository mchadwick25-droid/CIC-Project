Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a living scene, "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit," with real stakes. X's opening is strong too, but it then retells the same siege: "used the council's vote to slip out almost all the besieged" comes back later as "he saved almost all the besieged."
2. Y credits each witness where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently," and "In his telling, the soldiers hurried John on."
3. X's Easter section is dense for a listener ("must not be counted from a new moon before the spring equinox"; "an Easter past the moon's twentieth day"), and it ends on a later writer: "anyone skilled could very easily tell whether that was true."

X faults: the siege is told twice. "whose bishop, the same deacon, had died" gives a fact the story never set up. "Of the siege, Eusebius tells one of his deeds, as others told it to him" is a how-we-know sentence. The geography is confusing ("besieged quarter" / "palace quarter" / "free part"). The script names about nine people (Paul, Hild, Gregory, Colman...). "Rome's Easter" is never explained.

Y faults: the return of John's followers is told twice ("came back only when his body came home" / "those who had left the church... came back"). "Eutropius made Theophilus... ordain him" gives no reason at that point. "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk" is nested, and its "they" is unclear. The "they" in "they tell it differently" is also unclear. Keeping Easter "in the public baths" gets no reason.

Grades: X: C. Y: B+.
