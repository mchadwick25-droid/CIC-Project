Verdict: Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y is easier to follow on one hearing. Its sentences are short and linked, and its key quote lands as a concrete image: "It was not spiritual water but real water, the kind you can touch with your hand." X uses dense, long sentences ("Eusebius of Caesarea, who tells most of Origen's life about two generations after his death, from letters and from people who knew him, says...") and a long abstract quote from On First Principles that is hard to take in by ear.
2. Both credit their sources well where the sources differ. Y has Socrates against Sozomen on Theophilus's motive; X has Eusebius against Jerome on Demetrius. Y keeps its question alive (the fight over Origen) through to the end, while X drifts into a list of later readers.
3. Y closes on the subject's own words ("Christ's holy bride"). X ends on a later council.

Faults in X:
- It tells the same thing twice: "He set the Hebrew text beside the Greek translations," then the columns paragraph later.
- "fought over them for centuries" is a summing-up given early.
- "a presbyter, an elder, a priest of the church" piles up three glosses.
- "and of Cornutus" is never explained.
- The quote "All these I have spoken of hold false and impious opinions..." is too long and abstract to follow by ear.

Faults in Y:
- It tells the same thing twice: "from Augustine to a council at Nicaea... took his book as their list," and the same is retold in the closing paragraph.
- "He had not, and had judged them by report" is stated in the narrator's voice, with no witness credited.
- "against church rules" is the narrator's judgment, not credited to a source.
- "A book written to answer the council's argument" is vague.

Grades: X: C. Y: B.
