X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X has a story with a question carried to its end. The Origen fight builds through Jerusalem, Theophilus and Constantinople to "He sailed for Cyprus and died at sea." Y is a set of topics with no risk or choice carried through.
2. X credits sources where they differ, and does it well: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead..."
3. X explains what it names: "deacons, the clergy who serve under priests" and "the sacraments, the church's holy rites."

X faults:
- Paragraph 2 tells the ending and then tells it again: "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea..." and "carried the same fight to Jerusalem and Constantinople" both come back later.
- "He had not, and had judged them by report" is one historian's account told as fact.
- "against church rules" is a judgment in the narrator's voice.
- "He wrote the Panarion about 375" may be an editor's date.
- "led astray ever since Adam" is unclear.

Y faults:
- The fear of philosophy is told three times (paragraphs 2, 7 and 9). Jerome is told twice ("Jerome, charged with defiling the church" and paragraph 6).
- It does not move in order. Valentinus breaks into the philosophy thread.
- "In Eusebius's account, which holds most of what is told of his life" reads like a stock how-we-know sentence.
- "who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning" is clumsy and nested.
- "two blessed men... one of them" leaves the other vague. "Severus" and "Alexander, a bishop" are never placed.
- "Christian and philosophers' views" is ungrammatical.
- It ends on a later writer, Jerome, not on Clement.

Grades: X B, Y C-.
