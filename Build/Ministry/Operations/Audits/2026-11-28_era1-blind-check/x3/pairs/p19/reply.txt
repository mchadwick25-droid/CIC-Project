Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y opens on a vivid line with the name in its first words ("I am bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers"). X's opening is nested and slow: "defending himself in a letter against men who, he wrote, were spreading lies about him".
2. Y gives the listener a clear map ("Three messages run through them") and explains what it names ("one thanksgiving meal of one bread and one cup"). X's doctrine is hard to follow by ear: "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit."
3. Both credit their sources well. Y has "as the church historian Eusebius wrote two centuries later" and "by report". X has "The old writers differ on what turned him", crediting each writer. X, though, has two Gregorys, Macrina, Rufinus, Valens and Benedict, which is too many names for one hearing.

Faults in X: the lodging is told twice (paragraphs 2 and 7) and so is the council ("died before a council" and "In 381, after his death"). The order breaks: "His accusers were filling the governor's ears" has no time. "a rival bishop claimed part of his churches" gives no reason. "the emperor's highest official" is vague. The ending rests on a later writer: "Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule".

Faults in Y: Polycarp sending the letters on is told twice (paragraphs 2 and 7). "later bishops quoted them for centuries" is vague. "the elders, the council who led a church with its bishop" is an awkward gloss. "They thought the same of his own sufferings" is unclear. "a port on the coast" is thin. The three themes put the journey out of order.

X: C+
Y: B
