X is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. X keeps one question alive: how to take back the fallen, and the split it caused. It runs from "the marks of Jesus in their bodies" to Nicaea. Y is a run of episodes: conversion, rules, quarrel, prefect, lodging, doxology.
2. X credits each witness where the sources differ: "Athanasius... an enemy of the Melitians, says..."; "Epiphanius... writing some sixty years later, says...". Y does this well too ("Gregory of Nazianzus says... out of envy"). But it lets praise go uncredited: "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself"; "The rich gave to it at his urging."
3. X glosses its terms well: "The marks were the scars of their torture"; "a place in the prayers and in the body and blood of Christ."

X faults:
- "At the fourth Easter of the persecution" is unclear when heard once.
- The canons paragraph is too dense to follow in one hearing.
- Melitius is introduced twice: "one of his own bishops" and later "a bishop of Upper Egypt, in the south".
- "as if Maximinus had ordered it" is a puzzling quote.
- It ends on a later council ("in 692 a council set its seal").

Y faults:
- The lodging is told twice: "built a great lodging" and "building a lodging for strangers".
- Two Gregorys are hard to tell apart by ear, and "Macrina" is named once and never acts.
- "the churches that held them equal": "them" is ambiguous.
- "When the province was divided" gives no reason.
- The doxology paragraph ("with the Son, together with... through the Son, in...") is hard to take in by ear.
- The essence tells the 381 ending early, and the script closes on Rufinus and Benedict.

X: B+
Y: B-
