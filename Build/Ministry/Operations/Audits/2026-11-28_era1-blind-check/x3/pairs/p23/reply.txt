X is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. X tells one life with a live quarrel that carries through to the end ("He would not pray with John unless John condemned Origen's books"). Y often reads as a report about a source ("His account runs to a few sentences, about 140 words of Latin").
2. X credits each side where the sources differ: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead..." Y does the same with Sulpicius ("Where Tacitus called the dead guilty").
3. X explains what it names in the sentence itself ("deacons, the clergy who serve under priests"). Y's Tertullian section drifts to a different quarrel ("worshiped a donkey's head") that does not serve the Nero story.

X faults: paragraph 2 gives the ending early and then tells it again ("Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea"; "carried the same fight to Jerusalem and Constantinople"). "He had not, and had judged them by report" is told in the narrator's voice, with no witness named. There are too many names (Origen, Jerome, Theophilus, Socrates, Sozomen, John Chrysostom, the Long Brothers). "Then Theophilus..." is an abrupt join. The close comes near a summing-up ("set one thing more").

Y faults: paragraph 2 gives things before they happen ("named Christus... under Pontius Pilate", then quoted again later; Tertullian and Sulpicius are trailed and then retold). "the fullest account from outside the church" is a modern judgment. There are sentences that only say what is missing ("naming no source for it", "he names none of them", "though he names no source"). "the Jewish people" is repeated clumsily. "the details of these deaths rest on him alone" is a stock how-we-know line.

X: B-. Y: C+.
