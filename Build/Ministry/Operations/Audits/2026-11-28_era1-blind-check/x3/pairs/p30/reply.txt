X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X has a real story with stakes, told in order: the asylum irony, Antioch, his election, the Oak, the statue, his exile, his death. Y reads like a report on a book.
2. X credits sources where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." It also explains each charge and each exile. Y flags the doubt over authorship well ("whether it is Lactantius's is disputed"), but its last third drifts to later writers.
3. X ends on the subject: "those who had left the church over John's fall came back." Y ends on Jerome's judgment and a Lucifer digression.

X faults:
- Paragraph 2 gives away the ending ("came back only when his body came home"), then tells it again at the close.
- "As he lay trembling" may be detail added for effect.
- "they tell it differently": "they" is unclear.
- "His sermons were read for centuries" is a summing-up claim.
- "They decked the altar, where the bread... body, with gold cups" is overloaded.
- Eutropius's story is dropped after the opening.

Y faults:
- The idea that religion cannot be forced is told twice ("cannot be forced, since it lives only in the will"; "Religion cannot be compelled").
- "He is the last on the tree of the Latin Apologists" is meta.
- "Most of his life outside his books rests on Jerome" is a how-we-know sentence.
- "Yet he asked who could stop him, Jerome, from reading" is garbled.
- "Lucius Caecilius" is never explained.
- "He called his Divine" is awkward.
- The Lucifer paragraph is long and pulls the end off the subject.
- "In extreme old age" is Jerome's claim, told as fact.

Grades: X B+, Y C+.
