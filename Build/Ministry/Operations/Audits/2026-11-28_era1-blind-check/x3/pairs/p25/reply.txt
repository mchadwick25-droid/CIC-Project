Verdict: X is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. X opens on one vivid moment in the subject's own words ("like a man roused from deep sleep, I turned my eyes...") and then states the essence plainly. Y opens on a cluttered list (Troy's taxes, German envoys at the theater) before it reaches its line.
2. X credits its sources where they differ: "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says... while the historian Rufinus says...", and "out of envy, Gregory of Nazianzus says".
3. X tells one life roughly in order. Y previews its ending ("Later, Christians complained... A Christian chronicle set that line down") and then tells both events again in its last two paragraphs. It also drops in the biography (paragraph 5) and Acts (paragraph 7) out of sequence. It reads as a report about the record.

Faults in X:
- Paragraph 2 gives away the end ("he died before a council... declared it"), and the lodging and the council are told twice.
- "moved against the churches that held them equal": "them" is unclear.
- "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit" is hard to follow in one hearing.
- "The whole city poured around Basil" and "tended them himself" are praise that is not credited to a witness.
- It ends on a later writer: "Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule..."

Faults in Y:
- Told twice: Tertullian and Bede.
- "He names no charge, number or kind of punishment" only says what is missing.
- "the name in his first line" has an unclear "his".
- "He has one more line like it... only the second names the Christians" is confusing.
- It ends on Bede, a later writer.

Grades: X B-, Y C.
