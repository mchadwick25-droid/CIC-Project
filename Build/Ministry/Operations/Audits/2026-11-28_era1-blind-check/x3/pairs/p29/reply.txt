X is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. X tells one story in order, from the book to Jerusalem, then Theophilus, then Constantinople, then death at sea. After paragraph 6, Y breaks into pieces: the disputed book, then "In extreme old age, Lactantius taught Constantine's son Crispus", then Jerome, then Lucifer, then Jerome again.
2. X credits each witness where they differ: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead". Y blurs who is speaking: "He was called to Bithynia to teach, he says, and was teaching when 'the temple of God was overthrown.'" Is that Jerome or Lactantius?
3. X explains what it names ("deacons, the clergy who serve under priests"; "sacraments, the church's holy rites"). It ends on the subject's own image of the church.

X faults:
- Paragraph 3 tells the later story before it happens, and then tells it again: "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea... took his book as their list"; "carried the same fight to Jerusalem and Constantinople". Calling the council a user of his "list" is also loose.
- "He wrote the Panarion about 375" may rest on an editor's reckoning.
- "He had not, and had judged them by report" reads as the narrator's verdict and does not credit a witness.

Y faults:
- The same point is told twice: "religion cannot be forced, since it lives only in the will" and "Religion cannot be compelled, he wrote".
- "He is the last on the tree of the Latin Apologists" is a report about the tree, not the story.
- The ending rests on a later writer: "Jerome called Lactantius the most eloquent man..."
- Some sentences are overlong or awkward: "Lactantius was teaching Latin public speaking at Nicomedia..." and "He called his Divine, a full course on..."
- The Constantine dream is told without saying which witness holds it.

Grades: X B, Y C+.
