Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y carries one question, flight against faithfulness and unity against Rome, all the way to a death scene: "Cyprian said, 'Thanks be to God.' ... he covered his eyes with his own hand." X breaks into separate episodes. The Jerome's-brother ordination and the curtain read as asides.
2. Y explains its terms inside the action: "baptism, the washing that makes a Christian, which is given once." X's gloss is clumsy: "a wrong belief held by people led astray ever since Adam."
3. Both credit witnesses well. X has "The historian Socrates says... Sozomen, says instead". Y has "Pontius, who writes as an admirer" and "Jerome, writing long after".

X faults:
- Too many names (Adam, Augustine, Origen, Jerome, Theophilus, Socrates, Sozomen, Long Brothers, John Chrysostom).
- Augustine and Nicaea are told twice, in the essence and again at the end.
- "He had not, and had judged them by report" is uncredited, though it rests on one historian.
- "A book written to answer the council's argument" is vague.
- One overlong sentence: "The monks of Jerome's monastery had complained for a year..."

Y faults:
- "His deacon, Pontius" opens on a pronoun.
- "would have run from that too, had fear made him run before" is tangled.
- "as that rival rose" and "Afterwards a dreadful plague" are vague on time.
- "an enemy of the Roman gods whose blood would make the law hold" is awkward.
- "presbyter" is not glossed.
- "Communion meant..." is dropped in abruptly.
- One overlong sentence: "So he withdrew for a while, thinking less..."
- It ends on Augustine, a later writer.

Grades: X B-, Y B+.
