Y is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. Y keeps one question alive (unity against Rome) and carries it to the end: "We judge no one... None of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops" leads into Augustine's "hold with us to his example of keeping unity." X drifts from topic to topic.
2. Y credits its witnesses with care and keeps the conflict: "His deacon, Pontius, who writes as an admirer"; the Roman letter's "hired man" sits beside Pontius's defence.
3. Y explains what it names in plain words: "baptism, the washing that makes a Christian, which is given once."

X faults:
- The 47-word opening sentence is too much to take in on one hearing.
- "The old writers differ on what turned him" goes back to his conversion after the essence.
- The prayer forms are hard to follow heard once: "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit."
- Praise is told in the narrator's voice: "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself"; "The whole city poured around Basil as he died."
- "His accusers" are never explained.
- "making more bishops" is given with no reason.
- It ends on a later writer: "Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule..."

Y faults:
- "Jerome, writing long after, says..." opens the essence by going back to before his baptism.
- "had done rightly" sits uneasily beside the "hired man" warning.
- "Afterwards" and "Utica, another city" are vague.
- "heretics" and "presbyter" are not glossed.
- "Communion meant..." is wedged in before the quote.
- It has many names (Decius, Novatian, two Julians, Sextus).
- It ends on Augustine rather than on Cyprian.

Grades: X C+, Y B+.
