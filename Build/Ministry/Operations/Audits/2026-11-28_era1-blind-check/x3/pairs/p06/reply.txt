Verdict: X is somewhat better.

Reasons:
1. X has the stronger opening. "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop... climbed into the pulpit" is a real scene with irony built in, because Eutropius is undone by his own law. Y opens on sourcing: "His deacon, Pontius, who writes as an admirer, says..." That reads like a report about the record.
2. X handles sources that differ cleanly and in the narrator's voice: "Socrates says... A later bishop... summed up the meeting's own written record, and they tell it differently." Y stacks attributions ("he says", "he wrote", "Jerome... says"), so much of it sounds like citation.
3. X carries one question all the way through: the preacher against the powerful. Y spreads across persecution, plague, unity and baptism. Its unity thread mostly holds them together.

Faults in X:
- It tells the ending twice: "came back only when his body came home" and later "those who had left the church over John's fall came back."
- One sentence is nested and hard to follow by ear: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk."
- No reason is given for "Eutropius made Theophilus... ordain him."
- The Arian church thread is dropped. Nothing comes of it.
- "kept Easter in the public baths" has no reason attached.
- "Some thought" is vague about who thought it.

Faults in Y:
- The essence names its source awkwardly: "Jerome, writing long after, says he had once been a teacher..."
- The death is told early ("in 258 he was put to the sword") and then told again.
- This is confusing heard once: "he did suffer afterwards, and would have run from that too, had fear made him run before."
- This is clumsy: "an enemy of the Roman gods whose blood would make the law hold."
- It has too many names (Jerome, Decius, the Julians).
- It ends on Augustine, a later writer, not on Cyprian.

Grades: X B+, Y B-.
