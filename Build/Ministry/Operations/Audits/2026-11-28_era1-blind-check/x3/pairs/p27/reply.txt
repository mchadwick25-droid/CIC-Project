Y is clearly better.

Reasons:
1. Y tells one story, and it holds a risk from start to finish: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop... climbed into the pulpit." X reads as a report about the record. It goes from the line, to a preview, to a biography, to Acts, to Nero, to Tertullian, and then to Bede.
2. Y credits its sources where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." It also keeps Palladius's partisan claim in his own voice.
3. X tells things twice. Paragraph 2 gives away "Chrestians" and "A Christian chronicle set that line down," and both come back later in full.

X faults:
- "He has one more line like it, under Nero" comes before the Nero passage.
- Paragraph 5 is biography dropped into the middle of the story.
- "so he wrote after Domitian's death" is the narrator's own inference.
- "Superstition was his word for a religion he despised" is the narrator's gloss.
- "He names no charge, number or kind of punishment" only says what is missing.
- "Tacitus tells at length..." is left hanging.
- "it was also a real name" sounds like a modern note.
- It ends on a later writer, Bede.

Y faults:
- Paragraph 2 gives away the ending: "came back only when his body came home."
- "They decked the altar": it is unclear who "they" are.
- The Arian episode never connects to his fall.
- "the empress took this as aimed at her" is not credited to any witness.
- "followers... met apart" is said again at the close.
- "soon called back" gives no reason.

Grades: X: D. Y: B.
