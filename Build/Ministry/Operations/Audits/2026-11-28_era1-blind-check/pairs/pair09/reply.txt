**Y is clearly better than X.**

**Why Y wins**
- **Opening.** Y opens on one scene from the record and brings the name in naturally: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit and preached against him." X opens strongly too, but its second paragraph turns into a report about the record: "His account runs to a few sentences, about 140 words of Latin."
- **Rigor.** Y credits its sources where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come… they tell it differently." It keeps the soldiers' motive in Palladius's voice ("In his telling…").
- **Order.** Y tells one story mostly in order. X previews Christus, Pilate, Tertullian and Sulpicius in its second paragraph, then tells each again later.

**Faults in X**
- Things told twice: "named Christus… put to death under Pontius Pilate" comes back as a quote two paragraphs later, and the second paragraph previews Tertullian and Sulpicius before they are told in full.
- Sentences that only say what is missing: "naming no source for it", "and he names none of them" and "though he names no source".
- A digression: the Tertullian passage is about a "donkey's head" and Jerusalem's temple, not about Nero's victims.
- A run of short one-act sentences: "First those who confessed were seized. Then…"

**Faults in Y**
- The ending is told early and then told again: "the followers who left the church over his fall came back only when his body came home" (second paragraph) repeats the final paragraph.
- A motive in the narrator's voice with no witness named: "the empress took this as aimed at her."
- An unclear "they": "summed up the meeting's own written record, and they tell it differently".
- A clumsy sentence: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk."
- A vague line: "John was silenced."

**Grades:** X: C. Y: B+.
