**X is clearly better than Y.** X grade: B+. Y grade: C+.

**Why X wins**
1. **Opening.** X opens on a real scene that sets up its question: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit and preached against him." Y opens on a quote with no context ("old women, infants and old men"). It misleads until the twist comes three paragraphs later, so it works as a held-back reveal.
2. **Rigor.** X credits its witnesses where they differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come… they tell it differently." It keeps Palladius's partisan claims in his voice ("In his telling…").
3. **One story.** X tells one story in order. Y splits into two loosely joined halves, the siege and then the Easter rule, and runs well past six names. Gregory and Hild do nothing in the story.

**Faults in X**
- The ending is told twice: "came back only when his body came home" in paragraph 2, then again in the last line.
- The paragraph-2 summary gets ahead of the story ("led to his exile").
- One tangled sentence: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk." It is also unclear who "They" is.
- "John was silenced" is vague.

**Faults in Y**
- The siege is told twice: "used the council's vote to slip out almost all the besieged" early on, then again in full later. The vote is also retold.
- "whose bishop, the same deacon, had died" is confusing.
- The Easter rule is too dense to follow by ear: "must not be counted from a new moon before the spring equinox"; "an Easter past the moon's twentieth day."
- "a Latin Easter book under Anatolius's name" hints at doubt about who wrote it but never explains it.
- It ends on a later writer, Bede, and its last line is unclear: "anyone skilled could very easily tell whether that was true."
