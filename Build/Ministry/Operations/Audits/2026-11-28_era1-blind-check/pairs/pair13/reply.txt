**Verdict: X is clearly better.**

**Why X wins**
1. **It holds one story with a real question.** It follows exile, then the dream, then the delay, then the death, then the book. It credits the sources where they clash: "Pontius says he turned down friends' offers... But Cyprian's own last letter says..." Wonders stay in their witness's voice: "Pontius read the one day as a year more of life."
2. **It ends well, on the subject's own words:** "I rejoice much, far too much, in his glory. Yet I grieve more that I remained behind."
3. **Y reads as a survey.** It has eight-plus names, two of them Gregorys, which will confuse a second-language listener. It ends on a later writer: "Benedict of Nursia closed his own Rule by pointing his monks to Basil's..."

**X's faults**
- Paragraph 2 tells events before they happen: "heard Cyprian's dream there, and was with him on his last night."
- Two passages report on the book instead of telling the story: "He began where Cyprian's faith began..." and "he sends readers to the court record." The second is close to a sentence that only says what is missing.
- "The book was copied with Cyprian's own writings" adds little.

**Y's faults**
- It opens on a flashback ("Years later, defending himself..."). The essence paragraph then sums up the whole life, so the rule, the lodging and the Holy Spirit are each told twice.
- The prayer paragraph is too dense to follow by ear: "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit."
- Two lines of praise from Gregory are told in the narrator's voice: "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself" and "The whole city poured around Basil as he died."
- "filling the governor's ears" is an idiom that second-language listeners may miss.
- The ending, with Rufinus' translation and Benedict, drifts away from Basil.

**Grades: X B+, Y C+.**
