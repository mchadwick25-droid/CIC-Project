**Verdict: X is somewhat better than Y.**

**Reasons**
1. **X has the stronger opening and image.** It opens on its own book's preface: "Alongside them we will fit antidotes." It closes the circle with "in his chest of cures: the church." Y opens well on Noetus, but it holds Hippolytus's name back to the second paragraph.
2. **X credits its sources where they differ.** "The historian Socrates says… Another historian, Sozomen, says instead…" Its scenes come from the record: the Euphrates water, and deacons holding a man's "mouth shut."
3. **Y's order wanders.** The Zephyrinus material splits around a quote ("While Zephyrinus still lived…"). The Easter table is a digression ("some keeping the moon's fourteenth day" is never explained). The ending becomes a correction of Damasus's verse.

**Faults in X**
- It tells events twice. Paragraph 2 previews "from Augustine to a council at Nicaea" and "carried the same fight to Jerusalem and Constantinople", and both are told again later.
- "He had not, and had judged them by report" is told as fact, with no source named.
- The "Long Brothers" are named before it is clear who they are.
- "a wrong belief held by people led astray ever since Adam" is awkward.
- "A book written to answer the council's argument replied…" is vague.
- About nine names are heavy for one hearing.

**Faults in Y**
- It tells the exile twice. "In 235 he was sent to Sardinia… Rome kept his day" comes early, then again near the end.
- It also tells the forgiveness quarrel twice.
- "the split of Novatus" against "Novatian's party" will confuse a listener.
- "Yet that split came later…" turns the narrator into a judge.
- "Jerome says it gave Eusebius the occasion" leaves Eusebius unexplained.
- "The high priesthood meant…" is a gloss tacked on after the quote.
- Its many dates are dense to hear once.

**Grades:** X: B; Y: C
