**Verdict:** X (John Chrysostom) is clearly better than Y (Clement).

**Reasons**
1. **Opening.** X opens on one strong moment with its own question: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit and preached against him." Y opens on a quote about finding a teacher, and the story never builds a risk or a choice to carry through.
2. **Crediting sources.** X credits each side where the sources differ: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come… the meeting's own written record… tell it differently." It also puts the death journey in "Palladius, who… took his side" and keeps it in his voice.
3. **Order.** X mostly runs in order from Antioch to 438. Y circles back and repeats itself.

**Faults in X**
- The second paragraph gives away the ending and then goes back: "led to his exile… came back only when his body came home." The last paragraph then tells that return again.
- A nested sentence with an unclear "They": "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk."
- Another unclear "they": "summed up the meeting's own written record, and they tell it differently."
- An odd phrase, "eaten greedily alone," and a vague one, "John was silenced."

**Faults in Y**
- The fear of philosophy is told twice ("He answered Christians who… feared Greek philosophy" and later "Many believers… feared philosophy as children fear masks").
- Jerome comes in twice. Paragraph 2 gives his defence in brief, then the close tells it again in full ("Jerome's critic had asked him…").
- It ends on a later writer when that is not the story's point.
- A digression that is hard to follow by ear: "who had taught under the emperor Hadrian, by Clement's reckoning."
- "Under his teacher," with no name and no reason given.
- It reads like a run of notes with no single story.

**Grades:** X B, Y D+
