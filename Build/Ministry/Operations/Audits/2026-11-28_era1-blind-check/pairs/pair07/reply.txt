**Verdict: X is clearly better.** X: B. Y: C-.

**Reasons**
1. **Opening.** X opens on a moment in Basil's own words: "like a man roused from deep sleep, I turned my eyes to the wonderful light..." Then it states the essence. Y opens on things that are not its subject (Troy's tax, German envoys at the theater), and it never says plainly what the story is about.
2. **Rigor.** X credits its witnesses where they differ: "The old writers differ on what turned him... His brother... says... the historian Rufinus says." It also attributes the envy charge and the confrontation to Gregory. Y credits Tertullian and Bede carefully, but it lets judgments sit in the narrator's voice.
3. **Order.** X mostly tells one story forward. Y jumps around: a preview of later reception, then the author's biography, then Acts, Nero, Tertullian and Bede.

**Faults in X**
- The ending comes first and is told again later. Paragraph 2 says "he died before a council... declared it in its creed," and the last paragraph repeats it.
- The lodging is told twice: "built a great lodging for the poor" and later "building a lodging for strangers."
- Praise in the narrator's voice: "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself."
- The timeline is unclear. "In Pontus... Basil founded a community" comes before "made him a priest... went back to Pontus."
- It ends on a later writer (Benedict) rather than on Basil.

**Faults in Y**
- Later reception is told twice: "Later, Christians complained... A Christian chronicle set that line down" is previewed, then told in full with Tertullian and Bede.
- Sentences that only say what is missing: "He names no charge, number or kind of punishment" and "there he names no Christians."
- A narrator judgment: "a religion he despised."
- A possible modern claim: "it was also a real name."
- The author's biography interrupts the story mid-way: "His father, in his own telling..."
- There is no clear essence or question running through it, and it ends on Bede.
