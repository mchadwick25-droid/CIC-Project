**Verdict: Y is clearly better.**

**Reasons**
1. **Opening.** Y opens on a scene with its own built-in question: Eutropius "lay trembling under the altar" while the bishop preached against him. X opens on a long quote from Eusebius about handing out books. The quote is pleasant but carries no tension.
2. **Rigor where sources differ.** Y credits both accounts of the Oak: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come… they tell it differently: some charges were heard, and then all forty-five bishops voted." It also keeps Palladius's charge in his own voice: "In his telling, the soldiers hurried John on…"
3. **Order.** Y tells its story mostly in order. X moves around: Pamphilus dies in paragraph 2, then the arrest comes in paragraph 7, then the death is told again in paragraph 11.

**Faults in X**
- The death is told twice: "put to death about 310" and later "sentenced them to death."
- The Defense is introduced twice, in paragraph 3 and again in paragraph 9.
- "He refused and ignored his threats" has an unclear pronoun.
- "Eusebius, whose pages hold nearly all of his life" comes late, after other witnesses have already spoken.
- The money-changer quote is long and hard to follow when heard once: "drench him in the shame of the heretics."
- The ending goes back in time (Jerome finding the volumes) and closes on a later writer.
- "were read for generations after him" is a summing-up line.
- "about 310" may rest on an editor's reckoning rather than the old sources.

**Faults in Y**
- Paragraph 2 gives the ending away ("came back only when his body came home"), and the last paragraph tells it again.
- "He preached plainly about the poor" is summary, not story.
- "They decked the altar" has an unclear "they," and the gloss on Communion inside that sentence makes it heavy.
- "John was silenced" is vague about what actually happened.

**Grades: X: C−, Y: B**
