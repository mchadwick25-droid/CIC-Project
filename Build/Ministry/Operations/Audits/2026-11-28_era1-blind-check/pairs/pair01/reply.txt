**Verdict: X (Epiphanius) is somewhat better than Y (Lactantius).**

**Why X is ahead**
1. **Stronger scenes that show the conflict.** "It was not spiritual water but real water, the kind you can touch with your hand," and the deacons who "seize the man and hold his mouth shut" let the record's force carry the story. The narrator adds no drama.
2. **Where sources differ, it credits each.** Socrates says Theophilus "used Epiphanius, a man so devout that he was simple in mind and manners." Sozomen "says instead" that Epiphanius had long loathed Origen's books. The judgment stays in each witness's voice.
3. **One question carried to its end.** The fight against Origen runs to "He had not, and had judged them by report," and the close returns to the opening image of the chest: "the church, which he calls Christ's holy bride."

**Faults in X**
- Paragraph 3 previews the ending and then tells it again: "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea…" and "he carried the same fight to Jerusalem and Constantinople." Both are told in full later, so these events come twice.
- The gloss of heresy is muddled: "a wrong belief held by people led astray ever since Adam."
- "the Long Brothers" is never explained beyond "those monks."
- "against church rules" names the rule broken without saying what it was.
- It is heavy on names: about ten.

**Faults in Y**
- The main idea is told twice: "religion cannot be forced, since it lives only in the will" in paragraph 2, then "Religion cannot be compelled" in paragraph 13.
- One sentence runs past 30 words: "Lactantius was teaching Latin public speaking at Nicomedia…in 303."
- The paragraph on the disputed book and Constantine's dream is a side trip. It breaks the order, and "In extreme old age…Crispus" is tacked onto it.
- The wording is awkward in places: "One, as he tells it, a philosopher who praised poverty."
- It ends on Jerome's remark about poverty, which is a later writer rather than the subject.
- "the last on the tree of the Latin Apologists" reads as a report about the site.
- The question the story raises (persecution and how to answer it) fades in the second half.

**Grades:** X: B+. Y: B−.
