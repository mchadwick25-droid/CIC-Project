**Verdict: X is clearly better.** It tells a story with a question running through it: a man who fights poison by report. Y reads like a survey of doctrines put together from what his enemies said.

**Reasons**
1. X lets its scenes carry the argument. "It was not spiritual water but real water..." sets up the turn at the end: "He had not, and had judged them by report." Y has no story question that runs to a resolution.
2. X credits sources where they differ. It gives Socrates ("so devout that he was simple") and then Sozomen ("says instead"). Y's crediting is also sound ("By his opponents' accounts"), but its sources rarely disagree.
3. X's close on the church is the subject's own image. Y ends on a new name ("Axionicus at Antioch") and a later writer's verdict.

**Faults in X**
- Tells events twice. Paragraph 3 previews "from Augustine to a council at Nicaea four centuries after," and paragraph 15 repeats it ("Four centuries on").
- Glosses are stacked as asides: "had deacons, the clergy who serve under priests, seize the man."
- Paragraph 9 crowds two incidents together (Jerome's brother, the curtain) with long sentences.
- More than six names (Adam, Jerome, Socrates, Sozomen...).

**Faults in Y**
- The opening quote is split, then picked up again three paragraphs later ("In the same letter he went on").
- The break and the two branches are previewed in paragraph 3, then told again in paragraphs 7 and 13.
- It is not in order: teaching, then arrival in Rome, then teaching again.
- The list of heavenly powers is too dense for one hearing: "Unspeakable and Silence... Father and Truth, then... Word and Life, and Man and Church."
- "A lesser maker" and "writings from these circles" are named but not explained.
- "Valentinus himself was now nowhere" is unclear.

**Grades:** X: B-. Y: C.
