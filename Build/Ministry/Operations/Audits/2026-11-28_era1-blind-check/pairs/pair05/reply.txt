**Verdict:** Y (Epiphanius) is clearly better than X (Tatian).

**Why**
1. **Opening.** Y opens on a concrete image in the subject's own words: "called his book the Panarion, which means a medicine chest... antidotes: cures for those who have been bitten." It closes on the same image ("one thing more after the eighty heresies in his chest of cures"). X opens on two thin claims, then gives a summary that tells the ending before going back ("In the 400s one bishop put away the copies").
2. **Order and repetition.** X tells several things twice. Tatian leaving the church comes in paragraphs 3 and 11, the Gospel harmony in 3, 15 and 17, and Theodoret's removal of the copies in 3 and 17. X also jumps back in time to "Born, he says, in the land of the Assyrians" after the conversion. Y moves forward in order.
3. **Crediting where sources differ.** Y credits each side: "The historian Socrates says... Another historian, Sozomen, says instead..."

**Faults in X**
- A narrator guess: "so he meant the writings of Moses and the prophets."
- A sentence that only says what is missing: "did not know how Tatian combined the Gospels."
- A source left vague: "A later Roman teacher gives Tatian's reason."
- Names that add nothing, such as Rhodon. More than 14 people are named in all.
- "their learning" comes before the Greeks are introduced.
- The Diatessaron is explained late.
- "aeons, invisible heavenly powers" is only thinly explained.
- There are runs of short one-act sentences.

**Faults in Y**
- The 787 council at Nicaea is previewed in paragraph 3 and told again in paragraph 15.
- "took his book as their list" overstates what Augustine and the council did.
- "A book written to answer the council's argument" is never explained.
- The explanations sit awkwardly inside the sentence: "he had deacons, the clergy who serve under priests, seize the man."
- The definition "led astray ever since Adam" is muddy.

**Grades:** X: C-. Y: B+.
