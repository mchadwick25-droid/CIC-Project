**Verdict: Y (Basil) is somewhat better overall.**

**Reasons**
1. **Opening.** Y opens on Basil's own words, with his name in the first sentence: "like a man roused from deep sleep, I turned my eyes to the wonderful light…" X opens on "His deacon, Pontius…", so the subject arrives as a pronoun before he is named. X's essence paragraph then lurches in with "Jerome, writing long after, says he had once been a teacher of public speaking."
2. **Credit where sources differ.** Y handles this well: "The old writers differ on what turned him… His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says… Rufinus says…" It also credits Gregory for the claim of "out of envy". X credits well too, with Pontius "as an admirer". But it leaves the Roman clergy's letter muddled: "had done rightly" followed by "asked Carthage's clergy not to be hired men." The force of that letter is unclear to someone hearing it once.
3. **Load and clarity.** X is longer and carries more names: Pontius, Jerome, Decius, Stephen, Novatian, Augustine, the Donatists and two Julians. Some of its sentences run long, such as "So he withdrew for a while, thinking less of his own safety… and from hiding he led his church by letter." (about 40 words).

**Faults in X**
- The ending is told twice: "in 258 he was put to the sword. Later the Donatists and Augustine both claimed him" appears early and is retold at the close.
- "Communion meant belonging to the church…" is wedged into the middle of the council scene.
- "the field of Sextus" is never explained.
- "over the whole city": the city is not named.
- It ends on a later writer (Augustine), not on Cyprian himself.

**Faults in Y**
- The ending is told twice: "he died before a council… declared it" comes early, and the 381 council is told again at the end.
- Praise is given in the narrator's voice: "Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself."
- "When the province was divided" is never explained.
- "with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit" is dense to follow in one hearing.
- Two Gregorys, which is confusing in audio.
- It ends on Benedict, a later writer.

**Grades:** X: C. Y: B.
