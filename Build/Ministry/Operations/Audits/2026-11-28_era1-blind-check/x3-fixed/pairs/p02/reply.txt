Verdict: Y is better, by a clear margin.

Why Y wins
1. Y has a story with a question that runs through it. It is a fight that grows: Jerusalem, then Theophilus, then Constantinople, then death at sea. X keeps switching between two books (Banquet, then the dialogue, then Banquet, then the dialogue, then Banquet). Its question, Origen and the body, never builds.
2. Y credits its sources where they differ. "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge… Another historian, Sozomen, says instead…" X also credits well ("though others, he adds, placed it under earlier emperors"), but it has less conflict to work with.
3. Y's scenes are vivid and come from the record. "It was not spiritual water but real water…" and the deacons "hold his mouth shut," given in his own account ("he says").

Faults in X
- The order breaks. Paragraph 3 introduces On the Resurrection, paragraph 5 goes back to the Banquet, and paragraph 7 returns to the dialogue.
- It tells the same thing twice. "A later bishop copied his words into his own case" comes back as the full Epiphanius paragraph. After the statue quote, "The flaws, that is, melt away, and the statue itself is not lost" repeats what the quote just said.
- "Origen, the great teacher" is praise in the narrator's voice.
- Too many names: "Decius and Valerian," Photius and Cain are not needed.
- The essence never comes plainly in the first minute.

Faults in Y
- Paragraph 3 gives away the ending ("Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea…", "In old age he carried the same fight…"), and paragraphs 13–15 then tell it again. Nicaea is told twice ("four centuries after," then "In 787").
- "He had not, and had judged them by report" states one witness's account as fact without crediting it.
- "so that no one could say he answered from report alone" gives a motive with no source shown.
- Many names: Augustine (twice), Sozomen and Adam could go.

Grades: X: C. Y: B.
