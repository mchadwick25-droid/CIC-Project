X is somewhat better than Y.

Why X wins
1. X's conflict drives it, and the sources are credited where they differ. For example: "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come. A later bishop... summed up the meeting's own written record, and they tell it differently." The partisan witness is also flagged: "Palladius, who wrote John's life and took his side."
2. Its opening image comes back at the end, so the church's question of shelter and mercy runs through the whole piece. It ends on the subject itself, with "Glory to God for all things" and the body coming home.
3. Y's order is broken. The surrender and the prophecy are told in paragraph 2 ("In 67 he gave himself up... When that came true, he lived on at Rome"), and then the story goes "Back in the cave" to the suicide pact. The surrender is told twice.

Faults in X
- The ending is given early, then the story goes back: "led to his exile... came back only when his body came home." That return is told again at the close.
- The sentence "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk" is nested, and "They" is unclear.
- The Arian-church episode is never tied to his fall.
- "John was silenced" is vague, and "eaten greedily alone" is awkward.
- "lay trembling" may be a detail added for effect.

Faults in Y
- The order breaks and the surrender is told twice, as above.
- The glosses are bolted on: "The Greeks are those who were not Jewish." "the high priest, the chief priest of the Temple."
- The tenses clash in "taught people who gladly receive the truth."
- "This man was the Christ" stands in the narration with the dispute about it only implied. "The passage itself says he was the Christ" doesn't settle it either.
- "Late in that century" is unclear, and so is "the north of the country."

Grades: X: B. Y: C+.
