Verdict: X is clearly better.

Reasons
1. X has a real story and stakes. It opens on a strong scene: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit." It carries the conflict through two councils, the exile and the death. Y never finds a risk or question, and its quarrel with Origen gets argued rather than lived.
2. X credits sources where they differ. "Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come... they tell it differently." "Palladius, who wrote John's life and took his side." Y also credits well on the martyrdom ("though others, he adds, placed it under earlier emperors"), but it has less to credit.
3. X keeps rough order, while Y keeps doubling back. Y moves from Banquet to Resurrection, back to Banquet, back to the dialogue, then the death, then Epiphanius, then the Banquet again.

Faults in X
- Paragraph 2 gives the ending away early: "the followers who left the church... came back only when his body came home." The last paragraph then tells it again.
- After the 399 opening it jumps back to his birth at Antioch, so the order breaks.
- One sentence is too nested to follow by ear: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk." It also leaves unclear who "they" are.
- "John was silenced" is vague.
- "Some thought" doesn't say who.

Faults in Y
- Epiphanius comes in twice: "A later bishop copied his words" and later "Epiphanius... setting out its words... word for word."
- The narrator restates the statue quote right after it: "The flaws, that is, melt away."
- Eusebius's challenge, "asked how Methodius dared," is never explained.
- "The virgin life" is glossed only in the third paragraph.
- "As Photius summed it up in his reading notes centuries on" breaks the flow.
- The close lands on the Banquet scene but no question has been resolved.

Grades: X: B. Y: C.
