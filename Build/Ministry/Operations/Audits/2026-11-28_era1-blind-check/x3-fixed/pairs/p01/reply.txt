Verdict: Y is somewhat better.

Reasons
1. Y keeps the ending in the right place. X's second paragraph tells the whole life in advance, up to "he died before a council… declared it in its creed". X then tells the council again ("In 381… a council at Constantinople declared") and the lodging again (paragraphs 2 and 6). Y opens on the Banquet garden and closes on it ("Virtue tells the guests to rise and stand under the tree"), which Part 1 allows.
2. Y credits carefully. "as Photius summed it up in his reading notes centuries on"; "Jerome… says… though others, he adds, placed it under earlier emperors". X mostly credits too ("Gregory says"), but it tells claims from Basil's side in the narrator's voice.
3. X has the stronger story question but keeps losing its order. The emperor's threat and the rags-and-books answer hold a listener. But the passage "The old writers differ on what turned him" breaks the story to go back to the conversion, and the lodging comes back after the bishopric.

Faults in X
- Paragraph 2 summarises the whole life, which leads to retelling (council, lodging).
- It ends on later writers ("Rufinus put his works… Benedict… pointing his monks to Basil's"). That is not the story's point.
- "His accusers were filling the governor's ears" is Basil's own view, uncredited.
- "The whole city poured around Basil" and "greeted them as brothers and tended them himself" are praise with no witness named.
- "moved against the churches that held them equal": "them" is unclear when heard.
- The doxology paragraph ("with the Son, together with… through the Son, in…") is too dense to follow by ear.

Faults in Y
- It alternates between the two works (Banquet, Resurrection, Banquet, Resurrection), so it is not one story in order.
- "A later bishop copied his words" is told again in the Epiphanius paragraph.
- After the statue quote, "The flaws, that is, melt away… not lost" repeats it.
- It has little risk or choice to carry through.

Grades: X: C. Y: B.
