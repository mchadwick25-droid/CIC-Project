## What it was about

1. **The famine relief: Ephrem appoints himself to run it.** Already explained. The rich said no one was honest enough to run it, so Ephrem took it on and ran it for a year. Witness: Palladius, Lausiac History 40, `cic/texts/palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt` 471. Documented.
2. **His prose "against rival teachers" (the Prose Refutations).** Explain. Each rival's teaching is the book's content, so the book is explained through items 3 to 5, not by a line of its own. Witness: Ephrem, Mitchell title (lines 3, 22) and 403. Documented.
3. **Mani's teaching.** Explain. Two powers, Light and Darkness, were there from the start and became mixed, and Darkness made the body a prison for the soul. Witnesses: Ephrem, as an enemy and credited as one (Mitchell 415, 418, 431, 719, 727, 771, 819). From Mani's own side, a later Manichaean confession-prayer from Central Asia (Le Coq's English; brief wording "a later prayer") speaks of the two Roots, Bright and Dark, mixed and to be parted again (Khuastuanift 16575-16585, 16643-16672). The brief tells Ephrem's account only, credited to him. The prayer's naming of the two Roots is a modern view and goes to the card (see Modern pass): calling it Manichaean and "later" is Le Coq's identification and dating of a text found in modern times, not anything the old sources say.
4. **Marcion's teaching.** Explain. A second God, a "Stranger" above the world's Maker, came down to lift souls out of this world. Witness: Ephrem only, as an enemy and credited as one (Mitchell 584, 592, 1003, 1549, 1559, 1603, 1635). No held text gives Marcion's own side. Documented as Ephrem's account. Brief wording: "a stranger to this world", with no quotation marks, so nothing new is quoted.
5. **Bardaisan's teaching, and the resurrection answer.** Explain. Ephrem wrote in a hymn that a book of Bardaisan's denied the body would rise. He answered that to deny it slanders God's justice and grace. Today the brief gives the answer without saying whose teaching it answers. The answer is Nisibene Hymn 51, a hymn against Bardaisan (Gwynn 11200-11214), not the prose refutations. Witness: Ephrem (enemy, credited). A modern editor's view, for the card and not the brief: Bardaisan "seems ... to have denied the proper resurrection of the body", though on Hort's reading he kept the common faith except on isolated points (McGiffert's note, Eusebius 26542-26556; an editor's view, not Eusebius's). Documented as Ephrem's charge. As Bardaisan's own teaching, Inferential-Thin: one modern editor's "seems", with Hort's caution beside it, and no work of Bardaisan's own held.
6. **Harmonius's songs: Greek ideas of the soul and of the body's birth, death and rebirth.** Already explained. Witness: Sozomen III.16 (Sozomen 32078-32094). Documented. Do not gloss "rebirth" as resurrection or as reincarnation; Sozomen gives only "regeneration ... taught by the Greek philosophers". Listening review: confirm it does not sound like a contradiction of paragraph 2.
7. **Who wrote the songs: Harmonius or Bardaisan.** Already explained. Sozomen blames the son (Sozomen 32082-32094); Theodoret IV.26 also names Harmonius (npnf203 15060-15065). That the homily's "He fashioned hymns" means Bardaisan is Gwynn's reading (Gwynn 11234-11238), not in Ephrem's held words, so it leaves the brief and goes to the card (see Modern pass).
8. **Ephrem's hymns "that held the church's teaching".** Already explained, as far as the held text goes. Sozomen says only that he wrote poems "in accordance with the doctrines of the Church" to answer Harmonius's ideas, plus hymns and praises of holy ascetics (Sozomen 32094-32100). No held line gives their content in more detail, so nothing more is added. Documented.
9. **His works in Greek, and Basil's admiration; Jerome's churches reading him after the Scriptures; Theodoret's martyrs' festivals.** Already explained: each says plainly what was done with his writings. Witnesses: Sozomen 32060-32067; Jerome 115 (`cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 41540-41543); Theodoret IV.26 (same file, 15063-15067). Documented.
10. **The school at Edessa, Narsai's school at Nisibis, and the students of Edessa going to him.** Already explained as told: what passed from Ephrem's school to Nisibis. Witness: Barhadbeshabba, "a later bishop" (title, Scher 17166-17169; he tells of Narsai's forty-five years and of Hnana after him, 20742, 21006), Scher 20434-20442, 20738-20743. The brief tells his sequence and his own words, "the schools multiplied in the Persian empire" (20740-20741). The summary "began with Ephrem" was rated Inferential-Thin and is cut (see Modern pass). No year is given for him: "around 600" is a modern dating. Leave out the reason Narsai left Edessa. The brief names no quarrel, and Barhadbeshabba gives none beyond "Satan ... y mit la discorde et le désordre" (Scher 20612-20613). The expulsion of Ibas's party is the editor's footnote (Scher, notes after 20613), not the witness. Naming the quarrel would bring in a new charge with nothing held to explain it.

Totals: 4 to explain (items 2 to 5, carried by three new or changed passages), 6 already explained, 1 left out (the cause of Narsai's leaving, which the brief does not name).

## Modern pass

Ruling: Decision-Log, "No modern opinion, guess or reconstruction in a story" (Mark, 2026-11-23). Five places found; every other sentence is unchanged.

1. **A modern-only date.** `Around 372, famine struck Edessa,` → `Famine struck Edessa,`. No old source gives the famine a year. Sozomen places it "shortly before his death" (npnf202 32138-32140); Palladius gives the year of relief and the death a month after (palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt 471), which the brief's last line already tells. The Chronicle of Edessa gives the death as June of the year 684 of the Greeks (chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt 103); turning that into 373 rests on Assemani's era reckoning (same file 34), and 372 for the famine is a modern sum. To the card: "Modern dating puts the famine about 372 and his death in 373, from the Chronicle of Edessa's year 684 (Cowper 103) by Assemani's era reckoning (Cowper 34)."
2. **A modern identification and dating of a found text.** `A later prayer of Mani's own followers names the same two powers.` → cut. The prayer's own lines name the two Roots, Bright and Dark, mixed and to be parted (Khuastuanift 16575-16585, 16643-16672), but the held lines do not name Mani; that it is a Manichaean prayer, and later, is Le Coq's identification and dating (file header, lines 1-7; Le Coq's introduction, 15401-15461). To the card: "A confession-prayer found at Tun-huang, which its editor Le Coq identifies as Manichaean (JRAS 1911), names two Roots, Bright and Dark, mixed and to be parted (Khuastuanift 16575-16585, 16643-16672)."
3. **An editor's reading given to the source.** `In his own homily, though, Ephrem blames Bardaisan himself.` → cut. The homily is held only in Gwynn's extract, whose lines ("He fashioned hymns ...") do not name Bardaisan; the referent is Gwynn's "attributing the work, however, to Bardesan solely" (npnf213 11234-11238). Mitchell's introduction says another of Ephrem's hymns gives the songs to Bardaisan's "sons" (ephraim_prose-refutations_mitchell1912-1921.txt 2690). The old sources that name the songwriter, Sozomen (npnf202 32082-32094) and Theodoret (npnf203 15060-15065), both give Harmonius, as the brief already says. To the card: "Gwynn reads Ephrem's Homily 53 as giving the songs to Bardaisan himself (npnf213 11234-11246); Mitchell notes Hymn 55 against Heresies gives them to his 'sons' (Mitchell 2690)."
4. **A modern date and a modern description of the witness.** `Around 600, Barhadbeshabba, the historian of the school of Nisibis, wrote that Ephrem had opened a school in Edessa, and that after his death his disciples made it grow.` → `Barhadbeshabba, a later bishop, wrote a book on how the schools were founded. It tells how Ephrem had opened a school in Edessa, and how after his death his disciples made it grow.` Held: the work's own title, "Cause de la fondation des écoles, composée par Mar Barhadbsabba 'Arbaya, évêque de Halwan" (Scher 17166-17169); "later" because he tells of Narsai's forty-five years and of Hnana after him (Scher 20742, 21006); the school and its growth (Scher 20435-20441). To the card: "His editor Scher dates him about 600 and has him study at Nisibis under Hnana (Scher 16929-16933; file header line 3); the census gives c. 585-605."
5. **A claim rated Inferential-Thin.** `In his telling, the schools that spread through the Church of the East under Persia began with Ephrem.` → `In his telling, the schools then multiplied in the Persian empire.` Held: "Les écoles se multiplièrent dans l'empire des Perses" (Scher 20740-20741), after the sequence from Ephrem's school to Narsai's (20434-20442, 20738-20740). The summary "began with Ephrem" was this outline's Inferential-Thin rating, and "the Church of the East" was not in his lines. To the card: none needed beyond Barhadbeshabba's own telling; the Inferential-Thin rating of Ephrem as founder of the schools stays on the card as a modern view.
