## What it was about

1. **The famine relief: Ephrem appoints himself to run it.** Already explained. The rich said no one was honest enough to run it, so Ephrem took it on and ran it for a year. Witness: Palladius, Lausiac History 40, `cic/texts/palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt` 471. Documented.
2. **His prose "against rival teachers" (the Prose Refutations).** Explain. Each rival's teaching is the book's content, so the book is explained through items 3 to 5, not by a line of its own. Witness: Ephrem, Mitchell title (lines 3, 22) and 403. Documented.
3. **Mani's teaching.** Explain. Two powers, Light and Darkness, were there from the start and became mixed, and Darkness made the body a prison for the soul. Witnesses: Ephrem, as an enemy and credited as one (Mitchell 418, 431, 719, 771). From Mani's own side, a later Manichaean confession-prayer from Central Asia (Le Coq's English) speaks of the two Roots, Bright and Dark, mixed and to be parted again (Khuastuanift 16575-16585, 16643-16672). Documented: both sides give the two powers. The body as Darkness's prison is Ephrem's wording of it, credited to him.
4. **Marcion's teaching.** Explain. A second God, a "Stranger" above the world's Maker, came down to lift souls out of this world. Witness: Ephrem only, as an enemy and credited as one (Mitchell 584, 592, 1003, 1549). No held text gives Marcion's own side. Documented as Ephrem's account. Brief wording: "a stranger to this world", with no quotation marks, so nothing new is quoted.
5. **Bardaisan's teaching, and the resurrection answer.** Explain. Ephrem wrote in a hymn that a book of Bardaisan's denied the body would rise. He answered that to deny it slanders God's justice and grace. Today the brief gives the answer without saying whose teaching it answers. The answer is Nisibene Hymn 51, a hymn against Bardaisan (Gwynn 11200-11214), not the prose refutations. Witness: Ephrem (enemy, credited). A modern editor's view, for the card and not the brief: Bardaisan "seems ... to have denied the proper resurrection of the body", though on Hort's reading he kept the common faith except on isolated points (McGiffert's note, Eusebius 26542-26556; an editor's view, not Eusebius's). Documented as Ephrem's charge; Widely Accepted as Bardaisan's teaching.
6. **Harmonius's songs: Greek ideas of the soul and of the body's birth, death and rebirth.** Already explained. Witness: Sozomen III.16 (Sozomen 32078-32094). Documented.
7. **Who wrote the songs: Harmonius or Bardaisan.** Already explained. Sozomen blames the son; Ephrem's own homily blames Bardaisan alone (Sozomen 32082-32094; Gwynn 11234-11246). Contested.
8. **Ephrem's hymns "that held the church's teaching".** Already explained, as far as the held text goes. Sozomen says only that he wrote poems "in accordance with the doctrines of the Church" to answer Harmonius's ideas, plus hymns and praises of holy ascetics (Sozomen 32094-32100). No held line gives their content in more detail, so nothing more is added. Documented.
9. **His works in Greek, and Basil's admiration; Jerome's churches reading him after the Scriptures; Theodoret's martyrs' festivals.** Already explained: each says plainly what was done with his writings. Witnesses: Sozomen 32060-32067; Jerome 115 (`cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 41540-41543); Theodoret IV.26 (same file, 15063-15067). Documented.
10. **The school at Edessa, Narsai's school at Nisibis, and the students of Edessa going to him.** Already explained as told: what passed from Ephrem's school to Nisibis. Witness: Barhadbeshabba (Scher 20434-20442, 20738-20743). Inferential-Thin for "began with Ephrem" as a historical claim; it is credited as Barhadbeshabba's telling. Leave out the reason Narsai left Edessa. The brief names no quarrel, and Barhadbeshabba gives none beyond "Satan ... y mit la discorde et le désordre" (Scher 20612-20613). The expulsion of Ibas's party is the editor's footnote (Scher, notes after 20613), not the witness. Naming the quarrel would bring in a new charge with nothing held to explain it.

Totals: 4 to explain (items 2 to 5, carried by three new or changed passages), 6 already explained, 1 left out (the cause of Narsai's leaving, which the brief does not name).
