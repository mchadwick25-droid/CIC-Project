## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Ephrem, a fourth-century deacon of the Syriac church at Edessa, from Nisibis or its countryside, who taught the church's faith in hymns.
2. **What happened:** in a famine around 372 he shamed the rich into trusting him with relief and ran it for a year. Earlier he had argued in prose against Mani, Marcion and Bardaisan. Sozomen says that when Harmonius's songs spread Greek ideas, Ephrem learned their meters and wrote hymns of church teaching, which the Syrians then sang. He died in his cell a month after the relief ended.
3. **Why it matters:** his hymns carried the church's teaching. They reached Greek readers and Basil, were read in some churches after the Scriptures (Jerome), and were still sung at the martyrs' festivals (Theodoret). Barhadbeshabba traces the schools of the Church of the East from Ephrem's school at Edessa through Narsai's at Nisibis.
