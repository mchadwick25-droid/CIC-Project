# Review: Ephrem the Syrian (approved text under review, batch 10)

Text reviewed: `ephrem-the-syrian.brief.txt` (identical to `ephrem-the-syrian.brief.approved.txt`). Judged against `Brief-Kit/Kit.md`. No outline exists; traced to `2026-11-16_eight-standard-v1/drafts/ephrem-the-syrian.refs.md`, its `renderings/eight/comparison.md`, the card, the census entry and `cic/texts/`.

## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Ephrem, a fourth-century deacon of Edessa, from Nisibis, in the Syriac-speaking church on Rome's border with Persia.
2. **What happened:** He wrote against rival teachers, and when Bardaisan's school spread its ideas in songs the Syrians loved, he learned their meters and wrote hymns of the church's teaching, which the Syrians then sang; near the end he ran Edessa's famine relief for a year and died soon after.
3. **Why it matters:** His hymns reached Greek readers and Basil, were read and sung in worship (Jerome, Theodoret), and a later historian traces the schools of the Church of the East, through Narsai at Nisibis, to the school Ephrem opened at Edessa.

No line is blank or wrong.

## Essence and what is also told

- **Essence** (census `why`, card `knownFor`, Sozomen III.16): a deacon who taught the church's faith in hymns the people sang, answering Bardaisan's songs with the church's teaching in the same meters. That is why he is on the tree.
- **Also told:** his prose against Mani, Marcion and Bardaisan, with the resurrection answer (present, light, fair weight); the reach of the hymns into Greek, Basil, Jerome and Theodoret (present); Barhadbeshabba's school at Edessa passing to Nisibis (present, given its weight); the famine relief (present, full weight).

## Scores

- **Voice 4.** Adult, documentary register; credits worn lightly inside sentences; no talking down. "Its church was part of Syriac Christianity" reads as a filed fact rather than narration.
- **Craft 2.** The parts are good but the order fails the one-hearing test: a seven-sentence hook that ends in his death, then a jump back, and the hymns (the reason he is on the tree) arrive near word 200.
- **Rigor 4.** Every claim and the quote trace to held text; the dispute over Harmonius is told in place and credited. One witness's motive drifts into the narrator's voice, and one framing line overstates.
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 8.4, reading ease 79.7 (names out), 16.6 words a sentence, longest 28: pass. Plain words throughout.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. The essence is not told within the first minute

The hook (paragraph 1) runs 119 words and seven sentences on the famine; the hymns first appear at about word 185 ("Sozomen says Bardaisan's son Harmonius...") and Ephrem's own hymn-writing at about word 197. A listener at one minute knows a famine and a death, not why Ephrem is on the tree.

**Change:** after `he answered: "Look, on your behalf I appoint myself to run the shelter."` (end of the hook, about word 97), insert one new sentence:
`Yet he is remembered for something else: he taught the church's faith in hymns.`
(Held: Sozomen III.16, npnf202 32092-32102, "composed similar poems in accordance with the doctrines of the Church ... From that period the Syrians sang the odes of Ephraim"; census `why`.) The essence then lands near word 111.

### BLOCKING 2. The ending is told first, then the story jumps back

Paragraph 1 ends `He ran the relief for a year, and a month after it ended, Palladius says, he died in his cell.` Paragraph 2 then opens `His years in Edessa had been spent...`, going back years. The kit lets the hook reach ahead in about three sentences, but not tell the death and then return.

**Change:** move that sentence out of paragraph 1 to stand alone as the story's last paragraph, with `He` changed to `Ephrem` (after the Narsai paragraph, "He" would read as Narsai):
`Ephrem ran the relief for a year, and a month after it ended, Palladius says, he died in his cell.`
This also fixes MINOR 3: the story closes on the opening's image and his death, as the kit asks, rather than on the schools.

### MINOR 3. The story ends on a later institution

`In his telling, the schools that spread through the Church of the East under Persia began with Ephrem.` is the last line. The schools are not the story's point; the hymns are. Fixed by the move in BLOCKING 2; no separate change.

### MINOR 4. A framing line the sources do not hold

`His years in Edessa had been spent against rival teachers.` Palladius (Lausiac History 40, line 471) says he spent many years in "the quiet life ... continuing to edify his visitors", and no held text dates the Prose Refutations to Edessa or says his years there were spent against rivals.

**Change:** replace `His years in Edessa had been spent against rival teachers. He wrote prose against Mani, Marcion and Bardaisan of Edessa.` with `He had written prose against rival teachers: Mani, Marcion and Bardaisan of Edessa.` (Mitchell's Prose Refutations, title and lines 3, 403.) Saves 8 words, which pays for the essence sentence.

### MINOR 5. Sozomen's claim of motive told in the narrator's voice

After `In his own homily, though, Ephrem blames Bardaisan himself.`, the next sentence, `Ephrem feared the people would absorb those ideas, so he learned the same meters...`, gives Sozomen's account of motive as fact, just as the narrator has stepped out of Sozomen to Ephrem's own homily. (Four faults: one witness's claim told as fact.)

**Change:** `Ephrem feared` becomes `In Sozomen's account, Ephrem feared`; and `From that time on, Sozomen says, the Syrians` becomes `From that time on, he says, the Syrians` (name each source once).

### Length

The changes net to about +8 words (+14, +3, -1, -8). The text is not to be lengthened. To hold it at about 334, replace `Its church was part of Syriac Christianity. Ephrem, a deacon of Edessa from Nisibis or its countryside, left his cell.` with `Ephrem, a deacon of its Syriac church, from Nisibis or its countryside, left his cell.` (-6; it also removes the one flat line and keeps the tradition in a clause). The net is then about +2.

## Not findings

- The hook's setting sentence stacks year, city, language, region and border. That is a craft note only, and no change is asked.
- Names: Ephrem, Palladius, Bardaisan, Harmonius, Sozomen, Basil, Jerome, Theodoret, Barhadbeshabba and Narsai each act, witness or link. Mani and Marcion are each heard once, but both link (`manichaeism`, `marcion-of-pontus` on the tree). Twelve names is a lot for 334 words, but the listener is not lost. Spellings match the census.
- Siblings: Theodoret's batch brief does not mention Ephrem and shares no quote. `Overlap_Report.txt` is clear.

## Source check

- **The quote.** "Look, on your behalf I appoint myself to run the shelter." This is the settled FINAL rendering of Palladius's printed "See, on your behalf I appoint myself hospitaller." (`palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt` line 471, printed text re-checked verbatim), per `renderings/eight/comparison.md` Q8. The card carries both the printed text and the uncertainty about "on your behalf".
- **The famine scene** (the question to the rich, no one honest, "what do you think of me", "man of God", a year, death a month after, the cell) is all at Palladius 471. It is credited to him.
- **Around 372.** Sozomen says "shortly before his death" (npnf202 32138-32140), and the census gives 373.
- **Deacon of Edessa** is in Palladius and Jerome. **Nisibis or its countryside** is in Sozomen (32051).
- **The resurrection answer** is Nisibene Hymn 51 (npnf213 11206-11215), loosely told without quotation marks. It is fair.
- **Harmonius and the Greek ideas** are in Sozomen (32080-32102): "the soul, the generation and destruction of the body, and the regeneration". Ephrem's homily blames Bardaisan alone, in the extract Gwynn prints (npnf213 11236-11246). Theodoret also names Harmonius (npnf203 15060).
- **Greek in his lifetime, and Basil** are in Sozomen (32060-32067). **Read after the Scriptures** is in Jerome 115 (npnf203 41540-41543). **The martyrs' festivals** are in Theodoret IV.26 (npnf203 15065-15067).
- **Barhadbeshabba** (Scher, lines 20435-20442 and 20738-20743) has the school at Edessa, its growth after his death, Narsai at Nisibis, most of the brothers of Edessa going to him, and the schools multiplying in Persia. "By then inside Persia" is held by "la ville de Nisibe fut livrée aux Perses". The census dates him c. 585-605, so "around 600" holds.

No factual error found beyond MINOR 4 and MINOR 5.

## Do not say (for an outline, should one be written)

There is no outline. These lines are for one, should it be written:

- Do not open with the famine and close that scene on his death, then go back. Do say: the famine scene leads straight to the hymns, and his death in the cell closes the story.
- Do not say "his years in Edessa were spent against rival teachers". Do say: "he had written prose against rival teachers". Palladius gives his Edessa years as a quiet life.
