## The four lines

- **Who:** Ephrem, a fourth-century deacon of the Syriac church at Edessa, from Nisibis, who wrote against rival teachers and taught the faith in hymns.
- **What happened:** He wrote prose against Mani, Marcion and Bardaisan. When Harmonius's songs spread Greek ideas among the Syrians, Ephrem wrote hymns in the same meters that held the church's teaching, and the Syrians sang his songs. Late in life he ran famine relief at Edessa for a year and died a month after.
- **What it was about:** Answering rival teachings: Mani's two powers and the body as Darkness's prison, Marcion's second God above the Maker, and Bardaisan's denial that the body would rise. He answered them in song as well as prose.
- **Why the connection matters:** His hymns became the church's songs. They went into Greek in his lifetime, were read after the Scriptures and sung at martyrs' festivals. Barhadbeshabba traces the schools of the Church of the East, through Narsai's school at Nisibis, back to Ephrem's school at Edessa.
