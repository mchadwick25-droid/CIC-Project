# Pelagius: outline check

## My account, set before reading Outline.md

From the census card, the slate job line, the source sheet and what he is known for:

**Essence.** A monk from Britain, long settled at Rome, who taught that God made human nature able to choose the good, so that a person who wills it can live without sin. He urged it on the young Demetrias in a letter that tells her her spiritual riches can come only from herself. Bishops in Palestine (Diospolis, 415) accepted him into communion; after Augustine had answered him book by book, Africa, the emperor and Rome condemned him in 418, and his name became the name of a condemned teaching. He is on the tree because the Latin church's teaching on grace was defined against him.

**Also told.** What the letter says, in his words, beside Augustine's verdict on it (food and poison). His own claim that he always added "the help of God's grace", against Augustine's reading that he meant only nature, teaching, forgiveness and example (Contested). The Diospolis scene: accusers absent, Greek-speaking judges through an interpreter, his disowning of Caelestius's propositions, the verdict, and Augustine's view that the judges judged rightly on what they heard. Zosimus's acceptance in 417, then the reversal in 418: the emperor's banishment order, the council at Carthage (canons on grace), Rome's condemnation. What came: the name and the condemnation held (Ephesus 431, Rome to the Irish c. 640); Gaul kept arguing against him (Cassian, Vincent, Faustus); the teaching reached Britain (Germanus, 429); the letter survived among Jerome's works. Nothing held tells his birth, death or life after 418.
