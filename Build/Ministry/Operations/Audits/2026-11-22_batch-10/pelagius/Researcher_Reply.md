# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Essence:** A monk from Britain who taught at Rome held that God made human nature able to choose the good and live without sin, and he wrote to the young Demetrias on how to live it. Bishops in Palestine cleared him in 415. After Augustine answered him book by book, Africa and Rome condemned him in 418.

**Also told:** what the letter says; Augustine's reading of it; the Diospolis scene; Rome taking his side under Zosimus in 417, then turning in 418, with the emperor's exile order; then Ephesus, Cassian, Britain, and the letter surviving as Jerome's.

**Shape:** Teacher or thinker. **Length:** about 4 minutes, about 600 words, 640 at most: quotes about 80, glosses about 45, credited witnesses about 40.

**Connection:** the African bishops' rulings of 418 say that grace does more than forgive past sins or teach. Those are exactly the two things Augustine said Pelagius meant by grace.

**Quotes:**
1. Pelagius: "But spiritual riches no one can give you except yourself. In these, then, you are rightly praised. In these you are rightly set above others. They cannot exist except from you and in you."
2. Augustine and Alypius: "'These good things can only be in you': that was very well and very truly said. That is clearly food. But when he says 'only from you', that is wholly poison."
3. Zosimus: "Is there any passage in which God's grace or help has been left out?"

All three rendered from Latin, each confirmed by a second held print; none used elsewhere on the tree.
