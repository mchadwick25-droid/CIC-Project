## Caelestius's two statements (item 8, overriding the earlier Leave out)

Sound and fair to Pelagius. The synod record, as Augustine quotes it, reads the statements out as "charged against Pelagius, which are said to be found among the opinions of his disciple Cœlestius". It gives "Adam's sin injured only himself and not the human race" and "new-born infants are in the same condition as Adam was before the fall" (NPNF105 9882-9883). The charges came from the indictment of Heros and Lazarus (NPNF105 9487, 10501). Pelagius answered: "The rest were not said by me ... I anathematize those who either now hold, or have ever held, these opinions". The synod accepted that he had condemned "the opinions which were not his" (NPNF105 9899).

The new line credits the statements to Caelestius and tells them as charged against Pelagius and disowned by him. It never presents them as his teaching, so the Do not say on original sin still holds. Telling their content removes the gap of an accusation told with its content withheld. It also gives the listener the Diospolis scene's real stakes, and it sets his own claim ("a man can be without sin") apart from what he rejected. "Documented" is right for this.

The confidence split is right: Documented that Cassian charged it, Inferential-Thin that Pelagius himself taught it.
