**S5. Tradition name.** Prosper names the teaching after him: PROSPER 53725 "dogma nominis sui" (the teaching of his own name). Census: `pelagianism`, "The Pelagians".

**E4. His teaching as he stated it himself.** own (as recorded by synods, quoted by Augustine, English 1887). **Documented.**
- NPNF105 10388 (John of Jerusalem's account of the Jerusalem hearing, July 415, quoted in Proceedings 54): "‘I did not say that man’s nature has received the power of being impeccable,—but I said, whosoever is willing, in the pursuit of his own salvation, to labour and struggle to abstain from sinning and to walk in the commandments of God, receives the ability to do so from God.’"
- NPNF105 10383 (Proceedings 54, his answer at Diospolis): "“We said, that a man is able to be without sin, and to keep the commandments of God, if he wishes;”".
- NPNF105 9524 (Proceedings 5, at Diospolis): "“This I stated in the interest of free will. God is its helper whenever it chooses good; man, however, when sinning is himself in fault, as under the direction of a free will.”"

**E5. Augustine answers him in books, first without naming him.** contemp. **Documented.**
- NPNF105 10294 (Proceedings 47): "when there was actually placed in my hands, by those faithful servants of God and honourable men, Timasius and Jacobus, the treatise in which Pelagius dealt with the question of God’s grace ... simply describing the grace of God as nature created with a free will ... ## 3. What came of him, heaviest first

**I1. His name became the name of a condemned teaching, and the condemnation held.** contemp. and later. **Documented.**
- PROSPER 53725 "dogma nominis sui"; NPNF214 36418 "Synod against the heresy of Pelagius and Celestius".
- Ephesus, 431: NPNF214 18748-18753 (the council's letter to Celestine of Rome): "When there had been read in the holy Synod what had been done touching the deposition of the most irreligious Pelagians and Cœlestines, of Cœlestius, and Pelagius, and Julian, and Præsidius, and Florus, and Marcellian, and Orontius, and those inclined to like errors, we also deemed it right ... that the determinations of your holiness concerning them should stand strong and firm. And we all were of the same mind, holding them deposed." The canons name Celestius, not Pelagius: NPNF214 17745 "if he has adopted, or shall hereafter adopt, the doctrines of Celestius".
- Two centuries on, Rome to the Irish: BEDE-HE 5428-5440 (letter written while John was pope-elect, kept by Bede): "And we have also understood that the poison of the Pelagian heresy again springs up among you ... it has not only been abolished these two hundred years, but it is also daily anathematised for ever by us ... For who will not detest that insolent and impious proposition, * That man can live without sin of his own free will, and not through God's grace ? '". Latin BEDE-CHRON 25627-25629. "[Irish]" at BEDE-HE 5372 is the editor's gloss for "Scots".

**I2. Augustine's long answer.** contemp. **Documented.** The anti-Pelagian books (NPNF105 contents; corpus map `pelagianism.yaml`: On the Merits, On the Spirit and the Letter, On Nature and Grace, On Man's Perfection, On the Proceedings of Pelagius, On the Grace of Christ and Original Sin, and later books). What the West built on them is `augustine-of-hippo`'s job (Orange 529, Luther, Calvin); here a clause at most.

**I3. Gaul kept the argument going, against him by name.** contemp. and later. **Documented.**
- John Cassian, Against Nestorius 1.3 (about 430): NPNF211 48715: "that heresy mentioned above which sprang from the error of Pelagius; viz., that in saying that Jesus Christ had lived as a mere man without any stain of sin, they actually went so far as to declare that men could also be without sin if they liked." NPNF211 48740: "observes that he says exactly the same thing which the Pelagians said before him". Latin: `cic/texts/cassian_institutes-and-contra-nestorium_petschenig1888.txt` 20681, 20732, 20771.
- Vincent of Lérins, 434: NPNF211 13962 and 14013: "For who ever before that profane Pelagius ... attributed so much antecedent strength to Free-will, as to deny the necessity of God’s grace to aid it towards good in every single act?" (the sentence is split by Heurtley's note).
- Gennadius, about 480: NPNF203 42970: "Pelagius ... the heresiarch, before he was proclaimed a heretic wrote works of practical value for students: three books On belief in the Trinity, and one book of Selections from Holy Scriptures bearing on the Christian life."
- Faustus of Riez, about 474 (live story `faustus-of-riez`): `cic/texts/faustus-riez_de-gratia-and-collected-works_engelbrecht1891.txt` 3646: "Qiiod  Pelagii  seiisus,   qui  gratiam  iiegaiiit,   primo   loco    iie-" (that the view of Pelagius, who denied grace, must first be condemned).
- Editor's label: Warfield (NPNF105 954) on "semi-Pelagian" Gaul and Cassian as "the leader of this new movement". Editor's, not to be given to a source.

**I4. Britain: the teaching came home.** contemp. and later. **Documented** (Prosper); story told in `germanus-of-auxerre`.
- PROSPER 54151-54156 (set under 429): "Agricola Pelagianus, Severiani episcopi Pelagiani filius, / ecclesias Brittaniae dogmatis sui insinuatione corrumpit. sed / ad insinuationem Palladii diaconi papa Caelestinus Germa- / num Autisidorensem episcopum vice sua mittit et deturbatis / hereticis Britannos ad catholicam fidem dirigit."
- CONST 2833-2834 (Constantius of Lyon, about 480): "hinc  divina  auctoritas^,  inde  humana  prae* / sumptio;  hinc  fides,  inde  perfidia*;  inde  Pelagius^  auctor,  bine  Christus." Copied by Bede, BEDE-HE 1471-1473: "on the one side Divine faith, on the other human presumption ; on the one side piety, on the other pride ; on the one side Pelagius, on the other Christ." (Levison, in the Germanus story, says Bede copied Constantius, so no second witness.)
- Later legend: the Lives of David place a synod at Brefi against a Pelagian revival (live story `david-of-wales`). Late legend; Leave out.

**I5. His own letter survived under his opponent's name.** editor and later. **Documented** (that it was printed among Jerome's works).
- PL30 header 3-4: "Epistola I: Ad Demetriadem (in Patrologiae Cursus Completus, Series / Latina, Tomus XXX - printed among the spuria attributed to Jerome)".
- PL30 43-45 (Monitum; two columns interleaved): "Diu nutilumqua est , quod magno Hieronymo htee ... nequissimi hostis Pelagii epi- / stola , sire liber ascribitur." (It has long been ... that this letter or book of Pelagius, that most wicked enemy, is ascribed to the great Jerome.) OCR damaged.
- PL30 43-45 (Monitum, quoting Bede on the Song of Songs): "Hunc, inqutt , librum nonnulli ... s.incii el catholici doctoris Hieronymi esse ... (emere arbitrantur"; and PL30 68-69: "et cumprimis Ju- / liano ; ut dudum laudatus Beda existimabat" (Bede thought it Julian's).

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

1. **What he meant by grace.** His own claim (NPNF105 11255: "always to add the help of God’s grace"; PL30 78-81) against Augustine's judgment (NPNF105 11236, 11255). **Contested.** Tell both.
2. **Diospolis.** The synod declared him in communion (NPNF105 10270). Augustine says the judges judged rightly on what they heard, but he was not cleared (NPNF105 9486, 10277). Warfield's "disingenuousness" (NPNF105 910) is the editor's own view and stays out.
3. **The "easily" letter.** Augustine doubts it is Pelagius's (NPNF105 10381).
4. **Britain or "Scots".** Prosper and Mercator say Briton; Jerome's "Scots" phrase is held only through Holmes and Fremantle, and Fremantle's identification of the "hound of Albion" is his own.
5. **Arrival at Rome.** Warfield about 400; Fremantle 409.
6. **Diospolis date.** 415 (Warfield, note in NPNF105, Fremantle's table); 416 in Fremantle's note to the Dialogue (card).
7. **Carthage 418.** Prosper 214 bishops; Warfield about 200.
8. **Demetrias letter date.** 413 (Warfield); 413 or 414 (Migne's Monitum).
9. **Bethlehem violence.** Augustine: "are said"; Innocent: the women did not name the man.
10. **Late sources.** Bede (731) and Constantius (about 480) are later; the Lives of David are late legend.
11. **Birth and death.** Not held.
