## The four lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Pelagius, a monk from Britain who taught at Rome in the early 400s.
- **What happened:** He taught that God made human nature able to choose the good and live without sin. Bishops in Palestine cleared him in 415. Augustine wrote against him, Rome under Innocent condemned his followers in 417, and Zosimus briefly took his side. In 418 the emperor, the African bishops and Rome all turned against him.
- **What it was about:** Grace. Does goodness come from ourselves, using a nature God made able, or does God's help have to work inside the will itself?
- **Why the connection matters:** The church in the West defined grace against him. At Carthage in 418 the bishops ruled that grace does more than forgive and teach: it helps us act. That sentence held in Britain, in Gaul and at Ephesus in 431, and his own letter survived under Jerome's name.

No line is blank or wrong.

## Step 2: the diff against v3

There are four changes, each traced:

1. "Augustine even warned" → "Augustine warned". This is a cut and adds no claim.
2. The sentence "Augustine answered a book of his on nature ..." is cut. "Hurried on across the sea, to Palestine" becomes "sailed on to Palestine". NPNF105 10286 has "hastened away across the sea", and Beat 2 holds Palestine. Nothing is added.
3. "the old Lydda" is cut. The Caelestius sentence is replaced by "Some charges came from Caelestius: that Adam's sin harmed only Adam, and newborn babies are as Adam was before he sinned. Pelagius said these were not his, and condemned them." The source is NPNF105 9882-9883: "sundry statements charged against Pelagius, which are said to be found among the opinions of his disciple Cœlestius: ... that Adam's sin injured only himself and not the human race ... that new-born infants are in the same condition as Adam was before the fall". NPNF105 10263 has the disowning. This holds, under the outline's "What it was about" item 8. The wording is MINOR (finding 2).
4. The Cassian sentence: "charged the bishop of Constantinople with the Pelagians' teaching. Both, he wrote, made Christ a mere man who lived without sin, so others could too." The sources are NPNF211 48715-48718: "in saying that Jesus Christ had lived as a mere man without any stain of sin, they actually went so far as to declare that men could also be without sin if they liked". And 48740-48744: "he says exactly the same thing which the Pelagians said before him ... so he must maintain ... that all men can of themselves be without sin". This holds. It is credited to Cassian as his charge, and it is said of "the Pelagians", not of Pelagius himself, as item 20 requires.

No changed sentence carries a quote.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**1. BLOCKING: a jump back after the essence.** Augustine's warning to Demetrias's mother is dated by the held editor to the end of 417 or the start of 418 (PL33 62249-62250). It is told third, and then the story goes back to Rome before 411. The fix moves the paragraph to its place in the held order, between Zosimus (September 417) and the emperor's order (30 April 418). It does not date it. The hook and the first minute are untouched, and the count is unchanged.
- Delete the third paragraph from its place, the one beginning "Writing with a fellow bishop, Augustine warned Demetrias's mother against the book." and ending "but did not come from her."
- Insert it as a paragraph of its own after the paragraph ending "For a few months, Rome itself took his side." and before "On 30 April 418 ...".
- In it, "Writing with a fellow bishop, Augustine warned Demetrias's mother against the book." → "Writing with a fellow bishop, Augustine warned Demetrias's mother against Pelagius's letter." Use "Pelagius's", not "his": after the Zosimus paragraph, "his" could be heard as Zosimus's letter. Once the hook is far away, "the book" no longer points back clearly.
- Checked on a scratch copy with findings 1 and 2 applied: 675 words, grade 7.4, reading ease 78.9, longest sentence 28 words, pass.
- Cost: the warning no longer stands directly beside the letter. The quote itself repeats the letter's words ("These good things can only be in you"), so the word-against-word reading still holds.
- **Do not say line for the outline** (Opening 1 left room for this error): "Augustine's warning to Juliana told after the essence and before Beat 1. Say: in the hook before the essence only if the essence still ends within the first minute; otherwise in its held order, after Zosimus's letter of September 417 and before the emperor's order of 30 April 418 (Maurist date, PL33 62249-62250), with no date given in the story."

**2. MINOR: "Some charges came from Caelestius" can be heard as Caelestius accusing him.** The record says the charges were "said to be found among the opinions of his disciple Cœlestius" (NPNF105 9882). The accusers were the two bishops from Gaul.
- "Some charges came from Caelestius:" → "Some charges were Caelestius's views:" (the same five words).
