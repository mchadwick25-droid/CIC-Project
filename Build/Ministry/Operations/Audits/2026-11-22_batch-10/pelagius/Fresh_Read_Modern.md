## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Pelagius, a monk from Britain who lived long and taught at Rome in the early 400s.
- **What happened:** he taught that human nature, God's gift, can choose the good and live without sin; bishops in Palestine cleared him at Diospolis, and Rome under Zosimus took his side for a few months; but after Augustine answered him, the emperor, some two hundred African bishops and Rome condemned him in 418.
- **What it was about:** grace. Is God's help only the nature he made, his teaching, forgiveness and Christ's example, or does it help the will itself to do the good?
- **Why the connection matters:** the 418 rulings defined grace against him, point by point. The sentence held, in Britain, in Gaul (Cassian) and at Ephesus in 431. His own letter outlived him under Jerome's name.

Each named charge, teaching, book, quarrel or decision can be explained after one hearing: the letter to Demetrias, his teaching, the Diospolis charges (Caelestius's two views, given in full), his answer, the verdict and Augustine's doubt, the Bethlehem report, Innocent's condemnation, the letter to Innocent, Zosimus's letter, the emperor's order, the Carthage rulings, Rome's sentence, Augustine's reading of his grace, Britain 429, Cassian's charge, Ephesus 431. None is BLOCKING.
