# Pelagius: review

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Pelagius, a monk from Britain who taught at Rome and held that God made human nature able, by God's gift, to choose the good and live without sin.
- **What happened:** about 413 he told the young Demetrias that her spiritual riches could come only from herself; Augustine, with a fellow bishop, called "from you" poison. Bishops at Diospolis cleared him in 415; Innocent condemned his followers early in 417; Zosimus defended him for a few months; in 418 the emperor ordered him driven from Rome, the African bishops at Carthage ruled on grace, and Rome condemned him.
- **Why the connection matters:** the West said what grace is against him (it helps us do good, not only forgives and shows the way), the sentence held at Ephesus in 431 and in Cassian's Gaul, and his own letter survived only under Jerome's name.

**Essence** (census, and what he is known for): the teaching that human nature, as God made it, can choose the good and live without sin; the letter to Demetrias; cleared at Diospolis, then condemned in Africa and at Rome in 418 after Augustine's answer, so that a condemned teaching bears his name. **Also told:** what the letter says beside his opponents' reading of it; the Diospolis scene and Augustine's doubt; Rome's turn under Zosimus; the 418 rulings; the sentence holding; the letter's survival as Jerome's.
