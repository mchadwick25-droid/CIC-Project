## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Pelagius, a monk from Britain who taught at Rome in the early 400s. He held that God made human nature able, by God's gift, to choose the good and live without sin.
2. **What happened:** He wrote to the young Demetrias that her spiritual riches came "from you and in you", and Augustine called that "poison". Bishops at Diospolis cleared him in 415, and Zosimus took his side for a few months in 417. In 418 the emperor ordered him driven from Rome, the African bishops ruled on grace, and Rome condemned him.
3. **Why the connection matters:** His quarrel with Augustine made the Western church say what grace is. The African rulings answer, point by point, what Augustine said Pelagius meant by grace. Cassian and Ephesus kept the sentence, and his letter survived under Jerome's name.

No line is blank or wrong.
