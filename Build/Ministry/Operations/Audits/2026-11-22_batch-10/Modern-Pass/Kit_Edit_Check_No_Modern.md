## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: Kit Part 1, Rigor. The new line adds a list the ruling does not give

The ruling says "A modern editor's or historian's judgment". The edit adds "of a date, an author, a cause or what happened". A reader can take that list as complete, and so let through a modern judgment of a motive, a meaning or what a teaching held. The ruling's first sentence covers everything, so the list only narrows it. It also takes the sentence to about 38 words, with a clause nested inside it.

Replace:
> A modern editor's or historian's judgment of a date, an author, a cause or what happened, and any claim the outline rates Dominant Modern Reconstruction or Inferential-Thin, goes to the card as a modern view, never into the story.

With:
> A modern editor's or historian's judgment, and any claim the outline rates Dominant Modern Reconstruction or Inferential-Thin, goes to the card as a modern view, never into the story.

### 2. BLOCKING: researcher.md. "Leave out or the card" contradicts Part 1 and restates its rule

Part 1 now sends a modern judgment "to the card as a modern view". The researcher line sends it to "Leave out or the card", which allows it to be dropped entirely. The line also restates where the claim goes, which Part 1 already says. Ruling item 3 asks for this only: the researcher "marks a modern-only claim modern and keeps it out of the beats".

Replace:
> Where a claim rests only on an editor or a modern historian, mark it modern: it goes to Leave out or the card, never into a beat.

With:
> Where a claim rests only on an editor or a modern historian, mark it modern and keep it out of the beats (Part 1, Rigor).

### 3. MINOR: Kit Part 3, Who tells it. The field is now one sentence of about 70 words

The old/modern clause belongs in the field, but it should be a sentence of its own.

Replace:
> - **Who tells it:** for each main source, who wrote it and whether in the person's own time or later, and whether it is old (the original sources and the writers of the record itself) or modern (an editor, a translator's note or a historian writing on the record); where it matters, what the listener must know about the witness. Mark the claim each witness is credited beside.

With:
> - **Who tells it:** for each main source, who wrote it and whether in the person's own time or later; where it matters, what the listener must know about the witness. Mark each source old (the original sources and the writers of the record itself) or modern (an editor, a translator's note or a historian writing on the record). Mark the claim each witness is credited beside.

### 4. MINOR: reviewer.md step 3 and checker.md item 1. The definition of "modern" is repeated, and the checker leaves fields out

The reviewer line repeats the definition from Part 3 in brackets and runs to about 35 words. Part 3 holds that definition once, so the role should point to it. In `roles/reviewer.md`, replace:

With:

In `roles/checker.md`, replace:

With:

### 5. BLOCKING: checker.md item 7. The old line now allows what the rule forbids

"No claim rests on an editor where a primary text would carry it" implies that a claim may rest on an editor when no primary text carries it. The new rule says such a claim is marked modern and kept out of the story.

Replace:
> no held primary text or major later witness is missing, and no claim rests on an editor where a primary text would carry it.

With:
> no held primary text or major later witness is missing, and every claim that rests only on an editor or a modern historian is marked modern.

### 6. BLOCKING: Kit Part 2, Outside witness. The shape asks the story to carry modern scholarship

"The impact is in how later readers used the passage: the Christians who answered it, and the historians who still weigh it" and "Say plainly ... what about it is disputed" ask the story to tell modern historians' judgments, for example of a passage's authenticity. Under the new Part 1 line, those go to the card. Neither the ruling nor the edit touches this shape. Kit Part 4 says "a new ruling rewrites the line it touches", and this is a line the ruling touches. The replacement below keeps the dispute audible as an absence in the old sources, which the ruling allows ("the story says so"), and sends the modern views to the card. Changing a Part 2 shape is a method change, so Mark should confirm the wording.

Replace:
> The impact is in how later readers used the passage: the Christians who answered it, and the historians who still weigh it. Trap: making the witness a friend or a foe of the church, or stretching a few lines past what they say. Say plainly how short the passage is, and what about it is disputed.

With:
> The impact is in how later readers used the passage: the Christians who answered it, copied it or argued over it. Trap: making the witness a friend or a foe of the church, or stretching a few lines past what they say. Say plainly how short the passage is, and what the old sources leave open; the card carries the modern views on it.

### 7. MINOR: Kit Part 1, Rigor, the uncertainty line. "Two held sources" now covers modern ones

"Where two held sources differ, say so and credit each, rather than choosing one" would, read literally, have the story credit a modern editor who differs from an old source. The new line, two bullets above, forbids that.

Replace:
> Where two held sources differ, say so and credit each, rather than choosing one; the card carries what the story has no room for.

With:
> Where two old sources differ, say so and credit each, rather than choosing one; the card carries what the story has no room for.
