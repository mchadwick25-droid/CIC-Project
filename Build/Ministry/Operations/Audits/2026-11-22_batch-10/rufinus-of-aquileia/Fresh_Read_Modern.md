## Step 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Rufinus of Aquileia, a monk from northern Italy who spent nearly thirty years among the monks of Egypt and at Jerusalem, then came home and put Greek Christian books into Latin.
- **What happened:** He translated Origen's First Principles, leaving out or rewording what he judged others had added. Jerome took the preface as aimed at him, made a literal translation to expose Origen, and the bishop of Rome cut Rufinus off. Rufinus also translated Eusebius's Church History and carried it on, and he ended in Sicily, still translating Origen.
- **What it was about:** How to translate a suspect author. Rufinus cut or reworded what he believed heretics had put into Origen. Jerome charged that a sound-sounding Trinity left readers off guard against Origen's other teachings (souls sinning before birth, the devil saved, this flesh not rising), so he set down the Greek as it stood.
- **Why the connection matters:** The Latin church received much of Greek Christian writing, Origen and Eusebius's History among it, through Rufinus's Latin. The same books broke his friendship with Jerome and his standing with Rome, and the Greek historian Socrates built on his History, then corrected it.

**Named things, each explained on one hearing:** Jerome's letter (to draw him to Syria); Origen (Greek Bible scholar of the 200s, admired and attacked); First Principles (Origen's book on God and creation); Eusebius's history and Rufinus's two added books; the persecution in Egypt (the emperor against the monks for their creed); the Cypriot bishop's charges (three, stated); his letter (prayed Rufinus be freed from Origen's heresy); the short book (heretics falsified Origen); the preface and its method (quote 2, rule of faith glossed); Jerome's charge (Son and Spirit ranked lower; readers off guard); Jerome's literal translation (quote 3); Rufinus's defence to Rome (no champion; flesh rises, devil burns); the bishop of Rome's letter (Origen undermining the faith, the emperors' ban, Rufinus apart); the two defences; the History's purpose (comfort under the Goths); Socrates's rewrite (trusted the bishop who suffered); the Numbers sermons. All pass.

**Essence:** what he did (carried Greek Christian books into Latin: Origen and Eusebius), what it changed (the Latin church read them through him; it broke his friendship with Jerome), why he is on the tree (translator, the bridge from Greek to Latin, and the Jerome quarrel). Also told: why he changed the text, Jerome's answer, the cost at Rome, the History and Socrates, Sicily. The hook (Jerome's letter) leads straight to the essence, which is complete by the end of paragraph 2, about 130 words: inside the first minute. Each part of what is also told is present with its weight.

**Order:** after the essence, Egypt, Jerusalem, home, the preface, Jerome, Rome, the History, Sicily, told once each. The two "what came" sentences (Gennadius, Socrates) sit before the Sicily scene, which is the kit's sanctioned ending on the person after the connection lands; not a jump back in the life. Ends on the person, in his own hope for his eyesight.
