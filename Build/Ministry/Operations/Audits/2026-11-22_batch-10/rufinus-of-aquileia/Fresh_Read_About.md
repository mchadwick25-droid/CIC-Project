## The four lines

- **Who:** Rufinus of Aquileia, a monk from northern Italy in the late 300s. He spent nearly thirty years among the monks of Egypt and at Jerusalem, then came home and put Greek Christian books into Latin.
- **What happened:** He translated Origen's First Principles and said he had left out or reworded what he believed heretics had added. He also carried Eusebius's history of the church on in two books of his own. Jerome made a literal translation of his own to expose what was cut. The bishop of Rome disowned Rufinus, and the friendship never recovered. He died in Sicily, still translating Origen.
- **What it was about:** How to bring a disputed teacher into Latin. Rufinus cut what he judged was not Origen's. Jerome translated everything to show it. Behind that lay charges against Origen, each credited to the man who made it: the bishop from Cyprus (souls sinning before birth, the devil saved, this flesh not rising), Jerome (the Son below the Father, the Spirit below the Son), and the bishop of Rome (that Origen meant to undermine the faith).
- **Why the connection matters:** The Latin church read Origen and Eusebius largely through his Latin, and that Latin was shaped by his own judgment. The same books broke him from Jerome and from Rome.

All four come from one hearing. None is blank or wrong.
