# Outline check: Rufinus of Aquileia

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** A monk baptized at Aquileia who spent nearly thirty years in Egypt and Jerusalem (where he lived beside Melania the Elder and received pilgrims), then came home to Italy and put Greek Christian books into Latin: above all Origen's First Principles, which he says he cleared of passages he believed heretics had added, and Eusebius's Church History, to which he added two books of his own. His preface to First Principles drew Jerome, once his close friend, into a quarrel that ended their friendship. He is on the tree because much of Origen survives only in his Latin, and his History was the West's Eusebius for centuries.

**Also told.** Egypt: Didymus and the desert monks, and (his own word) prison and exile under Valens. The preface's method and Jerome's answer (a literal translation, and the charge that softening the Trinity let the rest slip by unwarned); the two Apologies; the bishop of Rome's cold letter; Augustine's grief at the break. The History written for Chromatius as the Goths broke into Italy, with Frumentius's story from Edesius; Socrates first followed it, then corrected it against Athanasius. The Commentary on the Creed of Aquileia. Last work: Origen on Numbers in Sicily, Rhegium burning across the strait (410, editors).

## Verdict: FIX

The heart matches mine in substance: the long years in Egypt and Jerusalem, the two translations (First Principles reworded, the Church History carried on in two books), Origen surviving in his Latin, and the preface that ended the friendship with Jerome. The essence comes within the first minute, after Opening 1. The beats follow the life once, in order. The ending is not told early. Every quote and almost every line holds at its cited lines. I checked N203, N206, N202, N201, N101, ANF4, PL23, MOM, BAE, CHR and PAL at each citation. One finding is BLOCKING, on length. The rest are MINOR.

My renderings, made before reading the outline's:

- Q2 (N203 45775-45780): "Wherever I found in his books anything against the truth about the Trinity, which elsewhere he states soundly, I either left it out as not his own, or set it down to agree with the rule of faith, which we find him holding to again and again." Same sense as the outline's two-sentence version. Nothing is added and no clause is lost.
- Q3 (PL23 31111-31115): "In the books On First Principles I simply set down what was in the Greek. I did not want the reader to believe what I translated, but not to believe what you had translated before." Same as the outline's. "I.7" is the right section (N203 51308).
