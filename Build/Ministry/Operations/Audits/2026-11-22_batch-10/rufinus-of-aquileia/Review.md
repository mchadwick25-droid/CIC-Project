# Review: Rufinus of Aquileia (`rufinus-of-aquileia`), 2026-11-23

## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Rufinus, a monk of Aquileia in northern Italy, who spent nearly thirty years among the monks of Egypt and at Jerusalem, then came home and put Greek Christian books into Latin.
2. **What happened:** He translated Origen's First Principles, leaving out or rewording what he judged corrupt, and Eusebius's Church History, adding two books of his own. The preface to First Principles drew Jerome, once his close friend, into a quarrel: Jerome made a literal translation to expose the rest, the bishop of Rome cut Rufinus off, and the friendship never recovered.
3. **Why it matters:** Much of Origen survives chiefly in his Latin, and his History was the West's Eusebius through the Middle Ages; the Greek historian Socrates built on it and then corrected it. The break with Jerome came through those same books, over how to translate.

**Essence, set from the census and the record:** a Latin monk who carried Greek Christian learning (Origen, Eusebius) into Latin, shaping the text as he went, and whose way of translating ended his friendship with Jerome. **Also told:** why he changed the text (heretics' additions, Jerome's own precedent); Jerome's answer; the cost at Rome; the History, written as the Goths broke in, and Socrates's use of it; the last work in Sicily. ## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The narrator carries Rufinus's side.** "He followed that method:" states as fact the very claim the quarrel turned on: that Rufinus did what Jerome had done. That is Rufinus's own account in his preface (N203 45750-45755, 45775-45780); Jerome denied it, and the editor judges "the use made of his previous action can hardly be justified" (N203 45702-45706). Change to: "He said he followed that method." (a full stop, not a colon, so the quote stands as its own sentence; item 9 below).
*Do not say line for the outline:* "He followed that (Jerome's) method" in the narrator's voice. Say: he said he followed that friend's method; whether he did is the quarrel.

**BLOCKING 2. Length.** 740 words by the scorer against the approved 675 (Mark's ruling for this batch). The cuts are below.

**MINOR 1.** "And its preface broke his friendship with Jerome" follows the Church History sentence and can be heard as that book's preface. Say "the preface to First Principles" (item 2).

**MINOR 2.** "He wrote to draw him east": Jerome wrote from Antioch (N206 3759-3760), and Rufinus was already in Egypt, so "east" misleads. Say "to Syria" (item 4).

**MINOR 3.** "and told Rufinus why": the line is in Jerome's Apology, written to Pammachius and Marcella, turning on Rufinus as "you". Drop the clause (item 14).

**MINOR 4.** The Deuteronomy sentence hangs "if God gave him back his eyesight" on "were left". The source: Deuteronomy alone was wanting, and he hoped to add it if God restored his eyesight (N203 59011-59013). Recast (item 22).

**MINOR 5.** "Chromatius wanted listeners to forget their sufferings for a while": the source says "to some extent become oblivious" (N203 58747-58750). Say "a little" (item 18).

**MINOR 6.** "from here each man wrote against the other" and, a paragraph later, "Each man wrote a full defense against the other" say the same thing twice. Keep the second (item 12); it still credits both partisans once.

**MINOR 7.** Socrates "reading the account of a bishop who had suffered in those events" leaves "those events" and "the account" vague; the shorter sentence (item 20) keeps the lifted job (more credit to the one who suffered, N202 5640-5660) in the narrator's words.
