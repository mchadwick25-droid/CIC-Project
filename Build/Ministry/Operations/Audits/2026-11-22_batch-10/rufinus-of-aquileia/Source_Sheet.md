## 1. Setting

**S1. Baptized at Aquileia, taught the creed there.** Own. Documented (as his own account).
- N203 56738-56740 (Commentary on the Creed): "We, however, follow that order which we received when we were baptized in the Church of Aquileia."
- N203 45998-46000 (Apology to Anastasius): "This is the doctrine which has been handed down to me by those from whom I received holy baptism in the Church of Aquileia"
- N203 46563-46575 (Apology to Apronianus I.4): "I was already living in a monastery, where, as both he and all others know, about 30 years ago, I was made regenerate by Baptism, and received the seal of the faith at the hands of those saintly men, Chromatius, [note omitted] Jovinus [note omitted] and Eusebius, [note omitted] all of them now bishops ... and to me a spiritual father, my teacher in the creed and the articles of belief."

**S2. The Aquileian circle: Jerome's word for its clergy.** Contemporary (Jerome's Chronicle). Documented.
- CHR 46257-46258 (Latin): "Aquileienses clerici quasi chorus bea- / torum habentur." Plain sense: the clergy of Aquileia are held to be like a choir of the blessed.
- Editor: N203 46247-46250 (Fremantle): "They had been close friends in early youth ... and had together formed part of a society of young Christian ascetics at Aquileia in the years 370-3."

**S3. Native town.** Contemporary + editor. Widely Accepted.
- N206 18428-18429 (Jerome, Letter 81, 399): "would have drawn you to your native country, [note: Concordia, near Aquileia.] had not grief for your mother deterred you". The name Concordia is the editors' note, not Jerome's word here. Fremantle, N203 44045-44048, puts the birth at Concordia "about the year 344 or 345" (editor's reckoning).

**S4. Away nearly thirty years.** Own. Documented.
- N203 45950-45955 (Apology to Anastasius, 400 by Fremantle): "I have just returned to my family [note omitted] after an absence of nearly 30 years"
- Editor: N203 44037-44039: "an Italian who had lived some 25 years in Greek lands"

## 2. Events, in order

**E1. 374: Jerome writes to him in Egypt.** Contemporary (Jerome, own letter). Documented. Latin of Jerome's letters not held; English printed 1893.
- N206 3759-3760 (editor heading): "Written from Antioch, 374 a.d., to Rufinus in Egypt."
- N206 3769-3774: "now, dearest Rufinus, I have had proof of it in my own case. For I who fancied it too bold a wish to be allowed by an exchange of letters to counterfeit to myself your presence in the flesh, hear that you are penetrating the remotest parts of Egypt, visiting the monks and going round God's family upon earth."
- N206 3936-3942: "I beseech you do not let me pass wholly out of sight and out of mind. A friend is long sought, hardly found, and with difficulty kept. ... Love is not to be purchased, and affection has no price. The friendship which can cease has never been real. Farewell in Christ."
- N206 3976-3979 (Letter 4, to Florentius, same time): "Our brother, Rufinus, is said to have come from Egypt to Jerusalem with the devout lady, Melanium. He is inseparably bound to me in brotherly love". 3984-3986: "He has but now washed himself [note omitted] and is clean, yea, is made white as snow".

**E2. Egypt: six years, then two more; Didymus and the desert monks.** Own. Documented (his claim, written against Jerome).
- N203 49434-49444 (Apology to Apronianus II): "But I, for the sake of God's work, stayed six years, and again after an interval for two more, where Didymus lived, of whom alone you boast ... and, to come to the teachers of the desert, on whom I attended frequently and earnestly, Macarius the disciple of Anthony, and the other Macarius, and Isidore and Pambas, all of them friends of God, who taught me those things which they themselves were learning from God."

**E3. Prison and exile in Egypt.** Own; repeated by a later historian from his account. Documented as his claim; one witness.
- N203 45966-45969 (Apology to Anastasius): "My faith, indeed, was sufficiently proved when the heretics persecuted me. I was at that time sojourning in the church of Alexandria, and underwent imprisonment and exile which was then the penalty of faithfulness".
- MOM 29405-29407 (Church History XI.4, Latin): "quae praesens / vidi, loquor et eorum gesta refero, quorum in passionibus socius esse / promerui." Plain sense: what I saw when present I tell, and I report the deeds of those whose sufferings I was found worthy to share.
- N202 13725-13727 (Socrates IV.24, c. 439, about 30 years after Rufinus's death): "The emperor Valens having issued an edict commanding that the orthodox should be persecuted both in Alexandria and in the rest of Egypt". 13741-13742: "Rufinus declares that he was not only a witness of these cruelties, but also one of the sufferers."

**E4. Jerusalem beside Melania the Elder; priest; host to pilgrims.** Later, friendly (Palladius, c. 420, about ten years after). Documented for the fact of the house; the praise is Palladius's.
- PAL 501 (Lausiac History 46): "With her lived also the most noble Rufinus, from Italy, of the city of Aquileia, a man similar to her in character and very stedfast, who was afterwards judged worthy of the priesthood. A more learned man or a kinder than he was not to be found among men. [6] So these two during twenty-seven years receiving at their own charges those who visited Jerusalem in pursuance of a vow, bishops and monks and virgins, edified all who visited them, and they reconciled the schism of Paulinus, some 400 monks in all".
- Editor: PAL 1223 (Clarke's note 292): "Palladius takes Rufinus' part unhesitatingly in the famous quarrel between him and Jerome."
- Editor only: "Mount of Olives" is Fremantle's (N203 44094-44097, 44102-44104), not Palladius's word.

**E5. The first rift in Palestine, and the peace before he sailed (397).** Contemporary (Epiphanius; Jerome). Documented.
- N206 10976-10979 (Epiphanius to John of Jerusalem, Letter 51, in Jerome's Latin, tr. 1893): "But may God free you, my brother, and the holy people of Christ which is intrusted to you, and all the brothers who are with you, and especially the presbyter Rufinus, from the heresy of Origen, and other heresies".
- N203 46255-46260 (editor, Fremantle): "they became estranged, Jerome siding with Epiphanius and Rufinus with John ... They were reconciled before Rufinus left Palestine in 397".
- N203 55905-55908 (Jerome, Apology III.33, 402-403): "To sin is human, to lay snares is diabolical. What! Was it for this that I joined hands with you over the slain lamb in the Church of the Resurrection, that I might 'steal your manuscripts at Rome'?" Latin PL23 37479-37481: "Peccare enim hominis est: insidias tendere, dia- / boli. Ergo ideo in Anastasi? immolato agno dex- / leras junximus". Plain sense: we joined right hands in the Anastasis (the Church of the Resurrection) over the sacrificed lamb, that is, at the Eucharist.
- N203 55557-55560 (Jerome, Apology III): "It is true we gave you the signs of peace, but not to embrace heresy; we joined hands, we accompanied you as you set forth on your journey, on the understanding that you were catholic not that we were heretical."

**E6. Back in Italy; friends ask for Origen; the Defence of Origen.** Own; editor for dates. Documented.
- N203 44204-44207 (editor): "He arrived in Italy, in company with Melania, early in the spring of 397." Fremantle dates the First Principles preface 397 (heading N203 45693-45694) and its publication "early in the year 398" (44222). Sources: one editor, two dates; tell no year for it.
- N203 45742-45745 (preface to First Principles): "But your influence is such, my most faithful brother Macarius, that even the consciousness of my unfitness is not sufficient to make me resist. I have therefore yielded to your importunity though it was against my resolution".
- N203 45759-45765: "The cause of these variations I have set forth very fully for your information in the Apology which Pamphilus wrote for the books of Origen, to which I have appended a very short treatise [note omitted] showing by proofs which seem to me quite clear that his books have been in very many cases falsified by heretical and ill-disposed persons. This is especially the case with the books which you now require me to translate, namely, the Περὶ ᾽Αρχῶν".
- Authorship of that Defence: Jerome later named Eusebius (N203 44326-44328, editor's summary). Told in `pamphilus-of-caesarea`; here only "a defence of Origen he gave as the martyr Pamphilus's".

**E7. The preface to First Principles: Jerome praised, unnamed; Rufinus's method.** Own. Documented. Latin not held; two English prints of 1892 and 1893 agree.
- N203 45721-45726: "Among these was that brother and associate of mine to whom this request was made by bishop Damasus, and who when he translated the two homilies on the Song of Songs from Greek into Latin prefixed to the work a preface [note omitted] so full of beauty and so magnificent that he awoke in every one the desire of reading Origen".
- N203 45750-45755: "He, after translating into Latin above seventy of the books of Origen which he called Homiletics, and also a certain number of the 'Tomes,' proceeded to purge and pare away in his translation all the causes of stumbling which are to be found in the Greek works; and this he did in such a way that the Latin reader will find nothing in them which jars with our faith."
- **N203 45775-45780 (quoted):** "Wherever therefore I have found in his books anything contrary to the truth concerning the Trinity which he has in other places spoken of in a strictly orthodox sense, I have either omitted it as a foreign and not genuine expression or set it down in terms agreeing with the rule of faith which we find him constantly assenting to."
- Second print, N206 18367-18372: "Wherever then in his books I have found a statement concerning the Trinity contrary to those which in other places he has faithfully made on the same subject, I have either omitted the passage as garbled and misleading or have substituted that view of the matter which I find him to have frequently asserted."
- N203 45783-45787: "in these cases I have made the passage plain by adding words which I had read in other books of his where the matter was more fully treated. I have done this in the interest of clearness: but I have put in nothing of my own; I have only given him back his own words, though taken from other passages."
- N203 45802-45805 (his charge to copyists): "that he should neither add nor take away, that he should neither insert nor change, anything in that which is written but that he should compare his copy with that from which it is copied and correct it critically letter for letter".
- Editor: N203 45702-45706 (Fremantle): "The Preface to them was intended to remove prejudices by showing that Jerome (who though not named is clearly described) had been Rufinus' precursor in translating Origen. The compliments paid to Jerome were no doubt sincere: but the use made of his previous action can hardly be justified."
- Editor: ANF4 21996-22002 (1885): "The work has come down to us in the Latin translation of his admirer Rufinus; but, from a comparison of the few fragments of the original Greek which have been preserved, we see that Rufinus was justly chargeable with altering many of Origen's expressions, in order to bring his doctrine on certain points more into harmony with the orthodox views of the time."

**E8. Jerome's answer: a literal translation and a charge.** Contemporary, hostile (Jerome, Apology I, 402 by Fremantle). Documented as Jerome's charge; Contested as to Rufinus's intent.
- N206 18445-18447 (Letter 81, to Rufinus, 399): "The short preface to the books περὶ ᾽Αρχῶν which has been sent to me I recognize as yours by the style. You know best with what intention it was written; but even a fool can see how it must necessarily be understood. Covertly or rather openly I am the person aimed at."
- N203 51287-51303 (Apology I.6, quoting his letter to Pammachius): "As soon as I had run my eye over these documents, I at once noticed that the impious doctrine enunciated by Origen about the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, to which the ears of Romans could not bear to listen, had been changed by the translator so as to give a more orthodox meaning. His other doctrines ... I found that he had either translated as they stood in the Greek, or had stated them in a stronger and exaggerated manner in words taken from the books of Didymus ... The effect of all this is that the reader, finding that the book expressed the catholic doctrine on the Trinity, would take in these heretical views without warning." Latin PL23 31077-31091: "Quaecum legissem, contulis- / semque cum Craco, illico animadverti ... ut qui in / Trinitate catholicum legerat, in aliis hereticum / non caveret."
- **N203 51325-51331 (quoted):** "I expressed in Latin just what I found in the Greek text of the books Περὶ ᾽Αρχῶν, not wishing the reader to believe what was in my translation, but wishing him not to believe what was in yours." **Latin PL23 31111-31115 (rendered from):** "in libris περὶ / ᾿Αρλῶν simpliciter quod in Greco habebatur ex- / pressi; non ut crederet lector his qui interpreta- / bar; sed ne crederet illis, que tu ante transtule- / ras."
- Editor: ANF4 22126-22128: "Jerome undertook a new translation of the work; but only small portions of his version have reached our day."

**E9. Rufinus's defence; the bishop of Rome against him.** Own (Rufinus) and contemporary, hostile (Anastasius). Documented.
- N203 46059-46065 (Apology to Anastasius, 400): "I admit that I put something of my own into the work; as I stated in my Preface, I used my own discretion in cutting out not a few passages; but only those as to which I had come to suspect that the thing had not been so stated by Origen himself". 46070-46071: "I am not a defender or a champion of Origen; nor am I the first who has translated his works."
- N203 46150-46152 (Anastasius to John of Jerusalem, 401 by Fremantle): "Rufinus, about whom you have done me the honour to ask my advice, must bring his conscience to the bar of the divine majesty."
- N203 46156-46159: "As for Origen, whose writings he has translated into our language, I have neither formerly known, nor do I now seek to know either who he was or what expression he may have given to his thought."
- N203 46203-46208 (Anastasius quoting his letter to Venerius): "the decree issued by our Emperors, [note omitted] by which every one who serves God is warned against the reading of Origen, and all who are convicted of reading his impious works are condemned by the imperial judgment.' In these words my formal sentence was pronounced."
- N203 46222-46226: "he is so completely separate from all part or lot with us, that I neither know nor wish to know either what he is doing or where he is living. I have only to add that it is for him to consider where he may obtain absolution."
- Editor: N203 44414-44416 (Fremantle): "there is no reason to suppose, as has been often done, that the Pope passed sentence upon him, still less that he summoned him to Rome."

**E10. The two Apologies; the friendship ends.** Own, contemporary, later, editor.
- Rufinus's Apology to Apronianus, two books (N203 46229-50868); Jerome's Apology, three books (N203 50868-56448).
- N203 42490-42494 (Gennadius, Lives 17, c. 480-490, some 75 years later): "Moreover he responded to a detractor of his works, in two volumes, arguing and proving that he exercised his talent with the aid of the Lord and in the sight of God, for the good of the church, while he, on the other hand, incited by jealousy had taken to polemics."
- Editor: N203 44366-44368 (Fremantle): "This book closed the controversy. Rufinus did not reply, Jerome did not relent. Nothing in Rufinus' subsequent writings reflects on Jerome".
- Editor: N206 18417-18422 (heading of Letter 81): "Being sent in the first instance to Pammachius this latter treacherously suppressed it and thus put an end to all hope of the reconciliation of the two friends." Editor's view; not carried.

**E11. Augustine's grief at the break (404).** Contemporary, outside the quarrel. Documented.
- N101 32949-32951: "In short, where is the friend who may not be feared as possibly a future enemy, if the breach that we deplore could arise between Jerome and Rufinus?" Latin CSEL34 21514-21516: "quis denique amicus / non formidetur quasi futurus inimicus, si potuit inter Hiero- / nymum et Kufinum hoc, quod plangimus, exoriri?" (OCR "Kufinum" for Rufinum). Date: N101 heading "(a.d. 404.)".

**E12. Church History for Chromatius, as the Goths broke into Italy.** Own. Documented; date differs among editors.
- MOM 26045-26056 (Latin preface): "quod tu quoque, venerande pater Chromati, ... tempore, quo diruptis Italiae claustris Alarico duce Gothorum se pestifer / morbus infudit ... iniungis mihi ut ecclesiasticam histo- / ram, quam vir eruditissimus Eusebius Caesariensis Graeco sermone / conscripserat, in Latinum verterem".
- N203 58747-58750: "You thought that the mind of those who heard it read to them might be so held fast by it that, in its eager desire for the knowledge of past events, it might to some extent become oblivious of their actual sufferings."
- MOM 26099-26102: "decimum vero vel undecimum librum nos / conseripsimus partim ex maiorum traditionibus, partim ex his, quae / nostra lam memoria conprehenderat et eos velut duos pisciculos / supra seriptis panibus addidimus." English N203 58783-58786: "The tenth and eleventh books I have myself compiled, partly from the traditions of the former generation, partly from facts within my own memory; and these I have added to the previous books, like the two fishes to the loaves."
- N203 58789-58791: "my own two books those from the days of Constantine when the persecution came to an end on to the death of the Emperor Theodosius."
- Dates differ: Fremantle 401 (N203 44550-44555); the MOM file header says 402-403. Tell no year.

**E13. The Frumentius story, from Edesius himself.** Own; later historian dependent on him. Documented that Rufinus says so.
- MOM 27383-27385: "quae nos ita gesta non opinione vulgi, sed ipso Edesio Tyri / presbytero postmodum facto, qui Frumentii comes prius fuerat, referente / cognovimus." Plain sense: we learned these things not from common report but from Edesius himself, Frumentius's companion, later a priest at Tyre.
- N202 4372-4374 (Socrates I.19): "Rufinus assures us that he heard these facts from Edesius, who was afterwards ordained to the priesthood at Tyre." Greek HUS 11715-11716.

**E14. The Commentary on the Creed.** Own; later praise. Documented.
- N203 57400-57402: "it should be known that the clause, 'He descended into Hell,' is not added in the Creed of the Roman Church, neither is it in that of the Oriental Churches."
- N203 42482-42484 (Gennadius): "published an Exposition of the Apostles' creed so excellent that other expositions are regarded as of no account in comparison."

**E15. 410: Sicily, Rhegium burning, Origen on Numbers.** Own. Documented; the year is the editor's.
- BAE 2429-2431 (Latin): "In conspectu etenim, ut videbas etiam / 10 ipse, nostro Barbarus, qui Regini oppidi miscebat incendia, angustissimo / a nobis freto, quod Italiae solum Sieulo dirimit, arcebatur." English N203 58989-58992: "As you yourself saw, the Barbarian was within sight of us; he had set fire to the city of Rhegium, and our only protection against him was the very narrow sea which separates the soil of Italy from Sicily."
- BAE 2434-2436: "proponitur? Tamen si qua nox auimos minore metu hostilis concussit / 15 exeidii et breve saltem lucubrandi otium dedit, quasi ad solamen ma- / ᾿ orum levandaeque peregrinationis gratia". English N203 58995-58998: "However, when there was a quiet night, and our minds were relieved from the fear of an attack by the enemy, and we got at least some little leisure for thought, I set to work, as a solace from our troubles, and to relieve the burden of our pilgrimage".
- N203 59011-59015 (English only; Latin damaged at BAE 2477-2484): "the short comments upon Deuteronomy alone are wanting; these, if God so will, and if he restores my eye-sight, I hope to add to the body of the work. Indeed, my very loving son Pinianus, whose truly Christian company I have joined in their flight".
- Editor: N203 58972-58975: "Written in 410. The date is fixed by the burning of Rhegium by Alaric". N203 44447-44449: "This translation of Numbers was his last work."

**E16. Death.** Contemporary hostile line + editors' reading. Inferential-Thin for the year and place; Dominant Modern Reconstruction among the editors here.
- N206 47992-47998 (Jerome, preface to Ezekiel I, 410-414): "and Rufinus [note: who died a.d. 410, in Sicily ...] the scorpion lies beneath the ground with [note] Enceladus and Porphyrion, and the many-headed Hydra has at length ceased to hiss at us". The name Rufinus and the date are the editors' inserted note; Jerome's text does not name him.
- N203 42466 (Richardson's note to Gennadius): "Born 345, at Jerusalem about 390, died 410." Editor.

## 3. Impact, heaviest first

**I1. Origen in Latin.** Later witness + editors. Widely Accepted.
- N203 42467-42470 (Gennadius): "had a fine talent for elegant translation from Greek into Latin. In this way he opened to the Latin speaking church the greater part of the Greek literature". 42480-42481: "Not all of Origen, however, is his work, for Jerome translated some which are identified by his prologue."
- Editor: ANF4 22122-22124: "It is much to be regretted that the original Greek of the De Principiis has for the most part perished. We possess it chiefly in a Latin translation by Rufinus."
- Editor: N203 44027-44030, 44041-44043 (Fremantle): "it is to his translations that we are indebted for our knowledge of many of the works of Origen, including the greatest of them all, the Περὶ ᾽Αρχῶν ... a considerable part of the works of the great Alexandrian have floated down across the ocean of the Dark Ages, and, while lost in their native Greek, have in their Latin garb come to enrich the later civilization of the West."
- What he defended (marked): Origen's books as corrupted by others, and his own right to translate them (N203 45759-45765; 46053-46073).

**I2. The Church History carried Eusebius to the West, and fed the Greek historians.** Later + editors. Documented (Socrates); Widely Accepted (editors).
- N202 5640-5660 (Socrates II.1): "Rufinus, who wrote an Ecclesiastical History in Latin, [note omitted] has erred in respect to chronology. ... Now we in the first place wrote the first two books of our history following Rufinus ... Afterward, however, we perused the writings of Athanasius ... and judged that more credit was due to him who had suffered, and to those who were witnesses of the things they describe, than to such as have been dependent on conjecture, and had therefore erred." 5666-5668: "On this account we were compelled to revise the first and second books of this history, using, however, the testimony of Rufinus where it is evident that he could not be mistaken." Greek HUS 15491-15493, 15529-15543. Lifted on the slate for this job; narrated here, not quoted.
- Editor: N201 5232-5233 (McGiffert): "The version of Rufinus had a large circulation, and became in the Western Church a substitute for the original throughout the Middle Ages."
- Frumentius: E13.

**I3. The Creed.** E14; Gennadius's praise. Also the census tradition's own sourcing (the creed of Aquileia survives through this work).

**I4. How the quarrel was remembered.** Contemporary. E11 (Augustine). Editor-only and not held: Pope Gelasius's list, quoted by Fremantle N203 44473-44479 from Migne PL 59 (not in the Library); not carried.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **His method.** Rufinus: he cut or reworded only passages he believed heretics had put into Origen's books, as Jerome had done before him (E7, E9). Jerome: he made the Trinity sound and left the rest, so readers would not be on guard (E8). Editors (ANF4): he did alter Origen's expressions. Contested as to intent; Documented that he changed the text, by his own account.
- **The Defence of Origen.** Rufinus: Pamphilus's. Jerome: Eusebius's. Told in `pamphilus-of-caesarea`; not carried here.
- **Prison and exile.** His own word only; Socrates repeats it as his claim (E3).
- **The bishop of Rome.** Anastasius's letter says Rufinus is separate from them and must answer to God; Fremantle finds no formal sentence or summons (E9).
- **Dates.** Birth about 344-345 (Fremantle) or 345 (Richardson): editors only. First Principles 397 or 398 (one editor, two dates). Church History 401 (Fremantle) or 402-403 (file header). Numbers preface 410 (editor, from Rhegium). Death 410 in Sicily: editors' reading of Jerome's unnamed "scorpion".
- **Witnesses' sides.** Rufinus and Jerome are each partisan on the other. Palladius took Rufinus's side (Clarke's note). Gennadius praises him and blames his "detractor" (E10).
