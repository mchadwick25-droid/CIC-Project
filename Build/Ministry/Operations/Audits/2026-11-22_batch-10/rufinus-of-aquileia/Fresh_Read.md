## The three lines

1. **Who:** Rufinus, a monk of Aquileia in northern Italy, who spent nearly thirty years among the monks of Egypt and at Jerusalem, then came home and put Greek Christian books into Latin.
2. **What happened:** he translated Origen's First Principles, leaving out or rewording what he judged others had added, and Eusebius's Church History, adding two books of his own. His preface praised an unnamed friend's method. Jerome took it as aimed at him, made his own literal version to expose the book, and their friendship ended. The bishop of Rome broke with Rufinus too.
3. **Why it matters:** the Latin church read Origen and Eusebius largely through his Latin, and the way he translated broke him from Jerome's circle. Both sides of the quarrel come through in their own words.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **"Rufinus was wholly apart from them" reads as "apart from the emperors."** In the brief: "But the bishop of Rome wrote to the bishop of Jerusalem that the emperors had banned reading Origen. Rufinus was wholly apart from them and must answer to God, he wrote, ..." The nearest "them" is the emperors. A listener hears that Rufinus stood apart from the emperors, or defied them. Anastasius wrote that Rufinus "is so completely separate from all part or lot with us" (N203 46222-46224): apart from the bishop of Rome's own communion. This is a misleading implication on one hearing, on the record's conflict.
   **Change:** "Rufinus was wholly apart from them and must answer to God, he wrote," → "Rufinus was wholly apart from Rome and must answer to God, he wrote," (13 words for 13; the count stays at 675).
   **Do not say line to add to the outline** (its own Do not say wording, "wholly apart from them", left room for it): *"Rufinus was wholly apart from them" next to the emperors or any group other than the bishop of Rome's own. Say: wholly apart from Rome (Anastasius's "us"), so that it cannot be heard as apart from the emperors (N203 46222-46224).*

### MINOR

1. **"six years in Egypt and then two more."** Rufinus writes "stayed six years, and again after an interval for two more" (N203 49435-49436); the outline says "later two more". "Then" makes it one unbroken stay of eight years.
   **Change:** "He spent six years in Egypt and then two more, he writes," → "He spent six years in Egypt and later two more, he writes," (same count).
2. **"had made the Trinity sound to put readers off guard."** Heard aloud, "sound" can be taken as the verb ("made the Trinity sound ..."), and the sentence stalls for a beat. Optional.
   **Change, if taken:** "Jerome charged that Rufinus had made the Trinity sound to put readers off guard against the rest." → "Jerome charged that Rufinus had set the Trinity right to put readers off guard against the rest." (same count; Jerome: "changed by the translator so as to give a more orthodox meaning", N203 51290-51292).
3. **The Frumentius clause the slate gave this story is gone.** The batch header gives this story "a clause here" for Frumentius. The brief drops it under the outline's approved Cut first item 1, as the batch's length ruling allows. No change to the brief; the card should carry the tie (Rufinus says he had the story from Frumentius's companion, MOM 27383-27385) so the link to `frumentius` is not lost.
