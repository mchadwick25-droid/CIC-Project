# What-and-why scan: earlier batches (2–9), live scripts

Scanned 2026-11-23. 24 live Church Family Tree briefs, three from each of batches 2–9, each read once as a listener would hear it. One question only: does the listener come away knowing what this life and its conflicts were *about*? That means what the person taught and why it mattered, what they were accused of, and what a quarrel, exile, council or book was over. Nothing was rewritten.

Verdicts: CLEAR (no real gap, or one minor one), SOME GAPS, MANY GAPS.

## Batch 2

### benedict-of-nursia: CLEAR
The Rule's content comes through: lifelong vows of stability, conversion of life and obedience, work and holy reading, the abbot's balance ("the strong have something to long for, and the weak do not run away"), guests received as Christ. Its weight is shown too: Carloman's council in 742 and Charlemagne at Aachen in 802 make it law. No real gap.
Relevance: good. The listener learns he matters because his small rule became the rule of Western monks.

### hild-of-whitby: SOME GAPS
- "the two kept Easter on different dates … the kings met in her house to settle the date of Easter". The script says what the synod was over, but not why a date was worth a royal council: which church the English would follow, and one Easter for one kingdom.
- "Colman, his teaching rejected". The listener can only guess that "teaching" means the Easter reckoning.
- Hild's own convictions come through well: shared goods, Scripture study, peace.
Relevance: good on her house (five bishops, Caedmon). Whitby is told as an event, not as a turning point.

### martin-of-tours: SOME GAPS
- "Arian opponents had him beaten in public and driven from a city". Arians taught what? And why did they oppose Martin?
- "prosecuting Priscillian and his followers for heresy". Heresy of what? The script's point (a secular court judging the church) is clear, but the charge has no content.
- "sharing communion for one day with the bishops behind the deaths … grieved over it". The listener is not told why sharing communion with them was a compromise for Martin, though it can half be inferred.
- "I am Christ's soldier. I am not allowed to fight" is fully clear.
Relevance: good. Soldier-monk-bishop, model saint's life, Tours as a shrine.

## Batch 3

### alopen: MANY GAPS
- "the man who first brought the books of their church". Which books? Of what faith?
- "the emperor studied the teaching and ordered it spread". The teaching was what? The listener never hears a word of what Alopen preached.
- "the Church of the East". Never explained: what kind of Christianity, or from where.
- "Buddhists spoke out against it and others mocked it". Over what?
- "an edict of 845 that sent more than three thousand foreign teachers … back to ordinary life". Why? (It was a purge of foreign religions, mostly aimed at Buddhism.)
Relevance: the "first mission to China" and the rediscovered stone come through. What was brought, and why it mattered to the Chinese who received it, does not.

### moses-the-black: CLEAR
The repentance, the long struggle with lust, non-violence ("I am not allowed to hurt anyone") and the humility under racial scorn all have plain content.
- Minor: "counted among their great ones … left seventy disciples". Great for what teaching? The humility sayings carry it, but barely.
Relevance: good. He matters as proof of what repentance can do.

### vardan-mamikonian: CLEAR
The conflict is fully stated: the Persian order to worship the sun and keep the laws of the Magi, the pretended conversion, the refusal, the Maccabees, Avarayr, and the freedom of worship won later. The stakes are spoken in his own words.
- Minor: "the laws of the Magi" could use one clause on what they required.
Relevance: strong. A lost battle that won a people's freedom to keep their faith.

## Batch 4

### antony-the-great: SOME GAPS
- "The Arians taught that the Son of God was created … he taught that the Son was not created." The listener hears what was said, but not why it mattered to a desert monk enough to come down to the city.
- "his doubt was gone". Augustine's doubt about what? (Whether he could give up his old life.)
- "directed them as a father". What he taught his monks is absent: prayer, discernment, the fight with demons.
Relevance: strong on influence (the Life as the law of monks, and Augustine's conversion). Thinner on Antony's own teaching.

### dhuoda: SOME GAPS
- The book's content is well given: seventy-three chapters, love of God, loyalty, justice, prayer.
- "his sons … went to war over the empire". Over what? And which side was Bernard on?
- "her husband Bernard had the baby brought to him, with the bishop of Uzès". The listener is not told that this took her child from her, or why.
- "In 844 the Franks judged Bernard guilty of treason". Treason how?
- "In 848 William seized Ampurias and Barcelona by trickery". Why? (To avenge his father, presumably.) The family conflict that frames the book stays opaque.
Relevance: good on the book's afterlife (oldest writing by a Frenchwoman, the catechism-before-Luther point).

### radegund: SOME GAPS
- "under it a nun stayed inside the walls for life". This is clear.
- "the bishop of Poitiers would not receive the relic". Why not?
- "if anyone, even the city's own bishop, tried after her death to break the Rule or replace the abbess". The convent-versus-bishop tension is the heart of the letter, but it is never explained.
- "forty or more nuns walked out in revolt". Revolt over what?
Relevance: good. Captive queen, founder, guardian letter.

## Batch 5

### athanasius-of-alexandria: SOME GAPS
- The doctrine and its stakes are well done: "so that people might be made divine … Only a Son who was truly God could do that."
- "a council of bishops at Tyre … removed him from office". On what charge? This is the "accused of what?" pattern.
- "the Melitians, a breakaway church in Egypt". Broke away over what?
- "The emperor Julian drove him out in 362". Why?
- "all of Nicaea's statement but its one word". The listener has to work out that the word is "of one being".
Relevance: strong. Nicaea, the canon list, the Life of Antony.

### john-wesley: SOME GAPS
- Aldersgate is well glossed: assurance of forgiveness.
- "told him he had no business preaching in his area". Why it mattered (parish order, the church's authority) is only half given.
- "Wesley ordained two preachers for America himself, though only bishops normally did so". The consequence, a step toward a separate church, is left unsaid.
- "The work drew violence … a mob". Why did mobs hate Methodists?
- His teaching beyond assurance (holiness) is absent.
Relevance: good. Societies, classes, a worldwide church.

### william-tyndale: SOME GAPS
- What he did and why is very clear: Scripture "plain before their eyes in their own tongue".
- "Another book, on the bishops' dealings with kings, angered the king most". Why? (It opposed the king's divorce.)
- "he was condemned under the law of the emperor". Condemned for what teaching?
- "Some readers were later burned as heretics, for teaching the church condemned as false". Which teaching?
Relevance: strong. The English Bible and the farmer who knew it by heart.

## Batch 6

### bardaisan-of-edessa: CLEAR
The free will, fate and nature argument is fully explained, with examples and a living scene (Avida's question).
- Minor: "the first beings he said the world was made from" and "the Seven". The condemned cosmology is named but not explained, so "condemned for what?" is only half answered.
Relevance: good. A Syriac thinker on freedom whose name was later condemned.

### pliny-the-younger: CLEAR
What was punished ("stubbornness … obstinacy", the name itself), what the worship was, the test of incense and cursing Christ, Trajan's ruling, and Tertullian's attack on it are all clear.
Relevance: strong. A Roman judge's record and the legal position of the faith.

### valentinus: CLEAR
The teaching is given in competing versions (hidden Father, paired powers, lesser maker, "love is not love unless there is something to love", saved by nature). So is why it mattered: it forced the church to say one God made the world, and its writings were found in 1945. The inn image and the lost bishopric give it life.
Relevance: good. It leans toward idea summary but stays alive.

## Batch 7

### anatolius-of-alexandria-and-laodicea: SOME GAPS
- "The palace quarter of the city held out against a Roman army". Who rebelled against Rome, and why? The siege has no cause.
- "judging its own bishop, Paul of Samosata, for his teaching about Christ". What teaching?
- The Easter rule is explained, but not why getting Easter right mattered.
- "Before the Great Persecution". Undefined.
Relevance: mixed. The siege rescue is vivid. Why his reckoning mattered is mostly carried by the Whitby echo.

### methodius-of-olympus: SOME GAPS
- The quarrel over the body is clearly given: body as chain versus body as temple raised whole.
- "Origen had been dead some fifty years, and his books still had critics and defenders". Who was Origen, and why did he matter? He is never introduced.
- "attacked Origen in many places and then … praised him". On what points?
- "the Anatolian church of the third century". This says nothing to a listener.
- Opposite failure, partly: almost no life. The script is mostly who said what about him. "died a martyr at Chalcis" has no detail.
Relevance: weak. The listener hardly learns why Methodius is worth knowing.

### tertullian-s-voice: CLEAR
The Apology, the prescription argument, the New Prophecy (with its demands listed), Praxeas, and substance/person are all explained.
- Minor: "Marcion … Valentinus". Named without a clause on what they taught.
- Leans toward the opposite failure: it is a survey of works, with little life beyond the voice of the quotations.
Relevance: strong. The Latin words for the Trinity and "the blood of Christians is seed".

## Batch 8

### ambrose-of-milan: SOME GAPS
- Church versus emperor is fully clear ("The palaces belong to the emperor, the churches to the bishop"). So is the Thessalonica penance.
- "against his custom, he had men put to torture, and then fled". Why? The listener is left puzzled (he was trying to look unfit).
- "Christians … burned a synagogue … Ambrose … would not go to the altar until the emperor promised to change the order". Why a bishop opposed rebuilding it is never said. The listener cannot judge the act.
Relevance: strong. A bishop who held an emperor to account.

### huldrych-zwingli: SOME GAPS
- Scripture as judge, the Anabaptists' reason, and the Marburg disagreement are clear.
- "urged Bern … to war on the five Catholic cantons … cut the sinews of the rule of the few". War over what? Whose "rule of the few"?
- "signed one agreement on communion with John Calvin". What did it say, and how did it stand to Zwingli's view?
Relevance: good. A founder of the Reformed churches.

### thomas-cranmer: MANY GAPS
- "signed a series of papers taking back his teaching". Which teaching? The whole climax turns on a recantation of a teaching never stated.
- "Cranmer was condemned for teaching what the church held false". What did he teach?
- "argued against a man named Lambert, tried over the bread and wine of communion". What did Lambert hold? What did Cranmer hold then, and later?
- "his revised book of 1552 dropped the word Mass". Why it mattered (a changed belief about communion) is unsaid.
- The prayer book's purpose (worship people understand) is clear.
Relevance: the prayer book's legacy comes through. Cranmer's beliefs, the cause of his death, do not. This is the clearest case of "condemned for what?" in the sample.

## Batch 9

### apollinaris-of-laodicea: CLEAR
The teaching (the Word in place of a human mind), the reason it was condemned ("what he did not take on, he did not heal") and its hidden afterlife (Cyril's "one nature") are all given.
- Minor: "the one-nature party of his day". What was at stake is not said.
Relevance: strong. A condemned man who supplied an orthodox watchword.

### eunomius-of-cyzicus: CLEAR
The argument from "unbegotten" is explained, with Basil's reply and the reason for the banishment (private meetings, his books).
- Minor: why it mattered to ordinary believers that the Son is "unlike" (worship, salvation) is unsaid. Sozomen's line does carry the scale.
- Leans toward argument: the parsing of "or rather" is close to idea summary.
Relevance: good.

### hilary-of-poitiers: SOME GAPS
- "one in substance" and "like in substance" are explained.
- "a faction had him exiled on false reports". Reports of what?
- "Hilary accused Auxentius". Of what? (Arian teaching is implied but not stated.)
- "showed them the devil's fraud and the heretics' plots". This is vague.
- Why the Son's full divinity mattered is never stated.
Relevance: good on Gaul brought back to Nicaea, and on Augustine's debt.

## Pattern

- Yes, it has been true all along, but unevenly. The problem shows in every batch except 6. It neither steadily worsens nor improves: batches 4–5 are uniformly SOME GAPS, batch 6 is all CLEAR, and batch 8 has the worst case (Cranmer).
- It tracks shape more than length. Teachers whose ideas are the story (Bardaisan, Valentinus, Tertullian, Apollinaris, Eunomius) come out CLEAR. People of action and conflict (bishops in power struggles, martyrs, founders, reformers) lose the content of the charges and quarrels around them.
- The repeated gap is a secondary conflict told as an event: "condemned for heresy", "removed from office", "exiled on false reports", "taking back his teaching", "revolt", "war". Priscillian, Tyre, Lambert, Cranmer, Tyndale's readers, Paul of Samosata and Hilary's exile all follow it. A second, quieter gap is a doctrine that is stated but given no stakes. Athanasius and Apollinaris are the exceptions that say why it mattered.
- Length does not explain it. The two shortest scripts are thin (Alopen 368 words, Methodius 332), but Cranmer at 672 words has the worst gap.
- The opposite failure (ideas with little life) is mild and confined to teacher scripts: Methodius, and leanings in Tertullian and Eunomius.
