# Second Readings, Blind (Reader C, B10-21 to B10-29)

## B10-21

Source: `cic/texts/athanasius_opera-omnia-pg25-grc-lat_migne1857.txt`, lines 92812-92814. Greek.

Text as read: τὸν Φρουμέντιον τοῦτον εἰς ταύτην τὴν τάξιν τοῦ βίου κατέστησεν Ἀθανάσιος, μυρίοις ἔνοχος ὢν κακοῖς

Rendering: "[that] it was Athanasius who appointed this Frumentius to this rank in life, a man guilty of countless crimes."

Notes: The passage depends on a ὅτι ("that") just before it on line 92812 ("you surely know and remember ... that"). The scan reads βίον for βίου and ὧν for ὢν. The OCR uses χ for κ throughout (κατέστησεν appears as χατέστησεν, split across the line). "Rank in life" (τάξιν τοῦ βίου) means his office or station, here his office as bishop. "Guilty of countless crimes" describes Athanasius, not Frumentius.

## B10-22

Source: `cic/texts/langlois_collection-historiens-armenie-tomes1-2-fra_1867-1869.txt`, lines 55853-55883. French.

Text as read: Aussitôt il les aperçut, non pas en songe pendant le sommeil, non pas dans une vision pendant qu'il veillait, mais dans son cœur, où elles apparurent aux yeux de son esprit, tracées par le poignet d'une main droite écrivant sur la [footnotes, lines 55858-55882] pierre

Rendering: "At once he saw them. He did not see them in a dream while he slept, nor in a vision while he was awake. He saw them in his heart. There they appeared to the eyes of his mind, traced by the wrist of a right hand writing on the stone."

Notes: The sentence is broken across a page by footnotes 1-3 (lines 55858-55882); "la" on line 55857 joins "pierre" on line 55883. "Them" are the letters Mesrob had prayed for (line 55852). The scan reads "apercut" for "aperçut". The sentence goes on past the 42 words ("in the same way that one sees on snow the smallest strokes of a line"), which is not part of the passage.

## B10-23

Source: same file, lines 55910-55912. French.

Text as read: qui s'en réjouirent, comme si [c'eût été] les Tables divines

Rendering: "who rejoiced over them as if they had been the divine Tablets."

Notes: "Who" refers to Sahag, the holy Catholicos of the Armenians, and King Vram-Schapouh (lines 55909-55910). "Them" are the letters of the alphabet Mesrob presented. "The divine Tablets" means the tablets of the Law given to Moses. The bracketed "[c'eût été]" is the editor's supplement, not the source's. The scan reads "c'eüt".

## B10-24

Source: same file, lines 55914-55916. French.

Text as read: Mesrob réunit des enfants intelligents, instruits, ayant la voix douce et la respiration longue

Rendering: "Mesrob gathered children who were intelligent and educated, with sweet voices and long breath."

Notes: "Long breath" means they could sustain long phrases when reading or chanting aloud. The word "enfants" is split across lines 55914-55915. The sentence goes on ("he founded schools in all the provinces ..."), which is not part of the passage.

## B10-25

Source: `cic/texts/auxentius-durostorum_epistula-de-fide-vita-obitu-wulfilae_kauffmann1899.txt`, lines 15273-15277. Latin.

Text as read: sicuti deus per Moysem de potentia et uiolentia Faraonis et Egyptiorum populum suum liberauit et per mare transire fecit et sibi seruire prouidit, ita et per sepe dictum deus confessores sancti fili sui unigeniti de uarbarico liberauit et per Danubium transire fecit et in montibus secundum sanctorum imitationem sibi seruire.

Rendering: "Just as God, through Moses, freed his people from the power and violence of Pharaoh and the Egyptians, made them cross through the sea, and provided for them to serve him, so too God, through the man so often mentioned, freed the confessors of his holy only-begotten Son from the barbarian land, made them cross through the Danube, and [made them] serve him in the mountains, imitating the saints."

Notes: The scan reads "poptäum" for "populum", followed by marginal noise ("^''•"); line 15274 is blank. "The man so often mentioned" (per sepe dictum) is Ulfila, named on line 15271. "The barbarian land" (uarbarico, for barbarico) means the territory outside the empire. In the last clause the verb is understood: "serve" (seruire) depends on "made" (fecit) or "provided" (prouidit) carried over from the earlier clauses. "Imitating the saints" renders "secundum sanctorum imitationem", literally "according to the imitation of the saints".

## B10-26

Source: same file, line 15324. Latin.

Text as read: et filium subditum et oboedientem suo in omnibus deo patri

Rendering: "and [I believe] that the Son is subject and obedient in all things to his God and Father."

Notes: This is inside Ulfila's creed (from line 15304, "I, Ulfila, bishop and confessor, have always believed thus"). The words "[I believe] that" carry over from "Credo" at line 15308; the clause is grammatically governed by it, and the preceding line says the same of the Holy Spirit with respect to the Son. "Suo ... deo patri" could also be read "to God his Father"; the Latin puts "his" (suo) with "God" and "Father" together. The line continues "eique similem secundum scribturas" ("and like him according to the Scriptures"), which is past the ten words.

## B10-27

Source: `cic/texts/priscillian_quae-supersunt-lat_schepss1889-csel18.txt`, lines 4415-4417. Latin.

Text as read (scan errors corrected in brackets): nee [nec] prohibere si quis contemptis parenti- T)us [parentibus] liberis facultatibus dignitate et adhuc et aiiima [anima] sua deum malluerit [maluerit] amare quam saeculum, uec [nec]

Rendering: "and [we have urged] not to forbid it if someone, having scorned parents, children, property, rank, and even his own life, would rather love God than the world, nor ..."

Notes: The scan is damaged: "nee" for "nec", "parenti- T)us" for "parentibus" (split across the line), "aiiima" for "anima", "malluerit" for "maluerit", "uec" for "nec", and a stray "\" at the end of line 4416. The infinitive "prohibere" depends on "admonuimus et admonemus" ("we have urged and urge") on line 4394, continuing a list that starts "that wicked behaviour ... be condemned" (lines 4395-4415). The twentieth word, "nec", opens the next item ("nor to take away hope of pardon", line 4417), which falls outside the passage. "Life" renders "anima", which can also mean "soul"; the editor's note cites Luke 14:26 (line 4398), where the same list ends with one's own life.

## B10-28

Source: `cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml`, lines 11642-11643. English.

Text as read: And my feeling indeed is, that the accusers were as distasteful to me as the accused.

Rendering: "To be honest, I found the accusers as offensive as the accused."

Notes: "The accusers" are the bishops Ydacius and Ithacius (lines 11639-11640); "the accused" are those brought before the king in the trial. "Distasteful" could also be rendered "disagreeable" or "displeasing".

## B10-29

Source: `cic/texts/npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml`, lines 3276-3278. English.

Text as read: for even the leaders of the world so abhorred this profane folly that they laid low its originator, with most of his disciples, by the sword of the public laws.

Rendering: "For even the rulers of the world hated this unholy folly so much that they put its founder to death, with most of his followers, by the sword of the state's laws."

Notes: "This profane folly" is the heresy described just before (line 3274, "this abominable heresy"), which held people to be ruled by the stars. "Laid low ... by the sword" means executed. "The public laws" are the laws of the state, as against the Church's own discipline.
