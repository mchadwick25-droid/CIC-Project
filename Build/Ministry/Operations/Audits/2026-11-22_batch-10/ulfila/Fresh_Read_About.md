## The four lines

1. **Who:** Ulfila, a bishop of the Goths in the three-hundreds, who led persecuted Gothic Christians across the Danube onto Roman soil.
2. **What happened:** Made bishop at thirty, he taught the Goths for seven years. A persecution drove him and a great crowd of believers over the river. He gave the Goths letters and the Scriptures in Gothic. He joined the creed of 360, taught it to his death, and died at Constantinople about 383, leaving his creed in writing.
3. **What it was about:** the faith he taught: the Father alone unbegotten, the Son God but like the Father and subject to him, and Nicaea's "of one being" refused. His opponents called that faith Arian. Three historians disagree on when he took it.
4. **Why the connection matters:** the Goths got their Christianity from him, in their own letters and language and in his creed. They still held it in the historians' day. Sozomen traces that to Ulfila; Socrates traces it to Valens.

All four lines can be told after one hearing, provided the BLOCKING fix below is made.
