# Review: Ulfila

## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Ulfila, a bishop of the Goths, descended from Christians who had been taken captive in Cappadocia. He gave the Goths letters and put the Scriptures into Gothic.
2. **What happened:** a persecution drove him across the Danube with a crowd of believers onto Roman soil. There he taught the creed that called the Son "like" the Father, joined the council at Constantinople in 360, and died at Constantinople about 383, leaving his creed by testament.
3. **Why the connection matters:** the Goths took their Christianity from him, in their own letters and in his creed, which his opponents called Arian. The Greek historians trace the Goths' faith to his choice and disagree on when he made it.

**Essence (census, and what he is known for):** the Goth who led his persecuted people across the Danube, gave them an alphabet and a Gothic Bible, and taught them the Homoian creed until he died. He is on the tree as the source of Gothic Homoian Christianity. **Also told:** his Cappadocian forebears and consecration by "Eusebius"; the persecution; the Bible and its survival; what he taught, against Nicaea; the doubt over when he took the creed; his death, and the creed he left. All are present, each with its weight.

**Structure.** The hook (the crossing, then Auxentius's Exodus) leads straight into the essence. The crossing comes in the first sentence. The letters, the Bible and the creed arrive at words 165 to 200, about 1:10 to 1:20 (MINOR 1). After the essence, the life runs once, in order: forebears, consecration, teaching, persecution, Bible, teaching, 360, the doubt, Athanaric, death, testament. The persecution paragraph comes back to the moment the hook told. This is the hook reaching ahead, not a jump back, and it adds the judge. No event is told twice. The story ends on the person, with his own testament answering the historians' doubt. Nowhere did it read as a children's book or talk down.

**Where I got lost:** "his opponents were having second thoughts about the council the emperor had promised". The listener has not heard of any council, only "a debate". (Cut 4 removes it.)

**Names heard once that neither act nor link:** "Constantine" (in "Constantine's son Constantius"). MINOR; Cut 3 removes it.

## Scores

- **Voice 4.** An adult narrator's voice, clear and steady. The witnesses come in "X says" runs (Philostorgius three times in one paragraph), but each run carries a claim the listener needs credited.
- **Craft 4.** The opening is good, and the ending, with his own "always believed so" answering the four historians, is strong. The doubt scene is real. The creed is heard three times: in the essence, in the teaching paragraph and in the testament (MINOR 2).
- **Rigor 4.** Every sentence traces to the outline, and every witness is credited. Two faults: a narrator's date on the Bible (B2), and a quote that runs past the lifted phrase (B1).
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 9.3 (names out 8.0), reading ease 69.7, longest sentence 29 words. Every gloss comes where its word is first heard. Q1 is long, but its plain sense follows at once.

## Verdict: REVISE

## BLOCKING

**B1. The creed quote runs past the lifted phrase.** The brief has: "subject and obedient in all things to his God the Father." The slate lifts only "subject and obedient in all things", as the live `arius` story quotes it ("yet "subject and obedient in all things" to the Father"). Mark's ruling (item 2) is that the creed is told "in the narrator's words, with the two lifted phrases quoted". "to his God the Father" is the barred creed's own wording (AUX 15324: "filium subditum et oboedientem suo in omnibus deo patri"). The outline's Q3 carried the longer span, so the outline left room for this.
*Change:* close the quote after "all things": of the Son, "subject and obedient in all things" to his God the Father. No word cost.
*Do not say (add to the outline):* "Q3's second phrase quoted past 'in all things' → the lifted span is 'subject and obedient in all things'; 'to his God the Father' follows in the narrator's words, outside the quote marks."

**B2. The narrator puts a date on the letters and the Bible.** "In the middle of the three-hundreds, Ulfila gave the Goths letters and the Scriptures in their own tongue." No witness dates them that way. Socrates sets them under Valens, in the 360s and 370s (NP2 14448-14450). The outline marks the date Contested (beat 3), and its Setting gives "in the middle of the three-hundreds" only as a phrase for the period as a whole. This is one of the four faults: a date moved onto an event, with a contested date told as settled.
*Change:* Cut 1 below (delete the clause).
*Do not say (add to the outline):* "Any date on the letters and the Bible in the narrator's voice ('in the middle of the three-hundreds', 'after the crossing') → no date; the date is Contested, and Socrates's 'under Valens' is told only as his."

## MINOR

1. **The essence lands just after the first minute.** The hook's three sentences and Q1 run to word 133. The gloss sentence follows, so the letters arrive at word 165 and the creed and "Arian" end at about word 200. Cuts 1 and 2 bring the letters to word 156 and the essence's end to about word 188. That is close enough once the crossing has opened the story.
2. **The creed is heard three times.** "Like the Father, according to the Scriptures" is said word for word in the essence and again in the teaching paragraph, and "alone unbegotten" comes back in the testament. The testament's return is the ending's point. The teaching paragraph's "and is like the Father, according to the Scriptures" could go if the reviser needs more words, but "The Greek for like" would then stand a long way from the essence's "like". Optional.
3. **"They trace the Goths' faith to that choice."** Sozomen does this (NP2 40169-40181), and so does Theodoret (THD 15309-15319). Socrates, the next name heard, gives the cause as the emperor Valens (NP2 14444-14449). The outline's essence keeps "Socrates gives a share to the emperor Valens", and the brief drops it. Optional, if words allow: "Socrates says many Goths still held it in his day, for the emperor's sake".

## Cuts to 675

The brief is 722 words by the scorer. These seven cuts take it to **669** (checked by running the scorer on a copy with all seven applied). The heart, the force of the first persecution, Athanaric's punishments and martyrs, "a devilish invention", the doubt scene and the testament ending all stay. Cut 7 is from the writer's candidates (Sozomen's tents). Cut 2 trims the gloss the writer offered. The rest are beyond the writer's list, because the list alone would cut the doubt scene or the Athanaric beat, and those carry the record's conflict and force.

1. **Paragraph 3:** "In the middle of the three-hundreds, Ulfila gave the Goths letters" → "Ulfila gave the Goths letters". Saves 6. (Also fixes B2 and MINOR 1.)
2. **Paragraph 3:** "the barbarians' land was the Goths' country beyond the frontier" → "the barbarians' land was the Goths' country". Saves 3.
3. **Paragraph 4:** "A modern historian takes this Eusebius for the bishop of Nicomedia, at the court of Constantine's son Constantius." → "A modern historian takes this Eusebius for the bishop of Nicomedia." Saves 7. (Philostorgius's "the emperor's court" still holds the place, as the outline's Do not say allows.)
4. **Paragraph 11:** "As he entered the city, his opponents were having second thoughts about the council the emperor had promised, and at once he fell ill." → "As he entered the city, he fell ill at once." Saves 14. (Removes the point where the listener gets lost.)
5. **Paragraph 9:** "Sozomen says he signed in 360 from thoughtlessness" → "Sozomen says he signed from thoughtlessness". Saves 2. (The year is in the paragraph before.)
6. **Paragraph 6:** "their editor in 1908 had no doubt they were Ulfila's, made from the Greek." → "their editor in 1908 had no doubt they were Ulfila's." Saves 4.
7. **Paragraph 10:** delete the whole sentence "Sozomen reports a story that Athanaric's men burned those who refused to sacrifice, together with their tents." Saves 17. (Socrates's "punished the Christians, so that many died as martyrs" keeps the force.)

Total saved: 53. 722 − 53 = 669, under 675.
