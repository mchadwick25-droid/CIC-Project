# Outline check: Ulfila

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** Ulfila, a Goth of Cappadocian captive descent, was made bishop for the Christians in the Gothic land (by "Eusebius", Philostorgius; Eusebius of Nicomedia in the editors' reading). He made the Goths letters and put the Scriptures into Gothic, and he taught the Homoian creed ("like the Father according to the Scriptures") that the Goths carried into the kingdoms after Rome. He is on the tree as the source of the Gothic church and its faith, the line that gave rise to gothic-and-vandal-homoian-christianity.

**Also told.** Seven years as bishop beyond the Danube; a persecution by a Gothic judge; he led a crowd of confessors across the Danube onto Roman soil, received with honour under Constantius ("the Moses of his day"). Forty years preaching in Greek, Latin and Gothic, with treatises and interpretations. At Constantinople in 360 he confirmed the creed of Ariminum. The Greek historians disagree on when he took that creed (Socrates: first then; Sozomen: later, under pressure or conviction; Theodoret: under Valens, by Eudoxius's eloquence and bribes); his pupil Auxentius gives his deathbed creed, "I have always believed so". He died at Constantinople, about 383 (Kauffmann's June 383), having gone at the emperor's summons to a debate. What came: the Goths' Homoian faith that the historians say lasted in their day; the Gothic Bible (fragments, Codex Argenteus, editor-attributed); his pupils Auxentius and Selinus; the Lesser Goths of Moesia (Jordanes). Told from Auxentius, with each Greek historian credited.

## Heart compared

The outline's essence and Also told match my account in substance: the crossing, the letters and Scriptures, the creed he taught to his death, the Goths who kept it, the doubt over when he took it, and Auxentius as the main witness. It is more careful than the card on two points, and the sources back it on both: "Eusebius" is Philostorgius's word, and the full name is Duchesne's (PHI 120; DUC 20993-20994); and the Cappadocian descent is Philostorgius's alone. Nothing major is missing. The opening and the essence carry the heart inside the first minute.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Q3's deathbed sentences are already on the tree, and the slate has not lifted them.** The tradition page `homoian-arian-christianity`, `documentedStories[1]`, has "I, Ulfila, bishop and confessor, have always so believed, and in this, the one true faith, I make my journey to my Lord" as both its title and its text. That is the same event (the creed he left at his death) and the same job (his faith in his own words). The slate lifts only "alone unbegotten" and "subject and obedient in all things". It also says "Any other quote already on the tree stays barred; a researcher who wants one asks." The outline's Gaps note raises the question, but it still plans these sentences as the recommended ending. A different English rendering of the same sentence is still the same quote.
*Correction:* put a one-line lift request to Mark: "Ulfila's 'I, Ulfila, bishop and confessor, have always believed so. In this faith, the only true one, I make my crossing to my Lord' (on the `homoian-arian-christianity` tradition page) as the brief's ending, where it answers the Greek historians' dispute over when he took the creed." Until he lifts it, Q3 carries only the two lifted phrases ("alone unbegotten", "subject and obedient in all things to his God the Father"). In that case, Ending 1 reads: "He fell ill as he entered Constantinople, and left his faith in writing to his people. He wrote that he had always believed so, and that in this faith alone he made his crossing to his Lord (AUX 15301-15306; narrator's words, no quote marks)." Then rewrite the Gaps entry to record the request and Mark's answer.

**B2. Sozomen's embassy is told in two beats, and the second telling jumps back.** Beat 4 says Sozomen has him join the other side "later, on an embassy". Sozomen's own text makes this the embassy he led after the Huns drove the Goths over the river: "forward the object of his embassy" (NP2 40158-40166, after NP2 40141-40146). Beat 5 then tells that same embassy again ("Ulfila led their embassy to the emperor"). Beat 4 also gives Theodoret's answer with no time attached, though Theodoret sets it under Valens (THD 15300-15301).
*Correction:* in beat 4, replace "and joined the other side later, on an embassy, under pressure or perhaps from conviction (NP2 40158-40171)" with "and joined the other side years later, under Valens, when he went to Constantinople on the Goths' embassy: under pressure, or perhaps from conviction (NP2 40141-40171)". Replace "Theodoret says a bishop of the emperor's party won him over" with "Theodoret puts it under Valens too: a bishop of the emperor's party won him over". In beat 5, delete "he also says that when the Huns drove the Goths across the Danube, Ulfila led their embassy to the emperor (NP2 40141-40146)". In Cut first, delete item 9 and renumber. That clause has already left the count: 900 − 17 = 883 before the list, the list now saves 217, and the count after stays 666.

**B3. Sozomen's burnings drop his own qualifier.** Sozomen introduces the statue and the burning with the tents with "It is said that the officers appointed by Athanaric..." (NP2 40188-40193). The tent church comes separately, under "I have heard" (40193-40194). Beat 5 and the Do not say list both tell the first as Sozomen's plain statement.
*Correction:* in beat 5, replace "Sozomen says Athanaric's men burned those who refused to sacrifice together with their tents" with "Sozomen reports a story that Athanaric's men burned those who refused to sacrifice, together with their tents". In Do not say, replace the tent-church entry with: "Sozomen's burnings told as fact → both are reports in Sozomen: the burning with the tents is 'it is said', and the tent church full of women and children is 'I have heard'. Tell each as something Sozomen reports."
