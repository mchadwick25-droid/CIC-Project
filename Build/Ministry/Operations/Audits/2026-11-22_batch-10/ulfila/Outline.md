# Outline: Ulfila

File abbreviations and row numbers (S, E, I, L) as in `Source_Sheet.md`. All files under `cic/texts/`.

- **The essence:** Ulfila, bishop of the Goths, led a crowd of persecuted Gothic Christians across the Danube into the Roman Empire (AUX 15268-15277). He gave the Goths letters and the Scriptures in their own language (PHI 120; NP2 14448-14459, 40178-40179), and to his death taught them the creed that called the Son "like" the Father, according to the Scriptures (AUX 15174-15176, 15211-15213, 15324). The Nicene historians, who called that faith Arian, trace the Goths' holding of it to him; Socrates gives a share to the emperor Valens (NP2 14446-14449, 40180-40181). Nearly all of his life comes from his pupil Auxentius; the Greek historians who also tell it disagree on when he took that creed (Source_Sheet §5.1). Documented; the letters and the Bible Widely Accepted; the date of the creed Contested.
- **Also told:**
  - Where he came from: in Philostorgius alone, a family of Christian captives from Cappadocia, carried off by Gothic raiders (PHI 120). Made bishop at thirty, from the office of reader, by "Eusebius and the other prelates" in Philostorgius; a modern historian names him Eusebius of Nicomedia (AUX 15240-15241; PHI 120; DUC 20991-20995). Documented that each says it; consecrator and date Contested; the name Eusebius of Nicomedia Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
  - The persecution and the martyrs that drove the crossing (AUX 15261-15268). Documented as Auxentius tells it.
  - The letters and the Bible: the witnesses who say he made them (PHI 120; NP2 14448-14459, 40178-40179), Philostorgius's report that he left out Kings (PHI 120), and the editor's judgment that the surviving Gothic Bible is his (STR 603-607). Widely Accepted; the attribution Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
  - What he taught, in his pupil's words: the Father alone unbegotten and the one true God, the Son God and maker of all creation yet with the Father as his God; Nicaea's "of one being" refused with force (AUX 15114-15118, 15130-15142, 15167-15169). Documented.
  - The doubt as a scene: four witnesses on when he took the creed of 360 (NP2 9606-9612; NP2 40158-40171; THD 15309-15323; AUX 15304-15306). Contested.
  - His death at Constantinople and the creed he left in writing (AUX 15283-15306). Documented; the year Kauffmann's.
  - What came of him: the Goths' faith in three historians' day (I1); the Gothic Bible's survival (I2); his successors teaching in Gothic (I3); his people still in Moesia in 551 (I4).
  - The sources hold all of this. They do not hold the card's "made bishop by Eusebius of Nicomedia" as a fact: Philostorgius says only "Eusebius", and the full name is the modern historian's (E4, E4a).
- **Length:** At most 4:30 (675 words), approved by Mark (2026-11-23). Counted by Length_Count.md's method: 872 before the Cut first list, 667 after, once B1 and B2 are applied.
- **Setting:**
  - About 341 to 383 (Kauffmann's dates, worked from Auxentius's numbers: AUX 3752-3755, 3735). Auxentius gives no years. Spoken: "in the middle of the three-hundreds"; "about 383".
  - The Goths' land north of the Danube, which Auxentius calls the barbarians' land (AUX 15271); the Roman side, "Romania", later Moesia and Thrace (AUX 15272; PHI 120; NP2 40145); Constantinople at the end (AUX 15283). Plain words: "the Goths' land beyond the Danube"; "Roman soil"; "Moesia, the Roman province south of the lower Danube" (DUC 21018-21020: "Mésie inférieure").
  - Tradition: census `homoian-arian-christianity`, spoken name "Homoian ('Arian') Christianity", informal "the Homoian churches". Plain spoken words: "the faith that called the Son like the Father, according to the Scriptures". "Arian" only as its opponents' name.
- **Words to explain:**
  - *confessor*: one who held to the faith under persecution. Needed in Q1 (rendered "those who confessed") and Q3 ("bishop and confessor").
  - *reader* (lector): "a reader, the man who read the Scriptures aloud in church" (AUX 15241; AUX 3768-3770, Kauffmann).
  - *unbegotten*: "never begotten by anyone, without beginning" (AUX 15117-15118: "solum esse ingenitum, sine principio").
  - *like the Father*: the creed's word, Greek *homoios*, from which the name Homoian comes (census). Auxentius: "like his Father ... according to the divine Scriptures" (AUX 15174-15176).
  - *of one being* (homoousios): Nicaea's word that the Son shares the Father's very being. Tree wording, as in the live `eusebius-of-nicomedia` story.
  - *the barbarians' land* (barbaricum): the land outside the empire's frontier. Needed in Q1.
  - *Moesia*: the Roman province south of the lower Danube.
  - *Arian*: the name his opponents gave the faith (NP2 14446-14449, 40180-40181); never the narrator's own label for it.
- **Shape:** Missionary or translator. Trap: telling it only from the sender's side. The story says whose record it is (Auxentius, a pupil) and lets the Goths' later faith and the three hostile historians carry the receiving side.
- **Sibling:** none in this batch. On the tree, each with its own job: `arius` (two sentences on Ulfila's creed and Bible, to show Arius's words living on among the Goths; here, what Ulfila himself taught, with the lifted "alone unbegotten" and "subject and obedient in all things"); `eusebius-of-nicomedia` (one beat: Philostorgius's "Eusebius" and the modern historian's reading; here one clause, with the same credits). Later: `auxentius-of-durostorum` (his pupil's own story: the letter, Maximinus, the numbers), `councils-of-ariminum-and-seleucia-359` (the councils; here one clause on Constantinople, 360). The live `arius` story ends at Toledo in 589; this one does not retell it.
- **Openings:**
  1. **The crossing (recommended).** After seven years as bishop, with a persecution raging and many men and women already dead as martyrs, Ulfila was driven out with a great crowd of believers (AUX 15261-15271). They crossed the Danube and were received with honour on Roman soil, in the reign of the emperor Constantius (AUX 15271-15272). His pupil Auxentius told it as a new Exodus: Q1 (AUX 15272-15277). Leads straight into the essence. If used, beat 2 does not tell the crossing again.
  2. **The silver book.** In the library at Uppsala lies a book written in silver and gold on purple parchment, with the Gospels in Gothic (STR 1348-1356). It carries no translator's name; its modern editor has no doubt it is the remains of Ulfila's Bible (STR 603-607). If used, What came 2 does not describe the codex again.
- **Story beats:**
  1. **A reader made bishop (c. 341).** Philostorgius, a historian of the next century, says his forebears were Christian captives from Cappadocia, carried off by Gothic raiders, who won many Goths to the faith (PHI 120). Auxentius says he was made bishop at thirty, from the office of reader, "in the Gothic people" (AUX 15240-15241). Philostorgius says it happened on an embassy from the Gothic ruler to the emperor's court, "by Eusebius and the other prelates that were with him" (PHI 120). A modern historian takes this Eusebius for the bishop of Nicomedia, and sets it at the court of Constantine's son Constantius (DUC 20991-20995). Philostorgius calls Ulfila the Goths' first bishop; Socrates names an earlier one, Theophilus, who signed at Nicaea (PHI 120; NP2 9609-9612). Documented that each says it; Contested.
  2. **Seven years, and a persecution (c. 341-348).** Auxentius: he taught the Gothic people, who had been starved of preaching, and made Christians into true Christians (AUX 15254-15259). Then "an irreligious and sacrilegious judge of the Goths" raised a persecution with "tyrannical terror", and many men and women died as martyrs (AUX 15261-15268). The crossing is told in the opening; here only that they lived on Roman soil "in the mountains" (AUX 15277), and that Philostorgius says the emperor settled them in Moesia and often called Ulfila the Moses of his day (PHI 120; narrator's words, no quote marks). Documented as Auxentius tells it.
  3. **Letters and the Scriptures.** Why, in the sources: Philostorgius makes it part of his care for them; Socrates says it was to teach them the Scriptures (PHI 120; NP2 14456-14459). Philostorgius says he invented letters for them and translated all of Scripture except the Books of Kings, because those were stories of war and the Goths were too fond of war already (PHI 120). Socrates and Sozomen also credit him with the letters and the translation; Socrates sets it in the reign of Valens (NP2 14448-14459, 40178-40179). Auxentius says he preached in Greek, Latin and Gothic, and left many writings in all three (AUX 15211-15213, 15220-15222); he does not mention the Bible. Widely Accepted that he made both; the date Contested.
  4. **What he taught, and the quarrel over when he took it (360).** In his pupil's words: the Father alone is unbegotten and the one true God; the Son is God and maker of all creation, yet has the Father as his God; the Son is "like" the Father, according to the Scriptures (AUX 15117-15118, 15167-15169, 15174-15176). He spurned Nicaea's "of one being" as "a devilish invention" (AUX 15139-15142). At Constantinople in 360 (the usual date; Sozomen gives the reign, not the year) he joined about fifty bishops who confirmed a creed that banned the word "substance" for God (NP2 34561-34567). When did he take it? Socrates says only then; before, he held Nicaea's faith (NP2 9606-9612). Sozomen says he signed in 360 out of thoughtlessness, and joined the other side years later, under Valens, when he went to Constantinople on the Goths' embassy: under pressure, or perhaps from conviction (NP2 40141-40171). Theodoret puts it under Valens too: a bishop of the emperor's party won him over with eloquence and bribes (THD 15309-15315). Sozomen, though he calls it Arian, still writes Q2 (SOZ-G 17731-17734). Auxentius gives the answer of the man himself, held for the ending (Ending 1). Contested; each credited.
  5. **Under Valens (370s).** Socrates says he taught the subjects of the Gothic chief Athanaric too, and Athanaric punished the Christians, so that many died as martyrs (NP2 14460-14465). Sozomen reports a story that Athanaric's men burned those who refused to sacrifice, together with their tents (NP2 40188-40194). Documented as each tells it.
  6. **Constantinople (c. 383).** After forty years, at the emperor's order, he went to Constantinople for a debate (AUX 15283-15284). As he entered the city, his opponents were having second thoughts about the council the emperor had promised (AUX 15288; AUX 15108-15110, Maximinus citing Auxentius), and at once he fell ill (AUX 15288-15290; AUX 15108-15110). Auxentius renames the city Christianople, and says he was honoured by his fellow bishops among a great crowd of Christians (AUX 15292-15301; narrator's words, no quote marks). Documented; the year 383 is Kauffmann's (AUX 3735).
- **What came of him:** (about a third: beats 3 and 5, the ending, and these)
  1. **The Goths kept his faith.** Socrates says many Goths still held it in his own day; Sozomen says this was why the barbarians on the Danube followed Arius; Theodoret says the Goths "up to this day" call the Father greater than the Son but will not call the Son a creature (NP2 14446-14449, 40180-40181; THD 15316-15319). A bishop of Gothic Spain two centuries on, Isidore of Seville, still names "Gulfilas" as the maker of their letters and their Bible (ISI 118051-118055; STR 1292-1296). Documented. *Defended:* that the Son is like the Father according to the Scriptures, and that the Father alone is unbegotten.
  2. **The Gothic Bible.** Its fragments survive without the translator's name; the editor who printed them in 1908 has no doubt they are Ulfila's, made from the Greek (STR 603-607, 1684-1686). One of them, the Codex Argenteus at Uppsala, was written in the fifth or sixth century in silver and gold on purple parchment (STR 1348-1356). Dominant Modern Reconstruction for the attribution; Documented for the codex.
  3. **His school.** His pupil Auxentius, raised by him from boyhood, wrote his life (AUX 15234-15238). His secretary Selinus succeeded him and could teach in Gothic and in Greek (NP2 41606-41612). Documented.
  4. **His people.** Jordanes, a historian of the Goths writing in 551, says the Lesser Goths, "whose priest and primate was Vulfila", still lived in Moesia, poor and unwarlike (JOR 3288-3296). Documented. One clause at most.
  - Who keeps him today: no community; the census says the tradition's churches are gone (I5). Leave out.
- **Quotes:**
  - **Q1** (Auxentius, the crossing as Exodus). Rendered from the Latin, AUX 15272-15277, OCR-damaged ("poptäum"); the second print, STR 838-842, reads "populum" and otherwise agrees: "Through Moses, God freed his people from the power and violence of Pharaoh and the Egyptians. He made them cross the sea, and made sure they would serve him. So too, through this man, God freed those who confessed his holy only Son from the barbarians' land. He made them cross the Danube, and serve him in the mountains, after the saints' example." Not on the tree. Job: the opening; the pupil's own voice, which says whose record this is. Plain sense alongside: "the barbarians' land" is the Goths' land outside the empire.
  - **Q2** (Sozomen, a Nicene historian's tribute). Rendered from the Greek, SOZ-G 17731-17734, confirmed by STR 1204-1206 and Hartranft's English (NP2 40175-40178): "What is more, he gave the fullest proof of his goodness. He bore countless dangers for the faith while those barbarians were still pagans." Not on the tree. Job: the hostile witness's praise, so the doubt scene does not turn the historians into simple foes. Cut first if the length needs it.
  - **Q3** (Ulfila's own words, his written creed, kept by Auxentius). Rendered from the Latin, AUX 15304-15306, 15310, 15324, OCR-damaged ("sie" for "sic"); STR 881-886, 902 and DUC 21073-21075 read "sic" and agree: of the Father, "alone unbegotten"; of the Son, "subject and obedient in all things to his God the Father." Until Mark lifts the creed's opening sentences, Q3 carries only these two lifted phrases; the opening sentences are told in the narrator's words (Ending 1). "Alone unbegotten" and "subject and obedient in all things" are lifted by the slate from `arius` (its rendering kept word for word); there they show Arius's words living on among the Goths; here they show what Ulfila taught, in the creed he died with. "Transitum facio" is "I make my crossing" (the same verb, *transire*, Auxentius uses for the Danube in Q1); the writer lets the words stand and does not explain the echo. See Gaps.
  - Every other phrase in quote marks in this outline is the source's wording for the writer to tell in the narrator's words, not a fourth quote.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** What passed from Ulfila to the Goths was a Christianity in their own language and in one creed. The writer must make three things plain. First, the Goths received the faith through him, in letters he made and Scriptures he put into Gothic (PHI 120; NP2 14448-14459). Second, the creed he taught was the one his opponents called Arian, and he taught it to his death in his own words: the Father alone unbegotten, the Son "like" him according to the Scriptures and subject to him (AUX 15174-15176, 15324). Third, the Goths held it after him, as three Nicene historians complain of their own day and a bishop of Gothic Spain still recalls (I1). The listener should not have to infer why the historians care when he took the creed: it is because they trace the Goths' whole faith to that choice (NP2 40169-40171, 40180-40181). Carry it through acts: a reader made bishop, a crossing, letters made, a creed signed, a creed left by testament, a people who kept it.
- **Ending:**
  1. **His creed (recommended).** He left his faith in writing to his people, 'by testament'. He wrote that he had always believed so, and that in this faith alone he made his crossing to his Lord (AUX 15301-15306; narrator's words, no quote marks).
  2. **Christianople.** Auxentius renames the city where he died "Christianople", and says he was honoured there among a great crowd of Christians (AUX 15292-15301). In the narrator's words; use only if Q3 is placed earlier.

Beside the consecration at thirty, the seven years, the persecution, the crossing (Q1), the three languages, the teaching, the death and the creed (Q3). **The whole life rests mostly on him. The story says so once, inside the sentence that brings him in (the opening or the essence): his pupil, who wrote in his praise.**
  - Ulfila himself: the creed (Q3), kept only in Auxentius's letter, copied by a bishop of their party into one manuscript (I3a).
  - Philostorgius: about 45 years after his death; of a related party that admired him; known only through the summary of a later patriarch who disliked him (PHI 52). Beside the Cappadocian forebears, the embassy and "Eusebius", "first bishop", the Moses of his day, the letters and the Books of Kings.
  - Socrates: about 60 years later, Nicene. Beside Theophilus, the creed "only then", the letters under Valens, Athanaric's persecution, the Goths' faith in his day.
  - Sozomen: a few years after Socrates, Nicene, and using him. Beside the 360 signing from thoughtlessness, the later change under pressure or conviction, the second crossing and embassy, the tents burned, Q2.
  - Theodoret: about 70 years later, Nicene and hostile. Beside the eloquence and bribes, and the Goths' faith "up to this day".

  - The editor of the Gothic Bible (Streitberg): beside the attribution of the fragments and the codex.

- **Do not say:**
  - "Eusebius of Nicomedia made him bishop" as fact → "Philostorgius says he was made bishop by 'Eusebius and the other prelates'; a modern historian takes this to be Eusebius of Nicomedia."
  - "At Constantine's court" or "at Constantius's court" alone → "Philostorgius sets it at Constantine's court; the modern historian, at Constantius's." Or "at the emperor's court".
  - Any year as Auxentius's (311, 341, 348, 383) → the years are his modern editor's, worked from Auxentius's numbers; say "about".
  - "Forty years as bishop" as a plain count → Auxentius's forty is seven years among the Goths and thirty-three on Roman soil (AUX 15270, 15279-15281); his numbers echo Scripture. If told, credit it to Auxentius.
  - "The Goths' first bishop" as fact → "Philostorgius calls him the first; Socrates names an earlier one."
  - "He was a Cappadocian", "half Cappadocian", or "Cappadocian on his mother's side" → "Philostorgius says his forebears were Christian captives from Cappadocia." No held text names his mother.
  - "Athanaric persecuted the Christians in 348" → the first persecutor is unnamed in Auxentius: "an irreligious and sacrilegious judge of the Goths". Athanaric's persecution in Socrates and Sozomen is the later one, under Valens. Do not name Hermanaric either (DUC 21013, the editor's).
  - "Constantius welcomed them" → Auxentius: they "were received with honour on Roman soil", in the reign of Constantius (AUX 15272: "athuc beate memorie Constantio principe, honorifice est susceptus"). The verb has no named subject.
  - "Constantius called him the Moses of his day" → Philostorgius says "the emperor", whom his text names Constantine; say "the emperor". Not in quote marks.
  - "Auxentius called him the Moses of the Goths" → Auxentius compares the crossing to Moses leading Israel through the sea (Q1); the "Moses" name is Philostorgius's report of the emperor.
  - "Auxentius says he translated the Bible" → Auxentius says he preached in three languages and left many writings in them. The letters and the Bible come from Philostorgius, Socrates and Sozomen.
  - "He translated the whole Bible" → Philostorgius says all of it except the Books of Kings; the surviving fragments are mostly the Gospels and Paul's letters (STR 1348-1356, 1402-1404).
  - "He made the alphabet from Greek and Latin letters" or "replaced the runes" → editors' notes (NP2 14450-14453, Zenos; DUC 20999-21000). The sources say only that he invented letters.
  - "He wrote the Codex Argenteus" or "the silver Bible is his own copy" → it was written in the fifth or sixth century (STR 1352).
  - "The Gothic fragments name him" → they carry no translator's name; the attribution is the editor's.
  - "He went to Constantinople to debate the Pneumatomachi / the Spirit-fighters" → the opponents' name is damaged in the manuscript and restored by the editor; say "for a debate" (AUX 15284; STR 866-868).
  - "He died of old age" or any cause → Auxentius says only that he fell ill at once as he entered the city.
  - "He became an Arian", "Ulfila the Arian", "the Arian Goths" in the narrator's voice → "the faith his opponents called Arian"; Socrates's and Sozomen's words stay theirs.
  - "Ulfila called himself Homoian" → the name is the census's, from the creed's word "like".
  - "Ulfila was bribed" as fact → "Theodoret says a bishop of the emperor's party won him over with eloquence and bribes."
  - "Valens converted the Goths" → Socrates: a Gothic chief took the emperor's religion out of gratitude for his help.
  - "Ulfila led the Goths across in 376" as the same event as the opening → Sozomen's crossing, under Valens and fleeing the Huns, is told as Sozomen's; Auxentius's crossing, under Constantius and fleeing persecution, is told as his. Do not merge them, and do not say there were two: no held text says so.
  - Sozomen's burnings told as fact → both are reports in Sozomen: the burning with the tents is 'it is said', and the tent church full of women and children is 'I have heard'. Tell each as something Sozomen reports.
  - Softening his own side's force → Auxentius has him call Nicaea's teaching "a devilish invention" and every other gathering "synagogues of Satan" (AUX 15139-15142, 15217-15218). Keep the first if beat 4 has room; never present him as tolerant.
  - "The Goths kept his faith until 589" → that is the live `arius` story's ending; here, the three historians' "to this day" and Isidore.
  - Any feeling or motive not in the record (e.g. "longing to give his people the Word").
  - "He left out the Books of Kings" as fact → "Philostorgius says he left them out". Philostorgius is the only witness (PHI 120).
  - Any word under Words to explain (confessor, reader, unbegotten, "like" the Father, of one being, the barbarians' land, Moesia, Arian) used without its plain sense the first time it is heard.
  - "The oldest book in a Germanic language", or what his name means → no held text in this outline says either; leave them out.
  - Q3's second phrase quoted past 'in all things' → the lifted span is 'subject and obedient in all things'; 'to his God the Father' follows in the narrator's words, outside the quote marks.
  - Any date on the letters and the Bible in the narrator's voice ('in the middle of the three-hundreds', 'after the crossing') → no date; the date is Contested, and Socrates's 'under Valens' is told only as his.
  - "The historians trace the Goths' faith to his choice" for all of them → "Sozomen traces it to Ulfila; Socrates puts it down to Valens" (NP2 14444-14449, 40160-40181).
