## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Ulfila, a bishop of the Goths, whose forebears (Philostorgius says) were Christian captives from Cappadocia, made bishop at thirty by "Eusebius", whom a modern historian takes for the bishop of Nicomedia.
2. **What happened:** when a persecution broke out among the Goths, he led a crowd of believers across the Danube onto Roman soil. He gave the Goths letters and the Scriptures in Gothic. He taught them the creed that called the Son "like" the Father, joined the bishops at Constantinople in 360, and died there about 383, leaving that creed in writing.
3. **Why the connection matters:** the Goths took their Christianity from him, in their own language and in his creed, which his opponents called Arian. The Greek historians argue over when he took it because the Goths still held it in their day.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

The outline's Do not say supports calling it the later one. Change it to: "Under Valens, Socrates says, Ulfila also taught the people of the Gothic chief Athanaric, who in a later persecution punished the Christians, so that many died as martyrs."
