## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

1. **When he took the creed of 360** (the doubt that is the story's scene): Auxentius's "always" (E20); Socrates, "then for the first time" (E15a); Sozomen, at 360 thoughtlessly but still Nicene, then later on an embassy, under pressure or by conviction (E15b); Theodoret, under Valens, by Eudoxius's words and bribes (E15c). Auxentius is a pupil and partisan; the three historians are Nicene and write 60 to 70 years after his death.
2. **First bishop of the Goths or not:** Philostorgius, first (E4); Socrates, Theophilus before him (E4b).
3. **Who made him bishop, when:** Philostorgius, "Eusebius and the other prelates", in Constantine's time (E4); Duchesne and Kauffmann, Eusebius of Nicomedia under Constantius, Kauffmann's 341 (E4a).
4. **When he made the letters:** Philostorgius ties it to his care for them after his consecration (E8); Socrates sets it under Valens (E9).
5. **The crossing:** Auxentius, after seven years as bishop, under Constantius, fleeing a persecution (E7); Sozomen, under Valens, fleeing the Huns, with Ulfila leading the embassy (E17a). These may be two crossings; no held ancient text says so. Tell each as its witness tells it.
6. **The persecutor of the first persecution:** unnamed in Auxentius (E6); Duchesne's Hermanaric is the editor's (E6a). Athanaric's persecution in Socrates and Sozomen is the later one, under Valens (E17).
7. **The numbers:** Auxentius's thirty and forty echo Scripture; Kauffmann notes the age used numbers so but counts them as real years (AUX 3755-3760, 3780-3781). All years (311, 341, 348-349, 383) are Kauffmann's.
8. **The creed's form:** Waitz's doubt that it was left in Latin as we have it (E20a). The manuscript is damaged and the letter's first line is lost (STR 633).
9. **Late witnesses:** Isidore (c. 624), Jordanes (551) and Walafrid (9th c.) carry him on but add nothing about his life that the earlier witnesses lack.
