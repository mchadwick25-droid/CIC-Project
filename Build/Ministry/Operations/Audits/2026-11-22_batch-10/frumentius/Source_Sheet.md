## A. The one narrative: Rufinus, from Edesius

**A1. Who tells it and from whom.** Later (Rufinus wrote about 401, by Fremantle, or 402-403, by the RUF file header; the events about 330 to 350), but from an eyewitness. Documented that Rufinus says so; the whole early life rests on this one chain.
- RUF 27383-27385: "quae nos ita gesta non opinione vulgi, sed ipso Edesio Tyri / presbytero postmodum facto, qui Frumentii comes prius fuerat, referente / cognovimus."
- English, N202 4372-4374 (Socrates, tr. Zenos 1890): "Rufinus / assures us that he heard these facts from Edesius, who was afterwards / ordained to the priesthood at Tyre."
- Date, editor: N203 44550-44552 (Fremantle): "The date is probably 401, since in the / Preface Rufinus says that he had been requested to translate Eusebius / at the time when Alaric was invading Italy." RUF header (file line 8): "Books X-XI (written 402-403". Tell no year; "around 400" at most.

**A2. Setting: a land no apostle reached; the time of Constantine.** Later (Rufinus). Documented as Rufinus's frame.
- RUF 27268-27269, 27302-27303: "sed longo interior tractu India ulterior iacet, multis variisque hnguis et / gentibus habitata, quam velut longe remotam nullus apostolicae prae- / ... dieationis vomer inpresserat, quae tamen temporibus Constantini tali / quadam ex causa semina fidei prima suscepit." Plain sense: far beyond lies further India, home to many peoples and tongues; no plough of apostolic preaching had cut it, yet in the times of Constantine it received the first seeds of faith, for this reason.
- "India": N202 4298-4300 (Zenos, editor): "The Indians / mentioned in this chapter are no other than the Abyssinians. The name / India is used as an equivalent of Ethiopia." N203 7701-7703 (Jackson, editor): "The term “India” is used vaguely ... Here the Indians are Abyssinians." Widely Accepted (editors). The emperor's letter (C1) names the city: Auxumis.

**A3. The voyage and the killing.** Later (Rufinus, from Edesius). Documented as Rufinus tells it.
- RUF 27305-27314: "cuius exemplo invitatus etiam Meropius qui- / dam Tyrius filosofus simili ex causa adire Indiam voluit, habens secum / duos puerulos, quos liberalibus htteris utpote propinquos instituebat. / quorum unus, qui erat iunior, Edesius, alter Frumentius vocabatur. / igitur pervisis et in notitiam captis his, quibus animus pascebatur, cum / philosofus redire coepisset, aquae vel ceterorum necessariorum causa ad / portum quendam navis qua vehebatur adplicuit. moris est inibi bar- / barorum, ut si quando foedus sibi cum Romanis turbatum vicinae nun- / tiaverint gentes, omnes, qui apud eos ex Romanis inventi fuerint, iugu- / lentur. invaditur navis filosofi, cuncti cum ipso pariter perimuntur."
- Plain sense: Meropius, a philosopher of Tyre, went to India, taking two small boys, his relatives, whom he was teaching; the younger was Edesius, the other Frumentius. On the way home the ship put in at a harbour for water. It was the custom there that when neighbours reported the treaty with Rome broken, every Roman found among them was killed. The ship was attacked and all were killed with him.
- Note: Rufinus never says "shipwreck", "merchant" or "brothers". "Tyre": Meropius is Tyrian; Edesius later goes to Tyre to his parents (A6). Sozomen: "Tyre in Phœnicia" (N202 29870).

**A4. The boys under the tree; the king's court.** Later (Rufinus). Documented as Rufinus tells it.
- RUF 27315-27319: "pueruli reperti sub arbore meditantes et lectiones suas parantes, bar- / barorum miseratione servati ducuntur ad regem. horum 1116 alterum, / id est Edesium sibi pincernam fecit, Frumentio vero, quem quasi per- / spicacem deprehenderat et prudentem, rationes suas scriniaque commisit. / ex quo et in honore magno apud regem habiti sunt et in amore." (OCR "1116" stands for "ille".)
- Plain sense: the boys, found under a tree going over and preparing their lessons, were spared out of the barbarians' pity and taken to the king. He made Edesius his cup-bearer; to Frumentius, whom he found sharp and wise, he gave his accounts and his records. From then on they were held in great honour and affection.
- English, N202 4333-4336 (Socrates): "constituted one of them, whose name was Edesius, / cup-bearer at his table; the other, named Frumentius, he entrusted with / the care of the royal records."

**A5. The queen; the Roman merchants; places of prayer.** Later (Rufinus). Documented as Rufinus tells it.
- RUF 27319-27330: "at / vero morlens rex uxorem cum parvo filio regni reliquit heredem, adu- / lescentibus autem quid vellent agendi dedit liberam facultatem. quos / tamen regina suppliciter exorat ... ut secum usque quo adulesceret filius, regendi regni solh- / citudinem partirentur, et praecipue Frumentius ... idque dum agerent et regni gubernacula / Frumentius haberet in manibus, deo mentem eius et animos instigante / requirere sollieitius coepit, si qui inter negotiatores Romanos Christiani / essent, et ipsis potestatem maximam dare ac monere, ut conventicula per / loea singula facerent, ad quae Romano ritu orationis causa confluerent."
- RUF 27363-27366: "sed et ipse multo magis eadem facere et ita ceteros cohortarı, favore / et beneficiis invitare, praestare quidquid opportunum fuisset, loea aedi- / fieiis aliaque necessaria praebere et omni modo gestire, ut Christianorum / inibi semen exurgeret."
- Plain sense: the dying king left his wife and small son as heirs, and set the young men free. The queen begged them to share the work of ruling until her son grew up, Frumentius above all. With the kingdom's helm in his hands, moved by God, he sought out Christians among the Roman traders, gave them wide freedom, and urged them to make meeting places in each town where they could gather to pray in the Roman way. He did the same himself, gave sites for buildings and whatever else they needed, so that a Christian seed might spring up there.
- "Roman way", editor: N203 7739-7742 (Jackson): "to assemble to worship after the manner civilized / citizens of the Empire ... The expression has / nothing to do with the customs of the Church of Rome, in the later / sense of the word".

**A6. Home again: Edesius to Tyre, Frumentius to Alexandria.** Later (Rufinus). Documented as Rufinus tells it.
- RUF 27366-27374: "sed cum adolevisset regius puer, cui procura- / tionem regnr gerebant, expletis omnibus et ex fide traditis, multum licet / detinentibus et rogantibus, ut manerent, regina vel filio, ad orbem tamen / nostrum denuo revertuntur. et Edesio festinante Tyrum parentes pro- / pinquosque revisere, Frumentius Alexandriam pergit, dicens aequum non / esse opus occultare dominicum. igitur rem omnem, ut gesta est, exponit / episcopo ac monet, ut provideat virum aliquem dignum, quem congre- / gatis iam plurimis Christianis et ecelesiis constructis in barbarico solo / episcopum mittat."
- Plain sense: when the prince had grown, they handed everything back faithfully and, though the queen and her son begged them to stay, returned to our world. Edesius hurried to Tyre to see his parents and kin; Frumentius went to Alexandria, saying it was not right to hide the Lord's work. He told the bishop everything and urged him to find a worthy man to send as bishop, since many Christians were now gathered and churches built on barbarian soil.

**A7. Athanasius's answer and the consecration.** Later (Rufinus). Documented as Rufinus tells it; Athanasius's words are Rufinus's report.
- RUF 27374-27379: "tum vero Athanasius, nam is nuper sacerdotium / susceperat, attentius et perpensius Frumentii dieta gestaque considerans, / in concilio sacerdotum ait: >et quem alium invenimus virum talem, in / quo sit spiritus dei in ipso sieut in te, qui haec ita possit implere?- / et tradito ei sacerdotio, redire eum cum domini gratia, unde venerat, / iubet."
- Plain sense: Athanasius, who had lately become bishop, weighed Frumentius's words and deeds, and said in a council of priests: what other man like this could we find, with the Spirit of God in him as in you, who could carry this out in the same way? He gave him the bishop's office and told him to go back, with the Lord's grace, where he had come from.
- Athanasius became bishop on 8 June 328: N204 1519 (Robertson): "suppose that on June 8, 328, he was not much beyond his thirtieth year."

**A8. Bishop in "India": wonders and churches.** Later (Rufinus). The wonders are "it is said" in Rufinus's own voice.
- RUF 27379-27383: "quique cum episeopus perrexisset ad Indiam, tanta ei data esse / a deo virtutum gratia dieitur, ut signa per eum apostoliea fierent et / infinitus numerus barbarorum converteretur ad fidem. ex quo in Indiae / partibus et populi Christianorum et ecclesiae factae sunt, et sacerdotium / coepit."
- Plain sense: when he had gone to India as bishop, it is said, God gave him such grace of powers that signs like the apostles' were done through him, and a countless number of barbarians turned to the faith. From that time, in those parts of India, Christian peoples and churches came into being, and a priesthood began.
- Rufinus does not say the king was converted, does not name the king or queen, and gives no death.

## B. The later Greek retellings

**B1. Socrates follows Rufinus almost word for word.** Later (Socrates, about 439); editor's judgement on dependence.
- N202 4287-4288 (Valesius, in Zenos): "‘In this chapter Socrates has translated / Rufinus (<i>H. E.</i> I. 9) almost word for word".
- N203 7713-7716 (Jackson, editor): "The / version adopted by Rufinus, the earliest extant authority for this / story, is followed, in the main, by Socrates, Sozomen, and Theodoret."
- Socrates adds only small turns, e.g. N202 4346-4348: "In the course of a little while he built a house / of prayer; and having instructed some of the Indians in the principles / of Christianity, they fitted them for participation in the worship."

**B2. Sozomen: "no less than an apostle".** Later (Sozomen, about 440s), from Rufinus. Documented as Sozomen's praise.
- N202 29925-29930: "Frumentius, therefore, returned to India, and, it is said, discharged / the priestly functions so admirably that he became an object of / uni[versal] admiration, and was / revered as no less than an apostle. God highly honored him, enabling / him to perform many wonderful cures, and to work signs and wonders. / Such was the origin of the Indian priesthood."
- Sozomen calls Frumentius the older (N202 29880-29881): "He appointed the younger / one his cup-bearer; the older, Frumentius, he put over his house".

**B3. Theodoret: a different killing, a different line for Athanasius.** Later (Theodoret, about 449). Where sources disagree.
- N203 7719-7724: "The ship being compelled to / put in to land in order to obtain a fresh supply of water, the / barbarians fell upon her, drowned some of the crew, and took the others / prisoners. The uncle was among the number of those who were killed, and / the lads were conducted to the king." Rufinus has all killed (A3).
- N203 7754-7757: Athanasius "heard the story, and then “Who,” said he, / “better than you yourself can scatter the mists of ignorance, and / introduce among this people the light of Divine preaching?”" Not used: a later reworking of Rufinus's line.

**B4. The editors on the name and the later church.** Editors, citing the Dictionary of Christian Biography.
- N203 7767-7770 (Jackson): "“Frumentius received the / title of Abbana, or Abba Salama” (cf. Absalom), “the Father / of Peace.” “The bishopric of Auxume” ... “assumed a metropolitan character.”"
- N203 7771-7774 (Jackson): "Constantius afterwards / wrote to the Ethiopian Prince to ask him to replace Frumentius by / Theophilus, an Arian, but without success (Ath. Ap. ad Const. / 31)." The chapter he cites (C1) names no Theophilus and says nothing of success: an editor's error. Not used.

## C. The outside check: the emperor's letter, kept by Athanasius

**C1. Constantius to the rulers of Auxumis.** Contemporary and hostile (the emperor Constantius, about 356; preserved by Athanasius in his Apology to Constantius, 356-357 by Robertson). Documented. This is the one record from his own lifetime, and it confirms independently of Rufinus that Frumentius was bishop of Auxumis and that Athanasius had made him so.
- Date, editor: N204 7299 (Robertson): "(15) 356–7: *<span ...>Apol. ad Constantium</span>."
- Athanasius's frame, N204 36001-36005: "behold a third report reached me, to the effect that / letters had been written to the Princes of Auxumis, desiring that / Frumentius, Bishop of Auxumis, should be brought / from thence, and that search should be made for me even as far as the / country of the Barbarians". (Already a "voice" quote on the tradition card; not quoted here.)
- Address, N204 36111-36112: "Constantius Victor Maximus Augustus, to / Æzanes and Sazanes." Greek address line garbled in PG25 92793.
- Greek, PG25 92806-92814: "Οὐχοῦν Φρουμέντιον (36) / τὸν ἐπίσχοπον ἐχπέμπετε τὴν ταχίστην εἰς Αἴγυπτον / παρὰ τὸν σεμνότατον Γεώργιον τὸν ἐπέσχοπον καὶ / τοὺς ἄλλους τοὺς χατ᾽ Αἴγυπτον, οὗ τοῦ χειροτονεῖν / χαὶ χρίνειν τὰ τοιαῦτα χύριοι μᾶλλόν εἶσιν. “ἴστε γὰρ / δήπου χαὶ μέμνησθε, εἰ μὴ λίαν τὰ παρὰ πᾶσιν ὁμο- / λογούμενα μόνοι ἀγνοεῖν προσποιεῖσθε, ὅτι τὸν Φρου- / μέντιον τοῦτον εἰς ταύτην τὴν τάξιν τοῦ βίον χατ- / ἔστησεν ᾿Αθανάσιος, μυρίοις ἔνοχος ὧν χαχοῖς"
- English, N204 36144-36151: "Send / therefore speedily into Egypt the Bishop Frumentius to the most / venerable Bishop George, and the rest who are there, who have especial / authority to appoint to these offices, and to decide questions / concerning them. For of course you know and remember (unless you alone / pretend to be ignorant of that which all men are well aware of) that / this Frumentius was advanced to his present rank by Athanasius, a man / who is guilty of ten thousand crimes".
- The demand and the threat, N204 36142-36144 (already a "voice" quote on two tradition cards; not quoted here): "we command / that the same doctrine be professed in your Churches as in theirs." And N204 36160-36162: Frumentius, examined, "he shall / receive his appointment from them, if it shall indeed appear that he / has any right to be a Bishop"; N204 36166-36167: "our fear is lest he should pass over into Auxumis / and corrupt your people".
- George: the bishop the emperor set up at Alexandria in Athanasius's place. N204 5958-5960 (Robertson): "one denouncing him to the Alexandrians and recommending a new / bishop, one George, as their future guide".
- No held source says how the rulers answered, or what Frumentius did. Jackson's "without success" (B4) rests on nothing in the text.

**C2. When was he consecrated? Rufinus against the editors.** Where sources disagree. Rufinus: soon after Athanasius became bishop (A7, "nuper"), in the times of Constantine (A2). Editors: later.
- N202 4294-4296 (Valesius): "there are / reasons for thinking that this / conversion of the Indians by Frumentius happened in the reign of / Constantius and not of Constantine’".
- N204 5674-5684 (Robertson): "The / visit of Frumentius to Alexandria, and his consecration as bishop for / Auxume, are referred by Rufinus i. 9 (Socr. i. 19, &amp;c.) to the / beginning of the episcopate of Athanasius. But the chronology of the / story (Gwatkin, pp. 93 <i>sqq.</i>, D.C.B. ii. 236 where the argument / is faulty) forbids this altogether, while the letter of Constantius (p. / 250) is most natural if the consecration of Frumentius were then a / comparatively recent matter, scarcely intelligible if it had taken / place before the ‘deposition’ of Athan. by the council of / Tyre."
- N204 10405-10408 (Robertson's chronology, under the end of 346): "Earliest possible date for <i>consecration of / Frumentius by Athanasius.</i>"
- Confidence: Contested. Tell both, credited; no year as fact.

**C3. The king's own stone (modern identification).** The inscription is contemporary (first half of the fourth century, by Littmann); the match of names is the editors'. Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
- LIT 483: "Alleinstehende Platte aus schwarzem Granit, in situ; erste Hälfte des 4. Jahrh. n. Chr."
- LIT 567-569 (Littmann's German of the Greek, Nr. 4): "Ezanä, der König von Aksum ... der König der Könige, der Sohn des unbesiegten Gottes / Ares. Als sich einmal das Volk der Begä empört hatte, schickten wir unsere Brüder, den Safazänä und den Hade-" [fän]. Greek OCR (damaged) at LIT 497, 505-507, 511-512.
- LIT 1500-1501 (editor): "Mit Recht verweist Nöldeke darauf, daß in Iaia£avctg / das cu wahrscheinlich = e sei und daß die Schreibung 2aC,avccs im Briefe des Constantius damit übereinstimme." Plain sense: Nöldeke rightly notes that the spelling Sazanas in Constantius's letter matches the brother's name on the stone.
- BUD 8826-8829 (Budge, editor): "it seems to be certain that the king over whom Frumentius obtained / such great influence was ‘Ezana". Budge's own view; the stone itself names no Frumentius, and on it the king still calls himself son of Ares.

**D1. The Ethiopian Synaxarium: Abba Salama, 26 Hamle.** Late legend (the Ge'ez compilation is fifteenth century or so, by Littmann via Budge, SYN1 1089-1094; a thousand years after the events). Documented as the church's memory.

And the king made / ADYOS (sic) director of his household, and FERi MfeNATOS / keeper of the Laws and Archives of 'aksum ... And he arrived in the country of / Ethiopia during the reign of 'abrehA and 'asbeha, and he / preached the peace of Christ in all the regions thereof, and / because of this he was called ''Abba sal Am A." And after he / had saluted the men of Ethiopia he died in peace."
- Day heading: SYN 43470 "XXVI. UAULt" (26 Hamle); month, SYN 43481 "ELEVENTH MONTH— HAML1& (JULY 5-AUGUST 3)".
- Differences from Rufinus: names the king Alameda; the youths "taught ... the work of war"; they teach the children the faith; Athanasius "had been restored to his office" (SYN 43602); arrival under Abreha and Asbeha. Late, unsupported by the early witnesses.

**D2. The Ethiopians counted their bishops from him.** Outside, 1681 (Ludolf, from his Ethiopian informant Gregory). Later, about thirteen centuries. Documented as Ludolf's report.
- LUD 18719-18724 (raw OCR): "The first Metropolitan of Habessinia was Frumentiis, the / ... Ethiopic Apostle... - LUD 14858: "ther Frumentius and Edesfius were call'd Fremonatus and' Sydrac". And N202 4300-4302 (Zenos): "The christianization of / Ethiopia is attributed by the Ethiopians in their own records to / Fremonatos and Sydracos."
- The tree's own tradition entry (census `post-aksumite-ethiopian-christianity`, `relationsSummary`): the church "continuing to receive its bishops from the Coptic patriarchate in Alexandria." And `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity`: "It gave Aksum its first bishop".

**D3. Kept by other churches' calendars.** Later; reception. Documented.

NIKO 35442-35443: "Ούτος λοιπόν ό ϊσαπόστο- / λος Φρουμεντιος" (this Frumentius, equal to the apostles).
- Latin: BRV 83714, 83723-83726 (Roman Martyrology, 27 October, Bute 1908): "In Ethiopia, holy Frumentius, first / ... Bishop of that country, who was first / a captive there, then was ordained / Bishop by holy Athanasius, and spread / the Gospel in that country."

**D4. Carried on by Syriac and Egyptian chroniclers.** Later. Documented as reception.

## Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **A bishop for Aksum from Alexandria.** Rufinus: from then on churches and a priesthood began there (A8). The emperor himself treated Aksum's bishop as Alexandria's business: he sent Frumentius to the bishops of Egypt, "who have especial authority" to appoint (C1). The Ethiopians counted their metropolitans from him (D2); the tree's tradition entries carry the line on to Alexandria's later bishops for Ethiopia (D2).
2. **The story itself, carried on.** Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret retold Rufinus (B1-B3); Syriac chroniclers took it from them, and an Egyptian chronicler remembered "Afrudit" (D4).
3. **His name kept.** The Ethiopian church's Synaxarium as Abba Salama, the Revealer of the Light (D1); the Greek calendar on 30 November as equal to the apostles, and the Roman Martyrology on 27 October (D3).
4. **What he defended.** No source records his answer to the emperor. What is recorded is the demand: that he leave his see, go to Egypt, and be judged and re-appointed by the emperor's bishop (C1).

## Links

- Athanasius of Alexandria: made him bishop (A7, C1); kept the emperor's letter (C1).
- Rufinus of Aquileia: wrote down his story, from Edesius (A1).
- Constantine the Great: Rufinus sets the story "in the times of Constantine" (A2).
- Socrates of Constantinople, Sozomen, Theodoret of Cyrrhus: retold Rufinus (B1-B3).
- John of Nikiu: remembered him (D4).
- Philostorgius: tells of a rival envoy at Aksum (E1), not of him.
