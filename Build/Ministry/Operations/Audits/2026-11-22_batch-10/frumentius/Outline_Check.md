# Outline check: Frumentius (`frumentius`)

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** A boy from a family of Tyre, spared when the rest of his ship's company was killed on a foreign coast (Rufinus's "India", which the editors read as Aksum), rose at the king's court to keep its accounts and records and then to help the queen rule. He sought out the Christian Roman traders there and gave them places to pray. Grown, he went to Alexandria and asked Athanasius for a bishop for them; Athanasius made him that bishop and sent him back. That is why he is on the tree: the church of Aksum takes its first bishop, and its tie to Alexandria, from him (RUF 27305-27383; C1; D2).

**Also told.**
- The whole early life rests on one chain: Rufinus, writing about 400, says he had it from Edesius, Frumentius's companion, later a presbyter at Tyre (RUF 27383-27385). Documented that he says so.
- The outside check: Constantius's letter of about 356 to Aezanes and Sazanes, rulers of Auxumis, kept by Athanasius, which names Frumentius bishop of Auxumis, made so by Athanasius, and demands he be sent to Egypt to George to be examined (N204 36111-36167). Contemporary, hostile; Documented. No source gives the answer.
- The date of his consecration: Rufinus puts it soon after Athanasius became bishop; Robertson and Valesius put it later (346 at earliest). Contested.
- Bishop in Aksum: churches and a priesthood begin; the wonders are "it is said" in Rufinus's voice.
- Reception: retold by Socrates, Sozomen ("no less than an apostle"), Theodoret; Ethiopian memory as Abba Salama; the Ethiopians count their metropolitans from him; feasts in Greek and Roman calendars.

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. Robertson's argument is overstated (Also told, item 4; beat 5).** Robertson (N204 5679-5684) says the letter "is most natural if the consecration of Frumentius were then a comparatively recent matter, scarcely intelligible if it had taken place before the 'deposition' of Athan. by the council of Tyre." That is "most natural", not "makes sense only if". His chronology gives the end of 346 as the "Earliest possible date" (N204 10405-10408). Also, the year 328 is Robertson's (N204 1519), not Rufinus's. Rufinus says only "nuper" (RUF 27374).
- In Also told item 4, replace "Rufinus puts it just after Athanasius became bishop in 328; Athanasius's English editor, Robertson, holds that the story's own timeline rules this out, and that the emperor's letter makes sense only if the consecration was recent." with: "Rufinus puts it just after Athanasius became bishop, which was in 328 by his editor's reckoning; Athanasius's English editor, Robertson, holds that the story's own timeline rules this out, and that the emperor's letter reads most naturally if the consecration was then fairly recent: no earlier than the end of 346, he reckons."
- In beat 5, replace "Rufinus sets this just after Athanasius became bishop, in 328; Athanasius's English editor, Robertson, argues that the story's own dates forbid it, and that the emperor's letter of about 356 makes sense only if the consecration was recent: after 346, he reckons." with: "Rufinus sets this just after Athanasius became bishop, which was in 328 by his editor's reckoning; Athanasius's English editor, Robertson, argues that the story's own dates forbid it, and that the emperor's letter of about 356 reads most naturally if the consecration was then fairly recent: no earlier than the end of 346, he reckons."
- In Do not say, replace "Say: Rufinus puts it just after Athanasius became bishop, in 328; Robertson puts it after 346." with: "Say: Rufinus puts it just after Athanasius became bishop (in 328, by his editor's reckoning; Rufinus gives no year); Robertson puts it no earlier than the end of 346, and calls the later date the more natural reading of the letter, not a certainty."
