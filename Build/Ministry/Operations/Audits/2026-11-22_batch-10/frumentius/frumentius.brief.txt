Two small boys, Frumentius and Edesius, were found under a tree, going over their lessons, Rufinus says. Everyone else on their ship had just been killed. The people of that coast spared them out of pity and took them to their king.

Frumentius, from a family of Tyre, grew up a captive at that court and rose to share in ruling it. He gathered the Christian traders there to pray, then went to Alexandria to ask its bishop, Athanasius, for a bishop, and came back as that bishop himself. Rufinus calls the land India. A hostile letter from the emperor, which Athanasius kept, names its city Auxumis, that is Aksum, a kingdom in what is now Ethiopia and Eritrea. It calls Frumentius their bishop, made so by Athanasius.

Rufinus of Aquileia wrote the story down around 400, and the whole early life rests on him. In the times of Constantine, he writes, a philosopher from Tyre sailed there to see the land, with two boys, his relatives, whom he was teaching. On the way home the ship put in at a harbor for water. The people had heard their treaty with the Romans was broken, and by custom they killed every Roman they found.

The king made Edesius his cup-bearer, and gave Frumentius, whom he found sharp and wise, charge of his accounts and records. Dying, he left his wife and small son as heirs, and set the young men free. But the queen begged them, Frumentius above all, to stay and help her rule until her son grew up.

With the kingdom in his hands, Rufinus says, God stirred him to seek out Christians among the Roman traders. He urged them to set up meeting places to pray in, and gave them sites for buildings, so that a Christian seed might grow there.

When the prince came of age, the two handed everything back and came home. Edesius went to his parents at Tyre, and that is where Rufinus's story comes from. "We learned that these things happened so, not from common talk, but from Edesius himself, who told it. He had been Frumentius's companion, and was later made a priest at Tyre."

Frumentius went to Alexandria instead, saying it was not right to hide the Lord's work. He told Athanasius everything, and urged him to send a worthy man as bishop. Rufinus reports Athanasius answering before a council of priests: "What other man like this could we find, with the Spirit of God in him as in you, who could carry it through in this way?" He made Frumentius bishop and sent him back.

When is disputed. Rufinus sets it just after Athanasius became bishop, in 328 by his editor's reckoning. But Athanasius's English editor, Robertson, says the story's own timeline rules that out. He puts it no earlier than the end of 346, as the more natural reading of the emperor's letter.

Back as bishop, it is said, Rufinus writes, signs like the apostles' were done through him, and countless people turned to the faith.

That tie to Alexandria was fought over. About 356, with Athanasius driven from his city, the emperor Constantius wrote to the rulers of Auxumis. He demanded the same teaching in their churches as in the empire's, and ordered: "So send Frumentius the bishop to Egypt with all speed." There George, whom the emperor had set up as bishop in Athanasius's place, would judge him, and restore him only if he was found fit. And the letter named the man who had made him: "This Frumentius was set in this rank by Athanasius, a man guilty of countless crimes." No source we hold says how the rulers answered, or what Frumentius did.

The church of Aksum kept taking its bishops from Alexandria. Centuries later, the Ethiopians counted their chief bishops in order from him, ninety-five by the count a German scholar recorded in 1681. Three Greek church historians retold Rufinus's account in the next century, Socrates almost word for word, one editor says. And the church of Aksum lives on in Ethiopia's church today.

That church's book of saints, written down about a thousand years later, tells his story again, with new names and turns. It calls him the Revealer of the Light. It says he preached the peace of Christ in all the regions of Ethiopia, and was called Abba Salama for it, Abba meaning father. And after he had greeted the people of Ethiopia, it says, he died in peace.
