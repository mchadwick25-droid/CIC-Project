# Review: Frumentius (`frumentius`), 2026-11-23

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Frumentius, a boy from a family of Tyre, held captive at the court of Aksum (in what is now Ethiopia and Eritrea), who became its first bishop.
- **What happened:** Spared when his ship's company was killed, he rose to help rule the kingdom, gathered the Roman Christian traders to pray, went to Alexandria to ask Athanasius for a bishop, and was sent back as that bishop. About 356 the emperor Constantius ordered him sent to Egypt to be judged by Athanasius's replacement.
- **Why the connection matters:** Aksum's church took its bishop from Alexandria and went on doing so. Because of that tie, its bishop was caught up in Athanasius's quarrel with the emperor.

**Essence** (census and record): he brought the Christians of Aksum to the bishop of Alexandria and came back as their bishop, so the church of Aksum began as a daughter of Alexandria's. **Also told:** Rufinus's record from Edesius; the places of prayer for the traders; the emperor's hostile letter and its stakes; the disputed date; Abba Salama in the Ethiopian book of saints. All present with their weight. The hook leads straight into the essence, which is told by word 120. After the essence, the life runs once, in order (the voyage, the court, the traders, home, Alexandria, the date, bishop, the letter). The story ends on the person ("he died in peace").

Where I got lost: only at "By 1681 ... in the next century" (finding 1). Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice 4.** An adult narrator's voice throughout, with Rufinus's own turns kept ("a Christian seed might grow there"). The double attribution in "it is said, Rufinus writes" is clumsy when heard.
- **Craft 4.** Strong hook, early essence, clean order, and the letter carries the stakes in its own words. One ambiguous time phrase in What came of them.
- **Rigor 4.** Every sentence traces to the outline. All three quotes check against the Latin (RUF 27376-27377, 27383-27385) and the Greek (PG25 92806-92807, 92812-92814). The Do not say list is honored. Two small imprecisions (findings 3 and 4).
- **Accessibility 4.** Grade 8.2, reading ease 79.1, longest sentence 30 words. The glosses (India, Auxumis, Abba) are worked into the sentences.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING (misleading time).** "By 1681, a German scholar recorded, ... Three Greek church historians retold the story in the next century". Heard straight after 1681, "the next century" points to the 1700s. Change it to "in the century after Rufinus" (or move the historians' sentence before Ludolf's).
   *Do not say line for the outline:* "'In the next century' for the Greek historians, unless Rufinus or 400 is the time named just before it. Say: in the century after Rufinus."
2. **MINOR.** "Back as bishop, it is said, Rufinus writes, signs ..." Two attributions stacked. Say: "Back as bishop, Rufinus writes, it was said that signs like the apostles' were done through him ..." (keep "it is said" in Rufinus's voice).
3. **MINOR.** "which an editor puts in 328. Athanasius's English editor, Robertson, says ..." The 328 date is Robertson's too (N204 1519), so the listener hears two editors in dispute. Say "which was in 328" or "which Robertson himself puts in 328".
4. **MINOR.** "He puts it no earlier than late 346, as the more natural reading of the emperor's letter." Robertson's "earliest possible date" is a chronology table entry (N204 10405-10408), and the letter is "most natural" if the consecration was recent (N204 5680-5684). Say: "He puts it no earlier than late 346, and says the emperor's letter reads more naturally if it was recent."
5. **MINOR.** "the two handed everything back and came home. ... Frumentius went to Alexandria instead". "Came home" then "instead" jars. Say "came back to the Roman world" (RUF 27369).
6. **MINOR (names heard once).** George is named once and does not act beyond the letter's plan; the outline gives him by role. "the emperor's bishop in Athanasius's place" alone does the job. Robertson is named once and is not a link; "Athanasius's English editor" alone would do. Neither loses the listener.

## Writer's note

The writer also dropped "the Revealer of the Light" and the phrase "in the Roman way". Neither was needed: the name Abba Salama and its reason stay, and dropping "in the Roman way" removes a phrase the outline had to guard with a Do not say line. Not blocking.
