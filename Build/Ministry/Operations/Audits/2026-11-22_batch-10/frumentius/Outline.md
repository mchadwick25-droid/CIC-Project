# Outline: Frumentius (`frumentius`)

Source keys and full verbatim lines: `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder (RUF, PG25, N204, N202, N203, LIT, SYN, and the rest). Every line below cites its file and lines.

- **The essence:** Frumentius, a boy from a family of Tyre, was taken captive when his ship put in at a foreign harbour and everyone else aboard was killed; he rose at the king's court in what Rufinus calls India, gathered the Christian traders from the Roman lands there into places of prayer, and went to Alexandria to ask Athanasius for a bishop, and came back as that bishop himself. A hostile letter from the emperor to the rulers of Auxumis, that is Aksum, which Athanasius kept, calls him their bishop and says Athanasius made him so. (RUF 27305-27315, 27322-27330, 27369-27379; N204 36108-36109, 36148-36151; PG25 92811-92814.) Documented as Rufinus tells it; the bishopric is Documented by the letter; "Aksum" for Rufinus's "India" is the editors' and the letter's (Widely Accepted).
- **Also told:**
  - Whose record it is: Rufinus of Aquileia, writing around 400, says he had it all from Edesius, Frumentius's companion, who became a priest at Tyre. The early life rests on that one chain. (RUF 27383-27385; N203 44550-44552, editor.) Documented that Rufinus says so.
  - What he built before he was a bishop: with the kingdom in his hands, he sought out Christians among the Roman traders and urged them to set up meeting places to pray in the Roman way, and gave them sites for buildings. (RUF 27326-27330, 27363-27366.) Documented as Rufinus tells it.
  - The outside check and its stakes: after Athanasius was driven from his city in 356, the emperor Constantius ordered the rulers at Aksum to send Frumentius to Egypt, to be examined by the bishop the emperor had put in Athanasius's place and given his post again only if found fit, and called Athanasius "guilty of countless crimes". (N204 36111-36112, 36144-36154, 36159-36162; PG25 92806-92814; N204 64351-64368, the index.) Documented; hostile.
  - When it happened: Rufinus gives no year, but puts it just after Athanasius became bishop ("nuper"), which was in 328, the year of the consuls Januarius and Justus by the Alexandrian index to Athanasius's Easter letters. No old source gives another date. (RUF 27374-27375; N204 64076-64081, the index.) Documented as Rufinus tells it. Robertson's later date (N204 5676-5684, 10405-10408) is a modern view, to the card (Modern pass).
  - How his church remembered him: as Abba Salama, in the Ethiopian church's book of saints, centuries later. (SYN 43570-43612; SYN 24977-25005.) Late legend.
  Checked against the census: teaser and job line hold. Two census words do not: "shipwrecked" and "tutor" (census `aksumite-christianity-conversion-era`, `why`). Rufinus has the ship put in for water and attacked, not wrecked (RUF 27310-27314), and gives Frumentius the king's accounts and records, then a share in ruling (RUF 27317-27318, 27322-27325). "A boy from Tyre": Rufinus calls Meropius a Tyrian and has Edesius go home to his parents at Tyre (RUF 27306, 27369-27370); he never names Frumentius's own birthplace. So the story says "from a family of Tyre" or "with a philosopher from Tyre".

- **Setting:**
  - Time: the 330s to the 350s. Rufinus says "in the times of Constantine" (RUF 27302-27303); Athanasius became bishop in 328 (N204 1519); the emperor's letter after Athanasius's flight of February 356 (N204 36151-36154, the letter; N204 64351-64362, the index). Spoken: "the early 300s"; the letter after Athanasius was driven out in 356, by the letter's own words and the Alexandrian index (Modern pass).
  - Places: Tyre, a port of Phoenicia (N202 29870, Sozomen; RUF 27306); a harbour on a foreign coast, where the ship put in for water (RUF 27310-27311); the king's court in what Rufinus calls India (RUF 27268-27269, 27316); Alexandria in Egypt (RUF 27370). The letter names the city Auxumis (N204 36109, 36166), Aksum (census `aksumite-christianity-conversion-era`, `placeLabel`). Spoken: "a kingdom on the Red Sea, Aksum, in what is now Ethiopia and Eritrea", from the census (`aksumite-christianity-conversion-era`, `region`, `why`), not from Rufinus.
  - Tradition: Aksumite Christianity (conversion era) (census `aksumite-christianity-conversion-era`, `name`; informal name "The Conversion of Aksum"). Spoken: "the church of Aksum". Its line: it grew out of the church of Alexandria, which gave it its first bishop (census `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity`, `relationsSummary`).
- **Words to explain:**
  - India (Rufinus's word): say "what Rufinus calls India"; his editors take it for Ethiopia. (RUF 27268; N202 4298-4300, editor.)
  - Auxumis: the emperor's name for the city, Aksum. (N204 36109; census.)
  - Alexandria's bishop: the head of the church of Egypt, in Egypt's great city; say "the bishop of Alexandria, Athanasius". (RUF 27370-27374.)
  - George: the bishop the emperor had set up at Alexandria in Athanasius's place. (N204 5958-5960, editor; N204 36145-36146.)
  - priest: for Rufinus's "presbyter" (RUF 27384). No gloss needed.
  - Abba Salama: the name the Ethiopian book of saints gives him; it says he was so called because he preached the peace of Christ. "Abba" is "father". (SYN 43610-43611; N203 7767-7769, Jackson's "the Father of Peace", editor.)
  - the Ethiopian book of saints: the Synaxarium, the church's calendar of saints' lives. Say "centuries later, the Ethiopian church's book of saints": it tells of bishops of Alexandria in the 1100s (SYN 18812-18830, 25000-25005). Its editors' fifteenth-century date (SYN1 1089-1094) is a modern view, to the card (Modern pass).
- **Shape:** Missionary or translator. The journey: a captive's road from a foreign harbour to the king's court, to Alexandria, and back. The bridge: a church, first places of prayer for Roman traders, then a bishop from Alexandria. The receivers: the court and the people of Aksum, whose own record is late (the book of saints) or a stone that names its king (beat 7). Whose record it is: Rufinus's, from Edesius, a Roman companion; the one outside check is the emperor's hostile letter. Say both.
- **Sibling:** `rufinus-of-aquileia` (in the batch; its outline read): tells Rufinus's translating, his quarrel with Jerome and his Church History, and gives Frumentius one clause. This story credits Rufinus only as its witness, says once that he had it from Edesius, and does not retell his History's purpose or his life; it shares none of his outline's quotes. `athanasius-of-alexandria` (live, read): tells the night of 356 and his years in hiding, and does not tell Frumentius; here the emperor's letter shows the hunt for him reaching to Aksum, in one sentence, without retelling the night. `constantine-the-great` (live): Rufinus's "in the times of Constantine" only.
- **Openings:**
  1. (Recommended.) Two small boys were found under a tree, going over their lessons, Rufinus says. Everyone else on their ship had just been killed. The people of that coast spared them out of pity and took them to their king. (RUF 27313-27316.) Then the essence, with the name.
  2. After Athanasius was driven from his city in 356, an emperor's letter went to two rulers at Aksum, Aizanas and Sazanas. It ordered them to send their bishop, Frumentius, to Egypt with all speed. Athanasius, who had made him bishop, copied it into his own defence. (N204 36108-36112, 36144-36146; PG25 92806-92807; N204 7299, editor.) If this opening is used, beat 7 tells only what the letter goes on to say.
- **Story beats:**
  1. **The voyage.** Meropius, a philosopher from Tyre, sailed to what Rufinus calls India to see the land, taking two small boys, his relatives, whom he was teaching; Edesius was the younger, Frumentius the other. On the way home the ship put in at a harbour for water. The people there had heard their treaty with the Romans was broken, and by custom killed every Roman they found. All aboard were killed but the boys. (RUF 27305-27316.) Documented as Rufinus tells it. Theodoret, a later historian, has some drowned and others taken prisoner (N203 7719-7723): optional half-clause, or to the card.
  2. **The court.** The king made Edesius his cup-bearer, and gave Frumentius, whom he found sharp and wise, charge of his accounts and records. Dying, the king left his wife and small son as heirs and set the young men free. The queen begged them to stay and share the work of ruling until her son grew up, Frumentius above all. (RUF 27316-27325.) Documented as Rufinus tells it.
  3. **The traders.** With the kingdom in his hands, Rufinus says, God stirred him to seek out Christians among the Roman traders. He gave them great authority and urged them to make meeting places, place by place, where they could gather to pray in the Roman way, which an editor reads as the way of the empire's citizens, not of the church of Rome. He did the same himself, and gave sites for buildings, so that a Christian seed might grow there. (RUF 27326-27330, 27363-27366; N203 7739-7742, editor.) Documented as Rufinus tells it; "God stirred" stays in Rufinus's voice.
  4. **Home.** When the prince came of age, they handed everything back and, though the queen and her son begged them to stay, came home to the Roman world. Edesius hurried to Tyre to see his parents; later he became a priest there, and that is where Rufinus's story comes from: Quote 2. (RUF 27366-27370, 27383-27385.) Documented that Rufinus says so.
  5. **Alexandria.** Frumentius went to Alexandria instead, saying it was not right to hide the Lord's work. He told its bishop, Athanasius, everything, and urged him to send a worthy man as bishop, since there were now many Christians and churches there. Athanasius, newly bishop, weighed what Frumentius had said and done, and told a council of priests: Quote 1. He made Frumentius bishop and sent him back. Rufinus gives no year, but sets this just after Athanasius became bishop, which was in 328 by the Alexandrian index to his Easter letters. (RUF 27370-27379; N204 64076-64081.) Documented as Rufinus tells it. The editors' later dates (Robertson, N204 5676-5684, 10405-10408; Valesius, N202 4294-4296) are modern views: to the card, not the story (Modern pass).
  6. **Bishop in "India".** Back as bishop, it is said, Rufinus writes, God gave him such power that signs like the apostles' were done through him, and countless people turned to the faith; from then on there were Christian peoples, churches and a priesthood in those parts. (RUF 27379-27383.) Documented as Rufinus tells it; the wonders stay "it is said", Rufinus's own hedge.
  7. **The emperor's letter.** After Athanasius was driven from Alexandria in February 356 (the Alexandrian index, N204 64351-64362) and, the letter says, wandering from country to country, the emperor Constantius wrote to the rulers of Auxumis, Aizanas and Sazanas. He commanded the same teaching in their churches as in the empire's, and ordered: Quote 3. Frumentius was to be judged by George, the bishop the emperor had set up in Athanasius's place, and those with him, "who have especial authority" to appoint to such offices, and given his post again only if he had any right to be a bishop. Athanasius copied the letter into his own defence to the emperor, as proof that the reports of the hunt for him were true (N204 36015-36018). No held source says how the rulers answered, or what Frumentius did. George in Athanasius's place: the letter (N204 36145), Athanasius's "intrusion of George" (N204 35958-35961) and the index, "Georgius entered" (N204 64366). (N204 36001-36005, 36108-36112, 36142-36154, 36159-36162; PG25 92806-92814.) Documented; hostile. Not for the story (Modern pass): A royal stone at Aksum, carved in Greek in the first half of the 300s by its editor's dating, names a king Aizanas and his brother Saiazanas, and its editors match the brother to the letter's Sazanas (LIT 483, 567-569, 1500-1501). Dominant Modern Reconstruction: to the card only.
- **What came of them** (about a third of the story), heaviest first:
  1. **A bishop for Aksum from Alexandria.** Rufinus dates the church and its priesthood there from him (RUF 27381-27383). That tie is why the emperor's letter (beat 7) sent him to Egypt to be judged: told once, in beat 7, where "who have especial authority" to appoint goes (N204 36145-36148). Centuries later, the Ethiopian church's book of saints still has the bishops of Alexandria making the bishop of Ethiopia: Cosmas, fifty-eighth archbishop of Alexandria, consecrates Peter (SYN 18812-18830), and Michael, bishop of Ethiopia, may appoint no bishop without the command of the archbishop on the throne of Mark (SYN 25056-25069). Documented (Rufinus, the letter, the book of saints). Ludolf's count of ninety-five bishops (LUD 18719-18724, 1681) and the tree's tradition entries (census) are not story sources: to the card (Modern pass). **Defended:** none recorded. What is recorded is the demand that he leave his see and be judged by the emperor's bishop (N204 36144-36151); the writer must not say he refused.
  2. **The story carried on.** The Greek church historians Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret retold Rufinus's account in the next century; Socrates credits Rufinus, who had it from Edesius (N202 4372-4374); one editor's "almost word for word" is to the card; Sozomen adds that Frumentius was "revered as no less than an apostle". Syriac chroniclers took it from them (Mingana's view, card only), and a chronicler in Egypt, John of Nikiu, remembered him three centuries on. (N202 4287-4288, Valesius; N203 7713-7716, Jackson; N202 29927-29928; MIN 1152-1155; NIK 4674-4677.) Documented; the dependence is the editors'.
  3. **His name kept.** The Ethiopian church's book of saints remembers him on 26 Hamle as Abba Salama, "the Revealer of the Light": it calls him Fremenatos, names the king Alameda, and says he arrived in the reign of Abreha and Asbeha. The Greek calendar keeps him on 30 November as "equal to the apostles", and the Roman Martyrology on 27 October. (SYN 43470, 43570-43612; NIKO 23208-23209, 35442-35443; BRV 83714, 83723-83726.) Late legend (the Ethiopian details); Documented as calendars. If Ending 1 is used, the name Abba Salama and its reason are told there only; What came 3 then says just that the Ethiopian book of saints keeps his day. Who keeps him today: the tree's tradition entry says the church of Aksum is the same church as later Ethiopian Christianity, which carries the tradition today (census `aksumite-christianity-conversion-era`, `livingEvidence`); one clause at most, never the closing line.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** what passed was a church's head. Frumentius brought the Christians of Aksum, first Roman traders gathered in places of prayer (RUF 27326-27330), to the bishop of Alexandria, and came back as their bishop by Athanasius's hand (RUF 27376-27379). The emperor's letter shows that this tie ran both ways and was fought over: Aksum's bishop was answerable to Alexandria, so when Alexandria's bishop fell from the emperor's favour, the emperor demanded that Aksum's bishop come to Egypt to be judged by the man who replaced him (N204 36144-36151, 36159-36162). The listener needs to understand that Aksum's church began as a daughter of Alexandria's, and that this was why its bishop was caught in Athanasius's quarrel with the emperor. Tell it through the acts and the letter's own words, not a "this matters because" sentence.
- **Ending:**
  1. (Recommended.) Centuries later, the book of saints of the Ethiopian church tells his story again with new names and turns, and in it later bishops of Alexandria still make the bishop of Ethiopia (SYN 18812-18830, 25056-25069). It says he preached the peace of Christ in all the regions of Ethiopia, and was called Abba Salama for it. "And after he had saluted the men of Ethiopia he died in peace." (SYN 43608-43612.) That closing line is a fourth quoted span, from Budge's English of 1928; tell it in the narrator's words unless the writer drops a quote. Late legend; credit it.
  2. Rufinus's own last word on him: from then on there were Christian peoples, churches and a priesthood in those parts (RUF 27381-27383). Use only if beat 6 does not already carry it.

  - "Frumentius was born in Tyre." Say: he sailed with a philosopher from Tyre, his relative; Edesius went home to his parents at Tyre.
  - "Meropius was a merchant", or "the two were brothers", or "on the Red Sea" as Rufinus's. Say: a philosopher, his relatives, a harbour; Rufinus names no sea (RUF 27306-27311). Budge's "Syrian merchant" and "brothers" are not in Rufinus (BUD 8730-8733).

## What it was about

Added 2026-11-23 for the "Explain it or leave it out" pass (Decision-Log, 2026-11-23). Files under `cic/texts/`: N204 `npnf204_athanasius-select-works-letters.xml`, N203 `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`, RUF as above. The explanations are in the narrator's words; no new quotes.

1. **The teaching the emperor demanded ("the same teaching in their churches as in the empire's").** Explain. Plain sense: Athanasius called the emperor's bishops Arians, after Arius, who taught that the Son had a beginning, before all time, and God had none; Athanasius held that the Son shares the Father's own being. Witnesses: the letter itself, which demands "the same doctrine" but never names it (N204 36142-36144); Athanasius, who says the Egyptian churches were handed to "the professors of Arianism" (N204 35938-35940), credited as the hunted party; Arius in his own letter to Eusebius of Nicomedia, kept by Theodoret: "we say that the Son has a beginning, but that God is without beginning", and his opponent's "God always, the Son always" (N203 6043-6047, 6025-6027); the Nicene creed as Athanasius gives it, "from the essence of the Father ... One in essence with the Father" (N204 19675-19680); Robertson's note tying the letter's "supreme God" to the wording of Arius's Thalia (N204 36115-36121, editor). Confidence: each side's teaching Documented in its own words; the label "Arians" for the emperor's bishops of 356 is Athanasius's and Contested, since his own editor says the East mostly rejected the Nicene wording without being Arian (N204 5741-5744), so the brief gives it as what Athanasius called them.
2. **"Athanasius driven from his city" and "a man guilty of countless crimes": what he was charged with.** Explain. Plain sense: the emperor's side had charged Athanasius, among other things, with once turning the emperor's brother against him, and writing to a rebel emperor; he denied it all. Witnesses: the letter, saying he could not clear himself of any charge (N204 36151-36153), the enemy's view, credited to the emperor; Athanasius's Apology to Constantius, answering four charges: setting Constans against Constantius (N204 34917-34924; Constans was dead by 356, "of blessed and everlasting memory"), writing to "the tyrant" Magnentius (N204 35101-35104), using the great church before it was dedicated (N204 35398-35405), and refusing to leave his church against an imperial order (N204 35620-35624); Robertson on Constans murdered in 350 and Magnentius's bid for Egypt (N204 5748-5752, editor). That he was driven out is already in the brief; Athanasius says bishops were banished for refusing "to subscribe to my condemnation" (N204 35929-35930). Confidence: that the charges were made, Documented (both sides); whether they were true, Contested (only Athanasius's answer and the emperor's assertion are held). The two lesser charges (the church, the order) are left out for room, covered by "among other things".
3. **"restore him only if found fit": the examination.** Already explained in the brief: George was to judge his appointment and give him the post again only if he had any right to be a bishop (N204 36157-36162). Documented; hostile.
4. **The broken treaty with Rome.** Already explained: the coast's custom of killing every Roman once they heard the treaty was broken (RUF, beat 1 as cited above). Rufinus gives no more of what the treaty was; nothing held adds to it. Documented as Rufinus tells it.
5. **The disputed date.** Already explained: Rufinus's setting just after 328 against Robertson's "no earlier than late 346" (beat 5 sources above). Contested.
6. **Frumentius's request at Alexandria ("the Lord's work must not be hidden") and Athanasius's choice.** Already explained, with Rufinus's reason and Athanasius's words (RUF 27369-27379). Documented as Rufinus tells it.
7. **The book of saints and the name Abba Salama.** Already explained (SYN as above). Late legend.
8. **Philostorgius's Arian envoy Theophilus at Aksum, Gaps-adjacent.** Leave it out: the brief does not name it, it never names Frumentius, and it survives only in a ninth-century summary (PHI 160); to the card, as the Leave out list already says.
