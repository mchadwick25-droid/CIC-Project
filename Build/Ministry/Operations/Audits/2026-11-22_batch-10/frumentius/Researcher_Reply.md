# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Essence:** Frumentius, from a Tyrian family, was taken captive when his ship put in at a foreign harbour and everyone else aboard was killed. He rose at the king's court in what Rufinus calls India and gathered the Roman Christian traders into places of prayer. He went to Alexandria to ask Athanasius for a bishop and came back as that bishop. The emperor's hostile letter, which Athanasius kept, names him bishop of Auxumis (Aksum).

**Also told:** Rufinus had the story from Edesius. Constantius demanded Frumentius be sent to Egypt to be judged. The consecration date is disputed (Rufinus: just after 328; Robertson: after 346). Ethiopia remembers him as Abba Salama.

**Shape:** Missionary or translator. **Length:** about 3:30, about 525 words: quotes about 80, glosses about 55, credited witnesses about 50; the hostile letter must be heard beside Rufinus.

**Connection:** Aksum's church began as a daughter of Alexandria's. That is why its bishop got caught in Athanasius's quarrel with the emperor.

**Quotes:**
1. Athanasius, as Rufinus reports him (from the Latin): "What other man like this could we find, with the Spirit of God in him as in you, who could carry it through in this way?"
2. Rufinus (from the Latin): "We learned that these things happened so, not from common talk, but from Edesius himself, who told it. He had been Frumentius's companion, and was later made a priest at Tyre."
3. Constantius (from the Greek): "So send Frumentius the bishop to Egypt with all speed." "This Frumentius was set in this rank by Athanasius, a man guilty of countless crimes."

**Gaps:** No source gives his answer to the emperor, his death, or the rulers' reply. The census's "shipwrecked" and "tutor" are not in Rufinus; Budge's errors go to Do not say. Quotes re-read; four faults checked; overlap check clear.
