## 1. Basil of Caesarea (`basil-of-caesarea`)

### The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Basil of Caesarea, a fourth-century teacher of public speaking who became a monk and then bishop of Caesarea, an inland city in Asia Minor.
- **What happened:** he turned to the gospel, wrote rules for monks living together, built a lodging for the poor and the sick, stood up to the emperor's official, and defended the Spirit's place beside the Father and the Son. He died just before a council confessed it.
- **Why it matters:** his rule passed into Latin through Rufinus, Benedict pointed his monks to it, and a council in 787 told the double monasteries to keep it. The Spirit's place he defended was confessed in 381.

### Heart and shape

- **Essence** (from the census entry and what he is known for): he gave up rhetoric for a monk's life, wrote the rules for monks living in community, built the great house for the poor, and as bishop defended the Spirit's equal honour. It is all told by word 143, under the 150-word mark. The hook, his own waking "from deep sleep", leads straight into it.
- **Also told:** the rules (the scene, Quote 2), the house (Letter 94 and Gregory's lepers), the Spirit (the two forms of praise, Quote 3, Gregory's admission), and the cost (the official's threats, the quarrel, the rival bishop). Each is present with its weight.
- **Order:** after the essence the life runs once, in order: rhetoric and the turn, Pontus, priest, bishop, the house, the Spirit, death. Nothing is told twice and nothing jumps back. The waking is not retold in the turn, as the outline asks.
- **Ending:** on the person, at his death ("the whole city poured around Basil").
- **Names heard once that neither act nor link:** none. Valens acts; Macrina and Benedict are links.
- **Where I got lost:** "Basil came back to serve the bishop" (paragraph 5). The story never says he left. The outline's Beat 3 holds that he went back to Pontus rather than split the church (P2); without it, "came back" has nothing to come back from. Also, at Sasima, the listener must work out that "his friend" is the Gregory who "said" it was a hurt. Not lost, but a beat behind.
- **Children's book or talking down:** nowhere.

## 2. Epiphanius of Salamis (`epiphanius-of-salamis`)

### The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Epiphanius, a fourth-century monk from Palestine who became bishop of Salamis in Cyprus and wrote the Panarion, a "medicine chest" against the heresies.
- **What happened:** he named eighty heresies, each with its cure. In old age he fought the followers of Origen, preaching at Jerusalem, using force on Jerome's brother and on a church curtain, banning Origen's books in Cyprus, and confronting John Chrysostom at Constantinople. He died at sea on the way home.
- **Why it matters:** his list became the standard that later writers worked from. Augustine followed its order, and four centuries later both sides of the quarrel over images argued from his book and his curtain letter.

### Heart and shape

- **Essence:** the bishop of Salamis who wrote the Panarion, eighty heresies each with its cure, which later writers took as their list, and who carried the fight against Origen's followers to Jerusalem and Constantinople. It is all told by word 151, at the 150-word mark. The hook is his own preface (Q1), and it leads straight to it. The opening runs five sentences, counting the three-sentence quote, a little past the kit's "about three", but it reads as one moment.
- **Also told:** what the book holds (sources, the copied letter, Apollinaris, the church at the end); why he fought Origen (the charges, the river, Q2); the force (Jerome's brother, the curtain, the deacon at Constantinople, the refusal to pray with John); how others used him (Theophilus; Socrates and Sozomen set against each other and each credited); the Long Brother's question; and the later use (Augustine, 787, the Frankish reply). Each is present with its weight.
- **Order:** birth, bishop, Panarion about 375, Jerusalem in the 390s, the same letter's acts, Theophilus, Constantinople, death at sea, then what came of him. Nothing is told twice and nothing jumps back. The curtain is kept inside "the same letter", with no date, as the Do not say asks.
- **Ending:** on the opening's image seen again, the church after the eighty heresies. This is on him and his book, as the outline recommends.
- **Names heard once that neither act nor link:** Charlemagne, inside the outline's own gloss for the Caroline Books. This is MINOR and does not lose the listener.
- **Where I got lost:** "During a service near his monastery, he says". The nearest "he" is Jerome's brother, so "his monastery" first sounds like the brother's. A beat later it resolves.
- **Children's book or talking down:** nowhere.
