# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Essence:** Priscillian was a rich, learned layman of Spain. He called men and women to give up family, wealth and rank for God, and was made bishop of Abila. Other bishops accused him, and the usurper Maximus's court at Trier had him put to death by the sword in about 385. His followers honored him as a martyr.

**Also told:** his own book to Damasus; Saragossa told two ways (Sulpicius: condemned in his absence; Priscillian: no one was); Damasus and Ambrose turning him away; his appeal to the emperor and Martin's plea, in one clause, without Martin's quote; the sorcery verdict, credited to the court; the martyr's cult, fifteen years of quarreling among the bishops, Leo's praise of the sword, Braga in 561, and his writings found in 1885.

**Shape:** Martyr. **Length:** about 3:45, roughly 560 words (five beats, three quotes, six credited witnesses).

**Connection:** the church split over whether a ruler's sword belonged in a church quarrel. His followers kept him as a martyr, his accusers were cut off from their fellow bishops, and Leo later held up the killing as rightful.

**Quotes:**
1. "No one should be forbidden, if he despises parents, children, wealth and rank, even his own life, and would rather love God than the world."
2. "My own view is that the accused displease me as much as the accusers." (the slate's lifted quote, rendered afresh from the Latin)
3. "Even the rulers of the world loathed this unholy folly. So they cut down its founder, with most of his followers, by the sword of the public laws." (Leo; Latin not held)
