## 6. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Saragossa.** Sulpicius: condemned in his absence (3.2). Priscillian: no one of his group accused, convicted or condemned (3.3). Hydatius: "heard at several councils of bishops" (3.10). Each credited; do not choose.
- **Martin.** Sulpicius: Martin begged that their blood not be shed, holding a bishops' sentence enough (3.16). Hydatius: Martin and other bishops judged him a heretic (3.10). Not contradictory, but neither says Martin defended his teaching.
- **The second deposed accuser.** Sulpicius: Ithacius deposed, Ydacius resigned. Prosper: Ithacius and Ursacius excommunicated in 389 (4.5).
- **Roberts's English vs Halm's Latin.** "Arles" for Abila (3.4); "as long as Martin survived" for "while Martin was at Trier" (3.17); "evil conduct" for *maleficium* (3.18); "between them and our friends" for *inter nostros* (4.4); "serve as a terrible example" for *pessimo exemplo* (3.21). The story follows the Latin.
- **Year.** 385 (editors), 386 (Fremantle's table), a later year in Hydatius (1.4). Tell "about 385".
- **First execution of a Christian on a church charge (the slate's question).** No primary held source says it was the first. Martin called a secular judge in a church case unheard of (barred quote; SulpL 5905-5907) — that is about the court, not about death. B. Jackson's note to Theodoret (JerVir file 16387-16391, editor, 1892) calls it "the first capital punishment of a heretic"; Fremantle's table (JerEp 2970) says "for heresy"; Boyd (6941-6942) calls it "the case frequently cited as typical." Hefele (3.19) reports Bernays's view that he was executed for sorcery and night meetings, "not for heresy." The record holds the claim only as editors' views, and one editor disputes its ground. The story should not say it (see Outline, Leave out).
- **Late and hostile.** Everything about his teaching beyond his own tractates comes from opponents: Sulpicius, Orosius, Jerome's later letter, Leo, Braga. Braga (561) attributes teachings to him 176 years later.
- **Authorship of the tractates.** Schepss's judgment (4.14); no held text tests it further.

## 7. Notes on prints

- 7.1 The Würzburg tractates survive in one manuscript; Schepss's 1889 print is the only held print. The lines quoted are legible in the OCR (letter misreadings marked in square brackets above). Quotes from them need a reading against the page image at second reading.
- 7.2 Halm's Latin is the original for Sulpicius; Roberts (1894) is English printed before 1931 and confirms the sense except where noted in 6.
- 7.3 Leo's Letter 15 is held only in Feltoe's English (1895).
- 7.4 Toledo (400) is held only in Mansi's long-s facsimile OCR; no second print. Tell it in the narrator's words.
