## Findings

This is the first time the brief uses the word itself, since earlier it gives only the gloss "the followers of the Persian teacher Mani". Words to explain lists *Manichaeans*, and the kit bars a listed word heard without its plain sense the first time. Replacement for the Room for it line:
   - Added: "They asked for it, Priscillian wrote, against false bishops and Mani's followers." (12)
   - Cut 9: "put to death by the sword" becomes "put to the sword" in the essence (2).
   - Count after: 674 (checked on a scratch copy by `wc -w`).

2. **MINOR. Item 4, Saragossa: the council's content is held.** `Gaps.md` (batch scan 3) says Saragossa's charge is "Not held". That is only partly true. Sulpicius names no charge (SulpE 11502-11510). But Priscillian's own account, in the outline's cited range, says Hydatius gave the council a memorandum that "as it were set down a rule for how to live", and that "none of us was censured there under it" (Pr 4389-4391: "Datum nescio quod ab Hydatio ibi commonitorium est quod uelut agendae uitae poneret disciplinam: nemo illic nostrum inter illa repraehensus"). Hefele also gives eight canons of a Saragossa synod, on women's meetings, Sunday fasting, absence from church in Lent and so on. He assigns them to this synod only as a supposition (Hef2 17955-17958, 18003-18020). The brief's line still holds without these, so no brief change is needed. Replacement for item 4:
   - "4. **Saragossa's condemnation.** Explain. Sulpicius says it condemned them in absence as heretics, naming no charge; Priscillian says no charge was laid against his group's name, aim or life, and that the rules for living the council issued censured none of them. Witness: Sulpicius (SulpE 11502-11508); Priscillian (Pr 4382-4391). Contested. Card: Hydatius's memorandum (Pr 4389-4391) and the eight canons Hefele assigns to this synod by supposition (Hef2 17955-17958, 18003-18020, Dominant Modern Reconstruction at most)."

## Checked and sound

- "Their teaching, he wrote, put each life under the power of demons and stars." Leo says "rest their religious faith and their moral conduct upon the power of demons and the influences of the stars", and "all that men do is through the agency ... of stars" (Leo 3259-3267). The brief's plain sense is fair, and it is credited to Leo.
- "attacked for bringing the case before the courts". Sulpicius says Ithacius defended the prosecution at first "by his right of appeal to the tribunals" and was then harassed with invectives (SulpE 11705-11719, with Roberts's note 396; SulpL 5966-5968). The brief's line holds, and he was the only one "thrust out of the episcopate".
- Braga's ", among them that souls sinned in heaven and were cast down into bodies" matches canon VI, "sicut Priscillianus dixit" (Bruns 2513-2515), and the brief keeps "teachings it called his".
- "to clear his name" is held by Pr 4675-4676 ("ne taciturnitas metus conscientiae iudicaretur") and SulpE 11561-11563.
- Item 11 leaves out Orosius and the Marcus genealogy. That is sound: the genealogy names no teaching, and Braga carries the soul teaching.
