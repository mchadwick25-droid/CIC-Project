## The Saragossa clause

Lines opened: Pr 4382-4391 (Schepss); Hef2 17945-18020 (Hefele, 1876).

- Pr 4389-4391: Hydatius gave the council "some memorandum" that "as it were set down a rule for how to live", and none of Priscillian's group was censured under it. This says rules for living were issued. It does not say what they forbade.
- Hef2 17955-18020: Hefele gives eight canons of a Saragossa synod. He assigns them to this synod only as a supposition: they "are plainly directed against the Priscillianists, it may well be supposed that they belong to the same Synod". Canon 1: "All Christian women shall avoid conventicles." Canon 2: "No one shall fast on Sunday, nor may any one absent himself from church during Lent and hold a conventicle of his own."

So what the rules forbade comes only through Hefele, and only as his supposition. One plain clause can say it, credited to "a modern historian", with "thinks" carrying his supposition. I chose canons 1 and 2 because the story already meets both concerns: many women coming to him, the court's "night meetings of women", and Ithacius's charge against anyone who loved fasting. I did not join the canons to Hydatius's memorandum, because no held line makes that link.

## First-time listener read

- Charge: heresy, which his enemies called the Gnostics' heresy of secret knowledge.
- Book: a defence, written to clear his name. It gives his call, his creed and his curse on Mani's followers.
- Saragossa: a historian thinks its rules banned Sunday fasting and women's private meetings. Sulpicius says it condemned him as a heretic, and Priscillian denies any charge against his group.
- Emperor's order: against false bishops and Mani's followers, Priscillian says.
- Trial: sorcery, with the court's findings.
- Quarrel: the bishops divided over the deaths, and Ithacius was attacked for taking the case to the courts.
- Leo: lives put under demons and stars.
- Braga: souls that sinned in heaven.

Each one can be said back.
