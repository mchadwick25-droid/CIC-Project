## The four lines

- **Who:** Priscillian, a rich, learned layman of Spain who became bishop of Abila.
- **What happened:** he called men and women to a strict life given wholly to God; bishops accused him of heresy, Damasus and Ambrose turned him away, and at Trier the usurper Maximus's prefect found him guilty of sorcery and he was put to the sword in about 385 with several companions.
- **What it was about:** whether anyone may leave family, wealth and rank for God; his enemies called it the Gnostic heresy and later Manichaean, astrological and soul-fall teaching; he cursed Mani and claimed a hearing before judgment.
- **Why the connection matters:** his death split the church: his followers kept him as a martyr, his accuser lost his see and the bishops quarreled, while Leo sixty years on praised the sword; his own voice came back only in 1885.

## Charges, teachings, books, quarrels, decisions

Each can be explained after one hearing: the call (his own words); the Gnostic label (glossed); the boyhood-magic rumour (credited, "believed"); the book to Damasus (to clear his name; call, creed, curse on Mani); Saragossa (condemned as a heretic in absence, Sulpicius; no charge laid, Priscillian; and, credited to a modern historian, the bans on Sunday fasting and on women's private meetings); the emperor's order (against all heretics; asked for, he says, against false bishops and Mani's followers); the appeal at Bordeaux; Ithacius's suspicion of readers and fasters; Martin's plea; the sorcery verdict and the court's findings; the martyr cult and Toledo; the bishops' quarrel and Ithacius's fall (attacked for taking it to the courts); Leo's letter (demons and stars); Braga (souls that sinned in heaven); the 1885 treatises. None blocks.

Essence: a man who called people to leave everything for God, was made bishop, was accused by bishops and executed by a usurper's court, and was kept as a martyr.
