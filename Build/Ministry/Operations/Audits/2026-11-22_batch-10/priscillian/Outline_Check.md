# Priscillian — Outline Check

## My own account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** Priscillian, a rich and learned Spanish layman, drew men and women into a strict ascetic life of fasting, reading and giving up the world. Spain's bishops condemned the movement, and after his appeals to Damasus and Ambrose failed, he was tried at the usurper Maximus's court at Trier, convicted of sorcery (*maleficium*) and beheaded about 385, with companions. He is on the tree because his followers honored him as a martyr, and because his death, a bishop-prosecuted case ending in a secular death sentence, divided the Western bishops and was later cited by Leo as a precedent.

**Also told.** What he taught, in his own surviving tracts: no one who gives up family, wealth and rank for God should be stopped by the churches; a creed that curses Arians, Manichaeans and other heresies. Made bishop of Abila by Instantius and Salvianus. The Saragossa dispute (condemned in absence, says Sulpicius; no one accused, says Priscillian). Martin pleaded at Trier for no bloodshed. The witness: Sulpicius, who disliked accusers and accused alike. What came of it: the martyr cult and burial in Spain (Toledo 400 still hearing clergy call him a holy martyr); the accusers deposed or cut off and fifteen years of bishops' discord; the church's continued condemnation (law of 407, Leo 447, Braga 561); his own voice recovered in 1885. Not said: "the first Christian executed for heresy" (editors only; batch ruling).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **What came 1, "What he defended": "trial by bishops, not by the world" is misleading.** In his book to Damasus, Priscillian asked for a hearing before bishops "nee refugientes tamen iudicium publicum, si ipse malluisset": he did not refuse a public court if his accuser wanted one (Pr 4668-4669). Sulpicius says he later appealed from the bishops to the emperor (SulpL 5857). The phrase makes him a defender of church courts against the state, and his own act at Bordeaux contradicts it.
   Replace the line with: *What he defended, marked:* the right of anyone to leave family, wealth and rank for God (Pr 4415-4417); a hearing before judgment, with no sentence on the absent and unheard (Pr 4390-4392); in a cause of faith, the bishops' judgment sought first (Pr 4672-4674), though he did not refuse a public court (Pr 4668-4669).

2. **Beat 5, "a noblewoman, Euchrotia": unsupported.** No held text calls her noble. Sulpicius says only that the party stayed for a while on her land in Aquitaine (SulpL 5785; SulpE 11554). Prosper calls her the wife of the rhetorician Delphidius (Pros 53290, "cum Euchrotia Delfidi rhetoris coniuge").
   Replace with: "among them Euchrotia, a woman of Aquitaine who had taken the party in on her land (SulpL 5785; SulpE 11554)". Add to Do not say: "a noblewoman" or "a rich widow". Say instead: a woman of Aquitaine, on whose land they had stayed.

4. **"Gnostics" is not glossed.** Beat 1 and Do not say use "the heresy of the Gnostics" in Sulpicius's words, and a listener will not know the term.
   Add to Words to explain: "*Gnostics*: Sulpicius's name for the heresy, taken from older teachers who claimed a secret knowledge (Jerome names Basilides and Mark, JerVir 41630-41631). Gloss once: 'a heresy of secret knowledge'." This adds about 8 words, for 674. That is still under 675. If more room is needed, move the "communion" gloss to Cut first alongside item 2.
5. **Words to explain, maleficium.** "Harmful magic" is not Hefele's phrase. He writes "sorcery, magic, and the like" (Hef2 23504). Change the gloss to: say "sorcery"; if a gloss is needed, "sorcery and magic" (Hef2 23504, Hefele reporting Bernays).
6. **Words to explain, communion.** Prosper's phrase is "ecclesiae communione", the church's communion, not only a fellowship of bishops. Change the gloss to: "the church's fellowship at the Eucharist; to be cut off from it is to be put out of the church's fellowship."
7. **Beat 4 leaves out the reason for the appeal.** The Kit asks each beat to give the reason for the act, and the only stated reason is a hostile one: "ne ab episcopis audiretur", to avoid being heard by the bishops (SulpL 5857). Add to beat 4: "Sulpicius says he did it to avoid being judged by the bishops (SulpL 5857; credited, hostile)." Add to Do not say: "He appealed to escape the bishops" as narrator's fact. Say: Sulpicius says so.
8. **Connection, citations.** "Martin and Sulpicius held that a bishops' sentence should have been enough" cites Pros 53318-53319, which says nothing of the kind. Sulpicius's own view is at SulpE 11625-11633. Also, Cut first item 2 removes Prosper, and then the Connection's "cut off from their fellows" loses its support. Replace with: "His chief accuser, Ithacius, lost his bishopric (SulpE 11718-11719); Martin held that a bishops' sentence was enough (SulpL 5901-5905), and Sulpicius that the bishops should never have handed the case to the emperor (SulpE 11625-11633)."
9. **When Sulpicius wrote.** "About fifteen years after the deaths" rests on his "iam per quindecim annos" (SulpL 6004-6005), which dates the quarrel, not the writing. Mark it in beat 1 and Who tells it as "Inferential-Thin (from his 'fifteen years')", or say "some years after the deaths".
10. **What came 2, "among the bishops".** The Latin "inter nostros" means among his own side, and the next sentence names "discordiis episcoporum" above all (SulpL 6004-6008). Say "among his own side, the bishops above all" in What came 2 and in the Do not say line on the quarrel.
11. **What came 1, Toledo.** "a holy martyr" is set in quotation marks, but sheet 7.4 says Toledo is told in the narrator's words (one long-s print). Remove the quotation marks: Spanish bishops heard of clergy who had called him a holy martyr.
12. **Library sweep, Prosper's chronicle entry 1187 is missing** (Pros 53284-53290). It is a second witness to the Bordeaux appeal, the hearing by the prefect Evodius at Trier, and the sword for Priscillian with Euchrotia and Latronianus. Add it to Source Sheet §3, credited "later +60", beside 3.12 and 3.18-3.20. In Who tells it, change "Almost the whole life rests on him" to "Almost the whole life rests on him; a later chronicler, Prosper, briefly confirms the appeal and the trial." This needs no new beat.
