## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Priscillian, a rich, learned layman of Spain who became bishop of Abila and led a movement calling men and women to a strict life given wholly to God.
- **What happened:** Other bishops accused him of heresy. He was condemned at Saragossa (Sulpicius) or not (his party's book), turned away at Rome and Milan, appealed to the usurping emperor Maximus, and was convicted of sorcery by a prefect at Trier and put to the sword with companions, in 385 or 387.
- **What it was about:** whether anyone may leave family, wealth and rank for God, and whether his teaching was heresy and magic, as his enemies said, or the catholic faith, as his party said, cursing Mani's followers.
- **Why the connection matters:** his death split the church. His followers kept him as a martyr. The bishops quarreled and Ithacius lost his see over bringing the case to court, and Martin had said the bishops' sentence was enough. Yet Leo, sixty years on, praised the sword. Braga still cursed his teachings in 561.

**Charges, teachings, books, quarrels, decisions named, and whether one hearing explains each:**
- The heresy charge, "the heresy of the Gnostics, a heresy of secret knowledge": explained as far as Sulpicius goes. He gives no teaching, only that it hid under secret rites (SulpE 11455-11456).
- Magic from boyhood: clear, credited ("was believed").
- The book to Damasus: clear. It states the call, a creed, and a curse on every heresy, Mani's followers above all.
- Saragossa's condemnation: clear. The two accounts are set side by side.
- The emperor's order against heretics: clear. It drove them from the cities and beyond his lands, and was asked for against false bishops and Mani's followers.
- The appeal at Bordeaux: clear. It was from the bishops to the emperor, to escape their judgment (Sulpicius).
- Martin's plea: clear.
- Sorcery and the court's findings: clear, and credited to the court.
- The bishops' quarrel and Ithacius's fall: clear enough ("attacked for bringing the case before the courts").
- Leo's charge: clear (demons and stars).
- Braga's curses: clear (souls sinned in heaven).
- The 1885 find: clear as an event. The tie the ending names to make the book his party's is **not** clear on one hearing. See finding B1.

**Essence and what is also told.** Essence: a lay ascetic leader of Spain, made bishop, accused by bishops and executed under a usurper's court, honored by his followers as a martyr. His party's book gives the call in their own words. Also told: the book's teaching, the bishops' campaign (Saragossa, the order, Rome and Milan), the trial, and what came of it. The hook is the book's call, told in three sentences. The essence is complete at word 109, inside 150. Each part of what is also told is present with its weight. After the essence the life runs once, in order: Sulpicius and the call, Saragossa, the order, Rome and Milan, Trier, Bordeaux, Martin, the trial, the funerals, the quarrel, Leo, Braga, the find. Nothing is told twice and nothing jumps back. The essence's "honored him as a martyr" reaches ahead, which the kit allows. The ending is on his party heard again. It closes on the opening's image.

**Names heard:** Priscillian, Damasus, Sulpicius Severus, Mani, Ambrose, Maximus, Ithacius, Martin, Leo. Each acts or links. Nine names is past the warning of six, but none is idle.

**Sentences resting on a modern source, or on a claim rated Dominant Modern Reconstruction or Inferential-Thin:** none remain. Each point below was checked against the old texts:
- **"in 385 by one old chronicle and 387 by another."** Prosper enters the death under the consuls Arcadius and Bauto (Pros 53277, 53287-53290). Hydatius enters it in Theodosius's ninth year, "VIIII" (Hyd 1143-1145). The AD years are a plain reckoning of each chronicle's own dating, not anyone's judgment.
- **"In 400."** The Toledo professions are headed "Era 438" (Mansi3 96554), and Herenas's clergy called him "fanctumque martyrem" (Mansi3 96780-96781).
- **"In 561."** Braga is headed "HABITUM ERA DXCIX" (Bruns 2369), and canon VI is at Bruns 2513-2515.
- **"sixty years on."** The deaths fall in 385 or 387. Hydatius records Leo's writings sent to Spain in 447 (Hyd 1916-1918).
- **"bishop of Abila."** SulpL 5739-5740, "in Abilensi oppido"; Hyd 1133, "Avila episcopus".
- **"Nearly all we know of him comes from Sulpicius Severus."** This is the kit's one-witness sentence, from Who tells it, and is placed inside the sentence about the witness. It is not a modern opinion of the person.
- **"a heresy of secret knowledge."** This glosses Sulpicius's label. "Secret" stands on "concealed itself under mystic rites" ("arcanis occultata secretis", SulpE 11455-11456).
- **"In 1885, a scholar opened an old manuscript at Würzburg and found eleven unsigned treatises."** A modern event, told as an event (Pr 85-90). No judgment is attached since the modern pass.
