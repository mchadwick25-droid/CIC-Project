# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Home tradition: the claim holds, so his home goes back to Mark.** Timothy Aelurus, bishop of Alexandria, cursed Nestorius and Eutyches aloud in church about 475 (zachariah-rhetor_chronicle_hamiltonbrooks1899.txt 5638-5643). Peter Mongus and the Life of Severus say the same. But at Chalcedon in 451, Egypt's bishops would not name him (Evagrius, Walford's English, 5063-5093).

**Essence:** An aged abbot in Constantinople fought Nestorius by teaching one nature after God became man. He was condemned in 448, restored in 449 and condemned again in 451, and left a name both sides of the split cursed.

**Also told:** what he taught; his appeal beside his judges' record; the shouting council of 449; Egypt's bishops at Chalcedon; "Eutychians" as one side's label for the other.

**Shape:** Teacher or thinker. **Length:** 3 min 50 s, about 575 words: quotes 47, glosses about 70, credited witnesses about 60.

**Connection:** the churches that refused Chalcedon were not his followers. They sided with him against Nestorius but cursed him over Christ's body.

**Quotes:**
- "I confess that our Lord was from two natures before the union. But after the union I confess one nature."
- "He had no regard for my grey hairs. I have grown old with them in wars against heretics, and in confessing the true faith."
- "This he certainly never did."

**Gaps:** No held source gives his death or his fate after 451. The lifted Cyril quote is unused, because neither held appeal of his calls on it. Quotes re-read; four faults checked, three fixed.
