# Eutyches: outline check

## My account of the heart, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (card), the slate's job line, the batch's ruling on his home, and the source sheet.

**Essence.** Eutyches, an old priest and head of a monastery in Constantinople, taught against Nestorius that Christ was "of two natures before the union" but of one nature after it, and he called on Cyril and on Nicaea as his warrant. His case set the frame of two councils: Flavian's synod condemned him in 448, his appeal brought Leo's Tome and the council of 449 at Ephesus under Dioscorus, which restored him (and which Leo called a robbery), and Chalcedon condemned him again in 451. He is on the tree because the fight over him shaped the definition of 451, and his name became a label of heresy that both sides of the split cursed.

**Also told.**
- What he taught, in his own words through the minutes (two natures before the union, one after; "spare me" speaking of two natures; his line on Christ's body), and what he condemned (Apollinaris, Nestorius, the flesh "from heaven").
- His side and his judges' side, each credited: his appeal to Leo (Letter 21, "seventy years ... in continence"), his petition to the emperors and to the council (the minutes falsified, his grey hairs), against Flavian's minutes and letters ("this he certainly never did") and Leo's Tome ("very unwary and exceedingly ignorant"). Both disputes (the appeal; the minutes) are Contested.
- Ephesus 449: Basil of Seleucia's denial of a sentence recorded as his; the restoration; Flavian, Eusebius and (sibling clause) Theodoret deposed.
- Chalcedon 451: Leo's letter received against him; "Eutychians"; Egypt's bishops would not name him.
- What came of it: his name became the other side's label, and the churches that refused Chalcedon cursed him too (Timothy Aelurus, c. 475; Peter Mongus; Severus; Philoxenus).
- Setting by the ruling: Constantinople, the Greek East, told within no tradition (the river). No birth, death or later life held.

Confidence: the events Documented as recorded; the appeal and the minutes Contested.

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart matches mine in substance: the teaching in his own words, the three verdicts, his side and his judges' side each credited, and the name both sides cursed. The setting follows Mark's ruling (the river, the Greek East). Every quote is verbatim at its lines. ## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. Ending 1 says the refusing churches cursed his sentence. They did not.
Ending 1: "What was left was his sentence at the trial, one nature after the union, the words both churches learned to curse." This is unsupported and misleading. The council of 449 assented to that very sentence (Perry 23846-23848: "We all assent to this,—yes, all of us"), and it shouted down two natures after the union (Perry 23931-23934). The refusing churches cursed his name and his account of Christ's body (Zach 4095-4097), not "one nature after the union". The outline's own Connection field says so.
**Correction.** Replace Ending 1's second sentence with: "What was left was his name. Chalcedon's side used it for the churches that refused the council, and those churches cursed it too, while still holding the one nature after the union that the council of 449 had cheered (Perry 23846-23848; Councils 20172-20174; Zach 5638-5643)." Add to **Do not say**: "Both sides cursed his 'one nature' sentence" → the churches that refused Chalcedon cursed his name and his account of Christ's body; the council of 449 assented to his sentence (Perry 23846-23848).

### BLOCKING 2. Beat 1 misreads his own words.
Beat 1: "who says he kept the faith from childhood to old age" (Perry 23346-23347). The line says: "My main end and object, from my childhood up to a ripe old age, has been to lead a life of quiet, apart from any business". That is about a quiet life, not about keeping the faith.
**Correction.** "An abbot of many years in Constantinople, who says that from childhood to old age he had wanted only a quiet life, apart from business (Perry 23346-23349)."

### BLOCKING 3. The Library sweep misses a held contemporary witness.
`nestorius_bazaar-of-heracleides_driver-hodgson1925.txt` (Driver and Hodgson's English) is in the `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity` bucket that the sheet says it checked. It names Eutyches 64 times and tells the trial of 448 and the council of 449 from Nestorius's side (for example 18570-18590 and 18650-18790, where Eutyches's dispute with Eusebius is quoted). The sheet neither uses it nor sets it aside. It is a contemporary account by the man he fought, and a third voice beside "his side and his judges'". Theodoret's letters in `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` (30 hits; contemporary, deposed at the same council) are also not named.
**Correction.** In `Source_Sheet.md`, Library sweep: read the Bazaar's passages on Eutyches. Then either list them under Used (with what they hold, witness kind contemp, hostile) or under Set aside with the reason. Do the same for Theodoret's letters (sibling: the `theodoret-of-cyrrhus` story may carry them). Where the Bazaar bears on the Contested items (the appeal, the minutes) or on Who tells it, add one line to the outline. Otherwise the outline stands.

### MINOR 4. A wrong cite for Timothy's recall.
What came 1 cites "Zach 5632" for Basiliscus recalling Timothy. Line 5632 is about Timothy's gifts. The order to return is at Zach 5248 ("So Basiliscus issued an order that Timothy should return").
**Correction.** "(Zach 5248; the chronicle gives no year, *Widely Accepted*)".

### MINOR 5. Once the cuts are made, What came 2 repeats beat 5, and the Tome is never named.
Cut first item 5 drops the Tome from What came 2. That leaves only "Chalcedon's definition received it against his teaching", which beat 5 already tells ("It received Leo's letter for the removal of Eutyches's teaching"). The event is then told twice, and the slate's line "the Tome answered him" is lost by name.
**Correction.** In beat 3, credit Leo's verdict to the Tome: "Leo, answering in his long letter to Flavian, called the Tome, called him 'very unwary and exceedingly ignorant' (narrator's words, credited to Leo; Leo 5125-5128; Zach 1250-1254)." Replace Cut first items 5 and 6 with one item: "5. What came 2 entire (the Tome is named in beat 3; Chalcedon's receipt is in beat 5). To Leave out. Saves 11." Keep the Tome gloss. The count after stays under 675: 735 − 91 + 8 = 652.

### MINOR 6. Eusebius's "once exposed Nestorius" can be misread.
He exposed Nestorius "while still practising as a rhetorician" (Evag-E 1724-1726), not as a bishop.
**Correction.** Add to **Do not say**: "Eusebius, as bishop, exposed Nestorius" → he exposed Nestorius earlier, while still a lawyer (Evag-E 1724-1726).

### MINOR 7. His "recantation" at Ephesus.
Beat 4 is right, but a writer could take the chronicle's word as fact.
**Correction.** Add to **Do not say**: "He recanted at Ephesus" → the minutes of 449 record that he declared himself orthodox and was restored (Perry 24016-24020); the Syriac chronicle calls his petition a recantation (Zach 1308-1310).

### MINOR 8. Flavian's death.
Leave out drops it, but a writer could add it for colour.
**Correction.** Add to **Do not say**: "Flavian was killed at the council" or "Dioscorus trampled him" → leave out; it is a later charge reported by Evagrius (Evag-E 2822-2824), and the Chalcedon and Dioscorus stories carry it.
