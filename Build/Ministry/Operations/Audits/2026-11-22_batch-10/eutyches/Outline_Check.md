# Eutyches: outline check

## My account of the heart, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (card), the slate's job line, the batch's ruling on his home, and the source sheet.

**Essence.** Eutyches, an old priest and head of a monastery in Constantinople, taught against Nestorius that Christ was "of two natures before the union" but of one nature after it, and he called on Cyril and on Nicaea as his warrant. His case set the frame of two councils: Flavian's synod condemned him in 448, his appeal brought Leo's Tome and the council of 449 at Ephesus under Dioscorus, which restored him (and which Leo called a robbery), and Chalcedon condemned him again in 451. He is on the tree because the fight over him shaped the definition of 451, and his name became a label of heresy that both sides of the split cursed.

**Also told.**
- What he taught, in his own words through the minutes (two natures before the union, one after; "spare me" speaking of two natures; his line on Christ's body), and what he condemned (Apollinaris, Nestorius, the flesh "from heaven").
- His side and his judges' side, each credited: his appeal to Leo (Letter 21, "seventy years ... in continence"), his petition to the emperors and to the council (the minutes falsified, his grey hairs), against Flavian's minutes and letters ("this he certainly never did") and Leo's Tome ("very unwary and exceedingly ignorant"). Both disputes (the appeal; the minutes) are Contested.
- Ephesus 449: Basil of Seleucia's denial of a sentence recorded as his; the restoration; Flavian, Eusebius and (sibling clause) Theodoret deposed.
- Chalcedon 451: Leo's letter received against him; "Eutychians"; Egypt's bishops would not name him.
- What came of it: his name became the other side's label, and the churches that refused Chalcedon cursed him too (Timothy Aelurus, c. 475; Peter Mongus; Severus; Philoxenus).
- Setting by the ruling: Constantinople, the Greek East, told within no tradition (the river). No birth, death or later life held.

Confidence: the events Documented as recorded; the appeal and the minutes Contested.
