# Eutyches: source sheet

Census id `eutyches`. Census home `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity`, spoken name "The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt" (short "Church of Alexandria"; `cic-website/data/world-census.json`, movement `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity`, fields `name`, `shortName`).

All files are in `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are the file's own. Witness kinds: **own** (his own words), **contemp** (contemporary), **later +N** (later, rough gap in years), **legend** (late legend). Confidence: Documented / Widely Accepted / Dominant Modern Reconstruction / Contested / Inferential-Thin.

- **Evag-E** = `evagrius-scholasticus_ecclesiastical-history_walford1846.txt` (Walford's English, 1846); **Evag-G** = `evagrius-scholasticus_historia-ecclesiastica-grc_bidez-parmentier1898.txt` (Greek). Evagrius wrote c. 593, on Chalcedon's side, from the council acts.

## Home tradition

**Ruling tested:** the two editors' claim that the churches that rejected Chalcedon cursed Eutyches by name.

The editors' claims, as held:
- Payne Smith, preface to John of Ephesus (1860), `john-ephesus_ecclesiastical-history-part3_paynesmith1860.txt` 225-228: "though his party are not to be confounded with the fol / lowers of Eutyches, whom they anathematized by name, / yet they refused to receive the council of Chalcedon, at / which he was condemned". Editor.
- J. A. Douglas, "Relations with Mar Ignatios", *The Christian East* 3.1 (March 1922), `christian-east_v3-no1-march-1922.txt` 3909-3912 (article from 3658 per the file's content note): "The balance of probability is that the Jacobite and its sister / Churches ... have never been so to the core. They / -anathematized Eutyches, and their rejection of Chalcedon cannot be / taken as proof of their heresy." Editor.

**Result: the claim holds in held primary texts, for the church of Alexandria itself and for the Syriac side, from about 475 on.** It does not hold for 451: at Chalcedon Egypt's bishops would not name him.

1. **Timothy Aelurus at Alexandria, c. 475-477.** Zach 5638-5643 (Book V ch. 4, Timothy's return under Basiliscus; "he" is Timothy, see 5555-5560, 5610-5612): "But the people heard that the prefect there, Boetius by / name, was an Eutychian; and they cried out in the church, / * Pope! * pronounce an anathema upon Nestorius and Eutyches." / And he at once anathematised them: by word of mouth in the / presence of the prefect. And thereby he was cleared from the / suspicion of associating with the prefect as an Eutychian." Later +c. 15-20 (Zacharias Rhetor's history) / +c. 90 (the compilation). Sympathetic to Timothy. **Documented** (one sympathetic witness, told as a public act).
2. **Timothy wrote against Eutyches's followers.** Zach 4093-4097: "But Timothy, when in banishment, wrote not alone against / the Nestorians, but also against the Eutychianists. And this / appears from his letters to Alexandria and Palestine, against / those who hold the opinions of Eutyches". The letter Zach then gives (4120-4212) does not name Eutyches. Documented for the letters' target; the chronicle's own reading. (Timothy's own Armenian work, `timothy-aelurus_widerlegung-der-lehre-von-chalcedon-xcl_ter-mekerttschian-ter-minassiantz1908.txt`, names Eutyches at 15916 and 16281, but the OCR is uneven and no translation is held; not used.)
3. **Peter Mongus of Alexandria accepts Zeno's Henotikon, c. 482.** Zach 5987-5991 (narrator): "and it also agreed / to the twelve Heads of Cyril; and it anathematised Nestorius / and Eutyches". Peter's own public reading, Zach 6006-6008: "It is / well and faithfully written, inasmuch as it accepts the twelve / Heads of Cyril, and it anathematises Nestorius and Eutyches". Peter's address, Zach 6047-6049: "accepting the twelve Heads of the blessed / Cyril, and anathematising Nestorius and Eutyches". The Henotikon's text, Zach 6161-6163: "This Nestorius, together with Eutyches, inasmuch as they / held doctrines contrary to what have been here declared, / we anathematise." Documented (the document is the emperor's; the acceptance is Alexandria's patriarch's).
4. **The Syriac side.** Zacharias Scholasticus, Life of Severus (written in Severus's lifetime; French of Kugener 1903), `zacharias-scholasticus_vie-de-severe-po2-syr-fra_kugener1903.txt` 7363-7366: the eastern monks Flavian of Antioch persecuted "anath@matisaient egalement les heresies de Nestorios, d'Eutyches, eelle d'Apol- / Iinaire" (OCR as printed); 6855-6858: Severus "composa contre les heresies / d'Eutyches un discours ... il attaquait Eutyches, / Apollinaire et Nestorios." Severus himself, `severus-of-antioch_select-letters-book6-part1_brooks1903.txt` 711-722: "unnecessarily extended the / heresy of Eutyches ... those who were / infected with the belief in a phantasy". Philoxenus of Mabbug's list of heresies (Budge's English, `philoxenus-of-mabbug_discourses_budge1894.txt` 1569-1573): "Mani and Marcion, and Eutyches deny the / Incarnation of the Word God of the holy Virgin Mary ... and they / say that the Word passed through the Virgin as through / a tube taking nothing at all from her." All contemp or later +c. 40-70. Documented.
5. **Much later, Armenia.** Ormanian (1912), a former Armenian patriarch, `ormanian_church-of-armenia_gregory1912.txt` 5307-5310: "The name of the latter is officially / and solemnly anathematised by the Church, under / the same head as those of Arius, of Macedon, and / of Nestorius." The church's own later voice. Documented as its claim.
6. **But not at Chalcedon, 451.** Evag-E 5063-5085: Egypt's bishops' petition anathematized "every heresy, both those of Arius, of / Kunomius, of Manes, of Nestorius, and that of those / who say, that the flesh of our Lord was derived from / heaven"; the synod cried "Why have they not anathematised the / doctrine of Eutyches? Let them subscribe the epistle / of Leo, anathematising Eutyches and his doctrines." The bishops asked to wait for their new archbishop (5085-5093). Later +c. 140, from the acts. Documented.

**Implication for Mark:** the census tells Eutyches within the church whose own patriarch, Timothy Aelurus, cursed him by name in Alexandria's church about 475. The claim holds, so his home comes back to Mark on the hearts page.

## Setting

- **Constantinople, the emperor's city, under Theodosius II.** Zach 1123-1125: "There was, in the days of king Theodosius, one Eutyches, / a presbyter and archimandrite, a recluse belonging to those / who dwell in Constantinople." Later +c. 50. Documented.
- **Head of a monastery.** Flavian, Leo 4711-4712: "Eutyches, for many years a presbyter and / archimandrite", with Feltoe's gloss 4712-4713 "head of a monastery ... or abbot" (editor's gloss). Perry 23989-23990 (Flavian's sentence: "from the government of a / Monastery"). Contemp. Documented.
- **Old.** His own words, Leo 4609-4611: "who has spent his / seventy years of life in continence and all chastity". Perry 23346-23347: "from my childhood up | to a ripe old age". Own. Documented (as his own claim of his life; no source gives his birth).
- **After Nestorius.** Zach 1128-1130: visitors came "For at that time Nestorius, who was ejected, was / being justly reviled". Leo 4475-4479, Leo to Eutyches, 1 June 448 (4489): "through the activity of some ... the / heresy of Nestorius has been again reviving." Contemp. Documented.
- **Year 448** for the trial: Leo's letter to him is dated June 448 (Leo 4489); Flavian's letters follow. The month (November) is the editors' (Perry 19121, editor's note). Use "448" only.
- **Tradition word.** The story is told within "The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt" (census). For the listener: "the church of Alexandria" and "the churches that refused Chalcedon".

## Events

### 1. Before the trial: Leo praises him (June 448)
- Leo 4480-4485 (Letter 20): "We reply that your / solicitude in this matter has pleased us, since the remarks we have / received are an indication of your mind." Contemp, Leo's own. Documented. (Feltoe's note at 4475-4477, that Eutyches had accused Domnus and Theodoret, is Quesnel's opinion: editor.)
- Zach 1125-1128: "This man was visited by / many (who resorted to him ostensibly on account of his / chastity and piety) ... and / especially by the soldiers of the palace". Later, hostile to his teaching. Documented as the chronicle's account.

### 2. What he taught
- **His line at the trial.** Evag-G 2284-2289: "᾿Ομολογῶ ἐκ δύο φύσεων γεγενῆσθαι τὸν Κύριον / ἡμῶν πρὸ τῆς ἑνώσεως, μετὰ δὲ τὴν ἕνωσιν μίαν φύσιν / ... ὁμολογῶ" (garbled accent line at 2287 between). Confirmed in three held prints: Evag-E 1729-1731 ("I allow that our Lord was produced / from two natures before their union, but I confess only / one nature after their union"); Perry 23843-23845 ("I confess that our LORD was of Two Natures before / the Union, but after the Union I confess Him to be of / One Nature."); Leo's Tome, Leo 5597-5598 ("I confess that our Lord had two natures before the union but after the union / I confess but one"). Own words through the synod's minutes, which he said were falsified (below). Documented as recorded.
- **On Christ's body.** Perry 23821-23824 (Flavian's minutes read at Ephesus): "Because I confess the body of God, I do not confess / that the body of God is the body of man. But it isa / human body, and I affirm that our Lord took body of / the Virgin." Flavian's summary, Leo 4746-4751 (to Leo): "he said that the Virgin who bare him was / indeed of the same substance with us according to the flesh, but the / Lord Himself did not assume from her flesh of / the same substance with us". Contemp, his judges' record. Documented as recorded.
- **His refusal to go further.** Perry 23816-23817: "He said—spare me speaking of Two Natures in our / LORD or investigating into the Nature of my GOD." His own account to Leo, Leo 4560-4561: the fathers "rejected the phrase "two natures," and not daring to / discuss the nature of God the Word". Own and judges' record agree. Documented.
- **His own appeal to Cyril.** Perry 23397-23402 (petition to the council of 449): the Synod "whose President was our Father, of Blessed / memory, the Bishop Cyril ... a copy of which, in / writing, the afore-mentioned and Blessed Father the / Bishop Cyril sent to me, which I hold in my hands." Leo 4635-4638 (confession sent to Leo): "of which Synod the leader and chief was Cyril of blessed memory". Own. Documented. **Neither appeal uses the words "one incarnate nature of the Word"**, so the slate's lifted Cyril quote does not apply; not used.
- **What he anathematized.** Leo 4600-4603: "anathematizing Apollinaris, Valentinus, Manes, and Nestorius, and those / who say that the flesh of our Lord Jesus / Christ, the Saviour, descended from heaven". Same in Perry 23407-23411. Own. Documented.
- **The other side's account of his teaching.** Zach 1167-1172, 1195-1197: "Eutyches, wishing / to affirm the one Nature in Christ, rejected the truth of the / body derived from the Virgin ... this same Eutyches affirmed / an inaccurate dogma, not having been well instructed." ... "the atmosphere assumes bodily form and becomes rain or / snow under the influence of the wind, or as water by reason / of the cold air becomes ice." The words before (1174, "(the Word became flesh)") are supplied by the translators from Michael the Syrian and Barhebraeus (note 5, 1186-1187); the text is corrupt at 1172 (note 4). Later +c. 50, anti-Chalcedonian. Documented as the chronicle's charge; not his own words. Michael the Syrian repeats it (French, `michael-the-syrian_chronique-tome2-livres-viii-xi-fra_chabot1901.txt` 1706-1712; later +c. 700).

### 3. The trial at Constantinople (448)
- **Accused by Eusebius of Dorylaeum.** Evag-E 1721-1726; Leo 4519-4521 (his own: "aroused Eusebius, bishop of the town of Dorylæum, / against me"); Zach 1199-1203. Documented.
- **Slow to come.** Zach 1206-1213: "at one time saying that it / was his fixed determination to remain in perpetual seclusion, / and again, that he was sick, and had a cough, and was old". His own, Leo 4529-4532: "though I was delayed by a serious illness besides / my advanced age, I came to clear myself, knowing well that a faction / had been formed against my safety." Both. Documented.
- **Zach's charge of insincerity.** Zach 1214-1217: "he did not recant his doctrine / with whole-hearted sincerity, but kept on saying, "Just as you / teach two Natures in Christ, so do I say."" Later, the other side. Documented as the chronicle's view.
- **Sentence.** Perry 23978-23992 (Flavian, read at Ephesus): "he was infected with the false. / doctrines of Valentinus and Apollinarius ... that he be / alien from the whole Order of the Priesthood and from / Communion with us and from the government of a / Monastery". Flavian to Leo, Leo 4758-4760: "we deprived him ... of his priesthood, of the management of his monastery / and of our communion". Contemp. Documented.
- **Monks made to sign.** His own, Leo 4577-4582: "Then they began to force / the heads of other monasteries to subscribe to my / degradation (a thing which was never done either towards those who have / professed themselves heretics, nor even against Nestorius himself)". Perry 23525-23527 same. Own. Documented as his complaint.

### 4. The appeal, and the judges' answer (448-449)
- **His appeal to Leo.** Leo 4597-4598: "I take refuge, therefore, with you the defender of / religion". Leo 4604-4611: "I beseech you ... nor let one be / expelled and banished from the number of the orthodox who has spent his / seventy years of life in continence and all chastity, so that at the / very end of life he should suffer shipwreck." Own. The Greek is lost; Feltoe's note, Leo 4502-4505: "though the Gk. original is not found. It is from a collection / of documents" (Latin). English printed 1895. Documented.
- **His claim that he appealed at the trial.** Leo 4532-4535: "together with a / writ of appeal to which my / signature was appended" (with Feltoe's note "this is referred to / by Flavian ... and denied", editor). Perry 23493-23494 (to the council): Flavian "did not make any mention at all of the appeal I had / made".
- **Flavian's denial.** Leo 5024-5030 (Letter 26): "the same Eutyches had sent / you a letter full of falsehood and cunning, saying that at the time of / trial he had presented letters of appeal to us ... and had appealed to your / holiness: this he certainly never did, but in this matter, too, / he has been guilty of deceit". Contemp, his judge. **Contested** (each side's word; no third witness).
- **The minutes falsified, by his account.** Perry 23306-23311 (petition to the emperors): "the Documents that were imposed upon me by the Ven- / erable Bishop Flavian ... you found that the opposite of what was actually done, / was put on paper. For, what he said to me was not put / on paper, and what I did not say, they put into the / Documents." Evag-E 1738-1745: "on his presenting a petition to / Theodosius, on the plea that the acts, as set forth, had / been concocted in the hands of Flavian ... when the truth of the acts had been / confirmed, the second synod at Ephesus is summoned." Contested (his claim; Evagrius says the acts were confirmed).
- **His grey hairs.** Perry 23494-23504: "nor had he any regard for my grey / hairs, with which I have grown old in wars against / Heretics and in the Confession of the Orthodox Faith". Own, through the Syriac version. Documented as his words.
- **Leo's verdict.** Tome, Leo 5125-5128: "Eutyches, who / used to seem worthy of all respect in virtue of his priestly office, is / very unwary and exceedingly ignorant". Leo 5703-5705: "that Truth on / which he is blind may be brought home to / the ignorant old man". Contemp, a judge. Documented as Leo's view.
- **The Tome was written in his case.** Zach 1250-1254: "they also wrote and sent the Acts of the / Council concerning Eutyches to Leo. And the latter wrote to / Flavian the letter called the Tome". Documented.

### 5. Ephesus, 449: restored
- **His petition moved the emperor.** Perry 23291-23293, heading in the Syriac: "The PETITON of the Archimandrite, EUTYCHES, / which was presented to the Christ-loving Emperor / Theodosius, and which moved him to convoke the Holy / Synod of Ephesus." The emperors' own letter blames Flavian, Perry 23250-23252: "since the God-loving Bishop Flavian resolves on / agitating a question touching The Holy Faith, with the / Venerable Archimandrite Eutyches". Documented (heading is the Syriac compiler's).
- **Presided by Dioscorus, by court intrigue (Chalcedonian view).** Evag-E 1752-1755: "Dioscorus ... was appointed president, by an / intrigue, in enmity to Flavian, of Chrysaphius". Later +c. 140. Documented as Evagrius's view.
- **Basil of Seleucia denies his recorded words.** Perry 23860-23877: when "if you do not speak of Two Natures after the / Union, you speak of mixture and of a con-fusing" was read, "Basil stood up and denied that sentence to be his"; Juvenal: "This sentence, therefore, was altered."; Basil: "I do not remember and I do. not know if I did say so." Contemp. Documented.
- **Shouts.** Perry 23913-23915: "Take and burn Eusebius—this man ought to be burnt / alive—this man ought to be divided into two, as he / divided." Perry 23931-23934: "Whoever affirms Two Natures let him be Anathema- / tized". Contemp, the council's own minutes. Documented.
- **Restored.** Perry 24016-24020 (Juvenal): "Inasmuch as he (Eutyches) has frequently made the / declaration that he is Orthodox ... I also decree and vote that he be / restored to his Monastery and to his Grade." Dioscorus, Perry 24070-24075. Zach 1308-1310: "But Eutyches the archimandrite was received, / because he presented a libel of recantation to the Synod ... and confessed the true faith." Evag-E 1778-1781. Documented. (The council's report, Flemming 7896-7906, wept "nicht sowohl über den, der ungerecht verdammt / worden war, als wie über diejenigen, welche verdammt hatten".)
- **Others deposed.** Evag-E 1779-1787: Flavian and Eusebius sentenced; "they depose Theodoret, bishop of / Cyrus, and even Domnus of Antioch". Documented. (Sibling clause.)
- **Force alleged later.** Evag-E 4309-4313 (at Chalcedon): Acacius "added, that they had all subscribed a blank / paper by force and compulsion ... surrounded by soldiers with / deadly weapons." Documented as testimony at Chalcedon, by the winning side.

### 6. Chalcedon, 451: condemned again
- **Leo's letter received against him.** Councils 20265-20268 (the Definition; barred as a quote, tell in narrator's words): Leo's letter to Flavian "for the removal / of the false doctrines of Eutyches". Documented.
- **His minutes read again.** Evag-E 4318-4347. Documented.
- **"Eutychians".** Councils 20172-20174 (bishops' cry, fifth session): "We believe as / Leo. Those who contradict are Eutychians." Documented.
- **Egypt's bishops would not name him.** Evag-E 5063-5093 (above, Home tradition 6). Documented.
- **His later life.** No held primary text gives his exile, death or burial. The card's note on exile rests on Feltoe's summary of an untranslated letter (editor). Honest limit.

## Impact, heaviest first

1. **His name became the other side's label, and both sides cursed it.** Chalcedon's side named the refusers after him: Councils 20173; Evag-E 6794-6797 ("the genuine followers / of Dioscorus and Eutyches were reduced to a very / small number"). The refusers cursed him by name: Timothy at Alexandria (Zach 5638-5643), Peter Mongus (Zach 6006-6008), the eastern monks and Severus (Life of Severus 7363-7366, 6855-6858), Philoxenus (1569-1573). Documented. *What he defended* (the one Christ against Nestorius's split, and Nicaea with nothing added) was kept by the churches that cursed him; what they rejected was his account of the body (Zach 1167-1172).
2. **His case set the frame of 449 and 451.** The Tome was written on his case (Zach 1250-1254); his petition moved the council of 449 (Perry 23291-23293); Chalcedon received Leo's letter against him (Councils 20265-20268). Documented.
3. **Later councils listed him with the great heretics.** Councils 30126-30129 (canon 95 of the council of 692): converts "anathematize each his own heresy, and also Nestorius, Eutyches, / Dioscorus, Severus". Documented (Hefele's doubts on the canon's text, 30147-30166, are an editor's).
4. **Later memory on the refusing side carried the chronicle's charge.** Michael the Syrian (above). Documented.

## Where sources disagree or come late

- **His appeal.** He says he appealed at the trial (Leo 4532-4535; Perry 23493-23494); Flavian says "this he certainly never did" (Leo 5028). Contested.
- **The minutes.** He says they were falsified (Perry 23306-23311); Basil of Seleucia at Ephesus denied a sentence written as his (Perry 23866-23877); Evagrius says the acts were confirmed (Evag-E 1744-1745). Contested.
- **Sincerity at Ephesus.** The council of 449 found him orthodox (Perry 24016-24046); Zach says he presented a recantation and confessed the true faith (1308-1310); Chalcedon's side held his doctrine heretical.
- **A late legend.** The Coptic History of the Patriarchs (`severus-al-muqaffa_history-patriarchs-coptic-church-v1_evetts1904.txt` 34513-34514) has "Eutyches, the monk" at the emperor's side against Nestorius in 431. Late (compiled centuries later); not used.
- **Theopistus.** `theopistus_histoire-de-dioscore-journal-asiatique-1903-fra_nau1903.txt` 222-236 gives the trial; Nau's note 282-287 (editor) explains why the miaphysites approved him in 449 and condemned Eutychians later. Late; set aside.
- **Chrysaphius his godson.** Feltoe's introduction, Leo 588-589 (editor), and Liberatus (rough Latin scan). Not in a held primary in clean print. Leave out.

Used in the outline only as a line under Who tells it, for the card; the story keeps its two sides (his and his judges').
- **Set aside, added after the outline check:** Theodoret's letters and Jackson's introduction, `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` (30 matches). Most are Jackson's editorial notes (for example 2907, 2946-2957, 34215, 34284), an editor's narrative. Theodoret's own words on Eutyches come in the acts of Chalcedon's eighth session as Jackson quotes them from Labbe (3315-3325: "I anathematize Nestorius and Eutyches"), a scene of Theodoret's own case; the `theodoret-of-cyrrhus` story carries it.
- **Set aside:** Mansi tomes 5-7 (the Greek and Latin of the acts, the fullest originals, but the OCR renders long s as f and the Greek only in part; no clean quotable line found for his words); Hefele, *History of the Councils* v3 (an editor's narrative); Liberatus (rough scan, later Latin); Timothy Aelurus's Armenian work (no translation, uneven OCR); the Coptic History of the Patriarchs and Theopistus (late, legend-shaped); Collectio Avellana, Facundus, Theophanes, John of Damascus and the later Latin and Byzantine writers (they repeat the condemnation and add nothing about the man); Boethius, Avitus (works against his teaching, not about him, and they have their own stories).
