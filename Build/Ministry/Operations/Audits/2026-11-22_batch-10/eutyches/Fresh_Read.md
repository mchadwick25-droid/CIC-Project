## The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Eutyches, an old abbot in Constantinople who taught that Christ, from two natures before the union, had one nature after it.
- **What happened:** Flavian's meeting condemned him in 448; the council at Ephesus under Dioscorus restored him in 449; Chalcedon condemned him again in 451. He said he had appealed and the record was false; Flavian said he never appealed.
- **Why it matters:** his name became a curse word on both sides of the split: Chalcedon's side called its opponents "Eutychians", and the churches that refused Chalcedon, though holding one nature against Nestorius, cursed him too (Timothy at Alexandria, about 475).

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING (misleading implication).** Para 7: "A bishop who had judged him said the record gave him words he had never spoken, then said he did not remember." Heard once, both "him"s point to Eutyches, so a judge seems to confirm that the record falsified Eutyches's words. In the source Basil of Seleucia denied a sentence recorded as his own (Perry 23858-23877: "Basil stood up and denied that sentence to be his"). Change to: "A bishop who had judged him denied a sentence the record gave as his own, then said he did not remember."
*Do not say* line to add to the outline: "A judge said the record put false words in Eutyches's mouth" → a bishop who had judged him denied a sentence recorded as his own (Basil's), then said he did not remember (Perry 23858-23877).

**M1. MINOR (order).** Para 5: "He later told the council that he had been handed to a crowd ready to drag him through the city as a heretic." This reaches forward to 449 inside the 448 appeal, then para 6 begins 449. Move it to para 6, after "He told the emperors his trial record was false.", as: "To the council he wrote that he had been handed to a crowd ready to drag him through the city as a heretic, and that Flavian had shown him no pity:" followed by Q2 (and drop the current "Flavian, he wrote to the council, had shown him no pity:").

**M2. MINOR (pronoun).** Para 6: "its Syriac record says Eutyches's petition moved him." The nearest man is Dioscorus; the source's "him" is the emperor (Perry 23291-23293). Change to "its Syriac record says Eutyches's petition moved the emperor to call it."

**M3. MINOR (listener).** Para 6: "He told the emperors" after "The emperor called a council". The plural is the source's (two reigning emperors) but is heard as a slip. Change to "He told the emperor his trial record was false."

**M4. MINOR (outline support).** Para 9: "They kept what he had defended: one Christ against Nestorius, and the creed of Nicaea with nothing added." Eutyches's own "not wishing to take away or to add one word" to Nicaea is held (Leo 4540-4545), and the one Christ against Nestorius is held (Zach 1150-1160), but the outline cites no line showing the refusing churches kept "Nicaea with nothing added" (its cites, Zach 1167-1172 and 4095-4097, do not say it). The researcher should add the supporting line to the outline (the Henotikon's acceptance at Alexandria, Zach 5987-6163, if it holds), or the clause becomes "one Christ against Nestorius".

**M5. MINOR (ambiguity).** Para 9: "They rejected his account of Christ's body, which, their own chronicle says, did not share our nature." "Which" can be heard as the chronicle saying Christ's body did not share our nature. Change to "They rejected his account of Christ's body: in their own chronicle, he would not confess it shared our nature." (Zach 1167-1169, 4095-4097.)
