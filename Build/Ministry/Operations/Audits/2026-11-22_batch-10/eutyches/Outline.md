# Eutyches: outline

Files are in `cic/texts/`; short names (Perry, Leo, Zach, Evag-E, Evag-G, Councils) as in `Source_Sheet.md`.

- **The essence:** Eutyches, an aged abbot in Constantinople, fought Nestorius's divided Christ by teaching that after God became man there was one nature only. Condemned in 448, restored in 449 and condemned again at Chalcedon in 451, he left a name that both sides of the split came to curse. *Documented:* teaching, Evag-G 2284-2289, Perry 23843-23845; the three verdicts, Perry 23978-23992, 24016-24020, Councils 20265-20268; cursed by Chalcedon's side, Councils 20172-20174, and by the church of Alexandria, Zach 5638-5643.
- **Also told:**
  - What he taught and would not say: one nature after the union; Christ's body "the body of God", yet "a human body" taken from the Virgin; he would not speak of two natures nor curse the fathers who, he said, had rejected the phrase. Perry 23816-23824; Leo 4540-4561. *Documented, as his judges recorded it and as he told it.*
  - His appeal beside his judges' record: he says he appealed and that the minutes were falsified; Flavian says he never appealed; Leo judges him "very unwary". Leo 4532-4535, 5024-5030, 5125-5128; Perry 23306-23311. *Contested.*
  - The council of 449 restored him among shouts against his accuser, and deposed Flavian and Theodoret of Cyrrhus. Perry 23860-23934, 24016-24020; Evag-E 1778-1787. *Documented.*
  - At Chalcedon Egypt's bishops would not name him; within about 25 years Alexandria's own bishop cursed him by name, while Chalcedon's side called the churches that refused it "Eutychians". Evag-E 5063-5093; Zach 5638-5643; Councils 20172-20174; Evag-E 6794-6797. *Documented.*
- **Length:** 3 minutes 50 seconds, about 575 words at 150 a minute (675 at most). Must-carry count: three quotes, 47 words; glosses (nature, the union, abbot, synod or council, curse/anathema, minutes, orthodox), about 70; witnesses credited (the trial minutes, Eutyches's own letter, Flavian, Leo, the minutes of 449, the bishops at Chalcedon, the Syriac chronicle), about 60. Beats: hook and essence about 105; beats 1-5 about 290; what came of him about 150; ending about 30.
- **Setting:**
  - 448 to 451, with a look ahead to about 475. Leo 4489 (June 448); Perry 22858-22859 (449); Councils 20265 (451); Zach 5555-5643 (Timothy's return).
  - Constantinople, "the emperor's city", where Eutyches headed a monastery; Ephesus (449); Chalcedon, across the water from Constantinople (451). Zach 1123-1125; Leo 4711-4713.
  - Under the emperor Theodosius II, "the emperor". Perry 23250-23252.
  - Tradition: told within no tradition, on the river, in the Greek East (Mark, 2026-11-23, Decision-Log, "Batch 10 hearts approved, with the lengths and a cut-first line in the kit", item 4). For the listener: "the churches that refused Chalcedon", and "the church of Alexandria".
- **Words to explain:**
  - nature: "what Christ is, as God and as man" (a person is who he is). Work it in at the hook.
  - the union: "the moment God the Word became man in the Virgin's womb". Perry 23843-23845 context.
  - archimandrite: say "abbot", "head of a monastery". Leo 4712-4713.
  - synod / council: "a meeting of bishops".
  - anathema: say "curse" or "condemn by name", with "cut off from the church" where needed.
  - orthodox: say "right-believing" or "the true faith" (avoid the modern church name).
  - minutes / acts: "the written record of the meeting".
  - Tome: "Leo's long letter to Flavian, called the Tome".
- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. Show the idea through his own words at the trial.
- **Sibling:**
  - `theodoret-of-cyrrhus` (batch, researched after this outline): deposed at the same council of 449. Here, one clause only.
  - `leo-of-rome` (live): tells the Tome and Leo's verdict on 449. Here, Leo appears as a judge answering Eutyches's appeal; the Tome's content is not retold.
  - `council-of-chalcedon-451` (live): tells the council and its definition. Here, Chalcedon is one beat about Eutyches's condemnation and Egypt's bishops.
  - `cyril-of-alexandria`, `nestorius`, `dioscorus-of-alexandria`, `timothy-aelurus` (live): linked; none of their quotes is used. Timothy's story tells his writings against Eutyches's followers; this one tells his curse at Alexandria.
- **Openings:**
  1. *The trial line (preferred).* In 448 an old abbot stood before a meeting of bishops in Constantinople. He had said he was ill and old. Pressed on what Christ is, he said: Q1. (Evag-G 2284-2289; Leo 4529-4531; Perry 23843-23845.) Leads into the essence: for that sentence he was deposed, restored and condemned again.
  2. *The doubt as a scene.* At Ephesus in 449 the record of his trial was read aloud. A bishop who had judged him stood up and said the words written as his were not his. Then he said he did not remember. (Perry 23860-23877.)
- **Story beats:**
  1. *Who he was, and the fight with Nestorius.* An abbot of many years in Constantinople, who says that from childhood to old age he had wanted only a quiet life, apart from business (Perry 23346-23349). Nestorius, once the city's bishop, had been deposed at Ephesus for dividing Christ (Zach 1153-1163), and his teaching was said to be reviving. In June 448 Leo of Rome wrote to thank Eutyches for warning him of it. Zach 1123-1130; Perry 23346-23347; Leo 4479-4489. *Documented.* Leo praised his concern, not his teaching.
  2. *The trial, 448.* A bishop who had once exposed Nestorius, Eusebius of Dorylaeum, accused him before Flavian, bishop of Constantinople. Eutyches came late, saying he was ill and old; the Syriac chronicle from the side that refused Chalcedon says he leaned on friends among the palace soldiers. Asked to say two natures and curse those who denied it, he would not. Flavian's meeting stripped him of his priesthood, his monastery and communion, as one who revived old heresies. Evag-E 1723-1726; Leo 4529-4541; Zach 1206-1213; Perry 23978-23992. *Documented.* The heresy charge is the judges'.
  3. *The appeal and the judges' answer.* He wrote to Leo that a faction had condemned him before the hearing and forced other abbots to sign; to the council of 449 he wrote that he had been handed to a crowd ready to drag him through the city as a heretic (Perry 23513-23516). He said he had appealed at the trial. Flavian told Leo: Q3. Leo, answering in his long letter to Flavian, called the Tome, called him 'very unwary and exceedingly ignorant' (narrator's words, credited to Leo; Leo 5125-5128; Zach 1250-1254). Leo 4573-4582, 4532-4535, 5024-5030, 5125-5128. *Contested* on the appeal; the rest Documented as each side's words.
  4. *Ephesus, 449.* The emperor called a council at Ephesus under Dioscorus of Alexandria; the Syriac record of it says Eutyches's petition moved him (the emperors' own letter blames Flavian's pressing the case, Perry 23250-23252). He told the emperors the record of his trial was false (Perry 23306-23311); to the council he wrote that Flavian showed him no pity: Q2. The minutes were read; a bishop denied his own words in them; the council shouted that his accuser should be burned alive. The council restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian and, with others, Theodoret of Cyrrhus. Leo begged the emperor to put everything back as it was and to call a council in Italy (Leo 6765-6766, 6783-6784). Perry 23291-23293, 23306-23311, 23494-23504, 23860-23915, 24016-24020; Evag-E 1778-1787. *Documented* from the minutes of the council that restored him.
  5. *Chalcedon, 451.* After Theodosius died, a new emperor called a new council (Evag-E 2656-2660, 2799, 2826). It received Leo's letter for the removal of Eutyches's teaching, and his trial record was read again. When Egypt's bishops condemned those who said Christ's flesh came from heaven, but not Eutyches by name, the council demanded they curse him and sign Leo's letter; they asked to wait for their new archbishop. Councils 20265-20268; Evag-E 4318-4347, 5063-5093. *Documented* (Evagrius, writing about 140 years later from the acts). No held source tells what became of him after this.
- **What came of him** (about a third):
  1. *Heaviest: a name both sides cursed.* Chalcedon's bishops cried that those who opposed Leo were "Eutychians", and later writers on that side called the refusing churches followers of Dioscorus and Eutyches (Councils 20172-20174; Evag-E 6794-6797). But the churches that refused Chalcedon cursed him too. About 475, when the emperor Basiliscus had recalled Timothy from exile (Zach 5248; the chronicle gives no year, *Widely Accepted*), Timothy Aelurus, bishop of Alexandria, was told by his people in church to curse Nestorius and Eutyches, and at once did so aloud before the prefect (Zach 5638-5643). Severus of Antioch wrote against him (Life of Severus 6855-6858). *What he defended*, one Christ against Nestorius and the creed of Nicaea with nothing added, those churches kept; what they rejected was his account of Christ's body, which in their chronicle did not share our nature (Zach 1167-1172; 4095-4097). *Documented.*
  2. *His case framed two councils.* Leo wrote the Tome on his case (Zach 1250-1254); Chalcedon's definition received it against his teaching (Councils 20265-20268). *Documented.*
  3. Who keeps him today: no held source names anyone. Leave it out.
- **Quotes** (three):
  - **Q1.** "I confess that our Lord was from two natures before the union. But after the union I confess one nature." Rendered by me from the Greek, Evag-G 2284-2289 (Evagrius quoting the acts; a damaged print with garbled accent lines between, confirmed by three other held prints: Evag-E 1729-1731, Perry 23843-23845, Leo 5597-5598). Job: the hook; his idea in his own words. Not on the tree.
  - **Q2.** "He had no regard for my grey hairs. I have grown old with them in wars against heretics, and in confessing the true faith." Rendered by me from Perry's English (1881) of the Syriac version, Perry 23494-23504; the Greek is held only in Mansi's damaged OCR and is not used. Job: his own voice at the council that restored him; his view of himself beside his judges'. Not on the tree.
  - **Q3.** "This he certainly never did." Feltoe's English (1895) of Flavian's letter, Leo 5028, quoted word for word; it needs no change. Job: the judge's flat denial beside Eutyches's claim; each side credited. Not on the tree.
  - Not used: Cyril's "one incarnate nature of the Word". The slate lifts it only where Eutyches's own appeal calls on it, and neither held appeal does (Perry 23397-23402; Leo 4635-4638). Leo's "not a trial but a robbery" and "not of judges but of robbers", and Chalcedon's definition, stay barred.

- **Connection to carry into the story:** Eutyches gave the divided churches a name to reject, from both sides. Chalcedon defined Christ as known in two natures against his teaching, and its side called those who refused "Eutychians" (Councils 20172-20174, 20265-20268). The churches that refused Chalcedon held with him against Nestorius and against "two natures after the union", yet cursed him by name (Zach 5638-5643). The writer must explain why: in their own chronicle he "rejected the truth of the / body derived from the Virgin" (Zach 1167-1169), and Timothy wrote against those who "do not confess / Christ to be of the same nature with us in the flesh" (Zach 4096-4097). So the listener must hear that the refusing churches were not his followers, and that his name became the label one side used for the other.
- **Ending:**
  1. *The opening line seen again.* The record goes quiet after 451; no held source tells where or when he died. What was left was his name. Chalcedon's side used it for the churches that refused the council, and those churches cursed it too, while still holding the one nature after the union that the council of 449 had cheered (Perry 23847-23850; Councils 20172-20174; Zach 5638-5643). (Leo 4609-4611 shows him already at the very end of life in 448; no death is recorded.)
  2. *The last held scene.* At Chalcedon the council demanded that Egypt's bishops curse him by name, and they asked to wait (Evag-E 5079-5093). After that, no held source tells of him again. Use only if beat 5 holds back the bishops' answer for the close.
- **Links:** Leo of Rome: wrote to him, and he wrote to Leo; Leo's Tome judged his case. Cyril of Alexandria: he called Cyril his master and held the definition Cyril sent him (Perry 23397-23402). Nestorius: the teaching he fought. Dioscorus of Alexandria: presided at the council that restored him. Theodoret of Cyrrhus: deposed at the same council. Council of Chalcedon 451: condemned him again. Timothy Aelurus: cursed him by name at Alexandria.
- **Who tells it:**
  - The trial minutes (448), kept by his judges and read at Ephesus in 449 in Perry's English, and at Chalcedon as Evagrius reports: credited beside his words at the trial. He said they were falsified; tell that as his claim (beat 4).
  - Eutyches himself: his letter to Leo (Latin version; the Greek is lost) and his petitions of 449: credited beside his appeal and Q2.
  - Flavian: his letters to Leo, credited beside Q3 and the charge of reviving old heresies.
  - Leo of Rome: credited beside "very unwary".
  - The minutes of the council of 449, the side that restored him: credited beside the shouts and the restoration.
  - Evagrius, about 140 years later on Chalcedon's side, from the acts: beside Egypt's bishops at Chalcedon.
  - The Syriac chronicle known as that of Zachariah of Mitylene, from the side that refused Chalcedon: beside the palace soldiers and Timothy's curse.
  - Nestorius, in exile and hostile to Eutyches and the emperor, a contemporary third voice on the minutes: he says the emperor had men suborned to call the records of 448 false (Bazaar 18910-18914). To the card; the story does not credit him.
  - The doubt as a scene: the bishop at Ephesus who denied, then could not remember, his own recorded words (beat 4).
- **Do not say:**
  - "He founded Monophysitism" or "the churches that refused Chalcedon followed him" → they cursed him by name; Chalcedon's side called them "Eutychians".
  - "Egypt cursed him at Chalcedon" → at Chalcedon Egypt's bishops would not name him; Timothy cursed him about 475.
  - "He said Christ was not human" or "he denied Christ's body" → he said it was "the body of God", yet "a human body" taken from the Virgin; that it was not like ours is his judges' and the chronicle's charge.
  - "He taught 'one incarnate nature of the Word'" → no held appeal of his uses those words.
  - "The minutes were forged" → he said so; a bishop at Ephesus denied his own words in them; Evagrius says the record was confirmed.
  - "He appealed to Rome at the trial" → he said he did; Flavian said he never did.
  - "In November 448" → "in 448" (the month is the editors').
  - "Seventy years old" → he wrote that he had spent seventy years of life in self-control and chastity; no source gives his birth.
  - "Leo praised his teaching" → Leo praised his concern over Nestorius's teaching reviving.
  - "Chrysaphius, his godson" → leave out.
  - "He died in exile" or any death date → no held source gives it.
  - "The Robber Council" or Leo's "robbery" lines → say Leo begged the emperor to undo it and call a new council.
  - "Rain or snow" or "ice" as Eutyches's own image → it is the chronicle's account of his teaching, not his words.
  - "The bishops were forced at sword point in 449" as fact → at Chalcedon a bishop said they had signed a blank paper under force, with soldiers around them (Evag-E 4309-4313); credit it.
  - Timothy's curse without its setting → the people demanded it in church because the prefect was said to be an Eutychian, and he cursed both men aloud, which cleared him of suspicion (Zach 5638-5643).
  - "Both sides cursed his 'one nature' sentence" → the churches that refused Chalcedon cursed his name and his account of Christ's body; the council of 449 assented to his sentence (Perry 23847-23850).
  - "Eusebius, as bishop, exposed Nestorius" → he exposed Nestorius earlier, while still a lawyer (Evag-E 1724-1726).
  - "He recanted at Ephesus" → the minutes of 449 record that he declared himself orthodox and was restored (Perry 24016-24020); the Syriac chronicle calls his petition a recantation (Zach 1308-1310).
  - "Flavian was killed at the council" or "Dioscorus trampled him" → leave out; it is a later charge reported by Evagrius (Evag-E 2823-2825), and the Chalcedon and Dioscorus stories carry it.
- **Leave out:**
  - Chrysaphius as his godson (editor and a rough Latin scan only).
  - His fate after 451 (only an editor's summary of an untranslated letter).
  - The second inquiry into the minutes in 449 (Evag-E 1741-1745): a side step.
  - Flavian's death after 449, Barsumas, the blank paper in detail: the stories of Chalcedon, Dioscorus and Leo carry them.
  - Peter Mongus and the Henotikon of 482 (Zach 5987-6163), the eastern monks and Severus's letters, Philoxenus's list, Armenia's anathema (Ormanian): to the card, as more evidence of the curse.
  - The chronicle's "rain or snow" image (corrupt text, words supplied by translators): to the card.
  - Leo's "the ignorant old man", the seventy years and the shipwreck line, if not used: to the card.
  - The Coptic History's legend of "Eutyches, the monk" at the emperor's side in 431, and Theopistus: late.
  - Canon 95 of 692: to the card.
