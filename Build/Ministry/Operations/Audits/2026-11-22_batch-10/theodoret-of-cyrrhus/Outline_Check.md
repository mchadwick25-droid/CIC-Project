# Outline check: Theodoret of Cyrrhus

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** Theodoret, bishop of the small Syrian town of Cyrrhus, was the leading writer of Antioch's side against Cyril of Alexandria: he answered Cyril's twelve anathemas, holding one Christ, God and man, unmixed. That stand cost him his see in 449; at Chalcedon in 451 he was restored only after the bishops made him curse Nestorius, his friend. He is on the tree as that voice and as a historian: his Church History carried the church's story on from where Eusebius stopped, and his Religious History kept the Syrian monks, Simeon on his pillar among them.

**Also told.** His own account of his life (promised to God before birth, a monastery, made bishop unwillingly); his work at Cyrrhus (bridges, a conduit, villages of Marcion's followers won, stoned more than once), in his own words written to answer accusers; Ephesus 431 on John of Antioch's side; the 433 peace (he accepted Cyril's union letter but would not assent to Nestorius's deposition); confinement to his diocese and the sentence of 449 in his absence; his appeal to Leo; the Chalcedon scene; what came of him: the History joined to Socrates and Sozomen as the continuation of Eusebius (Cassiodorus, Evagrius, the Syriac chronicle, from both sides of the split), his anti-Cyril writings condemned in 553, the hostile memory of the churches that refused Chalcedon, and the Religious History as the record of Simeon and Maron. Whether his views changed at Chalcedon is Contested.

## Verdict: FIX

The outline's heart agrees with mine in substance: the answer to Cyril, 449, Chalcedon's curse, and the two histories, with the History's later use on both sides, 553, the hostile memory and the Syrian monks. No major contribution is missing. The beats tell the heart in the first minute and then follow the life once, in order (433 is pointed back to from the hook, which the kit allows). The quotes are verbatim in their prints, the cited lines hold almost everywhere, and the length works: 884 less the Cut first list's 233 is 651, and about 668 with the clause added in B1, under the approved 675. Two findings block. Both concern what the record says about his refusal and his curse.

## BLOCKING

**B1. "A curse he had refused for eighteen years", and the "broken vow".** No held line supports it, and his translator says the opposite. Jackson, NP3 2859-2870 (EDITOR), says he "refused to accept the deposition of Nestorius and his individual condemnation, and it was not till several years had elapsed that ... Theodoret found means of attaching a meaning to the current anathemas on Nestorianism, not, as he said, on Nestorius, which allowed him to submit." So he gave way, on his own terms, within a few years of 433. Letter 172 refuses to assent to what was done to Nestorius. It does not refuse a curse. The outline as it stands leads the writer to "he held out from 433 to 451, then broke his vow". That is a misleading implication, and Do not say actually allows it ("Not 'the eighteen years' as exact unless counted from 433 to 451, as here"). The sheet holds the right account. The heart's substance, that he was restored only after cursing Nestorius by name, stays. Only the unsupported qualifier goes. Corrections:
- Also told, "What happened to him": replace "restored in 451 at the price of a curse he had refused for eighteen years (E7, E8, E12; *Documented*; the eighteen years run from the peace of 433, *Widely Accepted*)" with "restored in 451 only after he cursed Nestorius by name before the council (E7, E8, E12; *Documented*)".
- Quote 1, Job: replace "and the vow the essence says he broke at Chalcedon" with "and the loyalty that Chalcedon later made him curse by name".
- Beat 3, after "But he would not accept that Nestorius had been rightly removed.", add: "Some years later, his English translator says, he found a way to accept the curses on Nestorius's teaching, though not, he said, on the man (NP3 2859-2870; EDITOR, *Widely Accepted*). One clause, credited to the translator." (About 17 words. The count comes to about 668.)
- Do not say: replace the line "Not 'the eighteen years' as exact unless counted from 433 to 451, as here." with "Not 'he refused for eighteen years', 'he held out until Chalcedon' or 'he broke his vow'. His translator says that some years after 433 he found a way to accept the curses on Nestorius's teaching, 'not, as he said, on Nestorius' (NP3 2859-2870)."

**B2. The Chalcedon scene leaves out that he had already offered a curse on his own terms.** In the acts, NP3 3322-3325, before the shouting he said he had come "to satisfy you that I am orthodox, and that I anathematize Nestorius and Eutyches, and every one who says that there are two Sons." The bishops rejected that and demanded a plain curse with no statement of his faith. Beat 6 as written ("He said he could not speak without setting out his faith in full; they cried that he was a heretic. Then he said it") implies that he resisted cursing Nestorius at all. It also softens what the scene's conflict was actually about: they would not hear his explanation. Corrections:
- Beat 6: replace "He asked for his papers to be read; they wanted nothing read. He said he could not speak without setting out his faith in full; they cried that he was a heretic (NP3 3305-3334; narrator's words)." with "He asked for his papers to be read; they wanted nothing read. He offered to condemn Nestorius and Eutyches together, with his faith explained; they shouted for a plain curse. He said he could not speak without setting out his faith in full; they cried that he was a heretic (NP3 3305-3334; narrator's words)."
- Do not say, add: "Not that he refused to curse Nestorius at Chalcedon. The acts show him offering to condemn 'Nestorius and Eutyches' with his faith explained, and the bishops demanding the bare curse instead (NP3 3322-3334)."

## MINOR

**M1. Quote 1's rendering says more than the Latin.** "procul dubio divina mihi cooperante gratia et animae imbecillitatem roborante" is an ablative absolute that qualifies the refusal ("with God's grace working with me"). The outline turns it into a separate assertion ("Without doubt, God's grace works with me ..."). Jackson renders it "God's grace helping me and supporting my infirmity". My rendering: "Not even if someone were to cut off both my hands will I ever agree to what was done against you, unjustly and wickedly, while God's grace works with me and strengthens my weak soul." Replace the outline's rendering with: "I will never agree to what was done against you, unjustly and wickedly, not even if someone cuts off both my hands, as God's grace works with me and strengthens my weak soul." (Change the note to match: the ablative absolute is rendered as an "as" clause, not as a second sentence.)

**M2. The letter's date in Opening 1.** "After the peace with Cyril" is the researcher's inference from the letter's own words. Jackson's note puts the letter "not long after the receipt by Theodoret of the Chapters of Cyril" (NP3 37985-37986). The sheet marks the date *Inferential-Thin*. Opening 1: replace "after the peace with Cyril, Theodoret wrote to Nestorius" with "Theodoret wrote to Nestorius that he had found Cyril's writings free of heresy (NP3 37999-38003); give the letter no year". Do not say, add: "No year for the letter to Nestorius, and not 'after the peace' as fact: tie it to his own words about accepting Cyril's writings."

**M3. "His books ordered burned" (Also told).** The acts ask the emperors to command the burning (P449 15641-15645). No held line says it was ordered or done. Replace "deposed in his absence and his books ordered burned in 449" with "deposed in his absence in 449 by a council that asked the emperors to burn his books". Do not say, add: "Not 'his books were burned' or 'ordered burned': the council asked the emperors to order it."

**M4. The Arabs "to be baptized" (beat 4).** RH 113926-113935 has the Ishmaelites renouncing their idols and receiving "the divine mysteries" (τῶν θείων ἀπολαύουσι μυστηρίων). Baptism is said there of the Iberians, Armenians and Persians (RH 113924-113925). Replace "He says he saw Arabs come in hundreds to be baptized there" with "He says he saw Arabs come in bands of hundreds to break their idols before him and join the church". Cut first item 12 stays as it is.

**M5. "Evagrius, a historian on Chalcedon's side."** No line given. Either cite Evagrius's own defense of Chalcedon (EVW, his Book 2 chapter upholding the council) or say "a Greek historian about 140 years later" (What came 1; Who tells it).

**M6. The short phrase "our very Blessed Father" (beat 5)** is not on the Quotes list of short phrases that may stand. Add it to that list or put it in the narrator's words ("against Cyril, whom it called their blessed father").

**M7. "With the gift which blinds the eyes of the soul"** stands already on the `leo-of-rome` card (legacy, remembered), crediting the same chronicle. It is a card line, not a story quote, so it is not barred. But the Quotes note "none is used on the tree" is not right. Correct the note to say so. To be safe, the narrator may carry it as "a bribe", credited to the chronicle.

**M8. Library sweep, spot-check.** The corpus-map bucket rows and the name-form search match. `assemani_bibliotheca-orientalis-tomus1-...` and `acta-sanctorum_maii-tomus-5` hold other accounts of Simeon the Stylite. They are not needed for Theodoret's life and do not change a claim. Name them under Set aside ("other lives of Simeon; the story uses only Theodoret's own account"), for the card.
