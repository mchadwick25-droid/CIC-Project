# Outline check: Theodoret of Cyrrhus

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** Theodoret, bishop of the small Syrian town of Cyrrhus, was the leading writer of Antioch's side against Cyril of Alexandria: he answered Cyril's twelve anathemas, holding one Christ, God and man, unmixed. That stand cost him his see in 449; at Chalcedon in 451 he was restored only after the bishops made him curse Nestorius, his friend. He is on the tree as that voice and as a historian: his Church History carried the church's story on from where Eusebius stopped, and his Religious History kept the Syrian monks, Simeon on his pillar among them.

**Also told.** His own account of his life (promised to God before birth, a monastery, made bishop unwillingly); his work at Cyrrhus (bridges, a conduit, villages of Marcion's followers won, stoned more than once), in his own words written to answer accusers; Ephesus 431 on John of Antioch's side; the 433 peace (he accepted Cyril's union letter but would not assent to Nestorius's deposition); confinement to his diocese and the sentence of 449 in his absence; his appeal to Leo; the Chalcedon scene; what came of him: the History joined to Socrates and Sozomen as the continuation of Eusebius (Cassiodorus, Evagrius, the Syriac chronicle, from both sides of the split), his anti-Cyril writings condemned in 553, the hostile memory of the churches that refused Chalcedon, and the Religious History as the record of Simeon and Maron. Whether his views changed at Chalcedon is Contested.
