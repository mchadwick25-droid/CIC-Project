## The four lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Theodoret, a fifth-century bishop of Cyrrhus, a small town in northern Syria. He was a leading voice of Antioch's teaching about Christ, and a historian.
- **What happened:** He answered Cyril of Alexandria's twelve curses and stood with John of Antioch's side at Ephesus in 431. He accepted the peace of 433 but never Nestorius's removal. He was confined to Cyrrhus, deposed in his absence in 449, and let back at Chalcedon only after he cursed his friend Nestorius by name.
- **What it was about:** whether God and man in Christ stay distinct and unmixed (his teaching) or are run together (his charge against Cyril's curses). It was also about whether that teaching split Christ into two sons (his accusers' charge, and the charge in the curse Chalcedon demanded). Running through it: his loyalty to a friend against a council's demand.
- **Why the connection matters:** his Church History carried on Eusebius's story, and writers on both sides of the split over Chalcedon picked it up where he stopped: Evagrius in Greek, and the Syriac chronicle of the churches that refused Chalcedon. The same chronicle remembers him as an enemy who bribed Leo, and a council in 553 condemned what he wrote against Cyril.

None is blank or wrong.
