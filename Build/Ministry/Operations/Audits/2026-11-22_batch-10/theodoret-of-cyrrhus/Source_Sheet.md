# Source sheet: Theodoret of Cyrrhus

Files (all under `cic/texts/`), with the short names used below:

- **NP3**: `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`. Blomfield Jackson's English (1892) of Theodoret's Church History, Counter-statements and Letters, with his Prolegomena (EDITOR), and Gennadius's Lives of Illustrious Men (Richardson's English, 1892).
- **RH**: `theodoret_opera-omnia-t3-historia-religiosa-pg82-grc-lat_migne1864.txt`. Migne, PG 82 (1864): the Greek of the Church History and the Religious History. OCR with systematic letter confusions (χ for κ, δ for β); the facing Latin column is unreadable OCR.
- **P449**: `council-of-ephesus-449_second-synod-of-ephesus-syriac-acts_perry1881.txt`. Perry's English (1881) of the Syriac acts of the session of 22 August 449.
- **LEO**: `npnf212_leo-great-gregory-great.xml`. Feltoe's English (1895) of Leo's letters.
- **NP14**: `npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml`. Percival's English (1900) of the councils.
- **EVW**: `evagrius-scholasticus_ecclesiastical-history_walford1846.txt`. Walford's English (1846) of Evagrius Scholasticus (wrote about 593).
- **EVG**: `evagrius-scholasticus_historia-ecclesiastica-grc_bidez-parmentier1898.txt`. The Greek of Evagrius (damaged accent lines between the text lines).
- **M5**: `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-5-431-441-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt`. Mansi's Latin, with the Synodicon (damaged OCR, long s read as f).
- **ZACH**: `zachariah-rhetor_chronicle_hamiltonbrooks1899.txt`. Hamilton and Brooks's English (1899) of the Syriac chronicle under the name of Zachariah of Mitylene, from the side that rejected Chalcedon.
- **CASS**: `cassiodorus_opera-institutiones-pl70-lat_migne1847.txt`. Cassiodorus, Institutions (damaged OCR).
- **NP2**: `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`. Zenos's notes to Socrates (EDITOR).

Witness kinds: OWN (his own words), CONTEMP (written in his lifetime by someone else), LATER (with gap), EDITOR (a modern editor's claim). Confidence: the project's five levels.

## Setting

- **S1. Cyrrhus, a small town in Syria; a large rural diocese.** OWN, Letter 113 to Leo, NP3 33165-33166: "I have done pastoral duty in eight hundred churches, for so many parishes does Cyrus contain". OWN, Letter 172 to Nestorius, NP3 37993-37995: "the very solitude of the city over which I am called to preside". EDITOR, Jackson NP3 2576-2594: the town lay in the district of Syria called Cyrestica, "subject to the Metropolitan of Hierapolis", and "Cyrus itself was a wretched little place". *Documented* (the size of the diocese, his own words); the description of the town is Jackson's. Spoken word: "Cyrrhus, a small town in northern Syria". The English of the letters spells it Cyrus; the census spells it Cyrrhus.
- **S2. The tradition.** Census entry `antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce`: informal name "Antioch in Chrysostom's Day"; its why-line says Antioch's teachers "insisted on what the words actually said". Spoken word: "the church of Antioch", "Antioch's way of reading". Corpus map (`antiochene-exegetical-christianity-chrysostom-ce.yaml` 1446-1458) calls him its last major voice, sitting "at its edge": the Library thread's note, not a source.
- **S3. Dates.** Born 393 by Tillemont's reckoning, which Jackson follows; Garnier 386; Möller 390 (EDITOR, NP3 2393-2407). Bishop from 423 by Jackson (NP3 2603-2606). OWN, Letter 81 (written 449), NP3 31501-31502: "Five and twenty years I so lived"; Letter 113, NP3 33158-33159: "Six and twenty years have I ruled the church". *Widely Accepted* for about 423; the year of birth is *Contested*.

## Events, in order

- **E1. Promised to God before birth; a monastery; bishop against his will.** OWN, Letter 81 to Nomus, NP3 31496-31501: "Even before my conception my parents promised to devote me to God; from my swaddling-band, they devoted me according to their promise and educated me accordingly; the time before my episcopate I spent in a monastery and then was unwillingly consecrated bishop." OWN, Religious History 13 (on the monk Macedonius), RH 108893-108898: Greek, "Τριακαίδεκα ἔτη συνοικήσασα τῷ ἐμῷ πατρὶ ἡ μήτηρ παίδων οὐκ ἐγένετο μήτηρ" (for thirteen years of marriage to my father my mother bore no children); RH 108921-108923: the holy man promised the father a son; RH 108990-108991: "Ὑπέσχου δὲ ἀντιδώσειν τὸ δοθησόμενον, καὶ εἰς τὴν θείαν αὐτὸ θεραπείαν καθοσιώσειν" (you promised to give back what would be given, and to dedicate it to God's service). *Documented* as his own account; the answered prayer is a wonder in his voice. Birth at Antioch: Jackson, from the Religious History (NP3 2344-2345, 2357-2359): EDITOR. The monastery: Nicerte, near Apamea, OWN, Letter 119, NP3 33481-33485 ("my monastery", the note naming Nicerte is Jackson's).
- **E2. Bishop of Cyrrhus: what he built and whom he won.** OWN, Letter 81, NP3 31502-31516: "Five and twenty years I so lived that I was never summoned to trial by any one nor ever brought accusation against any ... In so many years I never took an obol nor a garment from any one ... From the revenues of my see I erected public porticoes; I built two large bridges; I looked after the public baths. On finding that the city was not watered by the river running by it, I built the conduit, and supplied the dry town with water. But not to mention these matters I led eight villages of Marcionists with their neighbourhood into the way of truth; another full of Eunomians and another of Arians I brought to the light of divine knowledge ... many a time I shed my blood; many a time was I stoned by them and brought to the very gates of death." OWN, Letter 113, NP3 33162-33165: "more than a thousand souls did I rescue from the plague of Marcion". *Documented* as his own claim, written to answer his accusers (NP3 31496: "My accusers compel me to speak"). Marcion's followers: a church of its own, older than his day, that set aside the Old Testament (gloss for the writer: "followers of Marcion, a rival church").
- **E3. 430-431: John of Antioch asks him to answer Cyril's twelve curses.** OWN, Letter 150 to John, NP3 36088-36090: "I have been much distressed at reading the anathematisms which you have sent to request me to refute in writing". NP3 36093-36094: Cyril "is both himself unsound, and that to a terrible degree"; NP3 36107-36109: he renews "that vain and impious teaching of Apollinarius". NP3 36143-36145: "Setting down each of the anathematisms by itself, I have annexed the counter statement". Cyril's first anathema, NP3 4616-4619. Dating February 431: Jackson's note, NP3 36079-36081 (EDITOR). *Documented*.
- **E4. His answer: one Christ, God and man not mixed.** OWN, Counter-statement I, NP3 4724-4727: "Now it is plain from these words that the form of God was not changed into the form of a servant, but, remaining what it was, took the form of the servant. So God the Word was not made flesh, but assumed living and reasonable flesh." NP3 4733-4734: "we style that holy Virgin θεοτόκος, not because she gave birth in natural manner to God, but to man united to the God that had fashioned Him." Counter-statement II, NP3 4760-4762: "We ... confess one Christ; and, on account of the union, we name the same both God and man"; NP3 4767-4771: a "mixture of flesh and Godhead" brings "confusion; and the admission of confusion destroys the individuality of each nature." *Documented*. Greek original not held; Jackson's English from Schulze's edition (NP3 4704-4705). EDITOR verdicts to leave alone: Jackson's "were substantially at one" (NP3 2756; barred by the slate) and Bright's "prima facie Nestorian" (NP3 2766-2768).
- **E5. Ephesus, 431.** EDITOR, Jackson NP3 2826-2838: Theodoret arrived with his metropolitan, Alexander of Hierapolis; Cyril's side opened the council without John; Theodoret was one of the deputies of the Easterners sent to the emperor, who "were allowed to proceed no further than Chalcedon". The petitions from Chalcedon are printed as letters of the Commissioners "among whom was Theodoretus" (NP3 37183, the editor's heading). *Documented* in outline; the Ephesus story holds the council.
- **E6. The peace of 433: Cyril's faith yes, Nestorius's removal no.** OWN (with his synod), Letter 171, NP3 37939-37947: they read "the Egyptian Letter" and found it "quite in accordance with our own statements, and entirely opposed to the Twelve Chapters"; NP3 37953-37958: it shows "two natures, and the distinction between them; an unconfounded union, made not by mixture". OWN, Letter 172 to Nestorius, Latin, M5 88746-88765: "His autem quae contra tuam iniuste et inique facta sunt sanctitatem, nec si ambas manus meas quilibet abscindat, patior assentire, procul dubio divina mihi cooperante gratia et animae imbecillitatem roborante" (read with long s restored: "fa&a" = facta, "abícindat" = abscindat). Jackson's English, NP3 38007-38010: "But to what has been done unjustly and illegally against your holiness, not even if one were to cut off both my hands would I ever assent, God's grace helping me and supporting my infirmity." NP3 38012-38014: "no time has changed me like the centipedes and chameleons". Jackson NP3 37972-37974: "Of this letter the Greek copies have perished. Three Latin versions exist." Date: Jackson's note puts it "not long after the receipt by Theodoret of the Chapters of Cyril" (NP3 37985-37986), but the letter itself speaks of having accepted "the Egyptian writings" (NP3 37999-38003), the peace letter: the date is *Inferential-Thin*; the refusal is *Documented*. EDITOR, Jackson NP3 2859-2870: he "refused to accept the deposition of Nestorius", and only "several years" later found a way to curse "Nestorianism, not, as he said, Nestorius", moved by "the entreaties of his beloved flock". Ephesus outline (sibling) holds the same clause (its E18: BH 1271-1273, 1326-1343, the editors' chronology).
- **E7. 448-449: confined to his own diocese.** OWN, Letter 81, NP3 31453-31458: "by the imperial decree I am forbidden to travel beyond the boundaries of Cyrus. For this apparent punishment cause there is none, except the fact of my convening an episcopal synod. No indictment was published; no prosecutor appeared; the defendant was not convicted; but the sentence was given." NP3 31476-31480: "Then let there be a council ... Let me state what I hold". *Documented*. Who moved the emperor (Dioscorus, Eutyches, Chrysaphius): Jackson, NP3 2906-2916 (EDITOR).
- **E8. August 449: deposed at Ephesus in his absence.** CONTEMP, the acts, P449 13316-13323: "The Deposition of Theodoret, the Bishop of Cyrus. Pelagius, Presbyter of Antioch, said: I have a Libel (Bill of Indictment) against Theodoret and Domnus". Dioscorus's sentence, P449 15581-15603: "Theodoret, who is bent on the destruction of souls without number, who has brought trouble on all the Churches of the East ... who, moreover, has ventured to hold tenets and write in opposition to those Positions which have been laid down by our very Blessed Father and Bishop Cyril,—must be (estranged) removed from the whole Function of the Priesthood". P449 15641-15645: the emperors are asked to command "these Treatises of the impious Theodoret ... to be committed to the burning of the fire." Juvenal, P449 15700-15709: "not even a single communication between him and them ought to take place, nor ought they to offer salt, or (address even) a mere word, to him." LATER (Evagrius, about 140 years on), EVW 1786-1787: "they depose Theodoret, bishop of Cyrus". OWN, Letter 113, NP3 33141-33149: "me too in my absence he stabbed with a pen, without summoning me to the bar ... when all the while I was five and thirty days' march away." *Documented*, from the acts of the council that deposed him. Date: the session of 22 August 449 (P449 header, line 2 and 8); Jackson gives 8 August for the opening (NP3 2956-2957) and 11 August for the deposition (NP3 33036): the sources differ; say "in August 449".
- **E9. He writes to Leo of Rome.** OWN, Letter 113, NP3 33174-33177: "But I await the sentence of your apostolic see. I beseech and implore your holiness to succour me in my appeal"; NP3 33189-33193: "If you bid me abide by the sentence of condemnation, I abide; and henceforth I will trouble no man". NP3 33201-33205: "during all the time of my episcopate I have not acquired a house, not a piece of ground, not an obol, not a tomb, but of my own accord have embraced poverty". NP3 33216-33218: "I would hasten to you myself were I not kept back by the chains of the imperial order". *Documented*. What "appeal" meant is an editors' quarrel (Jackson NP3 3098-3133, against Garnier and Newman): leave out.
- **E10. Retreat to his monastery.** OWN, Letter 119, NP3 33476-33485: he asks to go "to the West, and there plead my cause", or "at least command me to inhabit my monastery". That the second was granted: Jackson, NP3 3064-3069 (EDITOR). *Widely Accepted*.
- **E11. 451, Chalcedon, first session: let in, shouted at.** CONTEMP (the acts, Labbe iv. 102-103, in Jackson's English), NP3 3231-3258: the officials ordered that Theodoret enter "because the holy Archbishop Leo had restored the bishopric to him"; the bishops of Egypt, Illyricum and Palestine called out "Cast out the teacher of Nestorius"; the Eastern bishops shouted "We had to sign a blank paper; we were scourged, and so we signed." NP3 3259-3262: he "now occupies the place of an accuser". NP3 3271-3272: "Do not call him a bishop! He is not a bishop! Cast out the fighter against God! Cast out the Jew!" NP3 3290-3291: the officials, "The vulgar cries are not worthy of bishops". LATER, EVW 4377-4385 (Evagrius from the acts): the senators said Theodoret "had been admitted in the character of an accuser". *Documented*. How Theodosius died and the edict was lifted: Jackson NP3 3186-3205 (EDITOR).
- **E12. 451, a later session: the curse on Nestorius.** CONTEMP (acts, Labbe iv. 621, Jackson's English), NP3 3305-3338: the bishops "stood before the rails of the most holy altar, and shouted 'Theodoret must now anathematize Nestorius'"; he asked that his documents be read; "We want nothing to be read—only anathematize Nestorius"; he: "I was brought up by the orthodox, I was taught by the orthodox, I have preached orthodoxy"; they: "Speak plainly"; he: "Unless I set forth at length my faith I cannot speak. I believe"; they: "He is a heretic! He is a Nestorian! Away with the heretic!"; he: "Anathema to Nestorius and to whoever denies that the Holy Virgin Mary is the Parent of God, and who divides the only begotten Son into two Sons. I have subscribed the definition of faith, and the epistle of the most holy Archbishop Leo." LATER, EVG 9075-9087 (Greek): "κατὰ τὴν ἐνάτην σύνοδον ἐκινήθη τὰ κατὰ Θεοδώρητον, καὶ ἀνεθεμάτισε Νεστόριον εἰρηκώς· Ἀνάθεμα Νεστορίῳ ... καὶ ἀπὸ διαλαλιᾶς πάντων ἀπέλαβε τὸν οἰκεῖον θρόνον"; EVW 5242-5249: "He anathematised Nestorius ... Upon this he was restored to his see". *Documented*. Disagreement: Jackson calls it "the eighth session" (NP3 3304), Evagrius "the ninth meeting" (EVW 5242; EVG 9075): say "a later session". Glossed: "anathema", a solemn curse that cuts someone off from the church; "Parent of God", the title Mother of God (the term under dispute, as in the Ephesus outline).
- **E13. Did he change his mind?** EDITOR, Jackson NP3 3339-3342: "Bertram (... 1883) thinks Theodoret changed his views; Möller ... that he retained them, though necessarily modified in expression by stress of circumstances." *Contested*.
- **E14. Leo writes to him after the council.** CONTEMP, Leo, Letter 120, LEO 10293-10297: "we ascertained, beloved, the victory you and we together had won by assistance from on high over the blasphemy of Nestorius, as well as over the madness of Eutyches." LEO 10506-10507: "God ... has shown you free from all taint of heresy in the judgment of the Apostolic See". *Documented*.
- **E15. Death.** LATER (Gennadius, within the century), NP3 43772-43776: the history runs "until the accession of the elder Leo in whose reign he died." EDITOR, Jackson NP3 3408-3412: "there is some probability that Theodoret survived till 458; none that he lived longer." Richardson's note gives 457 (NP3 43760-43761). No held line gives the year. *Widely Accepted* for the reign of the emperor Leo (457-474); the year is *Inferential-Thin*.

## His works

- **W1. The Church History, from where Eusebius stopped.** OWN, Prologue, NP3 5219-5224: "Eusebius of Palestine has written a history of the Church from the time of the holy Apostles to the reign of Constantine ... I shall begin my history from the period at which his terminates." NP3 5208-5213: "I too shall attempt to record in writing events in ecclesiastical history hitherto omitted, deeming it indeed not right to look on without an effort while oblivion robs noble deeds and useful stories of their due fame." NP3 18270-18274: "The narrative now embraces a period of 105 years, beginning from the Arian madness and ending with the death of the admirable Theodorus and Theodotus." Greek held in RH (not checked line by line: not quoted). Written about 438-440 or a little later: Jackson's note, NP3 17855-17864 (EDITOR). Gennadius gives "ten books" (NP3 43772); the held text has five: the sources differ. *Documented*.
- **W2. What the History holds that the tree uses.** Ephrem's hymns, NP3 15050-15066 ("These songs are still used to enliven the festivals of our victorious martyrs"; used by `ephrem-the-syrian`); Chrysostom's life and the return of his body to Constantinople in 438, NP3 17845-17866 (Jackson's date in the note); Ambrose and the emperor Theodosius after Thessalonica, chapter at NP3 16499 (used by `ambrose-of-milan`). Link facts only; no quote taken from them here.
- **W3. The Religious History: the Syrian monks he knew.** OWN, Simeon, RH 113105-113107 (Greek): "Συμεώνην τὸν πάνυ, τὸ μέγα θαῦμα τῆς οἰκουμένης" (the great Simeon, the great wonder of the world). RH 113130-113134: "Ἐγὼ δὲ καὶ πάντας ... ἀνθρώπους μάρτυρας ἔχων τῶν ὑπὲρ λόγον ἀγώνων, δέδοικα τὸ διήγημα, μὴ τοῖς ἐσομένοις μῦθος εἶναι δόξῃ, πάμπαν τῆς ἀληθείας γεγυμνωμένος" (though I have all men as witnesses of his struggles past reason, I fear the story may seem a myth to those to come, wholly stripped of truth). RH 113181-113182: "Ταῦτα δὲ τῆς ἱερᾶς αὐτοῦ διηγουμένης ἀκήκοα γλώττης" (I heard this from his own holy tongue). RH 113729-113738: because the crowds all tried to touch him and take a blessing from his leather clothes, he devised standing on a pillar: "Πρῶτον μὲν ἓξ πήχεων τμηθῆναι κελεύσας, εἶτα δύο καὶ δέκα, μετὰ δὲ ταῦτα δύο καὶ εἴκοσι, νῦν δὲ ἓξ καὶ τριάκοντα· ἀναπτῆναι γὰρ εἰς οὐρανὸν ἐφίεται, καὶ τῆς ἐπιγείου ταύτης ἀπαλλαγῆναι διατριβῆς." RH 113926-113940: Arabs ("Ἰσμαηλῖται") came in bands of two hundred, three hundred, sometimes a thousand, broke their idols before him and were baptized: "Καὶ τούτων αὐτόπτης ἐγενόμην ἐγώ, καὶ ἀκήκοα" (of these I was an eyewitness, and I heard). OWN, Maron, RH 109564-109569 (chapter heading "ιστ΄. ΜΑΡΩΝ" at 109562): Maron took a hilltop once a pagan shrine and lived in the open air. *Documented* as his own eyewitness account; the wonders stay in his voice. The print: one held print only, with the steady OCR letter confusions named above; no second held print of these chapters. The chapter number for Simeon is damaged in the OCR (RH 113103).
- **W4. Gennadius's list.** LATER, NP3 43762-43772: "is said to have written many works. Such as have come to my knowledge are the following: On the incarnation of the Lord, Against Eutyches the presbyter and Dioscorus bishop of Alexandria". *Documented*.

## What came of him (heaviest first)

- **I1. His History became part of the church's one continuous story.** LATER, Cassiodorus (sixth century), CASS 83303-83307 (damaged Latin): "historiam vero Eusebii apud Graecos Socrates, Sozomenus et Theodoretus sequentia conscripserunt, quos a viro disertissimo Epiphanio in uno corpore duodecim libris fecimus ... transferri" (Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret wrote what followed Eusebius's history among the Greeks; we had them put into Latin by the learned Epiphanius, in one body of twelve books). Confirmed by Zenos, NP2 1695-1699 (EDITOR): "Epiphanius, surnamed Scholasticus, translated the history of Socrates, together with those of Sozomen and Theodoret, under the auspices of Cassiodorus ... under the name of Historiae Ecclesiasticae Tripartitae". LATER, Evagrius (about 593), EVW 1106-1116, EVG 1255-1259: Eusebius, "Sozomen, Theodoret, and Socrates have produced a most excellent record ... down to a certain period of the reign of Theodosius", and EVW 1136-1140: he will carry the story on from there. LATER, the Syriac chronicle (sixth century), ZACH 932-937: it gathers "the rest of the events ... after those chronicled in the three Ecclesiastical Histories of Eusebius, Socrates, and Theodoret"; ZACH 1057-1062. *Documented*. The writers who carried his History on came from both sides of the split over Chalcedon.
- **I2. Chalcedon restored him; a century later his writings against Cyril were cursed.** LATER, the council of 553, NP14 23656-23661: "we condemn and anathematize ... those things which Theodoret impiously wrote against the right faith, and against the Twelve Chapters of the holy Cyril, and against the first Synod of Ephesus, and also those which he wrote in defence of Theodore and Nestorius." EVW 9578-9583 (Evagrius) gives the same sentence. The council itself, NP14 23636-23638: at Chalcedon they "required him to anathematize those things of which he was accused". EDITOR, Jackson NP3 3511-3513: "Theodoret was reinstated at Chalcedon in spite of what he had written, and what he had written was anathematized at Constantinople in spite of his reinstatement" (an editor's verdict: paraphrase only, credited). Also condemned at synods in 499 and 512, Jackson NP3 3465-3467 (EDITOR). *Documented*. The `second-council-of-constantinople-553` story already tells the condemnation: one clause here.
- **I3. The churches that refused Chalcedon remembered him as the head of the other party.** LATER, the Syriac chronicle, ZACH 2211-2225: Theodoret was deposed "because he wrote twelve censures upon Cyril's Heads against Nestorius"; he "went up to Leo of Rome ... and, with the gift which blinds the eyes of the soul, he got the better of him"; ZACH 2272-2276: "the Nestorian party ... besought the king that Theodoret should be appointed the president of the Synod"; at Chalcedon he "lived there boldly, like an honoured bishop". ZACH 2429-2436: "the story goes" that Dioscorus, seeing Theodoret on the throne, sat on the pavement, saying "I will not sit with the wicked". *Documented* as that church's hostile memory; the bribe is the chronicle's charge, not a fact. Hostile view: credit it.
- **I4. His Religious History kept the Syrian monks' memory.** LATER, Evagrius, EVW 1961-1966: of Simeon's wonders there is "an eloquent record by Theodoret, bishop of Cyrus" (Evagrius adds a story the inhabitants of the desert still told). Maron: his chapter (W3), and the census story `maronite-christianity`: "The Maronites take their name from a Syriac monk, Maron". The corpus-map note (`maronite-christianity.yaml` 28-36) calls it "the one contemporary account": the Library thread's note, not a source. *Documented* (Evagrius); *Widely Accepted* (Maron).
- **I5. Leo's ally.** E14. Leo's letter calls Chalcedon a victory "you and we together had won".
- **Who keeps him today:** no held source names anyone. Leave it out.

## Links to other people on the tree

- Nestorius: his friend; Theodoret's letter to him (E6); cursed him at Chalcedon (E12). Friendship as such rests on Jackson (NP3 2714-2716); the letter's warmth is his own.
- Cyril of Alexandria: answered his twelve anathemas (E3, E4); accepted his peace letter (E6).
- Council of Ephesus 431: present on John's side; sent to the emperor (E5).
- Dioscorus of Alexandria: pronounced his sentence in 449 (E8).
- Eutyches: deposed at the same council that restored Eutyches (EVW 1786-1787; P449); Gennadius lists his books against him (W4).
- Leo of Rome: wrote to him (E9); Leo wrote back (E14).
- Council of Chalcedon 451: let in, made to curse Nestorius, restored (E11, E12).
- Eusebius of Caesarea: his History begins where Eusebius's ends (W1).
- Socrates of Constantinople, Sozomen: his fellow historians, joined with him in the Latin history (I1).
- Evagrius Scholasticus: carried the story on from them; used his Simeon (I1, I4).
- Cassiodorus: had his History put into Latin (I1).
- Second Council of Constantinople 553: condemned his writings against Cyril (I2).
- Ephrem the Syrian: his History tells Ephrem's hymns (W2). John Chrysostom: his History tells Chrysostom's life and return (W2). Ambrose of Milan: his History tells the emperor's penance (W2).
- Methodius of Olympus, Tatian: live stories that cite him; their passages (his Eranistes florilegium; his heresy book on the Diatessaron, not held, known only through an editor, `anf09` 1922-1923) are not used here.
- Gennadius of Marseilles: lists his works (W4).

## Where sources disagree or come late

- Birth year: 386, 390 or 393 (EDITOR, NP3 2393-2407).
- Death: in Leo's reign (Gennadius); 457 (Richardson); probably not after 458 (Jackson).
- The session at Chalcedon: eighth (Jackson) or ninth (Evagrius).
- The date of the deposition in 449: 22 August (the Syriac acts' session) against 8 and 11 August in Jackson's notes.
- Books of the History: five held, ten in Gennadius.
- Whether his views changed at Chalcedon: Bertram against Möller, through Jackson.
- The synod's letter of 430 urging Nestorius to accept "Mother of God": "has been generally attributed to Theodoret" (Jackson NP3 2729-2730); unsettled.
- The letter on Cyril's death (NP3 38162): genuine or not is disputed (NP3 2459-2493, 2876-2882): leave out.
- Whether he went back to Cyrrhus after Chalcedon: "Some doubt hangs over the question" (Jackson NP3 3350-3375).
- The bribe of Leo: only in the hostile Syriac chronicle (ZACH 2219-2221).
