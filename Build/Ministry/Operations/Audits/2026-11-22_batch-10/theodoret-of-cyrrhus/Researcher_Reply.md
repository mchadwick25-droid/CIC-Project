# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Essence:** He was bishop of Cyrrhus and a leading voice of Antioch's teaching that Christ is one, God and man, with neither mixed into the other. He answered Cyril's twelve curses and was deposed at Ephesus in 449. At Chalcedon in 451 he got his see back only after cursing his friend Nestorius. His Church History carried on from Eusebius, and his Religious History tells the Syrian monks, Simeon among them.

**Also told:** his building and preaching as bishop, in his own letters; the peace of 433, when he refused to accept Nestorius's removal; his books ordered burned in 449; later historians on both sides of Chalcedon picked up his History, and Cassiodorus had it put into Latin; the council of 553 condemned his writings.

**Shape and length:** Teacher or thinker. About 4 minutes (about 610 words counted; 675 at most): three quotes about 95, glosses about 55, credited witnesses about 60.

**Connection:** his History became a link in a chain that both sides kept using, even while one side cursed him. And he could only sit at Chalcedon by cursing his friend.

**Quotes (renderings):**
1. "I will not agree to what was done against you, unjustly and wickedly, not even if someone cuts off both my hands. Without doubt, God's grace works with me and strengthens my weak soul." From the damaged Latin in Mansi, confirmed by Jackson's 1892 English.
2. "The form of God was not changed into the form of a servant. It stayed what it was, and took the form of the servant." From Jackson's 1892 English; the Greek is not held.
3. "First he ordered it cut six cubits high, then twelve, after that twenty-two, and now thirty-six. For he longs to fly up to heaven, and to be set free from this life on earth."
