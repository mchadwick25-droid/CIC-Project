## The four lines

- **Who:** Theodoret, a fifth-century bishop of the small town of Cyrrhus, a leading voice of Antioch's side, and a historian of the church and of the Syrian monks.
- **What happened:** He answered Cyril's twelve curses and stood with John of Antioch's side at Ephesus. After the peace he still refused to accept that Nestorius had been rightly removed. He was confined, then deposed in his absence in 449, and at Chalcedon in 451 he got his church back only after he cursed Nestorius.
- **What it was about:** How God and a man are one in Christ without either being mixed away. God stayed God and took on a human life, so Mary is Mother of God because of the union. His accusers charged that he split Christ into two sons.
- **Why the connection matters:** His Church History became a link in the church's one continuing story, carried on by both sides of the split over Chalcedon, even by the side that called him an enemy. What he wrote against Cyril was condemned in 553.
