# Review: Theodoret of Cyrrhus

Brief reviewed: `theodoret-of-cyrrhus.brief.txt`, 817 words by the scorer.

## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Theodoret, a fifth-century bishop of Cyrrhus, a small town in northern Syria. He was a leading voice of Antioch's teaching that Christ is one, God and man, unmixed, and he was also a historian.
2. **What happened:** he answered Cyril's twelve curses and stood with John of Antioch's side at Ephesus in 431. He accepted the peace of 433 but not Nestorius's removal. In 449 he was confined to his town and then deposed in his absence. At Chalcedon in 451 he was given back his see only after he cursed Nestorius, his friend, by name.
3. **Why it matters:** his Church History carried the church's story on from Eusebius, and historians on both sides of the split over Chalcedon carried it on from him. His Religious History kept the Syrian monks, Simeon on his pillar among them. A century later a council condemned his writings against Cyril.

All three lines can be given after one hearing.

**Essence, from the census and what he is known for:** the Antiochene answer to Cyril, the price paid for it in 449 and at Chalcedon, and the two histories. **Also told:** what he held (Quote 2, Mother of God), his work as bishop in his own words, the Church History and the Religious History, the confinement and deposition, the curse at Chalcedon, and what came of him (the continuous history, the hostile memory, 553). Every part is present and given its weight.

**Hook and first minute.** The hook is the vow to Nestorius, in his own words. The essence is complete at about 140 words ("Simeon among them"). Pass.

**Told once, in order.** The life runs once, in order, from the monastery to his death. One soft spot: the hook's first sentence ("had found Cyril of Alexandria's writings free of heresy") is the same acceptance that beat 3 tells again ("found that Cyril's letter agreed with his own faith"). A hook may reach ahead, but this one gives the listener the same finding twice, with no clue that it is the same event, before they know who Cyril is. MINOR. Cut 1 below removes it.

**Ending.** It ends on the person: his own words to Leo ("not a tomb") and his death in the emperor Leo's reign. "To Leo he had written" steps back to 449, but it is the outline's chosen ending in his own words, not the ending told early. Pass.

**Names heard once.** Paul, Arius, Sozomen, Socrates, Marcion, Gennadius and Constantine each come up once. Each is a link or a plain reference, and none loses the listener. No finding.

**Where I got lost.** "Because of the union, not because she bore God by nature" (beat 2) is the hardest sentence on one hearing: "the union" is not yet explained. Cut 6 removes the second half. Nothing reads as a children's book, and nothing talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** The narrator sounds like an adult documentary voice, and his own words (the vow, the answer to Cyril, the pillar, "not a tomb") carry the story. A few credit chains read like a report ("Its acts, his opponents' record, say").
- **Craft: 4.** It has a strong hook, the essence lands in the first minute, and the Chalcedon scene is well built: the offer, the shouting, the curse, "a curse on his friend". Against that, the hook repeats beat 3, and the brief runs 142 words over the approved length.
- **Rigor: 3.** Every quote is verbatim, the Do not say list is honoured, and the witnesses are credited (his own letters, the acts, the translator, the chronicle). But two details go past their sources: the baths and the year 448 (B1, B2).
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 8.1 and reading ease 78.2 are well inside the limits, and the glosses are worked into the sentences. The Mother of God sentence is hard to follow on one hearing.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING. "He built bridges, baths and a channel."** Letter 81 (NP3 31507-31511) says: "I erected public porticoes; I built two large bridges; I looked after the public baths. ... I built the conduit". He looked after the baths. He did not build them. Change: cut 2 below ("he built bridges and a water channel for the town").
- Do not say, add: "Not 'he built baths': he says he 'looked after the public baths'. He built bridges and the conduit (NP3 31507-31511)."

**B2. BLOCKING. "In 448 an order from the emperor kept Theodoret in Cyrrhus."** No held line gives a year for the order. Jackson (NP3 2912-2916) puts the edict a year after the deposition of Irenaeus (17 February 448), which points to 449. The sheet's own heading gives "448-449". The outline left room for this. Change: cut 10 below, which gives the order no year.
- Do not say, add: "No year for the order confining him to Cyrrhus, and not 'in 448': no held line dates it. Say 'an order from the emperor then kept him in Cyrrhus'."

**M1. MINOR. The hook repeats beat 3.** See the test above. Fixed by cut 1.

**M2. MINOR. The Mother of God sentence.** Cut 6 shortens it. The reviser adds nothing to it.

**L1. Length (the batch's ruling).** At 817 words the brief is 142 over the 675 approved. The cuts below bring it to 671.
