# Batch 10: what-and-why scan (2026-11-23)

The question for every script: does the listener hear what each accusation, book, condemnation, quarrel or exile was *about*, or only that it happened? The scan reads each final `<slug>.brief.txt` once, as a listener would. It then checks the story's `Outline.md` and `Source_Sheet.md` for the missing answer. Chrysostom and Ephrem were approved before the kit, so they have no Outline or Source Sheet. For those two the check used `Review.md`, `Fresh_Read.md`, the card, and the vendored texts the reviewer read. Line refs are to the file named. "SS" means Source_Sheet.md. Nothing in the scripts was changed.

---

## council-of-ephesus-431

1. "Nestorius would not simply call her that" — **why not?** The listener hears Cyril's reason, not Nestorius's. **Held:** SS E1 says he found one party calling Mary Mother of God and another mother of a man, and urged "Mother of Christ" as a word everyone could accept (BH 6927-6948). The Outline (beat 1) sends it to the sibling: "the name's history is the `nestorius` story's"; Leave out: "One clause on the name only."
2. "It charged that Cyril's twelve curses ... held old heresies" — **which ones?** **Held:** SS E10 has John's sentence, which names "your Apollinarian, Arian, and Eunomian heresies" (ACTS 17886-17903). The Outline beat 4 paraphrases it down to "old heresies". This was a writing choice, not a cut.
3. "upheld the condemnation of Pelagius and his followers" — **for what?** Not held in this story. It is a one-clause link to `pelagius` (Outline beat 5; SS E14). Cut first 11 also sent "made earlier at Rome" to the card.

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** The spine (Mother of God, and how God and man are one) is clear. Nestorius's own side of it is missing.

## eutyches

1. "Flavian's meeting stripped him ... for reviving, in his judges' words, old heresies" — **which?** **Held:** SS line 61 has Flavian's sentence: "infected with the false doctrines of Valentinus and Apollinarius" (Perry 23978-23992). The Outline (Who tells it) credits "the charge of reviving old heresies" but never carries its content.
2. "Nor would he curse the fathers who, he said, had rejected that phrase" — **which phrase?** Heard aloud, "that phrase" has no clear referent. **Held:** "two natures" (SS line 52; Leo 4560-4561). The brief's wording just leaves it ambiguous.
3. "A bishop who had judged him denied a sentence the record gave as his own" — **what sentence?** That sentence was the case against one nature. **Held:** SS line 76: "if you do not speak of Two Natures after the Union, you speak of mixture and of a confusing" (Perry 23860-23877). The Outline beat 4 reduces it to "a bishop denied his own words". Not cut, never carried.
4. "Leo, answering in a long letter ... called the Tome" / "received Leo's letter against Eutyches's teaching" — **what did Leo teach?** Sent to the sibling: the Outline says `leo-of-rome` "tells the Tome ... the Tome's content is not retold." Cut first 5 also moved What came 2 (the Tome framing two councils) to Leave out.
5. "Flavian deposed" in 449 — **why?** Only implied. **Held:** SS line 77 has the council's shout, "Whoever affirms Two Natures let him be Anathematized"; the Outline beat 4 has the emperors' letter blaming Flavian's pressing of the case (Perry 23250-23252). Neither reached the brief.
6. "he would not confess it shared our nature" sits against the earlier "a human body taken from the Virgin". Those are two sides' accounts, but the listener is not told what the difference is. **Held:** SS line 55 (Zach 1167-1172: he "rejected the truth of the body derived from the Virgin", with the rain and ice images).

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** His own sentence is clear. What his judges thought was wrong with it (mixture, Valentinus and Apollinarius) is held but never said.

## theodoret-of-cyrrhus

1. "He answered Cyril of Alexandria's twelve curses" — **what was wrong with them, to him?** Quote 2 gives his positive faith but not his charge. **Held:** SS E3: Cyril renews "that vain and impious teaching of Apollinarius" (NP3 36107-36109). The Outline beat 2 itself says "add no account of what it taught". **Cut first 7** then sent even the Apollinarius point to the card.
2. "letters he wrote to answer his accusers" — **accused of what?** Never stated. **Held:** SS E8: the 449 indictment, he "has ventured to hold tenets and write in opposition to ... Cyril" (P449 15581-15603). SS E11: at Chalcedon the cry "Cast out the teacher of Nestorius". The Outline beat 6 kept that cry, and Cut first 16 explicitly kept it, but the final brief has only "cried that he was a heretic". It was lost in writing or the fresh-read trims.
3. "An order from the emperor then kept him in Cyrrhus, with no charge" — **why?** **Held:** SS E7 gives his own stated reason: "cause there is none, except the fact of my convening an episcopal synod" (Letter 81). It is not in the Outline beat. Leave out also drops who procured the order ("only his translator").
4. "he would not accept that Nestorius had been rightly removed" — **why not?** Held only as "unjustly and wickedly" (Letter 172, SS E6). The sources hold no fuller reason.

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** His theology is well told. What his enemies charged him with, and his objection to Cyril, are held but cut or dropped.

## pelagius

1. "He said the statements drawn from Caelestius were not his, and condemned them" — **what statements?** **Held:** SS line 57 (Vincent: Caelestius "denied that the whole human race is involved in the guilt of Adam's sin"). The Outline sends them to **Leave out**: "Caelestius's propositions on Adam and infants ... his story, and `paulinus-of-milan`'s" (Outline line 117). Do not say (line 107) bars giving them to Pelagius. Defensible, but the listener hears an accusation with its content withheld.
2. "two bishops from Gaul charged him in writing" — **with what?** The same charges as item 1, under the same Leave out.
3. "Cassian ... charged that the bishop of Constantinople taught what the Pelagians had taught" — **what?** **Held:** SS line 161 (Cassian, Against Nestorius 1.3: Christ lived "as a mere man without any stain of sin", so men too "could be without sin"). Not carried here. The brief half-supplies it elsewhere ("live without sin").

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** The central quarrel (grace as help to the will, not just teaching and forgiveness) is the clearest in the batch. Only the charges at Diospolis are withheld.

## john-cassian

1. "his thirteenth Conference ... drew an attack from Prosper" / "Prosper had rightly blamed him" — **for what exactly?** The brief quotes Chaeremon, but never says which point drew the attack. **Held:** SS lines 110-111 (XIII.8, XIII.11: a good will that "arose from our own efforts"; Zacchaeus and the thief came "by their own desires"). Prosper's book is **not held**; only Gibson's summary (SS line 135, twelve propositions). The Outline Do not say rightly bars "semi-Pelagian", but nothing replaces it with the point at issue.
2. "his teaching on God's choosing ran against the Fathers" — **what teaching?** **Held:** SS line 118 (Prosper, Ep. 225.2: "de vocatione electorum secundum dei propositum", the calling of the chosen by God's purpose). The brief carries no plain sense of it.
3. "Nestorius ... whose teaching on Christ was under attack" / "Cassian tied that error to Pelagius's followers" — **what error?** **Held:** SS lines 127-128 ("born a mere man"). The Outline gloss for Nestorius chose only to name the topic.

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** The listener hears both sides' words but not the point they disagreed on.

## john-chrysostom (no Outline or Source Sheet; approved before the kit)

1. "He rebuked men in office too. Socrates says that turned the tide against him" — **rebuked for what?** Never said. Socrates VI.3-5 is held (`npnf202` 17128-17384) and the reviewer read it.
2. "Theophilus ... cited John to appear ... deposed him for refusing" — **cited on what charges?** Behind the grudge: **held** in Socrates VI.7-15 (`npnf202` ~18206-18300), the quarrel over Origen's books and the monks Theophilus drove out. At the Oak, "no mention was now made of Origen, but all were intent on urging a variety of criminations, many of which were ridiculous." The card mentions Theophilus condemning Origen, but no sheet researched this for the brief.
3. "After a clash with the empress Eudoxia, a second council silenced him" — **a clash over what?** **Held:** Socrates VI.18 (`npnf202` 18422-18460): her silver statue by the church, the games held at it, his attack on those who allowed them, and the sermon "Again Herodias raves". `Review.md` cites exactly these lines as checked, but the brief and the card (`briefReferences`: "the clash with Eudoxia") carry no content.
4. "in 404 an imperial decree sent him into exile" — **on what ground?** It follows from the second council, but neither the brief nor the card says what that council judged.

**Verdict: MANY GAPS.** Each step of his fall is told as an event without its cause. The causes sit in a vendored text the reviewer had open, but were never researched into the story.

## rufinus-of-aquileia

1. "Origen ... much admired and by now much attacked" — **attacked for what?** **Not held.** No line in the Source Sheet or Outline states which of Origen's teachings were disputed. The nearest is Anastasius's "his impious works" (SS line 100) and the preface's "against the truth about the Trinity".
2. "In the 390s he and Jerome took opposite sides in a dispute over Origen" — **about what?** Held only in outline form: SS E5 has Epiphanius's prayer that Rufinus be freed "from the heresy of Origen", and Fremantle's line that Jerome sided with Epiphanius and Rufinus with John of Jerusalem. Leave out sends Epiphanius beyond one clause to the card.
3. "When the monks there were persecuted, he says, he was jailed" — **by whom, why?** **Held:** Outline Words to explain (Valens's order against the monks who held to Nicaea, N202 13725-13727). **Cut first 9** sent the gloss to Leave out.
4. "the emperors had banned reading Origen" — **why?** Not held beyond the decree's text (SS line 100).

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** The friendship quarrel (honest translation or not) is clear. What made Origen dangerous was never researched.

## frumentius

1. "the emperor Constantius ... demanding the same teaching in their churches as in the empire's" / "Athanasius driven from his city" / "a man guilty of countless crimes" — **what teaching, and why was Athanasius driven out?** The Arian quarrel is never named or stated. **Not held as content.** The Outline beat 7 and SS carry the letter and George, but no gloss of the teaching at stake. The "same doctrine" line was set aside as a quote already on other cards (Outline Quotes note), and nothing replaced it with plain content.

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** One gap, but it is the story's only conflict.

## mesrop-mashtots

1. "He fought the Borborites, a sect Koriun calls rash and insolent" — **what did they teach?** Held only as an editor's note (second-century Gnostics who denied the Last Judgment, T2 3814-3816). The Outline withholds it on purpose: "Give only that; the editor's note ... is the editor's." The listener hears torture and burning over a teaching never stated.
2. "a man it had shut out, who came with heretical books" — **whose books, saying what?** Identity: "[de Mopsueste]" is the translator's bracket, barred by Do not say. Content: **not held.**

**Verdict: SOME GAPS.** The letters' purpose is clear. Both quarrels are told without their content.

## ulfila

1. "the emperor ordered Ulfila to Constantinople for a debate" — **about what?** Held only as a damaged, editor-restored name ("Pneumatomachi"). Do not say bars it (Outline line 94).
2. "a godless Gothic judge raised the persecution" — why is not in any held source (Auxentius gives no cause).

**Verdict: CLEAR.** The creed in dispute is told in plain terms (the Son "like" the Father against Nicaea's "of one being"), and so is the quarrel over when he took it. Both gaps are minor and unanswerable from the record.

## priscillian

1. "Other bishops accused him" — **of what?** The script never says what the bishops charged. **Held:** SS line 33 (Prosper: he founded a heresy "ex Manicheorum et gnosticorum dogmate"). SS 3.7, line 66: the rescript was obtained "against pseudo-bishops and Manichaeans". Neither is used.
2. "Sulpicius called the teaching the heresy of the Gnostics, a heresy of secret knowledge" — **what teaching?** The gloss names a category, not a content. **Held:** Orosius's report that the soul comes down through circles and is caught by evil powers and thrust into bodies (SS 4.9, line 97) went to **Leave out: card**. Sulpicius's genealogy from Marcus of Memphis (SS line 48) went to **Leave out: card** ("a hostile genealogy").
3. "Bishops met at Saragossa ... condemned in his absence" — **condemned for what?** **Not held**: SS 3.2 gives the sentence but not its charge.
4. "In it he wrote what he and his friends had always urged" / "the book Priscillian had carried to Damasus" — **what was the book arguing?** The brief gives the ascetic call, the creed, and the curse on Mani, but not that the book is a defence against a charge of Manichaeism. **Held:** the Outline's Opening 1 says "when he went there to clear his name" (Pr 4672-4676), and SS 3.7 has his own account of the false petition. The brief drops "to clear his name", so the listener does not learn why the book curses Mani.
5. "an order from the emperor that drove all heretics" — **which heretics?** **Held:** SS line 66 ("against pseudo-bishops and Manichaeans"). Not used.
6. "a council at Braga still cursed teachings it called his" — **which?** **Held, and in the Outline itself:** What came 4 says "among them that souls sinned in heaven and were cast down into bodies" (Bruns 2513-2515). Cut first 1 removed only the 407 law, yet the brief dropped the content. It was lost in writing or trimming, not cut.
7. "Leo ... wrote to Spain against the Priscillianists" / "this unholy folly" — **what folly?** Leo's Letter 15 is held (SS 4.11) but only for the praise of the sword.
8. "Its bishops above all quarreled for fifteen years" — **over what?** It is only half-implied ("the deaths split the other side"). **Held:** SS 4.5 (the accusers cut off from communion for his death). **Cut first 2** sent Prosper's line to the card.

**Verdict: MANY GAPS.** The listener learns he was accused, condemned, and remembered as a heretic, without hearing one thing he was said to teach. Every answer except Saragossa's charge is held. Most were set aside because only hostile witnesses report them.

**Pattern.**
- **Hostile witnesses.** The largest share of missing answers was set aside on purpose because only an opponent or an editor reports them: Priscillian's teaching (Orosius, Sulpicius's genealogy, Braga), the Borborites, Theodore's books. The Outlines treat "only the enemy says it" as a reason to leave the content out, when it could be told and credited to the enemy.
- **Sent to the card or a sibling.** Contents went out by Cut first, or to the story that "owns" them: the Apollinarius charge, Caelestius's propositions, the Tome, Nestorius's reason, the Valens gloss. Each move is local and reasonable, but together they empty the conflicts.
- **Held in the Outline but lost in writing or trimming.** Braga's "souls sinned in heaven", "the teacher of Nestorius", the content of Eutyches's "old heresies" and of Basil's sentence. Writers and fresh readers kept the event and dropped the clause that said what it was about.
- **Never researched.** The two pre-kit stories (Chrysostom most of all) and the doctrinal background the Outlines assumed: what Origen was attacked for, what the Arian teaching was in Frumentius. The kit's beats ask for what happened and who says so, but no field asks "what was it about?", so no check catches the gap. The one exception is Pelagius's "What he defended", which is why that conflict reads clearest.
