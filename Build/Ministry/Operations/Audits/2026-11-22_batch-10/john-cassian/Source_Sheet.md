## 1. Setting

**S1. Name.** Own words call him John in Egypt; Gennadius calls him only Cassianus. Editor's note on the names.
- N211 15037-15040, editor (Gibson): "In his own writings he represents himself as addressed by the monks in Egypt more than once by the name of John. Conference XIV. ix.; Institute V. xxxv."
- N203 43243, later (c. 480, about 50 years): "Cassianus, ... Scythian by race". Richardson's footnote in the same line ("Johannes Cassianus died 450.") is the editor's, not Gennadius's.
- MOM 56423-56427, **late (fifteenth-century interpolation)**: "lohannes nionachus cognomento Cassianus / Massiliae insignis et facundus scriptor / habetur." Mommsen prints this under "VI. INTERPOLATIONES PROSPERI CHRONICIS INSERTAE SAEC. XV." (56178-56179), and warns these additions have often misled (56181-56190: "ne fallerent deinceps, ut adhuc saepe fefellerunt"). **Finding:** Gibson (N211 15040-15042) gives "Joannes cognomento Cassianus" as Prosper's, from "Chronicle." In the held edition that line is a fifteenth-century interpolation, not Prosper's. Do not credit Prosper with the double name.
- Confidence: "John" in Egypt, Documented (own words). The census spelling is **John Cassian**.

**S2. Birth and origin.** Contested. Gennadius says "Scythian by race"; Gibson doubts it.
- N203 43243, later: "Scythian by race".
- N211 15025-15028, editor: "it appears to the present writer to be the most probable view that Cassian was of Western origin, and, perhaps, a native of Provence, although it must be freely acknowledged that it is impossible to speak with certainty on this subject."
- N211 15062, editor: "Various considerations point to the date of his birth as about the year 360."
- N211 15063-15064, editor: "Of his family we know nothing, except that in one passage of his writings he incidentally makes mention of a sister".
- Confidence: birth about 360, Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Gibson). Origin, Contested.

**S3. Monastery at Bethlehem with Germanus.** Own words.
- N211 36924-36925, own (Conf. XI.5, Germanus speaking): "we made haste to come here after our first beginning in the monastery of Bethlehem".
- N211 19691-19696, own (Inst. IV.31): "he sought out our own monastery ... which was at no great distance from the cave ... in which our Lord vouchsafed to be born of a virgin."
- N211 36779-36782, own (Conf. XI.1): "When we were living in a monastery in Syria after our first infancy in the faith, and when after we had grown somewhat we had begun to long for some greater grace of perfection, we determined straightway to seek Egypt". (Cassian's "Syria" here covers Palestine; Gibson's note at 15073-15078 rules out Jerome's house.)
- Confidence: Documented (own words).

**S4. Dates of the Egyptian years.** Editor's reckoning.
- N211 15408-15412, editor: "it is certain that Cassian was actually in Egypt at the time of the Anthropomorphite controversy raised by the letters of Theophilus in 399; and, as the other notices of events previously mentioned coincide very fairly with this, we cannot be far wrong in placing the two visits to Egypt between 380 and 400."
- Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

**S5. Marseilles and Gaul.** Gennadius: presbyter at Marseilles.
- N203 43244-43246, later: "a presbyter at Marseilles, founded two monasteries, that is to say one for men and one for women, which are still standing."
- N211 16433-16435, own (Inst. pref.): "you are anxious that the institutions of the East and especially of Egypt should be established in your province, which is at present without monasteries".
- Tradition's spoken name (census `gallic-monastic-ascetic-christianity`): shortName "Gallic Monasticism"; worldName "The Monk-Bishops of Gaul"; dates c. 360-450; place "Tours, Lérins, Marseilles".

## 2. Events

**E1. Egypt: the promise and the seven years.** Own words.
- N211 41027-41030, own (Conf. XVII.2, Germanus): "our promise given to the Elders does not allow us to choose what is helpful. ... were it not that the terms of our promise compelled us to return at once to the monastery."
- N211 42239-42249, own (Conf. XVII.30): "made up our minds to stop in Egypt. ... yet when seven years were over we were very glad to fulfil it. For we hastened to our monastery ... we entirely removed the sting of our broken promise and returned to the recesses of the desert of Scete, as they themselves forwarded us with joy."
- N211 36789-36790, own (Conf. XI.1): "we came by a very lengthy voyage to a town of Egypt named Thennesus".
- Confidence: Documented (own words; no second witness).

**E2. What the Conferences are, and the doubt on them.**
- N211 15403-15407, editor: "the Conferences were not written till many years later, and it is impossible to determine with certainty how far they really represent the discourses actually spoken by the Egyptian Fathers, or how far they are the ideal compositions of Cassian himself."
- Confidence: that the talks are Cassian's telling, Documented; how close to the elders' real words, Contested (Gibson's judgment).

**E3. Deacon at Constantinople under John Chrysostom.**
- N211 55139-55145, own (On the Incarnation VII.31, written 430): "although I cannot claim a place as Teacher among those illustrious Bishops of Constantinople, yet venture to claim the zeal and enthusiasm of a disciple. For I was admitted into the sacred ministry by the Bishop John, of blessed memory, and offered to God, and even though I am absent in body yet I am still there in heart".
- PET 184-185 and 510-511 (Petschenig's prolegomena quoting it, clean): "adoptatus a beatissimae memoriae Iohanne episcopo in ministerium sacrum atque oblatus deo"; PET 28325-28327 (the text itself, damaged OCR): "adoptatus enim a beatissimae incmoriuc lohaune cpiscopo in ministeriuin sacruin atquo ohlatus dco, ctsi corpore ahsuiu, alfectu illic suin" [adoptatus enim a beatissimae memoriae Iohanne episcopo in ministerium sacrum atque oblatus deo, etsi corpore absum, affectu illic sum].
- N203 43244, later: "ordained deacon by bishop John the Great, at Constantinople".
- Confidence: Documented (own words plus Gennadius). Cassian does not say "deacon" or "Chrysostom"; Gennadius supplies "deacon"; the context (bishops of Constantinople, VII.30 at N211 55117-55122) makes the John Chrysostom.

**E4. Bearer of the clergy's letters to Rome after John's exile (c. 404-405).** Two contemporaries.
- PAL 2640-2654, contemporary (Palladius, c. 408): "After Palladius arrived Germanus, a priest, and with him Cassianus, a deacon, of John's party, both discreet men, presenting letters from the whole of John's clergy. They wrote that their Church had been subjected to violence and tyranny; their bishop had been expelled with the help of the military, and sent into exile ... They also presented a receipt, showing that they had deposited with the magistrates, as witnesses to the deed, ... valuables in gold, silver, and clothing, by way of clearing Bishop John of the charges laid against him."
- PG47 8194-8195, Greek of the same: "Γερμανὸς πρεσδύτερος ἅμα Κασιανῷ διαχόνῳ κῶν Ἰωάννου, ἄνδρες εὐλαδεῖς" [Γερμανὸς πρεσβύτερος ἅμα Κασιανῷ διακόνῳ τῶν Ἰωάννου, ἄνδρες εὐλαβεῖς].
- N202 44326-44330, contemporary (Innocent's reply, kept by Sozomen): "From the letters of your love that you forwarded to me through Germanus, the presbyter, and Cassianus, the deacon, I have learned, with anxious solicitude, the scenes of evil which you have placed before our eyes."
- PAL 2661-2669 is Moore's footnote (editor), not Palladius.
- Confidence: the mission, Documented. That this deacon Cassianus is our Cassian, Widely Accepted (Gibson, N211 15421-15427; matching names, friend and order).

**E5. The two houses at Marseilles.**
- N203 43244-43246 (above). Gibson's "built it is said over the tomb of S. Victor" (N211 15479-15480) is hearsay in the editor; leave out.
- No held source dates the founding. Gibson places his settling "about the same time" as Lérins, 410 (N211 15449, 15468-15469): editor, Inferential-Thin.

**E6. The Institutes, for Castor.** Own words; date the editor's.
- N211 16443-16448, own: "you charge me to declare, although with inexpert pen, the customs of the monasteries which we have seen observed throughout Egypt and Palestine, as they were there delivered to us by the Fathers; not looking for graceful speech, ... but wanting the simple life of holy men to be told in simple language to the brethren in your new monastery."
- N211 16503-16514, own: "Nor certainly shall I try to weave a tale of God's miracles and signs, although we have not only heard of many such among our elders, and those past belief, but have also seen them fulfilled under our very eyes; yet, leaving out all these things which minister to the reader nothing but astonishment and no instruction in the perfect life, I shall try ... faithfully to explain only their institutions and the rules of their monasteries, and especially the origin and causes of the principal faults, of which they reckon eight, and the remedies for them ... since my purpose is to say a few words not about God's miracles, but about the way to improve our character".
- PET 7949-7962, Latin of the same: "nec plane mirabilium dei signorumque narrationem studebo contexere ... propositum siquidem mihi est non de mirabilibus dei, sed de correctione morum nostrorum et consummatione uitae perfectae secundum ea, quae a senioribus nostris accepimus, pauca disserere."
- N211 16526-16534, own: "where I find anything in the rule of the Egyptians which, either because of the severity of the climate, or owing to some difficulty or diversity of habits, is impossible in these countries, or hard and difficult, I shall to some extent balance it by the customs of the monasteries which are found throughout Pontus and Mesopotamia; because, if due regard be paid to what things are possible, there is the same perfection in the observance although the power may be unequal."
- PET 7996-8003, Latin: "ut ea, quae secundum Aegyptiorum regulam seu pro asperitate aerum seu pro difficultate ac diuersitate morum inpossibilia in his regionibus uel dura uel ardua conprobauero, institutis monasteriorum, quae per Palaestinam uel Mesopotamiam habentur, aliquatenus temperem, quia, si rationabilis possibilium mensura seruetur, eadem obseruantiae perfectio est etiam in inpari facultate." (Petschenig has Palestine and Mesopotamia; Gibson's English has Pontus and Mesopotamia. Follow the Latin.)
- N211 15519-15526, editor: Castor, "Bishop of Apta Julia ... As Castor died in 426 ... it must have been written some time between the years 419 and 426."
- N203 43249-43254, later (Gennadius's list): "On dress, also On the canon of prayers, and the Usage in the saying of Psalms ... three books. One of Institutes, eight books On the origin, nature and remedies for the eight principal sins, a book on each sin."
- Confidence: the request and contents, Documented. Dates, Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

**E7. The Conferences, in three parts.** Own words.
- N211 25881-25898, own (Part I pref.): the promise to Castor kept; "the aforesaid Bishop has left us and departed to Christ"; ten Conferences dedicated to Leontius and Helladius.
- N211 25945-25952, own: "And so may your prayers gain from Him, Who has deemed us worthy both to see them and to learn from them and to dwell with them, that He will vouchsafe to grant us a perfect recollection of their teaching, and a ready tongue to tell it, that we may explain them as beautifully and as exactly as we received them from them and may succeed in setting before you the men themselves incorporated, as it were, in their own Institutes, and what is more to the point, speaking in the Latin tongue."
- N211 36717-36737, own (Part II pref.): "you, O holy brothers Honoratus and Eucherius, ... one of you, presiding as he does over a large monastery of the brethren, is hoping that his congregation ... may be instructed in the precepts of those fathers, while the other has been anxious to make his way to Egypt to be edified by the sight of these in the flesh, that he might leave this province that is frozen as it were with the cold of Gaul ... I should not decline the danger and peril of writing, if only to the one there may be added authority among his children, and from the other may be removed the necessity for so risky a journey."
- N211 36750-36752, own: "seven other Conferences, which are to be sent to the holy brethren living in the islands of the Stœchades".
- N211 15544-15569, editor: the three parts completed "between the years 426 and 428".
- Latin of the Conferences is not held: the quotes rest on Gibson's English of 1894.
- Confidence: Documented (own words); dates, Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

**E8. Conference XIII: grace and free will.** Own words (in the mouth of Abbot Chaeremon, as Cassian writes him).
- N211 37489-37500, own (XIII.1): Germanus troubled that the old man "had by the addition of a single sentence broken down the claims of man's exertions, adding that man even though he strive with all his might for a good result, yet cannot become master of what is good unless he has acquired it simply by the gift of Divine bounty".
- N211 37572-37578, own (XIII.3): "the initiative not only of our actions but also of good thoughts comes from God, who inspires us with a good will to begin with".
- N211 37833-37837, own (XIII.8): "And when He sees in us some beginnings of a good will, He at once enlightens it and strengthens it and urges it on towards salvation, increasing that which He Himself implanted or which He sees to have arisen from our own efforts."
- N211 37995-37999, own (XIII.11): "But if we say that the beginning of our free will is always due to the inspiration of the grace of God, what about the faith of Zaccheus, or what are we to say of the goodness of the thief on the cross, who by their own desires brought violence to bear on the kingdom of heaven".
- N211 38028-38034, own (XIII.11): "These two then; viz., the grace of God and free will seem opposed to each other, but really are in harmony, and we gather from the system of goodness that we ought to have both alike, lest if we withdraw one of them from man, we may seem to have broken the rule of the Church's faith: for when God sees us inclined to will what is good, He meets, guides, and strengthens us".
- N211 38582-38591, own (XIII.18): "now He puts into us the very beginnings of salvation, and gives to each the zeal of his free will; ... and assists some who are already willing and running, while He draws others who are unwilling and resisting, and forces them to a good will."
- N211 15684-15691, editor: "it is impossible to doubt that the thirteenth Conference ... was intended to meet what he evidently regarded as a serious error; viz., the implicit denial by the Augustinians of the need of effort on man's part." (Editor's reading of purpose.)
- Confidence: what the text says, Documented. Its purpose and how far it parts from Augustine, Contested.

**E9. Marseilles reported to Augustine (429).** Contemporary.
- GOLD 16932-16936, contemporary (Prosper to Augustine, Ep. 225.2): "Multi ergo seruorum Christi. qui in Massiliensi urbe consistunt, in sanctitatis tuae scriptis, quae aduersus Pelagianos haereticos condidisti, contrarium putant patrum opinioni et ecclesiastico sensui, quicquid in eis de uocatione electorum secundum dei propositum disputasti." (Many servants of Christ living at Marseilles think that what you argued on the calling of the chosen runs against the Fathers and the church's mind.)
- N211 15721-15724, editor: "though Cassian's name is never mentioned in the correspondence, yet it is easy to read between the lines and see that he is referred to." Editor's inference.
- N211 15700-15701, editor: Prosper "also a layman and an ardent Augustinian".
- Confidence: the report, Documented. That it means Cassian, Inferential-Thin to Widely Accepted (Gibson).

**E10. On the Incarnation, against Nestorius (430), at Leo's request.**
- N211 48484-48487, own (preface): "you have overcome my determination and purpose by your commendable earnestness and most urgent affection, my dear Leo, my esteemed and highly regarded friend, ornament that you are of the Roman Church and sacred ministry".
- N211 48477-48484, own: on finishing the Conferences he "had contemplated and almost determined on taking refuge in silence".
- N203 43268-43272, later: "and finally at the request of Leo the archdeacon, afterwards bishop of Rome, he wrote seven books against Nestorius, On the incarnation of the Lord, and writing this, made an end, both of writing and living, at Marseilles, in the reign of Theodosius and Valentinianus."
- N211 48713-48718, own (I.3): "that heresy ... which sprang from the error of Pelagius; viz., that in saying that Jesus Christ had lived as a mere man without any stain of sin, they actually went so far as to declare that men could also be without sin if they liked."
- N211 48738-48741, own: "this new author of a heresy that is not new, who declares that our Lord and Saviour was born a mere man, observes that he says exactly the same thing which the Pelagians said before him".
- N211 15599-15603, editor: "published in 430, before the Council of Ephesus (for Cassian speaks of Nestorius throughout as still Bishop of Constantinople)".
- N211 15620-15622, editor: "to Cassian belongs the credit of having been the first to point it out." Editor's claim; leave out.
- Confidence: Leo's request, Documented (own words and Gennadius). Date 430, Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

**E11. Prosper's book against him (432 or later).** Prosper's book is **not held**. Its title and Gennadius's report are.
- MOM 42762-42764, editor (Mommsen listing Prosper's works, with the book's transmitted title): "contra collatorem qui dicitur sive 'pro defensione sancti Aurelii Augustini Hipponensis episcopi contra Cassiani presbyteri librum qui titulo de protectione dei praenotatur'." (Against the Conferencer, or "In defence of Augustine ... against the book of Cassian the presbyter that is headed On the Protection of God".)
- N211 15774-15785, editor: "Prosper's work 'Contra Collatorem,' against the author of the Conferences, a treatise of considerable power and force, although not scrupulously fair. ... his antagonist never once names him directly, but merely speaks of him as a man of priestly rank who surpassed all his companions in power of arguing. The work consists of an examination of the thirteenth Conference ... from which Prosper extracts twelve propositions, the first of which he says is orthodox while all the others are erroneous."
- N211 15905-15907, editor: date "not earlier than 432; for Celestine only died in April in that year."
- N211 15909-15912, editor: "Cassian was evidently still living when this attack upon him was made; but, so far as we know, he made no reply to it. Its publication is the last event in his life of which we have any knowledge."
- Confidence: the attack, Documented (Gennadius, the title). Its contents and date, Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Gibson, from a text not held). No reply known, Inferential-Thin (argument from silence).

**E12. Death.**
- N203 43270-43272 (above): "made an end, both of writing and living, at Marseilles, in the reign of Theodosius and Valentinianus."
- N211 15912-15916, editor: "He probably died shortly afterwards".
- N203 43243, Richardson's note: "died 450" (editor). Census dates "c. 360-432" (end year approximate).
- Confidence: place Documented; year unknown. Say so.

## 3. Impact, heaviest first

**I1. Benedict's Rule has his books read to the monks (sixth century, about a century later).**
- RB 6838-6841, Latin (ch. 42): "mox ut surrexerint a coena, sedeant omnes in unum, et legat unus Collationes, vel Vitas Patrum, aut certe aliquid quod aedificet audientes".
- RB 6844-6846: "Si autem jejunii dies fuerit, dicta Vespera, parvo intervallo, mox accedant ad lectionem Collationum, ut diximus".
- RB 6880-6886, English: "let them sit down all together as soon as they have risen from supper ... and let one of them read the 'Conferences' [of Cassian], or the lives of the Fathers, or something else which may edify the hearers." The bracket "[of Cassian]" is Hunter Blair's; the Rule says only "Collationes".
- RB 6892-6893: "four or five pages being read, or as much as time alloweth".
- RB 10409-10412, Latin (ch. 73): "Nec non et Collationes Patrum, et Instituta et Vitae eorum ; sed et Regula sancti Patris nostri Basilii, quid aliud sunt, nisi bene viventium et obedientium monachorum instrumenta virtutum ?"
- RB 10444-10448, English: "Moreover, the Conferences of the Fathers, their Institutes and their Lives, and the Rule of our holy Father Basil — what are these but the instruments whereby well-living and obedient monks attain to virtue?"

- Confidence: the reading orders, Documented. That "Collationes" and "Instituta" are Cassian's books, Widely Accepted (titles match; the Rule names no author).

## 5. Where the sources disagree or come late

- **Origin:** Gennadius "Scythian"; Gibson a Westerner, perhaps Provençal; no certainty (S2).
- **Prosper's "Joannes cognomento Cassianus":** Gibson's attribution rests on a fifteenth-century interpolation in the held print (S1).
- **Cassiodorus's wording:** Gibson "frequenter audite"; print "libenter audite" (I2).
- **Institutes preface:** Petschenig's Latin "Palestine or Mesopotamia"; Gibson's English "Pontus and Mesopotamia" (E6).
- **Death year:** unknown. Gennadius gives a reign; Richardson's note 450; Gibson "shortly" after 432; census end 432 (E12).
- **"Semi-Pelagian":** a later name, used by Gibson and Bright (N211 15574, 15675, 15940-15943), never by Cassian or his contemporaries in any held text. Not told as his.
- **The Conferences' speakers:** how close to their real words cannot be known (E2).
- **The Rule of Benedict:** names "Collationes" and "Instituta", not Cassian (I1).
- **Rome and priesthood:** Gibson guesses he was made priest at Rome (N211 15428-15431). Innocent's letter to Alexander of Antioch (c. 415) names "compresbyter noster Cassianus" (`facundus-ferrandus-caesarius-dionysius-pl67-lat_migne1865.txt` 20136); no held source makes this our Cassian. Leave out.
- **Irish line:** one eighth-century tract, which also places him wrongly at Lérins (I7).
