## The three lines (from one hearing)

1. **Who:** John Cassian, a monk who lived for years among the desert monks of Egypt, then settled at Marseilles and wrote their rules and talks in Latin, in the Institutes and the Conferences.
2. **What happened:** after Bethlehem and Scete, John Chrysostom made him a deacon and he carried John's clergy's letters to Rome; at Marseilles he founded two houses, one for men and one for women, wrote the Institutes and twenty-four Conferences, and wrote against Nestorius at Leo's request. His thirteenth Conference, on grace and free will, drew a book from Prosper of Aquitaine against "the Conferencer".
3. **Why it matters:** his Latin books carried the desert's way of life to the monks of the West. Benedict's Rule had them read, and Gennadius defended them. Cassiodorus kept them but warned against their teaching on free will. This was influence through reading, not descent.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.
