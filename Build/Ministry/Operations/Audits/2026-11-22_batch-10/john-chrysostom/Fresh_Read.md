## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** John, a preacher from Antioch in the Antiochene tradition of plain Scripture reading, who became bishop of Constantinople in 398.
2. **What happened:** he spoke bluntly to the powerful, even over the fallen Eutropius hiding under the altar, and urged care for Christ in the poor. Theophilus and a synod at the Oak deposed him. He was recalled, then condemned again after a clash with the empress, exiled in 404, and died on the road in 407.
3. **Why the connection matters:** his deposition split the church at Constantinople. His followers stayed apart until Proclus brought his body home in 438 and so won them back. He is the Antiochene tradition's best-known voice, trained under Diodore.

## Findings

1. **MINOR (rigor, date).** "That year the emperor's chief eunuch, Eutropius, had a law passed" gives the law the year of the fall, 399. The held source dates neither the law nor the fall. Socrates says only that "scarcely had the law been promulgated" before Eutropius fled (npnf202 17335-17337). Change "That year the emperor's chief eunuch" to "Not long before, the emperor's chief eunuch".
2. **MINOR (force left out).** Socrates says an imperial decree expelled John at once after the Oak, before the people rose (npnf202 18297-18305). The brief jumps from deposition to recall. Change "they deposed him for refusing. The people rose, and he was recalled." to "they deposed him for refusing, and the emperor sent him away. The people rose, and he was recalled."
3. **MINOR (one hearing).** The Oak can be heard as a tree. Change "meeting at the Oak near Chalcedon" to "meeting at a place called the Oak, near Chalcedon" (Socrates: "a place called 'The Oak'", npnf202 18275).
