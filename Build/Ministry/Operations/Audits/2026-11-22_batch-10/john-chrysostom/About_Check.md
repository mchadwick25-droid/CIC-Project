## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: item 11 picks one witness where two held sources differ

Socrates says the bishops deposed John "without assigning any other cause ... but that he refused to obey the summons" (`npnf202` 18293-18297). The minutes as Photius gives them say the first and second counts were examined (3946), then more charges, and then 45 bishops voted (4024-4031). The brief says "they deposed him for refusing" in the narrator's voice, as fact. That was always one witness's claim. Once item 10 names the charges from the minutes, it also contradicts the source the new lines cite. The confidence is not Documented.

Replace item 11 with:

> 11. **"Deposed him for refusing".** Explain, credited. He refused judges who were his enemies and asked for a general council (Socrates VI.15, 18282-18297). Socrates says he was deposed only for refusing (18293-18297). The minutes, in Photius, record charges examined and then a vote (3946, 4024-4031). Contested between the two. The brief says: "When John refused them as his enemies, they deposed him, Socrates says, for refusing." (+2 words). The card carries the minutes' account. Do not say he was deposed for refusing as the narrator's own claim, and do not say he was condemned on the charges.

Both explanations can be shorter without losing what they explain. Replace item 9 with:

> 9. **Theophilus's fuller quarrel: the monks.** Explain. Plain sense: monks Theophilus had driven out of Egypt, in a quarrel over whether God has a human body, came to John for help. Socrates says that when Theophilus heard, falsely, that John had taken them into communion, he resolved to unseat him. About 42 words. Witness: Socrates VI.7, 17578-17592 and 17655-17693; VI.9, 17790-17843. Credit Socrates, who is no friend of Theophilus (VI.13, 18122-18125). The expulsion and the arrival are Documented. Socrates's reading of Theophilus's motive is Contested. Leave out Origen and Theophilus's condemnation of Origen's books. Socrates says that was a cover (17841-17843; VI.10, 17897-17900), and "no mention was now made of Origen" at the Oak (VI.15, 18271-18272). Naming Origen would need him glossed and would add a name past ten. Card. Do not say Theophilus's motive as fact. Do not say Theophilus believed God has a human body: Socrates says he held the opposite (17660-17663). Do not say John was charged with following Origen. Do not name Isidore, Dioscorus or the Tall Monks.

The false report matters. John gave the monks "an honorable reception" but "postponed their communion of the sacred mysteries". Theophilus acted on "a false report" that John had admitted them (17833-17840). The outline's "When John received them, Theophilus resolved" puts the cause on an act John did not do.

"Eaten alone" drops what the charge was: "ate gluttonously alone, living like a Cyclops" (3929). Photius wrote in the ninth century, about 450 years after the Oak, so it is "more than four centuries", not "about four".

### 3. MINOR: cut 7's "home" misleads

John's home was Antioch, which the brief names. "Carried John's body home" can be heard as a return to Antioch. Socrates says the body went from Comana to Constantinople (21463-21465). Replace cut 7 with:

> 7. Last paragraph: "of Constantinople" from "Bishop Proclus of Constantinople" (2), and "back to the city in procession" becomes "back to Constantinople" (3).

Also strike the overflow cut of "himself" in the hook. It carries the scene's turn: the man who stripped refugees of sanctuary takes sanctuary.

### 4. MINOR: Gainas must not be placed as the rebuke that "turned the tide"

Socrates says rebuking men in office turned the tide (17319-17321). He puts Gainas "afterwards", with "It is said" (17368-17369). Add to item 7:

> Tell Gainas after "Socrates says that turned the tide against him", as "It is said he was as blunt with Gainas ...". Do not make Gainas the cause of the turn.

### 5. MINOR: item 12's leave-out reason is incomplete

The leave-out is sound, but not because items 9 and 10 "carry the cause". Socrates gives the immediate cause of the Oak as the emperor, after Eudoxia's complaint, authorizing Theophilus to call a synod (18239-18249). Replace the reason with:

> Leave out. The brief does not name it, and there is no room to explain it. The brief must not say or imply that Theophilus called the Oak on his own authority: say "he and his bishops", as now. Card: the sermon against women, Eudoxia's complaint and the emperor's authorization.
