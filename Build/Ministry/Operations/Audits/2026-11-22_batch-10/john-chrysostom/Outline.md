This story was approved before the kit and has no outline or source sheet. This file holds only the "what it was about" pass (2026-11-23). Socrates is `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml` (Zenos's translation); Photius is `cic/texts/photius_library-v1_freese1920.txt` (Codex LIX, the minutes of the Oak); Palladius is `cic/texts/palladius_dialogue-life-of-chrysostom_moore1921.txt`.

## What it was about

1. **Diodore's way of reading Scripture.** Already explained: "the literal sense" (Socrates VI.3, `npnf202` 17180-17183; Sozomen VIII.2, 42694-42698). The Antiochene sentence goes (Room for it, cut 1). The tradition label "Antiochene" is out of the story (Modern pass, place 1): it is the modern editors' label, not the old sources' words.
2. **Eutropius's sanctuary law.** Already explained: it stripped refugees of shelter in churches. Socrates gives its aim as revenge on people who had fled into churches (Socrates VI.5, 17326-17334). Documented.
3. **The sermon over Eutropius.** Already explained, from both sides: Socrates, "added insult to cruelty", and John's own reason (Homily I on Eutropius, `npnf109`, about 17698-17722). Documented.
4. **Homily 50 on Matthew.** Already explained: honor Christ's body by not neglecting Him naked (`npnf110` 29929-29931). Documented.
5. **The hospital spending.** Already explained: Palladius says John moved the bishop's lavish spending to the hospital (Palladius 3745-3751). Credited to Palladius, his partisan. Documented.
6. **The ordination: "Eutropius made Theophilus ordain him".** Already explained. "Made" carries the force. Socrates gives the means: Eutropius threatened Theophilus with a trial on charges against him (VI.2, 17097-17108). That goes to the card. Documented.
7. **"He rebuked men in office too": rebuked for what?** Explain. Plain sense: one was the Gothic general Gainas, who asked the emperor for a church in the city for the Arians. The Arians taught that the Son of God had a beginning. Socrates says John censured many others of high rank too, "for a variety of causes", and gives no others. Witness: Socrates VI.5, 17319-17323 and 17369-17376; Arius's teaching, I.5, 2093-2096. Documented (Socrates's "it is said" on Gainas: Widely Accepted). Tell Gainas after "Socrates says that turned the tide against him", as "It is said he was as blunt with Gainas ...". Do not make Gainas the cause of the turn.
8. **Theophilus's grudge: the lost see.** Already explained: his favorite, Isidore, lost the see to John (Socrates VI.5, 17377-17381). Socrates's view of motive: Contested.
9. **Theophilus's fuller quarrel: the monks.** Explain. Plain sense: monks Theophilus had driven out of Egypt, in a quarrel over whether God has a human body, came to John for help. Socrates says that when Theophilus heard, falsely, that John had taken them into communion, he resolved to unseat him. About 42 words. Witness: Socrates VI.7, 17578-17592 and 17655-17693; VI.9, 17790-17843. Credit Socrates, who is no friend of Theophilus (VI.13, 18122-18125). The expulsion and the arrival are Documented. Socrates's reading of Theophilus's motive is Contested. Leave out Origen and Theophilus's condemnation of Origen's books. Socrates says that was a cover (17841-17843; VI.10, 17897-17900), and "no mention was now made of Origen" at the Oak (VI.15, 18271-18272). Naming Origen would need him glossed and would add a name past ten. Card. Do not say Theophilus's motive as fact. Do not say Theophilus believed God has a human body: Socrates says he held the opposite (17660-17663). Do not say John was charged with following Origen. Do not name Isidore, Dioscorus or the Tall Monks.
10. **The Oak: "cited John to appear": on what charges?** Explain, as the accusers' charges. Plain sense: his accusers charged that he had sold church property, insulted the clergy, struck a man and eaten greedily alone. About 19 words. Witness: the synod's own minutes, in Photius, Codex LIX (3872-3876, 3929, 3933). The spoken credit is "his accusers charged". The card names Photius, writing more than four centuries later, and his calls of "unlawfully" and "this holy man" (3843, 3939). Documented that the charges were laid; nothing held proves them. Socrates calls many of them "ridiculous" (VI.15, 18272-18274). That goes to the card. Do not say he was condemned on these charges: Socrates says the bishops deposed him only for refusing to come (18293-18297). Photius says they examined some of the counts (3946). Leave the difference to the card.
11. **"Deposed him for refusing".** Explain, credited. He refused judges who were his enemies and asked for a general council (Socrates VI.15, 18282-18297). Socrates says he was deposed only for refusing (18293-18297). The minutes, in Photius, record charges examined and then a vote (3946, 4024-4031). Contested between the two. The brief says: "When John refused them as his enemies, they deposed him, Socrates says, for refusing." (+2 words). The card carries the minutes' account. Do not say he was deposed for refusing as the narrator's own claim, and do not say he was condemned on the charges.
12. **The trigger of the Oak: his sermon against women, heard as aimed at Eudoxia** (Socrates VI.15, 18239-18249). Leave out. The brief does not name it, and there is no room to explain it. The brief must not say or imply that Theophilus called the Oak on his own authority: say "he and his bishops", as now. Card: the sermon against women, Eudoxia's complaint and the emperor's authorization.
13. **"A clash with the empress Eudoxia": a clash over what?** Explain. Plain sense: public games were held at a silver statue of Eudoxia beside the cathedral. John mocked those who allowed them, and she took his words as aimed at her. Witness: Socrates VI.18, 18427-18444. Documented (Socrates).
14. **The sermon "Again Herodias raves".** Leave out. The brief does not name it. Socrates quotes it (18456-18457), but the editor notes that Savile thought the sermon spurious (18452). Naming it would need its doubt and its Herodias image explained, and there is no room.
15. **The second council: "silenced him": on what ground?** Explain. Plain sense: it condemned him for taking back his see after his deposition without a church council's leave. Witness: Socrates VI.18, 18468-18473 and 18484-18489. Documented. John's reply, that the rule was an Arian canon (18474-18479), is left out: it would need the Antioch council explained. Card.
16. **The 404 exile decree: on what ground?** Explained by item 15. The emperor's ground was that two synods had condemned him (18486-18488). Documented.
17. **The fire his followers set on the day he left** (18497-18500). Leave out. It is an event, not named, and there is no room. Card.
18. **The followers who left "over John's deposition".** Already explained: the brief gives the cause. Socrates VI.18 says his party kept Easter in the public baths and met apart as "Johannites" (18491-18496). VII.45 (21455-21466) gives the reconciliation. Documented.

Explained: 5 (items 7, 9, 10, 13, 15). Left out: 4 (12, 14, 17, and John's Arian-canon reply within 15). Already explained: 10 (items 1-6, 8, 11, 16, 18).

## Modern pass

Done 2026-11-23 under the Decision-Log entry "No modern opinion, guess or reconstruction in a story" (Mark, 2026-11-23). Every sentence of the brief was checked against the cited lines, dates included. Five places rested on a modern source; three changed in words, two stand on the old sources once re-read.

1. **"in the Antiochene way"** (paragraph 2). Old: "…who kept to the literal sense of Scripture, in the Antiochene way." → New: "…who kept to the literal sense of Scripture." The label rests on the modern editors: Zenos's note, "the Antiochian system of interpretation" (`npnf202` 17173-17179), and Prevost, "the so-called Antiochian school" (`npnf110` 730-732). No old source calls it that. The words that stay are held by Socrates VI.3, Diodore "limited his attention to the literal sense of scripture, avoiding that which was mystical" (`npnf202` 17180-17183), and Sozomen VIII.2, he "avoided allegory" (42694-42698). To the card: the modern view that Diodore founded an Antiochene school or system of reading Scripture, from Zenos (`npnf202` 17173-17179) and Prevost (`npnf110` 730-732).
2. **"In 403"** (paragraph 5, the Oak). Old: "In 403 he and his bishops, meeting at the Oak…" → New: "He and his bishops, meeting at the Oak…". No held old source gives the Oak's year: Socrates VI.15 (`npnf202` 18262-18300) and the minutes in Photius, Codex LIX (3843-3850), give none. Socrates VII.45's "thirty-fifth year after his deposition" (21465) also says "the thirty-fifth year after his death" (21478-21479), so it fixes no year. The order stays as Socrates tells it. To the card: the year 403, from Freese's note citing Hefele (Photius, 3862-3864) and Moore's chronology (Palladius, 678).
3. **"In 407, Palladius says,"** (paragraph 6). Old: "In 407, Palladius says, soldiers hurried him toward Pityus, hoping he would die. He died on the way, with his usual words…" → New: "Palladius says soldiers hurried him toward Pityus, hoping he would die. He died on the way in 407, with his usual words…". Palladius gives no year (`palladius_dialogue…moore1921.txt` 6392-6432); the old wording gave the year to him and to the march. The year moves to the death, which Socrates VI.21 dates "on the 14th of September, in the following consulate, which was the seventh of Honorius, and the second of Theodosius" (`npnf202` 18609-18612). To the card: the march's year, 407 (June), from Moore's chronology (Palladius, 706).
4. **"There," (the Matthew sermon at Antioch)** (paragraph 2). No change in words. The review and the fresh read placed it from the editor's preface, "undoubtedly delivered at Antioch" (`npnf110` 533-535). Re-read, the place rests on John's own words in the same course of homilies: "our city first crowned itself with the name of Christian" (Homily VII, `npnf110` 5302-5303), the city of Acts 11:26. Homily 50 itself names no city (`npnf110` 29598-30037). To the card: the editor's view that the series was preached late in his years as a presbyter (`npnf110` 535-536).
5. **"who kept to the literal sense of Scripture"** (paragraph 2). No change in words. The review's M1 traced it to Zenos's note and Prevost's preface; Socrates VI.3 holds it himself (`npnf202` 17180-17183), so the card's reference should cite Socrates, not the notes.

**Checked and held, no change.** "In 398" (Socrates VI.2, "the 26th of February, under the following consulate, which the Emperor Honorius celebrated", `npnf202` 17110-17115), "In 399" (Eutropius fell in his own consular year, Socrates VI.5, 17363-17366; Homily I on Eutropius, "Consul", "thy consulship", `npnf109` 17589, 17594), "in 404" (Socrates VI.18, the sixth consulate of Honorius, `npnf202` 18500-18502) and "In 438" (Socrates VII.45, the sixteenth consulate of Theodosius, 21470-21471).
