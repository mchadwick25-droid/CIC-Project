Socrates is `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml` (Zenos's translation); Photius is `cic/texts/photius_library-v1_freese1920.txt` (Codex LIX, the minutes of the Oak); Palladius is `cic/texts/palladius_dialogue-life-of-chrysostom_moore1921.txt`.

## What it was about

1. **The Antiochene way of reading Scripture** (with Diodore). Already explained: "plain way", "the literal sense". The Antiochene sentence goes (Room for it, cut 1), but its tradition link moves into the Diodore sentence.
2. **Eutropius's sanctuary law.** Already explained: it stripped refugees of shelter in churches. Socrates gives its aim as revenge on people who had fled into churches (Socrates VI.5, 17326-17334). Documented.
3. **The sermon over Eutropius.** Already explained, from both sides: Socrates, "added insult to cruelty", and John's own reason (Homily I on Eutropius, `npnf109`, about 17698-17722). Documented.
4. **Homily 50 on Matthew.** Already explained: honor Christ's body by not neglecting Him naked (`npnf110` 29929-29931). Documented.
5. **The hospital spending.** Already explained: Palladius says John moved the bishop's lavish spending to the hospital (Palladius 3745-3751). Credited to Palladius, his partisan. Documented.
6. **The ordination: "Eutropius made Theophilus ordain him".** Already explained. "Made" carries the force. Socrates gives the means: Eutropius threatened Theophilus with a trial on charges against him (VI.2, 17097-17108). That goes to the card. Documented.
7. **"He rebuked men in office too": rebuked for what?** Explain. Plain sense: one was the Gothic general Gainas, who asked the emperor for a church in the city for the Arians. The Arians taught that the Son of God had a beginning. Socrates says John censured many others of high rank too, "for a variety of causes", and gives no others. Witness: Socrates VI.5, 17319-17323 and 17369-17376; Arius's teaching, I.5, 2093-2096. Documented (Socrates's "it is said" on Gainas: Widely Accepted).
8. **Theophilus's grudge: the lost see.** Already explained: his favorite, Isidore, lost the see to John (Socrates VI.5, 17377-17381). Socrates's view of motive: Contested.
9. **Theophilus's fuller quarrel: the monks and Origen's books.** Explain. Plain sense: monks Theophilus had driven out of Egypt came to John for help. Socrates says Theophilus turned on them in a quarrel over whether God has a human body, calling them followers of Origen, a teacher dead for nearly two hundred years. When John received them, Socrates says, Theophilus resolved to unseat him. He condemned Origen's books to hide his real motive. Witness: Socrates VI.7, 17578-17592 and 17655-17693; VI.9, 17790-17843; VI.10, 17897-17900. Credit Socrates, who is no friend of Theophilus and defends Origen (VI.13, 18122-18125). The editor thinks an Origenist misled him on Epiphanius (17874). The facts of the expulsion and the arrival are Documented. Socrates's reading of Theophilus's motive is Contested. Do not say Theophilus's motive as fact. Do not name Isidore, Dioscorus or the Tall Monks.
10. **The Oak: "cited John to appear": on what charges?** Explain, as the accusers' charges. Plain sense: his accusers charged that he had sold church property, insulted the clergy, struck a man and eaten alone. Witness: the synod's own minutes, as Photius summarized them about four centuries later (Photius, Codex LIX, 3841-3855, 3872-3876, 3929 and 3932-3934). Photius calls the synod "unlawfully" summoned and John "this holy man" (3843 and 3939). Credit it as the accusers' charge, "his accusers charged". Documented that the charges were laid; nothing held proves them. Socrates calls many of them "ridiculous" (VI.15, 18272-18274). That goes to the card. Do not say he was condemned on these charges: Socrates says the bishops deposed him only for refusing to come (18293-18297). Photius says they examined some of the counts (3946). Leave the difference to the card.
11. **"Deposed him for refusing".** Already explained: he refused judges who were his enemies and asked for a general council (Socrates VI.15, 18282-18297). Documented.
12. **The trigger of the Oak: his sermon against women, heard as aimed at Eudoxia** (Socrates VI.15, 18239-18249). Leave out. It is not named in the brief, and items 9 and 10 carry the cause. Card.
13. **"A clash with the empress Eudoxia": a clash over what?** Explain. Plain sense: public games were held at a silver statue of Eudoxia beside the cathedral. John mocked those who allowed them, and she took his words as aimed at her. Witness: Socrates VI.18, 18427-18444. Documented (Socrates).
14. **The sermon "Again Herodias raves".** Leave out. The brief does not name it. Socrates quotes it (18456-18457), but the editor notes that Savile thought the sermon spurious (18452). Naming it would need its doubt and its Herodias image explained, and there is no room.
15. **The second council: "silenced him": on what ground?** Explain. Plain sense: it condemned him for taking back his see after his deposition without a church council's leave. Witness: Socrates VI.18, 18468-18473 and 18484-18489. Documented. John's reply, that the rule was an Arian canon (18474-18479), is left out: it would need the Antioch council explained. Card.
16. **The 404 exile decree: on what ground?** Explained by item 15. The emperor's ground was that two synods had condemned him (18486-18488). Documented.
17. **The fire his followers set on the day he left** (18497-18500). Leave out. It is an event, not named, and there is no room. Card.
18. **The followers who left "over John's deposition".** Already explained: the brief gives the cause. Socrates VI.18 says his party kept Easter in the public baths and met apart as "Johannites" (18491-18496). VII.45 (21455-21466) gives the reconciliation. Documented.

Explained: 5 (items 7, 9, 10, 13, 15). Left out: 4 (12, 14, 17, and John's Arian-canon reply within 15). Already explained: 10 (items 1-6, 8, 11, 16, 18).
