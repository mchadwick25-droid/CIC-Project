# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Essence:** In 431 the emperor called the bishops to Ephesus over whether Mary is Mother of God. Cyril opened the council before John of Antioch's bishops arrived, over the emperor's officer's protest, and deposed Nestorius. John's assembly deposed Cyril, and peace came only in 433. The verdict held in the empire. The Church of the East never made the council its own (Wigram).

**Also told:** the council forbade any other creed beside Nicaea's and upheld Pelagius's condemnation. The emperor arrested Nestorius, Cyril and Memnon, then dissolved the council. In 433 John's side accepted Nestorius's deposition, while Theodoret refused it. Chalcedon built on Cyril's letters.

**Shape:** Council.

**Length:** 4:30 (585 words). The researcher's count at the fewest words comes to about 550. The torches, the envoys, "Let the heavens rejoice" and other material go to the card.

**Connection:** a verdict and a confession that held in the empire, and stopped at its border.

**Quotes:**
1. Candidian (Latin, two prints): "I warned you of this, then, not once but many times, and humbly begged you, and I gained nothing."
2. Nestorius (opening; from the 1925 English and checked against Nau's French): "Who was judge? Cyril. Who was the accuser? Cyril. Who was bishop of Rome? Cyril. Cyril was everything."
3. The 433 confession (Greek): "A union of two natures has taken place. So we confess one Christ, one Son, one Lord. In keeping with this idea of a union without mixing, we confess the holy Virgin as Mother of God."

**Gaps:** the Syriac of Nestorius's book is not held. The Mansi and Pusey texts are damaged, so each quote is confirmed by a second print. The commissioner's report and the emperor's edict reach us only through Hefele's English. Quotes re-read; four faults checked; removed "more than five hundred bishops" at Chalcedon, Theodoret as Nestorius's "friend", and an unglossed "Mar".
