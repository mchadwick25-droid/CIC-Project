## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who or what:** the council of bishops the emperor Theodosius II called to Ephesus in 431 over whether Mary could be called Mother of God, which was really a question of how God and a man are one in Christ.
2. **What happened:** Cyril opened it before John of Antioch's bishops came, over the emperor's officer's protest, and deposed Nestorius. John's assembly deposed Cyril. The council also barred any creed beside Nicaea's and upheld Pelagius's condemnation. The emperor arrested all three leaders and dissolved it, and in 433 John's side and Cyril made peace at the price of Nestorius's deposition.
3. **Why the connection matters:** the verdict held in the empire, where Chalcedon later took Cyril's letters as its guide. Beyond the empire, the Church of the East kept its distance, never formally accepting or rejecting the council.

All three lines can be written from one hearing. Line 3 is the weakest: "his letter to the Eastern bishops" does not tie Chalcedon back to the peace of 433 (finding B1).

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING: the connection from 433 to 451 does not land.** "Cyril's letters, to Nestorius and to the Eastern bishops" names a letter the story never told and a group it never called "Eastern". The listener heard "Syria's bishops" and "John's bishops", and heard that Cyril accepted their confession, not that he wrote a letter. So the outline's connection, that the letter of peace became Chalcedon's guide, is lost on one hearing.
- **Change:** say the letter as the story told it, for example: "In 451 the council of Chalcedon took two of Cyril's letters as its guide: the one to Nestorius, and his letter of peace to John's bishops." Source: I1, ACTS 20256–20262, with the letter to the Easterns being the letter of peace (E18). Find the extra words elsewhere to stay within 585 (see M2).
- **Do not say (add to outline):** Not "the Eastern bishops" or "the Easterns" unexplained for the letter Chalcedon took. Say "his letter of peace to John of Antioch's bishops" (I1, E18).

**M1. MINOR: the essence lands just past the first minute.** The core acts land at about word 140. The reach line ("not beyond it, in Persia") ends at about word 163, against about 130 for a council.
- **Change:** tighten paragraph 2, for example by dropping the second "the emperor" clause, so the essence lands nearer 130. Keep all three essence beats.

**M2. MINOR: clumsy joins.**
- (a) "The emperor Theodosius II called that council, a meeting to settle a question, to Ephesus." The gloss interrupts the verb. "Council" was also first heard, unglossed, in the opening. Put the gloss where "council" is first heard, or recast as "called the bishops to Ephesus, to a council that would settle one question".
- (b) "the emperor's new commissioner had the emperor's letter read": "the emperor's" twice. Say "had his master's letter read", or "had the emperor's letter read" with the commissioner by a shorter role.

Either change also frees words for B1.

**M3. MINOR: Wigram's "had not" told as "never".** WIG 9272–9274 describes the Church of the East around 540: it "had certainly not accepted Ephesus — nor had they formally rejected it — nor had they ever been asked to do either." Only the last clause is "ever". The brief's "never accepted ... never formally rejected" widens his time-bound claim.
- **Change:** "wrote in 1910 that it had not accepted the council, nor formally rejected it, and had never been asked to do either."
- **Do not say (add to outline):** Not "never accepted" or "never formally rejected" for Wigram. Say "had not accepted, nor formally rejected, and had never been asked to do either" (WIG 9272–9274).
