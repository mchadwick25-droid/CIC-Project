## 1. The clause against its lines

Lines re-read in `cic/texts/`:

- NPNF214 36418–36421: "Synod against the heresy of Pelagius and Celestius. In the consulate of ... Honorius for the XIIth time and Theodosius for the VIIIth ... on the Calends of May, at Carthage". These are the consuls of 418, so the condemning council is correctly identified. Verbatim.
- NPNF214 36504–36506 (Canon CXI; it ends at 36507, "him be anathema."): "avails only for the remission of past sins, and not for assistance against committing sins in the future". Verbatim.
- NPNF214 36522–36527 (Canon CXII): "helps us only in not sinning by revealing to us and opening to our understanding the commandments ... but that through it we are not helped so that we are able to do what we know we should do". Verbatim.
- NPNF214 18748–18755, the council's letter to Celestine, upholding the condemnation: verbatim.
- NPNF105 10383: "We said, that a man is able to be without sin, and to keep the commandments of God, if he wishes;". Correct. 10381 holds the disputed letter's "easily" form. The fix's correction stands.

**Scope.** "only forgive past sins" is Canon CXI. "show what to do" is Canon CXII's "revealing ... the commandments, so that we may know what to seek, what we ought to avoid". Taken together, the canons condemn holding that grace does no more than these two things, so joining them under one "only" says no more than the canons do.

**Credit.** The charge is credited to "his accusers", the model wording in Kit Part 1. The canons condemn "whoever should say" these things and do not name him. The synod's heading does name him, so "his accusers charged" is right: it is the condemning side's charge, and the narrator does not tell it as what Pelagius taught. The outline rates it Documented as the charge and Contested as his own teaching (pelagius SS E13). The brief says nothing beyond that.

**FIX 1. "he made grace only forgive" can be misheard, once, as Pelagius causing something to happen to grace.** "Made" is a causative verb. Heard once, "he made grace only forgive past sins" first sounds as if he did something to grace, not as a claim he put forward. The canons' own verb is "say" ("whoever should say"), so that verb is both plainer and closer to the source. The word count stays the same. I checked it with `profile_readability` on a scratch copy: 585 words, grade 8.1, names out 7.3, reading ease 68.4, longest sentence 28, pass.

- Brief, para 7. Old: "his accusers charged that he made grace only forgive past sins and show what to do." → New: "his accusers charged that he said grace only forgives past sins and shows what to do."
- Outline item 11 (in "What it was about"), the same words. Old: ""his accusers charged that he made grace only forgive past sins and show what to do."" → New: ""his accusers charged that he said grace only forgives past sins and shows what to do.""

**Not a finding: the listener hears what grace was said to do only, not the other side's view.** The "only" makes the limit plain, and the canons' positive claim (that grace helps people do what they know they should) would cost about six words the brief cannot spare at 585. The card can carry it.

**Would it mislead?** No, once finding 1 is applied. It names the charge, gives what the charge was about, credits it to the condemning side, and does not say "he denied grace".
