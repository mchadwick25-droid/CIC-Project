## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: item 11, Pelagius, is held and needed

The outline says the only held account of what Pelagius taught "here" is Percival's summary (ACTS 18018–18036). That is wrong in two ways.

- **Held.** The sibling's Source Sheet (`../pelagius/Source_Sheet.md`) holds Pelagius's own words. At NPNF105 10381 he says "a man is able to be without sin, and to keep the commandments of God, if he wishes", and at NPNF105 9524 he says God "is its helper whenever it chooses good". It also holds the condemning council's own canons, in the same file this outline cites as ACTS. Under "Synod against the heresy of Pelagius and Celestius", Carthage, 1 May 418 (NPNF214 36418–36421), Canons CXI and CXII (NPNF214 36504–36506, 36522–36527) rule that grace does more than forgive past sins and show us the commandments: it helps us not to sin and to do what we know.
- **Needed.** The slate gives this story one clause linking to `pelagius`. That link was the Outline's Sibling line, beat 5 and the Shape's "Other business". The Kit's Council shape also tells us to keep other essential business, so that one dispute does not stand for the whole council. The sibling brief ends on this very event, and it names the issue as "over grace, God's help freely given".

One plain clause can say what he was condemned over without carrying either side forward. It names the issue the condemning canons ruled on. It does not give his judges' reading of him as fact, since what he meant by grace is Contested (pelagius SS E13). It also matches the sibling's wording.

Replace item 11 with:

> 11. **The condemnation of Pelagius upheld.** Explain: it was a condemnation over grace, God's help freely given (the sibling's own gloss). Witnesses: the council's letter to Celestine, which upheld Rome's sentence on Pelagius and his followers (ACTS 18748–18755). The condemning council of Carthage, 418 (ACTS = NPNF214 36418–36421), whose canons rule that grace does more than forgive past sins and teach the commandments: it helps people not to sin (NPNF214 36504–36506, 36522–36527). Documented that it was upheld; Documented that the condemnation was over grace. What Pelagius himself meant by grace is Contested (pelagius SS E13) and is the `pelagius` story's; say only what the sentence was over, never "he denied grace". Percival's summary (ACTS 18018–18036) is the editor's and is not used. One clause; link to `pelagius`.

Also replace the tally line with:

> Seventeen items: four to explain (2, 7, 10, 11); one left out (8); twelve already explained.

### 2. BLOCKING: Room for it leaves a sentence with no subject

Room for it cuts "The council also" and puts item 10's clause at the head of the sentence. The brief then reads: "Shown a rival statement of how God's Son became man, ruled that no one may put forward any creed but Nicaea's." That sentence has no subject. It was not counted as written either. There is no room for both item 10's occasion and item 11's clause.

The ruling already says plainly what it was: no creed but Nicaea's. The occasion is an event, not its meaning. So the occasion goes to the card, and the sentence becomes:

> "It also ruled that no one may put forward any creed but Nicaea's, and upheld Pelagius's condemnation over grace, God's help freely given."

"It" refers to the sentence before it, "Cyril's council cut John's men off in turn", and that is the right council.

Replace item 10 with:

> 10. **No creed but Nicaea's** (Canon VII). Already plain: the council banned any other creed beside Nicaea's. Witness: the council's own decree. ACTS 18146–18170. Documented. The occasion, a rival statement of how God's Son became man (Theodore of Mopsuestia's, brought by Charisius), goes to the card for room.

### 3. BLOCKING: cut 2 makes the essence mislead

After cut 2, the essence reads: "It deposed Nestorius, removing him from office; peace came two years later." Heard once, this tells of a peace with no quarrel. It even suggests a peace for Nestorius, who was never restored. It also drops the counter-deposition, which is the essence's conflict, out of the first minute. The essence may look ahead, so it can name the counter-deposition again in the John beat. The peace is told in the 433 paragraph.

Replace cut 2 with:

> 2. Essence: "John's smaller assembly deposed Cyril in turn; peace came only two years later." → ", and John's assembly deposed Cyril." The line reads "It deposed Nestorius, removing him from office, and John's assembly deposed Cyril." The peace stays in the 433 paragraph. Saves 8.

Delete the "For the writer" bullet. It no longer applies.

### 4. MINOR: item 2, "both sides", and the missing credit

"Offering Mother of Christ, a title both sides could share" sits right after Nestorius and Cyril. A listener would hear "both sides" as those two men. In fact BH 6908–6961 speaks of two parties in Constantinople. The sentence also tells his own account of his motive as fact, but the outline says to tell it "as his side". The new clause is the same length.

Replace the item 2 addition with:

> Item 2, replacing "but" after "would not simply call her that,": "offering Mother of Christ, he wrote, to settle his city's dispute." (10)

### 5. MINOR: the Names rule now contradicts cut 7

Under Names, the outline says John of Antioch is "Always in full, to keep him apart from Count John." Cut 7 drops "of Antioch", and the new room also needs it dropped once more, after Candidian. The commissioner is given by role only, so the clash that rule guarded against never arises.

Replace that Names line with:

> - John of Antioch: acts. Not a page on the tree; the tree's texts spell "John of Antioch". In full when first named; "John" after, since the commissioner is given by role only and never as "John".
