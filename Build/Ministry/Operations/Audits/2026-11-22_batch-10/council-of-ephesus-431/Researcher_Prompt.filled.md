Its other business (no new creed beside Nicaea's, the Pelagians' condemnation upheld), the emperor's arrest of both leaders, and the peace of 433. **Lifted (may be used, for the job named):**
- Ulfila: his creed's "alone unbegotten" and "subject and obedient in all things" (arius). They are his own words, kept by Auxentius. In Arius's story they show Arius's words living on among the Goths; here they show what Ulfila taught.
- Priscillian: Sulpicius's "the accusers were as distasteful to me as the accused" (sulpicius-severus, old text, and Priscillian's own old text), to credit the witness's view of both sides. Checked again when Sulpicius is rebuilt in batch 13.
- John Cassian: Gennadius's "brands as injurious" and "which the church of God finds salutary" (gennadius-of-marseilles, prosper-of-aquitaine, both old texts, and Cassian's own old text), for how Gaul answered Prosper's attack. Checked again when Prosper (batch 13) and Gennadius (era 3) are rebuilt.
- Rufinus: Socrates's "that more credit was due to him who had suffered" (socrates-of-constantinople, old text), for how the Greek historians used and corrected his History. Checked again in batch 13.
- Eutyches: Cyril's "one incarnate nature of the Word" (cyril-of-alexandria), only where Eutyches's own appeal to Cyril calls on it.
- Theodoret: his own History's words in alexander-of-alexandria ("as one of the retinue of Alexander", "that admirable bishop") and ossius-of-cordoba ("most highly distinguished", "the first place"), only to show his History at work. Both are old texts; checked again in batch 11.

**Stay barred:**
- For Ephesus: every quote in nestorius and cyril-of-alexandria ("the earth purged of heretics", "a mangled hand and side", "no commingling, no confusion", and the rest). They tell the same council from each man's side. "Mother of God" is the term under dispute, glossed, not a quote.
- For Eutyches and Theodoret: Leo's "not a trial but a robbery" (leo-of-rome) and "not of judges but of robbers" (dioscorus-of-alexandria): the same judgment on the same council of 449. Chalcedon's definition and the Syriac chronicle's "robe of Christ" (council-of-chalcedon-451): the council's text and its story's ending.
- For Rufinus: his own "Who but you would have laid hands on God's gift, and on the inheritance of the apostles?" (jerome). It is Rufinus's line, and he touched both men, but in Jerome's story it already shows Rufinus's charge against the translation, and here it would show the same charge in the same quarrel. His "is said to have written" (pamphilus-of-caesarea) stays for the same reason. Mark kept this bar (2026-11-23).
- For Priscillian: Martin's "It was a cruel wrong, never heard of before, for a judge of this world to try a case of the church" (martin-of-tours): the same plea at the same trial.
- For Cassian: Evagrius's own lines and Gennadius's "or among the first at least" (evagrius-ponticus).
- Editors' verdicts: Jackson's "were substantially at one" (Theodoret's old text) and Abbott's "generally believed" (ammianus-marcellinus).
- Every quote in Chrysostom's and Ephrem's live texts stays with them.
