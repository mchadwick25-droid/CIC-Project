## The four lines

- **Who or what:** a council of bishops the emperor Theodosius II called to Ephesus in 431, led by Cyril of Alexandria in the place of Rome's bishop.
- **What happened:** Cyril opened it before John of Antioch's bishops came, over Candidian's protest; it deposed Nestorius, and John's assembly deposed Cyril and Memnon; the emperor arrested all three and dissolved it; in 433 John's side confessed Mary Mother of God and accepted the deposition, and Cyril accepted their "two natures".
- **What it was about:** whether Mary could be called Mother of God, and so how God and a man are one in Christ.
- **Why the connection matters:** the verdict and the peace held in the empire and guided Chalcedon in 451; the peace cost Nestorius's deposition, which Theodoret would not accept; the Church of the East, in Persia, never made the council its own.

All four are present and right.
