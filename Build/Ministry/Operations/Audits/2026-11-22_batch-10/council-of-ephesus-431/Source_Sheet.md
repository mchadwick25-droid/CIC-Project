# Source sheet: Council of Ephesus (431)

Census id `council-of-ephesus-431`, kind `major-council`, name "Council of Ephesus" (short "Ephesus 431"). A council has no home tradition. ## Setting

**S1. When and where.** ACTS 18564–18573 (the council's letter to Celestine): "in accordance with the orders of the most pious and Christ-loving Emperors, we assembled together in the Metropolis of the Ephesians from many and far scattered regions, being in all over two hundred bishops. Then, in accordance with the decrees of the Christ-loving Emperors by whom we were assembled, we fixed the date of the meeting of the holy Synod as the Feast of the Holy Pentecost". ACTS 18597–18599: "the Holy Council sat in the great church of Ephesus, which bears the name of Mary." Cyril's letter home, M4 109167–109170 (Latin, damaged): "sanctam synodum in magna civitatis Ephesi ecclesia, quae appellatur deipara Maria, vigesima octava mensis Pauni fuisse factam" (the council met in the great church of Ephesus called Mary Mother of God, on 28 Payni). HEF 2952–2953: "the 28th day of the Egyptian month Payni (=June 22) 431" (EDITOR's reckoning). SOC 20944–20945: "These things were done on the 28th of June, under the consulate of Bassus and Antiochus." CONTEMP / OWN. Documented for 431 and the church; the day Contested (D1). Plain words: "in June 431", "at Ephesus, on the coast of Asia Minor", "in its great church named for Mary". The sheet holds no line for "on the coast"; say "the city of Ephesus" only.

**S2. Who called it.** HEF 2703–2713 (EDITOR, from the circular in Mansi IV 1111): "the Emperor Theodosius II, so early as November 19, 430 ... issued a circular letter, bearing also the name of his Western colleague, Valentinian III., addressed to all the metropolitans, in which he summoned them, for the Pentecost of the following year, to an Œcumenical Synod at Ephesus ... whoever should arrive too late should be gravely responsible before God and the Emperor." SOC 20877–20879: "Not long time elapsed before a mandate from the emperor directed the bishops in all places to assemble at Ephesus." EV 1294–1299 (LATER, late sixth century): the synod met "at the injunction of the younger Theodosius ... naming, at the same time, as the day of meeting, the sacred Pentecost". Documented. Metropolitans = chief bishops of provinces; say "the leading bishops".

**S3. The emperor's officer.** ACTS 15632–15660 (editor's note giving Hefele's synopsis of the edict): "Theodosius II. appointed the Count Candidian, Captain of the imperial bodyguard, the protector of the council"; "Candidian is to take no immediate part in the discussions on contested points of faith"; he was to remove curious monks and laymen, keep the debate orderly so "that every statement should be heard with attention", and stop anyone leaving early. EDITOR's synopsis of the edict (Mansi IV 1118, not read in the OCR). Widely Accepted. Plain words: "the emperor's officer", "Count Candidian".

**S4. The traditions.** Census names: "The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt" (short "Church of Alexandria"); "Antiochene Exegetical Christianity (Chrysostom-centered)" (short "Antioch"); "The Church of the East under Persia" (short "Church of the East"). Say "the bishops of Egypt", "the bishops of Syria under John of Antioch", "the Church of the East, in Persia beyond the empire". Do not say the census labels aloud.

## Events

**E1. The question (what called it).** SOC 20733–20739 (CONTEMP): Nestorius "delivered several public discourses on the subject, in which he assumed a controversial attitude, and totally rejected the epithet Theotocos." SOC 20706–20707 (editor's gloss): "Θεοτόκον, i.e. 'Mother of God.'" SOC 20739–20747: "the discussion which ensued divided the church ... so great a clamor was raised by the contention that it was deemed requisite to convene a general council". Nestorius's own account (BH 6927–6948, OWN at two removes, per the `nestorius` sheet E4): he found one party calling Mary mother of God and another mother of a man, and urged a word all could accept, Mother of Christ. Cyril's second letter to Nestorius, ACTS 15795–15799 (OWN): the Fathers "ventured to call the holy Virgin, the Mother of God, not as if the nature of the Word or his divinity had its beginning from the holy Virgin, but because of her was born that holy body with a rational soul, to which the Word being personally united is said to be born according to the flesh." Documented. **The stake**: whether God and a man are one in Christ (Cyril) or kept distinct in one Christ (Nestorius); each side charged the other with heresy (E5, E7). Credit each. Not to be quoted: the Cyril letter's lines used in `cyril-of-alexandria` (barred).

**E2. Rome before the council.** ACTS 17269–17280 (the decree against Nestorius, OWN of the council): "Compelled thereto by the canons and by the letter ... of our most holy father and fellow-servant Cœlestine, the Roman bishop". ACTS 15374–15421 (Bossuet, EDITOR, 17th century, abridged): Celestine condemned Nestorius unless he recanted within ten days and charged Cyril to carry it out. HEF 2766–2771. The emperor's call held everything over: ACTS 15451–15456 (Bossuet, quoting the emperor): "that before the assembling and common sentence of the most holy Council, no change should be made in any matter at all, on any private authority." Documented that Celestine had written against Nestorius; the details are EDITOR. One clause.

**E3. Arrivals and the wait.** SOC 20886–20890 (CONTEMP): "Immediately after the festival of Easter therefore Nestorius, escorted by a great crowd of his adherents, repaired to Ephesus ... Cyril bishop of Alexandria making some delay, did not arrive till near Pentecost. Five days after Pentecost, Juvenal bishop of Jerusalem arrived. While John of Antioch was still absent". BH 861, 894–900 (EDITOR): Nestorius "with ten bishops"; "Cyril with fifty bishops"; Juvenal with the bishops of Palestine; Flavian of Philippi with those of Macedonia. HEF 2883 (EDITOR): Nestorius "with his sixteen bishops"; HEF 2886: Cyril "with fifty bishops". HEF 2933–2941 (EDITOR, from John's letter in Mansi IV 1121): John wrote that "the length of the road and the death of several of their horses had delayed the journey, but that nevertheless he was close at hand, and would appear at Ephesus in five or six days." BH 905–906 (EDITOR): "Memnon closes the churches of Ephesus to the Nestorians". Documented for the arrivals; Nestorius's number Contested (ten or sixteen).

**E4. "Do not put off the Synod."** ACTS 18579–18599 (the council's letter, OWN of Cyril's side): "we put off the assembling [of the council] after the appointed day of the Holy Pentecost for sixteen whole days; in the meanwhile many of the bishops and clerics were overtaken with illness, and much burdened by the expense, and some even died"; two of John's metropolitans arrived and said, "He gave us command to announce to your reverence, that if anything should happen to delay him, not to put off the Synod, but to do what was right." The council read this as refusal, "whether out of friendship to Nestorius" or otherwise (18594–18597), and charged John with a hidden plan (18574–18578). EV 1303–1307 (LATER): John came late "not intentionally, as his defence has been thought by many to have sufficiently proved". HEF 2992–2997 (EDITOR, from Mansi V 765): "sixty-eight Asiatic bishops, among whom were, in particular, Theodoret of Cyrus and the two above-mentioned metropolitans ... had requested that they would be pleased to defer the opening of the Synod until the arrival of bishops from Antioch." Documented for the message; John's motive Contested (D3).

**E5. Candidian's protest, 22 June.** Candidian's own protest, Latin, held in two prints: M5 76777–76779 (heading: "Contestatio Candidiani Comitis, quam publice, mane audiens synodum celebrari, in Epheso proposuit", his protest posted publicly in Ephesus that morning on hearing the synod was meeting) and 76784–76946, dated M5 76870–76877 "Propositum est decimo Kalendas Julii in Ephesina civitate" (22 June); and BH 7694–7784 (Lupus's Latin in the editors' note, with Driver and Hodgson's English of the Syriac at 7739–7856). OWN. Key lines, M5 76897–76922 (OCR long s normalized) and BH 7724–7770 agree:
- "deprecans reverentiam vestram et poscens nihil novum fieri priusquam cuncti sanctissimi patres atque Episcopi ad synodum convenirent, sed sustineretis quatuor tantummodo dies sanctissimum Episcopum Antiochenorum metropoleos cum aliis qui pariter sunt, necnon et illos qui cum sanctissimo Nestorio sunt" (BH English 7804–7813: "testifying unto you that you should introduce no innovations before all the holy bishops were gathered together unto the Council, but that you should wait four days only for the holy John, bishop of Antioch ... and those again who were with the holy and pious Nestorius").
- "Haec igitur non semel sed etiam saepius admonens, et suppliciter postulans, nihil profeci. Verum quia nihil eorum quae a me sunt dicta servatum est, sed a vobis injuriose ac violenter expulsus sum, necessarium duxi hoc mea contestatione vobis constituere manifestum" (M5 76917–76923; BH 7767–7771; BH English 7821–7826: "Not only then have I surely counselled [you] but I have also persuaded [you] concerning these things. But because you have not accepted aught of what has been said, while I also have surely been driven out by you, I have considered it needful by means of this testimony to make known unto you").
HEF 2997–3002 (EDITOR): "His demand, that they should wait four days longer, remained disregarded, and the first solemn session began under the presidency of Cyril". BH 929–948 (EDITOR): "Candidianus protests, reads his Imperial instructions, utters his contestatio, and on being overruled withdraws". Documented (his own text, two prints).

**E6. Nestorius summoned; soldiers at his door.** ACTS 18600–18606 (OWN, Cyril's side): "the holy Synod sent him admonition in accordance with the canons by bishops, a first, second, and third time. But he surrounding his house with soldiers, set himself up against the ecclesiastical laws, neither did he shew himself". HEF 2986–2991 (EDITOR): his house "surrounded with troops, who prevented the bishops, by threats of blows, from entering, and Nestorius sent them word that 'he would appear as soon as all the bishops were assembled.'" Nestorius's letter to the emperor "de his quae tunc in Epheso gesta sunt" (on what was then done at Ephesus), M5 76549–76555 (heading, Caput VIII), written after Cyril's party had met "inter semetipsos" (among themselves, 76655–76656), signed by Nestorius and his bishops (76715–76771); Latin, OWN, OCR long s normalized: "Secum vero seditionarios quosdam dispergentes in forum, civitatem confusione impleverunt, domos nostras publice circumdantes ... quaedam quoque intolerabilia comminantes. Princeps vero seditionis factus est Memnon Episcopus. Et sanctas Ecclesias sanctaque ecclesiastica martyria et sanctam Apostoli basilicam nobis quidem clausit ... Et mortem minatur omnibus nobis" (M5 76673–76689: scattering agitators in the forum, they filled the city with confusion, openly surrounding our houses and threatening things not to be borne; the leader of the riot was Memnon the bishop; he shut the holy churches, the martyrs' shrines and the Apostle's church to us; and he threatens all of us with death); and M5 76709–76711: "Alias jubete nos absque periculo redire ad propria. Minantur enim et ipsa nos vita privare" (otherwise order us to go home without danger, for they threaten even to take our lives). BH 1036–1038 (EDITOR): after the first session "Cyril, Nestorius, and Candidianus all write to Emperor". Documented as each side's claim. Keep both: soldiers at his house (Cyril's side) and the mob at their houses, the shut churches and the threats of death (his side).

**E7. The first session.** ACTS 17324–17326 (editor's note on the acts): "in the Acts Cyril is described as having 'the place of the most holy and sacred Archbishop of the Roman Church Cœlestine.'" EV 1337–1339 (LATER): "under the presidency of the divine Cyril, occupying the post of Celestine". ACTS 15669–15671: the Nicene Creed recited first. ACTS 15814–15828: Cyril asked whether his letter agreed with "the creed set forth by the holy and great synod formerly assembled at Nice"; the bishops agreed. ACTS 15834–15847: Nestorius's letter read; "All the bishops cried out together: Whoever does not anathematize Nestorius let him be anathema". ACTS 17237–17247: the letter of Capreolus of Carthage, who could not come, asking that "novelties" be rejected. HEF 3060–3068 (EDITOR): 126 bishops spoke for Cyril's letter, 34 against Nestorius's. Documented.

**E8. The sentence.** ACTS 17263–17280 (OWN, the decree): "As, in addition to other things, the impious Nestorius has not obeyed our citation, and did not receive the holy bishops who were sent by us to him, we were compelled to examine his ungodly doctrines ... we have come, with many tears, to this sorrowful sentence against him, namely, that our Lord Jesus Christ, whom he has blasphemed, decrees by the holy Synod that Nestorius be excluded from the episcopal dignity, and from all priestly communion." Greek, HEF 3238–3253: "δακρύσαντες πολλάκις, ἐπὶ τὴν σκυθρωπὴν κατ᾽ αὐτοῦ ἐχωρήσαμεν ἀπόφασιν" (weeping often, we came to this sorrowful sentence against him). HEF 3268–3271 (EDITOR): "subscribed by 198 bishops who were present. Some others afterwards took the same side, so that altogether over 200 subscribed." Documented. Plain words: "removed him from office", "deposed".

**E9. The torches.** Cyril's letter to his clergy and people (M4 109151–109189; Latin, OCR damaged; OWN): "Cum autem totus dies insumptus ibidem esset ... depositionis sententia subjecimus ... episcopi autem qui convenimus, ducenti circiter eramus, plus, minus. Universus vero civitatis populus a primo diei ortu usque ad vesperam sanctae synodi judicium praestolabatur. Ut autem auditum est, illum blasphemiarum auctorem sua dignitate privatum esse, omnes una voce sanctam synodum laudare, Deumque glorificare coeperunt ... nos quoque, ubi primum ex ecclesia egressi fuimus, cum facibus et taedis usque ad diversorium deduxerunt: erat enim vespera: multaque passim laetitia, multa etiam luminaria accensa, ita ut mulieres quoque thuribula gestantes antecederent nos." (The whole day spent there; about two hundred bishops; the whole people waited from dawn to evening; on hearing, they praised the synod; they led us with torches to our lodging, for it was evening; many lights lit, so that women carrying censers went before us.) HEF 3273–3291 (EDITOR) confirms: the population "waited the whole day"; they accompanied the bishops "with torches and censers to their houses. The city was also illuminated in many places. This is joyfully related by Cyril". Documented as Cyril's account; his letter is partisan. The damaged Latin is confirmed in substance by Hefele's English; paraphrase only.

**E10. John's assembly, 26 or 27 June.** ACTS 17807–17809 (Hefele in the editor's excursus, EDITOR): "it was only on June 26th or 27th, that John of Antioch arrived at last at Ephesus." ACTS 17834–17846: John held "a Conciliabulum with his adherents, at which, first of all Count Candidian related how Cyril and his friends, in spite of all warnings, and in opposition to the imperial decrees, had held a session five days before"; Candidian "declared that Nestorius had been condemned unheard." ACTS 17852–17854 and 17904: "forty-three members". BH 1042–1050 (EDITOR): "Forty-three bishops are present, and Candidianus. They depose Cyril and Memnon, and excommunicate all their adherents who will not repudiate Cyril's XII Anathematisms." John's sentence, Latin in M4 111477–111521 (Mansi's Latin beside a garbled Greek column), English in ACTS 17886–17903 (Hefele's translation): "because you held a separate assembly from a heretical, insolent, and obstinate disposition, although we were already in the neighbourhood ... in order to prevent the examination of your Apollinarian, Arian, and Eunomian heresies, and have not waited for the arrival of the holy bishops of all regions, and have also disregarded the warnings and admonitions of Candidian, therefore shall you, Cyril of Alexandria, and you Memnon of this place, know that you are deposed". Hefele inserts his own sneer, "[as if they had surrendered it!]" (17900): EDITOR, not the sentence. OWN of John's side (through Hefele's English, checked against M4's Latin "scitote vos depositos", 111515–111519). John's assembly's own report to the emperor, NPNF 3 Letter CLII (npnf203…xml 36847–36880; OWN of John's side, English; the editor's notes at 36839–36846 and 36862–36864 are EDITOR): deposition of Cyril and Memnon; Cyril's Chapters 'full of the Eunomian and Arian heresies'. Documented. Plain words: Cyril's twelve anathemas = "twelve curses Cyril had attached to his last letter to Nestorius" (ACTS 15964, 16291 headings).

**E11. Cyril's council answers.** ACTS 17710–17733 (circular letter, OWN): John's men were "a little more than thirty in number ... having for the leader of their schism John, Bishop of Antioch"; "the Holy Synod, by one common decree, deposed them from all ecclesiastical communion". ACTS 18650–18660: John met "before he had even shaken off the dust of the journey"; the letter calls his thirty partly men "without sees" and "Pelagians and the followers of Celestius". ACTS 18688–18714: John summoned three times, did not come; the council deprived him and his of communion and "all priestly power", leaving the final sentence "to the decision of your holiness" (Celestine). Theodoret of Cyrrhus is named among the thirty-odd (ACTS 17719). Documented. Count disputed (D4).

**E12. Rome's legates.** ACTS 17321–17323: Celestine's legates "Arcadius and Projectus, as also ... Philip, a presbyter" entered at the second session. ACTS 17615–17625 (third session, Philip): "We have found from the minutes that all things have been decided in accordance with the canons and with ecclesiastical discipline." ACTS 17657–17659 (Arcadius): "Let Nestorius know that he is deprived of all episcopal dignity, and is an alien from the whole Church". ACTS 17531–17537 (acclamation): "To Cœlestine, a new Paul! To Cyril a new Paul! ... One faith of the Synod! One faith of the world!" Documented. Plain words: "legates" = "the bishop of Rome's envoys".

**E13. No other creed beside Nicaea's.** ACTS 18157–18162 (Canon VII, OWN): "the holy Synod decreed that it is unlawful for any man to bring forward, or to write, or to compose a different (ἑτέραν) Faith as a rival to that established by the holy Fathers assembled with the Holy Ghost in Nicæa." ACTS 18163–18178: penalties; the Exposition brought by the presbyter Charisius and Nestorius's doctrines condemned. ACTS 18185–18188: the canon "is found verbatim in the Acts—Actio VI." Documented. Plain words: "no one may put forward any other statement of faith beside the creed of Nicaea", the council of 325.

**E14. Pelagius's condemnation upheld.** ACTS 18748–18755 (letter to Celestine, OWN): "When there had been read in the holy Synod what had been done touching the deposition of the most irreligious Pelagians and Cœlestines, of Cœlestius, and Pelagius, and Julian ... we also deemed it right (ἐδικαιώσαμεν) that the determinations of your holiness concerning them should stand strong and firm. And we all were of the same mind, holding them deposed." ACTS 17742–17751 (Canon I): any metropolitan who joins "the assembly of the apostates" or adopts "the doctrines of Celestius" is cut off. Documented. Link: `pelagius`.

**E15. Other business (for the card).** ACTS 18395–18427 (Canon VIII): the bishops of Cyprus to ordain their own bishops without the bishop of Antioch; "the liberty which Our Lord Jesus Christ ... hath given us by his own Blood" (18417–18419). ACTS 18769–18803: the Messalians condemned and their book the "Asceticon" anathematized. Documented. Not told for length (Leave out).

**E16. The emperor steps in: three bishops arrested, August 431.** BH 1136–1141 (EDITOR): "Count John, the imperial commissioner, arrives at Ephesus. He announces the deposition of Nestorius, Cyril, and Memnon, puts them all under arrest, and reports the fact to the Emperor." HEF 4959–4997 (EDITOR, paraphrasing Count John's own report, Mansi IV 1397): he had "succeeded, by persuasion and force, as he must plainly declare, and in spite of the opposition of Cyril's party, in having the imperial letter read"; "Thus the deposition of Cyril, Nestorius, and Memnon had been proclaimed"; Candidian took custody of Nestorius, Cyril was given to Count Jacobus, and Memnon "had been there arrested". HEF 4932–4935: John arrived "at the beginning of August" (EDITOR). Documented (Widely Accepted for the month).

**E17. The council dissolved.** HEF 6184–6199 (EDITOR's English of the emperor's edict, Mansi IV 1465 Greek, V 805 Latin): "I command that the Oriental bishops return to their churches, and that the Ephesine Synod dissolve. Cyril, too, is to return to Alexandria (to his diocese), and Memnon shall remain bishop of Ephesus. At the same time we also give it to be known that, as long as we live, we shall not condemn the Orientals, for they have not been confuted in our presence". HEF 6166–6167: Maximian ordained bishop of Constantinople. HEF 6205–6207: Cyril "arrived at Alexandria on the 3d of Athyr, that is, October 30, 431". BH 264–269 (EDITOR): the council "was dissolved in September ... without the two parties ... having come to an agreement. Nestorius was bidden to return to his monastery at Antioch, and Maximian was consecrated". BH 1178–1185 (EDITOR): Theodosius received eight delegates from each side at Chalcedon on 11 September, no agreement. Documented.

**E18. The peace of 433.** HEF 7140–7143, 7158–7163 (EDITOR): John gave Paul of Emisa a form of faith for Cyril; "this ... is quite the same formula which the Antiochenes at Ephesus had previously presented through Count John to the Emperor". BH 1146–1153 (EDITOR): at Ephesus "the Orientals draw up as a basis of reconciliation ... a letter including the formulary which is later known as the Formulary of Reunion." The confession as Cyril copied it into his letter to John:
- Greek, PUS 3517–3525 (OCR damaged): "ὁμοούσιον ἡμῖν κατὰ τὴν ἀνθρωπότητα· δύο γὰρ φύσεων ἕνωσις γέγονε· διὸ ἕνα Χριστὸν, ἕνα Υἱὸν, ἕνα Κύριον ὁμολογοῦμεν. κατὰ ταύτην τὴν τῆς ἀσυγχύτου ἑνώσεως ἔννοιαν ὁμολογοῦμεν τὴν ἁγίαν παρθένον θεοτόκον, διὰ τὸ τὸν Θεὸν Λόγον σαρκωθῆναι καὶ ἐνανθρωπῆσαι". Confirmed by a second Greek print, HEF 7243–7245, also damaged (OCR: "δύο γὰρ φύσεων ἕνωσις: γέγονε" ... "κατὰ ταύτην τὴν τῆς ἀσυγχύτευ ἱνώσεως" ... "ἁγίαν παρθένον διοτόκον, διὰ τὸ τὸν Θεὸν Λόγον σαρχωθῆναι"; the words between are cut off at the line ends), with Hefele's English at HEF 7187–7192 ("For two natures are united together ... Therefore we acknowledge one Christ, one Lord, and one Son"), and by Pusey's English, PUS 5217–5223: "for an Union hath taken place of two natures, wherefore we confess one Christ, One Son, One Lord. According to this idea of inconfused Union we confess the holy Virgin Mother of God, by reason that God the Word was made Flesh and made Man". Also ACTS 19487 (read at Chalcedon).
- Who composed it: PUS 5175–5178 (Cyril, OWN): Paul handed him "a paper, which had an irreprehensible confession of the Faith, and affirming that this had been composed both by your Holiness and the most devout Bishops with you."
- Cyril's answer: PUS 3217–3221 (Greek, damaged), 5120–5124 (English): "Let the Heavens rejoice and the earth be glad, for the mid-wall of partition is undone"; ACTS 19428–19430: "'Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad' for the middle wall of partition has been taken away". OWN.
- The price: HEF 7463–7477 (EDITOR's English of John's letter, Mansi V 285): "we also determined to agree to the judgment pronounced against Nestorius, to recognize him as deposed, and to anathematize his infamous doctrines ... We also agree to the consecration of the most holy and God-fearing bishop, Maximian of Constantinople". HEF 7224–7225: Nestorius's condemnation "was the conditio sine qua non of their Church communion" (EDITOR). BH 1323–1325 (EDITOR): "The question of the XII Anathematisms is left unmentioned by both sides".
- The date: HEF 7582–7589 (EDITOR): Cyril's sermon of 28 Pharmuthi, "April 23 (probably of the year 433)"; Tillemont infers "probably concluded in March 433". BH 1250, 1340 (EDITOR): 433. Widely Accepted for 433.
- Not all followed: BH 1271–1273 (EDITOR): "Theodoret agrees on the doctrinal question, but dislikes the abandoning of Nestorius. Andrew wavers and Alexander stands out." BH 1326–1343: at the synod of Zeugma, "Theodoret, Andrew, and John of Germanicia acknowledge the orthodoxy of Cyril, but refuse to accept the deposition of Nestorius. Alexander and some Cilician bishops renounce both Alexandria and Antioch." Documented (EDITOR's chronology from the letters in Mansi V).
- SOC 20949–20952 (CONTEMP): John at home "deposed Cyril ... but soon afterwards, having set aside their enmity and accepting each other as friends, they mutually reinstated each other in their episcopal chairs." Documented as Socrates's summary.

## Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

**I1. In the empire, the verdict on Nestorius held, and Chalcedon built on Ephesus.** ACTS 20214–20217 (Definition of Chalcedon, 451): "observing the order and every form relating to the faith, which was observed by the holy synod formerly held in Ephesus, of which Celestine of Rome and Cyril of Alexandria, of holy memory, were the leaders". ACTS 20256–20262: Chalcedon "receives the synodical letters of the Blessed Cyril, Pastor of the Church of Alexandria, addressed to Nestorius and the Easterns ... for the refutation of the frenzied folly of Nestorius". ACTS 20237–20239: it condemns those "refusing [to use] the name Mother of God (Θεοτόκος)". OWN of Chalcedon. Documented. Paraphrase only: Chalcedon's text is its own story's (slate bar). The letter "to the Easterns" is the letter of peace to John (E18). Defended: Mary as Mother of God; the creed of Nicaea unchanged (E13). Nestorius's later fate: BH 270–274 (EDITOR): edicts of August 435 ordered his writings burned, and the next year he was banished (the `nestorius` story's; one clause at most).

**I2. Evagrius carried the council's account on.** EV 1333–1370 (LATER, about 593): he retells the sixteen days (fifteen in his count), the three summonses and the decree word for word ("we have of necessity proceeded to the investigation of his impieties"). Documented that the decree circulated as the council's verdict. For the card.

**I3. The Church of the East, beyond the empire, never took the council as its own.** WIG 9271–9274 (1910, EDITOR / modern): "The Assyrian Church had certainly not accepted Ephesus — nor had they formally rejected it — nor had they ever been asked to do either." WIG 6738–6741: "men's minds were thus prepared for the separation that was to follow fifty years later. For the time being, however, the Church took no corporate action." BAD1 7676–7681 (1852): the Easterns, "refusing to obey, and choosing rather to espouse the cause of Nestorius, they were cut off from the communion of the Catholic Church, by the decree of the general council of Ephesus, a.d. 431". BAD2 5921–5929 (1852): "It is to me a matter of great surprise that the Nestorian rituals contain no formal condemnation of the Council of Ephesus. The excommunication of Nestorius is frequently referred to and censured, but no mention whatever is made of the Council which expelled him from the Church." Contested (D6): Badger and Wigram disagree on the decree; both are modern outsiders; credit each.

**I4. How the Church of the East remembered it.** BB 31905 (chapter list): "Histoire du pieux Mar Nestorius" (the history of the pious Mar Nestorius; the same title again at 41331); BB 31911–31912: "Reponse aux accusations de l'impie Cyrille. On connaitra par la les belles actions de saint Nestorius" (reply to the accusations of the impious Cyril; by it the good deeds of saint Nestorius will be known); BB 34044–34056 (LATER, about 600, Nau's French; Nau dates the work to the end of the sixth century, BB 31577): Cyril and Memnon "oserent porter un jugement inique"; "ils se reunirent comme en un theatre (θέατρον) de folie, ils s'assemblerent dans un coin (γωνία) d'une eglise, et ils menaient tout le monde signer, les uns a l'aide de l'or et les autres par des coups" (they gathered as in a theatre of madness, in a corner of a church, and brought everyone to sign, some with gold and others with blows). Hostile; Contested as events; Documented as the Church of the East's memory. BAD2 5932–5969 (1852): the account of Sliba ibn Yohannan of Mosul, 1332, Arabic in Syriac letters, in Badger's English, with "numberless mistakes" (5935): John's bishops refused the verdict, "maintaining that it was not right that an adversary should be judge" (5969). LATER (about 900 years). Documented as late memory only.

**I5. Nestorius's own verdict on the council.** BH 8643–8648 (OWN, written in exile, Syriac translation in Driver and Hodgson's English): "And I was summoned by Cyril who had assembled the Council, even by Cyril who was the chief thereof. Who was judge? Cyril. And who was the accuser? Cyril. Who was bishop of Rome? Cyril. Cyril was everything. Cyril was the bishop of Alexandria and took the place of the holy and saintly bishop of Rome, Celestinus." NAU 7136–7140 (same Syriac, French): "Je fus (ensuite) convoqué par Cyrille qui a réuni le concile; par Cyrille qui en était le chef. Qui était juge? Cyrille. Quel était l'accusateur? Cyrille. Qui était évêque de Rome? Cyrille. Cyrille était tout. Cyrille était évêque d'Alexandrie et il tenait la place du saint et vénérable évêque de Rome, Célestin." BH 8627–8640: "Cyril then is the persecutor and the accuser ... He did all things with authority, after excluding from authority him who had been charged by the emperor" (the editors' note 8657: "Viz. Candidianus"). Documented as his view; written about twenty years later (the `nestorius` sheet, E13: about 451).

## Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. The day.** Cyril: 28 Payni (M4 109169–109170), which Hefele reckons 22 June (HEF 2952–2953); Candidian's protest dated 22 June (M5 76870–76877). Socrates: the deposition on 28 June (SOC 20944–20945). Say "in June 431".

**D2. The order of events.** Socrates (SOC 20900–20914): Nestorius's party deposed Cyril and Memnon before John arrived; John then censured Cyril, and Cyril and Juvenal deposed John. The acts and Hefele (ACTS 17834–17903): John's own assembly deposed Cyril after he arrived. Socrates also has Nestorius cry "Let Mary be called Theotocos" (SOC 20915–20921), a retreat no other witness gives (Contested; the `nestorius` sheet D5). Follow the acts for the order, crediting them; Socrates's order for the card.

**D3. Why John was late.** The council: a hidden plan, friendship with Nestorius (ACTS 18574–18578, 18594–18597). John: the road and dead horses (HEF 2937–2941, his letter). Evagrius, a century and a half later: not intentional (EV 1303–1307). Contested. Tell the council's charge and John's reason, credited.

**D4. How many.** Cyril's council: "over two hundred" (ACTS 18567); "about two hundred, more or less" (Cyril, M4 109175–109177); 198 signatures (HEF 3268–3269). John's: forty-three (Hefele, ACTS 17852–17854; BH 1042); "a little more than thirty" (ACTS 17711–17712); "thirty men" (18654–18655). Nestorius's own: ten (BH 861) or sixteen (HEF 2883). Say "about two hundred" and "forty or so, by its own signatures; Cyril's council called them some thirty".

**D5. What Candidian did after.** John's assembly heard Candidian say Nestorius was "condemned unheard" (ACTS 17846). Hefele, hostile, says Candidian set soldiers on the council's members and cut off their food (ACTS 17830–17834): EDITOR, from the council's side; for the card.

**D6. The Church of the East.** Badger (BAD1 7676–7681): cut off by the decree of 431. Badger again (BAD2 5921–5929): its rites never formally condemn the council. Wigram (WIG 9271–9274): it never accepted, never formally rejected, never was asked; the real separation some fifty years later (6738–6739). Contested; credit each.

**D7. Did the bishops of the East submit willingly?** Barhadbeshabba (BB 34053–34055): signatures by gold and blows, about 600, hostile. The acts: votes freely given (ACTS 15822–15828). Contested. Barhadbeshabba's charge is the Church of the East's memory, credited to him.

**D8. Hefele's verdicts.** HEF and the ACTS excursus call John's charges "impudent and false accusations" and his reply "hypocritical meekness" (ACTS 17871–17872), and defend Cyril's presents (HEF 7384–7390). EDITOR; never in the narrator's voice.
