# Outline: Council of Ephesus (431)

Sheet items (S, E, I, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each. ACTS is the English of Cyril's council's acts (NPNF 14); M4 and M5 are Mansi's Latin (damaged OCR); BH is Nestorius's book in Driver and Hodgson's English (1925), with their dated chronology (EDITOR); NAU the same book in Nau's French; PUS Cyril's letters in Pusey's Greek and English; SOC Socrates; EV Evagrius; BB Barhadbeshabba (Nau's French); HEF Hefele (a historian, on Cyril's side); WIG Wigram; BAD1, BAD2 Badger.

- **The essence:** In June 431, at Ephesus, bishops called by the emperor Theodosius II met over one question: could Mary rightly be called Mother of God, the one who gave birth to God, and so how God and a man are one in Christ (S1, S2, E1; Documented). Cyril of Alexandria opened the council before John of Antioch and the bishops of Syria arrived, over the protest of the emperor's officer, and it deposed Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople; John's smaller assembly deposed Cyril in turn, and the two sides made peace only in 433 (E5, E8, E10, E18; Documented). Its verdict on Nestorius held in the empire; the Church of the East, in Persia, never made the council its own (I1, I3; Documented for the empire, Contested for the Church of the East, credited to Wigram).
- **Also told:**
  - **How it ran.** The emperor's officer, Count Candidian, begged the bishops to wait four days, and says they drove him out (E5; Documented, his own protest). Nestorius, summoned three times, would not come until all were there; Cyril's council says soldiers guarded his house, and Nestorius told the emperor his life was threatened (E6; each side's claim, credited). Cyril sat in the place of Rome's bishop (E7; Documented).
  - **What it decided.** Cyril's letter agreed with the creed of Nicaea and Nestorius's did not; he was removed from office and cut off from communion (E7, E8; Documented). No one may put forward any other statement of faith beside Nicaea's (E13; Documented). The condemnation of Pelagius and his followers was upheld (E14; Documented). John's men were cut off in turn, their final sentence left to Rome (E11; Documented).
  - **What the emperor did.** His new commissioner proclaimed Nestorius, Cyril and Memnon deposed and arrested all three; then the emperor dissolved the council, sent Cyril home and named a new bishop for Constantinople (E16, E17; Documented, from the commissioner's report and the edict as Hefele gives them).
  - **The peace of 433, and what it did to the churches.** John's side sent a confession that Christ is one, from a union of two natures, and Mary Mother of God; Cyril accepted it, and John accepted Nestorius's deposition (E18; Documented). Not all of John's bishops followed (E18; EDITOR's chronology from the letters). At Chalcedon in 451, the empire's churches built on Ephesus and on Cyril's letter of peace (I1; Documented). The Church of the East never accepted the council, nor formally rejected it (I3; Contested: Wigram's view, credited to him; Badger's contrary claim of 1852 is for the card).
- **Length:** about 4 minutes 30 (about 585 words at 130 a minute, the most the kit allows; the parts below, each counted at its fewest words with everything marked kept, come to about 550). Part by part, counting the quotes, the plain sense of each word to explain and each witness credited: the opening about 45 (Quote 2, 17 words); the essence about 75; beat 1 about 40; beat 2 about 20; beat 3 about 110 (Quote 1, 19 words; "with insult and by force"; the soldiers by the council's record; the sentence; Nestorius's letter on Memnon); beat 4 about 40; beat 5 about 25; beat 6 about 35; beat 7 about 75 (Quote 3, 34 words; the gloss of "nature"; Theodoret's clause); What came 1 about 30; What came 2 about 40; the ending about 25. Quotes together about 70; words to explain about 45; witnesses credited about 50. The record would carry more than 4:30. Cut for length, all under "For the card, not the story": the torches at nightfall, the dead horses and John's reason for delay, Evagrius's later verdict on it, Socrates's different order of events, Rome's envoys and their acclamations, Cyprus and the Messalians, Memnon kept in Ephesus, Nestorius's return to his monastery, the new bishop's name, the eight delegates at Chalcedon in 431, Cyril's "Let the heavens rejoice", Theodoret's earlier request to wait, Barhadbeshabba's "gold and blows", Badger's claim of 1852 that the decree cut the Easterns off, Sliba's late account, and Hefele's counts beyond "about two hundred" and "forty or so". Never cut Quote 1, the soldiers and Memnon's threats told both ways, John's sentence on Cyril, the arrest of all three, Quote 3 with John's acceptance of the deposition, Theodoret's refusal, or Wigram's verdict.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: June 431; the council met on 22 June by Hefele's reckoning of Cyril's Egyptian date (S1; D1). The emperor's summons, November 430 (S2, Hefele). Dissolved in the autumn of 431 (E17). Peace in 433 (E18). Plain words: "in June 431", "two years later, in 433".
  - Place: Ephesus, in its great church named for Mary (S1). Plain words: "the city of Ephesus", "the great church of Ephesus, named for Mary". Add no geography the sheet does not hold.
  - Tradition: the bishops of Egypt under Cyril; the bishops of Syria under John of Antioch; Rome's envoys; the Church of the East in Persia, beyond the empire (S4). Do not say the census labels aloud.

**Words to explain** (the first time each is heard; plain word or short gloss, worked into the sentence)

- **Mother of God / Theotokos**: "Mother of God, the one who gave birth to God" (E1). Use "Mother of God"; do not say "Theotokos" aloud. Not a quote: it is the term under dispute.
- **council**: "a meeting of bishops called to settle a question" the first time (census statusDescription).
- **deposed**: "removed from office" the first time; "deposed" may follow.
- **cut off from communion / excommunicated**: "shut out of the church's fellowship". Prefer "cut off".
- **the Word**: "God's Son, whom John's Gospel calls the Word" (E1; the `cyril-of-alexandria` story's gloss), only if beat 1 uses it.
- **nature**: "what Christ is, as God and as man" (the `council-of-chalcedon-451` story's gloss), in Quote 3's beat.
- **creed of Nicaea**: "the statement of faith made at the council of Nicaea, a century before" (E13; 325).
- **Count, commissioner**: "the emperor's officer" (S3); "the emperor's new commissioner, Count John" (E16). Keep the two Johns apart: say "Count John, the emperor's officer" once, and "John of Antioch" always in full.
- **anathemas** (in beat 4): "the twelve curses Cyril had added to his last letter to Nestorius" (E10).
- **envoys / legates**: "envoys of the bishop of Rome". Never "legates" aloud.
- **"Who was bishop of Rome? Cyril."** (Quote 2): the narrator says plainly, beside it, that Cyril presided in the place of Celestine, bishop of Rome (E7).
- **"without mixing"** (Quote 3): the narrator need add nothing; the rendering is already plain.

- **Shape:** Council. What called it: Nestorius's refusal to call Mary Mother of God, Cyril's answer, Rome's condemnation, the emperor's summons. The major issue and the spine: whether Mary is Mother of God, and so how God and a man are one in Christ; every beat returns to it, through the deposition, John's charge against Cyril's curses, and the confession of 433. Other business: no other creed beside Nicaea's; Pelagius's condemnation upheld; John's men cut off. Decisions: told in beats 3 to 5. Impact: whether the decisions held (the peace, Chalcedon) and whom they divided (Theodoret's refusal, the Church of the East). Voices followed: Candidian (his protest), Nestorius (his book), John of Antioch (his sentence and his confession), with Cyril through his own council's acts and letters. The trap is one dispute swallowing the council: Nestorius's own life, teaching and exile are his story's; here he is the one judged.
- **Sibling:** `nestorius` (live; job: his life and teaching, from his own words and the Church of the East's memory). It tells the quarrel over the name through Socrates and Nestorius, his exile, his book's pen name, "Let Nestorius be anathema" and Abdisho's "followed them, and not they him"; here the name is one clause, his exile one clause at most, and none of his quotes is used. `cyril-of-alexandria` (live; job: his life, Alexandria, Hypatia, his Christ, his legacy among the Miaphysites). It tells his letter of Mary, the gifts to the court and "the gold of iniquity"; here his council is told, his letter is paraphrased in one clause, and the gifts are left to his story. Both live texts' quotes stay barred (slate). `theodoret-of-cyrrhus` (batch; his part here is a clause: in 433 he accepted Cyril's faith but not Nestorius's deposition; his earlier request to wait is for the card). `pelagius` (batch; a clause: the council upheld his condemnation). `council-of-chalcedon-451` (live; tells 449 and 451); here 451 is one sentence on what it kept from Ephesus, in the narrator's words, with none of its quotes. `leo-of-rome` tells Ephesus 449: never confuse the two; say "the council of 431" or "this council". `john-cassian`: link only, not told.
- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen. "Who was judge? Cyril."** (I5; BH 8643–8648, NAU 7136–7140). In about three sentences: Nestorius, once bishop of Constantinople, writing in exile of the council that had removed him; Quote 2; then the plain sense, that Cyril of Alexandria had led it, sitting in the place of the bishop of Rome. Then straight into the essence. It is his later verdict, not an event, so no beat repeats it. Credit it as his own view, written years later.
  2. Not chosen: the torches at nightfall (E9). A strong scene, but it tells the deposition before the beats reach it, and it is Cyril's own partisan account; cut for length, for the card.

**Story beats** (the opening, the essence and beat 1 are the first minute, about 160 words; beats 2 to 7 deepen it)

1. **What called it** (E1, E2, S2, S3). Nestorius would not simply call Mary Mother of God; Cyril held that she was, because the one born of her was God's own Word made flesh (E1; one sentence, paraphrase, both credited; the name's history is the `nestorius` story's). Rome's bishop, Celestine, had already condemned Nestorius (E2; one clause; the name may go). The emperor, who summoned the leading bishops for Pentecost 431 (the essence tells the summons), sent Count Candidian to keep order, not to judge the faith (S2, S3; Documented; the edict as Hefele summarizes it).
2. **The wait** (E3, E4). Cyril came near Pentecost, with fifty bishops (E3; Socrates, and the editors' count). John of Antioch was late; after sixteen days, two of his bishops brought word not to put off the council for him (E4; Documented). Keep the council's charge that John delayed on purpose out of the narrator's voice (D3).
3. **22 June: the protest and the sentence** (E5, E6, E7, E8, E9). Candidian came to the church, read the emperor's letter and begged the bishops to wait just four days for John and those with him (E5). Then Quote 1, from his protest posted that day, with the narrator's clause that they drove him out "with insult and by force" (his words; short phrase). Nestorius, summoned three times, said he would come when all the bishops had come; Cyril's council says he had soldiers guard his house (E6; credit the council). Cyril presided, in the place of Rome's bishop (E7). The creed of Nicaea was read; the bishops judged that Cyril's letter agreed with it and Nestorius's did not (E7). "With many tears", the sentence says, they removed him from office and cut him off; about two hundred bishops signed (E8; "with many tears" may stand as a short phrase). Nestorius and his bishops wrote to the emperor that agitators had surrounded their houses, that Memnon, bishop of Ephesus, had shut the churches to them, and that he threatened them all with death (E6; their own letter; credit them). Documented as each side's account.
4. **John's assembly** (E10, E11). John of Antioch arrived some days later. In his lodging, forty or so bishops, by their signatures, heard Candidian say Nestorius had been condemned unheard (E10). They deposed Cyril and Memnon for holding their council apart, against the emperor's orders, and charged that Cyril's twelve curses held old heresies (E10; John's sentence; paraphrase). Cyril's council, which called them some thirty, cut them off in turn (E11; D4 credited both ways). Documented.
5. **The council's other business** (E13, E14). The council ruled that no one may put forward any other statement of faith beside the creed of Nicaea (E13). It upheld the condemnation of Pelagius and his followers, made earlier in the West (E14; link to `pelagius`; one clause). Documented.
6. **The emperor steps in** (E16, E17). In August the emperor's new commissioner had the emperor's letter read "by persuasion and force", as he reported (E16; short phrase, through Hefele's English of his report; credit "he reported"; give him by role, not as "Count John"). He proclaimed Nestorius, Cyril and Memnon deposed and put all three under arrest (E16). The emperor then dissolved the council and sent Cyril home to Alexandria, and a new bishop was made in Constantinople (E17). Documented.
7. **The peace of 433** (E18). John's side sent Cyril a confession, one it had first offered the emperor at Ephesus (E18; EDITOR). The narrator glosses "nature" before it. Quote 3. Cyril accepted it, and for his part John recognized Nestorius as deposed and the new bishop of Constantinople (E18; Documented; Hefele, a historian, says Cyril made the deposition the condition, HEF 7224–7225: do not say "in return" unless credited). Not all followed: Theodoret of Cyrrhus accepted Cyril's faith but not Nestorius's deposition (E18; the editors' chronology from the letters; one clause). Keep the price: the peace was bought with Nestorius's deposition.

**What came of them** (about a third of the story, with beat 7; heaviest first)

1. **In the empire, the verdict held.** At Chalcedon in 451, the next great council of the empire's churches kept the order of faith set at Ephesus under Celestine and Cyril, and took Cyril's letters to Nestorius and to the Eastern bishops as its guide (I1; paraphrase only; the letter "to the Eastern bishops" is the letter of peace). Chalcedon also condemned those who refused Mary the name Mother of God (I1, ACTS 20237–20239; optional clause). **The connection**: tell it here and in beat 7.
2. **Beyond it, the council divided.** The Church of the East, in Persia, kept Nestorius's memory; its own history, around 600, calls him "the pious Mar Nestorius" (I4; BB 41331; short phrase, credited to Barhadbeshabba). W. A. Wigram, an English historian of that church, wrote in 1910 that it never accepted the council, never formally rejected it, and was never asked to do either (I3; WIG 9271–9274; credit Wigram; paraphrase).

Defended: that Mary is Mother of God, because the one born of her is God made man (E1, E18); that no other creed may stand beside Nicaea's (E13). Against it, John's assembly defended that Cyril's curses were heresy and that a council must wait for all its bishops (E10, E5); the Church of the East held to Nestorius (I4). No held source in this sheet says who keeps the council today: do not say "today".

3. **The confession of 433.** Greek, as Cyril copied John's side's confession into his letter of peace: PUS 3519–3523 (damaged OCR), confirmed by a second Greek print, HEF 7243–7245: "δύο γὰρ φύσεων ἕνωσις γέγονε· διὸ ἕνα Χριστὸν, ἕνα Υἱὸν, ἕνα Κύριον ὁμολογοῦμεν. κατὰ ταύτην τὴν τῆς ἀσυγχύτου ἑνώσεως ἔννοιαν ὁμολογοῦμεν τὴν ἁγίαν παρθένον θεοτόκον". (Pusey's English, PUS 5217–5222: "for an Union hath taken place of two natures, wherefore we confess one Christ, One Son, One Lord. In keeping with this idea of a union without mixing, we confess the holy Virgin as Mother of God." John's side's own words, which Cyril accepted: both halves of the quarrel in one confession, "two natures" and "Mother of God". PUS 5175–5178 says John and his bishops composed it; the narrator credits it to them.

**Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from this council was a verdict and a confession. The verdict: Nestorius was removed and cut off, and the empire's churches held to it (E8, E17, I1). The confession: two years later, John of Antioch's bishops, who had deposed Cyril, accepted that verdict and confessed Mary Mother of God, while Cyril accepted their words "a union of two natures" (E18; PUS 5175–5223; HEF 7463–7477). At Chalcedon in 451, that letter of peace and Cyril's letter to Nestorius became the guide for the empire's churches (I1; ACTS 20256–20262). The listener must understand three things. First, that the quarrel over a name was a quarrel over Christ: whether the one born of Mary was God himself, made man (E1). Second, that the peace was real but had a price, Nestorius's deposition, and that some of John's own bishops, Theodoret among them, would not pay it (E18). Third, that the council's reach ended at the empire's border: the Church of the East, beyond it, kept Nestorius's name and never made the council its own (I3, I4). Tell it through what the bishops decided and what each church kept, not as a "this matters because" sentence.

**Ending:**
1. **Chosen. The rites that never name the council** (I3, D6; BAD2 5921–5929). George Percy Badger, an English writer on the Church of the East's rites, in 1852, was surprised: its rites often refer to Nestorius's being cut off, and blame it, yet contain no formal condemnation of the council, and make no mention of the council that expelled him. One or two sentences, credited to Badger by role or name; paraphrase. Do not say "lament" or "curse": Badger says "referred to and censured". It closes on the council's own reach, held at arm's length, which is the story's point.
2. Not chosen: Wigram's verdict as the last line (I3). It is the connection's evidence, and ends on a modern writer; keep it in What came 2.

**Do not say**

- Any word in "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard.
- Not "Theotokos" aloud. "Mother of God, the one who gave birth to God."
- Not that Nestorius "denied Christ was God", or "taught two Christs", in the narrator's voice. Cyril's council judged that he split Christ in two; he denied it (E1; the `nestorius` story's floor). Credit the charge.
- Not "the Council of Ephesus decided Mary is Mother of God" as a new decree. It judged Cyril's letter, which called her so, to agree with Nicaea, and condemned Nestorius; it wrote no new creed, and forbade one (E7, E13).
- Not "the council wrote a creed" or "the Ephesine Creed" for 431. The confession is of 433, from John's side, in Cyril's letter (E18).
- Not "Cyril refused to wait for John" as the narrator's judgment of motive, nor "John delayed on purpose". Tell what each side said: the council charged a hidden plan; John blamed the road (D3).
- Not "the emperor's officer was thrown out" in the narrator's voice. Candidian says they drove him out "with insult and by force": credit him (E5).
- Not "Nestorius's house was surrounded by Cyril's soldiers". Cyril's council says Nestorius surrounded his own house with soldiers; Nestorius says his opponents threatened his life (E6). Credit each.
- Not "two hundred bishops voted unanimously". About two hundred signed; Hefele counts 198 at first; say "about two hundred" (D4).
- Not "forty-three" as fact for John's assembly, nor "thirty". "Forty or so, by their signatures; Cyril's council called them some thirty" (D4).
- Not that Rome's envoys took part in the first session. They arrived after it and confirmed it (E12).
- Not "the pope" for Celestine. "The bishop of Rome".
- Not "the emperor sided with Nestorius" or "with Cyril" as fact. He had all three arrested, then dissolved the council (E16, E17).
- Not "John" in any form for the emperor's commissioner. Give him by role, "the emperor's new commissioner" (E16), so no listener confuses him with John of Antioch.
- Not "the council ended in September" or a day for the dissolution. "In the autumn of 431" (E17; the editors say September, Hefele has Cyril home on 30 October).
- Not "Cyril and John were reconciled because Cyril dropped his twelve curses". The record says only that neither side mentioned them (E18; EDITOR).
- Not "all of Syria accepted the peace". Theodoret accepted Cyril's faith but not the deposition; others refused (E18).
- Not "Cyril bribed the court" in the narrator's voice, nor any figure in gold: the gifts are the `cyril-of-alexandria` story's (Leave out).
- Not "the Church of the East rejected Ephesus" or "was condemned at Ephesus" as fact. Wigram: neither accepted nor formally rejected; Badger, once, says its bishops were cut off by the decree (I3, D6).
- Not "Nestorians" for the Church of the East in the narrator's voice. "The Church of the East" (the `nestorius` sheet, I3).
- Not "Chalcedon confirmed Ephesus" in Chalcedon's own words: its definition is its own story's. Paraphrase: it kept the order of faith set at Ephesus and took Cyril's letters as its guide (I1).
- Not the council of 449 at Ephesus, Leo's "robbery", or Dioscorus: another council, told in `leo-of-rome` and `council-of-chalcedon-451`.
- Not "today" for any church's keeping of the council.
- Not the peace of 433 before the emperor's arrests, and not Chalcedon before the peace.

**Leave out**

- Nestorius's sermons, the name's history in Constantinople, his exile, his book's pen name, his death: the `nestorius` story's. One clause on the name only.
- Cyril's life, Alexandria, Hypatia, his twelve curses in full, his gifts to the court and the debt in gold: the `cyril-of-alexandria` story's. The curses are one gloss in beat 4.
- The edict of 435 burning Nestorius's books: the `nestorius` story's.
- Socrates's retreat, "Let Mary be called Theotokos" (D2): Contested, one witness; for the card.
- Hefele's charges that Candidian starved the council and set soldiers on its envoys (D5): one hostile historian, from one side; for the card.
- The acclamations "One Cœlestine! One Cyril!" and Philip's words on Peter (E12): Rome's standing is a side story; for the card.
- John's men's "Pelagians" in the council's letter (E11): a charge, and it would blur the real Pelagius clause.
