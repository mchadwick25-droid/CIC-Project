# Outline check: Mesrop Mashtots

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** A royal secretary turned monk who, finding he could not preach to new Armenian converts without letters for their tongue, searched for and made the Armenian letters with the catholicos Sahak and King Vramshapuh, began putting the Scriptures into Armenian, and taught the letters in schools on both the Persian and the Roman side of a divided Armenia. That changed the Armenian church from one whose worship and learning ran in Syriac (and Greek), which the people did not understand, to one that read, sang and wrote in its own language; it is why he is on the tree, and nearly all of it rests on his pupil Koriun.

**Also told.** The search: Syrian Mesopotamia, the Syrian bishop Daniel's letters that failed, the vision of the letters in his heart (Koriun's wonder), the shaping with the hermit Rufinus. The first translation (Proverbs first) and the gift received by Sahak and the king "as if the divine Tablets". The schools, on the Persian side and then under Roman rule with the general's public support, and the force used against the Borborites in the same campaign. The Georgian and Albanian letters credited to him by Koriun (contested by the Georgian chronicle). The pupils sent to Edessa and Constantinople who came back with sound copies and the canons of Nicaea and Ephesus, and the correction of the first hasty versions. What he defended: Ephesus's faith, against "Theodore"'s books. His late life of teaching, hermit retreats, freeing captives; Sahak's death and his own, ten months after, with the cross of light (wonder). Kept as one of the Holy Translators.

## Verdict: FIX

One BLOCKING finding (the order of the Georgian and Albanian letters); the rest MINOR. The heart matches mine in substance: the letters, the Scriptures begun, the schools on both sides of the border, all resting on Koriun, with the search through the Syriac world, the force against the Borborites, the contested Georgian and Albanian letters, the pupils' sound copies and the canons of Ephesus. Nothing major is left out. Every quote is verbatim in both OCR copies (T2 3590-3594, 3620-3621, 3647-3648, 3651-3653; T12 55852-55857, 55883-55884, 55910-55916), with the T2 page break inside "sur la ... pierre" rightly closed. My own renderings: Q1 "At once he saw them, not in a dream as he slept, not in a vision as he watched, but in his heart, where they appeared to the eyes of his mind, traced by the wrist of a right hand writing on stone, just as one sees on snow the smallest strokes of a line"; Q2 "who rejoiced over them as though they were the divine Tablets"; Q3 "Mesrop gathered children who were bright and taught, with sweet voices and long breath". The outline's renderings agree on every clause and add nothing. No quote is on the tree: the census has "sweet voices" only in this story's own old text, and "defenders of the true faith" only as Mommsen's phrase in another person's card, which is a different source and not a shared quote. Names keep the census spelling (Koriun, Eznik of Kolb, Moses of Khoren, Lazar Parpetsi). The Library sweep holds: a search over `cic/texts/` (66 files counting Koriun's name forms) found nothing primary missing. Kiracos's retelling of Daniel and the emperor's rebuke (`brosset_deux-historiens-armeniens-kiracos-oukhtanes-fra_1870.txt` 6890-6910, 7075-7095) is a 13th-century retelling, rightly set aside as late. Eghishe's hits are all in Neumann's preface (an editor). The Ukhtanes part on the Georgian schism has no hit. The length after Cut first (645 by the independent method) is under 675.

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: the Georgian and Albanian letters are moved after the Roman journey

Koriun tells the Georgian alphabet and the renewal of the Aghouank letters right after the schools on the Persian side, then "il revint en Arménie", and only then the turn west to the Greek part (T2 3657-3664, then 3693-3704). Moses keeps the same order: the Georgian alphabet in III.54 (T2 23913-23933) comes before the letters to Theodosius (T2 24205-24340). The outline's beat 6 puts the Georgians after beat 5 (Constantinople, the treasury schools, the Borborites). Heard once, that tells a listener the Georgian letters came after Constantinople. That is a detail moved onto the wrong point in the life, and the beats then fail to follow the life in order.

Correction. Move the Georgian and Albanian sentences from beat 6 to the end of beat 4, and retitle both beats:
- Beat 4 title → **Letters to Scripture, schools, and the neighbours.** Append to beat 4: "Then, Koriun says, he went to the Georgians and gave them an alphabet, and to the Albanians, whose alphabet he renewed, leaving teachers in both lands, before he came back to Armenia (T2 3657-3664, 3693). Contested: the Georgian chronicle gives Georgia's script to an ancient king, Pharnavaz (KART 2228-2230), and its translator notes that the chronicle is silent on Mesrop (KART 7040, editor's)."
- Beat 6 title → **The pupils, and the defence.** Delete from beat 6 the two sentences beginning "Koriun says he went to the Georgians" and "Contested: the Georgian chronicle".
- In **Also told**, move item (6) to stand between (3) and (4), so the summary follows the same order.

### 2. MINOR: the Length field contradicts the outline's own count

The field still says "about 3 minutes 20 seconds, about 500 words", and its carry figures (85, 55, 90) are not the independent count's. Replace the field's first two sentences with: "About 645 words, about 4 minutes 18 seconds, once the Cut first list is applied (cap 675; the independent count before cuts is 784, `Length_Count.md`)." Delete "About 230 words carried, leaving about 270 for six beats, the essence and the ending." The margin is about 30 words. Nothing more may be added to the beats.

### 3. MINOR: What came 4 and Cut first 4 say he drove out "books"

Koriun says Sahak and Mesrop drove out the man himself "[et ses adhérents]" (T2 4105-4108). The books were his; they followed "the heresies of Paul of Samosata and Nestorius" (T2 4100-4102). "Condemned books" is not Koriun's word.
- What came 4: "he drove out the condemned teacher's books" → "he and Sahak drove out the man the council had shut out, who came with heretical books".
- Cut first 4: "him driving out the condemned teacher's books" → "Sahak and Mesrop driving out the man the council had shut out".
- Beat 6: "a man spreading condemned books" → "a man shut out from the Council of Ephesus, who came with heretical books".
- Do not say, the "Theodore of Mopsuestia" line: "a teacher of condemned books" → "a man who came with heretical books".

### 4. MINOR: the Connection says more than its passages

(a) "the faith reached Armenians through Syriac and Greek, which most could not follow". Lazar says the people could not follow Syriac (T2 36234-36240). Koriun says the Persian governors barred Greek learning (T2 3700-3704). No held line says the people could not follow Greek. Replace with: "the faith reached Armenians through Syriac, which, Lazar says, the people could not follow, while on the Persian side, Koriun says, Greek learning was barred".
(b) "That is how the Syriac and Greek churches shaped the Armenian one." The passages show one way, not the whole of it. Replace with: "That is one way the Syriac and Greek churches shaped the Armenian one: the search in Syrian Mesopotamia, the Greek model for the letters, and the pupils' work in Edessa and Constantinople."

### 6. MINOR: Do not say lacks three easy errors

Add:
- "Rufinus of Aquileia", or any hint that this is the translator on the tree → "a hermit named Rufinus". Koriun's text calls him a man of Samos and a disciple of an Epiphanius. He is not Rufinus of Aquileia, whose story is in this batch (slate caution).
- "The Persian governors burned the Greek books" → Koriun says the books had been burned earlier, by Meroujan, "à l'époque du partage de l'Arménie" (T2 3697-3700). The governors barred Greek learning.
- "Sahak and Mesrop went to Constantinople" → Sahak sent Mesrop and his nephew Vartan to the emperor and the patriarch (T2 3711-3726). Sahak stayed in the Roman part of Armenia.

### 7. MINOR: Words to explain lacks two words the story may use

Add:
- *vartabed* (only if the liturgy clause is used): "a teacher of the church; in old Armenian, the title of its learned fathers" (editor's note, T2 3757-3764). Or drop the word and say "Mesrop the teacher".
- *Syrian Mesopotamia* and *Edessa*: "the Syriac-speaking lands south of Armenia" and "a city of those lands, a centre of Syriac learning". Edessa as a seat of Syriac translation stands on T2 3808-3812 (pupils sent there to translate from the Syriac).
