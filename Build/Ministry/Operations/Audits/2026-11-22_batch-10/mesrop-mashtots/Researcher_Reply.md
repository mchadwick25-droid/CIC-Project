# Researcher reply (2026-11-23)

**Essence:** Koriun, his pupil, says Mesrop Mashtots, a royal secretary turned monk, made the Armenian letters so the faith could be preached, written and taught in Armenian. With the catholicos Sahak and King Vramshapuh he began putting the Scriptures into Armenian and set up schools on both the Persian and the Roman side.

**Also told:** why the letters were needed (Lazar adds that services ran in Syriac no one understood); the search in Syrian Mesopotamia, the failed letters of a Syrian bishop, and the letters made with Rufinus after the Greek alphabet; the trip west (Moses has the letters first refused there); the force used against the Borborites; the Georgian and Albanian letters, which the Georgian chronicle contests; the pupils bringing back the canons of Nicaea and Ephesus.

**Shape:** Missionary or translator.

**Length:** about 3 min 20 s, about 500 words. The quotes, glosses and credited witnesses carry about 230 of them.

**Connection:** the script, and through it Scripture and teaching in Armenian, shaped by the Syriac and Greek churches. His pupils carried it on through Edessa and Constantinople.

**Quotes** (renderings from Emine's French, 1869):
1. "At once he saw them. It was not in a dream while he slept, nor in a vision while he was awake, but in his heart. There they appeared to the eyes of his mind, traced by the wrist of a right hand writing on stone. It was just as one sees the smallest strokes of a line on snow."
2. "They rejoiced over them as if they were the divine Tablets."
3. "Mesrop gathered children who were bright and well taught, with sweet voices and long breath."

**Gaps:** Koriun's Armenian is not held, and no English Koriun is held, so the blind second readings carry the load. Nothing Mesrop wrote himself is held.

Checked for the four faults; that check moved the hermit years off "his last years".
