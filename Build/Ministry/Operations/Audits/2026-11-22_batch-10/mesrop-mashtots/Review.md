# Review: Mesrop Mashtots (2026-11-23)

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Mesrop Mashtots, a royal secretary who became a monk and, by his pupil Koriun's account, made the letters of the Armenian language.
- **What happened:** When a Syrian bishop's letters failed, he made the letters with a hermit, Rufinus. With Sahak and the king he started putting the Scriptures into Armenian and opened schools on both the Persian and the Roman side. Koriun also credits him with Georgian and Albanian letters. He fought the Borborites by force. His pupils brought back true copies and the canons of Nicaea and Ephesus.
- **Why it matters:** The faith could now be preached, written and taught in Armenian. The search ran through the Syriac world, and the letters were modelled on the Greek. His pupils became the next Armenian writers.

**Essence and what is also told.** The essence: he made the Armenian letters so that the faith could be preached and taught in Armenian, began the translation of Scripture and set up schools on both sides of the border. He is on the tree because the Armenian church took its written language, and through it its Scripture, from him. The story must also tell why letters were needed, the search through Syrian Mesopotamia, the welcome and the schools, the Georgian and Albanian claim and the rival chronicle, the Roman side, the force used against the Borborites, and the pupils and the canons. The hook is three sentences plus the vision quote, and it leads straight into the essence. The essence is complete at about 125 words, inside the first minute. Every part of what is also told is present and carries its weight. After the essence the life is told once and in order: Daron, monk, Koghten, Sahak, Mesopotamia, Proverbs, the schools, Georgia and Albania, the Roman side, the Borborites, the pupils, Ephesus, then the deaths. "Where the bishop's letters failed" and "Rising from prayer" point back to the hook without retelling the vision, as the outline directs, so this is not a double telling. The story ends on the opening image of the hand writing on stone. Nowhere does it read as a children's book or talk down.

**Names heard once that neither act nor link:** Daron and Koghten (places, harmless). Every named person either acts or links. I was not lost anywhere. The one stumble was the Borborites sentence (see B1).

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** The narrator is adult and documentary throughout. The quotes are Koriun's and they sound like him. The hook is strong. The close is quiet and earned.
- **Craft: 4.** The essence comes early and the life follows in order. One weak spot: the Persian bar on Greek learning is told twice (M1).
- **Rigor: 4.** The witnesses are credited well, and the contested Georgian claim and Moses's harder welcome are both told. One wording misstates who suffered what in the Borborite campaign (B1). Smaller slips are listed under M2 and M3.
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 8.7, reading ease 76.0, 18.2 words a sentence, longest sentence 31 words: pass. The glosses (catholicos, Syrian Mesopotamia, Albanians, Edessa, Tablets) are worked into the sentences.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**B1 (BLOCKING): misleading implication about the record's force.** The story says "Those who would not yield, he writes, were put in prison and chains and tormented, and the rest were burned, shut up and driven from the land." Heard once, "the rest" means the other people, that is, those who did yield. Koriun describes one sequence suffered by the same people (T2 3781-3787): those who would not yield were imprisoned, chained and tormented, and "lorsqu'après cela ces hommes ... refusaient", when they still refused after that, they were burned, shut up and expelled. Change it to: "...were put in prison and chains and tormented; those who still refused were burned, shut up and driven from the land." The outline left room for this error, since its beat 5 itself says "the rest". Fix that beat too, and add to Do not say:
- "the rest were burned" → "those who still refused were burned, shut up and driven from the land" (T2 3784-3787). It was the same people, after the torture, not a second group.

**M1 (MINOR): one condition told twice.** "On the Persian side of the divided country, Koriun says, only Syriac learning was allowed" (paragraph 4) and "On the Persian side Greek learning was barred" (paragraph 9) state one fact twice. In Koriun the bar is the reason Sahak and Mesrop turned west (T2 3700-3708). Keep it once. Either keep the later sentence and cut the earlier one, or keep the earlier one for the connection and open paragraph 9 with "So Sahak and Mesrop went west into Roman Armenia".

**M2 (MINOR): Constantinople.** "Sahak and Mesrop sent pupils to Constantinople and to Edessa." In Koriun they sent Eznik and Joseph to Edessa, and from there the pupils "passèrent" to Byzantium to study (T2 3818-3828). Say: "Sahak and Mesrop sent pupils to Edessa, a center of Syriac learning, and from there they went on to Constantinople." Add to Do not say:
- "sent pupils to Constantinople" → "sent pupils to Edessa; from there they went on to Constantinople" (T2 3818-3828).

**M3 (MINOR): a date placed on the wrong event.** "In the early 400s, Mesrop went to Sahak." This attaches the editor's frame to the meeting with Sahak, which Koriun does not date, and the editor's dates for Sahak's see begin in 390. If a date is kept, put it on the letters: Koriun dates them under Theodosius the Younger, and the outline gives "in the early 400s" as the editor's spoken frame. Otherwise drop it.

**Writer's dropped details.** None of them was needed. The optional Lazar line on services in Syriac would strengthen the outline's first connection point, but "only Syriac learning was allowed" carries enough of it. "By order of the catholicos and the king" is covered by "With the king and Sahak." "Eznik among them" is covered by Eznik's naming among the pupils.
