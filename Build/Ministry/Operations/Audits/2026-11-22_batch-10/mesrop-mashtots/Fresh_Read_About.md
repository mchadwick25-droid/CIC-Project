## The four lines

- **Who:** Mesrop Mashtots, a royal secretary who became a monk in early fifth-century Armenia, known almost entirely through his pupil Koriun.
- **What happened:** He found no way to write Armenian. A Syrian bishop's letters failed. With a hermit, Rufinus, he made new letters after the Greek alphabet, in what Koriun tells as an inner vision. With the catholicos Sahak and the king he began translating the Scriptures and opened schools in Persian and Roman Armenia. He fought a sect by force, sent pupils for true copies of Scripture, drove out a follower of Nestorius, and died blessing his disciples.
- **What it was about:** Giving the faith a written Armenian of its own, so it could be preached, read and taught in the people's language instead of Syriac.
- **Why the connection matters:** The search ran through the Syriac world and the letters followed the Greek. His pupils (Koriun, Eznik, Moses of Khoren) became the next Armenian writers, and Lazar says the churches filled once the letters and Scriptures came.
