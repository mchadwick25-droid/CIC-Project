With the king and the catholicos Sahak he began translating Scripture, starting with Proverbs. He opened schools on the Persian side, took the work west into Roman Armenia, and fought the Borborites by force. Koriun credits him with the Georgian and Albanian letters. His pupils brought true copies of the Scriptures and the canons of Nicaea and Ephesus home from Edessa and Constantinople. He died blessing his disciples.

## Findings

1. **MINOR: a "So" that hangs far from its cause.** In "So Sahak and Mesrop went west into Roman Armenia", the cause (only Syriac learning allowed on the Persian side) was heard four paragraphs earlier. This sentence comes straight after the Georgian chronicle. Heard once, the "So" seems to follow from Georgia. Koriun gives the cause here ("Pour cette raison", T2 3704). Change it to: "Back in Armenia, with Greek learning barred on the Persian side, Sahak and Mesrop went west into Roman Armenia."
2. **MINOR: "letters" in two senses.** In "Sahak sent Mesrop with letters to the emperor and the bishop of Constantinople", "letters" means correspondence. Two sentences later, "the letters were first refused" means the alphabet. Change the first to: "Sahak wrote to the emperor and the bishop of Constantinople and sent Mesrop to them, and the bishop named him a preacher."
3. **MINOR: an unclear "eager".** In "Mesrop went to Sahak, who was even more eager", the listener has not been told what Mesrop wanted. Change it to: "Mesrop went to Sahak about letters, and found him even more eager." (T2 3527-3530: "pour l'entretenir au sujet de l'alphabet ... encore plus désireux que lui".)
4. **MINOR: one sentence past 30 words.** "With the king and Sahak, the catholicos who led the Armenian church, he began putting the Scriptures into Armenian and founded schools on both sides of the Persian and Roman border" is 31 words. Split it after "church": "He worked with the king and with Sahak, the catholicos who led the Armenian church. Together they began putting the Scriptures into Armenian and founded schools on both sides of the Persian and Roman border."

None of these is an error the outline left room for, so no Do not say line is added.
