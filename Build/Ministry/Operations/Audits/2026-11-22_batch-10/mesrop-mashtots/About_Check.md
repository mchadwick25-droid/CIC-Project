## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: item 8, the Borborites. The planned line would mislead a listener

Planned: "He fought the Borborites, a sect Koriun calls rash and insolent, without saying what they taught. Latin and Syriac church writers of that century charged a sect of that name with foul rites and sexual license."

Each half matches its source on its own. Augustine (AUG 1257-1259) says some call the Gnostics "Borboritas ... quasi caenosos", for the foulness "quam in suis mysteriis exercere dicuntur". Marutha's list (MAR 3563-3567) refuses to describe the "Borborianer" for their "Unsittlichkeit und grossen Unzucht". The brief, though, puts these charges straight after Koriun's sect and straight before the prison, chains and burning. Heard once, that order says three things the record does not hold:

- **That the two groups are one.** The outline itself rates that link Inferential-Thin: the name is all they share. The listener hears no rating. The order alone tells the link as fact, which is the first of the kit's four faults, "one witness's claim told as fact".
- **That the charges were the reason for the force.** Koriun gives no reason beyond his own words "rash and insolent" (T2 3779-3787). Hearing "sexual license" just before "were burned" gives the force a cause, and the kit's Rigor rules say any implied cause "must be in the sources".
- **That the gap is answered.** The question was what they taught. The planned line gives no teaching, only rumors about rites. Augustine's own "dicuntur" ("are said to") marks his as hearsay, and he applies the name to Gnostics in general. Hearing a foul charge in place of a teaching would leave the listener feeling answered when nothing is known.

A spot-check of `cic/texts/` confirms the teaching is not held for Koriun's sect. The other hits are a different group or another writer's charge. The ANF Irenaeus chapter heading "Barbeliotes or Borborians" (anf01 33686) is the translator's heading. The Theodosian Code lists "Borboriani" among banned heretics without saying what they held (theodosianus-16 88533, 88560). Langlois's notes (T2 3536-3537, 3814-3820, 23363-23365) are the editor's. Moses's "secte criminelle" (T2 24418-24424) is already on the card. The kit says a story names a teaching only with its plain sense, and that it says plainly what is uncertain. The right course here is to state the honest limit, not to fill it with enemies' charges about what may be another group. The force stays in full, so nothing is softened. The name stays too: it is Koriun's word, and the card already carries it.

**Replacement for Room for it, item 8 bullet:**

> - Item 8, after "rash and insolent.": "Koriun never says what they taught." (+6)

The brief then reads: "He fought the Borborites, a sect Koriun calls rash and insolent. Koriun never says what they taught. Those who would not yield, he writes, were put in prison and chains and tormented; those who still refused were burned, shut up and driven from the land."

**Replacement for What it was about, item 8:**

> 8. **The Borborites (Gaps 1).** Explain the limit: Koriun never says what they taught (T2 3779-3787); say that plainly and tell nothing more. Not used: Augustine's and Marutha's charges against a sect of that name (AUG 1257-1259; MAR 3563-3567). Told beside Koriun's sect, they would sound like his sect's teaching and the reason for the force, but the link is the name only (Inferential-Thin; Langlois's note, T2 3814-3820, makes it and is the editor's). To the card, credited as those writers' charges, with the link rated Inferential-Thin. Also not used: MAR's bracketed "[Obscönitäten]" and "Blut [von Kindern]", which are the translator's, and Moses's "secte criminelle" (T2 24418-24424), already on the card. Documented (Koriun's silence).
