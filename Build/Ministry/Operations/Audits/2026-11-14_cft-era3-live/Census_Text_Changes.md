## Open-Gaps 253: `the-church-of-the-east-under-persia`, documentedStories.1.text

- Old: The school split. Barhadbshabba, writing the school’s own history, and the church’s synods of 585, 596 and 605 record the sequence: the catholicos Ishoyahb condemned Henana’s teaching without naming him; Henana kept his post under the protection of the Persian governor; and finally, by the traditional count, three hundred students walked out in a body rather than sit under him, leaving the school with a remnant.
- New: The school split. Barhadbshabba, writing the school’s own history while Henana still taught, praises him. The church’s synods of 585 and 596 condemned teaching that Jérôme Labourt takes as aimed at Henana and his followers, though the acts never name him. The Chronicle of Seert tells it as a fight inside Nisibis. Gregory, metropolitan of the city, condemned Henana’s books, and Henana sought help from the king’s physicians. In the Chronicle the catholicos Sabrisho takes Henana’s side, though Labourt reads Sabrisho’s own synod as against the Henanians. Moved by Sabrisho’s decision, about three hundred students walked out, and only twenty people, and scarcely as many boys, were left in the school.
