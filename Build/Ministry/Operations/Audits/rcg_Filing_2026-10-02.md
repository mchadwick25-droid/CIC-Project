### `Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md`

> **Living Tradition Status:** Not confirmed. Per this project's established practice (`Build/worlds/ijc/Doc_01...md` §1, citing `Build/worlds/syr/Open_Gaps_Tracking.md`, item 10, the "PENDING project-lead confirmation" precedent dated 2026-07 in that log), Living Tradition Status confirmation is a project-lead act this document cannot perform. Noted for the record, not decided here: Gregory I is a Doctor of the Church specifically in the Roman Catholic tradition; Eastern Orthodoxy has no equivalent formal "Doctor of the Church" category, though Gregory is venerated there as a saint, known as Gregory the Dialogist — a comparatively direct and traceable line of living continuity across two traditions, on different formal terms in each, though whether and how to name it is the project lead's own call, not this document's. **Status: PENDING.**

> **A counter-example directly engaged, not assumed away — the abbot Equitius (Dialogues, Book I).** Round 1 review raised, correctly, whether this document's one-strand finding too quickly conflated single authorship with a single ground of authority, citing Gregory's own account of Equitius: an unordained abbot who preached and taught "not having received holy orders, nor yet licence of the Bishop of Rome," his authority instead resting on a vision commissioning him directly. This is real, and it names a genuinely different ground of authority — charismatic, vision-authorized, independent of ecclesiastical office — appearing within this world's own Native corpus. It does not, on examination, change this document's own strand finding, for a reason worth stating precisely rather than assumed: **Strand Determination asks what ground of authority *this world's own defining voice* — Gregory himself — claims for himself**, not what authority structures Gregory, as narrator, records other figures holding. Gregory himself never claims charismatic, vision-authorized authority of this kind for his own office anywhere in this world's Native corpus; his own claim remains Petrine and pastorally-exercised throughout, exactly as this document already argues.

> **What this world transmits to subsequent worlds/history:** the institutional and administrative pattern of the medieval papacy more broadly (well beyond this project's own window); the *Pastoral Rule*'s own long reception (Carolingian episcopal legislation; Alfred the Great's Old English translation, three centuries on); the Gregorian mission to Kent, which `anglo-saxon-christianity`'s own build (not yet started) would need to treat as an inheritance from this world, not an independent origin; the Benedictine Rule's own wider diffusion, promoted in part through the Dialogues' own popularity, a transmission question `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism` will need to address from its own side. None of these continuations is this document's own to reconstruct — noted for orientation only.

### `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md`

> All four of the works this world's own Doc_01 names are vendored, confirmed present in `cic/texts/` with confirmed rights entries in `cic/texts/REGISTRY.yaml`, and already assigned in `cic/corpus-map/roman-church-gregorian.yaml` at `role: tradition`, `confidence: assigned` — the corpus-map work was done by a prior source-research pass (2026-09-24) and is used here, not redone. Full classification (Type, Confidence, Boundary Status, Licensed-For) is in `Source_Registry.md`.

> - **The Istrian schism / Three Chapters material.** The Book I summary locus (`NPNF212`, ~line 23070) states Gregory's "ineffectual attempts, though apparently supported by the Emperor, to bring the Istrian bishops to submission in the matter of the 'Three Chapters' (see Ep. 16)." A second apparatus passage (`NPNF212`, lines 22458–22463) is explicit that these efforts failed: *"He in vain endeavoured, either by remonstrance or by trying to enlist the emperor's aid, to bring back the Istrian bishops to conformity."* That same passage names Pope Vigilius's own later assent to the Three Chapters' condemnation as the schism's actual origin — it names Vigilius specifically ("as did his successors... including Gregory"); it does not name Pelagius I or state a specific date, and this document does not attribute the c. 554–557 dating to this apparatus passage itself (that dating remains a reasonable general-history claim, per Doc_01 §2, but is not this specific source's own statement). The same passage does name, verbatim, a genuinely striking detail: even the Lombard queen **Theodelinda** — "so orthodox a Catholic," "whom he esteemed so highly," and corresponded with "cordially" — could not be persuaded to accept the Three Chapters' condemnation; the apparatus's own further comment that this "must have been distressing to him" is the editor's own inference, not a fact Gregory states, and is flagged as such rather than repeated as documented. **Directly relevant to Doc_01's own account of continuing imperial partnership (Doc_01 §1, §3):** in a separate letter, Book II Epistle XLVI ("To John, Bishop"), Gregory's own words at locus `iii.v.ii.xxiv-p8` state plainly: *"I have learnt the truth of all you had told me in your letters from the commands which have come to me from the most pious princes, bidding me abstain for the present from compelling them."* This is Gregory himself, not the apparatus, directly confirming that the emperor ordered him to stop pressing the Istrians — the single best piece of primary evidence in this world's own holdings for the "continuing imperial partnership, not imperial absence" account of this world's own distinctiveness, and the only Confidence A primary quotation this document has for the Istrian material specifically (the "ineffectual... in vain" passage above is apparatus text, Confidence B, not Gregory's own words).

> - **Surviving North African Donatism** — the correct locus is `NPNF212` ~line 23072 (the same Book I summary sentence as the Istrian material above). That locus names Gregory's own "invoking of the secular arm for suppression of what remained of the Donatist schism in Africa (see Ep. 74)" — a direct, specific pointer to a Register letter on this exact topic. This bears directly on the Turchi *Epistolae Selectae* double-placement question (Doc_01 §7 item 5): the Register itself, independent of the Turchi Latin selection, already contains at least one Donatism-specific letter (Ep. 74), which is Doc_02's own evidence, not yet individually re-traced to its own locus this session, that the two collections may cover genuinely different material rather than duplicate one another — named as a real lead for whoever next resolves that question, not resolved here.

> - **Lombard material generally** — the Register's own extensive correspondence concerning truces, tribute, and the Lombard king Agilulf is confirmed present in volume (the introductory apparatus, lines 22132–22206, surveys it at length), and is Native to this world at Confidence B; this document does not claim to have independently re-traced every specific Lombard-related letter to its own locus this session, and that granular tracing is named as an open item for whichever thread first draws vivid, quotable Lombard material for Doc_05.

> **The Moralia in Job** (`bliss1844`, `bliss1845`, `bliss1847`, `bliss1850`). Gregory's largest single work, roughly six times the length of the Register by direct word count (1,022,290 words across the four volumes against the Register's own ~160,000). The work's own dedicatory epistle to Leander of Seville (properly the *Epistola Missoria*, "prefixed to the completed treatise," per the Register's own endnote at `iii.v.i.xxx-p3`) opens the work (file `bliss1844`, from line ~384) and is locatable despite the file's own OCR irregularities. Verified word for word, lines ~384–548: *"When I knew you long since at Constantinople, my most blessed brother, at the time that I was kept there... I sought the haven of the monastery, and having left all that is of the world, as at that time I vainly believed, I came out naked from the shipwreck of human life. For as the vessel that is negligently moored, is very often (when the storm waxes violent) tossed by the water out of its shelter on the safest shore, so under the cloak of the Ecclesiastical office, I found myself plunged on a sudden in a sea of secular matters, and because I had not held fast the tranquillity of the monastery when in possession, I learnt by losing it, how closely it should have been held."*

> The file's actual artifacts at this locus are footnotes interleaved directly into the main text flow (a biographical note on Leander breaks the sentence "...as at that time I vainly [believed]" across two widely separated line ranges), stray misrecognized characters ("iVom ihe" for "from the"), and scattered extra spacing. This document quotes only the continuous sentence, silently closing the footnote interruption and normalizing the misrecognized characters, with no wording added, omitted, or substantively altered — consistent with `cic/texts/README.md`'s own whitespace-normalization convention, extended here, as disclosed, to also close an inline footnote interruption and correct clear OCR character errors, not merely whitespace.

> **The Dialogues** (`gregory-great_dialogues_gardner1911`). Confirmed directly against the file's own body text, not merely the vendoring note's front-matter description: "The End of the First Book" (file line ~3379) is followed immediately by the heading "The Second Book / Of the Life and Miracles of St. Bennet" (file lines ~3390–3396) and its opening line, verified word for word: *"There was a man of venerable life, blessed by grace, and blessed in name, for he was called Benedict or Bennet: who, from his younger years, carried always the mind of an old man; for his age was inferior to his virtue"* (the file's own OCR renders this "Benedicts" and "blesse#by"; normalized here per the same whitespace/character-error convention used throughout this document, no wording altered) — the only surviving connected *Life of St. Benedict of Nursia*. This is Native to this world (Gregory's own voice) and is the specific, named point of potential overlap with `early-benedictine-italian-monasticism`, per Doc_01 §6 and the vigilance obligation carried forward from Step 0.
