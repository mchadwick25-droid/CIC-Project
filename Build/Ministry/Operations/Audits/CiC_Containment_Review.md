# Independent Adversarial Review — Step 2 Containment Defect and Proposed Fix

Reviewer stance: no prior involvement, no stake in the outcome. Scope is the containment defect, the proposed fix, and whether either reveals a broader pattern worth catching before Step 2 work proceeds. I have used only the facts supplied in the brief; where I reason past them I say so.

## A. The fix itself

**Containment is essentially clean.** The replacement removes every internal identifier the original carried: the revision number (V7.4), the interim step name (Step 2B), the world (Alexandria), the Representative (Theon), the borrowed-from figure (Gregory of Nyssa), and the described defect. Nothing world-specific or incident-specific survives. A fresh thread reading the replacement learns a general mechanism and a general hazard; it learns nothing about any candidate it might later be asked to assess. On the core requirement — no residual leak — the fix passes.

**One residual, and it is stylistic, not a leak.** The phrases "not as a separate later pass" and "not as something scheduled after the fact" are the anonymized ghost of the old revision narrative. They leak nothing (no identifier, no candidate), but they are backward-looking — they still faintly narrate a history ("it used to be deferred"). By the fix's own stated rationale (revision-history narration belongs in the registers), these can be tightened into a purely forward instruction. There is a real procedural reason to keep the substance, though: a builder genuinely needs to be told not to defer the Registry, or a builder will defer it. So keep the instruction, drop the retrospection.

**Nothing procedurally important is lost.** The original's second paragraph did two jobs: it stated the limit of the Registry (it classifies proposed candidates; it cannot stop generation reaching for an unproposed source) and it located the real constraint downstream (the Step 10 grounding-anchor in the Permanent Prompt). Both survive intact. The incident in the original was *illustration* of the limit, not the *content* of the limit — the operative claim, "it cannot stop generation from reaching for a source nobody proposed in the first place," carries the entire load on its own and is arguably sharper without a dramatic anecdote competing for attention. A builder who never saw the original still understands why the Registry exists, what it does not guarantee, and where constraint actually happens.

**The quarry/wall metaphor still lands.** The metaphor was never anchored to the incident; it is anchored to the two sentences immediately preceding it — Registry as the catalogued raw material (quarry), Step 10 anchor as the built constraint (wall). Those sentences are retained, so the metaphor remains motivated by its own local context and reads as intended. It is not orphaned.

**Suggested tightening** (keeps every operative instruction, removes the retrospection, reintroduces no internal identifier):

> Step 2 produces the Source Ecology and the Source Registry together, as two outcomes of one step. Produce both in this step; do not defer the Registry to a later pass.
>
> The Registry classifies sources a builder has already proposed as candidates. It cannot, by itself, stop generation from reaching for a source nobody proposed in the first place. The mechanism that actually constrains generation is downstream, at Step 10: the grounding-anchor paragraph distilled from this Registry into the Representative's own Permanent Prompt. This step builds the quarry; Step 10 builds the wall.

This is a marginal improvement over an already-adequate fix, not a required change.

## B. The "Native doesn't mean exclusive" paragraph

**The judgment holds: the Calvin/Augustine example is different in kind, not merely in degree.** The containment risk that mattered in the defect is loss of *independence through exposure to the project's own prior conclusions* about a candidate a thread must assess. Calvin's use of Augustine is world-general public knowledge that any competent thread already possesses; reading it primes nothing that independent research would not already supply. It reveals no build decision, no defect, no Representative, no prior assessment, no tiering. The mechanism that made the Alexandria material dangerous — an internal, non-public conclusion attached to a named candidate — is simply absent here. Confirmed different.

**Two residual points, both low but worth recording.**

First, there is a thin scope-signal. The *choice* of "the standing example" is not random: a project reconstructing Christian formation worlds reaching for Calvin↔Augustine plausibly reflects what is live in the builders' minds. That is far weaker than the Alexandria case — it is a neutral historical fact, not an assessment — but a future thread could read it as an inadvertent hint about intended phase scope. Nonzero, and different in character (a scope-signal, not assessment-contamination).

Second, and more concrete: the example straddles the survey window in an asymmetric way. Calvin (16th c.) is safely outside the 70–461 CE window and can never be a Step 0 candidate, so he cannot contaminate a survey that will never contain him. **Augustine (d. 430), however, is inside the window** and is a plausible Ancient Church candidate in his own right. So the "different world entirely / public domain" defense is cleanest for Calvin and marginally less clean for Augustine — a thread later assessing an Augustine/Hippo candidate has been told, in a mandatory-reading document, that the project canonically frames Augustine through his downstream influence. Still low risk, because no internal *conclusion* about Augustine leaks — only a public fact — but the in-window status is the reason this paragraph is not perfectly analogous to the (also in-window) Nyssa material that was removed.

**Recommendation:** uphold leaving it, with awareness of the in-window point. The example could in principle be neutralized by choosing a pair whose *earlier* figure is also outside the window — but the pedagogical value here comes precisely from illustrating the *legitimate* version of cross-window borrowing (a later figure lawfully drawing on an ancient one), which is exactly the lawful counterpart of the defect the framework worries about. That fit is worth keeping. Treat any swap as optional hardening, not a fix.

## C. Systemic risk — pattern, not one-off

**This is a likely-recurring failure mode, and the mechanism is structural.** Two properties of the project combine to generate it. First, the project accumulates real operational history — defects, restructurings, lessons — over time. Second, it has a visible cultural instinct (evident in how carefully its Constitution and Step 0 methodology argue their own rules) toward transparent, non-arbitrary rationale rather than bare assertion. Wherever a rule was adopted *because* of an incident, that transparency instinct pulls toward writing "we do X because incident Y taught us Z" — and it writes it into the methodology document, because that is where the rule lives. But methodology documents are exactly the documents fresh independent threads are *required* to read. So at every future point where a lesson-generating incident meets a mandatory-reading methodology document, the same collision recurs. The Construction Framework is not special here; the Forces Framework, Lexicon Development Framework, and Source Registry Template are all mandatory-reading surfaces that will each accrue their own incident histories. Expect recurrence.

**A documentation rule is warranted, but the blanket version is miscalibrated.** The blanket rule — methodology documents may *never* embed a named world, Representative, or specific internal incident inline, and rationale must live *only* in the registers, referenced by entry/date, never restated — over-corrects. Its cost falls precisely on the value the transparency instinct protects: a rule stated with no inline reason ("do X, see Decision Log 2026-04-12") forces every reader to fetch the log to understand why, and can make rules feel arbitrary at the point of use — eroding the trust the transparency was there to build. Sometimes the rationale is a genuinely *general* principle that needs no incident at all; those should be stated inline, in full.

The sharp observation is this: **the containment risk lives entirely in the internal *identifiers*, not in the *rationale*.** An abstract lesson ("the Registry cannot constrain generation, because it only classifies proposed candidates") primes nothing an independent researcher would not derive anyway. A *named prior conclusion about a named candidate* is what destroys independence. The rule should therefore target identifiers, not reasoning.

**Calibrated rule I recommend:**

1. Methodology documents *may and should* state the general principle or lesson inline, in full — but abstractly, stripped of internal identifiers.
2. Methodology documents *may not* state inline the specific internal incident that occasioned the lesson: no named world, Representative, defect description tied to a build, revision number, or prior internal assessment/conclusion.
3. For provenance, a document *may* carry a bare pointer to the register ("see Change Orders Register, entry YYYY-MM-DD") without restating the incident's particulars.
4. Screening test, applied when any document is placed on a fresh thread's mandatory-reading list: *would a thread required to read this, and later assigned to assess candidate X independently, be primed with an internal conclusion by anything named here?* If yes, that identifier moves to the register.

This preserves self-justifying rationale (the *why* stays, deracinated of internal identifiers) while removing the priming vector (there is nothing internal left to prime on). It also cleanly explains Section B: Calvin/Augustine is a *public* identifier, not an *internal* one, which is exactly why that paragraph is fine and the Alexandria paragraph was not — the rule bites on internal-history identifiers specifically.

**Enforcement point matters.** The exposure happened because the mandatory-reading set was not itself screened. A rule with no gate will decay. Attach the screening test (item 4) to the act of adding any document to a fresh thread's required reading, and make that a checked step, not an aspiration.

## D. Does this cast doubt on the Step 0 seed survey content?

**Timing clears the survey of production-contamination — but that answers a narrower question than the project may be assuming.** Backward causation is not available: the survey research was completed before the exposure occurred, so the exposure cannot have altered what the survey produced. As an artifact, the survey's tiering and candidate assessments are not retroactively contaminated. For that specific claim, the timing argument is sufficient and correct.

It is *not* sufficient as blanket assurance, for two reasons the timing claim does not reach.

**First, it does not preserve future independent auditability of the Alexandria row.** Independence is a property of an information state, not of a calendar. Any *future* thread that must independently re-examine, validate, or extend the Alexandria candidate — including the Step 2 source-ecology work this whole review is upstream of — is now compromised the moment it reads the exposing material. So while the survey's *content* stands, the project's ability to independently *audit* the Alexandria row going forward is degraded. That is a real retroactive cost; it just falls on validation, not on the original production.

**Second — and this is the catch the timing framing obscures — there is an unresolved substantive question about the Alexandria candidate that predates and is independent of the exposure.** The Step 0 survey lists "Alexandria" (the Catechetical / Christian-Platonist tradition of Clement and Origen) as a candidate. The project *also* has a prior *built* world named "Alexandria," with a Representative "Theon," that carried a documented defect. From what I have been given I **cannot determine whether these are the same tradition** — "Alexandria" names several distinct Christian currents, and the brief specifies the tradition for the candidate but not for the prior build. I will not invent that fact. But either resolution surfaces something the survey may not reflect:

- If the built "Alexandria/Theon" is the *same* tradition as the candidate, then the candidate is not a fresh, never-built prospect — it has undisclosed prior-build history and a known defect. A survey that assessed it as if novel would be missing material that bears on its tiering, and its own prior review may have shared that blind spot.
- If it is a *different* Alexandrian current, then the project is carrying two distinct things both named "Alexandria" — a naming collision that is itself the proximate cause of the contamination that just occurred, and a standing hazard for exactly this kind of cross-priming.

Either way there is something to catch, and it is a *completeness* question about the survey, not a contamination question — which is why "the research predated the exposure" does not dispose of it. I also note that whether the survey's original reviewers were themselves aware of the Theon incident is not stated; if they were not, then "already reviewed" is weaker assurance for the Alexandria row specifically than for the other rows, because that review could not have raised the prior-build question.

**Recommendation for D:** the survey's general content need not be reopened. But the project should determine — *from the append-only registers, not from any fresh independent thread* — whether the built "Alexandria/Theon" world is the same tradition as the Step 0 Alexandria candidate, and treat that as a prerequisite to any Step 2 work on Alexandria (see E).

## E. Bottom line — getting independent Step 2 work done on Alexandria

**The recursion is the whole difficulty and it rules out the naïve fix.** "Just recruit another fresh thread" is precisely what failed — the second thread was exposed through the very document it was required to read for onboarding. Spinning up a third fresh thread, today, reproduces the same defect a third time. So something has to change *first*; there is no independent-Alexandria path within the documents as they currently stand.

A workable path does exist, in sequence:

1. **Land the fix first.** No fresh Alexandria thread can be commissioned while the exposing text is still in mandatory reading. Necessary, not sufficient.

2. **Scrub the *entire* mandatory-reading set, not just the Construction Framework.** Independence requires that *nothing* a Step 2 source thread is required to read names the Alexandria/Theon/Nyssa material. The Construction Framework was the surface that caught this thread; it may not be the only one. Apply the Section C calibrated rule and screening test across the whole required-reading set.

3. **Resolve the same-tradition-vs-name-collision question from Section D**, using the registers only. This determines what "independence" should even mean here, and forks the path:
   - *If it is a name collision (different tradition):* blind independence is the right goal and is achievable post-scrub — a fresh thread can learn the general hazard without any Alexandria-specific prior. Also **rename one of the two "Alexandria"s** to remove the collision that caused this incident.
   - *If it is the same tradition:* blind independence is the *wrong* goal. You do not want a Step 2 thread ignorant that this exact tradition has already been built and already failed in a specific way — ignoring that history invites repeating the defect. Redefine independence for this candidate as *independent reviewer with full disclosure*: assessed by someone other than the original builder, with the prior build history furnished explicitly as evidence rather than concealed.
   - *If it remains unknown:* do not commission any Alexandria Step 2 thread until it is resolved.

4. **Retire the already-exposed thread from Alexandria work.** It stopped and reported correctly; that judgment was right. But it is now burned for independent Alexandria source-evidence. Reassign it to non-Alexandria candidates or to methodology work, and do not reuse it for Alexandria.

**So: yes, there is a workable path, but not the obvious one.** It is not "find a cleaner thread"; it is "make the documents safe to onboard from, and decide what independence means for a candidate that may already have build history — before any thread touches Alexandria again." The deeper reason the fix (move the incident to the registers, keep only the deracinated lesson) is the right move is that it is exactly what makes an onboarded thread and an *independent* thread compatible: the thread can learn the general hazard that generation may reach for unproposed sources without learning that a world named Alexandria did so via a specific figure. Post-fix and post-scrub, that compatibility is restored — conditional on resolving the Section D question, which no amount of thread-hygiene can substitute for.

---

**Summary of recommendations.** The fix is sound; adopt it (optionally with the tightened wording in A). Leave the Calvin/Augustine paragraph, noting Augustine's in-window status as a low, different-in-kind residual. Treat the defect as a structural pattern and adopt the *calibrated* documentation rule in C — bar internal *identifiers* inline, not rationale — with a screening gate at the point documents are made mandatory reading. The survey's content need not be reopened, but resolve whether the Alexandria candidate and the prior built "Alexandria" are the same tradition before any Step 2 Alexandria work; that question, not the exposure timing, is the real open item. And do not commission independent Alexandria source work until the fix lands, the full mandatory-reading set is scrubbed, and that same-tradition question is answered.
