# Library search log: era 10 (1906 to about 2010), batch 2, 2026-11-15

The search record for the Library thread's second pass on the era 10 wants. Batch 1 is in
`Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-15_library-era10/` (its Search_Log, with 34 cautions, and its Handover).
This batch took what that handover proposed: a second pass by region for the thin rows (Africa, Oceania, the Orthodox
and Oriental churches in their own languages), more scans of the printed Catholic Worker, and a hand-fetch list for
Mark (`Hand_Fetch_List.md`, beside this file). It also settled batch 1's shelf rows that stood at `needs-ruling`.
The vendored files and the rows not filled are in `Build/Ministry/Features/Source-Library/CFT-Wants.md` ("Era 10 ...
batch 2").

## The rights rule, as applied

As batch 1 applied it (R1 to R12, `Build/Ministry/Features/Source-Library/Decision-Log.md`; `cic/texts/INTAKE.md`
section 2). Every file of this batch is public domain by its printed date. No file rests on R7 (non-renewal) or on an
owner's declaration.

## Searches, by region

Five Sonnet searchers, one scratch directory each, worked from one written brief (the rights test, the allowed
commands, the spec to hand back). A sixth read the uncertain title pages on the page images. This thread chose the
picks, checked each byte count against the host's file list by script, and stripped host marks by script. Four Sonnet
mappers then read the assembled files for title-page words, parts and passages by line number.

**Africa.** Nothing first-hand was found on archive.org to 1930 for William Wadé Harris, Orimolade, Kimbangu,
Babalola, Lekganyane, Spartas or Kivebulaya. Outside witnesses printed by 1930 were found and taken: the
International Review of Missions for 1922 (Lerrigo on the "prophet movement" in Congo), 1923 and 1928 (Thompson on
the Harris movement); Marty's study of Islam in the Ivory Coast (1922), with a section on "le harrisme"; Gollock's
Sons of Africa (1928); Smith's report of the Le Zoute conference (1926); Tucker's Eighteen Years in Uganda (1908).
African voices and church prints taken: Jabavu's The Black Problem; the American Zulu Mission's hymnal (1911); a Zulu
Catholic catechism (Mariannhill, 1912). The volumes of the review for 1914-1921, 1924-1927, 1929 and 1930, the
Proceedings of the Church Missionary Society for 1910-1921, The Moslem World and the Allgemeine Missions-Zeitschrift
were searched for the names and gave nothing more than roll entries.
Rejected: Samuel Johnson's History of the Yorubas (all three scans print "Reprinted 1937 ... 1960" or later);
Casely Hayford's Ethiopia Unbound (`ethiopiaunbounds00hayf`: no year on the title page, no dated preface, no
copyright line); Plaatje's Native Life in South Africa (two scans, no year); the hymn book of the National Church of
Nigeria and the Cameroons (`hymnsprayersforu00abaniala`: an African church's own hymnal with no year anywhere; the
catalogue says "[19--?]"); Cooper's Indigenous Principles in Nigeria (no year; the text names December 1922 against a
catalogue date of 1920). Marginal and not taken: Tucker of Uganda (1929), the Uganda pageant book of 1927.
Not found: Casely Hayford's William Waddy Harris (1915); Lloyd's Apolo of the Pygmy Forest (1923) and More about
Apolo; Walker's Story of the Ivory Coast (1926); Uganda Notes; the Church Missionary Review; the Journal des missions
évangéliques; Belgian colonial reviews on Kimbangu; The Negro Churchman; the report on the Nyasaland rising (1916);
Mofolo in Sesotho; Bokwe's Ntsikana; an Amharic church hymnal. Seen and not taken: a Luganda prayer book of 1905
(`EkitaboEkyokusabaKwabantuBona`, a year before the window) and a Sesotho communion service of 1926
(`mokhoa-oa-ho-etsa-komunione-e-halalelang`), unchecked.

**Oceania.** Nothing in Rātana's own voice, and no pamphlet, church yearbook or official report on him to 1930, was
found on archive.org in about twelve differently worded searches. One outside witness was taken: Te Karere, the
Latter-day Saint mission's Māori paper, for April to June 1930, which prints a letter about Rātana's petition on the
Treaty. Its volumes for 1926 and 1928 have no such matter; those for 1918-1925, 1927 and 1929 are not on the host.
Not taken: two Māori tracts by Wiremu Takana and Te Paranihi Karakama (`hekupuwhakamaram00gard`,
`hekupuwhaiwhakaa00kara`: no year is printed on any leaf; the catalogue says 1916); Latter-day Saint hymnals and a
handbook in Māori, Tahitian and Hawaiian (no census entry is theirs after 1906, and they say nothing of the wanted
people); the four items named under the calls. Not found for 1896-1930: Te Pipiwharauroa, Te Kopara, Te Toa Takitini;
Ringatū prints; a Māori prayer book or catechism; Samoan prints; a Fijian Methodist hymnal; a Free Church of Tonga
hymnal; Melanesian Brotherhood prints; F. E. Williams's The Vailala Madness (only a 1977 reprint, lending). The
London Missionary Society's Chronicle for 1926-27 was searched and gave nothing.

**The Catholic Worker.** No further scan of an issue of the New York paper is on archive.org. The held issue of June
1969 (`sparrowsnest-10315`) is the only one. `sparrowsnest-3729` is a pamphlet of 1988 about the movement by living
authors. `CatholicWorkerNewOrderMay1947`, `CatholicWorkerOwnersOrSlavesJuly1947` and
`catholic-worker-design-for-democrats` are pamphlets of the Melbourne paper of the same name, a different paper. The
items `catholic_worker_2022...` to `2025` are modern issues. The record `pub_catholic-worker` has no files.

**The Orthodox churches.** The council of 1917-18: every scan of the Деяния on archive.org is a reprint. One
(`svyashchennyy-sobor-pravoslavnoy-rossyskoy-tserkvi.-deyaniya.-tom-2.-vyp.-1`) reproduces a title page reading
"Петроградъ 1918" and ends with a colophon of 1994; another carries front matter of 2018. None was taken. Taken: the
Paris journal Путь, numbers 1 to 25 (September 1925 to December 1930), with the Paris school's own articles; a New
York Russian volume for Patriarch Tikhon's jubilee (1922); Vvedensky's Церковь и государство (Moscow, 1923), the
renovationist side; Valentinov's Чёрная книга (Paris, 1925); twelve issues of The Christian East (1920-1930), an
Anglican quarterly that prints Orthodox documents in English; Zankov's book on the Orthodox Church in German (1928)
and English (1929); five books of Nikolaj Velimirović in English (1915-1917); the Ecumenical Patriarchate's Μαύρη
Βίβλος (1920); and Bell's Documents on Christian Unity (1924), which prints the encyclical of January 1920 in English.
Not taken: Velimirović's Two Churches in One Nation (no year, no dated preface); other scans of Bell's volumes
(lending-only, printings of 1948-1955); Путь from number 26 (1931 on); the Orthodox Catholic Review of 1927, Hapgood's
Service Book (1906), Douglas (1921) and Gavin (1923), found, dated and left as marginal to the wants. Thirty-two more
issues of The Christian East to 1930 are on the host and were not taken. Located and not examined: Lototsky's
Церковне право (Prague 1923, `cerkovne-pravo-lotockyj-1923`); Евразийский временник 1923 (`evraziskivremenn53unse`).
Not found: the Собрание определений of 1918; Tikhon's epistles as a separate print; the declaration of 1927 in a
Russian print; Свет невечерний; Церковные ведомости and Богословский вестник for 1917-18; Kyrion II, Khelaia,
Tamarati, Durnovo, the Genoa memorandum of 1922; Ἐκκλησιαστικὴ Ἀλήθεια; the acts of the Pan-Orthodox congress of
1923; Serbian, Bulgarian and Romanian church journals; Cristea; Trifa in an original print; Sheptytsky, Slipyj,
Lypkivsky; the Arabic journals of Antioch.

**The Oriental churches and the Church of the East.** Batch 1's unchecked leads were checked. `1918-12` is a bound
set of the journal Էջմիածին for 1918 and early 1919; its masthead prints no Gregorian date and its OCR is unusable:
not taken. The Malankara Edavaka Pathrika (three issues, volume 20) and the Suriyani Sabha issue print only
Malayalam-era numerals on their covers, not read with certainty: not taken. Habib Girgis's أسرار الكنيسة السبعة
(`AAlexandrina-102649`) carries a preface dated April 1970 that names Kyrillos VI: a printing of 1970, rejected.
Taken: Naayem's Shall This Nation Die? (1921), a Chaldean priest's account; Austin's The Baqubah Refugee Camp (1920)
and Wigram's Our Smallest Ally (1920), outside witnesses; Kyriakos Mikhail's Copts and Moslems under British Control
(1911); Manassa Yuhanna's history of the Coptic Church in Arabic (1924); the collected works of Catholicos Khrimian
in Armenian (New York, 1929); an English Divine Liturgy of the Armenian Church (1908); Panjikaran's The Syrian Church
in Malabar (1912), a Catholic account; and two Malayalam church monthlies, the Malankara Sabha Tharaka for 1910 and

Not taken: Yohannan's The Death of a Nation (1917, `deathofnation0000yoha`: the text lacks about pages 29-115);
Richards's Indian Christians of St. Thomas (1908, `indianchristians0000rich`: an outside account whose record carries

log, kept in the wants table). Not found: Balakian's Հայ Գողգոթան (1922); Surma's book of 1920; al-Karma; Barsoum's
memorandum; al-Hikma; Armalet's print of 1919; Zaven, Teotig, Guleserian; Ormanian's Azgapatum; the Coptic Congress

## Cautions for profile writers

1. **The African prophets.** No held file prints Harris's, Kimbangu's or Kivebulaya's own writing. What is held is
   what missionaries and one colonial writer printed about them between 1908 and 1928. Say so in any profile.
2. **Spellings.** The prints spell Kibangu (never Kimbangu), "William Vade Harris" (Marty), "William Wade Harris"
   and "William Wadé Harris", Kivubulaya (Gollock) and Kivebulaya (Tucker), Braid (for Braide).
3. **Kibangu's call (the review of 1922).** Lerrigo gives it in the first person, but he had it from Budimbu, who
   said he had it from the prophet. Two hands stand between the words and the man.
4. **Harris's last charge (the review of 1928).** Thompson gives the words as the missionary Benoit brought them from
   Liberia. The print does not say how Benoit took them down.
5. **The review of 1923 is not a second witness.** Allegret's long quotation on Harris is Marty's wording; a footnote
   says so.
6. **Marty.** He sums up Harris's preaching and quotes none of it. He writes as an observer of Islam.
7. **Gollock, Sons of Africa.** Written for study groups, with no bibliography. The Apolo section names no source.
   Several of its subjects were living when it was printed.
8. **Tucker.** Volume II only. Apolo Kivebulaya is seen through his bishop; none of his own words are given.
9. **Smith on Le Zoute.** Only the recommendations and resolutions are the conference's; the rest is Smith's reading,
   by his own preface.
10. **Jabavu.** A second edition; its section on the Native Affairs Act was shortened. Merriman's foreword is not
    his. "Native" and "black problem" are the print's words.
11. **The Zulu hymnal.** Many hymns are adaptations, by the preface. It names no Zulu writer; the index of first
    lines names some writers. The date rests on a copyright notice.
12. **Wanger's catechism.** A leaf of 1915 allows its sale "as corrected" and reserves its use to missionaries and
    priests. It is not evidence of what lay Zulu Catholics were given to read.
13. **Te Karere.** The letter that speaks of Rātana is broken in the OCR and its signer cannot be read. It is another
    church's paper. Do not quote it without the page image. The numbers in the file are out of order.
14. **Naayem.** The print does not say who made the English or from what language. Part II is other people's
    depositions. Surma is not named anywhere in the book.
15. **Austin and Wigram.** Outside witnesses of 1920. Austin spells the Patriarch "Mar Polos Shimun". Wigram's third
    chapter is headed "CHAPTER II" in the OCR.
16. **Kyriakos Mikhail.** An advocacy book written for English readers. The Patriarch's encyclical is signed with his
    office only; the book does not name him there. Chapters I and II are by other hands.
17. **The Arabic history of the Coptic Church (1924).** Poor OCR; the author's name is the host record's, not read
    in the print. Use it to find a place, then read the page image.
18. **Khrimian.** A collection made in 1929 that leaves out his encyclicals and his writing in periodicals. Poor OCR
    in the body. His years and his office are the host record's.
19. **The Armenian liturgy in English.** The two translators are not named and do not say what text they used.
20. **The Malayalam monthlies.** Poor OCR in both. Which church each belongs to is the host record's word, not read
    in the print. Neither names the events of 1912.
21. **Panjikaran.** It argues one side and stops at 1599.
22. **Путь.** The cover prints "VOIE, Revue religieuse russe"; the Russian name stands in the journal's own notes.
    Each header lists the articles with line numbers; some first lines are approximate. Number 5 lacks its last
    fifteen pages. In number 9 the OCR runs two columns together at about lines 5236-5330.
23. **Путь, the dates.** The OCR garbles the masthead year of number 2 ("192©") and misreads that of number 17 as
    "1939". Both dates rest on the numbers on either side. The pack has no page images to check them on.
24. **Lossky.** Every "Лосскій" in Путь to 1930 is Н. Лосскій, the philosopher N. O. Lossky. Nothing of Vladimir
    Lossky is held.
25. **Sergius's message of 1927.** Held only in English (The Christian East, winter 1927), headed a "Letter", dated
    16/29 July 1927; the print names no translator. Путь number 9 dates the message 29 July 1927 and number 10
    "19-го августа 1927". Batch 1's caution 3 (Emhardt) still holds. Still no full text of a rejection.
26. **Tikhon.** The New York volume of 1922 tells his words at the trial at second hand. His epistle of 19 January
    1918 is held in Russian only inside a book by his opponent Vvedensky, in the new spelling; check it against
    Spinka's and Emhardt's English. The pastoral of 1923 in The Christian East was translated from a Belgrade
    newspaper.
27. **Vvedensky.** A renovationist leader writing against the Patriarch. The first leaves are garbled; the clean text
    begins at about line 178.
28. **Valentinov.** A compilation from the press, made in Paris. Only Appendix 1 is a full document of the Patriarch.
29. **The Christian East.** An Anglican journal. Its Orthodox documents are translations, some with no translator
    named. Ambrose's memorandum breaks off where the scan lacks a page. In the number for July 1922 the print dates a
    law "March 27, 1919", which looks like a slip. The number for September 1920 does not print the encyclical of
    January 1920.
30. **Bell.** The encyclical of January 1920 is in English with no translator named. Some headings are garbled in
    the OCR, so check a document's number against the contents.
31. **Zankov.** The English edition folds most footnotes into the text and drops the rest; quote the German for
    anything that turns on a note. The OCR prints the English edition's year as 1939.
32. **Velimirović.** The books of 1915-1917 style him "Father" and professor; give no later office from them. One
    is a translation by Fanny S. Copeland. Christianity and War names no author at all: the attribution is the
    library's. The forewords are by others.
33. **The Patriarchate's Black Book (1920).** Poor OCR in Greek. The title is the host record's.
34. **Loisy 1904.** The third edition, with no preface of its own. Do not cite it as the text of 1902.
