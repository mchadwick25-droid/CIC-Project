# Source sheet: Polycarp of Smyrna (writer's copy)

## 1. Setting

S1. Polycarp was bishop of the church in Smyrna. Martyrdom ch. 16 (ANF 4167–4169) calls him "an apostolic and prophetic teacher, and bishop of the Catholic Church which is in Smyrna". Ignatius addresses him as "Polycarp, Bishop of the Church of the Smyrnæans" (ANF 8534–8535). Documented.
S2. Smyrna is a city "in Asia" (Ignatius to the Smyrnaeans, ANF 7938), meaning the Roman province of Asia. The crowd calls Polycarp "the teacher of Asia" (Martyrdom ch. 12, ANF 4055). Documented.
S3. Tradition: the Library's census name for his tradition is **Post-Apostolic Household-Church Christianity**. The writer may use this name.
S4. His own letter is from "Polycarp, and the presbyters with him, to the Church of God sojourning at Philippi" (ANF 3232–3234). He led together with presbyters, and his letter also names deacons (ANF 3365, 3392–3393). Documented.
S5. Irenaeus says Polycarp was "instructed by apostles, and conversed with many who had seen Christ", and was appointed bishop of Smyrna "by apostles in Asia" (Against Heresies III.3.4, ANF 39857–39860; the same passage is quoted in Eusebius IV.14.3, EUS 22924–22926). HEARER. Widely Accepted. (For who appointed him, see D1.)
S6. Polycarp's letter says the church at Smyrna came to faith after Philippi did: "we [of Smyrna] had not yet known Him" when Paul praised the Philippians (ANF 3555–3557). This chapter survives only in Latin (ANF note 391 at 3501). OWN. Documented, with the caution in D9.
S7. The situation at his death was persecution. A proconsul held trials in Smyrna. Christians were scourged, and some were thrown to wild beasts in the stadium (Martyrdom ch. 2–4, ANF 3781–3849). The crowd shouted "Away with the Atheists" against the Christians (ch. 3, ANF 3834–3835). CHURCH. Documented.
S8. Persons in the account: the proconsul Statius Quadratus; Herod, the Irenarch (a local police officer); his father Nicetes; Philip the Asiarch, also called high priest, from Tralles; a centurion. Sources: ch. 6, 8, 12, 18, 21 (ANF 3883–3886, 3940–3941, 4059, 4217, 4296–4298). CHURCH. Documented.
S9. The crowd at the end is "the whole multitude both of the heathen and Jews, who dwelt at Smyrna" (ch. 12, ANF 4053–4054). CHURCH. Documented as the church's account. The church's letter is hostile to the Jews of Smyrna (see M24, M27).
S10. The date the Martyrdom itself gives: "the second day of the month Xanthicus just begun, the seventh day before the Kalends of May, on the great Sabbath, at the eighth hour" (ch. 21, ANF 4284–4288). The Greek names March, not May (GEB 996–998). No year is given. See D2.
S11. Eusebius dates the martyrdom to the reign of Marcus Aurelius (Church History IV.14.10–IV.15.1, EUS 23032–23074): "At this time, when the greatest persecutions were exciting Asia, Polycarp ended his life by martyrdom." LATER (about 150 years). Contested as to the exact year (D2).
S12. **Century.** The ancient witnesses give no year count. They place him by rulers and bishops. (a) His life overlapped with Ignatius of Antioch, who wrote to him while Ignatius was a prisoner on the way to Rome (ANF 8534–8566; CONTEMP). (b) Irenaeus says Polycarp knew people who had seen Christ, and he names John, "the disciple of the Lord" (ANF 39857–39859, 54579–54581; EUS 30814–30815). Irenaeus also says John lived at Ephesus "until the times of Trajan" (ANF 39896–39898). HEARER. (c) Polycarp visited Rome when Anicetus was bishop (ANF 39871–39873; EUS 31591–31601). Irenaeus wrote Against Heresies when Eleutherius was bishop, two bishops after Anicetus (ANF 39849–39851), and wrote to Victor in the tenth year of Commodus (EUS 31002–31003, 31436–31442). Eusebius sets the Rome visit under Anicetus, and then says that Marcus Aurelius succeeded Antoninus Pius after Antoninus "had completed the twenty-second year of his reign" (EUS 22846–22850, 23032–23036). Jerome sets the visit under Antoninus Pius (JER 39844–39845). (d) Eusebius dates the death to the reign of Marcus Aurelius and Lucius (EUS 23032–23074). Jerome dates it to "the reign of Marcus Antoninus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus" (JER 39850–39851). LATER. The witnesses themselves state that he visited Rome under Anicetus (Irenaeus; Jerome adds Antoninus Pius) and died under Marcus Aurelius (Eusebius, Jerome): Documented as their placing. That his life reached back to Trajan's time is only an inference from Irenaeus (John lived until Trajan, and Polycarp spoke of knowing John): Inferential-Thin. That this was the **second century AD** comes from the modern standard chronology of those reigns, not from any of the held sources: Widely Accepted. The sources do not dispute the century. D2 is about the year and the month, not the century: Eusebius and Jerome agree on the reign. Plain ways to say it that the sheet supports: "in the second century"; "he had known people who had seen Jesus" (Irenaeus). The sheet does not support "about a hundred years after Jesus": no held source counts the years from Jesus to Polycarp.


## 2. Recorded moments

M1. **Ignatius on his way to Rome.** Ignatius of Antioch, a prisoner on his way to Rome, wrote from Smyrna and sent greetings from Polycarp. Ephesians 21: "loving Polycarp even as I do you" (ANF 5582–5583). Magnesians 15: "along with Polycarp, the bishop of the Smyrnæans" (ANF 6222–6223). CONTEMP. Documented.
M2. **Ignatius had met him face to face.** "I have been thought worthy [to behold] thy blameless face" (Ignatius to Polycarp 1, ANF 8551–8552). Eusebius says Ignatius "came to Smyrna, where Polycarp was" (III.36.5, EUS 19961–19962). CONTEMP; Eusebius LATER (about 200 years). Documented.
M3. **Ignatius wrote to Polycarp from Troas.** He told him to press on, keep unity, bear with all, pray without ceasing, and "Stand firm, as does an anvil which is beaten" (Ignatius to Polycarp 1–3, ANF 8549–8566, 8646–8656). CONTEMP. Documented.
M4. **Ignatius asked Polycarp to call a council and send a messenger to Syria,** because the church at Antioch was at peace. He also asked him to write to the nearby churches, because Ignatius "must suddenly sail from Troas to Neapolis" (Ignatius to Polycarp 7–8, ANF 8806–8819, 8854–8862). Eusebius adds that Ignatius "commended to him ... the flock at Antioch" (III.36.10, EUS 20031–20035). CONTEMP. Documented.
M5. **Polycarp wrote to the Philippians.** They had invited him to write about righteousness (ch. 3, ANF 3305–3307). He praised them for receiving "those who were bound in chains" (ch. 1, ANF 3243–3245). OWN. Documented.
M6. **He sent the Philippians Ignatius's letters.** He sent "The Epistles of Ignatius written by him to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us", subjoined to his own letter. He promised to carry their letter to Syria, or send someone, and asked for news of Ignatius and those with him (Philippians 13, ANF 3608–3630; Eusebius III.36.14–15, EUS 20090–20101). OWN. Documented. For the reading "were"/"are", see D8.
M7. **He grieved over Valens,** a former presbyter at Philippi, and his wife, apparently over love of money. He told the church to "call them back as suffering and straying members" (Philippians 11, ANF 3531–3563, Latin only). OWN. Documented.
M8. **He warned against false teaching.** Whoever denies "that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh" or "the testimony of the cross", or says there is no resurrection or judgment, "is the first-born of Satan" (Philippians 7, ANF 3433–3439). OWN. Documented.
M9. **Visit to Rome under Anicetus.** Polycarp visited Rome when Anicetus was bishop there. They could not persuade each other about the Easter observance. Polycarp kept it as he had "with John the disciple of our Lord, and the other apostles". Anicetus followed the presbyters before him. They stayed in communion, and Anicetus "conceded the administration of the eucharist in the church to Polycarp ... as a mark of respect". They parted in peace (Irenaeus to Victor, in Eusebius V.24.16–17, EUS 31591–31612, 31655–31657; also ANF Irenaeus fragment III, 54651–54666). HEARER, writing about 40 years later (Victor became bishop in Commodus's 10th year, EUS 31002–31003). Documented.
M10. **In Rome he turned many away from Valentinus and Marcion.** He came "to Rome in the time of Anicetus [and] caused many to turn away from the aforesaid heretics to the Church of God", proclaiming the one truth received from the apostles (Irenaeus III.3.4, ANF 39871–39875; EUS 22948–22953). HEARER. Widely Accepted.
M11. **Meeting with Marcion.** Marcion met him and asked if Polycarp knew him. Polycarp answered, "I do know thee, the first-born of Satan" (Irenaeus III.3.4, ANF 39882–39884; EUS 22962–22982). Irenaeus does not say where they met. Jerome (late 4th c.) puts it in Rome (JER 39846–39849). HEARER. Widely Accepted. For the wording, see D5.
M12. **The John and Cerinthus story** came from people "who heard from him": John, the disciple of the Lord, ran out of a bath-house in Ephesus because Cerinthus was inside (Irenaeus III.3.4, ANF 39877–39882; EUS 22955–22959). This is a story Polycarp told, passed on secondhand. HEARER at second hand. Inferential-Thin as fact about John; Documented as something Irenaeus reports.
M13. **Teaching in Asia, as Irenaeus remembered it.** Irenaeus, "while I was yet a boy", saw Florinus with Polycarp in Lower Asia. He could still describe "the place where the blessed Polycarp used to sit and discourse", how he came and went, how he lived and looked, and his talks to the people. He remembered how Polycarp "would speak of his familiar intercourse with John, and with the rest of those who had seen the Lord", and told what he had heard from them about the Lord's miracles and teaching, "in harmony with the Scriptures" (Irenaeus to Florinus, EUS 30792–30819; ANF fragment II, 54568–54584). HEARER, writing many years later. Documented as Irenaeus's memory.
M14. **His habit when he heard false teaching:** he would cry out, stop his ears and say, "O good God, for what times hast Thou reserved me, that I should endure these things?" (ANF 54590–54592; EUS 30825–30829). Irenaeus says he would have done this "as he was wont to do". HEARER. Documented as Irenaeus's memory.
M15. **He wrote other letters,** "to the neighbouring Churches to confirm them, or to certain of the brethren" (ANF 54593–54596; EUS 30836–30842). HEARER. Documented. None survives in the Library except the letter to the Philippians.
M16. **Death demanded.** After Germanicus died bravely in the arena, the crowd shouted, "Away with the Atheists; let Polycarp be sought out!" (Martyrdom ch. 3, ANF 3818–3835). CHURCH. Documented.
M17. **Quintus.** A Phrygian named Quintus had pushed himself and others to come forward. He lost courage at the sight of the beasts, swore and sacrificed. The church says it does not praise those who give themselves up (ch. 4, ANF 3843–3849). CHURCH. Documented.
M18. **He left the city when friends asked.** At first he wanted to stay, but "in deference to the wish of many" he went to a farm near the city. There he prayed night and day "for all men, and for the Churches throughout the world, according to his usual custom" (ch. 5, ANF 3859–3865). CHURCH. Documented.
M19. **WONDER, told by the Smyrna church: the burning pillow.** Three days before his arrest, while praying, he saw in a vision his pillow on fire. He said, "I must be burnt alive" (ch. 5, ANF 3865–3869; recalled in ch. 12, ANF 4065–4068). Eusebius's retelling says the vision came "at night", that he spoke "upon this awakening", and that it meant he would die by fire "for Christ's sake" (EUS 23216–23222). CHURCH. Documented as the church's report.
M20. **Betrayal.** He moved to another farm. The searchers seized two young slaves, and one confessed under torture. "Those that betrayed him were of his own household" (ch. 6, ANF 3878–3886). CHURCH. Documented.
M21. **Arrest.** Police and horsemen came on Friday evening ("the day of the preparation") "as if going out against a robber". He could have fled from the upper room, but said, "The will of God be done." He came down and talked with them. He had food and drink set before them and asked for an hour to pray. He prayed for two hours, and many of them repented that they had come for "so godly and venerable an old man" (ch. 7, ANF 3899–3926). CHURCH. Documented.
M22. **On the road.** He remembered in prayer "all that had at any time come in contact with him, both small and great", and the whole church throughout the world. They brought him to the city on a donkey, "the day being that of the great Sabbath". Herod and Nicetes took him into their carriage and urged him to say "Lord Caesar" and sacrifice. He refused, and they threw him out of the carriage. He hurt his leg (ANF: "dislocated"; Greek "scraped the shin", see Q5 note) and walked on to the stadium (ch. 8, ANF 3935–3963). CHURCH. Documented.
M23. **WONDER, told by the Smyrna church: the voice from heaven.** As he entered the stadium a voice said, "Be strong, and show thyself a man, O Polycarp!" No one saw the speaker. "those of our brethren who were present heard the voice" (ch. 9, ANF 3972–3976). Eusebius's retelling adds that "not many heard" it because of the noise (EUS 23295–23300). CHURCH. Documented as the church's report.
M24. **Trial before the proconsul.** He was told to respect his age, swear by Caesar's Fortune, and say "Away with the Atheists". He pointed at the crowd and said it. Told to revile Christ, he answered with the eighty-six years (Q8). He said "I am a Christian" and offered to explain Christianity. He refused to defend himself before the crowd, though he would give account to the authorities. Threatened with beasts and then with fire, he would not give way (ch. 9–11, ANF 3977–4038). CHURCH. Documented.
M25. **His face and the herald.** "he was filled with confidence and joy, and his countenance was full of grace". The proconsul, astonished, had the herald cry three times, "Polycarp has confessed that he is a Christian" (ch. 12, ANF 4047–4052). CHURCH. Documented.
M26. **The crowd's charge.** "This is the teacher of Asia, the father of the Christians, and the overthrower of our gods" (ch. 12, ANF 4053–4058). They asked Philip the Asiarch for a lion. He refused because the beast shows were over, so they shouted for burning (ch. 12, ANF 4058–4065). CHURCH. Documented.
M27. **The pyre.** The crowd gathered wood from workshops and baths, "the Jews especially, according to custom, eagerly assisting them in it" (ch. 13, ANF 4079–4082). This is the church's own hostile wording. He undressed and tried to take off his sandals, which he had not done before, because the faithful always hurried to touch his skin. He asked not to be nailed, and they bound him instead (ch. 13–14, ANF 4082–4105). CHURCH. Documented.
M28. **His last prayer,** bound "like a distinguished ram [taken] out of a great flock for sacrifice" (ch. 14, ANF 4104–4124; Eusebius IV.15.33–35, EUS 23447–23469). CHURCH. Documented. For the wording, see Q14.
M29. **WONDER, told by the Smyrna church as eyewitnesses: the fire like a sail.** The fire formed "the form of an arch, like the sail of a ship when filled with the wind" around his body. He looked "not like flesh which is burnt, but as bread that is baked, or as gold and silver glowing in a furnace" (ch. 15, ANF 4136–4146). The writers say they were "preserved that we might report to others" (ANF 4141–4142). Eusebius's retelling has the sail and the gold and silver but not the bread (EUS 23477–23489). CHURCH. Documented as the church's report.
M30. **WONDER, same tellers: the fragrance.** "we perceived such a sweet odour [coming from the pile], as if frankincense or some such precious spices had been smoking there" (ch. 15, ANF 4147–4149; EUS 23489–23491). CHURCH. Documented as the church's report.
M31. **Death by the dagger; WONDER, Smyrna church: the dove and the blood.** The fire did not consume him, so an executioner stabbed him with a dagger. "there came forth a dove, and a great quantity of blood, so that the fire was extinguished" (ch. 16, ANF 4158–4165). The dove is doubtful: Gebhardt brackets it in the Greek (GEB 890), and Eusebius has the blood but no dove (EUS 23500–23511). CHURCH (dove: Contested; see D3).
M32. **"Every word ... accomplished."** The church says that every word from his mouth "either has been or shall yet be accomplished" (ch. 16, ANF 4169–4170). CHURCH. This is a claim of prophecy, Documented as their claim.
M33. **The body withheld.** Nicetes asked the governor not to give up the body, "lest ... forsaking Him that was crucified, they begin to worship this one". The church says he did this at the urging of the Jews (ch. 17, ANF 4189–4196; again a hostile claim by the church). The church answers that it worships Christ alone and loves the martyrs as disciples (ANF 4198–4208). CHURCH. Documented.
M34. **Bones gathered.** The centurion burned the body. The church "took up his bones, as being more precious than the most exquisite jewels", laid them "in a fitting place", and planned to gather there each year on "the anniversary [Greek: birthday] of his martyrdom" (ch. 18, ANF 4217–4229). CHURCH. Documented.
M35. **Twelfth martyr.** He was "the twelfth that was martyred in Smyrna (reckoning those also of Philadelphia)" (ch. 19, ANF 4238–4240). Eusebius: "with the eleven from Philadelphia" (EUS 23608–23610). CHURCH. Documented, with D7.
M36. **The letter is sent.** The church sent "this summary account through our brother Marcus" to Philomelium and asked them to send it on "to the brethren at a greater distance". Evarestus wrote it (ch. 20, ANF 4263–4275). CHURCH. Documented. For the messenger's name, see D6.
M37. **WONDER, told only in a later epilogue: the trumpet voice.** An alternative epilogue printed by Gebhardt (the "Epilogus alius") says that at the hour of the martyrdom, Irenaeus, then in Rome, heard a voice "like a trumpet" saying "Polycarp has been martyred" (GEB 1056–1060). It is anonymous and of unknown date, and is not in the ANF text. Inferential-Thin. **Do not tell this as fact.** If it is used at all, it must be named as a late legend.
M38. **WONDER claim by a later copyist: Pionius's revelation.** The closing note in ANF says that a man named Pionius copied the text after "the blessed Polycarp having manifested them to me through a revelation" (ANF 4321–4332; GEB 1024–1033). This is a later copyist's claim. Inferential-Thin.


## 3. Quotes (final readings)

Each quote is in our modern English, settled from two independent renderings. Use the final reading exactly. Where a quote carries a warning, the warning says how it may be used.

**Q1. Martyrdom ch. 1: he waited to be handed over**
- Source: ANF 3767–3772; GEB 564–567.
- Final reading: "He waited to be handed over, as the Lord did. He did it so that we too might imitate him, looking not only to what concerns ourselves but also to what concerns our neighbors."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q2. Ch. 4: against volunteering**
- Source: ANF 3848–3849; GEB 636–638.
- Final reading: "So, brothers and sisters, we do not praise those who hand themselves over. The gospel does not teach that."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q3. Ch. 5: the vision**
- Source: ANF 3865–3869; GEB 647–650.
- Final reading: "While he was praying, three days before his arrest, he had a vision. He saw his pillow being burned up by fire. He turned to those who were with him and said, 'I must be burned alive.'"
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: a wonder reported by the Smyrna church. Do not add ANF's "prophetically", which in the Greek belongs only to the ch. 12 recall.

**Q4. Ch. 7: arrest**
- Source: ANF 3907–3926; GEB 678–691 (the sheet gives no Greek for the food line; it is GEB 684–687).
- Final reading (escape): "He could have gone from there to another place, but he did not want to. He said, 'Let the will of God be done.'"
- Final reading (onlookers): "Those present were amazed at his age and his calm. They marvelled that there had been such haste to arrest so old a man."
- Final reading (food): "At once, in that very hour, he had food and drink set before them, as much as they wanted. He asked them to give him an hour to pray in peace."
- Final reading (prayer): "He stood and prayed, full of the grace of God. For two hours he could not fall silent. Those who heard him were amazed. Many were sorry they had come out against such a godly old man."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q5. Ch. 8: in the carriage**
- Source: ANF 3944–3954; GEB 701–704.
- Final reading: "What harm is there in saying 'Caesar is Lord', and offering sacrifice, and the rest that goes with it, and so saving your life?" / Reply: "I am not going to do what you advise."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q6. Ch. 9: the voice**
- Source: ANF 3973–3976; GEB 724–727.
- Final reading: "A voice came from heaven: 'Be strong, Polycarp, and be brave.' No one saw who spoke. But those of our people who were there heard the voice."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: a wonder reported by the Smyrna church. Eusebius's retelling says "not many heard" it (D12).

**Q7. Ch. 9: "Away with the atheists"**
- Source: ANF 3979–3986; GEB 731–738.
- Final reading: "'Respect your age.' ... 'Swear by the Fortune of Caesar. Change your mind. Say, "Away with the atheists."' Polycarp looked with a grave face at the whole crowd of lawless pagans in the stadium. He waved his hand at them, groaned, looked up to heaven and said, 'Away with the atheists.'"
- Use: USABLE.

**Q8. Ch. 9: eighty-six years**
- Source: ANF 3988–3992; GEB 738–746.
- Final reading: "The proconsul pressed him: 'Swear, and I will set you free. Curse Christ.' Polycarp said, 'For eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my King, who saved me?'"
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the text does not say whether the 86 years are his age or his years as a Christian (D4).

**Q9. Ch. 10: "I am a Christian"**
- Source: ANF 4003–4008; GEB 747–753.
- Final reading: "You fool yourself if you think I will swear by the Fortune of Caesar, as you put it. You pretend not to know who I am. So hear me say it boldly: I am a Christian. If you want to learn what Christianity teaches, give me a day, and listen."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the opening Greek is hard. It means either "you vainly imagine I will swear" or "you vainly press me to swear".

**Q10. Ch. 10: whom he would answer**
- Source: ANF 4009–4015; GEB 754–757.
- Final reading: "The proconsul said, 'Persuade the people.' Polycarp said, 'You I would count worth an answer. We have been taught to give rulers and authorities appointed by God the honor that is due, so long as it does us no harm. But I do not think those people deserve a defense from me.'"
- Use: USABLE.

**Q11. Ch. 11: the beasts and the fire**
- Source: ANF 4025–4038; GEB 772–783.
- Final reading (beasts): "'I have wild beasts. I will throw you to them unless you change your mind.' He said, 'Call them. For us, a change of mind from better to worse is impossible. But it is good to change from cruelty to justice.'"
- Final reading (fire): "'If you scorn the beasts, I will have you burned up by fire, unless you change your mind.' Polycarp said, 'You threaten fire that burns for an hour and soon goes out. You do not know the fire of the judgment to come and of eternal punishment, kept for the ungodly. But why are you waiting? Bring what you want.'"
- Use: USABLE.

**Q12. Ch. 12: the crowd**
- Source: ANF 4055–4058; GEB 797–800.
- Final reading: "This is the teacher of Asia, the father of the Christians, the destroyer of our gods. He teaches many not to sacrifice and not to worship."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q13. Ch. 13: undressing; the nails**
- Source: ANF 4083–4096; GEB 820–830.
- Final reading (undressing): "He took off all his clothes himself and untied his belt. He also tried to take off his sandals. He had not done this before, because each of the faithful was always eager to be the first to touch his skin. For he had been honored in every way for his good life, even before his martyrdom."
- Final reading (nails): "Leave me as I am. The one who lets me endure the fire will also let me stay on the pyre without flinching, without the safety of your nails."
- Use: USABLE.

**Q14. Ch. 14: the prayer (core lines)**
- Source: ANF 4104–4124; GEB 834–853.
- Final reading (ram): "He was bound like a choice ram from a great flock, taken for an offering: a whole burnt offering, made ready and pleasing to God."
- Final reading (thanksgiving): "Lord God Almighty, Father of your beloved and blessed child Jesus Christ. Through him we have come to know you. You are the God of angels and powers and of all creation, and of the whole race of the righteous who live in your presence. I bless you, because you have counted me worthy of this day and this hour. You have given me a share among the martyrs, in the cup of your Christ. It leads to the resurrection to eternal life, of soul and body, in the immortality of the Holy Spirit."
- Final reading (acceptance): "May I be received among them before you today, as a rich and pleasing sacrifice. You prepared this beforehand, you showed it beforehand, and you have fulfilled it. You are the God who does not lie, the true God."
- Final reading (doxology): "For this reason, and for all things, I praise you, I bless you, I glorify you. I do this through the eternal and heavenly high priest Jesus Christ, your beloved child. Through him be glory to you, with him and the Holy Spirit, now and for the ages to come. Amen."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: quote this reading, not ANF's. The Greek and Eusebius have "I bless you" (ANF "I give Thee thanks") and "through the ... high priest" (ANF drops it). παῖς means "child" or "servant", and ANF and Eusebius print "Son".

**Q15. Ch. 15: the wonder in the fire**
- Source: ANF 4136–4149; GEB 869–884.
- Final reading: "When he had sent up the Amen and finished his prayer, the men in charge of the fire lit it. A great flame flashed out, and we saw a wonder, we to whom it was given to see. We were preserved to tell the others what happened. The fire took the shape of a vaulted room, like a ship's sail filled by the wind. It walled in the martyr's body all around. He was in the middle, not like flesh burning, but like bread baking, or like gold and silver glowing in a furnace. And we caught a fragrance so strong, it was like the breath of frankincense or some other precious spice."
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: wonders reported by the Smyrna church as eyewitnesses. Eusebius's retelling lacks the bread.

**Q16. Ch. 16: the dagger; what the church called him**
- Source: ANF 4158–4170; GEB 886–899.
- Final reading (dagger): "In the end the lawless men saw that the fire could not consume his body. They ordered an executioner to go up to him and drive in a dagger. When he did, out came a dove and so much blood that it put out the fire."
- Final reading (what he was): "The most wonderful Polycarp was one of these chosen ones. In our own times he was a teacher like the apostles and the prophets, and bishop of the universal church in Smyrna. Every word that came from his mouth has come true, or will come true."

## 4. Impact as the record gives it

I1. **His martyrdom ended the persecution in Smyrna,** in the church's words: he "put an end to the persecution, having, as it were, set a seal upon it by his martyrdom" (Martyrdom ch. 1, ANF 3762–3765; EUS 23148–23152). CHURCH.
I2. **The church held up his death as a martyrdom "becoming the Gospel"** (ch. 1, ANF 3766–3767). They set it against Quintus, who came forward of his own accord and then gave way (ch. 4). Martyrs were not to hand themselves over (ANF 3848–3849). CHURCH.
I3. **The church kept his bones and planned a yearly gathering** on the day of his martyrdom, to remember those who had died and prepare those to come (ch. 18, ANF 4219–4229). CHURCH.
I4. **He was remembered widely:** "he is everywhere spoken of by the heathen themselves", and his martyrdom is one "all desire to imitate" (ch. 19, ANF 4241–4245). CHURCH.
I5. **The account was sent on.** Philomelium had asked for a full account. Smyrna sent a summary and asked that it be forwarded to churches farther away (ch. 20, ANF 4263–4269). Eusebius had the letter and copied most of it into his history (IV.15, EUS 23078–23638), "in the same volume" with other Smyrna martyrdoms (EUS 23643–23679). CHURCH; LATER (about 150 years).
I6. **The copy chain the text itself records:** Gaius copied it from Irenaeus's copy, Socrates copied it at Corinth, and Pionius copied it again (ANF 4315–4332; GEB 1019–1033). The note is by later copyists; see M38. LATER, date not given in the source.
I7. **He gathered and sent on Ignatius's letters.** He sent the Philippians Ignatius's letters to Smyrna and "all the rest ... which we have by us" (Philippians 13, ANF 3613–3618). Eusebius quotes this passage (III.36.13–15, EUS 20073–20101). OWN.
I8. **He taught Irenaeus.** Irenaeus heard him as a boy and treasured his teaching "not on paper, but in my heart" (ANF 54585–54588; EUS 30821–30824). Eusebius: Irenaeus "in his youth ... was a hearer of Polycarp" (V.5.8, EUS 27956). The Martyrdom's closing note calls Irenaeus "a disciple of Polycarp" (ANF 4316–4317). HEARER; LATER.
I9. **Irenaeus used him as a witness of the apostles' teaching against the heretics.** Polycarp was "a more stedfast witness of truth, than Valentinus, and Marcion, and the rest", and "all the Asiatic Churches testify" to what he taught, as do his successors (III.3.4, ANF 39867–39871). Irenaeus uses this inside his argument from the bishops' succession (ANF 39851–39855). HEARER.
I10. **Irenaeus used him against Florinus.** Irenaeus wrote to Florinus, who had once sought Polycarp's approval, that Polycarp would have stopped his ears at Florinus's teaching (ANF 54563–54592; EUS 30784–30829). HEARER.
I11. **His peace with Anicetus became the precedent Irenaeus used to restrain Victor of Rome,** who tried to cut off the churches of Asia over the Easter date. Irenaeus "fittingly admonishes Victor that he should not cut off whole churches", citing Polycarp and Anicetus (Eusebius V.24.9–17, EUS 31415–31612). Eusebius calls Irenaeus "a peacemaker in this matter" (EUS 31659–31663). HEARER; Eusebius LATER.
I12. **Polycrates, bishop of Ephesus, named him among Asia's "great lights"** who "observed the fourteenth day of the passover according to the Gospel": "And Polycarp in Smyrna, who was a bishop and martyr" (letter to Victor, Eusebius V.24.2–6, EUS 31324–31366). LATER (Polycrates writes to Victor, who became bishop of Rome in the tenth year of Commodus, EUS 31002–31003; roughly a generation after the death).
I13. **Irenaeus recommended his letter:** "a very powerful Epistle of Polycarp written to the Philippians, from which those who choose to do so ... can learn the character of his faith" (III.3.4, ANF 39889–39896). HEARER.
I14. **His letter draws on 1 Peter,** as Eusebius notes: it "has made use of certain testimonies drawn from the First Epistle of Peter" (IV.14.9, EUS 23024–23027). LATER (about 150 to 200 years). For the writer, this is a card point at most.
I15. **His letter was still read in church services in Jerome's day:** "a very valuable Epistle to the Philippians which is read to the present day in the meetings in Asia" (Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 17, JER 39853–39855). LATER (about 230 years).
I16. **Papias is named as his companion.** Irenaeus calls Papias "the hearer of John, and a companion of Polycarp" (Against Heresies V.33.4, ANF 54080–54081; Eusebius III.39.1, EUS 20354–20355). HEARER. This is a link, not a stated impact.
I17. **Smyrna's own church records,** as Tertullian reports them: "the church of Smyrna, which records that Polycarp was placed therein by John" (Prescription against Heretics 32, TERT 21462–21463). Tertullian uses this, like Irenaeus, as proof of succession from the apostles. LATER (about 40 to 50 years).
I18. **What he defended, in his own words:** that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, the witness of the cross, the resurrection and the judgment, and "the word which has been handed down to us from the beginning" (Philippians 7, ANF 3433–3444). OWN.
I19. **What he urged in church life, in his own words:** compassionate presbyters who bring back the wandering, visit the sick and care for widows, orphans and the poor (Philippians 6, ANF 3403–3406). He urged gentleness toward a fallen presbyter (ch. 11), and prayer for rulers and persecutors (ch. 12). OWN.


## 5. Disputes and unclear points

D1. **Who made him bishop.** Irenaeus says "by apostles in Asia" (ANF 39859). Tertullian says the Smyrna records name John (TERT 21463). Jerome says he was ordained by John (JER 39840–39841). Eusebius says "by those who had seen and heard the Lord" (EUS 19682–19684). The earliest witness (Irenaeus) names no single apostle. Tell it as Irenaeus's claim; "John" is a later and narrower claim.
D2. **The date.** The Martyrdom gives a day and month but no year (ch. 21). The month is unclear: ANF prints "the seventh day before the Kalends of May" (ANF 4287–4288). Gebhardt's Greek reads Μαρτίων, March (GEB 998 "Mooriwv", GEB0 1084 "Meotiwv"; both OCR, most likely Μαρτίων). ANF's own note calls the translation "very doubtful" (card only). Eusebius puts the death under Marcus Aurelius (EUS 23032–23074). Jerome puts it under "Marcus Antoninus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus" (JER 39850–39851). The day is "the great Sabbath" (ch. 8, ch. 21). Tell only that it was a Saturday, called the great Sabbath, in the time the sources give. No exact year is in the record.
D3. **The dove.** ANF prints "there came forth a dove" (ANF 4160). Gebhardt's Greek brackets "[περιστερὰ καὶ]" as doubtful (GEB 890), and his note says Eusebius omits it (GEB 912). Eusebius's text indeed has only the blood (EUS 23500–23511). ANF's own note reports the same omission and a proposed corrected reading (card only). Contested: if told, tell it as doubtful.
D4. **His age.** "Eighty and six years have I served Him" (ANF 3990). The Greek is present tense, "I serve" (GEB 742–745). The record does not say whether this is his age or his years as a Christian. Irenaeus says only that he "tarried [on earth] a very long time" and died "a very old man" (ANF 39860–39861). The age at death is Inferential-Thin. The ANF editor's note turning it into an age and a date ("about the year 167") is card only (ANF 39862–39864).
D5. **The words with Marcion.** ANF (Irenaeus's Latin tradition): "Dost thou know me?" / "I do know thee" (ANF 39883–39884). Eusebius: "Knowest thou us?" / "I know the first born of Satan" (EUS 22977–22982). The alternative Martyrdom epilogue: "Ἐπιγίνωσκε ἡμᾶς" ("Recognize us") / "Ἐπιγινώσκω, ἐπιγινώσκω τὸν πρωτότοκον τοῦ σατανᾶ" ("I recognize, I recognize the firstborn of Satan") (GEB 1053–1056). McGiffert's note on the manuscript readings is card only (EUS 22977). The answer "the firstborn of Satan" is the same in all three. Polycarp's own letter uses the same phrase of false teachers (ANF 3439), without naming anyone.
D6. **The messenger's name.** ANF: "through our brother Marcus" (ANF 4265). Gebhardt's Greek: "Μαρκίωνος", Marcion (GEB 981–983), with "Μαρκιανοῦ" noted as another editor's reading (GEB 1013, "Mapnıovov Lf."). Name him only as "a brother", or give the doubt.
D7. **How many martyrs.** The Martyrdom says "the twelfth that was martyred in Smyrna (reckoning those also of Philadelphia)" (ANF 4239–4240). Eusebius: "with the eleven from Philadelphia" (EUS 23609–23610). These can agree (eleven others plus Polycarp), but Eusebius makes all eleven Philadelphians.
D8. **Ignatius alive or dead in Polycarp's letter.** Ch. 9 counts Ignatius among those who "are [now] in their due place in the presence of the Lord" (ANF 3487–3492). Ch. 13 asks for news "respecting both Ignatius himself, and those that were with him" (ANF 3620–3622). The ANF says the Latin reads "are", which the translator "corrected" to "were" (ANF note 415 at 3622, card only). The letter as printed leaves it unclear what Polycarp knew. Do not resolve it.
D9. **Parts of the letter survive only in Latin.** Ch. 10–12 and the end of ch. 13 to the end exist only in a Latin version (ANF notes 391 and 414, at 3501 and 3619; card only). Quotes Q23 and Q24 and S6 come from that Latin.
D10. **Vision details.** The Martyrdom has no "at night" and no "for Christ's sake". Eusebius's retelling adds both (EUS 23217–23222). Follow the Martyrdom.
D11. **"Before his martyrdom" or "before his gray hairs".** The Martyrdom says he was honored "even before his martyrdom" (ANF 4088–4089; GEB 826, "πρὸ τῆς μαρτυρίας"). Eusebius's text reads "even before his gray hairs came" (EUS 23430–23431). Follow the Martyrdom.
D12. **Who heard the voice.** The Martyrdom: "those of our brethren who were present heard" (ANF 3975–3976). Eusebius: "not many heard ... but many of our people heard" (EUS 23295–23300). Both limit it to Christians who were present.
D13. **Where he met Marcion.** Irenaeus does not say (ANF 39882–39883). Jerome places it in Rome (JER 39846–39849). Leave it unplaced, or name Jerome as the late source.
D14. **Why he went to Rome.** Irenaeus to Victor says only that he "was sojourning in Rome in the time of Anicetus" and that they disagreed "as to certain other points" as well as Easter (ANF 54651–54653). Eusebius IV.14.1 calls it "a conference with Anicetus on a question concerning the day of the paschal feast" (EUS 22849–22869). Jerome says he went "on account of certain questions concerning the day of the Passover" (JER 39843–39845). The earliest witness does not give Easter as the purpose of the trip.
D15. **The address of the Martyrdom.** The Martyrdom is addressed to Philomelium and "all the congregations of the Holy and Catholic Church in every place" (ANF 3749–3755). Eusebius says it went "to the parishes in Pontus" (EUS 23088). McGiffert's note judges Eusebius mistaken (card only).
D16. **"Teacher of Asia".** ANF's note 451 says some manuscripts read "ungodliness" instead of Asia; the translator prefers "Asia" (ANF 4055–4056). Gebhardt's Greek has "ὁ τῆς ᾿Ασίας διδάσκαλος" (GEB 797–798) and Eusebius's text has "the teacher of Asia" (EUS 23395–23396). Widely Accepted; the variant is card only.


## 6. What the record does not tell us

N1. Nothing in the Library gives his birth, birthplace, family or conversion. The "86 years" may or may not mean his age (D4).
N2. The record does not give the year he became bishop or the year he died (D2).
N3. Irenaeus says Polycarp spoke of "John". The sources I read do not say more about which John, or where and when they met, beyond "John, the disciple of the Lord" (ANF 39878, 54580, 54657; EUS 31604).
N4. No letter of his survives in the Library except the one to the Philippians, though Irenaeus says he wrote others (ANF 54593–54596).
N5. The record does not say what the Philippians did with his letter or with Ignatius's letters after receiving them.
N6. The record does not say whether Polycarp sent a messenger to Syria as Ignatius asked. Philippians 13 only promises to do it "if I find a fitting opportunity" (ANF 3611).
N7. No source gives the outcome of the Rome visit beyond peace and shared communion. Nothing says how many people he turned back, by name or number.
N8. Valens's offence is not stated plainly. The letter only links him with covetousness (ANF 3531–3548).
N9. The record does not name the "few [friends]" at the farm, or the young slave who betrayed him.
N10. The account names no one who saw the fire wonders. "we, to whom it was given to witness it" (ANF 4140–4141) speaks for the whole church. Evarestus wrote the letter down (ANF 4274–4275); the record does not say he was present.
N11. Plausible but unstated (not for the story): that Polycarp's letter was written soon after Ignatius passed through; that he collected Ignatius's letters into the collection later known; that Irenaeus took the Martyrdom westward. The sources do not state any of these. Only the copy note says Gaius copied "from the copy of Irenæus" (ANF 4316).
N12. The Martyrdom says the Jews of Smyrna helped gather wood and urged that the body be withheld. These are the church's own hostile claims, and no other source tells that side. The account does not give their reasons.
