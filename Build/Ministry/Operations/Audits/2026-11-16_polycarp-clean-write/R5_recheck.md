## 1. Legacy: does a listener come away knowing what he left the church?

By word share the ask is met: the last three paragraphs (gathering, Irenaeus and Easter, the letter) run about 140 of 363 words, a little over a third. By substance it is not.

What a listener now takes away:
- His church kept his bones and a yearly day (I3). Good, and now clear: "to remember those who had died and to prepare those still to come."
- His peace with Anicetus later restrained Victor (I11). Good; the strongest told impact.
- His letter called readers back "to the word handed down to us from the beginning" and was still read in Jerome's day (I18 in part, I15).

What a listener does not take away:
- **What he defended.** "calls its readers back from false teaching" names no content. The sheet gives it plainly (I18, M8): that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, the witness of the cross, the resurrection and the judgment. A listener hears that he opposed something, not what he stood for. This is the "what they defended" the Shape line asks for.
- **Why he mattered to the church after him (I9).** Irenaeus used him as a witness to the apostles' teaching against Valentinus and Marcion, with "all the Asiatic Churches" testifying to what he taught. This is the heaviest HEARER impact line on the sheet. The brief reduces it to "Irenaeus carried his teaching on, and wrote that he kept it in his heart, not on paper", which tells us Irenaeus remembered him, not what that memory was used for. The opening's "had known people who had seen Jesus" sets this up and the ending never pays it off.
- **His death as the church's model (I1, I2, I4).** The Smyrna church held his death up as a martyrdom "becoming the Gospel", one that "put an end to the persecution" and that "all desire to imitate". The brief tells the gathering but not why this death, above others, was the one to imitate. "could have escaped. He said, 'Let the will of God be done'" half-shows it, but the church's own reading of it is gone.

Chosen by weight? No. I15 (Jerome, LATER, about 230 years, one sentence of reception) holds a full sentence and the closing position, while I9 (HEARER, the core of his legacy) and I1/I2 (CHURCH, the church's own verdict) are absent or reduced to a clause. The Jerome line is true and traced; it is the lightest of the lines kept.

## 2. Listener read (once through)

The story half still reads well: the cold quote, then who, where, when; the crowd; the farm; the arrest; the hospitality and prayer. Grade 5.8 is plain, not flat: images survive ("my King, who saved me", "like a ship's sail"). A few joins have gone choppy where cuts were made:

- **The dagger.** "The church in Smyrna says its own people saw the flames curve around him like a ship's sail. An executioner killed him with a dagger." With "the fire could not consume his body" cut, the dagger arrives with no reason. A listener who has just heard he was to be burned asks why a knife, and the wonder (the fire would not touch him) is lost with it. Q16 and M31 support restoring the reason in a few words. Not wrong, but a genuine snag at the climax.
- **"false teaching"** is unexplained (see section 1); a newcomer cannot tell what it was.
- **"Irenaeus carried his teaching on ... Polycarp had once visited Rome."** The Irenaeus sentence sits apart from what follows; it reads as a fragment of a point. Folding I9 in would give it work to do.
- **Jerome:** now introduced ("writing in the late fourth century", M11). Fixed.
- **One farm vs two:** losing the second farm costs nothing a listener needs; "Searchers seized two young slaves of his household" holds without it (M20 places this at the second farm, but the brief does not say where, so nothing is wrong).
- "Because many wished it" is thin (who wished it?) but holds; "his friends" would be clearer (M18: "the wish of many").
