Quoted words: sentences 1–2 use Q8. Sentence 7 uses the crowd's cry as in M16 and Q7. Sentence 12 uses Q4 (escape). Sentence 18 uses Q18 ("keep the birthday of his martyrdom") and keeps its reason ("prepare those still to come") without quote marks. Sentence 23 uses Q21 ("the word handed down to us from the beginning"). Without quote marks: sentence 8 keeps Q12's key words ("the destroyer of our gods"); the crowd makes this charge in chapter 12, and the story sets it beside the cry of chapter 3 to explain "atheists". Sentence 14 keeps Q4's "such a godly old man", the view of the men sent to arrest him. Sentence 16 keeps Q15's image ("a ship's sail") as the church's report. Sentence 24 retells I15 ("read to the present day in the meetings in Asia") in modern words.

Order: the story opens on the answer in the stadium (chapter 9), goes back to the cry, the farms and the arrest (chapters 3–7), and returns to the stadium at sentence 15.

Left out on purpose: the dove (D3), the vision of the pillow (M19), the voice from heaven (M23), the fragrance (M30), the Marcion meeting (M11, D5), Irenaeus's use of Polycarp against Valentinus and Marcion (I9), the claim that apostles made him bishop (S5, D1), Ignatius and his letters (M1–M6, I7), the sending of the account (M36, I5), his answers to the threats of beasts and fire (Q11), the burning of the body by the centurion (M34), and the church's hostile wording about the Jews of Smyrna (M27, M33, N12).

## What is uncertain

- **The century.** No ancient witness counts the years. They place Polycarp by rulers and bishops: in Rome under Anicetus, and dead under Marcus Aurelius. "The second century" comes from the modern standard dating of those reigns (S12).
- **Known people who had seen Jesus.** This is Irenaeus's claim. He also says apostles taught Polycarp and that "apostles in Asia" made him bishop, naming no single apostle. Tertullian and Jerome later name John (D1). The sources do not say more about which John Polycarp knew, or where and when they met (N3).
- **Irenaeus's memories.** Irenaeus wrote them down many years after he heard Polycarp as a boy (M13, Q28).
- **The visit to Rome.** Irenaeus, the earliest witness, does not give Easter as the reason for the journey, and says the two men also differed on "certain other points". Eusebius and Jerome make Easter the purpose (D14). Irenaeus says the two shared communion, which the story leaves out, and that Anicetus gave Polycarp the Eucharist "as a mark of respect". This may mean Polycarp presided, or only that he took part (Q29).
- **Eighty-six years.** The text does not say whether this is Polycarp's age or his years as a Christian. The Greek verb is in the present tense, "I serve" (D4). Nothing in the record gives his birth, family or conversion (N1).
- **The date.** The Martyrdom gives a day, a Saturday it calls "the great Sabbath", but no year. The month is unclear: the printed English says May and the Greek says March. Eusebius places the death in the reign of Marcus Aurelius, about 150 years after the event (S10, S11, D2).
- **The letter still read.** This rests on Jerome alone, writing about 230 years after Polycarp's death (I15).
- **The wonders.** The fire shaped like a sail, and the body the fire could not consume, are told by the Smyrna church as what its own people saw. The account names no single eyewitness (M29, N10). Eusebius's retelling keeps the sail (Q15).
- **The dove.** The printed English text adds that a dove came out with the blood when Polycarp was stabbed. The Greek edition marks the dove as doubtful and Eusebius leaves it out. The story does not tell it (D3, C5).
- **The betrayal.** The account says two young slaves were seized and one confessed under torture. It does not name them (M20, N9).
- **The account of his death.** The Smyrna church sent it to the church at Philomelium. Eusebius says it went to churches in Pontus (D15). The printed English names the messenger Marcus. The Greek reads Marcion, with another reading, Marcianus (D6).
- **The yearly gathering.** The Greek calls the day "the birthday of his martyrdom" (M34, Q18).
- **The letter to the Philippians.** The quoted line is rendered from one English translation, with no Greek in the Library (Q21). Parts of the letter survive only in Latin (D9). Irenaeus says Polycarp wrote other letters. None of them is in the Library (M15, N4).
- **Forty years.** Irenaeus wrote to Victor about forty years after the Rome visit. The record gives no exact year for either (M9, I11).
- **Modern scholarship.** The editors' notes on the date (one puts the death "about the year 167"), on the dove, and on the manuscripts are not ancient witnesses, and are kept out of the story (C2, C4, C5, C6, C8, C9).
