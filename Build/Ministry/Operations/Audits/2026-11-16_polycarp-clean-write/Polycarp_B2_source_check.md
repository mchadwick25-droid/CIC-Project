# Source check: Polycarp_B2.brief.txt

2026-11-16. A sentence-by-sentence check against the primary source files in `cic/texts/`. It is not a style review. Source_Sheet.md and Renderings_Compared.md were used only to find lines. Every passage was read in the source file itself.

File short names: **ANF** = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml`; **GEB** = `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt` (Greek of the Martyrdom, OCR); **EUS** = `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml`; **JER** = `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`. **Mart.** = Martyrdom of Polycarp. **RC Qn** = the final reading for quote n in Renderings_Compared.md.

## Paragraph 1: the answer

**S1.** "For eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong."
- Source: Mart. 9.3. GEB 738–745 (Ὀγδοήκοντα καὶ ἓξ ἔτη δουλεύω αὐτῷ, καὶ οὐδέν με ἠδίκησεν); ANF 3988–3992; EUS 23328–23336 (IV.15). Matches RC Q8 exactly.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** The Greek gives years of *service* (δουλεύω). It does not give his age.

**S2.** "How can I blaspheme my King, who saved me?"
- Source: GEB 745–746 (τὸν σώσαντά με); EUS 23335–23336 ("my king who saved me"). ANF 3992 has "my Saviour". Matches RC Q8 exactly.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

## Paragraph 2: who and where

**S3.** "An old man gave that answer in a stadium in Smyrna, a city in the Roman province of Asia, in the second century."
- Source:
  - Old age: Mart. 7.2 "so old a man" (GEB 682–685; ANF 3915–3926); Mart. 9.2 "Respect your age" (GEB 731–732; ANF 3980–3981); Irenaeus III.3.4 "a very old man" (ANF 39860).
  - The stadium: Mart. 9.2, "the whole crowd ... in the stadium" (GEB 735; ANF 3984–3985).
  - Smyrna: the Martyrdom's opening greeting (ANF 3753).
  - Asia: Mart. 12.2, "the teacher of Asia" (GEB 798–799).
  - The second century: modern standard chronology, not a held source (Source_Sheet S12, Widely Accepted; see C13).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - Placed straight after "For eighty-six years", "An old man" lets a listener hear 86 as his age. The sentence does not say it, so it stands.
  - The Martyrdom gives years of service. Whether they count from his birth or his baptism is not stated (D4; the NPNF note at EUS ~23320–23328 says the same).
  - ANF's editor's note "His great age of eighty-six years" (ANF note 3314, beside 39860) is a modern inference. Neither this brief nor its card may adopt it.
  - "Roman province" is background knowledge. The sources say only "Asia".

**S4.** "The Roman governor had offered to free him if he would curse Christ."
- Source: Mart. 9.3, the proconsul's words "Ὄμοσον, καὶ ἀπολύω σε· λοιδόρησον τὸν Χριστόν" (GEB 738–740), in ANF "Swear, and I will set thee at liberty, reproach Christ" (ANF 3988–3989).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - In the Greek, the release is joined directly to "Swear" (by the Fortune of Caesar, 9.2). "Curse Christ" is a second command placed beside it.
  - Making cursing Christ the condition is a fair compression, because Polycarp's answer replies to that demand. The source itself asks for two things: swear, and curse Christ.
  - "Governor" stands for the proconsul (ἀνθύπατος).

**S5.** "The old man was Polycarp, the bishop of the church there."
- Source: Mart. 16.2, "bishop of the catholic church in Smyrna" (GEB 893–897; ANF 4165–4168); Irenaeus III.3.4, "appointed bishop of the Church in Smyrna" (ANF 39859–39860).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

**S6.** "Irenaeus, who heard him as a boy, wrote that Polycarp had known people who had seen Jesus."
- Source:
  - Irenaeus to Florinus: "while I was yet a boy". He can describe Polycarp's "discourses which he delivered to the people" and "his familiar intercourse with John, and with the rest of those who had seen the Lord" (ANF 54568–54581; EUS 30792–30815).
  - III.3.4: "conversed with many who had seen Christ ... whom I also saw in my early youth" (ANF 39857–39859).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

## Paragraph 3: the crowd

**S7.** "In a time of persecution, the crowd had shouted, 'Away with the atheists!'"
- Source:
  - The persecution: Mart. 1.1 "put an end to the persecution" (ANF 3764).
  - The cry: Mart. 3.2, after Germanicus's death, "Αἶρε τοὺς ἀθέους· ζητείσθω Πολύκαρπος" (GEB 621–623), in ANF "Away with the Atheists; let Polycarp be sought out!" (ANF 3833–3835).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - The words match RC Q7's settled rendering of the same phrase. Q7 is the ch. 9 instance; the ch. 3 cry has no separate entry.
  - RC Q7 asks for one gloss on "atheists": people who reject the gods. In the brief, S8 carries that sense only by implication.

**S8.** "They demanded Polycarp, whom they called the destroyer of their gods."
- Source:
  - The demand: Mart. 3.2, "let Polycarp be sought out" (GEB 623; ANF 3835).
  - The title: Mart. 12.2, "ὁ τῶν ἡμετέρων θεῶν καθαιρέτης" (GEB 797–800; ANF 4055–4058; RC Q12). The crowd shouted this in the stadium, after the herald announced that he had confessed. That is chapters later, after his flight, arrest and trial.
- Verdict: **NOT SUPPORTED (as placed).**
  - In ch. 3 the crowd says only "let Polycarp be sought out".
  - The brief joins the ch. 12 title to the ch. 3 demand, inside a pluperfect told before his flight. It moves the cry out of its place in the order of events.

## Paragraph 4: flight and arrest

**S9.** "Because many wished it, he withdrew to a farm, and then to another."
- Source: Mart. 5.1, "οἱ δὲ πλείους ἔπειθον αὐτὸν ὑπεξελθεῖν ... εἰς ἀγρίδιον" (GEB 641–643; ANF 3859–3863, "in deference to the wish of many ... a country house"); Mart. 6.1, "μετέβη εἰς ἕτερον ἀγρίδιον" (GEB 652–653; ANF 3879).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** ἀγρίδιον, "small farm", is used both times. (RC Q4's warning against "farm" concerns χωρίον in ch. 7, not this word.) He first wished to stay in the city (GEB 641–642); leaving that out does not change the sense.

**S10.** "There searchers seized two young slaves of his household, and one gave him away under torture."
- Source: Mart. 6.1, "εὐθέως ἐπέστησαν οἱ ζητοῦντες αὐτόν· καὶ μὴ εὑρόντες συνελάβοντο παιδάρια δύο, ὧν τὸ ἕτερον βασανιζόμενον ὡμολόγησεν ... οἱ προδιδόντες αὐτὸν οἰκεῖοι ὑπῆρχον" (GEB 653–657; ANF 3879–3883).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - "There", meaning at the second farm, follows ANF's "whither". The Greek says only that the searchers arrived at once and, not finding him, seized two παιδάρια. Where they seized them is not stated.
  - παιδάρια can mean "slave boys" or "young servants" (ANF "youths").
  - "Of his household" rests on οἰκεῖοι, said of "those who betrayed him".

**S11.** "When the police came, he could have escaped."
- Source: Mart. 7.1, "διωγμῖται καὶ ἱππεῖς" (GEB 665; ANF 3896–3898); "κἀκεῖθεν δὲ ἠδύνατο εἰς ἕτερον χωρίον ἀπελθεῖν, ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἠβουλήθη" (GEB 678–680; ANF 3905–3908).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** διωγμῖται were the mounted police. He could have left as they arrived, and he refused.

**S12.** "He said, 'Let the will of God be done.'"
- Source: GEB 680, "Τὸ θέλημα τοῦ θεοῦ γενέσθω"; ANF 3908–3910 (note: "Some read 'the Lord'"). Matches RC Q4 exactly.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

**S13.** "He had food and drink set before them and asked for an hour to pray."
- Source: Mart. 7.2 (GEB 684–687; ANF 3917–3920).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

**S14.** "He prayed for two hours, and many were sorry they had come for such a godly old man."
- Source: Mart. 7.3, "ἐπὶ δύο ὥρας μὴ δύνασθαι σιγῆσαι ... πολλούς τε μετανοεῖν ἐπὶ τῷ ἐληλυθέναι ἐπὶ τοιοῦτον θεοπρεπῆ πρεσβύτην" (GEB 687–691; ANF 3920–3926).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** The sentence is not a quotation. It paraphrases RC Q4's "come out against".

## Paragraph 5: the trial and the verdict

**S15.** "The governor threatened him with wild beasts, and then with fire."
- Source: Mart. 11.1–2 (GEB 772–778; ANF 4025–4034). Beasts come first and fire second, in that order.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

**S16.** "Polycarp answered, 'You threaten fire that burns for an hour and soon goes out.'"
- Source: GEB 779–781, "Πῦρ ἀπειλεῖς τὸ πρὸς ὥραν καιόμενον καὶ μετ᾽ ὀλίγον σβεννύμενον"; ANF 4035–4036. This is the first sentence of RC Q11 (fire) and matches it exactly. The quote ends at a full stop in the settled reading, so nothing inside it is cut.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

**S17.** "The crowd asked for a lion, but the beast shows were over, so they shouted for him to be burned."
- Source: Mart. 12.2–3 (GEB 797–806; ANF 4052–4065). After the herald's proclamation, the crowd shouted and "asked Philip the Asiarch to let loose a lion on Polycarp". He said it was not lawful, "since he had finished the hunts" (τὰ κυνηγέσια; ANF "shows of wild beasts were already finished"). Then they cried with one voice that he be burned alive.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** The order matches the source: threats, the herald, the crowd's cries, the lion refused, the call for fire, then the pyre (Mart. 13).

## Paragraph 6: the fire and the yearly gathering

**S18.** "The church in Smyrna says its own people saw the flames curve around him like a ship's sail."
- Source: Mart. 15.1–2, "θαῦμα εἴδομεν, οἷς ἰδεῖν ἐδόθη ... καμάρας εἶδος ποιῆσαν, ὥσπερ ὀθόνη πλοίου ... κύκλῳ περιετείχισεν τὸ σῶμα" (GEB 870–878; ANF 4136–4146; RC Q15).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** The wonder is told as the Smyrna church's report, as RC Q15 requires.

**S19.** "The fire could not consume his body, they say, so an executioner killed him with a dagger."
- Source: Mart. 16.1, the lawless men "saw that his body could not be consumed by the fire" and ordered the κομφέκτωρ (executioner) to drive in a ξιφίδιον, a dagger (GEB 886–888; ANF 4158–4160).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** Leaving out the doubtful dove (D3) is correct.

**S20.** "Later they laid his bones in a fitting place, and resolved to gather there each year and 'keep the birthday of his martyrdom.'"
- Source: Mart. 18.2–3, "ὕστερον ἀνελόμενοι ... ὀστᾶ αὐτοῦ, ἀπεθέμεθα ὅπου καὶ ἀκόλουθον ἦν· ἔνθα ὡς δυνατὸν ἡμῖν συναγομένοις ἐν ἀγαλλιάσει καὶ χαρᾷ παρέξει ὁ κύριος ἐπιτελεῖν τὴν τοῦ μαρτυρίου αὐτοῦ ἡμέραν γενέθλιον" (GEB 936–951; ANF 4219–4226). The quoted words match RC Q18.
- Verdict: **OVERSTATED.**
  - The church does not say it "resolved" to gather. It says the *Lord will grant* that they gather there "as far as we can" (ὡς δυνατόν), in gladness and joy.
  - RC Q18 reshaped Rendering 1's "we will gather" for exactly this reason.
  - "Each year" is a fair reading of γενέθλιον, a birthday or anniversary (ANF "anniversary", note "Literally, 'the birth-day'"). The text implies it rather than saying it.
  - RC Q18 also asks for one gloss on "birthday": the day of death seen as a birth into life. The brief gives none.

**S21.** "They would do this 'to remember those who have already competed, and to train and prepare those still to come.'"
- Source: GEB 951–953, "εἴς τε τὴν τῶν προηθληκότων μνήμην καὶ τῶν μελλόντων ἄσκησίν τε καὶ ἑτοιμασίαν"; ANF 4226–4229. Matches RC Q18 exactly.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** This is the yearly gathering's stated purpose, and it is reported correctly.

## Paragraph 7: Rome, Anicetus and Victor

**S22.** "Years before, Polycarp had visited Rome, where Anicetus was bishop."
- Source: Irenaeus III.3.4, "coming to Rome in the time of Anicetus" (ANF 39871–39873); Irenaeus to Victor, "sojourning in Rome in the time of Anicetus" (ANF 54651–54652; EUS 31591–31601). Eusebius tells the visit (IV.14, EUS 22846–22869) before the death under Marcus Aurelius (IV.14–15, EUS 23032–23074).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - No held source gives the gap between the visit and the death.
  - Eusebius's placing supports "years". The modern dating printed in EUS's editor's notes (death 155/156, Anicetus from about 154; EUS 22849–22867 note) would put the visit only shortly before the death. That is a contested point.
  - "Before this" or "Earlier" claims nothing about the length of the gap.

**S23.** "The two could not agree about keeping Easter."
- Source: Irenaeus to Victor (EUS 31591–31612; ANF 54651–54662); Eusebius IV.14.1, "a question concerning the day of the paschal feast" (EUS 22867–22869); the context of the dispute is V.23–24 (EUS 31136–31140).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - The clause in Irenaeus does not itself name the feast. It says "what he had always observed" (RC Q29 warns about this).
  - Irenaeus also says that "they disagreed a little about certain other things" and "immediately made peace" on those (EUS 31599–31602; D14).
  - "Easter" stands for the Pascha.

**S24.** "Polycarp kept it as he had with John and the other apostles he had known."
- Source: EUS 31603–31606, "what he had always observed with John the disciple of our Lord, and the other apostles with whom he had associated"; ANF 54654–54657.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** This is Irenaeus's claim.

**S25.** "Anicetus said he must follow the customs of the elders before him."
- Source: EUS 31606–31608; ANF 54658–54659.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** The wording follows RC Q29 and is not presented as a quote.

**S26.** "'Even so, they shared communion,' Irenaeus wrote."
- Source: EUS 31609, "they communed together"; ANF 54659–54660. Matches RC Q29 exactly.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

**S27.** "'And they parted from each other in peace.'"
- Source: EUS 31655–31656; ANF 54661–54662. The words match RC Q29 exactly.
- Verdict: **NOT SUPPORTED (hidden cut).**
  - In RC Q29, one sentence stands between S26 and S27: "In the church, Anicetus gave Polycarp the celebration of the Eucharist, plainly as a mark of respect." (EUS 31609–31611; ANF 54660–54661).
  - The brief leaves that sentence out. It closes the gap with "Irenaeus wrote", and the second fragment opens with "And". A listener hears the two fragments as one unbroken quotation.

**S28.** "Some forty years later Victor, another bishop of Rome, tried to cut off the churches of Asia over the Easter date."
- Source:
  - Victor's action: Eusebius V.24.9, "Victor ... immediately attempted to cut off from the common unity the parishes of all Asia ... and declared all the brethren there wholly excommunicate" (EUS 31415–31420).
  - The question: whether the feast falls on the fourteenth day of the moon (V.23.1, EUS 31136–31140).
- Verdict: **NOT SUPPORTED (the interval).**
  - "Tried to cut off ... over the Easter date" is supported.
  - "Some forty years" is in no held source. Eusebius's own figures give less:
    - Anicetus held office eleven years and was succeeded in Marcus's eighth year (IV.19, EUS 24366–24390).
    - Marcus reigned nineteen years (V.9, EUS 28466–28469).
    - Victor became bishop in Commodus's tenth year (V.22, EUS 31002–31003).
    - So the visit falls about 21–32 years before Victor's accession, and Victor's action falls some time after it.
  - The modern dates in the editor's notes (Anicetus about 154; Victor 189) give about 35 years.
  - Source_Sheet M9's "about 40 years" is not grounded in a source line.

**S29.** "Irenaeus wrote to remind him that Polycarp and Anicetus had kept communion though they differed."
- Source: V.24.11, "He fittingly admonishes Victor that he should not cut off whole churches of God", followed by the Polycarp–Anicetus precedent (EUS 31424–31430, 31591–31612; ANF 54600–54666).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.**

## Paragraph 8: the letter

**S30.** "Polycarp's letter to the church at Philippi survives."
- Source: the letter itself (ANF, Epistle to the Philippians); Irenaeus III.3.4 names it (ANF 39889–39892).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** It survives, but not whole in Greek. ANF's notes say chapters 10–12 are "preserved only in a Latin version" and "Henceforth, to the end, we have only the Latin version" (ANF 3502, 3619–3620; D9). The sentence is true as it stands, but the card must carry this.

**S31.** "It calls its readers back from false teaching to 'the word handed down to us from the beginning.'"
- Source: Philippians 7, "forsaking the vanity of many, and their false doctrines, let us return to the word which has been handed down to us from the beginning" (ANF 3439–3443). The quoted words match RC Q21.
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED.** In the source it is "let us return", and the writer includes himself. "Calls its readers back" is a fair report of that.

**S32.** "More than two hundred years later, Jerome wrote that it was still read when the churches of Asia met."
- Source: Jerome, *Lives of Illustrious Men* 17, "a very valuable Epistle to the Philippians which is read to the present day in the meetings in Asia" (JER 39853–39855). Jerome dates his own work to "the fourteenth year of the Emperor Theodosius" (JER 38785).
- Verdict: **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - No held source counts the interval. It comes from modern chronology: Theodosius's fourteenth year is AD 392. The NPNF note at JER 38785 misprints it as "492", and that figure must not be used.
  - From the death under Marcus Aurelius (Eusebius; JER 39850–39851), or from the earlier writing of the letter, the gap is over two hundred years by any modern dating. So "more than two hundred" is safe.
  - "When the churches of Asia met" fairly renders "in the meetings in Asia".
