## 1. Listener read (once through, no going back)

The opening works. The quote lands cold, and the next sentence answers the questions it raises: who ("An old man"), where ("a stadium in Smyrna, a city in the Roman province of Asia"), when ("in the second century"), and why it matters ("offered to free him if he would curse Christ"). Then the name, his office, and the link back to Jesus. Pulled in, then oriented, within four sentences.

Time order holds. "the crowd had shouted" tells the ear we have stepped back. The farms and the arrest run in plain past. "After his answer" brings me back to the stadium without effort, because the answer was the first thing I heard.

The ending lands on what came of him: his peace became Irenaeus's argument to Victor, and his letter was "still read when the churches of Asia met" two centuries on. That is "how far it reached", told as an event, with no moral.

Where I drifted:
- **"More than two hundred years later, Jerome wrote..."** Jerome is the one person never introduced. A newcomer hears a name with no role. Not lost (he is plainly a later writer), but the standard says to say once who each person is. (m5)
- **"to prepare those still to come."** Prepare them for what? A second-language listener may not hear "future martyrs" in it. The clause still eases the round-3 "birthday" puzzle. (m6)
- **"In Rome, Polycarp and the bishop, Anicetus..."** The trip to Rome now arrives with no step into it; a half-beat to place him there. "the bishop" carries "of Rome" only by the place just named. (m7)
- **"over it"** reaches back across a sentence to "keeping Easter". It holds, but the listener is not told the quarrel was over the date. (m8)

Flat? No. Grade 6.4, but the sentences carry real content and keep the world's images ("my King, who saved me", "like a ship's sail").
