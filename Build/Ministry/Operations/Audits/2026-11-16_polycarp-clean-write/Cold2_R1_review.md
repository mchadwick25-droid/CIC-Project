## 1. Listener read (once through, no going back)

The opening works at once: a stadium, an old man, a governor, an offer of life, and the eighty-six-years answer. Present tense, then "His name was Polycarp", which is the documentary narrator's move and it lands.

Where I drifted or stumbled:

- **Paragraph 2, "This was the church in the time after the apostles, and Polycarp had known people who had seen Jesus."** On the ear, this is an aside that sounds like a label being explained. The "and" joins two things that pull against each other (after the apostles / knew people who saw Jesus), so for a second I wondered which it was. It reads as filler, not story.
- **Paragraph 2 to 3, "Then the governor put the Christians of Smyrna on trial."** "Then" follows a memory of Irenaeus as a boy. For a moment I did not know when "then" was. One time-anchor from the sheet fixes it (Q26: "He was a very old man when he died").
- **Paragraph 5, "So he was burned." followed by "could not consume his body" and the dagger.** I was lost here. The narrator says he was burned; two lines later the fire could not consume him; then a dagger killed him. A listener cannot tell what happened, or whether the narrator stands behind the wonder. (Blocking, see section 4.)
- **Paragraph 7, "Irenaeus also used Polycarp's example to keep a peace."** Slightly odd English ("keep a peace"), and abstract before the story it introduces. Then "could not agree about the keeping of Easter": a newcomer is left asking, disagree about what? The sheet gives the reason (Q29, M9) and it is the best part: Polycarp kept it as he had kept it with John.

Everywhere else I followed easily. Is it told by someone who knows the record? Yes. The arrest scene (food for the police, the two-hour prayer, "such a godly old man") shows the person, not a summary of him. The impact third is told as events, with Irenaeus as a person doing things, not a list of uses.

**Grade 6.4: plain or flat?** Plain, not flat. Sentence length varies (longest 30, 18% short), the images are the record's own (the stadium, the sail of flame, the shared table with the arresting men), and the quotes carry the voice. The only flat lines are the three above: "This was the church in the time after the apostles", "So he was burned", "to keep a peace". The low score is earned by short sentences and plain words, not by thinning the thought. Pass by ear.

No AI tells beyond the paragraph 2 aside, which has the cadence of an inserted explanation.

## 2. The three parts

- **Opening.** Pulls in: yes. Century ("In the second century", S12 supported wording), place ("Smyrna, a city in the Roman province of Asia", S1, S2): yes, within sentences 4–5, told as story. **Tradition: only half met.** The census name (S3, "Post-Apostolic Household-Church Christianity") cannot be spoken; the writer glossed its first half as a period ("the time after the apostles") and dropped "Household-Church" entirely, because nothing on the sheet says what that half means. This is a sheet/page failure more than a writer failure (section 6).
- **Story.** Told well. The order is clear: the cry for him, the farm, the arrest, the stadium, the fire, the dagger, the account sent out. Good choices of what to leave out (Quintus, the carriage, the voice, the pillow vision, the dove, the hostile claims about the Jews of Smyrna).
- **Impact.** Does a listener now know:
  - *What he left the church:* his witness to the apostles' teaching through Irenaeus (I9), the precedent of peace (I11), his letter (I18), his church's account of his death (I5). Yes.
  - *What he defended:* all three DEFENDED lines (I9, I11, I18) are used, each with its carrying quote. Yes.
  - *How far it reached:* shown for the letter (Jerome, I15) and the account (sent on, I5) and the peace (used against Victor a generation later). For rank 1 (I9) the reach is not shown: only "Irenaeus called on Polycarp". The sheet gives "all the Asiatic Churches testify" (I9) as the cheapest reach line if a few words are free. Minor.
  - *Chosen by weight:* selection follows the ranking (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8; rank 6 left out with a sound reason). Space does not: rank 3 (I11) gets five sentences, rank 1 (I9) two. I11 needs a story to make sense, so this is defensible; the fix below (cut sentence 23) narrows the gap.
  - Proportion: impact is paragraphs 6–8 plus sentence 20, about 210 of 515 words (40%). Slightly over the "about a third"; acceptable.
- **Ending.** On what came of him, as an event: the letter still read in Asia more than two hundred years later, named to Jerome as a late witness. No moral, no summing up. Good.

## 6. The page

**Did the two replaced lines work cold?**

- **The Shape's impact line** ("what they left the church and what they defended, then how far it reached. Weight is what later witnesses carried on and used..."): worked. The writer used every DEFENDED line with its carrying quote, kept the ranked order for selection, and ended on reach as an event. One gap: the writer read "weight" as what to include, not how much space to give it (rank 3 got the most room). Defensible here because I11 needs a story; not worth a page change on one case.
- **The re-ranked sheet** (rank lines with "carried on" reasons and carrying quotes): worked. The writer's section (d) follows the ranks and gives a reason for each line left out. That is what the re-rank was for.

**Line still failing: the tradition.** The Shape asks for "the place and the tradition" in the first few sentences. The sheet gives only the census name (S3), which cannot be said aloud. Both cold writers had to gloss it, and this one glossed half of it into a period ("the time after the apostles") that sits awkwardly beside the sheet's own S5, and dropped "Household-Church" because nothing on the sheet explains it. The century already has a solved pattern: S12 gives "plain ways to say it that the sheet supports". The tradition needs the same. Two replacements:

1. **The Shape, second sentence. Replace** "Within the first few sentences, set the context: the century or the year, the place and the tradition, told like a story, not given as a list." **with:** "Within the first few sentences, set the context: the century or the year, the place, and the tradition in the plain words the sheet gives for saying it aloud, told like a story, not given as a list."
2. **The three hands, researcher sentence. Replace** "The researcher builds the source sheet, with headings in the record's own words:" **with:** "The researcher builds the source sheet, with headings in the record's own words: the setting, with plain spoken words for the century and the tradition and the lines that support them;" (the rest of the sentence unchanged).

The census name then stays on the card, and the writer is never left to invent a gloss.
