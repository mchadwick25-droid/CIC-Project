# Polycarp of Smyrna, clean write: review round 1

Reviewer, 2026-11-16. Judged against `Standard_draft.md`, `Writer_Sheet.md` (with `Source_Sheet.md` and `Renderings_Compared.md` behind it), and the source lines where a point needed checking. No other brief was opened.

**Verdict: REVISE.** One blocking finding, about the story's shape. The trace is clean: nothing is invented, every quote matches its final reading, and the disputes are handled well. The problem is that the story stops at the burial and never tells what came of Polycarp. The listener is never told why he matters.

## 1. Listener read

**Opening.** It opens well on where (Smyrna, the Roman province of Asia) and on whose church. The when is soft ("in the years just after the apostles"), and the sheet gives the writer little more to work with. A radio listener will hear "Asia" as the continent unless something stops them. The sheet says "the Roman province of Asia", and the brief keeps that, which is right.

**Flow.** Paragraphs 3 to 6 (persecution, the farm, the arrest, the trial) move well. Each sentence leads to the next, and reasons sit in the same sentence as the acts ("Because many friends wished it, he withdrew..."). Paragraphs 1 and 2 are two separate scenes with no link to each other or to what follows. The Rome visit floats: it is told, but nothing in the story says why it matters (see B1). The jump "Then persecution reached Smyrna" works as a scene change.

**Voice.** It sounds like a person telling it, not like generated text. There is no hedging filler and no assistant cadence. The last two sentences turn into a report ("laid them in a fitting place, and planned to gather...", "It sent its account to another church..."), but they are plain rather than generic.

**Strongest sentences.**
- "He had food and drink set before them, asked for an hour of prayer, and prayed for two, until many regretted coming out against such a godly old man." It is concrete, it moves, and it shows the man.
- The eighty-six years exchange, quoted exactly as the final reading gives it, with the teller left out where no teller is needed.

**Weakest sentences.**
- "Irenaeus, who heard him as a boy, wrote that apostles had instructed Polycarp, and he could still describe the place where Polycarp used to sit and tell of his time with John and the others who had seen the Lord." At 46 words it carries two claims from two different works. "He" and "his" each have two possible people to point to. A listener will lose track of who is describing whom.
- "Threatened with beasts and then with fire, he would not give way, so the crowd built a pyre." This is the climax of the trial, and it is told as a summary. The record gives his answer about fire (Q11), and here it is summed up instead of shown.

**The third part (what came of him).** It is missing. The standard asks for impact on the church, what he saw differently, and what he defended. The brief gives only the bones, the yearly gathering and the sending of the account (I3, I5), and even those are told as closing events, not as impact. Section 4 of the sheet holds what a listener needs:
- **I11:** his peace with Anicetus became the example Irenaeus used to stop Victor of Rome from cutting off the churches of Asia over the Easter date. This is the reason the Rome story is worth telling.
- **I8, I9:** he taught Irenaeus, and Irenaeus held him up as a living witness to what the apostles taught, against Valentinus and Marcion.
- **I18:** what he defended, in his own letter: that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, the witness of the cross, the resurrection and the judgment, and "the word handed down to us from the beginning".
- **I7:** he gathered Ignatius's letters and sent them on. This also means Ignatius (M1–M4) is entirely absent from a story about a man whose surviving writing is bound up with him.
- **I2, I4:** the church held up his death as a model martyrdom, one that did not hand itself over, unlike Quintus, and as one "all desire to imitate".

**The ending.** The last sentence (the account sent on) is a real event, not a summing-up or moral, so the ending rule is kept. But it is the end of the death narrative, not the end of the story the standard asks for.

## 2. Trace

Every sentence traces to the sheet. The writer's trace table is accurate. Quoted wording matches the final readings in every case: "Away with the atheists" (M16/Q7), "Let the will of God be done" (Q4) and the whole of Q8. The dove and the other omitted wonders are left out, as D3 and the warnings allow. The fire wonder is told with its teller. "Taught by apostles" is told as Irenaeus's claim, and "John" is kept in the source's own words ("John and the others who had seen the Lord"), with no "apostle" added, which is right under D1 and N3. The purpose of the Rome trip is not stated, which is right under D14. The Eucharist clause, which is disputed (Q29), is left out instead of being told as settled.

### Blocking

**B1. The third part of the shape is absent, so the story misleads by what it leaves out.** This is not "could be stronger". The standard requires the story to say what came of the person, and section 4 of the sheet gives a full record of it. As written, a listener learns how Polycarp died but not why the church remembers him. The Rome episode is set up and left without its consequence (I11), so it reads as a pleasant aside. To fix it, add a closing movement drawn from I11, I8/I9, I18 and I7 (and I2 if room allows). Pay for it by trimming paragraphs 1 and 4 so the brief stays near two minutes.

### Minor

- **M1. Betrayal (sentence 8).** "until a young slave of his household betrayed him under torture" fuses two statements. The record says the searchers seized two young slaves at a second farm, one confessed under torture, and "those that betrayed him were of his own household" (M20; ANF 3878–3886, checked). "Until" also places the betrayal at the first farm. The meaning mostly survives, but a more exact version is short: "He moved to another farm. The searchers seized two young slaves of his household, and one gave him away under torture."
- **M2. "Anniversary" (sentence 16).** This is a paraphrase, not a quote, and M34 does give "anniversary", so it is not wrong. But the final reading of Q18 deliberately chose "the birthday of his martyrdom". That phrase is the world's own idea (death as birth), and "anniversary" flattens it. Use "birthday of his martyrdom".
- **M3. The pyre (sentence 14).** In "he would not give way, so the crowd built a pyre", the causal link skips a step. The crowd asked for a lion, Philip refused because the beast shows were over, and so the crowd cried for burning (M26). As written, the brief implies his refusal alone sent the crowd to the woodpile.
- **M4. The wonder's witness (sentence 15).** "The church in Smyrna, which recorded his death, says..." names the teller, as the standard asks. It drops that the church claims its own members saw it ("we saw a wonder, we to whom it was given to see", Q15). That claim is exactly what tells the listener how far to trust the account. Something like "says its own people saw the flames curve around him..." would carry it.
- **M5. "Away with the atheists!" (sentence 7).** A listener will not know why Christians are called atheists. The sheet gives the reason (Q12: "the destroyer of our gods... teaches many not to sacrifice"). The trial's reversal, where Polycarp points at the crowd and says the same words (Q7), is also dropped, so the cry never pays off.
- **M6. "Many friends" (sentence 8).** The source says "the wish of many". "Friends" is a small addition. It is harmless, but it is not on the sheet.
- **M7. "In the years just after the apostles" (sentence 1).** The next sentence says apostles taught him, so his life overlapped theirs. "In the generation the apostles taught" would fit the record better.
- **M8. "Friday" and "governor".** Both are supported. M21 gives "Friday evening" for "the day of the preparation" (ANF's own note 431 says "That is, on Friday"), and M33 uses "governor". No finding. "Friday" earns little on its own, though, because the story never says he died on the Saturday, the "great Sabbath". Keep both days or cut Friday.

## 4. The standard itself

The writer followed the standard faithfully. The main fault traces back to it.

1. **"End when the story ends, with no moral, no summing up and no line picked because it sounds good."** This line pulls against "Then what came of it". The writer read "the story" as the death narrative and ended at the burial, and the impact was lost. Replace with: *"End on the last thing that came of them, told as an event (who carried their witness on, and what was done with it), with no moral, no summing up and no line picked because it sounds good. The death or the last scene is not the end if the record shows what came after."*
2. **"Keep the names the record uses, and say once who each person is."** The writer could not say who Irenaeus became, because the sheet never says. Add to the researcher's duties in "The three hands": *"a one-line 'who' for every person the story may name, from the record."*
3. **Wording when paraphrasing.** "Use plain modern words, and tell old English and other languages in our own modern English" does not say which words to use when retelling a quoted passage without quote marks. "Anniversary" (the ANF word) came in that way. Add: *"When you retell a quoted passage in your own words, keep the final reading's key words, not the printed translation's."*

Top change: number 1.
