## (c) What is uncertain (for the card)

- **The century and the year.** No ancient source gives a year. They place him by rulers and bishops: Rome under Anicetus, death under Marcus Aurelius (Eusebius, Jerome). "Second century" comes from the modern standard dating of those reigns (S12, D2, C13).
- **The eighty-six years.** The text does not say whether this is his age or his years as a Christian (D4).
- **The apostles and John.** That he knew John and others who had seen the Lord rests on Irenaeus, who heard him as a boy and wrote many years later. The sources do not say more about which John (S5, M13, N3). Who made him bishop is told differently by Irenaeus, Tertullian, Jerome and Eusebius; the story leaves it out (D1).
- **The wonders at the fire.** The sail of flame, and the body that would not burn, are the Smyrna church's own report. No eyewitness is named (N10). Our text also has a dove coming from the wound; the Greek edition brackets it and Eusebius omits it, so the story leaves it out (D3). The other wonders (the vision of the pillow, the voice, the fragrance) are also left out.
- **The Jews of Smyrna.** The church's letter makes hostile claims about them. No other source tells that side, and the story does not repeat them (N12).
- **The Rome visit.** Irenaeus does not say Easter was the reason for the trip, and says they differed on other points too (D14). His words about Anicetus giving Polycarp the Eucharist may mean that Polycarp presided or only that he took part; the story leaves that clause out (Q29).
- **The letter.** It is the only letter of his the Library holds, though Irenaeus says he wrote others (N4). Chapter 7 is rendered from one English translation only (Q21). The record does not say what the Philippians did with it (N5).
- **Who received the account.** It was addressed to Philomelium; Eusebius says Pontus (D15). The messenger's name is uncertain (D6).

## (d) Impact lines (section 4)

**Used**

- I9 (rank 1, defended): Irenaeus's witness against false teachers, with its carrying quote. Sentences 21–22.
- I8 (rank 2): Irenaeus heard him as a boy. Sentence 7. Used as the listener's reason to trust Irenaeus. The "not on paper" phrase was left out for length.
- I11 (rank 3, defended): the peace with Anicetus, used to restrain Victor. Sentences 23–27.
- I5 (rank 4): the account sent on. Sentence 20. Eusebius's copying is left for the card.
- I18 (rank 5, defended): his own words on the word handed down. Sentences 28–30.
- I15 (rank 8): the letter still read in Asia in Jerome's day. Sentence 31. Chosen as the ending because it is an event that shows how far rank 5 reached.

**Left out**

- I2 (rank 6): the martyrdom "in keeping with the gospel", set against Quintus. It needs the Quintus story to make sense, and the length would not carry it.
- I13 (rank 7): Irenaeus's recommendation of the letter. It supports rank 5, which I15 already shows reaching further.
- I12 (rank 9), I17 (rank 10): single later witnesses repeating uses already told. I17 also touches the dispute over who made him bishop (D1).
- I10 (rank 11): the use against Florinus. A narrower case of rank 1.
- I7 (rank 12): sending on Ignatius's letters. No later witness says what came of it, and it would need Ignatius introduced.
- I4 (rank 13), I3 (rank 14), I1 (rank 15): the church's own claims at the time, with nothing later added on the sheet.
- I19 (rank 16): his counsel on church life. No later witness takes it up.
- I6, I14, I16: card only.
