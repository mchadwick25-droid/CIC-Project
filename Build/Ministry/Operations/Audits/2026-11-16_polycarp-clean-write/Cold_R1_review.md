# Polycarp of Smyrna, cold write: review round 1

Brief: `Polycarp_cold.brief.txt` (with `Polycarp_cold.references.md`). Judged against `Standard_draft.md` and `Writer_Sheet.md`, with `Source_Sheet.md` and `Renderings_Compared.md` for lookups. No other draft or review in this folder was opened.

**Verdict: REVISE.** Three blocking findings, eight minor.

---

## 1. Listener read (once through, no going back)

The opening works. "Swear, and I will set you free. Curse Christ." is a hard, clean start, and the answer about eighty-six years lands before the listener knows who is speaking. That is the standard's "pull in" done well.

I drifted twice.

- **Paragraph 2 into 3.** "It was the second century, in the church that came after the apostles." I could not hear what this phrase was. A place? A period? A group? The next sentence says he "had been taught by apostles", so the church "after the apostles" seems to contradict it. Then: "Now that church was under attack." I took "that church" to mean the whole church of that age. Only the next sentence pulled me back to Smyrna.
- **Paragraph 6 into 7.** We have just buried him and sent his letter out. Then: "His witness reached further still. Polycarp had once visited Rome..." For a second I thought this happened after his death. The only marker that it is earlier is "had once", a pluperfect that a second-language listener easily misses at speaking pace. I re-found my place at "Years later... Victor".

Everywhere else I stayed with it. The arrest (food set before the men who came for him) and the fire (the sail) are the two moments that stay with a listener, and both are told plainly.

**Story or report?** For paragraphs 1 and 3 to 6, a story told by someone who knows the record. Paragraph 2 slips into a report: three facts in a row, then a name tag ("according to Irenaeus") that is needed but sits at the end of a long sentence. Paragraph 7 is half story, half summary, and its last line repeats "parted in peace" from three sentences earlier. Heard aloud, the echo sounds like a moral being pressed home, though the words are factual.

**Is grade 5.9 plain or flat?** Mostly plain, not flat. The sentences are short (14.3 words) but they vary, and the voice is warm. The one choppy stretch is paragraph 3 and the start of paragraph 4: "Now that church was under attack. ... The police found him on a Friday evening. He could have escaped, but he did not want to." With 0.7 joining words per 100, the links between acts are often missing: why he left the city, how the police found him, why the men were sorry. The listener gets the acts without the thread. This is a reasons problem (see M1 to M3 below), not a score problem. No rewrite for score is needed.

## 2. The three parts

**Opening, then context.** Yes, the opening pulls in. Century, place and tradition all arrive in paragraph 2: "the second century", "Smyrna, a city in the Roman province of Asia", and a tradition. But the tradition is given as a phrase no listener can hold (B2).

**The story.** Told well. The choices are good: the refusal to flee, the hospitality, the two threats, the prayer, the sail, the dagger. Wonders are attributed ("The Christians of Smyrna say"). The doubtful dove is left out. The 86 years are quoted without being turned into an age. What weakens it is reasons dropped in compression (M1 to M3).

**What came of him.** Paragraphs 6 and 7 are 124 of 415 words, about 30%. By length, the third is there. By weight, it is not. Of those 124 words, 43 are Smyrna's own acts after his death (bones, letter). The rest is one strand: the Easter peace with Anicetus, used later by Irenaeus with Victor. A listener finishes without knowing:

- what Polycarp **left** the church (his letter, still read in Asia in Jerome's day, I13, I15; Ignatius's letters gathered and sent on, I7);
- what he **defended** (Christ come in the flesh, the cross, resurrection and judgment, "the word handed down to us from the beginning", I18, Q21; the "firstborn of Satan" answer to Marcion, M11, Q27);
- what his **legacy** was in the record's own use of him: Irenaeus's living link to the apostles, the witness he set against Valentinus and Marcion (I8, I9, M10).

The writer's own trace says the Easter strand was picked as "the one with a clear event and a peaceful end", and calls the loss of I18 "the main loss". That is choosing by tellability, not by weight (B1).

**Does it end on what came of him?** Yes, as an event: Irenaeus writes to Victor and names Polycarp. That is the right kind of ending. The repeated "parted in peace" weakens it (M7).

## 5. Findings

### Blocking

**B1. What came of him is there by length but not by weight. A listener does not learn what Polycarp left the church or what he defended.**
The third is one strand (the Easter peace and its later use) plus Smyrna's burial. The record's heaviest lines are untouched: I18 and Q21 (what he defended, in his own words), I9 and I8 (Irenaeus's witness to the apostles' teaching, set against Valentinus and Marcion), I13 and I15 (his letter, still read in Asia). Even inside the strand used, the reason for Polycarp's Easter custom ("as he had kept it with John the disciple of our Lord and the other apostles", Q29) is cut, and that reason is the one thing in it that shows what he held to.
*Fix:* Give the third two weighted beats and then the ending. (a) What he defended, briefly in his own words from Q21 ("Let us return to the word handed down to us from the beginning"), or through Irenaeus's use of him (I9). (b) The Easter peace, with the reason restored in the same sentence. (c) End on Irenaeus and Victor, as now. Pay for it by cutting from the story part (e.g. sentence 17 can fold into 18; sentence 23 can go to the card, as the writer offered).

**B2. "In the church that came after the apostles" misleads, and "Now that church was under attack" widens the persecution.**
The phrase is a loose gloss of S3's census name. Heard once, it seems to contradict the next sentence ("taught by apostles"). It then becomes the antecedent of "that church", so the attack sounds church-wide. The sheet gives persecution in Smyrna (S7), with Eusebius's "Asia" as a later witness (S11).
*Fix:* Set the tradition in words a listener can hold and that the sheet supports, e.g. the generation after the apostles, still meeting as household churches led by a bishop with presbyters (S3, S4). Then make the attack local: "In Smyrna the Christians were under attack."

**B3. "His witness reached further still" is not on the sheet, and it sends the listener the wrong way in time.**
The line is chosen for its sound, which the standard rules out. It also implies that the Rome visit came after his death. The listener drifts (section 1).
*Fix:* Remove it. Mark the time shift plainly at the head of the Rome sentence, e.g. "Years before, while he was still living, Polycarp had visited Rome."

### Minor

**M1.** Sentence 16: the reason the men were sorry (they heard him pray, full of grace, for two hours, Q4) is cut. Add it in the same sentence, or cut sentences 15–16 together if B1 needs the room.

**M2.** Sentence 19: "So" skips the crowd's demand for fire (M26). Either "The crowd shouted for him to be burned" or drop "So".

**M3.** Sentence 22: give the sheet's reason for the yearly gathering in the same sentence (Q18: to remember those who had died and to prepare those to come). "The birthday of his martyrdom" puzzles a newcomer, and the sheet's reason answers it.

**M4.** Sentence 11: "his friends" is the sheet's heading, not its quote ("the wish of many"). Say "many asked him". Restoring "At first he wanted to stay" also explains sentence 13: he left the city because others asked, not from fear.

**M5.** Sentence 8 is the longest in paragraph 2 and ends on its attribution. Read aloud, "according to Irenaeus, who heard him teach as a boy" arrives after the claim it qualifies. Move the speaker first: "Irenaeus, who heard him teach as a boy, says..."

**M6.** Sentence 25: "the bishop, Anicetus" — say once that he was the bishop of Rome. It is implied, but sentence 27's "another bishop of Rome" relies on it.

**M7.** Sentences 26 and 29 both end "parted in peace". The second sounds like a moral. Let the ending say what Irenaeus named, without repeating the earlier line.

**M8.** Length: 415 words, about three minutes against "about two". Bring it to roughly 300–320 after the B1 fix.

## 6. The page: what failed cold, and the one replacement

The writer followed the page closely. The opening, the attributions, the dispute handling, the quote discipline and the card notes are all what the page asks for, without having seen any earlier round. So most of the page works cold. One line did not.

**The line that failed:** "Then what came of it, given real weight, about a third of the story: their impact on the church, what they saw differently, what they defended, chosen by weight from the record."

The writer met "about a third" exactly, by word count. But they read "their impact... what they saw differently, what they defended" as a menu, and took one item from it. "Chosen by weight" gave them no test for weight, so they used their own: a clear event with a peaceful end. Their trace even names I18 as "the main loss", so they saw the gap and the page let them make the trade.

**How the sheet contributed:** Section 4 gives 19 impact lines in one flat list. Weighty legacy (I9, I18) sits beside small acts and card points (I6 copy chain, I14, I16). Nothing on the sheet marks which lines later witnesses actually carried and used. The writer also judged I18 to need "a second story line", because it lives in the letter to the Philippians, which the story does not tell. The sheet does not show that one quoted line from Q21 can carry it alone. The sheet made the menu reading easy. The page is what allowed it.

**Single replacement (for the standard's shape line):**

> Then what came of it, about a third of the story. It must tell what they left the church and what they defended, and then how far it reached. Weight is what later witnesses carried and used, not what is easiest to tell.

A second, smaller change belongs in "The three hands", for the researcher, not the writer: "the impact as the record gives it" should become "the impact as the record gives it, heaviest first, with what they defended marked". That fixes the sheet's flat list at its source. If only one line may change, change the shape line: it is the one the writer reads as instructions.
