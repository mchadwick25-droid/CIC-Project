# Rendering 2 — Polycarp quotes (blind second rendering)

Rendered from Gebhardt 1902 Greek where given; otherwise from the printed English (ANF 1885 / NPNF 1890).

**Q1. Martyrdom ch. 1: he waited to be handed over**
- Rendering 2: He waited to be handed over, as the Lord had been. He did this so that we too might become people who copy him. We should look not only to our own good, but also to the good of those around us.
- Note: "people who copy him" renders μιμηταί ("imitators"); ANF's "followers" is weaker than the Greek. "Those around us" is τοὺς πέλας, "those near us", the biblical "neighbours".

**Q2. Ch. 4: against volunteering**
- Rendering 2: So, brothers and sisters, we do not praise those who come forward and hand themselves over. The gospel does not teach us to do this.
- Note: ἀδελφοί is the standard address to a mixed community. "Brothers and sisters" avoids "brethren" and is the usual modern sense. τοὺς προσιόντας ἑαυτοῖς literally means "those who come forward of themselves". ANF's "[to suffering]" is the translator's addition.

**Q3. Ch. 5: the vision**
- Rendering 2: While he was praying, three days before his arrest, he fell into a trance. He saw his pillow being burned up by fire. He turned and said to those with him, "I must be burned alive."
- Note: Gebhardt's Greek has no word for ANF's "prophetically", which is not rendered. ANF's "behold" is also absent from the Greek. ἐν ὀπτασίᾳ γέγονεν means "he came to be in a vision". "Fell into a trance" is a fair reading, though "saw a vision" is also possible.

**Q4. Ch. 7: arrest**
- Rendering 2 (escape): From there, too, he could have gone away to another farm. But he did not want to. He said, "Let the will of God be done."
- Rendering 2 (the onlookers): Those present were amazed at his age and his calm steadiness. They wondered why there had been such a hurry to arrest an old man like this.
- Rendering 2 (food and prayer, from GEB 684–687): At once, at that very hour, he ordered food and drink to be set before them, as much as they wanted. He asked them to give him an hour to pray in peace.
- Rendering 2 (the prayer): He stood and prayed, full of the grace of God. For two hours he could not stop speaking. Those who heard him were amazed. Many of them regretted that they had come out against such a godly old man.
- Note: χωρίον is a country place or farm, more specific than ANF's "place". In the onlookers' line the Greek is indirect ("wondering whether there was such haste"), not a direct question. ANF turns it into a speech, and its "venerable" has no Greek word: πρεσβύτην ἄνδρα means just "old man". σπουδή can mean "haste" or "effort"; ANF's own footnote gives "diligence". In the prayer, μὴ δύνασθαι σιγῆσαι means "he could not fall silent". θεοπρεπῆ πρεσβύτην is literally "an old man fitting for God"; "godly" is plain and does not mislead. ANF's "venerable" again has no Greek word. ἀδεῶς means "without fear" or "freely"; "in peace" covers both senses. The ANF narrative line has no Greek on the sheet; I rendered it from GEB 684–687, which matches ANF in substance.

**Q5. Ch. 8: in the carriage**
- Rendering 2: "What harm is there in saying 'Caesar is Lord' and offering sacrifice, along with the rest of it, and so saving your life?"
- Rendering 2 (reply): "I am not going to do what you advise."
- Note: Κύριος Καῖσαρ can be read as "Lord Caesar" (an address) or "Caesar is Lord" (a statement). Both readings are defensible. The statement fits the confession formula better. τὰ τούτοις ἀκόλουθα means "the things that go with these". διασῴζεσθαι means "be kept safe", and in context this means saving his life. In GEB the reply is introduced by "At first he did not answer them, but when they kept on he said". That clause is outside the quote.

**Q6. Ch. 9: the voice**
- Rendering 2: A voice came from heaven: "Be strong, Polycarp, and be brave." No one saw who had spoken. But those of our people who were there heard the voice.
- Note: The Greek includes "a voice came from heaven", but the ANF line as quoted begins after it. Ἴσχυε ... ἀνδρίζου is "be strong ... act like a man", which echoes Joshua 1. "Be brave" keeps the sense without the dated gendered phrase. If the scriptural echo matters, "be strong and courageous" is the familiar Bible wording.

**Q7. Ch. 9: "Away with the atheists"**
- Rendering 2: "Respect your age." ... "Swear by the Fortune of Caesar. Change your mind. Say, 'Away with the atheists.'" Polycarp looked with a grave face at the whole crowd of lawless pagans in the stadium. He waved his hand at them. Then he groaned, looked up to heaven, and said, "Away with the atheists."
- Note: "Atheists" here means people who reject the gods, which is how pagans saw Christians. It does not mean modern unbelief. A short gloss may be needed where the quote is used. The Fortune (τύχη) of Caesar was the emperor's guardian spirit, sworn by as a loyalty oath. μετανόησον is "repent" in the sense of "change your mind". ἀνόμων ἐθνῶν means "lawless gentiles"; "pagans" is the plain modern equivalent, but "lawless" is harsh and belongs to the text's own voice. ANF's "wicked heathen" overstates ἀνόμων a little.

**Q8. Ch. 9: eighty-six years**
- Rendering 2: The proconsul pressed him and said, "Swear, and I will let you go. Curse Christ." Polycarp said, "For eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my King, who saved me?"
- Note: τὸν σώσαντά με is "the one who saved me", a past act, not ANF's title "my Saviour". It is unclear whether the eighty-six years count from birth or from baptism. λοιδόρησον means "revile" or "curse"; ANF's "reproach" is weaker.

**Q9. Ch. 10: "I am a Christian"**
- Rendering 2: "You are wasting your effort if you expect me to swear by the Fortune of Caesar, as you put it. You pretend not to know who I am. So listen, and I will tell you plainly: I am a Christian. If you want to learn what Christianity teaches, give me a day and hear me."
- Note: εἰ κενοδοξεῖς ἵνα ὀμόσω is hard Greek. The likeliest sense is "if you vainly imagine that I will swear". It can also mean "if you take empty pride in making me swear". μετὰ παρρησίας is "with boldness" or "openly". τὸν τοῦ Χριστιανισμοῦ λόγον means "the account (or teaching) of Christianity".

**Q10. Ch. 10: whom he would answer**
- Rendering 2: The proconsul said, "Convince the people." Polycarp said, "You, I would have thought worth an answer. We have been taught to give fitting honour to rulers and authorities set in place by God, so far as it does us no harm. But I do not consider those people worth a defence from me."
- Note: Σὲ μὲν κἂν λόγου ἠξίωσα is a conditional: "you I would have judged worth a word". ANF's "I have thought it right to offer an account" smooths this. τὴν μὴ βλάπτουσαν ἡμᾶς ("which does not harm us") limits the honour. It probably means honour that does not compromise faith.

**Q11. Ch. 11: the beasts and the fire**
- Rendering 2 (beasts): "I have wild beasts. I will throw you to them unless you change your mind." He said, "Call them. For us, changing from better to worse is impossible. But it is good to change from what is wrong to what is right."
- Rendering 2 (fire): "If you scorn the beasts, I will have you burned up by fire, unless you change your mind." Polycarp said, "You threaten me with a fire that burns for an hour and soon goes out. You do not know about the fire of the coming judgment and eternal punishment, kept for the ungodly. But why are you waiting? Do what you want."
- Note: The Greek line about beasts plays on μετάνοια, "change of mind" or "repentance". ἀμετάθετος γὰρ ἡμῖν ἡ ... μετάνοια means "for us a change from better to worse is not possible". ANF's "we are not accustomed to repent" softens this. ἀπὸ τῶν χαλεπῶν literally means "from harsh or cruel things"; "what is wrong" is the plain contrast with τὰ δίκαια. φέρε ὃ βούλει is literally "bring what you wish".

**Q12. Ch. 12: the crowd**
- Rendering 2: "This is the teacher of Asia, the father of the Christians. He is the destroyer of our gods. He teaches many people not to sacrifice and not to worship."
- Note: "Asia" here means the Roman province in western Turkey, not the continent. The Greek's last clause has no object. ANF's "the gods" is supplied.

**Q13. Ch. 13: undressing; the nails**
- Rendering 2 (undressing): He took off all his clothes and loosened his belt. He also tried to take off his own shoes. He had not done this before, because each of the faithful was always eager to be the first to touch his body. Even before his martyrdom, he had been honoured in every way for his good life.
- Rendering 2 (the nails): "Leave me as I am. The one who lets me endure the fire will also let me stay on the pyre without flinching, without the safety of your nails."
- Note: χρωτός is "skin" or "body". "Body" avoids an odd sound in modern English. ἐν παντί ... ἐκεκόσμητο means "he had been adorned in every way". "Honoured" is one reading. "His character was in every way well ordered" is another. πολιτεία means "way of life" or "conduct". ἄσκυλτον means "unmoved" or "without struggling". The Greek has "the one who gave me to endure" (ὁ δούς ... δώσει): God gives the endurance.

**Q14. Ch. 14: the prayer (core lines)**
- Rendering 2 (the ram): He put his hands behind him and was bound. He was like a choice ram from a great flock, taken for an offering. He was a whole burnt offering, made ready and acceptable to God.
- Rendering 2 (thanksgiving): "Lord God Almighty, Father of your beloved and blessed child Jesus Christ. Through him we have come to know you. You are the God of angels and powers and all creation. You are the God of the whole race of the righteous who live in your presence. I bless you that you have judged me worthy of this day and this hour. You have let me take a share among your martyrs, in the cup of your Christ. This share leads to the resurrection of eternal life, of soul and body, in the incorruption of the Holy Spirit."
- Rendering 2 (acceptance): "Among them may I be welcomed before you today, as a rich and acceptable sacrifice. You prepared this beforehand and showed it to me beforehand, and now you have fulfilled it. You are the God who does not lie, the true God."
- Rendering 2 (doxology): "For this reason, and for all things, I praise you, I bless you, I glorify you. I do this through the eternal and heavenly high priest Jesus Christ, your beloved child. Through him be glory to you, with him and the Holy Spirit, now and for the ages to come. Amen."
- Note: παῖς can mean "child" or "servant"; the Servant language of Isaiah and Acts 3–4 sits behind it. ANF's "Son" narrows it. εὐλογῶ σε means "I bless you", not ANF's "I give thanks". In the doxology the Greek reads "through the ... high priest Jesus Christ". ANF has "along with ... Jesus Christ" and drops "high priest". This is a real gap between ANF and Gebhardt's text, and the rendering follows the Greek. The ram line uses προσδεθείς (GEB 834), which is inside the cited range. "Incorruption" (ἀφθαρσία) means freedom from decay; it may need a gloss.

**Q15. Ch. 15: the wonder in the fire**
- Rendering 2: When he had offered up the "Amen" and finished his prayer, the men in charge of the fire lit it. A great flame blazed up, and we saw a wonder, we who were allowed to see it. We were kept safe so that we could tell the others what happened. The fire took the shape of a vaulted room, like a ship's sail filled by the wind. It walled in the martyr's body all around. He was in the middle of it, not like flesh burning, but like bread baking. Or he was like gold and silver being refined in a furnace. We also caught a fragrance as strong as burning incense, or some other precious spice.
- Note: καμάρα is a vault or arched room; ANF's "arch" is close. περιετείχισεν means "walled around". ANF's "in great fury" and "[coming from the pile]" have no Greek words. οἱ ἐτηρήθημεν is "we who were kept"; it may mean kept alive or kept for this task. λιβανωτοῦ πνέοντος is literally "frankincense breathing out".

**Q16. Ch. 16: the dagger; what the church called him**
- Rendering 2 (dagger): At last the lawless men saw that his body could not be burned up by the fire. They ordered an executioner to go up to him and drive in a dagger. When he did this, a dove came out, and so much blood that it put out the fire.
- Rendering 2 (what he was): This most remarkable Polycarp was one of them. In our own times he was a teacher in the way of the apostles and the prophets. He was bishop of the universal church in Smyrna. Every word that came from his mouth has come true, or will come true.
- Note: Gebhardt brackets "a dove and" (περιστερὰ καί). Most editors treat it as a later addition to the text, and Eusebius' quotation of this chapter lacks it. Use the dove only with that flag. κομφέκτωρ is a Latin loanword for the man who finished off victims in the arena. καθολικῆς ἐκκλησίας is rendered "universal church" because "Catholic" now suggests the later Roman Catholic Church, which misleads. If "catholic" is kept, gloss it. Gebhardt also brackets μάρτυς ("martyr") after "most remarkable". "Teacher in the way of the apostles and the prophets" renders ἀποστολικὸς καὶ προφητικός. A shorter alternative is "an apostolic and prophetic teacher".

**Q17. Ch. 17: worship and love**
- Rendering 2 (the fear): "Otherwise," he said, "they will abandon the one who was crucified and start to worship this man."
- Rendering 2 (worship and love): We worship Christ because he is the Son of God. But we love the martyrs, as is right, as disciples and imitators of the Lord. We love them for their unmatched devotion to their own King and Teacher. May we also become their companions and fellow disciples.
- Note: The subject of φησίν ("he said") is unclear. The surrounding Greek suggests that people were urging Nicetes to say this, rather than Nicetes speaking for himself. ANF's "(Nicetes)" is an interpretation. The Greek uses two different verbs: σέβεσθαι (to revere or worship) for the feared cult, and προσκυνοῦμεν (we worship) for Christ. The key distinction in the passage is between worship (Christ) and love (the martyrs). εὐνοίας ἀνυπερβλήτου is "devotion that cannot be surpassed".

**Q18. Ch. 18: the bones and the yearly gathering**
- Rendering 2: So afterwards we gathered up his bones. They are more precious than costly stones and more tested than gold. We laid them in a fitting place. There, as far as we are able, the Lord will let us gather in gladness and joy. We will celebrate the birthday of his martyrdom. It will remember those who have already finished the contest. It will also train and prepare those who are still to come.
- Note: ἡμέραν γενέθλιον is "birthday", meaning the martyr's day of death seen as a birth into life. ANF's "anniversary" loses this. προηθληκότων is "those who have already competed", an athletic image; ANF's "finished their course" changes the sport. δοκιμώτερα means "more proven" or "more refined", as metal is tested. ANF's "purified" is close. ANF's "walk in their steps" has no Greek words: τῶν μελλόντων is simply "those to come".

**Q19. Ch. 19: how he was remembered**
- Rendering 2: Even the pagans talk about him everywhere. He was not only a famous teacher, but also an outstanding martyr. Everyone wants to imitate his martyrdom, because it happened in keeping with the gospel of Christ.
- Note: ἐθνῶν is "gentiles" or "nations"; "pagans" is the plain sense here, as against ANF's dated "heathen". ANF's "altogether consistent" overstates κατὰ τὸ εὐαγγέλιον, which means simply "according to the gospel".

**Q20. Polycarp to the Philippians 3 (Greek not in the Library)**
- Rendering 2: Brothers and sisters, I am writing to you about righteousness. I do not do this on my own authority, but because you asked me to. Neither I nor anyone like me can match the wisdom of the blessed and glorious Paul. When he was with you, he taught the word of truth accurately and firmly to the people of that time. And when he was away from you, he wrote you a letter.
- Note: Rendered from ANF only. "Not because I take anything upon myself" probably means "not on my own initiative" or "not presuming". The Greek of this chapter does survive, but it is not in the Library, so this cannot be checked against it. "In the presence of those who were then alive" is rendered "to the people of that time".

**Q21. Philippians 7: the word handed down**
- Rendering 2: "Anyone who does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is an antichrist." Anyone who does not confess the witness of the cross belongs to the devil. Some twist the Lord's sayings to suit their own desires, and say there is no resurrection and no judgment. Anyone who does this is the firstborn of Satan. So let us leave behind the empty talk of the many and their false teaching. Let us turn back to the word handed down to us from the beginning.
- Note: Rendered from ANF only. The third "whosoever" clause is split in two to keep sentences short; nothing is added. "The testimony of the cross" is ambiguous: it may mean the witness the cross gives, or the witness given about it. "Oracles of the Lord" means the Lord's sayings. "Lusts" in older English means desires generally, not only sexual ones.

**Q22. Philippians 9: the examples of endurance**
- Rendering 2: So I urge you all to obey the word of righteousness. I urge you to practise all endurance. You have seen it with your own eyes in the blessed Ignatius, Zosimus and Rufus. You have seen it in others from among you, and in Paul himself and the other apostles. Be sure that none of them ran in vain. They ran in faith and righteousness. They are now in the place owed to them, with the Lord, with whom they also suffered. For they did not love this present world. They loved the one who died for us and whom God raised from the dead for our sake.
- Note: Rendered from ANF only. "Patience" (ὑπομονή in the probable Greek) means endurance, not calmly waiting, so "endurance" avoids a misleading modern sense. ANF's bracketed "[set]", "[This do]" and "[now]" are translator's supplies. Some ANF brackets are kept in sense, such as "now".

**Q23. Philippians 11: Valens (Latin only)**
- Rendering 2: So, brothers and sisters, I am deeply grieved for Valens and his wife. May the Lord give them true repentance. In this matter, then, be moderate. "Do not count such people as enemies." Call them back as members of the body who are suffering and have gone astray. That way you will save your whole body. By doing this you will build yourselves up.
- Note: Rendered from ANF, which translates the surviving Latin only. "(Valens)" is ANF's clarifying insertion; the Latin context makes it clear. "Edify" is rendered "build up", which is its root sense. "Members" means limbs or parts of a body.

**Q24. Philippians 12: pray for persecutors (Latin only)**
- Rendering 2: Pray for all God's people. Pray also for kings, rulers and princes. Pray for those who persecute and hate you, and for the enemies of the cross. Then your fruit will be plain to everyone, and you will be complete in him.
- Note: Rendered from ANF, which translates the Latin only. "The saints" in this period means all believers, so it is rendered "God's people" to avoid the modern sense of canonised saints. "Perfect" (Latin perfecti) means mature or complete, not flawless.

**Q25. Ignatius to Polycarp (shorter version; Greek not in the Library)**
- Rendering 2 (opening): I have come to know well that your mind is set on God as on an unshakeable rock. I give great praise that I was found worthy to see your face, which is without fault. May I always have joy in it in God! ... Take care to keep unity; nothing is better than this. Bear with everyone, as the Lord bears with you.
- Rendering 2 (the times): The times need you, as sailors need the winds. They need you as a storm-tossed sailor needs a harbour. Then both you and those in your care may reach God.
- Rendering 2 (the anvil): Stand firm, like an anvil under the hammer. A great athlete takes blows and still wins.
- Note: Rendered from ANF only. "[His name]", "[to behold]" and "[and those under thy care]" are translator's supplies, kept in sense. "Pilots" in the times passage means ship's helmsmen, not aircraft pilots. The image is unclear: either helmsmen seek winds, or the times need Polycarp as helmsmen need winds. Both readings are possible. ANF's own note says "noble" is literally "great", which the rendering follows.

**Q26. Irenaeus, Against Heresies III.3.4 (ANF 39857–39875)**
- Rendering 2: Polycarp too was taught by apostles. He spent time with many who had seen Christ. Apostles in Asia also appointed him bishop of the church in Smyrna. I saw him myself when I was very young. He lived a very long time. When he was a very old man, he died a martyr's death, gloriously and nobly. He had always taught what he learned from the apostles. This is what the Church hands down, and it alone is true.
- Note: Rendered from ANF (a Latin-based English translation, with Greek fragments). "Tarried [on earth]" means "lived on". The Eusebius cross-check agrees. ANF's note says the Greek has "which the Church hands down", while the Latin has "which he also handed down to the Church". This matters only for the last clause.

**Q27. Irenaeus on Marcion (ANF 39882–39884; EUS 22962–22982)**
- Rendering 2 (ANF): Polycarp himself once met Marcion, who said to him, "Do you know me?" Polycarp answered, "I know you: the firstborn of Satan."
- Rendering 2 (EUS): Marcion once met Polycarp and said, "Do you recognise us?" Polycarp replied, "I recognise the firstborn of Satan."
- Note: The two witnesses differ: "me" (ANF/Latin) against "us" (Eusebius' Greek). In ANF Polycarp says "I know you". In Eusebius, "I recognise the firstborn of Satan" never names Marcion directly, which makes the insult sharper. Eusebius' own note gives a further variant. Most manuscripts read the question ἐπιγινώσκεις; three read ἐπιγίνωσκε, an imperative ("Recognise us!"). Use one version and say which.

**Q28. Irenaeus to Florinus (EUS 30807–30819; ANF 54572–54584)**
- Rendering 2: I remember what happened then more clearly than recent events. What we learn as children grows along with the mind and becomes part of it. So I can describe the very place where the blessed Polycarp used to sit and talk. I can describe how he came and went, how he lived and what he looked like. I can describe the talks he gave to the people. And I can describe what he told of his time with John and with the others who had seen the Lord.
- Rendering 2 (the cry): "Good God, for what times have you kept me, that I should have to bear these things?"
- Note: "Intercourse" in the 1890 English means dealings or company, so it is rendered "his time with". The source says "boys", but the Greek (παῖδες) means children generally. "Children" here is a judgment call; keep "boys" if strictness matters. "Discourses" means talks or teachings. Both versions of the cry agree in substance. "Spared" (EUS) and "reserved" (ANF) both mean "kept alive for".

**Q29. Irenaeus to Victor on Anicetus (EUS 31603–31612; ANF 54655–54664)**
- Rendering 2: Anicetus could not persuade Polycarp to stop keeping the day as he had always kept it. Polycarp had kept it with John, the Lord's disciple, and with the other apostles he had known. Nor could Polycarp persuade Anicetus to keep it. Anicetus said he must follow the customs of the elders who had come before him. Even so, the two of them shared communion together. In the church, Anicetus gave way to Polycarp in celebrating the Eucharist, clearly as a mark of respect. And they parted from each other in peace.
- Note: "What he had always observed" refers to the date of the Easter (Pascha) celebration. The text itself does not name it here, so "keeping the day" assumes the context. "Communed together" (ἐκοινώνησαν) is rendered "shared communion together", meaning they stayed in fellowship. The clause about the Eucharist is disputed, and NPNF's own note says so. It may mean Anicetus let Polycarp preside at the Eucharist, or it may mean something narrower. Treat this clause as uncertain. "Presbyters" means the church's elders; "elders" is plain, though "presbyters" may be kept where office matters.
