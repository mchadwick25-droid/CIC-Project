# Renderings compared: Polycarp of Smyrna, Q1–Q29

Comparer's sheet, 2026-11-16. Inputs: Source_Sheet.md section 3 (printed English, Greek, Rendering 1) and Rendering2.md (blind Rendering 2). I re-read every printed English passage and every Gebhardt Greek passage against the files in `cic/texts/` before deciding. File short names follow the source sheet: ANF = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml`, GEB = `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt`, EUS = `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml`.

**Totals.** Verdicts: AGREE 9, MERGED/CORRECTED 17, MEANING DIFFERS 3 (Q9, Q25, Q27). Use flags: USABLE 12, USE WITH WARNING 17, DO NOT USE 0.

**Consistency choices across quotes.** ἀδελφοί / "brethren" addressed to a church is "brothers and sisters" throughout (Q2, Q20, Q23). μετανοέω in the proconsul's mouth is "change your mind" (Q7, Q11), so that Polycarp's answer in Q11 can turn the word back on him.

## Greek bracket pairs reported reversed

The sheet's own rule is OCR text first, with the correction in [brackets]. In the two places Rendering 2 reported, the sheet prints the correction first and the OCR noise in brackets. I checked both against GEB:

- **Q8, GEB 738:** the file reads "éyxeuuévov". The correct Greek is **ἐγκειμένου** ("pressing him", genitive absolute with τοῦ ἀνθυπάτου). The sheet's "ἐγκειμένου [éyxeuuévov]" is reversed. The sheet also silently corrects GEB 739 "Ὄνμοσον" to Ὄμοσον, which is right.
- **Q16, GEB 886 and 888:** the file reads "οἱ ἄνομον" and "κομφέχτορα". The correct Greek is **οἱ ἄνομοι** ("the lawless men", nominative plural) and **κομφέκτορα** (the arena's *confector*; EUS 23503 prints Κομφέκτορα). The sheet's "ἄνομοι [ἄνομον]" and "κομφέκτορα [κομφέχτορα]" are reversed. The source sheet's fault list F3 agrees: "line 886 'ἄνομον' for ἄνομοι".

The same reversal also appears, unreported, in these places: Q9 προσποιῇ [προσττοιξῖ]; Q10 Πεῖσον [Ilstoov]; Q11 μετανοήσῃς [μεταγοήσης] and κρειττόνων [χρειττόνων]; Q13 ἀποθέμενος [ἐποϑέμεroc] and Ἄφετέ [Ayers]; Q14 δι᾽ [Ov] οὗ; Q15 αὐτοῦ [οὐτοῦ]; Q18 λίθων [λέϑων]. In each case the form outside the brackets is the correct Greek. None of them changes a rendering.

**Q1. Martyrdom ch. 1: he waited to be handed over**
- Source: ANF 3767–3772; GEB 564–567.
- Final reading: "He waited to be handed over, as the Lord did. He did it so that we too might imitate him, looking not only to what concerns ourselves but also to what concerns our neighbors."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Rendering 1 is the base. "Our own good" becomes "what concerns ourselves" (τὸ καθ᾽ ἑαυτούς), because "good" is added. Rendering 2's "We should look" turns a participle into a command, so it is dropped.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: Both follow μιμηταί ("imitators") over ANF's "followers". "Him" may mean Polycarp or the Lord, in the Greek as in English.

**Q2. Ch. 4: against volunteering**
- Source: ANF 3848–3849; GEB 636–638.
- Final reading: "So, brothers and sisters, we do not praise those who hand themselves over. The gospel does not teach that."
- Verdict: AGREE. Rendering 1 is taken. Rendering 2's "come forward and hand themselves over" says the same thing twice for one Greek phrase.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: ANF's "[to suffering]" is the translator's addition, and both leave it out.

**Q3. Ch. 5: the vision**
- Source: ANF 3865–3869; GEB 647–650.
- Final reading: "While he was praying, three days before his arrest, he had a vision. He saw his pillow being burned up by fire. He turned to those who were with him and said, 'I must be burned alive.'"
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. "Fell into a vision" (Rendering 1) is not idiomatic, and "fell into a trance" (Rendering 2) brings in a modern hypnotic sense. Both become "he had a vision" (ἐν ὀπτασίᾳ γέγονεν).
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: a wonder reported by the Smyrna church. Do not add ANF's "prophetically", which in the Greek belongs only to the ch. 12 recall.
- Reason: The Greek has no "behold", "under his head" or "seemed to him", and both renderings rightly leave them out.

**Q4. Ch. 7: arrest**
- Source: ANF 3907–3926; GEB 678–691 (the sheet gives no Greek for the food line; it is GEB 684–687).
- Final reading (escape): "He could have gone from there to another place, but he did not want to. He said, 'Let the will of God be done.'"
- Final reading (onlookers): "Those present were amazed at his age and his calm. They marvelled that there had been such haste to arrest so old a man."
- Final reading (food): "At once, in that very hour, he had food and drink set before them, as much as they wanted. He asked them to give him an hour to pray in peace."
- Final reading (prayer): "He stood and prayed, full of the grace of God. For two hours he could not fall silent. Those who heard him were amazed. Many were sorry they had come out against such a godly old man."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. In the escape line, Rendering 1's "place" is kept over Rendering 2's "another farm" and "too", because χωρίον can mean either and "farm" claims more. In the food line, Rendering 2's "in that very hour" (ἐν ἐκείνῃ τῇ ὥρᾳ, also in ANF) is restored where Rendering 1 dropped it. In the prayer line, Rendering 2's sense of σιγῆσαι ("fall silent") is kept, worded more plainly.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: The onlookers' words are reported speech, not ANF's direct question. ANF's "venerable" (twice) has no Greek word behind it. σπουδή can mean "haste" or "effort", and either is fair.

**Q5. Ch. 8: in the carriage**
- Source: ANF 3944–3954; GEB 701–704.
- Final reading: "What harm is there in saying 'Caesar is Lord', and offering sacrifice, and the rest that goes with it, and so saving your life?" / Reply: "I am not going to do what you advise."
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: ANF's own note says every manuscript has κύριος, so "Caesar is Lord" is better supported than ANF's printed "Lord Caesar". Tell the injury as a scraped shin, not a dislocation.

**Q6. Ch. 9: the voice**
- Source: ANF 3973–3976; GEB 724–727.
- Final reading: "A voice came from heaven: 'Be strong, Polycarp, and be brave.' No one saw who spoke. But those of our people who were there heard the voice."
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: a wonder reported by the Smyrna church. Eusebius's retelling says "not many heard" it (D12).
- Reason: ANF's sentence also opens with "a voice from heaven" (ANF 3973), so including it adds nothing.

**Q7. Ch. 9: "Away with the atheists"**
- Source: ANF 3979–3986; GEB 731–738.
- Final reading: "'Respect your age.' ... 'Swear by the Fortune of Caesar. Change your mind. Say, "Away with the atheists."' Polycarp looked with a grave face at the whole crowd of lawless pagans in the stadium. He waved his hand at them, groaned, looked up to heaven and said, 'Away with the atheists.'"
- Verdict: AGREE. Rendering 2's "Then" is dropped, because the Greek joins the verbs with participles.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: "Atheists" means people who reject the gods, and needs one gloss where it is used.

**Q8. Ch. 9: eighty-six years**
- Source: ANF 3988–3992; GEB 738–746.
- Final reading: "The proconsul pressed him: 'Swear, and I will set you free. Curse Christ.' Polycarp said, 'For eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my King, who saved me?'"
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the text does not say whether the 86 years are his age or his years as a Christian (D4).
- Reason: Both follow the Greek "who saved me" over ANF's "my Saviour"; EUS 23334 agrees. The bracket pair is confirmed above.

**Q9. Ch. 10: "I am a Christian"**
- Source: ANF 4003–4008; GEB 747–753.
- Final reading: "You fool yourself if you think I will swear by the Fortune of Caesar, as you put it. You pretend not to know who I am. So hear me say it boldly: I am a Christian. If you want to learn what Christianity teaches, give me a day, and listen."
- Verdict: MEANING DIFFERS on the opening clause, εἰ κενοδοξεῖς ἵνα ὀμόσω:
  - Rendering 1: "you fool yourself if you think I will swear", meaning the proconsul is deluded.
  - Rendering 2: "you are wasting your effort if you expect me to swear", meaning his pressing is futile. This is close to ANF's "thou art vainly urgent".

  The final reading takes Rendering 1, because κενοδοξέω means to hold a vain opinion. The card carries the other reading as uncertain. The rest of the quote agrees; "hear me say it boldly" renders μετὰ παρρησίας ἄκουε without Rendering 2's added "I will tell you".
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the opening Greek is hard. It means either "you vainly imagine I will swear" or "you vainly press me to swear".
- Reason: The Greek has "who I am", not ANF's "who and what I am".

**Q10. Ch. 10: whom he would answer**
- Source: ANF 4009–4015; GEB 754–757.
- Final reading: "The proconsul said, 'Persuade the people.' Polycarp said, 'You I would count worth an answer. We have been taught to give rulers and authorities appointed by God the honor that is due, so long as it does us no harm. But I do not think those people deserve a defense from me.'"
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Both renderings have "I would have thought you worth an answer". Read as a past unreal condition, that suggests he does not think so now. But he has just offered the proconsul an explanation. So it becomes the present "I would count".
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: Otherwise the two renderings match each other and the Greek.

**Q11. Ch. 11: the beasts and the fire**
- Source: ANF 4025–4038; GEB 772–783.
- Final reading (beasts): "'I have wild beasts. I will throw you to them unless you change your mind.' He said, 'Call them. For us, a change of mind from better to worse is impossible. But it is good to change from cruelty to justice.'"
- Final reading (fire): "'If you scorn the beasts, I will have you burned up by fire, unless you change your mind.' Polycarp said, 'You threaten fire that burns for an hour and soon goes out. You do not know the fire of the judgment to come and of eternal punishment, kept for the ungodly. But why are you waiting? Bring what you want.'"
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Rendering 1 keeps the play on "change of mind" and is the base. "Cruel things to just ones" is tightened to "cruelty to justice" (χαλεπῶν / δίκαια). Rendering 2's "wrong to right" weakens χαλεπά, so it is not used. Rendering 2's "threaten me" follows ANF's added "me", which is not in the Greek, so it is dropped. "Bring what you want" (φέρε) is kept over "Do what you want".
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: ANF's own note gives the literal "a change impossible to us", which supports both renderings against ANF's text.

**Q12. Ch. 12: the crowd**
- Source: ANF 4055–4058; GEB 797–800.
- Final reading: "This is the teacher of Asia, the father of the Christians, the destroyer of our gods. He teaches many not to sacrifice and not to worship."
- Verdict: AGREE.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: ANF's closing "the gods" is added. "Asia" is the Roman province. The variant "ungodliness" for Asia goes on the card only (D16).

**Q13. Ch. 13: undressing; the nails**
- Source: ANF 4083–4096; GEB 820–830.
- Final reading (undressing): "He took off all his clothes himself and untied his belt. He also tried to take off his sandals. He had not done this before, because each of the faithful was always eager to be the first to touch his skin. For he had been honored in every way for his good life, even before his martyrdom."
- Final reading (nails): "Leave me as I am. The one who lets me endure the fire will also let me stay on the pyre without flinching, without the safety of your nails."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. In the undressing line, Rendering 2's "because" is restored; the Greek says διὰ τὸ, and Rendering 1 split off the reason. Rendering 1's "skin" (χρωτός, ANF "skin") is kept over Rendering 2's "body". In the nails line, Rendering 2's structure is taken. Rendering 1's added "I will not need" is dropped.
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: "Honored in every way" is one fair reading of ἐκεκόσμητο, and both renderings chose it. ANF has "adorned with every kind of good". Follow "before his martyrdom", not Eusebius's "gray hairs" (D11).

**Q14. Ch. 14: the prayer (core lines)**
- Source: ANF 4104–4124; GEB 834–853.
- Final reading (ram): "He was bound like a choice ram from a great flock, taken for an offering: a whole burnt offering, made ready and pleasing to God."
- Final reading (thanksgiving): "Lord God Almighty, Father of your beloved and blessed child Jesus Christ. Through him we have come to know you. You are the God of angels and powers and of all creation, and of the whole race of the righteous who live in your presence. I bless you, because you have counted me worthy of this day and this hour. You have given me a share among the martyrs, in the cup of your Christ. It leads to the resurrection to eternal life, of soul and body, in the immortality of the Holy Spirit."
- Final reading (acceptance): "May I be received among them before you today, as a rich and pleasing sacrifice. You prepared this beforehand, you showed it beforehand, and you have fulfilled it. You are the God who does not lie, the true God."
- Final reading (doxology): "For this reason, and for all things, I praise you, I bless you, I glorify you. I do this through the eternal and heavenly high priest Jesus Christ, your beloved child. Through him be glory to you, with him and the Holy Spirit, now and for the ages to come. Amen."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED.
  - Ram: Rendering 1 dropped "bound" (προσδεθείς; ANF "being bound"), which is restored. Rendering 2's "He put his hands behind him" comes before the quoted range, so it is left out. It is attested in both ANF and GEB if the writer extends the quote.
  - Thanksgiving: Rendering 2's "your martyrs" follows ANF's added "Thy". The Greek has "the martyrs". Rendering 1's "undying life" adds "life", and Rendering 2's "incorruption" is jargon. Both become "immortality" (ἀφθαρσία; EUS 23458 has "immortality").
  - Acceptance: Rendering 2's "showed it to me" follows ANF's added "to me", which is not in the Greek, so Rendering 1 is taken.
  - Doxology: Rendering 2's split is taken, keeping sentences under 25 words.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: quote this reading, not ANF's. The Greek and Eusebius have "I bless you" (ANF "I give Thee thanks") and "through the ... high priest" (ANF drops it). παῖς means "child" or "servant", and ANF and Eusebius print "Son".
- Reason: Each change follows the Greek where ANF adds or drops a word.

**Q15. Ch. 15: the wonder in the fire**
- Source: ANF 4136–4149; GEB 869–884.
- Final reading: "When he had sent up the Amen and finished his prayer, the men in charge of the fire lit it. A great flame flashed out, and we saw a wonder, we to whom it was given to see. We were preserved to tell the others what happened. The fire took the shape of a vaulted room, like a ship's sail filled by the wind. It walled in the martyr's body all around. He was in the middle, not like flesh burning, but like bread baking, or like gold and silver glowing in a furnace. And we caught a fragrance so strong, it was like the breath of frankincense or some other precious spice."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED.
  - "Kept safe" (Rendering 1) claims more than ἐτηρήθημεν does, so it becomes "preserved", as in ANF.
  - "Being refined" (both renderings) becomes "glowing" (πυρούμενος; ANF "glowing").
  - Rendering 1's "like breathing incense" wrongly makes the witnesses the ones breathing, and Rendering 2's "burning incense" adds "burning". λιβανωτοῦ πνέοντος becomes "the breath of frankincense".
  - Rendering 1's sentence order is taken over Rendering 2's split "Or he was like".
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: wonders reported by the Smyrna church as eyewitnesses. Eusebius's retelling lacks the bread.
- Reason: ANF's "great miracle", "in great fury" and "[coming from the pile]" have no Greek words behind them, and both renderings rightly leave them out.

**Q16. Ch. 16: the dagger; what the church called him**
- Source: ANF 4158–4170; GEB 886–899.
- Final reading (dagger): "In the end the lawless men saw that the fire could not consume his body. They ordered an executioner to go up to him and drive in a dagger. When he did, out came a dove and so much blood that it put out the fire."
- Final reading (what he was): "The most wonderful Polycarp was one of these chosen ones. In our own times he was a teacher like the apostles and the prophets, and bishop of the universal church in Smyrna. Every word that came from his mouth has come true, or will come true."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED.
  - "One of these chosen ones" (Rendering 1) is kept over "one of them" (Rendering 2). The antecedent "the elect" stands in the same source sentence (ANF 4165; GEB 893).
  - Rendering 2's "or will come true" is kept over Rendering 1's "and". In English, "and" says that each word both has come true and will. The Greek καί here, like ANF's "either ... or", is distributive.
  - The dove text is the same in both renderings.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING. The dove is doubtful: Gebhardt brackets it, Eusebius omits it, and ANF's note gives a proposed correction. If told at all, tell it as doubtful (D3). "Catholic" means universal, not Roman Catholic. "Every word ... come true" is the church's claim.
- Reason: The bracket pairs are confirmed above. "Martyr", bracketed by Gebhardt, is not rendered.

**Q17. Ch. 17: worship and love**
- Source: ANF 4191–4208; GEB 917–932.
- Final reading (Nicetes): "'Otherwise,' he said, 'they will leave the crucified one and start worshiping this man.'"
- Final reading (worship and love): "Christ we worship, because he is the Son of God. The martyrs we love, as they deserve, as disciples and imitators of the Lord. We love them for their unmatched devotion to their own King and Teacher. May we too become their companions and fellow disciples."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Rendering 1's "They deserve it" moves ἀξίως onto a new subject, so it goes back to "we love, as they deserve". Rendering 2's "devotion" (εὐνοίας) is taken over "loyalty".
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: Both follow the Greek "imitators" and "Teacher" over ANF's "followers" and "Master". Rendering 2's note on who says φησίν overreaches. The Greek says Nicetes said this at the urging of the Jews (GEB 921–922), which is the church's hostile claim (N12).

**Q18. Ch. 18: the bones and the yearly gathering**
- Source: ANF 4219–4229; GEB 936–953.
- Final reading: "Later we took up his bones, more precious than costly stones and more refined than gold, and laid them where it was fitting. There the Lord will let us gather, as far as we can, in gladness and joy, and keep the birthday of his martyrdom. We will do this to remember those who have already competed, and to train and prepare those still to come."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED.
  - Rendering 1's "we will gather" is reshaped, because in the Greek the Lord grants the gathering.
  - Rendering 1's "run the race ... run it next" names a sport and adds "next", so it becomes "competed" (προηθληκότων).
  - Rendering 2's "It will remember" gives the memory to the day, so it is not used.
  - δοκιμώτερα is rendered "more refined" (ANF "more purified", note "more tried").
- Use: USABLE.
- Reason: "Birthday" (γενέθλιον; ANF's note "Literally, 'the birth-day'") needs one gloss: the day of death seen as a birth into life.

**Q20. Polycarp to the Philippians 3**

When he was away from you, he wrote you a letter."

- Use: USE WITH WARNING: rendered from one English translation only, with no Greek in the Library. ANF's note says "letter" is plural in the original, though one letter is probably meant (ANF 3311).
- Reason: Otherwise the renderings agree.

**Q21. Philippians 7: the word handed down**
- Source: ANF 3433–3444.
- Final reading: "'Anyone who does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is antichrist.' Anyone who does not confess the witness of the cross is of the devil. Anyone who twists the Lord's sayings to suit his own desires, and says there is no resurrection or judgment, is the firstborn of Satan. So let us leave behind the empty talk of many people and their false teaching. Let us return to the word handed down to us from the beginning."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Rendering 1 keeps the three parallel "anyone who" clauses, so it is the base, trimmed to 25 words. Rendering 2's "an antichrist" adds an article that is not in ANF or 1 John 4:3. "The many" is changed to "many people" (ANF "the vanity of many").
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: rendered from ANF only. "The witness of the cross" is ambiguous; ANF's note gives "martyrdom of the cross" and "His suffering on the cross".

**Q22. Philippians 9: the examples of endurance**
- Source: ANF 3484–3494; cross-check EUS 20077–20087 (Eusebius III.36.13).
- Final reading: "So I urge you all to obey the word of righteousness and to practice all endurance. You saw it with your own eyes in the blessed Ignatius, Zosimus and Rufus. You saw it in others from among you, and in Paul himself and the other apostles. Be sure that none of them ran in vain, but in faith and righteousness. They are now in their rightful place with the Lord, with whom they also suffered. For they did not love this present world. They loved him who died for us and was raised by God for our sake."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Rendering 1's "the place they have earned" adds an idea of merit, and Rendering 2's "owed to them" leans the same way. ANF has "due place" and EUS "rightful place", so it becomes "rightful place". Rendering 2's "none of them ran in vain" is taken.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: do not use this to state that Ignatius was dead. Ch. 13 asks for news of him (D8).
- Reason: Two English translations agree in substance. EUS has "be obedient" without "to the word of righteousness", which is a card note only.

**Q25. Ignatius to Polycarp (shorter version)**
- Source: ANF 8549–8557, 8613–8615, 8649–8651.
- Final reading (opening): "I have had good proof that your mind is fixed in God, as on an immovable rock. I give loud praise that I was counted worthy to see your blameless face. May I always enjoy it in God! ... Take care to keep unity, for nothing is better. Bear with everyone, as the Lord bears with you."
- Final reading (the times): "The times call for you, as ship's pilots call for the winds, and as one tossed by a storm looks for a harbor. Then you may reach God."
- Final reading (anvil): "Stand firm, like an anvil under the hammer. It is the mark of a great athlete to be wounded and still win."
- Verdict: MEANING DIFFERS in "the times":
  - Rendering 1: "so that you may reach God". Polycarp alone reaches God.
  - Rendering 2: "both you and those in your care may reach God". This keeps ANF's bracketed supply.

  The final reading follows Rendering 1, because ANF's bracket is the translator's addition. The card notes that ANF's unbracketed "both" points to a second party. Without the Greek, this cannot be settled.
  - Opening: Rendering 1's added "So" is dropped, and ANF's "loudly" is kept. Rendering 2's "set on God" is not used; ANF has "fixed in God".
  - Anvil: "It is the part of" (ANF) is restored as "It is the mark of", and "wounded" (ANF) replaces "takes the blows".
  - "Ship's pilots" is kept, because Rendering 2's "sailors" loses the helmsman. "Storm-tossed sailor" (both renderings) adds "sailor", so it becomes "one tossed by a storm".
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: rendered from ANF only, with no Greek in the Library. "Those under thy care" is ANF's supply.
- Reason: These are the only content differences between the two renderings.

**Q26. Irenaeus, Against Heresies III.3.4**
- Source: ANF 39857–39864; cross-check EUS 22924–22938.
- Final reading: "Polycarp too was taught by apostles and talked with many who had seen Christ. Apostles in Asia also appointed him bishop of the church in Smyrna. I saw him myself in my early youth, for he lived a very long time. He was a very old man when he died a glorious and most noble martyr's death. He had always taught what he had learned from the apostles, what the Church hands down, and what alone is true."
- Verdict: AGREE. Rendering 1 is the base, with "most noble" restored from ANF's "most nobly" (EUS "most illustrious").
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: "appointed by apostles in Asia" is Irenaeus's claim. The naming of John is later and narrower (D1).
- Reason: Rendering 2's note puts ANF's Greek/Latin note ("which he also handed down to the Church") on this passage's last clause. That is wrong. The note belongs to the later sentence about Rome (ANF 39875), and EUS's "which the Church also hands down" matches the clause here.

**Q27. Irenaeus on Marcion**
- Source: ANF 39882–39884; EUS 22960–22982.
- Final reading (Eusebius): "Once Marcion met Polycarp and said, 'Do you recognize us?' Polycarp replied, 'I recognize the firstborn of Satan.'"
- Verdict: MEANING DIFFERS between the two witnesses, and the renderings follow them faithfully:
  - ANF (Latin tradition): "Do you know me?" / "I know you, the firstborn of Satan." Polycarp names Marcion directly.
  - EUS (Greek): "Do you recognize us?" / "I recognize the firstborn of Satan." The insult is not addressed to him.

  The final reading follows Eusebius, since it carries Irenaeus's Greek. The card gives the ANF form as the other reading.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING. The wording differs between the Latin and Greek traditions (D5). Three manuscripts read the question as a command, "Recognize us!" (EUS note, card only). The place of the meeting is not given (D13).
- Reason: The answer "the firstborn of Satan" is the same in every witness.

**Q28. Irenaeus to Florinus**
- Source: EUS 30805–30829; ANF 54572–54592.
- Final reading: "I remember the events of that time more clearly than those of recent years. What we learn as children grows with the mind and becomes part of it. So I can describe the very place where the blessed Polycarp used to sit and teach. I can describe how he came and went, how he lived and what he looked like. I can describe his talks to the people. And I can describe what he told of his time with John and with the others who had seen the Lord."
- Final reading (the cry): "Good God, for what times have you kept me, that I must endure these things?"
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED. Rendering 2's repeated "I can describe" is taken, because it keeps the source's single list under "able to describe". Rendering 1 changed it to "I remember". "Children" is supported by ANF ("experiences of childhood"), though EUS has "boys". In the cry, Rendering 1's "kept me alive for" and "put up with this" are too casual and interpretive. It becomes "kept me" and "endure", the word both translations use.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: this is Irenaeus's memory, written many years later. The cry is what Polycarp "would have" said, given as his habit ("as he was wont"), not a single recorded moment.
- Reason: The two printed translations agree in substance.

**Q29. Irenaeus to Victor on Anicetus**
- Source: EUS 31603–31612; ANF 54655–54664.
- Final reading: "Anicetus could not persuade Polycarp to stop keeping what he had always kept with John the disciple of our Lord and the other apostles he had known. Nor could Polycarp persuade Anicetus to keep it, because Anicetus said he must follow the customs of the elders before him. Even so, they shared communion. In the church, Anicetus gave Polycarp the celebration of the Eucharist, plainly as a mark of respect. And they parted from each other in peace."
- Verdict: MERGED/CORRECTED.
  - "Keeping the day" (both renderings) assumes Easter, which the clause does not name, so it becomes "what he had always kept".
  - Rendering 1's "his way" is dropped; it is ANF's bracketed supply.
  - The Eucharist clause follows both printed translations: EUS "conceded the administration of the eucharist ... to Polycarp", ANF "conceded to Polycarp ... the celebration of the Eucharist". Rendering 1's "leading" says the same thing. Rendering 2's "gave way to Polycarp in celebrating" keeps the verb παρεχώρησεν but leaves the sense open.
- Use: USE WITH WARNING: the Eucharist clause is disputed. It may mean that Anicetus let Polycarp preside (the common reading, and NPNF's) or only that he let him take part (Heinichen, in the NPNF note at EUS 31612). Tell it with that doubt.
- Reason: "Elders" here means the earlier bishops of Rome (EUS 31528–31530).
