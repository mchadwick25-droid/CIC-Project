## (c) What is uncertain: notes for the card

- **The year.** The account of his death gives a day and a month but no year, and the month itself is unclear (March in the Greek, May in the English edition). Eusebius and Jerome place the death under Marcus Aurelius. "Second century" comes from the modern dating of those reigns, not from the ancient sources (S10, S11, S12, D2, N2, C13).
- **The eighty-six years.** The text does not say whether this is his age or his years as a Christian. Irenaeus says only that he died "a very old man" (D4, N1).
- **Taught by apostles.** This is Irenaeus's claim; he heard Polycarp as a boy and wrote many years later. Later writers (Tertullian, Jerome) narrow it to John (S5, D1, N3).
- **The wonders.** The fire like a sail is reported by the Smyrna church, which says it saw it; it names no single witness. Eusebius's retelling keeps the sail. The same account also tells of a vision of a burning pillow, a voice from heaven, a sweet smell and a dove. The story leaves these out. The dove is doubtful in the text itself: the Greek edition brackets it and Eusebius omits it (M19, M23, M29–M31, D3, N10).
- **The day.** The account calls the day of his death "the great Sabbath" (S10, D2). The story gives only the Friday evening of the arrest.
- **The letter's messenger and address.** The messenger's name differs between editions (Marcus or Marcion), and Eusebius gives a different address for the letter (D6, D15). The story names neither.
- **Why he went to Rome.** The earliest witness, Irenaeus, does not say Easter was the purpose of the trip, only that the two disagreed about it and about other points. Later writers make Easter the reason (D14). The story says only that he visited and that they disagreed.
- **The Eucharist in Rome.** Irenaeus says Anicetus gave Polycarp "the celebration of the Eucharist" as a mark of respect. Whether Polycarp presided or only took part is disputed (Q29). The story says only that they shared communion.
- **What came of Irenaeus's letter to Victor.** The sheet does not say. The story stops at the letter.
- **The Jews of Smyrna.** The church's letter makes hostile claims about them (gathering wood, urging that the body be withheld). No other source tells that side (S9, M27, M33, N12). The story does not repeat these claims.
- **Not known at all.** His birth, birthplace, family and conversion; the year he became bishop (N1, N2).

## (d) Impact lines (sheet section 4): used and left out

**Used**

- **I3** (bones kept, yearly gathering): the church's first act after his death, with its own phrase "the birthday of his martyrdom" (Q18).
- **I5** (the account sent on): how the story travelled. Used only as far as the Smyrna church's own request; Eusebius's copying is on the card.
- **I8** (he taught Irenaeus): used to say who Irenaeus is, and why his word carries weight.
- **I11** (his peace with Anicetus used to restrain Victor): the ending. It is the heaviest effect the record gives beyond Smyrna, and it is an event, not a summary.

**Left out, and why**

- **I1** (his death ended the persecution): the church's own claim, in a figure of speech ("set a seal"). It would need a speaker tag and an explanation, and the time went to I11.
- **I2** (a martyrdom in keeping with the gospel; against volunteering): a strong line, drafted and then cut for length. It needs Quintus or Q1 to make sense, about 45 words. A reviewer may want it back in place of sentence 23.
- **I4** (remembered even by pagans): praise from the church's own letter; adds no event.
- **I6** (the copy chain): later copyists' note; card material.
- **I7** (he sent on Ignatius's letters): needs Ignatius introduced, a second story line that two minutes cannot carry.
- **I9, I10** (Irenaeus used him against the heretics and against Florinus): the same kind of use as I11. I11 was chosen as the one with a clear event and a peaceful end.
- **I12** (Polycrates names him among Asia's great lights): a further name and the fourteenth-day custom to explain.
- **I13, I14, I15** (his letter recommended, its use of 1 Peter, still read in Jerome's day): the story does not tell his letter, so these have nothing to rest on. I14 is marked card only.
- **I16** (Papias his companion): a link, not an impact, as the sheet says.
- **I17** (Smyrna's records name John): a later and narrower claim (D1).
- **I18, I19** (what he defended and urged, in his own words): left out with the letter to the Philippians. This is the main loss: the story shows what he did and what came of it, not what he taught in his own words.
