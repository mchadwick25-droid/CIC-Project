# Outline: Cyril and Methodius

Every line cites `Source_Sheet.md` (S = setting, M = moment, I = impact, D = disagreement, N = not held).

- **Length:** about 2½ minutes (about 380 words). Reason: two lives, not one. The argument they started moved from Moravia to Venice to Rome and on into two later churches, and the record is full enough to carry it. Do not stretch to 3.
- **Setting, in words to say aloud:** "In the ninth century, in the 860s" (S4); "two brothers from Thessalonica, a Greek city where Slavic was spoken too" (S2, VM 12212–12213); "sent from Constantinople to Moravia, in central Europe, in what is now the Czech Republic" (S3); tradition: "the mission of Cyril and Methodius", "the Slavic church that grew from it" (S5). Name him Constantine until Rome; "Cyril" is the monk's name he took weeks before he died (S1).
- **Thread:** They gave the Slavic peoples letters of their own, and with them the Gospel and worship in their own speech, and they argued, against those who said God is rightly praised in only three languages, that every people may praise God in its own (M4–M9, M17, I2).
- **Shape:** Missionary or translator. The trap is the sender's side only: give the Moravians' request (S6), the hostile Salzburg voice (M14), and the Bulgarians who received the work (I1).
- **Openings:** (a) The emperor asks the tired scholar to go. He says he will go gladly "if they have letters for their language"; without them, he asks, "who can write words on water, and who wants to be called a heretic?" (M4). (b) Venice: Latin bishops, priests and monks gather against him "like crows against a falcon" (M9), and he answers with Q1.

**Story beats**
1. Two brothers of Thessalonica. Constantine, the youngest, studied in Constantinople, was made librarian at Hagia Sophia, then taught philosophy. The Lives call him "Constantine the Philosopher" (S1, M1). Methodius governed a Slavic district for the emperor, then left it to become a monk on a mountain of monks called Olympus, where Constantine later joined him (S2, M2). Say "a mountain of monks called Olympus"; do not place it in Greece, and give no location (N4). Documented as the Lives' account.
2. In the 860s Rastislav of Moravia sent to Emperor Michael: teachers from Italy, Greece and Germany teach us different things; we are simple people with no one to teach us in our own language (S6). The emperor chose the brothers because people of Thessalonica speak Slavic (S2). Constantine asked whether the Slavs had letters; they had none. After prayer he made letters and began with "In the beginning was the Word" (M4, M5). The making of the letters: Widely Accepted. That God revealed them: the Lives' claim, told in their voice. Which alphabet: Contested (D6); say "Slavic letters", not "Cyrillic".
3. In Moravia they taught disciples and turned the church's services into Slavic (M6). Latin clergy objected that God chose only three languages, Hebrew, Greek and Latin, the three on the sign Pilate put over the cross (M7). Documented as the Lives' account. Give no figure for the years in Moravia (D8).
4. At Venice the bishops pressed him: no apostle, pope or Church Father had made such letters. He answered with Q1 and named peoples who already praised God in their own letters: Armenians, Persians, Goths, Arabs, Egyptians, Syrians and others (M9). Documented as VC's account.
5. In Rome Pope Hadrian came out to meet them, because they carried relics of Clement, an early bishop of Rome. The Slavonic books were laid on an altar, and the disciples sang the liturgy in Slavonic in St Peter's (M10). Constantine fell ill, took the monk's habit and the name Cyril, and died on 14 February 869, aged 42 (M11, S4). His last words to his brother: Q2 (M11). He was buried in the church of St Clement (M12). Documented as the Lives' account. Which pope: Contested (D3); VC names Hadrian.
6. Methodius went back as bishop for the Slavs of Pannonia (M13). Frankish bishops said he was teaching in their land; he said the land belonged to St Peter. They held him for two and a half years in Swabia, until the pope stopped them saying Mass (M14). A Salzburg writer of his own day complained that this "Greek" with "newly invented Slavic letters" had partly cheapened in the people's eyes the Masses and Gospels of the priests who held them in Latin (M14). The Moravians then asked Rome for him as archbishop (M15). Documented as VM's account. The Salzburg complaint: written in his lifetime, by his opponents.
7. In 879 Pope John VIII summoned him and repeated an earlier ban on singing Mass in Slavonic. He could still preach in it (M16). In 880, after questioning him in front of the pope's own bishops, he found him sound, praised the letters "found by Constantine the Philosopher", allowed Slavonic Mass and Scripture with the Gospel read first in Latin, and wrote Q3 (M17). Documented: these are the pope's letters. Methodius died on 6 April 885. His disciples held the funeral in Latin, Greek and Slavonic, and crowds came, rich and poor, free and slave (M21, S4). Death and funeral: Documented as VM's account. The year 885 comes from a modern historian (S4).

**What came of them** (about a third of the story)
1. Heaviest: the work went on with their disciples. Wiching, whom Methodius had condemned, took the see, and the disciples were driven out and scattered (I1, LCl 12972–12974, PG 98734–98736). Clement, Naum and Angelarius crossed the Danube to Bulgaria. Prince Boris thanked God for sending them. Clement became "the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue" and wrote plain sermons for every feast, because Bulgarian priests could barely follow Greek (I1). The *Life of Clement* says, in paraphrase, that Clement handed down to "us Bulgarians" all that belongs to the Church (PG 99590–99594).
2. **Defended:** that God may be praised in every language (I2). Cyril's dying prayer asked God to destroy "the three-language heresy" (M11). The pope's ruling of 880 (M17) and the Kievan Chronicle both carried it on (I2).
3. The Rus took the letters as their own. In paraphrase: the Kievan Chronicle says this Slavic writing was first made for the Moravians and their neighbouring Slavs (the Czechs, the Lyakhs and the Polyanians), and is found in Rus and among the Danube Bulgarians too (I3, RPC 7867–7873).
4. Memory: in Rome a painted image over Cyril's tomb, with lamps kept burning day and night (I4). In Moravia in 1380 a bishop ordered their feast kept, calling them "Fathers, Apostles and our chief Patrons" (I4).
End on 1 or 3, as an event: who carried their letters on, and how far they reached.

**Quotes**
- **Q1.** VC 20307–20310: "nonne pluvia a deo aequaliter super omnes cadit? et sol nonne eodem modo lucet omnibus (Matth. 5. 45)? nonne aerem omnes aequaliter spiramus ? quomodo igitur vos non pudet tres tantum linguas statuere, reliquos populos et Stirpes caecos et surdos esse iubentes ?" Rendered from Dümmler and Miklosich's Latin translation of the Slavonic Life (the Slavonic is not readable in the Library): "Does God's rain not fall on all alike? Does the sun not shine the same on everyone? Do we not all breathe the air alike? Then how are you not ashamed to fix on three languages only? You would order every other people and tribe to be blind and deaf." Job: his case in his own voice, at the turn of the story.
- **Q2.** VM 12251–12255 (the editor's notes: "the mountain" is Olympus, 12288; "going to judgment" means to death, 12286): "Ecce frater nos consortes eramus, unum sulcum imprimentes atque ego in agro cado, postquam diem meum terminavi, tu autem amas montem valde, noli relinquere montis gratia disciplinam tuam, hac re enim potes melius salvus fieri." Rendered from Miklosich's Latin translation of the Slavonic Life of Methodius: "Look, brother, we were partners ploughing a single furrow. And I fall in the field, having finished my day. You love the mountain very much. Do not give up your teaching for the mountain's sake. By this you can better be saved." Job: the handover from one brother to the other.
- **Q3.** J8 13974–13976: "qüoniam qui fecit tres linguas principales, bebream [hebream] scilicet, grecam et latinam, ipse creavit et alias omnes ad laudem et gloriam suam." Rendered from the Latin original: "For he who made the three chief languages, Hebrew, Greek and Latin, also himself created all the others, for his praise and glory." Job: the argument won, in a pope's own words.

**Links:** Photius, his teacher (heard him teach; M1). Anastasius the Librarian, at the Slavonic liturgy in Rome; wrote of him (met; wrote of him). Popes Hadrian II (met) and John VIII (met; wrote to Methodius). Clement of Rome (kept his relics). Gorazd, Clement of Ohrid, Naum (heard them teach; carried on their work). Boris of Bulgaria (received their disciples). Wiching (opposed). Sheet §4.

**Who tells it**
- *Life of Constantine* and *Life of Methodius*: written in Slavonic by the brothers' own followers, judged by their editors to be close to the events, but kept only in copies made centuries later, from the 14th and 15th centuries on; Ginzel disputes the Life of Methodius (D1). Say aloud: "Most of what we know comes from two Lives written by their own followers. They survive only in copies made centuries later."
- Pope John VIII's letters (879–881) and the Salzburg complaint (870s): written in the brothers' own time, by a supporter and by opponents.
- *Life of Clement*: either by a Bulgarian who had lived with Clement, in the early tenth century, or by Archbishop Theophylact, around 1100. Disputed (D2). If used, say: "a Life of Clement, written in Bulgaria a generation or more later."
- *Russian Primary Chronicle*: compiled in Kiev about 230 years later, by 1116 (I3). Say: "Writing in Kiev more than two centuries later, the chroniclers..."

**Leave out:** the childhood dream of Sophia and the lost falcon (side stories); the Khazar mission and the cup inscription (side story, M3); the paralytic healed and other wonders at Rome (can stay only in VC's voice, and is not needed); Methodius's prophecies (VM 11); the whole Bible in six months (Contested, D5); "the Cyrillic alphabet" (D6); calling Cyril a bishop (D9); 24 years as bishop (D10); Constantine in Bulgaria (D11); Hadrian's letter and Stephen V's letter (both disputed, D4, D7); Borivoj's baptism (a modern guess); the Photius anecdote from Anastasius (card); the Life of Clement's attacks on Svatopluk's morals (polemic); exact years in Moravia (D8).

- **Do not say** (added from the round-two review, 2026-11-17): The Salzburg writer says Methodius had "partly" cheapened the Latin Masses in the people's eyes; never drop "partly".
