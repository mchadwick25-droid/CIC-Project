# Outline check: Cyril and Methodius

**Verdict: FIX**

I checked the outline on its own terms. I reopened the cited lines in VC, GZ (VM, CONV, J8, LCl and Ginzel's notes), PG, RPC and VCI. I made my own renderings of Q1–Q3 before I read the outline's.

## What holds

- All three quotes match the files word for word. The only changes are the sheet's stated ones: double spaces collapsed, line-end hyphens joined, footnote marks dropped, and "bebream" corrected to [hebream]. Q1 is at VC 20307–20310, Q2 at GZ 12251–12255 (the editor's notes are at 12286 and 12288), and Q3 at GZ 13974–13976.
- The Q1 and Q2 renderings keep every clause, add nothing, and keep one thought to a sentence. Q3 is sound too (see 7).
- Setting, thread, shape, length and reason are all supported. "Slavic letters, not Cyrillic" (D6) is handled correctly, and so is "do not call Cyril a bishop" (D9).
- The impact ranking holds. The heaviest item really is the disciples carrying the letters and books into Bulgaria (PG 98734–99594), and the defended item is marked. The Leave-out list is sound.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. Beat 6, the Salzburg complaint, says more than the text.** The outline says the Salzburg writer complained that Methodius "made the Latin Mass look worthless". The source (CONV, GZ 13641–13643 and 13662–13663) says *vilescere fecit cuncto populo ex parte missas et evangelia ecclesiasticumque officium illorum, qui hoc latine celebraverunt*. That means he made the Masses, Gospels and services of the Latin clergy seem cheap to the people, "in part". "Worthless" is stronger than *vilescere*, and *ex parte* has been dropped. Correction: "A Salzburg writer of his own day complained that this 'Greek', with 'newly invented Slavic letters', had partly cheapened in the people's eyes the Masses and Gospels of the priests who held them in Latin (M14)."

2. **BLOCKING. Beat 1 claims a cause the source contradicts.** The outline says he "was made librarian at Hagia Sophia, then taught philosophy, so he was called 'the Philosopher'". In VC the logothete already calls him *philosophe* (VC 19659). That comes before the librarian post (19674) and before the teaching chair (19678–19679). So the source gives no cause for the name, and its order of events rules this one out. Correction: delete "so". Write: "...then taught philosophy. The Lives call him 'Constantine the Philosopher' (S1, M1)."

3. **MINOR. Beat 1 uses a place name that misleads when heard.** "Mount Olympus" will make a listener think of the mountain in Greece. The sheet itself warns against this (M2, N4), and the held files do not say where the mountain is. Correction: add "Say 'a mountain of monks called Olympus'. Do not place it in Greece, and give no location (N4)."

4. **MINOR. Beat 7 misdates the ban.** The 879 letter says *iam litteris nostris, per Paulum episcopum Anconitanum tibi directis prohibuimus* (J8 13799–13802). That means "we have already forbidden it in our letter sent through Paul of Ancona". So the ban came before 879, and the 879 letter repeats it. Correction: "In 879 Pope John VIII summoned him and repeated an earlier ban on singing Mass in Slavonic. He could still preach in it (M16)."

5. **MINOR. Beat 7 is unclear about whose bishops.** It says "before his bishops". The source, *coram positis fratribus nostris episcopis* (J8 13902–13903), means the pope's own bishops. Correction: "after questioning him in front of the pope's own bishops".

6. **MINOR. Several beats carry no confidence label.** The template asks for one on every beat. Beat 4 has none. Beat 5 has none, and Hadrian as the pope who welcomed them is Contested (D3). Beat 6 has none. In beat 7, the death and funeral come from VM, but the beat is labelled only as the pope's letters. Corrections:
   - Beat 4: "Documented as VC's account."
   - Beat 5: "Documented as the Lives' account. Which pope: Contested (D3); VC names Hadrian."
   - Beat 6: "Documented as VM's account. The Salzburg complaint: written in his lifetime, by his opponents."
   - Beat 7: add "Death and funeral: Documented as VM's account. The year 885 comes from a modern historian (S4)."

7. **MINOR. Q3 drops a word.** The rendering leaves out *ipse* ("himself"), which carries the point. Correction: "...also himself created all the others..." or "...was the one who created all the others too...". Nothing else in Q3 needs to change.

8. **MINOR. Opening (a) uses a word whose modern sense misleads.** *Orationem scribere* (VC 20227) means to write out a text or words, not "a speech". Correction: "who can write words on water?" If the second half of the line is used, keep it: "and who wants to be called a heretic?" (VC 20228).

9. **MINOR. There are more quotes than the cap allows.** Item 1 under "What came of them" quotes a full sentence from the *Life of Clement*: "Clement handed all this down to us Bulgarians" (PG 99590–99594). Item 3 quotes a sentence from RPC. Together with Q1–Q3, that is past the cap of three. The PG line is also given with no Latin and no checked rendering. Its sense is "all that belongs to the Church... Clement handed down to us Bulgarians". Correction: turn both into marked paraphrase. Or, if the PG line is kept as a quote, give it a Q-number and remove another quote.

10. **MINOR. "Who tells it" has two loose dates.**
    - *Life of Clement*: "a Bulgarian follower of Clement or Archbishop Theophylact, around 1100" lets "around 1100" cover both possible authors. A follower who lived with Clement (died 916) would have written in the early 10th century (D2, PG 96729–96746). Correction: "either by a Bulgarian who had lived with Clement, in the early tenth century, or by Archbishop Theophylact, around 1100. Disputed (D2)."
    - The two Lives: "copies of the 15th century and later" is supported for the *Life of Constantine* (VCI 17951–17956: 1469 and 1479). For the *Life of Methodius*, Ginzel dates the Old Russian copy by its language to the 14th century (GZ 1551–1553). Correction: "kept only in copies made centuries later, from the 14th and 15th centuries on." The spoken line can stay as it is.

## Researcher's response

All ten findings accepted and applied; no disagreements. One correction to finding 10: Ginzel's 14th-century dating of the Old Russian text of VM is at GZ 1555–1556, not 1551–1553; the sheet and outline cite those lines.
