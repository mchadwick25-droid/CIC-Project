# Second Readings: cyril-and-methodius

## Release 50 fixes

No second readings existed for this piece, so every quotation in the brief is read here, with the new narrated paraphrases added by the fixes.

## 1. if they have letters for their language
Source: cic/texts/vita-constantini_die-legende-vom-heiligen-cyrillus-in-denkschriften-wien-19-chu-lat-deu_duemmler-miklosich1870.txt 20224-20225
Second rendering: Though I am tired and sick in body, I will go there gladly, if they have letters suited to their language.
Ours: if they have letters for their language.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Accomodatas" (fitted, suited) is carried by "for their language"; the brief gives the "gladly" in its own lead-in, and "tired" in "a tired scholar" (scio, te esse fatigatum, 20223).

## 2. who can write words on water
Source: cic/texts/vita-constantini_die-legende-vom-heiligen-cyrillus-in-denkschriften-wien-19-chu-lat-deu_duemmler-miklosich1870.txt 20227-20228
Second rendering: Who can write speech on water, and who wants to win himself the name of heretic?
Ours: who can write words on water, and who wants to be called a heretic?
Verdict: same
Reason: Same meaning; "be called a heretic" plainly carries "haeretici sibi nomen comparare".

## 3. Does God's rain not fall on all alike?
Source: cic/texts/vita-constantini_die-legende-vom-heiligen-cyrillus-in-denkschriften-wien-19-chu-lat-deu_duemmler-miklosich1870.txt 20307-20310
Second rendering: Does not God's rain fall equally on everyone? And does not the sun shine in the same way for all? Do we not all breathe the air equally? How then are you not ashamed to lay down three languages only, and order the other peoples and tribes to be blind and deaf?
Ours: Does God's rain not fall on all alike? Does the sun not shine the same on everyone? Do we not all breathe the air alike? Then how are you not ashamed to fix on three languages only? You would order every other people and tribe to be blind and deaf.
Verdict: same
Reason: Every clause is carried. "Fix on" for statuere and the split into a closing sentence ("You would order…") change nothing.

## 4. Look, brother, we were partners
Source: cic/texts/ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt 12251-12253
Second rendering: Look, brother, we were yoke-fellows, cutting a single furrow, and I fall in the field, now that I have finished my day.
Ours: Look, brother, we were partners ploughing a single furrow. And I fall in the field, having finished my day.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Consortes … unum sulcum imprimentes" is two oxen or ploughmen sharing one furrow; "partners ploughing a single furrow" carries it.

## 5. "Greek" … "newly invented Slavic letters" (quoted words and narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt 13641-13643, 13662-13663
Second rendering: until a certain Greek named Methodius, with newly invented Slavic letters, cleverly pushing aside the Latin language, the Roman teaching and the authoritative Latin letters, made the Masses, the Gospels and the church services of those who held them in Latin partly lose their worth in the eyes of all the people.
Ours: A Salzburg writer of his own day complained that this "Greek", with his "newly invented Slavic letters", had partly cheapened the Latin Masses in the people's eyes.
Verdict: minor
Reason: The quoted words are exact. The paraphrase keeps "partly" (ex parte) but narrows "missas et evangelia ecclesiasticumque officium" to "the Latin Masses", and leaves out "philosophice superducens" (cleverly pushing aside Latin). The complaint's force and its voice are kept; nothing is softened that the listener needs.

## 6. In 879 Pope John the Eighth repeated an earlier ban (new narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt 13799-13805
Second rendering: We also hear that you sing Mass in a barbarian, that is the Slavonic, tongue. So by our letter sent through Paul, bishop of Ancona, we have already forbidden you to celebrate the sacred Mass in that language, but in Latin or Greek … But you may preach or speak to the people in it.
Ours: In 879 Pope John the Eighth repeated an earlier ban on singing Mass in Slavic, though Methodius could still preach in it.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Iam … prohibuimus" names the earlier ban and the letter repeats it; "Predicare vero … tibi licet" is the preaching allowance. The letter's date (Indictione XII, June) fits 879.

## 7. In 880, John questioned him before his own bishops and found him sound (new narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt 13902-13913
Second rendering: We questioned this Methodius, your venerable archbishop, in the presence of our brother bishops, whether he believed the creed … and finding him orthodox and useful in all church teaching, we have sent him back to you.
Ours: In 880, John questioned him before his own bishops and found him sound.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Coram positis fratribus nostris episcopis" is the pope's bishops; "orthodoxum … reperientes" is "found him sound". On one hearing "his own" could be taken as Methodius's bishops; see Recheck_R50.md.

## 8. He allowed Mass in Slavic, with the Gospel read first in Latin (new narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt 13970-13979
Second rendering: Nothing in sound faith or teaching stands against singing Mass in this Slavonic tongue … We order, however, that in all the churches of your land, for greater honour, the Gospel be read in Latin, and afterwards be proclaimed in Slavonic translation to the people who do not understand the Latin words.
Ours: He allowed Mass in Slavic, with the Gospel read first in Latin.
Verdict: same
Reason: Both the permission and its condition are carried. The letter's further allowance (Latin Mass for Svatopluk and his court, if they prefer) is card material.

## 9. he who made the three chief languages
Source: cic/texts/ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt 13974-13976
Second rendering: for he who made the three principal languages, namely Hebrew, Greek and Latin, also himself created all the others, for his praise and glory.
Ours: he who made the three chief languages, Hebrew, Greek and Latin, also himself created all the others, for his praise and glory.
Verdict: same
Reason: Word for word with the Latin; "scilicet" (namely) is dropped.

## 10. the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue
Source: cic/texts/theophylact-ohrid_opera-t4-with-vita-clementis-pg126-grc-lat_migne1864.txt 99371-99372
Second rendering: and so Clement is appointed the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue.
Ours: the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue
Verdict: same
Reason: Word for word; "Bulgariez lingue" is the OCR for Bulgariae linguae.

## 11. most likely the script now called Glagolitic (new narrated claim)
Source: cic/texts/russian-primary-chronicle_cross1930.txt 5667-5672
Second rendering: Constantine did invent a Slavic alphabet early in his mission. It was not the so-called Cyrillic alphabet, but the more difficult Glagolitic, made from ninth-century Greek cursive.
Ours: Because the Slavs had no letters, Constantine made them: most likely the script now called Glagolitic, not the Cyrillic alphabet later named after him.
Verdict: same
Reason: Cross states it as conceded; Lützow (LUT 1337-1340) says Cyrillic, so the source sheet marks it Contested (D6). "Most likely" states the uncertainty once and plainly without presenting the point as settled. "Later named after him" rests on the census (CEN 42695).
