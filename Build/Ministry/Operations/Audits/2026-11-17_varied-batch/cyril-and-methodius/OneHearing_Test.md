# One-hearing test: Cyril and Methodius

Brief tested: `cyril-and-methodius.brief.txt` (440 words). Test: Mark's one-hearing test ("An interested non-specialist can understand who this was, what happened, and why the connection matters after one hearing"; "i don't want simpler"). Date: 2026-11-17.

## Step 1: three lines from one hearing

- **Who was this?** Two brothers from Thessalonica, a Greek city where Slavic was also spoken. Constantine (later called Cyril) was a scholar. His brother was Methodius. In the 860s the emperor in Constantinople sent them to Moravia, in today's Czech Republic.
- **What happened?** The Slavs had no letters, so Constantine made some, and the brothers turned the church's services into Slavic. Latin clergy said God was praised rightly only in Hebrew, Greek and Latin. Constantine argued back at Venice that God's rain falls on all alike. He died in Rome in 869. Methodius returned as a bishop, was held for two and a half years by Frankish bishops, and in 880 a pope wrote that God made all languages for his praise. He died in 885.
- **Why does the connection matter?** After he died, his disciples were driven out. Clement and Naum went to Bulgaria, where Clement became a bishop and wrote sermons in the people's tongue. Later the Kiev chroniclers said the letters were in use among the Rus and the Bulgarians too. On one hearing it took effort to be sure this "Clement" was a disciple and not the early bishop of Rome whose relics the brothers carried, and to work out that the disciples took the letters and the Slavic services with them.

### Where the brief loses it

Paragraph 7, sentence 2: "Clement and Naum crossed the Danube to Bulgaria, where, a later Life of Clement says, Prince Boris thanked God for them."

- Two paragraphs earlier the listener heard "Clement, an early bishop of Rome" whose relics the brothers carried. With no marker that this new Clement is one of the disciples, a hearer, especially one in a second language, can blur the two at the very moment the story hands on.
- The sentence says only that they *crossed*. It never says what they took with them. So the listener does not hear plainly that the letters and the Slavic services passed from Moravia to Bulgaria, which is the link the ending's Kiev line depends on.

### Smallest fix (outline only; clearer, not simpler)

Replace the start of that sentence:

> "Clement and Naum crossed the Danube to Bulgaria, where, ..."

with:

> "Two of them, Clement and Naum, carried the letters and the Slavic services across the Danube to Bulgaria, where, ..."
