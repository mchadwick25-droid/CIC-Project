# Review: Cyril and Methodius

Reviewer's read of `cyril-and-methodius.brief.txt`, judged on its own against `kit/Standard.md`, `kit/Shapes.md`, `Outline.md`, `Outline_Check.md` and `Source_Sheet.md`.

**Verdict: REVISE** (one BLOCKING finding)

## 1. Listener read (one pass, before anything else)

**Where I got lost or drifted**
- Paragraph 2 ends "The mission ... began with his question", and paragraph 3 then opens "The call came from Rastislav". The story steps back in time with no signal, and the question I just heard was itself an answer to that call. For a moment I wasn't sure which came first.
- Venice, then Rome. I am in Moravia, and then suddenly "At Venice Constantine answered the bishops", then "In Rome Constantine fell ill". Nothing says why they left Moravia or why they were going to Rome. On one hearing the journey reads as a jump cut.
- "The three on the sign Pilate put over the cross": a listener who doesn't know the Gospel detail can't tell which three languages these are until the pope names them two paragraphs later.
- "Frankish bishops held him for two and a half years": I heard that he was held, but not why, so the "though" linking it to his return as bishop carried no weight.

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down**
- Nowhere seriously. There are no runs of one-act sentences (4% short sentences), and no definitions are tacked on after a comma. The appositives ("a Greek city where Slavic was spoken too", "in what is now the Czech Republic") are the outline's spoken setting words, and each one does work.
- The thinnest stretch is paragraph 6. "Methodius went back as a bishop for the Slavs, though Frankish bishops held him for two and a half years" tells what happened without what was at stake: the claim to the land and the pope stepping in. It is flat, but it doesn't talk down.

## 2. Scores

Profiler: 434 words, grade 8.1 (names out 7.0), reading ease 75.7, 17.4 words a sentence, longest sentence 29 words: pass.

- **voice: 4.** The register is an adult narrator's throughout. Ideas are linked by cause and contrast ("Because the Slavs had no letters...", "Yet in 880..."). The weak links are the "began with his question" framing and the empty "though" in paragraph 6.
- **craft: 3.** It has a strong hook from opening (a), and the century, place and tradition all come within the first three paragraphs. It avoids the sender's-side trap: it gives the Moravians' request, the hostile Salzburg voice and the Bulgarians who received the work. But the impact is about a quarter of the story, not a third: the last paragraph is 76 of 434 words, and even counting the 880 ruling as "defended" it stays under a third. The stakes are thin at the detention, and at Rome, where the story never says why they went. At 434 words it also runs past the outline's "about 380", close to the "do not stretch to 3" line.
- **rigor: 3.** The brief is otherwise well grounded. The quotes match the outline's renderings and the source files, the wonder stays in the followers' voice, and the "Who tells it" line is spoken as the outline gives it. But it reverses a correction the outline check made, on the Salzburg complaint (finding 1). There are smaller narrowings at the Kiev ending and over who drove the disciples out.
- **accessibility: 4.** Grade 8.1 and reading ease 75.7 are well inside the limits, and the narration doesn't drop into short acts. One hearing is mostly enough. The exceptions are the unexplained move to Venice and Rome, and the three unnamed languages.

## 3. Trace against the outline, and the quote check

All three quotes were checked word for word against `cic/texts/`. Q1 is at VC 20307–20310 (`vita-constantini_..._duemmler-miklosich1870.txt`), Q2 at GZ 12251–12255 and Q3 at GZ 13974–13976 (`ginzel_..._1861.txt`). The Latin matches the outline, and the brief's English matches the outline's renderings. Q2 stops after "having finished my day", which is a clean cut of the outline's rendering with nothing changed. Opening (a) matches VC 20224–20228 and the corrected outline wording ("write words on water"). The Salzburg phrases "Greek" and "newly invented Slavic letters" match CONV (*quidam graecus*, *noviter inventis sclavinis litteris*, GZ 13641–13642).

**The writer's three flagged calls**
- **Cyril named once before Rome: accepted, no finding.** The outline's own tradition line requires "the mission of Cyril and Methodius" in the setting. A listener who hears "Cyril" there with no link to Constantine would be lost. "Remembered by his later name, Cyril" keeps what the instruction protects: it doesn't suggest he was called Cyril during the mission. The Rome sentence then gives the name its true place ("took the monk's name Cyril").
- **Wiching left unnamed: accepted, no finding.** He is not a witness, and the listener needs only his relation to Methodius, which "a man he had condemned" gives. See finding 4 on the verb that follows.
- **885 stated plainly: accepted, no finding.** The outline doesn't mark 885 as uncertain. It records where the year comes from, and the sheet treats the AD years as Widely Accepted (S4). 869 rests on the same modern conversion and is also stated plainly. Under the Standard, scholars and editions go on the card, not in the story.

## 4. Findings

1. **BLOCKING. The Salzburg complaint says more than the source, and undoes an outline correction.** The brief says the Salzburg writer complained that Methodius "had cheapened the Latin Masses in the people's eyes". The source says *vilescere fecit cuncto populo ex parte missas et evangelia ...* (CONV, GZ 13662–13663), so the cheapening was "in part". Outline_Check finding 1 (BLOCKING) required exactly this qualifier, and the outline's beat 6 now reads "had partly cheapened in the people's eyes the Masses and Gospels of the priests who held them in Latin". Dropping "partly" turns a qualified complaint into a sweeping one. **Change:** restore the qualifier. For example: "...had partly cheapened, in the people's eyes, the Masses and Gospels of the priests who said them in Latin." Or keep it shorter: "...had partly cheapened the Latin Masses in the people's eyes."

2. **MINOR. The journey to Venice and Rome has no reason, so the listener drifts.** The outline gives the reason for Rome: they carried the relics of Clement, Pope Hadrian came out to meet them, the Slavonic books were laid on an altar and the liturgy was sung in St Peter's (beat 5). That is also the moment when the argument won its first hearing. **Change:** give one clause for why they travelled, or one sentence of the Rome welcome, before "Constantine fell ill". To stay inside length, take the words from finding 5's trims.

3. **MINOR. The time order steps back between paragraphs 2 and 3.** "The mission ... began with his question" is followed by "The call came from Rastislav", which happened first. **Change:** make the order explicit ("That question answered a call from Rastislav of Moravia..."), or end paragraph 2 on the tradition line without "began with his question".

4. **MINOR. "Drove his disciples out" makes Wiching the agent, which the outline doesn't.** The outline says Wiching "took the see, and the disciples were driven out and scattered" (I1). The source gives the opponents as a group: they removed Gorazd, set Wiching up, and the disciples "metu coacti" scattered (LCl 12970–12974, PG 98730–98736). **Change:** "...took his place as bishop, and his disciples were driven out." (The outline's wording also lets you drop "his place", since Gorazd came between.)

5. **MINOR. The impact runs short of a third, and the brief runs long.** The impact is about a quarter of 434 words, against the outline's "about 380" and "about a third". **Change:** trim the setting and moment beats, for example the Rastislav sentence or the detention line. Spend part of the saving on the Bulgaria or Kiev ending, for instance "the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue" (I1). Keep the total near 380.

6. **MINOR. The Kiev ending narrows the outline's paraphrase.** The brief says the letters were "first made for the Moravians". The outline's item 3, as the Outline_Check recheck corrected it, says "first made for the Moravians and their neighbouring Slavs" (RPC 7866–7873). In the Chronicle those neighbours include the Polyanians, "now called Russes", which is the point of the Rus claim. **Change:** "first made for the Moravians and the Slavs around them".

7. **MINOR. Boris's thanks come from a much later source but are told as plain fact.** The Bulgaria material is the *Life of Clement*'s account, and its date and author are disputed (D2). The Standard says to name a witness for "a source written much later", and the outline supplies the line: "a Life of Clement, written in Bulgaria a generation or more later." **Change:** attribute the Boris clause briefly, using the outline's words.

## Recheck

This is a targeted recheck of the revised brief (437 words). I looked only at what changed, against findings 1–7 and the outline. Profiler: grade 8.2 (names out 7.1), reading ease 75.2, longest sentence 28 words: pass.

**Final verdict: PASS**

- **Finding 1 (BLOCKING): resolved.** The brief now says "had partly cheapened the Latin Masses in the people's eyes". That matches CONV *ex parte* (GZ 13662–13663) and outline beat 6.
- **Finding 2: resolved.** "In Rome the pope came out to meet them, because they carried relics of Clement, an early bishop of Rome" matches beat 5 (M10). Leaving the pope unnamed keeps clear of the contested question of which pope it was (D3).
- **Finding 3: resolved.** The opening now runs in time order: Thessalonica, then the emperor sends them to Moravia, whose ruler had asked for teachers in his people's own language. The "So" is supported, because VM 12212–12213 gives Thessalonica's Slavic speech as the reason the brothers were chosen (beat 2, S6). The phrase "Constantine made them, and so began the mission" leaves out the claim that God revealed the letters. Leaving it out is allowed, and the making of the letters is Widely Accepted.
- **Finding 4: resolved.** "A man he had condemned became bishop, and his disciples were driven out" matches I1 (LCl 12970–12974).
- **Finding 6: resolved.** "First made for the Moravians and their neighbouring Slavs" matches impact item 3 (RPC 7866–7873).
- **Finding 7: resolved.** "A later Life of Clement says" now credits Boris's thanks to the Life of Clement. The credit also covers the next sentence. In that sentence, "the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue" is the outline's own phrase (I1, PG 99370–99372, *Bulgariae linguae Clemens primus constituitur episcopus*). It is a short phrase and stays within the quote cap.
- **Finding 5:** this is withdrawn for length, per the coordinator's ruling. The impact share now gets close enough to a third.
- **Unchanged text:** "Latin clergy said God had chosen only Hebrew, Greek and Latin" and "Later, at Venice, Constantine answered such bishops" both trace to beats 3–4. Q1, Q2 and Q3 are unchanged and still match the source files.

No wording in the brief is unsupported, misquoted or past a limit, and none reads as a children's book.
