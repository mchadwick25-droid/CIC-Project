# Source sheet: Cyril and Methodius

Researcher's sheet for the 2026-11-17 varied batch, era 4. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

**Files and short names used below** (all in `cic/texts/` unless stated):

- **GZ** = `ginzel_geschichte-der-slawenapostel-cyrill-und-method-deu-lat_1861.txt` (Ginzel 1861). - **PG** = `theophylact-ohrid_opera-t4-with-vita-clementis-pg126-grc-lat_migne1864.txt` (the full *Life of Clement of Ohrid*, Migne's Latin, from 96725)
- **RPC** = `russian-primary-chronicle_cross1930.txt` (*Russian Primary Chronicle*, English tr. S. H. Cross, with his introduction)
- **LUT** = `lutzow_bohemia-historical-sketch_1920.txt` (Lützow, *Bohemia*, 1910/1920)
- **AS** = `acta-sanctorum_martii-tomus-2-lat_palme1865.txt` (Bollandists, March vol. II)
- **TH** = `theiner_vetera-monumenta-slavorum-meridionalium-v1-lat_1863.txt` (Theiner's dedication, 1863)
- **CEN** = `cic-website/data/world-census.json` (not in `cic/texts/`; setting and spoken name only)

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own. Latin quotes keep the OCR's spelling, with obvious OCR faults corrected in [brackets]. I collapsed the OCR's double spaces, joined words split across line ends, and left out footnote markers and stray marks; nothing else is changed. Editors' chapter numbers (VC "XIV", VM "5") are used for reference; chapter summaries in GZ (e.g. the heading of J8 no. 5) are Ginzel's, not the authors'.

**Languages.** Neither brother's own writing is held. The two Lives were written in Church Slavonic; the Library holds **no readable Slavonic** (VC's Slavonic is unreadable OCR; VM is held only in Latin). So every quote from VC and VM is from a 19th-century Latin translation, and any rendering is made from that Latin. John VIII, Anastasius and the Salzburg account wrote in Latin; those are originals. The Life of Clement was written in Greek; PG's Greek is badly garbled, so it is read in Migne's Latin.

**Witness kinds used.** OWN = nothing held (see N1). CONTEMP = writers who met them or wrote in their lifetime: John VIII's letters (papal register copies), Anastasius the Librarian, the Salzburg *Conversio*, and the Italian Legend if it is Gauderich's. VC and VM are the **mission's own memory**: their editors judged them the work of well-informed contemporaries or disciples (D1), but they survive only in copies made centuries later (VC: 1469 and 1479, VCI 17951–17956; VM: an Old Russian text Ginzel dates by its language to the 14th century, GZ 1555–1556), and Ginzel disputes VM. They are marked CONTEMP-disputed. LATER = the *Life of Clement* (a generation to two centuries later, D2), the *Russian Primary Chronicle* (compiled by 1116, about 230 years later). Modern = Cross (1930), Lützow (1910), Dümmler, Ginzel: used only for dates and judgements, never for story events.

**Time gaps.** Rough gaps rest on the dates in S4, which come from the modern writers in these files.

## 1. Setting

S1. **Names.** VC calls him "Constantinus philosophus" and heads its text "Vita beati doctoris nostri Constantini philosophi, primi institutoris slovenicae gentis" (VC 19555–19556). He took the name Cyril only at the end: "indidit sibi nomen Cyrilli" (VC 20414; also LI 11478, "imposuit sibi nomen Cyrillum"). Methodius is "Methodius" throughout (VM 11986–11987, "Vita beati patris nostri et doctoris Methodii archiepiscopi Moravici"). Documented. Note for the writer: through most of the story he is Constantine; "Cyril" is his monk's name, taken in Rome weeks before he died.
S2. **Home city.** Their father Leo was an officer at Thessalonica: "In Thessalonicensi urbe vir erat quidam, ingenuus et dives, nomine Leo, obtinens dignitatem drungarii sub stratego" (VC 19577–19578). Constantine was the youngest of seven: "genuit Septem liberos, quorum minimus, septimus, Constantinus philosophus erat" (VC 19579–19580). The emperor tells them "vos estis Thessalonicenses, Thessalonicenses vero omnes pure Slovenice loquuntur" (VM 12212–12213). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as the Lives' account; the seven children is the kind of detail VCI flags for its "Vorliebe für die Zahl sieben" (VCI 18159). Lützow glosses Solun as "The present Salonike" (LUT 1330, footnote at 1364). Plain: "Thessalonica, a Greek city where Slavic was spoken too."
S3. **Where the work happened.** Constantinople (their schooling and the emperor's court, VC IV, XIV); Moravia, ruled by Rastislav (VC 20217; VM 12201); Pannonia, ruled by Kocel (VC 20286; VM 12259); Venice (VC 20292); Rome (VC 20379). The census places the tradition in "Moravia, Bulgaria, Ohrid" and lists the Czech Republic and Bulgaria as the modern countries (CEN 42612–42630). Plain: "Moravia, in central Europe, in what is now the Czech Republic."
S4. **Century and years.** The Lives date by the year of the world: Constantine died "mensis februarii die XIV, indictione II, a creatione totius mundi anno 6377" (VC 20430–20431); the *Life of Clement* gives Bulgaria's conversion as 6377, which Ginzel's note turns into "octingentesimo sexagesimo nono a Christo nato" (LCl 12810–12812, note 12844). Cross: "their journey of 867 from Moravia to Rome, where Constantine died on February 14, 869" (RPC 5655–5656). Lützow: "In 862 the Moravian Prince Rostislav ... sent a mission to the court of the Greek Emperor at Constantinople" (LUT 1297–1301); "In 885 Methodius, 'the apostle of the Slavs,' died" (LUT 1479). The census gives 863 for the sending (CEN 42615, 42653). VM's own year for Methodius's death is garbled in the OCR ("sexta die mensis Aprilis tertia indictione ... trecentesimo nonagesimo tertio", VM 12540–12541). Widely Accepted for "the ninth century" and "the 860s"; the exact AD years rest on the modern writers (Cross, Lützow), not on any 9th-century AD date. The embassy year differs between LUT (862) and CEN (863): say "the 860s".
S5. **Tradition.** Census name **Cyrillo-Methodian Slavic Christianity**, informally "The Mission of Cyril and Methodius" (CEN 42612, 42614). Plain spoken forms: (1) "the mission of Cyril and Methodius" (CEN 42614); (2) "the Slavic church that grew from their mission" (CEN 42653: disciples "carried the work south to Ohrid and Preslav"); (3) the Lives' own self-description, a people "cujus nemo unquam curam gessit" (VM 12126), "whom no one had ever cared for". Do not say "Cyrillo-Methodian" aloud. Do not call it "the Orthodox Church" or "the Catholic Church": the papal letters and the Byzantine court both claimed it (J8, VC XIV).
S6. **The question they faced.** Moravia's request: "intraverunt ad nos doctores multi christiani ex Italia et ex Graecia et ex Germania, docentes nos contrario modo, verum nos Sloveni simplices homines sumus neque habemus quempiam, qui nos in veritate instituat et sensum (scripturae) interpretetur" (VM 12203–12207). VC's version: "doctorem non habemus, qui nos nostra lingua veram fidem christianam edoceat" (VC 20219–20220). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as the Lives' account. Lützow quotes the VC version in English and thinks it "express[es] the true purpose of Rostislav", while adding a political motive, freedom from "the Frankish hierarchy" (LUT 1304–1324). Modern view, card only.

## 2. Recorded moments

M1. **Constantine's schooling.** In Constantinople he learned "Homerum et geometriam et apud Leonem et apud Photium dialecticam et omnes disciplinas philosophicas" (VC 19642–19643). Asked what philosophy is: "divinarum, inquit, et humanarum rerum cognitio, ut, quantum homo potest, deo appropinquet, et docet hominem operibus similem fieri creatori" (VC 19660–19662). Made librarian to the patriarch at Hagia Sophia, "ut sit bibliothecarius apud patriarcham in sancta Sophia" (VC 19674), he hid in a monastery, and then accepted a chair to "doceret indigenas et peregrinos philosophiam" (VC 19676–19679). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VC's account.
M2. **Methodius: governor, then monk.** The emperor made him govern a Slavic principality: "imperator, sagacitate ejus cognita, principatum Slovenicum eum tenere juberet" (VM 12150–12151, OCR "priVicipa/wm Shvcnicum ... teuere"). After many years he left it and "ivit in Olympum, ubi sancti patres vivunt", took the black habit and gave himself to books (VM 12158–12165). Constantine later joined him there: "in Olympum autem profectus ad Methodium, fratrem suum, ibi habitare coepit" (VC 19822). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as the Lives' account. Note: Olympus here is the monks' mountain in Asia Minor, not Greece's; the held files do not say where it is (N4).
M3. **The Khazar mission.** Both brothers went to the Khazars, where "erant enim ibi Judaei, qui christianam religionem admodum blasphemabant"; Methodius said "Paratus sum pro christiana religione mori" and served his younger brother "velut servus" (VM 12169–12175). VC tells it at length (VC VIII–XII, 19825–20203). Side story; see Leave out.
M4. **"If they have letters for their language."** Asked to go to Moravia, Constantine answered: "licet fatigatus sim et aeger corpore, tamen laetus illuc ibo, si litteras habent linguae suae accomodatas" (VC 20224–20225). The emperor: his grandfather and father had looked and not found them. Constantine: "quis potest in aqua orationem scribere, et quis vult haeretici sibi nomen comparare?" (VC 20227–20228). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VC's account. (The point: to preach without written letters is like writing on water, and to invent letters risks being called a heretic.)
M5. **The letters are made.** WONDER claim, VC's voice: after prayer "mox autem deus ei apparuit ... et illico composuit litteras et orationem evangelicam scribere coepit: in principio erat verbum, et verbum erat apud deum, et deus erat verbum" (VC 20231–20233). VM's voice: "Ibique manifestavit Deus philosopho Slovenicas litteras [OCR 'liiieras'], et illico litteris formatis et sermone composito cum Methodio iter aggressus est Moravicum" (VM 12217–12219). LI says the Moravians had heard they brought "Evangelium in illorum linguam a Philosopho praedicto translatum" (LI 11419–11420). CONTEMP-disputed. The making of the letters: Widely Accepted (Cross: "It is, on the other hand, conceded that Constantine, at some early stage of his mission, did invent a Slavic alphabet", RPC 5666–5668). The revelation: the witnesses' claim. Which alphabet: Contested (D6).
M6. **Teaching in Moravia.** "cum magno honore excepit eum Rastislavus, et discipulos collectos tradidit ei erudiendos . mox vero totum ordinem ecclesiasticum vertit et edocuit eos officium matutinum, horas et vesperas et breve officium vespertinum et sacramentorum officium" (VC 20256–20259); "aures surdorum ... audierunt verba scripturae sanctae, et plana facta est Lingua balborum" (VC 20259–20260). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VC's account. The record from the receiving side is thin: the Moravians' own words exist only as the Lives report the embassy (S6). LI: they taught "parvulos eorum litteras", and left behind "scripta ... omnia, quae ad Ecclesiae ministerium videbantur esse necessaria" (LI 11423–11430).
M7. **The three-language objection in Moravia.** Opponents said God "tres tantum linguas elegit: hebraicam, graecam, latinam, quibus fas est laudare deum. erant autem qui talia loquebantur, clerici latini, archipresbyteri, sacerdotes et discipuli" (VC 20264–20266). Constantine called them "trium linguarum cultores (et Pilaticos), quoniam Pilatus ita scripsit in titulo domini" (VC 20267–20268). The reference: the sign on the cross was written in Hebrew, Greek and Latin (VM gives the same, VM 12231–12234, "secundum titulum Pilati"). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as the Lives' account.
M8. **Kocel and the captives.** On the way to Rome, Kocel of Pannonia "adamatis admodum slovenicis litteris ita, ut eas edisceret, tradidit ei ad quinquaginta discipulos" (VC 20286–20287). From neither prince did Constantine take gold or silver, "captivos tantum nongentos ab ambobus impetravit et dimisit" (VC 20288–20290). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VC's account.
M9. **Venice.** "Venetiis vero cum esset, convenerunt contra eum latini episcopi et presbyteri et monachi, sicut corvi contra falconem" (VC 20292–20293). They asked how he could make letters "quae nemo alius antea invenit, neque apostoli, neque papa romanus, neque Gregorius theologus, neque Hieronymus, neque Augustinus" (VC 20304–20306). His answer: see Q1 (VC 20307–20311). He names peoples who already praise God in their own letters: "Armenos, Persas, Abasgos, Iberos, Sugdos, Gotthos, Avares, Tyrsos, Kozaros, Arabes, Aegyptios, Syros" (VC 20313–20314), and then quotes the Psalms, the Gospels and 1 Corinthians 14 at length (VC 20315–20377). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VC's account.
M10. **Rome.** Pope Hadrian came out to meet them "cum omnibus civibus, cereos ferentes, quia sancti Clementis quoque reliquias ferebat" (VC 20380–20381). (Clement was an early bishop of Rome; Constantine had found relics believed to be his near Cherson: LI 11241–11375.) WONDER claim, VC's voice: "nam paralyticus ibi sanatus est" (VC 20382). "papa autem libros slovenicos consecratos deposuit in ecclesia sanctae Mariae, quae dicitur Phatne, et super eos sanctam liturgiam cecinerunt" (VC 20384–20385). Two bishops, "Formosum et Gondricum", ordained the Slavic disciples, who then sang the liturgy "in ecclesia sancti apostoli Petri slovenica lingua" (VC 20385–20387), helped by "Arsenio episcopo ... et ab Anastasio bibliothecario" (VC 20390–20391). VM: the pope "sanxit doctrinam amborum, evangelio Slovenico in altari sancti apostoli Petri deposito, et ordinavit presbyterum beatum Methodium" and condemned the "trilingues" (VM 12226–12234). CONTEMP-disputed; LCl (LATER) tells the same laying of books on the altar, "Libros versos divino altari imponit" (LCl 12684–12685). Documented as the Lives' account. Which pope: D3.
M11. **Constantine becomes Cyril and dies.** Ill, he took the monk's habit "et lucem luci addens indidit sibi nomen Cyrilli . et in hoc habitu permansit quinquaginta dies" (VC 20413–20414). Before that: "ab hoc tempore non sum servus neque imperatoris neque hominis cuiuspiam in terra, sed dei tantum omnipotentis" (VC 20411–20412). His prayer asks God to "perde trium linguarum haeresim" (VC 20421). He died "quadraginta duos annos natus" (VC 20429–20430). VM gives his last words to Methodius: Q2 (VM 12250–12255). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as the Lives' account. LI says forty days, not fifty (LI 11479), and the date "sexto decimo Kalendas Martias" (LI 11480), i.e. 14 February, as VC.
M12. **The burial dispute.** Methodius asked to take the body home: "mater nos obtestata est, ut, uter e nobis prius moreretur, alter afferret eum in monasterium sui fratris, et ibi eum sepeliret" (VC 20433–20435). The Roman bishops objected; the pope offered his own tomb in St Peter's; Methodius asked instead "recondatur in ecclesia sancti Clementis, quocum huc venit" (VC 20436–20441). Buried "ad dexteram partem altaris in ecclesia sancti Clementis" (VC 20445). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VC's account; LI tells the same request and burial (LI 11490–11550, partly garbled).
M13. **Methodius sent as bishop.** Kocel asked the pope for him; the pope: "Non tibi tantum, sed omnibus partibus illis Slovenicis mitto illum magistrum a Deo et a sancto apostolo Petro" (VM 12259–12262). A letter follows (HAD; D4). Kocel sent him back to Rome "ut illum ordinaret episcopum in Pannonia in sedem sancti Andronici apostoli, qui ex septuaginta fuit" (VM 12319–12322). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VM's account. A Roman letter of 875 confirms he had been ordained by the apostolic see for the Pannonians: "liceat fratri nostro Methodio, qui illic a sede apostolica ordinatus est, secundum priscam consuetudinem libere, quae sunt episcopi, gerere" (J8 no. 1, 13737–13740). CONTEMP. Documented.
M14. **Arrest by the Frankish bishops.** The Moravian [Frankish] king and his bishops said: "In nostro (inquiunt) imperio doces". Methodius: "Ego quoque, si intelligerem, vestrum id esse, abscederem, sed sancti Petri est" (VM 12327–12330), and warned them "cavete ne ferreum montem osseo vertice pertundere conati cerebrum vestrum effundatis" (VM 12332–12333). "illum vero miserunt in Suevos et detinebant annos duos et dimidium" (VM 12358–12359). The pope laid an interdict on them until they let him go: "anathema contra illos misit, ne ullus regis episcopus caneret missas id est officia, dum eum detinerent" (VM 12363–12365). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VM's account. (The VM text has "hostis Moravici regis", VM 12327: the enemy of the Moravian ruler, i.e. the Frankish king.) The hostile side: the Salzburg *Conversio*, in his lifetime, complains that "quidam graecus, methodius nomine, noviter inventis sclavinis litteris, linguam latinam doctrinamque romanam atque litteras auctorales latinas philosophice superducens, vilescere fecit cuncto populo ex parte missas et evangelia ecclesiasticumque officium illorum, qui hoc latine celebraverunt" (CONV 13641–13643, 13662–13663; OCR "methodms", "Hlieris", "vilescerc fecil cunclo"). CONTEMP (hostile). Documented as Salzburg's complaint.
M15. **Archbishop in Moravia.** The Moravians expelled the German priests and asked Rome: "Quoniam antea patres nostri baptisma a sancto Petro jam acceperunt, da nobis Methodium archiepiscopum et doctorem" (VM 12369–12373). Svatopluk "commendavit ei omnes ecclesias et clericos in omnibus oppidis" (VM 12374–12376). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VM's account.
M16. **John VIII: summons and ban, 879.** "Audimus etiam, quod missas cantes in barbara, hoc est in sclavina lingua. Unde iam litteris nostris, per Paulum episcopum Anconitanum tibi directis prohibuimus, ne in ea lingua sacra missarum solempnia celebrares, sed vel in latina, vel greca lingua" (J8 no. 3, 13799–13802): the ban was already sent earlier, by Paul of Ancona, and this letter repeats it; while "Predicare vero, aut sermonem in populo facere tibi licet" (13804–13805). Dated "XVIII. Kalendas Julii, Indictione XII" (13807–13808), Ginzel's heading 879 (13786). CONTEMP. Documented.
M17. **John VIII: approval, 880.** After questioning him "coram positis fratribus nostris episcopis" (J8 no. 5, 13902–13903), the pope found him "in omnibus ecclesiasticis doctrinis et utilitatibus orthodoxum et proficuum" (13915–13916), confirmed his archbishopric (13918–13920), and wrote: "Litteras denique sclaviniscas a Constantino quondam philosopho repertas, quibus deo laudes debite resonent, jure laudamus; et in eadem lingua Christi domini nostri preconia et opera enarrentur jubemus" (13948–13961); "Nec sanae fidei vel doctrinae aliquid obstat, sive missas in eadem sclavinica lingua canere, sive sacrum evangelium vel lectiones divinas novi et veteris testamenti bene translatas et interpretatas legere aut alia horarum officia omnia psallere" (13970–13973); and Q3 (13974–13976). With a condition: the gospel read "latine" first, then in Slavonic "in auribus populi, latina verba non intelligentis" (13976–13980), and Latin Mass for the prince if he prefers it (13980–13982). Dated "mense Junio, indictione XIII" (13982), Ginzel 880 (13877). Same letter consecrates "Vichinum" (Wiching) bishop of Nitra under Methodius (13927–13930). CONTEMP. Documented.
M18. **John VIII: consolation, 881.** The pope praises "orthodoxe fidei te cultorem strenuum" and denies sending other secret letters to Svatopluk: "neque alie littere nostre ad eum directe sunt" (J8 no. 6, 13994–14023). "de aliis temptationibus, quas diverso modo perpessus es, noli tristari" (14041–14043). CONTEMP. Documented.
M19. **Visit to Constantinople.** The emperor wrote, "Pater venerande valde desidero te videre" (VM 12451–12452), received him "cum magno honore et gaudio", "atque ex discipulis ejus presbyterum et diaconum cum libris retinuit" (VM 12454–12457). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VM's account.
M20. **Translation of the Bible.** With two priests "qui valde velociter scribebant, vertit brevi tempore omnes libros (scripturae) plene, exceptis Maccabaeis, ex Graeca lingua in Slovenicam, intra sex menses, a Martio mense inchoans usque ad vicesimam sextam diem Octobris mensis" (VM 12472–12477). "Psalterium enim tantum et evangelium cum apostolo et electis officiis ecclesiasticis cum philosopho antea converterat. Tunc nomocanonem quoque id est regulam legis et patericon transtulit" (VM 12480–12483). (Nomocanon: the church's law book; patericon: sayings of the desert monks.) CONTEMP-disputed. The earlier work with Constantine (Psalter, Gospel, Apostle, chosen offices): Widely Accepted. The whole Bible in six months: Contested (D5).
M21. **Successor and death.** Asked whom he named: "Gorazd ... Hic est vestrae patriae vir ingenuus atque in Latinis libris apprime eruditus et orthodoxus" (VM 12514–12518). On Palm Sunday he blessed the people and said "Custodite me, proles, usque ad tertium diem" (VM 12520–12522); he died "sexta die mensis Aprilis" (VM 12540). "Discipuli ejus ... officium ecclesiasticum Latine, Graece, Slovenice instituerunt" and buried him "in synodali ecclesia" (VM 12543–12545). Mourners came, "viri et mulieres, parvi et magni, divites et pauperes, liberi et servi, viduae et orphani, peregrini et domestici, infirmi et sani" (VM 12548–12551). CONTEMP-disputed. Documented as VM's account. Year: LUT 885 (S4).

## 3. What came of them (heaviest first)

I1. **The letters and books went on with their disciples, to Bulgaria.** After Methodius died, his opponents removed Gorazd and set up Wiching, "a Methodio anathemati Satanae traditum" (LCl 12972–12974). The disciples scattered: "alius alio sunt dispersi, Deo ita disponente, ut plures regiones Evangelii beneficio dotarent" (PG 98734–98736). Clement, Naum and Angelarius crossed the Danube to Belgrade, and the governor sent them on to Prince Boris of Bulgaria (PG 98738–98739, 98960–98972), who thanked God "quod tales ipsius adoratores Bulgariae benefactores misisset" (PG 98976–98977). Boris gave Clement houses and "ad Achridam et Glavenitzam" places of his own (PG 99117–99121). Clement became the first bishop for the Bulgarian tongue: "et sic Bulgariae linguae Clemens primus constituitur episcopus" (PG 99370–99372). He wrote "sermones ... simplices, perspicuos" for every feast, because Bulgarian priests "difficile intelligere Graeca" (PG 99550–99560); "omnia ad Ecclesiam pertinentia ... haec Clemens nobis Bulgaris tradidit" (PG 99590–99594). LATER (D2). Documented as the *Life of Clement*'s account; the move to Ohrid and Preslav is also the census's summary (CEN 42653). Carried on and used.
I2. **DEFENDED: God may be praised in every language.** This was the brothers' own fight in the Lives (M7, M9, M11's prayer) and the point a pope wrote into his letter (M17, Q3). The *Russian Primary Chronicle*, compiled in Kiev about 230 years later, repeats the pope's rebuke: "Whosoever condemns the Slavic writing shall be excluded from the Church until he mend his ways. For such men are not sheep but wolves" (RPC 7912–7914). LATER. Documented as what later witnesses carried on. The defence was not settled at once: John VIII forbade Slavonic Mass in 879 before allowing it in 880 (M16–M17), and a letter under Stephen V's name later spoke against Methodius (D7).
I3. **The Rus took the letters as their own.** "It was for these peoples that Moravian books were first written. They are also termed Slavic writing. This writing exists also in Rus and among the Danubian Bulgarians" (RPC 7870–7873). The Chronicle calls Methodius successor of Andronicus, "the apostle of the Slavic race", and ends: "the Slavs and the Russes are one people" (RPC 7931–7940). LATER (about 230 years; Sylvester's colophon "in the year 6624 (1116)", RPC 4384–4385). Documented as the Chronicle's claim; Cross says the Chronicle's account "is based upon the so-called Pannonian Life of Methodius" (RPC 5613–5615). The census adds that the Slavonic books "passed to Kievan Rus', and on to the Russian, Ukrainian, Serbian and Bulgarian churches" (CEN 42695) (census summary, card only).
I4. **Kept their memory and feast.** In Rome: "imagine eius picta super sepulchrum eius coeperunt lumina alere super eum dies noctesque" (VC 20447). The Slavonic book keeps Methodius's day: "Mensis Aprilis VI. die Commemoratio et Vita" (VM 11986). Olomouc, 1380: the bishop ordered their feast kept "VII. Idus Martii", calling them "beatissimi et gloriosissimi confessores Christi et episcopi Cyrillus et Methodius, Patres, Apostoli et Patroni nostri praecipui" (GZ 1532–1538, in Ginzel's footnote; OCR garbled around it). LATER (about 500 years). In 1863 Theiner writes of the "solemnia in memoriam millenariam sanctorum Cyrilli et Methodii, gentium Tuarum Apostolorum, quam hoc anno Ecclesia celebrat" (TH 157–159). Documented.
I5. **A church order in Moravia.** VM: "Ab isto tempore coepit doctrina Dei valde crescere et tonsi multiplicari in omnibus civitatibus" (VM 12376–12377). LCl: at his death he left "ducentos" priests, deacons and subdeacons in his diocese (LCl 12954–12956). Documented as the Lives' claims; the numbers are not checkable. The Moravian church itself did not last under Wiching (I1).

## 4. Links to other people

- **Photius** (later patriarch): taught Constantine, per VC ("apud Photium dialecticam", VC 19643). Anastasius calls Constantine Photius's "fortissimo ejus amico" who rebuked him (ANA 13007–13008). Met / heard him teach.
- **Anastasius the Librarian** (Rome): helped at the Slavonic liturgy (VC 20391); wrote of Constantine in 869 and 875 as "vir magnus et apostolicae sedis [or vitae] praeceptor Constantinus Philosophus" (ANA 13041–13042). Met; wrote of him.
- **Pope Nicholas I**: summoned them (VM 12226; LI 11434–11436). Wrote to.
- **Pope Hadrian II**: received them, laid the books on the altar, ordained Methodius (VC 20380–20385; LI 11446). Met.
- **Pope John VIII**: summoned, examined and defended Methodius (J8). Met; wrote to.
- **Formosus and Gauderich (Gondricus)**: ordained the disciples (VC 20385). Gauderich of Velletri is the Bollandists' suspected author of the Italian Legend, addressed "Joanni" [John VIII] (AS 17308–17318). Met; wrote his story.
- **Emperor Michael III** and Bardas: sent the brothers (VM 12209–12212; VC 20222, 20228).
- **Rastislav, Kocel, Svatopluk**: the rulers who asked for and received them (VC 20217, 20286; VM 12201, 12374).
- **Clement of Rome**: they carried his relics to Rome; Cyril is buried in his church (VC 20381, 20441). Kept his relics.
- **Gregory the Theologian [of Nazianzus]**: Constantine memorised his books and wrote him a praise poem: "memoriae mandans libros sancti Gregorii theologi ... et encomium sancto Gregorio scripsit" (VC 19618–19619). Read his books.
- **Gorazd, Clement, Naum, Angelarius, Sava**: disciples, "Gorasdus et Clemens et Naum et Angelarius et Sabbas" (LCl 12660–12661). Heard them teach; carried their work.
- **Wiching**: bishop of Nitra under Methodius (J8 13927–13929), later his opponent (LCl 12972). Opposed.
- **Boris of Bulgaria**: received the disciples (PG 98966–98977). Received their disciples.
- **Ludmila and Borivoj of Bohemia**: Lützow thinks Methodius "probably" baptized Borivoj (LUT 1457–1460). Modern guess; card only.

## 5. Where sources disagree, and how late they are

D1. **How close are the Lives?** Dümmler on VC: "dass wir es mit dem Werke eines wohl unterrichteten Zeitgenossen zu thun hatten" (VCI 18457–18458); its author was probably one of Constantine's own disciples (VCI 18144–18146); it ends at his death, so its date cannot be fixed (VCI 18147–18149). Manuscripts: the oldest dated are of 1479 and 1469 (VCI 17951–17956); a Russian miscellany of 1076 already recommends reading it (VCI 17945–17947). On VM, Ginzel reports Dümmler's view that it was written in the second half of the ninth century in Pannonia by a disciple of Methodius, "einer schlichten und ungeschminkten Darstellung wirklicher Thatsachen" (GZ 1566–1588, OCR "fd^Iid^ten unb ungefd^minften 3)arjiellung iDirfüd^er ^f)ai\a6:}en"), and rejects it: he holds VM to be the work of a Greek schismatic who made it look contemporary (GZ 1588–1592). Contested. Spoken line: "Most of what we know comes from two Lives written by their own followers. They survive only in copies made centuries later."
D2. **Who wrote the Life of Clement, and when.** It carries Theophylact's name (died 1107); Miklosich, reported in PG's Monitum, denies it, since the author says he lived with Clement (died 916) and calls himself Bulgarian (PG 96729–96746). The Monitum leaves it to the reader. Contested. LATER either way: a generation after Methodius at the earliest.
D3. **Which pope.** VM names Nicholas as receiving them and ordaining Methodius priest (VM 12226–12229). VC names Hadrian (VC 20380). LI: Nicholas invited them but had died; Hadrian received them (LI 11434–11447). Cross: the brothers came to Rome on the 867 journey (RPC 5655). Contested; the story can say "the pope" for the summons and Hadrian for the welcome.
D4. **Hadrian II's letter.** VM quotes it (VM 12264–12318), including the rule to read the Apostle and Gospel "Romane, dein Slovenice" (VM 12306–12307). Ginzel prints it as "spuria" (GZ 13061; VM note 4 at 12346). Dümmler counts it "ein besser beglaubigtes Schreiben" (VCI 18452–18453). Contested. Not quoted in the outline.
D5. **The whole Bible in six months.** VM claims it (M20). Cross: the tradition "that Methodius translated the previously unrendered sections of the Bible between March 20 and October 6 of one year is generally regarded as apocryphal" (RPC 5664–5666; Cross's dates differ from VM's own "usque ad vicesimam sextam diem Octobris"). Contested.
D6. **Which alphabet.** Lützow: the letters "became known as the Cyrillic alphabet" (LUT 1337–1340). Cross: "This was not the so-called Cyrillic alphabet ... but the more curious and difficult Glagolica" (RPC 5668–5672). The census agrees with Cross (CEN 42689: "devised the Glagolitic alphabet"; CEN 42695: Cyrillic "named for one brother, though their followers gave it its shape"). Contested; Cross's view is the one the census follows. The story should say "Slavic letters" and not "the Cyrillic alphabet".
D7. **Stephen V's letter.** It praises Wiching and speaks against Methodius and Slavonic Mass (STE 14067–14250, the key lines garbled). Ginzel prints it as "spuria" (GZ 14069). Contested. Card only.
D8. **How long in Moravia.** VM: "tribus annis elapsis" (VM 12221). VC: "quadraginta vero mensibus" (VC 20284). LI: "per annos quatuor et dimidium" (LI 11428). Contested; do not give a number.
D9. **Were both made bishops?** LI: "consecraverunt ipsum et Methodium in Episcopos" (LI 11470–11471). VC and VM: Constantine took the monk's habit and died; Methodius was made priest, later bishop (VC 20413; VM 12229, 12321). Ginzel holds LI's statement fully credible ("für vollkommen glaubwürdig", GZH 3480–3483, garbled Fraktur) and reports Assemani's view that Hadrian offered both the rank and Cyril refused it ("Cyrillum tamen oblatum sibi honorem recusasse, et solum Methodium fuisse Episcopum creatum", GZH 3489–3491). Contested. The story should not call Cyril a bishop.
D10. **Length of Methodius's episcopate.** LCl: "quatuor et viginti annos" (LCl 12950). From 869/870 to 885 (S4) is about sixteen; the census says "sixteen years after Cyril's death" (CEN 42690). LCl is late and loose here. Do not use 24.
D11. **Did Constantine go to Bulgaria?** RPC says he "went to instruct the people of Bulgaria" (RPC 7918). Cross: "no basis" (RPC 5658–5660). Leave out.

## 6. Not held, or not in the record

N1. Nothing by either brother is held: no Slavonic translation, no letter, no sermon. VC mentions Constantine's praise of Gregory (VC 19619–19622) and a report of the Khazar debate by Methodius (VCI 18151–18153); neither is held as a separate text.
N2. No readable Church Slavonic of either Life. Kantor's English translation and the treatise of Hrabar (*On the Letters*), both named by the census (CEN 42657, 42662), are **not held**.
N3. Nothing held describes the letters' shapes or how they were built.
N4. Where Mount Olympus was: not stated in the held texts read here.
N5. The Moravians' own voice survives only as the Lives report the embassy (S6, M15). The Salzburg *Conversio* is the only held voice from the opposing clergy written in Methodius's lifetime (M14).
