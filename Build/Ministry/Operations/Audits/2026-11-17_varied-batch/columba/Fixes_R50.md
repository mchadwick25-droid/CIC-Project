# Fixes, release 50: Columba

Ruling: Mark, 2026-11-17, "yes no softening, we are a witness not a judge … for this round 1 but also the should fixes too". Released text kept as `columba.r50.brief.txt`.

## Piece items

1. **MUST: "he left Ireland for good" was wrong.** Old: "Then, in about 563, though records differ by a few years, he left Ireland for good with twelve disciples." New: "Then, in about 563, he left Ireland for Britain with twelve disciples." No "for good": he was back in Ireland at Druim Cett in 574. Sources: S6, AD 2222–2225 ("wishing to make a pilgrimage for Christ from Ireland to Britain, he sailed forth"; opened and confirmed in `adomnan_life-of-saint-columba_huyshe1905.txt`); E11, AU 5187–5188 (Druim Cett, 574).
2. **"whose monastic church was led by abbots rather than city bishops" cut.** Old: "He came from sixth-century Ireland, whose monastic church was led by abbots rather than city bishops." New: "He came from sixth-century Ireland." Source: outline setting (time and place kept: S3, S4).
3. **One dating hedge on 563.** "though records differ by a few years" cut (same sentence as item 1); "about 563" carries the uncertainty in the phrase itself. Source: D1. The one tag left in the piece is "in June 597 by the usual reckoning" (D2), on a different date.
4. **Easter was Iona's reckoning, not his personal method.** Old: "Columba's way of dating Easter clashed with Rome's, so at the synod of Whitby in 664 a bishop of Iona's line defended "our most reverend Father Columba and his successors"." New: "Iona's way of reckoning Easter differed from Rome's. At the synod of Whitby in 664, a bishop of Iona's line defended it in the name of "our most reverend Father Columba and his successors"." The custom is Iona's; Columba is named as the founder whose successors kept it, which is how Colman frames it. Sources: I3, BEDE 8121–8128 (Columba "and his successors … who kept Easter after the same manner"); I4, BEDE 5876–5878 ("This manner of keeping Easter continued among them").
