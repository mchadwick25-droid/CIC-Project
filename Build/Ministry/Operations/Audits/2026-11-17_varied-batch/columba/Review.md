# Review: Columba (Colum Cille)

Reviewer, 2026-11-17. Brief: `columba.brief.txt` (416 words). Bar: `kit/Standard.md`, `kit/Shapes.md`, the approved Polycarp brief for craft only.

## 1. Listener read (one pass, written before anything else)

- Hook works at once: an old man copying the Psalms, stopping mid-page, handing on the rest. Clear and quiet. But "Let Baithene write" names someone I have never heard of, and I carry the question until paragraph 6.
- Paragraph 2 is heavy for one hearing: two names for him, Ireland, Iona, Britain, Scotland, the abbots-not-bishops line, then Adomnán. I held most of it; the tradition line went by fast.
- Paragraph 3 is easy. "A pilgrimage for Christ" is explained right away. Good.
- Paragraph 4: the hours of prayer and the stone pillow are vivid. Then "Brude, king of the Picts" — who are the Picts? And the gates "opened of their own accord": I heard the narrator say that as fact, and I did not know the gates had been shut, so the wonder had no point for me.
- Paragraph 5: the white horse laid its head on his chest. I didn't know why, and it slowed me. The last words, the midnight bell and Diormit raising his hand are strong, and I followed every step.
- Paragraph 6: Baithene comes back, and the hook makes sense. Aidan and the English are clear.
- Paragraph 7: "Iona kept its own country's way of dating Easter" — whose country, Iona's? I took a beat. Whitby is clear enough.
- Ending: Bede's verdict lands. It ends on what he left behind, not a moral.
- What I'd remember: he stopped copying and left the page to Baithene; he slept on rock; he sailed as a pilgrim; "love one another without pretence"; the hand raised to bless; Aidan to the English; Bede's "he left successors".

## 2. The low grade and the length

**Grade 6.4 (names out 5.5), reading ease 82.4, 14.9 words a sentence, longest 25.** The low grade comes from short, common words, not from choppy sentences. Sentence length runs from 6 to 24 words, and most paragraphs mix a long joined sentence with a short one: "Here I must stop..." (10) after "At the words... he stopped" (16); "and with it Columba blessed his monks and died" closes a 24-word sentence. Only 11% of sentences are short, and openings vary ("On Iona", "That night", "At the midnight bell", "Still"). Concrete detail carries the voice: bare rock, a stone pillow, the milk pails, the midnight bell, the lifted hand. By ear it is clear, not flat.

The one place it edges toward a report is paragraph 4: four plain acts in a row ("he built... He slept... He went... The king honoured"). Finding 2's fix, which puts the shut gates in, also breaks that run. The low grade is not a finding.

**Length: 416 words against about 375 (about 11% over).** Most of the extra is earned. The outline set aside room for one line on how late the record is (present: Adomnán, a hundred years later) and for the Whitby defence (present, with Colman's own words). The impact section is 137 words, 33%, the third the standard asks for. The one sentence that does not earn its place is the white horse (finding 4): it costs 20 words and leaves the listener asking why. Cut it or give its reason. With that, and the few words findings 1–3 add, the brief lands near 400, which is "about 375" for a 2½-minute piece.

## 3. Shape

The outline names Founder or leader. The brief opens on his last day and returns to it, as Polycarp opens on his trial. But this is not Polycarp's arc. There is no arrest, no trial, no stand. The last day here is a handing-on (the page left to Baithene, the blessing on his monks), and it leads straight into who carried Iona on. The founder material (what he built, how it lived) is thinner than the death, about 90 words against about 140. That stays inside the record, though: Adomnán's last-day chapter is the fullest scene the outline holds. Not a finding.

- Q3, BEDL 18882–18885: "Uerum qualiscumque fuerit ipse, nos hoc de illo certum tenemus, quia reliquit successores magna continentia ac diuino amore regularique institutione insignes" (OCR mark removed). Verbatim. Giles at BEDE 5866–5869 matches.
- Short phrases from the outline's beats: "to make a pilgrimage for Christ" (AD 2224), "of their own accord" (AD 7886), "our most reverend Father Columba and his successors" (BEDE 8122–8123). All verbatim.

## 5. Scores

- **Craft: 4.** Strong hook taken from the outline. Setting comes in paragraph 2. A third on impact, ending in Bede's own words. Varied sentences, no "he said" chains. It is held back by the unexplained horse and the list-like run in paragraph 4.
- **Rigor: 3.** Quotes verbatim and few. Witnesses named only where needed (Adomnán as the late source, Bede as the critic, Colman as defender). Uncertainty is plain ("by the usual reckoning", "about 716"). But one wonder is told in the narrator's voice, and one line says more than the outline holds (findings 1, 2).
- **Accessibility: 4.** Within every limit. Clear by ear. Three things puzzle a newcomer on one hearing: Baithene with no tag, the Picts, and "its own country's way" (findings 3, 5, 6).

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. "Nothing he wrote survives."** This says more than the outline. The outline says "there is no word of his own" and "no writing by Columba... is held". The sentence turns that into a settled fact. Bede, in the passage the brief quotes, says "some writings are said to be preserved by his disciples" (BEDE 5865–5866), and later tradition credits books and hymns to him. Change to the outline's ground: "We have no word of his own, and our fullest account comes from Adomnán..."

2. **BLOCKING. The gates, told as fact.** "whose fortress gates opened 'of their own accord'". Quotation marks can't be heard, and the nearest "Adomnán says" is two sentences back, attached to another claim. A listener hears the narrator stating the wonder. The standard and the outline (beat 5: "in Adomnán's voice") both require the witness's voice. The brief also drops the shut gates (beat 5: "the king first kept the gates shut"), so the wonder has no point. Change to, for example: "Adomnán says the king kept his gates shut against him, until they opened 'of their own accord'. From that day the king honoured him for the rest of his life."

3. **MINOR. Baithene in the hook.** The name comes with no tag and is not explained until paragraph 6. Opening 1 and Links give "his disciple Baithene". Add it once, in the hook's framing sentence: "...he stopped, and left the rest of the page to his disciple Baithene." Or put it in the quote's lead-in.

4. **MINOR. The white horse.** The act puzzles on one hearing, and the outline gives no reason for it. It is 20 words of the overrun. Cut it. Or, if the researcher wants it kept, the outline needs Adomnán's reason added to beat 6 (the horse weeps, knowing he will leave, AD 10743–10758, a wonder in Adomnán's voice) before the writer uses it.

5. **MINOR. "Iona kept its own country's way of dating Easter."** This can be heard as Iona's or Scotland's country, and it loses the link to Columba. The outline: "His way of dating Easter was the custom of his country, in Bede's words." Change to "Columba's way of dating Easter, the custom of his own country, clashed with Rome's."

6. **MINOR. "Brude, king of the Picts."** "Picts" will puzzle a newcomer. The outline gives no gloss. Ask the researcher for one plain clause, from S3's support: Pictland covered most of what is now Scotland (AD 2505–2506; AD-ed, Widely Accepted). Or say "a king in the north of Britain".

7. **MINOR. "many houses in Britain and Ireland grew from Iona".** Bede says they grew "from both which monasteries", meaning Durrow and Iona (BEDE 5851–5853), and the outline says "grew from his". Change to "grew from his houses" or "grew from his", and keep "Iona, which led them all".
