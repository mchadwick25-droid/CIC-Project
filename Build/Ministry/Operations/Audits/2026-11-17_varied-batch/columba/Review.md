# Review: Columba brief (columba.brief.txt)

## 1. Listener read (one hearing, before the trace)

**Where I got lost or drifted**

- Paragraph 3, "With twelve disciples he went 'to make a pilgrimage for Christ', leaving home for good, in about 563, though the old records differ." This sentence stacks four add-ons with commas. By the time "though the old records differ" arrives, I could not hear what it qualified: the year, the pilgrimage, or the twelve.
- Paragraphs 2 to 4. I never heard that he *founded* a monastery on Iona. I was told Iona was "his home" and that he had founded Durrow, then the story moved to Adomnán's portrait. On one hearing, the central act of a founder's life is missing, and I had to infer it.
- Paragraph 4, the Brude sentence. Without the king's pride, I could not tell why the gates were shut, so the wonder lands with no stakes. It went past as one more fact.

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down**

- Paragraph 3, "leaving home for good": a definition tacked on after a comma, which the Standard names directly.
- Paragraph 2, "The abbot was Columba, and his home was Iona": a storybook reveal. It is mild, and the next sentence recovers the adult register.
- Paragraph 2, "where the monastic church was led by abbots rather than city bishops": it borders on a tacked-on gloss. It does no work later, because the abbot-over-bishops point from Impact 1 never returns.
- I found no run of short one-act sentences. The death scene (paragraph 5) reads as adult narration. Short sentences are 9%.

- **Craft: 3.** It has a strong hook from Opening 1, and the setting comes early. Impact takes about a third (paragraphs 6–8) and ends on the record's own words. But very little of what was at stake is told. Leaving Ireland for good is buried in an aside. The king's resistance is dropped, so the gates have no weight. The founding of Iona, the core act of the Founder shape, is never told.

But the gates wonder is told in the narrator's voice, not Adomnán's. "June 597" is stated as settled. "Grew from Iona" says more than Bede does. There are eight quoted fragments in 413 words, and several do no work a narrator's words could not.

The grade sits at the floor of the aim, but the listener read finds it is not flat. It loses a point for the overloaded paragraph 3 sentence and for the missing Iona founding.

## 4. Verdict: REVISE

## 5. Findings

1. **BLOCKING. Paragraph 4, the gates wonder is told as fact.** "When he went to Brude, king of the Picts, the gates stayed shut until they opened 'of their own accord'." The "who says" of the sentence before governs only that sentence. Heard aloud, the quotation marks vanish, so the narrator endorses the wonder. The outline says to use it "in Adomnán's voice", and the Standard says a wonder stays in its witness's voice. **Change:** put it in Adomnán's voice, for example "Adomnán tells how the proud king kept his gates shut, until they opened 'of their own accord'". Then give the king's lasting honour. Restoring the king's pride (AD 7874–7878, outline beat 5) also gives the moment its stakes.

2. **BLOCKING. Paragraph 3, a definition tacked on after a comma, in a sentence that lost the listener.** "With twelve disciples he went 'to make a pilgrimage for Christ', leaving home for good, in about 563, though the old records differ." "Leaving home for good" is exactly the after-a-comma gloss the Standard forbids. It is also the stake of his whole life, and it is reduced to an aside. **Change:** work the meaning into the act and give the date its own clause, for example "In about 563, though the old records differ on the year, he left Ireland for good with twelve disciples, as Adomnán puts it 'to make a pilgrimage for Christ'." Or split it into two sentences.

3. **BLOCKING. Paragraphs 2–3, the founding of Iona is never told.** The outline's thread and beat 3 are that he built a monastery on Iona. The shape is Founder, with the trap "titles in place of acts". The brief gives the title ("The abbot was Columba, and his home was Iona") but never the act. On one hearing, the listener does not learn what he built, and the impact (houses that "grew from Iona") then rests on something never told. **Change:** state plainly that after the voyage he founded a monastery on Iona (outline beat 3). The contested giver can be left out, or given in one plain clause.

4. **MINOR. Paragraph 5, "in June 597" is stated as settled.** The outline marks the year Widely Accepted, with the annals differing, and gives "in 597 by the usual reckoning". **Change:** "in June 597, by the usual reckoning", or a similarly plain qualifier.

5. **MINOR. Paragraph 6, "many houses ... grew from Iona" says more than the outline does.** Bede says they grew "from both which monasteries", Durrow and Iona, "through his disciples" (BEDE 5851–5853). The outline says "grew from his". **Change:** "many houses in Britain and Ireland grew from his monasteries, and Bede called Iona 'the principal of them all'".

6. **MINOR. Paragraph 1, "he laid down his pen".** No source or outline line gives this act. The record has him say he must stop. It is a small detail added for effect. **Change:** "he stopped", or let the quote carry the stop.

7. **MINOR. Quoted fragments.** The brief has eight quoted pieces in 413 words. "The principal of them all" and "to make a pilgrimage for Christ" do nothing the narrator's own words could not. Spoken aloud, they blur who is talking. **Change:** keep the three main quotes and the one wonder phrase, and tell the rest in the narrator's words, keeping Bede's attribution where praise needs a name.

## Recheck (round 2, targeted)

It reads at grade 8.0 (7.0 with names out), reading ease 76.3, 17.7 words a sentence, with 9% short sentences. Its longest sentence is 30 words, at the limit. The profiler passes it.

Cause and contrast carry the links ("then", "although", "so", "yet"), and there is no run of short one-act sentences. No new problems.
