## Step 1: three lines from one hearing

The brief was read once, straight through, before the outline.

- **Who was this?** Columba, a sixth-century Irish monk of noble birth, who founded a monastery at Durrow in Ireland and then the monastery on Iona, a small island off what is now Scotland. He was its abbot.
- **What happened?** In about 563 he left Ireland for good with twelve disciples, as a pilgrimage for Christ, and built on Iona a monastery of constant prayer, reading, writing and work. He went to Brude, king of the Picts, who first shut him out and then honoured him for life; Bede says he turned the northern Picts to the faith. He died in June 597. He left his copying of the Psalms for Baithene to finish, charged his monks to love one another and live in peace, and blessed them from the altar.
- **Why does the connection matter?** Iona became the head of a family of monasteries in Britain and Ireland. Baithene succeeded him. In 635 Iona sent Aidan to Northumbria, and Aidan carried its faith to the English. Columba's Easter custom was defended in his name at Whitby in 664, and lost, though Iona kept it until about 716. Even Bede, a critic of that custom, was sure Columba left successors marked by self-control, love of God and life under a rule.
