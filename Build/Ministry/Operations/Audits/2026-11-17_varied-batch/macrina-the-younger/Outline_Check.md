## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. Setting: "by a river that runs to the Black Sea."** Gregory never places Macrina's house on the Iris. LIFE 144 puts *Naucratius's* solitary spot on the Iris. LIFE 150 says that when he died "His mother was far away, three days distant from the scene of the tragedy." LIFE 188 ("the retreat in which Macrina led her angelic and heavenly life") names no river. The placing on the Iris, across from Basil, is Clarke's (LIFE 78: MODERN, Widely Accepted, not CONTEMP Documented as sheet S2 has it). **Correction:** "In the fourth century, in Pontus, a region on the Black Sea, in what is now Turkey." (LIFE 144 shows that Pontus reaches the Black Sea.) Otherwise mark the river as a modern placing and drop it from the spoken line.

2. **BLOCKING. Impact 1: "Its Greek title was simply her name: 'the Macrinia'."** PG46 556-564 reads "ΠΕΡῚ ΨΥΧΗΣ ΚΑΙ ἈΝΑΣΤΆΣΕΩΣ Ὁ ΛΟΓῸΣ / Ο ΛΕΓΟΜΕΝῸΣ / Τὰ ΜΑΚΡΙΝΙΑ", which means "The dialogue on the soul and resurrection, the one called the Macrinia". The title is not simply her name. **Correction:** "In Greek the dialogue was also called by her name, 'the Macrinia' (PG46 556-564; cited that way at Ferrara, PO15 2340)."

3. **BLOCKING. Impact 3: "mistress and former slaves."** No text says the women were freed. LIFE 138 says "treating all her slave girls and menials as if they were sisters", and LIFE 158 says "on a footing of equality with the staff of maids". **Correction:** "mistress and slave women living as sisters."

4. **BLOCKING. Impact 3: "Later scholars read Gregory's account as one of the clearest early pictures of such a house."** Clarke says only that the *Life* "throws a light on the arrangements of a double monastery in primitive times" (LIFE 84). "One of the clearest" is not in any held text. **Correction:** "Later scholars use Gregory's account as an early picture of how such a house was run (LIFE 84). Dominant Modern Reconstruction."

5. **BLOCKING. Beat 7: "she tried to sing the evening thanksgiving ... could not, and died with the prayer."** LIFE 234: "her voice failed and she fulfilled her intention in the heart and by moving her hands ... when she had finished the thanksgiving, and her hand brought to her face to make the Sign had signified the end of the prayer, she drew a great deep breath and closed her life and her prayer together." She did finish it, silently. "Could not" inverts that. **Correction:** "Her voice failed, so she finished the thanksgiving in her heart, moving her hands and lips. She made the sign of the cross, drew a deep breath, and her life and her prayer ended together."

6. **MINOR. Beat 7: "She owned only a cloak, a head-covering and worn shoes."** That is Lampadia's word (LIFE 284), and an iron cross and ring were also found on her body (LIFE 296). **Correction:** "Lampadia said that all she owned was a cloak, a head-covering and worn shoes."

7. **MINOR. Beat 3: "baked bread ... with her own hands" cites sheet 72-76,** where Clarke has "prepare meals" and "prepared food". The bread is in the Greek: "ταῖς ἰδίαις χερσὶ πολλάκις τῇ μητρὶ παρασκευάζειν τὸν ἄρτον" (PG46 70300-70301, "often preparing bread for her mother with her own hands"). **Correction:** add the PG46 70300-70301 citation. "Prepared bread" is closer than "baked", and "often" can be kept.

8. **MINOR. Quote 3 rendering: "Churches send for you and call on you."** In "σὲ ... Ἐκκλησίαι πέμπουσι, καὶ καλοῦσι", πέμπουσι means "send": churches send him out as well as call him in. "Send for you" folds the two into one meaning. My rendering: "Churches send you out and call you in, to help them and set them right." The rest matches mine. "The gift" for τὴν χάριν is fine, because Clarke's "of God" and "in it all" are not in the Greek.

9. **MINOR. Quote 2 rendering: "Everything she put by is right here"** drops the clause "Ἐν χερσὶν ἔχεις", "you have [it] in hand". My rendering: "You have in front of you everything she put by." The rest matches mine. Quote 1's rendering matches mine clause for clause.

10. **MINOR. Who tells it: "wrote her life soon after."** The date of the *Life* is Clarke's reconstruction (LIFE 64, 402; sheet: Dominant Modern Reconstruction). **Correction:** "wrote her life a few years later, as scholars date it."
