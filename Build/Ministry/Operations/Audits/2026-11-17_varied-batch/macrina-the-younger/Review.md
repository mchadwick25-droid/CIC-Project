# Review: Macrina the Younger

Reviewer's report, varied batch, 2026-11-17. Judged against `kit/Standard.md` and `kit/Shapes.md` (Monk or ascetic), the outline, the outline check and the source sheet. Quotes re-checked against `cic/texts/`.

**Verdict: REVISE**

## 1. Listener read (one hearing, written before the trace)

**Where I got lost or drifted.**
- The last paragraph. "The Latin side quoted Macrina speaking" comes in a 32-word sentence, and the next sentence has the Greeks answer that *Gregory* was a man and could err. On one hearing I did not know why Gregory was suddenly the one on trial. The link that would carry me (they were quoting Macrina's words from Gregory's book) is never said. I also did not know what she had said that was worth arguing over in 1438.
- Paragraph 3 to paragraph 4. The story jumps from her mother's household to her death with nothing between. I came away not knowing that her family's house became a community, who joined it, or why that mattered. "Why did the church remember her?" was answered only by "her brother wrote about her".

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down.**
- "Her name was Macrina" has a storybook cadence, though one line of it is tolerable after a strong opening.
- No run of short one-act sentences, and no definition tacked on after a comma beyond the outline's own spoken setting line. The sentences are linked and adult.
- The real weakness is stakes. The refusal of marriage and the mistress living as an equal with her slave women are told as plain facts. Nothing says what these acts meant or risked in their time, or what they turned into.

## 2. Scores

- **Voice: 3.** Adult register throughout, and sentences carry cause and contrast ("because she believed...", "although nobody knows..."). Held back by "Her name was Macrina" and by the flat telling of the two acts that define her life.
- **Craft: 2.** The hook (the deathbed reunion) is strong and the setting arrives in the second paragraph. But the shape's middle is missing: the life she chose gets one sentence and "the others drawn after her" (Peter, the foundlings, the community of women and men) gets none. With the death given a full paragraph and the legacy the last, the arc reads as reunion, death and legacy, not as an ascetic life. The impact share (93 of 343 words, about 27%) is close to a third, and it ends on an event, which is right.
- **Rigor: 3.** All quotes match the outline's renderings and the source. Gregory is named once and plainly, and the uncertainty about the dialogue is said cleanly. But "sharing their beds" says more than the record, and the Ferrara passage drops the fact that makes the Greek reply make sense.
- **Accessibility: 3.** Grade 8.4 (7.5 names out), reading ease 77.6, 20.2 words a sentence, all inside the limits. The longest sentence is 32 words, past the standard's "about 30". The Ferrara ending lost me on one hearing.

## 4. Findings

1. **BLOCKING. The Ferrara ending loses the listener.** "The Latin side quoted Macrina speaking" (32 words in all) omits the outline's "from Gregory's book" (impact item 2; PO15 2304-2306). Without it, the Greek reply that "Gregory was a man and could be mistaken" has no clear subject. **Change:** split the sentence, and say the Latins quoted Macrina's words from Gregory's dialogue. For example: "In 1438, more than a thousand years later, Latin and Greek church leaders met at Ferrara to argue over what happens to souls after death. The Latin side quoted Macrina's words from Gregory's book." Linking the dialogue sentence to this one helps too. The outline gives what she defends in it (the soul's life after death and the body's rising, and not grieving like those "without hope"). One clause of that lets the listener know what was being argued over. Do not say she taught purgatory.

2. **BLOCKING. "sharing their food, their beds and their prayer" says more than the record.** Gregory says she led her mother to share "the same food, the same kind of bed" (LIFE 158; outline beat 5: "the same kind of bed"). "Sharing their beds" tells a listener they slept in the same beds. **Change:** "eating the same food, sleeping on the same kind of bed, and praying with them by night and day", or similar.

3. **BLOCKING. The ascetic life, and why it mattered, is missing.** The outline's thread is that "she turned her family's house into a community of prayer and shared work". The shape is Monk or ascetic: the turn, the life chosen, the others drawn after. The brief gives the turn and one sentence of the life. It never says the house became a community, and it drops all of "the others drawn after her": Peter, whom she raised and who led the men's side, and the babies she picked up by the roadside in a famine (beat 5). It also drops impact item 3, the house itself. Nothing tells the listener what it meant for a mistress to live as an equal with her slave women. So the listener leaves without knowing why she mattered beyond her brother's book, and the arc falls back to reunion, death and legacy. **Change:** give beat 5 two or three linked sentences: the household as equals (with what that reversal of rank meant, from the outline), Peter, and the foundlings. Give impact item 3 a sentence: the house of women and men in one community, which later scholars use as an early picture of how such a house was run. To stay near 300 words, trim the death paragraph. Quote 1's second sentence, or the separate "She died in 379" framing, can be folded down.

4. **MINOR. "She died in 379" is stated as settled.** The outline gives "379, as scholars date it, some nine months after her brother Basil". The brief's only "as scholars date it" attaches to the writing of the *Life*. **Change:** say "in 379, as scholars date it", or tie it to Basil's death ("months after Basil's death"), which the record states directly.

5. **MINOR. "Instead she stayed with her mother, whom she persuaded..." folds years into one step.** She led her mother into the shared life after her father's death and once the children were settled (LIFE 158; beats 3 and 5). "Instead" makes it sound like the immediate answer to the refused offers. **Change:** mark the passage of time ("She stayed with her mother, and after her father died she led her...").

6. **MINOR. "Her name was Macrina"** has a storybook ring. **Change:** name her inside a sentence that carries information, for example "His sister was Macrina, the eldest child in the family of...".
