# Review: Macrina the Younger

Brief: `macrina-the-younger.brief.txt` (352 words, about 2 minutes 20 seconds spoken).

## Listener read

I read it once, straight through, without going back. These notes were written before I read the standard or the outline.

- The opening works. Eight years apart, then the sack on a board and a sister who cannot get up. I wanted to know who she was.
- Paragraph 2 places her at once: the family, the fourth century, Pontus on the Black Sea, and who tells the story. I knew the Gregory of the opening was her brother.
- Paragraph 3: "her promised husband was only away" took a beat. Away where? I worked it out from "rising again", but only just.
- Paragraph 4 is clear and concrete: the slave women, the shared food, the babies by the roadside.
- Paragraph 5 is where I stumbled. "She died in 379" comes first, flatly, and then the story goes back to the evening. The prayer line heard well. Then "she finished with her hands and lips": finished what? I did not hear the moment she died. The paragraph stops before the death it announced.
- Paragraph 6: Lampadia's words land. "Everything she put by" needed a second thought (put by = saved). "Who had lived beside her" told me little about who she was.
- Paragraph 7 heard clearly, and the uncertainty was plain: the words are his, no one knows how much is hers. I did not learn what she taught in it, only its subject.
- Paragraph 8: "More than a thousand years later" pulled me back in. The Greek answer, "a man and could be wrong", and the Latin reply are easy to follow. The last line, her name used as a guarantee, lands as an event.
- What I would remember: the floor and the board; the betrothed who was "only away"; the babies from the roadside; the cloak, the head-covering and the worn shoes; Ferrara and "blessed Macrina".

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. Paragraph 5 loses the listener at the climax.** "She died in 379, as scholars date it. At evening she prayed." states the death flatly and then goes back in time. "Then her voice failed, and she finished with her hands and lips" has no object, and the paragraph stops before she dies. The listener does not hear the moment the whole story has led to. The sentence also moves the failed voice from the lamp-lighting thanksgiving, where the outline puts it (Beat 7), to the prayer of Quote 1. **Change:** keep the year, but let the scene end in her death, in Beat 7's terms. For example: "She died in 379, as scholars date it. At evening she prayed, [Quote 1]. When a lamp was brought, she wanted to sing the evening thanksgiving. Her voice failed, so she finished it in her heart, moving her hands and lips. Then she drew a deep breath, and her life and her prayer ended together." Pay for the added words elsewhere (see finding 4).

2. **MINOR. "Everything she put by".** "Put by" is a British idiom that a second-language listener may not hear as "saved". It is in the outline's rendering, so do not change the quote. Set it up instead: "When Gregory asked what she had stored away for her burial, Lampadia ... answered". Then "put by" lands on "stored away".

3. **MINOR. "Only away" without "not dead".** Restore the outline's contrast: "her promised husband was not dead but only away, because of the hope of rising again." It costs two words and makes the sentence clear when heard once.

4. **MINOR. The community is only hinted at.** The thread is that she turned the house into a community of prayer and work, and the shape asks for "the others drawn after her". The brief has the mother, the slave women and the babies, but never says that a community formed. "Lampadia, who had lived beside her" can become "Lampadia, who led the sisters of the house" (LIFE 278). That carries the community at no cost in words. To pay for finding 1, cut "in Greek" from P7 or shorten P2's tradition line, not the impact third.

5. **MINOR. Paragraph 7 names the dialogue's subject but not her side of it.** Impact 1 marks what she defends: the soul lives on after death, the body will rise, and he should not grieve like those "without hope". One short clause would do it, for example "with Macrina as the teacher, telling her grieving brother that the dead will rise." Add it only if finding 1 leaves room. Otherwise the last lines of Quote 1 cannot carry it either, and the brief stays sound without it.
