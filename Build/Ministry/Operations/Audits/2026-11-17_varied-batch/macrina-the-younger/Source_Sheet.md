Researcher's sheet, varied batch, 2026-11-17. Evidence only; no story prose.

**Files and short names** (all in `cic/texts/` unless noted):

- **PG46** = `gregory-nyssa_opera-omnia-pg46-grc-lat_migne1863.txt` (Migne PG 46: the Greek of the *Life*, from line 69890; and the Greek title of the dialogue, line 564)

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own. Quotes are exact, except that in LIFE I leave out Migne column markers such as "[962A]" and Clarke's page markers such as "|20"; nothing else is changed. Footnote numbers are left out of quotes and given after them where they matter. In PG46 the Greek column is interleaved with garbled Latin; I give only Greek lines, join words split at line ends, and correct the OCR's wrong letters, giving the OCR's own letter in [brackets] right after the corrected one, or the OCR's whole word as [OCR ...] (the OCR often prints χ for κ and δ or θ for β). Section headings in LIFE ("MACRINA'S PARENTS" and so on) are Clarke's, not Gregory's (LIFE 68).

**Witness kinds.** Macrina wrote nothing that survives; there is no OWN witness. Her words reach us only as her brother reports them (CEN 35236: "Macrina led a women's community, but her words reach us only through her brother.").
- **CONTEMP** = Gregory of Nyssa, her younger brother, in the *Life* and the dialogue. He was with her for her last two days and her funeral. Gregory of Nazianzus, Basil's friend, is also CONTEMP, but he does not mention her in the held texts.
- **LATER** = the Ferrara debate of 1438 (about 1060 years later) and the Florence-era defence (GEN, 15th century).
- **MODERN** = editors and scholars (Clarke, Moore and Wilson, Jackson, Morison, Duchesne, Delehaye). Card material, not story voices.
- **Wonder stories.** The *Life* tells some wonders as reports Gregory heard from others after her death. They are CONTEMP as reports, and each stays in its witness's voice (M17, M18).

**When the *Life* was written.** Gregory wrote it after he met Olympius at Antioch "on my way to Jerusalem" (LIFE 108). Clarke's note puts that journey "a year or two later", after his visit to Babylon (LIFE 402). Clarke judges that the plain style of the later pages shows "a true account of actual facts, written down shortly after their occurrence" (LIFE 64). So: written within a few years of her death. Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

## 1. Setting

S1. **Her name.** CONTEMP. Documented.
> LIFE 112: The virgin's name was Macrina; she was so called by her parents after a famous Macrina some time before in the family, our father's mother, who had confessed Christ like a good athlete in the time of the persecutions.

The census lists her as "Macrina the Younger", also "Macrina", "Saint Macrina" (CEN 92066, 92094-92095).

S2. **Her place: Pontus, by the river Iris.** CONTEMP. Documented. The *Life* never names the estate. The family's retreat lay by the Iris (where Naucratius lived alone; Gregory reaches "the retreat in which Macrina led her angelic and heavenly life", LIFE 188).
> LIFE 144: a solitary spot on the banks of the Iris----a river flowing through the midst of Pontus. It rises actually in Armenia, passes through our parts, and discharges its stream into the Black Sea.

Clarke names the estate "Annesi, on the opposite side of the river" from Basil's community (LIFE 78; also LIFE 38). MODERN. Widely Accepted. The census puts her region as "Pontus" (CEN 92070) and the tradition's modern country as Turkey (CEN 35132, 35139).

S3. **Century and year.** The *Life* gives no year. It dates her death relative to Basil's: "the ninth month or a little longer after this disaster" (LIFE 184). Clarke gives the years: "On January 1, 379, Basil died; in September of the same year Gregory attended a Council at Antioch" (LIFE 52). Moore and Wilson agree: "This and Basil's death occurred in the year 379" (SOUL 40781-40782). The census: "died 379" (CEN 92067). The year 379 is MODERN: Widely Accepted. Her birth year is not in the record. Spoken form the sheet supports: "in the fourth century"; "in the year 379, as scholars date it". Clarke calls Gregory's Greek "the Greek of the fourth century A.D." (LIFE 66).

S4. **Tradition.** Census name: **Cappadocian Christianity** (CEN 35123); world name "The Cappadocian Churches" (CEN 35167). The census's own description: "The Nicene-confessing churches of Cappadocia and Pontus lasted from about 325 to 394 CE. One family and one friendship stood at their center. They were Basil of Caesarea; his sister, Macrina; his brother, Gregory of Nyssa; and Basil's friend, Gregory of Nazianzus." (CEN 35170). Plain spoken forms: (1) "the churches of Cappadocia and Pontus, in what is now Turkey"; (2) "the family of Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa", from the census teaser "Eldest child of the family of Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa" (CEN 92089). Do not say "Nicene-confessing" aloud without explaining it; the story does not need it.

S5. **A Christian family under past persecution.** CONTEMP (reported as Macrina's own account to Gregory). Documented as the family's memory.
> LIFE 212: My father's parents had their goods confiscated for confessing Christ. Our maternal grandfather was slain by the imperial wrath, and all his possessions were transferred to other masters.

S6. **"Philosophy" means the Christian ascetic life.** Clarke's note:
> LIFE 404: The use of the word "philosophy" to designate Christianity is common in the writings of the fourth century, and may perhaps be traced back to Origen's synthesis of the Gospel and philosophy. It is employed in a twofold sense, of the Christian religion generally and of asceticism in particular.

The writer must not say "philosophy" to a listener without this meaning; better to say "a life of prayer and simple work".

## 2. Life events (in order)

M1. **The secret name Thecla.** CONTEMP, a dream of her mother's as Gregory tells it. Documented as the family's story. Wonder-adjacent.
> LIFE 116: some one in form and raiment more splendid than a human being appeared and addressed the child she was carrying by the name of Thecla, that Thecla, I mean, who is so famous among the virgins.

Clarke's note: Thecla "was a contemporary of St. Paul, according to the Acts of Paul and Thecla" (LIFE 406).

M2. **Taught Scripture, not poetry; the Psalms all day.** CONTEMP. Documented.
> LIFE 120: The education of the child was her mother's task; she did not, however, employ the usual worldly method of education, which makes a practice of using poetry as a means of training the early years of the child.
> LIFE 120: the Psalter was her constant companion, like a good fellow-traveller that never deserted her.

M3. **Betrothed at twelve; the young man died; she refused all other marriage.** CONTEMP. Documented.
> LIFE 128: she would say that it was absurd and unlawful not to be faithful to the marriage that had been arranged for her by her father, but to be compelled to consider another; since in the nature of things there was but one marriage, as there is one birth and one death.
> LIFE 128: she considered him who lived to God, thanks to the hope of the resurrection, to be absent only, not dead; it was wrong not to keep faith with the bridegroom who was away.

Age: "the growing girl reached her twelfth year" (LIFE 124).

M4. **She stayed with her mother, baked with her own hands, and shared the estate's cares.** CONTEMP. Documented.
> LIFE 132: in the time that was left she prepared food for her mother by her own toil.
> LIFE 132: For she had four sons and five daughters, and paid taxes to three different governors, since her property was scattered in as many districts.

(The "she" with nine children is the mother; her father "had by this time departed this life", LIFE 132.)

M5. **She drew Basil from pride in his speaking to a life of work.** CONTEMP (Gregory). Contested (D1).
> LIFE 136: He was puffed up beyond measure with the pride of oratory and looked down on the local dignitaries, excelling in his own estimation all the men of leading and position. Nevertheless Macrina took him in hand, and with such speed did she draw him also toward the mark of philosophy that he forsook the glories of this world and despised fame gained by speaking, and deserted it for this busy life where one toils with one's hands.

M6. **She led her mother to live as an equal with the household's slave women.** CONTEMP. Documented.
> LIFE 138: Macrina persuaded her mother to give up her ordinary life and all showy style of living and the services of domestics to which she had been accustomed before, and bring her point of view down to that of the masses, and to share the life of the maids, treating all her slave girls and menials as if they were sisters and belonged to the same rank as herself.
> LIFE 158: She induced her to live on a footing of equality with the staff of maids, so as to share with them in the same food, the same kind of bed, and in all the necessaries of life, without any regard to differences of rank.
> LIFE 158: Nothing was left but the care of divine things and the unceasing round of prayer and endless hymnody, co-extensive with time itself, practised by night and day. So that to them this meant work, and work so called was rest.

M7. **Her brother Naucratius died suddenly; she held her mother up.** CONTEMP. Documented. He was killed on one of the trips by which he fed old people in his care (LIFE 150); Gregory does not say how. (Clarke says "killed on a hunting expedition", LIFE 44; Jackson's note says "killed by an accident while hunting, c. 357", BAS 37464-37465. MODERN.)
> LIFE 154: Facing the disaster in a rational spirit, she both preserved herself from collapse, and becoming the prop of her mother's weakness, raised her up from the abyss of grief, and by her own steadfastness and imperturbability taught her mother's soul to be brave.

M8. **She raised the youngest brother, Peter.** CONTEMP. Documented.
> LIFE 164: the eldest of the family, the subject of our story, took him soon after birth from the nurse's breast and reared him herself and educated him on a lofty system of training
> LIFE 164: Thus having become all things to the lad---- father, teacher, tutor, mother, giver of all good advice

M9. **Famine: crowds came; she took in abandoned babies.** CONTEMP. Documented. Peter fed the crowds (LIFE 164: "crowds from all quarters were frequenting the retreat where they lived, drawn by the fame of their benevolence"). At her death:
> LIFE 264: Saddest of all in their grief were those who called on her as mother and nurse. These were they whom she picked up, exposed by the roadside in the time of famine. She had nursed and reared them, and led them to the pure and stainless life.

("Exposed" = babies left out by the road to die, as unwanted infants then were.)

M10. **Her mother's death and blessing.** CONTEMP. Documented. The mother died "in the arms of her two children" (LIFE 168), Macrina and Peter.
> LIFE 172: "To Thee, O Lord, I give the fruit of my womb as both first-fruits and tenths. For this my eldest is the first-fruits and this my last-born is the tenth.

M11. **Basil's death; Gregory's visit.** CONTEMP. Documented (year: S3). They had not met for "no less than eight years, or very nearly" (LIFE 184), because Gregory had been "constantly driven out from my own country by the leaders of heresy" (LIFE 184); later "my exile at the hands of the Emperor Valens on account of the faith" (LIFE 214).

M12. **She lay on boards on the floor.** CONTEMP, eyewitness. Documented.
> LIFE 192: She was lying not on a bed or couch, but on the floor; a sack had been spread on a board, and another board propped up her head
> LIFE 192: Now when she saw me near the door she raised herself on her elbow but could not come to meet me, her strength being already drained by fever.

M13. **Her teaching from her deathbed.** CONTEMP, eyewitness; content summarised by Gregory. Documented that she taught; the words are his (D2).
> LIFE 196: how she was uplifted as she discoursed to us on the nature of the soul and explained the reason of life in the flesh, and why man was made, and how he was mortal, and the origin of death and the nature of the journey from death to life again.

Clarke's note: "In the long dialogue, De Anima et Resurrectione ... Gregory purports to reproduce this conversation" (LIFE 412).

M14. **She told him to see God's gift in his fame.** CONTEMP, reported speech. Documented as Gregory's report.
> LIFE 216: "Will you not cease to be insensible to the divine blessings? Will you not remedy the ingratitude of your soul?
> LIFE 218: "But you," she said, "are renowned in cities and peoples and nations. Churches summon you as an ally and director, and do you not see the grace of God in it all?

Greek (PG46 71493-71496, 71505-71506, 71536): «Οὐ παύσῃ, φησὶν ἡ Μεγάλη, ἀγνωμόνως ἐπὶ τοῖς θείοις ἀγαθοῖς διακ[χ]είμενος; Οὐ θεραπεύσεις τῆς ψυχῆς τὸ ἀχάριστον;» ... «Σὺ δὲ,» φησὶ, «πόλεσι, καὶ δήμοις, καὶ ἔθνεσιν ὀνομαστὸς εἶ· καὶ σὲ πρὸς συμμαχίαν τε καὶ διόρθωσ[τ]ιν Ἐκκλησίαι [OCR Ἐχχλησίαι] πέμπουσι, καὶ καλ[χ]οῦσι· καὶ οὐχ ὁρᾷς τὴν χάριν;» Note: the Greek says "the Great one says" (ἡ Μεγάλη) and "the grace" (τὴν χάριν); Clarke's "of God" and "in it all" are his additions.

M15. **Her dying prayer.** CONTEMP, eyewitness ("we could just hear what was said", LIFE 228). Documented as Gregory's record. Her couch "had been turned towards the East" (LIFE 228).
> LIFE 230: "Thou, O Lord, hast freed us from the fear of death. Thou hast made the end of this life the beginning to us of true life. Thou for a season restest our bodies in sleep and awakest them again at the last trump.

Greek (PG46 71687, 71690 [Greek part only], 71730-71732): «Σὺ,» φησὶν, «ἔλυσας ἡμῖν, Κύριε, τοῦ θανάτου τὸν φόβον [OCR φόθον]. Σὺ ζωῆς ἀληθινῆς ἀρχὴν ἡμῖν ἐποίησας τὸ τέλος τῆς ἐνταῦθα ζωῆς. Σὺ πρὸς καιρὸν διαναπαύεις ὕπνῳ τὰ σώματα, καὶ πάλιν ἀφυπνίζεις ἐν τῇ ἐσχάτῃ σάλπιγγι.

M16. **Her death at lamp-lighting.** CONTEMP, eyewitness. Documented.
> LIFE 234: Meanwhile evening had come and a lamp was brought in. All at once she opened the orb of her eyes and looked towards the light, clearly wanting to repeat the thanksgiving sung at the Lighting of the Lamps.
> LIFE 234: she drew a great deep breath and closed her life and her prayer together.

("The Lighting of the Lamps" = the evening prayer of thanks sung when lamps were lit.)

M17. **No burial clothes: Lampadia's witness.** CONTEMP. Documented. Lampadia was "leader of the band of sisters, a deaconess in rank" (LIFE 278).
> LIFE 284: "Store-cupboard indeed!" said she; "you have in front of you all her treasure. There is the cloak, there is the head-covering, there the well-worn shoes on the feet. This is all her wealth, these are her riches.

Greek (PG46 72082-72085): Ποίοις, εἶπεν, ἀποκ[χ]ειμένοις; Ἐν χερσὶν ἔχεις πᾶν τὸ ἀπόθετον· ἰδοὺ τὸ ἱμάτιον, ἰδοὺ τῆς κ[χ]εφαλῆς ἡ καλύπτρα, τὰ τετρι[υ]μμένα τῶν ποδῶν ὑποδήματα· οὗτος ὁ πλοῦτος· αὕτη ἡ περιουσία. (Lit. "What stored things? In hand you have all the store.")

Also on her body: "a cross of iron and a ring of the same material" (LIFE 296); Vestiana says the ring held "a particle of the Cross of Life" (LIFE 298). Card only.

M18. **Wonders, each in its witness's voice.** CONTEMP reports, told to Gregory after her death. Inferential-Thin as events; Documented as reports.
- A tumour on her breast, told by Vestiana, "a lady of gentle birth" who lived with the sisters (LIFE 274-276): she prayed all night in the sanctuary and asked her mother to make the sign of the cross on it; "the sign worked and the tumour disappeared" (LIFE 310).
- A soldier's daughter's diseased eye, told by her father at Sebastopolis (LIFE 348): his wife cried, "it is the healing that comes from prayer" (LIFE 374).
- Gregory himself leaves out more: "I omit that extraordinary agricultural operation in the famine time, how that the corn for the relief of need, though constantly distributed, suffered no perceptible diminution" (LIFE 384); "lest the unbeliever should be injured by being led to disbelieve the gifts of God, I have abstained from a consecutive narrative of these sublime wonders" (LIFE 388).
- Gregory's own dream of martyrs' relics shining (LIFE 186), which he later reads as about her (LIFE 204, 316).

M19. **The funeral: a vigil "as in the case of martyrs' festivals".** CONTEMP, eyewitness. Documented.
> LIFE 322: When the all-night vigil for her, accompanied by hymn-singing, as in the case of martyrs' festivals, was finished, and the dawn came, the multitude of men and women that had flocked in from all the neighbouring country were interrupting the psalms with wailings.

The local bishop Araxius led; Gregory and Araxius carried the bier (LIFE 326-328). She was buried "by the side of the mother" in the chapel of "the Holy Martyrs, in which also the bodies of our parents were laid" (LIFE 332, 342).

## 3. The community (what she built)

C1. **A house of women and men, with Peter leading the men.** CONTEMP. Documented. Gregory: "the whole company of the men came streaming out ... the band of virgins on the women's side modestly waited in the church" (LIFE 192). The soldier:
> LIFE 356: I went to the men's department, presided over by Peter, your brother; while my wife went to the women's side and conversed with the saint.

Clarke calls it a double monastery: "In the Life of St. Macrina we find a double monastery, the men presided over by Peter, the women by Macrina" (LIFE 80). MODERN reading; Clarke also says "It is not clear whether Basil's monastery on the far side of the Iris was still existing when Gregory visited Macrina" (LIFE 80). Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

C2. **Who lived there.** CONTEMP. Documented. The former household maids (M6); foundlings (M9); Vestiana, a senator's daughter widowed young, who "chose the great Macrina as protector and guardian of her widowhood" (LIFE 274, 276); Lampadia, deaconess (LIFE 278).

C3. **Her own share of the family wealth.** CONTEMP (Gregory, reporting her account). Documented.
> LIFE 212: she kept nothing of the things assigned to her in the equal division between brothers and sisters, but all her share was given into the priest's hands according to the divine command.

## 4. Impact, ranked heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

I1. **Her teaching was kept in her own voice, as "the Teacher", and was argued over a thousand years later.** Heaviest. DEFENDED by her: that the soul survives death and the body will rise, and that Christians should not grieve "as men without hope".
- Gregory wrote the dialogue *On the Soul and the Resurrection* with her as teacher. CONTEMP. Documented.
> SOUL 38087-38089: Basil, great amongst the saints, had departed from this life to God; and the impulse to mourn for him was shared by all the churches. But his sister the Teacher was still living
> SOUL 38096-38100: and then she tried to check me by speaking, and to correct with the curb of her reasonings the disorder of my soul. She quoted the Apostle's words about the duty of not being "grieved for them that sleep"; because only "men without hope" have such feelings.
- The Greek title of the dialogue is her name: "Τὰ ΜΑΚΡΙΝΙΑ" ("the Macrinia", PG46 558-564, under "ΠΕΡῚ ΨΥΧΗΣ ΚΑΙ ἈΝΑΣΤΆΣΕΩΣ Ὁ ΛΟΓῸΣ / Ο ΛΕΓΟΜΕΝῸΣ"), and the Ferrara Greek cites it "ἐν τοῖς Μακρινίοις" (PO15 2340; OCR garbled: "ἐν τοῖς Meazoıwtoıs"). Documented.
- **Ferrara, 1438.** LATER (about 1060 years). Documented. At the council's session on purgatory on 5 June 1438, Cardinal Julian Cesarini's Latin case cited Gregory of Nyssa among Eastern witnesses (PO15 349-358). The Latin statement:
> PO15 2304-2306: Gregorius Nyssenus in dialogo de Consolatione et statu animarum post mortem", introdueit loquentem et dieentem Macrinam sororem beati Basilii - et suam, post ipsius Basilii mortem
(OCR: "introdueit" = introducit, "dieentem" = dicentem. "Gregory of Nyssa, in the dialogue on consolation and the state of souls after death, brings in Macrina, sister of blessed Basil and his own, speaking after Basil's death".) The words they cite are hers in the dialogue: "Not in hatred or revenge for a wicked life, to my thinking, does God bring upon sinners those painful dispensations" (SOUL 39603-39604; the speaker is "she", SOUL 39465).
- The Greek reply said it would have been better to pass over Gregory in silence, and that he was a man and could err (Responsio Graecorum, PO15 8664, 8681-8688: "Homo erat; homini vero, etiam si ad culmen sanctimoniae pervenerit, non est impossibile errare"). Mark of Ephesus suggested such passages might be interpolations by Origenists (PO15 5449-5470). Contested in that debate.
- The Latin rejoinder defended Gregory partly through his family:
> PO15 10731-10734: Item Gregorius Nyssenus, vir ille eximius et inter doctores praestantissimus, ubi sanctam Macrinam loquentem introdueit, purgatorium ignem luculentissime tradidit
> PO15 10758-10760: quippe frater erat Basilii magni beataeque Macrinae et familiaris Gregori Theologi.
("for he was brother of Basil the Great and of blessed Macrina, and a friend of Gregory the Theologian.")
- Caution for the writer: the 1438 debate used her words for a doctrine (purgatory) that the Greek side rejected; the story should say her words were cited and argued over, not that she taught purgatory as later defined.
- GEN 8136 (15th-century defence of Florence, Latin translation): cites Gregory "in oratione in sororem Macrinam" (OCR "fororem Macrinana") on the saints seeing God. LATER. Documented as a citation; card only.
- MODERN: Moore and Wilson: "It was right, too, that the Church should possess her Phædo, or Death-bed Dialogue; and it is Gregory who has supplied this in his On the Soul and the Resurrection." (SOUL 247-249). ("Phaedo" = Plato's dialogue on Socrates' death and the soul.) Card only; do not say "Phaedo" aloud without explanation.

I2. **The pattern of her household: mistress and slave women living as equals in prayer and work, men and women in one community.** CONTEMP for the facts (M6, C1, C2); MODERN for its later weight. 
- MODERN: Clarke: "The Life of St. Macrina throws a light on the arrangements of a double monastery in primitive times, and supplements the account given in the Pachomian and Basilian Rules." (LIFE 84). Morison: "Gregory of Nyssa tells us that in the convent over which Macrina presided there was a perpetual sequence of prayer and praise." (MOR 4202-4206). Clarke 1925: "Afterwards they received disciples and were organised as a sisterhood with Macrina as Superior." (BRC 3000-3004). Duchesne: "après elle, sa fille aînée, Macrine, dirigea le monastère d'Annesi" (DUC 21588-21589).
- Clarke on Basil's rules reaching Benedict (LIFE 82) is about Basil's rules, not hers. Any line from her house to later Western monasteries is Inferential-Thin; do not tell it.

I3. **She shaped her brothers: Basil (contested), Peter, Gregory.**
- Basil: LIFE 136 (M5). Contested (D1).
- Peter: "always looking to his sister as the model of all good, he advanced to such a height of virtue that in his subsequent life he seemed in no whit inferior to the great Basil" (LIFE 164). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Gregory: her rebuke and teaching (M14, I1). He calls her "the great Macrina" (LIFE 154) and "the Teacher" (SOUL 38089).
- MODERN: Morison: "Basil, it is to be remembered, was induced to adopt the religious life by a woman, his sister Macrina, so that he was not likely to underestimate the monastic value of womankind." (MOR 6530-6534). Card only.

I4. **The *Life* itself: written so that she would not be forgotten; she was honoured at burial "as in the case of martyrs' festivals" (M19).** Gregory wrote it so that her life would not be "unknown to our time" (LIFE 108).
> LIFE 108: to prevent such a life being unknown to our time, and the record of a woman who raised herself by "philosophy" to the greatest height of human virtue passing into the shades of useless oblivion
(Clarke's footnote number "2" after "philosophy" is left out of this quote.)
- MODERN: Delehaye lists the *Life* among the eulogies that mark "ce moment de l'évolution où le culte des saints s'élargit insensiblement, où l'on voit les honneurs, d'abord réservés aux martyrs, se partager également avec les évo[ê]ques, les ascètes, les vierges" (DEL 7819-7823; words split at line ends joined; OCR "évoques" for évêques). Delehaye does not call it the first of its kind, and nor should the story. The writer may say only: Gregory honoured her as the church then honoured martyrs (LIFE 322).

**Feast day.** Not found in the held texts searched. Do not give one.

## 5. Links to other people

- **Macrina the Elder**, grandmother: she was named after her (LIFE 112). Basil says this grandmother "taught me the words of the blessed Gregory" [Gregory the Wonderworker] (BAS 37060-37063). Kind: named for her.
- **Emmelia**, mother: lived with her, led into the ascetic life by her, died in her arms (LIFE 132, 138, 158, 168). Kind: lived with; was buried beside her (LIFE 342).
- **Basil of Caesarea**, brother: she drew him to the ascetic life, per Gregory (LIFE 136; contested, D1). Basil ordained Peter priest (LIFE 178). Kind: taught (contested).
- **Naucratius**, brother: died young; she steadied their mother (LIFE 150-154).
- **Peter of Sebaste**, youngest brother: she raised and taught him; he led the men's side (LIFE 164, 356). Kind: raised and taught him. (Bishop of Sebaste: Clarke, LIFE 44.)
- **Gregory of Nyssa**, brother: visited her deathbed, buried her, wrote her life and the dialogue (LIFE 184-346; SOUL 38087). Kind: wrote her life; heard her teach.
- **Gregory of Nazianzus**: no held text of his names her (searched NAZ and PG37: none). Link only through Basil. Do not link.

## 6. Disagreements, doubts and gaps

D1. **Who turned Basil to the ascetic life?** Gregory of Nyssa says Macrina did (LIFE 136). Gregory of Nazianzus, Basil's friend, says that at Athens already "philosophy was our aim" (NAZ 41486-41487; the translator's note at 41487: "Philosophy, here, a truly Christian life"), and that they lived "having retired from this world, before our actual departure hence" (NAZ 41527-41528). Neither mentions the other's account. Jackson gives both and reads Macrina as giving Basil "the resolution, with which he now carried out the determination" (BAS 1253-1264). Contested. The story may say "Gregory says she drew Basil..." and should not tell it as settled.

D2. **How much of the dialogue is hers?** Gregory "purports to reproduce this conversation" (Clarke, LIFE 412). The dialogue was "probably composed in 380" (Moore and Wilson, SOUL 40782-40783). It is Gregory's written form; it does not say how much of the argument is hers and how much his. Contested. Say "Gregory wrote her teaching down as a conversation, with her as the teacher."

D3. **Basil's own letters do not name his sister.** A search of BAS found Macrina named only as Basil's grandmother (BAS 37062, 39214) and in Jackson's prolegomena. Basil speaks of "my own mother and sister" only through Jackson's paraphrase (BAS 1389). Inferential-Thin as to why; card only. Do not speculate in the story.

D4. **Number of children.** The *Life*: "four sons and five daughters" (LIFE 132) and "nine parts according to the number of the children" (LIFE 212). Clarke: "ten children, of whom four sons and five daughters survived infancy" (LIFE 44). Moore and Wilson: "ten children, five of whom were boys and five girls" (SOUL 492). Say "nine children" only if needed, or "the eldest of a large family".

D5. **One witness.** Almost everything comes from one brother who loved her, writing in her honour, with a rhetorical opening (Clarke, LIFE 62) and plain later pages (LIFE 64). The census: "Her life is known from her brother Gregory" (CEN 92115). Delehaye notes that in the *Life* Gregory "évite de parler d'elle comme de sa sœur : c'est « la sœur de Basile »" (DEL 7848-7849), putting this down to rhetoric. MODERN; card only.

D6. **Annesi, the "double monastery", and 379** are modern placings (S2, S3, C1).

D7. **Not held / not found:** any writing by Macrina herself; any feast-day or calendar entry; any text by Gregory of Nazianzus or Basil that names her.
