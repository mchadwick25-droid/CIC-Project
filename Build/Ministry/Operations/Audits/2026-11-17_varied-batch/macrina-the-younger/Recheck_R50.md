## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Macrina, the eldest child of a fourth-century Christian family in Pontus, sister of the bishops Basil and Gregory.
2. **What happened:** after her betrothed died she refused marriage, led her mother to live as an equal with their slave women, and turned the family house into a community of prayer and shared work; she died poor in 379, and her brother Gregory sat with her and wrote her life.
3. **Why the connection matters:** Gregory wrote a dialogue with Macrina as the Teacher on the soul and the resurrection, and more than a thousand years later, at Ferrara, the Latin side quoted her part in it while Latins and Greeks argued over souls after death.

All three lines come clear on one hearing.

## Findings

- **BLOCKING (misleading implication, in a changed sentence).** "Two of her brothers became bishops: Basil, of the great church of Caesarea, and Gregory himself, of Nyssa." Three of her brothers became bishops. The writer's own source for the sentence, Clarke's introduction (LIFE 44), says "Gregory and Peter, the two youngest sons, became bishops eventually of Nyssa and Sebaste", and Peter is in the brief two paragraphs later. Heard once, "two of her brothers" says only two. Change to: "Her brothers included two famous bishops: Basil, of the great church of Caesarea, and Gregory himself, of Nyssa."
- **MINOR (note, not text).** The `Fixes_R50.md` item 4 citation calls PO15 10249–10254 "the Greek reply"; it is the Latins' reply. Cite PO15 308–309 and 1312–1314.
