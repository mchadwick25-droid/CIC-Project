# One-hearing test: Macrina the Younger

## Step 1. Three lines from one hearing

- **Who was this?** Macrina, the sister of Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa, a Christian woman of the fourth century in Pontus, on the Black Sea. Almost all we know of her comes from her brother Gregory.
- **What happened?** After the man she was promised to died, she refused to marry. She turned the family house into a community of prayer and shared work, where her mother lived as an equal with the slave women. She raised her brother Peter and babies abandoned in a famine, and died very poor in 379, praying that God had freed us from the fear of death.
- **Why does the connection matter?** Gregory's account of her house is used as an early picture of how such communities were run. He wrote a dialogue with her as the Teacher on the soul's life after death and the body's rising, and in 1438 the Latin side at Ferrara quoted her words from it, against the Greeks. But I could not say from the hearing why her words are in Gregory's book at all, or whether they are really hers.

## Step 2. Judgments against the outline

### Who was this? CLEAR

The outline's setting and tradition lines are both spoken: "the family of Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa" and "in the fourth century, in Pontus, a region on the Black Sea, in what is now Turkey." The witness line follows the outline's "say aloud" wording. The opening's "Gregory" and "his sister" join up with the second paragraph without effort.

### What happened? CLEAR

Story beats 2, 5 and 7 are all present and in order: the refused marriage and its reason (the hope of rising again), the mother living as an equal with the slave women, Peter and the foundlings, the dying prayer (Quote 1) and Lampadia's list (Quote 2). Beats 1, 3, 4 and 6 are left out. That is within the outline's 2-minute target, and leaving out the contested Basil beat is right. One small loss: the brief does not say why Lampadia is listing Macrina's things (Gregory asked her for burial clothes). The quote still does its job, so this does not lower the line.

### Why does the connection matter? WEAK

The outline's thread is that "her teaching on death and the hope of rising again was kept, in her voice, by the brother who sat with her as she died." Its heaviest legacy item is "Her teaching, in her voice": Gregory's dialogue is his "writing of their talk." The brief gives the dialogue and Ferrara, but it never tells the listener that the talk took place.

**Where it is lost:** last paragraph, sentences 2 and 3. "He also wrote a dialogue in which Macrina, as the Teacher, defends the soul's life after death and the body's rising. The words are Gregory's, so how much is hers is not known." Nothing earlier in the brief says that Macrina taught Gregory anything. The deathbed paragraph gives her only a prayer (outline beat 6, the talk on the soul, death and rising again, is cut). So "He also wrote a dialogue" sounds like a separate work of Gregory's in which he cast his sister. The next sentence ("how much is hers is not known") then lands as doubt that there is any of her in it at all. That leaves the Ferrara ending resting on a person whose link to the teaching the listener has just been told to doubt. The chain of what she passed on (her deathbed teaching, to Gregory, into his book, to the council a thousand years later) has its first link missing.

A smaller drag: the paragraph opens with "Later scholars use Gregory's account of her house as an early picture of how such a community was run." That is legacy item 3 put first, before the heavier items 1 and 2, and "such a community" asks the listener to recall the house from three paragraphs back. This alone would not make the line WEAK. The missing talk does.

## Verdict: FAIL

Two lines CLEAR, one WEAK.

## Fix (smallest, from the outline only)

Give back the first link of the chain, from outline story beat 6 and legacy item 1 (sheet 101-117, 165-169; LIFE 196 shows she "discoursed to us on the nature of the soul ... and the nature of the journey from death to life again").

In the last paragraph, replace:

> He also wrote a dialogue in which Macrina, as the Teacher, defends the soul's life after death and the body's rising.

with:

> As she lay dying, she had talked with him about the soul, death and rising again. From that talk he wrote a dialogue in which Macrina, as the Teacher, defends the soul's life after death and the body's rising.

The next sentence ("The words are Gregory's, so how much is hers is not known") stays as it is. It now reads as an honest limit on a teaching the listener knows happened, not as doubt about whether there was one.

This adds about 15 words (to about 400, well inside 3 minutes). It adds no new fact beyond the outline. It makes the story clearer, not simpler: the listener can now follow what passed from her, to whom, and on to Ferrara.

Not part of the fix: the "Later scholars use Gregory's account of her house" sentence comes before the heavier legacy items. It can stay where it is, since the outline's suggested last line is the end of the Ferrara exchange, and moving it would take that ending away.
