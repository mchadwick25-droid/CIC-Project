# Second Readings: macrina-the-younger

## 1. You, Lord, have set us free
Source: cic/texts/gregory-nyssa_opera-omnia-pg46-grc-lat_migne1863.txt 71687, 71690
Second rendering: "You, Lord," she said, "have freed us from the fear of death."
Ours: You, Lord, have set us free from the fear of death.
Verdict: same
Reason: Same meaning; ours drops the "she said" frame, which the brief supplies around the quote.

## 2. Here is the cloak, here is
Source: cic/texts/gregory-nyssa_opera-omnia-pg46-grc-lat_migne1863.txt 72083-72085
Second rendering: Look, here is the cloak, here is the veil for her head, and the worn-out shoes for her feet. This is her wealth.
Ours: Here is the cloak, here is the head-covering, and the worn-out shoes on her feet. This is her wealth.
Verdict: minor
Reason: Ours says the shoes are "on her feet", which could suggest she is wearing them; the Greek says only "shoes of the feet", and Lampadia is pointing to them as all that Macrina owned. Smallest fix if wanted: "the worn-out shoes for her feet".

## 3. met at Ferrara to try to heal the split between their churches (new narrated clause)
Source: cic/texts/patrologia-orientalis-tomus15_petit-documents-concile-de-florence-purgatoire-grc-fra_graffin-nau1927.txt 308-309, 1312-1314, 10249-10254
Second rendering: At Ferrara in 1438 the Pope asked the Greek bishops to take up, in private meetings, the questions of doctrine on which the two Churches differed. The delegates of the Western and Eastern Church met there to discuss how to restore the holy union of Latins and Greeks; the Latin side says they had come together for the sake of holy union, so that every discord might be removed.
Ours: More than a thousand years later, in 1438, Latin and Greek church leaders met at Ferrara to try to heal the split between their churches.
Verdict: same
Reason: "To try to heal the split" fairly carries "restituendi sanctam Latinorum Graecorumque unionem" (1314) and "sanctae unionis gratia convenimus … ut … omnis deleatur discordia" (10249-10254), with Petit's "divergence entre les deux Eglises" (308-309). One correction to the writer's note: 10249-10254 is the Latins' reply to the Greeks (heading "LATINORUM RESPONSIO AD LIBELLUM GRAECORUM", just above 10220), not the Greek reply. The clause still holds; 1312-1314, the opening of the Latin statement to both deputations, is the plainest support.

## 4. the Latin side quoted Macrina's part in the dialogue (reworded narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/patrologia-orientalis-tomus15_petit-documents-concile-de-florence-purgatoire-grc-fra_graffin-nau1927.txt 2304-2306
Second rendering: Gregory of Nyssa, in his dialogue on the state of souls after death, brings in Macrina, sister of blessed Basil and his own, speaking and saying...
Ours: Arguing there over what happens to souls after death, the Latin side quoted Macrina's part in the dialogue.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Introducit loquentem et dicentem Macrinam" is carried as "Macrina's part in the dialogue", which keeps Gregory as the author the brief has just named.
