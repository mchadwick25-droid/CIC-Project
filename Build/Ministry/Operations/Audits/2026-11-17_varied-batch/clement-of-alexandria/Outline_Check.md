## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. "Alexander, his pupil" is an editor's inference, but the outline marks it Documented. The rendering "master" leads the listener to the same inference.** The outline states it in What came of them item 3 ("Alexander, his pupil, remembered him. ... Documented"), in the Links ("Alexander, a bishop — taught him"), and through Q3's "who became my master". The Greek word is κύριος, "lord", here a courtesy title. Three things show this:
   - In the same sentence Alexander gives the same title to Origen, "κύριόν μου καὶ ἀδελφόν" (EUSG 3052). Origen was not his teacher.
   - Jerome's translator glosses the matching title in the Antioch letter as "literally 'lordly' perhaps like the conventional formula 'Lords and brethren'" (NP3 note at 40270–40272).
   - McGiffert says the pupil link is his own conclusion: "The epistle does not state this directly, but the conclusion seems sufficiently obvious" (EUS 34355–34358).

   Corrections:
   - Item 3: write "Alexander, a bishop who knew him, honoured him and remembered him". Confidence: Documented for the honour. If the pupil claim is kept at all, mark it Dominant Modern Reconstruction and say it is a modern inference.
   - Links: change "taught him" to "met; carried his letter; Clement dedicated a book to him". The dedication is at EUS 34056–34112 and NP3 40259–40261.
   - Q3 rendering: avoid "master" in the sense of teacher. Suggested wording: "Among them are Pantaenus, truly blessed and my lord, and the holy Clement, who became my lord and did me good." Add a note for the writer: "lord" is a courtesy title, as in "my lords and brothers". It does not mean a teacher.
   - Item 3: "through whom Alexander came to know Origen" names Clement alone. The text's "through whom" (δι᾽ ὧν) covers all the men named. Write "through these men".

2. **MINOR. In beat 7, "gone before" sits in quotation marks, but neither text has those words.** McGiffert has "trodden the way before us". The outline's own rendering has "went on ahead of us". Correction: use "who had gone on ahead of us", taken from the Q3 rendering, or drop the quotation marks. The beat also quotes McGiffert's English of the Antioch letter, whose Greek is held. Tell the writer that these short phrases are wording from the record, not further quotes. If one of them is spoken as a quote, it needs a rendering from EUSG 2401–2403 and 2490–2494.

3. **MINOR. Q2's "first of all" misleads.** In Wilson, "directly and primarily" is set against God giving philosophy "by consequence" (ANF2 27888–27890). The word names a direct cause, not "to begin with". Correction: "Perhaps philosophy was also given to the Greeks directly, as God's own gift, until the Lord should call them." Alternatively, "directly and in the first place". My own rendering otherwise matches theirs clause for clause. Q1 has nothing added or dropped. "Bee of Sicily" keeps the original's open question and adds nothing.

4. **MINOR. Beat 4 says "The Greek word he used meant the household servant who walked a child to school."** Clement's Greek is not held (sheet N2), so the beat cannot mark this Documented as a fact about his word. The support is the Galatians 3:24 echo (ANF2 note 1853 at 27893), Wilson's "leading as a pædagogue to Christ" for the same idea (ANF2 34509–34510), and the ANF note on *pædagogus* (ANF2 18697–18699). Correction: "Paul's word in Galatians, which Clement echoes here, meant the household servant who walked a child to his teacher." Cite those three lines.

5. **MINOR. Beat 1 and Opening 1 add to Clement's account of his search.** Clement does not say the search was "long". "Across the Greek world" leaves out the teachers "in the East", in Assyria and in Palestine (ANF2 27483–27487). Correction: drop "long" and write "from Greece to southern Italy and the East".

6. **MINOR. The Thread claims an effect the record does not hold.** "He showed the Christians of Alexandria that they could keep their learning and their faith together" says he changed his hearers. The record holds his argument (S5, T1, T3), not its effect on Alexandria's Christians. Correction: "...and he argued that a Christian could keep his learning and his faith together."

7. **MINOR. Beat 6 sets Eusebius's "all were driven away" right after Clement's teaching on flight.** Placed there, it implies that Clement left, which Leave out forbids (D3). Correction: drop that sentence from the beat, or tell the writer not to place it next to Clement's teaching on flight.
