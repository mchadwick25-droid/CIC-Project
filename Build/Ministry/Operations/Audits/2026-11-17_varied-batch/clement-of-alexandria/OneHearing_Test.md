# One-hearing test: Clement of Alexandria

Brief: `clement-of-alexandria.brief.txt` (347 words). Test date: 2026-11-17.

Mark's test: "An interested non-specialist can understand who this was, what happened, and why the connection matters after one hearing." Not simpler.

## Step 1. From one hearing alone

- **Who was this?** Clement, a Christian teacher and one of the elders who led the church at Alexandria in Egypt, around the year 200.
- **What happened?** After searching for a teacher across Greece, Italy and the East, he found one in Egypt (Pantaenus, Eusebius says), wrote his notes as the *Miscellanies*, and argued against fearful Christians that Greek philosophy was a guide God gave the Greeks to lead them to Christ, as the Law led the Hebrews. He lived through daily martyrdoms, carried a jailed bishop's letter to Antioch, and had died by the bishop's later letter.
- **Why does the connection matter?** Later church writers kept and trusted him: Eusebius used him as a witness, Jerome pointed to him to defend quoting pagan books, and Origen, a boy pupil (of Clement, most likely, though the pronoun wobbles), copied his *Miscellanies*. On one hearing the teacher-to-pupil line from Clement to Origen is the part I was least sure of.

### Where the connection line is lost

Last paragraph, final sentence: "And Origen, his pupil as a boy, followed his example with ten books of Miscellanies of his own..."

1. It comes straight after Jerome's quote, so "his pupil" and "his example" can attach to Jerome, the man just speaking. A listener hearing it once, perhaps in a second language, cannot go back to check. The brief never says earlier that Origen studied under Clement; the outline puts that in beat 3, but the brief leaves it to this one sentence.
2. The pupil claim is given as plain fact. The outline gives it as "by Eusebius's and Jerome's account" (Widely Accepted; D4: Origen's own words name Pantaenus). Naming the witnesses also tells the listener whose pupil is meant.

## Verdict

**FAIL.** Who and What are CLEAR; the connection line is WEAK.

## Smallest fix

Replace the final sentence with this:

> And Origen had been Clement's pupil as a boy, by Eusebius's and Jerome's account. He followed his teacher's example with ten books of Miscellanies of his own, setting Christian views beside those of the philosophers.

This names Clement, so "his" can no longer mean Jerome. It says "teacher", which makes the line from Pantaenus to Clement to Origen plain. It also attributes the claim as the outline does. The material all comes from the outline (beat 3, I1). It adds about 9 words, so the brief runs to about 356, well under 450. Nothing is simplified.
