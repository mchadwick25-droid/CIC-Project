# Fixes R50: Clement of Alexandria

Ruling: Mark, 2026-11-17 ("yes no softening, we are a witness not a judge … for this round 1 but also the should fixes too"). Released text kept as `clement-of-alexandria.r50.brief.txt`.

## Fix-list items

1. **Cut the stock "dangerous time" phrase; show danger with the sourced detail.**
   - Old: "It was a dangerous time to be a Christian, and Clement wrote that he saw martyrs burnt, impaled and beheaded every day."
   - New: "Clement wrote that every day he saw martyrs, people who died rather than deny Christ, burnt, impaled and beheaded."
   - Source: Stromata II.20, ANF2 34507–34509 ("every day abundant sources of martyrs that are burnt, impaled, beheaded"); the gloss of "martyr" is Outline beat 6 and Source Sheet S4. Opened and confirmed.

2. **Replace "a city of libraries and lecture halls" or cut it.** Cut: the phrase traces only to the census (CEN 32632), not to a held text.
   - Old: "As one of the elders who led the church at Alexandria beside its bishop, Clement taught in a city of libraries and lecture halls."
   - New: "Clement was one of the elders who led the church at Alexandria beside its bishop, and he taught there."
   - Source: S2 (Jerome, "presbyter of the Alexandrian church", NP3 40249–40250); M4 (taught at Alexandria, EUS 33015–33080). Outline beat 3.

3. **Say what Eusebius did.**
   - Old: "Eusebius kept his books and trusted him as a witness to the earliest church."
   - New: "Eusebius listed his books, quoted them, and trusted him as a witness to the earliest church."
   - Source: I2 and M5 — list at EUS 33868–34112 (opened: "All the eight Stromata of Clement are preserved among us…"); quotation of Clement's John story, "This extract from Clement I have inserted here … for the benefit of my readers" (EUS 17379–17381, opened); trustworthy witness, EUS 17044–17049 (opened).

4. **End on Clement and what passed from him, not on a chain of later names.** The last paragraph is split and reordered. Eusebius and Origen (who carried on his method) now come first; the piece ends on Jerome naming Clement as his precedent for using pagan learning, so the last words are about Clement's own books and the idea he argued for.
   - Old order: Eusebius → Jerome → Origen ("…setting Christian views beside those of the philosophers.").
   - New order: Eusebius → Origen (one paragraph); Jerome (closing paragraph), ending "…whose books were drawn 'from the very heart of philosophy'."
   - Added two words to the Jerome sentence for one-hearing clarity: "Jerome pointed to Clement" → "For his answer, Jerome pointed to Clement". Source: I4, JER 16477–16479 (Magnus's question) and 16626–16636 (the answer).
