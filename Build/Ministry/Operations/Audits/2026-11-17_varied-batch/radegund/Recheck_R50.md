- **Who:** Radegund, a Thuringian princess taken as spoil by the Franks in the sixth century and made King Lothar's wife, who became a deaconess and the founder of a monastery for women at Poitiers.
- **What happened:** After Lothar had her brother killed she left the court and made Bishop Medard consecrate her. With the king's backing she founded the house at Poitiers under Caesarius's Rule, brought wood of the Cross from the East, and wrote to the bishops to guard the house after her death. Soon after she died, forty or more nuns revolted.
- **Why the connection matters:** Her letter and her customs were what the abbess used to hold the house against the revolt, and Gregory of Tours kept her letter by copying it into his History.

## Findings

### M1. MINOR: her own act of founding dropped from the letter's sentence

"Her own letter says King Lothar founded and enriched her monastery for women at Poitiers." The letter's subject is Radegund: "I established at Poitiers a monastery for nuns, founded and enriched by ... King Lothar" (DAL 19846–19848; "instituente atque remunerante ... rege Chlothario, monastirium puellarum Pectava urbe constitui", 31498–31499). The fix list was right that she did not build it alone. The new wording now tips the other way and leaves the king as the only founder, in a sentence credited to her own letter. Not blocking: "her monastery" keeps it hers, and the next sentence has her give it everything. But one word set right makes it the letter's own sentence. Change to:

> Her own letter says she set up her monastery for women at Poitiers, founded and enriched by King Lothar. She gave it all the property the king had given her.
