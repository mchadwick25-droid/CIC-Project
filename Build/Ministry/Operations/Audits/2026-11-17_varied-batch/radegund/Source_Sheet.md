Researcher's sheet for the varied batch, 2026-11-17. The writer gets the outline; this sheet is the evidence behind it. No story prose.

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own. English quotes from DAL are exact, with Dalton's footnote markers (* and superscript figures) left out and words split across lines joined; where a quote crosses a page break, the page head or footnote between is left out and the citation says so. The Latin files are raw OCR, with the editors' variant marks (^, ', ", *, and letter-flags) printed inside the words and many OCR misreadings (r read as ra, n as u, e as c). **In sections 1–7 every Latin quote is a reading**: apparatus marks and margin numbers left out, OCR misreadings corrected, and the main misreadings named after the citation ("OCR ..."). **Section 8 gives the raw file lines for every Latin quote, exactly as in the file**, so the checker can re-verify each reading against the file text. Nothing else is changed.

**No English of the Lives is held.** Every rendering from VR, GRL and VF below is mine, from the Latin. DAL is a printed English translation; where I render a passage of Gregory, I render from GRL's Latin and say so.

**Witness kinds used.**
- OWN = her own letter to the bishops (kept by Gregory, DAL IX.42 / GRL IX.42).
- CONTEMP = Gregory of Tours (knew her, visited her, buried her; DAL 17576–17577; GRL 38121–38122). Fortunatus (her friend; wrote the Life after her death in 587, VR-K 28175–28176). Baudonivia (a nun of her house whom Radegund raised "from the cradle", VR-B 29449–29450; Krusch dates her Life after 600, VR-K 28186, so some 15 years or more after the death).
- LATER = modern editors (Dalton 1927, Krusch 1885/1888/1896, Leo 1881). Card only, except where a date or place rests on them; then it says so.

**Time gaps.** Gregory wrote within a few years of her death (he says he was present at her burial, DAL 17576–17577). Fortunatus wrote after 587 (VR-K 28175–28176). Baudonivia wrote after 600 (VR-K 28186–28191). All three knew her. None is written a century later. The later witnesses are friends and members of her own house; their praise is praise from inside (see D6).

## 1. Setting

S1. **Her people and family.** Gregory: three brothers ruled the Thuringians; "Hermanfrid overcame his brother Berthar by force of arms and slew him. At his death he left an orphan daughter, Radegund, with sons, of whom I shall hereafter speak" (DAL 4191–4195, III.4). CONTEMP. Documented. Fortunatus: "Beatissima igitur Radegundis natione barbara de regione Thoringa, avo rege Bessino, patruo Hermenfredo, patre rege Bertechario" (VR-F 28558–28559), her grandfather King Bessinus, her uncle Hermenfred, her father King Bertechar. CONTEMP. Documented.

S2. **Capture.** "Lothar, returning home, took with him as his captive Radegund, daughter of King Berthar ; her he wedded, though afterwards he caused her brother to be slain by evil men." (DAL 4379–4381, III.7). CONTEMP. Documented. The year: Dalton's footnote to this campaign reads "About A. D. 531." (DAL 4362). LATER (editor). Widely Accepted. No held source of her own time gives the year of the capture or of her birth.

S3. **The king's name.** Gregory's English (Dalton) calls him "Lothar" (DAL 4379); the Latin Lives call him "Chlotharius" (VR-F 28565) and "Chlotario" (VR-B 29745). Say "King Lothar" or "the Frankish king Lothar", one form throughout.

S4. **Places.** Raised "Adteias in villa regia" (VR-F 28565), the royal estate at Athies (Krusch's note: "Athies (Somme, arr. Péronne ...)", VR 28617–28618, LATER). Consecrated at Noyon, "ad beatum Medardum Novomago" (VR-F 28798). Her monastery at Poitiers, "monastirium puellarum Pectava urbe constitui" (GRL 31499–31500, her letter; OCR "consfifxii") / "I established at Poitiers a monastery for nuns" (DAL 19846–19847). OWN. Documented.

S5. **Century and death.** Gregory: "In this year the most blessed Radegund passed away from this world, leaving behind her great lamentation in the monastery which she had founded ; I myself was present at her burying. She died on the thirteenth day of the sixth month, and was buried three days afterwards." (DAL 17574–17578, IX.2). Dalton's footnote gives the month as "August" (DAL 17602, OCR "3 August"). Book IX opens "IN THE TWELFTH YEAR OF KING CHILDEBERT" (DAL 17549–17551). Baudonivia: "Quarta feria mane primo Idus Agustas, quod fecit idem mensis dies 13, clausi sunt eius oculi, et obscurati sunt nostri" (VR-B 30521–30522). CONTEMP. Documented (day and month). The year 587 is Krusch's reckoning: "S. Radegundis a. 12. Childeberti regis, i. e. a Chr. n. 587, die 13. Augusti, feria quarta obiit" (GRL 61607, OCR "quarfa"). LATER (editor). Widely Accepted. Plain ways to say it: "in the sixth century"; "she died in 587". Do not give a birth year or an age at death: none is held.

S6. **Tradition.** Census name **Merovingian Gallic Christianity**; short name "Merovingian Gaul"; informal name "The Church of Merovingian Gaul" (CEN 39517–39519). The census describes Gaul after the Frankish king Clovis "accepted baptism as a Nicene Christian around the turn of the sixth century" (CEN 39579). (a) Plain spoken forms that the record supports: (1) "the church of Frankish Gaul". Support: Gregory's work is the *History of the Franks* (DAL title page); the bishops who wrote to her held sees at "Tours, Rouen, Paris, Nantes, Angers, Rennes, and Le Mans" (DAL 19473–19474, Dalton's note). (2) "the kingdom of the Franks, in what is now France". "France" is the census's modern country (CEN 106693), not a word of her time. (3) Her own phrase for the church's leaders: "the holy fathers in Christ and most worthy occupants of their apostolic seats, the bishops" (DAL 19830–19831). (b) Do not say "Merovingian" aloud (a dynasty name the listener does not need). Do not say "Catholic" in its modern sense: her letter's "Catholic king" (DAL 19945–19946) means a king of the common, Nicene faith of Gaul's bishops.

## 2. Life events (in order)

L1. **Kings quarrel over the captive.** "Tunc inter ipsos victores, cuius esset in praeda regalis puella, fit contentio de captiva, et, nisi reddita fuisset, transacto certamine, in se reges arma movissent." (VR-F 28562–28564; OCR "fuissef"). The victors quarrelled over whose spoil the royal girl was; had she not been handed over, the kings would have fought each other. CONTEMP (Fortunatus). Documented as his account. Krusch repeats it (VR-K 28109–28110).

L2. **Raised at Athies; taught to read.** "Quae veniens in sortem praecelsi regis Chlotharii, in Veromandensem ducta, Adteias in villa regia nutriendi causa custodibus est deputata. Quae puella inter alia opera, quae sexui eius congruebant, litteris est erudita" (VR-F 28564–28567; OCR "Veroraandensera", "Qutriendi", "congi'uebant"). She fell to King Lothar's lot, was taken to the Vermandois, and was put in the care of guardians at the royal estate of Athies to be brought up; among other work fit for a girl she was taught letters. CONTEMP. Documented as Fortunatus's account. Fortunatus adds that as a child she talked with other children of wanting to be a martyr, and led them in play processions with a wooden cross (VR-F 28567–28568, 28573–28575). Childhood anecdotes: Inferential-Thin as fact (a friend's Life, told of a childhood he did not see); keep out of the story (see Leave out).

L3. **Married the king.** Gregory: "her he wedded" (DAL 4380). Fortunatus: "Nubit ergo terreno principi, nec tamen separata caelesti" (VR-F 28629; OCR "Nubif"), she married an earthly prince, yet was not parted from the heavenly one. Fortunatus says the king had tried to fetch her to Vitry and she slipped away from Athies by night (VR-F 28624–28625). CONTEMP. Marriage Documented; the flight to avoid the wedding is Fortunatus's alone.

L4. **A queen who served the poor.** At Athies she set up a house where she gathered poor women, bathed them herself, and tended their sores: "Adteias domum instruit, quo, lectis culte compositis, congregatis egenis feminis, ipsa eas lavans in termis morborumque curans putredines" (VR-F 28643–28645; OCR "eass"). "Sic devota femina nata et nupta regina, palatii domina pauperibus serviebat ancilla." (VR-F 28647). So the devout woman, born and married a queen, mistress of the palace, served the poor as a maid. Baudonivia: "in redemptione captivorum sollicita, in egenorum erogatione profusa" (VR-B 29660–29661), anxious to ransom captives, lavish in giving to the needy. CONTEMP. Widely Accepted (two witnesses of her house agree on the pattern; details are each one's own).

L5. **"A nun rather than a queen."** She rose at night from the king's bed to pray on the cold floor in a hair shirt (VR-F 28698–28702). "De qua regi dicebatur, habere se potius iugalem monacham quam reginam. Unde et ipse irritatus, pro bonis erat asperrimus" (VR-F 28702–28704; OCR "?-el", ":juam"). It was said to the king that he had a nun for a wife rather than a queen; this angered him, and he was very harsh to her for her good deeds. CONTEMP. Documented as Fortunatus's report of a saying at court. Note: the Latin is "dicebatur", "it was said to the king" (the courtiers' saying, or the king's own complaint; the grammar allows "it was said of her by the king"). Krusch paraphrases it as the king's own view, "ut rex 'potius iugalem monacham quam reginam habere' sibi videretur" (VR-K 28113). Tell it as "people at court said", or "the king complained", but say which reading is used; see D4.

L6. **Burning a shrine.** On her way to a meal with a lady named Ansifrida, a mile from her road "fanus, qui a Francis colebatur" (a shrine worshipped by the Franks); "iussit famulis fanum igni conburi" (she ordered her servants to burn it); the Franks tried to defend it "cum gladiis et fustibus"; she sat her horse unmoved "Christum in pectore gestans" until the shrine burned and, at her prayer, the people made peace with each other (VR-B 29663–29676). CONTEMP (Baudonivia; she writes "quod audivimus dicimus et quod vidimus testamur", VR-B 29667). Documented as Baudonivia's account; told only by her. Side story: keep out (see Leave out), or use only with her name and in her voice.

L7. **Her brother killed; she leaves the court.** Gregory: Lothar "afterwards he caused her brother to be slain by evil men" (DAL 4381). Fortunatus: "frater interficitur innocenter" (VR-F 28797), her brother is killed, innocent. Then: "Directa igitur a rege veniens ad beatum Medardum Novomago, supplicat instanter, ut ipsam, mutata veste, Domino consecraret." (VR-F 28797–28800). She came to blessed Medard at Noyon and begged him earnestly to change her dress and consecrate her to the Lord. CONTEMP. Documented. "Directa a rege" is unclear: it can mean "sent by the king" or "setting out from the king" (see D3). Medard was bishop of Noyon (DAL 25329–25331, Dalton's note: "It was to Médard, then bishop of Noyon, that Radegund went first when she abandoned the Court of Lothar I").

L8. **The altar at Noyon.** Medard held back, remembering the apostle: "Si qua ligata sit coniugi, non quaerat dissolvi" (VR-F 28800–28801; Paul, 1 Cor 7). "Adhuc beatum virum perturbabant proceres et per basilicam graviter ab altari retrahebant, ne velaret regi coniunctam, ne videretur sacerdoti, ut praesumeret principi subducere reginam non publicanam sed publicam." (VR-F 28801–28804). The nobles harassed the holy man and dragged him roughly back from the altar through the church, so he would not veil the king's wife, nor seem as a priest to dare take from the prince a queen, not a "publicana" but a public one. ("publicana" = a harlot, Krusch's note, VR 28838, LATER; a pun the story does not need.) "Quo sanctissima cognito intrans in sacrarium, monachica veste induitur, procedit ad altare, beatissimum Medardum his verbis alloquitur" (VR-F 28804–28806; OCR "induituri"): she learned of it, went into the sacristy, put on a nun's dress, came out to the altar and spoke to Medard (her words: Q1). "Quo ille contestationis concussus tonitruo, manu superposita, consecravit diaconam." (VR-F 28808–28809). Shaken by the thunder of her charge, he laid his hand on her and consecrated her a deaconess. CONTEMP. Documented as Fortunatus's account. Gregory does not tell this scene; Baudonivia refers to it only as her leaving the king (VR-B 29677). "Diaconam" = deaconess (a woman set apart for church service; here, by Fortunatus's account, the rite that made her a consecrated woman). Say "consecrated her a deaconess"; do not say "ordained her a deacon" in a modern sense.

L9. **Giving away the queen's clothes.** At once she laid her festal robe on the altar, and gave a heavy gold belt, broken up, "opus in pauperum" (for the poor) (VR-F 28844–28847; OCR "iii pauperum"). At the cells of saints she gave her ornaments to the altars, then sailed to Tours and gave more at the shrine of Martin and at Candes, where Martin died (VR-F 28847–28861). CONTEMP. Documented as Fortunatus's account. Dalton notes her gifts to Martin's cell at Candes from Baudonivia (DAL 29006–29008).

L10. **The king wants her back.** At her estate of Saix ("Suedas ... quam ei rex dederat", VR-B 29678) word came "quasi eam rex iterum vellet accipere" (as if the king wanted to take her back) (VR-B 29687–29688). She wrote to the recluse John of Chinon to pray "ne iterum ad saeculum reverteretur" (that she not go back to the world); "ipsa ante optaret vitam finire quam regi terreno iterum iungi" (she would rather end her life than be joined again to an earthly king) (VR-B 29732–29738). Later the king came to Tours meaning to go on to Poitiers "ut suam reginam acciperet"; she wrote to Germanus, bishop of Paris; Germanus fell at the king's feet at Martin's tomb; the king repented and sent Germanus to ask her pardon, which she gave (VR-B 29799–29822). CONTEMP (Baudonivia). Documented as her account; only she tells it. Dalton: "he appears to have treated Radegund with consideration after she left him, and contributed with generosity to the accomplishment of her designs" (DAL 29616–29618). LATER.

L11. **Founding at Poitiers; the king's endowment.** Her own words: "I established at Poitiers a monastery for nuns, founded and enriched by the most excellent lord King Lothar ; this monastery after its foundation I myself endowed by the gift of all the property which the royal munificence had bestowed upon me." (DAL 19846–19850). Latin: "instituente atque remunerante praecellentissimo domno rege Chlothario, monastirium puellarum Pectava urbe constitui, conditumque, quantum mihi munificentia regalis est largita, facta donatione, dotavi" (GRL 31496–31500; OCR "consfifxii" = constitui). OWN. Documented. Baudonivia: Bishop Pientius and Duke Austrapius built it quickly "per ordinationem dominicam" (by the king's order) (VR-B 29744–29746). Gregory: "This queen turned to God and, changing her habit, built for herself a monastery in Poitiers" (DAL 4382–4383). CONTEMP.

L12. **Agnes made abbess; Radegund obeys her.** "With the approval of the most blessed bishops of Poitiers and the other sees, and by the choice of our own community, I appointed as abbess the lady Agnes, my sister, whom I have loved and brought up from her earliest youth; and I submitted myself in regular obedience to her authority next to that of God." (DAL 19856–19860). OWN. Documented. "Sister" is the religious sense, not blood: Dalton, "Agnes is called sister in the religious sense" (DAL 29620–29621). Latin: "loco filiae colui et eduxi" (raised her as a daughter) (GRL 31506–31508; OCR "Agnitem", "filiue"). Agnes was consecrated by Germanus of Paris (DAL 19888–19890). Gregory, about her humility: "how conspicuous for humility was the blessed Radegund, foundress of that monastery" (DAL 19429–19430). CONTEMP.

L13. **The Rule of Caesarius.** "I appointed for this community gathered together under Christ's protection the Rule according to which the holy Caesaria lived, and which the care of the blessed Caesarius, bishop of Arles, had compiled to suit her needs from the institutions of the holy Fathers" (DAL 19851–19855). OWN. Documented. A "Rule" = the written law of life for a monastery. Caesarius of Arles wrote it for his sister Caesaria's convent (DAL 29560–29567, Dalton's note). The rule kept the nuns inside the walls for life: the bishops' letter, "it shall never be permitted to her to leave it, seeing that, as ordered by the Rule, she hath entered of her own desire" (DAL 19543–19545); Krusch cites Baudonivia, "quia instituerat, ut nulla vivens foras monasterii ianuam egrederetur" (GRL 61694, Krusch's note). How the Rule came: Gregory says she "was forced to go to Arles with the abbess whom she had appointed. There they received the Rule of the holy Caesarius and the blessed Caesaria" (DAL 19627–19629). Contested (D2).

L14. **All goods given up.** "I myself and my sisters, when we entered the monastery, made over by deed all our substance in earthly possessions, reserving nothing for ourselves, from fear of that which befell Ananias and Sapphira." (DAL 19860–19871; a footnote and page head fall between 19862 and 19868). OWN. Documented. (Ananias and Sapphira: a couple in the book of Acts who kept back money and died for it.)

L15. **Humble work inside.** Fortunatus: she swept the floors, cleaned the latrines, carried wood, cooked in her turn, washed the sick (VR-F 29073–29079 and the chapter after). Baudonivia: "ipsa suis manibus lavit et tersit infirmantum vestigia" (she washed and wiped the feet of the sick with her own hands) (VR-B 29875). CONTEMP. Widely Accepted as the pattern (two witnesses). Her ascetic self-punishments (iron chains grown into the flesh, VR-F 29093–29140): Fortunatus's account; leave out (see Leave out).

L16. **The cross from the East.** Gregory: "In the time of Sigibert, when Maroveus had obtained the see, the blessed Radegund, moved by her faith and devotion, sent clerics into the East to procure wood of the True Cross, and relics of the holy apostles and the other martyrs; they took with them letters from King Sigibert. They set forth, and duly returned with the relics. Upon the delivery of these, the queen requested the bishop himself to deposit them in the convent with chanting of psalms and all due honour. But he disregarded her proposal, mounting his horse, and going off to a country estate." (DAL 19601–19610). Then King Sigibert "enjoined the blessed Eufronius, bishop of Tours, to perform this task ; who, coming with his clergy to Poitiers, in the absence of the bishop of that city, brought the holy relics to the monastery with much chanting of psalms, with pomp of gleaming tapers and incense." (DAL 19620–19625). CONTEMP. Documented. Baudonivia: she asked Sigibert's leave "pro totius patriae salute et eius regni stabilitate" (for the welfare of the whole country and the stability of his kingdom) to ask the emperor for the wood of the Cross; she sent no gifts, only messengers and prayers; the emperor sent the Cross adorned with gold and gems, and gospels (VR-B 30246–30256; OCR "Htteras", "euangeHis"). CONTEMP. Fortunatus's poem to the Emperor Justin and the Empress Sophia thanks them: "regina poscente sibi Radegunde Thoringa / praebuit optatae munera sacra crucis" (VF 23582, 23584; OCR "poseente", "Tlioringa"), at the asking of the Thuringian queen Radegund, [Sophia] gave the holy gift of the longed-for Cross. CONTEMP. Documented. The emperor's name as Justin II with Sophia: Dalton's note (DAL 29579–29580) and the poems' running head "II. AD IVSTINVM" (VF 23413, 23577). The bishop's refusal: Baudonivia tells it differently from Gregory. She says the bishop and all the people wished to receive the Cross devoutly, but "inimicus humani generis per satellites suos egit, ut pretium mundi repellerent nec in civitatem recipere vellent" (the enemy of mankind worked through his followers so that they rejected the price of the world and would not receive it into the city) (VR-B 30257–30259; OCR "huinani", "satelHtes", "repcnerent"). Gregory says the bishop himself rode off (DAL 19608–19610). Contested as to who refused; see D5.

L17. **Like Helena.** Gregory: "Huius reliquias et merito et fide Helenae conparanda regina Radegundis expetiit ac devote in monasterium Pictavensim, quod suo studio constituit, collocavit" (GRL 38071–38072, 38103; OCR "Helcnae"). Queen Radegund, to be compared with Helena in merit and faith, sought relics of the Cross and placed them devoutly in the monastery at Poitiers which she founded by her own effort. Baudonivia: "Quod fecit illa in orientali patria, hoc fecit beata Radegundis in Gallia" (VR-B 30245; OCR "Kadegundis in GalHa"). What Helena did in the East, Radegund did in Gaul. CONTEMP (two witnesses). Documented as their comparison. (Helena: the emperor Constantine's mother, said to have found the Cross at Jerusalem, GRL 38070.)

L18. **Praying and writing for peace.** Baudonivia: "Semper de pace sollicita, de salute patriae curiosa, quandoquidem inter se regna movebantur, quia totos diligebat reges, pro omnium vita orabat" (VR-B 29987–29988; OCR "soUicita", "movebautur"); "quales litteras uni, tales alteri dirigebat, ut inter se non bella nec arma tractarent, sed pacem firmarent, et patria ne periret" (VR-B 29990–29991). Always anxious for peace and the country's welfare when the kingdoms rose against each other, she loved all the kings and prayed for the life of all; she sent the same letters to one as to the other, that they not make war but make peace, so the country would not perish. She had the nuns keep vigils for them (VR-B 29993–29994). CONTEMP (Baudonivia only). Documented as her account. Dalton: "Radegund likewise strove to prevent intestine war" (DAL 26020). LATER. The kings were Lothar's sons (her letter names "Charibert, Guntram, Chilperic, and Sigibert", DAL 19902–19903).

L19. **Her letter to the bishops.** "I remit to you, apostolic fathers, in my lifetime, and with all due devotion, this page containing my prayer to you" (DAL 19874–19876). "And since I cannot in person throw myself at your feet, I make prostration vicariously through this letter, and by the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and by the tremendous Day of Judgement, I adjure you, as if ye stood before me, to protect us from any tyrant, and secure to us the favour of our rightful king." (DAL 19878–19883). She names who might threaten the house after her death: "whether the bishop of the city, or a royal officer, or any other person" who would "trouble the sisterhood, or to break the Rule, or appoint any other abbess than my sister Agnes" (DAL 19884–19888). She asks to be buried in the basilica of Mary she had begun (DAL 19974–19980) and that the letter "signed by my own hand, be preserved among the archives of the cathedral church" (DAL 19983–19985). OWN. Documented. The date of the letter is not in the text; "in my lifetime" (DAL 19875) only. Card: Sirmond's dating (card "uncertain").

L20. **Her greeting.** Dalton's address reads "Radegund of Poitiers" (DAL 19831). The Latin text prints "omnibus episcopis Radegundis peccatrix" (Radegund, a sinner) (GRL 31490; OCR "omnibiis", "Radeytmdis"), with a manuscript variant "Pictavensis" (of Poitiers) in the apparatus (GRL 31527, OCR "7)5" = D5). The two printed texts differ; see D7.

L21. **Leading a recluse to her cell.** Gregory: a nun saw a vision and asked to live shut in a cell; "all the virgins assembled with much chanting, having their lamps lighted ; thus was the maid escorted to her place, the blessed Radegund herself leading her by the hand." (DAL 12338–12341, VI.29). CONTEMP. Documented. Side scene.

L22. **"Unseemly that a maid dedicated to Christ should return."** King Chilperic wanted to send his daughter from the monastery to Spain to be married. "But she declined, the blessed Radegund above all opposing the project, declaring: 'It is unseemly that a maid dedicated to Christ should return to the pleasures of the world.'" (DAL 12626–12630, VI.34). CONTEMP. Documented. Shows her defending the vow against a king.

L23. **Her name trusted.** The pretender Gundovald, besieged, said: "make inquiry of Radegund at Poitiers and Ingitrude at Tours, for they will confirm my words as true." (DAL 14986–14987, VII.36). CONTEMP. Documented. Side detail; Dalton's note on it (DAL 28436–28440) LATER.

L24. **Death and burial.** S5 for the date. Gregory came because the local bishop was away: Baudonivia, "non erat ibi pontifex loci. Ambulavit nuntius ad virum apostolicum domnum Gregorium Turonicae civitatis episcopum, et advenit" (VR-B 30536–30538). Gregory: "Ecce frater noster Maroveus huius urbis episcopus non est coram, eo quod illum causa visendarum parochiarum elongaverit" (GRL 61646–61647; OCR "Marovous", "parocliiarum"): Maroveus, bishop of this city, is not here, being away visiting his parishes. Gregory blessed the altar in the burial place himself ("altare in cellula ipsa sacravi", GRL 61658) and buried her. CONTEMP. Documented. Burial in the basilica of Mary: Baudonivia as Krusch quotes her, "in basilica sanctae Mariae nomine condita, ubi sacra virginum corpora de monasterio suo conduntur, eam cum digno sepelivit honore" (GRL 61690–61691; OCR "couduntur", "lionore"). CONTEMP. Dalton thinks Maroveus "may have preferred not to be in Poitiers at the time of her death" (DAL 29079–29080). LATER; a guess; card only.

L25. **The two hundred at the bier.** "Stabant enim circa feretrum multitudo inmensa sanctimonialium ad numerum circiter ducentarum, quae per illius praedicationem conversae, vitam sanctam agebant, quae secundum saeculi dignitatem non modo de senatoribus, verum etiam nonnullae de ipsa regali stirpe hac religionis forma florebant." (GRL 61577–61580; OCR "praedicationera", "agebants", "dignitatera"). Around the bier stood a great crowd of nuns, about two hundred, turned to religion by her teaching, living a holy life; by worldly rank some were of senatorial families, and some of royal stock itself. CONTEMP (Gregory, eyewitness). Documented. Their lament: Q3.

L26. **Wonders at the death (in the witnesses' voices).** Gregory: her face at the bier "ita fulgebat, ut liliorum rosarumque sperneret pulchritudinem" (shone so that it scorned the beauty of lilies and roses) (GRL 61576–61577). As the body was carried, the possessed cried out that she tormented them (GRL 61659–61660). Baudonivia: stone-cutters on a hill heard an angel's voice at her death (VR-B 30527–30529). Fortunatus: a tax official dreamed of her on the day she died, and freed seven prisoners (VR-F 29367–29383). CONTEMP. Documented as their reports. WONDER claims: keep in the witness's voice, or leave out.

## 3. Quotes (verbatim; for the outline's 2–3)

Q1. **Her words at the altar (Fortunatus, Latin).** "Si me consecrare distuleris et plus hominem quam Deum timueris, de manu tua, pastor, ovis anima requiratur." (VR-F 28806, 28808; line 28807 is blank; the mark after "pastor" left out; "cf. Gen. 9, .5." at the head of line 28808 is the editor's margin reference, not text). CONTEMP. Q2. **Her letter (her own words).** Latin: "Et quia praesens non valui, quasi vestris provoluta vestigiis, epistulae vicarietate prosternor, coniurans per Patrem et Filium et Spiritum sanctum ac diem tremendi iudicii" (GRL 31516–31517, 31568; OCR "Ef quia", "epistidae", "Fatrem", "Fiiium"). Q3. **The nuns at her bier (Gregory, Latin).** "Cui nos orfanas, mater sancta, relinques? Cui nos desolatas conmendas? Reliquimus parentes facultatesque ac patriam et te secutae sumus." ## 4. Impact as the record gives it (heaviest first)

I1. **The house and its Rule outlasted her, and her own letter defended it.** DEFENDED: the Rule, the enclosure, the abbess, the property. She wrote to the bishops to guard it from "any tyrant" (DAL 19882) and from "the bishop of the city, or a royal officer" (DAL 19884–19885), and asked that "none may ever avail to diminish or to change in anything either our Rule or the possessions of the monastery" (DAL 19910–19919; a page head falls between 19910 and 19918). After her death the house was tested. Soon after (Book IX covers 587–589, DAL 17543), "forty or more nuns" led by Clotild walked out (DAL 19411–19413). Gregory used the bishops' early letter to Radegund against them, warning "lest that be wantonly dispersed which the holy Radegund with perpetual fasts and prayers and with constant charity brought together" (DAL 19440–19442), and read it to them: "After the reading of this letter Clotild said" (DAL 19446–19449, 19584). "The abbess also read out the letter which the blessed Radegund addressed to the bishops of her own time. Of this letter she now again sent copies to the bishops of the neighbouring cities." (DAL 19821–19824). At the bishops' trial the abbess defended the house's ways as Radegund's: the bath given to the servants because "the lady Radegund, fearing harm to the health of the nuns" (DAL 21394–21396); "she had not introduced any new usage, but had continued the custom of the lady Radegund" (DAL 21416–21418). CONTEMP. Documented. This is the heaviest item: later witnesses carried her letter, quoted it, copied it and argued from it. Gregory kept the full text in his *History* (DAL 19827–20001).

I2. **Women drawn after her.** The bishops: "many virgins have eagerly flocked out of our dioceses to accept thy Rule" (DAL 19528–19530); "each abandoning her kin chooseth thee rather than her own mother" (DAL 19514–19515). At her death about two hundred nuns, some of senatorial and some of royal families (GRL 61577–61580, L25). King Chilperic placed his daughter there (DAL 12626–12627). CONTEMP. Documented. The bishops' decree, at her asking, made the nun's vow binding for life (DAL 19537–19545), and was meant to be "observed for evermore" (DAL 19582). VR-K summarises the decree as asked by her: "Radegunde petente, episcopi sanxerunt" (VR-K 28133–28134; OCR "epi.^copi"). LATER (editor).

I3. **The relic of the Cross, and what it drew.** Gregory saw a wonder at it with his own eyes: he visited "huius reginae ... colloquia" (to talk with this queen), knelt before the Cross, and saw a lamp before it overflow with oil, "plus quam unum sestarium" in an hour from a vessel that held "quartarium" (GRL 38121–38134). He ends: "Admiratusque silui ac virtutem adorandae crucis deinceps praedicavi" (GRL 38134–38135), I was amazed and silent, and from then on I preached the power of the holy Cross. CONTEMP. Documented as his own account. WONDER, in his voice. Baudonivia: at the Cross "caecorum oculi lumen recipiunt" (the eyes of the blind receive light) and others healed; "Quis queat dicere, quantum et quale donum huic urbi beata contulit?" (who could say how great a gift the blessed one gave this city?) (VR-B 30319–30323). CONTEMP. WONDER, in her voice. The house took the name of the Cross: Dalton's footnote, "The monastery of the Holy Cross, founded by Radegund" (DAL 19420); Krusch, "Monasterium autem quod construxit Pictavicum nomen S. Crucis accepit" (VR-K 28153). LATER (editors). Widely Accepted. The relic in its gold case "is still at Poitiers in the present convent of the Holy Cross" (DAL 29592–29593, Dalton 1927). LATER. Card only.

I4. **Hymns for the Cross.** Dalton: the relic's arrival "is celebrated by Fortunatus in several hymns (Caym. ii. 1-7), the most famous of which is that beginning : Vewilla Regis prodeunt" (DAL 29581–29583; OCR "Caym." = Carm., "Vewilla" = Vexilla). LATER (editor's link). The hymn is held only in unreadable OCR ("Vexillii regis imideinit", VF 4424; heading "IImiiiihs iii liniiiiic sjiiictac cnicis" = Hymnus in honore sanctae crucis, VF 4422). The census calls Fortunatus the poet "whose hymns to the Cross are still sung" (CEN 39587). Widely Accepted that the hymn was written for the relic; the held contemporary record that ties it to her is only Fortunatus's poem to Justin (VF 23582, 23584). Say it as: "Fortunatus wrote hymns for the Cross; one, 'The banners of the King go forth', is still sung" only if the writer accepts that the link to her relic rests on the editor. Safer, in my words: the poet Fortunatus wrote verses to thank the emperor (VF 23582, 23584).

I5. **Two Lives, and a second asked for by her nuns.** Fortunatus wrote first, after 587 (VR-K 28175–28176). The nuns felt it left things out, the Cross and her death (VR-K 28177–28180, Krusch's judgement, LATER). Baudonivia: "Iniungitis mihi opus ... ut de vita sanctae dominae Radegundis, quam obtime nostis, aliquid dicere praesumamus" (VR-B 29439–29441; OCR "obtime"), you lay on me the task of saying something of the life of holy Lady Radegund, whom you knew best. Addressed to the abbess "DEDIMIAE ABBATISSAE VEL OMNI CONGREGATIONI" (VR-B 29433–29435, split across lines). After 600 (VR-K 28186). CONTEMP; Documented. A woman of her house wrote her Life a second time.

I6. **A friend and poet who stayed.** Fortunatus: "Martinum cupiens voto Radegundis adhaesi" (VF 16035; OCR "Mixrtiiiuni ... adliaesi"), wanting Martin, by Radegund's wish I stayed close. CONTEMP. Documented, but the OCR is so damaged that it should not be quoted aloud; card only. Dalton: Fortunatus "frequently enjoyed their hospitality" and wrote poems to her and to Agnes (DAL 29601–29607). LATER.

I7. **Peacemaker among the kings.** L18. CONTEMP (Baudonivia only). Documented as her account. Lighter: one witness, no later use recorded in held texts.

## 5. Disputes and unclear points

D1. **Her own words.** The letter (DAL IX.42) is given by Gregory as hers and read out by her abbess (DAL 19821–19824). Treat as OWN. The poems in her voice printed with Fortunatus's works ("De excidio Thoringiae", VF 23128ff., Dalton: "the poem being placed in the mouth of Radegund", DAL 24444–24445) are Fortunatus's work in her name. Do not quote them as her words.

D2. **Did she go to Arles for the Rule?** Gregory: "was forced to go to Arles with the abbess" (DAL 19627–19628). Krusch in 1888 follows him: "cum Agnete abbatissa Arelatensem urbem petivit" (VR-K 28154–28155). Krusch in 1896: "sed eum falli Arnold l. l. p. 418, recte monuisse mihi videtur" (but Arnold rightly warned, I think, that he is wrong), comparing her letter with Caesaria's; Malnory keeps to Gregory (SR3 35706–35708; OCR "Amold" = Arnold). Contested. The story says she gave her house the Rule Caesarius wrote for his sister (her own words, Documented) and does not say she went to Arles.

D3. **"Directa a rege."** VR-F 28797–28798. Whether the king sent her on to Medard, or she simply left him, the Latin does not settle. Baudonivia: "a rege terreno discessit" (she left the earthly king) (VR-B 29677). Inferential-Thin as to the king's part. Story: "she left the court and went to Medard".

D4. **Who said "a nun rather than a queen"?** VR-F 28702: "regi dicebatur" (it was said to the king). Krusch reads it as the king's view (VR-K 28113). Story: "at court it was said that the king had married a nun, not a queen" keeps to Fortunatus's words.

D5. **Her quarrel with Bishop Maroveus.** Documented that he would not receive the relics (DAL 19608–19610) and was absent at her death (GRL 61646–61647). Why: not said. Gregory hints at "some resentment" afterwards (DAL 19642–19643). Baudonivia says the bishop and people wanted to receive the Cross and blames the devil's followers (VR-B 30257–30259); Gregory says the bishop rode off (DAL 19608–19610). Contested as to who refused. Dalton's guess about the burial (DAL 29078–29080) is card only. Story: "the bishop of Poitiers would not receive it" is Gregory's; say "Gregory says" if told.

D6. **Praise from inside.** All three narrative witnesses loved her: a friend (Fortunatus), a nun she raised (Baudonivia), a bishop who was her ally (Gregory). Krusch notes Baudonivia copied passages from the Life of Caesarius, "Praeterea Vitam S. Caesarii Arelatensis c. 8. 9. 19. 20. exscripsit" (VR-K 28222–28223), and built her prologue from Fortunatus's words (VR-K 28221–28222). Scenes found only in Baudonivia (shrine burning, the king's return, peace letters) rest on one partisan witness. Say "her nun Baudonivia tells" when using them.

D7. **"Radegund of Poitiers" or "Radegund, a sinner".** DAL 19831 vs GRL 31490 and apparatus 31527. Dalton followed a manuscript reading "Pictavensis". The Latin edition's text reads "peccatrix". Card only; the story need not give the greeting.

D8. **The year 587.** Editors' reckoning from Gregory's "twelfth year of King Childebert" (GRL 61607; Dalton, "This wasin 587", DAL 29609). Widely Accepted. The birth year and the capture year are not in any held text of her time; "about 531" is Dalton's (DAL 4362).

## 6. What the record does not tell us

- Her birth year, her age at capture, at marriage or at death.
- Her brother's name (Gregory and Fortunatus do not give it in the held lines).
- Whether she and Lothar had children: no held source says they did; do not say either way.
- The year she founded the house, and the year of her letter to the bishops.
- Why Maroveus opposed her.

## 7. Card only

- Dalton's and Krusch's dates and judgements (S2, S5, D2, D5, D8).
- The relic still at Poitiers (DAL 29592–29597).
- The Baptistère Saint-Jean in Poitiers (CEN experienceToday) is not in her record.
- The poems in her name (D1).
- The nuns' revolt of 589–590 in detail (DAL IX.39–X.16), beyond the use of her letter (I1).
