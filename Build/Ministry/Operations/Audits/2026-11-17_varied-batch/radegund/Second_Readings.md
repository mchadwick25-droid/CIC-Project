# Second Readings: radegund

## 1. If you put off consecrating me,
Source: cic/texts/fredegar-liber-historiae-francorum-vitae-mgh-srm-2-lat_krusch1888.txt 28806, 28808
Second rendering: If you delay consecrating me, and fear a man more than God, then, shepherd, may the soul of your sheep be required at your hand.
Ours: If you put off consecrating me, and fear a man more than God, then, shepherd, may the soul of your sheep be demanded from your hand.
Verdict: same
Reason: Same meaning; "demanded from your hand" carries requiratur and keeps the wish form.

## 2. Because I could not come in
Source: cic/texts/gregory-tours_history-of-the-franks-v2_dalton1927.txt 19878-19879; original cic/texts/gregory-tours_opera-mgh-srm-1-lat_arndt-krusch1885.txt 31516-31517
Second rendering: I could not come in person. So, as if lying at your feet, I bow down before you through this letter, which stands in my place.
Ours: Because I could not come in person, I bow down through this letter in my place, as if fallen at your feet.
Verdict: same
Reason: Same meaning: absence, the image of lying at their feet, and the letter standing in for her are all carried.

## 3. the custom of the lady Radegund
Source: cic/texts/gregory-tours_history-of-the-franks-v2_dalton1927.txt 21416-21418
Second rendering: She had brought in no new practice. She had kept up the custom of the lady Radegund.
Ours: the custom of the lady Radegund
Verdict: same
Reason: Verbatim fragment of Dalton's printed English, which is plain modern English.

## 4. King Lothar carried her off as his captive and made her his wife (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/gregory-tours_history-of-the-franks-v2_dalton1927.txt 4379-4381; original cic/texts/gregory-tours_opera-mgh-srm-1-lat_arndt-krusch1885.txt 9201-9202
Second rendering: Returning home, Lothar took Radegund, daughter of King Berthar, away with him as a captive and joined her to himself in marriage.
Ours: King Lothar carried her off as his captive and made her his wife.
Verdict: same
Reason: "secum captivam abduxit sibique eam in matrimonio sociavit": led her away captive, joined her to himself in marriage. "Carried her off" carries "abduxit" and "made her his wife" carries "sibi ... sociavit", with the king as the one acting, as in the Latin. Nothing softened.

## 5. Her own letter says King Lothar founded and enriched her monastery (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/gregory-tours_history-of-the-franks-v2_dalton1927.txt 19846-19850; original cic/texts/gregory-tours_opera-mgh-srm-1-lat_arndt-krusch1885.txt 31497-31500
Second rendering: With the most excellent lord King Lothar founding and endowing it, I set up a monastery for girls in the city of Poitiers. Once it was founded, I gave it all that the king's generosity had granted me.
Ours: Her own letter says King Lothar founded and enriched her monastery for women at Poitiers. She gave it all the property the king had given her.
Verdict: minor
Reason: The king's part ("instituente atque remunerante") and her gift of all the royal property are both carried. But her own verb, "constitui" (I set up), drops out, so the king becomes the only founder, where the letter makes it a joint act with her as its subject. A listener could take the house as Lothar's gift to her rather than her own foundation with his backing. See Recheck_R50.md, M1.

## 6. led by Clotild, who called herself a king's daughter and said they were insulted and humiliated there (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/gregory-tours_history-of-the-franks-v2_dalton1927.txt 19406-19416, 19427-19428; original cic/texts/gregory-tours_opera-mgh-srm-1-lat_arndt-krusch1885.txt 30864-30873
Second rendering: Clotild, who claimed to be the daughter of the late King Charibert, left with forty or more nuns, saying: "I am going to my royal kin, to tell them of the insult done to us, because here we are humbled not as kings' daughters but as if we were born of low maidservants."
Ours: led by Clotild, who called herself a king's daughter and said they were insulted and humiliated there.
Verdict: minor
Reason: "Called herself" fairly carries "se ... filiam adserebat" (Dalton's "boasted herself" is sharper than the Latin). "Insulted and humiliated there" carries "contumeliam nostram" and "in hoc loco humiliamur". What is thinned is the shape of the grievance: they were treated not as kings' daughters but as servants' children. "Called herself a king's daughter" keeps a trace of it. The rebels' side is heard, and Gregory's hostile frame ("The Devil seduced the heart of Clotild") is rightly left out of the narrator's voice. No change needed.
