# Review: Radegund brief

Reviewer's report on `radegund.brief.txt`, 2026-11-17. Judged against `kit/Standard.md` and `kit/Shapes.md`, traced to `Outline.md`, `Outline_Check.md` and `Source_Sheet.md`. Quotes re-checked against the files in `cic/texts/`.

## 1. Listener read (one pass, no going back)

**Where I got lost or drifted.**
- The first sentence, "Her friend the poet Fortunatus tells the scene", names a scene before there is one. For a moment I did not know whose friend, or which scene.
- "walked out to face him": on one hearing "him" could be the bishop or the king, since both were just named. I settled on the bishop only when paragraph 3 came back to the altar.
- The Noyon scene is told twice, in two pieces. The opening gives the nobles and the dress. Paragraph 3 gives the request and the charge, but not the nobles. I had to join the two halves myself.
- "Its written Rule ... kept each nun inside its walls for life, and she sent to the emperor in the East for wood of the Cross." Two unrelated acts joined by "and". I drifted here, and did not hear why the Cross mattered until the last paragraph.
- Gregory arrives late with no word on who he is. I did not know why his copying of the letter, or his lamp, carried weight.

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down.** Nowhere seriously. There is no run of short one-act sentences, and no definition tacked on after a comma. The closest is paragraph 4, which reads as a list of what she did (built, gave, Rule, Cross) with little sense of what each meant or risked.

## 2. Scores

- **Voice: 4.** Adult register throughout. Ideas are mostly linked by cause and time ("When Lothar had her brother killed", "though he hesitated because", "To guard the house", "so the abbess read"). Paragraph 4 slips into listing acts.
- **Craft: 4.** A strong hook, and the setting comes in the second paragraph. The stakes at the altar are clear. The tradition ("the church of Frankish Gaul") is never said. The Cross gets no stake when it first appears. The ending is in the record's own words, as the outline suggests. The material after her death, with the letter written for that time, is about a third of the brief.
- **Rigor: 3.** All three quoted passages are exact (see the trace below). But one sentence states an outcome the outline does not hold, and one number says more than the source does.
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 8.2, reading ease 76.9 (names out), 20.3 words a sentence, longest 32 words: pass. Followable on one hearing apart from the opening "him" and the split scene.

## 4. Verdict: REVISE

## 5. Findings

1. **BLOCKING. "more than forty of them" overstates the source.** Gregory says "forty or more nuns" (DAL 19412–19413), and the outline follows him. "More than forty" rules out forty. Change to "forty or more of them".

2. **BLOCKING. "The Rule, the abbess and the property all held" is not in the outline.** The outline's "DEFENDED: the Rule, the abbess, the property" lists what her letter defended, not how the revolt ended. The source does show the bishops found "no cause shown for the degradation of the abbess" (DAL 21462–21463). But the outline does not carry the outcome, and nothing at all is held on the property. Either cut the sentence, or tell only what the outline holds, for example: "At the bishops' hearing, the abbess defended the house's ways as 'the custom of the lady Radegund'." If the outcome is wanted, it goes back to the outline first, with its citation.

3. **MINOR. The opening sentence and "him".** "Tells the scene" points at nothing yet, and "to face him" can be heard as the king. Name the bishop in the opening ("walked out to face the bishop"), and attach Fortunatus to the scene once it is told rather than before it.

4. **MINOR. The tradition is never said.** The standard asks for century, place and tradition early. Work "the church of Frankish Gaul" into paragraph 2, in the outline's words.

5. **MINOR. The lamp wonder is half in the narrator's voice.** "Gregory saw a lamp overflow with oil" states it as fact and gives the witness only in the next sentence. Keep it in his voice: "Gregory wrote that, before the Cross she had brought, he saw a lamp overflow with oil."

6. **MINOR. Gregory is never introduced.** He carries the letter and the closing wonder, and he is a link on the tree. Say once, plainly, that he was the bishop who knew her and buried her (beat 7; "Who tells it").

7. **MINOR. Paragraph 4 lists acts, and the Cross has no stake.** The Rule and the Cross are joined by a bare "and". Split them. Give the Cross one clause of meaning from the outline, for example that the bishop of Poitiers would not receive it, said as "Gregory says" (D5), or that she was compared to Helena.

8. **MINOR. Longest sentence 32 words.** This is the Q1 sentence, so the quote carries most of it. It is within the score's pass, but slightly over the standard's "about 30". Putting "At the altar she warned him" into the sentence before would bring it under.
