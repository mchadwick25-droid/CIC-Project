# Fixes, release 50: Radegund

Ruling: Mark, 2026-11-17, "yes no softening, we are a witness not a judge … for this round 1 but also the should fixes too". Released text kept as `radegund.r50.brief.txt`.

## Piece items

1. **MUST: the king's backing of her house restored.** Old: "With her royal wealth she built a monastery for women at Poitiers and gave it everything she owned." New: "Her own letter says King Lothar founded and enriched her monastery for women at Poitiers. She gave it all the property the king had given her." No longer says she built it with her own wealth alone. Sources: Source_Sheet L11; her letter, DAL 19846–19850 ("a monastery for nuns, founded and enriched by the most excellent lord King Lothar; this monastery after its foundation I myself endowed by the gift of all the property which the royal munificence had bestowed upon me"); Latin GRL 31496–31500. Lines opened and confirmed (DAL).
2. **The captive marriage made plain.** Old: "Radegund was a Thuringian princess, taken as spoil when the Franks destroyed her people's kingdom, and King Lothar married her." New: "Radegund was a Thuringian princess, taken as spoil when the Franks destroyed her people's kingdom. King Lothar carried her off as his captive and made her his wife." Sources: S2, DAL 4379–4381 ("took with him as his captive Radegund … her he wedded"); L1 (spoil, VR-F 28562–28564).
3. **The rebel nuns' side, one clause.** Old: "Soon afterwards forty or more of her nuns walked out in revolt, so the abbess read Radegund's letter aloud…" New: "Soon afterwards forty or more of her nuns walked out in revolt, led by Clotild, who called herself a king's daughter and said they were insulted and humiliated there." Sources: I1 (DAL 19411–19413, "forty or more nuns" led by Clotild); DAL 19409–19416 and 19423–19424, opened and confirmed: Gregory says Clotild "boasted herself the daughter of King Charibert" and left "saying: 'I am going to my royal kinsfolk, to make known to them the insults put upon us, how we are humiliated…'". Note for the second reading: the complaint itself sits two lines past the range the source sheet cites (19411–19413); it is in the same passage. "Called herself a king's daughter" keeps Gregory's doubt ("boasted herself") without the narrator taking his hostile framing ("The Devil seduced the heart of Clotild"). The old "so" (abbess read the letter because of the revolt) was dropped with the split.
4. **Setting.** Old: "This was the sixth century, in the kingdom of the Franks, in what is now France, and the church of Frankish Gaul." New: "This was the sixth-century kingdom of the Franks, in what is now France." Cuts "and the church of Frankish Gaul" and the stacked "in X, in Y, in what is now Z". Sources: outline setting; S1, S6.
5. **Not ending on the lamp.** The lamp sentence moved up into the Cross paragraph, where it belongs in time, and is split for one hearing: "Gregory of Tours, a bishop who knew her, wrote that he saw a lamp before her Cross overflow with oil. From then on, he says, he preached the power of the holy Cross." (Gregory is now introduced here, so the death paragraph says only "and Gregory buried her".) The last paragraph now ends on what passed from her, in the record's words: "At the bishops' hearing the abbess defended the house's ways as "the custom of the lady Radegund"." The Gregory-copied-the-letter clause moved before it. Sources: I3 (GRL 38121–38135); I1 (DAL 19821–19824; 21416–21418).
