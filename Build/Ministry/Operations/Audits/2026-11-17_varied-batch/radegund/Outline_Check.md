Checker's report on `Outline.md`, 2026-11-17. I checked every cited line against the held files in `cic/texts/`: DAL (Dalton), GRL (Arndt and Krusch), VR (Krusch 1888) and VF (Leo).

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. Beat 6 calls the bishop a tyrant, which her letter does not do.** The outline says: "protect us from any tyrant, even the city's own bishop". The letter has two separate requests. First, protect us "from any tyrant" (DAL 19882). Second, stop anyone after my death, "whether the bishop of the city, or a royal officer, or any other person", who tries to trouble the sisters, break the Rule or appoint another abbess (DAL 19883–19888). She names the bishop as a possible threat. She does not call him a tyrant. A writer working from the outline alone could tell it as her calling her bishop a tyrant.
   Correction: "She wrote to the bishops: protect us from any tyrant. And after my death, if anyone, even the city's own bishop or a royal officer, tries to trouble the sisters, break the Rule or replace the abbess, stop him (Q2)."

2. **MINOR. Beat 4: "With land and money the king gave" adds detail.** Her letter says the house was "founded and enriched by the most excellent lord King Lothar", and that she endowed it with "all the property which the royal munificence had bestowed upon me" (DAL 19846–19850; GRL 31498–31500). Land and money are not named.
   Correction: "Backed by the king and with the wealth he had given her, she built a monastery for women at Poitiers."

3. **MINOR. Beat 6: "the same letters to rival kings" is ambiguous.** Coming straight after the bishops' letter, it can be heard as the same letter she sent the bishops. Baudonivia's "quales litteras uni, tales alteri dirigebat" (VR-B 29990) means she sent the same kind of letter to each king.
   Correction: "Her nun Baudonivia adds that when the kings turned on each other, she wrote to each of them alike, begging them to make peace, not war."

4. **MINOR. Beat 2: one confidence label covers two kinds of claim.** "Widely Accepted" fits the serving-the-poor pattern, where Fortunatus and Baudonivia agree (VR-F 28643–28647; VR-B 29660–29661). Only Fortunatus gives the night prayer and the "nun rather than a queen" saying (VR-F 28698–28704).
   Correction: split the label. Care for the poor: Widely Accepted. Night prayer and the saying: Documented as Fortunatus's account.

5. **MINOR. Links: "Fortunatus ... stayed at Poitiers for her" says more than the held line.** The line is "Martinum cupiens voto Radegundis adhaesi" (VF 16035, badly damaged OCR): wanting Martin, by Radegund's wish I stayed close. It does not name Poitiers. Dalton says he "frequently enjoyed their hospitality" (DAL 29601, LATER).
   Correction: "her friend; by her wish he stayed near her (his own line, damaged in the held text), and was often her guest (Dalton)".

6. **MINOR. Who tells it: "wrote her Life soon after her death" adds "soon".** Krusch says only that Fortunatus wrote after her death in 587 ("postquam a. 587. mortua est", VR-K 28175–28176).
   Correction: "wrote her Life after her death".

7. **MINOR. Q1 rendering: "a sheep's soul".** My blind rendering: "If you put off consecrating me and fear a man more than God, shepherd, the soul of your sheep will be required from your hand." Latin has no article. Still, in English "a sheep's soul" sounds like any sheep, when she means herself, the sheep in his care. Nothing is added or dropped.
   Correction: "then, shepherd, may the soul of your sheep be demanded from your hand."
