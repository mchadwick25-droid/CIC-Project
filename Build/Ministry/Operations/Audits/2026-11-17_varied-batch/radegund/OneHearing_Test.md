## Step 1. Three lines from one hearing

- **Who was this?** Radegund, a Thuringian princess taken captive when the Franks destroyed her people's kingdom in the sixth century. She was married to King Lothar, then left him and became a deaconess.
- **What happened?** After Lothar had her brother killed, she left court and made the bishop of Noyon consecrate her, though his nobles tried to stop him. She built a monastery for women at Poitiers, gave it a strict Rule from Caesarius of Arles, and got wood of the Cross from the emperor in the East. She wrote to the bishops asking them to protect the house after her death.
- **Why does the connection matter?** Her letter outlived her. When nuns revolted after her death, the abbess used it to defend the house's ways as "the custom of the lady Radegund". Gregory of Tours copied it into his History and preached the power of the Cross. What I could not hear was who carried her house on: the brief gives forty rebels but never says how many women followed her.
