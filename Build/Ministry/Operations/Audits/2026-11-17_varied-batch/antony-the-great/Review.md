# Review: Antony the Great

Reviewer of `antony-the-great.brief.txt`. The earlier brief and its review were not read.

## Listener read

I read it once, straight through, without going back.

**Where I got lost or drifted.**

- Paragraph 3 opens with "Most of what we know comes from one book…". It breaks into the life just after he gives everything away. Then it goes straight back to the hermits. For a moment I lost the thread of the story.
- Paragraph 4: "When people who knew him forced the door…" I did not know why they forced it. "So many followed him" then comes from nowhere. The brief never says that many now wanted to live as he did.
- Paragraph 5 jumps from the mountain to "Near a hundred and five" in one step. The decades between them go by without a word. Nothing is said about his stand in Alexandria, and the brief never says what he stood for.
- Paragraph 7: "Trier" is not placed. A listener anywhere does not hear that the book had reached the far west of the empire, so "how far it reached" is left for the listener to work out. The long opening sentence also stacks Milan, Pontitianus, Trier, "soon after" and the garden. That is a heavy load to take in on one hearing.

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down.**

- No childish wording. The sentences are adult in length (20.6 words on average, 5% short).
- Paragraphs 2 and 4–5 chain acts with "and" and "then": he gave, sold, left; he went, walled up, came out; crowds drove him; he asked to be buried. Each act is clear, but little is said about what it meant or risked. The joining-word count (1.1 per 100) matches what I heard.
- Two definitions are tacked on after a comma: "He became one of the desert monks of Egypt, later called the Desert Fathers and Mothers" (a label, not an event) and "In his Confessions, a book he addressed to God, he wrote". The outline asks for the second one to be made clear, but it should be worked into the sentence, not set off by commas.

## Scores

Profile: 453 words, grade 8.1 (7.3 with names out), reading ease 79.3, longest sentence 30 words: pass.

- **voice: 3.** It is a serious, adult narrator who never condescends. But the middle runs as a chain of acts joined by "and", with little cause or contrast. Two labels are tacked on after commas.
- **craft: 3.** The hook is strong and comes from opening 1. Place, rulers and tradition arrive in the first paragraph. The impact is about a third of the story (paragraphs 6–8) and ends on an event. Against that, what was at stake is thin in the middle. The source line interrupts the story. The outline's marked DEFENDED item is missing (finding 1).
- **rigor: 4.** Every sentence traces to the outline, and the quote matches the outline's rendering word for word. I checked the Latin at AUGL 13470–13476 and 13501 and the English at AUG 13117–13134 myself. Athanasius is named once, the right way. One "so that" claims a purpose the record does not give (finding 3).
- **accessibility: 4.** Grade 8.1 and reading ease 79 are inside the limits, and the brief is easy to follow for most of its length. The listener loses the thread at paragraph 3 and at the jump in paragraph 5. Trier is not placed.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: what he defended is missing.** The outline marks his stand against the Arians as DEFENDED. In beat 7 he came down to Alexandria and taught that the Son of God was not created (E14). Impact item 4 says it is "already told in beat 7; one clause at most here", so the outline expects the story to carry it. The brief carries none of it. A listener does not learn that he stood for the church's faith in the Arian quarrel. That is one of the reasons the outline gives for why he mattered to the church (Standard, "Before it is done": "why this person mattered to the church").
   - A note on the basis for this finding: the requirement comes from the outline and from `kit/Outline_Template.md` ("What they defended is marked"). `Standard.md` does not itself say that the impact must tell what they defended.
   - **Change:** add one or two sentences to paragraph 5, before the burial. When Arians claimed he agreed with them, he came down from the mountain to Alexandria and taught that the Son of God was not created. Use E14's wording and no more. Keep the brief near its length by tightening elsewhere, for example by dropping the Pontitianus name (finding 5).

2. **MINOR: the door is forced with no reason given.** The outline (beat 4, E8) gives the reason: many now wanted to live as he did. Without it, "forced the door" has no cause, and "So many followed him" comes from nowhere.
   - **Change:** use the outline's order. Many wanted to live as he did, so people who knew him came and forced the door.

3. **MINOR: "walled up the door so that bread had to be let down to him" makes the bread the purpose of the wall.** The record says only that he walled up the entrance and that bread was let down twice a year (E7, NP4 31565).
   - **Change:** "walled up the door, and bread was let down to him twice a year."

4. **MINOR: the source line breaks into the life.** "Most of what we know comes from one book…" sits between the giving away and the hermits.
   - **Change:** move it to the point where the *Life* first speaks as a witness, for example just before "he came out, the Life says". Or move it to the start of the life story, so it does not cut across the giving away and the learning.

5. **MINOR: two labels are tacked on after commas, and one name is not needed.** The labels are "…desert monks of Egypt, later called the Desert Fathers and Mothers" and "In his Confessions, a book he addressed to God, he wrote". Pontitianus is not a link on the tree and is not a witness the listener needs.
   - **Change:** work the tradition into what happens, for example "the life of the desert monks of Egypt". Introduce the quote as Augustine writing to God, for example "Writing to God in his Confessions, he remembered:". Say "a fellow official who had been at Trier" in place of the name, and split the Milan sentence in two.

6. **MINOR: Trier is not placed, so the reach goes unsaid.** The outline offers the *Life*'s own reach, "Spain, Gaul, Rome and Africa" (I1b), and the book "soon read in Latin".
   - **Change:** add the *Life*'s claim of its reach in its own voice: how a man hidden on a mountain came to be heard of in Spain, Gaul, Rome and Africa (outline impact item 1). That lets the listener hear how far the book had travelled. Do not place Trier yourself (for example "in Gaul"), because the outline does not give its location.
