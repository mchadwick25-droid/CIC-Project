## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: "Who tells it", Augustine's line to say aloud, misleads about the time gap.** "Not long after Antony's death, in Milan, Augustine heard his story" hides a gap of about thirty years. The sheet itself marks Augustine LATER: the Milan events about 30 years after Antony's death, and the writing about 40. Augustine's "so recent, and almost in our own" times (AUG 12565–12566) is his own feeling, not the narrator's dating. Correction: "A generation after Antony died, in Milan, Augustine heard his story." If the writer wants Augustine's own sense of nearness, quote it as his: "deeds, he wrote, 'so recent, and almost in our own' times."

2. **MINOR: in beat 4, "Friends passed him bread twice a year" adds who brought the bread.** The text has "received loaves, let down from above, twice in the year" (NP4 31565) and names no one. Correction: "Bread was let down to him twice a year."

3. **MINOR: in beat 4, who broke the door is merged with who wanted to copy him.** The text has "when many were eager and wishful to imitate his discipline, and his acquaintances came and began to cast down and wrench off the door by force" (NP4 31602–31604). Correction: "Many now wanted to live as he did. People who knew him came and forced the door."

4. **MINOR: in beat 4, the quoted "the desert was colonised by monks" carries a misleading modern sense if said aloud.** To a modern listener "colonised" means conquest and empire. The Greek is "ἡ ἔρημος ἐπολίσθη ὑπὸ μοναχῶν" (VAG 70548–70549), "the desert was made a city by monks". Correction: render it as "the desert became a city of monks", marked as from the Greek, in place of the 1892 English.

5. **MINOR: in beat 6, the baskets lose their exchange.** The text says "he used to weave baskets, which he gave to those who came in return for what they brought him" (NP4 32532–32533). "Wove baskets to give visitors" makes them free gifts. Correction: "wove baskets to give visitors in return for what they brought him."

6. **MINOR: the Q3 rendering has one sentence past about 25 words.** "It was as though the words read were spoken to him: 'Go, sell all that you have, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me.'" runs to about 32 words. Correction: split it. "It was as though the words read were spoken to him. 'Go, sell all that you have, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. And come, follow me.'" My own rendering from the Latin agrees with theirs otherwise. Nothing is added or dropped, and "divine word" for *oraculum* is right.

7. **MINOR: "everywhere" in the Thread goes beyond the record.** "The pattern that monks everywhere copied" says more than the record does. The held sources give the *Life*'s claim of fame in Spain, Gaul, Rome and Africa (NP4 33571–33575), plus Robertson's card-only judgment about the West. Correction: "the pattern that monks far beyond Egypt copied."

8. **MINOR: the Hilarion link does not name its witness.** "Met him; learned from him; kept his feast at his mountain" rests only on Jerome's *Life of Hilarion*, written about 35 years later, which Robertson calls "highly idealised" (NP4 30942–30943). Correction: add "(Jerome's account, written later)" to the link.

9. **MINOR: the Q1 Greek line reference starts one line late.** "Μὴ" stands at VAG 77107, and the word "θαυμάζετε" (OCR "θχυμάξετε") follows on 77108. Correction: cite VAG 77107–77112.

## Chronology check

This checks the brief's opening after the fix. It now reads "In Egypt, a young man walked to church…" and ends "…and he lived on into the time of the emperor Constantine and his sons."

**Verdict: PASS**

- **The new placement is supported.** In VA §81 (NP4 33187–33189), "Constantine Augustus, and his sons Constantius and Constans the Augusti" write to Antony long after his withdrawal into the desert. Jerome's "He flourished during the reign of Constantinus and his sons" (NP3 41181–41182) agrees.
- **The order matches the *Life*.** "Lived on into" puts those reigns after his youth. That fits the *Life*'s own order: the Gospel reading (§2), the tombs at "about thirty-five" (§10), nearly twenty years in the fort (§14), the Maximinus persecution (§46), and then the emperors' letters (§81).
- **Nothing false is implied.** No year is given, and the opening no longer dates the youth scene to those emperors. "Led him into the life of Egypt's desert monks" does not claim that he was the first monk (see D3).
