# Second Readings: antony-the-great

## 1. the desert became a city of
Source: cic/texts/athanasius_opera-omnia-pg26-grc-lat_migne1857.txt 70548-70549
Second rendering: And the desert was made a city by monks.
Ours: the desert became a city of monks.
Verdict: same
Reason: Same meaning: the desert turned into a city peopled by monks; ours avoids the misleading "colonised".

## 2. For I had heard this of
Source: cic/texts/augustine_confessiones-lat_knoll-csel33.txt 13470-13473, 13501
Second rendering: For I had heard this about Antony. He came in by chance while the Gospel was being read. He took the reading as a warning meant for him, as if the words were spoken to him: "Go, sell everything you have, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me." By that word from God he turned to you at once.
Ours: For I had heard this of Antony. He had come in by chance during the Gospel reading, and was urged on by it. It was as though the words read were spoken to him. 'Go, sell all that you have, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. And come, follow me.' By such a divine word he was at once turned to you.
Verdict: minor
Reason: Ours renders admonitus as "urged on", which suggests being spurred forward; the Latin means warned or prompted. A listener still takes away that the reading moved him at once to turn to God.

## 3. For I had heard this of (trimmed)
Source: cic/texts/augustine_confessiones-lat_knoll-csel33.txt 13470-13472
Second rendering: For I had heard about Antony that he came in by chance on the Gospel reading and took it as a warning, as if what was being read was said to him.
Ours: For I had heard this of Antony. He had come in by chance during the Gospel reading, and was urged on by it. It was as though the words read were spoken to him.
Verdict: minor
Reason: The trim stops at "tamquam sibi diceretur quod legebatur" and keeps the earlier rendering unchanged, so the earlier finding stands: "urged on" is a shade stronger than admonitus (warned, prompted). Dropping the Gospel words and "tali oraculo confestim ad te esse conuersum" loses nothing the brief does not already say: the opening gives the Gospel call, and "taken as spoken to him" is in the kept words.

## 4. The Life says the Arians ... lied that he agreed with them (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf204_athanasius-select-works-letters.xml 32924-32932
Second rendering: The Arians falsely claimed that Antony thought as they did. Called down by the bishops and all the brothers, he came from the mountain into Alexandria and taught the people that the Son of God was not a created being.
Ours: The Life says the Arians, who taught that the Son of God was created, lied that he agreed with them. Then, it says, the bishops summoned him, and he came down to Alexandria to teach that the Son was not created.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Lyingly asserted" is carried as "lied", and kept in the Life's voice. "And all the brethren" is dropped, which changes nothing. The gloss on the Arians rests on the same section ("there was a time when He was not", 32935).

## 5. In a tomb outside the village ... they beat him (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf204_athanasius-select-works-letters.xml 31454-31469
Second rendering: One night the enemy came with a crowd of demons and beat him so badly that he lay on the ground unable to speak. The next day the friend who brought his bread opened the door and found him lying as though dead.
Ours: In a tomb outside the village, it says, they beat him until a friend found him lying as though dead.
Verdict: minor
Reason: "Until" suggests the beating went on up to the friend's arrival. In the Life the beating is one night, and the friend comes the next day. The scene and its force are kept, and it is in the Life's voice. See Recheck_R50.md.
