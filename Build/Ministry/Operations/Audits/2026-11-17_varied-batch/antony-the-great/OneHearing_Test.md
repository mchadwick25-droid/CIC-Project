# One-hearing test: Antony the Great

Brief tested: `antony-the-great.brief.txt` (455 words). Test: "An interested non-specialist can understand who this was, what happened, and why the connection matters after one hearing." Not simpler.

## Step 1: three lines from one hearing

- **Who was this?** Antony, a young Christian in Egypt in Constantine's time, left his family farm to become one of Egypt's desert monks.
- **What happened?** He gave everything away after a Gospel reading, learned from local hermits, walled himself in a desert fort for about twenty years, and came out unchanged. So many followed him that the desert became "a city of monks." Crowds then drove him to a mountain further out. He came down once to Alexandria to teach against the Arians, and he died near 105, buried in secret.
- **Why does the connection matter?** A Life of him, credited to Athanasius, was written as a pattern for monks and spread west in Latin. At Trier it made two court officials leave the court. That story reached Augustine in Milan, who remembered Antony's Gospel reading and was turned to God.

### Where the connection line is lost

1. Paragraph 6, sentence 1: "Augustine heard **that story** from an official who had been at Trier." After the Trier sentence, "that story" can be heard as either Antony's story or the Trier story. A listener has to work out which one, and the brief moves on before they can.
2. Last line: "Augustine opened Paul's letters and read, and his doubt was gone." This is the payoff, but it does not tie Augustine's turn back to Antony's. The listener hears that a book was opened, not that the same thing happened again: one reading, taken as spoken to him, turned him. The outline's thread (E2, I1) and I2 are about exactly this mirror. As it stands, "his doubt" floats free.

## Verdict

**FAIL** (Connection is WEAK; Who and What happened are CLEAR).

## Smallest fix (from the outline only)

- Paragraph 6: "Augustine heard that story from an official who had been at Trier" becomes "Augustine heard the Trier story from Pontitianus, an official who had been there" (I2 names him). Word count stays the same.
- Last line: "Augustine opened Paul's letters and read, and his doubt was gone." becomes "Like Antony, he let one reading turn him. He opened Paul's letters, read, and his doubt was gone." (I2, Q3: Augustine remembers Antony's reading at the moment he opens the book.)
- To pay for it, drop "Soon after," from "Soon after, while he wept in a garden".

Net change: about +4 words (455 to about 459), still inside 3 minutes. Nothing is simplified: the fix makes the brief's own mirror, between the opening and the ending, audible.
