# How a brief is made

1. **Research.** A researcher builds the source sheet from held texts, then the one-page outline to `Outline_Template.md`, including the connection to carry into the story and the Do not say list.
2. **Outline check.** An independent checker tests the outline against the source texts. Blocking faults are fixed and independently rechecked.
3. **Write.** A fresh writer works only from the outline, `Shapes.md` and `Standard.md`.
4. **Review.** A fresh reviewer first answers the acceptance test from one hearing, then runs the separate source check: every sentence traced to the outline, every quote to its source. A clear story can still hold a factual error.
5. **One revision, one recheck.** A brief that still fails comes to Mark with the reason.

## Acceptance test

After one hearing, an interested non-specialist can explain who or what this was, what happened, and why the connection matters.

## Revision discipline

- Fix problems upstream in the outline, not by wholesale rewriting afterwards. When a review finds a writer's error the outline left room for, add it to the outline's Do not say list.
- Make targeted corrections and preserve approved text.
- Reading scores, quotation shares and attribution counts are warnings, never quotas.
- Block publication only for unsupported claims, misleading implications, missing essential material, or a genuine failure to understand on one hearing.
- Do not reopen finished briefs for optional polishing.

The goal is a repeatable process that produces faithful, understandable, worthwhile stories, not unattainable perfection.
