# The one-page standard

A story of about two minutes that lets anyone, anywhere, meet one person from the church's history. It is heard once, by adults, many of them listening in a second language. Clear is not the same as simple.

## Voice

- The narrator is an adult documentary narrator, in the register of a BBC or National Geographic documentary: serious, vivid, and speaking to a grown listener as an equal. No listener should feel talked down to.
- Tell it as a story by someone who knows the record well, not a report about the record. Each sentence leads to the next.

## Craft

- Open on something that draws a listener in, from the outline's openings. Within the first few sentences, give the century or year, the place and the tradition, in the outline's spoken words.
- Tell what was at stake, not only what happened: what an act meant in its own time, what it risked and what it changed. The outline gives the reasons; use them to show the meaning.
- Link ideas the way an adult narrator does, with cause and result, contrast and time in the same sentence (because, although, while, until, so that, which). Let a longer sentence carry a chain of events, and keep a short one for the moment that needs it. No sentence past about 30 words. Never a run of short sentences that each tell one act.
- Tell it in your own words. Use only the outline's quotes, and only where a voice does what your words cannot.
- About a third of the story is what came of them. End there, as an event or in the record's own words: who carried their witness on, or how far it reached. No moral, no summing up.

## Rigor

- Tell only what the outline holds. Add nothing for effect: no feelings, weather or thoughts the record does not give.
- Name a writer or witness only when the listener needs to know who is speaking: praise or blame, a wonder, a source written much later, or a link on the tree. Say it once, plainly, in the story's own voice.
- A wonder stays in its witnesses' voice, with no sneer and no endorsement.
- Where the outline marks something uncertain, say so plainly. Do not present it as settled, and do not bury it in hedging.

## Accessibility

- Plain modern words. Simplify the language, never the thought. Use the precise word a grown listener knows (persecution, council, monastery, heresy, bishop) without stopping to define it.
- Work the meaning of a puzzling word or custom into the sentence that uses it, as part of what happens. Never tack a definition on after a comma.
- One main idea per sentence, with each subject close to its verb; the clauses around it carry its reasons and results.
- Aim for reading grade 8 to 9. Grade 10 and reading ease 60 are the limits. A grade under 8 is a warning: before accepting it, check that the voice has not become a list of short acts.
- Scholars, editions and debates go on the card, not in the story.

## Before it is done

- Heard once, could an adult anywhere follow it?
- Would an educated adult feel talked down to anywhere in it?
- Do they come away knowing why this person mattered to the church?
- Can every sentence be traced to the outline?
