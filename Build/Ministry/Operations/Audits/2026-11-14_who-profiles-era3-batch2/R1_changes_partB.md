# R1 changes, part B: era 3 batch 2

Each part B finding, what was done, and where. Every cited line was opened in its vendored file before the change was made. The three audio scripts were changed with their briefs and still match them once the two tags are removed; the tags are where they were.

| Finding | Done |
|---|---|
| Brigid B1 (the Life not said plainly to be written long after her; no confidence for it) | Taken as worded. Brief l.3: "He wrote her Life in Latin long after her death, at the brothers' request, from what he says elders had handed down." Brief l.9 now opens "How long after her Cogitosus wrote is disputed." Card `uncertain` 1 now says "written long after her death". Records: a new claim, Widely Accepted, that Cogitosus wrote long after her (Cogitosus l.58492–58500, 59384–59403, 59484–59493; Basnage "non coaevum, sed recentiorem… a majoribus tantum", l.58466–58472; White l.3741–3750, 3768–3770; Stokes's index "A.D. 800-835", l.21226), and a Documented claim for Basnage's "tota miraculis scatet" (l.58446, OCR "descripsittotamiraculis scatet"). The contested claim is renamed "How long after her Cogitosus wrote" and its sides end "All put the Life well after her death." To hold the length, the Lismore Life's Bishop Mél sentence left the brief; card `remembered` 2 and the records keep it. For the README: its sentence that the brief marks Cogitosus's Life as written long after her is now true; no change is needed. |
| Caesaria B2 (false absence: "All that is known of her comes from her brother's Life") | Taken as worded in brief l.3 ("Her story comes almost wholly from her brother's Life.") and card `uncertain` 1, which adds "Bruno Krusch adds that Venantius Fortunatus held her up as an example." Krusch l.35667–35668 ("neque vero Casaria ipsius artem effugit pia soror, quam non semel proposuit exemplum imitandum") and note 8, l.34757–34758, confirmed. Records: a new claim for the Fortunatus report, tagged Dominant Modern Reconstruction as Krusch's, with "Fortunatus not held". |
| Brigid M1 (Kildare given to Cogitosus) | Taken as worded in brief l.1 ("In her great church on the plains of the Liffey, Cogitosus writes,") and l.13 ("crowds came to her church"), and in card `traces` 1 and 2. Cogitosus "in compestribus campi Liffeim" (l.58512–58513) confirmed; no "Kildare" in the Life. The narrator's "Brigid founded the monastery of Kildare in Ireland" stays. A records claim now names where "Kildare" comes from: White's "a Life of St. Brigid of Kildare" (l.3748–3749) and the Lismore Life (l.18604), both confirmed. The first records claim no longer says "In the church at Kildare". |
| Brigid M2 (miracles "at her tomb") | Taken: "in her church" in brief l.9, card `uncertain` 2 and the Basnage records claim. Basnage "in ejus templo operari solita" (l.58466) confirmed. |
| Brigid M3 (bare "Columba") | Taken: brief l.7 "the year Saint Columcille was born", card `dates` and `uncertain` 4 "Columcille", and the records' death-year sides. Annales Cambriae "Sanctus Columcille nascitur" (l.2399) confirmed. The Nennius extracts say "Columbae" (Stokes l.13494), so the brief's next sentence now reads "put four years between his birth and her death", which keeps them one man and needs no second name. |
| Brigid M4 (Stokes's 800–835 missing) | Taken as worded in card `uncertain` 2 and the records' contested claim (Stokes, Tripartite Life v2, index l.21226, "Cogitosus (A.I). 800-835)", confirmed). |
| Brigid M5 ("one mind") | Taken: "one heart and one counsel" (Stokes l.5921–5923, "unum cor consiliumque haberent unum", confirmed). |
| Brigid M6 (439 told as a date) | Taken as worded in card `dates`: "reports her birth as 439 and gives her death as 518" (Basnage "Nata dicitur Brigida anno 439", l.58468, confirmed). |
| Caesaria M7 ("until the day she died") | Taken as worded: "They were enclosed so that none might leave by the convent door until they died." ("usque diem transitus earum nulli liceat… lanuam egredi", l.37216–37217, confirmed.) |
| Caesaria M8 (length) | Taken as worded: "Caesarius wrote a Rule for them." Card `traces` 2 keeps the address. |

| Caesarius M10 ("He signed first" heard as Boniface) | Taken as worded. |
