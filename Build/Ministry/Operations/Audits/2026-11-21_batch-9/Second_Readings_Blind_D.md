# Second Readings, Blind D

## B9-30

**Source:** `cic/texts/eunomius_first-apology_whiston1711.txt`, line 38 (section VII of Whiston's 1711 English).

**Passage as read (42 words):**

> if it be demonstrated that neither could God be before himself, nor could any thing else be before him, but that he is before all things, it thence follows that he is the Unbegotten, or rather, he is himself the Unbegotten Substance.

**Plain modern English:**

Suppose it has been shown that God could not exist before himself. Suppose it has also been shown that nothing else could exist before him, and that he exists before everything. Then it follows that he is the Unbegotten. Or, more exactly, he himself is the Unbegotten Substance.

**Clause check:**

- "if it be demonstrated that" → "Suppose it has been shown that" (the condition carries over to all three clauses that follow).
- "neither could God be before himself" → "God could not exist before himself."
- "nor could any thing else be before him" → "nothing else could exist before him."
- "but that he is before all things" → "he exists before everything."
- "it thence follows that he is the Unbegotten" → "Then it follows that he is the Unbegotten."
- "or rather, he is himself the Unbegotten Substance" → "Or, more exactly, he himself is the Unbegotten Substance." ("or rather" is read as a correction toward the stronger claim: not only that God is unbegotten, but that he is identical with unbegotten being.)

**Scan condition:** clean. No damaged or unclear characters in this passage. The sentence opens with "Wherefore," just before the requested start; it is not part of the 42 words and is not rendered here, though it marks the passage as the conclusion of the argument before it.

**Rendering notes:**

- "be before" is rendered "exist before." The argument is about priority of existence (whether anything is prior to God), and plain "be before" in modern English could read as position in space.
- "Unbegotten" and "Unbegotten Substance" are kept as terms. They are technical terms in the source (Whiston's English for Eunomius's *agennētos* and *ousia agennētos*), and turning them into a paraphrase would lose the claim.
