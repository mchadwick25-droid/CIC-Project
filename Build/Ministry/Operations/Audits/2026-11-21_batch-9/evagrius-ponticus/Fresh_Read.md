# Fresh read: Evagrius Ponticus (`evagrius-ponticus`)

Read cold against the Kit, `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md` and `cic/texts/`. No earlier review, revision or recheck was read.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Evagrius Ponticus, a deacon of Constantinople in the late fourth century who became a monk in the Egyptian desert.
- **What happened:** In love with a married woman and unable to break away, he swore an oath in a vision to leave the city. At Jerusalem Melania bound him to the monk's life. In the desert he named eight tempting thoughts and taught that prayer is the mind's own work. Jerome attacked him, and later councils listed him as condemned.
- **Why the connection matters:** His eight thoughts and his teaching on prayer went on being read and translated: Gennadius put a book into Latin, Cassian's Institutes follow the same eight, and a Syriac monk-bishop quoted him by name, even though he had been condemned.

My own essence before comparing: a city churchman who fled to the desert, wrote the map of the eight tempting thoughts and taught unceasing prayer; condemned long after with Origen, yet read East and West. It matches the outline's. The hook (the vision and oath, five short sentences with the quote) leads straight into the essence, which is complete by about word 135. Every "also told" part is present: the eight in order with the quote, acedia glossed, the law of prayer, the second book, the desert regime, Socrates's "philosophy of deeds", Jerome's hostile charge. After the essence the life runs in order. The vision is referred back to in one clause ("The vision came"), as the outline allows, not retold. The ending is on the person: his last words on desire and his death at Epiphany, which echoes the opening. I was not lost anywhere, and nothing talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** An adult narrator's voice with real texture ("a pint of oil lasted him three months", "the city debater learned the philosophy of deeds"). The opening run of short sentences ("He woke and reasoned with himself... He sailed.") is a deliberate turn, not a list. It is a little plain in the What-came section.
- **Craft: 4.** Strong hook, clean essence, one wonder kept in Palladius's voice, the disagreement over the husband told as two witnesses, and a closing image that returns to the desire he fled. In What came of him, Thomas of Marga (840) comes before Jerome (415). That follows the outline's ranking, and the dates carry the listener, but the step back in time is felt.
- **Rigor: 4.** Every sentence traces to the outline. All three quotes, and "or among the first at least" and "the holy Evagrius", are verbatim in their files (Clarke 1918 line 457; Dysinger lines 197 and 373-375; NPNF 2/6 lines 27824-27826; NPNF 2/3 lines 42330-42331; Budge 1893 line 22401). Do not say is honoured throughout: the period is given only for the oil, Cassian is not named as a debtor, 553 is not stated as fact, and "an angel" stays with Palladius. One small slip is noted below.
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 7.2 and reading ease 79.2. On a listener's read it is not flat: the sentences vary and the imagery carries it. "The Cells", "acedia" and "freedom from passion" are glossed. "Passions" is not (see below). Ten names, each a witness or a link.

Scorer: 673 words, grade 7.2 (names out 6.0), reading ease 79.2, 15.3 words a sentence, longest 32 words: pass. That is under the 675 cap.

## Findings

1. **MINOR: who loved first.** The brief says "A married woman of the highest rank fell in love with him, and he with her." Palladius (Clarke line 457, §3) has his desire first and says "the woman loved him in return". The outline's Do not say gives the right wording: "he fell in love with a married woman of high rank, and she with him." Change to: "He fell in love with a married woman of the highest rank, and she with him."
2. **MINOR: "passions" first heard without its plain sense.** It is first heard in the Praktikos quote ("stir up passions"). The outline lists it under Words to explain. The plain English sense carries, so this is not blocking. If a reviser touches the paragraph, add the gloss after the quote: "the strong, unruly feelings the monks fought".
3. **MINOR: sentence length.** "On prayer he writes that no law makes a monk work or fast all the time, but a law tells him to pray without ceasing, since the mind is made for prayer." is 32 words. Optional: split it after "all the time".
4. **MINOR: what was condemned.** The listener hears that councils condemned him with Origen, but hears only Jerome's charge of why. The council of 692's charge (myths of bodies and souls going round in cycles) would tell what the councils held against him. If words are ever freed, it is the first dropped item worth restoring. No change is required.
5. **MINOR: "eighty years on".** The outline says "about eighty years later". Optional: "writing some eighty years on".
6. **MINOR: names heard once.** Didymus is heard once, only inside the council list, which the outline allows. Sozomen and Socrates are each heard once, as credited witnesses. None loses the listener.
