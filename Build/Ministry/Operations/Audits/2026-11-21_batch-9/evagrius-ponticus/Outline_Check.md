# Outline check: Evagrius Ponticus (`evagrius-ponticus`)

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry ("A deacon of Constantinople who became a monk of the Egyptian desert, and wrote of the eight tempting thoughts and of prayer"), the slate's job line, and the source sheet:

**Essence.** A gifted deacon of Constantinople, made deacon by Gregory of Nazianzus, fled the city after a love affair and a vision; at Jerusalem Melania the Elder brought him to the monk's life, and in the Egyptian desert (Nitria, then the Cells) he wrote the Praktikos, naming eight tempting thoughts and teaching ceaseless prayer. He is on the tree because that scheme of the eight and his teaching on prayer passed to later monks, east and west, even though he was condemned as a follower of Origen long after his death.

**Also told.**
- The turn: the affair, the vision of arrest and the oath on the gospel, as he himself told Palladius (Palladius, D as his report; Sozomen differs on the husband).
- Jerusalem: wavering, six months' illness, confession to Melania, her vow; then Nitria two years and the Cells fourteen (Palladius alone for Melania, WA).
- The desert life: disciple of the Macarii, "philosophy of deeds" (Socrates, D); spare diet, copying for his bread; a circle of disciples including Palladius.
- What he wrote: the eight thoughts (Praktikos 6, own, D), what is and is not "up to us"; prayer as the law to pray ceaselessly (Praktikos 49); apatheia giving birth to love.
- What came of it, heaviest first: the eight carried west (Gennadius: "first ... or among the first"; Cassian's same eight, never naming him, DMR that Cassian learned it from him); his chapters read on in Greek, Latin (Rufinus) and Syriac (Thomas of Marga), and in the Philokalia; the charge of Origenism (Jerome 415, hostile) and the later councils naming him from 680 on (D that they say so), with whether 553 itself named him Contested (Hefele).
- Ending: his death at fifty-four after communion at Epiphany, and his last words on three years free of desire (Palladius, D).

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING: Thomas of Marga does not quote "the mind is made for prayer".** Thomas quotes only the first part of Praktikos 49 (no command to watch and fast always, a law to pray continually), plus his own form of the clause on healing the passions through the body (budge1893 : 22400-22404). He does not quote "the mind is naturally made for prayer" (dysinger : 375). As written, the Connection field would have the writer tell an unsupported quotation.
- Connection, replace "In the East, his line that the mind is made for prayer reached a Syriac monk-bishop four and a half centuries later, quoted by name (budge1893 : 22401-22404)." with: "In the East, his line that no one is commanded to watch and fast at all times, but that praying continually is a law, reached a Syriac monk-bishop four and a half centuries later, quoted by name (budge1893 : 22401-22404)."
- What came of them 2, replace "the very words of Praktikos 49 above." with: "the opening of Praktikos 49: no command to watch and fast at all times, but a law to pray continually. He does not quote the line on the mind made for prayer."
- Do not say, add: "Thomas of Marga quoted 'the mind is made for prayer'." Say: he quoted the law to pray continually (budge1893 : 22401-22404).

**B2. BLOCKING: "a second book, now lost".** The sources say only that the Antirrhetikos is not held (Source_Sheet E5; "Not held"). No held text says it is lost, and the outline's own Also told says "not held". Telling it as lost is an unsupported claim.
- Beat 5, replace "A second book, now lost, set verses of Scripture" with "A second book set verses of Scripture".
- Do not say, add: "his lost book" / "now lost" for the Antirrhetikos. Say: "a second book" (Socrates, Gennadius); no claim about whether it survives.

**M1. MINOR: what he defended.** "the faith of Nicaea in the capital's debates" says more than Palladius, who writes only "against every heresy" (457 §2). Replace it with: "What he defended (marked): the faith against every heresy in the capital's debates, 'speaking with youthful zeal against every heresy' (clarke1918 : 457 §2)."

**M2. MINOR: his clothes in Jerusalem.** Clarke calls the text behind "changing his clothes ... back to his old ways" "difficult and probably corrupt" (1167, note 264).
- Beat 3, replace "went back to his old clothes and his old talk" with "slipped back into his old ways and his old talk".
- Do not say, add: "he put his clerical dress aside" or "his old clothes" as fact. Say: he slipped back into his old ways (Clarke calls the text probably corrupt).

**M3. MINOR: freedom from passion is heard only through its accuser.** The listener hears apatheia only through Jerome's "a stone or a god". His own claim, that love is born of it, is held (dysinger : 161, 497) and costs about 10 words. Beat 5, after the prayer sentence, add: "Out of freedom from passion, he writes, love is born (dysinger : 161, 497)."

**M4. MINOR: the Origenists gloss tells a charge as fact.** The cited lines are the condemners' charge (Trullo canon 1; Cyril of Scythopolis). Replace the gloss with: "followers of Origen, a third-century teacher of Alexandria, whom later critics charged with teaching, among other things, that souls existed before bodies".

**M5. MINOR: opening 2 makes Jerome's mockery the book's claim.** Replace "a monk's book claims a calm in which the mind 'becomes either a stone or a god' (Q3)" with: "Jerome mocked a monk's book: in the calm it taught, he said, the mind 'becomes either a stone or a god' (Q3)."

**M6. MINOR: the Links field.**
- Gregory of Nazianzus: "heard him teach" is cited to Socrates's "Gregory the Just" (13634), but Socrates does not say which Gregory that is. Cite Sozomen instead: "heard him teach (Sozomen, 'studied the Sacred Scriptures under Gregory, bishop of Nazianzen', npnf202 : 39667-39669)".
- Justinian I: add the qualifier: "condemned him, per Evagrius Scholasticus (Walford : 9723-9725); that this Evagrius is meant is the later witnesses' reading (Widely Accepted)".
