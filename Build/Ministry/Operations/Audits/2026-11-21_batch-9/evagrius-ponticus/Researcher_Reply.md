**Essence:** Evagrius Ponticus was a gifted deacon at Constantinople. He fled the city over a love affair and became a monk in the Egyptian desert, where he named the eight tempting thoughts and taught that prayer is the mind's own work. Long after his death, councils named him with Origen among the condemned, yet monks in the Latin West and the Syriac East went on using his teaching.

**Also told:** his book (the eight, what is up to us, acedia, prayer); the lost Scripture-against-each-thought book; his life at the Cells; Jerome's attack; the condemnation and the doubt about 553.

**Shape:** Monk or ascetic.

**Length:** about 4 minutes, about 590 words. That covers 56 quote words, about 60 for glosses and about 100 for credited witnesses, so about 215 before any narration.

**Connection:** Gennadius put the eight into Latin. The same eight fill Cassian's Institutes, though Cassian never names him. Thomas of Marga quoted his line on prayer in 840. The condemnation and this reading run side by side.

**Quotes:**
- "Even if the oath was sworn in a trance, I still swore it."
- "Whether these thoughts can trouble the soul is not up to us. But whether they linger, and whether they stir up passions, is up to us."
- Jerome: "In that state the mind is stirred no more. To put it simply, it becomes either a stone or a god."

**Gaps:** These texts are not held: the Greek of Palladius and the Praktikos, Jerome's Latin, the Antirrhetikos, and the acts of 553. All three quotes are rendered from English.
