## B9-13
Source: Jerome (Migne PL 23, Latin), lines 27958-27960.

Rendering (from the Latin): "Make me bishop of the city of Rome, and I will become a Christian at once."

Scan: the OCR prints "Πο- mans urbis", with Greek letters and a line break. I read this as "Romanae urbis" ("of the city of Rome"). It is the only damaged word. Context: Praetextatus, a pagan, used to say this as a joke to Pope Damasus.

## B9-14
Source: Codex Theodosianus 16.1.2 pr. (Latin), line 6883.

Rendering (from the Latin): We want all the peoples ruled by our gentle government to live in the religion that the divine apostle Peter handed down to the Romans. The faith he brought in, still kept to this day, shows this plainly. It is clearly the religion that the pontiff Damasus follows, and Peter, bishop of Alexandria, a man of apostolic holiness.

Note: "quamque pontificem damasum sequi claret" is literally "and which it is clear the pontiff Damasus follows". "Religio usque ad nunc ab ipso insinuata declarat" is literally "the religion brought in by him and lasting until now declares [it]".

Scan: clean.

## B9-15
Source: Liber Pontificalis, Duchesne ed. (1886), line 128214. A Latin verse inscription quoted in Duchesne's French commentary.

Rendering (from the Latin): After the ashes, he will make Damasus rise, for I believe it.

Note: the clause is in the order "because I believe" (quia credo) that he will make Damasus "rise" (surgere). Read plainly: "I believe that after [my] ashes he will make Damasus rise again." The subject "he" comes from the lines before, which are about Christ raising Lazarus.

Scan: clean on this line. The line before has "lenebras" (for "tenebras") and a stray "f".

## B9-16
Source: Epiphanius, Panarion, proem (Holl, GCS 1915, Greek), lines 12387-12391.

Rendering (from the Greek): We are about to tell you the names of the heresies and to expose their lawless practices, which are like poisons and venoms. Along with them we will also supply antidotes: cures for those already bitten, and safeguards for those who might fall into this later.

Note: the sentence opens with "since" (epeidē gar) and finishes in the next line ("we write out this proem for those who love the good"), outside the 39 words. "Prokataleptika" is literally "pre-emptive" or "forestalling" remedies.

Scan: OCR prints "TE" for "τε", "zal" for "καί", and "προχαταληπτικὰ" for "προκαταληπτικὰ". All are readable. The bracketed "[τὰ]" and stray "ἵ" fall in the line before the passage.
