# Outline check: Eusebius of Nicomedia

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, the city where the emperor lived, took up Arius's cause when Alexander of Alexandria put Arius out, and used his standing at court to rally the eastern bishops for him. Exiled after Nicaea, he came back and led the campaign that drove Athanasius and other Nicene bishops from their churches, and he died bishop of Constantinople. Nearly everything we know of him was written by his enemies. Documented.

**Also told.**
- Arius's letter to him, "fellow-Lucianist", and his own letter to Paulinus: the Son not made from the Father's substance (Documented, own words kept by an opponent).
- Nicaea, told three ways: signed (Philostorgius), refused (Socrates), signed the creed but not the condemnation (his own plea; Sozomen) (Contested).
- Constantine's angry letter and the exile; the reason told two ways (Contested).
- The plea and the return, c. 328 (Documented).
- The campaign: Eustathius, the Melitians and Tyre (a clause), the grain charge and Athanasius's exile to Gaul (Documented that he led it; each charge Contested).
- Constantine's baptism near Nicomedia, 337, by him in Jerome's account (Widely Accepted).
- Constantinople and the synod of Antioch, death c. 341 (Documented; how he got the see Contested).
- What came of him: the party "those around Eusebius" carried on his authority; Ulfila's consecration (Dominant Modern Reconstruction).

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING. Do not say does not carry the home-tradition ruling.** The Setting line gives the census name "Homoian ('Arian') Christianity", so a writer could easily call him "Homoian". The ruling is that the Homoian creed came after his death. Add to Do not say:
> - "Homoian", or "a Homoian bishop", for him or his party → "Arius's side" or "the party that took up Arius's cause". The tradition is named Homoian for a creed made after his death; he is its forerunner, not a Homoian. Do not name the tradition by its census name in the story.

**M1. MINOR. The Length line says "Nine beats".** There are seven. Replace "Nine beats of narration at 35-45 words each make about 390" with "Seven beats of narration, with the hook and the ending, at about 45-55 words each make about 390."

**M2. MINOR. Beat 7's date "c. 341" has no held source, and Philostorgius dates it otherwise.** PHI 120 puts the embassy at "the court of the emperor Constantine". Duchesne puts it at the court of "Constance", that is Constantius (DUC 20993-20994). Change the beat heading to "**The Goths (date uncertain; placed here as the modern historian dates it).**" Add to Do not say:
> - Any year for Ulfila's consecration, or "under Constantine" as fact → "Philostorgius sets it at Constantine's court; the modern historian who names this Eusebius sets it under his son Constantius."

**M3. MINOR. Eustathius's charge has three accounts, but the outline gives two.** Athanasius, the only witness from that time, says the charge was "that he had insulted his mother", meaning the emperor's mother (ATH 37929-37931). Beat 4's "was removed on a woman's charge" would put the later version in the narrator's voice. Replace it with "was removed; two later historians tell of a woman's charge: one says Eusebius's party bribed her, Philostorgius tells the charge as true". Add to Leave out:
> - Athanasius's version of the charge against Eustathius, an insult to the emperor's mother (ATH 37929-37931): a third account the length cannot carry. The narrator does not state the woman's charge as fact.

**M4. MINOR. Philostorgius's "secret word" is not barred.** PHI 102-104 says he signed while "under the term homoousios they secretly introduced that of homoiousios". That is Photius's framing, and a writer could easily add it for color. Add to Leave out:
> - The claim that he signed while secretly meaning "of like being" (PHI 102-104): Photius's framing of Philostorgius, and a second technical word the story cannot gloss. Card.

**M5. MINOR. The grain charge and the Constantinople deaths could slip into fact.** Socrates tells the grain threat as a "calumny" (SOC 5359-5364). The deaths came after Eusebius died, in the fight over Macedonius (SOC 6177-6192). Add to Do not say:
> - "Athanasius threatened the grain ships" as fact → "they told the emperor Athanasius had threatened them; Socrates calls it slander."
> - Lives lost in Constantinople put in his lifetime or on his acts → "after his death, in the fight over the city's bishop, Socrates says many lives were lost."

**M6. MINOR. The Connection field states an editor's view as fact, and overstates Paul.** "This was not a school with its own name" is Robertson's (ATH 4125-4127, editor). "Nicaea's leading defenders removed from Antioch, Alexandria and Constantinople" goes beyond SOC 5885-5890, where Constantius removes Paul and Eusebius is moved in. Replace both with:
> Its name came from its enemies: Athanasius called it "those around Eusebius" (ATH-G 2591). Through the court, first at Nicomedia and then at Constantinople, his party saw the bishops of Antioch and Alexandria removed (beat 4), and he himself took Constantinople when the emperor's son put out its bishop, Paul (beat 6).

**M7. MINOR. Do not say invites a quote that is barred.** The entry "Arius calls him 'fellow-Lucianist'" points the writer to a quoted word that `antiochene-church-third-century` already uses ("'fellow-Lucianists'", census `/movements/15`). The slate does not list it. Replace the entry with:
> - "Arius's friend" → "a fellow pupil of Lucian", in paraphrase; do not quote "fellow-Lucianist", which another story on the tree uses.

**M8. MINOR. The Licinius gloss says more than its source.** "The rival emperor Constantine had overthrown" goes beyond GEL 3686-3697, where Licinius marches against Constantine. Replace it with "Licinius, the rival emperor who had made war on Constantine (GEL 3686-3697, a later witness)".

**M9. MINOR. Photius's role line is ambiguous.** "A later patriarch who disliked him" reads as dislike of Eusebius. Photius's stated dislike is of Philostorgius, whom he calls "a liar" (PHI 52). Replace it with "a later patriarch who distrusted Philostorgius".

**M10. MINOR. Ending 2 brings in Arius's death, which Leave out removes.** "The priest who had written to him as a fellow pupil (THD 6064) was long dead" has no cited line, and Arius's death is left to `arius`. Replace it with "The bishop who had answered a hounded priest's letter died in the emperor's new city (THD 6064; SOC 6175-6177)." A small citation fix too: Do not say's "ninety" cites SOC 5906-5907; the line is SOC 5905-5906.
