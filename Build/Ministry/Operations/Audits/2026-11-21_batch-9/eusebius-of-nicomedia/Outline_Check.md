# Outline check: Eusebius of Nicomedia

## My account, written before reading Outline.md

**Essence.** Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, the city where the emperor lived, took up Arius's cause when Alexander of Alexandria put Arius out, and used his standing at court to rally the eastern bishops for him. Exiled after Nicaea, he came back and led the campaign that drove Athanasius and other Nicene bishops from their churches, and he died bishop of Constantinople. Nearly everything we know of him was written by his enemies. Documented.

**Also told.**
- Arius's letter to him, "fellow-Lucianist", and his own letter to Paulinus: the Son not made from the Father's substance (Documented, own words kept by an opponent).
- Nicaea, told three ways: signed (Philostorgius), refused (Socrates), signed the creed but not the condemnation (his own plea; Sozomen) (Contested).
- Constantine's angry letter and the exile; the reason told two ways (Contested).
- The plea and the return, c. 328 (Documented).
- The campaign: Eustathius, the Melitians and Tyre (a clause), the grain charge and Athanasius's exile to Gaul (Documented that he led it; each charge Contested).
- Constantine's baptism near Nicomedia, 337, by him in Jerome's account (Widely Accepted).
- Constantinople and the synod of Antioch, death c. 341 (Documented; how he got the see Contested).
- What came of him: the party "those around Eusebius" carried on his authority; Ulfila's consecration (Dominant Modern Reconstruction).
