## Findings

### B1. BLOCKING: Ulfila is dated by the modern historian only; Philostorgius's own setting is dropped

The brief says: "A modern historian takes this to be Eusebius of Nicomedia, and dates it under Constantius." Philostorgius sets the embassy "to the court of the emperor Constantine" (PHI 120). The modern historian sets it "à la cour de Constance" (DUC 20993-20995). The outline's Do not say gives the right wording for both sides: "Philostorgius sets it at Constantine's court; the modern historian who names this Eusebius sets it under his son Constantius." Two held sources differ, and the brief credits only one of them. A listener hears that Philostorgius gave no setting, or the same one.

**Change** paragraph 9 to:
> Philostorgius says Ulfila, sent by the Goths to Constantine's court, was made their bishop by a bishop named Eusebius. A modern historian takes this to be Eusebius of Nicomedia, and sets it under Constantius instead.

That adds 4 words.

**Do not say line to add to the outline:** "The modern historian's date for Ulfila given alone → give both: 'Philostorgius sets it at Constantine's court; a modern historian sets it under Constantius.'"

### B2. BLOCKING: Alexander's answer to Eusebius is dropped, although the cut-first list says to keep it

Cut-first item 4 cuts only the Berytus charge and says: "keep that Alexander told bishops to ignore his letters (SOC 2158-2172)." The brief cuts both. With it goes the record's conflict in beat 1: Alexander attacked Eusebius by name (SOC 2381-2383) and told the bishops to "pay no attention to anything that Eusebius should write to you" (SOC 2168-2169). Without it, Eusebius acts in paragraph 3 and no one answers him.

**Change** paragraph 3's last sentence to:
> He also wrote to Alexander of Alexandria, and to bishops everywhere, asking them to take Arius back. Alexander told the bishops to ignore his letters.

That adds 8 words.

### M2. MINOR: "to bishops everywhere, asking them to take Arius back" goes a little past Socrates
