# Fresh read: Eusebius of Nicomedia (`eusebius-of-nicomedia`)

Read cold against the Kit, `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md` and `cic/texts/`. No earlier review, revision or recheck was read.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, the emperor's city, in the 320s and 330s. He was Arius's ally, and he died bishop of Constantinople.
- **What happened:** He took up Arius's cause. At Nicaea he signed, or did not sign, depending on the witness. He was exiled, came back, and led the bishops who removed Eustathius and Athanasius. Jerome says he baptized the dying Constantine. He was moved to Constantinople, called the council of Antioch, and may have made Ulfila bishop of the Goths.
- **Why the connection matters:** He built a party that outlived him: his allies took up "all his authority", and his enemies named it after him. Through the court, that party held the eastern churches. Nearly everything we know of him comes from those enemies.

My own essence before comparing: Arius's chief ally and the court bishop who turned Nicaea's aftermath against its defenders, known almost only through his opponents. It matches the outline's. The hook (Arius's letter, with the lifted quote) leads into the essence, which is complete by about word 115, including the hostile-witness sentence. Every "also told" part is present: his own letter's teaching, the three accounts of Nicaea with his plea last, the emperor's charge and the exile, the return and the campaign, Jerome on the baptism, and the party, its name and Ulfila. The life is told once, in order. Ulfila sits between Antioch and the death, as the outline places it, and both dates are given as the Do not say requires. It ends on the person: the friendly and the hostile verdict side by side. Nothing talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** A serious, even narrator. "That choice is why Eusebius is on the tree" is a little meta for a documentary voice (see the minor finding below). The closing pair of verdicts lands well.
- **Craft: 3.** The doubt is made a scene, as the kit asks, with three accounts of one signature. The emperor's letter gives the record's force. But the hostile-witness record means about a dozen sentences turn on "X says". With only 0.1 joining words per 100, long stretches (the Eustathius and Athanasius paragraph above all) come close to a report about the record rather than a story told through it. It still passes the one-hearing test. That is a warning, not a block.
- **Rigor: 5.** Every sentence traces to the outline. Q1 is checked against the Greek (THD-G 70615-70616), Q2 against the Greek and Zenos's English (SOC-G 10555-10558; SOC 3955-3959), and Q3 against the Greek (THD-G 74947-74949, "τῆς τυραννικῆς ὠμότητος συμμύστης") and Jackson's English (THD 7500-7501). The short spans are verbatim: "the prelates" (EUS 67817), "all his authority" and "that disturber of the public peace" (SOC 6180-6181), "the Great" (PHI 103), "turned aside" (Jerome's "declinat", JER 45289). Each Do not say line is honoured. Nothing is stated as fact that a single hostile witness carries.
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 9.2 and reading ease 73.6. The terms are glossed where first heard: of one being, the condemnation, the tyrant, Lucian. Thirteen names, though, plus "another Eusebius", is a heavy load for one hearing.

Scorer: 673 words, grade 9.2 (names out 6.6), reading ease 73.6, 14.6 words a sentence, longest 30 words: pass. That is under the 675 cap.

## Findings

1. **MINOR: meta line.** "That choice is why Eusebius is on the tree." reads as the narrator talking about the format. Optional: "That choice set the course of his life."
2. **MINOR: the bishop of Rome's answer.** "before the bishop of Rome's answer about Athanasius came back" refers to a question the story never sets up. Optional: "before the bishop of Rome had ruled on Athanasius."
3. **MINOR: names heard once.** Lucian and Licinius are heard once, each inside a gloss, which the outline requires. Eustathius is heard once and acts, and the outline allows the name once. Given the load of names, "the bishop of Antioch" alone would do.
4. **MINOR: the run of attributions.** In the paragraph from "After three years his plea won his recall" to "the emperor exiled him", four of six sentences open on a witness. If a reviser touches the paragraph, carry one of those witnesses inside a clause, with cause or time linking the acts. No wording change is required for PASS.
