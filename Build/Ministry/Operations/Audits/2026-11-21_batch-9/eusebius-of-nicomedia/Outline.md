# Outline: Eusebius of Nicomedia

File abbreviations as in `Source_Sheet.md` (SOC, SOC-G, THD, THD-G, PHI, ATH, ATH-G, GEL, JER, PRO, EUS, DUC). All under `cic/texts/`.

- **The essence:** When Alexander of Alexandria put Arius out, Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, the city where the emperor lived, took up Arius's cause (SOC 2123-2126, 2381-2391). After a short exile that followed Nicaea, he led the bishops who drove Arius's opponents from their churches, and he died bishop of Constantinople (SOC 4662-4668, 5885-5890, 6175-6177). Nearly everything we know of him was written by his enemies (Source_Sheet §5.1). Documented.
- **Also told:**
  - What he held, in his own letter: the Son was not made from the Father's own being; everything came to be by God's will (THD 6155-6160, 6192-6194; THD-G 70713-70715, 70894-70897). Documented.
  - Nicaea: he signed the creed but not the condemnation of Arius, by his own plea; Philostorgius and Socrates tell it otherwise (SOC 3955-3957; PHI 102-104; SOC 2950-2954). Contested.
  - The emperor's fury and the exile, then the return and the campaign against Athanasius (THD 7500-7560; SOC 3939-3943; ATH 24808-24822; SOC 5356-5368). Documented that it happened; the charges on each side Contested.
  - Constantine's baptism: Jerome, about forty years on, says Eusebius gave it, and blames it for what followed (JER 45287-45293). Widely Accepted.
  - What came of him: his allies took up his authority after him (SOC 6179-6184); Athanasius's name for the party, "those around Eusebius" (ATH-G 2591 and passim); Philostorgius says a bishop "Eusebius" made Ulfila bishop of the Goths (PHI 120). Documented / Dominant Modern Reconstruction for the Ulfila link.

  3. Beat 1: the meeting of bishops in Bithynia that wrote the same as he did (SOC 27633-27636). About 11 words.

- **Setting:**
  - About 320 to 341 (PHI 108; SOC 3932-3933 editor's 328; SOC 5928-5934 with Zenos's 341).
  - Nicomedia in Bithynia, where "the emperor resided" (SOC 2384-2386); plain words: "Nicomedia, the emperor's city in north-west Asia Minor". Before that Berytus, which Jackson glosses "Beyrout" (THD 6094-6096, editor): "Berytus, today's Beirut". At the end Constantinople, "the newly erected metropolis" (PHI 130): "the emperor's new city, Constantinople".
  - Tradition: Homoian ('Arian') Christianity (census). Plain spoken words: "Arius's side"; never "the Arians" as their own name.
- **Words to explain:**
  - *of one being* (homoousios): Nicaea's word that the Son shares the Father's very being. The tree's wording (live `athanasius-of-alexandria`, `council-of-nicaea-325`) is "of one being".
  - *synod*: say "a meeting of bishops".
  - *the condemnation*: Nicaea's formal curse on Arius and his teaching (SOC 2962-2963). Say "the condemnation of Arius".
  - *Lucian*: "Lucian, a teacher at Antioch who died a martyr" (PHI 138: "this martyr Lucian").
  - *the tyrant* in Constantine's letter: "Licinius, the rival emperor who had made war on Constantine" (GEL 3686-3697, a later witness). Must be said, or the quote misleads.
  - *those around Eusebius* (οἱ περὶ Εὐσέβιον): his enemies' name for his party (ATH-G 2591). "Eusebians" is the later English (ATH 4118-4122, editor); prefer the plain phrase.
- **Shape:** Founder or leader. What he built was a party and its hold on the eastern churches; the trap is offices in place of acts, so each beat is an act.
- **Sibling:** none in this batch. On the tree, each with its own job: `arius` (Arius's teaching; the lifted line here shows instead the letter that drew Eusebius in); `council-of-nicaea-325` (the council; here only how Eusebius signed); `constantine-the-great` (tells the late baptism without naming who gave it; here, who Jerome says gave it); `athanasius-of-alexandria`, `eusebius-of-caesarea` and `melitian-schism` (all tell Tyre in 335; here one clause). Later in the run: `ulfila`.
- **Openings:**
  1. **Arius's letter (recommended).** A priest driven out of Alexandria wrote to the bishop of Nicomedia, greeting him as a fellow pupil of Lucian (THD 6062-6066; THD-G 70621-70622). He told him why he was being hounded: the lifted quote (Q1) (THD 6045-6047; THD-G 70615-70616). The bishop was Eusebius, and he answered by taking up the priest's cause (THD 6127-6130). Leads straight into the essence.
  2. **Their enemies' name for them.** For years Athanasius wrote of the men against him simply as "those around Eusebius" (ATH-G 2591; ATH 21241-21245). If used, the naming is not told again under What came of them.
- **Story beats:**
  1. **He takes up Arius's cause (c. 320-324).** Why: Arius wrote to him, and Alexander's letter attacked him by name (SOC 2381-2384). He wrote to Paulinus of Tyre, urging him to write for their side: the Son was not made from the Father's own being, and all things came to be by God's will (THD 6142-6153, 6155-6160, 6192-6194). He wrote to Alexander and to bishops everywhere to take Arius back; a meeting of bishops in Bithynia wrote the same (SOC 2387-2391; SOC 27633-27636). Alexander's circular told bishops to ignore his letters and said he had deserted Berytus for Nicomedia (SOC 2158-2172). His weight came from the emperor living at Nicomedia (SOC 2384-2387). Documented.
  2. **Nicaea, 325: what did he sign?** Philostorgius, the one friendly historian, says he signed (PHI 102-104). Socrates says he was one of five who would not (SOC 2950-2954). His own account, written from exile some three years later, is Q2 (SOC 3955-3957; SOC-G 10555-10558). Say that it was written later. Contested.
  3. **The emperor turns on him (325).** Within three months of the council he was exiled (PHI 108). Constantine wrote to the people of Nicomedia: Q3 (THD 7500-7501; THD-G 74947-74949). The letter's stated cause: he had sheltered men from Alexandria whom Constantine had ordered seized (THD 7553-7560). Socrates gives another reason: he would not join in condemning Arius (SOC 2960-2966). Another bishop was put in his church (THD 7572-7577). Documented exile; reason Contested.
  4. **Back, and against Arius's opponents (c. 328-335).** After three years his plea won his recall, and he took his church back (PHI 124; SOC 3939-3943). The bishop of Antioch, Eustathius, was removed; two later historians tell of a woman's charge: one says Eusebius's party bribed her, Philostorgius tells the charge as true (THD 7611-7635; PHI 124). He pressed Athanasius, now bishop of Alexandria, to receive Arius, with threats, in Athanasius's own account (ATH 24808-24822; SOC 4662-4668). Socrates names him first among the men behind the charges that saw Athanasius removed by a council at Tyre in 335 (SOC 4943-4948, 5068-5069, 5208-5211). Then, Socrates says, he and his allies told Constantine that Athanasius had threatened Alexandria's grain ships, and the emperor exiled him (SOC 5356-5368). Documented that he led it; the charges Contested.
  5. **Constantine's baptism (337).** Dying near Nicomedia, Constantine called the bishops and asked for baptism; his biographer, another Eusebius, writes only that "the prelates" gave it (EUS 67782-67784, 67817-67819). Jerome, writing in Latin about forty years later, names Eusebius of Nicomedia, and says that with it Constantine "turned aside" to Arius's teaching and that seizures of churches and discord across the world followed (JER 45287-45293). A later chronicle copied him (PRO 52369-52372). Widely Accepted for the name; the verdict is Jerome's.
  6. **Constantinople and Antioch (c. 338-341).** Socrates says Constantine's son Constantius removed the city's bishop, Paul, and moved Eusebius to Constantinople (SOC 5885-5890); other accounts date the move otherwise (card). In 341 Eusebius had a meeting of ninety bishops called at Antioch; Socrates says its aim was to undo "of one being" (SOC 5899-5906, 5928-5934). Documented as Socrates tells it.
  7. **The Goths (date uncertain; placed here as the modern historian dates it).** Philostorgius says Ulfila, on an embassy from the Goths, was made their bishop "by Eusebius and the other prelates that were with him" (PHI 120). A modern historian takes this to be Eusebius of Nicomedia (DUC 20994-20995). Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
- **What came of him:** (about a third: beats 5-7 and the ending)
  1. **The party he led outlived him.** "Those who had formerly co-operated with Eusebius" took up "all his authority": the bishops of Nicaea, Chalcedon and Heraclea, and two from the Danube (SOC 6179-6184); in Constantinople the quarrel cost lives (SOC 6190-6192). Athanasius's name for it, "those around Eusebius" (ATH-G 2591). Documented. *Defended:* Arius's cause and the Son's being made by the Father's will, not from his being.
  2. **The baptism, and Jerome's reading of it** (beat 5). Widely Accepted / Jerome's verdict.
  3. **The first bishop of the Goths** (beat 7). Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
  4. **How each side kept him:** Philostorgius called him "the Great" (PHI 102-104); Socrates called him "that disturber of the public peace" (SOC 6180-6181). One clause, with the ending.
  - Who keeps him today: none held; the census marks the tradition's carriers as ended. Leave out.
- **Quotes:**
  - **Q1** (lifted by the slate; Arius to Eusebius). Tree wording kept word for word: "We are persecuted because we said: the Son has a beginning, but God has no beginning." Rendered on the tree from the Greek, which I checked: THD-G 70615-70616 ("Διωχόμεθα ὅτι εἴπαμεν· ᾿Αρχὴν ἔχει ὁ Υἱὸς, ὁ δὲ Θεὸς ἄναρχός ἐστι"), confirmed by a second print, EPI 45736-45738. Both OCR-damaged; they agree. Job here (new): it is the letter that drew Eusebius into the quarrel, the opening. On `arius` it shows Arius's teaching.
  - **Q2** (his own words, the plea of Eusebius and Theognis; Socrates I.14). Rendered from the Greek, SOC-G 10555-10558: "We signed the creed, but we did not sign the condemnation. We were not finding fault with the creed. We did not believe the accused man was what he was said to be." The Greek is OCR-damaged ("TQ" for τῷ); the Latin on the same page (SOC-G 10583-10586) and Zenos's English (SOC 3955-3959) confirm the reading. "The creed" renders τῇ πίστει, "the faith", meaning the statement of faith. Not on the tree elsewhere. Job: his one surviving word on Nicaea, in his own voice.
  - **Q3** (Constantine to the Nicomedians; Theodoret I.19 in NPNF). Rendered from the Greek, THD-G 74947-74949: "Who taught these things to so innocent a crowd? Eusebius, plainly: the partner in the tyrant's cruelty." Both held Greek prints are OCR-damaged and read alike (THD-G 74947-74949; GEL 15991-15993); Jackson's English confirms (THD 7500-7501). Not on the tree elsewhere. Job: the force of the emperor's charge, from his chief opponent of that year. The narrator must say who the tyrant was (Words to explain).

- **Connection to carry into the story:** What passed from Eusebius was a party and its hold on the eastern churches. Socrates names the men who "had formerly co-operated with Eusebius" and says they took up "all his authority" after his death (SOC 6179-6184). Its name came from its enemies: Athanasius called it "those around Eusebius" (ATH-G 2591). Through the court, first at Nicomedia and then at Constantinople, his party saw the bishops of Antioch and Alexandria removed (beat 4), and he himself took Constantinople when the emperor's son put out its bishop, Paul (beat 6). The thread to the Goths runs through Philostorgius's "Eusebius" (PHI 120), so the writer must say it is a modern historian who names him. Carry it through acts: who he brought back, who he removed, who he made bishop, who took up his place.
- **Ending:**
  1. **His death (recommended).** He died soon after the meeting at Antioch, before the bishop of Rome's answer about Athanasius came back (SOC 6175-6177; SOC 31174-31175). At once the people of Constantinople brought back the bishop he had replaced, and his allies took up his authority (SOC 6177-6184). Close on him: Philostorgius called him "the Great"; Socrates, "that disturber of the public peace" (PHI 102-104; SOC 6180-6181).
  2. **The opening image again.** The bishop who had answered a hounded priest's letter died in the emperor's new city (THD 6064; SOC 6175-6177). Use only if the death is told plainly.

- **Do not say:**
  - "He refused to sign" or "he signed" as fact → "Philostorgius says he signed; Socrates says he refused; his own plea says he signed the creed but not the condemnation."
  - The plea as spoken at Nicaea → "written from exile, some three years later".
  - "Eusebius baptized Constantine" in the narrator's voice → "Jerome, writing about forty years later, says Eusebius baptized him." Keep that the emperor's biographer names no one.
  - "Constantine became an Arian" → it is Jerome's verdict: "Jerome says he turned aside to Arius's teaching."
  - "He plotted with Licinius" or "he spied on Constantine" as fact → "Constantine charged that...".
  - "He framed Eustathius" as fact → "a historian of the next century says his party bribed the woman; Philostorgius tells the charge as true."
  - "He consecrated Ulfila" as fact → "Philostorgius says a bishop Eusebius; a modern historian takes him to be this one."
  - "He was exiled to Gaul" → the held ancient texts give no place for his exile (Gaul is an editor's note, ATH 22258).
  - "The Arians" or "the Eusebians" as their own name → "Arius's side"; "those around Eusebius" is his enemies' name.
  - "Philostorgius's history" as if we have it → "what survives of it, in a hostile summary".
  - Confusing the two Eusebii → say "another Eusebius" for the biographer, once.
  - "He died in 341" credited to Socrates → Socrates says soon after the meeting at Antioch, in the fifth year after Constantine's death; 341 is the editor's year (SOC 5931-5934).
  - "About ninety bishops" → Socrates says "ninety" (SOC 5906-5907).
  - Any feeling, ambition or motive not in the record (e.g. "hungry for power"). Alexander's and Athanasius's charges of greed for a bigger city stay theirs (SOC 2158-2161; ATH 22225-22236).
  - "Arius's friend" → "a fellow pupil of Lucian", in paraphrase; do not quote "fellow-Lucianist", which another story on the tree uses.
  - The eunuch Eusebius of Constantius's court (SOC 5712-5713; PHI 208) is a different man; give him no act.
  - "Homoian", or "a Homoian bishop", for him or his party → "Arius's side" or "the party that took up Arius's cause". The tradition is named Homoian for a creed made after his death; he is its forerunner, not a Homoian. Do not name the tradition by its census name in the story.
  - Any year for Ulfila's consecration, or "under Constantine" as fact → "Philostorgius sets it at Constantine's court; the modern historian who names this Eusebius sets it under his son Constantius."
  - "Athanasius threatened the grain ships" as fact → "they told the emperor Athanasius had threatened them; Socrates calls it slander."
  - Lives lost in Constantinople put in his lifetime or on his acts → "after his death, in the fight over the city's bishop, Socrates says many lives were lost."
