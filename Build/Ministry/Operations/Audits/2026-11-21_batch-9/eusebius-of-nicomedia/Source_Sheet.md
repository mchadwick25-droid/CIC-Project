## 1. Setting

**S1. First bishop of Berytus, then of Nicomedia.** Documented (several witnesses agree; all hostile).
- SOC 2123-2126, Socrates I.6, later (~100 yrs): "Eusebius in particular was a zealous defender of it: not he of Cæsarea, but the one who had before been bishop of the church at Berytus, and was then somehow in possession of the bishopric of Nicomedia in Bithynia."
- SOC 2158-2161, Alexander of Alexandria's circular letter, kept by Socrates, contemporary and hostile: "But since Eusebius, now in Nicomedia, thinks that the affairs of the Church are under his control because, forsooth, he deserted his charge at Berytus and assumed authority over the Church at Nicomedia with impunity".
- ATH 22225-22227, letter of the Egyptian bishops (338) in Athanasius, Defence against the Arians 6, contemporary and hostile: "For he had first the See of Berytus, but leaving that he came to Nicomedia. He left the one contrary to the law, and contrary to the law invaded the other".

**S2. Why Nicomedia gave him weight: the emperor lived there.** Documented (Socrates).
- SOC 2384-2387, Socrates I.6: "Now at this juncture Eusebius possessed great influence, because the emperor resided at Nicomedia. For in fact Diocletian had a short time previously built a palace there. On this account therefore many of the bishops paid their court to Eusebius."
- SOC 27625-27626, Sozomen I.15, later (~120 yrs): "particularly Eusebius, president of the church of Nicomedia, a man of considerable learning and held in high repute at the palace".
- DUC 5180-5181, Duchesne (1907), editor/modern: "très bien vu de l'impératrice Constantia, sœur de Constantin et femme de Licinius". Duchesne's, not an ancient witness.

**S3. A pupil of Lucian of Antioch, like Arius.** Documented for the bond Arius claims; the list of pupils is Philostorgius's, through Photius.
- THD 6062-6066, Arius to Eusebius, in Theodoret I.4 (NPNF numbering), own words of Arius, contemporary: "I bid thee farewell in the Lord, remembering our afflictions, my fellow-Lucianist, and true Eusebius."
- THD-G 70621-70622, the same in Greek: "Συλλουχιῶ-γιστὰ ἀληθῶς, Εὐσέδιε" (OCR damaged for Συλλουκιανιστά, Εὐσέβιε).
- PHI 138, Philostorgius II.14, later (~90 yrs) via Photius: "Philostorgius recounts many other disciples of this martyr Lucian, and especially Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, Maris, bishop of Chalcedon, and Theognis, bishop of Nicaea".

**S4. Date frame.** Nicaea 325; exile within three months of it (PHI 108); recall after three years (PHI 124), which Zenos dates 328 (SOC 3932-3933, editor); Constantine baptized and died at Nicomedia, 337 (THD 8286, Jackson's date, editor); the synod of Antioch "in presence of the emperor Constantius in the consulate of Marcellus and Probinus ... which was the fifth year after the death of Constantine" (SOC 5928-5934, Socrates; Zenos's note gives 341 a.d.); death "a short time after that Synod" (SOC 6175-6177).

## 2. Events, in order

**E1. Arius's letter draws him in (c. 320).** Documented (Arius's own letter).
- THD 6014-6018, Arius to Eusebius: "Ammonius, my father, being about to depart for Nicomedia, I considered myself bound to salute you by him, and withal to inform that natural affection which you bear towards the brethren for the sake of God and His Christ, that the bishop greatly wastes and persecutes us".
- THD 6045-6047: "We are persecuted, because we say that the Son has a beginning, but that God is without beginning."
- THD-G 70615-70616 (Greek, OCR damaged): "Διωχόμεθα ὅτι εἴ-παμεν· ᾿Αρχὴν ἔχει ὁ Υἱὸς, ὁ δὲ Θεὸς ἄναρχός ἐστι." Second print: EPI 45736-45738, Epiphanius Panarion 69.6: "Διωκόμεϑα δὲ ὅτι εἴπομεν, ᾿Δρχὴν ἔχει ὁ υἱὸς, ὁ δὲ ϑεὸς ἀναρχός ἐστι." The two prints agree.
- Tree wording (live `arius`, slate-lifted): "We are persecuted because we said: the Son has a beginning, but God has no beginning."

**E2. He takes up the cause: his letter to Paulinus of Tyre.** Documented (own words, kept by an opponent).
- THD 6127-6130, Theodoret, later (~130 yrs), hostile: "When Eusebius received the epistle, he too vomited forth his own impiety, and wrote to Paulinus, chief of the Tyrians".
- THD 6155-6160, Eusebius to Paulinus, own words: "We have never heard that there are two unbegotten beings, nor that one has been divided into two ... but we affirm that the unbegotten is one and one also that which exists in truth by Him, yet was not made out of His substance".
- THD 6192-6194: "There is, indeed, nothing which is of His substance, yet every thing which exists has been called into being by His will."
- THD-G 70713-70715 (Greek, OCR damaged): "᾿Αλλ᾽ ἐν μὲν τὸ ἀγέννη-“Ὁν, ἕν δὲ τὸ ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ ἀληθῶς, χαὶ οὐκ ἐκ τῆς οὐ-σἷας αὐτοῦ γεγονὸς". THD-G 70894-70897: "Οὐδὲν γάρ ἔστιν ἐκ τῆς οὐσίας αὐτοῦ πάντα [δὲ] βουλήματι αὐτοῦ γενόμενα ἕχαστον ὡς χαὶ ἐγέ-γετό ἐστιν". No second Greek print held.
- ATH 57215-57218, Athanasius, On the Councils 17, contemporary and hostile, quoting Eusebius to Arius: "‘Since your sentiments are good, pray that all may adopt them; for it is plain to any one, that what has been made was not before its origination; but what came to be has a beginning of being.’"

**E3. He rallies bishops for Arius; Alexander names him.** Documented.
- SOC 2387-2391, Socrates I.6: "And he repeatedly wrote both to Alexander, that he might set aside the discussion which had been excited, and again receive Arius and his adherents into communion; and also to the bishops in each city, that they might not concur in the proceedings of Alexander."
- SOC 27633-27636, Sozomen I.15: "A synod having been convened in Bithynia, they wrote to all the bishops, desiring them to hold communion with the Arians, as with those making a true confession".
- SOC 2161-2165, Alexander's circular (see S1): "and has put himself at the head of these apostates, daring even to send commendatory letters in all directions concerning them".
- DUC 5190-5194 (modern, Duchesne's summary): "il inonda l'Orient et l'Asie-Mineure de lettres adressées aux évoques pour les décider à se ranger du côté d'Arius".

**E4. Nicaea, 325: did he sign? Told three ways.** Contested.
- PHI 102-104, Philostorgius I.9 via Photius (Photius's framing, hostile to Philostorgius): "But the rest of the band of Arian bishops, such as Eusebius of Nicomedia, (whom Philostorgius calls the Great,) Theognis of Nicaea, end Tharis of Chalcedon, and all the others, embraced the sentence of the council, though with a fraudulent and treacherous purpose, (as Philostorgius admits,) for under the term o9moou&sioj they secretly introduced that of o9moiou&sioj."
- PHI 108, Philostorgius I.10: "Philostorgius adds that Secundus, on going into exile, said to Eusebius, "You subscribed, Eusebius, in order to escape being sent into banishment: but I place my confidence in a revelation made to me by God, that within a year you will be sent into exile too." In point of fact, within three months after the conclusion of the synod, Eusebius was |434 sent into exile".
- SOC 2950-2954, Socrates I.8: "Five only would not receive it, objecting to the term homoousios, ‘of the same essence,’ or consubstantial: these were Eusebius bishop of Nicomedia, Theognis of Nice, Maris of Chalcedon, Theonas of Marmarica, and Secundus of Ptolemaïs."
- SOC 2960-2962: "Thus having scoffed at the word consubstantial, they would not subscribe to the deposition of Arius."
- SOC 28019-28022, Sozomen I.21: "It ought to be known that although Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, and Theognis, bishop of Nicæa, assented to the document of this faith set forth by the council, they neither agreed nor subscribed to the deposition of Arius."
- His own plea (see E6) says he signed the creed but not the condemnation.
- PHI 544, Walford's note, editor: "Socrates states that five bishops declined to subscribe to the exposition of faith at Nicaea. It is clear, however, that he is mistaken". Walford's view, not a source.
- AMB 27041-27050, Ambrose, On the Faith III.15, later (~55 yrs), English only: "This is betrayed in the letter of Eusebius of Nicomedia. “If,” writes he, “we say that the Son is true God and uncreate, then we are in the way to confess Him to be of one substance (ὁμοούσιος) with the Father.” When this letter had been read before the Council assembled at Nicæa, the Fathers put this word in their exposition of the Faith, because they saw that it daunted their adversaries". Ambrose's claim alone; Inferential-Thin as cause.

**E5. Exile, 325, and the emperor's letter to Nicomedia.** Documented that he was exiled; the reason is Contested.
- THD 7500-7501, Constantine to the Nicomedians, in Theodoret I.19, contemporary and hostile: "“Who has taught these doctrines to the innocent multitude? It is manifestly Eusebius, the co-operator in the cruelty of the tyrants."
- THD-G 74947-74949 (Greek, OCR damaged): "Τίς ἔστιν ὁ ταῦτα διδάξας: οὕτως ἄχαχον πλῆθος ; Ἑῤσέδιος δηλαδὴ, ὁ τῆς τυραννιχῆς ὠμότητος συμ-μύστης." Second print: GEL 15991-15993: "τίς ἐστιν ὁ ταῦτα διδάξας οὕτως ἄχαχον πλῆϑος; Εὐσέβιος δηλαδὴ ὁ τῆς τυραννικῆς ὠμότητος συμ-μύστης." (ἄχαχον is the OCR's reading of ἄκακον in both.) Also GEL 3704-3705.
- THD 7502-7503: "For that he was the creature of the tyrant has been clearly shown". THD 7511-7513: "But he went so far as to send spies to watch me, and scarcely refrained from raising troops in aid of the tyrant."
- THD 7553-7560, the stated cause: "I had sent orders for the apprehension of certain individuals in Alexandria who had deserted our faith ... But these good gentlemen, forsooth, bishops, whom, by the clemency of the council, I had reserved for penitence, not only received them under their protection, but also participated in their evil deeds. Hence I came to the determination to punish these ungrateful men, by apprehending and banishing them to some far-distant region."
- Who "the tyrant" was: the letter does not name him in the held excerpt. GEL 3690-3698, Gelasius (c. 475), later: Licinius "μισϑοδοτεῖ τὸν τῆς Δικομηδέων ἐπίσκοπον Βυσέβιον πάλαι πρόσφυγα αὐτοῦ ὄντα χατὰ τοῦ εὐσεβοῦς βασιλέως Κωνσταντίνου" ("pays the bishop of Nicomedia, Eusebius, long his client, against the pious emperor Constantine"; the "Λικιννίου" stands at GEL 3698, where Eusebius "συντίϑεται οὖν αὐτῷ", agrees with him). SOC 28037-28038, Sozomen: "because he had previously adopted the opinions of the tyrant, and had engaged in his plots." That the tyrant is Licinius: Widely Accepted, resting on Gelasius, a later witness; the letter itself, as held, does not name him.
- SOC 3671-3675, Socrates I.9 summarising the letter: "he censures the misconduct of Eusebius, not only on account of his Arianism, but because also having formerly been well-affected to the ruler, he had traitorously conspired against his affairs."
- SOC 2964-2966, Socrates I.8, the other reason: "At the same time an edict of the emperor sent Arius himself into exile, together with Eusebius and Theognis and their followers".
- SOC 3932-3936, Zenos's note, editor: "It appears also from the accounts of the other historians of this period that Socrates does not give the correct reason for the banishment of Eusebius and Theognis."
- THD 7572-7577: "The above-mentioned bishops were then deposed and banished. Amphion was entrusted with the church of Nicomedia".
- ATH 22258-22259, Robertson's note, editor: "Constantine banished them to Gaul; they were recalled in the course of two or three years." Gaul is the editor's; not used.

**E6. The plea and the return (c. 328).** Documented (own words, kept by Socrates).
- SOC 3939-3943, Socrates I.14: "and Theognis having sent a penitential confession to the principal bishops, were by an imperial edict recalled from exile and restored to their own churches, displacing those who had been ordained in their places; Eusebius [displacing] Amphion, and Theognis Chrestus."
- SOC 3955-3959, the plea, own words (tr. Zenos): "we subscribed the declaration of faith; we did not subscribe the anathematizing; not as objecting to the creed, but as disbelieving the party accused to be such as was represented, having been satisfied on this point, both from his own letters to us, and from personal conversations."
- SOC-G 10555-10558 (Greek, Hussey; OCR damaged, "TQ" for τῷ): "ὑπεσημηνάμεθα τῇ πίστει" TQ δὲ ἀναθεματισμῷ οὐχ ὑπεγράψαμεν, οὐχ ὡς τῆς πίστεως κατηγοροῦντες, ἀλλ᾽ ὡς ἀπιστοῦντες τοιοῦτον εἶναι τὸν κατηγορηθέντα". The Latin on the same page confirms the reading, SOC-G 10583-10586: "fidei quidem subscripsisse, anathematismo vero noluisse subscribere; non quod fidem reprehenderemus, sed quia minime credebamus, illum qui accusatus fuerat, ejusmodi esse". No independent second Greek print held; Zenos's English (above) agrees.
- SOC 3962-3963: "and this we do not as wearied with our exile, but to shake off the suspicion of heresy."
- PHI 124, Philostorgius II.7: "Philostorgius says, that Eusebius, Maris, and Theognis, after a period of three years spent in banishment, were recalled by command of the emperor, and immediately put forth an heretical form of faith, and sent it in every direction, in order to counteract the Nicene Creed."

**E7. The campaign after the return (c. 328-336).** Documented that he led it; each charge's truth Contested.
- SOC 4662-4668, Socrates I.23: "then Eusebius exerted himself to the utmost insidiously to cause the removal of Athanasius and to bring Arius back to Alexandria ... Eusebius therefore wrote to Athanasius, desiring him to re-admit Arius and his adherents into the church. Now the tone of his letter indeed was that of entreaty, but openly he menaced him."
- ATH 24808-24822, Athanasius, Defence against the Arians 59, contemporary, his target: "Upon learning this, Eusebius, who had the lead in the Arian heresy, sends and buys the Meletians with large promises, becomes their secret friend ... At first he sent to me, urging me to admit Arius and his fellows to communion, and threatened me in his verbal communications, while in his letters he [merely] made a request. And when I refused ... he caused the Emperor also, Constantine, of blessed memory, to write to me, threatening me".
- EPI 44161-44166, Epiphanius, Panarion 68.5-6 (c. 375, later, on Athanasius's side; OCR damaged): the Melitian leaders "φιλιοῦνται ἐπισκόπῳ Νικομηδείας τῷ Εὐσεβίῳ" and he asks them to receive Arius into communion. Not used in the story.
- Eustathius of Antioch. THD 7611-7617, Theodoret I.20, later (~120 yrs), hostile: "They bribed a low woman, who made a traffic of her beauty, to sell them her tongue, and then repaired to the council ... She held a babe in her arms, of which she loudly and impudently affirmed that Eustathius was the father." THD 7633-7635: "they succeeded in obtaining the banishment of this champion of piety and chastity". PHI 124, Philostorgius II.7, from Eusebius's side: "Eustathius, bishop of Antioch, was deposed by them on different grounds, namely, on a charge of having attempted the chastity of a certain woman, and seduced her; upon this the emperor condemned him to banishment". ATH 37929-37931, Athanasius, Arian History 4, contemporary: "is falsely accused before the Emperor Constantine, and a charge invented against him, that he had insulted his mother". Three accounts; Contested.
- Tyre, 335 (a clause only; siblings tell it). SOC 4943-4948, Socrates I.27: "The chief conspirators against him were Eusebius bishop of Nicomedia, Theognis of Nicæa, Maris of Chalcedon, Ursacius of Singidnum in Upper Mœsia, and Valens of Mursa in Upper Pannonia. These persons suborn by bribes certain of the Melitian heresy to fabricate various charges against Athanasius". SOC 5208-5211, Socrates I.32: "they voted to depose him". PHI 132, Philostorgius II.11, the mirror story, with Eusebius "regarded as the head and chief of the assembly".
- Athanasius's first exile. SOC 5356-5368, Socrates I.35: "But Eusebius, Theognis, Maris, Patrophilus, Ursacius, and Valens, having gone to Constantinople ... informing the emperor that Athanasius had threatened to prohibit the sending of corn which was usually conveyed from Alexandria to Constantinople ... Hence the emperor being deceived, and excited to indignation against Athanasius by this charge, at once condemned him to exile, ordering him to reside in the Gauls." ATH 21241-21245, Athanasius, Encyclical Letter 7 (339): "Eusebius and his fellows have always been the supporters and associates of the impious heresy of the Arian madmen, by whose means they have ever carried on their designs against me, and were the authors of my banishment into Gaul."
- SOC 29639-29647, Sozomen II.21, later, hearsay ("it is said"): "it is said that Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, and Theognis, bishop of Nicæa, bribed the notary ... effaced their signatures ... Eusebius was accused of these irregularities before the emperor, and he replied with great boldness as he showed part of his clothing. “If this robe,” said he, “had been cut asunder in my presence, I could not affirm the fragments to be all of the same substance.”" Sozomen's Greek for Book II is not held. Inferential-Thin; Leave out.

**E8. Constantine's baptism, 337.** That Constantine was baptized near Nicomedia at the end: Documented. That Eusebius of Nicomedia gave it: Widely Accepted, on one held ancient witness who names him (Jerome, ~43 yrs later, hostile), copied by Prosper.
- EUS 67782-67784, Eusebius of Caesarea, Life of Constantine IV.61, contemporary: "After this he proceeded as far as the suburbs of Nicomedia, and there, having summoned the bishops to meet him, addressed them in the following words." EUS 67817-67819: "After he had thus spoken, the prelates performed the sacred ceremonies in the usual manner, and, having given him the necessary instructions, made him a partaker of the mystic ordinance." Names no bishop.
- SOC 5566-5567, Socrates I.39: "removing from Helenopolis to Nicomedia, he took up his residence in the suburbs, and there received Christian baptism." Names no one.
- THD 8287-8289, Theodoret I.30 names no one; the name is Jackson's note, editor: "At the hand of Eusebius of Nicomedia."
- JER 45287-45293, Jerome, Chronicle, later (~43 yrs), hostile: "Constantinus extremo uitae suae tem-pore ab Eusebio Nicomedensi episcopo baptizatus in Arrianum dogma declinat, a quo usque in praesens tempus ecclesiarum rapinae et totius orbis est secuta discordia." The text is clean.
- PRO 52369-52372, Prosper, Chronicle (5th c.), copying Jerome: "Constantinus cxtremo vitae suae tempore ab Eusebio Nicomedensi episcopo baptizatus in Arrianuni dogma declinat."
- PHI 142, Philostorgius II.16 via Photius: Constantine's testament "was intrusted to Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia". SOC 5585-5595 gives the will to an unnamed presbyter; Zenos's note (SOC 5588-5592, editor) thinks Eusebius "also probably baptized Constantine". Leave out.

**E9. Bishop of Constantinople; the synod of Antioch (c. 338-341).** Documented that he held Constantinople; when and how Contested.
- SOC 5885-5890, Socrates II.7: "having convened an assembly of bishops of Arian sentiments, he divested Paul of his dignity, and translating Eusebius from the see of Nicomedia, he appointed him bishop of Constantinople."
- THD 7472-7475, Theodoret I.19 (NPNF I.18): "Eusebius, the propagator of impiety ... without delay quitted Nicomedia and seized upon the see of Constantinople". PHI 130, Philostorgius II.10: "on the decease of Alexander, the bishop of this city, Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia, was translated to the episcopal chair of the newly erected metropolis." PHI 578, Walford's note, editor: "This would seem to be a mistake".
- ATH 22231-22233, the Egyptian bishops (338): "For, behold, withdrawing himself from the second, again he takes possession of another’s".
- SOC 5899-5906, Socrates II.8: "Eusebius, however, could by no means remain quiet, but as the saying is, left no stone unturned, in order to effect the purpose he had in view. He therefore causes a Synod to be convened at Antioch in Syria, under pretense of dedicating the church ... but with the real intention of subverting and abolishing the doctrine of the homoousion. There were present at this Synod ninety bishops".

**E10. Ulfila made bishop (c. 341).** That Ulfila was consecrated by "Eusebius": Philostorgius alone, via Photius. That this Eusebius is the bishop of Nicomedia: Dominant Modern Reconstruction (editors).
- PHI 120, Philostorgius II.5: "Being sent by the then king of the Goths on an embassy to the court of the emperor Constantine ... he was ordained bishop of the Christians among time Goths, by Eusebius and the other prelates that were with him."
- AUX 15240-15241, Auxentius, Ulfila's pupil: "Gothorum de lectore triginta annorum episkopus est ordinatus" — names no consecrator.
- DUC 20994-20995, Duchesne: "Eusèbe de Nicomédie le vit, et, augurant bien de ses aptitudes, le consacra évoque pour sa nation." AUX 3766-3767, Kauffmann, editor: "durch Eusebius und die Eusebianer die Ordination als bischof der Goten empfangen habe."

**E11. Death (c. 341).** Documented.
- SOC 6175-6177, Socrates II.12: "But Eusebius did not live to learn the decision of Julius concerning Athanasius, for he died a short time after that Synod was held. Whereupon the people introduced Paul again into the church of Constantinople".
- SOC 31174-31175, Sozomen III.7: "But before he had been able to ascertain the sentiments of Julius, and, indeed, not long after the council of Antioch, Eusebius died."

## 3. Impact, heaviest first

**I1. The party that bore his name carried on after him.** Documented. What he defended: Arius's cause, and the teaching that the Son was not made from the Father's own being.
- SOC 6179-6184, Socrates II.12: "This those who had formerly co-operated with Eusebius (that disturber of the public peace) brought about, assuming all his authority. These were Theognis, bishop of Nicæa, Maris of Chalcedon, Theodore of Heraclea in Thrace, Ursacius of Singidunum in Upper Mysia, and Valens of Mursa in Upper Pannonia."
- SOC 6190-6192: "By this intestine war among the Christians, continuous seditions arose in that city, and many lives were sacrificed in consequence of these occurrences."
- ATH-G, PG 25: Athanasius's own name for the party is "οἱ περὶ Εὐσέβιον", "those around Eusebius" (143 matches in the file; e.g. ATH-G 2591: "Καὶ οἱ περὶ Εὐσέδιον χαὶ πρὸς Ἰούλιον ἔγραψαν", OCR damaged).
- ATH 4118-4131, Robertson's Prolegomena, editor: "It should be observed once for all that the term ‘Eusebians’ is the later and inexact equivalent of the last named Greek phrase, which ... has reference to Eusebius of Nicomedia only ... they are the ‘ring,’ or personal entourage, of one man, a master of intrigue". Robertson's view; do not give it to Athanasius.

**I2. The baptism of Constantine, and how his enemies read it.** Widely Accepted that he gave it (Jerome, then Prosper); the reading is Jerome's own hostile judgment. JER 45287-45293; PRO 52369-52372 (above, E8).

**I3. The Gothic church's first bishop.** Philostorgius says "Eusebius"; Duchesne and Kauffmann say this Eusebius (E10). The Gothic church took the non-Nicene form (live `ulfila` story).

**I4. The synod of Antioch, 341, convened by him.** Documented as Socrates tells it (E9, SOC 5899-5906).

**I5. How each side remembered him.** Philostorgius called him "the Great" (PHI 102-104; PHI 52, Photius: "He also praises Eusebius of Nicomedia (whom he calls the Great)"). Socrates: "that disturber of the public peace" (SOC 6180-6181). Athanasius, ATH 21241-21243 (above). Jerome (above).

## 4. Links to other people on the tree

- **Arius**: wrote to him (THD 6005-6066); he took up Arius's cause (SOC 2123, 2387-2389).
- **Alexander of Alexandria**: named him in his circular (SOC 2158-2172).
- **Council of Nicaea**: signed the creed but not the condemnation, by his own plea (SOC 3955-3957); told otherwise by Socrates and Philostorgius.
- **Constantine the Great**: exiled him (THD 7500-7560), recalled him (SOC 3939-3941), and was baptized near Nicomedia, by him in Jerome's account (JER 45287-45290).
- **Athanasius of Alexandria**: pressed him to admit Arius, then led the charges against him (ATH 24808-24822; SOC 4943-4948, 5356-5368).
- **Eusebius of Caesarea**: Arius names him as "your brother" (THD 6031); both sat at Tyre (sibling `eusebius-of-caesarea`); his Life of Constantine names no baptizer (EUS 67817-67819).
- **Melitian Schism**: the Melitians' charges against Athanasius were his doing, in Athanasius's and Socrates's account (ATH 24808-24811; SOC 4946-4948).
- **Ulfila**: consecrated, in Philostorgius and on Duchesne's reading (PHI 120; DUC 20994-20995).
- **Philostorgius**: his only friendly witness (PHI 102-104, 52).
- **Socrates of Constantinople**, **Theodoret of Cyrrhus**, **Sozomen**, **Jerome**: hostile later witnesses (above).

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

1. **Nearly the whole record is hostile.** Alexander, Athanasius and Constantine wrote against him in his lifetime; Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret wrote a century later on the Nicene side; Philostorgius, the one friendly historian, survives only in the epitome of Photius, who calls him "a liar" (PHI 52). His own words survive in three pieces, each kept by an opponent: the letter to Paulinus (Theodoret), the plea (Socrates), and a line to Arius (Athanasius).
2. **Signing at Nicaea**: signed (Philostorgius); refused (Socrates); signed the creed but not the condemnation (his plea; Sozomen agrees).
3. **Why he was exiled**: sheltering Alexandrians and old ties to "the tyrant" (Constantine's letter); refusing to condemn Arius (Socrates). Zenos sides against Socrates (editor).
4. **How he got Constantinople**: after Constantius deposed Paul (Socrates); on the death of the city's bishop Alexander (Theodoret, Philostorgius). Walford calls the latter a mistake (editor).
5. **Who baptized Constantine**: no one named by Eusebius of Caesarea, Socrates or Theodoret; Eusebius of Nicomedia by Jerome (c. 380) and Prosper. Jackson's note to Theodoret names him (editor).
6. **Eustathius**: a bribed false witness (Theodoret); a true charge (Philostorgius); a slander about the emperor's mother (Athanasius).
7. **Ulfila's consecrator**: "Eusebius" only (Philostorgius); the bishop of Nicomedia (Duchesne, Kauffmann). Philostorgius says "Constantine"; Duchesne says "Constance" (DUC 20993-20994).
8. **The word "of one being" at Nicaea**: Ambrose alone says Eusebius's letter, read out at the council, made the bishops adopt it (AMB 27041-27050).
9. **The robe**: "it is said", in Sozomen only (SOC 29639-29647).
