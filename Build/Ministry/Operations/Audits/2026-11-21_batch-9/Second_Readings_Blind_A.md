# Second Readings, Blind A (batch 9, B9-01 to B9-10)

## B9-01
Source: Lietzmann 1904, lines 11480-11481. Greek.

Text read: καὶ εἰ ἀνθρώπῳ (φησὶ) συνήφθη ὁ θεός, τέλειος τελείῳ, δύο ἂν ἦσαν, εἷς μὲν φύσει υἱὸς θεοῦ εἷς δὲ θετός.

Rendering: "And if God," he says, "had been joined to a human being, the complete to the complete, there would have been two: one Son of God by nature, and the other by adoption."

Notes: θετός is literally "adopted" or "made a son by adoption". Scan is clean here.

## B9-02
Source: NPNF 2.2 (Sozomen), lines 39027-39028. English.

Text read: Men sang his strains at convivial meetings and at their daily labor, and women sang them while engaged at the loom.

Rendering: Men sang his songs at their dinner parties and while they worked each day, and women sang them as they worked at the loom.

## B9-03
Source: Lietzmann 1904, lines 12986-12987. Greek.

Text read: μίαν φύσιν τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένην

Rendering: one nature of God the Word, made flesh

Notes: The phrase sits inside a longer sentence: "[not two natures, one worshipped and one not worshipped,] but one nature of God the Word made flesh, and worshipped together with his flesh in one act of worship." Only the six words are rendered above. σεσαρκωμένην is feminine, agreeing with φύσιν ("nature"), so grammatically it is the nature that is "incarnate", though English readers will hear it as the Word. Scan is clean.

## B9-04
Source: Lietzmann 1904, lines 5754-5756. Greek, inside a German page (Polemon fr. 174).

Text read (23 words): τὴν γὰρ ᾿Απολλιναρίου προσηγορίαν ὡς σαφῆ δυσσεβείας ἔλεγχον δολερῶς ἀπωθούμενοι τὰς τῶν ἁγίων πατέρων ἐπωνυμίας τοῖς τούτου συγγράμμασι καὶ τοῖς ἑαυτῶν πλάσμασιν ἐπιτιθέασι

Rendering: For they craftily push away the name of Apollinarius, since it is a plain proof of impiety, and they put the names of the holy Fathers on his writings and on their own fabrications.

Notes: The sentence goes on past the 23 words: "...stealing away the simpler people and running off from the road of truth" (κλέπτοντες τοὺς ἁπλουστέρους καὶ τὴν ὁδὸν τῆς ἀληθείας διαδιδράσκοντες). That continuation is not in the rendering above. ἐπωνυμίας could be "names" or "titles/attributions"; I take it as the Fathers' names used as false authorship labels. Scan is clean for these words.

## B9-05
Source: NPNF 2.8 (Basil, Letter 223), lines 39133-39135. English.

Text read: Then once upon a time, like a man roused from deep sleep, I turned my eyes to the marvellous light of the truth of the Gospel,

Rendering: Then one day, like a man waking from a deep sleep, I turned my eyes to the wonderful light of the truth of the Gospel.

## B9-06
Source: Migne PG 31 (Basil), lines 72547-72549. Greek.

Text read: Τίνα οὖν ἀπονίψεις; τίνα θεραπεύσεις; τίνος ἔσχατος ἔσῃ, αὐτὸς καθ᾽ ἑαυτὸν διάγων; Τὸ δὲ καλὸν [scan: χαλὸν] ...

Rendering: Whose feet, then, will you wash? Whom will you care for? Whose servant, last of all, will you be, if you live by yourself alone? [But the good thing ...]

Notes: I count 12 words in the three questions; the request says 15, which would take in the first words of the next sentence, "Τὸ δὲ καλὸν" ("But the good [and pleasant thing]"), which breaks off mid-sentence and is bracketed above. "Whose feet" fills out ἀπονίψεις ("will you wash off"), which here follows directly on Christ washing the disciples' feet; the Greek says only "whom will you wash". θεραπεύσεις can mean "serve" or "tend" as well as "heal"; "care for" covers it. τίνος ἔσχατος ἔσῃ is literally "of whom will you be last", echoing "the last of all and servant of all". The OCR uses χ for κ throughout (χα-λὸν for καλὸν); the question words themselves read clearly.

## B9-07
Source: NPNF 2.8 (Basil), lines 33483-33485. English.

Text read: As we were baptized, so we profess our belief. As we profess our belief, so also we offer praise.

Rendering: We state our faith in the same terms as we were baptized. And we give praise in the same terms as we state our faith.

## B9-08
Source: NPNF 2.7 (Cyril of Jerusalem, intro note 315, citing Catechetical Lecture xiii. 23), lines 4013-4015. Greek.

Text read: ῾Ορᾶς τοῦ Γολγοθᾶ τὸν τόπον;

Rendering: Do you see the place of Golgotha?

Notes: The English line in the main text gives it as "Seest thou this spot of Golgotha?"; the Greek has no "this", only "the place".

## B9-09
Source: same note, lines 4016-4018. Greek.

Text read: ᾽Επιβοᾶς ἐπαίνῳ ὡς συντιθέμενος.

Rendering: You shout out your praise, as one who agrees.

Notes: Singular in Greek: "You (one hearer) cry out in praise, as one agreeing." The editor's English paraphrase is "the hearers answer with a shout of approval". συντιθέμενος = "assenting, agreeing".

## B9-10
Source: NPNF 2.7 (Cyril, Mystagogical Lecture 1.1), lines 20639-20640. English.

Text read: since I well knew that seeing is far more persuasive than hearing, I waited for the present season;

Rendering: because I knew well that seeing convinces far more than hearing, I waited until now.

Notes: "the present season" could mean "this time of year" (Easter week) as well as simply "now".
