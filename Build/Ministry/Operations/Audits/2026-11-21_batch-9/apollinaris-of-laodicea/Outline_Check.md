# Outline check: Apollinaris of Laodicea (`apollinaris-of-laodicea`)

Checker, batch 9, 2026-11-21.

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

**Essence.** Apollinaris, a scholar and bishop of Laodicea in Syria and a firm ally of Athanasius and of Nicaea, tried to answer how Christ could be one, and taught that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind. The church condemned the teaching (at Rome under Damasus, and at Constantinople in 381), his followers formed churches of their own, and his writings, kept alive under orthodox names, carried the formula "one nature of God the Word made flesh" to Cyril of Alexandria and into the later quarrels over Christ. He is on the tree as the great Nicene teacher whose answer about Christ was rejected, and whose words shaped the next two centuries anyway.

**Also told.** Twice put out of the church at Laodicea (the hymn to Dionysus; then by George, for Athanasius's friendship or the sophist's, the historians differ). With his father, recast Scripture in classical forms when Julian barred Christians from the classics (who wrote what is contested). Taught Jerome at Antioch. His songs sung at work and at the loom, which drew followers; Vitalius at Antioch; separate churches under their own bishops. Epiphanius, his old admirer, at first would not believe it. Gregory of Nazianzus's reply ("what is not assumed is not healed": the sibling's text, a clause credited to Gregory). Condemned at Rome and at Constantinople 381; imperial laws against his followers. Died under Theodosius, year unknown. Afterward: followers drifting back into the church carrying the one-nature teaching; the forgeries exposed in the sixth century; Vincent's warning of the great teacher who fell.

Source basis: census entry (teaser and longDescription), slate job line, Hearts.md ruling 1 (researcher's wording holds), Source_Sheet.md sections 2 and 3.

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING. Library sweep: Rufinus's Church History is held, and the sheet says it is not.

The sheet's sweep says "Rufinus's Church History II.20 (cited by Hartranft): not held in English or Latin in the Library." It is held: `eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-rufinus-x-xi-grc-lat_schwartz-mommsen1908.txt` (RUF), book 11, lines 30635-30653. The sibling `damasus-of-rome` already cites it (its Source_Sheet E24). Rufinus wrote about 402: Apollinaris's own lifetime, or just after it, and the earliest narrative of the teaching. He is the source behind Socrates's two stages, and he is the earliest witness for the essence's last clause.

Read at RUF 30635-30653: Apollinaris, bishop at Laodicea in Syria, "vir sane in ceteris instructus", bred a heresy "ex contentione" (from love of argument); he first said the Lord took "solum corpus, non etiam animam"; pressed with the Gospels, he granted a soul, but not its reasoning part, "ad supplementum vero rationabilis partis ipsum verbum dei fuisse perhibebat"; condemned first at Rome "a Damaso et Petro Alexandrino episcopo concilio congregato", then confirmed at Alexandria and at Constantinople by a council's decree; "ex illo Apollinaristae ab ecclesia declinantes et episcopos sibi suae partis et dogmata propria ecclesiasque defendunt".

### 2. BLOCKING. Connection: the outline names the wrong group behind the false names, and adds a cause the source does not give.

The Connection says: "His followers, condemned and barred from meeting, kept his writings alive by giving them the names of honoured bishops (LIETZ 4150-4160)." Lietzmann, LIETZ 4151-4157, says something else. Before Cyril's time, the people "die im herzen apollinaristisch dachten, aber zur grosskirche hielten" (who were Apollinarian at heart but held to the main church) gave the master's works orthodox-sounding names and "so vor der vernichtung bewahrt" (so saved them from destruction). These were not the condemned separate churches. And "condemned and barred from meeting" ties the renaming to the laws, which no held line does. A listener would hear the renaming as the outlawed sect's answer to persecution. What came of them 1 has the same slip in milder form ("His followers put the names").

Corrections:
- Connection, replace the sentence with: "Some who held his views at heart but stayed inside the main church gave his writings the names of honoured bishops, and so, says Lietzmann, saved them from destruction (LIETZ 4151-4157)."
- What came of them 1, replace "His followers put the names of honoured bishops, Athanasius among them, on his writings, says Lietzmann, and so saved them from destruction (LIETZ 4133-4160)." with "Men who shared his views but stayed inside the main church put the names of honoured bishops, Athanasius among them, on his writings, says Lietzmann, and so saved them from destruction (LIETZ 4151-4157)."
- Do not say, add: "'His banned followers forged the names to escape the laws.' Say: some who shared his views but stayed in the main church put the names on (LIETZ 4151-4157); no source ties it to the laws."

### 3. MINOR. Opening 2 sets the drinking parties inside the churches.

"Sozomen says that in the churches of his followers, men sang his songs at drinking parties and at work, and women at the loom." Sozomen, SOZ-ENG 39023-39028, separates the two: the churches sang "metrical songs" besides the customary order; men sang his strains at "convivial meetings" and at work, and women at the loom.
Correction: "Sozomen says his followers sang his songs in their churches, and that men sang them at drinking parties and at work, and women at the loom."

### 4. MINOR. Beat 4 tells Gregory of Nazianzus's answer ahead of its time.

The letter to Cledonius belongs to Gregory's retirement after 381 (GNAZ 23222-23235, the editors' account of his last years). Beat 4 tells it before Jerome's lectures of 374 (beat 5) and the condemnations (beat 6). No date is spoken, so a listener is not misled, but it breaks the time order.
Correction: move "Gregory of Nazianzus answered that what Christ did not take on, he did not heal (GNAZ 44690-44691)" from beat 4 to the end of beat 6, after the 381 sentence, as "After 381, Gregory of Nazianzus answered that what Christ did not take on, he did not heal (GNAZ 44690-44691; GNAZ 23222-23235)."

### 5. MINOR. Beat 5 and Names: "made the priest Vitalius bishop".

Theodoret (THDT 15538-15539) says only that he "had already ordained Vitalius". Sozomen (SOZ-ENG 39011-39013) says Vitalius "presided over those at Antioch". Neither held line says "bishop".
Correction (beat 5 and cut-first item 2, with its card line): "At Antioch he ordained the priest Vitalius, who led his followers there (THDT 15538-15539; SOZ-ENG 39006-39015)."

### 6. MINOR. Quote 3's rendering can be heard the wrong way round.

My rendering from LIETZ 5754-5756: "For, craftily pushing aside the name of Apollinaris as a plain proof of ungodliness, they put the names of the holy Fathers on his writings and on their own inventions." The outline's rendering matches word for word in sense. But "They cunningly push aside the name of Apollinaris, as plain proof of ungodliness" can be heard as saying that pushing the name aside is the proof. The Greek says the name itself is the proof.
Correction: "They cunningly push aside the name of Apollinaris, because it plainly proves ungodliness. They put the names of the holy Fathers on his writings and on their own inventions."

Quotes 1 and 2 checked: my renderings ("If God was joined to a man, complete to complete, they would be two: one Son of God by nature, and one adopted"; "Men sang his songs at drinking parties and at work, and women at the loom") agree with the outline's. Fragment 81 is confirmed in NYSSA-PG45 88005-88007. None of the three is on the tree already (search of `cft-website/` and `cic-website/data/`). The phrase "one nature of God the Word made flesh" appears on the tree only as a description (`theodosius-i-of-alexandria`), not as a quote.

### 7. MINOR. Ending 1: Socrates's verdict covers only the works written under Julian.

Socrates's "now of no greater importance, than if they had never been written" (SOC-ENG 11253-11254) is about the classical-form works of father and son. Sozomen's high view (SOZ-ENG 36705-36707) is about the same works. A writer closing on it after the forged-names impact could stretch it to all his writings.
Correction, add to Do not say: "'His writings were forgotten' or 'as if never written' about his books in general. Say: Socrates said this of the poems and dialogues written under Julian."

### 8. MINOR. Words to explain: three words are missing.

Beat 1 uses "sophist", beat 2 "synod", and What came of them 1 "confession". None is glossed.
Correction, add: *sophist*: "a paid teacher of public speaking" (in beat 1 he is already "a pagan teacher"; keep that and drop "sophist", or gloss it); *synod*: say "a meeting of bishops"; *confession* (to Jovian): "a statement of faith".
