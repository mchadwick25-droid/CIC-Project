## The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who:** Apollinaris of Laodicea, a fourth-century Christian scholar and teacher in Syria. He was a friend of Athanasius and stood for the creed of Nicaea.
- **What happened:** When Julian shut Christians out of the classics, he (and by one historian's account his father too) recast Scripture as epic, plays and dialogues. He then taught that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind. He was condemned at Rome and at Constantinople in 381. His followers formed their own churches, and imperial laws then outlawed them.
- **Why the connection matters:** His writings survived under honoured bishops' names. One, carried as Athanasius's, gave Cyril of Alexandria the phrase "one nature of God the Word made flesh", which Cyril's side held as a watchword. A century and a half later Justinian charged the one-nature party with still using the false names.

None is blank or wrong.
