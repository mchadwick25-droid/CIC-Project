## 1. Setting

**S1. Laodicea in Syria; father and son of one name; their church offices.**
SOC-ENG 9930-9938 (Socrates, Church History II.46; later, gap about 60-80 years). Widely Accepted.
> "There were two men of the same name at Laodicea in Syria, a father and son: their name was Apollinaris; the former of them was a presbyter, and the latter a reader in that church. Both taught Greek literature, the father grammar, and the son rhetoric. The father was a native of Alexandria, and at first taught at Berytus, but afterwards removed to Laodicea, where he married, and the younger Apollinaris was born."

Sozomen agrees on the offices: SOZ-ENG 39066-39067: "for they both belonged to the clergy, the father being a presbyter, and the son a reader of the Holy Scriptures."

**S2. The tradition.** Census entry `apollinarian-christianity`: name "Apollinarian Christianity", informal name "The Followers of Apollinaris", dates "c. 360-430", place "Laodicea · Syria and beyond" (`cic-website/data/world-census.json`, movement `apollinarian-christianity`). The person's census entry: `toldWithin: apollinarian-christianity`, region "Laodicea, Antioch".

**S3. Jerome's Chronicle names him bishop and writer in the 360s.**
JER-CHRON 46043-46044 (Jerome, Chronicle, entered under Valentinian and Valens; contemporary). Documented.
> "Apollinaris Laodicenus cpiscopus [episcopus] multimoda / nostrae religionis scripta componit."
(Apollinaris, bishop of Laodicea, writes works of many kinds on our religion.)

**S4. When he became bishop is not known.** LIETZ 823-835 (editor): Jerome, Philostorgius, his pupil Timothy and the synod of 362 all call him bishop; "wann und unter welchen umständen er die würde erlangt habe, lässt sich dagegen nur vermuten" (when and how he got the office can only be guessed). LIETZ 489-491 chronology: "360 ... Apollinaris vielleicht gegenbischof" (perhaps a rival bishop). Editor; Inferential-Thin for any date. Richardson's note at THDT 41392: "Bishop 362, died about 390" (editor).

## 2. Events, in order

**E1. Put out of the church by Bishop Theodotus, then restored (the hymn to Dionysus).**
SOZ-ENG 39052-39070 (Sozomen VI.25; later, gap about a century). Documented in Sozomen alone.
> "For when Theodotus, the predecessor of George, regulated the church of Laodicea, Epiphanius, the sophist, recited a hymn which he had composed in honor of Dionysus. Apolinarius, who was then a youth and a pupil of Epiphanius, went to hear the recitation, accompanied by his father ... With respect to Apolinarius, father and son, he convicted them both publicly of their sin, and ejected them from the church ... After some time had elapsed, and when the father and son had evinced by tears and fasting a degree of repentance adequate to their transgression, Theodotus restored them to their offices in the church."
Lietzmann reads the act as aimed at their stand for Nicaea (LIETZ 763-768, editor: "im grunde gegen die kirchenpolitische parteistellung der verurteilten gerichtet"). Editor's view; Dominant Modern Reconstruction at most.

**E2. Put out again by Bishop George. The two historians give different reasons.**
- Sozomen, SOZ-ENG 39041-39050 and 39070-39075: Athanasius "had to pass through Laodicea, and that while in that city he formed an intimacy with Apolinarius, which terminated in the strictest friendship. As, however, the heterodox considered it disgraceful to hold communion with Athanasius, George, the bishop of the Arians in that city, ejected Apolinarius in a very insulting manner from the church, under the plea that he had received Athanasius contrary to the canons and holy laws." And: "It is said that Apolinarius besought him repeatedly to restore him to communion, but that he was inexorable."
- Socrates, SOC-ENG 9944-9952: George "excommunicated them" because they would not stop seeing Epiphanius the sophist; "Nevertheless some affirm that it was not for the reason above assigned that they dissented from George, but because they saw the unsettledness and inconsistency of his profession of faith".
- Date: LIETZ 479 "346 oct.: Apollinaris durch Georg v. Laodicea excommuniciert", argued at LIETZ 806-813 (Athanasius passing through on his way home from his second exile). Editor.
Confidence: the expulsion, Documented; its reason, Contested; the date 346, Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Lietzmann).
Note: Sozomen says Athanasius was returning "from the place whither he had been banished by Constantine" (SOZ-ENG 39042-39043). Lietzmann puts the visit in 346 (return from the second exile). Do not name the emperor in the story.

**E3. An ally of Athanasius and of Nicaea.**
- His envoys at Athanasius's synod of 362. ATH 60171-60173 (Athanasius, Tome to the Antiochenes, §9; own time, by Athanasius). Documented.
> "And there were present certain monks of Apolinarius the bishop, sent from him for the purpose."
- Sozomen on the Holy Spirit, SOZ-ENG 38725-38731 (VI.22): those "who believed that the Son is consubstantial with the Father, held also the same view about the Spirit. This doctrine was nobly maintained in Syria by Apolinarius, bishop of Laodicea; in Egypt by Athanasius". Documented (Sozomen).
- Socrates says his later followers still held the Nicene Trinity: SOC-ENG 9961-9964: "Those who followed them and bear their name at this day affirm that this is their only point of distinction [from the Catholics]; for they recognize the consubstantiality of the persons in the Trinity." Documented (Socrates).
- Epiphanius: "always dear to us and to the blessed pope Athanasius": see E7.
- Philostorgius (lifted quote, Apollinaris only): PHIL 472 (Philostorgius as kept in the Suda; later, gap about 40-60 years; a historian of the Eunomian party the three men fought). Documented as Philostorgius's view.
> "These three individuals were strenuous in their defence of the consubstantial faith against the doctrine of dissimilarity of substance, and so wholly confuted all those who previously and up to my own day were leaders of that heresy, that Athanasius must be judged a mere child in comparison with them. ... this was the case most especially with Apollinarius, for he was skilled also in Hebrew."
- An editor's view of the friendship: ATH 7240-7242 (Robertson, editor): "Apollinarius, bishop of the Syrian Laodicea, and a former close friend of Athanasius, whose legates in 362 had joined in condemning the Arian Christology".

**E4. Julian's law, and Scripture recast in classical forms. The two historians differ on who wrote what.**
- Socrates, SOC-ENG 11229-11246 (III.16): "The imperial law which forbade Christians to study Greek literature, rendered the two Apollinares ... much more distinguished than before. ... the former, as a grammarian, composed a grammar consistent with the Christian faith: he also translated the Books of Moses into heroic verse; and paraphrased all the historical books of the Old Testament, putting them partly into dactylic measure, and partly reducing them to the form of dramatic tragedy. ... The younger Apollinaris, who was well trained in eloquence, expounded the gospels and apostolic doctrines in the way of dialogue, as Plato among the Greeks had done."
- Socrates's verdict, SOC-ENG 11251-11254: "the law became wholly inoperative; and the works of these men are now of no greater importance, than if they had never been written."
- Sozomen, SOZ-ENG 36678-36707 (V.18): Julian "forbade the children of Christians from frequenting the public schools, and from being instructed in the writings of the Greek poets and authors. He entertained great resentment against Apolinarius the Syrian, a man of manifold knowledge and philological attainments ... Apolinarius, therefore, employed his great learning and ingenuity in the production of a heroic epic on the antiquities of the Hebrews to the reign of Saul, as a substitute for the poem of Homer. He divided this work into twenty-four parts ... He also wrote comedies in imitation of Menander, tragedies resembling those of Euripides, and odes on the model of Pindar. ... Were it not for the extreme partiality with which the productions of antiquity are regarded, I doubt not but that the writings of Apolinarius would be held in as much estimation as those of the ancients."
- Sozomen also names a work "The Truth" against the emperor and the philosophers, SOZ-ENG 36720-36726.
- Year: LIETZ 497-499 places the writings "nach antiken mustern" in 362 (editor).
Confidence: Julian's ban and their response, Documented (both historians). Which of them wrote what, Contested (Socrates gives the verse to the father; Sozomen gives the epic, plays and odes to Apollinaris). Editor Hartranft's note, SOZ-ENG 36720-36721, sets apart Apollinaris of Hierapolis, a different and earlier man.

**E5. What he taught about Christ: the divine Word in the place of a human mind.**
- His own words, fragment 81 of the Demonstration, as Gregory of Nyssa quotes it. LIETZ 11480-11481 (own words, kept by an opponent; clean Greek). Documented.
> "81 καὶ εἰ ἀνθρώπῳ (φησὶ) συνήφθη ὁ θεός, τέλειος τελείῳ, δύο ἂν ἦσαν, εἷς μὲν φύσει υἱὸς θεοῦ εἷς δὲ θετός."
  Confirmed in a second held print, Migne's Gregory of Nyssa, NYSSA-PG45 88005-88007 (OCR): "Καὶ εἰ ἀνυιὠώπῳ [ἀνθρώπῳ], φησὶ, συγήφθη [συνήφθη] ὁ Θεὸς, τέλειος τε- / “είῳ [τελείῳ], δύο ἂν ἦσαν, εἷς μὲν φύσει Υἱὸς Θεοῦ, εἷς / δὲ θετός." The word φησί ("he says") is Gregory's, not Apollinaris's. Justinian's tractate quotes the same thought from "the discourse on the divine incarnation", LIETZ 11489-11492.
  Book title and the fragments' ascription: Lietzmann's reconstruction (editor); the card's note that the main work was "Demonstration of the Divine Incarnation" rests on him.
- Socrates, SOC-ENG 9957-9961: "at first they asserted that in the economy of the incarnation, God the Word assumed a human body without a soul. Afterwards, as if changing mind, they retracted, admitting that he took a soul indeed, but that it was an irrational one, God the Word himself being in the place of a mind." Documented as Socrates's report; the two stages are Contested.
- Gregory of Nazianzus to Nectarius, about 383 (contemporary opponent): GNAZ 44513 "the boldness of the Apollinarians"; Sozomen's copy SOZ-ENG 39256-39262: "the man who came down from heaven was destitute of intellect (νοῦς), but that the Deity of the only begotten Son fulfilled the nature of intellect ... but the Word of God filled the place of intellect."
- Theodoret, THDT 15446-15457 (V.3; later, about 70 years; hostile): "Besides these Apollinarius of Laodicea constituted himself leader of a third party, and though he assumed a mask of piety, and appeared to defend apostolic doctrines, he was soon seen to be an open foe. ... affirmed that the reasonable soul, which is entrusted with the guidance of the body, was deprived of the salvation effected. For according to his argument God the Word did not assume this soul". And THDT 15470-15473 on his wavering about the flesh: "At one time even he was ready to confess that of the Holy Virgin the flesh had been taken, at another time he represented it to have come down from heaven with God the Word".
- Editor's caution on Gregory's account: GNAZ 44443-44448 (Browne and Swallow, editors, citing Ullman): Gregory "perhaps unconsciously, exaggerated the heretical character of its contents, pushing its statements to consequences which Apollinarius would have repudiated." Editor's view.
- Editor Lietzmann's summary of his aim: LIETZ 915-935 (two theories in turn; the second "lässt den logos nur die stelle des νοῦς ... einnehmen"). Editor.
- Rufinus, RUF 30635-30646 (Church History 11.20, about 402; contemporary, hostile): first no soul, then a soul without its reasoning part, 'ad supplementum vero rationabilis partis ipsum verbum dei fuisse perhibebat'. Documented. Socrates's two stages follow Rufinus.
Confidence for the core teaching (the Word in the place of the mind): Widely Accepted (his own fragment, Socrates, Gregory, Theodoret).

**E6. Gregory of Nazianzus's answer (sibling's text: a clause here, credited to Gregory).**
GNAZ 44688-44691 (Letter 101 to Cledonius; contemporary): "If anyone has put his trust in Him as a Man without a human mind, he is really bereft of mind, and quite unworthy of salvation. For that which He has not assumed He has not healed; but that which is united to His Godhead is also saved." Documented. Not quoted here; Gregory's story tells it.

**E7. Epiphanius of Salamis, his old admirer, at first would not believe it.**
EPIPH-PG42 48853-48857 and 48959-48966 (Panarion 77.2, written about 376 by Lietzmann's date; contemporary). Damaged scan: OCR Greek with interleaved garbled Latin. Documented, with the scan caveat.
> "Β΄. Ὁ πρεσδύτης [πρεσβύτης] καὶ σεμνοπρεπὴς, ὁ ἀεὶ ἡμῖν ἀγα- ... κητὸς [ἀγαπητὸς], χαὶ [καὶ] τῷ μαχαρίτῃ [μακαρίτῃ] πάπᾳ ᾿Αθανασίῳ, καὶ πᾶ- σιν ὀρθοδόξοις, ᾿Απολλινάριος ὁ ἀπὸ Λαοδιχείας [Λαοδικείας]" ... "Καὶ τὰ πρῶτα μὲν ἐνηχούμενοι ἀπό τινων τῶν ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ μεμαθητευμένων, ἠπιστοῦμεν, εἰ ἄρα ὁ ἀνὴρ τοιοῦτος τυγχάνων τοῦτο παρεισήγαγε τῷ βίῳ τὸ ῥῆμα"
(The aged and venerable Apollinaris of Laodicea, always loved by us, by the blessed Pope Athanasius and by all the orthodox ... At first, hearing it from some of his pupils, we did not believe that such a man had brought this teaching into the world.)
Other held prints confirming: LIETZ 3498-3499 quotes "Epiphanius 77, 2 πρεσβύτης καὶ σεμνοπρεπής" (confirms the first words only); editor's summary at VINC 12906-12910: "Epiphanius (Hær. lxxvii. 2), referring to his fall into heresy, says that when it first began to be spoken of, people would hardly credit it". No held print confirms the full Greek; not quoted, told in the narrator's words, credited to Epiphanius.
Epiphanius also says he suffered exile for refusing the Arians: LIETZ 900-903 (editor, citing Epiphanius 77.24): "die verbannung, die Apollinaris nach dem zeugnis des Epiphanius 77, 24 erlitt »weil er nicht mit den Arianern zusammengehn wollte«". Not checked in the Greek; Leave out.

**E8. Jerome heard him lecture at Antioch.**
JER-L 18996-19000 (Jerome, Letter 84; contemporary pupil, writing about 400). Documented.
> "At Antioch I frequently listened to Apollinaris of Laodicea, and attended his lectures; yet, although he instructed me in the holy scriptures, I never embraced his disputable doctrine as to their meaning."
JER-L 19013-19016: "The doctrines of Apollinaris and of Didymus are mutually contradictory. The squadrons of the two leaders must drag me in different directions, for I acknowledge both as my masters."
JER-L 18976-18979: "Apollinaris is the author of a most weighty book against Porphyry ... yet of these the former has mutilated Christ’s incarnate humanity". Date of the lectures, 374: LIETZ 3460-3464 (editor).

**E9. A church of his own: Vitalius at Antioch, separate churches, his songs.**
- Theodoret, THDT 15536-15540 (V.4): "Apollinarius after thus failing to get the government of the churches, continued, for the future, openly to preach his new fangled doctrine, and constituted himself leader of the heresy. He resided for the most part at Laodicea; but at Antioch he had already ordained Vitalius". Documented.
- Sozomen, SOZ-ENG 39005-39030 (VI.25): "He induced many persons to secede from the Church, and formed separate assemblies. Vitalius, a presbyter of Antioch ... joined Apolinarius and presided over those at Antioch who had embraced the same opinions; by the sanctity of his life he attracted a great number of followers, who are still called Vitalians by the citizens of Antioch. ... From that period the members of this sect have formed separate churches in various cities, under their own bishops, and have established laws differing from those of the Catholic Church. Besides the customary sacred order, they sang some metrical songs composed by Apolinarius; for, in addition to his other learning he was a poet, and skilled in a great variety of meters, and by their sweetness he induced many to cleave to him. Men sang his strains at convivial meetings and at their daily labor, and women sang them while engaged at the loom."
  The Greek, SOZ-GRC 13389-13392 and 13406-13414 (damaged scan: accent noise between lines; the words legible): "λεσι χωρὶς ἐκκλησίαζον ὑπὸ ἐπισκόποις ἰδίοις" ... "Ανδρες [Ἄνδρες] τε παρὰ τοὺς πότους καὶ ἐν ἐργοις [ἔργοις], καὶ γυ- ... ναῖκες παρὰ τοὺς ἱστοὺς τὰ αὐτοῦ μελη [μέλη] εἐψαλλον [ἔψαλλον]." Confirmed by the held English, SOZ-ENG 39027-39028. Songs: Documented, Sozomen alone.
- Sozomen gives a motive for Vitalius with "It is said" (SOZ-ENG 39015-39020); Gregory of Nazianzus's editors tell of an Apollinarian bishop set up at Nazianzus while Gregory was away (GNAZ 44469-44473, editor's introduction).
- Basil, about 377, BAS 43311-43314 (Letter 263, to the Westerns; contemporary opponent): "Next comes Apollinarius, who is no less a cause of sorrow to the Churches. With his facility of writing, and a tongue ready to argue on any subject, he has filled the world with his works". BAS 43445-43447 (Letter 265): "He has all the more distressed me from the fact that he seemed at the beginning on our side."

**E10. Condemned at Rome.**
- Sozomen, SOZ-ENG 39030-39039: "Damasus, bishop of Rome, and Peter, bishop of Alexandria, were the first to learn that the heresy was creeping among the people, and at a council held at Rome they voted it to be foreign to the Catholic Church." Documented.
- Damasus's letter, kept by Theodoret, THDT 16094-16096 and 16117-16120 (V.10; contemporary document): "Know then that we have condemned Timotheus, the unhallowed, the disciple of Apollinarius the heretic" ... "Here, by the judgment of the apostolic see, in the presence of Peter, bishop of Alexandria, he was condemned, together with his teacher, Apollinarius". Documented.
- Date: Hartranft's note SOZ-ENG 39033-39035 (editor): "Held a.d. 377 (Rade), 374 (Hefele)." LIETZ 560: "377 ... mitte: Apollinaris und Timotheus in gegenwart des Petrus v. Alex. in Rom verdammt" (editor). Contested (377 or 374); Lietzmann's 377 is the reconstruction the card uses.
- Rufinus, RUF 30646-30653: condemned first at Rome by Damasus and Peter of Alexandria in council, confirmed at Alexandria and at Constantinople; from then the Apollinarians kept their own bishops, teaching and churches. Documented.

**E11. Antioch, 381: an imperial officer, Flavian's challenge.** THDT 15485-15506 (V.3). The officer Sapor came with the emperor's edict; Apollinaris claimed to side with Damasus; Flavian challenged him: "you deprive our intelligence of its salvation". Date 381: LIETZ 3474-3481 (editor). Documented (Theodoret). Leave out (length).

**E12. Condemned at Constantinople in 381.**
COUNC 13970-13976 (canon 1 of 381): "every heresy shall be anathematized, particularly that of the Eunomians ... and that of the Photinians, and that of the Apollinarians." Documented. The canon's words are barred as a quote (slate); tell it in the narrator's words.
The bishops' letter of 382, THDT 16060-16063 (V.9): they "openly condemned the innovation of Apollinarius in the phrase, 'And we preserve the doctrine of the incarnation of the Lord, holding the tradition that the dispensation of the flesh is neither soulless, nor mindless, nor imperfect.'" Documented. A council's text: not quoted.
- Rufinus, RUF 30646-30653: condemned first at Rome by Damasus and Peter of Alexandria in council, confirmed at Alexandria and at Constantinople; from then the Apollinarians kept their own bishops, teaching and churches. Documented.

**E13. Imperial laws against his followers.**
CTH 7075-7080 and 7125-7126 (Theodosian Code XVI.5.12-14 and 33; contemporary laws). Documented.
- XVI.5.12, dated "(383 dec. 3)": the sects "eunomiana scilicet, arriana, macedoniana, apollinariana" may not gather or build churches "neque ullas creandorum sacerdotum usurpet adque habeat ordinationes"; the houses used "fisci nostri dominio iurique subdantur".
- XVI.5.13, "(384 ian. 21)": "eunomiani, macedoniani, arriani nec non apollinariani ... pellantur" (are to be driven out of the capital).
- XVI.5.14, "(388 mart. 10)": "apollinarianos ceterosque diversarum haeresum sectatores ab omnibus locis iubemus inhiberi, a moenibus urbium ... nulla his episcoporum faciendorum praebeatur auctoritas; ipsi quoque episcopi nomine destituti appellationem dignitatis huius amittant."
- XVI.5.33, "(397 apr. [?] 1)": "doctores apollinariorum ... ex aedibus carae nobis abscedere civitatis".

**E14. Death.**
THDT 41392-41397 (Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 104; contemporary pupil): "Apollinarus, bishop of Laodicea, in Syria, the son of a presbyter, applied himself in his youth to the diligent study of grammar, and afterwards, writing innumerable volumes on the Holy Scriptures, died in the reign of the Emperor Theodosius. There are extant thirty books by him Against Porphyry, which are generally considered as among the best of his works." Documented.
LIETZ 3484-3488 (editor): "Über das todesjahr des Apollinaris wissen wir nichts näheres"; Gregory of Nyssa's book against him, written after 385, treats him as alive. LIETZ 601 chronology: "zwischen 385 und 395 Apollinaris †". Year: Inferential-Thin. Richardson's "about 390" (THDT 41392, editor).

## 3. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

**I1. His writings lived on under other men's names, and one of them gave Cyril of Alexandria the formula "one nature of God the Word made flesh".** Heaviest: it was used and fought over for two centuries.
- The text. LIETZ 12943-12949 and 12986-12988 (the confession to the emperor Jovian, 363; own words by Lietzmann's ascription; clean Greek): "ΠΡΟΣ ΙΟΒΙΑΝΟΝ ... καὶ εἶναι τὸν αὐτὸν υἱὸν θεοῦ καὶ θεὸν κατὰ πνεῦμα, υἱὸν δὲ ἀνθρώπου κατὰ σάρκα, οὐ δύο φύσεις τὸν ἕνα υἱόν, μίαν προσκυνητὴν καί μίαν ἀπροσκύνητον, ἀλλὰ μίαν φύσιν τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένην καὶ προσκυνουμένην μετὰ τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ μιᾷ προσκυνήσει".
- The manuscripts' title gives it to Athanasius: LIETZ 12958-12962: "ἔκθεσις ἀθανασίου ἐπισκόπου ἀλεξανδρείας περὶ τῆς θείας σαρκώσεως τοῦ λόγου".
- Cyril cited it as Athanasius's, 429 (Lietzmann, editor): LIETZ 4714-4719: "Cyrill ... ist der erste uns erreichbare zeuge, welcher apollinaristische schriften ahnungslos als werke älterer orthodoxer väter citiert ... Im jahre 429 beruft er sich in der denkschrift »de recta fide ad reginas« unter anderem auf die epistula ad Jovianum mit der formel ᾿Αθανασίου ἐπισκόπου ᾿Αλεξανδρείας".
- A sixth-century witness (Leontius of Byzantium, as Pusey translates him; later, gap about 180 years): PUSEY 1279-1290: "They [the party of Severus ...] put forward another passage as S. Athanasius', from his treatise on the Incarnation. It is on this wise, 'And that the Same is Son of God after the Spirit, Son of man after the flesh; not that the one Son is two natures, the one to be worshipped, the other not to be worshipped, but One Nature Incarnate of God the Word.' ... Next it is not S. Athanasius'." PUSEY 1296-1299: "But what can we say, when they put forward blessed Cyril, citing this against Theodore, as being S. Athanasius?' To this we say, that it does indeed lie in the blessed Cyril's utterings against Theodore, yet it is an old error."
- Editors differ on who wrote it: Hefele, HEF3 876-893 (editor): "this writing is not genuine, and belongs rather to Apollinaris than to S. Athanasius, as is acknowledged not only by Montfaucon ... the phrase ... was by Cyril and Dioscurus referred to Athanasius, and held as an acknowledged watchword of orthodoxy." Pusey (editor) defends Athanasius's authorship, PUSEY 1251-1277 and 1340-1345. Lietzmann gives it to Apollinaris, LIETZ 5793 (his German rendering of Justinian's argument: "erweist sich auf verschiedene weise als vollendete fälschung").
- Cyril's use of the phrase, HEF3 7747-7749: "one Christ, one Son, and one incarnate nature of God (μίαν τὴν τοῦ Θεοῦ φύσιν σεσαρκωμένην)" (Hefele's English of Cyril's letter to Eulogius). The tree's own `cyril-of-alexandria` entry tells Cyril's "one nature".
Confidence: that the confession under Athanasius's name is Apollinaris's, Dominant Modern Reconstruction (Lietzmann, Hefele, Montfaucon), contested by Pusey; that Cyril cited it as Athanasius's, Documented (Leontius; Lietzmann; Hefele).

**I2. The forgeries were found out in the sixth century.**
- Justinian's tractate against the Monophysites, 542/3 by Loofs's date (LIETZ 5718-5723, editor), quoted in LIETZ 5754-5763 (later, gap about 150 years; clean Greek). The subject "they" is Justinian's opponents of his own day, the Acephali, a party that held one nature (LIETZ 5752-5753, Lietzmann's German "die gegner"; LIETZ 5764 "Τούτων παρὰ τῶν ἀκεφάλων προφερομένων"), not Apollinaris's own pupils:
> "τὴν γὰρ ᾿Απολλιναρίου προσηγορίαν ὡς σαφῆ δυσσεβείας ἔλεγχον δολερῶς ἀπωθούμενοι τὰς τῶν ἁγίων πατέρων ἐπωνυμίας τοῖς τούτου συγγράμμασι καὶ τοῖς ἑαυτῶν πλάσμασιν ἐπιτιθέασι κλέπτοντες τοὺς ἁπλουστέρους καὶ τὴν ὁδὸν τῆς ἀληθείας διαδιδράσκοντες. προφέρουσι γὰρ δύο ἐπιστολὰς ψευδεπιγράφους, μίαν μὲν ὡς δὴ παρὰ Ἰου λίου τοῦ ἐπισκόπου Ῥιύμῆς [Ῥώμης] ... ἑτέραν δὲ ὡς δὴ παρὰ ᾿Αθανασίου τοῦ ἐν ἁγίοις πρὸς Ἰοβιανὸν τὸν βασιλέα"
  No other held print of Justinian's Greek. The OCR of the quoted clause is clean.
- The conference with the Severians at Constantinople, MANSI8 84764-84766 ("SEVERIANIS, Habita Constantinopoli anno domini DXXXI"), the bishop Hypatius speaking, MANSI8 85328-85331 (damaged facsimile): "nam & beati Julii famosam illam epistolam ... manifeste Apollinaris ostendimus fuisse scriptam ad Dionysium" (we showed plainly that the famous letter of the blessed Julius to Dionysius was written by Apollinaris). Documented, damaged scan.
- Lietzmann's summary, LIETZ 4133-4160 (editor): his doctrinal books were "derartig gründlich vertilgt" (so thoroughly destroyed) that even the work against Porphyry is lost; followers inside the great church put his works under orthodox names, "Gregorius Thaumaturgus, Julius und Felix von Rom, Athanasius", and "so vor der vernichtung bewahrt" (so saved them from destruction); they were cited "als orthodoxe zeugnisse für die einnaturenlehre" until the fraud was found out. Editor; Dominant Modern Reconstruction.

**I3. His followers carried his Christ-teaching back into the church.**
- THDT 15476-15482 (Theodoret V.3; hostile): "As time went on, when they saw their own insignificance, and beheld the splendour of the Church, all except a few were gathered into the Church’s communion. But they did not quite put away their former unsoundness, and with it infected many of the sound. This was the origin of the growth in the Church of the doctrine of the one nature of the Flesh and of the Godhead".
- THDT 18040-18046 (V.37): at Antioch Bishop Theodotus admitted "the sect of Apollinarius ... to fellowship with the rest of the sheep on the earnest request of its members to be united with the flock. Many of them however continued marked by their former unsoundness." Documented (Theodoret's view).

**I4. Remembered as the great teacher who fell: a warning.**
VINC 12904-12944 (Vincent of Lérins, Commonitorium 11, written 434; later, gap about 45 years). Documented as Vincent's view.
> "he gave rise to great burning questions and sore perplexities among his disciples, the Church’s authority drawing them one way, their Master’s influence the opposite" ... "For what could exceed his acuteness, his adroitness, his learning? How many heresies did he, in many volumes, annihilate! ... that most noble and vast work, of not less than thirty books, in which, with a great mass of arguments, he repelled the insane calumnies of Porphyry." ... "his teachings to be pronounced the Church’s trial instead of the Church’s edification."
Latin original of the Commonitorium not held.

**I5. Named again among the condemned in 553.** COUNC 23852-23856 (Constantinople 553, anathema XI): "If anyone does not anathematize Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius, Apollinaris, Nestorius, Eutyches and Origen, as well as their impious writings". Documented. A council's text: not quoted.

**I6. Reached the West.** Augustine, Confessions VII.19, npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml 11449-11455 and 11478-11482: his friend Alypius thought the church held "that there was no soul in Christ" and learned "that this was the error of the Apollinarian heretics". Documented. Leave out (side story).

**I7. A modern editor's verdict.** COUNC 14084-14087 (Schaff, quoted by Percival, editor): "Apollinaris was the first to apply the results of the Nicene controversy to Christology proper". An editor's view; card only.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

1. **Why George put him out.** Sozomen: friendship with Athanasius. Socrates: friendship with the sophist Epiphanius, or (as "some affirm") George's wavering creed. Credit each.
2. **Who wrote what under Julian.** Socrates: father's verse, son's dialogues. Sozomen: one Apollinaris wrote the epic, plays, odes. Lietzmann thinks Sozomen the more credible (card; I did not find the line in the OCR and do not rely on it).
3. **How his teaching began.** Socrates and Sozomen both trace it to resentment at being put out; Sozomen marks it "It is said" (SOZ-ENG 39039-39041; 39073-39083); Socrates states it (SOC-ENG 9947-9950). Hostile, later. Credit each; never tell as fact.
4. **What he taught about the soul and the flesh.** Socrates: first no soul, then a soul without reason. Theodoret: he spoke more than one way about the flesh. Gregory to Nectarius: flesh from eternity, Godhead suffering. Ullman (via Browne and Swallow, editors): Gregory may have pushed the pamphlet further than Apollinaris meant.
5. **Date of the Roman condemnation.** 377 (Rade; Lietzmann) or 374 (Hefele), per Hartranft's note.
6. **Bishop of Laodicea: when.** Unknown; Lietzmann, perhaps from about 360; Richardson 362.
7. **Death.** Under Theodosius (Jerome); year unknown (Lietzmann); "about 390" (Richardson, editor).
8. **His writings' worth.** Sozomen: would rank with the ancients. Socrates: now as if never written. Vincent: would have placed him among the church's chief builders.
9. **Who wrote the confession to Jovian.** Lietzmann and Hefele: Apollinaris. Pusey: possibly Athanasius. Leontius and Justinian in the sixth century: not Athanasius.
10. **Almost every account is by opponents.** His own words come down through Gregory of Nyssa and other opponents, florilegia, and works under other names (LIETZ 4133-4160).
