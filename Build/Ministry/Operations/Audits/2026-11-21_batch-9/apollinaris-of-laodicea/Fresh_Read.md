## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** a fourth-century Christian scholar and poet of Laodicea in Syria, a friend of Athanasius and defender of the creed of Nicaea.
- **What happened:** when Julian barred Christians from the classics, he recast Scripture in classical forms. He then taught that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind. Rome and Constantinople condemned him in 381, laws barred his churches, and he died under Theodosius.
- **Why it matters:** his writings survived under honoured bishops' names. One gave Cyril of Alexandria, as Athanasius's, the phrase "one nature of God the Word made flesh". A century later Justinian charged the one-nature party with still using the false names.

All three lines come clear from one hearing. The third needs the listener to hold several names at once (see findings).

**My essence, before comparing:** Apollinaris stood with Athanasius for Nicaea, then taught that the divine Word replaced a human mind in Christ. He was condemned at Rome and Constantinople, and his followers formed their own churches; his books lived on under other names. **Also told:** Julian's ban and his poems, his reason in his own words and Gregory's answer, the songs and the laws, and the forged names reaching Cyril and Justinian. This matches the approved heart.
