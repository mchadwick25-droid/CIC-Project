**Essence:** Apollinaris of Laodicea was a learned Christian teacher in Syria. He stood with Athanasius for the creed of Nicaea, then taught that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind. He was condemned at Rome and at Constantinople in 381, and his followers formed churches of their own.

**Also told:** the Scripture he rewrote in classical forms under Julian (Socrates and Sozomen disagree on who wrote what); his reasoning in his own words; Gregory's answer, in one credited clause; his churches and their songs; the laws of 383-388 against them; and his writings living on under honoured bishops' names, which gave Cyril of Alexandria "one nature of God the Word made flesh" as Athanasius's words.

**Shape:** Teacher or thinker.

**Length:** about 4 minutes, about 600 words (650 at most). About 265 words are fixed: three quotes, five glosses, nine credited witnesses, two disagreements and the laws.

**Connection:** the confession to Jovian, passed under Athanasius's name to Cyril in 429 (Lietzmann; Hefele; Pusey dissents). Justinian later charged the one-nature party with using these false names.

**Quotes:**
1. "If God was joined to a man, complete to complete, they would be two. One would be Son of God by nature, and one adopted."
2. "Men sang his songs at drinking parties and at work. Women sang them at the loom." (damaged scan; held English confirms)
3. "They cunningly push aside the name of Apollinaris, as plain proof of ungodliness. They put the names of the holy Fathers on his writings and on their own inventions."
