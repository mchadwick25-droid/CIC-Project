# Outline: Apollinaris of Laodicea (`apollinaris-of-laodicea`)

Researcher, batch 9, 2026-11-21. File and line references are to `cic/texts/` and to the short names defined at the head of `Source_Sheet.md`.

**The essence:** Apollinaris of Laodicea was a learned Christian teacher in Syria who stood with Athanasius for the creed of Nicaea, and then taught that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind. The church condemned that teaching at Rome and at Constantinople in 381, and his followers formed churches of their own.
(SOZ-ENG 39041-39046, 38725-38731; ATH 60171-60173; SOC-ENG 9957-9961; LIETZ 11480-11481; SOZ-ENG 39030-39039; COUNC 13970-13976; SOZ-ENG 39021-39023; RUF 30646-30653.)

**Also told:**
- When the emperor Julian shut Christians out of the Greek classics, he, and by Socrates's account his father too, rewrote Scripture in the classics' own forms; the two historians differ on who wrote what (SOC-ENG 11229-11246; SOZ-ENG 36678-36702).
- His reason, in his own words: a complete God joined to a complete man would make two Sons (LIETZ 11480-11481). Gregory of Nazianzus answered that what Christ did not take on, he did not heal (GNAZ 44690-44691).
- His churches sang his songs (SOZ-ENG 39023-39028; SOZ-GRC 13406-13414). Roman and imperial power came down on them: condemned at Rome and Constantinople, then barred by law from meeting, ordaining and living in the cities (CTH 7075-7080).
- His writings survived under the names of honoured bishops, and one of them gave Cyril of Alexandria, as Athanasius's, the formula "one nature of God the Word made flesh"; in the 540s Justinian charged the one-nature party of his day with still using the false names (LIETZ 12986-12988, 4714-4719, 5754-5756; PUSEY 1279-1299; HEF3 876-893).
Checked against the Library: every claim above is held. "Ally of Athanasius against the Arians" rests on Sozomen (friendship; the Spirit), Athanasius's own synodal letter (his envoys in 362) and Epiphanius; no source has him write against the Arians in a held text, so say "stood with Athanasius for Nicaea", not "wrote against the Arians".

**Setting:**
- Century: the fourth; he was put out of the church in 346 by Lietzmann's date (LIETZ 479, 806-813); Julian reigned 361-363 (LIETZ 494, 502); condemned 381 (COUNC 13970-13976); died under Theodosius, year unknown (THDT 41395; LIETZ 3484). Spoken: "in the fourth century".
- Place: Laodicea in Syria, a port city (SOC-ENG 9931); Antioch, where he lectured and where he ordained Vitalius, who led his followers there (JER-L 18997-18998; THDT 15538-15539; SOZ-ENG 39011-39013). Spoken: "Laodicea in Syria", "Antioch".
- Tradition: census `apollinarian-christianity`, spoken name "Apollinarian Christianity", informal "the followers of Apollinaris" (world-census.json). He first stood within the Nicene party (SOZ-ENG 38725-38731; ATH 60171-60173).

**Words to explain:**
- *Arians*: "the party of Arius, who held that the Son of God was created and lower than the Father." (Not glossed in a held line; this is the tree's settled usage.)
- *the creed of Nicaea*: "the creed of the council of Nicaea, which said the Son is of one being with the Father" (SOC-ENG 9963-9964 "consubstantiality").
- *the Word*: "the divine Word, the Son of God as John's Gospel names him".
- *reader*: "a junior office in the church: he read Scripture aloud" (SOZ-ENG 39067).
- *sophist*: "a paid teacher of public speaking" (in beat 1 he is already "a pagan teacher"; keep that and drop "sophist", or gloss it).
- *synod*: say "a meeting of bishops".
- *confession* (to Jovian): "a statement of faith".
- *the holy Fathers* (in Justinian's quote): "the church's honoured early bishops and teachers".
- *one nature of God the Word made flesh*: "that God the Word and his flesh are one nature, not two" (LIETZ 12949, 12986-12988).
- *Monophysite*, *anathema*, *consubstantial*, *Christology*: do not use; say "those who held one nature", "condemned", "of one being", "teaching about Christ".

**Shape:** Teacher or thinker.

**Sibling:** `gregory-of-nazianzus` (in the batch; read its approved text). It tells his letter to Cledonius: "whatever Christ did not take on, He did not heal". Here that is one clause, credited to Gregory, in the narrator's words, never quoted. Gregory's story spells the name "Apollinarius"; the slate fixes it to "Apollinaris". `damasus-of-rome` (link, in the batch) shares the Roman condemnation: here one clause, credited to Damasus's council. `epiphanius-of-salamis` (link, in the batch): here one clause on his disbelief. `gregory-of-nyssa` (in the batch) kept the fragment quoted here; his approved text does not use it.

**Openings:**
1. (Recommended.) When the emperor Julian barred Christian children from the schools of the Greek poets, a scholar at Laodicea in Syria answered with poems of his own. The historian Sozomen says he wrote an epic of Hebrew history in twenty-four books, to stand in for Homer, with plays and odes beside it. His name was Apollinaris, and he was already known as a friend of Athanasius and the creed of Nicaea. (SOZ-ENG 36678-36702; SOZ-ENG 39041-39046.) Leads straight into the essence: the same man then taught a new thing about Christ.
2. Sozomen says his followers sang his songs in their churches, and that men sang them at drinking parties and at work, and women at the loom. They were the songs of Apollinaris of Laodicea, a scholar who had fought for Nicaea and then broke with the church over Christ's mind. (SOZ-ENG 39023-39028.) Reaches ahead to the separate churches; the hook may do so, the beats may not.

**Story beats:**
1. *Born to the church at Laodicea; put out of it.* His father came from Alexandria and taught grammar; the son taught rhetoric and served as a reader (SOC-ENG 9930-9938). Bishop George put him out of the church. Sozomen says it was for his friendship with Athanasius, passing through on his way home from exile; Socrates says it was for friendship with a pagan teacher, the sophist Epiphanius, or, as some told it, over George's wavering creed (SOZ-ENG 39041-39050; SOC-ENG 9944-9952). Reason: George's party shunned Athanasius (Sozomen). Gloss *reader*. Date 346, Lietzmann's. Confidence: the expulsion Documented; its reason Contested; the date Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
2. *A defender of Nicaea.* In 362 he sent envoys to Athanasius's synod at Alexandria, which calls him "the bishop" (ATH 60171-60173). Sozomen ranks him with Athanasius and Basil as a defender of the Spirit's equal place (SOZ-ENG 38725-38731). Gloss *Arians* and *the creed of Nicaea* here. Confidence: Documented. When he became bishop is not known (LIETZ 823-835).
3. *Julian's ban.* Under Julian, father and son, Socrates says, rewrote Scripture: the father the books of Moses in epic verse, the son the Gospels as dialogues like Plato's (SOC-ENG 11236-11246). Sozomen gives the epic, comedies like Menander's, tragedies like Euripides', and odes like Pindar's to Apollinaris himself (SOZ-ENG 36693-36702). Reason: Julian thought Greek learning made Christians strong in argument (SOZ-ENG 36690-36693). If opening 1 is used, this beat holds only what the opening did not tell: Socrates's different account. Confidence: the ban and the response Documented; who wrote what Contested.
4. *The teaching.* He held that in Christ the divine Word stood in the place of a human mind (SOC-ENG 9957-9961; SOZ-ENG 39256-39262). His reason, in a book that Gregory of Nyssa quoted to refute: Quote 1 (LIETZ 11480-11481). Rufinus, who lived through those years and wrote not long after his death, and Socrates after him, say he first denied Christ a soul, then granted one without its reasoning part (RUF 30638-30646; SOC-ENG 9957-9961). Epiphanius of Salamis, who had loved him, at first would not believe the reports (EPIPH-PG42 48853-48857, 48959-48961). Gloss *the Word*. Confidence: the core Widely Accepted; the stages Contested.
5. *Teacher and church-builder.* Jerome heard him lecture at Antioch and says he learned Scripture from him but never took his teaching (JER-L 18996-19000). At Antioch he ordained the priest Vitalius, who led his followers there (THDT 15538-15539; SOZ-ENG 39006-39015). His followers formed churches in many cities under their own bishops and sang his songs: Quote 2 (SOZ-ENG 39021-39028; SOZ-GRC 13406-13414). Confidence: Documented; the songs rest on Sozomen alone.
6. *Condemned, then outlawed.* A council at Rome under Damasus, with Peter of Alexandria present, condemned him and his pupil Timothy (THDT 16094-16120; SOZ-ENG 39030-39039); Lietzmann dates it 377, others 374. In 381 the council at Constantinople named the Apollinarians among the heresies it condemned (COUNC 13970-13976). After 381, Gregory of Nazianzus answered that what Christ did not take on, he did not heal (GNAZ 44690-44691; GNAZ 23222-23235). Laws of Theodosius from 383 forbade them to meet or ordain; in 388 their bishops lost the very name of bishop and were barred from the cities (CTH 7075-7080). Confidence: Documented; the Roman date Contested.
7. *Death.* Jerome says he died in the reign of Theodosius; the year is not known (THDT 41395; LIETZ 3484). Confidence: Documented; year Inferential-Thin.

**What came of them** (about a third of the story):
1. *His books lived on under other names, and gave Cyril his formula.* Men who shared his views but stayed inside the main church put the names of honoured bishops, Athanasius among them, on his writings, says Lietzmann, and so saved them from destruction (LIETZ 4151-4157). A confession to the emperor Jovian, carried as Athanasius's, says Christ is "not two natures ... but one nature of God the Word made flesh" (LIETZ 12949, 12986-12988, 12958-12962). Cyril of Alexandria cited it as Athanasius's in 429 (LIETZ 4714-4719; PUSEY 1296-1299). In the 540s the emperor Justinian charged the one-nature party of his own day with still passing his writings off under the Fathers' names: Quote 3 (LIETZ 5718-5723, 5754-5756). Justinian's "they" are those opponents (LIETZ 5752-5753, Lietzmann's German "die gegner"; LIETZ 5764, "παρὰ τῶν ἀκεφάλων", the Acephali, a one-nature party), not Apollinaris's own pupils. Who wrote the confession is Lietzmann's and Hefele's reconstruction; Pusey thought it might be Athanasius's (HEF3 876-893; PUSEY 1251-1277). Confidence: Dominant Modern Reconstruction (authorship); Documented (Cyril's citation, Justinian's charge). What he defended: the unity of Christ (fragment 81).
2. *His followers carried the teaching back into the church.* Theodoret, hostile, says most returned to the church but kept their views, and from them grew "the doctrine of the one nature" (THDT 15476-15482). Confidence: Documented as Theodoret's view.
3. *Remembered as a warning.* Half a century later, a monk of Lérins in Gaul held him up as a great teacher whose fame made his error the church's trial: his pupils were pulled one way by the church and the other by their master (VINC 12904-12944). Confidence: Documented as Vincent's view.
4. *Named again in 553* among the condemned (COUNC 23852-23856). Card or a clause.
No held source says who keeps him today: leave out the "today" line.

**Connection to carry into the story:** A phrase passed from Apollinaris to Cyril of Alexandria under Athanasius's name. Some who held his views at heart but stayed inside the main church gave his writings the names of honoured bishops, and so, says Lietzmann, saved them from destruction (LIETZ 4151-4157). One of them, a confession to the emperor Jovian, says Christ is not two natures but "one nature of God the Word made flesh" (LIETZ 12986-12988). In 429 Cyril cited it as Athanasius's (LIETZ 4714-4719), and Leontius in the sixth century confirms that Cyril cited it so (PUSEY 1296-1299). The writer must make plain: Cyril believed he was quoting Athanasius; the words were, on the modern editors' reading, Apollinaris's own; so the man the church condemned in 381 supplied, unseen, a phrase that Cyril's side held, in Hefele's words, "as an acknowledged watchword of orthodoxy" (HEF3 889-893; an editor's view). Then Justinian's charge against the one-nature party of his day (Quote 3) shows the false names found out. Do not say Cyril held Apollinaris's teaching on the mind: Cyril denied it (HEF3 7858-7862, his letters against those who said he denied Christ "a reasonable soul"). Tell it through what people did: the followers renamed, Cyril cited, Justinian charged.

**Do not say:**
- "He was put out for befriending Athanasius" as fact. Say: Sozomen says so; Socrates gives another reason.
- "Out of resentment he invented a heresy", or that he taught it from love of argument (Rufinus's charge), as fact. Say, if told at all: Socrates and Sozomen give the resentment, Sozomen as "it is said"; Rufinus gives the love of argument. Better in Leave out.
- "Father and son rewrote the Bible" as settled. Say: Socrates gives the work to both; Sozomen to Apollinaris alone.
- "He denied that Christ was human" or "denied Christ a soul". Say: he taught that the divine Word took the place of a human mind. Rufinus and Socrates say he first denied a soul; credit them.
- "He wrote against the Arians" or "the greatest foe of Arius". Say: he stood with Athanasius for Nicaea.
- "He was bishop of Laodicea from 362" or any year. Say: he became bishop, when is not known.
- "Condemned at Rome in 377" without a qualifier. Say: by Lietzmann's date, 377; or "in the 370s".
- "Died in 390". Say: under the emperor Theodosius; the year is not known.
- "His books were burned." The held lines say his doctrinal writings were destroyed so thoroughly that they are lost (LIETZ 4134-4137); no held line says burned.
- "Cyril was an Apollinarian" or "Cyril knowingly used a forgery". Say: Cyril cited it as Athanasius's.

- "Apollinarius". The tree spells it "Apollinaris".

- 'a confession to the emperor Jovian'. Say 'a statement of faith to the emperor Jovian'.
