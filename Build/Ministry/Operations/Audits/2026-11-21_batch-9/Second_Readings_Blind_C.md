# Second Readings — Blind Reader C (B9-21 to B9-29)

All nine passages are in English in the vendored files, so each is rendered from that English. No scan damage was found in any of them.

## B9-21
Source: `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`, lines 3956-3958. The passage runs from "we subscribed the declaration of faith" to "such as was represented" (30 words).

Rendering: We signed the statement of faith. We did not sign the curses against him. This was not because we objected to the creed. It was because we did not believe the accused man was what he had been made out to be.

Note: the sentence continues past the 30 words: they were satisfied on this point from his letters and from talking with him in person.

## B9-22
Source: `cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`, lines 7500-7502. Constantine's letter to the people of Nicomedia (21 words).

Rendering: Who taught these doctrines to the innocent common people? Clearly it was Eusebius, who took part in the tyrants' cruelty.

## B9-23
Source: `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`, lines 6180-6181. A phrase in brackets describing Eusebius (6 words).

Rendering: that troublemaker who broke the public peace

Note: in context it describes Eusebius, whose former allies ordained Macedonius.

## B9-24
Source: `cic/texts/palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt`, line 457. Evagrius speaking to himself after his vision (13 words).

Rendering: "The oath may have been sworn in a trance, but I still swore it."

## B9-25
Source: `cic/texts/evagrius_praktikos_dysinger.txt`, line 197. Comes right after the list of the eight thoughts (34 words).

Rendering: We do not get to decide whether these thoughts trouble the soul. We do get to decide whether they stay, and whether they stir up passions.

## B9-26
Source: `cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`, lines 42330-42331. A phrase that softens a claim (6 words).

Rendering: or at least one of the first

Note: the full sentence says he was the first, "or at least one of the first", to name and teach the eight main sins.

## B9-27
Source: `cic/texts/npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml`, lines 27825-27826. Jerome glossing "apathy" (19 words if the dash-joined words are counted as one).

Rendering: a state where the mind is no longer stirred up and, put simply, turns into either a stone or a god.

## B9-28
Source: `cic/texts/npnf209_hilary-poitiers-john-damascus.xml`, lines 9881-9885. Hilary speaking of himself (39 words). The two Greek terms are kept as the source gives them.

Rendering: I was baptized and reborn long ago, and I have been a bishop for some time. Even so, I never heard of the Nicene Creed until I was on my way into exile. But the Gospels and the Epistles taught me what homoousion ("of the same substance") and homoiousion ("of like substance") mean.

Note: the English glosses in brackets are mine. The source gives only the Greek words.

## B9-29
Source: `cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml`, lines 11433-11434 (26 words).

Rendering: Everyone agrees that Hilary's kind efforts, and his alone, freed our part of Gaul from the guilt of heresy.
