# Outline: Cyril of Jerusalem (`cyril-of-jerusalem`)

File keys: G = `cic/texts/npnf207_cyril-jerusalem-gregory-nazianzen.xml`; SS = `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`; TJ = `cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`; EG = `cic/texts/egeria_pilgrimage-of-etheria_mcclure-feltoe1919.txt`; EB = `cic/texts/egeria_pilgrimage-of-silvia_bernard1896.txt`; JC = `cic/texts/jerome_eusebii-chronici-canones-lat_fotheringham1923.txt`; JW = `cic/texts/npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml`. - **The essence:** Cyril of Jerusalem taught the people preparing for baptism through the forty days of Lent, in the great church beside Golgotha. He went through the creed line by line, and after Easter he explained the rites they had just been through. His lectures survive because some of his hearers took them down as he spoke, an old note in one copy says. As bishop he was driven from his city three times in the quarrels over Arius's teaching, and Jerusalem's way of making new Christians became a pattern that a pilgrim from the West wrote home about.
  - Lent lectures, creed, Easter-week rites: G 4188–4192, 4361–4364, 20485–20498 (L10, L11). Documented.
  - Taken down as spoken: G 20590–20593 (L3), a later manuscript note of unknown date. Widely Accepted; Gifford rejects the note's date (G 20603).
  - Three removals: Jerome's Chronicle names him four times as bishop, with others between (JC 45497–45505); Gifford counts three exiles (G 949–957, 1086–1091, 1260–1261). Widely Accepted.
  - The pilgrim: EG 5816–5928 (I5, I6). Documented; she never names the bishop.
- **Also told:**
  - What the lectures held: the creed put into "a few lines" because not all could read, learned by heart and not on paper (G 8815–8829, L9); Golgotha and the tomb pointed to as witnesses (G 14404–14407, 15436–15442, L4); secrecy toward the unbaptized (G 5991–5996, L7); a welcome even for those who came for the wrong reasons (G 5839–5852, L5). Documented.
  - The Easter-week lectures in the tomb chapel: renouncing Satan facing west, turning east, the stripping, the way to take communion (G 20661–20673, 20713–20716, 20924–20929, 21000–21026, 22172–22190). Documented as text; their author Contested (some manuscripts name John, his successor, G 5207–5240; Gifford with Touttée holds them Cyril's, G 5252–5268).
  - The bishopric and its fights: how he became bishop is disputed (B1–B6, Contested); the quarrel with Acacius over rank, the famine sale and the deposition (X1, X2, X4, Documented as each historian tells it); his appeal to the emperor (X4); two more removals and returns (X8, X10–X12); in 381 among the leaders at Constantinople, with the doubt over his earlier views (C1–C3, Contested); the bishops' letter of 382 clearing him (B6, Documented).
  - What came of them: the lectures copied for the baptized and cited by Jerome and Theodoret (I1–I3); the same Lent-and-Easter pattern kept under his successor and described by Egeria (I4–I6).

- **Setting:**
  - Time: Lent of about 348 for the lectures, by modern reckoning (G 4134–4142, 4169–4179, L1); bishop from about 350 (G 763–768, editor) to his death about 386 (SS 15642–15646). Spoken: "around the year 348"; "the middle of the fourth century".
  - Place: Jerusalem; the great church that Constantine had built over the place held to be Christ's tomb, consecrated in 335 (G 3916–3919); the hill of Golgotha close by (G 14405, 15436). Spoken: "Jerusalem", "the great church Constantine built at the tomb".
  - Tradition: census `jerusalem-liturgical-pilgrimage-christianity`, informal name "Jerusalem and Its Pilgrims". Spoken: "the church of Jerusalem and the pilgrims who came to it".
- **Words to explain:**
  - Lent: the forty days of fasting before Easter (G 5828–5830, "forty days").
  - creed: the short statement of faith the candidates learned by heart (G 8818–8819).
  - candidates / catechumens: say "people preparing for baptism"; "catechumen" is not needed.
  - Golgotha: the hill where Jesus was crucified.
  - Arius's teaching: that the Son of God had a beginning and was less than the Father; the "Arians" are its followers. Say "those who followed Arius".
  - metropolitan: say "the senior bishop of the province, at Caesarea" (SS 34706–34709). Sozomen says only that Cyril claimed metropolitan rights; for Acacius's rank, the editor's line: "the Metropolitan Acacius" (G 727, editor).
  - the mysteries: say "the rites of baptism, anointing and communion" (G 20496–20505).
  - the Anastasis / Holy Sepulchre: say "the small chapel over the tomb itself" (G 3943–3948).
  - homoousion: do not use the Greek; say "the word for the Son being of one being with the Father" (SS 15086; G 1328–1336).

- **Openings:**
  1. A lecture on the cross, in the great church. Cyril turns his hearers to the hill outside: 'Do you see this place, Golgotha?' They shout, and he answers the shout in his next words: 'You answer with a shout of praise, as if you agree' (G 14972–14975). Lead straight into the essence, where the old note says hearers took these lectures down as he spoke (G 20590–20593). (Quote 1, first two sentences.)
  2. The jeer he warned them of: someone will ask, "Are you going down into the water? Has the city no baths?" (G 6145–6148, paraphrase only). Then the essence.
- **Story beats:**
  1. **Lent, around 348.** A priest of Jerusalem teaches those to be baptized at Easter, in the great church by Golgotha. Why him: the record does not say; his editor assumes he was a priest of some years' standing (G 737–742, editor). Leave the reason unsaid. He welcomes even a man who came to court a woman: Jesus, he says, is angling for him (G 5839–5852). He gives the creed in a few lines, since not all can read the Scriptures, to be learned by heart and not written on paper (G 8815–8829). Tell no one unbaptized what you hear (G 5991–5996). The places themselves are his witnesses: Golgotha above them, the tomb, the old garden's traces (G 14404–14407, 15436–15442, 15617–15621). Source: Cyril's own lectures. Documented; the year is Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
  2. **Easter week, in the tomb chapel.** Only after baptism does he explain the rites: Quote 2 (G 20639–20640). They had faced west, the side of darkness, stretched out a hand and renounced Satan, then turned east toward the light (G 20661–20673, 20713–20719, 20924–20929). They had stripped and stood naked and unashamed (G 20999–21026). Take communion with the left hand a throne for the right, "as for that which is to receive a King" (G 22172–22174, paraphrase). Explain: these are the rites of baptism, anointing and communion. Note in one sentence: some old copies name John, his successor, as the author of these five; his editors hold them Cyril's, since the last Lent lecture promises them (G 5207–5240, 5252–5268, 20485–20498). Documented text; author Contested.
  3. **Bishop, about 350, and how.** Two church historians of the next century, Socrates and Sozomen, say the party of Acacius, bishop of Caesarea, pushed out the old bishop and put Cyril in (SS 8973–8975; 34142–34143). Jerome, who was hostile to him, says worse: that he gave up his ordination as priest to win the see from Arius's followers (JC 45505–45517). The bishops who met in 382 wrote that he was rightly ordained by the province's bishops (TJ 16041–16043). Tell all three, credited; settle nothing. Contested.
  4. **The quarrel and the first removal, about 357.** Acacius was the senior bishop of the province; Cyril claimed rank for Jerusalem as an apostle's see (SS 34706–34709). In a famine, Sozomen says, the poor came to him and he sold the church's veil and sacred ornaments to feed them; it is said a man later saw his own gift on an actress; Acacius used this as a pretext to depose him (SS 34717–34727). Socrates says Cyril had not appeared for two whole years, though summoned again and again, 'owing to fear'. Once deposed, he gave notice that he would appeal to a higher court, and the emperor Constantius allowed the appeal (SS 9432–9443). He was the first churchman, Socrates says, to take such a case to appeal like a lawsuit (SS 9440–9443). He went to Tarsus, where the people enjoyed his teaching (TJ 10710–10715, credit "another historian" by role). Date: Gifford's (G 949–957). Documented as each tells it.
  5. **Two more removals.** At the council of Seleucia in 359 he would not withdraw when Acacius demanded it, and Acacius walked out (TJ 10718–10724; credit "another historian" by role). In 360 a synod at Constantinople deposed him again (SS 9653–9657). The emperor Julian recalled the exiles (SS 10191–10194). Under Valens he was driven out a third time, probably about 367 by modern reckoning (G 1260–1271; SS 36279). By 379, Sozomen says, Jerusalem's was the only church in the East not in Arian hands (SS 40491–40492). Widely Accepted for three removals; dates Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
  6. **381 and 382.** At the great council at Constantinople in 381 he sat among the leaders (SS 15084–15092; 40790–40795). Socrates says he had by then accepted the word for the Son being of one being with the Father, giving up his former view; his modern editors reply that the lectures taught this from the start, without the word (SS 15086–15092; G 1332–1338). The next year the bishops wrote to Rome that he was bishop, rightly ordained, and had fought a good fight against the Arians in several places (TJ 16041–16044). He died about 386 (SS 15642–15646). Contested (his earlier views); Documented (the letter).
- **What came of them** (about a third of the story):
  1. **The lectures were kept and read** (heaviest). A note at the end of the first lecture lets them be lent to the baptized and to those preparing for baptism, but never given to outsiders (G 6190–6196); whether Cyril or a later copyist wrote it is not known (G 6178–6180). Jerome listed them about six years after his death (TJ 41505–41507). Theodoret of Cyrrhus, some sixty years after Cyril's death, quoted the fourth lecture as a testimony in his argument about Christ (TJ 24428–24445). Documented. (Translations and later disputes go to the card: I9–I11.)
  2. **The pattern held, and a pilgrim wrote it down.** Under his successor John the custom went on. Jerome, writing against John about 397, says the custom 'among us' is forty days of public teaching for those to be baptized, and speaks of what John delivered to the candidates through Lent (JW 41070–41080; Latin, PL 23 file lines 28292–28296). Contemporary, hostile to John. Documented. Egeria, a pilgrim from the West, described for the women at home the bishop's chair in the great church, the candidates with their fathers and mothers, the Scriptures taught from Genesis, the creed after five weeks, and in Easter week the bishop in the tomb chapel with the doors shut (EG 5816–5858, 5908–5923). Quote 3 (EG 5923–5926). She never names the bishop, and her editors date her visit to the end of the fourth century, under Theodosius (379–395); one scholar argued for the sixth (EG 160–167, 393–402), so it may have been Cyril or John. Documented; the bishop's identity not given.
  3. **His name cleared** by the bishops' letter of 382 (in beat 6). Defended: Jerusalem's rank as an apostle's see, against Acacius (SS 34706–34709); and, the bishops of 382 say, the faith against the Arians (TJ 16043–16044).

- **Connection to carry into the story:** to the tradition of Jerusalem and Its Pilgrims. What passed: a way of making Christians tied to the holy places, forty days of teaching in the great church through Scripture and the creed, then, after baptism at Easter, a week of teaching in the tomb chapel on what the rites had meant (G 4188–4192, 20485–20498; Cyril's own course). Jerome, writing against his successor John, says forty days of public teaching was the custom among them, and that John gave it through Lent (JW 41070–41080); Egeria, a stranger from the West, saw the same order and wrote it down (EG 5816–5928). The listener must understand that the lectures and Egeria's letter are two views of one local custom, the first from the teacher inside it and the second from a visitor; Egeria does not say whose teaching she heard. The lectures also passed to later readers as an authority: Theodoret quoted one as testimony (TJ 24428–24445).
- **Ending:**
  1. The opening image again: the same lecture, the shout at Golgotha, and the line that followed, "See that you do not take it back when persecution comes" (G 14974–14975). It closes on his own words to his hearers, without comment.
  2. His death about 386, after eight unbroken years as bishop under Theodosius, as Jerome, who was no friend, recorded: often driven out, and at last received back (TJ 41502–41505; SS 15642–15646). Paraphrase only. If this ending is used, take "He died about 386" out of beat 6, so the death is not told twice.

- **Do not say:**
  - "Cyril wrote his lectures" → "his hearers took them down as he spoke, an old note in one copy says".
  - 'The shout stays in the text because they took him down as he spoke' → the shout is in the text because Cyril answers it; the note says only that the lectures were taken down when spoken.
  - 'John wrote of our custom' → 'Jerome, writing against John, says it was the custom among them'
  - "In 348" as plain fact → "around 348", or "in about 348, by modern reckoning".
  - "Egeria heard Cyril" or "Egeria described Cyril's teaching" → "Egeria described the bishop's teaching; she never names him, and it may have been Cyril or his successor".
  - Egeria's applause during the Lent lectures → the applause she describes is in Easter week, in the tomb chapel (EG 5908–5926). (Egeria does also say the faithful cheer louder at the Lent teaching than at sermons, EG 5858–5863; keep the two apart.)
  - "The Arians made him bishop" → "Socrates and Sozomen say the party of Acacius put him in"; with Jerome's charge as Jerome's, and the 382 letter beside them.
  - "He sold church treasures to an actress" → "he sold the church's veil and ornaments to feed the poor in a famine, Sozomen says; it is said a man later saw his own gift worn by an actress".
  - "Acacius deposed him for the sale" → "Acacius used the sale as a pretext, Sozomen says"; Socrates says he does not know the charges.
  - "He fled out of fear" as fact → "Socrates says he stayed away owing to fear".
  - "He refused to answer the summons" → "Socrates says he had not appeared for two whole years, though summoned again and again" (SS 9434–9437).
  - "He appealed to the emperor" → "he gave notice that he would appeal to a higher court, and the emperor Constantius allowed the appeal" (SS 9437–9440).
  - "He was the first bishop ever to appeal to the emperor" as fact → "Socrates says he was the first and only churchman to appeal in this way".
  - "Three exiles in 357, 360 and 367" as fact → "three times"; dates "by modern reckoning", and "about".
  - "The council of 381 cleared him" → "the bishops who met the next year, in 382, wrote that ...".
  - "He presided over the council of 381" → "he was among its leaders".
  - "He always held the Nicene faith" or "he changed his mind" as fact → give Socrates's claim and the editors' reply, each credited.
  - "The rites of the Easter week lectures are certainly his" → "some old copies name his successor, John; his editors hold them Cyril's".
  - "He was born in Jerusalem" / "he saw the tomb uncovered as a boy" → leave out (Gifford's inference, G 591–624).
  - "Constantine finished the church" → "Constantine built it"; Cyril says the present emperors had finished adorning it (G 4134–4140). Keep to "built".
  - "the only course of its kind" → leave out, or "the fullest such course to survive, in the judgment of his English translator".
  - 'Christ's tomb' in the narrator's voice → 'the tomb', or 'the place held to be Christ's tomb' (G 3916–3919); Cyril's own words may say more, credited to him.
  - Do not add the crowd's feeling, the lecture hall's look, or any detail of his person; none is in the record.
