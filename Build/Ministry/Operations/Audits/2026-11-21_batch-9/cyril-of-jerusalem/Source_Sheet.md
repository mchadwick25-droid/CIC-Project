# Source sheet: Cyril of Jerusalem (`cyril-of-jerusalem`)

Line numbers are file lines. "G" = `cic/texts/npnf207_cyril-jerusalem-gregory-nazianzen.xml` (Gifford's revised English of the Oxford translation, 1894, with his introduction). "SS" = `cic/texts/npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`. "TJ" = `cic/texts/npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`. "EG" = `cic/texts/egeria_pilgrimage-of-etheria_mcclure-feltoe1919.txt`. "EB" = `cic/texts/egeria_pilgrimage-of-silvia_bernard1896.txt`. Where G's XML wraps a footnote inside a sentence, the verbatim text below leaves the note out and keeps every word of the sentence.

**Original language.** No Greek text of Cyril is held. Every quote of Cyril below is Gifford's English. No Latin text of Egeria is held (the corpus map says so too); two independent English translations are held (EG, EB). Jerome's Chronicle (Latin) and Rufinus (Latin) are held; Socrates (Greek, Hussey), Sozomen books 5 to 9 (Greek, Hussey) and Epiphanius, Panarion 73 (Greek, Migne) are held. Sozomen book 4 is not held in Greek. Cyril's Letter to Constantius is not held.

Witness kinds: OWN = Cyril's own words; CONT = contemporary; LATER (+gap); EDITOR = a modern editor's view only.

## 4. Links to others on the tree

- **Constantine the Great** (`constantine-the-great`): built the church where Cyril taught (G 3916–3919); Cyril says the present emperors finished it (G 4134–4140, Cat. XIV.14). Kind: taught in the church he built.
- **First Council of Constantinople** (`first-council-of-constantinople-381`): Cyril attended, among the leaders (SS 15084–15092; 40790–40795). Kind: attended.
- **Socrates of Constantinople**, **Sozomen**, **Theodoret of Cyrrhus**, **Rufinus of Aquileia**, **Jerome**: wrote of his life (rows above). Theodoret also quoted his book (I3).
- **Egeria** (named in the Jerusalem tradition entry, not a person entry): described Jerusalem's rites; never names the bishop.
- **Athanasius of Alexandria** (`athanasius-of-alexandria`): Gifford says Athanasius passed through Jerusalem in 346 (G 711–735); Cyril never names him (G 729–730). Not used.
- **Basil of Caesarea**, **Epiphanius of Salamis** (both in this batch): Basil visited Jerusalem about 357 (G 872–877, editor); Epiphanius tells of the Cyril–Eutychius feud (X13). Not used in the story, so no shared quote.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **How he became bishop.** Socrates and Sozomen: Acacius's party ejected Maximus and installed Cyril (B1, B2). Theodoret: he followed Maximus at his death (B3). Jerome, hostile: he bought the see from the Arians by repudiating Maximus's ordination (E1). Rufinus: an ordination "already confused" (B4). The 382 synod: canonically ordained by the province's bishops (B6). Gifford: the accounts are irreconcilable (B5).
- **Why he was deposed.** Sozomen: a pretext over the famine sale (X2). Socrates: he does not know the charges, and says Cyril stayed away "owing to fear" (X4). Theodoret: "some small occasion" (X5); later Acacius's robe tale (X7). Gifford's view that the real cause was Acacius's hatred is the editor's (G 1143–1146).
- **His doctrine.** Socrates says he "retracted his former opinion" in 381; Sozomen says he once held with the Macedonians (C1, C2). Rufinus says he wavered in faith (B4). Gifford and Touttée reject this (C3). Sozomen IV.25 says Cyril was suspected of "siding with those who maintain that the Son is in substance like unto the Father" (SS 34711–34714).
- **The Easter-week lectures' author.** Some manuscripts give them to John, his successor (G 5207–5240); Gifford, with Touttée, holds them Cyril's from the cross-references (G 5252–5268).
- **Exile dates and successors.** Dates are Gifford's reckoning; successors' names differ (X12).
- **Egeria's bishop and date.** She never names him; her date is reconstructed (I5).
- **The Temple prophecy.** Socrates and Rufinus vs. Gifford (X16).
