# Review: Cyril of Jerusalem (`cyril-of-jerusalem`)

Reviewed: `cyril-of-jerusalem.brief.txt`, the first draft. Read against Kit Parts 1 to 4, `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md`, `Outline_Check.md`, the batch header, and the texts in `cic/texts/`.

## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Cyril, bishop of Jerusalem in the middle of the fourth century. He taught the people preparing for baptism at Easter, in the great church beside Golgotha and the tomb.
2. **What happened:** his Lent and Easter-week lectures survive because hearers took them down. His election was disputed. He was driven out three times in the quarrels over Arius's teaching. He sat among the leaders at Constantinople in 381 and died about 386.
3. **Why it matters:** his way of teaching new Christians at the holy places is the custom a Western pilgrim, Egeria, saw and described. Later readers kept his lectures and quoted them.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

**My essence, before comparing:** a bishop who taught baptism at the holy places, whose spoken lectures were kept and read; driven out three times in the Arian quarrels; his church's way of making Christians became the pattern a pilgrim wrote down. It matches the approved heart.

- **Hook to essence.** The hook is the Golgotha shout, in two sentences, with the name in the first. It leads straight into the essence. The essence is told by word 168 by plain count. Its core (the Lent teaching, the rites after Easter, taken down as spoken, three removals) is in by word 150. That is within "about 150". Passes.
- **Order.** Told once, in order: 348, Easter week, about 350 (with the 382 letter reaching ahead as part of the three-voice dispute, as the outline places it), about 357, 360 and 367, 381, death, then what came. The ending returns to the opening image, which the kit allows. No event is told twice. Passes.
- **Ending.** On Cyril's own words to his hearers. Passes.
- **Names.** Cyril, Constantine, Arius, Socrates, Sozomen, Acacius, Jerome, John, Valens, Theodoret and Egeria make eleven. Each acts, links or is credited, except as noted in MINOR 2.
- **Where I got lost.** Paragraph 6 only. Sozomen's reason for the deposition and Socrates's reason come as two flat statements, so they do not sound like two different accounts. See BLOCKING 2.
- **Children's book or talking down.** Nowhere.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** The register is a documentary narrator's. Cyril's own words carry the opening, the rites and the close, and the shout gives the story a live room. Paragraph 6 drops into a run of one-act sentences.
- **Craft: 3.** The hook, the order and the ending are strong, and the election is told well as three voices side by side. But several parts of the approved heart were dropped that are not on the cut-first list (BLOCKING 1), and the deposition paragraph does not tell two witnesses' accounts as two.
- **Rigor: 3.** All three quotes are verbatim, and the credits are mostly right, including the "may have been Cyril or John" caution and the disputed authorship. But one motive is told in the narrator's voice (BLOCKING 2). Without the Jerome witness, "The pattern held too" stands on nothing in the story (part of BLOCKING 1).
- **Accessibility: 4.** Every gloss on the outline's list is given its plain sense where it is first heard (Lent, creed, Golgotha, Arius's teaching, the senior bishop, the rites, the tomb chapel, the 381 word). No sentence runs past 27 words. The grade is low only because of paragraph 6.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Parts of the approved heart dropped that are not on the cut-first list.** All five cut-first items are out (the old garden, the fathers and mothers, Tarsus, Sozomen on 379, Socrates's "first churchman"). Beyond that list, these "Also told" items (Hearts.md, as the outline held them on 2026-11-21) are missing:

- (a) **What the lectures held: the welcome for those who came for the wrong reasons** (beat 1; G 5839–5852). Restore in paragraph 3, as its first sentence: "He welcomed even a man who had come only to court a woman: Jesus, he said, was angling for him." (19 words.)
- (b) **The Easter-week rites: the stripping and the way to take communion** (beat 2; G 20999–21026, 22172–22174). Restore in paragraph 4, after "turned east toward the light": "They had stripped and stood naked, unashamed, and he taught them to make the left hand a throne for the right at communion." (23 words.)
- (c) **The returns between the removals** ("two more removals and returns"; beat 5; SS 10191–10194). The story never says he came back. Paragraph 7 becomes: "Two more removals followed. In 360 a synod at Constantinople deposed him again. The emperor Julian recalled the exiles, but under Valens he was driven out a third time, probably about 367." (+7 words.)
- (d) **What came 1, the heaviest: the lectures copied for the baptized, and Jerome's listing** (G 6190–6196, a note whose author is not known; TJ 41505–41507). Restore in paragraph 9, before Theodoret: "A note in the copies lets them be lent to the baptized and to those preparing, never to outsiders. Jerome listed them within a few years of his death." (about 29 words.)
- (e) **What came 2: the pattern kept under his successor, in Jerome's voice** (JW 41070–41081). This is the Connection's own witness to continuity, and without it "The pattern held too" is unsupported. Replace "The pattern held too." with "The pattern held too. Jerome, writing against Cyril's successor John, says forty days of public teaching was their custom." (+16 words.)

**What pays for restoring them.** No cut-first item is left in the story. These parts the story does not need, with the words each saves:
- Hook: "He takes it up in his next words:" → "He answers it:" (−5). The Do not say line on the shout still holds.
- Paragraph 2: "The lecture is from Lent, around the year 348 by modern reckoning." Fold "Around 348, by modern reckoning," into the next sentence, and split that sentence at "going through the creed" so it stays under 30 words (about −5).
- Paragraph 9: "Egeria, a pilgrim from the West, described it" → "Egeria, that pilgrim, described it", since the essence has already said "a pilgrim from the West" (−4).
- Paragraph 6: "This was about 357." folded as "Once deposed, about 357, he gave notice ..." (−2).
- Paragraph 5: "Jerome, who was hostile to him, says worse" → "Jerome, hostile to him, says worse" (−2).

That pays for about 18 words. The restorations need about 94, or about 65 if (a) is cut to "He welcomed even a man who had come only to court a woman" (13) and (d) to "A note lets them be lent to the baptized, never to outsiders, and Jerome listed them" (17). Even the lean set leaves the story about 45 words over 675. The reviser should restore (e) and (c) first, since they are cheapest and carry the connection and the life's shape. Then restore (d), (b) and (a) in that order, and tighten the narration elsewhere without touching the heart, the credits or the glosses. If the heart still cannot fit under 675, this cannot be closed by cutting more of the heart (Mark, 2026-11-21: "dropping from that list and not by their own choice"; no second round of outline cuts). It goes to Mark with the count, as a length ruling.

Do not say line to add to the outline: "Dropping an Also told item to meet the length → drop only from Cut first, in order; if the heart still runs past 675, say so and stop."

**B2. A motive told in the narrator's voice, and two witnesses' accounts of the deposition not told as two.** Paragraph 6: "Acacius used the sale as a pretext to depose him." This is Sozomen's claim (SS 34715–34727, "contrived to depose Cyril under the following pretext"), and it stands in its own sentence with no credit. The outline's Do not say gives the right wording: "Acacius used the sale as a pretext, Sozomen says"; Socrates says he does not know the charges (SS 9432–9434). The next sentence then gives Socrates's different reason, non-appearance "owing to fear" (SS 9434–9437), with nothing to mark it as a second account. This is one of the four faults: one witness's claim told as fact. Change it to: "Acacius, Sozomen says, used the sale as a pretext to depose him. Socrates does not know the charges. He says Cyril was deposed for staying away two whole years, though summoned again and again, owing to fear." (Net about +8; it also links the flat run.)

Do not say line to add to the outline: "'Acacius used the sale as a pretext' in its own sentence, even after a 'Sozomen says' sentence → credit it in the same sentence, and give Socrates's reason as Socrates's second account."
