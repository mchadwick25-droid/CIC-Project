# Outline check: Cyril of Jerusalem (`cyril-of-jerusalem`)

## My own account, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (teaser, live story), the slate's job line and the source sheet.

**Essence.** A priest and then bishop of Jerusalem who, in Lent about 348 (Gifford's date), taught those preparing for Easter baptism in Constantine's church beside Golgotha and the tomb, giving them the creed to learn by heart; his course survives because hearers took it down, and is the one complete course of its kind that survives (Gifford's judgment). He is on the tree for that teaching and for the worship of Jerusalem's holy places that grew round it, which a pilgrim from the West (Egeria) described without naming her bishop.

**Also told.** (1) What the lectures teach and how: the creed article by article, "a few lines" for those who cannot read, secrecy toward outsiders, and the Easter-week lectures on the rites in the tomb chapel (renouncing Satan facing west, turning east, stripping, the hand as a throne), whose authorship is disputed (some manuscripts give them to John). (2) How he became bishop, told as the witnesses disagree (Socrates/Sozomen: Acacius's party installed him; Theodoret: succeeded Maximus at death; Jerome, hostile: bought it; 382 synod: canonically ordained). (3) The quarrel with Acacius over the see's rank, the famine sale and its use as pretext, and three removals (Gifford's dating), with his appeal to the emperor; restored under Julian, removed again under Valens. (4) 381: attended Constantinople, said by Socrates and Sozomen to have changed his view, which Gifford rejects; cleared by the 382 letter; died about 386. (5) Impact: the lectures copied and kept for the baptized, listed by Jerome, quoted by Theodoret; the forty-day custom continued under John; Egeria's account of Jerusalem's Lent and Easter teaching.
