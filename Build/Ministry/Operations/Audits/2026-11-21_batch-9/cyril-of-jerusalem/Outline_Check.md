# Outline check: Cyril of Jerusalem (`cyril-of-jerusalem`)

## My own account, written before reading the outline

From the census entry (teaser, live story), the slate's job line and the source sheet.

**Essence.** A priest and then bishop of Jerusalem who, in Lent about 348 (Gifford's date), taught those preparing for Easter baptism in Constantine's church beside Golgotha and the tomb, giving them the creed to learn by heart; his course survives because hearers took it down, and is the one complete course of its kind that survives (Gifford's judgment). He is on the tree for that teaching and for the worship of Jerusalem's holy places that grew round it, which a pilgrim from the West (Egeria) described without naming her bishop.

**Also told.** (1) What the lectures teach and how: the creed article by article, "a few lines" for those who cannot read, secrecy toward outsiders, and the Easter-week lectures on the rites in the tomb chapel (renouncing Satan facing west, turning east, stripping, the hand as a throne), whose authorship is disputed (some manuscripts give them to John). (2) How he became bishop, told as the witnesses disagree (Socrates/Sozomen: Acacius's party installed him; Theodoret: succeeded Maximus at death; Jerome, hostile: bought it; 382 synod: canonically ordained). (3) The quarrel with Acacius over the see's rank, the famine sale and its use as pretext, and three removals (Gifford's dating), with his appeal to the emperor; restored under Julian, removed again under Valens. (4) 381: attended Constantinople, said by Socrates and Sozomen to have changed his view, which Gifford rejects; cleared by the 382 letter; died about 386. (5) Impact: the lectures copied and kept for the baptized, listed by Jerome, quoted by Theodoret; the forty-day custom continued under John; Egeria's account of Jerusalem's Lent and Easter teaching.

## BLOCKING

**B1. Opening 1 states a cause the sources do not give.** "His hearers shout, and the shout stays in the text, because they were taking him down as he spoke (G 14972–14975; G 20590–20593)." The shout is in the text because Cyril himself answers it ("Thou answerest with a shout of praise", G 14974). That would stand in any copy of his words, written out or taken down. The Munich note (G 20590–20593) says the lectures were taken down when spoken. It says nothing about the shout. Joining the two with "because" is the narrator's inference, and the Kit bars a "because" the sources lack.
Correction: replace Opening 1 with: "A lecture on the cross, in the great church. Cyril turns his hearers to the hill outside: 'Do you see this place, Golgotha?' They shout, and he answers the shout in his next words: 'You answer with a shout of praise, as if you agree' (G 14972–14975). Lead straight into the essence, where the old note says hearers took these lectures down as he spoke (G 20590–20593)." Add to **Do not say**: "'The shout stays in the text because they took him down as he spoke' → the shout is in the text because Cyril answers it; the note says only that the lectures were taken down when spoken."

**B2. "Our custom" is Jerome's statement, not John's words quoted back.** What came 2 says: "His successor John wrote of 'our custom', as Jerome quotes him back." The Connection says: "His successor John kept 'our custom' of forty days." In the held English (JW 41070–41073) the sentence has no quotation marks, though the same paragraph does mark John's own words ("stir you up", JW 41077). The held Latin (`cic/texts/jerome_opera-pl23-part1-with-regula-pachomii-lat_migne1883.txt` 28292–28296) reads "Consuetudo autem apud nos istiusmodi est, ut his qui baptizandi sunt, per quadraginta dies, publice tradamus sanctam et adorandam Trinitatem", so Jerome is describing the custom "among us" in his own voice. What carries John's practice is Jerome's next lines: what John "was accustomed to deliver in church to the candidates for baptism during the forty days of Lent" (JW 41079–41081). As written, this gives a claim to the wrong witness.
Correction, What came 2, first sentence: "Under his successor John the custom went on. Jerome, writing against John about 397, says the custom 'among us' is forty days of public teaching for those to be baptized, and speaks of what John delivered to the candidates through Lent (JW 41070–41080; Latin, PL 23 file lines 28292–28296). Contemporary, hostile to John. Documented." Connection: replace "His successor John kept 'our custom' of forty days (JW 41070–41073)" with "Jerome, writing against his successor John, says forty days of public teaching was the custom among them, and that John gave it through Lent (JW 41070–41080)." Source sheet I4: change "(John's words, as Jerome quotes them back to him)" to "(Jerome's words, of the custom 'apud nos'; John's own Lent teaching at JW 41079–41081)". Add to **Do not say**: "'John wrote of our custom' → 'Jerome, writing against John, says it was the custom among them'".

## MINOR

1. **Q1 punctuation.** Gifford's lecture text prints "Thou seest this spot of Golgotha!" (G 14972), but his own introduction gives it twice as a question, "Seest thou this spot of Golgotha?" (G 2194, G 4011–4012), and his Greek there has the question mark (῾Ορᾶς ... τόπον;). The quote stays verbatim to the lecture text. The rendering should follow the question: "Do you see this place, Golgotha? You answer with a shout of praise, as if you agree. See that you do not take it back when persecution comes." Note in Quotes 1: "rendered as a question, per Gifford's Greek at G 4011–4012".
2. **Beat 4, Socrates's wording.** "Cyril had refused to answer the summons for two years ... and then appealed to the emperor" → "Socrates says Cyril had not appeared for two whole years, though summoned again and again, 'owing to fear'. Once deposed, he gave notice that he would appeal to a higher court, and the emperor Constantius allowed the appeal (SS 9432–9443)." "Refused" adds will, and the appeal went to "a higher jurisdiction", which the emperor sanctioned. Add both to Do not say.
3. **Beat 6, the 382 letter.** "had fought many times against the Arians" → "had fought a good fight against the Arians in several places" (TJ 16043–16044).
4. **Ending 2.** "after eight years in peace under Theodosius" → "after eight unbroken years as bishop under Theodosius". "In peace" is not in Jerome (TJ 41502–41505: "eight consecutive years"). Also, if Ending 2 is used, take "He died about 386" out of beat 6 so the death is not told twice.
5. **Egeria's date.** What came 2: "her visit is dated only roughly, to the 380s or later" → "her editors date her visit to the end of the fourth century, under Theodosius (379–395); one scholar argued for the sixth (EG 160–167, 393–402)". The point that it may have been Cyril or John stands.
6. **What they defended** is marked as "his ordination and his faith (B6)". Those are what the bishops of 382 defended for him. Correction: "Defended: Jerusalem's rank as an apostle's see, against Acacius (SS 34706–34709); and, the bishops of 382 say, the faith against the Arians (TJ 16043–16044)."
7. **Theodoret's distance.** What came 1: "some sixty years later" → "some sixty years after Cyril's death" (TJ 24428–24445, about 447).
8. **Gloss, metropolitan.** Sozomen says Cyril claimed metropolitan rights. He does not say Acacius held them. Add the editor's line for Acacius's rank: "the Metropolitan Acacius" (G 727, editor).
9. **Length line.** It says "seven beats" (there are six) and puts the total at about 523 words. Set it to the independent count: "about 680 words before cuts (Length_Count.md), about 620 after the cut-first list; writer's ceiling 675."
10. **Do not say, the tomb.** Add: "'Christ's tomb' in the narrator's voice → 'the tomb', or 'the place held to be Christ's tomb' (G 3916–3919); Cyril's own words may say more, credited to him."
