## Findings

### BLOCKING

The writer dropped every item on the outline's **Cut first** list, which is allowed. The writer also dropped these outline items, which are not on that list. B1 to B4 are part of the approved heart or of the connection. The rest are named at the end of this section.

**B1. How he knew what he wrote (Also told 1; HOLL1 13426-13430, E5).** The preface says some heresies he knew from study, some by hearsay, and some with his own ears and eyes. The story drops this. It keeps "He copied his opponents' own writings, so that no one could say he answered hearsay", which also drops the source's "alone" (HOLL1 34006-34011, "ἐξ ἀκοῆς μόνον"). Together these suggest the book never rests on hearsay. His own preface says some of it does, and the story later shows him judging the Long Brothers "by report". This is both missing heart and a misleading implication. Change paragraph 3's sentence to, for example: "Some heresies he knew from books, some by hearsay, and some with his own eyes and ears. Where he could, he copied his opponents' own writings, so that no one could say he answered from hearsay alone." (about +15 words)

**B2. Apollinaris (Also told 1; PG42 48853-48857, E7).** The story drops the clause in which he lists the teaching of Apollinaris's followers although he calls Apollinaris always dear to him. This is the outline's one picture of the remedy applied even to a friend, and the batch link to `apollinaris-of-laodicea`. Restore it in paragraph 3 as a paraphrase, not a quote, for example: "He listed even the teaching of the followers of Apollinaris, a man he says was always dear to him." (about +18 words)

**B3. The rising of this flesh (Also told 2; What came 1, marked Defended; J6 10838-10840, 10899-10901).** The story gives his charges about fallen souls and Eden, but not the charge he says matters most, that Origen denied the rising of this flesh. Without it the stake of the whole quarrel is missing, and the story never says what he defended. Add it to the charges in paragraph 4, for example: "Origen, he writes, taught that souls were angels who fell into bodies, denied that this flesh would rise again, and read the garden of Eden as hidden meanings." ### MINOR

**M1. Augustine's count (paragraph 8).** "Augustine counted twenty heresies from before Christ and sixty after" makes the count Augustine's own. Augustine reports Epiphanius's own division: "separatis viginti, quas ante Domini adventum exstitisse, sicut ei visum est, computavit" (AUG 2420-2424). Say instead: "Augustine, who reports that Epiphanius set twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after, followed his order." The outline's "by Augustine's count" left room for this.
- **Do not say** (add to the outline): "Augustine counted twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after." Say: Augustine reports that Epiphanius counted twenty before Christ and sixty after (AUG 2420-2424).

**M2. The reasons he gave (paragraph 5).** Beat 5 gives his own reasons. For the ordination, the monks had no priest, and he feared they would be cut off from the church and its ancient faith (J6 10655-10659, 10704-10705). Add one short clause each only if the length allows after B1-B4. Otherwise leave this for the card.

**M3. Setting stacked (paragraph 3).** "born near Eleutheropolis in Palestine and chosen bishop by the people of Cyprus" puts two places in one early sentence. The variety report flags this. The merge suggested under "What pays" settles it.

**M4. Longest sentence, 31 words (paragraph 4, Jerome at Jerusalem).** This is at the edge of "about 30". If it needs touching, split before "while" ("... mocked him as an old fool. Yet crowds pressed to kiss his feet."). Optional.

**Do not say line for B1** (add to the outline, since its Also told 1 dropped the "alone" as well): "He copied his opponents' writings so no one could say he answered hearsay." Say: some heresies he knew by hearsay, he says; he copied an opponent's own writing so that no one could say he answered from hearsay alone (HOLL1 13426-13430; 34006-34011).
