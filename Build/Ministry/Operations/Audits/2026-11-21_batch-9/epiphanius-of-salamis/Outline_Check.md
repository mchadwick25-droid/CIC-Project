# Outline check: Epiphanius of Salamis (`epiphanius-of-salamis`)

## My own heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry, the slate's job line, the source sheet and what he is known for.

**Essence.** A monk trained among the ascetics of Palestine and Egypt, chosen bishop of Salamis in Cyprus, who wrote the Panarion, a "medicine chest" setting a cure beside each of eighty heresies; the book became the list later writers on heresy followed (Augustine says he followed its order). In old age he took the fight against Origen's teaching to Jerusalem and then to Constantinople, where he refused to pray with John Chrysostom. He is on the tree as the church's great cataloguer of heresy and as a leading accuser of Origen in the quarrel that ended in John's fall.

**Also told.**
- What the book holds and how he made it: eighty heresies as wild beasts with antidotes; what he learned by study, hearsay and his own eyes; copying opponents' own texts whole (Ptolemy to Flora); his youthful brush with a sect and the eighty names he gave the bishops.
- His charges against Origen, in his own letter (souls once angels, the devil restored, the resurrection of the flesh, Eden allegorised; the rivers he drank from), and what he defended (the flesh's resurrection, the literal paradise, the "ancient faith").
- His force, told plainly: the seized and gagged ordination of Paulinian; the torn curtain at Anablatha; the decree in Cyprus against Origen's books at Theophilus's request; ordaining outside his see at Constantinople and refusing John.
- The record's conflict, each side credited: Jerome's account of the mocking in Jerusalem; Socrates' "simple mind" and Theophilus using him vs Sozomen's long aversion; the Long Brothers' "have you read our works?"; the contested denunciation in the Church of the Apostles.
- What came of him: the Panarion as the template (Augustine, Photius); the Origen campaign and John's fall (followed, not caused by him in any source); both sides of the image quarrel claiming him at 787 and in the Caroline Books; remembered as holy (Sozomen, Jerome).
- Ending: death at sea on the voyage home, with the hearsay parting words.

Confidence: book and its preface Documented (own words); Jerusalem Documented as Jerome's partisan account; Cyprus/Constantinople Documented as the historians' reports, with the denunciation Contested; parting words hearsay.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **"A great blessing" is attached to the wrong event (Beat 6).** Letter 91 (J6 19779-19782) calls the blessing on his last days the approval of "the principles which I have always proclaimed by the testimony of so great a prelate", meaning Theophilus's letter. It is not about the ban in Cyprus. As written ("Epiphanius called it 'a great blessing' on his last days"), a writer will say he called the ban a blessing.
   - In Beat 6, replace `Epiphanius called it "a great blessing" on his last days (J6 19779-19782).` with: `He wrote to Jerome that the Lord had blessed his last days, because so great a bishop now approved what he had always taught (his own letter, J6 19779-19782).`
   - Add to **Do not say**: `"He called the ban a great blessing." Say: he wrote that the Lord had blessed his last days, because Theophilus now approved what he had always taught (J6 19779-19782).`

### MINOR

2. **"Ancient faith" is moved off the ordination.** J6 10657-10659 is his reason for ordaining Jerome's brother: the monks were growing to dislike communion with the bishop, and he acted so that they would not be cut off "from the Church with its ancient faith". It is not one of his charges against Origen. This change also gives Beat 5 the reason for the ordination that it now lacks.
   - In Also told 2, replace `He says he defended the church's "ancient faith" and the rising of this flesh (J6 10657-10659, 10899-10901).` with: `He says he defended the rising of this flesh (J6 10838-10840, 10899-10901).`
   - In Beat 5, after `...as he cried that he was unworthy (J6 10676-10700).` insert: `His reason, he says: the monks there had no priest, and he feared they would be cut off from the church and its ancient faith (J6 10655-10659, 10706-10708).`
   - In What came 1, replace `**Defended:** the church's "ancient faith" and the rising of this flesh (J6 10657-10659, 10899-10901).` with: `**Defended:** the rising of this flesh and a garden of Eden that was real (J6 10899-10901, 10922-10927).`
3. **The curtain's timing (Beat 5).** The letter places the curtain at Anablatha "when I accompanied you to the holy place called Bethel" (J6 11096-11098), that is, while he and the bishop were still together. "What he did next" moves it after the quarrel.
   - Replace `In the same letter he defends what he did next.` with `In the same letter he defends two acts.`
   - Replace `At a village church called Anablatha he found` with `Earlier, travelling with the bishop to Bethel, he found at a village church called Anablatha`, and add `(J6 11096-11098)` to that sentence's citations.
   - Add to **Do not say**: `"After the quarrel he tore down a curtain." Say: he tore it while travelling with the bishop of Jerusalem (J6 11096-11098); the letter gives no date.`
4. **"Tore it down".** The source says "tore it asunder". In Beat 5 replace `he tore it down` with `he tore it apart`. Under Do not say, after the icon entry, add: `Say "tore it apart", not "tore it down".`
5. **Beat 1's reason is the researcher's inference.** No source says "here is where he learned to hunt a sect by its own books." Replace `Reason: here is where he learned to hunt a sect by its own books.` with: `Reason, in his words: God rescued him, and after reading their books he set his mind on the truth and was not drawn in; then he exposed them (HOLL1 22740-22747).` Add to **Do not say**: `"This is where he learned to hunt heretics." Say only what he says he did.`
6. **The sect's name.** "Which he calls the Gnostics" is cited to Holl's running head, which is the editor's. His own heading is HOLL1 21041 (Κατὰ Γνωστικῶν τῶν καὶ Βορβοριτῶν). In Beat 1 replace `running head 22655` with `his heading, HOLL1 21041`.
