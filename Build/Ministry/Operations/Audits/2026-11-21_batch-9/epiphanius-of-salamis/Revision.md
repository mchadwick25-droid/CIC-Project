## Findings and what was done

- **B1 (how he knew what he wrote).** Replaced the "copied his opponents' own writings" sentence with the review's wording: some heresies from books, some by hearsay, some with his own eyes and ears; where he could, he copied opponents' own writings, so no one could say he answered "from hearsay alone".
- **B2 (Apollinaris).** Added as a paraphrase after that sentence: "He listed even the teaching of the followers of Apollinaris, a man he says was always dear to him."
- **B3 (rising of this flesh).** Added "denied that this flesh would rise again" to the charges in paragraph 4, in the review's wording.
- **B4 (his name gave weight).** Added to the last sentence of paragraph 6, credited to Sozomen: "...and that Theophilus saw no danger in following his example, because the people praised him." Placed with Sozomen's other claim so Sozomen is not named a third time.
- **M1 (Augustine's count).** Now "Augustine, who reports that Epiphanius set twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after, followed his order."
- **M2 (his reasons for the ordination and curtain).** Not added: the length does not allow it, and the review says leave it for the card. Left for Mark's length ruling.
- **M3 (setting stacked).** Settled by dropping "in Palestine" from the Sozomen sentence; Palestine is now said once, in paragraph 2.
- **M4 (31-word sentence).** Split before "while": "...mocked him as an old fool. Yet crowds pressed to kiss his feet."
- **Tightenings the review named:** "a council of bishops at Nicaea" became "a council at Nicaea"; "and its bishops argued that" became "arguing that"; "Epiphanius had the bishops of Cyprus forbid the reading of". Not taken: "In a letter to that bishop, he writes that Origen taught", because "he" would be unclear after Jerome and the bishop of Jerusalem; "though he had once accused", for the same reason.
