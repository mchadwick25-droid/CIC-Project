# Source sheet: Eunomius of Cyzicus (`eunomius-of-cyzicus`)

Census: `cic-website/data/world-census.json`, entry `eunomius-of-cyzicus` (told within `anomoean-eunomian-christianity`, `name` "Anomoean (Eunomian) Christianity", `shortName` "Anomoeans", `informalName` "The Eunomians"). File paths below are under `cic/texts/` unless given in full. Line numbers are the file's own.

Witness kinds: **own** = Eunomius's own words; **contemporary** = a witness who knew him or wrote in his lifetime; **later (gap)** = a later writer, with the rough gap after the 380s; **editor** = a modern editor's note or introduction, never the source's own words; **law** = an imperial law.

Main files:
- `eunomius_first-apology_whiston1711.txt` (Whiston's 1711 English, re-edited by Pearse with Vaggione's chapter numbers): the Apology, chapters I-XXVII, and the short Eunomian Confession (XXVIII).
- `basil_opera-omnia-pg29-grc-lat_migne1857.txt` (PG 29, Greek, OCR): Basil's Against Eunomius, Books 1-3 (Book 1 from line 98136, Book 2 from 105664). Basil quotes the Apology line by line, each line headed ΕΥ̓͂Ν., his reply headed ΒΑΣ. This is the only held Greek of the Apology. Header (line 7): "SCAN QUALITY: second witness only ... nothing here is quotable until the passage is checked against the scanned page." Every Greek line used here is confirmed in sense by Whiston's English (own words) and, for Basil's reply, by Jackson's summary (editor).
- `philostorgius_ecclesiastical-history_walford1855.txt` (Walford 1855): Photius's summary of Philostorgius, Eunomius's admirer. Greek not held.
- `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml` (Zenos; Hartranft): Socrates and Sozomen.
- `npnf205_gregory-nyssa-dogmatic-treatises.txt` (Moore and Wilson): Gregory of Nyssa, Against Eunomius, with Eunomius's Apology for the Apology quoted inside it.
- `npnf208_basil-letters-select-works.xml` (Jackson): Jackson's Prolegomena summary of Basil's Against Eunomius (editor).
- `photius_library-v1_freese1920.txt` (Freese): Photius, Library, codices 4-7, 40, 137-138.
- `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`: Theodoret, Church History; Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 120, 128, 135.
- `codex-theodosianus_latinlibrary.txt` and `theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt`: Theodosian Code 16.5 (two prints of the same laws).
- `npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml`: Constantinople 381, canon 1.

Not held: the Greek original of the Apology in its own manuscripts (PG 30 appendix); Philostorgius's Greek; Vaggione's edition and study.

Quotes: marginal page markers ("|7"), column letters, footnote numbers and OCR carets ("^") are left out, line-end hyphens are joined, and Greek OCR misreadings are kept with the right reading in [brackets]; every word is otherwise as the file prints it, OCR errors included.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **D-a. When he became bishop.** Philostorgius and Theodoret: under Constantius, about 360. Socrates and Sozomen: under Valens. Zenos and Walford (editors) note it.
- **D-b. Why he left Cyzicus.** Four accounts (C4-C7): he refused Eudoxius's terms (Philostorgius); the people drove him out (Socrates); he would not go back to people who suspected him (Sozomen); Eudoxius told him to flee (Theodoret).
- **D-c. The trial behind the Apology.** Gregory of Nyssa says no court was ever opened, and mocks "an apology before an imaginary court" (`npnf205...txt` 3549-3556); Eunomius, as Gregory quotes him, says "that we were condemned by default: there was a packed panel of evil-disposed persons where a jury ought to have sat" (3524-3526). Card.
- **D-d. Basil's death.** Philostorgius says Basil died of remorse after reading the first of Eunomius's five books; Photius calls this falsehood (Philostorgius 316). Photius elsewhere says the reply was kept hidden until Basil died (T7). Card.
- **D-e. "God knows no more of his own substance than we do."** Socrates quotes it as Eunomius's (`npnf202...xml` 12513-12519). The words are not in the held Apology. Card.
- **D-f. Why Theodosius banished him.** Socrates: house meetings and readings (T4). Philostorgius ties it to chamberlains at court who held his views (382). No source says the creed of 383 was the cause.
- **D-g. Who began single immersion.** Sozomen gives both views and prefers Theophronius and Eutychius (I6).
- **D-h. Burial.** Sozomen: Dacora. Philostorgius: moved from Dacora to Tyana. Not in conflict: Sozomen gives the first burial.
- **D-i. Eutropius's office.** Walford's Philostorgius calls him prefect of the praetorium; the law of 398 (I5) is addressed to the prefect Eutychianus. The law and Philostorgius's edict are not shown to be the same act.
- **Late or partisan.** Every life-source but his own Apology is an opponent, or Philostorgius (an admirer born about 368, per Walford's editor note at line 48) surviving only in Photius's hostile ninth-century summary.
