# Review: Eunomius of Cyzicus (`eunomius-of-cyzicus.brief.txt`)

Reviewer, first pass, 2026-11-22. Judged against the Brief Kit (Parts 1, 2 and the bar in Part 4), `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md`, and the texts in `cic/texts/`.

## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Eunomius, a fourth-century teacher from Cappadocia, Aetius's pupil, leader of the party called Anomoean, briefly bishop of Cyzicus.
2. **What happened:** he taught that "unbegotten" is what God is, so the begotten Son cannot share the Father's being; Basil and then Gregory of Nyssa answered him; Theodosius kept only the creed of one being in 383 and banished him; his books were burned by law and by edict.
3. **Why it matters:** his argument forced Basil to say that "unbegotten" tells how the Father is, not what God is, and Sozomen judged most of the church would have followed Eunomius without that answer.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

## My essence, then the comparison

- **Essence (mine):** a teacher who argued that "unbegotten" names God's very being, so the begotten Son is not of the Father's being; this drew the answers of Basil and Gregory of Nyssa that shaped how the church speaks of God; his followers took his name, and he was banished under Theodosius.
- **Also told (mine):** the argument in his own words; Basil's answer; the cost: Cyzicus told three ways, the exiles, 383 and the banishment, the books destroyed.

This matches the approved heart (Hearts.md, ruling 1; Outline essence and Also told). The hook (his preface to readers now and later, two sentences) leads straight in, and the essence is complete by word 112 ("he was banished"). Within the first minute.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **Part of the approved heart dropped: the two exiles under Valens.** The heart's "The cost" names "two exiles under Valens" (Outline, Also told; Beat 5, X5). The story has none, and it is not on the Cut first list. Restore, in order between Basil's answer and Theodosius, as Philostorgius's: e.g. "Twice, Philostorgius says, officials of the emperor Valens sent him into exile." (about 12 words; "the second time condemned unheard" if room). What pays (all six cut-first items are already out):
   - Cut "His followers came to be called Eunomians." (6 words). It repeats the essence's "His followers took his name", and "Eunomian clergy" in the law of 398 is still understood.
   - "In his Apology, a written defence of his faith, he reasoned" → "In his Apology he reasoned" (saves 6). The opening has already said he "wrote a defence of his faith"; the name Apology is the only new thing.
   - "Basil quoted the Apology piece by piece" → "Basil quoted it piece by piece" (saves 1); "read his books to them" → "read them his books" (saves 1).
   Together about 14 words, enough to carry the Valens line and the fixes below within 675.

2. **The death is put on the wrong event, and told out of order.** "A law of 398 ... He died soon after." makes him die soon after 398. Sozomen says "He died not long after his banishment" (D1, `npnf202` 41531); no year of death is held (D3). Moving only the words would leave a jump back (398, then the death after 383). Fix: tell the death and burial right after the banishment paragraph: "Not long after his banishment, Sozomen says, he died, and was buried at Dacora, his birthplace in Cappadocia." Then Sozomen's judgment and the law of 398, then "Later, Philostorgius says, a powerful court official had the body moved to Tyana and ordered his books destroyed by public edict," and the closing line on the Apology. The ending stays on the person.
   - **Do not say line to add:** "He died soon after" placed after the law of 398 → "He died not long after his banishment" (Sozomen, D1), told before the law of 398.

3. **"His book survives only in a summary by Photius" is heard as Eunomius's book, and "hostile" is dropped.** The paragraph is about Eunomius; the nearest "his" is heard as his. The ending then says his Apology survives, which seems to contradict it. The outline's Who tells it asks for "a hostile summary by Photius"; that is where the story says the friendly witness reaches us through an enemy's hand. Fix: "Philostorgius's own history survives only in a hostile summary by Photius, a patriarch centuries later." (+2 words.)
   - **Do not say line to add:** "his book survives only in a summary" said of Philostorgius where Eunomius is the subject → "Philostorgius's history survives only in a hostile summary by Photius" (Who tells it; Philostorgius 52).

### MINOR

4. **"Eunomius came for his own."** Heard once, "his own" reads as his own creed, which is close to the Do not say "Eunomius wrote the creed himself". Say "Eunomius came for his own party" (T3).
5. **Socrates and Sozomen are never introduced.** The outline's Who tells it says to name them once together as opponents writing about sixty years later. A listener may hear "Socrates says" as the philosopher. If the word budget allows: "Socrates, a church historian writing some sixty years later, says the people tired of his showy talk..." and let Sozomen follow as "another, Sozomen".
6. **The year 360 is credited to Philostorgius.** "By about 360, Philostorgius says, ..." puts the year in his mouth; the year comes from the tree's tradition entry (Widely Accepted), and Philostorgius gives the council at Constantinople (E5, Walford 230). Better: "At a council in Constantinople, Philostorgius says, Eunomius and Aetius led the party ... That was in 360." Or keep the year outside the credit.
7. **"Theodosius gave the churches of Constantinople to the party of one being"** is Philostorgius's (T1) and uncredited, and his "driving out of the city the party of Arius and Eunomius" is dropped, which also drops the `arius` link. Consider "Then Theodosius gave the churches of Constantinople to the party of one being, and drove out his, Philostorgius says." Not blocking: the force of the period is carried by 383, the banishment and the law of 398.
8. **"a reply he had worked on"**: say "a reply to Basil he had worked on" (T7, Photius 11996-12035), so the listener knows whom it answers.
9. **Cyzicus appointment.** "The bishop of Constantinople made him bishop of Cyzicus. An imperial edict put out the city's bishop..." leaves the listener to join the edict to his installing. Socrates: "Eleusius should be ejected, and Eunomius installed in his place" (C1). E.g. "He came with an imperial edict that put out the city's bishop..." Also "his patron" (next paragraph) is the bishop of Constantinople; the listener has to infer it.
10. **Outline items dropped that are not on the Cut first list, none of them the heart or the ending:** his clergy's charge that he made the Son unlike the Father (C3: it is what Sozomen's "people who suspected him" refers to); that he took over the churches (C1); the break with the bishop of Constantinople over Aetius and Sozomen's "renewed Aetius's teaching, more boldly" (X1, X2); Socrates's "condemned" and Sozomen's "destroyed" (T3); Socrates's "Theodosius punished only Eunomius" (T4); the route to the Danube, Caesarea and Dacora (T5); what he defended, the Father's sole rule (What came of them 2, A11); the council of 381 and the laws of 381 and 383 (What came of them 3, I4); the later reply known only from Gregory's quotations (What came of them 4, I8). The record's force is still carried by 383, the banishment, the law of 398 and the edict, so none is blocking; the card carries them.
