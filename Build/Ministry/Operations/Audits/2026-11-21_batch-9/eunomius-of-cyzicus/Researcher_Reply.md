**Essence.** Eunomius taught that "unbegotten" is God's very being, so the begotten Son cannot share the Father's being. His Apology drew Basil's answer, and his reply to Basil drew Gregory of Nyssa's. His followers took his name. He was briefly bishop of Cyzicus, and was banished under Theodosius and died soon after.

**Also told.** The argument in his own words; Basil's answer that God's being is unbegotten, but "unbegotten" is not his being; the cost: three accounts of Cyzicus, two exiles under Valens, the creeds of 383, banishment, his body moved and his books ordered destroyed.

**Shape.** Teacher or thinker. Opens on his preface to readers then and later. Six beats in order, then impact, then his burial.

**Length.** 4 minutes, about 600 words (650 at most): about 220 for quotes (63), glosses and credited witnesses, and 380 for the beats.

**Connection.** Basil quoted the Apology passage by passage and set out that "unbegotten" says how the Father is, not what God is. Gregory of Nyssa answered the next book.

**Quotes.**
1. "It has been shown that he was not before himself, nor anything else before him, and that he is before all things. Then 'unbegotten' goes with him. Or rather, he himself is unbegotten being." (from the Greek)
2. "I too would say that God's being is unbegotten. But I would not say that 'unbegotten' is his being." (from the Greek)
3. "a style well suited to learners" (slate reuse)
