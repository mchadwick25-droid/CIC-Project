# Outline check: Damasus of Rome

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

**The essence.** Damasus became bishop of Rome in 366 (or 367) after an election his and Ursinus's parties fought out with violence, in which the dead were counted in a church, and which the city prefects and the emperors settled in his favour. As bishop he held Rome, and through Rome's councils and letters the Nicene bishops of East and West, to the faith of Nicaea, until a law of 380 named the faith he followed as the empire's test of the right faith. That is why he stands in Church and Empire: a bishop whose see the state backed and made the measure.

**Also told.**
- The Roman councils that confirmed Nicaea and set aside Ariminum because Rome had not assented (c. 369; Sozomen, Theodoret's copy of the letter); communion letters to Peter of Alexandria; Basil's appeals and his complaint of Western pride.
- The condemnation of Apollinaris and Timothy at Rome with Peter of Alexandria present (Rufinus, Sozomen, his own letter), cited later in the East.
- Rome as a court over bishops, enforced by the state (Gratian's rescript, asked by a Roman council after Ursinus's party had him accused).
- His verses on the martyrs' graves (his own inscriptions; Liber Pontificalis; Vigilius).
- Jerome as his secretary, and his request that Jerome revise the Latin Gospels (383).
- The force on his side of the record: the election (both counts, credited), and the Luciferian charge against his clergy.
- Death in December 384, almost eighty, and his own epitaph's hope of rising.

Source weight: the sources (source sheet E1–E55) hold this account. The law of 380 and the Apollinaris condemnation are Documented; the election blame is Contested and must be credited by witness.

## Verdict: FIX

Two BLOCKING findings, both quick to fix. Everything else holds against the held texts.

## The heart, compared

It leaves out no major contribution. The councils and letters to the East that I put under "also told" sit in beat 3, which is enough. The approved heart stands (Hearts.md, ruling 1).

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Beat 2 tells St Agnes after the state's settlement, a jump back and a misleading order.** In the Ursinian account the St Agnes attack comes straight after Ursinus's first exile, on 16 November, in the same consular year as the 26 October siege, 366 (AVE 5157–5159, 5223–5241; the consuls at AVE 5160–5161; the exile at AVE 5224–5225). That is before the emperors' order of November 367 to January 368 (AVE 7965) and before Praetextatus calmed the city (AMM 23972–23986). "The Ursinian account says the fighting did not stop there" therefore tells it after a settlement it came before, and claims a continuation the text does not give.
*Correction.* Delete from beat 2 the sentences from "The Ursinian account says the fighting did not stop there." to "(AVE 5223–5241; Contested, one side's charge)." Add at the end of beat 1, after the 160 dead: "The same account says that after Ursinus was sent into exile that November, Damasus and his armed men fell on Ursinus's people gathered at the shrine of St Agnes, and struck many down (AVE 5223–5241; Contested, one side's charge)." Beat 2 then opens: "Later the city's governor, Praetextatus, calmed the tumult…"

**B2. Impact 1 ties the Macarius case to the law of 380 without support.** "The force behind it fell on dissenters too" makes the Luciferian case a result of the law that branded heretics. The petition gives no date for the raid, does not cite the law, and says the judge acted "ueluti sub imperiali rescripto", as if under an imperial order (AVE 6679–6680). Its authors also claim the true faith for themselves (AVE 5270–5273), so they were not the "heretics" the law named. Told this way, the listener hears that the law's penalties fell on them.
*Correction.* Replace "The force behind it fell on dissenters too. A petition from the followers of Lucifer of Cagliari says" with "The state's force stood behind him against Christians who refused his communion. A petition from two priests of a strict group, followers of Lucifer of Cagliari, who claimed the true faith for themselves (AVE 5270–5273), says". Add to **Do not say**: "the law of 380 was used against the Luciferians" or "the Luciferians were punished as heretics" → "their petition says a judge acted 'as if under an imperial order' to force Macarius into Damasus's communion; it gives no date and cites no law (AVE 6680–6684)."

### MINOR

**M1. Beat 1, who was hired.** The siege passage says Damasus *called in* arena men, charioteers and gravediggers (AVE 5158–5159, "inuitat"). The paying is told of the charioteers (AVE 5107, "pretio") and the arena men (AVE 5148, "ingenti corrupit pretio"), not the gravediggers. *Correction:* "It says he paid charioteers and arena fighters (AVE 5107, 5148), and with them and gravediggers stormed a church held by the other side with axes, swords and fire, and killed 160 men and women, with no dead on his own side (AVE 5157–5168; Contested)."

**M2. Essence citation for "clubs".** No witness but the Ursinian account names clubs (AVE 5107, 5159). Ammianus gives "actual battle" (AMM 23176–23179). The approved wording stays. *Correction to the citation:* "(Election: AMM 23176–23189, Documented; the clubs: AVE 5107, 5159, the Ursinian account, Contested; …)". Add to **Do not say**: "Damasus's men used clubs" as fact → the clubs are in the Ursinian account; Ammianus says only that both sides fought until men were wounded and killed. Also in the essence, "an emperor's law" → "the emperors' law" (three emperors issued it, CTH 83596–83597), as beat 5 already has it.

**M3. Beat 5, order inside the beat.** The only held date for the Roman council's letter is Duchesne's "en 380" (LPD 127980, editor), and he puts the rescript after the letter (LPD 127995–127996). Telling the letter and rescript before "After the Arian emperor Valens died" (THD 15397–15398; August 378 is the translator's note, THD 15402) implies the court came first. *Correction:* reorder beat 5 as (a) Valens's death and the churches handed over to those in communion with Damasus (THD 15395–15423), (b) the council's letter and the rescript, adding "about 380, by its editor's date (LPD 127980)", (c) the law of February 380. Retitle the beat "A law and a court, 378 to about 380."

**M4. Impact 1, Antioch.** Flavian told Paulinus to show how his doctrine agreed with Damasus's, and told Apollinaris only to "embrace the teaching of Damasus" (THD 15491–15506). *Correction:* "Theodoret tells how, at Antioch, before the emperor's general, a priest challenged two rival bishops to show they agreed with Damasus, and told one of them, Apollinaris, to 'embrace the teaching of Damasus' (THD 15486–15506)."

**M5. Quoted spans beyond the three quotes.** Besides Quotes 1–3, the beats and impact put four more spans in quotation marks: Basil's "lofty souls…" (beat 3), "by the judgment of the apostolic see" (beat 4), "embrace the teaching of Damasus" (impact 1) and "the thrice blessed Damasus deposed" (impact 2). The kit allows 2 or 3 quotes. *Correction:* add one line under **Quotes**: "Every other quoted span in the beats and impact is for the narrator to paraphrase, not to quote: Basil's 'lofty souls' line, 'by the judgment of the apostolic see', 'embrace the teaching of Damasus', and 'the thrice blessed Damasus deposed'." In beat 4, change "(THD 16117–16119; narrator may paraphrase)" to "(THD 16117–16119; the narrator paraphrases)".

**M6. Quote 1, a second print.** Mansi prints the saying in Latin with "Romane" whole: "Facite me Romane urbis episcopum, & ero protinus Christianus" (MAN3 42058–42060, OCR long s). That confirms the word damaged in PL23. *Correction:* after "confirms the reading" add "; Mansi's Latin, MAN3 42058–42060, prints 'Romane urbis' whole." My own rendering of the Latin, "Make me bishop of the city of Rome, and I will be a Christian right away", agrees with the outline's.

**M7. Quote 1's job line adds "rich".** Jerome's passage says nothing of wealth (PL23 27955–27960). The wealth is Ammianus's (AMM 23191–23197), which is barred and left out. *Correction:* "rich and powerful enough for a pagan to joke about" → "worth so much that a leading pagan could joke about wanting it". Add to **Do not say**: "the rich see of Rome" with the joke → the joke shows only that the office was worth wanting; the bishops' wealth is Ammianus's, barred here.

**M8. Quote 2, the rendering's third sentence.** My rendering: "It is our will that all the peoples whom our mild rule governs live in that religion which the divine apostle Peter handed down to the Romans, as the religion he brought in, kept to this day, declares; and which, it is plain, Bishop Damasus follows, and Peter, bishop of Alexandria, a man of apostolic holiness." The outline's rendering keeps every clause. But "The religion he brought them, kept until now, declares it." does not make plain sense heard once, because "it" has nothing clear to point back to. *Correction:* "It is our will that all the peoples under our merciful rule live in the religion that the divine apostle Peter handed down to the Romans. The faith he brought them, kept to this day, shows that he did. It is plain that Bishop Damasus follows it, and Peter, bishop of Alexandria, a man of an apostle's holiness." (59 words; the count is unchanged.) Add to **Do not say**: "the law named Damasus alone" → it names Damasus and Peter of Alexandria (CTH 83600–83601).

**M9. Quote 3 and the ending, who composed the epitaph.** That Damasus wrote it is De Rossi's attribution, printed by Duchesne ("composée par lui", LPD 128201–128202, editor). The outline also says the brother line (LPD 128210) is not to be used, then uses "the brother" from it. "fratrem" is legible there; the damage is at "lenebras" and "f". *Correction:* under Ending 1, "He had written his own epitaph: Quote 3" → "The epitaph on his grave, which its editors take to be his own (LPD 128201–128202; Widely Accepted): Quote 3." Under Quote 3, "lines 128206 and 128210 are damaged in the scan and are not to be used" → "line 128206 is damaged; line 128210 is legible at 'fratrem', which gives 'the brother'."

**M10. Words to explain, "Arian".** "The teaching that the Son is unlike the Father" is Sozomen's account of Auxentius's view (SS 38802–38804). As a general gloss it is wrong for Valens's party, which said the Son was *like* the Father. *Correction:* "*Arian*: the party, named for Arius, that set aside Nicaea's creed (SS 38800–38806). Gloss: 'who rejected the creed of Nicaea'."

**M11. Beat 3, Rimini.** "set aside a later council at Rimini" can be heard as later than this council. Ariminum met in 359, before 369, and Sozomen says its *decrees* were annulled (SS 38808–38811). *Correction:* "It annulled the decrees of an earlier council at Rimini, because the bishop of Rome had not agreed to them." Make the same change in **For the card, not the story**.

**M12. Names.** Theodoret is named in impact items 1 and 2, but **Names** gives him by role. Macarius acts in impact 1 but is not listed. *Correction:* add "Theodoret of Cyrrhus: link (witness); in the story 'Theodoret', or by role as 'a church historian of the next century'" and "Macarius: acts (the Luciferian priest). Not on the tree."

**M13. Length note.** "against the 144 by which `Length_Count.md` puts this outline over 640" uses the wrong ceiling. The count is 784 against a 675 ceiling, 109 over (Length_Count.md, damasus row). The cut-first list frees about 200, which is enough. No cut-first item is the essence, an also-told part, the force (the election, St Agnes and Macarius stay), a credited disagreement, or the ending. *Correction:* "against the 109 by which `Length_Count.md` (784) puts this outline over the 675 ceiling".
