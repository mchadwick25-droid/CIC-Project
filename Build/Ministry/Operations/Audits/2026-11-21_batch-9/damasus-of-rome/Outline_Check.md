# Outline check: Damasus of Rome

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

**The essence.** Damasus became bishop of Rome in 366 (or 367) after an election his and Ursinus's parties fought out with violence, in which the dead were counted in a church, and which the city prefects and the emperors settled in his favour. As bishop he held Rome, and through Rome's councils and letters the Nicene bishops of East and West, to the faith of Nicaea, until a law of 380 named the faith he followed as the empire's test of the right faith. That is why he stands in Church and Empire: a bishop whose see the state backed and made the measure.

**Also told.**
- The Roman councils that confirmed Nicaea and set aside Ariminum because Rome had not assented (c. 369; Sozomen, Theodoret's copy of the letter); communion letters to Peter of Alexandria; Basil's appeals and his complaint of Western pride.
- The condemnation of Apollinaris and Timothy at Rome with Peter of Alexandria present (Rufinus, Sozomen, his own letter), cited later in the East.
- Rome as a court over bishops, enforced by the state (Gratian's rescript, asked by a Roman council after Ursinus's party had him accused).
- His verses on the martyrs' graves (his own inscriptions; Liber Pontificalis; Vigilius).
- Jerome as his secretary, and his request that Jerome revise the Latin Gospels (383).
- The force on his side of the record: the election (both counts, credited), and the Luciferian charge against his clergy.
- Death in December 384, almost eighty, and his own epitaph's hope of rising.

Source weight: the sources (source sheet E1–E55) hold this account. The law of 380 and the Apollinaris condemnation are Documented; the election blame is Contested and must be credited by witness.
