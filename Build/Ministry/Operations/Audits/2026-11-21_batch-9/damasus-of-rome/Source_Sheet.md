## 3. Impact, ranked heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **Communion with Damasus became the empire's test of the right faith.** The law of 380 names him as the standard (E34, CTH 83598–83601), and it was kept in the Theodosian Code (CTH book 16, title 1, law 2). Sozomen retells it in the 440s (E35). Theodoret has Gratian's general hand the churches to those "adopting communion with Damasus" (E32), and Flavian at Antioch tells rival bishops to "embrace the teaching of Damasus" (E33). *Defended: the Nicene faith, Father, Son and Spirit one Godhead.* Documented.
2. **His condemnation of Apollinaris became a precedent the East cited.** Rufinus (E25), Sozomen (E26) and Theodoret (E27) record it. In 431 Eastern bishops wrote that "the thrice blessed Damasus deposed the heretics Apollinarius, Vitalius, and Timotheus" (THD 37893–37895; letter of the Easterns to Rufus). Theodoret, about 449, quotes his anathema against the Apollinarians: "If anyone allege that on the cross pain was undergone by the Godhead and not by the body with the soul … let him be anathema" (THD 34899–34902; date per Garnerius, THD 34776–34778, editor). Theodoret lists him among "illustrious teachers of grace" (THD 35169–35171). *Defended: that Christ took a whole human being, mind included.* Documented.
3. **Rome as a court over bishops, enforced by the state.** Gratian's rescript (E30), asked for by a Roman council (E29). Documented.
4. **The martyrs' shrines and his verses.** The sixth-century Liber Pontificalis (E37) and Pope Vigilius's inscription (E41) carry it on; Duchesne says his inscriptions marked almost every shrine and were copied by pilgrims (LPD 127921–127925; LPD 127915–127918, editor). Widely Accepted.
5. **The Gospel revision.** Jerome's preface (E45). The tree's `jerome` story carries what it became. Documented.
6. **Remembered as a man of good life.** Theodoret: "a man of most praiseworthy life" (THD 15417–15420); Ambrose: "elected by the judgment of God" (E49); Jerome (E47, E48). Documented.
7. *Optional, kept today:* the Roman Breviary of 1908 keeps his feast on 11 December: "11. Damasus, Pope of Rome, Confessor. Semi-double." (`roman-breviary_bute1908.txt` 2624). A 1908 calendar, not "today": use only as "the Roman calendar kept his day".

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Who started the killing.** Ammianus blames both (E1, E2). Jerome's Chronicle, Rufinus, Socrates and Sozomen put it on Ursinus (E3–E6). The Ursinian account puts it on Damasus and names his hired men (E7–E11). Counts: 137 in a day at Sicininus (Ammianus); 160 at the basilica of Liberius, none on Damasus's side (Ursinian). The same church? The Ursinian account calls the besieged church "basilica Liberii"; Jerome and Rufinus place the fighting at the Sicininum; whether these are one building is not settled in the held texts. Say both counts, credited, and do not merge them.
- **The year.** 366 (Avellana's consuls; Hartranft; Duchesne) or 367 (Jerome's Chronicle, per Zenos) (S1–S4).
- **The rival's name.** Ursinus (Ammianus, Avellana, Socrates, Jerome, Rufinus); Ursicius (Sozomen, SS 38781).
- **His death.** December 384 (Fremantle, Duchesne); Richardson's note "380" is wrong against both (E52–E53). Jerome: under Theodosius, almost eighty.
- **Birthplace.** Spaniard (late Liber Pontificalis); Duchesne thinks Roman (S7).
- **Late material.** The Liber Pontificalis is sixth-century; its election lines are an interpolation (E12) and its "adultery" charge is modelled on Symmachus (E31). The apocryphal Damasus–Jerome letters on the Psalms are Duchesne's subject (LPD 128153–128163), not his.
- **Editors' views not to be given to sources.** Jackson's "He was the nominee of the Arian party" (THD 10208) is the editor's, unsupported in the held texts: not for the story. Duchesne's and Mommsen's view that Damasus confused two splits in the Hippolytus verse is theirs. The "Coryphaeus = Damasus" reading of Basil is Ceillier's via Jackson. Lietzmann's 377 for the Apollinaris council is Lietzmann's (used on the Apollinaris story).
