# Review: Damasus of Rome (`damasus-of-rome`)

## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Damasus, bishop of Rome from 366 to 384.
2. **What happened:** he won the office in a deadly fight with Ursinus, held the West to Nicaea, condemned Apollinaris with the bishop of Alexandria, and was named in the law of 380 as the measure of the right faith. He also marked the martyrs' graves with verses and urged Jerome to correct the Latin Gospels.
3. **Why it matters:** in his years the empire's officers settled the church's quarrels, and a law used his name to sort Christians from heretics, at a cost to those who refused.

None is blank or wrong.

## Essence and what is also told (mine, then compared)

- **Essence:** a bishop of Rome who won a bloody election, then held his church to Nicaea until an imperial law named him as the standard of the faith. He is on the tree for Church and Empire: the state settled his election and enforced his communion.
- **Also told:** the Apollinaris condemnation; the court the state enforced; the martyrs' graves and verses; the Gospels request to Jerome.

This matches the approved heart. The hook (three sentences, Praetextatus's joke) leads straight in, and the essence is complete by about word 115. Every part of what is also told is present with its weight. After the essence the life runs once, in order: 366, 367–368, 369, the 370s, about 380, February 380, the undated petition, the verses and 383, then the death in 384. Nothing is told twice and nothing jumps back. It ends on the man, with his epitaph.

Names heard once: Liberius (a link), Gratian and Theodosius (they act), Lucifer of Cagliari (a link), Martha (inside the epitaph's paraphrase). None loses the listener. I never got lost, and it never talks down.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

### BLOCKING

1. **"when emperors first took sides in its quarrels"** (paragraph 2). This is false to the record. Emperors had taken sides since Constantine. Liberius himself, Damasus's predecessor, was sent into exile (S6, AVE 5052–5058). The outline's Setting line carries this wording, but its cited lines (AVE 7933–7951; CTH 83597–83607) show the state's acts in Damasus's time, not a first. Replace `when emperors first took sides in its quarrels.` with `when emperors took sides in its quarrels.`
   - *Do not say:* "when emperors first took sides in its quarrels" → "when emperors took sides in its quarrels" (emperors took sides from Constantine on; Liberius was exiled, AVE 5052–5058).

2. **"The cost fell on those who refused."** This bridge comes straight after the law's "bear the shame of heretical teaching". Heard there, it ties the Luciferian case to the law of 380, which the Do not say bars. It also states the petitioners' account as fact before crediting them. The later "names no law" does not undo what the bridge has already told. Delete the sentence and let the paragraph begin "Two priests of a strict group…". This saves 7 words.
   - *Do not say:* a bridge from the law of 380 into the Macarius case, such as "The cost fell on those who refused" → begin the petition with no bridge to the law (AVE 6673–6684, undated, citing no law).

3. **"Over his grave stands an epitaph"** (the ending). No held source says the epitaph stands over his grave. Duchesne gives its text from De Rossi's collection (LPD 128200–128201). His church on the Via Ardeatina is named only as his burial place (LPD 127458, late). The present-tense "stands" is a detail added for effect. Replace `Over his grave stands an epitaph that its editors take to be his own. It praises the one who` with `His epitaph, which its editors take to be his own work, praises the one who`. This saves 4 words.
   - *Do not say:* "his epitaph stands over his grave" or "can still be seen" → "his epitaph" (text from De Rossi's collection, LPD 128200–128201).

4. **A dropped outline item that is the record's force and is not on the Cut first list: the churches handed over under a general.** Beat 5 and the Connection ("it should come through the acts: the law, the churches handed over, the challenge at Antioch, and its cost") both hold this. After Valens died, an edict ordered the churches given to those "adopting communion with Damasus", and Gratian sent the general Sapor to enforce it (THD 15410–15423, read again). It is the state's coercion in his name, and it is missing. Restore it at the start of the paragraph that begins "An emperor ordered…":
   `After the Arian emperor Valens died, a general enforced an edict handing the churches to those in communion with Damasus.` (20 words)
   - **What pays for it:** no Cut first item is left in the story, so the payment comes from other parts. Finding 2 saves 7 words and finding 3 saves 4. Changing "the emperors Gratian and Theodosius issued the law" to "the emperors issued the law" saves 3; the law was issued in three names, Gratian, Valentinian and Theodosius (CTH 83596). Dropping the second "it says" in "They used axes, swords and fire, it says," saves 2. Finding 1 saves 1. Net: about 666 + 20 − 17 = 669 by the scorer. Re-run the scorer.

### MINOR

1. **The flogged deacon: "Theodoret tells us".** At THD 14599–14605 the words are the bishop of Alexandria's own letter, which Theodoret quotes ("the deacon who had been sent by our beloved Damasus… to bring us letters"). The outline credits "the bishop later wrote". Change `At Alexandria, Theodoret tells us, the deacon` to `At Alexandria, the bishop there later wrote, the deacon`.
2. **"Church writers of the next generation blame Ursinus."** Jerome's Chronicle, one of them, is from Damasus's own time (CHR 46055–46088). The outline says "of his own and the next generation". Change it to `Church writers of his own time and the next blame Ursinus instead.`
3. **The rescript drops its condition.** It reached condemned bishops who unjustly held on to their church or would not come when summoned (AVE 8444–8449). As written, every bishop condemned by Damasus is sent to Rome. Add `who held on to his church` after `five or seven bishops`. If the scorer then runs past 675, cut `writes from outside the church and` from the Ammianus sentence, since "most likely a pagan" already marks him as an outsider.
4. **"no answer from Damasus's side survives"** claims more than the outline ("is held"). Change it to `and we have no answer from Damasus's side.`
5. **"In 380 an emperors' law named him"** is awkward when heard. Change it to `In 380 a law of the emperors named him`.
6. **The sentence about the book of the popes' lives runs to 32 words.** Split it after "later": `The book of the popes' lives was written about 150 years later. It says Damasus searched out the graves of the martyrs, in the burial galleries outside the city, and marked them with verses.` Then begin the next sentence `At his urging, too, Jerome…`, so the Gospels request is not heard as the late book's claim. (This adds about 3 words. Check the scorer.)
7. **Dropped, not on the Cut first list, not part of the heart:** Juventius driven out to the suburbs (AMM 23180–23182); the St Agnes attack (AVE 5223–5241), where the Ursinian violence is still told at full force through the 160 dead; Socrates' "not about doctrine" (SS 14210–14211); the order opening the last church (AVE 7933–7951), whose point "the empire's officers ended it" survives; the challenge at Antioch (THD 15486–15506); the Eastern bishops' "the thrice blessed Damasus deposed" (THD 37893–37895), which Cut first item 3 says to keep; the council's letter about Ursinus's accuser (MAN3 62301–62316); and the burial near his mother and sister (LPD 127458). Each goes to the card. None is needed for the test.
