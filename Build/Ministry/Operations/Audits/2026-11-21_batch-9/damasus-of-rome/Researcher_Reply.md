# Researcher reply: Damasus of Rome

**Essence.** Damasus was bishop of Rome from 366 to 384. He won the office in an election fought with clubs and killing. He then held his church to Nicaea's faith, and in 380 an emperor's law named him its standard.

**Also told.** He condemned Apollinaris; emperors made his judgment a court their officers enforced; he marked martyrs' graves with verses; he urged Jerome to correct the Latin Gospels.

**Shape.** Founder or leader. Opens on Praetextatus's joke; six beats, the election told by three conflicting accounts; ends on his own epitaph.

**Length.** About 4 minutes, about 610 words: quotes 80, seven credited witnesses 85, glosses 55, beats 390.

**Connection.** The law made communion with him state-enforced: churches handed over, rivals tested against his teaching, dissenters coerced (the Luciferian petition).

**Quotes.**
1. "Make me bishop of the city of Rome, and I will be a Christian at once."
2. "It is our will that all the peoples under our merciful rule live in this religion. It is the religion the divine apostle Peter handed down to the Romans. The religion he brought them, kept until now, declares it. It is plain that Bishop Damasus follows it, and Peter, bishop of Alexandria, a man of an apostle's holiness."
3. "I believe that, after the ashes, he will make Damasus rise."
