## 3. Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

1. **On the Trinity read and quoted, in Latin and in Greek.** *Defended: the full divinity of the Son, the faith of Nicaea.*
   - Augustine, On the Trinity VI.10.11: NPNF103 9185-9192. later (c. 400-420). Documented. "A certain writer, when he would briefly intimate the special attributes of each of the persons in the Trinity, tells us that 'Eternity is in the Father, form in the Image, use in the Gift.' And since he was a man of no mean authority in handling the Scriptures, and in the assertion of the faith, for it is Hilary who put this in his book (On the Trinity, ii.); I have searched into the hidden meaning of these words as far as I can"
   - John Cassian, On the Incarnation VII.24 (430): NPNF211 54733-54742. later. Documented. "Hilary, a man endowed with all virtues and graces, and famous for his life as well as for his eloquence, who also, as a teacher of the churches ... remained so steadfast during the storms of persecution that through the fortitude of his unconquered faith he attained the dignity of being a Confessor,—he testifies in the First book on the faith"
   - Theodoret, Dialogues (Eranistes, c. 447), Greek: NPNF203 24464-24465. later. Documented. "Testimony of the holy Hilarius, bishop and confessor"
   - Socrates, Church History III.10 (c. 440), Greek: NPNF202 10946-10953. later. Documented. "Hilary being a very eloquent man, maintained with great power the doctrine of the homoousion in books which he wrote in Latin. In these he gave sufficient support [to the doctrine] and unanswerably confuted the Arian tenets."
   - Sozomen V.13: NPNF202 36212-36217. later. Documented ("it is said"). "he expressed himself with great eloquence in the Latin tongue, and wrote many admirable works, it is said, in refutation of the Arian dogmas."
2. **Gaul brought back from Ariminum to Nicaea, by persuasion and pardon.** *Defended: the Nicene faith, and readmitting those who had signed rather than shunning them.*
   - The Paris letter (2f above), contemporary. Jerome's Chronicle (JCHRON 45885-45886). Sulpicius (CSEL1 5656-5658).
   - Rufinus, Church History X.32 (c. 402): RUF 28728-28739. later. Documented as Rufinus's view. "Nisi quod Hilarius, vir natura lenis et placidus simulque eruditus et ad persuadendum commodissimus, rem diligentius et aptius procu- rabat. qui etiam libros de fide nobiliter scriptos edidit, quibus et haereticorum  versutias et nostrorum deceptiones et male credulam [5] simplieitatem ita diligenter exposuit, ut et praesentes et longe positos, quibus ipse per se disserere viva voce non poterat, perfectissima in- struetione corrigeret. ita duo isti viri velut magnifica quaedam mundi lumina Illyricum, Italiam Galliasque suo splendore radiarunt" (the "two" are Hilary and Eusebius of Vercelli).
3. **Martin.** Sulpicius, Life of Martin 5 and 7 (2a, 2f above). The monastery near Poitiers; Gregory of Tours later pairs "the blessed confessors Hilary and Martin" (GT 19934).
4. **Remembered as confessor and patron at Poitiers.** Jerome calls him "a confessor and bishop of my own day" (NPNF206 16732-16733); Gregory of Tours tells that before Clovis fought the Arian Goths in 507, "a fiery beacon issue[d] from the church of the holy Hilary" over the king (GT 3708-3713). late legend, about 80 years after the event and two centuries after Hilary; kept in Gregory's voice.
5. Augustine names him among those who brought "gold and silver" out of Egypt, the learning of the world put to Christian use (NPNF102 51718-51722): "Do we not see with what a quantity of gold and silver and garments Cyprian ... was loaded when he came out of Egypt? How much Lactantius brought with him? And Victorinus, and Optatus, and Hilary". Card material.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **Year of exile.** Sulpicius's consuls (Arbitio and Lollianus) give 355; Watson and the PL23 editor give 356. Say "in the mid-350s" or credit 356 as the usual date.
- **How he came home.** Sulpicius's Chronicle says he was sent back as a troublemaker "while the sentence of exile against him remained uncanceled" (NPNF211 11408-11411). Sulpicius's own Life of Martin says "through penitence on the part of the king, permission to return had been granted" (NPNF211 965-967). Gregory of Tours says Constantius "ordered his release" (GT 1289-1291). Tell it as the Chronicle does, or say the accounts differ.
- **Why he was exiled.** Hilary: faction and false reports, not a crime (CSEL65 15365-15368), and not merely over Athanasius (CSEL65 10289-10294). Sulpicius: the exiles offered to suspend Athanasius if the faith were first examined (NPNF211 11141-11145). The census `why` line "for refusing to condemn Athanasius" is not what either source says.
- **The deacon's office.** Hilary tried to make Martin a deacon and failed (Latin). Roberts's English wrongly says he did it.
- **Saturninus "party".** NPNF203's English of Jerome reverses the sense; the Latin says the faction of Saturninus banished him.
- **The Paris letter's verb.** "entrusted to" (Feder) or "sent to" (Maurist) Hilary.
- **The council's place and Saturninus's deposition.** The letter's heading names Paris; Sulpicius says "frequent councils" and that Saturninus was cast out; joining the two is Watson's.
- **The search for God in On the Trinity I** is, Watson warns, too finished a piece of writing to read as literal history (NPNF209 765-773). Editor's view.
- **The daughter Abra** comes from a spurious letter (NPNF209 812-814, 3856). Editor's view.
- **Rufinus's tale of a falsified book** (NPNF203 45533-45555), told c. 397 to argue that Origen's books were tampered with; single, interested witness; its own editor finds "no means of throwing light upon this story" (NPNF203 45536).
- **Wonders** (raising the dead; the fiery light over Clovis) are Gregory of Tours's, two centuries on.
