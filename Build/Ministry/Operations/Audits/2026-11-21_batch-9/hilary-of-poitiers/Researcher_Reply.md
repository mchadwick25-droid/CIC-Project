**Essence.** Exiled to Phrygia in 356 after Saturninus's party accused him, Hilary learned the Eastern quarrel over the Son. He wrote On the Trinity and On the Councils, which explained the Eastern creeds to the West, then led Gaul's bishops back to Nicaea.

**Also told.** "One in substance" and "like in substance" can stand together; Seleucia; Gaul's pardon of the Ariminum signers; Milan, his failure.

**Shape.** Teacher or thinker, seven beats.

**Length.** About 4 minutes, 600 words; floor 250 (quotes 110, glosses 70, credits 70).

**Connection.** Martin. Hilary made him an exorcist after he refused the deacon's office, and Martin later set up a monastery near Poitiers.

**Quotes** (from the Latin):
1. "I was baptized a good while ago, and I had been a bishop for some time. I never heard the faith of Nicaea until I was going into exile. But the Gospels and the Apostles showed me what 'one in substance' and 'like in substance' mean."
2. "From your letters, which you entrusted to our dear brother and fellow bishop Hilary, we have learned the devil's fraud. We have learned how the minds of heretics plotted against the Lord's church. So we were split between East and West, and misled by each other's differing opinions."
3. "All agree on this: by the good work of Hilary alone, our provinces of Gaul were freed from the guilt of heresy."
