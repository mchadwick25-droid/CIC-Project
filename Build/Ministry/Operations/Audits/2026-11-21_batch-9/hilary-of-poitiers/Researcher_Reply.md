**Essence.** Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, was exiled to Phrygia by Constantius in 356, after Saturninus's party accused him. There he learned the Eastern quarrel over the Son, wrote On the Trinity against the Arians and On the Councils to explain the Eastern creeds to the West, and came home to lead Gaul's bishops back to Nicaea. Martin had sought him out before the exile.

**Also told.** That "one in substance" and "like in substance" could stand together; Seleucia and his dismissal as a troublemaker; Gaul's councils and the pardon of those who signed at Ariminum (with Lucifer opposed, Saturninus cast out); Milan, his failure.

**Shape.** Teacher or thinker. Seven beats; the opening is his own admission.

**Length.** About 4 minutes, about 600 words (650 at most). The floor is about 250 words: three quotes (110), glosses (70) and credits (70).

**Connection.** Martin: Hilary tried to make him a deacon, made him an exorcist when he refused, and Martin later followed him home and set up a monastery near Poitiers.

**Quotes** (rendered from the Latin):
1. "I was baptized a good while ago, and I had been a bishop for some time. I never heard the faith of Nicaea until I was going into exile. But the Gospels and the Apostles showed me what 'one in substance' and 'like in substance' mean."
2. "From your letters, which you entrusted to our dear brother and fellow bishop Hilary, we have learned the devil's fraud. We have learned how the minds of heretics plotted against the Lord's church. So we were split between East and West, and misled by each other's differing opinions."
3. "All agree on this: by the good work of Hilary alone, our provinces of Gaul were freed from the guilt of heresy."

**Gaps.** The Latin of On the Trinity is not held. Two English translations are wrong (Martin's deacon's office; Jerome's line on Saturninus's party). I checked for the four faults and re-read every quote in its file.
