# Fresh read: Hilary of Poitiers (`hilary-of-poitiers`)

Read cold against the Kit, `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md` and `cic/texts/`. No earlier review, revision or recheck was read. Mark's ruling of 2026-11-22 that the Gallic bishops' letter is told in the narrator's words is applied.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Hilary, bishop of Poitiers in Gaul, in the 350s and 360s.
- **What happened:** A rival bishop's faction had him exiled to Phrygia in 356. There he learned the Eastern quarrel over the Son, wrote On the Trinity against the Arians, and wrote On the Councils to explain the Eastern creeds to the West. He spoke at Seleucia and was sent home with his exile not lifted. Back in Gaul he brought the bishops who had signed at Ariminum back to the faith of Nicaea, by repentance rather than shunning.
- **Why the connection matters:** Gaul returned to Nicaea through him; even his supporter says "by the good work of Hilary alone". Martin, later of Tours, first came into the church's service through him and followed him home.

My own essence before comparing: an exiled Western bishop who learned the Eastern debate, explained each side to the other, and brought Gaul back to Nicaea by pardon; a teacher whose books were read by later Latin and Greek writers. The brief's essence matches the outline's and is complete by about word 135. The hook is his own admission in quote 1. Every "also told" part is present: like and one in substance, On the Councils as a report and as a plea, Seleucia and Constantinople, the bishops' letter and the excommunications, the choice to pardon with Saturninus cast out, and Milan in 364. The life runs once, in order. The ending is on the person: his death at Poitiers. Nothing talks down.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** Clear, adult and grounded. Martin's exorcist office, "so lowly Martin could not refuse it without seeming proud", and "Only Hilary tells it." are exactly the documentary register. The close, "Nothing is recorded of how.", is honest but flat.
- **Craft: 3.** A good hook and essence, and the Martin connection is carried through acts, as the outline asks. But What came of him stops at Gaul and Martin. For a Teacher or thinker, the kit puts the impact "in their students and later readers", and the later readers are gone (finding 1). The letter sentence runs to 39 words. Ariminum is told after Constantinople as background in the pluperfect, which can be followed.
- **Rigor: 4.** Every sentence traces to the outline. Quote 1 is checked against the Latin (PL10 43844-43847, "Regeneratus pridem... fidem Nicaenam numquam nisi exsulaturus audivi: sed mihi homousii et homoeusii intelligentiam Evangelia et Apostoli intimaverunt") and Watson's English (NPNF209 9881-9884). Quote 3 is checked against the Latin (CSEL1 5656-5657, "illud apud omnes constitit unius Hilarii beneficio Gallias nostras piaculo haereseos liberatas") and Roberts's English (NPNF211 11433-11434). The letter's paraphrase is checked against CSEL65 6815-6818. Two small wording points are noted below.
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 7.9 and reading ease 75.8, and not flat on a listener's read. "Exorcist", "communion", "Arian" and Nicaea are glossed in the sentence. "One in substance" and "like in substance" are heard in the hook but given their plain sense only about 350 words later (finding 4).

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: the later readers are missing.** For a Teacher or thinker the kit puts the impact "in their students and later readers". The outline ranks "His books were read in Latin and in Greek" first in What came of him and marks it *Defended*. It is not on Cut first, and Mark's length ruling says the writers drop "from that list and not by their own choice". As told, the listener never hears that his books outlived him. That is missing essential material.
   - **Restore**, from the outline's own wording (NPNF103 9185-9192), at about 27 words: "Augustine, writing his own On the Trinity, studied Hilary's account of the three persons, and called him a man of real authority in reading the Scriptures and in stating the faith."
   - **Place** it with What came of him, after quote 3 and before Milan or the death.
   - **Pay** with the story's 18 spare words. Add about 9 words from splitting and tightening the letter sentence (finding 2). If room allows, add the Constantinople historian in one clause, which comes to about 15 words (NPNF202 10950-10953).
2. **MINOR: a 39-word sentence, and whose letters.** "The bishops of Gaul wrote to the East that the letters Hilary brought had shown them the devil's fraud and the heretics' plots against the church, which had split East from West and misled each side about the other." This sentence is past the kit's limit of about 30 words. It also says "the letters Hilary brought". The letter says "your letters, which you entrusted to... Hilary" (CSEL65 6815-6816), and Do not say bars "carried home" as fact; the Maurist text reads "sent to". As heard, the listener may also take them for Hilary's own letters. Replace with: "The bishops of Gaul wrote to the East that the letters it had entrusted to Hilary showed them the devil's fraud and the heretics' plots against the church. Those plots had split East from West, each side misled about the other." That is two sentences of 23 and 13 words, saving 3.
3. **MINOR: "terrified by threats" at Ariminum.** Sulpicius's words "the best priests being either terrified by threats, or driven into exile" (NPNF211 11396-11398) describe the Seleucian deputies at Constantinople. For Ariminum (chs. 43-44, NPNF211 11290-11330) he tells of the emperor's orders, banishment for holdouts, seven months shut up, "threats and terrors" that the steadfast resisted, and a deceptive proposal. The gist (pressure) holds, but the phrase belongs to the other event, and the outline's gloss carries the same citation. Change to: "Western bishops, pressed by the emperor's threats, had signed a creed that dropped the word 'substance.'" Add a Do not say line to the outline: *"terrified by threats" for Ariminum. Say: pressed by the emperor's threats (Sulpicius II.43-44); "terrified by threats" is his phrase for the Eastern deputies at Constantinople (NPNF211 11396-11398).*
4. **MINOR: the two Greek terms come late.** The hook's "'one in substance' and 'like in substance'" get their plain senses only in the On the Councils paragraph. The essence's gloss of Nicaea uses a third form, "of one substance". Optional: "Nicaea was the council of 325, whose creed said the Son is 'one in substance' with the Father: he shares the Father's very being." The "like" gloss can stay where it is.
5. **MINOR: Seleucia.** The outline keeps "vouched for the West". The brief's "set out a faith matching Nicaea and was received" leaves out whose faith was in question. Optional: "set out a faith matching Nicaea, vouched for the West, and was received" (+3 words).
