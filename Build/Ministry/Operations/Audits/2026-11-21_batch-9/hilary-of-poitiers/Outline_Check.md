# Outline check: Hilary of Poitiers (`hilary-of-poitiers`)

## My own account, written before reading the outline

**Essence.** Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, was driven into exile in Phrygia in the mid-350s (usually dated 356) by the faction of Saturninus of Arles with the emperor Constantius's backing. He had never heard the creed of Nicaea before he went; in the East he learned the Greek debate from inside, wrote On the Trinity (twelve books against the Arians) and On the Councils, which explained the Eastern creeds to the bishops of Gaul and urged the Eastern "like in substance" party that "like" need not reject "one in substance". Sent home about 360 as "a troubler of the East", he brought the bishops of Gaul to condemn Ariminum and return to Nicaea by calling them to repentance and pardoning them, not shunning them. He is on the tree as the Latin West's bridge to the Greek debate, the teacher whose books Augustine, Cassian and the Greek historians read, and the bishop Martin of Tours came to learn from.

**Also told.** Martin's coming to him and the exorcist's office (Hilary tried to make him a deacon and failed); Béziers and his own plea that he was exiled by faction, not crime, and not over Athanasius alone; Seleucia (vouching for the West's faith) and the refused hearing at Constantinople; the Paris letter (contemporary) and Sulpicius's "by Hilary alone"; Lucifer's opposite line; Martin following him home and the monastery near the town; the failed accusation of Auxentius at Milan (364); death at Poitiers in the sixth year after his return (367 by Watson).

## Findings

**B1 (BLOCKING). Library sweep: Hilary's Against Constantius is held but not accounted for.** PL10 holds the Liber contra Constantium imperatorem (PL10 46205 on; the content note at PL10 line 9 calls it "Hilary's own denunciation of the emperor"). Watson calls it "one of the bitterest documents of a controversy" (NPNF209 2165-2175). Jerome lists "another On Constantius which he wrote after his death" (NPNF203 41344-41345). The sheet's Used and Set aside lists name only To Constantius II. The outline's picture of a patient bridge-builder leaves out his open attack on the emperor, with no reason recorded. That risks softening the record's conflict. Correction: add a row to Source_Sheet 2f: "Against Constantius: an invective against the emperor; Jerome says it was written after Constantius's death (NPNF203 41344-41345); Watson dates its plan to Constantinople, 360 (NPNF209 2478); PL10 46205 on." Then either add one clause to beat 6, "After the emperor died, Jerome says, Hilary published a book against him (NPNF203 41344-41345). Documented (Jerome); the date is disputed by the editor", or add to Leave out, with the reason given: "Against Constantius (PL10 46205 on): his invective against the emperor; card, with Jerome's and Watson's differing dates."

**M1. Quote 1, "a good while ago".** The Latin "pridem" means "some time ago", and Watson warns that "long ago" and "for some time" are "rather too strong" (NPNF209 1077-1079). Replace the first sentence with: "I was baptized some time ago, and I had been a bishop for a while."

**M2. Opening 1, who the words are addressed to.** On the Councils 88-91 turns to the Eastern bishops ("brethren, Basil, Eustathius, and Eleusius", NPNF209 9850). Replace "he wrote this in exile, to the bishops of Gaul and Britain" with "he wrote this in exile, in a book to the bishops of Gaul and Britain, in a passage turned to the Eastern bishops (NPNF209 7746-7752, 9850)".

**M3. Opening 2, "No one had ordered him there".** Sulpicius says he "is compelled to be present" ("adesse compellitur") and that the emperor gave no special order about him (NPNF211 11253-11262; CSEL1 5452-5457). Replace it with: "The emperor had given no order about him; the governors, following the general order to gather all the bishops, sent him with the rest, Sulpicius Severus says."

**M4. Also told, bullet 1, "so neither side had to curse the other".** This is not in PL10 43685-43687. Replace it with: "that the Eastern 'like in substance' need not mean rejecting the Nicene 'one in substance' (PL10 43685-43687; NPNF209 9860-9861)".

**M5. Beat 2, "the emperor's man in Gaul" and "had broken communion".** No cited line calls Saturninus the emperor's man. Sulpicius says he "harassed our country of Gaul" for the Arians, "whom [the emperor] favored" (NPNF211 11175-11176, 11187). Hilary dates the refusal of communion as "for three whole years now" up to the time he wrote (PL10 38616-38617), not as something that came before Béziers. Replace the sentence with: "Hilary and the bishops of Gaul refused communion to Saturninus, bishop of Arles, who, Sulpicius says, harassed Gaul for the Arian party."

**M6. Beat 3, "our On the Trinity", confidence.** That Jerome's "twelve books against the Arians" are On the Trinity is an identification by the editor and the tradition, not Jerome's own words. Confidence: "Documented (the twelve books); Widely Accepted (that they are On the Trinity)". Also change "from city after city" to "from several cities" (NPNF209 7755).

**M7. Beat 6, the dropped "almost" and the confidence.** Sulpicius says "almost all the bishops publicly owned the error" ("omnibus fere episcopis"), and that Hilary condemned Ariminum (NPNF211 11421-11423; CSEL1 5625-5626). Replace with: "in frequent councils, almost all the bishops confessed their error, and he condemned Ariminum." Replace "Confidence: Documented" with: "Documented (the bishops' letter); Documented as Sulpicius's account (the councils, Saturninus, Lucifer, Martin)". Sulpicius is openly hostile to Saturninus.

**M8. Beat 6 and Connection, "followed him to Poitiers".** Sulpicius says only "not far from the town" ("haut longe ... ab oppido", CSEL1 6500-6501; NPNF211 977-980). Say "followed him home and set up a monastery not far from Hilary's town".

**M9. What came 3, "began the monks' Gaul that grew up beside these bishops".** NPNF211 977-980 does not say this. It echoes the census relations text. The editor says only that "a few monasteries had been founded in the district of the Loire by S. Martin and S. Hilary" (NPNF211 15444-15446). Replace it with: "Martin set up a monastery not far from Hilary's town (NPNF211 977-980). Carried in the connection below."

**M10. Connection, "the monk who later became bishop of Tours".** This has no cited line. Add: "(Sulpicius, Life of Martin 9-10; NPNF211 1111 on)".

**M11. Essence, "the young Martin".** No cited line gives his age (CLAUDE.md: no invented age). Delete "young".

**M12. Names against What came 2.** Names gives Rufinus by role, but What came 2 names him ("A historian of the next generation, Rufinus,"). Pick one: either delete ", Rufinus," or move Rufinus of Aquileia to "link" in Names.

**M13. Words to explain, Ariminum: no source.** Add: "(Sulpicius, Chronicle II.43-44: the creed 'abolished the use of the word Ousia', bishops 'terrified by threats'; NPNF211 11296-11306, 11396-11398)".

**M14. Do not say, gaps a writer could fill wrongly.** Add these:
- "The emperor Constantius took communion with Auxentius at Milan." Say "the emperor" or "the new emperor". In 364 it was Valentinian (NPNF209 3933-3935), not Constantius.
- "Ligugé" or "the first monastery in Gaul". The name and the date are the editor's (NPNF211 15445-15446). Say "a monastery not far from the town".
- "The 'like in substance' bishops were not Arians" in the narrator's voice. Hilary wrote to them as brothers (NPNF209 9850-9861), but Sulpicius counts "like in substance" as an Arian twist (NPNF211 11160-11170). Replace the outline's current "They were not" line with: keep "Arian" for the party Hilary opposed, and say Hilary wrote to the "like in substance" bishops as brothers.
- "He died in 367" as a record. Say "in the sixth year after his return" or "about 367".
- Ending 2 says "only if beat 4 did not use them". The words are in beat 3. Change "beat 4" to "beat 3".
