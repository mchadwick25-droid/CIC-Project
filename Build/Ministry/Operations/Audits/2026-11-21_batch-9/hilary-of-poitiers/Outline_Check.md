# Outline check: Hilary of Poitiers (`hilary-of-poitiers`)

## My own account, written before reading the outline

**Essence.** Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, was driven into exile in Phrygia in the mid-350s (usually dated 356) by the faction of Saturninus of Arles with the emperor Constantius's backing. He had never heard the creed of Nicaea before he went; in the East he learned the Greek debate from inside, wrote On the Trinity (twelve books against the Arians) and On the Councils, which explained the Eastern creeds to the bishops of Gaul and urged the Eastern "like in substance" party that "like" need not reject "one in substance". Sent home about 360 as "a troubler of the East", he brought the bishops of Gaul to condemn Ariminum and return to Nicaea by calling them to repentance and pardoning them, not shunning them. He is on the tree as the Latin West's bridge to the Greek debate, the teacher whose books Augustine, Cassian and the Greek historians read, and the bishop Martin of Tours came to learn from.

**Also told.** Martin's coming to him and the exorcist's office (Hilary tried to make him a deacon and failed); Béziers and his own plea that he was exiled by faction, not crime, and not over Athanasius alone; Seleucia (vouching for the West's faith) and the refused hearing at Constantinople; the Paris letter (contemporary) and Sulpicius's "by Hilary alone"; Lucifer's opposite line; Martin following him home and the monastery near the town; the failed accusation of Auxentius at Milan (364); death at Poitiers in the sixth year after his return (367 by Watson).
