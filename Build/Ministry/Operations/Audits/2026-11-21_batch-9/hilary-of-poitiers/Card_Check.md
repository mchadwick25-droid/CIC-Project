- **No clean print stands behind four words:**
  - "dear" (*dilecto*): "dilccto" in both prints, the same damage. No other word fits, but neither print shows it.
  - "fraud" (*fraudem*): Feder "(raudem", Migne "fiau-dem".
  - "West" (*Occidentis*): Feder "(lccidcntis", Migne "Occidenlis".
  - "misled" (*falleremur*): Feder "faUeremur", Migne "falleremiir".
  For "fraud", "West" and "misled", the letters lost in one print survive in the other, so a letter-by-letter splice gives the word. For "dear" it does not.
- **"entrusted" stands on Feder alone, against Migne.** Feder's "credidistis" and Migne's "direxistis" ("sent to") are each clean, and they differ. The card notes this. Feder's own apparatus for the line (6834) is damaged. It seems to report other manuscript readings, including "creditur", and a correction, so the verb may be a manuscript problem and not only a difference between two editors. That cannot be settled from the scan.
- Every other word in the rendering stands clean in at least one print (for example *Ecclesiam* and *invicem* in Migne, the rest in Feder), and the two prints agree on it.

The card's `uncertain` note is accurate: both prints are damaged, and the verb differs. `Card_Edits.md` says "second print confirms". That overstates it, for the four words above and the verb. Whether this quotation can stand under the rule for damaged prints ("prints its English alone unless another held print confirms the reading"), given that no English is held, is the point for Mark. It is the same kind of point as batch 8's "three quoted spans with no clean print".
