# Outline: Hilary of Poitiers (`hilary-of-poitiers`)

All lines are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives every text verbatim. File short names are those of the sheet (NPNF209, PL10, CSEL65, NPNF211, CSEL1, NPNF203, PL23, JCHRON, NPNF206, RUF, NPNF202, NPNF103, GT).

**The essence:** Hilary, bishop of Poitiers in Gaul, was sent into exile in Phrygia, in Asia Minor, by the emperor Constantius in 356, after a rival bishop's party had accused him. There he learned the Eastern bishops' quarrel over how the Son is God. He wrote On the Trinity against the Arian party and On the Councils to explain the Eastern creeds to the West, then came home and led the bishops of Gaul back to the faith of Nicaea. Before the exile, Martin, later of Tours, had sought him out and stayed with him.
(Exile, place, and who drove it: NPNF209 7773-7775; PL23 60465-60466; NPNF211 11253-11254. The quarrel learned in exile: PL10 43844-43848; NPNF209 7754-7757. On the Trinity: PL23 60466-60467; JCHRON 45722-45725. On the Councils: NPNF209 7746-7752, 7843-7846. Gaul back to Nicaea: CSEL65 6815-6820; NPNF211 11414-11425; JCHRON 45885-45886. Martin: CSEL1 6382-6384.)

**Also told:**
- What he defended, in his own words: that the Eastern 'like in substance' need not mean rejecting the Nicene 'one in substance' (PL10 43685-43687; NPNF209 9860-9861).
- On the Councils was a report written at the Gallic bishops' own request, telling them what the Eastern bishops had confessed (NPNF209 7843-7846), and also a plea to those Eastern bishops (NPNF209 9860-9861).
- At Seleucia in 359 he vouched for the West's faith before the Eastern bishops; at Constantinople he asked for a hearing, was refused, and was sent home as a troublemaker (NPNF211 11266-11277, 11399-11411).
- At home, the bishops of Gaul wrote to the Eastern bishops that the letters given to Hilary had shown them how East and West had been set against each other, and they cut off the leaders of the other side (CSEL65 6815-6820, 6918-6920). He chose to bring back the bishops who had signed at Ariminum, rather than shun them; Saturninus was cast out (NPNF211 11414-11433).
- His failure at Milan in 364, told only by himself (PL10 49253-49280).

Where the slate's job and the sources part: the sources hold On the Councils, not On the Trinity, as the book that explained East to West; On the Trinity is held as twelve books "against the Arians" (PL23 60466-60467). The sources hold that Martin "sought" Hilary and "stayed with him for some time" (CSEL1 6382-6384), not that he came "to learn". The story says what they hold.

**Length:** about 4:30 at most (about 675 words by the scorer), aiming at 4:00. What it must carry: three quotes (about 110 words); plain sense for Nicaea and its two Greek terms, Arian, exorcist, Ariminum and Phrygia (about 70 words); credits to Hilary himself, the Gallic bishops' letter, Sulpicius Severus, a chronicler, Augustine, a Latin monk-writer, two Greek writers and a historian of the next generation (about 70 words). That floor of about 250 words, with seven short beats, a third for what came of him, and a death to end on, comes to about 600. The record is full (his own letters, a council's letter, and a near-contemporary historian), so it earns the extra minute; it does not earn more.

**Setting:**
- When: the 350s and 360s; exile 356 to 360; died about 367 (NPNF209 1422-1423, 4520-4528; NPNF211 11441-11442).
- Where: Poitiers, in Aquitania, western Gaul, now France (PL23 60464-60465); exile in Phrygia, in Asia Minor, now central Turkey (PL23 60466; NPNF211 11254).
- Tradition: the Nicene bishops of Gaul. Spoken name: "Nicene Gaul" (census shortName); "Hilary's Gaul" (informal).
- Power: the emperor Constantius, who favoured the Arian party and wanted one agreed faith (NPNF211 11185-11189).

**Words to explain:**
- **Nicaea / the faith of Nicaea:** the council of 325, whose creed said the Son is "of one substance" with the Father.
- **one in substance / like in substance:** the two Greek words, homoousion and homoiousion. The first says the Son shares the Father's very being; the second says his being is like the Father's. Use the English; the Greek only if needed.
- **Arian party:** those who, following Arius, held the Son was less than the Father. Do not use it for the Eastern "like in substance" bishops.
- **exorcist:** a lowly church office, of those who prayed over people thought troubled by evil spirits.
- **Ariminum:** the council in Italy in 359 where the Western bishops were pressed to sign a creed that dropped the word "substance" (Sulpicius, Chronicle II.43-44: the creed 'abolished the use of the word Ousia', bishops 'terrified by threats'; NPNF211 11296-11306, 11396-11398).
- **Phrygia:** a region of Asia Minor.
- **communion:** sharing the sacrament, as the sign that churches belong together. "Cut off from communion" for excommunicate.
- **confessor:** one who suffered for the faith and lived.

**Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The idea shown through his own words: the confession in quote 1.

**Sibling:** none. (Live links, not siblings: `martin-of-tours` tells Martin going to Hilary in one sentence; this story tells it from Hilary's side, from Sulpicius's Latin. `athanasius-of-alexandria` does not tell Hilary. Later stories `councils-of-ariminum-and-seleucia-359`, `liberius-of-rome`, `council-of-sardica-343` do not yet tell Hilary.)

**Openings:**
1. *His own admission.* Quote 1, then: he wrote this in exile, in a book to the bishops of Gaul and Britain, in a passage turned to the Eastern bishops (NPNF209 7746-7752, 9850), from the far side of the empire. It leads straight to the essence: what a bishop who had never heard the creed of Nicaea came to do for it.
2. *Seleucia, 359.* An exiled bishop from Gaul arrives at a council of Eastern bishops. The emperor had given no order about him; the governors, following the general order to gather all the bishops, sent him with the rest, Sulpicius Severus says. The Eastern bishops' first question was what the bishops of Gaul really believed (NPNF211 11253-11268). If this is used, beat 5 does not tell Seleucia again.

**Story beats:**
1. **Martin comes (before 356; no year).** After leaving the army, Martin sought Hilary out and stayed with him. Hilary tried to bind him to the church by making him a deacon. Martin refused, crying that he was unworthy. So Hilary made him an exorcist, an office so lowly Martin could not refuse it without seeming proud. Martin left to visit his parents, bound by Hilary's prayers and tears to come back. Source: Sulpicius, Life of Martin 5 (CSEL1 6382-6397; NPNF211 872-895). Confidence: Documented as Sulpicius's account.
2. **Béziers and exile (356).** Hilary and the bishops of Gaul refused communion to Saturninus, bishop of Arles, who, Sulpicius says, harassed Gaul for the Arian party. At a council at Béziers Hilary denounced the leaders of the other side; Saturninus then, Hilary says, deceived the emperor, and Hilary was sent to Phrygia (NPNF209 7773-7777, 7787-7789; PL23 60465-60466). Hilary later told Constantius he was exiled not for any crime but by a faction and false reports, and that he was still a bishop in communion with all of Gaul (paraphrase, not a quote) (CSEL65 15331, 15364-15368). Sulpicius says the exiles had offered to cut off Athanasius, if the bishops would first examine the faith; the other side preferred to remove them (NPNF211 11141-11145). Confidence: Documented (his claim; Sulpicius's account). Date: Widely Accepted; Sulpicius's consuls point to 355.
3. **In Phrygia (356-359).** He never heard the creed of Nicaea until he was going into exile (quote 1, if not the opening). There he learned what the Eastern bishops believed and wrote home about it from several cities (NPNF209 7754-7757). He wrote twelve books against the Arians, our On the Trinity, and said his writings would speak from his exile (PL23 60466-60467; JCHRON 45722-45725; NPNF209 22075). Confidence: Documented (the twelve books); Widely Accepted (that they are On the Trinity).
4. **On the Councils (358-359).** Bishops of Gaul asked him to tell them what the Eastern bishops had confessed. He wrote to them, and to the bishops of Britain, setting out the Eastern creeds. To the Eastern "like in substance" bishops he wrote that to accept "like" they need not reject "one in substance" (NPNF209 7843-7846, 9860-9861; PL10 43685-43687). Confidence: Documented.
5. **Seleucia and Constantinople (359-360).** In the fourth year of exile the governors sent him to the Eastern council at Seleucia. Rumour had it the Gauls held Sabellius's teaching, that Father, Son and Spirit are only three names for one; Hilary set out a faith that matched Nicaea, vouched for the West, and was received (NPNF211 11253-11277). At Constantinople he asked three times, in writing, to debate before the emperor; the Arian party blocked it, and he was sent back to Gaul as a troublemaker in the East, his exile not lifted (paraphrase; the Latin phrase is not one of the story's quotes) (NPNF211 11399-11411; CSEL1 5619-5624). Confidence: Documented as Sulpicius's account.
6. **Home: Gaul back to Nicaea (360-361).** Many wanted nothing to do with the bishops who had signed at Ariminum. Hilary chose to bring them all back to repentance; in frequent councils, almost all the bishops confessed their error, and he condemned Ariminum (NPNF211 11414-11425). The Gallic bishops wrote to the East (quote 2), and cut off Auxentius, Ursacius, Valens and others, as Hilary had declared he would have no peace with them; any bishop in Gaul who resisted would lose communion and his see (CSEL65 6918-6920, 6927-6930). Saturninus resisted, and was cast out of the church; the rest were pardoned (NPNF211 11425-11433). Another exile, Lucifer of Cagliari, refused communion even with those who took the signers back; Sulpicius will not say who was right (NPNF211 11434-11440). Martin followed Hilary home and set up a monastery not far from Hilary's town (NPNF211 977-980). Confidence: Documented (the bishops' letter); Documented as Sulpicius's account (the councils, Saturninus, Lucifer, Martin).
7. **Milan (364).** Hilary accused Auxentius, the Arian bishop of Milan. Auxentius signed a confession that sounded orthodox, and the emperor took communion with him. Hilary said it was all a pretence, and was ordered out of Milan, since against the emperor's will he had no freedom to stay (PL10 49253-49260, 49277-49280). Only Hilary tells it. Confidence: Documented as Hilary's account; year Watson's (NPNF209 3933).

**What came of them** (about a third of the story):
1. **His books were read in Latin and in Greek.** *Defended: that the Son is fully God, the faith of Nicaea.* Augustine, writing his own On the Trinity, studied Hilary's formula for the three persons, and called him a man of real authority in reading the Scriptures and in stating the faith (paraphrase; NPNF103 9185-9192). A Latin monk-writer, John Cassian, quoted his books in 430 as a confessor's (NPNF211 54733-54742). In the Greek East, a historian at Constantinople said he defended "one in substance" in Latin books that confuted the Arians (NPNF202 10950-10953), and a Syrian bishop, Theodoret, quoted him under the title of bishop and confessor (NPNF203 24464-24465). Give these by role except Augustine.
2. **Gaul held to Nicaea, by pardon rather than shunning.** *Defended: the faith of Nicaea, and taking back those who had signed.* Quote 3 (Sulpicius). A chronicle written twenty years on records, in one line, that through Hilary Gaul condemned the tricks of Ariminum (paraphrase; JCHRON 45885-45886). A historian of the next generation calls him gentle and calm by nature, learned, and most able at persuading, and says his books corrected even those far off whom he could not speak to in person (RUF 28728-28739).
3. **Martin.** Martin set up a monastery not far from Hilary's town (NPNF211 977-980). Carried in the connection below.

**Quotes:**
1. *Rendered from the Latin*, Hilary, On the Councils 91 (PL10 43844-43848; Migne OCR with letter errors, confirmed by Watson's English, NPNF209 9881-9884, and by Watson's own Latin of its first clause, NPNF209 1078):
   "I was baptized some time ago, and I had been a bishop for a while. I never heard the faith of Nicaea until I was going into exile. But the Gospels and the Apostles showed me what 'one in substance' and 'like in substance' mean."
   Job: the turn of the story in his own voice; a bishop who came to the creed late, through Scripture, and so could stand between the two words. Not on the tree.
2. *Rendered from the Latin*, the Gallic bishops' letter to the Eastern bishops, 360-361 (CSEL65 6815-6820, Feder's critical text; OCR rough, confirmed by the Maurist text, PL10 56742-56744, which reads "sent to" for "entrusted to"):
   "From your letters, which you entrusted to our dear brother and fellow bishop Hilary, we have learned the devil's fraud. We have learned how the minds of heretics plotted against the Lord's church. So we were split between East and West, and misled by each other's differing opinions."
   Job: proof, in the West's own words, that his bridge worked; the essence's "explain East and West to each other". Not on the tree.
3. *Rendered from the Latin*, Sulpicius Severus, Chronicle II.45 (CSEL1 5656-5658; Google OCR with letter errors, confirmed by NPNF211 11433-11434):
   "All agree on this: by the good work of Hilary alone, our provinces of Gaul were freed from the guilt of heresy."
   Job: the outcome, kept in the voice of a witness openly on Hilary's side, so the narrator does not claim "alone". On the tree only in this subject's own existing text; no other story uses it.

**Connection to carry into the story:** Martin. Sulpicius says Martin, fresh from the army, sought Hilary out and lived with him for a while; Hilary tried to bind him to the church as a deacon, and when Martin refused, gave him the lowly office of exorcist, which he would not refuse (CSEL1 6382-6392). When Hilary came home from exile, Martin went to find him, followed him home, and set up a monastery not far from Hilary's town (NPNF211 965-980). The listener must understand that this is how the monk who later became bishop of Tours (Sulpicius, Life of Martin 9-10; NPNF211 1111 on) first came into the church's service: through Hilary, who saw how to hold a man who thought himself unworthy. Tell it through those acts, in beat 1 and beat 6; let what came of him (item 3) say only that the monastery stood not far from Hilary's town. Do not say Martin came "to learn"; do not say Hilary "ordained him a deacon".

**Ending:**

2. Or his own words from exile, kept for the close only if beat 3 did not use them: he said his writings would speak from his exile (NPNF209 22075). This is a paraphrase, not a fourth quote.

**Who tells it:**
- **Hilary himself**, in On the Councils (358-359), To Constantius (360) and Against Auxentius (364): his own exile, its cause, his learning, Milan. Credited beside beats 2, 3, 4 and 7.
- **The Gallic bishops' letter** (360-361), contemporary: quote 2 and the excommunications in beat 6.
- **Sulpicius Severus**, an Aquitanian, writing about 403, some forty years on, openly on Hilary's side and hostile to Saturninus; also Martin's friend and biographer. Credited beside beats 1, 5 and 6 and quote 3. Most of the outward story of exile and return rests on him and on Hilary's own letters: say so once, inside the sentence that first credits Sulpicius.
- **Jerome**, Chronicle (380-381) and Lives of Illustrious Men (392): the exile to Phrygia by Saturninus's faction, the books, the death at Poitiers.
- **Rufinus** (c. 402), Augustine (c. 400-420), Cassian (430), Socrates (c. 440), Theodoret (c. 447): later readers, credited in what came of him.

**Do not say:**
  - "terrified by threats" for Ariminum. Say: pressed by the emperor's threats (Sulpicius II.43-44); "terrified by threats" is his phrase for the Eastern deputies at Constantinople (NPNF211 11396-11398).
- "Hilary ordained Martin a deacon." Say: Hilary tried to make him a deacon; Martin refused; Hilary made him an exorcist.
- "Martin came to learn from him." Say: Martin sought him out and stayed with him.
- "He was exiled for refusing to condemn Athanasius." Say: Hilary says he was exiled by a faction, on false reports; Sulpicius says the exiles offered to cut off Athanasius if the faith were first examined.
- "Hilary was a member of Saturninus's party" (a mistranslation in the English Jerome). Say: Saturninus's faction had him banished.
- "The emperor pardoned him" or "recalled him." Say, with Sulpicius's Chronicle: he was sent back to Gaul as a troublemaker, his exile not lifted. (Sulpicius's Life of Martin and Gregory of Tours speak of leave to return; if room, say the accounts differ.)
- "He learned Greek in exile." Say: he learned the Eastern bishops' debate. No source says when he learned Greek.
- "On the Trinity explained East and West to each other." Say: On the Trinity was written against the Arians; On the Councils explained the Eastern creeds.
- "Hilary was at Ariminum." He was at Seleucia.
- "The council at Paris deposed Saturninus." Say: the letter cut off Auxentius, Ursacius, Valens and others; Saturninus, Sulpicius says, was cast out of the church.
- "Hilary alone freed Gaul" in the narrator's voice. Keep it in Sulpicius's voice (quote 3).
- "The 'like in substance' bishops were not Arians" in the narrator's voice. Hilary wrote to them as brothers (NPNF209 9850-9861), but Sulpicius counts "like in substance" as an Arian twist (NPNF211 11160-11170). Say: keep "Arian" for the party Hilary opposed, and say Hilary wrote to the "like in substance" bishops as brothers.
- "Hilary carried the Eastern bishops' letters home" as fact. Say: the Gallic bishops wrote of letters the Eastern bishops had given him (one manuscript reading: "sent to him").
- A flat "356" for the exile is allowed; do not add a month or a season.
- No birth year, no age, no wife or daughter, no feelings in exile (homesick, lonely) that the letters do not state. His plea that the bishops remember his exile in their prayers (NPNF209 9905) is the most the record gives; paraphrase it, do not quote it.
- "The emperor Constantius took communion with Auxentius at Milan." Say "the emperor" or "the new emperor". In 364 it was Valentinian (NPNF209 3933-3935), not Constantius.
- "Ligugé" or "the first monastery in Gaul". The name and the date are the editor's (NPNF211 15445-15446). Say "a monastery not far from the town".
- "He died in 367" as a record. Say "in the sixth year after his return" or "about 367".
- "Raised the dead", or the fiery light over Clovis: not in this story.
- "A young man named Martin" or "the young Martin". Say: "Martin, fresh from the army". No source gives his age.
- "Only like the Father's" for the "like in substance" bishops. Say: "his being like the Father's".
- Dropping beat 7 (Milan), the letter's excommunications, or Lucifer's opposite line for length. They are in the heart or are the record's conflict, and are not on Cut first. Cut only from Cut first, in order.
