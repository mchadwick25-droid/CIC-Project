# Outline check: Basil of Caesarea (`basil-of-caesarea`)

## My own account, written before reading `Outline.md`

From the census entry, the slate job line, the source sheet and what Basil is known for.

**The essence.** A Cappadocian teacher of rhetoric who turned to the ascetic life and wrote the rules that taught monks to live together and serve others rather than alone (Longer Rule 7; Or. 43.34, 62). As bishop of Caesarea from 370 he built a "new city" for the poor, the sick and lepers just outside the town (Letter 94; Or. 43.63), and defended the Spirit's place beside the Father and the Son from the church's own prayer (On the Holy Spirit; Letter 159). He is on the tree because his rule is the one later monks were told to keep (Benedict 73; Nicaea II canon 20) and because his case for the Spirit was confessed in 381, after his death.

**Also told.**
- The turn from rhetoric, with its three credited accounts: his own (Letter 223), his brother's for Macrina, Rufinus's for Gregory of Nazianzus (Contested which).
- The life in Pontus and the Rules as answers to questions: community over solitude; "whose feet will you wash?"
- The quarrel with his own bishop, withdrawing rather than splitting the church; the return under Valens's pressure; the famine of 368 and his soup kitchen.
- Bishop against the prefect: confiscation, exile, death refused (Gregory's telling; Sozomen confirms the substance).
- The divided province; the new bishoprics, with Sasima and Nyssa as clauses only.
- The house for the poor and the sick: what it held and whom it served; still bearing his name in Sozomen's day.
- The Spirit: the doxology quarrel, the opponents' view as he reports it, his argument from practice; his reserve over the bare phrase "the Spirit is God" (Gregory defends it).
- Death before 381 (exact date Contested), the city mourning.

**Force and conflict the record holds.** The armed rival bishop on the road; Basil's own use of new sees as a weapon (Gregory's lasting pain); accusers before the governor; the crush at the funeral.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Benedict's century is wrong.** What came of them 1 says "In the next century Benedict of Nursia...". The century before is Rufinus's translation of 397 or 398 (I1), so "the next century" would be the fifth. The census gives Benedict as c. 480-543 (`benedict-of-nursia`, `dates`), and the sheet gives about 150 years after Basil (I2).
Correction: replace "In the next century Benedict of Nursia, closing his own Rule," with "In the sixth century Benedict of Nursia, closing his own Rule,".

**B2. The Connection field claims more than its sources say.** The sources do not say that the Rule "reached Latin readers only through Rufinus's translation". Gennadius (`npnf203...xml` 42466-42471) and Fremantle (44607-44609) say only that Rufinus translated it. Benedict's chapter 73 (Gasquet 4289-4292) does not say which Latin text he read. The field also says that a council "bound monasteries to it". Canon 20 of 787 (`npnf214...xml` 41052-41062) bans new double monasteries and tells only those "already in existence" to keep Basil's rule.
Correction: replace the field's second and third sentences with: "Rufinus translated the Rule into Latin in 397 or 398 (I1). In the sixth century Benedict of Nursia, at the close of his own Rule, set 'the Rule of our holy Father Basil' before his monks as an example (I2). In 787 a council told the double monasteries already standing to keep his rule (I3). The writer must make plain that Rufinus put the Rule into Latin, and that Benedict names it as a model to read, not as the rule his monks kept. Do not say which Latin text Benedict read."

### MINOR

**M1. The date of death.** The outline gives "at the end of 378 or early in 379" in the Essence, the Setting, Ending 1 and Do not say. One held reckoning puts the death a year earlier. Hefele dates the Antioch synod to September 378, "in the ninth month after the death" (`hefele...v2...txt` 17893-17895). Gregory of Nyssa's "eight years after" Basil's election of 370 (Life of Macrina 180) also points to 378.
Correction: in every one of those places, read "in 378 or 379". The Do not say line becomes: "'He died on 1 January 379' → 'in 378 or 379'; the exact date is disputed (D2)."

**M2. Quote 3 comes from a letter, not from the treatise.** Beat 6 puts Quote 3 straight after "At a friend's request he wrote On the Holy Spirit", so a writer could present it as a line from the book. It comes from Letter 159, which the editor places about 373 (`npnf208...xml` 33452-33453).
Correction: in Beat 6, replace "His answer came from what the church already did (Sp5): Quote 3 (Sp6)." with "His answer came from what the church already did (Sp5). In a letter he put it this way: Quote 3 (Sp6)." Add to Do not say: "Quote 3 as words from On the Holy Spirit → 'in a letter' (Letter 159)."

**M3. Opening 1 and Beat 1 tell the waking twice.** Opening 1 gives Quote 1, the waking. Beat 1 then tells it again: "In his own telling, he woke to the gospel".
Correction: add to Beat 1: "If Opening 1 is used, do not tell the waking again. Go straight to 'he read in the gospel of selling goods and sharing with the poor, and went looking for monks'."

**M4. "There he taught rhetoric."** Rufinus says only that Basil taught rhetoric (`eusebius_werke...txt` 29989-29992). Jackson, an editor, places it at Caesarea (`npnf208...xml` 1239-1241).
Correction: in Beat 1, read "He taught rhetoric, the historian Rufinus says (E3)", and drop "There".

**M5. The Setting puts unsourced words in quotation marks.** The Setting quotes "in the heart of Asia Minor, in what is now Turkey" and "near the Black Sea", but neither phrase is in the cited lines. S6 gives only "Cappadocia" and "Turkey", and E7 and R1 do not mention the sea.
Correction: remove the quotation marks and mark both as plain geographic glosses: "Place: Caesarea in Cappadocia, an inland city (S2, S3), in what is now Turkey (S6). Plain gloss, not from a source: in the middle of Asia Minor. His retreat: Pontus, by the river Iris (E7, R1). Plain gloss: near the Black Sea."

**M6. The Spirit part of Also told.** It says he "was attacked for praising God the Father 'with' the Son 'together with' the Spirit". Basil says he was attacked for using both forms, as "novel and at the same time mutually contradictory terms" (Sp1, `npnf208...xml` 8793-8799). Beat 6 has this right, so this is a wording fix to the outline. It does not change the approved heart.
Correction: replace it with "he was attacked for closing prayer in two forms, 'with the Son together with the Spirit' and 'through the Son in the Spirit', as new words that contradicted each other."

**M7. Do not say leaves three openings for error.** Add:
- "In the crush many died" or "tens of thousands" in the narrator's voice → "Gregory says"; give no number (D6, Or. 43.80).
- "Gregory never forgave Basil" or "they were never reconciled" → "a pain, Gregory said in his funeral speech, that time had not healed" (B5). The same speech praises Basil.
- "A council ordered all monasteries to keep Basil's rule" → "the double monasteries already standing" (I3).

**M8. The Length line's glosses do not match Words to explain.** The Length line counts Cappadocia, Pontus and the plain sense of Quote 2. Words to explain lists double monastery and "the other party" instead. The total is still seven either way, and the independent count (646) holds.
Correction: list the same seven words in both places.
