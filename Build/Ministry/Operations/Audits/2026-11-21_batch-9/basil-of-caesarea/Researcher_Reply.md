**Essence.** Basil gave up teaching rhetoric for prayer and shared work. He wrote rules teaching monks to live together and serve, built a great lodging for the poor and sick outside Caesarea, and as bishop from 370 defended the Spirit's place beside the Father and the Son. He died at the end of 378 or early 379, before the council of 381.

**Also told.** Monks live together because love does not seek its own. The house took in travellers, the sick and lepers. He argued the Spirit from baptism and prayer, under threat of death.

**Shape.** Founder or leader. Opens on his waking line; six beats in order; impact; ends on the funeral.

**Length.** 4 minutes, about 600 words (650 at most): about 175 for quotes, glosses and witnesses, 425 for the beats.

**Connection.** Rufinus put the Rule into Latin; Benedict pointed his monks to it; a council in 787 bound monasteries to it.
