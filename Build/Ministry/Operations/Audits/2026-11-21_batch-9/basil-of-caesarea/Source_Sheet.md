# Source sheet: Basil of Caesarea (`basil-of-caesarea`)

Census: `cic-website/data/world-census.json`, entry `basil-of-caesarea` (told within `cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition`, spoken name "Cappadocian Christianity"). No card yet. File paths below are under `cic/texts/` unless given in full. Line numbers are the file's own.

Witness kinds: **own** = Basil's own words; **contemporary** = a witness who knew him or wrote in his lifetime; **later (gap)** = a later writer, with the rough gap after his death (c. 379); **editor** = a modern editor's note, introduction or chronology, never the source's own words.

Main files:
- `npnf208_basil-letters-select-works.xml` (NPNF II/8, tr. Blomfield Jackson, 1895): Basil's Letters, On the Holy Spirit, Hexaemeron; Jackson's chronology and Prolegomena (editor).
- `basil_ascetic-works-longer-shorter-rules_clarke1925.txt` (W. K. L. Clarke, 1925): the Longer and Shorter Rules in English, with Clarke's notes (editor).
- `basil_opera-omnia-pg31-grc-lat_migne1857.txt` (PG 31, Greek, OCR): Greek of the Longer Rules. Greek column legible; the Latin column is garbled (file header, line 7).
- `npnf207_cyril-jerusalem-gregory-nazianzen.xml` (NPNF II/7, tr. Browne and Swallow, 1894): Gregory of Nazianzus, Oration 43, the funeral oration on Basil (contemporary, a close friend, delivered after his death; a eulogy). Greek: `gregory-nazianzen_orationes-27-45-pg36-grc-lat_migne1858.txt`.
- `gregory-nyssa_life-of-macrina_clarke1916.txt`: Gregory of Nyssa, Life of Macrina (contemporary, his brother, written in her honor).
- `npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml`: Socrates and Sozomen (later, c. 60-70 years). Sozomen Greek: `sozomen_historia-ecclesiastica-tomus2-libri-5-9-grc-lat_hussey1860.txt`.
- `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml`: Theodoret (later, c. 70 years), Jerome's Lives of Illustrious Men (later, c. 13 years), Gennadius (later, c. 100 years), Fremantle's notes on Rufinus (editor).
- `eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-rufinus-x-xi-grc-lat_schwartz-mommsen1908.txt`: Rufinus, Church History book 11, Latin (later, c. 23 years).

Not held: the Greek of Basil's Letters and of On the Holy Spirit (PG 32). Only PG 29 and PG 31 of Basil are in the Library. Quotes from the Letters and On the Holy Spirit are rendered from Jackson's English.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

- **D1. Who turned him.** Three held accounts. Basil himself (Letter 223.2, written c. 375 to defend himself against Eustathius) tells of his own waking and of the monks he sought out. His brother Gregory of Nyssa credits their sister Macrina (Life of Macrina 136). Rufinus, about 23 years after his death, credits Gregory of Nazianzus, who took him from the teacher's chair (HE 11.9, 29991-29994). A late moralist repeats Rufinus's version with a scolding line (`eucherius-lyon_de-contemptu-mundi_vaughan1654.txt` 643-647, an English translation of 1654; late, set aside).
- **D2. Date of death.** Jackson and Clarke: 1 January 379. Gwynn (NPNF II/13): end of 378. Hefele dates the Antioch synod, nine months after the death, to September 378, which puts the death a year earlier. Jerome: in Gratian's reign. All agree it was before 381.
- **D3. Who wrote the ascetic rules.** Sozomen reports that some gave them to Eustathius of Sebaste (`npnf202...xml` 31861-31863: "Some assert that he was the author of the ascetic treatises commonly attributed to Basil of Cappadocia."). Clarke's 1925 edition treats them as Basil's. Card item.
- **D4. Who made him bishop.** Socrates says Meletius of Antioch (`npnf202...xml` 13929-13930). Gregory, who was there, says bishops brought from a distance, his own father among them (`npnf207...xml` 42055-42061). Socrates is the later and weaker; story follows Gregory.
- **D5. "The Spirit is God."** Gregory concedes Basil held back the bare phrase in writing and defends it (Or. 43.68-69); Jackson's note says "some have blamed S. Basil for this" (`npnf207...xml` 42948-42950, editor).
- **D6. Valens and the poor-house.** Only Theodoret (c. 70 years later) says Valens gave lands; Jackson's chronology repeats it. Card, not story.
- **D7. The Modestus dialogue** is Gregory's telling in a eulogy; Sozomen gives a shorter version with the same substance (threat of death; Basil's refusal). Credit Gregory.
