Read-only check of `Build/Ministry/Operations/Audits/2026-11-12_who-scan-era9/Who_Scan_Era9.md` (256 rows; row numbers below are the file's line numbers: 13-45 WHO/STORY, 46-213 WANTED, 214-268 LISTED). No repo edits, no paid calls.

Method. Every file key was resolved to its file in cic/texts/ (all resolve). For the 33 WHO/STORY rows and all 168 WANTED rows, every quoted string was matched by script against the cited lines (window of plus or minus 3 lines, OCR-normalised), and every miss was opened by hand. For WANTED, a name pattern per row was searched in all 1,649 cic/texts filenames and in file contents (ripgrep, incl. pre-reform Cyrillic forms for the Russian rows), and in cic/corpus-map/WORKS.yaml and AUTHOR-IDS.yaml (neither has any era 9 person). Earlier-era overlap was grepped in the era 1-7 Who_Scan files and Era8_Carry_Rows.md.

## Rule questions

1. **Pobedonostsev (row 45), STORY on Conybeare alone.** Two rules meet here. "Rulers/officers: Story unless own voice" (era 3) makes him STORY, since no writing of his is held (11 hits in CONY, none his own). Against that, the later-retelling rule (era 4, Vladimir and Olga: "Listed for now") would make him LISTED if Conybeare 1921 counts as a later retelling. My reading: Conybeare is a near-contemporary (his events run to 1903-1905, CONY 10585), not a retelling centuries later. So the officers rule applies and STORY holds, with the single hostile witness marked (CONY 10684, "arch-persecutor"). The file is inconsistent on this, though: row 235 (Ryaboshapka and Ratushny, also Conybeare alone) is LISTED. Ambrose of Sarajevo (row 44) is STORY on Conybeare via Ivanovski. Pick one treatment for "Conybeare alone".
2. **Popes and rulers.** Pius IX, Leo XIII and Gregory XVI (official texts only) as STORY, and Lewicki and Bahouth as STORY, apply "Story unless own voice" correctly. Mazloum as WHO (synodal acts, letters cited and a full life) and Semashko as WHO (a signed petition and report, two witnesses) are defensible under the own-voice and two-witness rules. Gregory V as STORY is right: his MSS and printed Epistles are named in WAL1 15279-15283 but not held. Minh Mang (157) is "STORY type" but WANTED; a ruler with nothing held should be LISTED or WANTED by name of edict. It is fine as is.
3. **Hostile inside writers.** None is labelled OUTSIDE WITNESS (Pelesz on Semashko, Ivanovski, Marnas on Nikolai, the Cornelius tone on Waldenström, Montero on Gomburza): correct. The only label in play is Mackenzie's "candidate", which should go (row 32 above). Tolstoy is rightly not labelled (a polemicist against the church, era 2 line 3).
4. **The recorder rule is applied two ways in this file.** Mouravieff, Mackenzie and Underwood are WHO on a title-page life, but Dallet, Marnas, Pelesz, Montero, Badger, Whitehouse and Philipos are LISTED on the same evidence. Era 2 ("Recorders are Whos ... stays WHO as a judgment call") favours WHO for all of them. Era 6 (Walsingham, Knighton "LISTED until their introductions are read") is the only precedent for LISTED, and it applies only where the introduction was not read.
5. **The Melanchthon re-sort (era 7) was not applied to the WANTED rows that hold an own work.** Carey, Channing, Filaret and Holly each have their own held text plus independent witnesses or a following. Either make them WHO (thin life flagged), or have the coordinator state why the re-sort stops at era 7.
6. **"Later retelling alone" for Cornelius (Laestadius, Rosenius, Waldenström).** Cornelius wrote in 1886, inside the window and inside the same church. Era 4 meant a retelling centuries later (Mouravieff on Vladimir). Laestadius and Rosenius look like WHO on this file's own Dallet standard. Waldenström stays WANTED, because his only teller is hostile and gives one witness.
