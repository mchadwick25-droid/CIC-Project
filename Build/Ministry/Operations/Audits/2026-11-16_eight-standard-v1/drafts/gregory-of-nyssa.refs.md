## (a) Moved to the card

- "But it was his sister who persuaded him to join the family's monastery" (and "Macrina had turned him around once before"). Rests on William Moore and Henry Austin Wilson's Prolegomena, NY 651-655; no ancient text held says it. Card.
- "In 372 Basil made him bishop of Nyssa." The year is the editors' (NY 403, 710; Clarke, CL 50). v1 keeps the fact without the year, from Basil's own Letter 225.
- "In 376 Gregory was banished, until the emperor Valens died in 378." 376 is the editors' (NY 781-785; CL 50). v1 tells the exile in Gregory's own words and Valens's death from Socrates.
- "In 379, after his brother Basil died": Basil's death year is the editors' (CL 52). v1 uses Gregory's own "the ninth month or a little longer" after Basil's death, and opens on 378 (Socrates, by consulate and Olympiad).
- "Gregory defended the Son's eternal generation, and said that in this life we can never know God's name." The editors' summary (NY 185-200). Replaced by Gregory's own words from Against Eunomius.
- "In 381 he brought a treatise against Eunomius to the council." The editors' (NY 864-868). Jerome says only that Gregory read the work to him and Gregory of Nazianzus (npnf203 41712-41714); for the card.
- "Yet a sermon on loving the poor warns the harsh and unmerciful of eternal fire, with no hint that everyone will at last be restored." The sermon is not held; it is known here only as Moore and Wilson cite it (NY 1526-1535), with their remark that hortatory sermons weigh less (NY 1535-1536). Card, What is uncertain (already there).
- "When he was later charged with following Origen, Germanus of Constantinople answered that others had added to his books." Germanus's work is not held; it is the editors' report (NY 1414-1419). Card.
- "the second council of Nicaea called him the Father of Fathers." Rests on an editor's footnote (NY 942, "Council. Nic. II. Act. VI"; NY 458-464); the Nicaea II extracts in SC do not carry the phrase (searched). Card.

The restoration question: the held ancient record gives Gregory's own words and nothing on how they were received. The brief tells his words and does not say he taught that all would be saved. The dispute (sermons against the Great Catechism and the dialogue; Germanus; the later Origenist charge) now rests only on the editors and lives on the card. **Gap for the reviewer:** this stance has no sourced consequence in held files. I did not fill it.

No modern scholar is named in the brief.

## (c) Changes from the bar text

- Opening: the setting now comes first (378, Valens's death, the exiles recalled, Cappadocian Christianity, Asia Minor), then the Macrina scene. Standard v1 opening. "In 379" dropped (editors' year); "some nine months after his brother Basil died" from Gregory.
- Added "When he told her of his exile under Valens", Gregory's own account, which ties the scene to the setting.
- "Macrina had turned him around once before ... But it was his sister who persuaded him to join the family's monastery" removed (editors only).
- "In 372 Basil made him bishop of Nyssa over his protest" becomes "He was made bishop of Nyssa by force, as Basil wrote" (Basil's Letter 225; year to the card).
- "Basil answered that ... the blame lay with those who ordained him, not with a man forced to serve" shortened to "any fault lay with those who ordained him"; the force is already told.
- "In 376 Gregory was banished, until the emperor Valens died in 378" removed; the exile and Valens's death are in the setting.
- Eunomius: "a gulf separated the Father ... Gregory defended the Son's eternal generation, and said that in this life we can never know God's name" becomes Gregory's own words (his letter to Peter; his report of Eunomius, named as his; "is above every name").
- Added his book on the Holy Spirit, in his own words.
- "In 381 he brought a treatise ... to the council, and the emperor named him" becomes the law of 381 itself, with the plain note that it gives no reason for choosing him (Standard v1: the record shows what followed, not why).
- Great Catechism: modern telling kept as the bar has it. "There Christ frees humanity from evil and heals even the one who brought evil in" kept, with the restoration and the thanksgiving of all creation added from the same chapter.
- "In his dialogue on the soul, where Macrina speaks, those who have parted with evil are united with God, who will be all in all" dropped for length (NY 39688-39690 is held, if a reviewer wants it back).
- The sermon on eternal fire and Germanus removed to the card (see (a)).
- Close: "A later council made one of his letters part of church law, and the second council of Nicaea called him the Father of Fathers" becomes Justinian's letter of 553 and the council in Trullo of 692, both ancient texts held. Father of Fathers to the card.

## Stances and what came of them

1. Against Eunomius, the Son's full divinity and God beyond every name; with it the Spirit's equal rank. What came of it: the law of 30 July 381 named Gregory among the bishops whose communion marked the Catholic churches. The law gives no reason, and the brief says so.
2. Hope of restoration and endless growth toward the good, in his own words. No sourced consequence in held files (gap noted in (a)).
3. Church order: his canons. What came of it: the council in Trullo in 692 set its seal on them. In 553 Justinian named him among the Fathers he followed.
