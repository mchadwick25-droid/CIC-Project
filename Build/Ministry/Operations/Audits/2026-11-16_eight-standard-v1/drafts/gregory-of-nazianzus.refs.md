## (a) Moved to the card

All from the Prolegomena of Charles Gordon Browne and James Edward Swallow in NZ (from line 22270), modern translators.

- "so, his translators Browne and Swallow judge, he probably never served there" (Sasima). NZ 22992-22997. The card's What is uncertain already holds the Sasima question with Gregory's two letters (NZ 46434-46437, 47707-47709).
- "Another bishop held the village with troops, and his father begged him to stay home." NZ 22992-22997 (Browne and Swallow). Card.
- "The five orations that earned him the name of the Theologian." The causal link from the five orations to the title is the Prolegomena's (NZ 23130-23132). v1 keeps the title only as an ancient council used it (Justinian, 553).
- "They defend the Trinity against those who blurred the three Persons together and those who split the one substance." The Prolegomena's summary (NZ 23136-23138). v1 replaces it with Gregory's own words from Oration 39.
- "When its president, Meletius of Antioch, died, Gregory took the chair. Egyptian bishops objected, citing a rule from Nicaea against moving a bishop from one see to another, so he gave up the see and went home." The presidency, Meletius's death, the Nicene canon and the return home are the Prolegomena's (NZ 23167-23184). v1 keeps what Socrates and Sozomen both say: bishops objected and he gave up the see. The two differ on when and why (Socrates V.7: at the emperor's arrival, because some murmured he was a stranger, SS 15001-15004; Sozomen VII.7: at the council, the Egyptian bishops, "in strict obedience to the laws of the fathers", SS 40811-40832). For the card's What is uncertain.
- "The councils of Ephesus and Chalcedon later adopted them" (the Cledonius letters). NZ 23227-23229, Prolegomena only. The Chalcedon acts held (SC 19274-19278) cite the "canonical epistles and ectheses of the holy fathers Gregory, Basil, Athanasius" without naming which Gregory or which letters. Card, under What is uncertain or Sources.
- "Gregory's father, the bishop of Nazianzus, ordained him a priest against his will, and Gregory fled to Basil in Pontus before returning." The bar text took this from the Prolegomena (NZ 22844-22857). Gregory's own Oration 1 speaks of "the noble tyranny which I have suffered" (NZ 23456) and the title of Oration 2 of his flight to Pontus (NZ 23578), but v1 drops the episode for length rather than re-source it.
- "He lodged with a relative" (NZ 23122-23125, Prolegomena). Dropped; Sozomen carries the house of prayer.
- "whom he had known as a boy" (NZ 22612-22615, Prolegomena). Dropped.
- "almost by force", "a miserable little village" (NZ 22981-22984, Prolegomena). Dropped.

No modern scholar is named in the brief.

## (c) Changes from the bar text

- Opening: now the setting first (380, Constantinople, the eastern capital; forty years of Arian control; Cappadocian Christianity; Asia Minor), then the Anastasia scene. Standard v1 opening. "In 379 ... came to Constantinople" becomes "In 380", the year Socrates and Sozomen date by consuls; the arrival year 379 rests on the Prolegomena.
- "He lodged with a relative ... named Anastasia, for the faith that was to rise again" becomes Sozomen's account of the house of prayer and his belief about the name, with Sozomen named, since it is his opinion.
- "earned him the name of the Theologian ... defend the Trinity against those who blurred ..." replaced by Gregory's own words from Oration 39 (Sabellius and Arius), glossed with his own following sentence.
- Added the consequence: Theodosius's order and the Arian bishop's departure (Socrates, Sozomen), and what Gregory himself said came of his work, from his farewell oration. Standard v1: what the person believed came of it.
- "The emperor Theodosius gave him the cathedral, and the council of 381 made him archbishop ... Meletius ... took the chair. Egyptian bishops objected, citing a rule ... went home" becomes "Some bishops then objected to Gregory's election, and he gave up the see", the part both ancient historians agree on. The rest moved to the card.
- The friendship paragraph: "whom he had known as a boy and met again" becomes "from their student days in Athens". The hazing story is dropped for length; the "one soul" line is kept.
- The ordination by his father and the flight to Pontus are dropped (Prolegomena source; see (a)).
- Sasima: "almost by force. Gregory was angry. Another bishop held the village with troops, and his father begged him ..., so, his translators Browne and Swallow judge, he probably never served there" becomes Gregory's own letter to Basil refusing to fight for it. The doubt moves to the card.
- "The councils of Ephesus and Chalcedon later adopted them" replaced by Pope Agatho's use of Gregory in 680 on Christ's human will, the consequence the held acts carry.
- "Jerome, who heard him preach, called him his teacher" becomes the quote "my instructor in the Scriptures", with Rufinus's Latin translation added (Gennadius) as the thread west.
- New close: Justinian's letter of 553, naming "Gregory the Theologian" among the Fathers he followed. The bar closed on the Cledonius line; the close now says what he defended and how the church kept it.

## Stances and what came of them

1. The Trinity at Constantinople, against Sabellius's fusion and Arius's division. What came of it: in November 380 the Arian bishop left the churches at Theodosius's order (Socrates, Sozomen); Sozomen believes the Nicene faith rose again through Gregory's sermons; Gregory himself says the city's churches were full again and credits grace. The record does not tie the emperor's order to Gregory's sermons, and the brief does not claim it.
2. Christ's full humanity, against Apollinarius. What came of it: in 680 Pope Agatho cited Gregory to show that Christ had a human will.
3. The thread west and the close: Jerome and Rufinus; in 553 Justinian names him "Gregory the Theologian" among the Fathers he followed.
