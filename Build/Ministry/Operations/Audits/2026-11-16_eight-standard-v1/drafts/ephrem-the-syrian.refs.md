## (a) Moved to the card

Each item rests on John Gwynn's introductory essay (npnf213_gregory-great-ephraim-syrus-aphrahat.xml), a modern editor. None is told in the v1 brief.

- "He learned from its bishop, Jacob." Gwynn, GW 10608-10612 ("a disciple of St. Jacob, Bishop of that city"). Theodoret (Ecclesiastical History II.26, npnf203 11160-11175) puts Ephrem beside Bishop Jacob during a Persian siege, but in a wonder story (the gnats); he does not say Ephrem was Jacob's pupil. For the card's What is uncertain or Sources.
- "Lived through three Persian sieges." Gwynn, GW 10612-10614. Theodoret tells one siege (npnf203 11120-11160). For the card.
- "Its Christians left, and Ephrem settled ... in a cell on a hill outside Edessa." The flight of Nisibis's Christians and the hill are Gwynn's (GW 10614-10620). The v1 brief keeps only the cell (Palladius) and the cession (Gennadius). Barhadbeshabba's account of the move is told in the brief under his name.
- "Bardaisan, a Syrian who had lived some hundred and fifty years earlier." The bar text took this from Gwynn (GW 11184-11186, "about the year 200") and an endnote in npnf203 (15058-15060, "born in 155"). v1 drops the span of years. The Chronicle of Edessa gives his birth on 11 July 154 (chronicle-of-edessa_cowper.txt line 49), if the card wants a date.
- Gwynn's statement that Ephrem himself, in Homily LIII, blames Bardaisan (GW 11237-11240) is now told in the brief from Ephrem's own words as Gwynn prints them (GW 11241-11246), which is a held text of Ephrem, not Gwynn's judgment.
- Palladius "about 420" and Jerome 392 (GW 10656-10657): dates by Gwynn; card only, as before.

No modern scholar is named in the brief.

## (c) Changes from the bar text

- Opening: the famine scene now follows a setting of year (around 372), place and region (Edessa, upper Mesopotamia, Rome's border with Persia), tradition (Syriac Christianity) and what was going on (the cession of Nisibis to Persia). Standard v1 opening.
- "Ephrem left his cell" now carries "a deacon of Edessa from Nisibis or its countryside", from Sozomen and Palladius, so the listener knows who he is in the first scene.
- The death in the cell moved from the close to the end of the famine scene. The close is now the impact, as Standard v1 asks.
- "He had come to Edessa years before, from Nisibis, where he learned from its bishop, Jacob, and lived through three Persian sieges. In 363 ... its Christians left, and Ephrem settled in a cell on a hill": removed. Jacob as teacher, the three sieges, the Christians' flight and the hill rest on Gwynn (moved to the card). The cession of Nisibis is kept from Gennadius.
- "Manichaeans, Marcionites, and the followers of Bardaisan, a Syrian who had lived some hundred and fifty years earlier": now "prose against Mani, Marcion and Bardaisan of Edessa", from the title of his own Prose Refutations. The span of years rested on Gwynn and an editor's note.
- Added Ephrem's own hymn line on the book of Bardaisan ("It tainted my pure ears"), so the stance against Bardaisan is in his own words.
- "the body's death and rebirth" becomes "the body's birth, death and rebirth", to match Sozomen's "generation and destruction of the body, and the regeneration".
- Added, where it arises: in his own homily Ephrem blames Bardaisan himself, against Sozomen's Harmonius. A contested point told in place (pattern, "Disputes in place").
- "wrote hymns that held the church's doctrine and used the same melodies" becomes "learned the same meters and wrote hymns that held the church's teaching", closer to Sozomen ("applied himself to the understanding of the metres of Harmonius").
- Added: his works put into Greek in his lifetime and Basil of Caesarea's admiration (Sozomen). This carries the stance beyond Syria and names a person on the tree.
- "In the day of Theodoret of Cyrrhus" becomes "In the next century, Theodoret of Cyrrhus says".
- Added a second stance with its consequence: Barhadbeshabba's account of Ephrem's school at Edessa and its move with Narsai to Nisibis, the school of the Church of the East. Named as Barhadbeshabba's, since it rests on one later witness from that school.
- "Ephrem ran the relief for a year" kept; "returned to his cell" kept as "died in his cell".

## Stances and what came of them

1. He defended the church's teaching, the resurrection of the body above all, against Bardaisan's followers, with hymns on their own tunes. What came of it: Sozomen says that from then on the Syrians sang Ephrem's songs; his works reached Greek readers and Basil; Theodoret and Jerome attest their use in worship a generation or two later. The causal link is Sozomen's ("From that period").
2. He taught at Edessa. What came of it, in Barhadbeshabba's account about 600: the school grew under his disciples and passed with Narsai to Nisibis, where the schools of the Church of the East spread. One later witness, named.
3. The famine relief is told as personal story. No held source says what came of it beyond the year's relief.
