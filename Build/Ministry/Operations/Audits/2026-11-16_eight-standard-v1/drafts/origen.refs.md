## (a) Moved to the card

- The year 231 rests on McGiffert's note converting Eusebius's "tenth year" of Alexander Severus (E 36329-36333). It is a reign-year conversion, kept in the brief as the opening setting's practice (as Gregory of Tours's "573"); the card's What is uncertain should say the year is the editor's reckoning from Eusebius's regnal year. Likewise "around the year 202" converts "the tenth year of the reign of Severus" (E 32523-32524; McGiffert's note at 32400 gives the edict of 202).
- The Rufinus caveat ("We read On First Principles mostly in Rufinus's Latin, and Jerome said Rufinus changed it") leaves the brief, because the brief no longer quotes the Rufinus line on the Son. It stays on the card's What is uncertain, now with Rufinus's own admission as its ancient witness: in his preface, quoted in his Apology, he says that where he found "erroneous definitions of the Trinity" he had "either left them out as passages which had been falsified or inserted, or else have changed the expression in accordance with the rule of faith" (J3 47040-47046). The souls-before-birth line is still read in his Latin (A4 28653-28655), so the card should keep the caveat.
- Socrates's statement that Athanasius often cited Origen (npnf202 18167-18177) leaves the brief; Athanasius's own words now carry the point. Card, How they're remembered, may keep Socrates, a defender of Origen, as a second witness.
- No modern scholar was named in the bar text; none is named now.

## (b) What the brief draws on (claims told without a named witness, and the named ones)

- Origen's longing for martyrdom, his mother hiding his clothes, the letter and its one sentence: Church History VI.2.3-6, E 32536-32562. Quote "Take heed not to change your mind on our account." E 32562, verbatim.

- Left Alexandria for Caesarea in the tenth year of Alexander: VI.26, E 36329-36333.
- Stretched in the stocks under Decius: VI.39.5, E 37486-37495 (Eusebius, named at the start). Died at Tyre aged sixty-nine: Jerome, Lives 54, J3 40589-40591.
- Learned Hebrew against the habit of his time and people: Jerome, Lives 54, J3 40554-40557. Hebrew text set beside the Greek versions, the Hexapla: Eusebius VI.16, E 34715-34718.
- Marked the Old Testament where the old Greek fell short; Jerome's defense of his Job against critics: Jerome, Preface to Job, J6 47003-47012.
- Scripture has body, soul and spirit; plain sense for the simple, soul for those advancing, spiritual law for the perfect: On First Principles IV, A4 32389-32405. Told without marks (a close telling of the English, not a rendering).
- The Philocalia opens with chapters on how Scripture is read, the first "From the work on 'Principles'": Ph, contents and line 44 (Chap. I heading), chapters I-XIV.
- The Son always with the Father; Athanasius "defending the council of Nicaea" calls on "the labour-loving Origen": Athanasius, De Decretis 27, N4 28647-28651 (quote verbatim; the endnote between "labour-loving" and "Origen" is a note, not text), with Origen's words "as being the likeness of the Father, never was it not" at N4 28680-28691. - Methodius wrote On the Resurrection against Origen: Jerome, Lives 83, J3 41097-41100.
