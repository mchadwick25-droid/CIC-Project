- The tax riot as told in the bar ("John, by then a priest, preached to the frightened crowds, comforting them and urging them to change their lives, until Flavian ... won the emperor's pardon") rested on Philip Schaff's prolegomena (N9 1237-1264), including the year 387 and the count of twenty-one sermons. v1 re-sources the riot and Flavian's embassy to Sozomen VII.23 and John's preaching to his own Homilies on the Statues. The year 387 and Schaff's summary go to the card.
- The years 399, 398, 403, 404, 407 and 438 are printed in the editors' notes of SS, but Socrates dates each by consulate or by its own count (Eutropius's consulship with Theodore; "the 26th of February, under the following consulate"; "the sixteenth consulate of the Emperor Theodosius"). They are told plainly as widely accepted years. 403, 404 and 407 rest on the editors' conversion; no reckoning dispute is known that bears on the story.

## (c) Changes from the bar text

- Opening: the setting first (399, Constantinople, the eastern capital, John of the Antiochene church of Syria, and Eutropius's consulship and law against sanctuary), then the scene under the altar. Standard v1 opening.
- Added Socrates's "first of all eunuchs ... admitted to the dignity of consul", which places the scene in its world.
- Antioch paragraph: added his turn from the law courts and his training under Diodore, who kept to the literal sense (Socrates). This names what he inherited from his tradition.
- The riot: re-sourced from Schaff to Sozomen and John's own Homilies on the Statues; "comforting them and urging them to change their lives" removed (Schaff's summary).
- "Then, in February of 398, John was made bishop of Constantinople, the court's own choice" becomes "In 398 the court had chosen John".
- Palladius on the hospital: added "and built more hospitals" (PD 3773-3776), the consequence of that stance.
- Added Socrates's causal statement: his rebukes of men in office turned the tide against him, and the Eutropius sermon made it worse. This joins the opening scene to his fall, as Standard v1 asks.
- "Socrates says Theophilus used a quarrel over Origen's books against him" dropped for length; Socrates also says no mention of Origen was made at the Oak (SS 18272-18273). Card.
- Oak: "he and his bishops, meeting at the Oak near Chalcedon" makes Theophilus's part plain (Socrates).
- Cucusus: "Palladius says he spent a year there while a great famine struck Armenia, and that John fed the poor more with words than with grain. Then his enemies had him moved on" dropped for length (PD 6290-6298 is held).
- Death: "He died on the road, Palladius says" now carries his last words from Palladius, "Glory to God for all things".
- Close: "Proclus ... carried John's body back ... and John's followers returned to the church" becomes Socrates's account with its cause: Proclus restored him to communion, and by this the followers came back. The close now says what was vindicated.

## Stances and what came of them

1. Plain rebuke of the powerful, the Eutropius sermon among them. What came of it: Socrates says it turned the tide against him; Theophilus's synod at the Oak, the clash with Eudoxia and exile followed.
2. He refused judges who were his enemies. What came of it: deposition, the people's rising and his recall; at last, in 438, Proclus restored him to communion, and by this, Socrates says, his followers came back to the church.
3. Care for the poor. What came of it: the bishop's spending moved to the hospital and more hospitals built (Palladius, partisan); his own sermon on honoring Christ in the naked.
