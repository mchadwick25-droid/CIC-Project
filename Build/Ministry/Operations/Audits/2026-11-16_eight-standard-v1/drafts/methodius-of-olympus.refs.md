## (a) Moved to the card

- William Clark's date "about the year 312" for the martyrdom (AN 29761-29763): out of the brief. The brief says only what Jerome says, "at the end of the last persecution" (J3 41102-41105). Card, What is uncertain: Clark's 312; the J3 header note "Died 311 or 312" (editor's).
- Clark's rejection of the Decius-Valerian date, "since he wrote not only against Origen long after the death of Adamantius" (AN 29767-29769): out of the brief. The brief now leaves out Jerome's second date altogether, as a date dispute that bears on nothing else; the card keeps both of Jerome's dates and Clark's reasoning, naming Clark.
- Clark's "the only complete work ... is his Banquet" (AN 29774-29776): card only; the brief names the Banquet without that claim.
- The opening "Around the year 300" rests on no single statement. Basis: Jerome puts his death at the end of the last persecution (J3 41102-41105), and Eusebius, writing his Apology after Pamphilus's (J3 40975), speaks of Methodius writing against Origen "now" (J3 51488-51489). Card should say the year is approximate and drawn from these.

## (b) What the brief draws on

- Bishop in Lycia, of Olympus: Socrates VI.13, S 18141-18142; Jerome, Lives 83, J3 41095-41097. "In the south of Asia Minor": census `methodius-of-olympus` regions "Asia Minor" (plain geography).
- Tradition: census toldWithin `anatolian-church-third-century`, name "The Anatolian Church in the Third Century".
- Origen dead some fifty years: Origen died under Gallus aged sixty-nine (J3 40589-40591; npnf201 39162-39163).
- Critics and defenders: Methodius against (J3 41097-41100); Pamphilus's and Eusebius's Apologies (J3 40975; J3 51485-51489).
- Socrates's story (attacked, then as if retracting praised Origen in a dialogue, the Xenon) and his "Worthless characters": S 18135-18154.
- Eusebius's complaint, "How could Methodius dare to write now against Origen, after having said this thing and that of his doctrines?", in the sixth book of his Apology, as Jerome quotes it: Jerome, Apology against Rufinus I.11, J3 51484-51490. Told without marks.
- Bishop of Olympus and later of Tyre; martyr at Chalcis at the end of the recent persecution; the Banquet of the ten virgins: J3 41095-41105. Ten women each speak in praise of chastity: AN 29720, 29930-29933, and the ten Discourse headings (AN 29984 onward).
- Origen's teaching that the body was a fetter given after the fall; Methodius: the body is no fetter; man is soul and body joined: Photius, Bibliotheca 234, AN 35864-35879 and 35893. "Chain" is our plain word for "fetter", without marks.
- Fig tree in the temple's stones, torn out so the temple may be preserved: On the Resurrection, AN 35384-35397. Quote "with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed." AN 35400-35402, verbatim (the phrase spans a line break at "same / parts").
- Epiphanius chose to rest on Methodius and set his words out word for word (κατὰ λέξιν), and called him a learned man who fought hard for the truth: Panarion 64.10-12 and 64.63, EpH 32500-32506 and 39240-39241 (Greek); Latin of Petavius at EpO 19228-19237 and 27050-27057. Told without marks, so no rendering. "Decades later": Epiphanius's census dates c. 310-403.
- Answered Porphyry; ably answered; ten thousand lines: Jerome, Letter 70 to Magnus, J6 16535-16545; books Against Porphyry: J3 41097. "That reply is lost" rests on the J6 editor's note at 9759-9760 and AN 29779-29784; card, if the reviewer wants it out of the brief, the sentence reads "Jerome counted it among..." without "lost".
- Theodoret kept a passage of the sermon on martyrs, "the Holy Methodius, bishop and martyr": J3 20517-20526; Methodius named among "bishops and martyrs" whose testimony on the incarnation is set out: J3 20250-20262 (Eranistes). "A century and more later": Theodoret's census dates 393-c. 458.

## (c) Changes from the bar text

1. Opening now gives the year, the region, the tradition and the Origen quarrel first (Standard v1), then the Socrates scene.
2. Eusebius, as Jerome quotes him, added as a second partisan witness to Methodius's change toward Origen; "neither is a neutral witness" now covers both.
3. Clark's 312 and his rejection of the Decius date removed (no modern scholar in the brief); Jerome's second date moved to the card.
4. Epiphanius's praise, which the bar took through Clark's quotation, now comes from Epiphanius's own Panarion, with its consequence: he copied On the Resurrection word for word instead of arguing it himself.
5. New stance: the answer to Porphyry, with Jerome's judgment.
6. Theodoret's passage moved to the close and joined to his naming Methodius among bishops and martyrs.
7. "Epiphanius of Cyprus" now "Epiphanius of Salamis", the census name.
8. The Banquet named in the personal part; no consequence is claimed for it (see notes).

## Notes for the coordinator
