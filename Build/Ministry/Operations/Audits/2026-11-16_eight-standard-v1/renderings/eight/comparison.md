# Comparison of the two renderings, Q1 to Q21

Compared by an independent Opus reader. I checked both renderings against printed.md and against the cic/texts files themselves. I made no model calls and changed no repo file.

Verdicts:
- **same**: the wording differs but the meaning does not.
- **minor**: a nuance differs but no claim changes.
- **changes meaning**: one reading says what the source does not, drops a clause, or settles a contested sense.

"Unchanged" means the printed English is already plain modern English and is kept as printed.

Original-language checks. "In file" means the file shows the form, with the line number. "NOT IN FILE" means a renderer named it from memory and the file does not support it. A claim that is not in the file may still be right, but it cannot go on a card as verified.

## Q1 (Athanasius; Pusey 1872, lines 4567-4574)
- **Printed:** "Begotten not made," (part of the Nicene Creed)
- **A:** Unchanged: "begotten, not made."
- **B:** "begotten, not made."
- **Verdict:** same
- **FINAL:** "begotten, not made." (unchanged; only the modern comma is added)
- **Original check:** The Greek column at lines 4568-4570 is OCR noise. But the same file prints the creed's Greek at **line 1698**: "γεννηθέντα, οὐ ποιηθέντα, ὁμοούσιον τῷ Πατρί". This supports γεννηθέντα οὐ ποιηθέντα in both A and B. A says the Greek "can't be compared". That is true only of lines 4567-4574, not of the file as a whole.

## Q2 (Athanasius; NPNF 2.4, lines 18737-18744)
- **Printed:** "He was made man that we might be made God"
- **A:** "He became human so that we might be made God."
- **B:** "He became human so that we might become God." (B's own note then prefers the passive "be made God")
- **Verdict:** minor. B's "become God" drops the passive of θεοποιηθῶμεν, which marks God as the one who deifies. Both renderers agree that the passive is the stronger and closer form.
- **FINAL:** "He became human so that we might be made God."
- **Card note (uncertain):** "We might be made God" keeps the passive of the Greek: God does the deifying. "We might become God" is a second reading that loses this.
- **Original check:** θεοποιηθῶμεν is in the file (the endnote at 18744). B's ἐνηνθρώπησεν for "was made man" is **NOT IN FILE** at this passage.

## Q3 (Athanasius; Pusey 1872, lines 5393-5394)
- **Printed:** "our blessed and all-celebrated Father Athanasius"
- **A:** "our blessed Father Athanasius, praised by all."
- **B:** "our blessed and highly honoured Father Athanasius."
- **Verdict:** minor. B's "highly honoured" loses the "all" (παν-) in the Greek word, so it names the degree of honour, not how widely it is given.
- **FINAL:** "our blessed Father Athanasius, praised by all."
- **Card note (uncertain):** "All-celebrated" means praised by everyone. "Highly honoured" is a weaker second reading.
- **Original check:** The Greek is in the file at **line 3923**: "τοῦ μακαρίου καὶ πανευφήμου πατρὸς ἡμῶν Ἀθανασίου". This supports A's πανεύφημος and B's πανευφήμου. A says the Greek "isn't shown" and is "not verified". In fact it is shown, 1,470 lines earlier.

## Q6 (Constantine; NPNF 2.2, lines 2912-2919)
- **Printed:** "of the same essence" (the editor's endnote gloss on ὁμοούσιον; the creed text itself says "consubstantial")
- **A:** Unchanged: "of the same essence,"
- **B:** "of one being with the Father," (B also offers "of the same essence")
- **Verdict:** minor. B's main reading adds "with the Father", which is not in the span. It also swaps in the modern liturgical formula. The doctrine stays the same.
- **FINAL:** "of the same essence," (unchanged)
- **Card note (uncertain):** "Of the same essence" is the printed gloss on ὁμοούσιον. "Of one being" is a second, liturgical reading of the same word.
- **Original check:** ὁμοούσιον is in the file (endnote 169, unaccented "ομοουσιον"). B's contrast with ὁμοιούσιος is **NOT IN FILE** at this passage. It is standard background, not a claim the file supports.
- **Attribution flag:** These words are not Constantine's, and not the creed's. They are the 19th-century translator's footnote. If the brief presents them as Constantine's quotation, that is a misattribution. The creed's own word at line 2914 is "consubstantial".

## Q7 (Constantine; NPNF 2.2, lines 30670-30677)
- **Printed:** "the first of the emperors who began to be zealous for the Church, and to bestow upon her high benefactions."
- **A:** Unchanged: "the first of the emperors who began to be zealous for the Church." (A also offers a plainer "the first emperor who began to show zeal for the Church.")
- **B:** "the first of the emperors to become zealous for the Church."
- **Verdict:** same
- **FINAL:** "the first of the emperors who began to be zealous for the Church" (unchanged)
- **Span flag:** In the printed text the sentence goes on: "and to bestow upon her high benefactions." The brief's full stop after "Church" makes a coordinated clause look like the end of the sentence. End the quotation with an ellipsis or no full stop, or include the clause.
- **Original check:** Neither renderer names any Greek. The file shows none.

## Q8 (Ephrem; Palladius, Lausiac History, Clarke 1918, lines 464-471)
- **Printed:** "See, on your behalf I appoint myself hospitaller."
- **A:** "Look, I appoint myself, on your behalf, to run the shelter."
- **B:** "Look, I will take this on for you myself. I will run the hostel." As one sentence: "Look, for your sake I am making myself the keeper of the guesthouse."
- **Verdict:** changes meaning. The two readings settle "on your behalf" differently. A reads it as "acting for you, as your agent", while B's one-sentence form reads it as "for your sake". B's "take this on" also drops the formal act of appointing himself. Its "hostel" carries a modern sense (cheap lodging for travellers) that misleads, since this was famine relief with beds and nursing.
- **FINAL:** "Look, on your behalf I appoint myself to run the shelter." ("On your behalf" is already modern English and keeps both senses open.)
- **Card note (uncertain):** "On your behalf" may mean "as your agent, with your money" or "for your sake". "Hospitaller" means one who runs a house of care for strangers, the poor and the sick, so readings include "shelter" and "guesthouse".
- **Original check:** ξενοδόχος (A and B) is **NOT IN FILE**. The file is Clarke's English only, with no Greek.

## Q11 (Gregory of Nazianzus; NPNF 2.7, lines 45777-45784)
- **Printed:** "Why should I fight for sucking pigs and fowls, and those not my own,"
- **A:** "Why should I fight over piglets and chickens, and ones that are not even mine,"
- **B:** "Why should I fight over piglets and chickens, and not even my own,"
- **Verdict:** same. Both add "even" for tone. A flags this, and the FINAL drops it.
- **FINAL:** "Why should I fight over piglets and chickens, and ones that are not my own,"
- **Original check:** No Greek is named. Both renderers mention road tolls and revenues. The printed lines show only the seized mules and the guarded pass, so the tolls are context from memory, not from these lines.

## Q12 (Gregory of Nazianzus; NPNF 2.7, lines 45778-45785)
- **Printed:** "as though for souls and canons?"
- **A:** "as if they were souls and church canons?"
- **B:** "as if I were fighting for souls and church law?" (B also offers "as though they were souls and canons?")
- **Verdict:** minor. The printed text means "as though I were fighting for souls and canons". A's "as if they were souls" makes the animals stand in for souls, which is a slightly different comparison. A's "church canons" keeps the technical term, and B's "church law" generalises it.
- **FINAL:** "as if I were fighting for souls and church canons?"
- **Card note (uncertain):** "Canons" may mean church rules or a bishop's canonical rights of jurisdiction, which were at stake in the quarrel. Both readings are kept.
- **Original check:** B's κανόνων is **NOT IN FILE**.

## Q13 (Gregory of Nazianzus; NPNF 2.7, lines 37401-37408)
- **Printed:** "equally far from the confusion of Sabellius and from the division of Arius,"
- **A:** "equally far from Sabellius's merging and from Arius's dividing," (A also offers a fuller form that adds "God")
- **B:** "keeping equally far from Sabellius, who merges the Persons into one, and from Arius, who splits them apart," (B's closer form: "staying equally far from the merging of Sabellius and from the splitting of Arius,")
- **Verdict:** minor. B's main reading adds "the Persons" and a gloss of each error, and its "splits them apart" leaves out the inequality that Gregory's next sentence names ("cut Him up into inequality"). The closer forms of A and B agree. Both rightly take "confusion" in its technical sense of fusing.
- **FINAL:** "equally far from the merging of Sabellius and from the division of Arius,"
- **Card note (uncertain):** "Confusion" here means fusing Father, Son and Spirit into one, not bewilderment. "Division" means splitting God into unequal parts.
- **Original check:** σύγχυσις and διαίρεσις (A and B) are **NOT IN FILE**. The Greek terms that the nearby notes do print are οὐσία, ὑπόστασις and πρόσωπον (lines 37382-37397).

## Q15 (Gregory of Nyssa; NPNF 2.5, lines 2781-2788)
- **Printed:** "I received the legacy of Eunomius' controversy,"
- **A:** "inherited the dispute with Eunomius."
- **B:** "and the dispute with Eunomius was handed down to me." (active form: "and I took over the dispute with Eunomius as my inheritance,")
- **Verdict:** minor. Both read "Eunomius' controversy" as the dispute against Eunomius, which the context (Basil's death) supports. B's main reading turns Gregory's active "I received" into a passive and adds "and".
- **FINAL:** "inherited the controversy with Eunomius."
- **Card note (uncertain):** "Eunomius' controversy" means the dispute against Eunomius that Basil left unfinished. This is read from the context. The English alone could mean a dispute that belonged to Eunomius.
- **Original check:** No Greek is named, and the file shows none.

## Q18 (John Chrysostom; NPNF 1.9, lines 1990-1997)

## Q19 (Methodius; ANF 6, lines 35395-35402)
- **Printed:** "with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed."
- **A:** "with the same parts, unharmed and immortal, while sin is completely and entirely destroyed."
- **B:** "with the same parts, unharmed and immortal, while sin is completely destroyed."
- **Verdict:** minor. B drops the doubled intensifier ("and entirely"). The claim stays the same, but the emphasis weakens.
- **FINAL:** "with the same parts, uninjured and immortal, while sin is utterly and entirely destroyed." (unchanged; every word is current English)
- **Card note (uncertain):** The printed text doubles the emphasis ("utterly and entirely"). One reading keeps the doubling, and the other has the plain "completely destroyed".
- **Original check:** B's μέλη is **NOT IN FILE**, and so is its statement that the passage survives in Greek through Epiphanius. The only note here is a Bible reference (Deut. 32:39).
