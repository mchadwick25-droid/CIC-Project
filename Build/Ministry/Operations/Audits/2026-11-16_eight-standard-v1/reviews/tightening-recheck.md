## origen

- **BLOCKING (broken reference).** "The Philocalia, a book of passages chosen from him, opens with those chapters." The cut of "on how Scripture should be read" leaves "those chapters" pointing at nothing. The sentence before it names a teaching, not chapters. **Fix:** restore the before wording, "opens with those chapters on how Scripture should be read."
- **Check 1.** The ordination and Demetrius's attack were cut, so the move to Caesarea now has no reason given. Neither was a stance of Origen's, and the brief holds without them.
- **Sentences that don't serve:** 0.

## methodius-of-olympus

- **NOTE (does not serve).** "He also wrote a Banquet of the Ten Virgins, in which ten women in turn praise the chaste life." It carries no stance with a consequence and leads nowhere. The writer kept it knowingly. **Fix:** cut it to the card's key works. That would bring a thin-record brief closer to Methodius's old 240-word model.
- **NOTE (join).** "Both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness." The cut of "Socrates called Origen's critics worthless" removed the only shown ground for Socrates's leaning. The claim is still sourced in the refs, so this is not a new claim. The listener now takes the leaning on trust. Acceptable as it stands.
- **Sentences that don't serve:** 1.

## constantine-the-great

- **Sentences that don't serve:** 0.

## athanasius-of-alexandria

- **BLOCKING (reference to something cut).** The opening clause "ten years after the council of Nicaea condemned him" was cut, and it was the only place Nicaea was introduced as a council. Now "He had been a deacon at Nicaea" and "Nicaea answered that the Son is ..." name a place that answers. **Fix:** "He had been a deacon at the council of Nicaea, where ..." This is the before's own wording and adds no claim.
- **NOTE (pronoun after a rejoin).** "Gregory of Nazianzus, praising him in a sermon, says that ... so bound them together. He also set before the emperor a written confession ..." The main subject of the sentence before is now Gregory, so "He" can be heard as Gregory. **Fix:** "Athanasius also set before the emperor ..."
- **NOTE (slight shift).** "though some said it was because he would hold no communion with Arius." Socrates (I.35, S 5368-5371) says the emperor acted "with a view to the establishment of unity in the church, since Athanasius was inexorable in his refusal to hold any communion with Arius." The emperor's aim (unity) has gone, and the refusal now reads as the cause. **Fix:** restore "though some said it was for the unity of the church, since he would hold no communion with Arius."
- **NOTE (checklist item 8).** "Constantine" appears without "the Great" at first mention. Other Constantines bear on Athanasius's exiles. **Fix:** restore "Constantine the Great".
- **Sentences that don't serve:** 0.

## ephrem-the-syrian

- **NOTE (lost witness to a kept consequence).** Cut: "Jerome wrote that some churches read his writings aloud after the Scriptures." The refs (line 64) count Theodoret and Jerome together as witnesses to one consequence, use in worship, and Theodoret still carries it. Jerome's line is the stronger of the two, since it is reading in church next to Scripture, and it costs 14 words. The writer flagged this cut. **Recommend restoring it**, as the before sentence, at the end of that paragraph.
- **Check 4.** The joins are sound. "Nisibis, by then inside Persia" still reads correctly without the Jovian clause.
- **Sentences that don't serve:** 0.

## gregory-of-nazianzus

- **NOTE (lost witness to a kept consequence).** Cut: "and Rufinus of Aquileia put his works into Latin". The refs (lines 59 and 70) treat Jerome and Rufinus together as "the thread west", and Jerome still carries it. Rufinus is the textual route, a different kind of reception from Jerome's personal debt. This is Mark's call, not a defect.
- **NOTE (rough join).** "Jerome called him "my teacher in the Scriptures," and in 553 the emperor Justinian told a council at Constantinople that he followed the holy Fathers, and among them he named Gregory the Theologian." That is 33 words joining two unrelated facts 150 years apart. **Fix:** split it back at "Scriptures," into two sentences, as in the before text. The quotation keeps its comma.
- **Sentences that don't serve:** 0.

## gregory-of-nyssa

## benedict-of-nursia

- **NOTE (rough join).** "Gregory, by then pope, praised the Rule as "outstanding for its sound judgment and clear in its language," and in 742 the first council of Carloman, duke of the Franks, told monks and nuns to live by Benedict's Rule." That is 39 words joining Gregory's praise to a council some 150 years later with a bare "and". **Fix:** end Gregory's sentence after the quotation, with the quotation mark and a full stop. Then begin "In 742 the first council of Carloman, ...". The quotation's trailing comma sits inside the quote, so check that ending on it reads correctly, or keep the comma and split anyway.
