## How this list was made

- Rows: every row with the verdict WANTED in the tables of `Who_Scan_Era1.md` to `Who_Scan_Era10.md` and `Who_Scan_Supplement.md`, read by script (464 rows). A person carried between eras (al-Tabari and Ibn Ishaq, Adam of Bremen, Nestor the chronicler, Herman of Alaska) counts once, in the era whose audit scores them. Era is the audit's era.
- Re-run first (coordinator's ruling): every row was searched again across `cic/texts/` with whitespace-tolerant patterns, because many OCR files print names with double spaces. The findings are in `Rerun_Findings.md`. One row proved held and is dropped (Charles Simeon, era 9).
- Merged: rows that want the same source for two people are one row, with both names.
- Major: the audits' "Major?" mark (yes first inside each era). The audits of eras 1 to 5 marked only WHO rows, and a few later rows carry a conditional note ("yes if ruled WHO", "judgment call"). For those 175 rows a Sonnet pass proposed yes or no by the audits' own test (the person anchors or founded a tradition, or is the figure the census most ties to it), at most about one in five yes. A mark that reads "(proposed here)" is that proposal and not an audit mark; Mark has not seen it. The reasons are in `Major_Proposals.md`.
- "Would move to": taken from the audit row's own wording (Route and ruling cell). Where the row does not say, the cell reads "inferred" and gives the rule's default (a ruler or pope is STORY; anyone else WHO). The Library thread should treat inferred cells as a guess.
- Source names are as the audits wrote them, many from the workers' memory and tagged "[suggested; to confirm]" there. Confirm every edition before acquiring.
- Every row ends with its audit row (`file:line`). `trace_check.py` in this folder checks that each row's person is named on that line.
