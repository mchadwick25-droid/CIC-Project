### B2. "He corrected his clergy sharply and put some of them out of the church"

Listen_FirstTime.md, section 4: "a. "He corrected his clergy sharply and put some of them out of the church" — corrected them for what? An act of punishment with no reason. Change: add the reason the outline gives, or keep as is only if the sources give none."

Listen_FirstTime.md, section 4, lead-in: "The reason may be absent from the old sources; I cannot see them. If the trust read finds no reason in the sources, each stands as told and the finding drops."

Trust.md, the line for this sentence: "6. **(12)** "He corrected his clergy sharply and put some of them out of the church, and many came to hate him." SS 17262-17267: "designing to correct the morals of the clergy ... disliked by them ... others became his bitter enemies". Sozomen, SS 42798-42799: "He also ejected some of the clergy from the Church". Socrates says "expelled many of them" (SS 17273). "Some" stands on Sozomen and does not soften the record, since the hatred is kept in the same sentence."

### M8. Machine-sounding lines (Listen_Skeptic.md, 6b; counted, 0 blocking)

1. "He preached plainly about the poor, and his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile." This has the balanced thesis-sentence cadence of a summary paragraph. It is allowed as the essence, but it is the most generated-sounding line in the piece.
2. "His sermons were read for centuries…" This is a stock impact claim. Nothing in the story shows a later reader. Keep it only if the outline holds it.
3. "…and those rebukes made him powerful enemies." "Made powerful enemies" is a cliché, and it stands in for content (see 4).
4. "Both kinds were kept and read." A tidy short sentence that closes off an aside (see 6).
5. The last sentence echoes the essence almost word for word ("those who had left…came back"). That bookend symmetry is a machine habit. It is not a fault under Part 1, so no change is required.
