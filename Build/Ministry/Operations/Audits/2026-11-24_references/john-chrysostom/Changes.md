# Changes: John Chrysostom (`john-chrysostom`)

Revision of the live brief against Kit Part 1, `kinds/person.md` and `Outline.md`. The live text before this pass is kept unedited as `john-chrysostom.brief.v0.txt`. Each entry gives the old sentence, the new one, the outline line it stands on, and the rule broken or beat carried. "Kept" sentences are not listed.

Length: the draft with Cut first items 1-14 ran to 770 words; items 15, 16 and 17 were then dropped in order. The text stands at 690 by the scorer (about 4:36 at 150 a minute), above the outline's 650 and the kind kit's 675. The Cut first list is used up; see the last section.

## Opening

1. Cut: "In 399 the bishop of Constantinople, the eastern capital, was John, a preacher from Antioch."
   Outline: Openings 1 (line 69). Rule: Part 1 opens on one moment in about three sentences; this was a set-up sentence ahead of the moment, and "the eastern capital" is not the outline's wording. The year moves into the fall of Eutropius; the name comes in at the pulpit.
2. Before: "Not long before, the emperor's chief eunuch, Eutropius, had a law passed that stripped refugees of the right to shelter in churches."
   After: "The emperor's chief eunuch, Eutropius, had a law passed that stripped people who fled into churches of the right to shelter there."
   Outline: Openings 1; sanctuary gloss (line 49). Rule: "refugees" carries a modern sense (people fleeing a country) that misleads; "Not long before" hung on the cut sentence. "Chief eunuch" kept (Socrates, Source_Sheet line 80).
3. Before: "Then he fell from favor and fled into the cathedral."
   After: "Soon after, in 399, he fell from favor and fled into the cathedral of Constantinople himself."
   Outline: Openings 1; Setting (spoken "in 399"). Beat: carries the year and city from the cut sentence.
4. Before: "As he lay trembling under the altar, John climbed into the pulpit and preached against him."
   After: "As he lay trembling under the altar, the bishop, John Chrysostom, climbed into the pulpit and preached against him."
   Outline: Openings 1 ("The bishop, John Chrysostom"). Beat: brings the name in at the moment.
5. Before: "Socrates of Constantinople says some thought John added insult to cruelty."
   After: "Some thought he had added insult to cruelty."
   Outline: Openings 1; Do not say ("Socrates says" anywhere but Beats 4 and 5).
6. Before: "John said he spoke to soften the people's hearts toward him, for the church, like a mother, had hidden her enemy under her cloak."
   After: "John said he spoke to soften the people's hearts, for the church had sheltered its enemy: \"When everyone ignored him in his desolation, she alone, like a loving mother, hid him behind her curtains. She stood against the emperor's anger, the people's fury and unbearable hatred.\""
   Outline: Openings 1; Q1 (lines 89-91). Rule: "cloak" is a detail the source does not hold (curtains before the altar); Q1 now carries John's own reason, with the church named first.

## Essence

7. Added: "His sermons were read for centuries, and the followers who left over his fall came back only when his body came home."
   Outline: The essence (line 5), in Cut first 14's shortened form (line 31). Beat: what he changed, which the kind kit's essence requires.

## Antioch

8. Before: "At Antioch John had trained under Diodore, who kept to the literal sense of Scripture."
   After (merged with the new Antioch line): "John was born at Antioch, in Syria, and as a priest there for twelve years he preached on what the rich owed the poor."
   Outline: Beat 1 (line 73); Cut first 17 (line 38) sends the Diodore clause to the card. Rule: "the literal sense" was unexplained (Explain it or leave it out); the beat adds birthplace and the twelve years.
9. Before: "In a sermon on Matthew, he told his listeners that if they wanted to honor the body of Christ, they must not neglect Him when He is naked."
   After: "They honored the bread of Communion on the altar with gold cups and silk, and in a sermon on Matthew he told them to honor Christ in the poor first: \"Do you want to honor Christ's body? Do not neglect him when he is naked. While you honor him here with silk clothes, do not neglect him outside, dying of cold and nakedness.\""
   Outline: Beat 1; Q2 (lines 92-93); Words to explain, Christ's body (line 51); Do not say, gold cups (line 157). Rule: the old line paraphrased Q2 without the plain sense of "the body of Christ"; the quote now stands word for word, with its gloss, and "first" as the Do not say line asks.

## Bishop

10. Before: "In 398 Eutropius made Theophilus of Alexandria ordain him."
    After: "In 398 the court chose him as bishop of Constantinople, and Eutropius made Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, ordain him."
    Outline: Beat 2 (line 74); Names ("Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria"). Beat: the court's choice; the threat goes to the card (Cut first 9).
11. Before: "Palladius, who wrote John's life and took his side, says he moved the bishop's extravagant spending to the hospital."
    After: "As bishop he cut the bishop's own spending and gave the money to the hospital."
    Outline: Beat 2; Who tells it, the Dialogue (line 135: the hospital told plainly, without its judgment words, Palladius credited once in Beat 7); Do not say, "extravagance". Rule: judgment word and an extra spoken credit.
12. Added: "He corrected his clergy sharply and put some of them out of the church, and many came to hate him."
    Outline: Beat 2 ("the record's force on his own side: it stays"). Rule: the old text softened the record by leaving this out (fourth fault).

    Outline: Cut first 16 (to the card). Length.

## The Oak

17. Before: "He and his bishops, meeting at the Oak near Chalcedon, cited John to appear."
    After: "Theophilus and his bishops met at a place called the Oak, outside Chalcedon, and summoned John."
    Outline: Beat 5 (line 77); Setting, spoken place (line 44). Rule: "cited" is legal jargon; "the Oak" needed its gloss; "Theophilus" named since the paragraph before now ends on him with "he" ambiguous.
18. Before: "When John refused them as his enemies, they deposed him, Socrates says, for refusing."
    After: "John refused to come before judges he called his enemies, and asked for a council of the whole church. The accounts differ. Socrates says they removed him from office only for refusing to come. The meeting's own minutes, as a later patriarch summed them up, say some charges were heard, and then all forty-five bishops voted to remove him."
    Outline: Beat 5; Who tells it (line 134); Do not say ("They deposed him for refusing" as one account only). Rule: first fault, one account where the old sources differ; the general council request added. "Synod" became "the meeting", so no gloss is needed.
19. Before: "The people rose, and he was recalled."
    After: "The emperor ordered him into exile, and John gave himself up, but he was soon called back."
    Outline: Beat 5 (SS 18302-18307, 18341-18365); Cut first 3 (night watch to the card); Leave out, the causes of the recall. Rule: "The people rose" set a cause the old sources differ on.

## The statue and the second council

    After: "Public games were held at a silver statue of the empress Eudoxia, just across the street from the cathedral."
    Outline: Beat 6 (line 78). Rule: detail placed wrong (third fault).
21. Before: "John mocked those who allowed them, and she took this as aimed at her."
    After: "John mocked those who allowed them, calling the games an insult to the church, and the empress took this as aimed at her and sought another council."
    Outline: Beat 6; Do not say (Eudoxia only sought another council). Rule: the mockery's reason (Explain it); "she" would be heard as the church after Q1.
22. Before: "A second council condemned him for taking back his see without a council's leave, and in 404 the emperor sent him into exile."
    After: "It condemned him for taking back his place as bishop, after his removal, without a council's leave." ... "In 404 he was taken into exile by force."
    Outline: Beat 6 (SS 18467-18472, 18496-18502). Rule: "see" unexplained; the outline's wording for the exile.
23. Added: "Since two councils had condemned him, the emperor barred him from the church, and John was silenced." and "His followers kept Easter in the public baths, and from then on they met apart from the church."
    Outline: Beat 6 (SS 18486-18496); Words to explain, Johannites; Connection (line 112). Beat: the split that the ending resolves.

## Exile and death

24. Added: "He was first exiled to Armenia."
    Outline: Beat 7 (line 79); Cut first 12 kept form.
25. Before: "Palladius says soldiers hurried him toward Pityus, hoping he would die."
    After: "Palladius says bishops in Syria, seeing the church of Antioch still turning to John, had him moved to Pityus, a more distant place on the Black Sea. In his telling, the soldiers hurried John on, saying they had been told his death would bring them promotion."
    Outline: Beat 7; Cut first 5 and 12 (Palladius's reason stays); Do not say (soldiers). Rule: "hoping he would die" went past what Palladius says; the move needed its reason.
26. Before: "He died on the way in 407, with his usual words, \"Glory to God for all things.\""

    Outline: Beat 7 (PALL 6439-6499; SS 18609-18612); Setting ("near Comana"). Beat: the place and the last acts.
