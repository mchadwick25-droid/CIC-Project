## 1. The test

- **Who:** filled. A preacher at Antioch who became bishop of Constantinople.
- **What happened:** filled. He made enemies, two councils removed him, he was exiled and died on the way, and his body came back.
- **What it was about:** filled. His preaching on the poor, the sanctuary sermon, and his quarrels with Theophilus and the empress.
- **Why it matters (person: what they did, what it changed, why on the tree):** filled, if thinly. I can say he spoke for the poor and rebuked the powerful, paid with exile, and that his followers split off and came back when his body did. "His sermons were read for centuries" claims the impact but never shows it (see 6b).

## 2. The opening

After three sentences I know who (Eutropius, then John Chrysostom by name), where (the cathedral of Constantinople), when (399) and why there (Eutropius fell from favor and fled to the church). Nothing is missing. John's reason for preaching comes in sentences 4 and 5, which is soon enough.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

- **BLOCKING:** "Socrates says Theophilus also held a grudge…" (and again "Socrates says they removed him…"). A listener with no church background hears the Greek philosopher. Nothing says this is a church historian. That misleads.
  **Change:** at the first mention, give his role as the outline gives it, e.g. "Socrates, a church historian [of the next generation, if the outline holds it], says…".
- MINOR: "Palladius says bishops in Syria…" Palladius is never introduced. **Change:** give his role as the outline names it (for example, the bishop who wrote John's life) at the first mention.
- MINOR: "Some thought he had added insult to cruelty." The pun on "insult to injury" makes me stop and work out whose cruelty is meant. I lose a beat. **Change:** say it plainly, e.g. "Some thought he was attacking a man already brought low," if the outline holds that sense.
- MINOR: "The meeting's own minutes, as a later patriarch summed them up, say some charges were heard…" The nested clause about where the minutes come from loses me on one hearing. **Change:** "A later patriarch's summary of the meeting's own minutes says some charges were heard, and then all forty-five bishops voted to remove him."

## 4. Bare statements

  **Change:** say what he rebuked them for, in one clause from the outline. If the outline gives no content, cut the sentence and join "Socrates says Theophilus also held a grudge…" to the paragraph before.

## 6. Part 1 tells

- **BLOCKING (an event out of order):** "He published some of his sermons, and shorthand writers took down others as he spoke. Both kinds were kept and read." This comes after his death in 407 and jumps back into his working life, between the death and the return of his body. Under the bar, a jump back after the essence is a failure on one hearing. It also reads as a how-we-know aside dropped in before the ending.
  **Change:** cut the paragraph. Or move it, unchanged, to follow the Antioch paragraph or the start of his time as bishop, wherever the outline places it.
- No "not X but Y", no dash, no question asked only to answer it, no stock words, and no moral close. The ending (his body laid in the Church of the Apostles, his followers coming back) is an event, not a lesson.

### 6b. Lines that sound machine-made

1. "He preached plainly about the poor, and his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile." This has the balanced thesis-sentence cadence of a summary paragraph. It is allowed as the essence, but it is the most generated-sounding line in the piece.
2. "His sermons were read for centuries…" This is a stock impact claim. Nothing in the story shows a later reader. Keep it only if the outline holds it.
3. "…and those rebukes made him powerful enemies." "Made powerful enemies" is a cliché, and it stands in for content (see 4).
4. "Both kinds were kept and read." A tidy short sentence that closes off an aside (see 6).
5. The last sentence echoes the essence almost word for word ("those who had left…came back"). That bookend symmetry is a machine habit. It is not a fault under Part 1, so no change is required.
