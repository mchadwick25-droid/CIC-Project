## Sentences marked SUMMARY HOLDS

1. **(5)** "John said he spoke to soften the people's hearts, for the church had sheltered its enemy: [Q1]". N109 17699-17700: "from a desire to soften your minds towards him, and to induce you to compassion"; 17718-17719: "having received her enemy as a captive, she spares him". The "for" fairly joins his reason to his answer to those indignant "because he who continually made war upon the Church has taken refuge within it" (17708-17709). **Q1 against the Greek** (PG52 823-827): "πάντων αὐτὸν ἐν ἐρημίᾳ παριδόντων, μόνη καθάπερ μήτηρ φιλόστοργος, ὑπὸ τὰ παραπετάσματα αὑτῆς ἔκρυψε, καὶ πρὸς βασιλικὴν ὀργὴν ἔστη, πρὸς δήμου θυμὸν, καὶ πρὸς μῖσος ἀφόρητον." Every clause is there and nothing is added. "Behind" for ὑπὸ fits the altar curtains (N109 17724-17725).
2. **(6)** The essence. SS 17319-17321: "when he proceeded to rebuke many of those in public office also with immoderate vehemence, the tide of unpopularity began to set against him". SS 18419-18421 is the chapter title: "On account of it John is exiled a Second Time". The essence keeps the poor and the exile apart, as the Do not say list asks.
3. **(7)** "His sermons were read for centuries, and the followers who left over his fall came back only when his body came home." The sermons: SS 17303-17308 ("must read for themselves"), AUGC 880 ("Chrysostom says..."), CALV1 18508-18509. The followers: SS 21459-21469, "Proclus the bishop brought back to the Church those who had separated themselves from it on account of Bishop John's deposition ... By this means the admirers of that prelate were conciliated". On "only": Atticus earlier put John's name in the prayers "by which means he trusted that many would be induced to return" (SS 20199-20205). Socrates gives no result for that, and he tells the reconciliation under Proclus. "Only" is a fair compression. It is not an addition.
4. **(8)** "John was born at Antioch, in Syria, and as a priest there for twelve years he preached on what the rich owed the poor." SS 17130: "a native of Antioch in Syria-Cœle". PALL 3442-3443: "For twelve years he was a shining light in the Church of Antioch". The Matthew homilies were preached at Antioch (N110 5301-5303, "our city first crowned itself with the name of Christian").
5. **(9)** "They honored the bread of Communion ... with gold cups and silk ... he told them to honor Christ in the poor first: [Q2]". N110 29913-29914: "we offer for this table a gold and jewelled cup". N110 29952-29954: "not forbidding such offerings ... but requiring you, together with them, and before them, to give alms". **Q2 against Prevost** (N110 29931-29934): "Wouldest thou do honor to Christ's body? Neglect Him not when naked; do not while here thou honorest Him with silken garments, neglect Him perishing without of cold and nakedness." The rendering is faithful.
6. **(12)** "He corrected his clergy sharply and put some of them out of the church, and many came to hate him." SS 17262-17267: "designing to correct the morals of the clergy ... disliked by them ... others became his bitter enemies". Sozomen, SS 42798-42799: "He also ejected some of the clergy from the Church". Socrates says "expelled many of them" (SS 17273). "Some" stands on Sozomen and does not soften the record, since the hatred is kept in the same sentence.
7. **(14)** "Socrates says Theophilus also held a grudge, since his own choice for bishop had lost out to John, and that he resolved to unseat him." SS 17374-17381: "Theophilus ... was plotting his overthrow ... not so much the boldness ... as his own failure to place his favorite presbyter Isidore in the episcopal chair". Socrates makes the grudge weigh more than the rebukes, and "also" is a little weaker than that. The credit and the content are both intact.
8. **(22)** "Public games were held at a silver statue of the empress Eudoxia, just across the street from the cathedral." SS 18432-18435: "neither near nor far from the church named Sophia, but one-half the breadth of the street separated them. At this statue public games were accustomed to be performed".
9. **(29)** "Palladius says bishops in Syria, seeing the church of Antioch still turning to John, had him moved to Pityus, a more distant place on the Black Sea." PALL 6337-6351: "bishops of Syria ... set to work to get him again transferred elsewhere ... when they saw the Church of Antioch migrating to the Church of Armenia". PALL 6397-6401: "produced a rescript ... John was to be transferred to Pityus ... on the shore of the Black Sea". "More distant" comes from Sozomen, SS 44494-44496: "a remoter place of banishment".
10. **(34)** "Both kinds were kept and read." SS 17305-17308: "both those which were published by himself, and such as were noted down by short-hand writers ... must read for themselves".
