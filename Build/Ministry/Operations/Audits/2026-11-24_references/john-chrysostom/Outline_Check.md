# Outline check: John Chrysostom (`john-chrysostom`)

## My account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`why`, `card.knownFor`), the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **The essence:** John, a preacher of Antioch made bishop of Constantinople, told rich hearers plainly that honoring Christ meant spending their wealth on the poor, and as bishop turned the household's spending to the sick; his blunt rebukes of clergy, men in office and the empress's circle made enemies, and two synods and the emperor drove him into exile, where he died on the road to a remoter one. He is on the tree as the best-known voice of the church of Antioch, whose sermons the church kept reading.
- **Also told:** what he preached (Homily 50: Christ's body is honored in the naked and hungry, not in gold cups, E1); what he did with the see's money (the hospital, E3); his shelter of Eutropius under the altar and the sermon over him, read two ways (E5); his rebukes of the powerful, Gainas and the Arians among them (E6); Theophilus's turning against him and the Oak, with the accusers' charges and the two accounts of the deposition (E7-E9); the statue clash, second condemnation, the Johannites and the exile of 404 (E10-E11); the forced march toward Pityus and death near Comana with "Glory to God for all things" (E12-E13).
- **What came of him:** heaviest first, his sermons kept, read and quoted (Socrates; Cassian holding him up against Nestorius; the Reformers); his name restored and his body brought back in 438, which brought his followers back into communion (Socrates VII.45, Theodoret); the poor's claim on the church's wealth defended (E1, E3); sanctuary defended even for its enemy (E5); the city's churches kept from the Arians (E6).

The census `why` and the batch header's approved heart agree with this. The sources hold every part, with the motive of Theophilus Contested and the ground of the deposition differing between Socrates and the minutes.
