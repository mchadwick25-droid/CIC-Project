# Outline check: John Chrysostom (`john-chrysostom`)

## My account of the heart (written before reading `Outline.md`)

From the census entry (`why`, `card.knownFor`), the source sheet and what he is known for.

- **The essence:** John, a preacher of Antioch made bishop of Constantinople, told rich hearers plainly that honoring Christ meant spending their wealth on the poor, and as bishop turned the household's spending to the sick; his blunt rebukes of clergy, men in office and the empress's circle made enemies, and two synods and the emperor drove him into exile, where he died on the road to a remoter one. - **What came of him:** heaviest first, his sermons kept, read and quoted (Socrates; Cassian holding him up against Nestorius; the Reformers); his name restored and his body brought back in 438, which brought his followers back into communion (Socrates VII.45, Theodoret); the poor's claim on the church's wealth defended (E1, E3); sanctuary defended even for its enemy (E5); the city's churches kept from the Arians (E6).

### MINOR 2. "Household" is added to the bishop's spending.

The Dialogue says "the bishop's expenditure" (PALL 3749-3750; Greek PG47 9228-9229, ἀνάλωμα τοῦ ἐπισκόπου). In Also told 2, Beat 2 and the Do not say line on "extravagance", replace "the bishop's own household spending" with "the bishop's own spending".

### MINOR 3. The Do not say list lacks the qualifier in Homily 50.

John says he does not forbid gifts to the altar (N110 29948-29951: "not forbidding such offerings to be provided; but requiring you, together with them, and before them, to give alms"). Add to Do not say:

> - "He told the rich to stop giving gold cups to the church", or "instead of decking the altar". He says he does not forbid such gifts, but asks for alms with them and before them (N110 29948-29951). Say he told them to honor Christ in the poor first.

### MINOR 4. Opening 1 dates the law and widens Socrates's "some".

- Socrates dates only the fall, by Eutropius's consular year (SS 17362-17365, note 399). The law came "scarcely" before it, with no year given.
- Socrates says he displeased "some" (SS 17357-17360), and does not say they were those who heard the sermon.

Replace "In 399 Eutropius, the emperor's chief chamberlain, made a law" with "Eutropius, the emperor's chief chamberlain, had a law made". Replace "Soon after, he fell from favor" with "Soon after, in 399, he fell from favor". Replace "Some who heard it thought" with "Some thought".

### MINOR 5. Cassian's ordination as deacon is cited to a line that does not say "deacon".

CASS 55143-55144 says only "admitted into the sacred ministry by the Bishop John". Gennadius says "ordained deacon by bishop John the Great, at Constantinople" (THDT 43243-43245; old, later). In Cut first 2, What came 1, Links and the card note, add "Gennadius, THDT 43243-43245" beside the CASS lines.

### MINOR 6. The spoken credit for the Oak's minutes hides how late they reach us.

The minutes are known only from Photius's ninth-century summary (PHOT 3843-3848). In Who tells it and Beat 5, replace the spoken credit "the synod's own record" with "the synod's own minutes, as a later patriarch summed them up".

### MINOR 7. The spoken place for the Oak is unclear.

In Setting, "a place called the Oak, outside the city" suggests Constantinople. Socrates says "one of the suburbs of Chalcedon" (SS 18275-18276), and "across the water" comes from Freese's note (PHOT 3862-3864, modern). Replace the spoken form with "a place called the Oak, outside Chalcedon", and drop "across the water".
