## SUMMARY HOLDS, with evidence

1. "Some thought he was attacking a man already brought low." Socrates: "he seemed to create greater displeasure in some, as he not only denied compassion to the unfortunate, but added insult to cruelty" (SS 17357-17360).
2. "John said he spoke to soften the people's hearts, for the church had sheltered its enemy: [Q1]". John: "from a desire to soften your minds towards him, and to induce you to compassion" (N109 17699-17702); "having received her enemy as a captive, she spares him" (N109 17721-17722), offered in answer to those angry that the man "who continually made war upon the Church has taken refuge within it" (17708-17710). Q1 checked against the Greek (PG52 822-827): παριδόντων "ignored", μήτηρ φιλόστοργος "loving mother", ὑπὸ τὰ παραπετάσματα "behind her curtains", πρὸς βασιλικὴν ὀργὴν ἔστη ... μῖσος ἀφόρητον "stood against the emperor's anger ... unbearable hatred". Every clause, nothing added.
3. "He preached plainly about the poor, and his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile." N110 29931-29950; SS 17317-17321, 17372-17374; SS 18486-18490. Ties the exile to the rebukes, not the preaching on the poor, as the Do not say list requires.
4. "His sermons were read for centuries, and the followers who left the church over his fall came back only when his body came home." Socrates: "must read for themselves" (SS 17303-17308); the Augsburg Confession (AUGC 880-881) and Calvin (CALV1 18508-18510) carry the centuries. Socrates VII.45: Proclus "brought back to the Church those who had separated themselves from it on account of Bishop John's deposition ... By this means" (SS 21459-21469); Sozomen shows them still refusing communion with Atticus (SS 44093-44097), which holds "only".
5. "John was born at Antioch, in Syria, and as a priest there for twelve years he preached on what the rich owed the poor." SS 17130-17131 ("a native of Antioch in Syria-Cœle"); PALL 3444-3446 ("For twelve years he was a shining light in the Church of Antioch"); Homily 50 is Antioch's by Homily VII's "our city first crowned itself with the name of Christian" (N110 5301-5303).
6. "They honored the bread of Communion, which Christians call Christ's body, on the altar with gold cups and silk." N110 29908-29909 ("offer for this table a gold and jewelled cup"), 29931-29933 ("honorest Him with silken garments"), 29960-29962 ("cloths bespangled with gold"). See MINOR 1.
7. "Both kinds were kept and read." Socrates's "both those which were published by himself, and such as were noted down by short-hand writers ... must read for themselves" (SS 17304-17308).
8. "He corrected his clergy sharply over how they lived and put some of them out of the church, and many came to hate him." Socrates: "more than proper superciliousness, designing to correct the morals of the clergy ... others became his bitter enemies ... expelled many of them from the church" (SS 17262-17275); Sozomen: "ejected some of the clergy" (SS 42798-42800), "hated by the clergy" (SS 43186). The force on his own side is kept; Socrates's word of blame stays his.
9. "A later bishop of Constantinople summed up the meeting's own minutes, and they tell it differently: some charges were heard, and then all forty-five bishops voted to remove him." PHOT 3946 ("The first and second counts were then investigated"), 4029-4032 ("The members present, forty-five in all ... beginning with bishop Gymnasius and ending with Theophilus ... unanimously decided"). Credited beside Socrates, as the outline requires.
10. "Public games were held at a silver statue of the empress Eudoxia, just across the street from the cathedral." SS 18427-18438: "one-half the breadth of the street separated them. At this statue public games were accustomed to be performed".
11. "Palladius ... says bishops in Syria, seeing the church of Antioch still turning to John, had him moved to Pityus, a more distant place on the Black Sea." PALL 6339-6353 ("they set to work to get him again transferred ... when they saw the Church of Antioch migrating to the Church of Armenia"), 6390-6401 (the rescript: "John was to be transferred to Pityus ... on the shore of the Black Sea"). Credited; their wish "to cut short his life" is carried by the soldiers' line that follows.

## Findings

**MINOR 1 (optional; apply word for word or note).** "They honored the bread of Communion, which Christians call Christ's body, on the altar with gold cups and silk." The homily puts the gold cup and the gold cloths on the table ("offer for this table a gold and jewelled cup", N110 29908-29909; "furnish His table with cloths bespangled with gold", 29960-29961), not on the bread itself. Exact change: "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk." Not blocking: the meaning and force hold, and the quote that follows says "honor him here with silk clothes".
