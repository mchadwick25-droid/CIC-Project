# Second Readings, Blind (A)

## REF-01

Source: `cic/texts/npnf109_chrysostom-priesthood-ascetic-homilies-statutes.xml`, file lines 17722-17727. No original language is named, so this renders the NPNF English. The passage runs 37 words, from "when all have despised him in his" to "their overwhelming hatred."

Source text: "when all have despised him in his desolation, she alone like an affectionate mother has concealed him under her cloak, opposing both the wrath of the king, and the rage of the people, and their overwhelming hatred."

Rendering: Everyone despised him when he was ruined and alone. She alone, like a loving mother, hid him under her cloak. She stood against the king's anger, the people's fury and their crushing hatred.

Notes: "She" is the Church, from the sentence before the passage. "King" is kept as the translation has it; the passage does not say who the king is. The translator's endnote 808 sits on "cloak": it may point to the curtain drawn in front of the altar in Eastern churches. The note is not part of the passage. The scan is clean; nothing is unclear.

## REF-02

Source: `cic/texts/npnf110_chrysostom-homilies-matthew.xml`, file lines 29931-29933. No original language is named, so this renders the NPNF English. The passage runs 30 words, from "Wouldest thou do honor to Christ's body?" to "cold and nakedness."

Source text: "Wouldest thou do honor to Christ's body? Neglect Him not when naked; do not while here thou honorest Him with silken garments, neglect Him perishing without of cold and nakedness."

Rendering: Do you want to honor Christ's body? Then do not ignore him when he is naked. Do not honor him here with silk clothing while you ignore him outside, dying of cold and nakedness.

Notes: "Without" is old English for "outside". The passage sets "here" against "outside" but does not name the place; the next lines suggest the church and its table. The scan is clean; nothing is unclear.
