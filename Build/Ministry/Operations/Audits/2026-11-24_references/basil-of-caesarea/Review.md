## BLOCKING

### Trust.md, 22

**22. "Later a rival bishop claimed part of his churches and once blocked his road with armed men." ASSUMPTION ADDED.**
Gregory gives no time word that sets this after the prefect. Or. 43.58 opens "There was also a further cause of dissension ... When our country had been divided into two provinces" (`npnf207` 42569-42571). The prefect scene is Or. 43.48-50 (42305-42367). Only the editor's chronology orders the two (`npnf208` 694-695, 716-720). The rest of the sentence holds: "claimed those newly added, as belonging to him" and "to seize his mules by the bridle and bar the passage with a robber band" (42575-42603).
Exact change: cut "Later": "A rival bishop claimed part of his churches and once blocked his road with armed men." (If length allows, the outline's own cause can be used instead: "When the province was divided, a rival bishop claimed...", Or. 43.58.)

### Listen_FirstTime.md, B1; Listen_Skeptic.md, 3a

Listen_FirstTime.md:
- **B1 (BLOCKING, thread lost) — paragraph 3, the whole run:** "He taught rhetoric, the historian Rufinus says. In his own telling, he read in the gospel of selling goods and sharing with the poor. His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, gives the credit to their sister Macrina. Rufinus says his friend Gregory of Nazianzus took him from the teacher's chair." I lost what these four sentences are about. Nothing tells me they are differing accounts of why he turned. "Gives the credit" — credit for what? "Took him from the teacher's chair" is an idiom I did not follow. It also goes back to the turn already told in the opening, so I heard it as a second, different event. **Change:** open the paragraph by saying the old writers differ on what turned him, then credit each in plain words: Basil says he read in the gospel of selling goods and sharing with the poor; his brother Gregory of Nyssa says their sister Macrina turned him; Rufinus says his friend Gregory of Nazianzus drew him away from teaching. Cut "He taught rhetoric, the historian Rufinus says" (paragraph 2 already told it plainly).

Listen_Skeptic.md:
a. **BLOCKING** (loses the thread of the turn, which is the heart): "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, gives the credit to their sister Macrina." Credit for what? Change: say what she is credited with, as the source gives it, e.g. "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says it was their sister Macrina who turned him to this life." (Trust read to confirm the wording.)

### Listen_FirstTime.md, B2; Listen_Skeptic.md, 4a

Listen_FirstTime.md:
- **B2 (BLOCKING):** "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, though they quarreled." A quarrel is named with nothing about what it was. **Change:** say what they quarreled over, as the outline gives it; if the old sources give only the quarrel, tell it as they give it; otherwise cut "though they quarreled".

Listen_Skeptic.md:
a. "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, though they quarreled." A quarrel named with no content or reason. Then "Basil came back" — from where? Change: give what the quarrel was and that he left, as the source gives it; if the source gives neither, cut "though they quarreled" and "came back" becomes "Basil stood by the bishop."

### Listen_FirstTime.md, B3 (also Listen_Skeptic.md, 3c and 3d, which that read marks MINOR)

Listen_FirstTime.md:
- **B3 (BLOCKING, thread of the heart lost) — paragraph 8:** "Basil closed prayers with praise to the Father with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and at other times through the Son, in the Holy Spirit." On one hearing the two forms sound the same; I cannot hear which one ranks the Spirit equal. Then "He answered in a book, On the Holy Spirit, from what the church already did in baptism" does not say what the church did. **Change:** say in plain words what the first form claims (the Spirit praised alongside, as equal) and what the church did in baptism, as the outline gives it, so the quote that follows ("As we were baptized, so we confess our faith...") lands.
- **B3 (above):** "from what the church already did in baptism" names the book's argument without saying what it is.

Listen_Skeptic.md:
c. MINOR: "Basil closed prayers with praise to the Father with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and at other times through the Son, in the Holy Spirit." The difference between the two forms is the whole quarrel, and it cannot be heard in one pass. Change: add a clause saying what the first form did, e.g. "...the first form set the Spirit side by side with the Father and the Son."
d. MINOR: "He answered in a book, On the Holy Spirit, from what the church already did in baptism." What did it do? Change: say it, e.g. "from baptism, which the church gave in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit together" (trust read to confirm).

### Listen_Skeptic.md, the jump-back close

- **BLOCKING (the bar: a jump back after the essence)** — the close, "The whole city poured around Basil as he died." It follows 381, Rufinus and Benedict in the sixth century, then jumps back to the deathbed. A one-line coda set apart for effect is also a stock generated button. Change: move the line to open the last paragraph ("The whole city poured around Basil as he died. In 381, after his death, a council..."). Note for the verdict: the story would then end on Benedict, a later writer; Kit Part 1 allows a death as an end, so the reviser may instead keep the death last by telling the 381 council, Rufinus and Benedict before it only if the order then holds. The order must hold either way.

Listen_FirstTime.md, closing note, MINOR: the last line returns to his death after Benedict in the sixth century. It is an allowed end (a death), and I understood it, but it is a jump back in time.

## MINOR

### Opening: when is missing

Listen_FirstTime.md: After the first three sentences I have **who** (Basil of Caesarea) and **why there** (a man looking back on his waking, though "men who were blackening his name" stays vague). **Where** arrives in sentence 4 ("in Caesarea, an inland city in Asia Minor"), so I am not lost. **When** is missing: no century or era until "In 381" near the end. MINOR: if the outline fixes the century, let it in by a clause early (paragraph 2).

Listen_Skeptic.md: After sentence 3 the listener has who (Basil) and why (a man woken to the gospel). Missing: where (the city is placed in sentence 4, so not lost) and when. No century is given anywhere until "In 381, after his death" near the end. MINOR: let the time come in a clause early, if the outline gives a date the old sources fix or a rough one they allow.

Listen_Skeptic.md: The opening also sets up a question it drops: "men who, he wrote, were blackening his name." The listener never learns who they were or what they charged. "His accusers had the governor's ear" may be the same men, but nothing ties them. See 4b.
