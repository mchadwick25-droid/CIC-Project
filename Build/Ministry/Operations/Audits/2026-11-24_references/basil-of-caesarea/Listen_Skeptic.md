## 1. The test (person)

- Who: filled — a teacher of rhetoric turned monk, founder and bishop of Caesarea.
- What happened: filled — the turn, the community in Pontus, the lodging, the fight with the emperor's party.
- What it was about: filled but thin — the Holy Spirit's place is heard, but the prayer forms that carry it are hard to follow (3c).
- Why on the tree: filled — the council of 381, Rufinus' Latin, Benedict's Rule.

## 2. Opening

After sentence 3 the listener has who (Basil) and why (a man woken to the gospel). Missing: where (the city is placed in sentence 4, so not lost) and when. No century is given anywhere until "In 381, after his death" near the end. MINOR: let the time come in a clause early, if the outline gives a date the old sources fix or a rough one they allow.

The opening also sets up a question it drops: "men who, he wrote, were blackening his name." The listener never learns who they were or what they charged. "His accusers had the governor's ear" may be the same men, but nothing ties them. See 4b.

## 3. Sentences that fail on one hearing

a. **BLOCKING** (loses the thread of the turn, which is the heart): "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, gives the credit to their sister Macrina." Credit for what? Change: say what she is credited with, as the source gives it, e.g. "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says it was their sister Macrina who turned him to this life." (Trust read to confirm the wording.)
b. MINOR: "Rufinus says his friend Gregory of Nazianzus took him from the teacher's chair." Idiom, and an ESL listener may hear a literal chair. Change: "drew him away from teaching."
c. MINOR: "Basil closed prayers with praise to the Father with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and at other times through the Son, in the Holy Spirit." The difference between the two forms is the whole quarrel, and it cannot be heard in one pass. Change: add a clause saying what the first form did, e.g. "...the first form set the Spirit side by side with the Father and the Son."
d. MINOR: "He answered in a book, On the Holy Spirit, from what the church already did in baptism." What did it do? Change: say it, e.g. "from baptism, which the church gave in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit together" (trust read to confirm).
e. MINOR: "Gregory says that in writing Basil held back the bare words that the Spirit is God, from caution, though he often preached it." "Bare words" and "from caution" stack up; the listener loses who is cautious of what. Change: "Gregory says that Basil, out of caution, never wrote outright that the Spirit is God, though he often preached it."

## 4. Bare statements (Explain it) — all BLOCKING

a. "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, though they quarreled." A quarrel named with no content or reason. Then "Basil came back" — from where? Change: give what the quarrel was and that he left, as the source gives it; if the source gives neither, cut "though they quarreled" and "came back" becomes "Basil stood by the bishop."
b. "His accusers had the governor's ear." Accusers of what? The charge is never given. Change: give the charge in its own content, as the source gives it, or cut the clause and open on Basil's letter.
c. "Basil answered by making more bishops, Gregory says." The listener cannot tell why making bishops answers a rival who claims his churches. Change: add the purpose the source gives (e.g. to hold those towns), or cut the sentence.

## 5. Citations read aloud not called for — MINOR

- "He taught rhetoric, the historian Rufinus says." The narrator already stated it plainly in paragraph 2; the credit makes an agreed fact sound doubtful. Narrator's voice: cut the sentence (paragraph 2 holds it), or "He had been a teacher of rhetoric."
- "Basil answered by making more bishops, Gregory says." A plain act. Narrator's voice: drop "Gregory says" (after the fix in 4c).

The other credits (Macrina's role, the envy, the rags reply, the caution) mark differing sources or a witness's judgment and are called for.

## 6. Tells and machine-made lines

No named tell in the narrator's voice: no dash, no "not X but Y", no stock word, no moral close. The three questions are Basil's own, quoted.

Lines that sound machine-made:
- **BLOCKING (the bar: a jump back after the essence)** — the close, "The whole city poured around Basil as he died." It follows 381, Rufinus and Benedict in the sixth century, then jumps back to the deathbed. A one-line coda set apart for effect is also a stock generated button. Change: move the line to open the last paragraph ("The whole city poured around Basil as he died. In 381, after his death, a council..."). Note for the verdict: the story would then end on Benedict, a later writer; Kit Part 1 allows a death as an end, so the reviser may instead keep the death last by telling the 381 council, Rufinus and Benedict before it only if the order then holds. The order must hold either way.
- Paragraph 3, four sentences in a row each built "X says / in his telling / gives the credit / Y says." It reads as a report about the record, quotations linked by commentary. MINOR once 3a, 3b and 5 are applied; joining them with contrast ("Rufinus says...; Basil himself says...; his brother says...") would make one narrator weigh the accounts.
- Paragraph 2, "Basil gave up... He wrote rules... He built... As its bishop, he..." — four parallel "He did X" sentences, a summary list read aloud. MINOR (see 7).
- "That waking set the course of his life" — a stock writerly bridge, but it does real work and is understood. Not counted.

## 8. Attention

The opening pulls: a man answering slander by confessing a wasted youth is a real voice. Attention first drifts at "He taught rhetoric, the historian Rufinus says," because the story stops to cite instead of move. It drifts again in paragraph 5, where church politics come without reasons (4a). The Pontus paragraph and the foot-washing quote win it back. I would listen to the end.
