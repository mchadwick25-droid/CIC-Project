## HELD and SUMMARY HOLDS, with the line each rests on

- **S1** "Years later, defending himself in a letter against men who, he wrote, were spreading lies about him, Basil of Caesarea looked back." HELD. `npnf208` 39108-39117 (Letter 223.1): "my slanderers ... sullying my reputation among the brethren ... the bitter torrent of falsehood that has gushed out against me."
- **S2** "...a wisdom God had made foolish, the learning of this world." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 39131-39137 (223.2): "acquiring the wisdom made foolish by God ... I perceived the uselessness of 'the wisdom of the princes of this world, that come to naught.'"
- **S4** "...Caesarea, an inland city in Asia Minor, in the fourth century." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 41972-41976 (Or. 43.34): "an inland city like ours". The century rests on the reign of Valens (`npnf207` 41836, Or. 43.30) and the council of 381 (`npnf214` 13393-13396). "Asia Minor" is the outline's plain gloss.
- **S5** "Basil gave up teaching rhetoric, the art of public speaking, for a life of prayer and shared work." SUMMARY HOLDS. Rufinus, `eusebius_werke-bd2-2...txt` 29989-29994: "ad profitendam rhetoricam rogabantur. quod opus magnifice quidem implebat Basilius ... de doctoris cathedra deponeret ac secum ad monasterium ... perduceret"; Life of Macrina 136: "deserted it for this busy life where one toils with one's hands"; Rufinus 30048-30049: "psalmis et hymnis et orationibus docuit vacare". Gloss from the outline.
- **S6** "He wrote rules that taught monks to live together and serve one another, and he built a great lodging for the poor and the sick a little way outside the city." SUMMARY HOLDS. Clarke 1925, 13190-13191: "the life of a number lived in common is more useful"; `npnf207` 42734-42736 (Or. 43.63): "Go forth a little way from the city, and behold the new city".
- **S7** "As its bishop, he defended the Holy Spirit's place beside the Father and the Son, and he died before a council, a meeting of bishops, declared it in its creed." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 42046-42050 (Or. 43.37), raised "to the lofty throne of a Bishop"; `npnf208` 10556-10558 (On the Holy Spirit 10.24), the view he fought: "not permissible ... for the Holy Spirit to be ranked with the Father and Son"; `npnf214` 13394-13395: "who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified". No cause is claimed.
- **S8** "The old writers differ on what turned him." SUMMARY HOLDS. The three accounts below (Letter 223.2; Life of Macrina 136; Rufinus 29992-29994).
- **S9** "In his own telling, he read in the gospel of selling goods and sharing with the poor." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 39143-39145: "Then I read the Gospel, and I saw there that a great means of reaching perfection was the selling of one's goods, the sharing them with the poor". Note, not a finding: in the letter this reading follows the waking of S3; as the turn toward the monks' life it is fair.
- **S10** "His brother, Gregory of Nyssa, says their sister Macrina turned him, while the historian Rufinus says his friend Gregory of Nazianzus drew him away from teaching." SUMMARY HOLDS. Life of Macrina 136: "Macrina took him in hand, and with such speed did she draw him also toward the mark of philosophy that he forsook ... fame gained by speaking"; Rufinus 29992-29994: "ut sedentem Basilium de doctoris cathedra deponeret ac secum ad monasterium manu iniecta perduceret" (took him down from the teacher's chair and led him off, laying hold of him, to a monastery). Note, not a finding: "drew him away" keeps the agent and what he left, though not the hand laid on him.
- **S11** "In Pontus, near the Black Sea, Basil founded a community and led it." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 41819-41821 (Or. 43.29): "presided over the abodes of contemplation there. He himself too founded one"; Rufinus 30043-30048: "Basilius Ponti urbes et rura circumiens ... monasteria construere ... docuit". Note, not a finding: Gregory places his own founding line at the withdrawal after the quarrel; Rufinus and Letter 14 (`npnf208` 23993, "I departed into Pontus in quest of a place to live in") give the earlier Pontus life. The story gives no time word, so no date is moved.
- **S12** "His rules were answers to his brother monks' questions, and one asks whether to live alone or with others." HELD. Clarke 1925, 13180-13184: "whether the man who has retired from such should live privately by himself, or join with like-minded brethren"; Rufinus's preface, `benedict-aniane_concordia...txt` 40487-40489: "instituta monachorum; quae interrogantibus monachis ... responsa statuit".
- **S13** "Love does not seek its own good, Basil answers, and Christ washed his disciples' feet, but a man alone has no one to serve." SUMMARY HOLDS. Clarke 1925, 13209-13214: "the love of Christ does not allow us to look each at his own good ... 'seeketh not its own.' Now the solitary life has one aim, the service of the needs of the individual"; PG 31, `basil_opera-omnia-pg31...txt` 72546-72547: "αὐτὸς περιζωσάμενος ἕνιψε τοὺς πόδας τῶν μαθητῶν". The Quote 2 that follows (v0 wording) was checked against the Greek at 72547-72549, "Τίνα οὖν ἀπονίψεις ; τίνα θεραπεύσεις ; τίνος ἔσχατος ἔσῃ, αὐτὸς καθ᾽ ἑαυτὸν διάγων ;": it holds, with no "feet".
- **S17** "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, but they quarreled, and Basil went back to Pontus." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 41760-41762 (Or. 43.27): "proceeded to praise the Lord in the seat of the Presbyters"; 41774-41776 (43.28): "There arose a disagreement between him and his predecessor in the rule over this Church"; 41819-41820 (43.29): "he set out from the place with me into Pontus". "Back" rests on Letter 14 (`npnf208` 23993) and Rufinus 30043.
- **S18** "When the emperor Valens, who favored the party that ranked Christ, the Son, below God the Father, moved against the churches that held them equal, Basil came back to serve the bishop." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 41836-41852 (Or. 43.30): "an Emperor ... most hostile to Christ ... dishonouring His offspring with his unequal degrees of Godhead"; 41900-41902 (43.31): "He immediately returned with me from Pontus ... devoted himself to the service of his mother, the Church"; 41929-41933 (43.33): "While obedient to his wishes, he was everything to him".
- **S19** "When the old bishop died, Basil was made bishop, though the bishops of the province fought it out of envy, Gregory of Nazianzus says in his speech in Basil's memory." HELD. `npnf207` 42046-42050 (Or. 43.37): "having sweetly breathed his last in Basil's arms, he was raised to the lofty throne of a Bishop, not without difficulty or without the envious struggles of the prelates of his native land".
- **S20** "The emperor's highest official ordered him to accept the emperor's faith, or else lose his goods and face exile, torture and death." HELD. `npnf207` 42318 (Or. 43.48): "In refusing to respect the religion of your Sovereign"; 42337 (43.49): "Confiscation, banishment, torture, death." Sozomen 6.16 (`npnf202` 38325-38328): "commanded him to embrace the religious sentiments of the emperor, menacing him with death".
- **S21** "In Gregory's telling, Basil answered that a man who owns only rags and a few books cannot be robbed, and that death would only send him sooner to God." HELD. `npnf207` 42340-42350 (Or. 43.49): "a man who has nothing, is beyond the reach of confiscation; unless you demand my tattered rags, and the few books ... Death is my benefactor, for it will send me the sooner to God".
- **S22** "When the province was divided, a rival bishop claimed part of his churches and once blocked his road with armed men." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 42569-42603 (Or. 43.58): "When our country had been divided into two provinces ... claimed those newly added, as belonging to him ... to seize his mules by the bridle and bar the passage with a robber band."
- **S23** "Basil answered by making more bishops for the province, Gregory says." HELD. `npnf207` 42609-42612 (Or. 43.59): "the disaster, under his most able management, resulted in the multiplication of the Bishops of the country."
- **S24** "His accusers were filling the governor's ears, and Basil wrote to ask what harm the state suffered from him, or from building a lodging for strangers, travelers and the sick, with doctors and transport." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 30010-30022 (Letter 94): "those who are besieging your impartial ears ... what harm the government suffers from me? ... to whom do we do any harm by building a place of entertainment for strangers, both for those who are on a journey and for those who require medical treatment ... physicians, doctors, means of conveyance".
- **S25** "The rich gave to it at his urging." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 42736-42742 (Or. 43.63): "the common treasury of the wealthy, in which the superfluities of their wealth, aye, and even their necessaries, are stored, in consequence of his exhortations".
- **S26** "Lepers driven from their towns were taken in, and Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself." HELD. `npnf207` 42755-42776 (Or. 43.63): "driven away from their cities and homes ... no longer brought before us ... saluted them as brethren ... taking the lead in approaching to tend them".
- **S27** "Basil closed prayers by giving glory to the Father in two forms: with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit, and through the Son, in the Holy Spirit." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 8793-8797 (On the Holy Spirit 1.3): "using the full doxology to God the Father in both forms, at one time 'with the Son together with the Holy Ghost,' and at another 'through the Son in the Holy Ghost'".
- **S28** "The first set the Spirit beside the Father and the Son." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 13294-13296 (25.59): "to say that the Son is with the Father is to exhibit ... the inseparability of the fellowship ... the preposition 'with' declares ... the communion in action"; 14160-14172 (27.68): "the ascription of glory 'with' the Holy Spirit". Note, not a finding: Basil also denies that "in" means a lower rank (13175-13177); the sentence does not say it does.
- **S29** "Some attacked these as new, contradictory words, holding that the Spirit could not be ranked with the Father and the Son." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 8797-8799 (1.3): "attacked by some of those present on the ground that I was introducing novel and at the same time mutually contradictory terms"; 10556-10558 (10.24): "It is not permissible, they assert, for the Holy Spirit to be ranked with the Father and Son".
- **S30** "He answered in a book, On the Holy Spirit, that the church already baptized new believers in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit together." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf208` 14162-14168 (27.68): "combine in our confession of the faith Him who is numbered with Them at Baptism ... They must now instruct us either not to baptize as we have received, or not to believe as we were baptized, or not to ascribe glory as we have believed"; 8816-8823 (1.3), the book written as the answer.
- **S31** "A letter of his puts the same thought in two lines: 'As we were baptized, so we confess our faith.'" HELD. `npnf208` 33483-33485 (Letter 159.2): "As we were baptized, so we profess our belief. As we profess our belief, so also we offer praise." Rendered from Jackson's English; the Greek (PG 32) is not held. The meaning holds. Told as a letter, not as the reply to the attackers.
- **S33** "Gregory says that Basil, out of caution, held back from writing in plain words that the Spirit is God, though he often preached it." SUMMARY HOLDS. `npnf207` 42927-42944 (Or. 43.68): "The enemy were on the watch for the unqualified statement 'the Spirit is God' ... His treatise on this subject makes it further manifest ... He postponed for the time the use of the exact term"; 42963-42965 (43.69): "his often having publicly preached this truth". Note, not a finding: Gregory does not say "in writing" in so many words. He sets the restraint beside the treatise and against the public preaching, so the reading holds. "Admits" is now "says", as the outline asks.
- **S34** "The whole city poured around Basil as he died." HELD. `npnf207` 43240-43242 (Or. 43.79): "Around him poured the whole city, unable to bear his loss".
- **S35** "In 381, after his death, a council at Constantinople declared in its creed that the Spirit is worshipped and glorified with the Father and the Son." HELD. `npnf214` 13393-13396: "the Holy Ghost ... who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified". Death before 381: Life of Macrina 180 and Source_Sheet D2 (all agree). No cause from Basil is claimed.

Rufinus's preface, `benedict-aniane_concordia...txt` 40470-40489: "poposcisti ut hoc opus in Latinum verterem ... sancti Basilii ... instituta monachorum ... protuli"; Gennadius 17, `npnf203` 42469-42470: "translating the works of Basil of Cæsarea in Cappadocia".
