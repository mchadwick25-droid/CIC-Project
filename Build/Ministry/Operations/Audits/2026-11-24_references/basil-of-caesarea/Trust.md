## SUMMARY HOLDS (not blocking)

1. "Years later, defending himself in a letter against men who, he wrote, were blackening his name, Basil of Caesarea looked back." Letter 223.1: "my slanderers ... devise means for sullying my reputation among the brethren" (`npnf208` 39108-39112). In 223.2 he looks back on his youth (39131).
4. "That waking set the course of his life in Caesarea, an inland city in Asia Minor." Letter 223.2 runs straight from the waking to reading the gospel and looking for brethren (39133-39150). "Inland": "an inland city like ours" (`npnf207` 41975). "Asia Minor" is the outline's plain gloss for place.
5. "Basil gave up teaching rhetoric, the art of public speaking, for a life of prayer and shared work." Rufinus: Gregory took him "de doctoris cathedra ... ad monasterium" (29993-29994). Life of Macrina 136: "deserted it for this busy life where one toils with one's hands". Longer Rule 37 asks whether to "neglect work for the sake of the prayers" and says "one should work diligently" (Clarke 16968-16982).
6. "He wrote rules that taught monks to live together and serve one another." Longer Rule 7: "TO LIVE WITH LIKE-MINDED PERSONS"; "love ... seeketh not its own" (Clarke 13174-13215).
7. "He built a great lodging for the poor and the sick a little way outside the city." Letter 94: "building a place of entertainment for strangers ... and for those who require medical treatment" (`npnf208` 30011-30014). Or. 43.63: "Go forth a little way from the city, and behold the new city" (`npnf207` 42734-42736).
8. "As its bishop, he defended the Holy Spirit's place beside the Father and the Son, and he died before a council, a meeting of bishops, declared it in its creed." Creed: "who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified" (`npnf214_seven-ecumenical-councils.xml` 13393-13396). Before the council: Socrates 5.8 names "Helladius, the successor of Basil" at it (`npnf202_socrates-sozomen-ecclesiastical-histories.xml` 15173).
15. "Love does not seek its own good, Basil answers, and Christ washed his disciples' feet, but a man alone has no one to serve." Clarke 13213-13215: "the solitary life has one aim, the service of the needs of the individual"; 13458-13462: "washed the feet of the disciples ... Whom wilt thou care for?"
17. "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, though they quarreled." Or. 43.27: "proceeded to praise the Lord in the seat of the Presbyters"; 43.28: "There arose a disagreement between him and his predecessor" (`npnf207` 41764-41779). Gregory's own line is used, not the editor's P1.

Or. 43.30: "dishonouring His offspring with his unequal degrees of Godhead"; 43.31: "He immediately returned with me from Pontus ... devoted himself to the service of his mother, the Church"; 43.33: "While obedient to his wishes, he was everything to him" (`npnf207` 41836-41936).
24. "His accusers had the governor's ear, and Basil wrote to ask what harm it did the state to build a lodging for strangers, travelers and the sick, with doctors and transport."
