## BLOCKING findings

### B1. Trust.md, SOFTENED OR CHANGED

**1. SOFTENED OR CHANGED.** Story: "The monks there had no priest, he explains, and he feared they would be cut off from the church."
- Source, J6 10671-10676: "the monastery contained a large number of reverend brothers, and ... the reverend presbyters, Jerome and Vincent, through modesty and humility, were unwilling to offer the sacrifices permitted to their rank"; J6 10706-10708: "For a year before I had heard many of them complain that they had no one to celebrate for them the sacraments of the Lord"; J6 10657-10659: "too great strictness or scrupulosity on my part might have the effect of alienating them from the Church with its ancient faith".
- The monastery had two priests. They would not celebrate. "Had no priest" changes the fact. The second half holds.
- Change to: "The monks there had complained for a year that no one celebrated the sacraments for them, he explains, and he feared they would drift from the church."
- **Do not say** (add to `Outline.md`; beat 5 says "the monks there had no priest"): "The monks there had no priest." Say: for a year they had complained that no one celebrated the sacraments for them, because its priests, Jerome and Vincent, held back from it out of humility (J6 10671-10676, 10706-10708).

### B2. Listen_FirstTime.md, bare statement 1; Listen_Skeptic.md, bare statement 1

Listen_FirstTime.md:

1. "monks he had driven out of Egypt" (s30): an expulsion with no reason. If the old sources give Theophilus's reason, add it in a clause; if they give only the act, it stands as told and this item falls (the trust read decides).

Listen_Skeptic.md:

1. **The Long Brothers.** "monks he had driven out of Egypt, the Long Brothers" and "unless John condemned Origen's books and sent the Long Brothers away." The expulsion is an act of punishment, and no reason is heard. The listener is also never told the monks had come to Constantinople, or that John had taken them in, so "sent... away" from where is a gap. **Change:** in one clause, give why Theophilus drove them out and that they had come to John in Constantinople, as the old sources give it ("monks he had driven out of Egypt, the Long Brothers, who had fled to John at Constantinople"). If the old sources give no reason for the expulsion, tell the act as they give it, with no reason added. If the outline does not hold the Constantinople link, cut "and sent the Long Brothers away." (The trust reader confirms against the source.)

### B3. Listen_FirstTime.md, bare statement 2

2. "Augustine followed the order of his list." The listener does not know what Augustine wrote, so the act is empty. Change: name what Augustine did, in the outline's terms (for example, that he wrote his own list of heresies in the same order), or cut the sentence and let the earlier mention carry Augustine.

## MINOR findings

### M1. Listen_FirstTime.md, failing sentence 1; Listen_Skeptic.md, failing sentence 1

Listen_FirstTime.md:

1. "A heresy, in his book, is a wrong belief, as he judged it, held by people led astray since the first man." Lost: "since the first man" (the listener does not know this means Adam, or that the list starts at the beginning of the world). Change: "...held by people led astray, in his telling, ever since Adam, the first man."

Listen_Skeptic.md:

1. "A heresy, in his book, is a wrong belief, as he judged it, held by people led astray since the first man." Three asides in one sentence. "Since the first man" is lost on one hearing, since the listener does not yet know he counts heresies from before Christ.

### M2. Listen_FirstTime.md, failing sentence 2

2. "Jerome's brother had fled the priesthood several times." Lost: it sounds as if he was a priest who ran away, so "then a priest" two sentences on contradicts it. Change: "Jerome's brother had several times run away from being made a priest."

### M3. Listen_FirstTime.md, failing sentence 3; Listen_Skeptic.md, failing sentence 3

Listen_FirstTime.md:

3. "During a service near his monastery, he says, he had deacons seize the man and stop his mouth." Lost: whose monastery, and "stop his mouth" (an idiom many second-language listeners do not know). Change: "...near Epiphanius's own monastery, he says, he had deacons seize the man and hold his mouth shut."

Listen_Skeptic.md:

3. "During a service near his monastery, he says, he had deacons seize the man and stop his mouth." Whose monastery, and who is "he"? There are three men in two sentences: Epiphanius, Jerome and Jerome's brother. Suggested: "During a service near the brother's monastery, Epiphanius says, he had deacons seize the man and stop his mouth." (Use the right monastery, as the outline gives it.)

### M4. Listen_FirstTime.md, failing sentence 4; Listen_Skeptic.md, failing sentence 4

Listen_FirstTime.md:

4. "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge on monks he had driven out of Egypt, the Long Brothers, and used a man of simple mind and manners." Lost: that "a man of simple mind" is Epiphanius. Change: "...and used Epiphanius, a man of simple mind and manners."

Listen_Skeptic.md:

4. "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge on monks he had driven out of Egypt, the Long Brothers, and used a man of simple mind and manners." The listener may not catch that "a man of simple mind" is Epiphanius. Suggested: "...and used Epiphanius, a man of simple mind and manners."

### M5. Listen_FirstTime.md, failing sentence 5

5. "One of those monks asked him whether he had read any of their books." Lost: whose books, the monks' or Origen's; and why the Long Brothers are now in Constantinople. Change: "One of the Long Brothers, who had come to Constantinople, asked him whether he had read any of their books." (Only if the outline holds where they were; otherwise keep the clause out.)

### M6. Listen_FirstTime.md, failing sentence 6

6. "Then Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, wrote to win him against Origen." Lost: Epiphanius is already against Origen, so "win him" puzzles. Change: "...wrote to win him to his own campaign against Origen."

### M7. Listen_Skeptic.md, failing sentence 2

2. "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea four centuries on, took his book as their list." "Four centuries on" from what? A listener who knows only one Nicaea may think of 325.

### M8. Listen_Skeptic.md, failing sentence 5

5. "In 787 the council at Nicaea ruled that the passage was not his..." "The passage" does not match the "writings" of the sentence before. Use one word for both.

### M9. Listen_FirstTime.md, citation read aloud; Listen_Skeptic.md, unexplained words

Listen_FirstTime.md:

- "Sozomen says instead..." Socrates and Sozomen are rightly named, since the old sources differ. But Sozomen arrives with no role, and "Socrates" alone suggests the Greek philosopher. MINOR. Change: "Another historian of the time, Sozomen, says instead..."

Listen_Skeptic.md:

- **Unexplained words:** "deacon" and "ordained" (a listener with no church background is not told what a deacon is); "Augustine" (a name with no role given); "Jerome" (a role is needed, e.g. "the scholar Jerome"); "Sozomen" ("the historian" is given for Socrates but not for him: "the historian Sozomen"). Names heard are about nine: Epiphanius, Origen, Augustine, Jerome, Theophilus, Socrates, Sozomen, John Chrysostom and the Long Brothers. That is past the kit's six, but each one acts or links.

### M10. Listen_FirstTime.md, words

Unexplained words: "deacon", "ordained", "Genesis", "paradise", "hearsay" (heard twice, hard in a second language), "Long Brothers" (why "Long"), "Christ's holy bride" (an image a listener with no church background will not decode), "Jerome" (heard once, neither acts nor is placed). Each MINOR; give "Genesis, the first book of the Bible" and "the church, which he calls Christ's holy bride".

### M11. Listen_Skeptic.md, machine-sounding line 1

1. "The same letter defends two acts of force." A signpost that announces what is coming. It reads as a report about the record. Let the acts come straight: "In the same letter he defends what he did to Jerome's brother."

### M12. Listen_Skeptic.md, machine-sounding line 2

2. "In old age he carried the same fight..." then, one paragraph later, "In his old age he preached..." The same phrase twice sounds templated. Cut "In his old age" from the second.

### M13. Listen_Skeptic.md, machine-sounding line 3

3. "He knew some heresies from books, some by hearsay, some with his own eyes and ears." A neat triple, the cadence a skeptic hears first. Keep it if it follows his own words. Otherwise loosen it.

### M14. Listen_Skeptic.md, machine-sounding line 4

4. "...a wrong belief, as he judged it..." The aside sounds like a safety hedge more than a narrator's voice. Suggested: "A heresy, to Epiphanius, was a wrong belief..."

### M15. Listen_Skeptic.md, short one-act run

- **Short one-act run:** the Constantinople paragraph ends "He had not, and had judged them by report. He sailed for Cyprus and died at sea before he reached home." Acceptable as a turn. The run that flattens is the last full paragraph: "Augustine followed the order of his list. Four centuries on, ... Opponents of images quoted writings under his name." Join the first two with cause or time.

### M16. Listen_FirstTime.md, attention; Listen_Skeptic.md, attention

Listen_FirstTime.md: Attention first drifts at "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge..." : three new names (Theophilus, Socrates, Sozomen) and the Long Brothers arrive in four sentences, with two competing motives. Slow it by giving each historian a role and fixing fault 4 above.

Listen_Skeptic.md: Attention first drifts at "Then Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, wrote to win him against Origen." Four new names (Theophilus, Socrates, the Long Brothers, Sozomen) come in four sentences, and the story pauses for the sources' quarrel before the voyage.
