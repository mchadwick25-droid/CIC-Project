## Finding

**1. BLOCKING (SOFTENED OR CHANGED).** "The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge on those monks, the Long Brothers, and used Epiphanius, a man of simple mind and manners."
- Source, SS 17886-17888 (Socrates 6.10): "Epiphanius being on account of his extraordinary piety a man of simple mind and manners was easily influenced by the letters of Theophilus".
- Socrates gives the simplicity as the result of great piety. That makes it half praise. Cut loose from that cause, "a man of simple mind" sounds to a modern listener like "dim-witted". That is a harsher judgment than Socrates makes. The rest of the sentence holds: revenge on "Dioscorus and his brethren" (SS 17898-17899), and "Theophilus had in this way deluded Epiphanius" (SS 17893).
- **Exact change:** replace "and used Epiphanius, a man of simple mind and manners." with "and used Epiphanius, a man so devout that he was simple in mind and manners." (+4 words.)
- Do not say line for the outline: "a man of simple mind" without its cause. Say: Socrates says his great piety made him simple in mind and manners (SS 17886-17888).
