Baseline: `epiphanius-of-salamis.brief.v0.txt` (714 words, never edited). Revised: `epiphanius-of-salamis.brief.txt` (762 words). Every sentence not listed here is kept word for word.

## Changed

1. **Before:** A heresy, in his book, is a teaching the church judged false.
   **After:** A heresy, in his book, is a wrong belief, as he judged it, held by people led astray since the first man.
   **Stands on:** Words to explain, heresy (HOLL1 13420-13425); Do not say, "A heresy is a teaching the church judged false."
   **Rule broken:** the outline's Do not say list, word for word; it also made the judging the church's when it is his.

2. **Before:** The historian Sozomen, writing later, says he was born near Eleutheropolis and chosen bishop by the people of Cyprus.
   **After:** He was born near Eleutheropolis and lived as a monk near his birthplace, and the people of Cyprus chose him as their bishop.
   **Stands on:** Beat 1 and Setting, Tradition (SS 39793-39794); Beat 2 (SS 39797-39802); Who tells it, Sozomen ("Told plainly ... birth and training, the Cypriots' choice"); Cut first 8 ("Keep the birthplace and the monk's life near it").
   **Rule broken:** Part 1, Rigor, "name a witness only where the listener would otherwise be misled"; no old source differs here.

3. **Before:** He wrote the Panarion about 375, by his own dating.
   **After:** He wrote the Panarion about 375.
   **Stands on:** Setting, Century ("his Panarion 'about 375', told plainly with no credit").
   **Rule broken:** the outline's Setting line, which tells the year with no credit.

4. **Before:** Each chapter names a heresy, tells its teaching and answers it.
   **After:** Each chapter names a heresy, tells its teaching and answers it, twenty from before Christ's coming and sixty after.
   **Stands on:** Also told 1 (HOLL1 12512-12536); Do not say, "Augustine counted twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after" ("Say plainly, with no credit: Epiphanius set twenty before Christ's coming and sixty after").
   **Rule broken:** none in this sentence; the count moves here from the old Augustine sentence (change 12), which gave it to Augustine against the Do not say list.

5. **Before:** He listed even the followers of Apollinaris, a man he says was always dear to him.
   **After:** He listed even the followers of Apollinaris, a man he says was always dear to him, for teaching that Christ took no human mind.
   **Stands on:** Also told 1 and Beat 3 (PG42 48828-48833, 48853-48857); Sibling ("here he lists the followers' teaching in one clause").
   **Rule broken:** Part 1, Craft, "Explain it or leave it out": a teaching was named as a heresy without saying what it was.

6. **Before:** In the 390s he preached against Origen in the church at Jerusalem.
   **After:** In his old age he preached against Origen in the church at Jerusalem.
   **Stands on:** Beat 4; Do not say, any decade for the Jerusalem visit, "in the 390s" included ("say 'in the fourth century' or 'in old age'").
   **Rule broken:** the Do not say list; the decade is an editor's chronology (J6 3042), a modern view told as the record (fault 2).

7. **Before:** The scholar Jerome, who was there and on his side, says the bishop of Jerusalem's party mocked him as an old fool. Yet crowds pressed to kiss his feet.
   **After:** The scholar Jerome, who was there and on his side, says the bishop of Jerusalem's party mocked him as an old fool, while crowds pressed to kiss his feet.
   **Stands on:** Beat 4 (J6 40982-40996); Who tells it, Jerome ("Credit beside: the Jerusalem scene, a partisan's praise and blame (the mockery, the crowds)").
   **Rule broken:** Part 1, Rigor, fault 1: as its own sentence, the crowds' praise read as the narrator's, outside Jerome's credit.

8. **Cut:** If the rivers were real, so was Eden.
   **Stands on:** Do not say, "A narrator's line after Q2 drawing its lesson ('If the rivers were real, so was Eden')."
   **Rule broken:** the Do not say list, word for word; a narrator's summing-up of the quote.

9. **Added:** The monks there had no priest, he explains, and he feared they would be cut off from the church.
   **Stands on:** Beat 5 (J6 10655-10659, 10704-10705).
   **Rule broken (by the old paragraph):** Part 1, Craft, "Each act of conflict or punishment ... says why it happened, where the old sources give the reason"; the forced ordination was told with no reason, though his letter gives one.

10. **Before:** He tore it apart and asked that such curtains be banned.
    **After:** Holding that an image of a man in church went against Scripture, he tore it apart and asked that such curtains be banned.
    **Stands on:** Beat 5 (J6 11106-11107, 11099-11120); Do not say, "tore it apart".
    **Rule broken:** Part 1, Craft, the same reason rule: the tearing was told with no reason, though his letter gives one.

11. **Before:** Sozomen says one of those monks asked whether he had read any of their books.
    **After:** One of those monks asked him whether he had read any of their books.
    **Stands on:** Also told 5 and Beat 7 (SS 43604-43610); Who tells it, Sozomen ("Told plainly ... the Long Brother's question").
    **Rule broken:** Part 1, Rigor, "name a witness only where the listener would otherwise be misled"; no old source differs here.

12. **Before:** Augustine, who reports that Epiphanius set twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after, followed his order.
    **After:** Augustine followed the order of his list.
    **Stands on:** Also told 6 and What came 1 (AUG 2414-2415); Cut first 5 ("Keep that Augustine followed his order").
    **Rule broken:** the Do not say list, "Augustine counted twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after"; the count is now told plainly in change 4.

13. **Before:** Four centuries on, both sides in the quarrel over holy images claimed him.
    **After:** Four centuries on, Christians quarreled over pictures of Christ and the saints in churches, and both sides claimed him.
    **Stands on:** What came 3 and Words to explain, the quarrel over holy images (MEN 8494-8495, 26219-26227, 26231-26239).
    **Rule broken:** Part 1, Craft, "Explain it or leave it out": the quarrel was named without saying what it was about.

14. **Before:** A reply written at Charlemagne's order answered with his own curtain letter.
    **After:** A book written to answer the council's argument replied with his own curtain letter.
    **Stands on:** What came 3 (MEN 26282-26316); Words to explain, the Caroline Books ("a book written to answer the council's argument"; "Do not name Charlemagne").
    **Rule broken:** the Do not say list, "written at Charlemagne's order"; that claim is only Mendham's title page, a modern view told as the record (fault 2).
