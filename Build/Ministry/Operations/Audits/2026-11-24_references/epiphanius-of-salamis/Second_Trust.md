## Sentences in scope

1. A heresy, to Epiphanius, was a wrong belief held by people led astray ever since Adam, the first man.
2. Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea four centuries after Epiphanius, took his book as their list.
3. He wrote the Panarion about 375.
4. Each chapter names a heresy, tells its teaching and answers it, twenty from before Christ's coming and sixty after.
5. He knew some heresies from books, some by report, some with his own eyes and ears.
6. He copied one opponent's own letter whole, so that no one could say he answered from report alone.
7. He preached against Origen in the church at Jerusalem.
8. In a letter to the bishop of Jerusalem Epiphanius says why.
9. Origen, he writes, taught that souls were angels who fell into bodies, denied that this flesh would rise from the dead, and read the garden of Eden as hidden meanings.
10. Genesis, the first book of the Bible, names the Euphrates among the rivers of Eden, and he writes: (the quote after it is unchanged from v0).
11. In the same letter he defends what he did to the scholar Jerome's brother, who had several times run away from being made a priest.
12. During a service near Epiphanius's own monastery, he says, he had deacons, the clergy who serve under priests, seize the man and hold his mouth shut.
13. The monks there had complained for a year that no one celebrated the sacraments, the church's holy rites, for them, he explains, and he acted from the fear of God, to provide what the church needed.
14. Holding that an image of a man in church went against Scripture, he tore it apart and asked that such curtains be banned.
15. Then Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, wrote to win him to his own campaign against Origen.
16. His letter said he had driven monks who followed Origen out of Egypt.
17. The historian Socrates says Theophilus wanted revenge on those monks, the Long Brothers, and used Epiphanius, a man so devout that he was simple in mind and manners.
18. Another historian, Sozomen, says instead that Epiphanius had long loathed Origen's books, and that Theophilus saw no danger in following his example, because the people praised Epiphanius.
19. There he made a man a deacon in the territory of its bishop, John Chrysostom, against church rules.
20. He would not pray with John unless John condemned Origen's books and drove the Long Brothers from the city.
21. One of the Long Brothers asked him whether he had read any of their books.
22. Augustine wrote his own book on the heresies in the order of Epiphanius's list.
23. Four centuries on, when Christians quarreled over pictures of Christ and the saints in churches, both sides claimed him, and opponents of images quoted writings under his name.
24. In 787 the council at Nicaea ruled that those writings were not his, arguing that his eighty chapters never listed the making of images.
25. A book written to answer the council's argument replied with his own letter about the torn curtain.
26. Epiphanius set one thing more after the eighty heresies in his chest of cures: the church, which he calls Christ's holy bride and says was there from the beginning.

## Non-HELD findings

**13. SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.** "The monks there" follows "near Epiphanius's own monastery", so the complaint lands on his own monks. The letter puts it in another house: the monastery whose priests were Jerome and Vincent, whose deacons came to Epiphanius with the brother, and to which he wrote afterward.
- Brief: "The monks there had complained for a year that no one celebrated the sacraments..."
- J6 10672-10674: "I saw that the monastery contained a large number of reverend brothers, and that the reverend presbyters, Jerome and Vincent, through modesty and humility, were unwilling to offer the sacrifices permitted to their rank".
- J6 10682-10684: "he came to me with the deacons of the monastery and others of the brethren".
- J6 10702-10705: "After this I wrote to the reverend presbyters and other brothers of the monastery ... For a year before I had heard many of them complain that they had no one to celebrate for them the sacraments of the Lord."
- J6 10688-10689 (the place of the ordination): "the church of the villa which adjoins our monastery". Jerome, Letter 82, J6 18736-18739, says this "old monastery" of Epiphanius lies near Eleutheropolis.
- The outline already holds this (Do not say: "because its priests, Jerome and Vincent, held back").
- Exact change: "The monks of Jerome's monastery had complained for a year that no one celebrated the sacraments, the church's holy rites, for them, he explains, and he acted from the fear of God, to provide what the church needed."

**16. SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.** The sentence credits the claim to Theophilus's letter, but the letter does not say he drove anyone out of Egypt. It says he cut down the followers of Origen among the monks of Nitria, and that a few men were put out of the church. It adds that three of them took ship of their own accord.
- Brief: "His letter said he had driven monks who followed Origen out of Egypt."
- J6 19717-19719: "has with the sword of the gospel cut down the Origenist serpents crawling out of their caves, and has delivered from their deadly contagion the fruitful host of the monks of Nitria."
- J6 19746-19748: "no small joy has filled the hearts of the people both in Alexandria and throughout all Egypt, because a few men have been expelled from the Church".
- J6 19729-19731: "Ammonius, Eusebius, and Euthymius ... have taken ship for Constantinople".
- Exact change: "His letter said he had put a few monks who followed Origen out of the church in Egypt."

## HELD and SUMMARY HOLDS

1. SUMMARY HOLDS. HOLL1 13422-13424: "αἱρέσεών τε καὶ κακοδόξου γνώμης ἀνθρωπείας ὑπὸ πεπλανημένων ἀνδρῶν ἐν κόσμῳ ἐσπαρμένης, ἐξότε ἄνθρωπος ἐπὶ γῆς πέπλασται" (heresies and wrong human opinion, sown in the world by men led astray, since man was formed on earth). On Adam, HOLL1 13591 (Barbarism, the first): "ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς Ἀδὰμ τῇ ἕκτῃ ἡμέρᾳ πλασθεὶς". The source says "sown by" these people where the brief says "held by". That is a fair compression.
2. HELD. AUG 2415: "Cujus ego in commemorandis haereticis non modum, sed ordinem sum secutus"; MEN title line: "held A.D. 787"; HOLL1 13424-13426 (the regnal date of about 375), so 787 is four centuries on.
3. HELD. HOLL1 13424-13426: "βασιλείας Οὐαλεντινιανοῦ καὶ Οὐάλεντος ἑνδεκάτου ἔτους καὶ Γρατιανοῦ ἑβδόμου". This is his own regnal date. The year is plain arithmetic on it (Holl's note, HOLL1 13447), so the rough "about" is right.
4. HELD. HOLL1 12510-12531: "ὁμοῦ δὲ πᾶσαί εἰσιν ὀγδοήκοντα", with the list running through "εἰκοστὴ Ἡρῳδιανῶν" and then "ἡ ἔνσαρκος αὐτοῦ παρουσία"; Augustine agrees, AUG 2421-2423 ("separatis viginti ... reliquas ... sexaginta"). For names, teaching and answer, see HOLL1 12388-12391.
