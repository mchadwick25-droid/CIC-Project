# Dossier: the Marburg Colloquy (1529)

Research dossier for the council profile drafter. All files are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are from the vendored file as it stands on this branch. Translations marked "(my tr.)" are mine. Spelling in quotes is the file's own, OCR errors included.

**Headline.** The colloquy's own record is **not held**: no protocol, no participant's account written at the time (Osiander, Brenz, Hedio, Collin, Melanchthon, the anonymous report), no Luther letter from Marburg, and no signed text of the Marburg Articles. What **is** held:
- three participants writing afterwards: Zwingli in 1530, twice; Oecolampadius in 1531; Luther in 1538, through an editor's quotation;
- Luther's and Zwingli's own teaching on the Supper from just before and just after the meeting;
- an English summary of the fifteen articles by John Foxe (16th c.);
- two rival Catholic histories, Sarpi (1619) and Pallavicino (1656/1664);
- several modern editors who give the dates.

A non-thin profile is possible if it says early that the colloquy's own minutes and the signed articles are not in the Library, and that the article list we have is Foxe's. The two famous lines (the chalk "Hoc est corpus meum" and "you have a different spirit") are **not in any held file** and must not be used.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. Zwingli preaches in the prince's castle (participant).** In 1530 Zwingli wrote to Philip of Hesse:
> "You ask a thing, most pious Prince, which you might fairly have claimed or demanded — the sermon that I delivered in your castle at Marburg." (ZW2 6752-6754; Hinke's English)

Hinke, the editor, places the sermon "at Marburg during the Marburg Colloquy, October 1-4, 1529" (ZW2 6733-6734). That date is Hinke's.

**B. The prince calls them together (later narrative).** Sarpi:
> "the Landgrave of Ha/a ... procured: a Conference, follicited the Suiſſes to ſend their men, and aſſigned Marpzig for the place of the diſputation ... the month of Ofeber of the fame year 1529. There came out of Saxony, Luther and his two Scholars; and out of Suifſerland, Zuinglins and Ecolampadius." (SARPI 12292-12306; the OCR reads "Marpzig" for Marburg and "Ofeber" for October)

**C. The long journey (friendly later narrative).** Myconius, 1532:
> "He made also the long journey to Marburg, to the colloquy with Luther and Melanchthon upon the Lord's Supper. He feared nothing there, for the place was suitable." (ZW1 1342-1345)

Walter's note (1848), after Merle d'Aubigné, adds: "On the 31st of August Zuingle quitted Zurich to proceed toward Marburg; but they did not meet there till Sept. 30th." (WALT 1574-1576). This is the editor's dating.

**D. Luther's charge at the table (participant, hostile to Luther).** Oecolampadius to Bucer, 1531, on Servetus's book:
> "quasi scilicet Luthero probetur, qui Marpurgi obiiciebat de nobis tale quid sparsum esse" (OEC 57018-57020)

(my tr.) "as if, of course, it had Luther's approval, who at Marburg charged that some such thing was being spread about us."

Here Luther charges the Swiss and Strasbourg men with unsound talk of the Trinity. The letter's subject is the book *De Trinitatis erroribus* (57014-57015). This line shows the session's mood, but it is a single remark recalled later.

Recommendation: scene A, Zwingli's own words. It is the only held line that puts a participant in a room at Marburg in his own voice. Use B for who came.

## 3. Issue

- **The question.** What the words "This is my body" mean.
  - Sarpi: "Luther taught that the words of our Lord (This is my Body) ought to be received in a naked and plain ſenſe; and, on the contrary, Zuinglins taught that the words were figuratively, ſpiritually, and ſacramentally, not carnally to be underſtood" (SARPI 12274-12280).
  - Foxe: Luther "dissented from Zuinglius in this one matter of the sacrament" (FOXE 24304-24305).
- **Luther's side, in his own words (1529, the year of the meeting).** "Now here stands the Word of Christ: Take, eat; this is My body; Drink ye all of it; this is the new testament in My blood, etc. Here we abide, and would like to see those who will constitute themselves His masters, and make it different from what He has spoken." (LC 4099-4102)
  - The same passage names opponents as "a hundred thousand devils, together with all fanatics" asking "How can bread and wine be the body and blood of Christ?" (LC 4094-4097).
  - Luther in 1528 (quoted in BOC): "Beware, beware, I say, of the alloeosis; for it is a mask of the devil" (BOC 33405-33411).
- **Zwingli's side, in his own words (1530, nine months after).** "the words: 'This is my body,' should not be understood naturally, but figuratively, just as the words: 'This is Jehovah's passover'" (ZW2 2826-2828). Article 8: "the true body of Christ is present by the contemplation of faith ... But that the body of Christ in essence and really ... is either present in the supper or masticated with our mouth and teeth, as the Papists or some* who look back to the fleshpots of Egypt assert, we not only deny..." (ZW2 2660-2670). In Hinke's notes the asterisk elsewhere in this work marks "The Lutherans" (ZW2 2695).
- **The politics.** Sarpi gives Philip's motive: to keep "his side in peace, with hope to reconcile the contrary opinions, as well to maintain his promise, as to withstand future dangers" (SARPI 12292-12296). Pallavicino says Philip sought "con la concordia" to strengthen "le forze degl'Innouatori contra la parte Cattolica" (PAL 16548-16550). (my tr.) "by agreement ... the forces of the innovators against the Catholic side." Waterworth says the aim was reconciliation, and that a political union followed in part (WATER 2161-2171). Buchheim (1885) says the alliance scheme "was frustrated by the well-known theological difference between the adherents of Luther and Zwingli" (BUCH 3286-3289).

## 7. Decided

The acts are not held. The only held list of what was agreed is Foxe's English summary (FOXE 24305-24325):
> "yet in all other states of doctrine they did accord, as appeared in the synod holden at Marburg ... where both Luther and Zuinglius were present, and conferring together, agreed in these articles: I. In the Unity and Trinity of God. 2. In the incarnation of the Word. 3. In the passion and resurrection of Christ. 4. In the article of original sin. 5. In the article of faith in Christ Jesus. 6. That this faith cometh not of merits, but by the gift of God. 7. That this faith is our righteousness. 8. Touching the extern word. 9. Likewise they agreed in the articles of baptism, 10. Of good works. 11. Of confession. 12. Of magistrates. 13. Of men's traditions. 14. Of baptism of infants. 15. Lastly, concerning the doctrine of the Lord's Supper; this they did believe, and hold: first, that both kinds thereof are to be ministered to the people ... that the mass is no such work for which a man may obtain grace both for the quick and the dead. Item, that the sacrament (which they call of the altar) is a true sacrament of the body and blood of the Lord. Item, that the spiritual manducation of his body and blood is necessary for every christian man. ... &c."

Caution for the drafter:
- Foxe's article 15 stops at "&c." (24325). **It does not give the article's closing statement that they had not agreed on whether the body is bodily in the bread.** No held file gives that clause.
- Bensow names "femton artiklar", "in essentials drawn up by Luther", adopted 4 Oct 1529 and "därför äfven kallades Marburgartiklarna" (BENS 31258-31261) (my tr.) "and therefore also called the Marburg Articles". The phrase "likaledes i det väsentliga af Luther uppställda" (my tr.) is "likewise in essentials drawn up by Luther". This is Bensow's claim.
- Sarpi: "It was neceſſary to end the Colloquy without concluſion, but that by the means of the Landgrave they a[greed] in this, that being of accord in all other points, ... hereafter to abſtain from bitterneſs in this particular, praying to ſhew ſome light of agreement" (SARPI 12330-12336).
- No held source names who signed. The "agreement on 14 of 15" figure does not appear as such in any held source. Foxe and Sarpi say "all other points" (Foxe, Sarpi).

## 8. Outcome

- **Schwabach and Augsburg.** Bensow: the 17 Schwabach Articles were adopted at Schwabach on 16 Oct 1529 as the condition for joining the evangelical estates' alliance. They are "egentligen blott en utvidgning af" the fifteen Marburg articles, and Melanchthon built the Augsburg Confession's doctrinal articles on them (BENS 31249-31261). (my tr.) "really only an expansion of".
- **No alliance.** Buchheim: "Luther and his more immediate followers decided that it would not be justifiable to form an alliance with the Zwinglians" (BUCH 3300-3302). He does not name Marburg.
- **Augsburg 1530, separate confessions.** Hinke: "neither the Catholics nor the Lutherans would have anything to do with them." He quotes Bucer to Zwingli, 23 June 1530: "Nothing more intolerant can be imagined than the hatred which the Lutherans have against us" (ZW2 1969-1973). Hence the Tetrapolitana and Zwingli's own Fidei ratio (ZW2 1973-1983).
- **Catholic reading.** Eck (1530): "the peace patched up at Marburg was a flimsy affair" (ZW2 3601-3602). Waterworth (1848): "they separated, after much violent discussion, as irreconcilable enemies as ever" (WATER 2164-2165).
- **Bitterness renewed.** Sarpi says the truce was "not followed by their Successors" (SARPI 12336-12340). Bonnet records Luther's *Short Confession* of 1544 renewing "his invectives against the adversaries of the sacrament", insulting Zwingli's memory (calvin_letters-vol1 19593-19596).
- **Reconciling moments.** Luther in 1538 praised Zwingli and Oecolampadius (BON 3300-3304; see §13). Bucer kept working for formulas with Luther (Hinke note, ZW2 6839-6843).
- **The lasting break.** The Formula of Concord (1577): the "Zwinglian teachers are not to be reckoned among the theologians who acknowledge and profess the Augsburg Confession, as they separated from them" (BOC 26604-26607).
- **Reach beyond the two parties.**
  - Hutterite chronicle: the reformers "find sie bald des Sakraments halb in zwei ruchlose Völker zerteilet" (HUT 5387-5388; "find" is the OCR for "sind") (my tr.) "were soon split into two godless peoples over the sacrament". It sets "Der Luther mit seinem Anhang" against "Der Zwingel und die Seinen" (5394-5398). It does not name Marburg.
  - Helweg (Danish, 1851): "ligesom Zwingli var rede til i Marborg at rokke Luther sin Broderhaand, saaledes vare de schweitseriskt reformerede Menigheder altid rede til at anerkjende Samfundet med de lutherske" (HELW 8452-8455; OCR uses long-s, "ſ"). (my tr.) "just as Zwingli was ready at Marburg to offer Luther his brotherly hand, so the Swiss Reformed congregations were always ready to acknowledge fellowship with the Lutherans."
  - Schwenckfeld's editor: Schwenckfeld was a critic "of the Marburg Colloquy" (SCHW 26439-26440).

## 9. The refusing party's voice

Neither side was condemned at Marburg. Each refused the other's reading.
- **Luther's refusal**, in his own words of 1529: "Here we abide" (LC 4100). In 1528: "a mask of the devil" (BOC 33410-33411). **His Marburg-day words are not held.**
- **Zwingli's**, in his own words: the Fidei ratio (ZW2 2660-2671, 2826-2828). His Marburg dedication also argues that difference "upon other points" should not break charity (ZW2 6768-6773, quoted in §10).
- Hostile views of the two:
  - Pallavicino says Zwingli yielded on many articles and Luther's "superbia" yielded none: "Zvvinglio ... fi lafciafle efpugnare in affai articoli ... non potè impetrarne veruno dalla fuperbia dell'emulo" (PAL 16558-16561) (my tr.) "Zwingli let himself be won over on many articles ... but could not obtain a single one from his rival's pride". This is hostile polemic against both.
  - Sarpi offers three possible reasons for the failure, one being that Luther "would not by Zuinglius his form of words, which the Romaniſts ſo much abhorred, make his Princes more odious" (SARPI 12314-12322). Sarpi attributes this to a letter Luther wrote "a little after ... to a Friend". **That letter is not held.**

## 11. Contested points

1. **Who debated whom.**
   - Sarpi: "Only Luther and Zuinglius disputed" (SARPI 12306).
   - Myconius: a colloquy "with Luther and Melanchthon" (ZW1 1343).
   - BCU: Melanchthon and Oecolampadius did not debate each other in person (BCU 27288-27290).
   - Pallavicino names eight men in two parties (PAL 16555-16557).
2. **How it ended.**
   - A truce and agreement on all but one point: Sarpi (SARPI 12330-12336) and Foxe (FOXE 24305).
   - Unreconciled: Waterworth, "as irreconcilable enemies as ever" (WATER 2164-2165); Youngs/Mosheim, "nor could either of the contending parties ... be persuaded to abandon, or even to modify, their opinion" (YOUNGS 18806-18810).
   - A "flimsy" patched peace: Eck (ZW2 3601-3602).
3. **Who gave ground.** Pallavicino says Zwingli yielded many articles and Luther none (PAL 16558-16561). Pallavicino then says "il Soave" (Sarpi) makes two errors in telling this, the first being that Zwingli and Luther, independently, agreed in everything but the Eucharist (PAL 16572-16590; OCR badly damaged). No friendly held source settles it.
4. **Why it failed.** Sarpi weighs three reasons and settles none (SARPI 12310-12322).
5. **Start date.** 30 Sept (Walter after Merle, WALT 1575) against 1 Oct (Hinke, ZW2 6734). Probably arrival against sessions. Keep both by name.
6. **Length.** "Many days" (Sarpi; Pallavicino) against "four days" (Youngs after Mosheim). Hinke's 1-4 Oct implies four.
7. **Myconius as a "Zurich writer"** (the census phrase). Not established by any held line: see §14.

## 12. Absences (honest limits), with the searches run
