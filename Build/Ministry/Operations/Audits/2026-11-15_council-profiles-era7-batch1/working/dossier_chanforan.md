# Dossier: Synod of Chanforan (Angrogna, Piedmont), 1532

Research dossier for the council profile drafter. Every line cited is `file:line` in `cic/texts/`. Translations marked [my tr.] are mine. OCR spellings are kept as in the file (long s shows as `f` in Perrin 1619, Gilles 1644, Morland, Léger). "Verbatim" here means as the OCR reads; every quote still needs the page-image check the file headers ask for.

**The headline for the drafter:** the meeting's own record is held only in two forms. One is later printings of the articles (Perrin 1619, Morland 1658, Léger 1669, Gilles's summary of 1644). The other is two short quotations of a manuscript at Trinity College Dublin, given by Muston (1866) and Montet (1885). The held witnesses disagree on the year: most say **12 September 1532**, Perrin says **12 September 1535**, Léger's heading reads (in OCR) **December 1531**, and Montet's quotation of the manuscript reads **1533**. The name "Chanforan(s)" enters the held record through one witness: a guard's deposition, which Gilles reports and places in the inquiry of **1535**. Farel's presence rests on that deposition.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The guard on the path (deposition; Gilles).** GILLES-81:2440-2452 (= GILLES-44:3084-3092):
> "Jeanet Peiret d'Angrogne, l'un des surpris par Bersour faisant la garde, déposa le 22 de Septembre qu'ils faisoyent la garde pour les Ministres qui enseignent la bonne loy, qui estoyent assemblez en la bourgade des Chanforans au milieu d'Angrogne, et dit qu'entre les autres il y en avoit un qui s'appeloit M. Farel, qui avoit la barbe rouge, et un beau cheval blanc"

[my tr.] "Jeanet Peiret of Angrogne, one of those Bersour caught standing guard, testified on 22 September that they were keeping guard for the ministers who teach the good law, who were gathered in the hamlet of Chanforans in the middle of Angrogne. He said that among them was one called Master Farel, who had a red beard and a fine white horse."

It continues: "deux autres en sa compagnie, desquels l'un avoit un cheval quasi noir, et l'autre estoit de grande stature, un peu boiteux" ("two others with him, one with an almost black horse, the other very tall and slightly lame"). Another prisoner said the barbes' synod "avoit duré six jours. Le procez Latin dit Capitulum" (2464-2467).
**Caution:** Gilles places this deposition in his account of Bersour's commission of 28 August 1535 (2328-2330). The "22 September" follows Bersour's raid at Rocheplate (2348-2352). Muston uses the same details for 12 September 1532 (6403-6410). The census edge (§14) labels its locus "year 1535". A profile that opens here must say that the deposition was taken in an inquiry three years after the meeting, or tell the dating as contested.

**B. The first question of the first day (the manuscript; Montet and Muston).** MONTET:979-981, quoting the Dublin manuscript:
> "Le propositione chesonostate disputât, en Angroma Vanno del Segnor 1533, e a di 12 de septemhro en presentia di tuti li ministri e eciam dio del populo. El primo di fo dtëputato se hora licito al Xristiano de jurar en alcun modo..."

[my tr.] "The propositions that were disputed at Angrogna in the year of the Lord 1533 [sic], on the 12th of September, in the presence of all the ministers and also of the people. On the first day it was disputed whether it was lawful for a Christian to swear in any way..."
Muston's footnote quotes the same phrase from "MS. of George Morel, Dublin, C, V, No. 18": "En presencia de tuti li ministri et eciam Dio del populo" (6392-6393). Muston also says the meeting was held "in the open air, at the hamlet of Chanforans, in presence of all the people" (6383-6384). Muston's line on the setting ("shady pieces of level ground ... the Pra du Tour, then crowned with sparkling snows", 6385-6388) is his own description and has no witness. **Caution:** the "1533" in Montet's quotation stands against his own "Synode d'Angrogne de 1532" a few lines up (940) and at 9125-9126. It may be a misprint or an OCR slip; a page image is needed. Do not quote the year from this line.

**C. The letters read aloud, the oath sworn (printed articles; Perrin, Morland).** PERRIN-19:12001-12012:
> "Et là apres auoir leu les lettres desdits Oecolampade & Bucer ... les propositions ou articles suiuans furent dressés, leus, approuués, signés, & iuré par tous les assistans vnanimement de les conseruer, obseruer, croire & retenir parmi eux inuiolablement"

[my tr.] "And there, after reading the letters of Oecolampadius and Bucer ... the following propositions or articles were drawn up, read, approved, signed and sworn by all present, with one voice, to keep, observe, believe and hold among them without breach."
MORLAND:7944-7947: "The following Articles having been then framed, read, approved, and signed by all that were present, they with one accord did swear to believe, hold and observe them inviolably". (Perrin dates this assembly 1535; Morland 1532.)

**D. The priests walk out (Perrin).** PERRIN-19:12136-12154: the articles "estonnerent les Prestres qui estoyent parmi eux pour perceuoir les reuenus des Cures ... ils se retirerent sans sonner mot. Ainsi en leur retraitte la Messe se bannit d'elle mesme des vallees". [my tr.] "astonished the priests who were among them to collect the parish revenues ... they withdrew without a word. So with their going, the Mass banished itself from the valleys." PERRIN-47:5375-5382 ("they departed without speaking a word"). This is Perrin's alone; no other held witness has it.

## 3. Issue

- **What happened before.** The Waldensians of Provence and Dauphiné sent two barbes, George Morel of Freissinières and Pierre Masson of Burgundy, to the Reformers in 1530: BEZA-T1:3312-3320 (Oecolampadius at Basel, Capito and Bucer at Strasbourg, Haller at Berne); PERRIN-19:14134-14145 (same four); MORLAND:21825-21829; LEGER:22912-22914; MONTET:8991-8994 ("C'est en 1530"). Masson was taken at Dijon (MORLAND:21828-21829; PERRIN-47:3318); Muston gives his death date as "10th of September, 1530" (6367-6368), with no held primary line. Gilles names an earlier traveller, "Barbe Martin du Val Luserne" in 1526, from a prisoner's deposition (GILLES-81:1856-1866).
- **The barbes' own questions (Waldensian voice).** Morel to Oecolampadius, in Occitan, PERRIN-19:14164-14182, and in French at 14214-14224: "Nous tels que nous sommes, pauures instructeurs de ce petit peuple, auons demeuré plus de quatre cens ans parmi des cruelles espines" ([my tr.] "We, such as we are, poor teachers of this little people, have lived more than four hundred years among cruel thorns"). On their practice, MONTET:9086-9101, quoting Morel's Latin: "Inter nos nemo ducit uxorem" ("Among us no one marries"); "Sacramentorum signa plebeculae nostrae non nos, sed Antichristi membra administrant" ("The signs of the sacraments are given to our little people not by us but by the members of Antichrist"). On predestination, Morel's Latin quoting Luther: "Si omnia necessitate contingunt ... quorsum tot scripturae, et praedicatores?" (MONTET:9135-9139; Muston's English paraphrase 6347-6358). Montet counts "quarante-sept questions" (9102). Zezschwitz: in 1530 they "wissen ... noch nichts von Prädestination" (6332-6333).
- **The Reformers' demands (Reformed voice).** Oecolampadius, 1530, against dissimulation: "Nous auons sceu que la peur d'estre persecutés vous fait dissimuler en vostre foy & que vous la cachés" (PERRIN-19:14281-14283; [my tr.] "We have learned that fear of persecution makes you dissemble your faith and hide it"). And: "si vous communiqués auec les infidelles en allant à leurs Messes abominables, vous y entendés des blasphemes" (14296-14298). Gilles sums up the Reformers' advice as three heads: settling points of doctrine on which some disagreed; setting up church order and assemblies "plus à descouvert"; and forbidding members to attend Mass or use Roman priests (GILLES-81:1886-1899).
- **The two sides at the meeting.** The majority, with the visiting ministers, wanted the changes (GILLES-81:1913-1925). Some barbes held that the new rules "n'estoyent pas totalement nécessaires", that they dishonoured the memory of earlier leaders, and that going public would provoke persecution (2030-2041). Their own words are not held (§9).

## 4. Called by

No held line names a convener. Gilles writes only "fut convoquée une Assemblée générale en Angrogne" (GILLES-81:1914), after "quelques principaux Pasteurs de ces pays-là estans venus és Valées pour aider à establir le tout" (1911-1913). Perrin: "ils s'assemblerent de toutes leurs vallees" (PERRIN-19:11971-11972), with no convener. Muston: "a synod was held in the commune of Angrogna, to which representatives of all the Vaudois parishes repaired" (6378-6380). Absence searched (§12).

## 5. Attended

- **Who, as each witness puts it:**
  - Perrin: "tous les chefs de famille auec leurs Pasteurs", from all their valleys (PERRIN-19:11974-11975).
  - Morland: "the Ministers, and Heads of Families of the Churches of the Valleys of Piemont" (7937-7940).
  - Léger: "les Pasteurs & Chefs des Familles" (13066).
  - Gilles: "des Pasteurs, et principaux des Eglises Vaudoises, tant des Valées, que des pays circonvoisins" (GILLES-81:1916-1918).
  - The manuscript: "tuti li ministri e eciam dio del populo" (MONTET:981).
  - Muston: representatives "not only from the valleys, but also from Calabria, Saluces, Provence, and Dauphiny" (6380-6382). Muston also has the lords "of Miradol, Rivenoble, and Solaro" appear (6399-6401), with no cited witness. Treat that line as Muston's alone.
- **Visitors from Switzerland:** Farel, with two unnamed companions on horseback, in Peiret's deposition (GILLES-81:2446-2450; dated by Gilles's context to 1535). Muston has "Farel ... Saulnier accompanied him" at 1532 (6403-6405) and later "The Synod of Angrogna, at which Farel and Saulnier had been present" (7484-7485). The Baum and Cunitz note (BEZA-T1:2434-2435, from Herminjard): "En 1532 Olivetan visita avec Farel et Saunier les vallées". Olivetan going to the Waldensians with Farel, journey undated: COP10:48140-48141 (Libertet to Farel, 1539). Saunier worked among the Waldensians "comite Olivetano", undated: COP10 editors' note 26288-26291. Gilles names no visitor for 1532 (only "quelques principaux Pasteurs de ces pays-là"). The two dissenters blamed "quelques Ministres venus d'Alemagne" (GILLES-81:2055-2056), and the Bohemian letter speaks of "certains Docteurs Suisses" (2093-2094).
- **Counts:** none held. Perrin and Morland say "all present" signed; no number is given. Muston gives no count. Gilles has one prisoner's "six jours" for the duration, not a count.
- **Lists of names:** no subscription list is held. Names held for the meeting are Farel (deposition), Daniel de Valence and Jean de Molines (Gilles), and Saunier and Olivetan (Muston, and the editors' notes). Barbes named by Bersour's prisoners as synod leaders, "Louys le plus vieux, Estiennes, Daniel, et Luc", come from the 1535 inquiry and are not tied to the 1532 meeting (GILLES-81:2301-2302).

## 7. Decided

**From the printed articles** (PERRIN-19:12019-12133; PERRIN-47:5299-5373; MORLAND:7952-8036; LEGER:13076-13174, the same seventeen in the same order). Morland's numbering:
1. Worship "in spirit and in truth" (7952-7954).
2. The saved were "elected of God, before the Foundation of the World" (7965-7966).
3. "It is impossible that those that are appointed to salvation, should not be saved" (7968-7969).
4. "Whosoever upholds Free-will denieth absolutely Predestination, and the Grace of God" (7971-7972).
5. No work is good unless God commands it, and none bad unless he forbids it (7974-7975).
6. A Christian may swear by God's name, and before magistrates (7977-7983).
7. Auricular confession is not commanded; three kinds of confession are set out (7985-7993).
8. Rest on the Lord's Day (7995-7998).
9. No revenge (8000-8001).
10. A Christian may be a magistrate over Christians (8003-8004).
11. No fixed fast days (8006-8008).
12. Marriage is forbidden to no one (8010-8011).
13. Forbidding marriage is "a Diabolical Doctrine" (8019).
14. Whoever lacks the gift of chastity must marry (8021).
15. Ministers are not to move from place to place except for the church's good (8023-8024).
16. Ministers may own property to keep their families (8026-8028).
17. Two sacraments only, Baptism and the Eucharist (8030-8036).

**Gilles's summary** (GILLES-81:1920-1985) adds what the articles do not: the meeting decided "de rejetter toutes les dissimulations ... sans participer plus en aucune sorte ... aux cérémonies superstitieuses de l'Eglise Romaine" (1922-1926). Gilles says his list is only "en substance le principal" and that the full proofs were "par les actes et manuscrit qui en fut dressé" (1993-1997). He also mentions "autres nécessaires règlemens ... pour la conduite extérieure de l'Eglise" (2023-2026), not listed.

**The manuscript, as Muston and Montet quote it**, differs from the printed seventeen. Its order begins with oaths ("El primo di fo disputato se hora licito al Xristiano de jurar", MONTET:981; Muston 6454-6458). It has struck-out items: Sunday work, laying on of hands ("an external thing, in which every one is left at liberty"), and usury (Muston 6472-6500). It has at least 23 numbered articles (Muston gives XVIII-XXIII, 6495-6522). It ends with a closing declaration: "we have with one consent agreed to the present declaration ... nothing may henceforth divide us" (Muston 6529-6538, Muston's English). Montet's Italianate lines are at 9141-9182, for example "El christiano po jurare licitament al nome de Dio" (9141) and "Nos non havemo si non doy signi sacramentals, che Cristo noy ha lasat" (9181).

**The Bible.** Muston says the synod resolved to print a French Bible, and that the Swiss urged a translation "carefully compared with the original texts" (6411-6440). His only source is Olivetan's "Apologie du translateur" (6440-6441), which is not held. Perrin has the decision come after the articles: "ils se resolurent de faire imprimer promptement toute la Bible" (PERRIN-19:12155-12158).

## 8. Outcome

- **The Mass ends in the valleys** (Perrin alone, PERRIN-19:12136-12154).
- **Persecution.** Perrin puts the ducal commissioner Bersour's raid with 500 men right after the 1532 decision to preach openly (PERRIN-47:5238-5262). Gilles dates Bersour's patents to 28 August 1535 and the raid at Rocheplate after them (GILLES-81:2328-2352). Martyrdoms in 1535 follow, among them Catelan Girardet, burned (2402-2430).
- **The Dissenters and Bohemia.** The Brethren wrote on 27 June 1533. The Val S. Martin assembly of 15 August 1533 confirmed "la conclusion faite l'année précédente en Angrogne" (GILLES-81:2152-2180). The two dissenters withdrew "pour vivre en leur particulier" and carried off "ce qu'ils peurent des manuscripts, et mémoires anciennes des Vaudois" (2181-2189).
- **Gilles on the pace:** persecution and wars kept the decisions from being carried out "qu'avec une assez longue suite de temps" (GILLES-81:2191-2195).
- **The Bible of 1535.**
  - Perrin: "quinze cens escus d'or à vn Imprimeur" at Neuchâtel, "la premiere impression de la Bible Françoise qui s'est veue en France" (PERRIN-19:12160-12164).
  - Morland: printed "at their own proper cost and charges" in 1535 (5740-5746), and he deposited a copy (4355-4357).
  - Léger: Olivetan "aux frais des Vallées de Piémont a traduit & fait imprimer, à Neufchâtel" (19299-19300; the year in the OCR is unreadable).
  - Beza's history: "à leur despens, à Neufchastel ... par Pierre Robert Olivetan avec l'aide de Jean Calvin" (BEZA-T1:3330-3340).
  - Muston, from the book itself: the preface is dated "from the Alps, this seventh of February, 1535"; the colophon gives Neuchâtel, Pierre de Wingle, 4 June 1535; the cost was 1500 golden crowns (6595-6622). Muston: "originated through the influence of Farel" (6626).
  - The Waldensians' authorship of the commission is in Olivetan's own preface: "par un certain pauure peuple le tien amy & frere en Jesus Christ ... A donne cette charge & commission de tirer & desployer iceluy thresor hors des armaires & coffres Ebraicques & Grecz" (MORLAND:5957-5969). The preface does not name the Waldensians in the held lines; Morland and Léger identify the "poor people" as the valleys.
- **Gonin's mission of 1536** to Geneva for books; he was drowned at Grenoble (PERRIN-19:12164-12175; Muston 6650-6730, who has letters of Farel and Saulnier on him, 6721-6723).
- **Temples built.** Open preaching began at Angrogne in 1555 and a temple was built; more followed in Val Luserne in 1555 and Val S. Martin in 1556 (GILLES-81:3030-3047). Foxe has open preaching from August 1555 and a church "in the midst of Angrogne" (FOXE4:38786-38793).
- **The Reformed union, told through events.** Montet dates "le triomphe du Protestantisme ... absolu et définitif" to the Union des Vallées of 11 November 1571, quoting its oath "de continuer tous en la profession de la vraie Religion chrétienne réformée" (9165-9170). He cites the Provence confession of 1543 to Sadoleto as "un véritable symbole protestant" (9158-9164). Gilles's preface: the churches joined "aux autres églises réformées" about 1530 (238-241).
- **Reach beyond one tradition.** The Bible was given to the French-speaking church. Morland says "for the benefit of the Evangelical Churches where this Language was in use" (5746), and Beza's history says "la France est redevable" to Olivetan (BEZA-T1:2405). The Unity of the Brethren in Bohemia answered the dissenters (Gilles; Blahoslav in GOLL 7159-7186).

## 9. The refusing party's voice

**Not held in their own words.** No writing by Daniel de Valence or Jean de Molines is held (searched §12). Their case reaches us three ways:
1. **Gilles (opponent, 1644):** their reasons were that the new rules were not wholly necessary, that they "deshonoroit la mémoire de ceux qui avoyent tant heureusement conduit ces Eglises", and that openness would provoke persecution (GILLES-81:2030-2041). They "partirent sans congé de l'assemblée" (2051-2052). They complained of "prétendues innovations ... à l'instigation (disoyent-ils) de quelques Ministres venus d'Alemagne, ausquels on avoit trop facilement donné entrée et audience" (2053-2057). Gilles's later charge that they carried off manuscripts (2185-2189) is his alone.
2. **The Bohemian Brethren's letter of 27 June 1533, as Gilles summarises it** (a third party taking their side): the Brethren marvelled "qu'en ces Eglises ... on eust si facilement donné entrée à des Docteurs estrangers, et incognus" (GILLES-81:2109-2112). They urged the Alpine churches to reassemble and weigh all "par la seule règle de la Parole de Dieu, sans s'esmouvoir pour les gloses des hommes" (2113-2120).
3. **The Brethren's own chronicle** (Blahoslav 1556, in GOLL 7159-7186): the two came "a coetu Waldensium" and reported "quandam mutationem atque dissidium seu discordiam exortam". Their question was whether it was true "mutationem quandam accidisse in hac Fratrum Bohemorum Unitate" and that ministers "qui ante caelibes vixerant, uxores duxisse". They stayed "per integrum semestre unum", praised all they saw, and went home to France. Goll's note names them as Valence and Molines (7223-7226). COMEN §78 has the same in Lasitius's words (2805-2816).
Zezschwitz notes "verschiedne Berichte über diese Reise" and a view (Herzog, after Gieseler) that the warning letter came from the stricter small opposition party among the Brethren. He himself holds it came from the main body (11560-11575).

## 11. Contested points

1. **The year.** 1532 (Gilles 1644; Morland 1658; Muston; Montet; Zezschwitz) against 1535 (Perrin 1619, French and English) against December 1531 (Léger 1669, as the OCR reads). Montet's quotation of the MS reads 1533 (likely a slip). No held writer discusses Perrin's 1535 (searched `perrin` near `153|synod|angrog|date` in Muston, Montet, Gilles, Zezschwitz). Perrin's narrative itself carries 1532 as the year of the decision to preach openly (11956). Tell the date as contested: most held witnesses say 12 September 1532, and Perrin, the earliest printed history, says 1535.
2. **Whether Farel was there in 1532, and whether "Chanforans" names this meeting.** The only primary-type line is Peiret's deposition, which Gilles places in 1535 (GILLES-81:2328-2452). The 1532 placement is Muston's (6403-6410) and the Baum and Cunitz note's, from Herminjard (BEZA-T1:2434-2435). Gilles in ch. V names no visitor. Gilles's own phrasing, "des Pasteurs qui continuoyent de venir des quartiers de Suisse" (2450-2451), allows repeated visits. Muston also borrows "six days" from the same 1535 depositions.
3. **What the dissenters did in Bohemia.** Perrin (PERRIN-19:6542-6550, 14672-14680; PERRIN-47:3296-3305, 6832-6836), Morland (21810-21820) and Léger (22897-22906) say the two were *sent* by the barbes to serve the Bohemian churches and *betrayed* them to their enemies, causing persecution. Léger softens this to "par une trop grande imprudence". Gilles says they left the 1532 assembly in protest, and that the Brethren's letter "ne parle qu'honorablement, et ne fait aucune plainte des deux Barbes" (GILLES-81:2146-2148). The Brethren's chronicle (GOLL 7159-7186) has them warmly received for six months, with no betrayal.
4. **Who wrote the Bohemian warning letter:** the main Brethren (Zezschwitz) or the strict minority party (Herzog, after Gieseler, as Zezschwitz reports, 11560-11575).
5. **Turning point or no change?** Léger: at the Reformation "Je ne trouve point qu'ils y ayent changé la moindre chose", except on bearing arms (LEGER:23072-23080); his Book I argues continuity "sans interruption, & necessité de Reformation" (62). Gilles: the Reformers found "quelques défauts" to correct (GILLES-81:1888-1890), and a minority of barbes objected. Montet: "La doctrine protestante était en fait une nouveauté pour les Vaudois" (9214); "ce qui était erreur avant 1530 avait été proclamé vérité quelques années après" (9218-9220). Zezschwitz: in 1530 they knew nothing of predestination; the 1532 decrees "athmen den entschiedensten Prädestinatianismus" (6304-6312, 6332-6333). Montet makes 1571, not 1532, the point of full Protestant triumph (9165-9167). **Do not tag "joined the Reformation in 1532" Widely Accepted.** Held writers disagree about what 1532 changed, and Gilles's own preface says "environ l'année 1530" (238-241).
6. **Authenticity of the printed articles.** No held writer doubts that the 1532 articles are genuine (searched Montet, Zezschwitz, Muston, Goll and Haupt for doubt near the synod). But the printed seventeen (Perrin, Morland, Léger) differ in order and number from the manuscript described by Muston (at least XXIII, with struck items) and Montet. Gilles says his own list is a summary. Montet's charge of rewriting concerns other Waldensian manuscripts, not the articles (8370-8372). Tag the content as Documented through later printings, with the manuscript's difference noted.
7. **Pral on 14 August (Muston 6571-6572) or Val S. Martin on 15 August (Gilles 2153-2154; Montet 9154-9155), 1533.**
8. **Where the dissenters came from.** Gilles: "venus de dehors des Valées" (2049-2050). Muston: "to Dauphiny" (6546-6547). Perrin lists them under "De Provence" (PERRIN-19:6541-6542; PERRIN-47:3296-3297). Léger: Molines "de la Vallée de Queyras" (22897).
9. **Did all sign?** Perrin and Morland say all present signed and swore. Muston says "signed by the greater part" and "two pastors having refused to sign" (6539-6542), and attributes the refusal to Gilles, who in fact says they "partirent sans congé" (2051) and does not mention signing.
