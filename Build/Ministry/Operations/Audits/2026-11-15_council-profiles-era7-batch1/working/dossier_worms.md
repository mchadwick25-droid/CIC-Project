# Dossier: Diet of Worms (1521)

Research agent, 2026-11-15. All files are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines (grep -n). Translations marked "(my tr.)" are mine. OCR is quoted as it stands (long s as f, broken words) and marked [sic] only where it would mislead.

**Bottom line.** A non-thin, two-sided profile is possible. The imperial side's own act is held: the Edict of Worms, in its German text, in Mirbt no. 341. The draft grievances of the German nation are held there too (no. 340). Luther's speech is held in Foxe's full English version. Luther's own later account is held in the Table Talk. The Catholic side's narrative is held in Pallavicino (Jesuit, 1664, built on Aleander's letters) and Waterworth (1848, after Pallavicino). Three things are not held in their own words: Charles V's French declaration of 19 April 1521, Aleander's dispatches, and the official record (Reichstagsakten). Each reaches us through a named later writer. None of them is in `CFT-Wants.md` (no Worms row exists; see section 12).

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The second hearing, evening of 18 April (Foxe).** The princes are seated. It is after six. The room is so full Luther is "almost smothered for the press" (FOXE4 21843). Eck asks for a plain answer. Luther:
> "my  conscience is  so  bound  and  captived  in  these  Scriptures  and  the  word  of  God  which  I  have alleged,  that  I  will  not,  nor  may  not  revoke  any  manner  of  thing ;  considering it  is  not  godly  or  lawful  to  do  any  thing  against  conscience.  Hereupon  I  stand and  rest:  I  have  not  what  else  to  say.     God  have  mercy  upon  me!'" (FOXE4 22075-22079)

Then, on the way out: "divers  Spaniards  scorned  and  scoffed  the  good  man  in  the  way  going toward  his  lodging,  hallooing  and  whooping  after  him" (22130-22131). Witness: Foxe, a later Protestant compiler.

**B. The arrival, 16 April (Catholic side).** Pallavicino: Luther came with "forse cento caualli", but "Entrò in Vormazia con otto caualli soli" and on getting down from the carriage said loudly, "Idio sarà per mi" (PALL1 9971-9976; OCR "Idio fard per mi»"). (my tr.: perhaps a hundred horse, entered Worms with only eight; "God will be for me"). Waterworth gives the same, dated: "On alighting at Worms, on the sixteenth of April, he cried out that 'God would be with him'" (WAT 1730-1733).

**C. The young emperor watches (Catholic side).** At the first hearing Charles said: "Costui certamente non mi farebbe mai diventar' Eretico" (PALL1 10010; OCR "Cq^/w cerramaue non mi farebbe mai dinentar' Eretico") (my tr.: "This man would certainly never make me a heretic"). WAT 1742-1744 has it in English: "This man at least would never make me a heretic." Both are Catholic, and Waterworth follows Pallavicino.

**D. Luther's own memory (Table Talk).** On the road, the herald asks if he will still go: "although in Worms there were as many devils as there are tiles on the houses, yet, God willing, I will go thither" (TT 3508-3509). Then in the hall: "Then Dr. Eck (the Bishop of Trier's fiscal) began, and said unto me, 'Martin, thou art called hither to give answer, whether thou acknowledgest these writings to be thy books or no?' (The books lay on a table which he showed unto me.)" (TT 3551-3554). The tiles saying is also in FOXE4 21731-21733 and SARPI 9614-9616. Bacon calls it "the famous saying imputed to Luther" (LHYM 858-859).

Recommendation: A or D for the room; C gives the other side within the first lines.

## 3. Issue

- **What had happened.** Leo X's bull *Exsurge Domine*, 15 June 1520 (MIRBT 15393), condemned Luther's articles and gave him 60 days to recant (MIRBT 15508-15512). *Decet Romanum pontificem* declared the ban: "2. Jan. 1521" by MIRBT 15394; "January 3, 1521" by LV3 287-288; "the third of January, 1521" by WAT 1661-1662. Luther burned the bull on 10 December 1520 (FOXE4 21652-21655).
- **The papal side's question.** Aleander, the nuncio, pressed for the Ban of the Empire without a hearing. PALL1 9939-9945: "vn' Eretico notorio già sentenziato dal Papa non doueua esser'vdito" (my tr.: a notorious heretic already sentenced by the Pope ought not to be heard), and a safe-conduct was not owed. WAT 1655-1659 and 1710: Aleander "was indeed averse to this summons".
- **The Estates' question.** Buchheim: the Estates warned that violent measures would cause "the greatest commotion" and asked for Luther to be summoned "not for the sake of any argumentative or learned disputation, but merely for a summary interrogatory" (FP 2373-2381). They also brought their own grievances against Rome (FP 2426-2436; MIRBT 15525-15580; TT 1919-1923).
- **Luther's question.** Recant only if convinced by Scripture (FOXE4 22073-22078; TT 3558-3562).
- **The Elector's part.** Frederick of Saxony asked that Luther's error "might first be known, before he were made a heretic" (FOXE4 21624-21632, Foxe's report of his answer to Aleander at Cologne). WAT 1711-1715: the Elector said the works "could not, he was persuaded, be from that individual's pen".

## 4. Called by

Charles V called the Diet. FOXE4 21660-21662: "the  emperor  had  commanded  and  ordained  a  sitting or  assembly  of  the  states  of  all  the  empire  to  be  holden  at  the  city  of Worms,  against  the  6th  day  of  January  next  ensuing". The same date is in the Herberstein editor (HERB 5815-5818). Luther was cited by the emperor "through the instigation of duke Frederic" (FOXE4 21667-21668). Buchheim: Frederick declined to summon him himself, so the citation came from emperor and Estates, "issued on March 6, 1521" (FP 2395-2402). TT 3498 (Luther): "On Tuesday in the Passion week... I was cited by the herald". Not disputed.

## 5. Attended

- **No list of names is held.** The Edict's own words: "unser und des heiligen reichs churfürsten, fürsten und stende jetzo hie versamelt" (MIRBT 15602-15603) (my tr.: our and the Holy Empire's electors, princes and estates now assembled here).
- **Counts held (all round figures in narratives):** the escort, "forse cento caualli", entering "con otto caualli soli" (PALL1 9971-9974); WAT 1724-1726 ("a hundred horsemen... only eight mounted attendants"). Luther: the lords had given "four hundred articles" to the emperor against the clergy (TT 3531) but, in 1530, "some hundred and four statements" (LV4 16043-16045). These are Luther's two figures and they disagree; MIRBT's excerpt of the draft numbers articles to at least 101 (15576-15579), but it is an excerpt, so do not count from it.

## 7. Decided

1. **The Diet heard Luther and did not argue his case.** Eck's two questions, as Foxe gives them "in manner as ensueth, or like in effect" (FOXE4 21765-21778): "'First,  Whether  thou  confessest  these  books... be  thine...' 'Secondly,   Whether  thou  wilt  recant  and  revoke  them'" (21771-21777).
2. **The emperor's resolve (19 April).** Foxe's English "in effect": "we  be  resolved  to  pursue  Martin  Luther  and  his  adherents, by  excommunication,  and  by  other  means... Nevertheless  we  will  not  violate  our  faith,  which  we  have  promised  him" (FOXE4 22143-22146). Pallavicino: "non voler più ascoltar Lutero... E che di poi era fermo di perseguitar costui come notorio eretico" (PALL1 10170-10177) (my tr.: he would not hear Luther any more... and was then resolved to proceed against him as a notorious heretic). He also says "Tutta la Dieta concorse nella sentenza di Cesare" (10181) (my tr.: the whole Diet agreed with the emperor's sentence). That is Pallavicino's claim; see section 11.
3. **Twenty (or twenty-one) days to go home**, without preaching on the way (FOXE4 22456-22460; PALL1 10356-10358). See section 11.
4. **The Edict of Worms** (the act, MIRBT 15600-15630). It puts Luther under the ban "zu volstreckung des decrets, Sentenz und verdamnus laut der bullen", declaring him "als von gots kirchen abgesündert gelide und einen verstopten zertrenner und offenbarn ketzer" (15601-15604). From the end of twenty days, "die sich auf den vierzehenden tag ditz gegenwurtigen monats mai enden" (15613, 15620), no one is to house, feed or help him. They are to take him prisoner and send him to the emperor (15620-15628). His followers' goods are to be seized (15629-15636). (my tr.: to carry out the decree, sentence and condemnation of the bull; "a limb cut off from God's church, an obstinate schismatic and open heretic"; "the twenty days, which end on the fourteenth day of this present month of May".) The Edict also bans his books and orders that no work on the faith be printed without the Ordinary's consent. This is in SARPI 9780-9788 and WAT 1827-1831; it is not in Mirbt's excerpt.

## 8. Outcome

- **Luther hidden, not seized.** See section 13 and scenes. FOXE4 22552-22554: Frederick "conveyed  Luther  a little  out  of  sight  secretly".
- **The Edict not enforced in Germany.** FP 2574-2575: "The  Edict  of  Worms  found,  in  general,  no  responsive reception  there"; FP 2591. WAT 1846-1849 (Catholic): the absence of Charles and the Elector's authority "rendered the Ban ineffectual". Luther himself (1530): the decree "had  to  be  changed  at  the  diet  at  Nuremberg" (LV4 15969-15971); TT 3616-3617: "the tyrants, not long after, were constrained to recall the same again".
- **Used where it could be.** It was posted with the bull against Friar Henry at Bremen (LV4 9022-9023, Luther 1525). It was cited at Augsburg in 1530: "to carry out unconditionally the Edict of Worms" (FP 3482). The Council of Regency's edict of 20 January 1522 demanded it be carried out (LV3 11681-11682).
- **Reach beyond Lutherans.** At the Diet of Speyer (1526) Ferdinand announced the emperor's resolve "to maintain and carry into execution the edict passed at Worms, five years before, against the Lutheran heresy" (`anjou_history-of-the-reformation-in-sweden_mason1859.txt` 6614-6618, Anjou's history of Sweden, telling German events). The Low Countries: Marnix cites Charles's placards of 1521 (MARNIX 16038-16044). The Peasants echoed Luther: the last of the Twelve Articles "seems to be an echo of Luther's own declaration at the Diet of Worms" (FP 2984-2986, Buchheim). Hus's words were compared to it (`hus_letters_workman-pope1904.txt` 12790-12791, Workman). The Palatine Elector at Augsburg in 1566 was compared to it (SCHAFF-HC 2658-2660).
- **Rome and the emperor allied.** FP 2522-2527: the postdate of 8 May matches the alliance of emperor and pope "to have the same friends and without exception the same enemies". FOXE4 22549-22552: by the decree "the emperor procured no small thanks with the pope".

## 9. The refusing party's own voice

Luther is the refusing party, and his voice is held in five ways:
- **The speech**, in Foxe's English, in full (FOXE4 21789-21806 first day; 21871-22061 second day; 22071-22079 the plain answer; 22102-22117 the answer to Eck's reply). A Cattley note reads "See Edition 15-J3 [OCR; presumably 1563], p. 410" (21910), and his source list is at 22529. Short lines: "I  will  acknowledge  nothing but  what  1 [sic: I]  have  written,  and  that  which  I  have  written  I  will  not  deny" (21905-21906); "For  I  make myself  no  saint,  and  I  dispute  'not  of  my  life,  but  of  the  doctrine  of  Christ" (21957-21958).
- **Lambert's quotation (via Köstlin)**, dated 18 April: "Yes, to me it is a precious thing to see that zeal and strife are stirred up concerning God's Word" (LV3 10535-10536).
- **His own later account** (TT 3498-3620), at second hand through the table-talk recorders and Bell's English.
- **His own later words**: "Yea, I could have started such a little game at Worms that even the emperor would not have been safe" (LV2 14933-14934, sermon of March 1522); "At Worms, too, our dear Emperor Charles, that noble youth, had to do what you wanted, and condemn me" (LV4 15959-15961, 1530).
- **No edition of the original Latin or German speech is held** (no Weimar, Erlangen or Förstemann; ZKG14 25846-25853 names the editions).

The imperial and papal side's own voice:
- **The Edict** (MIRBT 15600-15630), German, is the act itself.
- **Charles's declaration** reaches us only through three writers. Buchheim gives an English extract: "that as a descendant of the most Christian German Emperors, and the Catholic Kings of Spain, he had resolved to maintain everything which had been adopted by his ancestors, more especially at the Council of Constance. . . . That he will not hear Luther again" (FP 2468-2474). He calls it "a French document written in his own hand" (2468-2469). Pallavicino paraphrases it in Italian (PALL1 10142-10177): "vna scrittura di sua mano ben lunga vn foglio" (my tr.: a paper in his own hand a full sheet long), later sent to Rome and read in consistory (10143-10146). Foxe has it "in effect" (22137-22146). The French original is not held.
- **Aleander's words** are not held. Pallavicino reports him and used his letters (6157; 10410-10412). Buchheim quotes one letter in English from the "Documenta Lutherana recently issued by the Vatican" (FP 2324-2327, 2700-2704; see 11 for a doubt).
- **Eck's words** are held only as Foxe's and Luther's versions of what he said (FOXE4 21765-21778, 21812-21817, 21846-21861, 22083-22099; TT 3551-3561, 3609-3611).

## 10. Short quotable lines

1. Edict (act): "den gedachten Martin Luther, als von gots kirchen abgesündert gelide und einen verstopten zertrenner und offenbarn ketzer" (MIRBT 15602-15604; OCR spaced, shown closed up). (my tr.: "the said Martin Luther, as a limb cut off from God's church, an obstinate schismatic and open heretic".)
2. Edict: "den vorgemelten Martin Luther nit hauset, hoffet, etzt, drenket, noch enthaltet" (MIRBT 15620-15621). (my tr.: "that you do not house, harbour, feed or give drink to the said Martin Luther, nor keep him".)
3. Draft grievances (act): "achtet man von nöten, das derhalben ain besserung und gemain reformation geschehe" (MIRBT 15566). (my tr.: "it is held necessary that there be an amendment and a general reformation".)
4. Eck (Foxe): "Whether  thou  wilt  recant  and  revoke  them,  and  all  that  is  con- tained  in  them,  or  rather  meanest  to  stand  to  what  thou  hast  written?" (FOXE4 21777-21778).
5. Luther (Foxe): "Hereupon  I  stand and  rest:  I  have  not  what  else  to  say.     God  have  mercy  upon  me!" (FOXE4 22078-22079).
6. Luther (Table Talk): "God's Word is not my word, therefore I know not how to give it away" (TT 3565-3566).
7. Luther (Table Talk): "although in Worms there were as many devils as there are tiles on the houses, yet, God willing, I will go thither" (TT 3508-3509).
8. Charles (Foxe, "in effect"): "we  will  not  violate  our  faith,  which  we  have  pro- mised  him" (FOXE4 22145-22146).
9. Charles (Pallavicino): "non mi farebbe mai diventar' Eretico" (PALL1 10010, OCR repaired). (my tr.: "would never make me a heretic".) Show the OCR form in the record.
10. Charles to Aleander on the placard of 400 nobles: "quei quattrocento doueuano esser come i trecento di Muzio, cioè vn solo" (PALL1 9041-9042; OCR "cllèr", "va folo"). (my tr.: "those four hundred must be like the three hundred of Mucius, that is, just one".)
11. The Chancellor of Trier (Table Talk): "Martin Luther, you are disobedient to the Imperial Majesty; therefore you have leave and licence to depart" (TT 3609-3611).
12. Buchheim's report of Luther's words that evening: "Ich bin durch!" (FP 2466). (my tr.: "I am through!") Buchheim is the only held witness.

## 11. Contested points

1. **"Here I stand."** Three held tellings give it:
   - Foxe's English: "Hereupon I stand and rest... God have mercy upon me!" (FOXE4 22078-22079).
   - Herrnschmid in Wesley's magazine: "He added these German words, 'Hier stehe ich: ich kan nicht anders: Gott helfe mir! Amen.'" (ARM 9300-9311). He gives them as words added in German after the Latin answer.
   - Carlyle, quoted by Bacon: "I cannot and will not recant. Here I stand - I cannot do otherwise - God be my help, Amen." (LHYM 649-652). **This is Carlyle's, not Bacon's editorial note** (LHYM 604: "The words of Thomas Carlyle"). The era 7 Who scan calls LHYM 651 an "editor's note"; that should be corrected.
   - Zinzendorf (ZINZ 30871-30872) repeats it.

   The phrase is absent from Luther's own later account (TT 3551-3620), from Pallavicino (PALL1 10076-10135), from Sarpi (SARPI 9625-9660), from Waterworth (WAT 1770-1793) and from Buchheim. **No held editor says it is doubtful or a later addition.** The only held caution is Bacon's: he calls the *tiles* saying "imputed" (LHYM 858). The scholarly doubt is real, but it is not in the Library: no source on the text of the speech is held (see 12). The profile should say the words come down in Foxe's and later tellings, and give both forms. It must not say a held editor doubts them.
2. **Who Eck was.** Foxe makes him the Leipzig opponent: "Then  John  Eckius  above  mentioned,  who  then was  the  bishop  of  Treves'  general  official" (FOXE4 21752-21753). Pallavicino says he was **another** man of that name: "vn'altro Giouanni Echio (nome fatale alle condannazion di Lutero) Vicario dell'Arciuescouo di Treueri" (PALL1 10015-10017) (my tr.: another Johann Eck, a name fatal to Luther's condemnations, vicar of the Archbishop of Trier). TT 3551: "Dr. Eck (the Bishop of Trier's fiscal)". Buchheim's Aleander letters are addressed "to Dr. Eck" (FP 2324, 2702), apparently the Ingolstadt Eck (unsure).
3. **The day of the first hearing.** The day after arrival, 17 April (PALL1 10012-10013; SARPI 9617; WAT 1733-1734; ARM 9149) against "The fourth day after his repair" (FOXE4 21734).
4. **Twenty or twenty-one days.** Twenty-one: FOXE4 22457-22458, 22484-22485; SARPI 9706-9707; Sleidan per WAT 1809 ("Sleidan says twenty-one days"). Twenty: PALL1 10357 ("fra venti giorni"); WAT 1803-1804 (citing Pallavicino); the Edict itself, twenty days ending 14 May (MIRBT 15613, 15620).
5. **The Edict's date and whether the Diet consented.**
   - Mirbt: "datiert vom 8. Mai 1521, wurde von Karl V. am 26. Mai unterzeichnet" (MIRBT 15590-15591; citing Wrede, Tesdorpf, Brieger, Kalkoff).
   - Pallavicino: signed in church on Trinity Sunday, "il dì ventesimosesto di Maggio (benché la segnatura... si ritraesse al giorno ottauo)" (PALL1 10501-10503). Before that, Brandenburg answered for all the Electors that it pleased them all and was the Diet's common view (10496-10500).
   - Buchheim: "promulgated on May 25, without the formal sanction of the Diet... post-dated to the 8th of May" (FP 2518-2521).
   - Waterworth: "On the eighth of May, Luther was publicly placed under the Ban of the Empire, with the consent of the Germanic Diet" (WAT 1824-1826).
   - Sarpi (Brent): "the 8. of April" (SARPI 9713), apparently an error.
   - Zwingli's editor: "the Emperor's edict at Worms, May 26, 1 521 [sic]"; he adds that Kidd prints the Latin (`zwingli_latin-works-correspondence-vol1_jackson1912.txt` 9847-9849).

   So the day of signing is 25 May (Buchheim) or 26 May (Mirbt, Pallavicino). Whether the Diet consented is disputed on confessional lines: Buchheim says it did not; Pallavicino and Waterworth say it did. The Edict itself claims "mit einhelligem rat und willen" of the Electors, princes and Estates (MIRBT 15598-15600).
6. ***Decet Romanum*.** 2 January (MIRBT 15394) or 3 January 1521 (LV3 287; WAT 1661-1662).
7. **Who drafted the Edict.** Aleander, by Pallavicino: "fece commettere all'Aleandro, che distendesse la contenenza del Bando: E così egli pose in effetto" (PALL1 10448-10449, OCR "connnectere", "dilhndelTe") (my tr.: he had Aleander draw up the content of the Ban, and he did so). ARM 9404-9405 seems to say the same ("drawn up by... Aleander, the Pope's Legate"), but the OCR is broken. Then it was revised in the emperor's councils (PALL1 10451-10454). No Protestant held source disagrees; none confirms either.
8. **Where Luther stood.** "the Senate House" (TT 3547); "the earl palatine's palace" (FOXE4 21743-21744). Minor.
9. **How many grievances.** "four hundred articles" (TT 3531) against "some hundred and four statements" (LV4 16044), both Luther's.
10. **Buchheim's Aleander letter.** Buchheim says it describes Luther's popularity "in particular at Augsburg" (FP 2701-2704). I could not confirm the place or the addressee from a held text. Use it only as Buchheim's report, and not for a Worms scene.

## 13. Close candidates: later memory

- **The Wartburg** (sources disagree on detail):
  - Pallavicino: five trusted nobles of Frederick, "con sembianze contrafatte", stopped the carriage, threw down and beat the driver, "presero con simulata forza Lutero", and brought him by night to "Vvartberga [OCR 'Vvafibecga'] Fortezza del Sassone" (PALL1 10392-10400). Frederick "non sapeua in quali de' suoi Castelli Martino fosse nascosto", so that he could truly swear he did not know (10401-10404). (my tr.: in disguise... took Luther with a show of force... to the Wartburg, the Saxon's fortress... did not know in which of his castles Martin was hidden.) The length of stay at 10400 is OCR-damaged ("none meli"), unsure.
  - Waterworth: "a number of horsemen in masks, who, having beaten his drivers, and made a show of violence to Luther, carried him away into the fortress of Wartburg" (WAT 1819-1822).
  - Herrnschmid: "seized on the road by a party of Horsemen, and violently carried away to a Castle" (ARM 9456-9458).
  - Steimle: "taken by the friendly 'foe'... he entered the Wartburg on May 4, 1521" (LV2 14413-14416).
  - Buchheim: "by means of a stratagem" (FP 2501); "Junker Georg" (2598); "Saxon fox", Aleander's words reported by Buchheim (2510).
  - Foxe gives no castle name (22552-22554).
  - No witness in Luther's own words is held.
- **The Bible begun there**: "the great work of his life, the translation of the Bible from the original text" (FP 2599-2600). The census already uses this.
- **"The official proclamation of the Reformation"** (FP 2463), Buchheim.
- **The monument**: "the statue of Hus, which forms part of the Luther monument at Worms" (LUTZOW 13846-13847).
- **Campion's Catholic reading**: Luther "accitus Wormatiam (a. 1521)... nonne Caesaris verba tutus fuit?" (CAMP 2162-2165) (my tr.: summoned to Worms in 1521, was he not safe on Caesar's word?).
- **Gillett (1871)** has Charles refusing to break the safe-conduct: "'No!' said he, 'I should not like to blush like Sigismund.'" (GILLETT 2766-2771). It is a late, unsourced telling; flag as tradition. Sarpi gives the Palatine Elector, not Charles, as the one who protested (SARPI 9742-9748).

## 16. Traditions touched (census ids at 1521)

- **Gathered**: none as a church body. The Diet was an imperial assembly. The Estates' grievances are its own act (MIRBT 15525-15580).
- **Condemned**: `lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations`. The Edict outlaws Luther "and his followers": "gegen seinen mitverwandten, anhengern, enthaltern, fürschiebern, gönnern und nachvolgern" (MIRBT 15629-15631). The person is `martin-luther`.
- **Refused**: `martin-luther` / `lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations`: "I will not, nor may not revoke any manner of thing" (FOXE4 22076-22077).
- **Affected**:
  - `the-tridentine-church`: the emperor bound himself to Rome and the Edict carried out the papal bull (MIRBT 15603-15606; FP 2522-2527). Waterworth's Trent introduction tells Worms as part of that church's own history (WAT 1648-1835).
  - No other census tradition is shown by a held line to have been directly affected in 1521. (I checked and rejected two: Anjou 6614-6618 is about Speyer 1526, not Sweden; Zwingli's editor at `zwingli_latin-works-correspondence-vol1_jackson1912.txt` 9847-9849 only dates the Edict.)
