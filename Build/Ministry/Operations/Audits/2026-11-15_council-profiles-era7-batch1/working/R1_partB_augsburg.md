## Blocking

**B-B1. Blocking (wrong in the brief: "the signed confession" is not what is held, and the brief contradicts its own close).** Brief paragraph 2: "Part of the Diet's own record is held: the signed confession, two clauses of its final decree, and the other confessions." Brief close: "The two copies the emperor received, Bensow writes, have never been found." The entry repeats the first claim in `uncertain[0]` ("the signed confession"), and its `record` note for the confession reads "The confession as handed to the emperor".
- Evidence. The signed copies are lost: BENS l.31317-31319, "De båda till kejsaren öfverlämnade exemplaren hafva trots ifriga efterforskningar icke kunnat återfinnas". What is held is printed text. BOC l.1466-1468: "This Translation is made from the Latin Editio Princeps of 1530-31". Bensow says Melanchthon kept reworking the text for his printings (l.31330-31338). He says the German "säkerligen betydligt afviker från den i Augsburg upplästa" (surely differs considerably from the one read at Augsburg), while the Latin is "i det hufvudsakliga oförändrad" (mainly unchanged) (l.31340-31342). He says the Book of Concord's German comes from a Mainz manuscript once taken for the original but since shown to be a copy "hvars noggrannhet är underkastad starka tvifvel" (whose accuracy is in strong doubt) (l.31344-31348). AUG's print basis is not stated (header l.8-14). So the brief tells the listener that the signed document is held, then that it is lost.
- A smaller point in the same sentence: "the other confessions" comes before the brief has said there were other confessions (they arrive in paragraph 5).
- Fix, brief paragraph 2 (scratch copy): "Part of the Diet's own record is held: the confession in printed texts, part of the final decree, and the two other confessions." (23 words.)

**B-S1. Substantial (a contested point given from one side in the brief, with a sentence that has no witness).** Brief paragraph 8: "The final decree of 19 November gave the Lutherans until 15 April to return, by C. A. Buchheim's account. They refused."

Waterworth puts the term in the proposed edict of 22 September: "time was now allowed them till the 15th of the ensuing April, to consider whether they would return" (WAT l.2497-2505). Buchheim puts it in the decree "finally issued on the 19th of November" (FPR l.3489-3493). Pallavicino is a third witness, on Waterworth's side. In his account the first recess gave the Protestant princes and cities time until April ("fin ... d'Aprile", OCR "fin Vi 5J d'Aprile", day unclear), "Essendo queste condizioni ripudiate da principali Protestanti", and only then came "un secondo Editto" (PALL l.17567-17569, 17580-17582, 17600-17601). The pattern says a disputed point is told where it arises, with its witnesses named. The brief names only Buchheim. The next sentence, "They refused", is not Buchheim's: he says only that the decree "amounted to an open declaration of war" (FPR l.3497). Waterworth's opposition ("This edict was opposed by the Protestant princes", WAT l.2541) and Pallavicino's "ripudiate" are both about the September terms.

- Fix, records K10: add a side, "The first recess, refused by the principal Protestants, before a second edict: Pallavicino (PALL l.17567-17569, 17580-17582, 17600-17601; the day of April unclear in the OCR)". **B-S3. Substantial (an acts text misread, in the entry and records).** `decided[14]`: "Its fourth section binds the elector, five princes and six cities to the emperor. The other estates, it says, had agreed to this." Records C74: "Section 4 says the elector, five princes and six cities should join the emperor and the other estates against those who do not hold the sacrament and the Anabaptists" (same witness, acts, Documented).
