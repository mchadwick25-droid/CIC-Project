# Dossier: the Diet of Augsburg (1530) and the Augsburg Confession

Research dossier for the era 7 council profile. The subject is the Diet and its process (summons, the reading of 25 June, the Confutation, the separate Reformed confessions, the talks, the recess), not the Confession's doctrine. All paths are under `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines. Translations marked "(my tr.)" are mine. OCR spellings are kept in quotes.

## 2. Candidate opening scenes

**A. The reading, Saturday 25 June, afternoon (Bensow; Sarpi; Pallavicino)**
- BENS l.31286-31300: "Uppläsningen ägde rum lördagen clen 25 juni kl. 3 e. m., då man för detta ändamål samlades i kapitelsalen i det biskop -liga palatset, där kejsaren bodde. De båda kursachsiska kanslererna Brück och Beyer framträdde midt i rummet, den ene med det tyska, clen andre med det latinska exemplaret af bekännelsen." ... "Uppläsningen tog två timmar i anspråk, och det berättas, att den skett med så klar och tydlig röst, att alla de personer, som befunno sig på det biskopliga palatsets borggård, förstått hvarje ord." (my tr.: "The reading took place on Saturday 25 June at 3 p.m., when they gathered for it in the chapter hall of the bishop's palace, where the emperor lodged. The two Electoral Saxon chancellors Brück and Beyer stepped into the middle of the room, one with the German and the other with the Latin copy of the confession." ... "The reading took two hours, and it is said that it was done in so clear a voice that everyone in the courtyard of the bishop's palace understood every word.") Bensow says the German was chosen after the Elector of Saxony recalled that they were on German soil (l.31290-31293), and Beyer read it (l.31293-31298).
- SARPI l.12716-12719: "the Princes being ... before the Emperour in an Hall, capable to receive about 200 [persons], it was read with a loud voice." The legate was absent: "when the Legate would not be [present]" (l.12714-12715, OCR broken). PALL l.17743 agrees: "il Legato né interuenne alla Dieta quel giorno che fù letta la confession degli Eretici" (my tr.: "the legate did not attend the Diet on the day the heretics' confession was read").
- Afterwards (BENS l.31306-31309): the emperor kept the Latin copy, gave the German to the Elector of Mainz for the imperial archive, "Samtidigt förbjöd han bekännelsens offentliggörande" (my tr.: "At the same time he forbade the confession's publication").
- Strength: vivid, held in a modern editor; "det berättas" (it is said) marks the clear-voice detail as tradition. Where the room was is contested (see 11).

**B. Luther at Coburg, writing to the clergy he cannot face (Luther's own words)**
- LW4 l.15752-15758: "It is not fitting, dear sirs, that I should appear in person at this diet; and even though I had to appear, or were to do so, it would serve no useful purpose, for it would make no difference amid all the splendor and bustle. Nevertheless, beside my spiritual presence ... I have undertaken to be among you with this mute and feeble written message." Why he was absent: C. M. Jacobs (LW4 l.15661-15665): "He was under the ban of the empire, and it was not safe to take him farther. On April 23, he took up his residence at the castle — Feste Coburg". BENS l.31232-31234 agrees (my tr.: "there they thought it wisest to leave Luther behind, who had been declared under the imperial ban").

**C. The princes reach Augsburg first (the Confession's own preface)**
- AUG l.66-70: "inasmuch as we, the undersigned Elector and Princes, with others joined with us, have been called to the aforesaid Diet the same as the other Electors, Princes, and Estates, in obedient compliance with the Imperial mandate, we have promptly come to Augsburg, and--what we do not mean to say as boasting--we were among the first to be here." BENS l.31234-31236: the Elector arrived 2 May and waited "öfver sex veckor" (my tr.: "over six weeks") for the emperor.

**D. The Corpus Christi procession and the sword (Sarpi; Buchheim)**
- SARPI l.12562-12566: the emperor "came thither the thirteenth of June, the Eve of Corpus Christi day, and went in procession the day following; but was not able to obtain that the Protestants would content themselves to be there." Then (l.12583-12592, OCR broken) he ordered the Elector of Saxony "to lay the Sword before him ... according to his office, and to stand at the Mass", and the Elector's divines advised him he might do it "assisting as at a civil, not as at a religious Ceremony".
- FPR l.3366-3371 (Buchheim, no source named): Margrave George of Brandenburg: "Sire, before renouncing the word of God, I would rather kneel down on this spot and let my head be cut off" and the emperor answered "No heads off! no heads off, my dear Prince!" Later narrative only; no held participant gives it.

## 3. Issue

- The emperor's summons, in the estates' words: AUG l.48-63: the Diet was summoned "to deliberate concerning measures against the Turk ... and then also concerning dissensions in the matter of our holy religion and Christian Faith, that in this matter of religion the opinions and judgments of the parties might be heard in each other's presence". The Tetrapolitan preface says the same in Latin (NIEM l.46400-46402, OCR verbatim: "[;rave et naxium illud circa religioDem dissidium, qaod Qtr- maniam iam aliquot amos exagitaesel, componerel"; read: "grave et noxium illud circa religionem dissidium, quod Germaniam iam aliquot annos exagitasset, componeret"; my tr.: "that he might settle that grave and harmful religious division which had troubled Germany for some years"). The preface is the 1531 printed one: it speaks of the Diet held "superiori anno" (l.46397, OCR "Enperiori anno").
- The Lutheran case: they had appealed to a "general, free Christian Council" and still held to it (AUG l.144-161). The Confession asks only "that they allow the Gospel to be purely taught, and that they relax some few observances which cannot be kept without sin" (AUG l.1521-1523).
- The emperor's side: Buchheim (FPR l.3328-3337) gives the writ's aim "to settle the prevailing discord, and to learn and graciously to consider everybody's conviction" but holds that eradicating "German heresy" was a main motive. Waterworth (WAT l.2196-2201) says that at Bologna Charles had "declared his resolution to enforce, if possible, the Edict of Worms, and to use all his efforts to reduce the Lutherans to the unity of the Church". Sarpi (SARPI l.12541-12545): he left Bologna resolved that the separated princes "should return to the obedience of the Church of Rome".
- The papal side: Sarpi says Clement VII instructed Vergerius to work so "that there should be no disputation in the Diet; nor consultation concerning Religion, nor any resolution taken to call a Council in Germany" (SARPI l.12549-12553). Anti-curial witness.
- Bensow on the background (BENS l.31193-31221): at Speyer in 1529 Charles had promised Clement VII to restore unity; his first plan was a general council, which the pope resisted; needing help against the Turks, he invited the estates "i mycket försonliga ordalag" (my tr.: "in very conciliatory words").
- The Reformed outside the Lutheran confession: Hinke (ZW2 l.1969-1976): "neither the Catholics nor the Lutherans would have anything to do with them", citing Bucer to Zwingli, 23 June: "Nothing more intolerant can be imagined than the hatred which the Lutherans have against us."

## 4. Called by

- Charles V. AUG l.48-49 ("Your Imperial Majesty has summoned a Diet of the Empire here at Augsburg"). SARPI l.12538-12540 (from Bologna "he intimated an Imperial Diet for the 8. of April"). BENS l.31216-31221 (invited the estates for 8 April 1530). No held source disputes it.

## 5. Attended

- **No list of attendees is held.** No held source gives a total count of estates present.
- Sarpi: the reading hall held "about 200" (SARPI l.12718). This is the size of the room, not a count.
- Pallavicino: the Diet met "con frequenza inusitata di Principi ecclesiastici e secolari" (PALL l.17036-17037; my tr.: "with an unusual attendance of princes, church and lay").
- The emperor came "accompanied by the Papal Legate, Campeggio ... with a large retinue of princes, ambassadors, and electors" (WAT l.2203-2205).
- **Signed list (acts)**: the Confession's subscription, identical in three held witnesses (AUG l.1557-1567; BOC l.3253-3263; BENS l.2475-2491): John, Duke of Saxony, Elector; George, Margrave of Brandenburg; Ernest, Duke of Lüneburg; Philip, Landgrave of Hesse; John Frederick, Duke of Saxony; Francis, Duke of Lüneburg; Wolfgang, Prince of Anhalt; "Senate and Magistracy of Nuremburg"; "Senate of Reutlingen". That is 7 princes and 2 cities.
- **The recess's count (acts)**: BOSS l.1013-1014: "der gemeldt churfurst zu Sachsen, die fünf fürsten und sechs städt" (my tr.: "the said Elector of Saxony, the five princes and six cities"). Bossert's footnote (l.1026-1030), reading §1 of the recess: Elector John; Margrave George; Ernst and Franz of Brunswick-Lüneburg; Landgrave Philip; Wolfgang of Anhalt; and the cities Nuremberg, Reutlingen, Kempten, Heilbronn, Windsheim and Weissenburg. Keep this list apart from the subscription. It omits John Frederick and adds four cities that came in after 25 June. The held texts do not say when those four joined.
- **The Saxon party**: C. M. Jacobs (LW4 l.15652-15654): the Elector took "Luther, Melanchthon, Spalatin, Justus Jonas and Agricola". Bensow (BENS l.31263-31266) names the theologians at Augsburg as "Justus Jonas, Agricola, Spalatin, Schnepf, Brenz m. fl." and the chancellor Brück (l.31274). Foxe v8 l.4903-4904 (editor's note): Jonas "attended the diet at Augsburgh in 1530, in company with Melancthon, Agricola, and G. Spalatinus."
- **Catholic theologians**: see Decided, item 2 (the Confutation).
- **The talks (August)**: PALL l.17436-17449 (OCR rough) names seven a side. Catholics: Christoph, bishop of Augsburg; Duke Erich [Henry?] of Brunswick, replaced by Duke George of Saxony; the chancellors of the Elector of Cologne and of the Margrave of Baden; the theologians Eck, Konrad Wimpina and "Giovanni [C]ocko" (OCR; probably Cochlaeus, unsure). Lutherans: John Frederick and George of Brandenburg; the jurists Gregor Brück and "D. Heller"; the theologians Melanchthon, Brenz and Schnepf. WAT l.2442-2445 and SARPI l.12918-12921: seven a side, then three.
- Others named at the Diet: Archbishop Albrecht of Mainz (TT l.721-728, 3662-3666; SARPI l.12693-12698); Cardinal Matthäus Lang of Salzburg (SARPI l.12870-12874; TT l.1118); Ferdinand, King of Bohemia and Hungary (BENS l.31438); Frederick the Palatine as imperial spokesman (PALL l.17377-17379, 17388-17390); the imperial secretary Alexander Schweiss, who read the Confutation (BENS l.31393-31394); the papal nuncio Vincenzo Pimpinello (SARPI l.12630); Cornelius Scepperus, imperial secretary (SARPI l.12874-12876); Joachim Camerarius, who took notes during the Confutation (BENS l.31426-31427); for the four cities, Jacob Sturm (ZW2 l.1977) with Bucer and Capito (ZW2 l.1975-1976; WAT l.2488-2490); Vadian (ZW2 l.5703-5705).

## 7. Decided (item by item)

1. **The Confession was read, then received.** It was read in German and both copies handed to the emperor, who forbade publication (BENS l.31306-31309, modern). The princes were asked whether they dissented in anything more and "answered, that the document presented contained all that they dissented from or complained of" (WAT l.2263-2268; PALL l.17303-17306 agrees).
2. **A Catholic reply (the Confutation) was ordered, drafted, sent back and read.**
   - Who ordered it: "it was resolved, at a meeting of the Catholic members of the Diet, at which the Legate and many theologians were present" (WAT l.2344-2351); Bensow says the Catholic estates gave it to Campeggio, who handed it to "ett tjugutal i Augsburg närvarande teologer, bland hvilka voro Luthers hätskaste motståndare såsom Eck, Fabri, Cochlæus, Dietenberger, Wimpina m. fl." (BENS l.31366-31370; my tr.: "some twenty theologians present in Augsburg, among them Luther's bitterest opponents such as Eck, Fabri, Cochlaeus, Dietenberger, Wimpina and others"). Sarpi says the legate advised that a confutation be read but not given out in copies (SARPI l.12857-12862).
   - The first draft was too harsh and was sent back for rewriting (BENS l.31383-31390; WAT l.2445-2448: "it was required to be made shorter and less violent").
   - It was read on 3 August by Alexander Schweiss "i samma rum, där förut de evangeliskas bekännelse blifvit föreläst" (BENS l.31393-31395; my tr.: "in the same room where the evangelicals' confession had been read"), and "accepted by the Emperor as his own" (WAT l.2449-2450).
   - Melanchthon (participant), BOC l.3371-3376: "After the Confession of our princes was publicly read, certain theologians and monks prepared a confutation of our writing; and when His Imperial Majesty had caused this also to be read in the assembly of the princes, he demanded of our princes that they should assent to this confutation."
3. **No copy for the Lutherans except on terms they refused.** Melanchthon: "this our princes could not obtain, unless on the most perilous conditions, which it was impossible for them to accept" (BOC l.3384-3386). Bensow, 5 August: a copy if they would make no reply, show it to no one and not print it (BENS l.31417-31421). Pallavicino gives the imperial answer: the copy was withheld because their preachers had mocked the Edict of Worms (PALL l.17393-17398).
4. **Talks were tried and failed.** Seven a side, then three (WAT l.2442-2445; SARPI l.12918-12926; PALL l.17436-17449, 17545-17549). Sarpi: "some few small points of Doctrine ... were agreed on, yet in conclusion ... the conference could produce no concord at all" (SARPI l.12923-12926). Pallavicino says the agreement was "in principalissimi articoli" (on very important articles), against Sarpi (PALL l.17455-17457).
5. **The Reformed confessions were answered and set aside.** Sarpi: the emperor had a confutation made of the cities' writing too, and refused them a copy (SARPI l.12880-12884, 12925-12936). Hinke on Zwingli's: "The Diet paid no attention to it, the emperor probably never read a line of it" (ZW2 l.1997-1998, editor's judgment). Eck answered Zwingli in print (ZW2 l.3301-3305).
6. **The Apology was refused.** Melanchthon: "This Apology they finally [at last when they took their departure from Augsburg] offered to His Imperial Majesty ... But His Imperial Majesty did not receive the offered writing" (BOC l.3412-3416). Bensow: on 22 September Brück handed it over; the emperor was about to take it "då ärkehertig Ferdinand tillhviskade honom något, hvarefter han vägrade att mottaga aktstycket" (BENS l.31436-31439; my tr.: "when Archduke Ferdinand whispered something to him, after which he refused to receive the document").
7. **The recess (acts, excerpt).** BOSS l.1013-1020 (§4): the Elector, five princes and six cities were to join the emperor and the other estates "wider dieienen, so das hochwürdige sacrament nicht halten, und die widertäufer" (my tr.: "against those who do not hold the most worthy sacrament, and the Anabaptists"). §40 (l.1022-1046): the existing law against Anabaptists stands, and children are to be baptized with chrism and the old ceremonies. The rest of the recess is not held; its terms come only from historians:
   - The term to 15 April 1531 to decide whether to return "at least until the meeting of a General Council" (WAT l.2501-2505); a council to be called within six months and held within a year (WAT l.2530-2532; SARPI l.12986-12988; PALL l.17591-17596).
   - No new books, no further changes, church property restored, Anabaptists and Sacramentarians banished (WAT l.2532-2539). Buchheim: the decree announced "to carry out unconditionally the Edict of Worms" (FPR l.3481-3482).
   - Final form approved on 19 November by "all the Catholic princes and members of the Diet": Zwinglians proscribed, married priests to be degraded, monasteries rebuilt, enforcement by the Imperial Chamber (WAT l.2544-2556; SARPI l.12970-12990 parallel).
   - Melanchthon called the recess a "horrible decree" (BOC l.11951, with H. E. Jacobs's note l.11994: 'The "Recess" of November 19th').

## 8. Outcome (through events)

- **The Lutheran estates refused.** "This edict was opposed by the Protestant princes, except as regards the clauses against the Anabaptists and Zuinglians; and the dissentients unanimously came to a resolution to refuse their aid against the Turk" (WAT l.2541-2544). Buchheim: "This amounted to an open declaration of war" (FPR l.3497).
- **The League of Smalcald.** "as early as the 22nd of December, those princes assembled at Smalcald, and signed a provisional treaty for their mutual defence" (WAT l.2599-2601; form drawn up 31 December 1530, signed 4 January 1531, note l.2606-2607). Buchheim: "the Protestant Princes to form the 'Convention' or 'League of Smalkald' in December 1530. Even Luther was induced to approve of it" (FPR l.3522-3524). Peace of Nuremberg, summer 1532 (FPR l.3549-3556).
- **The Confession became a church's charter.** The Apology was endorsed at Schweinfurt in 1532 and signed by all the theologians at Smalcald in 1537 (BENS l.31457-31462). The 1580 Book of Concord preface roots everything in "a brief and succinct confession ... which at the Diet of Augsburg, in the year 1530, was offered, by our most godly ancestors, in the German and Latin languages to the Emperor Charles V." (BOC l.468-473). It also says the original copies were compared with the one kept in the imperial archive (BENS l.399-408, Swedish).
- **The Reformed parting fixed.** The 1577 Formula of Concord: the "Zwinglian teachers ... separated from them when this Confession was presented" (BOC l.26604-26606). Pallavicino: the recess "rifiutando in questa maniera la Confessione delle quattro Città Zvvingliane" (PALL l.17589-17590; my tr.: "thus rejecting the Confession of the four Zwinglian cities").
- **Rome's reaction.** Sarpi: Clement VII was grieved that the emperor had received confessions, held talks and promised a council within six months (SARPI l.12994-13014). Waterworth (WAT l.2586-2590): the emperor's hope of "the intervention and authority of a General Council" was confirmed by "the events and proposals at Augsburgh".
- **Reach beyond Germany (later events):**
  - Sweden, Uppsala 1593 (acts): "then äldste, rätte och oförrandrede Ausburgiske confession, hvilken som af chur-, furster och städer blef öfverantvardet käijser Carl then fempte på then store rikesdagh i Ausburg år efter Christi börd 1530" (BENS l.31033-31037; my tr.: "the oldest, true and unaltered Augsburg confession, which the electors, princes and cities handed to Emperor Charles the Fifth at the great diet in Augsburg in the year after Christ's birth 1530"). Also the Uppsala preface, l.30936-30938. Anjou (`anjou_history-of-the-reformation-in-sweden_mason1859.txt` l.27855-27857): ordinands still swore to "the Augsburgh confession of 1530, as it was received by the council of Upsala, in 1593". A Swedish jubilee was held on 25 June (new style) 1730 (Cornelius l.23065-23068).
  - Denmark-Norway: the Kongelov of 1665 binds the kings to the faith "som den reent og uforfalsket er bleven foresat og fremstillet i den augsb. Conf. Aar 1530" (`helweg_den-danske-kirkes-historie-efter-reformationen-v1-dan_1851.txt` l.18866-18873; my tr.: "as it was set forth pure and unfalsified in the Augsburg Confession of 1530").
  - England: a provincial council decree of 1530-1538 (per Reusch, citing Wilkins) lists among banned books the "Confessio exhibita Caesari in comitiis Augustae, an. Dom. MDXXX" (`reusch_indices-librorum-prohibitorum-sechzehnten-jahrhunderts-deu-lat_1886.txt` l.808-818, 988; OCR "Angnstae"). A later index in the same volume lists the Tetrapolitan (l.3106-3110).
  - Bohemia, 1618: a Catholic argument, quoted in the estates' Apology, that the Confession "na valném římském sněme, v Augsburku léta 1530 držaném, i hned jak podána byla, od císaře Karla ... poražena a zavržena byla" (l.7528-7531; my tr.: "at the general imperial diet held in Augsburg in 1530 was, as soon as it was handed in, refuted and rejected by Emperor Charles"). Who is speaking needs checking; this is unsure.
  - Brandenburg Reformed at Leipzig, 1631: they said they would subscribe to the Confession handed over "Anno 1530. den 25. Junii, Kayser Carolo" at the Augsburg Diet (NIEM l.42040-42044).
  - Imperial law: the 1555 Religious Peace and the 1648 Westphalia text name the "Augustanae confessionis status" (Mirbt no. 351, l.16247; no. 430, l.21639, 21673, 21692).
  - Constantinople: the Tübingen-Jeremias II exchange on a Greek Augsburg Confession, 1573-1581 (census entry orthodoxy-under-the-ottomans; held as `acta-et-scripta-wirtembergensium_jeremias-ii-correspondence-lat_1584.txt`, not opened for this dossier).

## 9. The condemned or refusing party's own voice

Two parties refused. (a) The Lutheran estates refused the Confutation and the recess. (b) Zwingli and the four cities stood outside both.
- **Lutheran refusal in its own words.** Melanchthon: "This our princes declined to do. For in a matter pertaining to religion, how could they assent to a writing into which they had not looked?" (BOC l.3395-3398). And "the adversaries are treating the case in such a way, as to show that they are seeking neither truth nor concord, but to drain our blood" (BOC l.3439-3441). Luther, warning beforehand: "it is no fault of ours if you are dashed to pieces, when your pride fails you. Your blood be on your own head!" (LW4 l.18672-18675). The Confession's own conditional offer: "if they make no concession, it is for them to see how they shall give account to God for furnishing, by their obstinacy, a cause for schism" (AUG l.1523-1526).
- **The Reformed in their own words.** Zwingli: "as we have already seen the confession of the others and even their refutation by their opponents ... I believed that it would not be improper if I alone should forthwith submit an account of my faith" (ZW2 l.2025-2029, Hinke's tr.). The Tetrapolitan in Latin (NIEM l.46380 on).
- **The Catholic (majority) side's own voice**, which the profile also needs: the Confutation is **not held** (see 12). What is held from that side: Eck's own Repulsio of Zwingli (ZW2 l.3330-3405); the recess excerpt (BOSS); the Mansi papal life (MANSI32 l.105276-105279); Pallavicino and Waterworth, who cite Cochlaeus, Coelestinus and Corpus Reformatorum. The Confutation's content otherwise reaches us **through its opponent**, Melanchthon's Apology, which quotes it article by article (e.g. BOC l.8066-8069, l.12614-12616; APOL l.4145, 7861). Melanchthon says he wrote from notes taken "during the reading" and only later saw it (BOC l.3405-3406, 3448-3449). Luther: "the Papists' confutations are kept in darkness, and do stink" (TT l.3684-3685).

## 11. Contested points (both sides)

1. **Where the reading took place.** BENS l.31287: "kapitelsalen i det biskopliga palatset, där kejsaren bodde" (the chapter hall of the bishop's palace, where the emperor lodged). WAT l.2257-2258: "in a chamber of the Palatinate palace". SARPI l.12718: "an Hall, capable to receive about 200". These may name the same building (the bishop's residence was called the Pfalz), but no held line says so. **No held source says "chapel"** (searched BENS, WAT, FPR, BOC, LW4, SARPI, PALL for chapel/Kapelle/kapell/cappella). Tell it as a hall in the bishop's palace by Bensow's account, and keep Waterworth's wording beside it.
2. **The emperor's arrival.** 15 June (PALL l.17051; WAT l.2205; BENS l.31277) against "the thirteenth of June, the Eve of Corpus Christi" (SARPI l.12562). Both Sarpi and Pallavicino call it the vigil of Corpus Christi, so Sarpi's number may be an error or misprint. I am unsure; tell 15 June as the dating of Pallavicino, Waterworth and Bensow, and note Sarpi.
3. **Who wrote the Confutation, and how many.** "a body of theologians, amongst whom the most distinguished were Faber, Eck, and Cochlaeus" (WAT l.2349-2351). "dal Coeleo, dal Fabri, e dall'Echio" (PALL l.17314). "ett tjugutal" (about twenty), naming Eck, Fabri, Cochlaeus, Dietenberger and Wimpina (BENS l.31368-31370), with Eck the driving and harshest hand (l.31372-31387). Eck "with seventy other theologians collaborated in the Confutatio pontificia" (Hinke, ZW2 l.3303-3304). H. E. Jacobs calls "John Faber, the chief composer of the Confutation" (BOC l.12852-12853, editor's note). Twenty against seventy, and Eck against Faber as main author, stay contested. Melanchthon names no one: "certain theologians and monks" (BOC l.3372).
4. **The first draft.** 12 July (BENS l.31383) against 13 July (WAT l.2445).
5. **Count of Lutheran estates.** 7 princes and 2 cities sign the Confession (AUG, BOC, BENS). The recess counts the Elector, 5 princes and 6 cities (BOSS l.1013-1014, 1026-1030). Different dates, different documents: keep them apart, settle neither.
6. **How close the talks came.** Sarpi: only "some few small points" (SARPI l.12921-12926). Pallavicino: agreement on "principalissimi articoli" (PALL l.17455-17457), and Waterworth on Melanchthon's concessions (WAT l.2352-2433, 2476-2486). Buchheim blames Campeggio for the failure (FPR l.3447-3454). Partisan on every side.
7. **Luther "at" Augsburg.** TT l.1118-1119 has Luther say "At the Imperial Assembly at Augsburg, in the year 1530, the Bishop of Salzburg said unto me ...". TT l.2207-2208 has "at the Imperial Assembly at Augsburg, anno 1530, when I was forsaken of every man". But C. M. Jacobs (LW4 l.15661-15666) and Bensow (BENS l.31232-31234) put Luther at Coburg for the whole Diet. The Table Talk lines are late, compiled recollections (and the first is headed "shortly before his Death", l.1114). Do not use them as evidence that Luther spoke with Lang at Augsburg.
8. **How the Confession was received.** Luther: "there the adversaries were constrained to confess that our Confession was upright and true" (TT l.3640-3642) and "Dr. Eck confessed openly ... 'The Protestants cannot be confuted ... out of Holy Scriptures'" (TT l.3662-3663). The recess, as the 1618 Catholic argument quotes it, and Waterworth say the reverse: the Confession was refuted from Scripture (Bohemian l.7531-7535; BOC l.3417-3418, where Melanchthon reports that the adversaries "boast that they have refuted our Confession from the Scriptures"). Polemic on both sides.
9. **Landgrave Philip's exit.** "suddenly left Augsburg at the beginning of August" and the emperor had the gates watched (FPR l.3458-3462). "under the plea of his wife's illness" (WAT l.2594-2595). "privily by night slipped out of the city" (Foxe v4 l.26239-26240). The day is not held.
10. **Torgau date and Luther's departure.** See the date table (Bensow against C. M. Jacobs).
11. **Eck's reply to Zwingli.** Eck dates it 17 July (ZW2 l.3405). Hinke elsewhere says Vadian reported "Eck's answer of the 16th of August" (ZW2 l.5705). This may refer to the printed copy. Unresolved.

## 13. Close candidates (later memory, from held sources)

- Luther, table talk: "The Imperial Diet held at Augsburg, 1530, is worthy of all praise; for then and from thence came the Gospel among the people in other countries, contrary to the wills and expectations both of Emperor and Pope" (TT l.3624-3626). Partisan.
- Buchheim (1885): "The Diet of Augsburg in 1530 may be considered, in some respects, as the key-stone in the religious and political course of the Reformation" (FPR l.3503-3504); all later events "as far down as the Peace of Westphalia in 1648" follow from it (l.3510-3513). Modern, Protestant.
- Uppsala 1593 (acts, BENS l.31033-31037), quoted above: a kingdom bound itself to the confession "handed to Emperor Charles the Fifth at the great diet in Augsburg".
- Bensow: the two copies handed to the emperor "hafva trots ifriga efterforskningar icke kunnat återfinnas och synas tyvärr vara spårlöst försvunna" (BENS l.31317-31319; my tr.: "despite eager searches have not been found and seem sadly to have vanished without trace"). He adds that Philip II may have destroyed them on purpose, which is his conjecture ("Möjligt är"). A strong quiet close: the meeting's own original copies are lost.
- The Formula of Concord, 1577: the Confession "was received by their opponents with disfavor, but, thank God, remains to this day without refutation" (BOC l.27964-27966).

## 16. Traditions touched (census ids at the date)

**Gathered**
- lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations (1517-1580): its estates presented and signed the Confession: "Your Imperial Majesty's faithful subjects: John, Duke of Saxony, Elector ..." (AUG l.1555-1559).
- the-tridentine-church (1517-1650; nearest id for the Catholic estates and legate): its theologians drafted the Confutation, "accepted by the Emperor as his own" (WAT l.2448-2450).

**Condemned**
- the-anabaptist-movements (1525-1650): named in the recess "und die widertäufer" (BOSS l.1015), with §40 renewing the law against them (l.1022-1046); also condemned in the Confession (AUG l.314-315).
- the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva (1519-1650): Zwinglians proscribed in the recess (WAT l.2548); Pallavicino: the recess rejected the Confession of the four Zwinglian cities (PALL l.17589-17590). Zwingli's Fidei ratio was answered by Eck (ZW2 l.3381-3382).

**Refused**
- lutheran-wittenberg-and-its-congregations: refused the Confutation ("This our princes declined to do", BOC l.3395) and the recess (WAT l.2541-2544).
- the-reformed-cities-zurich-and-geneva (with the four cities, no own id): presented separately and were refused a copy of the reply (SARPI l.12927-12936).

**Affected (later, through events)**
- scandinavian-reformation-kingdoms (1520s-1650): Uppsala 1593 bound Sweden to the Confession handed over "på then store rikesdagh i Ausburg år ... 1530" (BENS l.31033-31037); Denmark-Norway's Kongelov of 1665 (Helweg l.18866-18873).
- the-english-reformation-and-elizabethan-settleme (1530s-1650): an English provincial decree of 1530-38 bans the "Confessio exhibita Caesari in comitiis Augustae" (Reusch l.808-818, 988). Thin; unsure of the decree's exact date.
- czech-churches-last-century (1517-c.1627): the 1618 Bohemian Apology argues over whether the Augsburg Confession was refuted at the 1530 Diet (l.7528-7545). Unsure who speaks.
- orthodoxy-under-the-ottomans-parish-millet-and-n (1453-1650): the Greek Augsburg Confession sent to Jeremias II, 1573-1581 (census text; held acta file not opened here).
