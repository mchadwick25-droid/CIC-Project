# Outline check: Athanasius of Alexandria

## My own account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`why`, `teaser`, `card.knownFor`, `keyWorks`), the source sheet and what he is known for.

**Essence.** Athanasius was bishop of Alexandria from 328 to 373, forty-five years. Through them he held to the creed of Nicaea, that the Son is "of one being" with the Father, against the party of Arius, and was forced out of his city five times for it. He held to the word because, as he taught, only a Son who is truly God could join humankind to God and free it from death ("He was made man that we might be made God"). He is on the tree as the chief defender of Nicaea's creed in the generation after the council, and as a bishop whose writings carried both the faith and the monks' pattern of life outward.

**Also told.**
- The conflict had force on both sides: a synod at Tyre (335) deposed him, emperors exiled him, soldiers came for him at night in the church of Theonas (356); a Melitian letter of 335 charges his own followers with violence (hostile, credited).
- In exile he wrote; in *De Synodis* (359) he treats as brothers those who doubt only the one word, while still calling those who reject the council foes of Christ.
- He wrote the *Life of Antony* for monks abroad (authorship Widely Accepted); it went into Latin and Syriac and became the pattern for monks.
- His Easter letter of 367 listed the books of Scripture, 27 for the New Testament; later church law (Trullo, 692) received his canons, and a 1920–21 Church of England proposal to the Orthodox named his canon.
- Optional link: Frumentius, made bishop of Aksum by him (Constantius's hostile letter; Socrates).

## BLOCKING

**B1. The essence ties the exiles to his teaching.** Line 5: "That could happen, he wrote, only if the Son were truly God (T1, T2 …). Five times he had to leave his city for it, by order or in flight." Coming straight after the teaching, "for it" makes the teaching, or the creed, the cause of every departure. Mark's ruling (Hearts_Plain, Athanasius "Added") says: "The story ties the teaching to the word, as his own lines do, not to the exiles." The outline's own Do not say and T2 say the same. The sources give mixed causes: for Trier, a grain charge or the emperor's wish for unity (D3, Contested); for 362, Julian's own reasons (HA 61734–61751; and see B4). The approved heart says only "Five times he had to leave his city."
*Correction:* line 5, replace "Five times he had to leave his city for it, by order or in flight" with "Five times he had to leave his city, by order or in flight". Then add to Do not say: "Not 'for it', 'for his faith' or 'because of the creed' after the count of exiles. The causes differ and some are contested (D3); give the count without a cause."

**B2. The gloss on "apocryphal" says more than its source.** The Words list (line 39) gives "books with names like the true ones, kept secret, he said". Also told (line 12), beat 7 (line 68) and the Connection (line 93) call them "secret books". Festal Letter 39 does not say they were secret. It says they are named like the true books (NP4 68754–68759) and are "an invention of heretics, who write them when they choose, bestowing upon them their approbation, and assigning to them a date, that so, using them as ancient writings, they may find occasion to lead astray the simple" (NP4 68824–68829). In other words, he calls them recent forgeries passed off as old. "He said" puts a claim in his mouth that he does not make. The listener needs this word in beat 7.
*Correction:* line 39: "*apocryphal books*: books he said heretics wrote when they chose and dated falsely, to pass them off as ancient, with names like the true books (E14, NP4 68754–68759, 68824–68829); not today's Protestant 'Apocrypha'." In lines 12, 68 and 93, replace "secret books" with "books made up by heretics and passed off as old" (or "forged books"). In line 12, "rejected secret books as inventions of heretics" becomes "rejected others as heretics' forgeries, made to pass as old". Add to Do not say: "Not 'secret books' or 'hidden books'. He calls them heretics' inventions given false dates (NP4 68824–68829)."

**B3. A quote already used on the tree.** In beat 5's optional clause (line 66), Constantius's letter is quoted: "guilty of ten thousand crimes". The live `frumentius` story (census `longDescription`) already quotes the same span: "was advanced to his present rank by Athanasius, a man who is guilty of ten thousand crimes." The kit (Part 3, Quotes; role item 6) bars this without Mark's word. The outline's search line (line 80) covered only Q1–Q3.
*Correction:* line 66, replace "that Athanasius, "guilty of ten thousand crimes", had made their bishop Frumentius" with "that Athanasius, whom it called a man of countless crimes, had made their bishop Frumentius". This is a paraphrase, not quoted. Add to Do not say: "Not 'guilty of ten thousand crimes' as a quote: the Frumentius story has it." Add `frumentius` to Sibling, with its job: the boys, the court of Aksum, his consecration, and the two datings; here, one clause.

**B4. The Library sweep misses held witnesses to told events.** The full spot-check found three gaps.

The editor marks several of these as "calumny" (lines 580–582, 726). The sheet neither uses nor sets it aside.
