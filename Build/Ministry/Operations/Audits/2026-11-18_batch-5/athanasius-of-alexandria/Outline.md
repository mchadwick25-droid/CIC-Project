# Outline: Athanasius of Alexandria

Sheet = `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder; tags (S1, E7, I3, D2 …) are its entries, which give file and lines. Job (Mark, 2026-11-18): the long defense of Nicaea's creed after the council: five exiles, the *Life of Antony*, and his list of the books of Scripture.

- **The essence:** For forty-five years Athanasius was bishop, the church's leader, in Alexandria. He held to the creed of Nicaea, the statement of faith bishops agreed in 325: that the Son is "of one being" with the Father, fully God as the Father is. He held it against the party of Arius, who taught that the Son had a beginning. For Athanasius the word mattered because of what God had done: God's Son became human so that human beings might be made divine, joined to God and freed from death. That could happen, he wrote, only if the Son were truly God (T1, T2; Documented, his own words). Five times he had to leave his city for it, by order or in flight (S1, S2, E4–E13; Documented each time; the count of five is Widely Accepted, D2). In those years he wrote the *Life of Antony*, the story of an Egyptian hermit, which carried the desert monks' way of life to readers abroad. And in 367, in the yearly letter that set the date of Easter, he listed the books to be read as Scripture, with the twenty-seven of the New Testament (E11, E14; the letter Documented; the *Life* as his work Widely Accepted, D1).

- **Also told:**
  - *The exiles, and their force.* A council of bishops at Tyre removed him from office in 335, and the emperor Constantine sent him far away to Trier, in Gaul (E4). In 339 a rival bishop came in with soldiers, and he went to Rome (E5). In 356 soldiers broke into his church at night and he hid for six years, while searchers tortured a woman vowed to God to find him (E7, E8). The emperor Julian expelled him in 362, and in 365 he hid four months outside the city until the emperor let him back (E12, E13). Each return is Documented in the two chronicles of his life. The force ran both ways: in 335 a monk of the Melitians, a breakaway church in Egypt, wrote that his followers, with soldiers, beat their monks bloody (E3; Documented as the Melitian's charge).
  - *His teaching, and his defense of the creed.* Early, by the editor's dating before Nicaea, he wrote that the Son "was made man that we might be made God" (T1; Documented, his own words; the date is the editor's). In hiding in the 350s he argued that a creature could not join creatures to God, so humankind is made divine only if the Son is "very God" (T2, Q2; Documented). Yet he would treat as brothers those who accepted all of Nicaea's statement but its one word, "of one being" (E10; Documented, his own words).
  - *Frumentius (optional, one clause).* The emperor Constantius complained in a letter that Athanasius had made Frumentius bishop of Aksum, a kingdom in today's Ethiopia (F1; Documented, the emperor's letter as Athanasius preserved it; Socrates agrees).
  - *The* Life of Antony. He wrote it for monks abroad, men living apart in prayer, who asked how Antony lived; it was to be a pattern for their own life (E11; Documented as the book's own preface). It went into Latin within about twenty years, Jerome says, and later into Syriac, the language of the eastern churches (I1; Documented).
  - *The list.* In his Easter letter of 367 he named the books "handed down" as Scripture: twenty-two of the Old Testament and twenty-seven of the New. He set other books apart, for new believers to read, and rejected secret books as inventions of heretics, teachers he held false (E14, Q3; Documented). Later Greek church law kept his rulings (I3a; Documented that a council of 692 sealed "the Canons of Athanasius", meaning his rulings; that the list is among them, Widely Accepted). In 1921 a Church of England proposal for union with the Orthodox churches took the list of books "as it is defined by St. Athanasius" (I3c; Documented).

- **Length:** about 3 minutes 15 seconds (about 490 words at 150 a minute); never past about 3:30. Reason: a rich record and a three-part job (the exiles, the *Life*, the list), each with its later witnesses, five departures that must be told without becoming a list, and now his teaching, which needs two or three sentences to be understood (Mark, 2026-11-18: "the 3 minutes is a pressure not a cap"). The exiles are told as one movement, with weight on 335 and 356; 339, 362 and 365 take a clause or two each. Nicaea is one sentence.

- **Setting:** "Alexandria, in Egypt" (S1, CEN); bishop "from 328" to his death "in 373" (S1, S2). The church of Alexandria (S3); do not say "Catechetical" or "Christian-Platonist". The emperors change through his life: Constantine, his son Constantius, Julian, Valens (E4, E7, E12, E13). Trier is "Trier, in Gaul" (E4). An Easter letter was the letter the bishop of Alexandria sent each year to the churches of Egypt and Libya, announcing the date of Easter (S4).

- **Words to explain** (each glossed where it is first heard; plain word preferred where given):
  - *bishop*: the leader of a city's church. In Alexandria then, he oversaw the churches of all Egypt and Libya (S4, NP4 64063–64069), more than a modern local bishop.
  - *deacon*: a church officer who served under the bishop (E1).
  - *priest*: say "priest"; it stands for presbyter, a church elder under the bishop.
  - *creed*: a short statement of faith that bishops agreed on.
  - *Nicaea / the council of Nicaea*: a meeting of bishops called by the emperor in 325 (CEN, council-of-nicaea-325).
  - *"of one being"* (homoousios): the Son is God in the same full sense as the Father (CEN, council-of-nicaea-325, "God as the Father is").
  - *Arius / the Arians*: a priest of Alexandria who taught the Son had a beginning (CEN, arius: "the Son has a beginning"). "Arians" was his opponents' name for that party; its bishops refused it (CEN, arius).
  - *council / synod*: say "a council of bishops", a meeting that judged church matters.
  - *deposed*: say "removed from office".
  - *exile*: sent away from home by order; distinguish from his flights (D2).
  - *communion*: sharing the bread and wine of the Eucharist, the sign of belonging to one church.
  - *Melitians*: a breakaway church in Egypt, with its own bishops and monks (E3; CEN melitian-schism).
  - *vigil*: a night of prayer before a service (E7, NP4 37296–37297).
  - *the bishop's chair* (throne): the seat from which a bishop led his church (Q1).
  - *psalm*: a song from the Bible's book of Psalms.
  - *monk / hermit / the desert monks*: men who left ordinary life to live by prayer, alone or together, in Egypt's desert.
  - *consecrated virgin*: say "a woman vowed to God" (E8, "a perpetual virgin").
  - *Easter letter* (festal letter): the bishop's yearly letter to Egypt's churches setting Easter's date (S4).
  - *Scripture*: the books the church reads as God's word.
  - *canon* (of Scripture): the fixed list of books read as Scripture. Not "canon" as church law, except in the 692 council: there "the Canons of Athanasius" means his rulings, kept as church law (I3a).
  - *apocryphal books*: books with names like the true ones, kept secret, he said; not today's Protestant "Apocrypha" (E14, NP4 68754–68759).
  - *heretics*: teachers he held false; the word is his.
  - *godliness* (Q3): true reverence for God.
  - *the Fathers*: the bishops who met at Nicaea (E10).
  - *the Son / the Word*: Christ, God's Son; "the Word" is his name for him in John's Gospel. Say "the Son".
  - *made divine / deified / "made God"* (θεοποιέω, T1, T2): in his own text, joined to God, freed from sin and death, raised to live for ever (Orat. II.69, NP4 49683–49698). Never "turned into gods".
  - *creature* (Q2): anything God made, as opposed to God himself. Not "animal".
  - *"very God" / true God*: fully God, not a lesser being.
  - *kingdom of heaven* (Q2): God's rule, the life with God that Christians hope for.
  - *Aksum*: a kingdom in today's Ethiopia and Eritrea (F1; CEN frumentius).
  - *orthodoxy* (Gregory's "the law of orthodoxy"): right belief, as the church came to hold it.
  - *Syriac*: the language of the churches of Syria and Mesopotamia.
  - *Anglican / Church of England* and *Orthodox*: say "the Church of England" and "the Orthodox churches of the East".

- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen.** His own account of a night in 356. Soldiers surrounded his church in Alexandria while the people kept a night vigil. He sat down in the bishop's chair, had a psalm read, and sent the people home first (E7, Q1). The monks and clergy then dragged him away, and he slipped out among the soldiers. Then straight to the essence: this was one of five times he left his city.
  2. Reserve: the reckoning in the chronicle written within a generation of his death: of his first forty years as bishop, about seventeen and a half were spent away from his church (E16).

- **Story beats** (the hook and essence are the first minute; then the life once, in order):
  1. **The hook and the essence.** The night of 356, in his words (Q1), then the essence above. The night is told here only; beat 5 begins after his escape. E7: Documented as his own account; the "more than five thousand soldiers" is his count.
  2. **His teaching, Nicaea, bishop, and the first refusal.** Early, by the editor's dating around 318 (NP4 12090–12091), he wrote *On the Incarnation*: God's Son "was made man that we might be made God" (T1; Documented; date the editor's). Say what he means by it, from T2's lines: joined to God, freed from death. At Nicaea in 325, as a deacon of Alexandria, he opposed Arius's party, Socrates says (E1; Widely Accepted). Do not say his bishop brought him there: that is the late boyhood story (D8). In 328 he became bishop (S1; Documented). When Arius was allowed home, Athanasius refused to take him back into communion; Constantine wrote that he would depose and exile him if he kept anyone out (E2; Documented as Socrates's account). Communion means sharing the Eucharist, the sign of belonging to one church.
  3. **Tyre and Trier, 335 to 337.** His enemies used the Melitians, a breakaway church in Egypt, to bring charges against him, Socrates says (E3). A Melitian monk's letter from 335 tells the other side: his followers came with soldiers and beat their monks bloody (E3; Documented as that writer's charge). A council of bishops at Tyre deposed him in 335. He sailed to Constantinople and spoke plainly to Constantine, but the emperor sent him to Trier, in Gaul (E4; Documented). Socrates gives two reasons: a charge that he threatened Egypt's grain ships, or the emperor's wish for unity, since he would not receive Arius (D3; Contested). After Constantine died in 337 he came home (E4).
  4. **Rome, 339 to 346.** In 339 he was pursued by night, and the next day he fled from a church in Alexandria; four days later a rival bishop, Gregory, entered the city (E5; Documented). Socrates says soldiers escorted Gregory in (E5). Athanasius went to Rome, where its bishop, Julius, restored him to his place, Socrates says (E5). When Gregory died he came home, and in 346 the people and officials met him a hundred miles from the city (E5; Documented).
  5. **Six years hidden, 356 to 362.** Constantine's son Constantius now ruled, and a new rival bishop, George, held the churches by force (E8). Athanasius hid, in the city and beyond it, for about six years, the chronicle says (E8). The chronicle of his church records that searchers tortured a consecrated virgin, a woman vowed to God, to find him (E8; Documented as the chronicle's record). From hiding he wrote his defenses. In his *Discourses against the Arians* (356–360, the editor's dating) he set out why the word mattered: only a Son who is true God could join humankind to God (T2, Q2; Documented). Yet in 359 he wrote that those who accepted all of Nicaea but its one word should be met "as brothers with brothers" (E10; paraphrase or this short phrase only). In the same book he called those who rejected the council outright enemies of Christ (E10): keep the limit. Optional, one clause: the emperor Constantius wrote to the rulers of Aksum that Athanasius, "guilty of ten thousand crimes", had made their bishop Frumentius, and ordered Frumentius sent to Egypt (F1; Documented as the emperor's letter, which Athanasius kept in his own defense). In these years, by the editor's dating, he wrote the *Life of Antony*, for monks abroad who had asked how Antony lived, so that they might imitate him (E11; the dating is the editor's; the book's purpose Documented).
  6. **Julian and Valens, 362 to 366.** Julian, the next emperor, let exiled bishops home, then ordered Athanasius out within the year; he went up the Nile until Julian died (E12; Documented). In 365 a new imperial order, under the emperors Valentinian and Valens, sent such bishops out again, and Alexandria rioted (E13). He left by night and stayed four months at a country house near the city, until an imperial officer and a crowd of Christians led him back to his church (E13; Documented, HA; Socrates says a family tomb, D4).
  7. **The Easter letter of 367.** In his yearly Easter letter he wrote that some were reading "apocryphal" books, secret books with names like the true ones, and set out the books "handed down" as divine (E14; Documented). He named twenty-two of the Old Testament and twenty-seven of the New. Then he wrote Q3. Other books, such as Wisdom and Tobit, were for new believers to read, not part of the list; the secret books he called heretics' inventions (E14). Canon here means the fixed list.

- **What came of them** (about a third; heaviest first):
  1. **The *Life of Antony* travelled.** Evagrius, a priest of Antioch, put it into Latin, Jerome says (I1a; Documented). Within a few years Jerome himself wrote a life of another hermit, because "both Greek and Roman writers" had already told Antony's story (I1b). A church historian of the next century would not retell Antony's life, since Athanasius had "devoted an entire book" to it (I1c). It reached Syriac readers too (I1d). Leave Augustine and Trier to the sibling.
  2. **He defended Nicaea's creed** (DEFENDED). Gregory of Nazianzus, preaching at Constantinople a few years after his death, called his teaching "the law of orthodoxy" (I2a; Documented). Gregory also praised him for joining two parties that used different words for the same faith (E12; optional).
  3. **His list entered church law.** In 692 a council at Constantinople sealed "the Canons of ... Athanasius" among the church's laws, and the Greek collections of those canons carry his list (I3a; Documented, the canon; Widely Accepted, that the list is among them). In 1921 Anglicans proposing union with the Orthodox Church wrote that they accepted the canon of Scripture "as it is defined by St. Athanasius", with the twenty-seven books of the New Testament (I3c; Documented, a proposal).
  Optional line on who keeps him: the Coptic church's medieval book of saints' days remembers his death on the 7th of Bashans, and calls him "the apostolic" for the persecution he suffered (I4; LATE-LEGEND source; card only unless needed).

- **Connection to carry into the story:** Two things passed on from him in writing. A book: the *Life of Antony*, written so monks abroad could copy Antony's life, put into Latin and Syriac, and read so widely that later writers sent their readers to it (E11, I1). And a list: the books "handed down" as Scripture, set out in an Easter letter, which later Greek church law kept among his rulings and which Anglicans in 1921 still named as the shared canon (E14, I3). The listener must be told what an Easter letter was (the bishop's yearly letter to Egypt's churches setting Easter's date, S4), so it is clear why a list of books came in it; and that he did not invent the books but named those he said were "handed down", separating them from books for beginners and from secret books. Tell it through what Evagrius, Jerome, the council of 692 and the 1921 Anglicans did, not through a "this matters because" sentence.

  2. Or the opening image again: the bishop who sent his people home before he would leave, who died in his own city (E7, E15).

- **Do not say:**
  - Not "people become gods" or "he taught that humans turn into God". Say "made divine", with his own sense: joined to God and freed from death (T2).
  - Not "this is why he endured five exiles" or any motive sentence. His own lines link the teaching to the word "of one being" (T2), not to his exiles. Let the order of the story show it.
  - Not that *On the Incarnation* was written in a given year as fact; "around 318" is the editor's dating (T1).
  - Not that Athanasius converted Aksum or sent missionaries there. He made Frumentius its bishop (F1).
  - Not any word listed under "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard (Mark, 2026-11-18).
  - Not "exiled five times by the emperors". Say "five times he had to leave his city" or "driven out five times". Gregory counted three banishments; the others were flights (D2).
  - Not that the psalm and the escape happened in 339. Socrates puts them there; Athanasius's own account and both chronicles put the night attack in 356 (D5).
  - Not "five thousand soldiers" as fact. Say "more than five thousand soldiers, he wrote" or leave the number out (E7).
  - Not "he escaped by a miracle". The index says "in a miraculous manner"; his own account says the monks and clergy dragged him out and he passed "under the Lord's guidance" (E7). Keep any wonder in a witness's voice.
  - Not that he ordered the beating of the Melitians. The letter blames "the adherents of Athanasius" with soldiers (E3). Credit it to the Melitian writer, and do not drop it.
  - Not that he murdered Arsenius, or that the charges at Tyre were proven or simply false. Socrates, who favors him, says the supposed victim was found alive; tell any charge as a charge (E4, D6).
  - Not one reason for Trier given as fact (D3).
  - Not that he lived with Antony, served him, or was his disciple. The text is uncertain (D1).
  - Not that he wrote the *Life* "in the desert" or "while hiding with Antony's monks". Its date is the editor's ("between 356 and 362"), and the hiding place is not given (E11).
  - Not that the *Life* was written for monks "in the West" or "in Rome". It says "monks in foreign parts" (E11).
  - Not that he was in danger in 367. The man the crowd wanted to kill that year was Lucius, the rival bishop (E14 caution).
  - Not "the first to list the twenty-seven books", "he fixed" or "he closed the canon", and not that churches had to obey his list. No held source says so (N2, N3). Say "he listed", "he named the books handed down".
  - Not "his list is our Bible". His Old Testament has no Esther and joins Baruch and a letter to Jeremiah; Esther, Wisdom and Tobit are among the books "to be read" (E14).
  - Not "the Catholic Church accepted his list" on the 1921 document's word. It was an Anglican proposal (I3c).
  - Not "Athanasius against the world", and not the "Athanasian Creed" (N6).
  - Not that he welcomed everyone who doubted Nicaea. The welcome was for those who accepted everything but the one word; those who rejected the council he called enemies of Christ (E10).
  - Not "forty-six years" or "forty-seven years". Say "forty-five years", or "from 328 to 373" (S2).
