**Conventions.** Line numbers are each file's own. English quotes are printed as in the file, with editors' note markers and notes left out; where a note matters it is given after the quote and marked ED. Editors' dates in brackets or notes (for example "Feb. 8–9, 356") are the editor's conversions of the chronicles' Egyptian dates and consuls; they are marked "(ED date)".

**Witness kinds.** OWN = Athanasius's own writings (all of them partisan: he writes in his own defense). CONTEMP = written in his lifetime or within a generation: the Melitian letter (335, BELL's date), Ammianus, HA and FI (ED: "both belong to the generation after the death of Athanasius", their agreed dates resting on a contemporary source, NP4 9208–9214), Gregory of Nazianzus (a panegyric, 379 or 380 by ED, NP4 1224–1226), Julian's letters (362, JUL's dates). LATER = Socrates (about 70 years after his death, my estimate), Jerome (*Lives*, about 20 years; Letter 127, 412 by its translator, NP6 25944, about 40 years), Rufinus (402–403, RUF header, about 30 years), Philostorgius (a follower of Eunomius, PHIL 48, of the party opposed to him; his history ran to 425, PHIL 36; held only through Photius's hostile epitome, PHIL 8). LATE = the Council in Trullo (692, ED date, NP14 1602), the Copto-Arabic Synaxarium (medieval, SYN header), the 1920–21 Anglican terms. LATE-LEGEND = the Synaxarium's tales.

**How much rests on his side.** ED: "The writings of Athanasius himself ... furnish almost all the primary information as to the facts of his eventful life" (NP4 1221–1224), and the church historians "draw largely from Athanasius himself" (NP4 1235–1236). ED's statement; it bears on every conflict below.

## 1. Setting

S1. **His city and office.** FI preamble: Alexander "having departed this life ... he [Athan.] succeeded him after the Paschal festival on xiv Pauni" (NP4 64077–64080; ED converts the year to 328). FI I: "This was the first Letter he [Athan.] sent; for he was ordained Bishop in the preceding year after the Paschal feast" (NP4 64087–64089). CONTEMP. Documented. Spoken: "bishop of Alexandria, in Egypt, from 328".

S2. **How long.** He died "on the seventh of Pachon" in the year 372–3 (FI XLV, NP4 64530–64533; ED: "May 2, 373"); HA: "on Pachon viii he falls asleep" (NP4 61937–61938; ED: "May 3, 373"). CONTEMP. Documented (373); the day differs by one (D7). Socrates says he governed "forty-six years" (SZ 13320–13321). Census: "328-373" (CEN). Spoken: "for forty-five years".

S3. **Tradition.** Census `toldWithin`: alexandria-catechetical, spoken name "Alexandrian Christianity" (informalName) or "Alexandria" (shortName) (CEN). Spoken: "the church of Alexandria". Do not say "Catechetical" or "Christian-Platonist" aloud.

S4. **What an Easter letter was.** FI heading: "The Festal Letters of Athanasius, Bishop of Alexandria, which he sent year by year, to the several cities and all the provinces subject to him; that is, from Pentapolis, and on to Libya ... Egypt ... the upper and middle Thebais" (NP4 64063–64069). CONTEMP. Documented. Each year the bishop of Alexandria wrote to the churches of Egypt and Libya to announce the date of Easter; the index records, year by year, whether he could send it. Spoken: "his yearly Easter letter to the churches of Egypt".

## 2. Events, in order

E1. **Nicaea, a deacon (one sentence only; the council is the sibling's story).** Socrates: the party of Arius "were powerfully opposed by Athanasius, a deacon of the Alexandrian church, who was highly esteemed by Alexander his bishop, and on that account was much envied" (I.8, SZ 2792–2797). LATER. Widely Accepted. A **deacon** is a church officer under the bishop. Rufinus, in Latin: "sed et Athanasius eodem tempore Alexandri diaconus Alexandrini episcopi aderat, consiliis senem quam plurimis iuvans" (RUF 26805–26807; OCR line-break hyphens joined, "luvans" = iuvans): "Athanasius too, deacon of Alexander, bishop of Alexandria, was there at that time, helping the old man with a great many counsels" (my rendering). And: "cum ad concilium Nieaeam cum episcopo suo sene Alexandro venerat" (X.15, RUF 27815–27816; OCR "Nieaeam" = Nicaeam): "when he had come to the council of Nicaea with his bishop, the old Alexander" (my rendering). LATER (402–403). Documented as Rufinus's account. With Theodoret's "retinue" line (sibling `alexander-of-alexandria`), two later witnesses say he came with his bishop; Socrates, the witness used here, does not. Bears only on the outline's beat 2 bar, which leaves this to Alexander's story.

E2. **Refuses to readmit Arius; the emperor threatens.** Socrates: "as Athanasius would not receive him, but turned away from him as a pest ... Athanasius nevertheless wholly refused to receive them, and wrote to inform the emperor in reply, that it was impossible for those who had once rejected the faith, and had been anathematized, to be again received into communion" (I.27, SZ 4916–4924). Constantine's letter, as Socrates gives it: "I will forthwith send some one who at my command shall depose you, and drive you into exile." (SZ 4930–4933). LATER. Documented as Socrates's account.

E3. **Charges, and force used against the Melitians.** Socrates: his enemies "suborn by bribes certain of the Melitian heresy to fabricate various charges against Athanasius" (SZ 4946–4948). LATER, partisan for Athanasius. From the other side, a Melitian letter of 335 (BELL's date, "May-June, A.D. 335(?)", BELL 4112, 4347): "So the adherents of Athanasius, hearing of it, came bringing with them soldiers of the Dux and of the Camp ... they beat them and made them all bloody, so that they were in danger of death" (BELL 4693–4706); "he carried off a Bishop of the Lower Country and shut him in the Meat Market ... and on the twenty-seventh he caused seven Bishops to leave the country" (BELL 4736–4741; "he" is Athanasius in Bell's reading of the letter). CONTEMP, hostile. Documented as the Melitian writer's charge. The **Melitians** were a breakaway church in Egypt (census: melitian-schism). Must be told, credited (do-not-soften).

E4. **Tyre, 335, and the first exile (335/6–337).** FI VIII: "he went to that Synod of his enemies which was assembled at Tyre ... when a discovery was made of the plot against him, he removed thence and fled in an open boat to Constantinople ... presented himself before the Emperor Constantine, and spoke plainly. But his enemies, by various secret devices, influenced the Emperor, who suddenly condemned him to exile, and he set out ... to Gaul" (NP4 64143–64155; ED date Feb. 5, 336). FI IX: "He was in Treviri of Gaul" (NP4 64161). CONTEMP. Documented. Socrates: the synod "voted to depose him"; Arsenius, said to have been murdered by him, "was found alive to assist in deposing him" (I.32, SZ 5208–5220). Socrates on the cause: Constantine, told Athanasius "had threatened to prohibit the sending of corn", "at once condemned him to exile ... Now some affirm that the emperor came to this decision with a view to the establishment of unity in the church, since Athanasius was inexorable in his refusal to hold any communion with Arius and his adherents. He accordingly took up his abode at Treves" (I.35, SZ 5360–5372). LATER. The cause: Contested (two reasons in one witness, D3). **Trier** (Treves, Treviri) is in today's Germany. Return: "Constantine having died ... Athanasius, now liberated, returned from Gaul triumphantly" (FI X, NP4 64168–64171; ED date Nov. 23, 337). Documented.

E5. **Second exile (339–346): Gregory, Rome.** FI XI: "he was pursued in the night, and the next day he fled from the Church of Theonas, after he had baptized many. Then, four days after, Gregorius the Cappadocian entered the city as Bishop." (NP4 64183–64187; ED date March 18–19, 339). CONTEMP. Documented. Socrates: Syrianus and "the corps of heavy armed soldiers, five thousand in number, conducted Gregory to Alexandria" (II.11, SZ 6125–6128); he "immediately hastened to Rome" (SZ 6148). Julius, bishop of Rome, "restored to each his own place" (II.15, SZ 6303–6317). LATER. Documented as Socrates. Return: Gregory "having died ... he returned from Rome and Italy ... the people and all those in authority met him a hundred miles distant" (FI XVIII, NP4 64264–64270; ED date Oct. 21, 346); HA: entered Alexandria "Paophi xxiv" 346 (NP4 61568–61571). CONTEMP. Documented.

E6. **In Rome, the monks' story reaches Marcella.** Jerome, Letter 127.5: "It was from some priests of Alexandria, and from pope Athanasius, and subsequently from Peter, who, to escape the persecution of the Arian heretics, had all fled for refuge to Rome ... it was from these that Marcella heard of the life of the blessed Antony, then still alive, and of the monasteries in the Thebaid founded by Pachomius" (NP6 26087–26094). LATER (412, NP6 25944). Documented as Jerome's account. ("Pope" was then a title for the bishop of Alexandria too.) Marcella's own live story already tells this with the quote "publicly to call herself a nun" (CEN, marcella-of-rome): no quote here, one clause at most.

E7. **The third exile (356–362): the night in the church of Theonas.** OWN, *Defence of his Flight* 24 (NP4 37296–37324; ED dates the work 357–8, NP4 36360–36368): "It was now night, and some of the people were keeping a vigil preparatory to a communion on the morrow, when the General Syrianus suddenly came upon us with more than five thousand soldiers, having arms and drawn swords, bows, spears, and clubs ... Now I considered that it would be unreasonable in me to desert the people during such a disturbance, and not to endanger myself in their behalf; therefore I sat down upon my throne, and desired the Deacon to read a Psalm, and the people to answer, 'For His mercy endureth for ever,' and then all to withdraw and depart home." (NP4 37296–37307). "But I refused, declaring that I would not do so, until they had retired one and all ... saying that it was better that my safety should be endangered, than that any of them should receive hurt. So when the greater part had gone forth ... the monks who were there with us and certain of the Clergy came up and dragged us away. And thus (Truth is my witness), while some of the soldiers stood about the sanctuary, and others were going round the Church, we passed through, under the Lord's guidance" (NP4 37311–37320). Greek, PG25 97994–98000, 98024–98027 (legible): "Ἐγὼ δὲ ἄλογον ἡγούμενος ἐν τοσαύτῃ συγχύσει καταλεῖψαι τοὺς λαοὺς, καὶ μὴ μᾶλλον προκινδυνεύειν αὐτῶν, καθεσθεὶς ἐπὶ τοῦ θρόνου, προέτρεπον τὸν μὲν διάκονον ἀναγινώσκειν ψαλμόν· τοὺς δὲ λαοὺς ὑπακούειν, Ὅτι εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα τὸ ἔλεος αὐτοῦ, καὶ πάντας οὕτως ἀναχωρεῖν, καὶ εἰς τοὺς οἴκους ἀπιέναι." and "βέλτιον εἶναι λέγων ἐμὲ κινδυνεύειν ἢ βλαβῆναί τινας ἐξ ὑμῶν" (OCR "ἔιιεος" = ἔλεος, "βλαδλῆναί" = βλαβῆναί; the Greek reads "of you", the English "of them"; the OCR does not settle it). OWN. Documented as his account; the "more than five thousand" is his count. Independent dating: HA: Syrianus "entered the Church of Theonas with their whole force of soldiers by night ... But bishop Athanasius escaped their hands" (NP4 61648–61656; ED date Feb. 8–9, 356). FI XXVIII: "he escaped in a miraculous manner" (NP4 64356–64360). CONTEMP. Documented. **Theonas** is the name of a church in Alexandria.

E8. **In hiding, and the cost to others.** FI XXIX: George "entered ... and acted with excessive violence. But Athanasius, the Bishop, had fled, and was sought for in the city with much oppression, many being in danger on this account." (NP4 64366–64369; ED date Feb. 24, 357). FI XXXII: the prefect and the duke, "having entered a private house and a small cell, in search of Athanasius the Bishop, bitterly tortured Eudæmonis, a perpetual virgin" (NP4 64392–64395). CONTEMP. Documented. HA's reckoning of his hiding: "At Alexandria [and] in uncertain places in hiding ... lxxii months and xiv days" (NP4 61922–61924). Gregory, a panegyric: he "betook himself to the holy and divine homes of contemplation in Egypt" (*Or.* 21.19, NP7 29932–29937); the monks, when searchers came, "offered their necks to the sword" (21.20, NP7 29968–29972). CONTEMP (praise). Documented as Gregory's praise; the monks' risk is his claim.

E9. **A hostile pagan report (optional).** Ammianus, of 355 (the running head, AMM 4161): Athanasius "was a man who sought to magnify himself above his profession, and to mix himself up with affairs which did not belong to his province, as continual reports made known, an assembly of many of his sect met together ... and deprived him ... For it was said that he, being very deeply skilled in the arts of prophecy and the interpretation of auguries and omens, had very often predicted coming events." (AMM 4181–4192). CONTEMP, a non-Christian soldier-historian reporting rumor. Documented as Ammianus's report of the charges.

E10. **His defense of the creed, written in exile: *De Synodis*.** ED: "Written 359 ... the last of the great and important group of writings of the third exile" (NP4 55961–55964). OWN, §41: "those, however, who accept everything else that was defined at Nicæa, and doubt only about the Coessential, must not be treated as enemies; nor do we here attack them as Ario-maniacs, nor as opponents of the Fathers, but we discuss the matter with them as brothers with brothers, who mean what we mean, and dispute only about the word." (NP4 58852–58859). Greek, PG26 63383–63390 (legible): "τοὺς ἀποδεχομένους τὰ μὲν ἄλλα πάντα τῶν ἐν Νικαίᾳ γραφέντων, περὶ δὲ μόνον τὸ ὁμοούσιον ἀμφιβάλλοντας, χρὴ μὴ ὡς πρὸς ἐχθροὺς διακεῖσθαι· καὶ γὰρ καὶ ἡμεῖς οὐχ ὡς πρὸς Ἀρειομανίτας, οὐδ᾽ ὡς μαχομένους πρὸς τοὺς Πατέρας ἐνιστάμεθα, ἀλλ᾽ ὡς ἀδελφοὶ πρὸς ἀδελφοὺς διαλεγόμεθα, τὴν αὐτὴν μὲν ἡμῖν διάνοιαν ἔχοντας, περὶ δὲ τὸ ὄνομα μόνον διστάζοντας." OWN. Documented. "Coessential" (ὁμοούσιον) is Nicaea's word "of one being" (the Nicaea story's spoken form, CEN). "The Fathers" are the bishops of Nicaea. In the same section he still calls those who reject the council outright "foes of Christ" (NP4 58848–58850): the welcome has a limit.

E11. **The *Life of Antony*.** Heading: "The life and conversation of our holy Father, Antony: written and sent to the monks in foreign parts by our Father among the Saints, Athanasius, Bishop of Alexandria." (NP4 31149–31152; ED: "This heading, preserved in the Evagrian version, is probably the original one", 31154–31156). Preface: "You have entered upon a noble rivalry with the monks of Egypt ... For by this time there are monasteries among you ... since you asked me to give you an account of the blessed Antony's way of life ... that you also may bring yourselves to imitate him ... seeing that for monks the life of Antony is a sufficient pattern of discipline." (NP4 31170–31185). OWN (on the ascription, D1). ED heading: "(Written between 356 and 362)" (NP4 30421–30422): editor's date. Its end: "Read these words, therefore, to the rest of the brethren that they may learn what the life of monks ought to be" (§94, NP4 33583–33584). Antony's deathbed charge in it: "Have no fellowship with the schismatics, nor any dealings at all with the heretical Arians" (sibling sheet, NP4 33500–33503): the book also served his fight.

E12. **Julian: the fourth exile (362–363).** HA: on Feb. 4–8, 362 (ED dates) Julian ordered exiled bishops home, and Athanasius "entered the Church in the same month Mechir" (NP4 61734–61742); then the prefect "published an Edict of the Emperor Julianus, that Athanasius, Bishop, should retire from Alexandria, and no sooner was the Edict published, than the Bishop left the town" (NP4 61748–61751; ED date Oct. 23–24, 362). He went up the Nile to Hermopolis and Antinoopolis, and on Julian's death met the emperor Jovian (NP4 61771–61782). CONTEMP. Documented. Gregory: Julian "openly banished the Bishop from the city" (21.32, NP7 30320–30323). Gregory, on the council of 362 (ED date): the Greek and Latin terms "Hypostases" and "Persons" were "taken to indicate a difference of faith", and Athanasius, "after he had carefully weighed the meaning of their expressions, and found that they had the same sense ... by permitting each party to use its own terms, he bound them together in unity of action" (21.35, NP7 30404–30433). CONTEMP (praise). Documented as Gregory's account. Optional.

J1. **Julian's own words on the fourth exile (362).** Letter 24, "To the Alexandrians, an Edict" ([362, Constantinople], editor's date, JUL 374): "For we have not, even now, granted to the Galilaeans who were exiled by Constantius of blessed memory to return to their churches, but only to their own countries. Yet I learn that the most audacious Athanasius, elated by his accustomed insolence, has again seized what is called among them the episcopal throne, and that this is not a little displeasing to the God-fearing citizens of Alexandria. Wherefore we publicly warn him to depart from the city forthwith, on the very day that he shall receive this letter of our clemency." (JUL 376; note markers left out). Editor: "Athanasius withdrew from Alexandria, but not from Egypt, in consequence of this edict" (JUL 386). Letter 46, to Ecdicius, prefect of Egypt ([362, About October, from Antioch], JUL 692): "you ought at least to have written about that enemy of the gods, Athanasius ... if Athanasius the enemy of the gods does not depart from that city, or rather from all Egypt, before the December Kalends, I shall fine the cohort which you command a hundred pounds of gold ... Infamous man! He has had the audacity to baptise Greek women of rank during my reign! Let him be driven forth!" (JUL 694). Letter 47, to the Alexandrians ([362, Nov. or Dec. From Antioch], JUL 708), refuses their request for him: "it would have been unreasonable for you to demand back Athanasius" (JUL 710); "I am informed that the man is a clever rascal —then you must know that for this very reason he has been banished from the city. For a meddlesome man is unfit by nature to be leader of the people ... as I long ago gave orders that he depart from the city, I now say, let him depart from the whole of Egypt." (JUL 716). Editor: "Athanasius had left Alexandria on October 24th, 362, and, not long after, the Alexandrians petitioned Julian for his return. This is his answer to them." (JUL 722). CONTEMP, hostile, the emperor's own letters. Documented. Agrees with HA on the edict and the date of his leaving (E12). The reasons Julian gives are his own (D10). "That enemy of the gods" is in no census `longDescription` (searched).

E13. **Valens: the fifth exile (365–366).** HA: a decree of Valentinian and Valens (ED date May 5, 365) ordered bishops exiled under Constantius and returned under Julian cast out anew; riot followed (NP4 61865–61888). "the Bishop Athanasius left the Church secretly by night, and retired to a villa near the New River ... staying at the aforesaid property ... iv whole months" (NP4 61890–61900; ED: Oct. 5, 365–Jan. 31, 366); the imperial notary Bresidas, with "a multitude of the people of the Christians", "led him in to the Church" (NP4 61901–61913). CONTEMP. Documented. Socrates instead: "concealed himself for four entire months in an ancestral tomb" (IV.13, SZ 13058–13061) (D4).

E14. **367: the Easter letter that lists the books.** FI XXXIX (366–7): "In this year he [Ath.] wrote, forming a Canon of the Holy Scriptures." (NP4 64482–64483). CONTEMP. Documented. The letter, §§2–7 (NP4 68752–68829): he writes "lest ... some few of the simple should be beguiled ... and should henceforth read other books—those called apocryphal—led astray by the similarity of their names with the true books" (68754–68759); "to set before you the books included in the Canon, and handed down, and accredited as Divine" (68771–68773). Old Testament: "twenty-two books in number" (68779), with Baruch, Lamentations and "the epistle" joined to Jeremiah (68792–68793), and no Esther. New Testament: the four Gospels, Acts, seven general letters, fourteen of Paul including Hebrews, "And besides, the Revelation of John" (68797–68806): twenty-seven books. §6: "These are fountains of salvation, that they who thirst may be satisfied with the living words they contain. In these alone is proclaimed the doctrine of godliness. Let no man add to these, neither let him take ought from these." (68808–68811). Greek, PG26 113044, 113081–113084 (legible): "Ταῦτα πηγαὶ τοῦ σωτηρίου, ὥστε τὸν διψῶντα ἐμφορεῖσθαι τῶν ἐν τούτοις λογίων· ἐν τούτοις μόνοις τὸ τῆς εὐσεβείας διδασκαλεῖον εὐαγγελίζεται. Μηδεὶς τούτοις ἐπιβαλλέτω, μηδὲ τούτων ἀφαιρείσθω τι." §7: other books "not indeed included in the Canon, but appointed by the Fathers to be read by those who newly join us ... The Wisdom of Solomon, and the Wisdom of Sirach, and Esther, and Judith, and Tobit, and that which is called the Teaching of the Apostles, and the Shepherd ... nor is there in any place a mention of apocryphal writings. But they are an invention of heretics" (68817–68826). OWN. Documented. A **canon** here means the fixed list of books read as Scripture.
   Caution: FI XXXIX also tells of Lucius in 367: "he left the city, and was rescued in a wonderful manner, while the multitude sought to kill him" (64480–64482). "He" there is Lucius, the rival bishop, not Athanasius (HA 61940–61970 tells the same of Lucius).

E15. **Death, 373.** HA: "the v day before he fell asleep, he ordained Peter, one of the ancient presbyters, Bishop, who carried on the Episcopate, following him in all things" (NP4 61972–61975). FI XLV: "he [Athan.] departed this life in a wonderful manner" (NP4 64530–64533). CONTEMP. Documented. Gregory: "In a good old age he closed his life ... the honours at his departure surpassed even those of his return from exile; the object of many tears" (21.37, NP7 30465–30470). CONTEMP (praise). Socrates: "after being engaged in so many and such severe conflicts on behalf of the church, departed this life ... having governed that church amidst the greatest perils forty-six years. He left as his successor Peter" (IV.20, SZ 13310–13321). LATER.

E16. **His time away, counted.** HA, written within a generation: of forty years as bishop to 368 he was at Trier "[ii years iv months xi days]", "in Italy and the West" "xc months and iii days", in hiding "lxxii months and xiv days", on journeys "xv months and xxii days", at the villa "iv months": "The result will be exactly vi months and xvii years and xx days" (NP4 61915–61928; bracketed words restored by ED). CONTEMP. Documented as HA's reckoning: about seventeen and a half years of his first forty away from his church.

## 2a. Added after Mark's ruling (2026-11-18): his teaching, and Frumentius

T1. **Why the Son must be true God: his own words.** *On the Incarnation* 54 (ED dates it "in or shortly before 318", NP4 12090–12091, with *Against the Heathen*; editor's date): "For He was made man that we might be made God" (NP4 18743–18744; ED note gives the Greek θεοποιηθῶμεν, 18744). OWN. Documented. The PG25 OCR did not yield this line (searched θεοποιηθῶμεν); English only. This line is the existing card's quote (Card.existing.json); not found in any other census story.

T2. **The link to Nicaea's word, in his own words.** *Discourses against the Arians* II.69–70 (ED: "Written Between 356 And 360", the third exile, NP4 40865–40872). §69: "Again, if the Son were a creature, man had remained mortal as before, not being joined to God; for a creature had not joined creatures to God" (NP4 49673–49675). §70: "For therefore did He assume the body originate and human, that having renewed it as its Framer, He might deify it in Himself, and thus might introduce us all into the kingdom of heaven after His likeness. For man had not been deified if joined to a creature, or unless the Son were very God" (NP4 49717–49730). Greek, PG26 23404–23409 (legible): "οὕτω γὰρ καὶ προσελάβετο τὸ γενητὸν καὶ ἀνθρώπινον σῶμα, ἵνα, τοῦτο ὡς δημιουργὸς ἀνακαινίσας, ἐν ἑαυτῷ θεοποιήσῃ, καὶ οὕτως εἰς βασιλείαν οὐρανῶν εἰσαγάγῃ πάντας ἡμᾶς καθ᾽ ὁμοιότητα ἐκείνου. Οὐκ ἂν δὲ πάλιν ἐθεοποιήθη κτίσματι συναφθεὶς ὁ ἄνθρωπος, εἰ μὴ θεὸς ἦν ἀληθινὸς ὁ Υἱός". OWN. Documented. This is the link he himself makes: only a Son who is true God can join humankind to God. What "deified" means in his own text: "joined to God", freed "from sin and from the curse", abiding "for ever, risen from the dead and clothed in immortality and incorruption" (§69, NP4 49683–49698). He does not, in these lines, say that this is why he endured exile; the story must not say so either.

F1. **Frumentius made bishop of Aksum.** Constantius's letter "to the Princes of Auxumis respecting Frumentius", as Athanasius copied it into his *Apology to Constantius* 31: "this Frumentius was advanced to his present rank by Athanasius, a man who is guilty of ten thousand crimes" (NP4 36108–36109, 36149–36151); the emperor orders Frumentius sent to Egypt to George, the rival bishop (36144–36147). CONTEMP, hostile, preserved by Athanasius. Documented. Socrates: "Athanasius ... requested Frumentius himself to accept the bishopric" (I.19, SZ 4365–4367; Socrates calls the land "India", 4369). LATER. Documented. Rufinus, Socrates' source: "tum vero Athanasius, nam is nuper sacerdotium susceperat, attentius et perpensius Frumentii dicta gestaque considerans, in concilio sacerdotum ait: »et quem alium invenimus virum talem ...«" (RUF 27374–27376; OCR "dieta" = dicta): "Then Athanasius, for he had lately taken up the priesthood [the bishop's office], weighing Frumentius's words and deeds with care, said in a council of priests: 'And what other man do we find like this?'" (my rendering). LATER (402–403). Documented. Bears on nothing the outline tells beyond F1's one clause. **Aksum** was a kingdom in today's Ethiopia and Eritrea (census: frumentius, aksumite-christianity-conversion-era).

## 3. What came of him (heaviest first)

I1. **The *Life of Antony* travelled.** (a) Latin: Jerome, *Lives* 125: Evagrius of Antioch "translated also the Life of the blessed Anthony from the Greek of Athanasius into our language" (NP3 41678–41682). LATER (about 20 years). Documented. Evagrius's own preface, quoted by ED: "I have at your request rendered the Life of the blessed Antony in such a way as to give the full sense, but cut short somewhat of the words" (NP4 31158–31167; ED's quotation, the Latin not held). (b) Jerome, *Life of Paul* 1 (374 or 375 by its translator, NP6 29736): "inasmuch as both Greek and Roman writers have handed down careful accounts of Antony, I have determined to write a short history of Paul's early and latter days" (NP6 29758–29761). LATER. Documented. (c) Socrates sends his readers to it: "Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria, has anticipated us, having devoted an entire book to his biography" (I.21, SZ 4507–4512). LATER. Documented. (d) Syriac: a Syriac *Life* "composed by Saint Athanasius, the Archbishop of Alexandria" (BUD 609); Budge's manuscript is a copy of one "written probably in the thirteenth or fourteenth century" (BUD 43, editor). Documented that a Syriac version exists; its date is not given. (e) Jerome's *Lives* 87 lists "Life of Anthony the monk" among his works (NP3 41160–41170). (g) Rufinus declines to retell Antony's life because of "ille libellus ... qui ab Athanasio scriptus etiam Latino sermone editus est" (RUF 27258–27259): "that little book, written by Athanasius and published in Latin as well" (my rendering). LATER (402–403). Documented: by 402 a Latin witness credits the *Life* to him and knows it in Latin (adds to D1 and I1a). Not needed in the story; Socrates' line (c) does the same work. (f) The *Life*'s own claim: Antony was heard of "into Spain and into Gaul ... into Rome and Africa" (§93, NP4 33572–33573). Augustine and the officials at Trier: told by the sibling `antony-the-great`; leave to it.

I2. **DEFENDED: the creed of Nicaea.** (a) Gregory, preaching at Constantinople (379 or 380, ED): "his life and habits form the ideal of an Episcopate, and his teaching the law of orthodoxy" (21.37, NP7 30461–30463); "the illustrious warrior must needs conquer in three struggles" (21.32, NP7 30323–30324). CONTEMP. (b) Cyril of Alexandria, 433: "our blessed and all-celebrated Father Athanasius" (card; `cyril-alexandria_three-epistles-grc-eng_pusey1872.txt` 5393–5394). LATER (60 years). Documented. Card only (Cyril is a batch-5 story of its own). (c) Trullo, below, set his rulings among the church's canons.

I3. **His list of books carried into church law.** (a) The Council in Trullo, canon 2 (692, ED date, NP14 1602): "we set our seal likewise upon all the other holy canons set forth by our holy and blessed Fathers ... Likewise too the Canons [i.e. the decretal letters] of ... Athanasius, Archbishop of Alexandria" (NP14 26731–26746). LATE. Documented. That the Festal Letter 39 extract counts among "the Canons of Athanasius" rests on the Greek canon collections as the editor prints them: "Canons and Rulings ... Approved by Name in Canon II. of the Synod in Trullo" (NP14 42292–42293), including "The Epistle of the Same Athanasius Taken from the XXXIX. Festal Epistle" (43328–43333), and ED (Robertson) that its Greek was "preserved, with the heading of the Letter ... by Theodorus Balsamon", a Byzantine canonist (NP4 68743–68748). Widely Accepted (editors). (b) ED notes the letter's first part survives in Coptic and a Syriac version of the rest (NP4 68723–68728, 68748–68752). Editor's. (c) 1920–1921: terms of intercommunion between the Church of England and the Orthodox, "drawn up at the request of the Eastern Churches Committee appointed by the Archbishop of Canterbury and ... (1921) published at their request" (BELLU 4655–4657): "We accept the Canon of Scripture as it is defined by St. Athanasius, and as it has been received by the whole Catholic Church; namely, the twenty-two books of the Old Testament which are contained in the Hebrew Canon, and the twenty-seven books of the New Testament." (BELLU 4675–4679; OCR "Canon-of" and a stray footnote mark after "Athanasius," normalized; first printed in 1920 as by "A Theologian of the English Church", CE 2919–2942). LATE. Documented as that proposal; it was a proposal, not an agreement reached.

I4. **The Coptic church's memory (optional, LATE-LEGEND).** The Copto-Arabic Synaxarium, under 7 Bachons: "En ce jour mourut notre père le patriarche Athanase" (SYN 18319); "Il fut appelé apostolique à cause des fatigues et de la persécution qu'il souffrit." (SYN 18411–18412). Medieval compilation (SYN header). Its life of him has him born of pagans, ruling 47 years, and Antony finding him for the faithful (SYN 18320–18410): legend; not story material. Held only in French, OCR.

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

D1. **Did he write the *Life*?** ED, 1892: "the question being still in dispute, although the balance of qualified opinion is on the side of the Athanasian authorship" (NP4 30432–30434); "the external evidence for the Vita is too strong to allow us to set it aside as spurious" (30447–30449). Jerome (I1a, e), Socrates (I1c) and Gregory name him as writer. Budge: "Many authorities have denied the authenticity" (BUD 123, editor). Widely Accepted. The sibling story says "which the church has long credited to Athanasius"; this story may name him as writer once, with that qualifier available. The line "I was his attendant for a long time" is textually uncertain (ED note, NP4 31200–31204): do not say he served Antony.

D2. **Five exiles, or three?** The chronicles (HA, FI) record five departures: 335/6 (Trier), 339 (Rome), 356 (hiding), 362 (Julian), 365 (the villa). ED counts "the five exiles of Athanasius" (NP4 7104; section titles 1459–1493). Gregory counts "three struggles" (21.32, NP7 30323–30324; ED note: "He was thrice banished. a.d. 336 ... 356 ... 362", 30324–30327). Five is Widely Accepted as a count of times he left his city under pressure; only some were formal banishments by an emperor.

D3. **Why Constantine sent him to Trier.** Socrates gives the grain charge and "some affirm" church unity (E4). FI: "his enemies, by various secret devices, influenced the Emperor" (NP4 64150–64152). Contested; tell both or neither.

D4. **Where he hid in 365.** HA: a villa near the New River (E13). Socrates: an ancestral tomb (E13). Card only.

D5. **Which flight had the psalm?** Socrates sets the psalm and the escape at Gregory's arrival in 339 (II.11, SZ 6139–6148). Athanasius's own *Defence* (E7), HA and FI set the night attack on Theonas under Syrianus in 356. Tell it in 356, on his own word.

D6. **Arsenius.** Socrates: providence brought Arsenius to Tyre (SZ 5086–5097); Sozomen (card) gives a divine prompting or, "as it is said", a plan of Athanasius. Card only.

D7. **Death day.** FI 7 Pachon, HA 8 Pachon (S2); Socrates's consulship gives 371 (ED: "The later date is now universally accepted", SZ 13312–13317). Say "in 373".

D8. **The boy who played bishop.** Rufinus, as Socrates reports it, "nor is it improbable" (I.15, SZ 4002–4019). Late, single chain. Leave out.

D9. **The virgin who hid him.** The Syriac *Paradise* says a virgin hid him "for the whole period of thirty years" (BUD 1119). Impossible against HA and FI. Leave out.

D10. **Why Julian drove him out in 362.** Julian's own letters (J1): he came back to his church without an imperial order; he baptised Greek women of rank; a clever, meddlesome man unfit to lead the people. Rufinus, a generation later and on Athanasius's side: "magorum, filosoforum, haruspicum augurumque manus profana, omnes pariter allegant nihil suis artibus suecessurum, nisi prius Athanasium velut horum omnium obstaculum sustulisset" (X.34, RUF 28796–28798; OCR "suecessurum" = successurum): "the unholy band of magicians, philosophers, soothsayers and augurs all alike urged that their arts would not succeed unless he first removed Athanasius, as the obstacle to them all" (my rendering). Contested: tell none as fact; Julian's reasons may be credited to him.

D11. **Six years in a dry cistern.** Rufinus: "interea sex continuis annis ita latuisse fertur in lacu eisternae non habentis aquam, ut solem numquam viderit" (X.19, RUF 28148–28149; OCR "eisternae" = cisternae): "meanwhile he is said to have hidden for six whole years in a cistern without water, so that he never saw the sun" (my rendering). Rufinus sets it after Tyre and his own "fertur" marks it as report; HA places the six years "at Alexandria [and] in uncertain places in hiding" (E8). Leave out, as D9.

## 6. Not held, or not found

N1. Birth year, family, education: not in any held contemporary witness (the Synaxarium's account is legend, I4).
N2. No held source calls his New Testament list the first of its kind. Do not say "first".
N3. No held source says the 367 list was binding on the churches when he sent it, or that "the canon was closed" by it.
N4. Rufinus's *Church History* (the source of the boyhood story) is held in Latin only (RUF, X.15, from 27809); Socrates' report used.
N5. Jerome's Latin of Letter 127 and Evagrius's Latin *Life* are not held.
N6. "Athanasius against the world" and the "Athanasian Creed": no held source ties either to him. Do not use.
