## The three lines (after one hearing)

- **Who:** Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria in Egypt for forty-five years in the 300s, who held to the creed of Nicaea, that the Son is "of one being" with the Father, against the party of Arius.
- **What happened:** He had to leave his city five times: removed at Tyre and sent to Trier, fled to Rome, hid six years after the night of 356, was driven out by Julian, and hid four months in 365. In those years he wrote the *Life of Antony*. In his Easter letter of 367 he listed the books of Scripture, twenty-seven for the New Testament.
- **Why the connection matters:** He defended Nicaea's creed because, he taught, only a Son who was truly God could join people to God. His list of books became church law in the East and still counted in 1921. His *Life* carried the hermit's life to Latin and Syriac readers.
