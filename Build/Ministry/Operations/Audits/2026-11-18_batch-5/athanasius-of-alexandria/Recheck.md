**2. Epiphanius, *Panarion* 68 (the Melitians) and 69 (Arius).** Opened at PG42 14355–14400 (Greek) and Oehler 44425–44453 (Greek), 44380–44545, 44655–44850 and 46228–46250 (Petavius's Latin). The two Greek OCRs agree on the key lines. Epiphanius wrote in 374–377 (file header) as a Nicene writer outside Egypt, on Athanasius's side. Witness kind: CONTEMP. He says that after Athanasius became bishop, "Ἔπειθε, παρεκάλει, καὶ οὐκ ἠνείχοντο· ἠνάγκαζεν, ἐβιάζετο" (PG42 14371–14372; Oehler 44440–44441): "He persuaded, he pleaded, and they would not bear it; he compelled, he used force" (my rendering). Then once, when the Melitians met, a deacon from Athanasius's following burst in with some of the people and broke a lamp (κανδήλα), "ὡς ἔχει ὁ λόγος", "as the account goes", and a fight broke out. From this came the plot against him (PG42 14374–14379; Oehler 44448–44453).

It does not change what the story may say. It confirms, from his own side, the force on both sides that beat 3 already carries through the 335 Melitian letter. It does not support saying he ordered the beating: the incident is a deacon's, and it is given as report. So the Do not say on E3 stands, and so does the bar on softening. Its order is confused: Theonas succeeds Alexander in 68.7, Achillas in 69.11 (Oehler 46236–46244); Athanasius is "deposed in his absence" and stays "twelve or thirteen years in Italy" after Tyre (Oehler 44824–44826). Those details are not story material.
