## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** William Tyndale, an English priest and scholar who put the New Testament into English from the Greek, when an English Bible needed a bishop's leave.
2. **What happened:** He printed it abroad and smuggled it in, and the king had it burned. He offered the king his life if the bare text of the Bible could go out in English. He was betrayed at Antwerp, held at Vilvoorde, and strangled at a post in 1536, and his body was burned.
3. **Why the connection matters:** The book reached ordinary people no one had allowed to have it, and within a year of his death the king allowed an English Bible that editors find was mostly his.

None is blank or wrong. But one hearing leaves two things loose: the Cologne press is stopped and the story never says the Testament was then printed in full, and the "why the king turned against him" beat is heard only as "of his books, one… had angered the king most", with no turn to hear (BLOCKING 1 and 2).
