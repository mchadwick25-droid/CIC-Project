## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** William Tyndale, an English priest and scholar who put the New Testament into English from the Greek, at a time when an English Bible needed a bishop's leave.
2. **What happened:** He printed it abroad and had it smuggled home. The bishop of London and the king had the copies called in and burned. He offered the king his life if the bare text of the Bible could go out in English. He was betrayed at Antwerp and strangled at a post in 1536, and his body was burned.
3. **Why the connection matters:** The book reached ordinary readers who had no one's permission to have it. Within a year of his death the king allowed an English Bible that was mostly Tyndale's work under another name.

All three lines can be drawn from one hearing. None is blank or wrong.

Also told: his reason, that ordinary people needed the Bible in their own tongue; the Old Testament work from the Hebrew; his offer to stop writing and die if the king allowed the bare text; and what readers suffered for the book.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

### BLOCKING 1. The readers' burnings are left out

The approved heart (Hearts_Plain, Also told) says "readers were burned as heretics". The outline's Also told says "some were later burned as heretics", and its Connection and What came 1 give Maundrel "burned at Salisbury in 1556, under Queen Mary" (F8 8612–8616, "the furious flames of fire"; "John Maundrel, husbandman"; A.D. 1556). The brief stops at "whipped, jailed and questioned". That softens the record's force (Kit, Rigor), and it is the heaviest part of What came of him.

Change: after the Maundrel sentence (para 8), add for example: "Some readers were later burned as heretics, for teaching the church condemned as false. Maundrel himself was burned at Salisbury in 1556, when the old church was restored under Queen Mary." Keep the outline's bar: do not say he was burned for reading or owning the Testament. Glosses needed: heretic, Queen Mary. About 30 words.

Do not say line to add: *Not "whipped, jailed and questioned" alone, leaving out the burnings. Say some readers were later burned as heretics, and that Maundrel was burned at Salisbury in 1556 under Queen Mary. Do not say it was for reading the book (Foxe gives his charges as words against the Mass and purgatory).*

### BLOCKING 2. Only the New Testament is told as his work, so the 1537 finding is unclear

The outline's Also told holds "the exile's work": he put "the first five books of the Bible into English from the Hebrew in 1530" (M10, DT 1665–1676) and kept correcting his English against the Greek (M7a, DT 30040–30044, 1534). Its Defended item adds that his own work stays open to correction by Scripture (M15, DT 2802–2811). None of this is in the brief.

Without it, the listener hears that "most of" a whole English Bible was Tyndale's, having heard only of his New Testament. One hearing cannot square those two. The translator's work also shrinks to one book.

Change: in para 5, in time order, add a clause such as "In 1530 he printed the first five books of the Bible, put into English from the Hebrew they were written in." In para 7, before the betrayal, add for example: "In 1534 he put out his Testament again, compared once more with the Greek, and asked readers to refuse it wherever the Bible itself disallowed it." Gloss "first five books" as the outline gives it. About 40 words.

Do not say line to add: *Not the New Testament alone as his work. Tell the first five books of the Bible from the Hebrew (1530), so that the editors' finding that the 1537 Bible was mostly his can be understood.*

### MINOR 1. Q1 loses its "why"

Para 3 gives Q1 in narration, which is fine and says no more than the source (DT 25719–25724). But it drops "This alone moved me to translate", so it does not land as his reason. Change: "That alone, he wrote, moved him to translate: people outside the church's ordained ranks could hold no truth unless…".

### MINOR 2. "He called it" (para 4) can be heard as Tyndale

"He" has meant Tyndale throughout. Change to "The bishop called it the work of 'Luther's sect'…".

### MINOR 3. The mercy line is the minister's, not the king's (source finding)

Para 6 has "showed him the king's word: Tyndale's newest book was full of lies, yet the king would show mercy if he submitted." The verdict on the book came through Cromwell for the king (DT 1950–1990). The mercy clause is Cromwell's own assurance about the king ("I doubt not but the king's highness would be much joyous… is so inclined to mercy", DT 2068–2083). The outline has it right: "the minister added a line saying the king would show mercy".

This sentence is also 30 to 31 words. Split it and fix the credit, for example: "In 1531 that envoy met him outside Antwerp. The king, through his minister, called Tyndale's newest book full of lies. But the minister added that the king would show mercy if he submitted, and the envoy showed Tyndale that line."

### MINOR 4. Foxe is credited three times in four sentences (para 7)

"Foxe tells… In Foxe's account… Foxe says" reads as a report on the record. Keep "Foxe says he cried out" (Do not say D5). Credit the rest once, for example: "In 1535, as Foxe tells it, an Englishman… After more than a year…, he was tied to a post and strangled, and his body was burned." Saves about 3 words. See Length.

### MINOR 5. Listening snags

- "No reply is held" (para 7) is the project's own word. Say "No answer from the king is known."
- "wrote on the king's own matters" (para 5) is vague when heard. Say "wrote about kings and their power".
- "faster than bishops could buy and burn it" (para 8): the buying was never told, so "buy" puzzles. Either drop "buy and", or keep it and accept the small puzzle. The phrase comes from the outline's Connection, so this is optional.

### MINOR 6. The "New Testament" gloss comes three sentences late

The phrase is first heard in sentence 1 and glossed in sentence 4. Most listeners know the phrase, and the gloss comes before anything depends on it, so this is minor. If wanted, open with "an English New Testament, the second part of the Bible, was on the press", and keep para 2's gloss short.
