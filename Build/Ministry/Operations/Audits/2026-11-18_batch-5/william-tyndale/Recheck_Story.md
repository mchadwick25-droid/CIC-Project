## Verdict: FIX (one wording)

### The flagged line: "That alone, he wrote, moved him to translate…"

**Ruling:** the reviser is right, and it needs a fix. The sentence comes straight after "He was born near the Welsh border and schooled at Oxford and Cambridge." Heard once, "That" points back, and the nearest thing it can point to is his schooling. The story would then say that Oxford and Cambridge moved him to translate. The source says no such thing: in Tyndale's preface, "Which thing only" (DT 25719) refers to the clergy's riddles, which the story does not tell. The outline renders the line forward, "This alone moved me to translate…" (Q1). A forward-pointing "this" before the colon keeps the reason and its "only", and the listener cannot attach it to the schooling.

**Replace** (para 3):

> That alone, he wrote, moved him to translate: people outside the church's ordained ranks could hold no truth unless the Bible lay plain before their eyes in their own tongue.

**with:**

> Only this, he wrote, moved him to translate: people outside the church's ordained ranks could hold no truth unless the Bible lay plain before their eyes in their own tongue.
