## The three lines (after one hearing)

- **Who:** John Wesley, a priest of England's state church, who felt in 1738 that he trusted Christ alone and was forgiven.
- **What happened:** from 1739 he preached outdoors to thousands, against his own sense of church order and the bishop's advice; he gathered converts into societies and, from 1742, weekly classes of about twelve under a leader; the work drew mobs; ordinary men who were not ordained preached for him.
- **Why the connection matters:** his written Rules and the class went to America almost unchanged, and the societies became the Methodist churches; Richard Allen chose Methodism for its discipline and saw it turned against Black members.
