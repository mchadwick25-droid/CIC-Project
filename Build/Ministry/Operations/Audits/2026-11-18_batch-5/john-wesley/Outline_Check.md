# Outline check: John Wesley

Checker's record, batch 5, 2026-11-18. Written before reading `Outline.md`.

## My own account of the heart (from the census entry, the source sheet and what he is known for)

**Essence.** John Wesley, a priest of the Church of England and an Oxford college fellow, came back from a failed mission in Georgia unsure of his own faith. At a society meeting in Aldersgate Street, London, on 24 May 1738, he felt that he trusted Christ alone and was assured his sins were forgiven. From April 1739 he preached in the open air to crowds of thousands, outside any parish of his own, and gathered those who responded into weekly societies and, from 1742, classes of about twelve under a leader. With lay preachers and a yearly Conference, these became the Methodist churches in Britain and America, though he meant them to stay inside the Church of England.

**Also told.** The Moravians in the storm and Peter Böhler (the doubt before 1738); Whitefield's example in the fields at Bristol; "all the world as my parish" and the bishop of Bristol telling him to leave; the classes arising from a penny-a-week scheme and turning into pastoral oversight, with members put out; mob violence (Wednesbury 1743); the first Conference and lay preachers (1744); his claim to the end that Methodism was not a separate party; his ordaining preachers for America in 1784; his death in 1791. What came of him: his Rules and the class meeting carried to America; the Conference that kept his discipline; the American church formed in 1784 on his plan; more than 130,000 members counted in 1791; lay preachers drawn from his hearers; Richard Allen's choice of Methodist discipline, and its abuse against Black members.

## BLOCKING

**B1. His death is told twice, and the post-death count leads the listener past the ending.** Beat 7 ends: "He died at his house in City Road, London, on 2 March 1791, nearly eighty-eight." Ending 1 tells the same death again: "He died the next morning, 2 March 1791, at his house in City Road". On top of that, What came of them item 3 ("At the first Conference after his death ...") must be heard before the Ending, so a writer who follows the outline either tells the death twice or tells it and then goes back to it. By the kit's bar, that is a failure to understand on one hearing.
*Correction:*
- (a) Delete the last sentence of beat 7. The death is told once, in the Ending. Move "nearly eighty-eight" (LEE 7567) into Ending 1.
- (b) Replace item 3 with: "**The count.** In July 1791 his preachers counted more than 130,000 members of the societies in Europe and America. (I4. MIN 35398–35412. Documented; the total is checked by sum, Sheet I4.)" Move the promise "to follow and imitate him in doctrine, discipline, and life" (MIN 33770–33777) to the card.
- (c) Add to Do not say: "Do not mention his death, or 'after his death', before the Ending. Give the 1791 count as 'in 1791'."

**B2. The gloss for "sacrament" leaves out what the listener needs to follow the 1784 ordinations.** Beat 7 gives the reason for the ordinations as "the sacraments were no longer given there". The list glosses "sacrament" only as "the shared bread and wine ... also called Communion". The source's point is that his preachers in America were not ordained, so they could not give the church's rites: "Even the ablest and most revered preachers ... could not administer the rites of the Church" (J7 240–252, Curnock, editor). Without that, ordaining men does not answer the problem, and the 1784 act cannot be understood on one hearing.
*Correction:*
- Replace the gloss with: "**sacrament(s):** the church's rites, chiefly the Lord's Supper (the shared bread and wine, also called Communion) and baptism, which only an ordained minister could give (PA 244–252; J7 240–249, Curnock's note)."
- In beat 7, after "no longer given there", add: "and his preachers there, not being ordained, could not give them (J7 248–250, editor)".
