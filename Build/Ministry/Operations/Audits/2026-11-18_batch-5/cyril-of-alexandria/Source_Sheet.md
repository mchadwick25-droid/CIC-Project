# Source sheet: Cyril of Alexandria

Census id `cyril-of-alexandria`. Told within **The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt** (census id `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity`; spoken short name "the Church of Alexandria"). Carried on by the churches that refused Chalcedon: the Coptic church of Egypt, the Syriac Orthodox (census short name "Syriac Orthodox"), and the Armenian church. ## Setting

**S1. Nephew of Theophilus; bishop in 412.** SOC 19092–19122: Theophilus "died on the 15th of October" (editor's note 19094–19096: "412 a.d."); "A great contest immediately arose about the appointment of a successor, some seeking to place Timothy the archdeacon in the episcopal chair; and others desiring Cyril, who was a nephew of Theophilus. A tumult having arisen on this account among the people, Abundantius, the commander of the troops in Egypt, took sides with Timothy." (The bracketed "Yet the partisans of Cyril triumphed" at 19104–19105 is the translator's addition, note 19106–19108.) "Whereupon on the third day after the death of Theophilus, Cyril came into possession of the episcopate, with greater power than Theophilus had ever exercised. For from that time the bishopric of Alexandria went beyond the limits of its sacerdotal functions, and assumed the administration of secular matters." CONTEMP. Documented (the year is the editor's, from Socrates's consuls).

**S2. Early life: nothing firm.** CIO 120–124 (Pusey's preface): "of S. Cyril's previous life we have only a few scattered notices. We do not know in what year he was born, nor any thing of his parents, nor where he was brought up." EDITOR. The Coptic tradition fills the gap: HP 32171–32176, 32343–32358: Theophilus sent his "sister's son" to "the desert of Saint Macarius", where "Cyril dwelt there five years in the monasteries", learned "all the Scriptures by heart", and pricked himself with an iron sword to stay awake. Same in SYN17 55215–55229. LATER (tenth-century compilation and the medieval *Synaxarium*), Inferential-Thin as fact; Documented as the Coptic tradition's memory.

**S3. Tradition and spoken names.** Census: told within "The Church of Alexandria and Miaphysite Egypt", short name "Church of Alexandria"; its live text says "Both sides claim the same teachers" and "Chalcedon itself took Cyril's letters as a guide to the faith." Spoken words: "Alexandria", "Egypt", "the Coptic church", "the Syriac church", "Armenia".

**S4. Dates.** Bishop from October 412 (S1). Hypatia killed March 415 (E3, editor's year). Council of Ephesus June 431 (sibling). Peace with John of Antioch 433 (Ephesus sibling; HEF). Died 444: HEF 9932–9935 ("died in the year 444, and had as his successor Dioscurus"); THD 38191–38193 (Jackson quoting Venables: "the death of Cyril, June 9, or 27, 444"); SYN17 55276–55278 (the Coptic calendar keeps his death on 3 Abib, "after thirty-two years on the patriarchal throne"; Basset's heading gives 27 June). EDITOR and LATER; Widely Accepted for 444.

## Events

**E1. The Novatian churches.** SOC 19119–19122: "Cyril immediately therefore shut up the churches of the Novatians at Alexandria, and took possession of all their consecrated vessels and ornaments; and then stripped their bishop Theopemptus of all that he had." CONTEMP. Documented as Socrates's report. "Novatians" = a strict Christian body outside the bishop's communion, with its own churches.

**E2. The Jews expelled; Ammonius.** SOC 19389–19449: after Orestes the prefect had a supporter of Cyril, Hierax, tortured in the theatre, "Cyril... sent for the principal Jews, and threatened them with the utmost severities"; some Jews raised a false cry that a church was on fire at night and killed Christians who ran out; "At daybreak... Cyril, accompanied by an immense crowd of people, going to their synagogues... took them away from them, and drove the Jews out of the city, permitting the multitude to plunder their goods. Thus the Jews who had inhabited the city from the time of Alexander the Macedonian were expelled from it, stripped of all they possessed". SOC 19466–19470: Cyril held out the book of the Gospels to Orestes for reconciliation; Orestes refused. SOC 19484–19519: about five hundred monks of Nitria came "to fight in behalf of Cyril"; one, Ammonius, struck Orestes with a stone; Orestes had him tortured to death; Cyril laid the body in a church, named him "Thaumasius" ("wonderful"), "ordering him to be enrolled among the martyrs"; "the more sober-minded, although Christians, did not accept Cyril's prejudiced estimate of him... And Cyril himself being conscious of this, suffered the recollection of the circumstance to be gradually obliterated by silence." CONTEMP. Documented as Socrates's report. NIK 6465–6481 (the Coptic-side chronicle, seventh century) tells the same massacre and expulsion: the Christians "marched in wrath to the synagogues of the Jews and took possession of them, and purified them and converted them into churches", and expelled the Jews "wholly despoiled". LATER. Documented.

**E3. The murder of Hypatia (415).** SOC 19537–19596: "There was a woman at Alexandria named Hypatia, daughter of the philosopher Theon... Having succeeded to the school of Plato and Plotinus... it was calumniously reported among the Christian populace, that it was she who prevented Orestes from being reconciled to the bishop. Some of them therefore, hurried away by a fierce and bigoted zeal, whose ringleader was a reader named Peter, waylaid her returning home, and dragging her from her carriage, they took her to the church called Cæsareum, where they completely stripped her, and then murdered her with tiles. After tearing her body in pieces, they took her mangled limbs to a place called Cinaron, and there burnt them. This affair brought not the least opprobrium, not only upon Cyril, but also upon the whole Alexandrian church. And surely nothing can be farther from the spirit of Christianity than the allowance of massacres, fights, and transactions of that sort. This happened in the month of March during Lent, in the fourth year of Cyril's episcopate" (editor's note 19595–19596: "415 a.d."). CONTEMP. Documented as Socrates's report. Socrates names a reader as ringleader and does not say Cyril ordered it. "Reader" = a minor church office. For the other witnesses and the editors, see D1.

**E4. His teaching against Nestorius: the second letter.** The quarrel began when Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople, would not simply call Mary "Theotokos", "the one who gave birth to God" (Nestorius sibling). Cyril wrote. ACTS 15730–15745 (English): "we do not say that the nature of the Word was changed and became flesh... but rather that the Word having personally united to himself flesh animated by a rational soul, did in an ineffable and inconceivable manner become man... the two natures being brought together in a true union, there is of both one Christ and one Son; for the difference of the natures is not taken away by the union". ACTS 15753–15756 and EP3 951–957 (Greek): "οὐ γὰρ πρῶτον ἄνθρωπος ἐγεννήθη κοινὸς ἐκ τῆς ἁγίας παρθένου, εἶθ᾽ οὕτω καταπεφοίτηκεν ἐπ᾽ αὐτὸν ὁ Λόγος· ἀλλ᾽ ἐξ αὐτῆς μήτρας ἑνωθεὶς, ὑπομεῖναι λέγεται γέννησιν σαρκικὴν" (Pusey's English, EP3 4332–4335: "For not mere man was first born of the holy Virgin, and then the Word of God came down upon Him, but united from the very womb, He is said to have undergone birth in the Flesh"). ACTS 15784–15785: "We must not, therefore, divide the one Lord Jesus Christ into two Sons." ACTS 15795–15798: "therefore they ventured to call the holy Virgin, the Mother of God, not as if the nature of the Word or his divinity had its beginning from the holy Virgin, but because of her was born that holy body with a rational soul". OWN. Documented. Plain sense: the eternal Word did not come to live in a man already born; from the womb he made a human body and soul his own, so the one born of Mary is God, and she may be called Mother of God.

**E5. The Twelve Anathematisms.** ACTS 16298–16304: "If anyone will not confess that the Emmanuel is very God, and that therefore the Holy Virgin is the Mother of God (Θεοτόκος), inasmuch as in the flesh she bore the Word of God made flesh... let him be anathema." HEF 2280–2283: a synod at Alexandria "summed up the whole in the celebrated twelve anathematisms, composed by Cyril, with which Nestorius was required to agree." OWN. Documented. "Anathema" = cut off, under the church's curse. Theodoret of Cyrrhus wrote against them (census Theodoret story; ZAC 2213–2218).

**E6. Ephesus, 431 (sibling tells it).** ACTS 15816–15829: after his letter was read, Cyril asked the synod whether "rightly and blamelessly and in accordance with that holy synod I have written these things or no"; "all the rest of the bishops... so believed, according as the Fathers had set forth, and as the Epistle of the most holy Archbishop Cyril to Nestorius the bishop declared." SOC 20887–20913: Cyril "began a sharp skirmish of words, with the design of terrifying Nestorius, for he had a strong dislike for him"; his party deposed Nestorius; Nestorius's party deposed Cyril and Memnon; John of Antioch "pronounced unqualified censure on Cyril as the author of all this confusion". CONTEMP. Documented. Here: one sentence only.

**E7. Gifts to the court, and the charge of bribery.** MAN5 97081–97092: "Epistola... Epiphanii Archidiaconi & Syncelli Cyrilli ad Maximianum Episcopum Constantinopolitanum" (the letter of Epiphanius, Cyril's archdeacon, to Maximian of Constantinople; Latin, the Greek lost). MAN5 97149–97155: Cyril has written to "Pulcheriae", "Praeposito Paulo", "Romano Cubiculario", "Marcellae Cubiculariae", "Droseriae. Et directae sunt benedictiones dignae eis" (fitting "blessings", i.e. gifts, were sent to them). MAN5 97228–97238: Pulcheria and those in the palace should be roused "& quicquid avaritiae eorum deest. Quamquam non desint & ipsi diversae benedictiones" (and whatever their greed lacks, though they too have had various gifts). MAN5 97300–97311: "Subjectus autem brevis ostendit quibus hinc directae sint eulogiae... Clerici enim qui hic sunt contristantur quod Ecclesia Alexandrina nudata sit hujus causa turbelae, & debet praeter illa quae hinc transmissa sunt Ammonio Comiti auri libras mille quingentas" (the list below shows to whom gifts were sent; the clergy here grieve that the Alexandrian church is stripped bare by this trouble, and owes, besides what was sent, 1,500 pounds of gold to Count Ammonius). CONTEMP, from Cyril's own household. Documented as the letter's content. HEF 7323–7378 summarises it, and adds "They had even been compelled to obtain a loan for the purpose." HEF 6362–6398 (Hefele's own judgment): "these presents are not all bribes; very many are simply customary recommendations". EDITOR. BH 15815–15817 (Nestorius, some twenty years later): "both he who sold the truth, that is John, and he who [did] more than he, that is Cyril, when the gold of iniquity was exacted" (translators' note: "he was bribed"). OWN of a hostile witness. The card said these letters were "not among the sources used"; Epiphanius's letter is held in MAN5.

**E8. One nature of the Word, incarnate.** After the peace of 433 (Ephesus sibling), Cyril had to explain himself to his own side. HEF 7870–7890: "The apology for his Eirenicon was put forth by Cyril principally in his letters to Bishop Acacius of Melitene, to his own envoy at Constantinople, the priest Eulogius... and also in two letters to Bishop Successus or Succensus of Diocesarea"; in the second "he shows that his words: μία φύσις τοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένη, did not lead to the Apollinarian (better, Monophysite) consequences" which Succensus feared. EDITOR. HEF 7743–7749, quoting the letter to Eulogius (Mansi V): Nestorius "does not acknowledge with us an ἕνωσις of the natures. We unite them and thus receive one Christ, one Son, and one incarnate nature of God (μίαν τὴν τοῦ Θεοῦ φύσιν σεσαρκωμένην)." OWN (in the historian's English). CIO 19832–19848 (Pusey's note, quoting the second letter to Succensus): "They who pervert right things know not that there is in truth One Incarnate Nature of the Word... He will not therefore be severed into two persons and sons, but hath remained One yet not without flesh nor without body, but having the body as His own by Union which may not be plucked asunder. And he who says this, full surely he indicates no commingling, no confusion". OWN. JD 33083–33095 (John of Damascus, eighth century, a Chalcedonian) quotes the same letter, where Cyril says that if one said "the one nature of the Word" and stopped, without "incarnate", the question would be fair. LATER. Documented. Plain sense: after the union there is one Christ, the Word himself, now with a body and soul of his own; "one nature" names that one living subject, not a mixture.

**E9. The burning bush (That Christ is One).** CIO 4328–4333 (Pusey's preface): the treatise "must have been written after the condemnation of Nestorius... not later than A.D. 441". EDITOR. CIO 19860–19883 (dialogue; A is Cyril's voice): "In highest degree, my friend, is he an idle talker who says that confusion and commixture have place, if one Nature of the Son Incarnate and made man, is confessed by us... it were not impossible for God Who loves man to make Himself endurable to the measures of the manhood. And this He foresignified to us darkly, when initiating Moses and limning the mode of the Incarnation as yet in types, for He came in likeness of fire on the bush in the wilderness, and the fire kept playing on the shrub yet was it not consumed. And Moses marvelled at the sight. Yet how is not a tree a thing that has no alliance with fire? and how is the readily consumed wood patient of the onslaught of flame? But this matter was (as I said) a type of a mystery". OWN (Pusey's English; the Greek is not held). Documented.

**E10. Where the formula came from.** MAN5 5225–5241 (Cyril's defense of the Twelve Chapters, Latin): "Itaque scribit aeternae memoriae pater noster & episcopus Athanasius... in libris de incarnatione: ... non duas naturas... sed unam naturam Dei Verbi incarnatam". OWN. LIE 4712–4718 (Lietzmann, editor, 1904): Cyril "ist der erste uns erreichbare zeuge, welcher apollinaristische schriften ahnungslos als werke älterer orthodoxer väter citiert" (the first witness we can reach who unknowingly cites Apollinarian writings as works of older orthodox fathers), citing in 429 the Letter to Jovian under Athanasius's name. PAR 13731–13737 (Parry, quoting Bright): "The phrase of Cyril, recalling that of Apollinaris". EDITOR, Widely Accepted among the editors held. "Apollinarius" = a fourth-century bishop whose teaching (that Christ had no human mind) the church had condemned.

**E11. Death.** SYN17 55272–55278 (Coptic *Synaxarium*, 3 Abib, French): "Il établit douze chapitres où il exposait la foi, et ensuite il composa des discours et des épîtres qui sont jusqu'à ce jour entre les mains des fidèles. Il établit que Dieu le Verbe est d'une seule nature, d'une seule essence, incarné, et excommunia quiconque diviserait le Messie ou sortirait de cette opinion. Quand son labeur fut accompli, il tomba malade légèrement et mourut en paix après être resté trente-deux ans sur le siège patriarcal." LATER (medieval calendar). Documented as the Coptic church's memory. HEF 9932–9935 and THD 38193: 444 (S4).

## Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

**I1. His words as the church's law, for those who refused Chalcedon.** SEV1 2105–2113 (Severus of Antioch, bishop 512–518; *Select Letters* VI.i.9, to Stephen, bishop of Tripolis, headed "From those during episcopacy" and dated 513–518, SEV1 2063–2065): "This we may learn easily and without trouble from the epistle of the holy Cyril (every utterance of whom one would say is a law of the church) to Nestorius". SEV1 6078–6082: "Cyril the chief champion of orthodoxy, a man who in apostolic contests endured dangers even to death". SEV2 8922–8925: "the holy Cyril the teacher of exact orthodoxy". SEV2 6840–6845: Severus condemns a monk who said "It is the same thing for us to speak of two natures and of one incarnate nature of God the Word". LATER (about 80 to 100 years; Brooks's English from the Syriac). Documented. ZAC 13527–13555 (Anthimus of Constantinople to Severus, 535–536): Cyril "in his twelve chapters overthrew Nestorius... To these chapters I assent together with all his writings and embrace them as a holy law, while together with these holy teachings of Cyril I receive also the formula of Zeno uniting the Churches, which aims at... the annulling of the Synod of Chalcedon and the impious Tome of Leo." LATER. Documented. MIC3 16180–16182, 16241–16243 (Michael, patriarch of the Syriac church, answering the Greek emperor about 1170–1172): "Nous désirons beaucoup, et nous ne fuyons pas l'union avec quiconque ne change pas la doctrine des Pères et confesse, avec Athanasius et Cyrillus, une nature du Verbe incarnée!" LATER (some 730 years). Documented.

**I2. Egypt's own memory: Cyril among its fathers.** HP 38467, 38575–38581, 38776–38781 (Theodosius I of Alexandria, refusing the demand of "the prince Justinian" to accept the Tome of Leo and Chalcedon): "But as for me, I follow my fathers who have preceded me, the doctors of the apostolic Church, Athanasius and Cyril and Dioscorus and Timothy, and those who were before them, whose deputy I am, although unworthy." LATER (tenth-century compilation of earlier lives). Documented as the *History*'s account. ZAC 5726–5746: Timothy Salophaciolus, Chalcedon's bishop, returned "by the command of King Zeno" and "exerted himself by all ways and means to keep the people on his side... he wrote in the diptych the names of Cyril and Dioscorus, and read them out; and he did more besides, and yet he was unable to draw the people to himself" ("diptych" = the list of names read aloud at the altar, ZAC 5744). LATER. Documented as the chronicle's account. SYN1 14328–14378 (Coptic *Synaxarium*, 7 Thout, the death of Dioscorus): at Chalcedon Dioscorus "cita à l'appui les paroles du Père Cyrille: L'union du Verbe avec la chair est comme l'union de l'âme avec le corps, ou du feu avec le fer... de même, notre Seigneur le Messie est un Messie un, une nature une, une volonté une." LATER. Documented as the calendar's account. SYN17 55215–55216: the calendar calls him "colonne de la foi et lumière de l'église orthodoxe". HP 35101, 40826, 41089: "Cyril the Wise". LATER. Documented.

**I3. The emperor's peace formula takes his chapters.** EVA 5993–5999 (Zeno's *Henotikon*, 482, as Evagrius copies it): "receiving at the same time the twelve chapters of Cyril, of holy memory, formerly archbishop of the holy Catholic church of the Alexandrians." LATER (copied a century after). Documented. Plain sense: an imperial attempt to reunite the churches without Chalcedon named Cyril's chapters as its measure.

**I4. Both sides claim him (the measure is shared).** ACTS 20255–20262 (Chalcedon's Definition, 451): "it receives the synodical letters of the Blessed Cyril, Pastor of the Church of Alexandria, addressed to Nestorius and the Easterns, judging them suitable, for the refutation of the frenzied folly of Nestorius". Documented. The Chalcedon story already tells the bishops' cry that Leo and Cyril taught the same thing (do not reuse). Census (Church of Alexandria): "Both sides claim the same teachers." Census (Chalcedon story, live text): "The Coptic, Syriac and Armenian churches condemned Eutyches too. They confessed Christ as one nature from two, the faith, they said, of Athanasius and Cyril, and to them \"two natures\" divided him." Armenia's own texts on Cyril are not readable here (Timothy's *Refutation* survives only in Armenian; see the sweep), so Armenia rests on the census text. The quarrel after 451 was over whose reading of Cyril was faithful: Chalcedon's "in two natures" against his "one incarnate nature" (Chalcedon sibling; I1–I2).

**I5. A rival's verdict at his death (contested).** THD 38224–38226 (Letter 180, "ascribed to Theodoret by the Fifth Œcumenical Council"): "At last and with difficulty the villain has gone." THD 38180–38216: Jackson quotes Bright rejecting it and Venables doubting it, with Neander inclined to accept it. Contested.

## Links to other people

- **Nestorius** (sibling): the opponent; Cyril's letters, chapters and Five Tomes answer him (E4, E5).
- **Theodoret of Cyrrhus**: wrote against the Twelve Chapters (ZAC 2213–2218; census Theodoret story); Letter 180 ascribed to him (I5).
- **Socrates of Constantinople**: wrote of him in his lifetime (S1, E1–E3, E6); hostile, perhaps from Novatian sympathy (D2).
- **Athanasius of Alexandria**: the earlier bishop Cyril named as his source for "one nature" (E10); paired with Cyril by later witnesses (I1, I2).
- **Dioscorus of Alexandria**: his successor and archdeacon (HEF 9932–9936); cited Cyril at Chalcedon (I2).
- **Timothy Aelurus**: ordained priest by Cyril, by Zachariah's account (census Timothy story; ZAC 3288). (The diptych act at ZAC 5741–5745 is Timothy Salophaciolus's, not his; I2.)
- **Severus of Antioch**: called his words a law of the church (I1).
- **Theodosius I of Alexandria**: named him among his fathers (I2).
- **Zachariah Rhetor**, **Evagrius Scholasticus**, **John of Nikiu**, **Severus ibn al-Muqaffa**: later witnesses (I1–I3, E2, D1).
- **Leo of Rome**: his Tome set beside Cyril's letters at Chalcedon (I4; Chalcedon sibling).
- **Council of Ephesus (431)**, **Council of Chalcedon (451)**: siblings; one or two sentences each.

## Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. Cyril and Hypatia.** Socrates (CONTEMP) names a reader, Peter, as ringleader of a Christian mob and says the deed brought shame "upon Cyril, but also upon the whole Alexandrian church" (E3); he does not say Cyril ordered it. John of Nikiu (seventh century, Coptic side, NIK 6382–6387, 6481–6492) calls Hypatia a pagan "devoted at all times to magic" who "beguiled" the prefect, has a crowd led by "Peter the magistrate" strip her and drag her through the streets till she died and burn her body, and says "all the people surrounded the patriarch Cyril and named him 'the new Theophilus'; for he had destroyed the last remains of idolatry in the city." The chronicle's chapter summary (NIK 1203–1209) says they burned her body "by the command of the patriarch, Abba Cyril"; the chapter's own text does not say so. SOC 19574–19585 (Zenos's note): "Walch, Gibbon, and Milman incline to hold him guilty"; Robertson gives "indirect responsibility"; Bright: "Cyril was no party to this hideous deed, but it was the work of men whose passions he had originally called out." EDITOR. Whether Cyril bore responsibility: Contested. That a Christian mob from his church killed her: Documented.

**D2. Socrates's bias.** SOC 1075–1083 (Zenos, editor): Socrates's "severe censure of Cyril of Alexandria" is "more easily accounted for on the ground of a more intimate relation between the historian and the Novatians, as both of the above-named eminent men were declared enemies of Novatianism." EDITOR. Credit Socrates's judgments to him.

**D3. Bribery.** Nestorius says Cyril won with "the gold of iniquity" (BH 15817). Cyril's archdeacon's letter (E7) shows lavish gifts to court and a debt of 1,500 pounds of gold. Hefele calls such presents customary, not all bribes (HEF 6387–6389). The gifts are Documented; whether they were bribes is Contested.

**D4. Did Ephesus adopt the Twelve Anathematisms?** ACTS 15867–15880 (Percival): "There has been some difference of opinion among the learned as to whether St. Cyril's Synodal letter which has at its end the anathemas... was formally approved at the Council of Ephesus." Contested. Card only.

**D5. The source of "one nature".** Cyril cited it as Athanasius's (E10); Lietzmann and Bright judge the text Apollinarian. EDITOR. Card only, or one clause credited to modern scholars.

**D6. Early life.** Only the late Coptic tradition (S2) gives his years in the desert; Pusey says nothing is known. Leave out, or in the *History*'s voice.

**D7. The arrest at Ephesus.** HP 34059–34061: Candidian "seized Cyril"; the census Ephesus story has an imperial officer arrest Nestorius, Cyril and Memnon. Sibling's material.
