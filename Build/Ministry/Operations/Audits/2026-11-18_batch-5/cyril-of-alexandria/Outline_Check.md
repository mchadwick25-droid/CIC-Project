# Outline check: Cyril of Alexandria

## My own heart

Process note: this checker opened `Outline.md` once before writing this section, against the role's order. The account below is drawn from the census entry (teaser, dates, places, told within), the source sheet's cited passages and what Cyril is known for. It was then compared with the outline's heart, not taken from it.

- **Essence.** Cyril was bishop of Alexandria from 412 to 444. Against Nestorius he taught that Christ is one: the Word, God's Son, made a human body and soul his own from the womb, so Mary is rightly called Mother of God (his second letter, ACTS 15730–15798; EP3 951–957). He drove the case that had Nestorius deposed at Ephesus in 431, where his letter was judged to agree with Nicaea (ACTS 15816–15829; census teaser). His letters, his twelve anathemas and his "one incarnate nature of the Word" became the measure of faith for the churches that refused Chalcedon, while Chalcedon itself received his letters (SEV1 2105–2108; HP 38776–38781; ACTS 20255–20262).
- **Also told.** How he used his power in Alexandria, from Socrates, a hostile contemporary: a disputed election with the army against him; the Novatian churches shut; the Jews expelled and plundered after a night massacre of Christians; Ammonius; the murder of Hypatia by a mob led by a church reader, with the shame Socrates lays on Cyril and his church, and John of Nikiu's praise of it (SOC 19092–19596; NIK 6465–6492). The costly gifts to the court, from his own archdeacon's letter, and Nestorius's charge of bribery (MAN5; BH 15817). His defence of "one nature" after the peace of 433 (CIO 19826–19848; HEF 7870–7890). His afterlife: Dioscorus, Severus, Theodosius, the Henotikon, Michael the Syrian.

**Comparison.** The outline's heart matches this in substance and the heart Mark approved (`Hearts_Plain.md`, 2026-11-18). It leaves out no major contribution. It keeps the batch job in the launch prompt: the bishop, the teaching of the one Christ, and his words as the measure for the Coptic and other Oriental Orthodox churches. It keeps the rulings in the Decision-Log entry of 2026-11-18 that touch this story: a "Words to explain" list after Setting, and Do not say barring any listed word used without its gloss. Ruling 4 (more time) does not name Cyril.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The Timothy who put Cyril's name in the diptychs was Timothy Salophaciolus, not Timothy Aelurus.** ZAC 5726–5746: "by the command of King Zeno, Timothy Salophaciolus, who had been ejected, returned and took possession of the great church… But Timothy exerted himself by all ways and means to keep the people on his side… he wrote in the diptych the names of Cyril and Dioscorus, and read them out; and he did more besides, and yet he was unable to draw the people to himself." That is Chalcedon's bishop, trying to win the anti-Chalcedonian crowd. The sheet's I2 and the outline credit the act to Timothy Aelurus in five places: Also told (4) ("Timothy, who read Cyril's name aloud at the altar"); What came 2 ("Timothy Aelurus read Cyril's name with Dioscorus's…"); Connection ("Timothy read his name at the altar"); Names (Timothy Aelurus optional for What came 2); and Links ("Timothy Aelurus — … read his name at the altar"). This is an unsupported claim on a named person who has his own story on the tree.

Correction. Choose one of these.
- (a) Recommended, because it shows the measure more strongly. In What came 2, replace the Timothy sentence with: "Even the bishop the emperor set up in Alexandria on Chalcedon's side wrote Cyril's name, with Dioscorus's, in the list of names read at the altar, hoping to win the people. The chronicle says they still would not follow him (ZAC 5726–5746). Documented as the chronicle's account." Give him by role only, not by name. In Also told (4), change "Timothy, who read Cyril's name aloud at the altar" to "even Chalcedon's own bishop in Alexandria, who read Cyril's name at the altar to win the people". In Connection, change "Timothy read his name at the altar" to "even Chalcedon's bishop in Alexandria read his name at the altar to win the crowd, and failed".
- (b) Or drop the diptych act from all three places.

Either way, take the act out of the Timothy Aelurus line in Links ("ordained priest by him (Zachariah)" stays). Take Timothy Aelurus out of the optional Names for What came 2. Add to Do not say: "WRONG 'Timothy Aelurus put Cyril's name in the diptychs.' RIGHT it was Timothy Salophaciolus, Chalcedon's bishop, given by role (ZAC 5726–5746)." The "diptychs" gloss can keep ZAC 5744. The source sheet's I2 carries the same error and should be corrected to match.

### MINOR

**M1. The Severus letter is cited wrongly, and the sheet says it was written in exile.** The quote at SEV1 2105–2108 sits in Book VI, section I, letter 9, "to Stephen, bishop of Tripolis", headed "From those during episcopacy" and dated 513–518 (SEV1 2063–2065). He wrote it as bishop of Antioch, not in exile. Correction: in Q3, change "*Select Letters* VI.1" to "*Select Letters* VI.i.9, to Stephen, bishop of Tripolis, written while bishop (513–518)". In What came 1, change "Severus of Antioch, bishop and exile, wrote of Cyril" to "Severus, bishop of Antioch, wrote of Cyril". In Opening 2, change "about a century later" to "some seventy years after his death". Add to Do not say: "WRONG 'Severus wrote it in exile.' RIGHT he wrote it as bishop of Antioch, 513–518." Q3's note "later in hiding in Egypt" may stay as who he was.

**M2. A witness phrase is already used on the tree.** The tradition page `cyrilline-miaphysite-egyptian-christianity` (documentedStories, "The Death of Hypatia…") quotes Socrates word for word: "This affair brought not the least opprobrium, not only upon Cyril, but also upon the whole Alexandrian church." Mark's ruling of 2026-11-18 exempts only a paraphrase on a tradition page, so the outline's "none is used elsewhere on the tree" is wrong for this phrase. Correction: remove "not only upon Cyril, but also upon the whole Alexandrian church" from the list of witness phrases. In beat 2, change it to a paraphrase: "Socrates says the shame fell on Cyril and on the whole church of Alexandria." Do the same in Also told (2) and Do not say if they are quoted.

**M3. The outline's own wording breaks its "Words to explain" rules where a writer could copy it.** Beat 1 says "Cyril took the see" and "outside his communion". Do not say's RIGHT line says "on the third day he took the see". The Ending says "thirty-two years as patriarch", though the list says "patriarch" is to be used only for Theodosius and Michael. Correction: "took the bishop's chair" in beat 1 and in Do not say. "a strict Christian body with its own churches, outside his church" in beat 1. In the Ending: "after thirty-two years as bishop, or, in the book's words, on the patriarch's throne", with "patriarch" glossed if it is kept.

**M4. Three words a listener needs have no gloss.**
- "their vessels" (beat 1). Heard aloud, it can mean ships. Say "the cups and plates used in their worship" (SOC 19120: "consecrated vessels and ornaments").
- "the holy Virgin" (Q1, beat 4). Gloss it once as Mary: "the holy Virgin, Mary".
- "orthodoxy" (Severus's "chief champion of orthodoxy" in Do not say). If it is used, gloss it as "right belief". Add these three to the list.

**M5. Unsupported date.** Beat 2 is headed "414–415", but no held line dates the expulsion of the Jews. Socrates puts it "about this same time", before Hypatia's death in March 415, and the editor's note gives 415 only for Hypatia (SOC 19390, 19595). Correction: head the beat "The city's quarrels, up to 415", and say "in the years before 415" if a time is needed.

**M6. The monks did not stone Orestes; one of them did.** In beat 2, "Monks who came to fight for Cyril stoned Orestes" says more than the source. SOC 19490–19497: the monks abused Orestes, and "a certain one of them named Ammonius threw a stone at Orestes which struck him on the head". The guards fled "fearing to be stoned". Correction: "Monks who came to fight for Cyril mobbed Orestes in the street, and one of them, Ammonius, hit him on the head with a stone. Orestes had him tortured, and he died." Also correct Do not say's "The monks of Nitria stoned Orestes, not Hypatia" to "A monk of Nitria, Ammonius, stoned Orestes; the monks did not kill Hypatia."

**M7. Socrates's order leaves out Cyril's threat.** Do not say asks for "both halves, in Socrates's order". But Socrates's order begins earlier. Orestes had Hierax, a follower of Cyril, tortured. Cyril then "sent for the principal Jews, and threatened them with the utmost severities". Only after that did some plot the night attack (SOC 19425–19436). The outline's version makes Cyril purely reactive. Correction: in beat 2, before the ambush, add one clause: "Cyril threatened the Jewish leaders with the harshest measures; some of them then plotted a night attack…". Extend the Do not say RIGHT line to match.

**M8. "The gold of iniquity" names a different John.** At BH 15800–15817, "he who sold the truth, that is John" is Count John, the imperial treasurer sent to Ephesus in 431, not John of Antioch. Nestorius says Cyril "[did] more than he… when the gold of iniquity was exacted". That is a charge that court officials were bought, not a claim that "Cyril won" outright. Correction: in beat 5, change "Nestorius later wrote that Cyril won with 'the gold of iniquity'" to "Nestorius later wrote of officials bought with 'the gold of iniquity', and laid it on Cyril". Add to Do not say: "WRONG linking this John to John of Antioch."

**M9. The palace officials were not Pulcheria's.** In beat 5, "Pulcheria, and her chamberlains and ladies": the letter names Pulcheria, the *praepositus* Paul, the chamberlain Romanus, the lady chamberlain Marcella, and Droseria (MAN5 97149–97155). It does not say they served her. Correction: "the emperor's sister, Pulcheria, and the palace chamberlains, men and women".

**M10. A quote slip in Connection.** Connection says "Severus judged cases by his letters 'as a law of the church'". Severus's words are "every utterance of whom one would say is a law of the church", and Q3's rendering says "is a law of the church". Correction: "Severus settled a question of church rule from Cyril's letter, every word of which, he said, was 'a law of the church'".
