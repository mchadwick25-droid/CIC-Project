## Loose end: the Sibling line's "the impious Cyril"

FIX. The Sibling line (Outline 42) lists "the impious Cyril" among the Nestorius story's "witness phrases". It does not say whose words they are, so a writer could take them as Nestorius's. The phrase is the Syriac translator's, in his preface to Nestorius's last book (BH 2287–2288: "[a book] which he sets as a judge between him|self| and the impious Cyril"). It is the only place it occurs in BH. "As though it were some terrible phantom" is Socrates's (SOC 20756).

- **Replace** `nor its witness phrases "the impious Cyril", "as though it were some terrible phantom")` **with** `nor the witness phrases that story uses: the Syriac translator's "the impious Cyril", from his preface to Nestorius's last book, not Nestorius's own words (BH 2288), and Socrates's "as though it were some terrible phantom")`.
