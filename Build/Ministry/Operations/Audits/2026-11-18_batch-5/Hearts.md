# The hearts, as the researchers set them (for Mark)

## Patrick
Patrick was a Briton from a Roman family, carried off to Ireland as a slave at about sixteen (Documented, his own words). He escaped after six years, and later went back of his own will as a bishop, to the people who had enslaved him (Documented). Almost everything known of him comes from his two short Latin writings ("only voice" Widely Accepted).
Also told: his work as he tells it, "many thousands" baptized, clergy ordained, chiefs' children taking vows (Documented as his claim; Christians were already in Ireland in 431). What it cost: fourteen days in irons, payments to kings and judges, his elders against him, a sin of his youth used against him (Documented). The raid by Coroticus's soldiers on new converts, and his Letter against them; the outcome unknown (Documented). His writings copied at Armagh, and a Bangor hymn calling him "teacher of the Irish" (Documented; the hymn's date Contested).
Missionary. About 2:45 (about 410 words). Opening: his own first line, "I am Patrick, a sinner." Death year uncertain (457, 461, 491 or 492 in the annals).

## Athanasius of Alexandria
Bishop of Alexandria for 45 years (328 to 373), he held to Nicaea's creed against Arius's party (Documented). He left his city five times, by order or in flight (each Documented; the count of five Widely Accepted). He wrote the Life of Antony for monks abroad (that it is his, Widely Accepted). His Easter letter of 367 listed the books of Scripture, the 27 of the New Testament among them (Documented).
Also told: the exiles with their force on both sides: in 335 a Melitian letter says his followers, with soldiers, beat their monks bloody; in 356 soldiers broke into his church, and searchers tortured a woman to find him (Documented, each credited). He would treat as brothers those who doubted only Nicaea's one word, "of one being" (Documented). The Life went into Latin and Syriac (Documented). Greek church law in 692 kept "the Canons of Athanasius", the list among them (Widely Accepted, from the editors); a 1921 Anglican proposal to the Orthodox named the canon "as defined by St. Athanasius" (Documented).
Founder or leader. About 3:00 (450 words). Opening: the night of 356 in his own words, sitting on the bishop's chair while the psalm is read. Gregory of Nazianzus counts three banishments, not five. No held source calls the list "first" or binding.
Sibling question: the Marcella story already tells that she heard of Antony from the exiles in Rome; the researcher kept it to one optional clause.

## John Calvin
A French exile who wrote the Institutes to defend France's persecuted Protestants (Documented). In 1536, passing through Geneva for one night, he was held there by Farel's curse (his own account, written 21 years later). He gave Geneva a written church order of pastors, teachers, elders and deacons, with a consistory (Documented). Churches in France and Scotland built on that order, and his name became the label for the whole family (Documented).
Also told: the Institutes began as a short handbook in 1536 and grew over later editions (Documented). Expelled in 1538, recalled in 1541; the city councils amended his Ordinances and fought the consistory until 1555 (Documented). The force: a man jailed, a man beheaded, and Servetus burned, with Calvin wanting his death (Documented). Paris 1555, the secret French synod of 1559, and Scotland's 1562 ruling to follow "the Kirk of Geneva" (Documented).
Founder or leader. About 3:00 (430 to 470 words). Opening: the one-night stop and Farel's curse. Not held: the full Psalms preface, Beza's Life of Calvin, Calvin's Latin letters of 1540 to 1541. Whether Calvin wrote the French Confession is disputed (Kidd against Schaff), so it stays off the audio.

## Nestorius
In 428 Nestorius became bishop of Constantinople. He taught that in Christ, God and the man born of Mary stay two natures in one Christ, given one worship, so he would not simply say "Mother of God", and offered "Mother of Christ" (Documented, his own words; Socrates says he rejected the name outright). Cyril of Alexandria and the council at Ephesus in 431 judged that this split Christ in two, and deposed him; he denied it and died in exile (deposition Documented; what he really taught, Contested).
Also told: his teaching in his own sermon image (Documented). Why he lost his see: the heresy hunting Socrates reports, a quarrel over Mary's name that split the city, a council led by his enemy (Documented). His exile letters (Documented), and his late defense written under another man's name (Documented as the translator's account). The Church of the East kept that book in Syriac after the empire ordered it burned (Widely Accepted), named him in its prayers and refused to curse him, while saying its faith was not his invention (Documented as its own texts).
Teacher or thinker. About 3:00. Opening: the book's borrowed name. The Syriac of the book is not held; two renderings come from the English, checked against the French. Narsai's homily on the three teachers is not held.
Sibling question: Babai's line naming Nestorius among the "three Doctors": with this story or with Babai's?

## John Wesley
An Oxford priest of the Church of England, on 24 May 1738 he felt that he trusted Christ alone and was forgiven (Documented, two witnesses). From 1739 he preached in the open air to crowds in the thousands (Documented). He gathered those who answered into societies, and the societies into weekly classes of about twelve, which became the frame of the Methodist churches (Documented).
Also told: the bishop of Bristol told him to leave his diocese (Documented as Wesley's own record, kept by a later biographer). The Wednesbury mob, and members put out of the societies (Documented). The class began in 1742 as a penny a week to pay a debt (Documented). Lay preachers allowed "only in cases of necessity" (Documented). He ordained men for America in 1784 (Documented). More than 130,000 members in 1791 (Documented).
Founder or leader. About 3:00. Opening: Aldersgate. Not held: Wesley's "Thoughts upon Methodism" and Moore's Life.
Sibling question: "strangely warmed" is already quoted in the Methodist Revival's live story. The researcher used other words ("I felt that I did trust in Christ"); may the phrase also stand here?

## Augustine of Hippo
A teacher of rhetoric who turned to faith in Milan and became bishop of Hippo in North Africa. There he wrote the Confessions and the City of God, and taught that God's grace comes first: even the start of faith is God's gift (Documented, his own words and Possidius, his friend).
Also told: seized by the crowd at Hippo and made a priest while he wept (Possidius). The old bishop, Greek by birth, let him preach. The Confessions as a book spoken to God; the City of God written after the Goths sacked Rome. Early as bishop he changed his own mind on grace. Councils, two bishops of Rome and the emperor's laws condemned the Pelagians (Possidius's account, Documented). The Vandals' siege at his death. Later, Orange in 529, Luther, Calvin and Jansen each carried the teaching on, on opposite sides (each Documented; that Orange's chapters came from his writings is its editors' finding, Widely Accepted).
Teacher or thinker. About 3:00 (about 450 words). Opening: the speech praising the emperor, full of lies, and the cheerful beggar he envied (Confessions VI.6).
Sibling question: Luther and Calvin are in this batch. Should their stories also tell what they took from Augustine, or does that stay here?
