# Outline: Nestorius

Sheet = `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder; tags (S1, E5, I3, D2 …) point to its entries, which give file and lines. His own words survive in three ways: sermon fragments quoted by his opponents, letters quoted by a hostile later historian, and one late book kept only in Syriac.

- **The essence:** Nestorius was bishop of Constantinople, the empire's capital, from 428. He taught that Christ is both God and man, and that the two stay distinct, joined in one Christ whom Christians worship as one. So he would not simply call Mary "Mother of God", the one who gave birth to God, and offered "Mother of Christ" instead (E4, E5: Documented in his own words; Socrates says he rejected the name outright, E3). Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, and a council of bishops at Ephesus in 431 judged that he split Christ in two, and removed him from office; he denied it, and died in exile, his last known years spent in the Egyptian desert (E9, E12: Documented; the place and date of death unknown, D4; what he really taught is Contested, D2).
- **Also told:** (1) **What he taught**, in his own image: he honours the man who is seen for the sake of the God hidden in him; he keeps God and man distinct but gives the one Christ one worship (E5); as God Christ cannot suffer, as man he can (E6). (2) **Why it cost him his post as bishop:** he came in demanding the earth "purged of heretics", people the church judged to teach falsely, and took churches from rival Christian groups (E1, E2: Documented as Socrates's report); a quarrel over Mary's name split the city (E3, E4); and a council led by his enemy removed him (E9: Documented). Socrates, a historian of the city who read his sermons, says he did not deny Christ was God but feared the word "as though it were some terrible phantom" (E8: Documented as Socrates's view). (3) **His late defense:** in exile, hurt and moved from place to place (E12, his own letters: Documented), he wrote a book he set as a judge between himself and Cyril, each side speaking in its own defense (the translator's account), under another man's name because his own was hated (E13, E14: Documented as the translator's account). In it he is willing to be cut off under the church's curse, if only they speak rightly of God, and he praises Leo, bishop of Rome (E15, E16: Documented). (4) **How it was carried east:** the emperor ordered his writings burned (E11: Documented, the edict itself, CTh XVI.5.66; the year 435, the editors', BH 268–271). The Church of the East, the church of Persia beyond the Roman empire, later kept the book in Syriac, its own church language (I1: Widely Accepted; date of translation Contested, D3). It named him in its prayers as a teacher who suffered for the faith (I2: Documented as their texts), and refused to cut him off. Yet its leaders insisted its faith was not his invention: "Nestorius, then, followed them, and not they him" (I3: Documented as the Church of the East's own account).
- **Length:** about 3 minutes 15 (about 490 words); the first cuts are beat 1's optional clause, beat 4's optional E10 and What came 4. The job has three parts that each need room (the teaching, the fall, the book and its journey east), the record is rich in his own words, and the carrying-east runs from Narsai to 1298. The Ephesus council itself is one sentence, since its sibling tells it; Cyril's side is the Cyril sibling's.
- **Setting:** Century: the fifth; bishop 10 April 428 (S1, S4); deposed June 431; edict ordering his writings burned 435 (editors, BH 268–271; the edict itself, CTh XVI.5.66: Documented); banished 436 (editors, S4); died in exile, about 451 (D4). Place: a native of Germanicia, a monk-priest at Antioch, brought to Constantinople (S1, S2; the priesthood, Gennadius, npnf203 43121–43123); exile at the Oasis in Upper Egypt, then Panopolis and toward Elephantine (E12). Tradition: told within **the church of Antioch** and its teachers (census "Antiochene Exegetical Christianity", short name "Antioch"); carried on by **the Church of the East**, in Persia, outside the Roman empire, which spoke Syriac (S3). Spoken words: "Constantinople", "Antioch", "the Egyptian desert", "the Church of the East".
- **Words to explain** (each glossed in the sentence where it is first heard; or use the plain word given):
  - **bishop** — the leader of a city's churches. Keep the word; Constantinople's bishop led the capital's church.
  - **"his see" / "the see of Constantinople"** — say "his post as bishop" instead.
  - **council** — a meeting of bishops called to settle teaching. Here the emperor called it (Ephesus sibling).
  - **deposed** — say "removed from office" (E9).
  - **heretics** — people the church judged to teach falsely (E1). Socrates's word, not the narrator's.
  - **Arians** — Christians who followed Arius, who taught that the Son of God was made, and not eternal; his teaching was condemned at the Council of Nicaea in 325 (census Arius entry, `teaser`).
  - **Macedonians, Novatians** — say "rival Christian groups"; do not name them.
  - **monk / monastery** — a man vowed to prayer, living with others in a house of monks (S2).
  - **Mother of God (Greek "Theotokos")** — "the one who gave birth to God"; a title many already gave Mary (E3).
  - **Mother of Christ** — the title Nestorius says he offered, taken from the Gospel's own words (E4).
  - **natures** — what Christ is as God, and what he is as man (E5, E6). Not "nature" in the modern sense of the natural world.
  - **creature / Creator (beat 3)**: say 'a created being cannot give birth to her own Creator; Mary gave birth to a man, the instrument of God' (MM 59091–59095). Do not say 'the creature'.
  - **God the Word** — the Son of God, as John's Gospel names him; say "the Son of God" if room is short.
  - **worship (in Q1)** — the bow of worship Christians give to Christ (Greek "proskynesis", LF 14312).
  - **blasphemy / blasphemous** — insulting God; the council's charge against his sermons (E9).
  - **communion ("as communion broke apart", beat 4)** — say "as the churches broke fellowship"; "communion" here means sharing the bread and wine of the church's service, the sign of being one church.
  - **anathema (Q2)** — cut off from the church under its solemn curse (E15).
  - **edict** — say "the emperor's law" (E11).
  - **exile** — keep; forced to live far away under guard (E12).
  - **Syriac** — the Aramaic language the Church of the East used for worship and books (I1).
  - **the Church of the East** — the church of Persia, beyond the Roman empire, with its own head (S3).
  - **catholicos** — say "the head of the Church of the East" (SYN 32697: "catholicos, patriarche de l'Orient").
  - **patriarch ("Nestorius was not their Patriarch")** — the head bishop over a whole church (I3); here, their church's head.
  - **metropolitan** — say "the chief bishop of Nisibis" (BAD 18317: "Metropolitan of Nisibis").
  - **Mar** — a Syriac title, "my lord", given to saints and bishops (NAR 6169; BAD 16360).
  - **liturgy** — the set prayers of the church's service of holy communion (BAD 1554–1562; NAR 6168).
  - **rites** — the church's set services, here marriage and the making of bishops and clergy (BAD 12305, 16357).
  - **martyr ("the persecuted martyr")** — here one who suffered for the faith; no source says he was put to death. The modern sense, someone put to death, would mislead (BAD 16360; E12).
  - **bishop of Rome** — say so for Leo; avoid "pope" (E16).
  - **Upper Egypt** (beat 5): say "southern Egypt". "Upper" misleads.
  - **the governor of the Thebaid** (Names, beat 5): say "the governor of southern Egypt".
  - **litter** (What came 4): "a bed carried by bearers" (BB 36027, "litière").
  - **clergy** (beat 1): "the city's priests and deacons".
  - **the resurrection**: the rising of the dead at the end of time, as Christians believe.

**Openings**

1. **(Chosen.)** The book with a borrowed name (E14, Q3). A book about Christ came down to us under the name of Heracleides, a man who lived near Damascus. Its Syriac translator explains: the writer took this man's name because his own was hated, and he feared no one would read it (Q3). The writer was Nestorius, once bishop of Constantinople. It leads straight into the essence: whose name, and why it was hated.
2. (Alternative.) His sermon (E5, Q1): a bishop in Constantinople tells his people he separates the natures but unites the worship; within three years he was deposed for it.

**Story beats** (the opening, the essence and beat 1 fall in the first minute, about 150 words)

1. **A preacher from Antioch** (first minute, briefly). Socrates, a church historian of the city writing in his lifetime, says that in 428 the court, wanting no one from the city's quarrelling clergy, sent for a stranger from Antioch, a native of Germanicia, prized for his voice (S1). Socrates says that in his first sermon he asked the emperor for "the earth purged of heretics", and promised heaven in return; within days he set out to pull down a chapel of the Arians, who set fire to it themselves, and he took churches from other groups (E1, E2). Optional clause: Socrates adds that he caused a riot in which many died at Miletus and Sardis, and passes over it (E2). Documented as Socrates's report. "Arians" = Christians who followed Arius, who taught that the Son of God was made, and not eternal (census Arius entry).
2. **A name for Mary.** "Theotokos", "Mother of God", was the name many used. Socrates says a priest Nestorius had brought from Antioch preached against it, and Nestorius backed him and rejected the name (E3). Nestorius, in his later book, says he found two parties at his door, one saying "Mother of God", the other "Mother of man", both believing the same; he offered "Mother of Christ", a word from the Gospel that both could share (E4). The witnesses disagree; tell both, credited (D1). Documented as each one's claim.
3. **What he taught.** In a sermon kept by his opponents he said the creature did not bear the Creator, but bore a man, the instrument of God (E5, Mercator's Latin), and spoke Q1. As he put it later, in exile, in his last book: as God, Christ cannot suffer or die; as man, he does; he is not God in both natures, nor man in both (E6). "Natures" = what Christ is as God and what he is as man. Documented in his own words. His opponents heard two Christs; Socrates, who read his sermons, says he did not deny Christ was God, but feared the word "Theotokos" "as though it were some terrible phantom" (E8). Documented as Socrates's view.
4. **Deposed** (one sentence; the sibling tells the council). At Ephesus in 431 a council led by Cyril of Alexandria judged his sermons blasphemous and deposed him, while his friends met apart and deposed Cyril (E9). Optional, in Socrates's voice only: Socrates says that as communion broke apart he cried, "Let Mary be called Theotokos, if you will," but no one took notice (E10; one witness, D5). He was sent back to his monastery near Antioch (S2, E11). Documented.
5. **Exile.** In 435 an imperial edict ordered his writings burned (E11: the edict itself, CTh XVI.5.66; the year, the editors', BH 268–271); the next year he was banished to the Oasis in Upper Egypt (E11). Desert raiders sacked it, took him captive, then let him go; he went to Panopolis and showed himself in public, writing to the governor, so that no one could say he had fled; soldiers dragged him toward Elephantine, then back, old and sick, with "a mangled hand and side" (E12, his own letters, kept by Evagrius, a historian a century and a half later who saw his sufferings as God's punishment). Documented.
6. **The late defense.** Near the end of his life, perhaps in 451 (E13: Dominant Modern Reconstruction), he wrote his defense in Greek, under Heracleides's name; its Syriac translator says he set the book as a judge between himself and Cyril, each speaking in his own defense (E13, E14; the translator calls Cyril "the impious Cyril"). He says it was not for his own sake: 'who is Nestorius?' (E16). He hails Leo, bishop of Rome, as a preacher who taught the truth without fear, though he says Leo agreed to his condemnation unheard (E16). And he offers his own name to the curse: Q2 (E15). Documented in his own words.
7. **His death** (end, not told early). He bids the desert rejoice for him, and calls the land of his exile his mother, which will guard his body after death until the resurrection (E17). When and how he died is not known; about 451 is a modern reckoning (D4).

**What came of them** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **His book went east** (what the Church of the East kept). The empire ordered his writings burned (E11; BH 268–271 and the edict itself, CTh XVI.5.66: Documented); his Greek is lost. The Church of the East, in Persia, put the book into Syriac, perhaps in the sixth century (I1, D3). One damaged copy survived, in its patriarch's library in Kurdistan; Western scholars noticed it in the 1890s, and it was printed in 1910 (I1). Widely Accepted (editors). The 1843 massacre and the place name Kotchanes: card, or one clause.
2. **His name in its prayers.** Narsai, a teacher of the Church of the East at Nisibis, writing within about two generations, says the priest prays "imitating Mar Nestorius" (Widely Accepted as Narsai's (Connolly); the manuscript reads 'Nestorius', which its first editor changed: card); a liturgy that bears his name was directed to be said five times a year, an English visitor reported in 1852; its rites name him "the persecuted martyr" (I2). Documented as their texts.
3. **Not their founder** (defended by the Church of the East: the faith in two natures, and his name). Around 680 its catholicos wrote that his faith was "not the invention" of Nestorius and Theodore, whose names "scandalize the simple" (I3). In 1298 a metropolitan of Nisibis wrote that they were wrongly called "Nestorians": Nestorius was not their patriarch, and they did not speak his language; they had held the faith in two natures already, and "Nestorius, then, followed them, and not they him"; they refused to cut him off (I3). Documented as their own account.
4. **Two memories of his end.** In the empire, Evagrius heard from a writer that worms ate his tongue before he died (I5, hostile hearsay). In the East, Barhadbeshabba, around 600, told his exile as a saint's life: called back, he answered that the desert pleased him, and he died on the litter that carried him (I4, legend). Optional. If used, tell it inside beat 7, after his own last lines and the plain 'when and how he died is not known', each in its witness's voice. Otherwise card.

Only let them say of God what I beg them to say." The narrator gives "anathema" its plain sense: cut off, under the church's curse. **Connection to carry into the story:** What passed east was his book and his name. The empire ordered his writings burned in 435 (E11), and his Greek is lost. His last book, written after that, survives only because the Church of the East later put it into Syriac, perhaps in the sixth century, and kept one copy (I1, D3, Q3). It named him in its prayers and rites as a teacher and a persecuted martyr (I2), and refused to cut him off (I3). The listener must be told what the label hides, in the Church of the East's own words: it says it did not take its faith from him and was never under him. Its metropolitan of Nisibis wrote that he had never been its patriarch and that its people did not understand his language. They say it already held the faith in two natures in Christ, and honoured him as one who taught and suffered for it: "Nestorius followed them, and not they him," as its metropolitan of Nisibis put it in 1298 (I3). The name "Nestorian" was fixed on it by its opponents (census, Church of the East). Carry this through what they did (translated, prayed, refused, wrote), not a "this matters because" sentence.
