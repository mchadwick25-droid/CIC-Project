# Outline check: Nestorius

## My own heart (written before reading Outline.md)

From the census entry (`why`, `legacy`, `floorNote`, `relationsSummary`, `voices`) and the source sheet.

**Essence.** Nestorius, a famous preacher from Antioch, was made bishop of Constantinople in 428. Within three years his refusal to call Mary "Mother of God" (Theotokos), and his teaching that Christ's divine and human natures stay distinct, set him against Cyril of Alexandria; the council at Ephesus in 431 deposed him, and he died in exile in Egypt, about 451. He is on the tree because the church that came to bear his name, the Church of the East, kept his own defense when the empire burned his books, and honoured him as one of its three teachers, while saying its faith was not his invention.

**Also told.**
- A zealous, harsh start: the first sermon ("Give me the earth purged of heretics"), the Arian chapel the Arians themselves set on fire, the pressure on other groups (Socrates, contemporary, hostile on this point).
- The quarrel over Mary's title told two ways: Socrates (he backed his priest Anastasius and rejected the word outright) against his own account (he found two factions and offered "Mother of Christ" as a word for agreement).
- What he taught, in his own words: two natures, one Christ, one worship ("I separate the natures, but I unite the worship"); Cyril's charge that he split Christ in two; his denial; Socrates' verdict that he did not make Christ a mere man but was "scared" of the word. The question is still argued (Contested).
- Deposition at Ephesus (told by the sibling), return to his monastery near Antioch, the edict of 435 ordering his writings burned, banishment to the Egyptian desert; raiders, forced marches, his letters of complaint (kept by a hostile later historian).
- The late book written in exile under another man's name (the Bazaar of Heracleides), so that readers would not be put off by his own; "let Nestorius be anathema" so long as God is rightly praised; praise for Leo of Rome; the closing farewell to the desert.
- What came of him: the Syriac translation (date uncertain) and one damaged copy at Kotchanes, harmed in 1843, noticed by Western scholars in the 1890s and printed in 1910; his name in the Church of the East's liturgy and rites, one of the "three Doctors"; that church's own insistence that it was "unjustly styled Nestorian"; in the empire, condemned and cursed.
- Death: when and how unknown; about 451 is a modern reckoning.

Shape I would expect: Teacher or thinker (or Martyr, as the Church of the East remembers him); the story's point lies in the book that survived and the church that kept it.

## Verdict: FIX

The heart is right. It matches my own account and the heart Mark approved in plain words on 2026-11-18 (`Hearts_Plain.md`). It also holds the launch job as set: what he taught about Christ and Mary, why it cost him his post, and how his name and his late defense were carried east. The three Words-to-explain rulings that touch this story are kept: every church word is glossed, Doctors is not needed, and Leo is "bishop of Rome". Babai's "three Doctors" line is out of this story and of Babai's own outline, as ruled. The quotes hold verbatim and the renderings are sound. The order is right: the opening reaches ahead, then the life runs 428, 431, 435–436, the late book, death. Three faults block: a witness phrase given to the wrong speaker, a misleading time link in the Connection, and one gloss the listener needs.

### Heart comparison

The outline's essence and Also told match mine in substance. My account put the Church of the East's keeping of the book into the essence, as the reason he is on the tree. The outline puts it in Also told (4), What came 1–3 and the Connection, and Mark approved it that way. That leaves out nothing major, so there is no finding.

## BLOCKING

**B1. "The impious Cyril" is the Syriac translator's phrase, not Nestorius's.** The phrase appears once in the whole file, in the translator's preface (BH 2287–2289: "[a book] which he sets as a judge between him|self| and the impious Cyril"; NAU 1462: "entre lui et l'impie Cyrille"). A search of BH turns up no other place where Nestorius uses it. Yet beat 6 puts it inside Nestorius's own defense, and Do not say gives it to him ("RIGHT … 'in his book Nestorius calls him "the impious Cyril"'"). Credit for a hostile phrase is exactly what the kit says must be right.
- Beat 6. Replace "setting himself and "the impious Cyril" before a judge, under Heracleides's name (E13, E14)" with: "under Heracleides's name; its Syriac translator says he set the book as a judge between himself and Cyril, each speaking in his own defense (E13, E14; the translator calls Cyril "the impious Cyril")".
- Also told (3). Replace "a book setting himself and Cyril before a judge" with "a book he set as a judge between himself and Cyril, each side speaking in its own defense (the translator's account)". This also fixes a smaller drift: in the source the book is the judge. The two men are not set before a judge.
- Do not say, the Cyril line. RIGHT becomes: "if told at all, credit the book's Syriac translator, who calls him 'the impious Cyril'. Nestorius's own charges against Cyril belong to the Cyril and Ephesus siblings."
- Quotes preamble. After "the impious Cyril", add: "(the Syriac translator's phrase, BH 2288, not Nestorius's)".
- Outside this check: the Cyril outline's Sibling line lists "the impious Cyril" as one of Nestorius's witness phrases. Its own checker should note that this is the translator's phrase.

**B2. The Connection makes the Syriac translation sound like a reply to the burning.** It reads: "When the empire ordered his writings burned (E11), the Church of the East … put his last book into Syriac and kept it." But the edict came in August 435 (BH 268–271). The book was written after 450, "perhaps in 451" (BH 314–316). The translation is put about 535, or between 525 and 533, or after 638 (D3; BH 330–333, 372–373, 403–411). The "When" invents both a timing and a motive. A writer who carries it ("to save it from the flames, the Church of the East…") states something no source holds. Also told (4) and What came 1 set the two facts side by side the same way.
- Connection, first two sentences. Replace them with: "What passed east was his book and his name. The empire ordered his writings burned in 435 (E11), and his Greek is lost. His last book, written after that, survives only because the Church of the East later put it into Syriac, perhaps in the sixth century, and kept one copy (I1, D3, Q3)."
- Do not say. Add: "WRONG 'to save it from the fire, the Church of the East translated it', 'the Church of the East rescued his book from the burning', or any wording that makes the translation a reply to the edict. RIGHT 'the empire ordered his writings burned in 435; the book he wrote later survives because the Church of the East, perhaps a century on, put it into Syriac' (E11, E13, D3)."

**B3. "The creature did not bear the Creator" (beat 3) has no gloss.** This is the beat that carries his teaching, and a writer will likely take its wording as given. Heard once, "the creature" sounds like an animal or a monster, not Mary as a created human being (MM 59091–59095: "Non peperit creatura Creatorem, sed peperit hominem Deitatis instrumentum").
- Words to explain. Add: "**creature / Creator (beat 3)**: say 'a created being cannot give birth to her own Creator; Mary gave birth to a man, the instrument of God' (MM 59091–59095). Do not say 'the creature'."
- Add "creature" to the Do not say ruling line's list of words.

## MINOR

**M1. The edict should rest on the primary text, which is held.** E11 and the Setting rest the edict of 435 on the editors (BH 268–271). The edict itself is in `cic/texts/theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt`, lines 88695–88725 (Codex Theodosianus XVI.5.66, dated in Theodosius's fifteenth consulship, 435). It orders his "impios libros" to be hunted down "ac publice conburi", publicly burned, and bans his followers' meetings. Cite it beside BH 268–271 in Setting, E11 and What came 1, and raise "ordered his writings burned" to Documented. In Leave out, the "Simonians" nickname is also in this edict ("Nestorii Simoniani vocentur"), not "historian only". Change that line to: "(in the edict itself, CTh XVI.5.66; a side issue)".

**M2. Library sweep: some held witnesses are not accounted for.** A count of name hits across `cic/texts/` shows these files with no line in Searched, Used or Set aside:
- Liberatus's *Breviarium causae Nestorianorum*, a sixth-century account of the whole affair (`liberatus-victor-tunnunensis-primasius-junillus-pl68-lat_migne1866.txt`, 58 hits).
- Facundus, *Pro defensione trium capitulorum* (`facundus-ferrandus-caesarius-dionysius-pl67-lat_migne1865.txt`, 328 hits).
- Gennadius, *Lives of Illustrious Men* LIV, "Nestorius the heresiarch" (`npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` 43115–43130).
- Theodoret's letters to and about Nestorius (same file, 37969–38145).
- Zachariah Rhetor's chronicle and Leo's letters (`npnf212`).
- Nau's three appendices: the Syriac letter to Cosmas on his deposition and wonders, and Nestorius's own letter to the people of Constantinople (NAU from 19027 and 19540).

None of these changes the heart. Add each to Set aside with its reason: later and hostile, or a sibling's material, or legend. Gennadius also supports the Setting's "monk-priest" (M3).

**M3. Setting: "a monk-priest at Antioch" is not in the cited lines.** S1 and S2 give a stranger from Antioch and his own monastery outside the city (EV 1527–1534). The priesthood comes from Gennadius: "while presbyter of the church at Antioch, … a remarkable extemporaneous teacher" (npnf203 43121–43123). Cite it there, or say "from a monastery near Antioch".

**M4. Ending: he does not "ask" the desert to guard his body.** BH 20701–20732 and NAU 17665–17667 read: "Rejoice for me, O desert, … and my mother [the land of] my exile, who even after my death will guard my body unto the resurrection." Change Ending (1) and beat 7 to: "he bids the desert rejoice for him, and calls the land of his exile his mother, which will guard his body after death until the resurrection." Add a gloss: "**the resurrection**: the rising of the dead at the end of time, as Christians believe."

**M5. Beat 6: "He calls himself nothing" says more than the source.** In BH 20465–20468 he says God brought these things about not on his account: "For who is Nestorius? Or what is his life?" Replace it with: "He says it was not for his own sake: 'who is Nestorius?' (E16)". Also drop "(paraphrase)", or say plainly that those words, used at all, count against the three quotes.

**M6. Beat 3 reaches ahead to the last book.** E6 ("as God, Christ cannot suffer or die …") is from the late book, which beat 6 tells. Either move E6 to beat 6, or mark it in beat 3 as "as he put it later, in exile" so that the book is not introduced twice as an event.

**M7. What came 4 and the ending.** Both of its stories, the worms and the litter, are about his death. If What came 4 is told before the chosen ending, the death is told early. If it is told after, the story does not end on him. Fix: "Optional. If used, tell it inside beat 7, after his own last lines and the plain 'when and how he died is not known', each in its witness's voice. Otherwise card."

**M8. What came 3, Abdisho's "two Persons".** Abdisho's own sentence is "the doctrine of the two Natures and two Persons, one Son of God, one Christ" (BAD 19152–19154). The outline paraphrases this as "the faith in two natures", which is acceptable. Add to Do not say: "WRONG putting 'two persons' in the narrator's voice as the Church of the East's or Nestorius's teaching. RIGHT 'the faith in two natures'; if Abdisho's wording is used, credit it to him ('two Natures and two Persons, one Christ', Badger's English)." Also, in What came 3, Also told (4) and the Connection, change "asked to curse him, they refused" and "refused to curse him" to "refused to cut him off" (BAD 19158: "when called upon to excommunicate him, they refused"). In the Connection, change "Its writers say he had never been its patriarch" to "Its metropolitan of Nisibis wrote that he had never been its patriarch". Only Abdisho says this.

**M9. Narsai.** The Library holds the homily as Narsai's only by Connolly's judgement (NAR 2414–2416: "little doubt", after the doubts at 426–435). Its Syriac editor printed "Barsamya" where the manuscript had "Nestorius" (NAR 6194–6197: "'Nestorius' was in the codex, and we have altered it"). In What came 2, add: "Widely Accepted as Narsai's (Connolly); the manuscript reads 'Nestorius', which its first editor changed: card." Change "the Church of the East's great teacher" to "a teacher of the Church of the East at Nisibis". "Great" is the narrator's verdict.

**M10. Passing words missing from Words to explain.**
- **Upper Egypt** (beat 5): say "southern Egypt". "Upper" misleads.
- **the governor of the Thebaid** (Names, beat 5): say "the governor of southern Egypt".
- **litter** (What came 4): "a bed carried by bearers" (BB 36027, "litière").
- **clergy** (beat 1): "the city's priests and deacons".
- **communion**: the gloss defines it with "the sacrament". Say "sharing the bread and wine of the church's service, the sign of being one church".
- **rites**: BAD 16357 is the service for ordaining bishops and clergy (header "The Ordering of Bishops"). Change "the making of priests" to "the making of bishops and clergy".
- **martyr**: change "he was not killed for it" to "no source says he was put to death". The record of his death is uncertain (D4).
- **Arians**: the gloss has no source line. Add one, such as the census Arius entry, or Athanasius's heart for this batch ("Arius's party, who taught that the Son had a beginning").

**M11. Beat 5: "he went to the governor at Panopolis".** He went to Panopolis, showed himself in public and wrote to the governor (EV 1617–1623). Change this to "he went to Panopolis and showed himself in public, writing to the governor, so that no one could say he had fled".

**M12. What came 1: "in the mountains of Kurdistan".** The source says "at Kotchanes, in Kurdistan" (BH 374–375). Drop "mountains".

**M13. Do not say: Heracleides.** Add: "WRONG 'his friend Heracleides', 'Heracleides wrote it', or a pen name made up from nothing. RIGHT the translator says Heracleides was a real man near Damascus, 'honoured for his conduct and esteemed for his knowledge', known for 'the justice of his words', and the writer borrowed his name (BH 2276–2287)."

**M14. Length.** With seven beats, four What-came items, the Connection and about two dozen glosses to work in, 450 words leaves little room for the Connection. That is the part the kit says must become understandable. Set Length to "about 3 minutes 15 (about 490 words); the first cuts are beat 1's optional clause, beat 4's optional E10 and What came 4". Do not fill to the length.
