# Source sheet: Nestorius

Told within **Antiochene Exegetical Christianity (Chrysostom-centered)** (spoken short name "Antioch"); carried on by **The Church of the East under Persia** (spoken "the Church of the East"). ## Setting

**S1. From Antioch to Constantinople, 428.** SOC 20521–20527, 20544, 20549–20556: the emperors resolved that none of the Constantinople clergy should fill the see; "They therefore sent for a stranger from Antioch, whose name was Nestorius, a native of Germanicia, distinguished for his excellent voice and fluency of speech; qualifications which they judged important for the instruction of the people." "Being ordained on the 10th of April, under the consulate of Felix and Taurus" (the editor's margin: 428 A.D.). CONTEMP (Socrates wrote in Nestorius's lifetime; HEF 8313–8314 puts Socrates's writing about 439). Documented.

**S2. His monastery at Antioch.** EV 1527–1534: after Ephesus "he was allowed to retire to his own monastery, situated without the gates of the city now called Theopolis [Antioch]... the monastery of Euprepius... not more than two stadia from that city." LATER (Evagrius, late sixth century, summarising Nestorius's own lost account). Documented as Evagrius's report.

**S3. Tradition and the eastern church.** Census: told within "Antiochene Exegetical Christianity (Chrysostom-centered)", short name "Antioch"; the Church of the East is "The Church of the East under Persia", short name "Church of the East". BH 1380–1381 (editors' chronology, 436): "Nestorianism begins to spread in the East outside the Empire, e.g. in Persia." EDITOR. Abdisho (BAD 19147–19149): the Easterns "were unjustly styled 'Nestorians,' since Nestorius was not their Patriarch, neither did they understand his language". LATER (1298).

**S4. Dates.** Bishop from 10 April 428 (S1). Deposed at Ephesus, June 431 (SOC 20944–20945: "These things were done on the 28th of June, under the consulate of Bassus and Antiochus"; the Ephesus sibling gives 22 June by Hefele's dating: leave the day out). Edict against his writings, August 435; banished 436 (BH 1370–1376, editors' chronology: "August Edict of Theodosius proscribing the writings of Nestorius and meetings of his followers"; "436. Nestorius is banished to Arabia, but actually sent to Upper Egypt"). EDITOR, Widely Accepted. Death: unknown, about 451 (D4).

## Events (what he taught and why it cost him his see)

**E1. The first sermon: "the earth purged of heretics."** SOC 20556–20564: "he immediately uttered those famous words, before all the people, in addressing the emperor, 'Give me, my prince, the earth purged of heretics, and I will give you heaven as a recompense. Assist me in destroying heretics, and I will assist you in vanquishing the Persians.'" Greek SOCG 18278–18281 (spelling normalized from the OCR, which reads "Aos" for "Δός"): "Δός μοι, φησὶν, ὦ βασιλεῦ, καθαρὰν τὴν γῆν τῶν αἱρετικῶν, κἀγώ σοι τὸν οὐρανὸν ἀντιδώσω· συγκάθελέ μοι τοὺς αἱρετικοὺς, κἀγὼ συγκαθελῶ σοι τοὺς Πέρσας." CONTEMP, reported by Socrates, who dislikes him on this point. Documented as Socrates's report.

**E2. The Arian chapel and the fire; the Macedonians.** SOC 20585–20596: "on the fifth day after his ordination, having determined to demolish a chapel in which the Arians were accustomed to perform their devotions privately, he drove these people to desperation; for when they saw the work of destruction going forward in their chapel, they threw fire into it, and the fire spreading on all sides reduced many of the adjacent buildings also to ashes." "From that time, however, they branded Nestorius as an 'incendiary,' and it was not only the heretics who did this, but those also of his own faith." SOC 20597–20601: he "annoyed the Novatians also... but the emperor by his admonitions checked his fury." SOC 20601–20604: "With what calamities he visited the Quartodecimans throughout Asia, Lydia, and Caria, and what multitudes perished in a popular tumult of which he was the cause at Miletus and Sardis, I think proper to pass by in silence." SOC 20680–20687 (VII.31): after the Macedonians killed a bishop who persecuted them, "Nestorius took occasion from it to increase his violence of conduct against them, and prevailed on the emperor to take away their churches." CONTEMP. Documented as Socrates's report. Note: the Arians set the fire, not Nestorius.

**E3. The quarrel over Mary's name: Socrates's account.** SOC 20701–20705, 20711, 20716: Nestorius's presbyter Anastasius, "whom he had brought from Antioch," preached "'Let no one call Mary Theotocos: for Mary was but a woman; and it is impossible that God should be born of a woman.'" (This line is already used in the Cyril story: do not quote it.) SOC 20733–20739: "Nestorius, eager to establish Anastasius' proposition... delivered several public discourses on the subject, in which he assumed a controversial attitude, and totally rejected the epithet Theotocos." SOC 20739–20747: the church divided, "resembled the struggle of combatants in the dark"; Nestorius "acquired the reputation among the masses of asserting the blasphemous dogma that the Lord is a mere man". CONTEMP. Documented as Socrates's report. "Theotokos" = "one who gave birth to God", "Mother of God".

**E4. The quarrel over Mary's name: Nestorius's own account.** BH 6915–6919, 6927–6948, 6955–6962 (written in exile, some twenty years later): "in Constantinople, when I found that men were inquiring and in need of being taught... factions of the people who were questioning this came together to the bishop's palace". "Those on the one hand who called the blessed Mary the mother of God they called Manichaeans, but those who named the blessed Mary the mother of a man Photinians.... But when they were questioned by me, the former denied not the humanity nor the latter the divinity, but they confessed them both alike, while they were distinct only in name". "I said that neither the latter nor the former were heretics". "'When you call her the Mother of Christ, [Christ] by union and inseparate, you speak of the one and of the other in the sonship. But make use of that against which there is no accusation in the Gospel and settle this dispute among you, making use of a word which is useful toward agreement.'" "When they heard these things, they said: 'Before God has our inquiry been solved.'" OWN (at two removes). Documented as his claim. Cross-check NAU (same passage). EV 1513–1521 summarises the same book: "one maintaining that Mary ought to be styled Mother of Man; the other, Mother of God; and he devised the title, Mother of Christ". **Where they disagree:** Socrates has Nestorius backing his priest and rejecting the name outright; Nestorius has himself settling a quarrel he found. Tell both, credited.

**E5. What he taught: two natures, one worship (his sermon).** LF 14309–14312 (Greek; the OCR is damaged, so spelling is normalized here, and the one word read differently is shown in brackets): "διὰ τὸν φοροῦντα τὸν φορούμενον σέβω, διὰ τὸν κεκρυμμένον προσκυνῶ τὸν φαινόμενον. ἀχώριστος τοῦ φαινομένου θεός· διὰ τοῦτο τοῦ μὴ χωριζομένου τὴν τιμὴν οὐ χωρίζω. χωρίζω τὰς φύσεις, ἀλλ᾽ ἑνῶ τὴν [τεροσχύνησιν = προσκύνησιν]." Loofs's apparatus (LF 14270–14280) lists many witnesses who quoted the passage, his opponents among them. Mercator's Latin of the same sermon (MM 59091–59099, Sermo I, two columns interleaved): "Non peperit creatura eum qui est increabilis"; "Non peperit creatura Creatorem, sed peperit hominem Deitatis instrumentum"; "propter absconditum adoro quod foris videtur, inseparabilis ab eo qui foris paret est Deus"; "Divido naturas, sed conjungo reverentiam." OWN, kept by opponents. Documented. HEF 1314–1316 (historian's English of the same): "I separate the natures and unite the reverence." Plain sense: in Christ, God the Word and the man born of Mary stay two natures; but the one Christ receives one worship.

**E6. What he taught, in his last book.** BH 20251–20257: "so that Christ in truth and in nature may be confessed to be the saviour and the saved, God and man, who is in nature immortal and impassible as God and mortal and passible in nature as man. He is not God in both the natures nor yet man in both the natures." OWN. Documented. Plain sense: as God he cannot suffer or die; as man he does.

**E7. "Two or three months old."** SOC 20889–20897: at Ephesus, before the council met, "When many had declared that Christ was God, Nestorius said: 'I cannot term him God who was two and three months old. I am therefore clear of your blood, and shall in future come no more among you.'" Greek SOCG 18771–18775. ACTS 18633–18636 (the council's own report): "he was bold enough to say, 'I do not confess a two or three months old God'". CONTEMP, two hostile witnesses. Documented as reported.

**E8. Socrates's verdict.** SOC 20748–20756: "Having myself perused the writings of Nestorius, I have found him an unlearned man... I cannot then concede that he was either a follower of Paul of Samosata or of Photinus, or that he denied the Divinity of Christ: but he seemed scared at the term Theotocos, as though it were some terrible phantom." SOC 20816–20821: "for that he does not assert Christ to be a mere man, as Photinus did or Paul of Samosata, his own published homilies fully demonstrate." CONTEMP. Documented as Socrates's view. A witness from neither camp: he blames the man's temper and learning, not his faith in Christ's divinity.

**E9. Deposed at Ephesus, 431 (the sibling tells it).** SOC 20900–20906: Cyril's party "summoned Nestorius: but he refused to meet them, and put them off until the arrival of John of Antioch"; they examined his sermons and "after deciding from a repeated perusal of them that they contained distinct blasphemy against the Son of God, they deposed him." SOC 20907–20909: his party deposed Cyril and Memnon. CONTEMP. Documented. Census floorNote: the charge was that "his teaching on Christ split him into two"; "In his own defense Nestorius denied it."

**E10. "Let Mary be called Theotokos" (Socrates only).** SOC 20915–20921: "Nestorius saw that the contention which had been raised was thus tending to the destruction of communion, in bitter regret he called Mary Theotocos, and cried out: 'Let Mary be called Theotocos, if you will, and let all disputing cease.' But although he made this recantation, no notice was taken of it; for his deposition was not revoked, and he was banished to the Oasis, where he still remains." Greek SOCG 18835–18837: "Λεγέσθω, φησὶ, καὶ θεοτόκος ἡ Μαρία, καὶ παυσάσθω τὰ λυπηρά." CONTEMP, one witness. Contested as an event (no other witness); "bitter regret" is Socrates's reading. If told, in Socrates's voice.

**E11. Back to Antioch, then exile.** BH 264–281 (editors): Nestorius "was bidden to return to his monastery at Antioch"; "In August 435 imperial edicts forbade the meetings of Nestorians and decreed heavy penalties against all who should copy, preserve, or read the writings of their master, which were ordered to be burned. By a rescript of the following year Nestorius himself was banished to Arabia, but he was actually sent to Egypt". EDITOR, Widely Accepted. The edict itself, CTh XVI.5.66 (`theodosianus-16_mommsen-meyer1905.txt` 88695–88735): his followers are to be called "Nestorii Simoniani"; "Nec vero impios libros nefandi et sacrilegi Nestorii ... scribtos habere aut legere aut describere quisquam audeat: quos diligenti studio requiri ac publice conburi decernimus" (no one may have, read or copy his impious books; they are to be sought out and publicly burned); his followers' meetings are banned. Dated "D. N. Theod[osio] A. XV et qui fuerit nuntiatus coss." (88733–88735), Theodosius's fifteenth consulship: 435. Mommsen's heading, as the OCR reads it, prints "436 Aug. 3" (88694). His headings for other laws of the same consulship read 435 (I.1.6, 2997 and 3023; X.8.5, OCR "485", 57751 and 57779, with the same "et qui fuerit nuntiatus"), so the heading is not cited. Documented for 435, by the edict's own date and the editors (BH 270). The year 436 is the banishment's (BH 273–274; NAU 19061–19062). Documented that the emperor ordered his writings burned. EV 1538–1541: "during a residence there of four years, he received every mark of respect and distinction; and that, by a second edict of Theodosius, he is banished to the place called Oasis." EV 1544–1547 (Evagrius's own charge): "in his retirement he did not cease from his peculiar blasphemy; so that John, the president of the church of Antioch, was led to report the circumstance". LATER. HEF 8320–8323 (historian): the emperor "ordered all the writings of Nestorius to be burnt". EDITOR.

**E12. The Oasis, the raiders, and the forced marches (his own letters).** EV 1600–1609, Nestorius to the governor of the Thebaid: "Oasis, further called Ibis, has been appointed as the place of my residence by an imperial decree." "Inasmuch as the beforementioned place has fallen into the hands of the barbarians, and been reduced to utter desolation by fire and sword, and I, by a most unexpected act of compassion, have been liberated by them". EV 1617–1635: at Panopolis he "shewed myself in public, for fear lest any one... should raise a charge against me... of escaping"; "I entreat your highness to... not sacrifice a prisoner of war to the malice and evil designs of men: lest there should hence arise this melancholy story with all posterity, that it is better to be made captive by barbarians, than to fly for refuge to the protection of the Roman sovereignty." EV 1651–1672 (second letter): "I was conducted by barbarous soldiers from Panopolis to Elephantine... and when, sorely shattered, I had accomplished the greater part of the journey, I am encountered by an unwritten order... to return to Panopolis. Thus, miserably worn with the casualties of the road, with a body afflicted by disease and age, and a mangled hand and side..."; "another merciless order was suddenly issued for a fourth deportation." OWN, quoted by a hostile later writer (Evagrius, late sixth century, who calls this God's "vengeance", EV 1554–1558). Documented. Evagrius's summary names the captors "the Blemmyes" (EV 1564); the letter itself says "barbarians" and "Nomades".

**E13. The late defense: the book.** BH 314–327 (editors citing Bethune-Baker): "The book must have been written by Nestorius in the year 451 or 452, seeing that there are references to the death of Theodosius II in 450, and to the flight of Dioscorus of Alexandria"; Nestorius, "though speaking of the triumph of the orthodox faith of Flavian and Leo, does not seem to be aware of the formal decisions of the Council of Chalcedon." EDITOR; Dominant Modern Reconstruction (the vendored file's own note says "the 440s"; say "in exile, near the end of his life"). BH 366–368: "Nestorius himself wrote his defence in Greek; his works were condemned to be burnt and only a few sermons and letters have survived in Greek and Latin." EDITOR. BH 2287–2290 (Syriac translator's preface): a book "which he sets as a judge between him[self] and the impious Cyril, speaking and defending themselves." LATER.

**E14. The pen name.** BH 2264–2289 (translator's preface): "It is called indeed the Bazaar of Heracleides... but it is not evident who Heracleides [was]... Heracleides was a man honoured for his conduct and esteemed for his knowledge, and he dwelled in the neighbourhood of Damascus." "It seemed [therefore good] to the writer to entitle the book with the title of this man's name, lest on account of his own name, in that many abhorred it, they should not be willing to read [it] and be converted to the truth". NAU 1451–1462 (same): "il parut bon à l'auteur de mettre son nom sur le titre du livre. Il craignait en effet que son propre nom, abhorré de beaucoup, ne les empêchât de le lire et de se tourner vers la vérité". LATER (the Syriac translator, sixth century or after, D3). Documented as the translator's account. Editors: "Bazaar" is a mistranslation of a Greek word for "treatise" (BH 442–447). EDITOR.

**E15. "Let Nestorius be anathema."** BH 20262–20266: "Therefore my own aim and my zeal is that God may be blessed and glorified even on earth as in heaven. But may Nestorius be anathematized; but may they say what I pray them to say concerning God. For I am of those who [are] with God and not of those who [are] against God". BH 20268–20271: "for this reason on his account I am pledged to endure and to suffer everything, so that by my own anathema would that every man might be [ready] enough to be reconciled unto God". NAU 17281–17283: "Mon but et mon souci sont donc que Dieu soit béni et loué sur la terre comme dans le ciel. Que Nestorius soit donc anathème, mais qu'ils disent de Dieu ce que je les prie de dire". OWN. Documented. "Anathema" = cut off from the church under its curse.

**E16. "Who is Nestorius?" and Leo.** BH 20465–20475: "For who is Nestorius? Or what is his life? Or what is his death in the world? But [he has brought them to pass] because of the truth which he has given unto the world... God appointed for this [purpose] a preacher who was guiltless of this suspicion, Leo, who used to preach the truth undaunted." BH 20644–20652: he did not write to Leo "so that I might not hinder from his running him who was running fairly because of the prejudice against my person." BH 20689–20691: Leo "has indeed held well to the faith but has agreed to the things which these have unjustly committed against me without examination and without judgement". OWN. Documented. Leo of Rome is on the tree.

**E17. The end of the book.** BH 20696–20703, 20731–20732: "But I have endured the torment of my life and all my [fate] in this world as the torment of one day and lo! I have now already got me to [the time of my] dissolution, and daily every day I beseech God to accomplish my dissolution, whose eyes have seen the salvation of God. Conclusion. Rejoice for me, O desert, my beloved and my foster-parent and the home of my habitation, and my mother [the land of] my exile, who even after my death will guard my body unto the resurrection by the will of God. Amen." NAU 17658–17667: "Réjouis-toi en moi, désert, mon ami, mon nourricier et ma demeure, (et toi aussi) exil, ma mère, qui, même après ma mort, garderas mon corps jusqu'à la résurrection". OWN. Documented.

## Impact, heaviest first (what later witnesses carried on and used)

**I1. The Church of the East kept his book, in Syriac, after the empire banned it.** BH 366–380 (editors, from Bedjan): "The Syriac translation of the latter work was made about 535 under the patriarch Paul, according to Bedjan; of this there is extant only one mutilated manuscript, which is preserved in the library of the Nestorian patriarch at Kotchanes, in Kurdistan. This manuscript has suffered considerable damage, chiefly at the hands of the Kurds on the occasion of the massacre of Nestorian Christians by the Kurdish chief Bedr Khan Bey in 1843." BH 334–345, 358–361: noticed "in the last decade of the nineteenth century by two German scholars"; "in 1910 a Syriac text was published in Leipzig by P. Bedjan and a French translation in Paris by F. Nau." BH 270–273 with 366–368: the empire ordered his writings burned (E11). EDITOR. Widely Accepted; the translation's date Contested (D3). Census `legacy` field says the same.

**I2. His name in the Church of the East's worship.**
- Narsai (died c. 503), NAR 6168–6169, *Homily on the Mysteries*: "Of all these the priest makes mention before God, imitating Mar Nestorius in his supplication." LATER (within about two generations). Connolly argues the homily is Narsai's (NAR 2414–2416: "there can be little doubt that Homily xvii is a genuine work of Narsai"), after doubts he records (NAR 426–435). Widely Accepted as Narsai's (Connolly's judgement). NAR 670–672: Narsai also wrote a homily "on the three doctors Diodore, Theodore, and Nestorius" (not held).
- A liturgy that bears his name. BAD 1554–1562 (Badger, 1852, an outsider's report of the church's books): "Liturgies; of these the Nestorians possess three, viz. that known as the Apostles', that of Theodore the Interpreter, and the Liturgy of Nestorius. The Liturgy of Nestorius is directed to be used five times during the year, viz. on the festival of the Greek Doctors, on the last day of the Baootha d'Ninwaye, on the feast of S. John the Baptist, on the Epiphany, and on the Thursday before Easter." BAD 17527–17528 (Abdisho's catalogue, 1298, Badger's English): "He wrote, moreover, a large liturgy which was translated [into Syriac] by Tooma and Mar Awa." LATER. Documented as their reports; authorship as Abdisho's claim.
- Named in the rites: BAD 16357–16361 (ordination): "Mar Nestorius the persecuted martyr"; BAD 12305–12308 (marriage rite): "The enemy strove to hinder the ascent of thy spirit to heaven, O Mar Nestorius". LATER (rite as Badger found it). Documented as the rite's words; their age not given.
- One of the "three Doctors." SYN 42081–42082, 42224–42238 (Chabot's French of Babai's *Life of George*, seventh century): the anathema on anyone who "méprise ou blâme les trois Docteurs œcuméniques, bienheureux et vénérables, colonnes de l'Église... le bienheureux Diodore, le bienheureux Théodore et le bienheureux Nestorius, illustre entre les martyrs". LATER. Babai the Great is a sibling in this batch.

**I3. But the Church of the East said its faith was not his invention.** (Defended by the Church of the East: the faith in two natures, and his name against the label.)
- Catholicos George I to Mina, about 680 (SYN 32697–32711: "en la présente année 60e" of the Arab rule; 33283–33286): "les choses que je écris ne sont pas l'invention des bienheureux Nestorius et Théodorus, qui sont calomniés par la multitude des impies, qui sont tournés en dérision sans motif, et dont les noms scandalisent les simples et les ignorants: mais ce sont les choses que la bouche vivifiante même du Christ a fait entendre". LATER. Documented. The year is Chabot's heading reckoning (year 60 of the Arabs).
- Abdisho of Nisibis, *The Jewel*, 1298 (BAD 18315–18318 title and date; 19147–19162): "As to the Easterns, however, because they never changed their faith, but kept it as they received it from the Apostles, they were unjustly styled 'Nestorians,' since Nestorius was not their Patriarch, neither did they understand his language; but when they heard that he taught the doctrine of the two Natures and two Persons, one Son of God, one Christ, and that he confessed the orthodox faith, they bore witness to him, because they themselves held the same faith. Nestorius, then, followed them, and not they him... Therefore when called upon to excommunicate him, they refused, maintaining that their excommunication of Nestorius would be equivalent to their excommunication of the Sacred Scriptures and the holy Apostles". LATER (about 850 years). Documented as the Church of the East's own self-account. Note: Badger prints the same passage twice with "Mother of God" (BAD 3098–3099) and "Mother of Christ" (BAD 19156–19157); the second is the full translation in the appendix; avoid the phrase.
- Census, the Church of the East's own entry: "Its opponents fixed the name 'Nestorian' on it."

**I4. Barhadbeshabba told his exile as a saint's life.** BB 35705–35713, 36025–36031 (about 600, Nau's French): Cyril's friends call him "le chien Nestorius"; he replies he barks for Christ. Marcian calls him back; "Nestorius répondit en refusant de revenir et il dit ainsi: 'La solitude, dans sa désolation, me réjouit; le désert, avec ses fleurs, me plaît, et l'entretien avec les animaux m'est agréable'"; brought by force "sur une litière, il alla vers le Christ son ami". Nau's note on one story (BB 35949–35950): "La présente rédaction est légendaire." LEGEND. Inferential-Thin as events; Documented as how the Church of the East remembered him. (The Ephesus sibling already quotes Barhadbeshabba's "pious Mar Nestorius": do not reuse it.)

**I5. In the empire: condemned and cursed.** EV 1222, 1503–1506: "that God-assaulting tongue" (already used in the Evagrius story), "the father of the blasphemy". EV 1686–1690: "I learn from one who wrote an account of his demise, that, when his tongue had been eaten through with worms, he departed to the greater and everlasting Judgment". LATER, hostile, hearsay. Theodoret's story (census) has Chalcedon make his friend Theodoret say "Anathema to Nestorius" (link only).

## Links to other people

- **Cyril of Alexandria** (sibling): his chief opponent; the Bazaar is set as a judge between them (E13).
- **Council of Ephesus 431** (sibling): deposed him (E9).
- **Socrates of Constantinople**: contemporary witness (S1, E1–E3, E8, E10).
- **Leo of Rome**: Nestorius praised his teaching in exile (E16).
- **Evagrius Scholasticus**: kept his exile letters, hostile (E12, I5).
- **Narsai of Nisibis**: named him in the prayer of the liturgy (I2).

## Where sources disagree or come late

**D1. Who began the quarrel.** Socrates: his priest Anastasius, and Nestorius backed him (E3). Nestorius: he found factions and offered a word for agreement (E4). Evagrius's summary follows Nestorius's book (EV 1513–1521). Hefele (HEF 1297–1299) sides with Socrates; a historian's view. Contested.

**D2. What he really taught.** Cyril and Ephesus: he split Christ in two (census floorNote; ACTS). Nestorius: two natures, one Christ, one worship; he denied the charge (E5, E6). Socrates: he did not make Christ a mere man (E8). Census floorNote: "Historians still argue over what he really taught; a 1908 study, Nestorius and his Teaching, reopened the question" (BH 310–311, Bethune-Baker 1908). Contested.

**D3. When the Bazaar was put into Syriac.** Bedjan: about 535 under Paul (BH 372–373); Bethune-Baker: perhaps under Mar Aba, 525–533 (BH 330–333, "no absolute certainty"); the editors' note: possibly after 638, from the name "Aleppo" (BH 403–411). Contested. Say "in Syriac, perhaps in the sixth century".

**D4. When and how he died.** Socrates (c. 439): still in the Oasis (E10). HEF 8319–8320: "When and where he died is unknown." Nau (BB 36042–36049, editor): most likely about June 451, reckoning from stories that he died as he set out for Chalcedon. Evagrius: worms (I5). Barhadbeshabba: on a litter, refusing to return (I4). Inferential-Thin for any date; "about 451" Dominant Modern Reconstruction (census dates 428–c. 451).

**D5. The "recantation."** Only Socrates (E10). Contested.

**D6. Who held him captive.** His letter: "barbarians", "Nomades", with the Mazices coming after (EV 1603–1610, 1647–1648); Evagrius's summary: the Blemmyes (EV 1564). Say "desert raiders".
