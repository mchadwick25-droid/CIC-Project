## The three lines (one hearing, as a listener)

- **Who this was:** Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople, the empire's capital, from 428, who taught that Christ is God and man, kept distinct yet worshiped as one, and so would not simply call Mary "Mother of God".
- **What happened:** in 431 a council at Ephesus led by his enemy Cyril of Alexandria judged that he split Christ in two and removed him; he denied it. His writings were ordered burned, he was dragged about in exile in southern Egypt, and near the end he wrote his defense under a borrowed name, offering his own name to the curse if only they spoke rightly of God.
- **Why the connection matters:** his book survives only because the Church of the East, in Persia, put it into Syriac; it honored him as "the persecuted martyr" and refused to cut him off, yet said its faith was its own: "Nestorius, then, followed them, and not they him."
