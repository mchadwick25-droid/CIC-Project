# Outline check: Martin Luther

Checker, batch 5, 2026-11-18. Job as re-set by Mark (Decision-Log, "Batch 5 hearts: church words explained, and Luther's job re-set", 2026-11-18): grace and why he protested; the break with Rome and Worms; the German Bible and hymns; the hard turns of 1525; up to about 4 minutes.

## My own heart (written before reading Outline.md)

**Essence.** Martin Luther, a friar of the Augustinian order who taught theology at Wittenberg in Saxony, taught that forgiveness is God's gift, received by trusting God's promise, not something bought (Theses 36–37, KD 2572–2578; answer to Cajetan 1518, KD 3311–3316). So in 1517 he protested to his archbishop against the preaching and sale of indulgences, letters of pardon sold by the church's preachers (W1 1014–1021). Condemned by the pope and the emperor after he would not take back his books at Worms in 1521 unless shown wrong from Scripture or plain reason (RTA 36588–36595), he put the New Testament into German while in hiding, and the churches that grew from Wittenberg, the Lutheran churches, came from his protest (census `toldWithin`, `legacy`).

**Also told.** The theses spread fast in print (W1 488–491). The break of 1520: the pope's decree gave him sixty days to recant, and he burned the papal law books, the bull with them by the editors' account (KD 5619–5628; W3 5285–5286; CUR 5653–5655). The Edict of Worms outlawed him; he was hidden (Wartburg, editors) and the movement grew meanwhile (KD 6049–6072; CUR 6021–6028; KD 6153–6160). The whole Bible in German by 1534, editors' date (W6 17321–17351). German hymns and a German service for the people, kept for centuries (KD 8307–8314; W6 13417–13466; FH, MOR). 1525: he blamed the princes and warned the peasants, then urged rulers to strike the rebels down; many of his own friends were offended (W4 10396–10403, 11958–11962, 12298–12304). His marriage to Katharina von Bora, a former nun, in June 1525 (CUR 9791–9818). The catechisms, a clause (SC 51; BC 25517–25521). Card only: his late writings against the Jews (treatises not held).

## BLOCKING

**B1. The Danish quote is a misreading of the OCR.** In What came of him, item 3 quotes "the Danish tongue in which we were born" (DK 6547–6548). The OCR reads "de Cc- defía Danica in qua nati ſumus". In this same file "ecdefía" and "ccdefíae" stand for *ecclesia* (DK 1089, 1515, 2020). So the text reads *de Ecclesia Danica*: "the Danish church in which we were born". It does not say "the Danish tongue". (The argument about language is carried by the lines around it, DK 6543 "Danice" and DK 6550–6553: the Gospel and the sacraments are not given "lingua non intellecta".) Second method: the Danish word forms and the context agree, but no clean edition is held, so the reading stays an OCR inference. **Correction:** drop the quotation marks and say: "A Danish church order he approved, in 1537, urged that the Lord's Prayer and Christ's words at the Supper be said in Danish, to help those simple and untaught in the faith (DK 6540–6546), since it was as foolish to give the sacraments in words not understood as the Gospel in an unknown tongue (DK 6550–6553)." Make the same change in the source sheet's I2 note ("lingua Danica" should read "Ecclesia Danica", OCR "Cc- defía").

**B2. Beats 4 and 7 jump back in time.** Beat 4 reaches ahead to 1534 ("he finished the whole Bible by 1534"). Beats 5 and 6 then tell 1525. Beat 7 goes back to the 1523 Mass order and the 1524 hymnbook. The kit's bar counts a jump back after the essence as a failure to understand on one hearing. **Correction (this keeps Mark's seven beats and their order):** (i) Move the M14 and M15 items (the 1523 wish for German songs; the 1524 hymnbook, its four parts, "by one editor's count") to the end of beat 4, and retitle it "The hidden year, the New Testament and the first German songs, 1521–1524". (ii) Take "with colleagues he finished the whole Bible by 1534" out of beat 4 and put it at the end of beat 7, after the catechism clause (M12, Jacobs, Widely Accepted). (iii) Retitle beat 7 "The German Mass, the hymns and the catechism, 1526–1534", beginning with the 1526 German Mass.

**B3. His actual excommunication is missing, and the outline invites the wrong inference.** The outline says only that the 1520 decree "threatened" excommunication (beat 2, the excommunication gloss, and Do not say). A writer may then say, or let the listener infer, that he was never cut off. The record holds that he was. The W3 editor: "It was not until January 3, 1521, that the second bull, Decet Romanum Pontificem, launched the curse of Rome upon the stiff-necked heretic" (W3 288–290, editor: Widely Accepted). The Edict of May 1521 names him "membrum ab Ecclesia Dei separatum", a member cut off from the church of God (KD 6046–6048: Documented). The source sheet's B2 cites W3 260–267 and stops just short of this. This is part of the break with Rome, which is in the job. **Correction:** add to the end of beat 2: "In January 1521 a second papal decree cut him off from the church (W3 288–290, editor: Widely Accepted)." Change the gloss to: "excommunication: being cut off from the church. The June 1520 decree threatened it (W3 260–264); a second decree carried it out in January 1521 (W3 288–290)." Replace the Do not say line with: "Not 'excommunicated at Worms' or 'the June 1520 decree excommunicated him'. The June decree threatened it, the January 1521 decree did it, and Worms was the emperor's ban (M5, M9; W3 260–264, 288–290)."

**B4. The Connection field gives the writer a judgment that no held source makes in a neutral voice.** It says the listener must understand "that 1525 showed the cost of his reliance on rulers". That is the hostile Hutterite chronicle's view (HUT 5345–5349). The editor's "seemed to be a defence of their cruelties" (W4 12295–12296) is not that view either. It also conflicts with the outline's own Do not say line ("Not that Luther … 'sided with' the princes … in the narrator's voice"). **Correction:** replace the clause with: "and that 1525 is told through what he wrote, in order (the call to both sides, the tract, the July letter), and through what his friends and a later hostile chronicle answered, each credited. The narrator draws no conclusion about him and the rulers."

## MINOR

**M1. "Taught the Bible."** The essence says "taught the Bible at Wittenberg", and the Setting says "taught the Bible and theology". The held line says he was master and regular lecturer in theology, "S. Theologiae Magistro … lectore ordinario" (KD 2441–2442). **Correction:** "who taught theology at Wittenberg" in both places.

**M2. The emperor's paraphrase drops his charge of error.** Beat 3 says "a single friar stood against a thousand years of Christendom". The emperor's words are that a single friar "erre en son opinion", is in error in his opinion, against all Christendom of the past thousand years and more and of the present (RTA 39105–39108). **Correction:** "declared that a single friar was in error against all Christendom of the last thousand years and of the present."

**M3. Q3 is addressed to everyone, not only rulers.** Beat 5 and Also told say he urged "the princes" or "rulers" to strike the rebels down. Q3 says "everyone who can", and the lines before it say "every man is his judge and executioner" (W4 11949–11951). **Correction:** "urging rulers, and anyone who could, to strike them down: Q3."

**M4. The Admonition's blame is cut short.** Beat 5 reads "To the princes and lords: they alone were to thank for the rebellion". The text goes on: "and especially you blind bishops and mad priests and monks" (W4 10396–10399). **Correction:** "the princes and lords, and especially the bishops and clergy, were alone to thank for the rebellion, for in their rule they did nothing but flay and rob their subjects."

**M5. The July letter's force is left out while its mercy is kept.** Beat 5 keeps "show grace, even to the guilty". It leaves out that he restated that rulers should strike the rebel bands quickly "regardless of whether they struck the guilty or the innocent" (W4 13546–13549). **Correction:** "That July he held to it: rulers should strike the rebel bands quickly, guilty or innocent; but once they had won they should show grace even to the guilty…". Add to Do not say: "Do not present the July letter as a retraction (W4 13540–13553)."

**M6. *The Freedom of a Christian* is out of order within beat 2.** It came out in the middle of November 1520, after the June decree (W2 11170–11173, editor). **Correction:** in beat 2, place the free lord and dutiful servant idea after the June decree and before the December burning ("That November he wrote…").

**M7. Beats 5 and 6 cross in time.** Beat 5 ends with the July letter and a later chronicle, and beat 6 then tells June. **Correction:** open beat 6 "That June, while the war was ending", or move the July letter and the chronicle to the end of beat 6. Either way, keep both in the story.

**M8. Glosses.**
- (a) *choir*: "clergy and boys" is not in KD 8309–8310, which says only "solus chorus". Use "the trained singers who sang the service".
- (b) *Moravians*: "of German origin" misleads. The census tells refugees from Bohemia who built Herrnhut on a Saxon nobleman's land (`the-moravian-church-at-herrnhut`). Use "a church begun by refugees from Bohemia, in Saxony".
- (c) *excommunication*: the gloss uses "sacraments", which is not glossed. See B3 for the new wording.
- (d) Add glosses for these words, which are heard but not explained: "Anabaptist" (beat 5, a witness the listener must know is hostile: "a later church of believers baptized as adults, hostile to him"; census `the-anabaptist-movements`); "New Testament" (essence: "the gospels and letters of the early church", as the sibling Tyndale outline glosses it); "Scriptures" (Q2: "the Bible"); "Revelation" (Patmos gloss: "the Bible's last book"); "cloister" (beat 6: "a convent"); "apostles" (impact 1: "Christ's first followers"); "Rome", meaning the papacy ("the pope's church"); "Augustinian" ("an order named for Augustine", a passing word).

**M9. The Moravian hymnal is a printed book.** Impact 1 says the Moravians "sang" the hymn. The held book prints it, headed "A Parody of Dr. Luther's Hymn", the old word for a version of it (MOR 31911–31913, 459). **Correction:** "the Moravians printed his Ten Commandments hymn in English".

**M10. Ending (a) says a little more than the preface.** The 1539 preface hoped for "less writing, and more studying and reading of the Scriptures" (W1 280–282). It does not say "his books less". **Correction:** "he had hoped there would be less writing and more reading of Scripture, so that each might 'drink for himself from the fresh spring'". The separate wish that all his books were forgotten (W1 260) may stand beside it.

**M11. *Ein feste Burg*'s date is Contested (D4), but beat 7 gives no confidence level.** **Correction:** "(M17: Bacon's date, Contested; Kidd prints it undated in 1523–4)".

**M12. Why he is on the tree is missing from the essence.** Neither the essence nor What came of him says that the Lutheran churches grew from his protest (CEN `toldWithin`, `legacy`; I4: the Archbishop of Mainz, July 1521, "augentur copiae Lutheranorum", KD 6153–6160). **Correction:** add to the end of the essence: "The churches that grew from his protest, the Lutheran churches, carry his name (CEN; I4: Documented)."

**M13. The length is under-set for what the beats now hold.** The essence and beat 1 take about 150 words, What came of him about 140, and the three quotes about 130. That leaves under 150 words for beats 2 to 7 at 560 words. **Correction:** set the length to "about 4 minutes (about 600 words)", within Mark's exception. Mark as optional, so that a cut falls on them first and never on the force of 1525: the Louvain and Cologne burnings, the emperor's "stake" line, the Gospel-means-good-news line, Walter's memory, and *Ein feste Burg*.
