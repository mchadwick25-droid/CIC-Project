## The three lines (from one hearing)

1. **Who:** Martin Luther, a friar who taught at the university in Wittenberg, whose protest began the churches that carry his name, the Lutheran churches.
2. **What happened:** In 1517 he protested against the sale of pardons, teaching that forgiveness is God's free gift, received by trusting God's promise. In 1520 he broke with the pope, and in 1521 he would not take back his books before the emperor at Worms. Outlawed and hidden, he put the New Testament into German, and he wrote hymns for the people to sing. In the Peasants' War of 1525 he first warned both sides, then urged the rulers to kill the rebels. He married a former nun, and he wrote a catechism for households.
3. **Why the connection matters:** He spread his teaching through Scripture, song and a home catechism in the people's own German. His churches kept his hymns for two centuries, and they carry his name.

None of the lines is blank or wrong. What a listener cannot give after one hearing is the German Bible as a whole. They would say "the New Testament", because nothing in the story tells them the whole Bible followed.
