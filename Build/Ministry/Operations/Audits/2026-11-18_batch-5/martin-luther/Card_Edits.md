## Quoted spans (7), all awaiting second reading

1. "Every true Christian, living or dead, has a share in all the good things of Christ and the church. God gives it to him, even without letters of pardon." Printed Latin KD 2576-2578 (Thesis 37). Awaiting second reading. **Scan fault**: the file reads "Uteris" for "literis" at KD 2577; the card gives the corrected "literis". The check will fail on this span until `tools/quote_scan_readings.json` carries a reading (file KD, lines 2577, scan "Uteris", reads "literis", kind OCR misread). Not edited here, as asked. If the entry is not wanted, the card must carry "Uteris" instead.
2. "Unless I am convinced by the witness of the Scriptures or by clear reason, I cannot and will not take back anything." Printed Latin in Kidd's version KD 5945-5950, which includes the parenthesis on pope and councils that the brief leaves out. Awaiting second reading. The Latin Acta (RTA 36588-36595) reads the same in sense with spelling differences (evidenti, quicquam, "capta conscientia"); its scan is too garbled to stand as the verbatim wording, so Kidd's printing is used.
3. "God help me. Amen." Printed German KD 5952 ("Gott helff mir. Amen."), inside the Latin answer. Awaiting second reading.
4. "Here I stand, I cannot do otherwise" Printed German RTA 36651 ("Ich kann nicht änderst, hie stehe ich"), Wrede's note giving the words of the two Wittenberg prints. Word order differs from the brief's English. Kidd's wording (KD 5951-5952, "Hie stehe ich. Ich kan nicht anders", scan "lean") is described in `uncertain`, not quoted. Awaiting second reading.
5. "So let everyone who can strike, kill and stab, in secret or in the open. Remember that nothing can be more poisonous, harmful or devilish than a rebel." Printed English W4 11958-11961 (Jacobs's translation: "smite, slay, and stab", "hurtful"). German not held. Awaiting second reading.
6. "furious, raving, senseless tyrants" Printed English W4 13558-13559. Awaiting second reading.
7. "drink for himself from the fresh spring" Printed English W1 285-286 ("every one may drink for himself from the fresh spring"). German not held. Awaiting second reading.

## Conflicts with the entry's existing fields

None contradicts. Two census claims are not found in the held sources used here, and the card does not repeat them:
- `legacy`: "His hymn tunes began a line of church music that runs to Bach." The sheet's Bach mention (Coleridge, Heine, Bach: "card") has no cited line; Bacon says only that many tunes were plausibly Luther's own and that their authorship is disputed (HY 714-735).
- `legacy`: "His Small Catechism is still learned by heart at home." Held: the parts' heading about a father presenting them to his household (SC 51) and the Formula of Concord's "Bible of the laity" (BC 25517-25521); nothing on present practice.
- `why` calls him "a miner's son from Eisleben": the card keeps it as the editor Morley's statement (TT 97), not Luther's own.
