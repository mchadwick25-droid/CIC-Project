# Review: John Calvin

Brief: `john-calvin.brief.txt` (585 words; about 3:54 at 150 a minute). Reviewer did not write or check it. Read once, straight through, before the outline.

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** John Calvin, a French scholar who left France to hide, wrote the Institutes, and was held at Geneva in 1536 by Farel's curse.
- **What happened:** he was thrown out of Geneva and called back. He gave the city a written plan with pastors, teachers, deacons and lay elders, and the elders sat with the pastors as a church court. The city's councils punished, beheading one man and burning Servetus, with Calvin wanting Servetus dead. His book taught that God chooses beforehand who will be saved, and a doctor who called that God a tyrant was jailed and sent away.
- **Why the connection matters:** churches in France, meeting in secret and in danger, and the church of Scotland built the same offices. His name became the label for the whole family of churches that began at Zurich before him.

All three lines can be written from one hearing. None is blank or wrong.

## The heart

**My own essence, written before reading the heart.** Calvin, a French scholar in exile, wrote the Institutes to defend believers burned in France. Held at Geneva in 1536, he gave the city a written order for its church: pastors, teachers, deacons and lay elders, who sat weekly with the pastors as a consistory. Churches in France and Scotland built on that order, and his name became the label of the family. **Also told:** his teaching that God chooses beforehand who is saved, and the quarrel with Bolsec; the councils' hold on punishment and the force, Servetus among it; the French and Scottish churches.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The Institutes come after the first minute.** The heart's second sentence is first heard at word 185 and completed at word 193. The outline's Opening 1 already holds the fix (Outline 71). In P1, make the scholar "already the author of a book that defended his persecuted fellow believers". In P2, after "The scholar was John Calvin.", name it: "His book was the Institutes, a book of instruction in the Christian faith." P3 then tells why he wrote it and drops its own naming sentence: end P3 at "...to clear their name." The essence is then complete by about word 160. No Do not say is needed: the outline's beat 1 already says this.

**B2. "The plan, and the fight over it" is missing from Also told** (Outline 11, 77–78; Mark's heart: "the catechism, a question-and-answer book, and the church discipline, correcting members, with the fight until 1555 over barring people from communion"). Without it, the councils' contest of the consistory's power is gone. Add it in time order, about 40 words:
- P4, after "he went": "...he went back in 1541, but only once the city swore to keep a catechism, a book of questions and answers on the faith, and a church discipline, a system for correcting members' lives."
- P5, after "But the councils kept the power to punish.": "Who could bar people from communion stayed in dispute between them for years."
- P8, opening the Paris sentence: "In 1555, the year Geneva's councils confirmed the consistory's rules, believers meeting in a Paris house..."

This keeps the order (1541, a running dispute, 1547, 1551, 1553, 1555). It also honours the Do not say on "twelve years" (Outline 159). No new Do not say line is needed.

**B3. Bern's reply is missing from the Bolsec beat** (Outline 10, 80; Mark's Added note: Bern's pastors said he was "not so bad a man"). The story credits the record to Geneva's pastors, but it then tells only their side. That is the one-sided record the outline set Bern's line against. After "He was jailed,", add: "and when Geneva asked other Swiss churches, Bern's pastors wrote that some said he was not so bad a man, and hoped for agreement. Geneva still sent him away for good." That is about 30 words. Tell it in narration, not as a quote. Say nothing of the other churches' replies (Outline 152).

### MINOR

- **m1 (cut).** P2: "Thrown out two years later and called back in 1541," is told again in P4, and the heart does not need it in the essence. Cut it: "...He stayed, and gave Geneva a written plan..." This saves about 7 words.
- **m2.** P8: "ruled to baptize, marry and bury as Geneva's church did" drops communion. The Assembly's line covers "the Sacraments, ... Mariages and Buriall" (PET 1208–1210). Say "to give baptism and communion, marry and bury as Geneva's church did."
- **m3.** P7: the outline's Right for Servetus includes "after asking the advice of four Swiss churches" (Outline 81, 135). Add it after "burned Michael Servetus alive". It is 7 words, and it is part of why "the city council" is the right subject.
- **m4.** P5 lists the offices but shows none at work. The outline's shape asks for the court shown through its acts (Outline 66, 79). Consider one clause from beat 5: "when a man refused to answer to Mr Calvin, the council sent him to prison" (1546, in narration). This is optional. If added, it costs about 15 words.
- **m5.** P8: the 1559 assembly adopted "a statement of belief and a rulebook" (Outline 13). Add "a statement of belief and". It is 5 words.
- **m6.** P2: "Churches in France and Scotland built theirs the same way" is safe: it states likeness, which is Documented. Do not strengthen it in revision to "copied Geneva" or "took it from him". That reading is Widely Accepted and must be credited to Hooker (Outline 6, 144).
- **m7.** P5: "Teachers taught true teaching" is flat. Use the gloss with a verb: "teachers taught sound teaching" or "teachers taught the faith truly" (CO10 1864–1866).
- **m8 (outline).** The outline's essence (Outline 5) includes the teaching on God's choice, but its beat 1 and Mark's plain heart do not. Align Outline 5 with beat 1 so the next writer is not pulled two ways.
