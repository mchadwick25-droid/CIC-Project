## S. Setting

S1. **Birth, France.** "John Calvin ... was born at Noyon, in Picardy, 10 July 1509. His father was a notary in the ecclesiastical court" (K 28822–28825). EDITOR (Kidd). BEZA1 1474: "Jean Calvin, natif de Noyon en Picardie" (CONTEMP, Histoire 1580). Documented. Spoken: "born in northern France, at Noyon, in 1509".

S2. **Law, at his father's wish.** Psalms preface (1557), Latin: "Theologiae me pater tenellum adhuc puerum destinaverat. Sed quum videret legum scientiam passim augere suos cultores opibus, spes ilia repente eum impulit ad mutandum consilium. Ita factum est, ut revocatus a philosophiae studio, ad leges discendas traherer" (K 28923–28929). OWN, written c. 21 years after. Documented. (His father meant him for theology, then switched him to law because law made men rich.)

S3. **Persecution in France, 1534–35.** Kidd: placards against the Mass, Oct. 1534; Calvin's flight; to Basel early 1535 (K 28883–28896). EDITOR. The Institutes dedication speaks of the doctrine "ought to be punished with confiscation, exile, imprisonment, and flames" (INST1 4774–4776). OWN. Documented.

S4. **Geneva in 1536.** Psalms preface: "Paulo ante huius optimi viri et Petri Vireti opera profligatus erat papatus : sed res adhuc incompositae et urbs in pravas et noxias factiones divisa." (K 29982–29984). OWN. Documented. (The papacy had just been driven out by Farel and Viret, but things were still unsettled and the city split into factions.) Hooker (LATER, c. 58 years): civil government "popular ... officers chosen by the people yearly"; "For spiritual government, they had no laws at all agreed upon" (HOOK 4266–4271). Geneva had sworn to the Reformation in 1536 (K 28672; Bern acknowledged its independence, K 28728). Spoken: "Geneva, a small free city on the lake at the edge of the Swiss lands, which had just broken with Rome".

S5. **Tradition's spoken name.** "the Reformed cities of Zurich and Geneva" (census). Spoken plainly: "the Reformed churches" for the family; "the Reformed cities" for the tradition entry.

## M. Events (time order)

M1. **Conversion.** "animum meum, qui pro aetate nimis obduruerat, subita conversione ad docilitatem subegit." (K 28935–28936). OWN (1557). Documented as his own account; Kidd dates it "late in 1532 or early in 1533" after Walker (K 28862–28865, EDITOR). How far it took him "was, perhaps, as doubtful to himself as to us" (K 28866–28867, EDITOR).

M2. **He wanted a hidden scholar's life.** "Ego qui natura subrusticus umbram et otium semper amavi, tunc latebras captare" (K 28941–28942); "relicta patria in Germaniam concessi, ut in obscuro aliquo angulo abditus quiete diu negata fruerer." (K 28946–28948). OWN (1557). Documented.

M3. **Why he wrote the Institutes (1535–36).** At Basel, "unknown", he saw French martyrs slandered at court as Anabaptists and rebels "to calm the hatred" that the burnings had stirred in Germany (K 28950–28956). "Haec mihi edendae Institutionis causa fuit : primum ut ab iniusta contumelia vindicarem fratres meos, quorum mors pretiosa erat in conspectu Domini" (K 28964–28966). It was then "breve duntaxat enchiridion", only a short handbook (K 28968–28970). OWN (1557). Documented.
- The dedication to Francis I itself: "My intention was only to furnish a kind of rudiments, by which those who feel some interest in religion might be trained to true godliness. And I toiled at the task chiefly for the sake of my countrymen the French" (INST1 4744–4749); "a summary of the very doctrine which, they vociferate, ought to be punished with confiscation, exile, imprisonment, and flames" (INST1 4774–4776). OWN. Dated "Basle, 1st August 1536" in the 1559 text (INST1 5837); the 1536 edition's date was 23 August 1535 (INST1 370–381, EDITOR; K 28898–28900, EDITOR). Printed March 1536 (K 28900, EDITOR). Documented.
- Beza's Histoire (CONTEMP, 1580): the persecution "donna occasion à Jean Calvin, estant lors à Basle, de dresser ce livre incomparable intitulé l'Institution de la religion Chrestienne, dedié au Roy mesmes, pour luy faire entendre que faussement & calomnieusement ses plus loyaux subjects estoient chargés des crimes d'heresie, & de rebellion" (BEZA1 2478–2483). Admirer's judgment ("incomparable").

M4. **What the Institutes say (one idea).** "Our wisdom, in so far as it ought to be deemed true and solid wisdom, consists almost entirely of two parts: the knowledge of God and of ourselves." (INST1 6642–6644). OWN (1559 edition). Documented. The catechism opens the same way: "WHAT is the chief end of man? A. Child. It is to know God his Creator." (CAT 229–232). OWN. Documented.

M5. **Held in Geneva, August 1536 (the opening).** Psalms preface, Latin (K 29974–29995): "donec Genevae non tam consilio vel hortatu quam formidabili Gulielmi Farelli obtestatione retentus sum, ac si Deus violentam mihi e caelo manum iniiceret. Quum rectum iter Argentoratum tendenti bella clausissent, hac celeriter transire statueram, ut non longior quam unius noctis mora in urbe mihi foret. ... Unus homo, qui nunc turpi defectione iterum ad papistas rediit, statim fecit ut innotescerem. Hic Farellus (ut incredibili zelo promovendi evangelii flagrabat) statim ad me retinendum obnixe nervos omnes intendit. Et quum privatis et occultis studiis me intelligeret esse deditum, ubi se vidit rogando nihil proficere, usque ad exsecrationem descendit, ut Deus otio meo malediceret, si me a ferendis subsidiis in tanta necessitate subducerem. Quo terrore perculsus, susceptum iter ita omisi ut mihi et verecundiae et timiditatis meae conscius obeundo certo muneri fidem meam non obstringerem." OWN, written 1557, about 21 years later. Documented as his own account. The man who made him known is Louis du Tillet (K 30020, Kidd's note, EDITOR).
- Contemporary letter (13 Oct. 1536): "I was detained at Geneva by the brethren, until they extracted from me a promise to return" (L1 2264–2265). OWN, CONTEMP. Documented.
- **Disagreement on his road:** Calvin says war had closed the direct road to Strasbourg (K 29980). Bonnet's note says he was going "back to Basle by the way of Geneva" (L1 2252–2256), EDITOR. Follow Calvin: he meant to stay one night.

M6. **"That Frenchman": lecturer at St Peter's.** Council register, 5 Sept. 1536: "Mag. Guil. Farellus exponit sicuti sit necessaria illa lectura qualem initiavit ille Gallus in S. Petro : propterea supplicat videri de ipso retinendo et sibi alimentando." (K 29999–30002). CONTEMP. Documented. (Farel says the lecture "that Frenchman" began at St Peter's is needed, and asks that he be kept and paid.) Kidd: Calvin was "reader in Holy Scripture at Geneva" (K 30008–30009, EDITOR citing Herminjard).

M7. **First attempt at order, 1537; the oath; expelled 1538.** Hooker (LATER, c. 1594): Calvin and two ministers "persuaded with much ado, the people to bind themselves by solemn oath, first never to admit the Papacy amongst them again; and secondly, to live in obedience unto such orders ... as those their true and faithful ministers of God's word had agreeably to scripture set down" (HOOK 4272–4297); date "20 July, 1537" (HOOK 4329, Keble's bracket, EDITOR). Council registers, 21–23 April 1538: Farel and Calvin preached despite a ban for refusing Bern's ceremonies; the General Council voted "qu'il doyjent vuyder laz ville dans troys jours prochaien" (K 31846–31880). Their reply when told: "Est bien ! az laz bonne heure ! Si nous heussions servy les hommes, nous fussions mal recompenser : mes nous servons ung grand maystre que nous recompenseraz. Calvinus az respondus cecy dessus." (K 31882–31886). CONTEMP register. Documented. (Right then. If we had served men, we would be badly rewarded, but we serve a great master who will reward us.)
- His own later account (farewell, 1564): "Puis après je fus chassé de ceste ville, et m'en allay à Strasbourg" (K 35747–35748). OWN, reported.

M8. **Strasbourg, 1538–41.** Pressed again, this time with Jonah: "quand les plus modérez me menacent que le Seigneur me trouveroit aussi bien que Jonas" (H5 8357–8359), letter to Du Tillet, 1538, OWN; Herminjard's note names the "most moderate" as Bucer (H5 8385–8386, EDITOR). English, L1 4627–4628. Kidd: pastor of French refugees at Strasbourg from 8 Sept. 1538, lecturer in theology Jan. 1539; second, enlarged Institutes, 1 Aug. 1539 (K 31902–31910, EDITOR). His own farewell: at Strasbourg he wrote the baptism form because Anabaptists brought their children to him "de cinq et de dix lieues à la ronde" (K 35796–35799). OWN.

M9. **Recalled, and unwilling.** Geneva's councils, 22 Oct. 1540: "vous pryons très assertes vous volloyër transporter par devers nous et en vostre prestine plache et ministère retourner. ... Voyeant que nostre peuple grandement vous desire." (K 32259–32273). CONTEMP. Calvin to Farel, 29 March 1540: "rather would I submit to death a hundred times than to that cross, on which one had to perish daily a thousand times over." (L1 8109–8111). OWN (Bonnet's English; Latin not held for 1540). Zurich's pastors, 4 April 1541, cite Jonah and Geneva's place "in confiniis ... Galliae, Italiae, Germaniae" (K 32277–32296). CONTEMP. August 1541, to Farel: "when I remember that I am not my own, I offer up my heart, presented as a sacrifice to the Lord." (L1 12761–12763). OWN. Documented.

M10. **Return and terms, 13 Sept. 1541.** Council register: he asked "mectre ordre sus l'église et que icelluy fusse par escript redigye" and offered "d'estre tousjour serviteur de Genève"; six councillors named to confer (K 32317–32338). CONTEMP. To Farel, 16 Sept.: "exposui non posse consistere ecclesiam, nisi certum regimen constitueretur, quale ex Verbo Dei nobis praescriptum est, et in veteri Ecclesia fuit observatum." (K 32398–32401). OWN. His farewell (1564): "je ne voulus jamais accepter le ministère qu'ils ne m'eussent juré ces deux points, assavoir de tenir le Catéchisme et la Discipline." (K 35787–35789). OWN, reported. Documented.

M11. **The Ordinances, 1541: four offices.** Draft (Sept.–Oct. 1541): "Il y a quatre ordres d'offices, que Nostre Seigneur a institué pour le gouvernement de son Eglise. Premièrement les pasteurs, puis les docteurs, après les anciens, quartement les diacres." (K 32448–32452; archive minute, CO10 1368–1370, 1421–1422). Drafted by Calvin and his colleagues with six councillors, with additions in another hand and a few words in Calvin's own (CO10 1378–1388, EDITOR). Official text variants: "Premièrement il y a ..."; "asscavoir"; elders "aultrement nommes commys pour la seigneurie", an addition in another hand (CO10 1446–1447, 1472–1474). The councils amended it and refused to let the ministers see the changes; passed by the General Council 20 Nov. 1541 "sans contradiction" (K 32426–32445). CONTEMP registers. Documented.
- Elders: "Leur office est de prendre garde sur la vie dun chascun, dadmonester amyablement ceulx quilz verront faillir ou mener vie désordonnée" (CO10 1947–1951); twelve chosen from the councils, "quil y en ait en chascun quartier de la ville, affin davoir loeil par tout" (CO10 1955–1963). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Each office (CO10, draft text): pastors "dannoncer la parole de dieu pour endoctriner, admonester, exhorter et reprendre tant en publiq comme en particulier, administrer les sacremens et faire les corrections fraternelles avec les anciens et comys" (CO10 1493–1499); teachers "denseigner les fidelles en saine doctrine, affin que la pureté de levangile ne soit corrompue" (CO10 1864–1866); deacons of two kinds, some "a recevoir, dispenser et conserver les biens des pouvres", others "pour soigner et penser les mallades et administrer la pitance des pouvres", that is, the hospital's procurators and hospitallers (CO10 2030–2042). The elders "sassemblent une fois la sepmaine avec les ministres, assavoir le Ieudy matin, pour veoir sil ny a nul desordre en lesglise" (CO10 2540–2544). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Pastors chosen by the ministers, then presented to the Council, then to the people (K 32514–32522). CONTEMP.
- Calvin's own review (Jan. 1542): "Intra viginti dies formulam composuimus"; "Constitutum deinde Iudicium quod morum censuram exerceat ... Volui enim, sicut aequum est, spiritualem potestatem a civili iudicio distingui. Ita in usum rediit excommunicatio." (K 34654–34658). "Non enim memini ex quo hic sum, duas horas sine interpellatione mihi datas esse." (K 34667–34668). OWN. Documented.
- Institutes IV.12.1 (1559): "as the saving doctrine of Christ is the life of the Church, so discipline is, as it were, its sinews" (INST3 11139–11140). OWN. Documented.

M12. **Catechism and liturgy, 1541–42.** Farewell: "A mon retour de Strasbourg, je fis le Catéchisme à la haste" (K 35787); Bonnet's English adds that printers fetched pages "as big as my hand" (L4 17345–17351). "Quant aux prières des Dimanches, je prins la forme de Strasbourg" (K 35792–35793). OWN, reported. Kidd says ordinances, catechism and liturgy "powerfully influenced all, save Lutheran, protestantism everywhere" (K 32383–32385). EDITOR.

M13. **The Consistory at work, and its force.** Minutes 1542 (K 34806–34845): a man asked if he goes to sermons and told to learn his Confession; a woman who still went to Mass "remise comme hors la foy" and "n'a pas voulu renonser à la Messe" (K 34819–34828). 17–18 June 1546: Gaspard Favre would not answer "à M. Calvin"; Calvin said "Nous sommes ici par dessus vous"; the Council sent Favre to prison (K 34849–34883). CONTEMP. Documented. Kidd's summary (EDITOR, citing the registers): Ameaux made to do penance for insulting Calvin, 1546; Franchequine Perrin imprisoned for dancing, 1547; Jacques Gruet beheaded, 1547 (K 34712–34723). The Gruet sentence itself, primary: for blasphemy, offences against the magistrate, and having "oultragé, menasse et mesdict des serviteurs de Dieu", "mené au lieu de Champel et illect ... tranché la teste" (K 35265–35277). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Excommunication contested: 19 March 1543 the Sixty ruled the Consistory had no power to bar from the Supper (K 34888–34893); Calvin: "decretum hoc aut morte mea aut exilio sanciri oportere" (K 34899–34900), OWN; right conceded 24 Jan. 1555 (K 35634–35673; Kidd K 34785–34787). Documented.
- Kidd (EDITOR): after 1555 Calvin's party "proceeded at once by banishment and executions ... to wipe out their opponents" (K 34788–34790). Editor's summary; card only.

M14. **Servetus, 1553.** Calvin to Farel, 13 Feb. 1546: "Nam si venerit, modo valeat mea auctoritas, vivum exire nunquam patiar." (K 35575–35579; L2 1781–1782 English). OWN. August 1553: "I hope that sentence of death will at least be passed upon him ; but I desire that the severity of the punishment may be mitigated." (L2 19433–19435). OWN. Company of Pastors register, 27 Oct. 1553: after the advice of Bern, Basel, Zurich and Schaffhausen, the council condemned "ledict Servet a estre mené en Champey et la estre bruslé tout vif" (K 35619–35624; CO8 54713–54718). Small Council, 26 Oct.: for "grands erreurs et blaffemes" (CO8 54352–54358). CONTEMP. Melanchthon, 1554: "Affirmo etiam vestros magistratus iuste fecisse" (K 35626–35632). CONTEMP. Documented. Tree link: `anti-trinitarian-currents-servetus-polish-brethr`.

M15. **The Academy, 5 June 1559.** Its laws read before a crowd; Calvin spoke in French and called all to prayer; Beza named Rector; Calvin had taught theology for years, Beza now alternating with him (K 35675–35714). CONTEMP. Documented. Kidd: it "made Geneva the intellectual and the missionary centre of the Reformed"; 1200 junior and 300 senior students by 1564 (K 34795–34799, EDITOR citing Borgeaud).

M16. **Farewell to the ministers, 28 April 1564.** Taken down by the minister Pinant "as nearly as the memory could preserve it word for word" (L4 17138–17146), with a second account by "B. B., called Corneille" that "fully confirms" it (L4 17156–17158, EDITOR). French text (K 35728–35800): "Quand je vins premièrement en ceste Eglise, il n'y avoit quasi comme rien. On preschoit et puis c'est tout. On cherchoit bien les idoles et les brusloit-on ; mais il n'y avoit aucune réformation. Toute estoit en tumulte." (K 35731–35735). "J'ay vescu icy en combats merveilleux : j'ay esté salué par mocquerie le soir devant ma porte de 50 ou 60 coups d'arquebute. Que pensez-vous que cela pouvoit estonner un pauvre escholier timide comme je suis, et comme je l'ay tousjours esté, je le confesse ?" (K 35741–35745). "je n'ay pas corrompu un seul passage de l'Escriture" (K 35767–35768). "Je vous prie aussi ne changer rien, ne innover." (K 35781–35782). Reported OWN words, CONTEMP record. Documented as Pinant's record.
- **Disagreement:** Kidd's French has "50 ou 60 coups"; Bonnet's English has "forty or fifty shots" (L4 17221–17222). Two printings of the same minute. Use the French; say "dozens of shots" if in doubt.
- Bonnet's English adds at the end that the brethren took his hand "one after the other, all melting into tears" (L4 17391–17393). Pinant's record.

M17. **Last letter, and death.** To Farel, 2 May 1564: "It is enough that I live and die for Christ, who is to all his followers a gain both in life and death." (L4 16768–16769). OWN. Farel, old, came to see him (Beza's Vita, quoted in Bonnet's note, L4 16781–16784: EDITOR quoting a CONTEMP). Died 27 May 1564 (K 34799–34800, EDITOR); the Consistory minute "Gone to God, May 27th" (L4 17409–17412, Bonnet quoting the minute). Documented.

## I. Impact, heaviest first

I1. **The French churches took Geneva's order.** Paris, Sept. 1555 (Histoire ecclésiastique, CONTEMP, 1580): a gentleman would not have his newborn baptised with Roman ceremonies, and "qu'il luy estoit impossible d'aller à Genève pour cest effect"; the assembly chose la Rivière as minister, "& fut aussi dressé quelque petit ordre ... par l'establissement d'un Consistoire composé de quelques Anciens & Diacres, qui veilloient sur l'Eglise, le tout au plus près de l'exemple de l'Eglise primitive du temps des Apostres." (BEZA1 6536–6587; K 36467–36506). The Histoire names the early church, not Geneva, as the model; the 1883 editors' note ties it to "l'organisation que Calvin avait donnée à l'Eglise de Strasbourg" (BEZA1 6592–6597, EDITOR). La Rivière had studied at Lausanne and Geneva (BEZA1 6541–6542, EDITOR).
- First National Synod, Paris, 26 May 1559: "fut escrite la Confession de Foy, ensemble fut dressée la Discipline Ecclésiastique au plus près de l'institution des Apostres" (K 36509–36522). CONTEMP. Discipline Art. 1: "Aucune Église ne pourra prétendre primauté ni domination sur l'autre" (K 36948–36951). Confession XXIX: "there should be pastors, overseers, and deacons" (SCHAFF 1971–1976). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Calvin's letter of 17 May 1559 names ministers Geneva is sending ("Arnold is substituted in his place ... Peter Gilbert") and is cool about the haste for a confession (L4 1939–1985). OWN. Bonnet: "some differences ... between the ministers of Paris and those of Geneva respecting the expediency of this meeting" (L4 1957–1959, EDITOR).
- What it risked: the Edict of Compiègne, 24 July 1557, ordered that those who broke the ban "de n'aller à Genève", among others, "seront punis de peine de mort" (K 36421–36444). CONTEMP. The 1559 synod met at Paris as the city "plus commode pour recevoir secrettement beaucoup de Ministres et Anciens" (K 36559–36561). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Kidd (EDITOR): ministers "either asked or sent from Geneva, to the number of 19 in 1559, 12 in 1560, and 90 in 1561"; seventy-two congregations by 1559 (K 36325–36330).
- **Disagreement on the French Confession's author:** Schaff: "prepared by Calvin and his pupil, De Chandieu" (SCHAFF 14–15, EDITOR). Kidd: "not Calvin's but the work of his pupil Antoine de la Roche Chandieu" (K 36342–36343, EDITOR). Contested; keep off the audio.

I2. **Scotland took "the order of Geneva".** Knox (CONTEMP): he went to the English at Frankfurt "at the commandiment of that notable servand of God, JOHNE CALVYNE", then "returned to Geneva, from thense to Deape, and thairafter to Scotland" (KNOX1 6571–6588). The English congregation's book: "The Form of Prayers ... used in the English Congregation at Geneva ; and approved by the famous and Godly-learned Man, M. John Calvin" (KIRK 15957–15960, the editors quoting the title, EDITOR-reported). General Assembly, December 1562: "It is concluded, That ane uniforme order sall be taken or keeped in ministration of the Sacraments, and solemnization of Mariages and Buriall of the Dead, according to the Kirk of Geneva." (PET 1208–1210). CONTEMP. Documented. The Edinburgh Book of Common Order of 1611 still prints "The Catechisme ... Made by the excellent Doctor & Pastour in Christs Church, Iohn Calvin" (KIRK 14515–14525). LATER, c. 47 years. Documented.

I3. **A critic's witness to the pattern (LATER, c. 30 years, England).** Hooker, writing against those who wanted Geneva's discipline in England: "Of what account the Master of Sentences was in the church of Rome, the same and more amongst the preachers of reformed churches Calvin had purchased ; so that the perfectest divines were judged they, which were skilfullest in Calvin's writings. His books almost the very canon to judge both doctrine and discipline by. French churches, both under others abroad and at home in their own country, all cast according to that mould which Calvin had made. The church of Scotland in erecting the fabric of their reformation took the selfsame pattern." (HOOK 4653–4669). Also: "incomparably the wisest man that ever the French church did enjoy, since the hour it enjoyed him" (HOOK 4244–4252). An opponent of the discipline, praising the man. Documented as Hooker's judgment.

I4. **The Institutes as the rule.** Hooker above (I3). Beza's "ce livre incomparable" (M3). The Institutes grew from six chapters (1536) to seventeen (1539) (INST1 1691–1692, EDITOR). Kidd: the 1539 edition "already placed him in the first rank among theologians" (K 31910–31911, EDITOR).

I5. **His name became the family's label, with friend and foe.** Synod of Jerusalem, 1672 (LATER, 108 years): "THE SYNOD OF JERUSALEM AGAINST THE CALVINISTS" (DOS 332–338); "so long as they follow Calvin the heresiarch, as a leader" (DOS 579–583). Voetius at Utrecht, lessons printed 1662 (LATER, c. 98 years): "Willen wy wel Calvinisten genoemt worden? A. Neen. ... Om dat Calvinus ons hooft niet en is." ... "Dat is soo, maer daerom en is hy ons hooft niet." (VOET 20605–20625). Documented. Both are already used on the census card's `legacy` field and on the tree's edges; take them as narration only.

I6. **Geneva as a school.** The Academy (M15). Kidd's "missionary centre" claim is the editor's.
