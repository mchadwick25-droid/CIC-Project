# Outline check: John Calvin

Checker's own heart, written before reading `Outline.md` (from the census entry, the source sheet, the job, and what Calvin is known for).

**Essence.** A French scholar, driven out by the burnings of Protestants in France, wrote the Institutes at Basel to defend them, and in 1536 was held in Geneva by Farel's curse to rebuild its church. Back in 1541 after his expulsion, he gave the city a written church order (pastors, teachers, elders, deacons, and a weekly consistory of elders and pastors), and the French and Scottish churches built on that order and his book, so that his name became the label of the Reformed family. He is on the tree as the teacher and organiser of the Reformed churches.

**Also told.** The Institutes and what they say (knowing God and ourselves; discipline as the church's sinews), growing from a short handbook; his teaching on predestination, God's choosing beforehand, and the bitter quarrels it brought (Bolsec, 1551, jailed and banished; Bern's "not so bad a man"); the expulsion of 1538 and Strasbourg; the catechism and the discipline, with the fight until 1555 over barring from communion; the force of Geneva's discipline, with Gruet and Servetus burned in 1553 with Calvin's agreement; the Academy of 1559; the French synod of 1559 and its confession and discipline, done under threat of death; Scotland's "order of Geneva"; his farewell and death in 1564.

**B2. The Servetus Do not say contradicts the held sentence.** The outline says: Wrong, "Servetus was burned for denying the Trinity", as the council's words. Right, the council condemned him for "great errors and blasphemies". It adds that his book against the Trinity is "the tree link's framing". But the formal sentence of 27 Oct. 1553 is held in CO8 at 54484–54690. It charges him first with a book "contre la saincte et individue Trinite" (54509–54512), then with a second book of "blasphemes contre la saincte Trinite, contre le Filz de Dieu contre le baptesme des petis enfans" (54532–54535). It condemns him for rising "contre la maieste divine et saincte Trinite" as "crime dheresie" (54658–54662). He is to be burned alive "avec ton livre" (54678–54683). The council's "grands erreurs et blaffemes" of 26 Oct. (CO8 54352–54358) is its working minute, not the sentence.
*Correction.* Replace that Do not say with: Wrong, "burned only for 'great errors' (the reason left vague)" or "burned on Calvin's order". Right, the city's sentence condemned him for books against the teaching that God is three persons in one, and against the baptism of infants, as heresy and blasphemy, and had him burned alive with his book (CO8 54484–54690).
- Beat 8: "for books against the teaching that God is three persons in one, which the city's judges called heresy and blasphemy".
- Words to explain, Trinity: cite CO8 54509–54512, not Kidd's note (K 34765, EDITOR).
- Add the sentence to the sheet at M14.

**B3. "General Council" has no gloss, and on this tree it will be heard as a church council.** Beat 3 says "the General Council gave them three days to leave" (K 31876–31880). To a listener of church history, a "General Council" is a council of bishops. Here it was the assembly of Geneva's citizens, voting "par plus grand voys". "The Sixty" (Also told; K 34888) and "the Council of Two Hundred" (Do not say) are in the same position.
*Correction.* Add to Words to explain:
- **General Council**: "the assembly of all the city's citizens", which voted them out (K 31874–31880). Never "the council" alone where a church council could be heard.
- **the Sixty / the Two Hundred**: "the city's larger councils".
Add to Do not say: Wrong, "the General Council" without its plain sense. Right, "the city's assembly of citizens".

**B4. "For twelve years the councils had denied the consistory the right to bar people from communion" (beat 8) is not supported, and the held record cuts against it.** The Sixty's ruling of 19 March 1543 (K 34888–34893) was followed at once by Calvin's letter to Viret of 24 March 1543. He writes that the dispute "statim fuit composita" and "obtinui nullo negotio quod petebam" (K 34896–34908). In 1546 the consistory's own minute records that Favre "luy futz deffendu la Cène" (K 34869–34870). What 1555 brought is Kidd's summary: "the long-disputed right ... was conceded" (K 34783–34787, EDITOR). The register of 24 Jan. 1555 shows only that the councils held to the edicts already passed (K 35634–35673). The same overstatement is in the Do not say ("refused his consistory the power to bar people from communion until 1555"). Also, the "death or exile" line was written to Viret, not to Farel. The outline names no recipient, which is fine.
*Correction.*
- Beat 5 or 8, one clause: "Who could bar people from communion stayed in dispute with the councils. In 1543, when one council denied the consistory that power, Calvin said such a decree would need his death or his exile first. In January 1555 the councils confirmed the consistory's rules." Cite K 34888–34908 and K 35634–35673. Confidence: Documented for 1543 and 1555; "long disputed" is the editor's (K 34783–34787).
- Do not say: replace "refused ... until 1555" with "contested that power, which was confirmed in 1555".
- Add Do not say: Wrong, "for twelve years the councils denied it". Right, the 1543 dispute was settled at once by Calvin's own account, and the right stayed disputed until 1555.
