# Review: Timothy I of Baghdad

Brief: `timothy-i-of-baghdad.brief.txt` (738 words). Judged against the Brief-Kit, the outline, Mark's batch 5 rulings (Decision-Log, 2026-11-18) and `Hearts_Plain.md`.

## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Timothy, head of the Church of the East (the church of Mesopotamia and Persia) for more than forty years from 780, leading it from Baghdad under five caliphs.
2. **What happened:** a much later historian says he won office by a trick with sacks of stones; a two-year split followed, with Christians killing and jailing one another. He argued for his faith before the caliphs, made a rule on electing a patriarch, went before the caliph six times to rebuild churches, and sent monks out as bishops south of the Caspian. He let two of them make bishops, with the Book of the Gospels in place of the missing third bishop.
3. **Why the connection matters:** he let the power to make bishops work far from Baghdad, so the church could take root among new peoples, reaching toward the Turks and China. His letters are how we know.

All three lines come clear from one hearing.

## The heart

**My own essence, from the census and what he is known for:** for four decades he led the Church of the East from Baghdad under the caliphs. He defended the faith at their court. He pushed the church to its widest reach by sending monks as bishops and letting them make bishops far from the centre. **Also told:** the disputed election and the split it caused; the rule on electing a patriarch; the six visits to the caliph; the failed accusation by the chief bishop of Merv. ## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The rule's reason is attached to the wrong rule (paragraph 6).** The brief says: "he made a church rule that no candidate for patriarch could promise gifts, so that an election could not split the church again." In Timothy's letter, the reason he gives ("de peur que ... il ne se produise un schisme dans l'Église", TIM 40417–40422) belongs to his first rule: no one may elect a patriarch until the chief bishop of Elam and the other named chief bishops have come or sent their consent (TIM 40424–40460). The gifts rule (canon II, TIM 40462–40471) gives no reason of its own. Its link to the bribery charge is Chabot's guess (CH 40480–40486). Placed straight after the trick with the sacks, the brief's sentence suggests that Timothy said he was outlawing what he himself had done. The record does not say that. It also drops the rule that ties the letter to the chief bishop of Elam, the very man it is written to.

Change to, for example: "About 790, at a meeting of bishops, he made church rules for choosing a patriarch. No one could elect until the chief bishop of Elam and the others named had come or sent their consent, so that, he wrote, the church would not split again. And no one could promise gifts." The outline left room for this error (beat 5 joins the two rules under one "so that"). **Do not say line to add:** "Not 'so that an election could not split the church again' attached to the gifts rule alone. Timothy gives that reason for his rule that no one may elect until the chief bishop of Elam and the other named chief bishops come or send consent (TIM 40417–40460). The gifts rule (TIM 40462–40471) has no stated reason, and its link to the trick is Chabot's guess (CH 40480–40486)."

**MINOR 2. Also told is incomplete: the removal (paragraph 4).** The approved heart reads "A chief bishop he had removed accused him before the caliph, and failed." The brief drops that Timothy removed him, so the accusation has no cause, and one of Timothy's own acts of force is lost. Change to: "The man from Merv, whom Timothy had removed from office, accused him before the caliph al-Mahdi, without success." (MARI 13096–13098, 13014–13016.)

**MINOR 3. The Fars permission slips into the narrator's voice (paragraph 9).** "He let their chief bishop complete his own bishops' making" is part of Bar Hebraeus's hearsay report (BH, "Fertur"), but it is told as fact. Keep it inside the report, for example: "...until Timothy won them over, and that he let their chief bishop complete his own bishops' making, a custom that, this historian says, still held in his day." Split it at "won them over" if it runs past about 30 words.

**MINOR 4. Timothy's own list needs its carrier (paragraph 9).** "Timothy himself counts Indians and Chinese, Tibetans and Turks" comes from a letter known only through Mingana's English, from his own manuscript (MIN 1703–1704). The outline's beat 7 reads "In a letter a modern scholar quotes". Restore that phrase.

**MINOR 5. Ambiguous phrases.** In paragraph 4, "the chief bishops of the province of Elam and of Merv" should become "the chief bishop of Elam, a rival for the post, and the chief bishop of Merv" (AMR 13018–13019; CH 40262–40263). In paragraph 5, "A later historian says" follows a named Mari, and the listener may take it for him. Use "Another historian of his church, five centuries later, says" (Amr).

**MINOR 6. Craft (paragraph 8, opening sentence).** "Timothy's answer speaks of the throne ..., and of the Book of the Gospels ..." reads as a report about the record, with two glosses stacked before the quote. Keep both plain senses, but work them in as action, for example: "Timothy wrote back. The book of the four Gospels of the life of Jesus, read in worship, would stand in for the missing bishop, laid on the throne, the bishop's chair in the church:" and then Q3.

**Sentences:** none runs past about 30 words. The longest are three sentences of 30. No finding.
