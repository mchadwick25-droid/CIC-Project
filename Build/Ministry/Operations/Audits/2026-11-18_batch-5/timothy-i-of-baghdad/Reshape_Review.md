# Reshape review: Timothy I of Baghdad

Targeted review of the reshaped `timothy-i-of-baghdad.brief.txt` (486 words) against `timothy-i-of-baghdad.brief.v2.txt`, `Reshape.md`, `Outline.md`, the Timothy entry in `Hearts_Plain.md`, Mark's job for Timothy in `CFT_Batch5_Launch_2026-11-18.md` ("a patriarch under the caliphs, his debate before the caliph, and the bishops he sent toward Central Asia and China"), and Decision-Log "Batch 5 hearts…", 2026-11-18, items 1–10.

## The three lines (one hearing, as a listener)

- **Who:** Timothy, head of the Church of the East for more than forty years, ruling it from Baghdad under five Muslim caliphs, from about 780 to the early 820s.
- **What happened:** He won the office with a trick (sacks of "money" that held stones, as Mari tells it). A two-year split followed, with curse and counter-curse and Christians killing and jailing one another. Then he sent monks out as bishops south of the Caspian Sea and let two brothers make seven bishops there, with the Gospel book standing in for the missing third bishop.
- **Why the connection matters:** He handed the power to make bishops out to the far edges of his church, which reached toward the Turks and China, so far that a stone in China still named a dead patriarch.

All three lines can be written. But the second part of Mark's job, "his debate before the caliph", does not reach the listener: one hearing leaves only the passing clause "argued for his faith at the caliphs' court", with no caliph and no debate. See finding 1.

## Verdict: FIX

One BLOCKING finding (the court debates). The rest is MINOR.

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKING: the court debates are part of the job and the heart

`Reshape.md` drops the court paragraph whole. That paragraph is not a secondary episode:
- Mark's job names "his debate before the caliph" as one of its three parts.
- The approved heart (`Hearts_Plain.md`) gives it in the essence, with both witnesses: "A later list of his writings names a debate with the caliph al-Mahdi, and a later historian says the next caliph sent for him most days to talk about religion".
- The outline's essence, beat 4 and What came of him item 5 ("Defended: the Christian faith before the caliphs") all carry it, and its Length line counts "the court, the debate" as parts of the job.

What remains, "Historians of his church say he argued for his faith at the caliphs' court.", points ahead to a beat that never comes. It names no caliph and no debate. After the cut, the court gets one sentence while the election trick gets four.

**Restoration.** Insert v2's two sentences as their own paragraph, between the split paragraph (ending "…Christians fought, killed and jailed one another.") and "Thomas of Marga, a monk who wrote…". Use v2's wording, with one change. With the Merv accusation gone, al-Mahdi is no longer introduced, so he needs "the caliph", which is the outline's own wording (essence: "a debate with the caliph al-Mahdi"):

> A list of his writings, made five centuries later, names a debate with the caliph al-Mahdi. Another historian of his church, five centuries later, says the next caliph, al-Hadi, sent for him on most days to put hard questions to him about religion.

This keeps the two accounts apart as the outline's Do not say requires (D4), says nothing of what was said (N1), and keeps the order of reigns (al-Mahdi, then al-Hadi) after the split. "Another historian" points back to Mari, who is still in the story. It adds 43 words.

### 2. MINOR: the Merv bishop, named once and no longer acting

The record's force is not softened by dropping Merv's accusation and his removal. The trick, the curse and counter-curse ("he cut them off in turn": Timothy's own force), and the fighting, killing and jailing all stand, and the accusation was an Also-told item that failed. Dropping it is right.

But the sentence that keeps him now does no work, and it is hard to follow by ear. "Two opponents, the chief bishop of Elam, a rival for the post, and the chief bishop of Merv, refused…" stacks three appositions, so a listener can hear three or four people. The man from Merv never appears again. The next sentence goes on with Elam and the thirteen bishops, who carry the size of the opposition. The kit's rule that every name earns its place applies to a figure given by role, too.

Replace:
> Two opponents, the chief bishop of Elam, a rival for the post, and the chief bishop of Merv, refused to worship or work with him.

with:
> The chief bishop of Elam, a rival for the post, refused to worship or work with him.

The words are taken from the checked sentence and only the Merv clause is removed. It is held by M4: Timothy's own letter to Ephrem of Elam says he separated himself from the patriarch's communion "for two years, more or less". That is a stronger witness than the sentence had before. It saves 10 words.

### 3. MINOR: China is said once, then left

Mark's job names "the bishops he sent toward Central Asia and China". The heart's line "His letters name a church reaching toward the Turks and China" is kept in the essence, so this is not lost. But it is never told: the body stops at Gilan and Dailom, and the hook's stone in China is never picked up again. If time allows after finding 1, one v2 sentence would do it (held by outline beat 7, M14, D7; it follows "They made seven bishops."):

> From Timothy's letters, Thomas learned of a chief bishop of Beth Sinaye, which scholars take to be China.

It adds 18 words. This is optional, not required.
