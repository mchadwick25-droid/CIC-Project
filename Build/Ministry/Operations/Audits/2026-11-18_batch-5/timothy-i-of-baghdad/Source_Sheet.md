# Source sheet: Timothy I of Baghdad

Researcher's sheet for batch 5. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline. The job (Mark, 2026-11-18): the church at its widest: a patriarch under the caliphs, his debate before the caliph, and the bishops he sent toward Central Asia and China.

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own. Latin and French quotes keep the OCR's spelling, with obvious OCR faults corrected in [brackets]; double spaces are collapsed and words split at line ends are joined. Nothing else is changed.

**Languages.** Timothy wrote in Syriac. Of his own words the Library holds: the synod letter of 790 (TIM), in Syriac with Chabot's French; the Syriac OCR is only partly legible, so it is read in Chabot's French, checked against the legible Syriac where possible. His letter to Yahbalaha survives here only as Thomas of Marga quotes it, in Budge's English, with Assemani's Latin of the same lines (AS1). His letter to the monks of Mar Maron survives here only in Mingana's English; Mingana printed a Syriac extract, but the OCR of it is garbled (MIN 4064–4069). Mari's Arabic is not usable in the OCR (MARI file header), so Mari is read in Gismondi's Latin. Amr's Arabic is partly legible (AMRA), and is checked against Gismondi's Latin.

**Witness kinds.** OWN = Timothy's own words (TIM; the letter in TM; the letter in MIN). CONTEMP = the Xi'an stele, set up in China in 781 (LEG). LATER = Thomas of Marga (about 840, twenty to sixty years later; he was secretary to the catholicos Abraham, who told him some of these stories, TM 22645, 20163); Elias of Nisibis (about two centuries later); Mari ibn Sulayman (twelfth century, more than three centuries later); Michael the Syrian (twelfth century, of the Syriac Orthodox church, a rival church); Bar Hebraeus (thirteenth century, about four and a half centuries later, also Syriac Orthodox); 'Abdisho of Nisibis's catalogue and his *Collection of Canons* (MAI) (early fourteenth century); Amr ibn Matta (fourteenth century, about five centuries later). MODERN = Assemani (1725, 1728), Abbeloos and Lamy (1877), Legge (1888), Budge (1893), Chabot (1902), Malech (1910), Mingana (1926): used only for dates, glosses and judgements, and marked as the editor's.

## 1. Setting

S1. **Who and when.** EL 3716–3722: "Institutus est Timotheus die dominica 7° iyyar anni 1091 Graecorum; et erat 4 dies 27us sa'ban anni 163 Saracenorum. Et vixit in catholicatu 42 annos syriacos et 8 menses et 2 dies: et obiit die praeparationis 9° kanun II anni 1134 Graecorum; et erat dies 23us sa'ban anni 207 Saracenorum." LATER (two centuries). CH 40254–40261: "Timothée, originaire de Hazza, dans l'Adiabène, évêque de Beit Bagas, fut élu et intronisé à Séleucie, après huit mois de violentes contestations, le dimanche 7 [mai] 780". MODERN. Documented: enthroned Sunday 7 May 780. Plain: "in 780". The death date is Contested (D2).

S2. **Five caliphs.** MARI 12986: "vixit itaque dominantibus Mahdi, Hadi, Rasid, Amin, Mamun". BH 10261–10263: "quinque regum Arabum successive [a]equalis fuerat, nimirum Mohdii, Hadii, Raschidi, Amini et Mamonis." LATER. Documented as Mari's and Bar Hebraeus's account. AMR 12958 lists four (Hadi, Rashid, Amin, Ma'mun), because Amr puts his whole reign after al-Mahdi's death; but AMR 13018 itself dates the consecration "[sub Mahdi chalipha]". Plain: "under five caliphs, from al-Mahdi to al-Ma'mun".

S3. **Where.** Born to a leading family of Hazza in Adiabene: MARI 13068 "e genere optimatum Hazzae"; AMR 13009 "e proceribus Hazzae". First see Beth Beghash: TM 17227–17228. Enthroned at Seleucia (CH 40258; BH 9612 "deduxerunt Seleuciam") or al-Mada'in (AMR 13017 "Modainae"), the twin royal city on the Tigris. Lived and was buried at Baghdad: MARI 12988–12990 "sepultus est in coenobio Keliljesu quod ipse restauraverat, ibique sedem fixit, prius autem in praedio Umm Ga'far commorabatur"; AMR 12960 "sepultus est in coenobio Kaliljesu Bagdadi". LATER. Documented. AS2 42357–42364 (Assemani, MODERN) says his predecessor was the first patriarch elected at Baghdad, and that Timothy built the Kelilisho monastery there and fixed his home in it. Plain: "Baghdad, the caliphs' new capital"; "Seleucia, the old royal city where the patriarchs were enthroned".

S4. **The tradition.** CEN, entry `the-church-of-the-east-on-the-silk-road`: name "The Church of the East on the Silk Road"; the sibling story `alopen` says "the Church of the East". Plain: "the Church of the East". The census floor note: "'Nestorian' ... was given to it by opponents rather than chosen by itself." Avoid "Nestorian" in the narrator's voice.

S5. **Under a caliph who forced conversions.** MS 102–108: "En l'an 1090, Mahdî vint à Alep, et les Tanoukayê sortirent à sa rencontre; ils habitaient sous des tentes dans les environs d'Alep. ... Alors quelqu'un lui dit : « Tous ces gens sont chrétiens ». Il fut enflammé de colère et leur ordonna de se faire musulmans. Il les y contraignit par les tortures, et les hommes apostasièrent au nombre d'environ cinq mille". LATER (about four centuries), from a rival church, but the event concerns Christian Arabs, not either church. Documented as Michael the Syrian's account. Greek year 1090 is 778/779, the year before Timothy's election. Optional context for what was at stake at the caliph's court.

S6. **The patriarch and the caliph's court.** CH 40268–40271: Timothy "sut ramener peu à peu les dissidents et se concilier les faveurs des Khalifes". MODERN, Chabot's judgement. AMR 13024–13026: "apud chaliphas et reges in honore erat propter eximias [doctrinam et] virtutes, ac idoneitatem responsionum quaestionibus quas ipsi proponebant". LATER. Documented as Amr's account.

## 2. Recorded moments, in time order

M1. **Schooling and the old monk.** TM 17166–17170: his uncle George, bishop of Beth Beghash, "sent the boy Timothy from him to Rabban Mar Abraham the Expositor, when he was in Bashosh". At Beth 'Abhe an old monk told him (TM 17205–17212): "thou shalt become Patriarch of all the country of the East ... Forty and two years shalt thou stand at the head of all the pastures of Christ". LATER; a foretelling, Thomas's account. Keep in Thomas's voice if used at all; left out of the outline.

M2. **Bishop of Beth Beghash.** TM 17220–17228: George resigned on grounds of age, "And the young man Timothy was appointed Bishop of the country of Beth Beghash." LATER. Documented as Thomas's account.

M3. **The election by a trick (three versions).**
- MARI 13079–13086: "Timotheus vero pecunias pollicitus Bairaui ac scholasticis astute ostendit saccos saxis ac lapidibus plenos velut nummis ac denariis refertos ... Mox advenerunt metropolitae Bagarmae, Hulwanae, Damasci et Marw, episcopique complures et Timotheum octo post menses consecrarunt. Postmodum pecuniam repetentibus respondit ille: « sacerdotium nequaquam pecuniis venditur », eisque ostendit saccos non nisi saxa lapidesque continere: illi porro videntes fraudem ipsi e sententia successisse destiterunt." LATER (twelfth century).
- AMR 13013–13016: "versute egit cum archidiacono et scholasticis, et in suam domum introductis ostendit sacculos saxis et lapidibus repletos, suasitque pecuniam continere quam eis erogaturus esset." LATER.
- BH 9598–9613: "marsupia nummis aeneis implevit et sigillo munivit; porro ea secum ferens scholasticos nocte ad se clanculo arcessivit eisque dixit : « totam hanc copiam zuzarum et denariorum vobis collegi, illamque in scholam vestram expendere equidem cupio. ...» Illi autem decepti fidem verbo ejus adhibuerunt ... episcopos vi adduxerunt, Timotheum vero assumpt[um] deduxerunt Seleuciam et catholicum proclamarunt." LATER.
- TM 17292–17302 (chapter title at 17243: "OF HOW TIMOTHY OBTAINED POSSESSION OF THE PATRIARCHATE BY FRAUD"): "since the Patriarchate was set apart for Timothy, truth acted with subtlety and performed its work." He told the favourite, Isho'yahbh, privately: "do thou excuse thyself, and become one of my supporters, and I will make thee Metropolitan of Adiabene". LATER (about two generations), and approving.
- CH 40261–40262: the metropolitans "qu'il avait, dit-on, séduits par ses promesses". MODERN.
Documented that later witnesses of his own church tell of a trick; the means (stones, copper coins, a promised see) differ (D1). Q1 comes from Mari.

M4. **The schism and the bloodshed.** TIM 40412–40415 (Timothy's own words, to Ephrem of Elam): those who came "firent avec empressement l'ordination du patriarche; et vous qui, par négligence, n'étiez pas venus, vous avez été troublés, et vous vous êtes séparés vous-mêmes de la communion du patriarche pendant deux ans, plus ou moins." OWN. MARI 13087–13098: Ephrem and Solomon of Hadatha resisted; Joseph, metropolitan of Merv, to whom Timothy had broken a promise ("quem Timotheus quaedam ipsi pollicitus demum fefellerat"), joined them, ordained a rival bishop of Mosul, and "Timotheum exauctoratum pronuntiarunt"; Timothy deposed Joseph. MARI 13024–13028: Ephrem and thirteen bishops "Timotheo anathema dixerunt; Timotheus contra, convocatis episcopis quindecim, illos excommunicavit. Inde inter christianas plebes contentiones, caedes, hominumque incarcerationes consecutae sunt." LATER. Documented as Mari's account; the two years are Timothy's own words. Never leave out the killings and imprisonments.

M5. **Peace with Ephrem.** MARI 13029–13036: "Res demum in hunc modum cum Ephraemo composita fuit, ut is Timotheum ad ecclesiam 'Abbadi deduceret, atque super eumdem coram se consistentem preces ordinationis recitaret; quod Timotheus ad mala praecidenda, animi demissione acceptavit." Ephrem then fell on his knees before him, and Timothy raised him and embraced him. AMR 13021–13024 likewise: by patience and letters Timothy won his opponents, "adeoque metropolitae Gondisaporis morigeratus est ut ille eum in medio omnium constituerit precem fundens super caput eius". LATER. Documented as Mari's and Amr's account. BH 9722–9733 tells it of a "Thomas metropolita" (D3).

M6. **The accusation before the caliph.** MARI 13014–13018: Joseph, "nulla iam sibi spe relicta, Timotheum ut perderet apud Mahdi accusavit : quod ei minime successit, at ipsius Mahdi opera mahumetanam sectam amplexus, ab eodem splendide remuneratus". BH 9823–10021 (no caliph named): Joseph, now a Muslim, told the caliph that the Christians "pro triumpho regni Graecorum die noctuque preces fundunt"; when the caliph threatened them, a Byzantine prisoner said the Nestorians "Arabibus magis sunt propinqui quam nobis"; then Joseph forged "epistolam nomine Timothei catholici ad imperatorem Graecorum" urging him to attack, planned to plant it in Timothy's cell with a eunuch, but "detexit Deus illorum artificium", and the caliph learned that the charges against the Christians "fundamento carere". LATER. Documented as Mari's account (accusation before al-Mahdi) and Bar Hebraeus's (the forged letter). BH's charge against Joseph's morals (BH 9823–9825) is left out.

M7. **The debate with the caliph: what is held.**
- 'Abdisho of Nisibis's catalogue, as Assemani prints it, AS1 29750–29751: "De Astris. Et Disputationem cum Mohdio." The same entry is quoted by BHN 10402–10405: "Timotheus fecit librum de astris, et Disputationem cum Mohdio, et De judiciis ecclesiasticis, et tomos synodicos. Habet praeterea biscentum circiter epistolas in duos tomos distributas". LATER (early fourteenth century). Documented: a disputation with al-Mahdi was listed among his works.
- AMR 13026–13027 with 12935–12940: "Saepe admodum eum accersebat Hadi chalipha ut cum eo colloqueretur de religione, deque aliis multis inquireret, [disceptaretque] multaque proponebat problemata ardua et quaestiones implicatissimas, quibus [omnibus] satisfaciebat responsionibus [certis et] omnem controversiam prorsus dirimentibus; quot autem eos inter disquisitiones intercesserint enarrare longum esset : has excepit omnes [et singulas] liber eius magnus [sub eius nomine notus,] quem quisque legerit ac meditatus fuerit [mirabilium assequetur rerum notitiam,] et ipsi virtutis ac amplissimae doctrinae laudem profitebitur." Arabic, AMRA 3776–3781: "وكان ... الهادي في اكثر الايام ... به اليه ويحاوره في الدين ويبحث معه [وناظره] في اشياء كثيرة" ("al-Hadi on most of the days [summoned] him to himself and talked with him about religion and examined with him [and disputed with him] many things"; the verb is illegible in the OCR, Gismondi's Latin has "accersebat"). LATER (about five centuries). Documented as Amr's account.
- AS1 29814–29830 (Assemani, MODERN): "Disputationem cum Mohdio, hoc est, ab ipso Timotheo cum Mohdio Chalipha habitam. Amrus ... Hadium pro Mohdio legit". Assemani takes Amr's al-Hadi debates to be the same disputation. MODERN judgement (D4).
- MARI 12990–12992: "Et Timotheus quidem coram hominibus orationes habere non valebat, sed plures de qualibet facultate scripsit tractatus, nec non librum de sideribus." LATER. Documented as Mari's account.
- **The text of the disputation itself is not held** (N1).

M8. **Al-Hadi and Harun (left out).** MARI 13043–13050: al-Hadi ordered a reliquary of martyrs thrown in the river and fell ill three times (a wonder). AMR 12941–12955: Harun asked him, "quaenam e religionibus ea est quam Deus probat?" and he answered "ea cuius leges et praecepta conformia sunt rationi quam servat Deus agens cum suis creaturis". Already told in the tradition entry's documented story "Which Religion Does God Approve?" (CEN); left out.

M9. **Synod of 790: rules for choosing a patriarch.** TIM 40417–40422: "De peur que quelque chose de semblable n'arrive de nos jours ou après nous, ou que, par la négligence des uns ou la précipitation des autres, il ne se produise un schisme dans l'Église et que la division ne s'accroisse, nous avons établi cette définition". Canon I (40424–40460): when a patriarch dies, the bishop of Kashkar writes first to the metropolitan of Elam and the others named, and no one may elect before they come or send consent. Canon II (40462–40471): no bishop may ask the patriarch-elect for sees, monasteries or gifts; "Celui qui donnerait ou promettrait ... sera déposé". OWN. Documented. CH 40480–40486: canon II "fut sans doute inséré pour donner satisfaction aux Élamites qui accusaient Timothée d'avoir obtenu le patriarcat en corrompant les évêques ... par ses présents et ses promesses". MODERN, Chabot's guess. Date: Chabot 790 (D5).

M10. **Before the caliph, six times.** TIM 40540–40548 (Timothy's own words, to Ephrem): "nous ne nous sommes point abstenus d'aller près de vous ... par orgueil ou par négligence, mais à cause de la reconstruction des églises détruites et pour d'autres motifs urgents. Car si le Dieu tout puissant a permis, et si le roi — que Dieu conserve sa vie! — [a] consenti à la reconstruction des églises, nous avons [dû] néanmoins aller six fois en sa présence pour cette affaire. Quand les liens dans lesquels nous sommes enserrés comme dans des entraves seront brisés, nous irons vous visiter". The legible Syriac at TIM 40149–40151 has "ܡܠܟܐ" (the king), "ܐܠܗܐ" (God) and "ܫܬ ... ܙܒܢܝܢ" (six times). OWN. Documented. Which churches: CH's note to this line (40562–40566) quotes Mari: at Hamduna's charge that Christians worshipped bones, "Rasidus ecclesias dirui mandavit, quod et factum est Basrae et Ailae aliisque in locis" (also MARI 12930–12940, where Harun then orders them rebuilt). The link of this letter to Harun's order is Chabot's (MODERN). Q2 comes from here.

M11. **The first bishop to Gilan and Dailom, and his murder.** TM 21494–21505: Timothy, seeing that the monk Shubhhal-Isho' "was instructed in the Syriac language and learning, and also in the Arabic and Persian tongues, determined to anoint him ... and to send him as a shepherd and teacher to the barbarian nations who had never received the bridle of the teaching concerning God, and into whose country none of the preachers and evangelists of the kingdom of heaven had gone since the time of the Apostles until the present." TM 21529–21535: Thomas had the story from the catholicos Abraham, who said he entered "with exceeding great splendour, for barbarian nations need to see a little worldly pomp". TM 21554–21556: he baptized, "built churches, and established priests and deacons in them". TM 21729–21744: after many years, setting out for home, he was ambushed and killed; TM 21752–21757: his disciples gathered from the killers his vestments "and sent them to Mar Timothy". LATER (about 840); Documented as Thomas's account. Healings at TM 21540–21546 are WONDER claims, left out. Gilan and Dailom: the lands south of the Caspian Sea (Budge's and Assemani's notes, TM 21860, AS2 52755–52760, MODERN).

M12. **Two brothers made metropolitans, with the right to make bishops.** TM 21956–21967: "[Mar Timothy] anointed them both Metropolitans, Kardagh of Gilan and Yahbhlaha of the people of Dailom. ... there went with them ... fifteen monks ... and among those men were those who were appointed to be Bishops of the countries beyond Gilan and Dailom. For I have read the letter of Mar Timothy the Catholicus to Mar Yahbhlaha". Yahbalaha's letter, TM 21970–21974: "many nations have been converted to the belief of the truth, and we want to appoint Bishops over them from among the ascetics who have come with us." Timothy's answer, TM 21975–21986 and TM 21995–22001: "Appoint as Bishops whomsoever ye and the pious Kardagh shall choose, and in the place of the third [person who should be present], let the Book of the Gospels be laid on the [episcopal] throne on the right hand; thus by the hand of God perform the ordination of the first Bishop ... May the Divine Spirit direct and govern His sanctifications by your means even as He did with the blessed Apostles." AS1 30003–30007 (Assemani's Latin of the same Syriac): "Episcopum nempe quemcumque elegeritis tibi simul et Episcopo Cardago fas sit consecrare, et pro tertio Episcopo Codex Evangeliorum super sella ad dexterum latus collocetur". TM 22001–22005: they ordained "Thomas, Zacchaeus, Shem, Ephraim, Simon, Ananias and David, bishops of the countries which had been taught by their hands". OWN (the letter, as Thomas quotes it, having read it) and LATER. Documented. The letter's first clause is translated in opposite senses (D6). Q3 comes from here.

M13. **Elijah of Mukan, with the merchants.** TM 22581–22592: after sending Yahbalaha and Kardagh, Timothy "wisely determined that he would appoint to the city of Mokan also a shepherd"; Mukan is "on the border of the peoples of Dailom, and its inhabitants are barbarians". TM 22636–22654: Elijah asked to be ordained at his own monastery at Pentecost; Timothy waited, had him ordained there, confirmed him, "and sent him off with certain merchants who were going into the countries in which Mokan was situated. And the blessed Mar Abraham the Catholicus related to me concerning him, that instead of a cross on his neck he used to hang upon his breast a complete Book of the Gospels in a small size; and he took with him a cross of brass which ... he placed on the top of the staff which was in his hand, and he used to drive it into the ground in front of him and pray". LATER (Thomas, from Abraham). Documented as Thomas's account. Opening (b).

M14. **A metropolitan for China, a bishop for Yemen.** TM 20156–20164: from Thomas's monastery "Mar Eliya ... was elected Bishop of Mukan, David was elected to be Metropolitan of Beth Sinaye — now I have learned concerning this man from the Epistles of Mar Timothy — together with Peter his disciple, who was alive and held the office of Bishop of the country of Yaman and of San'a when I was secretary to Mar Abraham". Budge's note TM 20186: "i. e., China." AS1 29778–29783 (Assemani): "Peter in Jaman et Sana, hoc est, in Arabiam Felicem, et David in Sinarum regnum". LATER. Documented that Thomas learned of a David, metropolitan of Beth Sinaye, from Timothy's letters. Thomas does not say who ordained David or when; "China" is Budge's and Assemani's gloss (D7).

M15. **The bishops of Fars.** BH 9750–9821: "Fertur ad Timothei usque tempus episcopos regionis Persarum alba gessisse vestimenta, sicut presbyteri saeculares, carnem manducasse et duxisse uxores, et praeterea catholico Seleuciae non paruisse. Dicebant enim : « nos Thomae apostoli discipuli sumus, et nihil nobis cum sede Maris commune est. » Illos Timotheus sibi conciliavit et conjunxit, eisque metropolitam ordinavit aliquem nomine Simeonem ... Porro permisit ei ut quando ordinaret episcopos, ipse illos perficeret ita ut non adigerentur ... adire catholicum ut ab eo perficerentur. Atque haec consuetudo usque in praesentem diem viget." LATER, and given as hearsay ("Fertur"). MIN 1719–1748 (Mingana, MODERN): the Syriac means "the province of Fars", not all Persia; these were the metropolitans of Fars, whose see oversaw "the bishoprics of North-West India", and this is "the earliest reference to the Christians of Fars and North-West India, calling themselves Christians of St. Thomas." Documented as Bar Hebraeus's report; the scope is Contested (D8).

M16. **The Turks and other kings.** MARI 13041–13043: "Porro Timotheus Haqanum regni turcarum regesque alios ad fidem adduxit, a quibus epistolae ei adveniebant, homines praeterea quamplurimos christiana doctrina instituit." MARI 12986–12988: "nec fuit rex ad quem non scripserit aut quem ad fidem non perduxerit". LATER (more than three centuries), with no earlier held witness for the Khagan. Documented as Mari's claim; Inferential-Thin as fact. Assemani (AS1 29769–29783, MODERN) says Thomas of Marga's missions confirm what Amr and Mari say of kings brought to the faith.

M17. **His own word for the width of his see.** MIN 1684–1691: Timothy, "in his letter to the monks of Mar Maron, concerning the addition of the formula Crucifixus es pro nobis to the trisagion, writes as follows : 'And also in all the countries of Babylon, of Persia, and of Assyria, and in all the countries of the sunrise, that is to say, — among the Indians, the Chinese, the Tibetans, the Turks, and in all the provinces under the jurisdiction of this Patriarchal See, there is no addition of Crucifixus es pro nobis'". MIN 1703–1704: "This letter is not yet printed. I read the text in an unpublished MS. of my own collection". OWN, known here only through Mingana's English from his own manuscript. Documented as Mingana's report. The phrase "the Indians, the Chinese, the Tibetans, the Turks" is already quoted in the tradition entry's documented story (CEN); not used as a quote here.

M18. **Death and burial.** MARI 12983–12988: "Eodem anno quo Mamun Bagdadum ingressus est obiit Timotheus a suscepto primatu anno quadragesimotertio, aetatis suae nonagesimo quinto ... sepultus est in coenobio Keliljesu quod ipse restauraverat". EL 3720–3722: "obiit die praeparationis 9° kanun II anni 1134 Graecorum" (Friday 9 January 823). AMR 12959–12961: "Obiit anno lunari 205 ... et sepultus est in coenobio Kaliljesu Bagdadi, officio functus annis tribus supra quadraginta cum mensibus septem". BH 10258–10261: "obiit Timotheus Nestorianorum catholicus, anno Arabum biscentesimo quarto (inchoato 28 Jun. 819)". LATER. Burial at Baghdad: Documented. Year: Contested (D2). Age 95: Documented as Mari's account (CH 40361 follows it).

M19. **The stele in China names his predecessor.** LEG 3450–3451: "(In Syriac.) In the days of the Father of Fathers, my lord Hanan-Yeshu', Katholikos, Patriarch." LEG 3444–3446: "Erected in the second year of the period Chien-chung (a. d. 781) of the great T'ang dynasty, the year-star being in Tso-yo, on the seventh day of the first month, being Sunday"; Legge's note, LEG 3462: "Hsin-yu ( = A. D. 781)". CONTEMP (the stone itself, in Legge's English; the Syriac is not in the OCR). Legge's note, LEG 3466–3478: Hananisho died before 781, "whereas here is this monument erected in 781 ... The news of the patriarch's death had not reached them at Ch'ang-an." BHN 9519–9548 (Abbeloos and Lamy): the stone names Hananisho in Greek year 1092; Elias shows he died in 1091 and Timothy was elected in May 1091, and "satis prolixum tempus requisitum fuerit ut fama obitus Ananjesu ex Assyria ad regionem adeo dissitam pervenerit". The stone's naming is Documented; that the news had not yet arrived is the editors' explanation (MODERN). Hananisho's death dates differ by source (Legge follows Assemani's 778; BHN follows Elias), but every held reckoning puts it before the stone. Opening (a).

## 3. What came of him (heaviest first)

I1. **Bishops who could make bishops, far from Baghdad.** Timothy's letter let the two metropolitans ordain bishops in their own lands, with the Gospel book in place of the third bishop (M12); they ordained seven (TM 22001–22005), and bishops were appointed "of the countries beyond Gilan and Dailom" (TM 21965–21967). The metropolitan of Fars was allowed to complete his own bishops' ordinations without coming to the catholicos, "Atque haec consuetudo usque in praesentem diem viget" (BH 9814–9820, thirteenth century). Thomas lists many monks of his monastery sent out as bishops in Timothy's time: Mukan, Beth Sinaye, Yemen and San'a, Beth Slokh, Beth Garmai (TM 20156–20167). Documented as Thomas's and Bar Hebraeus's accounts. This is the heaviest because later witnesses carried it: Thomas read the letters and built two chapters on them; Bar Hebraeus saw the Fars custom still in use.

I2. **His letters became the record of the missions.** Thomas of Marga learned of David of Beth Sinaye "from the Epistles of Mar Timothy" (TM 20160–20161) and quotes his letter to Yahbalaha (TM 21967–21986). 'Abdisho counts "biscentum circiter epistolas in duos tomos" (BHN 10404–10405; AS1 29897–29900). CH 40366–40371: "La collection partielle de ses Lettres ... constitue un des plus précieux documents pour l'histoire de l'Église de Perse à cette époque." Documented (Thomas); Chabot's judgement MODERN.

I3. **The rule for choosing a patriarch.** **Defended:** the unity of the church against schism (M9). 'Abdisho cites the canon in his collection (CH 40425–40429, 40289–40291), and the held text of that collection carries it: MAI 18031–18057, headed "CANON ex synodo TIMOTHEI" (OCR "zx syxono ΤΙΜΌΤΗΕΙ"), beginning "Ne in posterum contingat , quod nostris hisce diebus accidit , et ex quorumdam negligentia , vel ex aliorum festinatione schisma oriatur in ecclesia, aut dissensiones augeantur , canonem etiam istum sanximus" and setting out the bishop of Kashkar's letters to the metropolitan of Elam and the others "descriptos in epistola pragmatica" (Mai's Latin of the same rule as TIM 40417–40460). LATER (early fourteenth century). Assemani, from 'Abdisho's epitome of the canons, reports two synods of Timothy, in the Arab years 170 and 189 (AS1 29865–29871). Documented.

I3a. **Six more provinces under chief bishops.** MAI 16273–16278 ('Abdisho of Nisibis, *Collection of Canons*, in Mai's Latin), after the provinces made by Saliba-Zacha: "Postea Timotheus alias sex sedes instituit, quarum quatuor interiere , reliquae duae manent: Armeniae nimirum, quae est XIII., et post istam Sciamae ( Syriae sive Damasci ), quae est XIV. Atque hae sunt sedes primariae provinciarum". LATER (early fourteenth century, about five centuries after Timothy). Documented as 'Abdisho's account. The parenthesis "Syriae sive Damasci" glosses "Sciamae" (Sham); whether it is 'Abdisho's or Mai's is not clear from the OCR. The four that died out are not named. Plain: "'Abdisho of Nisibis, five centuries later, says Timothy set up six more provinces under chief bishops; four had died out by his day, and two still stood, Armenia and Damascus." This changes what the story may say: it is the only held line on the provinces Timothy added, and it bears on the church at its widest (outline beat 7 and What came of him item 2).

I4. **The debates were remembered, not kept here.** 'Abdisho's catalogue names the disputation with al-Mahdi (M7); Amr says al-Hadi's debates filled "liber eius magnus" and that whoever reads it will acknowledge his learning (AMR 12938–12940). **Defended:** the Christian faith at the caliph's court, in Amr's words "a tuenda christianorum religione hic pater non supersedit" (AMR 12955). Documented as Amr's account; the text not held (N1).

I5. **The church under him.** CH 40362–40365: "L'Église nestorienne fut prospère et se développa sous sa direction, surtout par les missions dans l'Extrême-Orient." MODERN, Chabot's judgement; may be credited to "a modern editor".

I6. **Opponents.** Joshua bar Nun, who studied with him and succeeded him, hated him and wrote books against him, which he ordered burned when he fell ill (MARI 12955–12957, 12860–12861, 12882–12884; AMR 12965–12971; BH 10354–10361: he called him "Tolematheum, id est « Deo injuriosum »"). LATER. Side story; left out.

Who keeps him today: no held source says. Leave out.

## 4. Links to other people

- **Alopen** (on the tree; sibling link). The stele that tells of Alopen (sibling story) also names, in Syriac, the patriarch of its day: Timothy's predecessor (M19). Same church, same stone; no personal tie.
- **Thomas of Marga** (on the tree). Wrote of him; read his letters (TM 20160, 21967); his patron Abraham told him stories of Timothy's missionaries (TM 22645). Kind: read his letters; wrote of him.
- **Henana of Adiabene** (on the tree). Henana's live story cites "Timothy I, a later head of the church", on Sabrisho's condemnation of Henana's writings (CEN). Kind: wrote of him. Not used.
- **Nestorius** (on the tree, this batch). MARI 12880–12881 (in Mari's account of Joshua bar Nun's dispute): Timothy governed the church for forty-two years "ad normam doctrinae Diodori, Theodori ac Nestorii". Mari's claim. Kind: kept his teaching (Mari's word). Not used.
- Not on the tree, acting in the story: the caliphs al-Mahdi and al-Hadi; Ephrem of Elam; Joseph of Merv; Shubhhal-Isho'; Yahbalaha and Kardagh; Elijah of Mukan; David of Beth Sinaye; Simeon of Fars; Abraham the catholicos (Thomas's informant).

## 5. Where sources disagree, and how late they are

D1. **How he won the election.** Stones in sacks (MARI 13080; AMR 13015); sealed purses of copper coins promised to the school (BH 9599–9608); a promise of Adiabene to the favourite, Isho'yahbh (TM 17294–17302); promises to the metropolitans "dit-on" (CH 40261). All of his own church except Bar Hebraeus; all later. Tell one version, credited (Mari's, with Q1), and say the later histories tell it in different ways.

D2. **The year of his death.** Friday 9 January 823 (EL 3720–3721; CH 40361 "le vendredi 9 janvier 823"); Arab year 204, from June 819 (BH 10259–10260); Arab year 205 (AMR 12959; Budge's note TM 17318–17319 "A. D. 819–820, or 820–821"); the year al-Ma'mun entered Baghdad, Arab year 204 (MARI 12982–12984). CH's index gives "(7 mai 780–9 janv. 825)" (SO 49679), against his own note. Contested. Say "around 820" or "in the early 820s, one chronicle says on a Friday in January 823".

D3. **Who reconciled with him.** Ephrem, metropolitan of Elam (MARI 13024–13036; AMR 13019–13024); "Thomas metropolita" (BH 9722–9733). Follow Mari and Amr, or give the rival by role.

D4. **Which caliph the debate was with.** 'Abdisho names al-Mahdi (AS1 29751; BHN 10402); Amr tells of debates with al-Hadi (AMR 13026–13027); Assemani takes them to be one (AS1 29814–29830). Contested. Say both, each credited; never merge them.

D5. **The synod's date.** 'Abdisho, MAI 18711–18717: Timothy set it out "in sua synodica epistola quam in synodo ab ipso collecta anno Arabum 174., et ab eodem confirmata in altera synodo coacta anno Arabum 189." LATER (early fourteenth century); the primary held basis for 790 (Arab year 174 began in May 790). Chabot: 790, the year 174 of the Arabs, and a second synod in 804 (CH 40283–40287); he calls the Paris copy's 786 "une erreur" (SO 39953). BHN 10412–10413: "prima anno Christi 786, altera anno 804". AS1 29865–29871 (from 'Abdisho's epitome): Arab years 170 (786) and 189 (805), against MAI's 174 for the first. Contested. Say "about 790" or "early in his time".

D6. **The opening clause of the letter to Yahbalaha.** Budge: "the ordination of a Bishop doth not absolutely require [the presence of] three persons, and ye in your country are free from this" (TM 21975–21977). Assemani: "Episcopi ordinatio trium Episcoporum praesentiam omnino exigit; vos autem in iis terris estis, in quibus habere hunc Episcoporum numerum non potestis" (AS1 29997–30001). The two translations of the same Syriac say opposite things; the Syriac is not held legibly. Both agree on the Gospel book in place of the third bishop. Quote only that part.

D7. **China.** Thomas names David "Metropolitan of Beth Sinaye" and learned of him from Timothy's letters (TM 20158–20161). "China" is Budge's gloss (TM 20186) and Assemani's ("in Sinarum regnum", AS1 29884). Thomas does not say Timothy ordained or sent David. Say "a metropolitan for Beth Sinaye, which scholars take to be China", and "Thomas learned of him from Timothy's letters". Widely Accepted that Beth Sinaye means China; the sending is Inferential-Thin.

D8. **Fars or all Persia.** Bar Hebraeus's Syriac: Mingana reads "the province of Fars"; Assemani and Lamy rendered "the country of Persia" (MIN 1734–1737; BH 9751 "regionis Persarum"). Contested. Say "the bishops of Fars, in southern Persia", credited to Bar Hebraeus, who gives it as something "it is said".

D9. **Lateness.** Thomas wrote about 840, within living memory, from Timothy's letters and from the catholicos Abraham. Everything else about his life (election, schism, court) comes from histories written three to five centuries later: Mari (twelfth century), Bar Hebraeus (thirteenth, of the rival Syriac Orthodox church), Amr (fourteenth). Timothy's own words survive in his synod letter and in letters quoted by others.
