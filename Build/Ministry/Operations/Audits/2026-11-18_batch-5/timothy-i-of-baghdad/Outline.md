# Outline: Timothy I of Baghdad

Every line cites `Source_Sheet.md` (S = setting, M = moment, I = impact, D = disagreement, N = not held).

- **The essence:** For more than forty years, from 780, Timothy was the head of the Church of the East, the church of the Christians of Mesopotamia and Persia. He led it from Baghdad, under five caliphs, the Muslim rulers of the empire (S1, S2, S3: Documented). He argued for his faith at their court: a later list of his writings names a debate with the caliph al-Mahdi, and a later historian says the next caliph, al-Hadi, sent for him on most days to talk about religion (M7: Documented as those witnesses' accounts; the debate's text is not held, N1). And he sent monks out as bishops, church leaders for a region, to peoples south of the Caspian Sea. He let them make more bishops where they were, without the usual three bishops or a journey to him. His letters name a church that reached toward the Turks and China (M11, M12, M14, M17: Documented as Thomas of Marga's and Mingana's accounts; that any bishop he sent reached China is not held, N5).
- **Also told:** He won the office by a trick, as his own church's later histories tell it. His rivals then refused to worship or work with him for about two years, and Christians fought, killed and jailed one another (M3, M4: Documented as Mari's account; the two years are his own words). At a meeting of bishops he made a rule for choosing the head of the church, so that an election could not split it again. He also had to go before the caliph six times to get leave to rebuild churches that had been pulled down (M9, M10: Documented, his own letter). A chief bishop he had removed from office accused him before the caliph, and failed (M6: Documented as Mari's and Bar Hebraeus's accounts).
- **Length:** about 3 minutes (about 450 words). Reason: three parts of the job (the court, the debate, the missions), each with its own witness, plus the contested election and schism, which cannot be cut without softening the record. The debate is short because its text is not held. The essence fills the first 150 words.
- **Setting:** "around 780 to the early 820s" (S1, D2); "Baghdad, the caliphs' capital", "enthroned at Seleucia, the old royal city on the Tigris" (S3); "the Church of the East", the church of the Persian and Arab lands that looked to its patriarch, called the catholicos (S4); "under the Abbasid caliphs, from al-Mahdi to al-Ma'mun" (S2). He came from Hazza in Adiabene (S3); give no modern country, as no held source does.
**Words to explain** (work the gloss into the sentence where the word is first heard; or use the plain word instead)
- **Church of the East:** "the church of the Christians of Mesopotamia and Persia, which looked to its own head". Its heartland, not its whole reach (S3, S4).
- **patriarch / catholicos:** use "head of the church" or "patriarch, the head of the whole church". Avoid "catholicos" unless glossed: "catholicos, his church's title for its head" (TIM 40251, Timothy signs "catholicos, patriarche"). Not a pope of Rome; this church had its own head.
- **caliph:** "the caliph, the Muslim ruler of the empire, at Baghdad". In Q2 Timothy calls him "the king"; the narrator says the king is the caliph (M10).
- **Abbasid:** leave it out; "the caliphs at Baghdad" is enough.
- **bishop:** "a bishop, the church's leader for a city or region". Many of those Timothy sent were monks (M11–M13).
- **metropolitan:** use "chief bishop of a province", with the province named (TIM 40229–40240: Ephrem, "métropolitain" for "toute la province de Elam").
- **ordain / consecrate / enthrone:** "make a bishop" or "install as head of the church". At this time making a bishop needed three bishops present (Q3: "in place of the third bishop"; AS1 29997–30001) and, outside the patriarch's own provinces, his confirmation (BH 9815–9818). Say this; it is the point of the connection.
- **synod:** "a meeting of bishops".
- **canon:** "a church rule".
- **communion / out of communion:** "refused to worship or work with him" (TIM 40414–40415).
- **excommunicate / anathema:** "cut them off from the church" / "declared him cursed and cut off" (MARI 13026–13027).
- **depose:** "removed from office".
- **see:** "a bishop's city and region"; prefer "his first post, as bishop of Beth Beghash".
- **monk / monastery:** known; for Beth 'Abhe add nothing; say "a monastery".
- **apostles:** "Jesus's first followers", in "no preacher had come since the apostles" (M11).
- **Book of the Gospels:** "the book of the four Gospels, the life of Jesus", used in worship (M12, M13).
- **vestments:** "the robes and cloths he used at the altar" (TM 21754–21755).
- **ordination prayer** (M5): "the prayer said over him when he was made head of the church".
- **Father of Fathers** (opening): the stone's title for the head of the church; say so.
- **stele:** say "stone"; avoid "stele".
- **khagan:** "the khagan, the ruler of the Turks" (MARI 13041).
- **Fars:** "Fars, a province of southern Persia" (D8). **Gilan and Dailom, Mukan:** "lands south and west of the Caspian Sea" (M11, M13). **Beth Sinaye:** "Beth Sinaye, which scholars take to be China" (D7). **Seleucia:** "the old royal city on the Tigris, where the heads of the church were installed" (S3).
- **Shape:** Founder or leader. The trap is titles in place of acts: tell what he did (the trick, the six visits, the letter to the brothers, the bishops sent), not a list of offices.
- **Sibling:** `alopen` (on the tree). Its job: the first mission to China in 635, told through the Xi'an stele. This story's job: the church at its widest under Timothy, a century and a half later. Shared: the same stele. This story uses only the stele's Syriac line naming the patriarch (M19), never its Chinese text, its Alopen narrative, or any quote in Alopen's story.
- **Openings:** (a) Chosen. Early in 781, Christians in the Tang capital of China set up a great stone. On it, in Syriac, they named the head of their church, far away in Mesopotamia: Hananisho, "Father of Fathers". But Hananisho was dead; months before, a new patriarch had been enthroned (M19, S1: the stone is dated early in 781, the enthronement May 780). Then the essence: that patriarch was Timothy. Say the explanation (the news had not yet arrived) as the editors' reading: "the news, it seems, had not yet reached them". (b) Not chosen for the opening: the monk Elijah, sent to Mukan near the Caspian, walking behind a merchants' caravan with a small Book of the Gospels hanging on his chest instead of a cross (M13). Use in beat 6.

**Story beats** (beat 1 is the first minute)
1. **The essence, told at once.** After the stone: Timothy led the Church of the East for more than forty years, from about 780, under five caliphs (S1, S2). He lived at Baghdad, the caliphs' new capital, and was buried there (S3). His church's later historians say he argued for his faith at the caliphs' court (M7: Amr's and 'Abdisho's accounts). He sent monks far beyond the caliphs' lands as bishops (M11, M12). One sentence points ahead: his letters show a church that reached from Mesopotamia toward the Turks and China (M17, M14). Documented, as above.
2. **Bishop, then the trick.** He came from Hazza in Adiabene; his uncle, a bishop, sent him to school and later handed him his see of Beth Beghash (S3, M1, M2: Documented as Thomas of Marga's account). When the patriarch before him died, Timothy was one of several candidates (M3). Mari, a historian of his church writing more than three centuries later, says he showed the electors sacks he said held money for them, won, and was consecrated eight months later (M3). A chronicle of his church gives the day: Sunday, 7 May 780 (S1: Elias of Nisibis). When they asked for the money, he gave Q1 and showed them the sacks held only stones (M3). Other later histories tell the trick differently (D1). Documented as Mari's account.
3. **The split.** The metropolitan of Elam, a rival candidate, and a metropolitan of Merv, who, Mari says, had been promised something by Timothy and let down, refused him. They declared him deposed; he excommunicated them. Mari says fighting, killings and imprisonments followed among the Christians (M4). In his own letter Timothy says his rivals stayed out of communion "for two years, more or less" (M4: his own words). Peace came when the metropolitan of Elam said the ordination prayer over him again, and Timothy accepted it to end the trouble (M5: Documented as Mari's and Amr's account).
4. **Under the caliphs.** Shortly before Timothy's election, the caliph al-Mahdi had forced some five thousand Christian Arabs near Aleppo to become Muslims by torture, a later Syriac chronicle says (S5: optional, one sentence). The deposed metropolitan of Merv accused Timothy before al-Mahdi, failed, and became a Muslim (M6: Mari). Bar Hebraeus, a historian of a rival church writing much later, says this man forged a letter in Timothy's name urging the Byzantine emperor to attack, and that the plot came to nothing (M6). Then the debate: a list of his writings, made five centuries later, names his debate with al-Mahdi; a later historian says the next caliph, al-Hadi, sent for him on most days to talk about religion and to put hard questions to him, and that the debates filled a great book of his (M7, D4). Tell no word of what was said in the debate (N1). Mari adds that he could not speak well in public, but wrote on every subject (M7).
5. **The rule and the six visits.** At a synod, about 790 (D5), he made a rule: when a patriarch dies, no one may elect until the named metropolitans come or send consent, and no candidate may promise gifts, so that the church would not split again (M9: his own words). In the same letter he told the metropolitan of Elam why he had not visited: he was rebuilding destroyed churches. Q2 (M10). Documented, his own words.
6. **The bishops sent out.** He chose a monk who knew Syriac, Arabic and Persian and sent him as bishop to Gilan and Dailom, south of the Caspian Sea, to peoples Thomas of Marga says no preacher had reached since the apostles (M11). After many years, setting out for home, this bishop was ambushed and killed; his disciples sent his vestments to Timothy (M11). Timothy then made two brothers from the same monastery metropolitans, Kardagh of Gilan and Yahbalaha of Dailom; fifteen monks went with them (M12). When Yahbalaha wrote that many peoples had come to the faith and asked to make bishops, Timothy answered with Q3 (M12). They made seven bishops (M12). He sent another monk, Elijah, to Mukan with merchants; Elijah wore a small Book of the Gospels on his chest instead of a cross (M13: Thomas, from the catholicos Abraham). Documented as Thomas of Marga's account, written about 840 from Timothy's letters and Abraham's memories.
7. **The widest reach.** Thomas learned from Timothy's letters of a David, metropolitan of Beth Sinaye, which scholars take to be China (M14, D7); and of a bishop of Yemen (M14). Bar Hebraeus reports, as something "it is said", that the bishops of Persia (of the province of Fars, in southern Persia, as the modern scholar Mingana reads his Syriac) called themselves disciples of the apostle Thomas and refused the patriarch; Timothy won them over, gave them a metropolitan, and let him complete his own bishops' ordinations (M15, D8). Mari says he brought the khagan of the Turks to the faith and had letters from him (M16: Mari's claim, centuries later). In a letter Mingana quotes, Timothy himself counts the Indians, the Chinese, the Tibetans and the Turks among the peoples of his see (M17: paraphrase only).
8. **Death.** He died in the early 820s; one chronicle gives Friday, 9 January 823 (M18, D2). Mari says he was ninety-five. He was buried at Baghdad, in the monastery he had restored and made his home (M18, S3).

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; item 1 is pointed to in beat 1 and told in beats 6 and 7)
1. Heaviest: **bishops who could make bishops, far from Baghdad.** His letter let the brothers ordain bishops where they were, with the Gospel book in place of the missing third bishop; they ordained seven, and bishops were set over "the countries beyond Gilan and Dailom" (I1, M12). Bar Hebraeus, four and a half centuries later, says the custom he allowed in Fars, of a metropolitan completing his own bishops' ordinations, still held in his own day (I1, M15). The listener must hear why this mattered: a bishop's making normally needed three bishops (Q3 itself says so: "in place of the third bishop") and, outside the patriarch's own provinces, the patriarch's confirmation (M15), and these lands were remote (TM 22005–22006).
2. **His letters became the record.** Thomas of Marga, writing about twenty years after his death, built his account of the missions on Timothy's letters (I2). A later catalogue counts about two hundred of them (I2).
3. **Defended:** the unity of the church. His rule on electing a patriarch was meant to stop the kind of split he had lived through; a later canon collection cites it (I3, M9).
4. **Defended:** the Christian faith before the caliphs. The debates were remembered by title and by Amr's report; their text is not held here (I4, N1).
End on beat 8, his death and burial at Baghdad, after 1 has landed.

- **Q2.** TIM 40543–40546: "Car si le Dieu tout puissant a permis, et si le roi — que Dieu conserve sa vie! — [a] consenti à la reconstruction des églises, nous avons [dû] néanmoins aller six fois en sa présence pour cette affaire." Rendered from Chabot's French of the Syriac, checked against the legible Syriac words "king", "God" and "six times" (TIM 40149–40151): "Almighty God allowed it, and the king, may God keep him alive, agreed that the churches be rebuilt. **Connection to carry into the story:** Timothy passed the power to make bishops out from Baghdad to the edges of his church. To the metropolitans of Gilan and Dailom he gave leave to ordain bishops among the peoples they had taught, with the Gospel book standing in for the third bishop the rule required (M12, Q3); they made seven (M12). To the metropolitan of Fars he gave leave to complete his own bishops' ordinations, and Bar Hebraeus says the custom lasted to his own day (M15, I1). The listener must be told two things the record assumes: that a bishop was normally made by three bishops and confirmed by the patriarch, and that these lands lay very far from Baghdad: Thomas speaks of "the remoteness of the countries" (TM 22005–22006), and the stone of 781 still named a patriarch who had died before Timothy's enthronement (M19). Without both, the permission sounds like a formality. Carry it through the brothers' request and Timothy's answer, not a "this mattered because" sentence.

**Ending:** (a) Chosen: his death in the early 820s, aged ninety-five by Mari's count, under the fifth caliph, and his burial at Baghdad in the monastery he had restored and made his home (M18, S3). (b) Possible: the opening image seen again, the stone in China naming a patriarch who was already dead, set beside the letters that carried Timothy's word to the edges of his church (M19, I2). Use (b) only if it does not repeat the sibling story's ending on the stone.

**Who tells it**
- Timothy's own synod letter of about 790 (Chabot's edition): his own words, for the schism, the rule, and the six visits (M4, M9, M10).
- Thomas of Marga, a monk of the same monastery as the missionaries, writing about 840, about twenty years after Timothy's death, from Timothy's letters and from the catholicos Abraham (M11–M14). Say once, in beat 6: "Thomas of Marga, a monk who wrote about twenty years after Timothy's death and had read his letters".
- Mari ibn Sulayman (twelfth century) and Amr ibn Matta (fourteenth), historians of his own church, three to five centuries later: the election, the schism, the court and the debates (M3–M7). Credit the trick to Mari by name or as "a historian of his church, centuries later".
- Bar Hebraeus (thirteenth century), of the rival Syriac Orthodox church: the forged letter and the Fars bishops, the latter as hearsay (M6, M15).
- 'Abdisho of Nisibis's catalogue (fourteenth century): the title of the debate with al-Mahdi (M7). Say "a list of his writings, made five centuries later".
- The stele of 781: the church's own stone in China (M19).
- The doubt that is itself a scene: the election (three versions, D1). Tell one, credited, and one clause that later histories differ.

**Do not say**
- Any word in "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard (Mark, 2026-11-18).
- Not anything Timothy said to al-Mahdi, and not "he defended the Trinity", "he called Muhammad...", or any line from the *Apology* or *Dialogue*. Its text is not held (N1). Say only that a later list names the debate and that Amr says al-Hadi questioned him often and the debates filled a book (M7).
- Not "he debated al-Mahdi" in the narrator's voice as settled with details; and do not merge the al-Mahdi debate with al-Hadi's talks. Say "a list of his writings names a debate with al-Mahdi" and "a later historian says al-Hadi sent for him on most days" (D4).
- Not "Timothy sent bishops to China" or "Timothy ordained a metropolitan of China". Say "Thomas learned from Timothy's letters of a metropolitan of Beth Sinaye, which scholars take to be China" (M14, D7, N5).
- Not "he sent bishops to Central Asia" as fact. The bishops he sent went to Gilan, Dailom and Mukan, south and west of the Caspian Sea (M11–M13). The Turks are Mari's claim (M16) and, with the Tibetans, Timothy's own list of his see's peoples (M17).
- Not "he converted the Turkish khagan" in the narrator's voice. Say "Mari, centuries later, says..." (M16).
- Not "he bribed the electors". Say what Mari says: he showed sacks he said held money, and they held stones (M3). Not "with gold" (BH has copper coins, Mari stones, D1).
- Never drop the killings and imprisonments among the Christians during the split (M4), or the torture in al-Mahdi's forced conversions if that sentence is used (S5).
- Not "in 790 he held his first synod" as settled; say "about 790" (D5).
- Not "he died in 823" alone as settled; say "in the early 820s", and "one chronicle gives 9 January 823" (D2).
- Not "the Nestorian Church" in the narrator's voice; say "the Church of the East" (S4).
- Not "the news had not reached China" as the stone's own claim. It is the editors' explanation: "it seems the news had not yet reached them" (M19).
- Not "the first bishop he sent was martyred for the faith" as the narrator's verdict; say he was ambushed and killed on his way home, and his disciples sent his vestments to Timothy (M11). No healings (M11).
- Not "the caliph Harun destroyed the churches" in Q2's frame; Timothy names no caliph and no city. If the writer explains Q2, say only "destroyed churches" (M10).
- Not "he was born in 728" or any birth year (N4).
- Not "Patriarch of Baghdad" as a title; he was catholicos and patriarch of the Church of the East, enthroned at Seleucia, living at Baghdad (S3).
