# Outline check: Timothy I of Baghdad

## My own account, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (teaser and existing card), the source sheet, the job in the batch 5 launch, and what Timothy is known for.

**The essence.** Timothy was head (catholicos and patriarch) of the Church of the East for about forty-two years from 780. He led it from Baghdad under five caliphs. In those years the church reached its widest: he sent monks out as bishops to the peoples south of the Caspian Sea and gave them leave to make more bishops where they were, and his letters are how a later writer knew of a metropolitan for Beth Sinaye (China). He is on the tree as the patriarch of that widest church, and its voice at the caliph's court: a debate with the caliph al-Mahdi is listed among his works, and a later historian says al-Hadi sent for him most days to talk about religion. The text of the debate is not held.

**Also told.** He won office by a trick, as his church's later histories tell it, and the means differ. The election split the church: his opponents stayed out of communion with him for about two years (his own words), and Mari says Christians fought, killed and jailed one another. Peace came when he let the rival, Ephrem of Elam, pray over him. A metropolitan he had removed, Joseph of Merv, accused him before the caliph and failed. At a synod (about 790) he made a rule for choosing a patriarch so that an election could not split the church again. He went before the caliph six times to win leave to rebuild churches that had been pulled down. Bar Hebraeus reports, as hearsay, that he brought the bishops of Fars under the patriarch. He died in the early 820s and was buried at the Kelilisho monastery in Baghdad.

**What the sources hold.** All of this is held, at the confidence levels the sheet gives. The Aristotle translation that ruling 4 asked about is not held (sheet N7), so it cannot be told. Beth Sinaye as China is a modern gloss, and no held source says Timothy sent David there.

## Verdict: FIX

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Beats 3 and 4 put the Merv accusation after the peace, but Mari puts it before.** In Mari's text, which runs backwards by page, Joseph of Merv is removed from office, accuses Timothy before al-Mahdi, fails and becomes a Muslim (MARI 13088–13100, then 13009–13018). Only then do "the Christians" blame Timothy, Ephrem's thirteen bishops curse him, the killings follow, and peace is made (MARI 13018–13040). As written, beat 3 ends "Peace came..." and beat 4 then tells the accusation. A writer following the beats would imply it came after the reconciliation, and the listener would hear the split as already over. S5 (al-Mahdi's forced conversions, Greek year 1090, 778/779) also comes before the election, yet it sits in beat 4, after the events of 780.
*Correction:* Rewrite beat 3 as follows. Two opponents refused him: the metropolitan of Elam, himself a candidate (AMR 13018–13019; CH 40262–40263), and a metropolitan of Merv, whom Mari says Timothy had promised something and let down. The Merv man and his allies declared Timothy deposed, and Timothy removed him from office. He then accused Timothy before al-Mahdi, failed, and became a Muslim (MARI 13014–13018); Bar Hebraeus's forged letter stays optional, credited. After that, the metropolitan of Elam and thirteen bishops declared Timothy cursed and cut off, and he cut them off in turn. Fighting, killings and jailings followed (MARI 13024–13028). The split lasted "two years, more or less" (TIM 40414–40415), and peace came with the prayer (MARI 13029–13036). Beat 4 then holds only the court and the debate. Move the S5 sentence to the start of beat 2 as setting ("the year before, the caliph had..."), or cut it.

**B2. The Library sweep missed a held later witness: 'Abdisho of Nisibis's own *Collection of Canons*, in Mai's Latin.** The file is `cic/texts/mai_scriptorum-veterum-nova-collectio-tomus10-ebedjesu-synodus-timothei-lat_mai1838.txt`. The corpus map files it under `church-of-the-east-timur-to-hakkari`, as the volume of Timothy II's synod. The sheet's name search should have caught it. It holds three things the outline needs:
- Mai 16273–16278: "Postea Timotheus alias sex sedes instituit, quarum quatuor interiere, reliquae duae manent: Armeniae nimirum, quae est XIII., et post istam Sciamae (Syriae sive Damasci), quae est XIV." This is 'Abdisho, early fourteenth century, saying that Timothy set up six more chief bishops' provinces, and that two of them, Armenia and Damascus, still stood in his day. It bears directly on "the church at its widest", and nothing in the outline holds it.
- Mai 18024–18047: the text of Timothy's election canon, cited as "Canon ex synodo Timothei". I3 and What came of him item 3 ("a later canon collection cites it") rest on Chabot's note (CH 40425–40429), where this held text carries the claim.
- Mai 18711–18717: 'Abdisho dates the synod letter to Arab year 174 and its confirmation to a second synod in year 189. This is the primary basis for D5, which now rests on Chabot and Assemani.

*Correction:* Add the file to the sheet's Files list and its sweep under Used. Add a What came of him item, placed after item 1: "'Abdisho of Nisibis, five centuries later, says Timothy set up six more provinces under chief bishops; four had died out by his day, and two still stood, Armenia and Damascus (Mai 16273–16278). Documented as 'Abdisho's account." Allow one clause for it in beat 7. Add to Do not say: "Not 'he founded six provinces' in the narrator's voice; say ''Abdisho, five centuries later, says...'. Do not name the four that died out (not given)." Re-cite I3 and item 3 to Mai 18024–18047, and D5 to Mai 18711–18717.

**B3. "Throne" in Q3 has no gloss, and Q3 carries the connection.** In "let the Book of the Gospels be laid on the throne at the right hand", a listener will picture a king's throne. Budge marks it "[episcopal] throne" (TM 21982), and Assemani has "super sella" (AS1 30006). Under Mark's ruling, a word the listener needs must be explained the first time it is heard.
*Correction:* Add to Words to explain: "**throne** (Q3): the bishop's chair in the church (Budge's '[episcopal] throne', TM 21982; Assemani 'sella', AS1 30006). The narrator gives the plain sense beside the quote: the book was set on the bishop's chair, in the place of the missing third bishop." Add "throne" to the Do not say line on unglossed words.

### MINOR

**M1. The confirmation gloss says more than its source.** The ordain entry and the Connection say the patriarch's confirmation was needed "outside the patriarch's own provinces" (BH 9815–9818). Bar Hebraeus says only that bishops "of other provinces" had to go to the catholicos to have their ordination completed ("instar ceterorum episcoporum aliarum provinciarum adire catholicum ut ab eo perficerentur"). The editors' note calls this a confirmation. Thomas of Marga holds a nearer witness: Elijah, ordained at Beth 'Abhe, "went down to Mar Timothy who confirmed him" (TM 22642).
*Correction:* Write instead: "a new bishop normally went to the patriarch to have his making confirmed (TM 22642, Elijah; BH 9815–9818)." Drop "outside the patriarch's own provinces".

**M2. The length does not fit the beats.** Eight beats, three quotes, the four What came of him items and the B2 addition will not fit in about 450 words with the essence taking 150. Beat 6 alone needs about 120 words. That forces either cuts the outline does not name, or an overrun.
*Correction:* Set the length to about 3 minutes 30 (about 525 words), within Mark's "pressure, not a cap". Mark as first cuts, in this order: the S5 sentence, Bar Hebraeus's forged letter, Mari's line on public speaking, and the khagan. Do not fill to reach the length.

**M3. The Names field and the beats disagree.** Beats 4 and 7 name "Bar Hebraeus", and beat 7 names "Mingana". The Names field gives Bar Hebraeus by role and does not list Mingana.
*Correction:* In beats 4 and 7, use "a historian of a rival church, centuries later" and "a modern scholar".

**M4. Passing words without a gloss.**
- "Syriac" (opening): "Syriac, the language of his church".
- "Byzantine emperor" (beat 4): BH has "imperatorem Graecorum" (BH 9943–9944); say "the emperor of the Greeks" and add nothing to it.
- "Tang" (opening): "China's capital" is enough; drop "Tang" or say "under the Tang emperors".
*Correction:* Add these three to Words to explain.

**M5. A qualifier the S5 sentence drops.** Michael the Syrian says about five thousand *men* gave up their faith under torture, and that the women escaped (MS 105–107).
*Correction:* Add to Do not say: "Not 'five thousand Christians'; say 'about five thousand men', if the sentence is used."

**M6. Beat 6 nearly copies a phrase the Do not say list bars.** Beat 6 has "to peoples Thomas of Marga says no preacher had reached since the apostles". The list bars the tradition page's "peoples no preacher had reached since the apostles".
*Correction:* Write beat 6 as "to peoples who, Thomas says, had never been taught the faith since the time of Jesus's first followers (TM 21500–21505)".

**M7. "New" in "the caliphs' new capital" (beat 1) has no cited line.** The Setting gives "the caliphs' capital".
*Correction:* Drop "new".

**M8. Essence: "or a journey to him".** For Gilan and Dailom the record says only that the brothers ordained bishops in their own lands (TM 22001–22005). The explicit waiver of the journey to the patriarch is Bar Hebraeus's report about Fars (BH 9814–9818). The heart is approved, and the phrase is a fair reading of the act.
*Correction:* Add to Do not say: "Not that Timothy's letter to the brothers waived the journey to him. It gave leave to ordain where they were. The stated waiver is the Fars report, credited to Bar Hebraeus."

**M9. The Church of the East gloss has no cited line.** "The church of the Christians of Mesopotamia and Persia" is supported by Timothy's own "the countries of Babylon, of Persia, and of Assyria" (MIN 1687–1688).
*Correction:* Cite MIN 1687–1688 on the gloss.
