# Outline check: Timothy I of Baghdad

## My own account, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (teaser and existing card), the source sheet, the job in the batch 5 launch, and what Timothy is known for.

**The essence.** Timothy was head (catholicos and patriarch) of the Church of the East for about forty-two years from 780. He led it from Baghdad under five caliphs. In those years the church reached its widest: he sent monks out as bishops to the peoples south of the Caspian Sea and gave them leave to make more bishops where they were, and his letters are how a later writer knew of a metropolitan for Beth Sinaye (China). He is on the tree as the patriarch of that widest church, and its voice at the caliph's court: a debate with the caliph al-Mahdi is listed among his works, and a later historian says al-Hadi sent for him most days to talk about religion. The text of the debate is not held.

**Also told.** He won office by a trick, as his church's later histories tell it, and the means differ. The election split the church: his opponents stayed out of communion with him for about two years (his own words), and Mari says Christians fought, killed and jailed one another. Peace came when he let the rival, Ephrem of Elam, pray over him. A metropolitan he had removed, Joseph of Merv, accused him before the caliph and failed. At a synod (about 790) he made a rule for choosing a patriarch so that an election could not split the church again. He went before the caliph six times to win leave to rebuild churches that had been pulled down. Bar Hebraeus reports, as hearsay, that he brought the bishops of Fars under the patriarch. He died in the early 820s and was buried at the Kelilisho monastery in Baghdad.

**What the sources hold.** All of this is held, at the confidence levels the sheet gives. The Aristotle translation that ruling 4 asked about is not held (sheet N7), so it cannot be told. Beth Sinaye as China is a modern gloss, and no held source says Timothy sent David there.
