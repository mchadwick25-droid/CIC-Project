**Witness kinds.**
- OWN: the *Life of George* (SO-LG, LG-B), written after George's death in 615 (LG-B 852). The *Book of the Union*, only as quoted by Labourt (LAB-BU) and Wigram (WIG).
- CONTEMP: Ishoyahb III's letters to Babai and to Izla on his death (ISH). The Khuzistan Chronicle, about a generation later (KH).
- LATER, about 210 years: Thomas of Marga, writing about 840 at Beth Abe, the monastery founded by the monk Jacob whom Babai drove out of Izla (TM 2681–2683, 3430–3432). He admires Babai and says so; he also withholds things "out of respect" (TM 2760). A friendly but not neutral witness.
- LATER, two to four centuries: Chronicle of Seert (Arabic compilation, 9th–11th c., by its file header).
- LATE: 'Amr (1300s), Abdisho's catalogue (1298).
- MODERN editors: Budge 1893, Chabot 1902, Labourt 1904, Wigram 1910, Braun 1915, Scher 1919. Their views are marked as theirs.

## 1. Setting

S1. **Name.** "Mar Babhai the Great" (TM 2016); "Mar Babai le Grand" (CS 29609); census name "Babai the Great", other names "Mar Babhai", "Babai of Izla" (CEN card). Documented.

S2. **Place.** "Mar Babhai came from Beth 'Ainatha, a city of Beth Zabhdai" (TM 2059–2060). Budge: Beth Zabhdai "was a district on the western or right bank of the Tigris, adjacent to Gaziret ibn 'Omar" (TM-ed, note to 2060). His monastery: "the great monastery founded by Abraham of Kashkar on Mount Ezala, near Nisibis" (SO-ed 37360–37361, French: "le grand couvent fondé par Abraham de Kaškar sur le mont Ezala, près de Nisibe"). Census place: "Nisibis, Mount Izla", modern country Turkey (CEN). The tradition's own entry places Nisibis at "Nusaybin, Turkey" (CEN tradition `experienceToday`). Plain words: "a mountain monastery near Nisibis, on the Persian side of the Roman frontier, in what is now southeastern Turkey".

S3. **Tradition.** Census `toldWithin`: `the-church-of-the-east-under-persia`, short name "Church of the East" (CEN). Its entry: a church that "lived under a Zoroastrian shah" (CEN tradition `longDescription`). Plain word: "the Church of the East, the church of the Persian empire". Its head is the catholicos, "the head of the church".

S4. **Time.** Abbot "flourished about A.D. 569–628" (TM-ed note 4 to 2057, Budge's estimate). Census: "died after 628", active 612 (CEN). Plain: "the early 600s".

S5. **A church with no head.** Thomas: the king "swore by the Sun, his god, saying, 'As long as I live I will never have another patriarch in the country of the East,' which [threat] he actually carried out ... and so, until Khusrau died by the sword of the Christian children of the Church, the holy Church remained without a Patriarchate" (TM 3998–4008). LATER. Documented as Thomas's account. When it began: Gregory, the last catholicos, "died in the nineteenth year of the reign of Khusrau — A.D. 608–609" (TM-ed 4012; Budge). How long: "vingt années" (SO-ed 37353, Chabot's conjecture of about twenty); 'Amr's text "[Vacavit sedes post ipsum annis decem et septem]" (AMR 13428); Chabot: "'Amr et Mari disent dix-sept ans; Bar Hébréus, dix-huit ans" (SO-ed 37391–37392). It ended with the election of Ishoyahb II in 628 (SO-ed 37351–37353). **Contested as to length (17 to 20 years); the fact of the vacancy is Documented.** Plain: "for close to twenty years".

S6. **Why the king refused.** Thomas: the king's anger that the bishops had "craftily set up another" Gregory than the one he named (TM 3991–3997). Budge adds the Jacobite physician Gabriel of Sinjar's influence as "probable" (TM-ed note 3 at 4012ff). Babai's own account (SO-LG, see E6) has the king say: "Qu'ils sachent que tant qu'ils proclameront le nom de Nestorius, nous ne permettrons point qu'ils aient un chef" (SO 42415–42416). OWN. Documented as Babai's report of the king's words.

## 2. Life events, in order

E1. **Before Izla (late source only).** Chronicle of Seert: a notable of Zabdai, rich, with "beaucoup de biens et d'esclaves"; studied Persian books, then medicine at the hospital of Nisibis and church learning at the School, "quinze ans"; a "voix terrible" told him to go to Mount Izla to Abraham; he gave away his goods, kept "soixante statères d'or" to share among the monks, and took the habit (CS 29611–29644). He "ne coucha jamais sur une natte" (CS 29646). LATER (two to four centuries). The voice is a WONDER in the chronicle's voice. Whole beat: Inferential-Thin as fact; Documented as the chronicle's account. Thomas says only that he was "a disciple of Mar Abraham" (TM 2099–2100). Documented.

E2. **Abbot of the Great Monastery.** "when the holy Mar Dadh-Isho' had also departed to the life of blessedness, the holy Mar Babhai the Great became the head of that holy monastery" (TM 2015–2017); "being the third head in the order of succession" (TM 2106). Documented. **When:** Scher reckons 603/4 (CS-ed note 4 at 29693); Labourt puts Dadisho's death "entre 615 et 620" (LAB 17771–17772, a word before it unreadable); Labourt also notes the chroniclers' date for Beth Abe would make Dadisho dead "vers 592 au plus tard" (LAB 17866–17870). Contested: give no year. Seert: he ruled the monastery "vingt-quatre ans" (CS 29678–29679), and repaired its buildings (CS 29653–29654).

E3. **His temper, in his admirer's words.** "[And although he was] a learned and a wise man, he was by nature somewhat hasty of speech and harsh in command" (TM 2100–2102). LATER, friendly. Documented as Thomas's judgment. Labourt agrees: "d'un caractère absolu", reformed "avec une rigueur excessive, et parfois sans discernement" (LAB 17788–17796): editor's view.

E4. **Reform at Izla: the expulsions and the dispersal** (Thomas's account).
- Monks who had taken wives into the outer cells were exposed by the monk Elijah, who had the board struck without the abbot's leave (TM 2163, 2433–2475). Babai: "Thou must indeed shew me the proof of these thy words, and that which is proper ... I will do" (TM 2551–2554). Chapter title: "Of those things which Mar Babhai and all the fathers did" (TM 2570). "they removed from [the offenders] the garb of the ascetic life, and cut off the tonsure of their heads, and drove them forth and expelled them from thence, together with their wives and their children, and they kindled a flame and burned their cells with fire" (TM 2595–2599). Thomas adds that they later repented (TM 2613–2617). Documented as Thomas's account (written 200 years on).
- Then the monk Jacob, accused of having known and kept silent, "was he driven forth, and he went out from that monastery by the order and decree of the word of Mar Babhai" (TM 2681–2683). Thomas notes the angry speech is given to Elijah but "We should rather expect this to be the speech of Mar Babhai" (TM-ed 2671, Budge's note). Babai "laid a ban of many lines, [full] of denunciations and accusations upon him"; Thomas will not copy it "out of respect for Mar Babhai" (TM 2723–2726, 2760). FORCE: keep.
- The monks turned on the abbot: "Thou administerest the things of God unjustly and after the manner of men ... if thou dost not do so we all shall leave the convent" (TM 2770–2777). Babai "fell into great doubt" (TM 2843).
- Many left. Thomas, quoting an earlier historian: "many of the holy fathers of that monastery were scattered abroad in every place" and "they filled the country of the East with monasteries, and convents, and habitations of monks" (TM 3016–3023). Jacob founded Beth Abe (TM 3430–3432). Documented as Thomas's account. Expulsions of others "from Mount Izla by Babhai, the head of the monastery" (TM-ed 1716, Budge's note on Zekha-Isho').
- The Khuzistan Chronicle (c. 660) names Jacob of Beth Abe and Elijah among the "permulti laboriosi fratres" who came out of the monastery (KH 2032–2036). CONTEMP. Documented.

E5. **The church without a head; the visitor of the monasteries.** Three metropolitans (of Nisibis, Adiabene and Karka de Beth Slokh), afraid of the Messalians and "the blackness of the religion of the heretics", and unable "to visit the churches through fear of the government" (TM 4085–4095), persuaded him, "as it was evident to every one that he was a solitary and not a bishop", to "visit the monasteries, and convents, and monks, and repair the breaches and drive out and expel from the Church him that was sick and broken in wickedness ... and he ... ministered unto the Church, and guarded it, and glorified it until the murder of the wicked King Khusrau. And after a Catholicus was ordained he dwelt in quietness in his cell" (TM 4128–4141). He went "from village to village, and from monastery to monastery, visiting, and asking questions ... concerning the orthodoxy of belief" (TM 4363–4367). LATER. Documented as Thomas's account. Plain words: "Messalians" = a movement, condemned as heretics, of monks who held that unceasing prayer alone saves (Budge's gloss, TM-ed 4115–4120): use "wandering monks the church condemned" or "the Messalians, monks who said prayer alone was enough"; that gloss is the editor's.
- Seert: after Gregory's death, "les hérétiques purent se répandre partout, ils s'emparèrent des églises"; Babai, urged by metropolitans and bishops, "travailla avec ardeur à restaurer les affaires de l'Église et à empêcher ces (hérétiques) maudits de nuire aux chrétiens. Dans cette lutte, il eut l'appui de Yazdin le bon" (CS 29657–29678). Documented as the chronicle's account.
- 'Amr: "Sedem itaque administravit [citra ordinationem ut vicarius] Mar Aba archidiaconus una cum Mar Babaeo Magno" (AMR 13425–13426): the archdeacon Aba and Babai the Great ran the see. LATE. Documented as 'Amr's account. Chabot follows it (SO-ed 37359–37361). Labourt puts Aba over Seleucia in the south and Babai in the north (LAB 13026–13032, 13073–13081): editor's reading. Wigram: Babai "acted as patriarch in all ecclesiastical matters as far as authority went, though of course he could not ordain or consecrate" (WIG 12219–12223): editor's view.
- His means: Labourt says he leaned on "les officiers chrétiens de la Porte Royale" (LAB 13089–13091); editor's view, sources not given at that point. Not for the audio.
- One visit told as a scene: at Beth Abe he asked Jacob to drop the Sunday readings so that monks "should go forth to the congregation from their cells keeping silence, and that they should return to their cells in silence" (TM 4390–4406); during the service a paralysed man rose (WONDER, Thomas's voice), and Babai "confirmed his order of service" (TM 4446–4452). Optional; it shows the reform and the reconciliation in one scene.

E6. **612: the bishops before the king.** Babai's own account in the *Life of George*: the Jacobite physician (Gabriel of Sinjar) told the king the Henanians' master agreed with him; the king ordered a catholicos found (SO-LG 42260–42266). The bishops went to court; George of Izla went with them, sent off by the Izla community with psalms: "nous entrâmes dans l'église, toute la congrégation" (SO-LG 42316–42317; Babai speaks as one of the community). The king said: "D'abord qu'on examine quelle est la foi véritable, ensuite on leur donnera un chef" (SO-LG 42367–42368). George and the monk Henanisho "rédigèrent et écrivirent la profession véritable de la foi orthodoxe de concert avec les Pères évêques", putting in it: "Deux natures et deux hypostases de la divinité et de l'humanité, gardant leurs propriétés, en un seul prosopon du Christ Fils de Dieu" (SO-LG 42405–42410). Chabot: these words are not in the surviving document word for word, "mais bien quant au sens" (SO-ed 42431–42433). The king's answer: as long as they proclaim Nestorius's name, no head (SO-LG 42415–42416). OWN. Documented as Babai's account.
- Seert: Babai "qui n'avait pas pu se rendre à cette réunion à cause de sa faiblesse et de son âge avancé, leur avait été d'un grand secours par sa plume" (CS 29556–29558); Scher dates the meeting 612 (CS-ed 29565). LATER. Documented as the chronicle's account. **Do not say Babai wrote the 612 statement.** His own account credits George and Henanisho with the bishops.
- Plain words: "hypostasis" (Syriac *qnoma*) = "the concrete reality of each nature"; for the listener: "two natures, each fully real, in the one Christ". *Qnoma* as the key term: Wigram calls Babai "the theologian more responsible than any other for its introduction" (WIG 13891–13893): editor's view, Dominant Modern Reconstruction at most.

E7. **Henana, and the price of communion.** Context only (Henana is a sibling on the tree). Seert: Babai wrote a book "pour réfuter le commentaire de Hnana sur la doctrine des 318, lequel combattait l'enseignement de Théodore l'Interprète. Il envoya cet ouvrage aux Pères, qui l'acceptèrent" (CS 29560–29562). LATER. Documented as the chronicle's account. Babai's own words on the Henanians at Izla: "tant qu'ils n'avaient pas anathématisé Henana le chaldéen et l'hérétique, ainsi que toute sa doctrine impie, on ne leur donnait pas la communion dans notre congrégation" (SO-LG 42217–42219). OWN. Documented. His terms for Henana's teaching, "lie et cloaque de toutes les hérésies" (LAB 15730–15732, from the *Book of the Union* ms.; also SO-LG 42220). Braun calls him passionate, with exaggerations (CEN card; and see Henana's live story, which already tells the charges).

E8. **George's death, 615.** KH: the physician Gabriel accused George of Izla before the king of leaving the Magian religion; "per unius anni spatium in carcerem tradidit; deinde cruci adfixit in Behardasir (Seleucia)" (KH 2015–2019). Braun's heading: "Martyrium des Mönches Georg, † 14. Jan. 615" (LG-B 852). Babai wrote his Life at the request of a deacon and the faithful, on the feast of George's death; in it he says that about thirty-three years before he had written the Life of Abraham, founder of the monastery (LG-B 12428–12439). OWN. Documented. The Life's account of the crucifixion carries hostile language about Jews (LG-B 15040ff); not for the audio.

E9. **The quarrel with the other Babai.** KH: Satan "iurgia multa magnasque contentiones excitavit inter duas illas firmissimas religionis turres, duo Babhay, nec conquievit aut desiit usque ad finem agonis"; Babai the Great's monks "nemini permittebant ut in suum monasterium intraret, quin anathema diceret in eximium Mar Babhay nisibenum quem Babhay parvum ... cognominaverunt" (KH 2050–2056). CONTEMP, friendly to both. Documented as the chronicle's account. Seert also notes "de l'inimitié entre lui et Babai le Grand" (CS 30624–30625). Labourt: too brief to weigh (LAB 13092–13097).

E10. **628: the refusal.** After Khosrau's murder, "when a synod had been assembled, all the fathers entreated the holy Mar Babhai to be Catholicus; but he would not accept the office at all, for he preferred to end his days in his cell in the monastery to becoming head of the monastery by strife" (TM 5164–5168; "monastery" as Budge prints it). The new catholicos (Ishoyahb II) and bishops escorted him home (TM 5169–5170). LATER. Documented as Thomas's account. Labourt: the offer was made "que pour la forme" (LAB 13534–13535): editor's view.
- The angel at his cell, a horseman with a fiery sword on a white horse: "As long as thou wast the vicar of the Catholicus, from the first day even until now, I never departed from thy side; but now it is necessary for me to cleave to him that hath received this office." Babai: "If I had known that thou wast with me, I would, with all care, have accepted this great work; but now depart in peace, and pray for me" (TM 5172–5188). WONDER, Thomas's voice. Thomas ends Book I on it "in order that its end may be sanctified by the memory of the holy Mar Babhai" (TM 5190–5193). Labourt reads it as a sign of later regret (LAB 13530–13533): editor's view.

E11. **Death.** Seert: "Il mourut à l'âge de soixante-quinze ans, en la trente-huitième année de Kosrau" (CS 29679–29680). Scher: by Thomas he died after Khosrau (628); by Seert before; "Il serait né en 552/3" (CS-ed 29693–29695). Contested: "about 628". Ishoyahb III wrote to the monks of Izla "de obitu superioris eorum" (ISH 733), calling him "hunc ducem magnum et illustrissimum" (ISH 767). CONTEMP. Documented.

## 3. What came of him (heaviest first)

I1. **His teaching on Christ, kept as the church's own.** DEFENDED.
- The *Book of the Union*, on the union of God and man in Christ: in Abdisho's list, "a dissertation on the union of Christ's humanity and divinity" (BAD 17741–17743); Seert's list "Livre de l'union" (CS 29714); Scher: a manuscript survives (CS-ed 29727). OWN as quoted only (LAB-BU, WIG).
- Its teaching, in Labourt's French from the ms. (f. 122 B): "Ici aussi, de même, nous disons que le Christ a été crucifié, que le Christ est mort et est ressuscité, mais nous ne disons pas que Dieu a été crucifié et est mort" (LAB 15887–15890). The body-and-soul likeness: "Nous disons : ... J'ai froid, j'ai chaud, je sais, je comprends, un tel est mort ... et nous savons bien que tels attributs appartiennent au corps, et tels autres à l'âme" (LAB 15845–15852). Against mere "dwelling": "On ne dit pas ... d'un homme qui revêt sa tunique qu'il y habite, ni des poissons qui sont dans l'eau que c'est leur temple" (LAB 16086, 16101; a page break falls between). OWN via editor's translation. Documented.
- On Mary, in Wigram's English from the ms.: "It is just and right and proper that Mary should be called 'Mother of Christ' ... we do say that she was thus 'Mother of God' and 'Mother of the Manhood'" (WIG 14111–14123), with Wigram's note that Babai "expressly and repeatedly admits the lawfulness of the term if it be properly guarded" (WIG 14105–14107). OWN via editor. Documented as Wigram's rendering; optional.
- Kept: Thomas (840): "eighty and four of his works on various subjects are preserved, and are held in honour by the holy Church" (TM 4189–4190); Abdisho (1298): "eighty-three books" (BAD 17741). Documented as their statements. The fathers accepted his book against Henana (CS 29560–29562; LATER).
- Modern judgment: Labourt, "les écrits de l'archimandrite d'Izla contribuèrent par leur large diffusion au triomphe de la dogmatique officielle" (LAB 13097–13099); Wigram (WIG 13891–13894, above); census tradition entry: "abbot and theologian who gave the church its settled language about Christ in the early seventh century" (CEN tradition `voices`). **Dominant Modern Reconstruction** (editors' judgment); the primary sources hold that his books were kept and honoured, not that they became the creed.

I2. **A church held together without a head.** Thomas: he "guarded it ... until the murder of the wicked King Khusrau" (TM 4138–4140); the angel's "vicar of the Catholicus" (TM 5182, wonder). 'Amr: he and the archdeacon ran the see, and "uterque in libro mortuorum memoratur" (AMR 13426): both named in the church's book of the dead (its prayer list). LATE. Documented as 'Amr's statement. Ishoyahb III, contemporary, writing to him as "Sanctissimo Patri nostro benedicto Mar Babai" (ISH 378): "quam ergo gloriationem laudum non possidebit ille qui per procellas desperationis ducit navem fidei gubernaculo praedicationis suae ..." and "Huiusmodi enim periculum procellarum barbararum cum vicisset sollicitudo zeli tui per multos annos, o Pater sanctissime" (ISH 398–400, 403–404). CONTEMP. Documented.

I3. **Monasteries across the East.** The dispersal that followed his expulsions "filled the country of the East with monasteries" (TM 3022), Beth Abe among them (TM 3430–3432; KH 2032–2036). Labourt: between 610 and 630 the northern provinces filled with hermits and monks, "environ une soixantaine de monastères, réformés suivant les méthodes d'Abraham le Grand" (LAB 17830–17840): editor's view. Babai also wrote "a book for conventual Novices ... rules for Monks" (BAD 17749–17750); the texts are not held.

I4. **Remembered.** A gospel book finished in 1186 (SCH 211–216) carries a calendar (SCH 238–239) whose second Friday of the Dedication of the Church remembers "Mar Abraham, Babaï, Babaï, 'Abdišô', Jérémie, les fondateurs des Congrégations dans la montagne d'Izla" (SCH 345–347). Which two Babais is not said: Inferential-Thin that one is Babai the Great. Optional, card only.
- **Who keeps him today:** no held source names a present-day keeper of Babai. The tradition's entry names its heirs as the Assyrian Church of the East and the Chaldean Catholic Church (CEN tradition `legacy`), but not Babai's place among them. Leave out of the audio.

## 4. Links

- **Henana of Adiabene** (on the tree): Babai wrote against him (CS 29560; SO-LG 42217–42222). Henana's live story already tells Babai's charges (fate, the resurrection, "Chaldean", Origen) and quotes from the *Life of George*.
- **Barsauma of Nisibis** (batch 5 sibling): the synod of 605 that bound the church to Theodore is Barsauma's ending. Here, at most a clause. Not the catholicos Babowai of 484 (S, Barsauma outline) nor the catholicos Babai of 497 (TM-ed 2042).
- **Nestorius** (batch 5): the *Life of George* gives George's anathema on whoever "méprise ou blâme les trois Docteurs œcuméniques ... le bienheureux Diodore, le bienheureux Théodore et le bienheureux Nestorius, illustre entre les martyrs" (SO-LG 42221–42238). **These words are George's anathema, which Babai quotes**; Babai's own words follow: "Cet anathème est gardé et confirmé, et nous l'observerons" (SO-LG 42245–42247).
- **Thomas of Marga** (on the tree): wrote his life (TM Book I chs. 7, 10–14, 27–29, 35).
- **Mar Aba I** (on the tree): no link; a different Aba (the archdeacon of the vacancy is not Mar Aba I).
- **Barhadbeshabba 'Arbaya** (on the tree): no direct link in held texts.
- **Ishoyahb III** (not on the tree): wrote to him and on his death (ISH 378, 733).

## 5. Where sources disagree or come late

D1. Start of his abbacy: 603/4 (Scher), before 592 (Labourt's reading of the Beth Abe date), 615–620 (Labourt on Dadisho's death). Give no year.
D2. Death: before Khosrau (Seert) or after 628 (Thomas). Say "about 628".
D3. Length of the vacancy: 17 ('Amr, Mari), 18 (Bar Hebraeus), about 20 (Chabot).
D4. Who led during the vacancy: Thomas, the three metropolitans' visitor (TM); 'Amr, joint administrator with the archdeacon Aba (AMR). Both can stand: "he, with an archdeacon in the capital, ran the church's affairs, by later accounts".
D5. The 612 statement: Seert, "by his pen"; Babai's own Life credits George and Henanisho with the bishops.
D6. His harshness: Thomas (admirer) and the Izla monks' rebuke (as Thomas tells it); KH's quarrel. No source in Babai's favour on the Jacob affair except Thomas's view that God's will worked through it (TM 2924–2925, 3018–3022).
D7. Lateness: the life before Izla (Seert), the voice, the wonders, are late. The *Book of the Union* is held only in two editors' extracts.

- `cic/corpus-map/the-church-of-the-east-under-persia.yaml` (all entries; Babai's locus in Thomas noted at its line 530) and `the-church-of-the-east-on-the-silk-road.yaml` (line 62, same locus). `WORKS.yaml`, `AUTHOR-IDS.yaml` and `RETRIEVAL-HINTS.md`: no entry for Babai under any spelling.
- `cic/texts/` for the name forms Babai, Babaï, Babhai, Babhay, Babaeus/Babaeo/Babaei, Bābay, and for Izla, Izala, Ezala.

**What turned up.** About 60 files match the name forms; most are false hits (Tagalog *babai* "woman", Welsh, Gothic, Ethiopian chronicles) or other Babais (the catholicos of 497; Babai of Nisibis; Babowai of 484 in Michael the Syrian, Mari, Malech, Elias of Nisibis). Files that speak of **Babai the Great**: 14 — TM, LAB, CS, SO, BRS, LG-B, ISH, KH, AMR, BAD, WIG, SCH, `assemani_bibliotheca-orientalis-tomus3-pars1-de-scriptoribus-syris-nestorianis-lat_1725.txt`, and `malech_history-of-the-syrian-nation-and-old-evangelical-apostolic-church-of-the-east_1910.txt`.

**Used:** TM, CS, SO (SO-LG), LG-B, LAB, ISH, KH, AMR, BAD, WIG, SCH, BRS (one note only).

**Set aside:**
- Assemani, *Bibliotheca orientalis* III.1 (1725): Latin of the same Thomas chapters and Abdisho's list, which Budge's notes point to (TM-ed passim). The 1725 OCR is poor; nothing in it that TM and BAD do not give.
- Malech (1910): a modern popular history; its Babai pages retell Thomas (the angel) with additions not traced to a source ("a fine looking man", strict rules). Not used.
- Mari ibn Sulayman (Gismondi's Latin): the card cites him, but the held Latin of Gregory's life does not name Babai that I could find; 'Amr's parallel does (AMR 13425–13426). Mari's count of seventeen years is reported only by Chabot (SO-ed).
- Babai's *Book of the Union* in Syriac, Bedjan's Syriac of the *Life of George*, and Babai's monastic rules: **not held**.
- Bar Hebraeus (Abbeloos-Lamy vol. 3): only a place note on Izla; no Babai the Great passage found.

**Later witnesses who carried him on:** Ishoyahb III (his own time); the Khuzistan Chronicle (c. 660); Thomas of Marga (c. 840); the Chronicle of Seert; 'Amr and Abdisho (1300s); a 1186 calendar (if one Babai there is he).
