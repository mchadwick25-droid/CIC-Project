# Outline: Babai the Great

Sheet items (S, E, I, D) are in `Source_Sheet.md`, which gives the file and line for each.

- **The essence:** For close to twenty years in the early 600s, the Persian king Khosrau would not let the Church of the East have a catholicos, the head of the whole church (S5; Documented as Thomas of Marga's account; length Contested, 17 to 20 years). In those years Babai, abbot (head) of the Great Monastery on Mount Izla near Nisibis, a monk and not a bishop, went round the church's monasteries for its senior bishops. He tested what the monks believed and drove out those the bishops judged heretics, people teaching what the church had condemned (E5; Documented as Thomas's account, about 840). He wrote the *Book of the Union*, on how Christ is one person who is both fully God and fully human, and his church kept and honoured his books (E6, I1; the books' keeping Documented; that his writing settled his church's teaching on Christ is the judgment of modern historians, Dominant Modern Reconstruction).
- **Also told:**
  - His reform began at home, and its force was real. At Izla, monks found living with wives, against their vows, were stripped of the monk's robe and driven out with their families, and their huts were burned. Then he drove out a gentle monk, Jacob, with a long written condemnation, and the other monks rebuked him (E3, E4; Documented as Thomas's account). Many left. They spread out and founded new monasteries across the north, Jacob's Beth Abe among them (E4, I3; Documented as Thomas's account).
  - Visiting the monasteries while the church had no head (E5). Followers of Henana, a teacher at Nisibis whose books the bishops had condemned, were refused communion, the shared bread and wine of worship, at Izla until they solemnly cursed Henana, by Babai's own account (E7; Documented, his words).
  - The meeting before the king in 612. By Babai's account the bishops wrote down their faith: Christ has two natures, the divine and the human, each fully real, in one person. The king answered that while they honoured the name of Nestorius, a teacher condemned in the Roman empire, they would have no head (E6; Documented as Babai's own account). Old and weak, Babai helped them by his writing, a later chronicle says (E6; Documented as Seert's account). His own teaching, in his own words: Christ died, but God did not die (Q1).
  - George, a monk of Izla who had left the Persian state religion, was crucified in 615; Babai wrote the account of his life (E8; Documented).
  - In 628, after the king's murder, the bishops begged Babai to become head of the church, and he refused (E10; Documented as Thomas's account). He died about then (E11; Contested which side of 628).
  The sources will not hold "Babai wrote the bishops' statement of 612" (his own account credits two other monks with the bishops) or "Babai led the church as its head" (he was "a solitary and not a bishop", TM 4130). They hold that three senior bishops asked him to visit the monasteries, and that a late historian says he and an archdeacon, the chief church official in the capital, ran its affairs (E5).
- **Length:** about 2¾ minutes (about 410 words). Why: the heart has three parts (leading without a head, the teaching, the reform and its cost), and all must land early. His own words survive, but only in editors' extracts, and the life rests mostly on Thomas, two centuries later; the force of the Izla expulsions must be told plainly, with whose account it is. Not more: Henana's charges are Henana's story, the 605 synod is Barsauma's, and the wonders are cut to one.
- **Setting:**
  - Time: "the early 600s"; the vacancy from about 609 to 628 (S5); the court meeting, 612 (E6); George's death, January 615 (E8); the new head of the church, 628 (E10).
  - Place: "the Great Monastery on Mount Izla, near Nisibis" (S2), "on the Persian side of the border with Rome, in what is now southeastern Turkey" (S2; census places Nisibis at Nusaybin, Turkey). He came from Beth Zabdai, by the Tigris (S2).
  - Tradition: "the Church of the East, the church of the Persian empire", under a Zoroastrian king (S3). Its head is the catholicos: say "the head of the church".
  - Plain words: "catholicos" = "head of the church"; "abbot" = "head of a monastery"; "Messalians" = "monks the church condemned, who held that prayer alone was enough" (the gloss is Budge's, E5); "hypostasis" = "the concrete reality of each nature" (E6); "anathema" = "a solemn curse that cuts someone off".
- **Words to explain** (the writer works the gloss into the sentence where the word is first heard, or uses the plain word instead):
  - **catholicos** / **patriarch**: use "the head of the church", the Church of the East's own leader at the Persian capital. Not a pope; answered to no bishop outside Persia (CEN tradition entry). "Patriarch" is Thomas's other word for the same office (TM 3999, 5177).
  - **metropolitan**: use "senior bishop", a bishop over the other bishops of a province (TM 4085–4088 names three, of Nisibis, Adiabene and Karka).
  - **bishop**: "a church leader over a city and its region"; here, one ordained by the head of the church (E5).
  - **abbot**: "the head of a monastery".
  - **monk** / **solitary**: "a man vowed to prayer, poverty and an unmarried life". Thomas's "solitary" (TM 4130) means a monk, not a bishop; use "monk".
  - **cell**: use "hut" or "room"; at Izla, monks lived in separate cells around the monastery (TM 2238 "outside cells").
  - **the habit** / **tonsure**: use "the monk's robe" and "the monk's shaved head" (TM 2595–2596).
  - **ban** (on Jacob): use "a written condemnation" (TM 2725–2726).
  - **anathema** / **curse**: "a solemn curse that cut someone off from the church".
  - **communion**: "the shared bread and wine of Christian worship"; to be refused it was to be shut out of the community (SO-LG 42219).
  - **heretic** / **heresy**: "someone teaching what the church had condemned"; always the bishops' or Babai's judgment, never the narrator's.
  - **Messalians**: "wandering monks the church condemned, who said prayer alone was enough" (Budge's gloss, TM-ed 4115–4120).
  - **natures**: "what makes him God, and what makes him human".
  - **hypostasis** (Syriac *qnoma*): avoid the word; say "each nature fully real in its own right" (WIG 13851–13853, Babai's definition as Wigram gives it).
  - **person** (in "one person"): "one single Christ, one who acts and is worshipped" (LAB 15845–15862 sense; plain sense only).
  - **union** (*Book of the Union*): "how God and man are joined in the one Christ".
  - **archdeacon**: "the chief church official in the capital" (LAB 13026–13029).
  - **synod**: use "a meeting of bishops".
  - **the Life of George**: "the account he wrote of George's life", a book honouring a holy man.
  - **Magian** / **Zoroastrian**: "the Persian state religion, with its fire temples and priests called magi".
  - **feast**: "the yearly day the church remembered him" (LG-B 12434–12435).
  - **Nestorius**: "a bishop of Constantinople, condemned in 431, whom this church honoured as a teacher" (census; batch sibling).
- **Shape:** Founder or leader. Trap: titles and offices in place of acts. He had no office the king recognized; tell what he did: the visits, the expulsions, the book, the refusal.
- **Sibling:**
  - `barsauma-of-nisibis` (batch 5, outline read): its job is the synods of 484–486 and their afterlife; its ending is the synod of 605 that made Theodore required study. Here 605 is at most a clause, and is not needed. Its catholicos of 484 is "Babowai", a different man; so is the catholicos Babai of 497 (D, Links). None of its quotes reused.
  - `nestorius` (batch 5, outline read): shares the 612 dispute over his name. The "three Doctors" line from the *Life of George* is out of both stories (Mark, 2026-11-18). None of its quotes reused.
  - `henana-of-adiabene` (on the tree, live text read): it already tells Babai's charges against Henana (fate, the resurrection, "Chaldean", Origen) and Braun's and Labourt's views of Babai. Here Henana is a clause; none of those charges or quotes are repeated.
  - `thomas-of-marga` (on the tree): the witness; his story's own quotes are not used.
- **Openings:**
  1. **Chosen.** The king's oath. Thomas of Marga says the Persian king swore by the Sun, his god, that as long as he lived the Christians of his land would have no new head (S5; TM 3998–4000). He kept his word until his own death, close to twenty years. In those years the bishops turned to a monk who was not even a bishop: the abbot of a mountain monastery near Nisibis. Leads straight into the essence.
  2. The refusal (E10): in 628 the bishops begged an old abbot to become head of the church, and he went home to his cell. Not chosen: it is the end, and the angel scene belongs there.

**Story beats** (the opening and beats 1 to 2 are the first minute, about 150 words; the essence is said by the end of beat 1)

1. **The abbot of Izla.** A disciple of Abraham, founder of the Great Monastery on Mount Izla, he became its third head (E2; Documented). Give no year (D1). Thomas, who admired him, calls him learned and wise, but "somewhat hasty of speech and harsh in command" (E3; told in the narrator's words, credited). A later chronicle says he had been rich, studied medicine at Nisibis, and gave his goods away (E1; Inferential-Thin as fact): one clause at most, credited, or leave out.
2. **The reform at home** (E4). Monks were found living with wives in the outer cells. By Thomas's account, Babai and the fathers stripped them of the habit, cut off their tonsure, drove them out with their wives and children, and burned their cells. Then Babai drove out Jacob, a gentle monk accused of knowing and keeping silent, under a long written ban; Thomas will not copy it, "out of respect". The monks told the abbot he ruled God's house "after the manner of men" and threatened to leave. Many did. Say once that Thomas wrote at Jacob's own monastery, about two centuries later. Documented as Thomas's account. Thomas tells it soon after Babai became abbot; no held source dates it (D1), so do not date it or set it before or after the king's ban; it stands here in Thomas's order.
3. **The church without a head** (S5, E5). After the death of the last head of the church, about 609, the king allowed no successor. Three metropolitans (senior bishops), afraid of the Messalians and other teachings they judged heretical, and unable to travel for fear of the government, asked Babai, "a solitary and not a bishop", to visit the monasteries and drive out the wrong. He went from monastery to monastery, questioning monks on their faith (Documented as Thomas's account). A later historian says he and an archdeacon ran the church's affairs (E5, D4; Documented as 'Amr's account). At Izla, followers of Henana, a teacher at Nisibis whose books the bishops had condemned, got no communion until they cursed their master (E7; his own words; the condemnation, CS 29598–29603). Optional, one clause: a quarrel with another monk named Babai lasted to the end of their lives, and his monks demanded that visitors curse the other man (E9; Documented as the Khuzistan Chronicle's account, about 660).
4. **612: the faith set before the king** (E6). The king said: first let it be examined which faith is true, then they will get a head. The bishops wrote their faith down: in Christ, "two natures and two hypostases", each keeping what is its own, in one person. (Chabot notes that the surviving statement holds this in sense, not in these exact words: say "Babai says they wrote", not a quotation of the statement.) The king's answer: as long as they proclaimed Nestorius's name, they would have no head (all Documented as Babai's own account in the *Life of George*). Babai, too old and weak to go, helped them by his pen, a later chronicle says (Documented as Seert's account). Here his own teaching (Q1), in the *Book of the Union*: we say Christ died, not that God died. The narrator gives the plain sense: the man Jesus suffered; God, who cannot suffer, did not; yet both are one Christ.
5. **615: a monk crucified** (E8). George, a monk of Izla born to a noble Zoroastrian family, was accused before the king of leaving the Magian religion. The king held him a year in prison and had him crucified at Seleucia (Documented as the Khuzistan Chronicle's account). Babai wrote his Life, at the request of the faithful gathered for his feast (OWN). Two sentences.
6. **628: the refusal** (E10, E11). The king was murdered in 628, and the bishops met to choose a head of the church. All of them, Thomas says, begged Babai to take it; he would not, preferring to end his days in his cell. The new head of the church and some bishops escorted him home. He died about this time, old (D2: give "about 628"). The angel scene is the ending.

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **A teaching his church kept.** DEFENDED. His *Book of the Union* set out how Christ is one, God and man, each nature fully real (I1). Two centuries on, Thomas counted eighty-four of his works "preserved" and "held in honour by the holy Church" (Documented as Thomas's statement). Modern historians judge that his writings, by their wide spread, helped his church's official teaching on Christ prevail (Labourt; Wigram calls him more responsible than anyone for its key term): credit to "historians", Dominant Modern Reconstruction. Tell it inside beat 4 or straight after it.
2. **A church held together.** DEFENDED. A bishop of his own day, Ishoyahb, who later led the church, wrote to him in praise (Q2, optional), and on his death called him "this great and most illustrious leader" (I2; Documented). A late historian says he and the archdeacon were both named in the church's book of the dead, its prayer list (I2; Documented as 'Amr's account).
3. **Monasteries across the East.** Those who left Izla in the quarrel built monasteries across the north, Jacob's Beth Abe among them (I3; Documented as Thomas's account, which he reads as God's will working through strife). One sentence, in beat 2 or here.
- **Who keeps this today:** leave out. No held source names a present-day keeper of Babai.

**Quotes** (2 first-choice, 1 optional)

1. **Q1.** Verbatim (LAB 15887–15890, Labourt's French from the unpublished Syriac manuscript of the *Book of the Union*, f. 122 B; the Syriac is not held): "Ici aussi, de même, nous disons que le Christ a été crucifié, que le Christ est mort et est ressuscité, mais nous ne disons pas que Dieu a été crucifié et est mort." Rendering (from Labourt's French): "Here too, in the same way, we say that Christ was crucified, that Christ died and rose again. But we do not say that God was crucified and died." Job: his teaching in his own voice, in beat 4. The narrator gives the plain sense alongside.
2. **Q2 (ending).** Verbatim (TM 5185–5188, Budge's English; the Syriac is not held): "If I had known that thou wast with me, I would, with all care, have accepted this great work; but now depart in peace, and pray for me." Rendering (from Budge's English): "If I had known you were with me, I would have taken on this great work with all care. But now go in peace, and pray for me." Job: the ending, inside Thomas's wonder: an angel, who says he had stood beside Babai all the years he stood in for the head of the church, asks leave to go to the new one. Keep it in Thomas's voice ("Thomas tells that...").
3. **Q3 (optional).** Verbatim (ISH 398–400, Duval's Latin; the Syriac is bound in but not readable): "quam ergo gloriationem laudum non possidebit ille qui per procellas desperationis ducit navem fidei gubernaculo praedicationis suae". Rendering (from the Latin): "What glory and praise, then, will he not have, who steers the ship of faith through storms of despair, with his preaching as its rudder?" The next sentence applies it to Babai: his zeal overcame such storms "for many years" (ISH 403–404). Job: how a contemporary saw him, in What came 2. Optional; note that Barsauma's story uses a "stormy sea" quote, so the variety check may prefer the short line "this great and most illustrious leader" (ISH 767, narrator's words).

**Connection to carry into the story:** what passed from Babai was a written account of how Christ is one, which his church kept as its own: two natures, each fully real (two "hypostases"), in one person, so that Christ died as man and God did not die (Q1; I1). By Babai's own account, the bishops put the same teaching before the king in 612 (E6; Chabot: in sense, not word for word), his book against Henana was accepted by the bishops (E7), and two centuries later his works were still "held in honour by the holy Church" (TM 4189–4190). The listener must understand three things the record holds: the church had no head because the king forbade one, so the work fell to a monk with no office; this teaching set his church apart from its neighbours: Babai called those who opposed it "Theopaschites", people who make God suffer (SO-LG 42171, 42262), which is his charge, to be credited to him; and before the king it was tied to the name of Nestorius, which is why the king refused them a head; and that it became the church's settled teaching is historians' judgment, built on how widely his books were kept. Carry it through what he did (the visits, the book, the 612 meeting), not through a "this matters because" sentence.

**Ending:** one of these, from the record.
1. **Chosen.** The angel at the cell (E10; TM 5172–5188). Thomas tells that after the bishops left him at his monastery, an angel on a white horse, with a fiery sword, stood before his cell. It had stood beside him, it said, all the years he took the place of the head of the church; now it must go to the new one. Babai's answer is Q2. In Thomas's voice throughout.
2. His refusal and return to the cell (TM 5164–5170), if the wonder is cut.

**Who tells it**

- Babai himself: the *Life of George* (after 615), held in Chabot's French extracts and Braun's abridged German; the *Book of the Union*, held only as two historians quote it from a manuscript. Credit Q1 to "his Book of the Union".
- Ishoyahb, a bishop of his own day who later led the church: letters to him and on his death (contemporary, admiring).
- The Khuzistan Chronicle, about 660: the quarrel with the other Babai; George's death. Credit as "a chronicle written a generation later".
- Thomas of Marga, about 840: the main life. He admired Babai, wrote at the monastery Babai's victim Jacob founded, and held things back "out of respect". Say once that most of the story is Thomas's, two centuries on, and that he wrote at Jacob's house. Credit the expulsions, the visitation, the refusal and the angel to him.
- The Chronicle of Seert (Arabic, centuries later): his early life, "help by his pen" in 612, death at 75. Credit as "a later chronicle".
- 'Amr (1300s): the shared rule with the archdeacon; "a later historian".
- The doubt is not itself the story; no scene of doubt is needed.

**Do not say**

- Any word in "Words to explain" used without its plain sense the first time it is heard.
- Not "Babai was patriarch", "catholicos" or "bishop". He was a monk and abbot who refused the office (TM 4130, 5164–5168). "Stood in for the head of the church" only in the angel's words (Thomas).
- Not "Babai wrote the statement of faith of 612". His own account credits George and the monk Henanisho with the bishops; a later chronicle says he helped by his pen (E6, D5).
- Not "Babai founded the monastery on Mount Izla". Abraham founded it; Babai was its third head (E2).
- Not "Babai invented the formula 'two hypostases'" or "made it the church's creed" as fact. That his writings helped it prevail is historians' judgment (I1).
- Not "Babai burned the monks' cells" alone. Thomas: Babai "and all the fathers" (TM 2570, 2594–2599). But do not drop the burning, the families driven out, the ban on Jacob, or the monks' rebuke.
- Not "Jacob was guilty" or "Jacob was rightly expelled". Thomas says he was driven out "unjustly" (TM 2768).
- The angel is Thomas's story: "Thomas tells that an angel...", never "an angel appeared to him".
- Not "the king persecuted the church" in general. Say what the record holds: he forbade a new head; a physician at court accused George, and the king had him crucified.
- Not a year for when he became abbot (D1). Not "born in 552" (Scher's reckoning). Death: "about 628" (D2).
- Not "twenty years" as exact: "close to twenty years" (S5, D3).
- Not "Nestorian" in the narrator's voice; it is opponents' name (census tradition entry).
- Not "Henana was a heretic" in the narrator's voice; "teachings the bishops judged heretical".
- Not Henana's charges (fate, the resurrection, astrology, Origen): they are Henana's story.
- Not "Babai the Little" without saying it is the chronicle's name for another monk (E9).
- Not "Babai" for the catholicos of 484: he is "Babowai" (Mark approved, 2026-11-18), a different man, as is the catholicos Babai of 497. Neither is needed here.
- "Messalians": give the plain gloss, which is Budge's; do not add more about them.
- Not "Babai lived in a cave" or "never slept on a mat" as fact: late chronicle only.

**Leave out**

- The voice that sent him to Izla, his medical studies and his wealth (Seert): late; one clause at most, credited.
- Seert's miracles and conversions of Magians; the paralytic healed at Beth Abe (Thomas): wonders. The kit allows one; the angel is it. The Beth Abe dispute over Sunday readings may stand without the healing only if words allow, credited.
- The *Life of George*'s account of the crucifixion and its hostile words about Jews: George's story, and not fit to repeat.
- The "Mother of Christ" passage of the *Book of the Union* (WIG 14111–14123): real and his, but it pulls toward the Nestorius and Ephesus stories; card.
- The 1186 calendar naming "Babai, Babai" among Izla's founders (I4): which Babais is not said; card.
- Labourt's view that the 628 offer was "only for form", and his reading of the angel as regret: editor's opinions; card.
- The synod of 605: Barsauma's ending.
- Henana's school walk-out: the tradition's documented story and Henana's.
- The "three Doctors" line (George's anathema naming Diodore, Theodore and Nestorius, quoted in Babai's *Life of George*, SO-LG 42224–42238): Mark, 2026-11-18: out of both stories.
