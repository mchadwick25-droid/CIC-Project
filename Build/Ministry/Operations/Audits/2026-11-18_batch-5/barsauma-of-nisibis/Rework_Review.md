## 1. One hearing

Read once, straight through, as a listener who does not know him.

- **Who he was:** Barsauma, bishop of Nisibis, a frontier city between Rome and Persia, in the late 400s, in the church of the Persian empire.
- **What happened:** in 484 he led a synod of rebel bishops against the head of his church, Babowai, who was then put to death by the king (Barsauma's part disputed). The next year he took the whole synod back, rules included, and wrote to the new head of the church from a troubled frontier.
- **Why it matters:** what he stood for, ministers free to marry and Theodore as the church's sure guide, became his church's law anyway (497, and more than a century later); neighboring churches blamed him for setting it apart.

All three lines can be said after one hearing. The released story failed this test. The rework passes it: the "fought for two things" sentence gives the listener what he stood for, and the 497 and 605 rulings pay it off.

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The quarrel of 491 is missing, so the reconciliation reads as permanent.** The approved heart lists "the quarrel of 491" in what is also told, and Mark's rework ruling keeps the approved heart. `Rework.md` cut it. Without it, the listener hears him take everything back, plead with Acacius for help, and then set up rules with Acacius, the Armenian bishops say. The record has the two cutting each other off from 491, which the synod of 497 then lifted (Synodicon 20491–20496). That leaves a misleading picture of who he was, the rebel who submitted and then broke again. It sits in time order before 497 and adds a third date, within Mark's "two or three".

Insert as a new paragraph between the paragraph ending "...and expected new assistant ministers to take a wife." and the paragraph beginning "In 497 a synod...":

> From 491, he and Acacius were cutting each other off from the church.

No Do not say line is needed. The outline already lists the 491 quarrel; the rework dropped it.

**MINOR 2. Narsai is missing.** He is in the approved heart, in one clause, and is a link on the tree. Omitting him misleads no one, so this is not BLOCKING; it is Mark's call. If added, put it at the start of the third paragraph, before "By his own account" (the outline places it before 484):

> At Nisibis he welcomed the teacher Narsai and asked him to found a school, which counts them both as its founders.

**MINOR 3. "The next year" is anchored to an undated marriage.** Replace "The next year, with Acacius as the new head of the church," with:

> The year after the synod, with Acacius as the new head of the church,

**MINOR 4. A qualifier dropped.** The outline gives the letter behind the legend as written "about a century and a half later". Replace "drew on a letter written from old men's memories a century and a half later." with:

> drew on a letter written from old men's memories about a century and a half later.

**MINOR 5. "Barsauma fought for two things" is a touch stronger than the record.** The outline and the heart give it as what "his side" stood for, in a synod he later renounced. The sentence earns its place because it answers what he stood for, so keep its shape. Replace "Barsauma fought for two things:" with:

> Barsauma and his side stood for two things:
