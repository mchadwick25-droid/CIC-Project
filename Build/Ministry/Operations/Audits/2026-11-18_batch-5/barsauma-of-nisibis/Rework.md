## What changed and why

The fault: listeners lost the story after one hearing. It told the record, not the person. The rework follows one line: the frontier bishop and what he fought for, the quarrel and Beth Lapat, the taking back, the frontier letters, and his church keeping his rules anyway while its neighbors blamed him for the split.

- **What he stood for, early.** The essence now names his two causes in their own sentence ("Barsauma fought for two things..."), before the synod and the retraction. The 424 point moves out of its corrective aside and becomes background: by his time the church had governed itself for sixty years, and its synods were fixing its teaching and rules. That keeps line 130 without the bolted-on correction the AI skeptic heard, and without "since 424", which the scholar flagged as stated too firmly.
- **Dates.** The listener keeps two: 484 and 497. Everything else is given relative to these: "sixty years", "the next year", "early the next year", "more than a century after Beth Lapat", "a century and a half later". The witnesses' centuries (1100s, 1200s) go. Lateness is kept as "a later historian" and "a rival writer, later still".
- **Witnesses.** Five witnesses were credited in turn. Now the rival writers are one group, introduced once at Babowai's death ("Writers of the rival Syriac church"). The two marriage claims from that side then come from "a rival writer" and "another" (lines 46, 99). Each claim still has its own witness. The historian of his own church is introduced once and then called "the same historian". No new witness is brought in. Simeon and the Persian delegates at Dvin are left out, and so is Narsai. The Armenian bishops come in once, for the neighbors' refusal (line 56).
- **The marriage conflict, once.** It is one short paragraph: a lead-in ("told two ways"), Mari's version, Bar Hebraeus's version, and the hostile chronicle's charge (lines 46, 125, 129).
- **Babowai.** The old sentence holding two versions is split in two, so each version stands alone. All three versions are kept (line 46).
- **The taking back.** It is now joined to the letters. The frontier letters and the amputation plea follow it in time (lines 47, 48).
- **486.** The synod's order on the wandering men in black is back, as line 128 asks. The old story had dropped it under a MINOR note in `Reshape_Review.md`. Its teaching and its marriage rule stay (lines 49, 112).
- **The neighbors.** The hostile legend gets its own paragraph after 497, so it no longer sits inside the 497 paragraph (the skeptic's objection). It opens with the Armenian bishops' refusal, so that "neighboring churches" in the essence is borne out (lines 56, 57, 80).
- **Ending.** The story still closes on 605 and the book image (Ending 1, line 83). The last two sentences are now about him, not the synod: the title Mar, and "To those bishops he was 'the blessed Mar Barsauma.'" Line 50's death and 491 were weighed as an ending. They fall before 497 and 605, so ending on them would mean telling the end and then going back. The kit forbids that.
- **Glosses.** Each listed word gets its plain sense the first time it is heard, or the plain word is used instead. "Ministers" replaces "clergy". "Assistant ministers" replaces "deacons". "Rules" replaces "canons". "Cut off from the church" replaces "excommunicate". "Christ fully God and fully human, the two never mixed" replaces "two natures". "Wandering men in monks' black" replaces "ascetics". "Head of the church" replaces "patriarch". "The rival Syriac church" replaces "Syriac Orthodox". Synod, catholicos, nun and Mar are each glossed once. No gloss is chained to another.
- **Drafted, then cut.** These were drafted and then cut, because the line stands without them: the Edessa schooling (Simeon's account), the frontier feast, the sentence about destroying the synod's book, the 486 reason ("a mockery to outsiders"), and 491 with his death.
