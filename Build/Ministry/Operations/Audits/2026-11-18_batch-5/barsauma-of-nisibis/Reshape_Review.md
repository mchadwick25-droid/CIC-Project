## The three lines (one hearing, before the other files)

- **Who this was:** Barsauma, bishop of Nisibis, a city on the border between the Roman and Persian empires, in the late 400s.
- **What happened:** in 484 he led a synod at Beth Lapat against Babowai, the head of his church, which made rules on clergy marriage. Babowai was put to death that year, and Barsauma's part is disputed. Late writers tell his own marriage in different ways. In 485 he took the whole synod back, rules included. His letters show a pressed frontier and his plea to cut off his rebels.
- **Why the connection matters:** his church kept what his side stood for. In 497 it let every cleric marry, accepting Beth Lapat "but only on this point". In 605 it made Theodore required study and called the synod it had left out of its book "the divine assembly".

Note from the hearing: the third part of the heart, how his opponents remembered him, was heard only as one claim in the essence. Nothing later in the story showed it. That is B1.
