## 5. Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. The 486 synod is told as a marriage ruling only.** The outline's Also told and the approved heart give 486 three rulings:

- Christ confessed as fully God and fully human, the two never mixed;
- the wandering men in monks' black, called frauds teaching error, sent out of the towns and back to the monasteries under the bishops;
- clergy free to marry.

The story keeps only the third. The two-natures confession is the teaching half of what "his synods fixed" (outline, Also told). Without it, one dispute stands for the whole synod, and the listener is never told what doctrine set the Persian church apart. This is missing essential material.

Change: before the marriage ruling in the 486 sentence, add about 35 words. For example: "confessed Christ fully God and fully human, the two never mixed; ordered wandering men in monks' black, whom it called frauds teaching error, out of the towns and back to the monasteries; and barred bishops ..." (E11; SO 19739–19746, 19752–19795).

Do not say line to add: "Do not tell the 486 synod as a marriage ruling only. Tell its confession of Christ fully God and fully human, the two never mixed, and its order on the wandering men in black, with the marriage rule (E11)."

**B2. The hostile charge about the marriage rule is dropped.** Mark's approved heart names three parts of the marriage: Mari, Bar Hebraeus, and Michael the Syrian's chronicle, which charges that he made the marriage rule so he would not be blamed for the woman he kept. Outline beat 2 holds it too. The source is MS 33595–33597: "afin de n'être pas blâmé lui-même à cause de la courtisane".

That charge is why the marriage sits "beside Beth Lapat's marriage rule". Leaving it out softens the hostile view's force (Kit, Rigor).

Change: after the "concubine" sentence, add one credited sentence. For example: "A hostile chronicle of the 1100s, built on old men's memories, says he made the marriage rule so he would not be blamed for her." Keep it in that chronicle's voice.

Do not say line to add: "Do not drop the hostile chronicle's charge that he made the marriage rule so he would not be blamed for his woman. Tell it, credited to that chronicle alone (MS 33595–33597)."

**B3. "Set the Persian church apart" is left without the 424 point, which misleads.** The outline's Connection says the listener must understand that the Persian church already governed itself, since 424. So what these synods set apart was its teaching and its rules, not its government.

The story never says so. Heard once, "the man who set the Persian church apart" invites the very reading Do not say bars: that Barsauma made the Church of the East independent.

Change: add one clause, best beside the essence's last sentence or at the 506 refusal. For example: "The Persian church had run its own affairs since 424; what his synods set apart was its teaching and its rules" (S4).

Do not say line to add: "Do not leave 'set the Persian church apart' without the clause that it had governed itself since 424. What his synods set apart was its teaching and rules (S4, Connection)."
