## My own account (from the census, the sheet and the sources)

**Essence.** Barsauma was bishop of Nisibis, on the Roman–Persian frontier, in the late 400s. In April 484 he and Nanai of Perat led a synod at Beth Lapat against the catholicos Babowai. In 485 he retracted it, with all its canons, in letters to the new catholicos Acacius (SO 35255–35258, 35455–35461). Yet the church kept what that synod stood for. In 486 it allowed clergy to marry, in 497 it accepted Beth Lapat "only on this point", and in 605 it quoted Beth Lapat on Theodore and made his writings required study (SO 19812–19951, 20480–20486, 31682–31726). Hostile neighbours, Syriac Orthodox and the Persian Miaphysites who wrote to the Armenians, named him with Acacius as the men whose synods split the Persian church off (SIM 75090–75099; TM 36702–36716, 36823–36842; BH 3852–3859).

**Also told.** The frontier letters: drought, raids, the Roman general at Nisibis, the plea for excommunication "like the doctor who amputates". The disputed death of Babowai. The synod of 486, which he accepted in advance. Narsai and the school, as a clause. The renewed quarrel of 491. His marriage, all late and Contested.

**Against the outline.** The outline's heart matches mine in substance. One claim in its Connection field does not hold (B1).

## BLOCKING

**B1. Connection field: "Barsauma took the synod back for its attack on the catholicos, not for its rules."** His own third letter says the opposite. He calls the synod "contraire au christianisme, ainsi que tout ce qui y a été fait", and asks that it be annulled "afin que personne après nous ne puisse faire usage de ces canons" (SO 35455–35461). He also says they made the canons "par la passion humaine" (SO 35405–35406).
*Correction:* replace the sentence with: "Barsauma took back the whole synod, its canons included: 'so that no one after us may use these canons' (SO 35460). Yet the church later accepted its marriage rule (497) and quoted its ruling on Theodore (605)." Add a matching line to Do not say: "Not 'he took back only the attack on the catholicos' or 'he kept its rules': he renounced its canons too (SO 35455–35461)."

**B2. What came 3 (and sheet I4): the "27th year of Peroz" words are not the Armenian bishops' own.** At TM 36676–36742, Babken's letter reports a speech by the Persian Miaphysite delegates who came to Dvin. They are "Samuel, Archimandrit", the priest "Smaön" and others, and the editor identifies "Smaön ... von Berdosma" as Simeon of Beth Arsham (TM 36652–36656; TM-ed 36672–36673). Their speech says "unser Land" held the true faith until the 27th year of Peroz, when "Akak und Barsauma" held synods (TM 36702–36716). It closes at "uns bedränge." and the Armenians add "Dies alles haben wir gehört von jenen" (TM 36742). The Armenians' own words come later: they reject "der Kanones, welche Akak und Barsauma ... aufgestellt haben" (TM 36823–36842).
*Correction:* "About 506, Persian opponents of these synods, Simeon of Beth Arsham among them, told the Armenian bishops at Dvin that the true faith had held in Persia 'until the 27th year of Peroz', when 'Akak and Barsauma' and their companions held synods (TM 36702–36716). The Armenians' letter answers that they, the Greeks, the Iberians and the Albanians did not accept the canons that Akak and Barsauma had set up (TM 36823–36842)." Add a gloss: "**Akak**: the Armenian form of Acacius, the catholicos" (Arbela uses "Akak" for the catholicos of 486; ARB 3884). In Who tells it, credit the first claim to the delegates, not to the Armenian bishops.

**B3. Words to explain, "ascetics", and Also told / beat 5: "wandering holy men in black ... sent back to their monasteries."** The canon does not call them holy men. It calls them men "vêtus de l'habit des ascètes, mais loin d'avoir les vertus que suppose cet habit", who spread heresy and deceive the simple (SO 19699–19703). Canon II calls them "ces fourbes ... par leur habit mensonger" (SO 19752–19754). It bars them from towns and villages that have priests and puts them under the bishops (SO 19760–19797). Telling it as holy men sent home misstates why the synod acted. The approved heart carries the same words, but the sources do not hold them.
*Correction:* gloss: "**ascetics**: men who live by fasting and self-denial. Here, wandering men in monks' black whom the synod called frauds spreading false teaching (SO 19699–19703, 19752–19754)." Also told and beat 5 should read: "it ordered wandering men in monks' black, whom it called frauds teaching error, out of the towns and villages, back to the monasteries and the wild places, under the bishops."

**B4. Do not say: "The 486 and 497 rules allowed clergy to marry; they did not require it ... The only exception: new deacons were to be checked for a lawful marriage."** Canon III goes further than that. A man was to be ordained deacon only "s'ils sont mariés légitimement" (SO 19878–19880). The bishop must first check "s'il a une union légitime et des enfants" (SO 19898–19900). A man who chose not to marry had to live in a monastery (SO 19937–19939). Labourt sums this up: "Le célibat n'est permis qu'aux religieux cloîtrés" (LAB 8678–8679). "Only exception" and "checked for" therefore understate the rule.
*Correction:* "The 486 rule barred bishops from stopping clergy marrying, made a lawful marriage a condition for ordaining new deacons, and sent those who chose not to marry to the monasteries. The 497 rule allowed everyone, up to the head of the church, to marry. Not 'forced every priest to marry'. Not 'it only allowed marriage' either: for new deacons it expected it (SO 19878–19900, 19937–19939)."
