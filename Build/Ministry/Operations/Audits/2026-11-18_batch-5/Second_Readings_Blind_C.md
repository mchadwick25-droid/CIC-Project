# Second Readings, Blind C (batch 5, B5-41 to B5-61)

## B5-41

**Rendering:** there was nowhere in all England to do it. (The words just before the passage are scanned as "a^so *k&t *nere was"; I read them as "also that there was". They fall outside the passage.)

## B5-44
**Source (English):** "if it would stand with the king's most gracious pleasure to grant only a bare text of the scripture to be put forth among his people, like as is put forth among the subjects of the emperor in these parts, and of other Christian princes, be it of the translation of what person soever shall please his majesty, I shall immediately make faithful promise never to write more, nor abide two days in these parts after the same; but immediately repair into his realm, and there most humbly submit myself at the feet of his royal majesty, offering my body to suffer what pain or torture, yea, what death his grace will, so that this be obtained." (117 words)
**Rendering:** If it pleased the king's most gracious will to allow just a plain text of the Scripture to be published among his people, as it is published among the emperor's subjects in these lands and among those of other Christian princes, in whatever person's translation his majesty chooses, I will at once faithfully promise never to write again. I will not stay two days in these lands after that. I will go straight to his kingdom and there most humbly submit myself at his royal majesty's feet. I will offer my body to suffer whatever pain or torture, yes, whatever death his grace wishes, provided that this is granted.

## B5-46
**Source (Latin):** "ubi se vidit rogando nihil proficere, usque ad exsecrationem descendit, ut Deus otio meo malediceret, si me a ferendis subsidiis in tanta necessitate subducerem. Quo terrore perculsus, susceptum iter ita omisi" (31 words)
**Rendering:** When he saw that asking got him nowhere, he went as far as a curse: that God would curse my quiet leisure if I held back from giving help when the need was so great. Struck by this terror, I gave up the journey I had begun, in such a way that… (The 31 words stop mid-sentence; the sentence goes on with an "ut" clause on the next lines.)

## B5-48
**Source (French):** "J'ay vescu icy en combats merveilleux : j'ay esté salué par mocquerie le soir devant ma porte de 50 ou 60 coups d'arquebute. Que pensez-vous que cela pouvoit estonner un pauvre escholier timide comme je suis, et comme je l'ay tousjours esté, je le confesse ?" (46 words)
**Rendering:** I have lived here amid astonishing battles. One evening I was greeted in mockery outside my door with 50 or 60 arquebus shots. How do you think that could shake a poor, timid scholar like me? That is what I am, and what I have always been, I confess it.

## B5-53
**Source (Latin):** "et ecce audio uocem de diuina domo cum cantu dicentis et crebro repetentis quasi pueri an puellae, nescio: „tolle lege, tolle lege“." (22 words)
**Rendering:** And look, I hear a voice from a neighbouring house, chanting and repeating again and again, a boy's or a girl's, I do not know: "Pick it up and read, pick it up and read." (The scan reads "diuina", "divine"; I take it as "uicina", "neighbouring", the usual reading of this sentence. Taken as scanned, it would be "from a divine house".)

## B5-54
**Source (Latin):** "in cuius quaestionis solutione laboratum est quidem pro libero arbitrio uoluntatis humanae, sed uicit dei gratia, nec nisi ad illud potuit perueniri, ut liquidissima ueritate dixisse intellegatur apostolus: quis enim te discernit? quid autem habes, quod non accepisti?" (38 words)
**Rendering:** In solving this question, I did indeed work hard on behalf of the free choice of the human will, but God's grace won. The only conclusion that could be reached was this: the apostle is understood to have spoken with the clearest truth when he said, "For who makes you different? What do you have that you did not receive?" (The quotation goes on past the 38 words.)

## B5-55
**Source (Latin):** "Ego Patricius, peccator rusticissimus et minimus omnium fidelium et contemptibilis sum apud plurimos" (13 words)
**Rendering:** I am Patrick, a sinner, the most unlearned and the least of all the faithful, and despised by very many.

## B5-56
**Source (Latin):** "Rogamus te, sancte puer, ut uenias et adhuc ambulas inter nos." (11 words)
**Rendering:** We ask you, holy boy, to come and walk among us again.

## B5-57
**Source (Latin):** "Ingenuus fui secundum carnem, decoriorie patre nascor : uendidi enim nobilitatem mcain (non erubesco neque me penitet) pro utilitate aliorum : denique seruus sum in Christo [traditus] genti extere" (27 words)
**Rendering:** By birth I was a free man; I was born to a father who was a decurion, a town councillor. For I sold my noble rank, and I am not ashamed of it, nor do I regret it, for the good of others. In short, I am a slave in Christ, handed over to a foreign people. (Scan misreads taken as corrected: "decoriorie" as "decorione", "mcain" as "meam". "[traditus]" is the editor's bracketed supply, rendered as "handed over".)

## B5-58
**Source (Greek):** "Ἐγὼ δὲ ἄλογον ἡγούμενος ἐν τοσαύτῃ συγχύσει χα: ταλεῖψαι τοὺς λαοὺς, χαὶ μὴ μᾶλλον προχινδυνεύειν αὐτῶν" (15 words)
**Rendering:** But I thought it wrong, in such confusion, to abandon the people, rather than to face the danger first, on their behalf. (Scan misreads taken as corrected: "χα: ταλεῖψαι" as "καταλεῖψαι", "χαὶ" as "καὶ", "προχινδυνεύειν" as "προκινδυνεύειν"; the scan prints χ for κ throughout.)
