# Outline check: Augustine of Hippo

## My own heart, written before reading Outline.md

From the census entry (`why`, `teaser`, `relationsSummary`, `legacy`, `voices`), the job in the launch prompt, Mark's approved plain heart, and the source sheet.

**Essence.** A North African teacher of rhetoric, who by his own account sold the skill of winning with words, turned to Christ in Milan (386) and spent the rest of his life as priest and then bishop of one African port town, Hippo. There he wrote the Confessions and the City of God, and taught that grace, God's free gift, comes first: even the beginning of faith is God's gift. The Western churches kept returning to that teaching, from the council at Orange (529) to Luther, Calvin and Jansen.

**Also told.** The road there: Cicero's *Hortensius*, nine years with the Manichaeans, the post at Milan, Ambrose and baptism. The garden in his own account (the child's voice, "Take up and read", Paul's letter, telling Monica). Leaving the trade. Seized and ordained priest at Hippo in tears; allowed to preach against African custom; made bishop beside the living Valerius. The Confessions (praise of God for his bad and good, books 1–10 about himself) and the City of God (after the Goths sacked Rome, answering pagans; two cities made by two loves). His case for compelling the Donatists back by the emperors' laws, after first holding that no one should be forced (Letter 93, c. 408). His long fight with the Pelagians, and the change of mind he dates to the start of his time as bishop. The Vandal siege, the psalms on the wall, the kept library, death in 430. Defended: the grace of Christ (Prosper). Sibling material (Antony's Life, the 411 hearing) a sentence at most.

**Comparison with the outline.** The outline's essence and Also told match this account, and they match Mark's approved plain heart (`Hearts_Plain.md`, "Augustine of Hippo (revised, Mark, 2026-11-18)") almost word for word. No major contribution is left out. The heart holds against the sources: E3, E6a, E9–E13 and E16 are his own words or Possidius's; the Orange claim is credited to its editor (MGHC 4122–4127; NP5 957). **No heart finding.**

## BLOCKING

**B1. Q1's Latin is misreported: "diuina" is not an OCR fault, it is Knoll's printed reading.**
- Knoll's apparatus at AUGL 13489 reads: "diuina S: uicina BCGHMOPQVW edd., uicino F". So the CSEL text in the Library deliberately prints *de diuina domo*, "from a divine house", following manuscript S.
- The outline (Q1) and the sheet (E6a) both bracket it as "[uicina; OCR 'diuina']". That claims the held source says something it does not.
- The rendering "from the house next door" is still defensible: it follows every other manuscript, the earlier editors and Pilkington's English ("coming from a neighbouring house", AUG 13109).
- **Correction.** In Q1 and in E6a, replace the bracket with: "Knoll prints *diuina* ('divine') from one manuscript, S; all the others and earlier editors read *uicina* ('neighbouring'), as Pilkington's English does (AUG 13109). The rendering follows *uicina*." Keep the rendering. Add to Do not say: "Do not say the voice came 'from a divine house' or 'from heaven'; say 'from the house next door', the reading most manuscripts give." Record the variant on the card.

**B2. "Catholic" is glossed only in its 1600s sense, but Q2 uses it in its 408 sense.**
- The only "Catholic" entry is tagged "(Jansen)": "in union with the bishop of Rome... distinct from Luther's and Calvin's churches".
- Q2's rendering has "pretend Catholics" in a letter of 408. A listener needs that word to follow the quote. A writer who applies the listed gloss there would be anachronistic.
- **Correction.** Add to Words to explain: "**Catholic** (Q2, 408): Augustine's name for his own church, the one in communion with the churches across the empire, as against the Donatists (E17a; CEN `conference-of-carthage-411`: 'the church spread through the whole world')." Retag the existing entry "**Catholic** (Jansen, 1600s)". Add "Catholic (both senses)" to the ruling's Do not say list.

**B3. The length and the beat budgets contradict each other.**
- The Length field gives about 520 words (3:30). The beat budgets add up to 445 words.
- Beat 1 plus the opening and essence is "about 150 words", so the opening and essence take about 105. That makes about 550 words before What came of him (which the kit sets at "about a third", roughly 170 words) and the Ending.
- As written, the outline runs to about 700 words (about 4:40). The writer would have to choose what to cut, and the likeliest cut is the connection. That is the outline's decision to make, not the writer's.
- **Correction:** re-budget so the parts sum to the stated length, without cutting the force of the Donatist beat or the scene in the garden. A working set:
  - Opening about 40 words.
  - Essence about 70 (it already names Orange to Jansen).
  - Beat 1 about 30 (see M1).
  - Beat 2 about 70.
  - Beat 3 about 45.
  - Beat 4 about 45.
  - Beat 5 about 50.
  - Beat 6 about 55.
  - Beat 7 about 25.
  - What came of him about 70 (it adds only the editor's credit for Orange, Luther's book, Calvin's volume and Jansen's condemnation). The grace content of beats 4 and 6 also carries the connection.
  - Ending about 25.
  - Total about 525.
- If the researcher judges that the beats cannot hold at these sizes, set the length from the beats instead (about 4:00, about 600 words). Put that change to Mark in one line, because his approved plain heart names 3:30.

## MINOR

- **M1. The essence's line is repeated in beat 1.** The essence already says he "sold the skill of talking to win" (E3), and beat 1 says it again. Delete "In his own words he sold the skill of talking to win (E3)." from beat 1.
- **M2. "Envied" and "full of lies" go slightly past the text.** In the opening and Do not say, Augustine says he "sighed" and lamented that the beggar had the joy he was straining for (AUG 9241–9255: "he was joyous, I anxious"). "Envied" is not his word. "Plura mentirer" is "many a lie" (AUG 9234–9235), not "full of lies". Correct the opening to: "a speech in praise of the emperor in which, he says, he would tell many lies... he sighed: the beggar already had the happiness he was straining after." Correct the Do not say line on the beggar to match.
- **M3. "Defending the grace of Christ" is already the Prosper story's closing line.** What came of him, item 1, puts it in quote marks while saying "no quote". The `prosper-of-aquitaine` live story ends "still defending the grace of Christ", and the outline's own Sibling field lists that entry as not to be retold. Replace it with: "Prosper of Aquitaine, in Gaul, took up his defense (I2; link)". Drop the phrase.
- **M4. Beat 7 retells the fever, which the Sibling field reserves for the Possidius story.** "In the third month he fell ill" (POS 4146–4148) is the `possidius-of-calama` story's line. Say "In his last illness, during the siege," and go straight to the psalms (POS 4925–4932).
- **M5. Beat 5's "argued with it for decades" has no cited line.** Say "for years" (POS 3072–3075), or cite a line that gives the span.
- **M6. Vincentius belonged to a breakaway group.** In beat 5, "a Donatist bishop, Vincentius" should be "a bishop of a small Donatist breakaway group". The editor's title is "ad Vincentium Rogatistam" (AUG 2898; sheet E17a).
- **M7. "His own home town" is an inference.** The Do not say line says "Say 'his own home town'". The letter says "ciuitas mea", "my own town" (EPL 31546–31547). Reading it as Thagaste is an inference, not the text. Say "his own town", and keep "home town" out unless it is tagged as the editors' reading.
- **M8. Several glosses say more than their sources.**
  - **bishop**: "chosen with its people's loud consent" generalizes from one event at Hippo (POS 2133–2137). Use "the leader of the church in one town".
  - **baptized**: "adults then were usually baptized at Easter" is not in POS 1671–1673, which says only that he was baptized at Easter. Use "washed in water to join the church; he was baptized at Easter".
  - **Donatists**: "called itself the only pure one" goes past CEN `donatism`. Use "a rival African church, for a century the larger one, which held that bishops who had handed over the Scriptures in persecution had lost the church".
  - **Goths, Vandals**: "Germanic" has no cited line. Cite a held line or say "peoples from beyond the empire's frontier; the Vandals crossed from Spain" (POS 3945–3956).
- **M9. "Prefect" is missing from Words to explain.** It is a passing period word in beat 1. Add: "**prefect of Rome**: the city's governor (AUG 8514–8519)". Or say "the governor of Rome".
- **M10. "Popes" versus "bishops of Rome" is inconsistent.** Words to explain says to use "bishops of Rome, not popes". What came of him item 1 then says "Pope Boniface II", and item 3 says "Rome". Pick one: "Boniface II, bishop of Rome", or gloss "pope" once as "the bishop of Rome".
- **M11. Q1's tenses shift.** The rendering moves from present to past ("I hear... It was like..."). Make it "...singing and repeating it again and again, like a boy's or a girl's voice, I do not know which: 'Take it and read, take it and read.'"
- **M12. "When they are read" is closer to the Latin.** Beat 4 says "and still did when he read them". The Latin is "et agunt, cum leguntur" (RET 9435–9436). Say "and still do, he says, whenever they are read".
