## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Augustine, a paid teacher of public speaking in Milan who turned to Christ and became bishop of Hippo, a port town in Roman North Africa.
2. **What happened:** a child's voice in a Milan garden sent him to Paul's letters. The crowd at Hippo forced him into the priesthood, and he became its bishop. He changed his mind and came to hold that God's grace comes first. He backed the emperors' laws to force the Donatists back. He answered the sack of Rome with the City of God and fought Pelagius. He died with the Vandals at the walls.
3. **Why the connection matters:** his books carried the teaching that grace comes first into the Western churches. Bishops at Orange drew their rulings from it, Luther and Calvin read him against Rome, and Rome condemned a Catholic bishop's book named after him.

He taught that God's grace comes before anything a person does, and the Western churches carried that teaching on, and argued over it, for more than a thousand years.

## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B1. Half of the approved essence is not told in the first minute.** The essence paragraph gives the rhetoric, the garden, the bishop and grace. It leaves out two of the heart's parts: that at Hippo he wrote the Confessions and the City of God, and that the Western churches carried his teaching on. These first come at about words 530 and 685. A listener who stopped after one minute could not say why he is on the tree beyond "he taught grace". The kit allows the essence to reach ahead.
*Change:* after "He became bishop ... in Roman North Africa", add one sentence with the outline's own glosses, e.g. "There he wrote the Confessions, his life told as a prayer to God, and the City of God, on those who love God above themselves." After the grace sentence, add one more, from the outline's line 15: "The churches of western Europe carried that teaching on, and it both shaped and divided them." Paragraph 3 can then shorten "thirteen books addressed to God in praise and in owning up to sin" to "the moment he tells in his Confessions, written to God in praise and in owning up to sin". That keeps the "owning up" sense but does not gloss the word twice. Paragraph 7 keeps the two loves as the deepening.
*Do not say line for the outline:* "Do not hold back the two books (the Confessions, the City of God) or the Western churches carrying on his teaching until after the first minute. Both are part of the essence (Mark, 2026-11-18) and are told, briefly, by about word 150."
