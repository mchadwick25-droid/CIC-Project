## My own heart (from census, sheet, approved heart)

- **Essence.** A boy from a Roman family in Britain was taken to Ireland as a slave at about sixteen. He escaped after six years. Later he went back to the same people as a bishop, by his own choice. His two short Latin writings, the Confession and the Letter against the soldiers of Coroticus, are the one sure voice from the first church in Ireland. Later Irish and Welsh writers put him at the start of their story (CEN `why`, `teaser`, `sourcing`; E1, E3, E7, E13; I1).
- **Also told.** He says he baptized many thousands, had clergy appointed, and saw chiefs' children take vows. He was opposed by his own elders and seized and chained by kings. Raiders killed and enslaved his new converts, and he wrote the Letter. Armagh kept his memory. The Irish church sang of him as "teacher of the Irish". Legend grew later and is told as late.

## Verdict: FIX

### BLOCKING

**B1. The friend "made known" the sin: unsupported, and marked Documented.** In Also told: "Later they brought up against him a sin of his youth that his dearest friend had made known (E10; Documented …)". Patrick says he confided the matter to his dearest friend, and that the elders found it "after the lapse of thirty years" (PE 2157–2163). Separately, he says that friend later shamed him in public "in respect of an [office]" (PE 2226–2232). He never says the friend disclosed it. The outline's own Do not say forbids "his friend betrayed him" as the narrator's verdict, and this line states that same betrayal as a fact.
*Correction:* "Later they brought up against him a sin of his youth, something he had once confided to his dearest friend. That same friend later shamed him in public (E10; Documented as his account; he does not say how the elders learned of it, and the sin is never named)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'his friend revealed the sin'. He does not say how the elders learned of it."

**B2. "Armagh copied and kept" both writings: the held source shows only the Confession at Armagh.** White's list of the Book of Armagh's contents gives "An imperfect copy of St. Patrick's Confession" (PE-ed 1547–1551) and nothing of the Letter. The outline puts both writings at Armagh in four places:
- Also told: "his writings were kept and copied at Armagh".
- What came 1: "One copy is in the Book of Armagh … where the two are titled 'the Books of Saint Patrick'".
- Connection: "his two letters, which Armagh copied and kept".
- Words to explain, Armagh: "the church that kept his writings".

White does say the earliest title "of the two tracts" is the Book of Armagh's *Libri Sancti Patricii* (PE-ed 1657–1659). That is a heading, not a copy of the Letter.
*Correction:* What came 1: "His Confession was copied, incomplete, into the Book of Armagh by the scribe of Armagh between 807 and 846, under the heading 'the Books of Saint Patrick' (PE-ed 1532–1536, 1549–1550, 1657–1659). Other manuscripts carry both writings (PE-ed 1584, 1617–1619)." In Also told and Connection, change the wording to "his Confession was copied at Armagh, and both writings were kept" or "his writings were kept, and Armagh copied the Confession". In the Armagh gloss, change "the church that kept his writings" to "the church that copied his Confession". Add to Do not say: "Not 'Armagh kept both his writings'. Its book holds an incomplete Confession."

**B3. Deacon gloss: the period claim misstates its cited source.** The gloss says: "Then, deacons and priests could be married men with sons … (S1; White, PE-ed 414–430). Say so if the listener might find it odd." White's actual words: the family's clergy "reflects an age and a society in which disregard of the rule of celibacy for the secular clergy created no scandal" (PE-ed 413–416). White assumes a rule that was being disregarded. The outline gives a general permission. A listener would come away with a period fact the source frames the other way. (`Hearts_Plain.md` carries the same "who could marry then".)
*Correction:* "**deacon**: 'an assistant minister in the church'. His father was a deacon and his grandfather a priest (S1, Documented). If the listener might find it odd, say only that Patrick mentions it without comment. White notes that in that time and place it caused no scandal (PE-ed 413–416). Do not say 'clergy could marry then' as a rule."

### MINOR

**M1. A quote is already on the tree.** Beat 3 has "a bishop, appointed by God, in Ireland" in quotation marks. The tradition entry's documented story "Patrick Excommunicates a Warlord" already quotes "I am a bishop, appointed by God in Ireland". *Correction:* tell it in paraphrase without quotation marks: "He says only that God made him a bishop in Ireland; he never says who made him bishop, or when (E7)."

**M2. Too many quoted spans beyond the three Quotes.** The beats and the Ending also quote "with many thousands", "The Voice of the Irish", "fellow-citizens of demons", "what manner of man I am", "those strangers and captives", "Patrick the sinner, unlearned", "my confession before I die" and "teacher of the Irish". *Correction:* add one line under Quotes: "Only Quotes 1–3 are quoted aloud, plus at most two short phrases: 'The Voice of the Irish' and 'fellow-citizens of demons'. Tell the rest in paraphrase. 'Patrick the sinner, unlearned' at the end may echo Quote 1's words as the opening image seen again." Also, "those strangers and captives" is White's English for *proselitis et captiuis* (ST 7178). If it is kept, render it from the Latin ("with those newcomers and captives").

**M3. Missing glosses on words in the outline.**
- "annals" (Setting, Ending, Who tells it) needs a gloss: "year-by-year records, compiled and copied over centuries" (sheet, Witness kinds: AU).
- "barbarian nations" (What came 2) needs a gloss, because its modern sense misleads. Say "the peoples beyond the empire", or gloss "barbarian" as "outside the Roman world" (BAN 5053–5055).
- "Life", as in "wrote his Life" (Links, What came 3), needs a gloss: "a saint's life story, written to honour him".
- "ordained" is used in What came 1 ("ordained by the pope"), beat 4 and Connection, although the list says to avoid it. Say "appointed" ("made bishop by the pope", "had church ministers appointed").

**M4. Glosses that say more than their source.**
- Bishop: "a bishop for a people, not a city" has no cited support. White says only "missionary bishop" (PE-ed 841). Cut that clause.
- Hymn: "sung by the monks of Bangor". The source shows a hymn copied in the Bangor manuscript (BAN 3, 5011). Say "copied by the monks of Bangor", which matches What came 2.

**M5. Small overreaches in Also told and Connection.**
- "He paid kings, their sons and judges to move safely". He says he paid "that they might receive me" and for sons "who accompany me" (PE 2581–2591, 2602–2605). Say "to be received and escorted".
- "Senior churchmen in Britain tried to stop him going (E6)". E6 does not place the elders. Say "his own elders", or tag "in Britain" as Inferential-Thin.
- Connection: "a bishop had been sent to Irish believers just before him". "Just before him" rests on the annals' 432, which is Contested (D2). Say "in 431, before Patrick's own work there is dated".

**M6. Quote 3 drops Stokes's *[traditus]* without saying so.** The cut is fine, because the word is an editor's insertion (ST 7374). Add after the Latin: "(Stokes inserts *[traditus]*, 'handed over', after *in Christo*; left out as an editor's word.)"

**M7. Do not say gaps.** Add:
- "Do not quote Muirchu's 'My skill is small; my authorities are uncertain'. It is the Muirchu story's opening quote. Paraphrase it, as What came 3 does."
- "Do not name an author for the hymn (tradition gives it to Secundinus/Sechnall). Say 'a hymn copied at Bangor'."
- "'About 240 years later' for Muirchu assumes a death date. Say 'more than two centuries later'."

**M8. Length is tight for the beats as written.** About 410 words must hold the opening, the essence, six full beats, a third for What came, and the ending. Beats 3 to 6 alone carry the call, the elders, the cost, the attack and the raid. The claim that the opening, the essence and beats 1–2 fit in "about 150 words" does not add up (by my count about 200). This is not filling. The record has the scenes. *Correction:* "about 3 minutes (about 450 words)". Mark as optional, to be cut first: the Gaul/Franks comparison (beat 6), the fifteen people and the ornaments (beat 4), and What came 3.

**M9. Library sweep: one set-aside note is wrong.** The sheet says Adomnán's hits are "Huyshe's notes … not Adomnán on Patrick". But Adomnán's own second preface names "a disciple of the holy bishop Patrick, Mochta by name" (`adomnan_life-of-saint-columba_huyshe1905.txt` 2169–2171). That is a held witness from Iona, about 700, outside Armagh. Nothing in the outline rests on it, so this does not block. *Correction:* fix the note in the sheet. Optionally add it as a one-clause witness in What came 2.
