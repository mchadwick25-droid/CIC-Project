## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Patrick, a boy from a Roman family in Britain who was taken to Ireland as a slave and later went back there as a bishop. Nearly all we know comes from his own two short writings.
- **What happened:** he escaped after six years, answered a dream calling him back, and went against his seniors' doubts. He baptized many and saw chiefs' children take vows. Kings chained him and his own churchmen attacked him. When a warlord's soldiers killed and sold his new converts, he wrote a furious letter against them.
- **Why the connection matters:** he built a church among a people outside the empire, though he was not the first Christian there. Armagh copied his Confession and a later hymn called him teacher of the Irish, yet the sure Patrick is the one in his own two writings.

All three lines came from one hearing. None is blank or wrong.

## Verdict: REVISE

One BLOCKING finding, fixed by adding one sentence.

### BLOCKING

**B1. Later legend, credited as late, is missing.** It is part of the approved heart ("later legend credited as late", `Hearts_Plain.md`) and of the outline's Also told ("Legend grew around him centuries later, and it is credited plainly as late (I4)"). Many listeners know Patrick through the later legends. The story never tells them those legends are late and are not his. The outline's Length lets the writer cut What came 3 first, but that option is the fox story, not the one line the heart requires. **Change:** after the hymn sentence, add one line, for example: "Legends grew up around him centuries later, in the work of writers who came long after." (About 15 words, from I4: Muirchu, more than two centuries on; the *History of the Britons*, c. 829.) Do not add the fox, the snakes or any named legend unless you also credit it to its late witness.
**Do not say line to add to the outline:** "Cutting What came 3 cuts only Muirchu's fox story. One line that the legends came centuries later, from later writers, stays in every version (Also told, I4)."

### MINOR

**M1. "He grew up in fifth-century Roman Britain."** The outline gives "the fifth century" for his life and work. He gives no year (S3, D2). The only reckoning held, White's from Bury, puts his birth in 389, so his boyhood falls before the fifth century, and before the legions left in 410. **Change:** "He grew up in Roman Britain, then a province of the empire, where his father..." Then place the century where his work in Ireland is told, for example "In the fifth century he went back of his own will...". Alternatively, drop the century, since the 431 bishop already dates the story. A Do not say line for the outline: "Not 'fifth-century' for his childhood. Tie the century to his work in Ireland; his birth year is an editor's reckoning."

**M2. "Had church ministers appointed everywhere" is absent.** It is in the outline's Also told and in its Connection ("the bridge he built is a church: baptism, clergy and vowed lives"). The bishop gloss hints at it but does not tell it. **Change:** "He says he baptized many thousands, washing them in water to make them Christians, and had church ministers appointed everywhere." (+6 words; E8, PE 2329–2331, 2584–2586.)

**M3. The friend sentence is unclear at one hearing.** "At one time, churchmen held against him a sin of his youth, once told to his dearest friend, who later shamed him in public." The passive "once told" leaves the teller unclear, and the listener is pushed toward "the friend gave it away". The outline bars that as a claim ("he does not say how the elders learned of it"). **Change:** make Patrick the teller and split the sentence. For example: "At one time, some of his own senior churchmen held against him a sin from his youth, something he had once confided to his dearest friend. That same friend later shamed him in public." This uses E10 only.

**Sentences.** None runs past 30 words. Two reach exactly 30 (the dream, the raid); both carry one chain and read well aloud. No split is needed.
