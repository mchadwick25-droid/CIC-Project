## tree/the-union-of-brest-and-ruthenian-greek-catholic-
### Revised text
In 1596 most of the Orthodox bishops of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth met at Brest and accepted communion with Rome while keeping their Byzantine liturgy, Church Slavonic and married clergy. Their decree gives reasons: the church needs one head, built on Peter, and the patriarchs of Constantinople lived under the Turks. Their opponents met in the same town and rejected the union. They declared the bishops cast down for putting a church that answered to Constantinople under old Rome, against the holy canons. So the union produced two rival hierarchies claiming the same people and arguing in print for a century. The new church had to answer to Rome, to the Orthodox it had left, and to Latin bishops who never quite treated it as an equal. Archbishop Josaphat Kuntsevych, of the new church, was killed at Vitebsk in 1623. His biographer Jakub Susza writes that two men burst in crying that he was a Latin and a papist.

## tree/church-of-caucasian-albania
### Revised text
Beside Armenia and Georgia there was a third Christian kingdom in the Caucasus. This was Albania, in what is now Azerbaijan and the south of Dagestan. It had its own royal line, its own bishops and its own alphabet. In the late fifth century it held a council at Aghuen, which made rules for the clergy and for marriage. It was pressed between Persian, Byzantine and then Arab power, and between the rival loyalties of its Armenian and Georgian neighbors. In the early 700s the Armenian catholicos Elia held a council at Partav, on the Arab ruler Abd al-Malik's order. He had Nerses Bakur, the Albanian catholicos, brought there. Stepanos of Taron, an Armenian writer of about the year 1000, calls Nerses a false catholicos who tried to make the Albanians accept the Council of Chalcedon. The Armenian church rejected that council for teaching two natures in Christ. The old sources do not say what followed. Albania's written language was all but lost for more than a thousand years. Then, in 1996, palimpsests at Saint Catherine's Monastery on Sinai were found to hold Caucasian Albanian scripture. The pages had been scraped and written over with later Georgian text.

## tree/the-liturgical-movement
### Revised text
In September 1909 Dom Lambert Beauduin spoke at a Catholic congress in Malines, Belgium. The journal Orate Fratres later reported what he said. He pointed to how little ordinary people knew of the liturgy, the Church's public worship. He named the harm this did and a remedy. In all his work he urged a revival of the liturgy for religious and social reasons, and to defend the faith. The movement began at once at his abbey, Mont-César in Louvain, and spread rapidly. In 1926 Virgil Michel of Saint John's Abbey in Minnesota founded that journal, the movement's voice. He also set up the Liturgical Press. The journal's first number said it aimed to let worship shape each Catholic and each parish.
