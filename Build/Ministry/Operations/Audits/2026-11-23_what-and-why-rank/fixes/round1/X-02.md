## tree/the-liturgical-movement
### Revised text
In September 1909 Dom Lambert Beauduin, a monk of Mont-César in Louvain, spoke at a Catholic congress in Malines, Belgium. The journal Orate Fratres later reported what he said. He pointed to how little ordinary people knew of the liturgy, the Church's public worship. He named the harm this did and a remedy. He urged a revival of the liturgy for religious and social reasons, and to defend the faith. In 1926 Virgil Michel of Saint John's Abbey in Minnesota founded the journal Orate Fratres, the movement's voice. He also set up the Liturgical Press. Its first number said it aimed at an understanding of worship that would shape the life of the individual Catholic and of the parish.

## worlds-tree/cappadocian-nicene-pastoral-monastic-tradition/legacy
### Revised text
The teachers of these churches did not stay local. Their names and arguments became curriculum, liturgy and law for later traditions. Those traditions trace their roots through the councils these churches lived through and helped write.

Orthodox, Catholic and, more distantly, Protestant teaching all still argue in vocabulary this circle fought to place. It is the vocabulary of one God in three persons. Basil set out the two Greek terms in a letter to Count Terentius. Ousia, the being, is common to Father, Son and Spirit. Hypostasis, the person, is what is particular to each: Fatherhood, Sonship, the power to sanctify.

Did the three teachers ever share one settled scheme for these words? The old sources we hold do not settle it.
