## tree/augustine-of-hippo
Heart: Augustine taught rhetoric, the art of persuasive public speaking, and in his own words sold the skill of talking to win (E3). In a garden in Milan, by his own account, a child's voice sent him to the Bible, and he turned to Christ (E6a). He became bishop, the leader of the church, in Hippo, a port town in Roman North Africa (E9–E11). There he wrote the Confessions, his life told as a prayer to God, and the City of God. He taught that grace, God's free and unearned gift, comes first: no one turns to God, or even begins to believe, unless God gives it (E12–E16). Confidence: Documented (his own words; Possidius, his friend). The churches of western Europe carried this teaching on: a meeting of bishops at Orange in 529, then Luther, Calvin and the Jansenists. Each witness is Documented; that Orange's rulings came from his writings is its editors' finding, Widely Accepted (I1, I3, I4, D5).

## tree/cyprian-of-carthage
Heart: Cyprian was a teacher of public speaking at Carthage who became its bishop while still a new Christian, about two years after his baptism by modern reckoning (E1–E3; rhetoric Widely Accepted, from Jerome and Lactantius; "still a new Christian" Documented, Pontius; "two years" Dominant Modern Reconstruction, editors' dates of 246 and 248). For ten years, 248 to 258, he led the church of Roman Africa through persecution, plague and division, and some eighty letters, from him and to him, show how that church was run (S2; EP, Ep. 1 to 81; Documented). He taught that the church is one mother, and that no one has God as Father without her; he died by the sword at Carthage in 258, the first of its bishops to die a martyr (E8, E13; Documented; "first" is Pontius's claim).

## tree/irenaeus-of-lyon
Heart: Irenaeus heard the old bishop Polycarp as a boy in the Roman province of Asia, and became bishop of Lyon in Gaul after the martyrs of 177 (S1, E4, E5; Documented, his own words and Eusebius). There he wrote five books *Against Heresies*, about 180, the fullest answer we still have to the followers of Valentinus, who claimed a secret, higher teaching (E7, S6; the book Documented; its date under bishop Eleutherius Documented, "about 180" Widely Accepted; "fullest answer we still have", census, Widely Accepted). Against them he set three things: the faith every church received at baptism, the churches the apostles founded, with their bishops counted one after another back to the apostles, and four Gospels, no more and no fewer (T2–T6; Documented as his teaching).

## tree/ambrose-of-milan
Heart: In 374 the people of Milan, where the western emperors then held court, shouted for their governor, Ambrose, to be their bishop, though he was not yet baptized; within eight days he was baptized and made bishop (E1–E5; Documented). For twenty-three years he held that the church's buildings and its sacrament were not the emperor's to command: he would not give up a church to the empress's party, and he kept the emperor Theodosius from communion until he did public penance for a massacre (E8, E17, E18; Documented, his own letters and several witnesses).

## tree/jerome
Heart: Jerome, a Latin scholar of the late fourth century, learned Hebrew so that he could put the Old Testament into Latin from the Hebrew itself, not from the Greek translation the churches read; before that, at Rome, Damasus, the bishop of Rome, had set him to correct the Latin Gospels against the Greek (E1, E5, E12, E18; Documented, his own prefaces and letters; the Bethlehem dates 391–404 are the editor's, Dominant Modern Reconstruction). His Latin Bible was fought over in his lifetime, but in time it became the Western church's standard text (I1; Documented as Isidore's judgment and the Trent decree; that Trent's "old and Vulgate edition" is chiefly his work is the editor's and Widely Accepted).
