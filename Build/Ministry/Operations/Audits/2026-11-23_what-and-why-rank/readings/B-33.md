## tree/apollinaris-of-laodicea
Heart: Apollinaris of Laodicea was a learned Christian teacher in Syria who stood with Athanasius for the creed of Nicaea, and then taught that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind. The church condemned that teaching at Rome and at Constantinople in 381, and his followers formed churches of their own.

## tree/eunomius-of-cyzicus
Heart: Eunomius of Cyzicus taught that "unbegotten" is not only a word for God but God's very being, so the Son, who is begotten, cannot share the Father's being. His Apology drew an answer from Basil of Caesarea, and his reply to Basil one from Gregory of Nyssa; his followers were called by his name; he was briefly bishop of Cyzicus, and under the emperor Theodosius he was banished and died soon after.

## tree/damasus-of-rome
Heart: Damasus was bishop of Rome from 366 to 384. He won the office in an election fought with clubs and killing. He then held his church to the faith of Nicaea, and in 380 the emperors' law named him as that faith's standard. *(Election: AMM 23176–23189, Documented; the clubs: AVE 5107, 5159, the Ursinian account, Contested; the dates: S1, S4, E53, Dominant Modern Reconstruction; the law: CTH 83598–83601, Documented.)*

## tree/cyril-of-alexandria
Heart: Cyril was bishop of Alexandria, the head of the church in Egypt's great city, from 412 to 444. He taught that Christ is one: God's own Son, whom the Bible calls the Word, was himself born of Mary as a man, so she may rightly be called Mother of God, the one who gave birth to God (E4: Documented, his own words). He led the attack on Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople, which ended with a council of bishops at Ephesus in 431 removing Nestorius from office (E6: Documented). His twelve short statements of faith, each cursing those who denied it, and his phrase "one incarnate nature of God the Word", meaning one Christ, God become man and never divided, became the test of true faith for the churches of Egypt, Syria and Armenia that later refused the Council of Chalcedon (I1, I2: Documented for Egypt and Syria; Armenia from the census Chalcedon text, I4). Chalcedon, the council of 451 that said Christ is known in two natures, fully God and fully human, also took his letters as a guide (I4: Documented).

## tree/leo-of-rome
Heart: Leo, bishop of Rome from 440 to 461, wrote the letter called the Tome in 449 to say how Christ is one person, fully God and fully human; two years later the Council of Chalcedon took it as its rule of faith (E7, E17, E18, I1; Documented). He held that Peter still spoke and ruled through the bishop of Rome, and he would not accept the council's canon that ranked Constantinople next to Rome (E3, E21, E22; Documented, his own words).

## tree/councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251
Heart: When the persecution under the emperor Decius ended, many Christians who had given way under it asked to come back to the church (E1, E2, E6; Documented). In 251 the bishops of Africa met at Carthage under Cyprian, and then sixty bishops at Rome under Cornelius, and chose a middle way: the fallen would not be denied all hope of return, and none would come straight back; they would do long penance, and each case would be examined (E6, E8; Documented, from Cyprian, who called the first council, and from Eusebius's summary of Cornelius's letters). A stricter party would take none of them back to communion; it set up Novatian as a rival bishop of Rome, and the church he led split from the rest and lasted for centuries (E7, E8, I1; Documented).

## tree/council-of-nicaea-325
Heart: In 325 the emperor Constantine called bishops to Nicaea, where they condemned Arius's teaching that the Son was a creature with a beginning and wrote a creed calling the Son of one being with the Father; it became the church's first creed, though it divided the church for decades.

## tree/council-of-chalcedon-451
Heart: In 451 the council of Chalcedon, called by the emperor Marcian, confessed Christ as one person known in two natures, following Leo's Tome. The Greek and Latin churches kept it, while the churches of Egypt, Syria and Armenia refused it as a split of Christ and still stand apart.
