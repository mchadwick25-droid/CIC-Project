## tree/pamphilus-of-caesarea
Heart: Pamphilus, a presbyter of the church at Caesarea in Roman Palestine, gathered a library of sacred books for his church and, Jerome says, copied most of Origen's books with his own hand; Jerome held twenty-five of those volumes about eighty years later (E3, E4; the library Documented by Eusebius and Jerome; the copying Documented as Jerome's report, with Photius's "is said"). Arrested in the Great Persecution, he wrote a Defense of Origen in prison, working with his pupil and friend Eusebius, and was put to death for his faith about 310 (E7, E8, E11; Documented, Eusebius's own account).

## tree/francis-of-assisi
Heart: Francis, a trader's son of Assisi in central Italy, gave up his family's wealth in the first years of the 1200s, went to serve the lepers he had once loathed, and took to the roads to preach (E2–E6; Documented: his own Testament for the lepers, Celano for the rest). The brothers who joined him, the Friars Minor, the "lesser brothers", were to own nothing, work with their hands and beg when no wage came; by day they went among the townspeople, and he refused a cardinal's advice to live as a monk or a hermit (E7, E9–E11; Documented, his own Rule and Testament, Celano for the refusal; a contemporary bishop saw how they lived, E10).

## tree/bernard-of-clairvaux
Heart: In 1113 Bernard, a young Burgundian noble, brought more than thirty companions, his brothers among them, into the poor new monastery of Cîteaux (S3, E1; Documented, William, who knew him). Two years later he was made abbot of a new house, Clairvaux, and from there, by his sermons and his letters to popes, he became, in the tree's own words, the most listened-to churchman in Europe; popes and kings asked his word (S4, S6, E5, E6; Documented; "most listened-to" is the tree's own judgment, Widely Accepted).

## tree/thomas-aquinas
Heart: Thomas Aquinas, a Dominican friar who taught theology at Paris in the thirteenth century, wrote the Summa of Theology, a teaching book that sets out the case against each point and then answers it (E7, E9; Documented, his own words). He held that faith and reason cannot truly conflict: the opposite of a truth can never be proved, so every argument against the faith can be answered (E8; Documented, his own words). The church made him a saint in 1323 and ranked his feast with those of its great teachers (I1, I2; Documented).
