## tree/tertullian-s-voice
Heart: Tertullian of Carthage, a convert who knew Roman law well, as Eusebius says, was the foremost early writer of the faith in Latin, around the year 200 (S1, S2, S5, S7, D4; Carthage and the reigns Documented; "trained in Roman law" Eusebius's word, Documented as his, Contested that he practised as an advocate; "foremost" is rank, not order: Vincent, "as Origen holds by far the first place among the Greeks, so does Tertullian among the Latins", VIN 13555–13557; Coxe, "the founder of Latin Christianity", ANF4 17041–17042; Widely Accepted). He defended the Christians before Rome's governors, claimed the Scriptures for the churches the apostles founded, and gave Latin its words for God as one substance in three persons; then he turned to the strict New Prophecy, and two centuries later Augustine counted his followers at Carthage, the Tertullianists, among the heretics (E1, E4, T4, T5, E5, I5, E6; the books Documented; "gave Latin its words" Widely Accepted, census and editors; Augustine's list Documented, AUG 2827–2835, 2868–2873).
