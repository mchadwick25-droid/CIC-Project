## tree/josephus
Heart: Josephus was a priest from Jerusalem who commanded the Jewish forces in Galilee when the Jews rose against Rome. In 67 he gave himself up to the Roman general Vespasian, and he spent the rest of his life at Rome, under the emperors' favour, writing his people's history (E1, E2, E6, S5; Documented, almost all from his own books). In his *Antiquities of the Jews*, finished about 93, he wrote a few lines each on John the Baptist, on James "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ", and on Jesus himself (E11–E14; the John and James lines Widely Accepted as his). Christians kept and quoted those lines for centuries as a witness from outside the church. The passage on Jesus is the one they argued over. It has been disputed since the sixteenth century, an editor wrote in 1890 (EH-ed 9182–9185). Whether he wrote it as it stands is **Contested** (D2).

## tree/suetonius
Heart: Suetonius, a Roman scholar who wrote the lives of twelve of Rome's rulers, from Julius Caesar to Domitian, around 120, gives the Christians two short lines and no more. Under Claudius, he writes, the Jews were driven from Rome for rioting at the urging of "Chrestus"; under Nero, the Christians were punished as people of a new and harmful superstition (E1, E3: Documented as his words; the date of the book, S4: Dominant Modern Reconstruction). He is on the tree because these are among the very few outside notices of the first Christians at Rome, and readers still argue over who "Chrestus" was (I1, D1: Documented that they are read so; who Chrestus was, Contested).
