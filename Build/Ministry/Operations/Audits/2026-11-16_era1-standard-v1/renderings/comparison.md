# Comparison of the two renderings, E1 to E39

Compared by an independent Opus reader. I checked both renderings against printed.md and against the cic/texts files themselves, by line range. I made no model calls and changed no other file.

Verdicts:
- **same**: the wording differs but the meaning does not.
- **minor**: a nuance differs but no claim changes.
- **changes meaning**: one reading says what the source does not, drops a clause, or settles a contested sense.

"Unchanged" means the printed English is already plain modern English and is kept as printed. "= A" or "= B" means the final is that rendering word for word; "new" means neither.

Original-language checks. "In file" means the file shows the form, at the line given. "NOT IN FILE" means a renderer named it from memory and the file does not show it. A claim that is not in the file may still be right, but it cannot go on a card as verified. Many originals here are held only as later quotations (Mansi, Schanz, Spener, a martyr-act, a papal letter); I say so where it applies.

## E1 (josephus; Whiston 1859, lines 91005-91010)
- **Printed:** "the brother of Je- sus, who was called Christ, whose name was James"
- **A:** Unchanged: "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James"
- **B:** "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, a man named James"
- **Verdict:** same
- **FINAL:** "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James" (unchanged; only the scan's line break is mended)
- **Original check:** The Greek is in file in McGiffert's note, npnf201 lines 13594-13602: "τὸν ἀδελφὸν ᾽Ιησοῦ τοῦ χριστοῦ λεγομένου, ᾽Ι€κωβος ὄνομα αὐτῷ" (OCR "Ι€κωβος"). The writer's range "E 13592-13597" stops before the name; the name is at 13601-13602. B's second witness is in file: Origen, Lommatzsch, lines 3729-3730, "τὸν ἀδελφὸν Ἰησοῦ τοῦ λεγομένου Χριστοῦ".

## E2 (josephus; Whiston 1859, lines 81405-81407)
- **Printed:** "a wise man, if it be lawful to call hima man"
- **A:** "a wise man, if it is right to call him a man"
- **B:** "a wise man, if indeed one should call him a man"
- **Verdict:** same
- **FINAL:** "a wise man, if it is right to call him a man" (= A)
- **Original check:** B's Greek is in file, but only as a later quotation inside a martyr-act: Acta Sanctorum Maii V, line 80256, "Ἰἠσοῦς σοφὸς ἀνήρ· εἴγε ἄνδρα αὐτὸν λέγειν Xp" (OCR "Xp" for χρή). Josephus's own Greek is not held. A's English witness is in file: npnf201 lines 9229-9230, "if indeed it be proper to call him a man".

## E3 (josephus; Whiston 1859, line 81411)
- **Printed:** "He was [the] Christ"
- **A:** "He was the Christ"
- **B:** "This man was the Christ."
- **Verdict:** minor. B renders οὗτος as "this man"; A keeps "He". Both drop the brackets, and the Greek supports that.
- **FINAL:** "He was the Christ" (= A). The demonstrative is ordinary Greek for "he" here, and the plainer form is kept.
- **Original check:** In file, Acta Sanctorum Maii V, **line 80262** (B cites 80259, which is wrong): "Ὃ Χριστὸς οὗτος ἦν". The article is there, so Whiston's brackets go. This is a later quotation, not Josephus's own text. A's English witness is in file at npnf201 line 9232: "He was the Christ."
- **Card note (uncertain):** Whiston prints "the" in brackets, as if he added it; a Greek quotation of the passage has the article. Whether Josephus wrote this sentence at all is a separate, disputed question.

## E4 (josephus; ANF 9, lines 30627-30628)
- **Printed:** "though he did not accept Jesus as Christ"
- **A:** Unchanged: "did not accept Jesus as Christ" (the brief's "though" stands outside the marks)
- **B:** "even though he did not accept that our Jesus was Christ"
- **Verdict:** minor. B restores Origen's "our" (ἡμῶν), which the printed English drops.
- **FINAL:** "did not accept Jesus as Christ" (unchanged; = A). In a narrated brief, "our Jesus" inside the marks would sound like the narrator's own "our". The printed English adds or softens nothing; it leaves out a possessive.
- **Original check:** In file, Origen, Lommatzsch, lines 3731-3732: "τὸν Ἴη- / σοῦν ἡμῶν οὐ καταδεξάμενος εἶναι Χριστὸν". B's reading of ἡμῶν is confirmed.
- **Card note (uncertain):** Origen's Greek says "our Jesus": he speaks as a Christian about a Jewish writer.

## E6 (tacitus; Church-Brodribb 1876, line 68)
- **Printed:** "a class hated for their abominations, called Christians by the populace"
- **A:** "hated for their shameful acts, whom the common people called Christians." (the brief's "group" stands outside the marks)
- **B:** "people hated for their shameful deeds, whom the common people called Christians"
- **Verdict:** same
- **FINAL:** "hated for their shameful acts, whom the common people called Christians." (= A)

## E7 (tacitus; Church-Brodribb 1876, line 68)
- **Printed:** "Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus"
- **A:** "Christus, from whom the name came, suffered the death penalty in the reign of Tiberius, at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus."
- **B:** "Christus, from whom the name came, had been put to death in the reign of Tiberius by the procurator Pontius Pilate."
- **Verdict:** changes meaning. A keeps "one of our procurators"; the Latin says only "by the procurator".
- **FINAL:** "Christus, from whom the name came, was put to death in the reign of Tiberius by the procurator Pontius Pilate." (new: B's sense, in A's tense and shape). "Pilate" is the form the listener knows; "Christus" stays, as Tacitus spells it.
- **Original check:** In file only in Mansi's quotation, Mansi II, lines 41697-41699 (long-s OCR): "Auctor nominis ejus Christus, qui Tiberio imperante per procuratorem Pontium Pilatum supplicio affectus erat". B's claim is verified: "per procuratorem" has no "one of our". B's "imperitante" for the standard text is **NOT IN FILE**. Schanz's quotation skips this sentence, as B says.
- **Card note (uncertain):** Tacitus's Latin, as quoted in a later collection, says Christ was executed "by the procurator Pontius Pilate". The well-known English "one of our procurators" adds words the Latin does not have.

## E8 (suetonius; Rolfe 1914, lines 25907-25909)
- **Printed:** "Since the Jews constantly made disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus, he expelled them from Rome."
- **A:** "The Jews were constantly causing disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus," ... "and he expelled them from Rome."
- **B:** "Because the Jews kept rioting at the prompting of Chrestus, he expelled them from Rome."
- **Verdict:** minor. B keeps Rolfe's causal "because", which the Latin participle does not state; B's "rioting" is stronger than "causing disturbances".
- **FINAL:** "The Jews were constantly causing disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus," ... "and he expelled them from Rome." (= A). The Latin has no "since"; A follows it.
- **Original check:** In file, Rolfe, lines 25843-25844: "Iudaeos impulsore Chresto assidue tumultuantis / Roma expulit." Also Schanz-Hosius lines **24311-24312** (B cites 24313-24314): "Iudaeos impulsore Chresto assidue tumul- / tuantes Roma expulit." Mansi II lines 41693-41694 reads "Chri- / fio", that is "Christo", not "Chresto".
- **Card note (uncertain):** The Latin can mean that Claudius expelled all the Jews, or only those who kept rioting. Whether "Chrestus" means Christ is disputed.

## E9 (suetonius; Rolfe 1914, lines 28523-28525)
- **Printed:** "Punishment was inflicted [on] the Christians, a class of men given to a new and mischievous superstition."
- **A:** "The Christians were punished, a class of people with a new and harmful superstition."
- **B:** "The Christians were punished. They were a group of people given to a new and harmful superstition."
- **Verdict:** minor. "Class" (A) now suggests social class, which genus does not mean; "with" and "given to" both supply the Latin genitive.
- **FINAL:** "The Christians were punished, a group of people given to a new and harmful superstition." (new: A's shape, B's "group" and "given to")
- **Original check:** In file, Rolfe, lines 28458-28460: "afflicti / suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis / novae ac maleficae". Thomson-Forester's English is at lines 11613-11614 ("a sort of people who held a new and impious superstition").

## E10 (pliny-the-younger; NPNF 2.1, lines 19492-19494)
- **Printed:** "to come together on a fixed day before daylight and to sing responsively a song unto Christ as God"
- **A:** "to come together on a fixed day before daylight and to sing a song to Christ as God, answering one another."
- **B:** "to meet on a set day before dawn, and to sing a hymn to Christ as to a god, taking turns among themselves"
- **Verdict:** changes meaning. "As God" puts a Christian confession in a pagan governor's mouth; the Latin "quasi deo" is "as to a god".
- **FINAL:** "to come together on a fixed day before daylight and to sing a song to Christ as to a god, answering one another." (new: A's wording, with the Latin's "as to a god")
- **Original check:** In file only as a later quotation: Spener, Pia Desideria, lines 3700-3702: "stato / die ante lucem convenire: carmenque Christo, quasi Deo, dicere / secum invicem". Spener's capital D is his own printing; Latin of Pliny's day makes no such distinction. Tertullian's paraphrase is in file, Oehler line 11724: "coetus antelucanos ad canendum Christo et deo". B's claim is verified. "Song" stays: carmen does not require "hymn".
- **Card note (uncertain):** Pliny's Latin says the Christians sang to Christ "as to a god". The familiar English "as God" reads more into a pagan's report than his words say.

## E11 (pliny-the-younger; NPNF 2.1, lines 19505-19506)
- **Printed:** "nothing except a superstition depraved and immoderate"
- **A:** "nothing except a depraved and excessive superstition."
- **B:** "nothing but a perverse and excessive superstition"
- **Verdict:** minor. "Depraved" now suggests moral, often sexual, corruption, which Pliny says he did not find; pravus is "crooked, perverse".
- **FINAL:** "nothing except a perverse and excessive superstition." (new: A's shape, B's "perverse")
- **Original check:** In file as later quotations: Spener line 3710, "nihil aliud inveni, quam superstitionem pravam, immodicam"; Disselhoff lines **39685-39686** (B cites 39686-39687): "Nihil / aliud inveni quam superstitionem pravam inmodicam".
- **Card note (uncertain):** Pliny's word means a twisted or wrong-headed belief, not moral corruption. He had just reported finding no crimes.

## E12 (pliny-the-younger; NPNF 2.1, lines 19526-19527)
- **Printed:** "They are not to be searched for; if they are accused and convicted, they are to be punished"
- **A:** Unchanged (the brief lower-cases "They")
- **B:** "They must not be hunted down. If they are reported and proved guilty, they must be punished."
- **Verdict:** minor. B's "hunted down" is stronger than "searched for"; the rule is the same.
- **FINAL:** "they are not to be searched for; if they are accused and convicted, they are to be punished" (unchanged)
- **Original check:** In file, Schanz-Hosius line 24554: "conquirendi non sunt; st deferantur et arguantur, puniendi sunt" (OCR "st" for "si").

## E13 (pliny-the-younger; ANF 3, lines 1585-1586)
- **Printed:** "It forbids them to be sought after as innocent, and it commands them to be punished as guilty."
- **A:** "It forbids them to be sought out, as innocent, and it orders them to be punished, as guilty."
- **B:** "It forbids anyone to hunt them down, as if they were innocent. Yet it orders them punished, as if they were guilty."
- **Verdict:** minor. B's "as if" implies the ruling only pretends they are innocent; Tertullian's "ut innocentes" means "as innocent people", which is his point. B's "Yet" adds a contrast word for "et".
- **FINAL:** "It forbids them to be sought out, as innocent, and it orders them to be punished, as guilty." (= A)
- **Original check:** In file, Tertullian, Oehler lines 11728-11729: "Negat inquirendos ut inno- / centes, et mandat puniendos ut nocentes."

## E14 (ignatius-of-antioch; ANF 1, line 7087)

- **Original check:** Only the word λεοπάρδοις is in file, npnf201 line 19996 (OCR "λεοπ€ρδοις"). McGiffert's English is at 19995-19999: "being bound amidst ten leopards ... that is, a company of soldiers". B's "τάγμα" and full Greek line are **NOT IN FILE**.

## E19 (polycrates-of-ephesus; NPNF 2.1, lines 31324-31325)
- **Printed:** "We observe the exact day; neither adding, nor taking away."
- **A:** "We keep the day without tampering with it, adding nothing and taking nothing away." ("he wrote" stands between the halves)
- **B:** "We keep the day without altering it, neither adding anything nor taking anything away."
- **Verdict:** minor. "Tampering" keeps the Greek word's hint of fraud; "altering" is weaker.
- **FINAL:** "We keep the day without tampering with it, adding nothing and taking nothing away." (= A)
- **Original check:** The first clause is in file, ANF 8 lines 72579-72581: "᾽Αῤῥαδιούργητον ἄγομεν τὴν ἡμέραν". B's claim is verified: the Greek says "untampered", not "exact". The rest of the Greek is not held. Jerome's English is in file at NPNF 2.3 lines 40388-40389 ("inviolably").
- **Card note (uncertain):** Polycrates's Greek says the day is kept "untampered with", a word that hints others have tampered with it. "The exact day" is a translator's softening.

## E20 (justin-martyr; ANF 1, lines 15686-15692)
- **Printed:** "those who lived reasonably are Christians, even though they have been thought atheists"
- **A:** "those who lived with reason are Christians, even if they were thought to be atheists,"
- **B:** "those who lived by the Word are Christians, even though people thought them atheists"
- **Verdict:** changes meaning. The Greek carries both "reason" and "the Word"; each rendering settles one sense.
- **FINAL:** "those who lived with reason are Christians, even if they were thought to be atheists," (= A). "With" keeps the Greek's own preposition. The brief's sentence before names Christ as the Word, so the listener hears the link.
- **Original check:** In file, Rauschen lines 1232-1233: "οἱ μετὰ λόγου βιώσαντες Χριστιανοί εἰσι, κἂν / ἄθεοι ἐνομίσθησαν". The ANF note at lines 15686-15688 gives both senses: "with reason," or "the Word." B's claim that "reasonably" misleads is verified.
- **Card note (uncertain):** Justin's word logos means both "reason" and "the Word", Christ. He plays on both senses, and no single English word holds both.

## E23 (tatian; ANF 2, lines 6972-6974)
- **Printed:** "certain barbaric writings, too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their errors"
- **A:** "some non-Greek writings, too old to be compared with the teachings of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their error."
- **B:** "some foreign writings, older than the teachings of the Greeks and more divine than their errors"
- **Verdict:** changes meaning. A keeps "too ... to be compared", a claim that they cannot be compared; the Greek has plain comparatives.
- **FINAL:** "some non-Greek writings, older than the teachings of the Greeks, and more divine than their error." (new: B's comparatives, A's "non-Greek" and singular "error")
- **Original check:** In file, Tatian, Otto, lines **10388-10390** (both cite 10387-10389): "γρα- / φαῖρ τισιν ἐντυχεῖν βαρβαρικαῖς, πρεσβυτέραις μὲν ὗς πρὸς τὰ / Ἑλλήνων δόγματα, ϑειοτέραις δὲ αἷς πρὸς τὴν ἐκείνων πλάνην". B's claim is verified. πλάνην is singular. "Non-Greek" is kept over "foreign": "foreign" leaves the listener asking "foreign to whom?".
- **Card note (uncertain):** Tatian's Greek calls the writings "older" and "more divine" than Greek teaching. The old English "too old to be compared" makes a stronger claim than he does.

## E24 (marcion-of-pontus; ANF 1, lines 39882-39883)
- **Printed:** "Dost thou know me?"
- **A:** "Do you know me?"
- **B:** "Do you recognize us?"
- **Verdict:** changes meaning. A keeps "me"; every held witness has "us". The verb is "recognize, acknowledge", not simply "know".
- **FINAL:** "Do you recognize us?" (= B)
- **Original check:** Three held witnesses agree on "us". (1) Gebhardt line 1054, the Moscow epilogue of the Martyrdom of Polycarp, which cites Irenaeus: "Ἐπιγίνωσκε ἡμᾶς, Πολύχαρπε". (2) A Latin witness neither renderer found: the letter of Pope Hadrian I, MGH Epistolae 5, line 17230, quoting Irenaeus: "'Cognosce nos'". (3) McGiffert's English of Eusebius IV.14, npnf201 lines 22977-22981: "Knowest thou us?", with a note that most manuscripts read ἐπιγινώσκεις (a question) and three, with Rufinus and the Latin Irenaeus, read ἐπιγίνωσκε (a command). B's "Cognoscis nos?" is **NOT IN FILE**; the held Latin is "Cognosce nos". The question form follows most manuscripts of Eusebius.
- **Card note (uncertain):** Marcion asks Polycarp to recognize "us", that is, to own him as a fellow Christian. Some copies make it a command, "Recognize us."

## E25 (marcion-of-pontus; ANF 1, line 39884)
- **Printed:** "I do know thee, the first-born of Satan."
- **A:** "I do know you, the first-born of Satan."
- **B:** "I do recognize you: the firstborn of Satan."
- **Verdict:** minor. "Recognize" (B) answers the verb of E24; both keep the printed "you".
- **FINAL:** "I recognize, I recognize the first-born of Satan." (new). The held witnesses have no "you": Polycarp doubles the verb and names "the first-born of Satan" as its object. The sentence shape changes from A's for that reason.
- **Original check:** In file, Gebhardt lines 1055-1056: "Ἐπιγινώσχω, ἐπιγινώσχω τὸν τιρωτότοχον τοῦ / σατανᾶ" (OCR). Hadrian I, MGH Epistolae 5, lines 17230-17231: "'Cognosco, / cognosco primitivum Satanae'". McGiffert's English, npnf201 line 22982: "I know the first born of Satan." None has "thee" or "you". The Irenaeus Greek through Eusebius is not held.
- **Card note (uncertain):** Polycarp's reply repeats the verb, "I recognize, I recognize", and does not say "you". He names Marcion only as "the first-born of Satan".

## E30 (clement-of-alexandria; ANF 2, lines 27893-27895)
- **Printed:** "Philosophy, therefore, was a preparation, paving the way for him who is perfected in Christ."

## E38 (eusebius-of-caesarea; NPNF 2.4, lines 19660-19661)
- **Printed:** "One-in-essence"
- **A:** "one in essence"
- **B:** "of one being"
- **Verdict:** minor. "One in essence" can lean toward a single numerical essence; "of the same essence" and "of one being" are both fair. Whether the word meant one single essence or a shared kind was itself argued, and Eusebius's letter reads it narrowly.

The brief's sentence must change one word outside the marks: the Son is "of the same essence" **as** the Father, not "with". Lines 7157-7159 carry Constantine's narrow reading ("not according to the affections of bodies ... nor by division").
- **Card note (uncertain):** Homoousios means "of the same essence". Whether that meant one single being or a shared nature was argued at the time, and Eusebius reports Constantine reading it narrowly.
