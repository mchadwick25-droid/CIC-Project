# First renderings (the writers, Fable): E1 to E39

## E1 (josephus, Q1)
Rendering: unchanged ("the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James")

Notes: Whiston's wording is plain modern English. The brief puts the verbs around it in the present tense; nothing inside the marks is changed.

## E2 (josephus, Q2)
Rendering: "a wise man, if it is right to call him a man"

Notes: "lawful" now means allowed by law, which misleads; McGiffert's English of Eusebius's quotation has "if indeed it be proper to call him a man" (E 9229-9230). "Right" was chosen for that sense.

## E3 (josephus, Q3)
Rendering: "He was the Christ"

Notes: Whiston's square brackets around "the" are dropped. Eusebius's quotation in McGiffert's English reads "He was the Christ." (E 9232).

## E4 (josephus, Q4)
Rendering: unchanged for the words inside the marks ("did not accept Jesus as Christ")

Notes: none.

## E5 (josephus, Q5)
Rendering: unchanged ("was believed to be Christ")

Notes: the whole weight of the sentence rests on this phrase; the second renderer should see the full passage, J3 39724-39733.

## E6 (tacitus, Q1)
Rendering: "hated for their shameful acts, whom the common people called Christians."

Notes: The brief's own word "group" stands outside the marks for "a class". "Abominations" is told as "shameful acts": the printed word now carries a religious sense the chapter does not explain. "Populace" is "the common people". The second renderer has no Latin to check against.

## E7 (tacitus, Q2)
Rendering: "Christus, from whom the name came, suffered the death penalty in the reign of Tiberius, at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus."

Notes: "The extreme penalty" is told as "the death penalty". "Procurators" is kept, since "governors" would say more than the printed word. "Christus" and "Pontius Pilatus" keep the printed forms.

## E8 (suetonius, Q1)
Rendering: "The Jews were constantly causing disturbances at the instigation of Chrestus," ... "and he expelled them from Rome."

Notes: The Latin has a participle, "tumultuantis", with no word for "since"; Rolfe adds the cause, and the rendering joins with "and" instead. The Latin can also be read as "he expelled the Jews who were constantly causing disturbances", a smaller group; the rendering, like both printed versions, takes it of the Jews as a whole. "Impulsore" is "instigator"; "at the instigation of" follows both printed versions. Thomson and Forester print "one Chrestus" (ST 10640); the Latin has no word for "one".

## E9 (suetonius, Q2)
Rendering: "The Christians were punished, a class of people with a new and harmful superstition."

Notes: "Maleficae" is told as "harmful". Rolfe's "mischievous" now sounds light, and Thomson and Forester's "impious" (ST 11613) is a different sense; the Latin word can also carry the sense of sorcery, which neither printed version gives and the rendering does not claim. "Suppliciis" is plural, "punishments"; "were punished" keeps the passive and drops the plural. "Hominum" is "people". "Given to" is Rolfe's addition; the Latin has a bare genitive, told as "with".

## E10 (pliny-the-younger, Q1)
Rendering: "to come together on a fixed day before daylight and to sing a song to Christ as God, answering one another."

Notes: "responsively" becomes "answering one another". "Song" is kept; McGiffert did not write "hymn". The printed sentence goes on ("and to bind themselves with an oath"); the brief's full stop inside the marks closes our sentence, not Pliny's.

## E11 (pliny-the-younger, Q2)
Rendering: "nothing except a depraved and excessive superstition."

Notes: "immoderate" becomes "excessive". "Depraved" is kept as still current.

## E12 (pliny-the-younger, Q3)
Rendering: unchanged (the brief drops the capital on "They" to run it into the sentence)

Notes: the printed sentence continues with the proviso on pardon; the brief's full stop closes our sentence.

## E13 (pliny-the-younger, Q4)
Rendering: "It forbids them to be sought out, as innocent, and it orders them to be punished, as guilty."

Notes: "sought after" now suggests being in demand, so "sought out". "Commands" becomes "orders".

## E14 (ignatius-of-antioch, Q1)
Rendering: "bound to ten leopards, that is, a company of soldiers"

Notes: McGiffert's English of the same line in Eusebius is "bound amidst ten leopards that is, a company of soldiers" (E 19995-20000). "Company" for "band"; "that is" for "I mean".

## E15 (ignatius-of-antioch, Q2)
Rendering: "I am God's wheat, and let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, so that I may be found the pure bread of Christ."

Notes: The longer form (A1 7060) and Irenaeus's quotation (A1 53558-53560, "wheat of Christ ... bread of God") differ; the brief follows the shorter form. Some editions read "I am ground"; the printed English here has "let me be ground".

## E16 (ignatius-of-antioch, Q3)
Rendering: "If our Lord did these things only in appearance, then I too am bound only in appearance." (in the brief the words "he wrote to the church at Smyrna" stand between the two halves; the opening "But" is dropped)

Notes: passive "were done by our Lord" made active; nothing added.

## E17 (ignatius-of-antioch, Q4)
Rendering: "Let no one do anything that concerns the church without the bishop."

Notes: "no one" for "no man" (the Greek is not held to confirm the pronoun; the second renderer should check).

## E18 (hermas, Q1)
Rendering: "For the servants of God there is only one repentance."

Notes: word order only; "only one" for "but one".

## E19 (polycrates-of-ephesus, Q1)
Rendering: "We keep the day without tampering with it, adding nothing and taking nothing away." (in the brief "he wrote" stands between the two halves)

Notes: The Greek adjective means "not tampered with"; "exact" (McGiffert) and "scrupulously ... exact" (ANF: "As for us, then, we scrupulously observe the exact day, neither adding nor taking away", A8 72576-72582) are the translators' glosses, so the rendering follows the Greek. Jerome's Latin, in English: "We therefore celebrate the day according to usage, inviolably, neither adding anything to nor taking anything from it" (J 40388-40389). The second half is rendered from the printed English alone.

## E20 (justin-martyr, Q1)
Rendering: "those who lived with reason are Christians, even if they were thought to be atheists,"

Notes: μετὰ λόγου is "with reason" and also "with the Word"; the ANF note gives both. "With reason" keeps the plain sense and the sentence before it in the brief carries "the Word". "Reasonably" in modern English suggests "fairly well", so it was not kept.

## E21 (justin-martyr, Q2)
Rendering: "So then, you are a Christian?"

Notes: the Greek has no negative; οὐκοῦν draws a conclusion ("so then"). The second renderer should check whether λοιπόν wants its own word ("So then, after all, ...").

## E22 (justin-martyr, Q3)
Rendering: unchanged

Notes: none.

## E23 (tatian, Q1)
Rendering: "some non-Greek writings, too old to be compared with the teachings of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their error."

Notes: βαρβαρικαῖς means "of non-Greek peoples"; "barbaric" now means savage, so it misleads. δόγματα is "teachings" (the printed "opinions" is also fair). πλάνην is singular, "error". The Greek comparatives say "older, set against ..., more divine, set against ..."; "too old / too divine to be compared" follows the printed English, and the second renderer should weigh "older than ... and more divine than ...".

## E24 (marcion-of-pontus, Q1)
Rendering: "Do you know me?"

Notes: none.

## E25 (marcion-of-pontus, Q2)
Rendering: "I do know you, the first-born of Satan."

Notes: "first-born" kept; it is the point of the reply.

## E26 (marcion-of-pontus, Q3)
Rendering: unchanged

Notes: the printed sentence runs "teaching his disciples to believe in ..."; the brief's "he taught his followers" stands outside the marks.

## E27 (marcion-of-pontus, Q4)
Rendering: unchanged

Notes: the printed clause is "has caused many of every nation to speak blasphemies"; "won" in the brief is ours and stands outside the marks.

## E28 (valentinus, Q1)
Rendering: unchanged

Notes: "sanctified" kept as plain modern English; "made holy" is the alternative if the second renderer prefers it. "The only good Father" could also be read "the Father, who alone is good"; the printed order is kept.

## E29 (clement-of-alexandria, Q1)
Rendering: "When I had tracked him down, hidden in Egypt, I found rest."

Notes: The rendering covers only the span from "having tracked him out" to "rest"; the opening clause and the parenthesis are told in the lead-in ("the last and best of them"). "Hidden" for "concealed": the printed English does not say whether the teacher hid himself or was merely obscure; "hidden" keeps both open. "Tracked down" for "tracked out" (the hunting sense, as McGiffert's "hunted him out").

## E30 (clement-of-alexandria, Q2)
Rendering: "Philosophy, then, was a preparation," he wrote, "paving the way for the one who is made perfect in Christ."

Notes: "made perfect" for "perfected", which today suggests polish; the passive is kept. "The one who" for "him who". The brief breaks the sentence with "he wrote" after "preparation".

## E31 (dionysius-of-alexandria, Q1)
Rendering: "It would have been right to suffer anything at all rather than cut the Church of God apart."

Notes: ἔδει is past ("it was right", "one ought to have"), which McGiffert's "it were better" softens into a comparison; the rendering follows the Greek ("It would have been right"). πᾶν ὁτιοῦν is "anything whatever" ("anything at all"), not "everything". διακόψαι is "to cut through, cut apart"; "cut ... apart" keeps it, where "divide" is weaker. γάρ ("For") is dropped because the quotation opens the sentence in the brief; a second renderer should weigh that.

## E32 (hippolytus-of-rome, Q1)
Rendering: unchanged

Notes: The printed clause is already plain modern English. The full sentence is "But when I say another, I do not mean that there are two Gods, but that it is only as light of light, or as water from a fountain, or as a ray from the sun." The brief quotes the middle clause only and tells "another beside" without marks.

## E33 (novatian, Q1)
Rendering: unchanged

Notes: The printed sentence opens "Thus indeed"; the brief starts the quotation after those words. "As it were" is kept as plain modern English. The printed text goes on "even as He was formerly placed; and thus from either side He receives the sacrilegious reproaches of such heretics as these", which the brief does not quote.

## E34 (pontius-the-deacon, Q1)
Rendering: "I rejoice greatly, very greatly, at his glory, yet I grieve more that I remained behind."

Notes: "nimis multum" is an intensifier here ("exceedingly much"), so "very greatly"; "excessively" in modern English suggests a fault. "exulto" as "rejoice". "remansi" kept as "remained behind".

## E35 (anatolius-of-alexandria-and-laodicea, Q1)
Rendering: "no small or ordinary mistake."

Notes: the printed verb is plural ("those who ... commit"); the brief turns the subject to "anyone who ... makes" outside the marks. "Common" is rendered "ordinary", its sense here; "blunder" as "mistake".

## E36 (peter-of-alexandria, Q1)
Rendering: "did not come to this by their own choice, but were betrayed by the weakness of the flesh."

Notes: "choice" follows προαίρεσιν, where the printed English has "will". "Frailty" to "weakness" (ἀσθενείας). The printed "for" (γὰρ) is left outside the marks. The canon says the same of the flesh twice (AN 26611-26612 and 26614-26615); the marks follow the second.

## E37 (eusebius-of-caesarea, Q1)
Rendering: "Since I am the first to take up this subject," he wrote, "I am trying to travel what is, so to speak, a lonely and untrodden road."

Notes: "enter upon" to "take up"; "traverse" to "travel"; "as it were" to "so to speak"; "path" to "road". "Untrodden" kept.

## E38 (eusebius-of-caesarea, Q2)
Rendering: "one in essence"

Notes: unchanged but for capitals and hyphens. The bar's Constantine brief has "of the same essence" for the same word; the coordinator may want one form in both.

## E39 (councils-of-rome-and-carthage-251, Q1)
Rendering: "You should have suffered anything at all sooner than split the Church of God."

Notes: ἔδει is impersonal, "one ought to have"; the letter is addressed to Novatian in the second person in the sentences on either side, so the rendering says "You should have". A second renderer may prefer "It would have been right to suffer anything at all". "γὰρ" ("for") is left out, as the sentence stands alone in the brief. "διακόψαι" is "cut apart"; "split" is the plain word. The printed "everything" follows πᾶν; ὁτιοῦν gives "anything at all".
