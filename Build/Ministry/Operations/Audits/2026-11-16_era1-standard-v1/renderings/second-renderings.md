# Second, independent renderings (E1 to E39)

Line references are to files in `cic/texts/`. I did not see the first renderings, `spans.md`, the drafts or the briefs.

## E1
"the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, a man named James"

(a) Scan damage: "Je- / sus" is read as "Jesus". "Who was called Christ" keeps the Greek participle λεγομένου ("called"), which can be neutral or slightly distancing. McGiffert renders it "the so-called Christ". I kept the neutral "called", as the printed English does, because "so-called" sounds sneering to a modern listener and the Greek does not force that. "Whose name was James" becomes "a man named James" to keep one clause flowing. The Greek has "James was his name to him", so nothing is added.
(b) Greek held in McGiffert's note, `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` 13595-13602: τὸν ἀδελφὸν Ἰησοῦ τοῦ χριστοῦ λεγομένου, Ἰάκωβος ὄνομα αὐτῷ. A second witness in Origen, `origen_commentarii-in-evangelium-matthaei-tomus3-grc-lat_lommatzsch1834.txt` 3729-3730: τὸν ἀδελφὸν Ἰησοῦ τοῦ λεγομένου Χριστοῦ. The printed English follows the Greek.

## E2
"a wise man, if indeed one should call him a man"

(a) Scan damage: "hima man" is read as "him a man". "If it be lawful" misleads today: "lawful" now means "legal". The Greek χρή means "it is right" or "one ought". So "if indeed one should call him a man."
(b) Greek held, though only as a later quotation of Josephus inside a Greek martyr-act (Acts of St Donatus and companions, Macarius speaking to Diocletian): `acta-sanctorum_maii-tomus-5-lat_palme1866.txt` 80255-80256, Ἰησοῦς σοφὸς ἀνήρ, εἴγε ἄνδρα αὐτὸν λέγειν χρή (OCR: "εἴγε ἄνδρα αὐτὸν λέγειν Xp"). Josephus's own Antiquities in Greek is not held. Whiston follows this Greek; only "lawful" is dated.

## E3
"This man was the Christ."

(a) Whiston brackets "[the]" as if supplied. The held Greek has the article (ὁ Χριστός), so "the" belongs to the text and the brackets should go. The Greek also has οὗτος, "this man", which Whiston turns into "He". "This man" follows the Greek and is still plain.
(b) Greek held in the same quotation: `acta-sanctorum_maii-tomus-5-lat_palme1866.txt` 80259, Ὁ Χριστὸς οὗτος ἦν. The printed English underplays the article (brackets it) and softens οὗτος to "He".

## E4
"even though he did not accept that our Jesus was Christ"

(a) "Accept" is right for καταδεξάμενος ("receive, admit"). "Christ" stays without an article, as in the Greek.
(b) Greek held: `origen_commentarii-in-evangelium-matthaei-tomus3-grc-lat_lommatzsch1834.txt` 3731-3732, τὸν Ἰησοῦν ἡμῶν οὐ καταδεξάμενος εἶναι Χριστόν. The printed English drops ἡμῶν ("our"). The omission is small, but it hides Origen's own voice, so I restored it.

## E5
Unchanged: "and was believed to be Christ"

(a) Plain modern English. "Christ" without an article matches the printed line.
(b) Original not found in a held file. (From memory, not in file: Jerome's Latin is "et credebatur esse Christus".)

## E6
"people hated for their shameful deeds, whom the common people called Christians"

(a) "A class" is not in the Latin, which has only "whom" (quos). "Abominations" now sounds biblical or exaggerated. Flagitia means "shameful acts" or "disgraceful crimes". "By the populace": vulgus means "the common people" or "the crowd". I used "common people" both times only because the Latin says "hated" and "the crowd called". If the repeat of "people" jars, "whom the crowd called Christians" is equally faithful.
(b) Latin held twice, both as quotations: `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-2-305-346-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt` 41696-41697 (OCR long-s: "quos per fla~ / gitia invifos vulgus Chrifizanos appellabat") and `schanz-hosius-krueger_geschichte-der-roemischen-litteratur-teil3-deu_3e-aufl1922.txt` 24316-24317, "quos per flagitia invisos vulgus Christianos appellabat". The printed English adds "a class" and heightens "flagitia".

## E7
"Christus, from whom the name came, had been put to death in the reign of Tiberius by the procurator Pontius Pilate."

(a) "Suffered the extreme penalty" is a stiff rendering of supplicio affectus erat, "was executed" or "was put to death". "Procurator" is Tacitus's own title for Pilate and is kept. I used the English "Pilate" for "Pilatus", since the listener knows the name that way. "Christus" stays, since Tacitus spells it so.
(b) Latin held only in Mansi's quotation: `mansi_sacrorum-conciliorum-collectio-tomus-2-305-346-lat_welter-facsimile1901.txt` 41697-41699, read through the long-s OCR as "Auctor nominis ejus Christus, qui Tiberio imperante per procuratorem Pontium Pilatum supplicio affectus erat" (Mansi prints "imperante"; the standard text reads "imperitante", from memory, not in file). Schanz's quotation (24317) skips this sentence. The printed English adds "one of our": the Latin says only "by the procurator Pontius Pilate". That addition misrepresents the original.

## E8
"Because the Jews kept rioting at the prompting of Chrestus, he expelled them from Rome."

(a) "Made disturbances" is weaker than tumultuantis, "rioting". "Instigation" is fine but formal; impulsore Chresto means "with Chrestus as the one pushing them". "Chrestus" stays as spelled. Whether it means Christ is contested, and the rendering should not decide it.
(b) Latin held: `suetonius_lives-of-the-caesars-loeb_rolfe1914.txt` 25843-25844, "Iudaeos impulsore Chresto assidue tumultuantis Roma expulit." Also quoted in Schanz 24313-24314 and Mansi 41693-41694. The printed English follows the Latin.

## E9
"The Christians were punished. They were a group of people given to a new and harmful superstition."

(a) Scan damage: "inflicted a the Christians" is read as "inflicted on the Christians". "Mischievous" now means "playfully naughty", which badly misleads. Malefica means "evil-doing, harmful", with a hint of sorcery. "Harmful" is the plain choice; "wicked" would also serve. "Superstition" is kept as Suetonius's term (superstitio, an unsanctioned foreign cult). "A class of men" becomes "a group of people", since genus hominum is not about class or about men only.
(b) Latin held: `suetonius_lives-of-the-caesars-loeb_rolfe1914.txt` 28458-28460, "afflicti suppliciis Christiani, genus hominum superstitionis novae ac maleficae". The printed English follows it, apart from the misleading "mischievous".

## E10
"to meet on a set day before dawn, and to sing a hymn to Christ as to a god, taking turns among themselves"

(a) "Fixed day" and "set day" are equal; stato die means a regular, appointed day. "Responsively" is kept in sense as "taking turns among themselves" (secum invicem). "As God" with a capital reads a Christian creed into a pagan governor's words. Quasi deo means "as to a god" or "as if to a god". This matters for what Pliny reports.
(b) Pliny's Latin held only as quotations in later works: `spener_pia-desideria-deu_koehler1841.txt` 3700-3702, "quod essent soliti stato die ante lucem convenire: carmenque Christo, quasi Deo, dicere secum invicem". Tertullian's paraphrase, `tertullian_quae-supersunt-t1-apologetica-lat_oehler1853.txt` 11724, "coetus antelucanos ad canendum Christo et deo". The printed "as God" leans further than the Latin allows.

## E11
"nothing but a perverse and excessive superstition"

(a) "Depraved" now suggests sexual or moral degeneracy. Pravus means "crooked, perverse, wrong-headed". "Immoderate" is correct but stiff; immodicus means "beyond bounds, excessive". The Latin has no "and" between the adjectives; English needs one or a comma ("a perverse, excessive superstition"), and either is faithful.
(b) Latin held as quotations: `disselhoff_jubilate-denkschrift-kaiserswerth-deu_1886.txt` 39686-39687, "Nihil aliud inveni quam superstitionem pravam inmodicam"; `spener_pia-desideria-deu_koehler1841.txt` 3710, "nihil aliud inveni, quam superstitionem pravam, immodicam". The printed English follows it.

## E12
"They must not be hunted down. If they are reported and proved guilty, they must be punished."

(a) "Searched for" is weaker than conquirendi, "sought out, hunted down". "Accused" stands for deferantur, "reported, brought before you". "Convicted" stands for arguantur, "proved guilty". Two sentences keep one thought each.
(b) Latin held as a quotation in `schanz-hosius-krueger_geschichte-der-roemischen-litteratur-teil3-deu_3e-aufl1922.txt` 24554, "conquirendi non sunt; si deferantur et arguantur, puniendi sunt" (OCR: "st deferantur"). The printed English follows it.

## E13
"It forbids anyone to hunt them down, as if they were innocent. Yet it orders them punished, as if they were guilty."

(a) The subject is Trajan's ruling (sententia). "Sought after" now sounds like "in demand", so "hunt them down". Ut innocentes means "as innocent people": the ruling treats them as innocent in one clause and guilty in the next. "Yet" carries the Latin's contrast, which "and" flattens; if "yet" is felt to add, "and" is the literal word.
(b) Latin held: `tertullian_quae-supersunt-t1-apologetica-lat_oehler1853.txt` 11728-11729, "Negat inquirendos ut innocentes, et mandat puniendos ut nocentes." The printed English follows it.

## E14
"chained to ten leopards, I mean a unit of soldiers"

(a) "A band of soldiers" can now suggest a music band. The Greek word is, from memory, τάγμα, "a military unit". "Bound" becomes "chained", since a prisoner's bonds are meant. McGiffert's English witness (E 19995-19998) reads "a company of soldiers".
(b) Only the word λεοπάρδοις is held, in McGiffert's note: `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` 19996 (OCR "λεοπ€ρδοις"). The rest is not in a held file. (From memory, not in file: δεδεμένος δέκα λεοπάρδοις, ὅ ἐστιν στρατιωτικὸν τάγμα.)

## E15
"I am God's wheat. Let me be ground by the teeth of wild beasts, so that I may be found to be Christ's pure bread."

(a) "Wheat" and "bread" stay: the image is the whole point. "Be found" keeps the sense of being shown true at the end.
(b) Original not found in a held file. (From memory, not in file: σῖτός εἰμι θεοῦ καὶ δι᾽ ὀδόντων θηρίων ἀλήθομαι. If that memory is right, the Greek says "I am being ground", not "let me be ground". I cannot confirm it here, so I followed the printed English.)

## E16
"But if our Lord did these things only in appearance, then I too am in chains only in appearance."

(a) "In appearance" is the key phrase: it answers those who said Christ only seemed to suffer. It is kept twice, as in the original. "Bound" becomes "in chains".
(b) Original not found in a held file. (From memory, not in file: τὸ δοκεῖν, "in seeming", appears twice.)

## E17
"No one should do anything that concerns the church without the bishop."

(a) "Let no man" is generic and covers everyone, so "No one". "Church" is lower-case here because it means the local congregation's life; the printed capital is a later convention.
(b) Original not found in a held file.

## E18
"For the servants of God have only one repentance."

(a) "Repentance" keeps Hermas's term. It means one chance of forgiveness after baptism, but adding "chance" would add a word, so the sense belongs in a note. "Servants" is kept; the Greek word may mean "slaves" (from memory, δοῦλοι), but "servants of God" is the established phrase.
(b) Original not found in a held file.

## E19
"We keep the day without altering it, neither adding anything nor taking anything away."

(a) "Observe" becomes "keep", the ordinary word for keeping a festival. The held Greek ἀρραδιούργητον means "untampered, not falsified". The printed "exact" softens a word that accuses others of tampering. I followed the Greek.
(b) Only the first clause is held, in the ANF note: `anf08_twelve-patriarchs-clementina-apocrypha-edessa-syriac.xml` 72579-72581, Ἀῤῥαδιούργητον ἄγομεν τὴν ἡμέραν. The rest is not in a held file. The printed "exact day" does not quite follow the Greek.

## E20
"those who lived by the Word are Christians, even though people thought them atheists"

(a) "Reasonably" now means "fairly" or "moderately", which badly misleads. The Greek μετὰ λόγου means "with reason" and "with the Word" at once, and the sentence before it names Christ as the Word. "By the Word" keeps Justin's link. A note could add that λόγος also means "reason". "Atheists" is kept as the ancient charge (ἄθεοι, "rejecters of the gods"); a listener may need that explained nearby.
(b) Greek held: `justin-martyr_first-apology-grc_rauschen1911.txt` 1232-1233, οἱ μετὰ λόγου βιώσαντες Χριστιανοί εἰσι, κἂν ἄθεοι ἐνομίσθησαν. The printed "reasonably" misrepresents the Greek by losing the Word.

## E21
"So then, you are a Christian?"

(a) Οὐκοῦν λοιπόν is a leading question, "so, in the end, you are...?". "Are you not, then" is the old way of rendering it; the modern form keeps the sense with no negative.
(b) Greek held: `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt` 1353-1354, Οὐκοῦν λοιπὸν Χριστιανὸς εἶ; (OCR "Aowrov ... «Χριστιανὸς"). The printed English follows it.

## E22
Unchanged: "Yes, I am a Christian."

(a) Plain modern English.
(b) Greek held: `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt` 1354, Ναί, Χριστιανός εἰμι. The printed English follows it.

## E23
"some foreign writings, older than the teachings of the Greeks and more divine than their errors"

(a) "Barbaric" now means "savage". The Greek βαρβαρικαῖς means "non-Greek", so "foreign". "Opinions" stands for δόγματα, "teachings". The Greek has simple comparatives, "older" and "more divine". The printed "too old to be compared ... too divine to be compared" adds "to be compared" twice and changes the claim.
(b) Greek held: `tatian_oratio-ad-graecos-grc-lat_otto1851.txt` 10387-10389, γραφαῖς τισιν ἐντυχεῖν βαρβαρικαῖς, πρεσβυτέραις μὲν ὡς πρὸς τὰ Ἑλλήνων δόγματα, θειοτέραις δὲ ὡς πρὸς τὴν ἐκείνων πλάνην (OCR "γραφαῖρ ... ὗς πρὸς ... αἷς πρὸς"). The printed English misrepresents the comparatives.

## E24
"Do you recognize us?"

(a) The word is ἐπιγινώσκω, "recognize, acknowledge": Marcion asks to be acknowledged as a fellow Christian. "Know" loses that. The held Greek has "us" (ἡμᾶς), not "me".
(b) The Irenaeus Greek (via Eusebius IV.14) is not held. A related Greek witness is held: the Moscow epilogue of the Martyrdom of Polycarp, which reports this exchange from Irenaeus, `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt` 1054, Ἐπιγίνωσκε ἡμᾶς, Πολύκαρπε ("Recognize us, Polycarp", an imperative). (From memory, not in file: Irenaeus's Latin reads "Cognoscis nos?") The printed "me" does not follow either form.

## E25
"I do recognize you: the firstborn of Satan."

(a) "Recognize" answers E24's word and keeps Polycarp's bitter play on it. "Thee" becomes "you". "I do" carries the Greek's doubled verb.
(b) Related Greek witness held: `acta-martyrum-selecta_gebhardt1902.txt` 1055-1056, Ἐπιγινώσκω, ἐπιγινώσκω τὸν πρωτότοκον τοῦ σατανᾶ (OCR "τιρωτότοχον"). The printed English follows its sense. The Irenaeus Greek itself is not held.

## E26
Unchanged: "to believe in some other god greater than the Creator"

(a) "Believe in" fairly renders νομίζειν, "to hold, acknowledge". "Creator" stands for δημιουργός, "maker".
(b) Greek held: `justin-martyr_first-apology-grc_rauschen1911.txt` 731, ἄλλον τινὰ νομίζειν μείζονα τοῦ δημιουργοῦ θεόν. The printed English follows it.

## E27
"many people of every nation"

(a) The Greek says "among every race of humans", κατὰ πᾶν γένος ἀνθρώπων. "Nation" here means a people, not a modern state; "people" is added only because "many of every nation" leaves the noun hanging when heard. If that counts as adding, the printed form is acceptable as it stands.
(b) Greek held: `justin-martyr_first-apology-grc_rauschen1911.txt` 732-733, κατὰ πᾶν γένος ἀνθρώπων ... πολλούς. The printed English follows it.

## E28
"But when the only good Father visits it, it is made holy and shines with light."

(a) "Sanctified" becomes "made holy". "Gleams" becomes "shines", the plainer verb. "The only good Father" is kept exactly; it is Valentinus's own phrase for God.
(b) Original not found in a held file.

## E29
"Then I met the last of them, who was the first in ability. I tracked him down where he lay hidden in Egypt, and I found rest."

(a) "Came upon" becomes "met". "First in power" is likely δυνάμει, "in ability" or "in power"; McGiffert's English witness (E 28754-28756) reads "in ability truly he was first", and "ability" suits a teacher better for a modern listener. "Concealed in Egypt" means he lived out of public view. The printed sentence is one long chain; I split it, keeping every clause.
(b) Original not found in a held file.

## E30
"So philosophy was a preparation. It cleared the way for the one who is being brought to completion in Christ."

(a) "Perfected" now suggests "made flawless". The Greek verb (from memory, τελειόω) means "brought to maturity or completion". "Paving the way" becomes "cleared the way", a plainer verb with the same sense.
(b) Original not found in a held file. (From memory, not in file: the Greek may read ὑπὸ Χριστοῦ, "by Christ", rather than "in Christ". I followed the printed "in".)

## E31
"For we ought to suffer anything at all, rather than split the church of God."

(a) "It were better" is a dated subjunctive. The Greek ἔδει means "one ought" or "it was right", which is stronger than "better". Πᾶν ὁτιοῦν is "anything whatever". Διακόψαι means "to cut apart, split". "Divide" is fine; "split" is a little more concrete.
(b) Greek held: `eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-rufinus-x-xi-grc-lat_schwartz-mommsen1908.txt` 7363-7364, ἔδει μὲν γὰρ καὶ πᾶν ὁτιοῦν παθεῖν ὑπὲρ τοῦ μὴ διακόψαι τὴν ἐκκλησίαν τοῦ θεοῦ (OCR "πᾶν ὅτι οὖν παϑεῖν οὐπὲρ ... διαχόψαι"). Rufinus's Latin, same file, 7434-7435, "oportuerat quidem etiam pati omnia, ne scinderetur ecclesia dei". The printed "better" softens "ought".

## E32
Unchanged: "I do not mean that there are two Gods"

(a) Plain modern English. "Gods" keeps the capital, since Hippolytus is denying two divine persons are two Gods.
(b) Original not found in a held file.

## E33
"the Lord is, in a way, crucified between two thieves"

(a) "As it were" becomes "in a way". "Thieves" is the traditional English of the Gospel scene, so it is kept even though the Latin word (from memory, latrones) means "bandits".
(b) Original not found in a held file. The note in the same ANF file quotes Castalio's later Latin, "inter latrones Christus" (63460), which is not Novatian's text.

## E34
"I rejoice greatly, and more than greatly, at his glory. But I grieve still more that I stayed behind."

(a) "Excessively" now sounds like a fault. The Latin "multum ac nimis multum" means "much, and very much indeed". "More than greatly" keeps the doubling. "Remained behind" means he lived on when Cyprian died; "stayed behind" says the same plainly.
(b) Latin held: `cyprian_opera-spuria-vita-pontius-lat_hartel-csel3-pars3.txt` 41409-41410, "multum ac nimis multum de gloria eius exulto, plus tamen doleo quod remansi." The printed English follows it.

## E35
"make no small or ordinary mistake"

(a) "Blunder" now sounds clumsy or comic. The Greek ἁμαρτάνειν is "to err, make a mistake". "Common" stands for ὡς ἔτυχεν, "casual, everyday". "Ordinary" carries that.
(b) Greek held, contrary to the writer's note: the words are not lost in the page break. `eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-rufinus-x-xi-grc-lat_schwartz-mommsen1908.txt` 12608-12616 runs on after the gap to line 12616: κατ᾽ αὐτὴν λαμβάνοντας οὐ μικρῶς οὐδ᾽ ὡς ἔτυχεν ἁμαρτάνειν φαμέν (OCR "aurnv ... ἕτυχεν ἀμαρτανειν"). The printed English follows it.

## E36
"they did not come to this by their own choice, but were betrayed by the weakness of the flesh"

(a) "Of their own will" stands for κατὰ προαίρεσιν, "by deliberate choice". "Frailty" becomes "weakness" (ἀσθένεια). "The flesh" is kept as the Christian term for human weakness. Note: the span's words sit on ANF lines 26616-26617; lines 26613-26614 carry an earlier "betrayed by the frailty of the flesh".
(b) Greek held: `nikodemos-hagiorite_pedalion-canons-grc_1886.txt` 49720-49721, οὐ γὰρ κατὰ προαίρεσιν ἐν τούτῳ ἐληλύθασιν, ἀλλὰ καταπροδοθέντες ὑπὸ τῆς ἀσθενείας τῆς σαρκός. The printed English follows it.

## E37
"Because I am the first to take up this subject, I am trying to walk a lonely path, as it were, one no one has walked before."

(a) "Enter upon" becomes "take up". "Traverse" becomes "walk". "Untrodden" becomes "one no one has walked before". "As it were" is kept, since Eusebius marks the image himself.
(b) Original not found in a held file (the held Greek is Books VI to X only).

## E38
"of one being" (the word homoousios)

(a) "Essence" now suggests a perfume or "the gist". The Greek ὁμοούσιος means "of the same being or substance". "Of one being" is the modern liturgical form. Where the brief uses the word as a name, it reads best as: the single word "homoousios", "of one being".
(b) Greek held, contrary to the writer's note: Eusebius's letter is printed in Socrates, `socrates_historia-ecclesiastica-tomus1-grc-lat_hussey1853.txt` 7154-7155, ἑνὸς μόνου προσεγγραφέντος ῥήματος τοῦ ὁμοουσίου, and the word again at 7158, τὸ ὁμοούσιον. The printed English follows it.

## E39
"For we ought to suffer anything at all, rather than split the church of God."

(a) The same span as E31, rendered the same way, so the two briefs agree. "Ought" follows ἔδει, which the printed "better" softens.
(b) Greek held: `eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-rufinus-x-xi-grc-lat_schwartz-mommsen1908.txt` 7363-7364 (as E31); Rufinus's Latin 7434-7435, "oportuerat quidem etiam pati omnia, ne scinderetur ecclesia dei".
