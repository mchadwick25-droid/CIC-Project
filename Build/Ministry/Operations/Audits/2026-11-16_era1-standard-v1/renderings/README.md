# The rendering check for era 1's quotations

Every quotation in the 20 Who briefs and the council brief comes from English printed before 1931, or from Greek or Latin, so each is told in our modern English (Mark, 2026-11-16: "Yes, every quote before 1931 (Recommended)").

| File | Holds |
|---|---|
| `spans.md` | The 39 quoted spans, E1 to E39, with file and lines. |
| `printed.md` | Each span as printed, with the lines around it. This is all the second renderer saw. |
| `first-renderings.md` | The writers' renderings (Fable), from each brief's references record. |
| `second-renderings.md` | The independent second renderings (Opus), made without the first. |
| `comparison.md` | The comparison (Opus) against the printed lines and the held originals: a verdict and a final reading for each. |

Result: 19 same, 15 minor, 5 that change meaning (E7, E10, E20, E23, E24). The briefs carry the comparison's final readings, with two exceptions.

**E15 (Ignatius) and E34 (Pontius).** The comparison judged both renderings the same in meaning and then split each into two sentences. The split took both briefs to grade 7.9, below the floor of 8. The briefs keep the writers' joined forms, "and let me be ground" and "yet I grieve more", which the comparison passed as "same" and which follow the printed text's own joining ("and let me be ground", "but still more do I grieve"). No word differs otherwise.

E31 and E39 are one sentence of Dionysius of Alexandria, used in his own brief and in the council's, with one final reading.
