1. "To much he said and he rights": cut every attribution tag ("he said", "he writes", "X says", "Y tells") that carries no trust. A witness is named only where trust turns on it: the person's own words, a partisan or hostile witness, a point resting on one source, a wonder or legend, a contested point. A witness who carries several sentences is named once. Tags are removed, never replaced with synonyms ("reports", "notes", "relates", "according to").

Keep a quotation only where the person's own words carry something a retelling would lose: their stance in their own voice, a line that defines them. That is usually one, at most two, per brief. For an outside witness (Tacitus, Suetonius, Pliny, Josephus) the one or two lines that are the heart of what he says. Tell everything else plainly in the narrator's voice.
