## (e) Significance and stances

- He wrote the first history of the church, from documents. Consequence shown: Socrates and Theodoret each began where he stopped (S 1860-1869; TH 5219-5223), and Socrates used him as an authority (S 2866-2870) while doubting his Life of Constantine (S 1862-1866).
- At Nicaea he signed "one in essence" after hesitating and explained himself in a letter. Consequence shown: the charge of favoring Arius (S 6914-6920), Athanasius's use of the letter against his successors (AT 26780-26795), and Socrates's use of it as evidence for the council. The held sources do not settle how far he accepted the word; the brief does not judge it.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a scholar of Caesarea who came through the persecution wrote the church's first history, hesitated at Nicaea, and was both leaned on and doubted by the writers who followed.

1. Good storytelling. Weakest: "He became bishop of Caesarea and wrote the story of the church from the apostles to his own day." Bar: "So he wrote ten books of History himself, though his speech, he admits, was rude." (Gregory of Tours).
2. Flow. Weakest: "Later historians built on it." Bar: "That stand cost him his city again and again." (Athanasius).
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "Arius was the test of Eusebius himself." Bar: "His chief quarrel with Origen was over the body." (Methodius).
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: "That letter followed him." Bar: "The Rule is Benedict's own words." (Benedict).
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "He built the book from older writers and copied their own words into his pages." Bar: "He set the Hebrew text beside the Greek translations in the Hexapla and marked where the old Greek fell short." (Origen).

The old brief used the phrase the same way. If the coordinator wants the phrase tied only to its own passage, the close can read "Socrates built his account of Nicaea on Eusebius's words and treated them as an authority."
- "His own creed": the letter calls it "the formula of faith presented by ourselves" and says he received it from the bishops before him (AT 19565-19568 and following). The old brief had "his own statement of faith".
- "Each began a church history where his ended" is exact for Theodoret. Socrates says he begins with what Eusebius left out and writes "since his time to our own day", and he reviews Constantine's conversion first.
- The old brief's opening (the Thebaid, "the murderous sword was blunted") is sound but was cut for the line; I found no claim in the old brief that the held files contradict. Its statement that McGiffert "gives this reason for the name" moves to the card.
- Whether a pre-Constantinian tradition suits a brief whose stances fall in 325 and after: the opening places him in that church in 303, which the sources carry.
