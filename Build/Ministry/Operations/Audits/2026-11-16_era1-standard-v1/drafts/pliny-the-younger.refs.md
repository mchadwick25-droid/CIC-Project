## (a) Moved to the card

- McGiffert's description of Pliny, "a man of great literary attainments and an intimate friend of the Emperor Trajan", and the uncle Pliny the Elder: E 19450-19454. Out of the brief.
- McGiffert's dating (governor in 110 or 111; the letter "probably within a year"): E 19457-19459. Harnack's 111 to 113 (card as it stands). The brief says "around the year 112", the census `activeYear`. No ancient held source gives a year.
- Bithynia: the held letter does not name the province, and Tertullian says only "a province" (T 1567). That the letter "was written from Bithynia" is McGiffert's statement (E 19457). The brief tells it plainly as a documented fact; the card should say that in the Library it rests on McGiffert's note.
- McGiffert's judgment that Tertullian and Eusebius misread Trajan's reply as favorable, and that it made Christianity an unlawful religion: E 19561-19579. Out of the brief.
- From the letter, cut on the sentence test and kept for the card: the Roman citizens sent to Rome (E 19476-19478); the anonymous list of names (E 19480-19481); incense and wine to the emperor's image (E 19483-19485); "one even twenty years before" (E 19489-19490); the harmless common meal, given up after his edict against associations (E 19498-19502); the spread through cities, villages and countryside (E 19510-19512); the temples filling again (E 19513-19517); Trajan's bar on anonymous accusations (E 19529-19531); pardon for those who deny and pray to the gods (E 19527-19529).

## (e) Significance and stances

An outside witness, so no stance of his own. What he reports: how he tried Christians, and what former Christians told him of their worship and oath. What came of it: Trajan's ruling (E 19522-19531); Tertullian's use of the report as proof that inquiry found no crime, and his attack on the ruling (T 1565-1586); Eusebius's statement, from Tertullian, that the persecution was checked to a degree (E 19541-19552). The record stops there in the held files: no other ancient writer held in the Library was found using the letter, and Eusebius knew it only through Tertullian.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a governor who did not know how to try Christians tried them, reported what he learned of their worship to Trajan, and got a ruling that Christian writers then argued over.

1. Good storytelling. Weakest: "They also swore an oath to keep from theft, robbery and adultery." A report line. Bar: Benedict, "His monks promise 'stability, a changed way of life, and obedience'". Mine is flatter but short.
2. Flow. Weakest link: "...yet his own inquiry turned up no crime." to "Trajan answered that Christians ...". The reply follows the letter by order only. Bar: Gregory of Tours, "Gregory refused. Only the Son was made man ... he answered". A reviewer may want "Trajan answered" tied back to the request for advice.
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "Pliny writes as the judge who had already sent Christians to punishment, and he calls their faith insanity, yet his own inquiry turned up no crime." It is the trust sentence the outside-witness form asks for. Bar: Methodius, "Both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness."
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: "He found 'nothing except a depraved and excessive superstition.'" It ends a paragraph on a quotation, but it is the result of the torture in the sentence before. Close: Eusebius's judgment with its limit, which is where the held record ends.
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "Those who denied it he released, once they had prayed to the gods and cursed Christ." Kept because it shows what the test was. Bar: Constantine, "Maxentius was driven into the Tiber at the broken bridge."

Sentences cut on the sentence test: 8 (McGiffert's description and the uncle, the dating sentence, the image with incense and wine, "one even twenty years before", the harmless meal, the spread to villages and countryside, the pardon clause of Trajan's reply, Tertullian's argument as a separate sentence).

## Notes for the coordinator

- Place and year have no ancient held source (see (a)). They are told plainly because the opening rule needs them; this is the one place the brief rests on McGiffert without saying so. If that is not acceptable, the opening can read "Early in the second century, when Trajan was emperor, Pliny the Younger governed a Roman province", which Tertullian carries.
- "His own inquiry turned up no crime" is my reading of "I found nothing except a superstition" beside the oath "not with a view to the commission of some crime". Pliny does not say "I found no crime" in so many words. Tertullian reads him the same way (T 1575-1581).
- "Later Tertullian": no held line dates the Apology, so the brief gives no interval.
- Tertullian's summary differs from the letter: he says Pliny "condemned some Christians to death" and that the pledge forbade "murder", which the held letter does not say. The brief tells Tertullian's argument in his own terms ("a pledge against crime") and does not harmonize them. Card, What is uncertain.
- All four renderings rest on English only; no Latin is held for the second renderer to check against.
- Old brief: nothing found false. Its "pertinacious and inflexible obstinacy" and "was to a certain degree checked" quotations are verbatim in the file but were in the 1890 wording.
