## (b) What the brief draws on

- Early in the second century (the brief no longer names Trajan): Eusebius places Ignatius ("At that time", III.36.1-2) in his account of Trajan's reign, III.32-36 (E 19270, 19440-19443, 19679-19742); III.22 "At this time Ignatius was known as the second bishop of Antioch" follows III.21 on Trajan's accession (E 16977-16982, 17006-17008). "Early in the second century" is the plain reading of Trajan's reign (98-117, McGiffert's note E 14295).
- Bishop of Antioch in Syria: Eusebius III.36.2, E 19740-19742; Ignatius, Romans 2 and 5 ("From Syria even unto Rome"), A1 7083.
- Tradition: census `post-apostolic-house-church`, "Post-Apostolic Household-Church Christianity".
- Condemned for his faith; taken to Rome for the beasts; under guard through Asia: Romans 4 "I am but a condemned man" (A1 7048-7049); Eusebius III.36.3-4, "sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts on account of his testimony to Christ ... through Asia under the strictest military surveillance" (E 19935-19936, 19952-19953).
- From Smyrna a letter ahead to Rome, begging them not to save him: Eusebius III.36.5-6 (E 19961-19962, 19988-19990); Romans 4 (A1 7036-7040).
- Q1: Romans 5, A1 7083-7087. Q2: Romans 4, A1 7040-7042.
- Six more letters: Ephesus, Magnesia, Tralles (from Smyrna), Philadelphia, Smyrna, Polycarp (from Troas): Eusebius III.36.5, 10 (E 19961-19986, 20024-20032).
- Some said Christ only seemed to suffer: Smyrnaeans 2 (A1 8005-8009); Trallians 10 (A1 6671-6673).
- Q3: Smyrnaeans 4, A1 8102-8103.
- Theodoret calls on the letter to the Smyrnaeans: Dialogue 1, "Testimony of Saint Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, and martyr. From the letter to the Smyrnaeans ... really in the time of Pontius Pilate and of Herod the tetrarch crucified for our sake in the flesh" (T 20249-20290). He quotes chapter 1 of the letter; Q3 is from chapter 4 of the same letter. Theodoret quotes Smyrnaeans again at T 23246-23255 and 27122-27131. "Three centuries later": Theodoret's census dates 393 to about 458.
- Q4 and "so that, he said, whatever the bishop approved would be secure and valid": Smyrnaeans 8, A1 8277-8278 and 8286-8289 ("whatsoever he shall approve of, that is also pleasing to God, so that everything that is done may be secure and valid").
- No held witness for what came of the rule on bishops: a search of the ANF and NPNF files for the saying found only Coxe's note (anf05 41376) and the Apostolic Constitutions on deacons (anf07 13982-13984), which does not name him. Eusebius (E 19994-20065), Irenaeus (A1 53556-53560), Theodoret (above), Athanasius (On the Councils 47, npnf204 59204-59229) and John Chrysostom's sermon on him (npnf109 10329) quote him on his death or on Christ.
- Polycarp sent Philippi the letters: Polycarp, Philippians 13, A1 3613-3618 ("we have sent to you, as you requested"); in Eusebius III.36.14-15, E 20090-20101. "Soon": Polycarp still asks for news of Ignatius and those with him, A1 3619-3624. Polycarp bishop of Smyrna: Eusebius III.36.1, E 19679-19684.
- Irenaeus quotes the wheat saying as "a certain man of ours ... condemned to the wild beasts": Against Heresies V.28.4, A1 53556-53560; in Eusebius III.36.12, "As one of our people said", E 20067-20071. Told without marks.
- Eusebius: "Report says" he died at Rome (E 19935-19936); names the seven letters (E 19961-19986, 19988-19990, 20024-20032); "whose fame is still celebrated by a great many" (E 19741-19742).

## (e) Significance and stances

- Against those who said Christ only seemed to suffer: his own Smyrnaeans 2-4; taken up by Theodoret of Cyrrhus (T 20268-20290) as testimony to Christ's real flesh.
- Nothing in the church without the bishop: his own Smyrnaeans 8. The record stops there: no held ancient witness shows what the rule did in other churches.
- The letters as a body: Polycarp collected and sent them (A1 3613-3618), Irenaeus quoted him (A1 53556-53560), Eusebius listed all seven (E 19961-20032).

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a condemned bishop on the road to Rome writes letters defending Christ's real suffering and the bishop's place, and the letters outlive him in other men's hands.

1. Storytelling. Weakest: "On the road he wrote six more letters." Bar: Gregory of Nazianzus, "In Constantinople he preached the Trinity."
2. Flow. Weakest link: "Ignatius also stood for the bishop." after the Theodoret sentence. Bar: Gregory of Tours, "He held the same ground against the Arians of Spain."
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "The sources we have do not show what came of this rule in other churches." Bar: Gregory of Nyssa, "The sources we have do not show what came of this teaching in his own day."
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: the close, "Eusebius of Caesarea, two centuries later, gives his death at Rome only as a report, but he names all seven letters and says that many still celebrated his fame." Bar: Origen's close on the council list.
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "Polycarp, the bishop of Smyrna, sent the church at Philippi every letter of Ignatius he had." Bar: Methodius, "Decades later, Epiphanius of Salamis wrote against Origen and copied Methodius word for word."
