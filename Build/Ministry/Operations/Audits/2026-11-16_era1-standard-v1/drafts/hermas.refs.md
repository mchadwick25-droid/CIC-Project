## (e) Significance and stances

- One repentance after baptism, against teachers who allowed none: his own Commandment 4. Tertullian's On Modesty shows the book cited on the side of those who forgave adulterers and attacked for it.
- The book's standing: Scripture to Irenaeus; to be read but not in church, by the Muratorian list; rejected yet read in churches for beginners, in Eusebius.
- The record stops at the book: nothing outside it tells his life or his end, and no held witness says the churches adopted his rule of one repentance as a rule.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a Roman Christian accused in a vision of his own sin writes a book allowing one repentance after baptism, and the churches argue for two centuries over whether to read it.

1. Storytelling. Weakest: "Everything else about him comes from inside his own visions." Bar: Benedict, "Nearly all we know of his life comes from Gregory's Dialogues".
2. Flow. Weakest link: from the Rhoda vision to "Hermas had heard teachers say that the only repentance is the one made in baptism." Bar: Methodius, "His chief quarrel with Origen was over the body."
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "What became of Hermas himself, no source says." Bar: Gregory of Tours, "He does not say his stand cost him anything."
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: "Irenaeus of Lyon quoted the Shepherd as Scripture." Bar: Ephrem, "Jerome wrote that some churches read his writings aloud after the Scriptures."
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "The man who raised him, he says, sold him to a woman named Rhoda." Bar: Origen, "His mother hid his clothes to keep him home".

Sentences cut on the sentence test: 7 (the old woman who is the Church, two; McGiffert and Zahn; the "So begins the Shepherd" sentence; the river Tiber detail; Jerome and the Latins; a thesis line on sin after baptism).

## Notes for the coordinator

- "Muratorian Canon" is the document's usual name but it comes from its 1740 editor, Muratori. If that counts as naming a modern scholar, the brief can say "an early Roman list of the church's books" and drop the name in both places.
- "Around the year 150" is my reckoning from Eusebius's years for Hyginus and Pius; McGiffert's note cites Lipsius for 139 to 154 (E 22329). The Muratorian date itself is disputed (Zahn), so the opening says "by the one early report we have".
- The Rhoda scene is told as his own account, which the brief says no other source confirms. It is a vision book; the scan marks the life as Inferential-Thin.
- The brief leaves out that he saw her bathing in the Tiber and helped her out (A2 654-655); "one day he thought" covers it without the detail.
- Tertullian is a hostile witness and wrote On Modesty as a Montanist; the brief marks him by what he was attacking. His claim about councils is his alone.
- I did not find a held ancient witness saying the church took up "one repentance" as its rule; the scan's "shaped the Roman teaching on one repentance" is not told.
