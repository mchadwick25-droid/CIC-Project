## (e) Significance and stances

He held that the church could not restore those who sacrificed in persecution, and founded churches on that rule; he also defended Christ as God the Son against those who made him the Father or a mere man. Consequences: the Roman synod of sixty cut him off (E 38272-38294); the provinces divided (S 14091-14107); Nicaea legislated for his clergy in 325 (C14 3768-3791); his churches stood in Socrates's day (S 14059-14061, 19289-19294); his Trinity book was read under Cyprian's name (J3 40895-40897). The record stops at his own defense of the split: no letter of his on it is held, only Socrates's report of one.
