## (e) Significance and stances

- He set the terms on which those who fell in the Great Persecution came back. Consequence shown: the council in Trullo (692) made his canons church law (C14 26731-26744). The held sources show nothing of how the canons were received in his own lifetime.
- He stood against Meletius's ordinations in his church. Consequence shown: a lasting split, ruled on at Nicaea (S 3204-3211) and still alive in Athanasius's day, when Meletians brought charges against him (AT 24782-24806, 24838-24844). The cause of the break is disputed between Athanasius and Epiphanius, and the brief says so.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a bishop who took the fallen back on mild terms, lost part of his church to a stricter rival, and left both a split and a body of law behind him.

1. Good storytelling. Weakest: "That Easter Peter set the terms." Bar: "His great work was on Scripture." (Origen).
2. Flow. Weakest: "Peter's rules lasted too." Bar: "He held the same ground against the Arians of Spain." (Gregory of Tours).
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "Why the two broke is disputed." Bar: "Either way, the council deposed him." (Athanasius).
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: "He also forbade anyone to accuse those who had fled to save their lives." It carries a second canon and has no consequence of its own. Bar: "At Antioch John had trained under Diodore, who kept to the literal sense of Scripture." (John Chrysostom).
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "An old account kept with their letter says he then came to Alexandria and replaced priests Peter had appointed." Bar: "Sozomen adds a rumor that Athanasius had hidden the man himself." (Athanasius).

Sentences cut on the sentence test: 9 (see the side facts in (a), with the Bell, Hefele and McGiffert sentences of the old text).

## Notes for the coordinator

- The old brief said "Peter was in hiding." The account says the presbyters were "then in hiding" (AN 16836-16838), and that Meletius did not go "to the blessed Peter" (AN 16821-16822). It does not say Peter was in hiding. Removed.
- The old brief gave "from the times of the blessed Peter, Bishop and Martyr" to Athanasius. It is the Egyptian council's letter (see (d)). The brief now uses Athanasius's own words in Apology 59-60.
- "While he made room for the fallen": the held sources do not date the split against the canons of 306; the modern dates differ (see (a)). The sentence claims only that the two belong to the same years of persecution. If that is too much, it can read "In those years another Egyptian bishop...".
- "Most of the clergy there" stands for Epiphanius's "the greater number of the bishops, monks, presbyters and other orders".
- Bell and Hefele were not reopened; nothing in the brief rests on them.
- "Then be received" rests on the two Byzantine commentators' reading of Canon I; the canon itself says the forty days are "sufficient".
