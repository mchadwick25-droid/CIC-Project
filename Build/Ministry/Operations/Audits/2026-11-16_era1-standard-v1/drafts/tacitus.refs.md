## (b) What the brief draws on

- A great fire; people believed Nero had ordered it: Annals 15.44, TA 68 ("the sinister belief that the conflagration was the result of an order"); the emperor named as the one blamed, Sulpicius Severus, Sacred History II.29, SS 10654-10659. Year 64: see (a).
- Cornelius Tacitus, senator and historian, about fifty years later: name, T3 2570 and 10081; role and date, TA header 5, 8-9 (see (a)).
- Not a Christian; his chapter on the Christians treats them as criminals (the brief does not say it is his only one; see (a)): TA 68 ("a class hated for their abominations", "a most mischievous superstition", "criminals who deserved extreme and exemplary punishment").
- To end the rumor Nero put the blame on the Christians (Q1): TA 68 ("Consequently, to get rid of the report, Nero fastened the guilt ...").
- Christus, Tiberius, Pontius Pilatus (Q2): TA 68.
- A harmful superstition, checked for a moment, broke out again in Judaea where it began and in Rome: TA 68 ("a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out not only in Judaea, the first source of the evil, but even in Rome"). Told without marks.
- Those who confessed, he says, arrested first; on their word a vast number convicted, less for the fire than for hatred of the human race: TA 68 ("an arrest was first made of all who pleaded guilty; then, upon their information, an immense multitude was convicted, not so much of the crime of firing the city, as of hatred against mankind"). Told without marks. "He says" stands on the first sentence so that the unexplained "confessed" is marked as Tacitus's own; the chapter does not say what they pleaded guilty to, and the brief does not settle it.
- Skins and dogs, crosses, burned as lamps after dark: TA 68 ("Covered with the skins of beasts, they were torn by dogs and perished, or were nailed to crosses, or were doomed to the flames and burnt, to serve as a nightly illumination, when daylight had expired").
- He thought they deserved the harshest punishment; people pitied them; one man's cruelty, not the public good: TA 68 ("even for criminals who deserved extreme and exemplary punishment, there arose a feeling of compassion; for it was not, as it seemed, for the public good, but to glut one man's cruelty, that they were being destroyed").
- He does not say where he learned of Christus, and names none of the shameful acts: TA 68, read whole (the chapter gives no source and no particular of the "abominations").
- Tertullian, defending the Christians, on Tacitus: Apology 16, T3 2567-2588. "Cornelius Tacitus first put this notion into people's minds", the fifth book of the Histories, the wild asses and the consecrated head (2570-2580); "from this, I suppose, it was taken for granted that we too are devoted to the worship of the same image" (2580-2582); "the very opposite of tacit in telling lies" (2583-2584). Latin "sane ille mendaciorum loquacissimus", TL 17915-17916 (Apology) and 33311-33312 (To the Nations). Told without marks: "a great talker of lies" is a loose telling of "mendaciorum loquacissimus". "Around 200": the Apology speaks of Severus as the ruling emperor, T3 1781 ("Has not Severus ... but yesterday repealed"), with the ANF note at 1782-1785.
- Sulpicius Severus told of the fire with the same details in the same order; does not name Tacitus; calls the victims innocent: Sacred History II.29, SS 10654-10666 (English), SL 4783-4795 (Latin: "uertit inuidiam in Christianos, actaeque in innoxios crudelissimae quaestiones ... ferarum tergis contecti laniatu canum interirent, multi crucibus affixi aut flamma usti, plerique in id reseruati, ut cum defecisset dies, in usum nocturni luminis urerentur"; the file's scan is rough and I have corrected obvious misreadings here). "Of Aquitania": SS 349-351 and 471. "Around 400": the NPNF introduction says the Sacred History runs down to the year 400, SS 441-443, and puts his life at about 363 to 420, SS 349-352 (modern; see notes).
- "This was how the cruelty against Christians began": SS 10665-10666 ("In this way, cruelty first began to be manifested against the Christians"); Latin SL 4794-4795, "hoc initio in Christianos saeuiri coeptum". Told without marks, named as Sulpicius's.

## (e) Significance and stances

Tacitus is the outside report that Nero punished the Christians of Rome after the fire of 64, and that Christus was executed under Pontius Pilatus in the reign of Tiberius (TA 68). What came of it in the held sources: Tertullian knew and attacked Tacitus, though for the Histories and not for this chapter (T3 2567-2588), and Sulpicius Severus told the persecution with Tacitus's details in his order, without naming him, and made it the beginning of the persecutions (SS 10654-10666). The record stops there: no held ancient writer cites Annals 15.44 by name.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a hostile Roman historian tells how Nero blamed and killed the Christians for the fire and who Christus was, gives no source, and later Christian writers both attacked him and repeated his account.

- Good storytelling. Weakest: "He does not say where he learned of Christus, and he names none of the shameful acts." Bar: Methodius, "Both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness." Mine weighs the witness in one sentence and moves on, as the bar does, but it sits at a paragraph end and is plainer than the bar's.
- Flow. Weakest link: from "...Pontius Pilatus." to "He calls the faith a harmful superstition...". Bar: Gregory of Tours, "Gregory refused. Only the Son was made man and suffered, he answered...". The link is carried only by "He".
- No AI tells. Weakest: "This, Sulpicius wrote, was how the cruelty against Christians began." Bar: Ephrem, "From that time on, Sozomen says, the Syrians sang the songs of Ephrem." It is Sulpicius's own closing statement of what the event began, so it finishes the story; a reviewer may still hear it as a chosen last line.
- No hanging sentences. Weakest: "Tacitus thought they deserved the harshest punishment." Bar: Athanasius, "Either way, the council deposed him." It is short, but the next sentence turns on it ("Yet he records ...").
- Everything relevant. Weakest: "Around 400, Sulpicius Severus of Aquitania in Gaul told of the fire with the same details in the same order: the animal skins and the dogs, the crosses, the bodies burned for light when the day ended." Bar: Methodius, "Decades later, Epiphanius of Salamis wrote against Origen and copied Methodius word for word instead of arguing it himself." The list repeats details already told; it is kept because the match is the evidence that Sulpicius follows Tacitus.
- Sentences cut on the sentence test: 9 (the prayers and offerings to the gods; "Tacitus stops to explain the name"; Nero's gardens and the circus; "He writes as an enemy throughout"; "Christian writers read him"; Tertullian "never mentions the chapter on Nero"; "the rumor, the blame" in the list; and the old brief's Gibbon, Schiller and McGiffert sentences, which moved to the card).
