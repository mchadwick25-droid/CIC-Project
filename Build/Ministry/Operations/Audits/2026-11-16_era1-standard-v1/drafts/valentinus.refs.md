## (b) What the brief draws on

- "Around 140 ... came to Rome"; arrived under Hyginus, flourished under Pius, remained until Anicetus: Irenaeus III.4.3, A1 39968-39970. Hyginus became bishop in the first year of Antoninus Pius: Eusebius, Church History IV.10, E1 22120-22126, with Irenaeus quoted at IV.11, E1 22154.
- Followers called Valentinians: Tertullian, Against the Valentinians 4, A3 43104-43106, 43124-43125. Gnostic: Irenaeus I.11.1, "adapted the principles of the heresy called 'Gnostic' to the peculiar character of his own school", A1 31520-31521. Tradition: census `valentinian-and-other-gnostic-christianities`, "Valentinian & other Gnostic Christianities".
- A knowledge higher than faith: Clement, Stromata II.3, A2 32106-32111; Irenaeus I.6.2, "a mere faith, while they have not perfect knowledge", A1 30754-30757.
- The bishopric story, its reason ("an able man both in genius and eloquence"), the confessor chosen, the break; ambition and revenge: Tertullian, Against the Valentinians 4, A3 43109-43117. Told without marks. Tertullian alone: not in Irenaeus I.11 or III.4.3, nor in the Clement passages read.
- His own words only in passages Clement quotes, each followed by Clement's answer: A2 32722-32738; 34289-34314; 39090-39100; 46642-46652.
- The heart like an inn: Clement, Stromata II.20, A2 34296-34301. "Roadside inn" is our plain word for "caravanserai"; "fouled by careless guests" tells "has holes and ruts made in it, and is often filled with dung; men living filthily in it, and taking no care for the place as belonging to others". Told without marks.
- The Maker below a higher, unseen God. Irenaeus I.11.1: the Demiurge brought forth by the mother shut out of the Pleroma, A1 31535-31543; IV preface 3, "the Maker and Framer ... produced from a defect", A1 44340-44342. Clement IV.13: the world as far below "the living Aeon" as an image is below the living face; the Creator, "called God and Father", is the "Painter", an image "to the glory of the invisible One"; death "the work of the Creator of the world", A2 39097-39110.
- Members saved by nature, ordinary believers given faith. Clement: "he also ... supposes a class saved by nature" (IV.13, A2 39095-39096); "the followers of Valentinus assign faith to us, the simple, but ... knowledge springs up in their own selves (who are saved by nature)" (II.3, A2 32106-32109); "a saved nature, as he would have it" (II.20, A2 34308-34309). Irenaeus I.6.2: "We of the Church, they say, are these persons ... they are spiritual by nature", A1 30754-30763.
- Irenaeus alone on the fuller system: pairs, A1 31522-31527; one power "falling from its original condition, produced the rest of the universe", A1 31529-31531.
- The school carried on; Ptolemy and Heracleon: Irenaeus I preface 2, "the disciples of Ptolemaeus, whose school may be described as a bud from that of Valentinus", A1 29872-29873; II.4.1, "Ptolemy himself, and Heracleon", A1 34634; Clement IV.9, "Heracleon, the most distinguished of the school of Valentinians", A2 38778-38780; Tertullian, Against the Valentinians 4, A3 43117-43122 (the translation prints "Heraclean").
- Irenaeus read their books and met some of them; feared believers would be carried off, because their language resembles the church's while their meaning differs; so he wrote: I preface 2, A1 29844-29854, 29863-29867. Five books: Against Heresies I to V, A1 29777, 34224, 39621, 44299, 50523.
- Earlier writers failed for want of knowing the system; to overthrow the Valentinians is to overthrow every heresy: Irenaeus IV preface 2, A1 44326-44338.

## (e) Significance and stances

- Stance 1, a higher God above the world's Maker, with a heaven of paired powers (the first on Irenaeus and Clement, the second on Irenaeus alone). Stance 2, a people saved by nature, set above ordinary believers (Irenaeus and Clement).
- Consequence: a school that carried on under Ptolemy and Heracleon (Irenaeus, Clement, Tertullian), and the church's reply; Irenaeus's own prefaces say he wrote because of the Valentinians and held that refuting them refuted every heresy.
- The record stops at the replies: the sources read here do not tell what became of Valentinus after Rome, and the brief makes no claim about it. (Epiphanius, Panarion 31, in the held Greek of `epiphanius_ancoratus-panarion-haer-1-33-gcs-bd1-grc_holl1915.txt`, was not read for this brief.)

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a gifted teacher broke with the church at Rome, taught a higher God and a people saved by nature, and left a school large enough that Irenaeus wrote his great work to answer it.

- Good storytelling. Weakest: "Irenaeus and Clement wrote independently, and on his teaching they agree on two points." Beside Methodius, "Both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness." Mine is source talk, but the hostile-witness rule requires it.
- Flow. Weakest link: from "driven by ambition and revenge." to "Valentinus's own words survive only in a few passages". Beside Benedict, "The Rule is Benedict's own words."
- No AI tells. Weakest: "tells how the break came". Beside John Chrysostom's opening paragraph.
- No hanging sentences. The close: "Earlier writers had failed to refute the Valentinians, he says, because they did not know their system, and whoever overthrew them overthrew every heresy." Beside Gregory of Tours's close, "his is the only account we have." It ends on the consequence, in the witness's own claim.
- Everything relevant. Weakest: "says he arrived when Hyginus was bishop and stayed through the time of two more bishops". It is the whole life outline and is marked as Irenaeus's. Beside Methodius, "Jerome says he was bishop of Olympus and later of Tyre".
- Sentences cut on the sentence test: 7 (Hippolytus as second witness, the Eleutherus date, "the disciple of Platonism", the names of the pairs, "the simple" quotation, Tertullian's "very large body", Tertullian's list of earlier writers).

## Notes for the coordinator

- Old brief, weak support: it rested the teaching on Irenaeus and Hippolytus. Hippolytus copies Irenaeus, so that was one witness. The rewrite rests it on Irenaeus and Clement and narrows the agreed teaching to what both carry.
- Old brief: "Irenaeus and Clement of Alexandria both report that his followers divided people by nature" is supported, and Clement also says it of Valentinus himself (IV.13, A2 39095-39096).
- "Wrote independently" is our judgment: Clement quotes Valentinus's own letters and homily, which Irenaeus does not, and neither cites the other in the passages read. No ancient line states it.
- Clement's "things which proceed from a pair are complements" (A2 39110-39112) touches the pairs, but it is Clement's gloss and thin; the brief leaves the pairs to Irenaeus alone.
- "Around 140": Eusebius's placing of Hyginus depends on his own bishop lists; the year is approximate and should be said so on the card.
- "A man who had suffered for the faith" tells the translation's "a claim which confessorship had given him" (A3 43111-43112).
- Polycarp at Rome under Anicetus turning many back from Valentinus and Marcion (Irenaeus III.3.4, A1 39870-39875) is a held consequence left out for length.
- The brief's opening does not name a region for Rome; "Rome" was judged enough.
