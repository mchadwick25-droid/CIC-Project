## (e) Significance and stances

The two councils settled that Christians who fell in persecution could come back after penance, case by case, and that those who refused to receive them stood outside the church. What came of it: Rome agreed with Carthage (Cyprian, Antonianus 6; Eusebius VI.43); Novatian's churches kept their own rule into the fifth century (Socrates IV.28); the eastern churches, first divided, rejected Novatian (Eusebius VII.4-5); Carthage itself shortened the penance when persecution threatened again (the bishops' letter to Cornelius); and Nicaea made communion with the lapsed a condition for receiving Novatian's clergy (canon 8). The record stops at the acts: none are held for either meeting of 251, and no account from Novatian's side.
