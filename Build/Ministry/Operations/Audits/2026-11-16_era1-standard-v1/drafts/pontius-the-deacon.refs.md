## (e) Significance and stances

Pontius is a recorder. What he defended is that a bishop's life, and not only a martyr's death, should be written down (Life 1). What he recorded: Cyprian's conversion and giving (Life 2), the plague and the help given to all (Life 9-10), the exile and the dream (Life 11-13), and the death (Life 15-19). What came of the record, as far as the held sources show: Jerome names him and calls the book notable (Lives 68), and Bede repeats Jerome. There the record stops; no held ancient writer quotes the Life.

## Notes for the coordinator

- The Life says its writer was one of Cyprian's household and went into exile with him. It does not call him a deacon. The brief therefore calls him "one of his household" and "the writer" until Jerome is named.
- Old brief, "sold whole estates": this follows Wallis's English. Hartel's Latin text reads "distractis rebus suis ... tota prope pretia dispensans", he sold his goods and gave out nearly the whole price. I followed the Latin.
- Old brief, "People climbed into the trees to see": the Life says people who favored him climbed the trees (AN 28257-28259); correct, now cut.
- Old brief, "Wilhelm Hartel's Latin edition calls the Life commonly attributed to Pontius": correct, moved to the card.
- At Life 2 Hartel's Latin has "si quibus eius interfui, si qua de antiquioribus comperi" (V 40363-40364): what he was present at, and what he learned of earlier things or from older people. Wallis prints "if I have discerned any of his more illustrious labours" (AN 27711-27712). I left the sentence out; if it is wanted, it needs a rendering from the Latin.
- "One of his household ... a man of the Latin church of North Africa": the Life shows him among the Carthage clergy's circle ("we, his associates and friends", AN 28183-28184) but gives no birthplace.
- Bede is early medieval, not ancient. He adds nothing to Jerome; the sentence shows only that the attribution was passed on.
- I found no held ancient witness who uses the Life's content by name. Jerome's own chapter on Cyprian (Lives 67, J3 40818-40822) gives the conversion by the presbyter Caecilius and the gift of his goods to the poor, which match Life 4 and 2, but Jerome does not say where he learned them, so the brief does not claim it.
