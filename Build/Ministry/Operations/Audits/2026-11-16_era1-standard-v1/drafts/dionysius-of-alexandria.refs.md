## (e) Significance and stances

- Against Novatian he held that nothing justified splitting the church and that the fallen could be forgiven (Eusebius VI.44-45). What came of it, in his own report: the churches of the East were at peace (VII.4-5). No held source gives a reply from Novatian.
- Against Sabellius he stressed that the Son is other than the Father, was accused of making him a creature, and defended himself (Athanasius, On the Opinion 4-6, 13). A century later the Arians claimed him, Athanasius defended him, and Basil judged he had gone too far (Letter 9). The ancient judges disagree, and the brief says so.
- Against Nepos he rejected an earthly millennium and won over its leader at Arsinoe (Eusebius VII.24); from style he judged that the apostle did not write the Revelation (VII.25). Eusebius kept the argument and left the question open (III.39); the record does not say the one caused the other.
- On rebaptism the record stops before any outcome (see (a)).

## (f) Craft self-check

The line of the story: a pupil of Origen who became bishop of Alexandria in a persecution spent his years writing to hold the church together, against Novatian, against Sabellius and against Nepos, and each letter left a mark that later writers either leaned on or disputed.

1. Good storytelling. Weakest: "An Egyptian bishop, Nepos, found in the Revelation of John a thousand years of bodily pleasure on earth." It opens the third stance with a new name and no link to the last. Bar: Gregory of Nazianzus, "Apollinarius held that Christ had no human mind. Against him Gregory wrote...". The bar does the same thing, so I think it stands, but the brief is three cases in a row and a listener may hear a list.
2. Flow. Weakest link: from the wedding rescue to "Then Novatian set himself up as a rival bishop at Rome...". "Then" carries only the order of events. Bar: Athanasius, "That stand cost him his city again and again." The bar's link is causal; mine is not, because no source gives one.
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "Later he could tell the bishop of Rome that the churches of the East, once divided over Novatian, were all at peace." Bar: Gregory of Nazianzus, "In his farewell he said the city's other churches were full again". Same kind of report from the person's own letter.
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: the close, "Eusebius of Caesarea copied the argument out and himself left open which John saw the Revelation." Bar: Gregory of Nyssa's close, "In 692 the council in Trullo set its seal on his canons". Mine ends on the last consequence the sources show; it does not sum up the life, and a reviewer may find it abrupt.
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "He had been a pupil of Origen and had led the Christian school of Alexandria before he became bishop." Bar: John Chrysostom, "At Antioch John had trained under Diodore, who kept to the literal sense of Scripture." It is the thread of what he inherited and names the tradition, so it stays.

Sentences cut on the sentence test: 12. From the old brief's matter: the rescue told in six sentences is now two; the plague (three sentences); "Eusebius calls him the great bishop"; the synod on Paul of Samosata; the death notice; Robertson. From my own first draft: the letter to Antioch; Dionysius of Rome's letter; Germanus's name; Athanasius's "one letter is not the whole man".
