## (e) Significance and stances

- One stance the sources carry: Easter reckoned by a nineteen-year cycle begun at the spring equinox, in his own words as Eusebius quotes them. Consequence shown: at Whitby in 664 both parties cited him and Wilfrid called his cycle the whole church's (Bede III.25). The record stops there: no held ancient source shows who took up his reckoning in his own day, and the ruling at Whitby did not rest on him. ## Notes for the coordinator

- The brief runs 293 words. The assignment asked for a short brief; the Whitby paragraph is the only consequence and takes about 110 of them. If shorter is wanted, the siege paragraph is the one to cut.
- "Starts each cycle with the new moon at the spring equinox" compresses VII.32.14-15: the new moon falls on a day when the sun is already four days into the segment "they are accustomed to call" the equinox. Check that the compression is acceptable.
- "The old and the children": Eusebius has "old women and children and old men".
- "At Whitby in England": the name Whitby is the 1903 editor's gloss on Bede's Streaneshalch. Bede's "Scots" are left out; Colman is named only as bishop (B 7964, 8240-8244).
- "Among the bishops it called": Eusebius says the synod "called him to Antioch" after his ordination at Caesarea; he does not list others in that sentence.
- The old brief's quotations from Bede ("a holy man, and much commended in church history"; "a most holy, learned, and commendable man") were in the 1840 English. They are now told without marks.
