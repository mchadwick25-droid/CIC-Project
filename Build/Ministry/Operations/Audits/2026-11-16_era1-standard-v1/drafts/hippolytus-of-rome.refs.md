## (e) Significance and stances

He defended the distinction of the Son from the Father against a bishop of Rome, and a strict discipline for grave sins. Consequences the held sources show: by his own telling Callistus cut off Sabellius (A5 13816-13822) and yet kept the crowd (A5 13915-13919, 13957-13960); Jerome ties Eusebius's Easter cycle to his (J3 40692-40697); Theodoret cites him as a witness to the faith (J3 20255-20262). The record stops at how the quarrel ended: the exile and the burial are recorded (CM), their link to the quarrel is not.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a Roman presbyter fought his bishop over the Father and the Son and over forgiving grave sins, lost the crowd by his own account, was exiled beside a later bishop, and was kept by later teachers as a witness.

- Good storytelling. Weakest: "A calendar of martyrs in the same book records their burials at Rome in one entry." Bar: Athanasius, "In 366 he came back to his church for good, and died in 373." Mine is a record note where the bar has an event; the source gives no more.
- Flow. Weakest link: from "...out of fear of him." to "The second quarrel was over grave sins." Bar: Gregory of Tours, "He held the same ground against the Arians of Spain." Mine announces the second stance flatly.
- No AI tells. Weakest: "His books outlived the quarrel." Bar: Origen, "His great work was on Scripture." It is a short thesis line; it carries the turn to the close and the two sentences after it give the fact.
- No hanging sentences. Weakest: "The sources we have do not show how the quarrel ended." Bar: Gregory of Nyssa, "The sources we have do not show what came of this teaching in his own day." It is tied to the exile that follows.
- Sentence test. Weakest: "Jerome says his table for the date of Easter moved Eusebius of Caesarea to draw up one of his own." It is a second line of impact outside the quarrel; kept as the one sourced consequence of his books.

## Notes for the coordinator

- "a book now read as his". I weighed three wordings. "Come down under his name" is not true of this book: by MacMahon's account it was printed under Origen's name, so that wording would mislead. A bare "his Refutation" would tell a modern attribution flat. "Now read as his" says honestly that the attribution is today's, names no scholar, and leaves the detail to the card. It does rest on modern judgment; if Mark wants no such clause, the alternative is "a book against the heresies whose writer does not name himself", but the held file's own text does not show that either (only the editors do), so I did not use it.
- The two-gods charge and the opposition are told in the Refutation in the plural "we"; the brief says "him and his side".
- Against Noetus is taken as his on the anf05 title; Theodoret and Jerome list his works but do not name this one. The opening line of the brief's quote sentence ("In his own book against that teaching") depends on that.
- "Hippolytus claims he did it out of fear of him" renders "He acted thus from apprehension of me" (A5 13819). Told without marks.
- The calendar's date column is broken in the OCR, so I say only "in one entry" and do not give the day.
- CM is OCR Latin with many misread letters ("presbitcr", "Caliisti"); I read through them and quote none of it.
- Theodoret's passages are on the incarnation, so the close says "the church's faith in Christ", the same wording the bar uses for Methodius from the same list.
