## (e) Significance and stances

An outside witness, so no stance of his own. What he reports: James, "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ", stoned under Ananus (Antiquities XX.9.1), and the longer passage on Jesus (XVIII.3.3). What came of it: Origen argued from the James report against Celsus (OC 37932-37942); Eusebius argued from the longer passage against forged acts (E 9242-9246); Jerome gave him a chapter among the church's writers (J3 39708-39733). The record stops at how the longer passage came to read as it does; no held ancient source says.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a Jewish priest who was no Christian wrote two reports of Jesus at Rome; one was used early and holds, the other does not fit what Origen says of him, and the church's writers used both.

1. Good storytelling. Weakest: "There he wrote the Antiquities of the Jews, and twice in it he speaks of Jesus." Bar: "Methodius wrote against him, and two of Origen's admirers wrote about what he did." Comparable; it turns the life toward the reports.
2. Flow. Weakest link: from "...Josephus 'did not accept Jesus as Christ.'" to "Eusebius of Caesarea, early in the fourth century, quotes the passage as we have it". The link is by time only. Bar: Methodius, "Both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness." The bar gives the judgment; mine leaves the listener to hold Origen against Eusebius. A reviewer may want it joined.
3. No AI tells. Weakest: "The longer report cannot be trusted as it stands." It opens a paragraph on a judgment. Bar: Methodius, "Little else about Methodius is certain." Same kind of plain limit.
4. No hanging sentences. Weakest: "He was not a Christian." Short, but the assignment asks for it said plainly, and the next sentence carries on his life. Close: Jerome's version, which ends the trust story and the reception together.
5. Everything relevant. Weakest: "After Jerusalem fell, the emperor Vespasian gave him a house in Rome, citizenship and a pension." It says how a Jewish priest came to write at Rome. Bar: Benedict, "Benedict built twelve monasteries near Subiaco and later moved to Cassino".

Sentences cut on the sentence test: 9 from the old brief's matter (Titus and the ruins, the holy books, the birth year, Jotapata, the prophecy and Suetonius, the Jewish War as a title, John the Baptist, the objection of the citizens to Ananus, a transition "Christian writers kept using both reports").

## Notes for the coordinator

- "Cannot be trusted as it stands" is the brief's own judgment, built only on the two ancient facts that follow it (Origen's statement, Jerome's different wording). No held ancient writer says the passage was altered. If that sentence is too strong, the plain alternative is "The longer report is doubtful."
- Origen does not quote the Antiquities exactly: he says Josephus blamed the fall of Jerusalem on the death of James, which is not in Antiquities XX.9.1 as Whiston prints it (JW 90990-91035). The brief says only that Origen "read this report in Josephus and used it"; the gap is listed in (a).
- "He governed Galilee for them": "them" is the Jews who rose. Josephus says the Jerusalem leaders appointed him (Life, JW 5104-5116).
- Jerome's "was believed to be Christ" is Richardson's English of Jerome's Latin; the Latin is not held, so the second renderer cannot check it against the original.
- The Greek of the longer passage is not held; Q2 and Q3 rest on Whiston's English with McGiffert's English of Eusebius beside it.
- Old brief: nothing found false. Its "Vespasian's old house, as a Roman citizen with a pension" and the Titus scene are in the Life as told. Its "Origen ... wrote more than a century later" is true by the census dates but is not said by a held source.
