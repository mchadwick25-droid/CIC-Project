## (c) Quotations

- file: `anf02_hermas-tatian-athenagoras-theophilus-clement-alexandria.xml`
- lines: 6972-6974
- printed: "certain barbaric writings, too old to be compared with the opinions of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their errors"
- rendering: "some non-Greek writings, too old to be compared with the teachings of the Greeks, and too divine to be compared with their error."
- original: γραφαῖς τισιν ἐντυχεῖν βαρβαρικαῖς, πρεσβυτέραις μὲν ὡς πρὸς τὰ Ἑλλήνων δόγματα, θειοτέραις δὲ ὡς πρὸς τὴν ἐκείνων πλάνην (`tatian_oratio-ad-graecos-grc-lat_otto1851.txt` 10387-10389; the OCR is rough: "γρα- / φαῖρ τισιν ... ὗς πρὸς ... αἷς πρὸς")
- notes: βαρβαρικαῖς means "of non-Greek peoples"; "barbaric" now means savage, so it misleads. δόγματα is "teachings" (the printed "opinions" is also fair). πλάνην is singular, "error". The Greek comparatives say "older, set against ..., more divine, set against ..."; "too old / too divine to be compared" follows the printed English, and the second renderer should weigh "older than ... and more divine than ...".

## (e) Significance and stances

He argued that the Christians' scriptures are older than Greek learning (own Address); Clement of Alexandria used his dating of Moses and Eusebius judged the Address his most useful work. He made one gospel from four (Eusebius); the Teaching of Addai shows it read in the church of Edessa. His teaching against marriage is known from two opponents, Irenaeus and Clement. Rabbula's order for the separate Gospels and Theodoret's remark, both as Hogg quotes them, show the church leaders turning against it; Theodoret's full account is not held.

## (f) Craft self-check

Line of the story: a pupil of Justin who found the faith in old non-Greek books, argued they were older than Greek learning, broke with the church in his opponents' telling, and left one gospel that the church of Edessa read.

- Good storytelling. Weakest: "He belongs to Syriac Christianity, the church that grew up around Edessa and Nisibis, because of a gospel book he made." It states the tradition before the story earns it. Bar: Benedict, "He was Benedict of Nursia, and the early Benedictines of Italy begin with him."
- Flow. Weakest link: "... denied that Adam was saved. Clement, a second opponent, quotes a lost book ..." Bar: Methodius, "Both men admired Origen, so neither is a neutral witness."
- No AI tells. Weakest: "His later teaching comes to us through opponents." A paragraph opening on a statement about sources; it is there because the hostile-witness rule asks for it early. Bar: Benedict, "Nearly all we know of his life comes from Gregory's Dialogues".
- No hanging sentences. Weakest: "Eusebius says some still had it in his day." Short, between the making of the book and its reading at Edessa. Bar: Athanasius, "His Life of Antony told the hermit's story." The close is Theodoret's remark, which ends the book's story in the churches.
- Sentence test / everything relevant. Weakest: "Clement, a second opponent, quotes a lost book in which Tatian calls the union of husband and wife a fellowship in corruption." Kept because the teaching otherwise rests on Irenaeus alone. Bar: Methodius, "That reply is lost, but Jerome counted it among the answers that had met Porphyry well."
