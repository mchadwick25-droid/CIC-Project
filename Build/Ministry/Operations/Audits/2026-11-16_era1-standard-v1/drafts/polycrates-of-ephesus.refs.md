## (b) What the brief draws on

- "In the 190s": Victor succeeded Eleutherus in the tenth year of Commodus (Eusebius V.22, E 30996-31003); Polycrates bishop at Ephesus at that time (E 31093-31094 with the chapter's list); Jerome: "flourished in the reign of the emperor Severus" (J 40414-40416).
- Bishop of Ephesus; Asia: Eusebius III.31.3 (E 19075-19076), V.24.1 (E 31310-31312). "Western Asia Minor" is plain geography.
- Tradition: census `post-apostolic-house-church`.
- The fourteenth day, the day the Jews sacrificed the lamb; the fast ended that day "whatever day of the week"; the other churches ended it only on the day of the resurrection: Eusebius V.23.1 (E 31136-31222, see 31217).
- Synods "from Gaul to Palestine" ruled for the Lord's day: V.23.2-3, Palestine, Rome, Pontus, Gaul, Osrhoene, Corinth (E 31224-31300, Gaul at 31288).
- Called the bishops of Asia together at Victor's request: his letter, "the bishops who were present, whom I summoned at your desire" (V.24.8, E 31406-31411; A8 72624-72625). They decided to keep the old custom: V.24.1 (E 31310-31312). The letter was to Victor and the church of Rome, and Eusebius quotes it: E 31312-31316.
- Q1: V.24.2, E 31324-31325; A8 72576-72582 with the Greek of the first clause in the note.
- The great dead of Asia, "great lights"; John, "who reclined upon the bosom of the Lord", fell asleep at Ephesus; "All these observed the fourteenth day": V.24.2-6 (E 31325-31335, 31364-31366).
- Seven relatives bishops before him, all of whom kept the day; he the eighth: V.24.6, E 31385-31388 ("seven of my relatives were bishops; and I am the eighth. And my relatives always observed the day"); A8 72616-72617.
- Sixty-five years in the Lord; "not affrighted by terrifying words"; "We ought to obey God rather than man": V.24.7, E 31396-31400; A8 72618-72623. Told without marks.
- Victor "immediately attempted to cut off from the common unity the parishes of all Asia ... and declared all the brethren there wholly excommunicate": V.24.9, E 31415-31419.
- Other bishops rebuked him, among them Irenaeus: V.24.10-11, E 31431-31436 ("Words of theirs are extant, sharply rebuking Victor. ... Among them was Irenæus").
- Irenaeus kept the Lord's day and warned Victor "that he should not cut off whole churches of God which observed the tradition of an ancient custom": V.24.11, E 31436-31442.
- Eusebius does not say how Victor answered: V.24.18 says only that Irenaeus "became a peacemaker", and V.25 turns to the Palestinian letter (E 31661-31700). Nothing more of Polycrates in the held ancient files beyond Jerome's notice (J 40382-40416), which adds only the reign.
- Nicaea ruled one day for all; the practice "observed at once in the city of Rome ... in the dioceses of Asia and Pontus, and in Cilicia": Constantine's letter, Life of Constantine III.18-19 (E 64606-64609, 64677-64681, 64689-64691); Greek VC 11417-11421 ("Ἀσιανήν τε διοίκησιν καὶ Ποντικὴν καὶ Κιλικίαν", the OCR of the first word is damaged). The year 325 is the council's known date (McGiffert gives it, E 31187).
- John Chrysostom took churches from the Quartodecimans "in Asia and Lydia", on his journey to Ephesus: Socrates VI.19 (S 18561-18564) and VI.11 (S 17976-17977). They "affirm that the observance of the fourteenth day was delivered to them by the apostle John": Socrates V.22 (S 16317-16318). "Two centuries after Polycrates": Chrysostom was bishop from 398 (bar brief; Socrates VI.2).

## (e) Significance and stances

- He led the bishops of Asia in keeping Easter on the fourteenth day and refused Rome's demand, on the ground of John and the dead of Asia: his own letter, Eusebius V.24.
- What came of it: Victor's attempt to cut Asia off and Irenaeus's protest (Eusebius V.24.9-18); by 325 Asia is listed with Rome (Constantine's letter); a remnant kept his custom and his claim to John into Chrysostom's day (Socrates).
- The record stops: no held ancient source says how Victor answered Irenaeus, when Asia changed, or how Polycrates ended.

## Notes for the coordinator

- Known flag answered: "the Roman practice won" no longer stands on McGiffert. Constantine's letter lists the "dioceses of Asia" among those already keeping Rome's practice in 325. Two cautions. The civil diocese of Asia is wider than Polycrates's province. And the question at Nicaea was keeping Easter with the Jews' reckoning; the letter does not mention the fourteenth-day custom by name. The brief says only "the churches of Asia already kept it as Rome did", which is what the letter says.
- Constantine's letter is violently anti-Jewish in its wording (E 64613-64634); none of that is told.
- The brief says "the apostle John" only in the closing sentence, where Socrates uses those words. Polycrates himself calls John "a witness and a teacher, who reclined upon the bosom of the Lord"; the brief keeps to that.
- "Leaned on the Lord's breast" is a telling without marks of "reclined upon the bosom of the Lord".
- "At once" follows McGiffert's "immediately"; the Greek is not held to check.
- Q1 departs from both printed translations ("exact day") to follow the Greek word the ANF note preserves. If the comparer prefers the familiar "exact day", it must say so against the Greek.
- Theodoret's Church History I.9-10 and Socrates I.9 were not opened for the Nicene letter; the Life of Constantine carried the point.
