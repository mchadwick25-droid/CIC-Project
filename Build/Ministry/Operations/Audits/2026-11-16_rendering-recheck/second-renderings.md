# Second renderings (independent)

Rule used for the edges of each range: a sentence or clause is rendered when its main verb falls inside the given lines. Fragments at the edges whose main verb lies outside the range are named in the notes, not rendered.

## Item 1

So we walk the middle way, with Christ as our leader. We follow after grace, without which we are nothing, and we affirm the labor of dutiful service.

Notes:
(a) "per medium ... gradientes": "walking through the middle", that is, keeping a middle course (between the two errors named just before). "post gratiam": "after grace", meaning following behind grace, or second to it. It could also be read as "after grace has come". "officiosae seruitutis": "dutiful service", or "service that does its duty / renders its due". "adserimus": "we affirm", "we assert", "we maintain".
(b) "oulpam" read as "culpam". "omuimodo" read as "omnimodo". Edge fragments not rendered: line 11049 opens with the tail of the previous clause, "desidiam et perire potuisse per culpam" ("desidiani" read as "desidiam"; "...could fall through sloth and perish through fault"), whose main verb lies on line 11048. Line 11051 ends with the start of the next sentence, "sed omnimodo ad-[rogantiam]", which continues beyond the range.

## Item 2

So we walk the middle way, with Christ as our leader. We follow after grace, without which we are nothing, and we affirm the labor of dutiful service.

Notes:
(a) Same range as Item 1, so the same rendering. "per medium": a middle course. "post gratiam": "after grace", following it or second to it. "officiosae seruitutis": "dutiful service". "adserimus": "we affirm" or "we maintain".
(b) "oulpam" read as "culpam"; "desidiani" read as "desidiam"; "omuimodo" read as "omnimodo". The same edge fragments as Item 1 are left out.

## Item 3

For while God's faithful servants show the goodness of their devotion in our needs, we are owners without a home of our own, and rich without possessions. More than that, we make rich the very people who gain fruit from comforting us.

Notes:
(a) "nam dum": "for while" or "for as long as". "sine sede propria possessores": "owners without a seat (home, residence) of our own". "sedes" can mean dwelling or episcopal see; here, in exile, dwelling is the plain sense, but a hint of "see" cannot be ruled out. "de nostra ... consolatione": "from our consolation", meaning the comfort they give us, not comfort we give them. "fructum capiunt": "reap fruit", "gain profit". "ditamus": "we enrich".
(b) "l)onitatem" read as "bonitatem". The marginal "is" (a line number, 15) after "eos" ignored. The sentence starts on line 13402 ("nam dum fideles famuli dei in neces-") but its main verbs ("sumus", "ditamus") fall within the range, so the whole sentence is rendered.

## Item 4

He, in glorifying his saints, invites us by their example. And yet I call God to witness, who knows what is hidden: I have kept silent about many of the deeds of my lord Germanus, deeds known and proven. For this I confess I am guilty, because I hold back what divine power wonderfully worked for the good of all.

Notes:
(a) "qui": refers back to Christ in the previous clause ("...what Christ did not find it irksome to do"). "exemplo": "by example"; it is the saints' example, or the example of his glorifying them. "domni mei": "my lord", a title of respect for a bishop. "agnita et probata": "known and tested", "known and verified". "subprimendo": "by suppressing", "by holding back". "ad profectum omnium": "for the progress / benefit / advancement of all". "virtus": "power", possibly also "miraculous power".
(b) "Cbrisiom" read as "Christum"; "pi¬guit" joined as "piguit"; "fr" at the head of line 4578 taken as a stray margin mark; asterisks, carets, backslash and superscript-like marks taken as footnote signs. "subprimendo" is a genuine variant spelling of "supprimendo", not an OCR error. Edge fragment not rendered: line 4577 opens with "-guit", the end of "Sed non pigeat recensere, quod Christum praestare non piguit" ("But let it not be irksome to recount what Christ did not find it irksome to do"), whose main verb "pigeat" is on line 4576.

## Item 5

As for what you write, asking why I did not come to the synod, as we had agreed when we talked: illness did this, not unwillingness.

Notes:
(a) "sicut conlocutio habuit nostra": literally "as our conversation had it", that is, as we had arranged in person. "infirmitas": "illness", "weakness", "frailty". "uoluntas": "will", here the absence of will to come, so "unwillingness" or "choice".
(b) "nou" read as "non"; "uero" read with the line before as "quod uero scribitis". The sentence starts on line 23185 ("quod uero scribitis, cur ad synodum..."); its main verb "fecit" is within the range, so the whole sentence is rendered. "ipsi etenim" at the line's end begins the next sentence and is left out.

## Item 6

My soul has clung to you, excellent teacher.

Notes:
(a) "adhaesit ... post te": quotes Psalm 62:9 (Vulgate). Literally "clung after you", meaning followed close behind, held fast to you. "doctor eximie": "outstanding / excellent / distinguished teacher".
(b) "consumendo." at the start of the line ends the previous clause ("et spinas criminum consumendo", "and burning up the thorns of sins"), whose main verb is earlier; not rendered. "20" is a margin line number, ignored.

## Item 7

If the name of their cities gives others authority, the lowliness of our city ought not to take authority away from us. For it is much better, and much more distinguished, for a city to become known through its bishop than for a bishop to become known through his city.

Notes:
(a) "sacerdos": in fifth-century Gaul usually "bishop"; could also be "priest". "humilitas": "lowliness", "smallness", "obscurity". "notescere": "become known", "become famous". "de": "from", "through", "by reason of".
(b) "umltoque" read as "multoque"; "eminontius" read as "eminentius"; "sacer-dotem" joined. The clause "si aliis ..." starts on line 23198 after "quia"; its main verb "debet" is on line 23199, so it is rendered. "quia" ("because") links it to the clause before and is left out.

## Item 8

Let Gaul hold us together, I pray, since the kingdom of heaven will hold us, if we are of good merit. For we have one kingdom promised.

Notes:
(a) "Capiat ... capiet": "contain", "hold", "receive", "take in". "quos capiet regnum coelorum": literally "us, whom the kingdom of heaven will hold"; rendered with "since" for sense, but it is a relative clause, so "we whom the kingdom of heaven will hold" is the closer form. "si boni simus meriti": "if we are deserving", "if we are of good merit". "simul": "together", "at the same time".
(b) "s'cut" (context) read as "sicut". The sentence "Unum enim regnum habemus promissum" continues past line 20765 ("et unam spem vocationis in Christo, cum quo conregnabimus...", "and one hope of calling in Christ, with whom we shall reign together"); only the clause whose verb lies in range is rendered. "oremus ut debemus" at the start of line 20763 ends a sentence begun earlier and is left out. Context lines: "ia Ch':isto" read as "in Christo"; "eum quo" read as "cum quo".

## Item 9

Let Gaul hold us together, I pray, since the kingdom of heaven will hold us, if we are of good merit. For we have one kingdom promised.

Notes:
(a) Same range as Item 8, so the same rendering. "capiat / capiet": "hold", "contain", "receive". The relative clause is closer as "we whom the kingdom of heaven will hold". "si boni simus meriti": "if we are deserving".
(b) Same edge handling as Item 8. "ia Ch':isto" (context) read as "in Christo".

## Item 10

In the same way, when we speak of this union [of the Incarnation], we say: Christ died, the Son died, through the Son all things were created and made.

Notes:
(a) "union": the union of the two natures in Christ. "[de l'Incarnation]" is the editor's bracket and is kept. "tout": "all things", "everything". "créé et fait": "created and made".
(b) No OCR problems in the range. The sentence starts on line 15852 ("De même, quand nous parlons de cette"); its main verb "disons" is on line 15853, so the main clause is rendered. The concessive clause "bien que nous comprenions..." ("although we understand...") begins at the end of line 15854 and continues past the range, so it is left out.

## Item 11

And he said to me: "Truly, Abba John, let us study nothing more today. For we have been edified enough."

Notes:
(a) "studeamus": "let us apply ourselves to", "let us pursue", "let us busy ourselves with"; "study" in the sense of seeking out more. "aedificati": "edified", "built up" in spirit. "Vere": "truly", "indeed".
(b) "nibil" read as "nihil"; "edificati" read as "aedificati"; "studea-mus" joined. "Et dixit" stands at the end of line 10093; the sentence's speech falls within the range, so the whole sentence is rendered. "Hezc ergo scripsi-[mus]" ("So we have written these things...") begins at the end of line 10095 and continues past the range; left out.

## Item 12

For we have been edified enough.

Notes:
(a) "satis": "enough", "sufficiently", "fully". "aedificati": "edified", "built up" in spirit. "enim": "for", giving the reason for "let us study nothing more today".
(b) "edificati" read as "aedificati". "mus;" at the line's start ends "studea-mus" from the line before and is left out. "Hezc ergo scripsi-" read as "Haec ergo scripsimus", which continues past the range; left out.

## Item 13

My most faithful son, I offer it to you, and through you to all.

Notes:
(a) "eam": the crown ("corona") woven earlier in the sentence. "fidelissime fili": vocative, "most faithful son". "offero": "I offer", "I present".
(b) "libi" read as "tibi"; "fidelis-sime" joined. The line opens with the end of the sentence begun on line 3783 ("Ex his ego flores pulcherrimos decerpens, coronam tibi ex immarcescibili ac perenni prato contexui", "Picking the loveliest flowers from these, I have woven you a crown from an unfading and everlasting meadow"); only the clause whose verb ("offero") lies in range is rendered, with its vocative.

## Item 14

Give back, give back what you owe.

Notes:
(a) "quos": masculine plural, "those whom" or "the ones which". It may refer to persons or, more likely in this letter, to books ("libros" or "codices") the writer is asking for. Rendered "what you owe" to keep both open; the closer form is "those you owe". "Redde": "give back", "pay back", "render".
(b) "übus" read as the end of "interjacentibus". Edge fragments not rendered: "übus terris." ends "Idcirco audi vocem meam tot interjacentibus terris" ("So hear my voice across so many lands between us"; "Ideirco" read as "Idcirco"), whose main verb "audi" is on line 52557. "Nam servus" begins "Nam servus es Christi et Christianorum..." ("For you are the servant of Christ and of Christians"), whose verb "es" is on line 52559.
