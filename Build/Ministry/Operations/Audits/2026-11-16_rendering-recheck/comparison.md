# Comparison of Reading A and Reading B: 14 close renderings

I read each original myself in cic/texts, at the lines named in tools/quote_renderings.json, and checked how each quote is used in the era3/batch6 briefs. I made no model calls and changed no repo file.

## Per entry

**1. faustus-of-riez: "We walk the middle way, then, with Christ as our guide,"**
- Original (11049-50): "nos ergo per medium Christo duce gradientes"
- B: "So we walk the middle way, with Christ as our leader."
- Verdict: **SAME**. "dux" can be guide or leader. A turns the participle into a main verb, which is normal in English.
- Two-kinds check: passes. Every word is covered and nothing is added. See entry 2 for where the sentence is split.

**2. faustus-of-riez: "After grace, without which we are nothing, we affirm the labor of dutiful service."**
- Original (11050-51): "...gradientes post gratiam, sine qua nihil sumus, laborem officiosae seruitutis adserimus."
- B: "We follow after grace, without which we are nothing, and we affirm the labor of dutiful service."
- Verdict: **MINOR**. The Latin is one sentence. "post gratiam" can attach to either verb:
  - with "gradientes": we walk behind grace (B);
  - with "adserimus": after grace, we affirm labor (A).
  
  A's split into two quoted sentences settles that choice silently. Both readings keep grace first, so the semi-Pelagian claim does not change. B also allows a third sense, "once grace has come."
- Two-kinds check: covers every clause and adds nothing. The sentence break is A's own choice.
- Suggested card note: *Uncertain: "after grace" may go with "walk" (we walk behind grace) or with "affirm" (after grace, we affirm our labor). Both keep grace first.*

**3. faustus-of-riez: "We are owners without a home of our own, and rich without possessions."**
- Original (13402-04): "nam dum fideles famuli dei in necessitatibus nostris bonitatem suae deuotionis exercent, sine sede propria possessores, sine possessione diuites sumus, immo eos ... ditamus."
- B: "For while God's faithful servants show the goodness of their devotion in our needs, we are owners without a home of our own, and rich without possessions. More than that, we make rich the very people who gain fruit from comforting us."
- Verdict: **MINOR**. The quoted words are the same in A and B. Two things differ in substance:
  - The original gives a reason. The paradox holds *while* God's servants supply the exiles' needs. The card quotes it bare, so it reads as a pure spiritual paradox. That is a softer claim than the original's grounded one.
  - "sede" means home here, but "see" (Faustus's bishopric, lost in exile) cannot be ruled out. B notes this.
- Two-kinds check: the quoted clauses are complete and nothing is added. The governing "dum" clause, partly on 13403, is left out.
- Suggested card note: add the context in prose ("while faithful servants of God met their needs"), and *Uncertain: "home" may also carry the sense of his bishop's see.*

**4. constantius-of-lyon: "And yet I call God, who knows what is hidden, to witness that I have left out many deeds of my lord Germanus that were known and proved."**
- Original (4577-79): "Et tamen Deum testor conscium secretorum, me plura de domni mei Germani factis agnita et probata tacuisse"
- B: "And yet I call God to witness, who knows what is hidden: I have kept silent about many of the deeds of my lord Germanus, deeds known and proven."
- Verdict: **SAME**. "Left out" and "kept silent about" mean the same thing in a written Life.
- Two-kinds check: passes. The next clause ("for this I confess I am guilty...") lies outside the quote and is not implied.

**5. ruricius-of-limoges: "Infirmity did this, not will."**
- Original (23186): "fecit hoc infirmitas, non uoluntas."
- B: "illness did this, not unwillingness."
- Verdict: **SAME**. Both say his absence from the synod came from weakness, not choice. A is the more literal of the two. Nearby context ("fessum me", worn out) supports "infirmity".
- Two-kinds check: passes.

**6. ruricius-of-limoges: "My soul has clung to you, outstanding teacher,"**
- Original (20044): "adhaesit, doctor eximie, anima mea post te."
- B: "My soul has clung to you, excellent teacher."
- Verdict: **SAME**. Psalm 62:9 Vulgate. The brief says "borrowing a psalm".
- Two-kinds check: passes. Literal "after you" (following close behind) is absorbed into "clung to you", as in standard renderings of the psalm.

**7. ruricius-of-limoges: "It is far better and far more distinguished for a city to become known by its bishop than a bishop by his city."**
- Original (23199-201): "siquidem multo melius multoque eminentius est ciuitatem de sacerdote, quam sacerdotem de ciuitate notescere."
- B: same sense. B's version is preceded by the conditional clause about a city's name giving authority.
- Verdict: **SAME**. The connective "siquidem" (since) is dropped, which is acceptable for a sentence-start quote. "sacerdos" means bishop in this setting.
- Two-kinds check: passes.

**8. columbanus: "Let Gaul, I pray, hold us together,"**
- Original (20763-64): "Capiat nos simul, oro, Gallia,"
- B: "Let Gaul hold us together, I pray,"
- Verdict: **SAME**. "simul" can also mean "at the same time", but "together" is the plain sense here.
- Two-kinds check: passes.

**9. columbanus: "whom the kingdom of heaven will hold, if our merits are good."**
- Original (20764-65): "quos capiet regnum coelorum, si boni simus meriti."
- B: "since the kingdom of heaven will hold us, if we are of good merit"
- Verdict: **SAME**. A keeps the relative form, which B's own note calls closer. B's "since" is B's paraphrase.
- Two-kinds check: passes. The next clause ("For we have one kingdom promised") lies outside the quote.

**10. babai-the-great: "We say: Christ died, the Son died, through the Son all was created and made,"**
- Original (Labourt's French, 15853-54): "nous disons : Le Christ est mort, le Fils est mort, par le Fils tout a été créé et fait, bien que nous comprenions que tels attributs appartiennent à l'humanité ... et tels autres à la divinité..."
- B: "...we say: Christ died, the Son died, through the Son all things were created and made."
- Verdict: **SAME**. The concessive clause ("although we understand these belong to the humanity... and those to the divinity") is doctrinally essential. The quote leaves it out, but the brief's next sentence paraphrases it outside the quotation marks ("The death belongs to the humanity and the making to the divinity, he explains"). So the card does not change the meaning.
- Two-kinds check: passes. One note: this renders Labourt's 1904 French translation of Babai's Syriac, not Babai's own words. The brief says "which J. Labourt quotes", which covers this. Keep that wording.

**11. john-moschus: "Truly, Abba John, let us study no more today,"**
- Original (10094-95): "Vere, abba Joannes, hodie nihil ultra studeamus;"
- B: "Truly, Abba John, let us study nothing more today."
- Verdict: **SAME**. The speaker is Sophronius, and the brief attributes the line to him correctly.
- Two-kinds check: passes.

**12. john-moschus: "We have been edified enough."**
- Original (10095): "satis enim edificati sumus."
- B: "For we have been edified enough."
- Verdict: **SAME**. Only the connective "enim" (for) is dropped.
- Two-kinds check: passes.

**13. john-moschus: "I offer it to you, and through you to all,"**
- Original (3784-85): "...fidelissime fili, eamque tibi, ac per te omnibus offero."
- B: "My most faithful son, I offer it to you, and through you to all."
- Verdict: **SAME**. "it" is the crown, which the brief names. A drops the vocative ("most faithful son"), which is not a clause.
- Two-kinds check: passes. Separately, the brief never says who "you" is. From line 3775 it is Sophronius ("sancte ac fidelissime fili, Sophroni"). The brief could name him; this does not change any meaning.

**14. isidore-of-seville: "Give back, give back what you owe,"**
- Original (52558): "Redde, redde, quos debes."
- B: "Give back, give back what you owe."
- Verdict: **SAME**. "quos" is masculine plural ("those you owe"), almost certainly the books of the Etymologies. Both readings keep it open. The speaker is Braulio, and the brief attributes the line to him correctly.
- Two-kinds check: passes.

## Note outside the A/B check (for the caller, not a rendering finding)
- **Audio drift.** Some audio scripts no longer match the brief's quoted text: faustus-of-riez.audio.txt drops "then", babai-the-great.audio.txt adds "and" before "through the Son", and constantius-of-lyon.audio.txt drops "And yet". The quote is meant to read the same in brief and audio.
- **John Moschus source language.** The Moschus renderings (11-13) are made from the Latin version in Migne PG 87.3. Moschus wrote in Greek. The JSON correctly says "Latin", but this rendering is two translations removed from Moschus's own words.

## Report to Mark
1. **faustus-of-riez, "After grace, without which we are nothing..."** (MINOR). The Latin does not settle which verb "post gratiam" attaches to, and A's sentence split silently picks one. Either keep both readings in an uncertainty note, or quote the sentence whole as one unit.
2. **faustus-of-riez, "We are owners without a home of our own..."** (MINOR). The quote drops the "while God's faithful servants meet our needs" reason. Add it in the prose before the quote, and note that "home" may also hint at his see.

The other 12 can keep their quotation marks. Optional, not a rendering issue: name Sophronius as "you" in the Moschus dedication, and bring the three audio scripts back into line with the brief's quoted wording.
