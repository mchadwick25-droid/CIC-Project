## P01: Early house churches

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (3): "Ignatius's letters urged one bishop with elders beside him" (why, and against what?). "Being a Christian could bring real danger" (what danger, from whom, why?). "he was marched under guard toward death" (charged with what, by whom?).
2. Cites (3): "Ignatius's letters urged"; "At Rome, early letters show"; "Ignatius's letters say".
3. Access (2): "Ignatius" is never introduced. There's a run of short one-act sentences: "No central body ruled them. Letters carried by hand held them together." Then the closing three move just as stiffly.
4. AI tells (4): "No central body ruled them" (says only what is missing). "had not yet settled who should lead" (missing again). "Being a Christian could bring real danger" (a confident line that says nothing). The rhythm is even and generic all through.

**B. As myself**
- Opening: no. A date range and a list of cities reads like a textbook caption.
- First drift: "After the apostles died, these churches had not yet settled who should lead."
- Listen to the end: yes, only because it's eight sentences long.
- Machine. Decider: "Being a Christian could bring real danger."
- Trust: partly. Nothing looks wrong, but nothing is shown either.
- What would stop me: there's no person and no scene. It's a summary card.

## P02: Wittenberg reform

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (6): "In 1518 Luther was summoned to Augsburg" (why?). "would not take back his books of teaching" (which books, and why were they condemned?). "burned at Brussels after they refused to give up their teaching" (what teaching?). "The Confession rejected those who taught otherwise about the Supper" (who, and what did they teach?). "In 1525 Luther wrote against the peasants' rising" (what did he say?). "in 1543 he wrote a treatise against the Jews" (what was in it?).
2. Cites (6): "By his own account, told years later at his table"; "He told the story years afterward to his students at his own table"; "By his account"; "By the same account"; "At his table, Luther told his students"; "The sources behind this account hold the 1525 writings, but not the 1543 treatise."
3. Access (5): "indulgences" is unexplained; so are "absolution", "the Electors", "Diet" and "Melanchthon" (never introduced). The doctrine paragraph is dense and abstract for a listener.
4. AI tells (5): "At the heart of this tradition sits a frightened conscience" (dramatic). "The reform ran ahead of its founder" (dramatic topic line). "Luther's name is not among them" (says only what is missing). "and they gave no explanation of how" (missing). "The sources behind this account hold..." is the machine reporting on its own inputs.

**B. As myself**
- Opening: no. "From 1517 to 1580, a reform grew out of Wittenberg" is flat. Then comes a paragraph of doctrine before any story.
- First drift: "In their teaching, a sinner is freely justified for Christ's sake, through faith, and the sacraments carry that promise."
- Listen to the end: no.
- Mixed. Decider: "The sources behind this account hold the 1525 writings, but not the 1543 treatise." No human narrator says that aloud.
- Trust: partly. "Luther died in the mid-1540s" is a vague hedge on a date everyone knows (1546), and it makes me doubt the rest.
- What would stop me: the repeated "at his table" sourcing, plus the line about its own sources.

## P03: Tacitus (short)

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (0). The charges are given ("hatred of the human race") and so is Tertullian's reason.
2. Cites (3): "hated, he says, for their shameful acts"; "where, he says, everything shameful in the world gathers"; "They were convicted, he says". Tacitus is the subject here, so these are milder than elsewhere.
3. Access (0). Judaea, Tertullian and Sulpicius Severus are all glossed.
4. AI tells (3): "He despised them and said so" (tacked-on punch). "Almost everything known of these deaths rests on this one chapter, and Tacitus never says where he learned of Christus" (half of it says only what is missing). "But where Tacitus called them criminals, he called them innocent" (the balanced turn).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: yes. Bodies lighting a garden, and Nero dressed as a charioteer. Then the twist: the man telling it thought they deserved it.
- First drift: "About the year 200, Tertullian, defending the Christians, answered him by name over another of his histories."
- Listen to the end: yes.
- Person. Decider: the close hands the last word to Tacitus himself, "to feed one man's cruelty", instead of adding a moral of its own.
- Trust: yes.
- What would stop me: the neat criminals/innocent mirror line.

## P04: Basil of Caesarea

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (4): "defending himself in a letter" (against what?). "made him a priest, though they quarreled" (over what?). "A rival bishop once blocked his road with armed men" (why?). "Accused before the governor" (of what, exactly?).

Cites (10): "the historian Rufinus says"; "In his own telling"; "gives the credit to their sister Macrina"; "Rufinus says"; "Basil founded a community and led it, Gregory says"; "When the old bishop died, Gregory says"; "Gregory's speech in Basil's memory is praise. In his telling"; "a hurt, Gregory said"; "The rich filled it at Basil's urging, Gregory says"; "tended them himself, Gregory says"; plus the final "Gregory says the whole city poured around Basil".
3. Access (1): "rhetoric" is used in paragraph two and only explained in paragraph three.
4. AI tells (4): "Who turned him away?" (a question asked only to answer it). "A man alone has no one to serve" (a moral tacked on after the quote). "That waking set the course of his life" (a summarizing link). The middle summary paragraph previews the whole life, and then the piece tells it again.

**B. As myself**
- Opening: yes. A real quote, "like a man roused from deep sleep".
- First drift: "He taught rhetoric, the art of public speaking, the historian Rufinus says. Who turned him away?"
- Listen to the end: no. "Gregory says" grinds.
- Mixed. Decider: "A man alone has no one to serve." It's the machine underlining what Basil already said better.
- Trust: partly. The narration keeps flagging Gregory's praise as unreliable while leaning on it for nearly everything.
- What would stop me: the tenth "Gregory says".

## P05: Rufinus of Aquileia

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (2): "When the emperor turned on the monks there for their creed" (which creed, and why?). "jailed and exiled" (on what charge?).
2. Cites (9): "his editors note" (twice); "he writes"; "he says"; "Jerome later wrote"; "an editor judges"; "by his editor's date"; "he said"; "his editors judge".
3. Access (1): "the Trinity" and "heretics" are used without a gloss. The "rule of faith" gloss is good, but it comes after the quote.
4. AI tells (4): "Within thirty years, a book Rufinus translated had ended theirs" (a dramatic close to the opening). "And the preface to First Principles broke his friendship with Jerome" (dramatic, and a repeat). "He did." (a one-line punch, and it's ambiguous whether it means Rufinus or Chromatius). "and the friendship did not recover" (a summing-up).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: yes. "A friendship that can end was never real" is a great hook, even if it's set up a bit too neatly.
- First drift: "First Principles and many of Origen's other works survive chiefly in his reworded Latin, his editors note."
- Listen to the end: no. The editors keep interrupting.
- Mixed. Decider: Jerome's "I wanted him not to believe what you had translated before" reads human. "his editors judge" at the very end reads like a footnote voiced aloud.
- Trust: yes.
- What would stop me: the editors. I don't care what an editor judges; tell me what happened.

## P08: Epiphanius of Salamis

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (0). The heresies are defined, the charges against Origen are spelled out, and the reasons for each act of force are shown.
2. Cites (6): "The historian Sozomen, writing later, says"; "Jerome, who was there and on his side, says"; "The historian Socrates says"; "Sozomen says instead"; "Sozomen says one of those monks asked"; "Augustine, who reports".
3. Access (0). "Heresy" and "Panarion" are glossed.
4. AI tells (2): "If the rivers were real, so was Eden" (a tacked-on line). "Yet crowds pressed to kiss his feet" is a small effect line.

**B. As myself**
- Opening: yes. A medicine chest for heresies is a concrete, odd image, in his own words.
- First drift: "Later writers, from Augustine to a council at Nicaea four centuries on, took his book as their list."
- Listen to the end: yes.
- Person. Decider: "It was not spiritual water but real water, the kind you can touch with your hand and take into your mouth." Then the seized deacon and the torn curtain. Nobody generates those.
- Trust: yes.
- What would stop me: little. The Socrates-versus-Sozomen paragraph is the slowest stretch.

"enormously wealthy, woven into the monarchies of Europe" (stock words). The "Then... Then came..." summary rhythm. The opening line is confident but says nothing.

**B. As myself**
- Opening: no. A sweeping thesis line is the most machine-like opening possible.

- Listen to the end: no.
- Machine. Decider: "(in a plain English rendering)". A production note was left in the narration.
- Trust: partly. It jumps from 1814 back to 1656 with no reason given, and it has no person in it.
- What would stop me: the parenthetical rendering note. One of those and I distrust the whole site.
