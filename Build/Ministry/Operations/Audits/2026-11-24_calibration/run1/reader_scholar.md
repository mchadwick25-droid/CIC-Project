## P01 — Household churches, 70-200

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (2): "Ignatius's letters urged one bishop with elders beside him" (why he urged it, against what, is not given). "he was marched under guard toward death" (no charge, no reason).
2. Citations (0): "Ignatius's letters say" is justified, since his martyr's journey rests on his own letters.
3. Accessibility (3): Ignatius is never introduced (who, which city). "elders" unexplained as an office. A run of short one-act sentences: "No central body ruled them. Letters carried by hand held them together. After the apostles died..."
4. AI tells (2): "No central body ruled them." (only says what is missing). "Being a Christian could bring real danger." (stock dramatic line).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: No. A date range and a list of cities; nothing happens.
- Drift: "No central body ruled them."
- End: Yes, only because it is eight sentences.
- Trust: Partly. Nothing false, but "these churches had not yet settled who should lead" is the modern reconstruction of monepiscopacy told as fact; a Catholic or Orthodox listener would contest it, and it is not tagged as contested.
- Recommend: No. Too thin to teach from.
- Would make me stop: the flat run of declaratives with no person or scene.

## P02 — The Wittenberg reform, 1517-1580

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (7): "In 1518 Luther was summoned to Augsburg" (why). "stood before the Emperor and the Electors at the Diet of Worms" (why he was there, the ban, is missing). "The Confession rejected those who taught otherwise about the Supper" (who, what). "Luther's name is not among them" (why: he was an outlaw at Coburg). "In 1525 Luther wrote against the peasants' rising" (what he said). "in 1543 he wrote a treatise against the Jews" (what it said). "after they refused to give up their teaching" (which teaching).
2. Citations (3): "He told the story years afterward to his students at his own table. By his account... By the same account" (one attribution would do). "The sources behind this account hold the 1525 writings, but not the 1543 treatise."
3. Accessibility (7): "indulgences", "absolution", "sacraments", "Diet", "Electors", "Melanchthon" (never introduced), "the Turk" all unexplained. Run of short sentences: "Melanchthon wrote the Augsburg Confession for it. An elector, several princes and the senates of two cities signed it. Luther's name is not among them."
4. AI tells (5): "At the heart of this tradition sits a frightened conscience." (dramatic). "The reform ran ahead of its founder." (dramatic thesis). "and they gave no explanation of how" (tacked-on). "Luther's name is not among them." (only what is missing). "The sources behind this account hold the 1525 writings, but not the 1543 treatise." (only what is missing, and process narration).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: No. "From 1517 to 1580, a reform grew out of Wittenberg." is a heading, not an opening; the conscience line comes only after.
- Drift: the doctrinal paragraph, "The churches of this reform taught that the commandments lead a person to despair of his own powers..."
- End: No.
- Trust: Partly. Good care with Table Talk as late testimony. But errors and evasions: the 1524 eight-song book was printed at Nuremberg, not Wittenberg's first; the Small Catechism of 1529 has more than three parts; "Luther died in the mid-1540s" when the date is 18 February 1546 is a needless vagueness; and naming the 1543 treatise against the Jews with no content, then telling us the sources lack it, reads as evasion on exactly the point students ask about.
- Recommend: No.
- Would make me stop: "The sources behind this account hold the 1525 writings, but not the 1543 treatise."

## P03 — Tacitus and Nero's gardens

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (1): "First those who admitted it were seized" (admitted what: arson or being Christian? Left open without telling the listener the text is ambiguous).
2. Citations (0): the "he says" tags separate Tacitus's view from fact in a hostile single witness; they earn their place.
3. Accessibility (1): "A superstition, he calls it" (the Roman sense of superstitio, an illicit foreign cult, is not explained).
4. AI tells (3): "He despised them and said so." (tacked-on). "Tacitus never says where he learned of Christus." (only what is missing, though relevant). "But where Tacitus called them criminals, he called them innocent." (tidy X/Y turn).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. A scene, then at once the witness and his bias.
- Drift: "About the year 200, Tertullian, defending the Christians, answered him by name over another of his histories." The ass's-head digression leaves the deaths.
- End: Yes; it closes on Tacitus's own words.
- Trust: Yes. "Almost everything known of these deaths rests on this one chapter" is honest and correctly hedged (Suetonius and 1 Clement are thin).
- Recommend: Yes.
- Would make me stop: the Tertullian paragraph, if it ran any longer.

## P04 — Basil of Caesarea

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (2): "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, though they quarreled" (over what). "Accused before the governor" (of what, only implied).
2. Citations (3): "Gregory says" piles up: "The rich filled it at Basil's urging, Gregory says... tended them himself, Gregory says." "Gregory says the whole city poured around Basil as he died." Several could go once the encomium is flagged.
3. Accessibility (2): two Gregorys introduced and then "Gregory says" with no name ("led it, Gregory says" means Nazianzen, but the listener cannot tell). "rhetoric" is used in paragraph two and only explained in paragraph three.
4. AI tells (3): "Who turned him away?" (question asked only to answer it, though it does open a real source disagreement). "A man alone has no one to serve." (tacked-on). "He died in 378 or 379, before a council confessed it." (tacked-on).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. Letter 223 in his own voice is the right door.
- Drift: "He taught rhetoric, the art of public speaking, the historian Rufinus says." The narrative steps back after the summary.
- End: Yes.
- Trust: Yes. It sets out three accounts of his conversion side by side and says plainly that Gregory's oration is praise. One muddle: "a hurt, Gregory said, that time had not healed" does not say the hurt was Gregory's own, at Sasima.
- Recommend: Yes.
- Would make me stop: losing track of which Gregory is speaking.

## P05 — Rufinus of Aquileia

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (1): "When the emperor turned on the monks there for their creed" (which creed, which side).
2. Citations (5): "his editors note" (twice), "an editor judges", "by his editor's date", "his editors judge". Modern editors cited aloud where the listener did not need them.
3. Accessibility (2): "Trinity" assumed. Pronoun tangle: "Rufinus was wholly apart from Rome and must answer to God, he wrote" (the "he" is the bishop of Rome, never named).
4. AI tells (4): "Within thirty years, a book Rufinus translated had ended theirs." (dramatic). "And the preface to First Principles broke his friendship with Jerome." (dramatic repeat). "He did." (tacked-on). "and the friendship did not recover" (summing).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. Jerome's line on friendship set against what came after is a strong hook from the source.
- Drift: "First Principles and many of Origen's other works survive chiefly in his reworded Latin, his editors note."
- End: Yes, though the editors' paragraph sags.
- Trust: Yes. Both sides of the quarrel get their own words, and Rufinus's method is quoted, not characterised.
- Recommend: Yes.
- Would make me stop: the fifth "his editors".

## P06 — Afonso I of Kongo

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (2): "a friar had arranged for seven or eight white men to fire at him" (why). "his brother Pango stood against him" (why, beyond the faith in general).
2. Citations (3): "The report says... It says... the report adds... Then, it says" (one marker for the whole paragraph would do). "which a Portuguese chronicler later printed".
3. Accessibility (2): "Zaire River" not glossed as the Congo. Run of short sentences: "The shot passed by the skirts of his robe, he said. It killed one man and wounded two. That charge rests on his word alone."
4. AI tells (4): "His letter is a plea. It does not tell us what came of it." (only what is missing). "That charge rests on his word alone." (tacked-on, if honest). "in his letters Afonso set the one against the other" (summing). Closing line "calling the king of Portugal his brother and signing himself king" (flourish).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. Wine and flour for the Mass beside a plea about the slave trade is a real tension.
- Drift: "Not everyone at court welcomed the faith."
- End: Yes.
- Trust: Partly. Good on the outsider sources and on the single-witness charge. But "Portuguese ships reached the mouth of the Zaire River in 1491" is wrong: Diogo Cão reached it in 1482-83; 1491 is the mission that baptised the king. The 1526 quote, standing alone, makes Afonso an abolitionist; he sought to regulate the export, and himself took part in the trade, and his inspection board of late 1526 is what "came of it".
- Recommend: Yes, with a correction.
- Would make me stop: the date error, for anyone who knows it.

## P07 — Ulfila

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (1): "persecution drove him out" (whose persecution, why).
2. Citations (3): "A modern historian takes this Eusebius for the bishop of Nicomedia." "their editor in 1908 had no doubt they were Ulfila's." "In 360, by the usual date".
3. Accessibility (3): "Nicaea" named without saying it was a council. "unbegotten" and "substance" in a dense creedal paragraph heard once. The 360 section loops back on itself ("Socrates puts it down to... Socrates says Ulfila took the creed only in 360") and is hard to follow aloud.
4. AI tells (2): "But when did he take that faith?" (question asked only to answer it). "It matters because the Goths still held that faith in the historians' day." (signposting).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. The Exodus framing is Auxentius's own, and the piece says at once that nearly all rests on him.
- Drift: "Auxentius says he was made bishop at thirty. Philostorgius, a historian of the next century, says it was at the emperor's court..."
- End: Yes.
- Trust: Yes. The best handling here of disagreeing sources: Socrates, Sozomen and Theodoret each on the date and motive of his Homoian faith, and "Arian" correctly marked as the opponents' label.
- Recommend: Yes, though second-language listeners will struggle with the doctrine.
- Would make me stop: the second pass through Socrates and Sozomen on 360.

## P10 — Priscillian

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (2): "Other bishops accused him" (of what, in the summary). "Ithacius lost his bishopric" (why).
2. Citations (3): "Sulpicius says" and "he adds" recur about eight times. Some are needed because he is the main witness, but "Nobles, common people and many women came to him, Sulpicius says" and "Its bishops above all quarreled for fifteen years, Sulpicius says" could go.
3. Accessibility (2): "His enemies won an order from the emperor" (Gratian, but the only emperor named so far is the usurper Maximus, so the listener will merge them). "a prefect" unexplained.
4. AI tells (3): "The deaths split the other side." (dramatic). "These are the court's findings; no account from his side survives." (only what is missing). The close "with his call in his own words again" (summing ring).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. His own words before his enemies' words.
- Drift: "Bishops met at Saragossa."
- End: Yes.
- Trust: Yes. Saragossa is told from both sides, Sulpicius's double-edged verdict is quoted, the charges are flagged as the court's, and Leo's approval of the killing is not softened. One tension: "no account from his side survives" sits oddly beside the Würzburg treatises the piece ends on; and the 1885 attribution is still debated (Instantius has been proposed), which "He judged" only half signals.

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes. The cost of learning Hebrew, in his own words.
- Drift: "Augustine reported, and by the Greeks he means the Greek-speakers in the town:"
- End: Yes; the close returns to his own "sweet fruit", from the source.
- Trust: Yes, mostly. Palladius's hostile account of Paula sits beside Jerome's own, and Rufinus's charge is quoted. Overclaim at the end: the Vulgate Trent chose is not wholly Jerome's (the Psalter is the Gallican, and much of the New Testament beyond the Gospels is not his revision).

- Would make me stop: the tangled Oea quotation.

**A. Faults**
1. Bare (3): "First those who admitted it were seized" (admitted what). "said the passage was open to challenge" (on what grounds). "rejected the view of some historians that the victims were Jews, not Christians" (why anyone held it, why he rejected it).
2. Citations (3): "Historians a century ago read that to mean..." "In an 1876 debate, a speaker arguing that the Bible was not divine said..." "In 1890, a translator of Eusebius's history of the early church rejected..."
3. Accessibility (1): "superstition" in the Roman sense unexplained.
4. AI tells (6): "He despised them and said so." "Yet his one chapter became what later historians built on." (tacked-on). "not that they set the fire" (X not Y). "Tacitus never says where he learned of Christus." "But where Tacitus called them criminals, he called them innocent." "Later historians made the chapter their main outside record..." (summing).

**B. As myself**
- Opening: Yes, the same strong opening as P03.
- Drift: "In an 1876 debate, a speaker arguing that the Bible was not divine said the passage was open to challenge."
- End: Partly. The reception paragraph nearly loses me before Tacitus's close.
- Trust: Partly. A nineteenth-century reading slips into the account of the trial itself ("Historians a century ago read that to mean the inquiry proved them Christians"). The authenticity question is reduced to an obscure 1876 debate and one sentence of reassurance, which flattens a real discussion (Chrestianos/Christianos in the Medicean manuscript, the interpolation arguments) instead of stating it.
- Recommend: No. P03 is the better cut.
- Would make me stop: the 1876 debate.
