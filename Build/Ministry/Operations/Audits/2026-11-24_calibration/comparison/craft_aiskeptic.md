## Q1: John Chrysostom

**1. Test lines.** I can fill who (bishop John), what happened (he preached against a fallen minister, made enemies, was exiled and died) and what it was about (blunt preaching against the powerful). I can't fill **why it matters**. The close about the followers coming back hints at a split the piece never told me about. 1.

**2. Opening.** Who, where and when are all there: 399, Constantinople, John. Why there is half there, since Eutropius's law sets up the scene. What's missing is why we should care about this bishop. 1.

**3. Failing sentences (3).**
- "He came from Antiochene Christianity, with its plain way of reading Scripture." You can't "come from" a Christianity. The listener loses what this has to do with the scene, and it never pays off.
- "he moved the bishop's extravagant spending to the hospital" Whose extravagance? It sounds like John's own, which flips the meaning.
- "After a clash with the empress Eudoxia, a second council silenced him" The clash and the grounds are both lost.

**4. Bare statements (5).**
- "At Antioch John had trained under Diodore, who kept to the literal sense of Scripture." It stands alone as a paragraph and connects to nothing.
- "He rebuked men in office too." We aren't told for what.
- "cited John to appear" No charges are given.
- "After a clash with the empress Eudoxia" We aren't told what the clash was.
- "the followers who had left it over John's deposition" The split is never told.

**5. Citations read aloud (1).** "Palladius of Helenopolis". The "of Helenopolis" tag means nothing to a listener.

**6. AI tells (3).**
- Stock: "turned the tide against him".
- A tacked-on line: the Diodore paragraph.
- Generic rhythm in the "The people rose, and he was recalled." stretch.

**7. Accessibility (4).** "see", "deposed", "deposition" and "communion" go unexplained. "the Oak near Chalcedon" also needs a word of explanation.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes. The trembling man under the altar works.
- Attention drifts at "At Antioch John had trained under Diodore".
- Listen to the end: no. It's compressed into a list after the halfway point.
- Verdict: mixed. Deciding line: "He came from Antiochene Christianity, with its plain way of reading Scripture." That's a summary-tag sentence a person who knew John wouldn't write.

## Q2: Epiphanius

**1. Test lines.** All four fill. He wrote the heresy catalogue, fought Origen's followers, was used by Theophilus, and set the list later writers used. 0.

**2. Opening.** The first three sentences give the book and its purpose. They don't say who Epiphanius is, where he worked or when. 3.

**3. Failing sentences (2).**
- "A heresy, to Epiphanius, was a wrong belief held by people led astray ever since Adam, the first man." It's unclear whether "since Adam" belongs to the belief or to the people. The idea that his list starts before Christ gets lost.
- "The monks there had complained for a year that no one celebrated the sacraments, the church's holy rites, for them, he explains, and he feared they would drift from the church." The gloss and the "he explains" are nested inside it, and the cause-and-effect gets lost by the end.

**4. Bare statements (2).**
- "made a man a deacon in the territory of its bishop ... against church rules" We aren't told why he did it there.
- "A book written to answer the council's argument" The book is unnamed and has no context.

**5. Citations.** 0 needless. Socrates and Sozomen are named because they disagree, which earns them.

**6. AI tells (2).**
- The rounded close, "the church, which he calls Christ's holy bride", is slightly a summing close, though it's earned from the book itself.
- "Epiphanius held to the plain sense." is a short pivot line.

**7. Accessibility (1).** "the Long Brothers" is named but never said to be monks until the context implies it. The other terms are glossed: deacons, sacraments, Genesis.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes. The medicine chest is a real image.
- Attention drifts mildly at "Augustine wrote his own book on the heresies in the order of Epiphanius's list."
- Listen to the end: yes.
- Verdict: person. Deciding line: "It was not spiritual water but real water, the kind you can touch with your hand and take into your mouth." It's his own voice, chosen well. "a man so devout that he was simple in mind and manners" also lands.

## Q3: John Chrysostom

**1. Test lines.** All fill, including why it matters: his sermons were read for centuries, and the split healed only when his body came home. 0.

**2. Opening.** Who (Eutropius, then John), where (the cathedral of Constantinople), when (399) and why there (the law and his fall) are all present. 0.

**3. Failing sentences (2).**
- "They decked the altar, where the bread of Communion lies, which Christians call Christ's body, with gold cups and silk." Two glosses sit inside it. "They" (the rich) has to be held across it, and the verb's object arrives too late.
- "A later bishop of Constantinople summed up the meeting's own written record, and they tell it differently" "They" is ambiguous: the bishop or the record? The later bishop is also unnamed.

**4. Bare statements (1).** "He corrected his clergy sharply over how they lived" We aren't told what about how they lived. It's minor.

**5. Citations.** 0 needless. "Socrates, a church historian of the city writing a generation later" tells me why to trust him.

**6. AI tells (2).**
- The second paragraph is a preview thesis that gives away the ending: "the followers who left the church over his fall came back only when his body came home."
- "He preached plainly about the poor, and his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile." This is a summing line placed early.

**7. Accessibility (1).** "Chalcedon" isn't placed. The rest are glossed: Arians, altar, Communion.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes. The full mother quote, "hid him behind her curtains", carries it.
- Attention drifts briefly at "His accusers charged that he had sold church property, insulted the clergy, struck a man and eaten greedily alone." It's a list, but a vivid one.
- Listen to the end: yes. The white clothes at Comana are a detail only someone who read the source would keep.
- Verdict: person. Deciding line: "His followers kept Easter in the public baths, and from then on they met apart from the church."

## Q4: Basil of Caesarea

**1. Test lines.** All fill: the monk-rules, the lodging for the poor, the defense of the Spirit, and the influence on Benedict. 0.

**2. Opening.** The first three sentences give who (Basil) and the turn in his life. Where comes in sentence four, and there's no when until "fourth century". 1.

**3. Failing sentences (2).**
- "When the emperor Valens, who favored the party that ranked Christ, the Son, below God the Father, moved against the churches that held them equal, Basil came back to serve the bishop." It's about 35 words and nested. "Them" has to be untangled.
- "Basil answered by making more bishops for the province, Gregory says." Why that was an answer to an armed rival is lost.

**4. Bare statements (1).** "The bishop of Caesarea made him a priest, but they quarreled" We aren't told over what.

**5. Citations (1).** "in his speech in Basil's memory" is mildly surplus. The rest earn their place, because the sources disagree on what turned him.

**6. AI tells (1).** "That waking set the course of his life" is a slightly writerly hinge.

**7. Accessibility (1).** "creed" is unexplained. Rhetoric and council are glossed.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes. "like a man roused from deep sleep".
- Attention drifts at "Basil closed prayers by giving glory to the Father in two forms". It's dense, though it's then explained.
- Listen to the end: yes.
- Verdict: person. Deciding line: "Whom, then, will you wash? Whom will you care for? Behind whom will you be last, if you live by yourself?" It's placed so the source speaks.

## Q5: John Chrysostom

**1. Test lines.** I can't fill why it matters. The end mentions a split that is never shown. 1.

**2. Opening.** Who, where and when are there. Why we're there is half there. 1.

**3. Failing sentences (3).**
- "In 398 Eutropius made Theophilus of Alexandria ordain him." Without the court's choice, the reason Eutropius would force a rival bishop is lost.
- "he moved the bishop's extravagant spending to the hospital" Whose extravagance is unclear.
- "He and his bishops, meeting at the Oak near Chalcedon, cited John to appear." "He" must reach back past a paragraph break, and there's no year.

**4. Bare statements (4).**
- The Diodore line sits in its paragraph with no link to anything.
- "in a quarrel over whether God has a human body" The side each took isn't given.
- "the Eucharist" There's no reason given for why that would enrage anyone.
- The followers' split is never told.

**5. Citations (3 needless).** "Socrates says" comes six times: "Socrates says his rebukes", "Socrates says Theophilus", "Socrates says that when", "they deposed him, Socrates says, for refusing", "By this, Socrates says". At least three are surplus.

**6. AI tells (3).**
- Hedging filler: "It is said he was as blunt with the Gothic general Gainas".
- A sentence-fragment rhythm: "They taught that the Son of God had a beginning."
- The early thesis line: "his blunt words to the powerful led to his exile."

**7. Accessibility (5).**
- Unexplained words: "see", "Eucharist", "deposed", "communion" and "Arians" (explained, but on the next line).
- There's a run of short one-act sentences in the Socrates paragraph.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes.
- Attention drifts at "Socrates says Theophilus held a grudge, since his favorite had lost the see to John."
- Listen to the end: no. The repeated attribution wears.
- Verdict: mixed. Deciding line: "It is said he was as blunt with the Gothic general Gainas". A person who knows the record says who said it.

## Q6: Basil of Caesarea

**1. Test lines.** I can't fill why it matters with clarity. The Spirit defense is muddied by "He answered from what the church already did". 1.

**2. Opening.** There's no where in the first three sentences (the letter, the youth, the waking). 1.

**3. Failing sentences (4).**
- "He died in 378 or 379, before a council confessed it." "Confessed it" is strange English, and what was confessed has to be guessed.
- "Basil answered by making his brother bishop of Nyssa and his friend bishop of Sasima, a hurt, Gregory said, that time had not healed." The hurt was Gregory's own at being sent to Sasima, and that is lost.
- "He answered from what the church already did" The baptismal argument is lost. We only learn it from the quote that follows, and only half of it.
- "In 787 a council told the houses still standing where men and women lived as monks, side by side but apart, to keep his rule." It's garbled. "Houses still standing" reads as a building survey.

**4. Bare statements (3).**
- "though they quarreled" We aren't told over what.
- "Basil was chosen against the bishops of the province" We aren't told why they opposed him.
- "Accused before the governor" We aren't told of what.

**5. Citations (4 needless).** "Gregory says" or "Gregory said" appears seven times, plus "Gregory admits". "Lepers driven from their towns were taken in, and Basil greeted them as brothers and tended them himself, Gregory says." comes right after "at Basil's urging, Gregory says." "Gregory's speech in Basil's memory is praise." is a bare source-criticism note read aloud.

**6. AI tells (6).**
- A question asked only to answer it: "Who turned him away?"
- A tacked-on moral after the quote: "A man alone has no one to serve."
- The dramatic one-line close, moved out of order for effect: "Gregory says the whole city poured around Basil as he died."
- Generic rhythm in paragraph two: "He wrote rules ... He built ... As bishop from 370 ... He died ..."
- "Gregory's speech in Basil's memory is praise." is a confident line that does no work where it sits.
- "Gregory admits" puts the narrator's stance into the source.

**7. Accessibility (3).**
- There's a run of short one-act sentences in paragraph two.
- "confessed" is used in its technical sense, unexplained.
- "Sasima" and "Nyssa" are dropped in without context.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes.
- Attention drifts at "He taught rhetoric, the art of public speaking, the historian Rufinus says. Who turned him away?"
- Listen to the end: no.
- Verdict: mixed, leaning machine. Deciding line: "A man alone has no one to serve." It's the moral stapled after a quote that already said it better.

## Q8: Epiphanius

**1. Test lines.** All fill. 0.

**2. Opening.** Like Q2, it has no who, where or when in the first three sentences. 3.

**3. Failing sentences (3).**
- "Augustine, who reports that Epiphanius set twenty heresies before Christ and sixty after, followed his order." The structure of the book is lost inside a clause about Augustine.
- "A reply written at Charlemagne's order answered with his own curtain letter." "Curtain letter" is compressed. A listener can't tell what was answered with what.
- "Then Theophilus ... wrote to win him against Origen, though Theophilus had once accused Epiphanius of thinking God has a human form." The irony needs explaining and gets none.

**4. Bare statements (3).**
- "Jerome's brother had fled the priesthood several times. ... he had deacons seize the man and stop his mouth." There's no reason given. Q2 gives the monks' year without sacraments.
- "He tore it apart and asked that such curtains be banned." There's no reason. Q2 says why.
- "He listed even the followers of Apollinaris" We aren't told who Apollinaris was or what he taught.

**5. Citations (2 needless).** "The historian Sozomen, writing later, says he was born near Eleutheropolis". The birthplace adds nothing for an ear. "Sozomen says one of those monks asked" is surplus.

**6. AI tells (4).**
- A tacked-on summing line: "If the rivers were real, so was Eden."
- A dramatic line: "Yet crowds pressed to kiss his feet."
- Signposting: "The same letter defends two acts of force."
- An etic, smooth definition: "A heresy, in his book, is a teaching the church judged false."

**7. Accessibility (3).** "ordained", "deacons" and "Apollinaris" are unexplained. "Eleutheropolis" means nothing to a listener.

**8. Captivating.**
- Opening: yes.
- Attention drifts at "The historian Sozomen, writing later, says he was born near Eleutheropolis".
- Listen to the end: yes. The "old fool" and the crowds kissing his feet are vivid.
- Verdict: mixed. Deciding line: "If the rivers were real, so was Eden." The machine explains the joke the quote just told.
