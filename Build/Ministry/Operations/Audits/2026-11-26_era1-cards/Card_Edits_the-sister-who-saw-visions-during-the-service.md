## Listed for the fix (the story text and census fields; not changed here)

1. **Text, first sentence:** "Tertullian, defending the New Prophecy against the charge of disorder, described how it actually worked in his own congregation." On the Soul 9 answers no charge of disorder. Tertullian cites the sister to show that the soul has a body, "not simply from the assurance which reasoning has taught us of its corporeal nature, but also from the firm conviction which divine grace impresses on us by revelation" (ANF 3, lines 15798-15802). A claim its source does not hold.
2. **Text:** "What Tertullian wants his readers to notice is the discipline around it." His stated point is the soul's bodily form (same lines). Unsupported reading of his purpose.
3. **Text:** "She does not interrupt." Not stated; an inference from her reporting after the dismissal (lines 15816-15818).
4. **Text, last sentence:** "It is one of the very few descriptions of a Montanist gathering from a participant rather than an enemy, and it does not look like the frenzy the opponents described: a woman with a recognized gift, a congregation that valued it, and elders who tested every word before receiving it." A modern judgment told as the record ("one of the very few", the comparison with the opponents), and three details no held line gives: the congregation's valuing, "elders" (Tertullian says only "to us", lines 15817-15818), and "before receiving it". Fault 2 (a modern view told as the record).
5. **Text, quotation marks:** 'tender and lucid and of the colour of air' is a fresh rendering of the Latin "tenera et lucida et aerii coloris" (CSEL 20, lines 17500-17501), not a held English print (ANF 3 reads "soft and transparent and of an etherial colour", lines 15823-15824). Under the card rule, quotation marks go only around a source's own words as a cited file gives them.
6. **Text, punctuation:** the third quotation drops ANF's parentheses ("(for all her communications ... probed)", lines 15818-15820). Words verbatim; listed for release only.
7. **`when` "c. 207–212":** a modern dating of On the Soul. No old source gives it. Suggested: "in Tertullian's day" (Jerome: under Severus and Caracalla, lines 40498-40499).
8. **`teaser` "In Carthage":** the passage names no city; Carthage rests on Jerome (line 40496). Not wrong; the card says where it comes from.
9. **`sources[0]`:** gives the work only. The card adds file and lines (ANF 3, 15798-15828; CSEL 20, 17465-17501).

## Supported, kept

"She converses with angels, sometimes with the Lord" (lines 15808-15809); "she reads hearts and prescribes remedies for those who ask": "for those who ask" is the Latin "desiderantibus" (CSEL 20, line 17473), though ANF 3 has "to them who are in need" (lines 15809-15810); "On the day in question she had seen a soul in bodily form" (Latin "forte ... disserueramus, cum ea soror in spiritu esset", lines 17476-17477; the soul, lines 15820-15822); "which is why Tertullian cites her in a treatise on the soul" (lines 15798-15802).
