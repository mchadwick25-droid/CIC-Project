## 1. `montanism-the-new-prophecy` (tradition): FIX

### What holds

- **Timeline.** 45 lines opened at their cited lines. Apart from the findings below, each says no more than its source, enemies' charges, wonders and oracles stay in their witness's voice (lines 1, 4, 14, 16, 18, 24, 33, 42, 45), and no modern date or editor's note is used (McGiffert's 172/177 and his synod notes at E-en 29591-29629 and 29716-29738 are left out, as they should be).
- **briefSources.** All seven files exist, and every file the `quotationOriginals` cite is among them.
- **uncertain.** All 15 items are word for word the batch-11 card's (compared by script). None contradicts the final brief or the census `longDescription`.
- **quotationOriginals.** All three are word for word the batch-11 card's. Each `modern` is a quoted span in the `longDescription`. Each `printed` stands at its lines: E-en 29849-29852, CSEL20 15495-15499, ANF04 9773-9777 (`englishPrinted`), PanH 18758-18764.
- **Shown dates.** "2nd-6th c." is unchanged and meets the date rule: a second-century start (Epiphanius, PanH 17018-17019, and the reign of Antoninus Pius) and an end under Justinian (Procopius, 2195-2198).
- **Menu sections.** Each section in Card_Edits is filled from a real census field, or is plainly marked as nothing held (Kind's own sections, Modern works, and the census `sources`).

### Findings

**M1. shortDescription: BLOCKING.**

The draft runs to 46 words in two sentences. Mark's Montanism example has 36 words, and he asked for something simpler. The draft also carries more load than the example: three names, a place gloss, a promise clause and a trailing participle.

"The Holy Spirit Jesus had promised" fails the heard-once ruling. A listener can hear "Holy Spirit Jesus" as one name.

"Calling the church to a stricter life" sets them against the church by comparison. That comes close to the contrast Mark ruled out ("we dont say ... what they were not").

- Old: "In Phrygia, in the uplands of what is now western Turkey, Christians followed three prophets, Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla, whose words they called the New Prophecy. They held that the Holy Spirit Jesus had promised was speaking through these prophets, calling the church to a stricter life."
- New: "In Phrygia, in what is now western Turkey, Christians followed the prophets Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla. They held that the Holy Spirit spoke through them, and called their words the New Prophecy."
- The new text has 32 words in two sentences, with no negative, contrast or dash. Each phrase rests on a cited line:
  - "Phrygia": E-en 30184-30186 and 30275 ("small towns in Phrygia").
  - "what is now western Turkey": the final brief, paragraph 1, and the census `modernCountries`.
  - "the prophets Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla": ANF05 13113-13117 ("Priscilla and Maximilla, whom they supposed (to be) prophetesses ... Montanus as a prophet") and E-en 29266-29269.
  - "the Holy Spirit spoke through them": ANF05 13115-13116 ("into these the Paraclete Spirit had departed") and ANF03 47250-47251 ("the Paraclete utters ... by the mouth of the prophetess Prisca").
  - "called their words the New Prophecy": ANF03 28749 ("the word of the new prophecy which is a part of our belief"), and the brief's paragraph 2 ("the New Prophecy, their name for the prophets and their words").

**M2. Timeline line 31 (Firmilian): BLOCKING.**

The phrase "nor the Holy Spirit" is printed in italics at ANF05 39622-39623. The editor's note marks those words as "conjecturally interpolated, but have no authority." The line gives them as Firmilian's own words.

The line also says the Iconium bishops held the Cataphrygian point. In the letter, that point is Firmilian's own argument. What the bishops at Iconium confirmed was the wider rule that heretics cannot baptize (39629-39642).

- Old: "Bishops gathered at Iconium in Phrygia, Firmilian among them, refuse the baptism of the Cataphrygians, holding that they have neither the Father, nor the Son, nor the Holy Spirit."
- New: "Firmilian writes that the Cataphrygians, who claim new prophecies, can have neither the Father nor the Son. Bishops gathered at Iconium in Phrygia, he among them, had confirmed that heretics cannot baptize."
- Support: ANF05 39619-39623 ("they who are called Cataphrygians, and endeavour to claim to themselves new prophecies, can have neither the Father, nor the Son"); 39634-39640 ("neither may he baptize ... All which we some time back confirmed in Iconium, which is a place in Phrygia, when we were assembled together").

**M3. Timeline line 39 (Sozomen II.32): BLOCKING.**

This line moves a date onto the wrong event. At Soz 30570-30577 the suffering is the treatment of heretics under Constantine's law, which is the subject of that chapter. Only the Phrygians' numbers are "to the present day". The line puts the persecution in Sozomen's own day.

- Old: "Everywhere but Phrygia and the lands around it, they suffer what other heretics suffer. There they have been many since the time of Montanus, and still are."
- New: "Sozomen writes that under Constantine's law they suffered what other heretics suffered, everywhere but in Phrygia and the lands around it. There they had been many since the time of Montanus, and still are in his own day."
- Support: Soz 30574-30577 ("The Phrygians suffered the same treatment as the other heretics in all the Roman provinces except Phrygia and the neighboring regions, for here they had, since the time of Montanus, existed in great numbers and do so to the present day"). The chapter is on Constantine's law against all heresies (chapter title at 30514-30517; the law's effect, 30555-30573).

**M4. Period 3 headline: MINOR.**

"The end she foretold does not come" sums more than the period's lines. Line 17 tests the wars and anarchy she foretold, which is the elder's point. The unfulfilled "end" is Epiphanius's point, at PanH 17221-17226, which no line of the period cites.

- Old: "Maximilla dies, and the end she foretold does not come"
- New: "Maximilla dies, and her opponents say what she foretold has not come"
- Support: E-en 29880-29890 ("has not this been shown clearly to be false? ... neither a partial nor general war"); PanH 17221-17226 ("καὶ οὔπω ἡ συντέλεια", no end yet).

**M5. Period 1 headline: MINOR.**

The headline says the prophesying began "in Phrygia". Its own line 1 puts Ardabau "in Mysia on the border of Phrygia", from E-en 29577-29580.

- Old: "Montanus, then Priscilla and Maximilla, begin to prophesy in Phrygia"
- New: "Montanus, then Priscilla and Maximilla, begin to prophesy on the borders of Phrygia"
- Support: E-en 29577-29580 ("Ardabau in that part of Mysia, which borders upon Phrygia").

**M6. Line 35 (Epiphanius): MINOR.**

"He finds them" says Epiphanius found them himself. The Greek says only that the sect is in those places.

- Old: "He finds them in Cappadocia, Galatia, Phrygia, Cilicia, and most of all in Constantinople."
- New: "He says they are found in Cappadocia, Galatia, Phrygia, Cilicia, and most of all in Constantinople."
- Support: PanH 18514-18517 ("ἔστι γὰρ καὶ τὸ γένος ἐν τῇ Καππαδοκίᾳ ... ἐν Κωνσταντινουπόλει τὸ πλεῖστον").

**M7. Line 25 (On the Soul): MINOR.**

"Has every one examined" makes the sister the one who orders the examination. The source puts it in the passive.

- Old: "reports them after the people have gone, and has every one examined."
- New: "reports them after the people have gone, and every one is examined."
- Support: ANF03 15816-15820 ("all her communications are examined with the most scrupulous care").

**M8. Lines 40 and 41, `when`: MINOR.**

The quoted phrase "to the present day" comes from II.32 (Soz 30577). It does not stand at VII.18 (41731-41736) or VII.19 (41786-41790). The record's phrase is quoted where the record does not say it.

- Old, on both lines: `"\"to the present day\""`
- New, on both lines: `"in Sozomen's own day"`
- Support: Soz 41731-41733, where the present tense is used ("celebrate the Passover according to a strange fashion"); Soz 41788-41790 ("as I have myself observed ... among the Novatians and Montanists of Phrygia").

**Note, not a card change.** In Card_Edits, the shortDescription table gives ANF03 47250-47251 as "On Monogamy". That passage is On the Resurrection of the Flesh. The quotation itself stands at those lines.

### Findings

**A1. shortDescription (Drafts_A.md, card 2): BLOCKING.**

The rule is that every phrase is clear heard once. Three parts of the draft fail it, and one breaks the opening ruling:

- "newcomers" is left open: newcomers to the city, or to the faith?
- "higher ones for those who grow" hangs: it does not say grow in what.
- After the colon, the draft lays the three parts (body, soul, spirit) onto two ("a plain sense, and higher ones"), which muddles the image.
- The opening "Christian teachers taught the faith" is the kind of generic opening Mark ruled out ("i dont like these christians starting"), and "teachers taught" doubles the word.

At 44 words, the draft is also longer than the Montanism example (36). Mark asked for something simpler.

- Old: "Christian teachers taught the faith to newcomers, and students of Greek philosophy came to learn from them. One teacher, Origen, said Scripture is like a person, with a body, a soul and a spirit: a plain sense, and higher ones for those who grow."
- New: "In Alexandria, teachers led newcomers to the faith through Scripture, and philosophers came to study with Origen. He taught that Scripture has a plain story and deeper meanings beneath it."
- The new text has 30 words in two sentences, with no negative or contrast. Each phrase rests on a cited line:
  - "teachers led newcomers to the faith": E-en 32670-32674 ("some of the heathen came to him to hear the word of God") and 34407-34418 ("the elementary training of beginners").
  - "through Scripture": E-en 34838-34843 ("help to them in the study and understanding of the Divine Scriptures"), and the brief's paragraph 2 ("Teachers took pagans and catechumens through Scripture").
  - "philosophers came to study with Origen": E-en 34818-34823 ("not a few of the most distinguished philosophers, studied under him diligently").
  - "a plain story and deeper meanings beneath it": ANF04 33474-33486 ("the 'flesh,' as it were, of the Scripture, for so we name the obvious sense"), the Philocalia (`origen_philocalia_lewis1911.txt` line 76), and the brief's paragraph 2 ("read for a deeper sense beneath the plain story").

**A2. Timeline line 35, note: BLOCKING.**

"The conversion is legend" is the card's own judgment, given as content. No held source says it, and the Card_Edits quotes do not support it. Part 1 says "We are a witness, not a judge." The line already tells the story in the History's own voice ("tells how").

- Old: "A history compiled long afterward; the conversion is legend."
- New: "A history compiled long afterward."
- Support: the text of the line, and the History of the Patriarchs at 11706-11720 and 11884-11892.

**A3. Period 9 headline: MINOR.**

"In two minds" means "undecided", and a second-language reader will take it that way. The History is not undecided. It honors Dionysius and condemns Origen's books.

- Old: "The Coptic church remembers the teachers in two minds"
- New: "A later Coptic history honors Dionysius and condemns Origen's books"
- Support: `severus-al-muqaffa_history-patriarchs-coptic-church-v1_evetts1904.txt` 10942 ("lie wrote books full of lies"), 11706-11720 ("a distinguished teacher") and 11884-11892.

**A4. Shown dates: MINOR.**

There are two proposals for one field:

- Card_Edits proposes "c. 180 to 4th c."
- Drafts_A.md (card 2, Dates · place) proposes "late 2nd – 4th c."

Both meet the date rule. The card can show only one of them.

- Recommended: `dates` "c. 180 to 4th c.", with Drafts_A's line changed to match.
- Support for the start: E-en 28467-28476 and 28488 (Commodus's first year; "About that time, Pantænus"), and the brief's "From about 180". Support for the end: Rufinus, Schwartz 29895-29905, and Soz 31948-31962 (Didymus teaching under Athanasius).

**A5. Period 4 `when`: MINOR.**

"About 231" is a year converted from a regnal date. Card_Edits says such years from the Outline "are not shown as years", and period 5 keeps Eusebius's own phrase for the same event. The card should treat that event the same way in both periods.

- Old: "From the reign of Severus to about 231"
- New: "From the reign of Severus until Origen leaves"
- Support: E-en 36330-36332 ("in the tenth year of the above-mentioned reign that Origen removed from Alexandria to Cæsarea").

**A6. Timeline line 21 (Porphyry): MINOR.**

"Such readers" has nothing in its own line to refer to.

- Old: "charges that such readers boast that the plain words of Moses are riddles,"
- New: "charges that some readers of the Jewish Scriptures boast that the plain words of Moses are riddles,"
- Support: E-en 34974-34982 ("Some persons, desiring to find a solution of the baseness of the Jewish Scriptures ... boast that the plain words of Moses are enigmas").

### Findings

**P1. Timeline, period 3, line 11 (Martyrdom 16): BLOCKING.**

The line tells the wonder in the narrator's voice: "the fire does not consume his body". The rule is that a witness named at the head of a period does not cover a wonder: a line keeps its own note where it differs, and a wonder is named as such (Stage-Two-Kit 2.5). Line 10, the sail, does this. The card's own `uncertain` ("The wonders") counts the unconsumed body as a wonder too.

- Old text: "Since the fire does not consume his body, an executioner pierces him with a dagger."
- New text: "The church says the fire does not consume his body, and an executioner pierces him with a dagger."
- Add the note: "A wonder, in the church's own report."
- Support: ANF01 4158-4160 ("when those wicked men perceived that his body could not be consumed by the fire, they commanded an executioner to go near and pierce him through with a dagger").

**P2. uncertain, new item 13 ("The twelve martyrs"): BLOCKING.**

The item says Eusebius differs from the Martyrdom. That difference comes from the English translation, not from Eusebius. At E-en 23608-23630 McGiffert prints "with the eleven from Philadelphia", and his note says that is a free rendering of "with those from Philadelphia, the twelfth". That is the Martyrdom's own count. He adds that most manuscripts of Eusebius read "twelve".

"Which makes all the others Philadelphians" is an inference drawn from that free rendering. The item is therefore misleading.

- Old: "The twelve martyrs. His church calls him the twelfth martyred in Smyrna, counting those of Philadelphia. Eusebius has him die with eleven from Philadelphia, which makes all the others Philadelphians."
- New: remove the item. Timeline line 13 ("The church counts him the twelfth martyred in Smyrna, those of Philadelphia included", ANF01 4238-4240) already states the record. Card_Edits' Source_Sheet D7 should not carry the difference forward.

**P3. Timeline `reference` on every line: MINOR.**

Each reference gives the work and chapter but no file or lines. Montanism and Alexandria give both, and the Card_Edits table already holds them, so a reader can check a Polycarp line only by opening Card_Edits.

- Fix: append to each reference ", cic/texts/<file> lines <n>", using that row's file and lines in Card_Edits. For example: Martyrdom 9 becomes "Martyrdom of Polycarp 9, cic/texts/anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml lines 3988-3990".
- Line 2 (Ignatius from Smyrna) needs one more reference, for "a prisoner on his way to Rome": "; Eusebius, Church History III.36.4-5, cic/texts/npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml lines 19945-19962" ("under the strictest military surveillance ... when he came to Smyrna").

**P4. `datesNote`: MINOR.**

The proposed `datesNote` sits only in the prose of Card_Edits, not in the layout. It also ends without a full stop.

- New `card.datesNote`: "No ancient source gives a year for his birth or his death. His church's account dates his death to a great Sabbath, the second day of the month Xanthicus, and gives no year. Irenaeus says he was at Rome when Anicetus was bishop there, and Eusebius and Jerome put his death in the reign of Marcus Aurelius. The second century comes from the modern dating of those reigns."
- This is the census sentences word for word, with the fourth sentence dropped and a full stop added.
- Support: ANF01 4284-4290, 39872-39874; E-en 23043; Jerome 39850-39851.
