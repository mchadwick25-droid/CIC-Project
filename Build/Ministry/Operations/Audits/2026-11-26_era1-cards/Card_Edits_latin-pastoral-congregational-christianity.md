## shortDescription

> In Carthage and Hippo, in Roman Africa, Latin-speaking Christians gathered around one bishop. He taught them, cared for their poor, judged their quarrels, and decided with them when to take back those who had fallen away.

Two sentences, 36 words. No negative or contrast, no dash, no "These Christians" opening; it opens on the place. People named as what they were then: Latin-speaking Christians, their bishop, those who had fallen away. It follows the brief's paragraph 2 ("the Latin-speaking Christians of Roman Africa lived as congregations around one bishop, at Carthage under Cyprian and later at Hippo under Augustine. Their bishop taught them, fed their poor, bought back their captives, judged their quarrels and decided with them whom to take back after sin"). "Whom to take back after sin" is put as "when to take back those who had fallen away", so the listener hears who is meant; "fallen" is the brief's own word ("these fallen Christians").

## Shown dates

Census `dates`: `c. 240s-430`. Proposed: **`250 to 430`**, a change, by the date rule. No held line gives the 240s: Cyprian's election rests on modern reckoning (batch-11 `uncertain` item 6), and the census `start` 246 likewise. What the old sources hold: the start at Decius's persecution, which Eusebius tells (`npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` 37412-37415), its year 250 Widely Accepted (Source_Sheet S4), and the brief's "In 250 the emperor Decius began a persecution"; the end at Augustine's death, which Prosper, a witness of the same century, sets on the fifth day before the Kalends of September in the year of the consuls Theodosius XIII and Valentinian III (Prosper 54198, 54204-54208; Mommsen's margin reads 430), with the brief's "From the 250s to 430". The census teaser already says "the 250s to 430 CE". `start` 246 and `end` 430 only place the entry on the map. The layout carries `dates` so the change can be applied as a field.
