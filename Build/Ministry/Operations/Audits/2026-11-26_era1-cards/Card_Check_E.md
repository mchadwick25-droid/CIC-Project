### Findings

**M1. Timeline line 11: MINOR.** "From Asia" reads as the continent. The record means the Roman province, and the brief says so.
- Old text: "Rhodon, a writer from Asia taught by Tatian, writes a book against Marcion's teaching, and says that in his time Marcion's followers are divided into various opinions."
- New text: "Rhodon, a writer from the Roman province of Asia taught by Tatian, writes a book against Marcion's teaching, and says that in his time Marcion's followers are divided into various opinions."
- Support: E-en 28925-28926 ("a native of Asia, who had been instructed ... by Tatian"); the brief, paragraph 3 ("a writer from the Roman province of Asia named Rhodon").

**M2. Timeline line 25: MINOR.** Hippolytus names the man "Bardesanes, an Armenian". Writing "Bardaisan" ties him to the Bardaisan of Mesopotamia in line 17, an identification Hippolytus does not make. The card's own `uncertain` item 13 keeps "Bardesanes", and the Valentinian card keeps Hippolytus's spelling for the same reason.
- Old text: "Hippolytus writes that a follower of Marcion, Prepon, an Assyrian, set out his teaching in a work addressed to Bardaisan."
- New text: "Hippolytus writes that a follower of Marcion, Prepon, an Assyrian, set out his teaching in a work addressed to Bardesanes, an Armenian."
- Support: ANF5 12054-12059 ("a certain follower of Marcion, (namely) Prepon, an Assyrian ... in a work inscribed to Bardesanes, an Armenian").

**M3. Timeline line 34: MINOR.** "In public or in any private house" sits on the taking of the houses. In the edict it belongs to the ban on meeting. The line is not clear read once.
- Old text: "The edict forbids them to meet, takes the houses where they hold their meetings, in public or in any private house, and gives their houses of prayer to the catholic Church."
- New text: "The edict forbids them to meet, in public or in any private house, takes the houses where they hold their meetings, and gives their houses of prayer to the catholic Church."
- Support: E-en 66124-66128 ("deprived of all the houses in which you are accustomed to hold your assemblies ... to forbid the holding of your superstitious and senseless meetings, not in public merely, but in any private house or place whatsoever"), 66141-66149.

**M4. Timeline line 31 `when` and `reference`, by Mark's ruling on dates from the sources' clues: MINOR.** Eusebius's own clues narrow the year. The persecution began in the nineteenth year of Diocletian (E-en 45893). Chapter 8 opens on the sixth year (46751), and chapter 13 says the seventh year was then complete (47406). Chapter 10 lies between them, and Eusebius gives the day as "the third before the Ides of January" (47042-47043). So the pyre was in January of 309 or 310. McGiffert's "Jan. 11, 309" (his note at 47042) is not used.
- Old `when`: "on the eleventh day of the month Audynaeus". New `when`: "on the eleventh day of the month Audynaeus, in January, so 309 or 310".
- In `reference`, old tail: "the persecution begins in the nineteenth year of Diocletian, line 45893". New tail: "the persecution begins in the nineteenth year of Diocletian, line 45893; the day is the third before the Ides of January, lines 47042-47043; the year is worked out from Eusebius's sixth year of the persecution, line 46751, and its seventh year complete, line 47406, counting Diocletian's reign from 284".

### Findings

**V1. `uncertain` item 3: BLOCKING.** "Born on the coast of Egypt and taught at Alexandria" reads as Valentinus teaching there. Epiphanius says only that some said he was schooled in Greek learning at Alexandria. As written it is an unsupported claim, and it echoes the old brief's unheld "a teacher from Alexandria". The card's own line 7 has it right.
- Old: "Where he came from. Epiphanius, late in the 4th century, heard that some said he was born on the coast of Egypt and taught at Alexandria, and he calls the report uncertain. He also says Valentinus left the faith in Cyprus, while Irenaeus says he stayed at Rome until Anicetus."
- New: "Where he came from. Epiphanius, late in the 4th century, heard that some said he was born on the coast of Egypt and schooled in Greek learning at Alexandria, and he calls the report uncertain. ### Findings

**N1. Shown `dates`: BLOCKING.** "216 to c. 1000" shows an end about 1000. No source gives one. The last old witness the card cites says the faith was still held then: "most of the eastern Turks, of the people of China and Thibet and some of the Hindus, adhere still to his law and doctrine" (BIR 29145-29147; line 38). The held Chinese texts also go on past it: Lou Yeou's note, in Chavannes and Pelliot's text XLVII, tells of people in Fujian who follow "la religion de la Lumière" and print its many sacred books (CHI 16006-16010). So the end is an implication the record does not hold, and it breaks the date rule. The start, 216, is Widely Accepted and stands.
- Old: "216 to c. 1000". New: "216 to after 1000".
- Support: BIR 29140-29147 (the remnants and "adhere still"); CHI 16006-16010. "About 1000" for Biruni is Widely Accepted (Source_Sheet, BIR row; Outline, Setting). The map's `end` 650 stays as the writer flagged it.

**N2. Timeline line 10: MINOR.** "Forbid marriage" goes past the line. Biruni says Mani forbade the Elect sexual intercourse.
- Old text: "For the Elect, the saints and ascetics, his laws allow no property but food for one day and clothing for one year, forbid marriage, and send them wandering through the world to preach."
- New text: "For the Elect, the saints and ascetics, his laws allow no property but food for one day and clothing for one year, forbid them sexual intercourse, and send them wandering through the world to preach."
- Support: BIR 29077-29079 ("He forbade them to acquire any property except food for one day and dress for one year; he further forbade sexual intercourse, and ordered them continually to wander about in the world, preaching").

**N3. Timeline line 33: MINOR.** "Exiled to an island, at the pleading of those same Christians" reads as if the Christians asked for the exile. In Augustine they pleaded for him, so he got only the lightest penalty.
- Old text: "Christians bring Faustus and some others before the proconsul. Found to be a Manichaean, Faustus is exiled to an island, at the pleading of those same Christians, and not long after all are released together."
- New text: "Christians bring Faustus and some others before the proconsul. Found to be a Manichaean, Faustus is only exiled to an island, because those same Christians plead for him, and not long after all are released together."
- Support: Migne 16830-16840 ("eis ipsis christianis a quibus perducti sunt intercedentibus, levissima poena ... in insulam relegatus est"; "non multo post inde omnes ... dimissi sunt"); NPNF 1/4 4615-4616 ("on the intercession of the Christians themselves ... only banished to an island").
