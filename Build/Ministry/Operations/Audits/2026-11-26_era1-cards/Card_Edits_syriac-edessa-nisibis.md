## shortDescription

> In Edessa and Nisibis, between the Roman and Persian empires, Christians who spoke Syriac sang their teaching in Ephrem's hymns. Among them were men and women vowed to live unmarried and holy for God.

Two sentences, 35 words. No negative or contrast, no dash, no "These Christians" opening; it opens on the place. People named as what they were then: Christians who spoke Syriac; men and women vowed. It follows the brief's paragraph 2 ("in two frontier cities between the Roman and Persian empires ... In the 300s they sang their teaching in hymns and counted among them men and women vowed to God for life").

## Shown dates

Census `dates`: `200–410 CE`. Proposed: **`c. 201 to early 5th c.`**, a change, by the date rule.

- Start. The flood entry dates itself "In the year 513 ... in the month Tishrin the latter (i. e., November)" (CE 53), which is 201 by the Seleucid era (Source_Sheet S1, Widely Accepted; the brief's "In November 201"). "c." because the translator's notes give 202 and Dionysius 216 (batch-11 `uncertain` item 3). No held line gives 200.
- End. No held old line gives 410. The synod's own acts date it "En la 11° année du règne de Yezdegerd" (Chabot 16155), a regnal year, and its days by the Syrian months (16410-16422). Every "410" in the file is Chabot's (his headings "[A. D. 410.]" at 16077 and "Ann. 410." at 16394, 17856, and his note "6 janvier 410" at 16480), an editor's reckoning. So the end is given as roughly as the held lines allow: under Yazdegerd, early in the fifth century (Socrates, writing a generation later, sets Maruthas's mission there, npnf202 19135-19141, 19174-19176). If the checker rates the synod's year Widely Accepted, as the brief tells it, "c. 201 to 410" also meets the date rule; that is a call for the checker and Mark, and the brief is not changed either way.
- `start` 200 and `end` 410 only place the entry on the map and are not shown. The layout carries `dates` so the change can be applied as a field.
