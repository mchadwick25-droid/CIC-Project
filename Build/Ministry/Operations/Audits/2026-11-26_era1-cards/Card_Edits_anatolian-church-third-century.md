## Shown dates

Census `dates`: `c. 240-312`. Proposed: **`3rd to early 4th c.`**, a change, by the date rule.

- Start. No held line gives a year for Gregory's years with Origen or his becoming bishop. Eusebius sets his study "while Origen was carrying on his customary duties in Cæsarea" (HE 36568), and Origen moved there "in the tenth year of the above-mentioned reign" (HE 36331), a reign the card does not convert. Eusebius has Gregory leading the churches of Pontus in the time of Gallienus (HE 40566-40588). The 3rd century for both is Widely Accepted. "c. 240" rests on no held line.
- End. Jerome gives two reports of Methodius's death: "at the end of the recent persecution or, as others affirm, in the reign of Decius and Valerianus" (VI 41102-41105). The first is the last event the record holds, and the early 4th century for the persecution's end is Widely Accepted; the second is set beside it on the timeline (line 20) and in `uncertain` 6. "312", and the brief's "around 311", rest on no held line. Basil's 4th-century witness (period 5) is what came of them, not the tradition's own span.
- `start` 240 and `end` 312 only place the entry on the map and are not edited (both rest on no held line; flagged for Mark).

## shortDescription

> In Pontus, Gregory, a pupil of Origen, was a bishop and wrote rules for his churches after a raid by the Goths. In Lycia, Methodius, bishop of Olympus, wrote the Banquet, where ten virgins praise chastity.

## Not found, and conflicts with the census entry

The card must not contradict the entry's other fields. Each conflict, and each claim no held line opened supports, is listed for the checker and Mark; none is edited, and the brief is kept as it stands.

- **The brief (`longDescription`):** "went south to study law and came back a bishop" agrees with the held lines (ANF6 2864-2867; HE 36681-36684); "though some scholars doubt that he wrote it" (a modern view; Jerome, the held old witness, says Theodorus delivered it with Origen present, VI 40784-40787); "One story says he found seventeen Christians in the city and left seventeen pagans" (the first half is held, BAS 14657-14660 and PG46 66362-66366; "left seventeen pagans" is not found in the lines opened); "But that story comes from a century after his time" (Basil and Gregory of Nyssa wrote in the 4th century, Widely Accepted; "a century" is the brief's); "When Gothic raiders came through in the 250s" (no held line gives the decade); "one of the first substantial critics of Origen" (a modern judgment; Jerome names two books against Origen, VI 41098-41100); "Methodius died in the persecution around 311" (Jerome gives two reports and no year, VI 41102-41105).
- **`why`:** "In the 250s" and "around 311", as above; "one of the first serious critics of Origen", as above.
- **`teaser`:** "the church of Asia Minor left almost nothing behind" (a judgment no held line makes).
- **`legacy`:** "Gregory's Canonical Epistle entered the standard books of church law in the East, and it is still there" (not held); "an early landmark of Christian writing on virginity" (a judgment); "Jerome knew the Banquet by name" agrees (VI 41098); "When Basil ... cites Gregory of Neocaesarea's practice as precedent" agrees (BAS 14680-14691).
