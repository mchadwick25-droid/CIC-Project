## shortDescription

> In Antioch, Asia Minor and Rome, Christians met on Sunday, the Lord's day, to share bread and wine with thanks to God. Letters and messengers passing between their churches held them together.

Two sentences, 32 words. No negative or contrast, no dash, no "These Christians" opening; it opens on the place, as the approved Montanism and Alexandria texts do. The people are named as what they were then: Christians. It follows the brief's paragraph 2, which makes both claims ("Christians in Antioch, in the cities of Asia Minor and in Rome met together ... gave thanks over bread and a cup on the Lord's day ... Letters and messengers carried between their churches held them together").

For the checker: "Sunday" stands on Justin (Rome) and the Didache; Ignatius gives the Lord's day and the thanksgiving in Asia Minor; Antioch's own practice rests on the brief's paragraph 2 and on Ignatius, its bishop. "Bread and wine" leaves out Justin's "and water" and the Didache's order (cup, then bread), which `uncertain` item 2 carries. "Lord's day" is glossed by "Sunday" so it is clear heard once. "Asia Minor" is the census and brief name. Pliny, the elders' quarrel at Corinth and the Easter quarrel are left to the timeline: no room to explain them.

## Shown dates

Census `dates`: `70–200 CE`. Proposed: **`late 1st to late 2nd c.`**, a change, by the date rule. No held old source gives 70 or 200 (Source_Sheet S1-S12 never reaches either year). What the old sources hold: the start at Rome's letter to Corinth, placed by Irenaeus "In the time of this Clement" (anf01 39833-39836) and by Eusebius in Clement's time (npnf201 16634-16636, 16679-16681), "near the end of the first century", Widely Accepted on the sheet (S2, S3) and told so in the brief; the end at the Easter quarrel, "near the end of the century" in the brief, with Victor bishop of Rome from "the tenth year of the reign of Commodus" (npnf201 31003; sheet S10, Widely Accepted). The form follows Alexandria's proposed "c. 180 to 4th c." Drafts_A.md card 1 proposed "late 1st – 2nd c."; the end is given as "late 2nd c." because the last dated component is the Easter quarrel near the century's end. `start` 70 and `end` 200 only place the entry on the map and are not shown. The layout carries `dates` so the change can be applied as a field; it is the one key outside the five the header names.
