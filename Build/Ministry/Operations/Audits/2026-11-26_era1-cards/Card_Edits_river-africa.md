Dates: "Late in the second century" rests on Julian's becoming bishop in the first year of Commodus and Pantaenus "about that time" (HE V.9-10); 312 is the era's end on the map. The map year 150 for the Alexandrian tradition is a placing year only, and the card says so. Peter's death year, 311, is the census card's (Bell's date, per its `datesNote`); Eusebius gives no year at the cited lines.

## Listed for the fix

1. **Origin record `reason`, "it is first seen clearly late in the second century":** a judgment, not a line. Eusebius names bishops of Alexandria from Annianus, Mark's successor, in the eighth year of Nero (HE II.24, lines 13780-13795), though he gives Mark's own founding as report. The record could say "Eusebius names its bishops from Mark's successor on, and its school from the reign of Commodus". Not changed here.
2. **Africa river name and `common` label** ("Coptic, Ethiopian and other African churches", `cft-website/index.html` line 133070): in era 1 the river carries only Alexandria and Egypt; the label speaks for later eras. Noted, not a fault.

## Re-read for the four faults

Mark's founding is given as report, the school's antiquity as Eusebius's statement, and Peter's death as Eusebius tells it ("as if by command of Maximinus"). No year is moved onto another event: each line carries the record's own reckoning (Commodus's first year, Origen's eighteenth year, Philip's third year) or "date unknown". The persecutions and deaths are kept.
