## uncertain

Mapped across word for word from the batch-11 card (3 items). Checked against the final brief and the census `longDescription`: none contradicts them (the brief's "in what Eusebius calls a school", "As his opponents describe it", "In his own account, the only one left to us"). No word changed.
1. Whether there was one school. Eusebius, writing a century later, calls the instruction a school and names the teacher in charge of it in each generation. Clement's and Origen's own surviving words describe teachers and pupils, never a head's office.
2. What Nepos taught. His book is lost, so his teaching survives only as Eusebius and Dionysius, his opponents, describe it.
3. The three days. The only account is Dionysius's own, of his own success. Eusebius gives no year, and no held source says whether Dionysius was yet bishop.
