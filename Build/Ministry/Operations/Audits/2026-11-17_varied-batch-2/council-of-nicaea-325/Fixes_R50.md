## Main brief (`council-of-nicaea-325.brief.txt`)

**1. Separate the 325 creed from the creed said in churches today.**
- Added, in the Constantinople paragraph: "The longer creed said in churches today is tied to that later council; Nicaea's own was shorter and ended with its curses."
- Sources: Outline, Do not say ("The creed churches say today was written at Nicaea" → "The Nicaea creed is shorter … the creed said in churches today is the longer one tied to the council of Constantinople"); sheet I1 note and M8. NC 2418–2429 (the creed's belief ends "And [we believe] in the Holy Ghost", then the curses); NC 20225–20230 (Chalcedon recites "The Creed of the three hundred and eighteen Fathers at Nice" and, apart from it, "the Creed of the one hundred and fifty holy Fathers who were assembled at Constantinople"). I checked both passages in the file.

**2. Cut or make followable "They read the bishops' words as true of every creature…".**
- Old: "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defense, says Arius's friends were caught whispering and winking. They read the bishops' words as true of every creature, so in his telling a word was needed that could not be read that way."
- New: "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and later wrote in the creed's defense, says the bishops first chose words from Scripture, such as "from God". But Arius's friends read them as true of every creature too, so the bishops wrote again, more plainly."
- Sources: Outline beat 4; sheet M6 and H7. ATH 27875–27882 ("from God" read as true of all creatures), ATH 27887–27889, ATH 28015–28018 ("re-say and re-write … more distinctly still"). The "so" is Athanasius's own ("were forced", "were again compelled"), and the sentence sits inside "he says". "Whispering and winking" was cut for length.

**3. Tell the other business as a scene; put "did not hold" before the creed's later standing.**
- Other business. Old: "The council also settled other business. In Egypt, Melitius had ordained clergy of his own, and he kept his title but lost the power to ordain. Easter was the second trouble Eusebius names: the churches kept it on different days, so that on the same feast some Christians fasted while others feasted. Now Easter would fall on one day everywhere, as kept at Rome and Alexandria. Twenty rules…"
- New: "Then, as their letter to Egypt reports, the bishops turned to other business. Melitius, who had ordained clergy of his own, kept his title but lost the power to ordain. Easter, the second trouble Eusebius names, had some Christians fasting while others feasted; now it would fall on one day everywhere, as at Rome and Alexandria. Twenty rules set the order of church life, down to the last communion, never refused to the dying."
- Sources: Outline Story beats note (the council's letter reports the faith first, then Melitius, then Easter, NC 6338–6406); beats 6–7; sheet M12, M13, K2, M14. The record has no fuller scene of these sessions, so the scene is the council's own letter turning from the creed to Egypt's other trouble.
- Order. The impact now runs: "It did not hold at once" (Arius recalled; Antioch enforcing Easter; decades of division; Jerome on Rimini), then Constantinople 381 and Chalcedon 451, then Carthage. The old "Its creed became the standard for later councils" and the "Yet" that opened the next paragraph are gone.
- Sources: sheet I3, I5, I1, I4.

**4. Shorter, about 450 words (was 549).**
- Now 497 words by `wc` (494 by the profiler). That is about 10% over 450 and about 3 minutes 38 seconds at 137 words a minute. The cuts: Arius's opening sentence merged into the quote's frame; "As Eusebius reports it, the emperor told them" kept, but the summons sentence shortened; Eusebius's "peace as his aim" sentence (Q3, which the outline marks "only if room"); Eustathius's torn-up statement, so the debate now follows two witnesses, not three ("Two men who were there tell the debate differently"); "whispering and winking"; "The rules traveled as written copies"; Chalcedon's quote is kept, but "The council of Chalcedon" became "At Chalcedon".
- I did not cut Melitius, Easter or the canons, since the Council shape needs them so the creed does not stand for the whole council.

**5. Fix "wrote later in its defense" (unclear "its").**

- Source: sheet W3, I2 (Athanasius's book defending the creed's words).

**6. One sentence on the emperor's power over the outcome (exiles by his order).**
- Old: "…and a historian writing a century later says Constantine exiled Arius."
- New: "Historians writing a century later say the emperor ordered exile for anyone who resisted the verdict, and his edict sent Arius into exile."
- Sources: sheet H12, Sozomen: the emperor "ordained that any one who should be rebellious thereto, should forthwith be sent into banishment" (SOC 27958–27960). Sheet M11, Socrates: "an edict of the emperor sent Arius himself into exile" (SOC 2962–2966). I checked both in the file. Following the outline's Do not say, Eusebius of Nicomedia and Theognis are not named as exiled with him.

**9. Put the creed and the added word in Eusebius's voice, and show that the final creed differed in more than one phrase.**
- Old: "Eusebius read his church's creed, and the phrase "of one being" was added."
- New: "Eusebius says he read his church's creed, and the emperor urged that one word be added: "of one being". Then, he says, the bishops drew up a new statement, and he asked what its phrases meant."
- Sources: How_Story_Outline beat 3; sheet M5, H7. ATH 19655–19660 (the emperor advised all to subscribe "with the insertion of the single word, One-in-essence"); ATH 19666–19669 ("they, with a view to the addition of One in essence, drew up the following formula"); ATH 19697–19703 ("we did not let it pass without inquiry in what sense they introduced 'of the essence of the Father,' and 'one in essence with the Father.' Accordingly questions and explanations took place"). I checked them in the file. The story's Do not say allows "the emperor … urged the word 'of one being'".
- Length: 237 → 261 words by `wc`, about 10% over, from this fix.

## Card (`council-of-nicaea-325.entry.json`, the council object)

**10. The condemned "Homoian ('Arian') Christianity" item.**
- Note, old: "The council condemned Arius and the teaching that there was a time when the Son was not."
- Note, new: "The council condemned Arius and teachings it named, such as that there was a time when the Son was not. The Homoian churches came later."
- Sources: the creed's curses (NC 2418–2429) and the synodal letter's sentence on Arius (NC 6341–6347). The census row `homoian-arian-christianity` (CEN, `movements[36]`) dates the tradition "4th-6th c." with start 340, after 325.
- **Census row name.** The row's name, "Homoian ('Arian') Christianity", is right for what the row describes: the Homoian churches of the fourth to sixth centuries (informal name "The Homoian Churches"). It does not need to change. What is anachronistic is the link from this council to it with part "Condemned". The council's own traditions list in the census (`movements[473].council.traditions`) still carries the old note, so it must take the new note to match the card. The census owner should also decide whether "Condemned" is the right part for a tradition that formed after the council, or whether a row for Arius's own party should carry it. I did not edit the census.

  - uncertain[0]: "three eyewitnesses, Eusebius of Caesarea, Eustathius of Antioch and Athanasius of Alexandria" → "… Eustathius of Antioch and, by Socrates's account, Athanasius of Alexandria"
- The traditions note on Athanasius also has a copy in the census council object, which now differs from the card. That needs the same change there. I did not edit the census.
