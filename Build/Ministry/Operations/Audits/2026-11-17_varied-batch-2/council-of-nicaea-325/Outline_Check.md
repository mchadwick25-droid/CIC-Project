## What holds

- **Quotes are verbatim.** The English of Q1 (NP3 6045–6046), Q2 (EUS 64468–64471) and Q3 (ATH 19741–19744) matches the files. The Greek of Q1 (THDG 70615–70616), Q2 (EUSG 11167–11169) and Q3 (SOCG 7326–7328, 7405–7407, across a page break) matches the OCR, and the bracketed corrections are right. ## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: "young" is not in the record.** In *Who tells it*, the aloud line reads "Athanasius, who was there as a young deacon". No held text gives his age. Socrates says only "a deacon of the Alexandrian church" (SOC 2794–2795). This is an added personal detail. **Correction:** "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defence." (The same word sits in sheet W3; the writer sees only the outline, but the sheet should match.)

2. **BLOCKING: "only" overstates how Arius's letter survives.** *Who tells it* says the letter "survives only because a hostile historian, Theodoret, copied it". The held record shows only that Theodoret kept it (NP3 6002–6006). Nothing supports "only", and it is not true: the letter also survives in Epiphanius, though that text is not held here. **Correction:** "Arius's letter dates from before the council; it is kept in the history of Theodoret, who was against him."

3. **MINOR: "a few years" puts a measure on time that the record does not give.** The setting line reads "only a few years after the persecutions had ended". The note on that line admits it rests only on "forever removed" (EUS 64465–64467), which gives no span of time. **Correction:** "…met at the call of the emperor Constantine, soon after, in his words, the tyrants' hostility had been 'forever removed'." Or use the sheet's own phrase: "the churches of the empire, newly free of persecution" (S5).

4. **MINOR: Beat 5 overstates Athanasius.** The beat says Arius's friends "accepted every phrase from Scripture by reading it as true of all creatures". Athanasius names "from God" (ATH 27875–27882), and then "like", "always", "power" and "in Him" (ATH 27989–27995). Not all of these are phrases from Scripture, and he does not say "every". **Correction:** "Athanasius says Arius's friends read the bishops' words, like 'from God' and 'like the Father', as true of all creatures too, and were caught 'whispering … and winking', so the bishops chose plainer words."

5. **MINOR: one Do not say line misdescribes the creed's end.** It says the Nicaea creed "ends 'and in the Holy Spirit'". The text reads "And [we believe] in the Holy Ghost." (NC 2418), and the curses follow it (NC 2418–2429). **Correction:** "The Nicaea creed is shorter: its statement of belief stops at 'and in the Holy Spirit', and its curses follow…" (the rest of the line stands).

6. **MINOR: Do not say gap on "the 318 fathers".** The Thread and *What came of it* §1 use the later councils' name "the 318 fathers". A writer could easily turn that phrase into a head-count. **Add:** "'The 318 fathers came to Nicaea.' → When quoting Constantinople or Chalcedon, say '318 fathers' is the name later councils used for Nicaea, not a count."

7. **MINOR: Do not say gap on Constantine and the state religion.** The setting invites a common error. **Add:** "'Constantine made Christianity the empire's religion at Nicaea' / 'Constantine was baptized there.' → Nothing in the record. He called the council, sat in it and spoke at it."

8. **MINOR: Do not say gap on who presided.** The existing line covers Constantine and the pope. Ossius and Eustathius are named in Links and are the usual guesses. **Add:** "'Ossius presided' / 'Eustathius presided.' → Eusebius speaks only of 'those who presided' and names no one (EUS 64501)."

9. **MINOR: Do not say gap on when Eusebius of Nicomedia was exiled.** The line "Eusebius of Nicomedia … was exiled", together with Beat 7, could become "exiled with Arius at the council". Only Socrates says that. His editor says Socrates gives the wrong reason for that banishment and dates the recall to 328 (SOC 3932–3936; sheet D4). **Add:** "Do not say Eusebius of Nicomedia was exiled with Arius at the council; say he was exiled and later restored."

10. **MINOR: the Easter line widens "who formerly followed the custom of the Jews".** The Do not say line reads "so the churches of the East would stop following the Jewish reckoning". The letter says "all our brethren in the East who formerly followed the custom of the Jews" (NC 6402–6404), so only those who did. **Correction:** "…so that those in the East who had followed the Jewish custom would keep it on the same day as Rome and Alexandria."

11. **MINOR: "half a century" rests on a modern editor.** The Thread's "for half a century" and the Do not say "began half a century of conflict" echo Robertson (ATH 1844–1846). The sheet marks that source card only. The held after-history runs to Rimini in 359 (34 years on) and Constantinople in 381. **Correction:** use the sheet's own wording, "for decades" (I3), or name the span by its two events.

12. **MINOR: a qualifier on Athanasius is dropped in *Who tells it*.** That line says flatly that "Athanasius and Eustathius were there". The Links line says rightly that his presence rests on "a later historian's account" (C6). **Correction:** "Eustathius was there; Athanasius was there as a deacon, a later historian says; both were Arius's foes."

13. **MINOR: citations to add (the claims are supported).** Beat 2's "When that failed" is supported by EUS 64270–64272 ("his letter to the Alexandrian Christians had failed to produce its due effect"). Beat 3's "Thrace and Achaia" is supported by EUS 64331–64332. Neither line appears in sheet C2 or D-1. **Add both line references.** For Persia, the year 410 comes from the work Braun cites, Lamy's "anno 410" (MAR 894). "By Braun's account" would read more exactly as "dated 410 in the work Braun cites". The phrase "swore to accept" is a fair reading of "unter einem Anathem … annehmen zu wollen erklären" (MAR 865–866).

No problems found with Leave out: each item and its reason fits the record.
