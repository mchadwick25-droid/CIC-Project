# One-hearing test: the Council of Nicaea (325)

Test (Mark): "An interested non-specialist can understand who this was, what happened, and why the connection matters after one hearing." Not simpler.

Brief tested: `council-of-nicaea-325.brief.txt` (443 words). Read once, straight through, before the outline.

## Step 1. Three lines from one hearing

- **Who was this?** A meeting of bishops from across the Roman Empire, called by the emperor Constantine to Nicaea, in today's Turkey, in 325, soon after the persecutions ended, to settle a split that began in Alexandria.
- **What happened?** Arius, a priest, taught that the Son had a beginning and God did not. The bishops argued, and the witnesses tell it differently. They wrote a creed that called the Son "of one being" with the Father, "begotten, not made", and cut off anyone who said "there was a time when he was not". Nearly all signed. Arius was condemned and exiled. Something about Arius's friends "whispering and winking" made the bishops choose "plainer words", but I could not tell what they were winking about.
- **Why does the connection matter?** For decades it did not settle things: Arius came back, and by 359 "the whole world" seemed Arian. But Athanasius defended it, and later councils (Constantinople 381, Chalcedon 451) made its faith the standard that "shall not be set aside". Its church rules travelled as well: Carthage read its copy aloud and asked the East for exact copies.

## Verdict

**FAIL.** Who and Why are CLEAR. What is WEAK at paragraph 5.

## Fix (smallest, from the outline only)

Paragraph 5, replace:

> He says Arius's friends were caught "whispering ... and winking", so the bishops chose plainer words.

with:

> He says Arius's friends took words like "from God" as true of all creatures, and were caught "whispering ... and winking", so the bishops chose plainer words.

Source: outline story beat 5 (M6). Adds 11 words; the brief goes from 443 to 454, still about 3 minutes. It does not simplify: it supplies the reason the debate turned, which makes the word "of one being" mean something to the listener.
