## Findings

### BLOCKING

**B-1. Sentence 1 says the eyewitnesses are our only source for how the council decided, which is wrong.**
"…so we know how it decided only from men who were there."
- Later historians also describe how the council decided: Sozomen on the early sessions, the vote avoided and the seventeen (H2, H9), Socrates on the celibacy debate (H13), and Theodoret on the signing (H10).
- The card beside this story says so plainly: "how it worked comes from a few men who were there and from historians writing a century later."
- The outline chose to tell the story from eyewitnesses. It never claims that they are the only record. The story turns that choice into a false claim about the sources, and it contradicts the card the listener sees next to it.

**Fix:** "No minutes survive from the council of Nicaea, so we hear how it decided from men who were there."

### MINOR

**M-1. "On Easter they wrote" can be heard as "on Easter Day".**
The story never says that Easter was on the council's agenda.
**Fix:** "For the date of Easter they wrote 'It seemed good', but…"

**M-2. "one word, 'of one being', was added" calls three English words one word.**
**Fix:** "Eusebius read his church's creed, and one word was added: homoousios, 'of one being'." This also matches the main brief.

**M-3. "Eusebius says each member signed what they agreed, although a few would not accept all of it."**
- It can be heard as Eusebius saying both halves. He says all signed (D3). The few who held back come from the synodal letter and their own later letter (H11, M10).
- "Signed what they agreed" is also missing "on".

**Fix:** "Eusebius says each member signed what they had agreed on, although other records show a few would not accept all of it."

**M-4. "wrote later in its defence": "its" has no clear antecedent here.**
In the main brief, "its" follows "the council". Here it follows "his church's creed" and "one word".
**Fix:** "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and wrote later in the council's defence, says…"

**M-5. "plain words from Scripture could be evaded" leaves out who evaded them.**
This is the one point where a listener loses the thread of how the council decided.
**Fix (still in Athanasius's frame, so his hostile reading stays his):** "…says the bishops first chose plain words from Scripture, but their opponents slipped around them, so the bishops wrote again, more distinctly still."
If this pushes the sentence past 30 words, end the Athanasius introduction at "deacon" and move "and wrote later in the council's defence" to his second appearance: "Athanasius, writing later in its defence, argued…"

**M-6. "the creed was no new rule" says what the creed was not, but not what it was.**
"No new rule" can also be heard as "a rule, but not a new one". The outline's Connection gives both halves: "the apostles' faith, not a new rule" (H8: "not novel, but Apostolical").
**Fix:** "Athanasius later argued that the creed was the apostles' faith, not a new rule, pointing to its wording."

**M-7. Length.**
The story runs to 228 words, against the outline's 150–220. The fixes above add a few more. If it needs trimming, cut "but went on listening" to "but listened". Nothing of substance needs to go.
