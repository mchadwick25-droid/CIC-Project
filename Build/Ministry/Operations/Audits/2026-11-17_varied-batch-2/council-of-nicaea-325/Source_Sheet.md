**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own lines. English quotes are exactly as in the file, square brackets included (they are the translator's or editor's). The NPNF XML files print editors' footnotes inline in the text stream; I have left the notes out of quotes and give them separately where they matter, always marked "editor's note". In the OCR Greek files (EUSG, THDG, SOCG) I left out footnote markers, stray marks and page heads, and give OCR faults with the correct reading in [brackets]. Nothing else is changed.

**Time gaps.** "About N years later" figures are my rough estimates for the card, from the usual modern dates of the writers. Only the council's year (325) and the dates of later councils come from the files. They are not story material.

**Witness kinds used.** OWN = documents the council itself issued (creed, canons, synodal letter) and the emperor's own letter on its decisions. CONTEMP = a person who was there, writing in his own lifetime: Eusebius of Caesarea, Eustathius of Antioch, Athanasius; and Arius, writing before the council. LATER = a writer who was not there, with the rough gap. LATE-LEGEND = none used.

**Greek held.** Greek is held for Arius's letter (THDG), Eusebius's letter (SOCG) and Constantine's speech in the *Life* (EUSG); the three quotes in the outline are rendered from it. For the creed, NC prints only the key Greek words (ὁμοούσιον, ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν). Greek of Athanasius's *De decretis* exists in `athanasius_opera-omnia-pg25-grc-lat_migne1857.txt`, but I did not find the passage in the OCR and did not use it; Athanasius is paraphrased from the English only.

## 1. Setting

S1. **Place.** "The place, too, selected for the synod, the city Nicæa in Bithynia (named from “Victory”), was appropriate to the occasion." (EUS 64288–64290). The canons' heading: "The Canons of the 318 Holy Fathers Assembled in the City of Nice, in Bithynia." (NC 2810–2814; this heading is the later manuscript title, not a count, see D1.) CONTEMP. Documented. Modern country: the census places Nicaea in Turkey (CEN 95165–95171). Theodoret's editor gives the modern name "Isnik" (editor's note, NP3 6236–6237). Plain spoken form: "Nicaea, a city in what is now north-western Turkey" is NOT supported ("north-western" is in no held file); say "the city of Nicaea, in today's Turkey".

S2. **Year.** Socrates found the date in the council's record: "in the consulate of Paulinus and Julian, on the 20th day of May, and in the 636th year from the reign of Alexander the Macedonian." (SOC 3911–3914); the editor's note converts it: "May 20, 325 a.d." (SOC 3915). Eusebius ties it to Constantine's twentieth year: "About this time he completed the twentieth year of his reign." (EUS 64541). LATER (Socrates, about 115 years) and CONTEMP. Year 325: Widely Accepted. The exact days are Contested: Socrates gives 20 May; another editor's note says "it seems that it ended August 25, having probably begun June 14" (EUS 64400–64402); Hefele tries to reconcile them (HEF 17976–17989). Say only "in 325".

S3. **The danger just past.** Constantine's opening speech, as Eusebius gives it, speaks of "the impious hostility of the tyrants" as "forever removed by the power of God our Saviour" (EUS 64465–64467). Eusebius says some bishops were known for "patient fortitude of character" (EUS 64377–64378). Theodoret, much later, says many "bore in their bodies the marks of the Lord Jesus Christ"; Paul of Neocaesarea had lost the use of both hands "by the application of a red-hot iron"; "Some had had the right eye dug out, others had lost the right arm"; "the Council looked like an assembled army of martyrs" (NP3 6249–6271). The editor's note: "The “stigmata” here meant are the marks of persecution." (NP3 6251–6252). Theodoret is LATER (about 125 years). Scars: Documented as Theodoret's account; no contemporary held text describes wounds.

S4. **Who called it, and how.** Constantine "convoked a general council, and invited the speedy attendance of bishops from all quarters", and "allowed some the use of the public means of conveyance, while he afforded to others an ample supply of horses" (EUS 64282–64288). The synodal letter: the synod "was assembled at Niece through the grace of Christ and our most religious Sovereign Constantine, who brought us together from our several provinces and cities" (NC 6330–6333). CONTEMP; OWN. Documented.

S5. **Tradition (spoken name).** A council is not a tradition. The census: "A meeting of church leaders from more than one tradition, called to settle a question together. It is not a church of its own." (CEN 95187). The tradition that called it is "Imperial and Juridical Christianity", informally "Church and Empire" (CEN 35383, 35385), "the tradition that convened Nicaea and eventually made its creed imperial law" (CEN 35424); its era is one in which "bishops went from outlaws to office-holders beside a throne" (CEN 35426). The census lane for the council is "Greek East & Orthodoxy" (CEN 95179–95180). (a) Plain spoken forms: (1) "bishops from across the Roman Empire, called together by the emperor Constantine" (support: EUS 64282–64284; NC 6331–6333); (2) "the churches of the empire, newly free of persecution" (support: S3, EUS 64465–64467); (3) for the hosts, "the church of Constantine's empire". (b) Do not say "Imperial and Juridical Christianity" aloud. Do not say "the Catholic Church" or "the Orthodox Church" in the modern denominational sense: the creed's "Catholic and Apostolic Church" (NC 2429) is the council's own phrase for the whole church. Do not say "the whole church" came: the record names who came, and most came from the East (S7, S8).

## 2. The dispute

D-1. **How it began, in the emperor's words (before the council).** Constantine to Alexander and Arius: "When you, Alexander, demanded of the presbyters what opinion they severally maintained respecting a certain passage in the Divine law, ... or rather, I should say, that you asked them something connected with an unprofitable question, then you, Arius, inconsiderately insisted on what ought never to have been conceived at all, or if conceived, should have been buried in profound silence. Hence it was that a dissension arose between you, fellowship was withdrawn, and the holy people, rent into diverse parties, no longer preserved the unity of the one body." (EUS 63886–63902; editor's notes at 63888–63891, 63893–63897 and 63900–63901 left out; the "..." marks the omitted bracketed note). OWN (the emperor's letter, kept by Eusebius). Documented. Use: Alexander was bishop; Arius was one of his presbyters (priests). The emperor at first thought the question needless (D-2 below).
D-1a. **The letter failed.** Constantine "perceived that his letter to the Alexandrian Christians had failed to produce its due effect" and then acted (EUS 64267–64272). CONTEMP. Documented.
D-2. **Constantine's first view.** "It was wrong in the first instance to propose such questions as these, or to reply to them when propounded." (EUS 63904–63906). OWN. Documented. Note: an editor in ATH says Constantine told them "that they were disputing about a question of words" (editor's note, ATH 19891–19895): a paraphrase, not his words.
D-3. **Arius's side, in his own letter (written before the council).** "He has driven us out of the city as atheists, because we do not concur in what he publicly preaches, namely, God always, the Son always" (NP3 6025–6027). "We are persecuted, because we say that the Son has a beginning, but that God is without beginning. This is the cause of our persecution, and likewise, because we say that He is of the non-existent." (NP3 6045–6048). He also writes that the Son "has subsisted before time, and before ages, as perfect God, only begotten and unchangeable, and that before He was begotten, or created, or purposed, or established, He was not." (NP3 6042–6044). Addressed "To his very dear lord, the man of God, the faithful and orthodox Eusebius, Arius, unjustly persecuted by Alexander the Pope" (NP3 6005–6006), i.e. Eusebius bishop of Nicomedia (chapter title, NP3 6002–6003). "Pope" here was a title of the bishop of Alexandria (editor's note, NP3 6006–6011; card only). Greek (THDG 70615–70616): "Διωχόμεθα [Διωκόμεθα] ὅτι εἴπαμεν ᾿Αρχὴν ἔχει ὁ Υἱὸς, ὁ δὲ Θεὸς ἄναρχός ἐστι." Note the Greek "εἴπαμεν" is past ("we said"); the English has "we say". CONTEMP (OWN voice of the condemned side), kept by a hostile later writer (Theodoret). Documented.
D-4. **What the council said Arius taught.** "saying that he is from things that are not, and that before he was begotten he was not, and that there was a time when he was not, and that the Son of God is by his free will capable of vice and virtue; saying also that he is a creature." (NC 6343–6347). OWN. Documented as the council's summary of him. Compare D-3: Arius's own letter calls the Son "perfect God" and "unchangeable"; the council's letter says he taught the Son is "capable of vice and virtue". The two sides describe his teaching differently; the story should give his own words and the council's charge as two voices, not merge them.

## 3. Who came

C1. **How many: the witnesses disagree.** Eusebius (there): "the number of bishops exceeded two hundred and fifty" (EUS 64360–64361). Eustathius (there): "about two hundred and seventy bishops were convened. There were, however, so many assembled that I cannot state their exact number" (NP3 6387–6389). Athanasius (there): "the assembled Bishops who were three hundred more or less" (ATH 26762–26763). Socrates (later): "the number of bishops exceeded three hundred" (SOC 2742) and then "three hundred and eighteen" (SOC 2948–2949). Theodoret (later): "Three hundred and eighteen bishops were assembled." (NP3 6244). Latin list: "Et subscripseroiit [subscripserunt] CCCXVm [CCCXVIII], qui in eodem concilio conuenerunt." (GEL 4790–4792). Robertson, a modern editor: Athanasius "toward the end of his life ... acquiesces in the precise figure 318 (Gen. xiv. 14 ...) which a later generation adopted" (ATH 1849–1856; card only). Contested as to number; "more than 250" is the floor all eyewitnesses support.
C2. **From where.** "Syrians and Cilicians, Phœnicians and Arabians, delegates from Palestine, and others from Egypt; Thebans and Libyans, with those who came from the region of Mesopotamia. A Persian bishop too was present at this conference, nor was even a Scythian found wanting to the number." (EUS 64321–64325). "those who dwelt in the remotest districts of Thrace and Macedonia, of Achaia and Epirus, were notwithstanding in attendance" (EUS 64330–64332). "Even from Spain itself, one whose fame was widely spread took his seat" (EUS 64332–64333; the bracket "[Hosius of Cordova.—Bag.]" is the translator's). CONTEMP. Documented.
C3. **Rome.** "The prelate of the imperial city ... was prevented from attending by extreme old age; but his presbyters were present, and supplied his place." (EUS 64333–64337; the bracketed editor's note on whether Rome or Constantinople is meant is left out). Theodoret names him: "The bishop of Rome [editor: Sylvester], on account of his very advanced age, was absent, but he sent two presbyters" (NP3 6245–6246). Latin list: "Uictor et Uincentius presbyteri urbis Bomae [Romae] subscripserunt." (GEL 4796–4798). CONTEMP; LATER. Documented that Rome's bishop did not come. Names Contested (Theodoret's editor "Vitus and Vincentius", NP3 6246; the list "Victor"). Use "two priests from Rome".
C4. **The first signature in the Latin lists.** "Osius episcopus ciuitatis Corduben- sis prouinciae Spaniae dixit: ita credo sicut superius scrip- tum est." (GEL 4792–4796). Rendered from the Latin: "Ossius, bishop of the city of Córdoba in the province of Spain, said: I believe so, as it is written above." LATER copy of uncertain transmission; Eusebius's editor warns: "The extant signatures are of doubtful authenticity." (editor's note, EUS 64527–64529). Card only.
C5. **Carthage.** The African council of 419 speaks of "The copies of the Creed, and the statutes of the Nicene Synod which formerly were brought to our council through Cæcilean of blessed memory, the predecessor of your holiness (who was present at it)" (NC 32523–32525). LATER (about 95 years). Documented.
C6. **Athanasius.** Socrates (later): Arius's party "were powerfully opposed by Athanasius, a deacon of the Alexandrian church, who was highly esteemed by Alexander his bishop" (SOC 2794–2796). Hefele, modern: "S. Athanasius, who was an eye-witness, and a member of the Council" (HEF 17739–17740). LATER. Widely Accepted that he was present as a deacon. That he "led" the case is not in the record.
C7. **Arius's friends among the bishops.** Theodoret: "Menophantus, bishop of Ephesus, Patrophilus, bishop of Scythopolis, Theognis, bishop of Nicæa, and Narcissus, bishop of Neronias ... also Theonas, bishop of Marmarica, and Secundus, bishop of Ptolemais in Egypt" (NP3 6316–6321). Socrates: "Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia ... supported the opinion of Arius, together with Theognis and Maris" (SOC 2791–2794). LATER. Documented as their lists; the lists differ.

## 4. Recorded moments

M1. **The emperor enters.** "each member was present for this in the central building of the palace, which appeared to exceed the rest in magnitude. On each side of the interior of this were many seats disposed in order" (EUS 64397–64404, editor's notes left out). He came "clothed in raiment which glittered as it were with rays of light, reflecting the glowing radiance of a purple robe, and adorned with the brilliant splendor of gold and precious stones." (EUS 64413–64415). "when a low chair of wrought gold had been set for him, he waited until the bishops had beckoned to him, and then sat down, and after him the whole assembly did the same." (EUS 64426–64429). CONTEMP, by an admirer (see W2). Documented as Eusebius's account; the praise is his.
M2. **"Civil strife."** Constantine's speech: "for, in my judgment, intestine strife within the Church of God, is far more evil and dangerous than any kind of war or conflict; and these our differences appear to me more grievous than any outward trouble." (EUS 64468–64471). Greek (EUSG 11167–11169): "ὡς ἔμοιγε παντὸς πολέμου xcl [καὶ] μάχης δεινῆς καὶ χαλεπωτέρα ἡ τῆς ἐχκχλησίας [ἐκκλησίας] τοῦ ϑεοῦ ἐμφύλιος νενόμισται στάσις καὶ μᾶλλον ταῦτα τῶν ἔξωϑεν λυπηρὰ καταφαίνεται." "ἐμφύλιος στάσις" is the ordinary Greek for civil strife among one's own people. CONTEMP (Eusebius's report of the speech). Documented as reported.
M3. **Spoken in Latin.** "As soon as the emperor had spoken these words in the Latin tongue, which another interpreted, he gave permission to those who presided in the council to deliver their opinions." (EUS 64499–64502). Then "some began to accuse their neighbors, who defended themselves, and recriminated in their turn" (EUS 64502–64504); the emperor used "the Greek language, with which he was not altogether unacquainted" (EUS 64510–64511). CONTEMP. Documented. The record does not name "those who presided".
M4. **The burned petitions (later witness only).** Socrates: "many had even presented petitions to the emperor the day before. But he ... ordered these petitions to be burnt" (SOC 2829–2832). LATER (about 115 years); not in Eusebius. Widely Accepted as a story, Inferential-Thin as fact. Optional.
M5. **Eusebius's creed and the added word.** Eusebius of Caesarea read his own church's creed; "our most pious Emperor, before any one else, testified that it comprised most orthodox statements. He confessed moreover that such were his own sentiments, and he advised all present to agree to it, and to subscribe its articles and to assent to them, with the insertion of the single word, One-in-essence" (ATH 19655–19660). "but they, with a view to the addition of One in essence, drew up the following formula" (ATH 19666–19669, translator's bracket left out). CONTEMP. Documented as Eusebius's account; Contested as the full story (see D2).
M6. **Athanasius's account: the bishops pressed for clearer words.** The council wished "to use instead the acknowledged words of the Scriptures, that the Son is not from nothing but ‘from God,’" but "Eusebius and his fellows" read "from God" as true of all creatures too (ATH 27875–27882); so the Fathers "were forced to express more distinctly the sense of the words ‘from God.’" (ATH 27887–27889). When the bishops proposed "like", "always", "power", "in Him", "Eusebius and his fellows ... were caught whispering to each other and winking with their eyes, that ‘like,’ and ‘always,’ and ‘power,’ and ‘in Him,’ were, as before, common to us and the Son" (ATH 27989–27995). So the bishops "were again compelled ... to re-say and re-write what they had said before, more distinctly still, namely, that the Son is ‘one in essence’ with the Father" (ATH 28015–28018, 28037–28038; editor's note between). CONTEMP but written some decades later, by an opponent (W3). The held passage does not say which Eusebius leads "Eusebius and his fellows" (ATH 26749, 27880). An editor's note nearby cites Ambrose on a letter of Eusebius of Nicomedia (ATH 28019–28022), and the usual reading is Eusebius of Nicomedia's party, not Eusebius of Caesarea: Widely Accepted, not stated in the text. Documented as Athanasius's account.
M7. **Eustathius's account: a document torn up.** "When they began to inquire into the nature of the faith, the formulary of Eusebius was brought forward, which contained undisguised evidence of his blasphemy. ... After the Eusebian gang had been clearly convicted, and the impious writing had been torn up in the sight of all, some amongst them by concert, under the pretence of preserving peace, imposed silence on all the ablest speakers." (NP3 6390–6398). The Arians then "sprang forward to anathematize and condemn the doctrines condemned, and unanimously signed the confession of faith" (NP3 6398–6401). CONTEMP, hostile. Documented as Eustathius's account. Which Eusebius is not said in the passage. Theodoret's own narrative gives the torn document as one drawn up by Arius's supporters: "They drew up a formulary of their faith, and presented it to the council. As soon as it was read it was torn to pieces" (NP3 6348–6350). Contested which document and whose.
M8. **The creed.** "We believe in one God, the Father Almighty, maker of all things visible and invisible; and in one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the only-begotten of his Father, of the substance of the Father, God of God, Light of Light, very God of very God, begotten (γεννηθέντα), not made, being of one substance (ὁμοούσιον, consubstantialem) with the Father." (NC 2408–2413). Its closing curse: "And whosoever shall say that there was a time when the Son of God was not (ἤν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν), or that before he was begotten he was not, or that he was made of things that were not, or that he is of a different substance or essence [from the Father] or that he is a creature, or subject to change or conversion—all that so say, the Catholic and Apostolic Church anathematizes them." (NC 2418–2429; the editor's note on τρεπτὸν at 2425–2428 left out). Plain sense of "anathematizes": declares them cut off, under the church's curse. OWN. Documented. Eusebius's version uses "One in essence" (ATH 19679). The creed ends "And [we believe] in the Holy Ghost." with nothing more (NC 2418).
M9. **Eusebius signs: "peace being the object."** "On this account we assented to the sense ourselves, without declining even the term “One in essence,” peace being the object which we set before us, and stedfastness in the orthodox view." (ATH 19741–19744). Greek (SOCG 7326–7328, 7405–7407): "Διόπερ τῇ διανοίᾳ kai [καὶ] αὐτοὶ συντιθέμεθα, οὐδὲ τὴν φωνὴν τοῦ Ouoovciov [ὁμοουσίου] παραιτούμενοι, ToU [τοῦ] τῆς εἰρήνης σκοποῦ πρὸ ὀφθαλμῶν ἡμῖν κειμένου, kat [καὶ] TOU [τοῦ] μὴ τῆς ὀρθῆς ἐκπεσεῖν διανοίας." He explains he accepted it as "not in the way of bodies, nor like mortal beings" (ATH 19818–19819), and notes "even among the ancients, some learned and illustrious Bishops and writers have used the term “one in essence”" (ATH 19858–19864). Socrates (later) says Eusebius "who had held aloof for a short time, after mature consideration ... at length acquiesced in it" (SOC 2972–2975). CONTEMP (OWN voice). Documented.
M10. **The vote and the refusers: accounts differ.** Synodal letter: "it was unanimously decreed that he and his impious opinion should be anathematized" (NC 6341–6342); yet "he has even destroyed Theonas of Marmorica and Secundes of Ptolemais; for they also have received the same sentence as the rest." (NC 6352–6355). Eusebius: the decisions "received the signature of each several member" (EUS 64526–64527). Socrates: "Five only would not receive it, objecting to the term homoousios ... Eusebius bishop of Nicomedia, Theognis of Nice, Maris of Chalcedon, Theonas of Marmarica, and Secundus of Ptolemaïs." (SOC 2950–2954). Theodoret: "they all, with the exception of Secundus and Theonas, stood up and took the lead in publicly renouncing Arius" (NP3 6352–6354). OWN; CONTEMP; LATER. Documented that two bishops were condemned with Arius; Contested how many refused.
M11. **Arius sent away.** Socrates: "the Synod anathematized Arius, and all who adhered to his opinions, prohibiting him at the same time from entering into Alexandria. At the same time an edict of the emperor sent Arius himself into exile, together with Eusebius and Theognis and their followers" (SOC 2962–2966). The synodal letter does not name the penalty: "a man who has in fact received a fitting recompense for his own sin" (NC 6351–6352). LATER for the exile. Widely Accepted that Arius was exiled. Contested whether Eusebius of Nicomedia and Theognis were exiled then or later: the SOC editor's note says their recall "occurred in 328 a.d." and that "Socrates does not give the correct reason for the banishment of Eusebius and Theognis" (SOC 3932–3936).
M12. **Melitius.** The council, "being disposed to deal gently with Meletius (for in strict justice he deserved no leniency), decreed that he should remain in his own city, but have no authority either to ordain, or to administer affairs, or to make appointments; ... but should enjoy the bare title of his rank" (NC 6362–6367). His clergy were received under Alexander's bishops (NC 6367–6377). OWN. Documented. The record here does not explain who Melitius was; the census edge calls it "The Melitian Schism" (entry file only; not used).
M13. **Easter.** Synodal letter: "all our brethren in the East who formerly followed the custom of the Jews are henceforth to celebrate the said most sacred feast of Easter at the same time with the Romans and yourselves and all those who have observed Easter from the beginning." (NC 6402–6406). Constantine's letter: "it has been decided that the most holy festival of Easter should be everywhere celebrated on one and the same day" (NC 6481–6483). The same letter is openly hostile to the Jews: "it is our duty not to have anything in common with the murderers of our Lord" (NC 6469–6470); also NC 6432–6434, 6442–6447. OWN. Documented. No held text of the council gives a rule for computing the date (no equinox, no full moon).
M14. **Some canons.** Canon 6: "Let the ancient customs in Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis prevail, that the Bishop of Alexandria have jurisdiction in all these, since the like is customary for the Bishop of Rome also. Likewise in Antioch and the other provinces, let the Churches retain their privileges." (NC 3405–3409). Canon 8, on "those who call themselves Cathari": their clergy may stay clergy if they "profess in writing that they will observe and follow the dogmas of the Catholic and Apostolic Church" (NC 3769–3774). Canon 20: "it seems good to the holy Synod that prayer be made to God standing" on the Lord's Day and at Pentecost (NC 5499–5503). OWN. Documented. Card or one clause only.
M15. **The banquet.** The council ended near Constantine's twentieth year: "Not one of the bishops was wanting at the imperial banquet"; "Detachments of the body-guard and other troops surrounded the entrance of the palace with drawn swords, and through the midst of these the men of God proceeded without fear into the innermost of the imperial apartments"; "One might have thought that a picture of Christ’s kingdom was thus shadowed forth, and a dream rather than reality." (EUS 64545–64557, editor's notes left out). CONTEMP, admiring. Documented as Eusebius's account. A strong image of the change from persecution to palace; use only in Eusebius's voice.

## 5. What came of it (heaviest first)

I1. **The creed became the fixed reference of later councils.** [DEFENDED by Athanasius, I2.] Constantinople, 381 (the date is from the editor and Hefele, NC 13960–13964): "The Faith of the Three Hundred and Eighteen Fathers assembled at Nice in Bithynia shall not be set aside, but shall remain firm." (NC 13970–13972). Chalcedon, 451: "we do declare that the exposition of the right and blameless faith made by the Three Hundred and Eighteen holy and blessed Fathers, assembled at Nice in the reign of Constantine of pious memory, shall be pre-eminent" (NC 20217–20220); it then recites "The Creed of the three hundred and eighteen Fathers at Nice" and, separately, "the Creed of the one hundred and fifty holy Fathers who were assembled at Constantinople" (NC 20225–20230). LATER (56 and 126 years). Documented. Note: Chalcedon keeps two creeds apart; the longer one later recited in churches is the one tied to Constantinople (MAR's content note names it "Das Nicäno-Konstantinopolitanische Symbolum", MAR 9). Do not tell the Nicaea creed as the creed churches say today.
I2. **It was defended, in writing, by one who was there.** Athanasius wrote his defence of the council's phrases "from the essence" and "one in essence" (ATH 27759 chapter title, editor's; text at ATH 27874–28038). He gives the council's reason: "This then was the reason why the Council wrote ‘of the essence.’" (ATH 27981–27982). CONTEMP (decades later). Documented.
I3. **It did not hold at once; it divided the church for decades.** Eusebius of Nicomedia and Theognis "were by an imperial edict recalled from exile and restored to their own churches" (SOC 3939–3942). Their letter says "we subscribed the declaration of faith; we did not subscribe the anathematizing" (SOC 3956–3957). Socrates: "It appears also that Arius was recalled before them" (SOC 3978–3979). At Ariminum (Rimini) in 359 (date: editor, JER 32284), Jerome writes: "the Nicene Faith stood condemned by acclamation. The whole world groaned, and was astonished to find itself Arian." (JER 32412–32413). Robertson, a modern editor, sums it: the reaction "for well nigh half a century after, placed its results in jeopardy" (ATH 1844–1846; card only). LATER (Socrates about 115 years; Jerome about 55 years). Documented. Who it divided: those who held to "one in essence" and those who would not; Arius's friends among the bishops (C7); Athanasius later exiled (SOC 5341–5346, chapter title, editor's; card only).
I4. **Its rules were kept and checked as church law, far from Nicaea.** Carthage, 419 (NC 32157): the copies brought by Caecilian were read aloud: "Daniel the notary read the profession of faith of the Council of Nice and its statutes to the African Council." (NC 32538–32540); "The statutes also of the Nicene Council in twenty heads were likewise read" (NC 32544–32545); the bishops resolved to write to Antioch, Alexandria and Constantinople for "most accurate copies of the decrees of the Council of Nice" (NC 32529–32533), because a disputed rule on appeals was not found in their copy (NC 32490–32511). Persia: Braun reports from the Syriac acts that at a synod of Persian bishops under Isaac, Marutha read "den Band der Kanonen" (the volume of the canons), and "alle Bischöfe unter einem Anathem die Kanonen annehmen zu wollen erklären" (all the bishops declare, under an anathema, that they will accept the canons) (MAR 857–866); the acts end with "das nicänische Symbolum" (the Nicene creed) (MAR 877–879). Year: Braun cites Lamy, "Concilium Seleuciae et Ctesiphonti habitum anno 410" (MAR 894); the OCR at MAR 803 reads "Epiphanie 4 1 9", which I take as an OCR fault and do not use. LATER (about 85 and 95 years). Documented (Carthage); Widely Accepted (Persia, 410, via Braun). Braun himself says the acts are "vielleicht absichtlich, nicht ganz klar" (perhaps deliberately, not entirely clear) (MAR 808): card only.
I5. **One Easter.** The decision on Easter (M13). Later history of the Easter question is in an editor's excursus (NC 6498 onward; not read in full, not used). OWN. Documented that it was decided; how far it held is not taken from the record here.
I6. **The bishops of the empire's great sees.** Canon 6 (M14) became, with the African council's appeal dispute (I4), a ground of argument about which bishops ruled where. Card only; Inferential-Thin as a story claim.

## 6. Links to other people on the tree

- **Constantine the Great**: called the council, sat in it, spoke at it, wrote its letter on Easter (S4, M1–M3, M13).
- **Arius**: condemned by it; his own letter kept by Theodoret (D-3, M10–M11).
- **Alexander of Alexandria**: the bishop who put Arius out (D-1, D-3); named in the synodal letter as "a guide and fellow-worker in what has been done" (NC 6398–6399), who "at so great an age has undergone so great fatigue" (NC 6410–6411).
- **Athanasius of Alexandria**: present as deacon (C6, later witness); wrote its defence (I2).
- **Eusebius of Caesarea**: there; read his church's creed; signed; wrote the letter to his church and the *Life of Constantine* (M1, M5, M9).
- **Eusebius of Nicomedia**: Arius's correspondent (D-3); refused or hesitated; exiled and restored (M10, M11, I3).
- **Eustathius of Antioch**: there; wrote the account kept by Theodoret (M7); Theodoret says he gave the opening praise of the emperor (NP3 6289–6295), while Eusebius's editor takes it as Eusebius himself (bracketed note, EUS 64438–64439). Contested.
- **Ossius (Hosius) of Córdoba**: there (C2); first in the Latin lists (C4).
- **Sylvester, bishop of Rome**: did not come; sent two priests (C3).
- **Caecilian of Carthage**: there; brought the copies home (C5, I4).
- **Melitius of Lycopolis**: limited by the council (M12). The synodal letter does not give his city; "Lycopolis" is not found in the held lines used here.
- **Marutha of Maipherqat and Isaac of Seleucia**: carried the canons to the Persian church (I4).

## 7. Who tells it

W1. **The council's own documents** (creed, canons, synodal letter) and **Constantine's Easter letter**: OWN, 325. NC prints them from later collections (NC 2397–2402, 6320–6323, 6424–6425). The council's minutes are not held; NC prints no acts of debate.
W2. **Eusebius of Caesarea**, bishop, there. His letter to his church: written just after. The *Life of Constantine*: a panegyric of the emperor, written about a dozen years later (estimate). He had been slow to accept the word (SOC 2972–2974), so his letter explains himself. Say aloud: "Eusebius of Caesarea, a bishop who was there and admired the emperor".
W3. **Athanasius**, a deacon at the council, later bishop of Alexandria, wrote his account decades later (estimate) as the creed's champion and an enemy of Arius's party. Say aloud: "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defence".
W4. **Eustathius of Antioch**, bishop, there, hostile to Arius's party; his words survive only as quoted by Theodoret (NP3 6381–6385, editor's note).
W5. **Arius**: his letter is from before the council; it is kept in the history of Theodoret, who was against him.
W6. **Socrates and Theodoret**: church historians writing more than a century later (estimates: about 115 and 125 years). Say aloud for any detail only they give: "a historian writing a century later".
W7. **Jerome**: about 55 years later, on Ariminum.

## 8. Where sources disagree or are late

D1. Number of bishops: 250+ / about 270 / about 300 / 318 (C1). The number 318 is later.
D2. What happened in the debate: Eusebius tells of his own creed approved with one word added (M5); Athanasius tells of bishops forced to clearer words by evasions (M6); Eustathius tells of a "formulary of Eusebius" torn up (M7); Theodoret of a document by Arius's friends torn up (M7). These are not one story. Tell them as different witnesses' accounts or choose one and name its teller.
D3. How many refused (M10): two (synodal letter), five (Socrates), all signed (Eusebius, Eustathius).
D4. Whether Eusebius of Nicomedia and Theognis were exiled at the council or later (M11).
D5. Days of the council (S2).
D6. Who gave the first speech (Links: Eustathius).
D7. Who presided: not stated in Eusebius ("those who presided", EUS 64501). Not to be filled from memory.
D8. Not held: the council's minutes; any text of 325 giving Arius's own words at the council; the lost "Synodicon" of Athanasius (editor's note, SOC 3906–3908).
