## How the council worked

No minutes of the council survive, so how it worked comes from a few men who were there and from historians writing a century later. Eusebius of Caesarea, who was there, says the emperor opened the formal session in the palace, then handed the floor to "those who presided" (EUS 64501). He names no one, and no eyewitness names a president. Eusebius says Constantine listened and persuaded until the bishops reached "one mind" (EUS 64514–64515). He and Athanasius, there as a deacon by a later historian's account, describe words being questioned and redrafted. Every member signed, Eusebius says, but no eyewitness describes a vote or gives a count. Athanasius notes that the Easter ruling began "It seemed good", but the faith "Thus believes the Catholic Church" (ATH 56538–56542). The witnesses differ on who gave the opening speech. The dates conflict: Socrates, writing later, gives 20 May 325, while an editor suggests 14 June to 25 August. The council ended with a banquet for Constantine's twentieth year as emperor and a farewell speech (Eusebius).
