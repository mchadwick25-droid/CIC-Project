# Review: the Council of Nicaea (325)

Reviewer, varied batch 2, 2026-11-17. Brief judged on its own, against `kit/Standard.md` and `kit/Shapes.md` (Council shape), traced to `Outline.md`, `Outline_Check.md` and `Source_Sheet.md`, with each quote re-checked against `cic/texts/`.

**Verdict: REVISE** (4 BLOCKING, 5 MINOR)

## 1. Listener read (one pass, before anything else)

**Where I got lost or drifted**
- "lent them the public post for the journey": a listener in a second language will not know what "the public post" is. The meaning is not worked into the sentence.
- The count in paragraph 3 (more than 250; about 270 or 300; 318) comes as four numbers in one breath. I kept the sense ("the number is disputed") but lost the figures. Acceptable, but it is the densest moment.
- "which the emperor approved, although he asked for one word to be added": for a moment "he" could be Eusebius.
- "Athanasius, an enemy of Arius who was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defence, says...": the subject waits a long time for its verb. I had to hold the whole description in mind.
- "Arius's friend Eusebius of Nicomedia was brought back": I had just heard about a hesitant Eusebius who signed. On one hearing, I took this for the same man, and I had never been told anyone was sent away, so "brought back" from where?
- "Its rules travelled too.": what rules? The story never says the council made any. The ending lands on something I was never given.

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down**
- Nowhere, as a stretch. The short sentences ("Constantine entered the hall in purple and gold." "The witnesses tell the debate differently." "All but a few signed." "Yet the settlement did not hold at once.") each sit at a turn, between sentences of 25 to 30 words. None comes in a run. No definition is tacked on after a comma: "homoousios, 'of one being' with the Father" is worked in as the word the emperor asked for, and "cut off from the church" carries the meaning of the curse inside the act.
- The nearest thing to a list is paragraph 7: three acts in a row (brought back, recalled, divided) with no cause or result between them. It is thin, not childish.

**On the 24% short sentences:** it does not read as a list. Six of 25 sentences are short, and each marks a turn in the story. The one short sentence that fails is "Its rules travelled too." It fails because its subject was never introduced, not because it is short (finding 3).

## 3. Trace and quote check

All lines trace to the outline, except as the findings below note. Quotes re-checked against the files:

- Q2 Constantine: EUSG 11167–11169 and EUS 64468–64471. The brief matches the rendering exactly, credited "as Eusebius reports it".
- Q3 Eusebius: ATH 19741–19744 and SOCG 7326–7328, 7405–7407. The brief **cuts the rendering mid-sentence** (finding 2).
- Short quotes: "from all quarters" (EUS 64282–64284); "whispering ... and winking" (ATH 27991–27992); "God of God, Light of Light", "begotten, not made", "there was a time when he was not" (NC 2408–2419); "shall not be set aside" (NC 13970–13971); "pre-eminent" (NC 20220); Jerome (JER 32412–32413, 359 from the editor at JER 32284); Caecilian's copies (NC 32523–32533). - Hostile sources ("we witness their perspective, not carry it forward"): these are handled well. Athanasius is named as "an enemy of Arius", and both "whispering ... and winking" and the reason the bishops changed their words stay inside "Athanasius says". Jerome's "Arian" is his own word, in quotation marks; the narrator otherwise says "Arius's friends". The one exception is the narrator's own claim about Arius's expulsion (finding 5).

## 4. Findings

1. **BLOCKING: past the 3-minute limit.** At 480 words the brief runs about 3:12. That is past Mark's limit ("the target is 2 minutes with stretch to tell well and majors") and past the outline's 400–450. **Change:** cut to about 430–440 words, in this order:
   - (a) Cut the Eusebius quote sentence, "Eusebius, who was slow to accept the word, told his church, 'We kept peace before our eyes as our aim.'" (about 20 words). The outline says to use Q3 "only if room"; there is no room, and this also clears finding 2.
   - (b) Cut the second sentence of Constantine's quote, "These troubles seem more painful to me than any from outside." (11 words). It repeats the first.
   - (c) If still over, cut "Speaking in Latin through an interpreter," (6 words), or "while Rome's aged bishop sent two priests instead" (8 words). Both are true, and both are card material.

   Do not cut the after-history: it is already a little under a third.

2. **BLOCKING: Eusebius's quote is cut so that it says less than he did.** The outline's rendering is "We kept peace before our eyes as our aim, and also not to fall from the right meaning." The brief stops at "aim" and drops the second aim, which the source gives equal weight (ATH 19743–19744, "and stedfastness in the orthodox view"). With "who was slow to accept the word" in front of it, the cut makes him sign for peace alone. The outline's stated job for this quote is "agreement won *partly* for peace". **Change:** cut the sentence (finding 1a). If it is kept, quote the rendering whole.
   - **Do not say:** "Do not quote Eusebius's 'peace' line without its second aim ('and also not to fall from the right meaning'); quote it whole or leave it out."

3. **BLOCKING: "Its rules travelled too" has no antecedent, so the ending loses the listener.** The brief never says the council made rules (the outline's Beat 7: one Easter, the curb on Melitius). The closing paragraph then asks the listener to follow "its rules" and "the copy" to Carthage. **Change:** either name the rules in the sentence that sends them travelling, for example "The council had also made rules for the church, and these travelled too." Or end instead on the later councils (the outline's other permitted ending), cutting the Carthage paragraph. If you choose the second, take fewer of the cuts in finding 1 so the after-history stays near a third.
   - **Do not say:** "Do not say 'its rules' or 'the canons' in the after-history unless the story has first said the council made rules."

4. **BLOCKING: the two Eusebiuses run together, and "brought back" assumes an exile the story never told.** The listener hears "Eusebius" four times as the hesitant bishop who wrote the account, then "Arius's friend Eusebius of Nicomedia was brought back". On one hearing they merge. The outline warns that they are two different men, and the listener was never told that anyone but Arius was sent away. **Change:** for example, "Another Eusebius, bishop of Nicomedia and Arius's friend, was brought back from exile, and Arius himself was recalled." This stays within the outline's "say he was exiled and later restored".
   - **Do not say:** "Do not name Eusebius of Nicomedia without marking him as a different man from the Eusebius who wrote the account ('another Eusebius')."

5. **MINOR: the narrator states Arius's claim as fact.** "Arius ... wrote this after his bishop drove him and his friends from the city." The outline's Beat 1 has "Arius **says** Alexander drove him and his friends out". The expulsion from the city is Arius's own charge against his bishop (NP3 6025–6027); Constantine says only that "fellowship was withdrawn". **Change:** "Arius, a priest of Alexandria in Egypt, wrote this after, as he tells it, his bishop drove him and his friends from the city."

6. **MINOR: "the public post" is not followed on one hearing.** **Change:** work the meaning in, within the outline's words "the public post and horses": for example, "and let them travel by the empire's post horses".

7. **MINOR: ambiguous "he".** "which the emperor approved, although he asked for one word to be added": **Change:** "although **the emperor** asked..." (and "which he approved" before it).

8. **MINOR: Athanasius is held too long from his verb.** The 30-word sentence puts a 17-word description between "Athanasius" and "says". **Change:** move the description into its own clause, for example, "Athanasius, an enemy of Arius, was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defence. He says Arius's friends were caught..."

9. **MINOR: paragraph 7 stacks acts with no link.** "Brought back ... recalled ... For decades the word divided": give the cause or result in the sentence, for example, "so for decades the word divided...". Fold this into the rewrite for finding 4.
