**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down**

## The whole council (Mark's question)

It now tells the whole council, not only the Arian struggle. All five elements are there, in the record's order:
- **What called it:** the Easter split and the strife in Egypt, Constantine's failed letter, the summons (paras 1–2).
- **The major issue and its story:** Arius and Alexander; the debate in three witnesses' voices (paras 3–4). This is about a third of the story, not the whole.
- **Its decisions:** the creed, its curse, the signing, the condemnation and exile (para 5).
- **What else it did:** Melitius, one Easter, twenty canons with two named (para 6).
- **Impact:** the creed set first at Constantinople and Chalcedon; the decades of division; Easter enforced at Antioch; the canons carried home and checked at Carthage (paras 7–9, about 165 words, a third). It ends on an event.

Three voices run through it: Eusebius (what called it, the debate, signing), Constantine (his summons and speech) and Arius (his own words). This meets the Council shape. Length is fine for Daniel's voice; it never lost me.

## Findings

1. **MINOR: "twisted" gives the verdict without the charge.** The word is fair as Athanasius's (see above). But the listener never hears what the twisting was, so the need for "of one being" is left unexplained. Restore the outline's content: "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defence, says Arius's friends read the bishops' words, like 'from God', as true of every creature, and were caught whispering and winking." "Twisted" can stay if the content is kept, but the plain "read ... as true of every creature" is clearer and carries the charge on its own.
2. **MINOR: "On the same feast" reads oddly on one hearing.** Change to "On the same feast day, some Christians were fasting while others feasted ...".
3. **MINOR: the Melitius sentence is hard to hold.** Unfold the "leaving X his title but no power" construction, for example: "The council then turned to a second matter in Egypt. Melitius had ordained clergy of his own, and he kept his title but lost the power to ordain." (M12; NC 6362–6367.)
4. **MINOR: a tacked-on gloss.** "That was its name for Nicaea." stands alone after the quote. Work it into the sentence, e.g. "it ruled that the faith of the '318 fathers', as it called the bishops of Nicaea, 'shall not be set aside'."
5. **MINOR (rigor, noted for the outline):** "Arius was recalled" is stated flatly. Socrates, its only held witness, hedges it: "It appears also that Arius was recalled before them; but, although this may be true ..." (SOC 3978–3980). The outline marks it Documented. The brief may keep it, but the outline's tag should be Widely Accepted, not Documented.

**Blocking findings**

R1. **(a) "came before" can be heard as time.** In "That question about Jesus came before the council of Nicaea, which met in 325", the clause right after it gives a date. Heard once, that makes "came before" sound like "happened earlier than". On that hearing, the one sentence meant to tie the issue to the council does not do it.
- **Change to:** "That question about Jesus was taken up by the council of Nicaea, which met in 325, in today's Turkey, when bishops from across the Roman Empire came at the emperor Constantine's call."
- **Do not say (for the outline):** "The question came before the council" → "the council took up the question" (avoids the time reading).

R2. **(b) Leaving Easter out as a cause misleads.** Eusebius names two troubles before the summons: the strife in Egypt, and the Easter split, which "long afflicted the Church" (EUS 64241–64243; outline beat 1, beat 6). The brief says Eusebius "tells what brought them" and names only Egypt. Easter later appears only as "other business". A listener therefore hears one cause where the witness gives two. Putting Easter second is a fair choice of emphasis; leaving it out as a cause is not.
- **Fix,** keeping the spine: in the Easter sentence, "Easter was the second trouble Eusebius names: the churches kept it on different days, so that on the same feast some Christians fasted while others feasted."
- **Do not say (for the outline):** "Constantine called the council over Egypt" alone → Eusebius names two troubles, Egypt and Easter; if one is told as the cause, say the other was the second.

R3. **(c) A run of four short sentences at the turn into impact.** The run is: "That was its name for Nicaea. Chalcedon, in 451, called it 'pre-eminent'. Yet it did not hold at once. Arius, it seems, was recalled from exile." This is the children's-book pattern the standard rules out, and it holds the earlier tacked-on gloss (finding 4). Elsewhere the flow is now linked and does not skip around.
