# Outline: How Nicaea decided (short story)

Sheet = `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder; tags (H1–H16, M1–M15) point to its entries. Two Greek passages below are not yet on the sheet; their file and lines are given here.

- **Length:** about 1 to 1½ minutes (150–220 words). Reason: a single question (how did they decide?) told from eyewitnesses only; the held eyewitness record of procedure is thin, so keep it short.
- **Setting, in words to say aloud:** "In 325, at Nicaea, in today's Turkey, bishops from across the Roman Empire met in the emperor Constantine's palace." (S1, S2, H3).
- **Thread:** At Nicaea the emperor opened the debate and pressed for agreement; the bishops tested and rewrote their words until each signed, and they set down Easter as "It seemed good" but the faith as "Thus believes the Catholic Church".
- **Shape:** Council (one part of it: how it decided).
- **Openings:** (1) Athanasius's contrast between "It seemed good" and "Thus believes" (H8, Q1). (2) A question the record raises: no minutes survive, so how do we know how they decided? (H14, section D).

**Story beats**
1. *The opening.* On the appointed day the bishops sat by rank on both sides of the palace's largest hall. The emperor entered and spoke in Latin, through an interpreter. Eusebius (there). H3, M1, M3. Documented as his account.
2. *The emperor's part.* He gave the floor to "those who presided", whom Eusebius does not name. He listened, took each side's points in turn, and pressed for agreement (Q2). His own letter says he sat "as one among yourselves". H5, H6. Documented as their accounts; both praise him.
3. *Testing the words.* Eusebius read his church's creed; one word, "of one being", was added; then "questions and explanations took place". Athanasius says the bishops found plain words from Scripture evaded, so they wrote again, "more distinctly still". H7, M5, M6. Documented as each man's account.
4. *Two kinds of decision.* For Easter they wrote "It seemed good"; for the faith, "Thus believes the Catholic Church", to show it was not new (Athanasius). H8, Q1. Documented as Athanasius's account.
5. *The signing.* What they agreed was written down and each member signed (Eusebius). A few would not accept all of it. H10, H11, M10. Documented; numbers Contested.
6. *The banquet and leave-taking.* For his twentieth year as emperor, Constantine feasted the bishops, gave each gifts by rank, urged peace in a farewell speech, and let them go home. H15, H16. Documented as Eusebius's account.

**What came of it** (brief; the main Nicaea story carries the impact)
- Eusebius claims the agreement "continued to prevail" (H16); the later record shows it did not (I3). Say both, or leave the claim out.

**Quotes** (2 at most)
- **Q1. Athanasius** (the two kinds of decision). Greek, `athanasius_opera-omnia-pg26-grc-lat_migne1857.txt` lines 57164–57168 (OCR; footnote marks and stray quotation marks left out, words split across lines joined): "Οὐ γὰρ προέταξαν ὑπατείαν, καὶ μῆνα, χαὶ [καὶ] ἡμέραν ἀλλὰ περὶ μὲν τοῦ Πάσχα Ἔδοξε τὰ ὑποτεταγμένα τότε γὰρ ἔδοξε πάντας πείθεσθαι περὶ δὲ τῆς πίστεως ἔγραψαν οὐχ [οὐκ] ἜἜδοξεν [Ἔδοξεν], ἀλλ᾽, Οὕτως πιστεύει ἡ χαθολιχὴ [καθολικὴ] Ἐχχλησία [Ἐκκλησία]". English, ATH 56537–56542: "Without pre-fixing Consulate, month, and day, they wrote concerning Easter, ‘It seemed good as follows,’ for it did then seem good that there should be a general compliance; but about the faith they wrote not, ‘It seemed good,’ but, ‘Thus believes the Catholic Church;’". Rendered from the Greek: **"They put no consul's year, month or day at the top. About Easter they wrote, 'The following seemed good.' For then it seemed good that all should obey. But about the faith they did not write, 'It seemed good.' They wrote, 'This is what the universal Church believes.'"** ("Universal" renders καθολική, to avoid the modern denominational sense.) Job: the heart of the story, the difference between a rule and a statement of faith.
- **Q2. Eusebius** (the emperor's part). Greek, `eusebius_werke-bd1-vita-constantini-oratio-ad-sanctos-grc_heikel1902.txt` lines 11238–11240 (OCR): "μὲν συμπείϑον [συμπείθων], τοὺς δὲ καταδυσωπῶν τῷ λόγῳ, τοὺς ó Ev [δ᾽ εὖ] λέγοντας ἐπαινῶν, πάντας τ᾽ εἰς ὁμόνοιαν ἐλαύνων, εἰσόϑ’ ὁμογνώμονας καὶ ὁμοδόύξους [ὁμοδόξους] αὐτοὺς ἐπὶ τοῖς ἀμφισβητουμένοις ἅπασι κατεστήσατο". English, EUS 64512–64515: "persuading some, convincing others by his reasonings, praising those who spoke well, and urging all to unity of sentiment, until at last he succeeded in bringing them to one mind and judgment respecting every disputed question." Rendered from the Greek: **"He won some over and shamed others with his words. He praised those who spoke well and drove them all toward agreement. In the end he brought them to one mind and one belief on every point in dispute."** Job: the emperor's part, in an admirer's words. Say it is Eusebius's account.

**Connection to carry into the story**
- From the emperor to the bishops' decision: Eusebius shows Constantine steering the debate (H5), while the written decisions stand in the bishops' names, with each member's signature (H10). The listener needs to hear both: the emperor pressed, the bishops signed.
- From Nicaea's wording to later readers: Athanasius, decades later, used the council's own phrasing ("Thus believes", not "It seemed good") to argue that its creed was the apostles' faith, not a new rule (H8). Tell it as his reading of the documents.

**Links:** Constantine (opened it, steered it, feasted the bishops); Eusebius of Caesarea (there; wrote the account); Athanasius (there as a deacon, by a later historian's account; wrote the "It seemed good" contrast).

**Who tells it**
- Eusebius of Caesarea, a bishop who was there and admired the emperor; his *Life of Constantine* was written years later as praise. Aloud: "Eusebius, a bishop who was there and admired the emperor".
- Athanasius, there as a deacon (a later historian says), writing decades later in the creed's defence. Aloud: "Athanasius, who was there as a deacon and wrote later in its defence".
- Constantine's own letter to the churches, sent just after.
- No minutes survive.

**Do not say**
- "Ossius presided" / "Eustathius presided" / "the emperor presided" / "the pope's legates presided." → Eusebius speaks only of "those who presided" and names no one (EUS 64501). Say no one is named.
- "The bishops voted" / "The creed passed by 300 votes to 2." → No eyewitness records a vote. Say each member signed (Eusebius) and a few would not accept all of it.
- "The council met from 20 May to 25 August" / "for two months." → The dates are uncertain; say only "in 325". The length is an editor's guess.
- "Arius was questioned before the bishops" / "they avoided a vote" / "seventeen sided with Arius." → Sozomen, about 120 years later (H2, H9). Leave to the card, or say "a historian a century later".
- "The emperor burned the bishops' complaints." → Socrates and Sozomen, later (M4, H2). Leave to the card, or name them as later.
- "Paphnutius talked the bishops out of a celibacy rule." → Socrates, later, and no canon records it (H13). Leave to the card.
- "Eusebius gave the opening speech" / "Eustathius gave the opening speech." → Eusebius does not name the speaker; later writers differ (H4). Do not name him.
- "The emperor wrote the creed" / "dictated the word." → Eusebius says the emperor approved his creed and urged the word "of one being"; the bishops wrote and signed.
- "Unity lasted." → That is Eusebius's claim (H16); the later record shows decades of conflict (I3).
- "Thus believes the Catholic Church" means the Roman Catholic Church. → It means the whole church; say "the universal Church" if rendering.
- "Bishops cheered / wept." → No feelings beyond Eusebius's words.

**Leave out**
- Sozomen's and Socrates's stories (H2, H9, H12, H13, M4): later; card only.
- The rival dates (H14): card only.
- Constantine's death penalty for hiding Arius's books (H12): belongs to the main story's aftermath or the card.
