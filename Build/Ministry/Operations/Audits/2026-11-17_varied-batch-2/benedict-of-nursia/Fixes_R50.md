# Fixes, release 50: Benedict of Nursia

Ruling: Mark, 2026-11-17, "yes no softening, we are a witness not a judge … for this round 1 but also the should fixes too". Released text kept as `benedict-of-nursia.r50.brief.txt`.

## Piece items

1. **MUST: the Apollo temple at Monte Cassino restored, in Gregory's voice.** Added after "he moved to Monte Cassino.": "Gregory says country people there still worshipped Apollo at an old temple, with sacred woods around it. Benedict, he says, smashed the idol, overturned the altar and burned the woods, and built chapels in their place." The Rule's sentences now start a new paragraph. Sources: outline beat 5 and Do not say; Source_Sheet M12; GRG 4167–4184 ("beat in pieces the idol, overthrew the altar, set fire on the woods, and in the temple of Apollo, he built the oratory of St. Martin … an oratory of St. John"). Lines opened and confirmed. The devil's cry (WONDER) stays out.
2. **The sack of Monte Cassino attributed from the start.** Old: "The Rule survived even the sack of Monte Cassino. In Gregory's own lifetime the Lombards plundered it by night, yet not one monk was taken." New: "Long before those councils, Gregory reports, the Lombards plundered Monte Cassino by night, yet not one monk was taken." The unattributed claim "The Rule survived…" is cut; Paul the Deacon's line now carries the point in his own voice. Sources: M20, GRG 4657–4661 ("in the night time … they entered in, and spoiled all things, but yet not one man could they retain there"); I2, PAUL 7832–7835.
3. **Not ending on administration.** The two councils (742, 802) now come first in the last part, word for word, and the piece ends on Paul the Deacon: "Paul the Deacon, writing in the eighth century, adds that the monks fled to Rome "carrying with them the manuscript of the Holy Rule"." The ending is the Rule itself, carried out of the plundered house, in the record's words. Sources: I1a (MGH 2199, 2324–2325), I1c (MGH 7794–7805), I2 (PAUL 7832–7835). "Long before those councils" keeps the time order clear for one hearing (Gregory wrote in 593; the councils are 742 and 802).
4. **Card, uncertain: older rules.** A held text does name Benedict's use of older rules, so one line was added after the Gasquet entry: "The Rule points its readers to older guides: "the Collations of the Fathers, their Institutes, their Lives, and the Rule of our Holy Father St. Basil." Gardner says it was based in part on Basil's rule." Sources: R8, RBE 4290–4291 (confirmed: `benedict_rule-of-saint-benedict_gasquet1925.txt` 4290–4291); I5, GRG-ed 1162–1163 (confirmed: "based, in part, upon the eastern rule of St. Basil"). On the Rule of the Master: the Library holds its Latin, but no held scholarly source argues its relation to Benedict's Rule (Source_Sheet D4), so nothing was added about it.
