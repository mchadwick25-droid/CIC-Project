## Listener read (one pass, written before the trace)

**Where I got lost or drifted.**
- "Because idleness is an enemy of the soul, it sets times for work..." The sentence before ends inside a quote about "a school for the Lord's service", so for a moment "it" could be the school. The Rule is three sentences back.
- "That Rule outlived its house." "Its house" stalled me for a beat: the house of the Rule? The next sentence makes clear it means the monastery at Monte Cassino.
- "When he held them to their rule..." I had to infer that he had agreed to be their abbot; the brief says only that he warned them.

**Where it sounds like a children's book or talks down.** Nowhere that matters. There is no run of one-act sentences, and no definition tacked on after a comma. Sentences carry cause and contrast ("When he saw many students fall into loose living, he gave up..."; "yet not one monk was taken"). The short "That Rule outlived its house" is a deliberate turn, not a list. The last paragraph leans a little toward a chronicle ("In 742... Then, in 802..."), but each date carries a real act, so it reads as an adult narrator, not a primer.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** An adult register throughout. Ideas are tied by cause and contrast, and meaning is worked into the sentences rather than defined after them. It loses a point for the Rule paragraph, which drifts toward listing features ("it sets times... The community chooses...") rather than telling why they mattered.
- **Craft: 4.** It has a strong, concrete hook and sets the time, place and Gothic rule in the first paragraph. The stakes are clear at Rome and at the poisoning. The impact third builds to the Aachen order and ends on it, with no moral. It is slightly thinner on what the Rule's gentleness meant.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: the Rule is placed at Monte Cassino.** "...he moved on to Monte Cassino. There he wrote a rule for his monks" ties the writing to Monte Cassino. Gregory gives no place or year for the Rule (D3), and the outline's Do not say bars "He wrote the Rule at Monte Cassino". Change it to a sentence with no place, for example: "He also wrote a rule for his monks:" followed by Q1. Or credit it, as in "Gregory says he wrote a rule for his monks...". Do not join it to the Monte Cassino sentence with "there" or "where".
   **Do not say:** Wrong: "There he wrote a rule" / "where he wrote his Rule" right after Monte Cassino. Right: "He wrote a rule for his monks", with no place, in its own sentence.

2. **MINOR: "idleness is an enemy of the soul" and the stray "it".** These are the Rule's words (R4), but the brief speaks them in the narrator's voice, and "it" has no near antecedent. Change to: "The Rule calls idleness an enemy of the soul, so it sets fixed times for work, and other set hours for holy reading."

3. **MINOR: "That Rule outlived its house."** "Its house" is unclear on one hearing. Change to "That Rule outlived the monastery where he lived" or "...outlived Monte Cassino itself."

4. **MINOR: the abbot beat skips his consent.** After "though he warned that their ways and his would never agree", add that he gave in at last (GRG 3655–3665; outline beat 3). Then "When he held them to their rule" follows without the listener having to infer it.

5. **MINOR: Nursia unplaced.** The outline's setting line gives "Nursia, in central Italy". Saying "born in Nursia, in central Italy" costs three words and helps a listener anywhere.
