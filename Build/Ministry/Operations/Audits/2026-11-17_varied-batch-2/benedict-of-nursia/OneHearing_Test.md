# One-hearing test: Benedict of Nursia

Test (Mark): "An interested non-specialist can understand who this was, what happened, and why the connection matters after one hearing." Not simpler.

Brief tested: `benedict-of-nursia.brief.txt` (401 words). Read once, straight through, before the outline.

## Step 1: three lines from one hearing

- **Who was this?** Benedict, a young man from Nursia in central Italy, around 500 under the Gothic kings, who left his studies in Rome to serve God and became a monk. Pope Gregory the Great wrote his life about fifty years later, from four of his monks.
- **What happened?** He lived three years in a cave near Subiaco, fed by the monk Romanus. Others came to him. A nearby monastery made him abbot, and some of its monks tried to poison him when he held them to their rule (Gregory says the glass shattered). He built twelve monasteries at Subiaco, moved to Monte Cassino around 529, and wrote a Rule for his monks: a "school for the Lord's service", nothing harsh or heavy, set times for work and for holy reading, an abbot chosen by the community who balances the strong and the weak.
- **Why does the connection matter?** His Rule outlived him. When the Lombards sacked Monte Cassino, the monks escaped to Rome carrying it. In 742 Carloman's council ordered the monasteries of his lands to live by it, and in 802 Charlemagne had it read at Aachen and ordered the monasteries put right by it. What he passed on was the Rule, and Frankish rulers made it the law of their monasteries.

## Non-blocking notes (not required for a pass)

These cost a listener a moment, not the line. Each uses only what the outline holds.

1. **"Rule" heard twice for two different things.** Paragraph 3: "When he held them to their rule" refers to the other monastery's own rule. By ear there is no capital letter, so a listener may think the poisoning was over *his* Rule, which is not written until paragraph 4. Smallest fix, from beat 3: "When he held them to the regular life their own rule required, some put poison in his wine."
2. **The closing sentence is the hardest one to hear.** "He ordered that whatever had been done in the monasteries against it be put right by it" has two "it"s and a subjunctive, in the line that lands the connection. Smallest fix, keeping the record's terms (beat I1, MGH 7794–7805): "He ordered that whatever had been done in the monasteries against the Rule be put right by it." Same length plus one word.

Both fixes keep the brief well under 450 words.
