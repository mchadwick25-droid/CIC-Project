# Second Readings: Benedict of Nursia

## 1. So we must found a school
Source: cic/texts/benedict_rule-of-saint-benedict_gasquet1925.txt 768-770; Latin cic/texts/benedict_rule-of-st-benedict-lat-eng_hunter-blair1907.txt 1240-1245
Second rendering: So we must set up a school for the Lord's service. In setting it up, we hope to lay down nothing harsh and nothing heavy.
Ours: So we must found a school for the Lord's service. In founding it, we hope to lay down nothing harsh and nothing heavy.
Verdict: same
Reason: Both carry "constituenda est", "Dominici", "in qua institutione" as the act of founding, and both negatives. "Found" and "set up" are interchangeable here.

## 2. Let him so temper everything
Source: cic/texts/benedict_rule-of-saint-benedict_gasquet1925.txt 3960-3961; Latin cic/texts/benedict_rule-of-st-benedict-lat-eng_hunter-blair1907.txt 9527-9528
Second rendering: He should temper everything, so that the strong have something to strive for and the weak do not run away.
Ours: Let him so temper everything that the strong have something to long for, and the weak do not run away.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Long for" renders "cupiant" at least as closely as "strive for"; "do not run away" matches "non refugiant". No difference in sense.

## 3. carrying the manuscript of the Holy Rule
Source: cic/texts/paul-the-deacon_history-of-the-langobards_foulke1907.txt 7832-7835
Second rendering: They carried with them the handwritten copy of the Holy Rule that this father had composed.
Ours: carrying the manuscript of the Holy Rule
Verdict: same
Reason: Verbatim excerpt of the printed English with "with them" and the relative clause left out; nothing in the kept words shifts sense.

## Release 50 fixes

## 4. Gregory says country people there still worshipped Apollo (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/gregory-great_dialogues_gardner1911.txt 4171-4184 (the 1608 English; Gregory's Latin is not held)
Second rendering: On that mountain stood an ancient chapel where the simple country people, keeping the old pagans' custom, worshipped the god Apollo, with woods all round it where they still offered sacrifice. The man of God came, broke the idol to pieces, overturned the altar, set fire to the woods, and built a chapel of St Martin in Apollo's temple and a chapel of St John where Apollo's altar had stood.
Ours: Gregory says country people there still worshipped Apollo at an old temple, with sacred woods around it. Benedict, he says, smashed the idol, overturned the altar and burned the woods, and built chapels in their place.
Verdict: same
Reason: Every act is carried, in order, and credited to Gregory twice. "Sacred woods" is the worshippers' view where Gregory has "woods for the service of the devils"; that keeps the narrator out of Gregory's polemic and changes nothing a listener takes away. "Chapels in their place" folds the two oratories into one phrase. "Still" rests on "even to that very time".

## 5. carrying with them the manuscript of the Holy Rule
Source: cic/texts/paul-the-deacon_history-of-the-langobards_foulke1907.txt 7832-7835 (Foulke's English; Paul's Latin is not held)
Second rendering: The monks fled and made their way to Rome, carrying with them the handwritten copy of the Holy Rule that this father had composed.
Ours: carrying with them the manuscript of the Holy Rule
Verdict: same
Reason: Now verbatim with the printed English, "with them" restored. The relative clause and the bread and wine are left out, which shifts nothing. Supersedes entry 3, which read the shorter released wording.

## 6. Card: the Collations of the Fathers, their Institutes, their Lives, and the Rule of our Holy Father St. Basil
Source: cic/texts/benedict_rule-of-saint-benedict_gasquet1925.txt 4290-4291; Latin cic/texts/benedict_rule-of-st-benedict-lat-eng_hunter-blair1907.txt 10409-10411
Second rendering: the Conferences of the Fathers, their Institutes and their Lives, and also the Rule of our holy father Basil
Ours: the Collations of the Fathers, their Institutes, their Lives, and the Rule of our Holy Father St. Basil
Verdict: same
Reason: "Collationes Patrum, et Instituta et Vitae eorum; sed et Regula sancti Patris nostri Basilii" is carried item for item. The OCR's "Co//ations" is read as "Collations", which the Latin confirms. The card's framing, "points its readers to older guides", fits the passage: the Rule names them as examples of the monk's life beyond its own "little Rule for beginners" (RBE 4297-4300).
