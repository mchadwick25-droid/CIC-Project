# Outline check: Benedict of Nursia

Checker: independent outline checker, varied batch 2, 2026-11-17. I opened every cited place in GRG, GRG-ed, RBL, RBE, MGH and PAUL myself. Cited line numbers hold throughout.

**Verdict: FIX** (one blocking finding; the rest is minor).

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING — beat 6, work and reading.** "the day is divided between work with the hands and holy reading" says more than the Rule. The Rule says the brethren are busy "at specified times in manual labour, and at other fixed hours in holy reading" (RBE 3113–3116; RBL 7358–7364 "certis temporibus ... certis iterum horis"). It does not say the day is split between the two, and a listener would hear a day with no prayer in it. Correction: "Because 'idleness is an enemy of the soul', the Rule sets fixed times for work with the hands and other fixed hours for holy reading (R4; RBE 3113–3116)." Add to **Do not say**: Wrong: "the monk's day was work and reading" / "his day was divided between work and reading". Right: "set times for work, and other set hours for holy reading."

2. **MINOR — beat 2, distance.** Gregory says "distant almost forty miles from Rome" (GRG 3489–3490); the beat has "about forty miles". Correction: "almost forty miles from Rome". Add to Do not say: Wrong: "forty miles" / "over forty miles". Right: "almost forty miles".

3. **MINOR — setting line, Nursia.** "a province in the hills of central Italy": "in the hills" is in no cited line. Gregory gives only "the province of Nursia" (GRG 3414); Gasquet gives "a city of Umbria" (RBE-ed 170–171). Correction: "Nursia, in central Italy" (or leave Nursia as Gregory names it, with no landscape).

4. **MINOR — beat 7, foreknowledge of death.** "Benedict knew his death was near" softens a WONDER. Gregory says he "told the day of his holy death to his monks" (GRG 5577–5578), and the sheet marks it WONDER (M19). The Leave-out note says only two wonders are kept. Correction: start the death at the fact: "Six days before he died, Gregory says, he had his grave opened; then a fever took him" (GRG 5582–5584), or mark the foreknowledge WONDER, Gregory's voice. Add to Do not say: Wrong: "he knew the day he would die" as plain fact. Right: in Gregory's voice, or left out.

5. **MINOR — beat 7, the test.** The beat tells of the disguised guardsman but not the outcome, which is right, since Benedict seeing through it is a WONDER (M13a). A writer may finish it. Add to Do not say: Wrong: "Benedict saw through the disguise at once" as plain fact. Right: leave the test's outcome out, or "Gregory says he knew the man was not the king."

6. **MINOR — impact 1, extent of 742.** The 742 decree bound "the part of the kingdom of the Franks which is in his power" (MGH 2203–2204), not all the Franks. Correction to the impact line: "...ordered that monks and nuns in the monasteries of his lands...". Add to Do not say: Wrong: "In 742 the Franks made his Rule law" / "all Frankish monasteries". Right: "Carloman's council, for the lands he ruled".

7. **MINOR — impact 1 and Do not say, Aachen 802.** The annals say he gathered "universos abbates et monachos qui ibi aderant" (MGH 7793–7794): all the abbots and monks who were there. "gathered the abbots and monks" can be heard as every abbot in the realm. Correction: "gathered the abbots and monks who were at Aachen". Also, in Who tells it, say the 802 report is from the annals of Lorsch, a chronicle of the time, not the synod's own decree.

8. **MINOR — Who tells it, Paul the Deacon.** "about two centuries later" is about the sack of 589; from Benedict's life it is closer to two and a half centuries (Paul c. 720–799, PAUL 2; sheet witness kinds give 200–260). Correction: "about two and a half centuries after Benedict".

9. **MINOR — Q3, verbatim Latin.** The file reads "regu/am" (GRG-ed 12809), an OCR fault. The outline prints "regulam" silently. Correction, per the sheet's convention: "Nam scripsit monachorum regu/am [regulam], discretione praecipuam, sermone luculentam". The rendering is not affected.

10. **MINOR — impact 3, confidence.** No confidence level is given. Correction: Zacharias's Greek and Werferth's Anglo-Saxon, "Documented as Gardner's account (EDITOR)"; Paul's verses, "Documented (LATER)".

## Checked and sound

- **Quotes.** Q1 (RBL 1240–1245), Q2 (RBL 9527–9528) and the 1608 sentence in Q3 (GRG 5571–5572) are verbatim, with the sheet's bracketed OCR reading in Q1. Opening 3 is verbatim. PAUL 7832–7835 and MGH 2324–2325, 7794–7805 are verbatim.
- **Renderings.** My own renderings, made first: Q1 "So we must set up a school of the Lord's service. In setting it up, we hope to lay down nothing harsh and nothing heavy." Q2 "Let him so temper everything that the strong have something to long for, and the weak do not shrink back." Q3 "For he wrote a rule for monks, outstanding in good judgment and bright in its style." The outline's match in every clause, add nothing, and stay under 25 words. "do not run away" for *non refugiant* and "clear in its language" for *sermone luculentam* are fair; neither misleads.
- **Beats 1, 3, 4, 5 and the death scene** match GRG at the cited lines. The poisoning is "some of them", the glass is kept as a wonder in Gregory's voice, and the move from Subiaco is "to protect his young monks" ("gave place to envy", GRG 4091–4095). Monte Cassino is told in Gregory's terms, with the editors' 528 or 529 labelled.
- **Impact ranking.** The Rule taken up as law by Frankish rulers is rightly heaviest: it is what later witnesses used. DEFENDED (regular discipline at the nearby monastery) is marked at beat 3.
- **Shape, length, name, witnesses.** Monk or ascetic fits; 2½ minutes with a stated reason fits a major figure on a thin life record. "The monks of Italy" does not mislead, and "Benedictine Order" is rightly barred. Who tells it says plainly that Gregory wrote about fifty years later from four disciples.
- **Leave out** is sound: the wonder pile, Paul's ravens and angels, the bones, Estinnes (word supplied by the editor), the Rule of the Master, and Gasquet's claims.
