Near Subiaco, almost forty miles from Rome, a monk named Romanus lowered bread on a long rope down a rock face. A little bell was tied on, so that the young man in the cave below would know his food had come. He was Benedict, and this was Italy, around the year 500, when Gothic kings ruled it.

His life was written down by Pope Gregory the Great, about fifty years after his death, from the memories of four of his monks. Benedict was born in Nursia, in central Italy, and sent to Rome to study. When he saw many students fall into loose living, he gave up his books and his father's house to serve God alone.

For three years he lived in that cave, known only to Romanus, until others came to him for teaching. Monks of a nearby monastery begged him to be their abbot. He warned that their ways and his would never agree, but at last he gave in. When he held them to their rule, some put poison in his wine, but Gregory says the glass shattered as he made the sign of the cross over it.

He built twelve monasteries at Subiaco, and later, around 529 as modern editors date it, he moved to Monte Cassino. He wrote a rule for his monks: "So we must found a school for the Lord's service. In founding it, we hope to lay down nothing harsh and nothing heavy." The Rule calls idleness an enemy of the soul, so it sets times for work, and other set hours for holy reading. The community chooses its abbot, who must keep a balance: "Let him so temper everything that the strong have something to long for, and the weak do not run away."

The Rule survived even the sack of Monte Cassino. In Gregory's own lifetime the Lombards plundered it by night, yet not one monk was taken. Paul the Deacon, writing in the eighth century, adds that the monks fled to Rome "carrying the manuscript of the Holy Rule". In 742 a council of Carloman, ruler of the Franks, ordered the monasteries of his lands to live by the rule of Saint Benedict. In 802, Charlemagne gathered the abbots and monks who were at Aachen, where they read the Rule together. He ordered that whatever had been done in the monasteries against it be put right by it.
