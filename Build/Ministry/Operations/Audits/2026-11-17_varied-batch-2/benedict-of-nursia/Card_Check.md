## The removals

**Totila (Dialogues II.15).** The episode is true as Gregory's account and is on the sheet (M13: GRG 4464–4487; Gardner's dating "probably" 542 and his warning that Gregory could not be impartial about the Goths, GRG-ed 12724–12740). The old card's note that the Latin "Multa mala facis, multa mala fecisti" has no word for "daily" cited the Acta Sanctorum, which is not held; the sheet says Gregory's Latin is not held apart from one sentence (D10). Dropping that note was right. The brief no longer tells the scene, so its quotation record also goes. The episode itself is useful card material, the one scene of Benedict before a ruler, so keeping one line is recommended, not required. For example, in `legacy.remembered`: "Gregory says Totila, king of the Goths, came to him and fell to the ground, and Benedict rebuked him for his wickedness; after that, Gregory says, the king was less cruel. Edmund Gardner warns that Gregory could not see the Goths impartially."

**Gregory on the Rule (Dialogues II.36).** The card still quotes "excellent for discretion and also eloquent for the style" in `legacy.traces[0]`. That is verbatim (GRG 5567–5568), so nothing is misquoted. The old note that the Latin has no "also" was supported: Gardner prints Gregory's Latin, "discretione praecipuam, sermone luculentam" (GRG-ed 12809–12810). Losing the note costs a little precision, not accuracy. Optional.

**Cassian.** The source was removed because the brief no longer draws on the Collations. That is consistent. But one true and useful link went with it. The Rule sends its readers on to "the Collations of the Fathers, their Institutes, their Lives, and the Rule of our Holy Father St. Basil" (RBE 4290–4291), and Edgar Gibson takes the Collations for Cassian's Conferences (CAS 15506–15507). The sheet marks Benedict's debt to Basil and Cassian Widely Accepted (I5). On a family-tree card this link matters. Recommended, not required: add to `uncertain` (and restore the Cassian source entry): "The Rule points its readers on to the Collations, Institutes and Lives of the Fathers and the Rule of Basil. Edgar Gibson, Cassian's translator, takes the Collations to be John Cassian's Conferences; the Rule names no author."
