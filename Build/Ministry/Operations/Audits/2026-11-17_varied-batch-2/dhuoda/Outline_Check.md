## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: "What came of them" item 1, "A copy from her own century survives in fragments found at Nîmes."** Unsupported. Bondurand calls the Nîmes fragments "un manuscrit carolingien" and "un exemplaire ancien" (DH 212–213, 246). He gives no century, and the sheet itself says "gap unknown for N" (I1). **Correction:** "An early copy, from the age of Charlemagne's heirs, survives in fragments found at Nîmes in 1885." Or simply "an early copy". Do not say "from her own century" or "from her lifetime".

2. **MINOR: Beat 7, and the matching Do not say line, "In 844 Bernard was condemned to death for treason on Charles's order."** The annal separates the two acts. The Franks judged him guilty ("Francorum iudicio"). Then, on Charles's order, he "capitalem sententiam subiit": he suffered the death sentence, which means he was put to death (AB 2052–2053). "Condemned" drops the execution. **Correction (in both places):** "In 844 the Franks judged Bernard guilty of treason, and on Charles's order he was put to death." Keep "beheaded" out, as the outline already does.

3. **MINOR: Beat 3, and the Do not say line, "the family's lands and offices in Burgundy."** The sheet's ruling was "no owner" (E7): "honores quos idem in Burgundia habuit" (NIT 2306). "The family's" is a gloss. **Correction:** "if Charles would grant the lands and offices held in Burgundy". Or just "lands and offices in Burgundy".

4. **MINOR: Links, "Elefantus, bishop of Uzès (carried the baby to Bernard)."** The Latin says Bernard had the child brought to him "una cum Elefanto ... et cum ceteris fidelibus suis" (DH 2257–2260). The bishop went with the baby. It does not say he carried him. Beat 2's "with Elefantus" is correct. **Correction:** "Elefantus, bishop of Uzès (the baby was brought to Bernard with him)."

5. **MINOR: "What came of them" item 3, "The older copy reads 'the emperor', and that idea was dropped."** This makes the Nîmes copy the only reason, and it was not. Baluze, Dom Vaissete and Dom Rivet had already shown the idea had no foundation. Baluze had also read "imperatoris" in La Grasse's manuscript itself. The Nîmes copy confirmed them, and Delisle then said the idea must be given up formally (DH 1672–1678, 13195–13220). **Correction:** "Earlier scholars had already doubted it. The older copy reads 'the emperor', and the idea was given up."

6. **MINOR: Q1 rendering, last two sentences.** I rendered it myself first. My rendering is close to theirs throughout, except at the end. In the Latin, her gladness governs the whole clause: she is glad that the book, present though she is absent, will bring to his mind what he ought to do ("gaudens quod ... iste praesens libellus tibi ad mentem reducat"). The outline splits this two ways. Gladness falls only on "this little book is present", and the rest becomes a separate wish ("may it bring"). Separately, "to be of use to you" adds "to you"; "utilitatis desiderio" is a wish for usefulness or benefit. That is a defensible gloss, but it is still an addition. **Correction:**
   - Sentence 3: "...and full of the wish to do you good."
   - Last sentences: "I am glad of this: though I am absent in body, this little book is here with you. When you read it, my son, it will bring to your mind what you ought to do for me."

   Every clause is kept, and nothing runs past 25 words. The Q2 and Q3 renderings match mine. Nothing is added or dropped, and no word misleads.

7. **MINOR: Do not say, missing "hostage".** Many modern retellings call William a hostage at Charles's court. No held source says so: Nithard says he was sent to commend himself (NIT 2304–2308). **Add:** "a hostage" → **"sent to commend himself to Charles, as his sworn man"**.

8. **MINOR: Do not say, missing "she never saw her sons again" or "she wrote on her deathbed".** Both are easy colour, and neither is recorded. She writes of illness and says her time of release is near (DH 11138–11139, 11666). **Add:** "she never saw them again" / "she was dying as she wrote" → **"she writes of illness and says her end is near"**. Nothing records whether she saw them again (E13, E18).

No other omissions or over-tellings found. The Leave out list is sound. Its mention of Bernard's sister and brother is not in the sheet, but because those items are being left out, that is harmless.
