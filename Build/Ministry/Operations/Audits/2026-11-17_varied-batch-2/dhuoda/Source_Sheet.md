# Source sheet: Dhuoda

Researcher's sheet for varied batch 2, 2026-11-17. The writer works from the outline; this sheet holds the evidence behind it.

**Files and short names used below** (all in `cic/texts/` unless noted):
- **DH** = `dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt`. It holds Dhuoda's Latin text (the *Liber manualis*) and the editor Édouard Bondurand's French material: his Introduction (lines 205–1863), a French summary before each chapter, footnotes, and the *Éclaircissements* (12583 onward). Inside his Introduction and notes he quotes Léopold Delisle (1885), the 18th-century Benedictine *Histoire littéraire de la France*, and the 19th-century historians Henri Martin and Émile Mabille.
- **NIT** = `nithard_historiarum-libri-iv_pertz-muller1907.txt` (Nithard, *Histories*, Latin, MGH ed.)
- **AB** = `prudentius-troyes_annales-bertiniani_waitz1883.txt` (*Annals of Saint-Bertin*: Prudentius of Troyes to 861, Hincmar from 861; Latin, MGH ed.)
- **TRE** = `council-of-trent_roman-catechism_mchugh-callan1923.txt` (McHugh and Callan, translators' introduction, English, 1923)
- **PL115** = `eulogius-cordoba_opera-with-alvarus-vita-eulogii-pl115-lat_migne1852.txt` (Migne, *Patrologia Latina* 115, which reprints the *Annals of Saint-Bertin* with Bouquet's 18th-century notes)
- **AS** = `acta-sanctorum_maii-tomus-6-lat_1739.txt` (*Acta Sanctorum*, May vol. 6, 1739; badly garbled OCR)
- **CEN** = `cic-website/data/world-census.json` (not in `cic/texts/`; used only for the setting and the tradition's spoken name)

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own lines. Quotes are exactly as in the file, OCR faults included. Where an OCR fault matters I give the reading in [brackets] after the quote. In DH, NIT and AB, words are often split across lines with a hyphen. Footnote markers such as "(1)" are part of the file and are kept. Editors' margin numbers inside NIT lines ("20", "25") are left in place. In DH, Bondurand often prints a French summary first and the Latin after it. **I render only from the Latin.** His French summaries are paraphrases. In at least one place the French gets the Latin wrong (see D3).

**Witness kinds.** OWN = Dhuoda's own book (841–843). CONTEMP = Nithard (d. 844/845, per the file header, NIT 4) and the *Annals of Saint-Bertin* under Prudentius (kept year by year in these same decades). LATER = editors and scholars, with the rough gap. No LATE-LEGEND source is used. (Bondurand reports one legend, Bernard's stabbing by the king at Toulouse, from "la vieille chronique d'Odon Héribert" (DH 1803–1816). It is not used.)

**The two manuscripts.** Bondurand prints the Paris copy (P), a 17th-century copy of a lost old manuscript from the abbey of La Grasse. Where they survive, he prints the Carolingian fragments found at Nîmes (N) instead (DH 216–226, 276–283). The two copies are independent and agree (DH 402–410). Several key passages (the Prologue, the Preface, chapters I–VII, and the first half of chapter LXVIII) survive **only in P** (DH 357–376, 11141–11143). Their wording rests on one 17th-century copy.

**Census check.** The held chronicles name Bernard and William. I found no mention of Dhuoda in NIT or AB (search for "Dhuoda", "Duoda", "Dodana"). Outside her own book, the earliest held mention of her is in 17th- and 18th-century scholarship.

## 1. Setting

S1. **Her name.** In the Latin her name appears as "Dodanam" (DH 1894), "D[huoda]" (DH 3504) and "Duodane" (DH 11987, 12543). Bondurand explains that the short form "Dhuoda" is the subject form and the longer forms ("Dodana", "Duodana") are the other cases. He rules that in modern French only "Dhuoda" should be used (DH 12586–12626). OWN; LATER (editor). Documented.

S2. **Place: Uzès.** Her second son was born "in Uzecia urbe" (DH 2234). She stayed long in that city: "sub jussioiie senioris mei, in pnedieta ... residerem ui'])e" [sub jussione senioris mei, in praedicta ... residerem urbe: at my lord's command I stayed in the aforesaid city] (DH 2261–2263). Bondurand: the treatise was "écrit à Uzès, de 841 à 843" (DH 217). OWN. Documented.

S3. **Region.** Bernard was "duc de Septimanie" (DH 218–219, 288–289). For the south of France: Bondurand writes of "beaucoup de lieux du Midi" (DH 1663), and he calls the Manual the oldest surviving writing by a woman "nées sur le sol de la France" (DH 1854–1855). LATER (editor, about 1,045 years). Widely Accepted as a modern place-name. Plain spoken form: "Uzès, a town in the south of France".

S4. **Political setting: the empire after Louis.** Her own words: "Volvente itaque et crescente calamitatis miseria hujus sœculi, inter multas fluctua- tiones et discordias regni , imperator prœdictus viam omnium iisse non dii])ium est" [the emperor went the way of all flesh amid the growing misery of this age and the many upheavals and discords of the kingdom] (DH 2227–2230). In her opening verses: "Licet sit discors regni et patrise. / Tu tamen mânes solus immutabilis." [Though kingdom and homeland are in discord, you alone remain unchanging] (DH 2078–2079). The emperor is "Ludoico" / "Ludovici ... imperatori[s]" (DH 2221, 12534–12535), whom Bondurand identifies as Louis the Pious, Charlemagne's son, who died 20 June 840 (DH 2236–2237, 2243). OWN. Documented.

S5. **The war of 841.** Nithard: after "praedicti proelii" [the battle already described], Charles, Louis's son, went to Aquitaine; Bernard, duke of Septimania, had stayed about three leagues from the battlefield and helped neither side (NIT 2302–2304). Bondurand names the battle as Fontenoy ("Fontanet"), fought 25 June 841 (DH 905–906). CONTEMP (Nithard); LATER (editor). Documented.

S6. **Century.** She dates by Louis's reign years and his death (DH 2220–2234, 12534–12535). Bondurand converts these to 824, 826, 841 and 842–843 (DH 2239, 2241, 2251; 13244–13296). Bondurand speaks of Septimania "au IX'' siècle" and "au commencement du ix*" siècle" (DH 617, 650). Plain spoken forms: "in the 840s"; "in the ninth century". Documented.

S7. **Tradition.** Census name: **Carolingian Reform Christianity**, informally "The Carolingian Reform" (CEN 42262, 42264). Its region is "Francia", 751 to 888 (CEN 42266–42268). The census describes it as the church of the empire Charlemagne built, where "they treated the condition of the church as a matter of government" (CEN 42319). It calls Dhuoda "a noblewoman whose handbook of faith, written for her son, is one of very few lay voices to survive from the period" (CEN 42325). The census also notes that hers is "an aristocratic voice, not a non-elite one" (CEN 42303–42305).
(a) Plain spoken forms the record supports: (1) "the Frankish empire that Charlemagne had built" (CEN 42319: "his son Charlemagne built an empire across western Europe"). (2) "the church of Charlemagne's heirs". Support: the emperor in her dates is Louis, Charlemagne's son (DH 2236–2237), and King Charles is the king her son served (DH 2303–2304, 4104). (3) Her own phrase for the times: a kingdom and homeland "in discord" (DH 2078).
(b) Do not say "Carolingian Reform Christianity" aloud. Do not call her an ordinary or common believer: she was a noblewoman and the census calls her voice aristocratic (CEN 42303–42305). "A layperson" or "not a member of the clergy" is supported (CEN 42325). Do not give a general sketch of the reform (schools, script, decrees). That is the census's framing, not her record.

## 2. Life events (all OWN unless marked)

E1. **Marriage, 824.** "Anno féliciter, Christo propitio, XI, domino nostro Ludoico quondam fulgente in imperio (1), concurrente VIII Kalcndarum julii die (2), in Aquisgrani palatio, ad meum dominum tuumque genitorem B[ernardum] legalis in conjugio accessi uxor" (DH 2220–2224). Bondurand's note: "Cela fait le 24 juin 824" (DH 2239). "Aquisgrani palatio" is the palace at Aachen (French "Aix-la-Chapelle", DH 288). Documented. *Lawful wife* ("legalis ... uxor") is her own phrase.

E2. **William's birth, 826.** "et iterum in XIII anno-regni ejus, III Kalendarum decembrium (3), auxiliante, ut credo, Deo, tua ex me, desideratissime fili primogenite, in sseculo processit nativitas" (DH 2224–2227). Note: "Cela fait le 29 novembre 826" (DH 2241). Documented.

E3. **Her slip on Louis's reign.** She says Louis died within the 28th year of his reign (DH 2230–2232). Bondurand shows it must be the 27th (DH 2243–2249). Card only.

E4. **Second son born at Uzès, 841, and taken to his father before baptism.** "Post mortem quoque ejus, in anno sequente, nativitas fratris tui, qui exmeopost te secundus egressus est utero, XI Kalendas aprilis (5) in Uzecia urbe, ... Deo miserante, exorta est. Etenim parvulum illum, ante- quam baptismatis accepisset gratiani, dominiis et genitor utri[us]que vestrum B [ ernardus ], imà cum Elefanto, prsedictae civitatis episcopo (1), et cum cseteris fidelibus suis, in Aquitanise partibus ad suam fecit adduci prsesen- tiam." (DH 2232–2234, 2256–2261). Note: "Cela fait le 22 mars 841" (DH 2251). Meaning: the lord and father of you both, Bernard, had the little one brought to him in Aquitaine, together with Elefantus, bishop of that city, and his other faithful men, before he had received the grace of baptism. Documented. She states no complaint here.

E5. **She stays at Uzès at her lord's command and has the book copied and sent.** "Sed cùm diu, ob abseutiam prsesentiœ vestrse, sub jussioiie senioris mei, in pnedieta cum agone illius jam gaudens residerem ui'])e, ex desiderio utrorumque ves- trum hune codicillum secundùm parvitatis meœ intelli- gentiam tibi transcribi et dirigere curavi" (DH 2261–2265). Documented. The phrase "cum agone illius jam gaudens" is unclear. Bondurand's French gives "où je me suis réjouie de ses prospérités" (DH 2211–2212, preface summary). Leave it out.

E6. **She does not know the baby's name.** "fratremque tuum parvulum, cujus modo inscia suni nominis, cùm bap- timatis in Oliristo acceperit gratiam, insinuare, nutrire, amare, ac de bono in melius provocare non pigeas" (DH 3172–3175) [your little brother, whose name I do not yet know: when he has received the grace of baptism in Christ, do not be slow to teach him, nurture him and love him]. "modo" = now / as yet. Documented. She never names him in the book. The editors name him Bernard: "s'appelait Bernard, comme son père" (DH 12735–12736, Éclaircissement IV). LATER, editor; the held texts give no basis for the name except the editors and Hincmar's "Bernardus, Bernardi quondam tyranni ... filius" for 864 (AB 4496–4497), which does not name his mother. Inferential-Thin for "named Bernard" as far as held evidence goes.

E7. **William commended to King Charles, 841.** Nithard: "victoriam autem ut Karoli esse didicit, filium suum Willelmum ad illum 20 direxit et, si honores. quos idem iu Burgundia habuit, eidem donare vellet, ut se illi commendaret, praecepit" (NIT 2304–2308). [When he learned the victory was Charles's, he sent his son William to him, and ordered him to commend himself to Charles if Charles would grant him the honours (lands and offices) that he held in Burgundy.] CONTEMP. Documented. Whether "idem" (the one who held them) is Bernard or William is not certain from the Latin; Bondurand's Mabille note says the Burgundy lands came to William from Theodoric (DH 12703–12707). Say "lands and offices in Burgundy", no owner. Charles "benigne excepit ... et ... per omnia concessit" [received it kindly and granted everything] (NIT 2311–2313). Dhuoda heard of it: "Audivi enim quod genitor tuus B[ernardus] in manus domini te commendavit Karoli régis" (DH 2303–2304). Documented. "Commend into the hands" = the act of becoming a lord's sworn man.

E8. **She begins the book, 30 November 841.** Subscription: "Incoatio (I) liujus libcUi II ("2) anno oJjiLus Ludovic! coydam imperatori[s] (3) , II kalendas (4j decembri[s], sancti Andreœ missa, incoante sanctum (5) Domini Ad- ventum (6)." [The beginning of this little book: in the second year after the death of Louis, the late emperor, the day before the Kalends of December, at the Mass of Saint Andrew, as the holy Advent of the Lord begins] (DH 12534–12538). Bondurand: "commencé le 30 novembre 841" (DH 13226–13231, Éclaircissement XI). Documented.

E9. **She dictated it and had it written out.** "hoc opusculum ex nomine meo scriptum" (DH 1895–1896); "Finita sunt hujus verba libelli, qua^ ut valui, animo libenti(l) dictavi, et utiliter in tuam specie tenus formam tran(s)scribere jussi." The Paris copy names a scribe: "Et pro scrîplore Wislaberto, qui magno lahore hune codicem scripsit manualcm" (DH 1658–1659; also 12576–12577). Documented.

E10. **Her verses, finished when William turned sixteen, 30 November 842.** "Finiunt versiculi. Deo juvante, annis prseteritis octobinis deductos, incohans(14) december(15), Andrée sancti festa, Adventus Verbi (16)." [The verses end. With God's help, with twice eight years gone by, as December begins, on the feast of Saint Andrew, the Advent of the Word] (DH 11450–11454). Bondurand's note 16 dates it to 30 November 842 (DH 11485–11493). Documented.

E11. **Finished on 2 February; the year is missing.** "Finitus est autem, auxiliante Deo, IIIP nonas (7) februarii (8), Purificationis sancte (9) semper- que virginis Mari;e" (DH 12538–12541). Bondurand: the Nîmes copy shows a gap before the date. Earlier scholars took 842. He argues for 843, because chapter 69 was finished in late 842 (DH 13247–13294). LATER (editor). Confidence for "843": Dominant Modern Reconstruction, the census and corpus map follow it (CEN 114036–114038). Held texts give only Bondurand's argument. Safe spoken form: "she worked on it for more than a year" (from E8 and E10, Documented).

E12. **Her hope of seeing him grown.** Chapter 69, Latin: "Licetjuventus tua, florida virgis quadrans quaternis, com- putaris in (3) annis senioribus ... E13. **Illness and nearness of death.** Chapter 68 (P only): "Sed quia tempus resolutionis non tardât me meum, / Et œgritudo angustiarum corpus undique conterit" [Since my time of release does not delay, and sickness of distress wears down my body on every side] (DH 11138–11139). E16. **Godfather Theodoric.** "Nec hoc pi-setereundum est, fili, de illo qui te, ex meis suscipiens brachiis, per lavacrum regenerationis filium adoptavit in Christo. E17. **Her epitaph, composed by her, which she asks him to have carved.** "ut in loco in quo fuerim sepulta, super ipso tecto sepulchri quod meum(l) operuerit corpus, hos versiculos jubé tran(s)scri- bere" (DH 11966–11968, 11975). ## 3. What she taught (OWN; for beats, not a list)

T1. **Why she writes.** "Cernens plurimos cum suis in sgeculo gaudere proies, et me Dodanam, ofili Willelme, a te elongatam conspiciens procuLobid quasi anxia, et utilitatis desiderio plena, hoc opusculum ex nomine meo scriptum in tuam specie tenus formam legendum dirigo, gaudens quod, si absens sum cor- pore, iste prsesens libellus tibi ad mentem reducat quid ergame, cum legeris, fili, debeas agere." (DH 1893–1899) [proies = proles; procuLobid = procul, ob id; ergame = erga me]. P only. Documented.

T2. **A mirror and a board game.** "ut, veluti tabu- larum lusus maxime juvenibus inter cœteras artium par- tes mundanas congruus et aptus constat ad tempus, vel certè inter aliquas ex parte in sijeculis muliemm demons- tratio apparere soleat vultu, ut sordida extergant, exhiben- tesque nitida, suis in sseculo satagunt placere maritis, ita te opto ut, inter mundanas et seculares actionum turmas oppressus, hune libellum a me tibi directum fréquenter légère, et ob memoriam mei velut in speculis atque tabulis joco, utnounegligas." (DH 2155–2168) [sijeculis = speculis, mirrors; utnounegligas = ut non negligas]. "Tabularum lusus" is a board game; Bondurand says chess ("le jeu des échecs", DH 2127–2128). The Latin says only "board game". P only. Documented.

T3. **Seeking God like a puppy under the table.** "Certè et ego, quanquam indigna fragilisque, adumbram, ut valeo, eum quairo, etejus adjutorium, ut scio et intelligo, indesinen- ter precor. Est enim mihi valde per omnia necesse, nam solet fieri ut aliquoties importuna catula, sub mensa domini sui, inter calulos alteros, micas cadentes valeat carpere et mandere (2)" (DH 2620–2626) [adumbram = ad umbram; quairo = quaero; calulos = catulos]. She goes on: under God's table, within the holy Church, she can see the "catulos, hoc est sancti altaris ministres" [the puppies, that is, the ministers of the holy altar], and gather crumbs of spiritual understanding "mihi et tibi, o pulcher fili W[illelme]" (DH 2644–2647). Bondurand's note links the image to the Gospel's "catelli" [little dogs] eating crumbs (DH 2632–2635). P only. Documented.

T4. **No teacher will teach like a mother.** "Fili, habebis doctores qui te plura et ampliora utilitatis doceant documenta, sed non jfiquali conditione, animo ardentis in pectore, sicut ego genitrix tua." (DH 3168–3171) [jfiquali = aequali]. P only. Documented. Just before: "si defuerim deflciens, qiiod fiiturum est, habes hic memoriale libellum" [if I fail and am gone, as will happen, you have here a little book to remember me by] (DH 3164–3165).

T5. **Prayer, short and pure.** "Ego autem tepida et desidiosa D[huoda] fra- gilisque et declinans semper ad yma, non solum longa sed nec brevis mihi delectatur oratio." [But I, Dhuoda, lukewarm and lazy, frail and always sinking low, take no delight in prayer, not long, not even short.] She tells him to pray "in brevi et lixo puroque ser- mone" [in short, fixed, pure words] (DH 3504–3509). P only. Documented.

T6. **Loyalty to Charles.** "Seniorem quem habes K[arolum]. quandoDeus, ut credo, et genitor tuus B [ernardus], in tuse inchoationis juventute florigeram vigorem tibi ad serviendum elegit (2), adhuc tene quod est generis ex magno utrumque nobi- litatis exorto progenie (3), non ita serviens ut tantum pla- ceas oculis, sed etiam sensui capax" (DH 4104–4110). Documented.

T7. **Her summary verses (ch. 69).** Their first letters spell "VERSI AD VVILLELMVM F(ilium)" (DH 460–462; N's note, 11319–11321). Among them: fear and love God first, then "genitorem tuum inde per cuncta" [your father after that, in all things] (DH 11310–11312); love the great, stand level with the humble, "junge hencvolis" [join the kind], avoid the proud (DH 11357–11359); "Viduis et piipillis subleva frequens, et peregrinis victum potumque largire, para hospitia, nudis namque vesti- tum porrige manum." [Often help widows and orphans; give food and drink to pilgrims; prepare lodging; hold out clothing to the naked.] (DH 11364–11366); be a just judge, take no gifts (DH 11368–11370). Documented.

T8. **Read it, and teach your brother.** "atque hune codicillum manualis a me comprehensum, et in tuo nomine conscriptum, cùm perfectum loquendi vel legendi acceperit tempus, illi ostende, et admone legendo." (DH 3175–3178). Documented.

T9. **Her opening verses are an acrostic.** Delisle found that their first letters spell "DHVODA DILECTO FILIO VVILHELMO SALVÏEM [SALVTEM] LEGE" [Dhuoda to her beloved son William, greeting: read] (DH 435–438). She tells the reader: "A littera delta incipe legendo ; / Moida hactenus conclusa sunt." [Begin reading from the letter delta (D); up to M it is closed] (DH 2107–2108). In the same verses she calls herself "fragilis et exul" [frail and an exile] (DH 1995). LATER (Delisle, 1885) for the finding; OWN for the verses. Documented.

T10. **Her reading.** Bondurand: the Manual paraphrases Pliny the Elder and quotes Prudentius, Donatus, Ammonius and countless Bible passages (DH 485–490); he found her Prudentius lines in the *Cathemerinon* (DH 1169–1171). LATER (editor). Documented as his identification. Card.

## 4. Impact as the record gives it (heaviest first)

I1. **The book was copied and kept.** A Carolingian copy (32 leaves of fragments found at Nîmes in 1885) and a 17th-century copy made from an old manuscript of the abbey of La Grasse, since lost (DH 208–226, 228). The two are independent and agree, which "met ... hors de toute discussion" the text's authenticity (DH 402–410). The Paris copy keeps the name of a scribe, Wislabertus, "qui magno lahore hune codicem scripsit" (DH 1658–1659). LATER witnesses (copyists, gap unknown for N; 17th c. for P). Documented. *Defended*: Delisle and Bondurand defend the text's authenticity from the two copies.

I2. **17th- and 18th-century scholars printed and praised parts of it.** Delisle (quoted by Bondurand): only "la préface, la table et treize chapitres publiés en 1677 par Mabillon" were known; "Bal'jze avait, de son côté, en 1688, fait im- primer la préface et la souscription finale"; the *Histoire littéraire* regretted it was not printed whole; Migne reprinted Mabillon's fragments in the *Patrologia latina* (DH 296–306). The Benedictines of the *Histoire littéraire*: « Un monument respectable de literature et de piété, qu'on a de la façon de cette Dame, lui mérite à juste titre une place dans notre histoire littéraire » (DH 348–351), and "Nous n'avons point d'écrit de ce temps-là dont la date soit mieux connue et plus certaine" (DH 1688–1694). An *Acta Sanctorum* note of 1739 mentions "Dodana, uxrr [uxor] Bernardi, Cujuc [cujus] liber ... ad Wilbelmum filium ... extat apud Mabilipatm [Mabillonium]" (AS 33161–33163, garbled). LATER (about 830–900 years). Documented. *Defended*: the *Histoire littéraire* defends the work's worth against its neglect (DH 353–356).

I3. **An error corrected: she was not Charlemagne's daughter.** The Paris copy reads "Ludovici quondam mei fralris" [of Louis, formerly my brother], so "Plusieurs auteurs avaient donc considéré Dhuoda comme fille de Charlemagne" (DH 1672–1674). The Nîmes copy reads "Ludovici aondam imperalori[s]" [of Louis, the late emperor] (DH 1676). Delisle: « Il faut donc renoncer formellement à l'idée de mettre Dhuoda au nombre des enfants de Charleraasne. » (DH 13219–13220). Delisle: "Dhuoda, dont l'origine est inconnue" (DH 285). LATER. Documented. *Defended*: Baluze, dom Vaissete and dom Rivet had already rejected it (DH 13197–13199).

I4. **The first full edition, 1887, and the editor's claim.** Bondurand: "De tous les écrits dûs à des femmes nées sur le sol de la France, le Manuel de Dhuoda est le plus ancien qui nous soit parvenu. En le tirant d'un sommeil de plus de dix siècles" (DH 1854–1857). He calls it a text "aussi important pour l'histoire de l'éducation en France" (DH 1860–1861). LATER (about 1,045 years). The claim is his. Contested/Inferential-Thin as a general claim; say it as his.

I5. **Used in a defence of catechesis before Luther, 1923.** Answering "the further claim that the catechism originated with Luther", the translators list works of popular instruction "From the ninth century on", including "the manual of Dhuoda to his son, explaining Christian faith and morality (9th cent.)" (TRE 812–830). LATER (about 1,080 years). Documented as their use. Note the error "his son". *Defended*: they use it to defend the claim that Christian instruction for ordinary people existed before Luther.

I6. **Modern framing.** The census: "one of very few lay voices to survive from the period" (CEN 42325). Card; the census is the project's own framing, not a historical witness.

## 5. Links to other people

- **Bernard of Septimania** — her husband; married at Aachen, 824 (DH 2220–2224). She calls him "meum dominum" and "senioris mei" (DH 2223, 2262). Prudentius recorded his death sentence (AB 2051–2053).
- **William** — her elder son; the book is written to him (DH 1893–1894; subscription "liber manualis Wilelmi", DH 12544–12545). Nithard recorded his sending to Charles (NIT 2304–2306); Prudentius recorded his death (AB 2518–2520).
- **The younger son** — born at Uzès 841; she did not know his name (DH 3172–3173). Editors call him Bernard (DH 12710–12715).
- **Charles (the Bald)** — king; William's lord: "domini ... Karoli régis" (DH 2304); "Seniorem quem habes K[arolum]" (DH 4104).
- **Louis the Pious** — the emperor in her dates (DH 2220–2221, 12534–12535).
- **Elefantus, bishop of Uzès** — carried the baby to Bernard (DH 2258–2259).
- **Theodoric** — William's godfather, now dead (DH 10536–10539); on her prayer list (DH 11844).
- **Heribert** — William's uncle, to be added to the list when he dies (DH 11876–11877).
- **Wislabertus** — the scribe named in the Paris copy (DH 1658–1659).
- **Prudentius (the poet)** — she quoted his book (DH 1169–1171, Bondurand's identification).
- **Mabillon, Baluze** — printed parts of her book (DH 296–301).
- **Léopold Delisle** — read her acrostic and settled her parentage question (DH 435–438; 13219–13220).
- **Édouard Bondurand** — edited her whole book (DH 1854–1861).

## 6. Disagreements, late sources, and traps

D1. **The year of completion.** 842 (older view, from the Paris subscription) vs 843 (Bondurand, from the gap in N and the date in ch. 69) (DH 13254–13296). Say "more than a year".

D2. **William's age when commended.** Delisle says she wrote for William "alors âgé de quinze ans" at the end of 841 (DH 290–291). By her dates he turned fifteen on 29 November 841 (E2). Nithard places the commendation soon after the battle of June 841 (NIT 2302–2306) and gives no age. So he was fourteen then, by arithmetic only. Do not give his age at the commendation.

D3. **"Cat" vs "puppy".** Bondurand's French says "une chatte importune" (DH 2608, 1015–1016). The Latin is "importuna catula ... inter calulos [catulos] alteros" (DH 2624–2625). "Catula" means a young female dog, and his own note ties the image to the Gospel's little dogs (DH 2632–2635). The Latin governs: a puppy, a little dog.

D4. **How Bernard died.** The annal says only "capitalem sententiam subiit" (AB 2053). "Beheaded" is Bondurand/Mabille (DH 1800–1801). The Toulouse stabbing is a legend from a later chronicle (DH 1803–1816).

D5. **Her origins.** Unknown (DH 285, 644–645). Bondurand guesses at a Septimanian family from a name in an 813 will (DH 613–645). Leave out.

D6. **The empress Judith and Bernard.** Bondurand reprints Henri Martin's 19th-century account of Bernard's affair with the empress Judith (DH 658–812). LATER, gossip-history; nothing in her book. Leave out.

D7. **"Relégation" and "exile".** Bondurand calls her stay at Uzès "la relégation qu'elle subissait" (DH 919–920). Her own words: she stayed long "sub jussione senioris mei" (DH 2261–2263), and in verse she calls herself "exul" (DH 1995). Use her words, not his.

D8. **Bondurand's French summaries of P-only passages.** Several summaries soften or add (e.g. "chess" for "board game", T2; "cat" for "puppy", D3). Render from the Latin.

## 7. Wanted but not held

- Carol Neel's English translation (cited by the census, CEN 42303) is not held.
- No held source of her own time names Dhuoda outside her book.
- No held source records her death, burial, or whether the epitaph was carved.
- No held source shows the book being read or used between the 9th century and Mabillon (1677), beyond the existence of the copies.
