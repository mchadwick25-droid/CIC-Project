## Step 1. Three lines from one hearing

The brief was read once, straight through, before anything else.

- **Who was this?** Dhuoda, a Frankish noblewoman of the 840s, living at Uzès in the south of France. She was the wife of Bernard, a great lord of the south, and the mother of two sons. She was not one of the clergy.
- **What happened?** Civil war broke out among Charlemagne's grandsons. Her husband took her newborn second son away before he was baptized, and her elder son William was sent to serve King Charles. Left behind at Uzès, she wrote a handbook of faith for William. Later Bernard was put to death for treason, and William was killed at Barcelona. No record tells what became of her.
- **Why does the connection matter?** Her book was copied and kept, praised by later scholars, and printed whole in 1887 as the oldest writing by a French-born woman. In 1923 it was used in an argument about Luther and the catechism. What that argument was, and what her book stood for in it, I had to guess.

## Where the brief loses it

1. **Paragraph 3:** "She was a noblewoman, not one of the clergy, yet she claims her place as his teacher." This says she was not a cleric, but never says why that matters: hers is one of very few such voices that survive from her church. Without that, "not one of the clergy" sounds like a detail, not the reason the book counts.
2. **Last sentence:** "In 1923, two translators of the Catholic catechism named 'the manual of Dhuoda' to answer the claim that the catechism began with Luther." The brief drops what the translators named her book *as*: one of the works of Christian instruction from the ninth century on. So the brief ends on an argument whose point the listener has to supply. This is where the inheritance ought to land, and it is the hardest sentence in the brief to follow on one hearing.

## Smallest fix (outline material only)

1. Paragraph 3, after "...yet she claims her place as his teacher.", add:
   > Almost every voice that reaches us from the church of her day belongs to the clergy. Hers does not.

   (Outline, Thread: "one of very few voices from the church of Charlemagne's heirs that does not belong to the clergy", S7.)

2. Replace the last sentence with:
   > In 1923, two translators of the Catholic catechism named "the manual of Dhuoda" among the works that taught the faith to ordinary Christians from the ninth century on. With it they answered the claim that the catechism began with Luther.

   (Outline, What came of them 5: "among the works of Christian instruction from the ninth century on ... *Defended:* instruction for ordinary people before Luther", I5.)
