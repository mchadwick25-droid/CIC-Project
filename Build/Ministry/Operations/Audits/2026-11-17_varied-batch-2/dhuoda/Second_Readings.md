# Second Readings: Dhuoda

## 1. I see many people in this world
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 1893-1894
Second rendering: I see that many people in this world have the joy of their children with them. But I, Dhuoda, see myself kept far from you, my son William.
Ours: I see many people in this world who rejoice in their children with them. But I, Dhuoda, see myself kept far away from you, my son William.
Verdict: same
Reason: Both carry "cernens plurimos ... gaudere proles", "cum suis", and "a te elongatam". Ours drops the vocative "O", which carries no sense.

## 2. in discord
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 2078
Second rendering: Though kingdom and homeland are torn by discord.
Ours: in discord
Verdict: same
Reason: The fragment keeps the key word; the brief supplies "her kingdom and homeland" around it, so "licet" and the two nouns are carried by the narration.

## 3. at my lord's command
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 2261-2262
Second rendering: at the order of my lord
Ours: at my lord's command
Verdict: minor
Reason: Same sense; "senior" here is her husband Bernard. Heard aloud, "my lord" could be taken as God, but the brief names Bernard in the sentence before, which holds the sense.

## 4. My son, you will have teachers
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 3168-3171
Second rendering: My son, you will have teachers who will teach you more and greater lessons of use to you. But they will not do it on the same footing, with a heart burning in the breast, as I do, your mother.
Ours: My son, you will have teachers who will teach you more lessons, and greater ones, for your good. But they will not teach on equal terms, with a heart burning in the breast, as I do, your mother.
Verdict: same
Reason: "For your good" fairly renders "utilitatis"; "on equal terms" renders "aequali conditione". Every clause is carried.

## 5. a respectable monument of literature and piety
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 348
Second rendering: a monument of literature and piety worthy of respect
Ours: a respectable monument of literature and piety
Verdict: changes meaning
Reason: In 18th-century French "respectable" means worthy of respect, and the Benedictines go on to say it earns the book a place in their literary history. In present-day English "respectable" reads as merely decent or adequate, which turns praise into faint praise. Smallest fix: "a monument of literature and piety worthy of respect".

## Release 50 fixes

## 7. Bernard had him taken from her and brought to him in Aquitaine (new narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 2232-2234, 2256-2261
Second rendering: Your brother, my second child after you, was born at the city of Uzès. Before that little one had received the grace of baptism, my lord, the father of you both, had him brought into his presence in the region of Aquitaine, with Elephantus, bishop of that city, and his other followers.
Ours: In 841 she bore a second son at Uzès. Before the child was baptized, Bernard had him taken from her and brought to him in Aquitaine, with the bishop of Uzès.
Verdict: minor
Reason: The Latin says Bernard "had him brought into his presence"; "taken from her" is not her wording but is the plain fact the fix list asks for, since the baby was with her at Uzès and went to Bernard. It carries no complaint and none of the outline's barred verbs. Place, bishop and the timing before baptism are all carried.

## 8. When she wrote, she did not yet know the baby's name (new narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 3172-3175
Second rendering: your little brother, whose name I do not know as yet
Ours: When she wrote, she did not yet know the baby's name.
Verdict: same
Reason: "Cujus modo inscia sum nominis" is carried, in the outline's allowed form (E6).

## 9. she asked him to read it as often as one looks in a mirror (reworded narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt 2155-2163
Second rendering: Just as women look at their faces in mirrors to wipe away the dirt and show themselves bright, to please their husbands, so I wish that you, pressed by the crowd of worldly affairs, will read this little book I have sent you often, and, in memory of me, not neglect it, as with mirrors and board games.
Ours: She dictated it and had it written out and sent to him, and she asked him to read it as often as one looks in a mirror.
Verdict: minor
Reason: "Te opto" (I wish you) is carried as "she asked him"; "frequenter legere … velut in speculis" is fairly compressed to "as often as one looks in a mirror". The board-game half of her image is dropped, as in the released text. Meaning kept.

## 10. two translators … named "the manual of Dhuoda" (reworded narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/council-of-trent_roman-catechism_mchugh-callan1923.txt 812-830
Second rendering: But what of the claim that the catechism began with Luther? … From the ninth century on, many works of popular instruction have come down to us … among them the manual of Dhuoda to her son, explaining Christian faith and morality.
Ours: In 1923, two translators of the Catholic catechism set out to answer the claim that the catechism began with Luther. Among their examples of Christian instruction from the ninth century on, they named "the manual of Dhuoda".
Verdict: same
Reason: The narrator now reports only what they did: the claim they answered and the example they named. The "because it showed" and "ordinary people" are gone; "popular" is left unsaid, which removes the misleading sense without adding a claim.
