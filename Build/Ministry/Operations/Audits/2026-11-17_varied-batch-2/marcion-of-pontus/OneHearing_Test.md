# One-hearing test: Marcion of Pontus

Brief: `marcion-of-pontus.brief.txt` (364 words). Test: "An interested non-specialist can understand who this was, what happened, and why the connection matters after one hearing." Mark: "i don't want simpler."

## Step 1: three lines from one hearing (brief only, read once)

- **Who was this?** Marcion, a shipmaster from Pontus on the Black Sea, who joined the church at Rome in the middle of the second century and became a teacher the church fought hard against.
- **What happened?** Troubled by where evil comes from, he taught (Tertullian says) that the Creator of the Hebrew Scriptures was a different god from the good God shown in Christ; he set the gospel against the law of Moses, and his opponents say he shortened Luke and Paul, while he said he was restoring them. Rome expelled him, and his gift with him.
- **Why does the connection matter?** The church answered him for generations, arguing that one God made the world and sent Jesus and defending four Gospels; yet his followers spread across many nations and were still there two centuries later.

## Step 2: judged against `Outline.md`

| Line | Judgment | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Who | CLEAR | Matches the outline's setting and beat 1. The opening scene names Marcion before saying who he is, and paragraph 2's "a shipmaster from Pontus" is tied to the name only by the next sentence. A listener makes the link without effort, so this holds. |
| What happened | CLEAR | Beats 2-6 all land, each credited to its speaker, with his own claim given ("he was restoring it"). |

The brief carries the answer plainly but never says plainly that Marcion founded a church of his own. That fact rides only on Tertullian's wasp image ("just as wasps make combs, so these Marcionites make churches"). Heard once, perhaps in a second language, a metaphor is the weakest carrier for the key fact, and "Marcionites" is never named as his followers before it. A listener hears "people followed him" but may miss that a separate branch of churches split off the tree and lasted. |

## Verdict

**FAIL** (one line WEAK).

## Fix

**Where it is lost:** last paragraph, "Yet Justin saw people of every nation follow Marcion. Tertullian wrote that just as wasps make combs, so these Marcionites make churches."

**Smallest fix** (outline S4 and the Thread; adds about 12 words, brief stays near 376, well under 450):

> Yet Justin saw people of every nation follow Marcion, and Marcion founded churches of his own, the churches of the Marcionites. Tertullian wrote that just as wasps make combs, so these Marcionites make churches.

This states the fact plainly, then lets the wasp image colour it rather than carry it. It adds substance, not simplification.

**Optional, not needed for the verdict:** in paragraph 5, "cut down Luke's Gospel" follows the brief's good-tree/bad-tree image, so a second-language listener may hear "cut down" as felling. The outline's own RIGHT wording is "shortened" (Do not say, "ten letters of Paul"): "His opponents say he shortened Luke's Gospel and Paul's letters."
