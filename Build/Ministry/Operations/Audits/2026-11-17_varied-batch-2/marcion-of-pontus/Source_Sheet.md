# Source sheet: Marcion of Pontus

Researcher's sheet for the varied-batch-2 Who brief, 2026-11-17. The writer uses the outline and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

**Files and short names used below** (all in `cic/texts/` unless marked):
- **ANF** = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml` (Justin, *First Apology*; Irenaeus, *Against Heresies*; Polycarp, *To the Philippians*; English)
- **JG** = `justin-martyr_first-apology-grc_rauschen1911.txt` (Justin, *First Apology*, Greek)
- **T** = `anf03_tertullian.xml` (Tertullian, *Prescription against Heretics*, *Against Marcion* I–V; the anonymous *Against All Heresies* printed as an appendix; English)
- **OEH** = `tertullian_quae-supersunt-t2-polemica-lat_oehler1853.txt` (Tertullian, the same works, Latin, OCR)
- **EUS** = `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` (Eusebius, *Church History*, tr. McGiffert)
- **EPI** = `epiphanius_panarion-haer-34-64-gcs-bd2-grc_holl1922.txt` (Epiphanius, *Panarion* 42, Greek, ed. Holl, OCR)
- **CEN** = `cic-website/data/world-census.json` (not a source; used only for the tradition's spoken name)

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own lines. English quotes are printed exactly as in the file, square brackets included (they are the translator's). Editors' note markers, the notes themselves and XML tags are left out of quotes; where a note matters it is given after the quote and called a note. In the OCR files (OEH, EPI) I joined words split across lines and left out stray marks, note markers and running heads; OCR faults are corrected in [brackets]. Chapter titles in T and EUS are the editors', not the authors'.

**No word of Marcion's own is held.** Tertullian says Marcion wrote the *Antitheses* and at least one letter (T 22580–22581, 29245–29248). Neither is in the Library. Every source below is an opponent's. This is the main limit on the story.

**Time gaps.** Rough estimates for the card, from the usual modern dates of the writers; not story material. Justin wrote while Marcion was alive. Irenaeus wrote while Eleutherius was bishop of Rome (ANF 39849–39851), about a generation later. Tertullian's first book against Marcion is dated "A.D. 207" by the editor's note (T 22488–22489), about 60 years later. Epiphanius wrote about two centuries later.

**Witness kinds used.** CONTEMP = Justin, writing at Rome in Marcion's lifetime. LATER = a writer after his death, with a rough gap. LATE-LEGEND = a hostile story told only by late writers, with no earlier support (the bishop's-son story, D3).

## 1. Setting

S1. **His name and home.** Justin: "Marcion, a man of Pontus" (ANF 14943); Greek "Μαρκίωνα δέ τινα Ποντικόν" (JG 729–730). "Marcion of Pontus" (ANF 16131; Irenaeus, ANF 33572). Tertullian: "that shipmaster of Pontus" (T 21331–21332). Tertullian names the sea of Pontus as "The Euxine Sea, as it is called" (T 22516) and says "Marcion was born there" (T 22554–22555). CONTEMP; LATER. Documented. The city of Sinope is named only by Epiphanius (EPI 7476–7477; D3).

S2. **Century.** Justin addresses his *Apology* "To the Emperor Titus Ælius Adrianus Antoninus Pius" (ANF 13961–13962) and says Marcion "is even at this day alive" (ANF 14943–14944). Irenaeus: Marcion "flourished under Anicetus, who held the tenth place of the episcopate" (ANF 39983–39984). Tertullian: "in the reign of Antoninus for the most part" (T 21334–21335); "a heretic of the Antonine period, impious under the pious" (T 23722–23723). CONTEMP; LATER. Documented as their placing. That this is the **middle of the second century** comes from modern standard chronology, not from a held file: Widely Accepted. No held source gives a year for any event in his life (see N1).

S3. **Place.** He taught at Rome. Tertullian: he and Valentinus "at first were believers in the doctrine of the Catholic Church, in the church of Rome" (T 21335–21336). Irenaeus places Cerdo and Marcion by the bishops of Rome (ANF 39970–39984). Justin wrote his *Apology* to the emperor and Senate at Rome (ANF 13961–13965). LATER; CONTEMP. Documented. Note: "Catholic" here is the translator's English for Tertullian's "catholicae" (OEH 1724), meaning the whole church, not the later denomination.

S4. **Tradition.** The census movement is **"Marcion / Marcionism"**, informally **"Marcion and the Marcionites"** (CEN 33242, 33244); its own sourcing note: "Substantial, almost entirely via opponents" (CEN 33274). Plain spoken forms the record supports: (1) **"the church at Rome"**, for where he began (T 21335–21336). (2) **"the Marcionites"** or **"the churches that followed Marcion"**, for what he founded: "so also these Marcionites make churches" (T 29329–29330); "those called Marcionites, from the heresy of Marcion" (EUS 29928). Do not call his churches "the Marcionite Church" as one body with a head; the record says only that they made churches and later split into schools (I21).

S5. **His trade.** "that shipmaster of Pontus, the zealous student of Stoicism" (Tertullian, T 21331–21332; Latin "Ponticus nauclerus, Stoicae studiosu[s]", OEH 1722). Rhodo, writing later in the second century, as quoted by Eusebius: "as does the mariner Marcion himself" (EUS 29074–29086). LATER. Documented as their description. Nothing held says he was rich or owned ships; "shipmaster" (nauclerus) is the word.

## 2. Recorded moments

M1. **Before the break: he believed with the church at Rome.** Tertullian: "His disciples will not deny that his first faith he held along with ourselves; a letter of his own proves this" (T 22580–22581). And: "Marcion himself once believed it, when in the first warmth of faith he contributed money to the Catholic church, which along with himself was afterwards rejected, when he fell away from our truth into his own heresy" (T 29239–29243). Latin: "cum et pecuniam in primo calo[re] fidei catholicae ecclesiae contulit, proiectam mox cum ipso" (OEH 8454–8455): the money was "cast out soon, together with him". LATER (about 60 years). Documented as Tertullian's account. Note: the text does not say the money was handed back; it says it was rejected with him.

M2. **The gift.** "Marcion, indeed, [went] with the two hundred sesterces which he had brought into the church" (T 21339–21341). Latin "cum ducentis sestertiis quae ecclesiae intulerat" (OEH 1776). The "[went]" is the translator's. LATER. Documented as Tertullian's account. The file gives no value or modern equivalent for the sum.

M3. **The question he started from (Tertullian's account).** He took "the first idea of his conceit" from the Lord's saying that "the good tree bringeth not forth corrupt fruit, neither the corrupt tree good fruit" (T 22613–22619). "while morbidly brooding over the question of the origin of evil ... when he found the Creator declaring, “I am He that createth evil,” ... he now applied to the Creator the figure of the corrupt tree bringing forth evil fruit ... and then presumed that there ought to be another god, after the analogy of the good tree producing its good fruit" (T 22621–22632). "finding in Christ a different disposition, as it were—one of a simple and pure benevolence—differing from the Creator, he readily argued that in his Christ had been revealed a new and strange divinity" (T 22632–22639). LATER. Documented as Tertullian's reconstruction of Marcion's reasoning; it is a hostile reading, and how far it matches Marcion's own reasons is Inferential-Thin. The anonymous *Against All Heresies* gives the same starting text, "Every good tree beareth good fruit, but an evil evil" (T 55767–55769).

M4. **What he taught (all witnesses agree on the core).**
- Justin, in Marcion's lifetime: "teaching his disciples to believe in some other god greater than the Creator" (ANF 14944–14945); "teaching men to deny that God is the maker of all things in heaven and on earth, and that the Christ predicted by the prophets is His Son, and preaches another god besides the Creator of all, and likewise another son" (ANF 16131–16135). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Irenaeus: Marcion declared the God "proclaimed as God by the law and the prophets" "to be the author of evils, to take delight in war, to be infirm of purpose, and even to be contrary to Himself" (ANF 33574–33576); Jesus came from "that father who is above the God that made the world", "abolishing the prophets and the law, and all the works of that God who made the world" (ANF 33577–33582). LATER (a generation). Documented as Irenaeus's report.
- Irenaeus: "Salvation will be the attainment only of those souls which had learned his doctrine; while the body, as having been taken from the earth, is incapable of sharing in salvation" (ANF 33596–33598). LATER. Documented as report.
- Tertullian: "Marcion's special and principal work is the separation of the law and the gospel" (T 23734–23736); Latin "Separatio legis et evangelii proprium et principale opus est Marcionis" (OEH 4337–4338). "These are Marcion's Antitheses, or contradictory propositions, which aim at committing the gospel to a variance with the law" (T 23738–23741). LATER. Documented as report. ("The law" = the law of Moses and the Hebrew Scriptures; "the gospel" = the good news of Christ and the book that tells it.)
- Rhodo (via Eusebius): some followers "hold to two principles, as does the mariner Marcion himself" (EUS 29070–29086). LATER. Documented as report.

M5. **His Scripture: one Gospel, a shortened Paul.**
- Irenaeus: "he mutilates the Gospel which is according to Luke, removing all that is written respecting the generation of the Lord" (ANF 33581–33584); "In like manner, too, he dismembered the Epistles of Paul" (ANF 33589–33590). "this man is the only one who has dared openly to mutilate the Scriptures" (ANF 33615–33617). LATER. Documented as report.
- Irenaeus: "Marcion and his followers have betaken themselves to mutilating the Scriptures, not acknowledging some books at all; and, curtailing the Gospel according to Luke and the Epistles of Paul, they assert that these are alone authentic, which they have themselves thus shortened" (ANF 41660–41668). LATER.
- Tertullian: "Marcion, on the other hand, you must know, ascribes no author to his Gospel" (T 29088–29090); "of the authors whom we possess, Marcion seems to have singled out Luke for his mutilating process" (T 29097–29103). He "rejects his Apocalypse" (John's, T 29308–29309). He accepted the short letter to Philemon but "rejected the two epistles to Timothy and the one to Titus" (T 40391–40395). He titled Ephesians "to the Laodiceans" (T 39599–39601). LATER. Documented as report.
- The held texts do not give a count of the letters he kept (see Do-not-say in the outline).

M6. **His reason for editing (what he defended).**
- Tertullian reports Marcion's own argument from the *Antitheses*: the Gospel current in the church "was interpolated by the defenders of Judaism, for the purpose of such a conglomeration with it of the law and the prophets as should enable them out of it to fashion their Christ" (T 29249–29255). LATER. Documented as Tertullian's report of Marcion's claim.
- His followers' defence: "they allege that Marcion did not so much innovate on the rule (of faith) by his separation of the law and the gospel, as restore it after it had been previously adulterated" (T 23767–23770). LATER. **Defended by the Marcionites.**
- He appealed to Paul's letter to the Galatians, "wherein he rebukes even apostles for “not walking uprightly according to the truth of the gospel,”" (T 29149–29151), "in order, forsooth, to secure for his own Gospel the credit which he takes away from them" (T 29158–29159). Irenaeus: "He likewise persuaded his disciples that he himself was more worthy of credit than are those apostles who have handed down the Gospel to us" (ANF 33586–33588). LATER.

M7. **His discipline: no marriage.** "The flesh is not, according to Marcion, immersed in the water of the sacrament, unless it be in virginity, widowhood, or celibacy, or has purchased by divorce a title to baptism" (T 24533–24537). "he who has abolished the nuptial bond" (T 22574). Tertullian on Apelles, who "forsook the continence of Marcion, by resorting to the company of a woman" (T 21359–21361). LATER. Documented as report. ("The sacrament" here = baptism.)

M8. **Polycarp and Marcion.** Irenaeus: "And Polycarp himself replied to Marcion, who met him on one occasion, and said, “Dost thou know me?” “I do know thee, the first-born of Satan.”" (ANF 39882–39884). Irenaeus adds: "Such was the horror which the apostles and their disciples had against holding even verbal communication with any corrupters of the truth" (ANF 39884–39886). Eusebius, quoting Irenaeus: "‘Knowest thou us?’" ... "replied, ‘I know the first born of Satan.’" (EUS 22977–22982). LATER (Irenaeus knew Polycarp: "whom I also saw in my early youth", ANF 39860). Documented as Irenaeus's report. Place not given: Irenaeus says only "on one occasion". The sentence just before tells of Polycarp "coming to Rome in the time of Anicetus" (ANF 39872); that the meeting was at Rome is Inferential-Thin. Note: Polycarp's own letter uses "the first-born of Satan" of anyone who "perverts the oracles of the Lord to his own lusts" (ANF 3437–3439), without naming Marcion.

M9. **Expelled, more than once.** Tertullian, of Marcion and Valentinus together: "until on account of their ever restless curiosity, with which they even infected the brethren, they were more than once expelled" (T 21337–21339); "when banished at last to a permanent excommunication, they scattered abroad the poisons of their doctrines" (T 21342–21344). LATER. Documented as Tertullian's account. Note: "more than once" is said of both men together. No held text gives the year.

M10. **Offered peace, died first.** "Afterwards, it is true, Marcion professed repentance, and agreed to the conditions granted to him—that he should receive reconciliation if he restored to the church all the others whom he had been training for perdition: he was prevented, however, by death" (T 21344–21348). LATER (about 60 years). Inferential-Thin: one witness only, in a passage whose bishop is wrong by Irenaeus's list (D1).

M11. **Cerdo before him.** Irenaeus: "Cerdo was one who took his system from the followers of Simon, and came to live at Rome in the time of Hyginus" (ANF 33564–33566); "Marcion of Pontus succeeded him, and developed his doctrine" (ANF 33572–33573). "Cerdon, too, Marcion's predecessor, himself arrived in the time of Hyginus, who was the ninth bishop" (ANF 39970–39971). LATER. Documented as report.

## 3. What came of him (ranked heaviest first: what later witnesses carried on and used)

I1. **His churches spread and lasted.** Weight: every witness from his lifetime to two centuries later attests followers.
- Justin, in his lifetime: "he, by the aid of the devils, has caused many of every nation to speak blasphemies" (ANF 14945–14946); "And this man many have believed, as if he alone knew the truth, and laugh at us" (ANF 16135–16136). CONTEMP. Documented.
- Tertullian: "It too, of course, has its churches, but specially its own—as late as they are spurious ... Even wasps make combs; so also these Marcionites make churches" (T 29323–29330); Latin "Faciunt favos et vespae, faciunt ecclesias et Marcionita[e]" (OEH 8490). LATER. Documented.
- Epiphanius: "ἡ δὲ αἵρεσις ἔτι καὶ νῦν ἔν τε Ῥώμῃ καὶ ἐν τῇ Ἰταλίᾳ, ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ τε καὶ ἐν Παλαιστίνῃ, ἐν Ἀραβίᾳ τε καὶ ἐν τῇ Συρίᾳ, ἐν Κύπρῳ τε καὶ Θηβαΐδι, οὐ μὴν ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐν τῇ Περσίδι καὶ ἐν ἄλλοις τόποις εὑρίσκεται" (EPI 7464–7473; OCR "ἔτε" and "Ev" corrected): "the heresy is still found even now in Rome and in Italy, in Egypt and in Palestine, in Arabia and in Syria, in Cyprus and the Thebaid, and even in Persia and other places". LATER (about two centuries). Documented as his report for his own day.

I2. **His followers died as martyrs.** An anonymous anti-Montanist writer quoted by Eusebius: "those called Marcionites, from the heresy of Marcion, say that they have a multitude of martyrs for Christ; yet they do not confess Christ himself in truth" (EUS 29928–29930). Eusebius: "Metrodorus, who appears to have been a proselyte of the Marcionitic sect, suffered death by fire" at Smyrna (EUS 23680–23681); a woman martyr at Caesarea "is reported that she belonged to the sect of Marcion" (EUS 40473–40474). LATER. Documented. (The editor's note dates Metrodorus to Decius, 250, not Polycarp's time: EUS 23656–23658; card only.)

I3. **The church's writers answered him, book after book (defended against him: one God, maker of the world and Father of Jesus).**
- Justin wrote a book against him; Irenaeus quotes it: "I would not have believed the Lord Himself, if He had announced any other than He who is our framer, maker, and nourisher" (ANF 44907–44917). CONTEMP (Justin), kept by LATER (Irenaeus). Documented.
- Eusebius: Theophilus "contended against them ... from a certain discourse of no common merit written by him against Marcion" (EUS 25259–25261); Philip of Gortyna "likewise wrote a most elaborate work against Marcion, as did also Irenæus and Modestus" (EUS 25299–25304); Rhodo "published among the rest one against the heresy of Marcion" (EUS 28925–28926). LATER. Documented.
- Irenaeus promised a whole refutation "out of his own writings" (ANF 33622–33623); the editor's note calls it "A promise never fulfilled" (card only).
- Tertullian's *Against Marcion* is in five books and is his third attempt: "This present text, therefore, of my work—which is the third as superseding the second" (T 22509–22512). LATER. Documented.

I4. **The argument for four Gospels and for the older faith (defended against him).**
- Irenaeus: "the Gospel is quadriform" (ANF 41120); "all who destroy the form of the Gospel are vain ... who represent the aspects of the Gospel as being either more in number than as aforesaid, or, on the other hand, fewer ... For Marcion, rejecting the entire Gospel, yea rather, cutting himself off from the Gospel, boasts that he has part in the [blessings of] the Gospel" (ANF 41142–41150). LATER. Documented.
- Tertullian's rule of time, aimed at Marcion: "I say that my Gospel is the true one; Marcion, that his is ... authority lies with that which shall be found to be more ancient" (T 29205–29210). LATER. Documented.
- Irenaeus on the Roman bishops' list, "this Epistle is of older date than these men who are now propagating falsehood, and who conjure into existence another god beyond the Creator" (ANF 39843–39845). LATER.
- Whether Marcion caused the church's list of books (the canon) is not stated in any held source. The census legacy note calls it "still debated by historians" (CEN 33287). Contested; not story material as fact.

I5. **Polycarp turned many back.** "He it was who, coming to Rome in the time of Anicetus caused many to turn away from the aforesaid heretics to the Church of God" (ANF 39871–39874); the heretics just named are "Valentinus, and Marcion, and the rest" (ANF 39871). LATER. Documented as Irenaeus's report.

I6. **His school split after him.** Rhodo: "this heresy was divided in his time into various opinions" (EUS 28926–28928); Apelles "acknowledges one principle"; Potitus and Basilicus "hold to two principles, as does the mariner Marcion himself"; Syneros held "three natures" (EUS 28947–29104). Disciples named elsewhere: Apelles, "his instructor and moulder, Marcion" (T 21359–21360); Lucan, "a follower and disciple of Marcion" (T 55772–55773). LATER. Documented.

## 4. Links (by kind)

- **Polycarp of Smyrna** — met him (Irenaeus's report; M8).
- **Justin Martyr** — wrote against him, in his lifetime (ANF 14943–14948, 16130–16136; book quoted by Irenaeus, ANF 44907–44917).
- **Irenaeus of Lyon** — wrote against him; tells the Polycarp meeting (ANF 33572–33622, 39882–39884).
- **Tertullian of Carthage** — wrote five books against him (T 22484 onward). (Carthage is not in the held lines above; the census card gives it.)
- **Cerdo** — his teacher or forerunner at Rome (ANF 33564–33573, 39970–39971).
- **Apelles, Lucan** — his disciples (T 21359–21360, 55772–55777).
- **Theophilus, Philip of Gortyna, Modestus, Rhodo** — wrote against him (EUS 25259–25261, 25299–25304, 28925–28926).
- **Epiphanius of Salamis** — wrote against him two centuries later; tells the bishop's-son story (D3).

## 5. Where sources disagree or are late

D1. **Which bishop of Rome?** Irenaeus: Marcion "flourished under Anicetus" (ANF 39983–39984), and Irenaeus writes while Eleutherius "does now, in the twelfth place from the apostles, hold the inheritance of the episcopate" (ANF 39849–39851). Tertullian puts Marcion and Valentinus "in the church of Rome under the episcopate of the blessed Eleutherus" (T 21335–21337). By Irenaeus's own list, Eleutherius came two bishops after Anicetus. Contested; the story should say "the middle of the second century" and not name a bishop for the break.

D2. **The 115 years.** Tertullian: "from Tiberius to Antoninus Pius, there are about 115 years and 6-1/2 months. Just such an interval do they place between Christ and Marcion" (T 23723–23725). This is the Marcionites' own reckoning, as Tertullian reports it; not a date for Marcion. Card only.

D3. **The bishop's son (LATE-LEGEND).** The anonymous *Against All Heresies*: "one Marcion by name, a native of Pontus, son of a bishop, excommunicated because of a rape committed on a certain virgin" (T 55756–55764). The ANF note: "neither in the de Præscr. nor in the adv. Marc., nor, apparently, in Irenæus, is any such statement brought forward" (T 55765–55767). Epiphanius tells it at length: "Οὗτος τὸ γένος Ποντικὸς ὑπῆρχεν, Ἑλενοπόντου δέ φημι, Σινώπης δὲ πόλεως, ὡς πολὺς περὶ αὐτοῦ ᾄδεται λόγος" (EPI 7476–7477; OCR "Σινώ-ans" and "ᾷδεται" corrected), "He was a Pontic by race, of Helenopontus I mean, of the city of Sinope, as much talk about him goes round"; his father the bishop expelled him (EPI 7478–7491). Holl's note: against the story's credibility "spricht das Schweigen Tertullians u. insbes. das sanctissimus de praescr. 30" (EPI 7503–7505), that is, Tertullian's silence and his calling Marcion "most abstemious" (T 21361–21362). Contested; leave out of the story.

D4. **Epiphanius's Rome scene.** Epiphanius has Marcion arrive in Rome "after the death of Hyginus", ask the presbyters about new wine and old wineskins, and threaten "ἐγὼ σχίσω τὴν ἐκκλησίαν ὑμῶν" ("I will split your church") (EPI 7491–7620). About two centuries later, with no earlier support. Holl's note calls the Hyginus dating an "ungenaue Wiedergabe" (inexact rendering) of Irenaeus (EPI 7505–7506). LATE-LEGEND; leave out.

D5. **What Polycarp's question was.** Irenaeus (ANF): "Dost thou know me?" (ANF 39883). Eusebius's version of the same passage: "Knowest thou us?" (EUS 22977–22981; a translator's note between "Knowest" and "thou" reports other manuscripts reading a command, "recognise us", EUS 22977–22981, card only). Two translations from two transmissions; the Greek of Eusebius Book IV is not held. Small; quote the Irenaeus form.

D6. **The money.** Tertullian says the money was rejected "along with himself" (T 29241); the census says the church "gave back his money" (CEN 33277) and "returned his money" (CEN 33280). No held text says it was returned. Follow Tertullian.

## 6. Not held / gaps

N1. No year is held for his arrival at Rome, his expulsion or his death. "144" (CEN 33277, 33280) has no support in the held files.
N2. Neither the *Antitheses* nor his letter is held; nothing he wrote survives in the Library.
N3. The Greek of Irenaeus's Polycarp passage (kept in Eusebius, *Church History* IV.14) is not held: the Library's Eusebius Greek is Books VI–X only (`eusebius_werke-bd2-2-historia-ecclesiastica-vi-x-...`). The Latin of Irenaeus is not held either. Quotes from Irenaeus are rendered from the ANF English.
N4. Held but not used: Hippolytus, *Refutation* (anf05); Ephraim, *Against Marcion* (Mitchell); Chrysostom, *Homily against Marcionists*; the verse *Five Books in Reply to Marcion* (anf04). All later and from outside; none adds a life event the story needs.
