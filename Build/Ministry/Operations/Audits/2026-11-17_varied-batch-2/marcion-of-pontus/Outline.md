# Outline: Marcion of Pontus

Sheet = `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder; tags (S1, M3, I1 …) point to its entries, which give file and lines.

- **Length:** 2 minutes (about 300 words). He is a major figure, and his churches need their third of the story. But no word of his own survives, and the facts of his life are few and come only from opponents. So the story does not stretch toward 3 minutes.
- **Setting, in words to say aloud:** "In the middle of the second century, in the church at Rome, a shipmaster from Pontus, on the Black Sea…" Support: Justin writes to the emperor Antoninus Pius while Marcion "is even at this day alive" (S2); "in the church of Rome" (S3); "that shipmaster of Pontus" (S5); the "Euxine" sea of Pontus (S1). "Second century" rests on modern chronology (S2). The tradition: he began in **the church at Rome** and founded **the churches of the Marcionites** (S4).
- **Thread:** By splitting the law from the gospel and building churches of his own, Marcion drove the church's writers to argue, book after book, that one God made the world and sent Jesus.
- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker. The question he tried to answer, what he taught, who followed him, and the long answer his opponents wrote.
- **Openings:** (1) Polycarp, the old bishop of Smyrna, meets Marcion. "Do you know me?" "I do know you: the firstborn of Satan" (M8, Q2). (2) Justin, writing in Rome in Marcion's lifetime: he "is still teaching" (Q1, S2), a rare witness from his own day.

**Story beats**

1. **A shipmaster joins the church at Rome.** Tertullian calls him "that shipmaster of Pontus" and a student of Stoic philosophy. At first he believed as the Roman church did, and in his first warmth of faith he gave it money. Tertullian says a letter of Marcion's own showed this first faith. (S5, M1, M2.) Documented as Tertullian's account.
2. **His question: where does evil come from?** Tertullian says he brooded over the origin of evil. He read Jesus' saying that a good tree cannot bear bad fruit. Then he read God saying in Isaiah, "I am He that createth evil." So he judged the Creator a bad tree, and looked for another, good God, whom he found in Christ's kindness. (M3.) Tertullian's hostile reconstruction: say "Tertullian says". Inferential-Thin as to Marcion's real reasons.
3. **Two gods.** Justin, in Marcion's lifetime: he taught "some other god greater than the Creator" (Q1). Irenaeus: the God of the law and the prophets was, for Marcion, "the author of evils"; Jesus came from a higher Father, setting aside the law and the prophets. "The law" is the law of Moses and the Hebrew Scriptures. (M4.) Documented.
4. **The law against the gospel.** He wrote a book called the *Antitheses*, "contradictory propositions", setting the gospel against the law, point by point. Tertullian: splitting them was "his own work, and his chief one" (Q3). The book is lost; we know it through Tertullian. (M4, sheet note "No word of Marcion's own is held".) Documented as report.
5. **His own Scripture.** He kept one Gospel, Luke's, shortened, with no author's name, and a shortened set of Paul's letters. He dropped the letters to Timothy and Titus. His reason, in the *Antitheses* as Tertullian reports it: the church's Gospel had been altered by "the defenders of Judaism". His followers said he had not made something new; he had restored the faith. He leaned on Paul rebuking Peter in Galatians. (M5, M6.) Documented as report. **Defended by him and his followers.**
6. **Cast out.** Rome expelled him "more than once" (Tertullian says this of Marcion and Valentinus together), and his gift was cast out with him. His churches baptised only the unmarried, the widowed, or those who had parted from a spouse. (M9, M1, M7.) Documented as Tertullian's account. No year is held.
7. **(Optional, one sentence.)** Tertullian says Marcion later offered to repent, on terms that he bring his followers back, but died first. (M10.) Inferential-Thin: one witness, in a passage with a wrong bishop.

**What came of him** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **His churches spread and lasted.** Justin saw "many of every nation" follow him. Tertullian: "Even wasps make combs; so also these Marcionites make churches." Two centuries later Epiphanius still finds them in Rome and Italy, Egypt, Palestine, Arabia, Syria, Cyprus and even Persia. (I1.)
2. **They had their own martyrs.** A writer quoted by Eusebius says the Marcionites claimed "a multitude of martyrs"; Eusebius names one burned at Smyrna, and a woman martyr at Caesarea. (I2.)
3. **The church answered him in book after book** (defended against him: one God, maker of the world and Father of Jesus). Justin wrote against him in his lifetime: "I would not have believed the Lord Himself, if He had announced any other than He who is our framer, maker, and nourisher." Theophilus, Philip of Gortyna, Modestus, Irenaeus and Rhodo wrote against him. Tertullian's *Against Marcion* runs to five books, his third attempt. (I3.)
4. **Four Gospels, and the older faith** (defended against him). Irenaeus argued that the Gospel is fourfold and named Marcion among those who have fewer. Tertullian argued that the older Gospel must be the true one. (I4.)
5. **Polycarp turned many back** at Rome under Anicetus, Irenaeus says, from Valentinus, Marcion and the rest. (I5.)
6. **His school split after him** into those holding one, two or three first principles; Apelles and Lucan were his disciples. (I6.) Card or one clause only.

**Quotes** (3)

- **Q1.** Greek (JG 729–731): "Μαρκίωνα δέ τινα Ποντικόν, ὃς καὶ νῦν ἔτι ἐστὶ διδάσκων τοὺς πειθομένους, ἄλλον τινὰ νομίζειν μείζονα τοῦ δημιουργοῦ θεόν". Printed English (ANF 14943–14945): "And there is Marcion, a man of Pontus, who is even at this day alive, and teaching his disciples to believe in some other god greater than the Creator." **Rendering (mine, from the Greek):** "And there is a certain Marcion, from Pontus. Even now he is still teaching the people he has won over. He teaches them to believe in some other god, greater than the Creator." Job: the one voice from his own lifetime; it sets the date and the core teaching.
- **Q2.** Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* III.3.4 (ANF 39882–39884): "“Dost thou know me?” “I do know thee, the first-born of Satan.”" **Rendering (mine, from the ANF English; the Greek, kept in Eusebius Book IV, and Irenaeus's Latin are not held):** "“Do you know me?” “I do know you: the firstborn of Satan.”" Job: the opening, and a picture of how hard the church set itself against him. Say who tells it: Irenaeus, who knew Polycarp in his youth.
- **Q3.** Tertullian, *Against Marcion* I.19, Latin (OEH 4337–4338): "Separatio legis et evangelii proprium et principale opus est Marcionis". Printed English (T 23734–23736): "Marcion's special and principal work is the separation of the law and the gospel". **Rendering (mine, from the Latin):** "Separating the law from the gospel is Marcion's own work, and his chief one." Job: names his idea in one line, in an opponent's voice.

**Links**

- Polycarp of Smyrna — met him (Irenaeus's report).
- Justin Martyr — wrote against him in his lifetime; his book is quoted by Irenaeus.
- Irenaeus of Lyon — wrote against him; tells the Polycarp meeting.
- Tertullian — wrote five books against him.
- Cerdo — his forerunner at Rome; Marcion "succeeded him, and developed his doctrine".
- Apelles, Lucan — his disciples.
- Theophilus, Philip of Gortyna, Modestus, Rhodo — wrote against him.

**Who tells it**

- Justin, at Rome, in Marcion's own lifetime; an opponent. (Only witness from his own day.)
- Irenaeus, about a generation later; he had known Polycarp as a boy.
- Tertullian, in a book dated about 207 by its editor, some sixty years after Marcion.
- Epiphanius, about two centuries later.
- Line to say aloud: "Everything we know of Marcion comes from the men who wrote against him."

**Do not say**

- WRONG "Marcion of Sinope" / "from the port of Sinope". RIGHT "from Pontus, on the Black Sea". Sinope is named only by Epiphanius, two centuries later (D3).
- WRONG "a wealthy shipowner", "he made a fortune in shipping". RIGHT "a shipmaster from Pontus, as Tertullian calls him" (S5). No held text says he was rich or owned ships.
- WRONG "a huge donation", "200,000 sesterces", any modern value. RIGHT "he gave the church money" or "two hundred sesterces, Tertullian says" (M2). Do not convert or enlarge the sum.
- WRONG "the church gave back his money". RIGHT "his gift was cast out along with him" (M1, D6).
- WRONG "expelled in 144" or any year; "expelled by Bishop Anicetus / Eleutherus". RIGHT "Rome expelled him", "in the middle of the second century" (N1, D1).
- WRONG "Rome expelled him twice". RIGHT "more than once", and Tertullian says it of Marcion and Valentinus together (M9).
- WRONG "Polycarp met him in the street" / "in Rome". RIGHT "Polycarp met him once" (M8). Irenaeus gives no place.
- WRONG "Polycarp's letter calls Marcion the firstborn of Satan". RIGHT the words are Polycarp's reply to Marcion, as Irenaeus tells it (M8).
- WRONG "Marcion said the God of the Old Testament was evil" / "a demon". RIGHT "Irenaeus says Marcion called him 'the author of evils'", or "another god, greater than the Creator" (Justin) (M4).
- WRONG "Marcion hated the Jews", "anti-Jewish". RIGHT he claimed "the defenders of Judaism" had altered the Gospel, in Tertullian's report (M6). Give his claim, not a motive.
- WRONG "ten letters of Paul". RIGHT "a shortened set of Paul's letters", without the letters to Timothy and Titus (M5). No held text counts them.
- WRONG "Marcion created the first New Testament" / "forced the church to make its canon". RIGHT "Irenaeus argued for four Gospels against him" (I4). Whether he caused the canon is debated (I4); not in the story.
- WRONG "Marcion repented and was reconciled". RIGHT "Tertullian says he offered to repent, but died before he could" (M10).
- WRONG "Marcion was the son of a bishop", "excommunicated by his father", "for seducing a virgin". RIGHT leave out (D3).
- WRONG "Marcion wrote that…", quoting Marcion. RIGHT "Tertullian says Marcion argued…". Nothing of his own survives in the record.
- WRONG "about 115 years after Christ" as Marcion's date. RIGHT leave out; it is the Marcionites' own reckoning (D2).
- WRONG "Marcion was a Gnostic". RIGHT no label; his opponents group him with others, but call him only "heretic".
- WRONG "Christ only seemed to have a body" as plain fact of his teaching. RIGHT leave out, or "Irenaeus says Christ appeared 'in the form of a man'" (ANF 33579–33580); the sheet does not carry it as a beat.
- WRONG the Marcionite churches "died out by the fourth century" / "lasted into the fifth". RIGHT "two centuries later Epiphanius still found them…" (I1). No held end date.
- WRONG adding feelings: "angry", "humiliated", "bitter", "Polycarp sneered". RIGHT the words only.

**Leave out**

- The bishop's-son and seduced-virgin story, and Epiphanius's Rome scene with the presbyters and the wineskins (D3, D4): late, hostile, without earlier support; Holl, the Greek editor, doubted it.
- Tertullian's abuse of Pontus and its peoples (T 22516–22560): invective, not record.
- The Eleutherus dating and the 115 years (D1, D2): card only.
- Lists of his scriptural cuts (Ephesians as "Laodiceans", the Apocalypse): card only.
- Rhodo's names for the later schools (Potitus, Basilicus, Syneros): card only.
