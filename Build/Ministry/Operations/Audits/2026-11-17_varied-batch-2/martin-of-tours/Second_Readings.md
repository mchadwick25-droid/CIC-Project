# Second Readings: martin-of-tours

## 1. I am Christ's soldier. I am
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 841-842; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 6335-6337
Second rendering: I am Christ's soldier. I am not allowed to fight.
Ours: I am Christ's soldier. I am not allowed to fight.
Verdict: same
Reason: Same meaning, word for word with the Latin.

## 2. it was a cruel wrong, never
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 11657-11660; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 5905-5907
Second rendering: It was a savage outrage, never heard of before, for a worldly judge to try a case of the Church.
Ours: it was a cruel wrong, never heard of before, for a judge of this world to try a case of the church.
Verdict: minor
Reason: Ours renders nefas as "wrong", which is weaker than the Latin's sense of an outrage against what is sacred. A listener still takes away that Martin judged it gravely and unprecedentedly wrong.

## Release 50 fixes

## 3. Through the villages, Sulpicius tells, he tore down temples (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 1296-1298; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 6860-6861
Second rendering: Wherever he had destroyed shrines, he at once built churches or monasteries there.
Ours: Through the villages, Sulpicius tells, he tore down temples and built churches or monasteries in their place.
Verdict: same
Reason: "fana destruxerat ... statim ibi aut ecclesias aut monasteria construebat" is carried whole. "Through the villages" sets the scene from the same chapters (in uico quodam, Leprosum), and the act is credited to Sulpicius.

## 4. When he set about cutting down a pine held sacred (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 1244-1248; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 6792-6795
Second rendering: When he had pulled down a very old temple in a village and set about cutting down the pine beside it, the local priest and the rest of the pagan crowd began to resist him.
Ours: When he set about cutting down a pine held sacred, the local people rose against him.
Verdict: minor
Reason: Ours folds the priest ("antistes loci illius") into "the local people", and "rose against" is a shade stronger than "coepit obsistere", began to resist. The listener still hears the right thing: he went for their sacred tree and they opposed him. The record's own "held sacred" comes from the tree standing by the temple and Martin's argument that there was "nothing sacred in the trunk" (NP11 1250-1251).

## 5. Elsewhere a crowd drove him off and hurt him (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 1319-1323; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 6895-6897
Second rendering: At Leprosum, when he wanted to overthrow a rich temple, a crowd of pagans resisted him so hard that he was driven back, not without injury.
Ours: Elsewhere a crowd drove him off and hurt him.
Verdict: same
Reason: "restitit ei multitudo gentilium, adeo ut non absque iniuria sit repulsus" is driven back and hurt. Ours drops the place name and the temple's wealth, which changes nothing a listener needs.

## 6. Often too, he says, his preaching moved people to pull down their own temples (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 1365-1369; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 6954-6958
Second rendering: Often, when the country people pleaded with him not to destroy their shrines, he so softened them by his holy preaching that, once they saw the truth, they overthrew their temples themselves.
Ours: Often too, he says, his preaching moved people to pull down their own temples.
Verdict: same
Reason: "plerumque ... ipsi sua templa subuerterent" carried. Ours leaves out that they had first begged him to spare the shrines. That clause would add force, but its absence does not soften: the sentences before it already tell the force plainly.

## 7. They pressed him to confirm it in writing, and he refused (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 5196-5198; original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 11909-11910
Second rendering: Though the bishops strove hard to make him confirm that communion with his signature, it could not be forced out of him.
Ours: They pressed him to confirm it in writing, and he refused.
Verdict: same
Reason: "summe episcopis nitentibus, ut communionem illam subscriptione firmaret, extorqueri non potuit." "Pressed" carries "summe nitentibus"; "in writing" carries "subscriptione"; "he refused" carries "extorqueri non potuit".

## 8. Sulpicius, who was not there, heard that almost two thousand monks gathered (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/npnf211_sulpitius-severus-vincent-lerins-cassian.xml 2491-2495 (absence: 2362-2366); original cic/texts/sulpicius-severus_libri-qui-supersunt-chronica-vita-martini-lat_halm1866-csel1.txt 8333
Second rendering: The monks are said to have gathered that day, nearly two thousand of them.
Ours: Sulpicius, who was not there, heard that almost two thousand monks gathered at his funeral.
Verdict: same
Reason: "fere ad duo milia conuenisse dicuntur" keeps both "almost" and the report. "Who was not there" rests on Ep. III, where he sends readers to "those who were present", and on the "dicuntur" itself.

## 9. Gregory ... warned them that the judgment of the eternal King had pursued Maximus (narrated paraphrase)
Source: cic/texts/gregory-tours_history-of-the-franks-selections_brehaut1916.txt 5813-5818; original cic/texts/gregory-tours_opera-mgh-srm-1-lat_arndt-krusch1885.txt 16707-16710
Second rendering: When Maximus forced Martin to share communion with a bishop who had blood on his hands, Martin gave way to the wicked king so that he could more easily free the condemned from death. The judgment of the eternal King pursued Maximus: he was driven from the throne and condemned to the worst of deaths.
Ours: Gregory ... warned them that the judgment of the eternal King had pursued Maximus until he lost his throne and his life. Maximus had forced Martin to yield, he said, and Martin gave way only to free the condemned from death.
Verdict: minor
Reason: "Only" is not Gregory's. His "quo facilius addictos morte liberaret" (so that he might the more easily free the condemned from death) gives a purpose, not the sole one. "Lost his throne and his life" is fair for "driven from the imperial throne and condemned to the worst death". The homicide bishop is dropped, which the brief's Trier paragraph already covers. Cut "only" (see Recheck_R50.md).
