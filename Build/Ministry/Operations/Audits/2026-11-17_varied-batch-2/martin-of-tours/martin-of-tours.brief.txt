At the gate of Amiens, in a winter so cold it was killing people, a soldier met a beggar with no clothes. Since he had nothing left but his own cloak, he cut it in two with his sword and gave the man half. Some onlookers laughed at him, while others groaned, because they had done nothing.

The soldier was Martin, and this was the fourth century, in Roman Gaul, in what is now France. He would become one of the monks and monk-bishops of Gaul. Almost all we know of him comes from one admirer, Sulpicius Severus, who met him late in life and wrote his story while he still lived.

Martin was born to pagan parents in Pannonia, in what is now Hungary, and grew up in Italy. At ten, against their wishes, he asked the church to prepare him for baptism. At fifteen his own father, a soldier, informed on him under a law that made veterans' sons serve, so Martin was taken in chains and made to swear the soldier's oath.

Sulpicius says that the next night Martin saw Christ in a dream, wearing the half-cloak, and heard him tell the angels that Martin had clothed him. Martin was baptised soon after, yet he stayed nearly two more years in the army, because his tribune promised to leave the world with him.

Near Worms, before a battle, the Caesar Julian was handing each soldier a gift of money. Martin thought it wrong to take it if he meant to leave, so he asked for his discharge: "Until now I have served you as a soldier. Let me now serve God as a soldier. Let the man who is going to fight take your gift. I am Christ's soldier. I am not allowed to fight." When Julian called it cowardice, Martin offered to stand unarmed before the battle line. He was put in prison, but the next day the enemy surrendered, and Sulpicius saw the peace as Martin's victory.

Martin went to Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, who made him an exorcist after he refused to be a deacon, and later he founded a monastery near Poitiers. When the people of Tours wanted him as bishop, a citizen tricked him out with a story of a sick wife, and the crowds brought him in under a kind of guard. Some bishops objected to his shabby looks, clothes and hair.

As bishop he still lived as a monk, in a wooden cell between a cliff and the river Loire, where eighty disciples joined him and owned everything in common. Through the villages he tore down pagan temples and built churches in their place, although once a crowd drove him off and hurt him.

At the emperor Maximus's court in Trier, bishops were prosecuting Priscillian and his followers for heresy. Martin did not defend their teaching, but he said that "it was a cruel wrong, never heard of before, for a judge of this world to try a case of the church," and he begged that their blood not be shed. While he was at Trier the trial waited. After he left, they were put to death. Later, to save other lives, he shared communion for one day with the bishops who had pressed for those deaths, though he refused to sign his name. He grieved over it, and he never went to a gathering of bishops again.

Old and near his end, he still travelled to the village of Candes to make peace among its quarrelling clergy. When his monks begged him not to leave them, he is said to have answered, "Lord, if your people still need me, I do not refuse the work. Your will be done."

A later bishop built a great church over his grave, because miracles were reported there. Gregory, bishop of Tours almost two hundred years later, calls him "our blessed patron" and told the faithful to remember his burial day, 11 November. The Frankish king Clovis ordered his army to take nothing but grass and water from Martin's lands, and after his victory he brought gifts to Martin's church.

The book spread his name further, and Sulpicius claims it was read in Rome, Carthage and Egypt. Many of Martin's monks became bishops, and at his funeral, it was said, almost two thousand monks gathered.

Generations later, at a council in Paris, other bishops sat silent before an angry king. Gregory reminded them how Maximus had forced Martin to yield, and how the judgment of the eternal King pursued Maximus until he lost his throne and his life.
