# Fixes, release 50: Martin of Tours

Ruling: Mark, 2026-11-17, "yes no softening, we are a witness not a judge … for this round 1 but also the should fixes too". Released text kept as `martin-of-tours.r50.brief.txt`.

## Piece items

1. **MUST: temples and the sacred pine restored (outline beat 5, Do not say M13).** New paragraph after the Loire cell: "Through the villages, Sulpicius tells, he tore down temples and built churches or monasteries in their place. When he set about cutting down a pine held sacred, the local people rose against him. Elsewhere a crowd drove him off and hurt him. Often too, he says, his preaching moved people to pull down their own temples." In Sulpicius's voice throughout. The falling-pine wonder stays out, as the outline says. Sources: Source_Sheet M13; NP11 1244–1248 (pine), 1296–1298 (churches or monasteries where temples stood), 1319–1323 (Leprosum, driven back "not without bodily injury"), 1365–1369 (they overthrew their own temples). Lines opened and confirmed.
2. **"though he refused to sign it".** Old: "To save other lives, he shared communion for one day with the bishops behind those deaths, though he refused to sign it." New: "To save other lives, he shared communion for one day with the bishops behind those deaths. They pressed him to confirm it in writing, and he refused." Source: M16; NP11 5193–5198 ("the bishops strove to the uttermost to get him to confirm the fact of his communicating by signing his name, he could not be induced to do so").
3. **Last paragraphs in time order; Paris setup.** New order: funeral and his monks / the Life read abroad; the great church and Clovis; the council in Paris. The Paris council now has a setup: "At a council in Paris, the bishops sat silent before the fury of King Chilperic." (old: "other bishops sat silent before an angry king"). Sources: I4 (Gregory "urging silent bishops to speak to King Chilperic", GT 5796–5800); GT 5794–5798 ("not to burst out in fury at God's servant", "all were silent"). No years added for Clovis or Paris: neither 507 nor 577 is in the source sheet.
4. **Setting line.** Old: "The soldier was Martin, in the fourth century, in Roman Gaul, in what is now France, among its monks and monk-bishops." New: "The soldier was Martin, in Roman Gaul in the fourth century." One place, one century; "among its monks and monk-bishops" dropped; the only "in what is now" left is Hungary (S2). Sources: S3, S4.
5. **"the Caesar Julian".** Old: "When the Caesar Julian handed out money…" New: "When the Roman commander Julian handed out money…" Sources: L4 ("the commander he asked for discharge"), NP11 836–842. "Julian the Apostate" still not used (Do not say).
6. **"it was said" names its witness.** Old: "At his funeral, it was said, almost two thousand monks gathered." New: "Sulpicius, who was not there, heard that almost two thousand monks gathered at his funeral." Keeps "almost" and the report. Sources: M20, NP11 2491–2495 ("They are said to have assembled on that day almost to the number of two thousand"); Witness kinds (not present at the death, Ep. III, NP11 2364–2366).
7. **Not ending on Maximus's fall.** Old last sentences: "Gregory … reminded them how Maximus had forced Martin to yield. The judgment of the eternal King, he told them, pursued Maximus until he lost his throne and his life." New: "Gregory, bishop of Tours almost two hundred years later, warned them that the judgment of the eternal King had pursued Maximus until he lost his throne and his life. Maximus had forced Martin to yield, he said, and Martin gave way only to free the condemned from death." The retribution stays, in Gregory's voice, but the piece now ends on Martin's act and why he did it. Sources: I4; GT 5813–5818 ("Martin yielded to the wicked king in order the more easily to free the condemned from death").
8. **Card, uncertain: chronology.** Added one entry after "Sulpicius gives no years…": "The two writers' years do not fit together. Gregory puts Martin's birth in the eleventh year of Constantine's reign. Sulpicius has him sworn in at fifteen, baptized at twenty and out of Julian's army about two years later. Gregory's birth year would make him much older than that when he left the army." Sources: D1; GT 1414–1416 (born in Constantine's eleventh year); NP11 726, 807, 815–816, 836–842.

## Across the set
