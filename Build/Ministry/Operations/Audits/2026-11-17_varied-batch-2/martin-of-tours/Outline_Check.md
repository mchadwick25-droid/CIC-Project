# Outline check: Martin of Tours

**Verdict: FIX** (three small BLOCKING corrections; the outline is otherwise sound).

I opened every cited passage myself in NP11, HALM, GT and VF. All three quotes (Latin and Roberts's English) and the Fortunatus verse line are verbatim at the cited lines once the OCR and XML tags are cleaned as the sheet describes. The D3 point about Roberts's mistranslation at Trier is right: HALM 5907–5910 reads "quoad usque Martinus Treueris fuit" and "mox discessurus". Witness kinds, the length (2½ to 3 minutes for a major figure, with two turns and a large afterlife), the shape (Monk or ascetic), the setting lines and the "Who tells it" lines all fit the record.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: the sacred tree (Beat 5, and the M13 line in Do not say).** The outline says he "tore down pagan temples and a sacred tree". Sulpicius says he "had set about cutting down a pine-tree" and the people stopped him (NP11 1244–1248). Then the pagans cut it down themselves, inside the falling-pine wonder (NP11 1254–1288), which the outline leaves out. Outside the wonder, the record does not say he felled the tree. Correction, in both places: "he tore down pagan temples. Once he set about cutting down a pine held sacred, and the local people rose against him."

2. **BLOCKING: the funeral detail (What came of them, item 3).** "Almost two thousand monks walked behind his body" adds a detail the source does not give. Sulpicius says only that the monks "are said to have assembled on that day almost to the number of two thousand" (NP11 2493–2494). His next image is the shepherd "driving his own flocks before him", so "behind" even reverses it. Correction: "At his funeral, it was said, almost two thousand monks gathered."

3. **BLOCKING: Gregory's use of the Trier story (What came of them, item 4, the "strong ending" note).** The note says Gregory held up "Martin's stand against an emperor". Gregory says the opposite. Maximus "forced the blessed Martin to give communion", and Martin "yielded ... to free the condemned from death". Gregory's point is the punishment that followed for Maximus (GT 5813–5818). Gregory does not mention heresy or a secular judge at all. Correction: "A strong ending: Gregory, generations later, warning bishops afraid of a king with what happened to Maximus, the emperor who forced Martin's hand." Keep the DEFENDED principle tied to Sulpicius (Q2, M15–M16), not to Gregory.

4. **MINOR: the Thread overclaims.** "Made that pattern the West's picture of a holy man" goes past the record. The strongest held statement is Brehaut's: the Life "had a large circulation in Gaul and became a model for saints' lives" (GT 11934–11935). Correction: "...and the book his friend wrote about him made that pattern a model for later lives of saints."

5. **MINOR: "Modern historians" (item 2).** Only one modern historian is cited for "a model for saints' lives": Brehaut, 1916 (GT 11935). Correction: "a modern historian calls it a model for later saints' lives."

6. **MINOR: the July feast (item 1).** "His feast in July" is not exact. Gregory's July feast is the feast "of the church": the dedication of the church, the moving of his body there, and his ordination as bishop (GT 2209–2212). Correction: "a July feast for his church, the moving of his body and his ordination, and his burial day, 11 November." Add to Do not say. Wrong: "his feast day is in July." Right: the July feast marks his church, the moving of his body and his ordination.

7. **MINOR: "the heart of the church in Gaul" (item 1 heading).** This is a modern verdict, from Brehaut's "chief point of Christian pilgrimage in Gaul" and "religious Metropolis" (GT 119–131). Gregory's acts (the great church, the feast, Clovis) support it, but a writer may state it as fact in the narrator's voice. Correction: frame it as "Tours became the chief place of pilgrimage in Gaul", told through the acts Gregory reports, or credit it as a historian's judgment.

8. **MINOR, Do not say addition: Clovis.** Gregory frames Clovis's victory as won with Martin's favour: the omen at the church, and the hay story with "where shall our hope of victory be if we offend the blessed Martin?" (GT 2608–2627). Add to Do not say. Wrong: "Martin won Clovis his victory" / "Clovis was crowned at Tours." Right: "Clovis honoured Martin's lands and church, and after his victory he put on a consul's purple there" (GT 2705–2711).

9. **MINOR: "urged" Ithacius (Beat 6).** The Latin *increpare* (HALM 5902) means to rebuke, not just to urge. Roberts has "importune". Correction: "he kept rebuking the accuser Ithacius to drop the charge."

## Renderings (compared after making my own)

- **Q1.** My rendering: "Until now I have served you as a soldier. Let me now be a soldier for God. Let the man who means to fight take your gift. I am Christ's soldier. I may not fight." Theirs keeps every clause, adds nothing, and rightly renders *pugnaturus* as "the man who is going to fight". Pass.
- **Q2.** My rendering: "It was more than enough that heretics, judged by the bishops' sentence, be driven from the churches. It was a cruel and unheard-of wrong for a worldly judge to try the church's case." Theirs matches. "Cruel" follows *saeuum* in P; the *nouum* variant is covered by "never heard of before". Pass.
- **Q3.** My rendering: "Lord, if your people still need me, I do not refuse the work. Your will be done." Theirs is identical in substance. Pass.
