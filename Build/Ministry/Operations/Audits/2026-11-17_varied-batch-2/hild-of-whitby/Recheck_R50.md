## One hearing

- Who: Hild, a seventh-century Northumbrian noblewoman, King Edwin's great-niece, who became abbess of Whitby, a monastery of men and women.
- What happened: baptized under Paulinus, called home by Aidan, she ruled Hartlepool and then Whitby, made her people study the Scriptures, hosted the 664 synod where she held with the Scots and lost to Rome's Easter, took in Caedmon and had him taught, taught on through six years of fever, and died in 680.
- Why it matters: her house trained the young English church; Bede saw five bishops come from it, and Caedmon's English religious song began there.
