# The outline: Hild of Whitby

Sheet references (S, M, I, L, D, N) point to `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder, which gives each file and line.

- **Length:** about 2 minutes 30 seconds (about 375 words). Why it runs past 2 minutes: she is a leading figure of her church, and the story needs room for two scenes, the synod held in her house and Caedmon brought before her, plus the third that tells what came of her. Why it stops short of 3: almost everything rests on a single witness, Bede, and he gives no inner life to fill.

- **Setting, in words to say aloud:** the seventh century (S1), in Northumbria, the kingdom of the English north of the River Humber (S2). The English there had been converted within living memory, by two missions: one from Rome and one from the Irish monks of Iona, whom Bede calls the Scots (S3a 1–3). Her two houses were Hartlepool and Whitby. Say only "Hartlepool" and "Whitby", or "Streaneshalch, which Bede says means the Bay of the Lighthouse", now Whitby (S2). Nothing about coast or cliffs is in the record.

- **Thread:** Hild ran a house of men and women at Whitby where the Scriptures were read so closely that the young English church took five bishops from it, and where a man who could not sing was made a monk and set Scripture to English song.

- **Shape:** Founder or leader.

- **Openings:**
  1. A man at a feast who leaves the table whenever the harp comes near him, because he cannot sing. That night, Bede says, someone stood by him in his sleep and told him to sing the beginning of created things (M17). The next morning he is brought to the abbess (M18).
  2. A court where Easter fell twice in one year: the king feasting while the queen was still fasting (M13). The quarrel came to be settled in Hild's house.

- **Story beats:**
  1. **Birth and faith.** Of noble birth, the daughter of a nephew of King Edwin. She took the Christian faith with Edwin, at the preaching of Paulinus, the first bishop of the Northumbrians; Bede dates Edwin's baptism at York to Easter 627. M1, M2. Documented.
  2. **The turn, at thirty-three.** After thirty-three years of ordinary life she chose the monastic life. She went to the East Angles, meaning to cross to a monastery in Gaul where her sister was a nun, and live there "a stranger for the Lord": leaving home for good, for God's sake. Her sister's example drew her. After a year Bishop Aidan of Lindisfarne called her home. She lived a year with a few companions on a small holding by the River Wear. M3, M4, M5. Documented.
  3. **Abbess at Hartlepool.** She was made abbess of Heruteu, which Bede calls "the island of the hart" and an editor gives as Hartlepool. She set it in order under a rule, as learned men taught her; Aidan and others often came to teach her. In 655 King Oswy gave her his daughter Elfleda, not yet a year old, to raise for God. M6, M7. Documented.
  4. **Whitby.** Two years later she gained land enough for ten households at Streaneshalch, Whitby, and built, or set in order, a monastery there for men and women. She taught peace and charity above all. As in the first church of Jerusalem, no one there was rich and no one poor; all things were shared. She made her people read the Scriptures, so that many became fit to be priests. Ordinary people, and sometimes kings and princes, sought her advice (Quote 2). M8–M12. Documented (whether she founded it: Contested, D1).
  5. **The synod in her house, 664.** The Irish-trained clergy and those who followed Rome kept Easter on different dates, and cut the clergy's hair differently. The kings met at her monastery to decide. Hild and her people held with the Scots, Bishop Colman's side, the side of Aidan who had taught her. The priest Wilfrid argued for Rome. King Oswy ruled for the keeper of heaven's keys, Peter, and Bede says all present agreed; Colman went home. M13–M15. Documented. Bede gives no words of hers.
  6. **Caedmon.** A man of her house, older and unable to sing, received a gift of song in a dream, in Bede's telling. The steward brought him to her. Before many learned men she had him repeat his verses; they judged it a grace from God and set him a passage of Scripture, which he returned next morning in verse. She told him to become a monk, took him into the brotherhood, and had him taught the whole of sacred history, which he turned into English song. M17–M19. Documented as Bede's account; the gift is a wonder in Bede's voice.
  7. **Illness and death.** A fever held her for six years, and she kept teaching her people. At cockcrow on 17 November 680, aged sixty-six, she called the women of the house together and urged them to keep the peace of the Gospel among themselves and with all others. M20, M21. Documented. (Optional, in Bede's voice: that night a nun at Hackness, thirteen miles off, saw her soul carried up in light by angels. M22.)

- **What came of them** (about a third of the story):
  1. **A school of bishops.** Bede says he saw five bishops come from her monastery (Quote 1). One of them, Oftfor, studied in both her houses and went on to Canterbury and Rome. A sixth was chosen but died before he could be made bishop. Bede follows one of them, John, to Hexham and then York. I1. Documented.
  2. **Song in English.** Bede says Caedmon's verses turned many minds to heaven, and that others after him tried religious poems in English, but none could match him. I2. Documented.
  3. **Her house went on.** The king's daughter she raised became a teacher and later a head of Whitby. King Oswy, his queen and King Edwin were buried there. I3. Documented.
  4. **Her name.** All who knew her called her Mother (Quote 3); her fame reached many far away. Eight centuries later, York prayers still asked "Saint Hild" to pray for them. I4, I5. Documented.
  5. **DEFENDED:** at Whitby she held with the Easter of her Irish teachers, and the king ruled against it. I6. Documented.
  End on an event or the record's words: for example, Bede's five bishops, or the York litany.

- **Quotes, 2 or 3 at most** (all rendered by me from Bede's Latin in HEL, checked against Giles's English in HE):
  1. **Five bishops.** Latin: "Denique V ex eodem monasterio postea episcopos uidimus, et hos omnes singularis meriti ac sanctitatis uiros" (HEL 24946–24948; Plummer's margin note "underher." left out). Giles: "In short, we afterwards saw five bishops taken out of that monastery, and all of them men of singular merit and sanctity" (HE 10973–10975). **Rendering:** "Later we saw five bishops come from that same monastery. All of them were men of rare merit and holiness." ("Denique", a link word meaning "in short", is left to the narration.) Job: the measure of what she built, in an eyewitness's words. I1.
  2. **Kings and princes.** Latin: "Tantae autem erat ipsa prudentiae, ut non solum mediocres quique in necessitatibus suis, sed etiam reges ac principes nonnumquam ab ea consilium quaererent, et inuenirent" (HEL 24936–24939). Giles: "Her prudence was so great, that not only indifferent persons, but even kings and princes, as occasion offered, asked and received her advice" (HE 10964–10967). **Rendering:** "Her wisdom was so great that not only ordinary people in their needs, but even kings and princes, sometimes sought her advice and found it." Job: her standing, in beat 4. M12.
  3. **Mother.** Latin: "quam omnes, qui nouerant, ob msigne [read insigne] pietatis et gratiae matrem uocare consuerant" (HEL 24994–24995). Giles: "whom all that knew her called Mother, for her singular piety and grace" (HE 11014–11016). **Rendering:** "All who knew her used to call her Mother, for her outstanding devotion and grace." Job: how her own people remembered her. I4.

- **Links:** Edwin, king (*kin; took the faith with him*, L1). Paulinus (*heard him preach*, L2). Aidan (*taught her*, L3). Oswy and Elfleda (*received his daughter; taught her*, L6). Colman (*sided with him*, L7). Wilfrid of Ripon (*opposed at the synod*, L8). Five bishops: Bosa, Oftfor, John, Wilfrid of York and one more (*came from her house*, L9). Cedd (*sided with her, then changed*, L10). Caedmon (*received him*, L11). Bede (*wrote her life*, L12). Theodore (*a pupil of hers went to him*, L13).

- **Who tells it:**
  - **Bede**, monk of Jarrow, *Ecclesiastical History*, finished in 731: about fifty years after her death, in the same kingdom, while men from her house were still bishops. Aloud: "Almost all we know of Hild comes from Bede, a monk in the same kingdom, who wrote about fifty years after she died."
  - **Stephen**, *Life of Wilfrid*, written after 710 for Wilfrid's monastery: places the synod "in the presence of the most devout mother Hild" (M16). Not needed aloud.
  - **The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle**, put together about two centuries later: records her death in 680 (M21). Not needed aloud.
  - **The York litany**, 15th–16th century: "Saint Hild, pray" (I5). Say "centuries later" if used.

- **Do not say:**
  - "Hild was King Edwin's niece." → "the daughter of King Edwin's nephew" (M1).
  - "Paulinus baptized her in 627" / "she was baptized as a girl of thirteen." → "she took the faith with King Edwin, at the preaching of Paulinus; Bede dates Edwin's baptism to 627." No age, no day (M2).
  - "born in 614." → "born about 614", and only if a date is needed: it is worked out from her age at death (S1).
  - Anything about her first thirty-three years beyond birth, kin and faith: no marriage, husband, children or deeds (M3, N2).
  - "Bishop Aidan was called home" (Giles's English). → "Aidan called her home" (M5, D7).
  - "she was allied to the East Anglian king." → "related to" (M4).
  - "she meant to go to France." → "to Gaul", or "across the sea" (M4).
  - "King Oswy gave her Whitby" / "gave her the land." → "she gained land for ten households there"; Bede does not say from whom (M7, M8).
  - "Hild founded Whitby" as a plain fact. → "built, or set in order", or "ruled" (D1).
  - "on the cliffs", "by the sea", "a great abbey", "a library": not in the record (N6).
  - "indifferent persons sought her advice." → "ordinary people" (M12).
  - "kings came to her for advice" as a habit. → "even kings and princes, sometimes" (M12). Do not name a king.
  - "Hild presided over the synod" / "she chaired" / "she argued." → the synod met in the monastery she ruled; King Oswy ran the debate; Bede records no words of hers (M13, M14, N5).
  - "Colman and Agilbert were on the same side" (a misreading of Giles's punctuation). → Colman led the Scots' side, with Hild; Agilbert and Wilfrid were on the other (M14).
  - "the Celtic Church against the Roman Catholic Church." → "the Easter kept by the Irish-trained clergy, whom Bede calls the Scots, against the Easter of Rome" (S3b).
  - "Whitby settled the date of Easter for all England." → "King Oswy ruled for the Roman Easter, and all present agreed" (M15, S3b).
  - "Hild accepted the ruling graciously" / "Hild refused" / "Hild was bitter." → Bede says all present agreed and names her neither way (D4).
  - "an angel appeared to Caedmon." → "someone stood by him in his sleep, Bede says" (M17).
  - "Caedmon, a cowherd" / "a young cowherd." → an older man, who that night was keeping the animals; Bede says horses (M17, D8).
  - "Caedmon was the first English poet." → "Bede says others after him tried, but none could match him" (I2).
  - "Hild discovered Caedmon." → the steward brought him; learned men judged; then she made him a monk (M18).
  - "In 680 Caedmon came to Whitby." → no date is given (N4).
  - "She trained five bishops." → "five bishops came from her monastery"; she made her people study Scripture so that many were fit to be priests (M11, I1).
  - "Wilfrid of Ripon was one of her five bishops." → the Wilfrid from her house was a later bishop of York; Wilfrid of Ripon argued against her side (L9).
  - "sick for seven years." → "a fever for six years; she died in the seventh" (M20, M21).
  - Her last words in quotation marks. → reported speech only: she urged them to keep the peace of the Gospel among themselves and with all others (M21).
  - "her monks and nuns gathered at her deathbed." → Bede says she called together the servants of Christ in the monastery; his Latin word means the women (M21).
  - Begu's vision as fact ("angels carried her soul to heaven"). → "Bede tells that a nun named Begu, at Hackness, saw..." (M22).
  - Her mother's jewel dream as a prophecy that came true. → "Bede says her mother dreamed..., and that the dream was fulfilled in her daughter" (M23).
  - "Hild opposed Wilfrid for the rest of her life." → not in Bede; see Leave out (D5).
  - "Anglo-Saxon Christianity" said aloud as a church's name (S3b).

- **Leave out:**
  - **People sent by Hild to Rome to accuse Wilfrid.** One witness only: a pope's letter copied in Wilfrid's *Life*, a source on Wilfrid's side. Bede is silent, no outcome is given, and it would need Wilfrid's whole case to explain (D5).
  - **Her mother's jewel dream, and her father's exile and poisoning.** Too far from what she built; a second wonder would crowd Caedmon (M23, M1).
  - **Begu's vision**, unless the length allows one sentence in Bede's voice (M22).
  - **The names of the five bishops, and their sees**, beyond perhaps John or Oftfor: a roll of names. The second name differs between the Latin and the English (D3).
  - **Heiu, the first nun of Northumbria**, the founder of Hartlepool: a side story (M6).
  - **Trumwine's refuge, Elfleda's age at death, a later family line from Ely**: side details or late (I3, D2, D6).
  - **The late-August York calendar entry "hilde"**: the file does not say which Hild (I5).
