## Step 1. The three lines, from one hearing

- **Who was this?** Hild, a noblewoman of seventh-century Northumbria, kin to King Edwin, who became the abbess of a monastery for men and women at Whitby.
- **What happened?** She ran Whitby as a house with no rich and no poor, where people studied Scripture closely. In 664 the kings met there to settle the Easter quarrel; she took the Irish side of her teacher Aidan, and the king ruled for Rome. She had Caedmon's gift tested, made him a monk, and had him turn sacred history into English song. She taught through six years of fever and died in 680.

### Where the third line is lost

The final paragraph. The outline's thread is that Whitby read Scripture so closely that the young English church took five bishops from it, and that a man who could not sing set Scripture to English song. The brief holds both facts but never ties them to her or to the church after her:

1. The five bishops arrive as the last sentence, a bare quote, after three unconnected items (imitators of Caedmon, "Mother", the York prayers). Nothing links them back to paragraph 5, "many became fit to be priests". A listener hears a list, not a lineage.
2. "Others after Caedmon attempted religious poems in English, but none could match him" says only that he was unmatched. It leaves out what the outline holds in I2: that his verses turned many minds to heaven. So the song's effect, what it passed on, is missing.

## Retest (revised brief, 439 words)

### The three lines, from one fresh hearing

- **Who was this?** Hild, a noblewoman of seventh-century Northumbria, kin to King Edwin, who became abbess of a monastery for men and women at Whitby.
- **What happened?** She ran Whitby as a house with no rich and no poor, where people studied Scripture closely. The kings met there in 664 over Easter; she held with Aidan's Irish side, and the king ruled for Rome. She had Caedmon's gift tested, made him a monk, and had sacred history taught to him, which he turned into English song. She taught through six years of fever and died in 680.
- **Why does the connection matter?** What she built went on after her. Her Scripture schooling gave the young English church bishops from her house; Bede himself saw five of them. Caedmon's English song turned many minds to heaven and was imitated by others. Her people called her Mother, and York still prayed to her eight centuries later.
