# Outline check: Hild of Whitby

**Verdict: FIX** (three small BLOCKING fixes, all wording; the outline is otherwise sound).

Checked against HE (Giles/Jane), HEL (Plummer's Latin), VW, ASC and YP at the cited lines. The quotes, the five-bishop and Mother passages, the synod sides (HEL 21408–21414), Aidan calling Hild home (HEL 24866–24867), the women at her deathbed ("ancellis Christi, quae erant", HEL 25046), Edwin's baptism in 627 (HE 4996–5002) and the York litany (YP 11647, 1501–1503) all read as the outline says. "While men from her house were still bishops" in Who tells it holds: Bede's list for 731 has "Wilfrid in the church of York" (HE 15164).

## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: misquote (beat 2).** "a stranger for the Lord" is in quotation marks but is not verbatim. Giles reads "live a stranger for our Lord" (HE 10913). It is also a fourth quote beyond the limit of three. **Correction:** drop the quotation marks and paraphrase: "to live there as an exile for the Lord's sake, leaving home for good". Or, if it must be quoted, quote it exactly: "a stranger for our Lord".

2. **BLOCKING: qualifier dropped (Opening 2).** "A court where Easter fell twice in one year" states as fact something Bede gives as report, and only as something that happened sometimes: "Thus it is said to have happened in those times that Easter was twice kept in one year" (HE 7897–7899). The Latin is "nonnumquam contigisse fertur" (HEL 21328). **Correction:** "Bede says it was told that at times, at the king's court, Easter was kept twice in one year: the king feasting while the queen was still fasting." Add to Do not say: "Easter was kept twice in one year" as plain fact → "it is said that this sometimes happened" (HE 7897–7899).

3. **BLOCKING: qualifier dropped (beat 7, optional Begu line).** "thirteen miles off" leaves out Bede's "about": "These monasteries are about thirteen miles distant from each other" (HE 11108–11109). The Latin is "XIII ferme milibus passuum" (HEL 25125–25126), about thirteen Roman miles. **Correction:** "about thirteen miles away". Add to Do not say: "thirteen miles" → "about thirteen miles" (M22).

4. **MINOR (beat 3).** "to raise for God" goes a little past the record. Bede says Oswy gave Elfleda "to be consecrated to Him in perpetual virginity" (HE 7756–7758), and she "was put into the monastery ... where ... the Abbess Hilda presided" (HE 7767–7770). **Correction:** "In 655 King Oswy gave his daughter Elfleda, not yet a year old, to God for life, and she was placed in Hild's monastery."

5. **MINOR (What came of them 1).** "went on to Canterbury" is an inference. Bede says "he went into Kent, to Archbishop Theodore" (HE 10987–10988; Latin "Cantiam", HEL 24969). **Correction:** "went to Archbishop Theodore in Kent, and then to Rome".

6. **MINOR: Do not say gap (N7).** Item 3 says Elfleda became "later a head of Whitby". A writer could easily make that "she succeeded Hild as abbess". **Add:** "Elfleda succeeded Hild" → "by 685 Elfleda and her mother Eanfled ruled the house; Bede does not describe the handover" (I3, N7).

7. **MINOR: Do not say gap (Caedmon's hymn).** A writer may quote the well-known Old English hymn ("Nu sculon herigean ...") or give Bede's Latin paraphrase as Caedmon's own words. Neither is held as his words: Bede gives "the sense, but not the words in order" (HE 11187–11188). **Add:** any line of the hymn as Caedmon's words → "Bede gives only its sense: praise of the Maker of heaven, who made heaven as a roof and then the earth" (M17).
