## Sentence by sentence

**S1.** "In 963 the monk Athanasios founded the Great Lavra on the mountain peninsula of Athos in northern Greece." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- MEYER 5446-5455, Athanasios's own Typikon: Methodios brought "τὰ τοῦ βασιλέως γράμματα χαὶ χρυσίον"; "κατενεύσαμεν ... ὁρμηδέντες Χτίζειν"; "Οὕπω δὲ παρεληλυδότος τετραµήνου ἀχηκόαμεν ἀνηγορεῦσθαι αὐτὸν βασιλέα". So building began less than four months before Nikephoros was proclaimed emperor, which was in 963.
- MEYER 7647, Tzimiskes: "᾿Αὐανάσιον ... χανὺηγούμενον τῆς μεγάλης λαύρας".
- Date note: the year comes from the Typikon's link to Nikephoros's accession in 963, and it is Widely Accepted. Meyer's day-by-day reckoning (lines 1355-1367) is modern and stays out of the story, which tells only the year.

**S2.** "Later accounts say a Byzantine emperor backed him." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.**
- The source is not a later account. The founder says it himself, in his own charter. MEYER 5398-5401: Nikephoros "οὐκ ἐνέλιπε παρακαλῶν καὶ προτρεπόµενος ὥστε πεῖσαι τὴν ἡμῶν µετριότητα παραδέξασσαι δομη»Ίναι λαύραν"; MEYER 5446: he sent letters and "χρυσίον, ποσὸν λιτρῶν ἕξ". MEYER 6104-6106: the eighty monks were fixed "ἀπὸ τῆς τοῦ µαχκαριωτάτου βασιλέως τοῦ κυροῦ Νικηφόρου διαταγής ἐν τῷ εὐσεβεῖ χρυσοβουλλίφ αὐτοῦ".
- The Typikon also says he was "οὕπω τῖς βασιλείας ἐπειλημμένου" (not yet emperor) when he funded it.
- Change: "Athanasios's own charter says the general Nikephoros Phokas paid for it, and backed it again once he became emperor." Or cut "Later accounts say".

**S3.** "The loose scatter of hermits already living there began to take the shape it has kept ever since." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.**
- The hermits were already there: MEYER 5447-5450 names "κὺρ Στέφανος ὁ τηνικαῦτα κατὰ τὸ ὄρος πρωτεύων ... καὶ οἳ λοιποὶ Ὑέροντες", who gave a place to anyone "προαιρούμενον Ἡσυχαζειν".
- But the record shows a quarrel, not a quiet taking of shape. In MEYER 7572-7578, Tzimiskes's Typikon, the protos and the monk Paul tell the emperor of "σχάνδαλά τινα nal φιλονειχίας ἐφ᾽ ἵκανοὺς χρόνους ... μεταξὺ αὐτῶν τε καὶ ᾿Αθανασίου ... ὡς ὅτι περικόπτονταί τινες χαὶ ἀδικοῦνται παρ᾽ αὐτοῦ". Leaving that out drops the record's conflict (fault four).
- "Kept ever since" is not shown. MEYER's own later documents show change: Urkunde XIV, Pachomios "gegen das idiorrhythmische System" (about 1530), and the new constitution of 1864.
- Change: "The hermits already there resented the new house. They took their quarrel to the emperor, who sent a monk to make peace and laid down a rule for the whole mountain." Cut "the shape it has kept ever since".

**S4.** "Athos became a self-governing league of monasteries." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- MEYER 7701-7714, Tzimiskes: no hegumen may correct a fault "ἐχτὸς εἰδήσεως τοῦ πρώτου", and the protos may not act "ἄνευ συνελεύσεως τῶν Ἡγουμένων τοῦ ὄρους βουλῆς τε καὶ γνώμης αὐτῶν".
- MEYER 7590-7593: the monks' cases are not to be judged "παρὰ ἀρχοντικῶν προσώπων".
- MEYER 5789: no one is set over the Lavra, "μήτε πατριάρχου μήτε" (and so on).
- Note: the self-rule was laid down by an emperor's typikon and an emperor's envoy. "Self-governing" holds only with that understood.

**S5.** "Each ran on a written charter, called a typikon." **NOT FOUND. BLOCKING.**
- The Library shows typika for the Lavra (MEYER Urkunde I), for the mountain as a whole (Tzimiskes 972, Monomachos 1045) and for one kellion (Sabbas, about 1195, MEYER Urkunde VII). It shows nothing for each house.
- I searched MEYER for "τυπικ" and "Typikon" across the whole file.
- Change: "The Lavra had its own written rule, called a typikon, and an emperor's typikon governed the whole mountain."

**S6.** "In it the founder set down exactly what the house was for, down to the diet, the liturgy and the charity." **ASSUMPTION ADDED. BLOCKING.**
- The detailed diet and liturgy for the Lavra are in the Hypotyposis. MEYER 7346-7355: "ἰχνδύων ... τυροῦ καὶ ὠοῦ"; "οὔτε ἔλαιον ἐσθίομεν, οὔτε οἶνον πίνοµεν". MEYER 7016-7135 covers the order of matins, vespers and the hours. But its title is "[Υποτύπωσις χαταστάσεως τῆς λαύρας τοῦ ὁσίου Ἀθανασίου]" (MEYER 6966-6972), and the record leaves open whether the founder wrote it (it appears only in the later of the two old codices).
- Charity in the founder's own texts is thin: in the Diatyposis a newcomer's gift is to go "πτωχοῖς" (MEYER 6343), and the Hypotyposis mentions a "ξενοδοχεῖον" (MEYER 7255).
- A founder who does set down all three is Gregory Pakourianos, outside Athos (PAKOURIANOS 8118, alms "εἰς τοὺς πτωχοὺς"; 8156, a "γηροκομεῖον"; 8160, "τὰ τρία ξενοδοχεῖα"; 9198, food for the poor each day; 9206, the wine ration).
- Change: tell it as the record gives it. Either "The Lavra's rule set out its meals, fasts and services hour by hour," or name Pakourianos's typikon of 1083 for a founder's own charter covering diet, worship and charity.

**S7.** "In Constantinople at the same time, Symeon the New Theologian preached to his monks." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- CALECAS 19588-19590: "Symeon abbas sancti Mamantis in Xerocerco, dictus ... junior theologus". CALECAS 19740-19741: "Hinc crebrae Symeonis ad suos catecheses seu paraeneses". CALECAS 19752: his monks are "in urbe" (in the City).
- PHIL2 19936-19940: "νέος ἐπιχληθεὶς Θεολόγος ... περίπου τὸ χιλιοστὸν ἀπὸ Χριστοῦ ἔτος".
- "At the same time" is loose but fair: Symeon's abbacy overlaps Athanasios's last decades.

**S8.** "He taught that God can be known directly and consciously, in experienced light." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- PHIL2 20852-20855, Symeon's chapters: there is someone in this generation, like the apostles, "θείῳ ἐνεργούμενος, ха! χινούμενος Πνεύματι: ἢ ἐν ὀπτασίᾳ τούτου γνωστῶς καὶ ἐν αἰσθήσει γινόμενος".
- PHIL2 21080-21085: "ἔνδον αὐτοῦ τὸ φῶς τοῦ Παναγίου Πνεύματος ἔχων ... ὁρᾶν".
- CALECAS 19621, from the Life: "γνωστῶς χεχτῆσθαι ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ".
- Note: "experienced light" should be heard as the light of the Spirit, seen. That is Symeon's own term.

**S9.** "He pressed the claim so hard that he was pushed out of his monastery." **ASSUMPTION ADDED. BLOCKING.**
- The Life, as Combefis summarises it, gives a different cause: Symeon's yearly feast for his own spiritual father, Symeon the Studite, and the hostility of the synkellos Stephen. CALECAS 20117-20121: "donec Stephanus ... coepit exagitare, inque Symeone venerabili quaedam reprehendere, ipsumque proinde ejus religiosum cultum ... improbare". CALECAS 20268-20271: "jussus Symeon aut a Patris illis laudibus cessare, aut coenobio urbeque excedere. Nolenti cedere, decreta synodice exsilii poena. Deportatus ad Chrysopolim".
- He was exiled by a synod from the monastery and from the City, then recalled and offered a see. He chose to stay outside the City and built a new house (CALECAS 20276-20284).
- An earlier revolt by about thirty of his monks ended with them, not Symeon, being expelled (CALECAS 19743-19752).
- Change: "He was put on trial for honoring his own teacher as a saint. When he would not stop, a synod sent him into exile across the Bosporus." Or cut.

**S10.** "Between them, the houses of Athos and the monasteries of the capital kept the practice of inner prayer alive." **SUMMARY HOLDS** (as what came of them, on a reliable later witness).
- PHIL2 42213-42220, Palamas, Triads ("Ὑπὲρ τῶν ἱερῶς ἡσυχαζόντων"): "Συμεῶνος γὰρ τοῦ νέου Θεολόγου τὸν βίον οἶσθα ... τά τε συγγράμματα αὐτοῦ, συγγράμματα ζωῆς ... ха! Νικηφόρον δὲ τὸν ὅσιον ἐχεῖνον, ὅς πολυετῇ χρόνον ἐν ἠρεμίᾳ, καὶ ἡσυχίᾳ διενεγχών".
- The Athonite Nikephoros's own tract is at `cic/texts/nicephorus-callistus_historia-ecclesiastica-with-hesychast-tracts-pg147-grc-lat_migne1865.txt` 38202ff, "De sobrietate et cordis custodia".
- Note: "kept alive" implies the practice was near dying out. The witness does not say so. "Handed on" would be closer.

**S11.** "The next century would gather it up and name it hesychasm." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.**
- "Gather it up" holds for Palamas (S10), but his Triads are fourteenth-century. After a sentence set around 1000, "the next century" points the listener to the twelfth (fault three, a date moved).
- "Name it" is wrong. The name is already in the tenth-century documents. MEYER 7650-7651, Tzimiskes 972: "κελλιώτας τε καὶ ἠσυχαστὰς ἅπαντας". MEYER 5450: "προαιρούμενον Ἡσυχαζειν".
- Change: "On Athos those who prayed in stillness were already called hesychasts. In the fourteenth century Gregory Palamas defended their way and named Symeon among its fathers."

## The heart

**The heart is not right, and it is not complete.** The opening tells who, when and where by its first sentence, which is good. But:
- The subject is "Athonite and Comnenian" monasticism, about 963-1300, and the brief stops at about 1000. Nothing Comnenian is told, though the Library holds Comnenian material: MEYER Urkunde VI, "Erzählungen aus der Zeit der Kaiser Alexios und Johannes Komnenos (1081-1143)", and PAKOURIANOS (1083). Evergetis and Pantokrator are not vendored.
- What they faced is softened or changed. The hermits' quarrel with Athanasios is dropped (S3), and Symeon's exile is given the wrong cause (S9).
- No ordinary monk appears. That is a trap the kind kit names.
- At 152 words the brief is under the kind's two-minute floor. The record here is not too thin to carry more.

**Verdict: BLOCKING.** The heart should be the founding at the Lavra and the quarrel it caused, the typikon as the shape of life, Symeon's light and his exile as the Life tells them, and what the Comnenian founders added, all told by about word 150.
