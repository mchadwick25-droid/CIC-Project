## 4. Traps found

- **Two columns run together in the OCR of Ildefonsus's De viris illustribus** (ILD l.17040-17101): the Braulio and Eugenius II chapters interleave. Read Migne's two-column OCR column by column.
- **Amalarius, not Alcuin:** ALC l.101223 is Amalarius of Trier's letter, printed as a cross-reference only.
- **PO volumes held under misleading names:** the "chronicle-of-seert ... po5" file opens with Evetts's History of the Patriarchs (Agatho to Michael I). The "synaxaire ... po11" file also holds Agapius of Manbij (Kitab al-Unvan), the Life of Luke the Stylite, the Coptic Life of Isaac, and Ammonas. The "synaxaire ... po1" file holds notices of the Coptic patriarchs (Yusab at l.20273).
- **Held but missed:** the Serpos Compendio (t1-t3, 1786) is held; it was named "not held" in the councils draft. The Kyiv Synopsis (1762) holds a Vladimir and Olga retelling. Nikodemos's Synaxaristes (2 vols., Greek, accent-garbled OCR) holds feast-day notices, e.g. Methodios at vol. 1 l.45597. None of these is a WANTED source named in a row. They are late, second-witness texts, but each row should say so.
- **Heraclius's "disclaimer":** Mansi10 l.73363-73374 is an editor's note quoting a Latin version of a letter that calls itself a *jussio* to Pope John ("Hanc feci jussionem"). It is an imperial order denying authorship, not a personal confession, so Heraclius stays STORY.
- **Namesakes:** Thomas of Kashkar (Budge l.17352) is not Thomas of Marga. Erimbert, Gautbert's relative (ANJOU l.26035-26038), is not Rimbert (l.26046). Eugenius II is the man in ILD cap. XIV. The "Maximus monachus" among the Lateran signatures (Mansi10 l.92672, next to an "Anastasius monachus") is not identified as the Confessor in the text.
- **Dates that conflict inside held texts:** Alopen's arrival is 635 in Malech l.13690 and "about 630" in Wigram l.4188. Gregory of Narek's Song commentary was written at 26 (Chamich) or at 20 (Serpos). The 1054 bull is dated 16 July in the text but 13 July in Mirbt's heading. Hertford is 670, 673 or 674 depending on the edition and the line.
- **Specific doubts tested:** Alopen holds as WHO (judgment call). His one witness is the community's own stele, which gives his arrival, the translations, the 638 edict, a church and the later title "great conservator of doctrine" (Malech l.13741); the two-witness rule applies only to founders known through enemies. Charlemagne stays STORY: the Admonitio and the letter on studies are capitularies, and the Libri Carolini are not held. Hincmar holds as WHO: he is a recorder, and the editor's reasons for his authorship from 861 are at BERT l.200-219. Dhuoda's verdict is Mark's call. For the Coptic patriarchs, read lightly, the substance holds on the PO5 entries; the cell texts and the route are fixed above.
