## How it was done

1. Four drafting workers (Sonnet) took a quarter of the rows each. For every row they read the scan row, the Library's record and the combined list, opened the filled files in `cic/texts/` and cited the lines that carry a life or the person's own words. They also judged whether the life is early or a later retelling, from the volume's own statements.
2. One fresh Opus agent at full effort, which had not seen the drafting, opened every cited range for all 91 checked rows and checked each verdict against the rules (`Opus_Check_Notes.md` in this folder). It confirmed 63 rows and corrected 28. None was unsupported. Of the corrections, 9 fix line numbers, 15 fix a claim, a quotation or a flag, and 4 change a verdict or a label (one of them splits a pair in two). The verdicts in the tables below are the verdicts after the check.
3. Major marks are carried from `Wanted_Sources.md` and tested against the map's caps (below).

## The rules applied

These are the rulings blocks of the three scans (era 4 lines 15-21, era 5 lines 15-23, supplement lines 35-52), as the scans state them:

- WHO is church-shaping, with a body of work or a following and enough life to tell.
- Recorders are WHO, with a thin own life flagged.
- Women are WHO if they founded, taught or led.
- Rulers, and popes acting through official texts, are STORY unless they have their own voice.
- Founders known only through enemies need two witnesses for the teaching. The life may rest on one.
- The later-retelling rule: a later Life alone gives LISTED until an early source is held. Length is not the test.
- The pairing rule (Mark, 2026-11-11): "if two people are tied together in their sources with similar identity its ok to make that a single who".
- The Melanchthon rule: a person's own held work, plus a following or held witnesses, can carry a tellable life without a formal Life. The thin life is flagged.
- A WANTED row stays WANTED only while a named source that is not held would carry a life.
- OUTSIDE WITNESS is a WHO label for historians outside the church. A Christian writer is inside Christianity, whatever the church (Decision-Log, era 7 and era 8 scans).
- Mark's rights rulings (2026-11-14, relayed by the coordinator): Hadewijch only in the 1875 dbnl edition, and Anna Komnene in Greek only.

## Calls made in the re-score, for Mark

These are worker and coordinator calls under the settled rules. Each one names its alternative.

- **Þorgeir** is STORY, not LISTED. Ari wrote about 120 years later, from named informants. The Vladimir and Olga ruling waits for the Primary Chronicle, with a gap of the same size, as the early source, so Ari counts as early here. Þorgeir is a law-speaker with no own words, so he is STORY. The alternative is LISTED.
- **Giorgi Merchule** is WHO as a recorder with a very thin life. The Life names him as its redactor, gives its date (951) and speaks in his person. The alternative is LISTED.
- **Domentijan and Teodosije** are split. Domentijan is WHO, as a recorder. Teodosije stays WANTED: his reworked Life of Sava is not held, and Daničić's preface puts it about a century later, which would make him an era 6 row by date of work. The held preface also undercuts the pairing.
- **Marco Polo** loses the OUTSIDE WITNESS label. He is a Venetian Catholic, and the era 7 and era 8 calls keep Christian travelers and envoys inside Christianity. He is WHO as a recorder and traveler.
- **Anna Komnene** is WHO as a recorder, in Greek only, without the label. This matches the change order of Open-Gaps 230 (Decision-Log, "Change-order items of Open-Gaps 229 and 230"). The scan row's own text both applies the label and calls "WANTED without the label", and an earlier coordinator call gave it on the Procopius precedent. The era 7 rule now settles it for a Christian writer, but she and Procopius are treated differently, and Mark may want to see that.
- **Innocent III** is STORY under the pope rule. The contemporary Gesta now carries his life, but only his Registers are held. None of his own non-chancery works (De miseria, his sermons) is held anywhere in the Library. He is a live person entry, so he is flagged, as Heraclius was ("Story, keep live").
- **Constantine-Silvanus and Sergius-Tychicus** are LISTED. Their teaching rests on Peter of Sicily alone. Theophanes Continuatus gives the Paulician wars, not their doctrine, and Photius and Peter the Higumen are not held. For Sergius, his own letters quoted by Peter could be ruled a second voice, which would make him WHO.
- **Basil the Physician** is LISTED. Anna and Zigabenos both go back to Alexios's record of Basil's own statement, so the teaching has one witness.
- **Cyril III ibn Laqlaq** is LISTED. The only held life is Renaudot's retelling of 1713.
- **Berno** is LISTED. No Life exists, the Testament is not held, and the held glimpses are thin. The alternative is WHO with a thin life.
- **Needs-ruling Lives.** Some held Lives are shelved `needs-ruling`, because the Library could not date them. Where the held volume itself dates the Life, the table says so: Stephen the Younger, 42 years after his death; Michael's Life of Theodore the Studite, by a man who "cum ipso vixit"; the Vita Euthymii, 921 or soon after; the Life of John and Euthymius of Iviron, about 1042; Grigol's Life, 951. Clement of Ohrid's Life is disputed in the volume's own Monitum, and stays open. Robert of Molesme, Norbert, Agnes of Prague, Joachim, Stephen Harding and Gregory Pakourianos keep their flags. The detail is in the table.
