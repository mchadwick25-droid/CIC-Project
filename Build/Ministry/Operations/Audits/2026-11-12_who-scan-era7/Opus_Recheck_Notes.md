# Opus independent recheck: era 7 (1517-1650) WHO scan

Read-only. No paid calls, no repo edits. All files are in `cic/texts/`. Line numbers are file lines (grep -n / sed -n). Quotes are copied from the OCR and keep its errors. Where an OCR reading carries a claim, check the page image before canon.

Standard applied (coordinator): WHO = shaped the church, and own held work plus a following or held witnesses carries a tellable life. WANTED only if the Library holds nothing that carries the life. Women: one held source showing her shaping the church = WHO. Founders known through enemies need two independent witnesses for the teaching.

One gap in the standard, noted openly: some figures have a life carried by held witnesses but no held work of their own. The standard does not make them WANTED, since something carries the life. It also does not fully meet the WHO clause, since there is no own work. I mark these **WHO (flag: witness-only)**. That matches how final_table.md already treats Ferenc Dávid. The coordinator should confirm this reading.

## Task 3. Ferenc Dávid: CONFIRMED **WHO**. A second independent witness is held, and his chief opponent at that.

- Przypkowski's Life of Socinus (Bidle tr. 1653), `przypkowski_life-of-faustus-socinus_bidle1653.txt` 729-750: "the Transylvanian Churches were extremely infested with the opinion of Franciscus Davidis and others touching the Honour and Power of Christ ... - Lubieniecki 1685, `lubieniecki_historia-reformationis-polonicae-lat_1685.txt` 11662-11690 (chapter on Blandrata and Socinus against "Franciscum Davidis pro Christo invocando"; the 1578 Torda synod at 11683).

Przypkowski and Lubieniecki both lean on Socinus, so count Socinus as the second independent witness and the other two as support.

## Task 4. Jean Ingoro and Jean Tingoro: NOT SETTLED. The held evidence leans toward **two men** but does not prove it.
- Pagès indexes them separately. `pages_histoire-religion-chretienne-japon-annexes-fra_1870.txt` 23833: "IHCORO (lettre de Jean), A, 61." (OCR for INGORO); 25819: "TINGORO (lettres de Jean), A, 89 et 90."; 23198-23200: "FATTORI TINGORO (Jean), jifiaque, 78, 80, 101, 117, 139, 15?, martyr, 171." The narrative pages (78-171) are in the Texte volume, which is not held.
- Ingoro (Annexe 9, 3006-3131): a letter to the Christians of Yatsushiro (3006). His "patrie" is Mouro, granted to Augustin Konishi (3039-3041: "Augustin Fingonocami reçut en apanage les terres de Mouro, ma patrie"). He was converted at Yatsushiro by Diego Konishi Mimasaka after Korea (3050-3054). He was charged with taking the bodies of "Madeleine, Inès, Jean, et le petit Luis" to Nagasaki (3132-3135). The text does not say whether he died.
- Tingoro (Annexes 17 and 17 bis, 4282-4420): he writes "Après la sentence" (4284). He was baptized by Baeza about ten years before (4316-4318, 4391-4392), and he says "le Fingo n'étant pas ma patrie d'origine" (4337-4338). He had wife and children (4288-4289) and four years of prison (4298-4299), and he was executed on 11 January 1609 (4390-4391: "le martyre se trouva différé jusqu'au 11 janvier").
- What points to one man: the names differ by a single letter, both men were Christians held in Yatsushiro prisons in the same years, and both letters are taken from the Italian relation "Morte di IX Christiani". Tingoro's source note is at 4306 ("Morte di IX Christiani, p. 90"). Ingoro's note at 3060 is OCR-garbled ("Il6rte4i S Ourittiaiti, p. 104") and may be the same title.
- What points to two: Pagès's separate index entries (I and T), and the native-land statements, which may be in tension (Mouro under Konishi, against "Fingo not my native land"; whether Mouro lay in Higo is not shown in the held text). Ingoro also never mentions a sentence, a wife, or Baeza.
- Still doubtful: whether Pagès's narrative pages 78-171 treat them as one, and whether the p.104 note is "Morte di IX Christiani" (check the page image). Keep two rows, and add a cross-reference that a possible identity is Inferential-Thin.
