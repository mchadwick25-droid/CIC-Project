Then the sudden sail and the silence, Polycarp, the later voices, and Antioch's feast. The general-history lecture is gone, so Ignatius stays in view from start to finish. It sounds like a person telling it.

It runs four separate claims in a row (Polycarp's letters, the death, Irenaeus, three centuries), and a listener who is new to it hears a list. That is a "could be stronger" point, not a finding.
