**1. "...in seven letters that bear his name, though some have doubted that he wrote them."** (brief line 3)
- ANF 4528–4532 (v.i-p15): some "refused to acknowledge the authenticity of these Epistles in either of the recensions"; the prior check (ANF 4526–4568) also records those who denied any authentic remains. The new wording covers doubt about all seven, as the prior fix required.

The sentence does not say "only seven", so it does not misstate; the prior fix's "known under his name" was the tighter form.

**2. "The letters name no charge, no judge and no year."** (line 5)
- Eph 1, ANF 4703: bound "for the common name and hope" — a reason, not a charge. No judge named (prior check). No year: Rom 10 gives a day only (Source_Sheet D2, Q11); the emperor in Pol 8 is the translator's bracket (S9, ANF 8856–8858).
- **SUPPORTED.** It follows the prior fix exactly.

**3. "He urged the churches of Asia to hold together around their bishops, elders and deacons."** (line 11)
- Magn 6, ANF 5851–5856 ("your presbyters ... along with your deacons"); Trall 2, ANF 6308–6316 (bishop, presbytery, deacons); Magn 7, ANF 5888–5909.
- **SUPPORTED.** Now tied to Asia, so Rome is excluded. It states what he urged, not how the churches were led (S8(b)). The prior caution that Ephesians (shorter) does not urge deacons does not break a claim about the churches of Asia as a group.

**4. "In the same letter he asked for any surer news of Ignatius and those with him."** (line 13)
- Phil 13, ANF 3619–3623: "Any more certain information you may have obtained respecting both Ignatius himself, and those that were with him, have the goodness to make known". Note 414 (ANF 3619–3620): from here to the end "we have only the Latin version". Note 415: "The Latin version reads 'are,' which has been corrected as above."
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** "Surer news" fairly renders "more certain information". "Those with him" leaves the tense open, which avoids choosing between the Latin "are" and the editors' "were". Caution: the request survives only in Latin, and "the same letter" places it in a passage whose Greek is lost. Nothing in the sentence is wrong, but it should not be quoted as Polycarp's Greek.

**5. "By the late fourth century, Antioch kept his feast, and his remains had been brought home to the city."** (line 17)
- Feast: CHR 9955–9956, "To-day again, this blessed and noble martyr Ignatius has succeeded to her feast"; CHR 9800–9801, the homilies were "delivered by Chrysostom on the festivals of saints and martyrs".
- Remains brought home: CHR 10345–10372 ("ye were honoured with his remains", "his remains being brought back", "bearing him upon their shoulders as far as this city"). Jerome, Lives 16, NP3 39829–39831: "the remains of his body lie in Antioch outside the Daphnitic gate in the cemetery."
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** Both facts are in the held sources. "Late fourth century" rests on the standard modern dates of Chrysostom's Antioch preaching and of Jerome's *Lives*; the held passages do not state a year. "Antioch kept his feast" is attested for Chrysostom's own day; the sentence does not claim more.

**27. "Across three centuries, later writers quoted those letters in their arguments over who Christ was."** (line 15)

It now covers Athanasius on whether the Son was made (De Synodis 47, ATH 59204–59232), Theodoret on the two natures (NP3 20245–20292, 23238–23256, 27117–27131), and Jerome and Eusebius, as the prior check lists them.
- **SUPPORTED.**
