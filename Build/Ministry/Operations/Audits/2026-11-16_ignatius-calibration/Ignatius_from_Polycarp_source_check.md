# Source check: Ignatius_from_Polycarp.brief.txt

Sentence-by-sentence check of the brief against the source files themselves, 2026-11-16. Not a style review. The sheets in this folder were used only to find lines; every line below was read in the file named.

**Files** (all in `cic/texts/`):
- ANF = `anf01_apostolic-fathers-justin-irenaeus.xml` (Ignatius, shorter recension; Polycarp, *Philippians*; ANF introduction)
- EUS = `npnf201_eusebius-church-history-life-of-constantine.xml` (Eusebius, *Church History*, McGiffert)
- NP3 = `npnf203_theodoret-jerome-gennadius-rufinus.xml` (Jerome, *Lives* 16; Theodoret, *Dialogues*)

- ATH = `npnf204_athanasius-select-works-letters.xml` (Athanasius, *De Synodis* 47)

**Quotations.** All ten quoted passages were matched by script against `Renderings_Compared.md`. Each one is a single unbroken run of its settled reading, word for word. No quotation has a cut inside it. Where a quotation begins or ends partway through a settled reading, the cut falls at a sentence edge at the start or end (Q2, Q10, Q14, Q26). The one change is the final full stop of Q17 turned into a comma before "he wrote", which is normal punctuation for a quotation.

## Paragraph 1

**1.** "I am God's wheat. Let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, so that I may be found to be Christ's pure bread."
- Source: Ignatius, Romans 4, shorter recension. ANF 7040–7042: "I am the wheat of God, and let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of Christ."
- It matches the settled reading Q1 exactly.
- **SUPPORTED.** The wording differs between witnesses (Irenaeus has "wheat of Christ ... bread of God"; Jerome has "bread of the world"). The brief follows the shorter recension, as the sheet directs. That note belongs on the card, not in the brief.

## Paragraph 2

**2.** A prisoner wrote those words early in the second century.
- Prisoner: ANF 4702–4704 (Eph 1, "I came bound from Syria"); ANF 7083–7087 (Rom 5).
- Century: no held source gives a year (see 6). Eusebius sets him under Trajan (EUS 17004–17008, III.22); Jerome gives "the eleventh year of Trajan" (NP3 39829–39830).
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** "Second century" comes from modern dating of Trajan's reign. No held source says it. It is the plain form the sheet allows (S9).

**3.** He was Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, in Syria.
- His own words: "the bishop of Syria" (ANF 6961–6962, Rom 2). His church is "the Church which is at Antioch in Syria" (Philad 10 and Smyrn 11, per the sheet's lines).
- "Bishop of Antioch": Origen, "episcopum Antiochiae" (ORI 7169–7170); Eusebius, "chosen bishop of Antioch" (EUS 19740–19741).
- **SUPPORTED.** He never writes "bishop of Antioch" in the shorter letters. The phrase is the later witnesses' (Widely Accepted). That is fine for the brief.

**4.** Soldiers were taking him from Syria to Rome, where he expected to face the wild beasts.
- ANF 7083–7087 (Rom 5): "From Syria even unto Rome I fight with beasts ... being bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers."
- ANF 4703–4704 (Eph 1): "trusting ... to be permitted to fight with beasts at Rome."
- ANF 5584–5585 (Eph 21): "whence I am led bound to Rome."
- **SUPPORTED.**

**5.** He tells of the journey himself, in seven letters that bear his name, though some have doubted that all of them are his.
- Seven letters: Eusebius names seven and says where each was written (EUS 19961–19992, III.36.5–6). Jerome names the same seven (NP3 39785–39794).
- More letters bear his name: "There are, in all, fifteen Epistles which bear the name of Ignatius" (ANF 4490–4498). Eight of them are "by common consent set aside as forgeries" (ANF 4500–4506).
- The doubt about the seven: ANF 4526–4568. Daillé (1666) "refused to acknowledge the authenticity of these Epistles in either of the recensions". Others went "so far as to deny that we have any authentic remains of Ignatius at all". Others suspected interpolation. Cureton held that only the three Syriac letters show what he wrote.
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** Two points.
  - "Seven letters that bear his name" is true, but fifteen bear it. Seven are the set Eusebius knew.
  - "That all of them are his" makes the doubt sound smaller than it is. The source shows doubt about whether any of them are his, and in what form. That is the Contested ruling in D1.
- Fix the source requires: "seven letters known under his name, though some have doubted that he wrote them." That covers the doubt about all seven, and about their form.

**6.** The letters do not say why he was arrested or who condemned him, and they give no year.
- ANF 4703 (Eph 1): he came bound "for the common name and hope".
- ANF 7316–7319 (Rom 10): a day ("the day before the ninth of the Kalends of September"), but no year. The "twenty-third day of August" is a gloss (note at 7317).
- No letter names a judge.
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** "Do not say why" is slightly too strong. He does say he was bound "for the common name and hope", that is, as a Christian. What the letters lack is the charge or occasion, the judge and the place.
- Fix the source requires: "The letters say only that he was bound for the name of Christ. They do not say who condemned him, and they give no year." Or keep the sentence's shape but not the flat "why".

**7.** Later writers place him in the reign of the emperor Trajan, in a time of persecution.
- Eusebius: Trajan's accession (EUS 16977); "At this time Ignatius was known as the second bishop of Antioch" (EUS 17004–17008). Trajan's persecution and Pliny follow (EUS 19446–19451, III.33.1), before Ignatius's chapter (III.36, EUS 19676 ff.).
- Jerome: "condemned to the wild beasts during the persecution of Trajan" (NP3 39787–39789).
- Origen: "in persecutione Romae" (ORI 7170–7171).
- **SUPPORTED.**

**8.** Of the road he wrote, "I am chained to ten leopards, I mean a squad of soldiers."
- Romans 5. ANF 7083–7087: "being bound to ten leopards, I mean a band of soldiers."
- It matches the second sentence of Q4, whole.
- **SUPPORTED.** The source frames it as the road: "From Syria even unto Rome ... by land and sea".

## Paragraph 3

**9.** He came to Smyrna, a city in the Roman province of Asia, where Polycarp was bishop, and the two met.
- Smyrna "in Asia": ANF 7938 (Smyrn address).
- He wrote from Smyrna: ANF 5581–5582 (Eph 21, "whence also I write to you").
- Polycarp as bishop: ANF 6219–6223 (Magn 15, "along with Polycarp, the bishop of the Smyrnæans").
- They met: ANF 8550–8552 (Pol 1, "thought worthy [to behold] thy blameless face"; the note glosses this as personal acquaintance).
- Eusebius: "when he came to Smyrna, where Polycarp was" (EUS 19961–19962).
- **SUPPORTED.**

**10.** Other churches sent their leaders to see the prisoner.
- Magnesia: ANF 5673–5677 (Magn 2). "I have had the privilege of seeing you, through Damas your most worthy bishop", with presbyters Bassus and Apollonius and the deacon Sotio.
- Tralles: ANF 6278–6280 (Trall 1). "Polybius your bishop ... who has come to Smyrna".
- **SUPPORTED.**

**11.** Ephesus sent its bishop, Onesimus, and four others, and Ignatius wrote back to their churches.
- ANF 4708–4710 (Eph 1): "through Onesimus ... your bishop in the flesh".
- ANF 4746–4757 (Eph 2): Burrhus the deacon, Crocus, "together with Onesimus, and Burrhus, and Euplus, and Fronto". So Onesimus plus four: Burrhus, Crocus, Euplus and Fronto.
- ANF 5579–5581 (Eph 21): "those whom ... ye have sent to Smyrna".
- The letters to Ephesus, Magnesia and Tralles are written from Smyrna (Eph 21, ANF 5581; Magn 15, ANF 6219–6220; Trall 1, ANF 6280). See also EUS 19961–19986.
- **SUPPORTED.**

**12.** Then he wrote ahead to the Christians in Rome and begged them not to save him.
- Rome from Smyrna: ANF 7308–7309 (Rom 10).
- His plea: ANF 6957–6958 (Rom 2, "if you show your love to my flesh, I shall again have to run my race"). ANF 7036–7038 (Rom 4, "I shall willingly die for God, unless ye hinder me. I beseech of you not to show an unseasonable good-will towards me").
- Eusebius: "entreating them not to secure his release from martyrdom" (EUS 19988–19990).
- **SUPPORTED.** "Then" follows the order in Eusebius and Jerome, where Rome comes after the other three (EUS 19985–19990; NP3 39790–39791). Ignatius himself does not give an order.

**13.** "I beg you, do not show me kindness at the wrong time. Let me become food for the wild beasts. Through them I will be allowed to reach God."
- Romans 4. ANF 7036–7040.
- It matches the last three sentences of Q2, unbroken. It begins after Q2's first sentence.
- **SUPPORTED.**
- Order note: in the letter these words come directly before the wheat saying (sentence 1, ANF 7040). The brief quotes them later. Each quotation is framed on its own, and the brief makes no claim about their order, so nothing is misstated.

**14.** For his own people he asked this: "Remember in your prayers the church in Syria. It now has God for its shepherd instead of me."
- **The letter is Romans**, chapter 9. ANF 7273–7275: "Remember in your prayers the Church in Syria, which now has God for its shepherd, instead of me."
- It matches the first two sentences of Q10. The quotation ends there, at a sentence edge.
- The brief puts it in the paragraph on the Rome letter, which is correct.
- Eph 21 (ANF 5584), Magn 14 and Trall 13 ask for prayer for Syria too. Only Romans has these words.
- **SUPPORTED.**

## Paragraph 4

**15.** From Smyrna, and then from Troas, he sent warnings to the churches of Asia.
- From Smyrna: Trall 9–10 (ANF 6669–6682), Magn, Eph.
- From Troas: Philad 11 (ANF 7895–7896), Smyrn 12 (ANF 8466–8469), Pol 8 (ANF 8853–8854).
- The cities are in Asia: ANF 7938, and the sheet's addresses at ANF 4669, 5619, 6247, 7335.
- **SUPPORTED.**

**16.** Some, he said, taught that Christ only seemed to suffer.
- ANF 6670–6671 (Trall 10): "some that are without God ... say, that He only seemed to suffer".
- Also Smyrn 2 (ANF 8007–8008, per the sheet).
- **SUPPORTED.**

**17.** He would not write their names, and their side is not heard.
- ANF 8143–8145 (Smyrn 5): "I have not, however, thought good to write the names of such persons, inasmuch as they are unbelievers."
- No held source records their own words (N8).
- **SUPPORTED.** The refusal appears in one letter, to Smyrna, written from Troas. The sentence does not claim more.

**18.** He answered with his own chains.
- ANF 6671–6672 (Trall 10, "then why am I in bonds?").
- Smyrn 4 (ANF 8102–8109, per Q15, "then am I also only in appearance bound").
- **SUPPORTED.**

**19.** "But if they are right, why am I in chains? Why do I long to fight the wild beasts? Do I die for nothing, then?"
- **The letter is Trallians**, chapter 10, written from Smyrna. ANF 6669–6681: "But if, as some ... say, that He only seemed to suffer (they themselves only seeming to exist), then why am I in bonds? Why do I long to be exposed to [lit. 'fight with'] the wild beasts? Do I therefore die in vain?"
- It matches the third to fifth sentences of Q14, unbroken. Q14's last sentence ("Am I not lying about the Lord?") is left off at the end. Nothing is cut inside.
- "They" picks up "Some" in sentence 16, which matches the source's subject.
- **SUPPORTED.** This is a different passage from the "ten leopards" chains (sentence 8, Romans 5).

**20.** He urged each church to hold together around its bishop, its elders and its deacons.
- All three offices are urged in Magn 6–7 (ANF 5896–5898, per the sheet), Trall 2–3, Philad 7 (ANF 7717–7720), Smyrn 8 (ANF 8274–8277) and Pol 6.
- Ephesians, shorter recension: bishop and presbytery are urged. Deacons appear there only as the named deacon Burrhus (ANF 4746). The line at ANF 4904 that urges all three is in the longer recension.
- Romans: no such counsel. Its only "bishop" is Ignatius himself (ANF 6961–6962).
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** "Each church" holds only if it means each church of Asia, as the paragraph sets up. It does not hold for Rome.
- Fix the source requires: tie it to the Asian churches, for example "He urged the churches of Asia to hold together around ...". The sheet's S8(b) rule, that the letters show only what he urged and not how all churches were led, is kept.

**21.** "Let no one do anything that concerns the church without the bishop," he wrote.
- Smyrnaeans 8. ANF 8277–8278: "Let no man do anything connected with the Church without the bishop."
- It matches the fourth sentence of Q17, whole.
- **SUPPORTED.**

## Paragraph 5

**22.** No letter tells what happened in Rome.
- The letters were written before he reached Rome (Rom 10, ANF 7308–7314, "I am at hand").
- Polycarp's letter gives no account of it (ANF 3482–3494, 3605–3631).
- **SUPPORTED.**

**23.** Polycarp, writing to the church at Philippi, named Ignatius among those who are "in their rightful place with the Lord, with whom they also suffered."
- Phil 9, ANF 3484–3492: "not only in the case of the blessed Ignatius, and Zosimus, and Rufus, but also ... in Paul himself, and the rest of the apostles ... that they are [now] in their due place in the presence of the Lord, with whom also they suffered."
- Eusebius quotes it: "they are gone to their rightful place beside the Lord" (EUS 20076–20084).
- It matches the end of Q26's fourth sentence, unbroken. The bracketed "[now]" is the translator's and is rightly left out.
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION** (dispute D7).
  - The words are Polycarp's, and the brief gives them as his.
  - The same letter, in ch. 13, also asks for "more certain information ... respecting both Ignatius himself, and those that were with him" (ANF 3619–3627). Only the Latin survives for that sentence (note at 3619), and the Latin reads "are with him", which the translator changed to "were" (note at 3622).
  - The ANF editor's view that Polycarp "was aware of the death" but not the details is an editor's note (ANF 3624–3626). It is card only.
  - Read straight after "No letter tells what happened in Rome", sentence 23 sounds like Polycarp's news of his death. Sentence 26 quotes ch. 13 but stops before the request for news, so the brief never gives the other half of D7.
- Fix the source requires: keep Polycarp's words, and tell, once, that the same letter asks for surer news of Ignatius. That is the sheet's own "What is uncertain" line: "counts him among those now with the Lord, yet asks for surer news of him." Without it, the brief settles D7 by what it leaves out.

**24.** About two hundred years later the historian Eusebius wrote, "It is reported that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts because of his witness to Christ."
- *Church History* III.36.3. EUS 19935–19936: "Report says that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts on account of his testimony to Christ."
- It matches the first sentence of Q29, whole. It is rightly told as report.
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** "About two hundred years" is an estimate. It rests on the usual modern dates for Eusebius and a death early in the second century (sheet C11). No held file states it. It is acceptable as a rough figure.

## Paragraph 6

**25.** The church at Philippi had asked for his letters.
- Phil 13. ANF 3613–3616: "we have sent to you, as you requested".
- **SUPPORTED.**

**26.** Polycarp replied, "As you asked, we have sent you the letters Ignatius wrote to us, and all the others we have."
- Phil 13. ANF 3612–3616: "The Epistles of Ignatius written by him to us, and all the rest [of his Epistles] which we have by us, we have sent to you, as you requested."
- Eusebius: EUS 20101–20103.
- It matches the third sentence of Q27, whole. "To us" is Smyrna: the letters to the Smyrnaeans and to Polycarp (note at 3612).
- **SUPPORTED.** See 23 for what the quotation leaves out after it.

**27.** Across three centuries, later writers quoted those letters to show that Christ truly came in the flesh.
- Who quoted them, and for what:
  - Origen, third century: Eph 19, on why Mary was betrothed, so that her virginity was hidden from the devil (ORI 7166–7180).
  - Eusebius: Smyrn 3, quoted "concerning Christ", as a sample of the letters, not to argue a point (EUS 20038–20040).
  - Jerome: the same passage, "in respect of the person of Christ" (NP3 39795–39803).
  - Athanasius: Eph 7, in the dispute over the Son. He uses it to show that Christ "was originate on account of the flesh ... yet unoriginate, because He is not in the number of things made" (ATH 59204–59232). His point is that the Son was not created, not that the flesh was real.
  - Theodoret, fifth century: Smyrn 1, 3 and 7, on "the assumed nature" and the two natures (NP3 20245–20292, 23238–23256, 27117–27131).
- **OVERSTATED.** "To show that Christ truly came in the flesh" fits Jerome and Theodoret, and Eusebius's choice of passage. It does not fit Athanasius's use, and only loosely fits Origen's. "Across three centuries" (third to fifth) holds only if Origen and Athanasius are counted. So the span and the stated purpose cannot both stand as written.
- Fix the source requires: either keep the span and widen the purpose ("later writers quoted those letters in their arguments over who Christ was"), or keep the purpose and narrow the span to the witnesses who used them for it (Eusebius and Jerome in the fourth century, Theodoret in the fifth).

## Paragraph 7

**28.** Antioch kept his feast.
- CHR 9953–9956, preached at Antioch: "To-day again, this blessed and noble martyr Ignatius has succeeded to her [Pelagia's] feast."
- Evagrius reports a yearly public festival later (EVA 2258–2285, per the sheet).
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.** The feast is attested from Chrysostom's day, late in the fourth century. No earlier witness is held. Placed just before the Chrysostom sentence, it reads correctly. On its own it could suggest the feast ran from his death onward.

**29.** Preaching there on that day, almost three hundred years after Ignatius, John Chrysostom said, "You sent out a bishop, and you received a martyr."
- Preached at Antioch on the feast: CHR 9955–9956, and "the Church among us" (CHR 9992).
- The quotation: CHR 10355 ("Ye sent forth a Bishop, and received a martyr"). It matches the first sentence of Q33, whole.
- **SUPPORTED WITH CAUTION.**
  - "Almost three hundred years" is an estimate. Chrysostom preached at Antioch in the late 380s or 390s; the sheet's figure is about 280 (C11). No held file states it.
  - In the source, "received" means the city received back his remains (CHR 10343–10367: "restored him again to us ... ye were honoured with his remains"). The brief does not say so. That is a matter of clarity, not an error.

## One listen through

- **Where a listener could get lost.**
  - Sentence 5 stacks three ideas (his own account, seven letters, a doubt) in one sentence.
  - "Troas" in sentence 15 is the one place not placed for the listener, though Smyrna is.
  - Sentence 27's "to show that Christ truly came in the flesh" arrives two paragraphs after the "only seemed to suffer" sentence it answers. A listener may not connect them.
  - Sentence 29's "you received a martyr" makes full sense only if the listener knows his remains came back to Antioch.
- **Where the hedges pile up.**
  - Paragraph 2 runs three limits in a row: "though some have doubted that all of them are his." / "The letters do not say why he was arrested or who condemned him, and they give no year." / "Later writers place him in the reign of the emperor Trajan".
  - Paragraph 5 repeats the pattern: "No letter tells what happened in Rome." then "It is reported that ...".
  - Paragraph 4 adds "and their side is not heard."
