# Ignatius of Antioch: round 1 review

Reviewer: Opus, cold. Read: the standard (`../2026-11-16_polycarp-clean-write/Standard_draft.md`), the benchmark `Polycarp_final.brief.txt` and the Decision-Log entry "Polycarp of Smyrna approved to proceed as the benchmark brief" only. Then `Ignatius.brief.txt`, `Ignatius.references.md`, `Writer_Sheet.md`, with `Source_Sheet.md` and the texts for lookups, and the published-lives survey as a lens only. Sentence numbers follow the trace table in `Ignatius.references.md` (32 sentences).

**Verdict: REVISE.** Two blocking findings. Both are quick to fix, and most of the brief can stay. The opening line, the wheat quote, the chains answer, "Then he had to sail suddenly, and his letters end", Polycarp sending the letters on, and the Chrysostom close are all good.

## 1. Listener read (once through, no going back)

**Where I was pulled in.** The opening holds. "I am chained to ten leopards, I mean a squad of soldiers" is strange, funny and grim all at once. It shows the man before we know his name. Sentence 2 names him and his situation cleanly.

**Where I drifted.**
- **Paragraph 2 (s4–5).** "Christians then gathered in households, in Antioch, in the cities of Asia Minor and in Rome. No central body ruled them, and letters carried by hand held them together." The story stops here and a lecture starts. Ignatius drops out of view for two sentences. This is the sentence the lead cut from Polycarp, nearly word for word.
- **Paragraph 3 (s7–9).** Three limits in a row before the story has started: "The record does not say why he was arrested, or who condemned him. He says only that... Later writers place this... but the exact year is not known." By the third I was listening to a list of what we don't know, not to a man on a road. Then come "some have doubted" (s13), "we have only his side of that quarrel" (s18), "though not every church was led that way then" (s20), "They show only that..." (s23) and "gives it as a report" (s24). That is eight honest-limit clauses in 32 sentences. Polycarp has one ("who say they saw it"). Each one is true. Together they make it sound like a report on the record, not a story told by someone who knows it.
- **s14.** "begging them not to save him." Why would a man beg not to be rescued? A newcomer needs the answer here, and the brief gives only a metaphor (God's wheat, Christ's bread). The standard says to give the reason for an act in the same sentence. The sheet has the reason in plain words: through the beasts "I will be allowed to reach God" (Q2), and by dying he may "become the disciple" (M1).
- **s17.** "In the other letters he defended two things." This is the sheet's [DEFENDED] label showing through. It sounds like an essay's topic sentence.
- **s19.** "If our Lord did these things..." On first hearing, "these things" points at nothing. "Only seemed to suffer" just before it half rescues it.
- **s23–24.** "Later writers say that he did die there, killed by wild beasts, and the church historian Eusebius gives it as a report." The "and" made me think Eusebius disagreed. A listener working in a second language will not see why it matters that one historian called it a report. It reads like a footnote spoken aloud.
- **s29.** "They used his words about the wheat..." "They" seems to mean s28's writers in the disputes. The wheat writers are a different group (Irenaeus, Eusebius, Jerome, Chrysostom; I3).

**Grade 5.9: plain or flat?** It is plain, and mostly not flat. The concrete images keep it alive: leopards, wheat, chains, the sudden sail. In two places it gets choppy:
- s10–13, four short statements with no joins: "Along the way he wrote. Seven letters bear his name..."
- s22–25: "...his letters end. They show only... The letters lived on."

Joining words come to 0.8 per 100, against 0.6 for Polycarp. So the choppy feel comes from the run of short negatives, not from any lack of joins. A grade this low passes by ear.

**A story or a report?** The first line and the last three paragraphs are a story. Paragraphs 2–3 and s23–24 are a report.

## 2. Against the benchmark

**Craft.** Ignatius has a harder record than Polycarp: letters, not an eyewitness narrative of one day. The writer found a real arc in it: the road, the letters written along the way, the sudden sail, the silence, the letters carried on, Antioch's feast. On the lead's specific point, **yes, it repeats the fault.** Sentences 4–5 describe early Christianity in general. They come from the census era line (CEN 31918), not from Ignatius's own record, and that is the exact sentence Mark deleted from Polycarp. Polycarp's craft level is not reached until s4–5 go and the limits are thinned.

**Rigor.** It is close to Polycarp's level. Every quote matches its final reading. No wonder is told. The late *Martyrdom* is kept out. The disputes (the letters, the year, the death) are told as disputes. There is one misstated group claim (s28) and one pronoun that attaches a use to the wrong people (s29).

**Accessibility.** The score is fine (grade 5.9, reading ease 82.2 with names out). It is less fluent than Polycarp because of the hedge runs.

## 3. The three parts

- **Opening (s1–9).** It has a strong hook. It sets the context in the right order: century (s3), place (s3), the road. But it then spends s4–5 on the age in general and s7–9 on what is unknown. The Decision-Log note on Polycarp applies here: "the church and the city can carry the tradition". "The bishop of the church in Antioch, in Syria" already does that.
- **Story (s10–24).** It is present and in order. The defended points (s17–20) sit inside the story, which works.
- **What came of him (s25–32, about 140 of 473 words, near a third).**
  - What he left: the letters gathered and sent on (I1, rank 1, with its carrying quote).
  - What he defended: s28 ties his defence to later use (I2, rank 2).
  - How far it reached: three centuries of disputes, the wheat as the martyr's model (I3), and Antioch's feast (I5).
  - Choice by weight: I1–I3 and I5–I6 are used and I4 (rank 4) is skipped. That is defensible for length, but see minor fix M2. I4 would also mend s23–24.
- **Ending.** It ends on what came of him, as an event (the feast and the sermon) in the record's own words. It is not a moral. The Chrysostom line carries I5, so it was not picked only because it sounds good. The shape is complete.

## 6. The page

Two things on the page produced the faults here.

1. **The setting line in "The three hands"** asks the researcher for "plain spoken words for... the tradition". The sheet duly offered the census era description as form 2. It then argued that form 2 "fits his own record closely". The writer followed both. This is the same fault the lead cut from Polycarp, so it comes from the page, not from the writer.
   - Replace: "the setting, with plain spoken words for the century and the tradition and the lines that support them"
   - With: "the setting, with plain spoken words for the century, and the tradition told through the person's own church and city, with the lines that support them; never a description of the age in general"
2. **The Rigor line** reads "Where the record stops, say so plainly". The sheet's D and N entries each tell the writer to "say" a limit (D1, D2, N2). Each line obeyed on its own gives eight limits.
   - Replace: "Where the record stops, say so plainly."
   - With: "Where the record stops, say so plainly, once, at the point a listener would ask; a gap the listener would not miss goes on the card."

The other faults, s14's missing reason and the s28 and s29 compressions, are writer and sheet faults, not page faults. The standard already requires the reason. The I2 summary on the sheet overstates its own examples.

## 7. Against the published lives

**What they do better, which the sheet supports and we miss.**
- They say why he refused rescue. Christian History Institute's "he will have to run his race again" and OrthodoxWiki's freedom from the fear of death both answer this. Our s14 does not, and the sheet holds the answer twice: Rom 2, "I shall again have to run my race" (M6); Q2, "reach God".
- They end, or close, on his humility. Christianity Today and Christian History Institute use "Now I begin to be a disciple". The sheet has it (Q6, warning only on "begrudge"; and Q3, "They were apostles; I am a condemned man"). It would give the listener a person, not only a position. This is optional, not a fault.

**What we avoid that they get wrong.** The brief is cleaner than every one of them:
- no *Martyrdom* scene (Trajan at Antioch, the Colosseum or Circus Maximus lions)
- no 107
- no Peter succession
- no claim that he was John's disciple
- no "first to say Catholic Church"
- no Domitian backdrop
- no moral at the end
- the claim about the letters told as disputed

The one thing we share with the weakest of them, the Angelus piece's era backdrop, is s4–5.

## Fixes, in order

**Blocking**
- **B1.** Delete s4–5, the general history of early Christianity. Let "bishop of Antioch in Syria" carry the tradition, as in the Polycarp ruling.
- **B2.** In s28, replace "testimony that Christ truly suffered" with "testimony that Christ had truly come in the flesh" (or the equivalent). The sheet's I2 summary needs the same correction.

**Minor**
- **M1.** Thin the limits to the ones a listener would ask about:
  - Keep s7 (why arrested) and s13 (the letters doubted).
  - Cut "but the exact year is not known" (s9).
  - Cut "we have only his side of that quarrel" (s18).
  - Recast s8 or drop it.
- **M2.** Replace s23–24 with the nearest witness, then the later ones. For example: "Soon after, Polycarp wrote of 'the blessed Ignatius' as one now with the Lord. Later writers say he died at Rome, by the wild beasts." This brings in I4 (rank 4), drops the footnote about Eusebius, and leads straight into Philippi's request.
- **M3.** Give s14 its reason from the sheet (Q2 or Rom 2).
- **M4.** In s20, use the record's own guard (Rome's letter names no bishop) in place of the census clause, and give the reason for the counsel (S7, M17).
- **M5.** In s29, change "They used" to "Others used".
- **M6.** Recast s17 so it does not read as a topic sentence. Join s10–13 so the road reads as one movement.
