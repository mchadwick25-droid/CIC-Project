## (c) What is uncertain (for the card)

- **Are the letters his?** Seven letters were known under his name by the fourth century, and letters of his were already passing between churches in Polycarp's day. Whether the seven shorter letters are his own words is disputed; some scholars place them decades later. The story says "seven letters bear his name" and that some have doubted them. (D1)
- **The year.** His letters name no emperor and no year. Eusebius and Jerome place him under Trajan; Jerome gives Trajan's eleventh year; a late legend gives the ninth. "Second century" rests on modern chronology. (S9, D2)
- **Where and how he died.** His letters show only that he expected to die at Rome by the beasts. Polycarp says he suffered and is with the Lord. From Origen on, the held witnesses say he died by the beasts at Rome; Eusebius gives it as a report. One sixth-century writer, Malalas, places his death at Antioch. No early source describes the death itself. (D3, D4, N4)
- **Why he was arrested.** Not in the record. (N2)
- **Bishop "of Antioch".** In the shorter letters he calls himself "the bishop of Syria" and names the church at Antioch; later writers call him bishop of Antioch. Whether he was second or third after Peter, and who appointed him, is disputed and is left out of the story. (S2, D5, D14)
- **His opponents.** We hear them only through him; he would not write their names. (S7, N8)
- **One bishop.** The letters show what he urged and what he found in the churches of Asia, not how all churches were led. His letter to Rome names no bishop there. (S8b, N9)
- **Did Polycarp know he was dead?** Polycarp counts him among those with the Lord, yet asks for surer news of him. The story does not settle it. (D7)
- **His remains.** Chrysostom, Jerome and Evagrius say bones were brought to Antioch; no held witness before Chrysostom says who carried them or when. The story tells only the feast and Chrysostom's words. (D12, N11)
- **The wheat saying.** The wording differs between witnesses; the story follows the shorter recension. (D8)
- **Not known at all:** his birth, family, age, how he became a Christian, when he became bishop, and what "Theophorus" meant to him. (N1, N3, N10)

## (d) Impact lines used and left out

Used:
- **I1 (rank 1), the letters gathered and read.** Sentences 25–27, with its carrying quote Q27. This is what he left the church.
- **I2 (rank 2), defended: the real suffering of Christ.** Sentences 17–19 and 28, with its carrying quote Q15. The later writers are told as a group; their names are on the card.
- **I3 (rank 3), the wheat saying as the martyr's model.** Sentences 15–16 and 29, with its carrying quote Q1.
- **I5 (rank 5), Antioch's memory.** Sentences 30–32, with its carrying quote Q33. It closes the story as an event in the record's own words. Only the feast and the sermon are told; the moving of the bones and the shrine under Theodosius are left to the card.
- **I6 (rank 6), defended: unity around the bishop.** Sentence 20, told as what he urged, with the census limit that not every church was led so. Its carrying quote Q18 is left out: it brings in the wonder claim about the Spirit, which would need its own telling.

Left out:
- **I4 (rank 4), Polycarp's "blessed Ignatius".** Left out for length. Polycarp already carries I1 in the story, and Q26 would raise the open question of whether Polycarp knew of the death (D7).
- **I7 (rank 7), strengthening the churches on the road.** Only touched in sentence 10 (the churches came to meet him). Eusebius's line is his own reading of the letters (M26).
- **I8 (rank 8), the line from Peter.** Contested (D5); the sheet says to tell only "bishop of Antioch".
- **I9 (rank 9), care for Antioch from prison.** Only the news of peace is told (sentence 21). What came of the messengers is not recorded (N6).
- **I10 (rank 10), antiphonal singing.** One late witness and a wonder story (D11).
- **I11 (rank 11), "Theophorus".** The letters never explain the name (D10); it adds a puzzle the story cannot answer.
- **I12–I14.** Card only.

## (e) Card notes on the telling

- Length: 473 words, about three minutes aloud, inside the range and above the two-and-a-half-minute aim. Score: grade 5.9 (names out 5.4), reading ease 82.2 with names out, longest sentence 25 words. The grade is under 8, so the reviewer's listener read decides whether it is flat.
- The tradition is said in the sheet's plain form 2 (S8a). The census name is for the card only.
- "Catholic Church", "Post-Apostolic" and "the church that came after the apostles" are not used (S8b).
- Hard parts not told: his counsel on slaves (M15) and his language about the Jewish law (M19). Neither is part of the story told here, and neither stands among the sheet's impact lines. They are his own words and belong on the card.
- The late *Martyrdom of Ignatius* (M32, Q40), Malalas (M31), Socrates's vision (M30) and Jerome's roaring lions (Q32) are not in the story.
- Eusebius is the only later writer named for the death, because he marks it as a report. Chrysostom is named because the closing words are a preacher's, about three centuries on, not an eyewitness's.
- "Asia Minor" (the census's word) and "the Roman province of Asia" (S4) both appear; the second explains the first for the cities he passed through.
