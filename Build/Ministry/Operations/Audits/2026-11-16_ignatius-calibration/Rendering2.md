**Q1. Rom 4: the wheat**
- Rendering 2: I am God's wheat. Let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, so that I may be found the pure bread of Christ.
- Note: Shorter recension rendered. The longer recension, the Syriac and Irenaeus all end "the pure bread of God", and the longer has "and I am ground" rather than "let me be ground".

**Q2. Rom 4: "I shall willingly die for God"**
- Rendering 2: I am writing to the churches, and I am telling them all that I die for God willingly, unless you stop me. I beg you not to show me a kindness at the wrong moment. Let me be food for the wild beasts. Through them I will be allowed to reach God.

**Q3. Rom 4: "They were apostles; I am but a condemned man"**
- Rendering 2: I do not give you orders, as Peter and Paul did. They were apostles; I am a condemned man. They were free; I am still a slave, even now. But when I suffer, I will be a freed slave of Jesus. I will rise again free in him. And now, as a prisoner, I am learning to want nothing of this world, nothing empty.
- Note: "Servant" in the printed English means slave; "vain" means empty or worthless. The editor's footnote takes "free" as perhaps "free from human weakness".

**Q4. Rom 5: ten leopards**
- Rendering 2: From Syria all the way to Rome I am fighting wild beasts, on land and at sea, by night and by day. I am chained to ten leopards, that is, a squad of soldiers. Even when they are treated kindly, they only get worse.
- Note: The "fight with beasts" is figurative here, as in 1 Corinthians 15:32. The ANF footnote suggests the Christians gave the guards gifts. Eusebius's version says the same in nearly the same words.

**Q5. Rom 5: "May I enjoy the wild beasts"**
- Rendering 2: May I have the joy of the wild beasts made ready for me. I pray they will be found eager to rush at me. I will even coax them to devour me quickly. I do not want them to treat me as they have treated some, whom they were too afraid to touch. And if they are unwilling to attack me, I will force them to it.

**Q6. Rom 5: "Only let me attain to Jesus Christ"**
- Rendering 2: Forgive me. I know what is good for me. Now I am beginning to be a disciple. Let nothing, seen or unseen, begrudge me reaching Jesus Christ. Let fire and the cross come, and packs of wild beasts. Let bones be torn, broken and wrenched apart, and limbs cut off. Let my whole body be crushed. Let all the cruel tortures of the devil come upon me. Only let me reach Jesus Christ.
- Note: The editor says the verb rendered "envy" ("begrudge") is uncertain, and that "tearings" and "breakings" may be spurious. "Dreadful" is literally "evil".

**Q7. Rom 2: "The bishop of Syria ... from the east unto the west"**
- Rendering 2: So do not try to do me any greater favour than this: let me be offered to God while an altar is still ready. Then, gathered together in love, you may sing praise to the Father through Christ Jesus. You will praise him because God has judged me, the bishop of Syria, worthy to be summoned from the east to the west. It is good to set, away from the world and toward God, so that I may rise again to him.
- Note: "Set" is the setting of the sun, answered by "rise". The image is the sun going down in the west and rising again. "Sent for" is literally "found and sent for".

**Q8. Rom 6: "Better ... to die"**
- Rendering 2: All the pleasures of the world and all the kingdoms of this age will do me no good. It is better for me to die for Jesus Christ than to rule over the ends of the earth.
- Note: The printed text has "this earth"; its footnote gives "this age" as the literal sense, followed here.

**Q9. Rom 7: "My love has been crucified"**
- Rendering 2: I am alive as I write to you, yet I long to die. My love has been crucified. There is no fire in me that craves to be fed. But there is living water in me, and it speaks. Inside me it says, "Come to the Father." I take no delight in food that decays, or in the pleasures of this life. I want the bread of God, the bread of heaven, the bread of life. That bread is the flesh of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who was later born of the line of David and Abraham. And I want the drink of God, his blood. His blood is love that cannot decay, and life without end.
- Note: "My love" may mean Christ himself or Ignatius's own earthly desire; the editor records both readings. "Desiring to be fed" is literally "desiring material" (fuel). The editor calls the "living water" clause doubtful in text and sense. The Syriac is shorter: "no fire in me for another love", and it lacks the living water.

**Q10. Rom 9: the church in Syria**
- Rendering 2: Remember in your prayers the church in Syria. In my place it now has God as its shepherd. Jesus Christ alone will watch over it, and so will your love. As for me, I am ashamed to be counted as one of its members. I am not worthy, since I am the very least of them, like one born at the wrong time. But I have been shown mercy, to be someone, if I reach God. My spirit greets you. So does the love of the churches that welcomed me in the name of Jesus Christ, not as a passing traveller. Even churches that did not lie on my road, my earthly road, went ahead of me city by city to meet me.
- Note: "One born out of due time" echoes Paul in 1 Corinthians 15:8. The bracketed words "will also regard it" and "to meet me" are the translator's supplements.

**Q11. Rom 10: the date**
- Rendering 2: I am writing this to you on the day before the ninth day before the Kalends of September, that is, on 23 August.
- Note: The ANF footnote calls "that is, on the twenty-third day of August" a gloss that crept into the text. If it is cut, only the Roman date stands.

**Q12. Rom 3: "Not merely be called a Christian"**
- Rendering 2: Ask only this for me: strength inside and out. Then I will not only speak, but truly will it. And I will not only be called a Christian, but actually be found to be one.

**Q13. Eph 21: "Loving Polycarp"**
- Rendering 2: May my life be given for yours, and for the people you sent to Smyrna for the honour of God. I am writing to you from there. I give thanks to the Lord, and I love Polycarp as I love you. Remember me, as Jesus Christ remembered you. Pray for the church in Syria. I am being taken from there to Rome in chains, the least of the believers there.
- Note: "My soul be for yours" stands for a Greek word meaning a life offered in exchange, as a ransom. "Last" here means least in worth, not final in time.

**Q14. Trall 10: "Then why am I in bonds?"**
- Rendering 2: Some who are without God, that is, unbelievers, say that he only seemed to suffer. It is they who only seem to exist. But if they are right, why am I in chains? Why do I long to fight the wild beasts? Am I dying for nothing, then? Am I not then telling a lie about the Lord's cross?
- Note: The editor notes that some read the last two clauses as statements, not questions: "Then I die for nothing. Then I am lying about the Lord." "To fight" follows the footnote's literal sense for "to be exposed to". "Of the cross" is a bracketed supplement.

**Q15. Smyrn 4: "Only in appearance bound"**
- Rendering 2: If our Lord did these things only in appearance, then my chains are only an appearance too. And why did I hand myself over to death, to fire, to the sword, to the wild beasts? But whoever is near the sword is near God. Whoever is among the wild beasts is in God's company. That holds only if it is in the name of Jesus Christ. I endure all of this so that I may suffer with him. He who became a complete human being gives me strength within.

**Q16. Smyrn 3: "Handle Me"**
- Rendering 2: I know that after his resurrection he was still in the flesh, and I believe he is in the flesh now. When he came to Peter and those with him, he said to them, "Take hold of me, touch me, and see that I am not a spirit without a body." At once they touched him and believed. His flesh and his spirit convinced them both.
- Note: The Greek behind "incorporeal spirit" is daimonion asomaton, "a bodiless spirit"; "ghost" is avoided. Eusebius's version lacks "and I believe he is so now" and the final clause about flesh and spirit.

**Q17. Smyrn 8: "Wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church"**
- Rendering 2: All of you, follow the bishop, as Jesus Christ follows the Father. Follow the elders as you would the apostles. Respect the deacons as God's command. Let no one do anything that concerns the church without the bishop. Count as a valid Eucharist only one led by the bishop, or by someone he has entrusted with it. Wherever the bishop appears, let the people gather there too. In the same way, wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the whole, universal Church. It is not lawful to baptize or to hold a love feast without the bishop.
- Note: "Catholic" here is the Greek katholikos, "whole" or "universal". This is its first known use for the Church; it is not the later denominational name. "Institution" may be "command" (editor's alternative). "Proper" may be "firm".

**Q18. Philad 7: "I cried, I spoke with a loud voice"**
- Rendering 2: When I was with you, I cried out. I spoke with a loud voice: "Pay attention to the bishop, the elders and the deacons." Some suspected I said this because I already knew about the split some of you had caused. But he for whose sake I am in chains is my witness: I learned nothing of it from any human being. It was the Spirit who proclaimed these words. "Do nothing without the bishop. Keep your bodies as God's temples. Love unity. Avoid divisions. Follow Jesus Christ, as he follows his Father."

**Q19. Eph 19: Mary's virginity hidden from the prince of this world (Greek held through Origen)**
- Rendering 2: The virginity of Mary escaped the notice of the ruler of this age. So did her giving birth, and so did the death of the Lord. These are three mysteries of renown, which God worked in silence.
- Rendering 2 (Origen's frame, from the Greek): It is well written in one of the letters of a certain martyr. I mean Ignatius, the second bishop of Antioch after the blessed Peter, who fought wild beasts at Rome in the persecution. He wrote: "And the virginity of Mary escaped the notice of the ruler of this age."
- Note: Origen's Greek covers only the first clause; it reads "escaped the notice of" and "this age" where the printed English has "was hidden from" and "this world". The rest follows the printed English. The Greek fragment's OCR is damaged; the bracketed corrections were followed. The source file cited for ORI is named as Origen's commentary on Matthew (Lommatzsch vol. 5), though the passage is from the Homilies on Luke.

**Q20. Eph 7: "There is one Physician" (Greek held through Athanasius)**
- Rendering 2: There is one physician, both of the flesh and of the spirit. He came into being, and he never came into being. He is God in a human being, true life in death, both from Mary and from God. He was first able to suffer, and then beyond suffering: Jesus Christ our Lord.
- Note: Rendered from Athanasius's Greek as far as "from God", where it differs from the printed ANF ("God in man", not "God existing in flesh"; "came into being and did not", for genetos kai agenetos). The last clause is not in Athanasius's Greek and follows the ANF, whose "possible ... impossible" means passible and impassible. Later manuscripts read gennetos, "begotten", which shifts the sense; Athanasius reads it as "originate and unoriginate".

**Q21. Eph 20: "The medicine of immortality"**
- Rendering 2: ...each of you, one by one and all together, by grace, in one faith and in Jesus Christ. In human descent he was of the line of David. He is both the Son of Man and the Son of God. So obey the bishop and the elders with an undivided mind. Break one and the same bread. It is the medicine that makes us immortal, the antidote against dying. Through it we live for ever in Jesus Christ.
- Note: The quote begins mid-sentence ("you come together" precedes it). "The presbytery" is the council of elders. "Which causes" is the translator's supplement.

**Q22. Philad 8: "Jesus Christ is in the place of all that is ancient"**
- Rendering 2: I heard some people say, "If I do not find it in the ancient writings, I do not believe it in the gospel." I said to them, "It is written." They answered me, "That is just the question." But for me, Jesus Christ takes the place of everything ancient. His cross, his death, his resurrection, and the faith that comes through him are the untouched ancient records. By these I hope, through your prayers, to be made right with God.
- Note: The editor calls the sense very doubtful. Some read "the archives" (the original documents) for "the ancient writings". "If I do not find it ... I do not believe in the gospel" is another possible reading of the first sentence.

**Q23. Eph 10: "To their blasphemies return your prayers"**
- Rendering 2: Meet their anger with gentleness and their boasting with humility. Answer their slanders with your prayers. Against their error, stand firm in the faith. Against their cruelty, show your gentleness. Let us take care not to copy them. Let us be found their brothers in true kindness. And let us try to follow the Lord. Who was ever wronged more than he, or robbed more, or rejected more?
- Note: "Blasphemies" may mean their abuse of the Christians or their speech against God; "slanders" keeps the first. "Brethren" rendered "brothers"; the community addressed included women.

**Q24. Pol 3: the anvil**
- Rendering 2: Stand firm, like an anvil under the hammer. A great athlete takes wounds and still wins. Above all, we must bear everything for God's sake, so that he also may bear with us.

**Q25. Pol 7: "A Christian has not power over himself"**
- Rendering 2: Polycarp, most blessed in God, it would be right to call a solemn council. Choose someone you love dearly and know to be tireless. He could be named God's courier. Give him the honour of going to Syria. There he can make your tireless love known, to the glory of Christ. A Christian is not his own master. He must always be ready to serve God.
- Note: "Messenger of God" renders the rare Greek theodromos, "God's runner" or courier.

**Q26. Polycarp, Phil 9: "The blessed Ignatius"**
- Rendering 2: So I urge you all to obey the word of righteousness and to practise all endurance. You have seen it with your own eyes, not only in the blessed Ignatius, Zosimus and Rufus, but also in others of your own people, and in Paul himself and the other apostles. Do this, being sure that none of them ran for nothing. They ran in faith and righteousness, and now they are in their rightful place beside the Lord, with whom they also suffered. For they did not love this present age. They loved him who died for us, and whom God raised from the dead for our sake.
- Note: Eusebius's version agrees in substance; it puts Rufus before Zosimus and stops at "with whom also they suffered".

**Q27. Polycarp, Phil 13: the letters sent on**
- Rendering 2: You and Ignatius both wrote to me asking that anyone going from here to Syria should take your letter along. I will see to that if I find a good chance, either myself or through someone I send in my place, so that you get what you want. As you asked, we are sending you the letters Ignatius sent to us, and all the others we have here. They are attached to this letter. You will gain a great deal from them. They speak of faith and endurance and everything that builds people up in our Lord. Please let us know anything more certain you have learned about Ignatius himself and those who were with him.
- Note: The Greek of this chapter is lost; the printed English follows the Latin version. The bracketed words are the translator's supplements.

**Q28. Irenaeus, Against Heresies V.28.4: "As a certain man of ours said"**
- Rendering 2: That is why suffering is necessary for those who are saved. They are, in a way, broken up and ground fine. They are sprinkled through with the patience of God's Word, and fired. So they are made ready for the king's banquet. As one of our own said, when he was condemned to the wild beasts for his witness to God: "I am the wheat of Christ, and I am ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, so that I may be found the pure bread of God."
- Note: The printed English follows Irenaeus's Latin. Eusebius preserves the Greek of the quotation only: "I am God's wheat, and I am ground by the teeth of wild beasts, so that I may be found pure bread." "For purification" is the translator's supplement; the image is baking.

**Q29. Eusebius, Church History III.36.3–4: his journey and death, as report**
- Rendering 2: The story is that he was sent from Syria to Rome, and became food for wild beasts because of his witness to Christ. He travelled through Asia under the strictest military guard. In each city where he stopped, he strengthened the local churches with spoken sermons and encouragement. Above all, he warned them to be on their guard against the heresies just then beginning to spread. He urged them to hold fast to the tradition of the apostles. He also thought it necessary to set that tradition down in writing, giving it a fixed form for greater safety.
- Note: "Parishes" renders paroikiai, the local Christian communities, not modern parishes.

**Q30. Eusebius, Church History III.36.2 and III.22: "second in succession to Peter"**
- Rendering 2: So did Ignatius, who was chosen bishop of Antioch, the second after Peter. His fame is still celebrated by a great many people.
- Rendering 2 (III.22): At this time Ignatius was known as the second bishop of Antioch. Evodius had been the first.

**Q31. Eusebius, Church History III.37.4–38.1: writings still extant**
- Rendering 2: We have recorded, as was right, only the names of those who have handed the apostles' teaching down to us in writings that still survive. ... Ignatius did this in the letters we have mentioned.

**Q32. Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 16: his death and grave**
- Rendering 2: He had been condemned to the wild beasts. Eager for martyrdom, he heard the lions roaring and said, "I am Christ's grain. I am ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, so that I may be found the bread of the world." He was put to death in the eleventh year of Trajan. The remains of his body lie at Antioch, in the cemetery outside the Daphne Gate.
- Note: Jerome's quotation differs from the letter itself ("bread of the world", "grain of Christ"), and the roaring lions are his own detail. Trajan's eleventh year is about AD 108.

**Q33. Chrysostom, Homily on Saint Ignatius: "Ye sent forth a Bishop, and received a martyr"**
- Rendering 2: You sent out a bishop, and you received back a martyr. You sent him off with prayers, and you received him back with crowns. And not only you, but every city along the way.
- Rendering 2 (second passage): In the same way, the cities in turn received this saint from Rome. They carried him on their shoulders all the way to this city. They escorted the crowned one with praises, and celebrated the champion in song.
- Note: The "crowns" are the victor's wreaths of martyrdom.

**Q34. Chrysostom: "to a greater degree knit the Churches together"**
- Rendering 2: He summoned him from our city to Rome, making the road twice as long. He expected the length of the journey and the number of days to break his spirit. He did not know that Jesus was with Ignatius, as his fellow traveller and fellow exile on that long road. So Ignatius grew stronger instead. He gave fuller proof of the power that was with him, and he bound the churches together more closely. The cities along the road came running from every side. They encouraged the athlete and sent him on his way with plenty of provisions. They shared in his struggle through their prayers and pleas to God.
- Note: "He" at the start is the Devil, the subject of the sentences just before; Chrysostom sees the long road as the Devil's plan.

**Q35. Socrates, Church History VI.8: the antiphonal hymns**
- Rendering 2: Ignatius, the third bishop of Antioch in Syria from the apostle Peter, had known the apostles themselves. He saw a vision of angels singing hymns to the Holy Trinity in alternating choirs. So he brought the style of singing he had seen in the vision into the church of Antioch. From there it passed down by tradition to all the other churches. That is the account we have received of these responsive hymns.
- Note: Socrates counts Peter as first, so Ignatius is "third". The editor notes that some scholars doubt the attribution to Ignatius.

**Q36. Evagrius, Church History I.16: the relics moved**
- Rendering 2: At the same time came the transfer of the remains of the divine Ignatius. John the rhetorician records it among other things. Ignatius had found a tomb, as he himself wished, in the bellies of the wild beasts in the arena at Rome. Still, the harder bones were saved and carried to Antioch. There they lay for a long time in what is called the cemetery. Then the good God moved Theodosius to honour the bearer of the name Theophorus more highly. Theodosius dedicated a temple to the victorious martyr. It had long been given over to the demons, and the local people called it the Tycheum. So the former shrine of Fortune became a sanctuary and holy precinct for Ignatius. His sacred remains were placed there, after being carried on a carriage through the city in solemn procession. From this came a public festival, celebrated with the rejoicing of the whole people. It has continued to our own time.
- Note: "Ar" in the source is an OCR error for "At". The emperor is Theodosius II. "Theophorus" means "God-bearer"; "demons" is the Christian name for the old gods.

**Q37. Athanasius, De Synodis 47: his words used in the Arian dispute**
- Rendering 2: We are persuaded that the blessed Ignatius was right to write that Christ came into being because of the flesh, for he became flesh. Yet he did not come into being, because he is not one of the things that were made and came into being. He is Son from Father.
- Note: "Originate" and "unoriginate" are rendered "came into being" and "did not come into being".

**Q38. Theodoret: "the far-famed Ignatius"**
- Rendering 2: This is the teaching handed down to us by the divine prophets. This is the teaching of the company of the holy apostles. This is the teaching of the great saints of the East and the West. It is the teaching of the renowned Ignatius, who received his office as high priest from the right hand of the great Peter. Because he confessed Christ, he was devoured by savage beasts.
- Note: "Archpriesthood" means his office as bishop.

**Q39. Gregory the Great, Letters V.39, to Anastasius of Antioch: "not only yours, but also ours"**
- Rendering 2: As you see, I include these words, taken from what you wrote, in my own letters. I want Your Blessedness to see that Saint Ignatius is not only yours but ours as well. We share his teacher, the prince of the apostles. So none of us should keep the disciple of that same prince to himself alone.

**Q40. LATE-LEGEND, Martyrdom of Ignatius 2: "He who has Christ within his breast"**
- Rendering 2: Trajan answered, "And who is Theophorus?" Ignatius replied, "The one who carries Christ in his heart."
- Note: "Theophorus" means "God-bearer". "Breast" is rendered "heart"; "in his chest" would be more literal.
