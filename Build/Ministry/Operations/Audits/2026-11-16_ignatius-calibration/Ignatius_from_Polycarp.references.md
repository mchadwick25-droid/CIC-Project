## What is uncertain

- **Whether the letters are his.** Seven letters bore his name by Eusebius's day, and letters of his were passing between churches in Polycarp's day. Whether the shorter seven are his own words is contested (D1).
- **When he died.** His letters name no emperor and no year. Eusebius places him in Trajan's reign; Jerome gives Trajan's eleventh year. "Second century" rests on modern chronology. The exact year is not known (S9, D2).
- **Where and how he died.** His letters show only that he expected to die at Rome. Polycarp says he suffered and is with the Lord. Origen, and every held witness after him, says he fought the beasts at Rome; Eusebius gives it as report. One late witness, Malalas, puts his death at Antioch (D3, D4, N4).
- **What Polycarp knew.** Polycarp counts him among those now with the Lord, yet asks for surer news of him; the last sentence of that passage survives only in Latin (D7, Q27).
- **"Bishop of Antioch."** In the shorter letters he calls himself "the bishop of Syria" and names "the Church which is at Antioch in Syria". Origen and Eusebius call him bishop of Antioch. Whether he was second or third after Peter, and who appointed him, is contested (S2, D5, D14).
- **Why he was arrested.** Not in the record (N2).
- **His opponents.** We have only his account of them (S7, N8).
- **One bishop.** His letters show what he urged and what he found in the churches of Asia, not how every church was led. His letter to Rome names no bishop there (S5, S8, N9).
- **The wheat saying.** Its wording differs between witnesses; the brief follows the shorter recension (D8).
- **His remains.** That they were brought back to Antioch is a fourth-century report; Chrysostom is a preacher, not an eyewitness (D12, Q33).
- **Spans of years.** "About two hundred" and "almost three hundred" years are estimates from the usual modern dates of Eusebius and Chrysostom (C11).
