# Superseded Claims — Antiochene Exegetical Christianity (Chrysostom-centered)

Filed here on 2026-10-02. The documents named below are at `Build/worlds/aec/`, and the review files at `Build/worlds/aec/Step0_Review_Round<n>.md` and `Build/worlds/aec/Review-Artifacts/`. Section numbers and review-round names below are those of the documents as they stood before the filing.

Companion to `Step0_Movement_Scope_Confirmation.md`, `Doc_01_World_Identification_Boundaries_Orientation.md`, `Doc_02_Source_Ecology.md`, and `Source_Registry.md`. It holds the claims those documents have withdrawn that a reader of the current text might otherwise expect to find in them, so the live documents themselves can state current claims only, per CLAUDE.md's "keep the live/canonical surfaces clean" rule (the same treatment `worlds/lpc/Doc_04_Superseded_Claims.md` established for this project).

Entries record what was claimed, what was wrong with it, and which review round established that. They are not an account of any round's own full work — see `Review-Artifacts/RoundN_Library_Stage_Review.md` for that, and each document's own Document Log for the review sequence. Nothing here is a live claim.

## 1. Step 0

**1.1 The window "closes before the Nestorian controversy."** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found the controversy itself erupts in 428–430 (Nestorius's preaching against *Theotokos*, Cyril's Twelve Anathemas), inside the 350–430 window — only Ephesus's 431 verdict falls outside it. The window simply ends where the census says it does; it was not drawn to avoid the controversy.

**1.2 "None of Theodoret's own carried material is condemned content."** Withdrawn: Round 1 HIGH found the corpus-map's own Counter-statements of Theodoret are anathematized by name at the Second Council of Constantinople (553), canon XIII. (The first canon XIII quotation itself spliced in wording from a separate passage; caught and corrected by this build thread's own re-verification, not a distinct reviewer finding.)

**1.3 Theodoret's own material confidence "uniformly `provisional`."** Withdrawn: Round 1 M5 found the corpus-map carries his *Eranistes* and Letters at `assigned`, only the *Ecclesiastical History* and Counter-statements at `provisional`.

**1.4 The *Adversus Judaeos* homilies characterized as "harshly polemical against Christians who continued to observe Jewish festivals and practices," with a contested target asserted.** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found this asserted a specific content and target the vendored corpus cannot support — the primary text is not vendored. Also withdrawn in the same finding: a claim that the corpus-map's own Ignatius-related note already used "Judaizing" in this technical sense (no such note exists).

**1.5 The *Adversus Judaeos* dating stated as settled "approximately September 386."** Withdrawn: Round 3 MEDIUM found the `npnf109` apparatus dates only one homily to that date and itself discusses a dating difficulty; Socrates VI.3 separately places a work titled "Against the Jews" earlier, during Chrysostom's readership (pre-380/381). Dating is now tagged Contested rather than settled.

**1.6 The corpus-map bucket held "46" work-rows.** Withdrawn: corrected to 47 once the Ammianus Marcellinus row (added by a commit that had landed only on the sibling `library-stage/roman-church-gregorian` branch) was hand-applied to this branch at Round 3 — see §2 below for the fuller account.

**1.7 The Statues homilies' occasion described as "delivered daily through Lent."** Withdrawn: unsupported; the corpus-map's own note does not make this claim.

**1.8 B3's central premise: World #8 (`lpc`) is "not yet built."** Withdrawn: Round 1 HIGH found `worlds/lpc/` a complete, nine-document build with Living Tradition Status confirmed. The entire original B3 contrast (built on the premise that serial scriptural exposition is "essentially absent" from Cyprian and present in Augustine "only as one genre among several") was rebuilt against `lpc`'s own real Doc_01 §7 and Doc_04.

**1.9 `lpc` Doc_04 described as "cleared after nine independent review rounds."** Withdrawn: Round 2 found it went eleven rounds and never cleared; disposed Approved to proceed on the project lead's own direct instruction, with open findings inherited by anything that draws on it.

**1.10 `lpc` Doc_04's Candidate 4 ("Preaching and Catechesis") described as "a fifth candidate," cited at "§5."** Withdrawn: Round 2 found it is Candidate 4, tested in `lpc` Doc_04 §3 and classified in §4.

**1.11 "Passes B3, on the specific ground the Step 0 Conclusion required," stated as a settled pass.** Withdrawn: flagged Round 2, not actually corrected until Round 3 (the intervening text still asserted a flat pass). Restated as a testable hypothesis, not a settled finding — see the current §3 conclusion.

**1.12 The Meletian schism engaged only as a collegial-communion complication.** Withdrawn: Round 3 MEDIUM found Socrates II.44 grounds the schism specifically in the validity of Meletius's own ordination and his adherents' baptism at Arian hands — the same ground `lpc`'s own Candidate 6 (Sacramental/Ordination Validity) tests for, not only Candidate 3 (Collegial Communion).

**1.13 Antioch stated flatly as "the third city... by standard reckoning."** Withdrawn: Round 1 LOW found this needed qualifying — by this world's own 350–430 window, Constantinople's growth is Widely Accepted to have overtaken it.

**1.14 Diodore's and Theodore's own translation availability stated as a blanket "no PD path" for both.** Withdrawn: Round 1 LOW found Theodore's own 1932 Mingana translation carries an open rights question (not due to enter US public domain until 2028) rather than a confirmed absence of any path, and is in any case his later episcopal work, not the earlier Antiochene-presbyterate writing that would anchor this world.

## 2. Doc_01

**2.1 World #8 "not yet built," inherited from Step 0's own withdrawn premise (§1.8 above).** Withdrawn throughout §1 and §6 (Distinctive Contribution, world-continuity bullets); rebuilt against `lpc`'s own real record.

**2.2 Theodoret's Counter-statements and *Eranistes* described as arguing "against Nestorianism" generally.** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found the Counter-statements specifically answer Cyril, the *Eranistes* answers Eutychian-type Christology — not interchangeable descriptions.

**2.3 The Theodoret boundary question framed as a pure strand-classification deferral to Doc_04.** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found this a category error — whether the material is inside the boundary at all is Doc_01's own job, not something a strand deferral can substitute for. Replaced with a per-work boundary finding (current §4).

**2.4 The per-work boundary finding's own first version mixed two different tests** (composition date for the *Eranistes*/Letters, narrated span for the *Ecclesiastical History*) without naming either as the rule, and its own closing paragraph contradicted §7's open-items list on whether the question was closed or deferred to Doc_04. Withdrawn: Round 2 MEDIUM named the inconsistency; the fix that followed was itself claimed but not actually propagated to §5 (Cell 3B) and §7 (items 2, 6) until Round 3 HIGH caught the gap. One consistent subject-matter criterion now applies throughout, and the strand/inclusion question is closed by §4, not deferred.

**2.5 The Letters (all 181) treated as uniformly 440s Eutychian-controversy material, entirely out-of-boundary.** Withdrawn: Round 3 HIGH found the same volume also carries a run of 430–431 Nestorian-controversy correspondence (letters to and from Nestorius, reports from Ephesus and Chalcedon), which shares the Counter-statements' own edge-case status rather than the bulk Letters' exclusion.

**2.6 "Libanius's own former fellow-students" (Theodore and Maximus), reversing the actual relationship.** Withdrawn: Round 2 found Socrates VI.3 names them as Chrysostom's own fellow-students under Libanius.

**2.7 The Meletian schism described as "already running when this world's own window opens (350)," with an unsourced "resolved 415" date.** Withdrawn: Round 2 MEDIUM found Meletius was elected 360, Paulinus consecrated 362 — a decade after the window opens — and no source supports a 415 resolution date.

**2.8 The Meletian schism characterized as "a jurisdictional and personal dispute, not a doctrinal one," unsourced.** Withdrawn: Round 3 MEDIUM found Socrates II.44 grounds the split specifically in the validity of ordination and baptism across a boundary, not personality or jurisdiction.

**2.9 The Statues homilies described via an unsupported "Lenten preaching sequence" claim,** and §6's "deliberately kept outside" framing for World #6 material left stale after other corrections. Both withdrawn: Round 2, propagating Step 0's own fixes.

**2.10 Chrysostom's own actors described as "not themselves party to the Trinitarian controversy the way World #6's earlier figures were."** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found the vendored corpus includes an anti-Anomoean homily — the controversy's later phase is directly present in this world's own Native material.

**2.11 Diodore of Tarsus described flatly as Chrysostom's "own teacher in exegesis per the standard historical record."** Withdrawn: Round 3 found this stated as settled fact against §2's own correct Widely-Accepted/not-independently-verified framing (Socrates VI.3 names Theodore and Maximus, not Chrysostom, as Diodorus's direct disciples).

**2.12 The Socrates VI.3 fellow-students/Diodorus quote cited to locus `ii.ix.iv-p6`.** Withdrawn: Round 3 found `p6` is the NPNF editor's endnote; Socrates' own body text is at `p2`.

**2.13 The Source Registry Template's Boundary Check paraphrased as one sentence joining two separate sentences with an ellipsis, altering the second's own wording.** Withdrawn: Round 3 restored the verbatim two-sentence quotation.

**2.14 Antioch stated flatly as "the third city of the later Roman Empire by standard reckoning."** Withdrawn: Round 3, matching Step 0 §1.13 above.

**2.15 `lpc` described as "nine-round-reviewed."** Withdrawn: Round 2, matching Step 0 §1.9 above — imprecise for Doc_04 specifically (eleven rounds, never cleared).

## 3. Doc_02 + Source Registry

**3.1 Chrysostom's father described as from "a decurion-class family."** Withdrawn: Round 1 HIGH — an invented detail. The vendored Schaff introduction (`npnf109`, `iii.ii-p9`) states his father Secundus was "a distinguished military officer (*magister militum*)."

**3.2 The magister militum quotation truncated,** dropping "of Syria" and closing on a period the source does not have. Withdrawn: Round 2 LOW, restored to the source's own continuing sentence.

**3.3 Frances Young (1997) cited as settled, Dominant-Modern-Reconstruction support for a literal-vs-allegorical dichotomy, without having been independently re-read.** Withdrawn: Round 1 HIGH — an independent-review caution flagged that Young's own actual thesis may argue against exactly that dichotomy. Downgraded to Confidence C/Contested, flagged for priority second-opinion review.

**3.4 The core exegetical-method claim said to rest on an "ancient self-description" of Chrysostom's own, verified via Socrates VI.3.** Withdrawn twice: Round 2 MEDIUM found the source describes Diodore of Tarsus's own method, not a Chrysostom self-description, and that part of the wording quoted ("literal and historical meaning... Origen and the Alexandrians") is the NPNF editor's own 19th-century endnote, not Socrates' own words. The fix itself then cited the wrong locus (`p6`, the endnote, instead of `p2`, Socrates' own body text) — caught at Round 3.

**3.5 A single standalone-homily spot-check (Romans 12:20) offered as representative of the serial, verse-by-verse corpus the B3 argument rests on.** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found this non-representative for a claim about serial commentary specifically. Closed with a second spot-check against an actual serial work (*Homilies on Matthew*, Homily II).

**3.6 The holdings-tool `engine.m9.cli holdings aec` described as lacking "a coverage row" for this world.** Withdrawn: Round 1 HIGH — the tool in fact crashes (`FileNotFoundError`) without an empty `records/aec/` directory; not a later-build-stage prerequisite.

**3.7 The Anomoeans/Eunomians said to "survive only through Chrysostom's own framing," with no independent own-voice source.** Withdrawn: Round 1 MEDIUM found Eunomius's *First Apology* and Philostorgius's *Ecclesiastical History* both vendored in this corpus (though not yet assigned to this world's own corpus-map bucket).

**3.8 Theodoret's material treated as uniformly `provisional`/Native, without a per-work finding.** Withdrawn: propagated from Doc_01 §2.3–2.5 above; the corpus-map's own confidence tags, and the Registry's own rows, now follow the per-work finding (Ecclesiastical History Native/context-like; Counter-statements and a subset of the Letters a live edge case; the *Eranistes* and the bulk of the Letters out-of-boundary).

**3.9 Olympias said to be "independently attested, from outside Chrysostom's own circle," by Palladius's own *Lausiac History*.** Withdrawn: Round 3 MEDIUM found the vendored text itself states Palladius was "embroiled in the disturbance connected with the blessed John" and traveled to Rome "because of the blessed bishop John" — a partisan in the same controversy, not an outside witness. What survives: corroboration by a second author within Chrysostom's own circle, not independent attestation.

**3.10 The Sabaniana clause (Palladius, Ch. XLI) described as sitting in "the same chapter's own immediate context" as the Olympias material (Ch. LVI).** Withdrawn: Round 3 — a separate chapter, fifteen chapters earlier.

**3.11 `palladius_lausiac-history_clarke1918.txt`, Codex Theodosianus (×2), and Boyd absent from the holdings-disposition table entirely, or buried in a generic "not individually assessed" catch-all.** Withdrawn: Round 2 (Palladius) and Round 3 (Codex Theodosianus, Boyd) — all now named specifically in the holdings table.

**3.12 The Ammianus Marcellinus corpus-map row, and the "47 rows" count built on it, treated as already present on this branch.** Withdrawn: Round 3 MEDIUM found the row did not exist on `library-stage/antiochene-exegetical-christianity` — it had been added, by commit `4e1ff183`, only on the sibling `library-stage/roman-church-gregorian` branch, and had not reached `main`. Hand-applied to this branch at Round 3 (the corpus-map row, its `_staging/` fragment, and the matching cross-world dossier entry), re-verified with `corpus_map_merge.py --check`. This also means Round 1's own "47, not 46" finding did not describe what was actually on this branch at the time — logged per the cic-build-cycle skill's own rule on disagreements between reviews.

**3.13 Registry row 11a (the Counter-statements) rated Confidence D.** Withdrawn: Round 3 MEDIUM found D ("tradition/genre-level attribution, no specific text/author named") misapplied to a specifically named and located work — corrected to B. The same conflation (using the Citation Reliability scale to signal corpus-map `provisional` status rather than source specificity) also understated rows 6a and 8a at C; both corrected to B.

**3.14 Registry row 11b's Comparandum Note stating "do not read their prior provisional carriage" for the *Eranistes*/Letters.** Withdrawn: Round 3 — the corpus-map carries both at `assigned`, not `provisional`; reintroduced an error Round 1 had already found and fixed elsewhere.

**3.15 Registry row 11b's boundary framed as "deliberately drawn to close (430) before the Eutychian controversy."** Withdrawn: Round 3 — the same "drawn to avoid" framing already withdrawn for the Nestorian controversy at Step 0 §1.1 above. The boundary simply closes at 430, per the census.

**3.16 The corpus-map bucket's own row count given as "46" (Doc_02, Registry header, row 1, row 13).** Withdrawn: corrected to 47 throughout — see §1.6 and §3.12 above.
