The rule for the active year

A person's active year is the year of their best-known dated act in the held lines: a council they attended, a consecration, a dated writing. For an outside witness or a recorder, it is the year of the writing that makes them a Who. Where no held line gives such a year, the year is proposed with its reasoning and marked as proposed.

The year decides which river-era Who a person belongs to. It also orders the list "By date" and picks which five majors show when a river-era has more. It places on the map only the people told within no tradition. Everyone else sits beside their tradition.

The list sorts by the active year but shows each person's dates as the card gives them, so a row can look out of order against its own dates. That is expected. Please don't re-sort it.

A person is not major when their held words are one or two letters, when a larger figure carries the same tradition, or when they are known mainly through someone else's record.
