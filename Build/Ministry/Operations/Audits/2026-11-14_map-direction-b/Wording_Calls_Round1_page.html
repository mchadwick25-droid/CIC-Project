2. Three river names

  • Greek and Latin · Eastern Catholic. The river still holds "Persecuted church worlds of the communist era", which is Baptist and Orthodox, not Eastern Catholic. Its own record calls it a Greek East and Protestant span. Recommended: show "Greek and Latin" alone until that tradition's place is decided in the census (logged in Open-Gaps). Then "Eastern Catholic" is true of the rest.
  • Syriac and Protestant · Reformed churches of India. "Reformed" with a capital names the Calvinist family. The river holds the Mar Thoma Church and the Presbyterian and Baptist hill churches of northeast India. Recommended: "Mar Thoma and churches of northeast India".
  • Capitals and "other". "Outside the Nicene Creed" and "The church of the Roman Empire" take capitals. "Coptic, Ethiopian and African churches" reads as if Coptic and Ethiopian were not African, and "Church of the East and Syriac churches" as if the Church of the East were not Syriac. Recommended: "Coptic, Ethiopian and other African churches" and "Church of the East and other Syriac churches".

Options: take all three (recommended); take some; or keep the names as approved.

3. The era notes on the era card

These ten notes used to sit on the map under each era title. Under your ruling "map now, cards later" they are now the main text of the era card. They read at grade 11.6 to 19.6, with sentences up to 43 words. Three of them disagree with the map's own data, and one gives a popular simplification. The proposals split long sentences and use plainer words. Each keeps every claim, except where a line below says it narrows or drops one.

Era 1

Now

This band starts where the map does: the Jerusalem Temple falls in 70 CE, running to Constantine's Edict of Milan in 313 — the year the persecution these movements describe finally ends. For the Persian church east of Rome, that divide ran backwards: persecution only intensified after 312.

Proposed

This era starts where the map does, with the fall of the Jerusalem Temple in 70 CE. It runs to Constantine's Edict of Milan in 313, which gave Christians, and all others, freedom to follow the religion they chose. The Persian church east of Rome had the opposite experience. Persecution there grew worse after 312.

Narrows one claim. The old note says persecution "finally ends" in 313, but the map's own story of the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste is dated 320. The new line says what the Edict did, in the words of the Constantine entry.

Era 2

Now

The era historians call the 'imperial church' — Christianity moves from persecuted minority to the empire's own religion, and the creeds that define it (Nicaea, then Chalcedon just after this band) get settled because an emperor now has a stake in the outcome.

Proposed

Historians call this the era of the 'imperial church'. Christianity moves from a persecuted minority to the empire's own religion. Its defining creeds, from Nicaea to Chalcedon, are settled because an emperor now has a stake in the outcome.

The old note puts Chalcedon "just after this band". The era runs 312 to 451, and the same card lists Chalcedon 451 among its councils.

Era 3

Now

The parting of the communions: Chalcedonian, miaphysite, and East Syriac Christianity become distinct, self-aware families here, while the Latin West enters the post-Roman kingdoms and monasticism becomes its formative engine everywhere. For scale: Rome itself falls to invasion in 476, partway through.

Proposed

In this era the churches part into separate communions. Chalcedonian, miaphysite and East Syriac Christianity become distinct families, each with a sense of its own identity. The Latin West enters the post-Roman kingdoms. Monasticism becomes the force that shapes it everywhere.

Drops one sentence: "Rome itself falls to invasion in 476". In 476 the last Western emperor was deposed; the city was not invaded that year. Bringing back a corrected sentence would be new history, so it is left out.

Era 4

Now

Christianity becomes a minority faith across its old heartlands under the rise of Islam, converts new northern peoples, fights an internal war over religious images, and sends its longest missions east along the Silk Road.

Proposed

Christianity becomes a minority faith across its old heartlands as Islam rises. It converts new peoples in the north and fights an internal war over religious images. It sends its longest missions east along the Silk Road.

Splits one long sentence. No claim changes.

Era 5

Now

The Latin West's great creative explosion — new religious orders, new lay movements, and women's religious life at a scale not seen before — while Byzantium consolidates and the Christian East endures new pressures.

Proposed

In the Latin West, new religious orders and new lay movements appear. Women's religious life grows to a scale not seen before. Byzantium consolidates, and the Christian East comes under new pressures.

Gives the fragment a verb and drops "great creative explosion". No claim changes.

Era 6

Now

Plague, papal schism, and institutional crisis, met by the most interior, vernacular, lay-accessible devotional writing Christianity had yet produced. For scale: the Black Death sweeps Europe (1347–51) during this band.

Proposed

Plague, papal schism and institutional crisis mark this era. They are met by devotional writing more inward than any before it, and more often in everyday languages for lay people to read. The Black Death sweeps Europe from 1347 to 1351.

Gives the fragment a verb and drops "For scale:". No claim changes.

Era 7

Now

The Western church remade, several ways at once: this band holds the Reformation's many, often-rival movements side by side, each with its own direct descendants still living today.

Proposed

The Western church is remade, several ways at once. This era holds the Reformation's many movements, often rivals, side by side. Most have direct descendants still living today.

Narrows one claim. The old note says "each" movement has living descendants, but Family of Love is marked not living on the map.

Era 8

Now

Established churches settle into place; renewal moves into small groups, personal religious experience, and transatlantic revival. This is the era first-person spiritual autobiography becomes a mass genre.

Proposed

Established churches settle into place. Renewal moves into small groups, personal religious experience and revivals on both sides of the Atlantic. In this era, the spiritual life story told in the first person becomes a mass genre.

Splits one long sentence and plainer words. No claim changes.

Era 9

Now

Christianity globalizes under empire's shadow and against it. The era's honest theme is entanglement: mission and colonialism, revival and respectability, liberation and paternalism, held together rather than resolved.

Proposed

Christianity spreads around the world, both under empire and against it. Mission is tangled with colonialism, revival with respectability, and liberation with paternalism. None of these pairs is resolved in this era.

Drops "the era's honest theme is entanglement" and the closing "held together rather than resolved", which read as generated. The three pairs stay.

Era 10

Now

Christianity's center of gravity moves south and east, and the Pentecostal/Spirit-movement family becomes the largest new Christian reality since the Reformation. For scale: World War I (1914–18) breaks out early in this band.

Proposed

Christianity's center of gravity moves south and east. The Pentecostal and Spirit-movement family becomes the largest new form of Christianity since the Reformation. World War I (1914–18) breaks out early in this era.

Drops "For scale:" and "Christian reality". No claim changes.

Options: take all ten (recommended); fix only the four that disagree with the map (eras 1, 2, 3 and 7); or keep the notes as they are until the full era cards.
