## Hild of Whitby: FIX

2. One new fault. The move puts "In those days a bishop led the church of a whole region, and the English church was still young." just before "Ordinary people, he writes, and sometimes even kings and princes, sought her advice." Heard once, "he" now points to "a bishop", not Bede. That gives the praise to the wrong witness. Every other changed sentence is held by the Outline (heart; beats 2 and 5; M6, M8–M15).
3. Nothing needed lost. The essence is complete at word 163, a few words past the mark, since the hook holds Caedmon. Also told are all present: goods shared, kings and princes, and the synod in her house with her side losing ("King Oswy ruled for Rome's side ... Colman, his teaching rejected"). Without the Caedmon verses paragraph the essence still lands. Hild makes him a monk and has him taught, and the hook ends "which he turned into English song". That is the essence's claim. "Many were turned to heaven" was a further effect, not part of it.
4. Scorer: 420 words, grade 7.6 (names out 7.2), reading ease names out 77.3, longest sentence 29 words. Pass.

- Old: `Ordinary people, he writes, and sometimes even kings and princes, sought her advice.`

The Outline holds it in the heart ("Bede says ... that ordinary people, and sometimes even kings and princes, sought her advice", M12). The word count does not change.
