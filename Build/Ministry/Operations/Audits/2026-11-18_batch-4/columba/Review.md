## The test, from one hearing

1. **Who:** Columba, a sixth-century Irish monk of noble birth, who founded Durrow and then, leaving Ireland for good, the monastery on Iona.
2. **What happened:** His disciples spread a family of houses led from Iona, whose priest-abbot stood over even bishops. Bede says he turned the northern Picts to the faith. Forty years after his death Iona sent Aidan to the English. He died copying the Psalms and blessing his monks. His heirs kept an older Easter, lost at Whitby in 664, and Iona gave way about 716.
3. **Why the connection matters:** The monastic Christianity the English received at Lindisfarne came from Columba's house, and the custom defeated at Whitby was his heirs' custom.

All three lines come clear from one hearing. Nowhere did it talk down or read as a children's book. One small snag: "their first teacher" (word 145) does not name Columba, though the next sentence carries the listener on.

## The heart

**Mine, written before reading `Hearts.md`:** An Irish monk who left home for good as a pilgrim for Christ and founded Iona. Iona became the mother house of a family of monasteries in Britain and Ireland, led by a priest-abbot rather than a bishop. From it the faith went to the Picts (by Bede's claim) and, through Aidan a generation later, to the English of Northumbria. His heirs' custom was later defended at Whitby and lost.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**B1. BLOCKING. The Aidan part of the heart lands about 40 words past the first minute.** The sentence leads with Oswald's request, so "Iona sent its monk Aidan" arrives only at words 185–189, and "the English" not until 199. Change: lead with Iona's act, and move Oswald's request after it. For example: "In 635, nearly forty years after his death, Iona sent its monk Aidan to the English. Their king, Oswald of Northumbria, had asked the Irish for a bishop, and Aidan set up on Lindisfarne." This puts "Aidan" at about 175 and "the English" at about 178. Then trim a few words earlier so it lands by about 170, without cutting any part of the heart or the approved opening. For instance, "This was, Bede says," can become "This was", since "In Bede's day" already credits him. Keep "Iona sent" (not "Columba sent") and "asked the Irish" (not "asked Iona").
*Do not say line for the outline:* "Do not open the 635 sentence with Oswald's request. Lead with 'Iona sent its monk Aidan to the English', so it lands by about word 170; Oswald's request can follow."

**M1. MINOR. The Picts line arrives at words 155–164, just past the mark.** The fix in B1 brings it nearer 150. No further change is needed.

**M2. MINOR. "named the disciple who would succeed him to finish the page" (words 289–300).** Adomnán has him stop "at the foot of this page" and leave "what follows" to Baithene (AD 10787–10789). The quote that follows says so too, so the listener hears two different things. Change it to, for example: "and handed what came next to Baithene, the disciple who would succeed him." The root is in the outline: beat 6 says "leaving the rest of the page". It should say "leaving what followed".
*Do not say line for the outline:* "Not 'finish the page' or 'the rest of the page'. He stopped at the foot of the page; Baithene was to write what followed (AD 10787–10789)."

**M3. MINOR. Adomnán's smaller picture of the Picts is cut to the king alone.** The outline's Do not say asks for Bede's claim to be set beside what Adomnán shows: "a household, an old chief, a king who honoured him". The story keeps the king and "never says the king was baptized", which does hold the contest. But it drops the countryman baptized "with all his household" through an interpreter (AD 7609–7613). There is room within the length. Consider one clause, such as "He tells of a countryman baptized with all his household through an interpreter."
