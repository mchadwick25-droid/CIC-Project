## My heart, written before reading the outline

An Irish monk who left Ireland for good, in his forty-second year, "to make a pilgrimage for Christ" (AD 2224–2225), and founded a monastery on the small island of Iona, where he lived as a monk of prayer, reading, copying and work, and died and was buried (Documented as Adomnán's account, about a century later; the year Contested, 562/563/565). What he made outlived him as a family of monasteries: Bede says many houses in Britain and Ireland grew through his disciples from Iona and from Durrow, with Iona holding the headship, and Iona's abbot, a priest, ruled the province and even its bishops "according to the example of their first teacher" (Documented as Bede's account of his own day, BEDE 5851–5864). Bede says he turned the northern Picts to the faith (Contested in scope: Adomnán shows a king's honour and household baptisms through an interpreter, not a nation). His monastery, a generation on, sent Aidan to the English at Lindisfarne in 635 (Documented), and his way, chiefly his Easter, was defended in his name at Whitby in 664 and lost; Iona itself gave it up in 716 (Documented as Bede's account, from the winning side). The witnesses: Adomnán, a loving successor writing a book of wonders; Bede, an outsider unsure of the man and sure of his heirs.

Major contributions: (1) Iona and its family of houses, abbot-led; (2) the Pictish mission (scope contested); (3) through his monastery, the mission to the English; (4) his custom, defended at Whitby and lost.

## Findings

**M1. MINOR. "Bede calls them 'the monasteries of Columba'" (What came 1).** The words are Boisil's, spoken in a brother's vision that Bede reports (BEDE 12779–12799). Correction, in What came 1: replace "Bede calls them 'the monasteries of Columba'." with "In a vision Bede reports, Egbert is told to go and teach 'the monasteries of Columba' (A1)." Add to Do not say: "Not 'Bede called them the monasteries of Columba'. The phrase comes in a vision Bede reports, told to Egbert. Say 'in a vision Bede tells of, they are called the monasteries of Columba', or leave the phrase out."

**M2. MINOR. Who taught the English.** The heart says Aidan "set up on Lindisfarne and taught the English the monks' way of life". Beat 3 says "Monks from the Irish houses preached". Bede gives the teaching to "many of the Scots" who came daily, "their Scottish masters", most of them monks (BEDE 5782–5794). He does not say they came from Iona's houses. Corrections: in the heart, replace "who set up on Lindisfarne and taught the English the monks' way of life" with "who set up on Lindisfarne; Irish monks who came after him taught the English the monks' rule of life". In beat 3, replace "Monks from the Irish houses preached" with "Many Irish monks came and preached".

**M3. MINOR. Baithene "finished his page" (Links).** Adomnán has Columba say "what follows let Baithene write" and says Baithene "succeeded him not only as teacher, but also as a writer" (AD 10788–10789, 10801–10803). He does not say Baithene wrote the rest of that page. Correction: "Baithene: his disciple; succeeded him as abbot; named by him to write what followed on the page."

**M4. MINOR. The heart in the first minute.** The opening plus beats 1–3 run nearer 200 words than 150, so Aidan lands after the first minute. Beat 1 carries two disputes that are not the heart: the 562/563/565 year, and who gave the island. Correction: in beat 1, say only "about 563" and "He founded a monastery on Iona". Move "the annals say 562 and Bede 565 (D1, Contested)" and "Bede says the Picts gave him the island, the Irish annals a king named Conall (D4, Contested)" to the end of beat 4. Their Do not say entries stand.

**M5. MINOR. Whitby is in the heart but told at the close.** The heart's last sentence (Whitby, 716) is not in Mark's approved heart, and the beats place it in What came 3. Correction: end that sentence with "(told at the close, What came 3; not needed in the first minute)".

**M6. MINOR. Two Leave out reasons fall short of the kit's "never the record's force or conflict".**
- The annal of the battle of Cul-dreimne, where "3,000 fell" and "Through the prayers of Colum-Cille they conquered" (AU 4533–4540), is left out as "a side story and doubtful". That is the record's force, tied to his name. Correction: add one clause to beat 1, after "two years after a great battle in Ireland": "The Irish annals, kept and copied long after, say that battle was won 'through the prayers of Colum-Cille' (AU 4539–4540; Documented as the annal's statement; Adomnán does not say it)." Strike that sentence from Leave out. Add to Do not say: "Not 'he fought in', 'caused' or 'did penance for' the battle. The annals credit the victory to his prayers. Adomnán names the battle only to date his going."
- The Teltown excommunication (E3) may stay out. Replace its reason with: "No held source gives its cause, so it cannot be told without inventing one."

**M7. MINOR. Two Do not say gaps.** Add:
- "Not 'Bede doubted he was holy' or 'Bede thought little of him'. Bede says 'whatsoever he was himself' and goes straight on to the Easter dispute (BEDE 5866–5872). Elsewhere he calls Iona's house 'much honoured' (BEDE 12801–12803)."
- "Not 'Wilfrid called him a holy man'. Wilfrid granted it only on a condition: 'if he was Christ's servant' (BEDE 8186–8189)."
