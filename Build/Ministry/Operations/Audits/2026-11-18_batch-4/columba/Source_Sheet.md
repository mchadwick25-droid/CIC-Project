# Source sheet: Columba (Colum Cille)

Researcher's sheet for the 2026-11-17 varied batch, era 3. The writer uses the outline, the shapes and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

**Files and short names used below** (all in `cic/texts/` unless marked):

Huyshe's own Introduction (lines ~249–2057) and his NOTES after each chapter are a 1905 editor's words, marked **AD-ed** below, and are not Adomnán.

- **AC** = `annales-cambriae-rolls-series-20-lat_williams-ab-ithel1860.txt`: *Annales Cambriae*, Latin, ed. Williams ab Ithel (1860). The editor prints an AD year beside some entries only.
- **LIS** = `stokes_lives-of-saints-from-the-book-of-lismore-iri-eng_1890.txt`: the Middle Irish Life of Colomb Cille in the Book of Lismore, English of Whitley Stokes (1890). Used only to show where late legend departs from the earlier record.
- **CEN** = `cic-website/data/world-census.json` (not in `cic/texts/`; used only for the tradition's spoken name and the meaning of *peregrinatio*).

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own lines. Quotes are exact; where a word breaks across lines ("parent-/age") I join it, and I leave out page running heads ("LIFE OF SAINT COLUMBA 7"), page numbers, signature letters and footnote markers. Square brackets inside AD quotes are Huyshe's. OCR faults are left as printed, with my corrections in [brackets] where I give them. In the Latin of AC I leave out the editor's marks ^ and ' that flag variant readings.

**Greek / Latin.** No Latin text of Adomnán is held, so every Adomnán rendering is made from Huyshe's printed English (1905) and says so. Bede's Latin is held (BEDL), so Bede renderings are made from the Latin and checked against Giles.

**Witness kinds and gaps.** Columba left no writing that the Library holds. There is **no OWN and no CONTEMP witness** in this sheet.
- LATER (~100 years) = **AD**. Adomnán was the ninth abbot of Iona and wrote about 697–700 (AD file header, line 16; AD-ed 1999–2001 "between the two visits" of 692 and 697). He names his sources: earlier writings and old men's memory (AD 2199–2210), including Cuimine the Fair's book (AD 9474–9477) and a very old monk who spoke to him "at that time a youth" (AD 10930–10933).
- LATER (~130 years) = **BEDE / BEDL**. An English monk who finished in 731 (BEDE 15408, 15432–15436). An outsider to Iona, and hostile to its Easter custom.
- LATER, gap uncertain = **AU**, **AC**. Annals copied and compiled over centuries; the date an entry was written is not the date it reports (corpus-map note, `cic/corpus-map/insular-irish-monastic-christianity.yaml` lines 17–28). AC is a 10th-century compilation (AC header, line 2).
- LATE-LEGEND = **LIS**. Book of Lismore, compiled in "the latter half of the fifteenth century" (LIS 219); the file header calls the Lives "12th-15th c." (LIS header, line 18). Not used as evidence for any claim.

## 1. Setting

S1. **His name.** Adomnán: "having the same name as Jonas, the prophet ... what in Hebrew is pronounced Iona, in Greek is uttered Peristera and in the Latin tongue is called Columba" (AD 2134–2139; the dove, "columba (dove)", AD 2149). Bede: "he is now by some called Columbkill, the name being compounded from Columb and Cell" (BEDE 12804–12805). LATER. Documented as their statements. The Irish form in the annals is "Colum-Cille" (AU 4540, 5175, 5981). Meaning of "Cell" (a church) is Bede's etymology only; not glossed further in any held text.

S2. **Place, Ireland.** "Saint Columba, then, was born of noble parentage ; his father was Fedilmith, son of Fergus, and his mother was Aethne by name" (AD 2212–2214). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's statement. His birthplace in Donegal (Gartan) is AD-ed only (AD 9300–9302 note) and LIS; not used.

S3. **Place, Iona.** Bede: "That island belongs to Britain, being divided from it by a small arm of the sea" (BEDE 5798–5800); "it is not very large, but contains about five families, according to the English computation" (BEDE 5842–5844). Adomnán has him call it "this place, small though it be, and mean" (AD 10773–10774) and "this small and remote isle of the British Ocean" (AD 11067–11068, Adomnán's own voice). LATER. Documented. Plain modern placing: a small island off northern Britain, in what is now Scotland. Support for "what is now Scotland": AD-ed only, "Pictland in Columba's time comprised almost all of what we now call Scotland" (AD 2505–2506); Widely Accepted.

S4. **Century.** AC: "521. Lxxvii. Sanctus Columcille nascitur" (AC 2399; editor's AD figure). AU dates the voyage under 562 (AU 4810–4811) and the death under 594 (AU 5980–5981, 6114). Bede: "In the year of our Lord 565 ... there came into Britain ... Columba" (BEDE 5811–5815). All place him in the **sixth century**. Documented (as the annalists' and Bede's placing). Exact years are disputed (D1, D2).

S5. **Tradition, spoken name.** Census name: "Insular / Irish Monastic Christianity", informal "The Irish Monastic World" (CEN 39745, 39747). Its own description: "Ireland was never part of the Roman Empire, and its church grew up organized around monasteries and abbots rather than city bishops" (CEN 39791). Plain forms a narrator may say: **"the Irish monastic church"**, or **"the church of Ireland's monasteries, which was led by abbots rather than city bishops"**. The second is directly borne out for Iona by Bede (I7). Do not say "Insular" aloud. Do not call it "the Celtic Church" (no held source uses the phrase).

S6. **Pilgrimage for Christ.** Adomnán: "wishing to make a pilgrimage for Christ from Ireland to Britain, he sailed forth" (AD 2224–2225). The census glosses the custom: "peregrinatio, leaving home for good for God's sake. It sent Columba to Iona in 563" (CEN 39826). The census year 563 follows Huyshe (D1). Documented (Adomnán's phrase); the gloss is the census's, Widely Accepted.

S7. **His church's shape.** Bede: "That island has for its ruler an abbat, who is a priest, to whose direction all the province, and even the bishops, contrary to the usual method, are subject, according to the example of their first teacher, who was not a bishop, but a priest and monk" (BEDE 5856–5864, page head removed). Latin: "Habere autem solet ipsa insula rectorem semper abbatem presbyterum, cuius iuri et omnis prouincia, et ipsi etiam episcopi ordine inusitato debeant esse subiectij [subiecti]" (BEDL 18876–18879). LATER (~130 years). Documented as Bede's account of Iona in his own day.

## 2. Life events (in order)

E1. **Training under Finnian.** "the holy man, when yet young, went to the venerable bishop Finnian, his former master, by this time an old man" (AD 9397–9399). Earlier, as "a young deacon residing in Ireland with Finbarr, a holy bishop" (AD 2337–2339; a wine wonder follows, not used). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's statement. Which Finnian is AD-ed guesswork ("Probably St. Finnian of Magh-bile (Moville)", AD 9413–9414); card only.

E2. **Founding in Ireland: Durrow.** Bede: "Before he passed over into Britain, he had built a noble monastery in Ireland, which, from the great number of oaks, is in the Scottish tongue called Dearm-ach — The Field of Oaks" (BEDE 5847–5851). ("Scottish" here means Irish: compare Huyshe's bracket "the Scotic people [i.e. the Irish of Ireland and Britain]", AD 10775–10776.) AU 588: "Aedh, son of Brendan, King of Tethba, died. (It was he that presented Dermhagh to Colum-Cille.)" (AU 5822–5824). LATER. Documented. Note: Bede places the founding *before* the voyage; AU records only who gave the land.

E3. **Excommunicated by a synod, and defended.** "when St. Columba was excommunicated by a certain synod for some venial and quite excusable causes — not rightly, as afterwards in the end became clear — he came to the same Assembly which was convened against himself" (AD 9318–9323). Brendan of Birr rose and kissed him, and told the elders he had seen "a comet-like and exceedingly bright pillar going before this same man of God, whom ye despise, and holy Angels, also, accompanying him in his walk on the plain" (AD 9343, 9348–9350; he rose and kissed him, AD 9323–9327; full passage AD 9318–9357). "This thing was done in Teilte [Teltown, Meath]" (AD 9356–9357). LATER. Documented that Adomnán records an excommunication; the causes are not given by any held source: Inferential-Thin. WONDER (Brendan's vision), in Brendan's voice as Adomnán tells it. AD-ed: "There is no means ... of ascertaining with certainty the date of this synod, or the acts of Columba which it condemned. It was possibly in 561, after the battle of Cul Dreimhne" (AD 9363–9366); this guess is Contested and goes on the card only.

E4. **The voyage.** "In the second year after the battle of Culedrebina (Cooldrevny) and in the forty-second of his age, wishing to make a pilgrimage for Christ from Ireland to Britain, he sailed forth" (AD 2222–2225). "It was in those days that the Saint sailed over to Britain with twelve comrades, disciples of his" (AD 9409–9410). AU, under 562: "Voyage of Coluin-Cille to the Island of Ia\ in the 42nd year of his age" (AU 4809–4810; "Coluin" is OCR for Colum, "Ia\" as printed). AC: "562. cxviii. Annus. Columcille in Brittania exiit" (AC 2417). LATER. Documented that he sailed in his forty-second year, two years after the battle; the AD year is Contested (D1). **His reason**, in the only held words: "wishing to make a pilgrimage for Christ" (AD 2224). No held text before LIS gives any other motive (see Leave out, L1).

E5. **Iona received.** Bede: the island "had been long since given by the Picts ... to the Scottish monks, because they had received the faith of Christ through their preaching" (BEDE 5800–5803); "he also received of them the aforesaid island for a monastery" (BEDE 5841–5842). AU 573 (heading year): "The death of Conall, son of Comgall, in the 16th year of his reign, who granted the island of Ia to Colum-Cille" (AU 5173–5175). Contested (D4): who gave the island. That he founded a monastery there and was buried there: Documented (BEDE 5844–5846; AD 10984–10988).

E6. **How he lived.** "He could not pass the space even of a single hour without applying himself either to prayer, or reading, or writing, or also to some manual labour. By day and by night he was so occupied, without any intermission, in unwearied exercises of fasts and vigils that the burden of any one of these particular labours might seem to be beyond human endurance. And, amid all, dear to all, ever showing a pleasant, holy countenance, he was gladdened in his inmost heart by the joy of the Holy Spirit" (AD 2234–2243). He slept on "the bare rock for pallet and a stone for pillow" (AD 10810–10811). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's portrait; it is praise written by his successor (Widely Accepted as Adomnán's picture, not as a neutral report).

E7. **Copying and checking the Psalms.** Baithene asks for a brother "to look over with me and to revise the Psalter which I have written" (AD 3807–3809); Columba says only the vowel "I" is missing (AD 3810–3813; a WONDER of foreknowledge, Adomnán's voice). His last act was "transcribing the Psalter" (AD 10784; see E12). LATER. Documented that Adomnán shows Iona's monks copying the Psalms. The famous books later linked to him (the Cathach, Durrow, "three hundred" books) are AD-ed only, from later tradition (AD 1643–1652): not used.

E8. **Among the Picts: King Brude.** Bede: "Columba came into Britain in the ninth year of the reign of Bridius, who was the son of Meilochon, and the powerful king of the Pictish nation, and he converted that nation to the faith of Christ, by his preaching and example" (BEDE 5837–5841). Adomnán: at "his first journey to King Brude ... that king, elated by royal pride in his fortress, acting haughtily, did not open the gates at the blessed man's first arrival" (AD 7874–7878); Columba signed the cross on the doors and "the doors open in all haste of their own accord" (AD 7885–7888, WONDER, Adomnán's voice); "And from that day forth this ruler honoured the holy and venerable man with very great honour all the remaining days of his life ; as was proper" (AD 7894–7897). Druids tried to drown out the monks' evening psalms "outside the King's fortress" (AD 4650–4659; his voice "like terrible thunder", WONDER, AD 4661–4664). LATER. That he went to Brude's fortress and won the king's lasting respect: Documented as Adomnán's account. **Adomnán never says Brude was baptized.** That the whole northern Pictish nation was converted: Bede's claim, Contested in scope (D6).

E9. **Preaching through an interpreter.** "a certain countryman, with all his household, hearing the word of life through an interpreter when the holy man preached, believed, and, believing, was baptized" (AD 7609–7613). An old chief brought by boat, "having received the Word of God from the Saint through an interpreter, forthwith believing, was baptised by him" (AD 4295–4297). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's account. AD-ed: "St. Columba, therefore, did not know the Pictish language" (AD 4323–4324), a fair inference: Inferential-Thin as to how long this lasted.

E10. **Ordaining a king.** On Iona Columba "ordained as king Aedhan, who arrived there at that same time. And during the words of ordination he prophesied ... and laying his hand upon his head, he ordained him and blessed him" (AD 9477–9482; passage AD 9447–9482). Adomnán frames it with an angel bearing "a book of glass of the Ordination of Kings" who scourged him for preferring Aedhan's brother Iogenan (AD 9449–9459): WONDER, Adomnán's voice. Adomnán quotes Cuimine the Fair's book for the prophecy (AD 9484–9487). LATER, drawing on a source of ~657–669 (AD-ed 9548–9552: Cuimine "seventh Abbot of Iona, 657-669"). Documented as Adomnán's account. Side story; leave out of a 2½-minute brief except as a single clause, if at all.

E11. **The Convention of Drumceatt.** AU 574: "The great Convention of Druim-Ceta, at which were Colum-Cille, and Aedh son of Ainmire" (AU 5187–5188). Adomnán mentions "the Conference of the Kings at Drum Ceatt" (AD 6223–6224). LATER. Documented that he attended. Its business is AD-ed only (AD 5587–5606). Side story; leave out.

E12. **His last day** (Adomnán III.23, AD 10617–11000). In May, "the old man, weary with age, is borne on a wagon and goes to visit the Brethren while at their work" (AD 10623–10627). On the Saturday he and "his dutiful attendant, Diormit, go to bless the granary" (AD 10689–10691) and says, "Greatly do I congratulate the monks of my household that this year, also, if I should perchance have to depart from you, you will have enough for the year without stint" (AD 10694–10697). He tells Diormit in secret, "In the Sacred Volumes this day is called the Sabbath, which is, interpreted, Rest. And this day is truly a Sabbath day for me, because it is for me the last day of this present laborious life, on which I rest after the fatigues of my labours" (AD 10710–10721). The milk-carrying white horse "lays his head against his breast" and weeps; "Let him alone, let him alone, for he loves me" (AD 10739–10758; Adomnán: "inspired, as I believe, by God", AD 10743–10744 — WONDER in Adomnán's voice). From a knoll he blesses the monastery: "Upon this place, small though it be, and mean, not only the kings of the Scotic people [i.e. the Irish of Ireland and Britain], with their peoples, but also the rulers of barbarous and foreign races, with the people subject to them, shall confer great and no common honour" (AD 10773–10779; a prophecy, Adomnán's voice). Then "he sat in his hut transcribing the Psalter ; and coming to that verse of the thirty-third Psalm, where it is written: "But they that seek the Lord shall not want any good thing" [Ps. xxxiii. 11 in Vulgate]. "Here", he says, " I must stop at the foot of this page, and what follows let Baithene write."" (AD 10783–10789). Adomnán's comment: Baithene "succeeded him not only as teacher, but also as a writer" (AD 10801–10803). LATER (~100 years). Documented as Adomnán's account; Adomnán says parts came "from some who were there present" (AD 10859–10860).

E13. **Last words.** "These my last words I commend to you, O my sons, that ye have mutual and unfeigned charity among yourselves, with peace : and if, according to the example of the holy Fathers, ye shall observe this, God, the Comforter of the good, will help you ; and I, abiding with Him, will intercede for you" (AD 10815–10821). Heard by "his attendant alone" (AD 10814). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's account. "Charity" here means love (modern sense misleads; see Q2).

E14. **His death.** At the midnight bell "he goes to the church, and running faster than the others he enters it alone, and on bended knees falls down in prayer at the altar" (AD 10840–10843). Diormit "finds the Saint lying before the altar, and raising him up a little and sitting down by him he lays the holy head on his bosom" (AD 10854–10857). Diormit lifts his right hand; "the venerable Father himself at the same time moved his hand as much as he was able, so that what was impossible to him to do with his voice at his soul's departure he might still do by the movement of his hand, namely, give his blessing to the Brethren. And after thus signifying his holy benediction, immediately breathed forth his spirit" (AD 10871–10877). Diormit and others saw the church "filled within with Angelic light" (AD 10845–10849: WONDER, Diormit's voice as Adomnán reports it). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's account. The day: "on the night of the next Lord's Day" (AD 10685–10686), i.e. Saturday into Sunday. AU: "The repose of Colum-Cille, on the 5th of the Ides of June, in the 76th year of his age" (AU 5980–5981, 6123) — the 5th of the Ides of June is 9 June. Year Contested (D2).

E15. **Burial.** "for three days and as many nights his honourable obsequies are performed ... the venerable body ... wrapped in a fair shroud and placed in the tomb prepared for it, is buried with due reverence" (AD 10977–10987). Only his own monks were present; a storm kept others off (AD 11000–11014: Adomnán presents it as a fulfilled prophecy). Bede: "he was also buried therein" (BEDE 5845). LATER. Documented.

## 3. What came of him (ranked heaviest first: what later witnesses carried on and used)

I1. **Iona as the head of a family of monasteries, led by an abbot.** Bede: "From both which monasteries, many others had their beginning through his disciples, both in Britain and Ireland ; but the monastery in the island where his body lies, is the principal of them all" (BEDE 5851–5854). Iona's monastery "was for a long time the chief of almost all those of the northern Scots, and all those of the Picts, and had the direction of their people" (BEDE 5795–5798). The abbot-priest over bishops (S7). "His successors hold the island to this day" (BEDE 5844–5845). Successor named: AU 597 heading: "The repose of Baitheni, abbot of Ia" (AU 6138–6139; "la" in the OCR); Adomnán: Baithene "succeeded him" (AD 10799–10803). Later abbots are styled his heirs: "Dubhtach, comarb of Colum-Cille and Adamnan, rested in peace" (AU 937 heading, 35060–35061). LATER. Documented. Heaviest: every later witness uses it.

I2. **The mission to the English, through Aidan.** King Oswald "sent to the elders of the Scots" (BEDE 5735), "desiring they would send him a bishop" (BEDE 5741–5742), and they "sent him Bishop Aidan" (BEDE 5746). "Bishop Aidan was himself a monk of the island called Hii" (BEDE 5794–5795). Year: Bede's chapter heading "[a.d. 635.]" (BEDE 5729). Bede later dates Iona's change of Easter "about eighty years after they had sent Aidan to preach to the English nation" (BEDE 15028–15031). LATER. Documented. This is Iona's work a generation after Columba, not his own act; tell it as what his monastery did.

I3. **His way, defended at Whitby (664).** DEFENDED. Colman, bishop of the Northumbrians from Iona's line, at the synod at Streaneshalch (Whitby): "Is it to be believed that our most reverend Father Columba and his successors, men beloved by God, who kept Easter after the same manner, thought or acted contrary to the Divine writings ? Whereas there were many among them, whose sanctity is testified by heavenly signs and the working of miracles, whose life, customs, and discipline I never cease to follow" (BEDE 8121–8128). Latin: "Numquid reuerentissimum i^atrem [patrem] nostrum Colunil>aMi [Columbam] et successores eius uiros Deo dilectos, qui eodcni in..do [eodem modo] pascha fecerunt, diuinis paginis contraria ' sapuis.su [sapuisse] uel egisse credendum est ?" (BEDL 21572–21575, as printed by the OCR, my corrections in brackets). Wilfrid replied: "if that Columba of yours (and, I may say, ours also, if he was Christ's servant), was a holy man and powerful in miracles, yet could he be preferred before the most blessed prince of the apostles" (BEDE 8186–8189). King Oswy ruled for Peter, "lest, when I come to the gates of the kingdom of heaven, there should be none to open them" (BEDE 8205–8209). "This disputation happened in the year of our Lord's incarnation 664" (BEDE 8236–8237). Colman "took with him such as would not comply with the Catholic Easter and the tonsure ... and went back into Scotland" (BEDE 8227–8232). LATER. Documented as Bede's account (Bede writes for the winning side). Of Aidan, Bede says "he was wont to keep Easter Sunday according to the custom of his country" (BEDE 5749–5750). Plain gloss: Iona reckoned the date of Easter by an older Irish table, so its Easter could fall on a different Sunday from Rome's (BEDE 5751–5752: "from the fourteenth to the twentieth moon"; Bede's own correction that they still kept it "on Sunday, although not in the proper week", BEDE 5888–5889).

I4. **Iona kept his custom until 716.** "This manner of keeping Easter continued among them for the space of 150 years, till the year of our Lord's incarnation 715" (BEDE 5876–5878, "1 50" OCR). Then "those monks also of the Scottish nation, who lived in isle of Hii, with the other monasteries that were subject to them, were ... brought to the canonical observation of Easter, and the right mode of tonsure" (BEDE 14985–14989; chapter heading "[a.d. 716.]", BEDE 14983). LATER. Documented. Note: Bede gives both 715 and 716.

I5. **His memory and his name carried far.** Adomnán, ninth abbot, wrote the *Life* "Wishing to fall in with the urgent demands of the Brethren" (AD 2080–2081). Adomnán claims: "although he lived in this small and remote isle of the British Ocean, his name has deserved to be honourably made known, not only throughout the whole of our Ireland and Britain ... but to reach even as far as triangular Spain, and the Gauls, and Italy, that lies beyond the Pennine Alps, even to the City of Rome itself, wh ch is the head of all cities" (AD 11067–11074, "wh ch" as printed). Lugaid's vision names him "pillar of many churches" (AD 10897). LATER. Documented as Adomnán's claim; the reach to Rome is his assertion, not shown by any other held text: Inferential-Thin.

I6. **Bede's verdict: unsure of the man, sure of his heirs.** "But whatsoever he was himself, this we know for certain, that he left successors renowned for their continency, their love of God, and observance of monastic rules" (BEDE 5866–5869). Latin: "Uerum qualiscumque fuerit ipse, nos hoc de illo certum tenemus, quia reliquit successores magna continentia ac diuino amore regularique institutione insignes" (BEDL 18882–18885; "continentia ■*" OCR marker removed). Bede notes "of whose life and discourses some writings are said to be preserved by his disciples" (BEDE 5864–5866). LATER. Documented. Bede also calls him "the first teacher of Christianity to the Picts beyond the mountains northward" (BEDE 12799–12801).

I7. **The Picts.** Bede's claim of the nation's conversion (E8) and of the island given "because they had received the faith of Christ through their preaching" (BEDE 5801–5803). Contested in scope (D6).

## 4. Links to other people

- **Baithene** (Baithen): disciple; succeeded him as abbot; finished his copying. AD 10789, 10799–10803; AU 6111–6112. *Kind:* his disciple, succeeded him, finished his book.
- **Diormit**: his attendant on the last day; heard his last words. AD 10689–10690, 10814, 10844–10857. *Kind:* served him.
- **Bridius / Brude, son of Meilochon**, king of the Picts. BEDE 5837–5839; AD 7874–7897. *Kind:* met; the king honoured him.
- **Aedhan (Áedán), son of Gabran**, king. AD 9447–9481. *Kind:* ordained him king (Adomnán, via Cuimine).
- **Finnian**, bishop: "his former master". AD 9397–9399. *Kind:* his teacher.
- **Brendan of Birr**: defended him at the Teltown synod. AD 9323–9357; AD-ed 9372–9375. *Kind:* defended him.
- **Cuimine the Fair** (Cummeneus Albus), later abbot of Iona: wrote a book on his virtues. AD 9484–9487; AD-ed 9547–9552. *Kind:* wrote of him.
- **Adomnán**, ninth abbot of Iona: wrote his *Life*. AD header 16; AD 10930–10933. *Kind:* wrote his life.
- **Aidan**, monk of Iona, bishop in Northumbria. BEDE 5794–5795, 15028–15031. *Kind:* monk of his monastery; carried his community's faith to the English.
- **Colman**, bishop at Whitby: defended "our most reverend Father Columba". BEDE 8121–8128. *Kind:* defended his custom; quoted him as father.
- **Wilfrid**: argued against his custom at Whitby. BEDE 8151–8189. *Kind:* argued against his heirs.
- **Bede**: wrote of him. BEDE 5811–5878, 12799–12805. *Kind:* wrote of him.
- **Egbert**, English monk: brought Iona to the Roman Easter. BEDE 5880–5886, 14983–14990, 15028–15029. *Kind:* changed his community's custom.
- **Patrick**: AU 552 says, from the Book of Cuanu, that the relics of Patrick were enshrined "by Colum-cille" (AU 4250–4252); Adomnán's second preface has Mochta, "a disciple of the holy bishop Patrick", foretell Columba's birth (AD 2169–2176). LATER / LATE-LEGEND in kind. Card only.
- **Columbanus**: not linked to Columba in any held text read here; the census lists them separately (CEN 39831–39832). Do not join them.

## 5. Where the sources disagree, or are much later than the life

D1. **Year he sailed.** AU 562 (AU 4809–4810); AC 562 (AC 2417); Bede 565 (BEDE 5811, and the summary "In the year 565, the priest, Columba, came out of Scotland, into Britain", BEDE 15267–15269). Bede and Huyshe both anchor on "the ninth year of the reign of Bridius" (BEDE 5837–5838); Huyshe reckons that 563 (AD-ed 2511–2513, "Brude reigned 554 to 584"). Adomnán gives no AD year, only "In the second year after the battle of Culedrebina" (AD 2222–2223). AU enters Cul-dreimne under both 559 and 560 (AU 4531, 4533). Contested. Plain: "about 563, two years after a great battle in Ireland, as Adomnán dates it". Note: Bede's Latin says he came "de Hibernia", from Ireland (BEDL 18810). Giles's "out of Scotland" (BEDE 15268) follows Bede's usage, where the "Scots" are the Irish (Huyshe's gloss "the Scotic people [i.e. the Irish of Ireland and Britain]", AD 10775–10776). Never say "from Scotland" aloud.

D2. **Year he died.** AU under 594, on 9 June, in his 76th year (AU 5980–5981, 6123), and again as an alternative under 600: "Or, in this year, the repose of Colum-cille, on Sunday night" (AU 6294–6295). AC: "CLI. Annus. Columcille moritur" (AC 2516); counting from the editor's 521 = year 77 and 562 = year 118, year 151 = 595 (my count; Inferential-Thin). Bede: died "at the age of seventy-seven, about thirty-two years after he came into Britain" (BEDE 5845–5847), which from 565 gives 597. Adomnán: "one score and ten years of my sojourn in Britain are completed" plus "four years from this day onward" (AD 10570–10571, 10591–10592), and "thirty-four years" as an island-soldier (AD 2232–2233). Huyshe: 597, with Easter on 14 April that year (AD-ed 11104–11105). 597 is the modern standard date (Widely Accepted; held support is Huyshe's 1905 reckoning only). Plain: "in 597, by the usual reckoning".

D3. **His age at death.** AU 76th year (AU 5981); Bede 77 (BEDE 5846); LIS 77 (LIS 17793–17794). Minor; say "in his late seventies" or avoid.

D4. **Who gave him Iona.** Bede: the Picts (BEDE 5800–5803, 5841–5842). AU: Conall son of Comgall (AU 5173–5175); AU does not here say whose king Conall was. Contested. Plain: "Bede says the Picts gave him the island; the Irish annals say a king named Conall did."

D5. **Birth year.** Only AC gives one (521, editor's figure, AC 2399). Adomnán gives none. Inferential-Thin beyond "early in the sixth century".

D6. **How far he converted the Picts.** Bede: "he converted that nation to the faith of Christ, by his preaching and example" (BEDE 5839–5841). Adomnán shows households and single men baptized through an interpreter (AD 7609–7613, 4295–4297) and a king who "honoured" him (AD 7894–7897), but no baptism of the king or the nation. Contested in scope. Say: "Bede, writing later in England, says he turned the northern Picts to the faith. Adomnán tells of a countryman baptized with all his household, and an old chief, through an interpreter."

D7. **Companions and length of stay.** Adomnán: "twelve comrades, disciples of his" (AD 9410); 34 years (D2). LIS: "Forty-five years was he in Scotland, seventy-seven years was his full age, and the number that went with him was twenty bishops, forty priests, thirty deacons, fifty students" (LIS 17793–17795). LATE-LEGEND. LIS is not used.

D8. **Adomnán's own stance.** He writes as Columba's successor at the brothers' request (AD 2080–2081) and arranges his book by kinds of wonder: "the first will contain Prophetic Revelations ; the second Divine Miracles performed by him ; the third will contain Angelic Apparitions" (AD 2194–2198). He insists "Let no one, therefore, consider me as either lying about this so renowned a man" and that he writes from "the consistent record of our elders" and from "what we have been able to find was committed to writing before our time" (AD 2199–2210). A loving insider, writing about a century later. Bede is an outsider who doubts the man's Easter custom and says "whatsoever he was himself" (BEDE 5866).

## 6. Wanted but not held

- The Latin of Adomnán's *Life* (Reeves 1874 or any other edition). The AD file header says Reeves's annotated edition "was verified and not vendored" (AD header, lines 18–20).
- Any writing by Columba himself. The hymns ascribed to him (*Altus Prosator* and others) are mentioned only in AD-ed (AD 1672–1690); no text of them is held here. Not used.
- The *Amra Coluim Chille*, the early Irish elegy, is quoted only in a stanza in Huyshe's notes (AD 7677–7680). Not held as a text; not used.
- Cuimine's book on Columba's virtues survives only as Adomnán quotes it.

## Added for batch 4

Added 2026-11-18 so the heart of his life (what Iona became, and what it did) can be told in the first minute with its own supporting lines. Each line was re-read in its file.

A1. **"The monasteries of Columba", Bede's name for the family.** BEDE 12798–12803: "it is the will of God that he should rather go to instruct the monasteries of Columba.' " Now Columba was the first teacher of Christianity to the Picts beyond the mountains northward, and the founder of the monastery in the island Hii, which was for a long time much honoured by many tribes of the Scots and Picts". The first part is a vision told to Egbert, as Bede reports it; the second ("Now Columba was ...") is Bede's own voice. LATER (~130 years). Documented as Bede's account. "Scots" here means the Irish (see E2, D1).

A2. **The family of houses, in Bede's Latin.** BEDL 18870–18874: "Ex quo utroque monasterio plurima exinde monasteria per discipulos eius et in Brittania et in Hibernia propagata sunt, in quibus omnibus idem monasterium insulanum, in quo ipse requiescit corpore, principatum teneret." Giles's English is at BEDE 5851–5854 (I1). "Principatum teneret" means it held the first place, the headship, among them all. LATER (~130 years). Documented as Bede's account.

A3. **Iona's place among the northern houses.** BEDE 5794–5798: "Bishop Aidan was himself a monk of the island called Hii, whose monastery was for a long time the chief of almost all those of the northern Scots, and all those of the Picts, and had the direction of their people." (Already cited in I1; repeated here as the line the heart leans on.) LATER. Documented as Bede's account. Note the qualifier "almost all" for the Irish houses, and "for a long time", not for ever.

A4. **What Iona's mission did among the English: Lindisfarne.** Bede's chapter heading: "The same king Oswald, asking a bishop of the Scottish nation, had Aidan sent him, and granted him an episcopal see in the Isle of Lindisfarne. [a.d. 635.]" (BEDE 5726–5729; "asking-" as printed). "On the arrival of the bishop, the king appointed him his episcopal see in the isle of Lindisfarne, as he desired" (BEDE 5763–5764). "From that time many of the Scots came daily into Britain, and with great devotion preached the word to those provinces of the English, over which King Oswald reigned ... Churches were built in several places ; the people joyfully flocked together to hear the word ; money and lands were given of the king's bounty to build monasteries ; the English, great and small, were, by their Scottish masters, instructed in the rules and observance of regular discipline ; for most of them that came to preach were monks" (BEDE 5782–5794, "EngHsh" as printed). LATER. Documented as Bede's account. **Who was asked:** Oswald "sent to the elders of the Scots" (BEDE 5735); Bede does not say here that he wrote to Iona by name. That Iona sent Aidan is Bede's later line: "about eighty years after they [the monks of Hii] had sent Aidan to preach to the English nation" (BEDE 15028–15031). Say "asked the Irish for a bishop, and Iona sent Aidan".

A5. **The loss at Whitby, told with its force.** After the king ruled for Rome, "Colman, perceiving that his doctrine was rejected, and his sect despised, took with him such as would not comply with the Catholic Easter and the tonsure (for there was much controversy about that also), and went back into Scotland, to consult with his people what was to be done in this case" (BEDE 8227–8233). Bede, of Egbert's later work on Iona: he "came among them, corrected their error, and reduced them to the true and canonical day of Easter" (BEDE 5884–5886). LATER. Documented as Bede's account; Bede writes for the side that won ("error" is his word, not a neutral one).
