# Outline check: Radegund (batch 4)

## The heart of her life (checker's own, written before reading `Outline.md`)

A Thuringian princess carried off as war spoil and married by the Frankish king Lothar, Radegund left the king after he had her brother killed, was consecrated by Bishop Medard at Noyon, and founded a monastery for women at Poitiers, which Lothar endowed and to which she gave all the property the king had given her (her letter, Documented). She gave it a written Rule, the one Caesarius of Arles wrote for his sister Caesaria, under which a nun stayed inside for life; she made Agnes abbess and obeyed her herself (her letter, Documented). She sent to the emperor in the East for wood of the Cross, with King Sigibert's letters, against the resistance of the bishop of Poitiers (Gregory; Documented that it came, Contested who refused it); the house came to be known as Holy Cross (editors, Widely Accepted). About two hundred women had followed her by her death (Gregory, eyewitness, Documented). Her letter to the bishops bound them to defend the house, its Rule, its abbess and its goods against anyone, the city's bishop included; after her death, in the nuns' revolt, the abbess Leubovera read it aloud and sent out copies, and Gregory kept it whole (Documented). Her friend Fortunatus and her nun Baudonivia each wrote her Life.

Shape: Founder or leader. Major contributions: the house and its endowment; the Rule and enclosure; the Cross; the women drawn after her; the letter that defended it all.

## Verdict: FIX

One BLOCKING finding (the bishop's refusal of the Cross made optional). The rest of the outline holds against the sources.

### The heart

The outline's heart matches mine in substance: the house at Poitiers with the king's backing and her whole endowment, the Rule of Caesarius with lifelong enclosure, the Cross from the East and the name Holy Cross, about two hundred women, and the letter guarding the house, read aloud after her death. It matches Mark's approved heart (`Hearts.md`, 2026-11-18). Nothing in it says more than the sources. Her capture, her leaving the king and the altar at Noyon come after the heart, not in it; that is design, and they stay in the beats (4, 5). Beats 1–3 carry the heart in the first minute, and Opening 1 leads straight into it.

### Findings

**B1. BLOCKING. The bishop of Poitiers' refusal of the Cross is made optional (beat 3).** The old outline told it as part of the Cross beat. The new one makes it "Optional, one clause". This is the record's conflict with her own bishop: Gregory says he rode off rather than receive the relics (DAL 19606–19610), that she then sought his good grace "but she failed", and that she put the house under the king's protection "because they could find no care for their security in the man who should have been their pastor" (DAL 19625–19632). Without it, the heart's "even the city's own bishop" reaches the listener with nothing behind it. The template allows no conflict to be left out, and an optional clause lets a writer leave it out.
Correction: in beat 3, replace "Optional, one clause: the bishop of Poitiers would not receive the Cross, Gregory says, so the king sent the bishop of Tours, who brought it in with psalms, candles and incense (L16, D5)." with "Keep, in one or two clauses, in Gregory's voice: Gregory says the bishop of Poitiers would not receive the Cross, so the king sent the bishop of Tours, who brought it in with psalms, candles and incense (L16, D5; DAL 19606–19625). This is the record's conflict with her own bishop, and it lets the listener hear the letter's naming of the city's bishop as a real danger."
Add to Do not say: "Do not say the bishop's refusal caused her letter, or give his reason: no held line says either (D5). Gregory gives only that she failed to win his good grace and turned to the king for protection (DAL 19625–19632)."

**M1. MINOR. Beat 6 marks the year as Documented.** "She died on 13 August 587 ... (S5, L24, L25. Documented ...)". The day and month are Documented; the year is the editors' reckoning (S5, D8; GRL 61607).
Correction: after "Documented; Gregory was there." add "The year 587 is the editors' reckoning from Gregory's dating: Widely Accepted (S5)."

**M2. MINOR. No Do not say entry for "about two hundred".** Gregory writes "ad numerum circiter ducentarum" (GRL 61578). A writer could drop "about".
Correction: add to Do not say: "Say 'about two hundred' nuns, not 'two hundred' (GRL 61578, L25)."

**M3. MINOR. Who asked Baudonivia (What came of them, item 4).** "Later the abbess asked Baudonivia". Her prologue is addressed to the abbess Dedimia "and the whole congregation", in the plural ("Iniungitis mihi", VR-B 29433–29441), and Krusch has "Dedimia abbatissa et omnis congregatio" (VR 28180–28181). The old outline had the same narrowing.
Correction: "Later the abbess and the nuns asked Baudonivia, a nun Radegund had raised, to write it again."
