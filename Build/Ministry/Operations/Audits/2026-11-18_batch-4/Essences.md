# The essences, approved (Mark, 2026-11-18: "All yes")

Each essence is told within the first minute. Each "also told" part is told with its weight, not timed.

## Antony the Great

**Essence.** He gave up everything for the Egyptian desert, and so many followed him that "the desert became a city of monks". The Life written about him carried his way of life across the Roman world.

**Also told:** the Gospel reading taken as spoken to him; guiding the monks "as a father"; the Latin translation; Trier and Augustine; disciples in Palestine, Syria and Arabia.

## Benedict of Nursia

**Essence.** He founded houses of monks in Italy and wrote for them a short Rule for living together, "a school for the Lord's service". Frankish rulers later made it the law for their monasteries.

**Also told:** Subiaco and Monte Cassino; the abbot the community chooses; the vow for life; Carloman in 742 and Charlemagne in 802 by name.

## Clement of Alexandria

**Essence.** A teacher in Alexandria around 200, he answered Christians who feared Greek philosophy: God gave it to the Greeks as a guide that leads to Christ. Origen followed in his path.

**Also told:** "whatever has been well said"; Jerome using his books to defend reading pagan writers.

## Columba

**Essence.** An Irish monk who left home for good as a pilgrim for Christ and founded Iona. Iona became the head of many houses. Bede says it turned the northern Picts to the faith, and through Aidan it sent the faith to the English.

**Also told:** the abbot over even bishops; the Picts claim as Bede's, contested in scope; Whitby.

## Cyril and Methodius

**Essence.** They made letters for the Slavic language and put the Gospel, the Psalter and the services into it, arguing that every people may praise God in its own tongue. Driven from Moravia, their disciples carried the work to Bulgaria.

**Also told:** the pope's 880 ruling, with the 879 ban and the Latin-Gospel condition; the Kiev chronicle finding the writing among the Rus.

## Dhuoda

**Essence.** Kept apart from her sons, a Frankish noblewoman wrote a handbook of Christian life for her son William, meant as a mirror where he would see her. It survives as the voice of a lay mother, not a cleric.

**Also told:** what it asks of him (to love God, pray simply, honor his father, help the poor, honor priests, pray for the Church and the dead); "with a heart burning"; passing it to his little brother; more than a year's work.

## Hild of Whitby

**Essence.** She ruled a house of men and women at Whitby, where the Scriptures were read so closely that five bishops came from it. There she made Caedmon, a man who could not sing, a monk, and he turned the Bible into English song.

**Also told:** goods shared, no one rich or poor; kings seeking her advice; the synod of 664 in her house, and her side losing.

## Macrina the Younger

**Essence.** The eldest of the family of Basil and Gregory of Nyssa, she turned her family's household into a community of prayer and work, where her mother lived as an equal with the slave women. Dying, she taught Gregory about death and rising again, and he wrote it down with her as "the Teacher".

**Also told:** the women's and men's sides, with Peter; the famine babies and the young widow; Ferrara 1438.

## Marcion of Pontus

**Essence.** At Rome he taught that the God who made the world is not the Father of Jesus, and kept his own Gospel and letters of Paul. His churches spread far, and the church's writers answered him book after book.

**Also told:** the law set against the gospel; "cut down" or "restored"; Rome to Persia; what the writers argued (one God made the world and sent Jesus).

## Martin of Tours

**Essence.** A Roman soldier who said he was Christ's soldier and might not fight, he became a monk and then bishop of Tours, and never stopped living as a monk. His friend's Life of him became, in a modern historian's judgment, a model for later lives of saints.

**Also told:** the cloak at Amiens; the eighty disciples and monks made bishops; Trier, with heretics not to be killed; the honor of his tomb.

## Radegund

**Essence.** A captive queen who became a nun, she founded a house for women at Poitiers, gave it a written Rule and a relic of the Cross, and wrote the letter that guarded it after she died.

**Also told:** all the king had given her; the name Holy Cross; the bishop of Poitiers refusing the relic; about two hundred women; the letter read in the revolt.

## The Council of Nicaea (325)

**Essence.** Bishops met at Nicaea over whether the Son is God as the Father is. Their creed called him "begotten, not made", of one being with the Father, and they condemned Arius. Later councils set that creed first.

**Also told:** Constantine calling the bishops; Arius's exile; decades of division after.
