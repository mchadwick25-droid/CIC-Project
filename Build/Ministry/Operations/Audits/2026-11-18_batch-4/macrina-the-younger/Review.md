# Review: Macrina the Younger

Brief: `macrina-the-younger.brief.txt` (335 words by the profiler, 336 by a plain split). Reviewer did not write it and read no earlier draft, variety report, `Outline.r50.md` or `Heart_Test.md`.

## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Macrina, the eldest sister of Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa, in fourth-century Pontus, who turned her family's house into a community of prayer.
- **What happened:** She refused marriage after her betrothed died, led her mother to live as an equal with the slave women, and the house grew into one community (women under her, men under her brother Peter) that took in famine foundlings. Dying in 379, she taught Gregory about the soul and rising again and died at prayer.
- **Why the connection matters:** Gregory wrote her teaching down as a dialogue with her as the Teacher. In 1438 at Ferrara the Latin side quoted her from that book and defended it by naming Gregory's family, her own name among them.

All three lines came from one hearing. I did not get lost anywhere. One small snag: "She died in 379, as scholars date it, and at evening she prayed" reads for a moment as though the prayer came after the death. Nowhere does it talk down or read like a children's book.

**My heart (written before reading `Hearts.md`):** Macrina, eldest of the family of Basil and Gregory of Nyssa, turned the family estate in Pontus into a house of prayer where mistress and slave women lived as equals. She drew her mother and brothers into it: Peter led the men's side, and by Gregory's contested account she turned Basil too. Her deathbed teaching on the soul and the resurrection, written down by Gregory with her as the Teacher, carried her voice into later theology.

## Verdict: REVISE

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The heart's afterlife arrives at word 273, not by 150.** The approved heart ends on the dialogue being "still quoted and argued over at Ferrara in 1438". The brief holds all of that back for the last paragraph, and gives no look-ahead in the first minute, though outline beat 3 allows one. Put the look-ahead inside the deathbed paragraph, right after the dialogue, and bring the dialogue forward, so both land by about word 170 even after BLOCKING 2's clause is added. For example: "On her deathbed she taught Gregory about the soul, death and rising again, and he wrote her teaching down as a conversation, with her as the Teacher. More than a thousand years later, at Ferrara, her words were still being quoted. Almost all we know of her comes from her brother Gregory, who sat with her as she died, and how much of the teaching is hers, no one can say." The last paragraph can then open at "In 1438, Latin and Greek church leaders..." so the say-aloud line is not said twice. Keep the Ferrara close in full: it is where the connection is strongest.
- *Do not say (add to the outline):* Not a story whose first 150 words never look ahead to the afterlife: the approved heart includes her words still being quoted at Ferrara in 1438, so beat 3's look-ahead clause is required, not optional (Review, 2026-11-18).

**BLOCKING 2. The young widow is missing.** She is in the approved heart ("it took in babies left to die in a famine, and a young widow"), in outline beat 2 and in the Shape line ("the others drawn after her"). Add one clause after the famine babies, for example: "...she took in babies left to die, and a young widow came to live under her care." One widow only, per the Do not say list.
- *Do not say (add to the outline):* Not the house without the young widow: she is part of the approved heart, so beat 2 must name her (Review, 2026-11-18).

**MINOR 1. The dialogue part lands at word 171, 21 past the mark.** BLOCKING 1's reorder fixes this. Check the count after both fixes: every heart part should arrive by about word 170.

**MINOR 2. "She died in 379, as scholars date it, and at evening she prayed."** This reads briefly as though the prayer came after the death. Put the year on the dying, for example: "In 379, as scholars date it, she lay dying, and at evening she prayed."

**MINOR 3. "Work" never appears.** The heart's "given to prayer and work" is told only as "praying night and day". If it fits without delaying the heart, use Gregory's turn from beat 2, that for them prayer was their work. Optional; it is not a block.

Not findings: beats 5 and 7 (Basil, Lampadia's quote) and Quote 3 are left out. That is the writer's call within a "do not stretch" outline, and nothing left out misleads.
