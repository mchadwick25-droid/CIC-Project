# The hearts, as the researchers set them (for Mark)

## Clement of Alexandria
Around 200, Clement was a presbyter who taught in Alexandria (office and city Documented; that he taught, Widely Accepted). He answered Christians who feared Greek philosophy: God gave it to the Greeks as he gave the Law to the Hebrews, as a guide to Christ, and he kept "whatever has been well said" (Documented, his own words, held only in English translation). Eusebius and Jerome say Origen was his pupil and followed his example (Widely Accepted; Origen's own words name Pantaenus, not Clement). Jerome later used Clement's books against a critic who said quoting pagan books defiled the church (Documented).
Length about 2:20. Opening: Christians "frightened" of philosophy "as children are at masks".

## Columba
An Irish monk who left Ireland for good as a pilgrim for Christ and founded a monastery on Iona (Documented; the year Contested). Bede, about 130 years later, says many houses in Britain and Ireland grew from Iona and Durrow, with Iona at their head, and its abbot, a priest, over even bishops "according to the example of their first teacher" (Documented as Bede's account of his own day). Bede says he turned the northern Picts to the faith (Contested in scope). In 635 his monastery sent Aidan to the English at Lindisfarne (Documented).
Length about 2:30. Opening: Bede's doubt about the man set against his certainty about the heirs.

## Martin of Tours
A Roman soldier who asked to leave the army, saying he was Christ's soldier and might not fight. He became a monk, then bishop of Tours, and never stopped living as a monk: a wooden cell, eighty disciples, many of his monks later made bishops. At Trier he pleaded that heretics should not be killed and that no worldly judge should try a church's case. His friend Sulpicius wrote his life while he lived (all Documented as Sulpicius's account, resting on him alone; "a model for later saints' lives" Dominant Modern Reconstruction; the honor of his tomb Documented through Gregory of Tours).
Length about 3:00. Opening: his words at Worms, "Until now I have served you as a soldier".

## Hild of Whitby
Hild ruled a house of men and women at Whitby, in seventh-century Northumbria, on peace and shared goods, so no one there was rich or poor; she made her people read the Scriptures so closely that many were fit to be priests (Documented as Bede's account; whether she founded Whitby or set an existing house in order, Contested within Bede). Bede says he saw five bishops come from that house, and that even kings and princes sought her advice (Documented; no king named). She took in Caedmon, a man who could not sing, made him a monk and had him taught the Bible, which he turned into English song (Documented; the gift a wonder in Bede's voice). The synod of 664 met in her house, and she held with the Irish-trained side, which lost (Documented; no words of hers).
Length about 2:45. Opening: Caedmon brought to her, resolved within about 50 words.

## Radegund
A queen who became a nun founded a house for women at Poitiers with King Lothar's backing. She gave it everything he had given her, and a written Rule (from Caesarius of Arles) under which a nun stayed inside for life (Documented; her own letter). She sent to the East for wood of the Cross (Documented); the house came to be called Holy Cross (the name rests on the editors, Widely Accepted). About two hundred women followed her (Documented). Her letter to the bishops guarded the house against anyone, even the city's bishop, and after her death the abbess read it aloud during the revolt (Documented).
Length about 2:30. Opening: her letter in her own words, "I bow down through this letter in my place".

## Cyril and Methodius
Constantine made letters for the Slavic language (Widely Accepted; that God showed them to him is the Lives' claim; which alphabet, Contested). With them the brothers put the Gospel, the Apostle, the Psalter and chosen services into Slavic (Widely Accepted). Constantine argued that every people may praise God in its own language (Documented as the Lives' account), and Pope John VIII wrote that argument into his ruling of 880, a year after banning Slavonic Mass, and with a condition: the Gospel read first in Latin (Documented, his letters). Moravia did not keep the work: the disciples were sold, chained or driven out, and carried it to Bulgaria (Documented as the Life of Clement's account). The Kiev chronicle, about 230 years later, says the writing is found among the Rus (the Chronicle's claim).
Length about 2:30. Opening: "words on water", cut to about 35 words.
