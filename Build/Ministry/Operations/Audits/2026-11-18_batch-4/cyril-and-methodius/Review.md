# Review: Cyril and Methodius

Brief reviewed: `cyril-and-methodius.brief.txt` (447 words by whitespace count; 443 by the profiler).

## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Two brothers from Thessalonica, Constantine (later the monk Cyril) and Methodius, sent by the emperor in the 860s to Moravia, where the people had no teachers in their own language.
- **What happened:** Constantine made letters for Slavic, and the brothers put the Gospel, Psalms, apostles' readings and services into it. They argued against Latin clergy that every people may praise God in its own language. Methodius was imprisoned and opposed, but Pope John VIII allowed Slavonic Mass in 880, with the Gospel read first in Latin. After Methodius died, the disciples were sold, chained and driven out of Moravia.
- **Why the connection matters:** The driven-out disciples carried the letters to Bulgaria, where Prince Boris welcomed them and Clement became bishop for the Bulgarian tongue. The Kiev chronicle later says the writing was found among the Rus.

All three lines come through from one hearing. Where I got lost: "who loved the mountain of monks" (word ~255). The mountain is never introduced, because beat 2 (Methodius leaving his post for the monks' mountain of Olympus) is not in the story. Nothing reads as a children's book or talks down.

**My own heart, written before checking (from the census entry and what they are known for):** Constantine made an alphabet for Slavic, and with Methodius translated Scripture and the liturgy into it. They defended worship in a people's own tongue against the "three languages" party, and won papal approval. When the disciples were driven out of Moravia, they carried the work to Bulgaria, and from there it passed to the Rus. This matches Mark's approved heart (`Hearts.md`, 2026-11-18) in substance. The approved heart is more exact: the pope's ruling came a year after his own ban and with a condition, and the Rus link is only what the Kiev chronicle says.

## 4. Findings

**B1. BLOCKING: the approved heart does not land in the first minute.** The pope's 880 ruling (with the earlier ban and the condition), Moravia driving the work out, and the Kiev chronicle reach the first 150 words only as one pointer sentence, and that pointer arrives at words ~194–210. *Change:* Tell all six parts by about word 150. Each can be one plain clause in the outline's terms. For example: a pope who had banned Slavonic Mass allowed it the next year, with the Gospel read first in Latin; after Methodius died, Moravia drove his disciples out, and they carried the letters to Bulgaria; more than two centuries later a chronicle in Kiev says the writing is found among the Rus. To make room, tighten the setting paragraph (~100 words before the letters are made), and move the Venice quote back to beat 3, where the outline puts it. The later paragraphs then deepen each part, as they already do. This is an outline fault, not only the writer's: beat 1 reduces these parts to "One sentence points ahead", while the Length line says "The heart fills the first 150 words." *Do not say line to add to the outline:* "Do not reduce the pope's 880 ruling (with the 879 ban and the Latin-Gospel condition), the driving-out from Moravia to Bulgaria, or the Kiev chronicle to a pointer after the first minute. Each part of Mark's approved heart is told, briefly, within about the first 150 words. Beat 1 must carry them, not only point ahead. (Review, 2026-11-18.)"

**B2. BLOCKING: "their disciples would carry the letters to Bulgaria and the Rus"** (words ~198–210). No held source says the disciples went to the Rus. The Life of Clement takes them to Bulgaria (A1, I1). For the Rus, the Chronicle says only that the writing "exists also in Rus" (I3), and the outline's Do not say already limits the Rus to that claim. Beat 1 ("carry the letters to Bulgaria and on to the Rus") left room for it. *Change:* The disciples carry the letters to Bulgaria. The Rus come only as the Kiev chronicle's statement, credited to it. *Do not say line to add:* "Not 'their disciples carried the letters to the Rus' (or 'on to the Rus'). The disciples went to Bulgaria (A1, I1). For the Rus say only that the Kiev chronicle, more than two centuries later, says the writing is found among them (I3). (Review, 2026-11-18.)"

**M1. MINOR: "who loved the mountain of monks"** comes with no introduction, so the listener cannot tell what mountain is meant. *Change:* Give beat 2's single fact about Methodius early in a clause, as the outline words it: he left governing a Slavic district for "a mountain of monks called Olympus". Or say "the monks' mountain where he had lived".

**M2. MINOR: the Life of Clement's voice slips.** "The teachers were chained and jailed, then stripped and driven out by soldiers" comes after the "says" sentence and reads as the narrator's own fact. The Do not say says to tell it "as that Life's account, not as settled fact". *Change:* Keep the witness in that sentence, for example "Its teachers, it says, were chained and jailed..."

**M3. MINOR: "not to give up their teaching"** paraphrases Q2, where the text says "disciplinam tuam", your teaching (VM 12254–12255). *Change:* "not to give up his teaching for its sake", or "the teaching".

**M4. MINOR: the "every people" part ends at about word 166.** This is within 20 words of the mark. The B1 recast should bring it inside 150.

**M5. MINOR: what Bulgaria did with the work is thin.** The outline's Connection names Clement's plain sermons, written because Bulgarian priests could barely follow Greek (I1). That is the receivers' side the Missionary shape asks for. If the B1 recast leaves room, keep one clause on it. It is not required.

**Length.** 447 words, inside the ~460 ceiling. There is no padding to cut beyond what B1 moves.

## Verdict

**REVISE.** The approved heart's second half (the pope's ruling, the driving-out, the Kiev chronicle) is told only after the first minute (B1). One claim about the Rus is unsupported (B2). Both start in beat 1 of the outline, so add the two Do not say lines there before the revision.
