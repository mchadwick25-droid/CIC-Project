## 1. The heart, written before reading `Outline.md`

Constantine of Thessalonica made letters for the Slavic language, where none had existed (VC 20224–20233; VM 12217–12219; J8 13948–13949, "a Constantino quondam philosopho repertas"). With them he and his brother Methodius put the Gospel, the Apostle, the Psalter and chosen services into Slavic (VM 12480–12482), and in Moravia they taught disciples and turned the church's services into Slavic (VC 20256–20259). Against clergy who held that God may be praised only in Hebrew, Greek and Latin, Constantine argued that every people may praise God in its own tongue (VC 20264–20314). After his death Methodius, as bishop and archbishop, kept Slavonic worship going through Frankish imprisonment and a papal ban (VM 12327–12365; J8 13799–13805), until John VIII in 880 approved it, with the Gospel read first in Latin, and wrote the every-language argument into his own letter (J8 13948–13980). After Methodius died the disciples were sold, jailed and driven out of Moravia (PG 98396–98682); Clement, Naum and Angelarius carried the letters and books to Bulgaria, where Clement became the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue (PG 98960–99372). The Kiev chronicle, about 230 years later, says the writing is found in Rus and among the Danube Bulgarians (RPC 7870–7873).

## 5. Findings

**MINOR 1. The 879 ban was a repeat, not a first ban.** J8 13799–13802 says "iam litteris nostris, per Paulum episcopum Anconitanum tibi directis prohibuimus": the pope had already banned Slavonic Mass in an earlier letter, and the 879 letter repeats it. The heart says "though he had banned Slavonic Mass the year before", and Do not say says "a year after he had banned Slavonic Mass". Beat 6 has it right. Correction, in both places: "though the year before he had repeated his ban on Slavonic Mass".

**MINOR 2. "Methodius carried that claim on after him" has the wrong citations.** M7, M9 and M11 are about Constantine. What shows Methodius carrying it on is that he went on singing Mass in Slavonic under the ban (M16, J8 13799–13800) and was examined and approved for it (M17). Correction: "...and Methodius kept Slavonic worship going after him (M16, M17)".

**MINOR 3. "Led worship in Slavic" says a little more than VC.** VC 20256–20259 says he "turned the whole church order" and taught the disciples the offices ("vertit ... et edocuit eos officium matutinum, horas et vesperas ..."). Correction, in the heart and beat 1: "In Moravia they taught disciples and turned the church's services into Slavic, teaching them the daily prayers and the Mass (M6)".

**MINOR 4. "Moravia did not keep the work" is a reading of the expulsion, not a statement in a held source.** The *Life of Clement* tells the expulsion (A1) and the Chronicle places the writing in Rus and Bulgaria (I3), but no held text says Slavonic worship ended in Moravia. Mark's heart uses the phrase, and it is a fair reading, so keep the sense but tie it to what the source says. Correction, in the heart and Connection: "In Moravia the work was driven out with its people: after Methodius died his disciples were chained, sold or driven out (A1)". In Connection, change "the work did not survive in Moravia, where it began" to "the work was driven out of Moravia, where it began".

**MINOR 5. The heart's last sentence has no source or level.** "Slavic-language Christianity starts with them" leans on the census legacy summary (card only) and reads as an appended moral. What the held sources say is that no one had made Slavic letters before (VC 20227, 20304–20306; J8 13948–13949). Correction: replace it with "Written Slavic, and the Scripture and worship in it, begin with Constantine's letters (M4, M17: Documented as the Life's account and the pope's letter)", or cut it.

**MINOR 6. I3 paraphrase narrows the Chronicle.** RPC 7867–7871 says the books were first written for one Slavic race that included the Danube Slavs, the Moravians, the Czechs, the Lyakhs and the Polyanians, "the last of whom are now called Russes". "The Moravians and their neighbouring Slavs" adds "neighbouring" and hides that the Chronicle counts the Rus among the first readers. Correction, What came of them 3: "the Kievan Chronicle says this Slavic writing was first made for one Slavic people, the Moravians, the Czechs, the Lyakhs and the Polyanians, 'now called Russes', and is found in Rus and among the Danube Bulgarians too (I3, RPC 7867–7873)".

**MINOR 7. Do not say: whom the 880 letter was to.** J8 no. 5 is addressed "Dilecto filio Sfentopulcho glorioso comiti" (GZ 13878), not to Methodius, and the Links line says John VIII "wrote to Methodius" (true of 879 and 881). A writer could easily say the pope wrote Q3 to Methodius. Add: "Not 'the pope wrote to Methodius' for the 880 ruling. It was a letter to the prince Svatopluk, written after the pope had questioned Methodius in Rome (M17, GZ 13878)."
