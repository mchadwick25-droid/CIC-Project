## 1. The heart, before hearing the story

Two brothers from Thessalonica gave the Slavs letters of their own. With those letters they put the Gospel, the Psalter and the church's services into Slavic, so a people could hear and worship God in their own speech. They argued that God may be praised in every language, against clergy who allowed only Hebrew, Greek and Latin. In 880 a pope wrote that argument into a ruling. Their disciples then carried the letters and the Slavic services to Bulgaria, and from there the writing reached the Rus. That is why they are on the tree: Slavic-language Christianity starts with them.

What the sources hold for each part:

- **The letters.** That Constantine made a Slavic alphabet is Widely Accepted (M5, Cross). That God showed them to him is the Lives' own claim. Which alphabet it was is Contested (D6). Cross and the census say Glagolitic, not Cyrillic.
- **The translations.** The Gospel, the Apostle, the Psalter and chosen services, done by the two brothers, are Widely Accepted (M20, card keyWorks). The services turned into Slavic in Moravia are Documented as the Life of Constantine's account (M6). The whole Bible in six months is Contested (D5) and is rightly left out.
- **The defence of every language.** The three-language objection and Constantine's answer at Venice are Documented as the Lives' account (M7, M9). The Lives themselves are close to the events but survive only in late copies, and Ginzel disputes the Life of Methodius (D1, Contested). Pope John VIII's approval of 880, with Q3, is Documented: it is his own letter (M17).
- **What it changed.** The disciples' move to Bulgaria, Boris's welcome and Clement as "the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue" are Documented as the Life of Clement's account. That Life is later, and its author is Contested (D2). The writing found among the Rus is Documented as the Russian Primary Chronicle's claim, written about 230 years later (I3). The census summary of the later spread is card-only.

Nothing central is missing from the source sheet. The outline's Thread line carries the whole heart. One small gap: the outline's beats never name the brothers' translation of the Gospel, Apostle and Psalter (M20, Widely Accepted). Beat 2 has only "In the beginning was the Word", and beat 3 has the services. The material is there to use, so this is not FIX-OUTLINE. The census names Hrabar's treatise, but the Library does not hold it (N2), and it is not central.

## 2. Hearing the story

The story is 502 words. The first minute is words 1–150.

What words 1–150 carry:

- **1–50: opening scene.** The emperor asks a tired scholar to go north. "If they have letters for their language," and "who can write words on water". It is vivid and points at the letters, but it is a scene, not yet the work.
- **51–94: who and where.** Constantine, later Cyril; Methodius; Thessalonica; Moravia; the ruler's request for teachers in his people's own language.
- **95–118: the letters.** "Constantine made them" arrives at word 101. Then 15 words go to Glagolitic, not Cyrillic.
- **119–138: how we know.** Two Lives by followers, kept in late copies. That is 20 words.
- **139–150: worship in Slavic.** "Turned the church's services into Slavic" (words 139–147). The objection starts at word 148 and is cut off at the minute: "but Latin clergy".

Where each part of the heart first arrives:

- **The letters:** word 101. In full, but in one clause.
- **Scripture in Slavic:** never. The story names only "the church's services" (word 142). It never says they translated the Gospel or any Scripture. "In the beginning was the Word", in the outline, is dropped.
- **Worship in their own speech:** word 139, in one clause.
- **The defence of every language:** the objection runs from word 148 to 161, and Q1 from 165 to 211. It lands just after the first minute. It is given real weight there, in Constantine's own voice.
- **The pope's ruling:** words 340–395, with Q3. Given its weight.
- **What it changed, and why they are on the tree:** word 397 onward. Bulgaria, Clement, and the Rus fill the last 105 words. The first minute gives no sign that the work outlived Moravia.

So the heart lands **in part**. The letters and Slavic worship are there, each in a single clause. The argument for every language is promised but not stated. The reach of their work, the reason they matter to the tree, is not there at all.

Where the time goes in the first minute: about 50 words of scene, 44 of who and where, 20 of how we know, and 15 on which alphabet it was not. That leaves about 20 words for what they actually did.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.**

Missing from the first minute:

1. **The point of the letters: Scripture and worship in their own speech.** The outline's Thread line carries it: "letters of their own, and with them the Gospel and worship in their own speech". M5 ("In the beginning was the Word", the first words written) and M20 (Gospel, Apostle, Psalter and chosen services, Widely Accepted) carry the Scripture part.
2. **The claim they fought for: every people may praise God in its own language.** The Thread line carries it, as do M7 and M9. A short statement of it can come before Q1, which can stay where it is.
3. **What it changed: the letters outlived Moravia and reached Bulgaria and the Rus.** I1 and I3 carry it. A one-sentence forward pointer near the top is enough. The full telling can stay at the end.

What can make room, with no new claims: the opening scene can stay, but the "how we know" sentence (words 119–138) can move later, beside the Life of Clement or the Chronicle. The Glagolitic clause (104–118) can be shortened to "Slavic letters", which the outline already asks for (D6).
