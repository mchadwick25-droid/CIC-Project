# Outline: Cyril and Methodius

Every line cites `Source_Sheet.md` (S = setting, M = moment, I = impact, D = disagreement, N = not held, A = added for batch 4).

- **The heart of their life:** Constantine made letters for the Slavic language. That he made a Slavic alphabet is Widely Accepted (M5); that God showed it to him is the Lives' claim; which alphabet it was is Contested (D6). With those letters the two brothers put the Gospel, the Apostle (the book of readings from Acts and the apostles' letters), the Psalter and chosen church services into Slavic (M20, A3: Widely Accepted). In Moravia they taught disciples and turned the church's services into Slavic, teaching them the daily prayers and the Mass (M6: Documented as the *Life of Constantine*'s account). Latin clergy said God may rightly be praised only in Hebrew, Greek and Latin. Constantine answered that every people may praise God in its own language (M7, M9, M11: Documented as the Lives' account), and Methodius kept Slavonic worship going after him (M16, M17). In 880 Pope John VIII wrote that same argument into his ruling and kept a condition, though the year before he had repeated his ban on Slavonic Mass (M16, M17, A2: Documented, his own letters). What it changed: In Moravia the work was driven out with its people: after Methodius died his disciples were chained, sold or driven out (A1), and carried the letters and books to Bulgaria (I1, A1: Documented as the later *Life of Clement*'s account). More than two centuries later, the chronicle of Kiev says this writing is found among the Rus (I3: Documented as the Chronicle's claim). Written Slavic, and the Scripture and worship in it, begin with Constantine's letters (M4, M17: Documented as the Life's account and the pope's letter).
- **Essence:** They made letters for the Slavic language and put the Gospel, the Psalter and the services into it, arguing that every people may praise God in its own tongue. Driven from Moravia, their disciples carried the work to Bulgaria.
- **Also told:** the pope's 880 ruling, with the 879 ban and the Latin-Gospel condition; the Kiev chronicle finding the writing among the Rus.
- **Length:** about 2½ minutes (about 380 words). Reason: two lives, not one, and an argument that moved from Moravia to Venice to Rome and on into Bulgaria and the Rus. The record is full enough to carry it. Up to about 3 minutes for a person (about 460 words), about 4 minutes 15 for a council (about 550); craft comes before time (Mark, 2026-11-18). The heart fills the first 150 words.
- **Setting:** "In the ninth century, in the 860s" (S4); "two brothers from Thessalonica, a Greek city where Slavic was spoken too" (S2, VM 12212–12213); "sent from Constantinople to Moravia, in central Europe, in what is now the Czech Republic" (S3); tradition: "the mission of Cyril and Methodius", "the Slavic church that grew from it" (S5). Name him Constantine until Rome; "Cyril" is the monk's name he took weeks before he died (S1).
- **Shape:** Missionary or translator. The trap is the sender's side only: give the Moravians' request (S6), the hostile Salzburg voice (M14), and the Bulgarians who received the work (I1).
- **Openings:** (a) Chosen. The emperor asks the tired scholar to go. He says he will go gladly "if they have letters for their language"; without them, he asks, "who can write words on water, and who wants to be called a heretic?" (M4). Keep it to about 35 words, then go straight to the letters he made. (b) Not chosen for the opening: Venice, where Latin bishops, priests and monks gather against him "like crows against a falcon" (M9). Use it in beat 3.

**Story beats** (beat 1 is the first minute)
1. **The heart, told at once.** In the 860s Rastislav of Moravia sent to Emperor Michael: teachers from Italy, Greece and Germany teach us different things; we have no one to teach us in our own language (S6). The emperor chose two brothers from Thessalonica, because people there speak Slavic (S2). Constantine asked whether the Slavs had letters. They had none (M4). After prayer he made letters, and the first words he wrote were "In the beginning was the Word" (M5: the making is Widely Accepted; the prayer and God's showing are the *Life of Constantine*'s, told in its voice; the first words are its account). With Methodius he put the Gospel, the Apostle, the Psalms and chosen services into Slavic (M20, A3). In Moravia they taught disciples and turned the church's services into Slavic, teaching them the daily prayers and the Mass (M6). Latin clergy objected that God chose only three languages, Hebrew, Greek and Latin, the three on the sign Pilate put over the cross (M7). Constantine answered that every people may praise God in its own tongue (M9). One sentence points ahead: a pope would approve it, and their disciples would carry the letters to Bulgaria and on to the Rus (M17, I1, I3). Say "Slavic letters" (D6).
2. **Who they were** (brief). Constantine, the youngest, studied in Constantinople, was made librarian at Hagia Sophia, then taught philosophy. The Lives call him "Constantine the Philosopher" (S1, M1). Methodius governed a Slavic district for the emperor, then left it to become a monk on a mountain of monks called Olympus, where Constantine later joined him (S2, M2). Say "a mountain of monks called Olympus"; give no location (N4). Documented as the Lives' account. Then the how-we-know sentence (see Who tells it).
3. **Venice.** The Latin bishops, priests and monks gathered "like crows against a falcon" (M9). They pressed him: no apostle, pope or Church Father had made such letters. He answered with Q1, and named peoples who already praised God in their own letters: Armenians, Persians, Goths, Arabs, Egyptians, Syrians and others (M9). Documented as the *Life of Constantine*'s account. Give no figure for the years in Moravia (D8).
4. **Rome.** Pope Hadrian came out to meet them, because they carried relics of Clement, an early bishop of Rome. The Slavonic books were laid on an altar, and the disciples sang the liturgy in Slavonic in St Peter's (M10). Constantine fell ill, took the monk's habit and the name Cyril, and died on 14 February 869, aged 42 (M11, S4). His last words to his brother: Q2 (M11). He was buried in the church of St Clement (M12). Documented as the Lives' account. Which pope: Contested (D3); the *Life of Constantine* names Hadrian. The year 869 is Cross's; the Lives give a year of the world (S4).
5. **Methodius holds on.** He went back as bishop for the Slavs of Pannonia (M13). Frankish bishops said he was teaching in their land; he said the land belonged to St Peter. They held him for two and a half years in Swabia, until the pope barred them from saying Mass (M14). A Salzburg writer of his own day complained that this "Greek" with "newly invented Slavic letters" had partly cheapened in the people's eyes the Masses and Gospels of the priests who held them in Latin (M14). The Moravians then asked Rome for him as archbishop (M15). Prison: Documented as the *Life of Methodius*'s account. The Salzburg complaint: written in his lifetime, by his opponents.
6. **The pope's ruling.** In 879 Pope John VIII summoned him and repeated an earlier ban on singing Mass in Slavonic; he could still preach in it (M16). In 880, after questioning him before the pope's own bishops, he found him sound, praised the letters "found by Constantine the Philosopher", allowed Slavonic Mass and Scripture, with the Gospel read first in Latin, and wrote Q3 (M17, A2). Documented: these are the pope's letters. Methodius died on 6 April 885. His disciples held the funeral in Latin, Greek and Slavonic, and crowds came, rich and poor, free and slave (M21, S4). Death and funeral: Documented as the *Life of Methodius*'s account. The year 885 comes from a modern historian (S4).

**What came of them** (about a third of the story; item 1 is pointed to in beat 1 and told in full here)
1. Heaviest: the work went on with their disciples, but not in Moravia. Wiching, whom Methodius had condemned, took the see (I1, LCl 12972–12974). The *Life of Clement* says the younger priests and deacons were sold as slaves, the teachers were chained and jailed, and then soldiers stripped them and drove them out (A1). Clement, Naum and Angelarius crossed the Danube to Bulgaria. Prince Boris thanked God for sending them. Clement became "the first bishop of the Bulgarian tongue" and wrote plain sermons for every feast, because Bulgarian priests could barely follow Greek (I1). The *Life of Clement* says, in paraphrase, that Clement handed down to "us Bulgarians" all that belongs to the Church (PG 99590–99594). All Documented as the *Life of Clement*'s account, written a generation or more later (D2).
2. **Defended:** that God may be praised in every language (I2). Cyril's dying prayer asked God to destroy "the three-language heresy" (M11). The pope's ruling of 880 (M17, A2) and the Kievan Chronicle both carried it on (I2). It was not won at once: the same pope banned Slavonic Mass in 879 (M16).
3. The Rus took the letters as their own. In paraphrase: the Kievan Chronicle says this Slavic writing was first made for one Slavic people, the Moravians, the Czechs, the Lyakhs and the Polyanians, "now called Russes", and is found in Rus and among the Danube Bulgarians too (I3, RPC 7867–7873). Documented as the Chronicle's claim, about 230 years later.
4. Memory: in Rome a painted image over Cyril's tomb, with lamps kept burning day and night (I4). In Moravia in 1380 a bishop ordered their feast kept, calling them "Fathers, Apostles and our chief Patrons" (I4).
End on 1 or 3, as an event: who carried their letters on, and how far they reached.

**Quotes**
- **Q1.** VC 20307–20310: "nonne pluvia a deo aequaliter super omnes cadit? et sol nonne eodem modo lucet omnibus (Matth. 5. 45)? nonne aerem omnes aequaliter spiramus ? quomodo igitur vos non pudet tres tantum linguas statuere, reliquos populos et Stirpes caecos et surdos esse iubentes ?" Rendered from Dümmler and Miklosich's Latin translation of the Slavonic Life (the Slavonic is not readable in the Library): "Does God's rain not fall on all alike? Does the sun not shine the same on everyone? Do we not all breathe the air alike? Then how are you not ashamed to fix on three languages only? You would order every other people and tribe to be blind and deaf." Job: his case in his own voice, in beat 3. The heart states the claim plainly in beat 1; this gives it his words.
- **Q2.** VM 12251–12255 (the editor's notes: "the mountain" is Olympus, 12288; "going to judgment" means to death, 12286): "Ecce frater nos consortes eramus, unum sulcum imprimentes atque ego in agro cado, postquam diem meum terminavi, tu autem amas montem valde, noli relinquere montis gratia disciplinam tuam, hac re enim potes melius salvus fieri." Rendered from Miklosich's Latin translation of the Slavonic Life of Methodius: "Look, brother, we were partners ploughing a single furrow. And I fall in the field, having finished my day. You love the mountain very much. Do not give up your teaching for the mountain's sake. By this you can better be saved." Job: the handover from one brother to the other.
- **Q3.** J8 13974–13976: "qüoniam qui fecit tres linguas principales, bebream [hebream] scilicet, grecam et latinam, ipse creavit et alias omnes ad laudem et gloriam suam." Rendered from the Latin original: "For he who made the three chief languages, Hebrew, Greek and Latin, also himself created all the others, for his praise and glory." Job: the argument won, in a pope's own words.

**Connection to carry into the story:** The letters and the Slavic books passed from the brothers to their disciples, and through Clement, Naum and Angelarius to Bulgaria, where Clement became bishop for the Bulgarian tongue and wrote sermons Bulgarian priests could follow (I1). The Kiev chronicle later names the same writing as found among the Rus (I3). The listener needs to understand that the work was driven out of Moravia, where it began. It survived because the disciples who were driven out carried it to a ruler who welcomed it (A1, I1). It must come through what the disciples did, not a "this matters because" sentence.

**Links:** Photius, his teacher (heard him teach; M1). Anastasius the Librarian, at the Slavonic liturgy in Rome; wrote of him (met; wrote of him). Popes Hadrian II (met) and John VIII (met; wrote to Methodius). Clement of Rome (kept his relics). Gorazd, Clement of Ohrid, Naum (heard them teach; carried on their work). Boris of Bulgaria (received their disciples). Wiching (opposed). Sheet §4.

**Who tells it**
- *Life of Constantine* and *Life of Methodius*: written in Slavonic by the brothers' own followers, judged by their editors to be close to the events, but kept only in copies made centuries later, from the 14th and 15th centuries on; Ginzel disputes the Life of Methodius (D1). Say aloud, after the first minute (beat 2 or 3): "Most of what we know comes from two Lives written by their own followers. They survive only in copies made centuries later."
- Pope John VIII's letters (879–881) and the Salzburg complaint (870s): written in the brothers' own time, by a supporter and by opponents.
- *Life of Clement*: either by a Bulgarian who had lived with Clement, in the early tenth century, or by Archbishop Theophylact, around 1100. Disputed (D2). It is hostile to the Franks and to Wiching's party. Say: "a Life of Clement, written in Bulgaria a generation or more later."
- *Russian Primary Chronicle*: compiled in Kiev about 230 years later, by 1116 (I3). Say: "Writing in Kiev more than two centuries later, the chroniclers..."

**Do not say**
- The Salzburg writer says Methodius had "partly" cheapened the Latin Masses in the people's eyes; never drop "partly". (Round-two review, 2026-11-17.)
- Not "the Cyrillic alphabet", and not "Glagolitic" either in the first minute. Say "Slavic letters" (D6). Which alphabet is Contested; it belongs on the card. (Heart test, 2026-11-18.)
- Not "God gave him the letters" in the narrator's voice. Say "the Life says that after prayer God showed him the letters", or "he made letters" (M5).
- Not "they translated the Bible" or "the whole Bible". Say "the Gospel, the Psalms, the readings from the apostles and chosen services" (M20). The six-month Bible is Contested and left out (D5). (Heart test, 2026-11-18.)
- Do not say when or where the Gospel, Apostle and Psalter were translated: the *Life of Methodius* says only that the brothers did it together, earlier (A3). Do not say they finished it before leaving for Moravia, or in Moravia.
- Not "the first words ever written in Slavic". Say "the first words he wrote", as the *Life of Constantine* tells it (M5).
- Not "the pope allowed Slavonic worship" with nothing more. Say he allowed it with the Gospel read first in Latin, though the year before he had repeated his ban on Slavonic Mass (M16, M17).
- Not "the pope wrote to Methodius" for the 880 ruling. It was a letter to the prince Svatopluk, written after the pope had questioned Methodius in Rome (M17, GZ 13878).
- Not "the disciples left" or "moved on". The *Life of Clement* says they were sold, chained and driven out (A1); tell it as that Life's account, not as settled fact.
- Not "they converted the Slavs" or "brought Christianity to the Slavs". Moravia was already Christian and had teachers from Italy, Greece and Germany (S6).
- Not "the Rus learned to read from them" or that the letters spread to Serbia and Ukraine. Say only what the Chronicle says: the writing is found in Rus and among the Danube Bulgarians (I3). The wider spread is the census summary, card only. (Heart test, 2026-11-18.)
- Not "Hrabar defended the letters". His treatise is not held (N2). (Heart test, 2026-11-18.)
- Not "Cyril, bishop". He was never made bishop in the Lives (D9).
- Do not put the how-we-know sentence in the first minute. It belongs after the heart, in beat 2 or 3. (Heart test, 2026-11-18.)
- Do not reduce the pope's 880 ruling (with the 879 ban and the Latin-Gospel condition), the driving-out from Moravia to Bulgaria, or the Kiev chronicle to a pointer. Each part of Mark's approved heart is told, briefly. (Review, 2026-11-18.)
- Not 'their disciples carried the letters to the Rus' (or 'on to the Rus'). The disciples went to Bulgaria (A1, I1). For the Rus say only that the Kiev chronicle, more than two centuries later, says the writing is found among them (I3). (Review, 2026-11-18.)

**Leave out:** the childhood dream of Sophia and the lost falcon (side stories); the Khazar mission and the cup inscription (side story, M3); the paralytic healed and other wonders at Rome (can stay only in the Life's voice, and is not needed); the chains loosed by a wonder in the *Life of Clement* (A1); Methodius's prophecies (VM 11); the whole Bible in six months (Contested, D5); "the Cyrillic alphabet" (D6); calling Cyril a bishop (D9); 24 years as bishop (D10); Constantine in Bulgaria (D11); Hadrian's letter and Stephen V's letter (both disputed, D4, D7); Borivoj's baptism (a modern guess); the Photius anecdote from Anastasius (card); the *Life of Clement*'s attacks on Svatopluk's morals (polemic); exact years in Moravia (D8); Hrabar's treatise (not held, N2); the Life of Clement's naming of the slave buyers as Jews (A1): the sale is told, the buyers are left for the card, since naming them carries that Life's polemic into the story without anything to place it.
