# Outline: Marcion of Pontus

Sheet = `Source_Sheet.md` in this folder; tags (S1, M3, I1, B1 …) point to its entries, which give file and lines. No word of Marcion's own survives, and every witness below is an opponent.

- **The heart of their life:** Marcion taught at Rome, in the middle of the second century, that the God of the law and the prophets, who made the world, was not the Father of Jesus. Jesus came from another, higher God (Justin in his lifetime, M4, Q1; Irenaeus and Tertullian agree: Documented). He set the law against the gospel in a book, the *Antitheses* ("contradictory propositions"), now lost (Q3, M4: Documented as Tertullian's report; his followers owned and used the book, B1). He held a Scripture of his own: one Gospel with no author's name and the letters of Paul. His opponents say he cut both down from Luke and Paul; he said the church's Gospel had been added to by "the defenders of Judaism", and that he was restoring it (M5, M6: Documented as their report of each side). He founded churches of his own, which spread: in his lifetime Justin says he had led many "in every race of people", and two centuries later Epiphanius still finds them from Rome to Persia (I1, B2, B3: Documented). What it changed: the church's writers answered him, book after book, that one God made the world and is the Father of Jesus, and Irenaeus argued against him that the Gospel is fourfold (I3, I4: Documented). Whether he caused the church's list of books is Contested and stays out (I4).
- **Length:** about 2 minutes 30 seconds (about 375 words). He is a major figure, and the heart has four parts that each need room: the two Gods, the law against the gospel, his own Scripture, and churches that spread and were fought for two centuries. It does not stretch to 3: no word of his own survives, and the facts of his life are few and come only from opponents.
- **Setting:** Century: the middle of the second century. Justin writes to the emperor Antoninus Pius while Marcion "is even at this day alive" (S2); "middle of the second century" rests on modern chronology (Widely Accepted, S2). No year for any event (N1). Place: he came from Pontus, on the Black Sea ("The Euxine Sea", S1; "a man of Pontus", S1), and taught at Rome ("in the church of Rome", S3). Trade: "that shipmaster of Pontus" (S5). Tradition: he began in **the church at Rome** and founded **the churches of the Marcionites** (S4); spoken forms "the Marcionites" or "the churches that followed Marcion".
- **Shape:** Teacher or thinker, with a founder's reach. The question he answered (is the Creator the Father of Jesus?), what he taught, the churches that followed him, and the long answer his opponents wrote.
- **Openings:**
  1. **(Chosen.)** Justin's words to the emperor, while Marcion is alive (Q1): "Even now he is still teaching the people he has won over. He teaches them to believe in some other god, greater than the Creator." It leads straight into the heart: what he taught, and that people followed him. Say who speaks in the same breath: Justin, a Christian writing against him.
  2. (Kept for later in the story, not the opening.) Polycarp meets Marcion once; Marcion asks, "Do you know me?" and Polycarp answers, "I do know you: the firstborn of Satan" (M8, Q2).

**Story beats** (beats 1–3 and the first sentence of What came 1 are told in the first minute, about 150 words)

1. **Another god, above the Creator** (first minute). Justin, writing to the emperor in Marcion's lifetime, says he is still teaching (Q1). Then the setting in passing: a shipmaster from Pontus, on the Black Sea, teaching at Rome in the middle of the second century (S1, S2, S3, S5). Irenaeus, a generation later, says the same core: the God "proclaimed as God by the law and the prophets", the maker of the world, was for Marcion "the author of evils"; Jesus came from the Father "who is above the God that made the world" (M4). "The law" = the law of Moses and the Hebrew Scriptures. How we know, one sentence here: everything we know of him comes from the men who wrote against him; nothing he wrote survives (sheet head note, N2). Documented.
2. **The law against the gospel** (first minute). He wrote a book, the *Antitheses*, "contradictory propositions", setting the gospel against the law (M4). Tertullian names it his chief idea (Q3). The book is lost; we know it through Tertullian, who says Marcion's followers owned it as his and held it up (B1). Documented as Tertullian's report.
3. **A Scripture of his own** (first minute). He used one Gospel, with no author's name, and Paul's letters (M5). His opponents say he cut them down from Luke's Gospel and Paul (Irenaeus, Tertullian). His own claim, as Tertullian reports it from the *Antitheses*: "the defenders of Judaism" had added to the Gospel to tie it to the law and the prophets, and he was taking it back to what it was (M6). His followers said he had not made something new but restored the faith (M6). He leaned on Paul rebuking even apostles in Galatians, Tertullian says (M6). Documented as report. **Defended by him and his followers.**
4. **Before the break: one church with Rome** (after the minute). Tertullian: at first he believed as the church at Rome did, and "in the first warmth of faith" he gave it money, two hundred sesterces; a letter of his own showed this first faith (M1, M2). Documented as Tertullian's account.
5. **Cast out** (after the minute). Rome expelled him "more than once", Tertullian says, of him and Valentinus together, for a "restless curiosity" that spread to others; his gift was cast out with him (M9, M1). No year is held. Tertullian also says his churches baptised only the unmarried, the widowed, or those who had parted from a spouse (M7). Documented as Tertullian's account. Optional, one sentence: Tertullian says he later offered to repent, on terms that he bring his followers back, but died first (M10; Inferential-Thin: one witness, in a passage with a wrong bishop, D1).
6. **Tertullian's account of his question** (after the minute; optional if room is short). Tertullian says he brooded over where evil comes from. He set Jesus' saying that a good tree cannot bear bad fruit beside God's words in Isaiah, "I am He that createth evil", judged the Creator the bad tree, and found another, good God in Christ's kindness (M3). Tertullian's hostile reconstruction; as Marcion's real reasons, Inferential-Thin. Tell it only in Tertullian's voice.

**What came of them** (about a third of the story; heaviest first)

1. **His churches spread and lasted.** First minute, one sentence: Justin says he had led many "in every race of people" to deny that God made the world, and adds that the demons helped him (B3, I1). Later: Tertullian, who scorned them, says the Marcionites "make churches", as wasps make combs, with Marcion as their founder (I1, B2). Two centuries later Epiphanius still finds them in Rome and Italy, Egypt, Palestine, Arabia, Syria, Cyprus, Upper Egypt, even Persia, and other places (I1).
2. **The church answered him in book after book** (defended against him: one God, maker of the world and Father of Jesus). Name this in the first minute in one clause; tell it here. Justin wrote a book against him in his lifetime; Irenaeus quotes it: "I would not have believed the Lord Himself, if He had announced any other than He who is our framer, maker, and nourisher." Theophilus, Philip of Gortyna, Modestus, Irenaeus and Rhodo wrote against him. Tertullian's *Against Marcion* runs to five books, his third attempt (I3).
3. **Four Gospels, and the older faith** (defended against him). Irenaeus argued that the Gospel is fourfold, and named Marcion among those who make it fewer. Tertullian argued that the older Gospel must be the true one (I4).
4. **Polycarp's answer, and those he turned back.** Irenaeus, who saw Polycarp in his early youth, tells that Marcion met Polycarp, the bishop of Smyrna, once, and the exchange in Q2 (M8). Polycarp, coming to Rome under Anicetus, turned many back from Valentinus, Marcion and the rest (I5).
5. **They had their own martyrs.** A writer quoted by Eusebius says the Marcionites claimed "a multitude of martyrs". Eusebius tells of a man burned at Smyrna who "appears to have been" a Marcionite, and a woman martyr at Caesarea who, "it is reported", belonged to Marcion's sect (I2). No date, or "a century later" (the editor dates Metrodorus to 250).
6. **His school split after him** into those holding one, two or three first principles; Apelles and Lucan were his disciples (I6). Card or one clause only.

**Quotes** (3)

- **Q1.** Justin, *First Apology* 26, Greek (JG 729–731): "Μαρκίωνα δέ τινα Ποντικόν, ὃς καὶ νῦν ἔτι ἐστὶ διδάσκων τοὺς πειθομένους, ἄλλον τινὰ νομίζειν μείζονα τοῦ δημιουργοῦ θεόν". Printed English (ANF 14943–14945): "And there is Marcion, a man of Pontus, who is even at this day alive, and teaching his disciples to believe in some other god greater than the Creator." **Rendering (from the Greek):** "Even now he is still teaching the people he has won over. He teaches them to believe in some other god, greater than the Creator." Job: the opening; the one voice from his lifetime, giving the date and the core teaching at once.
- **Q3.** Tertullian, *Against Marcion* I.19, Latin (OEH 4337–4338): "Separatio legis et evangelii proprium et principale opus est Marcionis". Printed English (T 23734–23736): "Marcion's special and principal work is the separation of the law and the gospel". **Rendering (from the Latin):** "Separating the law from the gospel is Marcion's own work, and his chief one." Job: names his idea in one line, in an opponent's voice, inside the first minute.
- **Q2.** Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* III.3.4 (ANF 39882–39884): "“Dost thou know me?” “I do know thee, the first-born of Satan.”" **Rendering (from the ANF English; the Greek, kept in Eusebius Book IV, and Irenaeus's Latin are not held):** "“Do you know me?” “I do know you: the firstborn of Satan.”" Job: after the minute, a picture of how hard the church set itself against him. Marcion asks; Polycarp answers; Irenaeus tells it.

**Connection to carry into the story:** Marcion's teaching made the church's writers say, in book after book, what they had held without having to argue it: that the God who made the world, and spoke in the law and the prophets, is the Father of Jesus. Justin's book is quoted by Irenaeus: he would not have believed the Lord Himself if He had announced any other God than our maker (I3). Irenaeus answered Marcion's single Gospel by arguing that the Gospel is fourfold (I4). The listener needs to understand that this was not a quarrel about one man: Marcion's churches were real and widespread, in every region from Rome to Persia two centuries later (I1), so the answer had to be written again and again (Tertullian's five books, his third attempt, I3). Carry it through what the writers did, not a "this matters because" sentence.

**Who tells it**

- Justin, writing to the emperor, in Marcion's own lifetime; an opponent; the only witness from his own day.
- Irenaeus, about a generation later; he saw Polycarp in his early youth.
- Tertullian, in a book dated about 207 by its editor, some sixty years after Marcion.
- Epiphanius, about two centuries later.
- How-we-know is told in one sentence in the first minute, not as the story: "Everything we know of Marcion comes from the men who wrote against him." The doubt is not itself the story; the hostile witnesses are named as each speaks.

**Leave out**

- The bishop's-son and seduced-virgin story, and Epiphanius's Rome scene with the presbyters and the wineskins (D3, D4): late, hostile, without earlier support; Holl, the Greek editor, doubted it.
- Tertullian's abuse of Pontus and its peoples (T 22516–22560): invective, not record.
- The Eleutherus dating and the 115 years (D1, D2): card only.
- Lists of his scriptural cuts (Ephesians as "Laodiceans", the Apocalypse), and the editor's "eleven books" (B4): card only.
- Rhodo's names for the later schools (Potitus, Basilicus, Syneros): card only.
- Not left out: the expulsions, the gift cast out with him, Polycarp's "firstborn of Satan", Justin's demons, and the long war of books against him. These are the record's conflict and are told plainly, each credited to its speaker.
