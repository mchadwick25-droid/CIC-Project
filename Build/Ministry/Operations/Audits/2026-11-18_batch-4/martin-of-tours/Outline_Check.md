# Outline check: Martin of Tours (batch 4)

## My account of the heart (written before reading Outline.md)

A Roman soldier, forced into the army as a veteran's son, who at Worms asked Julian Caesar for his discharge: "I am the soldier of Christ: it is not lawful for me to fight" (M6). He turned to the monk's life under Hilary of Poitiers and founded a monastery near Poitiers (M7, M9). Drawn out by a trick and a crowd to be bishop of Tours, he stayed a monk: a wooden cell two miles outside the city, eighty disciples holding all in common, many of them later made bishops (M10, M11, I3). He worked against the country temples, by force and by persuasion (M13). At Trier he pleaded that the Priscillianists' blood not be shed and that no secular judge should try a church's case; later he took communion once with Ithacius's party to save lives, would not sign, and never again went to a synod (M15, M16, I4). Sulpicius, who met and questioned him, wrote his Life while he lived (M17, D2); it was read widely and carried on by Gregory and Fortunatus, and a modern historian calls it a model for later saints' lives (I2). His tomb at Tours became the centre of Gaul's devotion, used by bishops and kings (I1, through Gregory).

Confidence: all as Sulpicius's account (Documented, one witness); the tomb Documented through Gregory, about 180–195 years on; the Life as *the* model, Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
