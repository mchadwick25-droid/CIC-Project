## B4-01
**Source (Latin):** "audieram enim de Antonio, quod ex euangelica lectione, cui forte superuenerat, admonitus fuerit, tamquam sibi diceretur quod legebatur: uade, uende omnia, quae habes, da pauperibus et habebis thesaurum in caelis; et ueni, sequere me, et tali oraculo confestim ad te esse conuersum." (42 words)

**Rendering:** For I had heard this about Antony. He happened to come in while the Gospel was being read. He took what was read as a warning spoken to him: "Go, sell all that you have, give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me." By that word from God he turned to you at once.

**Note:** The passage runs across a page break. Footnotes, the critical apparatus, the running head ("confessionum lib. VIII, c. 12.") and the page number 195 sit between "et ueni," and "sequere me". The margin number "20" after "euangelica" is a line number, not text. The apparatus records variants: "21 fuerat W" (its lemma for that line is unclear to me; possibly "superuenerat" or "fuerit"), "dicebatur" for "legebatur" (H), and "et da" for "da" (several manuscripts and the editions). "oraculo" is a divine saying or answer; "word from God" renders it. "ad te" is addressed to God, as through the Confessions.

## B4-03
**Source (English translation):** "They demand bare faith alone" (passage: "bare faith alone", 3 words)

**Rendering:** Faith alone, with nothing added.

**Note:** The printed text is already English (ANF translation). "bare" means stripped of everything else; the rendering keeps that sense. The original Greek is not in this file, so I did not read it.

## B4-04
**Source (English translation):** "this was a schoolmaster to bring “the Hellenic mind,” as the law, the Hebrews, “to Christ.”" (16 words)

**Rendering:** Philosophy was a guardian that led the Greek mind to Christ. In the same way, the law led the Hebrews to Christ.

**Note:** "this" refers back to philosophy in the previous sentence; I named it. The sentence is elliptical ("as the law, the Hebrews"); splitting it into two sentences supplies the repeated verb. "schoolmaster" echoes Gal. 3:24 (footnoted in the source); the Greek word there means the household slave who took a child to school, not a teacher. The quotation marks are the translator's.

## B4-05
**Source (English translation):** "but whatever has been well said by each of those sects" (passage: "whatever has been well said", 5 words)

**Rendering:** Whatever has been said well.

**Note:** Clean. The full sentence: by philosophy Clement means not one school but whatever each school has said well.

## B4-06
**Source (English translation):** "Is there any want of learning in these, or are they not rather drawn from the very heart of philosophy?" (20 words)

**Rendering:** Do these works lack learning? Or are they not drawn from the very heart of philosophy?

**Note:** "these" = Clement's works just listed (the *Outline Sketches*, the treatise against the Gentiles, the three books of the *Pedagogue*). "want" means lack. Footnote markup interrupts the line before the passage, not within it.

## B4-07
**Source (English translation):** "wishing to make a pilgrimage for Christ from Ireland to Britain, he sailed forth." (14 words)

**Rendering:** He wanted to go on pilgrimage for Christ, from Ireland to Britain. So he set sail.

**Note:** "So" is a light link I added to join the two sentences; it could be dropped. The subject is Columba, in his forty-second year, two years after the battle of Cúl Dreimne ("Culedrebina", with the editor's gloss "(Cooldrevny)").

## B4-08
**Source (Latin):** "iuxta exemplum primi doctoris illius" (5 words)

**Rendering:** Following the example of that first teacher.

**Note:** "illius" can carry the sense "that famous". The next line identifies him: he was not a bishop but a priest and a monk (Columba). OCR damage just outside the passage: "subiectij", "episcoj)us".

## B4-09
**Source (English translation):** "came among them, corrected their error" (6 words)

**Rendering:** He came among them and corrected their mistake.

**Note:** The subject (a man who lived in exile in Ireland for Christ, learned in Scripture) is in the preceding line. The error is the dating of Easter, as the next words say.

## B4-10
**Source (English translation):** "a certain countryman, with all his household, hearing the word of life through an interpreter when the holy man preached, believed, and, believing, was baptized," (25 words)

**Rendering:** A man from the countryside heard the word of life when the holy man preached. He heard it through an interpreter. He believed, with all his household. And because he believed, he was baptized.

**Note:** "with all his household" attaches to the whole clause; the next line spells it out (husband, wife, children and servants). "and, believing, was baptized" could mean "as a believer" rather than "because"; I took the causal sense, but it is a choice. Setting: among the Picts.

## B4-11
**Source (English translation):** "saying also that he is a creature." (7 words)

**Rendering:** He also said that the Son is a created being.

**Note:** "saying" belongs to Arius, the subject of the surrounding charges. "he" is the Son of God; I named him to avoid an unclear pronoun. "creature" here means something made, not an animal.

## B4-12
**Source (English translation):** "they were caught whispering to each other and winking with their eyes," (12 words)

**Rendering:** They were caught whispering to one another and winking.

**Note:** "they" = Eusebius and his party. "winking with their eyes" is shortened to "winking"; nothing is lost in sense.

## B4-13
**Source (English translation):** "the impious writing had been torn up in the sight of all," (12 words)

**Rendering:** The ungodly document was torn up in front of everyone.

**Note:** Clean. Context: after the Eusebian party had been exposed.

## B4-14
**Source (English translation):** "The Faith of the Three Hundred and Eighteen Fathers assembled at Nice in Bithynia shall not be set aside, but shall remain firm." (23 words)

**Rendering:** The faith of the 318 Fathers who met at Nicaea in Bithynia must not be set aside. It must stand firm.

**Note:** "Nice" is the old English spelling of Nicaea. Canon I of the council. The editor's brackets "[*Anomæans, the Arians or*]" come just after the passage, not in it.

## B4-15
**Source (Latin):** "si litteras habent linguae suae accomodatas" (6 words)

**Rendering:** If they have letters suited to their own language.

**Note:** "accomodatas" is a spelling of "accommodatas". "they" are the people the philosopher is being sent to. "letters" means an alphabet, a script.

## B4-16
**Source (Latin):** "quis potest in aqua orationem scribere, et quis vult haeretici sibi nomen comparare?" (13 words)

**Rendering:** Who can write words on water? And who wants to earn himself the name of heretic?

**Note:** "scribere" is hyphenated across the line ("scri-bere"). "orationem" can mean speech, words or prayer; here it means words or a text, not prayer. "comparare" here means to acquire, not to compare.

## B4-17
**Source (Latin):** "Bulgariez lingue Clemens primus constituitur episcopus" (6 words)

**Rendering:** And so Clement became the first bishop in the Bulgarian language.

**Note:** OCR damage: "Bulgariez" for "Bulgaricae"; "lingue" is the medieval spelling of "linguae". "constituitur" is a present tense used for a past event ("is appointed"); I rendered it as past. "And so" renders the "et sic" that opens the clause, just before the passage. "bishop in (or of) the Bulgarian language" is ambiguous: a bishop for Bulgarian speakers, or the first bishop to use Bulgarian. This Latin is a translation set beside a Greek text in the Migne volume; I did not check the Greek.

## B4-18
**Source (Latin):** "nonne pluvia a deo aequaliter super omnes cadit?" (8 words)

**Rendering:** Does not God's rain fall equally on everyone?

**Note:** "a deo" is literally "from God". The line breaks "aequa-liter". Echoes Matt. 5:45, cited by the editor in parentheses just after.

## B4-19
**Source (Latin):** "Ecce frater nos consortes eramus, unum sulcum imprimentes atque ego in agro cado, postquam diem meum terminavi" (17 words)

**Rendering:** Look, brother, we were yoked together. We ploughed one furrow. And I am falling in the field. I have finished my day.

**Note:** "consortes" means partners who share a lot; "yoked together" follows the ploughing image the next words build. "cado" is present ("I fall"). A footnote marker sits on "Judicium*)" in the line before.

## B4-20
**Source (Latin):** "quidam graecus, methodms nomine, noviter inventis sclavinis Hlieris" (8 words)

**Rendering:** A certain Greek named Methodius, with newly invented Slavonic letters.

**Note:** Heavy OCR damage: "methodms" for "Methodius", "Hlieris" for "litteris". The passage is a fragment. Its verb comes after it ("superdu[cens]…" in the next line), so it does not make a full sentence on its own.

## B4-21
**Source (Latin):** "qüoniam qui fecit tres linguas principales, bebream scilicet, grecam et latinam, ipse creavit et alias omnes ad laudem et gloriam suam." (21 words)

**Rendering:** For the one who made the three chief languages, Hebrew, Greek and Latin, also created all the others. He made them for his own praise and glory.

**Note:** OCR damage: "qüoniam" for "quoniam", "bebream" for "hebream". "grecam" is a medieval spelling of "graecam". Damage just after: "V^Tubemus" for "Jubemus".

## B4-22
**Source (Latin):** "ut quasi in picturam speculi, me mente et corpore legendo et Deum deprecando intueri possis" (15 words)

**Rendering:** So that you can look at me, as if in a picture in a mirror, in mind and body. You do this by reading and by praying to God.

**Note:** Ambiguous: "mente et corpore" most likely goes with "me" (see me in mind and body). It could also be read as "with mind and body, by reading and praying". "in picturam" (accusative) is a medieval use for "in pictura". "you" is her son. OCR damage nearby, outside the passage ("qiiod fiiturum", "deflciens").

## B4-23
**Source (Latin):** "sed non jfiquali conditione, animo ardentis in pectore, sicut ego genitrix tua." (12 words)

**Rendering:** But they will not teach on equal terms. They will not have the spirit that burns in the heart, as I, your mother, do.

**Note:** OCR damage: "jfiquali" for "aequali". The sentence is elliptical; "they will not teach" and "they will not have" supply the sense from the clause before (the teachers he will have). "ardentis" is genitive ("of one burning"); it may stand for "ardenti" ("with a burning spirit"). Either way the sense is the same. "pectore" is literally "breast".

## B4-24
**Source (Greek):** "Μαρκίωνα δέ τινα Ποντικόν, ὃς καὶ νῦν ἔτι ἐστὶ διδάσκων τοὺς πειθομένους, ἄλλον τινὰ νομίζειν μείζονα τοῦ δημιουργοῦ θεόν·" (19 words)

**Rendering:** And there is a certain Marcion from Pontus. He is still teaching even now. He teaches those who are persuaded by him to believe in another god, greater than the Maker.

**Note:** "Μαρκίωνα" is accusative: it depends on a governing verb earlier in the passage, outside these lines, so "there is" supplies the frame. "νομίζειν" depends on "διδάσκων" (teaching them to think). "δημιουργοῦ" is the Craftsman or Maker of the world, that is, the Creator; I avoided the loanword "Demiurge". The second "ὃς" clause begins on the next line and is not part of this passage.

**Rendering:** Those who defended Judaism.

**Note:** This is Marcion's charge, reported by Tertullian, about who altered the Gospel. The translation is English; the Latin behind it is not in this file, so I did not read it.

## B4-26

**Rendering:** Among every race of people.
