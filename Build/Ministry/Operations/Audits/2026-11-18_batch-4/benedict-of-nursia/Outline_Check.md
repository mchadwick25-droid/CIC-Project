# Outline check: Benedict of Nursia (batch 4)

Checker, 2026-11-18.

## My account of the heart, written before reading the outline

Benedict left his studies at Rome to serve God, lived three years alone in a cave at Subiaco, and drew others after him until he built twelve small monasteries there and then the house at Monte Cassino, over a pagan shrine (Gregory, LATER, the only witness to the life). His lasting work is the Rule: a short book for monks living together under an abbot the community chooses, which calls itself "a school for the Lord's service" in which he hopes nothing harsh or heavy will be laid down, orders the day between prayer, work with the hands and holy reading, receives guests as Christ, asks the abbot to temper all things so the strong have scope and the weak are not scared off, and binds the monk for life by the promise of stability, turning of life and obedience (OWN, Documented). Gregory points readers to the Rule as his true life, since he could teach no other way than he lived. About two centuries later Frankish rulers made it the law for monasteries: Carloman's council of 742 and Charlemagne at Aachen in 802 (LATER, Documented), and by the ninth century it was in practice the only monastic rule of the West (editor, Widely Accepted).

## Findings

1. **MINOR: the abbot lands just after the first minute.** Mark's approved heart says "under an abbot the community chooses". Beat 1 runs to about 160 words without it, and beat 2 "may begin just inside the first minute or just after it". **Correction:** in beat 1, change "For his monks he wrote a rule (M18)" to "For monks living together under an abbot the community chooses, he wrote a rule (M18, R2, R6)". To make room, move "He called it 'this little Rule written for beginners' (R8; RBE 4297–4300)" from beat 1 into beat 2, with finding 2.

2. **MINOR: "little Rule for beginners" has lost its pointer.** The line means something only beside the older guides it points to. **Correction:** in beat 2, add: "He called it 'this little Rule written for beginners', and pointed those who wanted more to Basil's Rule and the Collations, Institutes and Lives of the Fathers (R8; RBE 4290–4300)."

3. **MINOR: the Connection says more than the sources.** "Benedict wrote for his own monks in Italy; he made no order and gave no law beyond his houses" and "rulers ... north of the Alps" are not in any held source. The Rule itself plans for monks in cold and hot regions: clothing is to be given "secundum locorum qualitatem ubi habitant ... quia in frigidis regionibus amplius indigetur, in calidis vero minus" (RBL 8079–8083). Gregory's Latin is "monachorum Regulam", a rule for monks (AS 80434–80435). **Correction:** replace those two sentences with: "What the listener needs: Benedict wrote a rule for monks, but no source has him make it binding beyond his own houses, and there was no order in his lifetime. Frankish rulers two centuries later took up his book and made it the measure of the monasteries in their lands."

   - `cic/texts/regularis-concordia_de-consuetudine-monachorum-in-anglia-13-lat-ang_logeman1891.txt`.
