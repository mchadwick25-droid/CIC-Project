# Review: Benedict of Nursia (batch 4)

Reviewer, 2026-11-18. Brief: `benedict-of-nursia.brief.txt` (455 words by `wc`, 452 by the profiler).

## The test, from one hearing

- **Who:** Benedict, a monk in Italy around 500, under Gothic kings, who founded houses of monks at Subiaco and Monte Cassino and wrote a rule for monks living together under an abbot.
- **What happened:** He left his studies in Rome for a cave, was asked to lead a nearby house whose monks (Gregory says) tried to poison him, founded his own houses, and wrote a Rule that hopes for nothing harsh yet binds a monk for life to stay, to turn his life to God and to obey, with set hours of work and holy reading. Later the Lombards sacked Monte Cassino and, by Paul the Deacon's account, the monks carried the Rule to Rome.
- **Why the connection matters:** About two centuries on, Frankish rulers made his book the measure of their monasteries: Carloman's council in 742 bound monks and nuns to it, and Charlemagne at Aachen in 802 ordered whatever went against it put right by it. And we know the man himself only through Gregory, who pointed to the Rule as his truest portrait.

All three lines came from one hearing. None is blank or wrong.

## My heart, written before checking

Benedict turned from Rome's schools to a monk's life and built communities of monks at Subiaco and Monte Cassino (Gregory, about fifty years later, the only witness). His lasting work is the Rule: a short guide for monks under an abbot their community chooses, "a school for the Lord's service" meant to be neither harsh nor heavy, but binding a monk for life by stability, turning of life and obedience, with prayer, work with the hands and holy reading, and guests received as Christ. Two centuries later the Frankish rulers made it the law of the monasteries in their lands (742, 802). It matches Mark's approved heart.

## Findings

1. **MINOR: the abbot's election lands at word 167, about 17 words past the mark.** The outline's beat 1 already says "For monks living together under an abbot the community chooses, he wrote a rule"; the brief dropped "the community chooses". **Change:** restore it: "For monks living together under an abbot they choose, he wrote a rule." Then open paragraph 3 with "Even the last monk to join may be chosen." so the point is not told twice.

2. **MINOR: Carloman and Charlemagne, named in the approved heart, first arrive at words 409 and 427.** The part itself, Frankish rulers making the Rule the measure of their monasteries, lands at word 149, and the outline's beat 1 deliberately gives the reach "in one sentence" and tells it in full under What came of them. I take the names as the evidence for that part, not a separate part, so this is MINOR. If Mark reads the names as part of the heart, this becomes BLOCKING. **Change (optional, about 6 words):** "About two centuries later, Frankish rulers, Carloman and then Charlemagne, ordered the monasteries of their lands to live by it."

3. **MINOR: the 802 sentence adds a purpose and a summons.** "Charlemagne gathered the abbots and monks at Aachen to read it together" says he called them to Aachen in order to read it. The annals of Lorsch say that within the synod he gathered "universos abbates et monachos qui ibi aderant", the abbots and monks who were there, "et ipsi inter se conventum faciebant et legerunt regulam" (MGH 7794–7796). **Change:** "And in 802, at Aachen, Charlemagne gathered the abbots and monks who were there, and they read it together. Then he ordered that whatever in the monasteries went against it be put right by it." **Do not say line to add to the outline:** Wrong: "Charlemagne gathered (or summoned) the abbots and monks to Aachen to read the Rule." Right: "gathered the abbots and monks who were at Aachen, where they read it together" (MGH 7794–7796).

4. **MINOR: "Carloman" is not identified.** The listener heard "Frankish rulers" some 250 words before. **Change:** "In 742, the council of Carloman, ruler of the Franks, bound the monks and nuns of his lands to the rule of Saint Benedict." (outline What came of them, item 1; MGH 2203, "dux Francorum"). If finding 2's change is made, this one may not be needed.

5. **MINOR: one Gregory claim moves into the narrator's voice.** "From then on, the king was less cruel" stands as its own sentence, outside "He tells too how...". The outline gives it as "From then on, Gregory says, the king was less cruel" (beat 6; AS 79164, "minus crudelis fuit"). **Change:** "From then on, Gregory says, the king was less cruel."
