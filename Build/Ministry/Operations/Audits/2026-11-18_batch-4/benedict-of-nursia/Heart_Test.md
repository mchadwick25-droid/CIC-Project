## 1. The heart, before hearing the story

Benedict founded monasteries at Subiaco and Monte Cassino, and he wrote a short, moderate Rule for monks who live together under an abbot they choose. He called it a little rule for beginners, a school for the Lord's service with nothing harsh in it. Within about 250 years Frankish rulers made it the law of their monasteries, and the Benedictines take their name from him. That Rule is why he is on the tree.

What the sources hold for each part:

- **He founded monasteries at Subiaco and Monte Cassino.** Gregory's *Dialogues* II: twelve houses at Subiaco (M9), then Monte Cassino on the site of Apollo's temple (M12). Documented as Gregory's account. The date of Monte Cassino, about 528 or 529, is the editors' (S3b, S3c): Dominant Modern Reconstruction.
- **He wrote the Rule.** Gregory says so (M18), and the Rule survives in Latin (RBL). Gasquet: no serious doubt it is substantially his. Documented (OWN). Where and when he wrote it: Gregory is silent; Gasquet's Subiaco-then-Cassino view is Dominant Modern Reconstruction (D3).
- **What the Rule is.** A school with nothing harsh (R1); stability, a turned life and obedience (R3); set times for work and holy reading (R4); guests received as Christ (R5); an abbot chosen by the community, who tempers all things (R6); "a little Rule written for beginners" that points to Basil and the Fathers (R8). All Documented (OWN). That it drew on Basil and Cassian: Widely Accepted (editors, I5).
- **What it changed.** Carloman's council of 742 bound the monks and nuns of his lands to it (I1a); Charlemagne's synod at Aachen in 802 had it read and the monasteries put right by it (I1c). Both Documented (LATER, about 200 and 260 years). Gasquet's judgment that it became "practically the only monastic Rule of the West" from the ninth to the twelfth century (I1d): Widely Accepted, an editor's view. The Benedictine name is later (S6b).

Nothing central is missing. The sheet and the outline both hold the Rule and its later force. The outline's thread line says it in one sentence: "He left the church a short, moderate Rule for monks living together under an abbot, which later rulers made the law of their monasteries."

## 2. Hearing the story once

The story is 433 words. The first minute is about 150 words.

What the first 150 words carry:

- **1–56: scene and setting.** Romanus lowers bread and a bell to the cave; "He was Benedict"; Italy around 500 under Gothic kings.
- **57–81: how we know.** Gregory wrote the life about fifty years later, from four of his monks.
- **82–113: early life.** Born in Nursia, schooled in Rome, left his books and his father's house.
- **114–150: the cave and the first abbacy.** Three years in the cave; others came for teaching; a nearby monastery begs him to be abbot.

The heart does not land in the first minute at all. Where each part first arrives:

- Founding monasteries: word 196 ("He built twelve monasteries at Subiaco"); Monte Cassino at word 212.
- The Rule: word 251 ("He wrote a rule for his monks"). The word "rule" at 171 is the Vicovaro monks' own rule, not his.
- What the Rule changed: word 333 (Carloman's council of 742), then Charlemagne at 357.

The heart is given its weight later. The Rule has words 251–331, about 80 words, with both quotes. The councils have words 333–390, about 58 words. That is real weight, not a passing clause. But a listener who stops at one minute has heard a hermit in a cave and has not heard that he wrote anything, founded anything, or changed anything.

Where the first minute's time goes: about 56 words of opening scene, 25 words of how-we-know, and about 70 words of life before any founding. The poisoned-wine story then runs to word 193. The story follows the life in order and saves the reason for the end.

## 3. Verdict

**FIX.** The material is in the outline and sources. The story does not put it in the first minute.

Missing from the first minute:

1. That he founded monasteries at Subiaco and Monte Cassino.
2. That he wrote a short, gentle Rule for monks living together under an abbot.
3. That later rulers made that Rule the law of their monasteries.

What in the outline can carry it, with no new claims:

- The thread line: "He left the church a short, moderate Rule for monks living together under an abbot, which later rulers made the law of their monasteries."
- Beat 4 and beat 5 (twelve houses at Subiaco; Monte Cassino) for the founding.
- Beat 6 and Q1 for the Rule's own voice: "a school for the Lord's service", "nothing harsh and nothing heavy", "this little Rule written for beginners".
- What came of them, item 1: Carloman's council of 742, for the lands he ruled, and Charlemagne's order at Aachen in 802.
- Opening 3 / Q3, Gregory's line that the holy man "could not teach in any other way than he lived", can tie the cave scene to the Rule early.

Room for it: the how-we-know sentence (words 57–81) can move later, near the Totila or Lombard material, and the opening scene can be shorter. Keep the outline's limits: no "Benedictine Order" in his lifetime, no "first rule in the West", Carloman's council for his lands only, and Charlemagne's order for his realm, not Europe.
