## Findings

1. **BLOCKING: a misleading pronoun in Hild's early life.** The brief says: "She was the daughter of King Edwin's nephew, and took the faith with him at the preaching of Paulinus." Heard once, "him" falls most naturally on the nearest man, her father. Bede says she took the faith with King Edwin (HE 10901–10906). Her father, Hereric, "lived in banishment, under Cerdic, king of the Britons, where he was also poisoned" (HE 11022–11024, sheet M1), and Bede never says he took the faith.
   - **Change to:** "She was the daughter of King Edwin's nephew, and took the faith with King Edwin at the preaching of Paulinus."
   - **Do not say line to add to the outline:** "'took the faith with him' after naming her father (the nephew)." → name the king: "took the faith with King Edwin"; Bede never says her father, Hereric, took the faith, and he died in exile (M1, M2).
2. **MINOR: two heart parts arrive just past the first-minute mark.**
   - The kings and princes come at word 151.
   - The synod comes at word 166, and its full clause ends at 189.
   - Both are within the 20-word allowance. If any trim is made in the opening (finding 4), it would pull both inside 150. No other change is needed.
3. **MINOR: "King Oswy ruled for Peter, keeper of heaven's keys" never ties Peter to Rome aloud.** The brief has just said Wilfrid "argued for Rome". A listener who does not know that Rome claimed Peter may not hear which side won.
   - **Change to (outline's own words, beat 5):** "King Oswy ruled for Rome's side, for Peter, keeper of heaven's keys."
4. **MINOR: the opening resolves by word 81, against Mark's "about 50".** The scene does resolve in her act, as the outline requires, and the heart is not delayed. The overrun comes from keeping the "taught sacred history ... English song" clause inside the opening, and that clause is part of the heart. Accept it as it stands, or tighten the first sentence. "No longer young" is a Do not say safeguard, so it must stay.
5. **MINOR: the synod paragraph runs as one act per sentence.** "At Whitby, Bishop Colman led ... King Oswy ruled ... Colman ... went home ... Bede does not say ..." The Standard asks for no run of short sentences that each tell one act.
   - **Change to:** join the ruling and Colman's departure with cause, which is in the record: "King Oswy ruled for Rome's side ..., and Bede says all present agreed; Colman, his teaching rejected, went home with those who would not accept it."
6. **MINOR: the connection rests on one cold gloss.** "In those days a bishop led the church of a whole region" sits between "Mother" and the closing quote without a link. The outline wants the listener to grasp that one house was supplying a church converted within living memory with its leaders.
   - **Change to (suggested):** tie the gloss to the young church already named at word 256. For example: "In those days a bishop led the church of a whole region, and the English church was still young."
   - Keep the closing quote as it is.
