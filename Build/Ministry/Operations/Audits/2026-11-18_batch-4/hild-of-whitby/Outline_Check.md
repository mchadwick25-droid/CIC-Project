## My account of the heart (from the census entry and the source sheet)

Hild, a noblewoman of King Edwin's house who took the faith with him, turned to the monastic life at thirty-three. Called home by Aidan, she ruled first Heruteu (Hartlepool) and then Whitby, which she built or set in order (Bede says both): a house of men and women run on the pattern of the first church in Acts, in peace and charity, with goods in common so that no one was rich or poor. She made her people study the Scriptures so that many were fit to be priests; Bede saw five bishops come from the house. Ordinary people, and sometimes kings and princes, sought her counsel. She received Caedmon, had his gift judged, made him a monk and had him taught the sacred history, which he turned into English song. The synod of 664 met in her house; she and her people held with the Scots (Colman's, Iona-trained side), and the king ruled against them. All who knew her called her Mother. She died in 680 after six years of illness, still teaching, urging gospel peace. Almost all of it rests on Bede, about fifty years later.
