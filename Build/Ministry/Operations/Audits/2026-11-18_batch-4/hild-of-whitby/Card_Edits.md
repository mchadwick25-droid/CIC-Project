## briefReferences

3. IV.23 entry. Old: "thirty-three years in the world; the plan to live in Gaul for the Lord's sake; Aidan calls her home; Hartlepool; Whitby, built or set in order; no one rich and no one poor;" New: "thirty-three years in the world; Aidan calls her home from the East Angles; Hartlepool; Whitby; peace and charity, and all goods shared after the example of the earliest church; no one rich and no one poor;". Evidence: brief, "after Bishop Aidan called her home from the East Angles, she ruled first Hartlepool, then Whitby" and "a monastery of men and women that she ran on peace and charity. After the example of the earliest church, all goods were shared"; the brief no longer names Gaul or "built or set in order". Source: HE 10923–10929, 10953–10964; HEL 24866–24867.
4. IV.23 entry. Old: "five bishops, among them Oftfor, who studied in both her monasteries, then went to Theodore in Kent and to Rome, and was later chosen bishop (Giles 10980-10990, 11002-11003);" New: "ordinary people, and sometimes kings and princes, seeking her advice; five bishops;". Evidence: brief, "Ordinary people, he writes, and sometimes even kings and princes, sought her advice" (HE 10964–10967; HEL 24936–24939); the brief no longer tells of Oftfor.
5. Removed the entry "Ecclesiastical History III.24: Oswy's daughter given to God in 655, Heruteu, and Whitby two years later." The brief tells none of it.
6. III.25 entry. Old: "Ecclesiastical History III.25: the synod of 664 at her monastery; Hild with the Scots; Oswy's ruling and all present assenting." New: "Ecclesiastical History III.25: the two Easters; the synod of 664 at her monastery; Colman leading the Scots' side and Wilfrid arguing for Rome; Hild with the Scots; Oswy's ruling for Peter, keeper of heaven's keys, and all present assenting; Colman going home with those who would not accept it; no word of how Hild took the ruling." Evidence: brief paragraph 4 ("the two kept Easter on different dates. At Whitby, Bishop Colman led the Scots' side ... Bede does not say how Hild took the ruling"). Source: HE 7955–7970, 8192–8218, 8227–8233; HEL 21408–21414.
7. IV.24 entry. Old: "the learned men" was preceded by "the steward, "; and "others after him attempted religious poems in English." New: "the steward, " dropped; "his verses turning many minds toward heaven; others after him attempted religious poems in English, but none could match him." Evidence: brief, "In the morning he was brought to the abbess" (no steward) and "Caedmon's verses, Bede says, turned many minds toward heaven, and none who tried religious poems in English after him could match him" (HE 11145–11150).
8. Old: "Bede on the Northumbrians north of the Humber, and on the Scots from Ireland and Aidan from Iona (Hii)." New: "Bede on the Scots from Ireland and Aidan from Iona (Hii)." Evidence: the brief no longer names the Humber.
