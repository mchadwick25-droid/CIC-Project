## Notes on the reader's flags

**B4-01, page break and apparatus.** The passage runs from line 13470 to 13501; the footnotes and page head between "et ueni," and "sequere me" are not text, as the card says. The margin "20" is a line number. The variants (dicebatur for legebatur, et da for da, the W reading at line 21) do not touch our sense. "Divine word" for oraculo and "you" as God both follow the card.

**B4-02, which Latin.** The card quotes from Gardner's 1911 English (5571-5572) and gives the Latin as `original`; the reader read the same sentence in the Acta Sanctorum (80438-80440). The Latin agrees word for word. "regulze" at 80438 is outside the quote.

**B4-03 and B4-25, English only.** The ANF files hold no Greek or Latin for these lines, and the cards carry no `original`. Nothing to settle.

**B4-04, "this".** The reader is right that "this" is philosophy. The brief's "it" follows "God gave philosophy to the Greeks", so the referent is clear.

**B4-07, "So".** The reader's own link; nothing in ours.

**B4-08, "illius".** Bede's Latin (Plummer 18879) has "primi doctoris illius". Giles gives "their"; "that (famous)" is also possible. The brief names Columba, priest and monk, as the next line does.

**B4-09, the subject.** Lines 5875-5885 make Egbert the one who "came among them, corrected their error"; the brief agrees.

**B4-10, "with all his household".** The reader is right that it goes with the whole clause. The next line (7614) names who: "the husband, with his wife and children and servants", baptized together. The brief's "a countryman baptized 'with all his household'" holds. The causal or participial "believing" is outside our quote.

**B4-11, "creature".** The brief says it of the Son; the modern sense "animal" is not invited there.

**B4-14, "Nice".** Old spelling of Nicaea; the brief says Constantinople's council and "the 318 fathers", not the place name.

**B4-16, orationem and comparare.** Agreed: words, not prayer; acquire, not compare. This Latin is Miklosich's translation of the Slavonic Life (the file is Slavonic, Latin and German); the card holds the Latin only.

**B4-17, OCR and the Greek.** "Bulgariez lingue" is OCR for Bulgaricae/Bulgariae linguae; the card says so. The Greek column at these lines (99300-99372) is garbled OCR and cannot be read, so the point rests on the Latin. "Of the Bulgarian tongue" keeps both senses the reader names (bishop for Bulgarian speakers, or first to serve in Bulgarian); "tongue" can also mean a people, which fits either. "Becomes" in the brief renders the present constituitur.

**B4-19, "cado".** Present, as in ours.

**B4-20, fragment.** Agreed; the card records that the verb comes at 13662-13663 and that the brief paraphrases the rest, keeping "partly" (ex parte).

**B4-22, the ambiguity.** Lines 3164-3167: "ut quasi in picturam speculi, me mente et corpore legendo et Deum deprecando intueri possis". The brief takes mente et corpore with "me", the reader's first reading.

**B4-23, ardentis.** Either case gives the same sense, as the reader says. "On equal terms" renders aequali conditione; the next clause shows it means the same footing of love, not rank.

**B4-24, accusative frame.** Agreed; "There is" supplies the frame, as the ANF English does ("And there is Marcion").

**B4-26, κατὰ.** "In" or "throughout"; no difference to a listener.
