# Review: Dhuoda

Brief: `dhuoda.brief.txt` (418 words by whitespace count; 414 by the profiler). Reviewed against `Standard.md`, `Shapes.md`, `Process.md`, the approved heart in `Hearts.md` (2026-11-18), the census entry `dhuoda`, `Outline.md`, `Source_Sheet.md`, `Outline_Check.md`, and `cic/texts/dhuoda_liber-manualis_bondurand1887.txt`.

**Verdict: REVISE.** No factual error was found and every quote holds. But four parts of the approved heart arrive late or not at all.

## The three lines, from one hearing

1. **Who.** Dhuoda, a Frankish noblewoman at Uzès in the 840s. She dictated a handbook of Christian life for her elder son William.
2. **What happened.** She was kept apart from William, who had been sent to serve King Charles, and from her newborn son, who had been brought to his father. This was during the war between Louis's sons. She wrote the book to stand in her place. Her husband was put to death in 844, William was killed in 850, and no record gives her death.
3. **Why the connection matters.** A mother who was not one of the clergy passed on the faith in writing, and the book outlived her family. It was copied, printed in 1677 and 1887, and named in 1923 among Christian instruction from before Luther.

All three lines can be filled in. None is blank or wrong.

## The heart

**My own, written first from the census and what she is known for.** She was a Frankish laywoman, separated from her sons in the civil war of Charlemagne's grandsons. From 841 to 843 she wrote the *Liber manualis*, a handbook of Christian life for her son William, meant as a mirror in which he would see her. It is a rare voice of faith from outside the clergy and a woman's voice. It was kept, and its editor called it the oldest writing by a woman born in France to reach us. The brief tells my version in its first minute, except for the separation from both sons and the year and more she spent on the book.

## Scores

- **Voice: 4.** An adult documentary narrator throughout, opening in her own words and never talking down. Not a children's book anywhere. The two "He must..." sentences lean towards a list of rules, but each carries several concrete asks.
- **Craft: 4.** The opening leads straight into what the book is. The stakes (war, the baby brought away before baptism, the execution, William's killing) are told plainly, and the ending lands on what was done with the book, with no moral. One stretch is out of place. "Almost all we know of her comes from her own book" sits between the chronology and the epitaph, so the line "In it she writes her own epitaph" follows a sentence about the chronicles (MINOR 3).
- **Rigor: 4.** Every sentence traces to the outline. All three quotes are word for word from the outline and match the Latin. Every Do not say line is honoured. The heart parts listed above are missing, and there are two small ambiguities in reference (MINOR 2 and 4).
- **Accessibility: 4.** Grade 7.2 (6.6 with names out), reading ease 79.9 with names out, 18.0 words a sentence, longest sentence 30 words. The profiler passes it. On the listener read the low grade is not flatness: the sentences carry cause and time, and the vocabulary is adult. Two pronouns ("In it", "there") make the listener stop.

## Source check

- **Q1** ("I see many people ... my son William."): the outline's rendering, word for word. The Latin is at DH 1893–1895 (`Cernens plurimos cum suis in sgeculo gaudere proies, et me Dodanam, ofili Willelme, a te elongatam conspiciens`), and the rendering is faithful. "So begins" holds: §2 follows straight after the incipit ("Incipit liber Dodanae Manualis quem ad filium suum direxit Willelmum", DH 1877–1880). The acrostic verses come after it.
- **Q2** ("mind and body, as in a picture in a mirror"): the outline's words, word for word. The Latin is at DH 3163–3166 (`ut quasi in picturam speculi, me mente et corpore legendo et Deum deprecando intueri possis`). "Reading it and praying" is the narrator's paraphrase of "legendo et Deum deprecando". It is not quoted, and it holds.
- **Q3** ("but they will not teach on equal terms ... your mother."): the outline's rendering, word for word, with a lower-case "but". The Latin is at DH 3168–3171 and holds.
- **Little brother** (T8): "illi ostende, et admone legendo ... cùm perfectum loquendi vel legendi acceperit tempus" (DH 3172–3178) supports "show the book to his little brother when the boy could read".
- **"Charles among them"**: Charles is Louis's son (S5, Nithard). Holds.
- **Baby brought to Bernard with the bishop of Uzès; stayed at her lord's command** (E4, E5). Holds, and follows the Do not say wording.
- **844 and 848–850** (E19, E20): these follow the outline wording. "Killed there" stands for the annal's "aput Barcinonem" (AB 2518–2520) (MINOR 4).
- **"Her book outlived the family"**: the outline's connection wording. Bondurand records that the younger son died in 872, "sans laisser plus de postérité" (DH 1849–1851). Holds.
- **1677, 1887, Bondurand's claim, 1923** (I2, I4, I5): these are credited as claims, and the 1923 use does not call the book a catechism. Holds. The phrasing joins the two printings to one editor (MINOR 2).

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The separation from both sons is not in the first minute.** The younger son's removal first arrives at word 238. Before word 150 the listener hears only that she is kept from William. *Change:* name the baby's absence in the opening paragraph, using the outline's allowed wording. For example, after the Q1 sentence: "Her second son, born that March, had been brought to his father before he was baptized." Then trim the later sentence in the war paragraph so it is not told twice.

**BLOCKING 2. "Worked on it for more than a year" is missing.** The brief says when she began but never that she kept at it. *Change:* add it to the sentence that dates the start: "She began it in November 841 ... and worked on it for more than a year." The outline's Do not say list forbids "finished it in 843", so do not give a year of completion.

**BLOCKING 3. Two of the approved asks are missing: honour priests, and pray for the Church and the dead.** *Change:* add both to the "He must..." sentences, as concrete asks and not chapter titles (A2, outline beat 2). For example: "honour priests, and pray for the Church, for kings and for the dead."

**BLOCKING 4. Passing the book to his little brother arrives at word 191.** *Change:* bring the instruction to show the book to his little brother forward, inside the first 150 words. It fits naturally if BLOCKING 1 puts the second son in the opening. Bring "a noblewoman, not one of the clergy" forward with it (MINOR 5).

These four together add about 35 words to the first 150. Make room by compressing in the first minute, not by cutting any heart part. For example: "In seventy-three chapters she set out a Christian life for a young man at court" can lose "for a young man at court", since "at court" is clear from Charles. The total stays well under the 460-word allowance.

**MINOR 1.** The approved heart says "help the poor". The brief says "widows, orphans and pilgrims". Add "the poor" if it fits.

**MINOR 2.** "Printed in part in 1677 and whole in 1887, by an editor who called it..." can be heard as one editor doing both. *Change:* split the sentence so that the editor belongs to 1887 only. For example: "...printed in part in 1677, and whole in 1887. Its editor called it..."

**MINOR 3.** The line "Almost all we know of her comes from her own book..." comes between the war paragraph and "In it she writes her own epitaph". The listener has to work out that "it" means the book and not the chronicles. *Change:* say "In the book she writes her own epitaph...", or move the epitaph sentence before the "Almost all we know" line.

**MINOR 4.** "Seized Ampurias and Barcelona ... killed there" leaves open where he died. *Change:* "killed at Barcelona", as the annal and the outline have it.

**MINOR 5.** "Taught with a heart burning" (word 162) and "a noblewoman, not one of the clergy" (word 174) are just past the mark. They will fall inside it once the opening is compressed (see BLOCKING 4).
