# Source sheet: Antony the Great

Researcher's sheet for the 2026-11-17 varied batch. The writer uses the outline and the standard only; this sheet is the evidence behind the outline.

- **NP6** = `npnf206_jerome-principal-works.xml` (Jerome, *Letters*, *Life of Paul the First Hermit*, *Life of Hilarion*).
- **AUG** = `npnf101_augustine-confessions-letters.xml` (Augustine, *Confessions*, tr. Pilkington); **AUGL** = `augustine_confessiones-lat_knoll-csel33.txt` (the same, Latin, CSEL 33, OCR).
- **PAR2** = `anan-isho_paradise-v2-sayings_budge1907.txt` (*The Paradise of the Holy Fathers*, vol. II, the Sayings, tr. Budge from Syriac).

**Conventions.** Line numbers are the file's own lines. English quotes are printed exactly as in the file, words joined across line breaks. Translators' and editors' footnotes and note markers are left out of quotes; where a note matters it is given after the quote. Section numbers (§) in NP4 are the printed edition's. In the OCR files (VAG, AUGL) I joined words split across lines and left out the interleaved Latin, apparatus lines and page heads; OCR faults are left in place and corrected in [brackets]. The PG 26 Greek numbers its chapters differently from Robertson's English in places; I cite the English §.

**Time gaps.** The "about N years later" figures are my rough estimates for the card, using the usual modern dates of the writers and a death about 356 (see D5). They are not story material.

**Witness kinds used.** OWN = his own words in a document he sent (none held; see G1). CONTEMP = written in or just after his lifetime by someone who claims to have known him or his circle: the *Life* (see D1 on authorship) and Athanasius's *History of the Arians*. LATER = a writer who did not know him, with rough gap. LATE-LEGEND = collections compiled centuries later. Words the *Life* puts in Antony's mouth are its report of his words, not documents of his own; they are marked "(as the *Life* reports)".

## 1. Setting

S1. **Who and where.** "Antony you must know was by descent an Egyptian: his parents were of good family and possessed considerable wealth, and as they were Christians he also was reared in the same Faith." (VA §1, NP4 31214–31218). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. The editor's note on "wealth" adds "At Coma in Upper Egypt, see Sozom. i. 13" (NP4 31216, note 985; card only).
S2. **Village.** He lived in a village with "the next village" nearby (VA §3, NP4 31279); he gave land "to the villagers" (VA §2, NP4 31249). The *Life* never names the village. Sozomen, about 90 years later: "He was an Egyptian by race, and belonged to an illustrious family of Coma, which was situated near the Heraclea which is on the Egyptian borders." (Soz. I.13, SZ 27322–27324). LATER (~90 years). Widely Accepted for "Coma" as Sozomen's report; not in the *Life* (D4).
S3. **Language.** He spoke "in the Egyptian tongue" (VA §16, NP4 31645); to Greek philosophers he spoke "by means of an interpreter" (VA §72, NP4 32987–32988; also §74, 33018; §77, 33096–33097). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Jerome: "sent seven letters in Coptic" (NP3 41178–41179). LATER (~35 years).
S4. **Century and rulers.** The *Life* names no year. It places him by events: "the persecution which then took place under Maximinus" (VA §46, NP4 32366–32368); "the blessed Bishop Peter had borne his testimony" (VA §47, NP4 32397–32398); "Constantine Augustus, and his sons Constantius and Constans the Augusti wrote letters to him" (VA §81, NP4 33188–33189); the Arian quarrels (VA §§68–69, 82, 86, 89). Jerome: "He flourished during the reign of Constantinus and his sons." (NP3 41181–41182). That these rulers belong to the **fourth century AD** is modern standard chronology, not a held source: Widely Accepted. Plain ways to say it the sheet supports: "in Egypt, in the time of the emperor Constantine and his sons"; "in the three-hundreds". Born 251 rests only on a modern editor's note (D5): do not say he was born in the third century as a sourced fact unless as "born about 251, by the usual reckoning".
S5. **Monks before him.** "For there were not yet so many monasteries in Egypt, and no monk at all knew of the distant desert; but all who wished to give heed to themselves practised the discipline in solitude near their own village. Now there was then in the next village an old man who had lived the life of a hermit from his youth up." (VA §3, NP4 31274–31280). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. He was not the first ascetic in the *Life*'s own telling (see D3).
S6. **Tradition.** The census name is **Desert Monasticism**, informally the **Desert Fathers and Mothers** (CEN 34585, 34587). Its own summary: "Between about 320 and 430, Christians in Egypt left their villages for the desert's edge. Some lived alone, as Antony did after he gave away his inheritance." (CEN 34710). (a) Plain spoken forms: (1) "the desert monks of Egypt"; (2) "the men and women later called the Desert Fathers and Mothers". Both rest on CEN 34587, 34710. (3) The *Life*'s own picture of them: "the desert was colonised by monks, who came forth from their own people, and enrolled themselves for the citizenship in the heavens" (VA §14, NP4 31624–31627). Said aloud, render the Greek "ἡ ἔρημος ἐπολίσθη ὑπὸ μοναχῶν" (VAG 70548–70549) as "the desert became a city of monks"; "colonised" now suggests conquest. (b) Do not say "Desert Monasticism" aloud as a proper name; do not say "the first monk" (S5, D3). The census dates the movement from about 320 (CEN 34588); Antony's withdrawal begins before that in the *Life*'s chronology, so the tradition is best told as what grew up around him.
S7. **What "discipline" means here.** The *Life*'s word (Greek ἄσκησις, NP4 note 995 at 31272) for the whole way of life: work with the hands, prayer, fasting, vigil. "He worked, however, with his hands, having heard, 'he who is idle let him not eat,' and part he spent on bread and part he gave to the needy. And he was constant in prayer" (VA §3, NP4 31288–31293). "He ate once a day, after sunset, sometimes once in two days, and often even in four. His food was bread and salt, his drink, water only." (VA §7, NP4 31421–31422). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Sozomen repeats it (SZ 27340–27342). Plain gloss for a newcomer: "a life of training", like an athlete's (the *Life* calls him "the athlete", NP4 31550).

## 2. Life events (in the *Life*'s order)

E1. **Parents' death.** "After the death of his father and mother he was left alone with one little sister: his age was about eighteen or twenty, and on him the care both of home and sister rested." (VA §2, NP4 31234–31236). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.
E2. **The Gospel reading.** "Now it was not six months after the death of his parents, and going according to custom into the Lord's House, he communed with himself and reflected as he walked how the Apostles left all and followed the Saviour ... Pondering over these things he entered the church, and it happened the Gospel was being read, and he heard the Lord saying to the rich man, 'If thou wouldest be perfect, go and sell that thou hast and give to the poor; and come follow Me and thou shalt have treasure in heaven.' Antony, as though God had put him in mind of the Saints, and the passage had been read on his account, went out immediately from the church, and gave the possessions of his forefathers to the villagers—they were three hundred acres, productive and very fair—that they should be no more a clog upon himself and his sister. And all the rest that was movable he sold, and having got together much money he gave it to the poor, reserving a little however for his sister's sake." (VA §2, NP4 31236–31258). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Note: "acres" renders ἄρουραι, an Egyptian land measure, "100 Egyptian cubits square" (note 991, NP4 31249–31251; card only). Greek of the verse: "Εἰ θέλεις τέλειος εἶναι, ὕπαγε, πώλησον ... πάντα τὰ ὑπάρχοντά σοι, καὶ δὸς πτωχοῖς, καὶ δεῦρο ἀκολούθει μοι, καὶ ἔξεις θησαυρὸν ἐν οὐρανοῖς." (VAG 68768–68770; OCR "θέϊεις" [θέλεις]).
E3. **Sister placed; the last goods given.** "And again as he went into the church, hearing the Lord say in the Gospel, 'be not anxious for the morrow,' he could stay no longer, but went out and gave those things also to the poor. Having committed his sister to known and faithful virgins, and put her into a convent to be brought up, he henceforth devoted himself outside his house to discipline" (VA §3, NP4 31263–31272). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Note 994 on "convent" (Greek Παρθενών): "the earliest use of the word in this sense ... at this time virgins generally lived with their families" (NP4 31267; card only). Writer should say "trusted women who had vowed to stay unmarried", not "a convent".
E4. **Learning from others.** The old hermit in the next village: "Antony, after he had seen this man, imitated him in piety" (VA §3, NP4 31280–31281). "if he heard of a good man anywhere, like the prudent bee, he went forth and sought him" (NP4 31282–31283). "He observed the graciousness of one; the unceasing prayer of another" (VA §4, NP4 31302). "For he had given such heed to what was read that none of the things that were written fell from him to the ground, but he remembered all, and afterwards his memory served him for books." (VA §3, NP4 31294–31297). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.
E5. **Temptation.** The devil "whispering to him the remembrance of his wealth, care for his sister, claims of kindred, love of money, love of glory" (VA §5, NP4 31326–31328). WONDER/vision material, the *Life*'s voice (§§5–6, NP4 31322–31391). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.
E6. **The tombs.** "Antony departed to the tombs, which happened to be at a distance from the village; and having bid one of his acquaintances to bring him bread at intervals of many days, he entered one of the tombs, and the other having shut the door on him, he remained within alone." (VA §8, NP4 31454–31458). Demons beat him; his friend found him "lying on the ground as though dead" and carried him to the village church (NP4 31461–31469); at midnight he asked to be carried back (NP4 31470–31473). WONDER, in the *Life*'s voice: after a night of beasts, "he saw the roof as it were opened, and a ray of light descending to him" and a voice said "Antony, I was here, but I waited to see thy fight; wherefore since thou hast endured, and hast not been worsted, I will ever be a succour to thee, and will make thy name known everywhere." (VA §10, NP4 31510–31520). "And he was then about thirty-five years old." (NP4 31522–31523). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Greek of the promise: "ἔσομαί σοι ἀεὶ βοηθὸς, καὶ ποιήσω σε ὀνομαστὸν πανταχοῦ γενέσθαι" (VAG 70111–70113; OCR "χαὶ" [καὶ], "Ὑγεγέσθαι" [γενέσθαι]).
E7. **To the mountain; the fort.** The old man would not come "on account of his great age, and because as yet there was no such custom" (VA §11, NP4 31531–31532). "having found a fort, so long deserted that it was full of creeping things, on the other side of the river; he crossed over to it and dwelt there ... he built up the entrance completely, having stored up loaves for six months ... and abode within by himself, never going forth nor looking at any one who came." (VA §12, NP4 31556–31564). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.
E8. **Twenty years; the door forced.** "And so for nearly twenty years he continued training himself in solitude, never going forth, and but seldom seen by any. After this, when many were eager and wishful to imitate his discipline, and his acquaintances came and began to cast down and wrench off the door by force, Antony, as from a shrine, came forth initiated in the mysteries and filled with the Spirit of God." (VA §14, NP4 31600–31606). "he had the same habit of body as before, and was neither fat, like a man without exercise, nor lean from fasting and striving with the demons" (NP4 31608–31610). "he persuaded many to embrace the solitary life. And thus it happened in the end that cells arose even in the mountains, and the desert was colonised by monks" (NP4 31623–31625). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Greek: "καὶ ἡ ἔρημος ἐπολίσθη ὑπὸ μοναχῶν" (VAG 70548–70549; "the desert was made a city by monks"); the rest of the Greek sentence is broken in the OCR (VAG 70549–70550).
E9. **Teacher of the monks.** "by the attraction of his words cells multiplied, and he directed them all as a father." (VA §15, NP4 31637–31638). His long address (VA §§16–43, NP4 31643–32308), opening: "The Scriptures are enough for instruction, but it is a good thing to encourage one another in the faith" (NP4 31646–31647). The *Life*'s picture of the result: "So their cells were in the mountains, like filled with holy bands of men who sang psalms, loved reading, fasted, prayed, rejoiced in the hope of things to come, laboured in alms-giving, and preserved love and harmony one with another." (VA §44, NP4 32318–32321). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account; the address is the *Life*'s rendering of his teaching, not a transcript.
E10. **Alexandria in the persecution.** "After this the Church was seized by the persecution which then took place under Maximinus, and when the holy martyrs were led to Alexandria, Antony also followed, leaving his cell ... And he longed to suffer martyrdom, but not being willing to give himself up, he ministered to the confessors in the mines and in the prisons." (VA §46, NP4 32366–32372). When the judge banned monks from the court, "he washed his garment, and stood all next day on a raised place before them, and appeared in his best before the governor." (NP4 32380–32382). Then, after "the blessed Bishop Peter had borne his testimony, Antony departed, and again withdrew to his cell, and was there daily a martyr to his conscience" (VA §47, NP4 32397–32400). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Gloss: "confessors" = Christians imprisoned or sent to the mines for the faith.
E11. **The inner mountain.** Crowds and healings made him fear being "puffed up" (VA §49, NP4 32431–32435). He went with "Saracens" (desert travellers) "three days and three nights" to "a very lofty mountain, and at the foot of the mountain ran a clear spring ... and a few uncared-for palm trees." (NP4 32455–32461). He grew his own grain so as to be "troublesome to no one" (VA §50, NP4 32476–32484), and herbs for visitors "that he who came to him might have some slight solace after the labour of that hard journey" (NP4 32485–32486). "he used to weave baskets, which he gave to those who came in return for what they brought him" (VA §53, NP4 32532–32533). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. WONDER (the voice that sent him, NP4 32439–32453) in the *Life*'s voice. Jerome later describes the place as Hilarion saw it: "Those vines and shrubs were planted by his own hand: that garden bed was his own design." (*Life of Hilarion* 31, NP6 30763–30765). LATER (~35 years).
E12. **Healing he would not claim.** To an officer whose daughter was possessed: "Man, why dost thou call on me? I also am a man even as you. But if you believe on Christ whom I serve, go, and according as you believe, pray to God, and it shall come to pass." (VA §48, NP4 32418–32421). "he ... besought the sufferer to be patient, and to know that healing belonged neither to him nor to man at all, but only to the Lord" (VA §56, NP4 32626–32629). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Many wonder stories follow (§§57–66, NP4 32638–32867): all in the *Life*'s voice; leave out of the story (see Outline "Leave out").
E13. **Humble toward clergy.** "he observed the rule of the Church most rigidly, and was willing that all the clergy should be honoured above himself. For he was not ashamed to bow his head to bishops and presbyters" (VA §67, NP4 32874–32880). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.
E14. **Against the Arians in Alexandria (DEFENDED).** "And once also the Arians having lyingly asserted that Antony's opinions were the same as theirs, he was displeased and wroth against them. Then being summoned by the bishops and all the brethren, he descended from the mountain, and having entered Alexandria, he denounced the Arians, saying that their heresy was the last of all and a forerunner of Antichrist. And he taught the people that the Son of God was not a created being" (VA §69, NP4 32924–32932). "And all the people in the city ran together to see Antony" (VA §70, NP4 32952–32953). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Fest. Index X (337–8): "Antony, the great leader, came to Alexandria, and though he remained there only two days, shewed himself wonderful in many things, and healed many." (NP4 64172–64174). LATER (anonymous index; its date of compiling is not stated in the file). Theodoret: "the great Antonius had acted in the same way in Alexandria, for he abandoned the desert and went up and down that city, telling all men that Athanasius was the preacher of the true doctrine and that the Arian faction were enemies of the truth." (NP3 14968–14983). LATER (~95 years). Gloss: the Arians taught that the Son of God was created; the *Life* gives their saying "there was a time when He was not" (NP4 32935).
E15. **Philosophers.** "although he had not learned letters, he was a ready-witted and sagacious man" (VA §72, NP4 32983–32984). "Antony said to them, 'What say ye? which is first, mind or letters? And which is the cause of which—mind of letters or letters of mind?' And when they answered mind is first and the inventor of letters, Antony said, 'Whoever, therefore, hath a sound mind hath not need of letters.'" (VA §73, NP4 33000–33005). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account (as the *Life* reports his words).
E16. **The emperors' letters.** "For Constantine Augustus, and his sons Constantius and Constans the Augusti wrote letters to him, as to a father, and begged an answer from him ... But when they brought him the letters he called the monks and said, 'Do not be astonished if an emperor writes to us, for he is a man; but rather wonder that God wrote the Law for men and has spoken to us through His own Son.'" (VA §81, NP4 33188–33195). He answered, at the monks' urging, telling them "to be merciful and to give heed to justice and the poor." (NP4 33197–33205). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Greek (VAG 77100–77112): "Μὴ θαυμάζετε [OCR θχυμάξετε], εἰ γράφει βασιλεὺς πρὸς ἡμᾶς, ἄνθρωπος γάρ ἐστιν [OCR ἔστιν]· ἀλλὰ μᾶλλον θαυμάζετε, ὅτι ὁ Θεὸς τὸν νόμον ἀνθρώποις ἔγραψε, καὶ [OCR χαὶ] διὰ τοῦ ἰδίου Υἱοῦ λελάληκεν [OCR λελάληχεν] ἡμῖν." Sozomen: Constantine "sought his friendship, honored him with correspondence" (SZ 27319–27321). LATER (~90 years).
E17. **The fish.** To a commander who begged him to stay: "'Fishes, if they remain long on dry land, die. And so monks lose their strength if they loiter among you and spend their time with you. Wherefore as fish must hurry to the sea, so must we hasten to the mountain. Lest haply if we delay we forget the things within us.'" (VA §85, NP4 33352–33355). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Greek (VAG 77522–77527, 77605): "Ὥσπερ οἱ ἰχθύες ἐγχρονίζοντες τῇ ξηρᾷ γῇ τελευτῶσιν, οὕτως οἱ μοναχοὶ βραδύνοντες μεθ᾽ ὑμῶν, καὶ παρ᾽ ὑμῖν ἐκδιατρίβοντες [OCR ἐχδιατρίδοντες] ἐκλύονται. Δεῖ οὖν, ὥσπερ τὸν ἰχθὺν εἰς τὴν θάλασσαν, οὕτως ἡμᾶς [OCR ἡγᾶς] εἰς τὸ ὄρος ἐπείγεσθαι [OCR ἐπείγεσθας]· μήποτε ἐμβραδύνοντες [OCR ἐμδραδύνοντες], ... ἐπιλαθώμεθα τῶν ἔνδον [OCR ἔνζην]." The last clause sits in a separate column (VAG 77605) after interleaved Latin; I read it as the continuation. Sozomen gives the same saying in other words (SZ 27382–27384). LATER.
E18. **A judges' and the poor's advocate.** "he was of profit to the judges, counselling them to prefer justice to all things" (VA §84, NP4 33335–33336). "he championed those who were wronged in such a way that you would imagine that he, and not the others, was the sufferer." (VA §87, NP4 33400–33401). "many soldiers and men who had great possessions laid aside the burdens of life, and became monks" (NP4 33402–33404). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Sozomen: "He above all others came forward spiritedly and most zealously for the defense of the injured" (SZ 27373–27375). LATER.
E19. **Last visit and charge.** "for I am near a hundred and five years old." (VA §89, NP4 33446–33447). He told the monks "to live as though dying daily ... to have nought to do with the Meletian schismatics ... Nor have any fellowship with the Arians" (NP4 33450–33455). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account. Gloss: the Meletians were a group that had broken from the bishop of Alexandria.
E20. **Burial against the custom.** The Egyptians kept the bodies of holy dead "on couches, and to keep them in their houses" (VA §90, NP4 33467–33468); he taught against it (NP4 33469–33480). He went back to the inner mountain so his body would not be kept so (VA §91, NP4 33485–33488). To his two companions: "Bury my body, therefore, and hide it underground yourselves, and let my words be observed by you that no one may know the place but you alone." (NP4 33511–33520). "To Athanasius the bishop give one sheepskin and the garment whereon I am laid, which he himself gave me new, but which with me has grown old. To Serapion the bishop give the other sheepskin, and keep the hair garment yourselves." (NP4 33522–33525). "For the rest fare ye well, my children, for Antony is departing, and is with you no more." (NP4 33531–33533). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.
E21. **Death.** "he lifted up his feet, and as though he saw friends coming to him and was glad because of them—for as he lay his countenance appeared joyful—he died and was gathered to the fathers ... And no one knows to this day where it was buried, save those two only." (VA §92, NP4 33539–33544). "each of those who received the sheepskin of the blessed Antony and the garment worn by him guards it as a precious treasure." (NP4 33544–33546). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.

## 3. Impact as the record gives it (heaviest first)

I1. **The *Life* itself became the pattern for monks, and it travelled.** (a) The *Life*'s own purpose: "for monks the life of Antony is a sufficient pattern of discipline." (Prologue, NP4 31184–31185); "Read these words, therefore, to the rest of the brethren that they may learn what the life of monks ought to be" (VA §94, NP4 33583–33584). CONTEMP. Documented. (b) Its reach, as it claims: "For from whence into Spain and into Gaul, how into Rome and Africa, was the man heard of who abode hidden in a mountain, unless it was God who maketh His own known everywhere, who also promised this to Antony at the beginning?" (VA §93, NP4 33571–33575). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s claim. (c) Latin within about twenty years: Evagrius of Antioch translated it; Robertson dates the dedication before Innocent's death in 374 (VA intro, NP4 30510–30516, 30525–30528; editor, card only). Evagrius's own preface, as Jerome quotes it: "I have tried to avoid this error in the translation which at your request I have made of the story of the blessed Antony. My version always preserves the sense although it does not invariably keep the words of the original." (Jerome, Letter 57, NP6 13399–13403). LATER (~15–20 years). Documented. (d) Gregory of Nazianzus (380, per Robertson): Athanasius wrote "τοῦ μοναδικοῦ βίου νομοθεσίαν ἐν πλάσματι διηγήσεως" (VA intro, NP4 30553–30559) — "a law for the monastic life in the form of a story". LATER (~25 years). Documented as Robertson quotes it (Gregory's own text not held). (e) Robertson's judgment (1892, card only): "Upon it Jerome modelled his highly idealised tales of Paul and Hilarion; at Rome and all over the West it kindled the flame of monastic aspirations" (NP4 30942–30944).
I2. **Augustine: the book at Trier and the reading at Milan.** Pontitianus, an official at the emperor's court, told Augustine and Alypius of "Antony, the Egyptian monk, whose name was in high repute among Thy servants, though up to that time not familiar to us" (Conf. VIII.6.14, AUG 12500–12562). At Trier two of his fellow officials found a cottage of monks "where they found a book in which was written the life of Antony. This one of them began to read, marvel at, and be inflamed by it; and in the reading, to meditate on embracing such a life, and giving up his worldly employments to serve Thee." (VIII.6.15, AUG 12612–12616). Both left the court: "Now have I broken loose from those hopes of ours, and am determined to serve God" (AUG 12643–12644). Then Augustine in the garden: "For I had heard of Antony, that, accidentally coming in whilst the gospel was being read, he received the admonition as if what was read were addressed to him, 'Go and sell that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven; and come and follow me.' And by such oracle was he forthwith converted unto Thee." (VIII.12.29, AUG 13117–13124). LATER (Augustine writing ~40 years after Antony's death; the events in Milan ~30 years after). Documented as Augustine's own account. Latin (AUGL 13470–13501): "audieram enim de Antonio, quod ex euangelica lectione, cui forte superuenerat, admonitus fuerit, tamquam sibi diceretur quod legebatur: uade, uende omnia, quae habes, da pauperibus et habebis thesaurum in caelis; et ueni, sequere me, et tali oraculo confestim ad te esse conuersum." (The verse breaks across a page at AUGL 13476/13501; apparatus lines between are left out.) Latin for Trier: "uenisse ibi codicem, in quo scripta erat uita Antonii" (AUGL 12787).
I3. **The desert filled; disciples far and wide.** (a) "the desert was colonised by monks" (E8, NP4 31625). CONTEMP. (b) Sozomen: "He had many renowned disciples, of whom some flourished in Egypt and Libya, others in Palestine, Syria, and Arabia" (Soz. I.13, SZ 27392–27394). LATER (~90 years). Widely Accepted as his report. (c) Jerome on Hilarion of Palestine: "At that time he heard of the famous name of Antony, which was in the mouth of all the races of Egypt. He was fired with a desire to see him, and set out for the desert. He no sooner saw him than he changed his former mode of life and abode with him about two months" (*Life of Hilarion* 3, NP6 30141–30145); he went home deciding "to begin as Antony had begun" (NP6 30153). Years later Hilarion kept "the anniversary of the blessed Antony's decease ... in the very place where the saint had died" (NP6 30746–30748). LATER (~35 years); Robertson calls Jerome's tale "highly idealised" (NP4 30942–30943). (d) Jerome, Letter 22: anchorites "who go from the monasteries into the deserts, with nothing but bread and salt. Paul introduced this way of life; Antony made it famous" (NP6 6724–6727). LATER. (e) Socrates: Athanasius "has anticipated us, having devoted an entire book to his biography" (Soc. I.21, SZ 4511–4512) — later historians sent readers to the *Life* rather than retell it. LATER (~85 years).
I4. **DEFENDED: the faith of Nicaea against the Arians, and his bishop.** E14 above; the deathbed charges (VA §89, NP4 33453–33455; VA §91, NP4 33500–33503: "Have no fellowship with the schismatics, nor any dealings at all with the heretical Arians."). Athanasius in his own *History of the Arians*: "Whenever, therefore, our Father Antony wrote to him from the mountains, as godliness is an abomination to a sinner, so he abhorred the letters of the holy man." (Hist. Ar. 14, of Gregory, the bishop set up in Athanasius's place; NP4 38211–38213). CONTEMP; Athanasius's undisputed work. Documented that Antony wrote to Alexandria's rival bishop.
I5. **Sayings carried on.** The Syriac *Paradise* collects sayings under his name, e.g. "453. Abbâ Anthony said, 'I saw all the snares of the Enemy laid out upon the ground, and I groaned and said, "Who can escape from these?"' And the devils said unto me, 'Humility "maketh a man to escape from these, for we cannot attain unto it."'" (PAR2 978). LATE-LEGEND (compiled centuries later; card only). Socrates, quoting Evagrius: "'My book, O philosopher,' replied Anthony, 'is the nature of things that are made, and it is present whenever I wish to read the words of God.'" (Soc. IV.23, SZ 13569–13571). LATER (Evagrius wrote ~40 years after). Card only.
I6. **His letters.** Jerome: "sent seven letters in Coptic to various monasteries, letters truly apostolic in idea and language, and which have been translated into Greek. The chief of these is To the Arsenoites." (NP3 41178–41181). LATER. Documented that Jerome knew of them. Not held (G1).
I7. **The keepsakes.** The two sheepskins guarded "as a precious treasure. For even to look on them is as it were to behold Antony" (VA §92, NP4 33545–33547). CONTEMP. Documented as the *Life*'s account.

## 4. Links to other people

L1. **Athanasius of Alexandria — wrote his life (traditional ascription, D1); received his sheepskin; claims to have seen him "many times".** Prologue: "what I myself know, having seen him many times, and what I was able to learn from him, for I was his attendant for a long time, and poured water on his hands" (NP4 31197–31200; text uncertain, D1). Bequest (NP4 33522–33524). Jerome: "Anthony the monk, whose life Athanasius bishop of Alexandria wrote a long work upon" (NP3 41177–41178). Theodoret: Antony told Alexandria "that Athanasius was the preacher of the true doctrine" (NP3 14982–14983).
L2. **Serapion, bishop — met; received his other sheepskin.** "he saw what was happening in Egypt, and told it to Serapion the bishop, who was indoors with him" (VA §82, NP4 33220–33223); bequest (NP4 33524).
L3. **The old hermit of the next village — learned from.** Unnamed (VA §3, §11; NP4 31279–31281, 31529–31532).
L4. **His sister — unnamed.** "his sister grown old in virginity, and that she herself also was the leader of other virgins." (VA §54, NP4 32567–32569).
L5. **Amun of Nitria — the *Life* says Antony saw his soul taken up** (WONDER; VA §60, NP4 32706–32707); Socrates repeats it (SZ 13443–13449).
L6. **Peter, bishop of Alexandria — martyr in the same persecution** (VA §47, NP4 32397–32398). Time marker only.
L7. **Constantine, Constantius, Constans — wrote to him** (E16).
L8. **Gregory (bishop put in at Alexandria) and Balacius the duke — he wrote to them** (Hist. Ar. 14, NP4 38211–38230; VA §86, NP4 33364–33392; D2).
L9. **Didymus the Blind — met.** Socrates (SZ 13840–13847) and Jerome, Letter 68 (NP6 15804–15816). LATER. D6.
L10. **Hilarion — visited and learned from him** (Jerome, I3c). **Isaac and Pelusianus** — "the former of whom had been one of Antony's attendants" (NP6 30750–30751).
L11. **Paul of Thebes — Jerome's *Life of Paul* has Antony visit and bury him** (NP6 29741–30040). LATER; D3. Not for the story.

L14. **Evagrius of Antioch — translated his *Life* into Latin** (I1c).

## 5. Where sources disagree, or are later than the life

D1. **Who wrote the *Life*, and whether the writer served him.** The heading names "Athanasius, Bishop of Alexandria" (NP4 31149–31152). Robertson (1892): "the question being still in dispute, although the balance of qualified opinion is on the side of the Athanasian authorship" (VA intro, NP4 30432–30434), and "the external evidence for the Vita is too strong to allow us to set it aside as spurious" (NP4 30447–30449). Editor's dating heading: "(Written between 356 and 362)" (NP4 30421–30422). On "I was his attendant ... and poured water on his hands", note 984: "The text is uncertain, as some mss., both Greek and Latin read, 'was able to learn from him who was his attendant'" (NP4 31201–31204). Robertson also grants: "there is idealisation, panegyric, an absence of sinfulness" (NP4 30919). Authorship by Athanasius: Widely Accepted (still noted as disputed in the held edition). That the writer personally served Antony: Contested (textual variant). Safe spoken line: "a Life written soon after his death, which the church has long credited to Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria."
D2. **Balacius and the letter.** *Life*: Balacius himself "laughed and threw the letter on the ground, and spit on it" (VA §86, NP4 33374). Athanasius, Hist. Ar. 14: Gregory "caused Duke Balacius to spit upon the letter" (NP4 38220–38221). The editor's note: "The incident is related differently in Vit. Ant." (NP4 38228–38229). Robertson lists Balacius among the *Life*'s "historical difficulties" (NP4 30894–30895). Contested in detail; leave out.
D3. **Was he the first hermit?** The *Life* itself shows hermits before him near villages (S5) and says only that "no monk at all knew of the distant desert" (NP4 31276–31277). Jerome, *Life of Paul*: "Others, and their opinion is that commonly received, maintain that Antony was the originator of this mode of life, which view is partly true. Partly I say, for the fact is not so much that he preceded the rest as that they all derived from him the necessary stimulus." (NP6 29746–29749); Jerome backs Paul of Thebes as first (NP6 29752–29753). Contested. Story must not call him "the first monk"; it may say others "derived from him" their start, as Jerome puts it, or use the *Life*'s "the desert was colonised by monks".
D4. **Birthplace.** "Coma, near Heraclea" is Sozomen's (S2), about 90 years later; the *Life* names no village. Widely Accepted as a report; say "Sozomen, writing much later" if used.
D5. **Dates.** "Born 251, died 356." is Richardson's 1892 note to Jerome (NP3 41177, note 2513). The *Life* gives his age near death, "near a hundred and five" (NP4 33446), and no year. NP6's chronological table: "356. Death of Antony." (NP6 2608; editor). Dominant Modern Reconstruction for 251–356. Spoken: "he died at about a hundred and five" (the *Life*'s words) is safer than any year.
D6. **The Didymus saying** differs between witnesses: Socrates has "gnats and flies" and Antony coming "on account of the Arians" (SZ 13840–13847); Jerome has "ants and flies and gnats" and says "Athanasius bishop of Alexandria had summoned the blessed Antony" (NP6 15804–15815). LATER, both. Card only.
D7. **Could he read and write?** The *Life*: "he could not endure to learn letters" (NP4 31221–31222); "he had not learned letters" (NP4 32983, 32999–33000); yet "he wrote an answer" to the emperors (NP4 33200) and "wrote a letter" to Balacius (NP4 33368). Jerome: seven letters in Coptic (NP3 41178–41179). The *Life* does not say whether he wrote with his own hand or dictated. Contested. Story should not say he was illiterate as a flat fact; it may say, with the *Life*, that he "had not learned letters" (schooling) and spoke Egyptian, needing an interpreter for Greek.
D8. **Wonders.** Visions, demons, healings and foreknowledge fill much of the *Life* (§§5–13, 48–66, 82, 86). The *Life* itself says "we ought not to doubt whether such marvels were wrought" (NP4 33308–33309); Robertson notes "the moderate value set on miracles (38, 56)" (NP4 30920–30921). All stay in the *Life*'s voice.
D9. **Later voices on his youth.** Sozomen's summary (SZ 27329–27348) follows the *Life* closely; it is not an independent witness.

## 6. Gaps

G1. **No document of his own is held.** Jerome's seven Coptic letters (NP3 41178–41181) are not in the Library (the census card says the same: "none is held in this Library", CEN 88336). Every word of his is reported by another writer.
G2. The *Life of Pachomius*, which Robertson says "has details of Antony's life independent of the Vita" (NP4 30574–30575), is not held.
G3. The Greek of the *Life* is held only as Migne OCR with interleaved Latin (VAG); several passages are broken. Renderings from Greek below are made from the lines cited; where the OCR fails I say so.
G4. The *Index to the Festal Letters* gives the 337–8 Alexandria visit, but its compiler and date are not in the file.
