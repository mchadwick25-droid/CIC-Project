## 1. The test, from one hearing

- **Who or what:** Antony, a young Christian in a village in Egypt in the three-hundreds, who became a desert monk and a father to the many monks who came after him.
- **What happened:** He heard one Gospel reading as spoken to him and gave his land away. He went ever further into the desert and lived walled in a fort for nearly twenty years. Friends forced the door, and he guided the many who wanted his life until the desert became a city of monks. Later he moved to a mountain, and he came down to Alexandria to oppose the Arians.
- **Why the connection matters:** A book about him, the *Life*, carried his way of life abroad. At Trier it turned two court officials from the court. Through their colleague Pontitianus the story reached Augustine in Milan. Augustine remembered Antony's Gospel reading, took up a reading of his own, and his doubt was gone. Augustine never met Antony. The book and a retold story passed the same act on to him.

None of the three lines is blank or wrong.

**My heart, written before reading `Hearts.md`:** A young Egyptian took one Gospel reading as meant for him, gave everything away, and went into the desert to live by prayer, work and fasting. He drew many after him and guided them as a father, so the desert filled with monks. The *Life* written about him made his way the pattern for monks far from Egypt, and it reached as far as Trier and Augustine. This matches Mark's approved heart in substance. Mark's heart also adds the spread of his disciples, reported by Sozomen.

## Findings

**BLOCKING 1. The book's reach and the spread east arrive after the first minute.** Mark's approved heart puts four things in the first minute: the *Life* sent abroad as a pattern, the Latin version, the lives it changed at Trier and in Milan, and the disciples in Palestine, Syria and Arabia. In the brief, "Latin" arrives at about word 188. Sozomen's spread arrives at about 270–283, Trier at 289, and Augustine in Milan at about 330. Nothing in the first minute points ahead to Trier or Augustine. Even the outline's own beat 3 asked for a pointer to Augustine.

*What to change:* Tighten paragraphs 1 and 2, which run to word 128 against the outline's budget of about 90. Then, by about word 150, carry the rest of the heart:
- the source line;
- "carried him abroad" as a pattern for monks in other lands, soon put into Latin;
- one sentence that its story turned two court officials at Trier and, through them, reached Augustine in Milan;
- one clause on the disciples in Egypt, Libya, Palestine, Syria and Arabia, credited to "a historian writing nearly a century later".

Gregory of Nazianzus is not part of Mark's heart. His sentence can move after the first minute to make room. Keep the full Trier, Pontitianus and Augustine chain and the Q3 ending where they are. That also cures the back-and-forth order in paragraph 4. Length stays under about 460.

*Do not say line to add to the outline:* "(review, 2026-11-18) Do not hold the book's reach until after the first minute. By about word 150, say it was soon in Latin, name Trier and Augustine in Milan (one sentence pointing ahead), and give the spread to Palestine, Syria and Arabia with its historian. Tell the full Trier–Augustine chain later. Beats 1–2 stay within about 90 words."

**MINOR 1. "Written in the middle of the three-hundreds" rests on an editor's dating (356–362, D1), which the outline does not turn into a phrase.** It is not wrong, and it brings the time in early. If kept, "soon after his death, in the three-hundreds" stays closer to the outline.

**MINOR 2. "Arians" is not glossed.** A clause would help a second-language listener, for example "who taught that the Son of God was created" (the outline's own gloss under beat 7). Gregory of Nazianzus could likewise be placed with a few words, such as "a bishop and preacher".

**MINOR 3. The 31-word sentence in paragraph 2** ("He went ever further ... nearly twenty years") sits at the edge of the "about 30" limit. It can split after "fasting" while the paragraph is being tightened for Blocking 1.
