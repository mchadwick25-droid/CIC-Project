## 1. The heart, my own account

The church was split over the Son. Arius, a presbyter of Alexandria, said "the Son has a beginning, but God is without beginning" (D-3, his own letter). The council's letter charged him with saying "there was a time when he was not" and that the Son is "a creature" (D-4). Bishop Alexander put him out. Constantine called bishops "from all quarters" to Nicaea in 325 (S4; year Widely Accepted). The council set out a creed: the Son is "God of God, Light of Light", "begotten, not made", "of one substance with the Father", with a curse on anyone who said there was a time when he was not (M8, the council's own text). It condemned Arius with Theonas and Secundus (M10). The emperor exiled Arius (M11, Socrates; Widely Accepted) and backed the sentence by force (H12). It also settled Melitius in Egypt, set one Easter and made twenty canons on church order (M12–M14). What it changed: Constantinople and Chalcedon, meeting over newer heresies, set its creed first (I1, B1). But it did not hold at once: recalls, and Rimini in 359, where "the whole world ... found itself Arian" (I3). Its canons travelled as written law, read aloud at Carthage and sworn to in Persia (I4).

**Against the outline.** The outline's heart (line 5) says the same in substance and carries every major part: the stake in two voices kept apart, the call, the creed, the condemnation and exile, the other business, the creed set first, the decades of division, the canons as law. It matches Mark's approved heart in `Hearts.md` line 44. Beats 1–2 put the stake, the decision and what it changed in the first minute, which answers the Heart_Test finding. The Arius opening stays. The spine is the question of the Son. Easter comes in the first minute only as "it also settled".

## 4. Findings

**MINOR 1. The spine-order labels say Arius is in beats 3–7; he is in beat 1.** Line 11 says beats 3–7 follow "Mark's spine order: what called it, Arius, the debate, the creed, then the other business". Line 94 says they "follow this note's order exactly". No beat in 3–7 is about Arius: he opens the story in beat 1. The order is sound, since Mark's note says to open on the major argument. Only the labels mislead a writer looking for an Arius beat.
- Correction, line 11: "beats 3–7 deepen it in Mark's spine order, with Arius already told in beat 1: what called it (beats 3–4), the debate (5), the creed and its condemnation (6), then the other business (7)".
- Correction, line 94: "after it, beats 3–7 follow this note's order, with Arius already told in beat 1."

**MINOR 2. Q3 rendering: "We kept peace before our eyes as our aim".** The Greek is a genitive absolute with a present participle: τοῦ τῆς εἰρήνης σκοποῦ πρὸ ὀφθαλμῶν ἡμῖν κειμένου, "the aim of peace lying before our eyes". It is concurrent with the present συντιθέμεθα ("we agree"). "We kept" shifts it into the past and adds an act of keeping that is not in the clause.
- Correction: **"So we too agree with this meaning. We do not refuse even the word 'of one being'. Our aim, set before our eyes, is peace, and not to fall from the right meaning."**
- Q3 is a voiced `modern_rendering`, so the new wording is for Opus to confirm under the rendering rule.
