## 1. The heart, before hearing the story

This is a council, so the heart is what was at stake, what was decided, and what it changed.

The church was split over Jesus: Arius said the Son had a beginning and God had none, so there was a time when the Son was not. The emperor Constantine called bishops from across the empire to Nicaea in 325. They set out a creed calling the Son "begotten, not made", of one being with the Father, and condemned Arius; they also fixed one day for Easter, settled the Melitian split in Egypt and made twenty canons. Later councils set its creed first as the faith that "shall not be set aside", though for decades its key word divided the church.

What the sources hold for each part:

- **At stake.** Arius's own letter, written before the council: "the Son has a beginning" (sheet D-3, Documented). The council's letter charges him with saying "there was a time when he was not" and that the Son is a creature (D-4, Documented as the council's charge). The two voices differ, and the sheet says to keep them apart.
- **Who called it, where, when.** Constantine called it (S4, K3, Documented). Nicaea, 325 (S1, S2: year Widely Accepted, exact days Contested). Number of bishops Contested (C1); "more than 250" is the eyewitness floor.
- **What was decided.** The creed's words and its curse (M8, Documented, from the council's own text). Arius and two bishops condemned (M10, Documented); how many refused, Contested. Arius's exile (M11, Widely Accepted, from a later historian). One Easter, Melitius, twenty canons (M12, M13, M14, Documented).
- **What it changed.** Constantinople 381, "shall not be set aside", and Chalcedon 451, "pre-eminent" (I1, Documented). It did not hold at once and divided the church for decades: recalls, Jerome on Rimini (I3, Documented). Its canons were read as law at Carthage (I4, Documented) and accepted in Persia (I4, Widely Accepted, through Braun).

Nothing central is missing. The outline's Thread line (Outline line 7) and its "Order and spine" note (Mark, 2026-11-17) both name this heart. One small point: the live text states the stake as "whether the Son is eternal like the Father". Mark's spine note puts it as whether the Son is God as the Father is, or a creature with a beginning. The sheet supports both; the narrower form is not wrong.

A listener at the one-minute mark knows the question and who met. They do not yet know what the council said, or that its answer became the standard later councils kept.
