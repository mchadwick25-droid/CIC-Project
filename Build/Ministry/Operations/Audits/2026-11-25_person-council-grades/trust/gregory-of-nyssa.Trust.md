## Sentence by sentence

**S1.** "In 378 the emperor Valens, who had driven bishops loyal to Nicaea into exile, was killed fighting the Goths." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- SOC 14620-14648 (IV.38): Valens marched against "the barbarians ... Having at that place again engaged the enemy ... he lost his life on the 9th of August", consulate dated 378 in the edition.
- SOC 12742-12746 (IV.11): Valens "did not desist from their relentless persecution of those who dissented from them in matters of faith ... many of the clerical body were sent into exile". SOC 14412-14417 (IV.33) names the barbarians across the Danube as "the Goths".

**S2.** "The new emperor Gratian called the exiles home." **HELD.**
- SOC 14760-14763 (V.2): "Gratian ... condemning the cruel policy of his uncle Valens towards the [orthodox] Christians, recalled those whom he had sent into exile."

**S3.** "One of them was Gregory of Nyssa, a bishop of Cappadocian Christianity, in Asia Minor." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- MAC 214, Gregory himself: "first my exile at the hands of the Emperor Valens on account of the faith". With SOC V.2 (S2), Valens's exiles are the ones Gratian recalled, so Gregory is among them. NPNF5 47102-47149 (Letter III to Ablabius) tells a return home to his people with "tears in abundance with their joy"; the dating of that letter to 378 is the editor's (note 2175), so it is used only as support, not as the witness.
- SOC 15173-15175 (V.8): "Helladius, the successor of Basil in the bishopric of Cæsarea in Cappadocia ... in conjunction with Gregory Basil's brother, bishop of Nyssa". "Cappadocian Christianity" is the tree's own tradition label.

**S4.** "His brother Basil of Caesarea wrote that Gregory had been made bishop of Nyssa by force." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- BAS 39566-39569 (Ep. 225): "the responsibility lies with the consecrators and not with him who is forcibly compelled to undertake the ministry." Ep. 225 does not name Nyssa; SOC V.8 (S3) and BAS 40868 (Ep. 237, "a second synod ... to be assembled at Nyssa") give the see. Greek not checkable (PG 32 not vendored).

**S5.** "When an imperial official, a friend of heretics in Basil's words, had him summoned on charges about church money and his ordination, Basil answered that any fault lay with those who ordained him." **HELD.**
- BAS 39520, 39545-39547 (Ep. 225, "To Demosthenes, Vicar of Pontus"): "your excellency, moved by the reproaches of Philochares, ordered my brother and fellow-minister Gregory to be haled before your judgment seat."
- BAS 39557-39569: "If there is any question of the wasteful expenditure of money, the treasurers of the Church funds are there ... If there is any other point of canonical order ... the responsibility lies with the consecrators".
- BAS 40849 (Ep. 237, of the same Vicar): "But he is certainly a friend of heretics".

**S6.** "After Basil died, Gregory wrote, he 'inherited the controversy with Eunomius.'" **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- PG45 16504-16505 (Letter to Peter, before Against Eunomius): "κατ᾽ αὐτὴν τοῦ ἁγίου Βασιλείου τὴν κοίμησιν τὸν τοῦ Εὐνομίου λόγον ὑπεδεξάμην" ("at the very falling asleep of holy Basil I took up Eunomius's treatise"). NPNF5 2787-2788 renders it "I received the legacy of Eunomius' controversy".
- PG45 17551-17554 (Against Eunomius I): "προσήκειν ἐμοί φημι τοῦ μεταστάντος τὸν κλῆρον ... οἰκειοῦμαι τὴν κληρονομίαν τοῦ λόγου" ("the inheritance of the departed belongs to me ... I claim as my own the inheritance of the argument").
- The quoted words join these two lines. "Inherited" is Gregory's own word (κληρονομία); the first line alone says only "took up". Meaning and force intact.

**S7.** "Eunomius held, as Gregory reports him, that being unbegotten was the Father's very essence, so the Son was unlike Him in being." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- NPNF5 8416-8417 (Against Eunomius I), quoting Eunomius "verbatim": "We say, or rather the Ungeneracy is His actual being".
- NPNF5 4798-4799: "they link on a conclusion ... namely, that the Son is alien in nature, unlike in being to the Father". NPNF5 12775-12777: they "apply the opposition of 'generate' and 'ungenerate' to the essence of the Father and the Son".

**S8.** "Gregory answered that the Deity 'is above every name.'" **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- NPNF5 8601-8607 (Against Eunomius): God is "incapable of being grasped by any term ... having but one name that can represent His proper nature, the single name of being Above every name". NPNF5 20984-20985: "He Who verily is is above every name." PG45 59626: "ὁ ὑπὲρ πᾶν ὄνομα".
- The exact wording "the Deity" matches To Ablabius rather than Against Eunomius: PG45 9023-9028, "ὑπὲρ πᾶν ὄνομά φαμεν εἶναι τὸ θεῖον". As an answer to Eunomius, the Against Eunomius lines hold it.

**S9.** "He also wrote that the Holy Spirit is 'of the same rank as the Father and the Son.'" **HELD.**
- PG45 94164-94165 (Against the Macedonians): "Ἡμῶν γὰρ συντετάχθαι τῷ Πατρὶ καὶ τῷ Υἱῷ τὸ Πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον ὁμολογούντων". NPNF5 27943-27944: "We ... confess that the Holy Spirit is of the same rank as the Father and the Son".

**S10.** "In 381 an imperial law handed the churches to bishops who confessed one majesty in Father, Son and Spirit." **HELD.**
- CTH 6887 (16.1.3): "episcopis tradi omnes ecclesias mox iubemus, qui unius maiestatis adque virtutis patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum confitentur ... dat. iii kal. aug. heracleae eucherio et syagrio conss. (381 iul. 30)."

**S11.** "It named Gregory as one whose communion marked them, though it gives no reason." **HELD.**
- CTH 6887: "in pontica dioecesi helladio episcopo caesariensi et otreio meliteno et gregorio episcopo nysseno ... communicare constiterit. hos ad optinendas catholicas ecclesias ... oportebit admitti". The law names the bishops and gives no reason for choosing them.

**S12.** "In the Great Catechism he wrote that we were made able to change, so we can keep moving toward the good without end." **SOFTENED OR CHANGED. BLOCKING.**
- PG45 3994-3996 (Great Catechism 21): "Τρεπτῆς δὲ φύσεως ὢν κατ᾽ ἀνάγκην· οὐ γὰρ ἐνεδέχετο τὸν ἐξ ἀλλοιώσεως τὴν ἀρχὴν τοῦ εἶναι ἔχοντα, μὴ τρεπτὸν εἶναι πάντως" ("being of necessity of a changeable nature, for one whose being began from an alteration could not fail to be changeable").
- PG45 4054-4060: "δύο λέγει τῆς τοιαύτης εἴδη κινήσεως· τὸ μὲν πρὸς τὸ ἀγαθὸν ἀεὶ γιγνόμενον, ἐν ᾧ πρόοδος στάσιν οὐκ ἔχει ... Τὸ [δὲ] ..." (two kinds of movement: one always toward the good, where the advance has no halt, the other toward the contrary). NPNF5 (42016+2028 to 2061) goes on: "it is a matter of risk whether we happen to choose the real beauty", and this is how the fall came.
- The brief: "we were made able to change, so we can keep moving toward the good without end." Gregory makes change a necessity of being created, not an ability given for progress, and it runs both ways. The "so" adds a purpose he does not give, and the half that explains the fall is dropped.
- Change: tell it as he gives it, e.g. "In the Great Catechism he wrote that anything created must change, and its change runs one of two ways: toward the good, where the advance never halts, or away from it." Or cut.

**S13.** "He looks there to a day when those now lying in sin are restored, after long and painful cleansing, and all creation gives thanks." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- PG45 4798-4806 (Great Catechism 26): "ταῖς μακραῖς περιόδοις ἐξαιρεθέντος τοῦ κακοῦ ... ἐπειδὰν ἡ εἰς τὸ ἀρχαῖον ἀποκατάστασις τῶν νῦν ἐν κακίᾳ κειμένων γένηται, ὁμόφωνος εὐχαριστία παρὰ πάσης ἔσται τῆς κτίσεως, καὶ τῶν ἐν τῇ καθάρσει κεκολασμένων". PG45 4812-4813: "ἡ τοῦ νοσήματος κάθαρσις, κἂν ἐπίπονος ᾖ".
- "Long" (μακραῖς περιόδοις) and "painful" (ἐπίπονος; those "punished in the cleansing") both stand in the text.

**S14.** "Christ, he writes, frees humanity from evil and heals even the devil, who brought evil in." **HELD.**
- PG45 4807-4812 (Great Catechism 26): the incarnate one, having passed through birth, growth and death, "τόν τε ἄνθρωπον τῆς κακίας ἐλευθερῶν, καὶ αὐτὸν τὸν τῆς κακίας εὑρετὴν ἰώμενος". NPNF5 (42016+2408 to 2410): "freeing both man from evil, and healing even the introducer of evil himself". The same chapters call him "the adversary" and "the enemy", and chapter 21 "the great advocate and inventor of vice".

**S15.** "Gregory also wrote the life of his sister Macrina, and a dialogue on the soul and the resurrection in which she, near death, answers his grief." **HELD.**
- MAC 214: "my great sister said"; the Life is told by Gregory in the first person throughout.
- NPNF5 38087-38099 (On the Soul and the Resurrection): "Basil ... had departed ... his sister the Teacher was still living ... she too was lying in a state of prostration even unto death ... she tried to check me by speaking, and to correct with the curb of her reasonings the disorder of my soul."

**S16.** "In 553 the emperor Justinian, writing to a council at Constantinople, named him among the holy Fathers whose writings on the true faith he followed." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- NPNF14 23062-23071, 23135-23138: "The Emperor Flavius Justinian ... to the most blessed bishops and patriarchs ... stopping in this royal city ... We further declare that we hold fast to the decrees of the four Councils, and in every way follow the holy Fathers, Athanasius, Hilary, Basil, Gregory the Theologian, Gregory of Nyssa ... and their writings on the true faith."
- This English is "the letter condensed, including Hefele's digest" (NPNF14 23071-23073). The list of Fathers is the letter's content, not Hefele's opinion, but the letter's own text is not vendored. A second reader should check it against Mansi IX if it is added.

**S17.** "In 692 the council in Trullo set its seal on his canons, making them church law." **HELD.**
- NPNF14 26731-26747 (Trullo, canon 2): "But we set our seal likewise upon all the other holy canons set forth by our holy and blessed Fathers ... of Gregory, Bishop of Nyssa". NPNF14 26756-26762: "no one be allowed to transgress or disregard the aforesaid canons ... he shall be subject to receive the penalty which that canon imposes". The date 692 is Widely Accepted (NPNF14 930, 1602).

## The heart

- The sources show the right material for a teacher or thinker: Gregory took up Basil's argument against Eunomius as his heir (Letter to Peter; Against Eunomius I), taught that God is above every name and that the Spirit ranks with the Father and the Son, and was named in the law of 381 as a bishop whose communion marked the true faith (CTh 16.1.3). The Great Catechism's hope of restoration, the dialogue with Macrina, and Justinian's and Trullo's later use of him are what came of him. The brief holds all of this.
- The essence is never said plainly: what he did, what it changed, and why he is on the tree. The first 150 words are the exile, the recall, the forced ordination and the charges of 375. Eunomius comes in only at about word 85, as a quote, and the stakes of the quarrel are never stated.
- The order goes back. The recall of 378 (S2, S3) is told before the ordination dispute of about 375 (S4, S5), which the kit's "never the ending and then back" forbids.
- At 314 words the brief is just over the person kind's two-minute floor, and the record is not too thin to carry the aim of about 450.

**Verdict: BLOCKING.** The heart should be Gregory taking up Basil's quarrel with Eunomius, and what he answered (God beyond every name, the Spirit of the same rank), with the law of 381 naming him. It should be said plainly by about word 150, and the life told once, in order.
