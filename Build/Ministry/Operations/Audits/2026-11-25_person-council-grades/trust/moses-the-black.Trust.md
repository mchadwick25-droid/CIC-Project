## Sentence by sentence

**S1.** "Around the year 400, the desert fathers of Egypt counted Moses among their great ones." **MODERN ONLY. BLOCKING.**
- The counting holds: LH 337, "he too was numbered among the great ones of the fathers."
- The year does not. No old source gives a year for Moses. Palladius came to Alexandria "in the second consulate of the great Emperor Theodosius" (LH 197; "I. e. 388" is Clarke's note, LH 713), but he does not say he met Moses, and he writes after Moses's death. The outline itself rates this time "the researcher's inference (Inferential-Thin)". Part 1 keeps such a date out of the story.
- The brief already places the witness in time in S3 ("a bishop writing around 420"), so nothing is lost by cutting the year.
- Change: "The desert fathers of Egypt counted Moses among their great ones." Or cut "Around the year 400,".

**S2.** "He was a priest at Scete, out in the desert, and he left seventy disciples." **HELD.**
- LH 337: "So he died in Scete seventy-five years old, having become a priest; and he left seventy disciples."
- LH 283: "the inner desert called Scete".

**S3.** "Yet he began as a household slave, whom Palladius, a bishop writing around 420, calls an Ethiopian by race, and black." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- LH 333: "an Ethiopian by race and black, was house-servant to a government official. His own master drove him out".
- SOZ 39499: "Moses was originally a slave, but was driven from his" master's house.
- Bishop: LH 179, "PALLADIUS THE BISHOP". Date: Palladius says he wrote in "the twentieth year of my episcopate, and the fifty-sixth of my whole life" (LH 185, prologue), to Lausus, chamberlain under Theodosius II (LH 629, 643). The year 419-420 is Clarke's note (LH 657), and it is the Widely Accepted date of the book, so "around 420" may be told plainly.

**S4.** "His master drove him out for immorality and robbery, and he was even said to have murdered." **HELD.**
- LH 333: "His own master drove him out because of his immorality and brigandage. For he was said to go even the length of murder."

**S5.** "Palladius tells of these crimes to show what his repentance could do." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- LH 333: "I am compelled to tell his wicked acts in order to show the virtue of his repentance." "Virtue" here means power or worth, so "what his repentance could do" keeps the sense.

**S6.** "People said Moses went on to lead a band of robbers by the Nile." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- LH 333: "Anyhow they used to say that he was leader of a robber-band". The one exploit told of the band is the swim across "the Nile" in flood, after which "he went off fifty miles further to where he had his band."
- SOZ gives the order: driven out, he joined "robbers, and became leader of the band".
- Note: "by the Nile" is the place of his one told exploit, not a place Palladius gives for the band itself. As a setting for his robber days it is a fair compression, not a finding.

**S7.** "In time, Palladius says only, one of his adventures left him conscience-stricken, and he gave himself to a monastery." **HELD.**
- LH 335: "In the end this abandoned man, conscience-stricken as a result of one of his adventures, gave himself up to a monastery".

**S8.** "Four robbers once attacked him in his cell, not knowing who he was." **HELD.**
- LH 335: "One day robbers attacked him as he sat in his cell, not knowing who it was. They were four in number."

**S9.** "Moses tied them up, carried them on his back to the church, and asked the brothers, 'I am not allowed to hurt anyone. So what do you tell me to do with these?'" **HELD.**
- LH 335: "He tied them all together and, putting them on his back like a truss of straw, brought them to the church of the brethren, saying: 'Since I am not allowed to hurt anyone, what do you bid me do with these?'"
- The rendering keeps every clause of Clarke's English. The Greek is not held.

**S10.** "When the robbers learned who had caught them, they confessed." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- LH 335: "these robbers, having confessed their sins and recognized that it was Moses the erstwhile renowned and far-famed robber". Palladius gives the confessing and the knowing together; "when ... learned" draws them into one turn without adding a cause the scene lacks.

**S11.** "'If a man so great and powerful in robbery has feared God,' they said, 'why should we put off our salvation?'" **HELD.**
- LH 335: "saying to themselves: 'If he who was so great and powerful in brigandage has feared God, why should we defer our salvation?'"

**S12.** "They left the world because of his conversion." **HELD.**
- LH 335: "themselves also glorified God and renounced the world because of his conversion".

**S13.** "His own fight was long, and by his own account his old lust nearly made him give up." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- LH 337: "This Moses was attacked by demons, who tried to plunge him into his old habit of sexual incontinence. He was tempted so greatly, as he himself testified, that he almost relinquished his purpose."
- Note: Palladius frames the temptation as the demons' attack. The brief tells it as his old habit, which is what the attack worked on. No cause or feeling is added. "Long" rests on the six years and the year of illness that follow in LH 337.

**S14.** "For six years he stood all night in his cell, but still he could not master it." **HELD.**
- LH 337: "So he remained in his cell for six years and every night he stood in the middle of the cell praying and not closing his eyes. And he could not master the thing."

**S15.** "In the end Isidore, the priest of Scete, told him his dreams had ceased and sent him to Communion." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- LH 337: "the great Isidore, the priest of Scete"; "In the name of Jesus Christ your dreams have ceased. Come to Communion then with confidence".
- The claim stays in Isidore's mouth, not the narrator's. The blow at the well and the year of illness between are left out, without changing what is told.

**S16.** "Two months later, Moses said he suffered nothing more." **HELD.**
- LH 337: "Afterwards when asked by Isidore, some two months later, he said that he no longer suffered anything."

**S17.** "Sayings gathered in the 600s remember the fathers of Scete scorning him." **SUMMARY HOLDS.**
- PAR2 164: "the Fathers in Scete were gathered together, and because certain folk were wishing to see Abbâ Moses, they treated him with contumely".
- Date: PAR1 105-118, Thomas of Marga (about 840), an old witness, says 'Anan-Isho made the collection at the order of the Patriarch Mar George. A seventh-century date for it is Widely Accepted. "Gathered" keeps the sayings older than the collection.

**S18.** "'Why does this Ethiopian come and go among us?' they said." **HELD.**
- PAR2 164: "saying, 'Why doth this Ethiopian come and go in our midst?'"

**S19.** "He kept silent, and afterwards said he had been afraid but had not said a word." **HELD.**
- PAR2 164: "But Moses hearing this held his peace. And when the congregation was dismissed, they said unto him, 'Abbâ Moses, wast thou not afraid?' And he said unto them, 'Although I was afraid I uttered not a word.'"

**S20.** "Once a bishop, wishing to test him, had the clergy drive him out of the sanctuary." **HELD.**
- PAR2 1028: "wishing to try him, the Bishop said unto the clergy, 'When Abbâ Moses goeth into the sacrarium drive him out'"; "they rebuked him and drove him out".

**S21.** "Outside, he said to himself, 'They have treated you rightly, you whose skin is dark and black. You shall not go back in as if you were a man.'" **HELD.**
- PAR2 1028: "and having gone forth he began to say to himself, 'They have treated thee rightly, O thou whose skin is dark and black; thou shalt not go back as if thou wert a [white] man.'"
- Rendering: Budge's square brackets mark words he supplies, as elsewhere in this book ("[only]", "[and say]", PAR2 88). The Syriac is not held. Leaving out "[white]" follows the text without Budge's supply, and a held parallel reads "not a man": DOR 136342-136344, "Sterquilinium cum sis et cinis, non homo; cur ad homines ingrederis?" "Go back in" is what the scene gives: he has just been driven out of the sanctuary. "Dark and black" is kept, and the address is not turned into a reason.

**S22.** "Palladius says he died at Scete, aged seventy-five." **HELD.**
- LH 337: "So he died in Scete seventy-five years old".

## The heart

**The heart is right, and it is told in time.**
- The sources show a slave driven out for crime who became one of the great fathers of Scete, a priest who left seventy disciples (LH 333, 337), whose life Palladius tells to show what repentance can do (LH 333), and whose change drew robbers out of the world (LH 335). That is the heart this brief tells.
- The essence is told by word 80 (S1-S5): who he became, what he had been, and why his life was told. The robbers who left the world "because of his conversion" close at about word 197, past the first minute, but the essence and its weight are already told by then.
- The shape is the monk's: the turn, the long fight, the life others followed. Wonder stays out of the narrator's voice; the sayings are credited; the scorn and his words against himself keep their force.
- At 353 words the brief sits inside the person range and near the approved length.

**Verdict: BLOCKING on S1 only.** Cut "Around the year 400," from the first sentence. Everything else holds.
