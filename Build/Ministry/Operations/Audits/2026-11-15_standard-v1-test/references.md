# References as the visitor will see them

## Gregory of Tours

### Sources
**Their own words**
- Gregory of Tours, *Historia Francorum* and *Miracula*, Latin, ed. W. Arndt and Bruno Krusch, MGH Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum 1 (1885)
- Gregory of Tours, *The History of the Franks*, vol. 2, tr. O. M. Dalton (1927)
- Gregory of Tours, *History of the Franks*, selections, tr. Ernest Brehaut (1916)

**Written about them**
- Paul the Deacon, *History of the Langobards*, tr. W. D. Foulke (1907)
- Venantius Fortunatus, Poems, ed. F. Leo and B. Krusch, MGH Auctores antiquissimi 4 (1881): V.3, on Gregory's arrival at Tours; the poem to Chilperic from the synod at Berny
- Ernest Brehaut, Introduction (1916); O. M. Dalton, Notes (1927); W. Arndt, Preface, and Bruno Krusch, Prolegomena (1885)

### What the brief draws on
- History of the Franks, first preface, tr. Brehaut: kings growing more cruel, liberal culture perishing, "Woe to our day", his rude speech.
- History of the Franks I, preface, tr. Dalton: the creed, "that whoso reads may doubt not that I hold the catholic faith".
- History of the Franks I.38, tr. Dalton: Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, banished at the persuasion of the heretics.
- Glory of the Confessors 3, Latin, ed. Arndt and Krusch: Eusebius of Vercelli, Hilary's great helper against heresy.
- History of the Franks V, preface, tr. Brehaut: the civil wars among the Frankish kings.
- History of the Franks V.31 (43), tr. Dalton: Agilan, envoy of King Leuvigild; the argument at Tours; his oath; his conversion in Spain under sickness.
- History of the Franks V.32 (44), tr. Dalton: Chilperic's decree, Gregory's answer, Salvius of Albi, "So the king desisted from his purpose".
- History of the Franks X.31, tr. Dalton: Gregory the nineteenth bishop; the cathedral burned and rebuilt; Martin consecrated in it; his ten books.
- Miracles of Martin II.1, Latin, ed. Arndt and Krusch: his consecration in Sigibert's twelfth year (573).
- Paul the Deacon, History of the Langobards III.1, tr. Foulke: "the books of the reverend man Gregory, bishop of Tours". Paul wrote in the late 700s, about two centuries after Gregory.
- World census, Merovingian Gallic Christianity: Gregory's tradition on the tree.

### Moved from the brief to What is uncertain
- How far to trust him. Ernest Brehaut calls the History often the sole authority for the events it tells, and warns that readers "must allow largely for error and prejudice in his statements of fact".
- His trial at Berny. Gregory's account is the accused man's own. Venantius Fortunatus's poem to Chilperic for that synod survives, and O. M. Dalton notes that it praises Queen Fredegund and says nothing of the affair.

## Benedict of Nursia

### Sources
**Their own words**
- *The Rule of Saint Benedict*, tr. Francis Aidan Gasquet (1925)
- *The Rule of St. Benedict*, Latin and English, ed. and tr. D. Oswald Hunter Blair (1907)

**Written about them**
- Gregory the Great, *Dialogues*, English translation of 1608, ed. Edmund G. Gardner (1911)
- *Monumenta Germaniae Historica, Capitularia regum Francorum* I (Hannover, 1881): Carloman's capitulary of 742, with Boniface's letter; the council of Estinnes; the annals of Lorsch for 802
- John Cassian, *Conferences*, in Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, second series, vol. 11
- Edmund G. Gardner, Introduction and notes (1911); Francis Aidan Gasquet, Introduction (1925)

### What the brief draws on
- Dialogues II, prologue: born in the province of Nursia, sent to Rome to study, students falling into loose living; his four disciples as Gregory's sources. Book II is the only early Life of Benedict, and Gregory tells it to show the miracles of a holy man.
- World census, Early Benedictine / Italian Monasticism: Benedict's tradition on the tree.
- Dialogues II.3: the monks who made him abbot, the poisoned wine and the broken glass; the twelve monasteries.
- Dialogues II.8: Cassino, the worship of Apollo, the chapel of Martin, the preaching.
- Dialogues II.15: Totila, "Much wickedness do you daily commit", the king less cruel after.
- Dialogues II.17: the Lombards' sack of Monte Cassino.
- Dialogues II.36: the Rule, "excellent for discretion and also eloquent for the style".
- Dialogues IV.30: Theodoric, king of the Goths in Italy.
- Rule, Prologue: "a school for the service of God, in which we hope nothing harsh nor burdensome will be ordained".
- Rule, ch. 48: manual labour and holy reading. Ch. 58: "stability, amendment of manners and obedience". Ch. 64: the abbot who tempers all things. Ch. 73: the Lives of the Fathers, the Rule of Basil, the Collations.
- Cassian, Institutes and Conferences: his own title for the Conferences of the Fathers, the book the Rule calls the Collations.
- MGH Capitularia I, no. 10: Boniface on Carloman, duke of the Franks, and the church trampled for sixty or seventy years; the council of 742 and the Rule for monks and nuns.
- MGH Capitularia I, no. 11 (Estinnes): the abbots and monks receive the Rule "ad restaurandam normam regularis vitae".
- MGH Capitularia I, the Aachen synod of 802, from the annals of Lorsch: the Rule read, and the monasteries put right by it.

### Moved from the brief to What is uncertain
- His years. Gregory gives none. Edmund Gardner gives his birth as about 480, and Francis Gasquet in 480, and the tradition of his order dates his death to 543. Gardner dates the Dialogues to 593 and puts Benedict's leaving Rome a few years before 500.
- What the Rule did. Gasquet, a Benedictine editor, judges that the Rule put a written code in place of an abbot's personal will, and calls it practically the only monastic Rule of the West from the ninth to the twelfth century.
- The second council. The edition records that Hefele and Hahn held the council of Estinnes to be Carloman's and Pippin's together; its editor argues it was Carloman's.
