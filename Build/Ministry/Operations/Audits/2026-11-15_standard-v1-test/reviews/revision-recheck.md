# Targeted recheck: Gregory of Tours v2, Benedict of Nursia v2

I compared v2 against final, sentence by sentence. I made no model calls and changed no repo file.

Changed in Gregory: opening (3 sentences), the Hilary sentence, the close. Changed in Benedict: opening (3 sentences), "Gregory tells that", the Subiaco sentence, the Cassian sentence, and the two Carolingian sentences.

## A. The three rules

**Gregory of Tours**
- Rule 1 (who says what): met. Every attribution left is Gregory's own words or his one-sided account ("he writes", "he admits", "Gregory writes", "In his telling", "Gregory reports"). Each one is warranted. Paul the Deacon appears as an event in the story, not as an attribution. No witness is named needlessly.
- Rule 2 (setting): met. Sentence 1 gives 573, Tours, Gaul and the rival Frankish kings. Sentence 2 gives what was going on. Sentence 3 is the scene.
- Rule 3 (later scholars): met. Dalton and Brehaut are both gone from the brief.

**Benedict of Nursia**
- Rule 1: mostly met. "Gregory tells that" is kept on the poison and glass wonder, and "Gregory says" is kept on the idol and on Totila. Boniface stays named; he is a single, interested witness. Lorsch and "its acts say" were dropped, which is fair (see B).
- Rule 1 also has one slip. The students' "loose living" moved into the plain setting sentence, ahead of "Gregory the Great tells". It is Gregory's detail alone (Dialogues II prol., gardner1911 l.3416–17). **NON-BLOCKING.** Fold it back inside his telling, e.g. "...sent to Rome to study. When he saw many of the students there fall into loose living, he left..."
- Rule 2: met. Sentence 1 gives around 500, Italy, Rome, the Arian Gothic kings and the student life.
- Rule 3: met. Gasquet is gone.

## B. Sources for changed and new sentences

**Gregory of Tours**
- "Kings were growing more cruel, he writes, and learning was dying." Supported by Brehaut l.842–844, Gregory's first preface: "liberal culture on the wane, or rather perishing ... kings were growing more cruel".
- "split among rival Frankish kings at war": Dalton l.7901, "the divers civil wars". "In 573": Brehaut l.12231, "It was in 573 that Gregory became bishop of Tours". Both fine.
- "It was the church where Martin of Tours had been made bishop ... rebuilt it larger." Dalton l.22655–22661. Fine.
- **Hilary.** Dalton l.1286–1288 (I.38) has "The most blessed Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, was sent into banishment at the persuasion of the heretics." Dalton's note at l.26986 reads V.44's Hilary as Hilary of Poitiers (cf. I.38). Reading him that way is fair:
  - Gregory names no other Hilary as a teacher of the faith.
  - Pairing him with Eusebius is the standard Nicene pairing.
  - Gregory himself ties the name to I.38.
  - "Heretics had ... driven into exile" is a fair paraphrase of "sent into banishment at the persuasion of the heretics".
  
  **NON-BLOCKING:** the card should cite I.38 and Dalton's note.
- "Much of what is known of the church in Gaul under the first Frankish kings comes from those books." This is widely accepted, and Brehaut l.65–69 calls the History "often the sole authority". Plain statement is fine. **NON-BLOCKING:** Brehaut belongs on the card.
- **"in the quarrels Gregory took part in, his is the only side we have."** No vendored file says this, and the corpus partly contradicts it. venantius-fortunatus_opera-mgh-aa-4-lat_leo-krusch1881.txt l.17820 has "Ad Chilpericum regem quando synodus Brinnaco habita est". That is Fortunatus's poem to Chilperic at the Berny synod where Gregory was tried, so another contemporary voice survives from at least one of Gregory's quarrels. The claim is also a sweeping "only", which pattern item 4 flags. It also quietly replaces Brehaut's warning to "allow largely for error and prejudice" with a broader claim. **BLOCKING (rigor).** Fix: narrow it to the quarrels the brief tells, e.g. "and for his stands against Chilperic and Agilan, his is the only account we have." That is true in the corpus and keeps the caution.

**Benedict of Nursia**
- **"Around the year 500, Italy was ruled by Gothic kings who followed the teaching of Arius."**
  - The Arian part is sourced: Dialogues IV.30, "the Arian king Theodoricus" (gardner1911 l.542, body l.10528ff). Totila is "king of the Goths" (l.6019), and Galla is "an Arian heretic, who, in the time of King Totilas" (l.5213–14).
  - The date is sourced: Gardner l.13219–13221 (Theodoric ruling from 493, and in Rome in 500), and l.12628–30 ("born at Nursia about 480 ... leaving Rome is disputed; it was probably a few years before 500").
  - "Around 500" is a fair and widely accepted hedge. Fine.
- "Seeing what became of the others, he left his books and his father's house." gardner1911 l.3416–3422. Fine.
- "He built twelve monasteries near Subiaco and later moved to Cassino ... Apollo." l.3847 and l.4174–4181. Fine. The earlier frame "Nearly all we know ... Gregory's Dialogues" covers it.
- **Cassian.** The Rule ch.73 (Gasquet l.4288–4290) names "the Collations of the Fathers, their Institutes, their Lives, and the Rule of our Holy Father St. Basil". npnf211 l.16050–16057 quotes this very chapter as "the Conferences of the Fathers" in its account of Cassian's works. Cassian's own Institutes announce his "Conferences of the Fathers" (l.15528). So the identification rests on Cassian's own titles, not only on a scholar, and plain statement is fair. Fine.
- **742 council.** Pertz l.2324 (c.7) has "monachi et ancillae Dei ... iuxta regulam sancti Benedicti". The next council (Estinnes), l.2450–2451, has "Abbates et monachi receperunt sancti patris Benedicti [regulam] ad restaurandam normam regularis vitae". These are the council's own acts, so dropping "its acts say" is fine. Fine.
- **802 Aachen.** Pertz l.7792–7805 quotes Ann. Lauresh. a.802 ("legerunt regulam sancti patris Benedicti ... contra regula sancti Benedicti factum fuisset, hoc ipsud iuxta ipsam regulam ... emendare"). The reading scene rests on this one annal. But the annal is contemporary and not partisan. The same order is corroborated by the 802 general capitulary ("Ut abbates regulares et monachi regulam intellegant et secundum regulam [vivant]", around l.7675), and Pertz prints the annal as the record of the synod. Plain statement is acceptable. **NON-BLOCKING:** the card must cite Ann. Lauresh. a.802.

## E. Craft on the new openings

I read each opening beside Athanasius ("In 335, at a council in Tyre, Athanasius's accusers held up a severed hand.") and John Chrysostom ("In 399, the emperor's chief eunuch, Eutropius, fled into the cathedral...").

**Gregory of Tours: still opens as story.**
- It sets date, place and the warring kings, then goes to the burnt cathedral and his decision to write. The story's line holds: "a bishop in a violent, unlettered Gaul rebuilds his church, writes its history, and holds his creed against a king and an Arian envoy."
- Weakest link: "Kings were growing more cruel, he writes, and learning was dying." It says early what the lament two sentences later pays off ("the pursuit of letters has perished"). That is a small double beat, and the cruelty clause does not lead into the cathedral. **NON-BLOCKING.** Cut "and learning was dying" so the lament does that work.
- "Gregory of Tours became bishop of Tours" is clunky, but rule 8 makes it near-forced. **NON-BLOCKING.**

**Benedict of Nursia: still opens as story.**
- Setting and scene merge in one move: the Goths, the students, one young man walking away. It is not a flat fact list.
- Weakest link: the Arian clause connects to nothing until Totila in paragraph 2, and the brief never calls Totila Arian. It reads as context, not a hanging sentence. **NON-BLOCKING.**

## Verdict

- **Gregory of Tours v2: DOES NOT CLEAR.** One BLOCKING finding: "in the quarrels Gregory took part in, his is the only side we have". No source supports it, it overstates, and the corpus partly contradicts it (Fortunatus's poem to Chilperic from the Berny synod). Narrowing it to the quarrels the brief tells clears it. Everything else holds.
- **Benedict of Nursia v2: CLEARS.** The non-blocking items are recommended: put "loose living" back inside Gregory's telling, cite the Lorsch annals on the card, and watch the length.
