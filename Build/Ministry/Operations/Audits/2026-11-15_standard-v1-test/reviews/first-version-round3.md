## BENEDICT OF NURSIA, draft 3

### Round-2 blocking findings

**B2-1, the wrong time span "Two centuries later": RESOLVED.** The sentence now reads "In 742 Archbishop Boniface wrote that Carloman, duke of the Franks, wanted to repair a church trampled for sixty or seventy years." The content is supported by pertz1881 l.2199-2205.

NON-BLOCKING: the held lines date the council (742, l.2199) but not the letter itself. The editor places the letter just before the 742 synod, whose canons he says confirm it ("Bonifacii illi epistolae argumento propinqui", l.2207). "In 742" is the editor's placement. "Before his council of 742" would claim only what the lines show. The year is not in doubt, so this does not block.

**B2-2, "Boniface" without an identifier: RESOLVED.** The draft now says "Archbishop Boniface", which matches "Bonifacius archiepiscopus" at l.2200.

### Other changed sentences, checked against the held files

- **"When the Gothic king Totila came to him, Benedict told him, 'Much wickedness do you daily commit.'"** "To test" is dropped, which removes the merging of the Riggo test with Totila's own visit. "Italy was at war" is dropped. Both take up round-2 non-blocking items. It is supported: Totila goes to him in person at Gardner l.4462-4471, and the quote is verbatim at l.4472-4473.
- **"Gregory the Great, by then pope, praised the Rule as 'excellent for discretion and also eloquent for the style,' though by the time he wrote, he says, the Lombards had sacked Monte Cassino."** This takes up the round-2 note that he was never called pope, and drops the unattributed "but the Rule went on".
  - **"By then pope":** the coordinator's l.1223 ("Gregory's pontificate") shows that Gregory was pope, but not when the Dialogues were written. The direct support is Gardner's introduction at l.1022-1026: the Dialogues "were written in 593, three years after his elevation to the papacy". That is a modern editor's date, but "by then pope" claims only what it gives. Supported.
  - **The quote:** verbatim, Gardner l.5565-5566.
  - **The sack:** Gardner l.4657-4659, "that very Abbey to be now suppressed by the Lombards. For not long since, in the night time ... they entered in, and spoiled all things". "He says" correctly gives the sack to Gregory.
  - NON-BLOCKING: this is the second full "Gregory the Great". The naming rule uses the short name, "Gregory", at second mention. Here the full form anchors "by then pope", so it reads fine.
- **"Carloman's first council told monks and nuns to live by Benedict's Rule, and at the next the abbots and monks took it up, its acts say, to restore the norm of the regular life."**
  - The first council is supported: l.2324-2325, "ut monachi et ancillae Dei monasteriales iuxta regulam sancti Benedicti ... vivere ... studeant". The editor calls it the first ("synodi, quae Germanica prima vulgo dicitur", l.2206).
  - "At the next" is supported: the editor calls Estinnes the other synod ("alterius synodi capita Liptinis", l.2342-2343) and gives Carloman as its author (l.2361-2364).
  - The quoted purpose is supported: l.2449-2451, "receperunt sancti patris Benedicti [regulam] ad restaurandam normam regularis vitae".
  - Round 2's "the year after" (an uncertain date stated as fact) and its "the council says" (a plural councils with a singular verb) are both fixed. Both take up round-2 non-blocking items.
  - NON-BLOCKING: the editor reports that some scholars (Hefele, Hahn) give Estinnes to Carloman and Pippin together (l.2359-2361). He rejects that, and "Carloman's" follows him. It is a dispute over who convened the council and bears on nothing else, so it belongs on the card under What is uncertain.

### Round-2 non-blocking items

- **Taken up:** "Italy was at war", the merged Totila visits, "the Rule went on", the missing "pope", "the year after", and the plural with the singular verb.
- **Not taken up:** the Gasquet clause naming Cassian, which places a census thread name and is fine to keep, and the "about fifty years" modern date, which is safe on both counts.

### Reports

- **CRAFT: PASS.**
  - Test 1, storytelling: the line runs from leaving Rome, to Gregory's wonders, to the Rule, to the Franks taking it up to restore their monasteries.
  - Test 2, flow: the joins are in the sentences ("when he held them to a strict life", "by his preaching", "From then on", "though by the time he wrote", "to restore"). The joining-word count is 0.5 per 100, which the counter undercounts, as round 2 noted.
  - Test 3, no AI tells: none found.
  - Test 4, no hanging sentences: none. The close on 802 finishes the story, with its witness named.
  - Test 5, everything relevant: every sentence serves the story.
- **RIGOR: PASS.** Every changed sentence is supported, and every wonder is told as Gregory's. Mark's source ruling is met: Benedict's own Rule, Gregory's Dialogues told as Gregory's, and the Frankish capitularies and annals for the impact. The modern editor appears only to place the Collations.
- **ACCESS: PASS.** Grade 9.0, reading ease 78.5. The longest sentence, about 33 words (the council sentence), reads in one breath.
- **SHAPE+LENGTH: PASS.** Personal first, then the stance (the Rule), then the impact through 742, its successor council and 802. At 416 words it is about 41 past the stretch. Round 2's two padding candidates are now cut. What remains is the sourced chain of consequences Mark asked for, so the length is earned.

**VERDICT, BENEDICT OF NURSIA: CLEARS.** No blocking finding remains.
